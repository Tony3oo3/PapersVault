## Problem

Quantization of Codec Avatar decoders is an unexplored problem: the paper reveals that even well-established 8-bit PTQ and QAT methods introduce massive temporal noise (random pixel flickering) into rendered avatars, which is invisible in static frames but severely degrades dynamic facial motion. The root cause is identified as the dynamically long-tailed distribution of facial texture activations, which stretches the quantization scaling factor and amplifies rounding instability across consecutive frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241062/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

POCA takes a pre-trained full-precision VAE decoder (texture + mesh) and calibrates per-layer quantization parameters using only 512 samples per identity (≤5% of the Multiface dataset) and ≤10% of each layer's activation pixels. The quantized decoder receives a latent code z and view direction v, producing a 1024×1024 texture map and mesh that are rendered into a 3D avatar. The view-dependent visibility mask is computed at calibration time by rasterizing the decoded mesh, interpolating UV coordinates, and generating a binary mask that selects only the pixels visible from the given viewing angle.

## Method

POCA combines two masking strategies applied to the layer-wise PTQ loss. First, view-aware PTQ computes a binary visibility mask Γ from the rendering pipeline (mesh rasterization → barycentric interpolation → UV sampling), restricting the quantization objective to only the pixels that will actually appear in the rendered avatar. Second, Shape-Wise Dynamics (SWD) filtering computes a per-pixel standard deviation score across channels and masks out the top τ% (optimal τ=0.9) of pixels with the highest tailedness, producing a sparse mask Θ. The final calibration loss is the MSE between full-precision and quantized outputs, weighted by the element-wise product Γ⊙Θ, so quantizers are learned from the most relevant and most quantization-friendly pixels. This keeps the learned scaling factor S^l small, reducing the interval between adjacent quantization levels and thereby suppressing the rounding instability that causes temporal noise. The entire calibration completes in ~13 minutes on a single A6000 GPU, compressing the decoder by 5.3× (6-bit) while matching full-precision FovVideoVDP scores.

## Results

On the Multiface dataset (8 avatars, 3 viewing directions, 65 expressions), POCA at W8A8 achieves FovVideoVDP scores within 0.05–0.15 of the FP32 baseline (e.g., 6.19 vs. 6.21 for avatar 2643814 Front), while SoTA PTQ (AdaRound+LSQ) drops by 0.3–0.6 and even W8A8 QAT drops by 0.2–0.5. At W6A6, POCA still outperforms SoTA PTQ W8A8 on most avatars (e.g., 6.06 vs. 5.81 for 2643814 Front), demonstrating that 6-bit quantization with POCA exceeds 8-bit without it. The decoder is compressed by 5.3× (32.78 MB → 6.15 MB) with only ~13 min calibration on a single A6000 GPU, versus >10 hrs for QAT on 2×A6000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POCA decisively outperforms SoTA PTQ (AdaRound+LSQ [2,18]) at both W8A8 and W6A6: at W8A8, POCA recovers FovVideoVDP scores within ~0.1 of FP32 while SoTA PTQ loses 0.3–0.6 points; at W6A6, the gap widens to 1.0–1.5 points. POCA also surpasses PyTorch native W8A8 QAT [20] by 0.2–0.5 FovVideoVDP points, and remarkably, POCA at W6A6 exceeds QAT at W8A8 on most avatars (e.g., 6.06 vs. 5.92 for 2643814 Front), showing that the proposed view-aware + SWD calibration is more effective than full retraining. For the super-resolution extension, POCA-SWD with 80% calibration sparsity outperforms SR-PTQ [25] on BSD100 (27.620 vs. 27.618 PSNR) and Urban100 (26.195 vs. 26.191 PSNR) at W8A6, and by a larger margin at W6A6 (27.565 vs. 27.561 on BSD100; 26.110 vs. 26.011 on Urban100), while using only 20% of the calibration pixels that dense baselines require.

## Relation to Prior Work

POCA builds directly on AdaRound (weight quantization) and LSQ (activation quantization) as its underlying quantization framework, but departs from prior PTQ work by introducing two task-specific mechanisms: view-dependent visibility masking (leveraging the rendering pipeline's mesh rasterization to restrict calibration to visible pixels) and Shape-Wise Dynamics filtering (suppressing high-variance activation regions). The paper explicitly positions itself as the first work on low-precision avatar decoding, noting that prior quantization research targeted classification, detection, NLP, and static image rendering, none of which address the temporal consistency requirement inherent to animated avatar sequences. The super-resolution experiments extend the SWD component to a second task, building on SR-PTQ [25] but achieving better quality with 80% calibration sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to identify and resolve temporal noise as a critical failure mode of quantized avatar decoders, which has direct practical implications for on-device AR/VR deployment (e.g., Quest Pro) where cloud offloading is undesirable. The 5.3× compression at 6-bit with quality on par with FP32, achieved in ~13 minutes on a single GPU versus >10 hours for QAT, makes instant user-to-avatar conversion feasible. Key limitations include the focus on VAE-based Codec Avatars (3D Gaussian-based avatars remain unaddressed), evaluation on a single dataset (Multiface) with 8 identities, and the absence of a full ablation isolating the individual contributions of view-aware masking versus SWD filtering. Open questions include whether the temporal noise phenomenon and the proposed solution generalize to other generative 3D avatar architectures and to even lower precisions (4-bit).
