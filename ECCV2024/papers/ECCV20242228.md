## Problem

fMRI-to-video decoding has been restricted to within-subject pipelines because anatomical and functional topography differences across individuals prevent a single model from generalizing to unseen subjects. This limitation is compounded by the scarcity of paired fMRI-stimulus data, with prior benchmarks containing only ~5.5k samples per subject. The paper tackles both barriers by proposing a learnable functional alignment that maps diverse brains into a shared space and by releasing a substantially larger multi-subject dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242228/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential fMRI frames (αT = 4 s window) that have been anatomically aligned to the 32k_fs_LR surface, restricted to 8,405 visual-cortex vertices from the HCP-MMP atlas, unfolded into 256×256 single-channel images with a 6-second hemodynamic lag correction. It produces a T-second reconstructed video (2 s or 4 s at 3–8 FPS depending on the source dataset) via a VQGAN decoder applied to denoised latent frames. For multi-run recordings of the same stimulus, fMRI frames are averaged before feeding the encoder.

## Method

The core contribution is Global-Local Functional Alignment (GLFA), a two-level projection that first applies a parcel-level global matrix (reducing the full voxel-to-voxel mapping to a compact W^parcel learned over H_p×W_p parcels) and then a locally connected 2D layer with unshared per-voxel weights to capture fine-grained neighborhood disparities. GLFA is trained with a combined alignment loss (Frobenius-norm distance between aligned fMRI of different subjects viewing the same stimulus) and a consistency loss (preserving original fMRI information), allowing it to be plugged into any gradient-based fMRI decoder. The full pipeline stacks a ViT-based fMRI-PTE encoder, an appended spatiotemporal attention module (using the network-inflation trick to interleave spatial and temporal attention without disturbing pretrained weights), and a ModelScopeT2V video generator whose UNet cross-attention is conditioned on the fMRI embedding. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule: (1) fMRI-PTE fine-tuning with reconstruction loss, (2) CLIP contrastive learning projecting fMRI embeddings into the CLIP latent space, and (3) joint denoising-diffusion training where the fMRI embedding replaces text conditioning in the video UNet, with L_GLFA added to the denoising objective to simultaneously align fMRI at both voxel and embedding levels.

## Results

On the cross-subject fMRI-to-video task, GLFA achieves an average 2-way video semantic accuracy of 84.1% on the benchmark dataset (Wen et al.) and 80.6% on the larger Fudan dataset, outperforming the LEA baseline by 3–5 percentage points. The best single cross-subject result is 84.7% 2-way video accuracy (task 1,2→3 on the benchmark). In within-subject decoding, fMRI-PTE-V (without GLFA) matches or slightly exceeds the state-of-the-art MinD-Video on 50-way accuracy (e.g., 0.225 vs. 0.216 for subject 3 on the benchmark) while surpassing LEA by 10+ points on 50-way metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLFA consistently outperforms the LEA baseline [25] across all cross-subject tasks and both datasets, with the largest gains on 50-way video accuracy (e.g., 0.193 vs. 0.136 on benchmark task 1,2→3, a 42% relative improvement) and SSIM (0.172 vs. 0.139). Against fMRI-PTE-V (the same architecture without GLFA), the alignment module yields modest but consistent improvements in cross-subject settings, particularly on SSIM (up to +0.030) and 50-way frame accuracy, confirming that functional alignment is the key differentiator for cross-subject generalization. In within-subject decoding, fMRI-PTE-V matches or slightly exceeds MinD-Video [6] on 50-way accuracy (0.225 vs. 0.216 for subject 3 on the benchmark) while being clearly superior to LEA by 10+ points on 50-way metrics, validating the fMRI-PTE + diffusion pipeline as a competitive within-subject approach. Notably, GLFA does not always beat fMRI-PTE-V on 2-way video accuracy in cross-subject settings (e.g., 0.838 vs. 0.844 for task 1,3→2 on the benchmark), suggesting the alignment benefit is more pronounced on finer-grained semantic and structural metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fMRI-to-video reconstruction line initiated by MinD-Video [6] and Wen et al. [40] from within-subject to cross-subject decoding by introducing GLFA, a differentiable functional alignment module that unifies the parcel-level global alignment of Bazeille et al. [2] with a locally connected 2D layer for voxel-level correspondence, inspired by Pairwise Cortical Alignment [7]. The encoder backbone is fMRI-PTE [26], and the video generation stage repurposes ModelScopeT2V [36] by substituting text embeddings with fMRI embeddings in the UNet's cross-attention, following the progressive training recipe (contrastive learning → co-training with diffusion) established by MinD-Video. The key conceptual departure is making functional alignment a trainable, loss-driven component integrated into the end-to-end decoding pipeline rather than a fixed preprocessing step, as in MindTuner [14] or MindBridge [37].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75k-sample, 8-subject fMRI-video dataset (4.5× the prior benchmark) is a substantial practical resource that directly addresses the data scarcity bottleneck in fMRI-to-video research and enables meaningful cross-subject evaluation. GLFA's formulation as a gradient-descent-compatible loss makes it a drop-in module for any fMRI decoding architecture, potentially benefiting image, text, and 3D-object decoding tasks beyond video. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 subjects and two video datasets, the 6-second BOLD lag correction is a coarse simplification, and the cross-subject baselines (LEA) are relatively weak, leaving open whether the alignment gains persist against stronger cross-subject decoders or scale to hundreds of subjects with diverse anatomies.
