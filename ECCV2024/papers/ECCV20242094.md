## Problem

Saliency prediction models are bottlenecked by the small size of available labeled datasets (at most ~10k images), since collecting ground-truth fixation data via psychophysical experiments is costly and slow. Standard data augmentation techniques (rotation, cropping, shearing) are not saliency-invariant — they alter scene composition and thus human gaze patterns — making them ineffective for this task. The paper asks whether image saliency can be modified in a predictable, controllable direction while preserving the rest of the scene, enabling principled data augmentation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a text prompt (MS-COCO caption), and a ground-truth saliency map as input. It produces two outputs: an edited image with a controlled photometric change (contrast, brightness, or color) applied to a specific salient region, and a predicted saliency map. Editing is performed entirely in the 4-channel latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.5, with a channel-wise scaling factor γ mapping RGB-space edit magnitudes into latent space to avoid oversaturation. The saliency-guided cross-attention mechanism automatically localizes the target region without requiring user-provided masks.

## Method

The architecture builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 encoder, decoder, and denoising U-Net. Intermediate U-Net features are aggregated with bottleneck layers (following Luo et al.) and split into low-level features (read out by 7 small MLPs predicting R, G, B, brightness, local contrast, and global contrast) and high-level features (read out by a single convolutional classifier for object semantics). A 7-layer convolutional Saliency Readout with SiLU activations and skip connections fuses both feature streams to predict the saliency map. For editing, a saliency-guided cross-attention mechanism multiplies the text-image cross-attention maps by the ground-truth saliency to identify the most salient region corresponding to a prompt word; the selected spatial map is injected into the U-Net alongside a modified prompt (e.g., an empty token for contrast/brightness edits, a target color word for color edits). Three latent-space edit operations — contrast scaling, brightness offset, and color association — are applied, with a constraining mechanism that reduces edit strength if the LLFR output deviates more than two standard deviations from the original. Training uses three losses: L2/cross-entropy readout losses, a saliency loss combining correlation coefficient and KLD, and a binary cross-entropy editing loss on the masked target region.

## Results

On the SALICON (LSUN 2017) test benchmark, the proposed method achieves AUC 0.870, KL 0.191, NSS 1.973, CC 0.914, SAUC 0.744, and SIM 0.805, outperforming state-of-the-art saliency predictors on 3 out of 6 metrics (AUC, CC, SIM). The data augmentation component consistently improves all four baseline models (SimpleNet, TempSAL, DeepGaze IIE, UNISAL) on the SALICON validation set across all four reported metrics (KL, NSS, CC, SIM). A user study with 8 participants and 150 edited images confirms that the photometric edits significantly shift human fixation toward the edited regions (p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best AUC (0.870 vs. 0.869 for DSCLRCN, TempSAL, SimpleNet, and RINet), best CC (0.914 vs. 0.911 for TempSAL and RINet), and best SIM (0.805 vs. 0.803 for RINet) on the SALICON test set. It trails RINet slightly on KL (0.191 vs. 0.189) and SAUC (0.744 vs. 0.746), and is substantially behind MDSEM on NSS (1.973 vs. 2.058). On the augmentation ablation (Table 2, SALICON validation), the method improves every baseline: SimpleNet gains +0.012 KL and +0.008 SIM, TempSAL gains +0.017 KL and +0.004 SIM, DeepGaze IIE gains +0.056 KL and +0.016 SIM, and UNISAL gains +0.014 KL and +0.026 SIM. The paper also reports that 12 classical augmentation methods (rotation, flipping, cropping, JPEG, blur, etc.) mostly degrade performance, with only horizontal flip showing a marginal benefit, underscoring the necessity of saliency-aware editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 architecture (encoder, decoder, and denoising U-Net) as a feature extractor for saliency prediction, adopting the multi-level feature aggregation strategy from Diffusion Hyperfeatures [42]. It departs from prior diffusion-based image editing work (Prompt-to-Prompt, InstructPix2Pix) by introducing a saliency-guided cross-attention mechanism that localizes edits to photometric properties (contrast, brightness, color) rather than semantic content. The key conceptual departure from prior saliency prediction is treating data augmentation as a controllable image-editing problem in latent space, where the direction of saliency change is known a priori, rather than relying on geometric transforms that are not saliency-invariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in saliency prediction—the scarcity of labeled data (only ~10k images in the largest dataset)—by showing that photometric edits in a diffusion latent space can generate new image-saliency pairs with predictable saliency shifts, validated by an eye-tracking user study. However, the user study involves only 8 participants and 150 images, and the performance gains over SOTA are modest (best on 3 of 6 metrics), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger datasets or more complex editing scenarios. A key open question is whether the controllability and interpretability of photometric edits can extend to more nuanced saliency factors (e.g., social cues, motion) or to video saliency prediction.
