## Problem

Large-scale multi-view roadside 3D perception datasets are scarce: existing infrastructure-side datasets (Rope3D, A9, V2X-Seq) cover only small areas with sparse, isolated camera layouts that produce blind spots, and rely on expensive LiDAR for 3D annotation. Moreover, current BEV detection methods—both explicit and implicit—degrade significantly when faced with the vast perception range (~6× larger than nuScenes) and the variation of sensor layouts across scenes, leaving a gap in both data and algorithmic readiness for real-world roadside deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6–12 synchronized 4K roadside camera images per scene sample (captured at 25 Hz from poles >10 m high with varying focal lengths and pitch angles) along with calibrated camera intrinsic/extrinsic parameters, operating within a fixed BEV cuboid of X∈[−400,400], Y∈[−40,40], Z∈[0,6] meters. It outputs per-vehicle 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, yaw), one of four class labels (car, van, bus, truck), and clip-level tracking IDs. The dataset itself provides 21.13 M annotated 3D boxes over 1.30 M images across 14 highway scenes, with an average of 123 boxes per scene sample and up to 567 vehicles in a single frame—roughly 3× denser than nuScenes or Rope3D.

## Method

RoBEV operates within the implicit BEV paradigm (no dense BEV feature map) and learns a set of 3D position queries that aggregate 2D image features via cross-attention. The key innovation is a feature-guided 3D position embedding: instead of the static sinusoidal or per-camera learnable embeddings used by PETRv2, RoBEV predicts a single 3D reference point per pixel along each camera ray conditioned on the image features, then passes both these points and the 3D queries through a shared learnable network φ(·) to produce camera-layout-aware spatial embeddings in a common feature space. This makes the 2D-to-3D feature assignment dynamic and adaptive to novel sensor configurations, while constraining the reference points to the BEV cuboid (rather than the full discretized frustum) simplifies learning and accelerates convergence. The backbone is VoVNetV2-99 pretrained on nuScenes, and the full pipeline is trained end-to-end with standard DETR-style Hungarian matching and focal loss.

## Results

On the RoScenes validation set, RoBEV achieves 0.753 NDS / 0.684 mAP on the Easy split and 0.672 NDS / 0.524 mAP on the Hard split, yielding an average NDS of 0.713 — the best among all tested methods. Implicit BEV methods (DETR3D, PETRv2, StreamPETR, RoBEV) outperform explicit methods (BEVDet, BEVDet4D, SOLOFusion, BEVFormer) by approximately 19.7% in average NDS, attributed to the avoidance of dense BEV feature map construction over the large 800×80 m² perception area. On the zero-shot unseen test set (scenes #005–#014), all methods suffer a severe drop, with none exceeding 0.308 NDS or 0.024 mAP, highlighting the poor generalization of current BEV architectures to novel sensor layouts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoBEV outperforms all eight baselines on both Easy and Hard validation splits. Against the strongest implicit baseline DETR3D, RoBEV gains +3.5% average NDS (0.713 vs. 0.678), and against PETRv2 the gap widens to +8.6% (0.713 vs. 0.627), which the authors attribute to PETRv2's static position embedding failing under varying sensor layouts. The advantage over explicit methods is even larger: RoBEV surpasses BEVFormer by +6.8% and BEVDet by +23.7% in average NDS, while explicit methods also incur 1.75× training time and 1.50× inference memory. On the zero-shot unseen test set, RoBEV ties with BEVDet and BEVFormer at 0.308 NDS but achieves the best mAP (0.024) and lowest mATE (1.000 tied), indicating marginally better localization under domain shift. BEVHeight (NDS 0.279) and SparseBEV (NDS 0.256) perform poorly due to their reliance on ground-plane and velocity assumptions that do not hold in RoScenes' non-planar highway geometry and high velocity variance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit BEV detection paradigm (DETR3D, PETR series) by replacing their static, camera-specific position embeddings with a feature-guided dynamic embedding that adapts to varying sensor layouts. On the dataset side, it builds on the small-scale roadside perception line (Rope3D, A9, V2X-Seq) but scales up dramatically in perception area (800×80 m² vs. ~100×100 m²), camera count (6–12 vs. 1–4), and annotation volume (21.13M boxes), while introducing a novel UAV-based BEV-to-3D annotation pipeline that eliminates the need for expensive LiDAR. The empirical study positions RoScenes as a stress test for both explicit (BEVDet, BEVFormer) and implicit (DETR3D, PETRv2, StreamPETR) BEV architectures, revealing that the large perception area and cross-scene layout variation are the dominant failure modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most consequential finding is that all tested BEV methods collapse to ~0.3 NDS on the zero-shot unseen-scene test set, exposing a critical generalization gap: current architectures cannot transfer across different sensor layouts, which is the norm in real-world roadside deployments. This reframes the evaluation question from 'which method is best on a fixed scene' to 'which method adapts to novel camera configurations,' a problem the field has largely ignored. The dataset itself (14 highway scenes, 21.13M 3D boxes, 1.3M images) fills a clear gap as the largest multi-view roadside benchmark, and the BEV-to-3D annotation pipeline (20k samples/day vs. 100–200 manually) offers a practical recipe for scaling roadside 3D labeling. Open questions include whether the feature-guided embedding generalizes beyond highway scenes, how methods perform under weather variation (absent here), and whether the 4-class taxonomy is sufficient for broader ITS applications.
