## Problem

Existing differentiable surface rendering methods (IDR, DMTet) assume local surfaces are planar and use linear interpolation to approximate surface points, causing these points to deviate from the true zero-level set of the SDF. This deviation degrades geometry fidelity and corrupts gradient backpropagation, particularly for complex or specular surfaces. Additionally, prior methods require background priors (masks or environment maps) and cannot robustly handle specular highlights without accurate surface normals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (800×600 or 1600×1200 on DTU; 768×576 on BlendedMVS) and optionally depth maps from a pre-trained CasMVSNet network with per-pixel confidence weights. It produces a triangle mesh extracted via Marching Cubes at 256³ (or 512³ when only color+depth supervision is used) and rendered RGB views. Notably, the method can operate entirely without foreground masks or background images by employing a one-point background model, making it applicable to unconstrained scenes.

## Method

CPT-VR first extracts a coarse mesh via Marching Cubes from a neural SDF (MLP with multi-resolution hash encoding), then applies the Closest Point Transform (x′ = x − d·n) to project each deviated vertex exactly onto the zero-level set, yielding an accurate differentiable mesh. This corrected mesh is rendered with Nvdiffrast, and per-pixel 3D position, geometry feature, and normal are interpolated via barycentric coordinates. For appearance, an RGBnet MLP takes the interpolated features plus view and reflection directions (encoded via Spherical Harmonics) to model specular highlights, while a separate one-point background MLP (sampled at x_o + 2F_p·v_p) handles background without any prior. Training combines L1 color loss, a normal-consistency regularization (aligning normals of deviated and projected points to stabilize CPT gradients), optional mask loss, and confidence-weighted depth loss from CasMVSNet.

## Results

On DTU, CPT-VR achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.56 (with mask) and 0.59 (with one-point background model), outperforming the best surface rendering baseline RegSDF (0.71) by 16.9% and the volume rendering baseline NeuS2 (0.70) by 20%. On BlendedMVS, CPT-VR achieves a mean CD of 0.45 versus NeuS2's 0.52, a 14.8% improvement. In terms of rendering speed, CPT-VR renders at 0.057 s per frame (with mask) or 0.104 s (with background model), approximately 19× faster than NeuS2 (1.979 s) and 260× faster than IDR (27.233 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPT-VR outperforms all eight baselines on DTU Chamfer Distance. Against surface rendering methods, it beats RegSDF (0.56 vs 0.71), FastMESH (0.56 vs 0.77), MVSDF (0.56 vs 0.89), IDR (0.56 vs 0.90), and DMTet (0.56 vs 1.24). Against volume rendering methods with similar SDF architectures, it surpasses NeuS2 (0.56 vs 0.70 with mask; 0.59 vs 0.75 with background model), HF-NeuS (0.56 vs 0.83), and LOD-NeuS (0.56 vs 0.76). On BlendedMVS, CPT-VR (0.45) beats NeuS2 (0.52) by 14.8%, winning on 5 of 8 object categories. Notably, even without any mask or background prior (w/BG1), CPT-VR still achieves 0.59 mean CD, remaining competitive with masked baselines. The paper further demonstrates that adding a reflection vector to DMTet and IDR actually degrades their CD (1.24→1.63 and 0.90→1.68 respectively), while CPT-VR benefits from it (0.72→0.65), confirming that accurate CPT-projected points are essential for reflection-based appearance modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPT-VR builds directly on NeuS2's SDFnet architecture (MLP with multi-resolution hash encoding) but departs from the linear-interpolation assumption shared by IDR, DMTet, and Marching Cubes-based methods. Its core contribution is applying the Closest Point Transform to project linearly-interpolated vertices onto the true zero-level set, a correction step absent from prior surface rendering pipelines. The view-reflection appearance modeling draws on RefNeRF's insight that reflection direction better captures specular appearance, while the one-point background model eliminates the mask dependency that constrains methods like IDR and MVSDF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CPT correction is a simple, differentiable, and broadly applicable fix for a fundamental geometric inaccuracy in rasterization-based surface rendering, and the resulting 19× speedup over volume-rendering baselines like NeuS2 makes high-resolution surface reconstruction practically accessible. The one-point background model removes a common practical dependency on foreground masks, increasing the method's applicability to in-the-wild settings. Open questions include whether a single CPT step suffices for highly curved or self-occluded surfaces, whether the approach extends to dynamic or multi-object scenes, and whether the Marching Cubes resolution remains a bottleneck for fine geometric details.
