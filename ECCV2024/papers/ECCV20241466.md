## Problem

DARTS-style differentiable NAS methods only implicitly model dependencies between edges during backpropagation, which is insufficient to capture inter-edge interactions and can lead to suboptimal architectures and susceptibility to local optima. Additionally, the high degree of weight sharing in the supernet causes inaccurate performance evaluations of individual subnets, a problem that existing pruning strategies (e.g., P-DARTS) address without considering edge dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241466/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a cell-based search space (e.g., 4-node cells with 14 edges and 8 candidate operations per edge) along with standard training and validation image datasets. Beyond the final architecture, it outputs a learned dependency matrix Σ (a symmetric positive semi-definite matrix encoding pairwise edge correlations) and the mean vector μ of the architecture distribution, which together enable dependency-aware search space pruning and motif identification for human-guided architecture design.

## Method

DaNAS models the architecture distribution as a multivariate normal with learnable mean vector μ (base architecture weights per edge) and correlation matrix Σ (inter-edge dependencies), replacing DARTS's direct optimization of architecture weights with sampling from this distribution. The reparameterization trick is extended to the multivariate case via Cholesky decomposition: the Cholesky factor L is learned directly (ensuring Σ = LLᵀ remains positive semi-definite), and architecture weights are sampled as α' = μ + Lε where ε ~ N(0,I), making the sampling process differentiable. The conditioning property of multivariate normals ensures that each edge's architecture weights are explicitly constrained by the learned dependencies to other edges during the forward pass. A dependency-aware pruning indicator combines a Selection Score (SS, from μ) and an Interaction Score (IS, derived from the off-diagonal structure of Σ) to dynamically remove low-scoring operations from the search space during training, alleviating undesired co-adaptation from weight sharing.

## Results

On NAS-Bench-201, DaNAS achieves 94.33% accuracy on CIFAR-10, 73.21% on CIFAR-100, and 46.35% on ImageNet16-120, setting new SOTA across all three datasets (optimal: 94.37/73.51/47.31). On the open-domain CIFAR-10 search space, DaNAS achieves 2.53% error rate (best: 2.39%) with 3.7M parameters in 0.30 GPU days. On ImageNet-1K, DaNAS reaches 24.21% Top-1 and 7.11% Top-5 accuracy with 5.5M parameters and 607M FLOPs in 0.30 GPU days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAS-Bench-201, DaNAS outperforms all 8 compared methods across all three datasets, beating the strongest baseline CDARTS by 0.31% on CIFAR-10 (94.33 vs. 94.02), 1.29% on CIFAR-100 (73.21 vs. 71.92), and 0.84% on ImageNet16-120 (46.35 vs. 45.51). On the open-domain CIFAR-10 search space, DaNAS (2.53% error) surpasses PC-DARTS (2.57%), DARTS+PT (2.61%), SDARTS-ADV (2.61%), and DARTS- (2.59%), while matching SGAS (2.52%) within 0.01%. On ImageNet-1K, DaNAS (24.21% Top-1) trails behind SNAS (27.3%), GDAS (26.0%), and PVLL-NAS (25.60%), indicating that the dependency-aware modeling provides the largest gains on smaller-scale benchmarks where the search space is more constrained. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that both dependency modeling (+2.17% over P-DaNAS) and dependency-aware pruning (+0.08% over O-DaNAS) contribute to the final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DARTS bi-level optimization framework by replacing direct architecture weight learning with sampling from a learned multivariate normal distribution, where the correlation matrix explicitly encodes inter-edge dependencies. It draws on the architecture distribution learning line (NAGO, GraphPNAS) but integrates distributional modeling directly into the differentiable search loop rather than using a separate generator. The reparameterization trick from variational inference is adapted to the multivariate case to enable gradient-based training of the distribution parameters, and the Cholesky factorization ensures positive semi-definiteness of the dependency matrix during optimization. For search space pruning, it departs from weight-based criteria (P-DARTS) by introducing a dependency-aware interaction score that accounts for how operations co-occur across edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit dependency modeling addresses a recognized gap in DARTS where edge interactions are only captured implicitly through backpropagation, and the learned correlation matrix provides an interpretable tool for identifying high-performance motifs that can guide human architecture design. However, the multivariate normal assumption may be too restrictive for capturing complex, non-Gaussian dependency structures in larger or non-cell-based search spaces, and the ImageNet results (24.21% Top-1) trail several baselines, suggesting the approach's advantage is most pronounced in smaller, well-structured search spaces. Open questions include whether the Gaussian dependency model generalizes to deeper or more heterogeneous architectures, and whether the pruning criterion remains effective as search space size grows substantially.
