## Problem

Text-driven image-to-image translation with diffusion models suffers from two fundamental issues: the reverse process starts from an incorrect initialization (the source image's noisy latent rather than the target's true noisy latent), and the abrupt switch from source to target text embeddings at the beginning of denoising causes structural distortion. Existing training-free methods address these through attention map injection or latent optimization, but lack a principled mechanism to selectively edit only the regions relevant to the target prompt while preserving the rest.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, an auto-generated source text prompt (via BLIP captioning), and a target text prompt (derived by word replacement or phrase insertion in the source prompt). It operates entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4 with 50 DDIM steps for both the forward inversion and reverse denoising, producing a single translated image. The noise correction is applied only during the first τ=25 steps of the reverse process, after which standard DDIM denoising resumes.

## Method

PIC revises the DDIM reverse process by replacing the standard noise prediction ε_θ(x_t^tgt, t, y^tgt) with a two-term formulation: the source-conditioned denoising output ε_θ(x_t^src, t, y^src) (which anchors structure and background) plus a scaled noise correction term γ·Δε_θ (which drives target-domain editing). The correction term is defined as the difference between noise predictions of the target latent under a progressively interpolated prompt embedding y_t and under the source prompt embedding, so it captures only the delta needed to shift the image toward the target domain. The prompt interpolation y_t transitions from y^src to y^tgt as t decreases, using linear per-token interpolation for word replacement and a token-aligned scheme for phrase addition. Because the correction term is computed as a difference, it naturally localizes to regions relevant to the target prompt while leaving the background near zero. The approach is training-free, orthogonal to existing methods, and can be plugged into Prompt-to-Prompt, Plug-and-Play, or Pix2Pix-Zero by substituting their respective noise prediction networks with the PIC-revised version.

## Results

On the LAION-5B dataset using Stable Diffusion v1.4, PIC achieves the best average Background Distance (BD = 0.071) and Structure Distance (SD = 0.034) across six editing tasks, substantially outperforming Prompt-to-Prompt (BD = 0.113, SD = 0.040), Plug-and-Play (BD = 0.120, SD = 0.036), and Pix2Pix-Zero (BD = 0.136, SD = 0.066). CLIP Similarity (CS) is competitive at 0.304, ranking second only to Plug-and-Play (0.305). PIC also achieves the fastest inference time at 18.1 seconds per image, compared to 31.2 s (PtP), 24.4 s (PnP), and 52.2 s (P2P) on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIC consistently outperforms all three baselines on Background Distance (BD), the metric measuring preservation of source-image background: average BD of 0.071 vs. 0.113 (PtP), 0.120 (PnP), and 0.136 (P2P), representing 37–48% reductions. On Structure Distance (SD), PIC also achieves the best average (0.034) versus 0.040 (PtP), 0.036 (PnP), and 0.066 (P2P). On CLIP Similarity (CS), PIC is competitive but not uniformly best: it ties PnP on dog→cat (0.293) and slightly trails PnP on the average (0.304 vs. 0.305), while beating PtP (0.302) and P2P (0.301). The largest absolute gains over baselines appear on the zebra→horse task, where PIC reduces BD from 0.183 (PtP) and 0.216 (P2P) to 0.126. When integrated into existing methods (Tables 2–4), PIC further improves BD and SD for PtP, PnP, and P2P, confirming its orthogonality and complementary nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIC sits within the family of training-free diffusion-based image-to-image translation methods that modify the DDIM reverse process without retraining the denoising network. Unlike Prompt-to-Prompt (which swaps cross-attention maps), Plug-and-Play (which substitutes self-attention and feature maps), or Pix2Pix-Zero (which optimizes target latents via gradient steps), PIC revises the noise prediction network itself by adding a correction term computed from progressively interpolated text embeddings. This makes the approach orthogonal to attention-manipulation or latent-optimization strategies, and the paper explicitly demonstrates that it can be layered on top of each of those methods to yield further gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that PIC is trivially simple (a linear interpolation of CLIP embeddings plus a difference of two noise predictions), requires no additional training, and is the fastest method tested (18.1 s vs. 52.2 s for Pix2Pix-Zero), making it an easy drop-in improvement for existing pipelines. However, evaluation is limited to a single backbone (Stable Diffusion v1.4), six editing tasks, and one dataset (LAION-5B), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer diffusion architectures, more complex multi-object edits, or video. The fixed hyperparameters (τ=25, γ=1.0, β=0.3/0.8) and the binary split between 'word replacement' and 'adding phrases' interpolation strategies suggest room for more adaptive or task-agnostic scheduling of the correction term.
