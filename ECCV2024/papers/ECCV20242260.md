## Problem

Existing continual object detection methods rely on replay buffers to combat catastrophic forgetting, which limits practical deployment and creates a suboptimal stability-plasticity tradeoff. Additionally, a fundamental challenge called background relegation—where object categories from earlier tasks reappear in future tasks without annotations and are implicitly treated as background—has gone unaddressed in all prior continual detection systems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores, operating in a class-incremental setting where the classification head expands from |C^{T_t}|+1 to |C|+1 logits (one shared background logit across all tasks). At inference, a single final model θ^{T_n} handles all previously seen classes simultaneously, with the Hungarian matching criterion assigning ground-truth boxes to predictions during training.

## Method

MD-DETR freezes the encoder and decoder of a pre-trained Deformable DETR and introduces a shared pool of memory units (N_m=100, L_m=10, D=256) accessed via a localized query retrieval function: a learnable ranking network g_ψ scores decoder proposals by relevance, and only the top-ranked proposals are averaged to form the query vector, which is then matched against memory keys via cosine similarity to produce a weighted linear combination of memory units. The retrieved memory is injected into the deformable attention module by concatenating its two halves to the key and value projection matrices at each attention head. To address background relegation, a background thresholding (BT) procedure generates pseudo-labels for unannotated past-class objects during training using a confidence threshold δ_bt=0.65, which are optimized concurrently with the current task's ground truth. Memory chunks are partitioned per task and frozen for non-current tasks, past-class gradients are masked, future-class logits are set to −∞, and bounding-box embeddings use a 10× lower learning rate to preserve shape knowledge.

## Results

On MS-COCO (4-task class-incremental setting), MD-DETR achieves mAP@A of 50.2 on Task 4, outperforming the strongest replay-buffer baselines PROB (39.9) and CL-DETR (39.2) by approximately 10 percentage points. On PASCAL-VOC, MD-DETR achieves mAP@A of 73.2 (10+10), 76.7 (15+5), and 76.1 (19+1), surpassing PROB by 6.7, 6.6, and 3.5 points respectively. MD-DETR is the only buffer-free method in the comparison, achieving these gains without storing any past-task samples. The method also maintains the highest mAP@P (forgetting metric) across all tasks, e.g., 51.5 on Task 4 vs. 42.6 for PROB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MD-DETR outperforms all eight baselines on both MS-COCO and PASCAL-VOC. On MS-COCO Task 4, it leads PROB [47] by 10.3 mAP@A points (50.2 vs. 39.9) and CL-DETR [22] by 11.0 points (50.2 vs. 39.2), while also achieving substantially higher mAP@P (51.5 vs. 42.6 for PROB), indicating far less catastrophic forgetting. On PASCAL-VOC, MD-DETR beats PROB by 6.7 points in the 10+10 setting (73.2 vs. 66.5 mAP@A) and by 6.6 points in 15+5 (76.7 vs. 70.1); in the 19+1 setting the gap narrows to 3.5 points (76.1 vs. 72.6). Compared to OW-DETR [11], MD-DETR gains 17.1 mAP@A on MS-COCO Task 4 (50.2 vs. 33.1) and 6.5–15.9 points across PASCAL-VOC settings. ROSETTA [41], the only other replay-free baseline, trails MD-DETR by 6.4–7.0 mAP@A on PASCAL-VOC. Notably, all competing methods except ROSETTA rely on replay buffers, whereas MD-DETR achieves superior results without storing any past-task samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

MD-DETR extends the Deformable DETR architecture by integrating a memory-augmented decoder, drawing on the prompt-based memory retrieval paradigm established by L2P and CodaPrompt for continual classification. The key departure is twofold: (1) it adapts memory networks—previously confined to classification—to the structurally richer setting of object detection, requiring a localized query function guided by Hungarian matching rather than simple cosine-similarity top-K selection; and (2) it eliminates the replay buffer entirely, positioning itself against the dominant buffer-based continual detection methods (OW-DETR, CL-DETR, PROB, ERD). The paper also introduces background thresholding as a training-time optimization specifically targeting the background relegation problem, which prior continual detection work has not formally addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a buffer-free memory-based approach can outperform all replay-buffer-based methods by 5–7% on MS-COCO and PASCAL-VOC, the paper challenges the field's near-universal reliance on exemplar storage for continual detection, with practical implications for privacy-sensitive and streaming deployment scenarios. The formal identification of background relegation as a fundamental, previously unacknowledged failure mode in continual detection opens a new line of inquiry for the community. However, the authors acknowledge that bounding-box deformation and confidence-score degradation for previously seen classes remain unresolved, and the method's dependence on a frozen pre-trained encoder limits its applicability when the backbone itself must adapt.
