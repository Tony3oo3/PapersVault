## Problem

Existing collaborative approaches for 3D Referring Expression Comprehension (3DREC) and 3D Referring Expression Segmentation (3DRES) rely on cascade structures where one task's output is entirely dependent on the other (e.g., TGNN derives 3DREC results solely from 3DRES), limiting effective collaboration and capping the performance ceiling of the dependent task. No prior work has explored separate, independent branches for both tasks with mechanisms to ensure their predictions remain mutually consistent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241233/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D point cloud (downsampled to 1024 visual tokens via PointNet++) and a natural language referring expression (256 text tokens via frozen RoBERTa-base). It simultaneously produces a 3D bounding box (6 parameters: center and size) from the 3DREC branch and a superpoint-level segmentation mask from the 3DRES branch, where superpoints serve as a bridge between the low-resolution backbone features and the dense point cloud. The final mask is an adaptive weighted combination of both branches' superpoint predictions, thresholded at 0.5 during inference.

## Method

MCLN employs a shared PointNet++ backbone and cross-modal encoder feeding into two independent decoder branches: a DETR-style 3DREC decoder (following EDA) for bounding box prediction, and a transformer decoder operating on superpoint features for mask prediction. The Relative Superpoint Aggregation (RSA) module generates superpoint features by encoding relative coordinates between each superpoint center and its K nearest visual points via an MLP, adding these to point features before max-pooling, thereby preserving spatial relationships that standard ball query ignores. The Adaptive Soft Alignment (ASA) module aligns the two branches by using the 3DREC query vector's similarity to superpoints as an indirect bounding-box representation (query mask), then enforcing consistency with the 3DRES mask through a point-level Focal Loss weighted by a normal-distribution-based confidence score and a mask-level Dice Loss weighted by mean inter-superpoint distance. A learned confidence score adaptively blends the two branches' superpoint masks into the final prediction, and the overall loss combines 3DREC decoder losses, 3DRES focal/dice losses, alignment losses, and a KPS loss.

## Results

On ScanRefer, MCLN achieves state-of-the-art performance on both 3DREC and 3DRES tasks. For 3DREC (two-stage setting), it reaches 57.17% Acc@0.25 and 45.53% Acc@0.5 overall, surpassing the previous best 3DRefTR by 2.05% in Acc@0.5. For 3DRES, it achieves 58.70% Acc@0.25, 50.70% Acc@0.5, and 44.72% mIOU overall, improving mIOU by 3.96% over 3DRefTR (40.76%) and by 5.22% over 3D-STMN (39.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCLN outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the primary ScanRefer benchmark. Against the closest competitor 3DRefTR [21] (which also jointly tackles 3DREC and 3DRES), MCLN gains +2.05% Acc@0.5 on 3DREC (45.53 vs 43.48) and +3.96% mIOU on 3DRES (44.72 vs 40.76), confirming that separate branches with adaptive alignment beat the cascade design. Against EDA [37], the 3DREC backbone it builds upon, MCLN improves Acc@0.5 from 42.26 to 45.53 (+3.27%), showing the collaborative 3DRES branch provides meaningful transfer. On 3DRES, MCLN surpasses 3D-STMN [36] by +5.22% mIOU (44.72 vs 39.50) and X-RefSeg3D [33] by +14.78% mIOU (44.72 vs 29.94), indicating the multi-branch design substantially benefits segmentation. Earlier baselines such as TGNN [14], InstanceRefer [41], 3DVG-Trans [42], and 3DJCG [3] are all exceeded by large margins (10–20+ points in Acc@0.5 for 3DREC). The only notable trade-off is in the single-stage 3DREC setting, where MCLN (42.64% Acc@0.5) slightly trails 3DRefTR (42.33%) by a negligible 0.31%, while still leading EDA (41.70%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EDA (CVPR 2023) as the architectural foundation for the 3DREC branch, adopting its DETR-like backbone, cross-modal encoder, and decoder structure. It departs from prior joint-training approaches like TGNN and 3DRefTR, which use cascade architectures where 3DREC performance is entirely dependent on 3DRES outputs, by introducing fully independent branches for each task. The superpoint-based feature aggregation draws on 3D-STMN and PointNet++ ball queries, while the adaptive alignment mechanism (quality-weighted Focal and Dice losses) is a novel contribution not present in prior multi-task 3D grounding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint training of 3DREC and 3DRES remains underexplored, and this paper demonstrates that separate branches with explicit alignment can improve both tasks simultaneously without one degrading the other—a practical recipe for future multi-task 3D grounding systems. The 3.96% mIoU gain on 3DRES is meaningful given the task's limited prior work, though the single-stage performance drop on unique scenes suggests the collaborative signal may overfit to complex multi-object scenarios. Open questions include whether the alignment strategy generalizes to other 3D tasks (e.g., 3D detection, panoptic segmentation) and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse scene collections beyond ScanRefer.
