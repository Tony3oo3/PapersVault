## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), the loss used in DreamFusion and many text-to-3D and image editing methods, decomposes into a text-alignment term and a manifold-projection term. The projection term suffers from a timestep-dependent frequency bias in the denoising model, producing noisy gradients that remove high-frequency detail rather than correcting toward the natural image manifold. This deficiency forces practitioners to use very high classifier-free guidance weights (ω=100) to compensate, causing oversaturation, repeated detail, and other artifacts.

## Input / Output

LMC-SDS is a loss function rather than a standalone model, so it is embedded within an optimization loop or network training procedure. The pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model operates at 128×128 pixel resolution; for higher-resolution image tasks, the optimization state is first processed at 128×128 and then random patches of arbitrary resolution are sampled in later iterations with a cosine-decayed learning rate. For 3D generation, the loss is applied to rendered views of a differentiable 3D representation (e.g., a NeRF), and the output is a 3D asset.

## Method

The paper decomposes the SDS gradient into L_cond (text-prompt alignment) and L_proj (single-step denoising toward the image manifold), then identifies that L_proj is corrupted by a timestep-dependent frequency bias in the denoising model's reconstruction. To cancel this bias, they train a shallow U-Net (b̂_ψ) on triplets (z, ẑ_t, t) sampled from OpenImages to predict the mean frequency bias of the denoising model at each timestep, using a loss computed in a normalized space to prevent drift in global image statistics. At optimization time, the corrected gradient L_lmc compares b̂_ψ(z, t) against the denoised image ẑ_t, yielding cleaner manifold-directed gradients that focus on global structure rather than high-frequency noise. They further propose fixing the noise ε in L_cond while continuing to sample ε in L_lmc, which stabilizes optimization and increases result diversity. The U-Net is trained once and kept frozen across all downstream tasks, adding negligible computational overhead to the SDS pipeline.

## Results

On the cats-to-others image translation task (Table 1), LMC-SDS achieves the highest CLIP-R-Precision of 0.592, substantially outperforming all baselines (SDS: 0.355, NFSD: 0.434, DDS: 0.333), while maintaining a low LPIPS of 0.178 relative to the source image. In optimization-based editing (Fig. 5), LMC-SDS achieves the highest CLIP score across all guidance weights ω, with the best balance between prompt adherence and structural preservation. For text-to-3D generation, LMC-SDS produces detailed, well-colored 3D assets at ω=20 (one-fifth of DreamFusion's original ω=100), avoiding the oversaturation and Janus problem artifacts of the original SDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMC-SDS outperforms all four baselines on the primary CLIP-R-Precision metric in the cats-to-others experiment: 0.592 vs. SDS (0.355), NFSD (0.434), DDS (0.333), and SDS with ω=100 (0.482). Against SDS, LMC-SDS also achieves a lower LPIPS (0.178 vs. 0.401), indicating it edits more faithfully without corrupting the source image. Compared to NFSD, LMC-SDS both edits more effectively (CLIP-R-Precision 0.592 vs. 0.434) and preserves structure better (LPIPS 0.178 vs. 0.332). DDS achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.143) but at the cost of minimal editing (CLIP-R-Precision 0.333), confirming its tendency to stay close to the source. In the editing experiment (Fig. 5), MS-SDS [40] performs similarly to vanilla SDS—corrupting the image with high LPIPS while offering only marginal CLIP improvements—whereas LMC-SDS consistently achieves the highest CLIP score at every tested ω while keeping LPIPS competitive with the structure-preserving baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss [29], decomposing its gradient into a conditioning term (L_cond) and a projection term (L_proj), and identifying that L_proj — intended to guide optimization toward the natural image manifold — actually removes high-frequency detail due to a timestep-dependent frequency bias in the denoising model. The proposed LMC-SDS fix (a small U-Net modeling this bias) is positioned against concurrent and prior attempts to correct SDS artifacts: DDS [8] removes L_proj entirely via a source-image difference, NFSD [13] replaces it with negative-prompt conditioning, SparseFusion [40] uses multi-step denoising, and ProlificDreamer [38] fine-tunes the diffusion model. LMC-SDS distinguishes itself as a general-purpose, prompt-agnostic loss with minimal computational overhead that preserves a meaningful manifold-corrective signal rather than eliminating it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a frequency bias in the SDS projection term reframes a long-standing practical issue (the need for ω=100 guidance) as a symptom of a broken component rather than an inherent requirement, enabling lower guidance weights and reducing oversaturation and Janus artifacts. The fix is remarkably lightweight — a small U-Net trained once on image triplets — making it easy to adopt across any SDS-based pipeline. Limitations remain: the method cannot recover when the optimization state drifts far off-manifold, it inherits all blind spots of the underlying diffusion model, and it sometimes overcompensates the frequency bias. Open questions include whether the learned corrective generalizes across diffusion model architectures and resolutions, and whether the fixed-ε strategy for diversity can be formalized more rigorously.
