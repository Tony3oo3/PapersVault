## Problem

Existing unsupervised visible-infrared person ReID methods rely on a single memory per identity to establish cross-modality correspondences, which fails to capture intra-class nuances such as perspective and attire, resulting in noisy correspondences. The paper further reveals via the Adjusted Rand Index metric that prior methods' cross-modality correspondences are unreliable despite achieving competitive retrieval scores, indicating a fundamental gap in how pseudo-label matching is performed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled visible (RGB) and infrared (thermal) person images resized to 288×144 pixels, with random horizontal flipping and random crop augmentation. It produces DBSCAN-based pseudo-labels for both intra- and inter-modality sample sets, a binary cross-modality correspondence matrix via bipartite graph matching, and a final ReID retrieval ranking. A key structural detail is that each identity's single cluster memory is subdivided into n sub-cluster memories (default n=4), and the inter-modality matching operates at this finer granularity rather than at the whole-cluster level.

## Method

MMM comprises three components: (1) a Cross-Modality Clustering (CMC) module that clusters both intra-modality and inter-modality samples with DBSCAN and optimizes features via ClusterNCE loss; (2) a Multi-Memory Learning and Matching (MMLM) module that subdivides each identity's memory into n sub-cluster centroids and formulates cross-modality matching as a weighted bipartite graph matching problem, where the cost between visible and infrared identities is the sum of minimum L2 distances across their respective sub-memory pairs; and (3) a Soft Cluster-level Alignment (SCA) loss that uses a two-component GMM to estimate per-sample label confidence, then applies confidence-weighted intra-modality alignment (pulling samples toward their cluster memory) and inter-modality alignment (squared MMD between visible and infrared feature distributions of the same identity with stop-gradient) to narrow the modality gap while down-weighting noisy pseudo-labels.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (All Search, USL-VI-ReID), MMM achieves 61.6% Rank-1 and 57.9% mAP, outperforming GUR* (61.0%/57.0%) and CHCR (59.5%/59.1%), though trailing PCLHD (64.4%/58.7%). On RegDB, MMM delivers substantially larger gains: 89.7% Rank-1 / 80.5% mAP (Visible→Thermal) and 85.8% Rank-1 / 77.0% mAP (Thermal→Visible), surpassing the previous best USL method GUR* by 15.8% and 10.8% Rank-1 respectively. When combined with PCLHD, MMM reaches 65.9% Rank-1 / 61.8% mAP on SYSU-MM01 All Search, setting a new state-of-the-art among unsupervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RegDB, MMM dominates all USL-VI-ReID baselines by wide margins: it improves Rank-1 by 15.8% over GUR* (73.9%→89.7%) and 5.4% over PCLHD (84.3%→89.7%) in Visible→Thermal mode, and by 10.8% over GUR* (75.0%→85.8%) in Thermal→Visible mode. On SYSU-MM01 All Search, MMM (61.6% Rank-1) edges out GUR* (61.0%) and CHCR (59.5%) but falls short of PCLHD (64.4%), though combining MMM with PCLHD yields the best overall result (65.9%). Notably, MMM surpasses all three SSVI-ReID methods (OTLA, TAA, DPIS) without using any annotations, and also outperforms several supervised SVI-ReID methods (AGW, NFS, LbA) on SYSU-MM01, though it remains below the top supervised methods (DEEN, PartMix, CAJ). The plug-and-play nature of MMM is demonstrated by ADCA+MMM, which improves ADCA's Rank-1 from 45.5% to 49.7% on SYSU-MM01 and from 67.2% to 77.8% on RegDB V→T, confirming that the multi-memory matching module is transferable to other USL-VI-ReID pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the clustering-based USL-VI-ReID paradigm by building on Cluster-Contrast's ClusterNCE loss for feature optimization and PGM's bipartite graph matching for cross-modality label alignment. The key departure is replacing the single-memory-per-identity representation (used by GUR, PGM, MBCCM, and others) with a multi-memory (sub-cluster) representation that captures individual nuances such as perspective and attire, then matching these sub-memories across modalities via a cost matrix. The Soft Cluster-level Alignment loss, combining intra-modality memory attraction with inter-modality MMD² distribution alignment weighted by GMM-based confidence, is a new formulation not present in prior USL-VI-ReID methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most notable contribution is revealing that prior USL-VI-ReID methods produce unreliable cross-modality correspondences (low ARI) despite achieving competitive retrieval scores, suggesting that inflated performance may stem from merging similar identities rather than precise matching. This reframes the evaluation landscape by introducing ARI as a diagnostic metric for pseudo-label quality. However, the approach still depends on DBSCAN for initial clustering, is validated on only two datasets, and the gap to supervised methods (e.g., PartMix at 77.8% vs. MMM at 61.6% Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01 all-search) remains substantial. Open questions include whether multi-memory matching generalizes to more than two modalities or to open-world settings with unseen identities.
