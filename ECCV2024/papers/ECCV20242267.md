## Problem

Prompt-based rehearsal-free continual learning methods rely on key-query matching to select trainable prompts, but the domain gap between pre-training and downstream datasets causes inaccurate prompt selection that hampers new-knowledge acquisition. Additionally, these methods require two forward passes through the pre-trained model (one for query generation, one for classification), resulting in lower training efficiency. The paper asks whether an adapter-based approach can eliminate key-query matching entirely while achieving better plasticity and faster training.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224 as input and outputs class labels via a linear classifier. It operates under the class-incremental protocol where task identity is unavailable at inference and class sets are disjoint across tasks. The ViT-B/16 backbone is frozen; only the adapter weights (total middle dimension 60 across two CALs per block, inserted in layers 0–4) and the classifier are trained, keeping the parameter overhead at ~0.7% of the backbone.

## Method

C-ADA introduces a Continual Adapter Layer (CAL) placed in parallel with the projection layer and MLP block of the first five ViT layers. Each CAL is a bottleneck (down-projection → ReLU → up-projection) whose weight matrices are extended along the middle dimension for each new task: new task-specific weights are initialized via Gram-Schmidt and trained, while all previously learned weights are frozen, preserving old knowledge without any stored samples. A lightweight Scaling & Shifting module (two per-channel vectors α, β) is trained on the first task to align the pre-trained feature space with the downstream distribution, then frozen. An orthogonal loss penalizes the L2-norm of inner products between the current task's weights and all prior weights, reducing interference between old and new knowledge. Because no key-query matching is needed, C-ADA requires only a single forward pass per sample, halving FLOPs compared to prompt-based baselines while achieving higher accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-R (10 tasks), C-ADA achieves 76.66% average accuracy (A_N) with only 0.7% trainable parameters and 17.6G FLOPs, surpassing the previous SOTA CODA (73.93%) by +2.73% and DualPrompt (71.32%) by +5.41%. On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks), C-ADA reaches 87.18% A_N, improving over CODA by +2.59%. In the domain-incremental setting, C-ADA achieves 89.45% on CORe50 and 53.00% on DomainNet, outperforming CODA by +4.04% and +5.44% respectively. Notably, C-ADA requires only a single forward pass (17.6G FLOPs) versus two forward passes (35.2G FLOPs) for prompt-based methods, halving the training cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-ADA outperforms all compared baselines across every dataset and setting. Against the strongest rehearsal-free prompt-based methods, C-ADA beats CODA [38] by +2.74% to +2.94% on ImageNet-R (5/10/20 tasks) and +2.59% on CIFAR-100, while also halving FLOPs (17.6G vs 35.2G). The margin over DualPrompt [50] is even larger at +4.88% to +5.60% on ImageNet-R. L2P [51] trails C-ADA by 7.10% on ImageNet-R P=5 and 4.68% on CIFAR-100. In the domain-incremental setting, C-ADA surpasses S-Prompts [49] (the SOTA for DIL) by +6.32% on CORe50 and +2.38% on DomainNet, and beats CODA by +4.04% and +5.44% respectively. Rehearsal-based methods (DER++ [53], Co²L [3], DyTox [11], GDumb [31]) and regularization methods (EWC [23], LwF [25]) are all substantially outperformed, with C-ADA exceeding the best rehearsal-based method (L2P at 81.07% on CORe50) by 8.38% while storing zero old samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant prompt-learning paradigm for rehearsal-free continual learning (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA), which relies on key-query matching to select prompts and requires two forward passes through the pre-trained backbone. Instead, it draws on the adapter tuning lineage (Houlsby et al., 2019) and introduces a parameter-extensible Continual Adapter Layer (CAL) that grows a new set of trainable weights per task while freezing prior ones, paired with a lightweight scaling-and-shifting module to bridge the pre-training/downstream domain gap. The orthogonal loss, which enforces orthogonality between new and frozen weights via Gram-Schmidt initialization and an L2-norm penalty, is a new regularization mechanism not present in prior adapter or prompt-based CL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating key-query matching, C-ADA removes a source of selection errors that degrades plasticity in prompt-based methods and halves the FLOPs per training step (17.6G vs. 35.2G), making rehearsal-free CL more practical for deployment. The parameter-extensible design offers a clean, modular solution to the stability-plasticity tradeoff that generalizes across class-incremental and domain-incremental settings without task-specific redesign. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT-L/H) or truly open-ended scenarios where the total number of tasks is unknown, and whether the orthogonal constraint remains effective as the number of accumulated frozen weight sets grows large.
