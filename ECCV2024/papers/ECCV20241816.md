## Problem

Existing large vision-language models (LVLMs) remain prone to hallucinations because standard visual instruction tuning provides only instruction-response pairs without intermediate reasoning steps, causing models to learn superficial shortcuts rather than internalize the underlying reasoning logic. No prior large-scale instruction-tuning dataset contains both positive and negative fine-grained rationale annotations needed to supervise this reasoning process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241816/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a text instruction as input. Output is structured as a two-turn conversation: the first turn produces the text response (e.g., a multiple-choice answer, short answer, or yes/no), and the second turn generates a rationale — either a positive rationale explaining why the correct response is justified (citing visual evidence and domain knowledge) or a negative rationale explaining why a given incorrect response is wrong. This decoupling ensures that at inference time, response prediction depends only on the image and instruction context, not on rationale generation.

## Method

Reflective instruction tuning extends standard visual instruction tuning by training the model to predict rationales in addition to responses, using a multi-turn conversational format where rationale generation is placed after response prediction to avoid interfering with inference. The approach trains on both positive rationales (providing step-by-step visual reasoning and domain knowledge justifying the correct answer) and negative rationales (highlighting discriminative details that expose errors in incorrect answers), with each rationale type placed in a separate training sample to prevent information leakage. The REVERIE dataset (115k instructions, 254k total training instances) is constructed by prompting Gemini-Vision-Pro to generate multi-step instructions, confusing negative responses, and corresponding rationales from Visual Genome, COCO, ScienceQA, and A-OK-VQA images, followed by a consistency-based filtering step using Gemini-Pro to remove samples where positive and negative rationales contradict each other. The method is applied via LoRA fine-tuning on LLaVA-1.0 and LLaVA-1.5, and ablations confirm that explicit guidance in rationale prompts, separate contexts for positive/negative rationales, and consistency filtering are all critical for performance.

## Results

On the LLaVA-1.0-7b-lora baseline, reflective instruction tuning with REVERIE yields a 12.7-point gain on POPE (83.8 vs. 71.1), a 348.3-point gain on MME (1168.1 vs. 819.8), a 28.2-point gain on MMBench (55.4 vs. 27.2), and a 27.4-point gain on ScienceQA (70.1 vs. 42.7). On the stronger LLaVA-1.5-7b-lora baseline, REVERIE-1.5 achieves 80.5 on ScienceQA (+12.1 over baseline), 67.3 on MMBench (+1.2), and 30.8 on MM-Vet (+0.6), while matching the baseline on POPE (86.4) and slightly trailing on MME (1474.9 vs. 1476.9) and GQA (61.8 vs. 63.0). Ablations confirm that combining both positive and negative rationales outperforms using either alone, with the largest gains on POPE F1 (87.10 vs. 86.90 for response-only) and MMBench accuracy (55.44 vs. 51.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the LLaVA-1.0-7b-lora baseline, REVERIE-1.0-7b-lora achieves substantial gains across nearly all benchmarks: +27.4 on ScienceQA, +12.7 on POPE, +348.3 on MME, +28.2 on MMBench, and +29.4 on GQA, though it slightly underperforms on MM-Vet (27.8 vs. 30.0). Against the stronger LLaVA-1.5-7b-lora baseline, REVERIE-1.5-7b-lora shows more modest but consistent improvements on ScienceQA (+12.1), MMBench (+1.2), and MM-Vet (+0.6), while matching POPE (86.4) and trailing by small margins on MME (−2.0) and GQA (−1.2). Compared to InstructBLIP (vicuna-7b), REVERIE-1.0 outperforms on ScienceQA (70.1 vs. 60.5) and MMBench (55.4 vs. 36.0) but falls short on GQA (36.5 vs. 49.2). Against Qwen-VL-Chat-7b, REVERIE-1.0 leads on ScienceQA (70.1 vs. 68.2) and MMBench (55.4 vs. 60.6 — actually trails here), but the comparison is limited since Qwen-VL-Chat reports MME (1487.5) far above REVERIE-1.0's 1168.1. The paper also shows that simply adding ScienceQA data to LLaVA-1.5 without rationales degrades MME and GQA performance, whereas REVERIE's rationale supervision avoids this shortcut-learning issue and yields net gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual instruction tuning paradigm (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, MiniGPT-4) by incorporating rationale learning, drawing on the chain-of-thought and rationale-augmented training literature from the LLM domain (Wei et al., Magister et al.). It departs from prior hallucination-mitigation work (balanced datasets, contrastive decoding, alignment modules) by targeting the training signal itself: rather than modifying decoding or architecture, it enriches the supervision with fine-grained positive and negative rationales. The closest prior work, LLaVA-Instruct-158k, includes step-by-step reasoning for a subset of complex tasks, but only as positive rationales on open-ended questions; REVERIE generalizes this to all task types and, critically, adds negative rationales explaining why incorrect answers fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REVERIE is the first large-scale (254k instances) visual instruction tuning dataset with both positive and negative rationale annotations, providing a resource that could accelerate future research on rationale-based training for LVLMs. The finding that simply adding ScienceQA data without rationales degrades performance on MME and GQA offers concrete evidence for the instruction-response shortcut hypothesis, suggesting that rationale supervision is not merely additive but corrective. Limitations include reliance on Gemini for annotation (introducing potential systematic biases), evaluation restricted to 7B LLaVA variants, and mixed results on MM-Vet. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger or differently-architected models, whether human-verified rationales would yield further gains, and whether the multi-turn decoupling design is optimal versus integrated reasoning.
