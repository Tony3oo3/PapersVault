## Problem

Learning-based SfM methods have significant practical limitations: they require coarse pose priors, sequential image inputs, or can only handle a few hundred images due to computational cost. The paper asks whether these constraints are inherent to neural scene representations or merely artifacts of prior formulations, and shows they are not by re-interpreting incremental SfM as iterated visual relocalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241504/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered collection of unposed RGB images (scaled to 480px height) with no pose priors, no sequential ordering, and no ground-truth correspondences. A monocular depth estimate (ZoeDepth) is used solely for the single-image initialization step and is discarded thereafter. The output consists of per-image 3×4 pose matrices (rotation + translation), a refined shared focal length, and a compact implicit scene representation (a 9-layer, 512-channel MLP consuming ~4 MB in 16-bit precision). The camera model is assumed to be pinhole with centered principal point, square unskewed pixels, and no distortion.

## Method

ACE0 re-frames incremental SfM as a loop between two stages: (1) neural mapping, where a scene coordinate regression network (frozen CNN backbone pre-trained on ScanNet + trainable 9-layer MLP) is trained on currently registered images by minimizing pixel-wise reprojection error, and (2) relocalization, where the trained network predicts 2D-3D correspondences for all unregistered images, whose poses are solved via RANSAC+PnP and accepted if the inlier count exceeds a threshold. Three key mechanisms distinguish it from the supervised ACE relocalizer: a pose-refinement MLP that predicts small additive offsets to initial poses (regularized with AdamW weight decay to prevent drift), a focal-length refinement parameter, and self-supervised training that bootstraps from a single image plus a depth estimate. Adaptive patch sampling (capping at 10 passes over the mapping set) and an early-stopping criterion (70% of reprojection errors below 10 px for 100 consecutive batches) keep each mapping iteration to minutes, enabling full reconstructions of 10k+ images in roughly one hour on a single V100 GPU.

## Results

On 7-Scenes (2k–12k images), ACE0 achieves 21.2 dB average PSNR via Nerfacto view synthesis, matching COLMAP default (21.2 dB) while reconstructing in ~1 hour versus COLMAP's 38 hours. On Mip-NeRF 360, ACE0 reaches 22.9 dB, substantially outperforming all learning-based baselines (DROID-SLAM 12.9, BARF 12.8, NoPe-NeRF 13.5) though slightly below COLMAP (24.7 dB). On Tanks and Temples with 4k–22k frames, ACE0 achieves 20.9 dB average, exceeding COLMAP fast (19.8 dB) and COLMAP default (19.9 dB) while running in 2.2 hours versus 74 hours for COLMAP fast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE0 matches COLMAP (default) on 7-Scenes (21.2 vs 21.2 dB) and slightly exceeds COLMAP fast on Tanks and Temples large-frame sets (20.9 vs 19.8 dB), while being 38× faster than COLMAP default on 7-Scenes (1h vs 38h). Against learning-based SfM methods, ACE0 dominates: on Mip-NeRF 360 it achieves 22.9 dB versus DROID-SLAM (12.9), BARF (12.8), and NoPe-NeRF (13.5), a gap of 9–10 dB. On 7-Scenes with 50 frames, ACE0 (17.9 dB) outperforms DUSt3R (17.1 dB). On Tanks and Temples, ACE0 is comparable to RealityCapture on small sets but significantly outperforms it on large sets where RealityCapture produces fractured reconstructions. In the relocalization evaluation (Table 2a), self-supervised ACE0 achieves 93.8% of poses within 5 cm/5° of COLMAP pseudo-GT, close to supervised ACE trained with COLMAP poses (97.6%), confirming that the self-supervised mapping produces poses nearly indistinguishable from feature-based SfM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-frames incremental structure-from-motion as an iterated application of visual relocalization, then replaces the traditional feature-matching relocalizer with a self-supervised scene coordinate regression network. It builds directly on ACE (Brachmann et al., CVPR 2023), a fast-learning scene coordinate regressor originally designed for supervised relocalization, and on the original scene coordinate regression formulation (Shotton et al., CVPR 2013). The key departure from prior learning-based SfM methods (BARF, NoPe-NeRF, DUSt3R) is the elimination of pose priors, sequential input requirements, and few-frame limitations by leveraging the incremental, self-bootstrapping loop inherited from classical incremental SfM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACE0 is the first learning-based SfM pipeline that handles thousands of unsorted images without pose priors or sequential ordering, achieving COLMAP-comparable pose quality in roughly 1 hour versus COLMAP's 38 hours on 7-Scenes. This opens a path toward neural, implicit scene representations as a practical alternative to sparse point clouds for large-scale reconstruction. However, the method still struggles with repetitive structures, very large scenes (requiring network ensembles), and extreme viewpoint or lighting changes, and it assumes a shared pinhole calibration across images—limitations that constrain its applicability to diverse real-world capture conditions.
