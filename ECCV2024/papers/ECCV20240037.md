## Problem

Existing drag-based image and video generators can only reposition an object as a whole, failing to capture nuanced part-level interactions such as opening a single drawer without moving the rest of the cabinet. Foundation models trained on internet-scale data entangle camera viewpoint, multi-object interaction, and part configurations, making it difficult to learn which parts should move in response to a drag. The paper asks whether a generative model can learn a generalist, template-free motion prior for articulated objects that responds to drags with physically plausible part-level deformations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single object-centric RGB image y at 256×256 resolution and a set of sparse drags D, where each drag is a 2D point-displacement pair (u, v) with u inside the image and v potentially outside the field of view. Unlike dense optical flow, only one drag per moving part is provided, and the model must infer the full part-level deformation. The primary output is a new image x reflecting the drag-induced deformation; secondary outputs include a coarse segmentation mask of the moving part (via feature differencing) and, for 3D articulated objects, the motion type and joint parameters recovered by grid-search optimization over rendered views.

## Method

DragAPart fine-tunes a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net, or a DiT trained from scratch) on the newly introduced Drag-a-Move dataset of 40 million synthetic image pairs rendered from 763 GAPartNet objects with part-level kinematic annotations. The key architectural contribution is a multi-resolution drag encoding: each drag's source and termination are assigned separate channels (with −1 padding for absent drags and sub-pixel fractional values for precise localization), and the encoding is injected at every transformer block at its native spatial resolution, preventing the overlap and diffusion artifacts of prior single-channel sparse flow encodings. The reference image y is passed through the same denoiser without noise, and its attention keys and values replace those of the noised target, converting self-attention into cross-attention for identity preservation. To bridge the sim-to-real gap, the model is jointly trained on renderings with random monochromatic per-part textures (domain randomization), which prevents the network from relying on texture cues and significantly improves generalization to real images and unseen categories.

## Results

On the in-distribution Drag-a-Move test set, DragAPart achieves PSNR 21.38, SSIM 0.925, and LPIPS 0.066, substantially outperforming the next-best method iPoke (PSNR 16.79, LPIPS 0.150) by ~4.6 dB in PSNR and 2.3× lower LPIPS. On Human3.6M, DragAPart reaches PSNR 23.82, SSIM 0.870, and LPIPS 0.091, again leading all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., LPIPS 0.091 vs. 0.258 for iPoke). The model also generalizes to real images from CO3D and ABO, producing physically plausible part-level deformations on unseen categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DragAPart wins on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across both evaluation datasets against all five quantitative baselines. The largest gap is against DragNUWA (PSNR 13.58, LPIPS 0.277 on I.D.), a large pre-trained video generator that focuses on whole-object repositioning rather than part-level motion. Against the closest competitor iPoke (trained with the same experimental setup), DragAPart improves PSNR by 4.6 dB and reduces LPIPS by more than half on Drag-a-Move, and by 2.4 dB / 2.8× on Human3.6M. Qualitatively, training-free methods (DragDiffusion, DragonDiffusion, Motion Guidance) tend to copy the handle's appearance to the target location without moving the entire part, while DragDiffusion and DragNUWA re-scale or reposition the whole object. InstructPix2Pix, conditioned on text instructions, fails to produce significant deformation, motivating the drag-based conditioning. Motion Guidance, even with a dense motion field, struggles to preserve object identity, whereas DragAPart faithfully maintains identity while exhibiting physically plausible part-level dynamics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DragAPart extends the line of drag-conditioned image editing (DragGAN, DragDiffusion, DragonDiffusion, DragNUWA) by shifting the target from whole-object repositioning to part-level articulation (e.g., opening a drawer rather than moving a cabinet). Architecturally, it fine-tunes a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion U-Net or DiT) using a cross-attention mechanism borrowed from MASActrl to inject the source image, and introduces a novel multi-resolution, per-drag-channel encoding that replaces the single sparse optical-flow image used by prior methods. The training data is rendered from GAPartNet's articulated 3D assets, and domain randomization (random per-part colors) bridges the sim-to-real gap, a strategy drawn from robotics but applied here to generative model fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a relatively small synthetic dataset (~763 objects, 40M image pairs) combined with domain randomization is sufficient to teach a pre-trained image generator nuanced part-level motion understanding that it did not acquire from billions of natural images — a finding that challenges the assumption that scale alone yields fine-grained physical reasoning. The drag encoding is architecture-agnostic and the learned motion prior enables downstream tasks (motion parameter estimation, moving-part segmentation) without explicit 3D models. Key limitations include the absence of cross-viewpoint consistency, separate models for objects vs. humans, and evaluation on real data that remains largely qualitative; a truly universal motion prior covering all object categories in a single model remains an open goal.
