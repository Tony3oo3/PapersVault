## Problem

Reconstructing animatable 3D animal avatars from casual monocular videos is challenging because animals exhibit unpredictable non-rigid motions and appearance details (fur, spots, tails), and existing template-based fitting methods rely on sparse keypoint correspondences that provide almost no supervision for rear or side views. Additionally, prior approaches conflate camera jitter with animal motion in the pose parameters, preventing effective temporal regularization, and none jointly optimize shape, pose, and texture in a single 4D solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of a dog (200 frames at 256×256), COLMAP SfM camera estimates, and foreground segmentation masks as input. It outputs a single SMAL shape vector β, a time-varying pose function θ_MLP(τ(t)) parameterized by a shallow MLP over positional encodings, and an implicit duplex-mesh texture ψ (a triplane feature grid decoded by an MLP for opacity and view-dependent color). The resulting model is fully animatable: the SMAL skeleton can be re-posed to generate new motions, and the texture can be transferred across different optimized scenes.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage per-scene optimization that jointly recovers SMAL shape, pose, and a novel implicit duplex-mesh texture. First, CSE descriptors are aligned onto the SMAL mesh via a customized ZoomOut procedure, and a pretrained image-to-CSE predictor provides dense pixel-to-vertex correspondences that replace sparse keypoints, enabling accurate PnP-RANSAC root-pose initialization and a dense reprojection loss valid for all viewpoints including rear views. Second, the camera extrinsics are factored into a fixed SfM component and an optimized animal root motion, so that temporal smoothness is enforced only on the animal pose (via an MLP over positional encodings) rather than on a conflated camera+animal parameter. For texture, the SMAL mesh is extruded into an ε-neighborhood bounded by offset inner and outer meshes; an opacity and radiance function (triplane MLP) is defined in this canonical volume and rendered via Emission-Absorption raymarching over canonical rays whose endpoints are obtained by inverse-mapping intersections with the posed boundary meshes, allowing the implicit surface to deform with the animal's articulation.

## Results

On CoP3D (50 dog videos), the method achieves IoU 0.84, IoUw5 0.79, PSNR 22.12, PSNRw5 19.40, and LPIPS 0.041, outperforming all baselines on every reported metric. On APTv2 (39 dog sequences), it achieves a tracking error of 0.035, the lowest among all compared methods. The most striking margin is in perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.041 vs. 0.164 for RAC) and worst-case silhouette consistency (IoUw5 0.79 vs. 0.59 for BITE), reflecting the benefit of dense CSE supervision and temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all three baselines on every metric where they are directly comparable. Against the template-based BARC, it improves IoU from 0.75 to 0.84 and IoUw5 from 0.47 to 0.79, with a tracking error reduction from 0.047 to 0.035 on APTv2. Against BITE, the gains are more modest on mean IoU (0.84 vs. 0.81) but substantial on worst-case IoUw5 (0.79 vs. 0.59), attributed to temporal consistency from the MLP-based pose predictor. Against the template-free RAC, the method wins across the board: IoU 0.84 vs. 0.76, PSNR 22.12 vs. 21.86, and a dramatic LPIPS improvement of 0.041 vs. 0.164 (roughly 4× lower perceptual error), while also halving the APTv2 tracking error (0.035 vs. 0.093). The authors attribute RAC's weaker tracking to its poor rendered shape quality, which degrades keypoint projection accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMAL parametric animal model (the quadruped analogue of SMPL) by integrating Continuous Surface Embeddings to replace sparse keypoint supervision with dense image-to-mesh correspondences, directly addressing the rear-view failure mode identified in prior template-based methods (BARC, BITE). It departs from the template-free video reconstruction line (RAC, BANMo) by arguing that a well-constrained parametric template with proper dense supervision outperforms learned deformable priors for in-category reconstruction. The implicit duplex-mesh texture is a novel architectural contribution that adapts the duplex radiance field concept to a deformable scaffold, bridging the gap between template-based shape fitting and implicit appearance modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first textured, animatable SMAL reconstruction from casual monocular video, enabling practical applications in AR/VR where poseable dog avatars are needed. The motion factorization insight—offloading camera jitter to SfM rather than absorbing it into the animal's root pose—is a practical design principle likely transferable to other articulated reconstruction tasks. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to quadrupeds, thin structures (ears) are poorly reconstructed due to CSE prediction errors, and the SMAL template's limited expressiveness caps reconstruction fidelity. Open questions include whether the CSE-plus-parametric-template paradigm generalizes to other animal categories and whether the implicit texture can handle more extreme non-rigid deformations beyond SMAL's deformation space.
