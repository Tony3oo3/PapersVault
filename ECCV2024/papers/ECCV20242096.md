## Problem

Virtual intervention platforms for coronary stenting require anatomies that are both realistic and controllable, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: parametric geometries allow isolation of specific morphological features yet cannot capture the intricate 3D micro-morphology of coronary lesions, while patient-specific digital twins are realistic but prevent causal disentanglement of correlated anatomic attributes. No prior method enables the controllable synthesis of topologically valid, multi-tissue 3D coronary arteries conditioned on clinically interpretable mid-level representations of both local morphology and global skeletal structure.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a set of cross-sectional morphological feature vectors (e.g., lumen area, calcium area, calcium thickness, calcium arclength) computed along the depth dimension of a straightened coronary artery, a 3D lumen skeleton heatmap encoding the branching topology, and optionally a spatial mask for region- or tissue-based editing. The output is a 4×128×128×128 volumetric segmentation map with four tissue classes (background, lumen, vessel wall, calcified tissue), where the lateral resolution exceeds the in-plane resolution, requiring interpolation for isotropic visualization. Conditioning signals are injected via channel-wise concatenation to the noised latent representation at each denoising step.

## Method

The framework builds on a Latent Diffusion Model with a 3D U-net denoiser (EDM parameterization) operating in the latent space of a VAE. Three key adaptations are introduced: (1) a topological interaction loss applied during VAE training that penalizes critical voxels where calcium or lumen are not fully enclosed by vessel wall in each 2D cross-section, reducing topological violations by an order of magnitude; (2) morpho-skeletal conditioning via non-differentiable regressors that extract cross-sectional morphological features and luminal skeletons, concatenated to the latent to condition the denoiser; and (3) Adaptive Null Guidance (ANG), a novel sampling strategy that adaptively updates the null morphological condition at each step by computing the error between the target morphology and the morphology measured from the current denoiser output using lightweight non-differentiable regressors, thereby avoiding the expensive backpropagation through the VAE decoder and regressors required by loss-based guidance methods like DPS or classifier guidance.

## Results

The proposed Adaptive Null Guidance (ANG) method achieves the best morphological and skeletal conditional fidelity among all tested guidance strategies while maintaining competitive morphological precision and recall. Computationally, ANG requires 46 s and 6.4 GB per sample, compared to 66 s / 12.5 GB (CG-Soft), 89 s / 15.1 GB (DPS-Soft), and 80 s / 8.6 GB (DPS-NN). Topological regularization of the VAE latent space reduces topological violations by roughly an order of magnitude (lumen: 17% → 0.5%; calcium: 15% → 1.1% in reconstruction; 9.21% → 2.61% in LDM sampling). Joint morpho-skeletal conditioning achieves a morphological fidelity MAE of 4.5 and skeletal fidelity MAE of 0.48, substantially better than unconditional sampling (117 and 3.2 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANG outperforms all four baselines (CFG, CG-Soft, DPS-Soft, DPS-NN) on both morphological and skeletal conditional fidelity across the full range of guidance weights (Fig. 6), while CFG degrades in visual quality as the guidance weight increases. Compared to DPS-Soft, which achieves comparable conditional fidelity, ANG produces fewer unphysiological artifacts and runs nearly 2× faster (46 s vs 89 s) with less than half the GPU memory (6.4 GB vs 15.1 GB). Against CG-Soft, ANG is ~30% faster and uses roughly half the memory, with better or equivalent fidelity. DPS-NN, which uses neural regressors to extend loss guidance to non-differentiable features, is slower (80 s) and more memory-intensive (8.6 GB) than ANG while achieving lower conditional fidelity. The key advantage of ANG is that it leverages non-differentiable morphological regressors, eliminating the need for backpropagation through the VAE decoder and regressor chain, making it the most efficient regressor-based guidance strategy tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Latent Diffusion Models (originally developed for 2D image synthesis) to the 3D multi-component segmentation domain, building on the VAE-then-diffusion architecture of Rombach et al. and the EDM parameterization of Karras et al. It extends the topological regularization line of work (persistent homology losses, critical-voxel penalties from Gupta et al.) by applying a cross-entropy-based topological interaction loss to the VAE latent space in a 3D multi-slice setting, a context where such losses had not previously been studied. For guidance, the paper departs from standard classifier-free guidance by demonstrating its failure on continuous morphological attributes and proposing Adaptive Null Guidance (ANG), which adaptively updates the null condition using a non-differentiable morphological regressor—a conceptual departure from both loss-based methods (CG, DPS) that require backpropagation and fixed null-condition CFG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework bridges a long-standing trade-off in virtual intervention platforms between the controllability of parametric geometries and the realism of patient-specific digital twins, enabling counterfactual reasoning about how anatomic variation affects device deployment outcomes. However, several limitations constrain near-term impact: the 128³ voxel resolution is low for clinical simulation, morphological conditioning is restricted to a single pre-defined direction, the decoder must be invoked at every sampling step (limiting throughput), and topological errors are reduced but not eliminated. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or more complex multi-branch topologies, whether ANG generalizes to other continuous-attribute conditioning tasks beyond coronary morphology, and whether the generated anatomies produce clinically meaningful differences in simulated device outcomes.
