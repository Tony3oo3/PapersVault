## Problem

Creating a relightable, pose-controllable digital avatar from multi-view video under a single static illumination remains unsolved: existing methods either ignore relighting entirely, require expensive light-stage setups, or recover only low-fidelity results from monocular video that lack self-shadows and space-varying BRDF. No prior work simultaneously models pose-dependent deformations (wrinkles, self-casting shadows), performs full intrinsic decomposition, and allows free-viewpoint rendering under arbitrary novel lighting from a single multi-view sequence.

## Input / Output

During training the method consumes multi-view calibrated video (up to ~100 cameras), foreground masks, 3D skeletal poses (J joint rotations plus global rotation/translation), and a static HDR environment map (16×32×3). At inference it takes an arbitrary driving skeletal pose, a new environment map, and a virtual camera position, producing a rendered image of the actor under novel lighting and pose. Internally it also outputs decomposed UV-space maps: a 512×512×3 normal map, a 256×256×512 visibility map (one channel per light source), and static 1024×1024 albedo and roughness maps that can be directly edited.

## Method

The method builds on a pose-driven implicit density field (adapted from Neural Actor) and performs intrinsic decomposition by projecting sparsely sampled normals and visibility from the density field into UV space, then densifying them with a partial-convolution NormalNet and a shallow VisibilityNet CNN. Albedo and roughness are represented as static UV maps optimized end-to-end via a differentiable microfacet renderer, regularized with L1 spatial-gradient smoothness. A UVDeltaNet MLP corrects residual mesh-registration errors by predicting per-point UV offsets conditioned on local coordinates and texture features. Training proceeds in three stages (geometry, then NormalNet/VisibilityNet pre-training, then joint intrinsic decomposition), and the final renderer uses importance sampling around the unbiased depth estimate to query all four UV components and integrate the environment map through the microfacet BRDF with per-light visibility weighting.

## Results

On the Relightable Dynamic Actors dataset (subject S1, 4000 evaluation frames across 5 test sequences), the method achieves PSNR of 18.60–19.46 dB and LPIPS of 0.163–0.173, consistently outperforming both baselines. The largest gains appear in the outdoor sequence (T6), where PSNR reaches 19.46 dB (vs. 18.36 and 18.70 for the two baselines) and LPIPS drops to 0.173 (vs. 0.203 and 0.186), reflecting the greater gap between training and test illumination. SSIM peaks at 0.800 for indoor sequences T3–T5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Because no prior method for pose-controlled human relighting provides a public implementation (RANA [17] has no code; Relighting4D [6] cannot generate novel poses), the authors construct two baselines derived from Neural Actor [32] with intrinsic decomposition: Neural Actor+IR (albedo/roughness predicted from deformed canonical 3D coordinates) and Neural Actor+IR+Tex (same but conditioned on texture features). The proposed method wins on every metric across all five test sequences. The advantage is most pronounced outdoors (T6), where PSNR improves by +1.10 dB over Neural Actor+IR and +0.76 dB over Neural Actor+IR+Tex, and LPIPS improves by 0.030 and 0.013 respectively, indicating that the UV-space material representation and NormalNet/VisibilityNet inpainting are especially beneficial when the test illumination differs substantially from training. On indoor sequences the margin is smaller but still consistent (e.g., +0.43 dB PSNR over the stronger baseline on T2). The only metric where a baseline ties or slightly edges the full method is LPIPS on T3 (0.167 vs. 0.169), which the authors attribute to UVDeltaNet adding capacity that can slightly overfit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Neural Actor [32] for its pose-driven implicit density field, but departs fundamentally from prior human relighting work (RANA [17], Relighting4D [6]) by simultaneously modeling pose control, self-shadows, and space-varying microfacet BRDF — capabilities that prior methods handled at most partially. The key conceptual departure from static-scene relighting (NeRFactor, NeRV, TensorIR) is the UV-space intrinsic decomposition strategy: rather than computing normals and visibility at every 3D coordinate (intractable for long dynamic sequences), the authors sparsely sample from the density field and densify with lightweight CNNs (NormalNet, VisibilityNet) in UV space, making the problem computationally tractable while preserving pose-dependent effects like wrinkles and cast shadows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve pose-controlled, relightable full-body human avatars from multi-view video under a single static light condition, and the first to provide quantitative benchmarks (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS) on real human relighting data. The Relightable Dynamic Actors dataset (4 identities, 6 lighting conditions, ~90k frames) fills a critical gap as the first in-the-wild benchmark for this task. Open questions include whether the UV-space decomposition generalizes to loose clothing (currently limited by coarse mesh registration), whether faster representations (e.g., Gaussian splats) can replace the NeRF-based density field, and whether the approach extends to multi-bounce or inter-reflection effects that are common in real scenes.
