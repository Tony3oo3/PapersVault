## Problem

Existing monocular 3D reconstruction methods handle either static scenes or dynamic humans in isolation, and naively combining them produces truncated bodies and human-scene interpenetration because neither component is aware of the other's spatial structure. The paper tackles the unsolved challenge of jointly reconstructing both a dynamic human and a static scene in a shared global coordinate system from a single RGB video, while correctly resolving occlusions and physical contacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241936/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular RGB video sequence, initial SMPL pose estimates per frame, and monocular depth/normal maps predicted by a pre-trained Omnidata model. It produces two SDF neural fields — a pose-conditioned human SDF in canonical space (deformed via LBS) and a scene SDF in global space — along with rendered RGB, per-pixel human/scene decomposition masks, depth, and normal maps. Notably, the human is modeled in a canonical, pose-independent space and deformed into the global scene coordinate system, rather than using a human-centric frame as in prior avatar methods.

## Method

HSR parameterizes the human and scene as two SDF-based neural networks in a shared 3D space, with the human network operating in canonical space and conditioned on SMPL pose for pose-dependent deformations (e.g., clothing wrinkles). A shape-aware sampling strategy restricts human-point sampling to the ray segment intersecting the SMPL bounding box, enabling clean human-scene separation even under heavy occlusion. The two components are composited via SDF-based volume rendering (scaled Laplace CDF for density) with sorted ray integration. Training is a global joint optimization over all K frames with six loss terms: photometric RGB, an interpenetration penalty on points simultaneously inside both SDFs, a mask loss on accumulated opacities, a self-decomposition loss (BCE + sparsity) to sharpen boundaries, monocular geometric priors (depth and normal consistency), and an Eikonal regularizer. Pose overfitting is mitigated by linear dimension reduction on pose inputs, delayed activation of pose conditioning, and periodic resets to zero.

## Results

On the SHSD dataset, HSR achieves the best performance across all human reconstruction metrics: PSNR 22.34, SSIM 0.937, LPIPS 0.057, Mask IoU 0.954, 3D IoU 0.757, Chamfer Distance 2.349 cm, and Normal Consistency 0.782. For holistic scene reconstruction, HSR achieves a normal cosine similarity of 0.936 and depth L1 error of 15.14 cm, substantially outperforming PPR (0.838 N-cos, 42.29 cm D-l1). The method also demonstrates qualitative superiority on real-world monocular sequences where Total-Recon requires additional RGB-D input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SelfRecon [33], HSR wins on every metric, with the largest gains in Mask IoU (+0.018) and Chamfer Distance (−0.655 cm), reflecting better human-scene separation and geometry. Against Vid2Avatar [21], HSR also leads on all metrics, with the most notable advantage in Mask IoU (+0.031) and 3D IoU (+0.025), attributed to the global coordinate system and shape-aware sampling that handle occlusions Vid2Avatar cannot. For holistic reconstruction, HSR dramatically outperforms PPR [67], achieving 0.936 vs. 0.838 normal cosine and 15.14 vs. 42.29 cm depth error, because PPR only models the ground plane in its physics simulation and neglects detailed scene objects. Total-Recon [53] is compared qualitatively only (Fig. 4), since it requires RGB-D/LIDAR input unavailable in the monocular setting; HSR still produces visibly superior geometry using RGB alone. PPR also fails on certain SHSD sequences, so its quantitative results are reported on a subset only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of research—SDF-based human avatar reconstruction (Vid2Avatar, SelfRecon) and monocular scene reconstruction with geometric priors (MonoSDF)—into a single global optimization framework. The key departure is treating the human and scene as co-dependent entities in a shared global coordinate system, with explicit physical constraints (interpenetration loss) and a shape-aware sampling strategy that leverages SMPL to guide where human-specific samples are placed along each ray. Compositional volume rendering, adapted from VolSDF, allows the two SDF fields to be independently optimized yet jointly rendered, enabling clean human-scene separation even under heavy occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: prior methods either ignore dynamic humans in scene reconstruction or assume unoccluded, human-centric viewpoints, producing artifacts like truncated bodies and interpenetration in real-world settings. The introduction of SHSD, a semi-synthetic dataset with ground-truth surface geometry for both humans and scenes, provides the first quantitative benchmark for holistic monocular human-scene reconstruction. Key limitations include reliance on SMPL (yielding blobby hands and blurred faces), single-person focus, and the need for a pre-trained monocular depth/normal estimator (Omnidata) as a geometric prior. Open questions include extending to multi-person scenes, integrating more expressive body models (SMPL-X), and reducing dependence on external geometric priors.
