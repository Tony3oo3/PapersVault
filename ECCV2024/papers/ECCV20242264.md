## Problem

Image-to-GIF (short video) generation remains under-explored despite its practical value in design. Existing video diffusion models generate entire frame sequences in one shot, forcing low resolutions (64×64) and offering poor fine-grained control over frame-to-frame temporal dynamics, while classical future-frame-prediction methods lack the generative quality of modern diffusion models and cannot accept diverse prompt types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242264/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single source image, a text caption, and a scalar motion magnitude (optical flow value) as input, and produces one future frame at a time in an autoregressive rollout to form a multi-frame GIF at 256×256 resolution. The motion magnitude is consumed in two ways: appended to the caption as a word-form phrase ("The optical flow is _") for CLIP encoding, and passed through a dedicated integer embedding layer. Training uses 78,692 frame pairs curated from TGIF with optical flow restricted to [2, 20], and evaluation is zero-shot on MSR-VTT and UCF-101.

## Method

Pix2Gif reformulates GIF generation as an image-to-image translation problem built on InstructPix2Pix (a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion v1.5 LDM), where each step produces one future frame conditioned on the source image, text, and motion magnitude. The key architectural addition is a Warping Module comprising a FlowNet (UNet-style) that predicts a 2-channel optical flow feature map from the single source latent conditioned on the combined text+motion embedding, and a WarpNet that applies this flow to spatially warp the source latent into a target-aligned feature. A VGG-based perceptual loss (modified for 4-channel latents) is added to the standard LDM noise-prediction loss to keep the warped feature in the same perceptual space as the target, enforcing content consistency. The warped feature is concatenated with the original source latent before feeding into the LDM's conditioning pathway, and the model is trained end-to-end at 256×256 for 25k steps on 16 V100 GPUs.

## Results

On UCF-101, Pix2Gif achieves an FVD of 285.02, substantially outperforming I2VGen-XL (563.12) and DynamiCrafter (527.06), and a PIC of 0.8763 versus 0.6329 and 0.6307 respectively. On MSR-VTT, Pix2Gif similarly leads with FVD 168.69 (vs. 278.62 and 271.63) and PIC 0.8521 (vs. 0.6018 and 0.6135). Pix2Gif trails slightly on CLIP-Sim (0.2815 on UCF-101 vs. 0.2865 for I2VGen-XL; 0.2573 on MSR-VTT vs. 0.2602 for DynamiCrafter), which the authors attribute to the metric's bias toward text-following over image fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pix2Gif decisively outperforms both I2VGen-XL and DynamiCrafter on FVD (lower is better) across both UCF-101 and MSR-VTT, with roughly 2× lower FVD than I2VGen-XL and ~1.8× lower than DynamiCrafter on UCF-101. The advantage is even more pronounced on PIC (Perceptual Input Conformity), where Pix2Gif scores 0.8763 vs. 0.6329 (I2VGen-XL) and 0.6307 (DynamiCrafter) on UCF-101, indicating far better preservation of the source image structure. On CLIP-Sim, Pix2Gif is marginally behind I2VGen-XL on UCF-101 (0.2815 vs. 0.2865) and DynamiCrafter on MSR-VTT (0.2573 vs. 0.2602), though the paper argues this metric is less informative for image-conditioned generation since the competing models sacrifice source-image fidelity to chase caption alignment. In the ablation (Table 2), Pix2Gif also substantially outperforms InstructPix2Pix on motion coherency (L2 loss 1.746 vs. 23.429 at cfg_img=1.4; PCC 0.995 vs. −0.229), confirming that the warping module and perceptual loss are critical for controllable motion generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pix2Gif builds directly on InstructPix2Pix's instructed image-to-image translation pipeline and the latent diffusion model (LDM) framework, but departs fundamentally from the dominant video generation paradigm of inflating 2D U-Nets to 3D or adding temporal attention layers. Instead of generating an entire frame sequence jointly, it reformulates image-to-video as iterative frame-to-frame translation, introducing a motion-guided warping module (FlowNet + WarpNet) and a VGG-based perceptual loss to explicitly control temporal dynamics. This positions the work at the intersection of future frame prediction (which lacked a powerful generation engine) and diffusion-based image editing (which lacked temporal structure), bridging the two with a unified motion-magnitude conditioning mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simpler autoregressive frame-by-frame translation approach can outperform joint 3D video diffusion models on FVD and PIC metrics, suggesting that explicit per-frame motion control may be more effective than implicit temporal attention for short, controllable animations. The emergent compositional action capability (combining multiple motions in a single caption) points toward a practical controllability advantage for design applications. However, significant limitations remain: 256×256 resolution with quality degradation across frames, reliance on a single curated dataset (TGIF), and the acknowledged weakness of CLIPSim scores raise open questions about whether this paradigm scales to longer, higher-resolution videos or generalizes beyond the training distribution.
