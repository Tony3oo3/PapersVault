## Problem

Editing 3D objects in neural rendering pipelines is difficult because geometry and appearance are encoded as network weights rather than explicit, manipulable representations. Existing parameterization-based editing methods (e.g., NeP, NeuTex) require 3D prior information such as tracked meshes and UV mappings, and still suffer from large distortions, while latent-code or palette-based approaches lack pixel-level controllability. This paper asks whether one can learn a high-quality, nearly bijective parameterization of a neural implicit surface to a simple 3D domain using only multi-view images, with no mesh or UV prior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images (of a single object or multiple objects sharing similar geometry) and a user-specified integer k (number of axis-aligned boxes for the polycube domain) as the sole inputs. It produces a learned parametric domain (sphere or k-box polycube) represented as an SDF, a bi-directional deformation mapping between the object surface and the domain, and a decomposed radiance field split into a view-independent material texture and a view-dependent shading texture, both defined on the parametric domain. Notably, the SDF network for the original surface is discarded after the domain-learning phase; the surface is thereafter represented entirely through the parametric-domain SDF composed with the forward deformation, so only one geometry sub-network is maintained.

## Method

The pipeline proceeds in two phases: first, a coarse SDF is trained for ~100 epochs, then a parametric domain (sphere or polycube) is fit by minimizing an SDF-matching loss plus a Laplacian-of-SDF regularizer that controls geometric complexity; the polycube SDF is made differentiable via a Kreisselmeier–Steinhauser approximation. In the second phase, two MLPs learn a forward deformation F_def (object → domain) and an inverse deformation F_inv-def (domain → object), enforced to be nearly bijective by a cycle loss weighted by the NeRF volume-rendering color weight, and kept smooth by an L2 penalty on displacement magnitudes. Angle distortion is controlled by a Laplacian regularizer that penalizes the tangential component of the discrete Laplacian of the forward map (m=6 neighbors, distance-weighted), while area distortion is managed by the user choosing k. Radiance is decomposed as r(p) = F_mat(p′, n, z_a) · exp(F_shd(p′, n, v, z_a)), with an L1 sparsity loss on the shading network to isolate local view-dependent effects, enabling independent pixel-level editing of material and shading without retraining.

## Results

The paper evaluates parameterization quality using angle distortion (E_angle) and area distortion (E_area) computed on extracted triangle meshes, where a conformal map yields E_angle = 1 and an isometric map yields E_area = 1. On the H3DS dataset, co-parameterization of human heads to a shared polycube (k=3) produces highly consistent parameterizations across subjects. For reconstruction quality, the method reports PSNR and CD on the FS-Syn dataset, acknowledging that reconstruction is slightly worse than standard SDF-based methods like VolSDF due to the additional deformation and decomposition modules. The inverse deformation is validated via Chamfer Distance (CD × 10⁻³) on point clouds mapped back from the parametric domain to the source surface.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeP [25], the primary neural parameterization baseline, the proposed method eliminates the need for a UV prior entirely; NeP produces poor texture maps without UV initialization and still exhibits significant distortion even with it (Fig. 4), whereas the proposed method learns texture maps directly from multi-view images with lower angle and area distortion. Compared to NeuMesh [54], the proposed method avoids the need for an explicit mesh and a mask of the editing region, and critically does not require network re-training after each edit, yielding more stable and visually superior editing results (Fig. 5). Against VolSDF [58], the standard SDF-based reconstruction baseline, the proposed method achieves slightly lower PSNR and higher CD on the FS-Syn dataset (Fig. 8b), a trade-off the authors attribute to freezing the SDF network after the parametric domain learning phase. Overall, the method wins on editing usability and parameterization quality (no prior needed, lower distortion) but concedes a small reconstruction-quality gap to pure SDF rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of neural parameterization for neural rendering (NeuTex, NeP) by replacing 2D UV-plane targets with 3D parametric domains (spheres, polycubes) and eliminating the dependency on tracked meshes or UV priors. It builds on the SDF-based volume rendering framework of VolSDF and the cycle-loss mechanism from NeuTex to enforce near-bijective mapping, while introducing a Laplacian regularizer (borrowed from classical conformal mapping theory) to control angle distortion in a differentiable, mesh-free setting. The radiance decomposition into view-independent material and view-dependent shading follows IntrinsicNeRF but is applied in the parametric domain to enable independent pixel-level editing of each component without re-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for 3D prior information (UV maps, tracked meshes), this work makes neural surface editing accessible in practical settings where such priors are unavailable, and the co-parameterization capability enables texture transfer across objects of similar geometry. However, the authors acknowledge that reconstruction quality is slightly degraded compared to standard SDF-based methods (VolSDF, NeuS) due to the frozen SDF network after domain learning, and the Lambertian/grayscale shading assumption limits applicability to complex materials like metals or clearcoat. An open question is whether the reconstruction-quality trade-off can be eliminated while retaining the parameterization, and whether the approach generalizes to non-Lambertian, multi-lobed BRDFs.
