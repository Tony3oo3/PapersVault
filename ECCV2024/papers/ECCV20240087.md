## Problem

A unified 3D diffusion pipeline remains unsettled: existing feed-forward 3D diffusion methods suffer from poor scalability (requiring 50+ views per instance with low-capacity MLP decoders), high computational cost from high-dimensional 3D latents, and limited generalizability (mostly unconditional, single-category generation). The paper tackles these gaps to enable fast, high-quality, and generic conditional 3D generation across diverse datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240087/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–6 posed RGB images (resized to 256×256) with Plucker coordinate camera conditioning, plus optional depth maps for category-free datasets like Objaverse, and encodes them into a compact tri-latent of shape 32×32×3×c. For conditional generation, it additionally accepts CLIP text embeddings (77×768 tokens) or CLIP image embeddings. The output is a high-resolution tri-plane neural field (128×128×3×32) from which 3D objects are rendered via volume rendering, supporting both monocular reconstruction and text/image-conditioned 3D synthesis.

## Method

LN3DIFF is a two-stage pipeline. In the compression stage, a convolutional encoder with Plucker-coordinate injection maps multi-view images into a KL-regularized 3D tri-latent space; a DiT-based decoder with interleaved self-plane and cross-plane attention blocks (inspired by Rodin but fully attention-based) decodes the latent, followed by a lightweight convolutional upsampler to produce the tri-plane, trained with differentiable rendering loss plus DINO-based adversarial supervision. In the diffusion stage, a score-based latent diffusion model (LSGM formulation with geometric mixing) is trained on the compact 3D latent using a time-dependent U-Net or DiT denoiser with velocity parameterization and importance sampling. Conditioning is injected via CLIP embeddings with classifier-free guidance (15% dropout during training). Operating in the compact 32²×12 latent space rather than high-dimensional neural fields yields 3× faster sampling while achieving state-of-the-art FID/KID on ShapeNet and enabling the first conditional 3D diffusion over the category-free Objaverse dataset.

## Results

On ShapeNet unconditional generation, LN3DIFF achieves FID@50K of 17.6 (Car), 8.84 (Plane), and 16.9 (Chair), substantially outperforming all GAN- and diffusion-based baselines. On the text-to-3D benchmark (Objaverse), LN3DIFF attains CLIP scores of 29.12 (ViT-B/32) and 27.80 (ViT-L/14), surpassing Point-E (26.35/21.40) and Shape-E (27.84/25.84). On FFHQ, LN3DIFF achieves an FID of 36.6 versus 59.3 for RenderDiffusion. Sampling speed is 5.7 s per instance (unconditional) and 7.5 s (conditional) on a single V100, roughly 3× faster than latent-free methods like RenderDiffusion (15.8 s) and DiffRF (18.7 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LN3DIFF outperforms both GAN baselines (EG3D and GET3D) by a wide margin on FID and KID across all three ShapeNet categories, while also achieving higher COV and lower MMD, indicating better 3D coverage and geometry. Against diffusion-based baselines, LN3DIFF with only V=2 views beats SSDNeRF V=3 on FID/KID for all categories (e.g., 17.6 vs 47.72 FID on Car), though SSDNeRF V=50 achieves a slightly higher COV on Car (67.82% vs 43.12%) at the cost of 25× more training views. DiffRF and RenderDiffusion are substantially outperformed on FID (e.g., 8.84 vs 101.79 on Plane for DiffRF). On FFHQ, LN3DIFF achieves FID 36.6 versus RenderDiffusion's 59.3, and supports 128×128 resolution where RenderDiffusion is limited to 64×64. For text-to-3D on Objaverse, LN3DIFF surpasses both Point-E and Shape-E on CLIP scores under both ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14 backbones. In terms of inference speed, LN3DIFF (5.7 s unconditional, 7.5 s conditional) is faster than SSDNeRF (8.1 s), RenderDiffusion (15.8 s), and DiffRF (18.7 s) on a single V100.

## Relation to Prior Work

LN3DIFF extends the Latent Diffusion Model paradigm (Rombach et al.) from 2D images to 3D generation by introducing a 3D-aware VAE that compresses multi-view images into a compact tri-latent space, on which a DiT-based denoiser performs diffusion. Architecturally, it builds on Rodin's 3D-aware attention for tri-plane decoding but replaces axis-pooling with fully parallel self-plane and cross-plane attention blocks, and adopts DiT's adaptive layer norm over standard ViT blocks. The method departs from prior 3D diffusion approaches (SSDNeRF, RenderDiffusion, DiffRF) that either perform diffusion directly in high-dimensional neural field space or require per-instance optimization for data preparation, by amortizing 3D encoding through a shared convolutional encoder and performing diffusion entirely in the compressed latent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a unified, feed-forward 3D diffusion pipeline can achieve state-of-the-art generation quality while being 3× faster and 25× more data-efficient (2 views vs. 50) than prior methods, and it is the first to support conditional (text/image) 3D generation over generic category-free datasets like Objaverse. The 3D-representation-agnostic design means the trained VAE tokenizer can be reused with new rendering backends (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting), making it a practical building block for downstream 3D vision tasks. Open questions include whether the tri-plane representation and volume rendering bottleneck can be replaced by more efficient alternatives, and whether the approach scales to even larger, more diverse datasets with additional control signals.
