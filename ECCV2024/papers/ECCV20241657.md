## Problem

Existing backdoor mitigation methods—unlearning (fine-tuning, distillation) and pruning—operate at a coarse granularity, either retraining the full model or removing entire neurons. This fails against advanced attacks that hide backdoor behavior inside hybrid neurons serving both benign and malicious functions, where pruning degrades clean accuracy while retaining the neuron preserves the backdoor. Additionally, these methods require meticulous per-attack parameter tuning, undermining generalizability.

## Input / Output

UNIT takes a potentially backdoored model and a small set of clean training samples (5% of the training set, split into training and validation subsets with a 4:1 ratio) without any knowledge of the trigger pattern. It outputs a set of per-neuron activation boundary values σ that are applied as clipping operations at every activation layer during inference, producing a defended model that outputs class labels. The defense is entirely post-training: no model weights are modified, only the activation values are constrained at inference time, adding roughly 2.5% to inference latency.

## Method

UNIT exploits the observation that backdoor neurons exhibit substantially higher activation values on poisoned inputs compared to clean inputs, and that this separability persists at the individual-neuron level even when layer-level features are indistinguishable. It initializes a loose per-neuron boundary (mean + 4σ of clean activations) and then runs an optimization loop that minimizes cross-entropy loss on clean training samples plus an L1 penalty on the boundary values, using gradient descent with Adam. A dynamic trade-off coefficient α is halved when the validation accuracy drop exceeds the defender's tolerance ε (default 2%) and doubled otherwise, automatically balancing backdoor suppression against benign accuracy. The best boundary σ* (lowest L1 norm while maintaining accuracy within ε) is retained and applied as a hard clip at inference: any activation exceeding its neuron-specific boundary is set to that boundary value. This per-neuron, optimization-driven tightening outperforms uniform percentile clipping, which cannot account for the heterogeneous contributions of individual neurons to backdoor behavior.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, UNIT achieves an average clean accuracy of 91.77% and average ASR of 3.20% across 14 backdoor attacks, using only 5% of clean training data. Against the two most advanced attacks (DFST and Adap-Blend), UNIT reduces ASR to 4.02% and 15.03% respectively, while the best baseline (SEAM) only reaches 12.93% and 24.19%. UNIT completes its defense in approximately 20 seconds with a negligible ~2.5% increase in inference time. When applied to clean models, accuracy degradation ranges from 0.32% to 2.83% across six dataset-network pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIT outperforms all 7 main baselines (FT, FP, NAD, ANP, NC, I-BAU, SEAM) on average ASR (3.20% vs. best baseline SEAM at 8.08%) while maintaining competitive clean accuracy (91.77%). The advantage is most pronounced against advanced attacks: on DFST, UNIT achieves 4.02% ASR versus SEAM's 12.93% and NC's 24.77%; on Reflection, UNIT reaches 6.63% while NC and ANP fail catastrophically at 93.31% and 93.28% respectively. UNIT's only loss is on WaNet, where ANP achieves 0.61% ASR versus UNIT's 2.44%, attributed to WaNet's pervasive sample-specific triggers that resemble natural features. In Appendix A.2, UNIT also outperforms five additional recent baselines (CLP, FST, RNP, FT-SAM, Super-FT) on the latest NARCISSUS and COMBAT attacks. The paper further demonstrates robustness against 3 adaptive attacks in Appendix A.4, confirming that the per-neuron distribution tightening generalizes beyond the 14 evaluated attack types.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNIT carves new ground at the intersection of two established backdoor mitigation paradigms—unlearning (fine-tuning, distillation) and neuron pruning—by operating at a finer granularity: per-neuron activation distribution tightening rather than whole-model retraining or whole-neuron removal. It builds on the shared observation (noted in prior work like ANP and pre-activation distribution analyses) that backdoor neurons exhibit elevated activations on poisoned inputs, but departs by showing this separability persists at the individual neuron level even when advanced attacks (DFST, Adap-Blend) diminish layer-level feature distinction. The optimization-based boundary tightening, guided by proxy accuracy on a small clean set, is a novel mechanism not present in either the unlearning or pruning lineages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNIT addresses a concrete and growing gap: existing defenses fail against advanced attacks that embed backdoor behavior in hybrid neurons serving both benign and malicious functions, where pruning or retraining cannot selectively remove only the malicious component. Its practical appeal is strong—~20 seconds of computation, 5% clean data, negligible inference overhead—making it deployable in real-world model supply-chain scenarios. However, it does not fully eliminate the most sophisticated attacks (Adap-Blend retains 15% ASR), and its reliance on a small clean sample set for distribution approximation raises open questions about robustness when clean data is scarce or distributionally shifted. Whether the per-neuron clipping strategy generalizes to non-visual modalities, larger-scale models, or multi-task settings remains to be explored.
