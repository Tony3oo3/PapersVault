## Problem

Accurately predicting a classifier's performance on a target domain is critical under distribution shift, yet most existing methods require access to source (training) data, which is often unavailable in privacy-sensitive or data-limited settings. The few source-free approaches that exist (e.g., Average Confidence, Nuclear Norm) perform substantially worse than even minimally source-dependent baselines, leaving a significant gap in reliable, source-free domain-invariant accuracy estimation.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a frozen trained classifier and a set of unlabeled target samples; no source data, target labels, network modifications, or additional fine-tuning are needed. It operates on the logits produced by the classifier for each target sample and outputs a single scalar: the predicted classification accuracy over the entire target dataset. The approach is fully deterministic, so repeated runs on the same target set yield identical predictions.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage source-free pipeline. First, a probabilistic generative model replaces the standard softmax: class-conditional Gaussian densities are estimated from target logits (clustered by pseudo-labels to approximate class means, with a shared covariance matrix computed over all target logits), and posterior probabilities are computed via Bayes' theorem, yielding calibrated predictions that adapt to the target distribution without any source data. This calibration is shown to be analogous to temperature scaling but is entirely unsupervised. Second, prediction correctness is assessed by computing two cross-entropy losses per sample—one against the pseudo-label (one-hot) and one against a uniform distribution—and comparing the L2 norms of their gradients with respect to the last linear layer's weights; a sample is deemed correctly predicted if the pseudo-label gradient norm is smaller. The final predicted accuracy is the fraction of samples classified as correct.

## Results

On single-source Digits (SVHN, USPS, SYNTH), the proposed method achieves 4.60% MAE, outperforming the best source-based baseline (Nuclear Norm-C at 8.31%) and the best source-free baseline (Nuclear Norm at 8.31%). On the Wilds benchmark (fMoW, Camelyon17), it reaches 2.61% MAE versus 6.34% for the best source-based method (COT-C) and 13.63% for the best source-free method (Nuclear Norm). On multi-source DomainBed datasets (PACS, VLCS, Office-Home, Terra Incognita), the method achieves 6.46% MAE, surpassing the best source-based baseline (Agree Score at 7.97%) and the best source-free baseline (Nuclear Norm at 15.95%). Notably, on SVHN and SYNTH, the proposed method outperforms all source-based baselines even when 100% of source data is available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and both single-source and multi-source settings. Among source-based methods, COT (Lu et al.) is the closest competitor on Wilds single-source (7.21% vs 2.61% MAE) but degrades severely in the multi-domain setup (26.72% MAE), where it underperforms even source-free baselines. ATC (Garg et al.) and Energy (Peng et al.) are the weakest source-based baselines, with MAE values of 24–41% on Digits, indicating extreme sensitivity to limited source samples. DOC (Guillory et al.) performs moderately on Digits (9.11% MAE) but struggles on Wilds (9.09%). Among source-free baselines, Nuclear Norm (Deng et al.) is the strongest (8.31% on Digits, 13.63% on Wilds), while Average Confidence (Hendrycks et al.) is consistently the weakest source-free method (9.21% and 14.81% respectively). Agree Score (Baek et al.) is the best source-based method on multi-source datasets (7.97% MAE) but still trails the proposed method (6.46%), and notably requires 100% source training data rather than just a validation subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the softmax-as-generative-model perspective (where logits correspond to log-likelihoods under Gaussian class-conditional densities) to construct an unsupervised calibration that replaces temperature scaling, which requires source validation data. The gradient-norm comparison to a uniform distribution draws on Huang et al.'s finding that gradient norms are more informative than loss values for detecting distributional shift, but applies it to a different decision problem (correctness vs. OOD detection) under the assumption of shared class labels. The approach departs from the dominant source-based performance prediction lineage (ATC, DOC, COT, Energy) by eliminating the need for any source samples at evaluation time, and extends the limited source-free methods (Average Confidence, Nuclear Norm) by adding both a generative-model calibration step and a per-sample gradient-based correctness criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in privacy-sensitive domains (medical imaging, satellite intelligence) or when source data has been consumed during fine-tuning, no source samples are available at evaluation time, yet model reliability must still be assessed. The method's determinism (no random sampling) and its ability to outperform source-based baselines even at 100% inclusion ratio on several datasets suggest that the threshold-setting instability inherent in source-based methods is a fundamental weakness, not merely a data-scarcity artifact. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-logit assumption holds for deeper or transformer-based architectures, how the method behaves when the class distribution genuinely differs between source and target (violating the stated assumption), and whether the shared-covariance simplification can be relaxed without reintroducing numerical instability at large class counts.
