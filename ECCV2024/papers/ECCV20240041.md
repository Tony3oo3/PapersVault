## Problem

Existing foundation models like SAM and CLIP are trained predominantly on terrestrial images and fail on marine data due to both distribution shift (underwater color distortion, low visibility, irregular creature boundaries) and intrinsic design limitations (SAM produces semantic-agnostic, over-segmented masks; CLIP is restricted to image-level scene understanding). No foundation model exists that provides instance-level segmentation with rich semantic descriptions specifically for marine ecosystems.

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB marine images and optionally point or box prompts, operating in either fully automatic mode (no user input) or interactive mode. It outputs a set of pixel-level instance masks, each paired with an open-ended semantic caption (up to 50 tokens) generated by a frozen VLM. Unlike category-label outputs from prior work, the captions are free-form text describing appearance, pose, color, and other attributes at multiple granularities, enabling downstream tasks like image storytelling and text-to-image synthesis.

## Method

MarineInst is built on SAM's ViT backbone with two key additions. First, a binary instance filtering branch is inserted into the mask decoder, using attention-based feature interaction between mask tokens, prompt tokens, and image embeddings to classify each generated mask as instance or non-instance, thereby suppressing over-segmentation and partial-segmentation. Second, a frozen VLM (BLIP2 or MarineGPT) is applied to each cropped instance region to produce open-ended semantic captions, replacing the category-level semantics of prior methods. Training follows an iterative scheme on MarineInst20M (2.4M images, ~20M masks): the model is first fine-tuned on human-annotated masks, then used to auto-generate masks for Internet images, with 2.65M binary-labeled mask samples (1.89M positive, 0.76M negative) specifically constructed to train the filtering branch. Both mask generation and binary filtering are optimized simultaneously, and the model is flexible to swap in different VLMs for the captioning stage.

## Results

Under the most challenging automatic (no-prompt) setting, MarineInst achieves 30.8/32.7 AP (bbox/segm) on 500 unseen marine images, substantially outperforming SAM (5.9/5.8) and Semantic-SAM (2.5/0.0). With a single point prompt, MarineInst reaches 73.1/75.4 AP, surpassing SAM (59.0/63.0) and OVSAM (46.8/49.0). Under the box-prompt setting, MarineInst attains 95.4 AP (segm) versus SAM's 93.5 and OVSAM's 79.4. On the USOD10K salient object segmentation benchmark, MarineInst (ViT-H) improves over SAM (ViT-H) by +0.7% S_m, +0.4% E_max, +0.6% maxF, and −0.3% MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MarineInst decisively outperforms SAM in automatic mode, achieving 30.8 AP (bbox) versus SAM's 5.9, a 5.2× improvement, by effectively eliminating over-segmentation and partial-segmentation through binary instance filtering. Against Semantic-SAM (granularity 3), MarineInst is superior by a wide margin (30.8 vs. 2.5 AP bbox in auto mode), as Semantic-SAM nearly fails on small and medium objects. In the point-prompt setting, MarineInst (73.1 AP bbox) beats both SAM (59.0) and OVSAM (46.8) by 14.1 and 26.3 points respectively, indicating stronger feature extraction and mask generation after marine-specific pre-training. In the box-prompt setting, MarineInst (95.4 AP segm) edges out SAM (93.5) and substantially surpasses OVSAM (79.4), whose knowledge distillation weakens both feature extraction and mask generation. In the user study (Table 5), MarineInst with MarineGPT achieves a satisfactory score of 3.52/5, far exceeding SSA (1.14), OVSAM (1.56), and MarineInst with BLIP2 (2.31), confirming that marine-specific VLMs yield more accurate and informative instance captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MarineInst builds directly on SAM's ViT-based architecture and mask decoder, adding a lightweight binary instance filtering branch to suppress over-segmentation and partial-segmentation that SAM exhibits on marine imagery. Conceptually, it departs from the SAM/CLIP lineage by unifying instance segmentation and open-ended instance captioning into a single 'instance visual description' objective, whereas prior semantic-SAM variants (Semantic-SAM, SSA, OVSAM, Grounded SAM) either assign pre-defined category labels or rely on user prompts for semantics. The instance captioning stage repurposes frozen VLMs (BLIP2, MarineGPT) as plug-and-play caption generators over cropped mask regions, extending the Q-Former/LLM paradigm from image-level to mask-level understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MarineInst20M (2.4M images, ~20M instance masks) is the largest marine image dataset to date and provides a much-needed resource for a domain where labeled data is scarce and appearance differs substantially from terrestrial imagery. The binary instance filtering technique is simple and potentially transferable to other domains where SAM's automatic mode produces spurious masks. Key limitations remain: the model struggles with crowded scenes containing many small instances, captioning quality is bounded by the frozen VLM's OCR and fine-grained recognition ability, and the approach has not been validated beyond marine/underwater settings. Open questions include whether the binary filtering can be replaced by learned confidence scoring, and whether the instance-captioning pipeline can be made end-to-end trainable rather than relying on frozen VLMs.
