## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering but requires per-scene optimization over minutes, limiting its use on unseen scenes. Existing generalizable Gaussian Splatting methods are either computationally expensive (epipolar Transformers), restricted to object-centric or human-centric reconstruction, or limited to image pairs. This paper addresses the gap of an efficient, generalizable Gaussian Splatting framework for novel view synthesis in arbitrary unseen scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–3 RGB source views with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input and produces a rendered novel view in a single feed-forward pass (21–24 FPS at 3-view, 24–300+ FPS after per-scene optimization). The generalizable model operates on a two-stage cascaded pipeline sampling 64 and 8 depth planes for coarse and fine stages, respectively. For per-scene optimization, the output is an optimized 3D Gaussian point cloud initialized from the generalizable model's multi-view predictions, refined in as little as 45 seconds.

## Method

MVSGaussian leverages a Multi-View Stereo pipeline to establish a pixel-aligned Gaussian representation: FPN-extracted features are warped onto the target frustum via differentiable homography to build a cost volume, regularized by 3D CNNs to yield depth; a pooling network then aggregates multi-view features per 3D point, and a 2D UNet adds spatial context before MLP heads decode scaling, rotation, opacity, and RGB color (bypassing spherical harmonics for better generalization). To address the many-to-many color mapping challenge inherent in splatting, the method introduces a hybrid rendering that averages the splatted view with a depth-aware volume-rendered view (one sample per ray), improving generalization. For fast per-scene optimization, a multi-view geometric consistency check computes reprojection errors across viewpoints to filter noisy Gaussians while retaining valid ones, yielding a compact, high-quality initialization that reduces optimization time to ~45 s (13.3× less than vanilla 3D-GS) while achieving superior PSNR.

## Results

After per-scene optimization, MVSGaussian achieves PSNR 26.98 / SSIM 0.913 / LPIPS 0.113 on Real Forward-facing in just 45 seconds at 350 FPS, outperforming 3D-GS (23.92 PSNR in 10 min) and NeRF (25.97 PSNR in 10.2 h). On NeRF Synthetic, it reaches 32.20 PSNR in 50 s, and on Tanks and Temples, 24.58 PSNR in 90 s. For direct generalization (no fine-tuning), MVSGaussian attains 24.07 PSNR on Real Forward-facing, 26.46 on NeRF Synthetic, and 23.28 on Tanks and Temples with 3 views, surpassing the best NeRF-based baseline ENeRF by 0.44, 0.29, and 0.75 dB respectively, while running at 21.5 FPS with only 0.876 GB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVSGaussian outperforms all generalizable NeRF baselines (PixelNeRF, IBRNet, MVSNeRF, ENeRF, MatchNeRF) on every dataset and metric in both the generalization and per-scene optimization settings; the closest competitor ENeRF is beaten by 0.44 dB PSNR on Real Forward-facing and 0.75 dB on Tanks and Temples in the 3-view generalization setting. Against the only other generalizable Gaussian method, PixelSplat, MVSGaussian achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 23.30 vs 22.99 on Real Forward-facing at 512×512) while using far less memory (0.866 GB vs 11.827 GB) and running faster (24.5 vs 1.13 FPS). In per-scene optimization, MVSGaussian surpasses vanilla 3D-GS (30k iterations) by 3.06 dB PSNR on Real Forward-facing (26.98 vs 23.92) while requiring only 45 s of optimization versus 10 min—a 13.3× speedup—and matches or exceeds 3D-GS on NeRF Synthetic (32.20 vs 31.87) and Tanks and Temples (24.58 vs 23.65). Compared to NeRF, MVSGaussian improves PSNR by 1.01 dB on Real Forward-facing and 3.16 dB on Tanks and Temples while reducing optimization time from 10.2 hours to under 2 minutes. The consistent aggregation strategy is critical: ablation shows it yields 0.26 dB higher PSNR and 2× faster optimization than simple voxel downsampling, and 0.80 dB higher PSNR than direct concatenation of multi-view point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVSGaussian sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: learning-based multi-view stereo (MVSNet's cost volume + 3D CNN regularization) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (anisotropic Gaussians with tile-based rasterization and adaptive density control). It departs from prior generalizable NeRFs (MVSNeRF, ENeRF, IBRNet) by replacing the implicit volume-rendering representation with an explicit pixel-aligned Gaussian representation, and from prior generalizable Gaussian methods (PixelSplat, GPS-Gaussian) by handling general scenes with multiple views rather than image pairs or single objects. A key methodological departure is the hybrid rendering that augments splatting with a one-to-one depth-aware volume rendering pass, motivated by the argument that splatting's many-to-many Gaussian-to-pixel color mapping is inherently harder to generalize than the one-to-one correspondence in volume rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving 300+ FPS rendering with 45-second per-scene optimization (13.3× faster than vanilla 3D-GS) while matching or exceeding its quality makes high-fidelity novel view synthesis accessible in near-real-time pipelines. The consistent aggregation strategy for initializing per-scene optimization is a reusable technique that could benefit other 3D-GS-based workflows. However, the method inherits MVS limitations (degraded depth in weak-texture or specular regions), and the reliance on a hybrid rendering path raises the open question of whether pure splatting can eventually generalize without the volume-rendering crutch. Whether the pixel-aligned, per-pixel Gaussian formulation scales to very large or unbounded scenes remains untested.
