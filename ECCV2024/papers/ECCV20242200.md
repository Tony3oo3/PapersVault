## Problem

While rectified flow has enabled one-step diffusion generation, it has not addressed model size reduction, limiting deployment in compute-constrained settings. Compressing the network introduces two specific challenges: the standard reflow initialization (copying teacher weights) is inapplicable when student and teacher have different architectures, and naive distillation underperforms on smaller-capacity student models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242200/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single sample of Gaussian noise (plus a class label for conditional ImageNet generation) and produces one generated image in a single forward pass, requiring only one NFE. Experiments use CIFAR-10 at 32×32, FFHQ at 64×64, and ImageNet at 64×64, with student models ranging from 15.7M to 80.7M parameters—substantially smaller than the 55.7M–295.9M teacher models they are distilled from.

## Method

SlimFlow comprises two stages. First, Annealing Reflow trains a small 2-rectified flow by smoothly interpolating between random-pair rectified flow training (β=1) and teacher-pair reflow (β=0) via a linearly decaying schedule β(k)=1−min(1,k/6K_step), providing a warm-start without requiring weight transfer between mismatched architectures. Second, Flow-Guided Distillation distills the 2-rectified flow into a one-step generator using a combined loss: standard distillation against pre-computed precise ODE solutions (LPIPS or ℓ₂) plus an online 2-step Euler regularization that approximates the flow trajectory cheaply, avoiding the need for expensive multi-step RK45 simulation while still providing effective supervision for the capacity-limited student.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, SlimFlow with 15.7M parameters achieves FID 5.02 with a single NFE, outperforming the previous best one-step model TRACT (FID 6.47, 19.4M params) and Consistency Distillation (FID 7.21, 15.7M params). With 27.9M parameters, SlimFlow reaches FID 4.53, surpassing the much larger 61.8M 2-Rectified Flow+Distill model (FID 4.85). On FFHQ 64×64, SlimFlow (15.7M) achieves FID 7.70, beating BOOT (66.9M, FID 9.00), and on ImageNet 64×64, SlimFlow (80.7M) achieves FID 12.34, comparable to 295.9M-parameter models trained with Consistency Training (FID 13.00) and BOOT (FID 16.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SlimFlow clearly wins among small one-step models on CIFAR-10: at 15.7M parameters it achieves FID 5.02, beating TRACT (6.47 at 19.4M) by 1.45 FID and Consistency Distillation (7.21 at the same 15.7M) by 2.19 FID. At 27.9M parameters, SlimFlow (FID 4.53) outperforms the 61.8M 2-Rectified Flow+Distill (FID 4.85) and the reproduced Consistency Distillation (FID 6.83), while using roughly half the parameters of the 55.7M DMD (FID 3.77) and Consistency Distillation (FID 3.55) baselines. On FFHQ 64×64, SlimFlow (15.7M, FID 7.70) surpasses BOOT (66.9M, FID 9.00) by 1.3 FID with less than a quarter of the parameters. On ImageNet 64×64, SlimFlow (80.7M, FID 12.34) is competitive with the 295.9M Consistency Training (FID 13.00) and BOOT (226.5M, FID 16.30), though it trails the larger DMD (295.9M, FID 2.62) and Diff-Instruct (295.9M, FID 5.57) by a wide margin. Overall, SlimFlow's advantage is most pronounced in the small-model regime (<30M params), where it consistently outperforms all competing one-step methods by 1–2+ FID points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rectified flow framework (Liu 2022; Liu et al. 2022), which introduced reflow and distillation for training one-step generators, by jointly compressing both inference steps and model size. Prior rectified flow applications maintained an invariant network structure throughout reflow and distillation; here, the student is deliberately smaller than the teacher, introducing two new challenges (initialization mismatch and reduced-capacity distillation) that the authors address with Annealing Reflow and Flow-Guided Distillation. The work also positions itself against the broader model-compression literature (pruning, quantization) by training compact models from scratch via distillation rather than post-hoc compression, and contrasts with MobileDiffusion by offering a general framework applicable to arbitrary efficient architectures rather than a single hand-designed network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SlimFlow provides a practical recipe for deploying high-quality one-step image generation on resource-constrained hardware, achieving FID 5.02 with only 15.7M parameters on CIFAR-10 and competitive results on ImageNet 64×64 with 80.7M parameters. A key limitation is that the student's quality is inherently bounded by the teacher's synthetic data pairs, capping the ceiling of the approach. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to transformer backbones, pruned networks, and large-scale models like Stable Diffusion, and whether the annealing schedule and two-step regularization generalize beyond the U-Net architectures tested here.
