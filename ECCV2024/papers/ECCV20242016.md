## Problem

Zero-shot 6D pose estimation of unseen objects has traditionally relied on extensive training over large-scale synthetic datasets (e.g., MegaPose renders 2 million images of 20,000 objects), making performance dependent on rendered data quality and diversity while incurring significant training cost. This paper asks whether task-specific training is truly necessary in the era of pre-trained foundation models, and answers no by showing that frozen geometric and vision foundation models can be combined to achieve state-of-the-art results without any training on pose estimation data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242016/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textured 3D model of a query object (sampled to 5K surface points) and an RGBD scene image (depth lifted to 1K points) as input, and outputs the 6D pose (rotation R ∈ R^{3×3}, translation t ∈ R^3) of the target object instance relative to the camera frame. DINOv2 processes 224×224 crops, and the query object's visual features are obtained by rendering the 3D model from multiple viewpoints and back-projecting pixel-level features onto the point cloud via nearest-neighbor correspondence. A zero-shot segmentation prior (CNOS or SAM6D) localizes the target in the scene before feature extraction.

## Method

FreeZe fuses two frozen foundation models—GeDi (a PointNet++-based geometric descriptor trained on indoor point clouds) and DINOv2 ViT-giant (a self-supervised vision transformer trained on web-scale images)—to produce discriminative 3D point-level descriptors without any task-specific training. Geometric features are extracted at two scales (30% and 40% of object diameter, concatenated to 64 dims), while visual features are reduced from 1536 to 64 dims via PCA; both are L2-normalized and concatenated per point. Pose estimation proceeds via RANSAC-based 3D-3D registration using nearest-neighbor matching in the fused feature space, followed by ICP refinement. A novel Symmetry-Aware Refinement (SAR) module resolves ambiguities for geometrically symmetric objects by rendering the posed model at all estimated symmetry rotations (found via Chamfer distance minimization) and selecting the best candidate through DINOv2 patch-level cosine similarity against the input crop.

## Results

FreeZe achieves a mean Average Recall (AR) of 69.3 across the seven BOP Benchmark core datasets (LM-O, T-LESS, TUD-L, IC-BIN, ITODD, HB, YCB-V) with CNOS localization and ICP+SAR refinement, and 70.9 with SAM6D localization. Without any refinement, FreeZe reaches 64.0 mean AR, already surpassing the best refined competitor SAM6D (63.2). The largest per-dataset gain is on TUD-L (93.6 AR), a dataset known for extremely challenging lighting conditions, where FreeZe outperforms SAM6D by +13.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeZe (training-free, RGBD) outperforms all six baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against the strongest RGBD competitor SAM6D (with CNOS masks and ICP refinement), FreeZe gains +6.1% mean AR (69.3 vs 63.2), with the largest margin on TUD-L (+13.6) and T-LESS (+5.7). Without refinement, FreeZe (64.0) already beats SAM6D with refinement (63.2), demonstrating that the geometric+visual feature fusion is inherently stronger than SAM6D's 3D-3D correspondence pipeline. Against RGB-only methods, the gap is even wider: FreeZe surpasses FoundPose (59.6 with refinement) by +9.7 and GigaPose (57.9) by +11.4. Notably, FreeZe with the less accurate CNOS masks (69.3) still edges out SAM6D with its own more accurate SAM6D masks (68.7), indicating that the quality of the localization prior matters less than the feature representation. The ICP+SAR refinement step contributes an additional +5.3% mean AR over the coarse RANSAC estimate, with the largest gains on IC-BIN (+2.6) where geometrically symmetric objects with discriminant textures are prevalent.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeZe departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot 6D pose estimation, where methods like MegaPose, FoundationPose, and SAM6D rely on extensive training on large-scale synthetic renderings (e.g., 2M images in MegaPose). Instead, it repurposes two frozen foundation models trained on entirely unrelated tasks—GeDi (indoor 3D point cloud registration) and DINOv2 (web-scale 2D self-supervised image features)—and fuses them into 3D point-level descriptors for RANSAC-based registration. Unlike concurrent training-free methods (ZS6D, FoundPose) that work with 2D-2D or 2D-3D correspondences, FreeZe exploits scene depth to operate in 3D-3D space, and is the first to demonstrate the synergy between geometric and vision foundation models for this task. The SAR symmetry-aware refinement module is a novel contribution that uses DINOv2 patch-level features to disambiguate geometrically symmetric objects, a problem prior methods handled less explicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for task-specific training data and large-scale synthetic rendering pipelines, FreeZe offers a modular, plug-and-play framework that can incorporate future foundation models without retraining, lowering the barrier to deploying zero-shot pose estimation in new domains. The consistent +5–10% AR improvements over the strongest trained competitors across seven diverse BOP datasets (industrial, household, cluttered, texture-less) suggest that the field's assumption that zero-shot pose estimation requires dedicated training may be outdated. Key limitations include the large model footprint (ViT-giant DINOv2) restricting edge deployment, and the reliance on depth input; the authors identify RGB-only geometric feature prediction and more sophisticated registration beyond RANSAC as important open directions.
