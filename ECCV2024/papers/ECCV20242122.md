## Problem

Practical egocentric body tracking on VR headsets is unsolved because surface-mounted cameras (the same ones used for SLAM) have blind spots, frequent occlusions, and limited field of view, making it impossible to see all body parts simultaneously. Existing approaches either rely on under-constrained sparse signals (headset + controller pose) or require impractical camera placements far from the face. Additionally, no large-scale real-image dataset exists with a realistic VR camera configuration to train and evaluate such systems.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a temporal sequence of 4 monochrome global-shutter camera images per frame (two 640×480 side cameras and two 1280×1024 front cameras, all flush-mounted 1.5 cm from the head), resized to 160×128 via a Gaussian pyramid. It outputs 26 3D body joint positions in world space. During training, sequences are chunked into 31-frame windows with the first 27 frames used as LSTM warm-up and only the last 5 frames supervised; at inference the temporal model runs recurrently on a single new frame per step, and the network is 8-bit quantized for on-device execution on a Qualcomm Hexagon Tensor Accelerator.

## Method

The architecture pairs a multi-view image encoder with a temporal reasoning module. The encoder (a U-Net-style CNN without skip connections) processes each of the 4 camera images independently with shared weights, then concatenates and jointly convolves/downsamples the features into a single latent vector per frame; it is pre-trained on 2D heatmap prediction before the decoder is removed. The latent is passed through a fully causal 1-layer temporal convolutional network (3-frame receptive field) and then a 256-dimensional LSTM to produce the 26 3D keypoints. Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining L2 pose error, a 4th-order pose-jerk smoothness term, and L2 regularization on the LSTM hidden and cell states to ensure stable predictions from short sequences. The key design insight is that multi-view latent features retain occlusion and context cues that 2D keypoint representations lose, enabling the temporal model to plausibly infer out-of-view joints.

## Results

On the EgoBody3M dataset, the proposed end-to-end latent+temporal model achieves 5.18 cm overall MPJPE and 12.2 cm out-of-view wrist MPJPE, outperforming the SOTA UnrealEgo baseline (7.41 cm and 23.7 cm respectively) by 30% and 48%. The temporal model also reduces mean per-joint velocity error (MPJVE) to 0.54 cm/frame, a 44% improvement over the per-frame lifting variant (0.97 cm/frame). On the synthetic UnrealEgo benchmark, the method achieves 6.70 cm MPJPE without any ImageNet pre-training, surpassing the best prior method (UnrealEgo with pre-training at 7.91 cm) by 1.2 cm (15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all three baselines on both evaluation datasets. Against UnrealEgo [6] (ECCV 2022), the proposed model reduces overall MPJPE from 7.41 cm to 5.18 cm (−30%) on EgoBody3M, and from 7.91 cm to 6.70 cm (−15%) on the UnrealEgo benchmark, even without ImageNet pre-training. Against xR-EgoPose [42] (ICCV 2019), the improvement on the UnrealEgo dataset is 4.59 cm (12.32 → 6.70 cm without pre-training) and 4.59 cm (11.29 → 6.70 cm with pre-training). Against EgoGlass [52] (3DV 2021), the gap is 4.07 cm (10.77 → 6.70 cm) without pre-training and 2.44 cm (9.14 → 6.70 cm) with pre-training. The advantage is particularly pronounced on out-of-view joints, where the temporal multi-view latent representation reduces wrist MPJPE by 48% compared to UnrealEgo, demonstrating the benefit of modeling temporal history and multi-view cues for occluded body parts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior egocentric body tracking work (EgoCap, EgoGlass, Mo²Cap², xR-EgoPose, UnrealEgo) by using the same four monochrome cameras already present on a VR headset for SLAM, rather than dedicated cameras mounted on rigid rods or temple frames. Architecturally, it synthesizes the multi-view latent feature extraction paradigm from VIBE-style models with temporal reasoning (TCN + LSTM), a combination not previously applied to egocentric tracking where occlusion and out-of-view joints are more prevalent than in outside-in settings. The approach also contrasts with sparse-signal methods (controller-based three-point tracking, IMU-only estimation) by providing a fully camera-based, controller-less solution that avoids the under-constrained nature of those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of EgoBody3M (30+ hours, 3.4M frames, 120 subjects, 4 realistic VR cameras) addresses a critical data bottleneck that has limited egocentric body tracking research, as prior real datasets were small (EgoCap: 60k images) or used impractical camera placements. The demonstrated on-device deployment (8-bit quantized, running on Qualcomm Hexagon) shows that accurate full-body tracking is feasible within VR hardware constraints, opening the door to controller-less avatar control in commercial headsets. Key open questions remain: handling near-field occlusions (long hair, hats), strong occlusions (tabletop covering legs), and whether incorporating the headset's own IMU/SLAM trajectory signal could further improve robustness.
