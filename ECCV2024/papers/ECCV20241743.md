## Problem

Learned-Soup, which learns optimal mixing coefficients for model soups via gradient-based optimization, requires simultaneously loading all fine-tuned models into memory and constructing a computational graph over every model, incurring over 200 GB of memory for 72 ViT-B/32 models and forcing training onto slow CPU. This memory and computation bottleneck makes learned soup infeasible for large models and hinders its practical adoption compared to the simpler Greedy-Soup.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K pre-fine-tuned model weight tensors (all sharing the same pretrained backbone but differing in hyperparameter configurations such as learning rate, weight decay, and data augmentation) along with a validation dataset for coefficient optimization. It outputs a set of learned mixing coefficients—unlike Learned-Soup, these are unconstrained scalars that can extrapolate beyond [0,1]—and a single combined model soup whose parameters are a weighted combination of the fine-tuned models. In the layer-wise variant (MEHL-Soup+), a separate coefficient vector is learned per network layer, and at any given iteration only a mini-batch of b models (e.g., 18 out of 72) needs to reside in GPU memory.

## Method

MEHL-Soup reformulates the learned soup objective as a hyperplane optimization: the combined model is expressed as θ* = θ̄ + Σ_k α_k(θ_k − θ̄), which removes the convex-hull constraint of the original softmax-parameterized Learned-Soup and permits extrapolated coefficients. A key algebraic insight is that the gradient with respect to each coefficient decomposes as ∇_{α_k} L = ∇_{θ*}^T L · (θ_k − θ̄), where the first factor is shared across all models, so only a single computational graph on the combined model θ* is needed rather than one per fine-tuned model. To avoid caching all K models, the authors apply block coordinate gradient descent: at each outer iteration a random mini-batch of b coordinates is sampled, only those b models are loaded, and the corresponding coefficients are updated for J inner steps before the next batch is loaded, reducing peak memory from O(KD) to O(bD). The layer-wise extension (MEHL-Soup+) assigns independent coefficient vectors to each of the L+1 layers, enabling finer-grained model combination. A convergence analysis establishes an O(1/√T) rate under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions, with the bound decreasing in the model mini-batch size b.

## Results

On ImageNet with CLIP ViT-B/32 (72 fine-tuned models), MEHL-Soup+ achieves 81.62% top-1 test accuracy, outperforming the best individual model (80.38%) by +1.24% and Greedy-Soup (81.03%) by +0.59%. In terms of efficiency, MEHL-Soup+ requires only 19 GB peak memory and 808 s soup construction time, compared to 253 GB and 7701 s for Learned-Soup+ — a 13× memory reduction and 9.5× speedup. On the larger ViT-L/14 model, MEHL-Soup+ consistently beats Greedy-Soup by +0.12% (CIFAR-10), +0.38% (CIFAR-100), and +0.39% (ImageNet), while Learned-Soup is infeasible (>256 GB OOM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEHL-Soup+ (81.62%) outperforms all five baselines on ImageNet ViT-B/32. Against Greedy-Soup (81.03%), it gains +0.59% accuracy while being 4× faster in soup construction (808 s vs 3501 s), since Greedy-Soup requires 143 validation epochs. Against Learned-Soup (80.88%) and Learned-Soup+ (81.39%), MEHL-Soup(+) wins by +0.32% and +0.23% respectively, attributed to the hyperplane formulation allowing extrapolated coefficients beyond [0,1]. Against Uniform-Soup (79.97%), the gain is a substantial +1.65%. Against Ensemble (81.19%), MEHL-Soup+ leads by +0.43% while requiring only single-model inference cost. The efficiency advantage is most dramatic versus Learned-Soup+: 13× less memory (19 GB vs 253 GB) and 9.5× faster (808 s vs 7701 s), enabling single-GPU execution where Learned-Soup+ requires CPU offloading.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Model Soups framework of Wortsman et al. (2022), specifically targeting the Learned-Soup variant that optimizes mixing coefficients via gradient descent. It departs from the original formulation in two key ways: (1) replacing the probability-simplex constraint (softmax parameterization) with a hyperplane formulation that permits extrapolated coefficients, drawing on recent findings that weight extrapolation beyond the convex hull improves two-model averaging (Croce et al. 2023, Rebuffi et al. 2023); and (2) repurposing classical block coordinate gradient descent to avoid loading all fine-tuned models simultaneously, connecting to the broader subspace training literature (Li et al. 2022, Wortsman et al. 2021) while avoiding its scalability bottleneck. The layer-wise extension (MEHL-Soup+) parallels the Learned-Soup+ variant but inherits the hyperplane and BCGD advantages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing peak memory from ~250 GB to ~19 GB and enabling single-GPU operation makes learned model soup accessible to practitioners who previously had to rely on the simpler Greedy-Soup or run on CPU. The sensitivity analysis showing MEHL-Soup+ is far less dependent on top-performing fine-tuned models (retaining 81% accuracy even after removing the top-42 models) has direct implications for reducing hyperparameter search costs. Open questions include whether the hyperplane extrapolation benefit persists with very large numbers of models (K >> 72), whether the approach extends naturally to non-ViT architectures or generative models, and whether the fine-tuning and soup-learning stages can be truly unified as the authors suggest in the conclusion.
