## Problem

PETL methods freeze pre-trained weights, leaving task-specific deep layers unmodified and redundant when downstream data differs significantly from the pre-training distribution. Existing structural pruning methods applied to PETL-transferred models suffer from two critical issues: they dramatically increase storage overhead because each dataset requires a different pruned structure, and they degrade accuracy because the pruned parameters are pre-trained weights unrelated to the downstream task. The paper asks whether layer-level pruning can eliminate this redundancy while preserving the low-storage characteristic of PETL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241925/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a PETL-transferred vision model (ViT or Swin backbone with Convpass or RepAdapter adapters) and a downstream dataset with class labels. It produces a pruned model where a contiguous block of top layers is removed, along with the integer pruning layer index. Crucially, only the pruning index and the adapter parameters are stored per dataset (not the full pruned weight structure), so the total storage across K datasets equals that of the unpruned PETL model. The t-SNE reduction operates on 2D projections of cls_token features, and the SC_Index is computed against the dataset's class labels as clustering ground truth.

## Method

SLS evaluates each layer's feature quality by extracting the cls_token from that layer's output, projecting it to 2D via t-SNE, and computing the Silhouette Coefficient Index (SC_Index) using the dataset labels as cluster assignments. A threshold T = α × SC_Index_N (top layer) is set, and traversing from the deepest layer downward, pruning stops at the first layer i where SC_Index_i < T; layers i+2 through N are then removed. This design ensures the classification head retains the lowest layer whose features it can still distinguish, while all layers above are deemed redundant. No additional supervised training is needed for the pruning decision—only the unsupervised t-SNE projection and clustering metric. The pruned model is retrained with a 1-CLR (cyclic learning rate restarting) strategy, which outperforms both training-from-scratch and standard fine-tuning for PETL models.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark, SLS-pruned Convpass achieves 75.4% average accuracy at 1.16× throughput with only 0.26M stored parameters, while SLS-pruned RepAdapter reaches 74.8% average accuracy at 1.42× throughput with 0.18M stored parameters. Compared to the unpruned counterparts (76.7% at 0.85× for Convpass; 75.8% at 1× for RepAdapter), the accuracy drop is modest (1.3% and 1.0% respectively) while throughput improves substantially. Against DepGraph-Hessian, SLS improves accuracy by 0.4% (Convpass) and 0.8% (RepAdapter) while reducing stored parameters from 66.6M/56.4M to 0.26M/0.18M — a reduction of over 99%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLS outperforms DepGraph-Hessian on all three metrics for both PETL backbones: accuracy (75.4% vs 75.0% for Convpass; 74.8% vs 74.0% for RepAdapter), throughput (1.16× vs 1.004×; 1.42× vs 1.18×), and stored parameters (0.26M vs 66.6M; 0.18M vs 56.4M). Against DepGraph-L2Norm the gap is even larger, with SLS gaining 3.0% and 3.8% accuracy respectively. Relative to the unpruned PETL models, SLS sacrifices 1.3% (Convpass) and 1.0% (RepAdapter) average accuracy but more than doubles throughput (0.85×→1.16× and 1×→1.42×). In the broader PETL comparison (Table 1), the SLS-pruned models still surpass VPT (72.0%), LoRA (74.5%), and AdaptFormer (74.7%) in average accuracy while offering higher throughput. On the structured sub-category, pruned RepAdapter actually matches or exceeds the unpruned model (60.8% vs 60.8%), confirming that the pruned deep layers were redundant for cross-domain data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLS sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) and structural pruning, but deliberately departs from both lines. On the PETL side, it builds on add-based (Convpass) and reparameterization-based (RepAdapter) methods as the base models to be pruned. On the pruning side, it rejects the parameter/filter-level granularity of magnitude-based (L2Norm) and gradient-based (Hessian/DepGraph) methods, as well as the per-layer classifier approach of Chen et al., in favor of an unsupervised feature-space evaluation (t-SNE + Silhouette Coefficient) that operates at the layer level. The key conceptual departure is reframing pruning as a deep-to-shallow operation (removing entire layers) rather than a wide-to-narrow one (removing filters/channels), motivated by the observation that PETL models' frozen pre-trained deep layers are the primary source of redundancy when downstream data diverges from the pre-training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical storage bottleneck that prior pruning work overlooked: because filter-level pruning produces different network structures per downstream dataset, the cumulative stored parameters balloon (66.6M vs. 0.26M in their comparison), undermining the parameter-efficiency promise of PETL. SLS's layer-index-only storage scheme directly resolves this, making it a practical complement to PETL pipelines. However, the limitation of losing all pre-trained information in pruned layers (even if irrelevant to the current task) remains, and the paper itself flags the open question of achieving finer pruning granularity without inflating storage. The modest accuracy trade-off (~1–2%) and the finding that iterative pruning offers negligible benefit over one-shot for PETL models suggest the method is best suited for throughput-constrained deployment rather than accuracy-critical settings.
