## Problem

Plane arrangement methods produce concise polyhedral decompositions suitable for low-poly surface and volume modelling, but their construction is prohibitively expensive: exhaustive arrangements scale as O(n³) and even adaptive variants cannot process more than ~1000 planar primitives without parallelisation. This limits their applicability to complex objects and entire scenes, where thousands of planes are detected from LiDAR or photogrammetry data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241986/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (e.g., 40k–2M points) and a set of detected planar primitives, each consisting of a supporting plane and its associated inlier points. It produces either a watertight, intersection-free polygon surface mesh with convex polygonal facets, or a volume mesh of intersection-free convex polyhedra. The surface output can be remeshed so each planar region is represented by a single facet (with Delaunay triangulation for holes), and the volume output can be further simplified by merging adjacent cells, optionally allowing controlled overlap via a threshold τ.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable adaptive plane arrangement built on a BSP-tree where each node stores inlier points and their associated planes. Two key mechanisms reduce unwanted splits: (1) a dynamic ordering scheme that selects the next split by first prioritising planes that would create terminal (un-splittable) cells, then maximising the product |L_p|·|R_p| to balance inlier distribution across child nodes; and (2) replacing expensive polyhedra–polygon intersection tests with hierarchical point–plane orientation tests that distribute inlier points through the tree, directly exploiting raw measurement precision. A post-processing stage aggregates coplanar facets via boundary-edge cycle detection and constrained Delaunay triangulation for surfaces, and merges same-sided BSP siblings plus near-convex adjacent cell pairs (controlled by a volume-difference threshold τ) for volumes, reducing cell counts by ~25% without geometric recomputation.

## Results

On the Thingi10k plane arrangement benchmark, the proposed method produces dramatically more concise decompositions: 73 cells (vs. 324 Adaptive, 161 KSR) and 53 surface facets on simple models, scaling to 705 cells and 478 facets on complex models with >250 planes, while maintaining competitive Chamfer (0.224×10⁻²) and Hausdorff (2.78×10⁻²) distances. Construction time drops from 504 s (Adaptive) and 1699 s (KSR) to 68.1 s on complex models, and the method handles 10k planar shapes in ~30 minutes. For surface simplification, the pipeline matches RLPM's triangle count while achieving lower Chamfer and Hausdorff distances and shorter runtime; for convex decomposition, it outperforms CoACD in both IoU and facet count for |C_V| > 500, and surpasses BSP-Net on both CD² and normal consistency across all 13 ShapeNet categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the exhaustive arrangement [12], the proposed method achieves a 78× reduction in cell count (73 vs. 5690) on simple models with only a marginal accuracy trade-off (CD 0.193 vs. 0.190, HD 1.15 vs. 1.05), while being 2.6× faster. Compared to the adaptive arrangement [21], the method wins on every metric: 4.4× fewer cells (73 vs. 324) and 1.3× fewer facets on simple models, 8.7× fewer cells (705 vs. 6150) and 7.4× faster (68.1 s vs. 504 s) on complex models, with slightly better CD and HD. Versus KSR [3], the method produces 2.2× fewer cells and 3.8× fewer facets on simple models, and 2.9× fewer cells with 25× faster construction on complex models, while matching or slightly improving accuracy. For surface simplification, the pipeline outperforms RLPM [8] in Chamfer, Hausdorff, and normal consistency at matched triangle counts, with shorter runtime, because planar primitives preserve local detail that iterative edge collapse degrades. Against CoACD [29] for non-overlapping convex decomposition, the method is both more accurate (higher volumetric IoU) and more concise (fewer facets per convex) for decompositions with |C_V| > 500, though CoACD retains an IoU advantage at very low cell counts (<400) where fewer input planes limit fidelity. Finally, against BSP-Net [9] on all 13 ShapeNet categories (8762 models), the method achieves lower squared Chamfer distance and higher normal consistency while producing fewer, more meaningful convexes that capture thin components (e.g., individual pillows) rather than large overlapping volumes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive plane arrangement framework of Murali & Funkhouser [21], which iteratively slices a 3D domain with detected planes to form a polyhedral decomposition. It departs from prior adaptive methods (which use static area- or orientation-based ordering and expensive polyhedra-polygon intersection tests) by introducing a dynamic, data-driven ordering scheme and replacing intersection queries with simple point-plane orientation tests on inlier points. The approach also relates to the kinetic shape reconstruction (KSR) [3] and building reconstruction [7] pipelines, but positions itself as the first mechanism to scale plane arrangements to 10k+ planar primitives without block decomposition or parallelisation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making plane arrangements practical for complex scenes (10k planes in ~30 minutes on standard hardware), this work removes the primary scalability bottleneck that has limited the adoption of arrangement-based modelling for real-world LiDAR and photogrammetry data. The competitive results against both learning-based (BSP-Net) and specialized (CoACD, RLPM) methods on surface simplification and convex decomposition suggest that well-designed geometric algorithms can match or exceed learned approaches for these tasks without training data. Open questions include whether the method can be extended to jointly detect and arrange planes (avoiding the separate detection step), how it behaves on highly non-planar or organic shapes, and whether the BSP-tree simplification can be made more aggressive without sacrificing the intersection-free guarantee.
