## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods rely on a single shared text prompt across all views of a 3D object, making it impossible to customize specific viewpoints (e.g., distinct patterns on the front and back of a T-shirt). This limitation restricts the ability to produce 3D assets with view-dependent appearances while maintaining instance-level geometric consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240665/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an overall text prompt (shared across all views) plus up to four view-specific text prompts (front, back, left, right) and a camera position as input. During training, view-specific texts are auto-generated by BLIP-2 from rendered 512×512 multi-view images of Objaverse objects, and the overall text is synthesized by GPT-4 from the merged view captions. The 2D variant outputs multi-view images at 256×256, while the 3D variant produces an implicit-volume (Mip-NeRF) representation optimized over 10,000 steps (~55 min on A100), with rendering resolution annealed from 64×64 to 256×256.

## Method

The core contribution is an adaptive guidance injection module plugged into every U-Net block of a Stable Diffusion v2.1 backbone. At each block, it computes cosine similarity between the globally-pooled image embedding and the CLIP class tokens of both the overall and view-specific text embeddings; whichever text has the lower similarity (i.e., is less 'absorbed' by the current features) is injected into the cross-attention layer, with a margin hyperparameter controlling the consistency–customization trade-off. The model is trained with a standard diffusion denoising loss on Objaverse rendered multi-view images combined with LAION data, using an expanded cross-view attention mechanism for multi-view generation. For 3D lifting, DreamView-2D replaces the text-to-image model in DreamFusion's score distillation sampling pipeline, and the 3D representation is optimized with an x₀-reconstruction loss where the diffusion model's gradient is detached, inheriting the view-specific customization and instance-level consistency learned in 2D.

## Results

On the Objaverse validation set (1,000 objects, 4 views each), DreamView-2D achieves the highest CLIP view-text score (32.1) and CLIP image-image score (0.73), demonstrating superior customization and overall fidelity compared to SD-v2.1 (26.8–29.4 / 0.48–0.53) and MVDream (28.6–30.1 / 0.65–0.67). MVDream slightly edges out DreamView on CLIP overall-text score (31.3 vs. 31.1), but DreamView's view-text score exceeds MVDream's by 2.0 points. In the user study (35 participants, 180 objects), 74.5% of users rated DreamView-3D as best fitting the text description and 67.9% preferred it overall, substantially outperforming all five baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamView-2D outperforms both SD-v2.1 and MVDream on CLIP view-text score (32.1 vs. 29.4 and 30.1 respectively), confirming its superior view-specific customization capability. On CLIP image-image score, DreamView (0.73) clearly surpasses MVDream (0.67) and SD-v2.1 (0.53), indicating a better joint balance of consistency and customization. MVDream marginally leads on CLIP overall-text score (31.3 vs. 31.1), but this gap is negligible while DreamView's view-text advantage is substantial (+2.0). SD-v2.1 achieves the highest Inception Score (15.6) but has the worst CLIP scores, reflecting its inability to handle 3D multi-view generation. In text-to-3D qualitative comparisons (Figure 7), DreamView-3D is the only method that correctly generates all specified contents (e.g., crossbody bag, scarf, horse) at their expected positions, while DreamFusion, SJC, Magic3D, MVDream, and ProlificDreamer either omit objects, misplace them, or exhibit 3D inconsistencies such as multiple faces or arms. In the user study, DreamView-3D receives 74.5% preference for text fidelity and 67.9% overall preference, far exceeding all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamView extends the 2D-lifting text-to-3D paradigm established by DreamFusion (SDS) and subsequent works like MVDream and ProlificDreamer, which all rely on a single shared text prompt across all views of a 3D object. The key departure is the introduction of view-specific text conditioning alongside the overall text, mediated by an adaptive guidance injection module that dynamically selects which text dominates each U-Net block based on image-text similarity. This builds on the Stable Diffusion architecture and CLIP text encoders, but repurposes the cross-attention mechanism to handle two competing text signals rather than one. The training data pipeline (Objaverse rendering + BLIP-2 captioning + GPT-4 merging) is a practical contribution that enables the view-specific text supervision at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DreamView addresses a practical gap in 3D content creation: the inability to specify different appearances for different viewpoints of the same object (e.g., distinct patterns on the front and back of a T-shirt). The margin-based trade-off between consistency and customization is a simple but effective control knob that artists can tune. However, the 4-view azimuth discretization is a coarse approximation, face details remain blurry at the 256×256 training resolution, and the method requires consistent object descriptions across views to avoid generation failure. Open questions include whether finer-grained view conditioning (beyond 4 sectors) is feasible, whether the approach scales to higher resolutions without the face-detail degradation, and whether the adaptive injection mechanism generalizes to other multi-condition diffusion settings beyond text-to-3D.
