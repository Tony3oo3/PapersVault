## Problem

Existing one-stage TAD models suffer from two coupled limitations: static feature extraction produces low-discriminability representations due to spatial redundancy across adjacent frames, and shared-weight detection heads cannot adapt their parameters to action instances of vastly different temporal lengths. Prior dynamic methods (deformable convolutions, dynamic convolutions) adapt only one of the kernel weights or the receptive field, leaving the other fixed and thus unable to fully resolve either issue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241229/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted 1D temporal feature sequences (e.g., from SlowFast, I3D, VideoMAE V2-giant, or EgoVLP backbones) rather than raw video frames, operating entirely in the temporal domain. At each timestamp and pyramid level, it outputs per-class action probabilities (via a 1D convolution + Sigmoid) and start/end boundary distances (via a 1D convolution + ReLU), from which absolute action boundaries are recovered as t ± d. During inference, detections above a score threshold are retained and refined with Soft-NMS.

## Method

The core contribution is the Dynamic Feature Aggregation (DFA) module, which decomposes a standard convolution into a feature-shifting step followed by a pointwise convolution, then learns an input-conditioned mask (via a lightweight module Ψ and a non-negative activation φ) that simultaneously zeros unimportant sampling positions and re-weights the remaining shifted features—effectively producing a fully dynamic convolution with data-dependent kernel weights and receptive fields. DFA is instantiated in two components: the DynE encoder layer, which uses dual-branch DFA_Conv (a global instance-dynamic branch and a multi-kernel-size branch) to aggregate temporal features within action ranges and sharpen boundary discriminability, and the DyHead, which applies DFA_Att across three paths (down, up, depth) to selectively fuse adjacent-scale pyramid features with input-adapted parameters at each detection level. Training follows the ActionFormer protocol with center sampling, focal loss for classification, and DIoU loss for boundary regression, all trained end-to-end with AdamW.

## Results

DyFADet achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across six TAD benchmarks. On HACS-Segment, it reaches 44.3% avg mAP with VideoMAE V2-giant features (new SOTA, +1.2% over TriDet) and 39.2% with SlowFast. On ActivityNet-1.3, it achieves 38.5% avg mAP with R(2+1)D features, surpassing TriDet by 1.7%. On THUMOS14, it obtains 69.2% avg mAP with I3D features (nearly matching TriDet's 69.3%), and on FineAction it sets a new SOTA of 23.8% with VM2-g, outperforming ActionFormer by 5.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyFADet outperforms TriDet [39] on HACS-Segment (39.2% vs 38.6% with SlowFast; 44.3% vs 43.1% with VM2-g) and ActivityNet-1.3 (38.5% vs 36.8%, a 1.7% gain), while on THUMOS14 it is essentially tied (69.2% vs 69.3%). Against ActionFormer [59], DyFADet wins on every dataset: THUMOS14 (69.2% vs 66.8%), ActivityNet-1.3 (38.5% vs 36.6%), HACS (39.2% vs 36.4%), Epic-Kitchen 100 verb/noun (25.0%/23.4% vs 23.5%/21.9%), and Ego4D-MQ1.0 (22.8% vs 21.6% with EV+SF). On FineAction, DyFADet with VM2-g achieves 23.8% avg mAP, surpassing ActionFormer (VM2-g) by 5.6% (23.8% vs 18.2%). Compared to TALLFormer [10] and TCANet [37], DyFADet leads by 2.7–3.3% on HACS and 2.9–3.0% on ActivityNet-1.3. The ablation (Table 7) confirms that the DynE encoder contributes +1.0% and DyHead contributes a further +1.4% over the all-convolution baseline (62.1% → 69.2% on THUMOS14), with the full DyFADet outperforming all individual dynamic-module alternatives (DeformConv, SE, Dyn Conv, TD3DConv).

## Relation to Prior Work

DyFADet builds directly on the encoder-pyramid-head framework established by ActionFormer and TriDet, adopting their transformer-based encoder architecture, multi-scale feature pyramid, and training protocol. The core novelty is the Dynamic Feature Aggregation (DFA) module, derived from the Shift decomposition of convolutions, which simultaneously adapts both kernel weights and temporal receptive fields in a data-dependent manner—unlike deformable convolutions (which only adapt receptive fields) or dynamic convolutions (which only adapt weights). The paper positions DFA as a unifying generalization: restricting the mask to binary values recovers 1-D deformable convolution, while removing the non-linear activation recovers dynamic convolution, placing both as special cases of the proposed formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing two well-identified failure modes in TAD—poor feature discriminability from static extraction and head inadaptation for diverse action lengths—DyFADet provides a principled dynamic mechanism that is both effective and relatively lightweight (18.8M params vs. 29.2M for ActionFormer, with comparable or lower latency). The consistent SOTA improvements across six diverse benchmarks (including egocentric and fine-grained settings) validate the approach broadly. Open questions include whether the dynamic design space (K vs. C vs. CK formations) can be further optimized, whether sparse convolution can reduce the computational cost of the mask generation, and whether DFA generalizes beyond TAD to other video understanding tasks such as action segmentation or video question answering.
