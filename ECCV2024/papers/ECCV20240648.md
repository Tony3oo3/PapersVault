## Problem

Existing camera-based 3D occupancy prediction methods construct dense 3D feature volumes (e.g., 200×200×16), incurring high computational costs (2–3 FPS) despite over 90% of voxels being empty. Moreover, the standard voxel-level mIoU evaluation suffers from inconsistent depth penalties and ambiguous labeling of unscanned regions, making it vulnerable to exploitation by predicting thicker surfaces. This paper addresses both the architectural inefficiency and the evaluation flaw simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes 6 surround-view RGB camera images at 704×256 resolution along with 7 or 15 history frames as input, processed through a ResNet-50 backbone and FPN. The output is a sparse 3D occupancy representation consisting of only K voxels (K=32000, roughly 5% of the 640000 dense grid), each assigned a semantic class label and a binary mask indicating segment membership. Unlike dense methods that produce a full W×H×D×C volume, SparseOcc outputs a variable-length set of voxel tokens with correlated embeddings, enabling direct extension to panoptic occupancy by swapping semantic queries for instance queries.

## Method

SparseOcc is a fully sparse two-stage architecture: a sparse voxel decoder reconstructs class-agnostic geometry via a coarse-to-fine pipeline where each layer applies self-attention, generates 3D sampling offsets, projects reference points to multi-view images for adaptive feature mixing, and prunes voxels by top-k occupancy score; a mask transformer then predicts per-segment masks and labels using N sparse queries that interact with 2D features through mask-guided sparse sampling (projecting 3D points within the previous layer's predicted mask to images via bilinear interpolation). Temporal modeling is achieved by warping sampled reference points to previous timestamps to retrieve history image features, avoiding the need for dense feature warping. The entire pipeline contains no dense 3D features, no sparse-to-dense modules, and no global attention. The paper also introduces RayIoU, a ray-casting evaluation metric that queries predicted 3D volumes along LiDAR-like rays and scores true positives by matching first-hit depth (within a threshold) and class, eliminating the inconsistent depth penalty of voxel-level mIoU.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes validation set, SparseOcc (8 history frames, ResNet-50, 704×256 input) achieves 34.0 RayIoU at 17.3 FPS, outperforming all prior methods under a weaker setting. With 16 history frames, performance improves to 35.1 RayIoU (12.5 FPS), and further to 36.1 RayIoU with 48 training epochs. Notably, SparseOcc is 5.8× faster than BEVFormer (17.3 vs. 3.0 FPS) and 1.7× faster than FB-Occ (17.3 vs. 10.3 FPS) while achieving higher RayIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseOcc (8f) achieves 34.0 RayIoU, surpassing BEVFormer (32.4, R101, 1600×900, 4f) by +1.6 RayIoU while being 5.8× faster (17.3 vs. 3.0 FPS). Against FB-Occ (33.5, the CVPR 2023 occupancy challenge winner using 16f and complex forward-backward view transformation), SparseOcc (8f) leads by +0.5 RayIoU with fewer frames and 1.7× higher throughput. SparseOcc also outperforms BEVDet-Occ-Long (32.6, 8f) by +1.4 RayIoU at 21.6× the speed (17.3 vs. 0.8 FPS). The gap over RenderOcc (19.5) and SimpleOcc (22.5) is substantial (+14.5 and +11.5 RayIoU respectively). However, SparseOcc reports lower voxel-level mIoU (30.1) than dense baselines (39.1–39.3), which the authors attribute to the dense methods' tendency to predict thick surfaces that inflate mIoU under the visible-mask evaluation protocol — a flaw their proposed RayIoU metric is designed to eliminate.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseOcc adapts the sparse query-based architecture of SparseBEV (originally for 3D object detection) to the fundamentally different problem of 3D occupancy, where every voxel must be accounted for rather than a sparse set of objects. The mask transformer component draws directly from Mask2Former's masked-attention design, but replaces dense cross-attention with a mask-guided sparse sampling mechanism to maintain the fully sparse pipeline. The paper departs from the dominant dense 3D feature paradigm (BEVFormer, FB-Occ, SurroundOcc) and from partially sparse methods like VoxFormer that still require a sparse-to-dense MAE module, positioning itself as the first architecture with no dense 3D operations at all. The RayIoU metric is a conceptual departure from the voxel-level mIoU standard established by Occ3D, motivated by a demonstrated vulnerability in that metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that only 5% of voxels (32,000 out of 640,000) are needed for competitive occupancy prediction, combined with a 5–8× speedup over dense baselines, makes real-time camera-only occupancy feasible on standard hardware and opens the door to deploying such systems in production. RayIoU exposes a concrete evaluation flaw (thick-surface hacking) that has likely inflated reported mIoU scores across the field, and its adoption could reshape how occupancy methods are compared. Key limitations remain: early-stage pruning errors are irrecoverable, and the mask transformer is constrained to an unreliable sparse space, which caps accuracy. Open questions include whether the sparse paradigm generalizes to larger scenes, more classes, or online HD-map-integrated settings where the road surface is removed.
