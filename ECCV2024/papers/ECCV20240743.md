## Problem

Large vision-language models like CLIP are predominantly trained on RGB images and are too computationally heavy for edge devices, yet edge robots and sensors routinely produce non-RGB data (depth, infrared) without human annotations. Existing work addresses cross-modal knowledge transfer and model compression in isolation, and naively combining them causes significant performance degradation. This paper tackles the joint problem of adapting a large VL model to a compact, quantized, modality-agnostic encoder using only unlabeled paired images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes unlabeled pairs of co-located RGB and non-RGB (depth or SWIR infrared) images captured by an edge device's sensors; no scene labels are required at any point. At inference, a single modality-agnostic image encoder processes either an RGB or non-RGB image and produces a feature vector that is compared via cosine similarity against text-encoder embeddings of candidate class names (generated from a ChatGPT-4 label superset) to yield an open-vocabulary scene class prediction. The final deployed model is an 8-bit integer encoder (56–86 MB) running on Jetson-class hardware via TensorRT.

## Method

EdgeVL is a two-stage adaptation pipeline. Stage 1 performs dual-modality knowledge distillation: a pre-trained CLIP ViT-G image encoder acts as teacher, and a single compact student (ViT-S, Swin-T, or DAT-T) with shared weights processes both RGB and non-RGB inputs, minimizing L1 distance to the teacher's RGB features on both modalities; an automatic curation step uses a ChatGPT-4-generated label superset and a confidence threshold (τ_c = 0.25) to discard noisy samples without human inspection. Stage 2 applies quantization-aware training (fake-quantization nodes, Int8) combined with a novel contrastive triplet loss that uses pseudo-labels from the pre-trained VL model and semi-hard negative mining to preserve and even improve feature discriminability after quantization. The key insight is that contrastive learning within the QAT loop compensates for the representational degradation that plain distillation or PTQ cannot recover, yielding up to 15.4% accuracy gains over baselines while reducing model size by up to 93×.

## Results

On ScanNet (21 indoor scene categories), EdgeVL (DAT-T, Int8) achieves 49.9% average open-vocabulary accuracy (47.9% non-RGB / 52.0% RGB), outperforming the strongest baseline SKD (34.5%) by 15.4% and pre-trained CLIP-G (26.8%) by 23.1%. On EuroSAT (10 land-cover classes), EdgeVL (Swin-T, Int8) reaches 64.2% average accuracy (61.3% SWIR / 67.1% RGB), surpassing the top baseline CQD (49.4%) by 14.8%. In terms of efficiency, EdgeVL (Swin-T) reduces model size from 330 MB (CLIP-B) to 56 MB (a 5.9× reduction) and cuts inference latency by 44–52% on Jetson Orin Nano and AGX Orin, while increasing throughput by 42–93% on RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EdgeVL (Int8) outperforms all seven baselines (all F32) on both datasets and both modalities. On ScanNet, the strongest baseline is SKD [52] at 34.5% average; EdgeVL (ViT-S) leads by 10.2% (44.7%), and EdgeVL (DAT-T) by 15.4% (49.9%). On EuroSAT, CQD [45] is the strongest baseline at 49.4% average; EdgeVL (ViT-S) leads by 15.4% (64.8%). The single-modality baselines CMKD [24] and Fida [46] show a stark modality imbalance (e.g., CMKD non-RGB gets 37.8% on depth but only 11.5% on RGB on ScanNet), whereas EdgeVL maintains balanced performance across both modalities. Pre-trained CLIP-G [40], despite being 93× larger (5,213 MB vs. 56 MB for Swin-T), is surpassed by EdgeVL on non-RGB accuracy (6.2% vs. 46.0% on ScanNet depth) and on RGB (47.3% vs. 48.7%), confirming that targeted adaptation with a compact student encoder can match or exceed the much larger teacher. Frank [17] and Gupta [23] are the weakest baselines, with average accuracies below 20% on ScanNet, highlighting the difficulty of cross-modal weight transfer without the dual-modality distillation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

EdgeVL sits at the intersection of cross-modality knowledge distillation and quantization-aware training, two areas that prior work has addressed in isolation. It builds directly on CLIP's pre-trained visual encoder as a teacher and on the QAT framework of Jacob et al., but departs from both by (i) distilling to a single dual-modality student that processes RGB and non-RGB images through shared weights rather than separate per-modality students, and (ii) augmenting QAT with a semi-hard triplet contrastive loss to preserve feature discriminability post-quantization. The automatic dataset curation via a ChatGPT-generated label superset and CLIP confidence scoring is a novel label-free filtering step not present in prior cross-modal KD methods, which typically assume labeled training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic framework to bring large VL models to edge devices across visual modalities without manual annotations, achieving up to 93× model-size reduction and ~50% latency cuts on Jetson hardware while improving open-vocabulary scene classification accuracy by up to 15.4%. A key open limitation is the observed trade-off: adapting the encoder for non-RGB modalities degrades RGB accuracy relative to the original CLIP encoder, suggesting the current distillation objective does not fully preserve the teacher's RGB generalization. Extending the framework beyond scene classification (e.g., to detection or segmentation) and to more than two modalities, as well as closing the RGB accuracy gap, are natural next steps.
