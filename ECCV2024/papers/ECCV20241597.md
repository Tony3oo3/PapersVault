## Problem

Conventional masked image modeling (MIM) for medical images relies on random masking that uniformly samples all regions, overlooking anatomically significant structures such as organs and tumors that are more challenging to reconstruct and more informative for downstream segmentation. This uniform strategy degrades pretraining efficiency, forcing MIM methods to require extensive training data to achieve competitive performance in data-constrained medical imaging settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241597/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D medical image volumes (CT, MRI, or PET/CT) resampled to 1.5×1.5×1.5 mm³ with z-score normalization, processed in 112×112×128 patches. During pretraining, the model reconstructs masked voxels; during finetuning, it produces pixel-wise segmentation masks for multi-organ (up to 104 structures) or tumor lesion delineation. The two-stage pipeline first performs self-supervised pretraining on unlabeled CT (TotalSegmentator) and then transfers to supervised finetuning on target datasets.

## Method

AnatoMask introduces a reconstruction-guided self-masking strategy within a self-distillation framework: a teacher network (maintained as an EMA of the student) receives a randomly masked input, computes patch-level reconstruction losses, and selects the top r_t regions with highest losses as anatomically significant areas to mask. The final mask combines these high-loss regions with randomly sampled voxels to maintain an overall masking ratio γ (60%). A masking dynamics function linearly increases r_t from 0 to 0.5 over training, implementing an easy-to-hard curriculum that prevents the model from over-focusing on difficult regions early. The backbone is STU-Net with sparse submanifold convolution in the encoder and a hierarchical multi-scale decoder for progressive reconstruction, enabling both local and global feature aggregation during the MIM objective.

## Results

On TotalSegmentator (1204 CT volumes, 104 structures), AnatoMask with STU-Net-B achieves 84.3% DSC and 87.8% NSD after 1000 pretraining epochs, surpassing the best prior SSL method UniMiSS (82.2% DSC) by 2.1% and the closest MIM baseline SparK (83.6% DSC) by 0.7%. Scaling to larger backbones yields 87.8% DSC (STU-Net-L, 440M params) and 89.0% DSC (STU-Net-H, 1.5B params). On transfer tasks, AnatoMask (STU-Net-B) leads on FLARE22 (94.0% DSC), AMOS22 (89.1% DSC), and AutoPETII (75.8% DSC), consistently outperforming all six baselines across CT, MRI, and PET modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnatoMask (STU-Net-B) outperforms all six baselines on TotalSegmentator, with the largest margins over contrastive methods BYOL (+5.5% DSC) and SelfMedMAE (+5.5% DSC), and a 2.1% DSC gain over the strongest prior SSL method UniMiSS. Against the closest MIM competitor SparK (same STU-Net-B backbone, random masking), AnatoMask improves DSC by 0.7% and NSD by 1.1%, confirming that reconstruction-guided masking is more effective than uniform random masking. On transfer to FLARE22, AnatoMask leads SparK by 0.2% DSC and UniMiSS by 2.3%; on AMOS22 it leads SparK by 0.6% and UniMiSS by 1.4%; on AutoPETII it leads SparK by 0.5% and UniMiSS by 11.8% DSC, indicating particularly strong gains in the challenging PET lesion segmentation setting. Scaling to STU-Net-H extends these advantages, reaching 89.0% DSC on TotalSegmentator and 78.1% on AutoPETII, demonstrating that the reconstruction-guided masking strategy scales effectively with model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnatoMask extends the masked image modeling (MIM) paradigm from natural images to 3D medical segmentation, building directly on SparK's hierarchical encoder-decoder with sparse submanifold convolution for CNN-based pretraining. Its key departure from prior MIM methods (MAE, SimMIM, SparK) is replacing uniform random masking with a reconstruction-guided, self-distilled masking strategy that adaptively targets anatomically significant regions. The self-distillation mechanism (teacher generates masks, student learns from them, teacher updated via EMA) repurposes the BYOL/MoCo-style momentum update not for representation alignment but for iterative mask refinement, a novel application of that architectural pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that reconstruction loss naturally highlights anatomically complex regions and can be exploited to improve pretraining efficiency, AnatoMask offers a practical recipe for data-constrained medical imaging where large-scale labeled data is unavailable. The demonstrated scalability to 1.5B-parameter backbones and cross-modality transfer (CT→MRI, CT→PET) suggests the approach could underpin future medical foundation models. However, pretraining is limited to CT, and the evaluation focuses on organ segmentation; whether the reconstruction-guided masking strategy generalizes to MRI pretraining or fine-grained tumor segmentation remains an open question.
