## Problem

Transformer-based super-resolution methods rely on self-attention to model non-local dependencies, but the quadratic dot-product attention is computationally prohibitive for low-power devices, and the inherent low-pass filtering nature of SA produces overly smooth reconstructions that lose local details. The paper asks whether an efficient feature modulation block can approximate SA-like non-local exploration while simultaneously capturing local high-frequency information at a fraction of the cost.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution output at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. Training uses 64×64 random crops with horizontal flips and rotations from DIV2K or DF2K, and upsampling is performed via a PixelShuffle layer after a 3×3 convolution that adjusts channels to match the target scale. A global residual connection is inserted before the reconstruction head to facilitate high-frequency learning.

## Method

SMFANet is a pure-CNN architecture built from stacked Feature Modulation Blocks (FMBs), each containing a Self-Modulation Feature Aggregation (SMFA) module and a Partial Convolution-based Feed-Forward Network (PCFN). The SMFA splits normalized input features into two parallel branches: an EASA branch that downsamples by 8×, applies a 3×3 depthwise convolution, modulates the result with the global variance of the original features via a 1×1 convolution, then upsamples and element-wise multiplies back to the full resolution to approximate self-attention at near-linear cost; and an LDE branch that uses a depthwise 3×3 convolution followed by two 1×1 convolutions with GELU to capture local high-frequency details. The PCFN further refines the fused features by splitting hidden channels, applying a 3×3 convolution to only half of them (partial convolution) to encode spatial context while reducing redundant computation, then recombining. Two model sizes are provided: SMFANet (8 FMBs, 36 channels) and SMFANet+ (12 FMBs, 48 channels), both trained end-to-end with Adam and cosine annealing over 1M iterations.

## Results

On the ×4 SR task, SMFANet+ (DF2K) achieves 32.51 dB PSNR on Set5, 28.87 dB on Set14, 27.74 dB on B100, 26.56 dB on Urban100, and 31.29 dB on Manga109, outperforming SwinIR-light by 0.14 dB on average across five testsets while using only 43% of its FLOPs (28G vs. 65G). Inference time is 17.39 ms for SMFANet+ versus 177.06 ms for SwinIR-light (~10× faster). The smaller SMFANet (197K params, 11G FLOPs) also surpasses all CNN-based lightweight baselines, e.g., gaining 0.27 dB over IMDN on ×4 Manga109 with only 27% of its FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CNN-based lightweight methods (Table 1, ×4), SMFANet (197K params, 11G FLOPs) outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset: it gains 0.27 dB over IMDN on Manga109, 0.08 dB over ShuffleMixer on Set5, and 0.07 dB over SAFMN on Set14, while using 27–75% fewer FLOPs. Against ViT-based methods (Table 2, ×4), SMFANet+ (DF2K) achieves the best or near-best PSNR on all five testsets, edging out SRFormer-light by 0.12 dB on Manga109 and SPIN by 0.03 dB on Set5, while using roughly half the FLOPs (28G vs. 42–88G). The headline comparison is with SwinIR-light: SMFANet+ (DF2K) gains 0.14 dB average PSNR across five testsets with 43% FLOPs and 10× faster inference (17.39 ms vs. 177.06 ms). SMFANet+ (DIV2K) is slightly behind SwinIR-light on Urban100 (26.45 vs. 26.47 dB) but leads on Manga109 (31.06 vs. 30.92 dB). ELAN-light and HPINet-S are competitive on individual datasets (e.g., ELAN-light leads on Urban100 at 26.54 dB) but trail SMFANet+ (DF2K) on average. Overall, SMFANet+ wins or ties on the majority of dataset-scale combinations while maintaining the lowest FLOPs among all ViT-based comparators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: lightweight CNN-based SR methods (SAFMN, ShuffleMixer, IMDN) that use non-local feature modulation via large-kernel or multi-scale convolutions, and ViT-based SR methods (SwinIR, ELAN, SPIN) that rely on window-based self-attention for long-range dependency. It departs from both by proposing a purely CNN architecture whose EASA branch approximates the non-local aggregation behavior of self-attention through downsampling, variance-based global modulation, and element-wise gating—without ever computing an attention matrix. The PCFN draws on partial convolution ideas from ShuffleMixer and Restormer to add spatial mixing at reduced cost, while the complementary EASA+LDE parallel design directly addresses the documented low-pass bias of attention mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment on resource-constrained devices: SMFANet+ achieves 10× faster inference than SwinIR-light with only 43% of the FLOPs while matching or exceeding PSNR, making Transformer-quality SR feasible on mobile and embedded hardware. The PSD analysis providing a clear frequency-domain explanation for why EASA and LDE are complementary (low-frequency structure vs. high-frequency detail) offers a useful design principle for future hybrid architectures. Open questions include whether the fixed 8× downsampling factor in EASA is optimal across scales and content types, whether the variance-based global descriptor generalizes to other restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising), and whether the approach can be extended to video SR without prohibitive temporal overhead.
