## Problem

Unsupervised 3D pose estimation remains difficult because existing methods either produce uninterpretable keypoints requiring supervised post-processing (SPP) to map to anatomical joints, or rely on unpaired pose annotations, manually designed templates, or other human-involving priors. This paper asks whether easily obtainable foreground masks—extractable via off-the-shelf unsupervised segmentation—can serve as a sufficient supervisory signal to directly predict physically interpretable 3D joints without any pose annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241160/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and outputs 18 3D joint coordinates (including thorax) via a 3D heatmap with integral regression. During training, it additionally consumes a binary foreground mask per frame, extracted by graph cut (Human3.6M), chroma key (MPI-INF-3DHP), or SAM fine-tuning (TikTok), but this mask is never needed at test time. The detector uses Group Normalization instead of Batch Normalization to avoid inference-time batch-statistics estimation that would degrade left-right disambiguation.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces ground-truth pose supervision with a mask reconstruction loss: predicted 2D keypoints are rendered into two differentiable mask representations and compared against the extracted foreground mask. The Skeleton Mask draws extended Gaussian line segments along each bone (encoding structural connectivity), while the Physique Mask is regressed from the Skeleton Mask by a lightweight U-Net (Shape Constraint Network) to capture full body shape. Training proceeds in a cascaded fashion—first optimizing only the Skeleton Mask (excluding arms/hands) to resolve joint identity ambiguity, then introducing the Physique Mask for shape refinement, and finally adding arms/hands—while geodesic distance weighting amplifies hard positive/negative pixels to smooth the optimization landscape. Left-right ambiguity is resolved by re-projecting predicted 3D joints into calibrated multi-view images and enforcing Skeleton Mask consistency across views via triangulation.

## Results

On Human3.6M, the proposed method (with SPP) achieves an MPJPE of 65.5 mm and P-MPJPE of 61.9 mm, outperforming the best prior SPP-based unsupervised method (Honari et al. [15], MPJPE 73.8 mm) by 8.3 mm. Without any SPP or supervised flip, the method reaches P-MPJPE of 90.4 mm, competitive with the best No-SPP baseline (Kundu et al. [24], P-MPJPE 89.4 mm) while requiring no unpaired poses or templates. On MPI-INF-3DHP, the method with SPP achieves MPJPE of 13.67 cm, surpassing Honari et al. [15] (14.57 cm), and without SPP achieves 19.36 cm with PCK of 60.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 14 baselines spanning SPP-based and No-SPP unsupervised 3D pose estimation. In the SPP group on Human3.6M, the proposed method (MPJPE 65.5 mm, P-MPJPE 61.9 mm) outperforms all prior methods, with the closest competitor Honari et al. [15] at 73.8/63.0 mm — a gap of 8.3 mm in MPJPE. In the No-SPP group, the method without any supervised flip (P-MPJPE 90.4 mm) is within 1 mm of Kundu et al. [24] (89.4 mm), which requires unpaired pose priors and supervised flip, while the method with supervised flip (P-MPJPE 79.3 mm) clearly surpasses all No-SPP baselines. On MPI-INF-3DHP, the SPP variant (MPJPE 13.67 cm) beats Honari et al. [15] (14.57 cm) and Denton et al. [6] (22.28 cm), while the no-SPP variant (19.36 cm) remains competitive with Sosa et al. [45] which uses unpaired 2D annotations. Notably, the proposed method is the only one requiring neither unpaired poses, templates, nor supervised flip in its fully unsupervised configuration, distinguishing it from all listed baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised 3D pose estimation line initiated by implicit keypoint discovery methods (Zhang et al., Lorenz et al., Suwajanakorn et al.) and explicit skeleton-based approaches (Kundu et al., Sosa et al.), but departs from all of them by replacing pose annotations, templates, and unpaired 3D priors with a single supervisory signal: the foreground mask obtained from off-the-shelf segmentation. The closest prior work, Kundu et al. [24], still requires unpaired 3D pose priors and paired same-background images; here, the Skeleton Mask (structure prior via differentiable bone rendering) and Physique Mask (shape constraint via a lightweight U-Net) form a coarse-to-fine supervision hierarchy that eliminates the need for any pose annotation or manually designed template. The backbone follows the Hourglass + integral regression paradigm from Sun et al. [48], and the geodesic weighting and cascaded optimization are novel training strategies specific to this mask-supervised setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a fully annotation-free pipeline that produces physically interpretable 3D joints without supervised post-processing, templates, or unpaired pose data, making it directly applicable to large-scale in-the-wild video. The scalability analysis (Fig. 6) reveals that a mask-supervised model trained on diverse in-the-wild data (TikTok) generalizes better to unseen outdoor scenes (MPII) than a fully supervised model trained on the more controlled Human3.6M dataset, suggesting that data diversity via mask supervision can compensate for the absence of 3D labels. Open questions include handling of body occlusions and head articulation, the residual left-right ambiguity (20.33% ratio even with triangulation), and whether the approach extends to multi-person or heavily cluttered scenes where mask extraction degrades.
