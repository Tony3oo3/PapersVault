## Problem

In Source-Free Object Detection (SFOD), the absence of labeled source data makes Mean Teacher self-training unstable: the teacher accumulates errors from unevolved student updates, while the student replicates incorrect pseudo labels and gets trapped in local optima, creating a harmful circular dependency that causes rapid performance degradation. Existing remedies such as tuning EMA weights or update intervals do not resolve this uncontrollable deterioration, particularly for transformer-based detectors that are highly susceptible to domain shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Deformable DETR detector (trained on the source domain, e.g., Cityscapes) and a stream of unlabeled target-domain RGB images (e.g., Foggy Cityscapes, BDD100k daytime, or Cityscapes car instances). Images are consumed through paired strong/weak augmentations and a masked-image branch (patch size 64, mask ratio 0.5) within the student model. The output is standard object detection predictions—bounding boxes and class labels—evaluated by mAP@0.5, with a confidence threshold of 0.3 applied to filter pseudo labels during training.

## Method

DRU introduces a co-evolutionary training loop that dynamically decides whether to update the teacher or retrain the student based on the student's prediction uncertainty (computed as variance across all decoder layers) over a buffer of historical target images. When the student has evolved (uncertainty decreased), the teacher is updated via EMA with a dynamic interval; when the student is trapped in a local optimum, only the decoder is re-initialized from the historical student while the backbone and encoder continue training, allowing the retrained decoder to escape the sub-optimal state guided by improved feature representations. A Historical Student Loss, formulated as a Quality Focal Loss over classification scores from the historical student model, provides an additional supervision signal that sets a performance lower bound and mitigates the influence of incorrect pseudo labels from a deteriorating teacher. The full pipeline is built on Deformable DETR with a Masked Image Consistency module for context learning, and the overall loss combines the teacher-guided detection loss (strong + masked branches) with the historical student classification loss.

## Results

On the Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes benchmark, DRU achieves 43.6% mAP, surpassing the best SFOD method (IRG, 37.1%) by 6.5 points and matching the best UDA method (DA-DETR, 43.5%). On Cityscapes → BDD100k, DRU reaches 36.6% mAP, outperforming the best SFOD (A2SFOD, 31.6%) by 5.0 points and the best UDA (MRT, 33.7%) by 2.9 points. On Sim10k → Cityscapes, DRU achieves 58.7% AP for car detection, exceeding the best SFOD (PETS, 57.8%) and the best UDA (MTM, 58.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRU outperforms all 18 baselines across all three adaptation scenarios. Among SFOD methods, the closest competitor is IRG [28] at 37.1% mAP on Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes, which DRU surpasses by 6.5 points; on Cityscapes → BDD100k, A2SFOD [4] (31.6%) and PETS [18] (31.3%) trail DRU (36.6%) by 5.0 and 5.3 points respectively. Among UDA methods, DA-DETR [35] (43.5%) and MTTrans [33] (43.4%) are the nearest on the foggy benchmark, with DRU edging ahead by 0.1–0.2 points; on Sim10k → Cityscapes, MTM [30] (58.1%) is the closest UDA competitor, beaten by 0.6 points. Notably, DRU uses a transformer-based detector (Deformable DETR) while most SFOD baselines use Faster R-CNN, and it achieves these gains without any access to labeled source data. The largest margins appear on the cross-scene task (Cityscapes → BDD100k), where DRU leads the best UDA method MRT [38] by 2.9 points and the best SFOD by 5.0 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mean Teacher (MT) self-training framework and the Masked Image Consistency (MIC) module, positioning itself within the Source-Free Object Detection (SFOD) line of work initiated by SED and extended by A2SFOD, IRG, PETS, and LODS. It departs from prior SFOD methods by diagnosing the root cause of self-training degradation as a circular dependency between teacher and student models, rather than addressing symptoms through additional losses or multi-teacher ensembles. The Dynamic Retraining-Updating mechanism draws on selective retraining ideas from MRT (which requires source data) and uncertainty-based evolution assessment from semantic segmentation, adapting both to the source-free setting where no ground truth is available for evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The co-evolutionary training perspective offers a principled explanation for why MT-based self-training degrades in source-free settings, and the DRU mechanism provides a practical, hyperparameter-light solution that eliminates the need for manual EMA tuning. Achieving parity with UDA methods (43.6% vs. 43.4–43.5% mAP on Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes) without source data validates SFOD as a viable alternative under privacy constraints. Limitations include evaluation only on driving/urban scenes with a single detector architecture (Deformable DETR), and the added training complexity of dynamic retraining and historical student supervision may be difficult to transfer to other self-training paradigms or tasks beyond detection.
