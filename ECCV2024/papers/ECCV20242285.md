## Problem

Existing diffusion-based methods for handwritten text generation (HTG) are limited in how they represent and handle unseen writing styles, typically conditioning on a fixed class embedding that restricts generation to previously seen writers. GAN-based alternatives suffer from mode collapse and training instability, producing insufficient stylistic diversity. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a few-shot style representation scheme into latent diffusion models for HTG, enabling generation of both seen and unseen writer styles from only 5 exemplar images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242285/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 5 style sample images from a target writer (resized and padded to 64×256 pixels) and a text prompt (a word, which may be in-vocabulary or out-of-vocabulary) as input, and produces a single handwritten text image in the target writer's style. The text prompt is processed by CANINE-C, a tokenization-free character-level encoder that assigns a unique token to each character, enabling OOV word generation without a fixed vocabulary. The 5 style images are passed through a frozen MobileNetV2 backbone, their d-dimensional feature embeddings are averaged, and the result is linearly projected to the model dimension to form the style condition.

## Method

DiffusionPen is a conditional latent diffusion model built on a UNet denoiser operating in the 4×W/8×H/8 latent space of a pretrained VAE encoder, using DDIM scheduling (1K timesteps for training, 50 for sampling). The key architectural contribution is a hybrid style encoder (MobileNetV2) trained jointly with triplet loss (metric learning) and cross-entropy classification, which produces a continuous, well-separated style embedding space that supports both seen and unseen style generation as well as smooth interpolation and multi-style mixture. The text condition comes from CANINE-C, and both conditions are injected into the UNet to guide the denoising process. Training uses a standard denoising objective L=‖ε−ε_θ(z_t, c_s, c_τ)‖², with the style encoder trained independently and frozen during diffusion training. The few-shot scheme (k=5 exemplars, averaged embeddings) combined with the hybrid loss is what enables high-diversity sampling from the posterior while maintaining style fidelity, avoiding the mode collapse observed in prior GAN and diffusion HTG methods.

## Results

On the IAM dataset, DiffusionPen achieves a FID of 22.54 and writer classification accuracy of 70.31%, substantially outperforming WordStylist (FID 34.20, Acc 68.25%) and GAN-based methods (FID 43.97–50.21, Acc 2.14–3.25%). For the HTR imitation task, DiffPen reaches CER 6.94% and WER 18.11%, closest to the real IAM performance (CER 5.16%, WER 14.49%), compared to WordStylist (CER 8.26%, WER 23.36%) and GANwriting (CER 39.94%, WER 73.38%). When used as data augmentation with only 55K synthetic samples, DiffPen improves the HTR baseline from CER 5.14%/WER 14.33% to CER 4.71%/WER 13.61%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionPen decisively outperforms all GAN-based baselines: GANwriting (FID 43.97, Acc 3.25%) and SmartPatch (FID 50.21, Acc 2.14%) fail to capture writer identity, with writer classification accuracy near random, while DiffPen reaches 70.31%. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor WordStylist [24], DiffPen improves FID from 34.20 to 22.54 and writer accuracy from 68.25% to 70.31%, and reduces HTR imitation CER from 8.26% to 6.94% and WER from 23.36% to 18.11%. The Transformer-based VATr [25] achieves CER 21.74%/WER 50.55% on HTR imitation, far behind DiffPen. For the HTR augmentation task, DiffPen with only 55K synthetic samples (CER 4.71%, WER 13.61%) outperforms CTIG-DM [43] which requires 1M synthetic samples (CER 5.19%, WER 13.37%), and substantially beats ScrabbleGAN [34] (100K samples, CER 13.42%) and Kang et al. [16] (CER 6.75%). The method also improves upon the no-augmentation HTR baseline of Retsinas et al. [29] (CER 5.14%, WER 14.33%), demonstrating that the generated data carries genuine stylistic diversity useful for recognition training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own WordStylist [24] (ICDAR 2023), which applied latent diffusion to styled handwritten text generation but was restricted to seen styles via explicit class embeddings. The key departure is a hybrid style encoder combining triplet metric learning and classification loss, producing a continuous style space that supports few-shot (k=5) conditioning on unseen writers, in contrast to GAN-based methods (GANwriting, SmartPatch, VATr) that rely on adversarial writer classifiers and typically require k=15 samples. The use of CANINE-C for vocabulary-free character-level text encoding enables out-of-vocabulary word generation, and the style variation strategies (interpolation, multi-style mixture, noise injection) represent a new axis of control not explored in prior HTG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact lies in data augmentation for HTR: 55K DiffusionPen-generated samples improve CER from 5.14% to 4.71%, demonstrating that diffusion-generated handwriting can meaningfully assist recognition training at modest scale. The few-shot unseen-style capability opens applications where a writer's data is scarce, and the style interpolation/mixture tools offer controllable generation for design or forensic purposes. Limitations include a hard length ceiling (words beyond ~20 characters fail), struggles with rare character combinations and complex cursive ligatures, and the ethical risk of handwriting forgery. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full sentences or paragraphs, whether the hybrid style encoder generalizes beyond the IAM writing population, and how to close the remaining gap between generated and real data quality (70% vs 92% writer classification accuracy).
