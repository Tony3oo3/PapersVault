## Problem

Most lightweight object trackers directly adopt pre-existing detection backbones (e.g., LeViT) without tailoring the architecture to tracking's specific demands, leaving a significant accuracy gap versus high-performance trackers. The paper identifies that in a one-stream tracker, the self-attention (feature extraction) component in deep layers is largely redundant for tracking, while the cross-attention (relation modeling) between template and search frames is what drives performance—yet prior work has not systematically decomposed and pruned these sub-modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240759/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a 128×128 template crop (from the first frame) and a 256×256 search crop (centered on the previous frame's prediction) as RGB images. Both are first passed through a CNN-based Dual Attention Unit for channel- and spatial-level feature enhancement before tokenization. The output is a single target bounding box, obtained by regressing the expected coordinates of top-left and bottom-right corner points from probability maps produced by a fully convolutional Corner Head.

## Method

FERMT is a one-stream tracker built on a ViT-Tiny backbone whose 12 attention layers are split into two functional stages: the first 6 layers use Mixed-attention Blocks (asymmetric self-attention plus one-way template→search cross-attention, following MixFormer's A₂ structure) for joint feature extraction and relation modeling, while the last 6 layers use Cross-attention Blocks (bidirectional template↔search cross-attention only, A₅ structure) that discard the computationally expensive self-attention terms, reducing attention FLOPs by 42% versus a full A₃ design. A CNN-based Dual Attention Unit (depth-wise conv → dilated depth-wise conv → 1×1 conv for spatial attention, combined with average-pooling + FC for channel attention) preprocesses both template and search images to compensate for the reduced feature-extraction capacity of the lightweight backbone. The model is trained end-to-end with AdamW (lr 4e-4, weight decay 1e-4) for 300 epochs on GOT-10k, TrackingNet, LaSOT, and COCO2017 using a combined classification + L1 + GIOU loss, and achieves 69.6% AO on GOT-10k at 51 fps on CPU—outperforming the prior best real-time tracker HiT by 5.6% AO while running 54% faster.

## Results

On GOT-10k, FERMT achieves an Average Overlap (AO) of 69.6%, the best among all real-time trackers, surpassing the previous best (HCAT at 65.1%) by 4.5% and HiT-B (64.0%) by 5.6%. On TrackingNet, FERMT records AUC 80.8%, PNorm 85.9%, and P 78.1%, all leading real-time results. On LaSOT, FERMT obtains AUC 65.1%, PNorm 74.6%, and P 69.1%, again the best real-time performance. In terms of speed, FERMT runs at 225 fps on GPU and 51 fps on CPU, which is 1.3× faster than HiT-B on GPU and 54% faster on CPU, while using fewer parameters (2.40G vs. 4.34G) and fewer FLOPs (7.98M vs. 42.14M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FERMT outperforms all real-time baselines on GOT-10k AO, achieving 69.6% versus HiT-B's 64.0% (+5.6%), HCAT's 65.1% (+4.5%), and MixformerV2-S (not reported on GOT-10k AO). On TrackingNet, FERMT leads all real-time trackers with AUC 80.8%, edging out HiT-B (80.0%) by 0.8% and MixformerV2-S (75.8%) by 5.0%. On LaSOT, FERMT's AUC of 65.1% surpasses HiT-B (64.6%) and HCAT (59.3%). In speed, FERMT runs at 225 fps on GPU and 51 fps on CPU, beating HiT-B (175/33 fps) by 29% on GPU and 54% on CPU, while using substantially fewer parameters (2.40G vs. 4.34G) and FLOPs (7.98M vs. 42.14M). Against non-real-time trackers, FERMT trails Ostrack384 (AO 73.7%) and ARTrack384 (AO 75.5%) on GOT-10k accuracy but runs 1.7× faster on GPU and 2.8× faster on CPU compared to Ostrack, effectively bridging the speed–accuracy gap. On NFS, UAV123, and LaSOT_ext (Table 2), FERMT also achieves the best real-time results (65.1, 67.5, and 46.1 respectively), confirming consistent superiority across diverse tracking scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

FERMT extends the one-stream tracking paradigm established by Ostrack (Ye et al., ECCV 2022) and the asymmetric attention design of MixFormer (Cui et al., CVPR 2022), but departs from prior lightweight trackers (HiT, HCAT, E.T.Track) that simply adopt off-the-shelf backbones like LeViT without tailoring them to tracking-specific needs. The key conceptual departure is a principled decomposition of the standard attention matrix into feature-extraction (self-attention) and relation-modeling (cross-attention) sub-modules, motivated by the observation that deep-layer self-attention is largely redundant when the goal is template–search interaction rather than general feature refinement. A CNN-based Dual Attention Unit is added as a lightweight preprocessing stage to compensate for the reduced representational capacity of the ViT-Tiny backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FERMT narrows the long-standing gap between real-time and high-performance trackers, achieving near-Ostrack accuracy (69.6 vs 71.0 AO on GOT-10k) at 2.8× faster CPU speed, which is practically significant for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The architectural principle that deep-layer feature extraction can be pruned in favor of pure cross-attention interaction offers a reusable design heuristic for other one-stream vision tasks. Remaining open questions include whether this pruning strategy generalizes beyond single-object tracking (e.g., multi-object or video understanding), whether the small residual AO gap can be closed by redesigning the prediction head, and whether the DAU's benefit persists when the backbone is scaled up.
