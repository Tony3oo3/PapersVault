## Problem

Training spiking neural networks via backpropagation through time (BPTT) suffers from two compounding issues: memory costs grow linearly with simulation time steps, and surrogate gradient approximations accumulate errors over time. Existing equilibrium SNN approaches address memory but require many time steps to converge to a fixed point, limiting their practical speed advantage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240912/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (28×28 grayscale for Fashion-MNIST, 32×32 for CIFAR-10/100) or spiking event streams (N-MNIST) as input. The input is injected only into the first implicit stream; all other streams initialize to zero and receive information through inter-stream fusion. The output is the firing rate of the smallest implicit stream at equilibrium, passed through a fully connected classifier to produce a class label. The firing rate is computed via a single-step DBReLU approximation rather than explicit multi-step spike simulation.

## Method

MPIS-SNNs decompose the temporal depth of a standard SNN into multiple parallel implicit streams (IS) of decreasing spatial resolution, each containing a feature extraction block (convolution, batch normalization, variational dropout, spiking neurons) and a transformation module. Streams are mutually fused via sliding downsampling/upsampling connections, so that input information propagates gradually from the first stream to the last as all streams converge to their respective fixed points simultaneously. In the forward pass, the network iterates until equilibrium (equivalent to infinite time steps); in the backward pass, gradients are computed via implicit differentiation of the fixed-point equation, requiring only a single-step simulation and yielding constant memory regardless of simulation length. The paper further derives DBReLU—the double-bounded rectified linear unit—as an exact firing-rate estimator at equilibrium, eliminating the need for surrogate gradients and their time-accumulated errors.

## Results

On N-MNIST, MPIS-SNNs achieve 99.51% accuracy at T=30, setting a new state-of-the-art. On Fashion-MNIST, MPIS reaches 93.83% accuracy with only T=1 time step, outperforming LTC-SNNs (93.58% at T=784) by a large margin in both accuracy and latency. On CIFAR-100, MPIS achieves 74.93% at T=5, surpassing all compared methods. On CIFAR-10, MPIS attains 93.27% at T=10, competitive with OTTT (93.73% at T=6) while using a different architecture. Memory cost remains constant (1.2G on Fashion-MNIST, 3.3G on N-MNIST) regardless of time steps, whereas BPTT grows to 12.8G or runs out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPIS-SNNs outperform IDE-Net on all four datasets with substantially fewer time steps: on CIFAR-10, MPIS achieves 92.79% at T=30 versus IDE-Net's 90.37% at T=30 (and 90.57% at T=100), while training in 2min34s vs. 12min10s. On N-MNIST, MPIS edges out both IDE-Net (99.47%) and HS-IF (99.44%) to reach 99.51%. On Fashion-MNIST, MPIS achieves 93.83% at T=1, beating LTC-SNNs (93.58%) which requires T=784, and IDE-Net (90.25%) by over 3.5 points. On CIFAR-100, MPIS (74.93% at T=5) surpasses AC2AS (73.61% at T=5), IDE-Net (73.43% at T=100), OTTT (71.11% at T=6), and Temporal Pruning (70.15% at T=1). The only comparison where MPIS does not lead is CIFAR-10, where OTTT achieves 93.73% at T=6 versus MPIS's 93.27% at T=10, though MPIS uses a different (convolutional) architecture. Overall, MPIS achieves the best or near-best accuracy across all datasets while maintaining constant memory cost and the lowest firing rates (e.g., 8.0e-4 vs. 5.06e-2 in the first layer on N-MNIST).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep equilibrium model (DEQ) framework [1] to spiking neural networks, building directly on IDE-Nets [37], which first applied implicit differentiation to train feedback SNNs at their equilibrium state. The key departure is the introduction of multi-parallel implicit streams (MPIS) that decompose the single deep weight-tied block into shallower, mutually-fused parallel branches, addressing the latency bottleneck of prior equilibrium SNNs. The theoretical derivation of DBReLU as an unbiased firing-rate estimator for equilibrium SNNs also departs from the surrogate-gradient approach used in standard BPTT-based SNN training, providing a principled alternative to the accumulated approximation errors of SG methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constant memory cost independent of simulation time steps is a practically significant advantage for SNN training, removing the hardware bottleneck that makes long-time-step BPTT infeasible. The multi-parallel architecture's ability to achieve higher accuracy with fewer time steps (e.g., 30 vs. 100 for IDE-Nets on CIFAR-10) addresses the latency concern that has limited equilibrium SNNs' practical deployment. Open questions include scalability to larger benchmarks (ImageNet), applicability to non-visual or sequential tasks, and whether the approach translates to hardware-efficient neuromorphic inference. The paper explicitly positions itself as demonstrating competitiveness rather than SOTA, leaving room for further architectural refinements.
