## Problem

Camouflaged object detection requires expensive pixel-level annotations (~60 min/image), while existing weakly supervised methods (points, scribbles, boxes) underperform significantly due to the high foreground-background similarity in camouflaged scenes. The paper tackles how to achieve near-fully-supervised COD accuracy using only 1–20% pixel annotations plus cheap box prompts, while addressing the severe noisy pseudo labels that inevitably arise when generating supervision from such limited data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240009/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, PNet takes a single 384×384 RGB image and outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask. During training, the auxiliary network ANet consumes both the RGB image and a box-masked proposal (image multiplied by the bounding box mask) through dual ConvNeXt branches, while the primary network PNet uses only the image through a single PVTv2-B4 branch. The training set is partitioned into a small fully-labeled subset (1–20%) and a large box-annotated subset (80–99%), with pseudo labels generated by ANet for the latter.

## Method

The WSSCOD framework is two-stage: ANet (dual-branch ConvNeXt + Frequency Transformer with DWT + ASPP + Reverse Fusion Decoder) is trained on the small fully-labeled set with box prompts to generate pseudo labels for the remaining images; PNet (single-branch PVTv2-B4 with the same decoder) is then trained on the union of clean and pseudo labels. The key contribution is the Noise Correction Loss L_NC, which uses a hyperparameter q∈[1,2] to switch between an IoU-form (q=2, early learning phase, accelerating convergence to correct pixels) and a MAE-form (q=1, memorization phase, providing uniform per-pixel gradients that prevent noisy pixels from dominating the risk gradient). The switch epoch is adapted per setup (20/20/40/60 for 1%/5%/10%/20% labeled data), and the loss's area-dependent denominator preserves spatial correlation exploitation while its sign-based numerator ensures noise-robust optimization up to 50% label corruption.

## Results

Using only 20% of fully pixel-annotated data (800 images) plus box annotations on the remaining 80%, PNet_F20 achieves F_β of 0.856, 0.861, 0.792, and 0.857 on CAMO, CHAMELEON, COD10K, and NC4K respectively, matching the fully supervised SOTA CamoFormer (F_β: 0.854, 0.880, 0.811, 0.868) within <1% while requiring only 1/5 the annotation effort. Even with just 1% fully labeled data (40 images), PNet_F1 surpasses all weakly supervised baselines by 16–80% across metrics. Adding 6,665 extra box-annotated images (PNet_F20†) further boosts COD10K F_β from 0.792 to 0.857 (+35%) and NC4K F_β from 0.857 to 0.888 (+3.6%), demonstrating scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against weakly supervised baselines (WSSA, SCWS, TEL, SCOD), PNet_F1 with only 40 fully labeled images outperforms the best WSCOD method SCWS by 79.6% in M, 16.3% in E_φ, 18.1% in F_β, and 16.0% in S_α on average, confirming that box prompts plus a small pixel-annotated subset dramatically outperform scribble, point, or box-only supervision. Against fully supervised SOTA methods, PNet_F20 matches CamoFormer within <1% on all four datasets (e.g., F_β 0.856 vs 0.854 on CAMO, 0.861 vs 0.880 on CHAMELEON) while using only 20% of the pixel annotations; it also surpasses FEDER, SINet, SINetv2, BSA-Net, BGNet, FSPNet, and MSCAF-Net on most metrics. PNet_F20 is slightly behind HitNet on CHAMELEON (F_β 0.861 vs 0.898) and COD10K (0.792 vs 0.823), but PNet_F20† closes or reverses these gaps (0.886 and 0.857 respectively). Against SAM with box prompts (SAM-B), PNet_F1 already achieves 2.4× higher F_β on CAMO (0.835 vs 0.346) and similar margins on other datasets. In the loss-function ablation, the proposed L_NC outperforms GCE [42] by 4% in F_β (0.792 vs 0.759) and CE+IoU by 1.2% (0.792 vs 0.780), validating its noise-correction design over existing robust losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing WSSCOD, a training protocol that repurposes box annotations as spatial prompts (drawing from weakly supervised segmentation and prompt-based methods like SAM) combined with a minimal set of pixel-level labels to generate pseudo labels for camouflaged object segmentation. The noise correction loss L_NC is positioned at the intersection of noisy-label learning (GCE, co-teaching) and segmentation loss design, but departs from prior work by explicitly modeling the two-phase learning dynamics (early learning vs. memorization) specific to pixel-level noise in every pseudo label, rather than sample-level noise in classification. The Frequency Transformer module adapts the DWT-based frequency decomposition from FDNet to a dual-branch architecture that fuses box-masked proposals with full-image features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving near-SOTA COD performance with only 20% (or even 1%) of pixel annotations dramatically reduces the ~60-minute-per-image labeling bottleneck, and the scalability demonstrated by PNet† (adding box-only data without new pixel labels) offers a clear path to further improvement at low cost. The universality of L_NC—improving both fully supervised SINetv2 and weakly supervised SCOD—suggests it could be adopted broadly in segmentation tasks where label noise is prevalent. Key limitations include the cumbersome two-stage training pipeline and sensitivity to box annotation quality, leaving open whether a single-stage or end-to-end formulation could achieve similar results, and whether more sophisticated cross-branch fusion could reduce reliance on precise box placement.
