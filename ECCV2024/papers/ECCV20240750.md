## Problem

Diffusion models produce high-quality text-to-image results but require dozens to hundreds of sampling steps, making them impractical for real-time or interactive use. Existing one-step distillation methods either produce blurry outputs via direct regression or rely on complex multi-stage training procedures. The paper identifies that the full potential of direct ODE distillation has been underexploited: with a carefully designed perceptual loss in latent space and a conditional GAN discriminator, a single-step generator can closely match multi-step teacher quality.

## Input / Output

The one-step generator takes a Gaussian noise latent (4×64×64 for SD 1.5, 4×128×128 for SDXL) and a text prompt as input, and outputs a single image latent in the same space as the teacher's VAE encoder. Crucially, the student and teacher share the same U-Net architecture, so the student is initialized from the teacher's weights. During training, the generator never decodes to pixel space; all losses (E-LatentLPIPS and the GAN loss) operate entirely in latent space, enabling a 4× larger batch size than pixel-space LPIPS.

## Method

The paper reframes diffusion distillation as a paired noise-to-image translation problem: it pre-computes millions of (noise, ODE-solution) pairs from the teacher's 50-step DDIM trajectory, then trains a conditional GAN to map noise+text to the target latent. The key regression loss, E-LatentLPIPS, is a VGG-based perceptual metric trained directly in the diffusion model's latent space (with max-pooling removed and 4-channel input), combined with random differentiable augmentations and cutout to eliminate the blind spots that cause wavy artifacts in plain LatentLPIPS. For adversarial training, the discriminator reuses the teacher's pre-trained U-Net weights, is extended with noise conditioning via a zero-initialized convolution, and employs a multi-scale in-and-out design that attaches three independent real/fake readout heads at each decoder scale. Additional stabilizers include single-sample R1 regularization (applied to one sample per batch every 16 steps to save memory) and mix-and-match augmentation that swaps latents, texts, or noises to enforce correct conditional alignment.

## Results

On COCO2014, Diffusion2GAN (distilled from Stable Diffusion 1.5) achieves FID-30k of 9.29, outperforming all one-step distillation baselines: DMD (11.49), UFOGen (12.78), and InstaFlow-0.9B (13.10), while approaching the multi-step teacher (8.74). On COCO2017, it reaches FID-5k of 19.5 and CLIP-5k of 0.311, slightly beating ADD-M (19.7) on FID but trailing on CLIP (0.326). For SDXL distillation, SDXL-Diffusion2GAN achieves FID-5k of 25.49 and CLIP-5k of 0.347, surpassing both SDXL-Turbo (28.10 / 0.342) and SDXL-Lightning (30.14 / 0.324) while matching the teacher's FID (25.56). All one-step models share the same 0.9B parameter count and ~0.09s inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diffusion2GAN clearly outperforms all one-step distillation baselines on COCO2014 FID: it beats DMD by 2.2 FID points (9.29 vs 11.49), UFOGen by 3.5 (9.29 vs 12.78), and InstaFlow-0.9B by 3.8 (9.29 vs 13.10). Against GigaGAN (the strongest one-step GAN baseline), Diffusion2GAN trails by 0.2 FID (9.29 vs 9.09) but is faster (0.09s vs 0.13s) and uses fewer parameters (0.9B vs 1.0B). On COCO2017, Diffusion2GAN edges out ADD-M/SDXL-Turbo on FID (19.5 vs 19.7) but loses on CLIP-score (0.311 vs 0.326), which the authors attribute to ADD-M's stronger text conditioning in its discriminator. For SDXL distillation, Diffusion2GAN dominates both SDXL-Turbo and SDXL-Lightning on FID (25.49 vs 28.10 and 30.14) and CLIP (0.347 vs 0.342 and 0.324), while also showing better ODE-trajectory preservation (DreamSim-5k of 0.284 vs 0.368 and 0.345). On CIFAR-10, ODE distillation with LPIPS (a component of Diffusion2GAN) achieves FID 3.16 with 1M pairs, beating Consistency Distillation's 3.67 at a fraction of the total compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes diffusion distillation (originally proposed by Luhman et al. as direct ODE regression) as a paired image-to-image translation problem, borrowing the conditional GAN framework from Pix2Pix and the perceptual loss philosophy from LPIPS. The key conceptual departure is moving the perceptual loss into the diffusion model's latent space (LatentLPIPS) and stabilizing it with random augmentation ensembling (E-LatentLPIPS), inspired by E-LPIPS. For the adversarial component, it repurposes a pre-trained diffusion U-Net as a multi-scale discriminator with noise conditioning, extending ideas from GigaGAN and concurrent diffusion-as-discriminator approaches (UFOGen, DMD, LADD). The overall 'disentangle correspondence-finding from mapping-learning' strategy is the paper's distinctive architectural choice relative to prior distillation methods that optimize a single end-to-end objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step text-to-image generation removes the primary latency bottleneck of diffusion models, enabling real-time interaction and opening the door to video and 3D applications that require thousands of forward passes. The E-LatentLPIPS technique is broadly applicable to any latent diffusion model and provides a ~10× speedup in perceptual loss computation, making it a practical tool beyond this specific distillation setting. The finding that a well-designed regression loss alone can approach the quality of more complex distillation objectives (consistency, progressive, distribution matching) suggests the field may have over-invested in elaborate distillation formulations. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger models (e.g., 4K resolution), whether the gap to the multi-step teacher can be further closed, and whether the framework transfers to video or 3D diffusion distillation.
