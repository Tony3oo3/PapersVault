## Problem

CLIP and similar contrastive vision-language models produce noisy, mis-segmented maps when adapted to open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, and prior work attributed this noise to self-attention layers without identifying its true architectural source. This paper asks where and how segmentation noise originates within CLIP's transformer blocks, revealing that the residual connection—not the attention mechanism—is the primary culprit, especially in larger backbones like ViT-L/14 where existing attention modifications actually degrade performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized so the shorter side is 448 px, processed via a sliding window) and a set of text class labels formatted into the prompt template 'a photo of a {label}.' No retraining, fine-tuning, or post-processing is required. The output is a dense per-patch segmentation map computed by argmax over cosine similarity between the modified visual patch features and the text encoder's class embeddings, evaluated with mIoU on eight benchmark datasets.

## Method

ClearCLIP decomposes the final ViT block output X_sum = X_res + X_attn and shows that X_res carries low-entropy, few-channel-dominated peak values that make local patch features nearly indistinguishable under cosine similarity, while X_attn retains high entropy and better spatial discriminability. The solution applies three modifications to the last transformer block: (1) removing the residual connection entirely, (2) replacing the standard q-k attention with self-self (q-q) attention to reorganize spatial information, and (3) discarding the feed-forward network whose output becomes unreliable once its input distribution shifts. The resulting visual feature is simply X_visual = Proj(Attn_qq · v) from the final block, requiring no additional parameters or training. Comparative entropy and norm analyses against DINO confirm that CLIP's image-level contrastive supervision is what concentrates energy into a few global channels in the residual path, explaining why the effect worsens with model depth.

## Results

On five datasets without a background class, ClearCLIP with ViT-B/16 achieves an average mIoU of 37.5, outperforming SCLIP (37.1) and TCL (33.1). With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, ClearCLIP reaches 34.5 mIoU average, substantially surpassing SCLIP (23.6) and MaskCLIP (13.7), which degrade significantly at this scale. On three datasets with a background class, ClearCLIP achieves 39.1 mIoU average, beating all weakly-supervised methods (best: TCL at 35.3) and SCLIP* (37.3), though SCLIP with class re-name tricks edges it out on VOC21 (59.1 vs. 51.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClearCLIP outperforms the strongest training-free baseline SCLIP by 0.4 mIoU on average (37.5 vs. 37.1) with ViT-B/16, winning on VOC20, Context59, Stuff, and ADE20K but losing on Cityscapes (30.0 vs. 32.2). Against the weakly-supervised TCL, ClearCLIP gains 4.4 mIoU on average across all five datasets. The advantage becomes dramatic with ViT-L/14: ClearCLIP achieves 34.5 mIoU while SCLIP drops to 23.6 and MaskCLIP to 13.7, confirming that prior attention-modification methods fail to scale. On datasets with a background class (Table 3), ClearCLIP beats TCL by 3.8 mIoU average and SCLIP* by 1.8, though SCLIP with its class re-name trick still leads on VOC21 (59.1 vs. 51.8) and overall average (40.0 vs. 39.1). Compared to the vanilla CLIP baseline, ClearCLIP improves average mIoU from 12.6 to 37.5 (ViT-B/16) and from 5.4 to 34.5 (ViT-L/14), representing roughly a 3× gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's ViT architecture and the line of training-free open-vocabulary segmentation methods that modify the final self-attention layer (MaskCLIP's identical attention, CLIPSurgery's value-value attention, SCLIP's query-query + key-key attention, GEM's generalized self-self attention). The key conceptual departure is reframing the problem: rather than treating attention modification as the solution, ClearCLIP identifies the residual connection as the primary noise source and explicitly removes it (along with the FFN) from the final layer, using self-self attention only to reorganize spatial information. The comparative analysis with DINO's self-supervised features provides a diagnostic lens that prior attention-modification works did not employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the residual connection as the dominant noise source in CLIP's dense predictions offers a clear architectural explanation for why prior attention-modification approaches fail at scale (ViT-L/14), where the residual norm grows substantially. The practical takeaway is a three-line modification (remove residual, use q-q attention, drop FFN) that is training-free and generalizes across CLIP and OpenCLIP backbones. Open questions include whether this decomposition insight extends to other dense tasks (detection, depth estimation) or to other VLM families, and whether the fundamental limitation of image-level contrastive training can be addressed without retraining.
