## Problem

Fully-supervised face forgery detectors tend to overfit to specific forgery methods or artifacts that are easily corrupted by perturbations, while existing self-supervised detectors impose strict auxiliary requirements such as audio-visual modalities or labeled pretraining datasets. The paper asks whether a general and robust representation can be learned from visual-only real face videos alone, without any fake videos or additional modalities, by exploiting the natural spatiotemporal consistency that forgery methods struggle to preserve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242231/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 20-frame face video clips at 224×224 resolution, with face extraction and alignment applied before processing; each video is sampled three times at random offsets and results are averaged. During self-supervised pretraining, only real videos (VoxCeleb2) are used with no fake samples or audio. At inference, a frozen CNN+Transformer backbone produces a 768-dim NACO representation that is passed through a two-layer MLP (256 hidden units, Softmax output) to yield a real/fake binary label.

## Method

NACO first extracts per-frame spatial features (256-dim) via a lightweight 3-layer CNN encoder, then feeds the resulting token sequence into a ViT-Base (12 blocks, 768-dim output) to capture long-range spatiotemporal dependencies, combining CNN local receptive fields with Transformer long-term memory. Two self-supervised tasks drive representation learning on real videos only: a Spatial Predictive Module (SPM) randomly masks 50% of spatial tokens and trains a CNN decoder to reconstruct them from the masked spatiotemporal representation (MSE loss), while a Temporal Contrastive Module (TCM) shuffles frame order to create negative pairs and applies a contrastive loss (temperature 0.5) that penalizes the model for treating temporally disrupted sequences as consistent. The total pretraining loss is a weighted sum (λ₁=1.0, λ₂=0.5) of SPM and TCM losses. For detection, the entire backbone is frozen and only the MLP head is fine-tuned with binary cross-entropy on labeled real/fake pairs, keeping just 4.7M trainable parameters.

## Results

On the primary generalization benchmark (train on FF++, test on four unseen datasets), NACO achieves 91.2% average video-level AUC, outperforming RealForensics (90.5%), FTCN (89.6%), and LipForensics (87.7%). On unseen manipulation generalization (leave-one-method-out on FF++), NACO reaches 99.7% average AUC, matching FTCN and exceeding RealForensics (99.0%) and AltFreezing (98.6%). Under seven perturbations, NACO suffers the smallest AUC drop (−3.7%) compared to RealForensics (−4.2%), AltFreezing (−5.7%), and FTCN (−9.9%), while requiring only 4.7M trainable parameters versus 21.4–36M for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NACO outperforms all 14 baselines on the average unseen-dataset AUC (91.2%), with the closest competitor being RealForensics at 90.5% (a 0.7-point margin) and FTCN at 89.6%. On the unseen-manipulation protocol (Table 2), NACO ties FTCN at 99.7% average AUC while exceeding RealForensics (99.0%) and AltFreezing (98.6%), and substantially outperforming frame-level methods like Xception (77.9%) and CNN-aug (72.9%). On robustness to perturbations (Table 4), NACO achieves the smallest average AUC drop (−3.7%), beating RealForensics (−4.2%), AltFreezing (−5.7%), and FTCN (−9.9%), with particularly large gains over FTCN on Gaussian noise (86.5 vs. 53.1) and compression (96.7 vs. 86.4). Notably, NACO requires only 4.7M trainable parameters during fine-tuning (just the MLP head), compared to 21.4M for RealForensics, 26.6M for FTCN, and 27.2M for AltFreezing, while matching or exceeding their detection accuracy. Frame-level detectors (Xception, CNN-aug, Patch-based, Face X-ray) and simple supervised video-level methods (CNN-GRU, Multi-task, DSP-FWA) are consistently outperformed by 10–20+ AUC points on unseen datasets, confirming that high-level spatiotemporal consistency is critical for generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by proposing a visual-only, self-supervised framework for face forgery video detection that departs from both fully-supervised detectors (which overfit to specific forgery artifacts) and prior self-supervised approaches like LipForensics (requiring labeled pretraining on mouth regions) and RealForensics (requiring audio-visual modalities). Architecturally, it repurposes the CNN-encoder + Transformer-decoder pattern—previously used in supervised video forgery detection (e.g., FTCN, ISTVT)—for self-supervised representation learning, adding two purpose-built auxiliary tasks (Spatial Predictive Module and Temporal Contrastive Module) that enforce sensitivity to spatiotemporal consistency rather than low-level artifacts. The conceptual lineage draws from masked autoencoding (MAE-style spatial prediction) and contrastive learning, but applies them jointly to the specific problem of preserving natural face-video consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-level natural spatiotemporal consistency is a more robust and generalizable detection signal than low-level artifacts, achieving the best average AUC (91.2%) on unseen datasets and the smallest perturbation drop (-3.7%) while fine-tuning only 4.7M parameters—far fewer than competitors (21–36M). This efficiency and generalization profile makes the approach attractive for practical deployment where new forgery methods emerge continuously. However, the evaluation remains limited to relatively controlled benchmark datasets; the authors themselves note the need to validate on more diverse real-world web videos and to extend to audio and multi-modal settings, leaving open whether the natural-consistency assumption holds under all manipulation paradigms (e.g., full video generation vs. face swap).
