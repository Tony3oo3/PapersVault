## Problem

Change captioning under distractors (viewpoint and illumination changes) is difficult because unchanged objects exhibit pseudo changes in scale and location, and overlapping objects weaken feature discrimination between the two images. Existing methods that directly subtract or compare image representations capture error-prone difference features, while recent contrastive-learning approaches (SCORER, SMART) only enforce non-perturbation of features but leave the discrimination-degraded problem unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241148/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of similar RGB images (a 'before' and an 'after' view of the same scene) and outputs a natural-language sentence describing the semantic change. Image features are extracted by a pre-trained ResNet-101 (1024×14×14 spatial grid), projected to 512 dimensions via 2D convolution with learnable positional encoding, and the caption is generated autoregressively by a transformer decoder using greedy decoding at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a Distractors-Immune Representation Learning (DIRL) module that, in a self-supervised manner, computes a channel correlation matrix between the two image representations and minimizes its ℓ₂-deviation from the identity matrix—correlating corresponding channels (non-perturbation) while decorrelating different channels (discrimination). Difference features are then obtained by using multi-head cross-attention to extract shared features from each image, subtracting them from the respective representation, and concatenating both per-image differences. A second contribution, Cross-modal Contrastive Regularization (CCR), reuses the attended difference features from the decoder's cross-attention layer, pools them to global vectors, and applies a bidirectional InfoNCE loss to maximize contrastive alignment between attended difference features and generated word embeddings. The full model is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining caption negative log-likelihood, the DIRL decorrelation loss, and the CCR contrastive loss, weighted by trade-off parameters tuned per dataset.

## Results

On Spot-the-Diff, DIRL+CCR achieves the best scores across all five metrics: BLEU-4 10.3, METEOR 13.8, ROUGE-L 32.8, CIDEr 40.9, and SPICE 19.9, surpassing the strongest prior methods SCORER+CBR and SMART by 8.2% and 4.7% on SPICE respectively. On CLEVR-Change (moderate distractors), it reaches BLEU-4 54.6, METEOR 38.1, ROUGE-L 71.9, CIDEr 123.6, and SPICE 31.8, leading on four of five metrics. On CLEVR-DC (drastic distractors), it obtains BLEU-4 51.4, ROUGE-L 66.3, CIDEr 84.1, and SPICE 16.8, with relative BLEU-4 improvements of 8.2%, 6.4%, and 4.0% over NCT, VARD-Trans, and SCORER+CBR. On Image Editing Request, it achieves BLEU-4 10.9 and ROUGE-L 41.0, the best among end-to-end methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIRL+CCR outperforms all 15 compared baselines on at least one metric across the four datasets. Against the two most recent and strongest competitors—SCORER+CBR [35] (ICCV 2023) and SMART [34] (TPAMI 2024)—it wins on every reported metric for Spot-the-Diff (e.g., SPICE 19.9 vs. 18.4 and 19.0) and on BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, and CIDEr for CLEVR-DC (51.4/66.3/84.1 vs. 49.4/66.1/83.7 for SCORER+CBR). On CLEVR-Change, it edges out SMART by 0.3 on BLEU-4 (54.6 vs. 54.3) and 0.7 on METEOR (38.1 vs. 37.4), while tying on CIDEr (123.6). Earlier subtraction-based methods (DUDA [24], DUDA+TIRG [9]) and similarity-based methods (M-VAM [26], MCCFormers-D [25], I3N [47]) are consistently surpassed by 3–10 points on BLEU-4 depending on the dataset. On Image Editing Request, DIRL+CCR beats VARD-Trans [31] by 0.9 on BLEU-4 (10.9 vs. 10.0) and 2.0 on ROUGE-L (41.0 vs. 39.0), and remains competitive with the pre-trained VIXEN [3] (BLEU-4 10.9 vs. 8.6 for VIXEN-CLIP). The ablation in Table 6 confirms that both DIRL and CCR contribute independently (BLEU-4 48.9 → 50.5 with DIRL alone, 49.3 with CCR alone, 51.4 with both) on the most challenging CLEVR-DC setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of prior work in change captioning: (1) methods that directly subtract or compute similarity between image representations to extract difference features (DUDA, VACC, MCCFormers-D, I3N), and (2) the most recent contrastive-learning approaches SCORER and SMART that enforce alignment between similar image pairs to handle distractors. DIRL departs from both by repurposing the Barlow Twins-style channel decorrelation objective [48] to simultaneously enforce non-perturbation (diagonal correlation) and discrimination (off-diagonal decorrelation) within a single image pair, rather than across a batch of similar/dissimilar pairs. The CCR module adapts the InfoNCE contrastive idea from single-image captioning [6,44] to a fine-grained, per-step alignment between attended difference features and generated words during decoding, rather than a coarse image-to-caption alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Change captioning has clear practical applications in surveillance reporting, medical image comparison, and accessibility, and the distractor problem (viewpoint and illumination shifts causing pseudo-changes) is a genuine bottleneck that limits real-world deployment. The paper's key insight—that non-perturbation and discrimination must be jointly enforced (neither alone suffices, per the ablation in Table 7)—provides a useful design principle for any paired-image understanding task. However, the evaluation is limited to relatively small or synthetic datasets (CLEVR scenes, 3.9K editing pairs), and the backbone remains a frozen ResNet-101, leaving open whether the channel-decorrelation strategy scales to high-resolution, multi-object real-world scenes or benefits from end-to-end backbone fine-tuning.
