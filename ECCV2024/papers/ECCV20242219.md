## Problem

Rendering photorealistic images from sparse, unevenly distributed point clouds remains challenging: graphics-based methods produce holes and bleeding, 2D-CNN restoration lacks 3D consistency, and NeRF-based approaches are computationally expensive. 3D Gaussian Splatting offers fast, high-quality rendering but requires multi-view images and per-scene optimization, making it inapplicable when only a point cloud is available. This paper bridges that gap by adapting 3DGS to the point-cloud-only setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242219/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a colored point cloud (3D coordinates and per-point RGB) of arbitrary sparsity (tested from ~50k to 120k points) along with camera intrinsics and poses, and produces a photorealistic rendered image at the target viewpoint. The point cloud need not be dense or uniformly distributed; the method is specifically designed to handle sparse and unevenly distributed points where traditional splatting would leave large voids.

## Method

PFGS first extracts multiscale neural descriptors per point via a PV-Convolution-based UNet that fuses features across downsampled scales. A point-wise regressor then predicts 3D Gaussian parameters (rotation quaternion, anisotropic scale, opacity) from these descriptors, and both the original colors and the neural features are rasterized onto the 2D image plane using standard Gaussian splatting with α-blending. The resulting image and feature maps are concatenated and fed into a multiscale recurrent UNet decoder that is run twice to progressively refine the prediction at three resolutions per loop. Training is two-stage: the Gaussian regressor is pretrained with L2 rendering loss for 8 epochs, then all modules are jointly optimized with a progressive multiscale L1 loss and an FFT-based frequency reconstruction loss that sharpens edges and high-frequency details.

## Results

PFGS achieves the best performance across all three datasets and all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). On ScanNet, it reaches 19.86 PSNR / 0.758 SSIM / 0.452 LPIPS; on DTU, 25.44 PSNR / 0.901 SSIM / 0.164 LPIPS; and on THuman2.0, 34.74 PSNR / 0.983 SSIM / 0.009 LPIPS. The largest gains over the strongest baseline (NPBG++) are +7.93 dB PSNR on THuman2.0 and +3.12 dB on DTU, while over the NeRF-based Trivol the advantage is most pronounced on DTU (+6.19 dB PSNR, +0.309 SSIM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFGS outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and metric. Against NPBG++ (the strongest learning-based baseline), PFGS gains +3.05 dB PSNR on ScanNet, +3.12 dB on DTU, and a substantial +7.93 dB on THuman2.0, with LPIPS reductions of 0.133, 0.163, and 0.053 respectively. Against the NeRF-based Trivol, the advantage is most dramatic on DTU where PFGS achieves 25.44 vs. 19.25 PSNR (+6.19 dB) and 0.901 vs. 0.592 SSIM, reflecting Trivol's difficulty with multi-class objects under sparse points; on ScanNet the gap is smaller (+1.30 dB PSNR) and on THuman2.0 PFGS leads by +8.77 dB PSNR. The paper attributes Trivol's weaker DTU performance to its fully implicit representation struggling across diverse object classes, while PFGS directly leverages point colors and geometry without class-specific assumptions. NPBG and NPBG++ are consistently the weakest learning-based baselines, with NPBG++ benefiting from multiview feature aggregation but still lacking 3D-consistent feature projection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: point cloud rendering (graphics-based, NPBG/NPBG++, and NeRF-based methods like Trivol and Point2Pix) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It repurposes 3DGS not as a per-scene optimization requiring multi-view images, but as a learned, point-wise parameterized projection mechanism: a regressor predicts Gaussian attributes from multiscale point features, and those attributes are used to splat both colors and neural descriptors onto 2D planes (following the feature-projection idea from Feature 3DGS). The departure from prior work is conceptual rather than architectural — prior point renderers either restore 2D images with CNNs (losing 3D consistency) or use NeRF volume rendering (sacrificing speed and point-cloud efficiency), whereas PFGS retains the explicit, efficient point representation while gaining 3D-consistent feature projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PFGS is the first point cloud renderer to incorporate 3DGS, offering a practical path to real-time, high-fidelity rendering from sparse points without per-scene optimization or multi-view supervision. The critical finding that joint training of all modules causes catastrophic collapse (PSNR 19.86 → 15.92) while two-stage training succeeds highlights an important practical constraint for future work combining learned Gaussian parameters with downstream decoders. The authors acknowledge that imprecise point clouds still yield unrealistic details, suggesting a generative refiner as a natural next step; more broadly, whether this Gaussian-splatting-as-projection paradigm can be extended to dynamic scenes or integrated with generative priors remains open.
