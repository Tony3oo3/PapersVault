## Problem

Existing event-based detection pipelines rely on fixed or rule-based sampling windows that are decoupled from the downstream detector, making the sampling stage non-differentiable and preventing end-to-end joint optimization of event representation and feature extraction. No prior work provides a learnable, adaptive sampling module that can be trained jointly with a spike-based detector to determine optimal per-pixel sampling times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241606/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous event stream E = {(x_i, y_i, p_i, t_i)} where each event carries spatial coordinates, binary polarity, and a microsecond-precision timestamp. A small early-aggregation window (Δt_m) converts the raw stream into T_m-step event-count tensors that serve as SNN inputs. The output is a set of bounding boxes with object categories (cars, pedestrians, two-wheelers) at the annotation timestamp, with the sampling module producing variable-length temporal windows per spatial-polarity position rather than a uniform global window.

## Method

The core module (ARSNN) assigns one LIF neuron per spatial-polarity position (x, y, p), each accumulating a convolved event-count input over time; when the membrane potential crosses threshold, events between the previous and current spike times are sampled as that neuron's local representation. Recurrent synaptic connections feed the previous spike back into both the input current and a learned sigmoid-gated decay factor, giving each neuron adaptive temporal memory. Two training techniques address degradation: Residual Potential Dropout discards all events after the last spike to prevent mode collapse toward non-firing, and Spike-Aware Training multiplies the aggregation by the firing spike to redirect surrogate gradients toward threshold-based firing-time updates. The entire pipeline—sampling, aggregation, and a PLIF/tdBN-based YOLOX SNN detector—is trained end-to-end via BPTT with surrogate gradients over only 3 time steps, achieving 5.85× lower energy than the ANN equivalent.

## Results

On the Gen1 dataset, EAS-SNN(M) achieves 0.731 mAP50 and 0.409 mAP50:95 with only 3 timesteps and 25.3M parameters, outperforming the best prior SNN method (EMS-ResNet34: 0.590 mAP50, 0.310 mAP50:95) by 14.1% in mAP50 and 9.9% in mAP50:95. The smaller EAS-SNN(S) variant (8.92M params) still reaches 0.687 mAP50 and 0.372 mAP50:95, surpassing all SNN baselines while using 38% fewer parameters than EMS-ResNet34. On 1Mpx, EAS-SNN(M) achieves 0.651 mAP50 and 0.362 mAP50:95, the first spike-based result on this dataset, exceeding the best ANN baseline (ASTMNet: 0.483 mAP50:95) by 17.9%. Energy consumption is reduced by 5.85× (YOLOX-M) and 3.73× (YOLOX-S) compared to their ANN counterparts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAS-SNN decisively outperforms all prior SNN-based detection methods on Gen1: the best SNN baseline EMS-ResNet34 [58] achieves only 0.590 mAP50 / 0.310 mAP50:95 with 5 timesteps and 14.4M params, while EAS-SNN(M) reaches 0.731 / 0.409 with just 3 timesteps. Against ANN-based methods, EAS-SNN(M) surpasses RVT [21] (0.472 mAP50:95), ERGO-12 [75] (0.504 mAP50:95), and ASTMNet [35] (0.467 mAP50:95) in mAP50:95, though ERGO-12's 0.504 mAP50:95 remains higher than EAS-SNN(M)'s 0.409 on that specific metric. On N-Caltech 101, EAS-SNN(M) achieves 0.664 mAP50 / 0.437 mAP50:95, beating ASYNet [44] (0.643 mAP50) and AEGNN [54] (0.595 mAP50). On 1Mpx, EAS-SNN(M) (0.651 mAP50 / 0.362 mAP50:95) outperforms all listed baselines including ASTMNet [35] (0.483 mAP50:95) and RVT [21] (0.474 mAP50:95). The energy analysis shows a 5.85× reduction in synaptic operations for the medium model and 3.73× for the small model relative to their ANN equivalents, confirming the efficiency advantage of spike-driven computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LIF neuron's spike-firing mechanism as a differentiable, pixel-wise adaptive sampling trigger for event streams, departing from prior rule-based (fixed-window) and search-based (ERGO-12, BEEF) sampling strategies that either truncate backpropagation or rely on pseudo-label feedback. It extends the SNN-based event detection line (EMS, TR-YOLO, VC-DenseNet) by making the sampling module itself a learnable recurrent convolutional SNN (ARSNN) with recurrent synaptic connections for temporal memory, rather than a fixed histogram aggregator. The training techniques RPD and SAT are novel contributions specific to stabilizing spike-driven sampling, addressing a degradation mode not encountered in standard SNN training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end learnable adaptive sampling framework for event-based detection, closing a gap where sampling was always decoupled from the detector. The 5.85× energy reduction over ANN counterparts and the demonstration that the adaptive sampling principle transfers to dense networks broaden its practical relevance beyond neuromorphic hardware. A key limitation is the reliance on early aggregation (Tm-step slicing), meaning the sampler cannot yet operate at raw microsecond event granularity; the authors explicitly frame this as the next frontier pending neuromorphic infrastructure advances. The synergistic (non-additive) interaction between RPD and SAT—where each alone degrades performance—suggests the training dynamics of spike-based samplers are more fragile than standard SNN training, an open question for the community.
