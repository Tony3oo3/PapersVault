## Problem

Existing visual prompt methods (e.g., RedCircle, blur masks) improve CLIP's zero-shot performance by editing the input image, but this inevitably destroys fine-grained visual information and can degrade accuracy on tasks requiring image fidelity. The paper asks whether the attention-guiding benefit of visual prompts can be achieved without altering the original image content, and seeks a principled understanding of why visual prompts work at all.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240256/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For referring expression comprehension, the method takes an RGB image, a text description, and a set of candidate bounding boxes (from a detector or ground-truth annotations), converting each box into a foveal attention mask in token space to score text-box similarity. For image classification, a single bounding box defines the region of attention. For 3D point cloud recognition, the point cloud is projected into six 2D depth maps, with foreground positions in each view converted into separate masks. The output is either the index of the best-matching box (REC) or a class label (classification/3D recognition).

## Method

FALIP inserts a foveal attention mask M directly into CLIP's multi-head self-attention computation as Softmax(QK^T/√d + M)V, leaving the input image untouched. The mask is a (N+1)×(N+1) matrix where only the first row (corresponding to the [CLS] token) is non-zero: tokens within the region of attention receive Gaussian-weighted coefficients (controlled by parameters σ for spatial spread and α for magnitude), while tokens outside receive zero. This reweights how the [CLS] token aggregates spatial information, mimicking human foveal attention's selective focus. The method is entirely train-free and plug-and-play, adding negligible computational cost. Additionally, the authors discover that attention heads in the last four layers of CLIP exhibit the largest response to visual prompts, and amplifying the change in these salient heads ('unleashing') further boosts performance by over 4% on REC.

## Results

On the primary referring expression comprehension task (gold boxes, without ensemble/post-processing), FALIP achieves 40.8% on RefCOCO Val, 44.5% on RefCOCO+ Val, and 51.3% on RefCOCOg Val, outperforming RedCircle (38.2/41.1/45.7) and PASTA (39.5/42.4/49.9). With ensemble and post-processing, FALIP reaches 49.8% (RefCOCO Val), 55.1% (RefCOCO+ Val), and 59.3% (RefCOCOg Val), surpassing RedCircle by ~3% across all splits. On image classification, FALIP improves over original CLIP on all four datasets (e.g., 67.3% vs. 64.9% Top-1 on ImageNet-S), while visual prompts like RedCircle and Blur degrade accuracy. For 3D point cloud recognition, FALIP lifts average accuracy from 15.6% to 17.0% over the PointCLIP baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FALIP consistently outperforms all visual-prompt baselines on the REC task. Against RedCircle [40] (the strongest visual-prompt method), FALIP gains +2.6% on RefCOCO Val, +3.4% on RefCOCO+ Val, and +5.6% on RefCOCOg Val under the gold/without-E-and-P setting, and maintains a ~3% lead with ensemble and post-processing. PASTA [61] is competitive on RefCOCOg (49.9 vs. 51.3) but trails FALIP by 1.3–3.4% on the other two datasets. CPT [56] (using VinVL) is the weakest baseline, lagging FALIP by 8–15 points across all splits. On image classification (Table 2), FALIP improves over original CLIP [35] by +1.8% (StanfordDogs), +0.1% (CUB), +2.4% (ImageNet-S), and +1.5% (Waterbirds), while RedCircle and Blur actually degrade accuracy by 2–15 points, confirming that image-altering prompts harm fine-grained classification. For 3D point cloud recognition, FALIP improves over the PointCLIP [62] baseline by +2.1% on ModelNet40 and +0.7% on ScanObjectNN.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALIP builds directly on CLIP's ViT image encoder and departs from the line of visual prompt methods (RedCircle, Blur, CPT, PASTA) that guide attention by editing the input image. Rather than altering pixel content, FALIP inserts a Gaussian-weighted foveal attention mask into the multi-head self-attention layer, drawing on the human foveal vision literature (Burt et al., Xia et al.) to reframe visual prompting as an internal attention manipulation. The paper also extends the analysis of attention heads in transformers by showing that specific heads in the last few layers are disproportionately sensitive to visual prompts, enabling a post-hoc 'unleash' amplification of their contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FALIP offers a train-free, plug-and-play mechanism to improve CLIP's region awareness without sacrificing input fidelity, which is practically important for fine-grained classification and high-fidelity applications where image editing is unacceptable. The empirical finding that RedCircle degrades zero-shot classification accuracy (e.g., CUB-200-2011 drops from 54.2 to 44.2) challenges the assumption that visual prompts are universally beneficial and highlights a task-specific trade-off. Open questions include whether the foveal mask design generalizes to other ViT variants or non-CLIP architectures, whether the 'unleash' head-amplification strategy can be made more principled, and whether the approach transfers to generative or multi-modal LLM settings.
