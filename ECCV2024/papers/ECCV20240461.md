## Problem

Supervised detectors of AI-generated images are tightly coupled to the specific generative architectures seen during training and fail to generalize to unseen models, making them impractical as new generators (DALL-E, Midjourney, SDXL, etc.) are released continuously. Few-shot and incremental methods reduce but do not eliminate this dependence. The paper asks whether a truly zero-shot detector—requiring no synthetic training data and no knowledge of any generator architecture—can achieve state-of-the-art detection performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of arbitrary size as input and internally produces four integer-valued resolution levels via successive 2×2 average pooling and rounding (the lowest being an 8×8 'prompt'). At each of the three higher levels, per-pixel negative log-likelihood (NLL) and entropy (H) maps are computed, then spatially averaged to yield scalar features NLL^(l) and H^(l) for l = 0, 1, 2. The final output is a binary real/synthetic label derived from one or more scalar decision statistics: the level-0 coding cost gap D^(0) = NLL^(0) − H^(0), its slope Δ^(01) = D^(0) − D^(1), and their absolute values, which together form the discriminative score.

## Method

ZED leverages the Super-Resolution based lossless Compressor (SReC), a multi-resolution CNN trained exclusively on real images to predict the conditional probability distribution of each pixel given a receptive-field context from the lower-resolution version. The key insight, borrowed from zero-shot LLM text detection, is to measure how 'surprising' a test image is under this real-image model: for real images the actual coding cost (NLL) closely matches the expected cost (entropy), while synthetic images deviate because their pixel statistics do not conform to the learned distribution. The most discriminative statistic is the full-resolution coding cost gap D^(0) = NLL^(0) − H^(0), which removes the bias of intrinsic image complexity (a crowd photo is harder to encode than a blue sky regardless of authenticity). The method requires no synthetic images, no generator-specific knowledge, and no fine-tuning; a single pre-trained SReC encoder (trained on Open Images, COCO, or LAION) serves as the universal real-image model, and detection reduces to thresholding the coding cost gap or its multi-resolution slope.

## Results

ZED achieves an average AUC of 90.0% across 30 test datasets (spanning 19 generative models) using the |Δ⁰¹| decision statistic, outperforming the best supervised baseline Corvi2023 (89.4%) and the CLIP-based Ojha2023 (88.4%). The method reports a +3.4% average improvement in balanced accuracy over the state of the art. Notably, ZED's AUC remains remarkably stable across all test sets (minimum 65.1% on GLIDE-R), and a single detection threshold yields near-optimal accuracy for all real-image domains simultaneously, unlike competing methods that require per-domain calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZED outperforms all 10 baselines in average AUC, with the closest competitor being Corvi2023 (89.4%) and Ojha2023 (88.4%). The advantage is most dramatic on newer diffusion-based generators: on DALL-E 3 (RAISE), ZED achieves 78.1% AUC while Wang2020 drops to 10.9% and Liu2022 to 0.2%; on Midjourney V5, ZED reaches 98.5% versus 40.2% for Wang2020. However, ZED's worst case (65.1% on GLIDE-R) is lower than Corvi2023's 92.2% on the same dataset, indicating that GLIDE's atypically low coding cost (negative D⁽⁰⁾) is not fully captured by the slope statistic. A key qualitative advantage is stability: ZED's AUC variance across the 30 test sets is far smaller than Corvi2023's, which exhibits scores ranging from 40% to 100% depending on the generator and real-image domain. Additionally, ZED is the only method where a single global threshold produces near-optimal balanced accuracy across all six real-image domains (LSUN, FFHQ, ImageNet, COCO, RAISE, LAION), whereas all supervised baselines require domain-specific threshold tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZED draws its conceptual inspiration from zero-shot machine-generated text detection methods (GLTR, DetectGPT, Binoculars), which measure how 'surprising' a text is under a language model's next-token distribution. The key departure is repurposing a lossless image compressor (SReC) — a tool designed for efficient encoding, not forensics — as an implicit one-class model of real images, eliminating the need for any synthetic training data. This contrasts with the dominant supervised paradigm (Wang2020, Corvi2023, Ojha2023) and even the closest training-free prior work (AEROBLADE), which relies on a specific latent diffusion autoencoder and only detects LDM-generated images. ZED thus carves a genuinely new methodological niche: using information-theoretic coding cost statistics from a multi-resolution lossless encoder as forensic features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As new generative models (DALL-E 3, Midjourney V5, SDXL, Adobe Firefly) appear continuously, the inability to retrain supervised detectors for each new architecture is a critical practical bottleneck; ZED's zero-shot design directly addresses this by requiring only real images for training. The finding that a single coding-cost-gap feature with one universal threshold achieves near-optimal accuracy across all 30 test datasets is practically valuable for deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that the method's reliance on an accurate real-image model makes it vulnerable to common web impairments (heavy JPEG compression, resizing), and extending robustness to in-the-wild conditions remains an open challenge. The unexpected GLIDE anomaly — where synthetic images exhibit lower-than-expected coding cost rather than higher — suggests the statistical signature of synthetic images is more heterogeneous than initially assumed, pointing to richer feature design opportunities.
