## Problem

Existing open-set semi-supervised learning (OSSL) methods rely on maximum softmax confidence for ID/OOD classification and ad-hoc, manually set thresholds that do not adapt to problem difficulty or the evolving state of the model. This leads to inaccurate and over- or under-confident ID/OOD decisions that degrade both closed-set accuracy and OOD detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241623/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of labeled RGB images (e.g., 1,000-10,000 labels) and a larger unlabeled set that may contain unknown OOD classes, at standard resolutions (32x32 for CIFAR, 64x64 for Tiny ImageNet, 224x224 for ImageNet). Beyond the usual class prediction, ProSub outputs a calibrated ID/OOD probability per sample derived from a Beta-distribution mixture model, along with a continuous subspace score in [0,1] that measures angular proximity to the ID subspace. The probabilistic output enables stochastic sampling of binary ID/OOD masks rather than a single hard threshold.

## Method

ProSub introduces a subspace score computed as the cosine of the angle between a sample's penultimate-layer feature and an ID subspace spanned by EMA-tracked class means, leveraging the neural collapse phenomenon. A batch version of the iterated method of moments (IMM) estimates the conditional Beta distributions of this score given ID or OOD data at every training step, enabling Bayes-rule probabilistic ID/OOD predictions without a fixed threshold. A subspace loss then uses stochastically sampled binary masks (drawn from the predicted probabilities) to push ID scores toward 1 and OOD scores toward 0, while a cosine-based self-supervision loss from DoubleMatch provides a learning signal from all unlabeled data. Pseudo-labeling follows the FixMatch confidence-threshold scheme but is additionally gated by the sampled ID mask, preventing OOD samples from receiving class labels.

## Results

ProSub achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA results across all seven benchmark settings. On CIFAR-100 as ID (2,500 labels) with CIFAR-10 as OOD, ProSub reaches 74.16% closed-set accuracy and 0.97 AUROC, outperforming the next best method (IOMatch) by 5.3% accuracy and 0.41 AUROC. On ImageNet50/50, ProSub achieves 71.15% accuracy and 0.96 AUROC, a 12.9% accuracy and 0.14 AUROC improvement over OpenMatch. On Tiny ImageNet 100/100, ProSub reaches 60.92% accuracy and 0.72 AUROC, the best among all compared OSSL methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProSub outperforms all five OSSL baselines (MTCF, T2T, OpenMatch, IOMatch, SeFOSS) on both closed-set accuracy and AUROC in 5 out of 7 settings. The most notable margins are on CIFAR-100-as-ID settings, where ProSub beats IOMatch by 5.3% accuracy and 0.41 AUROC (2,500 labels), and on ImageNet50/50, where it surpasses OpenMatch by 12.9% accuracy and 0.14 AUROC. OpenMatch is the only method that beats ProSub, doing so on both CIFAR-10-as-ID settings (94.82% vs 94.50% accuracy and 0.96 vs 0.93 AUROC at 4,000 labels), which the authors attribute to the cosine-based self-supervision being less effective for CIFAR-10. ProSub also surpasses the closed-set SSL baseline FixMatch on accuracy in most settings while dramatically improving AUROC (e.g., 0.97 vs 0.46 on CIFAR-100/CIFAR-10 2,500 labels). Against SeFOSS, the closest OSSL competitor, ProSub wins on every setting by 0.5–12.9% accuracy and 0.01–0.16 AUROC.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProSub builds on the neural collapse phenomenon (Papyan et al. 2020) to define an ID subspace spanned by class means, and on the pseudo-labeling and cosine-based self-supervision frameworks of FixMatch and DoubleMatch. It departs from concurrent subspace-based OOD detection methods (Vim, Neco) by computing the ID subspace cheaply from EMA-updated class means rather than PCA over the full training set, making it tractable for the iterative OSSL training loop. The key methodological departure from prior OSSL work is replacing ad-hoc or Otsu-based binary thresholds with a probabilistic model: the authors fit Beta distributions to the subspace score via a batch iterated method of moments (IMM) algorithm, yielding calibrated ID/OOD posteriors rather than hard decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic ID/OOD framework addresses a practical gap in OSSL where fixed or Otsu thresholds fail to adapt to the evolving model during training, and the ablation showing Otsu yields 0.67 AUROC versus 0.97 for the probabilistic approach quantifies this gap. Limitations include the reliance on the neural collapse assumption for the subspace score, the need to know the ID proportion π, and per-dataset tuning of the self-supervision weight. Open questions include whether the Beta distribution assumption generalizes to very high-dimensional feature spaces or extreme class counts, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or multi-modal OSSL settings.
