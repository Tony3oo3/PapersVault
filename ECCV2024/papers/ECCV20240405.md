## Problem

mmWave radar point clouds are inherently sparse and noisy due to limited spatial resolution, multi-path ghost points, and specular reflection, causing existing HPE methods to suffer from miss-detection of body parts and signal inconsistency that manifest as pose vibration and drift. No prior work has applied diffusion-based generative models to handle these specific noise characteristics of mmWave radar for pose estimation, leaving a gap between the denoising capability of diffusion models and the practical challenges of RF-vision sensing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240405/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time frame, the method consumes a 6-dimensional mmWave radar point cloud R_t ∈ R^{N×6} (Cartesian coordinates plus velocity, energy, and amplitude per point) where N is variable and can be as low as <100 points on mm-Fi, along with a sequence of Δt=8 historically estimated coarse poses for temporal conditioning. The output is a 17-joint 3D human pose H_t ∈ R^{17×3}; during inference, 5 diffusion hypotheses are generated and averaged to produce a single deterministic pose for fair comparison with non-diffusion baselines.

## Method

mmDiff is a conditional DDPM (K=25 steps, constant β=0.001) with a GraFormer GCN backbone that progressively denoises a pose from pure noise, guided by four radar-derived condition embeddings injected additively before each GCN block. The Global Radar Context module uses a transformer with 17 learnable joint feature templates (analogous to ViT cls tokens) to extract per-joint features from anchor-based PC features, isolating each joint's representation so that miss-detected parts do not corrupt detected ones. The Local Radar Context module performs point-level self-attention over KNN-selected local PCs around dynamic anchors taken from intermediate diffusion poses (rather than static coarse estimates), yielding higher-resolution spatial guidance. The Structural Limb-Length Consistency module decodes 16 limb-lengths from the global joint features as a physical size constraint, and the Temporal Motion Consistency module applies a 1D convolution over GCN-encoded historical poses to extract smooth motion trends. Training is two-phase: phase 1 jointly trains the PC encoder, GRC, and a pose decoder with L2 joint regression loss; phase 2 freezes phase-1 parameters and trains the diffusion model plus the remaining three modules with the standard DDPM noise-prediction loss plus an L1 limb-length regression loss (λ=5).

## Results

On the mmBody dataset, mmDiff(G,L,T,S) achieves 68.08 mm MPJPE and 53.71 mm PA-MPJPE on average across all scenes, outperforming the SOTA mmWave method P4Transformer (78.10 / 60.56) by 12.8% and 11.3% respectively. Under adverse environments (rain, smoke, dark, occlusion), the improvement widens to 14.7% (MPJPE) and 12.0% (PA-MPJPE). On the sparser mm-Fi dataset, mmDiff(G,S,T) reduces MPJPE by 6.29%–13.61% and PA-MPJPE by 7.15%–14.42% over the PointTransformer benchmark across random, cross-subject, and cross-environment splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mmDiff outperforms all six baselines on both datasets. Against the SOTA mmWave method P4Transformer [12], mmDiff reduces average MPJPE from 78.10 to 68.08 mm (−12.8%) and PA-MPJPE from 60.56 to 53.71 mm (−11.3%) on mmBody, with even larger gains (14.7% / 12.0%) in adverse scenes where radar noise is most severe. Compared to the camera-based PoseFormer [50] (72.87 / 56.07) and the diffusion-based DiffPose [14] (73.31 / 58.23), mmDiff still leads by 6.6% (MPJPE) and 4.2% (PA-MPJPE), confirming that the radar-specific conditioning modules are essential beyond generic pose refinement. On mm-Fi, mmDiff(G,S,T) beats the PointTransformer [47] benchmark by 6.29%–13.61% (MPJPE) and 7.15%–14.42% (PA-MPJPE) across all three data splits, and improves over DiffPose [14] by 4.19%–11.14% (MPJPE) and 4.49%–11.40% (PA-MPJPE). The MM-Mesh [43] baseline (94.13 / 70.79) is the weakest mmWave method, trailing mmDiff by over 26 mm in MPJPE. Notably, mmDiff's cross-subject performance on mm-Fi is comparable to its random-split performance, suggesting the learned structural and motion priors generalize to unseen subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM generative framework (Ho et al., 2020) from image generation to 3D human pose estimation, building on the diffusion-based HPE line initiated by DiffPose (Gong et al., 2023) and Diff3DHPE (Zhou et al., 2023). However, it departs fundamentally from those works by addressing the noisy, sparse, and occasionally incomplete input modality of mmWave radar point clouds, where prior diffusion-based HPE assumed stable and informative sources (2D poses or RGB images). On the mmWave HPE side, it replaces the direct-regression paradigm of P4Transformer, PointTransformer, and mmMesh with a conditional generative model, introducing four purpose-built modules (global/local radar context, limb-length consistency, temporal motion consistency) that have no direct analogue in prior radar-based pose estimators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first diffusion-based paradigm for mmWave radar HPE, mmDiff demonstrates that generative denoising can meaningfully outperform direct regression on inherently noisy RF-vision data, opening a path toward privacy-preserving, camera-free pose estimation in adverse environments (smoke, darkness, occlusion). The modular conditioning design—separating global context, local point-level features, structural priors, and temporal motion—provides a reusable template for injecting domain knowledge into diffusion models handling other imperfect sensor modalities. Key limitations remain: performance is still inferior to RGB-D under ideal conditions, multi-target scenarios degrade accuracy, and the two-phase training pipeline adds engineering complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-person estimation, real-time edge deployment, and integration with complementary RF sensors (Wi-Fi, UWB).
