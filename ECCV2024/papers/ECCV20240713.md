## Problem

Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting applied to thermal infrared images produces floaters and indistinct edge features because it ignores the physical imaging effects unique to thermal radiation—specifically, viewpoint-dependent atmospheric transmission attenuation and inter-object thermal conduction that blurs boundaries. Additionally, no dedicated large-scale dataset existed to benchmark thermal infrared novel-view synthesis methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240713/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view thermal infrared images at 480×720 resolution (captured by a Zenmuse XT uncooled vanadium oxide microbolometer in the 7.5–13.5 μm band) along with camera poses estimated via COLMAP, sampled at 3–4 fps from ~90-second video sequences. It outputs a rendered novel-view thermal infrared image at the same resolution, evaluated against held-out test views (every 8th frame) using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

## Method

Thermal3D-GS extends 3D-GS with two physics-informed modules and a corner-aware loss. The Atmospheric Transmission Field (ATF) is an 8-layer MLP (hidden dim 256) that takes positional-encoded Gaussian positions and normalized frame indices to predict per-Gaussian attenuation coefficients (μ_abs, μ_sca, d) following the Bouguer-Lambert-Beer law, which are then applied multiplicatively to the spherical harmonic coefficients to decouple geometry from viewpoint-dependent attenuation. The Thermal Conduction Module (TCM) is a 3-layer CNN that extracts second-order spatial gradients of the rendered image and fuses them with the original via residual addition, simulating the 2D heat equation (∂u/∂t = αΔu) to sharpen blurred edges caused by inter-object thermal conduction. A discontinuous loss weighted by normalized Harris corner response (with a 5000-iteration decay schedule) penalizes corner artifacts, and the total loss combines this with D-SSIM and L1 terms (λ_dis = λ = 0.2). Training runs 30,000 iterations with Adam, using exponential learning-rate decay for the ATF (8×10⁻⁴ → 1.6×10⁻⁶) while the TCM shares the 3D-GS learning rate.

## Results

On the TI-NSD dataset (20 scenes: 7 indoor, 7 outdoor, 6 UAV), Thermal3D-GS at 30K iterations achieves an average PSNR of 35.04 dB, SSIM of 0.955, and LPIPS of 0.187, outperforming the 3D-GS baseline (32.01 dB PSNR) by 3.03 dB. The method also surpasses InstantNGP-Big (24.91 dB) and Plenoxels (23.28 dB) by over 10 dB in PSNR. At 7K iterations, Thermal3D-GS achieves 31.44 dB PSNR, still competitive with 3D-GS-30k (32.01 dB) while using fewer training iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Thermal3D-GS decisively outperforms all three baselines across every metric. Against the primary baseline 3D-GS-30k, the method gains 3.03 dB in PSNR (35.04 vs. 32.01), 0.019 in SSIM (0.955 vs. 0.936), and reduces LPIPS from 0.206 to 0.187, with the largest per-category gains in outdoor scenes (+3.71 dB PSNR). Against InstantNGP (both Base and Big configurations), Thermal3D-GS leads by over 10 dB in PSNR, reflecting the fundamental advantage of explicit 3D Gaussian representation over hash-grid-based NeRF variants for thermal imagery. Against Plenoxels, the gap is even wider at ~11.8 dB PSNR, and the paper notes Plenoxels produces severe floaters unsuitable for thermal infrared. Notably, at only 7K iterations Thermal3D-GS (31.44 dB) slightly trails 3D-GS-30k (32.01 dB) in PSNR but already exceeds it in SSIM and LPIPS, suggesting faster convergence to perceptually better results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) by Kerbl et al. with two physics-informed modules—an Atmospheric Transmission Field (ATF) modeling the Bouguer-Lambert-Beer attenuation law and a Thermal Conduction Module (TCM) simulating 2D Fourier heat diffusion—specifically tailored to thermal infrared imaging. It departs from the standard 3D-GS assumption that view-dependent appearance is fully captured by spherical harmonics, arguing that thermal infrared radiance is additionally modulated by distance-dependent atmospheric absorption/scattering and inter-object heat transfer. The discontinuous loss, derived from Harris corner response, is a novel regularization that penalizes learned geometric artifacts at object boundaries, complementing the D-SSIM and L1 losses used in prior 3D-GS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of TI-NSD, the first large-scale thermal infrared novel-view synthesis benchmark (20 scenes, 6,664 frames spanning indoor, outdoor, and UAV settings), fills a critical gap that previously prevented systematic evaluation in this modality. The physics-informed formulation offers a principled path for handling domain-specific imaging artifacts (floaters from atmospheric attenuation, edge blurring from thermal conduction) that generic radiance-field methods cannot address, potentially informing similar adaptations for other sensor modalities with known physical imaging models. Limitations include reliance on a single self-collected dataset, simplified physical assumptions (uniform attenuation per Gaussian, 2D conduction only), and the discontinuous loss's inconsistent effect on SSIM, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes or multi-spectral thermal imaging.
