## Problem

Generating a talking avatar from pure text—where one text describes the avatar's appearance and another specifies what it should say—remains unsolved because no large-scale paired text-and-talking-avatar dataset exists to train data-driven models. Existing audio-driven or video-driven talking head methods require reference images, audio, or extensive paired training data, and 2D approaches struggle with view consistency under large head poses due to limited 3D information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242364/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two free-form text inputs: a description of the avatar's appearance (e.g., 'a happy young female with long blond hair') and the content the avatar should speak out, which is converted to audio via TTS and then to 3DMM expression coefficients via a pre-trained audio-to-expression model. The output is a temporally consistent talking avatar video at EG3D resolution where the face matches the description and lip movements align with the spoken content. The entire pipeline is zero-shot at inference time; the only trained component (InpaintNet) requires less than 5 hours on two 3090 GPUs with no paired data.

## Method

The approach integrates three pre-trained components—EG3D (3D-aware triplane GAN), CLIP (vision-language alignment), and 3DMM (parametric face model)—in a zero-shot pipeline. First, a latent code in EG3D's triplane space is optimized by minimizing CLIP distance between the rendered front-view image and the text description, yielding a pose-controllable 3D avatar. Second, the speech text is converted to 3DMM expression and pose coefficients, and a 3D motion flow is computed between source and target 3DMM meshes, then projected to a 2D warp field. Third, the EG3D latent feature map is warped by this 2D flow, and a novel InpaintNet (a residual CNN taking five consecutive warped frames) denoises the result to produce temporally consistent features for the EG3D decoder. InpaintNet is trained self-supervised via a double-warping strategy that synthesizes paired warped/clean data, with losses combining L2 reconstruction, LPIPS perceptual, SyncNet lip-sync, and CLIP consistency terms.

## Results

On the proposed text-driven talking avatar generation task, the method achieves the best FID (187.006), highest identity preservation CSIM (0.843), and lowest expression distance AED (0.492) among all compared methods. Lip synchronization (SyncNet score) reaches 3.907, which is competitive but below SadTalker (5.769) and DreamTalk (5.676). In the user study, the method scores highest on Image Fidelity (3.78/5) and Image Realness (3.38/5), while Lip Synchronization MOS (3.44) is slightly below SadTalker (3.58). Notably, the method requires no paired training data and trains in under 5 hours on 2 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SadTalker (audio-driven), the proposed method wins on FID (187.0 vs 196.7), CSIM (0.843 vs 0.831), and AED (0.492 vs 0.510), but trails on SyncNet score (3.907 vs 5.769), indicating SadTalker's dedicated lip-sync training gives it an edge in audio-visual alignment. Against DreamTalk (audio-driven), the method dominates on all image-quality metrics—FID (187.0 vs 200.9), CSIM (0.843 vs 0.027, a dramatic gap), and AED (0.492 vs 0.500)—though DreamTalk still leads on Sync (5.676 vs 3.907). Against OTAvatar (expression-driven), the method wins on every metric: FID (187.0 vs 193.3), CSIM (0.843 vs 0.571), AED (0.492 vs 0.506), and Sync (3.907 vs 2.880). In the user study, the method achieves the highest Image Fidelity (3.78) and Image Realness (3.38) MOS scores, surpassing SadTalker (3.48, 3.23) and DreamTalk (2.88, 2.71), while its Lip Synchronization MOS (3.44) is marginally below SadTalker (3.58) and DreamTalk (3.51). Overall, the method trades a modest lip-sync advantage for substantially better visual quality, identity preservation, and expression fidelity, all without any paired training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on EG3D's 3D-aware triplane latent space for high-fidelity face synthesis and repurposes it as a frozen backbone for a new animation pipeline, rather than training a new generator from scratch. It departs from prior talking-head methods (SADTalker, DreamTalk, OTAvatar) that require large paired audio-video or expression-video datasets by instead using CLIP for zero-shot text-to-appearance alignment and 3DMM for 3D motion-flow computation, eliminating all paired training data. The self-supervised InpaintNet, trained via a double-warping reconstruction strategy, is a novel component that bridges the gap between 3DMM-driven warping and EG3D's decoder, a combination not present in prior 3D avatar or talking-head literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a genuinely new task formulation—text-driven talking avatar generation—that unifies avatar creation and animation under a single text interface, with a practical zero-shot pipeline trainable in under 5 hours on two consumer GPUs. This lowers the barrier to personalized talking avatar creation and opens a path toward full-body text-driven digital humans. Key limitations remain: the 3DMM model used lacks eye-region control, the approach is restricted to the face, and the quality ceiling is bounded by EG3D's training distribution. Open questions include whether more expressive 3DMM variants or learned 3D representations can close the gap to photorealistic full-body animation, and whether the self-supervised inpainting strategy generalizes beyond the specific warping artifacts it was designed to fix.
