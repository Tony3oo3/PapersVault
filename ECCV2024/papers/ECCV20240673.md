## Problem

Scene Graph Generation suffers from a severe long-tailed relationship distribution, causing models to predominantly predict coarse-grained head relationships (e.g., 'on', 'has') while missing informative fine-grained tail ones (e.g., 'walking on', 'part of'). Existing debiasing methods such as DLFE and RTPB apply a single dataset-level correction vector uniformly to all object pairs, ignoring the distinct contextual characteristics of each pair. This paper introduces sample-level bias correction that predicts a dedicated correction vector per object pair to recover fine-grained relationships.

## Input / Output

The method operates within a standard two-stage SGG pipeline: a pre-trained Faster R-CNN (ResNeXt-101-FPN) detects objects, and a classic SGG model (Motif, VCtree, or Transformer) produces initial relationship logits. The BGAN generator then consumes three inputs per object pair — the union-region visual feature f_uni (extracted via a shared-weight feature extractor and fused with spatial features), the dataset-level global bias b^glo, and the original prediction vector z — to output a sample-specific bias vector of dimension M (number of relationship categories). The corrected logits z_hat = z + b_s are passed through softmax to yield the final fine-grained relationship assignment for each (subject, predicate, object) triplet.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bias-Oriented Generative Adversarial Network (BGAN) that learns to predict per-sample correction biases. A correction bias set S is first constructed by computing the margin needed to flip each sample's predicted label to its ground truth, with a small epsilon added to ensure strict inequality. The generator (5-layer 1D convolutional network) maps [f_uni, b^glo, z] to a predicted bias b^pre, while the discriminator (3-layer 1D convolutional network) distinguishes b^pre from the constructed b^tru. Training alternates 5 discriminator steps per generator step (Algorithm 1), with an additional cross-entropy loss on the corrected predictions to anchor the generator. The classic SGG model is trained first with beta=0, then frozen while BGAN trains with beta=1; at inference, the frozen SGG model and the BGAN generator jointly produce fine-grained scene graphs. This sample-sensitive approach contrasts with dataset-level methods that apply one fixed correction to all pairs, and the ablation shows union-region features outperform whole-image context for bias prediction.

## Results

On the VG dataset, SBG achieves the best A@50/100 across all three base SGG models (Motif, VCtree, Transformer) and all three tasks (PredCls, SGCls, SGDet). For example, on Transformer PredCls, SBG reaches A@50/100 = 44.6/46.7, outperforming the best baseline FPGL (43.6/47.4) while maintaining a much higher R@100 (57.6 vs. 54.6). Compared to dataset-level correction methods (DLFE, RTPB), SBG shows average A@K improvements of 5.6%, 3.9%, and 3.2% on PredCls, SGCls, and SGDet respectively. On the larger VG-1800 dataset with 1,800 relationship categories, SBG achieves F-Acc(Top-1) of 13.45 vs. 5.33 for CFA and 1.21 for the base Motif model, demonstrating that gains scale with dataset size and long-tail severity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBG outperforms all 17 compared baselines on A@50/100 across all three base models (Motif, VCtree, Transformer) on VG, with the largest margins over dataset-level correction methods DLFE and RTPB (e.g., +2.6 A@100 on Motif PredCls vs. DLFE's −1.8). Against the strongest individual baselines, SBG edges out Inf (43.8/45.4) on VCtree PredCls by 0.2/0.5 A@100, and beats FPGL (43.6/47.4) on Transformer PredCls on A@50 (44.6 vs. 43.6) while FPGL slightly leads on A@100 (47.4 vs. 46.7). A key differentiator is the balance: SBG achieves the smallest R@100 drop (−9.9 on Motif PredCls) while gaining +15.1 on mR@100, whereas RTPB drops R@100 by −24.6 and DLFE by −12.9. On GQA, SBG leads on Transformer PredCls A@100 (51.5 vs. CFA's 50.0 and RTPB's 49.1) but shows only marginal gains over CFA on Motif (48.7 vs. 48.6), attributed to GQA's smaller scale and weaker long-tail effect. On VG-1800, SBG's advantage is dramatic: F-Acc(Top-1) of 13.45 vs. CFA's 5.33, confirming that sample-level correction scales better with increasing category count. Efficiency overhead is minimal: only +0.3–0.5% parameters and +1.2–2.2% inference speed vs. RTPB/DLFE, while training time is actually 45% shorter than DLFE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the bias-correction framework established by DLFE (Chiou et al., 2021) and RTPB (Chen et al., 2022), which recover unbiased relationship predictions by applying a single dataset-level correction vector to all object pairs. The key departure is shifting from this sample-insensitive, dataset-level correction to a sample-level approach that predicts a unique bias vector per object pair using the union-region visual context. Architecturally, the authors introduce a Bias-Oriented GAN (BGAN) with one-dimensional convolutions to generate these sample-specific biases, departing from the simpler frequency-based or resistance-based corrections of prior work. The method is designed as a plug-and-play post-hoc correction module compatible with existing two-stage SGG backbones (Motif, VCtree, Transformer) and one-stage methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sample-level bias correction offers a more principled solution to the long-tailed relationship problem than dataset-level methods, and its consistent gains across three SGG architectures and three datasets (VG, GQA, VG-1800) demonstrate practical utility. The extension to one-stage SGG methods and even object detection on COCO suggests the bias-prediction paradigm could generalize beyond SGG. However, the approach remains a post-hoc correction that depends on a pre-trained SGG model, and the GAN-based generator adds training complexity; whether simpler regression heads could achieve comparable sample-specific correction remains an open question. The paper also leaves open how the method behaves under even more extreme long-tail distributions or with very large relationship vocabularies beyond the 50–1800 categories tested.
