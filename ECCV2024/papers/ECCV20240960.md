## Problem

Multi-sensor extrinsic calibration for self-driving vehicles currently relies on expensive controlled infrastructure (checkerboards, turntables, indoor facilities) and is difficult to scale across large fleets. Existing targetless methods typically address only a single sensor pair or modality and suffer from noisy correspondences, leaving no unified, high-accuracy approach for calibrating full multi-LiDAR, multi-camera platforms from ordinary outdoor driving data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240960/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short outdoor driving trajectory (e.g., figure-8 loops) of synchronized camera images and LiDAR sweeps, together with pre-computed vehicle IMU poses and fixed sensor intrinsics. It outputs the 6-DoF extrinsic matrix for every sensor relative to the vehicle frame, jointly with a learned implicit scene representation (multi-resolution hash grid + MLP producing SDF and appearance features). No calibration targets, structured scenes, or manual annotation are required; the only assumption is that localization provides the ego-vehicle trajectory.

## Method

UniCal jointly optimizes all sensor extrinsics and an implicit neural scene representation (Instant-NGP-style hash grid queried by an MLP yielding SDF and view-dependent features) through differentiable volume rendering of both camera RGB and LiDAR depth/intensity. The key calibration-specific contribution is a surface alignment loss: sparse cross-camera correspondences (via LightGlue) are ray-cast into the learned implicit surface, and the reprojection error of each 3D point into the other camera's image provides a dense geometric constraint that disambiguates camera poses without explicit depth. A coarse-to-fine ray sampling schedule progressively concentrates supervision on SuperPoint-detected interest regions to improve pose gradients in textureless areas. The full objective combines photometric (RGB + LiDAR intensity), geometric (LiDAR depth + surface alignment), and regularization (Eikonal constraint + weight concentration) losses, all optimized end-to-end in a self-supervised manner.

## Results

On the MS-Cal dataset, UniCal achieves the best LiDAR-Camera re-projection error (9.27 px), camera rotation error (0.186°), LiDAR rotation error (0.036°), and LiDAR translation error (0.008 m) across all baselines. On the PandaSet urban driving dataset, UniCal reduces camera rotation error to 0.267° and LiDAR rotation error to 0.048°, substantially outperforming SC-NeRF (1.127°/—) and INF (0.767°/0.136°). UniCal also achieves the best rendering quality with PSNR 31.96, SSIM 0.903, LPIPS 0.344, and LiDAR depth error 0.035 m on MS-Cal, and PSNR 25.14, SSIM 0.727, LPIPS 0.450 on PandaSet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCal outperforms all classical target-based methods: it achieves a lower LiDAR-Camera re-projection error (9.27 px) than Edge and Plane (9.83 px) and Pose Graph Optimization (11.14 px), while also delivering the best camera rotation accuracy (0.186° vs. 0.358° and 0.438°). Against the pairwise LiDAR-LiDAR baselines, Point-to-Plane ICP achieves a marginally lower registration error (2.811 cm vs. 2.847 cm), but ICP only handles a single pair and cannot produce a globally consistent multi-sensor calibration; UniCal's full-system LiDAR-LiDAR error (2.847 cm) is competitive while simultaneously calibrating all 5 LiDARs and 8 cameras. UniCal dramatically outperforms the NeRF-based baselines: compared to INF, it reduces LiDAR-Camera re-projection error from 47.27 to 9.27 px, camera rotation from 0.645° to 0.186°, and LiDAR rotation from 0.368° to 0.036°; compared to SC-NeRF (camera-only), it reduces camera rotation from 0.544° to 0.186° and adds LiDAR calibration entirely. On PandaSet, UniCal achieves 0.267° camera rotation and 0.048° LiDAR rotation, beating INF (0.767°/0.136°) and SC-NeRF (1.127°/—) by 2–4×, and also improves rendering PSNR from 23.64 (INF) to 25.14.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCal sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) targetless multi-sensor calibration, which has historically focused on single sensor pairs (LiDAR-camera or LiDAR-LiDAR) using feature matching, mutual information, or motion alignment; (2) neural rendering pose optimization (SC-NeRF, BARF, MOISST, SOAC), which refines camera or multi-sensor poses within a differentiable rendering loop; and (3) SDF-based implicit scene representations with multi-resolution hash encoding (Instant NGP, NeuS). The paper departs from prior neural-rendering calibration work (MOISST, SOAC) by introducing a surface alignment loss that explicitly enforces geometric consistency across camera views via sparse feature correspondences ray-cast onto the learned implicit surface, and by employing a coarse-to-fine interest-point-guided ray sampling schedule. Unlike classical target-based pipelines that decompose calibration into pairwise stages followed by PGO, UniCal jointly optimizes all sensor extrinsics in a single differentiable objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the autonomous driving industry: eliminating the need for expensive indoor calibration facilities, turntables, and fiducial infrastructure enables a 'drive-and-calibrate' workflow that scales to large vehicle fleets across multiple cities. The method's assumption that intrinsics and vehicle trajectory are known (via localization) is standard in SDV deployments but limits generality; jointly learning intrinsics, localization, and additional sensor modalities (radar, ultrasonic) remains open. The surface alignment loss addresses the shape-radiance ambiguity that plagues unregularized neural rendering in unstructured outdoor scenes, and its ablation (0.267° vs. 0.856° camera rotation error) confirms it is the critical component for camera calibration accuracy. A remaining question is robustness under highly dynamic scenes, occlusions, or degraded sensor conditions where sparse correspondences may be unreliable.
