## Problem

Current text-to-3D generation methods produce results that poorly align with human preferences in terms of text-3D correspondence, overall quality, and multi-view consistency. While RLHF has proven effective for aligning language models and text-to-image diffusion models, no prior work has applied human preference feedback to text-to-3D generation, leaving a gap in both preference-aware 3D evaluation and preference-guided 3D optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241873/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DreamFL pipeline takes a single text prompt and produces a NeRF representation optimized over multi-view rendered images (4 views spanning 360°) from the MVDream backbone. The Reward3D model takes a text prompt, a rendered multi-view image, and a camera parameter as input, outputting a scalar preference score via an ImageReward-based architecture with cross-attention and an MLP head. The training dataset comprises 2530 prompts (selected from Cap3D via graph-based clustering) each paired with 4–10 generated 3D assets, yielding 25,304 comparison pairs annotated on a 1–6 scale across alignment, quality, and consistency.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, Reward3D is trained with a Bradley-Terry ranking loss over 25k expert comparisons, using ImageReward as a frozen-partial backbone (some transformer layers frozen to prevent overfitting) with cross-attention between image and text features feeding an MLP scalar head. Second, DreamFL derives a modified SDS gradient by approximating the ideal preference-aligned noise ε_φ^r as the existing diffusion noise ε_φ minus a reward-gradient correction term λ·∂r(y, x̂_t, c)/∂x, yielding the composite loss L_Reward ≈ L_SDS − λ_r·r(y, x̂_t, c). This avoids fine-tuning the diffusion model entirely; instead, the reward signal directly steers the NeRF optimization toward higher-preference regions. The weighting λ_r is dynamically scaled to match SDS magnitude, and a final 200-step reward-only fine-tuning phase (with SDS deactivated) further refines aesthetics.

## Results

On 110 GPTEval3D prompts, DreamReward achieves the top rank across all four evaluation metrics: GPTEval3D score 1480 (vs. 1224 for MVDream, the next best), Reward3D score 2.594 (vs. 0.246 for MVDream), ImageReward score 1.833 (vs. −0.350 for Latent-NeRF), and 478 human-eval wins (vs. 375 for MVDream). In the user study (30 participants, 20 prompts, 1–6 scale), DreamReward scores 4.88 on alignment, 5.03 on quality, and 5.30 on multi-view consistency, yielding the highest average of 5.07. Reward3D achieves a Spearman ρ of 1.00 with human rankings, matching GPTEval3D and substantially outperforming ImageReward (0.80) and CLIP (0.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamReward outperforms all five baselines on every primary metric. The largest gaps are against DreamFusion (GPTEval3D 1480 vs. 1000; Reward3D 2.594 vs. −1.597) and Fantasia3D (GPTEval3D 1480 vs. 1006; Reward3D 2.594 vs. −1.582), which suffer from multi-face artifacts and poor text alignment. Against the strongest baseline MVDream, DreamReward leads by 256 GPTEval3D points (1480 vs. 1224) and by 2.35 Reward3D points (2.594 vs. 0.246), while MVDream retains a marginal edge on multi-view consistency in the user study (5.38 vs. 5.30). In the user study, DreamReward is preferred by 65% of raters overall and scores highest on both alignment (4.88 vs. 3.88 for MVDream) and quality (5.03 vs. 4.40). The ablation on a DreamFusion backbone confirms that DreamFL improves even weaker backbones, suggesting the reward-guided optimization is backbone-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the RLHF pipeline (preference collection → reward model training → reward-guided optimization) from NLP (InstructGPT) and text-to-image generation (ImageReward/ReFL, DiffusionDPO, DPOK) into the text-to-3D domain for the first time. On the generation side, it builds directly on Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion) and its multi-view extension MVDream, but departs from the ReFL-style approach of fine-tuning the 2D diffusion model; instead, DreamFL injects a reward gradient term directly into the SDS loss, avoiding costly diffusion-model retraining. The Reward3D model itself is initialized from ImageReward's backbone but retrained on 3D-specific multi-view preference data, giving it 3D-awareness that 2D reward models (ImageReward, CLIP) lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DreamReward establishes a practical, lightweight alternative to GPT-4V-based evaluators (GPTEval3D) for scoring text-to-3D outputs, which is valuable given the cost and latency of LLM-based evaluation. The DreamFL technique—adding a single reward-gradient correction to SDS—is simple enough to be adopted across existing SDS-based pipelines, as the ablation on DreamFusion confirms. However, the framework is currently tied to NeRF representations and a relatively small annotated set (2,530 prompts), limiting diversity; the authors themselves flag scaling to larger datasets and richer camera/orientation conditioning as key next steps. Whether this RLHF-for-3D paradigm remains relevant as the field shifts toward feed-forward 3D-native generators (e.g., Shap-E, Point-E) is an open question.
