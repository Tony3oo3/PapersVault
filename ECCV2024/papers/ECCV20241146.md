## Problem

Score distillation methods for text-to-3D generation (e.g., SDS, ISD, CDS) still produce assets with limited detail and low fidelity, yet the root causes have not been systematically identified. This paper connects consistency distillation theory to score distillation to pinpoint three specific deficiencies: inherent PF-ODE distillation errors, ineffective conditional guidance under classifier-free guidance, and the absence of pixel-domain constraints that prevent out-of-distribution artifacts. A separate over-saturation issue in 3DGS-based rendering, caused by brightness accumulation across training epochs, further degrades output quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting asset as output. The 3D Gaussians are initialized from Shap-E and Point-E point clouds, then optimized for 5000 epochs (~1 hour on an A800 GPU) using Stable Diffusion 2.1 as the frozen 2D prior. Rendering resolution and camera sampling follow LucidDreamer conventions, and the final asset is evaluated via multi-view renderings (120 views across 30 prompts) for CLIP score, FID, and user preference.

## Method

Guided Consistency Sampling (GCS) reformulates the score distillation objective into three complementary losses. The Compact Consistency (CC) loss enforces self-consistency along the PF-ODE trajectory using a DPM-Solver-based solution function G_θ, achieving a tighter error bound O((Δt)^p)(T−e) than prior CDS. The Conditional Guidance (CG) loss aligns the unconditional denoising trajectory from a midpoint e with the text-conditional trajectory from a higher timestep t, applying CFG in only one step to avoid accumulated error while providing precise text-conditional direction. The Constraint on Pixel (CP) domain loss re-computes the CG objective in pixel space via the VAE decoder to suppress out-of-distribution color artifacts. A separate Brightness-equalized Generation (BEG) scheme monitors the 85th-percentile brightness of rendered views each epoch and multiplicatively resets Gaussian brightness to 0.8× when it exceeds a threshold, preventing the progressive over-saturation observed in 3DGS optimization.

## Results

On 120 rendered views across 30 prompts from the DreamFusion gallery, GCS achieves the best FID score of 103.40 (lower is better), outperforming LucidDreamer (109.57) and ProlificDreamer (119.53). The CLIP score is 32.37, slightly below ProlificDreamer's 33.17 but above all other baselines. In the user study (30 volunteers), GCS dominates all three preference questions—brightness (46.32%), prompt alignment (44.21%), and fidelity (44.21%)—nearly doubling the next-best method (LucidDreamer at 24–31%). Notably, GCS achieves these results with a CFG weight of only 7.5, compared to 100+ for DreamFusion and ProlificDreamer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCS achieves the best FID (103.40) across all five baselines, indicating the closest match to the prior distribution and thus the lowest distillation error. On CLIP score, ProlificDreamer (33.17) edges out GCS (32.37) by 0.8 points, but GCS surpasses all other methods including LucidDreamer (31.30) and DreamFusion (30.68). In the user study, GCS wins decisively on all three subjective criteria—brightness, prompt alignment, and fidelity—scoring roughly 44–46% preference versus 24–31% for the runner-up LucidDreamer, and far ahead of DreamFusion (~6%) and DreamGaussian (~1–2%). Compared to LucidDreamer, which uses the same 3DGS backbone and similar CFG weight (7.5), GCS improves FID by ~6 points and more than doubles user preference scores, attributing gains to the CC loss (reduced distillation error), CG loss (better conditional guidance), and BEG (brightness equalization). Against ProlificDreamer, which requires fine-tuning the diffusion model and uses CFG weight 100+, GCS matches prompt alignment while achieving substantially better FID and user-perceived quality at a much lower CFG weight.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the optimization objectives of score distillation (SDS, VSD, ISD, CDS) under a common 'cross-consistency' framework by drawing an explicit theoretical connection to consistency distillation (CM), which enforces 'self-consistency' along PF-ODE trajectories. Building on the PF-ODE-based distillation line (LucidDreamer, Consistent3D) and the 3DGS representation (GaussianDreamer, DreamGaussian), the authors identify three specific deficiencies in current PF-ODE score distillation—distillation error bounds, ineffective CFG integration, and missing pixel-domain constraints—and address each with a corresponding component in GCS. The Brightness-equalized Generation (BEG) module is a practical addition specific to 3DGS rendering, orthogonal to the distillation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CM-to-SDS connection provides a principled lens for diagnosing and improving score distillation, potentially guiding future work beyond the specific GCS formulation. The BEG technique, while simple, addresses a persistent and visually prominent artifact (over-saturation) that affects all 3DGS-based text-to-3D pipelines, making it immediately adoptable. Limitations include reliance on a frozen 2D diffusion prior (Stable Diffusion 2.1), single-object generation, and the added computational cost of the pixel-domain constraint (CP). Open questions include whether the consistency-based framework extends to multi-object or scene-level generation, and whether BEG generalizes to other 3D representations like NeRF.
