## Problem

While MLLMs have shown strong visual understanding, their potential as flexible, interpretable, and text-driven IQA models remains largely unexplored. Prior work focused on constructing new IQA datasets with human quality descriptions to benchmark MLLMs, but overlooked that established human-rated IQA datasets are better suited for evaluating perceptual accuracy, and that the interaction between psychophysical testing procedures and NLP prompting strategies has not been systematically studied.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241974/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MLLM receives one or more RGB images (a single test image, a pair for double-stimulus comparison, or a set of L images for multiple-stimulus ranking) along with a structured text prompt that specifies the task, the visual attribute to assess, and optionally exemplar images with human quality scores for in-context prompting. In the FR scenario, a reference image is always supplied alongside the distorted image(s). The model outputs a scalar quality score in [0,100], a pairwise preference (with a 'comparable' option), a listwise ranking, or free-form descriptive text, depending on the prompting system used.

## Method

The paper constructs nine prompting systems by crossing three psychophysical testing procedures (single-stimulus, double-stimulus, multiple-stimulus) with three NLP prompting strategies (standard, in-context, chain-of-thought), then evaluates four MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.6, mPLUG-Owl2, InternLM-XComposer2-VL, and GPT-4V) across seven IQA datasets in both FR and NR settings. To make evaluation tractable given costly MLLM inference, a greedy difficult-sample selection procedure is proposed that iteratively picks images maximizing variance-normalized MSE between expert IQA model predictions and human scores, while enforcing diversity via CLIP feature-space distance and preferring samples with low human rating variance. Pairwise rankings from double- and multiple-stimulus methods are aggregated using maximum a posteriori estimation under Thurstone's Case V model. The key finding is that optimal prompting differs across MLLMs: open-source models perform best with single-stimulus standard prompting due to their inability to process multiple images, while GPT-4V benefits most from double-stimulus chain-of-thought prompting, which decomposes IQA into sub-tasks examining structure, texture, color, and luminance.

## Results

On uniformly sampled images, GPT-4V with double-stimulus chain-of-thought prompting achieves the highest SRCC across FR datasets: 0.818 on FR-KADID, 0.830 on Aug-KADID, and 0.786 on TQD. On the proposed difficult-sample subset, GPT-4V's FR performance drops to 0.669 (FR-KADID), 0.708 (Aug-KADID), 0.786 (TQD), and collapses to 0.122 on SPCD (color differences). In the NR scenario on difficult data, GPT-4V reaches 0.845 SRCC on SPAQ, surpassing both MUSIQ (0.769) and LIQE (0.781), but lags behind LIQE on NR-KADID (0.513 vs. 0.866). All three open-source MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.6, mPLUG-Owl2, InternLM-XC2-VL) remain below 0.5 SRCC on most FR datasets regardless of prompting strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the FR scenario on difficult samples, GPT-4V (0.669 SRCC) is competitive with FSIM (0.704) on FR-KADID and slightly edges out DISTS (0.708 vs. 0.701) on Aug-KADID, but is clearly outperformed by DISTS on TQD (0.786 vs. 0.911) and all MLLMs fail on SPCD color assessment (GPT-4V: 0.122 vs. DISTS: 0.454). In the NR scenario, GPT-4V surpasses both MUSIQ (0.845 vs. 0.769) and LIQE (0.845 vs. 0.781) on SPAQ, demonstrating strong generalization to authentic camera distortions, yet falls well short of LIQE on NR-KADID (0.513 vs. 0.866) and AGIQA-3K (0.783 vs. 0.703, where it actually leads). Q-Instruct [54], a quality-instruction-tuned LLaVA variant, consistently outperforms the base LLaVA-v1.6 across all NR datasets (e.g., 0.799 vs. 0.317 on SPAQ), confirming the benefit of targeted visual instruction tuning. All three open-source MLLMs remain far below expert IQA models in the FR setting, with SRCC values generally below 0.35, indicating they lack the multi-image comparison capability needed for reference-based assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prior MLLM-IQA line of work (DepictQA, Q-Bench, Q-Instruct) that focused on constructing new datasets with human quality descriptions or fine-tuning models, instead taking a step back to systematically study prompting strategies by crossing three psychophysical testing procedures (single-, double-, and multiple-stimulus) with three NLP prompting strategies (standard, in-context, chain-of-thought). It repurposes the Thurstone model for ranking aggregation from psychophysics and introduces a variance-normalized difficult sample selection procedure that uses expert IQA models as black-box proxies. The paper explicitly argues that prior benchmarking efforts overlooked the nontrivial problem of how to properly instruct, screen, and aggregate human quality descriptions, and that established human-rated IQA datasets are more suitable for evaluating the perceptual aspect of MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that different MLLMs require fundamentally different optimal prompting systems challenges recent claims that instruction-tuned open-source models have surpassed GPT-4V-level IQA, since those comparisons did not pair GPT-4V with its own optimal prompting configuration. The paper reveals that even GPT-4V struggles with fine-grained color discrimination and multi-image quality comparison—tasks humans perform effortlessly—highlighting substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether automatic prompt optimization can close the gap, how to extend difficult sample selection to unlabeled data, and whether continual learning or parameter-efficient tuning can improve IQA without catastrophic forgetting of general visual reasoning abilities.
