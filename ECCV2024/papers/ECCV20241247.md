## Problem

Training temporal action segmentation (TAS) models on long untrimmed videos requires dense framewise annotations that are extremely costly to collect. Existing semi-supervised and weakly-supervised approaches still demand labeling entire videos or all videos in the dataset, and no prior work has addressed the problem of efficiently selecting which videos and which frames to annotate. This paper is the first to study active learning for TAS, targeting both inter-video and intra-video selection to minimize annotation effort.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241247/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted I3D clip features (32 consecutive frames per clip, or 10 for the shorter GTEA dataset) from untrimmed videos, along with a small set of labeled frames (as low as 0.16% of total frames in main experiments). Output is frame-level action labels that partition each video into non-overlapping segments. Annotation is performed at the clip level by labeling the middle frame of each selected clip, and the framework iteratively grows the labeled pool across active learning rounds until a budget or target performance is met.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages per active learning iteration. First, a TAS model (ASFormer backbone) is jointly trained with learnable action prototypes using a regularized contrastive loss (L_cont + L_mu + L_sigma) that enforces discriminative prototype separation while maintaining balanced inter-prototype distances across iterations. For inter-video selection (VPA), each labeled video is compressed into a short sequence of action prototypes, and unlabeled videos are scored by their minimum drop-DTW alignment cost to these prototype sequences; videos with the highest cost (most dissimilar action orderings) are selected. For intra-video selection (VAS), a video-aligned summarization problem is formulated as finding a clip subset whose one-to-many DTW alignment with the full video is minimized, solved via a greedy algorithm that iteratively adds the clip yielding the greatest alignment improvement. This prototype-based compression reduces alignment complexity from video-length to action-segment-length, making the selection tractable.

## Results

With only 0.16% of frames labeled (0.5% for GTEA), the proposed two-stage active learning framework achieves 57.8% accuracy and 45.0 edit distance on 50Salads, 47.6% accuracy and 57.0 edit on GTEA, 63.5% accuracy and 58.6 edit on Breakfast, and 73.9% accuracy and 35.5 edit on CrossTask. This corresponds to 61.5%, 69.1%, 80.1%, and 77.2% of fully-supervised edit distance performance, respectively. The method improves f1@50 by 8.2%, 1.8%, 4.1%, and 1.2% over the best baseline on 50Salads, GTEA, Breakfast, and CrossTask. On Breakfast, with 0.23% labeled frames the method surpasses 90% of full-supervision f1@10, and reaches 100% at 0.47%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four intra-video selection baselines (split-random, split-entropy, equidistant from [87], and coreset from [122]) across all four datasets on every metric. The largest margin is on 50Salads, where f1@50 improves by 8.2% over equidistant (32.9 vs. 27.8) and 19.1% over coreset (32.9 vs. 13.8); on CrossTask the gap is smaller (1.2% over split-entropy on f1@50) due to the high background ratio (72%). Against weakly-supervised timestamp methods on Breakfast, the method achieves 54.3 f1@50 with 0.23% budget, surpassing D-TSTAS [69] (50.7 f1@50 at 0.29% budget) by 3.6% and EM-Gen/SkipTag [85] (45.2 f1@50 at 0.29%) by 9.1%, while using less annotation. Against transcript and action-set methods (TASL [73], POC [74], MuCon [106]), the method achieves 63.5 MoF and 39.2 IoU on Breakfast (vs. 48.5/40.9 for MuCon) and 73.9 MoF and 23.7 IoU on CrossTask (vs. 42.8/15.6 for POC), with only 0.16% of frames labeled. The coreset baseline [122] is consistently the weakest, confirming that spatial diversity alone is insufficient without temporal structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sequence alignment literature (DTW, drop-DTW) into a novel video-aligned summarization problem where the summary must be jointly selected and aligned, and adapts drop-DTW to a one-to-many matching regime for efficient video-prototype alignment. It departs from existing active learning work, which has focused on single-label sample classification, by formulating a two-stage selection problem (inter-video and intra-video) that accounts for the sequential, multi-label nature of TAS. The contrastive action prototype learning module builds on SimCLR-style contrastive losses but introduces regularization terms specifically motivated by the incremental nature of active learning, where prototypes are added over iterations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first work to study active learning for TAS, this paper opens a practical research direction for reducing the prohibitively expensive framewise annotation of long untrimmed videos. The finding that 0.16% frame budget achieves roughly 70–80% of fully-supervised edit distance, and that the method matches or exceeds weakly-supervised baselines (which require watching entire videos), suggests a viable path toward near-fully-supervised performance at a fraction of the annotation cost. Key open questions include whether the framework scales to very large video pools, how to handle the growing labeled dataset over iterations (the authors note this as a limitation), and whether pseudo-label noise can be better controlled in semi-supervised extensions.
