## Problem

Existing implicit neural representations (INRs) map individual pixel coordinates through a shared MLP, ignoring the rich semantic structure inherent in multi-dimensional data. This paper asks whether a data representation can be designed under the INR framework that explicitly exploits the semantic information within and across spatially coherent regions of the data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes spatial coordinates of data points (images at 256×256 or 512×768, 3D point clouds, or gridded weather data) and first segments them into K generalized superpixels via a custom clustering algorithm (GSSA) that enforces both feature similarity and spatial connectivity. Each superpixel's coordinates are then independently processed by its own MLP, and the per-superpixel outputs are aggregated into a single recovered data matrix. The framework is unsupervised, requiring only the observed (possibly incomplete or noisy) data without any external training set.

## Method

S-INR replaces the single shared MLP of standard INR with K exclusive attention-based MLPs—one per generalized superpixel—each augmented with Squeeze-and-Excitation-style channel attention after every layer to capture feature-dimension correlations within that superpixel. A shared learnable dictionary matrix D (of size s×r) is applied to the output of every superpixel MLP, enabling cross-superpixel commonality to be learned in a low-rank subspace. The generalized superpixels themselves are found by GSSA, a k-means++-initialized clustering that jointly minimizes feature distance and spatial-coordinate distance (weighted by α) to guarantee spatially connected blobs. Training minimizes a task-specific L2 loss (reconstruction, masked completion, or denoising) over all superpixels using Adam, with the sinusoidal activation inherited from SIREN providing high-frequency expressiveness.

## Results

On image reconstruction, S-INR achieves 36.08 dB PSNR / 0.965 SSIM on Kodim (512×768×3) and 39.10 dB / 0.949 on Pavia (256×256×32), outperforming the best INR baseline WIRE by ~2.9 dB and ~0.05 SSIM respectively. On image denoising (Lehavim, σ=0.15), S-INR reaches 33.70 dB PSNR / 0.867 SSIM, surpassing WIRE (33.20 dB / 0.863). On point data tasks, S-INR achieves 0.058 NRMSE / 0.900 R² on weather data completion (63°N,157°W) and 0.074 NRMSE / 0.944 R² on 3D surface completion (Scene1), consistently outperforming all regression and INR baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-INR consistently outperforms all four INR baselines (Fourier/ReLU+Pos.Enc, Gauss, SIREN, WIRE) across every image task. On image reconstruction (Kodim), S-INR gains ~2.9 dB PSNR over WIRE (36.08 vs. 33.20) and ~3.0 dB over SIREN (33.05); on image denoising (Lehavim, σ=0.2), it leads WIRE by 2.27 dB (32.39 vs. 30.12). S-INR also beats DIP (30.15 dB) by ~6 dB on Kodim reconstruction. For point data, S-INR reduces NRMSE by 19–25% relative to the best regression baseline (KNR) on weather completion and by 31–35% on 3D surface completion, while improving R² by 0.05–0.07 over KNR and 0.06–0.07 over RF. SIREN, the strongest single INR baseline, is outperformed by S-INR on all six point-data sub-tasks, confirming that the superpixel structure and attention/dictionary modules provide gains beyond what a standard sinusoidal-activation MLP can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SIREN implicit neural representation framework (sinusoidal-activation MLP mapping coordinates to values) but departs from the standard pixel-level paradigm by introducing generalized superpixels as the fundamental unit of representation. The exclusive attention-based MLPs draw on squeeze-and-excitation channel attention, while the shared dictionary matrix borrows the concept of a common codebook from dictionary learning, repurposed here as a learnable cross-superpixel interaction layer. The GSSA segmentation algorithm extends k-means++ with a spatial connectivity constraint, generalizing the image-specific superpixel notion to arbitrary point data (3D surfaces, weather grids).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core idea—that INRs can benefit from grouping coordinates into semantically coherent units rather than treating each point independently—opens a direction for incorporating structural priors into coordinate-based representations. However, the method introduces a non-trivial segmentation preprocessing step (GSSA) with its own hyperparameters (K, α), and the gains, while consistent across five tasks, are moderate (roughly 1–3 dB over SIREN). Open questions include how the approach scales to very large point clouds, whether the segmentation quality is a bottleneck, and whether the shared dictionary matrix can be replaced by more principled cross-unit interaction mechanisms. The unsupervised, single-image training paradigm limits direct comparison with supervised deep learning baselines.
