## Problem

Discrete pixel-wise segmentation methods are spatially inflexible and introduce discretization artifacts when scaling to arbitrary resolutions, while existing implicit (continuous) segmentation methods suffer from limited domain transfer due to weak pre-trained encoders and ignore frequency-domain information critical for precise boundary delineation in medical images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D medical image (RGB colonoscopy or CT slice) at a source resolution (e.g., 384×384 or 512×512) along with a coarse bounding box prompt, and produces a segmentation mask at an arbitrary target resolution by interpolating encoded features to match normalized coordinates in [-1,1]. Unlike discrete methods that lock output resolution to input resolution, I-MedSAM can generate predictions at 128×128, 384×384, or 896×896 from a single model without retraining.

## Method

I-MedSAM freezes SAM's ViT-B image encoder and adds two lightweight adapters: a Frequency Adapter (FA) that extracts DFT amplitude-spectrum features to capture high-frequency boundary information, and LoRA (rank-4) applied to the query and value projection layers of multi-head attention. A two-stage coarse-to-fine INR decoder then maps concatenated positional-encoded coordinates, interpolated image features, and prompt features to class probabilities: a shallow Dec_c produces coarse predictions and intermediate features, while a deeper Dec_f refines only the Top-12.5% highest-uncertainty points selected via MC-Dropout variance estimation (Uncertainty-Guided Sampling). The final mask merges coarse predictions on low-uncertainty pixels with refined predictions on high-uncertainty pixels, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with only 1.6M trainable parameters using a combined cross-entropy and Dice loss.

## Results

On binary polyp segmentation (Kvasir-Sessile), I-MedSAM achieves 91.49±0.52% Dice with only 1.6M trainable parameters, outperforming the best discrete method nnUNet (82.97%, 126.6M params) by 8.52 points and the best implicit method SwIPE (85.05%, 2.7M params) by 6.44 points. On multi-class organ segmentation (BCV), I-MedSAM reaches 89.91±0.68% Dice with 3.5M params, surpassing MedSAM (85.85%, 52.7M params) by 4.06 points and SwIPE (81.21%, 4.4M params) by 8.70 points. Cross-domain evaluation shows I-MedSAM maintaining 88.83% Dice on CVC-ClinicDB (vs. 84.91% for nnUNet) and 86.28% on AMOS (vs. 82.81% for SwIPE), while cross-resolution tests (384→128 and 384→896) yield 91.45% and 91.33% respectively, far exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-MedSAM outperforms all nine baselines on both primary tasks. Against discrete methods, it beats nnUNet by 8.52 Dice points on polyp segmentation (91.49 vs. 82.97) while using only 1.26% of nnUNet's trainable parameters (1.6M vs. 126.6M); it also surpasses MedSAM (82.88%) and PraNet (82.56%) by roughly 8–9 points. Among implicit methods, I-MedSAM leads SwIPE (85.05%) by 6.44 points on polyp segmentation and by 8.70 points on organ segmentation (89.91 vs. 81.21), and exceeds IOSNet (78.37%) by 13.12 points. In cross-domain transfer (Kvasir-Sessile→CVC), I-MedSAM achieves 88.83% Dice, outperforming the best discrete baseline nnUNet (84.91%) and the best implicit baseline SwIPE (70.10%) by 3.92 and 18.73 points respectively. In cross-resolution settings (384→128 and 384→896), I-MedSAM maintains near-constant performance (91.45% and 91.33%), whereas discrete methods like PraNet* drop to 43.92% at 896×896 and even SwIPE only reaches 84.33%. On boundary quality (Hausdorff Distance), I-MedSAM achieves 11.59 on Kvasir-Sessile, substantially lower than nnUNet (31.30), MedSAM (21.53), and IOSNet (51.72), confirming superior boundary delineation.

## Relation to Prior Work

I-MedSAM sits at the intersection of two research threads: SAM-based medical segmentation (MedSAM, SAMed) and implicit neural representation (INR) segmentation (OSSNet, IOSNet, SwIPE). It departs from prior SAM-based methods by replacing their discrete pixel-wise decoders with a continuous INR decoder, and from prior INR methods by replacing their weaker backbones (Res2Net-50, small CNNs) with SAM's ViT-B encoder. The two novel architectural contributions—a DFT amplitude-spectrum frequency adapter for PEFT and an MC-Dropout-based uncertainty-guided sampling strategy for coarse-to-fine INR decoding—are not present in either lineage and represent the paper's primary conceptual departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of SAM's strong pre-trained features with INR's resolution flexibility and a frequency-domain adapter yields a practical recipe for parameter-efficient (1.6M trainable params) medical segmentation that generalizes across domains and resolutions—properties that are critical for clinical deployment where annotation is scarce and imaging protocols vary. The frequency adapter finding that DFT amplitude information consistently improves boundary quality (HD distance 15.44→11.59) suggests a broader principle: foundation models fine-tuned with PEFT may benefit from explicit frequency-domain augmentation beyond spatial-domain adapters. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric segmentation, whether the UGS overhead scales to larger networks, and whether the 12.5% sampling ratio generalizes across tasks or requires per-dataset tuning.
