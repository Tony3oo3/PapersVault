## Problem

Training precise and diverse video editing models is hindered by the scarcity of supervised video editing data. Existing training-free approaches (SDEdit, cross-attention injection, feature extraction) are limited in both editing quality and the range of operations they support. This paper addresses the gap by training a state-of-the-art video editing model entirely without supervised video editing pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242045/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an input video (8-frame 512×512 clips), a textual editing instruction, and an output video caption, and produces an edited video. For videos longer than 8 frames, a sliding window approach is used. The image editing adapter is conditioned on the task label (Add, Remove, Background, Texture, Local, Style, Global) while the video generation adapter is conditioned on the first frame edited by the edit adapter. The full model fits on a single GPU and edits a video in approximately 6 seconds on an H100.

## Method

The core insight is that video editing factorizes into two independent criteria—precise per-frame editing and temporal consistency—each addressed by a dedicated adapter (a ControlNet-based image editing adapter and an Emu Video temporal-layer adapter) stacked on a shared frozen Emu text-to-image backbone. To align these separately trained adapters without supervised data, the paper introduces Factorized Diffusion Distillation (FDD): a student (LoRA weights over the backbone) generates edited videos, and each frozen teacher provides supervision via Score Distillation Sampling (the image editing teacher enforces per-frame fidelity, the video generation teacher enforces temporal coherence) combined with per-teacher adversarial losses using DINO-based discriminators. A K-Bin diffusion sampling strategy randomly selects timesteps from evenly sized bins during training to avoid train-test discrepancy when inference uses more steps. The entire procedure is unsupervised, requiring only a dataset of (output caption, instruction, input video) triplets.

## Results

On the TGVE+ benchmark, EVE achieves the best PickScore (20.88), ViCLIP_dir (0.198), and ViCLIP_out (0.251) among all methods. On the original TGVE benchmark, EVE attains the highest ViCLIP_dir (0.221) and ties for best ViCLIP_out (0.262) and PickScore (20.76). Human raters prefer EVE over every baseline by a significant margin, with average preference rates ranging from 59.6% (vs. InsV2V) to 77.2% (vs. TAV) on TGVE, and 60.2%–74.5% on TGVE+. The only metric where EVE does not lead is CLIPFrame, where STDF and Fairy score 0.933 versus EVE's 0.922/0.926.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVE outperforms the previous state-of-the-art, InsV2V [6], on PickScore (20.88 vs. 20.37 on TGVE+), ViCLIP_dir (0.198 vs. 0.174), and ViCLIP_out (0.251 vs. 0.236), while human raters prefer EVE over InsV2V by 60.2% on average. Against STDF [42], EVE trades a small CLIPFrame deficit (0.926 vs. 0.933) for substantially better ViCLIP_dir (0.198 vs. 0.093 on TGVE+) and a 73.8% human preference margin. Compared to Fairy [36], EVE wins on PickScore (20.88 vs. 19.81), ViCLIP_dir (0.198 vs. 0.140), and ViCLIP_out (0.251 vs. 0.197), with raters preferring EVE 74.3% of the time. EVE also surpasses TAV [37] on all automatic metrics except CLIPFrame and is preferred by raters 74.5% of the time on TGVE+. The only consistent weakness is CLIPFrame, where STDF and Fairy (both 0.933) edge out EVE (0.922–0.926), a metric the authors note favours static videos with minimal motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant training-free paradigm for video editing (SDEdit, cross-attention injection, feature-based methods) by proposing a learned, adapter-based approach. It builds directly on Emu Video's temporal layers and Emu Edit's ControlNet-based editing adapter, combining them on a shared frozen backbone. The core methodological departure is Factorized Diffusion Distillation, which repurposes Score Distillation Sampling (from DreamFusion) and adversarial distillation (from ADD) not to reduce diffusion steps but to learn a new capability—video editing—by simultaneously distilling from two frozen teacher adapters in an unsupervised setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EVE establishes that state-of-the-art video editing is achievable without any supervised video editing pairs, which is a significant practical barrier given the scarcity of such data. The factorization insight (per-frame precision + temporal consistency) and the multi-teacher FDD procedure are generalizable, as demonstrated by aligning the editing adapter with subject/style LoRAs for personalized image editing. Key limitations include the student being upper-bounded by teacher capabilities and the requirement for pre-trained adapters (training from scratch fails). Open questions include whether FDD can handle more than two teachers, longer videos, and whether the approach transfers to other backbone architectures beyond Emu.
