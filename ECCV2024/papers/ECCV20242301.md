## Problem

Existing diffusion-based virtual try-on methods, which adapt exemplar-based inpainting models, fail to preserve fine-grained garment details such as patterns, textures, and colors, limiting their utility in real-world e-commerce scenarios. GAN-based approaches generalize poorly to diverse poses and backgrounds, while diffusion-based methods that encode garments via pseudo-words or explicit warping networks lose identity-critical visual information. The paper also addresses the distribution shift encountered in in-the-wild settings where person and garment images differ substantially from training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image (x_p) and a garment image (x_g) at 1024×768 resolution, along with a segmentation mask isolating the garment region, a Densepose map of the person, and a detailed natural-language caption of the garment (e.g., 'short sleeve round neck t-shirts'). The person image is encoded by a VAE, the masked-out person image and Densepose are concatenated channel-wise with the noised latent (expanding the UNet input to 13 channels), and the garment is processed by both a frozen CLIP image encoder and a parallel UNet encoder. The output is a single virtual try-on image of the person wearing the garment, generated via 30-step DDPM sampling with classifier-free guidance (scale 2.0) that jointly conditions on both the low-level GarmentNet features and high-level IP-Adapter features.

## Method

IDM-VTON conditions a latent diffusion model (SDXL inpainting UNet, called TryonNet) on the garment through two complementary pathways: (1) an IP-Adapter that extracts high-level semantics via a frozen CLIP ViT-H/14 encoder and injects them through an additional cross-attention layer, and (2) a GarmentNet that passes the garment latent through a frozen SDXL UNet encoder and fuses the resulting low-level features into TryonNet's self-attention layer by concatenation (computing self-attention on the combined sequence and retaining only the TryonNet half). Detailed garment captions are fed as text prompts to both GarmentNet and TryonNet to exploit the T2I generative prior. For in-the-wild generalization, the paper introduces a lightweight customization step that fine-tunes only the decoder (up-block) attention layers of TryonNet on a single person-garment pair for 100 steps at lr 1e-6, which ablations show outperforms full-UNet fine-tuning and LoRA in preserving garment identity while improving reconstruction scores.

## Results

On VITON-HD, IDM-VTON achieves the best LPIPS (0.102), FID (6.29), and CLIP-I (0.883) among all compared methods, while on DressCode it achieves substantially lower LPIPS (0.062 vs. 0.107 for the best prior diffusion method) and FID (8.64 vs. 14.37). On the In-the-Wild dataset, IDM-VTON attains LPIPS 0.164, SSIM 0.795, and CLIP-I 0.901, and with customization (IDM-VTON†) further improves to LPIPS 0.150 and CLIP-I 0.909, outperforming even the customized StableVITON (LPIPS 0.259, CLIP-I 0.858).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDM-VTON outperforms all five prior baselines (HR-VITON, GP-VTON, LaDI-VTON, DCI-VTON, StableVITON) on both VITON-HD and DressCode across LPIPS, FID, and CLIP-I, with the largest margins on DressCode where GAN-based methods degrade severely (GP-VTON FID jumps to 55.21). Against the closest diffusion-based competitor StableVITON, IDM-VTON improves LPIPS from 0.133 to 0.102 and FID from 6.52 to 6.29 on VITON-HD, and from 0.107 to 0.062 and 14.37 to 8.64 on DressCode. On the In-the-Wild dataset, IDM-VTON achieves LPIPS 0.164 vs. 0.260 for StableVITON and 0.330 for HR-VITON, and the customization step (IDM-VTON†) further narrows LPIPS to 0.150 while StableVITON† barely improves (0.259). The concurrent work OOTDiffusion is also outperformed on all metrics in the appendix (e.g., FID 6.29 vs. 6.39 on VITON-HD, LPIPS 0.164 vs. 0.203 on In-the-Wild). Notably, IDM-VTON's advantage is most pronounced on DressCode and In-the-Wild, indicating stronger generalization to unseen poses, backgrounds, and intricate garment patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based virtual try-on line of work (LaDI-VTON, DCI-VTON, StableVITON) by introducing a dual-tier garment conditioning mechanism: high-level semantics via IP-Adapter's cross-attention and low-level features via a parallel UNet encoder (GarmentNet) fused through self-attention. It builds directly on the SDXL inpainting model as the base TryonNet and adopts the exemplar-based inpainting formulation from Paint by Example, but departs from prior methods that relied on pseudo-words, explicit warping, or single-encoder conditioning by combining both semantic and pixel-level garment information. The customization strategy (fine-tuning decoder layers on a single person-garment pair) draws from text-to-image personalization methods like DreamBooth but is the first application of such personalization to the VTON task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in diffusion-based VTON: prior methods generate natural images but lose fine-grained garment details (patterns, logos, textures), which is critical for e-commerce applications. The In-the-Wild dataset and the customization protocol provide a more realistic evaluation and deployment pathway than prior work, which relied on clean, studio-like benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to lower-body garments, whether the customization can be made more efficient (e.g., via LoRA or adapter-based methods), and how to preserve human attributes (tattoos, skin features) in masked regions, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation.
