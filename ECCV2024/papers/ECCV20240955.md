## Problem

Neural-based ISPs require paired raw-to-sRGB datasets collected for each specific camera model, making them inapplicable to new cameras with different sensor characteristics. Existing raw-to-raw translation methods are either supervised or semi-supervised (requiring aligned image pairs), and CNN-based approaches lack the ability to model long-range global semantics across camera domains. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a fully unsupervised Transformer-based method that maps raw images between cameras without any paired data.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw images from a source camera (demosaiced via the Menon algorithm and normalized after black-level subtraction) and produces raw images in the target camera's raw space, operating on 224×224 non-overlapping crops. Only unpaired raw images from both cameras are needed for training; no aligned pairs or sRGB ground truth are required. At inference, a single generator translates source-camera raw images, which can then be fed into a pre-trained neural ISP (e.g., LAN) to render sRGB output, enabling cross-camera ISP reuse without retraining.

## Method

Rawformer is a CycleGAN-based encoder-decoder Transformer where the generator uses stacked Contextual-Scale Aware Downsampler/Upsampler blocks, each combining a Condensed Query Attention (CQA) block—spatially compressing queries via average pooling and applying attention horizontally and vertically to reduce computation—with a Scale Perceptive Feed-Forward Network (SPFN) that fuses multi-scale (3×3 and 5×5) depth-wise convolutions. Composite downsampling (pixel-unshuffle + strided conv) and upsampling (pixel-shuffle + deconvolution) blocks handle resolution changes while preserving information, and a learnable Style Modulator injects camera-specific style tokens at each decoder stage. The discriminator employs a cross-domain attention-guided architecture with a cache-based minibatch discrimination mechanism (storing four feature caches for real/fake images from both domains) to stabilize training without large batch sizes. The generator is first self-supervised pre-trained for 500 epochs on an inpainting task (40% random masking, L1+SSIM+VGG loss), then fine-tuned for 500 epochs with adversarial, identity, and cycle-consistency losses.

## Results

On the Raw-to-Raw dataset (Samsung S9 ↔ iPhone X), Rawformer achieves 40.98/41.48 dB PSNR and 0.97/0.98 SSIM for Samsung-to-iPhone and iPhone-to-Samsung translation, respectively, outperforming the best baseline UVCGANv2 (36.32/36.46 dB) by ~4.7–5 dB. On the NUS dataset (Canon EOS 600D ↔ Nikon D5200), the method reaches 41.89/41.37 dB PSNR with 0.98 SSIM. For cross-camera ISP rendering (Table 6), Rawformer + LAN ISP shows only a ~3% accuracy drop compared to the camera-specific ISP, e.g., 22.08/0.80 PSNR/SSIM for S7-to-ZRR vs. 22.16/0.81 for the camera-specific case.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rawformer outperforms all six baselines across every metric on both the Raw-to-Raw and NUS datasets. Against the closest competitor UVCGANv2 (a CycleGAN+ViT hybrid), Rawformer gains ~4.7 dB PSNR and ~0.03 SSIM on the Raw-to-Raw dataset, while also using fewer parameters (26.1M vs. 32.6M) and running faster (26 ms vs. 31 ms). The prior raw-to-raw specific method SSRM [4] (semi-supervised CNN) is surpassed by ~11–13 dB PSNR, confirming the benefit of the fully unsupervised Transformer architecture. Classic GAN baselines (CycleGAN, UNIT, U-GAT-IT) lag by 12–17 dB PSNR, highlighting the difficulty of raw-domain translation without domain-specific inductive biases. In the cross-camera ISP rendering setting (Table 6), Rawformer + LAN ISP narrows the gap to the camera-specific ISP to within ~3%, whereas UVCGANv2 + LAN ISP shows a larger degradation (e.g., 17.72 vs. 18.97 dB PSNR for ZRR-to-AIM21). Against the few-shot domain adaptation method PDA [40], Rawformer achieves ~1.7–2 dB higher PSNR without requiring any paired examples from the target camera.

## Relation to Prior Work

Rawformer builds directly on the CycleGAN framework for unpaired image-to-image translation, replacing its U-Net generators with a Transformer-based encoder-decoder that incorporates condensed query attention (inspired by Restormer) and a style modulator (inspired by StyleGAN). It departs from the prior semi-supervised raw-to-raw mapping work of Afifi and Abuolaim (SSRM, BMVC 2021), which relied on a small set of aligned pairs and a CNN architecture with limited receptive field, by formulating the problem as fully unsupervised and exploiting global contextual dependencies through attention. The cross-domain attention-guided discriminator with a memory-bank cache extends the minibatch discrimination idea from UVCGAN to decouple batch size from diversity enforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a pre-trained neural ISP can now render raw images from an unseen camera with only ~3% accuracy loss compared to a camera-specific ISP trained on paired data, eliminating the costly collection of raw-sRGB pairs for every new phone model. A key open question is real-time feasibility: at 26 ms per frame on GPU and ~1 s on CPU, the method is not yet viable for live camera preview on resource-constrained mobile devices, and the authors explicitly flag lighter CPU-friendly models as future work. Additionally, the method has only been validated on a small number of camera pairs (two smartphone, two DSLR), leaving open how well it generalizes to sensors with very different spectral sensitivities or multi-spectral configurations.
