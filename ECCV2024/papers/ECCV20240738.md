## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inpainting methods like Stable Diffusion Inpainting rely heavily on high-quality text prompts to control generation, often producing unwanted foreground objects in masked regions rather than restoring the surrounding background. GAN-based inpainting methods, while prompt-free, struggle with complex textures and produce blurry or inconsistent results in large erased regions. The object erasure task thus requires a framework that generates harmonious background without demanding expert-level prompt engineering from ordinary users.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a binary mask delineating the object(s) to erase, operating at 512×512 resolution following Stable Diffusion v1.5. No user-provided text prompt is required at inference; instead, the system automatically constructs a short prompt (e.g., 'A photo of R* sky') from panoptic segmentation labels of the background. The output is a full-resolution inpainted image where masked regions are seamlessly replaced with contextually consistent background, evaluated against ground-truth original images using PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID.

## Method

MagicEraser is a two-phase diffusion framework built on Stable Diffusion Inpainting. Phase 1 (content initialization) uses a pretrained GAN-based inpainter (Big-LaMa) to roughly fill masked regions in pixel space, then encodes the result into the VAE latent to set the denoising starting point at s=0.9, reducing unwanted artifact generation. Phase 2 (controllable generation) introduces two plug-and-play modules: (a) prompt tuning, which learns a universal 'background completion' token R* via Textual Inversion and jointly fine-tunes LoRA adapters on a purpose-built Object-Level Removal Dataset (OLRD) constructed by shifting segmented objects onto background regions in Place2; and (b) semantics-aware attention refocus, a training-free module that uses panoptic segmentation labels to construct positive/negative masks and directly modulates self-attention values (Eq. 5–9) during early denoising steps, boosting attention from mask pixels toward background regions while suppressing attention toward foreground-like regions.

## Results

On OpenImages, MagicEraser achieves the best PSNR (28.12 dB) and FID (30.02), outperforming the next-best MAT (26.99 dB, FID 31.30) and SD Inpainting (26.10 dB, FID 31.10). On COCO, MagicEraser leads in PSNR (24.77 dB), SSIM (0.908), and FID (39.55), with the largest gap over GAN-based baselines (e.g., Co-Mod at 19.44 dB PSNR, FID 43.33). On the high-resolution RealHM dataset, MagicEraser attains 23.62 dB PSNR and 46.56 FID, surpassing SD Inpainting (21.76 dB, 45.05 FID) and all GAN-based methods by a wide margin. It also outperforms two commercial products (Adobe Generative Fill and Google Photos Eraser) on RealHM across all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicEraser consistently outperforms all five baselines on PSNR and FID across all three datasets, with the largest margins over GAN-based methods: on RealHM it gains +2.1 dB PSNR over MAT and +4.6 dB over LaMa, while reducing FID by 5–10 points. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor, SD Inpainting, MagicEraser improves PSNR by 1.1–2.0 dB and FID by 1.1–1.5 points on each dataset, confirming that the content initialization, prompt tuning, and attention refocus modules collectively yield more faithful background recovery. The only near-tie is on OpenImages SSIM, where MAT edges MagicEraser by 0.002 (0.949 vs. 0.947), and on COCO LPIPS, where MAT is slightly lower (0.056 vs. 0.062); however, MagicEraser still leads on the remaining three metrics for that dataset. Against the two commercial products evaluated on RealHM (Adobe Generative Fill and Google Photos Eraser), MagicEraser achieves +0.7 dB and +3.3 dB PSNR respectively, along with substantially lower FID (46.56 vs. 49.07 and 53.55). Qualitatively, the paper notes that MAT and Co-Mod leave ghost remnants, LaMa and CoordFill produce severe blurriness on complex textures, and SD Inpainting occasionally hallucinates new objects, whereas MagicEraser generates stable, contextually harmonious backgrounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicEraser builds directly on Stable Diffusion Inpainting (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as its generative backbone and on LaMa (Suvorov et al.) for pixel-space content initialization, but departs from both by reframing the objective from general inpainting to object-specific background restoration. It extends the diffusion inpainting line (SD Inpainting, SmartBrush, Imagen Editor) by eliminating the dependence on high-quality manual text prompts through a learned 'background completion' token (Textual Inversion + LoRA) and a training-free semantics-aware attention refocus module guided by panoptic segmentation. The data construction strategy—shifting segmented objects onto background regions rather than applying random masks—represents a deliberate departure from the random-masking paradigm used to train prior inpainting diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Object erasure is a high-demand, user-facing task (e.g., removing people from photos) where the usability barrier of crafting precise text prompts has limited adoption of diffusion-based methods; MagicEraser's prompt-free pipeline addresses this gap and outperforms both GAN-based and commercial alternatives (Adobe Generative Fill, Google Photos Eraser) on quantitative metrics. However, the 512×512 resolution cap inherited from Stable Diffusion v1.5 and the sensitivity of the attention refocus module to panoptic segmentation quality remain practical limitations. Open questions include whether the semantics-aware attention modulation generalizes to higher-resolution or video settings, and whether the OLRD construction strategy can be extended to multi-object or occlusion-heavy scenes without manual curation.
