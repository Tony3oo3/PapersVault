## Problem

Existing dataset-free Blind Image Super-Resolution methods assume a single spatially-invariant degradation kernel for the entire image, failing to capture the well-known variation in blur shape across flat versus textured regions. Spatially-variant degradation models that assign a unique kernel per pixel have been shown to improve reconstruction, but all such methods require training on large paired synthetic datasets, making them impractical for real-world deployment without ground-truth degradation information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (with unknown blur kernel and additive Gaussian noise) and produces a high-resolution reconstruction at scale factors 2, 3, or 4. No training dataset is required; instead, a 3-layer U-Net generator is optimized per-image via MCEM iterations (up to 5,000), with the degradation kernel dictionary and coefficient matrices jointly inferred from the observed LR image. Evaluation is performed on 256×256 crops for runtime comparison and full-resolution images (cropped to ≤1024×1024) for quality metrics.

## Method

Each pixel's degradation kernel is expressed as a linear combination of N_D=5 learnable anisotropic Gaussian atom kernels, where each atom is fully specified by only three parameters (rotation angle θ_i and two variances σ_i,1, σ_i,2), drastically reducing the parameter count compared to fixed 72-kernel dictionaries. The coefficient matrices are derived from fuzzy set theory membership functions applied to texture features (first-order gradient convolved with a median filter) of a tentative HR estimate, so that flat regions and high-texture regions automatically receive different kernel mixtures. Under a MAP framework, the model combines a dual-domain likelihood (spatial + frequency Gaussian), a Gaussian kernel prior on the atom parameters, and a U-Net-based image prior with Laplacian and Gaussian regularization to prevent overfitting. The MCEM inference algorithm alternates between an E-step that samples the latent code z via Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics and an M-step that updates the kernel parameters Γ and network weights φ via ADAM, with instance normalization and FFT-based skip connections in the generator to further mitigate overfitting in the dataset-free setting.

## Results

SVDSR achieves an average PSNR improvement of ~1 dB over the SOTA dataset-free BISR method BSRDM at 2× scale (e.g., 33.51 vs. 32.76 dB on Set5, 29.89 vs. 28.49 dB on Manga109). At 3× and 4× scales, SVDSR also consistently outperforms BSRDM by 0.3–1.1 dB across all five synthetic benchmarks. On real images (RealSRSet), SVDSR produces visibly sharper detail restoration than other dataset-free methods and is comparable to data-driven approaches like RCAN and ZSSR, which assume bicubic degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest dataset-free competitor BSRDM [41] (spatially-invariant Gaussian kernel prior), SVDSR wins on every dataset and scale factor, with the largest gains at 2× (up to +1.40 dB on Manga109) and moderate gains at 3× and 4× (0.3–1.1 dB). Compared to DIPFKP [21] (flow-based kernel prior), SVDSR leads by a substantial margin of 4.7–5.2 dB at 2×, confirming that spatially-variant modeling is far more effective than spatially-invariant kernel priors. DoubleDIP [18] performs poorly (17–20 dB) as it was designed for motion deblurring rather than general BISR. Data-driven methods RCAN [44] and ZSSR [33] degrade severely (22–27 dB) because they assume bicubic downsampling, making them unfair baselines under Gaussian blur. Notably, SVDSR at 2× approaches the non-blind ZSSR-NB (33.40 dB on Set5) despite not having access to the ground-truth kernel, demonstrating the effectiveness of the spatially-variant degradation model for kernel estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: dataset-free BISR (DIP, DIPFKP, BSRDM), which all assume a single spatially-invariant degradation kernel, and data-driven spatially-variant degradation models (LARPAR, DARM, KOALA, MANet), which assign per-pixel or per-patch kernels but require large paired training datasets. The key departure is making the spatially-variant model dataset-free by parameterizing each atom kernel with only three learnable parameters (angle and two variances of an anisotropic Gaussian) and deriving the per-pixel coefficient matrices from fuzzy-set membership functions applied to local texture, rather than learning them via a large network. The probabilistic formulation under MAP with a dual spatial/frequency likelihood and MCEM inference follows the BSRDM framework but replaces its single Gaussian kernel with a learnable Gaussian-mixture dictionary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a compact, learnable dictionary of just 5 atom kernels (versus 72 in LARPAR) can capture spatially-variant degradation without any training data, the paper opens a path toward practical per-pixel kernel estimation in deployment settings where paired data is unavailable. The remaining limitations are notable: performance gains shrink at 4× scale due to severe texture loss, color distortion persists under strong noise, and the 5000-iteration MCEM loop, while comparable in runtime to BSRDM, is still slow for real-time use. An open question is whether the fuzzy-set coefficient derivation generalizes to more complex, non-Gaussian degradation processes (e.g., motion blur, sensor-specific PSFs) where the anisotropic Gaussian atom assumption may break down.
