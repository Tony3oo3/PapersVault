## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models and pathological MLLMs operate at the patch level and cannot achieve slide-level understanding of gigapixel whole slide images (up to 100,000×100,000 pixels). Task-specialized multiple-instance-learning models each solve only one narrow problem (e.g., tumor grading), and no unified generative framework exists that lets pathologists query a WSI in natural language to obtain diverse clinical answers such as immunohistochemical grading, survival prediction, and tumor subtyping.

## Input / Output

The model takes a gigapixel WSI (preprocessed by HSV-thresholding to remove ~70% non-informative background, then tiled into 256×256 non-overlapping patches yielding typically >10,000 instances) and a short text question (average 9.2 tokens, up to 24). It produces a free-form text answer generated autoregressively word by word, plus a co-attention heatmap that overlays per-patch attention weights for a chosen keyword onto the WSI thumbnail, providing a spatial explanation of which regions drove the answer.

## Method

The Wsi2Text Transformer (W2T) couples a frozen visual encoder (ResNet-50, ViT-S, DINO, or HIPT) that maps each 256×256 patch to a 512-dim embedding with a text encoder (BioClinicalBert, PubMedBert, or trained from scratch) that produces word embeddings. A 3-layer transformer encoder applies self-attention across all patch embeddings to model mutual-instance correlations within the WSI, mimicking how pathologists switch regions of interest. A 3-layer transformer decoder then uses co-attention between the (linearly aligned) word embeddings and the encoded patch embeddings to generate the answer token by token under a negative log-likelihood objective. The co-attention matrix A ∈ R^{T×M} provides fine-grained patch-word similarity scores that can be visualized as heatmaps for interpretability. Training uses Adam (lr=1e-4, weight decay=5e-5) on a single A100-80G GPU, with beam search (size 3) at inference.

## Results

On the WSI-VQA benchmark, W2T with DINO visual extractor and scratch-trained text embeddings achieves the best performance across all three slide-level tasks: F1 of 85.2 on histological subtyping, F1 of 86.5 on PR prediction, and C-index of 59.7 on survival prediction. In the VQA evaluation (Table 1), the best NLG scores are Bleu-1 of 38.6 and ROUGE of 44.8 (DINO+Scratch), while the highest medical accuracy (ACC) reaches 50.8 and Factent reaches 94.1 (HIPT+BioClinicalBert). The dataset contains 977 WSIs with 8,672 QA pairs split into 804/87/86 for train/validation/test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W2T with DINO+scratch outperforms all three MIL baselines on every task. On histological subtyping, W2T achieves F1 of 85.2, surpassing TransMIL (80.6) by +4.6, CLAM-SB (79.7) by +5.5, and AB-MIL (77.1) by +8.1. On PR prediction, W2T reaches F1 of 86.5, edging out CLAM-SB (85.7) by +0.8 and TransMIL (80.6) by +5.9. On survival prediction, W2T obtains a C-index of 59.7, exceeding TransMIL (56.1) by +3.6, CLAM-SB (55.4) by +4.3, and AB-MIL (52.5) by +7.2. Even the weaker ResNet+scratch variant of W2T beats or matches the best MIL method on subtyping (F1 83.3 vs. TransMIL 80.6) and survival (C-index 56.3 vs. TransMIL 56.1), confirming that the generative VQA framework is competitive regardless of the visual backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the generative VQA paradigm from natural images (where inputs are ~256×256) to gigapixel whole slide images, a setting previously addressed exclusively through task-specific MIL classifiers (AB-MIL, CLAM-SB, TransMIL) or patch-level pathological MLLMs (MI-Zero, PLIP, CONCH). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, W2T repurposes a standard transformer encoder-decoder with co-attention to unify multiple slide-level tasks—subtyping, IHC grading, survival prediction—under one question-answering interface. The dataset construction pipeline, which leverages GPT to generate close-ended pairs from TCGA-BRCA captions and template-filling for open-ended clinical fields, is a novel curation strategy that departs from both manual annotation and patch-level text collection used by prior pathological VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first slide-level VQA dataset and framework, WSI-VQA provides a unified benchmark that could accelerate the development of general-purpose computational pathology MLLMs, reducing the need for task-specific models. However, the current scope is limited to a single cancer type (BRCA) with only 977 WSIs, survival prediction c-indices remain low (~56–60), and the reliance on frozen pre-trained visual extractors constrains end-to-end optimization. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes across cancer types, whether hallucination risks are manageable at clinical scale, and whether co-attention maps can substitute for more rigorous interpretability guarantees that pathologists require.
