## Problem

Existing post-hoc calibration methods for DNNs are optimized on in-distribution validation sets and fail to maintain calibration under distribution shift (covariate or semantic OOD). Prior robust calibration approaches, such as Tomani et al., handle OOD but worsen calibration in the complete ID setting, and none adapt the calibration factor to individual input samples. This paper addresses the gap of achieving robust calibration across the full spectrum from ID to OOD without sacrificing ID performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the raw logit vector z ∈ R^K from the last layer of a pre-trained classifier (no intermediate layer outputs required) and produces a single calibrated confidence score q̂ ∈ [0,1] per test sample. During the tuning phase, it additionally requires an ID validation set and a semantic OOD dataset (e.g., SVHN or Textures) to fit two Gaussian PDFs over the free-energy scores of correct and incorrect predictions. At inference, only the logit is needed, making the method lightweight and applicable to any pre-trained multi-class classifier.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the fixed temperature of standard Temperature Scaling with an instance-wise scaling factor h = T_ts − λ₁θ₁ + λ₂θ₂, where T_ts is the conventional TS temperature and λ₁, λ₂ are per-sample weights derived from the free-energy score F(z) = −log Σᵢ exp(zᵢ). Two Gaussian PDFs are fitted on the energy scores of correct and incorrect predictions (from ID validation and semantic OOD data, respectively), yielding λ₁ = P_correct(F(z)) and λ₂ = P_incorrect(F(z)). The two trainable parameters θ₁, θ₂ are optimized via an MSE loss between one-hot labels and the calibrated softmax probabilities. The key insight is that the energy score is more discriminative than softmax confidence in separating correct from incorrect predictions and ID from OOD samples, enabling the method to adaptively lower or raise the effective temperature for each individual input, thereby preserving accuracy while achieving robust calibration across all distribution shift regimes.

## Results

The proposed energy-based instance-wise calibration method achieves the lowest averaged ECE across all severity levels (0–5) on every dataset-architecture combination tested. On CIFAR-10 with DenseNet201, it reduces ECE from 12.90 (uncalibrated) to 4.47, outperforming the best baseline (IRM at 5.78) by 1.31 points. On CIFAR-100 with DenseNet201, ECE drops from 22.79 to 5.94, beating the next-best method (SPLINE at 7.52) by 1.58 points. On ImageNet-1k with DenseNet201, the method achieves 4.92 ECE versus 5.49 for TS/ETS, and it also outperforms DAC-augmented baselines (Table 2) without requiring additional layer outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baseline families across every dataset and architecture tested. Against Temperature Scaling (TS, Guo et al. [4]), the gains are largest on CIFAR-100 where TS achieves 11.21 ECE (DenseNet201) versus 5.94 for the proposed method — a 47% relative reduction. Against the Mix-n-match suite (ETS, IRM, IROvA, IROvATS; Zhang et al. [36]), IRM is typically the strongest single baseline (e.g., 5.78 on CIFAR-10/DenseNet201), yet the proposed method still leads by 1.31 ECE points; the ensemble variants (IROvA, IROvATS) are consistently weaker than IRM. Against Spline calibration (Gupta et al. [5]), the advantage is most pronounced on CIFAR-100 (5.94 vs. 7.52 for SPLINE with DenseNet201) and ImageNet-1k (4.92 vs. 6.44). Against DAC (Tomani et al. [30]), which requires additional intermediate-layer outputs, the proposed method alone (5.98 ECE on CIFAR-10-C) already beats ETS+DAC (8.59) and SPLINE+DAC (8.45), and on CIFAR-100-C it matches ETS+DAC (11.92 vs. 11.97) while using only the final-layer logits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two distinct lines of work: the temperature scaling (TS) framework for post-hoc calibration (Guo et al., ICML 2017) and the energy-based OOD detection model (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020). It departs from prior calibration methods (TS, ETS, IRM, SPLINE) that assume in-distribution test data, and from Tomani et al. (CVPR 2021) who addressed OOD calibration but degraded ID performance. The key conceptual move is repurposing the free energy score—originally designed for OOD detection—as a per-instance uncertainty estimator that drives adaptive scaling factors, combined with Gaussian PDFs fitted to correct/incorrect energy distributions to achieve instance-wise calibration without requiring intermediate network outputs (unlike DAC, ICML 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most post-hoc calibration methods fail under distribution shift, while the one prior method handling OOD (Tomani et al. 2021) actually worsens ID calibration. The proposed method's ability to maintain robust calibration across the full spectrum (ID, covariate shift, semantic shift) using only the final-layer logit makes it broadly applicable without architectural modifications. Limitations include the need for both ID validation and semantic OOD data during the tuning phase, the Gaussian PDF assumption for energy score distributions, and evaluation restricted to image classification on standard benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to regression, sequential prediction, or settings where no clean OOD proxy is available for tuning.
