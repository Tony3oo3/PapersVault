## Problem

No open real-world dataset exists that simultaneously supports all three V2X collaboration modes (Vehicle-to-Vehicle, Vehicle-to-Infrastructure, and Infrastructure-to-Infrastructure) for cooperative perception research. Existing real-world datasets are limited to a single collaboration mode, are vehicle-centric (ignoring infrastructure as an ego agent), and are collected in low-density suburban or rural areas with only 11–12 objects per scene, making them inadequate for testing V2X algorithms in the dense, multi-agent urban environments where they will actually be deployed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241398/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes multi-agent 128-beam LiDAR point clouds (10 Hz, 360° horizontal FOV) from up to four agents (two vehicles with four ZED2i stereo cameras each, and two infrastructure units with two Axis network cameras each), synchronized via GPS-locked timestamps. The output is 9-dimensional 3D bounding boxes (center x/y/z, dimensions w/l/h, orientation roll/yaw/pitch) across 10 object categories grouped into three super-classes (car, pedestrian, truck), evaluated with AP at IoU thresholds of 0.3 and 0.5—deliberately lower than standard KITTI thresholds to accommodate the extreme size range from sub-0.5 m pedestrians to over-20 m buses.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a large-scale real-world dataset (33K LiDAR frames, 171K camera images, 1.2M 3D boxes) collected over 75 hours in a smart intersection and urban V2V corridors, partitioned into four sub-datasets (VC, IC, V2V, I2I) based on ego agent type and collaboration mode. For benchmarking, they build a multi-class cooperative 3D detection pipeline on OpenCOOD using PointPillar as the LiDAR backbone, extending the original single-class head to multi-class heads following OpenPCDet's design. They systematically evaluate three fusion strategies—No Fusion (ego-only baseline), Late Fusion (NMS on shared detection proposals), Early Fusion (raw point-cloud aggregation in ego frame), and Intermediate Fusion (BEV feature sharing via AttFuse, F-Cooper, and V2X-ViT)—all trained with 80 epochs, batch size 2, Adam optimizer, and multi-step LR decay. A key annotation strategy combines the two infrastructure LiDAR point clouds into a holistic view to halve annotation workload, then projects boxes back per-agent with a minimum 5-point threshold to filter out sparsely observed objects.

## Results

Across all four sub-datasets, cooperative perception methods consistently outperform the No Fusion baseline, with mAP@IoU=0.3 gains ranging from ~3 to ~29 points depending on the collaboration mode. V2X-ViT achieves the best mAP on V2X-Real-VC (44.8/35.8), V2X-Real-V2V (46.5/37.3), and V2X-Real-I2I (62.7/50.3), while AttFuse leads on V2X-Real-IC (50.3/38.5). The I2I collaboration mode yields the highest absolute performance (up to 62.7 mAP@0.3), reflecting the complementary wide-field-of-view coverage of two infrastructure LiDARs. Car detection is consistently the strongest class, while pedestrian and truck APs remain notably lower due to sparse LiDAR returns and diverse size distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V2X-ViT [33] achieves the highest mAP on three of four sub-datasets: V2X-Real-VC (44.8 vs. AttFuse 42.5 at IoU=0.3), V2X-Real-V2V (46.5 vs. AttFuse 39.2), and V2X-Real-I2I (62.7 vs. AttFuse 56.5), with particularly large margins on the I2I setting (+6.2 mAP). AttFuse [34] is the sole winner on V2X-Real-IC (50.3 vs. V2X-ViT 45.9 at IoU=0.3), where the infrastructure ego perspective and its wider static field of view appear to favor its attention-based BEV fusion. F-Cooper [2] is consistently the weakest intermediate fusion method, trailing V2X-ViT by 6.7–15.8 mAP points across the four datasets, suggesting its simple pooling mechanism is insufficient for the dense urban traffic in V2X-Real. All three intermediate fusion methods outperform both Early and Late Fusion baselines on most sub-datasets, confirming that feature-level sharing strikes the best accuracy–bandwidth trade-off. Notably, on V2X-Real-I2I, even the simplest Late Fusion (52.2 mAP@0.3) surpasses Early Fusion (46.9), indicating that for infrastructure-to-infrastructure collaboration, sharing detection proposals is more effective than merging raw point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the V2X cooperative perception dataset lineage that began with simulated benchmarks (OPV2V, V2X-Sim, V2X-Set) and progressed to real-world but single-mode datasets (DAIR-V2X for V2I, V2V4Real for V2V). It departs from all prior work by being the first real-world dataset simultaneously covering all three collaboration modes (V2V, V2I, I2I) and by introducing an infrastructure-centric ego perspective, which prior datasets treated only as an auxiliary collaborator. The benchmark framework builds directly on the OpenCOOD pipeline and reuses established fusion methods (AttFuse, F-Cooper, V2X-ViT) as baselines, while extending the detection head from single-class to multi-class following OpenPCDet's design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

V2X-Real fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale real-world V2X dataset with dense urban traffic (36 objects/scene vs. 11–12 in prior datasets) and the first open multi-class cooperative 3D detection benchmark, enabling research on vulnerable road users that was previously infeasible. The infrastructure-centric sub-datasets (IC, I2I) open a new research direction for intelligent transportation systems that prior vehicle-centric datasets ignored. Key limitations include the absence of radar, only four agents, and collection at a single intersection plus corridors, raising open questions about scalability to larger agent counts, diverse geographic settings, and low-cost sensor suites for practical deployment.
