## Problem

Direct 3D hand motion prediction—forecasting individual finger articulations and hand joint trajectories—remains largely unexplored in computer vision, as prior work either predicts full-body skeletons or treats the hand as a single trajectory point. The compact skeleton size, rapid and diverse motion patterns, and limited range of hand movements make this task substantially harder than full-body prediction, and no dedicated benchmark or method existed for it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240171/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of H past frames of 21-joint 3D hand coordinates (15 frames / 0.5 s on FPHA, 20 frames on HO-3D) and produces K = 10 diverse future sequences of P frames (60 frames / 2.0 s on FPHA, 80 frames on HO-3D). Motion is represented in the DCT frequency domain, where the model predicts a residual vector over the first L = 20 DCT coefficients rather than raw Cartesian coordinates, ensuring temporal smoothness. Evaluation uses five metrics (APD, ADE, FDE, MMADE, MMFDE) that jointly measure diversity and accuracy of the K predicted sequences.

## Method

PromptFDDM comprises three networks: a Spatial-Temporal Extractor Network (STEN) built from stacked ST-GCN-SE blocks that predicts future motion, a Ground Truth Extractor Network (GTEN) that compresses future ground-truth motion into a 16-dim latent feature map Z^gt, and a Reference Data Generator Network (RDGN) that is a DDPM diffusion model trained to generate an approximation of Z^gt from observed motion alone. Training proceeds in two stages: first, STEN is trained with GTEN's ground-truth guidance; second, with STEN frozen, RDGN learns to denoise from Gaussian noise to produce the guidance latent, using interactive prompts (learnable parameters conditioned on the observed-motion latent Z via global average pooling and softmax-weighted dot products) injected at every layer of the TransLinear U-Net denoising network. At inference, only STEN and RDGN are used—the diffusion model generates the reference guidance in 100 DDIM steps, which is fused with the observed-motion latent and decoded into future DCT coefficients, then inverse-transformed to 3D joint coordinates.

## Results

On FPHA (first-person, 15 obs → 60 pred frames), PromptFDDM achieves state-of-the-art on all five metrics: ADE 0.413, FDE 0.535, MMADE 0.416, MMFDE 0.534, and APD 19.798, outperforming the next-best GSPS (ADE 0.494, FDE 0.654) by roughly 16% on ADE. On HO-3D (third-person, 20 obs → 80 pred frames), PromptFDDM attains ADE 0.163, FDE 0.203, MMADE 0.190, MMFDE 0.210, and the highest diversity (APD 4.541), striking a strong balance between accuracy and diversity. The ablation on DCT coefficient count (L=20) and the use of 10 diverse samples (K=10) are the key hyperparameter choices.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FPHA, PromptFDDM clearly dominates all five baselines: it reduces ADE from 0.494 (GSPS, the previous best) to 0.413 (−16%) and FDE from 0.654 to 0.535 (−18%), while also achieving the highest diversity (APD 19.798 vs. 9.738 for GSPS). On HO-3D, the picture is more nuanced: HumanMAC achieves a slightly lower ADE (0.155 vs. 0.163), but PromptFDDM wins on FDE (0.203 vs. 0.217), MMADE (0.190 vs. 0.209), MMFDE (0.210 vs. 0.236), and APD (4.541 vs. 1.821), indicating substantially better diversity and multi-modal coverage. Compared to MOJO, PromptFDDM trades a small ADE increase (0.163 vs. 0.180) for a large FDE improvement (0.203 vs. 0.255) and higher diversity. BeLFusion is only evaluated on FPHA and performs worst among all methods (ADE 0.696, FDE 0.869), while cVAE and DLOW are consistently outperformed by a wide margin on both datasets. The STEN-alone ablation (without diffusion guidance) confirms that the RDGN-generated reference data contributes meaningfully, improving ADE from 0.473 to 0.413 on FPHA and from 0.168 to 0.163 on HO-3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines ST-GCN-SE blocks (from Yan et al.) for compact skeleton encoding with a DDPM-based diffusion framework (Ho et al.) applied not to the full motion sequence but to a compact latent feature map, departing from prior diffusion-based motion predictors like HumanMAC and BeLFusion that denoise the entire trajectory. The DCT-based trajectory representation follows Mao et al., and the U-Net denoising architecture with skip connections is adapted from HumanMAC. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage 'future-driven' design: a GTEN extracts guidance from ground truth during training, and a separate RDGN (diffusion model) learns to generate that guidance from observed motion alone at inference, while interactive prompts from past motion are injected into the denoising network—a novel application of prompt learning to diffusion-based motion prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of direct 3D hand motion prediction (all 21 joints, including individual fingers), filling a gap between full-body motion prediction and hand trajectory forecasting that is critical for AR/VR and robotic control. The two-stage training paradigm (learn with future guidance, then generate that guidance via diffusion) offers a practical recipe for incorporating unavailable future information into prediction models. Limitations include the trade-off between diversity and accuracy, the absence of hand-object interaction modeling, and the relatively narrow scope of 21-joint hands; open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-hand or hand-object scenarios and whether the compact-latent diffusion strategy generalizes to other compact skeletal structures.
