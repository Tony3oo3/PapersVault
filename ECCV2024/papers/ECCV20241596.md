## Problem

Chamfer Distance (CD) is the dominant loss for point cloud learning due to its O(n log n) efficiency, but it converges to suboptimal solutions with severe point clustering because multiple source points can match a single target point. Previous CD modifications (DCD, HyperCD, InfoCD) focus on altering distance computation or loss-term weighting, while overlooking the correspondence search stage—the fundamental structural difference between CD and Earth Mover's Distance—and ignoring the dynamic behaviour of losses during iterative optimisation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241596/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The loss takes two point clouds of potentially unequal size (source X and target Y) and returns a single scalar distance. Correspondence search and density estimation are both restricted to a local kNN neighbourhood (k=32 by default), so the method only uses local geometric information per point. The density of a point in a cloud is computed as the inverse of the sum of squared distances to its k nearest neighbours in that cloud, and the density ratio between the two clouds modulates the correspondence assignment.

## Method

UniformCD modifies the correspondence search in bidirectional CD by multiplying each candidate distance by a relative density ratio: dens(y_i, X) / dens(y_i, Y), where dens measures local density via inverse kNN distance. This penalises matches where the source cloud is already denser than the target around the correspondence point, effectively discouraging multiple source points from collapsing onto a single target point. The search is limited to the k=32 nearest neighbours to preserve O(n log n) complexity, and the same kNN structure is reused for both correspondence selection and density computation. The result is a differentiable, architecture-agnostic loss that drives CD's dynamic behaviour (backward consistency, coverage, correspondence stability) significantly closer to EMD while maintaining near-CD training speed (~18% overhead).

## Results

On the MVP completion benchmark, VRC trained with UniformCD achieves an average EMD of 1.48 (×10²), substantially outperforming VRC-CD (3.08), VRC-EMD (2.08), VRC-DCD (2.21), and VRC-InfoCD (4.13). On the PCN benchmark, PointAttN fine-tuned with UniformCD reaches an average EMD of 1.61 (×10²), beating PointAttN-CD (1.93) and SeedFormer-CD (2.93). For self-supervised classification on ModelNet40, UniformCD achieves 87.1% accuracy, surpassing CD (83.9%), EMD (84.4%), SWD (86.8%), and InfoCD (86.3%). In point cloud reconstruction, UniformCD attains an EMD mean of 5.58 (×10²) versus 5.88 for CD and 9.44 for InfoCD, while incurring only ~18% additional training time over vanilla CD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniformCD consistently outperforms vanilla CD on EMD across all evaluated tasks (completion, reconstruction, parametric modelling) while incurring a modest ~18% increase in per-iteration training time. Against InfoCD, UniformCD achieves lower EMD on MVP (1.48 vs 4.13) and PCN (1.61 vs 2.84 for SeedFormer), and higher classification accuracy on ModelNet40 (87.1% vs 86.3%), though InfoCD retains a slight edge on the CD metric itself. Compared to DCD (BalancedCD), UniformCD achieves lower EMD on both MVP (1.48 vs 2.21) and PCN (1.61 vs 1.103 for SeedFormer-DCD, though DCD is not directly listed for PCN in the main table). Against HyperCD, UniformCD wins on EMD for PCN completion (1.61 vs 2.65 for SeedFormer-HyperCD). For reconstruction, UniformCD (EMD 5.58) beats SWD (8.31) and InfoCD (9.44) while matching CD's EMD (5.88) with a small CD trade-off (9.15 vs 8.54). Direct EMD optimisation still achieves the best EMD in parametric modelling (e.g., 1.01 vs 2.37 on elbow), but UniformCD narrows this gap far more than any other CD variant and does so at O(n log n) cost versus O(n³ log n) for EMD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of CD modifications (DCD, HyperCD, InfoCD, WeightedCD, LCD) that alter the distance computation between correspondences or the aggregation of loss terms. Instead, it targets the correspondence search stage itself, arguing that the fundamental distinction between CD and EMD lies solely in how correspondences are assigned. The density-aware correspondence criterion draws conceptually from EMD's injective mapping property and DCD's density normalisation, but implements it through a differentiable kNN-based local density ratio rather than a non-differentiable scaling factor or a separate regularisation term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniformCD offers a practical, architecture-agnostic drop-in replacement for CD that narrows the EMD gap while preserving O(n log n) complexity, making it broadly applicable to completion, reconstruction, self-supervised pre-training, and parametric optimisation pipelines. The ~18% training-time overhead and the observed trade-off (improved EMD at the cost of slightly worse CD) are modest but real limitations. An open question the authors flag is how the increased correspondence volatility near convergence affects downstream model quality, and whether the local-density heuristic generalises to non-uniform or heavily occluded point sets where the kNN neighbourhood may be unreliable.
