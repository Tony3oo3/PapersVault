## Problem

Existing sign language datasets are predominantly 2D, making them insufficient for 3D avatar applications due to depth ambiguity, while 3D SL annotation remains a manual, labor-intensive process that produces unnatural results. No large-scale, multi-prompt 3D holistic motion dataset with accurate hand mesh annotations exists, and there is no benchmark for 3D sign language production from diverse linguistic inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240108/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The annotation pipeline consumes monocular RGB video sequences and outputs per-frame SMPL-X parameters (body pose θ^b ∈ R^{23×6}, hand pose θ^h ∈ R^{30×6}, facial expression φ, yaw θ^f, and shape β̃) plus 2D/3D keypoints and a MANO hand-only subset. The SignVAE SLP model takes discrete linguistic prompts—spoken language sentences, HamNoSys glyph sequences, or individual words—and generates temporal sequences of SMPL-X motion parameters (upper body only, with a 4× downsampling window), decoded into full 3D mesh representations. The dataset spans 70K clips (8.34M frames, 117 hours) from 153 signers across four prompt groups: Word, ASL, HamNoSys (PJM/DGS/GRSL/LSF), and GSL.

## Method

The annotation pipeline performs iterative multi-objective optimization over SMPL-X parameters, initialized hierarchically by fusing OsX, ACR, and PARE predictions, then refined with a loss combining 2D reprojection, pose/shape priors, temporal smoothness, angle limits, and three biomechanical hand constraints (bone-length intervals, palmar curvature/angular-distance bounds, and convex-hull joint-angle priors in the flexion-abduction plane). For SLP, SignVAE employs a two-stage VQ-VAE architecture: a Parallel Linguistic Feature Generator (PLFG) quantizes diverse prompt embeddings (CLIP text or learned HamNoSys token embeddings) into a shared semantic codebook Z_l, while a separate motion VQ-VAE quantizes SMPL-X motion sequences into a motion codebook Z_m. An auto-regressive decoder then predicts motion code indices conditioned on the fused semantic code indices, enabling a single model to handle all prompt types through discrete code-space alignment rather than direct high-level feature regression.

## Results

On the EHF dataset, the annotation pipeline achieves 20.1 mm MPVPE (holistic) and 4.7 mm PA-MPVPE (hands), outperforming the best prior method (Motion-X with GT 3D keypoints) by 34% on holistic MPVPE (30.7 mm) and 76% on hand PA-MPVPE (19.7 mm). The biomechanical constraints further reduce hand error from 12.5 to 9.7 mm MPVPE. On the 3D SLP benchmark (HamNoSys subset), SignVAE achieves 0.516 DTW-MJE top-1 rank, more than doubling the best baseline Ham2Pose-3d (0.253) and the original Ham2Pose (0.092).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The annotation pipeline dominates all eight baselines on EHF. Against the strongest regression-based method Motion-X (with GT 3D keypoints), the paper achieves 34% lower holistic MPVPE (20.1 vs. 30.7 mm) and 40% lower PA-MPJPE on body (15.6 vs. 23.9 mm). Among optimization-based methods, SMPLify-X [37] is the weakest at 65.3 mm PA-MPVPE holistic, while the paper reduces this to 12.9 mm. Among regression/hybrid methods, OSX [31] (48.7 mm PA-MPVPE) and PyMAF-X [58] (50.2 mm) are the closest competitors but still trail by over 35 mm. For the SLP task, SignVAE more than doubles Ham2Pose-3d [4] on DTW-MJE top-1 (0.516 vs. 0.253) and nearly triples the original 2D Ham2Pose (0.092). The ablation (Table 5) further shows SignVAE outperforms SignDiffuse (MDM-based [50]) on R-Precision top-1 (0.429 vs. 0.285) and MM-dist (2.651 vs. 3.866), confirming the VQ-VAE + PLFG architecture's advantage over diffusion-based generation for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sign language production (SLP) line of work from 2D keypoint-based generation (Ham2Pose, Saunders et al.) to 3D holistic mesh motion, and from single-prompt (HamNoSys-only) to multi-prompt (HamNoSys, spoken language, word) settings. The annotation pipeline builds on the SMPL-X parametric model and fuses outputs from OSX, ACR, and PARE for initialization, while the generation architecture repurposes VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al.) for both motion quantization and a novel parallel linguistic feature generation (PLFG) module. It departs from the only prior 3D SL dataset (SGNify [14]), which covers isolated signs with HamNoSys prompts only, by including continuous co-articulated signs and diverse semantic annotations at a much larger scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SignAvatars fills a critical gap: no prior publicly available dataset provided 3D holistic mesh annotations (body, hands, face) for continuous sign language at this scale, and the manual annotation process that produced earlier 3D SL avatars was labor-intensive and yielded unnatural results. The first 3D SLP benchmark with multi-prompt evaluation opens a new research direction, though the authors candidly note that current methods are far from producing truly accurate sign motion, and the absence of a generic 3D back-translation method limits evaluation. Future work on 3D sign language translation, AR/VR integration, and large-scale 3D SL motion models could significantly benefit Deaf and hard-of-hearing communities.
