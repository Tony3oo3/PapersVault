## Problem

Quantifying evolutionary trait variations across large groups of species traditionally relies on labor-intensive expert visual inspection and subjective trait definitions, hindering rapid discovery of heritable characteristics across the tree of life. While latent diffusion models can generate high-quality images conditioned on text or image prompts, no prior method encodes the hierarchical structure of phylogenetic relationships as conditioning signals to enable controlled visualization of trait changes at specific evolutionary branching points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242377/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a species' phylogenetic position and encodes it as a 512-dimensional HIER-Embed vector (four 128-dim segments, one per discretized ancestral level), which conditions the diffusion model via cross-attention in the U-Net backbone. The output is a 256×256 RGB image of the species, generated in a VQGAN latent space with 4× downsampling. Perturbed embeddings—where specific level segments are replaced with Gaussian noise (trait masking) or swapped with a sibling node's segment (trait swapping)—yield images that visually isolate traits acquired at particular evolutionary levels.

## Method

Phylo-Diffusion introduces a HIERarchical Embedding (HIER-Embed) strategy that assigns each species a concatenated sequence of four learnable embedding vectors, one per discretized phylogenetic level, with a hard constraint that species sharing a common ancestor at level-k receive identical embeddings at that level via a shared lookup table. This 512-dim embedding conditions a latent diffusion model (VQGAN autoencoder with 4× downsampling + denoising U-Net) through cross-attention, trained for 400k iterations without classifier-free guidance. Two novel perturbation experiments enable trait discovery: Trait Masking replaces one or more level embeddings with Gaussian noise (analogous to gene knockout) to reveal which traits disappear, and Trait Swapping substitutes a level-l embedding with that of a sibling subtree (analogous to CRISPR gene editing) to visualize trait differences at specific branching points. The framework is validated on 38 fish species (GLIN dataset) and CUB-200-2011 birds, achieving FID 11.38 and 82.21% classification F1 on generated images, with embedding-space distances closely matching ground-truth phylogenetic distances.

## Results

On the GLIN fish dataset (38 species, 5434 images), Phylo-Diffusion achieves the best FID of 11.38, Inception Score of 2.53, and Recall of 0.367 among all conditioning strategies, while matching the Class Conditional baseline on Precision (0.654 vs. 0.679). For species classification of generated images, Phylo-Diffusion attains an F1 score of 82.21%, approaching the 85% accuracy of the base ResNet-18 classifier on real test images. The hierarchical embedding also closely aligns with ground-truth phylogenetic distances, unlike the flat Class Conditional encoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Phylo-NN (the GAN-based phylogeny-guided encoder-decoder from KDD 2023), Phylo-Diffusion substantially outperforms on every metric: FID drops from 28.08 to 11.38, Inception Score rises from 2.35 to 2.53, Recall jumps from 0.084 to 0.367, and classification F1 improves from 47.37% to 82.21%. Visually, Phylo-NN's trait-swapped images appear blurry or nearly identical to the source species, whereas Phylo-Diffusion produces clear, interpretable trait changes (e.g., loss of barbels, forked caudal fin). Compared to the two diffusion-based internal baselines, Phylo-Diffusion achieves the best FID (11.38 vs. 11.46 for Class Conditional and 11.76 for Scientific Name) and the highest Recall (0.367), though it trails slightly on Precision (0.654 vs. 0.679/0.683). The Scientific Name baseline, which leverages CLIP-encoded genus/species names, captures some inter-genus structure but cannot model inter-genus phylogenetic distances, a gap that HIER-Embed fills by aligning embedding-space distances with the ground-truth phylogenetic tree.

## Relation to Prior Work

Phylo-Diffusion extends the latent diffusion model framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) by replacing standard text or class conditioning with a novel HIERarchical Embedding (HIER-Embed) that encodes a species' position across four discretized phylogenetic ancestry levels. It is most closely related to Phylo-NN (Elhamod et al., KDD 2023), which also uses phylogenetic structure to learn image representations, but departs in three key ways: it operates at the species level rather than specimen level, uses hard constraints so all descendants of a shared ancestor receive identical embeddings at that level, and introduces perturbation experiments (trait masking and trait swapping) inspired by gene knockout and CRISPR editing. The work also positions itself within knowledge-guided machine learning (KGML), distinguishing itself from interpretable ML methods (ProtoPNet, PIP-Net, INTR) that lack structured biological priors and generative capabilities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework opens a pathway for biologists to rapidly visualize evolutionary trait changes across phylogenetic branches without the years-long timelines of traditional genetic experiments, potentially accelerating discovery of synapomorphies and candidate genes for evolutionary phenotypes. However, the current formulation is limited to discretized trees with a fixed number of levels, does not capture convergent evolution or perform ancestral state reconstruction with uncertainty, and phylogenetic signal diminishes toward the root, restricting effective analysis to levels near species nodes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to continuous (non-discretized) phylogenies, larger taxonomic scales, and whether the discovered trait changes can be validated against known genetic mechanisms.
