## Problem

End-to-end 3D trackers suffer from performance degradation because detection and tracking tasks conflict during joint optimization, yet previous works only vaguely attributed this to incompatible task-specific features. No prior method could truly jointly train a one-stage tracker from scratch under both detection and tracking supervision with a unified object feature representation, instead relying on pre-trained detectors, separate stages, or decoupled query representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240183/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images (6 cameras, cropped to 1600×640 for full-resolution or 800×320 for half-resolution settings) along with track queries propagated from the previous frame and 644 newly initialized detection queries per frame. It produces 3D bounding boxes with separate detection and tracking confidence scores and persistent tracking IDs, all in a single forward pass. Notably, the model uses two independent classification heads (one per task) but a shared regression head, and dynamically partitions queries into four polarity groups that determine which predictions are output for detection versus tracking.

## Method

OneTrack identifies that the detection–tracking conflict originates from partially conflicted classification gradients caused by divergent positive sample definitions: detectors assign the most precise prediction as positive, while trackers maintain 'locked' query–GT pairs across frames regardless of current accuracy. The solution involves three mechanisms: (1) gradient coordination, where queries positive for only one task backpropagate classification gradients exclusively through that task's head, eliminating contradictory gradients on Q^pn and Q^np groups; (2) a dynamic self-attention mask in each of the six decoder layers that blocks attention between Q^pn and Q^np queries to prevent cross-task competition; and (3) a modified tracking classification loss using Quality Focal Loss with distance-decayed assignment weights that suppress confidence for locked queries whose predictions have drifted far from ground truth. Built on StreamPETR's hybrid-attention architecture with ViT-99 backbone and memory queues, the model is trained end-to-end from scratch in a single stage using the Hungarian algorithm for bipartite matching, achieving state-of-the-art AMOTA on nuScenes while halving the training cost of prior trackers.

## Results

On the nuScenes camera-based 3D tracking benchmark, OneTrack-F (full resolution, 1600×640) achieves 0.548 AMOTA and 1.088 AMOTP on the validation set, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art PF-Track-F by 6.9% AMOTA. On the test set, OneTrack-F reaches 0.554 AMOTA and 1.021 AMOTP, outperforming DQTrack by 3.1% AMOTA while reporting fewer than half the identity switches (481 vs. 1204). Notably, OneTrack is the only method in the comparison trained entirely from scratch without relying on pre-trained detectors, and it also achieves competitive detection performance (0.566 NDS, 0.478 mAP at half resolution).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneTrack-F decisively outperforms PF-Track-F on the validation set by 6.9% AMOTA (0.548 vs. 0.479) and 0.139 AMOTP (1.088 vs. 1.227), while also achieving higher MOTA (0.479 vs. 0.435) and recall (61.8% vs. 59.0%). On the test set, OneTrack-F surpasses DQTrack by 3.1% AMOTA (0.554 vs. 0.523) and 0.075 AMOTP (1.021 vs. 1.096), with substantially fewer identity switches (481 vs. 1204). Against PF-Track on the test set, the margin is even larger: 12.0% AMOTA (0.554 vs. 0.434). OneTrack-S at half resolution (800×320) also beats DQTrack at the same resolution by 4.6% AMOTA (0.492 vs. 0.446) and 0.129 AMOTP (1.122 vs. 1.251). A key differentiator is that OneTrack is the only method trained entirely from scratch in a single stage, whereas PF-Track requires a separate detector pre-training phase and DQTrack relies on a frozen pre-trained detector for its tracking comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneTrack extends the 'tracking by track queries' paradigm established by MOTR, Trackformer, MUTR3D, and PF-Track, which propagate object queries across frames to perform end-to-end 3D tracking. It departs from DQTrack's strategy of decoupling detection and tracking into separate query streams and from MOTRv2's reliance on a frozen pre-trained detector, instead proposing a unified single-stage model with a shared object feature representation. The key conceptual departure is a precise diagnosis: the detection–tracking conflict is not a vague incompatibility of task-specific features but a partial conflict in classification gradients arising from divergent positive-sample assignments, which the paper resolves through query grouping, gradient coordination, and dynamic attention masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OneTrack is the first one-stage model that truly jointly trains detection and tracking from scratch with a unified feature representation, closing a gap that prior end-to-end 3D trackers could only address through multi-stage training or architectural separation. The gradient-conflict diagnosis and coordination mechanism offer a reusable diagnostic lens for other multi-task settings where positive-sample definitions diverge. Limitations include evaluation solely on nuScenes camera-based tracking, dependence on the StreamPETR/DETR query architecture, and the fact that the dynamic masking and query-grouping logic add implementation complexity that may not transfer trivially to non-query-based trackers.
