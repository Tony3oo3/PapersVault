## Problem

Cross-domain cellular nuclei recognition in digital pathology suffers severe performance degradation when models trained on one organ or staining protocol are applied to another, particularly for morphologically ambiguous cell types like neoplastic versus epithelial cells. Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods align low-level visual attributes or individual object semantics in a category-agnostic manner, ignoring the hierarchical latent structure and contextual correspondences across biological structures in the tumor micro-environment that remain invariant across domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241955/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes H&E-stained histology image tiles and produces per-nucleus class labels (neoplastic, epithelial, connective, inflammatory) along with instance segmentation masks, built on a Mask R-CNN backbone. During training, the input is additionally subjected to counterfactual pixel-level nuclei masking (replacing nucleus pixels with their mean value) and instance-level feature masking (removing the ROI feature of a central nucleus) to create surrogate restoration tasks. Evaluation uses a modified F-score that penalizes false positives and false negatives more heavily than standard F1, alongside panoptic quality (PQ) for segmentation.

## Method

The framework exploits domain-invariant biological correspondences through three complementary surrogate tasks sharing a backbone with the primary recognition branch. Tissue Correspondence Discovery (TCD) masks all nuclei pixels and restores them from surrounding tissue context via a convolutional decoder trained with pixel-matching and adversarial perceptual losses, implicitly learning nucleus-tissue dependencies. Nuclear Correspondence Discovery (NCD) masks the instance feature of one nucleus and reconstructs it from neighboring nuclei using a ViT encoder with self-attention and positional embeddings, capturing inter-nuclei spatial relationships. Self-Adaptive Dynamic Distillation (SDD) performs mutual distillation between the CNN and Transformer classification branches, weighting the distillation loss per nucleus by the ratio of predictive entropies (estimated via Monte Carlo dropout) so that morphologically ambiguous nuclei rely more on high-level contextual correspondences while unambiguous ones trust individual appearance. The overall loss aggregates the base recognition loss with all three surrogate terms, computed jointly over source and target domains.

## Results

On the primary cross-organ classification task (Table 1), the proposed method achieves class-averaged F-scores of 0.589 (Breast→Testis), 0.438 (Breast→Thyroid), and 0.488 (Breast→Bile-duct), outperforming the best baseline (HT) by 7.5, 7.2, and 5.7 percentage points respectively. For instance segmentation (Table 3), the method attains average PQ scores of 0.338, 0.220, and 0.256 across the three settings, surpassing the strongest competitor by 6.9, 4.4, and 2.6 points. Under the more challenging combined organ-and-stain shift (CoNSep↔PanNuke, Table 4), the method reaches 0.368 and 0.723 average F-scores in the two directions, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines across every cross-organ and cross-stain setting. The closest competitor is HT [5] (Harmonious Teacher), which achieves 0.514, 0.347, and 0.431 average F-scores on the three cross-organ tasks; the proposed method exceeds it by 7.5, 9.1, and 5.7 points respectively. Methods relying on category pseudo-labels (PSA [45], MGA [46], BAFA [41], CAPL-Net [24]) show particularly poor performance on epithelial cells (F-scores of 0.000–0.303), confirming the paper's argument that biased pseudo-labels cause error accumulation under domain shift. DA-RCNN [4] and PT-MAF [17], which perform instance-level or multi-scale alignment without class-aware context, trail the proposed method by 10–20 points on average F-score. The largest relative gains appear on morphologically ambiguous epithelial nuclei (e.g., 0.521 vs. 0.373 for HT on Breast→Testis), validating the core claim that contextual correspondences across biological structures are critical for distinguishing visually similar cell types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant UDA paradigm for object recognition that aligns instance-level morphological features (DA-RCNN, MGA, HT) or relies on class-conditioned pseudo-labels (PSA, CAPL-Net), arguing that both are ill-suited to histopathology where individual nuclei of different subtypes (e.g., neoplastic vs. epithelial) are morphologically near-identical. Instead, it repurposes the counterfactual masking/inpainting idea as a self-supervised pretext task to discover hierarchical biological correspondences (nucleus–tissue and nucleus–nuclei) grounded in a latent-variable causal model of pathology data genesis. The method also draws on the CNN-vs-Transformer complementarity literature, deploying a ViT branch for long-range inter-nuclei context and a Mask R-CNN branch for local instance attributes, unified via entropy-weighted mutual distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a principled, pseudo-label-free route to cross-organ and cross-stain nuclei recognition that directly addresses the most clinically relevant failure mode—confusion between morphologically ambiguous cell types—without requiring unreliable target-domain annotations. Limitations include evaluation restricted to H&E-stained 2D tiles from a handful of organs, the NCD component's adverse effect on segmentation quality due to global pooling, and the open question of whether the approach extends to concurrent class-distribution shifts or 3D volumetric data. The hierarchical correspondence framing could inform broader domain-adaptive perception tasks where object-level semantics are insufficient.
