## Problem

Existing 3D hand-object contact modeling methods rely on geometric constraints (e.g., penetration avoidance) that produce physically feasible but unrealistic contacts, such as all five fingers gripping a pair of scissors. These methods offer no mechanism for users to specify or control the contact pattern, and no dataset provides fine-grained natural language descriptions of hand-object contact to enable such control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240742/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object point cloud (2048 sampled points), a multi-level natural language contact description (spanning grasp action, grasp type, per-finger contact location, and free-finger status), and optionally an initial hand MANO pose (pose θ, shape β, translation, rotation) as input. It outputs per-point contact probability maps (values in [0,1]) on both the hand mesh (778 points) and object mesh (2048 points), which can then be used to refine the hand pose or generate a novel grasp. The text descriptions are generated by ChatGPT from hand-centered single-choice prompts, yielding five diverse sentences per grasp instance.

## Method

NL2Contact employs a two-stage latent diffusion architecture: the first stage generates a hand MANO pose from a noisy latent code conditioned on text and object features via a U-Net denoiser (using VPoser as the pose encoder/decoder), and the second stage generates the contact map conditioned on the generated hand pose, object point cloud, and text. A Text-to-Hand-Object Fusion module extracts features using BERT (text), VPoser (hand pose), and PointNet++ (object), then applies cascaded multi-head attention to fuse text with hand pose and per-point concatenation to fuse text with hand-object geometry. The generated contact map drives an iterative optimization loop that updates MANO parameters by minimizing the L2 difference between the current contact (computed via DiffContact) and the predicted contact, plus a penetration penalty. The staged design is motivated by the finding that direct text-to-contact mapping is non-trivial; decomposing into pose-then-contact significantly outperforms a single-stage diffusion or VAE alternative.

## Results

On the ContactDescribe dataset, NL2Contact achieves the lowest MPJPE of 21.7 mm, outperforming ContactOpt (25.1 mm) by 4.4 mm and S²Contact (29.4 mm) by 7.7 mm, while also reducing intersection volume to 7.1 mm³ (vs. 12.8 for ContactOpt). On the out-of-domain Described HO3D dataset, NL2Contact achieves 8.4 mm MPJPE, slightly beating S²Contact (8.7 mm) and ContactOpt (9.5 mm). For novel grasp generation on unseen HO3D objects, NL2Contact attains the lowest intersection volume (5.89 mm³), highest contact coverage (99%), highest diversity (5.91), and lowest simulation displacement (2.31 cm) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NL2Contact outperforms all geometry-only baselines on both datasets. On ContactDescribe, it beats ContactOpt [10] by 4.4 mm in MPJPE and reduces intersection volume by 44% (7.1 vs. 12.8 mm³), while also achieving substantially higher contact precision (49.2% vs. 38.7%) and recall (59.9% vs. 54.8%). S²Contact [42] is the strongest geometry-based competitor, achieving 29.4 mm MPJPE on ContactDescribe and 8.7 mm on HO3D; NL2Contact edges it out on both. On the out-of-domain HO3D set, S²Contact achieves a slightly lower intersection volume (3.52 vs. 4.39 mm³) thanks to its semi-supervised pseudo-labeling pipeline, but NL2Contact still wins on MPJPE (8.4 vs. 8.7 mm). For grasp generation, NL2Contact dominates ContactGen [27] on every metric (intersection volume 5.89 vs. 9.96, diversity 5.91 vs. 5.04, SD 2.31 vs. 2.70), while GraspTTA [18] and GrabNet [37] suffer from poor diversity (1.43 and 2.06 respectively) and higher penetration. TOCH [53] and the initial pose from Hasson et al. [14] serve as weaker references on HO3D, with MPJPEs of 9.3 and 11.4 mm respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant geometry-only paradigm for hand-object contact modeling (ContactOpt, S2Contact, ContactGen), which relies on penetration constraints and point-cloud features without any semantic control signal. It repurposes the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al.) and the VPoser/BERT/PointNet++ encoder stack to introduce natural language as a controllable conditioning input, a modality previously used only at the verb or affordance level in hand-object interaction datasets. The staged design—first generating a text-aligned hand pose, then generating the contact map conditioned on that pose—draws on the observation that direct text-to-contact cross-modal mapping is too complex for a single diffusion step, a finding validated by the ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a new control axis for contact modeling: free-form natural language descriptions that specify grasp action, finger status, and contact location, enabling applications like interactive grasp editing via ChatGPT and language-guided robotic teleoperation. The ContactDescribe dataset, while modest in scale (2,300 grasps, 25 objects), is the first to pair 3D contact maps with sentence-level descriptions, and its LLM-based annotation pipeline is a reusable recipe. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic/temporal contacts (acknowledged as future work), whether it generalizes beyond the 25 training objects without re-annotation, and whether the LLM-generated descriptions introduce systematic biases that limit diversity.
