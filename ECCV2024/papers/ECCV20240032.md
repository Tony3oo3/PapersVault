## Problem

Directly applying pre-trained feature extractors (trained on ImageNet/ShapeNet) to 3D industrial anomaly detection suffers from a severe domain gap, causing the model to either miss subtle geometric defects or misclassify abnormal regions as normal. Existing multimodal 3D anomaly detection methods perform cross-modal alignment only at the local (patch) level while neglecting intra-modal feature compactness and global (object-level) cross-modal consistency, limiting their ability to separate normal from anomalous features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240032/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired RGB images (H×W×3) and 3D point clouds (N×3) as input. 3D point features are remapped into 2D patch space via distance-based interpolation and camera projection, establishing a natural local correspondence with ViT image patches. At inference, only local (patch-level) adapted features are retained; anomaly scores are computed per pixel/voxel via PatchCore's k-NN memory bank lookup, and the two modality scores are averaged to produce the final pixel-level and voxel-level anomaly maps along with an image-level score.

## Method

LSFA inserts a single vanilla transformer encoder layer as a lightweight adaptor on top of frozen pre-trained backbones (ViT-B/8 + DINO for RGB, Point Transformer for 3D) and optimizes it with two self-supervised objectives. Intra-modal Feature Compactness (IFC) maintains dynamic-updated memory banks at both patch-level and prototype-level (via k-means clustering) and minimizes the distance between current-batch normal features and their nearest neighbors in the bank, tightening the normal-feature distribution. Cross-modal Local-to-global Consistency (CLC) applies InfoNCE-style contrastive losses at two granularities: a patch-level loss that maximizes similarity between same-location RGB and projected point-cloud features while minimizing cross-location similarity, and an instance-level loss on k-means-clustered global features. The total training loss is L_IFC + λ·L_CLC (λ=0.6), and at inference the adapted features are simply plugged into the off-the-shelf PatchCore algorithm, requiring no extra fusion module or memory bank at test time.

## Results

On MVTec-3D AD (3D+RGB modality), LSFA achieves 97.1% I-AUROC and 96.8% AUPRO, surpassing the previous SoTA AST [34] (93.7% I-AUROC) by +3.4% and M3DM [2] (94.5%) by +2.6%. On the 3D-only modality, LSFA reaches 92.1% I-AUROC, outperforming the best prior method (FPFH*/M3DM at 87.4%) by +4.7%. On the Eyecandies dataset (RGB+3D), LSFA obtains 90.6% I-AUROC, outperforming the strongest baseline PatchCore*/M3DM (89.7%) and EasyNet (86.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSFA outperforms all 15 baselines across every modality and dataset. On MVTec-3D AD (3D+RGB), LSFA (97.1% I-AUROC) beats M3DM [2] (94.5%) by +2.6% and AST [34] (93.7%) by +3.4%, with particularly large gains on challenging categories like Cable Gland (+3.0% over M3DM) and Tire (+10.1% over M3DM). On the 3D-only modality, LSFA (92.1%) surpasses FPFH*/M3DM (87.4%) by +4.7% and AST (83.3%) by +8.8%. On the RGB modality, LSFA (86.1%) edges out PatchCore*/M3DM (85.0%) and AST (88.0%) is actually higher on RGB alone, but LSFA's strength is in the multimodal setting. On Eyecandies (RGB+3D), LSFA (90.6%) outperforms PatchCore*/M3DM (89.7%) and EasyNet (86.9%). The reconstruction-based baselines (Depth/Voxel GAN/AE/VM from [11]) are consistently far behind, with mean I-AUROC below 61% on 3D+RGB, confirming the superiority of the feature-embedding paradigm with proper adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-embedding paradigm of PatchCore from 2D RGB anomaly detection to the multimodal 3D setting, but departs from prior multimodal approaches (M3DM, 3D-ST, FPFH-based baselines) that rely on explicit cross-modal feature fusion. Instead of fusing features, LSFA adapts each modality's pre-trained representations independently through two self-supervised objectives—Intra-modal Feature Compactness (IFC) and Cross-modal Local-to-global Consistency (CLC)—and simply averages the resulting per-modality anomaly scores at inference. The conceptual lineage traces from 2D feature adaptation methods (CFA's coupled-hypersphere fine-tuning) and memory-bank-based detection (PatchCore) into the 3D domain, where the authors identify and address a domain gap between ImageNet/ShapeNet pre-training and industrial inspection data that prior multimodal methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that full-network fine-tuning causes severe catastrophic forgetting (I-AUROC drops from 95.4% to 84.6% when training all blocks vs. only the last) provides important guidance for practitioners adapting pre-trained backbones to industrial anomaly detection. The lightweight adaptation strategy (a single transformer encoder layer per modality, no extra inference cost) makes the approach easy to deploy alongside existing PatchCore pipelines. Open questions include whether the memory-bank-based compactness objective generalizes to open-world industrial settings with highly variable normal distributions, and whether the k-means-based global feature construction is robust enough for categories with high intra-class variability. The method's dependence on camera parameters for 3D-to-2D projection also limits its applicability to uncalibrated or multi-view scenarios.
