## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods that train with auxiliary outlier data optimize only the value of the score function at training points, ignoring the local gradient structure of the score manifold. This leaves the network's behavior in the neighborhood of each training sample uncontrolled, making nearby points susceptible to misdetection. Additionally, when auxiliary datasets are very large, existing sampling strategies fail to select both informative and diverse OOD samples for training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240349/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a continuous energy score S_En(x) = −LSE(f(x)) that is thresholded to yield a binary ID/OOD decision. During training, it additionally consumes an auxiliary OOD dataset (300K RandomImages for CIFAR experiments; the remaining 990 ImageNet classes for the ImageNet experiment) from which informative samples are selected via clustering. The gradient of the score function with respect to the input image is computed and its norm is used as a regularization signal, requiring backpropagation through the full network.

## Method

GReg augments the standard energy loss with a gradient regularization term L_∇S that penalizes the norm of ∇_x S_En(x) only around correctly-detected samples (ID samples already below the energy threshold, OOD samples already above it), thereby promoting a locally smooth score manifold via first-order Taylor stability. The total training objective is L_CE + λ_S·L_S + λ_∇S·L_∇S, where the energy loss handles mis-scored samples and the gradient loss enforces local consistency on well-scored ones. GReg+ further couples this with an energy-based clustering sampler: auxiliary OOD features are normalized and clustered via K-Means into n_ID clusters, and from each cluster the lowest-energy sample is selected for the energy loss while the highest-energy sample is selected for the gradient loss, ensuring both informativeness and spatial diversity. The theoretical analysis connects the gradient norm to the local Lipschitz constant of the score function, showing that minimizing it increases the certified radius within which ID/OOD labels are provably stable.

## Results

On the ImageNet benchmark (10 ID classes, 990 auxiliary classes), GReg+ achieves an average FPR95 of 35.08% and AUROC of 92.06%, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art DOS by 14.23% in FPR95 and LINE by 3.68% in FPR95. On CIFAR-10, GReg reduces FPR95 to 7.93% (DenseNet), a 3.9% improvement over the Energy Loss baseline (11.26%). On CIFAR-100, GReg+ achieves 30.55% FPR95 on DenseNet, outperforming DOS (34.92%) by 1.8% on average across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GReg+ decisively outperforms DOS [ICLR24] on ImageNet, reducing average FPR95 from 49.32% to 35.08% (a 14.23% absolute improvement), while also achieving the best AUROC (92.06% vs. 91.98%). Against LINE [CVPR23], GReg+ improves FPR95 by 3.68% (35.08% vs. 39.48%) while matching its AUROC. On CIFAR-10, GReg (without sampling) beats Energy Loss [NeurIPS20] by 3.9% in FPR95 (7.93% vs. 11.26% on DenseNet) and OE [ICLR19] by 13.8% (7.93% vs. 21.76%). On CIFAR-100, GReg+ outperforms DOS by 1.8% average FPR95 and POEM [ICML22] by 28.8% (30.55% vs. 59.33% on DenseNet). GReg+ also maintains superior ID accuracy compared to DOS (89.89% vs. 87.06% on DenseNet), indicating that the gradient regularization and energy-based sampling do not degrade in-distribution performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based OOD detection framework of Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2020) by adding a gradient-norm regularizer to the score function, promoting local smoothness of the OOD score manifold around training samples. For sampling, it builds on DOS (Jiang et al., ICLR 2024) by retaining K-Means clustering over auxiliary features but replacing DOS's selection criterion with energy-score-based sampling within each cluster. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work that only optimizes the pointwise value of the score (e.g., OE, Energy Loss, OpenMix) by explicitly penalizing the local Lipschitz constant via the gradient norm, and it contrasts with Park et al. [52], which *increases* the Jacobian norm difference between ID and OOD, whereas GReg *decreases* the gradient norm in all regions to enlarge the certified detection radius.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient-regularization term is architecture- and score-function-agnostic, and the ablation showing consistent FPR95/AUROC gains when bolted onto OE, Energy, and POEM suggests it could become a standard component in OOD training pipelines. The Lipschitz-based certified-radius analysis (Eq. 4) provides a principled, if approximate, guarantee that small perturbations around correctly classified samples remain correctly classified, which is valuable for safety-critical deployment. Limitations include the continued dependence on a large auxiliary OOD dataset, a noticeable ID-accuracy drop under GReg+ (e.g., 83.06% vs. 94.84% clean on WRN-CIFAR-10), and the fact that CIFAR-100 gains are more modest than CIFAR-10, leaving open whether the approach scales to transformer backbones or truly novel, far-from-training OOD regimes.
