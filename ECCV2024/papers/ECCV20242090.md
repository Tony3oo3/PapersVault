## Problem

Existing adaptive compressed sensing methods are either heuristic or require training complex models on simulated subsampled data, making them domain-specific, costly to adapt to new modalities or subsampling schemes, and inapplicable when training data is restricted (e.g., medical imaging). No prior active acquisition algorithm combines generative priors with a training-free measurement selection strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model for the target domain (e.g., SDEdit for 256×256 faces, a custom DDPM for 640×368 knee MRI, or a DDPM for 256×256 CT) and sequentially queries the real image for linear measurements, r rows at a time over N steps. For constrained settings (MRI k-space, CT sinogram), the sensing matrix rows are restricted to rows of the Fourier or Radon transform, respectively. The output is the full adaptive sensing matrix H and a final reconstructed image, restored via posterior sampling (single sample, mean of multiple samples, or a modality-specific network).

## Method

AdaSense is a greedy sequential algorithm that, at each step, generates s posterior samples from p(x | y_{0:nr}) using a zero-shot diffusion-based sampler (DDRM), then applies PCA to these samples to estimate the top eigenvectors of the posterior covariance Cov[x | y_{0:nr}]. In the unconstrained case, the next r measurement directions are simply these top eigenvectors; in the constrained case (e.g., MRI/CT), it selects the r best rows from a predefined feasible set by maximizing the expected variance captured. A key efficiency insight is that because the posterior sampler is measurement-consistent, previously acquired directions have zero posterior variance, so the SVD of the accumulated sensing matrix reduces to the identity, eliminating costly repeated SVD computations. For large r (e.g., full k-space columns), a sub-optimal approximation fixes the reconstruction matrix to the pseudo-inverse, reducing the selection to an exhaustive scan over feasible rows.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ 256×256 face images with 192 measurements, AdaSense achieves PSNR 26.20 dB, SSIM 0.7515, and LPIPS 0.1950, outperforming the optimal non-adaptive PCA baseline (24.60 dB PSNR) by 1.6 dB and all other non-adaptive subsampling strategies. For active MRI k-space acquisition (R8-30L), AdaSense reaches PSNR 28.89 dB and SSIM 0.6108, approaching the Greedy Oracle upper bound (29.22 dB) while requiring no task-specific training. In sparse-view CT, AdaSense selects projection angles that yield consistent PSNR/SSIM gains over fixed-angle baselines across multiple acceleration factors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RL-based active MRI methods from Pineda et al. [51] (SS-DDQN and DS-DDQN), AdaSense trails by approximately 0.1 dB PSNR and 0.002–0.014 SSIM at both acceleration factors (R8-30L and R16-2L), while remaining within 0.33 dB of the Greedy Oracle upper bound. Compared to Bakker et al.'s ReducingUncertainty [78], AdaSense gains 0.23 dB PSNR and 0.009 SSIM at R8-30L, and 1.08 dB PSNR and 0.016 SSIM at R16-2L. The non-adaptive Low-to-High baseline [51] is outperformed by AdaSense by 0.57 dB PSNR at R8-30L and 1.85 dB at R16-2L. Crucially, AdaSense achieves these results without any task-specific training or exposure to degraded data during model training, whereas all RL-based competitors require extensive fine-tuning on simulated subsampled data for each specific subsampling scheme.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSense sits at the intersection of two research threads: classical adaptive compressed sensing (which sequentially selects measurements to minimize reconstruction error) and zero-shot diffusion-based posterior sampling for linear inverse problems (DDRM, SDEdit, DPS, etc.). The key conceptual departure from prior adaptive CS work is that instead of training a policy network or heuristic on simulated subsampled data, AdaSense simply draws posterior samples from a pre-trained diffusion model and applies PCA to the empirical covariance to choose the next measurement direction. This reframes the adaptive measurement-selection problem as a straightforward application of the PCA principle (already implicit in non-adaptive CS theory) to the conditional posterior, making the method training-free and domain-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single pre-trained diffusion model can drive active acquisition across modalities (faces, MRI k-space, CT sinogram) without any task-specific fine-tuning, which is particularly valuable in medical imaging where training data is scarce and acquisition protocols vary. Key limitations include the computational cost of generating multiple posterior samples per step, dependence on the quality of the underlying sampler, and restriction to linear measurements. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear or sequential degradation models, how to accelerate the sampling loop (e.g., reusing previously generated samples), and whether clinical validation is feasible given current inference speeds.
