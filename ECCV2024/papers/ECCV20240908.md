## Problem

Masked image modeling (MIM) methods like MAE exhibit a limited attention span, with attention maps showing that the encoder focuses only on near patches regardless of whether the query lies in foreground or background. This local dependency arises because the MIM loss supervises only masked tokens for pixel reconstruction, leaving unmasked tokens to be trained implicitly without explicit broader-context supervision, which hinders the encoder from learning comprehensive region-wide dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240908/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images patchified into 16×16 non-overlapping patches (196 tokens for ViT-B/16), with 75% randomly masked. During pre-training, the online encoder processes only the sparse unmasked patches while a momentum context encoder processes the full augmented image; the decoder reconstructs masked pixel values. Downstream, the pretrained encoder produces visual representations used for classification (ImageNet-1K, iNaturalist, FGVC datasets) or dense prediction (ADE20K segmentation via UperNet at 512×512).

## Method

LUT augments the standard MAE objective with a broader contextualization loss (L_BC) that explicitly supervises unmasked tokens using full-image context. A momentum-updated context encoder (mirroring the online encoder architecture) processes the entire augmented image, and its pooled token representation is matched to the pooled representation of the sparse unmasked tokens from the online encoder via cosine distance, weighted by α (0.25 for ViT-B/16). This forces the unmasked tokens to encode extended contextual information, which mask tokens can then leverage during reconstruction. The method uses simple resized crop (SRC) instead of random resized crop (RRC) to keep the two views geometrically aligned, and applies distinct MLP heads on each encoder to prevent optimization collapse. The total training cost increases by only ~18% (one extra forward pass) over baseline MAE.

## Results

LUT achieves 84.2% top-1 accuracy with ViT-B/16 on ImageNet-1K, a +0.5%p gain over MAE (83.7%), and 86.0% with ViT-L/16 (+0.4%p over MAE's 85.6%). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, LUT reaches 49.5% mIoU, outperforming MAE (48.1%) and matching supervised DeiT-III (49.3%). On iNaturalist 2019, LUT attains 81.0% fine-tuning accuracy, surpassing data2vec (80.6%) and MAE (80.2%). LUT also leads on robustness benchmarks, achieving 42.5% on ImageNet-A and 40.7 on SI-loc, both best among compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LUT outperforms its direct baseline MAE by +0.5%p on ViT-B/16 (84.2% vs 83.7%) and +0.4%p on ViT-L/16 (86.0% vs 85.6%), while also improving linear probing by +2%p. Against the strongest SSL competitor data2vec, LUT edges ahead on ViT-B (84.2% vs 84.1%) but trails on ViT-L (86.0% vs 86.6%), and surpasses it on ADE20K (49.5% vs 48.3% mIoU). LUT matches supervised DeiT-III (83.8%) and Cosub (84.2%) on ViT-B while using no labels, and exceeds both on ADE20K (49.5% vs 49.3%). On transfer tasks, LUT consistently leads: iNaturalist 2019 (81.0% vs 80.6% data2vec, 80.2% MAE), FGVC average (91.1 vs 90.7 iBOT, 90.1 MAE), and robustness metrics (42.5% ImageNet-A vs 41.6% data2vec; 40.7 SI-loc vs 38.7 MAE). The only notable loss is on ViT-L ImageNet-1K where data2vec reaches 86.6%, and on ADE20K where multi-crop iBOT achieves 50.0% mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

LUT builds directly on MAE's masked image modeling framework, adding a broader contextualization loss that explicitly supervises unmasked tokens via a momentum encoder processing the full (unmasked) image. This departs from standard MIM formulations where only masked tokens receive explicit reconstruction supervision, and from contrastive MIM methods (CMAE, ConMIM) that combine contrastive objectives with all patches. The momentum-encoder architecture borrows from BYOL/MoCo-style self-distillation, but repurposes it not for contrastive alignment or feature prediction (as in data2vec) but for delivering pooled contextual supervision to sparse unmasked tokens, enabling them to carry richer information for mask tokens to attend to during reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic diagnosis (local attention maps, short average attention distance) for a known weakness of MIM pre-training and offers a minimal, plug-and-play remedy that consistently improves across classification, segmentation, fine-grained transfer, and robustness benchmarks. The singular value spectrum analysis showing higher-rank feature spaces in later layers gives a useful interpretive lens for why broader context helps. However, the gains are modest (0.5–0.6%p on ImageNet-1K fine-tuning), scalability beyond ViT-L is unverified, and the ~18% training overhead, while small, is non-trivial at scale. An open question is whether the benefit persists or grows with model size (ViT-H/G) and whether the approach transfers to other MIM variants beyond MAE and SimMIM.
