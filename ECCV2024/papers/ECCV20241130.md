## Problem

Reward specification for visual RL in robotic manipulation is labor-intensive and often infeasible with dense hand-crafted rewards, while sparse rewards suffer from poor sample efficiency. Existing approaches that extract rewards from video prediction models (e.g., VIPER with VideoGPT) struggle to accurately model complex expert video distributions, producing uninformative or inconsistent rewards—especially on out-of-distribution trajectories and tasks with intricate dynamics. Extracting informative dense rewards from the superior generative capabilities of video diffusion models remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB observations (with ±4 pixel shift augmentation) and a short history of 2 prior frames as conditioning context, alongside a binary 0/1 sparse task reward from the environment. Expert videos used for pretraining are action-free, collected from multiple tasks without task labels, and compressed via VQ-GAN into discrete latent token sequences. The output is a dense scalar reward per timestep, computed by running a single DDIM denoising pass (10 steps) through the conditional diffusion model and estimating the variational bound of the negative conditional entropy, then combining it with an RND exploration term and the sparse reward via fixed coefficients.

## Method

Diffusion Reward pretrains a VQ-Diffusion model on action-free expert videos, conditioning the reverse denoising process on 2 historical latent frames to capture temporal structure. The key insight is that conditioning on expert-like trajectories yields lower generative diversity (lower conditional entropy) than conditioning on expert-unlike ones, so the negative conditional entropy serves as a discriminative reward signal. Because the conditional distribution has no closed form, the entropy is estimated via a variational bound using the closed-form discrete distribution of VQ tokens, averaged over M stochastic denoising samples. The entropy reward is standardized by its empirical mean and standard deviation over expert videos to mitigate cross-task scale variation, then combined with an RND novelty-seeking reward (coefficient α≈0.95) and the sparse task reward (coefficient β=1). The inherent stochasticity of the diffusion reverse process provides additional exploration signal, and DDIM sampling with only 10 denoising steps keeps per-step reward inference fast enough for online RL.

## Results

On 7 MetaWorld gripper manipulation tasks, Diffusion Reward achieves a 38% average success-rate improvement over the best-performing baseline, and on 3 Adroit dexterous manipulation tasks, a 35% improvement. The method also demonstrates zero-shot generalization to 5 unseen MetaWorld tasks, where it largely surpasses all baselines without any task-specific tuning. On the real-robot Button Press task, Diffusion Reward produces a more generalizable offline RL policy that successfully presses a button at an unseen position. Supporting video-model quality metrics show VQ-Diffusion outperforms VideoGPT (SSIM 0.856 vs. 0.768; PSNR 27.46 vs. 23.35 on real dexterous manipulation videos).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RND (novelty-seeking only), Diffusion Reward consistently outperforms across all 10 tasks, with the gap widening on complex dexterous tasks (Door, Hammer) where pure exploration in high-dimensional configuration spaces is insufficient. Against AMP (adversarial motion priors), Diffusion Reward wins on all tasks; AMP's state-only discriminator underutilizes temporal information and its performance degrades notably on Lever Pull and Hammer. Against VIPER (VideoGPT log-likelihood reward), Diffusion Reward achieves the largest margin: VIPER unexpectedly underperforms even RND and AMP on several tasks due to the varied scale of log-likelihood rewards, and even the standardized variant (VIPER-std) remains inferior, particularly on complex tasks like Coffee Push and Pen. On the 5 unseen MetaWorld tasks, Diffusion Reward generalizes zero-shot and largely surpasses VIPER, while AMP is excluded because it requires task-specific expert data for online reward learning. On the real-robot Button Press task, Diffusion Reward significantly outperforms VIPER in offline RL success rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-from-video line initiated by VIPER (Escontrela et al., NeurIPS 2023), which used VideoGPT's predicted log-likelihood as a dense reward signal. The key departure is twofold: replacing the autoregressive VideoGPT with a VQ-Diffusion latent video model (Gu et al., CVPR 2022) for superior distributional modeling, and replacing log-likelihood with the negative conditional entropy of the diffusion process, which captures generative diversity and is inherently stochastic. The paper also positions itself against adversarial reward methods (AMP) and pure novelty-seeking (RND), arguing that the combination of expert-instructed diffusion rewards with exploration rewards is necessary for complex manipulation tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a reward model pretrained solely on action-free expert videos can transfer zero-shot to unseen manipulation tasks, a property not previously shown for video-based reward learning. This opens a path toward scalable reward specification where a single pretrained diffusion model serves as a general-purpose reward oracle across diverse tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to 64×64-pixel images and 10 tasks from two simulators, and the real-robot experiments involve only two simple tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution, longer-horizon, or multi-modal (language-conditioned) settings, and whether the conditional entropy estimation can be made more efficient than the current M-sample Monte Carlo approach.
