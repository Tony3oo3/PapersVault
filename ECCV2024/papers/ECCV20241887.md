## Problem

Medical report generation from chest X-rays typically requires paired image-report data, but such pairs are scarce due to privacy constraints and labeling costs. Existing unpaired methods (KGAE, MedCycle) rely on hand-designed knowledge graphs or pseudo-report cycle-consistency, introducing external design choices that are hard to generalize. The key unresolved question is how to align image and report representations in a shared space when no pair correspondence exists, using only the multi-label pathology annotations available for each modality independently.

## Input / Output

At inference the model receives a single chest X-ray image and produces a free-text radiology report. During training, two separate unpaired datasets are used: CheXpert images (224K radiographs with 14 multi-label pathology annotations) and MIMIC-CXR reports (222K reports with the same label schema). The only cross-modal signal is the shared pathology label set; no image-report pairs are available. The model is evaluated zero-shot on IU X-ray, where it was never exposed to any data during training.

## Method

MedRAT combines report auto-encoding with multi-modal alignment through a shared Transformer encoder-decoder and a modality-agnostic memory module. The encoder produces local representations (per word or per image patch) that are aggregated into a global representation via learned self-attention weights. Two auxiliary tasks operate on the global representations in the shared space: (1) supervised contrastive learning that pulls together image-report pairs sharing at least one pathology and pushes apart non-matching pairs, and (2) multi-label classification that forces each sample's embedding to be independently decodable into its pathology labels, compensating for the imprecision of binary positive/negative pair definitions in a multi-label setting. A shared memory matrix, queried by cosine similarity and updated end-to-end, injects learned domain knowledge at the feature-extraction level. The decoder consumes both global and local representations, with dropout or Gaussian noise augmentation on local vectors to prevent overfitting during auto-encoding.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, MedRAT achieves BLEU-1 of 0.365, METEOR of 0.132, ROUGE-L of 0.251, RadGraph F1 of 0.138, and BERTScore of 0.398, outperforming both KGAE and MedCycle on all NLG metrics. On IU X-ray (zero-shot, no training data from this dataset), MedRAT reaches BLEU-1 of 0.455, RadGraph F1 of 0.204, and BERTScore of 0.427. For clinical efficacy on MIMIC-CXR, MedRAT achieves F1 of 0.227 (precision 0.285, recall 0.265), a ~10% recall improvement over the prior best unpaired method. With only 1% of paired data added, MedRAT reaches 99% of the best paired method's BLEU-1 score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KGAE [29], MedRAT wins on every reported metric on both MIMIC-CXR and IU X-ray, with the largest gains in BLEU-1 (+0.144 on MIMIC, +0.038 on IU) and BERTScore (+0.398 vs. not reported for KGAE on MIMIC). Against MedCycle [14], MedRAT dominates on MIMIC-CXR across all six NLG metrics (e.g., BLEU-1 0.365 vs. 0.309, BERTScore 0.398 vs. 0.333) and on clinical F1 (0.227 vs. 0.183). On IU X-ray, MedRAT wins on RadGraph F1 (0.204 vs. 0.196) and BERTScore (0.427 vs. 0.308) but trails MedCycle on BLEU-1 (0.455 vs. 0.461), BLEU-4 (0.129 vs. 0.143), and METEOR (0.171 vs. 0.182). Compared to fully paired methods (R2Gen-CMN, COMG+RL, KiUT, UAR), MedRAT with 0% pairs reaches 93% of the best paired BLEU-1 and 85% of best ROUGE-L; adding just 1% of paired data pushes BLEU-1 to 99% of the best paired score, substantially narrowing the paired–unpaired gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedRAT addresses unpaired chest X-ray report generation, a setting previously tackled by KGAE (which relies on a hand-constructed knowledge graph to bridge modalities) and MedCycle (which uses cycle-consistency and pseudo-reports). Rather than designing external structures or synthetic intermediates, MedRAT repurposes two standard auxiliary tasks—multi-label contrastive learning and multi-label classification—applied in a shared embedding space to achieve cross-modal alignment without any pair correspondence. The architecture also draws on shared-memory mechanisms from image captioning and self-attention-based global pooling, but combines them in a modality-agnostic encoder-decoder that simultaneously auto-encodes reports and aligns image-report representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for paired image-report data, MedRAT directly addresses the privacy and data-scarcity bottlenecks that limit medical report generation research, and the few-shot result (1% paired data nearly closing the gap to fully paired methods) offers a practical pathway for institutions that can only label a small subset. The ablation showing that both contrastive learning and classification are individually insufficient—each correcting a specific failure mode of the other in the multi-label setting—provides a useful design principle for multi-modal alignment under partial supervision. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond chest X-rays to other modalities (CT, MRI), whether the shared memory can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives, and how the method handles longitudinal reports that reference prior examinations.
