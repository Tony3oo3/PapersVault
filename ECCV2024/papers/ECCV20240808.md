## Problem

Translation-based multi-modal registration methods that use GANs (e.g., CycleGAN) to harmonize image appearance before registration frequently introduce geometric inconsistencies and suffer from mode collapse, degrading alignment accuracy. Existing unsupervised similarity metrics (SSD, NCC, NMI) each fail in specific multi-modal scenarios, making it difficult to define a single effective registration objective. The paper addresses both issues by replacing the GAN translator with an invertible architecture and augmenting NMI with a barrier formulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of unaligned 2D medical image slices (192×192) from different modalities—MRI T1/T2 or MRI/CT—and produces a 2D deformation field φ that warps the source image onto the target. Although the translation network is trained jointly with the registration network, only the registration network (a U-Net with residual connections) is deployed at inference. Training pairs are synthesized by applying random B-spline deformations to originally aligned scans from BraTS 2023 (60 pairs, 54/6 split) and the Harvard Whole Brain Atlas (920/92 split).

## Method

INNReg couples an 8-block RealNVP-style invertible translation network (affine coupling layers with shared DenseBlocks) with a U-Net registration network, trained end-to-end. The INN's forward pass translates the source image into the target modality domain while its inverse pass enforces content preservation via an L1 cross-translation loss, guaranteeing geometric consistency that GAN-based translators lack. A dynamic depthwise convolution (DDWConv) module is embedded in the INN to provide local attention, letting the translator focus on specific spatial regions for finer detail extraction. A novel barrier NMI loss, −log(b − I_NMI), is applied to the registration network: it grows unbounded as NMI approaches a threshold b, penalizing severe geometric distortion from the translator while remaining negligible when alignment is already good. The total objective combines pixel-wise L1 reconstruction, barrier NMI, INN cross-translation, and deformation smoothness losses with weights 1, 1, 0.1, and 10 respectively.

## Results

On the T1/T2 MRI dataset (BraTS 2023), INNReg achieves the best SSIM (90.56%), Dice (87.12%), and smoothness (0.101%), with near-best NCC (98.75%) and HD95 (101.37). On the MRI/CT dataset (Harvard Whole Brain Atlas), INNReg attains the best SSIM (73.07%), NCC (89.89%), and smoothness (0.179%), substantially outperforming Voxelmorph (54.74% SSIM, 55.90% NCC) and all translation-based baselines. The method consistently produces the smoothest deformation fields among methods with meaningful registration, avoiding the degenerate near-zero deformation seen in Arar et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Voxelmorph (VM), INNReg wins on every metric for both datasets, with particularly large gains on MRI/CT (SSIM 73.07 vs. 54.74; NCC 89.89 vs. 55.90) and a 27-point improvement in HD95 on T1/T2 (101.37 vs. 128.32). Against Arar et al. (CVPR 2020), INNReg dominates on SSIM, NCC, Dice, and HD95 for T1/T2 and on SSIM and NCC for MRI/CT; Arar's near-zero smoothness (0.007%) is an artefact of its translation network absorbing the deformation rather than the registration network performing meaningful alignment. Against RegGAN (NeurIPS 2021), INNReg improves SSIM by 0.58 (T1/T2) and 1.43 (MRI/CT), Dice by 2.47, and smoothness by 0.13 (T1/T2) and 0.59 (MRI/CT). Against Chen et al. (IJCAI 2022), INNReg leads on SSIM, Dice, HD95, and smoothness for T1/T2 and on SSIM, NCC, and smoothness for MRI/CT, while Chen retains a marginal NCC edge on T1/T2 (98.89 vs. 98.75). Overall, INNReg is the only method that simultaneously achieves top or near-top scores across all five metrics on T1/T2 and all three metrics on MRI/CT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the registration-by-translation paradigm for multi-modal image registration by replacing the GAN-based translation component (CycleGAN, RegGAN, Arar et al.) with an Invertible Neural Network built on RealNVP-style affine coupling layers. The key conceptual departure is that invertibility inherently guarantees geometric consistency during modality translation, addressing a well-known failure mode of GAN-based translators that introduce anatomical distortions. The local attention mechanism is adapted from Han et al.'s analysis of dynamic depthwise convolution as a lightweight local self-attention equivalent, and the barrier NMI loss wraps the standard NMI similarity metric in a barrier function to impose a hard upper-bound constraint on the registration network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric-consistency guarantee from invertibility offers a principled alternative to GAN-based translation in registration pipelines, potentially reducing the need for complex cycle-consistency or commutativity constraints. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D slices from two relatively small datasets (60 T1/T2 pairs, 920 MRI/CT pairs), and it remains open whether the approach scales to full 3D volumes or more challenging modality pairs (e.g., PET/MRI). The barrier NMI loss is a simple but effective trick whose optimal threshold selection (b=5 here) may not generalize across datasets or modalities without retuning.
