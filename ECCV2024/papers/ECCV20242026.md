## Problem

Existing conditional distribution alignment methods for domain adaptation rely on discrete label variables and cannot handle the continuous, infinite label space of regression tasks. Current DAR methods only align marginal distributions, which is theoretically insufficient and leads to misaligned local structure and negative transfer. This paper addresses the fundamental obstacle of characterizing and minimizing conditional distribution discrepancy when the conditioning variable is continuous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242026/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled RGB source images and unlabeled RGB target images (e.g., 64×64 dSprites, 128×128 Biwi Kinect, 64×64 MPI3D) and produces continuous regression values such as object scale, 2D coordinates, head pose angles (pitch/yaw/roll), or 3D rotation angles. A ResNet-18 backbone extracts representations, and a predictor head maps them to scalar continuous outputs. Evaluation uses the sum of MAE across multiple sub-regression factors per dataset.

## Method

The paper introduces the Conditional Operator Discrepancy (COD), a metric on conditional distributions defined in RKHS via the conditional mean operator U_{X|Y} and conditional covariance operator C_{XX|Y}, analogous to the Kernel Gaussian Wasserstein distance but applied to conditional statistics. A key theoretical result (Theorem 2) proves that aligning P_{Z|Y} across domains suffices to bound the joint source-target error for any predictor, extending the discrete classification sufficiency theory to continuous labels. The empirical COD is reformulated by removing intra-class similarity terms (which would push same-label samples apart) to preserve discriminability, yielding a modified COD that maximizes cross-domain intra-class alignment. The full training objective combines MSE on source labels, the modified COD for conditional alignment, and KGW for marginal alignment, with the marginal term stabilizing early pseudo-label quality before conditional alignment takes effect.

## Results

On dSprites, COD achieves an average MAE of 0.134 across six transfer tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline DARE-GRAM (0.164) by ~18% and reducing error to only 40% of SOTA methods on the hardest tasks (C→N, C→S). On Biwi Kinect, COD reaches 0.205 average MAE versus 0.260 for DARE-GRAM. On MPI3D, COD is competitive with DARE-GRAM (0.192 vs. 0.160 average MAE), winning on RL→T (0.11 vs. 0.15) but trailing on T→RL and T→RC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COD outperforms all eight baselines on every dSprites task, with the largest gains over classification-oriented methods (DANN: 0.315 avg, DAN: 0.377) and a consistent 18–40% MAE reduction over the strongest DAR-specific baselines RSD (0.237) and DARE-GRAM (0.164). On Biwi Kinect, COD (0.205 avg) beats DARE-GRAM (0.260) by 21% and RSD (0.280) by 27%, confirming the advantage of conditional alignment on real-world data. On MPI3D, COD (0.192 avg) trails DARE-GRAM (0.160) overall, losing on T→RL (0.37 vs. 0.24) and T→RC (0.29 vs. 0.24), but wins on RL→T (0.11 vs. 0.15) and is competitive on the remaining four tasks. The ablation confirms that the modified COD term (d_COD_mod) provides additional gains over the unmodified COD, and that combining KGW (marginal) with COD (conditional) is necessary for the hardest dSprites transfers where pseudo-labels are unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Kernel Gaussian Wasserstein (KGW) distance in RKHS [Zhang et al., 2019] to the conditional setting, defining the Conditional Operator Discrepancy (COD) as a conditional Wasserstein distance over RKHS-embedded distributions. It builds on the generalization error theory for domain adaptation from Zhao et al. [2019] and Tachet des Combes et al. [2020], which established sufficiency of conditional alignment for classification, and extends this to the continuous-label regression setting where per-class alignment is infeasible. The key departure from prior DAR methods (RSD, DARE-GRAM) is shifting from marginal distribution alignment to conditional alignment, using the conditional mean and covariance operators from kernel regression literature [Fukumizu et al., 2009] as the alignment targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a sufficiency guarantee for conditional alignment in regression and a practical metric (COD) that avoids the infinite per-label matching problem inherent to continuous outputs. The discriminability modification—removing intra-class similarity terms from the first-order statistic—addresses a known failure mode of MMD-style alignment and is validated empirically. Limitations include evaluation only on three datasets with ResNet-18, mixed results on MPI3D (worse than DARE-GRAM on T→RL and T→RC), and reliance on pseudo-labels whose quality depends on the predictor's early-training accuracy. Open questions include whether COD scales to deeper architectures, larger label spaces, or multi-output regression, and whether the Gaussian assumption in the RKHS embedding holds for complex real-world distributions.
