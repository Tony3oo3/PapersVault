## Problem

Existing 3D generative models produce objects in isolation, without adapting their appearance to the target scene where they will be placed. Prior works address individual sub-problems—2D scene-object compositing, single-image light estimation, or mesh texturing—but none jointly solve the coupled task of scene-conditional texture adaptation and photorealistic composition while preserving object geometry and identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241821/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a textured 3D mesh (with UV coordinates), a 2D scene image, and a 2D placement location as input. It produces a 5-channel PBR texture map (diffuse RGB, roughness, metalness) parameterized as a neural network over 3D coordinates, an HDR environment map for lighting, and a final photorealistic composite image. Because the texture is defined in 3D space, the adapted object can be re-posed or extracted as a standalone asset without re-optimization, and the HDR map is used solely to light the object rather than to re-render the full scene.

## Method

The framework optimizes a neural PBR texture and an HDR environment map jointly via differentiable ray tracing (Mitsuba3) guided by Stable Diffusion in latent space, using an interpolated VSD objective that blends SDS and a LoRA-tuned VSD network to counteract over-texturization. A key design choice is random rotation of the object during optimization, which prevents lighting from being baked into the texture and preserves 3D geometry, but necessitates a separate light-capturing apparatus: a virtual white diffuse sphere placed in the scene whose reflected intensity anchors the HDR light-scale estimation. Scene-conditional guidance is achieved through three complementary mechanisms: LLM-generated (GPT-4) text prompts describing environmental impact on the object, reference feature injection into the U-Net's attention layers for identity preservation, and global-view inpainting guidance with solid-color background augmentation for seamless blending. The LDR environment map is initialized differently for indoor scenes (depth-based 3D lifting and spherical unwrapping) versus outdoor scenes (single-view panorama outpainting), then converted to HDR by optimizing per-region light multipliers.

## Results

On 102 composite images (34 object-scene pairs × 3 views), the full method (T.A.+L.E.) achieves the best LPIPS of 0.0015 (lower is better, measuring controllability/identity preservation) and the highest CLIP_dir (0.0206) and CLIP_SI (0.2013) scores for composability. Texture adaptation alone (T.A.) already outperforms InstructPix2Pix on all three metrics (LPIPS 0.0014 vs. 0.0020, CLIP_dir 0.0201 vs. 0.0175, CLIP_SI 0.2006 vs. 0.1852). In a user study with 42 participants, the method is preferred over CDC in 71% of cases and over InstructPix2Pix in 74% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CDC [18] (scene-object compositing), the proposed method wins decisively: LPIPS is 3.8× lower (0.0014 vs. 0.0053), CLIP_dir is ~5× higher (0.0201 vs. 0.0042), and user preference is 71% vs. 9%. CDC loses structural details of the object and produces compositions where objects appear to float. Against InstructPix2Pix [3] (used as a copy-paste baseline for texture adaptation), the method wins on all three metrics and is preferred by 74% of users. The three mesh-texturing baselines—ProlificDreamer [55], Fantasia3D [7], and TEXTure [45]—are compared only qualitatively (Fig. 6); the paper reports that while they achieve realistic texture adaptation, they fail to preserve original structural details and do not leverage scene-level information, thus not meeting the full Environmental Influence + Identity Preservation + Blending criteria. Light estimation (L.E.) provides a small but consistent improvement over texture adaptation alone across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—3D mesh texturing via diffusion guidance (DreamFusion/SDS, ProlificDreamer/VSD), scene-object compositing (CDC, OST), and single-image light estimation (EVL, IGAN)—into a single differentiable rendering pipeline. It departs from prior 2D compositing methods by explicitly optimizing a PBR texture map in 3D space using Mitsuba3 ray tracing, and from prior texturing methods by conditioning the optimization on a specific 2D scene rather than text alone. The identity preservation mechanism adapts the reference feature injection from zero-shot image-to-image translation (Parmar et al., Tumanyan et al.) to the 3D texture optimization loop, while the virtual white-sphere 'light-capturing apparatus' is a novel conceptual bridge between classical inverse rendering and diffusion-model-based scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for 3D content pipelines (games, media design) where artists currently manually retune textures per environment; the output is a reusable PBR texture map plus an HDR environment map, not just a single 2D composite. Key limitations include the per-scene optimization cost, dependence on GPT-4 for prompt generation, and the fact that the method requires a textured mesh input (it does not generate geometry). Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object scenes with inter-object lighting, whether the LLM prompt step can be replaced by learned conditioning, and how robust the method is to geometrically complex or highly reflective objects.
