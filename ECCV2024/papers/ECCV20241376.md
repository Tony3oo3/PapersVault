## Problem

Existing rotation-equivariant point cloud methods face a trade-off: discretizing the SO(3) group loses rigorous continuous symmetry, while harmonic-analysis-based approaches (e.g., SE(3)-Transformer, Tensor Field Networks) enforce strict equivariance but are computationally prohibitive. No prior method achieves both continuous SO(3) equivariance and practical efficiency for irregularly sampled point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241376/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unordered set of N 3D points (coordinates in R³), which are L2-normalized onto S² to serve as spherical coordinates; the only initial per-point feature is the Euclidean distance from the centroid. For classification, the network outputs a 40-dim logit vector; for retrieval, it outputs an L2-normalized embedding in a learned metric space optimized with triplet loss. The global feature is rotation-invariant by construction, requiring no augmentation at test time.

## Method

CSEConv reformulates SO(3) group convolution as an equivalent operation on the quotient space S² = SO(3)/SO(2) via a section map, reducing the intractable 4D integration to a 2D spherical integral. The filter is constrained to depend only on the polar angle θ (the double coset space SO(2)\SO(3)/SO(2)), which automatically enforces SO(2) invariance and hence SO(3) equivariance without explicit symmetry operations. Instead of tensor-shaped grid parameters, the filter is a small weight-sharing MLP with Fourier feature mapping on θ, making it continuous, explicit, and parameter-efficient. Integration is approximated by KNN averaging over local neighborhoods, and the full model stacks CSEConv layers with farthest-point-sampling downsampling, batch normalization, and a shared PointNet-style MLP, followed by max-pooling to produce the global rotation-invariant descriptor.

## Results

On ModelNet40, CSEConv achieves 83.79% accuracy (I/I) and maintains 83.75% under random SO(3) test rotations without training augmentation (I/SO(3)), the best among all continuous-group equivariant methods. On ScanObjectNN, it reaches 72.29% (I/I), outperforming Vector Neurons (56.92%), SE(3)-Transformer (59.21%), and Frame Averaging (71.94%). For retrieval on ModelNet40, CSEConv attains 79.66 mAP without any rotation augmentation during training, matching EPN (79.7) and surpassing KPConv (77.5) and PointNet++ (70.3). In terms of efficiency, CSEConv uses only 3.03 GB training memory and processes 27.46 batches/sec, compared to 8.50 GB / 1.71 batches/sec for SE(3)-Transformer and 15.19 GB / 5.09 batches/sec for Frame Averaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSEConv outperforms all continuous-group equivariant baselines on both ModelNet40 and ScanObjectNN: it leads Vector Neurons by ~5.5 pp, Frame Averaging by ~1.5 pp, and SE(3)-Transformer by ~12 pp on ModelNet40 (I/I), and by 15.4 pp over VN and 13.1 pp over SE3-T on ScanObjectNN. The most striking comparison is against discrete-group methods EPN and E2PN under the I/SO(3) setting (no training augmentation, random test rotations): CSEConv holds at 83.75% while EPN collapses to 31.08% and E2PN to 44.47%, a gap of over 40 pp, confirming that group discretization introduces a severe bias toward the sampled rotations. Against non-equivariant methods (PointNet++, KPConv, PCT) trained with SO(3) augmentation, CSEConv trained without augmentation achieves comparable accuracy (83.75% vs. 80–84%), demonstrating that structural equivariance can replace data augmentation. On retrieval, CSEConv (79.66 mAP) matches EPN (79.7) while using no augmentation, and surpasses KPConv (77.5) and PointNet++ (70.3). In efficiency, CSEConv is 16× faster than SE(3)-Transformer and 5× faster than Frame Averaging in training throughput, with the lowest GPU memory footprint (3.03 GB vs. 8.50 GB and 15.19 GB respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

CSEConv builds directly on the homogeneous-space convolution framework of Cohen et al. [9,8], which rewrites group convolution on SO(3) as an equivalent operation on the quotient space S², and on E2PN [48]'s identification of the SO(2) invariance constraint on the filter. The key departure is in the filter implementation: rather than discretizing the group (EPN, E2PN) or computing expensive harmonic bases (SE3-T, Vector Neurons, Spherical CNN), CSEConv confines the filter to the 1D double coset space (the polar angle θ) and parameterizes it as a weight-sharing MLP with Fourier feature encoding, yielding a continuous, explicitly equivariant, and computationally lightweight convolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is the ~16× training speedup over SE3-T and ~3× over E2PN while maintaining equivariance error comparable to strictly equivariant methods, making continuous SO(3) equivariance feasible at scale for point cloud pipelines. However, the method still trails non-equivariant models trained with augmentation by several points on ModelNet40 (83.8% vs 91.3%), and the authors acknowledge difficulty with subtle local geometries (e.g., distinguishing flower_pot from vase). Open questions include extending the framework to genuinely equivariant tasks (normal/pose estimation) where the feature-space group action is non-trivial, and whether the single-coordinate filter can be enriched without sacrificing the efficiency advantage.
