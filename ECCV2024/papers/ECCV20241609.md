## Problem

Existing text-to-image customization methods (Textual Inversion, Dreambooth) rely on the model's adequate prior knowledge of common objects to localize and learn concepts, but logos—characterized by unique patterns, multilingual text, and diverse compositions—lack such shared prior knowledge. This creates two specific gaps: diffusion models cannot reliably convey the 'painted on' spatial relation for arbitrary objects, and standard class-name tokens fail to attend to logo regions in training images, making precise identity extraction infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user-provided logo image (containing fine-grained patterns, English or Chinese text, and intricate calligraphy) and a free-form text prompt describing the target context (e.g., 'a mug with <V> painted on it'). It produces a generated image at standard Stable Diffusion resolution where the logo appears coherently on the specified object, preserving its identity, colors, and textual details even on curved surfaces or under varied viewing angles. The pipeline also supports inpainting logos onto user-provided images and multi-concept customization by simply adding context images to the training set.

## Method

LogoSticker is a two-phase pipeline built on Stable Diffusion. Phase 1 (Actor-Critic Relation Pre-training) fine-tunes a special text token <painted> and the text encoder on a curated dataset of 20 objects (shirts, mugs, hats, etc.) with commonplace 'object1s' (dogs, apples, stars) to learn the 'painted on' relation; a CLIP model acts as a critic, scoring generation quality per object and dynamically adjusting sampling probabilities so under-learned objects receive more training, yielding a stronger and more balanced relation. Phase 2 (Decoupled Identity Learning) splits logo learning into two sub-steps: first, the logo is pasted on solid-color backgrounds and a special token <V> is optimized via Textual Inversion (U-Net frozen) to bind the token to the logo region; second, the logo is pasted on diverse natural scenes and the U-Net is fine-tuned with <V> in the prompt to distill the full logo identity into the token, enabling precise and contextually coherent logo generation.

## Results

On the logo insertion benchmark (20 logos × 20 contexts × 5 seeds), LogoSticker achieves the best identity fidelity (CLIP-I = 0.761) and the highest user-study scores for both prompt fidelity (4.37/5) and logo fidelity (4.46/5), outperforming DreamBooth (CLIP-I 0.739, User PF 3.01, User LF 3.21), DreamBooth+ReVersion (CLIP-I 0.721, User PF 3.03, User LF 2.79), and Textual Inversion (CLIP-I 0.648, User PF 2.44, User LF 1.46). DINO identity scores are nearly identical across all methods (0.227–0.232), which the authors attribute to DINO's insensitivity to textual and pattern elements. On prompt fidelity (CLIP-T), LogoSticker (0.289) slightly trails Textual Inversion (0.303) but exceeds DreamBooth (0.278) and DreamBooth+ReVersion (0.271).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three primary customization baselines, LogoSticker wins decisively on identity fidelity (CLIP-I: 0.761 vs. 0.739 for DreamBooth, 0.721 for DreamBooth+ReVersion, 0.648 for Textual Inversion) and on both user-study metrics (User PF 4.37 vs. ≤3.03; User LF 4.46 vs. ≤3.21), while Textual Inversion retains a narrow edge on CLIP-T prompt fidelity (0.303 vs. 0.289). Qualitatively, DreamBooth and DreamBooth+ReVersion corrupt logo identity when the logo is placed on curved or inclined objects, whereas LogoSticker preserves fine-grained details and per-character colors. In comparisons with large models, DALLE 3 (even with an uploaded logo image) retains only high-level shape and loses all fine details and Chinese text, while ControlNet preserves overall shape but corrupts colors and suffers from incoherent logo placement. Against text-generation specialists Textdiffuser-2 and AnyText, LogoSticker uniquely maintains per-character color specifications, overall handwritten/calligraphic style, and can render the logo text alongside other concepts (e.g., 'Darth Vader') on objects like hats and mugs, which neither text model can do.

## Relation to Prior Work

LogoSticker extends the diffusion-model customization lineage (Textual Inversion, Dreambooth) to a domain where those methods fundamentally break down: logos, which lack the shared prior knowledge that standard customization exploits. It builds on ReVersion's idea of encoding an object-to-object spatial relation into a single text token, but identifies two critical weaknesses (insufficient generalization from few exemplars, and uneven difficulty across target objects) and addresses them with an actor-critic sampling strategy using CLIP as a critic. The decoupled identity learning phase repurposes Textual Inversion's single-token optimization in a novel two-stage fashion—first binding a token to the logo region on simple backgrounds, then fine-tuning the U-Net on complex scenes—departing from Dreambooth's monolithic full-weight fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Logo insertion is a practically important task for marketing, branding, and advertising that prior customization methods handle poorly, and this paper provides the first systematic pipeline addressing it. The actor-critic relation pre-training and decoupled identity learning are modular ideas that could transfer to other 'no-prior-knowledge' customization scenarios (e.g., novel symbols, non-Latin scripts, or brand-specific design elements). Open questions include scalability beyond 20 logos, compatibility with newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the curated relation dataset can be replaced by automatic data generation. The paper is limited to Stable Diffusion and does not explore multi-logo simultaneous insertion in depth.
