## Problem

Unsupervised 3D instance segmentation faces a fundamental trade-off: a single clustering granularity inevitably under-segments complex objects while over-segmenting simple ones, and there is no reliable criterion to determine whether a point cluster constitutes a meaningful 3D instance. Existing methods relying on single-level graph-cut or clustering cannot achieve consistent segmentation across objects with vastly different geometry, color, and size in cluttered indoor scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240458/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D RGB-D point cloud (coordinates, surface normals, and per-point color) together with temporally consecutive 2D RGB frames and their corresponding camera poses. The 2D frames are processed by DINO (for features) and MaskCut (for unsupervised instance masks) to derive 3D objectness priors as bounding boxes, while the point cloud undergoes hierarchical clustering. The final outputs are class-agnostic 3D instance masks, constituent object-part masks, and the intermediate 3D bounding boxes that serve as objectness guidance during clustering.

## Method

Part2Object performs hierarchical clustering by first generating super-points via VCCS, then iteratively merging clusters across layers using a dual criterion of top-K feature similarity and spatial adjacency, with a stopping rule that rejects merging when 3D objectness-prior bounding boxes indicate the clusters belong to different objects. The 3D objectness priors are extracted via a grouping-first-then-projection pipeline: objects are co-segmented across consecutive 2D frames using temporal consistency of DINO-based mask features (MaskCut), and only then are the grouped 2D masks projected and merged in 3D, avoiding the fragility of point-level projection. Hi-Mask3D extends Mask3D with a hierarchical-aware detector that maintains separate object and part queries; in each decoder layer, part queries cross-attend to scene features while object queries cross-attend to the updated part queries, explicitly leveraging part-to-object composition. The model is trained via iterative self-training rounds on pseudo-labels (objects and parts) extracted from Part2Object, using bipartite matching with DICE and BCE losses.

## Results

In the training-free setting on ScanNet Val, Part2Object achieves 55.1% mAP@25, 26.8% mAP@50, and 12.6% mAP, surpassing the previous SOTA Unscene3D (19.9/10.0/5.9) by 35.2%, 16.8%, and 6.7% respectively. In the data-efficient fine-tuning setting, Hi-Mask3D reaches 72.1% mAP@50 with 20% annotated data, outperforming Unscene3D (60.7%) by 11.4%. For cross-dataset zero-shot generalization, Hi-Mask3D trained solely on Part2Object pseudo-labels beats fully-supervised Mask3D by 10.7% mAP@50 on ScanNet200, 4.8% on S3DIS, and 6.4% on Replica.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Part2Object decisively outperforms all training-free baselines on ScanNet Val: it beats Felzenswalb graph-cut (38.9/12.7/5.0) by 16.2/14.1/7.6 in mAP@25/mAP@50/mAP, and HDBSCAN (32.1/5.5/1.6) by 23.0/21.3/11.0. The closest prior method, Unscene3D (19.9/10.0/5.9), which also leverages DINO features but applies single-level graph-cut, is surpassed by 35.2% mAP@25 and 16.8% mAP@50, confirming the benefit of hierarchical clustering with 3D objectness priors over flat clustering. In the data-efficient fine-tuning setting, Hi-Mask3D with Part2Object pseudo-labels at 0% annotated data (32.6% mAP@50) already exceeds Unscene3D's 20% data result (60.7% vs. 32.6% is not the right comparison—actually Ours at 0% = 32.6 vs Unscene3D at 0% = 23.2, a 9.4% gain), and at 20% data achieves 72.1% vs. 60.7% (11.4% gain). Against fully-supervised Mask3D in cross-dataset zero-shot transfer, Hi-Mask3D trained only on pseudo-labels wins on all three downstream datasets (ScanNet200: 34.9 vs. 24.2; S3DIS: 16.5 vs. 11.7; Replica: 22.0 vs. 15.6 mAP@50), demonstrating that unsupervised pseudo-labels yield more generalizable representations than supervised training restricted to annotated classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mask3D [42] architecture by adding a hierarchical part-to-object attention mechanism, and departs from prior unsupervised 3D instance segmentation methods (Unscene3D [41], FreePoint [66]) that rely on single-level clustering or graph-cut on 3D point clouds. The key conceptual departure is the 'grouping-first-then-projection' pipeline for extracting 3D objectness priors from 2D frames, which inverts the projection-first-then-grouping strategy used by prior 2D-to-3D transfer methods. The hierarchical clustering with objectness-guided stopping criteria represents a new way to resolve the under/over-segmentation trade-off that plagues single-level clustering approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that an unsupervised pipeline (Part2Object + Hi-Mask3D) can outperform fully-supervised Mask3D on cross-dataset generalization, suggesting that pseudo-labels from principled clustering capture more generalizable 3D object structure than human annotations limited to specific class taxonomies. The hierarchical part-to-object formulation opens a path toward leveraging part-level structure for instance discrimination in cluttered scenes. Limitations include reliance on temporally consecutive 2D RGB frames (restricting applicability to single-frame or outdoor settings), evaluation confined to indoor scenes, and the 2D-to-3D mapping still being sensitive to occlusion and pose estimation errors.
