## Problem

Deepfake detectors trained on one dataset generalize poorly to unseen datasets and forgery methods, and existing forgery augmentation strategies fix both the proportion of pseudo-fake (p-fake) samples and the augmentation policy throughout training. This paper identifies that the optimal balance between original fake (o-fake) and p-fake samples, as well as the choice of augmentation operation, should evolve with the model's learning progress, yet no prior work has jointly optimized these two aspects during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242295/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on individual face images (video frames) extracted from datasets such as FF++, Celeb-DF-v2, DFDC, and WildDeepfake, and outputs a binary real/fake prediction per frame, aggregated to video-level AUC by averaging frame predictions. The approach is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing detector backbone (Swin Transformer V2, Xception, EfficientNet-b4) without modifying the architecture, and the training batch is dynamically composed of real samples, o-fake samples from the dataset, and p-fake samples generated on-the-fly by blending operations.

## Method

CDFA jointly trains a deepfake detector and a lightweight policy network via bi-level optimization. A Monotonic Curriculum (MC) schedule q(t)=sin(t/ε) progressively shifts the fake portion of each mini-batch from o-fake (easy, obvious artifacts) toward p-fake (hard, diverse artifacts) over training epochs. A Dynamic Forgery Search (DFS) policy network h_γ selects among three augmentation operations—Blending Image (BI), Self-Blended Image (SBI), and the newly proposed Self-shifted Blending Image (SSBI, which blends faces from different frames of the same video to simulate temporal inconsistency)—by relaxing the non-differentiable sampling into a weighted sum of feature representations, enabling gradient-based policy updates on a validation search batch. The detector and policy are alternately optimized every s steps, so the augmentation strategy adapts to the evolving model state rather than remaining fixed.

## Results

On cross-dataset video-level AUC (trained on FF++/HQ, tested on four unseen datasets), CDFA with Swin backbone achieves 97.22% on Celeb-DF-v2, 84.45% on WildDeepfake, 97.03% on DFDCP, and 83.84% on DFDC, outperforming the best prior method by 3.34% (CDF), 13.31% (Wild), 10.73% (DFDCP), and 3.44% (DFDC) respectively. On frame-level AUC, CDFA reaches 91.96% (CDF), 81.34% (Wild), 93.30% (DFDCP), and 81.45% (DFDC). The method also improves cross-manipulation AUC substantially, e.g., Swin+CDFA trained on DF achieves 90.64% on FaceSwap and 86.27% on NeuralTextures, surpassing CADDM and DCL by 10–30+ points on most manipulation types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDFA (Swin, HQ) achieves the best video-level AUC on all four cross-dataset benchmarks, beating the strongest competitors: CADDM (93.88% on CDF), DCL (71.14% on Wild), SeeABLE (86.30% on DFDCP), and LTTD (80.40% on DFDC). On frame-level AUC, CDFA also leads on CDF (91.96% vs. TALL's 90.79%), Wild (81.34% vs. SFDG's 69.27%), and DFDCP (93.30% vs. SeeABLE's 86.30%), but trails OST (83.30%) on DFDC by ~1.9 points; the authors note OST uses test-time adaptation with domain knowledge from the test set, which CDFA does not. In cross-manipulation settings (trained on FF++/DF), Swin+CDFA outperforms CADDM and DCL by 10–30+ AUC points on F2F, FS, NT, and FSh (e.g., 90.64% vs. 58.33% on FaceSwap). The plug-and-play nature is confirmed by Table 2: adding CDFA to Xception, EfficientNet-b4, and Swin yields average improvements of 15.35%, 13.65%, and 15.19% respectively over their un-augmented baselines. Among the 17 compared methods, CDFA is the only one reporting results on all four test datasets simultaneously, and it dominates in every reported cell except the single DFDC frame-level entry where OST's test-time adaptation provides a narrow edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the forgery augmentation paradigm established by Face X-Ray (BI, Li et al. 2020) and Self-Blended Images (SBI, Shiohara et al. 2022), which synthesize pseudo-fake samples by blending faces to train more generalizable detectors. The key departure is treating the *training schedule* of these augmentations as a learnable variable: rather than fixing the p-fake proportion and augmentation type throughout training, CDFA introduces a monotonic curriculum that progressively shifts from o-fake to p-fake samples, and a bi-level optimization (inspired by AutoAugment) that dynamically selects among BI, SBI, and a new SSBI operation per sample. The SSBI augmentation itself extends the spatial-domain blending of prior work into the temporal domain by blending frames from the same video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDFA offers a genuinely plug-and-play training strategy that lifts multiple backbone architectures (Xception, EfficientNet, Swin) by 13–15% on average across cross-dataset benchmarks, making it immediately adoptable by the deepfake detection community without architectural changes. The finding that temporal-inconsistency augmentation (SSBI) outperforms purely spatial blending, and that the optimal augmentation mix shifts over training, suggests that prior fixed-policy approaches were leaving generalization performance on the table. Open questions include whether the policy network's bi-level optimization scales to larger augmentation spaces, whether the approach transfers to audio-visual or 3D deepfake settings, and whether the reliance on landmark-based blending masks limits applicability to heavily occluded or non-frontal faces.
