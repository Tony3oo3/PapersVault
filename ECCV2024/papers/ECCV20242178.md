## Problem

Online vectorized map estimation methods encode multi-camera observations into rich BEV feature grids but then decode them into sparse vectorized elements (polylines, polygons), discarding much of the intermediate information and incurring substantial computational cost in the decoding stage. This information bottleneck prevents downstream behavior prediction from leveraging the full geometric and dynamic context captured in the BEV representation, and the expensive decoder dominates overall system runtime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242178/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-camera RGB images (6 views from nuScenes at 10 Hz) and 2 seconds of agent trajectory history, producing 6 predicted trajectories 3 seconds into the future. Rather than passing decoded vectorized map elements to the prediction model, the method directly accesses the intermediate BEV feature grid (e.g., 200×100 spatial resolution with 0.3 m per cell for MapTRv2) and processes it as a sequence of flattened patches (20×20 cells, covering 6 m × 6 m in the real world) projected to a fixed embedding dimension, which is then attended to by agent queries.

## Method

The paper proposes three strategies for injecting BEV features into trajectory prediction. (1) Agent-BEV Feature Attention: BEV features are divided into patches and a multi-head attention layer lets agent-position patches query all map patches, replacing vectorized lane encoding entirely in HiVT's local interaction module. (2) Lane Augmentation: BEV features at each decoded map vertex location are concatenated with the vertex's positional features before encoding in DenseTNT's VectorNet backbone, enriching lane representations without removing them. (3) Temporal BEV Substitution: StreamMapNet's one-step historical BEV fusion (which implicitly encodes dynamic agent motion) is used to replace agent trajectory subgraphs in DenseTNT, discarding explicit trajectory input. All three strategies are trained end-to-end with their respective prediction models on nuScenes, and the key finding is that temporally-informed BEV features (from BEVFormer-based encoders) yield substantially larger gains than static-only features (from LSS-based encoders), while also enabling 42–73% faster inference by eliminating the map decoding stage.

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset, directly leveraging BEV features from online mapping models improves trajectory prediction accuracy by up to 29% (minFDE) and 27% (minADE) over the vectorized-map-only baseline, with the largest gains observed when combining MapTR with DenseTNT (minADE 0.7608 vs. 1.0462, minFDE 1.4700 vs. 2.0661). The approach also achieves up to 73% faster end-to-end inference by eliminating the computationally expensive map decoding stage. Improvements are consistent across all four mapping models (MapTR, MapTRv2, MapTRv2-CL, StreamMapNet) and both prediction models (HiVT, DenseTNT), with the most substantial gains in Miss Rate (up to 25%) and minFDE, indicating particularly strong benefits for long-horizon endpoint prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's BEV feature approach consistently outperforms the uncertainty-enhanced baseline [13] (Gu et al., CVPR 2024) across virtually all mapping/prediction combinations, with the most dramatic wins when paired with MapTR (up to 29% minFDE reduction for DenseTNT) and StreamMapNet (up to 25% MR reduction for HiVT). Compared to the vectorized-map-only baselines using MapTR [22], MapTRv2 [23], and StreamMapNet [37], the integrated BEV approach achieves 3–27% improvements in minADE, 2–29% in minFDE, and 0–26% in MR depending on the combination. The one notable exception is MapTRv2-CL [23], where the already-superior centerline information leaves little room for BEV features to help (0–3% changes, with a slight 3% regression in DenseTNT minFDE). Temporally-informed BEV features (from BEVFormer-based MapTR and StreamMapNet) yield substantially larger gains (16–25%) than static-only features (from LSS-based MapTRv2, yielding only 2–6%), confirming that temporal dynamics in the BEV representation are the key driver of prediction improvements. In terms of inference speed, the integrated approach is 42–73% faster than the decoupled MapTR + HiVT pipeline and 35–62% faster than MapTRv2 + HiVT, with the speedup growing as the number of map elements increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the map-informed trajectory prediction line of work (HiVT, DenseTNT) by bypassing the vectorized map decoding stage entirely and instead consuming the intermediate BEV feature grids produced by online mapping encoders (MapTR, MapTRv2, StreamMapNet). It departs from the closely related uncertainty-enhanced approach of Gu et al. [13], which exposes decoded map element uncertainties to the predictor, by accessing features from an earlier pipeline stage (immediately after PV2BEV encoding) where richer geometric and temporal information is still preserved. The ViT-inspired patching of BEV tensors into a token sequence for multi-head attention provides a conceptual bridge between image Transformer architectures and BEV-based scene understanding, while the temporal BEV features from BEVFormer/StreamMapNet draw on the streaming mapping literature to implicitly encode agent dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 42–73% inference speedup (from skipping map decoding) combined with up to 29% accuracy gains makes this a practically relevant architectural pattern for real-time AV stacks, and the finding that temporal BEV features substantially outperform static ones for prediction provides clear guidance for choosing PV2BEV encoders. A notable limitation is the reduced interpretability of black-box BEV features compared to explicit vectorized map elements, which complicates debugging and safety certification. Open questions include whether co-training mapping and prediction jointly could further improve both, how to interpret BEV features at runtime without costly decoding, and whether these strategies generalize beyond nuScenes to other sensor suites or driving domains.
