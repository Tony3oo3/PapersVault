## Problem

Existing DDIM Inversion-based image editing methods (e.g., P2P, MasaCtrl) preserve the original image's layout too rigidly, making them unable to perform non-rigid edits such as pose or view changes. The paper identifies that high-frequency components in the DDIM latent space encode the original layout, and their persistence in the editing pipeline is the root cause of this inflexibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241690/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a source text prompt, a target text prompt, and a binary editing mask (either auto-derived from cross-attention maps at 16×16 spatial resolution or user-defined) as input, and produces a single edited RGB image. It operates on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with a DDIM schedule of T=50 steps, and the user can control the degree of layout change via a hyperparameter α ∈ [0.5, 0.9] and the re-inversion duration t_R, which is automatically calibrated based on mask area and PSNR similarity between the intermediate and original images.

## Method

FlexiEdit is a training-free, three-branch diffusion editing pipeline built on Stable Diffusion. First, Latent Refinement applies a 2D Fourier transform to the DDIM-inverted latent z_T, attenuates high-frequency components within the editing mask by a factor α (via a Gaussian low-pass/high-pass filter pair), and adds Gaussian noise scaled by (1−α), producing a refined latent z_T′ that decouples the editing region from the original layout. Second, Edit Fidelity Enhancement via Re-inversion introduces a retarget branch: the target branch denoises z_T′ independently (CFG=7.5, no source-branch feature injection) to produce an intermediate image I_mid, which is then re-inverted for t_R steps; the retarget branch denoises the re-inverted latent while injecting key/value features from the source branch (CFG=1) to recover original attributes. The re-inversion duration t_R is automatically set via a formula combining the mask area ratio and the PSNR ratio between I_mid and the reconstruction, with ranges of [10,30] for rigid edits and [30,50] for non-rigid edits.

## Results

On 30 non-rigid edit samples from PIE-Bench and ELITE, FlexiEdit achieves the highest CLIP similarity scores (25.28 whole-image, 24.88 edited-region), outperforming all baselines by at least 1.4 points on whole-image CLIP. In background preservation, P2P with Direct Inversion leads (PSNR 27.22, LPIPS 54.55), while FlexiEdit ranks second (PSNR 25.74, LPIPS 80.45, MSE 58.45). On the rigid editing PIE benchmark, FlexiEdit again achieves the highest edited-region CLIP similarity (22.87) while P2P retains the best structure distance (11.65×10⁻³) and background preservation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexiEdit outperforms all four baselines on CLIP similarity for non-rigid edits, achieving 25.28 (whole) and 24.88 (edited) versus the next-best ProxMasaCtrl at 23.85 and 22.53, a gap of 1.43 and 2.35 points respectively. Against P2P, FlexiEdit trades some background preservation (PSNR 25.74 vs. 27.22, LPIPS 80.45 vs. 54.55) for substantially better text-image alignment and the ability to perform non-rigid edits, which P2P cannot do at all. Compared to MasaCtrl, FlexiEdit improves CLIP whole-image by 1.74 points and CLIP edited by 2.56 points while also achieving better background preservation across all four metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, MSE, SSIM). Against ProxMasaCtrl, FlexiEdit leads on CLIP similarity (both whole and edited) and MSE (58.45 vs. 75.09) but trails slightly on PSNR (25.74 vs. 24.43) and SSIM (82.62 vs. 82.53). PnP is the weakest baseline overall, unable to perform non-rigid edits and scoring lowest on nearly every metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiEdit extends the two-branch DDIM Inversion editing paradigm established by Prompt-to-Prompt and MasaCtrl, where a source branch reconstructs the original and a target branch generates the edit with attention feature injection. The key departure is a frequency-domain diagnosis: the authors show that high-frequency components of the DDIM latent are the primary carriers of layout information, and that reducing them (combined with Gaussian noise) in the edit region is sufficient to unlock non-rigid layout changes without fine-tuning. The three-branch re-inversion scheme further departs from prior work by deferring source-branch feature injection to a retarget stage, rather than injecting throughout the target branch as in P2P or MasaCtrl.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-domain analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for why DDIM-inversion-based editors resist layout changes, offering a principled (rather than heuristic) lever for non-rigid editing. However, the method still lags P2P in background preservation, and the two key hyperparameters (α for frequency reduction and t_R for re-inversion duration) require manual or heuristic tuning per edit type, limiting fully automated deployment. Open questions include whether the high-frequency-as-layout insight generalizes to newer diffusion architectures (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based models) and whether the three-branch pipeline can be simplified without sacrificing fidelity.
