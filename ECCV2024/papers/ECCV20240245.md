## Problem

Prior Audio-Visual Video Parsing methods focus on improving audio-visual encoders but rely on a simple linear-layer decoder (MMIL) that maps semantically mixed latent features directly to event classes, offering no explicit mechanism to disentangle overlapping events. This makes the decoding process opaque and less effective when multiple events co-occur in the same temporal segment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240245/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1-second audio segments (16 KHz waveform → VGGish 128-D features), 1-second video segments (8 FPS frames → concatenated ResNet-152 and R(2+1)D features), and GloVe embeddings of all C event class names as an intermediary text modality. It produces per-segment audio and visual event probability matrices (T×C), video-level event labels (1×C) via a threshold of 0.5 on modality-specific predictions combined by logical OR, and temporal event boundaries by grouping consecutive positive segments. Training uses only weak video-level labels plus segment-level pseudo labels generated by a separate modality-independent teacher.

## Method

LEAP (Label semantic-based projection) replaces the linear MMIL decoder with an iterative Transformer cross-attention block where label embeddings serve as queries and audio/visual features serve as keys and values. Over N iterations (typically 2), each label embedding progressively aggregates semantics from all temporal segments, so that overlapping events in a segment are disentangled into their respective label embeddings via class-aware attention weights. The final cross-attention matrix (C×T) yields segment-level predictions, while the refined label embeddings (C×d) yield video-level predictions. A novel EIoU-based audio-visual semantic similarity loss (L_avss) further regularizes training by computing the Intersection-over-Union of event class sets for every audio-visual segment pair and aligning the cosine similarity of encoded features to this EIoU matrix via MSE, accommodating the typically different event densities across modalities.

## Results

On the LLP dataset, MM-Pyr+LEAP achieves new state-of-the-art performance with 64.8% Type@AV and 63.6% Event@AV at the segment level, and 60.2% Type@AV and 57.4% Event@AV at the event level. With the HAN encoder, LEAP reaches 62.5% Type@AV (segment) and 57.8% Type@AV (event), surpassing the strongest HAN-based baseline VALOR by 0.5% and 1.1% respectively. The LEAP decoder also generalizes to the AVEL task, improving CMBS by 3.4% F1 over its default decoding paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest HAN-based baseline VALOR [31] (NeurIPS'23), HAN+LEAP improves segment-level Type@AV from 62.0% to 62.5% and event-level Type@AV from 56.7% to 57.8%, with consistent gains across all individual modality metrics (A, V, AV). Against the strongest encoder-focused method CMPAE [5] (CVPR'23), MM-Pyr+LEAP achieves substantially larger margins: +1.5% on segment-level Type@AV (64.8 vs. 63.3) and +2.8% on event-level Type@AV (60.2 vs. 57.4), while also outperforming CMPAE on every individual modality column. Compared to the typical MMIL decoder with the same MM-Pyr encoder, LEAP yields +2.2% on segment-level Event@AV (63.6 vs. 60.6) and +3.6% on event-level Event@AV (57.4 vs. 53.0), confirming the decoding paradigm itself as the source of improvement. On the overlapping-event subset, LEAP with MM-Pyr improves event-level Type@AV by 1.4% over MMIL (49.7 vs. 48.3), demonstrating particular strength in disentangling concurrent events. LEAP also generalizes to the AVEL task, where it boosts CMBS by +3.4% F1 (77.9 vs. 74.5) and PSP by +2.3% (76.6 vs. 74.3) over their default decoding paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-centric paradigm in audio-visual video parsing (AVVP), where prior works (HAN, MM-Pyr, CMPAE, VALOR) focus on designing stronger audio-visual encoders while relying on the simple MMIL linear decoder introduced in [23]. LEAP repurposes the Transformer cross-attention mechanism—common in multimodal and NLP settings—as a decoding strategy, using pretrained GloVe label embeddings as queries to project audio/visual features onto semantically independent class representations. The EIoU-based semantic similarity loss extends the standard IoU concept from spatial bounding boxes to event-set overlap, providing a novel cross-modal regularization signal that prior AVVP methods have not exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that the decoding phase can be a meaningful source of improvement independent of encoder quality, this work opens a new axis of research for AVVP and related weakly-supervised temporal localization tasks. The interpretability benefit—cross-modal attention matrices directly visualizing which label embeddings a segment activates—could aid debugging and trust in deployed systems. Limitations include evaluation on a single 25-category dataset (LLP), reliance on a fixed 0.5 decision threshold, and the modest vocabulary coverage of GloVe embeddings; whether LEAP scales to hundreds of categories or benefits from richer contextual text encoders remains open. The generalization to AVEL (Table 5) is encouraging but tested on only three encoder variants.
