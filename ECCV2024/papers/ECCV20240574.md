## Problem

Frequency regularization has proven effective for positional-encoding (PE) NeRFs in camera pose optimization and few-shot reconstruction, yet its theoretical mechanisms in grid-based feature encoding (GFE) methods such as Instant-NGP and TensoRF remain unexplored. Specifically, it is unclear how frequency components are distributed in GFE representations, what determines their bandwidth, and how to properly extend coarse-to-fine frequency masking to discrete grid structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple RGB images with deliberately perturbed se(3) camera poses (additive Gaussian noise: N(0, 0.15I) for synthetic scenes, N(0, 0.01I) for real-world scenes) and jointly optimizes both the scene representation and camera parameters. For few-shot reconstruction, only a sparse subset is provided (10 images for NeRF-Synthetic, ~25% of images for Mip-NeRF 360). The output consists of refined camera poses (rotation and translation) and rendered novel-view images evaluated by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.

## Method

The paper first establishes that in GFE NeRFs, the supported frequency bandwidth is governed almost entirely by grid resolution rather than network depth, by showing that linear interpolation over grids decomposes into triangular pulse functions whose Fourier transforms scale inversely with resolution. Building on this, G²fR applies a smooth cosine-based mask across the resolution levels of multi-resolution hash encoding (or equivalent multi-resolution GFE), progressively introducing higher-resolution (higher-frequency) grid features during the first βT iterations of training. The authors further connect this to model generalization via the Lipschitz constant of the encoding function: early in training, fewer active resolution levels yield a smaller Lipschitz constant, constraining the model to low-frequency signals and avoiding local minima. The method is validated on both Instant-NGP and a multi-resolution CP-TensoRF (Mtrf) for concurrent camera pose optimization and few-shot novel view synthesis, achieving state-of-the-art pose accuracy with significantly fewer iterations (20k vs. 200k) and lower memory than prior methods.

## Results

On the Lego scene (NeRF-Synthetic), NGP+G²fR achieves the best rotation error (0.043°), translation error (0.143×10²), and PSNR (33.55 dB) among all compared methods, while requiring only 20k iterations (~5 min) versus 200k iterations (2–5 h) for baselines. Mtrf+G²fR also attains the lowest rotation error (0.039°) with minimal memory (~11 MB). Across 14 scenes in Table 1, G²fR consistently reduces rotation and translation errors for NGP (e.g., Chair: 0.101° vs 0.580°; Materials: 0.045° vs 2.234°) and improves PSNR in most cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NGP+G²fR outperforms all four baselines on every metric for the Lego scene: it reduces rotation error by 33% over the best baseline RCPR (0.043° vs 0.064°), halves the translation error (0.143 vs 0.280×10²), and gains 1.2 dB PSNR over CamP (33.55 vs 32.33). Compared to BARF, the closest frequency-regularization baseline, NGP+G²fR improves rotation error by 43% (0.043° vs 0.076°) and PSNR by 5.2 dB (33.55 vs 28.32), while training 60× faster (~5 min vs ~5 h). Against CamP, which uses the largest memory footprint (~525 MB), NGP+G²fR uses roughly one-third the memory (~150 MB) while achieving superior accuracy on all three metrics. Mtrf+G²fR achieves the lowest rotation error (0.039°) with only ~11 MB memory, though its PSNR (29.71) trails NGP+G²fR, reflecting the smaller model's limited memorization capacity at coarse frequency stages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of NeRF research: frequency regularization in positional-encoding (PE) NeRFs (BARF [18], FreeNeRF [41]) and grid-based feature encoding (GFE) methods (Instant-NGP [25], TensoRF [3]). While prior work applied coarse-to-fine masking to PE vectors and several GFE papers [11,17,27,38] adopted similar strategies empirically, no prior study provided a Fourier-domain theoretical analysis explaining *why* frequency regularization works in the discrete-grid setting. The paper adapts the expressive-power framework of Yüce et al. [44] (which characterized PE INRs as linear combinations of Fourier harmonics) to GFE, showing that linear interpolation over grids yields continuous frequency spectra governed by grid resolution rather than network depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that GFE frequency bandwidth is determined almost entirely by grid resolution (not MLP depth) gives practitioners a clear, interpretable knob for controlling model behavior, and the G²fR masking strategy is a drop-in, zero-overhead technique applicable to any multi-resolution GFE backbone. However, the paper honestly identifies a key limitation: for smaller-capacity models like Mtrf, restricting to low-frequency components early in training degrades scene modeling and worsens pose errors, and outdoor scenes show diminished gains. The open question of adaptive frequency-band scheduling in GFE (analogous to adaptive PE [10]) remains unresolved and is flagged as future work.
