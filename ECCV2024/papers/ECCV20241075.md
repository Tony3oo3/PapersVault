## Problem

Standard representation learning methods (contrastive learning, masked image modeling, and vision-language pretraining) face specific failures when applied to diffusion-generated synthetic images: random crops in contrastive learning conflate different objects into positive pairs, random masking in MIM wastes capacity on empty backgrounds, and object detectors for VLP fail on out-of-domain synthetic objects. The paper identifies that cross-attention layers in diffusion models inherently produce annotation-free attention masks aligned with text tokens, and asks how these free masks can be exploited to fix each of these problems.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synthetic RGB images (1.2M for CL/MIM, 0.3M–14M for VLP) generated by latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion 1.5 or DeepIF) from augmented text prompts, together with cross-attention masks extracted from every UNet layer and diffusion timestep during generation. These masks are resized to a common spatial resolution and averaged across layers and timesteps to form a single per-object mask. The output is a pretrained encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B) whose features are at the instance level (via attentive pooling) rather than the image level, plus, for VLP, position-aware text prompts derived from binary-thresholded masks (threshold 0.45) that encode object block locations.

## Method

Free-ATM repurposes the cross-attention maps A = Softmax(QK^T/√d) from the diffusion UNet as free object localization, adapting three representation learning frameworks without adding trainable parameters. For contrastive learning (SimCLR/MoCo-v2), attention masks are used for attentive pooling to extract instance-level features z^m, and the contrastive loss is redefined so that the same instance across two crops forms the positive pair while different instances in the same image serve as in-batch negatives. For masked image modeling (MAE), the masking strategy progressively shifts from random patch selection to attention-score-guided selection (ratio β linearly increases to 0.8 over training), so the model reconstructs foreground patches more heavily while still learning universal background representations. For vision-language pretraining (BLIP), masks are binarized to obtain bounding boxes, which are converted into position-aware prompts ("The [object] is in block [P]") concatenated with the original caption, eliminating the need for an external object detector. All three adaptations require no additional training cost beyond the standard framework, and the masks are obtained at zero extra expense during the image generation stage.

## Results

On contrastive learning (Table 1), Free-ATM with DeepIF synthetic images and MoCo-v2 achieves 82.5 AP50^b on PASCAL VOC, 40.6 AP^b on COCO, and 67.4 linear accuracy on ImageNet, matching or exceeding real-data MoCo-v2 (82.4, 39.8, 67.3). With mixed real+synthetic data, the best configuration reaches 83.1 AP50^b, 41.2 AP^b, and 68.0 linear accuracy, surpassing real-data-only training. For masked image modeling (Table 2), Free-ATM with DeepIF+mixed data achieves 51.0 AP^b on COCO and 84.2% ImageNet accuracy, exceeding real-data MAE (50.3, 83.6). For vision-language pretraining (Table 3), Free-ATM with DeepIF+mixed data attains 61.4 tr@1 and 47.1 ir@1 on COCO retrieval, outperforming real-data BLIP (58.1, 44.2), with zero-shot improvements of up to 8.7% over the synthetic-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Free-ATM consistently outperforms all four representation learning baselines on synthetic data: SimCLR gains +0.9 to +1.4 AP^b on COCO, MoCo-v2 gains +1.7 to +1.8 AP^b, MAE gains +1.0 AP^b and +0.7% ImageNet accuracy, and BLIP gains +2.6 to +5.9 tr@1 on COCO retrieval. With higher-quality DeepIF images, Free-ATM fully closes the gap between synthetic and real-data pretraining for contrastive learning (e.g., MoCo-v2: 40.6 vs. 39.8 COCO AP^b) and even surpasses real-data MAE on COCO detection (50.5 vs. 50.3 AP^b). Against PTP [65], which uses an offline Fast-RCNN detector for position grounding, Free-ATM achieves higher retrieval scores (54.9 vs. 53.8 tr@1 finetune; 31.8 vs. 28.2 tr@1 zero-shot), demonstrating that diffusion-derived attention masks provide superior localization for out-of-domain synthetic objects compared to detectors trained on real data. In terms of mask quality, Free-ATM's attention masks achieve 38.9 mIOU against SAM ground truth, essentially matching the purpose-built Slot Attention method (38.6 mIOU), validating that cross-attention maps from diffusion models are a reliable proxy for object segmentation without any additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model architecture (Stable Diffusion) and its cross-attention mechanism, which prior work (Hertz et al. 2022, Zhao et al. 2023) identified as encoding text-visual alignment but had not repurposed for representation learning. It extends the line of synthetic-data-for-recognition work (He et al. 2023, Azizi et al. 2023) by showing that the attention masks produced 'for free' during image generation can be directly plugged into three established representation learning paradigms—contrastive learning (SimCLR, MoCo-v2), masked image modeling (MAE), and vision-language pretraining (BLIP)—without requiring object detectors (unlike PTP), additional teacher networks (unlike HPM), or iterative clustering (unlike object-prior methods). The key conceptual departure is treating the diffusion model's internal cross-attention as a zero-cost annotation source that simultaneously addresses the distinct failure modes of each framework on multi-object synthetic images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: Free-ATM narrows or eliminates the performance gap between synthetic and real-data pretraining (especially with higher-quality generators like DeepIF), and it accelerates pretraining on synthetic images by 2–4×, making synthetic-data pipelines substantially more compute-efficient. A key open question is how well this approach generalizes to scenes with many overlapping objects, abstract concepts, or non-object-centric content where cross-attention masks may be less well-defined. The method's effectiveness is also contingent on the quality of the underlying diffusion model, raising the question of whether future, more text-aligned generators will make the attention masks even more reliable or whether the technique's gains will saturate.
