## Problem

Generating high-quality emerging images (EIs) from natural images faces two unresolved challenges: minimizing perceptual loss during 24-bit-to-1-bit color quantization, and quantitatively controlling the perceived difficulty of object detection and recognition in the resulting EIs. Additionally, prior EI-CAPTCHA studies only validated defense against traditional vision models, leaving open whether EIs can resist attacks from deep network-based solvers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single natural RGB image as input and produces a pair of complementary, resolution-independent vectorized EIs (T+ and T−) rendered as black-and-white speckle patterns. The two templates share identical edge information but differ in foreground/background color assignment, yielding different figure-ground segregation difficulties. Perceived difficulty is user-controllable via two scalar parameters (α for recognition difficulty, β for saliency contrast) and the choice of template.

## Method

HiEI comprises three modules. TTNet is a U-Net autoencoder that learns a 1-bit color palette by minimizing VGG19 content-feature loss (first 5 layers) between the natural image and its quantized version, preserving human-perceptual cues better than classical methods like MedianCut or Octree. The PDC module uses GFM matting to separate foreground/background, extracts four-connected speckles as semantic primitives and SLIC-like patches as scale primitives, then applies α-proportion sampling (texture- or shape-driven, determined by edge density in the foreground bounding box) and β-scaled background density to quantitatively control recognition difficulty and object saliency. The TV module vectorizes each speckle by computing approximate contour curvature, sampling contour pixels proportional to curvature, and fitting Bézier curves, producing scalable vector EIs free from raster distortion.

## Results

On the EI generation quality comparison (Table 2), HiEI(1,0,+) achieves the lowest content loss (1.02) and style loss (0.19), outperforming the best UST baseline AdaConv (1.16 / 0.28) and the best EI-specific baseline ArtThres (1.29 / 0.59). In the user study with 100 participants (40,000 ratings), HiEI(1,0,+/-) receives 89.94% content preference and 48.71% style preference, far exceeding the next-best methods (QuantArt: 2.65% and 16.57% respectively). For the CAPTCHA feasibility test, YOLO v8 fine-tuned on natural images drops from 70.9% to 10.3% success rate on EIs, while humans maintain 89% accuracy; similarly, AlexNet/VGG19/ResNet34 top-1 accuracy on STL-10 drops from 75.9%/69.1%/87.8% to 23.7%/20.5%/26.5% on EIs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiEI dominates all seven baselines on both content loss and style loss. Among the five UST models, AdaConv is the closest competitor (content loss 1.16 vs. HiEI's 1.02; style loss 0.28 vs. 0.19), while InST performs worst on content loss (1.46) and WCT/AdaIN are weakest on style loss (0.74/0.82). Among the two EI-specific methods, ArtThres (1.29/0.59) and EdgeEI (1.36/0.78) both trail HiEI by a wide margin on both metrics. In the user study, HiEI's 89.94% content preference is an order of magnitude above the runner-up QuantArt (2.65%), and its 48.71% style preference roughly triples QuantArt's 16.57%. The paper also notes that HiEI(1,0,−) is slightly worse than HiEI(1,0,+) on both losses, attributed to the foreground-background color swap amplifying luminance feature loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of color quantization, stylized image generation, and cognitive psychology. Rather than building directly on a single prior architecture, it repurposes a U-Net autoencoder (inspired by ColorCNN) for human-centered 1-bit quantization, borrows the perceptual organization framework from cognitive science (Li et al. 2023) to formalize perceived difficulty control, and departs from universal style transfer models by arguing they cannot balance the extreme style constraint of binary speckle rendering. The CAPTCHA validation extends prior EI-CAPTCHA work, which only demonstrated defense against traditional vision models, to deep network-based solvers (YOLO v8, AlexNet, VGG19, ResNet34).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic performance gap between humans (89% success) and deep networks (10–29% success) on EIs provides empirical evidence that a simple binary speckle representation can serve as a robust CAPTCHA against modern deep learning attacks, a question prior work left open. The perceived difficulty control (α, β parameters) offers a practical tool for cognitive psychology experiments requiring calibrated stimulus difficulty. However, the CAPTCHA evaluation is limited to a single detection architecture and three classification networks on relatively small datasets, leaving open whether more sophisticated or ensemble-based solvers could close the gap, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes or adversarially trained models.
