## Problem

Monocular depth and normal estimation is inherently ill-posed, and prior discriminative models (CNNs, Transformers) are constrained by the low diversity and poor quality of publicly available training datasets, resulting in limited generalization to out-of-domain images and an inability to capture fine geometric details. The paper asks whether the rich 3D world knowledge encoded in large-scale diffusion priors can be leveraged to overcome these data-driven limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240577/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image at 576×768 resolution along with a one-hot scene-type prompt (indoor, outdoor, or background-free object) and jointly produces an affine-invariant depth map and a surface normal map. The condition image is consumed in two complementary ways: CLIP image embeddings provide global guidance through cross-attention, while the VAE-encoded image latent is concatenated with the geometric latents in the U-Net for precise spatial control. Both depth and normal are generated from a single diffusion model via a geometry switcher, ensuring mutual consistency between the two outputs.

## Method

GeoWizard fine-tunes the pre-trained Stable Diffusion V2 U-Net to model the joint depth-normal distribution p(d,n) in latent space. A geometry switcher (one-dimensional positional-encoding vectors for depth vs. normal) routes the single U-Net to produce either modality, while a cross-domain geometric self-attention layer computes queries from one modality's latent but keys/values from the concatenation of both, enabling mutual spatial alignment. A Scene Distribution Decoupler encodes a one-hot scene-type vector into positional embeddings added to the time embedding, segregating the mixed scene distribution into distinct sub-distributions (indoor/outdoor/object) to prevent layout ambiguity. Training uses multi-resolution noise schedules to preserve low-frequency geometric structure and v-prediction as the denoising objective, with both geometry branches perturbed at the same timestep for stable joint learning.

## Results

On zero-shot affine-invariant depth estimation across six benchmarks, GeoWizard achieves AbsRel of 5.2/9.7/6.4/6.1/29.7/1.7 and δ1 of 96.6/92.1/96.1/95.3/79.2/99.9 on NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, ScanNet, DIODE-Full, and OmniObject3D respectively, trained on only 0.28M images versus 63.5M for DepthAnything. For zero-shot surface normal estimation, GeoWizard achieves mean angular error of 17.0°/15.4°/13.0°/20.6°/20.8° on NYUv2, ScanNet, iBims-1, DIODE-outdoor, and OmniObject3D, outperforming DSINE on ScanNet (15.4° vs 16.2°), iBims-1 (13.0° vs 17.1°), and OmniObject3D (20.8° vs 21.7°). The paper emphasizes that while DepthAnything leads on in-domain real images, GeoWizard generalizes far more robustly to out-of-domain and AIGC-generated images while capturing significantly finer geometric details.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For depth estimation, GeoWizard is competitive with but does not uniformly beat the strongest discriminative baseline DepthAnything, which leads on NYUv2 (AbsRel 4.3 vs 5.2) and ScanNet (4.2 vs 6.1); however, GeoWizard wins on ETH3D δ1 (96.1 vs 96.0), DIODE-Full δ1 (79.2 vs 78.0), and OmniObject3D AbsRel (1.7 vs 1.8), and is trained on 225× fewer images (0.28M vs 63.5M). Against the closest generative competitor Marigold, GeoWizard improves AbsRel on all six depth benchmarks (e.g., 5.2 vs 5.5 on NYUv2, 29.7 vs 30.8 on DIODE). For surface normal estimation, GeoWizard outperforms DSINE on ScanNet (Mean 15.4° vs 16.2°), iBims-1 (13.0° vs 17.1°), and OmniObject3D (20.8°/47.8% vs 21.7°/45.1%), but trails DSINE on NYUv2 (17.0° vs 16.4°) and DIODE-outdoor (20.6° vs 19.3°). The paper's key qualitative claim is that discriminative baselines (DepthAnything, DSINE) suffer severe degradation on in-the-wild and AIGC-generated images, whereas GeoWizard maintains correct spatial structure and recovers high-frequency details (hair, architectural texture, limbs) that all baselines miss. In downstream 3D reconstruction (MonoSDF on ScanNet), GeoWizard's geometric cues yield the best Recall (70.7%) and F-score (75.1) over Omnidata v2 (68.1%/73.3%) and DSINE (70.2%/74.7%).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoWizard extends the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) to the geometry estimation domain, building directly on Wonder3D's (Long et al., 2023) geometry switcher mechanism to enable a single U-Net to produce both depth and normal. It departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm (CNN/Transformer regressors like DPT, MiDaS, DepthAnything) and from prior diffusion-based depth estimators (Marigold, DDVM) by jointly modeling depth and normal with cross-domain self-attention, and by introducing a scene distribution decoupler that addresses the layout ambiguity problem identified but not resolved in Marigold. The work also draws on multi-resolution noise scheduling and v-prediction from the broader diffusion literature to handle the low-frequency, locally-uniform nature of geometric maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion priors pre-trained on billions of images can substitute for massive task-specific training data (0.28M vs. 63.5M for DepthAnything), offering a compelling path toward geometry estimation that generalizes to AIGC-generated and out-of-domain images where discriminative models fail. The scene distribution decoupler, while simple, identifies a concrete failure mode (mixed scene layouts causing spatial compression) that likely affects other multi-scene generative tasks. Open questions include whether the three-way scene categorization (indoor/outdoor/object) is sufficient for all real-world scenarios, how to eliminate the need for explicit scene-type prompts at inference, and how to reduce the iterative denoising cost to practical real-time speeds—the authors themselves flag latent consistency models as a promising direction.
