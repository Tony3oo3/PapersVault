## Problem

In semi-supervised multi-label learning, existing pseudo-labeling methods like CAP focus on designing class-wise thresholding strategies but neglect the quality of model predictions, which is equally critical for generating accurate pseudo-labels. When model predictions are imperfect—especially with few labeled examples—higher-ranked negative labels get mislabeled as positive and lower-ranked positives as negative, degrading pseudo-label quality regardless of the thresholding strategy. The paper addresses this by improving pseudo-label quality from two complementary perspectives: enhancing prediction quality and optimizing thresholds jointly.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images resized to 224×224, which are simultaneously processed in their original form and cropped into a grid of equal-sized patches (default 2×2). Each image and patch undergoes both weak augmentation (random horizontal flip) and strong augmentation (Cutout + RandAugment), producing separate prediction streams. The output is a binary multi-label prediction vector over K classes, obtained by averaging correlative (whole-image) and discriminative (patch-aggregated) probabilities from a dual-head classifier.

## Method

The proposed D2L framework performs two decouplings: (1) Correlative/Discriminative features Decoupling (CDD) splits feature learning into whole-image co-occurrence modeling and per-patch discriminative pattern capture, with patch probabilities aggregated via a temperature-controlled spatially-weighted sum; (2) Generation/Utilization Decoupling (GUD) employs two separate dual-head classifiers—one trained exclusively on clean labeled data to generate pseudo-labels, and another trained on pseudo-labels to exploit unlabeled data—preventing error accumulation. The MAT strategy replaces class-proportion-based thresholding by directly searching per-class thresholds over a discretized [0,1] interval to maximize a chosen metric (F_β with β=0.5) on labeled data, yielding thresholds that account for actual prediction quality rather than assumed label distributions. Training uses a ResNet-50 backbone with ASL loss, AdamW optimizer, EMA (decay 0.9997), and 40 epochs with early stopping.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2014, the proposed D2L+MAT method achieves 56.59% mAP at p=0.01 and 75.70% mAP at p=0.20, improving upon the state-of-the-art CAP by 4.19%–6.87% mAP across labeled proportions. On PASCAL VOC 2012, it reaches 49.09% (p=0.01) to 87.16% (p=0.20) mAP, outperforming CAP by 1.90%–4.03%. On NUS-WIDE, the method attains 38.09% (p=0.01) to 52.64% (p=0.20) mAP, a 1.58%–4.50% gain over CAP. The advantage is most pronounced at low labeled proportions, where model prediction quality is most limited.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins against all ten baselines across every dataset and labeled proportion. Against the strongest SSMLL competitor CAP (class-distribution-aware pseudo-labeling), the gains are 4.19–6.87% mAP on COCO, 1.90–4.03% on VOC, and 1.58–4.50% on NUS, with the largest margins at p=0.01 where model predictions are least reliable. The SSL-adapted methods ADSH and FM (which use dynamic thresholds designed for single-label settings) trail the proposed method by 4–12% mAP on COCO, confirming that single-label thresholding strategies do not transfer well to multi-label pseudo-labeling. DRML, the other deep SSMLL baseline, underperforms even the supervised ASL baseline on COCO (e.g., 39.12% vs. 44.87% at p=0.01), attributed to its failure to train the backbone end-to-end. The instance-aware thresholding method IAT shows a declining pseudo-labeling quality during training, while the proposed method's CF1 score on pseudo-labels remains consistently higher than CAP from the start of training, validating that the D2L framework's dual-head architecture and decoupled pseudo-label generation directly improve prediction quality beyond what threshold tuning alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends CAP (Class-Aware Pseudo-labeling), which introduced class-wise thresholding to handle the unknown label-count problem in SSMLL, by arguing that CAP's focus on matching class proportions is insufficient when model predictions are imperfect. It draws on the teacher-student and auxiliary-classifier paradigms from debiased self-training and imbalanced SSL to decouple pseudo-label generation from utilization, and borrows the patch-based discriminative feature idea from counterfactual multi-label classification. The metric-adaptive thresholding strategy repurposes the evaluation metric (F_β) as an optimization objective for per-class thresholds, a departure from prior SSL thresholding methods (ADSH, FreeMatch) that rely on fixed or EMA-based rules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-perspective decomposition—improving prediction quality via dual-decoupling and calibrating thresholds via metric-adaptive search—provides a practical recipe for SSMLL practitioners who need strong pseudo-labels under very low annotation budgets (1% labeled data). The MAT strategy is lightweight and metric-agnostic, making it potentially transferable to other pseudo-labeling settings beyond multi-label learning. Open questions include whether the patch-based discriminative branch scales to very large output spaces (e.g., LVIS with 1200+ classes), whether the approach benefits from modern vision-language backbones (the authors note this as future work), and whether the computational overhead of dual classifiers and multi-patch cropping is justified in resource-constrained deployments.
