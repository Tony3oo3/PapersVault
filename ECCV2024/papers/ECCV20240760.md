## Problem

Existing LiDAR-based human motion capture methods assume cleanly segmented human point clouds and degrade significantly under real-world noise from occlusions, sensor artifacts, and human-object interactions. Prior work (LiveHPS) treats noise points identically to human points and ignores global kinematic coherence, producing inaccurate and jerky motions in complex free environments where upstream segmentation is unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240760/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence (window length 32) of single-LiDAR point clouds, each resampled to 256 points via farthest point sampling and normalized by subtracting the per-frame centroid; the inter-frame trajectory relative to the first frame is also fed as auxiliary input. It outputs sequential SMPL parameters (24-joint pose θ, 10-dim shape β), coherent 3D joint positions, a global translation expressed as an offset from the point-cloud centroid (following LIP's formulation), and 6,890-vertex mesh geometry via the SMPL model.

## Method

LiveHPS++ comprises three cascaded modules followed by an SMPL solver. (1) The Trajectory-guided Body Tracker (TBT) uses a frozen guidance network trained on clean vertex+trajectory inputs and a learning network on noisy point clouds+trajectory, with a vertex-trajectory-guided adaptive distillation loss that aligns their fused features (PointNet-GRU point features + MLP trajectory features), enabling robust joint and translation prediction under noise. (2) The Noise-insensitive Velocity Predictor (NVP) applies cross-attention where each predicted joint queries the raw point-cloud features, learning to attend to true human points while suppressing noise, and regresses per-joint and translation velocities over the temporal window. (3) The Kinematic-aware Pose Optimizer (KPO) generates candidate joints by integrating predicted velocities over variable time spans, then uses cross-attention over these candidates to select the most kinematically consistent joints, balancing short-term smoothness against long-term drift. The authors also introduce NoiseMotion, a synthetic dataset combining 1,021,802 SURREAL motions with 51,300 ShapeNet objects to simulate diverse human-object interaction noise during training.

## Results

On the most challenging real-world dataset FreeMotion-OBJ, LiveHPS++ achieves a global vertex error (V Err PST) of 136.94 mm and a jitter of 30.96 ×10² m/s³, improving over LiveHPS* by 6.28% and 69.29% respectively. On the synthetic NoiseMotion dataset, it reduces V Err PST to 64.51 mm (23.05% improvement over LiveHPS) and jitter to 59.35 (13.54% improvement). Across all four datasets (NoiseMotion, FreeMotion, FreeMotion-OBJ, Sloper4D), LiveHPS++ attains the best scores on every metric (J/V Err, Ang Err, Accel Err, Jitter), with particularly large gains in jitter (e.g., 33.16 vs. 85.38 on FreeMotion) and acceleration error (e.g., 3.48 vs. 5.78 on NoiseMotion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiveHPS++ outperforms all three baselines (LiDARCap, LIP, LiveHPS/LiveHPS*) on every metric across all four datasets. Against the closest competitor LiveHPS, the gains are most dramatic on noisy data: on FreeMotion-OBJ, jitter drops from 117.79 to 30.96 (74% reduction) and V Err PST from 158.00 to 136.94; on NoiseMotion, V Err PST improves from 83.84 to 64.51 (23%) and jitter from 68.65 to 59.35 (13.5%). Compared to LiveHPS* (trained on clean synthetic data), LiveHPS++ is far superior on NoiseMotion (J/V Err PS 34.00/42.75 vs. 370.29/432.93), demonstrating that the proposed NoiseMotion synthetic dataset and noise-robust architecture are critical. Against LiDARCap and LIP, which were not designed for noisy inputs, LiveHPS++ achieves roughly 29–30% lower V Err PST and 50% lower jitter on FreeMotion-OBJ. Notably, LiveHPS++ also wins on the less noisy FreeMotion and Sloper4D datasets, confirming that the kinematic-aware optimization does not sacrifice accuracy in cleaner settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LiveHPS [33], which introduced scene-level LiDAR-based human pose and shape estimation with a vertex-guided distillation mechanism, by adding three modules (trajectory-guided body tracker, noise-insensitive velocity predictor, kinematic-aware pose optimizer) that explicitly model dynamic and kinematic features to handle severe noise. It also adopts the translation offset formulation from LIP [34]. The key conceptual departure from prior LiDAR-based methods (LiDARCap, MOVIN, PointHPS) is treating noise from imperfect segmentation and human-object interaction as a first-class problem rather than assuming cleanly segmented human point clouds, and introducing velocity as an intermediate kinematic representation to enforce temporal coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical deployment of LiDAR-based motion capture in unstructured outdoor environments has been blocked by the assumption of clean point clouds; this work demonstrates that a single LiDAR can produce coherent, low-jitter motion even when inputs contain substantial object noise, which is the realistic operating condition. The NoiseMotion dataset (51,300 ShapeNet objects × 1,021,802 SURREAL motions) is a substantial resource for training and evaluating noise-robust methods. Open questions include whether the single-person, single-LiDAR assumption generalizes to multi-person scenes, whether the 32-frame temporal window is sufficient for long-duration capture, and whether the approach maintains real-time inference speed given the added cross-attention and candidate-generation stages.
