## Problem

Existing ID-conditioned face generation methods either combine identity features with text prompts (making it difficult to decouple identity from language descriptions) or are trained on datasets too small in identity count to generalize to arbitrary subjects. The paper demonstrates that common high-resolution datasets like FFHQ (70K images) are insufficient for robust ID-to-face generation, and that million-scale face recognition datasets with significant intra-class variability are necessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240982/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 512-dimensional ArcFace embedding (extracted by a frozen IR-100 ArcFace trained on WebFace42M, L2-normalized) and produces 512×512 FFHQ-aligned face images. No text prompt is required at inference; the embedding is zero-padded to 768 dimensions and injected as a token into a fixed pseudo-prompt before passing through the CLIP encoder. Optionally, 3D face normals rendered from a FLAME model can be supplied via a ControlNet module to control pose and expression.

## Method

Arc2Face fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1.5 (UNet + CLIP text encoder) in two stages: first on ~21M GFPGAN-upsampled WebFace42M images at 448×448 for 5 epochs, then on FFHQ and CelebA-HQ at 512×512 for 15 epochs, using AdamW at lr=1e-6 on 8 A100 GPUs. The key conditioning mechanism repurposes the CLIP text encoder as a face encoder: the ArcFace vector is zero-padded to 768-dim and substituted for a placeholder token in the fixed prompt 'photo of a <id> person', so the encoder learns to project ID embeddings into the CLIP latent space that the UNet's cross-attention layers expect. This design deliberately discards all text conditioning, making the model purely ID-driven. Inference uses DPM-Solver with 25 steps and a classifier-free guidance scale of 3. An ablation confirms that using the pretrained CLIP encoder for projection outperforms a simple MLP, and PCA analysis shows that at least 300–400 principal components are needed to preserve facial fidelity.

## Results

On the primary ID-consistent generation task, Arc2Face achieves the best scores across all four metrics on both Synth-500 and AgeDB: LPIPS diversity of 0.506/0.508, expression diversity of 6.375/5.966, pose diversity of 0.317/0.273, and FID of 5.673/6.628. In the secondary FR-with-synthetic-data experiment, Arc2Face-trained models reach 91.73% average verification accuracy (0.5M regime) and 93.14% (1.2M regime), surpassing the best prior synthetic-data method DCFace by 2.17% and 1.93% respectively. A user study with 50 participants confirms strong preference for Arc2Face over InstantID in perceived ID resemblance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Arc2Face outperforms all six ID-conditioned generation baselines on every metric in Table 1. Against the closest competitor InstantID [82], Arc2Face achieves substantially higher pose diversity (0.317 vs 0.059 on Synth-500) because InstantID constrains generation with input facial landmarks, and also improves FID by 17.2 points (5.673 vs 22.859). CLIP-based methods (FastComposer, PhotoMaker, IPA-FaceID variants) show markedly lower ID similarity and diversity, confirming the paper's argument that text/CLIP features are insufficient for faithful ID reproduction without detailed prompts. In the FR-with-synthetic-data setting (Table 2), Arc2Face surpasses DCFace [41] by 2.17% average accuracy in the 0.5M regime (91.73% vs 89.56%) and by 1.93% in the 1.2M regime (93.14% vs 91.21%), with the largest gap on CFP-FP (91.87% vs 85.33% at 0.5M), indicating superior ID consistency under large pose variation. DigiFace [4] and SynFace [58] trail further behind, with DigiFace at 83.45% and SynFace at 74.75% average in the 0.5M setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

Arc2Face extends the latent diffusion framework (Stable Diffusion) by repurposing the CLIP text encoder as an ArcFace-to-CLIP projector, conditioning generation solely on 512-dim face recognition embeddings rather than the text+ID hybrid used by prior subject-driven methods (PhotoMaker, IP-Adapter, InstantID). It departs conceptually from the dominant paradigm of combining identity features with textual prompts, arguing that FR embeddings are a self-sufficient and more compact representation of facial identity. The dataset contribution—upsampling ~21M images from WebFace42M via GFPGAN—addresses a gap identified empirically: high-quality but small datasets like FFHQ lack the intra-class variability needed for robust ID-conditioned generation, a finding that distinguishes this work from prior inversion-based approaches (Vec2Face, Kansy et al.) trained on low-resolution FR data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first publicly released ID-conditioned face foundation model, Arc2Face provides a practical baseline for identity-consistent generation that removes the prompt-engineering burden and text-identity entanglement plaguing prior methods. The demonstration that synthetic images from this model can train FR models outperforming DCFace and DigiFace suggests a path toward privacy-preserving face recognition training. Key limitations remain: single-person generation, dependence on ArcFace's representational ceiling, and the tight facial crop inherited from WebFace42M. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes, higher resolutions, or other identity modalities (voice, 3D geometry), and whether the CLIP-encoder-as-projector design generalizes beyond the SD v1.5 architecture.
