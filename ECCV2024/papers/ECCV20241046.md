## Problem

Existing 3D shape generation methods using signed distance fields (SDFs) cannot represent open surfaces or complex topologies, while grid-based approaches to learning distance fields fail to provide accurate gradients, are resolution-limited, and incur high memory costs. This paper addresses the gap by being the first to employ Unsigned Distance Fields (UDFs) within a latent diffusion framework, enabling high-quality surface generation for arbitrary topologies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241046/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The autoencoder consumes 10,000 surface-sampled points (N=10000) from arbitrary-topology meshes and outputs a 64-dimensional latent code; the decoder then maps any 3D query coordinate to a UDF value clipped at δ=0.1 and normalized to [0,1]. For conditional generation, the model accepts category labels, 2D Canny-edge sketches, single RGB images, or text prompts (encoded via frozen CLIP) as conditioning signals. The final output is a continuous UDF field extracted into a 3D mesh at up to 512×512×512 resolution using MeshUDF bisection search, with a total model size of only 564M parameters.

## Method

Surf-D replaces discrete grid-based shape embedding with a point-based AutoEncoder: a Dynamic Graph CNN encoder compresses surface point clouds into a 64-dim latent code, and a Conditional Batch Normalization MLP decoder predicts UDF values at arbitrary query points, yielding a continuous field with reliable differentiable gradients essential for accurate mesh extraction. A curriculum learning strategy progressively adds harder samples (ranked by training loss) to the dataset, preventing model collapse to mean shapes. A DDPM latent diffusion model (T=1000 steps) is then trained in this compact latent space to learn the shape distribution. For conditional generation, cross-attention layers inject task-specific CLIP-encoded conditions (text, image, or sketch) into the UNet's intermediate layers, extending the standard denoising objective with a conditioning term.

## Results

On unconditional generation (Deepfashion3D), Surf-D achieves MMD of 8.91×10⁻³, COV of 52.44%, and 1-NNA of 57.93%, substantially outperforming SDFusion (MMD 14.36) and LAS-Diffusion (MMD 13.77). For sketch-conditioned generation, Surf-D reduces Chamfer Distance to 12.52×10⁻³ and EMD to 11.14×10⁻² while boosting Voxel-IOU to 44.06×10⁻², compared to 34.79×10⁻² for SDFusion. Notably, Surf-D operates at 512³ resolution with only 564M model size, versus 64³ at 4.29G for SDFusion and 128³ at 859M for LAS-Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Surf-D outperforms SDFusion [12] across all quantitative metrics: on unconditional generation, MMD drops from 14.36 to 8.91 (−38%) and 1-NNA improves from 95.73% to 57.93% (closer to the ideal 50%); on sketch-conditioned generation, Voxel-IOU rises from 34.79% to 44.06% (+9.3 pts). Against LAS-Diffusion [88], Surf-D similarly wins on every metric, with MMD improving from 13.77 to 8.91 and IOU jumping from 34.61% to 44.06%. The 1-NNA gap is particularly striking: baselines score above 95% (indicating generated shapes are nearly duplicates of training samples), while Surf-D's 57.93% confirms substantially greater diversity. Diffusion-SDF [28] is compared only qualitatively in the text-guided generation task (Fig. 8), where Surf-D produces shapes more faithfully aligned with textual descriptions. In terms of efficiency, Surf-D achieves 512³ resolution at 564M parameters, an 8× resolution increase over SDFusion (64³, 4.29G) at less than 1/7 the memory cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Surf-D extends the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) to 3D shape generation by replacing the standard SDF representation with Unsigned Distance Fields (UDF) and a point-based AutoEncoder (using Dynamic Graph CNN) in place of the discrete grid-based encoders used by SDFusion, Diffusion-SDF, and LAS-Diffusion. The key conceptual departure is that prior diffusion-based shape generators rely on fixed-resolution 3D feature grids that cannot provide stable gradients for UDF, whereas Surf-D learns a continuous field via a point-query decoder, enabling reliable mesh extraction through MeshUDF's bisection search. The curriculum learning strategy for shape embedding is borrowed from the broader curriculum learning literature but applied here specifically to prevent mode collapse in the latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Surf-D is the first diffusion-based shape generator to handle arbitrary topologies (open surfaces, non-manifold structures) via UDF, addressing a fundamental limitation of SDF-based methods that dominate the field. The practical efficiency gain is substantial: 564M parameters at 512³ resolution versus 4.29G at 64³ for SDFusion, making high-resolution surface generation more accessible. However, evaluation is concentrated on garments (Deepfashion3D) and a few ShapeNet categories (chairs, tables), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to highly diverse shape distributions. The reliance on bisection search for mesh extraction from UDFs, while effective, introduces an additional inference step that could be a bottleneck for real-time applications.
