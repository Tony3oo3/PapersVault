## Problem

Existing low-light image enhancement methods either require paired training data that is difficult to obtain, or rely on prior training on specific low-light distributions, making them brittle to unseen scenes and varying under-exposure levels. CNN-based approaches are further constrained by local receptive fields, limiting their ability to handle large dark regions in high-resolution images. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a zero-shot framework that requires no paired data or domain-specific pretraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-light RGB image, converts it to HSV space, and operates exclusively on the Value (brightness) channel. Training is performed at a downscaled 256×256 resolution for efficiency, and the resulting enhanced Value map is upscaled back to the original resolution via a guided filter. The final output is a composite RGB image formed by combining the original Hue and Saturation channels with the enhanced Value channel, alongside the estimated illumination map.

## Method

CoLIE formulates illumination estimation as a neural implicit representation problem: a two-branch MLP with SIREN activation layers maps 2D pixel coordinates and a 7×7 local context window (from the Value channel) to a residual that, when added to the input Value, yields the illumination component. The enhanced reflectance is then recovered via element-wise division per Retinex theory. Training is zero-shot, using a single-image loss that combines MSE fidelity, total-variation smoothness, a gamma-controlled exposure term targeting a target intensity threshold L, and an L1 sparsity penalty to prevent over-exposure of dark regions. The guided filter integration decouples the NIR inference (at low resolution) from the final upscaling, keeping runtime largely independent of input image size.

## Results

On the MIT dataset, CoLIE achieves the best PSNR (18.67) and SSIM (0.8317) among all 11 compared methods, outperforming the strongest supervised baseline HWMNet (17.03 PSNR, 0.8190 SSIM) by 1.64 dB in PSNR. On the UHD-LL dataset, CoLIE attains the highest PSNR (17.89) while achieving a competitive SSIM of 0.6252 (second only to RUAS-LOL at 0.6970). On the DarkFace dataset using the no-reference EMEE metric, CoLIE scores 6.7473, surpassing the best unsupervised method SCI-difficult (6.3856) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLIE outperforms all 10 baselines on the MIT dataset, achieving 18.67 PSNR and 0.8317 SSIM versus the best supervised method HWMNet (17.03 PSNR, 0.8190 SSIM) and the best zero-shot method LIME (14.98 PSNR, 0.5418 SSIM), a gap of 3.69 dB over LIME. On UHD-LL, CoLIE leads in PSNR (17.89) over SCI-difficult (17.85) and LIME (17.35), though RUAS-LOL achieves a higher SSIM (0.6970 vs. 0.6252). On the no-reference EMEE metric for DarkFace, CoLIE (6.7473) beats SCI-difficult (6.3856) and RUAS-DARK (6.2555), while PairLIE (1.7985) and EnGAN (2.9794) perform poorly. Notably, CoLIE operates in a zero-shot setting without any paired or domain-specific training data, whereas all other methods require supervised or unsupervised pre-training on specific low-light datasets. In the downstream detection task using Grounding DINO, CoLIE is the only method that reveals all individuals in the background of a DarkFace image, enabling complete detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLIE repositions low-light image enhancement within the neural implicit representation (NIR) paradigm, building on the SIREN-based MLP architecture from Li et al. (CVPR 2023) for metadata-based raw reconstruction, but repurposing it to estimate the Retinex illumination component rather than reconstruct raw sensor data. It departs from the dominant CNN-based LLIE literature (ZeroDCE, RUAS, SCI, PairLIE) by eliminating the need for paired or domain-specific training data entirely, and from the closest NIR-based competitor NeRCo (Yang et al., 2023) by using the implicit function directly for illumination extraction rather than as a pre-processing step before a ResNet. The formulation in HSV space—operating only on the Value channel while preserving Hue and Saturation—further distinguishes it from RGB-to-RGB mapping approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, no-paired-data requirement addresses a persistent practical bottleneck in LLIE: domain gaps between training and deployment conditions that force retraining for each new under-exposure regime. The guided-filter upscaling strategy makes the approach scalable to high-resolution imagery without proportional compute growth, and the microscopy vignetting correction demonstrates transferability beyond consumer photography. However, the method's restriction to the Value channel leaves it vulnerable to noise in Hue and Saturation, and the per-image optimization (100 epochs at 256×256) may still be too slow for real-time applications. Open questions include whether the context-window conditioning can be replaced by learned positional encodings for further speedup, and whether multi-channel NIR could address the noise limitation.
