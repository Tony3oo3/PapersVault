## Problem

Video understanding requires simultaneously handling local spatiotemporal redundancy and long-range dependencies, but 3D CNNs and video transformers each address only one of these challenges. Attention-based models suffer from quadratic complexity that becomes prohibitive for high-resolution long videos, while the question of whether Mamba's linear-complexity selective state space model can effectively replace attention for 3D video sequences remained unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240685/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB video clip of shape 3×T×H×W and projects it into L = t×h×w spatiotemporal tokens via a 1×1×16×16 3D convolution, prepending a learnable [CLS] token and adding separate spatial and temporal position embeddings. For long-video tasks it processes up to 64 frames at 384×384 resolution end-to-end, producing action class labels (K400, SthSthV2), activity scores (Breakfast, COIN), regression predictions (LVU), or video-text retrieval rankings. In the multimodal setting, video tokens are jointly encoded with a BERT text encoder and a cross-modal decoder for contrastive and matching objectives.

## Method

VideoMamba is a purely SSM-based encoder that strictly follows the vanilla ViT architecture (no downsampling, no attention) but replaces self-attention with stacked Bidirectional Mamba (B-Mamba) blocks operating over a Spatial-First 3D scan order—tokens are arranged spatially within each frame, then stacked frame-by-frame, and processed in both forward and backward directions. The key insight is that Mamba's selective scan mechanism (S6) with data-dependent B, C, and Δ parameters provides linear-complexity dynamic spatiotemporal modeling, enabling 6× faster throughput and 40× less GPU memory than TimeSformer for 64-frame videos. To counter the overfitting observed when scaling Mamba models, the paper introduces a self-distillation strategy where a smaller well-trained model guides the larger student via L2 alignment of final feature maps. For temporal sensitivity, a masked alignment pretraining phase uses row-based masking strategies (clip-row, frame-row, attention masking) tailored to the 1D convolution preceding the SSM, aligning unmasked tokens with CLIP-ViT features before joint video-text pretraining.

## Results

On Kinetics-400, VideoMamba-M (64×384², IN-1K pretraining) achieves 83.3% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming TimeSformer-L (80.7%) by +2.6% and ViViT-L (81.3%) by +2.0% with 6× faster throughput and 40× less GPU memory. With masked pretraining (CLIP-400M, 800 epochs), VideoMamba-M reaches 85.0% Top-1 on K400 and 71.4% on SthSthV2, surpassing UMT-B (85.7% / 70.8%). On long-term benchmarks, VideoMamba-M† achieves 97.9% on Breakfast and 90.4% on COIN, exceeding feature-based methods (ViS4mer: 88.2%/88.4%) by +9.7%/+2.0%. On ImageNet-1K, VideoMamba-M at 576² resolution reaches 84.0% Top-1 with only 74M parameters, outperforming ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) and DeiT-B (83.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On K400, VideoMamba-M (64×384²) beats TimeSformer-L by +2.6% and ViViT-L by +2.0% Top-1 while using far fewer parameters (74M vs. 121M/311M) and 6× less compute; it also edges out UniFormer-B (83.0%) by +0.3%. On SthSthV2, VideoMamba-M (16×288²) surpasses ViViT-L by +3.0% and TimeSformer-HR by +5.9%, confirming superior temporal sensitivity for fine-grained actions. In the masked-pretraining setting, VideoMamba-M 800e (64×384²) reaches 85.0% on K400, slightly trailing UMT-B (85.7%) by 0.7% but exceeding VideoMAE-B (81.5%) by +3.5%; on SthSthV2 it leads UMT-B (70.8%) by +0.6%. For long-term video, VideoMamba-M† (f32) achieves 97.9% on Breakfast, outperforming ViS4mer (88.2%) by +9.7% and Distant Supervision (89.9%) by +8.0%, and 90.4% on COIN vs. 90.0% for Distant Supervision. In multi-modal retrieval, VideoMamba (25M pairs) improves over UMT (25M) on MSRVTT (+0.4% @1) and shows a large gap on ActivityNet (+6.3% @1: 41.0 vs. 33.8), indicating stronger long-video cross-modal alignment. On ImageNet-1K, VideoMamba-M (576²) achieves 84.0% Top-1, beating ConvNeXt-B (83.8%), DeiT-B (83.1%), and VMamba-B (83.7%), while VideoMamba-Ti/S outperform Vim-Ti/S by +0.8%/+0.7% at 224².

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMamba extends the Mamba selective state space model (S6) from 1D NLP sequences through Vision Mamba's bidirectional 2D scanning to a purely SSM-based 3D video architecture, strictly following the ViT patch-embedding and stacked-block design. It departs from prior video models (TimeSformer, ViViT, UniFormer) that rely on quadratic-complexity attention or hybrid convolution-attention, and from VMamba which adds depthwise convolution, by using only bidirectional SSM blocks with a spatial-first 3D scan. A key methodological departure is the introduction of self-distillation to counter the overfitting that plagues larger Mamba models, a problem also observed in VMamba but not previously addressed in the video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-complexity SSM operator makes end-to-end training on long, high-resolution videos (64+ frames at 384×384) practically feasible, achieving 6× speedup and 40× less GPU memory versus TimeSformer while matching or exceeding accuracy. The self-distillation technique offers a general recipe for scaling SSM-based vision models without large-scale pretraining, which could accelerate the adoption of Mamba architectures across video and image tasks. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge they have not validated scaling to VideoMamba-g, integration with audio or LLMs for hour-level understanding, and whether SSMs can fully replace attention in all video subtasks as model and sequence lengths grow further.
