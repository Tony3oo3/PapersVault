## Problem

ControlNet and similar layout-controllable T2I methods assume the visual condition precisely matches the text prompt, but in practice users extract rough conditions from unrelated images, causing artifacts and misalignment. While reducing a global control scale can partially mitigate this, a single scalar cannot simultaneously satisfy regions that need strong structural adherence and regions that must be freed from conflicting constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241265/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a tiger') and a rough visual condition (depth map, Canny edge, segmentation mask, or HED edge) extracted from a different image whose subject class may differ from the prompt. It outputs a single photo-realistic image that follows the prompt's semantics while preserving compatible structural cues from the condition. The control scale predictor operates at every decoder block of the U-Net, producing a pixel-wise spatial map at the latent resolution (64×64 for 512×512 output), so the relaxation is applied per-region rather than globally.

## Method

SmartControl inserts a lightweight Control Scale Predictor (three conv+ReLU blocks followed by a sigmoid) into each decoder block of a frozen ControlNet. Instead of feeding raw text and condition into the predictor, it exploits generative priors by using the text-conditioned feature h^i and the combined feature h^i + h_cond^i as inputs, which already encode the semantic mismatch between prompt and condition. The predictor outputs a pixel-wise alpha map that scales the condition feature before it is added to the decoder state, effectively relaxing control in conflict regions while preserving it elsewhere. Training combines the standard LDM denoising loss with a mask-based regularization that pushes alpha below 0.2 in conflict regions and above 0.8 in background, using only 1,000–2,000 manually filtered samples constructed by iterating global alpha values over OpenImage-derived condition–prompt pairs with class-hierarchy-mismatched labels.

## Results

On the depth condition, SmartControl achieves the best CLIP score (0.274) and Class Confidence (0.742) among all methods, while maintaining a competitive Self-similarity distance (0.128). Across all four condition types (depth, Canny, segmentation, HED), SmartControl consistently attains the highest CLIP and Class Confidence scores, e.g., 0.277 CLIP and 0.780 CLASS on segmentation. In a GPT-4V-based pairwise evaluation on depth conditions, SmartControl is ranked better in 67% of cases versus ControlNet (α=α_fix), and in a 20-user study, 78.3% of users prefer SmartControl's outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SmartControl outperforms all five baseline configurations on both CLIP score and Class Confidence across all four condition types. Against ControlNet (α=1.0), which strictly follows rough conditions, SmartControl improves CLIP by 0.017 (depth) and CLASS by 0.140 (depth), at the cost of a slightly higher Self-similarity distance (0.128 vs. 0.100) — a trade-off the authors argue is desirable since lower Sim here reflects over-adherence to mismatched conditions. Against the fixed-scale baselines (ControlNet α=α_fix and T2I-Adapter α=α_fix), SmartControl gains 0.006 CLIP and 0.032 CLASS on depth, and 0.010 CLIP and 0.084 CLASS on segmentation, demonstrating that spatially adaptive control scales beat a single global weight. UniControl is the weakest baseline, particularly on HED (0.227 CLIP, 0.336 CLASS), where SmartControl achieves 0.276 and 0.768 respectively. The only metric where SmartControl does not dominate is Self-similarity distance, where ControlNet (α=1.0) scores lowest; however, the authors note this reflects excessive structural copying of the rough condition rather than genuine fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SmartControl extends the ControlNet/Stable Diffusion framework for controllable text-to-image generation by challenging the implicit assumption that visual conditions and text prompts are aligned. While prior methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, UniControl) operate under the premise of compatible inputs, and LooseControl relaxes control to a 3D bounding box (sacrificing pose fidelity), SmartControl introduces a spatially adaptive control scale predictor that identifies local conflicts between prompt and condition and modulates the condition's influence pixel-wise. The method leverages generative priors already embedded in the ControlNet backbone (features h and h+h_cond) to avoid training a large standalone conflict detector, and constructs a small unaligned-condition dataset (1,000–2,000 samples) via a class-hierarchy-based pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely practical gap: users routinely extract rough conditions from unrelated images, and existing methods either rigidly follow the condition (producing artifacts) or require manual per-image tuning of a global control weight. The finding that a lightweight predictor trained on only ~2k samples generalizes to unseen object classes is practically valuable for deployment. However, the dataset construction still relies on manual filtering, the evaluation is limited to single-object scenes with one class substitution, and the method is tied to the ControlNet architecture. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object compositions, more complex spatial conflicts (e.g., partial pose mismatches rather than whole-object swaps), and whether the conflict-detection signal degrades at very low control scales.
