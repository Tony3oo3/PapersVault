## Problem

Despite a decade of adversarial training research, the internal dynamics of how robust classifiers learn remain poorly understood, and robust overfitting lacks a mechanistic explanation. This paper asks: what happens to the energy landscape of a classifier during adversarial training, and can this perspective unify and explain the behavior of existing methods (TRADES, MART, MAIL) as well as the unexpected generative capabilities of robust models?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240080/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The classifier takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/SVHN, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces class labels; during training, inputs are perturbed with ℓ∞- or ℓ2-bounded adversarial examples generated via PGD or KL-divergence-based optimization. For the generative component, the method takes only a target class label and produces synthetic images by running momentum-augmented SGLD MCMC from a PCA-based initialization near the class manifold, requiring no additional generative training.

## Method

The paper reinterprets a standard softmax classifier as an Energy-based Model, defining joint energy E_θ(x,y) = −logit_y and marginal energy E_θ(x) = −logsumexp(logits), so that cross-entropy loss equals E_θ(x,y) − E_θ(x). It shows that untargeted PGD drives marginal energy downward (making adversarial points appear more in-distribution) while targeted attacks push it upward, and identifies three training phases in which robust overfitting manifests as a sharp divergence between natural and adversarial marginal energies. By rewriting the TRADES KL-divergence term as an energy expression (Proposition 1), the paper demonstrates that TRADES implicitly aligns natural and adversarial energies, explaining its superior overfitting resistance. Building on this, WEAT weights the entire outer loss (CE + β·KL) by a scalar function log(1+exp(|E_θ(x)|))^{−1} that up-weights high-energy samples and down-weights low-energy ones, with E_θ(x) detached from backpropagation to prevent trivial solutions. For generation, the paper proposes a PCA-based SGLD initialization that starts the MCMC chain near the class manifold using per-class principal components weighted by singular values, combined with a momentum term to accelerate convergence, yielding competitive IS and FID from a single robust classifier without generative retraining.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, WEAT_ADV achieves 53.35% PGD and 49.75% AutoAttack robust accuracy (81.00% clean), while WEAT_NAT achieves 52.43% PGD and 49.02% AA (83.36% clean). On CIFAR-100, WEAT_ADV reaches 30.90% PGD and 25.63% AA, surpassing TRADES (28.53% PGD, 24.29% AA) by over 2 points. On Tiny-ImageNet, WEAT_ADV attains 24.39% PGD and 18.45% AA, outperforming DyART (18.02% AA) and TRADES (17.24% AA). For generative evaluation, the PCA-initialized SGLD method achieves IS 8.97 and FID 30.74 using the Better DM classifier under ℓ₂, exceeding FID of several hybrid EBM models trained explicitly for generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WEAT_ADV outperforms SAT by 4.3 points on CIFAR-10 PGD (53.35 vs 49.03) and 3.8 points on CIFAR-100 PGD (30.90 vs 23.89), while WEAT_NAT matches or exceeds TRADES on clean accuracy across all datasets. On CIFAR-100, both WEAT variants surpass TRADES and MAIL-TRADES by 1.2–2.4 points in PGD accuracy and 0.6–1.3 points in AA accuracy. On Tiny-ImageNet, WEAT_ADV achieves 18.45% AA, beating DyART (18.02%), MART (17.79%), and TRADES (17.24%) without the extra computational cost of DyART's trajectory optimization. For generative evaluation, the PCA-initialized SGLD method using the Better DM [53] classifier reaches FID 30.74 and IS 8.97 under ℓ₂, surpassing JEM (FID 38.4), JEAT (FID 38.24), JEM++ (FID 37.1), and M-EBM (FID 21.1) in FID while matching or exceeding their IS scores, and outperforming the Gaussian initialization baseline (FID 27.71, IS 8.75) from the same model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EBM reinterpretation of adversarial training initiated by Grathwohl et al. [23] and Zhu et al. [66], pushing the analysis from a static energy decomposition to a dynamic, phase-based understanding of how AT reshapes the energy landscape over training. It builds directly on TRADES [62] and SAT [36] as the base training paradigms, but departs from prior sample-weighting approaches (MART [52], MAIL [34], GAIRAT [64]) by weighting on the label-independent marginal energy E_θ(x) rather than misclassification status or probabilistic margins. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical decomposition of TRADES's KL divergence into conditional and marginal energy terms (Proposition 1), which reveals that TRADES implicitly enforces energy alignment between natural and adversarial samples—a mechanism absent from SAT and prior weighting schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-phase energy dynamics analysis and the finding that robust overfitting corresponds to a drastic divergence between natural and adversarial marginal energies provide a unifying lens that reconciles several previously separate observations (flat minima, misclassification weighting, data augmentation benefits) under a single EBM framework. Practically, WEAT offers a simple, label-free weighting scheme that matches or slightly exceeds SOTA on standard benchmarks without extra compute, and the PCA-initialized SGLD method demonstrates that robust classifiers can serve as competitive generative models without any generative retraining. Open questions include whether the energy weighting function can be adapted to the data's energy distribution, how these insights scale to larger architectures and datasets like ImageNet, and whether the EBM framework can explain score-based unrestricted adversarial examples.
