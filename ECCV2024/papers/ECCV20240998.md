## Problem

In standard gait recognition pipelines, the classifier—which accounts for the majority of network weights (e.g., 92.1% in GaitBase on GREW)—is discarded at test time because training and test identities differ. This paper asks whether these well-trained classifier weights can be repurposed as a more discriminative and robust representation, arguing that gait identity is better captured through relationships to a set of reference gaits than through individual-specific features alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240998/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a gait silhouette sequence (video frames of a walking person) as input, processes it through a standard spatial-temporal backbone (e.g., GaitBase or GaitGL) to obtain a d-dimensional embedding, then projects this embedding onto a fixed set of pre-selected gait anchors (classifier weight vectors) to produce a relation descriptor vector of cosine similarities. The descriptor dimension equals the number of selected anchors and can be reduced to match the original embedding dimension via SVD, so no extra storage is incurred at inference. Identity matching is performed by Euclidean distance between two relation descriptors.

## Method

The core contribution is reinterpreting the BNNeck cosine-similarity classifier's weight matrix as a set of gait anchors (GAs) and using the normalized dot product between a test embedding and these anchors as the final representation (Relation Descriptor), rather than discarding the classifier. To handle dimension expansion on large datasets, a Farthest gait-Anchor Selection (FAS) greedy algorithm iteratively picks the classifier weight with the largest absolute cosine distance from already-selected anchors, maximizing the spanning space of the chosen subset; SVD then reduces the descriptor to the original embedding dimension when needed. To address GA overfitting on small datasets (C < d), an Orthogonal Regularization Loss (ORL) is added to the training objective, pushing cosine similarity to non-ground-truth class weights toward 0 (orthogonal) rather than −1 (anti-correlated), which CE loss alone encourages. The entire approach is model-agnostic, adds zero extra parameters, and requires only a one-time FAS pass after training.

## Results

On GREW (20k training identities), the proposed Relation Descriptor with FAS boosts GaitBase from 60.1% to 65.5% Rank-1 accuracy (+5.4%), and on Gait3D from 64.6% to 70.1% Rank-1 (+5.5%), 55.2% to 61.9% mAP (+6.7%), and 36.2% to 41.3% mINP (+5.8%). On smaller datasets, improvements are more modest: CASIA-B mean accuracy rises from 92.8% to 93.4% (with GaitGL), CCPG-G mean Rank-1 from 78.3% to 81.6% (+3.3%), and OU-MVLP remains comparable at ~91.3%. The method adds no extra parameters and the final descriptor dimension equals or is smaller than the original embedding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all 17 compared baselines across five datasets. Against the strongest prior methods on GREW, it surpasses MTSGait (55.3%) by 10.2% and CSTL (50.6%) by 14.9% in Rank-1 accuracy, while the primary GaitBase baseline improves by 5.4%. On Gait3D, the method beats SMPLGait (46.3%) by 23.8% and GaitSet (36.7%) by 33.4% in Rank-1, with the GaitBase baseline gaining 5.5%. On constrained indoor datasets (CASIA-B, CCPG-G, OU-MVLP), gains are smaller (0.6–3.3%) because covariates are well-controlled during training, reducing the relative advantage of relation-based projection. The method is model-agnostic: when applied to GaitPart, GPGait, QAGait, and DyGait on Gait3D, it yields improvements of +9.8%, +5.5%, +3.7%, and +3.1% respectively, confirming that the Relation Descriptor is a plug-and-play enhancement independent of the backbone architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a standard component of the gait recognition pipeline—the classifier weights trained with cross-entropy loss—as a set of 'gait anchors' whose cosine similarities with test features form a new Relation Descriptor. Conceptually, it draws from Archetypal Analysis for the Farthest gait-Anchor Selection algorithm and from open-set recognition and image denoising literature for the projection-based bias suppression argument. It departs from prior work (including GEINet, which used inner-product logits without justification) by providing a relational interpretation of classifier weights and showing that cosine-similarity logits, not raw embeddings, serve as a more discriminative test-time representation. The method is model-agnostic and plugs into any existing backbone (GaitBase, GaitGL, GaitPart, GPGait, QAGait, DyGait) without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'free lunch' framing is practically compelling: a ~5% absolute rank-1 improvement on large-scale unconstrained datasets (GREW, Gait3D) with zero additional parameters or inference cost, and consistent gains across six different backbones, makes this an easy adoption for practitioners. The finding that RD benefits are largest under unconstrained conditions (where covariate bias dominates) and smallest under controlled lab settings (OU-MVLP) clarifies when the technique is most valuable. Key limitations remain: the method requires labeled training identities (ruling out unsupervised settings), and the authors acknowledge that learnable GA generation and cross-domain generalization are open problems that could further extend the approach.
