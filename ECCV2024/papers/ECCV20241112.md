## Problem

State-of-the-art CNN-based stereo matching networks suffer significant performance degradation when evaluated on unseen domains, and existing domain generalization methods rely on target-domain priors or limited training data to learn domain-invariant features. Although foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, SAM) produce domain-robust representations, their coarse-grained ViT tokens lack the fine-grained, cross-view cost structure required by stereo matching pipelines, and no prior work has adapted a foundation model to this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of rectified RGB stereo images (randomly cropped to 448×448 during training) and produces a disparity map at 1/4 input resolution with a maximum disparity range of 192 pixels. The frozen ViT encoder splits each image into non-overlapping 14×14 patches, yielding N×D token sequences (where N = HW/p² + 1 including the [CLS] token), which are then decoded back to dense pixel-level feature maps before cost volume construction.

## Method

FormerStereo integrates a frozen ViT-based foundation model (DINOv2, SAM, or DAM) into a stereo matching pipeline via two key modules. First, a feature transformation decoder uses a U-shape multi-layer fusion network with Feature Convex Upsampling (FCU) blocks to recover fine-grained high-resolution representations from coarse ViT tokens, supervised by an additional image reconstruction MSE loss. Second, a Cosine-Constrained Concatenation (C4) cost space constructs a 4D concatenation volume while simultaneously training a 3D cosine-similarity score volume with a pixel-level infoNCE contrastive loss (masked for occluded regions) to enforce cross-view feature consistency. The resulting cost volume is passed to any standard cost aggregation backbone (PSMNet, GwcNet, CFNet, RAFT-Stereo), making the framework a plug-and-play generalization module. The total loss combines disparity Smooth-L1, reconstruction MSE, and cosine contrastive terms with fixed weights (λ₀=0.1, λ₁=λ₂=1) across all aggregation backbones.

## Results

FormerStereo achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot stereo matching performance when trained only on SceneFlow and evaluated without fine-tuning. The best configurations (Former-GwcNet with DAM, Former-PSMNet with DINOv2) achieve Bad error rates of 4.0% (ETH3D Bad 1.0), 6.6% (Middlebury half Bad 2.0), 4.9% (Middlebury quarter Bad 2.0), 3.9% (KITTI 2012 Bad 3.0), and 5.0% (KITTI 2015 Bad 3.0). The framework consistently improves all four tested baselines (PSMNet, GwcNet, CFNet, RAFT-Stereo) by 14–51% in Bad error rate across datasets. On DrivingStereo under adverse weather, Former-PSMNet reduces average Bad 3.0 from 55.4% (PSMNet) to 6.75%, outperforming even KITTI-finetuned models on rainy scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FormerStereo outperforms all domain-generalization baselines (DSMNet, PCWNet, FC-PSMNet, MS-PSMNet, ITSA, GraftNet, HVT, MaskNet) on the majority of metrics across ETH3D, Middlebury, and KITTI. The most notable gains are on Middlebury, where Former-GwcNet(DAM) achieves 6.6% Bad 2.0 (half) versus 10.3% for HVT-GwcNet and 19.8% for MS-PSMNet, reflecting the advantage of foundation-model semantics in indoor scenes. On KITTI 2012, Former-PSMNet(DINOv2) and Former-RAFT(DAM) both reach 3.9% Bad 3.0, slightly trailing HVT-RAFT (3.7%) but surpassing Graft-PSMNet (4.9%) and HVT-PSMNet (4.3%). The paper notes that HVT's KITTI 2015 numbers use the D1 metric rather than Bad 3.0; under a fair D1 comparison, FormerStereo still edges out HVT (4.77% vs. 4.9% for PSMNet, 4.94% vs. 5.0% for GwcNet, 4.82% vs. 4.9% for CFNet). Compared to the closest competitors GraftNet and MS-Net, which also use external features but rely on hand-crafted or task-oriented descriptors, FormerStereo's ViT-based foundation model features yield 20–35% relative reductions in Bad error on KITTI and Middlebury. The only clear loss is HVT-RAFT on KITTI 2012 (3.7 vs. 3.9), a 0.2-point margin the authors attribute to metric differences in the original HVT reporting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of domain-generalized stereo matching (DSMNet, ITSA, HVT, GraftNet, MaskNet) that learns domain-invariant representations from limited training data, instead repurposing pre-trained ViT-based foundation models (DINOv2, SAM, DAM) as frozen feature encoders whose all-purpose tokens already carry cross-domain invariance. It also distinguishes itself from CroCo/CroCo v2, which train a ViT from scratch for cross-view completion and are incompatible with existing aggregation pipelines, and from MS-Net/GraftNet, which use hand-crafted or task-oriented external features that lack matching-specific semantics. The key architectural contribution is a lightweight feature transformation decoder (U-shape multi-level fusion with convex upsampling) and a C4 cost space that bridge the gap between coarse-grained ViT tokens and the fine-grained, cross-view-consistent representations required by standard stereo aggregation modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a frozen foundation model plus a small trainable adapter can consistently improve zero-shot stereo matching across four different aggregation backbones (PSMNet, GwcNet, CFNet, RAFT-Stereo) and four realistic datasets, this work provides a practical, plug-and-play recipe for injecting domain generalization into any existing stereo pipeline without retraining the backbone. A notable limitation is that KITTI was included in DINOv2's pre-training corpus (flagged with a dagger), which may inflate those specific numbers, and the approach still requires training the decoder and C4 space on SceneFlow. Open questions include whether the frozen-encoder assumption remains optimal at larger scales, whether the framework extends to other dense prediction tasks (optical flow, depth), and whether the reconstruction constraint can be replaced by self-supervised objectives to reduce reliance on paired training data.
