## Problem

Existing diffusion sampling guidance methods (classifier guidance and classifier-free guidance) require additional training or external modules, reduce sample diversity, and are entirely unavailable in unconditional generation. This leaves a critical gap in downstream tasks such as inverse problems (inpainting, deblurring, super-resolution) and ControlNet with empty prompts, where no text condition exists to drive CFG, resulting in structurally collapsed or low-quality outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241939/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

PAG operates on pretrained diffusion models (ADM at 256×256 pixel space, Stable Diffusion 1.5 and SDXL in latent space) without any retraining or architectural modification. It accepts the same inputs as standard diffusion sampling—random noise, optional text prompts, spatial conditions (pose, depth), or noisy measurements for inverse problems—and produces the same outputs (generated or restored images). The only additional computation is a second forward pass through the U-Net with perturbed self-attention, which can be batched alongside the original pass, making the overhead comparable to CFG.

## Method

PAG frames sampling guidance through an implicit WGAN-style discriminator that separates desirable (real-data-like) from undesirable (structurally degraded) samples. The undesirable path is constructed by replacing selected self-attention maps in the diffusion U-Net with identity matrices (Perturbed Self-Attention, PSA), which removes inter-patch structural information while preserving per-patch appearance encoded in the value vectors. The guidance signal is the difference between the original and perturbed noise predictions, ε_θ(x_t) − ε̂_θ(x_t), which is added to the original prediction scaled by a guidance parameter s. This design avoids out-of-distribution issues that arise from perturbing inputs or value features directly, since the identity-matrix attention keeps downstream MLP inputs within their training distribution. PAG is shown to be a generalization of CFG (where the perturbed path is the unconditional model) and can be combined with CFG for further FID improvements, while also functioning standalone in fully unconditional settings.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with ADM, PAG achieves FID 16.23 and IS 88.53 in unconditional generation (vs. 26.21/39.70 without guidance and 20.08/45.56 with SAG), and FID 6.32 and IS 338.02 in conditional generation (vs. 10.94/100.98 baseline and 9.41/104.79 SAG). On Stable Diffusion v1.5 text-to-image (MS-COCO 2014 prompts), combining PAG with CFG yields FID 8.73, substantially outperforming CFG alone (15.00) and PAG alone (10.08). On FFHQ inverse problems via PSLD, PAG nearly halves FID for box inpainting (21.13 vs. 43.11) and motion deblurring (40.26 vs. 93.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAG [15] (the closest competing guidance method), PAG wins decisively on both FID and IS in both unconditional (FID 16.23 vs. 20.08; IS 88.53 vs. 45.56) and conditional (FID 6.32 vs. 9.41; IS 338.02 vs. 104.79) ImageNet settings, with the IS gap being particularly large. Against CFG [14], PAG is complementary rather than directly competitive: on Stable Diffusion text-to-image, PAG+CFG (FID 8.73) substantially outperforms CFG alone (FID 15.00), and PAG additionally operates in unconditional generation where CFG is inapplicable. Against PSLD [30] on FFHQ inverse problems, PAG improves FID across all four tasks (box inpainting, 8× SR, Gaussian deblur, motion deblur), with the most dramatic gain in motion deblurring (FID 40.26 vs. 93.39, a 2.3× reduction). PAG also slightly outperforms CFG on per-prompt diversity (LPIPS 0.68 vs. 0.64; IS 2.32 vs. 1.82), suggesting it does not sacrifice diversity as severely as CFG.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAG positions itself as a generalization of classifier-free guidance (CFG), showing that CFG's use of a jointly-trained unconditional model is one instance of a broader 'perturbed forward pass' framework where any perturbation can serve as the undesirable path. It departs from the closest prior method, Self-Attention Guidance (SAG), which used adversarial blurring of inputs to create degraded samples, by instead perturbing internal self-attention maps with an identity matrix—a choice motivated by the known role of attention maps in encoding structural (query-key) versus appearance (value) information. The energy-function formulation draws on WGAN's generator loss to unify the guidance signal, and the method inherits the implicit-discriminator perspective from CFG while removing the need for a separately trained unconditional branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAG's practical value lies in enabling quality-boosting guidance in settings where CFG is inapplicable—unconditional generation, sparse spatial conditioning (e.g., ControlNet with empty prompts), and inverse problems—without any additional training or external modules. The authors claim this is the first guidance method applied to diffusion-based inverse problem solving, opening a new avenue for leveraging generative priors in restoration tasks. Limitations include the two-forward-pass cost (analogous to CFG), the restriction to U-Net architectures with explicit self-attention (unclear applicability to DiT/transformer-based models), and the fact that the identity-matrix perturbation, while effective, is a single specific choice whose optimality is not theoretically characterized. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video diffusion, 3D generation, or architectures without explicit spatial self-attention.
