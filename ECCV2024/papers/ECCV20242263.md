## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality and speed but stores millions of high-dimensional Gaussian parameters in an unstructured list, resulting in files of several hundred megabytes that hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices. The paper exploits the fact that the ordering of Gaussians is arbitrary—many permutations represent the same scene—to reorganize this data into a compressible structure without sacrificing visual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242263/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard 3DGS inputs: a set of 2D RGB images of a scene and a corresponding SfM point cloud. It produces a compressed scene file consisting of multiple 2D attribute grids (position, scale, opacity, rotation, spherical harmonics) encoded with JPEG XL, which decompresses back into the exact same parameter structure as vanilla 3DGS, enabling drop-in integration with existing renderers for novel-view synthesis.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-time reorganization of 3DGS Gaussians into a 2D grid with local smoothness, achieved through two components: (1) Parallel Linear Assignment Sorting (PLAS), a GPU-parallel algorithm that iteratively assigns Gaussians to grid positions matching a progressively less-blurred target, using block-wise 4-element swaps (brute-forcing all 24 permutations) to preserve neighborhood structure across all attribute channels simultaneously; and (2) a smoothness regularization (Huber loss between each grid position and a Gaussian-blurred version of the grid) added to the standard 3DGS rendering loss, which guides the optimizer toward configurations that are both visually accurate and locally smooth in the 2D layout. After training, attributes are quantized and encoded as 2D images with JPEG XL (lossy for RGB/DC, lossless for other channels), achieving 17×–42× size reduction at equivalent PSNR, with the added benefit of fewer Gaussians and faster rendering due to the regularization's effect on densification.

## Results

The method achieves the highest PSNR across all four datasets (27.64 on Mip-NeRF 360, 25.63 on Tanks & Temples, 30.35 on Deep Blending, 33.70 on Synthetic-NeRF) while reducing storage by 17×–42× compared to vanilla 3DGS (e.g., 40.3 MB vs. 785 MB on Mip-NeRF 360). Without spherical harmonics, the size drops further to 16.7 MB (Mip-NeRF 360) and 2.0 MB (Synthetic-NeRF) with only a ~0.6 dB PSNR drop. Training time matches vanilla 3DGS, and rendering is actually faster (515 fps vs. 385 fps on the Truck scene) due to fewer Gaussians (1.595M vs. 2.58M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS, the method improves PSNR on all four datasets (e.g., +0.09 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, +0.09 dB on Tanks & Temples) while reducing file size by 17×–42×. Compared to the closest compression baselines, the method with SH always achieves the highest PSNR: it beats Compressed3D (26.98) by 0.66 dB on Mip-NeRF 360, LightGaussian (27.00) by 0.64 dB, and EAGLES (27.15) by 0.49 dB. In terms of size, the method without SH is the smallest across all datasets (16.7 MB on Mip-NeRF 360 vs. 28.80 MB for Compressed3D and 44.54 MB for LightGaussian), while the method with SH is second-smallest after Compressed3D. On the Truck scene (Tanks & Temples), the method also outperforms the blog-post compression approach [34] in both PSNR (25.57 vs. 25.05) and size (28.8 MB vs. 41.5 MB). A key differentiator is that the method achieves these results with the same training time as vanilla 3DGS and faster rendering (515 fps vs. 385 fps) due to producing fewer Gaussians, whereas post-hoc compression methods like Compressed3D and EAGLES require additional encoding/decoding steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework by integrating a periodic 2D grid-sorting step (adapted from Fast Linear Assignment Sorting and Self-Organizing Structured Maps) and a smoothness regularization loss directly into the training loop. Unlike concurrent 3DGS compression methods that apply vector quantization, codebooks, or hash grids as post-hoc encoding, this work exploits the permutation ambiguity of Gaussian parameters to reorganize them into a locally smooth 2D layout during optimization, making standard image codecs (JPEG XL) effective for storage. The conceptual departure is treating the unordered list of Gaussians as a compressible 2D signal rather than a high-dimensional point cloud, repurposing information-visualization sorting techniques for scene compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 17×–42× storage reduction with no quality loss and unchanged training time makes 3DGS scenes practical for resource-constrained devices and web delivery, a significant deployment barrier. The simplicity of the pipeline (sort → smooth → quantize → JPEG XL) means no custom decoder is needed, easing integration into existing renderers. Open questions include extending the approach to 4D/dynamic scenes with temporal dependencies, finding less-correlated representations for rotations and spherical harmonics, and performing quantization during training rather than as a one-shot post-processing step. A notable side effect is that the smoothness regularization reduces the total Gaussian count (e.g., 1.6M vs 2.6M on Truck), which accelerates rendering but may limit fidelity in highly detailed scenes.
