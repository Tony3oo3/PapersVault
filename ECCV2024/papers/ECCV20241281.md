## Problem

Existing vision-language pre-training methods (CLIP, SLIP, MaskCLIP) rely solely on cross-modality and intra-modality supervision, overlooking the potential of modality-fused supervision. Additionally, one-to-one contrastive learning assumes strict image-text pairing, pushing apart relevant but unpaired pairs within a batch and introducing training noise. The paper hypothesizes that a modality-fused representation, which augments image features with textual information, is more discriminative and can serve as a strong teacher for visual representation learning.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes an RGB image (224×224, split into 32×32, 16×16, or 14×14 patches depending on backbone) and two text captions per image (one randomly selected for contrastive learning, the other fed into the fusion encoder alongside image patches). Text is tokenized to a maximum of 77 tokens. At inference, the fusion encoder is discarded entirely, leaving a standard dual-encoder pipeline identical to CLIP that produces a single image embedding and a single text embedding in a shared d-dimensional space.

## Method

FuseTeacher introduces a lightweight fusion encoder (2 cross-attention transformer blocks) that jointly encodes image patches and text tokens into a single fusion embedding, which is more discriminative than either single-modality embedding. This fusion embedding supervises the image encoder through two distillation objectives: (i) Classification Distillation, where fusion embeddings are soft-assigned to K=4096 learnable cluster centroids (balanced via Sinkhorn-Knopp) and the image encoder is trained to reproduce these soft assignments; and (ii) Retrieval Distillation, where the full similarity distribution between each fusion embedding and all text embeddings in the batch serves as a pseudo retrieval label, replacing the hard one-to-one contrastive target and preventing the model from pushing apart relevant but unpaired pairs. The total loss combines standard contrastive terms (image-text and fusion-text) with both distillation losses at equal weight. The approach is modular and compatible with existing frameworks like CLIP and MaskCLIP, requiring no architectural changes at inference time.

## Results

On YFCC15M-Cap with ViT-B/32, FuseTeacher achieves 43.5% zero-shot ImageNet accuracy (vs. 41.1% for CLIP) and FuseTeacher+ (combined with MaskCLIP) reaches 44.9%, yielding a 2.0% average improvement over both baselines across six ImageNet variants and two retrieval benchmarks. Scaling to ViT-L/14 on Union23M, FuseTeacher+ attains 74.9% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy and 67.0% average, surpassing MaskCLIP (74.2%, 65.9%). With further scaling to Union65M, FuseTeacher+ reaches 77.1% ImageNet and 69.9% average, demonstrating consistent gains with more data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), FuseTeacher improves zero-shot ImageNet accuracy from 41.1% to 43.5% and average across all benchmarks from 36.4% to 38.4% on YFCC15M-Cap; on the larger Union23M dataset with ViT-L/14, the gap widens to 73.6% vs. 72.5% on ImageNet. Against MaskCLIP (Dong et al., CVPR 2023), FuseTeacher+ adds a further 2.0% average improvement (40.3% vs. 38.3%) on YFCC15M-Cap and 1.1% on Union23M (67.0% vs. 65.9%), with the largest gains on ImageNet-A (+3.7%) and ImageNet-R (+1.8%). Against SLIP (Mu et al., ECCV 2022), DeCLIP (Li et al., ICLR 2022), and UniCLIP (Lee et al., NeurIPS 2022) on YFCC15M, FuseTeacher+ (50 epochs) achieves 48.5% ImageNet zero-shot, outperforming UniCLIP (42.8%), DeCLIP (41.2%), and SLIP (38.3%) by 5.7%, 7.3%, and 10.2% respectively. On retrieval (Flickr30K I2T R@1), FuseTeacher+ reaches 78.1%, surpassing UniCLIP (55.7%), DeCLIP (51.3%), and SLIP (48.7%) trained on the same YFCC15M dataset. The method consistently wins across all 13 evaluated benchmarks in the broader comparison (Tables 3–4), with no losses reported against any baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

FuseTeacher extends the CLIP dual-encoder framework by introducing a third supervision signal—modality-fused supervision—alongside the cross-modality (CLIP) and intra-modality (SLIP, MaskCLIP) signals that prior work has explored. The lightweight cross-attention fusion encoder draws on architectures from ALBEF and BLIP, but is repurposed here as a training-time teacher rather than an inference component. The classification distillation mechanism borrows DINO's learnable cluster centroids and Sinkhorn-Knopp balancing, while the retrieval distillation reframes the standard contrastive objective as a soft-label approximation problem. Conceptually, the paper positions modality-fused supervision as an overlooked but natural complement to the two supervision types already in use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that FuseTeacher adds no inference cost (the fusion encoder is discarded after training) and plugs directly into existing CLIP or MaskCLIP pipelines, making it an easy upgrade for practitioners. The consistent ~2% gains across ViT-B/32 through ViT-L/14 backbones and multiple datasets suggest the modality-fused teacher signal is a robust, generalizable improvement rather than an artifact of a specific configuration. Open questions include whether the approach benefits further from stronger fusion encoders or larger-scale training (the Union65M results hint at continued gains), whether the LLaVA-generated second caption is essential or whether a single caption suffices, and how the method interacts with emerging generative or autoregressive vision-language pretraining paradigms.
