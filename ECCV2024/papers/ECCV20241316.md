## Problem

Multi-scale training is the standard strategy for handling large scale variations in aerial object detection, but it makes inference impractical by increasing computation ~7× and treats different scales independently, preventing cross-scale information interaction. The paper asks whether multi-scale knowledge can be integrated into a single-scale model at training time so that inference remains fast while retaining comparable accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241316/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method takes a single original-resolution aerial image (e.g., 1024×1024 patches) and outputs oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle) with class labels for 15 (DOTA) or 20 (DIOR-R) categories. During training, the original image is additionally resized to 0.5× and 1.5× scales and fed into parallel branches that share all detector weights, so the model sees three receptive-field views per iteration without any extra parameters at test time.

## Method

MSCD combines a Parallel Multi-Branch Architecture (PMBA) with an Adaptive Cross-Scale Knowledge Distillation (ACSKD) module. PMBA feeds the original and two resized images through the same two-stage detector (backbone + FPN + RPN + RoI head) in parallel, enabling cross-scale feature interaction via shared weights. ACSKD then distills RoI-level features from the auxiliary-scale branches into the original branch using an L2 loss, where each proposal receives an adaptive weight w_i = max(0, L_det(y_i, ŷ_i) − L_det(y_i^m, ŷ_i)) that up-weights proposals the other scale handles better and discards low-quality guidance. Positive and negative proposals are weighted separately to prevent background dominance. Training is two-stage: first the detector is trained with detection loss only, then ACSKD is added with a small learning rate (0.0005) for 5 epochs, following a self-distillation paradigm that requires no frozen teacher model.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, RoI Trans + MSCD achieves 80.49% mAP with single-scale inference, surpassing its multi-scale counterpart (79.77%) by 0.72 points and the best single-scale baseline (ReDet, 76.25%) by 4.24 points. On DIOR-R, RoI Trans + MSCD reaches 70.22% mAP, outperforming the strongest single-scale baseline (ReDet, 65.69%) by 4.53 points. On DOTA-v2.0, RoI Trans + MSCD achieves 60.74% mAP, a 6.59-point gain over its single-scale version (54.15%). Across all three datasets, MSCD consistently improves two-stage detectors by 4–7 mAP points over single-scale baselines while maintaining identical inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoI Trans + MSCD (80.49%) is the standout result: it beats its own multi-scale version (79.77%) by 0.72 points while using single-scale inference, and outperforms all single-scale baselines by at least 4.24 points (vs. ReDet at 76.25%). Oriented RCNN + MSCD (79.54%) and Rotated FR + MSCD (77.82%) also substantially beat their single-scale counterparts (+3.67 and +4.42 points respectively) but still trail their multi-scale versions by 1.33 and 0.33 points, which the authors attribute to MSCD's RoI-level distillation not benefiting the RPN stage. On DIOR-R, RoI Trans + MSCD (70.22%) dominates all single-scale baselines by a wide margin (best competitor ReDet at 65.69%, a 4.53-point gap), with particularly large gains (>10 points) on scale-variant classes like APL, BF, GF, and STA. On DOTA-v2.0, RoI Trans + MSCD (60.74%) improves over single-scale RoI Trans (54.15%) by 6.59 points. The paper notes that RoI Trans benefits most from MSCD due to its cascading two RoI modules, indirectly confirming the effectiveness of RoI feature distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-scale training strategies (SNIP/SNIPER) and knowledge distillation for object detection. It departs from prior KD-based scale-variation methods (e.g., [43, 47, 80]) that use a frozen high-resolution teacher to guide a low-resolution student, instead adopting a self-distillation paradigm where a single network processes multiple scales in parallel weight-shared branches. The RoI-level feature distillation loss is directly inspired by [33] (Mimicking Very Efficient Network), but is extended with separate positive/negative proposal weighting and an adaptive quality-based weight that selects which cross-scale features to distill. The parallel multi-branch architecture with shared parameters is a novel structural choice compared to the standard practice of training separate models at each scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear: multi-scale inference costs ~7× the time of single-scale inference on DOTA, making it impractical for deployment, yet MSCD closes most of that accuracy gap while retaining single-scale speed. However, the method is currently restricted to two-stage detectors (it relies on RPN-generated proposals for distillation), uses a two-stage training process that reduces knowledge-transfer efficiency, and operates at the RoI level rather than the feature-map level. Extending the approach to single-stage and DETR-based detectors, and moving to feature-map-level distillation, are the natural next steps that the authors identify as open problems.
