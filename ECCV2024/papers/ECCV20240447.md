## Problem

Power-law scaling makes uniform-sampling training prohibitively expensive, and while active learning could improve data efficiency, no existing algorithm simultaneously generalizes across model architectures and tasks, scales to web-scale datasets, and achieves net FLOP savings after accounting for the overhead of data selection. Prior model-based curation methods either spend as much compute on scoring as they save on training, or fail to scale beyond medium-sized datasets like ImageNet.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240447/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes uniformly sampled super-batches (size B) of labeled images (JFT-300M) or image-text pairs (ALIGN, LTIP, Webli at 128×128 resolution), scores each example with small proxy models, and retains a prioritized sub-batch (size b = B/2) for the learner update. The output is a trained ViT-L classifier or CLIP/SigLIP multimodal model that reaches the same held-out performance (ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, COCO retrieval) as a uniformly-trained baseline but with 46–51% fewer training updates and up to 25% less total computation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-model prioritized replay framework: a small pre-trained reference model θ*, a small online model θ_o trained in parallel with the learner, and the large learner θ_l. Data points are scored by their learnability — the difference between the online model's loss and the reference model's loss (s = ℓ(x|θ_o) − ℓ(x|θ*)) — which favors examples that are hard for the current learner but solvable by a well-trained model, thereby filtering out both trivially easy and irreducibly noisy data. The key insight is that learnability scores generalize across model scales: a ViT-Ti or ViT-Mu actor (up to 1000× smaller than the learner) produces comparable speedups to a ViT-B actor, making the scoring overhead negligible and the method compute-positive end-to-end. For multimodal learning (ActiveCLIP/ActiveSigLIP), the actor loss is simplified to negative dot-product similarity between image and text embeddings rather than the full contrastive objective, further reducing scoring cost. The framework is instantiated as ClassAct for supervised classification and ActiveCLIP/ActiveSigLIP for contrastive/sigmoid multimodal pretraining, and is shown to be complementary to prior data-curation pipelines like DataComp.

## Results

ClassAct requires 46% fewer training updates and up to 25% less total FLOPs to match uniformly-trained ViT-L on JFT-300M, while ActiveCLIP requires 51% fewer updates for multimodal pretraining on ALIGN. On multimodal transfer benchmarks, ActiveCLIP trained on only 3B examples achieves 71.3% ImageNet zero-shot top-1, 57.7 COCO im2txt R1, and 43.0 COCO txt2im R1, surpassing CLIP trained on 13B examples. ActiveSigLIP (3B examples) further improves COCO retrieval to 63.5 im2txt R1 and 45.3 txt2im R1, setting a new state-of-the-art over uniformly-trained SigLIP. Learner speedups of 18–48% are observed across evaluation metrics depending on reference model configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveCLIP (3B examples) decisively outperforms CLIP (13B examples) on all three metrics: 71.3 vs. 68.3 ImageNet ZS top-1, 57.7 vs. 52.4 im2txt R1, and 43.0 vs. 33.1 txt2im R1, using roughly 4× fewer training examples. Against OpenCLIP (34B examples), ActiveCLIP (8B) wins on all metrics (72.2 vs. 70.2 ZS, 60.7 vs. 59.4 im2txt, 44.9 vs. 42.3 txt2im) with less than a quarter of the training data. ActiveCLIP (8B) trails EVA-CLIP (8B+2B) on ImageNet ZS (72.2 vs. 74.7†) but wins on both COCO retrieval metrics (60.7 vs. 58.7 im2txt, 44.9 vs. 42.2 txt2im); the EVA-CLIP advantage is partly attributable to its additional 2B ImageNet21K pretraining. ActiveSigLIP (3B) matches SigLIP (3B) on ImageNet ZS (72.0 vs. 72.1) but substantially improves COCO retrieval by 2.8 and 2.6 points respectively (63.5 vs. 60.7 im2txt, 45.3 vs. 42.7 txt2im). On the classification side, ClassAct with ViT-Ti actors achieves a 26% learner speedup and 3% total compute speedup over uniform sampling on JFT, while the original RHO method with the same small reference model yields 0% learner speedup and −79% compute (i.e., 79% more FLOPs), demonstrating that learnability-based scoring with small proxy models is essential for compute-positivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RHO learnability score [27], which combines 'hard under the learner' and 'easy under a reference model' criteria, and on Online Batch Selection [22] for the prioritized replay mechanism. The key departure is replacing the expensive learner model in the learnability score with a much smaller 'online' model trained in parallel, making the scoring cost scale with the small model rather than the large learner. This enables the first demonstration of compute-positive active learning at large scale, whereas prior methods (RHO, loss-based pruning, easy-reference filtering) either incurred prohibitive scoring overhead or failed to generalize across model scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first active learning method shown to save total FLOPs (up to 25%) relative to uniform sampling in large-scale pretraining, addressing a long-standing barrier to adoption. The finding that learnability scores generalize across up to 1000× model scale while loss-based scores do not provides a principled reason for the method's robustness and suggests a broader principle about what transfers across capacity. Open questions include whether more aggressive filtering (>50%) can push toward exponential rather than power-law scaling, and whether the approach extends to video, language, and generative modeling. The method's complementarity with data curation (DataComp) and modern objectives (SigLIP) positions it as a drop-in efficiency layer for existing large-scale training pipelines.
