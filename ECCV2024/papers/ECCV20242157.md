## Problem

Existing automated symbolic optimizer design methods (RL-based search, evolutionary computation) must sample and evaluate many candidate optimizers, typically on small proxy tasks, leading to significant performance degradation when transferring to the target domain. No prior method can directly learn a high-performance symbolic optimizer on the target task itself without expensive discrete search or proxy-to-target transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242157/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SOL takes as input a feature vector constructed from the optimizee's current training state, including gradient (g), first-order momentum (m), second-order momentum (v), preprocessed gradient (ag), linear/cosine decay schedules, and constants (1, 2). During training it outputs per-parameter updates; after training, the network's weights and active computational units are extracted to yield a closed-form symbolic optimizer (a mathematical formula). Division is deliberately excluded from the operator set to avoid numerical instability (inf/NaN) in densely connected computation, with Adam's update included as a precomputed input operator instead.

## Method

SOL is a shallow multi-layered feed-forward network whose hidden layers replace standard activations (ReLU, Tanh) with a set of symbolic computational units—unary operators (exp, sign, squaring, negation) and binary operators (+, −, ×)—so that the trained network is itself a mathematical expression. Training uses a bi-level optimization formulation: the inner loop applies SOL to update the optimizee's weights over multiple steps, while the outer loop updates SOL's parameters to minimize the accumulated inner loss. To make this tractable, an unrolled optimization scheme (inspired by LSTM training) truncates backpropagation into fixed-length windows, balancing gradient quality against computational cost. Because the non-linearities are symbolic operators rather than learned activations, the final optimizer is read out directly from the network's weights and active units—no post-hoc symbolic regression is needed—yielding a scalable, interpretable formula that generalizes across tasks.

## Results

On the standard CIFAR-10 ConvNet benchmark, SOL achieves 68.75% ± 0.35 test accuracy, outperforming Adam (67.15%), Lion (67.02%), and ENOS/conv2 (67.65%). On ResNet-18 for CIFAR-10, SOL reaches 93.16% accuracy in 1.60h of search time, compared to Adam's 91.50% and SymbolicL2O's 93.45% in 5.65h. SOL also achieves the best attack success rate across all seven RobustBench defense models (e.g., 55.08% vs PGD's 54.69% on Wong2020/RN-18), outperforms Adam on all six GNN model-dataset combinations (e.g., 83.10% vs 82.48% on GAT/Cora), and surpasses AdamW on all five GLUE benchmarks (e.g., 66.83 vs 63.87 on RTE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOL decisively outperforms all manual optimizers (Adam, SGD, SGD+Momentum, Nesterov, RMSprop) across every task, with the largest gap over SGD (68.75% vs 43.22% on CIFAR-10 ConvNet). Against automated symbolic optimizer methods, SOL beats NOS-RL by ~33 percentage points (68.75% vs ~35%), Lion by 1.73 points, and ENOS/conv2 by 1.10 points on CIFAR-10. On ResNet-18, SOL narrowly trails SymbolicL2O by 0.29% accuracy (93.16% vs 93.45%) but is 3.5× faster in search time (1.60h vs 5.65h). For adversarial attacks, SOL surpasses PGD on all seven defense models and matches or slightly exceeds APGD (e.g., 41.80% vs 41.41% on Wu2020). On GNN and NLP tasks, SOL consistently outperforms Adam and AdamW respectively, with gains of 0.5–3.0% across all six GNN benchmarks and all five GLUE datasets, while also using substantially less GPU memory than the parametric L2LGD2 optimizer (2,125 MB vs 6,307 MB on ResNet-18).

## Relation to Prior Work

SOL inherits the bi-level optimization formulation from the Learning to Optimize (L2O) lineage [6], but departs sharply from both major branches of automated optimizer design. Against parametric L2O methods (L2LGD2, LSTM-based), SOL replaces recurrent architectures with a shallow feed-forward network whose activation layers are symbolic operators (exp, sign, squaring, addition), making the output directly extractable as a mathematical formula. Against symbolic search methods (NOS-RL, ENOS, Lion) that sample and evaluate thousands of candidate optimizers via RL or EC on proxy tasks, SOL eliminates the search entirely by training a single parametric model via unrolled backpropagation directly on the target, and unlike SymbolicL2O it avoids a costly symbolic-regression post-processing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of proxy-to-target transfer and symbolic-regression post-processing addresses two practical pain points that have limited adoption of automated optimizer design: performance degradation on the true task and prohibitive search/evaluation cost. The sub-linear GPU memory scaling (vs. exponential for L2LGD2) and the 3.5× speedup over SymbolicL2O make the approach viable for practitioners working with large models. Open questions remain: the unrolling length must be tuned per task, the learned symbolic expressions can be complex (the authors flag interpretability as future work), and it is unclear whether the approach extends to multi-objective or non-differentiable optimization settings.
