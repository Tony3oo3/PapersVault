## Problem

Reconstructing a 3D object and estimating camera poses from a small number of unposed, sparsely distributed views remains unsolved in an open-world setting. Existing sparse-view reconstruction methods require known poses or per-shape optimization, while single-image-to-3D methods hallucinate invisible regions with no user control. Prior pose-free approaches are either limited to predefined object categories or rely on costly iterative optimization prone to local minima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241705/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–6 unposed RGB images of a single object, which are tiled into a 3×2 composite grid (with empty padding for fewer views) and fed as conditioning to a finetuned Stable Diffusion UNet. The first input view establishes a canonical azimuth frame; camera intrinsics are assumed known for the downstream PnP solver. The output is a textured 3D mesh (extracted via marching cubes from a 128³ SDF volume) together with relative camera poses for all input views, produced in roughly 16–20 seconds in a single feedforward pass.

## Method

SpaRP finetunes Stable Diffusion 2.1 with a domain switcher to jointly predict two outputs from the tiled sparse-view input: (1) NOCS maps for each input view, from which camera poses are recovered via a RANSAC-based PnP solver, and (2) six multi-view images at fixed, uniformly distributed camera poses (alternating ±20°/−10° elevations, 60°-spaced azimuths relative to the first view). Conditioning uses reference-only attention for local spatial interaction and mean-pooled CLIP embeddings for global semantics. The generated multi-view images are lifted to 3D by the One-2-3-45++ two-stage coarse-to-fine reconstruction module (64³ occupancy → 128³ SDF with color). An optional refinement stage aligns the reconstructed mesh to input images via differentiable rendering, and a Mixture-of-Experts strategy (multiple NOCS predictions with different seeds, selecting the best by rendering loss) further reduces pose error for symmetric objects.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (1,030 objects), SpaRP with 6 sparse-view inputs achieves 96.9% F-Score, 78.1 CLIP-Similarity, 19.3 PSNR, and 0.123 LPIPS in 16 seconds, outperforming all single-image-to-3D and sparse-view baselines. With only 1 input view, it still reaches 95.7% F-Score and 78.2 CLIP-Sim. For pose estimation on GSO with 4 initializations, SpaRP achieves 5.28° median rotation error, 75.0% accuracy at 15°, and 0.23 translation error in 57 s, substantially beating iFusion (8.61°, 65.1%, 0.49 in 256 s) and the feed-forward baselines RelPose++ and FORGE (which exceed 100° rotation error).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-image-to-3D baselines (Zero123 XL, Shap-E, One-2-3-45, SyncDreamer, DreamGaussian), SpaRP with a single input view wins on every metric: +3.9–14.9 F-Score points, +5.1–9.3 CLIP-Sim, and lower LPIPS, while running in 16 s versus 2 min–20 min for the optimization-based methods. Against sparse-view baselines with 6 inputs, SpaRP outperforms iFusion by +8.4 F-Score, +11.4 CLIP-Sim, and +3.1 PSNR, and beats EscherNet by +2.1 F-Score and +12.2 CLIP-Sim, all in 16 s versus 9–28 min. For pose estimation, SpaRP (n_init=4) reduces median rotation error from 8.61° (iFusion) to 5.28° on GSO and from 8.55° to 5.80° on ABO, while improving Acc@15° from 65.1% to 75.0% and Acc@30° from 75.9% to 78.7% on GSO. The feed-forward baselines RelPose++ and FORGE are essentially non-functional on open-world inputs, with rotation errors exceeding 100° and Acc@15° below 2%, confirming that category-specific training does not generalize. SpaRP is also 4–17× faster than the optimization-based baselines across both reconstruction and pose tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaRP extends the line of work on leveraging 2D diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) for 3D tasks, building directly on Zero123++'s conditioning strategies and Wonder3D's domain switcher, while repurposing NOCS maps—originally designed for category-level pose estimation—as a diffusion-friendly surrogate output for camera poses. It departs from prior sparse-view reconstruction methods (which assume known poses) and single-image-to-3D methods (which hallucinate invisible regions) by jointly solving pose estimation and 3D reconstruction in a single feedforward pass. The 3D reconstruction stage reuses the One-2-3-45++ multi-view-to-mesh pipeline, and the pose refinement leverages differentiable rendering, but the key conceptual contribution is using a finetuned 2D diffusion model to implicitly infer spatial relationships among unposed views rather than merely generating novel views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpaRP addresses a practically critical gap: consumer and e-commerce capture scenarios where users have a handful of unposed photos of an object and need a 3D model with camera poses in seconds, not hours. The ~20-second feedforward inference with open-world generalization (no category restriction) makes this feasible for real-time applications, and the joint training of pose and reconstruction branches demonstrates that these tasks are mutually beneficial. Open questions include scalability beyond 6 views, handling of non-rigid or articulated objects, and whether the NOCS-based pose estimation generalizes to scenes with multiple objects or unknown intrinsics. The reliance on known camera intrinsics for the PnP step remains a practical limitation for fully unconstrained capture.
