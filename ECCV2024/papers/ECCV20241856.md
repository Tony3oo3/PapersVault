## Problem

In contrastive learning, multiple data augmentations cause positive pairs to sometimes appear dissimilar and negative pairs to occasionally appear similar, creating complex, overlapping similarity distributions. Existing methods rely on a single projection head and a global constant temperature, which cannot adequately separate these diverse similarity patterns, leading to degraded representation quality as the number of augmentation types increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241856/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR/STL-10, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet), applies stochastic augmentations to generate multiple views, and produces C sets of d′-dimensional projected feature vectors (one per head) along with pair-adaptive temperature scalars. Downstream, the backbone features (with projection heads removed) are used for linear probing, KNN classification, or end-to-end fine-tuning for detection and segmentation.

## Method

AMCL replaces the single MLP projection head with C independently-weighted MLP heads (typically C=3), each producing a distinct feature set for the same image pair, and introduces a pair-adaptive temperature computed as σ(⟨φ(z_i), φ(z_j)⟩) via a shared MLP layer φ, so each positive or negative pair receives its own temperature per head. The loss is derived from maximum likelihood estimation over head-wise posterior distributions, yielding temperature-weighted similarities for positive and negative pairs plus a regularization term Ω(τ) = (d′/2)log(τ) + 1/τ that prevents trivial zero-temperature solutions and connects temperature to uncertainty. For negative pairs, a Top-κ maximum operation selects the κ hardest negatives rather than a full softmax, balancing expressiveness with computational tractability. The framework is modular: setting temperature to a global constant and C=1 recovers standard NT-Xent, InfoNCE, or cross-correlation losses, while the multi-head adaptive version is applied to SimCLR, MoCo, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, CAN, and LGP with only ~5% additional computational cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, AMCL yields the largest average improvement of 4.58% over baselines (e.g., SimCLR 57.6→61.8%, MoCo 64.4→69.3%), while on CIFAR-10 the average gain is 2.43%. On ImageNet (linear probing, 100 epochs), AMCL improves SimCLR from 66.5% to 68.1%, MoCo from 67.4% to 69.3%, and Barlow Twins from 70.0% to 72.7%. Under the more demanding 1600-epoch end-to-end fine-tuning protocol on ImageNet, LGP+AMCL with ViT-L/16 reaches 87.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing MAE (85.9%) by 1.5 points. On COCO, MoCo+AMCL with ViT-L improves box AP from 49.2 to 53.3 and mask AP from 43.8 to 47.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMCL consistently outperforms all six contrastive-learning baselines it is applied to: SimCLR, MoCo, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, CAN, and LGP, with average gains of 2.43% (CIFAR-10), 4.58% (CIFAR-100), 1.65% (Tiny-ImageNet), and 1.67% (ImageNet) under linear probing. Against the MIM-based SOTA method MAE [20] on ImageNet (1600 epochs, end-to-end fine-tuning), LGP+AMCL with ViT-L/16 achieves 87.4% top-1, exceeding MAE's 85.9% by 1.5 points, while MoCo+AMCL (85.3%) still trails MAE (85.9%) on ViT-L but surpasses it on ViT-B (84.1% vs. 83.6%). Compared to the two dedicated temperature methods—TaU [52] (temperature as uncertainty) and TS [34] (cosine temperature schedule)—AMCL's pair-adaptive temperature is superior at every linear-probing epoch on STL-10 (e.g., 71.1% vs. 64.3% at 10 epochs for both TaU and TS). The gains are most pronounced when 4–5 augmentation types are used (up to 4.59% average improvement on CIFAR-100), validating the multi-head design's motivation. On COCO detection/segmentation, MoCo+AMCL with ViT-L improves box AP by 4.1 points and mask AP by 3.2 points over plain MoCo, confirming transferability beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard contrastive learning pipeline (SimCLR, MoCo, SimSiam, Barlow Twins, CAN, LGP) by replacing the single projection head with multiple independent heads and the global constant temperature with a pair-adaptive, head-wise temperature derived from maximum likelihood estimation over head-wise posterior distributions. The multi-head design draws on ensemble learning ideas [43], while the adaptive temperature connects to uncertainty quantification literature (Welsch functions, heteroscedastic aleatoric uncertainty) and prior temperature-related work such as temperature-as-uncertainty [52] and cosine temperature schedules [34], but differs by being jointly optimized per pair and per head. The MLE derivation unifies the loss with existing contrastive objectives (NT-Xent, InfoNCE, cross-correlation) as special cases under constant temperature, positioning AMCL as a strict generalization rather than a competing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AMCL offers a drop-in modification (≈5% extra compute) that consistently improves six popular SSL methods across CNN and ViT backbones, with gains scaling from ~1% (1–2 augmentations) to ~4.6% (5 augmentations), directly addressing the practical problem that diverse augmentations degrade positive/negative pair separability. The theoretical link between temperature and pair-distance variance provides a principled interpretation of a previously heuristic hyperparameter, potentially guiding future loss design. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-contrastive SSL paradigms (e.g., pure MIM or generative methods), whether the optimal number of heads should scale with dataset size or augmentation diversity, and whether the MLE framework can be extended to model inter-head correlations rather than treating heads as independent.
