## Problem

CNN-based Class Activation Maps (CAMs) in weakly supervised semantic segmentation suffer from under-activation and imprecise boundaries, while Vision Transformer-based CAMs mitigate under-activation but lack fine-grained local patterns due to the absence of inductive locality. Prior work has addressed ViT's locality gap in general vision tasks, but no method specifically tackles the degradation of CAM quality caused by this lack of locality in the WSSS setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241287/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and image-level multi-label class labels as the sole supervision signal, with no pixel-level annotations or external datasets. A frozen U-Net-based DDPM pretrained on the target dataset images provides diffusion features at multiple noise levels (t ∈ {0,1,2,3,4}), while a DeiT-S ViT with multiple class tokens processes the image into patch and class tokens. The outputs are refined CAMs at patch resolution (N×N×C), pseudo segmentation masks after post-processing (IRN or PSA), and class predictions from both class-token and patch-token pathways.

## Method

The framework introduces two diffusion-guided mechanisms. First, the Locality Fusion Cross Attention (LFCA) module aggregates bottleneck features from a frozen DDPM across multiple noise levels, then fuses them with ViT query tokens via cross-attention to produce semantically aligned Diffusion-CAMs; these Diffusion-CAMs supervise ViT-CAMs through an L1 loss after an initial alignment phase enforced by multi-label soft margin loss and KL-divergence distillation from ViT's class predictions. Second, Patch Affinity Consistency (PAC) leverages the DDPM's denoising process to generate a spatially and semantically consistent augmented image, then imposes an L1 constraint between the patch-affinity-refined CAMs of the original and denoised images, regularizing the otherwise unconstrained patch-to-patch attention. The total loss combines ViT classification, LFCA alignment, CAM guidance, and PAC terms, with the DDPM remaining frozen throughout training.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, the proposed method achieves 73.9% mIoU on the val set and 73.7% on the test set using a WideResNet38 backbone, and 71.8%/72.4% with ResNet-101. On MS-COCO 2014 val, it reaches 45.5% mIoU (WRN38) and 46.6% (RN101). In terms of CAM quality on the VOC 2012 train set, the method achieves 69.3% seed mIoU and 74.3% mask mIoU (with PSA post-processing), surpassing the previous best USAGE (67.7% seed, 72.8% mask).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 14 compared baselines across both datasets. On PASCAL VOC 2012 val (WRN38), it achieves 73.9% mIoU, beating the closest competitor OCR (72.7%) by 1.2% and LPCAM (72.6%) by 1.3%. On VOC test, it surpasses USAGE (72.8%) by 0.9% and LPCAM (72.4%) by 1.3%. On MS-COCO 2014 val with WRN38, it reaches 45.5%, edging out ACR (45.3%) by 0.2% and AEFT (44.8%) by 0.7%. With the RN101 backbone on COCO, the gap widens substantially: 46.6% vs. USAGE's 44.3% (+2.3%) and FPR's 43.9% (+2.7%). The CAM seed quality on VOC train also leads all methods at 69.3%, exceeding USAGE (67.7%) by 1.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViT-based WSSS line initiated by MCTformer (multi-class token transformer) by addressing a gap that prior ViT-based methods have largely ignored: the lack of locality in ViT's CAMs. It draws on the DDPM framework (Ho et al.) and the U-Net diffusion architecture (Dhariwal & Nichol) not as a generative tool but as a frozen feature extractor whose intermediate representations encode high-level semantic locality. The key departure from prior WSSS work is the use of cross-attention to semantically align diffusion features with ViT class tokens (LFCA) and the repurposing of DDPM denoising as a spatially/semantically consistent augmentation for patch affinity regularization (PAC), rather than the conventional use of diffusion for image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a pretrained, label-free diffusion model can serve as a locality prior for ViT-based CAMs, this work opens a path for leveraging generative model representations in discriminative localization tasks without additional supervision or external data. The PAC mechanism—a simple L1 consistency loss between patch affinities of original and denoised images—is a lightweight, transferable idea that could apply to other attention-based segmentation or detection pipelines. Limitations include the frozen DDPM (no fine-tuning), the heuristic choice of time steps t∈{0,1,2,3,4}, and the reliance on a U-Net diffusion model trained only on the target dataset, which may not scale trivially to larger or more diverse benchmarks. Whether fine-tuning the diffusion model or exploring alternative time-step schedules could yield further gains remains an open question the authors acknowledge.
