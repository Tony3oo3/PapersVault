## Problem

Existing dynamic stereo matching methods either process frames independently, producing severe flickering, or apply window-based temporal aggregation without alignment, causing low-frequency oscillations at the window scale. The fundamental gap is the absence of a proper alignment operation between adjacent frames, which is essential because matching point positions shift across time in dynamic scenes, making unaligned temporal attention sub-optimal for both cost volume construction and global aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241612/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of rectified stereo video sequences (left and right) and produces a temporally consistent sequence of disparity maps aligned to the left view. Processing operates on triple-frame windows (center frame plus one neighbor on each side) at three downsampled scales (1/16, 1/8, 1/4), with bidirectional optical flow estimated at full resolution and downsampled to match feature maps. Disparity predictions are iteratively refined (10 iterations in training, 20 in evaluation) and the final output is upsampled to original resolution via convex upsampling.

## Method

BiDAStereo applies bidirectional alignment at two complementary levels. Locally, a Triple-Frame Correlation Layer (TFCL) warps adjacent right frames toward the center frame using bidirectional optical flow, then builds cost volumes from a single left reference frame against the three aligned right frames, expanding the source matching options while avoiding ambiguity from multiple references. Globally, a Motion-propagation Recurrent Unit (MRU) maintains a per-frame motion hidden state that is bidirectionally aligned and fused with neighboring states at each iteration, enabling recurrent propagation of global consistency across the entire sequence. The architecture cascades three shared-weight update stages at different scales, each containing TFCL and MRU, with a super kernel updater (1×1×15 3D convolution) expanding the receptive field along the epipolar line. Training is supervised end-to-end with a weighted L1 loss over all intermediate disparity predictions, using SceneFlow and Dynamic Replica as training data.

## Results

On Sintel final pass (trained on SceneFlow + Dynamic Replica), BiDAStereo achieves a TEPE of 1.22, outperforming DynamicStereo (1.42) by 14.1% and RAFTStereo (1.91) by 36.1%. On Sintel clean pass, the TEPE is 0.75 vs. 0.76 (DynamicStereo) and 0.89 (RAFTStereo). On the in-domain Dynamic Replica test set, BiDAStereo achieves the lowest TEPE of 0.062 and δ₁ₚₓᵗ of 0.61, surpassing DynamicStereo (0.075 TEPE, 0.68 δ₁ₚₓᵗ) and RAFTStereo (0.082 TEPE, 0.84 δ₁ₚₓᵗ). The method also outperforms CREStereo (trained on 7 datasets including Sintel) on Sintel final pass TEPE (1.22 vs. 1.90) despite being trained only on synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiDAStereo consistently outperforms all four baselines on both out-of-domain (Sintel) and in-domain (Dynamic Replica) evaluations. Against DynamicStereo [18], the closest video-based competitor, BiDAStereo improves TEPE by 14.1% on Sintel final pass (1.22 vs. 1.42) and by 17.3% on Dynamic Replica (0.062 vs. 0.075), while also achieving lower δ₁ₚₓᵗ across all settings. Against RAFTStereo [24] (per-frame baseline), the gains are larger: 36.1% TEPE reduction on Sintel final pass and 24.4% on Dynamic Replica. Against CODD [23], the earliest video stereo method, BiDAStereo achieves substantially lower errors (e.g., 1.22 vs. 2.32 TEPE on Sintel final). Notably, BiDAStereo also beats CREStereo [22] on Sintel final pass TEPE (1.22 vs. 1.90) despite CREStereo being trained on 7 diverse datasets including Sintel itself, while BiDAStereo uses only two synthetic datasets—highlighting the value of explicit temporal modeling over broader data coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video stereo matching line initiated by CODD [23] and DynamicStereo [18], which introduced temporal fusion and transformer-based aggregation respectively. The key departure is identifying bidirectional alignment as the fundamental missing operation: prior methods apply per-frame matching followed by window-based temporal aggregation without aligning features across frames, which is sub-optimal when point positions shift over time. BiDAStereo repurposes the iterative refinement architecture of RAFTStereo [24] and the local correlation mechanism of CREStereo [22], but restructures them around a triple-frame correlation layer (local alignment) and a motion-propagation recurrent unit (global propagation), replacing the sliding-window paradigm with recurrent state updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal consistency in dynamic stereo is critical for AR/VR rendering, 3D reconstruction, and robot navigation, where flickering disparities are unacceptable. The paper's core insight—that alignment, not merely larger temporal receptive fields, is the bottleneck—reframes how practitioners should think about video stereo, and the optical-flow-based implicit alignment avoids the chicken-and-egg problem of scene flow or the pose-dependence of MVS. A stated limitation is the inability to explicitly separate dynamic from static regions, which the authors identify as the key to further consistency gains; integrating explicit geometry priors and developing a lightweight variant are flagged as future directions.
