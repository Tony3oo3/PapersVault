## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods either freeze CLIP entirely (sacrificing region-level sensitivity) or fine-tune only CLIP-V (constraining the optimization space and degrading zero-shot generalization). No prior work has jointly optimized both the vision and text representations of CLIP to better align the feature space for pixel-level segmentation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240884/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB image crop and a set of arbitrary text class names (e.g., noun words like 'sky', 'balcony') as input. A Mask2Former-based Proposal Generator produces 100 class-agnostic mask proposals, which are then classified by matching mask-pooled vision embeddings against image-conditioned text embeddings via cosine similarity. The final output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask or a panoptic segmentation mask with instance-level assignments, depending on the evaluation setting.

## Method

MAFT+ introduces a collaborative vision-text fine-tuning framework built on ConvNeXt-Large CLIP. The Content-Dependent Transfer (CDT) module applies 2 sequential Transformer layers with cross-attention between the text embeddings and the flattened last-stage CLIP-V features, producing image-conditioned text embeddings while keeping CLIP-T frozen (parameter-efficient). The Representation Compensation (RC) strategy maintains a frozen copy of CLIP-V whose last-stage features are compared against the fine-tuned features via multi-scale AvgPooling (grids 1×1, 2×2, 4×4) and a SmoothL1 loss, preventing catastrophic forgetting of zero-shot capability. Training uses a mask-aware loss that aligns CLIP classification scores with ground-truth IoU scores, combined with the RC loss (λ₂=0.1) and standard Mask2Former proposal losses; gradients from CLIP-V to the Proposal Generator are stopped to avoid overfitting on training categories.

## Results

MAFT+ achieves state-of-the-art open-vocabulary semantic segmentation with 15.1 mIoU on A-847, 36.1 mIoU on A-150, 21.6 mIoU on PC-459, 59.4 mIoU on PC-59, and 96.5 mIoU on PAS-20, outperforming the previous best (FC-CLIP with ensemble) by +0.3, +2.1, +3.4, +1.0, and +1.1 mIoU respectively. In the open-vocabulary panoptic setting on ADE20K, MAFT+ reaches 27.1 PQ, 73.5 SQ, and 32.9 RQ, surpassing FC-CLIP* (26.8 PQ, 71.5 SQ, 32.2 RQ) across all three metrics. The method uses ConvNeXt-Large CLIP as backbone with a Mask2Former-based proposal generator, trained for 60k iterations on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAFT+ outperforms all nine compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the closest competitor FC-CLIP* (which uses an ensemble of Mask2Former and CLIP scores), MAFT+ gains +0.3 mIoU on A-847, +2.1 on A-150, +3.4 on PC-459, +1.0 on PC-59, and +1.1 on PAS-20, while also exceeding FC-CLIP* in panoptic evaluation by +0.3 PQ, +2.0 SQ, and +0.7 RQ on ADE20K. Compared to its direct predecessor MAFT (ConvNeXt-L), MAFT+ improves by +2.0, +1.7, +4.6, +1.9, and +3.5 mIoU on the five semantic benchmarks, and eliminates the need for a separately fine-tuned CLIP-V, enabling an end-to-end pipeline. Against SAN (CVPR23), the gains are larger: +2.7 mIoU on A-847 and +1.7 on PC-59. The largest margins are over earlier methods: OpenSeg (ECCV22) trails by +6.3 mIoU on A-847 and +24.3 on PAS-20, while ODISE (CVPR23) is surpassed by +4.0 mIoU on A-847. In the panoptic setting, MAFT+ beats FC-CLIP (non-ensemble) by +5.2 PQ and +6.5 RQ, and OPSNet (ICCV23) by +8.1 PQ and +9.9 RQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAFT+ extends MAFT [18], which introduced mask-aware fine-tuning of CLIP-V to improve region sensitivity for open-vocabulary segmentation. The key departure is moving from unilateral CLIP-V fine-tuning to collaborative vision-text optimization: the Content-Dependent Transfer (CDT) module conditions text embeddings on input images via lightweight cross-attention transformer layers, while the Representation Compensation (RC) strategy uses multi-scale average pooling against a frozen CLIP-V copy to prevent catastrophic forgetting. This positions the work against the broader frozen-CLIP paradigm (Zegformer, ZSSeg, OVSeg, SAN, FC-CLIP) that sacrifices segmentation sensitivity to preserve zero-shot capability, and against MAFT's CLIP-V-only fine-tuning that constrains the optimization space and risks overfitting on fixed text embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The collaborative optimization framework offers a principled way to close the domain gap between CLIP's image-level pre-training and pixel-level segmentation while preserving zero-shot generalization, and the parameter-efficient CDT makes text-side optimization practical without requiring 8+ A100 GPUs. However, the paper itself acknowledges that the optimization upper-bound is fundamentally constrained by the pre-trained CLIP model's capabilities, leaving open whether this approach transfers to next-generation VLMs or whether the gains are merely squeezing out the last few points of CLIP's existing alignment. The negative result that LLM-generated descriptions (Llama-2) do not improve performance—because their feature space is misaligned with CLIP-V—highlights an open question about how to effectively leverage LLM knowledge in vision-language segmentation pipelines.
