## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods synthesize global motions directly from text, making it difficult to precisely control intricate trajectories, body postures, and long sequences involving multiple actions. These methods lack fine-grained, continuous control over individual action components, forcing users into iterative prompt-editing loops to approximate their mental imagery. The paper addresses this gap by introducing local actions as explicit, adjustable control signals within the diffusion generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language motion description (e.g., 'jogs and looks around') and one or more reference local actions—short 3D motion clips either retrieved from a repository or automatically generated by decomposing the description via semantic role parsing and feeding each sub-description to MLD. The output is a full 3D human motion sequence of L frames, decoded from a latent representation of 8 learnable query tokens (dimension 256) at the finest semantic level. A key distinguishing feature is that the guiding weight λ for each local action is a continuous, user-adjustable parameter, enabling fine-grained control over how strongly each action influences the final trajectory and posture.

## Method

GuidedMotion employs a local-to-global paradigm: a hierarchical semantic graph (motions → actions → specifics) is parsed from the text, and a graph attention network (GAT) over this graph estimates the guiding weight of each local action. The diffusion process is decomposed into three transformer-based denoising stages—motion, action, and specific—each operating on progressively finer latent representations (2, 4, and 8 query tokens). At the action level, an energy-guided correction term (L2 distance between the current latent and each reference local action's latent) is injected into the reverse diffusion step, weighted by the GAT-derived coefficients. The motion level provides a coarse initial denoising, the action level aligns the trajectory and posture with the chosen local actions, and the specific level refines the result to conform to the full original description rather than overfitting to a single reference action. Training is supervised with an L2 noise-prediction loss at each level, using a frozen CLIP text encoder for node initialization and a motion VAE (transformer encoder/decoder) for latent-space encoding.

## Results

On HumanML3D, GuidedMotion achieves the best FID of 0.057 (vs. 0.103 for ReMoDiffuse and 0.116 for T2M-GPT/GraphMotion), the highest Diversity of 9.864, and competitive R-Precision Top-1 of 0.503. On KIT, it attains the best MModality (4.138) and R-Precision Top-1 (0.430), with a FID of 0.213. On the complex-motion subset (≥3 local actions, ≥150 frames), GuidedMotion leads in both FID (0.144 vs. 0.314 for T2M-GPT) and R-Precision Top-1 (0.451 vs. 0.431), demonstrating the advantage of the local-to-global paradigm for multi-action sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GuidedMotion achieves the best FID on both HumanML3D (0.057) and the complex-motion subset (0.144), substantially outperforming the nearest competitors ReMoDiffuse (0.103) and T2M-GPT/GraphMotion (0.116) on HumanML3D. It also records the highest Diversity (9.864) on HumanML3D and the best MModality (4.138) on KIT, indicating that the local-action sampling strategy produces more varied and multimodal outputs than baselines like ReMoDiffuse (MModality 1.795) or T2M-GPT (1.856). However, GuidedMotion trails ReMoDiffuse (0.510) and GraphMotion (0.504) by a small margin on R-Precision Top-1 on HumanML3D, and loses to ReMoDiffuse on FID on KIT (0.213 vs. 0.155). On the complex-motion subset, GuidedMotion clearly dominates: it beats T2M-GPT by 0.020 in R-Precision Top-1 and by 0.170 in FID, and outperforms MLD and MDM on all metrics. Overall, the method trades a slight R-Precision gap for significantly better distributional fidelity (FID) and diversity, while uniquely offering continuous guiding-weight control that none of the compared baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion diffusion line of work (MDM, MLD, ReMoDiffuse) by introducing a local-to-global generation paradigm: rather than denoising a single global motion latent directly from text, it decomposes the description into local actions via semantic role parsing and uses their latent embeddings as energy-based guidance signals during the diffusion reverse process. The energy-guided conditional diffusion mechanism is borrowed from EGSDE and FREEDOM, while the hierarchical semantic graph structure (motions → actions → specifics) and GAT-based weight estimation build directly on the authors' prior GraphMotion work. The key conceptual departure is treating local actions as first-class control signals with individually adjustable guiding weights, rather than as implicit components of a monolithic text condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local action guidance mechanism offers a practical controllability lever that existing text-to-motion methods lack: users can swap, combine, or continuously re-weight individual actions (e.g., tightening a walking circle or amplifying arm sway) without re-prompting. The ablation on the complex-motion subset (≥3 actions, ≥150 frames) shows the local-to-global decomposition is most beneficial precisely where direct generation struggles, suggesting the approach could scale to longer, multi-action sequences. Open questions include whether the simple L2 energy function is sufficient for high-fidelity action matching, how the method behaves on substantially longer motions or with more than a handful of local actions, and whether the reliance on a separate text-to-motion model (MLD) for generating reference actions introduces a compounding error bottleneck.
