## Problem

Image stylization requires independently manipulating style and content, but existing approaches either demand multiple reference images, heavy optimization per style-content pair, or suffer from overfitting when fine-tuning diffusion models. There is no lightweight mechanism to implicitly decompose a single image into separable style and content components that can be freely recombined at inference without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240253/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (with center cropping as the only augmentation) and a generic text prompt 'A [v]' during training, producing two LoRA weight matrices (rank 64) corresponding to transformer blocks 4 and 5 of SDXL. At inference, these B-LoRA matrices are plugged into the frozen SDXL backbone and combined with either another image's B-LoRA, a text prompt, or both to generate stylized outputs. Training takes approximately 10 minutes per image on a single A100 GPU over 1000 steps, and the resulting B-LoRAs reduce storage by 70% compared to full LoRA.

## Method

The key insight is that SDXL's transformer blocks have an innate style-content specialization: a prompt-injection analysis reveals that blocks 2 and 4 dominate generated content while block 5 dominates color/style. B-LoRA exploits this by jointly optimizing LoRA weights only on blocks 4 and 5 to reconstruct a single input image, where ΔW⁴ implicitly captures content and ΔW⁵ captures style. Crucially, this separation emerges only from joint training of both blocks—training them independently fails to achieve the same disentanglement. At inference, the two B-LoRAs act as independent, plug-and-play modules: content from one image can be combined with style from another (or a text prompt) by simply updating the corresponding block weights in the frozen SDXL model, eliminating the need for per-pair optimization that methods like ZipLoRA require.

## Results

B-LoRA achieves the highest style alignment score (0.881 ± 0.05 DINO cosine similarity) in both multi-image and single-image settings, outperforming StyleAligned (0.855), DB-LoRA (0.863), StyleDrop (0.826), and ZipLoRA (0.796). However, content preservation scores are lower than ZipLoRA (0.841 multi / 0.933 single) and StyleDrop (0.817 multi / 0.874 single), which the authors attribute to overfitting in those baselines. In a user study with 34 participants (1020 total judgments), B-LoRA was preferred 94% of the time over StyleAligned, 91% over ZipLoRA, and 88% over StyleDrop for overall style transfer quality while preserving content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StyleAligned [21], B-LoRA wins on style similarity (0.881 vs. 0.855 multi, 0.881 vs. 0.829 single) and content similarity (0.790 vs. 0.779 multi), and is strongly preferred in the user study (94%). StyleAligned's main failure mode is style-image semantic leakage (e.g., generating two cats to match the number of people in the style reference). Against ZipLoRA [44], B-LoRA wins decisively on style similarity (0.881 vs. 0.796 multi, 0.881 vs. 0.782 single) but loses on content similarity (0.790 vs. 0.841 multi, 0.790 vs. 0.933 single); the authors note ZipLoRA's high content score reflects overfitting rather than true preservation, and ZipLoRA also requires an extra optimization stage per style-content pair. Against StyleDrop [45], B-LoRA wins on style (0.881 vs. 0.826 multi, 0.881 vs. 0.790 single) and is preferred by 88% of users; StyleDrop struggles to preserve content when trained on multiple images and loses style entirely with a single image. Against the DB-LoRA + ControlNet baseline [43,54], B-LoRA achieves a modest style advantage (0.881 vs. 0.863) while matching content scores (0.790 vs. 0.769), and avoids the need for a separate ControlNet module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm applied to SDXL, following the DreamBooth LoRA training recipe but restricting optimization to only two of eleven transformer blocks. It departs from ZipLoRA, which also combines style and content LoRAs but requires an additional optimization stage per combination, and from StyleDrop/StyleAligned, which handle style adaptation without explicit content separation. The key conceptual departure is the empirical discovery that jointly training LoRA weights on blocks 4 and 5 of SDXL induces an implicit style-content decomposition that cannot be achieved by training each block independently, leveraging the architecture's innate layer-wise specialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, single-image, ~10-minute pipeline for style-content separation that eliminates the need for additional optimization when combining style and content, addressing a key limitation of prior LoRA-based stylization. The architectural insight that specific SDXL blocks encode content vs. style is useful for the broader diffusion-model community, though it is validated only on SDXL and may not transfer to other architectures. Open questions include whether finer-grained sub-components (structure, texture, color) can be further disentangled, and whether the approach extends to multi-object or multi-style combinations without quality degradation.
