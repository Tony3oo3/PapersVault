## Problem

Patch-wise inference for ultra-high-resolution unpaired image-to-image translation introduces tiling artifacts because instance normalization layers compute statistics per patch, creating discontinuities at patch boundaries. Existing remedies—global thumbnail statistics (TIN) cause over/under-colorization and jitter artifacts, while kernelized statistics (KIN) require a two-stage pipeline and a manually chosen kernel size—fail to simultaneously eliminate tiling artifacts, preserve local hue and contrast, and operate in a single pass without hyperparameters.

## Input / Output

The method accepts UHR RGB images ranging from 3,024×4,032 (natural photos, Van Gogh paintings) to 50,000×50,000 pixels (pathological whole slide images) and outputs a seamlessly assembled translated image in the target domain. The input is split into 512×512 patches that are dispatched sequentially; the DN layer is a drop-in replacement for IN layers in pre-trained generators (CUT, CycleGAN, L-LSeSim) at inference time, requiring no retraining or hyperparameter tuning. GPU VRAM usage remains constant regardless of image resolution.

## Method

The Dense Normalization (DN) layer estimates per-pixel mean and standard deviation by interpolating from a 3×3 grid of neighboring patch-level statistics. A prefetching branch runs standard IN on a look-ahead patch and caches its statistics in coordinate-keyed tables; the inference branch queries its own and eight neighbors' statistics, then applies a fast interpolation that reformulates bilinear upsampling from 2×2 submatrices to N×N as a weighted sum of four precomputed N×N weight matrices (M_{k,l}), reducing the operation to GPU-accelerated matrix multiplication. The prefetching parallelism strategy dispatches two patches per step—one for caching, one for inference—so that the GPU processes both concurrently across all non-DN layers, hiding the caching latency and enabling single-pass execution. This yields a 44× speedup over naive pixel-level interpolation while remaining hyperparameter-free and compatible with any IN-based unpaired I2I framework.

## Results

On the FID metric (lower is better), DN achieves 97.73 on Kyoto summer2autumn (below the patch-wise IN lower bound of 98.28) and 130.06 on ANHIR lung lesion (below the lower bound of 130.67), while consistently outperforming TIN and KIN across all five datasets. On the domain differentiation task (higher is better), DN achieves the best accuracy in every experiment, reaching 0.986 on real2paint and 0.975 on both summer2autumn and ANHIR breast, compared to TIN's worst-case 0.556 on real2paint. In human evaluation by 40 participants and domain experts, DN is preferred for best quality (62–83%), fewest tiling artifacts (64–86%), and best color/hue (66–86%) across all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DN outperforms all three baselines on both FID and domain differentiation across all five datasets. Against patch-wise IN (the backbone's native normalization), DN achieves lower FID on summer2autumn (97.73 vs. 98.28 lower bound) and ANHIR lung lesion (130.06 vs. 130.67), while matching or exceeding its domain differentiation accuracy (e.g., 0.975 vs. 0.967 on summer2autumn). Against KIN [19], DN consistently achieves lower FID (e.g., 97.73 vs. 98.00 on summer2autumn; 237.20 vs. 238.56 on real2paint) and higher domain differentiation (e.g., 0.986 vs. 0.757 on real2paint), while also being hyperparameter-free and single-pass versus KIN's two-stage pipeline with a tunable kernel size. Against TIN [20], DN's advantage is most dramatic: TIN's global statistics cause severe over/under-colorization, yielding FID values 19–107 points worse than DN (e.g., 161.13 vs. 68.62 on ANHIR breast) and domain differentiation as low as 0.556 on real2paint versus DN's 0.986. In human evaluation, DN is preferred by experts at 71–86% for quality and artifact reduction, far exceeding KIN (3–11%) and TIN (7–29%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Instance Normalization (IN) framework by replacing its patch-level or global-level statistics with pixel-level estimates, directly addressing the tiling artifacts that arise when patch-wise IN is applied to ultra-high-resolution images. It builds on the normalization-based approaches of TIN (global thumbnail statistics) and KIN (kernel-smoothed patch statistics), but departs from both by performing dense per-pixel interpolation of statistical moments, eliminating the need for a kernel-size hyperparameter or a two-stage pipeline. The DN layer is designed as a drop-in replacement for IN in existing unpaired I2I translation backbones (CUT, CycleGAN, L-LSeSim), requiring no retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyperparameter-free, single-pass DN layer offers a practical path to UHR unpaired I2I translation without architectural changes or retraining, with demonstrated utility in digital pathology stain transformation where reducing expensive IHC staining costs is a real clinical need. However, the method still relies on patch-wise processing, so translated objects can be discontinuous across patch boundaries, and occasional jitter-type artifacts persist. Open questions include the lack of standardized metrics and large-scale benchmarks for UHR translation quality, and whether pixel-level normalization can be extended to handle semantic continuity across patches.
