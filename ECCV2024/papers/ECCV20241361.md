## Problem

Learning-based 3D point cloud registration methods are bottlenecked by training data: real-world datasets like 3DMatch require expensive manual annotation and suffer from error-prone gyroscope labels, while rendering-based synthetic data (e.g., FlyingShapes) introduces a domain gap that degrades real-world performance. No prior method automatically generates large-scale, realistic, and geometrically consistent point cloud registration pairs from real-world captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241361/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single real-world depth map (H×W) and its 3×3 camera intrinsic matrix as input, applies a randomly sampled SE(3) pose to re-project it into an incomplete target depth map, and outputs a complete target depth map that, together with the original, forms a partially-overlapped point cloud pair with exact ground-truth pose. The generated dataset comprises 160k pairs from 320k depth map fragments, all derived solely from 3DMatch training depth maps without any additional data sources.

## Method

PointRegGPT combines three modules: (1) re-projection converts the source depth map to a point cloud, applies a random SE(3) transform, and re-projects back to yield an incomplete depth map that guarantees 3D geometric consistency in overlap regions; (2) a pre-trained diffusion model inpaints the empty pixels using range-null space decomposition (adapted from DDNM) so that the overlap region is strictly preserved while new content is generated realistically, with camera intrinsics injected via cross-attention to a U-Net; (3) a depth correction U-Net with a depth augmentation front-end (min-pooling neighbor map + residual map) identifies and masks invalid depth values caused by the point penetration problem, where sparse re-projection causes background points to appear inside foreground objects. The corrected depth maps are converted to point clouds to form training pairs that supplement existing registration networks (PREDATOR, CoFiNet, GeoTrans), yielding consistent SOTA improvements on indoor and outdoor benchmarks.

## Results

Using GeoTrans as the registration backbone, PointRegGPT's generated data improves FMR/IR/RR from 97.7/70.3/91.5 to 98.7/71.9/93.3 on 3DMatch and from 88.1/43.3/74.0 to 89.4/45.6/77.2 on 3DLoMatch, outperforming the rendered-data baseline SIRA-PCR on IR by 2.1 pp (3DMatch) and 2.7 pp (3DLoMatch). On the outdoor ETH benchmark, PREDATOR+Ours gains 11.3 pp in FMR and 9.4 pp in RR over PREDATOR alone, while CoFiNet+Ours gains 6.8 pp in FMR and 7.9 pp in RR. Across all three registration backbones (PREDATOR, CoFiNet, GeoTrans), the generated data yields consistent improvements of 1–6 pp in FMR, 2–12 pp in IR, and 0.1–9.4 pp in RR depending on the dataset and overlap level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIRA-PCR [8] (rendered synthetic data), PointRegGPT wins on Inlier Ratio by 2.1 pp on 3DMatch (71.9 vs. 69.8) and 2.7 pp on 3DLoMatch (45.6 vs. 42.9), and on Registration Recall on 3DLoMatch (77.2 vs. 76.6), while SIRA retains a 0.8 pp edge on 3DMatch RR (94.1 vs. 93.3). Against the GeoTrans [39] baseline trained only on 3DMatch, the generated data improves FMR by 1.0 pp, IR by 1.6 pp, and RR by 1.8 pp on 3DMatch, with larger gains on the harder 3DLoMatch (FMR +1.3, IR +2.3, RR +3.2 pp). PREDATOR [21] benefits most substantially, gaining 1.7 pp FMR, 6.8 pp IR, and 1.8 pp RR on 3DMatch, and 3.8 pp FMR, 6.4 pp IR, and 3.2 pp RR on 3DLoMatch. CoFiNet [63] sees more modest but consistent gains (FMR +0.0–2.0 pp, IR +0.1–1.7 pp, RR +1.3–3.7 pp). On the unseen outdoor ETH benchmark, PREDATOR+Ours surpasses PREDATOR by 11.3 pp FMR and 9.4 pp RR, and CoFiNet+Ours surpasses CoFiNet by 6.8 pp FMR and 7.9 pp RR, demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization. The method also outperforms all other listed baselines (PerfectMatch, FCGF, D3Feat, SpinNet, YOHO, OIF-PCR, RoReg, PEAL) on the boosted configurations, confirming that the generated data is a drop-in improvement for any registration pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the range-null space decomposition framework from DDNM (Wang et al., 2022) to the specific problem of depth map inpainting under geometric consistency constraints, repurposing an image restoration technique for 3D data generation. It departs from two prior lines of work for point cloud registration data: real-world collection (3DMatch) which is annotation-heavy and label-inaccurate, and rendering-based synthesis (SIRA-PCR/FlyingShapes) which suffers from domain gaps. The depth correction module with its min-pooling augmentation is a practical CNN-based addition that addresses the point penetration artifact unique to re-projection-based pipelines, distinguishing this work from prior simple re-projection approaches (e.g., PRNet) that ignore realism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in learning-based point cloud registration: the scarcity of high-quality, realistic training pairs. By showing that a single source of real depth maps can be expanded into 160k+ realistic pairs that consistently improve multiple SOTA architectures (PREDATOR, CoFiNet, GeoTrans) on both indoor and outdoor benchmarks, it provides a practical recipe for practitioners facing data limitations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor depth maps or other sensor modalities, whether the diffusion model's training on 3DMatch-specific depth distributions limits cross-domain applicability, and whether the 250-step DDIM sampling is fast enough for very large-scale data generation. The finding that unconditional generation degrades performance (Tab. 6) underscores that anchoring to real geometry remains essential.
