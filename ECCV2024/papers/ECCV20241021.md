## Problem

Existing RL fine-tuning approaches for text-to-image generation treat multi-reward optimization as a single-objective problem via weighted sums, requiring manual weight tuning that becomes impractical as the number of quality metrics grows. This leads to over-optimization in one metric (e.g., aesthetics) at the expense of others (e.g., text-image alignment), and the reward trade-off is not controllable after training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training, the method takes a text prompt, expands it via a PaLM 2-L-IT prompt expansion network (LoRA-tuned, 77-token output), prepends a reward-specific identifier token, and generates 512×512 images with Stable Diffusion 1.5 (DDIM, 50 steps). At inference, the user provides the original prompt plus a concatenation of reward-specific preference tokens (e.g., '<reward 1>, <reward 3>') to control the trade-off, and the model produces a 512×512 RGB image using a dual-prompt guidance scheme that blends the original and expanded prompts.

## Method

Parrot replaces the standard all-samples policy gradient update with a batch-wise Pareto-optimal selection: for each mini-batch of N generated images scored on K reward models, only the non-dominated points (those not worse than any other sample on all rewards and strictly better on at least one) receive gradient updates, while dominated samples are assigned zero reward. This is applied jointly to both the T2I diffusion model (cross-attention layers of the UNet) and the prompt expansion network, a co-optimization strategy not explored in prior work. Reward-specific preference is encoded by prepending task-identifier tokens to the expanded prompt, enabling per-reward or combined-reward control at inference without retraining. At inference, an original prompt-centered guidance formula linearly combines the unconditional, original-prompt-conditioned, and expanded-prompt-conditioned score estimates (with separate guidance scales w₁ and w₂) to preserve fidelity to the user's input while incorporating the expanded details.

## Results

On the PartiPrompts benchmark (1632 prompts, 32 images each), Parrot achieves an average quality score of 0.3901, a 19.8% improvement over Stable Diffusion 1.5 (0.3254). Parrot leads on aesthetics (0.7396 vs. 0.5755 for SD 1.5), human preference (0.3411 vs. 0.1930), and image sentiment (0.3132 vs. 0.3010), while text-image alignment (0.1667) is lower than non-expansion baselines due to prompt expansion diluting the original prompt. A user study on 100 prompts confirms Parrot outperforms all baselines across all four quality dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Parrot substantially outperforms Stable Diffusion 1.5 (the unmodified baseline) across aesthetics (+28.5%), human preference (+76.8%), and sentiment (+4.1%), at the cost of lower text-image alignment (0.1667 vs. 0.2322) due to prompt expansion. Against DPOK with a weighted sum of rewards, Parrot achieves a 19.2% higher average score (0.3901 vs. 0.3273), with the largest gains in human preference (0.3411 vs. 0.1932) and aesthetics (0.7396 vs. 0.5813). Compared to Promptist, which only fine-tunes the prompt expansion network, Parrot wins on aesthetics (0.7396 vs. 0.6783), human preference (0.3411 vs. 0.2759), and sentiment (0.3132 vs. 0.2518), while Promptist retains a slight edge on text-image alignment (0.1449 vs. 0.1667 — actually Parrot wins here too). The user study confirms these quantitative findings, with Parrot preferred over all three baselines on every quality dimension. Notably, the ablation showing Parrot T2I Model Tuning Only (0.3992) slightly exceeds full Parrot (0.3901) on average, but full Parrot provides the best balance and the original prompt-centered guidance is critical for maintaining prompt fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Parrot extends the RL fine-tuning paradigm for T2I diffusion models established by DPOK (policy gradient on denoising steps) and Promptist (prompt expansion with reward signals), but departs from both by reframing multi-reward optimization as a multi-objective problem rather than a scalarized single-objective one. The batch-wise Pareto-optimal selection draws directly on Pareto set learning theory from multi-objective optimization (Lin et al., 2022), adapted here to the diffusion sampling context. The original prompt-centered guidance is a natural extension of classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) to the two-prompt setting, and the joint PEN + T2I optimization is a novel combination not explored by either DPOK (T2I-only) or Promptist (PEN-only).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Parrot offers a practical, interpretable solution to the weight-tuning burden in multi-reward T2I RL: by selecting non-dominated samples per batch, it avoids manual reward weighting while still allowing inference-time control via reward-specific prompt tokens. The finding that jointly optimizing the prompt expansion network and the T2I model yields substantially larger gains than either alone (+22.6% vs. +3–7% for single-component tuning) suggests that prior work may have been leaving performance on the table by freezing one component. The main limitation is that Parrot's ceiling is bounded by the quality and coverage of its reward models, and the approach has only been validated on Stable Diffusion 1.5 at 512×512, leaving open whether the Pareto-selection strategy transfers to larger or architecturally different diffusion models.
