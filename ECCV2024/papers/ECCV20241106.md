## Problem

Existing common subspace-based methods for incomplete multimodal learning project all modality combinations into a single deterministic embedding, imposing an implicit intra-class direction constraint that forces samples of the same class but different modality subsets to align in the same direction. This prevents the model from capturing modality-specific information, leading to degraded feature diversity and sub-optimal performance across modality combinations.

## Input / Output

The method accepts any subset of available modalities (RGB, depth, IR images at standard resolutions for CASIA-SURF; audio spectrograms of 257×299 or 257×1004 paired with single visual frames for emotion recognition; RGB-D pairs for NYUv2 segmentation), with modality availability randomized via Bernoulli dropout during training. At inference, the model must handle all 2^V possible modality combinations without requiring missing-modality imputation. Outputs are class labels (ACER for anti-spoofing, accuracy for emotion recognition) or pixel-wise segmentation masks (mIOU for NYUv2).

## Method

DMRNet replaces the deterministic fused embedding with a per-pixel multivariate Gaussian distribution (mean μ, std σ) estimated via 1×1 convolutions over the feature map, enabling dense prediction. During training, a reparameterized sample s_i = μ_i + εσ_i is fed to the prediction head for the task loss, while μ_i serves as the inference embedding; this decoupling blocks the cross-entropy gradient from constraining μ_i's direction, allowing non-parallel embeddings for different modality combinations of the same class. A KL-divergence regularizer (weighted by α) prevents σ from growing unboundedly, and a hard combination regularizer (weighted by β) identifies the top-V variance modality combinations and applies an auxiliary prediction loss through a parameter-shared head to address unbalanced training across combinations.

## Results

On CASIA-SURF (face anti-spoofing, 3 modalities: RGB/Depth/IR), DMRNet achieves the best average ACER of 3.58%, outperforming the strongest baseline MMANet (3.94%) and ShaSpec (5.59%) across all 7 modality combinations. On audio-visual emotion recognition (CREMA-D), DMRNet reaches 61.35% average accuracy, surpassing ShaSpec (57.94%) and MMANet (57.66%) by 3.41% and 3.69% respectively. On Kinetics-Sounds (34 actions), DMRNet achieves 56.01% average accuracy, a 4.39% gain over ShaSpec. For dense prediction on NYUv2 (RGB-D segmentation), DMRNet improves mIOU from 42.77% to 45.08% over ESANet-MD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMRNet consistently outperforms all six compared baselines on the primary CASIA-SURF benchmark. Against the common-subspace methods (HeMIS, LCR, RFNet, mmFormer), DMRNet reduces average ACER by 3.60%, 3.13%, 2.44%, and 2.35% respectively, with the largest gains on single-modality inputs (e.g., IR-only: 8.98% vs. 13.99% for mmFormer). Against the two strongest SOTA baselines, DMRNet beats ShaSpec by 2.01% average ACER and MMANet by 0.36%; notably, MMANet requires a separately trained teacher model with complete data, whereas DMRNet operates without any auxiliary model. On audio-visual tasks (CREMA-D, Kinetics-Sounds), DMRNet wins on average accuracy over ShaSpec and MMANet by 3.41%/3.69% and 4.39%/4.07% respectively, though it slightly trails ShaSpec on the audio-only CREMA-D combination (58.87% vs. 59.86%). For segmentation on NYUv2, DMRNet improves mIOU by 2.46% over ESANet-MD across all modality combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMRNet departs from the dominant common subspace paradigm for incomplete multimodal learning (HeMIS, LCR, RFNet, mmFormer, ShaSpec) by identifying an implicit intra-class direction constraint that forces all modality combinations of the same class to share a single embedding direction. It repurposes probabilistic embeddings—previously applied to face recognition quality uncertainty (Shi et al.) and cross-modal retrieval semantic uncertainty (Chun et al., Neculai et al.)—not to model input uncertainty but to *proactively inject* stochasticity that decouples the training embedding from the inference embedding. The reparameterization trick from VAEs enables backpropagation through the sampling, while a KL-divergence regularizer (borrowed from prior probabilistic embedding work) prevents the variance from collapsing or exploding. The hard combination regularizer is a lightweight, parameter-free addition that shares weights with the task predictor, distinguishing it from auxiliary-module-based regularizers in RFNet and mmFormer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The direction-constraint diagnosis provides a useful mechanistic explanation for why common subspace methods underperform on individual modality combinations, and the probabilistic decoupling offers a general, drop-in fix applicable to both classification and dense prediction (segmentation). However, the approach introduces non-trivial hyperparameter sensitivity (α, β) and the hard combination regularizer requires a pre-training variance-ranking pass, limiting plug-and-play convenience. Notably, on the audio-visual CREMA-D dataset DMRNet actually degrades unimodal performance (visual-only: 55.10 vs. 41.66 baseline), suggesting the relaxation mechanism can hurt when one modality is inherently weak. Open questions include scalability beyond three modalities, handling of continuous modality degradation (e.g., partial occlusion) rather than binary missing, and whether the 2D feature-map distribution estimation generalizes to higher-dimensional or non-CNN backbones.
