## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods assume test data shares the same class space as training data, but open-world test streams inevitably contain outliers from unknown semantic classes. This creates two risks: misclassifying outliers into known categories poses safety hazards (e.g., in autonomous driving), and self-supervised losses computed on outliers misguide optimization, degrading recognition on known classes. The paper introduces outlier-aware TTA, a scenario requiring simultaneous recognition of normal samples and rejection of outliers via a continuous OOD score.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images in an online streaming fashion (batch size 64), where the stream is a mixture of in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples at a configurable ratio (default 8:2). For each incoming sample, STAMP produces two outputs: a class prediction over the known label space and an OOD score defined as the entropy of the augmentation-averaged prediction (averaged over K=16 augmented views). A downstream detector compares the OOD score against a tunable threshold to decide whether to retain or reject the prediction, making the system adaptable to different safety requirements.

## Method

STAMP optimizes over a stable memory bank rather than the raw mini-batch, combining three mechanisms. First, a reliable class-balanced memory stores samples that pass both consistency filtering (prediction matches the frozen source model) and entropy filtering (below a threshold), using augmentation-averaged predictions for robustness; when full, it evicts samples from the most frequently updated class to preserve class balance. Second, self-weighted entropy minimization assigns each in-memory sample a differentiable weight w_i = exp(−H(f(x_i))), so the model adaptively learns to up-weight low-entropy (reliable) samples while pushing outliers toward higher entropy. Third, a stable optimization strategy couples sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) with cosine-annealed step-size decay during test time, enabling fast early adaptation while preventing long-term error accumulation. Only BN affine parameters are updated, with the top classification layer frozen.

## Results

On CIFAR10-C under outlier-aware TTA (averaged over 4 outlier datasets), STAMP achieves 81.6% ACC, 79.9% AUC, and 80.5% H-score, outperforming the best baseline CoTTA (80.8/75.1/77.2) by +0.8% ACC and +4.8% AUC. On CIFAR100-C, STAMP reaches 62.3% ACC and 83.1% AUC, surpassing the strongest competitor EATA (57.7/77.4) by +4.6% ACC and +5.7% AUC. On ImageNet-C, STAMP attains 57.2% and 55.6% H-score on Places365-C and Textures-C respectively, exceeding the best baseline EATA by +1.2% and +2.8%. Under the conventional closed-set TTA setting (no outliers), STAMP also leads with 84.3% and 64.4% average accuracy on CIFAR10-C and CIFAR100-C, a +2.9% and +2.7% improvement over the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAMP outperforms all eight baselines on both ACC and AUC across every dataset and outlier configuration. The most striking gap is against Tent [51], whose indiscriminate entropy minimization causes AUC to collapse from 66.3% (Source) to 57.9% on CIFAR10-C, while STAMP lifts AUC to 79.9% (+22.0% over Tent). Among the stronger TTA baselines, CoTTA [54] is the closest competitor on CIFAR10-C (H-score 77.2 vs. STAMP's 80.5), but STAMP pulls ahead by +4.8% AUC. On CIFAR100-C, EATA [38] and SAR [39] are the strongest baselines (H-score 65.9 and 65.8), yet STAMP exceeds them by +4.9% and +5.0% H-score respectively, driven primarily by a +5.7% AUC gain over EATA. OWTTT [28], the only prior method explicitly targeting open-world TTA, suffers severe accuracy degradation (62.0% on CIFAR10-C vs. STAMP's 81.6%), indicating its prototype-expansion strategy is fragile under distribution shift. Under the closed-set protocol (Table 4), STAMP still leads SoTTA [17] by +2.9% average accuracy on CIFAR10-C and SAR [39] by +2.7% on CIFAR100-C, confirming that the memory-replay mechanism does not sacrifice in-distribution performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAMP extends the entropy-minimization-based TTA paradigm established by Tent [51] and the high-entropy sample filtering introduced by EATA [38], but departs from both by optimizing over a curated memory bank rather than the incoming mini-batch. It also builds on sharpness-aware minimization [14] for gradient stability and borrows the class-balanced replay idea from continual learning, repurposing it to prevent dominant-class bias during adaptation. The key conceptual departure from prior outlier-aware TTA work (Lee et al. [26], Gong et al. [17], Li et al. [28]) is the simultaneous provision of a continuous OOD score alongside recognition, rather than binary outlier flags or ignoring rejection entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The outlier-aware TTA formulation is a more realistic deployment scenario than closed-set TTA, particularly for safety-critical systems like autonomous driving where misclassifying unknown objects as known classes carries real risk. The memory-bank-with-filtering approach offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism that practitioners can adopt, and the clean ablation separating the contributions of memory (ACC) from self-weighted entropy (AUC) gives clear guidance on which component to prioritize. Open questions include whether the approach scales to transformer backbones, video/sequential adaptation, and whether the fixed memory capacity of 64 remains sufficient for larger class spaces or longer test streams.
