## Problem

Existing Transformer-based layout generation methods (PLay, DLT, LayoutDM) rely on autoencoders to project geometric data into a latent space before applying diffusion, which distorts critical geometric features such as bounding box alignment. This paper asks whether diffusion can operate directly on the original geometric input space without any encoder-decoder bottleneck, and whether such a design generalizes beyond rectangles to other geometric structures like line segments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241364/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each layout object is encoded as a 4×4 tensor: the first row holds (x, y, h, w) of the bounding box, the second row holds the global layout height and width, and the remaining 8 entries encode the category as a ±1 pattern (all −1 indicates an inactive slot for variable-length layouts). All values are normalized to [−1, 1]. For conditional generation, specific tensor entries are selectively unmasked (e.g., fixing category or size) while the rest is generated. The model outputs complete layout bounding boxes with category labels, or, in the line-segment extension, pairs of endpoint coordinates per segment.

## Method

Dolfin is a DiT-based transformer diffusion model that applies Gaussian noise directly to the 4×4 object tensors in the original geometric space, eliminating the autoencoder entirely. Two variants are provided: a non-autoregressive (bi-directional) version where all tokens are denoised jointly in a single forward pass, and an autoregressive version (Dolfin-AR) that recursively predicts noise token-by-token, conditioning each step on previously predicted noises to capture local semantic correlations like alignment and overlap. Training uses standard MSE noise-prediction loss (plus KL divergence under DDPM), with a 4-layer, 8-head, 512-dim transformer. The key architectural insight is that operating in the original space preserves geometric structure without latent-space distortion, and the same pipeline extends naturally to line-segment generation by simply replacing the 4×4 tensor with endpoint coordinates.

## Results

On PublayNet unconditional generation, Dolfin achieves the best FID of 9.12 (vs. DLT 14.25, PLay 13.71) and the best MaxIoU of 0.35, while Dolfin-AR achieves the best alignment score of 0.042 (vs. DLT 0.110, LT 0.127) and best overlap of 2.4. On RICO, Dolfin attains the highest MaxIoU of 0.42 (vs. LayoutGAN++ 0.36, NDN-none 0.35) and FID of 15.38. For conditional generation, Dolfin achieves the best MaxIoU on RICO (0.529 Cate., 0.550 Cate.+Size) and the best DocSim scores across all settings (0.42–0.59), substantially outperforming BLT (0.11–0.30) and DLT (not reported for DocSim).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dolfin (non-autoregressive) wins on FID for PublayNet (9.12 vs. DLT 14.25, PLay 13.71) and on MaxIoU for RICO (0.42 vs. LayoutGAN++ 0.36, NDN-none 0.35), while Dolfin-AR wins on alignment for PublayNet (0.042 vs. DLT 0.110, LT 0.127) and ties LT on overlap (2.4). Dolfin-AR underperforms on RICO unconditional generation (FID 26.44, MaxIoU 0.17), losing to DLT (FID 17.80, MaxIoU 0.27) and LayoutGAN++ (MaxIoU 0.36). For conditional generation, Dolfin achieves the best MaxIoU on RICO (0.529/0.550) and the best DocSim across all four settings (0.42–0.59 vs. BLT 0.11–0.30), but LayoutDM(1) retains the edge on PublayNet Cate. MaxIoU (0.440 vs. Dolfin 0.336). On line segment generation, Dolfin dramatically outperforms reimplemented baselines LT (FID 92.1 vs. 317.4) and LayoutGAN++ (FID 92.1 vs. 400.0). The user study confirms Dolfin is preferred by 43.4% of participants for unconditional and 58.5% for conditional generation, versus the next-best LayoutDM at 22.6% and DLT at 26.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dolfin builds directly on DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2022) as its transformer-diffusion backbone but makes a deliberate architectural departure from prior layout diffusion models (PLay, DLT, LayoutDM) by eliminating the autoencoder or latent-space projection entirely. Where PLay encodes layouts into a continuous latent space, DLT separates discrete and continuous components with specialized noise processes, and LayoutDM decomposes objects into multiple discrete tokens, Dolfin simply applies Gaussian noise to the original geometric tensor and lets the transformer denoise it. The autoregressive variant (Dolfin-AR) further extends the standard single-pass denoising by recursively conditioning each token's noise prediction on previously predicted tokens, drawing on the sequential dependency structure that prior autoregressive layout models (LT, BLT) exploit without diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is transparency: operating in the original coordinate space means intermediate diffusion states are directly interpretable as (noisy) layouts, enabling straightforward constraint injection and cross-task interaction (e.g., pulling intermediate layouts into image generation pipelines). The extension to line segment generation from natural images is, to the authors' knowledge, the first diffusion-based approach to that problem, opening a path toward generative modeling of arbitrary geometric primitives. However, limitations remain: training cost is substantial (48–96 hours on 8 GPUs), the autoregressive variant underperforms the non-autoregressive one on FID, and small-item overlap persists in conditional generation. Whether the no-autoencoder principle generalizes to higher-dimensional or more complex geometric structures (e.g., 3D meshes, multi-page documents) remains an open question.
