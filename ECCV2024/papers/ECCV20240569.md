## Problem

Multi-modal face anti-spoofing methods built on Vision Transformers degrade severely when one or more modalities are unavailable at deployment. Existing missing-modality approaches either extract only modality-invariant features (e.g., AMA, MA-ViT) or only modality-specific features (e.g., MAP, MMANet), and none simultaneously capture fine-grained spoofing cues that are both shared across modalities and unique to each modality for distinguishing different attack types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240569/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any non-empty subset of three modalities—RGB, depth, and IR images—converted into patch embeddings via linear projection, with a Bernoulli mask simulating missing modalities during training (7 possible combinations). The frozen MultiViT-B backbone (12 blocks, D=768, N_t=589 patches) processes the masked multi-modal sequence, and a classification head outputs a binary live/spoofing prediction. Only the modality-disentangle adapters and classification head are trained, keeping the backbone frozen for efficiency.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Modality-Disentangle Adapter (MDA), which applies FFT-based frequency decomposition to the fused multi-modal features: a low-pass filter (f×f kernel) isolates modality-invariant cues processed by 5×5 convolutions, while a high-pass filter extracts modality-specific cues processed by 3×3 convolutions, followed by cross-modal fusion convolution. To further enhance these features, the paper introduces an LBP-guided contrastive loss that pairs batch-level masking (uniform modality combination per batch → positive pairs) with sample-level masking (per-sample random combinations → negative pairs), weighting positive pairs by LBP feature similarity to separate different attack types. An Adaptive Modal Combination Sampling (AMS) strategy dynamically adjusts per-combination sampling probabilities via a cosine function of KL-divergence-based imbalance scores, ensuring all modality combinations train synergistically rather than over-focusing on a single weak combination.

## Results

On the intra-dataset protocol, MMA-FAS achieves a mean ACER of 2.26% (±1.24) on WMCA ('seen') and 5.56% (±3.47) on CASIA-SURF, outperforming all baselines across all seven modality combinations. In the cross-dataset setting (trained on WMCA, tested on CASIA-SURF), MMA-FAS reaches 21.91% (±2.59) mean ACER, improving over the next-best method AMA (23.74%) by ~1.8 points. Under the cross-attack protocol (CeFA protocol4@3), MMA-FAS achieves 14.56% (±5.65) mean ACER, a substantial margin over the runner-up Vanilla ViT (24.40%). Notably, MMA-FAS uses only 10.48M trainable parameters versus 271M for FM-ViT and MA-ViT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMA-FAS outperforms all six baselines on both WMCA and CASIA-SURF intra-dataset protocols. Against the closest competitors FM-ViT (2.54%) and MA-ViT (3.00%) on WMCA, MMA-FAS (2.26%) leads by 0.28 and 0.74 points respectively, while using only ~4% of their trainable parameters (10.48M vs 271M). The gap widens dramatically against methods designed for missing modalities: MAP (6.56%) and AMA (8.43%) on WMCA, and MAP (21.55%) and AMA (25.19%) on CASIA-SURF, where MMA-FAS achieves 5.56%. In the cross-dataset protocol, MMA-FAS (21.91%) beats AMA (23.74%), MA-ViT (24.61%), and FM-ViT (24.70%), while MAP (36.98%), MMANet (54.24%), and mmformer (48.75%) degrade severely. Under the cross-attack protocol on CeFA, MMA-FAS (14.56%) leads the next-best Vanilla ViT (24.40%) by nearly 10 points, with all other baselines ranging from 24.81% to 40.59%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a middle ground between two existing lines of missing-modality FAS research: modality-invariant feature methods (MA-ViT, FM-ViT, AMA) that ignore fine-grained modality-specific spoofing cues, and modality-specific feature methods (MAP, MMANet, ShaSpec) that either neglect invariant features or rely on separate CNN encoders that cannot capture local FAS-relevant details. The key conceptual departure is the frequency-decomposition hypothesis—modality-invariant cues reside in low-frequency components while modality-specific cues reside in high-frequency components—implemented via FFT-based filtering inside lightweight adapters on a frozen MultiViT-B backbone, rather than through multiple parallel encoders or prompt-based mechanisms. The LBP-guided contrastive loss and the cosine-function-based adaptive sampling strategy further distinguish the work from prior masking-based approaches like MMANet's auxiliary-classifier balancing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Missing modality is a practical deployment bottleneck for multi-modal FAS systems, and this paper provides the most comprehensive benchmark to date across all seven modality combinations on three datasets, making it a useful reference for practitioners. The frequency-decomposition adapter is a lightweight, interpretable mechanism (10.48M trainable parameters vs. 271M for FM-ViT) that could be adapted to other multi-modal recognition tasks beyond FAS. Open questions include whether the low-frequency/invariant and high-frequency/specific assumption generalizes to more than three modalities or to fundamentally different sensor types, whether the frozen-backbone constraint limits ceiling performance, and whether hand-crafted LBP descriptors can be replaced by learned attack-type embeddings as datasets with explicit attack labels become more available.
