## Problem

Flow-based motion segmentation can discover moving objects but produces imperfect masks under partial motion, background distraction, articulation, and occlusion. While appearance streams offer stronger segmentation cues, they suffer a significant Sim2Real gap that prevents direct transfer from synthetic training to real videos. The paper tackles the question of how to exploit temporally consistent appearance information to correct flow-predicted masks without requiring human annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241036/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB frames (resized to 128×224), RAFT-estimated optical flow, and K=3 flow-predicted object masks per sequence from OCLR-flow. It produces refined per-frame, per-object segmentation masks for all T frames, along with 3-channel error maps (false positive, false negative, TP+TN) used to score and select t=10 exemplar masks per object. DINO ViT features (ViT-S/16 for the selector, ViT-S/8 for the corrector) provide semantic priors, and a CRF post-processing step sharpens final boundaries.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: OCLR-flow generates initial multi-object mask proposals, then an appearance-based refinement stage performs sequence-level mask selection followed by object-centric mask correction. The selector predicts per-frame error maps using DINO features concatenated with CNN-encoded RGB+mask features in a UNet-like architecture, identifying high-quality exemplars via temporal shape consistency and semantic content consistency. The corrector is a transformer decoder where object queries are initialized by weighted pooling of exemplar-mask features, then refined through two cross-attention layers (one attending to target-frame features, one to exemplar-frame features) with a Slot-Attention-like competitive mechanism between foreground and background queries. Training proceeds via synthetic pre-training followed by self-supervised pseudo-label adaptation to real videos, using a combined loss of BCE + boundary loss on masks and DINO feature reconstruction with alpha-channel supervision, requiring zero human annotations throughout.

## Results

On the multi-object motion segmentation benchmarks, the method achieves J&F of 65.2 on YTVOS18-m and 66.2 on DAVIS17-m, substantially outperforming the flow-only OCLR-flow baseline (45.3 and 55.1) and the test-time-augmented OCLR-TTA (50.6 and 64.4). With SAM prompting, performance further improves to J&F 70.6 (YTVOS18-m) and 71.5 (DAVIS17-m), approaching or matching semi-supervised methods that use first-frame ground truth. On single-object benchmarks, the method achieves J of 81.1 (DAVIS16), 76.6 (SegTrackv2), and 81.9 (FBMS-59), ranking top among human-annotation-free methods on two of three datasets, with Ours+SAM reaching 86.6/81.3/85.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all unsupervised flow-based baselines: OCLR-flow (J&F 45.3→65.2 on YTVOS18-m, 55.1→66.2 on DAVIS17-m), OCLR-TTA (50.6→65.2, 64.4→66.2), VideoCutLER (57.0→65.2, 57.3→66.2), and MG (33.3→65.2, 35.8→66.2). It also surpasses the per-frame SAM refinement of OCLR-flow (58.5→65.2 on YTVOS18-m), demonstrating that video-level temporal consistency is more effective than frame-independent prompting. Against semi-supervised methods (which receive first-frame ground truth), Ours+SAM (70.6/71.5) remains competitive with CRW (72.9/73.4) but trails DINO (75.4/78.7) and UVC (66.9/65.5) on YTVOS18-m/DAVIS17-m respectively. On single-object benchmarks, the method beats OCLR-flow, OCLR-TTA, DystaB, and GWM on DAVIS16 and FBMS-59, but underperforms LOCATE on SegTrackv2 (76.6 vs 79.9) due to noisy flow from low-quality video artefacts. Among supervised methods, Ours+SAM achieves top or matching performance (86.6 DAVIS16, 81.3 STv2, 85.7 FBMS-59), exceeding MATNet on STv2 (81.3 vs 50.4) and FBMS-59 (85.7 vs 76.1) but trailing DPA on DAVIS16 (86.6 vs 87.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OCLR-flow (Xie et al., NeurIPS 2022) as its flow-based proposal stage, extending it with an appearance-based refinement pipeline that the original method explicitly could not provide. The object-centric correction module adapts Slot Attention's competitive binding mechanism (Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020) into a transformer decoder that fuses local target-frame features with global exemplar information, while the selection mechanism departs from prior confidence-score approaches (BubbleNet, Yin et al.) by predicting explicit error maps (FP/FN/TP+TN) as an intermediate task. The two-stream (motion + appearance) design follows the broader neuroscience-inspired paradigm, but the specific combination of sequence-level exemplar selection, motion-initialised object queries, and self-supervised Sim2Real adaptation via pseudo-labeling is novel in its assembly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: flow-based motion segmentation fails on complex real-world scenarios (stationary objects, articulation, occlusion, background motion) while appearance-based methods suffer from Sim2Real domain shift. The selection-correction framework offers a principled middle ground that requires zero human annotations, and the new YTVOS18-m benchmark provides a more challenging multi-object evaluation setting for the community. Key limitations remain: exemplar selection degrades when most frames are problematic (e.g., long stationary segments), and frozen DINO features operate at the semantic level, struggling to distinguish entangled objects with similar appearances at the instance level. The complementary gains from SAM prompting suggest a productive direction for combining video-level object-centric consistency with per-frame foundation-model refinement.
