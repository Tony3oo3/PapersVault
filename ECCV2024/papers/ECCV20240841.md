## Problem

Trained UDA models frequently produce ambiguous predictions on target samples, where high probabilities are concentrated among a few closely related classes rather than a single confident class. Existing UDA methods lack an explicit mechanism to resolve this intra-class ambiguity, leaving target accuracy limited by the model's inability to disambiguate among the most probable candidates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240841/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a post-hoc refinement stage on top of any pre-trained UDA model (e.g., MDD, MCC, LeCo, SDAT, NWD). It consumes unlabeled target-domain RGB images and the trained model's own predictions to generate per-sample pseudo-candidate sets via a probability threshold τ. The output is a refined model whose target-domain predictions are sharpened: the correct class within the candidate set gains confidence while probabilities for non-candidate classes are suppressed.

## Method

UDPCS first generates a pseudo-candidate set Z for each target sample by selecting all classes whose predicted probability exceeds threshold τ, then trains the model with two complementary losses. The candidate loss applies KL divergence between a confidence vector w (initialized uniformly over Z, then iteratively updated to be proportional to the model's own predictions) and the model's output on a strongly augmented input (Cutout + RandAugment), but only when the maximum predicted probability falls below a second threshold γ to focus on genuinely ambiguous samples. The non-candidate loss penalizes the model via negative log-likelihood for assigning high probability to classes outside Z, computed on a weakly augmented (flip-and-shift) input. The overall objective is L_c + λ·L_nc, and the confidence vector w is re-normalized over Z after each iteration, progressively concentrating mass on the most likely true label and driving disambiguation.

## Results

UDPCS achieves consistent target accuracy gains across all three benchmark datasets when applied on top of existing UDA methods. On VisDA, the average gain is 2.0% (max 2.4% for MCC and NWD), on DomainNet the average gain is 1.6% (max 3.5% for MDD), and on OfficeHome the average gain is 0.9% (max 1.8% for MDD). The best absolute performances after refinement are LeCo+Ours at 88.0% mean accuracy on VisDA, MDD+Ours at 55.7% average accuracy on DomainNet, and MCC+Ours / NWD+Ours at 73.6% average accuracy on OfficeHome.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDPCS is applied as a post-hoc refinement on five UDA baselines (MDD, MCC, LeCo, SDAT, NWD) and consistently improves target accuracy across all three datasets with no cases of degradation. The largest gains appear on VisDA where MCC+Ours jumps from 83.8% to 86.2% (+2.4%) and NWD+Ours from 82.8% to 85.2% (+2.4%), while LeCo+Ours reaches the highest absolute mean accuracy of 88.0%. On DomainNet, MDD+Ours shows the most dramatic improvement (+3.5%, from 52.2% to 55.7%), whereas SDAT+Ours gains only 0.1% (51.3%→51.4%), suggesting the method is most effective when the base model has moderate confidence ambiguity. On OfficeHome, gains are smaller (0.3–1.8%) due to the limited sample size per domain, with MDD+Ours achieving the largest improvement (+1.8%, 69.5%→71.3%). Overall, UDPCS wins on every baseline–dataset combination tested, with the magnitude of gain depending on how much prediction ambiguity the base UDA model exhibits.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDPCS positions itself as a post-hoc refinement layer atop any existing UDA method (adversarial, self-training, or hybrid), rather than proposing a new adaptation paradigm. Its core mechanism—generating pseudo-candidate sets from model predictions and disambiguating within them—directly borrows the iterative label-refinement strategy from partial-label learning (PRODEN [25]), while the strong/weak augmentation and confidence-thresholding design follows FixMatch [39]. The paper explicitly frames the pseudo-candidate set as a first application of this PLL concept to the UDA setting, distinguishing it from hybrid UDA+PLL formulations like [49] that jointly optimize both objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that UDPCS is a drop-in refinement requiring no modification to the base UDA pipeline, with consistent (if modest) gains of 0.9–2.4% across five baselines and three benchmarks. However, the gains are incremental, the method adds refinement iterations on top of already-trained models, and it has not been evaluated on the most recent UDA approaches (e.g., foundation-model-based or source-free methods). An open question is whether the pseudo-candidate set approach generalizes to settings with larger domain gaps or open-set targets where the assumption that the true label lies within the top-τ predictions may break down.
