## Problem

Existing image-captioning datasets (Flickr30k, MS-COCO) contain only whole-image captions, making them insufficient for training controllable image captioning (CIC) models that must attend to arbitrary subsets of regions or semantic relationships. Additionally, SOTA CIC models rely on complex, overly descriptive control signals (e.g., grounded abstract scene graphs or verb-specific semantic roles) that are impractical for real-world deployment where users simply select objects of interest.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image, bounding boxes of entities of interest (spatial control), a coarse length-level token (e.g., 1–9 words, 10–19 words) paired with the exact desired word count, and an optional verb signal indicating the target action. Visual embeddings are extracted via a Faster R-CNN model trained on Visual Genome for object/attribute classification, providing position-aware region features and a global image vector. The output is a single natural-language caption that describes only the specified sub-region at the requested verbosity.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part system: (1) Structured Semantic Augmentation (SSA), a fully automatic data-augmentation pipeline that converts each original caption to an AMR graph, visually grounds AMR nodes via word-to-bounding-box alignment, merges all per-caption graphs into a single meta-vgAMR using UPGMA hierarchical clustering with node-merging criteria based on visual grounding and semantic similarity, samples event-focused subgraphs from predicate nodes, generates new captions via the SPRING AMR-to-Text model, and filters low-quality outputs with GRUEN; (2) CIC-BART, a transformer encoder-decoder built on VL-BART whose encoder input is extended with region embeddings and length/verb control tokens. The key insight is that AMR captures fine-grained semantic relations (beyond the geometric/possessive relations typical of scene graphs), enabling automatic generation of diverse, semantically coherent focused captions without new human annotation, while the model itself requires only simple bounding-box and length controls rather than complex scene-graph or verb-role specifications.

## Results

On COCO-Ent, CIC-BART-SSA achieves the best overall harmonic mean score (H=78.3), surpassing ASG (74.2), VSR (56.2), and SCT (55.8). It leads in text quality (G=74.8), self-CIDEr diversity (sC=82.5), and n-gram diversity (D-1=44.6, D-2=63.2), while being competitive in content controllability (IoU=77.2, second only to VSR's 77.6). On Flickr-Ent, CIC-BART-SSA again achieves the top H score (71.3) with the best G (86.0), sC (81.7), D-1 (47.0), and D-2 (62.6). Length controllability improves dramatically with SSA: MAE drops from 0.49 (CIC-BART) to 0.11 (CIC-BART-SSA) on COCO-Ent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIC-BART-SSA outperforms all baselines on the overall harmonic mean (H) on both datasets: 78.3 vs. ASG's 74.2 and VSR's 56.2 on COCO-Ent, and 71.3 vs. VSR's 62.5 and SCT's 54.6 on Flickr-Ent. VSR achieves the highest content controllability (IoU=77.6 on COCO-Ent) but at the cost of very poor text quality (G=39.0), reflecting its overly descriptive control signal. ASG [13] is competitive on COCO-Ent (H=74.2) but requires complex abstract scene graph control signals and was not evaluated on Flickr-Ent. SCT [18] is consistently the weakest baseline, with H scores of 55.8 (COCO-Ent) and 54.6 (Flickr-Ent), and notably poor diversity (sC=42.8, D-1=27.0). On the challenging SSA-only test samples (Table 2), CIC-BART-SSA shows the largest gains over SCT (e.g., H: 75.6 vs. 51.7 on COCO-Ent), confirming that SSA training specifically improves generalization to highly focused, sub-region captioning where original-dataset-trained models struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VL-BART (Cho et al., ICML 2021) as its encoder-decoder backbone, extending it with region-based and length-based control signals for the controllable image captioning (CIC) task. It departs from the prevailing CIC trend of increasingly complex control signals—scene graphs (ASG2Caption), verb-specific semantic roles (VSR), or GPT-2 prompt retrieval (ComPro)—by demonstrating that simple bounding-box and length controls suffice when paired with a structured data augmentation strategy. The key conceptual departure is the use of Abstract Meaning Representation (AMR) graphs, rather than the scene graphs common in prior CIC work, to encode richer spatio-semantic relations and automatically generate focused training captions via subgraph sampling and AMR-to-text generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SSA framework offers a general, fully automatic recipe for enriching any grounded image-caption dataset with focused, diverse captions, directly addressing the data scarcity that limits CIC model generalization to highly focused sub-regions. The finding that simple control signals (bounding boxes + length) achieve competitive or superior overall performance compared to models requiring detailed scene-graph or verb-role specifications has practical implications for deployment in settings where users cannot specify complex structures. Open questions include whether the AMR-based augmentation generalizes to other languages or to tasks beyond captioning (e.g., visual question answering), how sensitive the pipeline is to AMR parser errors at scale, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-image captioning.
