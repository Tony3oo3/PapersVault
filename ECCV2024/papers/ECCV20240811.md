## Problem

Optimization-based NLOS imaging methods offer versatility across diverse scanning setups (non-planar relay walls, sparse patterns, non-confocal systems) but incur prohibitive computational costs because the vast majority of the hidden volume is empty (less than 5% occupied by actual surfaces). This paper addresses the gap by identifying and eliminating the superfluous computations on empty regions without sacrificing the general applicability that distinguishes optimization-based approaches from FFT-based inverse solutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240811/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes time-resolved transient measurements from a relay wall scan as input, supporting sparse patterns as low as 16×16 scan points with no assumptions on relay wall geometry (planar or non-planar) or scanning configuration (confocal or non-confocal). It produces a high-resolution albedo volume and surface normal volume of the hidden scene (up to 128×128), reconstructed in under a minute on a single commercial GPU using less than 2 GB of memory. The key practical advantage is that 32×32 sparse scanning suffices for high-quality output, reducing acquisition time to under 3 minutes compared to ~46 minutes for dense raster scanning required by FFT-based methods.

## Method

The core formulation decomposes transients into a superposition of point-wise light propagation functions g_p, each encoding arrival time, distance fall-off, and Lambertian cosine factor for a given point p with albedo ρ and normal n. This decomposition enables rendering transients from an arbitrary number of sampled points via a matrix-free CUDA kernel, which is the key enabler for domain reduction. During optimization, the method periodically applies a soft domain reduction: a Gaussian low-pass filter is applied to the albedo volume, and grid bins below a threshold (5% of max albedo) are pruned from the active sampling domain, eliminating ~96% of computations. A coarse-to-fine strategy initializes with a low-resolution grid, prunes empty regions, then subdivides active bins to higher resolution with trilinear interpolation of variables, achieving ~20× total speedup. Noise robustness is handled via L1 sparsity regularization on albedo plus jointly optimized per-histogram noise parameters bounded by a sigmoid-activated learnable offset.

## Results

On the ZNLOS Bunny (confocal 32×32 transients), the proposed method achieves the best depth MAE of 0.0477 and RMSE of 0.1523 at 5% threshold, and the best normal MAE of 0.1147 and RMSE of 0.2394, outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, NeTF, achieves depth MAE 0.0679 / RMSE 0.1748 but cannot reconstruct surface normals. The method also achieves ~20× reconstruction speedup over the no-reduction baseline (54 s vs. 1087 s for 1000 iterations) and produces 128×128 outputs in under a minute using only 32×32 sparse scanning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on both depth and normal reconstruction metrics. Against the closest depth-only competitor NeTF [39], it reduces depth MAE from 0.0679 to 0.0477 (30% improvement) and RMSE from 0.1748 to 0.1523, while additionally reconstructing surface normals (MAE 0.1147) which NeTF cannot produce. DLCT [50], the only other method that outputs normals, is substantially worse: depth MAE 0.3189 vs. 0.0477 and normal MAE 0.3796 vs. 0.1147 at 5% threshold, indicating DLCT requires dense raster scanning to function properly. Among FFT-based methods, FK [22] (depth MAE 0.0719) and Phasor [25,26] (depth MAE 0.0818–0.1348) all trail the proposed method, and none can reconstruct normals. Gram [1] (depth MAE 0.0913) and LCT [32] (depth MAE 0.1977) are further behind. For non-planar relay wall and non-confocal settings (Fig. 5), FBP [44] produces noisy, distorted results while FK [22] yields blurry shapes with incorrect albedo; the proposed method delivers clean, accurate reconstructions in both scenarios, confirming its general applicability beyond the confocal planar-wall assumption shared by most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimization-based NLOS imaging lineage (Heide et al., Tsai et al., NeTF, Gram) by inheriting their general applicability to arbitrary relay walls and scanning patterns while addressing their dominant computational bottleneck: wasted computation on empty regions. The light propagation model with view-dependent cosine terms and distance fall-off is derived from DLCT's directional light-cone transform framework, but is reformulated as a superposition of point-wise propagation functions to enable variable-size inputs. The coarse-to-fine strategy borrows the progressive refinement philosophy from 3D reconstruction work (NeRF, PlenOctrees), and the matrix-free CUDA implementation departs from the fixed-size FFT-based solvers (LCT, FK, Phasor) that require dense raster scanning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20× speedup and sub-minute reconstruction of 128×128 volumes on a single commercial GPU make optimization-based NLOS imaging practically viable for real-world deployment, where dense scanning is often infeasible. The finding that 32×32 sparse sampling suffices for high-quality output challenges the field's reliance on exhaustive raster scanning. Open questions remain around handling self-occlusion, extending domain reduction to multi-object scenes, and further improving noise robustness, all of which the authors identify as future directions.
