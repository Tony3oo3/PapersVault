## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image generation methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) rely on a simple step-by-step reconstruction objective during diffusion training, which cannot directly enforce structural and visual consistency between the generated image and the reference images. This leads to unexpected changes in the object's visual details, color, and texture during the denoising process, limiting the fidelity of personalized generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240721/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4–6 reference images of a personalized subject (e.g., a pet, object, or character) and a free-form text prompt specifying desired pose, style, or background, and produces a 512×512 RGB image of that subject in the new context. Training operates entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion V1.4's autoencoder, and the lightweight reward model (0.26M parameters) also operates on latents, introducing negligible computational overhead relative to the 859.40M-parameter U-Net.

## Method

The paper reformulates personalized text-to-image generation as a deterministic policy gradient (DPG) problem, treating the diffusion U-Net as a policy that maps state {x_t, t, τ(y)} to an action (predicted noise ẑ_t), supervised by a learnable Q-function Q_φ that estimates cumulative reward. A key 'Look Forward' mechanism rewrites the reward in terms of the predicted clean latent x̂_{0,t} (recovered from x_t and ẑ_t via the diffusion schedule) rather than the one-step noise target, enabling the model to optimize for long-term visual consistency across timesteps. A second reward term uses DINO embedding similarity between the decoded generated image and reference images to capture unique subject-specific visual features. The Q-function is updated via a Bellman backup with discount rate γ, and the policy is updated by gradient ascent on Q_φ, with the final training objective combining the DINO reward (weighted by λ) and the original reconstruction reward.

## Results

On the DreamBooth benchmark, DreamBooth equipped with the proposed Look Forward reward achieves the best image-alignment scores of 0.738 (DINO) and 0.797 (CLIP-I), improving over the DreamBooth baseline (0.694 / 0.762) by 2.1% and 1.8% respectively, while maintaining comparable text-alignment (CLIP-T: 0.269 vs. 0.282). On the Custom benchmark, DreamBooth w/ Look Forward similarly leads with DINO 0.680 and CLIP-I 0.773 versus the DreamBooth baseline's 0.640 and 0.737. A user study confirms the advantage: 55.1% of participants preferred the proposed method for image fidelity versus 12.0% for DreamBooth, while text fidelity preferences were nearly tied (19.6% vs. 20.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: DreamBooth [34] (full-model fine-tuning) and Custom Diffusion [18] (cross-attention fine-tuning). Against DreamBooth, the proposed Look Forward reward yields a clear win on both image-alignment metrics (DINO +2.1%, CLIP-I +1.8%) at a small cost to text-alignment (CLIP-T drops from 0.282 to 0.269). The DINO reward variant also improves image-alignment (DINO +2.9%, CLIP-I +2.1%) with a similar slight CLIP-T decrease. Against Custom Diffusion, the proposed method wins on DINO (+0.069) and CLIP-I (+0.085) while matching or slightly exceeding its text-alignment (CLIP-T 0.322 vs. 0.321). The user study further validates the advantage: 55.1% of raters preferred the proposed method for image fidelity versus only 12.0% for DreamBooth, with text fidelity essentially tied (19.6% vs. 20.4%). The paper notes an inherent trade-off: higher visual fidelity (especially with larger DINO reward weight λ) can marginally degrade text-alignment, as shown in the ablation (CLIP-T drops from 0.239 to 0.211 when λ increases from 0 to 1 on the 'robot_toy' subset).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes personalized text-to-image generation (established by Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, and Custom Diffusion) from a step-by-step reconstruction objective into a deterministic policy gradient (DPG) optimization, treating the diffusion U-Net as a policy supervised by a learnable Q-function. It draws on the RL-for-T2I line of work (DPOK, DRaFT) that uses human-preference rewards for general generation, but departs by designing a personalized, learnable reward model suited to the few-shot (4–6 image) setting where training a separate reward model is infeasible. The 'Look Forward' mechanism is a novel repurposing of the diffusion process algebra to convert the one-step noise-prediction reward into a reward on the estimated final latent, enabling long-term structural consistency without explicit multi-step unrolling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DPG framework offers a practical, low-cost (0.26 M trainable parameters) plug-in that can be attached to existing personalization baselines (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) to improve visual fidelity without re-architecting the generator, and its flexibility to incorporate differential or non-differential rewards (e.g., DINO similarity) opens a path toward richer supervision signals. However, the paper acknowledges a persistent trade-off: stronger visual-fidelity rewards degrade text alignment, and the authors leave the design of text-alignment rewards as future work. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multi-concept personalization, whether the Q-function can be replaced by fixed (non-learnable) reward models, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the DreamBooth/Custom benchmarks to more diverse or larger reference sets.
