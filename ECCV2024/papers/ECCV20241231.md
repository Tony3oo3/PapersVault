## Problem

Sparse-view and monocular human performance capture is ill-posed due to self-occlusions and depth ambiguities, causing radiance fields to overfit when naively optimized on few views. While meta-learning has shown promise for learning neural field priors in static scenes, it fails for articulated humans because spatially-fixed hash-grid parameters map the same body point to entirely different table entries depending on pose, making the learned prior ineffective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241231/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During meta-learning, the method consumes multi-view video (up to ~80 calibrated cameras) with foreground masks and skeletal motion (joint rotations, root rotation/translation over a window of W frames). At inference, it fine-tunes on as few as 1–4 sparse in-the-wild RGB images with masks and motion, plus a fitted deformable template (DDC or SMPL) for space canonicalization. The output is a personalized SDF geometry and a hashgrid-parameterized appearance field that supports photoreal novel-view rendering, with fine-tuning converging in roughly 40 seconds (50 ms per step).

## Method

METACAP represents the clothed human as an SDF and appearance field encoded by a multi-resolution hashgrid, volume-rendered with unbiased alpha from the SDF. The key insight is to meta-learn the hashgrid weights in a pose-canonicalized space: sampled points are projected onto the nearest triangle of a posed deformable template, the per-vertex transformation is barycentrically interpolated, and the inverse is applied to map points into a canonical frame before hash encoding, so the same body region always queries the same hash entries regardless of articulation. Meta-learning uses Reptile (first-order MAML) with an inner loop of 24 gradient steps on randomly sampled frames and views, supervised by a combined loss of Huber color, Eikonal, binary-cross-entropy mask, and sparseness regularization. An occlusion-handling module pre-renders virtual proxy views from a coarsely fitted field and uses per-vertex visibility maps to inject additional rays into heavily occluded regions during monocular fine-tuning. The approach is agnostic to the specific template (SMPL or DDC) and supports arbitrary camera counts in both the meta-learning and fine-tuning stages.

## Results

On the DynaCap benchmark with 4 wide-baseline cameras, METACAP achieves state-of-the-art results across all appearance and geometry metrics. For subject S2, it reaches 26.53 dB PSNR, 0.841 SSIM, 0.249 LPIPS, and 0.679 Chamfer distance, outperforming the closest competitor ARAH (26.28 dB PSNR, 0.839 Chamfer). On the challenging loose-clothing subject S5, METACAP achieves 23.996 dB PSNR and 1.133 Chamfer distance, compared to ARAH's 23.532 dB and 1.620. On the in-the-wild WILDDYNACAP dataset, METACAP also outperforms ARAH on PSNR (19.156 vs 19.027 for S2; 22.080 vs 22.075 for S27) and LPIPS (0.351 vs 0.412; 0.260 vs 0.343).

## Baselines & Comparisons

METACAP outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across all three subjects. Against the closest competitor ARAH, METACAP improves PSNR by 0.25–0.62 dB, reduces Chamfer distance by 0.16–0.49, and increases IOU by 0.03–0.04, while also being image-only (ARAH requires 4D scans for meta-learning and videos for fine-tuning) and ~10× faster to fine-tune (minutes vs. 10+ hours on 4 GPUs). Compared to TransHuman, METACAP gains 0.7–1.4 dB PSNR and reduces Chamfer by 0.76–0.77, largely because TransHuman does not model geometry explicitly. DVA and the generalizable methods (DeepMultiCap, DiffStereo) lag further behind: DVA's texture un-projection is sensitive to template tracking errors, while DeepMultiCap and DiffStereo struggle with the wide-baseline 4-camera setup and only report geometry (no appearance metrics). Notably, METACAP is the only method that handles loose clothing (subject S5) with a deformable template, whereas all baselines rely on parametric body models (SMPL/SMPL-X) that cannot represent such garments.

## Relation to Prior Work

METACAP extends the meta-learning-for-implicit-representations line (MetaSDF, Tancik et al., MetaAvatar, ARAH) by shifting the meta-learned prior from MLP weights to multi-resolution hash-grid parameters and, critically, by meta-learning from images rather than 3D scans. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of pose-canonicalized space for the hash-grid encoding: because articulated humans map the same body point to entirely different spatial locations across poses, learning hash-grid parameters in world space is ineffective, so the authors warp sampled points into a template-defined canonical space before encoding. This hybrid explicit-template + implicit-field design, combined with Reptile-based optimization and an occlusion-handling strategy, distinguishes METACAP from ARAH (which uses a purely MLP-based SDF prior from scans and takes hours to fine-tune) and from generalizable radiance-field methods that lack per-subject fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

METACAP makes high-fidelity personalized human capture practical by reducing fine-tuning from hours to roughly 40 seconds and supporting as few as one input camera, which is a significant step toward deploying performance capture outside expensive studio domes. The WILDDYNACAP dataset fills a gap by providing paired dense-dome and in-the-wild sparse captures for robustness evaluation. Open questions remain: the method is sensitive to template fitting and motion-capture quality, ignores temporal coherence, and struggles with hands; the authors also note that cross-individual prior learning and real-time fine-tuning are future directions. Whether the space-canonicalization trick transfers to other articulated or deformable objects beyond humans is an interesting open question.
