## Problem

Existing multi-view facial capture systems like TEMPEH are strictly bound to the camera array used during training and fail catastrophically when applied to a different setup, requiring expensive re-collection and re-registration of data for each new array. Additionally, these data-driven methods are sensitive to common training-data noise such as inaccurate automatic mesh registration and scan artifacts, particularly on the scalp and neck regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241260/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 synchronized RGB images (padded and resized to 600×600) along with their calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, plus foreground masks extracted via PP-Matting. It outputs per-vertex 3D positions of a FLAME mesh in a fixed topology, predicted in two stages: a global 32³ voxel grid for coarse placement followed by a local 8³ grid (2 mm voxel edge) for per-vertex refinement. The visual hull used for initialization is computed on a 160³ grid with 5 mm voxel edges.

## Method

GRAPE replaces TEMPEH's learned head localizer with a projection-based visual hull computed from multi-view foreground masks, yielding a camera-array-agnostic bounding box and enabling visibility-aware 3D feature aggregation: each voxel's 2D image features are fused only from views where the voxel depth is within a margin ρ of the rendered visual-hull depth, preventing contamination from occluded or opposite-side views. The coarse-to-fine prediction uses two 3D UNet-style networks (global and local stages) sharing TEMPEH's architecture. For robustness to noisy supervision, an 'update-by-disagreement' strategy trains two identically-architected networks per stage and discards a vertex or scan point's gradient when both networks agree with each other but disagree with the ground-truth label, effectively filtering registration errors and scan outliers without manual curation.

## Results

On FaMoS, GRAPE (local) achieves 0.75 mm median / 0.81 mm mean point-to-surface distance on the complete head, matching TEMPEH (0.79 / 0.85 mm). On FaceScape, GRAPE achieves 0.76 mm median / 0.80 mm mean on the face region, dramatically outperforming TEMPEH (2.88 / 3.10 mm) and the registration baseline (1.29 / 1.37 mm). For cross-camera-array generalization (FaMoS→FaceScape, no fine-tuning), GRAPE achieves 1.92 mm median face error versus 32.49 mm for TEMPEH, and 1.70 mm (FaceScape→FaMoS) versus 25.63 mm for TEMPEH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TEMPEH (the primary multi-view baseline), GRAPE achieves comparable accuracy on FaMoS (0.75 vs. 0.79 mm median on the complete head) but dramatically outperforms it on FaceScape (0.76 vs. 2.88 mm median on the face region), where TEMPEH suffers from scan noise and inaccurate registration. The generalization gap is even more striking: when trained on FaMoS and tested directly on FaceScape without fine-tuning, GRAPE achieves 1.92 mm median face error while TEMPEH fails catastrophically at 32.49 mm; the reverse direction (FaceScape→FaMoS) shows 1.70 mm vs. 25.63 mm. Against DECA (a single-image 3DMM-based method), both multi-view methods substantially outperform it on all regions (e.g., GRAPE 0.76 mm vs. DECA 5.88 mm on FaceScape face region), confirming the advantage of multi-view geometry over monocular reconstruction for high-fidelity capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAPE directly extends TEMPEH (Bolkart et al., CVPR 2023), the state-of-the-art data-driven multi-view head capture system, by replacing its learned head localizer with a projection-based visual hull and introducing visibility-aware 3D feature aggregation to produce a camera-array-agnostic feature cube. The 'update-by-disagreement' training strategy adapts the small-loss filtering and consensus-based update paradigms from noisy-label classification (Co-teaching, Decoupling, Co-teaching+) to the regression setting of vertex and point-to-surface supervision. The coarse-to-fine 3D UNet architecture and FLAME-based mesh parameterization follow the ToFu/TEMPEH lineage, while the geometric initialization departs from the fully data-driven philosophy of those predecessors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for production pipelines in film and games: deploying a new camera array no longer requires re-collecting hundreds of thousands of frames and performing expensive mesh tracking, as TEMPEH demands. The finding that a simple geometric prior (visual hull) dramatically outperforms a learned localizer for cross-array generalization challenges the assumption that learned components are always preferable when domain shift is a concern. However, the method remains limited to the FLAME topology and still struggles with truly out-of-domain expressions, and generalization is validated on only two datasets with a single camera-array pair in each direction, leaving open whether the approach scales to more diverse capture setups or mesh topologies.
