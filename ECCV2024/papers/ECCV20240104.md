## Problem

Existing MLLMs, despite strong logical reasoning, cannot foresee future events or predict object movements from current visual observations, even when provided with multi-frame sequences. This gap stems from the difficulty of extracting spatiotemporal dynamics from redundant visual information, leaving MLLMs unable to model how subjects will behave next.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240104/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Merlin accepts single images or multi-frame video clips (resized to 448×448, yielding 1024 visual tokens per frame before 2D-convolution aggregation to 256 tokens) alongside text prompts and optional spatial coordinates or tracklets as initial subject observations. The initial observation can be a location description, appearance description, or action description of a target in the first frame. Outputs include structured per-frame bounding-box trajectories interleaved with frame indicators and ID tokens, natural-language future event predictions, and standard VQA answers.

## Method

Merlin pairs a CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder with a 3×3 stride-2 2D-convolution projector (chosen over 1D linear or cross-attention for spatial token clustering and convergence stability) feeding into a Vicuna-7B decoder. Training proceeds in two stages: Foresight Pre-Training (FPT) causally models trajectories interleaved with multi-frame images, requiring the model to predict an entire subject trajectory from a single initial observation, trained jointly with detection, tracking, grounding, and image-text data in a unified multi-task format; Foresight Instruction-Tuning (FIT) then appends a deduced future observation after the predicted trajectory, effectively using the trajectory as a Chain-of-Thought bridge between spatial perception and predictive reasoning. The key insight is that trajectory—a highly structured, low-redundancy representation—serves as an ideal learning objective to link past and future temporal contexts, enabling the LLM to internalize spatiotemporal dynamics without the intractable burden of next-frame reconstruction.

## Results

On the prediction reasoning benchmark (MMBench sub-tasks), Merlin (7B) achieves 64.4 average on the development set and 66.5 on the test set, outperforming the strongest baseline LLaVA-1.5 (59.6 dev) by ~5 points and Kosmos-2 (58.2 test) by ~8 points. On object tracking, Merlin reaches 51.4 AO on GOT-10K and 39.8 Success on LaSOT, substantially exceeding LLaVA-1.5 (23.5 AO / 19.4 Success) while approaching specialist trackers like SiamFC++ (59.5 AO). On general visual understanding, Merlin scores 66.2/65.5 on MMBench dev/test and 34.9 on MM-Vet, surpassing LLaVA-1.5 (64.3/59.5 and 30.5). On the POPE hallucination benchmark, Merlin achieves 91.58% accuracy (Random), 89.53% (Popular), and 84.10% (Adversarial), leading all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary prediction reasoning benchmark, Merlin (64.4 dev / 66.5 test) outperforms all 11 compared MLLMs, with the largest margins over LLaVA-1.5 (+4.8 dev) and Kosmos-2 (+8.3 test); it leads on 8 of 10 sub-metrics on dev and all 5 on test. On object tracking (GOT-10K), Merlin's 51.4 AO more than doubles LLaVA-1.5's 23.5 AO, though it remains ~8 points below the specialist tracker SiamFC++ (59.5 AO), indicating that trajectory-based pre-training enables competitive temporal reasoning without dedicated tracking architecture. On general visual understanding, Merlin surpasses LLaVA-1.5 on MMBench (66.2 vs 64.3 dev; 65.5 vs 59.5 test) and MM-Vet (34.9 vs 30.5) but trails slightly on GQA (60.5 vs 62.0), suggesting foresight training enhances holistic comprehension without degrading standard VQA. On the POPE hallucination evaluation, Merlin achieves the best accuracy in all three scenarios (Random 91.58%, Popular 89.53%, Adversarial 84.10%), exceeding the prior best Shikra by up to 5 points, which the authors attribute to trajectory-driven attention reducing misidentification. Overall, Merlin consistently wins or ties on the majority of reported metrics across all four evaluation axes (prediction reasoning, tracking, general understanding, hallucination), with the only notable losses being to specialist trackers on GOT-10K and a marginal GQA gap versus LLaVA-1.5.

## Relation to Prior Work

Merlin follows the standard MLLM architecture lineage (CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder + Vicuna-7B decoder + alignment projector) established by LLaVA, BLIP-2, and Flamingo, but departs in two key ways: it replaces the typical MLP or cross-attention projector with a 2D convolution for token compression, and it introduces trajectory prediction as the core pre-training objective rather than next-token or next-frame modeling. The two-stage Foresight Pre-Training (FPT) and Foresight Instruction-Tuning (FIT) paradigm mirrors the LLM pre-training + instruction-tuning recipe, but repurposes tracking and detection datasets (LaSOT, GOT-10K, Object365) as structured supervision for spatiotemporal reasoning. The 'Trajectory Chain-of-Thought' concept—using predicted bounding-box sequences as an explicit intermediate reasoning step before future-event prediction—is a novel bridge between the visual grounding literature (Shikra, ChatSpot) and the Chain-of-Thought prompting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Merlin is the first MLLM to natively perform object tracking and multi-frame identity association, opening a path toward MLLMs that reason over temporal dynamics rather than static single images. The incidental finding that trajectory-based foresight learning substantially reduces object hallucination (POPE scores near 50% 'yes' rate) suggests that structured spatiotemporal supervision may be a general-purpose remedy for the hallucination problem, a direction worth further investigation. Key limitations include handling only short video clips (a few frames), reliance on GPT-4 to generate future-event annotations, and evaluation on a relatively small future-reasoning benchmark; scaling to longer sequences and more diverse prediction scenarios remains open.
