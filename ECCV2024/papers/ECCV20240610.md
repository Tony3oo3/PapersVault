## Problem

SiamMAE achieves state-of-the-art video propagation performance by learning object-centric representations from motion in video frames, but it is restricted to video datasets (which are smaller and more expensive to decode than image datasets) and requires 2,000 epochs of training. This paper asks whether explicit video motion is truly necessary for learning such representations, or whether the implicit spatial transformations between two views of the same image suffice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (or a single video frame) and produces two augmented views via random cropping and optional horizontal flip; the second view is then masked at 98.5%, leaving only 2 visible patches out of 196 for a ViT/16. After pre-training, the decoder is discarded and the Siamese ViT encoder is used as a frozen or fine-tuned feature extractor for downstream propagation tasks (video object segmentation, pose propagation, semantic part propagation). The key unusual aspect is that the reconstruction target is always a spatial subset of the reference view, making the task directly tractable without any prior object knowledge.

## Method

CropMAE uses a Siamese ViT encoder to process two views of the same image: an unmasked global view V1 and a heavily masked local view V2 (created by a Global-to-Local random crop strategy, where V2 is a crop within V1). A 4-layer Transformer decoder with alternating self-attention and cross-attention layers reconstructs V2 from the 2 visible patches of V2 and the full tokens of V1, trained with L2 loss on normalized pixel values. The critical design choice is the extreme 98.5% masking ratio (vs. 95% in SiamMAE), which is optimal because the pretext task—locating and reconstructing a local crop from a global reference—is directly solvable from the visible patches without learning conceptual object representations. This tractability eliminates the need for the model to 'hallucinate' unseen object parts, enabling convergence in ~150 epochs on ImageNet versus ~350 for SiamMAE on K400, while also allowing a 23.8× speedup by training on standalone images instead of video pairs.

## Results

CropMAE with a ViT-S/16 encoder trained for 400 epochs on an ImageNet Subset achieves 60.4 J&Fm on DAVIS-2017, 33.3 mIoU on VIP, and 72.0 PCK@0.2 on JHMDB, outperforming the re-implemented SiamMAE (57.9 J&Fm, 33.2 mIoU, 74.0 PCK@0.2) under the same 400-epoch budget on DAVIS and VIP. Scaling to ViT-B/16 yields 60.9 J&Fm on DAVIS. The method uses an extreme 98.5% masking ratio (only 2 visible patches out of 196), and trains approximately 30% faster than SiamMAE on video frames and 23.8× faster when pre-training on still images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under a fair 400-epoch comparison, CropMAE (ViT-S/16, ImageNet Subset) outperforms the re-implemented SiamMAE (ViT-S/16, K400) by +2.5 J&Fm on DAVIS-2017 (60.4 vs. 57.9) and +0.1 mIoU on VIP (33.3 vs. 33.2), but underperforms by −2.5 PCK@0.1 and −2.0 PCK@0.2 on JHMDB (43.6/72.0 vs. 46.1/74.0), which the authors attribute to SiamMAE's use of two different video frames capturing complex pose changes. Against MAE-ST (ViT-L/16, 800 epochs), CropMAE achieves +5.8 J&Fm on DAVIS with a smaller backbone and fewer epochs. Compared to MAE (ViT-B/16, 1600 epochs on full ImageNet), CropMAE gains +6.9 J&Fm on DAVIS and +5.2 mIoU on VIP while training 4× fewer epochs. VideoMAE (ViT-S/16, 800 epochs) is substantially outperformed across all three tasks (e.g., +21.1 J&Fm on DAVIS). The original SiamMAE at 2000 epochs still leads on all metrics (62.0 J&Fm, 37.3 mIoU, 76.1 PCK@0.2), but CropMAE closes most of that gap at only 20% of the training budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

CropMAE directly extends the Siamese Masked Autoencoder (SiamMAE) paradigm by replacing its video-frame-pair input with two differently-cropped views of a single image, thereby decoupling the siamese reconstruction objective from temporal motion. It inherits the asymmetric masking and cross-attention decoder architecture from SiamMAE and the patch-level masking framework from MAE/VideoMAE, but introduces a fundamentally different pretext task: reconstructing a local crop from a global view at an extreme 98.5% masking ratio. The method also draws on contrastive learning's random-crop augmentation strategies, repurposing them not for invariance learning but for creating spatially tractable reconstruction targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: eliminating video decoding yields a 23.8× training speedup while matching or exceeding SiamMAE's performance on segmentation tasks, making high-quality pre-training accessible without large video corpora. The conceptual finding that object-boundary representations emerge from spatial transformations alone (without explicit motion) challenges SiamMAE's core narrative, though the weaker JHMDB results suggest motion still matters for pose-specific propagation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger ViT backbones and datasets, and what unique inductive biases video frames provide that still images cannot replicate at scale.
