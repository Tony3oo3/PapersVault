## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D texture generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: autoregressive multi-view inpainting approaches (TEXTure, Text2Tex) produce visible seams due to error accumulation, while score distillation and latent-space methods (TexFusion, ProlificDreamer) over-smooth textures to enforce view consistency. No prior method simultaneously achieves seamless view consistency and rich high-frequency texture detail.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed-geometry 3D mesh and a free-form text prompt as input, and produces a UV texture map in RGB space as output. The mesh is rendered from 8 azimuthally uniform viewpoints (elevation 0°) plus one top view, with UV coordinates computed via Xatlas. The texture map is iteratively refined across 40 DDIM denoising steps, with a complete RGB texture assembled at each step. A depth-aware ControlNet serves as the T2I backbone, conditioning generation on rendered depth maps.

## Method

TexGen interleaves texture assembly with diffusion denoising in RGB space, maintaining a time-dependent UV texture map that is progressively updated at each of T denoising steps. At each step, an Attention-Guided View Sampling (AGVS) module sequentially generates denoised observations for all views, using blended latent diffusion to inpaint unobserved regions and propagating Key/Value features from a reference view to other views for cross-view appearance consistency. A Text&Texture-Guided Resampling (T²GR) module then computes the noise for the next denoising step via a multi-conditioned classifier-free guidance formulation that fuses text-guided noise (preserving high-frequency details) with texture-conditioned noise (preserving view consistency), where the texture weight ω₂ decays linearly from ω to 0 over the denoising schedule. This eliminates the need for MLP post-processing or latent-space decoding that causes over-smoothness in prior methods.

## Results

On a collection of 45 meshes from Objaverse and ThreeDScans, TexGen achieves FID 84.65, KID×10⁻³ 4.27, and CLIPScore 22.83, outperforming all baselines by at least 10.4% in FID and 39.0% in KID. In a user study with 2,480 responses from 62 participants, TexGen is preferred over each baseline in 64.72%–71.46% of pairwise comparisons. The method also supports text-driven texture editing while preserving original identity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexGen outperforms all five baselines on every quantitative metric. Against the multi-view inpainting methods TEXTure (FID 99.06) and Text2Tex (FID 109.94), TexGen reduces FID by 14.5% and 22.9% respectively, addressing their prominent seam artifacts from autoregressive view assembly. Against the score distillation methods Fantasia3D (FID 108.58) and ProlificDreamer (FID 94.51), TexGen achieves 22.0% and 10.4% FID improvements, eliminating the over-saturation, blurry edges, and color artifacts characteristic of SDS/VSD pipelines. The closest competitor is ProlificDreamer, which TexGen surpasses by 39.0% in KID and 1.58 points in CLIPScore. TexFusion is compared only qualitatively (no public implementation); TexGen avoids its over-smoothed textures caused by the MLP post-processing step while maintaining comparable view consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

TexGen extends the interlaced multi-view sampling paradigm introduced by TexFusion, but departs fundamentally by maintaining the texture map in RGB space rather than latent space, thereby eliminating the need for an MLP post-processing step that caused over-smoothed textures. It incorporates the blended latent diffusion technique for mask-based fusion of observed and unobserved regions, and adapts the Key/Value attention propagation from MASACTRL to enforce cross-view appearance consistency. The method also builds on the multi-conditioned classifier-free guidance framework, extending it to jointly condition on both text prompts and the evolving texture map for noise resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TexGen addresses two persistent failure modes in text-driven 3D texture generation—view inconsistency (seams) and detail loss (over-smoothness)—within a single unified sampling loop, offering a practical pipeline that avoids lengthy score-distillation optimization. The T²GR formulation, which linearly interpolates the texture-conditioned guidance weight from full to zero across denoising steps, provides a principled mechanism for balancing 3D consistency against 2D detail richness. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge a quality gap relative to 2D image generation, the lack of material/lighting disentanglement, and failure under significant semantic mismatch between prompt and geometry, all of which limit direct deployment in production 3D pipelines.
