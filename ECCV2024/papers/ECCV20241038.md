## Problem

Prior open-vocabulary video classification methods handle only a single label per video, whereas real videos simultaneously contain multiple heterogeneous concepts (objects, actions, scenes). Two specific obstacles make multi-label open-vocabulary video classification difficult: VLM similarity scores for different concept types occupy incompatible ranges (preventing a single threshold), and adapting image-pretrained VLMs to video easily overfits, destroying zero-shot generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method accepts a video clip and an arbitrary user-specified set of class labels (the open vocabulary), which can be expanded post-training without retraining. Label embeddings are pre-computed via the LLM-prompted text encoder and stored in a database; the video encoder then produces a single unit-norm embedding that is matched against all stored label embeddings via inner product, yielding one similarity score per label. A single global threshold (calibrated on training data) converts these scores into binary multi-label predictions, and the paper demonstrates that this threshold generalizes across concept types (entities vs. actions) unlike frozen CLIP baselines.

## Method

The label encoder couples a frozen LLM with a frozen CLIP text encoder through two learnable components: N learnable prefix vectors that steer the LLM to generate 'soft attributes' (continuous token sequences) for each class, and a prompt transformer that maps these LLM output representations directly into CLIP's input embedding space, bypassing non-differentiable detokenization/tokenization so the entire text pipeline is end-to-end trainable. On the vision side, a parallel temporal branch of divided space-time attention blocks (spatial attention initialized from CLIP, temporal attention randomly initialized) is appended to the last T layers of the frozen CLIP image encoder, with a temporal token aggregating CLS tokens across frames. To prevent the temporal branch from degrading zero-shot performance, a stochastic weight regularization penalty matches the branch's spatial-layer predictions against a random convex combination of finetuned and original CLIP weights. Training uses a weighted binary cross-entropy loss over sampled positive and negative video-label pairs with a temperature-scaled sigmoid, jointly optimizing the label encoder and temporal branch.

## Results

The full method (CLIP + Learnable LLM Prompts + Temporal Modeling + Synthetic Labels) achieves AUPR of 16.7 on YouTube-8M, 43.2 on Kinetics-400, 65.5 on TAO (entities), 50.2 on ActivityNet (actions), and 13.2 on RareAct (entities+actions). Peak F1 scores reach 32.7, 46.6, 56.6, 53.8, and 25.1 respectively. The method outperforms the strongest frozen baseline (CLIP with Fixed LLM Prompts) by 9.8 AUPR on YouTube-8M and 15.3 on TAO, and the strongest trainable baseline (DualCoOp*) by 8.4 AUPR on YouTube-8M and 18.4 on TAO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's full method outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and both metrics (AUPR and Peak F1). Against the strongest frozen baseline (CLIP with Fixed LLM Prompts, [27]), the method gains 9.8 AUPR on YouTube-8M (16.7 vs 6.9), 15.3 on TAO (65.5 vs 50.2), and 3.4 on ActivityNet (50.2 vs 46.8). Against the strongest trainable baseline (DualCoOp*, [40]), gains are even larger: 8.4 AUPR on YouTube-8M, 18.4 on TAO, and 17.2 on ActivityNet. ViFi-CLIP* ([33]) performs well on TAO entities (58.3 AUPR) but collapses on actions (17.2) and closed-vocabulary sets, while CoOp* ([52]) is the weakest trainable baseline overall. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that the learnable LLM prompting + prompt transformer + CLIP text encoder combination is essential: removing the CLIP text encoder drops TAO AUPR from 65.5 to 25.2, and removing the LLM drops it to 60.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: LLM-guided prompting for VLMs (Menon & Vondrick, CuPL, WaffleCLIP), temporal modeling for CLIP-based video understanding (STAN, TimeSformer), and multi-label classification with vision-language models (DualCoOp). It departs from prior open-vocabulary video classification work (ViFi-CLIP, Open-VCLIP, Vita-CLIP) by addressing the multi-label setting, where the paper identifies that VLM similarity scores for different concept types (actions vs. objects) occupy incompatible ranges, breaking the single-threshold assumption. The key architectural departure is the prompt transformer that connects frozen LLM output representations directly to the frozen CLIP text encoder without detokenization, enabling end-to-end gradient flow through the LLM's semantic space—a mechanism absent from prior LLM+CLIP prompting pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper defines the first benchmark for open-vocabulary multi-label video classification across five datasets spanning entities, actions, and unusual object-action combinations, filling a gap where prior work addressed only single-label video or multi-label image settings. The score calibration finding—that a single well-chosen threshold can work across heterogeneous concept types after end-to-end training, whereas frozen CLIP+LLM cannot—is practically important for deploying multi-concept video classifiers as black boxes. Limitations include reliance on a frozen CLIP image encoder (no architectural changes to the vision backbone), a frozen LLM, and training data (YT-8M + K400) that is orders of magnitude smaller than image pretraining corpora, raising questions about scalability to longer videos, more diverse concept taxonomies, and whether the approach transfers to other VLM backbones beyond CLIP.
