## Problem

Existing pathology visual-language pretraining methods (e.g., PLIP, QuiltNet) rely on simple contrastive learning over web-crawled image-caption pairs, but these captions are short, noisy, unstructured, and lack domain-specific pathology knowledge. The varying granularities of free text introduce alignment ambiguities that make models highly sensitive to text prompts at inference time, limiting zero-shot diagnostic performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241392/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 pathology image patches (or 256×256 patches extracted from gigapixel whole slide images) and free-form text captions or generated prompts (template + disease synonym, 100 random prompts per evaluation) as input, producing aligned visual-language embeddings. At inference, these embeddings support cross-modal retrieval (image↔caption, image↔disease label), zero-shot patch-level disease classification, and zero-shot WSI tumor subtyping via Top-K patch prediction aggregation. The structured knowledge input is the PathKT tree containing 50,470 attributes (synonyms, definitions, histological and cytological features) across 4,718 diseases in 32 tissues.

## Method

The approach has three stages. First, a pathology knowledge tree (PathKT) is constructed by expanding OncoTree with disease descriptions from textbooks and professional websites, linking entities via UMLS CUIs. Second, a PubMedBERT-based knowledge encoder is trained with the AdaSP metric learning loss to project each disease's attributes into a shared embedding space where intra-disease attributes cluster tightly while inter-disease attributes are separated—AdaSP is chosen over triplet loss to handle large intra-class variation across attribute types and small inter-class gaps between fine-grained subtypes. Third, in the KEP pretraining stage, the text encoder is initialized from the frozen knowledge encoder and trained jointly with a ViT-B-32/16 visual encoder (initialized from CLIP or BiomedCLIP, with an added projection head) using a combined loss: standard infoNCE image-text contrastive loss plus a text-knowledge contrastive loss that continuously distills the frozen knowledge encoder's representations into the learnable text encoder, thereby anchoring image-text alignment to structured disease entities.

## Results

On cross-modal retrieval (Table 1), KEP-32 pretrained on OpenPath improves image-to-text Recall@10 from 0.067 (PLIP) to 0.098 on Arch-PubMed and from 0.038 to 0.071 on PathPair; KEP-16 further boosts these to 0.163 and 0.108 respectively. On disease retrieval (Table 2), KEP-32 achieves Recall@10 of 0.693 (label-to-text) and 0.162 (image-to-label) on OpenPath, versus 0.408 and 0.114 for PLIP. On zero-shot WSI tumor subtyping (Table 3), KEP-CTP reaches 0.443 Top-1 balanced accuracy on common BRCA subtypes, outperforming MI-Zero-Bio (0.390) despite MI-Zero using 550k+ in-house pathology reports for pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PLIP (Huang et al., Nature Medicine 2023) on OpenPath, KEP-32 wins on image-to-text retrieval across all three datasets (e.g., +3.1 pts R@10 on Arch-PubMed, +3.3 pts on PathPair) and on disease retrieval by a large margin (0.693 vs 0.408 R@10 for label-to-text). Against QuiltNet (Ikezogwo et al., NeurIPS 2024) on Quilt1M, KEP-32 improves text-to-image retrieval on Arch-book (0.275 vs 0.204 R@10) and PathPair (0.106 vs 0.071 R@10), while image-to-text gains are smaller but consistent. Against MI-Zero (Lu et al., CVPR 2023) on WSI tumor subtyping, KEP-CTP outperforms MI-Zero-Bio on common BRCA (0.443 vs 0.390 Top-1) and matches MI-Zero-Pub on rare BRCA (0.282 vs 0.282), despite MI-Zero leveraging 550k+ in-house pathology reports that KEP does not use. On zero-shot patch classification (Fig. 5), KEP variants consistently outperform both PLIP and QuiltNet across 7–8 of 8 datasets, with the largest gain of +0.137 wF1 on BACH, and show notably tighter prompt-sensitivity distributions (shorter box plots).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pathology visual-language pretraining line initiated by PLIP (Huang et al., 2023) and Quilt1M (Ikezogwo et al., 2024), which both apply standard CLIP-style contrastive learning to web-crawled image-caption pairs. The key departure is the introduction of a structured pathology knowledge tree (PathKT) and a dedicated knowledge encoder trained via AdaSP metric learning, which is then used to initialize and continuously distill domain knowledge into the text encoder during VLP. This contrasts with prior medical knowledge-enhanced learning (e.g., Zhang et al., 2023 on chest X-rays) that either designs model architectures around expert rules or feeds knowledge as auxiliary input, and with prior pathology VLP that treats captions as unstructured free text without leveraging the implicit disease taxonomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PathKT knowledge tree (50,470 attributes across 4,718 diseases in 32 tissues) is, to the authors' knowledge, the first comprehensive structured pathology knowledge base, making it a potentially reusable resource for the computational pathology community. The finding that a smaller, higher-quality dataset (OpenPath) can outperform a 5× larger one (Quilt1M) on disease retrieval suggests that caption quality and structured knowledge alignment matter more than raw data volume for pathology VLP. Open questions include whether the knowledge tree can be automatically maintained as new diseases and subtypes are discovered, whether the approach generalizes to other medical imaging domains (radiology, ophthalmology), and whether the frozen knowledge distillation branch can be replaced by end-to-end joint training without losing the alignment benefits.
