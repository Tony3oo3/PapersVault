## Problem

Despite widespread empirical success of GNNs, the influence of topology awareness on generalization performance—especially across structural subgroups in semi-supervised node classification—remains unexplored. The prevailing belief that increasing topology awareness is universally beneficial lacks theoretical grounding, and no unified framework exists to characterize topology awareness with respect to arbitrary structural features.

## Input / Output

The framework operates on a fixed undirected graph with node feature vectors and a small labeled subset, parameterized by a user-specified structural distance function (e.g., shortest-path distance) that must form a metric space over the vertex set. The key intermediate quantity is the distortion α of the GNN embedding with respect to this structural metric, which quantifies topology awareness. Beyond standard node predictions, the framework yields a generalization risk bound of the form O(α · D_s(V_i, V_0)) for any structural subgroup, and in the active learning application, outputs an initial labeled set selected to minimize the k-center objective over graph distance.

## Method

The paper introduces a structure-agnostic framework that characterizes GNN topology awareness via the distortion of the embedding map from the graph metric space to the Euclidean embedding space (Definition 3). Theorem 1 establishes that the generalization risk on any structural subgroup is bounded by the training loss plus O(α · D_s(V_i, V_0)), directly linking distortion (topology awareness) to generalization. Theorem 2 then shows that when two subgroups' structural distances to the training set differ by a factor of αδ, the GNN necessarily generalizes worse on the farther group, revealing that higher topology awareness amplifies accuracy disparities. The case study validates these results using shortest-path distance on GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, and GCNII across five benchmark datasets, confirming near-linear graph-to-embedding distance preservation and monotonic accuracy decay with hop distance. For the cold-start problem in graph active learning, the framework motivates a coverage-based sampling strategy formulated as a k-center problem and solved via farthest-first traversal, which outperforms random, degree, centrality, and PageRank baselines in both accuracy and maximum discrepancy across structural groups.

## Results

The proposed coverage-based sampling (OURS) achieves the best test accuracy and lowest maximum discrepancy (MD) across all 16 dataset–GNN combinations in the cold-start active learning experiment. For example, on Cora with GCN, OURS reaches 49.03% ACC with 8.23 MD, outperforming the best baseline PageRank (47.87% ACC, 9.52 MD) and random sampling (37.08% ACC, 13.23 MD). On Coauthor CS with GCN, the gap widens: OURS achieves 61.75% ACC vs. 55.50% for PageRank. The theoretical validation (Table 1) confirms that train/test pairs with smaller structural distance D_spd yield higher accuracy (e.g., 71.40% for Group 3 vs. 65.42% for Group 1 on Cora/GCN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its coverage-based sampling (solving the k-center problem via farthest-first traversal) against four structure-based baselines: uniform random sampling (R) [30], degree-based sampling [15], centrality-based sampling, and PageRank-based sampling [5]. OURS wins on both accuracy and fairness (MD) across all 16 dataset–GNN combinations tested (Cora, CiteSeer, CoraFull, Coauthor CS × GCN, Sage, GAT, GCNII). The largest accuracy gains appear on CiteSeer with GCN (+8.71 pp over PageRank, 50.98 vs. 42.27) and CoraFull with GCN (+2.77 pp, 15.25 vs. 12.48), where the graph structure is sparser and random/degree-based selection is most ineffective. On fairness, OURS consistently reduces MD by 1–5 points relative to the best baseline (e.g., 8.23 vs. 9.52 on Cora/GCN; 1.23 vs. 2.10 on CoraFull/GCN), confirming that minimizing structural distance to the training set simultaneously improves accuracy and reduces subgroup disparity. Random sampling (R) is the weakest baseline in every setting, with MD values up to 16.27 (Coauthor CS/GCN), highlighting the importance of structure-aware initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structural subgroup generalization framework of Ma et al. [25] by introducing a distortion-based (approximate metric embedding) characterization of GNN topology awareness, connecting it directly to generalization risk bounds. It departs from the dominant GNN literature focused on expressive power via the Weisfeiler-Lehman test, arguing that the question of *how much* structural information is preserved (and what that does to generalization) is distinct from *whether* structures can be distinguished. The cold-start application builds on existing graph active learning methods (AGE, FeatProp, GraphPart) but reframes initial set selection as a k-center problem solvable purely from graph structure, eliminating reliance on node features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely held assumption that increasing topology awareness universally benefits GNNs, showing theoretically and empirically that higher distortion sensitivity amplifies accuracy disparities across structural subgroups. This has direct implications for fairness-critical deployments (e.g., protein interaction prediction, social network analysis) and for practical design choices like training set composition. Key limitations include the restriction to the transductive setting and the lack of analysis into *why* certain GNN architectures achieve low distortion; extending the framework to inductive settings and understanding the internal dynamics that reduce distortion are important open questions.
