## Problem

Existing Tracking Any Point (TAP) methods either treat each point independently, ignoring inter-point correlations, or concatenate multiple heterogeneous features (flow vectors, embeddings, visibility, cost volume) into a blackbox vector fed to MLPs or Transformers, making the model conceptually opaque. Additionally, long-range temporal modeling remains difficult: optical-flow-based methods cannot handle occlusions, and sliding-window approaches suffer from feature drifting when content features are propagated across windows of varying length.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video (resized to 512×512 for training, 256×256 for evaluation) and a set of initial 2D point coordinates (700–800 points sampled per video during training on 24-frame synthetic clips). It processes frames in sliding windows of 8 with stride 4, and for each tracked point outputs a full trajectory: a per-frame (x, y) location sequence and a binary visibility sequence (0/1) across all T frames. Visibility is predicted only at the final decoder layer to avoid unstable intermediate supervision.

## Method

TAPTR reframes each tracking point as a DETR-style query consisting of a positional part (2D coordinate) and a content part (feature vector), refined iteratively over 6 decoder layers. Each decoder layer applies four operations: (1) cost-volume aggregation via grid sampling (RAFT-style) to inject local visual similarity, (2) 2D deformable cross-attention to sample multi-scale image features, (3) self-attention among all point queries in the frame with sinusoidal positional encoding at a low temperature to encourage local neighborhood attention, and (4) temporal self-attention along the window dimension for queries belonging to the same tracking point. Location is updated via sigmoid regression while content features use a residual update (concatenating the current and initial features) to preserve the reliable initial prior. Between windows, content features are propagated with a random-drop strategy during training and a dynamic gap schedule during inference to mitigate feature drifting, and cost volumes are recomputed only at window boundaries rather than per decoder layer, keeping the optimization target stable.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid-DAVIS benchmark, TAPTR achieves 63.0 AJ, 76.1 <δx_avg, and 91.1 OA, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art CoTracker2-All (60.7 AJ) by 2.3 AJ while running 1.3× faster (20.4 vs. 15.7 PPS). On DAVIS-S, TAPTR reaches 66.3 AJ and 91.0 OA, outperforming CoTracker2-Single (65.9 AJ) and BootsTAP (66.4 AJ, which uses 15M extra training clips). On RGB-Stacking, TAPTR achieves 60.8 AJ, a 5.3 AJ improvement over TAPIR (55.5 AJ), and on Kinetics it reaches 49.0 AJ with 85.2 OA, comparable to TAPIR (49.6 AJ, 85.0 OA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPTR outperforms CoTracker2-All (the prior SoTA tracking all points simultaneously) by 2.3 AJ on DAVIS (63.0 vs. 60.7) while being 1.3× faster in PPS (20.4 vs. 15.7). Against CoTracker2-Single, which tracks one point at a time for maximum accuracy, TAPTR still wins by 0.8 AJ (63.0 vs. 62.2) at 25× the speed (20.4 vs. 0.8 PPS). On DAVIS-S, TAPTR (66.3 AJ) edges out CoTracker2-Single (65.9) and is competitive with BootsTAP (66.4), which benefits from 15M additional training clips. On RGB-Stacking, TAPTR achieves 60.8 AJ, a 5.3 AJ gain over TAPIR (55.5) and 7.3 AJ over TAP-Net (53.5). On Kinetics, TAPTR (49.0 AJ, 85.2 OA) is slightly behind BootsTAP (54.7 AJ, 86.3 OA) but matches TAPIR (49.6 AJ, 85.0 OA) and outperforms CoTracker-Single (48.7 AJ, 86.5 OA) on AJ. Overall, TAPTR sets new state-of-the-art on DAVIS and DAVIS-S while maintaining the fastest inference speed among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAPTR reinterprets the Tracking Any Point (TAP) task through the lens of DETR-style object detection, where each tracking point becomes a query with a positional and content component refined iteratively across decoder layers. It builds directly on CoTracker's simultaneous multi-point Transformer tracking, RAFT's cost volume grid-sampling for local feature aggregation, and Deformable DETR's 2D deformable attention for cross-attention, but departs from prior TAP methods (TAPIR, CoTracker, PIPs) that concatenate heterogeneous features (flow vectors, visibility, cost volume) into a blackbox vector. The key conceptual shift is giving each point a clean, interpretable query structure rather than an opaque feature bundle, making the architecture's role at each layer transparent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAPTR demonstrates that a conceptually simple DETR-like framework can match or exceed the best TAP trackers while being substantially faster (20.4 PPS vs. 15.7 for CoTracker2-All; 25× faster than CoTracker2-Single), suggesting the detection-tracking analogy is more productive than previously exploited. The feature-drifting mitigation via random drop during training and dynamic gap during inference is a practical recipe for long-video tracking that generalizes beyond this specific architecture. Open questions remain: training relies entirely on synthetic Kubric data, the authors note that leveraging real-world detection/segmentation annotations for TAP is unexplored, and incorporating near-linear attention to further reduce cost is left as future work.
