## Problem

Latent Diffusion Models, while more efficient than pixel-space DPMs, still require many sequential forward passes through a large U-Net, making sampling slow and costly. Existing mixture-of-experts approaches either use fixed heuristic clustering and manual budget allocation (MEME, TMDA) or require training an expensive model zoo from scratch (OMS-DPM). This paper asks how to automatically prune a single pretrained LDM into a set of specialized, resource-efficient experts with end-to-end compute budget allocation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241619/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes random Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional ImageNet generation) and produces 256×256 RGB images. During sampling, the noise is passed sequentially through two specialized experts, each handling a distinct interval of the denoising path (e.g., timesteps 0–700 and 701–1000 for LSUN datasets). Each expert is a pruned sub-network of the original LDM U-Net with reduced depth and width, so the total compute per sample is the interval-weighted sum of the two experts' MACs.

## Method

DiffPruning operates in four stages: (1) it clusters denoising timesteps into intervals by computing pairwise cosine similarity of per-timestep gradient vectors of the denoising objective, revealing dataset-specific optimal splits; (2) it fine-tunes the pretrained LDM on each interval with elastic depth (randomly skipping layers) and elastic width (randomly dropping low-importance channels/heads), creating experts whose sub-networks are already well-adapted to their interval; (3) a lightweight Expert Routing Agent (ERA, ~0.5M params, GRU-based) simultaneously predicts continuous depth and width architecture vectors for all experts, optimized end-to-end via Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation and Straight-Through Estimators to balance denoising loss against a MACs budget regularization; (4) the selected sub-networks are hard-pruned and briefly fine-tuned. This avoids manual per-budget redesign and eliminates the need to train multiple architectures from scratch.

## Results

On LSUN-Bedroom, DiffPruning at 70%/50%/30% MACs budgets achieves FID scores of 5.90/6.73/9.22 (vs. 4.39 for the full LDM) while delivering 1.55×/1.87×/2.35× throughput speedups. On FFHQ, the 70% and 50% variants maintain near-parity FID (9.80 and 9.90 vs. 9.53) with 1.33× and 1.81× speedups. On LSUN-Church, DiffPruning 50% with DDIM-100 achieves 10.89 FID at 6.28 samples/sec, outperforming OMS-DPM (13.7 FID at 6.4 samples/sec) while requiring >7× fewer training iterations. On ImageNet, DiffPruning 50% reaches 8.45 FID with 1.75× throughput speedup over the full LDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pixel-space DDPM [18], DiffPruning 70% on LSUN-Bedroom achieves a lower FID (5.90 vs. 6.62) while using 3.5× fewer MACs (70.84G vs. 248.7G); on LSUN-Church, DiffPruning 70% (9.39 FID) also beats the full DDPM (10.58 FID) with ~17× fewer MACs. Against SP [9], DiffPruning 70% on LSUN-Bedroom improves FID from 18.6 to 5.90, and on ImageNet DiffPruning 50% (8.45 FID) outperforms SP (9.16 FID) at a comparable MACs budget (~54G vs. 52.7G). Against the full LDM [33], the 70% and 50% variants on FFHQ stay within 0.3–0.4 FID of the unpruned model (9.80/9.90 vs. 9.53) while gaining 33%–81% throughput. Against OMS-DPM [25] on LSUN-Church, DiffPruning 50% with DDIM-100 achieves 10.89 FID at 6.28 samples/sec versus OMS-DPM's 13.7 FID at 6.4 samples/sec, and DiffPruning 30% (11.39 FID) still beats OMS-DPM by 2.31 FID. Against ADM [8] on ImageNet, DiffPruning 70% (8.03 FID) trades 3.4 FID for a 15.6× reduction in MACs (76.24G vs. 1186.4G), illustrating the LDM's superior compute–quality frontier over pixel-space diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mixture-of-experts (MoE) diffusion model line initiated by eDiff-I, MEME, and TMDA, which partition denoising timesteps into intervals served by separate models. It departs from prior MoE methods in two key ways: (1) it replaces heuristic uniform or loss-based clustering with a data-driven scheme based on cosine similarity of per-timestep gradient alignment scores, and (2) it replaces manual per-interval architecture design (MEME, TMDA) or expensive model-zoo training from scratch (OMS-DPM) with elastic depth/width fine-tuning of a single pretrained LDM, creating an implicit model zoo within each expert. The differentiable architecture selection via Gumbel-Sigmoid and Straight-Through Estimator draws on neural architecture search techniques (Once-for-All, Gumbel-Softmax) but applies them jointly across multiple experts with a shared compute budget constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since LDMs underpin deployed systems like Stable Diffusion and DALL-E 3, reducing their inference cost without sacrificing quality has direct practical value; DiffPruning achieves up to 2.87× throughput speedup at 30% MACs with modest FID degradation across four datasets. The finding that optimal timestep clustering boundaries shift across datasets (e.g., [0,700] for LSUN vs. [0,400] for FFHQ) challenges the assumption that a single static partition generalizes, and the elastic fine-tuning strategy offers a cheaper alternative to OMS-DPM's model-zoo training. Limitations include the use of only two experts, restriction to U-Net LDMs at 256×256, and a linear denoising schedule assumption; open questions include whether the approach scales to transformer-based diffusion architectures (DiT), more than two experts, video generation, and non-linear schedules.
