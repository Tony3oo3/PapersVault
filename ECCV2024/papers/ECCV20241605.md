## Problem

Current vision-language datasets lack the fine-grained, coherent detail needed for rigorous model evaluation: COCO captions average ~10 words, DCI descriptions concatenate incoherent segment captions, and T2I test prompt sets lack ground-truth reference images. This makes it impossible to systematically probe model failures in spatial relations, counting, text rendering, and attribute binding against known visual targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241605/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For I2T, the input is a single RGB image (typically 2048×1536 px, landscape or portrait) and the output is a ~136-word, ~7-sentence compositional description covering objects, spatial relations, attributes, text rendering, and world knowledge. For T2I, the full long description serves as the prompt and the output is a generated image; the dataset's contrast sets—groups of near-identical images differing in orientation, count, or arrangement—enable testing whether models capture distinguishing details rather than generic scene content.

## Method

The core contribution is a 15k-image dataset with human-annotated descriptions produced via a three-stage pipeline: (1) extract key visual aspects and write short per-object descriptions, (2) consolidate into a coherent paragraph, and (3) enrich with colors, textures, and inter-element relationships, with background context provided to annotators when visual cues are insufficient. Images were intentionally curated by a single photographer to include contrast sets, 15 reoccurring entities, and targeted failure modes (spatial relations, counting, text rendering, mixed media). For I2T evaluation, PaLI 5B is finetuned on the 9,647-example DOCCI training split plus COCO, then compared zero-shot against InstructBLIP and LLaVA-1.5 7B using reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, CIDEr) and side-by-side human evaluation on precision (hallucinations) and recall (missing details). For T2I evaluation, Imagen, DALL-E 3, and SDXL generate images from DOCCI descriptions, assessed via FID, CMMD, FD_DINOv2, CLIPScore, and Davidsonian Scene Graph (DSG) metrics alongside human preference rankings, revealing that automatic metrics like FID and CLIPScore are unreliable for long descriptions while DSG aligns better with human judgment.

## Results

For I2T generation, PaLI 5B finetuned on DOCCI achieves BLEU@4 of 10.1, ROUGE-L of 29.1, METEOR of 17.9, and CIDEr of 16.0, substantially outperforming zero-shot InstructBLIP 7B (BLEU@4 3.5, CIDEr 5.9) and LLaVA-1.5 7B (BLEU@4 3.5, CIDEr 6.4) while generating longer descriptions (121.8 vs. ~85–90 words). For T2I generation, all three models (Imagen, SDXL, DALL-E 3) underperform random training image baselines on FID (28.13/23.69/32.37 vs. 13.71), and DALL-E 3 achieves the highest DSG_Human score (85.6) and user preference rank (1.42), while SDXL leads on FID (23.69) and CLIPScore (85.9). In SxS human evaluation, DOCCI descriptions are preferred over GPT-4v 89% of the time on recall, confirming the value of human-annotated detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaLI 5B finetuned on DOCCI decisively outperforms both InstructBLIP 7B and LLaVA-1.5 7B on all four reference-based I2T metrics (e.g., CIDEr 16.0 vs. 5.9 and 6.4; METEOR 17.9 vs. 10.6 and 11.3), despite being smaller and trained on only 9.6k examples. In SxS human evaluation, PaLI is preferred over LLaVA and InstructBLIP on both precision and recall, though it trails GPT-4v on precision (fewer hallucinations) while leading on recall (more spatial details and named entities). For T2I, DALL-E 3 achieves the best DSG_Human (85.6) and DSG_VQA (76.3) scores and the highest user preference rank (1.42), likely benefiting from its 4k-character context window versus Imagen's 128 tokens and SDXL's 77 tokens. SDXL leads on image quality metrics (FID 23.69, CMMD 0.823, FD_DINOv2 267.2) and CLIPScore (85.9), yet ranks last in human preference (2.38), highlighting the misalignment between automatic metrics and perceptual quality. All T2I models fall well short of the oracle test images (DSG_Human 91.7) and even random training images on FID, underscoring the difficulty of reconstructing detailed scenes from long descriptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOCCI carves new ground at the intersection of contrast-set evaluation [19] and dense image captioning, departing from prior datasets (COCO, DCI, Stanford Visual Paragraphs) that either lack fine-grained detail or rely on concatenated segment captions without coherent structure. The three-stage annotation protocol and the intentional curation of contrastive image groups (same objects in varied orientations, counts, and arrangements) represent a novel dataset-construction strategy not seen in prior vision-language resources. For evaluation, the paper repurposes the Davidsonian Scene Graph [11] as a text-image alignment metric, positioning it against CLIPScore and FID, and introduces a side-by-side human evaluation framework focused on precision (hallucination) and recall (detail) for long descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DOCCI is likely to become a standard benchmark for both I2T and T2I research because it provides ground-truth images paired with long, multi-challenge descriptions—something no prior prompt-only evaluation set (Parti Prompts, DrawBench) offers. The finding that a 5B model finetuned on just 9.6k high-quality descriptions outperforms 7B instruction-tuned models highlights the underexplored role of data quality and density in vision-language training. Open questions include developing reliable automatic metrics for long descriptions (current FID/CLIPScore metrics misalign with human judgment), extending T2I models to handle prompts beyond 77–128 tokens without information loss, and leveraging the contrastive structure for consistent character generation.
