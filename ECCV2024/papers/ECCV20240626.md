## Problem

Popular CLIP adaptation strategies (Adapters, Prompt Learning, Test-Time Prompt Tuning) substantially degrade the calibration of the zero-shot baseline under distributional drift, producing over-confident predictions that are particularly dangerous in safety-critical applications. This miscalibration issue has been overlooked in the CLIP adaptation literature, which has focused almost exclusively on improving discriminative accuracy. The paper further identifies the root cause as an increase in logit range (contrasting with prior work attributing miscalibration to logit norm in fully-supervised models).

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images paired with text class prompts, using a frozen CLIP backbone (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) to extract visual and textual embeddings. A key design choice is that the zero-shot prediction for each sample—specifically its minimum and maximum logit values—serves as a per-sample calibration reference, making the approach sample-adaptive and requiring no external validation set. The output is a set of calibrated class probabilities whose logit range is bounded by the corresponding zero-shot range, preserving the adapted model's discriminative direction while correcting its confidence magnitude.

## Method

The paper formalizes calibration as a constrained optimization problem (Eq. 3) that bounds each sample's adapted logits within the min–max range of its zero-shot logits, then proposes three practical approximations. ZS-Norm rescales the adapted logit vector to the zero-shot range before computing cross-entropy during training (Eq. 5). Penalty adds ReLU-based terms to the loss that activate only when logits exceed the zero-shot bounds, directly regularizing the range during backpropagation. SaLS applies the same per-sample logit rescaling at inference time as a post-hoc step, functioning as an unsupervised, sample-wise temperature scaling that requires no modification to the adaptation procedure. The theoretical contribution (Propositions 1–2) shows that increasing logit range—unlike increasing logit norm—directly inflates softmax confidence, explaining why adaptation degrades calibration even when logit norms decrease.

## Results

On the four ImageNet OOD benchmarks (V2, Sketch, A, R) with ResNet-50, zero-shot CLIP achieves 40.62% ACC with 7.18 ECE, while adapted methods substantially degrade calibration: CLIP-Adapter drops to 15.45 ECE, TIP-Adapter to 19.04 ECE, and TaskRes to 11.25 ECE. The proposed SaLS post-processing reduces ECE to 8.95, 8.13, and 9.03 respectively, while preserving accuracy. The Penalty-based training constraint achieves the largest ECE reductions for Adapters (e.g., −6.86 for TIP-Adapter) and also yields small accuracy gains (e.g., +2.28 for TIP-Adapter). On ViT-B/16, the miscalibration is even more severe (TIP-Adapter reaches 63.63 ECE), and Penalty reduces it to 40.98 ECE while improving accuracy from 25.86% to 49.23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline [45], all adaptation methods improve accuracy but consistently worsen ECE, confirming the miscalibration problem. Among Adapters, TIP-Adapter [63] suffers the most severe miscalibration (19.04 ECE on RN50, 63.63 on ViT-B/16), and the proposed Penalty constraint provides the largest correction (−6.86 ECE on RN50, −22.65 on ViT), while SaLS achieves the best final ECE (8.13 on RN50). CLIP-Adapter [10] shows moderate miscalibration (15.45 ECE) that SaLS nearly resolves (8.95 ECE), though ZS-Norm actually worsens it. TaskRes [60] is the best-calibrated Adapter (11.25 ECE), and all three proposed methods reduce it further to ~9 ECE. Among Prompt Learning methods, CoOp [66] is the most miscalibrated (10.97 ECE), reduced to 7.82 by SaLS; CoCoOp [65] and ProGrad [67] show smaller but consistent ECE reductions; MaPLe [22] is already well-calibrated (4.13 ECE) and SaLS slightly worsens it to 4.38. For test-time adaptation, TPT [49] is severely miscalibrated (11.27 ECE), and SaLS reduces it to 9.26; the concurrent C-TPT [59] method already improves calibration to 6.33, and further applying SaLS yields 5.22 ECE, outperforming C-TPT alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the calibration literature from fully-supervised models to the CLIP adaptation setting, building directly on temperature scaling (Guo et al., ICML 2017) by making it sample-adaptive and unsupervised (SaLS). It explicitly departs from Wei et al. (ICML 2022), who attributed miscalibration to logit norm inflation, by providing theoretical propositions and empirical evidence that logit range expansion is the operative cause in CLIP adaptation. The work also positions itself against concurrent calibration efforts for test-time prompt tuning (C-TPT, ICLR 2024), showing that its logit-range constraint is complementary and can be stacked on top of such methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As CLIP-based models are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains like healthcare, the finding that popular adaptation strategies (Adapters, Prompt Learning, TPT) substantially degrade calibration under OOD shifts is a practical warning that the field has largely ignored. The proposed solutions are simple, model-agnostic, and require no validation set, making them easy to adopt; however, the analysis is confined to CLIP with two backbones and ImageNet-derived OOD splits, leaving open whether the logit-range mechanism generalizes to other VLMs or more severe distributional shifts. An open question is whether constraining the range to zero-shot values is the right target, or whether task-specific calibration objectives could yield further gains without the accuracy trade-offs observed with ZS-Norm.
