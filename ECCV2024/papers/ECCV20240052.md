## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting represents scenes as millions of discrete, unorganized Gaussian blobs whose arbitrary orientations and positions do not align with the underlying surface, making direct mesh extraction unreliable. The prior method SuGaR attempts to enforce single-Gaussian dominance at each query point, but this produces blocking artifacts, bumps in textureless regions, and holes. The core gap is the absence of local structural hints that can guide Gaussian geometry toward the true surface during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images, COLMAP sparse point clouds for initialization, and pre-computed monocular normal and depth maps (from Omnidata) that are non-metric and require per-ray affine alignment. The base representation is Scaffold-GS, which uses learnable anchor points with tiny decoders to generate Gaussians, reducing storage to ~45 MB per scene versus >150 MB for standard 3DGS. The final output is a triangle mesh extracted via Poisson surface reconstruction from the optimized Gaussian means and their associated normals; the jointly trained neural implicit network is discarded after training and is not used for mesh extraction.

## Method

GSrec introduces two types of local structural hints. First, monocular normal cues guide Gaussian covariance learning by approximating each Gaussian's surface normal as the eigenvector of its smallest scaling factor, enforced via a rendered-normal consistency loss; monocular depth similarly guides Gaussian mean positions. Second, a signed distance field is constructed locally via an implicit moving least squares (IMLS) function that weighted-averages point-wise SDFs over nearby Gaussians (weighted by opacity × Gaussian density), and a Robust IMLS variant further down-weights Gaussians whose normals disagree with the neural implicit gradient. A neural implicit SDF network (Instant-NGP hash grid + MLP) is jointly trained to approximate this MLS function, with a loss combining zero-order value matching, first-order gradient-to-normal alignment, and an eikonal constraint; gradients flow back to both the Gaussians and the network, using the implicit representation's smoothness bias to regularize the discrete Gaussian field. Training proceeds in two stages: color + geometry-cue losses first, then the joint MLS loss is added once Scaffold-GS anchor growth is fixed.

## Results

On the Replica dataset (8 scenes), GSrec achieves an average Chamfer distance of 7.08 and F-score of 67.22, substantially outperforming SuGaR (SDF) at 9.57 Chamfer distance and 52.10 F-score, and SuGaR (density) at 10.78 and 46.06 respectively. On the real-world ScanNet dataset, GSrec achieves a Chamfer-L1 of 0.068 and F-score of 0.599, comparable to Manhattan-SDF (0.070 / 0.602) and MonoSDF-Grid (0.064 / 0.626) while training 12× faster than those neural implicit methods. Training time is approximately 40 minutes per scene on Replica, similar to SuGaR, with storage reduced to ~45 MB per scene (vs. >150 MB for SuGaR) thanks to the Scaffold-GS backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SuGaR (density), the primary 3DGS-based baseline, GSrec reduces average Chamfer distance by 34% (10.78 → 7.08) and improves F-score by 21 points (46.06 → 67.22) on Replica; SuGaR's density regularization causes blocking artifacts from its single-Gaussian dominance assumption. Against SuGaR (SDF), GSrec still achieves a 26% Chamfer reduction (9.57 → 7.08) and a 15-point F-score gain (52.10 → 67.22), with the gap most pronounced in textureless regions where SuGaR produces bumps. On ScanNet, GSrec (Chamfer-L1 0.068, F-score 0.599) is competitive with the strongest neural implicit baselines Manhattan-SDF (0.070 / 0.602) and MonoSDF-Grid (0.064 / 0.626), while MonoSDF-MLP (0.042 / 0.733) remains slightly better but requires >12 hours of training versus GSrec's <1 hour. GSrec decisively outperforms both SuGaR variants on ScanNet (Chamfer-L1 0.284/0.269, F-score 0.217/0.184), which are severely degraded by noisy camera poses and image quality. Against COLMAP SfM (0.141 / 0.537) and UNISURF (0.359 / 0.267), GSrec clearly wins on both metrics, confirming that the 3DGS-based approach with structural hints surpasses classical and early neural implicit methods on real captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) surface reconstruction line initiated by SuGaR, which first proposed density and SDF regularization to align Gaussians with surfaces. GSrec departs from SuGaR's single-Gaussian dominance assumption by instead exploiting local neighborhoods of Gaussians through a Moving Least Squares (MLS) function, and introduces a jointly trained neural implicit network (Instant-NGP backbone) as a structural regularizer for the MLS-based SDF. The base architecture is Scaffold-GS for storage efficiency, and monocular geometry cues from Omnidata provide normal and depth supervision during training—elements borrowed from the broader neural implicit surface literature (NeuS, MonoSDF) but repurposed to guide explicit Gaussian attributes rather than train an implicit field directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that 3DGS-based surface reconstruction can approach the quality of dedicated neural implicit methods (e.g., MonoSDF, Manhattan-SDF) while training 12× faster and using ~3× less storage, making high-quality mesh extraction practical for real-time and large-scale pipelines. The joint MLS + neural implicit regularization is a clean conceptual contribution: the implicit network serves only as a gradient-propagating regularizer, not the final surface, preserving 3DGS's rendering efficiency. Open questions include the acknowledged inaccuracy of depth rendering in 3DGS (which slightly degrades F-score in ablation), scalability to very large outdoor scenes, and whether the monocular cue dependency can be reduced for untextured or reflective surfaces.
