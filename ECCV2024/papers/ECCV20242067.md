## Problem

Class-incremental learning methods have not been effectively extended to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where standard 2D classifiers suffer large accuracy drops under occlusion, corruption, and domain shift. Neural mesh models (NeMo) demonstrate strong OOD robustness for classification and pose estimation but are limited to fixed training datasets. This paper bridges the gap by making neural mesh models learnable in a class-incremental setting while preserving their OOD advantages.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed by a ResNet-50 feature extractor (initialized with DINO-v1 weights) with two upsampling layers and skip connections, yielding a feature map at 1/8 input resolution. For classification, it outputs a class label via vertex matching with an uncertainty-aware scoring term that penalizes ambiguous features. For pose estimation, it outputs a 3D rotation (azimuth, elevation, roll) by iteratively refining an initial template-matching estimate through differentiable rendering and render-and-compare against the learned neural mesh.

## Method

iNeMo extends Neural Mesh Models to class-incremental learning by maintaining a growing set of cuboid meshes (each with ~1,100 vertices carrying 128-dim neural features) alongside a per-task ResNet-50 feature extractor. The key architectural insight is a latent space initialization strategy: the unit sphere is partitioned into N class regions using Equiangular Tight Frame centroids, and each new mesh's vertex features are randomly sampled from its assigned partition. Training combines three losses: a contrastive loss (L_cont) that pulls matched vertex-image features together while pushing them away from other vertices and unused partition features, a positional regularization (L_etf) that constrains image features to remain within their class centroid's region, and a KL-divergence distillation loss (L_kd) from the frozen previous-task feature extractor. A replay buffer with azimuth-angle-aware exemplar selection further mitigates forgetting, and the entire system is trained with a momentum-based update for the neural textures.

## Results

On in-domain Pascal3D+ (P3D), iNeMo achieves 98.21% mean classification accuracy (acc(1:i)), outperforming the best 2D baseline FeTrIL (96.82%) by 1.4%. Under severe occlusion (O-P3D L3, 60–80% occluded), iNeMo retains 71.55% accuracy versus 55.89% for FeTrIL and 16.93% for iCaRL, a gap of up to 54.6%. On the OOD-CV benchmark with severe domain shifts, iNeMo scores 80.82% versus 63.74% for FeTrIL. For the first incremental pose estimation setting, iNeMo achieves 51.73% accuracy at the tight π/18 threshold on P3D, more than doubling the best baseline (iCaRL at 22.76%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

iNeMo outperforms all six 2D baselines (LwF, FeTrIL, FeCAM, iCaRL, DER, PODNet) across every dataset and metric. FeTrIL is the strongest 2D classification baseline, reaching 96.82% on P3D and 88.34% on O-P3D L1, but iNeMo still leads by 1.4% and 5.9% respectively; the gap widens dramatically under heavier occlusion (L3: 71.55% vs. 55.89%) and on OOD-CV (80.82% vs. 63.74%). iCaRL is competitive on C-P3D (76.24%) but collapses under occlusion (16.93% at L3) and is far behind iNeMo on pose estimation (22.76% vs. 51.73% at π/18 on P3D). LwF and FeCAM are consistently the weakest baselines, with LwF dropping to 14.65% on O-P3D L3. For pose estimation, iNeMo leads by 21.5% (π/6) and 29.0% (π/18) over iCaRL on in-domain P3D, and by 10.7% (π/6) over LwF on O-P3D L1, though the advantage narrows at the most extreme OOD-CV shift (33.75% vs. 30.65% at π/6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neural Mesh Models (NeMo, Wang et al. ICLR 2021) and the discriminative render-and-compare classification framework (Jesslen et al. 2023) from fixed-dataset training to a class-incremental setting, adapting standard CIL techniques (replay buffers, knowledge distillation) to the 3D mesh representation. The key departure from both NeMo and prior CIL work is the introduction of a latent space partitioning strategy using Equiangular Tight Frames (ETF) to pre-allocate feature space for future classes, combined with a positional regularization loss that maintains class separation as new meshes are added. The momentum-based feature update follows Bai et al. (COKE, WACV 2023), and the overall architecture (ResNet-50 + DINO-v1 pre-training) is shared with the 2D baselines for fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D object-centric representations carry a fundamental robustness advantage over 2D CNNs in OOD scenarios, and that this advantage is preserved (and even amplified) under incremental learning, with gaps of 6–50% over the best 2D CIL baselines. It also introduces the first class-incremental pose estimation formulation, opening a new evaluation axis for continual learning. Open questions include scalability beyond cuboid approximations to full mesh geometries, whether the ETF partitioning strategy generalizes to very large class counts (beyond 100), and whether the approach transfers to real-world streaming scenarios where ground-truth mesh dimensions are unavailable at initialization time.
