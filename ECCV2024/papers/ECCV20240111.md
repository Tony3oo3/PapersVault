## Problem

Existing training-free distillation-aware architecture search (DAS) methods, such as DisWOT, rely on manually designed KD-based proxies that require extensive expert intuition and tuning, and fail to generalize across diverse architectures (e.g., they perform well on CNNs but poorly on Vision Transformers). The paper asks: how can one automatically discover effective zero-cost proxies without expert knowledge, and how can the optimal proxy be adapted to different search spaces?

## Input / Output

The method takes a search space definition (ResNet, PiT/ViT, NAS-Bench-101/201), a pre-trained teacher model, and a set of candidate student architectures with randomly initialized weights. The proxy search phase consumes pre-computed distillation accuracy ground truths from existing benchmarks (e.g., DisWOT's published results) to avoid retraining. The output is a discovered proxy expressed as a computation graph combining instinct statistics (student activations, gradients, weights) and interaction statistics (teacher-student features, embeddings, logits), which is then used to rank candidates and select the optimal student for final distillation.

## Method

Auto-DAS formulates proxy discovery as an evolutionary search over computation graphs. The search space comprises two input families—instinct (student's activations, gradients, weights) and interaction (teacher-student features, embeddings, logits)—combined with unary/binary operations, transformations, and distance functions (e.g., KL, L2, cosine). An Evolutionary Algorithm initializes a population of random proxy graphs, evaluates each by Kendall's tau correlation between proxy scores and ground-truth distillation accuracy, then iteratively applies mutation (replacing a random node with a new primitive operation) and selection. An adaptive-elite selection strategy compares each mutated offspring against a freshly random individual and retains the better one, preventing population deterioration and balancing exploration with exploitation. After converging on the best proxy, the method performs training-free student search by scoring all candidates with the discovered proxy and selecting the top architecture, which is then distilled from the teacher using standard KD loss.

## Results

Auto-DAS achieves state-of-the-art Spearman correlation across all search spaces: 98.44% on ResNet, 71.84% on NAS-Bench-101, 87.73% on NAS-Bench-201, 85.95% on NB-101-KD, and 74.66% on NB-201-KD, surpassing the previous best (DisWOT) by up to 40% in Kendall correlation. On the ViT-Bench (PiT search space), Auto-DAS reaches 90.32% Spearman on CIFAR-100 and 93.09% on Flowers, while DisWOT yields negative correlations (−28.83% and −30.33%). In terms of final distillation accuracy on NAS-Bench-201, Auto-DAS achieves 93.96% (CIFAR-10), 74.73% (CIFAR-100), and 47.50% (ImageNet-16-120), outperforming DisWOT by 0.41%, 0.52%, and 0.20% respectively at comparable search speed (180× speedup over multi-trial methods).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Auto-DAS outperforms all 13 baselines across every search space and metric. Against the closest competitor DisWOT (CVPR 2023), Auto-DAS improves Spearman correlation by 7.06 points on ResNet (98.44 vs. 91.38), 25.27 on NB-101 (71.84 vs. 46.57), 15.37 on NB-201 (87.73 vs. 72.36), 33.50 on NB-101-KD (85.95 vs. 52.45), and 9.76 on NB-201-KD (74.66 vs. 64.90). On the ViT-Bench, DisWOT produces negative Spearman correlations (−28.83% on CIFAR-100, −30.33% on Flowers), while Auto-DAS achieves 90.32% and 93.09%, demonstrating that handcrafted KD proxies fail to generalize to ViT architectures. Among zero-shot NAS baselines, Auto-DAS surpasses NWOT (45.66% on ResNet), SynFlow (88.30%), SNIP (85.07%), Fisher (81.37%), and EZNAS (93.63% on NB-201 CIFAR-10) in ranking correlation or distillation accuracy. Against training-based NAS methods (RL, BOHB, GDAS, DARTS), Auto-DAS achieves higher distillation accuracy on NAS-Bench-201 (e.g., 93.96% vs. 93.63% for RS, 92.83% for RL) while being 120–180× faster in search time (1.8K GPU-seconds vs. 216K for multi-trial methods). The gradient-free variant Auto-DAS(G-free) further reduces search cost to 0.82h while maintaining competitive accuracy (77.72% on CIFAR-100), outperforming DisWOT's 75.82%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends DisWOT (CVPR 2023), the first training-free distillation-aware architecture search framework, by replacing its handcrafted KD-based proxies with an evolutionary algorithm that automatically discovers proxy formulations from scratch. Conceptually, it adapts the Auto-Zero paradigm of evolving ML algorithms to the specific setting of distillation-aware NAS, incorporating both student instinct statistics (activations, gradients, weights) and teacher-student interaction statistics (features, embeddings, logits) as inputs to a computation-graph search space. It also builds on the broader zero-shot NAS literature (NWOT, SynFlow, SNIP, Fisher) by subsuming their proxy designs as candidate operations within the evolved search space, and on prior training-based DAS methods (AKD, gradient-based search) by eliminating their costly training loops entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that DAS no longer requires expert-designed proxies that fail to generalize across architectures (e.g., DisWOT's poor ViT performance), and the introduced ViT-Bench benchmark fills a gap in distillation-aware ViT evaluation. However, the search space itself (which operations and inputs are available) is still manually curated, and the proxy search requires ground-truth distillation results from existing benchmarks, limiting fully autonomous deployment. Open questions include whether the discovered proxies transfer across datasets and modalities without re-searching, whether the search space itself could be evolved, and whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks like detection or segmentation where distillation dynamics differ.
