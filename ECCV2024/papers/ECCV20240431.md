## Problem

Regression-based human mesh recovery methods typically compress image features via average pooling or rely on dense auxiliary annotations (part segmentation, IUV maps) to capture local body-part information, both of which disrupt spatial geometry and cause visual-mesh misalignment. Dense annotations are also labor-intensive and introduce uncertainty. The paper tackles how to preserve per-pixel spatial structure and capture fine-grained local dynamics for accurate mesh alignment without requiring dense labels or heuristic ROI pooling rules.

## Input / Output

The method takes top-down cropped RGB images at 256×256 pixels, encoded by HRNet-W32/48 to produce a 64×64 feature map at 1/4 resolution. Each of the 4096 pixel grids independently produces a full-body proposal consisting of a classification score, 17 2D keypoint coordinates, and SMPL rotation residuals; at inference the single prediction with the highest classification score is selected. Shape parameters and camera parameters are predicted once from the globally pooled feature, while rotation residuals are predicted per-pixel and added to the base rotation estimate.

## Method

GLNet follows a global-to-local prediction paradigm: a global estimation sub-network (average-pooled feature) predicts shape, base rotations, and camera parameters, and a pixel refinement sub-network uses a 2D Keypoint-Guided Local Encoding Module to predict rotation residuals. This module adapts AdaptivePose's 'Parts as Adaptive Points' representation, dividing the body into compositional parts each encoded by an unconstrained relevant point whose position is driven by 2D keypoint regression supervision, so that per-pixel features probe spatially-structured local context without dense annotations. A Dynamic Matching Strategy assigns positive/negative training labels via bipartite matching between the N pixel predictions and T repeated ground truths, computing matching cost from classification and 2D keypoint regression only (excluding 3D components to avoid instability). The matched positives receive classification, 2D keypoint, and 3D pose regression losses, while negatives receive classification loss alone, ensuring that the selected pixel prediction is visually aligned with the mesh.

## Results

GLNet achieves 29.4 mm PA-MPJPE and 48.8 mm MPJPE on Human3.6M, and 39.5 mm PA-MPJPE, 66.9 mm MPJPE, and 77.9 mm PVE on 3DPW (HRNet-W48 backbone). With the lighter HRNet-W32 backbone, it reaches 29.8 mm PA-MPJPE on Human3.6M and 39.7 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW. These results represent state-of-the-art performance among regression-based HMR methods, with particularly large margins on MPJPE and PVE metrics that reflect accurate visual-mesh alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLNet-W32 outperforms the strongest model-free baseline Graphormer by 8.4 mm MPJPE and 10.0 mm PVE on 3DPW, and by 4.7 mm PA-MPJPE on Human3.6M (29.8 vs 34.5). Among global regression methods, GLNet-W32 beats CLIFF-W48 by 2.7 mm MPJPE and 3.5 mm PVE on 3DPW without requiring holistic location information, and surpasses NIKI by 5.0 mm PA-MPJPE. Against local regression baselines, the margins are largest: GLNet-W32 reduces MPJPE by 12.8 mm and PVE by 16.5 mm over PARE, and by 2.5 mm MPJPE and 4.0 mm PVE over BOPR-W32 on 3DPW. On Human3.6M, GLNet-W32 improves PA-MPJPE by 13.1 mm over DaNet (29.8 vs 42.9) and by 4.9 mm over PLIKS (29.8 vs 34.7). The consistent advantage on MPJPE and PVE (which measure alignment-sensitive errors) over PA-MPJPE suggests the global-to-local design specifically improves visual-mesh correspondence rather than just global pose accuracy.
