## Problem

Class activation maps (CAMs) in weakly supervised semantic segmentation suffer from inconsistent and inaccurate activation, forcing existing methods to rely on offline refinement modules (CRF, PAMR, PAR) that decouple CAM optimization from the primary training objective. Single-stage approaches that incorporate such hand-crafted refinement struggle with optimization difficulty and poor generalizability, while multi-stage pipelines are complex and time-consuming. The paper asks whether CAMs can be explicitly optimized online in an end-to-end fashion, eliminating the need for separate refinement stages entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241506/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (cropped to 448×448) paired with image-level class labels, applying strong and weak augmentations to feed the online and assignment networks respectively. It produces pixel-level segmentation masks, guided class activation maps usable directly as pseudo-labels without post-processing, and image-level class predictions. The ViT-B backbone with LargeFOV decoder operates at 448×448 resolution, and the framework optionally supports CRF post-processing at inference (yielding only ~0.1–0.2% gain at significant speed cost).

## Method

CoSA employs a dual-stream architecture where an online network (ON) and an assignment network (AN, updated via EMA of ON) exchange swapped assignments: AN's CAM pseudo-labels (CPL) supervise ON's segmentation, while AN's segmentation pseudo-labels (SPL) supervise ON's CAMs, enabling mutual co-optimization without a pretrained teacher. Three techniques address learning dynamics: (i) Reliability-based Adaptive Weighting (RAW) computes per-pixel perplexity from CAM confidence distance to a threshold and re-weights the segmentation loss to penalize uncertain regions; (ii) dynamic thresholding fits a bimodal GMM over a queue of CAM confidences to adaptively determine the foreground/background split at each training step; (iii) contrastive separation extracts low-level CAMs from an earlier backbone layer and uses InfoNCE-style contrastive loss to separate coexisting classes (e.g., bird vs. branches) that high-level CAMs incorrectly merge. The key empirical finding is that CAMs are differentiable but non-deterministic due to spatial pooling in the classification head, and explicit guidance via SPL significantly improves CAM quality without degrading classification accuracy.

## Results

CoSA achieves 76.2% mIoU on PASCAL VOC 12 val and 51.0% mIoU on COCO val as a single-stage (end-to-end) method, surpassing the previous best single-stage method SeCo by 2.2% and 4.3% respectively. It also outperforms all multi-stage methods, including LPCAM (73.1% VOC val, 48.3% COCO val) and BECO (72.1% VOC val, 45.1% COCO val), making it the first single-stage WSSS approach to beat all multi-stage baselines. The multi-stage variant CoSA-MS with a Swin backbone reaches 81.4% VOC val and 53.7% COCO val, approaching fully supervised upper bounds (e.g., UperNet-Swin at 83.4% VOC val).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSA outperforms all 20 compared baselines on both VOC and COCO. Against the strongest single-stage competitor SeCo [66], CoSA gains +2.2% VOC val (76.2 vs 74.0) and +4.3% COCO val (51.0 vs 46.7). Over ToCo [51], the previous best ViT-B single-stage method, the margins are +5.1% VOC val and +8.7% COCO val. Among multi-stage methods, CoSA surpasses LPCAM [13] (best multi-stage on COCO at 48.3%) by 2.7% on COCO val and BECO [48] by 5.9% on COCO val. The gap over older single-stage methods is even larger: +10.2% VOC val over AFA [50] and +13.1% over ViT-PCM [49]. Notably, CoSA's single-stage result (76.2% VOC val) exceeds every multi-stage method in the table, including those using additional supervision such as saliency maps (L2G [26], Du et al. [18]) or language models (CLIP-ES [40], Xu et al. [63]).

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSA builds on the self-training/EMA momentum framework from BYOL and the self-distillation paradigm from DINO, repurposing them for a dual-stream WSSS setting where an assignment network generates swapped pseudo-labels (CPL for segmentation, SPL for CAMs) to train an online network. It departs fundamentally from the dominant multi-stage WSSS pipeline (CAM generation → offline refinement → segmentation) and from single-stage methods that rely on hand-crafted offline modules (CRF, PAMR, PAR) by showing that explicit online CAM optimization via segmentation pseudo-labels makes offline refinement redundant. The contrastive separation component draws on InfoNCE-style losses, while the dynamic thresholding borrows GMM fitting, but the core contribution is the end-to-end co-optimization loop that replaces the entire refinement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single-stage end-to-end model can outperform all multi-stage WSSS methods—including those augmented with saliency maps or language models—CoSA challenges the long-standing assumption that offline CAM refinement is indispensable, potentially simplifying the field's standard pipeline. The practical impact is significant: 240% faster training than BECO, 307% faster inference without CRF, and elimination of laborious hyperparameter tuning for thresholds. Open questions include whether the swapping-assignments framework generalizes to other weak supervision signals (bounding boxes, point annotations), whether it extends to instance or panoptic segmentation, and whether the coexistence problem solution transfers to datasets with more severe class co-occurrence patterns.
