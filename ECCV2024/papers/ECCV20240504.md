## Problem

Existing 3D human pose datasets are either single-person, confined to small lab settings with 1–2 subjects, or synthetic, leaving a critical gap for evaluating multi-person global pose estimation in expansive outdoor environments with coordinated group dynamics and moving cameras. No prior dataset provides accurate global trajectories for more than a handful of simultaneously active subjects in the wild, severely limiting progress on real-world multi-person pose and motion modelling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 16–18 static 1080p HD cameras covering a 105×68 m pitch, one moving broadcasting camera (1080p 50 Hz, 49.7 min / 150 k frames across 88 clips), and FIFA 3D LiDAR field measurements for calibration. Outputs are per-frame SMPL parameters (shape β∈ℝ¹⁰, body pose θ_b∈ℝ⁶⁹, root orientation θ_r∈ℝ³, world-space translation t∈ℝ³) for all players, yielding 2.5 M poses with global trajectories totalling >120 km, plus per-frame broadcasting camera intrinsics (focal length, principal point, radial distortion) and extrinsics (rotation, fixed translation).

## Method

The data acquisition pipeline has three stages. First, static cameras are calibrated via a three-step process: planar homography from hand-picked field-line correspondences, non-linear 3D-to-2D reprojection optimisation accounting for lens distortion and the ~20 cm field crown, and photometric refinement using a distance map to detected field-line edges for views with sparse corner visibility. Second, 2D detections (ByteTrack) and keypoints (ViTPose, fine-tuned via bootstrap on the low-resolution data) are triangulated across calibrated views, tracked with a point-to-ray distance affinity, refined by bundle adjustment jointly over field markings and player joints, and registered to SMPL via bone-length-based shape estimation followed by a multi-term optimisation (2D reprojection, temporal smoothness, shape prior). Third, the moving broadcasting camera is initialised by a commercial semi-automatic tool and then refined by minimising a Geman-McClure-robust reprojection loss over both field markings and 3D player keypoints, where player-to-keypoint matching uses a weighted bipartite assignment combining IoU and mean bone-direction cosine similarity.

## Results

The WorldPose data acquisition pipeline achieves 8.0 cm G-MPJPE and 6.6 cm PA-MPJPE when validated against a Vicon motion capture system, confirming high annotation accuracy. On the WorldPose benchmark, SOTA global pose estimation methods struggle significantly: GLAMR produces a G-MPJPE of 18,889 mm (≈18.9 m) with 53.3 cm/m Per-Meter Drift, while SLAHMR achieves 8,334 mm G-MPJPE and 17.6 cm/m drift. Even with ground-truth camera parameters, SLAHMR still yields 5,837 mm G-MPJPE, and per-person alignment (which removes relative-position errors) reduces GLAMR's G-MPJPE to 3,750 mm and SLAHMR's to 4,699 mm, revealing that incorrect inter-player relative positions are a major error source.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLAHMR substantially outperforms GLAMR on global trajectory accuracy, achieving 8,334 mm G-MPJPE versus GLAMR's 18,889 mm, and 17.6 cm/m Per-Meter Drift versus 53.3 cm/m. However, GLAMR's underlying initialization (HybrIK) yields a lower PA-MPJPE (78.8 mm) than SLAHMR's (163.9 mm), indicating that SLAHMR's global alignment step degrades per-frame pose quality. Substituting DROID-SLAM's predicted cameras with ground-truth values halves SLAHMR's G-MPJPE (8,334→5,837 mm) and Per-Meter Drift (17.6→10.7 cm/m), pinpointing camera estimation on the nearly textureless pitch as a key failure mode. Removing the HuMoR height-conditioning module from SLAHMR paradoxically improves PA-MPJPE from 163.9 to 140.9 mm, suggesting unreliable plane estimation undermines the height prior. Finally, per-person trajectory alignment reduces G-MPJPE by roughly 50–75% for both methods, confirming that a large fraction of global error stems from incorrect relative positioning between players rather than individual pose inaccuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on classic multi-view geometry (triangulation, bundle adjustment, homography-based calibration) and the SMPL parametric model, applying them at a scale and in a setting not previously addressed: 16–18 static cameras covering a full soccer pitch plus a calibrated moving broadcasting camera. It departs from the dominant learning-based paradigm for 3D pose estimation by relying on optimization-based pipelines (fine-tuned ViTPose/ByteTrack for 2D detection, then triangulation and SMPL fitting), justified by the need for pixel-level accuracy over a 1.75-acre capture area. The dataset itself extends the multi-person pose estimation task from lab-based or single-person in-the-wild settings to large coordinated groups (>10 subjects) with global trajectories observed from a moving camera, filling a gap the authors identify between prior multi-person datasets (Panoptic, BEDLAM) and prior global-pose datasets (EMDB, SLOPER4D).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldPose is the first dataset to combine >10 simultaneous subjects, accurate global trajectories over 120 km, and a calibrated moving camera in a real outdoor sporting context, making it a critical benchmark for multi-person global pose estimation that prior datasets simply cannot support. The benchmark results reveal that current SOTA methods (GLAMR, SLAHMR) produce global trajectory errors of 8–19 meters despite achieving ~80 mm local pose accuracy, exposing a fundamental weakness in how existing approaches handle relative positioning across many people in expansive, textureless environments. Limitations include male-only participants, dependence on stadium-scale static camera infrastructure, and costly manual intervention for occluded or blurry frames. Open questions include whether learning-based methods can close the global-trajectory gap, how to handle even larger crowds, and whether the pipeline generalizes to other sports or unstructured outdoor scenes.
