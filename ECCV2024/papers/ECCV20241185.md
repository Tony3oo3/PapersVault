## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF methods either learn deformation fields that only consider current-frame observations or directly interpolate time in feature space without motion priors. Both approaches overlook the temporal state-sequence structure of dynamic radiance fields, failing to exploit the complementary information available from both direct observations and physical motion dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241185/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with camera poses and timestamps, where each timestamp provides one image per camera (synthetic: single view per timestamp; real: 12 synchronized cameras, 11 for training and 1 held out for testing). It outputs novel-view rendered images at arbitrary timestamps, a per-point deformation field that warps real-space coordinates into a time-independent canonical space, and the underlying 4D radiance field encoded by a tri-plane representation.

## Method

KFD-NeRF models the 4D radiance field as a single-state dynamic system (state = deformation per frame) and estimates it by fusing a shallow two-layer MLP observation branch with a locally-linear motion prediction branch that approximates velocity from the two preceding frames. A learnable Kalman gain, computed by a linear layer from noise-related terms and timestamps, adaptively weights the two branches, automatically shifting emphasis from predictions (reliable priors early in training) to observations (fine-grained details later). The canonical space uses an efficient tri-plane representation that permits the shallow observation MLP to converge quickly, and training employs a chronological frame-release strategy, a soft canonical observation loss to regularize the canonical shape, a Kalman filter update loss aligning observations with estimated deformations, and total variation regularization on the tri-planes.

## Results

On the D-NeRF Synthetic Dataset, KFD-NeRF achieves the best PSNR of 35.73 dB and the best average score of 0.0131, outperforming the next-best methods V4D (33.54 dB) and 4D-GS (33.31 dB) by over 2 dB in PSNR. On the Nvidia Real Dynamic Scenes Dataset, KFD-NeRF achieves the best results across all metrics: PSNR 28.75 dB, SSIM 0.891, and LPIPS 0.115, surpassing the closest competitor V4D (28.16 dB PSNR) by 0.59 dB. The method trains in 30 minutes (small) to 3 hours (large) on a single RTX 3090, comparable to or faster than most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On synthetic data, KFD-NeRF achieves the highest PSNR (35.73 dB) and best average score (0.0131), beating V4D (33.54 dB) and 4D-GS (33.31 dB) by 2.2 and 2.4 dB respectively; however, it trails both on LPIPS (0.045 vs. 0.028 and 0.026), suggesting slightly less perceptual fidelity despite superior pixel-level accuracy. On real data, KFD-NeRF dominates all baselines across every metric, improving PSNR by 0.59 dB over V4D (28.75 vs. 28.16) and by 4.36 dB over TiNeuVox-B (28.75 vs. 24.39), while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.115 vs. 0.136 for V4D). Against the MLP-based D-NeRF, KFD-NeRF gains 5.48 dB in PSNR on synthetic data, highlighting the benefit of the tri-plane spatial representation over pure MLP encoding. The method also outperforms the feature-interpolation baselines KPlanes and V4D on both datasets, confirming that the Kalman-filter-guided deformation field captures motion more effectively than direct temporal feature interpolation. In terms of efficiency, KFD-NeRF-S trains in 30 minutes (faster than D-NeRF's 48 hours and KPlanes' 60 minutes) while KFD-NeRF-L takes 3 hours, comparable to V4D's 7 hours.

## Relation to Prior Work

KFD-NeRF extends the deformation-field line of dynamic NeRF (D-NeRF, TiNeuVox) by reframing per-point deformation estimation as a state-estimation problem in control theory, integrating a learnable Kalman filter that fuses an MLP-based observation branch with a locally-linear motion prediction branch. It departs from prior deformation methods that rely solely on current-frame observations by explicitly modeling temporal dynamics through a prediction step, and from prior deep Kalman filter work (which operates in learned latent spaces) by grounding the filter in physical motion priors. The tri-plane canonical-space encoding (from EG3D/TensorF) and the canonical-space regularization loss are borrowed from static NeRF literature but repurposed to stabilize the shallow observation MLP in a 4D setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in Kalman filter module offers a principled, interpretable mechanism for balancing temporal prediction and direct observation in dynamic scene reconstruction, which could be transplanted to other deformation-based or sequential 4D rendering methods. A key open limitation is the dependence on a well-reconstructed canonical space: scenes with significant scale or topological changes between frames degrade performance, and the authors acknowledge this cannot be resolved without a priori scene knowledge. Future work could explore adaptive canonical space selection, richer motion models beyond locally-linear dynamics, or extending the Kalman formulation to multi-state systems capturing velocity and acceleration explicitly.
