## Problem

NeRF-based semantic SLAM systems suffer from over-smoothing at object boundaries, making object-level segmentation and editing difficult, while also being computationally inefficient and prone to catastrophic forgetting in large scenes. Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM methods, though fast, lack any semantic understanding of the scene. No prior system combined the rendering speed and explicit geometry of Gaussian Splatting with semantic supervision for dense SLAM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240816/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes sequential RGB-D frames together with 2D semantic segmentation maps (sourced from dataset annotations or off-the-shelf 2D segmenters) as supervisory signals during both tracking and mapping. It outputs per-frame camera extrinsics, an explicit 3D Gaussian map where each Gaussian carries three parallel channels (spatial position/radius, RGB appearance color, and semantic color), and can differentially render novel views of color, depth, and semantic maps in real time. The semantic channel is stored as a per-Gaussian RGB triplet encoding the class label, enabling direct object-level grouping without additional segmentation at query time.

## Method

SGS-SLAM represents the scene as a set of isotropic 3D Gaussians, each parameterized by position, radius, opacity, RGB color, and a semantic color vector, all rendered to 2D via front-to-back volume splatting. The key novelty is a multi-channel optimization loss that jointly minimizes depth L1, appearance SSIM, and semantic SSIM terms simultaneously, so that geometric, photometric, and semantic gradients all flow directly to each Gaussian's parameters in a single rasterization pass. For keyframe selection, the system applies a two-level filter: first a geometric overlap ratio (fraction of sampled Gaussians visible in the candidate view) and second a semantic mIoU threshold that discards views whose semantic layout is too similar to the current frame, followed by an exponential uncertainty weight that down-weights older keyframes to counter accumulated tracking drift. This explicit, per-Gaussian semantic assignment also enables zero-training scene manipulation—objects can be moved, rotated, or removed by applying rigid transforms or visibility masks to the Gaussian subset sharing a given semantic label.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, SGS-SLAM achieves a mean PSNR of 34.66 dB, SSIM of 0.973, and LPIPS of 0.096 for reconstruction quality, outperforming the closest Gaussian-based baseline SplaTAM (33.98 dB PSNR) and all NeRF-based methods by a wide margin. For tracking, it attains a Depth L1 of 0.356 cm and ATE RMSE of 0.412 cm, surpassing SplaTAM (0.525 cm, 0.454 cm) and ESLAM (1.180 cm, 0.630 cm). For 3D semantic segmentation, SGS-SLAM reaches a mean mIoU of 92.72%, exceeding the best NeRF-based semantic SLAM baseline SNI-SLAM (87.41%) by over 5 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRF-based baselines, SGS-SLAM shows dominant improvements: over NICE-SLAM it gains ~10 dB in PSNR and reduces ATE RMSE from 2.503 cm to 0.412 cm; over Co-SLAM it improves PSNR by 4.4 dB and ATE RMSE by 61%; over ESLAM it improves PSNR by 5.6 dB and ATE RMSE by 35%. Against the closest Gaussian-based competitor SplaTAM, SGS-SLAM achieves modest but consistent gains: +0.68 dB PSNR, −0.169 cm Depth L1, and −0.042 cm ATE RMSE, while running at comparable FPS (2.11 vs 2.26). For semantic segmentation, SGS-SLAM outperforms all three NeRF-based semantic SLAM baselines (NIDS-SLAM 82.37%, DNS-SLAM 84.77%, SNI-SLAM 87.41%) by 5.3–10.4 mIoU points, with the largest gap over NIDS-SLAM. The paper attributes its advantage over SplaTAM to the additional semantic channel supervision and two-level keyframe selection (geometric + semantic), which prevents cumulative tracking errors from corrupting previously reconstructed regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGS-SLAM departs from the dominant NeRF-based SLAM lineage (NICE-SLAM, Co-SLAM, ESLAM) by replacing implicit MLP scene representations with explicit 3D Gaussian primitives, inheriting the fast rasterization and direct gradient flow from 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al.) and the Gaussian SLAM optimization framework from SplaTAM. It extends prior semantic SLAM work (DNSSLAM, SNI-SLAM, NIDS-SLAM), which all relied on NeRF volume rendering and separate semantic networks, by embedding semantic labels as an additional channel directly within the Gaussian field and jointly optimizing appearance, geometry, and semantics in a single differentiable rendering pass. The two-level keyframe selection (geometric overlap + semantic mIoU filtering) is a novel contribution not present in any prior Gaussian or NeRF SLAM system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first semantic SLAM system built on 3D Gaussians, SGS-SLAM bridges a gap between high-fidelity real-time reconstruction and object-level semantic understanding, which is critical for robotics and mixed-reality applications requiring both photorealistic rendering and scene manipulation. The ability to edit, move, or remove objects by semantic label without retraining is a practical advantage over NeRF-based systems that require full-network fine-tuning. Key limitations remain: the system depends on depth and 2D semantic inputs (limiting applicability to monocular or semantic-free settings), and memory consumption grows significantly for large-scale scenes, both of which constrain deployment in unconstrained environments.
