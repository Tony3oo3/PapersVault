## Problem

Standard NeRF-based novel view synthesis degrades significantly when input images contain rolling shutter (RS) distortions, which are ubiquitous in consumer and industrial CMOS cameras. Existing two-stage pipelines that first apply 2D RS correction and then train NeRF fail to exploit 3D scene geometry, producing multi-view inconsistencies and spatial misalignment. The paper asks whether the RS image formation process can be directly embedded into the NeRF rendering pipeline to jointly recover a clean 3D representation and per-row camera trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241221/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of RS-distorted RGB images (400×400 synthetic, 400×500 real) along with coarse initial camera poses (from COLMAP or the middle-row pose) and produces a NeRF volumetric scene representation, per-row camera extrinsics (H poses per frame, linearly interpolated in SE(3)), and corrected novel-view renderings. During training, RAFT optical flow between adjacent frames is computed to derive the PP-ratio used by the multi-sampling algorithm, and the model is trained for 200K iterations sampling 64 rows × 2 pixels per image per step on a single GPU.

## Method

RS-NeRF explicitly models the RS camera by assigning a distinct SE(3) pose to each image row, linearly interpolated in the Lie algebra between two optimizable endpoint poses, and renders each row's RGB through the NeRF MLP at that row-specific pose, minimizing photometric loss jointly over NeRF parameters and all row poses. Two key refinements address the vanilla model's weaknesses: (1) a trajectory smoothness regularization (L2 loss between the mid-point unit direction vector and the mean of adjacent frames' unit direction vectors) that enforces physically plausible camera motion across consecutive RS frames; and (2) a multi-sampling algorithm that computes a PP-ratio per pixel via RAFT optical flow—measuring how many intermediate camera poses share the same 2D RGB observation for a given 3D point—and randomly assigns each sampled pixel to a pose within that range, effectively augmenting training data per pose from a single row to potentially dozens of rows. Multi-sampling is disabled in the final training phase to avoid errors from inaccurate optical flow, ensuring the model converges on strictly correct correspondences.

## Results

On 5 synthetic scenes (TORII, WINE, POOL, FACTORY, TANABATA), RS-NeRF achieves an average PSNR of 27.93 dB (on-trajectory) and 28.65 dB (out-trajectory), with SSIM of 0.8393 and 0.8627 respectively. On out-trajectory views, RS-NeRF outperforms the closest competitor USB-NeRF by approximately 2.1 dB in PSNR (28.65 vs. 26.54) and DRSC+NeRF by 8.3 dB (28.65 vs. 20.36). The method also demonstrates robustness to pose initialization noise, maintaining or even slightly improving performance under U[-0.07, 0.07] perturbations in SE(3) space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-NeRF consistently outperforms all baselines across both on-trajectory and out-trajectory views. Against the two-stage 2D RS correction + NeRF pipelines (DeepUnroll, CVR, JAMNet), RS-NeRF gains 8–10 dB in PSNR on out-trajectory views, as the 2D methods introduce twist artifacts and spatial misalignment that propagate into the NeRF reconstruction. Compared to USB-NeRF (ICLR'24), which also models per-row camera poses but without the trajectory smoothness regularization or multi-sampling algorithm, RS-NeRF achieves +2.9 dB PSNR on on-trajectory views (27.93 vs. 25.02) and +2.1 dB on out-trajectory views (28.65 vs. 26.54). Against DRSC+NeRF, the gap is even larger: +7.6 dB on on-trajectory and +8.3 dB on out-trajectory PSNR, indicating that 2D correction followed by NeRF training is fundamentally limited by multi-view inconsistency. On real-world scenes captured with a Sony EO-1312C rolling shutter camera, RS-NeRF produces sharper edges and fewer artifacts than all baselines, while 2D correction methods degrade further in the real setting due to generalization issues.

## Relation to Prior Work

RS-NeRF extends the NeRF framework to handle rolling shutter distortions by jointly optimizing per-row camera poses and scene representation, departing from the two-stage paradigm of first correcting RS in 2D (DeepUnroll, CVR, JAMNet) and then training NeRF on the corrected images. It is closely related to the concurrent USB-NeRF (ICLR 2024), which also bakes RS camera motion into NeRF, but RS-NeRF adds trajectory smoothness regularization and a multi-sampling algorithm that exploit the PP-ratio to augment per-pose training data. The approach also draws on the broader line of NeRF-from-degraded-inputs work (Deblur-NeRF, BAD-NeRF) and on SE(3) Lie algebra interpolation for differentiable pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since rolling shutter sensors dominate consumer and industrial cameras, a method that reconstructs clean 3D representations directly from RS-distorted inputs without a separate 2D correction stage has clear practical value and avoids the multi-view inconsistency inherent to 2D correction pipelines. The PP-ratio insight—that RS exposure actually provides more per-pose training signal than the vanilla formulation exploits—is a useful conceptual contribution that could inform other degraded-input NeRF variants. Key limitations remain: the method only models camera motion and fails on dynamic scenes, and the evaluation relies on a small synthetic benchmark (5 Blender scenes) plus limited real captures, leaving open whether the gains hold at scale or in more complex real-world conditions.
