## Problem

Estimating full pose distributions over SO(3) for symmetric objects is critical for robotics, but existing contrastive-learning approaches require exhaustive training data from many viewpoints to cover all symmetry modes, which is impractical in realistic settings. Prior methods also lack a mechanism to incorporate readily available CAD shape priors into distribution learning, leading to slow convergence and blurry multi-modal posteriors, especially in low-data regimes.

## Input / Output

The network takes an RGB image crop and a rotation matrix sampled from an equivolumetric SO(3) grid (Yershova partitioning) as input, and outputs two unnormalized scalar scores (one SDF-based, one SurfEmb-feature-based) that are combined via softmax over the grid to yield the full posterior p(R|I). During inference, the grid is densely sampled (~2M rotations at 5 subdivision levels) to produce a multi-modal density; during training, K rotations per image are sampled with mode-focused bias from precomputed CAD-derived distributions. The output is an unnormalized empirical measure over SO(3) rather than a parametric distribution, supporting arbitrary multi-modality.

## Method

Alignist reformulates the rotation posterior as a distribution over visible CAD model points via a change-of-variables argument (Proposition 1), then models it as a product of two Boltzmann experts: a geometry expert using L0-norm of Deep-SDF values and a symmetry-aware feature expert using Frobenius distance of SurfEmb surface embeddings. A dual-branch MLP (ResNet50 image encoder + two MLP heads) is trained to predict both expert scores from (image, rotation) pairs, supervised by pre-trained SDF and SurfEmb networks that generate target scores for sampled rotations. The training loss is a generalized KL divergence between the unnormalized empirical measures, enabling direct distribution-to-distribution alignment without requiring normalized densities. A novel positional encoding rotates the vertices of a unit cube and applies sinusoidal encoding to the 3D corner coordinates, avoiding the sign-suppression artifacts of element-wise PE on rotation matrices. Mode-focused sampling from precomputed CAD-derived distributions (transferred across viewpoints via relative rotation) sharpens learned modes and accelerates convergence.

## Results

On SYMSOL-I (full 45k images), Alignist achieves an average log-likelihood of 10.64, outperforming SpyroPose (9.62), IPDF (6.39), and Normalizing Flows (6.88). In the low-data regime (10k images), Alignist retains a score of 9.69 while NF drops to 5.06, demonstrating the benefit of the CAD prior. On T-Less, Alignist achieves LL of 14.53, surpassing SpyroPose (14.1) and IPDF (12.0). On ModelNet10-SO3, Alignist reaches 70.5% AR@30°, trailing NF (77.4%) due to category-level supervision from a single CAD model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Alignist outperforms all baselines on SYMSOL-I by a large margin: +1.02 over SpyroPose (9.62) and +3.76 over NF (6.88) in the full-data setting, with the gap widening to +4.63 over NF in the low-data regime (9.69 vs. 5.06). On T-Less, Alignist (14.53) edges out SpyroPose (14.1) and IPDF (12.0), with the GKL loss contributing +0.6 over L1. On SYMSOL-II, NF (12.94) slightly outperforms Alignist (11.83) in the full-data setting, largely due to the Sphere object where the SDF expert is uninformative; however, Alignist surpasses both NF (6.04) and SpyroPose (7.76) in the 10k-image low-data regime. On ModelNet10-SO3, NF (77.4% AR@30°) outperforms Alignist (70.5%), attributed to category-level supervision from a single CAD model and the presence of symmetric models evaluated with only one GT rotation. Overall, the CAD-informed training pipeline provides the most significant gains in data-scarce scenarios, where contrastive and flow-based methods degrade substantially.

## Relation to Prior Work

Alignist extends the implicit distribution learning paradigm introduced by iPDF (Murphy et al., 2021) and refined by SpyroPose (Haugaard et al., 2023), but departs fundamentally from the image-only training paradigm by introducing a CAD model as a source of distributional supervision. The method repurposes two pretrained shape/feature networks—Deep-SDF for geometric alignment and SurfEmb for symmetry-aware correspondence—as product-of-experts scoring functions, a framework borrowed from energy-based models, to generate dense supervision over SO(3) without requiring exhaustive multi-view training data. The use of generalized KL divergence (Miller et al., 2023) to align unnormalized empirical measures, rather than contrastive or normalizing-flow objectives, represents a distinct training strategy that avoids the single-positive-label assumption of prior contrastive approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the low-data regime: with only 10k training images (vs. 45k), Alignist incurs a modest 0.95 LL drop while normalizing flows degrade more sharply, making the approach attractive for robotics settings where large posed-image datasets are infeasible to collect. However, the method's reliance on SurfEmb features as a proxy for texture information limits its effectiveness on objects where geometry alone is uninformative (e.g., the sphere in Symsol-II), and the approach does not explicitly model texture cues from either the image or CAD model. Open questions include whether the CAD prior generalizes to category-level settings with shape variation (as seen in the ModelNet10-SO3 results), and whether extending the framework to diffusion-based generation could further improve distributional fidelity.
