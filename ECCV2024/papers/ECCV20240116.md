## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF methods assume all-in-focus input videos, but real monocular video capture inevitably introduces defocus blur due to depth variation and large apertures, degrading reconstruction quality by disrupting temporal consistency and sharp detail. No prior dynamic NeRF approach models defocus blur during training, leaving the recovery of sharp novel views from defocused monocular video unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240116/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a defocused monocular video (940×360 frames) with COLMAP-estimated camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input, where the focal distance varies gradually across frames to mimic real focusing behavior. At test time, the trained NeRF renders sharp all-in-focus novel views by directly querying the radiance field without applying the DoF blur model, effectively inverting the defocus that was modeled during training. The evaluation dataset is synthesized from the VDW stereo dataset using BokehMe bokeh rendering, with left-view defocused sequences for training and right-view sharp sequences for evaluation.

## Method

The key insight is that the layer visibility weight in DoF rendering shares the same physical meaning as the opacity (transmittance) in NeRF volume rendering, enabling blur to be integrated directly into the volume rendering integral rather than applied as a post-process. The paper proposes layered DoF volume rendering, which reformulates the classical NeRF rendering equation to incorporate per-ray blur kernels, and transforms the traditional layer-based disk kernel into a ray-based sparse kernel (5 points within radius 10) whose positions and weights are predicted by an MLP conditioned on plane coordinates and time embedding. For dynamic scenes, two separate MLPs model static and dynamic content with a learned blending weight, and cross-time rendering warps rays via predicted scene flow under the same layered DoF volume rendering to enforce temporal consistency. Training is supervised by the defocused input views with additional data-driven priors from RAFT optical flow and DPT depth, and the entire pipeline is jointly optimized with Adam over 250k iterations.

## Results

On the synthesized VDW defocused dynamic dataset (8 scenes, 940×360), D²RF achieves PSNR 27.30, SSIM 0.816, and LPIPS 0.130, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest dynamic NeRF baseline, NSFF, reaches PSNR 27.01 / SSIM 0.803 / LPIPS 0.209, while the 2D-deblurring-then-NeRF pipelines (e.g., [42]+[8]) degrade to PSNR 24.52, confirming that image-space deblurring is insufficient for 3D-consistent reconstruction. D²RF's LPIPS of 0.130 is substantially lower than the next-best 0.196, indicating markedly better perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²RF outperforms all five baseline configurations on every metric. Against the strongest dynamic NeRF baseline NSFF (PSNR 27.01, SSIM 0.803, LPIPS 0.209), D²RF gains +0.29 dB PSNR, +0.013 SSIM, and reduces LPIPS by 0.079, with the LPIPS gap being the most notable. HyperNeRF (26.96) and RoDynRF (26.18) trail further behind, while DVS (25.43) is the weakest pure dynamic NeRF baseline. The two 2D-deblurring-then-NeRF pipelines ([42]+[22] at 25.79 and [42]+[8] at 24.52) actually underperform their un-deblurred counterparts in PSNR, demonstrating that single-image deblurring introduces cross-view inconsistencies that harm 3D reconstruction. The paper attributes D²RF's advantage to modeling defocus blur in 3D space via layered DoF volume rendering, preserving spatial-temporal geometry consistency that 2D preprocessing cannot guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

D2RF fuses two previously separate lines of work: dynamic NeRF (NSFF, HyperNeRF, DVS) for space-time view synthesis, and defocus-aware NeRF (DeblurNeRF, DP-NeRF, LensNeRF) for static scene deblurring. The key architectural departure is integrating the blur model *into* volume rendering rather than applying it as a post-processing step, as all prior defocus-NeRF methods do. The layered DoF volume rendering is derived from MPiB's visibility-based layered bokeh compositing, with the insight that layer visibility in DoF rendering is physically equivalent to opacity (transmittance) in NeRF volume rendering, enabling a seamless unification. The sparse optimized kernel follows DeblurNeRF's deformable kernel design but is reformulated from layer-based to ray-based to fit the NeRF ray-sampling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to recover sharp dynamic novel views from defocused monocular video, addressing a practically common but previously unhandled failure mode of dynamic NeRF. The elegant equivalence between DoF layer visibility and volume rendering opacity provides a principled framework that could inform future work on other optical degradations in NeRF. However, the evaluation relies on a small synthesized dataset (8 scenes from VDW stereo data with BokehMe-generated blur) rather than real defocused video, and the method cannot handle extreme defocus blur. The ~2-day training time per scene on a single GPU also limits practical adoption. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real-world defocused video with unknown aperture/focal parameters and whether the layered DoF formulation extends to other lens aberrations.
