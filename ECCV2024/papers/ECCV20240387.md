## Problem

Existing text-to-motion models face a fundamental trade-off: denoising models (diffusion or generative masking) produce high-quality, editable motion but require prior knowledge of sequence length, while autoregressive models can adaptively predict length but degrade in generation quality and editability due to strictly unidirectional sequential decoding. No prior framework simultaneously achieves high-fidelity generation, autonomous length prediction, and zero-shot temporal editing.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language text prompt (encoded via a pre-trained CLIP text encoder) and optionally a motion length constraint or a partial motion sequence for editing tasks (inpainting, outpainting, prefix/suffix completion). It outputs a discrete sequence of motion tokens decoded back into a 3D human pose sequence at 12.5 or 20 FPS (depending on dataset), with the sequence length determined adaptively by the model's prediction of an [END] token rather than being fixed a priori. For editing, the user provides a partial motion sequence with specific positions masked, and the model fills in the missing segments conditioned on the surrounding context and text.

## Method

BAMM combines a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer (encoding raw 3D poses into discrete latent tokens via a learned codebook) with a conditional masked self-attention transformer that unifies autoregressive and generative masked modeling through a hybrid attention masking strategy. Rather than substituting masked positions with [MASK] tokens as in standard BERT-style models, BAMM adjusts the attention score matrix using two causal masks: a unidirectional mask (only the text token is unmasked, enabling left-to-right autoregressive prediction and [END] detection) and a bidirectional mask (text and [END] tokens remain unmasked while a random subset of motion tokens are masked, allowing each masked position to attend to both past and future unmasked tokens). During training, the model randomly selects between the two masks (λ=0.5) and minimizes negative log-likelihood over the masked positions. At inference, a cascaded two-iteration decoding scheme first performs unidirectional autoregressive sampling to generate a coarse motion sequence and predict its length, then applies bidirectional autoregressive refinement by re-predicting low-confidence tokens with access to full surrounding context, using classifier-free guidance with scale 4 in the first iteration and scale 3 in the second. An optional residual vector quantization (RVQ) refinement transformer further reduces quantization error by predicting additional token sequences conditioned on the base sequence.

## Results

On HumanML3D, BAMM achieves the best R-precision Top-1 (0.525), Top-2 (0.720), Top-3 (0.814), and MM-Distance (2.919) among all compared methods, with an FID of 0.055 that is competitive with MoMask (0.045, which requires ground-truth length). On KIT-ML, BAMM leads on every metric: Top-1 0.438, FID 0.183, and MM-Dist 2.723, outperforming MoMask (FID 0.204) and MMM (FID 0.316). Critically, BAMM is the only method that simultaneously supports autonomous length prediction, length-constrained generation, and zero-shot motion editing, whereas denoising baselines (MoMask, MMM) degrade substantially when given predicted rather than ground-truth length (e.g., MoMask FID worsens from 0.045 to 0.090).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAMM outperforms all autoregressive baselines (T2M-GPT, AttT2M) by a wide margin on FID (0.055 vs. 0.116/0.112) and MM-Distance (2.919 vs. 3.118/3.038), while also achieving the highest R-precision Top-1 (0.525 vs. 0.491/0.499). Against the strongest denoising baselines, BAMM matches or beats MoMask on R-precision (0.525 vs. 0.521 Top-1) and MM-Distance (2.919 vs. 2.958), and beats MMM on FID (0.055 vs. 0.089) and all R-precision metrics; the only metric where a baseline edges ahead is FID with ground-truth length, where MoMask reaches 0.045 vs. BAMM's 0.055. However, when length is predicted rather than given, MoMask's FID degrades to 0.090 while BAMM remains at 0.055, making BAMM the clear winner in realistic deployment. On KIT-ML, BAMM dominates every metric, including FID (0.183 vs. MoMask 0.204, MMM 0.316). In zero-shot editing tasks (inpainting, outpainting, prefix, suffix), BAMM significantly outperforms MDM (e.g., FID 0.056 vs. 2.362 on inpainting) and matches or slightly exceeds MoMask, all without task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAMM unifies two previously competing paradigms in text-to-motion generation: denoising models (diffusion-based MDM, MotionDiffuse; masked-based MMM, MoMask) that achieve high fidelity but require known motion length, and autoregressive models (T2M-GPT, AttT2M) that adaptively predict length but suffer from unidirectional attention limiting quality and editability. The key architectural departure is the bidirectional causal mask, which allows masked tokens to attend to both past and future unmasked tokens while preserving the autoregressive [END] prediction mechanism. The motion tokenizer follows the VQ-VAE formulation from T2M-GPT, the masked modeling objective extends MMM and MoMask, and the cascaded inference with classifier-free guidance and RVQ refinement draws from audio generation literature (SoundStorm, EnCodec).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BAMM resolves the long-standing trilemma in text-to-motion generation—quality, usability (length prediction), and editability—by showing that a single model can simultaneously achieve all three without task-specific training. The zero-shot editing capability (inpainting, outpainting, prefix/suffix completion) is particularly practical for animation pipelines. Limitations include reliance on a VQ-VAE tokenizer that introduces quantization loss (mitigated but not eliminated by RVQ refinement), evaluation on relatively small datasets (HumanML3D ~14K, KIT-ML ~4K sequences), and the cascaded two-iteration decoding that doubles inference cost. Open questions include scalability to multi-person or long-horizon motions, real-time generation, and whether the bidirectional causal mask strategy transfers to other sequential generation domains.
