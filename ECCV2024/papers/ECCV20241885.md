## Problem

Event cameras offer high temporal resolution and no motion blur, but learning effective recognition models is hindered by the asynchronous, sparse nature of event data and the absence of large-scale labeled event datasets. While CLIP has demonstrated strong open-world few-shot recognition on images, its visual encoder cannot be directly applied to event data due to the significant modality gap, and no prior work has established a unified representation space spanning events, images, and text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241885/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Event streams are converted into T frames of size H×W×3 by aggregating N events per frame (N is a hyperparameter tuned via zero-shot performance), then split into P×P patches for ViT processing. Text class names are encoded via a hybrid prompt template ('A drafted image of a [class].' plus learnable tokens), and an optional 224×224 RGB image provides additional alignment signal. The model outputs a class label via softmax over the dot product of event and text embeddings, and the learned event embeddings enable cross-modal retrieval (image-to-event or text-to-event).

## Method

EventBind introduces a tailored event encoder (ViT initialized from CLIP's visual encoder) that incorporates temporal modeling—learnable temporal positional encodings plus cross-frame prompts that perform self-attention over CLS tokens across all T frames—and learnable event prompts inserted at each layer to bridge the modality gap without excessive parameters. A text encoder extends CLIP's text encoder with hybrid prompts (hand-crafted + learnable) and content prompts generated by a two-layer MLP from event/image embeddings. The core alignment mechanism is the Hierarchical Triple Contrastive Alignment (HTCA) module, which jointly minimizes InfoNCE contrastive losses between event–image, event–text, and text–text (semantic consistency) pairs, plus an MSE loss enforcing agreement between learnable and hand-crafted text embeddings. The total objective is a weighted sum of these four terms, and when image input is unavailable, only the event–text contrastive loss is active, preserving the framework's flexibility.

## Results

In fine-tuning with ViT-L-14, EventBind achieves 95.29% top-1 accuracy on N-Caltech101 (+5.34% over previous SOTA ACE-BET), 99.45% on N-MNIST (+0.35% over HATS), and 63.54% on N-ImageNet (+5.65% over MEM). In the 20-shot few-shot setting, EventBind reaches 92.11% on N-Caltech101 and 43.27% on N-ImageNet, outperforming EventCLIP by +1.70% and +3.99% respectively. Notably, the 20-shot N-Caltech101 result (92.11%) exceeds the full-data fine-tuning SOTA of ACE-BET (89.95%) while using only 46.09% of the training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventBind outperforms all 14 baselines across all three benchmarks in the fine-tuning setting. Against the closest competitor EventCLIP [53], EventBind gains +1.72% on N-Caltech101 (95.29% vs. 93.57%) and a substantial +5.65% on N-ImageNet (63.54% vs. 53.20%), attributed to the dedicated event encoder and triple-modality alignment rather than reusing CLIP's image encoder. Compared to the previous fine-tuning SOTA ACE-BET [34] (89.95% on N-Caltech101), EventBind improves by +5.34%, and against MEM [26] (57.89% on N-ImageNet), the gain is +5.65%. On N-MNIST, EventBind (99.45%) edges out HATS [45] (99.10%) by +0.35%. In the few-shot setting (Table 2), EventBind consistently beats EventCLIP across 5/10/20-shot on both N-Caltech101 (+0.92/+1.31/+1.70%) and N-ImageNet (+2.78/+2.73/+3.99%), with the 20-shot N-Caltech101 result (92.11%) surpassing the full-data ACE-BET SOTA (89.95%) using only 46% of training samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventBind extends the growing family of works that adapt CLIP to non-image modalities (audio, 3D point clouds, video, depth) by introducing event cameras as a third modality in a unified representation space. It builds directly on CLIP's ViT visual encoder as initialization and on hybrid text prompts (Yao et al., CVPR 2023) for the text encoder, but departs from the closest prior work EventCLIP (Wu et al., 2023) in three key ways: it replaces CLIP's image encoder with a purpose-built event encoder featuring cross-frame temporal modeling and event-specific prompts, it aligns three modalities (event, image, text) rather than just two (event, text), and it extends to event retrieval. The HTCA module's hierarchical contrastive alignment strategy is a novel loss design that goes beyond the pairwise alignment used in prior modality-extension CLIP works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical contribution is a principled recipe for transferring CLIP's open-world capability to event cameras without requiring large-scale event-text datasets, which remain scarce. The finding that a 20-shot EventBind model (92.11% on N-Caltech101) surpasses a fully fine-tuned SOTA (ACE-BET, 89.95%) is practically significant for deployment scenarios where labeled event data is limited. However, evaluation is confined to three relatively small benchmarks (10, 101, and 1000 classes), the event retrieval extension is only qualitative, and the approach has not been tested on more complex event tasks like segmentation or tracking. Open questions include whether the unified 3-modality space generalizes to longer event sequences, higher-resolution sensors, or multi-object scenes, and whether the prompt-based modality bridging scales to substantially larger event datasets.
