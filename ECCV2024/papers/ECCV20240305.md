## Problem

Prior video understanding approaches rely on end-to-end learning from raw pixels or task-specific visual encoders, leaving it unclear what representations are actually needed for temporal reasoning and question answering. The paper asks whether task-agnostic, free-form text descriptions (e.g., off-the-shelf image captions) can serve as effective video representations when paired with a large language model, and whether visual embeddings provide complementary information beyond what text already captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240305/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes sampled video frames (6–12 frames depending on the benchmark) processed by a frozen captioner (BLIP-2 or LLaVA-1.5) to produce concatenated frame-level text captions, optionally alongside CLIP ViT-L/14 visual embeddings linearly projected into the LLM's token space, plus task-specific text (questions, instructions, or future-action targets). Outputs include verb-noun action sequences for long-term anticipation, multiple-choice or open-ended answers for video QA, and—when the Token Bottleneck Model is active—a small set of selected evidence tokens (e.g., 40 out of ~644) that the LLM uses for its prediction.

## Method

Vamos is a two-stage framework that decouples perception (captioning + optional CLIP encoding) from reasoning (an LLM such as LLaMA-2/3 or GPT-4o). Text tokens and projected visual tokens are early-fused and fed to the LLM, which is fine-tuned with LoRA or LLaMA-Adapter for supervised tasks or used zero-shot for long-form QA. The key architectural contribution is the Token Bottleneck Model (TBM), which generalizes Concept Bottleneck Models to free-form tokenized text: a lightweight transformer encoder scores tokens within each of k uniform segments, and Gumbel-Softmax hard attention selects exactly one token per segment, condensing the input to ~6% of its original length. This yields interpretable evidence selection, supports human-in-the-loop test-time intervention, and achieves ~5× inference speedup while retaining competitive QA accuracy.

## Results

On NeXT-QA, Vamos (LLaMA3-8B) achieves 77.3% overall accuracy (77.2% causal, 75.3% temporal, 81.7% descriptive), setting a new state-of-the-art. On IntentQA, Vamos reaches 74.16% overall accuracy, a 28.7-point improvement over the prior best (CaVIR, 57.64%). On Ego4D LTA, Vamos achieves 0.868 action edit distance, outperforming AntGPT (0.877) and PALM (0.886). On EgoSchema zero-shot VQA, Vamos with GPT-4o reaches 53.55% accuracy, surpassing the best vision-language model (InternVideo, 32.1%) by 66.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vamos outperforms all 18 baselines across every benchmark. On NeXT-QA, it beats the strongest prior method LLaMA-VQA-33B (75.5%) by 1.8 points and SeViLA (73.8%, which uses additional temporal localization supervision) by 3.5 points, despite using a smaller 8B LLM. On IntentQA, the margin is dramatic: Vamos (74.16%) surpasses CaVIR (57.64%) by 16.5 points and BlindGPT (51.55%) by 22.6 points. On Ego4D LTA, Vamos (0.868 action edit distance) improves over AntGPT (0.877) and PALM (0.886) without relying on domain-specific video encoders. On EgoSchema zero-shot, Vamos with GPT-4o (53.55%) exceeds InternVideo (32.1%) by 21.5 points, demonstrating that decoupling perception (captioning) from reasoning (LLM) generalizes far better than end-to-end vision-language models in the zero-shot regime. On Spacewalk-18, Vamos with GPT-4o achieves 18.6% step recognition accuracy versus the prior best zero-shot of 13.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of end-to-end video understanding from raw pixels by proposing that task-agnostic text captions (generated by off-the-shelf models like BLIP-2 or LLaVA) can serve as effective video representations consumed by LLM reasoners. It generalizes the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) [36] from pre-defined discrete concepts with linear classifiers to a Token Bottleneck Model (TBM) that operates on free-form tokenized text with hard attention and a nonlinear LLM backend. The framework also sits within the broader trend of augmenting LLMs with visual inputs (e.g., Flamingo, VisualGPT, linear projection of CLIP features), but uses this as a controlled comparison to isolate the contribution of text versus visual modalities rather than as the primary mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that general-purpose captions consistently match or exceed CLIP visual embeddings across five diverse benchmarks challenges the assumption that fine-grained visual features are necessary for video reasoning in the LLM era, and opens the question of what visual information (if any) is truly complementary to language. The TBM provides a practical interpretability and efficiency tool (5× speedup, test-time intervention) that could be adopted broadly for LLM-based multimodal systems. Key open questions remain: whether better visual encoders (beyond CLIP) would close the gap, whether the results hold on tasks requiring fine-grained spatial or motion understanding, and whether improved captioning models can push the oracle upper bound (81.8% on EgoSchema) into practical range.
