## Problem

Generating high-fidelity 3D human textures with a consistent semantic UV layout remains difficult: existing optimization-based methods (e.g., AvatarCLIP, AvatarCraft) are slow (hours) and produce limited texture quality, while non-optimization approaches target generic objects rather than humans. Additionally, no prior dataset provides both high-resolution UV textures and paired text descriptions at scale, creating a data bottleneck for training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241248/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a free-form text prompt describing a human's appearance (race, gender, clothing, hairstyle, age) or a single RGB image of a person, and produces a 1024×1024 UV texture map conforming to the standard SMPL UV layout. Inference is zero-shot (no per-instance optimization), completing in under 0.2 minutes on a single GPU with 5.71 GiB memory for T2UV. The output texture can be directly applied to SMPL or more complex dressed meshes (e.g., TADA-generated avatars) for rendering.

## Method

TexDreamer uses a two-stage training pipeline built on Stable Diffusion 2.1. Stage 1 (T2UV) applies LoRA fine-tuning to the U-Net and text encoder, adapting the pretrained T2I model to the semantic UV structure while preserving its generalization; training samples are acquired via a novel two-stage process combining differentiable UV projection from multi-view images with project painting refinement. Stage 2 (I2UV) introduces a feature translator—a two-layer MLP followed by a three-layer transformer decoder with a learnable query sequence—that maps CLIP image encoder features into the text feature space of T2UV, allowing the frozen T2UV to generate UV textures conditioned on an input image without relying on DensePose segmentation. The key insight is bridging 2D image and UV layout through semantic textual features rather than pixel-to-surface correspondence. The authors also release ATLAS, a 50k-sample 1024×1024 texture dataset with text descriptions, synthesized by rendering T2UV-generated textures with AMASS motions, HDR IBL lighting, PBR materials, and diverse backgrounds in Blender.

## Results

On the text-to-UV (T2UV) task, TexDreamer achieves a CLIP score of 31.297, surpassing the best inference-based baseline Fantasia3D (30.557) and the optimization-based AvatarCLIP (29.422), while running in ~0.17 minutes on 5.71 GiB GPU memory—orders of magnitude faster than optimization methods (360–480 min). On the image-to-UV (I2UV) task, TexDreamer achieves MSE of 0.0442 and CLIP score of 27.334, outperforming Texformer (MSE 0.1148, CLIP 21.811) and SMPLitex (MSE 0.0783, CLIP 22.488). In a user study with 14 participants, TexDreamer receives the highest ratings for both text consistency (4.019/5) and image quality (4.244/5), roughly double the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TexDreamer T2UV outperforms all eight baselines on CLIP score. Among inference-based methods, it beats Fantasia3D (30.557) by 0.74 points and Text2Tex (29.962) by 1.34 points, while being ~85× faster than Fantasia3D (0.17 min vs. 14.50 min) and using less than half the GPU memory. Against optimization-based methods, TexDreamer surpasses AvatarCLIP (29.422) by 1.88 CLIP points while being ~2100× faster (0.17 min vs. 360 min); AvatarCraft's CLIP score is not reported due to prohibitive cost. SMPLitex, the only other human-specific method, trails significantly at 22.998 CLIP score, reflecting its limited training data (10 UV textures). For I2UV, TexDreamer reduces MSE by 61% over Texformer (0.0442 vs. 0.1148) and by 43% over SMPLitex (0.0442 vs. 0.0783), while also achieving the highest CLIP score (27.334 vs. 21.811 and 22.488), indicating superior texture fidelity and text-image alignment simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

TexDreamer builds on the Latent Diffusion Model (Stable Diffusion) as its generative backbone and adapts it to the specific UV layout of SMPL via LoRA fine-tuning, a strategy borrowed from LLM adaptation. It departs from per-instance optimization methods (AvatarCLIP, AvatarCraft) that require hundreds of minutes and from object-oriented texture synthesis (TEXTure, Latent-Paint, Text2Tex) that do not respect human UV semantics. For image-to-UV, rather than relying on DensePose segmentation and pixel-to-surface correspondence as in Texformer or SMPLitex, the paper introduces a feature translator that maps CLIP image features into the text-encoder feature space, effectively treating the image as a soft textual condition for the already-trained T2UV model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ATLAS dataset (50k textures at 1024×1024 with paired text descriptions) fills a significant gap in high-resolution 3D human texture data, enabling future research beyond the 100–921 textures available in prior datasets. The zero-shot multimodal formulation (text or image in seconds, no per-instance optimization) makes high-fidelity human texturing practical for interactive pipelines in gaming, film, and virtual try-on. Open questions include whether the feature translator generalizes to real-world photos with complex occlusions and garments (the authors note I2UV may not strictly align with input clothing patterns), and the ethical implications of generating photorealistic human avatars at scale.
