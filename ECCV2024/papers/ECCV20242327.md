## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image models produce images of everyday objects with limited geographic diversity, exhibiting heightened regional stereotypes and reduced variation in object type, color, and shape across regions. While prior work documented these disparities, no mitigation strategy had directly targeted geo-diversity in text-to-image generation. This paper addresses the gap by proposing an inference-time intervention that increases per-region representation diversity while maintaining image quality and text-image consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt of the form '{object} in {region}' and a small set of randomly selected real-world exemplar images (default: 2 per object) as input, and produces a sequence of N diverse generated images. Unusually, the generation is auto-regressive: a memory bank accumulates all previously generated images, and each new sample is steered to differ from all prior ones. The exemplar images are drawn from the evaluation dataset itself and serve as a real-world anchor to prevent the diversity-seeking signal from producing unrealistic outputs.

## Method

c-VSG modifies the DDIM reverse-diffusion score function at inference time by adding two Vendi Score gradient terms: a positive gradient (weight α) with respect to a memory bank of previously generated images to maximize the effective number of distinct samples, and a negative gradient (weight β) with respect to a small set of real exemplar images to constrain variation within realistic bounds. The Vendi Score is computed in CLIP ViT-B32 feature space, and the authors develop a differentiable formulation to overcome the non-differentiability of the original soft-rank computation. Guidance is applied every Gfreq-th diffusion step using the DDIM approximation of the denoised sample, avoiding the cost of full denoising at each step. The entire procedure requires no retraining—only a pretrained LDM, a memory bank, and a handful of exemplar images—making it a plug-and-play inference-time intervention.

## Results

On GeoDE, c-VSG achieves an average F1 of 0.455 and worst-region F1 of 0.444, representing a 25% and 37.9% relative improvement over the LDM baseline (0.364 / 0.322), respectively. On DollarStreet, c-VSG reaches 0.497 average F1 and 0.483 worst-region F1, outperforming all baselines by at least 8% relative F1. c-VSG also attains the highest CLIPScore (0.254 avg, 0.253 worst-region on GeoDE) and highest recall (0.493 avg, 0.476 worst-region), indicating simultaneous gains in diversity, quality, and text-image consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

c-VSG outperforms the vanilla LDM baseline [Rombach et al.] by 25% in average F1 and 37.9% in worst-region F1 on GeoDE, with the gap driven primarily by large recall gains (0.493 vs. 0.337 avg) while maintaining or improving precision and CLIPScore. Against the closest competitor, Semantic Guidance [Brack et al.; Friedrich et al.], c-VSG achieves 8.3% and 10.7% relative F1 improvements (avg and worst-region) on GeoDE, and 5.7% and 4.5% on DollarStreet, while also matching or exceeding its CLIPScore. Feedback Guidance (FG CLIP) [Hemmat et al.] achieves competitive recall but suffers significant precision drops (e.g., 0.321 worst-region precision on DollarStreet vs. 0.486 for c-VSG), resulting in lower overall F1. Textual Inversion [Gal et al.] improves precision over LDM but sacrifices recall (0.338 avg on GeoDE vs. 0.493 for c-VSG), yielding a lower F1. Notably, the Synonyms baseline actually degrades performance relative to LDM, suggesting that enforcing a fixed set of object-name variants is counterproductive for geographic diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Vendi Score—a dataset diversity metric originally proposed for evaluating GAN training diversity—into an inference-time guidance signal for latent diffusion models, extending the classifier-guidance framework of Dhariwal & Nichol by replacing the classifier gradient with a differentiable Vendi Score gradient computed over a memory bank of prior generations. It departs from prior geo-diversity work (Hall et al., 'Dig In') that diagnosed geographic representation gaps but offered no mitigation, and from broader bias-mitigation strategies (semantic guidance, textual inversion, feedback guidance) by targeting diversity directly through a diversity-aware objective rather than modifying prompts or attention. The contextualization step, which constrains generation to a small set of real exemplar images via a second Vendi Score term, is a novel combination that navigates the diversity–quality tradeoff without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first inference-time intervention specifically targeting geographic diversity in text-to-image generation, and it demonstrates that a simple, training-free guidance mechanism can substantially close worst-region diversity gaps while preserving or improving image quality and text-image consistency. Key limitations include reliance on CLIP ViT-B/32 feature space for the Vendi Score computation, evaluation on a single open-source LDM, and the absence of human evaluation for subjective diversity judgments. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other diversity axes (demographic, cultural), to more complex multi-object scenes, and to newer architectures like DiT-based models, and whether the memory bank scales practically for large-scale deployment.
