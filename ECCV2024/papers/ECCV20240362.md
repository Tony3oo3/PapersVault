## Problem

Existing CNN and attention-based spectral demosaicing methods fail to generalize across cameras with different wavelength distributions and MSFA patterns, because they do not explicitly encode wavelength information or the periodic MSFA sampling structure into the network. This results in significant performance degradation (e.g., 43 dB → 35 dB PSNR) when a model trained on one camera is applied to another, and in periodic artifacts in reconstructed spectra.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240362/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D raw spectral mosaic image Y (H×W) from an MSFA camera, where each pixel records only one of B spectral bands. It first applies MSFA-based reverse pixel shuffle to produce a low-resolution tensor (H/m × W/m × B), which is then processed by the network and upsampled via sub-pixel convolution to yield a full-resolution HSI cube (H×W×B). Experiments use 4×4 MSFA patterns with 16 bands (ARAD, KAIST) and a real IMEC camera at 1088×2048 spatial resolution covering 460–600 nm.

## Method

WeFAT is a Group Attention Transformer (GAT) that splits feature channels into two halves: one processed by a Wavelength-embedding Multi-head Self-Attention (We-MSA) module operating along the spectral dimension, and the other by a Shifted-Window MSA with MSFA-attention (MS2A) operating along the spatial dimension. We-MSA treats each spectral feature map as a token and injects normalized peak-wavelength values into attention via a diffusion-timestep-style embedding that re-weights the value vectors per head, giving the network explicit wavelength memory. The MSFA-attention Mechanism (MaM) encodes the periodic m×m filter array pattern through the same embedding pipeline and re-weights window-attention values to focus on spatial positions with known sampled spectral data. The two branches are fused by a pointwise convolution, and the full pipeline (shallow 3×3 conv → K GAT blocks → sub-pixel conv head with skip connection) is trained end-to-end with a supervised loss on a single dataset (ARAD), yet generalizes to cameras with different wavelength ranges and MSFA layouts.

## Results

On the ARAD dataset (50 scenes averaged), WeFAT-M achieves 48.03 dB PSNR, 0.994 SSIM, 0.015 SAM, and 0.018 MRAE, outperforming the best prior method MCAN (43.22 dB PSNR) by 4.81 dB. On the KAIST dataset with a different wavelength distribution and MSFA pattern, WeFAT maintains 43.63 dB PSNR while InNet and MCAN drop to 36.04 dB and 35.23 dB respectively, demonstrating strong cross-camera generalization. WeFAT-M uses only 1.71M parameters and 14.03G FLOPs, substantially fewer FLOPs than MCAN (29.90G) and InNet (1430.63G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeFAT-M outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both ARAD and KAIST. Against the strongest deep-learning baselines, WeFAT-M leads MCAN by 4.81 dB PSNR (48.03 vs. 43.22) and InNet by 5.15 dB (48.03 vs. 42.88) on ARAD, while also halving their FLOPs (14.03G vs. 29.90G for MCAN; 14.03G vs. 1430.63G for InNet). The generalization gap is most dramatic on KAIST: InNet and MCAN lose 6.84 dB and 7.99 dB respectively relative to their ARAD scores, whereas WeFAT actually improves slightly (43.63 dB on KAIST vs. 48.03 dB on ARAD), a 7.59 dB margin over InNet and 8.40 dB over MCAN. Traditional methods (WB, BTES, PPID, GRMR) remain 7–17 dB behind WeFAT on ARAD and 11–14 dB behind on KAIST, confirming that the wavelength-embedding and MSFA-attention mechanisms are the primary drivers of the performance gain rather than simply increased model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeFAT builds directly on the spectral-wise tokenization strategy from Cai et al. (CVPR 2022), which treats each spectral feature map as a token for transformer-based hyperspectral reconstruction. It departs from prior CNN and attention-based demosaicing methods (InNet, MCAN) that rely on MSFA-based rearrangement only at the input stage and lack explicit wavelength encoding, by borrowing the timestep-embedding mechanism from denoising diffusion models to inject precise wavelength information into multi-head self-attention. The MSFA-attention Mechanism (MaM) further extends the use of the filter array pattern from a one-time spatial operation to a learnable, per-block attention guide, a departure from all prior spectral demosaicing approaches that treat the MSFA purely as a sampling mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that a model trained exclusively on the 16-band ARAD dataset generalizes to cameras with different wavelength distributions and MSFA layouts (KAIST, real IMEC), outperforming same-domain-trained baselines by 7–8 dB in PSNR on the cross-domain test. This addresses a long-standing practical barrier: spectral imaging practitioners must retrain or fine-tune demosaicing networks for each new camera, which is costly and often infeasible. Open questions include scalability to true hyperspectral systems with 100+ bands, applicability to non-periodic or irregular MSFA patterns, and whether the wavelength-embedding strategy transfers to other physics-informed imaging tasks (e.g., polarimetric or thermal demosaicing). The real-camera evaluation remains visual-only, leaving quantitative cross-domain validation on hardware-acquired data as a gap.
