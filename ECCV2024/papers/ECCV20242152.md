## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D scene editing methods (e.g., IN2N, DreamEditor, NeRF-Art) require per-scene retraining for each new editing task and rely on editing all training views simultaneously, which introduces multi-view style inconsistency due to the inherent limitations of T2I diffusion models in producing prompt-consistent edits across multiple images. This inconsistency degrades 3D rendering quality and makes iterative retraining both necessary and computationally expensive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242152/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view RGB images with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, a single text editing prompt, and a target view pose. Only one randomly selected 'starting view' is actually edited by the 2D diffusion model (IP2P); the remaining M source views (8–12) remain unedited and provide geometric context. The output is a rendered edited target view at the specified pose, and a full 3D-edited scene is obtained by rendering 70–80 target views around the scene. No per-scene optimization is performed at inference time.

## Method

FREE-EDITOR builds on a Generalized NeRF (GNT/SRT) backbone and introduces an Edit Transformer that uses self-view attention to capture long-range editing effects within the single edited starting view, and cross-view attention to inject those editing features into the unedited source view feature maps (source tokens serve as queries, starting-view tokens as keys/values). These style-informed features are then aggregated via an Epipolar Transformer and converted to RGB by a Ray Transformer. Two novel losses enforce quality: a multi-view consistency loss using distance-weighted Jensen-Shannon Divergence between coordinate-aligned features of spatially neighboring rays, and a self-view robust loss that penalizes color discrepancies when the same target view is rendered from two different source-view subsets. The entire pipeline is trained once on multiple scenes and operates zero-shot at inference, achieving ~20× faster editing than SOTA with O(1) space complexity.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset, Free-Editor achieves the best Edit PSNR of 22.47 dB, outperforming IN2N (22.26), DreamEditor (22.34), C-NeRF (22.15), and NeRF-Art (20.89). On text-alignment (CTDS) and directional consistency (CDC), IN2N leads (0.2804 and 0.9882 respectively), while Free-Editor scores 0.2601 and 0.9781. In the generalization experiment on the edited LLFF-E dataset, Free-Editor achieves 22.47 PSNR, substantially outperforming the best prior generalized NeRF method (IBRNet at 19.05). In terms of runtime, Free-Editor completes editing in 3.2 minutes versus 62.1 minutes for IN2N and 70.5 minutes for DreamEditor, a ~20× speedup with O(1) space complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Free-Editor achieves the highest Edit PSNR (22.47 dB) among all four editing baselines, indicating superior preservation of scene structure during editing compared to IN2N (22.26), DreamEditor (22.34), C-NeRF (22.15), and NeRF-Art (20.89). However, IN2N leads on both CTDS (0.2804 vs. 0.2601) and CDC (0.9882 vs. 0.9781), suggesting slightly better text-alignment and frame-to-frame consistency, which the authors attribute to IN2N's iterative retraining advantage. Against the generalized NeRF baselines (PixelNeRF, MVSNeRF, IBRNet, NeuRay, GeoNeRF) on the edited LLFF-E dataset, Free-Editor achieves 22.47 PSNR versus a best of 19.05 (IBRNet), a 3.4 dB gap that demonstrates the necessity of purpose-built editing architecture over off-the-shelf novel view synthesis. In runtime, Free-Editor (3.2 min) is approximately 20× faster than IN2N (62.1 min) and DreamEditor (70.5 min), while matching DreamEditor's PSNR (23.06 vs. 23.18) and exceeding IN2N (22.98). The method also reduces space complexity from O(n) to O(1) by eliminating per-scene retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the generalized NeRF (Gen-NeRF) line of work (GNT, Scene Representation Transformer) that enables zero-shot novel view synthesis without per-scene optimization, and on the Epipolar Aggregated Transformer for multi-view feature aggregation. Its key departure from prior 3D editing methods (IN2N, DreamEditor, NeRF-Art, C-NeRF) is the elimination of per-scene re-training: rather than iteratively editing all training views and re-fitting a NeRF, FREE-EDITOR edits a single view and transfers the editing style to all other views via a novel Edit Transformer with cross-view attention. The 2D editing backbone (InstructPix2Pix) and captioning (BLIP) are borrowed from the IN2N pipeline, but the architectural integration into a feed-forward Gen-NeRF with a JSD-based multi-view consistency loss is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× runtime reduction (3.2 min vs. 62+ min) and O(1) space complexity make text-driven 3D scene editing practically deployable in interactive or near-real-time settings, a significant step beyond the batch-processing paradigm of prior methods. However, the approach is currently scoped to style transfer; object addition/removal and structural edits remain unaddressed, and the method still inherits quality limitations from the underlying 2D diffusion model. Open questions include whether the single-view editing paradigm generalizes to more complex structural modifications, whether the CLIP-based consistency check suffices for large scenes, and whether fine-tuning (rather than full re-training) can bridge the small performance gap to IN2N on CTDS.
