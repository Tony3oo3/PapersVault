## Problem

Single-stage multi-person multi-task human-centric perception (HCP) has not been fully explored due to the absence of a comprehensive benchmark dataset covering all representative HCP tasks simultaneously, and the lack of a unified representation that can handle diverse tasks (detection, segmentation, pose, attributes, 3D mesh) in one forward pass. Existing multi-stage pipelines suffer from early commitment to detection, runtime proportional to person count, and missed inter-task synergy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240465/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces, in one forward pass, per-person predictions across seven tasks: body bounding boxes, instance segmentation masks, 17 2D body keypoints, 3D SMPL pose and shape parameters, binary gender label, apparent age (formulated as 85-class classification with softmax expected value), and face bounding boxes. All outputs are instance-level, meaning each detected person receives a complete set of predictions, and the 3D mesh recovery uses gender-specific SMPL body models (male/female/neutral) selected via the predicted gender label.

## Method

HQNet learns a single shared content query per person instance, termed Human Query, that encodes instance-specific features of diverse granularity (local and global appearance, coarse and fine-grained localization) within a DINO-based transformer architecture with deformable attention. A task-shared transformer decoder refines these queries across layers, and lightweight task-specific heads (MLPs for coordinate regression, dot-product pixel embeddings for segmentation, direct query-to-class mapping for attributes) produce final predictions. Two key mechanisms enhance multi-task performance: HQ-Ins Matching, which uses combined detection, pose, and segmentation losses for bipartite matching to ensure consistent instance assignment across all tasks, and GaMS, which selects gender-specific SMPL models during training and inference to improve 3D mesh accuracy. The backbone is interchangeable (ResNet-50, Swin-L, ViT-L), and the shared architecture keeps per-task overhead minimal, enabling seamless addition of new tasks.

## Results

On the COCO-UniHuman val set, HQNet with ViT-L backbone achieves 78.0 AP for detection, 68.6 AP for segmentation, 75.3 AP for 2D keypoint estimation, 58.0 AP for gender classification, and 58.0 AP for age estimation, setting new state-of-the-art among single-stage multi-task HCP models. With the lighter R-50 backbone, HQNet still reaches 74.9 AP (det), 65.8 AP (seg), and 69.3 AP (pose), outperforming all multi-task baselines. On the challenging OCHuman benchmark, HQNet (ViT-L) achieves 35.8 AP detection, 38.8 AP segmentation, and 45.6 AP pose, surpassing previous SOTA by large margins. For the unseen face detection task, HQNet (R-50) achieves 68.4 AP after fine-tuning, outperforming ZoomNet (58.2 AP) by 10.2 AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For detection, HQNet (ViT-L) achieves 78.0 AP, surpassing DINO (R-50) by 4.7 AP and Mask DINO (R-50) by 5.7 AP. For segmentation, HQNet (ViT-L) reaches 68.6 AP, outperforming Mask DINO (R-50) at 64.8 AP and CondInst (R-50-FPN) at 54.8 AP. For pose estimation, HQNet (ViT-L) achieves 75.3 AP, trailing the top-down ViTPose (ViT-L) at 78.2 AP by 2.9 AP but exceeding PETR (R-50) at 68.8 AP and CID (HRNet-w32) at 69.8 AP. For gender and age classification, HQNet (ViT-L) achieves 58.0 AP on both, substantially outperforming Mask R-CNN baselines (46.7 AP gender, 37.4 AP age). On OCHuman, HQNet (R-50) outperforms SBL by 9.6 keypoint AP and CondInst by 11.0 segmentation AP on the test set, and with ViT-L sets new SOTA across all three tasks. For the unseen MOT task on PoseTrack21, HQNet (ViT-L) achieves 69.1 IDF1 without any MOT-specific training, beating FairMOT (63.2 IDF1, which was fine-tuned on PoseTrack21) by 5.9 IDF1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR/DINO family of query-based detection to a unified multi-task human-centric perception framework, where a single shared 'Human Query' replaces task-specific queries. It builds directly on DINO's mixed query selection and denoising training, Mask DINO's pixel-embedding segmentation head, and PETR's joint decoder for pose, but departs from prior multi-task HCP work (UniHead, Pose2Seg, PersonLab) by handling all tasks in a single forward pass with a task-shared decoder rather than separate task-specific models or multi-stage pipelines. The introduction of COCO-UniHuman as a comprehensive benchmark addresses a gap the authors identify: no prior dataset simultaneously covers detection, segmentation, pose, attributes, and 3D mesh in multi-person scenes, which had forced prior work to train on disjoint, biased single-task datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The COCO-UniHuman benchmark fills a genuine gap by providing the first large-scale multi-person dataset covering all four HCP task categories, enabling fair single-stage multi-task evaluation that was previously impossible. The demonstrated transferability of Human Query to unseen tasks (face detection, multi-object tracking) without task-specific training suggests the learned representation captures broadly useful instance-level features, opening the door to plug-and-play HCP task expansion. However, performance still lags task-specific SOTA on several tasks (e.g., 75.3 vs 78.2 AP for pose with ViT-L), and age estimation remains relatively weak, indicating the unified representation has not yet fully matched specialized models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video-based HCP, more complex attribute sets, or larger model families, and whether the inter-task synergy observed here persists at greater scale.
