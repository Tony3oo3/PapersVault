## Problem

Existing learned image compression methods employ either fixed learned kernels or image-level data-dependent transforms, but none perform region-specific adaptive transforms. Since contexts vary significantly across regions within a single image (e.g., textured vs. smooth areas), image-level transforms cannot capture fine-grained regional regularities. This paper is the first to introduce region-adaptive neural transforms guided by class-agnostic segmentation masks used as privileged information, achieving superior pixel-fidelity compression without incurring mask-related bitrate overhead at inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image as input (256×256 crops during training on COCO-Stuff; 768×512 Kodak and 2K CLIC images during evaluation) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. During training, class-agnostic panoptic segmentation masks are additionally fed to the encoder as privileged information; at inference these are replaced by 4×4 uniform grid partitions, so no mask bits are transmitted. The bitstream includes the quantized latent features, hyperprior, and region prototypes (extracted via Masked Average Pooling), all of which contribute to the total rate.

## Method

SegPIC builds on the Zou2022 VAE backbone (with Window Attention Module and ChARM entropy model) and adds two modules: the Region-Adaptive Transform (RAT) and the Scale Affine Layer (SAL). RAT introduces DPSConv, a depthwise-separable dynamic convolution where each spatial position receives its own kernel generated by a Kernel Generator from region prototypes and local latent context, with a Channel Attention Generator modulating the output; this replaces standard convolutions in the encoder, decoder, and entropy model. SAL, inspired by Spatial Feature Transform, inserts a 1×1-conv-GELU-1×1-conv block between adjacent sampling layers to produce per-region scale and bias that enrich features with cross-level semantics. Prototypes are extracted from mid-level encoder features via Masked Average Pooling over the class-agnostic masks, then compressed through a small prototype encoder/decoder and shared between encoder and decoder. The masks are used only during training as privileged information; at inference they are swapped for 4×4 grid partitions, and the model has internalized the semantic prior into its parameters, yielding near-equivalent performance without any mask transmission cost.

## Results

SegPIC achieves a BD-rate saving of 8.18% on Kodak and 8.26% on CLIC Professional Validation relative to the VTM-17.0 anchor, outperforming all compared learned and traditional compression methods. The closest prior LIC methods (Pan2022 at −5.42% and Contextformer at −5.38% on Kodak) trail SegPIC by roughly 2.8–3.0 percentage points in BD-rate. The simplified CNN-only variant (Ours-S, 29.2M params) still reaches +6.96% BD-rate on Kodak, approaching ChARM's +3.58% while using ~40% fewer parameters and faster inference (99 ms enc / 46 ms dec vs. 107/108 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegPIC outperforms every baseline on both Kodak and CLIC. Against the strongest traditional anchor VTM-17.0, it saves 8.18% (Kodak) and 8.26% (CLIC) BD-rate. Among learned methods, the closest competitors are Pan2022 (−5.42% Kodak) and Contextformer (−5.38% Kodak); SegPIC improves on both by approximately 2.8 percentage points. Compared to its own backbone Zou2022 (−2.89% Kodak, +0.14% CLIC), adding RAT and SAL yields an additional 5.45% (Kodak) and 8.50% (CLIC) BD-rate reduction. Against ELIC (−3.08% Kodak, −3.58% CLIC), SegPIC leads by over 5 points on both datasets. The simplified Ours-S variant (no self-attention, no auto-regressive entropy) narrows the gap to ChARM from +3.58% to +6.96% BD-rate on Kodak while halving inference time, confirming that RAT and SAL are effective even in lightweight CNN-only settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Zou et al. (CVPR 2022) window-attention backbone for learned image compression and adapts the dynamic convolution concept from Jia et al. (NeurIPS 2016) into a new Depth&Point-wise Separable Convolution (DPSConv). It departs from prior data-dependent transform work (Wang et al. 2022), which generates image-level kernels, by introducing region-level adaptive kernels guided by class-agnostic segmentation masks. Crucially, it diverges from earlier semantic-mask-based compression methods (Akbari 2019, Chang 2019/2021, Feng 2023) by treating masks as privileged information available only at training time, and by optimizing for pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, MS-SSIM) rather than perceptual or task-specific quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.2% BD-rate saving over VTM-17.0 is a meaningful advance for practical image compression, and the privileged-information framing offers a general recipe for leveraging auxiliary supervision without incurring inference-time bitrate or latency costs. The finding that coarse 4×4 grid partitions at inference approximate mask-guided performance suggests the network internalizes semantic structure into its parameters, raising the question of whether other auxiliary signals (e.g., depth maps, edge maps) could similarly be distilled into fixed architectures. Limitations include reliance on a specific training dataset (COCO-Stuff) for mask generation, the empirical choice of grid partition size, and the added ~8M parameters and ~20 ms latency over the baseline.
