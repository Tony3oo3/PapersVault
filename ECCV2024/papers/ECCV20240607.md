## Problem

Existing cardiac assessment methods from echocardiogram videos either capture global spatiotemporal features while neglecting local structural details, or are reconstruction-based approaches designed for CT/MRI/X-ray modalities with low-level abnormalities (tumors, fractures). Diagnosing cardiac diseases that manifest as either localized structural defects (e.g., atrial septal holes) or subtle motion abnormalities invisible in single frames (e.g., pulmonary arterial hypertension) remains unsolved, as no prior method simultaneously models local spatial and temporal cardiac motion patterns across diverse disease types.

## Input / Output

The method consumes echocardiogram videos in the apical four-chamber (A4C) view, sampling 16 equidistant frames from a 48-frame window (videos contain 100+ frames covering ≥2 heartbeat cycles at 720p), resized to 144×144 and center-cropped to 112×112. During training, frames are randomly masked at the patch level to simulate partial observation. At inference, the frozen feature extractor from the normal→abnormal network produces embeddings that are flattened and passed through a single linear layer for classification (PAH, ASD, EF abnormal/normal) or regression (EF score), while the full decoder produces a reconstructed video for quality evaluation via FID and DICE scores.

## Method

CardiacNet employs a bidirectional reconstruction pipeline with two networks (φ^A: normal→abnormal, φ^B: abnormal→normal) sharing a VQGAN-style architecture (feature extractor, codebook, decoder). The Consistency Deformation Codebook (CDC) quantizes features regionally using vector quantization augmented with learnable temporal positional encoding to preserve heartbeat-cycle consistency, and uses optimal transport (Wasserstein distance via Sinkhorn iteration) between EMA-updated memory banks to explicitly maximize the distributional separation between normal and abnormal feature spaces. The Consistency Deformation Discriminator (CDD) enforces spatiotemporal consistency through a two-level adversarial scheme: a global discriminator operating on the full video (temporal) and per-frame (spatial), plus a local patch-level discriminator that ensures each cardiac region reconstructs faithfully. Training combines L1 reconstruction loss, VQ quantization loss, optimal transport loss, and the multi-scale adversarial loss end-to-end, with the codebook updated via EMA (ω=0.01).

## Results

On the newly introduced CardiacNet-PAH dataset, CardiacNet achieves 89.32% AUC-ROC and 85.71% accuracy, surpassing the best classification baseline HiFuse (84.11% AUC, 83.67% ACC) by +5.21% and +2.04% respectively, while also achieving the best reconstruction quality (FID 14.73 vs. 16.12 for Wolleb et al.). On CardiacNet-ASD, it reaches 91.24% AUC-ROC and 89.63% accuracy, outperforming DeepGuide (85.02% AUC, 84.79% ACC) by +6.22% and +4.84%. For EF regression on CAMUS and EchoNet, CardiacNet achieves MAE of 5.97 and 3.83 respectively, a relative reduction of ~5.2% over HiFuse (6.34 and 4.08), while also attaining the best classification AUC (83.09% and 86.52%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CardiacNet outperforms all 14 baselines on every primary metric across both new datasets. On CardiacNet-PAH, it beats the strongest classification baseline HiFuse [9] by +5.21% AUC and +2.04% ACC, and the best reconstruction baseline Wolleb et al. [45] by 1.39 FID points. On CardiacNet-ASD, it surpasses DeepGuide [20] (the best classifier) by +6.22% AUC and +4.84% ACC, and achieves the best DICE score (73.52%) for structural consistency, exceeding CyTran [33] (70.21%) by over 3 points. On the public CAMUS dataset, CardiacNet reduces EF regression MAE from 6.34 (HiFuse) to 5.97 and improves classification AUC from 80.26% to 83.09%. On EchoNet, it achieves MAE 3.83 vs. 4.08 (HiFuse) and AUC 86.52% vs. 85.73%, though Wolleb et al. [45] marginally edges it on FID (13.18 vs. 13.25). Reconstruction-based baselines (Vanilla GAN, DAE, VTGAN, DeScarGAN, CyTran, etc.) consistently underperform CardiacNet by 15–35 AUC points, confirming that prior cardiac-structure-aware reconstruction is critical for echocardiogram disease assessment.

## Relation to Prior Work

CardiacNet builds directly on the VQGAN architecture (Esser et al., CVPR 2021) as its generative backbone, extending the vector-quantization codebook paradigm from static image synthesis to echocardiogram video reconstruction with temporal positional encoding. It departs from prior reconstruction-based anomaly detection methods (Wolleb et al., DeScarGAN, CyTran), which were designed for CT, MRI, and X-ray modalities with low-level structural lesions, by introducing a bidirectional normal-to-abnormal and abnormal-to-normal reconstruction pipeline specifically tailored to cardiac motion and morphology. The Consistency Deformation Codebook's use of optimal transport (Wasserstein distance via Sinkhorn iteration) to explicitly separate normal and abnormal feature distributions is a novel departure from prior codebook-based approaches that treat each sample independently. The Consistency Deformation Discriminator's dual global-and-patch-level adversarial training for both spatial and temporal consistency extends standard GAN discriminators into a video-specific regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of CardiacNet-PAH and CardiacNet-ASD provides the first publicly available echocardiogram video benchmarks specifically for pulmonary arterial hypertension and atrial septal defect assessment, filling a gap where only EF prediction datasets (CAMUS, EchoNet) existed. The reconstruction-based framing offers an interpretable pathway to cardiac disease assessment—physicians can inspect reconstructed 'normal' counterparts of abnormal cases—potentially increasing clinical trust in AI diagnostics. However, the method's computational cost (7.949 TFlops vs. 0.202 for ResNet3D) and the small dataset sizes (496 and 231 videos) limit immediate clinical deployment and generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the bidirectional reconstruction with optimal transport scales to larger, more diverse cardiac disease taxonomies, and whether the approach can be integrated with emerging multi-modal or LLM-based clinical decision support systems as the authors themselves suggest.
