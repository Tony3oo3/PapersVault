## Problem

Existing Sequential Brick Assembly (SBA) methods, particularly the state-of-the-art B³, are class-specific, require costly 3D voxel supervision, and suffer from O(n²) computational complexity due to graph-based modeling where each new brick is compared against all previous bricks. Additionally, step-wise action labels for real-world objects are expensive and tedious to annotate, limiting practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242291/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes three S×S binary silhouette images of a target 3D object as input and outputs a brick-wise action sequence of length N (up to 60 bricks for ModelNet-C), where each action encodes the connection type (x-offset, y-offset, relative rotation) and z-direction (top/bottom) between the current brick and its parent in the assembly tree. Actions are structured as a BFS traversal over a LEGO-Tree, and a binary occupancy voxel enforces 100% collision-free assembly during both training and inference.

## Method

The core architecture is a class-agnostic Tree-Transformer that replaces the O(n²) LEGO-Graph with a BFS LEGO-Tree, reducing complexity to O(n) by only modeling connections between consecutive layers. Each brick query combines a tree structure embedding (connection type, direction, depth) with a global geometry embedding (3D coordinates and orientation) to disambiguate bricks sharing identical tree positions. Training follows a two-stage synthetic-to-real pipeline: the model is first pre-trained on the synthetic RAD dataset with full cross-entropy supervision on action labels, then fine-tuned on real data via a self-supervised action-to-silhouette projection that converts predicted action probabilities into 3D voxel occupancy and projects them onto input camera views, using L2 loss against the input silhouettes as the sole supervision signal. An action reordering augmentation (randomly selecting the root brick) further bridges the gap between ordered synthetic assemblies and unordered real objects.

## Results

On MNIST-C, the class-agnostic TreeSBA model (pre-trained on RAD-S, self-trained with image silhouettes) achieves 63.9% mIoU, outperforming the state-of-the-art B³ (56.1%) by 7.8% without any 3D annotation on the target dataset. On ModelNet3-C, the class-agnostic model reaches 41.6% mIoU, surpassing B³ (30.3%) by 11.3%. On the synthetic RAD-1k dataset, TreeSBA achieves 71.2% IoU and 63.2% action accuracy in just 3.5 hours of training, compared to B³'s 42.0% IoU in 27 hours. The model also generalizes to ModelNet40-C (40 classes) with 45.4% mIoU using only 3-class self-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeSBA outperforms all four baselines across every dataset. Against B³ [6] (RL-based GNN requiring 3D voxel labels and class-specific training), TreeSBA gains 7.8% mIoU on MNIST-C and 11.3% on ModelNet3-C while being class-agnostic and requiring no 3D annotation. Against BO [16] (Bayesian optimization with voxel supervision), the improvement is 13.4% mIoU on MNIST-C and 12.0% on ModelNet3-C. On RAD-1k, TreeSBA (71.2% IoU, 63.2% action accuracy) surpasses SL [31] (23.0% IoU, 38.5% accuracy) by 48.2% in IoU and trains 4× faster than B³ (3.5 h vs. 27 h). Against SQ [24] (superquadric part assembly with point cloud supervision) on ModelNet3-C, TreeSBA achieves 41.6% vs. 29.2% mIoU. Notably, even without any fine-tuning on real data, the synthetic pre-trained model (56.9% mIoU on MNIST-C) already matches or exceeds B³, demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the LEGO-Graph formulation used by B³ [6] and Thompson et al. [31], where each new brick must be compared against all previous bricks (O(n²) complexity), by introducing a BFS LEGO-Tree that only models connections between consecutive layers (O(n) complexity). It also moves away from the class-specific, 3D-voxel-supervised paradigm of prior SBA methods toward a class-agnostic tree-transformer (ViT encoder + transformer decoder) trained via synthetic-to-real transfer. The action-to-silhouette projection for self-supervision is a novel loss design that repurposes multi-view silhouette images as a proxy for expensive action-level or voxel-level ground truth, drawing conceptually from silhouette-based 3D reconstruction losses but applied to sequential assembly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the need for 3D voxel or action annotations on real data, which is a major bottleneck for deploying assembly prediction in robotics and AR/VR. The finding that a self-supervised model outperforms 3D-supervised baselines by 7.8–11.3% mIoU suggests that image-level self-supervision can be more effective than geometric supervision for this task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond LEGO-like unit primitives to arbitrary part shapes, whether the BFS tree structure handles highly non-layered or cyclic assemblies, and whether the method scales to objects requiring hundreds or thousands of bricks without the search space becoming intractable.
