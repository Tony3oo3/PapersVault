## Problem

Embodied agents performing mobile manipulation (navigate to an object and interact with it) face two coupled challenges: scene representations that are rich in semantics, dynamically updatable, easily queryable, and generalizable to unseen scenes have not been jointly realized, and end-to-end neural policies are data-hungry and struggle when semantic targets are sparse in egocentric views over long trajectories. Existing map-based planners lack the flexibility to self-adapt for fine-grained object interaction, while pure neural approaches require extensive expert trajectories.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes egocentric RGB frames (from AI2THOR) and a natural language goal directive (e.g., 'Pickup Lettuce and put it on the DinningTable'), optionally augmented with step-by-step sub-instructions. Internally, three perception networks produce pixel-wise depth (50 bins of 10 cm), instance segmentation (85 classes), and affordance masks (1 navigability + 7 interaction classes). The system outputs a sequence of discrete navigation and manipulation actions, and maintains a 20 m × 20 m scene map discretized into 80 × 80 grids, each holding a 256-dim embedding that can be queried with semantic vectors to yield per-class probability maps.

## Method

DISCO builds differentiable scene representations as an 80×80 grid of 256-dim embeddings, queried via dot-product-plus-sigmoid against learned semantic vectors for each object and affordance class. At every step, projected semantic point clouds from the current egocentric frame produce soft grid-level labels (Eq. 1), and both grid embeddings and query vectors are updated by 10 gradient-descent iterations (lr = 0.01) with cross-entropy loss, restricted to visible grids. For action, a dual-level coarse-to-fine scheme is used: coarse control selects the grid of maximal object–affordance union probability, expands it to a 1 m neighborhood, and plans a BFS trajectory on the navigability map; fine control then applies a ResNet50-based behavior-cloning policy (trained on 316,935 short-horizon expert frames within 4 steps of interaction) that takes concatenated RGB, estimated depth, and target-object mask to predict the final manipulation actions. Perception modules (Mask R-CNN, two U-Nets) are trained with standard supervised losses on simulator-collected ground truth, and the entire pipeline is integrated into an embodied instruction-following application using BERT-based language parsing and template-based subgoal decomposition.

## Results

On the ALFRED benchmark test split, DISCO achieves 59.5% SR and 56.5% SR in seen and unseen scenes with step-by-step instructions, surpassing the previous best (Prompter at 53.2%/45.7%) by +6.3% and +10.4% respectively. Without step-by-step instructions, DISCO still reaches 58.0%/54.7% SR, outperforming the best baseline *with* instructions (Prompter) by +4.8%/+8.6%. In terms of efficiency, DISCO achieves 40.6/36.5 PLWSR (seen/unseen), roughly 1.57× and 1.75× the PLWSR of Prompter, indicating substantially shorter trajectories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCO outperforms all 13 baselines across every metric in both seen and unseen test splits, regardless of whether step-by-step instructions are provided. The closest competitor is Prompter [24], which uses LLM prompting for planning; DISCO beats it by +6.3% SR (seen) and +10.4% SR (unseen) with instructions, and by +8.6% SR (unseen) even *without* instructions. CAPEAM [26], the second-strongest baseline, is surpassed by +7.7% SR (seen) and +10.4% SR (unseen) with instructions. The efficiency gap is particularly striking: DISCO's PLWSR of 40.6/36.5 (seen/unseen) is roughly 1.57× and 1.75× that of Prompter (25.8/20.8), meaning DISCO completes tasks in substantially fewer steps. Model-free baselines (Seq2Seq, MOCA, E.T., LWIT, M-Track) trail far behind, with unseen SR below 20%, highlighting the advantage of the map-based coarse control. Among model-based methods without step-by-step instructions, DISCO leads Prompter (49.4/42.6) and CAPEAM (47.4/43.7) by 8.6–11.0% SR in unseen scenes, confirming that the differentiable scene representation and dual-level control generalize well to novel environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCO bridges the model-free (end-to-end neural policy) and model-based (map-based planning) schools of embodied navigation that have been largely treated as separate paradigms on ALFRED. It inherits the perception architecture and instruction-processing pipeline from FILM [36] and the semantic mapping procedure from Chaplot et al. [8], but departs fundamentally by replacing discrete cell-based scene representations with differentiable continuous embeddings optimized via gradient descent, and by coupling global map-based coarse navigation with a short-horizon neural fine-control policy rather than relying on either alone. The affordance module also replaces the hand-crafted openability rules used in prior map-based methods with learned pixel-wise predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that DISCO without step-by-step instructions outperforms the prior SOTA (Prompter) with step-by-step instructions by 8.6% in unseen scenes suggests that better scene modeling and control design can compensate for less explicit linguistic guidance, which has implications for reducing annotation costs in embodied AI. The differentiable scene representation offers a principled alternative to hand-crafted update rules, and the dual-level control reduces imitation data needs by ~3×. However, all experiments are confined to the AI2THOR simulator with ground-truth affordance oracles, and the language module relies on fine-tuned BERTs; generalization to real-world perception noise, open-vocabulary tasks, and more complex multi-step reasoning remains open.
