## Problem

Unsupervised depth completion and estimation rely on reconstruction-based supervision, but common geometric augmentations (rotation, resizing, translation) introduce resampling artifacts, intensity saturation, and sparse-point decimation that corrupt the training signal. As a result, existing methods are restricted to minimal photometric perturbations and flipping, leaving a large class of useful augmentations infeasible and limiting model performance and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241712/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For depth completion, the method consumes an RGB image paired with a sparse depth map (a 2.5D range map of roughly 500–1500 points) plus temporally adjacent frames for photometric reprojection; for monocular depth estimation, only the RGB image and adjacent frames are used. The network outputs a dense depth map at the input resolution, which is then inverse-warped back to the original camera reference frame before any loss is computed, so the supervision always targets the un-augmented inputs.

## Method

AugUndo applies a sequence of photometric and geometric augmentations to the input image and sparse depth map, lets the network predict depth from the augmented inputs, and then inverts the geometric transformations on the predicted depth map to warp it back into the original reference frame. Reconstruction losses (photometric reprojection and sparse-depth alignment) are then computed against the original, un-augmented image and sparse depth, bypassing all resampling and saturation artifacts. Geometric augmentations are modeled as changes in camera intrinsics (focal length, principal point) rather than extrinsics, which avoids scale ambiguity in the sparse-depth-grounded setting and keeps the 3D scene consistent. The framework supports eleven augmentation types—brightness, contrast, saturation, hue, patch occlusion, sparse-point removal, horizontal/vertical flip, resize (zoom in/out), rotation, and translation—combined in a plug-and-play manner with negligible extra training cost, and the inverse warping is fully differentiable.

## Results

On VOID1500 (depth completion), AugUndo yields an average 18.3% improvement across all three methods and all four metrics (MAE, RMSE, iMAE, iRMSE), with VOICED improving by 26.4%, FusionNet by 16.3%, and KBNet by 12.2%. On KITTI, the average gain is approximately 5.2%, with FusionNet showing the largest improvement at 6.41%. For zero-shot generalization, models trained on VOID with AugUndo transfer to NYUv2 and ScanNet with average improvements of 23.2% and 27.6% respectively, and monocular depth estimation models (Monodepth2, HR-Depth, Lite-Mono) show consistent gains across all accuracy and error metrics on both VOID and KITTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AugUndo is a plug-and-play augmentation framework applied on top of existing methods rather than a competing architecture, so every comparison is each baseline with vs. without AugUndo. For depth completion on VOID, VOICED [65] benefits the most (26.4% average improvement), likely because its scaffolding densification overfits to specific sparse point configurations that occlusion and geometric augmentations disrupt. FusionNet [63] improves by 16.3% and KBNet [67] by 12.2%, with KBNet already being the strongest baseline (MAE 38.11→33.32 mm). On KITTI, all three completion methods improve by ~5.2% on average, with the smaller gain attributed to the dataset's strong scene bias (planar ground, horizontal LiDAR scans). For monocular depth estimation, Monodepth2 [15] improves δ<1.25 from 0.717 to 0.724 on VOID and from 0.869 to 0.879 on KITTI; HR-Depth [35] and Lite-Mono [81] show similar consistent gains across all metrics. MonDi [33] and DesNet [70] are evaluated in the supplementary material with the same trend of improvement. Across all six baselines and both tasks, AugUndo never degrades performance, confirming its generality as a training-time augmentation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard practice in unsupervised depth completion and estimation, where augmentations are restricted to minor photometric perturbations and flipping due to artifacts corrupting the reconstruction-based supervision signal. The key conceptual departure is computing the unsupervised loss on the *original* (un-augmented) inputs by inverse-warping the predicted depth back to the original reference frame, rather than on the augmented inputs as prior methods do. This inverse-warping idea is borrowed from differentiable rendering and view synthesis, but its application to unlock geometric augmentations (rotation, translation, resizing) for unsupervised geometric tasks with sparse depth is new. The framework is explicitly positioned as plug-and-play over existing architectures (VOICED, FusionNet, KBNet, Monodepth2, HR-Depth, Lite-Mono), requiring only modifications to the data pipeline and loss computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: an 18.3% average improvement on VOID and consistent gains across six methods and two datasets, at negligible additional training cost, makes this a low-hanging fruit for anyone training unsupervised depth models. The zero-shot generalization gains (23–27%) and robustness to sparse depth density shifts (23.1%) suggest the technique addresses a real overfitting-to-augmentation-artifact problem that had gone unaddressed. Limitations include restriction to 2D augmentations (no 3D scene-level nuisances), single-image input (no extension to stereo or multi-frame tasks), and the observation that supervised methods may benefit less since depth map resampling artifacts are milder than image artifacts. Open questions include whether the approach extends to tasks with stricter geometric constraints (e.g., stereo's frontoparallel assumption) and whether 3D-aware augmentations could further close the gap.
