## Problem

Existing semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation methods perform consistency learning on a single representation (e.g., voxel grid only), which limits perturbation diversity and amplifies confirmation bias from imperfect pseudo labels. Additionally, their contrastive learning relies on a small random subset of embedding samples, preventing a comprehensive understanding of the latent space distribution across the full training set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240005/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point cloud scans (3 spatial coordinates plus features like intensity and elongation) and simultaneously transforms them into two peer representations: a range image (spherical projection, e.g., 32×1920 for nuScenes, 64×2048 for SemanticKITTI) and a cylindrical voxel grid (H×W×L). It produces per-point semantic segmentation maps in both representations, with the final prediction obtained by projecting back to the original point cloud. The labelled set provides ground-truth one-hot labels for both views, while the unlabelled set supplies only the raw scans.

## Method

IT2 enforces prediction consistency between two peer LiDAR representations (range via FIDNet/ResNet34 and voxel via Cylinder3D) by cross-projecting each network's softmax predictions into the other's coordinate space to generate hard pseudo labels and confidence maps, which supervise the opposite network on unlabelled data. For contrastive learning, the method fits class-specific Gaussian Mixture Models (M=5 components) over concatenated range and voxel embeddings using a confidence-weighted EM algorithm, then samples virtual positive and negative prototypes from these GMMs to compute an InfoNCE-style loss that is detached from the backpropagation graph. A representation-specific augmentation strategy (multi-box CutMix for range, single-inclination LaserMix for voxel) further improves generalisation. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end by jointly minimising the cross-view consistency loss and the GMM-based prototypical contrastive loss.

## Results

On the uniform sampling protocol (Table 1), IT2 achieves mIoU of 71.3% (range) and 72.1% (voxel) on nuScenes with 10% labelled data, outperforming the previous SOTA LaserMix by 3.1% and 2.2% respectively. The advantage is most pronounced in the low-label regime: at 1% labelled data on nuScenes, IT2 reaches 56.5% (range) and 57.5% (voxel) mIoU, surpassing LaserMix by 7.0% and 2.2%. On SemanticKITTI at 1% labels, IT2 achieves 51.9% (range) and 52.0% (voxel) mIoU, a gain of 8.5% and 1.4% over LaserMix. On ScribbleKITTI at 1% labels, IT2 reaches 46.6% (range) mIoU, an 8.3% improvement over LaserMix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IT2 outperforms all seven baselines across nearly every partition protocol and dataset. Against the previous SOTA LaserMix [27], IT2 gains approximately 3.4% (range) and 2.0% (voxel) mIoU on average across all nuScenes partitions, with the largest margin at 1% labels (+7.0% range, +2.2% voxel). On SemanticKITTI at 1% labels, IT2 surpasses LaserMix by 8.5% (range) and 1.4% (voxel). In the partial and significant sampling protocols on SemanticKITTI (Table 2), IT2 beats GPC [23] by 4.5% at 5% labels (partial) and lim3D [31] by 3.5% at 5% labels (significant), with TTA. For the contrastive learning component specifically, IT2's GMM-based cross-distribution approach outperforms the label-guided pixel-wise ContrasSeg [54] by ~1% mIoU on nuScenes at 10% and 20% labels. IT2 also consistently exceeds the supervised baseline by 10–18% mIoU in the 1% label regime, demonstrating the value of the semi-supervised framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised LiDAR segmentation line of work (LaserMix, GPC, lim3D, CPS) by repurposing the multi-view consistency idea from contrastive multi-view coding into a peer-representation consistency framework: instead of perturbing a single representation, IT2 enforces agreement between two structurally different LiDAR encodings (range image and voxel grid). The contrastive learning component departs from prior work that samples positive/negative pairs from actual mini-batch embeddings by instead fitting per-class Gaussian Mixture Models over the full training set and drawing virtual prototypes from those distributions. The representation-specific augmentation strategy (CutMix for range, LaserMix for voxel) is an empirical finding that builds on both the data augmentation literature and the multi-representation LiDAR processing tradition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The peer-representation consistency mechanism directly addresses the confirmation bias problem that plagues single-representation SSL: when one view systematically mislabels a category (e.g., Pole in range view, Sidewalk in voxel), the other view can correct it, yielding more robust pseudo labels. The GMM-based prototypical contrastive learning offers a principled way to overcome the limited-sample bottleneck in dense contrastive learning without the computational cost of full pairwise comparisons. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to three or more representations, whether the GMM fitting remains stable with very small labelled sets, and whether the two-network training overhead is justified in resource-constrained autonomous driving pipelines.
