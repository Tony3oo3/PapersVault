## Problem

Current query-based video segmentation methods model object emergence as a transition from background queries to foreground queries and disappearance as the reverse, creating a large feature transition gap that causes them to miss newly appearing objects and fail to detect when tracked objects vanish (recall below 45% on both). This paper identifies this static anchor design as the root cause and proposes a mechanism to close the gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241344/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of video frames processed by a shared image encoder (ResNet-50, Swin-L, or ViT-L with DINOv2 pretraining and ViT-Adapter for multiscale features). A segmenter extracts per-frame object queries, which are then propagated frame-to-frame by two lightweight trackers operating solely at the query level (adding <2% computational overhead). Outputs are per-object segmentation masks, class labels, and implicit tracking identities maintained through query propagation across frames.

## Method

The core contribution is Dynamic Anchor Queries (DAQ), which replace static background/foreground anchors with queries dynamically generated from candidate features: for emergence, mask-pooled appearance features of current-frame segmenter predictions serve as positional embeddings; for disappearance, momentum-weighted appearance features of tracked objects serve as positional embeddings, paired with initial segmenter queries as query features. Two trackers are instantiated: Tracker 1 receives continuously tracked queries plus emergence DAQs to handle tracking and new-object detection, while Tracker 2 receives disappearance DAQs plus learnable background embeddings to identify vanishing objects (with SoftMax along the query dimension to prevent duplicate assignments). A training-time Emergence and Disappearance Simulation (EDS) strategy removes random tracked or current-frame queries to synthesize emergence/disappearance events, providing abundant training signal without extra cost. The full system (DVIS-DAQ) is built on the DVIS baseline with Referring Cross-Attention replaced by standard Cross-Attention, and uses a modified Hungarian matching that preserves historical CTQ-to-GT assignments while assigning remaining ground truth to DAQs via candidate-based matching.

## Results

DVIS-DAQ achieves 57.1 AP on OVIS with ViT-L backbone and offline refiner, surpassing the previous SOTA DVIS++ (53.4 AP) by 3.7 AP. On YouTube-VIS 2019 and 2021, it reaches 69.2 and 64.5 AP respectively with ViT-L + refiner. On YouTube-VIS 2022 (long videos), DVIS-DAQ with ViT-L achieves 42.0 AP^L, outperforming DVIS++ (37.5 AP^L) by 4.5 AP. On VIPSeg, it attains 57.4 VPQ with ViT-L, a 1.4 VPQ improvement over DVIS++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On OVIS with ViT-L, DVIS-DAQ (53.7 AP) outperforms DVIS++ (49.6 AP) by 4.1 AP and even surpasses DVIS++ with offline refiner (53.4 AP); with the refiner, DVIS-DAQ† reaches 57.1 AP, a 3.7 AP gain. On YouTube-VIS 2019, DVIS-DAQ matches DVIS++ (68.3 AP) without refiner and leads by 0.9 AP with refiner (69.2 vs. 68.3). On YouTube-VIS 2021, DVIS-DAQ slightly trails DVIS++ without refiner (62.4 vs. 63.9 AP) but leads by 0.6 AP with refiner (64.5 vs. 63.9). On YouTube-VIS 2022 long videos, DVIS-DAQ with ViT-L achieves 42.0 AP^L, a substantial 4.5 AP improvement over DVIS++ (37.5 AP^L). On VIPSeg, DVIS-DAQ with ViT-L reaches 57.4 VPQ, exceeding DVIS++ (56.0 VPQ) by 1.4 VPQ and TarVIS (48.0 VPQ) by 9.4 VPQ. With ResNet-50, DVIS-DAQ consistently outperforms DVIS by 1.5–4.0 AP across OVIS and YouTube-VIS benchmarks, while matching or slightly trailing DVIS++ on the shorter YouTube-VIS clips.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the propagation-based query tracking paradigm established by DVIS and GenVIS, where object queries from the previous frame are iteratively refined via cross-attention to segment the current frame. The key conceptual departure is diagnosing that the static anchor query design—using a generic background query for emergence and a stale foreground query for disappearance—creates a large feature transition gap that fundamentally limits association quality. The proposed Dynamic Anchor Queries (DAQ) mechanism repurposes candidate object features (via mask-pooling or momentum-weighted appearance features) as anchor queries, and the EDS training strategy addresses the data scarcity of emergence/disappearance events (<20% of samples) without architectural changes. The work is positioned as a drop-in improvement to the DVIS decoupled tracker, requiring no backbone or segmenter modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies a persistent blind spot in SOTA video segmentation: recall below 45% on emerging and disappearing objects, which are common in real-world scenarios like autonomous driving and video editing. The EDS strategy is practically valuable because it generates abundant training signal for rare events at zero additional inference cost, a pattern applicable to other rare-event problems in video understanding. Open questions include whether the two-tracker design (one for emergence/tracking, one for disappearance) can be unified, whether the approach generalizes to longer videos with more complex appearance/disappearance patterns, and whether the gains persist when the segmenter backbone is weaker or the scene is more cluttered.
