## Problem

Current generative models are conditioned on modalities like text that cannot precisely specify the direction, distance, or location of object deformations, making them inadequate for predicting physically plausible outcomes of specific interactions. The paper asks how to give machines the ability to imagine the future state of a scene given a concrete action, enabling world modeling and action planning for robotics and interactive media.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240803/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a binary hand mask marking the hand's current position, and a query binary hand mask specifying the desired interaction, all at the same spatial resolution (H×W). The output is a single synthesized RGB image depicting the scene after the interaction has occurred. Because conditioning uses only binary masks rather than labeled action vectors, the approach is agent-agnostic: the same pipeline accepts robot gripper masks at inference without any retraining, and hand masks can be obtained automatically via off-the-shelf segmentation (Segment Anything) from unlabeled video.

## Method

CoSHAND fine-tunes a pretrained Stable Diffusion latent diffusion model by adding hand-based visual conditioning. The input image, current hand mask, and query hand mask are each encoded by a fixed VAE and channel-wise concatenated into a context latent (3c channels), which is then concatenated along the channel dimension with the noisy target latent at each diffusion step. A U-Net denoises in latent space using this context, while a frozen CLIP image embedding of the input provides cross-attention conditioning to preserve scene semantics. Training uses the standard DDPM noise-prediction objective with 5% random dropout of both the channel context and CLIP signal for classifier-free guidance (optimal scale ≈ 2.5). The key design choices—binary masks for agent-agnostic control, large-scale pretrained diffusion priors for hand-object interaction knowledge, and unlabeled video for scalable before/after pairs—enable strong zero-shot generalization to unseen objects, environments, and robot embodiments.

## Results

On the SomethingSomethingv2 validation set, CoSHAND achieves SSIM 0.414, PSNR 13.72, and LPIPS 0.116, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the In-the-wild test set (unseen objects and environments), CoSHAND reaches SSIM 0.576, PSNR 18.156, and LPIPS 0.125, again leading all comparisons. The method also demonstrates zero-shot generalization to robot gripper interactions on BridgeDataV2 without any robot-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSHAND outperforms all four baselines on every metric (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS) on both the SomethingSomethingv2 and In-the-wild test sets. Against MCVD (masked conditional video diffusion), CoSHAND improves SSIM from 0.231 to 0.414 and LPIPS from 0.307 to 0.116, attributed to the Stable Diffusion prior and explicit hand conditioning. Against InstructPix2Pix (text-editing), CoSHAND achieves substantially higher structural similarity (SSIM 0.414 vs. 0.289) and lower perceptual distance (LPIPS 0.116 vs. 0.296), demonstrating that text conditioning cannot capture the precise spatial control of hand-driven deformations. The two internal ablation baselines—UCG (unconditional generation with input-image conditioning) and TCG (text-conditional generation)—also lose to CoSHAND, confirming that the hand-mask action signal is the critical differentiator rather than architecture alone. On the In-the-wild set, the margins widen (e.g., PSNR 18.16 vs. 14.20 for TCG), indicating that the hand-conditioning advantage grows in out-of-distribution settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSHAND builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model architecture (Rombach et al., 2022) and DDPM training objective (Ho et al., 2020), fine-tuning a Stable Diffusion backbone with hand-mask channel concatenation and CLIP cross-attention as conditioning. It departs from text-conditioned editing methods (InstructPix2Pix, TCG) by replacing imprecise language with spatially explicit hand masks as the action signal, and from video prediction approaches (MCVD) by introducing an explicit action-conditional formulation. Conceptually, it bridges the world-model literature (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018) and generative diffusion models, using hand masks as a natural, agent-agnostic action representation that requires no annotation beyond off-the-shelf segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hand masks serve as a practical, scalable action interface for diffusion-based world modeling, eliminating the need for explicit action labels or physics simulators. The zero-shot transfer to robot grippers (BridgeDataV2) without any robot-specific training is a promising signal for cross-embodiment world models in robotics. Key limitations include poor performance on unrealistic or highly ambiguous interactions, and the single-step (one-frame-ahead) prediction horizon, which limits planning utility. Open questions include whether scaling to larger video datasets and multi-step prediction can close the gap to physically grounded simulators, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-object or long-horizon tasks.
