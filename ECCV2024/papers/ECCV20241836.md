## Problem

Diffusion models have achieved strong results on 2D images and small 3D objects, but generating large-scale 3D outdoor scenes remains largely unexplored due to the bulky size of scene data, the scarcity of comprehensive real-world 3D scene datasets, and the resulting instability of training a single diffusion model on such high-dimensional data. Existing attempts either rely on external guidance signals (scene graphs, 2D maps) that limit generalizability or produce scenes with undesired wholes and missing fine-grained objects.

## Input / Output

The model consumes random discrete noise (unconditional) or a coarse one-hot semantic scene / unlabeled point cloud (conditional) and outputs a 3D semantic scene in one-hot voxel format of size 256×256×16 with 10 semantic categories. Internally, the pyramid operates across scales from 32×32×4 (s1) up to 256×256×16 (s4), with the largest scale further split into four overlapping sub-scenes of 136×136×16 each. The output is a full-resolution semantic voxel grid suitable for downstream tasks like 3D segmentation.

## Method

PDD decomposes unconditional 3D scene generation into a cascade of L conditional discrete diffusion models, one per pyramid scale, where each model (except the first) takes the previous scale's denoised output as a condition via a Scale Adaptive Function (trilinear upsampling) and refines it to the next resolution. Each diffusion model uses a 3D-UNet backbone and is trained with a two-term KL divergence loss over categorical (one-hot) states, following the structured discrete diffusion framework. At the largest scale, a Scene Subdivision module splits the scene into four overlapping sub-scenes along the z-axis, generated autoregressively by a single shared diffusion model conditioned on already-generated overlap regions, then merged by voting to ensure continuity. This multi-scale design enables parallel training of independent models, cross-dataset transfer by fine-tuning only smaller-scale models on new data, and unbounded infinite scene generation by iteratively extending the subdivision with edge-conditioning.

## Results

On CarlaSC (unconditional generation), PDD achieves 68.02 mIoU (SparseUNet) and 33.89 mIoU (PointNet++), substantially outperforming DiscreteDiff (40.05 / 25.54) and LatentDiff (38.01 / 26.69). In terms of distributional fidelity, PDD attains F3D = 0.315 and MMD = 0.200, compared to 1.361 / 0.599 for DiscreteDiff and 0.331 / 0.211 for LatentDiff. In the conditional setting (coarse scene at s₁), PDD reaches 55.75 mIoU(V) and 29.78 mIoU(P), exceeding the same-condition DiscreteDiff baseline (52.52 / 27.93). On SemanticKITTI after cross-dataset fine-tuning, PDD achieves 44.7 mIoU(V) and MMD = 0.11, outperforming DiscreteDiff trained from scratch (29.1 mIoU(V), MMD = 0.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiscreteDiff [2] (single-scale unconditional), PDD improves mIoU(V) by 28 points (68.02 vs. 40.05) and reduces F3D by over 4× (0.315 vs. 1.361) and MMD by ~3× (0.200 vs. 0.599), indicating far better semantic coherence and distributional fidelity. Against LatentDiff [18] (VQ-VAE + latent diffusion), PDD leads on all segmentation metrics (mIoU(V): 68.02 vs. 38.01; mIoU(P): 33.89 vs. 26.69) and edges out on F3D (0.315 vs. 0.331) and MMD (0.200 vs. 0.211). In the conditional setting, PDD (coarse-scene condition) beats DiscreteDiff (same condition) on mIoU(V) by 3.2 points and mIoU(P) by 1.9 points, while the point-cloud-conditioned DiscreteDiff achieves a slightly lower MMD (0.261 vs. 0.274) but is worse on all segmentation metrics. On SemanticKITTI, PDD fine-tuned from CarlaSC (44.7 mIoU(V), MMD 0.11) outperforms DiscreteDiff trained from scratch on SemanticKITTI (29.1 mIoU(V), MMD 0.31) by a wide margin, demonstrating the cross-dataset transfer advantage of the multi-scale pyramid design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete diffusion framework of Austin et al. (NeurIPS 2021) from single-scale generation to a multi-scale pyramid architecture, directly borrowing the cascaded coarse-to-fine philosophy established in 2D image super-resolution (Ho et al., 2020; Ho et al., 2022). It departs from prior 3D scene diffusion work (Lee et al., 2023) that relies on a single large-scale model or external conditional signals (scene graphs, 2D maps), instead decomposing the unconditional generation problem into a sequence of smaller conditional sub-problems. The scene subdivision module and the cross-dataset transfer capability enabled by the multi-scale design are novel contributions not present in prior diffusion-based 3D generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Large-scale unconditional 3D outdoor scene generation is a bottleneck for downstream tasks in autonomous driving, VR, and robotics, and this paper demonstrates that a pyramid diffusion approach can produce semantically coherent scenes (mIoU ~68) without requiring auxiliary guidance signals. The cross-dataset transfer result—where a model trained on synthetic CarlaSC data outperforms a from-scratch model on real SemanticKITTI even without fine-tuning—suggests a practical path toward reducing the data bottleneck for real-world 3D scene synthesis. Open questions include scalability to much larger scene extents, integration with modern 3D representations beyond voxels, and whether the approach generalizes to indoor or more diverse outdoor environments.
