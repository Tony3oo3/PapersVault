## Problem

Quantizing image super-resolution networks to low bit-widths causes severe accuracy degradation because SR network activations exhibit large distribution mismatches across channels and input images (due to the absence of batch normalization), making a single fixed quantization range inadequate. Existing remedies—channel-wise quantization or input-adaptive dynamic modules—resolve the mismatch at test time but introduce non-negligible computational overhead that erodes the efficiency gains of quantization.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a pre-trained 32-bit SR network (EDSR, RDN, or SwinIR) and produces a quantized version at 2, 3, or 4-bit precision for both weights and activations of the high-level feature extraction module. At inference, the quantized network accepts a low-resolution RGB image and outputs a ×4 super-resolved image using only fixed, pre-determined quantization ranges—no per-image or per-channel adaptation is performed. The only additional storage is one scalar correction parameter per layer (≈0.06K–0.15K extra bytes), with zero extra bitOPs.

## Method

The paper proposes ODM, a quantization-aware training scheme with two key components. First, a cooperative mismatch regularization: the L2 distance between each activation and its quantized version is computed as a mismatch loss, but its gradient is scaled by the cosine similarity with the reconstruction-loss gradient (mapped to [0,1]), so the regularization only updates parameters when it aligns with reconstruction, suppressing updates during gradient conflict. Second, a weight clipping correction: instead of a fixed global policy (e.g., max) for the symmetric weight quantization range, a learnable per-layer scalar γ_w multiplies the j-th percentile of the current weight distribution, allowing each layer to adjust its range to avoid outlier-dominated grids. Together, these mechanisms reshape activation distributions to be quantization-friendly and select layer-appropriate weight ranges, all without any test-time dynamic modules.

## Results

On EDSR (×4 SR), ODM achieves 31.50 dB PSNR / 0.882 SSIM on Set5 at 2-bit, outperforming the 32-bit baseline (32.10 dB) by only 0.6 dB and surpassing the best prior method (DAQ, 31.01 dB) by 0.49 dB. At 4-bit, ODM reaches 32.00 dB on Set5, nearly closing the gap to the 32-bit network (32.10 dB). The gains are consistent across RDN (31.37 dB at 2-bit, +0.66 dB over DAQ) and SwinIR (31.44 dB at 2-bit, +0.43 dB over DDTB), with the largest improvements observed in the ultra-low 2-bit regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ODM consistently outperforms all three prior SR quantization methods (PAMS, DAQ, DDTB) across every bit-width (4, 3, 2-bit) and every tested architecture (EDSR, RDN, SwinIR). The advantage is most pronounced at 2-bit: on EDSR-Set5, ODM gains +1.99 dB over PAMS, +0.49 dB over DAQ, and +0.53 dB over DDTB; on RDN-Set5 the margin over DAQ widens to +0.66 dB. Against the concurrent work QuantSR (trained for 300K iterations), ODM* achieves +0.51 dB on 2-bit SRResNet-Set5 and +0.14 dB on 2-bit SwinIR-Set5. Notably, ODM achieves these gains without channel-wise quantization or input-adaptive modules, resulting in 0.5T fewer bitOPs than DAQ and ×31.7 smaller storage overhead than DDTB on EDSR, while PAMS—despite having the lowest computational cost—suffers severe degradation at 2-bit (29.51 dB vs. ODM's 31.50 dB on EDSR-Set5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the gradient-similarity mechanism from Du et al. [13] (adapting auxiliary losses via cosine similarity) and repurposes it to resolve a specific conflict in SR quantization: the mismatch regularization gradient opposing the reconstruction loss gradient. It departs from the dominant line of SR quantization work (PAMS, DAQ, DDTB) that handles distribution mismatch through test-time dynamic adaptation—channel-wise ranges or input-adaptive modules—by instead pre-shaping feature distributions during training so no dynamic module is needed at inference. The weight clipping correction further extends the standard max-policy for weight quantization ranges by introducing a per-layer learnable correction factor, addressing a layer-wise variation in quantization error that prior methods overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SR networks can be quantized to 2-bit with minimal accuracy loss (within ~0.6 dB of 32-bit) without the inference-time overhead of dynamic quantization modules, making low-bit SR deployment on hardware more feasible. The observation that naively adding mismatch regularization degrades reconstruction (−0.13 dB in ablation) and that the cooperative gating recovers and exceeds baseline (+0.51 dB) is a useful diagnostic for multi-objective training in quantization-aware settings. Open questions include whether the cooperative regularization principle generalizes to other low-level vision tasks (denoising, deblurring) or to fully quantized (weight+activation+gradient) settings, and whether the approach remains effective at even lower bit-widths (1-bit) or for video SR.
