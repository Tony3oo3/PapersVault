## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models frequently produce spelling errors—missing, incorrect, or extraneous characters—when rendering text, because standard text encoders (CLIP, T5) tokenize at the word/subword level rather than the character level, and the cross-attention mechanism suffers from catastrophic neglect of individual character tokens. This paper attributes the issue to a fundamental mismatch between the granularity of text conditioning and the per-character spatial structure required for accurate rendering.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 512×512 RGB image, a binary mask delineating the text region, and a target text sequence (up to 12 characters in experiments), and outputs a denoised image with the specified text rendered harmoniously within the masked region while preserving the surrounding visual context. The inpainting formulation means the unmasked background pixels are preserved, and the text is synthesized solely from the text condition and the local visual context. At inference, 50 Euler sampling steps with a classifier-free guidance scale of 5.0 are used, plus a per-timestep latent refinement pass.

## Method

UDiffText replaces the CLIP text encoder in Stable Diffusion v2.0 (inpainting) with a lightweight character-level encoder (~302M params) that maps individual characters through a learnable codebook into a transformer, trained via contrastive alignment with ViTSTR visual features plus a multi-label classification loss to ensure distinguishable per-character embeddings. During fine-tuning, only the 75.9M cross-attention parameters of the 891M U-Net are updated under a composite objective: denoising score matching loss, a local attention loss that penalizes attention mass outside character-level segmentation masks (with Gaussian smoothing to prevent over-concentration), and an auxiliary scene-text-recognition loss applied to the denoised output. At inference, a noised-latent refinement step (inspired by Attend-and-Excite) selects the best initial noise from N candidates and then, at each denoising timestep, performs a gradient update on the latent to maximize the minimum attention score across all character tokens within the mask, directly counteracting catastrophic neglect.

## Results

UDiffText achieves the highest sequence accuracy across all four evaluation datasets for both original-text reconstruction (SeqAcc-O: 90.3–93.3%) and substitute-text generation (SeqAcc-S: 79.0–84.3%), outperforming the strongest baseline TextDiffuser by 4.6–11.3 percentage points on SeqAcc-O and 2.7–18.6 points on SeqAcc-S. It also attains the best image quality with FID of 19.7 and LPIPS of 0.0574, compared to 34.1/0.0881 for TextDiffuser and 20.8/0.0634 for MOSTEL. In the T2I generation application, UDiffText improves average text rendering accuracy of SDXL from 8.0% to 60.0% on SimpleBench prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDiffText outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor TextDiffuser (NeurIPS 2024), UDiffText gains +10.3 pp on SeqAcc-O (ICDAR13: 91.0 vs 80.7) and +8.0 pp on SeqAcc-S (TextSeg: 84.3 vs 65.7), while also halving the FID (19.7 vs 34.1) and reducing LPIPS by 35% (0.0574 vs 0.0881). The GAN-based MOSTEL achieves reasonable FID (20.8) but lags severely on substitute-text accuracy (24.7–48.3% SeqAcc-S), indicating it struggles to render novel words in context. The raw Stable Diffusion inpainting baseline (SD-Inpainting) is the weakest, with SeqAcc-S below 7% across all datasets, confirming that off-the-shelf diffusion models cannot reliably render text. DiffSTE (dual-encoder diffusion) sits in the middle, with SeqAcc-S around 29–47%, but its FID of 52.8 reveals poor visual coherence. The ablation further shows that the character-level encoder alone lifts SeqAcc-O from 8.0% (base SD) to 40.0%, the local attention loss adds another 14 pp, the STR loss adds 11 pp, and the inference-time refinement contributes the final 11 pp to reach 76.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDiffText builds directly on the Stable Diffusion (v2.0) inpainting architecture, fine-tuning only its cross-attention blocks while replacing the CLIP text encoder with a purpose-built character-level encoder trained via contrastive learning against a ViTSTR scene text recognizer. The local attention loss draws on FastComposer's strategy of aligning cross-attention maps with spatial masks, and the inference-time noised latent refinement is motivated by Attend-and-Excite's semantic nursing. Among diffusion-based scene text synthesis methods (DiffSTE, TextDiffuser, GlyphDraw, GlyphControl), UDiffText is distinctive in treating the text encoder as the primary bottleneck and supervising attention at the individual character level rather than relying on rendered glyph images or segmentation masks as external conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically limiting failure mode of T2I diffusion models—spelling errors—and demonstrates that a lightweight (302M) character-level encoder plus targeted attention supervision can substantially close the gap without retraining the full backbone, updating only ~8.5% of U-Net parameters. The two-stage T2I pipeline (generate with SDXL, then inpaint text with UDiffText) improving text accuracy from 8% to 60% on SimpleBench prompts suggests a broadly applicable recipe for any T2I system. However, the method is limited to short text (≤12 characters in evaluation), cannot preserve the typographic style of existing text, and the evaluation is conducted on relatively small test sets (100 images per dataset), leaving open questions about scalability to longer passages, multilingual scripts, and diverse visual contexts.
