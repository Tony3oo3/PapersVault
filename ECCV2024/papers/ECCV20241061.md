## Problem

Existing interactive segmentation tools for biomedical imaging are either task-specific, require rigid interaction protocols (e.g., carefully placed clicks), or lack generalization to unseen modalities and anatomical structures. Researchers and clinicians frequently encounter novel segmentation tasks involving new regions of interest or image types, yet no single learning-based system can flexibly handle diverse interaction styles (scribbles, clicks, bounding boxes) across the full breadth of biomedical imaging without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241061/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each iterative step, the model receives a 2D biomedical image slice, a set of user interactions (positive/negative scribbles as pixel-level masks, positive/negative clicks as (x,y) coordinate pairs, and/or a bounding box), and the low-resolution logits of the previous prediction, and outputs a binary segmentation mask at the same spatial resolution. Scribbles are treated as thickened pixel regions (each scribbled pixel is effectively a click for the SAM variant), and the initial prediction is zero. The system is designed for 2D slices extracted from 3D volumes (using the slice with maximum label area or the middle slice).

## Method

ScribblePrompt is trained on 77 diverse biomedical datasets (54k+ scans, 16 image types, 711 labels) using a novel iterative training procedure that simulates k consecutive interaction steps, where corrections at step i are sampled from the error region between the previous prediction and ground truth. Two key training innovations enable generalization to unseen tasks: (1) synthetic label generation via superpixel partitioning, where with probability p_synth the ground truth is replaced by a randomly selected superpixel, and (2) a comprehensive interaction simulator that generates realistic scribbles (line, centerline, and contour types with random deformation and corruption), clicks (random, center, and interior-border strategies), and bounding boxes. The architecture is an 8-layer fully-convolutional UNet (192 features, PReLU, no BatchNorm) or a fine-tuned SAM ViT-b decoder, optimized for fast CPU inference (~0.27s per prediction). Training minimizes a linear combination of soft Dice and Focal Loss across all k iterative predictions.

## Results

In a user study with 16 experienced annotators, ScribblePrompt-UNet achieved 0.84 Dice (vs. 0.73 for SAM ViT-b, p=0.001) while reducing annotation time from 131.7 s to 94.2 s per segmentation (p=0.02), a 28% speedup with 15% higher Dice. On manually-collected scribbles (MedScribble, n=93), ScribblePrompt-SAM reaches 0.87 Dice and ScribblePrompt-UNet 0.84 Dice, outperforming all baselines. On the ACDC scribble dataset (n=1,140), ScribblePrompt-UNet achieves 0.84 Dice with HD95 of 1.80 px. Inference on a single CPU takes 0.27 s per prediction for ScribblePrompt-UNet, versus 13.59 s for SAM ViT-b and 130.79 s for SAM ViT-h.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ScribblePrompt outperforms all four baselines on both manual scribble benchmarks. Against SAM (ViT-b and ViT-h), ScribblePrompt-UNet gains 0.28–0.44 Dice on MedScribble (0.84 vs. 0.40/0.56) and 0.42–0.64 Dice on ACDC (0.84 vs. 0.20/0.42), reflecting SAM's poor generalization to scribble inputs it was never trained on. SAM-Med2D, despite being fine-tuned on 4.6M biomedical images, performs worse than vanilla SAM on ACDC (0.16–0.17 Dice) and still trails ScribblePrompt by 0.32–0.57 Dice on MedScribble. MedSAM (bounding-box only) reaches 0.70 Dice on both datasets but cannot exploit negative scribbles, causing it to miss structures with internal holes. MIDeepSeg with its CRF refinement step is the closest competitor (0.81 Dice on MedScribble, 0.73 on ACDC), yet ScribblePrompt-UNet still leads by 0.03 and 0.11 Dice respectively, while also being ~480× faster on CPU (0.27 s vs. >130 s for SAM ViT-h). In the user study, ScribblePrompt-UNet achieved 0.84 Dice in 94 s versus SAM ViT-b's 0.73 Dice in 132 s, and 15/16 participants preferred ScribblePrompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScribblePrompt extends the interactive segmentation paradigm from domain-specific biomedical models (MIDeepSeg, DeepIGeoS) and natural-image foundation models (SAM, SAM-Med2D, MedSAM) to a single generalist system that handles previously unseen tasks and modalities without retraining. It builds on ScribbleSeg's scribble simulation concepts for natural images and adapts iterative training strategies from natural-image interactive segmentation (Sofiiuk et al., Forte et al.) to the fragmented biomedical domain. A key departure from SAM-based approaches is the introduction of synthetic superpixel labels during training to prevent overfitting to specific anatomical tasks, combined with a multi-prompt training strategy (scribbles, clicks, bounding boxes) that prior biomedical interactive methods typically restricted to a single interaction type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.27-second CPU inference time (vs. >2 minutes for SAM ViT-h) makes interactive segmentation practical in resource-constrained clinical and research settings where data cannot be sent to a server, and the user study (28% time reduction, 15% Dice gain over SAM) demonstrates tangible annotation-efficiency gains for domain experts. The generalization to 608 unseen tasks across 8 modalities without retraining addresses a persistent bottleneck in biomedical imaging where new segmentation tasks arise constantly. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric segmentation, whether the synthetic-label strategy remains effective at larger scale, and whether the 2D slice-based evaluation captures the full complexity of clinical 3D annotation workflows.
