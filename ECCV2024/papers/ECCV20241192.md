## Problem

Existing Transformer-based multi-task visual grounding methods rely on the Transformer Encoder for cross-modal fusion, incurring quadratic computational cost O((N+L)²C) with respect to language length, which becomes prohibitive for long-context scenarios such as conversation-based reasoning segmentation. Additionally, most visual tokens correspond to background regions irrelevant to the single referred object, leading to redundant computation and attention distraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a visual backbone like ViT-B or Swin-B into N patch tokens) and a natural language query (processed by BERT into L linguistic tokens) as input. It simultaneously produces a 4-dimensional bounding box (x, y, w, h) for referring expression comprehension and a full-resolution binary segmentation mask (H×W) for referring expression segmentation. The key design constraint is that computational cost scales linearly with L rather than quadratically, and the number of visual tokens N is dynamically reduced per sample based on the target object's spatial extent.

## Method

EEVG replaces the conventional Transformer Encoder with a Decoder-only architecture for cross-modal fusion: visual tokens (concatenated with a learnable location token) serve as queries in self-attention and cross-attention, while linguistic tokens serve as keys and values in cross-attention, reducing complexity from O((N+L)²C) to O(N²C + NL·C). A parameter-free dynamic token elimination strategy computes attention scores between the location token and visual tokens at each Decoder layer, normalizes them, and removes tokens below a threshold α, with an adaptive spatial attention (ASA) module that averages attention scores over a (2k+1)² neighborhood to preserve object shape. A lightweight 2-layer MLP mask head (0.79M parameters, versus 8.1M for FPN-based heads) directly projects the remaining sparse 1-D token features into P²-dimensional spatial patches, which are then mapped to their original image positions, zero-padded at eliminated locations, and refined by a single 5×5 convolution to establish local pixel relationships. The framework is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining smooth-L1 + GIoU for detection and focal + dice for segmentation.

## Results

With a ViT-B backbone, EEVG achieves 88.08% accuracy on RefCOCO val (REC) and 78.23% mIoU on RefCOCO val (RES), outperforming the previous best ViT-B method VG-LAW by 1.46% and 2.61% respectively. In the pre-trained setting, EEVG (ViT-B) reaches 90.47% on RefCOCO val (REC) and 79.49% mIoU on RefCOCO val (RES), surpassing PolyFormer (Swin-B) by 0.74% and 3.53% on RES. On RefCOCOg, which contains longer and more complex expressions, EEVG shows the largest gains: +3.93% mIoU on RES test over VG-LAW. In speed, EEVG runs at 80.53 FPS, 28.19% faster than PolyFormer (62.82 FPS) and 15.14% faster than LAVT (69.94 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most recent SOTA, EEVG (ViT-B, pre-trained) outperforms PolyFormer (Swin-B) on RES across all splits (e.g., 79.49 vs 75.96 on RefCOCO val, 73.56 vs 69.36 on RefCOCOg val) while being competitive on REC (90.47 vs 89.73 on RefCOCO val). On REC, EEVG (Swin-B) slightly trails PolyFormer on RefCOCO+ testA/testB (87.34/74.00 vs 88.60/76.38) but leads on RefCOCO and RefCOCOg. Compared to VG-LAW [37] (the prior best ViT-B multi-task method), EEVG gains +1.46% REC and +2.61% RES on RefCOCO val, with the largest margin on RefCOCOg (+2.70% REC, +3.62% RES val). Against LAVT [43], EEVG is 15.14% faster in FPS (80.53 vs 69.94) and achieves higher RES mIoU. Older baselines (MAttNet, NMTree, MCN, TransVG, SeqTR, PVD) are consistently outperformed by 3–15+ points across all datasets and both tasks. The speed advantage over PolyFormer (28.19% faster) stems from the Decoder-only architecture and token elimination, while the performance gain on RefCOCOg highlights the benefit of linear (vs. quadratic) scaling with language length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Encoder-based paradigm in multi-task visual grounding (established by TransVG, RefTr, PolyFormer, and VG-LAW) by proposing the first Transformer Decoder-only framework for cross-modal vision-language fusion, where linguistic features serve as memory and visual features as queries. It repurposes token elimination ideas from image classification (Token Merging, Adaptive Token Sampling) into a dynamic, attention-score-driven strategy tailored to the variable-size nature of referring segmentation. The lightweight MLP mask head replaces the FPN-based architecture prevalent in prior RES works (EFN, LAVT, RefTr), shifting spatial prediction workload back into the Decoder itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic-to-linear scaling with language length is practically important as visual grounding moves toward LLM-era applications like conversation-based reasoning segmentation, where context windows can be hundreds of tokens long. The finding that a 0.79M-parameter MLP head outperforms an 8.11M FPN-based head—and even improves detection accuracy by ~2%—challenges the assumption that segmentation requires heavy convolutional post-processing. Open questions include whether the single-location-token design generalizes to multi-object grounding, how the dynamic elimination threshold behaves on very small or highly occluded referents, and whether the approach extends to truly long-form contexts (500+ tokens) beyond the 300-token experiments reported.
