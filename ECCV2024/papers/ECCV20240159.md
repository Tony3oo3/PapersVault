## Problem

3D hand poses are a compact, informative modality for recognizing fine-grained hand-object interactions, yet they remain largely unexplored for action recognition. Existing skeleton-based methods are designed for full-body poses where joints move independently and long-range spatiotemporal dependencies are critical, whereas hand joints are highly coupled (Pearson correlation 0.93) and lack a static reference joint. Moreover, hand poses alone cannot identify the objects being manipulated, and dense RGB video is too computationally expensive for resource-constrained AR/VR settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dense 3D hand pose sequence (21 joints × 2 hands × 3 coordinates per frame, temporally resized to 120 frames via linear interpolation) and a sparse set of RGB frames (as few as 1, up to 8, sampled at fixed intervals). Poses can originate from monochrome egocentric cameras (e.g., MEgATrack) or wearable sensors, while RGB frames come from fixed or egocentric views. The model outputs three labels via separate class tokens: an action label (verb + object), a verb label (attending only to pose features), and an object label (attending only to RGB features).

## Method

HandFormer factorizes the input into K micro-actions, each comprising N=15 dense pose frames and a single RGB frame, reflecting the observation that hand motion is best captured at short temporal scales. A Trajectory Encoder processes each joint's 3D trajectory (3×N) through a parameter-shared TCN to produce local trajectory tokens, supplemented by a Global Wrist Token from a separate TCN over the full 6D wrist pose sequence; self-attention over these tokens followed by spatiotemporal average pooling yields a per-micro-action pose embedding. A Multimodal Tokenizer fuses the pose embedding with a frozen ViT/ResNet RGB feature via a shared-space MLP projection, and a Temporal Transformer aggregates the resulting RGB and pose tokens across micro-actions with positional and modality embeddings. Training uses cross-entropy for action classification plus auxiliary verb/object losses (each attending to its relevant modality only) and a Feature Anticipation Loss that forces the model to predict the next micro-action's RGB feature from the current pose+RGB, encouraging the pose branch to encode state-change information. This design achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on Assembly101 and H2O while using 5× fewer FLOPs than prior skeleton-based methods in the pose-only setting.

## Results

On Assembly101, HandFormer-B/21×8 (Pose+RGB) achieves 41.06% action accuracy, 69.23% verb accuracy, and 51.17% object accuracy, outperforming the strongest late-fusion baseline MS-G3D+TSM (39.74% action) and the multimodal RGBPoseConv3D (33.61% action). The pose-only variant achieves 65.33% verb accuracy with only 4.2 GFLOPs, surpassing MS-G3D (63.46%) and ISTA-Net (62.70%) at 5× fewer FLOPs. On H2O, the full multimodal model reaches 93.39% action accuracy, exceeding H2OTR (90.90%) and ISTA-Net (89.09%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the pose-only setting on Assembly101, HandFormer-B/21 achieves 65.33% verb accuracy, beating MS-G3D (63.46%) by 1.87% and ISTA-Net (62.70%) by 2.63%, while using only 4.2 GFLOPs versus 21.2 and 35.2 respectively (≈5× fewer). For multimodal action recognition, HandFormer (41.06% action) surpasses the late-fusion MS-G3D+TSM baseline (39.74%) by 1.32% and the dedicated multimodal RGBPoseConv3D (33.61%) by 7.45%, indicating that general-purpose skeleton+RGB architectures do not transfer well to hand poses. On H2O, HandFormer (93.39%) outperforms H2OTR (90.90%) by 2.49% and ISTA-Net (89.09%) by 4.30%, though ISTA-Net additionally uses 6D object pose as input. The pose-only HandFormer also exceeds TSM's RGB-only action accuracy (28.80% vs. 35.27% is not directly comparable since TSM uses RGB; however, HandFormer with a single RGB frame at 35.46% matches TSM's 35.27% while providing substantially better verb accuracy). Overall, the paper demonstrates that 3D hand pose is a highly efficient and accurate modality for hand-object interaction recognition, particularly for verb understanding, and that combining it with sparse RGB frames yields state-of-the-art results at a fraction of the compute cost of dense video models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of skeleton-based action recognition and video understanding by repurposing transformer and TCN components for a modality (3D hand poses) that prior skeleton methods were not designed for. It departs from full-body skeleton approaches (MS-G3D, ISTA-Net, ST-GCN) by identifying that hand joints are highly coupled (Pearson 0.93 vs. 0.33 for full-body) and lack a static global reference, motivating a trajectory-based micro-action encoding rather than long-range spatiotemporal graph convolutions. The multimodal fusion strategy—dense poses for motion plus sparse RGB for semantics—draws conceptually from anticipative video transformers and two-stream architectures but applies them at a much lower temporal resolution for RGB, and the feature anticipation loss is adapted from prior video prediction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance for AR/VR and wearable applications is substantial: a 5× FLOPs reduction over existing skeleton methods while matching or exceeding accuracy, plus the ability to replace multi-view RGB with a single view plus poses, makes always-on hand-object interaction recognition feasible on resource-constrained hardware. The cross-view generalization property (training on one view, testing on another, outperforming a model trained directly on the target view) is a unique advantage of 3D poses that RGB-only methods cannot offer. Open questions include handling noisy or out-of-view pose estimates more explicitly, moving beyond uniform RGB frame sampling toward adaptive selection, and validating the approach on larger-scale or more diverse hand-interaction datasets beyond Assembly101 and H2O.
