## Problem

Understanding what individual units in a CNN's penultimate layer encode remains challenging: existing visualization methods are subjective, limited to a handful of features, or require computationally expensive (re-)training of generative models coupled to the classifier. No prior approach enables an automatic, objective, and systematic quantification of learned representations across all several thousand units simultaneously, nor does it reveal how distributed representations form a classifier's decision boundary in human-interpretable terms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242271/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN classifier (e.g., ResNet-50) and a pre-trained StyleGAN-XL as fixed components, and consumes unit activation perturbations in the classifier's 2,048-dimensional penultimate layer. The linking network is trained on 5,000 (w, r) pairs per class via simple linear regression. Outputs include sequences of generated images along perturbation trajectories, 45 semantic quantification measures (5 metrics × 9 segmentation labels) computed at 128×128 resolution, a label sparsity score per unit, counterfactual images with identity-preserved perturbations, and PUMP-based vector fields of local image changes.

## Method

The core contribution is a linking network—a linear regression model trained on 5,000 (w, r) pairs per class—that maps the classifier's penultimate-layer activation r to StyleGAN-XL's W-space, enabling a full cycle w → I → r → w̃ → Ĩ. This works because representation similarity analysis and k-means clustering confirm a congruent semantic ordering between R and W, making a simple linear mapping sufficient. For automatic quantification, the pipeline combines an unsupervised PUMP-based motion-tracking step (computing affine alignment then local vector fields) with a supervised few-shot segmentation model (5 labeled images per class, 3 convolutional layers, 100 epochs) that extracts area, luminance, entropy, eccentricity, and angle for each of 9 semantic labels. Single-unit analysis perturbs each of the 2,048 units independently and computes label sparsity and tSNE embeddings to reveal disentangled versus superimposed representations. For decision-boundary analysis, the method optimizes a perturbation Δr via gradient descent (up to 2,000 steps) minimizing a loss that maximizes target-class logit while penalizing original-class logit and W-space identity drift, yielding human-interpretable counterfactual trajectories.

## Results

The linking network (linear regression) achieves near-zero MSE in the StyleGAN-XL W-space after a full cycle (w→I→r→w̃→Ĩ), significantly outperforming a random-image baseline, and the perceptual MoCov2 distance between original and cycled images confirms high visual fidelity. Across all 2,048 units in the ResNet-50 penultimate layer, label sparsity follows a long-tail distribution with a small subset of highly sparse (disentangled) units encoding specific concepts such as leg length or ear size. Class-relevant units (average prediction-probability change > 0.15) are identified per class, with a few units shared across multiple dog breeds. Along counterfactual trajectories (e.g., Pug→Chihuahua), the quantification pipeline reveals continuous increases in ear area and luminance saturation after the decision boundary, features that standard similarity metrics (MSE, LPIPS) fail to capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline comparison is with GradCAM [58] for interpreting the classifier's decision boundary (Fig. 7B). GradCAM produces saliency maps that may emphasize less interpretable properties such as background features, whereas the proposed linking-network-plus-segmentation pipeline resolves specific semantic concept changes (e.g., ear area, head luminance, body eccentricity) at the decision boundary. The paper does not report a single scalar metric for this comparison; instead, it argues qualitatively that the segmentation-based quantification provides a 'more comprehensive picture' of decision-relevant features than GradCAM's gradient-based localization. For the linking network itself, the only quantitative baseline is a random-image control, against which the trained linear mapping achieves significantly lower MSE in W-space and lower MoCov2 perceptual distance in the image domain (Fig. 3C). No other interpretability methods (e.g., LIME, DeepLIFT, TCAV) are directly benchmarked, as the paper positions its contribution as a new analysis pipeline rather than a drop-in replacement for existing saliency tools.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior GAN-based interpretability work (Lang et al. 2021, Singla et al. 2022) that required incorporating a GAN into the classifier's training loop or extensive retraining, by instead using a simple linear linking network between a pre-trained ResNet-50 and a pre-trained StyleGAN-XL, decoupling the two training processes entirely. It builds on the semantic structure of StyleGAN-XL's multi-class latent space (Sauer et al. 2022) and repurposes few-shot GAN-based segmentation (Tritrong et al. 2021) and unsupervised local descriptor matching (PUMP) as automatic quantification tools, going beyond the visual-only inspection typical of GradCAM or saliency-map methods. The approach also connects to the long-standing debate on sparse vs. superimposed feature representations by providing a systematic, unit-by-unit quantification of label sparsity across thousands of units.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a computationally inexpensive, systematic pipeline to analyze all ~2,000 units in a classifier's penultimate layer simultaneously, moving beyond the subjective visual inspection of a handful of features that has limited prior interpretability work. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to Vision Transformers (where regional representations are encoded differently), whether it can be applied to real images once generative models improve, and how the discovered representational geometry (sparsity, superposition) relates to adversarial robustness and model performance. The finding that a simple linear mapping suffices between two architecturally distinct spaces suggests a broader principle about the geometric compatibility of learned representations across model families.
