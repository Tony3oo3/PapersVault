## Problem

Contextualized Image Captioning (CIC) generates captions conditioned on a full textual context, but a detailed context often supports many equally valid captions, leaving no mechanism for users to direct the model toward specific aspects they care about. Simply truncating the context to isolate highlights discards essential background cues, degrading caption quality. This paper introduces Controllable Contextualized Image Captioning (Ctrl-CIC), which lets users specify highlight segments within the context to steer caption generation while preserving the full context as background knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (encoded via CLIP-large into a single image token prepended to the text sequence), a full textual context C (e.g., a Wikipedia article), and one or more highlight segments H, each a continuous subsequence of C at word-level granularity or above. The output is a single contextualized caption that emphasizes the highlighted content while remaining grounded in the broader context. Highlights can range from individual words to complete sentences, and multiple non-overlapping highlights per sample are supported.

## Method

Two lightweight controllers are built on a LongT5-base encoder-decoder, both trained in a weakly-supervised manner using highlights derived computationally from existing CIC data via token-level cosine similarity between context embeddings (T5-large) and the target caption embedding. P-Ctrl selects words whose relevance score exceeds a threshold θ, assembles them into a <SEP>-delimited prefix, and fine-tunes the LLM to generate captions conditioned on this prefix, exploiting autoregressive decoding to internalize highlight-driven generation. R-Ctrl normalizes relevance scores to unit-interval weights and multiplies them element-wise onto encoder token embeddings during training; at inference, a separate weight predictor (LM encoder + linear + sigmoid) produces per-token weights, and highlighted tokens receive an additive boost α to sharpen focus. Neither controller modifies the base architecture, and both remain adaptable to standard CIC by omitting the highlight-specific conditioning.

## Results

On Wiki-Web2Mfull with a single highlighted segment, R-Ctrl achieves the highest Recall (64.88) and P-Ctrl achieves the highest Div-1 (10.23) and Div-2 (10.54), substantially outperforming all baselines on highlight-relevance metrics. On the multi-highlight setting (Table 2), P-Ctrl reaches CS-S of 62.94 and R-Ctrl reaches 63.00, both exceeding the CIC GT (55.89) and LLaVA-1.5 (51.88). Under GPT-4V evaluation, R-Ctrl scores 1.73 on Highlight Relevance and 1.30 on Overall Quality (relative to CIC GT = 1.0), while P-Ctrl scores 1.64 and 1.21 respectively, confirming strong controllability with only ~5% of LLaVA-1.5's parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CIC GT reference, both P-Ctrl and R-Ctrl dramatically improve Recall (57.07 and 64.88 vs. 8.28) and diversity (Div-1: 10.23/9.21 vs. 3.24), but sacrifice some image consistency as measured by CLIPScore (60.91/63.60 vs. 68.66), reflecting the trade-off of focusing on highlights. Compared to LLaVA-1.5 (zero-shot), the proposed methods achieve roughly 1.6–1.8× higher Recall and 1.4–1.5× higher Div-1/Div-2, while LLaVA-1.5 retains a CLIPScore advantage (65.56 vs. 60.91/63.60) and a higher GPT-4V Image Consistency score (1.05 vs. 0.75/0.71). Against the LongT5-Ext and LongT5-Tune baselines, both controllers yield 3–4× higher Recall and 1.7–1.8× higher diversity scores, demonstrating that simple context truncation is insufficient for fine-grained highlight control. GPT-4 (text-only, no image) achieves the highest Recall (86.10) and GPT-4V Highlight Relevance (2.81), but the paper positions it as a reference upper bound rather than a direct competitor since it does not process the image. Overall, R-Ctrl edges out P-Ctrl on Recall and GPT-4V Highlight Relevance, while P-Ctrl leads on diversity metrics, and both outperform LLaVA-1.5 on the core controllability metrics with far fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Contextualized Image Captioning (CIC) from Burns et al. (EMNLP 2023), which formalized captioning an image given its surrounding textual context, by introducing user-defined highlights that direct the model's emphasis within that context. Methodologically, it draws on controllable text generation techniques—prefix prompting and embedding recalibration—adapted to the multimodal captioning setting, while leveraging CLIP for image encoding and LongT5 as the backbone. The evaluation framework builds on GPT-4V-based comparative scoring (inspired by LLaVA's evaluation and G-Eval's chain-of-thought prompting) to assess controllability without requiring human-annotated highlight-caption pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ctrl-CIC addresses a practical gap: real-world contexts (Wikipedia articles, news) contain multiple valid caption angles, and users need a mechanism to steer which aspect is emphasized without truncating the full context. The weakly-supervised highlight construction via token-level relevance scores is a pragmatic solution to the annotation bottleneck, though it may not fully capture human intent. Open questions include whether the controllability generalizes beyond Wikipedia-style contexts, whether the CLIPScore drop (reduced image consistency) can be mitigated, and whether adaptive recalibration strategies can resolve R-Ctrl's repetitive highlight generation. The GPT-4V evaluation pipeline is a reusable contribution for assessing controllable multimodal generation more broadly.
