## Problem

Applying 2D diffusion-based text editing to individual frames of a NeRF scene produces multiview-inconsistent results, and existing methods like Instruct-NeRF2NeRF rely on slow iterative NeRF optimization to resolve these inconsistencies, leading to blurry textures and poor text alignment. The paper identifies that NeRF scene geometry (depth) provides a natural mechanism to unify 2D edits across views, but no prior method explicitly leverages this geometry during the diffusion editing process itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240285/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed NeRF (with its posed input images at 512×512, bilinearly upscaled to 1024×1024 for diffusion generation), per-view editing masks computed via 3D-consistent segmentation (unprojecting per-view SAM/Grounding DINO masks into 3D and reprojecting back), and a text prompt. It outputs a re-optimized NeRF scene that renders multiview-consistent edited images; the pipeline also supports 3D object insertion by rendering TSDF-based depth and occlusion-aware masks for the inserted object as additional ControlNet inputs.

## Method

DATENeRF conditions a Stable Diffusion inpainting pipeline (Blended Diffusion) on NeRF-derived depth maps via ControlNet, replacing the input-image conditioning used by Instruct-NeRF2NeRF to allow more drastic edits while preserving geometric alignment. The key novelty is a projection inpainting scheme: edited pixels from a reference view are reprojected onto subsequent views using NeRF depth, and a hybrid inpainting strategy preserves these reprojected pixels for the first N=5 denoising steps before switching to full-mask inpainting for the remaining steps, balancing geometric consistency with visual quality. A 3D-consistent segmentation step aggregates per-view masks through NeRF geometry to produce clean, occlusion-aware editing regions. After all views are edited, the NeRF is re-optimized in two stages: 1000 iterations on the full edited image set, followed by iterative dataset updates with moderate noise (0.5–0.8) for detail refinement, achieving convergence in ~400 iterations versus ~3000 for Instruct-NeRF2NeRF.

## Results

On 24 text-based edits across diverse NeRF scenes, DATENeRF (full method with ControlNet + projection inpainting) achieves a CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity of 0.1866 and CLIP Direction Consistency of 0.7069, the best on both metrics among all compared methods. In terms of convergence, DATENeRF produces a clearly edited NeRF by iteration 400 using only 87 diffusion-based image edits, whereas Instruct-NeRF2NeRF requires 300 edits and 3,000 iterations to reach qualitatively similar results. Each full experiment (4,000 NeRF iterations) takes approximately 20 minutes on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DATENeRF outperforms both baselines on both quantitative metrics. Against Instruct-NeRF2NeRF (the primary baseline), the full method improves CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity from 0.1407 (Instruct-Pix2Pix variant) or 0.1330 (ControlNet variant) to 0.1866, and CLIP Direction Consistency from 0.6349/0.6799 to 0.7069. Against ViCA-NeRF, DATENeRF gains 0.0183 in text-image similarity (0.1866 vs. 0.1683) and 0.0088 in direction consistency (0.7069 vs. 0.6981). Qualitatively, DATENeRF produces sharper high-frequency textures (e.g., zebra stripes, checkerboard patterns, tie-dye) that both baselines blur out, and it converges roughly 7× faster in NeRF iterations (400 vs. 3,000) while using fewer diffusion inferences (87 vs. 300 image edits). The ablation showing 'Ours w/o projection' confirms that projection inpainting is the key differentiator: without it, the ControlNet-based edits are geometrically aligned but photometrically inconsistent, preventing clean NeRF convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

DATENeRF builds directly on Instruct-NeRF2NeRF's text-based NeRF editing framework but departs from its core strategy: rather than relying on iterative NeRF optimization to resolve inconsistencies between independently edited views, it explicitly uses NeRF depth geometry to enforce multiview consistency at the image-editing stage. The method repurposes ControlNet (originally for 2D text-to-image generation) as a depth-conditioned inpainting backbone, replacing IN2N's image-conditioned Instruct-Pix2Pix, and introduces a hybrid projection-inpainting scheme that reprojects edited pixels across views before letting diffusion refine the full masked region. This positions the work as a geometry-aware refinement of the diffusion-based NeRF editing paradigm, complementing ViCA-NeRF's latent-code blending approach with a pixel-space reprojection strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DATENeRF achieves qualitatively similar results to Instruct-NeRF2NeRF in roughly 1/7 the NeRF iterations (400 vs. 3000) and 1/3 the diffusion inferences (87 vs. 300), making text-based NeRF editing substantially more tractable. The depth-conditioned ControlNet + projection inpainting pipeline is modular and extensible (demonstrated with Canny edges and 3D object compositing), suggesting it could become a standard component in NeRF editing toolkits. Key limitations remain: the method cannot alter scene geometry, depends on NeRF reconstruction quality, does not model view-dependent effects, and raises ethical concerns about realistic manipulation of human faces. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to 3D Gaussian Splatting representations and whether the hybrid inpainting schedule (N=5) generalizes across scene complexity.
