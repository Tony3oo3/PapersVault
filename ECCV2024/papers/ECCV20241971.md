## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to place objects at user-desired positions, forcing users to generate dozens of images per prompt. The paper identifies the root cause as a spatial mismatch between where 'winning tickets' (pixel blocks in initial noise predisposed to denoise into specific concepts) happen to appear and where the prompt requires them, and notes that noise initialization has been largely neglected in the diffusion literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241971/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt listing objects and user-specified bounding-box regions for each object, plus a pre-built collection of lottery tickets (4×4 pixel blocks scored against category pairs via cross-attention). It produces a 64×64 latent-space initial noise image constructed from selected winning tickets placed in the designated regions, which is then fed into standard Stable Diffusion v1.4 (50 DDIM steps) without any modification to the denoising pipeline. The constructed initial noise is explicitly non-Gaussian, yet the model still generates high-quality images.

## Method

The core mechanism identifies 'winning tickets' by partitioning random 64×64 noise into 256 4×4 blocks and reading their cross-attention scores at the first denoising step under contrastive two-object prompts (e.g., 'a c₁ and a c₂'), where a high softmax-normalized score for c₁ over c₂ signals a strong denoising tendency toward c₁. A large collection of such scored blocks is built by running single-step U-Net passes over many noise images and all category pairs, then winning tickets are selected via a threshold criterion requiring the block to outscore all other target concepts. The initial noise is assembled by randomly filling each user-specified region with the corresponding concept's winning tickets (reusing blocks if needed) and filling the remainder with background tickets (blocks scoring low on all concepts). Two key properties are empirically verified: tickets retain their concept association when transplanted to a different noise image (pixel values, not spatial position, carry the signal), and tickets selected under simple prompts remain effective under richer descriptive prompts, enabling prompt-agnostic pre-collection.

## Results

On MS COCO 2017 (6000 samples with 1–2 objects), SemI alone achieves an overall IoU of 0.26 and a layout success rate (R_suc, IoU>0.5) of 21.94%, comparable to the best existing layout-guidance method Soft (IoU 0.27, R_suc 22.31%) and clearly superior to vanilla Stable Diffusion (IoU 0.19, R_suc 12.14%). When combined with Soft, SemI boosts IoU to 0.31 and R_suc to 28.73%; combining with both Soft and Swap yields the best overall IoU of 0.32 and R_suc of 29.29%. For medium-sized objects, SemI alone already outperforms all baselines (R_suc_m = 24.68% vs. 22.38% for Soft and 22.75% for Swap).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemI alone is on par with Soft [23] (IoU 0.26 vs. 0.27; R_suc 21.94% vs. 22.31%) and Swap [24] (IoU 0.26 vs. 0.26; R_suc 21.94% vs. 21.58%), while clearly outperforming Paint [3] (IoU 0.26 vs. 0.24; R_suc 21.94% vs. 17.56%) and vanilla Stable Diffusion [32] (IoU 0.26 vs. 0.19; R_suc 21.94% vs. 12.14%). For medium-sized objects specifically, SemI leads all standalone baselines (R_suc_m 24.68% vs. 22.38% for Soft, 22.75% for Swap, 14.05% for Paint). The key advantage emerges in combination: SemI + Soft lifts Soft's IoU from 0.27 to 0.31 and R_suc from 22.31% to 28.73%, and SemI + Soft + Swap achieves the best overall IoU of 0.32 and R_suc of 29.29%, a ~7-point absolute gain over Soft alone. This demonstrates that winning-ticket initialization is orthogonal to and synergistic with cross-attention mask-based guidance methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the initial-noise manipulation idea from Mao et al. [24] (ACM MM 2023), which was limited to a single noise image and a known prompt, by demonstrating that winning tickets transfer across both images and prompts. Conceptually, it repurposes the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis from network pruning [10] to the denoising context, reframing generation failure as a spatial mismatch between winning-ticket locations and desired object placement. Unlike prior layout-to-image methods (Paint, Soft, Swap) that intervene during the denoising process via cross-attention masks, this work targets the initialization step, which the authors note has been 'long neglected,' and shows the two axes are orthogonal and complementary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pre-trained diffusion models tolerate non-Gaussian initial noise while still producing high-quality, well-guided images challenges a foundational assumption about the forward process and opens a practical design space for initialization-based control without retraining. Limitations remain: winning tickets selected by category name alone can impose unwanted attributes (e.g., a red bus), extremely small objects are still difficult to place, and extreme non-Gaussian deviation degrades image quality. Open questions include whether the winning-ticket property generalizes beyond SD v1.4 to other architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), whether a principled calibration of the constructed noise to the Gaussian manifold can eliminate quality artifacts, and whether the cross-prompt transferability holds for compositional or abstract concepts rather than simple object nouns.
