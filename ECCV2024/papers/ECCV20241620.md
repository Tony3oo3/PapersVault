## Problem

Existing camera-based 3D occupancy prediction methods rely on dense, uniform volume grids (e.g., BEVFormer), which treat all scene regions equally and can only infer the entire scene at once. This uniform sampling prevents adaptive focus on challenging or task-relevant areas, and the fixed grid structure makes it impossible to predict occupancy beyond the predefined perception range without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241620/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6 surround-view RGB images (original resolution) from the nuScenes dataset along with known camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters. Rather than producing a fixed [200×200×16] voxel grid, the output is a set of per-point semantic class predictions (18 classes) defined at arbitrary 3D coordinates called Points of Interest (PoIs). These PoIs can be standard grid centers for benchmarking, adaptively sampled locations for refinement, or manually placed points anywhere in 3D space (e.g., 200 m from the ego-vehicle), giving the output a flexible, non-uniform spatial structure.

## Method

OSP replaces the dense volume decoder with a point-based Transformer decoder that operates on sparse 3D point queries. Each PoI is encoded via normalized coordinates passed through sin/cos positional encoding and a small MLP, then processed by three decoder layers, each containing Point Cross-Attention (PCA) and Group Point Cross-Attention (GPCA). PCA projects each 3D query onto the relevant camera views using intrinsic/extrinsic parameters and performs deformable cross-attention with the corresponding 2D image features (from ResNet101 + FPN, d=256), while GPCA adaptively oversamples M=8000 local points around each PoI via a linear layer to capture neighborhood context. The framework supports three PoI strategies—standard grids, adaptive oversampling during training, and manual placement for out-of-range inference—and can be plugged into a frozen volume-based backbone (BEVFormer) to refine low-confidence regions, achieving 39.4 mIoU on Occ3D-nuScenes.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes benchmark, OSP achieves 39.41 mIoU with standard grid sampling, outperforming the paper's BEVFormer re-implementation (37.84 mIoU) by 1.57 mIoU and the strongest prior method BEVDet-stereo (38.0 mIoU) by 1.41 mIoU. When used as a plugin to refine BEVFormer's predictions (BEVFormer w/ OSP†), the combined system reaches 41.21 mIoU, surpassing all standalone methods. OSP also demonstrates unique flexibility: it predicts occupancy beyond the standard 80 m × 80 m scene (23.00 mIoU at 60–80 m) and supports adaptive sampling that reduces computation to 0.93× while retaining 39.08 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSP outperforms all nine baselines on the Occ3D-nuScenes mIoU metric. Against the closest competitors, OSP gains +1.57 mIoU over the paper's BEVFormer re-implementation (37.84) and +1.41 mIoU over BEVDet-stereo (38.0), with particularly large margins on small-object categories: bicycle (27.06 vs. 20.28), pedestrian (29.37 vs. 25.39), and traffic cone (29.68 vs. 26.16). Against earlier volume-based methods, the gap widens substantially: +3.31 mIoU over OpenOccupancy (36.1), +5.01 over SurroundOcc (34.4), +10.88 over CTF-Occ (28.53), and +17.48 over OccFormer (21.93). The plugin configuration (BEVFormer w/ OSP†, 41.21 mIoU) surpasses every standalone method, including OSP itself, confirming that adaptive refinement of low-confidence regions adds value beyond the point-based decoder alone. OSP also uniquely supports inference beyond the trained scene boundary (23.00 mIoU at 60–80 m), a capability none of the volume-based baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSP departs from the dominant dense volume-based paradigm (BEVFormer, BEVDet, OccFormer) that uniformly samples the entire scene into fixed-resolution voxel grids, instead adopting a sparse point-query formulation inherited from 3D detection (DETR3D, PETR) where 3D queries interact with 2D image features via camera projection. The core architectural mechanism—deformable cross-attention between 3D point queries and multi-view 2D features—directly builds on BEVFormer's deformable attention, but repurposes it for per-point occupancy prediction rather than BEV grid completion. The paper also positions itself against 3D Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) methods, noting that occupancy prediction targets visible regions in dynamic scenes rather than inferring occluded geometry in static ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The point-based representation offers a practical flexibility that volume-based methods structurally cannot provide: inference at arbitrary locations, adaptive resampling of low-confidence regions as a plug-in refinement, and prediction beyond the trained scene boundary without retraining. However, the 'beyond scene' results (23 mIoU at 60–80 m when trained on 0–60 m) are still modest, and the overall mIoU gain over a well-tuned BEVFormer baseline (~1.5 points) is incremental. Open questions include whether the adaptive oversampling strategy generalizes to much larger or higher-resolution scenes, whether the plugin refinement can be made end-to-end differentiable, and how the approach scales when the number of PoIs grows to match dense grid counts.
