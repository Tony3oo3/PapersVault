## Problem

Learning-based SNN training suffers from an accuracy gap relative to ANNs due to imprecise surrogate gradients for discrete spikes. Existing knowledge distillation methods (KDSNN, LaSNN) that directly align discrete SNN features with continuous ANN features struggle on complex datasets like ImageNet and with Transformer-based SNNs, because forcing discrete features to imitate continuous ones is fundamentally mismatched.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241325/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) or DVS event streams (CIFAR10-DVS) and produces a class label after T inference time-steps (typically 4). During training, the student SNN's intermediate feature before the last layer is averaged over the time-step dimension to yield a single spatial feature map, which is then blurred and restored for distillation against the corresponding teacher ANN feature. A pre-trained ANN (or a higher-time-step SNN for neuromorphic data) serves as the teacher, providing both logits and intermediate features.

## Method

BKDSNN introduces Blurred Knowledge Distillation (BKD), which bridges the discrete-to-continuous feature gap by first masking the time-averaged student SNN feature with a randomly sampled binary matrix B (generated by thresholding a uniform random matrix at ratio λ), then applying a lightweight restoration block G (two convolutional layers with a ReLU) to recover a continuous-valued feature that mimics the teacher ANN feature. The L2 loss between the restored student feature and the teacher feature is applied only to the intermediate feature before the last layer, rather than layer-wise as in prior work. This feature-based BKD loss is combined with a temperature-scaled logits-based distillation loss to form a mixed distillation objective, which is added to the standard cross-entropy task loss. The key insight is that blurring removes the discrete spike structure while the restoration block re-encodes sufficient information to approximate the continuous ANN representation, making the distillation signal more compatible with spiking neurons.

## Results

On ImageNet with 4 time-steps, BKDSNN achieves 72.32% top-1 accuracy with SEW ResNet-50 (CNN), surpassing the prior SOTA (SEW ResNet at 67.78%) by 4.51%. For Transformer-based SNNs, BKDSNN reaches 79.93% with Sformer-8-768+CML (+2.10% over prior SOTA) and 80.93% with Mformer-8-512 (+0.93% over Meta-SpikeFormer). On CIFAR-10/100, BKDSNN attains 96.06%/81.26% with Sformer-4-384, and on the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset it improves over prior SOTA by 1.0% (4 timesteps) and 0.7% (8 timesteps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BKDSNN outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. Against the strongest prior learning-based SNN (SEW ResNet [14]), BKDSNN gains +4.51% on ImageNet R-50, +2.18% on R-18, and +0.22–0.74% on CIFAR-10/100. Against the prior KD-based SNN methods KDSNN [53] and LaSNN [21], BKDSNN improves by +2.18%/+4.06%/+4.51% on ImageNet R-18/R-34/R-50 and by +0.29%/+1.03% on CIFAR-10/100 with Sformer-4-384, confirming that the blurred restoration mechanism is substantially more effective than direct layer-wise feature distillation. For Transformer-based SNNs, BKDSNN beats Sformer+CML [62] (the prior SOTA) by +0.86%/+0.70%/+2.10% on ImageNet Sformer-8-384/512/768, and surpasses Meta-SpikeFormer [57] by +1.21%/+0.72%/+0.93% on Mformer-8-256/384/512. On the neuromorphic CIFAR10-DVS dataset, BKDSNN leads KDSNN and LaSNN by +1.0–1.7% (CNN) and +1.0–0.7% (Transformer) at 4 and 8 timesteps respectively. The conversion-based baseline QCFS [2] requires 32 time-steps to reach 69.37% on R-34, whereas BKDSNN achieves 71.24% at only 4 time-steps, demonstrating both higher accuracy and 8× lower latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of knowledge distillation for SNN training initiated by KDSNN [53] and LaSNN [21], which applied layer-wise feature-based distillation between ANN teachers and SNN students. The key departure is the identification of a fundamental mismatch: prior methods directly distill discrete SNN features toward continuous ANN features, which the authors argue is implausible. BKDSNN introduces a random blurred masking matrix and a small restoration block (two conv layers + ReLU) to map the discrete student features into a continuous space before comparison, and applies this only to the intermediate feature before the last layer rather than across all layers. The method is further combined with standard logits-based distillation to form a mixed distillation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the accuracy gap between learning-based SNNs and their ANN counterparts is particularly acute on large-scale datasets (ImageNet) and Transformer-based architectures, where prior KD methods (KDSNN, LaSNN) provide only marginal gains. BKDSNN's 4.51% top-1 improvement on ImageNet with ResNet-50 and consistent gains across CNN/Transformer models and neuromorphic datasets suggest that the discrete-continuous feature mismatch was a previously underappreciated bottleneck. Open questions include whether the blurred restoration mechanism extends to conversion-based SNN training (which the authors mention as future work) and whether the single-layer distillation location generalizes to architectures with very different intermediate feature semantics.
