## Problem

Evaluating vision-language models for autonomous driving has relied on n-gram-based metrics (BLEU, METEOR, CIDEr) that poorly capture semantic correctness, while GPT-4-based evaluation is too slow for iterative development. Additionally, existing driving QA datasets focus on simple object presence or one-word answers, lacking the free-form reasoning, action justification, and attention questions needed to probe genuine driving understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242061/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 frames sampled at 1 Hz from a 4-second front-camera video clip (squashed to 224×224 to preserve full context) along with a free-form text question, and generates a free-form text answer averaging 17.2 words. For evaluation, Lingo-Judge operates purely on text—taking the question, a human ground-truth answer, and the model's predicted answer—and outputs a per-sample correctness probability, running over the full 1,000-question evaluation set in ~10 seconds on an A100 GPU.

## Method

The LingoQA Baseline architecture encodes each frame with a CLIP ViT vision encoder, passes features through a Q-Former (initialized from BLIP-2) to produce fixed-size visual tokens, and concatenates tokens from all 5 frames (late-fusion) with the chat history and question before feeding them into a Vicuna-1.5-7B LLM that autoregressively generates the answer. Training follows a two-stage recipe: Stage 1 pre-trains the LLM's self-attention layers, the vision encoder's QKV projections, all Q-Former parameters, and the linear projection on GQA and SVIT for feature alignment; Stage 2 fine-tunes the same parameters on the 419.9K-pair LingoQA Action and Scenery datasets with the vision encoder frozen. The evaluation metric, Lingo-Judge, is a DeBERTa-V3 classifier fine-tuned with LoRA that scores factual correctness of a predicted answer against human references, achieving 0.950 Spearman correlation with human ratings while running ~300× faster than GPT-4-based evaluation.

## Results

On the LingoQA benchmark, the proposed LingoQA Baseline (Vicuna-1.5-7B with CLIP + Q-Former, 5 frames, late-fusion) achieves 60.8% Lingo-Judge accuracy, compared to 96.6% for human annotators on the same 5-frame video inputs. The best zero-shot model, GPT-4V, reaches 59.6% Lingo-Judge, still 37% below multi-frame human performance. The Lingo-Judge evaluation metric itself achieves 0.950 Spearman and 0.993 Pearson correlation with human ratings, outperforming GPT-4 (0.941 Spearman), METEOR (0.876), CIDEr (0.853), and BLEU (0.835), while running in 10.5 seconds versus 812–3016 seconds for GPT-4-based evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LingoQA Baseline (60.8% Lingo-Judge) outperforms the best zero-shot model GPT-4V (59.6%) by 1.2 points and the fine-tuned single-frame LLaVA (59.0%) by 1.8 points, while substantially surpassing fine-tuned BLIP-2 (52.2%) by 8.6 points and the text-only Vicuna-7B lower bound (38.8%) by 22 points. Against zero-shot LLaVA (49.4%) and FUYU (45.4%), the baseline leads by 11.4 and 15.4 points respectively. However, a significant 35.8-point gap remains to multi-frame human performance (96.6%), and even single-frame human performance (81.8%) exceeds the best model by 21 points. On the evaluation metric side, Lingo-Judge (0.950 Spearman, 95.0% validation accuracy, 10.5 s) outperforms GPT-4 with chain-of-thought (0.932 Spearman, 91.2%, 3016 s) and GPT-4 alone (0.941 Spearman, 90.6%, 812 s), as well as all n-gram metrics (BLEU 0.835, METEOR 0.876, CIDEr 0.853 Spearman), while being 78–287× faster than GPT-4-based evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

The model architecture follows the CLIP + Q-Former + LLM pipeline established by BLIP-2 and LLaVA, but extends it with a late-fusion video strategy that concatenates per-frame Q-Former tokens before feeding them to the LLM. The evaluation methodology departs from the n-gram-based metrics (BLEU, METEOR, CIDEr) that have dominated driving VQA since 2015, instead adopting a learned DeBERTa-V3 classifier (Lingo-Judge) inspired by TruthfulQA's GPT-Judge. The dataset extends prior driving QA benchmarks (NuScenesQA, DriveLM, BDD-X) by introducing free-form multi-frame reasoning questions covering action justification, attention, and anticipation, rather than simple object-presence queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 37-point gap between GPT-4V (59.6%) and human performance (96.6%) on a 1,000-question evaluation set provides a concrete, reproducible target for the autonomous driving VLM community, and Lingo-Judge's 10-second runtime (vs. 50 minutes for GPT-4) makes iterative development feasible. Key limitations include reliance on a single front camera, 4-second video clips, and 7B-parameter LLMs, leaving open whether the observed gaps persist at scale or with multi-sensor inputs. The paper also does not evaluate action prediction or attention mechanisms, restricting the benchmark to question-answering rather than full driving decision-making.
