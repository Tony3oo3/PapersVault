## Problem

Existing visual instruction tuning methods bridge the vision-language modality gap by collecting increasingly diverse and costly instruction data, yet they under-exploit the rich image-relevant semantic information already embedded in the questions of existing datasets. The paper identifies that questions in visual instruction data carry higher CLIPScore (visual-text relevance) than their corresponding answers, suggesting that training models to predict questions—rather than only answers—could improve vision-language alignment without additional data collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240224/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image alongside a structured text sequence using special tokens: [usr] for standard question-answering turns, [vusr] for self-questioning turns, and [aswr] for answer generation, with delimiter tokens marking turn boundaries. In self-questioning mode, the model receives only the image and the [vusr] token (no explicit language prompt) and must spontaneously generate an image-relevant question followed by its answer. Outputs include free-form text answers, generated questions (multiple-choice, binary, reasoning), and single-sentence image captions for zero-shot captioning tasks.

## Method

SQ-LLaVA introduces visual self-questioning as a new training objective alongside standard answering: with probability δ=0.5, each conversation turn is converted from [usr] to [vusr], forcing the LLM to predict the question tokens conditioned on the image. A prototype extractor enhances visual representations by running two iterations of EM clustering (K=256 centers) over image token embeddings, then distributing weighted prototype information back to each token via cosine-similarity-weighted aggregation. For parameter-efficient training, LoRA adapters are applied to both the CLIP-ViT encoder (rank 32) and the Vicuna LLM (rank 128), with a two-stage protocol: pre-training freezes both backbones and trains only the prototype extractor and two-layer projector, while fine-tuning jointly optimizes LoRA modules, the prototype extractor, and the projector. The approach requires no new data collection—questions already present in LLaVA-instruct or ShareGPT4V-instruct serve as the self-questioning supervision signal.

## Results

On ten visual instruction tuning benchmarks, SQ-LLaVA-7B (trained on LLaVA-instruct) outperforms its LLaVA-v1.5 baseline on 9 out of 10 tasks, with notable gains on VizWiz (54.0 vs. 50.0), LLaVA-in-the-wild (66.3 vs. 63.4), and POPE (87.7 vs. 85.9). SQ-LLaVA*-7B (trained on ShareGPT4V-instruct) surpasses ShareGPT4V-7B on 6 out of 10 benchmarks, including ScienceQA (70.5 vs. 68.4) and LLaVA-in-the-wild (74.3 vs. 72.6). For zero-shot image captioning, SQ-LLaVA achieves a 2% average improvement over LLaVA-v1.5 across Flickr30K, Nocaps, and Conceptual Captions, while SQ-LLaVA* matches or exceeds ShareGPT4V on Nocaps and Conceptual. On COCO captioning with fine-tuning, SQ-LLaVA reaches 40.76 BLEU-4 and 136.78 CIDEr, surpassing all prior open-world baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-v1.5 (the direct architectural baseline), SQ-LLaVA-7B wins on 9 of 10 benchmarks, with the largest margin on VizWiz (+4.0) and LLaVA-in-the-wild (+2.9), while losing only marginally on MMBench-CN (58.1 vs. 58.3). Against ShareGPT4V, SQ-LLaVA*-7B wins on 6 of 10 benchmarks (notably ScienceQA +2.1, LLaVA-W +1.7, POPE +0.4) but trails on VizWiz (−1.9), MMBench (−2.2), and MMBench-CN (−2.2). SQ-LLaVA decisively outperforms InstructBLIP across all shared benchmarks (e.g., GQA 62.8 vs. 49.2, MMB 66.2 vs. 36.0) and Qwen-VL on most tasks, though Qwen-VL retains an edge on TextVQA (63.8 vs. 58.6). For image captioning, SQ-LLaVA surpasses ClipCap and DiscriTune by 73% and 66% average improvement respectively, and on COCO fine-tuning achieves 40.76 BLEU-4 / 136.78 CIDEr, exceeding eP-ALM (33.35 / 111.63) and MAPL (36.45 / 125.20) by substantial margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

SQ-LLaVA extends the LLaVA visual instruction tuning framework (Liu et al., 2023) by introducing self-questioning as an additional training objective alongside the standard question-answering paradigm. While prior work (LLaVA-v1.5, ShareGPT4V) focuses exclusively on predicting answers given image-question pairs, this paper repurposes the questions already present in instruction data as a learnable signal, guided by the observation that questions carry marginally higher CLIPScore visual relevance than answers. The architecture further departs from the standard LLaVA design by adding a prototype extractor (EM-based clustering over image tokens) and applying LoRA to both the vision encoder and LLM, rather than only the projector and LLM as in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-questioning training strategy offers a data-efficient way to improve vision-language alignment without collecting new annotations, which is practically appealing given the cost of visual instruction data. However, the performance gains are modest (1–3% over strong baselines), and the prototype extractor lacks pixel-wise supervision, leading to acknowledged inconsistencies on some benchmarks. Open questions include whether the self-questioning signal generalizes to other modalities or model scales, whether the tiny CLIPScore difference between questions and answers truly explains the gains, and whether supervised prototype learning (e.g., with SAM masks) could close the remaining gap.
