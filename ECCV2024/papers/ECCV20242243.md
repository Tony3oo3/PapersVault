## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for object detection are designed for medium- or small-sized teachers and fail when the teacher is a large-scale model (200M–1B parameters), because the severe representational gap between teacher and student causes distillation to degrade rather than improve student performance. The paper identifies that fully fine-tuning a large backbone on a specific dataset overfits and destroys the generalizable knowledge that makes the teacher useful for distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242243/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Both teacher and student are DETR-based detectors (default: DINO) that take a single RGB image and output 900 object queries, each decoded into a class probability vector and a bounding box (x, y, w, h). The distillation signal is extracted from the teacher's intermediate decoder states—specifically the spatial positions of all 900 queries and their N×N self-attention relation maps—rather than from final classification logits or feature maps. Experiments use COCO 2017 (118k train / 5k val) with standard COCO-style mAP evaluation.

## Method

DLIM-Det combines two key ideas. First, a frozen teacher strategy: when creating the teacher by fine-tuning a large pretrained backbone (Swin-Large, InternImage-Huge) on COCO, only the DETR task head is optimized while the backbone remains frozen, preserving generalizability and producing characteristically sparse query locations (reducing query density by ≥1/3). Second, a Query Distillation loss tailored to DETRs that operates on the 900 object queries after establishing one-to-one teacher–student matching via Hungarian algorithm on classification and localization costs. Query Position Distill (QPD) applies L1 and GIoU losses between matched teacher and student query boxes, providing dense localization supervision that compensates for the sparsity of ground-truth labels (only ~7 of 900 queries receive GT regression per image). Query Relation Distill (QRD) aligns the self-attention matrices of teacher and student queries via an L1 loss, teaching the student to replicate the pair-wise query relations that enable confident foreground queries to pop out while suppressing background queries. An additional head-inheritance step copies the attention and FFN parameters from the teacher's detection head into the student's head, transferring architecture-agnostic knowledge.

## Results

On MS COCO 2017, DLIM-Det achieves 54.4 mAP (+3.1 over the Swin-Tiny student baseline of 51.3) when distilling from a Swin-Large DINO teacher, and 55.9 mAP (+2.7 over the Intern-Tiny baseline of 53.2) when distilling from an Intern-Large teacher. Even with a 1.08B-parameter Intern-Huge teacher (30× the student's parameters), DLIM-Det still yields a +2.9 mAP gain, reaching 56.1 mAP. The method also generalizes to heterogeneous architectures, improving a Deformable DETR-Swin-T student by +3.5 mAP when distilled from a DINO-Swin-L teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLIM-Det decisively outperforms all four KD baselines on both backbones. On Swin Transformer, it surpasses DETRDistill (the strongest prior method) by +2.7 mAP (54.4 vs. 51.7), while FGD and MGD actually degrade the student by −0.2 mAP and FitNet yields no gain. On InternImage, DLIM-Det leads DETRDistill by +1.2 mAP (55.9 vs. 54.7) and MGD by +1.7 mAP (55.9 vs. 54.2); FGD again hurts performance (−0.4 mAP). Against the Teacher Assistant (TA) strategy, which requires two sequential distillation steps totaling 46.5 h (Swin) or 58.1 h (Intern), DLIM-Det achieves higher mAP (+0.7/+0.2) in roughly half the training time (20 h / 24.8 h). The paper attributes the failure of feature-based methods (FitNet, FGD, MGD) to the large semantic gap between 200M–1B parameter teachers and 30M students, which their feature-imitation or hand-crafted relation mechanisms cannot bridge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge distillation for object detection from the medium/small-model regime (where methods like DETRDistill, D3ETR, FGD, and MGD operate) to large-scale image models spanning 200M–1B parameters. It builds directly on DINO as the default DETR-based detector and departs from prior KD approaches in two key ways: (1) a frozen-teacher strategy that preserves the generalizability of large pretrained backbones rather than fully fine-tuning them, and (2) a Query Distillation method (QPD + QRD) that exploits the self-attention structure of DETR decoders to transfer spatial positions and pairwise query relations, rather than relying on feature-matching or hand-crafted relation modules. The work also positions itself against the Teacher Assistant paradigm, showing that direct distillation from large models is both more effective and more efficient than multi-step intermediate distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: large-scale detectors achieve state-of-the-art accuracy but are infeasible to deploy on edge or consumer hardware, making effective distillation from these models essential. The finding that larger teachers yield greater distillation gains (contradicting the prior assumption that larger teacher-student gaps hurt KD) is a useful conceptual shift, and the frozen-teacher recipe is simple enough to be widely adopted. Limitations include evaluation only on COCO, a focus on DETR-based architectures (though ResNet and Deformable DETR are also tested), and the reliance on a single student scale (~30M params). Open questions include whether the frozen-teacher and sparse-query insights transfer to other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, tracking) and whether the approach remains effective when the student is itself a large model.
