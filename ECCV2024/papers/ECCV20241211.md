## Problem

Existing visual information extraction (VIE) methods are limited to closed-set, pre-defined queries on seen document types, while LLM-based alternatives lack explicit layout modeling and suffer from hallucination. No prior approach simultaneously supports arbitrary open-vocabulary queries, unseen document layouts, and layout-aware contextual reasoning in a single efficient inference pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241211/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB document image and a variable-length set of open-vocabulary text queries (e.g., 'date', 'amount', 'license number') as input. Queries are tokenized and encoded by a frozen ERNIE 3.0 LLM, while the image is processed by a StrucTextV2 encoder at H/4 × W/4 resolution. Output is a set of bounding boxes for matched text segments, with all queries resolved simultaneously in one detection-style forward pass rather than sequential question-answering rounds, yielding ~20× faster inference than QwenVL.

## Method

The paper reformulates open-vocabulary VIE as a textual grounding task, pairing frozen-LLM query embeddings with layout-aware grounded segment embeddings for cross-modal text-region alignment. A lightweight segmentation head extracts explicit layout (bounding boxes of all text segments), which are then encoded via RoIAlign on cross-modal image features and further processed by a cross-region transformer encoder that injects both spatial (trigonometric coordinate) and index (reading-order) position embeddings. Training uses a two-stage contrastive pre-training: first aligning OCR text with its visual word crop (cross-modal semantic alignment), then aligning key queries with their value segments using a unified image-text-label contrastive loss that handles one-to-many key-value mappings. The entire pipeline is OCR-free during training, using grounded image crops instead of offline OCR tokens, enabling end-to-end optimization and avoiding recognition errors.

## Results

On the SVRD benchmark (open-vocabulary VIE across 10 document types), TG-VIE achieves an overall F-score of 41.98, outperforming the strongest baseline QwenVL (17.63) by 24.3 percentage points and QwenVL_tuned (27.68) by 14.3 points. Inference speed is 0.25 s per image, making it approximately 20× faster than QwenVL (5.33 s) and 34× faster than Qwen (8.66 s). The method also substantially outperforms the pure textual grounding baseline CLIP-VG_tuned (1.75) and the NER-based LayoutLMv2 (10.82), confirming the benefit of layout-aware context learning and document-tailored pre-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TG-VIE (41.98 F-score) decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the SVRD overall metric. Against the strongest multimodal LLM, QwenVL (17.63), the gap is 24.3 percentage points; even QwenVL fine-tuned on the same training data (27.68) trails by 14.3 points. The pure textual grounding method CLIP-VG_tuned (1.75) and the NER-based LayoutLMv2 (10.82) are far behind, confirming that neither open-vocabulary grounding alone nor closed-set NER suffices for layout-diversified documents. Among LLM-based baselines, Qwen (9.24) and UReader (14.07) show moderate performance but lack explicit layout modeling, while LLaMA2-7B/13B (1.23/2.09) and BLIP-2 (0.96) perform poorly due to their reliance on OCR text without spatial context. TG-VIE also achieves the fastest inference at 0.25 s, roughly 20× faster than QwenVL and 34× faster than Qwen, by evaluating all queries simultaneously in a single forward pass rather than iterating per-key QA rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the detection-based textual grounding paradigm (RoIAlign, Hungarian matching, contrastive alignment) for document information extraction, a task previously dominated by NER-style classifiers (LayoutLM series) or LLM-based question-answering pipelines (QwenVL, UReader). It departs from both: NER methods are locked to closed-set queries with fixed output dimensions, while LLM-based methods are layout-agnostic and hallucination-prone. The two-stage pre-training borrows CLIP-style cross-modal contrastive alignment for the first stage and unified image-text-label contrastive loss for the second, but applies them to grounded document segments rather than natural image patches, with StrucTextV2 as the document-specific image backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× inference speedup over QwenVL and the ability to evaluate multiple keys in a single forward pass make this approach practically attractive for high-volume document processing where LLM-based QA is prohibitively slow. The OCR-free end-to-end training eliminates a major error source in pipeline-based systems. However, the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (SVRD task 3, 10 document types), and the notably weak performance on the 'paper' subset (7.74 F-score vs. 41.98 overall) suggests fragility when answer granularity shifts. Open questions include robustness to poor illumination and font transformation (acknowledged by the authors), generalization beyond the 10 evaluated document types, and whether the grounding formulation can handle multi-line or tabular value extraction.
