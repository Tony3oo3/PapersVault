## Problem

Existing image-to-3D methods (e.g., NFI, Zero123XL, DreamGaussian) learn novel-view generation solely from RGB information in natural or latent space, without a deeper semantic understanding of vehicles such as model, manufacturer, or production year. This limits their zero-shot generalization: when in-the-wild observations contain occlusion or atypical viewing angles outside the training distribution, these methods produce incorrect geometry and appearance. The paper addresses this gap by transferring the robust zero-shot prediction ability of a VQA model into the novel-view rendering pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241769/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB vehicle image (potentially occluded or captured at a challenging angle) and produces 16 photorealistic multi-view renderings with fixed, equally spaced camera poses around the vehicle. Internally, the VQA model converts the image into a text prompt (model, manufacturer, year, features), which is then used to generate 512×512 composite images each containing four 256×256 sub-views. The raw image is segmented with SAM to isolate the vehicle before appearance transfer, and the final multi-view outputs can be fed into GS/NeRF pipelines for full 3D asset creation or used directly for training data augmentation.

## Method

VQA-Diff is a three-stage pipeline that uses text as an intermediary to bridge VQA's zero-shot knowledge with diffusion-based generation. First, BLIP-2 answers a carefully designed question ('What are the model, manufacture, production year, and main features of this vehicle?') to produce a detailed prompt. Second, a multi-expert DM strategy generates consistent multi-view structures: a text-to-image DM produces four 256×256 anchor views in a 512×512 composite, and four separate image-to-image DMs each expand one anchor into four additional views, yielding 16 total views with controllable poses; all are fine-tuned on ShapeNetV2 renderings. Third, a subject-driven structure-controlled generation mechanism renders photorealistic appearances: the BLIP-2 multimodal encoder encodes the SAM-segmented raw image and prompt into text embeddings for BLIP-Diffusion, while Canny edges extracted from the structure views condition an edge-to-image ControlNet attached to the UNet, ensuring geometric consistency with the generated structures.

## Results

On Pascal 3D+, VQA-Diff achieves an ITC score of 0.380 (vs. 0.401 ground truth), CLIP similarity of 0.856, FID of 117.49, and VQA score of 0.903, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On Waymo (where no competitor is pretrained), the method reaches ITC 0.418 (GT: 0.422), CLIP 0.840, FID 163.40, and VQA 0.854. On Objaverse, it attains ITC 0.412, CLIP 0.872, FID 114.75, and VQA 0.838, again leading all baselines across all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQA-Diff outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against NFI (pretrained on Pascal 3D+), VQA-Diff gains +0.096 ITC, +0.072 CLIP, −11.94 FID, and +0.304 VQA on Pascal 3D+, with even larger margins on Waymo (VQA: 0.854 vs. 0.194) and Objaverse (VQA: 0.838 vs. 0.669). Against Zero123XL (pretrained on Objaverse), the FID gap is most pronounced: 117.49 vs. 193.27 on Pascal 3D+ and 114.75 vs. 175.18 on Objaverse, while the VQA score advantage is +0.397 and +0.472 respectively. Against DreamGaussian, which relies on Zero123XL for unseen-view reconstruction, VQA-Diff achieves substantially lower FID (117.49 vs. 269.24 on Pascal 3D+) and higher VQA scores (0.903 vs. 0.519), indicating that DG's dependence on Zero123XL's geometry propagates errors into its novel views. The consistent wins across all datasets and metrics suggest the VQA-guided prompt + multi-expert diffusion pipeline provides a fundamentally stronger zero-shot generalization mechanism than pure image-to-image or SDS-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQA-Diff departs from prior image-to-3D methods (NFI, Zero123XL, DreamGaussian) that learn novel-view generation purely from RGB information in natural or latent space, by introducing a VQA model (BLIP-2) as a text-based intermediary that encodes real-world vehicle knowledge before any generation occurs. The architecture builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 for structure generation, BLIP-Diffusion's subject-driven mechanism for appearance transfer, and ControlNet for geometry conditioning, but the key novelty is the multi-expert DMs strategy that decomposes 16-view structure synthesis into a text-to-image stage (4 anchor views) followed by four image-to-image stages (12 surrounding views). This separates the 'understanding' problem (solved by VQA's LLM-inherited knowledge) from the 'generation' problem (solved by diffusion priors), rather than learning an end-to-end image-to-novel-view mapping as prior work does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving simulation: generating photorealistic 3D vehicle assets from in-the-wild observations without requiring a large-scale image-to-3D vehicle dataset covering all models, angles, and occlusions. The consistent zero-shot performance across Pascal 3D+, Waymo, and Objaverse (where no competitor was pretrained) demonstrates that VQA-mediated generation can generalize beyond training distributions. However, the method is explicitly vehicle-specific—the authors note that geometry is uniquely determined by model/make/year for cars, making VQA prompts effective structural constraints, whereas generic objects (e.g., teddy bears) lack this property. An open question is whether the VQA-as-intermediary paradigm can be adapted to object categories where textual descriptions do not fully constrain geometry.
