## Problem

Deep classifiers frequently rely on non-robust input features (irrelevant patterns, spuriously correlated attributes) that degrade generalization and interpretability. Existing input gradient regularization smooths the class-conditional or joint density, which only enforces attribution consistency under a specific label y=i and leaves class-specific non-robust features unaddressed. The paper identifies that model reliance on non-robust features correlates with the non-smoothness of the input marginal density, and proposes a regularization that targets this marginal density directly.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images across multiple datasets (BlockMNIST 28×28, CelebA-Hair 160×160, CIFAR-10/100 32×32, Waterbirds, SVHN) and outputs class labels via a standard softmax classifier. During training, the input image is consumed twice in the backward pass: once for the standard classification loss and once to compute the gradient of the log-marginal density ∇_x log Z_f(x) with respect to input features, which serves as the regularization signal. No architectural modification is required; the regularization is a loss-level addition applied to any CNN backbone.

## Method

The paper defines robust features as those whose attributions remain bounded under a metric across input instances, and non-robust features as those whose attributions fluctuate with input conditions. It then derives that the marginal density p_θ(x) = Z_f(x)/Z_θ (where Z_f(x) = Σ_i e^{f_i(x;θ)}) encodes the smoothness of output logits across the input space, so regularizing ∇_x log p_θ(x) = ∇_x log Z_f(x) suppresses reliance on non-robust features without imposing class-specific constraints. The key distinction from input gradient regularization is that ∇_x f_i(x;θ) equals ∇_x log p_θ(x|y=i) (conditional density), which only smooths attributions under one label, whereas ∇_x log Z_f(x) smooths the marginal density across all classes simultaneously. For stable and efficient implementation, the authors replace the intractable sum-of-exponentials with a softmax reformulation (∇_x f_i − ∇_x log-softmax) and apply a first-order Taylor approximation to compute the gradient difference in a single backward pass, reducing complexity from O(n) to O(1) per sample and avoiding numerical overflow in deep networks with batch normalization.

## Results

On BlockMNIST, the proposed marginal density smoothing regularization achieves the lowest feature leakage score (2.259) among all non-adversarial-training methods, outperforming InputGrad regularization (3.461) and Hessian regularization (4.258), while also attaining the best L∞ PGD-20 adversarial accuracy (29.36%) among regularization-based approaches. On CelebA-Hair, the method achieves 85.62% worst-group accuracy, surpassing InputGrad (82.66%), Group DRO (59.44%), and Score-Matching (59.78%). On Waterbirds, the method reaches 63.78% worst-group accuracy, and when combined with Group DRO, achieves 73.82% worst-group accuracy with 90.52% overall accuracy, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the lowest feature leakage (2.259) among all regularization-based baselines, substantially outperforming InputGrad (3.461), IG-SUM (3.497), SoftPlus (3.533), and Hessian (4.258), and even slightly beating PGD adversarial training (2.313). On L∞ adversarial robustness, the method (29.36%) surpasses InputGrad (21.14%), IG-SUM (22.73%), and AT(PGD) (28.05%), though it trails AT(PGD) on L2 adversarial accuracy (85.41% vs. 92.75%). On CelebA-Hair worst-group accuracy, the method (85.62%) clearly beats InputGrad (82.66%), Group DRO (59.44%), and Score-Matching (59.78%), while on Waterbirds it outperforms InputGrad (63.78% vs. 58.18%) and Score-Matching (63.78% vs. 58.19%) but is comparable to Group DRO (63.78% vs. 63.60%). The method trades some standard accuracy (93.05% vs. 99.12% for ST) for substantially improved robustness and interpretability. When combined with Group DRO, the method achieves the best Waterbirds worst-group accuracy (73.82%) and overall accuracy (90.52%), demonstrating complementary benefits with distributional robustness approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Bridle's probabilistic interpretation of feedforward network outputs to derive a marginal density formulation p_θ(x) = Z_f(x)/Z_θ, then uses it to motivate a new regularization on ∇_x log p_θ(x). It departs critically from the widely-used input gradient regularization (Ross & Doshi-Velez) by showing analytically that ∇_x f_i(x;θ) corresponds to gradients of the log-conditional or log-joint density under a fixed class y=i, not the marginal density—meaning it is inherently class-specific and blind to cross-class non-robust features. The paper also positions itself against Hessian/SoftPlus regularizations, Score-Matching, Group DRO, and adversarial training (FGSM/PGD), demonstrating that none of these fully address feature leakage or spurious correlation in a class-agnostic manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The re-interpretation of input gradient regularization as a conditional-density smoother rather than a general robustness mechanism is a useful conceptual correction for practitioners who have relied on it as a universal fix. The proposed marginal-density smoothing regularization offers a class-agnostic alternative with an efficient O(1) implementation, and its combination with Group DRO yields the best worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds, suggesting practical composability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large models or non-CNN architectures (e.g., transformers), how the regularization interacts with modern training techniques like label smoothing or mixup, and whether the theoretical link between marginal density smoothness and feature robustness holds under distribution shift.
