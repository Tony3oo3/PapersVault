## Problem

Existing grasp generation methods encode the hand as a monolithic representation and decode all MANO parameters in a single step, producing grasps that resemble touches rather than true multi-finger interactions. They also rely on continuous latent spaces (CVAE) that fail to capture the discrete, categorical nature of real hand postures, and they generalize poorly to unseen objects without expensive test-time optimization.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes only a 3000-point cloud sampled from the object mesh as input (no hand input required) and outputs a full hand mesh parameterized by MANO: 10 shape parameters, 45 joint rotations (posture), plus 3 translation and 3 rotation parameters (position). During training, both the object point cloud and 778 hand vertices are provided. The autoregressive PixelCNN generates a sequence of 6 discrete codebook indices (one per hand part) conditioned on the object's codebook index, which are then decoded into the final MANO parameters.

## Method

DVQ-VAE decomposes the hand into six spatially fixed components (five fingers and palm), each encoded by a dedicated PointNet encoder into its own discrete codebook, alongside a separate object codebook learned unsupervised via a type encoder. A dual-stage decoding strategy first predicts the 55-dim posture (shape + rotation) using a Posture Decoder that also applies skeletal physical constraints via a gating network and transformer correction layer, then predicts the 6-dim position using a Position Decoder conditioned on the frozen posture encoding and a separate pose encoder. At inference, a PixelCNN autoregressively generates the six hand-part codebook indices conditioned on the object's codebook index, enabling diverse grasp sampling without optimization. The total loss combines VQ-VAE codebook commitment losses, MANO-parameter reconstruction losses, and contact/penetration losses to enforce physical plausibility.

## Results

On four benchmarks (HO-3D, FPHA, GRAB, Obman), DVQ-VAE achieves the lowest penetration volume across all datasets (5.36, 4.58, 3.18, and 3.93 cm³ respectively) and a quality index of 3.54 on HO-3D, representing a 13.3% relative improvement over the best CVAE baseline (4.12). Inference time is 0.14–0.15 s per batch, a 99.8% reduction compared to ContactGen (119–237 s) and 99.3% reduction compared to GraspTTA (~20 s). The method also achieves the highest cluster size (3.84 on HO-3D, 3.98 on Obman), indicating superior grasp diversity, while maintaining a contact ratio above 97% on all datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GraspCVAE [12] (CVAE without test-time adaptation), DVQ-VAE wins on penetration volume (5.36 vs 7.23 cm³ on HO-3D) and quality index (3.54 vs 4.12, a 13.3% relative improvement), while matching contact ratio (99.50% vs 99.60%). Against GraspTTA [12] (CVAE with ContactNet refinement), DVQ-VAE achieves lower penetration (5.36 vs 9.00 cm³) and a substantially lower quality index (3.54 vs 4.56), at 1/140th the inference time (0.14 s vs 19.67 s); however, GraspTTA still achieves a perfect 100% contact ratio and slightly lower grasp displacement on HO-3D. Against ContactGen [16], DVQ-VAE dominates on penetration (5.36 vs 6.53 cm³), contact ratio (99.50% vs 90.10%), and inference speed (0.14 s vs 119.4 s), though ContactGen achieves a marginally higher cluster size (4.79 vs 3.84) on HO-3D. Against GraspingField [14], DVQ-VAE is superior on every metric except entropy, with penetration reduced from 20.05 to 5.36 cm³ and quality index from 8.93 to 3.54 on HO-3D. In human evaluation, DVQ-VAE scores 3.36/5 on average, outperforming ContactGen (3.25) and GraspTTA (3.23).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017) from its original single-codebook image generation setting to a part-aware decomposed architecture for structured 3D hand generation, drawing conceptual inspiration from Pi et al. [23] who segmented the human body into parts for motion synthesis. It departs from the dominant CVAE-based grasp generation paradigm (GraspTTA, ContactGen, GraspingField) by replacing continuous Gaussian latents with discrete codebook indices, arguing that the categorical nature of real grasps is better captured by discrete representations. The dual-stage decoding strategy—sequentially generating posture then position—represents a novel decomposition of the MANO parameter space not present in prior single-step decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99.8% inference time reduction over optimization-based methods (ContactGen, GraspTTA) makes high-quality grasp generation practical for real-time applications in robotics and AR, while the ~14% quality index improvement demonstrates that discrete, part-aware representations can outperform continuous ones for this task. However, the paper acknowledges failure cases on objects with complex geometric shapes where contact is insufficient, and suggests SDF-based object representations as a promising direction. Open questions include whether the decomposed codebook approach generalizes to multi-hand or multi-object scenarios, and whether the autoregressive PixelCNN component can be replaced by more modern sequence models without sacrificing the diversity gains.
