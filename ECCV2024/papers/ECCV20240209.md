## Problem

Existing temporal video alignment methods assume a suitable video pair is already given, severely limiting their practical applicability. Identifying which videos from a large collection are actually temporally alignable—beyond merely sharing an action label—remains unaddressed, as general videos (e.g., 'cutting pineapple') exhibit too much variability for action-category knowledge alone to suffice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240209/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single query video clip and a large database of video clips (e.g., Kinetics700 with ~650K videos). It first retrieves top-k candidates via cosine similarity on temporally aggregated clip-level embeddings, then outputs a DRAQ alignability score per candidate and a DTW warping path that temporally synchronizes the best match to the query. The DRAQ score is a dimensionless ratio (optimal DTW cost / average random-path cost), making it comparable across pairs with different appearances and lengths.

## Method

The AVR pipeline has three stages: (1) k-NN retrieval on clip-level NMS features, (2) re-ranking candidates using DRAQ, which compares the optimal DTW path cost to the average cost of k randomly sampled paths through the same cost matrix (with direction probabilities proportional to remaining distance, making random paths more challenging baselines), and (3) DTW alignment on contextualized frame-level features. The key feature design augments each frame embedding with the cumulative sum of all preceding frame features (concatenated along the channel dimension), then zero-centers per clip, injecting global temporal position information into otherwise local frame representations. This contextualization is general and applicable to any pre-trained frame-level encoder (BYOL, CARL, NMS), and DRAQ's ratio formulation removes the appearance bias that makes raw DTW cost unreliable as an alignability indicator.

## Results

On the full AVR pipeline evaluated via cycle-consistency, NMS features with DRAQ re-ranking achieve the best overall performance: FPE 9.5 / CPE 0.20 on PennAction, FPE 4.8 / CPE 5.9 on Penn↔UCF, and FPE 0.5 / CPE 0.0 on Kinetics700. DRAQ re-ranking consistently reduces cycle-consistency errors across all feature backbones and datasets (e.g., Kinetics FPE drops from 22.7 to 0.5 with NMS, and from 23.5 to 2.3 with CARL). On the Aligned Phase Agreement (APA) metric, contextualized NMS features reach 0.893 APA for the top-DRAQ candidate, outperforming non-contextualized NMS (0.868), CARL (0.856), and BYOL (0.814).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three frame-level feature backbones (BYOL, CARL, NMS) with and without DRAQ re-ranking in the full AVR pipeline (Table 1). NMS+DRAQ dominates on Kinetics700 (FPE 0.5, CPE 0.0) and Penn↔UCF (FPE 4.8, CPE 5.9), while BYOL+DRAQ achieves the lowest PennAction FPE (0.2) but struggles cross-dataset (FPE 50.6 on Penn↔UCF). CARL+DRAQ is consistently outperformed by NMS+DRAQ on all three settings, suggesting that the temporal Transformer in CARL does not add benefit over the simpler contextualization trick when combined with DRAQ. On the PennAction proxy tasks (Table 3), contextualized NMS features (0.918 phase classification, 0.882 frame retrieval, 0.825 Kendall τ) surpass all prior alignment methods—TCC (0.744/0.767/0.641), LAV (0.786/0.791/0.684), TCN (0.681/0.778/0.542), and SaL (0.682/–/0.474)—though CARL still leads on phase classification (0.931) and frame retrieval (0.922). The authors note that CARL's high Kendall τ (0.985) is likely a position-encoding shortcut rather than genuine alignment quality, as a randomly initialized BYOL+Transformer already achieves τ=0.995. DRAQ re-ranking also improves standard action-retrieval recall (Table 4): R@1 rises from 82.09 to 82.40 on PennAction and from 81.60 to 81.81 on UCF101, with R@10 gains of ~0.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing two well-studied but previously separate subproblems—large-scale video retrieval and temporal video alignment—into a unified pipeline. Prior alignment methods (TCC, GTA, LAV, CARL) all assume a suitable video pair is given and focus solely on finding the warping path, while retrieval methods (NMS, BYOL, CARL features) optimize for semantic similarity without regard to temporal alignability. The paper's conceptual departure is treating alignability as a first-class retrieval criterion, introducing DRAQ as a lightweight re-ranking signal and a simple cumulative-sum contextualization to make off-the-shelf frame features more alignment-friendly, rather than training a new end-to-end alignment model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing AVR, the paper opens the door to practical applications of video alignment (example-based retiming, audio transfer, clip replacement) that were previously infeasible because a suitable pair had to be manually selected. The finding that standard proxy metrics (Kendall Tau, phase classification) can be gamed by position encodings in temporal Transformers is an important methodological caution for the alignment community. However, the evaluation on Kinetics700 relies on only 91 annotated validation videos, and the pipeline's dependence on off-the-shelf features and a heuristic DRAQ threshold leaves open questions about robustness at scale and whether learned alignability scores could replace the random-path baseline.
