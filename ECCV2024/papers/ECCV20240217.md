## Problem

State-of-the-art monocular 3D human reconstruction methods treat the clothed human as a single entity driven by skeletal deformation, making them unable to capture loose garments whose large non-rigid deformations are topologically decoupled from the underlying body pose. This limits realistic avatar creation to subjects in tight-fitting clothing, excluding a significant portion of real-world wardrobes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240217/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular in-the-wild video sequence along with per-frame SMPL pose estimates and SAM-derived segmentation masks (which occasionally require manual boundary refinement). It produces a temporally consistent layered canonical-space representation — separate SDF and radiance fields for the inner body and outer garment — that can be deformed to arbitrary poses for rendering. The system supports up to two independently reconstructed garments (e.g., upper and lower), with complexity scaling linearly in the number of garment layers.

## Method

ReLoo introduces a layered neural implicit representation that decomposes the clothed human into separate SDF + radiance networks for the body and garment, composited via a min operation. For deformation, it employs a hybrid strategy: standard SMPL-based linear blend skinning for the body, and a non-hierarchical virtual bone module for the garment where an MLP predicts per-bone rotations and translations conditioned on bone position, body pose, and time embedding, with distance-weighted LBS driving garment points. Multi-layer differentiable volume rendering performs two-stage sampling per layer and re-orders all samples by depth before volumetric integration, handling garment-body occlusions. Training follows a two-stage global optimization: a warm-up phase regularizes virtual bone deformations toward skeletal motion, followed by full virtual bone activation with virtual bones extracted from MISE meshes and simplified to ~80 via quadric decimation, periodically regenerated to track topology changes. Losses combine L1 RGB, Geman-McClure-weighted segmentation, adaptive Eikonal regularization sampled near extracted surfaces, and a virtual bone deformation consistency term.

## Results

On the MonoLoose dataset, ReLoo achieves a Chamfer distance of 1.93 cm, normal consistency of 0.831, and volumetric IoU of 0.881 for surface reconstruction, outperforming the best single-layer baseline (Vid2Avatar: 2.34 cm, 0.794, 0.776) and the multi-layer baseline SCARF (3.13 cm, 0.711, 0.691). For novel view synthesis on MonoLoose, ReLoo reaches 29.2 dB PSNR, 0.970 SSIM, and 3.15 LPIPS, surpassing Vid2Avatar (25.9 dB, 0.968, 4.66) by a substantial margin. On DynaCap, ReLoo achieves 27.9 dB PSNR and 1.27 LPIPS, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLoo outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both datasets. Against the single-layer SDF-based SelfRecon [18], ReLoo improves PSNR by 6.7 dB on MonoLoose (29.2 vs. 22.5) and reduces LPIPS from 6.08 to 3.15, with SelfRecon producing severe garment-body interpenetration and missing dress parts. Against Vid2Avatar [13], the closest single-layer competitor, ReLoo gains 3.3 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 1.51 on MonoLoose, while on DynaCap the margin narrows to 0.8 dB PSNR and 0.55 LPIPS. Against the multi-layer SCARF [8] (SMPL-X + NeRF clothing), ReLoo leads by 5.9 dB PSNR on MonoLoose and 2.4 dB on DynaCap, with SCARF producing notably blurry renderings due to its reliance on explicit body templates. Against the template-based DeepCap [15], the comparison is qualitative only (no open-source code), but ReLoo recovers finer facial details, clothing wrinkles, and topological changes (e.g., empty space between dress layers) that the fixed-topology template cannot represent.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReLoo extends the neural implicit human reconstruction line (Vid2Avatar, SelfRecon) by introducing a layered representation that disentangles the inner body from outer clothing, moving beyond the single-entity formulation that limits prior methods. The virtual bone deformation module draws conceptual inspiration from physics-based cloth simulation (Pan et al., 2022) but departs fundamentally by learning bone placements and transformations entirely from 2D observations without requiring garment templates or 3D simulation data. Compared to the closest multi-layer method SCARF, ReLoo is fully implicit (no explicit SMPL-X body), supports arbitrary motions beyond self-rotation, and handles large non-rigid deformations via the non-hierarchical virtual bones rather than skeletal skinning alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Loose garments constitute a significant portion of real-world wardrobes, yet prior monocular reconstruction methods degrade severely on them, limiting the practical utility of 3D avatar creation. The MonoLoose dataset with multi-view ground truth fills a notable gap in evaluation resources for this setting. Key limitations remain: the method depends on external pose estimates and SAM segmentation masks (requiring occasional manual correction), scales linearly with the number of separately reconstructed garments (practically limited to ~2), and has not been tested on transparent or highly textured fabrics. Open questions include whether the virtual bone count and placement can be made adaptive per garment type, and whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes with interacting garments.
