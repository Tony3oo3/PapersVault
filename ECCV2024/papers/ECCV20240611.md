## Problem

SAM, despite its strong representation capability, performs poorly on colonoscopic video polyp segmentation due to (1) low polyp-background contrast caused by semantic entanglement and (2) large frame-to-frame variations in polyp size, position, and shape. Existing SAM adaptation methods either ignore temporal information entirely or rely on fixed-step cross-frame attention that cannot handle the large inter-frame variations typical of colonoscopic videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240611/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a colonoscopic video clip of L frames (default L=3) at 256×256 resolution, along with user prompts (point or bounding box, with 1 random point per frame used for fair comparison). It outputs per-frame binary polyp segmentation masks. During training, only the newly introduced SDA and STSN modules are updated while all original SAM parameters remain frozen, making the approach parameter-efficient for clinical deployment.

## Method

VP-SAM adds two modules to a frozen SAM backbone. The Semantic Disentanglement Adapter (SDA) performs FFT on the input clip, reconstructs a surrogate using only amplitude information (with fixed phase) to isolate low-level entanglement states (color/texture shared by polyps and background), encodes this via a CNN, and then multiplies SAM's patch embedding by the squared difference map (F − F_e)² to suppress background interference. The Spatio-temporal Side Network (STSN) first uses a Disentanglement Reference Mechanism that computes dot-product affinity between the disentangled embedding and a CNN-extracted spatio-temporal feature to establish a consensus representation, then a Spatio-temporal Injector applies decoupled spatial self-attention, deformable convolution for motion-offset learning, and temporal self-attention, finally injecting the result into SAM's transformer blocks via cross-attention with 3×3 convolutions to adapt to varying receptive fields across layers. Training uses BCE + Dice loss with AdamW for 10 epochs on a single GPU.

## Results

On SUN-SEG-Easy, VP-SAM (1 pt/frame) achieves 87.56 mDice, 80.04 mIoU, and 19.80 mHD, outperforming the best task-specific method (MSAF: 81.33 mDice) by over 6 points and the best SAM-based method (SAMUS: 84.83 mDice) by 2.73 points. On SUN-SEG-Hard, VP-SAM reaches 87.04 mDice and 79.20 mIoU, surpassing SAMUS (84.11 mDice) and MediViSTA-SAM (83.25 mDice). On CVC-612 and CVC-300, VP-SAM achieves 93.54/88.83 mDice/mIoU and 89.93/82.38 mDice/mIoU respectively, setting new state-of-the-art across all four test splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VP-SAM (1 pt/frame) outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest task-specific method MSAF (81.33 mDice on SUN-SEG-Easy), VP-SAM gains +6.23 mDice and +6.86 mIoU; against META-UNet (81.17 mDice), the gap is +6.39 mDice. Among SAM-based methods, VP-SAM surpasses the closest competitor SAMUS (84.83 mDice on SUN-SEG-Easy) by +2.73 mDice and +2.56 mIoU, and MediViSTA-SAM (84.34 mDice) by +3.22 mDice. On the harder SUN-SEG-Hard split, VP-SAM leads SAMUS by +2.93 mDice (87.04 vs 84.11) and MediViSTA-SAM by +3.79 mDice. In the spatio-temporal strategy comparison (Table 2), VP-SAM outperforms ST-Adapter (84.17 mDice) by +3.39 mDice on SUN-SEG-Easy, while point-tracker and mask-tracker approaches remain far behind (57.85 and 60.31 mDice respectively), confirming that simple tracking cues are insufficient for the large frame-to-frame variations in colonoscopic videos. On CVC-300, VP-SAM achieves 89.93 mDice vs. 87.17 for SAMUS and 87.09 for MediViSTA-SAM, with the largest mHD improvement (12.38 vs. 14.33 and 14.98), indicating more precise boundary delineation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM adaptation lineage for medical imaging by addressing two gaps that prior SAM-based methods (MedSAM, Polyp-SAM, SAMed, SAM-Med2D) leave unaddressed: low object-background contrast and the absence of temporal modeling. It departs from MediViSTA-SAM's fixed-step cross-frame attention by introducing a spatio-temporal side network with deformable convolution to handle large frame-to-frame variations, and from all prior SAM adapters by adding a Fourier-spectrum-based semantic disentanglement mechanism. The CNN-branch in the Disentanglement Reference Mechanism is directly borrowed from SAMUS [30], while the overall philosophy of freezing SAM and training lightweight adapters follows the broader parameter-efficient adaptation trend.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video polyp segmentation is clinically critical for colorectal cancer screening, and this work demonstrates that a frozen foundation model can be effectively adapted to a challenging low-contrast video task with only ~50M additional trainable parameters, making it more practical than full fine-tuning approaches. The Fourier-amplitude-based disentanglement is a creative signal-processing insight that could transfer to other low-contrast medical imaging tasks beyond colonoscopy. However, the method's computational overhead (470 GFLOPs, 38 FPS) and sensitivity to light interference and extreme shape variations limit immediate clinical deployment, and generalization to other endoscopic or surgical video domains remains untested.
