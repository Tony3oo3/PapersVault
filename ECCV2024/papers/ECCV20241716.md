## Problem

Existing audio-driven emotional talking face generation suffers from two critical flaws: (1) the emotion embedded in the reference ID image contaminates the target emotion, producing undesired blended expressions, and (2) a single categorical emotion label is applied uniformly across all frames, causing unnatural constant-intensity playback that ignores the audio's emotional flow. No prior method independently represents facial ID, expression type, and emotional intensity as disentangled components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241716/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single reference ID image (one-shot) and a driving audio clip (16 kHz, converted to mel-spectrograms with 640-sample window/hop) to produce a 25 Hz talking face video. Unlike prior emotional talking face methods that also require an emotional reference video or a single emotion label, EAP derives all emotional content—both the emotion type and its frame-wise intensity—exclusively from the driving audio, making the reference image purely an identity source.

## Method

EAP decomposes the emotional face latent into independent ID, expression, and intensity terms (z_face = z_id + α·z_exp) and trains two complementary modules. ANT (Audio-to-Neutral Translation) is pre-trained on VoxCeleb2 for lip-sync and then fine-tuned on MEAD with an N-pair metric loss that pushes generated features away from all non-neutral emotion classes, effectively erasing the reference image's original emotion. ERA (Emotional Representation from Audio) introduces two task branches: an LSTM classifier trained on categorical emotion labels to extract context emotion from a dedicated audio emotion encoder, and a frame-wise intensity regressor trained on pseudo valence-arousal labels (magnitude of the 2D Russell circumplex vector) to encode per-frame emotional intensity into the audio feature. This is the first application of continuous 2D emotion analysis to the ETF task, enabling frame-level intensity variation that tracks the audio's emotional dynamics.

## Results

On the LRW dataset, EAP achieves the best SSIM (0.82), LMD (1.41), and LSE-C (10.01) among all methods, outperforming the strongest emotional baseline EAT (SSIM 0.77, LMD 1.90, LSE-C 9.28). On MEAD, EAP-ANT attains the highest SSIM (0.73) and LMD (3.31), while full EAP leads in LSE-C (9.75) and LSE-D (10.95). In the user study, EAP scores 81.80% emotion accuracy (68.12% above ATVG) and a naturalness score of 0.43 (only 0.17 below ground truth), surpassing EAMM by 2.90% in emotion accuracy despite using only audio-derived emotion rather than reference emotional videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAP clearly outperforms the two dedicated emotional talking-face baselines (EAMM and EAT) on perceptual quality and lip-sync: on LRW, EAP improves SSIM from 0.77 (EAT) to 0.82 and LMD from 1.90 to 1.41, while on MEAD it raises LSE-C from 5.58 (EAT) to 9.75. Against non-emotional baselines, EAP wins on SSIM (0.82 vs. 0.72 for PC-AVS on LRW) and LMD (1.41 vs. 1.64 for PC-AVS), but trails MakeItTalk on PSNR (25.87 vs. 26.87 on LRW) and AVCT on FID (4.15 vs. 2.99 on LRW), suggesting a slight trade-off in pixel-level fidelity for improved perceptual and emotional quality. EAT remains competitive on LSE-D for LRW (8.99 vs. 9.45) and FID for MEAD (15.10 vs. 15.34), indicating that the lightweight emotion-adaptation module in EAT still captures some lip-sync advantage. In the user study, EAP's 81.80% emotion accuracy surpasses EAMM (78.90%) by 2.90% despite EAMM having access to a reference emotional video, and its naturalness score (0.43) is only 0.17 below ground truth, far ahead of the worst method (1.02). The ablation in Table 2 confirms that both the N-pair metric loss (neutralization) and the frame-wise intensity regression loss contribute: removing L_inten degrades RMSE from 0.23 to 0.69 and CCC from 0.61 to 0.32, while removing L_metric raises RMSE by 0.20.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-driven emotional talking face generation (ETF) line initiated by EAMM and EAT, which condition a talking-face generator on emotion labels or reference emotional videos. It departs from prior work by identifying two specific failure modes—emotion contamination from the reference ID image (input bias) and uniform emotional intensity across frames (intensity saturation)—and addressing them through a contrastive N-pair neutralization loss and, for the first time in ETF, frame-wise valence-arousal intensity regression based on Russell's circumplex model. The neutralization scheme repurposes metric learning from face recognition to strip emotion from the ID representation, while the intensity branch borrows continuous emotion analysis from affective computing and applies it to the audio-visual generation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The neutralization pre-processing step is a practical, modular contribution that any ETF pipeline could adopt to prevent reference-image emotion from contaminating the output, and the frame-wise intensity regression addresses a widely observed but previously unaddressed source of unnaturalness in generated videos. Limitations include reliance on pseudo VA labels (since MEAD lacks continuous annotations), a small in-the-lab dataset (60 IDs), and single-speaker evaluation, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to in-the-wild multi-speaker scenarios or scales to real-time deployment. The finding that audio-only emotion cues can match or exceed video-assisted methods in perceived emotion accuracy suggests that the driving signal, not the reference visual, should be the primary emotion source—a design principle that could reshape how future ETF systems are architected.
