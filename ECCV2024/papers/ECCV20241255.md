## Problem

Existing fMRI decoding methods are limited to reconstructing 2D images, ignoring the brain's capacity for 3D spatial perception. No dataset pairs fMRI signals with 3D objects, and no framework can extract the spatial-structural features (beyond semantics) needed to reconstruct 3D geometry from brain activity. This paper introduces the Recon3DMind task and the fMRI-Shape dataset to fill both gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241255/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 10 z-scored fMRI frames (with 6-second BOLD lag) per 8-second 360° video, projected onto 256×256 images from HCP-MMP visual ROIs in 32k_fs_LR surface space. During training, 6 fMRI frames and 8 video frames are randomly sampled; at inference, only the middle 6 fMRI frames are used without any visual input. The output is a 3D mesh produced by decoding a VQ latent vector via Argus's VQ decoder.

## Method

MinD-3D is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Neuro-Fusion Encoder uses LEA's pretrained MIM-based encoder to extract per-frame fMRI embeddings, aggregates them across frames via a Feature Aggregation module, and aligns the resulting latent with CLIP vision space through contrastive learning; (2) a Feature Bridge Diffusion Model (a transformer-based diffusion model with 100 timesteps) generates visual latent features conditioned on the fMRI latent, using self-attention to fuse the conditional signal; (3) a Latent Adapted Decoder fine-tunes Argus with adapter layers inserted every 4 transformer blocks, consuming both the fMRI features and the generated visual features to autoregressively produce VQ codes that are decoded into a 3D mesh. Training proceeds in two phases: first the encoder and diffusion model are optimized (2.5 days on one A100), then all parameters including adapters are fine-tuned while Argus's backbone is frozen (1 day on one A100).

## Results

On the fMRI-Shape Core Set (in-distribution, per-subject train/test), MinD-3D achieves 83.9% 2-way top-1 accuracy, 43.2% 10-way top-1 accuracy, LPIPS of 0.230, and SSIM of 0.734 at the semantic level, while reaching FPD of 3.157, CD of 1.742, and EMD of 3.833 at the structural level. In the more challenging Out-of-Distribution settings (Across-Person and Across-Person & Across-Class), MinD-3D still leads with FPD of 3.838/5.689, CD of 2.415/4.181, and EMD of 5.117/7.194 respectively, confirming robustness to individual and category shifts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MinD-3D outperforms both baselines across all seven metrics on the Core Set. Compared to LEA-3D [33], MinD-3D improves 2-way accuracy by 5.2 points (83.9% vs. 78.7%), reduces LPIPS by 56% (0.230 vs. 0.527), and lowers EMD by 28% (3.833 vs. 5.347). Against fMRI-PTE-3D [35], the gains are more moderate but consistent: +2.4 points on 2-way accuracy, LPIPS reduced from 0.433 to 0.230 (a 47% drop), and EMD reduced from 4.621 to 3.833. In the OOD Across-Person test, MinD-3D leads on all three structural metrics (FPD 3.838 vs. 4.501/5.362; CD 2.415 vs. 2.956/3.627; EMD 5.117 vs. 5.772/6.174), and the same pattern holds in the harder Across-Person & Across-Class test, where the gap over LEA-3D widens (e.g., FPD 5.689 vs. 6.958). The ablation rows in Table 2 further confirm that both the diffusion module and the contrastive alignment contribute meaningfully, with removing either degrading structural metrics by roughly 10–15%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fMRI decoding literature, which has focused almost exclusively on 2D image or video reconstruction (e.g., LEA, fMRI-PTE, Mind's Eye), to the novel setting of 3D object reconstruction. Architecturally, it repurposes the pretrained LEA encoder for fMRI feature extraction and the Argus autoregressive 3D shape generator as a decoder, bridging them with a transformer-based diffusion model that translates neural-space features into visual-space features. The use of CLIP contrastive alignment to anchor fMRI embeddings in a shared visual space, and the 360-degree video stimulus design inspired by ZeroNVS, are key conceptual departures from prior 2D-only fMRI decoding pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first fMRI-to-3D-shape dataset (fMRI-Shape) and demonstrating end-to-end 3D reconstruction from brain signals, this work opens a concrete research direction at the intersection of cognitive neuroscience and 3D vision that was previously undefined. However, the dataset is small (14 participants, 1,624 objects), OOD generalization degrades substantially (e.g., FPD nearly doubles in APAC), and absolute reconstruction quality remains modest (43.2% 10-way accuracy), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse populations and object categories. A key unresolved question is how much of the 3D structural information is genuinely encoded in the BOLD signal versus being inferred by the generative prior, which the current evaluation metrics do not fully disentangle.
