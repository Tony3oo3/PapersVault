## Problem

Both NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting struggle to reconstruct scenes containing mirrors because highly specular reflections cause severe appearance inconsistencies between front and back views that MLPs or SH functions cannot model. Existing NeRF-based mirror methods (MS-NeRF, Mirror-NeRF, TraM-NeRF) rely on expensive ray marching and MLP queries, making them impractical for real-time rendering. No prior method achieves both high-fidelity mirror reflection reconstruction and real-time rendering speed.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view calibrated images (resized to 1.6 MP for evaluation), camera poses and a sparse point cloud from COLMAP SfM, and mirror masks segmented by SAM. It produces real-time novel view renderings at 155 FPS, an explicit 3D Gaussian point cloud augmented with per-point mirror labels, a refined mirror plane equation, and mirror masks renderable from arbitrary viewpoints. The method requires mirror segmentation on all input images, and the dual-rendering strategy currently composites the real and mirror images over the full frame rather than only the mirror region.

## Method

MirrorGaussian exploits the mirror symmetry between the real-world scene and its virtual counterpart by reflecting the 3D Gaussian point cloud across the mirror plane to obtain a mirrored set, then rasterizing both sets independently and compositing them with a mirror mask. A three-stage optimization pipeline first trains vanilla 3DGS, then refines the mirror plane equation (initialized via RANSAC on SfM points along SAM mask borders) while freezing Gaussian parameters, and finally jointly optimizes all Gaussian attributes including a new per-point mirror label k∈[0,1] and the mirror plane. The mirror label is rendered via alpha blending to produce viewpoint-dependent masks, while a modified color formula multiplies opacity by k so that mirror-surface points do not occlude the mirrored Gaussians. View-dependent colors of mirrored Gaussians are handled by reflecting the camera view direction rather than transforming SH coefficients, and a distance regularization loss encourages mirror-labeled points to converge onto the plane.

## Results

MirrorGaussian achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS across all four mirror scenes, with average PSNR of 29.19 dB (Coffee-House 26.66, Meeting-Room 30.63, Market 28.35, Lounge 31.10). It renders at 155 FPS on a single V100 GPU, roughly 300–3000× faster than NeRF-based baselines (0.048–0.468 FPS), while training in 1 h 8 min versus 9–18 h for NeRF methods. Compared to vanilla 3DGS (271 FPS, 47 min training), MirrorGaussian trades a modest speed reduction for substantially better mirror-region quality (e.g., LPIPS 0.068 vs 0.086 on Coffee-House).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MirrorGaussian outperforms all three NeRF-based baselines (MS-NeRF, Mirror-NeRF, Ref-NeRF) on SSIM and LPIPS in every scene, with the largest gaps on the Market scene where Ref-NeRF drops to SSIM 0.560 and LPIPS 0.522 versus MirrorGaussian's 0.844 and 0.105. On PSNR, MirrorGaussian leads on three of four scenes but trails MS-NeRF on Meeting-Room (30.63 vs 31.09 dB), likely because MS-NeRF's eight sub-space decomposition captures that particular geometry well. Against vanilla 3DGS, MirrorGaussian improves SSIM by 0.002–0.012 and LPIPS by 0.001–0.022 per scene, confirming that the dual-rendering strategy specifically fixes the blurry-mirror and floater artifacts that plague standard 3DGS. In terms of speed, MirrorGaussian (155 FPS, 1 h 8 min training) is roughly 330× faster in rendering than MS-NeRF (0.048 FPS) and 14× faster than Mirror-NeRF (0.468 FPS), while training 9–17× faster than the NeRF baselines; it is about 1.7× slower in rendering and 1.4× longer in training than vanilla 3DGS due to the dual rasterization pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

MirrorGaussian extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to handle mirror reflections by exploiting the geometric mirror symmetry between the real-world scene and its virtual counterpart. It departs from prior NeRF-based mirror reconstruction methods (MS-NeRF, Mirror-NeRF, TraM-NeRF) that rely on expensive ray marching and MLP queries, replacing them with a rasterization-based dual-rendering strategy that reflects the 3D Gaussian point cloud across the mirror plane. Unlike reflection reconstruction approaches (Ref-NeRF, NeRF-ReN, UniSDF) that model real and reflected radiance fields separately and blend them, MirrorGaussian enforces an explicit geometric correspondence between the two, ensuring the reflected content is a strict mirror image of the real scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve both high-fidelity and real-time rendering of scenes with mirrors, opening the door to interactive applications (AR/VR, cinematography) that were impractical with prior NeRF-based approaches. The explicit point-cloud representation additionally enables scene editing (inserting objects or new mirrors), a capability absent in implicit NeRF methods. Key limitations include the requirement for per-image mirror segmentation (via SAM) and the assumption of a single planar mirror; extending to multiple mirrors, curved reflectors, or dynamic scenes remains open. The dual-rendering strategy currently processes the full frame, and restricting it to the mirror region is identified as a straightforward optimization path.
