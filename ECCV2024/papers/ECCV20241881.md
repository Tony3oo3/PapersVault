## Problem

In federated learning of embedding networks where each client holds data from a single class, sharing true class prototypes with the server or other clients risks leaking sensitive identity information through gradient inversion or model-inversion attacks. Existing approaches either expose true prototypes (FedAwS) or use fixed codewords that ignore inter-client data similarity (FedUV), leaving a gap between privacy preservation and discriminative performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-100), 64×64 face crops (VGGFace2), or 2-second audio segments converted to 40-dimensional Mel filterbank spectrograms (VoxCeleb1). Each client's embedding network produces a 512-dimensional L2-normalized instance embedding, while the learnable class prototype (also L2-normalized) is the quantity that must be shared across clients. The shared output is not the true prototype but a proxy prototype—a linear blend of the true prototype and its top-K nearest neighbors—designed to be informative for contrastive training yet insufficient to recover the original.

## Method

FedHide replaces the true class prototype with a proxy prototype w̄_c = α·w_c + (1−α)·(mean of top-K nearest-neighbor prototypes), L2-normalized, before sharing it with other clients. Training uses a two-term contrastive loss: a positive term pulls instance embeddings toward the client's own true prototype, while a negative term pushes the true prototype away from other clients' proxy prototypes, weighted by λ. The key insight is that the negative loss naturally drives each client's true prototype away from the neighbor regions of other clients, so the proxy (which lives in those neighbor regions) carries little information about the true prototype. The paper provides a convergence guarantee for the non-convex federated objective under standard smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions, and introduces a prototype leakage metric measuring the fraction of clients whose true prototype is the nearest neighbor to their own shared proxy. Empirically, FedHide matches FedAwS accuracy/EER while reducing prototype leakage from 100% to as low as 0.2–4% on VGGFace2, outperforming Gaussian-noise (FedGN) and cosine-similarity (FedCS) baselines on the accuracy–leakage trade-off.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, FedHide(α=0.01, K=10) achieves 58.0% classification accuracy with only 9.6% prototype leakage, matching FedAwS (57.8% ACC) while reducing leakage from 100% to under 10%. On VoxCeleb1, FedHide(α=0.1, K=10) attains 11.9% EER (vs. 12.3% for FedAwS) with 27.8% prototype leakage. On VGGFace2, FedHide(α=0.1, K=10) reaches 10.5% EER with 18.9% leakage, compared to FedAwS's 9.5% EER at 100% leakage. The best privacy-utility trade-off is achieved at α=0.01, K=20, where FedHide reaches 57.6% ACC / 4.3% PL on CIFAR-100, 15.0% EER / 0.8% PL on VoxCeleb1, and 12.9% EER / 0.2% PL on VGGFace2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is FedAwS, which shares true class prototypes with the server (100% prototype leakage by definition). FedHide matches or slightly exceeds FedAwS in utility while drastically reducing leakage: on CIFAR-100, FedHide(0.01, K=10) achieves 58.0% ACC vs. FedAwS's 57.8% at only 9.6% PL; on VoxCeleb1, FedHide(0.1, K=10) achieves 11.9% EER vs. 12.3% at 27.8% PL. The internal comparisons (FedGN with Gaussian noise, FedCS with cosine-similarity-constrained random selection) show a steeper accuracy-privacy trade-off: to reach comparable leakage levels (~10–30%), FedGN and FedCS suffer 10–40 percentage-point drops in accuracy or 5–10-point increases in EER, whereas FedHide maintains near-FedAwS performance at the same leakage budget. Specifically, at ~30–40% PL on CIFAR-100, FedHide(0.1, K=10) retains 57.5% ACC while FedGN(0.2) drops to 24.7% and FedCS(0.3) to 36.8%. On VGGFace2, FedHide(0.1, K=10) achieves 10.5% EER at 18.9% PL, whereas FedGN(0.2) at similar leakage (15.1% PL) only reaches 14.5% EER. Overall, FedHide dominates the accuracy-vs-leakage Pareto frontier across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedHide extends the federated prototype learning framework of FedProto [36] and the FedAvg aggregation scheme [27] to the single-class-per-client setting, where sharing true class prototypes is a privacy risk. It departs from FedAwS [40], which shares true prototypes with the server and relies on a spreadout regularization, and from FedUV [16], which uses fixed codewords that ignore data-driven similarity. The key conceptual departure is the 'hiding in the neighbors' mechanism: rather than adding noise (FedGN) or sampling at a fixed cosine distance (FedCS), the proxy prototype is constructed as a linear blend of the true prototype and the average of its top-K nearest neighbor prototypes, exploiting the fact that the contrastive loss naturally pushes true prototypes away from each other's neighborhoods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that near-FedAwS accuracy (57.6–58.0% vs. 57.8% on CIFAR-100) can be achieved while reducing prototype leakage from 100% to as low as 4.3%, and that this trade-off generalizes across image, audio, and face modalities. However, the privacy protection is heuristic rather than formally guaranteed: the authors acknowledge vulnerability to adaptive attackers who monitor the communication channel over multiple rounds and solve linear inverse problems, and they do not provide a differential-privacy-style bound. Open questions include data-dependent selection of K, formal privacy guarantees, and whether the method remains effective under stronger threat models (e.g., a colluding server with partial knowledge of other clients' prototypes).
