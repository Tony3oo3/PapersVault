## Problem

Existing video class-incremental learning (VCIL) methods rely on retaining exemplar frames or features from prior tasks to prevent catastrophic forgetting, which overlooks the geometric connection between base and novel classes and introduces privacy leakage risks. Moreover, conventional video representation strategies (e.g., mean-pooling frame features) fail to capture discriminative temporal-spatial structure needed for robust generalization in incremental settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip, from which T frames are randomly sampled and fed through a TSM backbone (ResNet-34 or ResNet-50) to produce per-frame feature embeddings of shape T×C×H×W. After global average pooling, the temporal feature matrix Z_i ∈ R^{C×T} is classified by projecting into per-class orthogonal subspaces, yielding a softmax probability over all classes accumulated across incremental sessions. Notably, the method is exemplar-free: no frames, prototypes, or prompts from previous tasks are stored, and inference requires no additional memory or operations beyond a standard forward pass.

## Method

STSP comprises two coupled components. First, a Temporal-based Subspace Classifier (TSC) replaces the standard FC layer by representing each class with an orthogonal subspace basis P_m ∈ R^{C×τ} (τ≪C); classification is performed by computing the Frobenius-norm distance between the temporal feature subspace Z_i Z_i^T and each class basis P_m P_m^T, followed by softmax. Intra-class and inter-class orthogonal constraints are imposed on both the temporal features and the subspace bases to enforce distinct, non-overlapping geometric structures per class. Second, a Spatial-based Gradient Projection (SGP) strategy prevents forgetting without exemplars: at each training step, the gradient of every linear layer and the TSC is projected onto the approximate null space of the mean covariance matrix M_{t-1} of input features from all prior tasks, computed via SVD with an energy-retention threshold η. This ensures that parameter updates do not alter the network's response to previously seen inputs, satisfying the preservation condition f(x_k; w_t) = f(x_k; w_k) for all prior tasks.

## Results

On UCF101, STSP achieves 81.15% accuracy under the 10×5 stages setting, 82.84% under 5×10 stages, and 79.25% under 2×25 stages, outperforming the strongest baseline FrameMaker by 3.02%, 6.46%, and 3.48% respectively. On HMDB51, STSP reaches 56.99% (5×5 stages) and 49.19% (1×25 stages), surpassing FrameMaker by 9.45% and 6.54%. On Something-Something V2, STSP achieves 69.68% (10×9 stages) and 70.87% (5×18 stages), a dramatic improvement of over 32 percentage points over the next-best method FrameMaker (37.25% and 30.98%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STSP outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and incremental setting. Against the strongest video-specific baseline FrameMaker [23], STSP gains 3.02–6.46% on UCF101, 6.54–9.45% on HMDB51, and a striking 32.43–39.89% on Something-Something V2, where FrameMaker scores only 37.25% (10×9) and 30.98% (5×18) versus STSP's 69.68% and 70.87%. The video-specific distillation method TCD [22] trails STSP by 6.26–10.65% on UCF101 and 4.65–11.65% on HMDB51. General-purpose CIL methods UCIR [12] and PODNet [8] are further behind, with UCIR reaching 74.31% and PODNet 73.26% on UCF101 (10×5) versus STSP's 81.15%. The earliest baselines LwFMC [16] and LwM [6] are substantially weaker (42–43% on UCF101 10×5), indicating that their regularization-only strategies are insufficient for the video domain. Notably, STSP achieves these gains without retaining any exemplars or prototypes from prior tasks, whereas FrameMaker and TCD rely on stored frames or condensed representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient projection framework for continual learning (Saha et al., ICLR 2021; Wang et al., CVPR 2021) from image CIL to the video domain, adapting the null-space constraint to both linear layers and a novel temporal-based subspace classifier. It departs from prior VCIL methods (TCD, FrameMaker, ST-Prompt) that rely on storing exemplars or prompts from previous tasks, instead using spatial-based gradient projection to constrain updates without retaining any class-specific data. The subspace classifier draws on the line of work using orthogonal subspace bases for class representation (Simon et al., Cheraghian et al., Akyurek et al.), but applies it to temporal video features with intra- and inter-class orthogonality constraints not present in prior subspace CIL methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exemplar-free formulation addresses a practical privacy concern inherent in prior VCIL methods that must store frames or prototypes from previous tasks. The method's significant advantage on Something-Something V2 (emphasizing subtle motion cues) suggests that explicit temporal subspace modeling is particularly valuable for fine-grained action discrimination, a direction prior VCIL work largely ignored by pooling frame features. A key limitation is the need to store and update mean covariance matrices for every linear layer and the TSC, which scales poorly with large backbones and remains an open question for practical deployment. Whether the gradient projection strategy generalizes to transformer-based video backbones or longer incremental sequences is unexplored.
