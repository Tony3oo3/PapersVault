## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained vision-language models on downstream tasks improves in-distribution accuracy but degrades out-of-distribution robustness. Existing parameter-space regularization methods (e.g., L2-SP, FTP) constrain parameter deviations uniformly across all weights, which is suboptimal because not all neurons contribute equally to the downstream task. This paper asks: can we achieve better ID/OOD trade-offs by constraining parameter deviations at a fine-grained, per-neuron level guided by task importance?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241808/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images fed into a pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/16 vision encoder and produces class labels for image classification. The fine-tuning process consumes a downstream task dataset (e.g., ImageNet, iWildCam, FMoW) to compute per-neuron importance scores via gradient magnitudes, and the regularizer is then applied during optimization. The method is agnostic to the supervised loss (cross-entropy or contrastive) and adds no extra parameters or architectural changes to the base model.

## Method

The paper introduces a neuron importance score defined as the mean absolute gradient of the empirical risk over the downstream dataset, derived from a first-order Taylor expansion of the risk change attributable to each parameter. Two fine-tuning strategies leverage this score: (1) importance-guided selective fine-tuning, which updates only the top-k% most important neurons while freezing the rest, and (2) importance-guided regularization, which adds a per-neuron penalty R(w_i^t, w_i^0) = r̄_i · |w_i^t − w_i^0| that disproportionately constrains the deviation of the most task-important neurons from their pre-trained values. The importance scores are normalized block-wise (within each transformer block) to account for large variance across layers. The key insight is that preserving the most important neurons' pre-trained weights while allowing less important neurons to adapt freely yields the best ID/OOD trade-off, and this regularizer is orthogonal to representation-space methods like FLYP, enabling additive improvements without extra computational overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet (CLIP ViT-B/16), the proposed importance-guided regularization combined with FLYP achieves 82.5% top-1 ID accuracy and 62.0% average OOD accuracy across 5 distribution shifts, improving OOD by 2.5% over FLYP alone (59.5%) while maintaining identical ID performance. Combined with WiSE-FT, the method reaches 63.0% OOD average. On iWildCam, FLYP + the regularizer yields 51.1% macro F1 (ID) and 36.1% (OOD), a 0.8% OOD gain over FLYP; on FMoW, it achieves 67.6% worst-case region accuracy (ID) and 42.2% (OOD), a 1.4% OOD improvement. In low-shot regimes (1-shot to 20%), the regularizer consistently adds 0.3–3.7% ID and 0.1–3.7% OOD over FLYP on ImageNet and iWildCam.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLYP (the strongest representation-space baseline), the importance-guided regularizer adds +2.5% OOD on ImageNet (59.5→62.0) with no ID loss, +0.8% OOD on iWildCam (35.3→36.1), and +1.4% OOD on FMoW (40.8→42.2), establishing new state-of-the-art in all three settings. Compared to parameter-space baselines that share the same intuition of constraining weight deviation—L2-SP and FTP—the method achieves comparable ID (within 0.3%) but substantially higher OOD: +4.2% over L2-SP (57.8→62.0) and +0.8% over FTP (61.2→62.0) on ImageNet. Against LP-FT, the method gains +0.8% ID and +1.7% OOD on ImageNet. The method also synergizes with WiSE-FT weight interpolation, yielding 63.0% OOD (the highest single OOD number in Table 1), though at a slightly lower ID (81.7 vs. 82.5 for FLYP+Ours). In low-shot regimes, the regularizer consistently improves both FLYP and vanilla fine-tuning by 1–3.7% ID and 0.1–3.7% OOD, with the largest gains in the 1-shot setting on ImageNet (+3.7% ID, +3.7% OOD over FLYP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-space robust fine-tuning lineage (WiSE-FT, L2-SP, FTP) by replacing their uniform or gradient-projection-based constraints with a per-neuron importance-weighted regularizer. The importance score itself is repurposed from the neural network pruning literature (Molchanov et al., CVPR 2019), where first-order Taylor expansion of empirical risk was used to identify prunable neurons; here the same gradient-magnitude proxy is used to modulate fine-tuning updates rather than to remove weights. Crucially, the method is positioned as orthogonal to representation-space approaches like FLYP and LP-FT, and the paper demonstrates that combining the regularizer with FLYP's contrastive objective yields the best results, bridging the two previously separate research directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in a drop-in regularizer that adds negligible computational overhead (16% faster than FLYP, 11% faster than FTP) while consistently improving OOD accuracy across ImageNet, iWildCam, and FMoW in both full-shot and low-shot regimes. The conceptual insight that the optimal strategy flips depending on how many parameters are allowed to deviate—prioritize updating important neurons when few can change, but protect them when many can—provides a useful design principle for future fine-tuning methods. Limitations include evaluation only on vision-language models (CLIP) and image classification; whether the per-neuron importance regularizer transfers to self-supervised backbones, generative tasks, or non-Transformer architectures remains open.
