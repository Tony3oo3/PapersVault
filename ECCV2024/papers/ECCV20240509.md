## Problem

Goal-oriented planning in instructional videos requires forecasting multi-step action sequences from visual observations to a target goal, but prior methods depend on extensive supervised training that introduces dataset bias and fails to generalize to unseen tasks or varying action granularities. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether LLMs, which encode broad procedural knowledge from web-scale pretraining, can serve as both the knowledge source and the evaluation tool for zero/few-shot planning without task-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240509/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For Visual Planning for Assistance (VPA), the input is an untrimmed video history segmented into 1-second clips plus a natural-language goal description; for Procedural Planning (PP), the input is a start-state image, a goal-state image, and a task name. Visual inputs are first converted to text via VideoCLIP action recognition (video) or a CLIP-based step classifier (images), so the LLM always operates on textual action descriptions. The output is an ordered sequence of T categorical action labels (e.g., 'break eggs', 'pour batter') with T=1, 3, or 4 for VPA and T=3 or 4 for PP, where each label is mapped from free-form LLM output to the nearest admissible action via Sentence-BERT cosine similarity.

## Method

VIDAssist follows a Socratic pipeline that converts all visual inputs into text, then generates the optimal action plan through a three-stage search loop: (i) Propose — sample K candidate next actions from Llama-2-70B conditioned on the observation, goal, and previously predicted steps; (ii) Assess — score each candidate with a weighted composite of four value functions: token-level text generation likelihood, cosine-similarity mapping confidence to admissible actions, an LLM-based partial-plan coherence check (extracting YES/NO logits from a dedicated evaluation prompt), and a few-shot task-graph transition probability; (iii) Search — run breadth-first search over the candidate tree, retaining only the top-K̃ (K̃<K) actions per step and backtracking to recover the optimal plan. The key distinction from prior LLM-prompting baselines is the deliberate, multi-candidate search with task-specific heuristic evaluation, which the ablations show is critical for long-horizon planning where naive autoregressive generation degrades sharply.

## Results

On the COIN dataset for Visual Planning for Assistance (VPA), few-shot VIDAssist (3 in-context examples) achieves 21.8% SR and 52.8% mAcc at T=3, and 13.8% SR and 38.3% mAcc at T=4, surpassing the fully supervised SOTA VLaMP by 3.5% and 4.8% SR respectively. For Procedural Planning (PP) on COIN, few-shot VIDAssist (10 examples) reaches 20.78% SR at T=4, outperforming the supervised SOTA LFP (15.97% SR) by 4.81%. In zero-shot settings, VIDAssist outperforms the LLM baseline by 12.9% SR (VPA, T=3, COIN) and 7.41% SR (PP, T=4, COIN), demonstrating the value of the deliberate search-based planning mechanism over simple LLM prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIDAssist consistently outperforms all zero-shot baselines. Against the LLM Baseline [25] (simple prompting), VIDAssist gains 12.9% SR on COIN and 6.6% on CrossTask for VPA T=3, and 7.41% and 6.62% SR for PP T=4, showing that naive LLM prompting degrades sharply over longer horizons. Against the LLM Agent Baseline [10] (which also samples multiple candidates but lacks the assess-and-search mechanism), VIDAssist still leads by 12.2% SR (VPA T=3, COIN zero-shot) and 4.29% SR (PP T=3, COIN zero-shot), confirming the contribution of the composite value functions and BFS pruning. Against fully supervised methods, few-shot VIDAssist (3 examples) beats VLaMP [16] by 3.5%/4.8% SR on COIN and 1.7%/3.0% on CrossTask for VPA T=3/T=4, and few-shot VIDAssist (10 examples) surpasses LFP [14] by 4.81% SR on COIN for PP T=4 (20.78% vs. 15.97%), though it trails LFP slightly on PP T=3 (29.20% vs. 30.64% SR). On CrossTask PP, few-shot VIDAssist (15.45% SR at T=4) approaches but does not fully match the supervised E3P [26] (16.49%) and LFP (15.97%), indicating the gap narrows with more in-context examples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Socratic model paradigm [31] for visual-to-text conversion and the LLM-as-zero-shot-planner approach [10] by introducing a deliberate search-based planning loop (propose-assess-search with BFS) that was absent in prior LLM-based video planning. It departs from the fully supervised procedural planning literature (DDN, VLaMP, LFP, etc.) by requiring only zero or a handful of in-context examples, and it unifies two previously separate task formulations—Visual Planning for Assistance and Procedural Planning—under a single framework. The key conceptual departure from the LLM Agent baseline [10] is the addition of task-specific value functions (partial plan evaluation, task graph, text mapping confidence) that guide a breadth-first search over candidate action sequences rather than relying on greedy autoregressive generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Demonstrating that a few-shot LLM-based planner can outperform fully supervised state-of-the-art methods (by 7.7% SR in VPA and 4.81% in PP on COIN) suggests that procedural knowledge embedded in LLMs, combined with structured search, can substitute for large-scale task-specific annotation—a significant practical advantage for deploying planning in diverse real-world settings. However, the ground-truth action history ablation reveals that visual understanding remains the dominant bottleneck (13–37% SR gains when replacing predicted with ground-truth actions), indicating that the framework's ceiling is currently limited by upstream video/image models. Open questions include whether the BFS search scales to much longer horizons or more complex multi-step tasks, and whether the value function design generalizes beyond the 180-task COIN and 18-task CrossTask benchmarks.
