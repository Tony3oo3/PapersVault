## Problem

Acquiring teacher supervision for ViT knowledge distillation is prohibitively expensive, as the teacher must process every training sample at full resolution. While token pruning and mask-based SSL exploit masking for computation savings, neither addresses the specific question of how masking a pre-trained teacher's input affects its supervision quality for a student, which is a fundamentally different objective from preserving the teacher's own prediction accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241799/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The student ViT receives the full 224×224 RGB image (196 patches of 16×16) or full audio spectrogram (600 patches), while the teacher ViT receives a masked version with a fraction of patches removed. The student's last-layer class-token attention scores determine which patches are masked for the teacher. Both models output class logits (e.g., 1000-way for ImageNet-1k, 50-way for ESC-50), and the distillation loss matches the student's full-image logits to the teacher's masked-image logits. When teacher and student have different patch counts, the student's attention is bilinearly interpolated to the teacher's patch grid.

## Method

MaskedKD masks the teacher's input patches based on the student's last-layer class-token attention scores: the student first predicts on the full image, its averaged multi-head attention from the class token to each patch is computed, and the lowest-scoring patches are removed before the teacher forward pass. The training loss combines standard cross-entropy on the student's full-image prediction with a flexible distillation loss (logit KL, manifold, or attention) between the student's full-image output and the teacher's masked-image output. The key insight is that student-guided masking acts as an implicit curriculum: early in training it degrades teacher accuracy (making supervision easier to fit, akin to a teaching assistant), while later it provides diverse image views that enrich supervision. Two critical design choices distinguish this from related work: masking must occur at the input (not intermediate layers, as in ToMe) to preserve supervision quality, and the teacher must be masked (not the student, as in mask-based SSL) to avoid degrading the student's final accuracy.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k supervised ViT distillation, MaskedKD reduces teacher supervision cost by 25–50% (measured in total PFLOPs) without degrading student top-1 accuracy across a wide range of student–teacher pairs. For example, distilling DeiT-S → DeiT-Ti with logit distillation at 50% masking achieves 75.0% top-1 at 866 PFLOPs (−51% vs. 1770 PFLOPs unmasked), while distilling DeiT3-H-21k → MAE-ViT-H at 75% masking reaches 87.2% top-1 at 5301 PFLOPs (−51%). In the most aggressive setting (DeiT3-S → DeiT-Ti at 25% masking), supervision cost drops by 75% (439 PFLOPs) with only a 0.4-point accuracy drop. On ESC-50 audio classification, MaskedKD at 83% masking preserves 86.4% accuracy while cutting teacher TFLOPs from 92.8 to 75.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla logit distillation (Hinton et al. [14]), MaskedKD matches or marginally exceeds student accuracy while cutting supervision cost by 25–50%; for instance, DeiT-Ti/DeiT-S logit distillation reaches 75.0% at 1770 PFLOPs unmasked versus 75.0% at 866 PFLOPs with 50% masking, and in several settings (e.g., DeiT-S/DeiT-B) masking actually yields a small accuracy gain (80.9% vs. 80.8%). Against ToMe (Bolya et al. [4]), which removes tokens in intermediate layers, MaskedKD achieves a better accuracy–computation tradeoff for distillation: although ToMe preserves the teacher's own prediction quality better in the high-compute regime, input-level masking retains supervision quality more effectively, as shown in Fig. 6. Against DINO (Caron et al. [5]) in the self-supervised setting, applying MaskedKD at 66% masking improves linear-probing accuracy from 73.7% to 74.2% while reducing total teacher FLOPs from 589 to 384 PFLOPs (−35%), demonstrating that the student-guided masking criterion transfers beyond supervised distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes input token masking—a technique well-studied in token pruning (ToMe, DynamicViT) and mask-based self-supervised learning (MAE, DINO)—for a fundamentally different objective: reducing the supervision cost of a pre-trained teacher in supervised ViT distillation. It builds directly on the classic logit distillation framework of Hinton et al. [14] but departs from both token pruning (which preserves teacher prediction quality) and mask-based SSL (which masks the student for pretext tasks) by masking the teacher's input guided by the student's attention scores. The key conceptual shift is that supervision quality, not prediction quality, is the quantity to preserve, which leads to different optimal masking strategies (input-level rather than intermediate-layer, teacher-side rather than student-side).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 25–50% reduction in teacher FLOPs without accuracy loss is practically significant for large-scale distillation (e.g., ViT-G on ImageNet costs ~10⁴ TPUv3-days), and the method is drop-in compatible with existing distillation algorithms (logit, manifold, attention, G2SD). The curriculum-learning interpretation—masking makes early supervision easier to fit and late supervision more diverse—offers a useful lens for understanding why the technique works. A key limitation is scope: MaskedKD is specialized to supervised distillation of transformer-based models, and generalizing to non-transformer architectures or unsupervised settings remains open. The paper also notes a potential negative societal impact: attention-based token selection may amplify spurious correlations in the training data.
