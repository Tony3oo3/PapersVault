## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods for human motion forecasting assume the target domain remains stationary, yet real-world deployment involves continuously shifting target distributions over time. This non-stationarity causes catastrophic forgetting and error accumulation when the model sequentially adapts to evolving domains, severely limiting practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240831/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 1-second sequence of 17-joint 3D coordinates (normalized to [-1,1]) and predicts the next 1-second pose sequence. Crucially, the model operates in a sequential online fashion where each test sample arrives individually, and the multi-domain augmenter generates H=24 diverse augmented copies of each input to estimate per-joint uncertainty before any adaptation step. No source-domain data is accessed during test-time, preserving data privacy.

## Method

HoCoTTA employs a teacher-student pair (both siMLPe) where the teacher is front-loaded with an adversarially trained multi-domain augmenter (9 GCN layers) that produces diverse novel-domain augmentations. The homeostasis assessment computes an uncertainty matrix U by measuring normalized differences between augmented and original teacher predictions, then a diagonal Fisher information matrix quantifies per-parameter sensitivity to the new domain. A τ-quantile threshold (τ=0.2) creates a binary mask that isolates the top 20% domain-sensitive parameters from the remaining domain-invariant ones. During adaptation, only the masked domain-sensitive parameters in the student are updated via a single gradient step toward the teacher's pseudo-ground-truth (weighted L2 + bone-length loss), while invariant parameters are frozen; the teacher is then refreshed via EMA (α=0.99). This selective update prevents catastrophic forgetting across sequentially arriving domain shifts.

## Results

On the standard data-splitting setup (Setup-1), HoCoTTA achieves the best MPJPE across all three datasets at the 1000 ms horizon: 98.4 mm on Human3.6M, 71.1 mm on CMU Mocap, and 131.8 mm on GRAB, outperforming the previous TTA-based SOTA H/P-TTP (103.7 / 74.4 / 138.0 mm) by 5.1–6.2 mm. For P-MPJPE at 1000 ms, HoCoTTA reaches 84.3 / 54.8 / 112.2 mm versus 90.2 / 60.1 / 117.3 mm for H/P-TTP. On PCK@150 mm at 1000 ms, HoCoTTA attains 76.1% / 81.3% / 72.3% on the three datasets, consistently exceeding all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoCoTTA outperforms all seven baselines on every metric and dataset. Against the strongest non-TTA baseline siMLPe [22], HoCoTTA reduces MPJPE by 11.0 mm on Human3.6M (98.4 vs 109.4) and by 5.7 mm on GRAB (131.8 vs 137.5) at 1000 ms. The most direct comparison is with H/P-TTP [12], the previous TTA-based SOTA: HoCoTTA improves MPJPE by 5.3 mm on Human3.6M, 3.3 mm on CMU Mocap, and 6.2 mm on GRAB, and P-MPJPE by 5.9 / 5.3 / 5.1 mm respectively. The GCN-based baselines (LTD, MSRGCN, PGBIG, SPGSN) are consistently 10–20 mm worse in MPJPE than HoCoTTA at 1000 ms, and the RNN-based Resi. sup. [49] is the weakest by a wide margin (165.0 mm on Human3.6M). The gains are most pronounced on GRAB (the cross-dataset setup), where the distribution shift is largest, confirming that the parameter-isolation strategy is most beneficial under severe domain drift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own H/P-TTP framework (ICCV 2023) for test-time personalizable motion forecasting by addressing the non-stationary target domain assumption that all prior TTA methods for motion prediction rely on. It draws on the CoTTA teacher-student continual TTA paradigm but replaces CoTTA's random restoration strategy with a principled Fisher-information-based parameter isolation mechanism, and adapts the multi-domain augmentation architecture from Mao et al. (ICCV 2021) to serve as a homeostasis probe. The key conceptual departure is treating domain-sensitive and domain-invariant parameters as separable via a τ-quantile threshold on the FIM diagonal, enabling selective updates that preserve cross-domain knowledge across a sequence of shifting distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continual domain drift scenario is arguably the realistic deployment setting for motion forecasting in autonomous driving or robotics, yet prior TTA methods implicitly assume a single static target distribution; this paper provides a concrete mechanism (FIM-based parameter isolation) to handle that gap without access to source data. Limitations include reliance on a single backbone (siMLPe), a diagonal FIM approximation that discards inter-parameter correlations, and evaluation limited to three motion datasets with 17-joint skeletons. Open questions include whether the τ-quantile threshold generalizes across architectures and task domains, how the method behaves under very rapid or adversarial domain shifts, and whether the multi-domain augmenter can be replaced by cheaper uncertainty proxies.
