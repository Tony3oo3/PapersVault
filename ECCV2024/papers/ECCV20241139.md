## Problem

Existing CLIP-based zero-shot semantic segmentation methods (e.g., ZegCLIP, DeOP, SAN) train with classification loss on seen classes, creating an 'objective misalignment' where the model overfits to seen-class recognition rather than generalizing to unseen classes. This misalignment is particularly severe in segmentation because pixel-level supervision provides a stronger signal that further entrenches seen-class dominance in the feature space, causing unseen classes to be misclassified as semantically similar seen categories.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (processed at 640×640 for the learnable image encoder and 320×320 for the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder) and a set of class label texts (encoded by CLIP's text encoder into class prototypes). It produces N=100 class-agnostic mask proposals, each assigned a class label from the union of seen and unseen categories plus a background class, yielding a full pixel-level segmentation map. The CLIP vision encoder and semantic decoder use the first 6 and last 3 layers of ViT-B/16 respectively, with prompt learning applied to adapt CLIP features to the target dataset.

## Method

AlignZeg restructures the segmentation pipeline around three components targeting objective misalignment. (1) Mutually-Refined Proposal Extraction (MRPE) fuses features from a learnable 8-layer transformer encoder and a prompt-tuned CLIP encoder, then applies bidirectional cross-attention between mask queries and visual features to extract high-quality class-agnostic masks. (2) Generalization-Enhanced Proposal Classification (GEPC) introduces a feature expansion strategy that interpolates both proposal features and class embeddings (manifold-mixup-style, with α=3 to push virtual samples away from seen classes) to reserve feature space for unseen categories, combined with M multiple background prototypes regularized by a pairwise distance loss to capture diverse backgrounds. (3) Predictive Bias Correction (PBC) trains a binary classifier on proposal features to flag potential unseen-class proposals (using top-K loss ranking against seen-class ground truths for negative selection), then suppresses seen-class scores for flagged proposals at inference via a threshold γ=0.3, explicitly correcting the residual prediction bias.

## Results

On the Generalized Zero-Shot Semantic Segmentation (GZS3) benchmark, AlignZeg achieves hIoU of 91.0 on PASCAL VOC 2012, 44.6 on COCO-Stuff 164K, and 57.4 on PASCAL Context, surpassing the previous SOTA by 5.3%, 3.8%, and 2.4% respectively. The gains are driven primarily by unseen-class mIoU improvements of 7.5%, 8.6%, and 5.1% over the best prior methods. In the strict Zero-Shot (ZS3) setting (unseen classes only), AlignZeg reaches 94.6 mIoU on VOC and 82.9 mIoU on COCO, outperforming ZegCLIP by 12.1% and SAN by 6.0% on COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignZeg outperforms all 12 compared baselines across all three GZS3 benchmarks. On PASCAL VOC 2012, it surpasses SAN (hIoU 85.1) and ZegCLIP (84.3) by 5.9 and 6.7 points respectively, with the largest gap in unseen-class mIoU (88.2 vs. 78.9/77.8). On COCO-Stuff 164K, the improvement over ZegCLIP (hIoU 40.8) is 3.8 points, driven by an 8.6-point jump in m(U) (50.0 vs. 41.4), while seen-class m(S) remains essentially unchanged (40.2 vs. 40.2). On PASCAL Context, AlignZeg beats SAN (hIoU 55.0) by 2.4 points and ZegCLIP (49.9) by 7.5 points. In the strict ZS3 setting, the advantage is even more pronounced: 82.9 mIoU on COCO vs. 70.8 for ZegCLIP (+12.1%) and 76.9 for SAN (+6.0%). For cross-dataset generalization (Table 3), AlignZeg leads ZegCLIP by 13.1 points on P-59 (54.3 vs. 41.2) and 1.1 points on VOC (94.7 vs. 93.6), confirming that the gains are not dataset-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

AlignZeg builds directly on the CLIP-based one-pass zero-shot segmentation paradigm established by ZegCLIP and SAN, reusing the CLIP image encoder as a semantic decoder and employing prompt learning to adapt CLIP features. Its Feature Expansion Strategy adapts manifold mixup from classification to the segmentation setting by interpolating both proposal features and class embeddings simultaneously, while its multi-background prototype idea extends the single-prototype background handling common in prior work. The key conceptual departure is the explicit diagnosis of 'objective misalignment'—the gap between optimizing seen-class classification loss and the true zero-shot goal—and the introduction of a proposal-level bias correction step (PBC) that has no direct analogue in prior zero-shot segmentation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'objective misalignment' framing provides a useful diagnostic for why pixel-level zero-shot segmentation underperforms relative to image-level zero-shot classification, and the 7.1% unseen-class mIoU gain demonstrates that the problem is addressable through pipeline-level redesign rather than merely scaling data or models. The PBC component introduces a genuinely new architectural step (proposal-level unseen-class detection) that could be adopted by other zero-shot segmentation methods. However, the approach remains tightly coupled to CLIP and requires careful threshold tuning (γ), and it is unclear whether the mutual refinement in MRPE generalizes to non-CLIP backbones or to open-vocabulary settings with arbitrary class sets.
