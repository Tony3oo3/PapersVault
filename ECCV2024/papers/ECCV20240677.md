## Problem

Existing safety measures for text-to-image generation are either simple text blacklists that are trivially circumvented by rephrasing, or LLM-based classifiers that impose high computational costs and remain vulnerable to adversarial attacks targeting the text encoder. No available solution simultaneously offers efficient inference, robustness to rephrasing and encoder-level adversarial perturbations, and the ability to update the blocked-concept list at test time without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240677/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a natural-language T2I prompt and a user-defined set of blacklisted concepts (e.g., 578–723 short phrases) as input. The prompt is encoded by the T2I model's frozen CLIP text encoder, then passed through a lightweight Embedding Mapping Layer conditioned on each concept; the output is a binary safe/unsafe decision based on whether any concept–prompt cosine distance falls below a tunable threshold γ. Because concept embeddings are pre-computed and the only added computation is the mapping layer (~1 ms, ~13 MB GPU memory for 578 concepts), the check runs orders of magnitude faster than the diffusion step it guards.

## Method

Latent Guard reframes safety as concept-presence detection in a learned latent space rather than direct safe/unsafe classification. An Embedding Mapping Layer—multi-head cross-attention (concept as query, prompt as key/value) followed by MLPs—sits on top of a frozen CLIP text encoder and is trained with a supervised contrastive loss that pulls the embedding of an unsafe prompt close to its associated concept while pushing it away from the corresponding safe prompt (created by an LLM removing the unsafe concept) and all other batch negatives. Training data (the CoPro dataset, 226k prompts over 723 concepts) is synthesized with Mixtral 8x7B, and only the ~1.3 M-parameter mapping layer is optimized (~30 min on one GPU). At test time the blacklist can be freely added to or reduced without any retraining, and the cross-attention mechanism provides robustness to synonym substitution and to adversarial prompts optimized in the CLIP encoder space.

## Results

On the CoPro dataset, Latent Guard achieves 0.868 accuracy (ID Explicit) and 0.985 AUC, outperforming all baselines across in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings. On the more challenging Synonym and Adversarial test sets, Latent Guard maintains 0.828/0.829 accuracy (ID) and 0.914/0.908 AUC, while baselines drop sharply (e.g., Text Blacklist falls to 0.549/0.587 accuracy). On unseen datasets (Unsafe Diffusion and I2P++), Latent Guard achieves 0.794 and 0.701 accuracy respectively, again leading all baselines. Inference cost is minimal: ~1 ms and 13 MB GPU memory for a single prompt with 578 concepts, versus seconds for the full Stable Diffusion generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent Guard outperforms CLIPScore by a large margin on every setting: e.g., 0.868 vs. 0.628 accuracy (ID Explicit) and 0.985 vs. 0.697 AUC, with the gap widening on Synonym (0.828 vs. 0.557) and Adversarial (0.829 vs. 0.504) sets where CLIPScore's AUC drops below 0.51. Similarly, BERTScore is consistently beaten (0.868 vs. 0.632 accuracy ID Explicit; 0.985 vs. 0.783 AUC), and BERTScore actually degrades below chance on Adversarial AUC (0.481). The LLM baseline (7B parameters, 0.383 s/prompt) is the strongest competitor, reaching 0.867 accuracy on ID Adversarial, but Latent Guard still leads on ID Explicit (0.868 vs. 0.747) and OOD Explicit (0.867 vs. 0.746) while requiring only ~1 ms and 13 MB of GPU memory. On unseen datasets (Unsafe Diffusion, I2P++), Latent Guard achieves 0.794/0.701 accuracy versus 0.752/0.650 for the LLM and 0.726/0.526 for CLIPScore, confirming generalization beyond the CoPro training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

Latent Guard departs from the dominant paradigm in T2I safety—binary safe/unsafe classification via text blacklists, LLM-based filtering (as in DALL-E 3), or post-hoc NSFW image classifiers—by reframing the problem as concept detection in a learned latent space on top of a frozen text encoder. It repurposes supervised contrastive learning and multi-head cross-attention (originally from vision transformers) to align embeddings of blacklisted concepts with prompts containing them, drawing on LLM-generated paired data (CoPro) rather than large annotated corpora. The approach is positioned as complementary to diffusion-level interventions like Safe Latent Diffusion [31] and unlearning methods [24,40], which require expensive finetuning, and explicitly targets the adversarial prompt optimization attacks [34,35] that bypass existing text-level filters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practically deployable safety layer with negligible overhead (~1 ms, 13 MB) and test-time blacklist adaptability without retraining, addressing a real operational gap in commercial T2I systems. The CoPro dataset (226K prompts, 723 concepts) is a useful public resource for concept-level safety research. Open questions include generalization beyond CLIP-based encoders to newer T2I architectures (e.g., SDXL's T5 encoder), scalability to very large or rapidly evolving blacklists, and whether the emergent safe/unsafe separation in the latent space (observed via t-SNE but not explicitly trained for) holds under distribution shift or multi-concept prompts. The paper does not evaluate multi-turn or iterative adversarial attacks, leaving robustness under sustained red-teaming untested.
