## Problem

Existing multi-task visual grounding methods learn REC and RES jointly but communicate between the two tasks only through generic self-attention in the decoder, without explicitly enforcing that both tasks must refer to the same object given identical image-text inputs. This paper argues that this simple yet strong constraint has been overlooked, and that the lack of explicit coordination leads to suboptimal information sharing between bounding-box and segmentation estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240184/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image resized to 640×640 and a free-form natural language expression (up to 20 tokens for Refcoco/Refcoco+, 30 for Refcocog) as input. It produces two outputs simultaneously: a bounding box decoded as four directional displacements relative to a predicted center point (rather than absolute coordinates), and a segmentation mask decoded from K=100 selected multi-level feature queries. The center point itself is an intermediate 2D coordinate prediction that acts as a shared spatial anchor for both output heads.

## Method

LoA-Trans builds on Deformable DETR with a Swin Transformer visual backbone and BERT text encoder, using cross-attention-based early fusion at each Swin layer to produce multi-level multi-modality features. A Location-Aware Network predicts a 2D center point from the last-layer features, which is fed as a common reference prompt to both the bounding-box and segmentation decoders, explicitly constraining them to target the same object. To mitigate center estimation errors, a query selection mechanism replaces random decoder queries: a pixel classification network scores features across three levels (F2, F3, F4), and the top-K features are re-ranked by cosine similarity with the global language embedding to form location-aware mask queries. A TaskSyn Network in the decoder updates segmentation and bounding-box queries with separate MLPs, then max-pools the updated segmentation queries and adds them to the bounding-box queries, enabling precise size-information transfer from segmentation to detection. Training uses four losses (center L1, pixel classification focal+dice, bounding-box L1+GIoU, segmentation focal+dice) with weights 2.0, 2.0, 5.0, and 2.0 respectively.

## Results

LoA-Trans-B achieves state-of-the-art performance on both REC (Pr@0.5) and RES (mIoU) across all three datasets. On REC, it reaches 90.60 on Refcoco testA, 84.95 on Refcoco+ testA, and 80.18 on Refcocog test, surpassing the previous best multi-task method VG-LAW by 1.28, 3.91, and 3.22 points respectively. On RES, LoA-Trans-B attains 78.60 mIoU on Refcoco testA, 73.59 on Refcoco+ testA, and 68.77 on Refcocog test, outperforming VG-LAW by 1.09, 3.21, and 2.69 points. The smaller LoA-Trans-S variant also outperforms all prior methods, e.g., 90.17 Pr@0.5 on Refcoco testA and 77.90 mIoU on Refcoco testA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoA-Trans-B outperforms all 11 compared baselines on both REC and RES across all three datasets. Against the strongest prior multi-task method VG-LAW [30], LoA-Trans-B gains +1.13 Pr@0.5 on Refcoco val, +3.91 on Refcoco+ val, and +3.90 on Refcocog val for REC, and +1.04 mIoU on Refcoco val, +2.77 on Refcoco+ val, and +3.38 on Refcocog val for RES. Single-task methods such as QRNet [40] (85.85 Pr@0.5 on Refcoco testA) and LAVT [37] (76.89 mIoU on Refcoco testA) are surpassed by margins of 4.75 and 1.71 points respectively, demonstrating that the joint multi-task framework with explicit location constraints is more effective than task-specific designs. Among multi-task baselines, RefTR [12] and SeqTR [46] trail LoA-Trans-B by 5–11 points on REC and 8–13 points on RES, indicating that their self-attention-only inter-task communication is less effective than the proposed center prompt and TaskSyn Network. The gains are most pronounced on the harder Refcoco+ and Refcocog splits, where richer or attribute-only descriptions make object localization more challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoA-Trans builds directly on Deformable DETR's deformable attention decoder and adapts LAVT's cross-attention-based early fusion for visual-language alignment. Its key conceptual departure from prior multi-task visual grounding methods (RefTR, SeqTR, VG-LAW) is making explicit the constraint that REC and RES must refer to the same object via a shared center prompt, rather than relying solely on self-attention for implicit information exchange. The query selection mechanism replaces Deformable DETR's random query initialization with location-aware queries derived from multi-level feature maps, and the TaskSyn Network introduces a structured, task-specific information pathway (segmentation queries → bounding-box queries) that goes beyond the undifferentiated self-attention used by prior joint-learning approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The center prompt is a simple yet effective architectural principle that makes an implicit assumption of multi-task visual grounding explicit, and the centerness analysis (Table 5) provides useful diagnostic insight into how robust the system is to localization errors. However, the paper acknowledges corner cases where high centerness coexists with low IoU (similar instances) and where very low centerness degrades performance significantly, suggesting the approach is not fully robust to ambiguous or crowded scenes. Open questions include whether the center-prompt paradigm generalizes to other multi-task vision-language settings (e.g., detection + segmentation + classification) and whether the query selection mechanism can be made more computationally efficient, as the 'No Selection' ablation revealed memory bottlenecks.
