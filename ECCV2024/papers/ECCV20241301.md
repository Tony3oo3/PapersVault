## Problem

Unsupervised video semantic compression (UVSC) aims to produce low-bitrate videos that support diverse downstream analysis tasks without task-specific training, but existing methods rely on single self-supervised learning objectives and limited training data, yielding semantic representations too weak to preserve the rich, multi-granularity information needed for tasks like action recognition, tracking, and segmentation simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241301/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a video clip (256×256 patches during training, 1080p at inference) and a quality parameter QP ∈ {0.5, 2, 3, 4} as input, and produces a compressed bitstream (GOP size 10, low-delay P mode) that decodes into photorealistic frames at very low bitrates (0.01–0.07 bpp). The decoded frames are directly fed into off-the-shelf task models (TSM, SlowFast, TimeSformer, ByteTrack, XMem) with no fine-tuning, and the VFMs used for semantic guidance are only active during training, not at inference time.

## Method

Free-VSC absorbs rich semantics from multiple pre-trained VFMs (VideoMAEv2, DINOv2, SSL-Swin) by introducing a Prompt-based Semantic Alignment Layer (Prom-SAL): a shared spatial-temporal Transformer whose behavior is modulated by VFM-specific learnable prompt tokens, mapping compressed features into each VFM's semantic space for an L2 alignment loss. For inter-frame compression, a dynamic trajectory-based entropy model replaces motion compensation: a recurrent historical state predicts region-wise probabilities over a pool of non-linear transformations, the highest-probability trajectory transforms the previous frame feature to form the coding context, and a Gaussian entropy model conditioned on this context encodes the current frame. During training, one VFM is randomly selected per iteration to save GPU memory, and the total loss combines the rate-distortion-semantic term with perceptual and PatchGAN losses for photorealism. The encoder employs a QP-conditioned semantic attention mask to adapt feature bitrate, and the entire system (encoder, decoder, Prom-SAL) is trained end-to-end in a single stage.

## Results

On action recognition with TSM, Free-VSC achieves 62.24% Top-1 on HMDB51@0.02bpp, 89.16% on UCF101@0.04bpp, and 67.83% on Diving48@0.07bpp, outperforming the strongest UVSC baseline SMC by 10.12%, 2.70%, and 7.67% respectively. On MOT17@0.01bpp (ByteTrack), it reaches 72.80% MOTA, surpassing SMC (70.84%) and DCVC-DC (65.22%). On DAVIS2017@0.01bpp (XMem) for VOS, it achieves 76.85% J&F, exceeding SMC (74.20%) and DCVC-DC (72.86%). On the fine-grained FineGym99 dataset at 0.02bpp, Free-VSC scores 71.51% Top-1 versus 62.43% (VVC) and 42.84% (SMC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Free-VSC consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every task and dataset. Against the strongest UVSC baseline SMC, gains range from 2.57% (Kinetics400@0.06bpp) to 12.01% (Diving48@0.05bpp) in Top-1 accuracy, with the largest margins on fine-grained temporal datasets. Compared to the best learnable codec DCVC-DC, improvements are even more pronounced: 27.02% on HMDB51@0.02bpp and 19.30% on Diving48@0.05bpp, confirming that plain visual-quality optimization is insufficient for semantic preservation. Traditional codecs (HEVC, VVC) and hand-crafted semantic methods (ROI, JPD-SE) trail by 10–25% on most settings, with JPD-SE performing worst on MOT (60.05% MOTA vs. 72.80% for Free-VSC). On MOT17, Free-VSC leads SMC by 1.96% MOTA and reduces false negatives from 11,710 to 10,445. On VOS (DAVIS2017), it improves J&F by 2.65% over SMC and 3.99% over DCVC-DC, while also achieving the best contour recall (92.77% vs. 91.10% for SMC). The paper further shows that applying its VFM-based semantic learning to DCVC-DC and SMC architectures yields significant gains, confirming the generality of the approach beyond its own network design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised video semantic compression (UVSC) paradigm introduced by SMC (Tian et al., ICCV 2023), which optimized a single self-supervised learning objective (MAE) as the semantic distortion term. The key departure is replacing that single SSL objective with multiple pre-trained Visual Foundation Models (VideoMAEv2, DINOv2, SSL-Swin) as semantic teachers, connected through a novel prompt-based semantic alignment layer (Prom-SAL) inspired by visual prompt tuning (Jia et al., ECCV 2022). For inter-frame compression, the paper departs from standard motion-compensation and ConvLSTM schemes by proposing a dynamic trajectory-based entropy model that selects from a pool of non-linear transformations, borrowing the candidate-selection mechanism from dynamic convolution (Chen et al., CVPR 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that off-the-shelf VFMs can serve as free, rich semantic sources for video compression, eliminating the need for task-specific training or large-scale supervised data at deployment. The mutual enhancement effect across heterogeneous VFMs (MAE-based, contrastive-based, hierarchical) suggests that diverse pre-training objectives yield complementary semantic representations that a single model cannot capture. Open questions include: how to efficiently select or adapt VFMs for different bitrate levels (the paper uses the same VFMs across all QPs), how to automatically judge whether a new VFM is beneficial, and whether the approach scales to the rapidly growing VFM ecosystem without manual curation. The empirical reliance on hyperparameters (trajectory count S=5, QP range) also limits immediate reproducibility.
