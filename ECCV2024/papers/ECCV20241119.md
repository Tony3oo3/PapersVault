## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLMs fail on complex VQA tasks (e.g., OK-VQA, A-OKVQA) that require reasoning over diverse world knowledge and multi-step logic, because they lack a mechanism to systematically collect the rich visual clues scattered across an image. The paper argues that explicitly mining question-answer pairs from multiple instances in the image can bridge the logic gap between perception and reasoning, rather than relying on external knowledge bases or simple keyword associations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241119/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text question as input. Internally, an image tagging model (RAM) extracts an average of 14 open-vocabulary tags per image (attributes, objects, actions, scenes), which serve as answers for a VQG model to generate corresponding questions; the top-P=8 questions most similar to the target question are retained. The final output is a text answer predicted autoregressively by a frozen LLM, conditioned on visual features, encoded Q&A prompt embeddings (fixed length k=32), and the question instruction.

## Method

Q&A Prompts is a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 trains a VQG model (InstructBLIP with frozen image encoder and LLM) on A-OKVQA/OK-VQA to learn P(Q|V,A), producing diverse, specific questions rather than generic ones. Stage 2 feeds RAM image tags as answers into the VQG model to generate questions, then selects the top-P most similar to the target question via sentence-BERT cosine similarity, forming Q&A prompt pairs. Stage 3 encodes these pairs through a visual-aware prompting module consisting of a frozen CLIP text encoder, a single-head cross-attention with gated fusion that adaptively blends language and visual embeddings, and a Perceiver-inspired resampler decoder that compresses the long prompt sequence (L_s≥100) into k=32 fixed-length embeddings before concatenation with visual features and the question instruction in the frozen LLM. The key insight is that Q&A pairs provide multiple complementary perspectives on the image and explicitly surface world knowledge needed to fill reasoning gaps, while the visual-aware encoding prevents the long prompt text from distracting the model from the target question.

## Results

On A-OKVQA, Q&A Prompts (built on InstructBLIP-7B) achieves 69.4% soft accuracy on the validation set and 68.1% on the test set, improving over the InstructBLIP baseline by +5.4% and +6.0% respectively. On OK-VQA, the method reaches 64.3%, a +2.2% gain over the same baseline. The method also generalizes across MLLMs: LLaVA-1.5 improves from 66.9% to 72.4% on A-OKVQA (+5.5%), and MiniGPT-4 improves from 54.2% to 61.2% (+7.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q&A Prompts outperforms all 25 compared baselines on both A-OKVQA and OK-VQA. On A-OKVQA test, it surpasses the previous best (Prophet, 61.1%) by 7.0 points and BLIP-2 (58.7%) by 9.4 points, while on OK-VQA it exceeds RA-VQA-v2 (62.1%) by 2.2 points and PromptCap (60.4%) by 3.9 points. The improvement is most pronounced on A-OKVQA (+5.4–6.0% over InstructBLIP) compared to OK-VQA (+2.2%), which the authors attribute to A-OKVQA's emphasis on visually-grounded reasoning over external knowledge retrieval—aligning with Q&A Prompts' philosophy of mining in-image visual clues. The method also consistently improves across different MLLM backbones (MiniGPT-4, LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP-T5), with gains of 5.5–7.0% on A-OKVQA and 1.9–3.4% on OK-VQA, demonstrating generalizability. Notably, the method requires no external knowledge base (Wikipedia, ConceptNet, Wikidata) or frozen GPT-3, relying solely on pre-trained model weights and in-image visual clues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the visual question generation (VQG) paradigm—previously used for dataset augmentation (SQuINT, SelTDA) or sub-question decomposition [48]—as a mechanism for mining visual clues at inference time. It departs from the dominant knowledge-based VQA approaches (KAT, REVEAL, REVIVE) that retrieve from external knowledge bases like Wikipedia or ConceptNet, and from caption-based LLM prompting methods (Img2LLM, Prophet, PromptCap) that convert images to text for frozen GPT-3. Instead, Q&A Prompts uses an image tagging model (RAM) to produce diverse tags as answers, trains a dedicated VQG model to generate specific questions for those tags, and encodes the resulting Q&A pairs through a novel visual-aware prompting module (gated fusion + Perceiver-style resampler) before feeding them into a frozen MLLM like InstructBLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly mining rich visual clues from the image itself—without any external knowledge base—can substantially close the reasoning gap in knowledge-based VQA, achieving 68.1% on A-OKVQA and 64.3% on OK-VQA. The generalizability across InstructBLIP, LLaVA-1.5, and MiniGPT-4 (consistent +5–7% gains on A-OKVQA) suggests the Q&A prompt strategy is a transferable plug-in for MLLMs. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in fine-grained counting, OCR, and potential dataset biases, and the approach's reliance on a strong image tagging model (RAM) as the source of 'answers' may cap its effectiveness on images with rare or compositional content not well-covered by tag vocabularies.
