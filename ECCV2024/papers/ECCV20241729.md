## Problem

Conventional end-to-end autonomous driving pipelines factorize the task into a serial perception → prediction → planning chain, which ignores high-order interactions between the ego vehicle and surrounding agents (e.g., an ego lane shift alters rear-car behavior that in turn affects ego planning). Additionally, these methods decode trajectories directly from features without modeling the strong structural prior that realistic trajectories are predominantly continuous, straight, or near-constant-curvature, leading to less authentic predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surrounding RGB camera images at 640×360 resolution over 5 past frames (2 s history), the ego vehicle's past trajectory waypoints, and a discrete direction instruction (go straight / turn left / turn right) injected via cross-attention. It produces a unified set of future trajectories for both the ego vehicle and all surrounding agents (evaluated at 1 s, 2 s, 3 s horizons) by sampling from a learned 512-dimensional Gaussian latent distribution and autoregressively decoding individual BEV waypoints, alongside auxiliary 3D object detection and vectorized semantic map outputs.

## Method

GenAD casts driving as a generative modeling problem in three stages. First, an instance-centric scene tokenizer transforms multi-camera images into BEV features (via BEVFormer-style deformable cross-attention), refines them into map and agent tokens, then adds an ego token and applies self-attention among all instance tokens to capture high-order ego–agent interactions before injecting map information via cross-attention. Second, a variational autoencoder encoder maps ground-truth future trajectories to diagonal Gaussians in a 512-dim latent space, encoding the structural prior of realistic trajectories. Third, a GRU-based generator autoregressively evolves the sampled latent state while an MLP decoder outputs one BEV waypoint per timestep, factorizing the joint trajectory distribution into per-waypoint conditionals. Training combines a KL-divergence loss matching the instance-conditioned distribution to the trajectory-prior distribution, trajectory losses (L1, collision, boundary, lane-direction), and auxiliary map/detection losses; at inference the trajectory encoder is discarded and futures are produced by sampling p(z|I) and running the GRU–decoder loop.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, GenAD achieves the best average L2 displacement error of 0.91 m (0.36 m at 1 s, 0.83 m at 2 s, 1.55 m at 3 s) among all camera-based end-to-end methods, with an average collision rate of 0.43% and an inference speed of 6.7 FPS. Using the averaged-frame evaluation protocol, GenAD further reduces the average L2 error to 0.52 m and collision rate to 0.19%. In closed-loop CARLA Town05 Long simulation, GenAD achieves a driving score of 34.53, surpassing even the LiDAR-based Transfuser (33.15) and VAD-Base (30.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenAD outperforms VAD-Tiny (its closest same-size competitor) by a large margin on both L2 error (0.91 m vs. 1.30 m) and collision rate (0.43% vs. 0.72%) while maintaining nearly identical inference speed (6.7 vs. 6.9 FPS). It also beats the larger VAD-Base on L2 (0.91 vs. 1.22 m) and collision rate (0.43% vs. 0.53%) at roughly 2× the speed (6.7 vs. 3.6 FPS). Against UniAD, GenAD achieves a lower L2 error (0.91 vs. 1.03 m) but a slightly higher collision rate (0.43% vs. 0.31%), which the authors attribute to UniAD's additional tracking and occupancy supervision signals. GenAD substantially outperforms ST-P3 (L2: 0.91 vs. 2.11 m; CR: 0.43% vs. 0.71%) and OccNet/OccWorld on both metrics. In closed-loop CARLA simulation, GenAD (DS 34.53) surpasses the LiDAR-based Transfuser (33.15) and VAD-Base (30.31), and also improves perception side-tasks over VAD (detection mAP 0.29 vs. 0.27; map mAP@1.0 0.56 vs. 0.44; EPA-car 0.59 vs. 0.56).

## Relation to Prior Work

GenAD builds directly on VAD's vectorized scene representation pipeline (map tokens, agent tokens, BEV features via BEVFormer-style deformable attention) and its trajectory loss formulation, but departs from the conventional serial perception→prediction→planning architecture by recasting driving as a joint generative modeling problem. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a VAE-learned structural latent space for trajectories, where motion prediction and ego planning are unified as distribution matching rather than sequential decoding. The instance-centric scene representation with explicit ego-agent self-attention addresses a gap in prior methods (UniAD, VAD, ST-P3) that treat prediction and planning as independent stages, ignoring high-order interactions between the ego vehicle's future actions and surrounding agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying prediction and planning in a probabilistic latent space with learned trajectory priors, GenAD offers a principled framework that naturally handles the uncertainty inherent in driving and produces diverse yet realistic futures. The ablation showing that ego-agent interaction is critical (collision rate nearly triples without it) validates the argument that serial pipelines fundamentally miss important dynamics. However, the paper is limited to nuScenes and a single model scale, the scaling behavior of the generative approach remains untested, and the authors themselves note that diffusion models represent a promising but unexplored direction. The gap with UniAD on collision rate (0.43 vs. 0.31) suggests that additional supervision signals (tracking, occupancy) still matter for safety-critical planning.
