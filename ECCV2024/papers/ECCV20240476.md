## Problem

Visual acoustic matching (VAM) and dereverberation are inherently reciprocal inverse tasks, yet existing methods train them independently, wasting the structural symmetry between their input-output spaces. Moreover, both tasks rely on paired anechoic-reverberant audio that is impractical to collect at scale, limiting the use of abundant unpaired natural recordings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240476/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For VAM, the method takes a panoramic RGB image of the target environment and an anechoic audio clip, producing a reverberant mel-spectrogram (128×128, FFT 1024, hop 256) that is vocoded via BigVGAN. For dereverberation, it takes the same visual scene plus a reverberant audio and outputs an anechoic spectrogram. The source spectrogram is concatenated with Gaussian noise as the content input to the Unet, while the visual scene is encoded into a 256-dimensional embedding by a pre-trained ResNet-18 and injected via cross-modal attention. Unpaired natural reverberant audios (with video but no anechoic counterpart) and anechoic audios (with no visual scene) are also consumed during training.

## Method

MVSD employs two visual scene-driven diffusion converters—a reverberator f_θ and a dereverberator g_φ—each built on a controllable U-Net with self-attention and selectively placed cross-modal attention blocks conditioned on 256-dim visual scene embeddings. The key mechanism is mutual learning: the output of one converter serves as pseudo-input to the other, forming a closed loop whose L1 cycle-consistency errors (Δ_v^{a_c} and Δ_v^{a_r}) act as feedback signals that jointly optimize both tasks without requiring aligned pairs. This loop extends naturally to unpaired data: a natural reverberant audio is first dereverberated to a pseudo-anechoic, then re-reverberated, and the reconstruction error against the original provides a training signal; anechoic-only audios are paired with randomly sampled visual scenes. The total training objective combines the standard diffusion denoising loss, the mutual learning cycle-consistency loss, and an L1 style-consistency loss, with 250 denoising steps found optimal for the quality-speed tradeoff.

## Results

On the SoundSpaces-Speech 'Seen' split, MVSD achieves 0.508 STFT-distance, 0.030 s RTE, and 0.142 MOSE, improving over the prior SOTA (AViTAR) by 0.157 STFT (23.6% relative), 0.004 s RTE (11.8%), and 0.019 MOSE (11.8%). On Acoustic AVSpeech 'Seen', MVSD reaches 0.112 STFT and 0.392 s RTE. For dereverberation on SoundSpaces-Speech, MVSD attains 2.53 PESQ, 4.27% WER, and 4.46% EER, outperforming VIDA (2.37 PESQ, 4.44% WER) and MetricGAN+ (2.33 PESQ, 7.49% WER).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVSD outperforms all six baselines across both tasks. Against the closest VAM competitor AViTAR [4], MVSD reduces STFT-distance by 0.157 (23.6% relative) on SoundSpaces-Speech Seen and by 0.032 on Acoustic AVSpeech Seen, while also improving RTE and MOSE consistently. The traditional RIR-based method AEE [69] and the image-to-impulse-response approach Image2Reverb [67] are substantially worse (STFT > 2.5 vs. 0.508), confirming the advantage of direct visual-conditioned generation. AV U-Net [19], the earliest visual-sound model, trails MVSD by 0.130 STFT on SoundSpaces Seen and 0.044 on AVSpeech Seen. For dereverberation, MVSD surpasses VIDA [6] by 0.16 PESQ and 0.17% WER, and MetricGAN+ [15] by 0.20 PESQ and 3.22% WER, demonstrating that the mutual learning loop with the VAM task yields measurably cleaner anechoic reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent tasks—visual acoustic matching (VAM) and dereverberation—into a single mutual learning framework, drawing the dual-learning game concept from machine translation (He et al., 2016) and adapting it to the audio domain for the first time. Architecturally, it replaces the GAN-based generators used in prior VAM work (AViTAR, Image2Reverb) with a visual scene-driven diffusion model, borrowing the controllable U-Net with cross-attention blocks from text-to-image diffusion (Saharia et al., 2022) and the simplified denoising objective from DDPM (Ho et al., 2020). The key departure from prior work is the closed-loop cycle-consistency mechanism: the output of one converter serves as pseudo-input for the other, generating feedback signals that enable training on one-way unpaired data without requiring aligned anechoic-reverberant pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual learning framework offers a practical path to reducing reliance on expensive paired recordings, since the 17.3% unpaired data augmentation yields a 9.1% relative RTE improvement—relevant for real-world deployments where aligned audio pairs are infeasible to capture. However, the evaluation is limited to speech on two datasets, and the mutual learning loop's benefit over simply training two strong single-task models with the same diffusion backbone is modest (0.573 → 0.508 STFT), leaving open whether the reciprocal signal provides genuine cross-task knowledge transfer or merely acts as an additional regularization. Extending the framework to non-speech audio (music, environmental sounds), 3D spatial audio, or more diverse visual conditioning (video, 3D scans) would be natural next steps to validate the generality of the reciprocity assumption.
