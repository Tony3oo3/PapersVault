## Problem

In vanilla Binary Neural Networks, over 50% of weights never flip their signs throughout training, a phenomenon the authors term 'silent weights.' These silent weights are not only at the tails of the weight distribution (as previously addressed by ReCU) but also universally present near zero, and the paper proves the root cause is the independence of BNN gradients from the latent weight magnitude distribution, which no prior method had identified or addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240862/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within a standard quantization-aware training loop: RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) are fed through a CNN whose weights and activations are binarized to {+1, −1} for the forward pass while real-valued latent weights are maintained for gradient computation. The output is a class label (10, 100, or 1000 classes). The OvSW module introduces no architectural change—it modifies only the gradient during backpropagation and adds a lightweight per-weight flipping-state tracker, so the input/output interface is identical to any standard BNN.

## Method

OvSW combines two orthogonal mechanisms to accelerate weight sign flipping. First, Adaptive Gradient Scaling (AGS) rescales each filter's gradient by the ratio of its weight Frobenius norm to its gradient Frobenius norm, clamped at a threshold λ, thereby establishing a direct coupling between gradient magnitude and weight magnitude that vanilla BNNs lack. Second, Silence Awareness Decaying (SAD) maintains an exponential moving average of each weight's sign-flipping indicator; weights whose flipping state falls below a threshold σ are identified as 'silent' and receive an additional penalty term γW added to their gradient, pushing them toward zero where sign changes become easier. Theoretically, the paper proves that two BNNs with identical binary weights, scaling factors, and BN parameters produce identical gradients regardless of real-valued weight magnitudes, explaining why large-magnitude weights can remain silent. The method is plug-and-play, adding negligible overhead to the training loop, and is compatible with existing BNN techniques such as AdaBin and RBNN.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, OvSW achieves 61.6% top-1 accuracy with binarized ResNet18 and 65.5% with ResNet34, outperforming the previous best single-stage method ReCU by 0.6% and 0.4% respectively. With two-stage training, OvSW reaches 66.6% top-1 on ResNet18, surpassing ReActNet (65.9%) and ReCU (66.4%). On CIFAR-10, OvSW attains 93.2% (ResNet18), 87.7% (ResNet20), and 92.8% (VGGsmall), reducing the gap to full-precision models to as little as 1.6%. Ablation on CIFAR-100 shows OvSW converges significantly faster, reaching 66.37% top-1 in just 60 epochs versus 55.28% for vanilla BNNs at the same epoch count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OvSW consistently outperforms all single-stage BNN baselines on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1K. On ImageNet ResNet18, OvSW (61.6%) beats ReCU (61.0%), FDA-BNN (60.2%), SiMaN (60.1%), RBNN (59.9%), and IR-Net (58.1%) by 0.6%, 1.4%, 1.5%, 1.7%, and 3.5% top-1 respectively. On ResNet34, OvSW (65.5%) surpasses ReCU (65.1%) and CMIM (65.0%) by 0.4% and 0.5%. On CIFAR-10 ResNet18, OvSW (93.2%) leads ReCU (92.8%), SiMaN (92.5%), RBNN (92.2%), and IR-Net (91.5%). In the two-stage training setting on ImageNet, OvSW (66.6%) edges out ReCU (66.4%) and ReActNet (65.9%). OvSW also demonstrates strong compatibility: when plugged into AdaBin it improves accuracy from 70.56% to 72.56%, and into RBNN from 67.15% to 69.98% on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as orthogonal to the two dominant BNN research threads—gradient approximation (STE, RBNN, IR-Net) and representation enhancement (Bi-Real Net, AdaBin)—by targeting the underexplored problem of inefficient weight sign flipping. Conceptually, AGS is an adaptive variant of LARS that restricts the lower bound of the gradient-to-weight norm ratio rather than the upper bound, while SAD repurposes the EMA-based oscillation tracking from Nagel et al. (ICML 2022) to detect and penalize 'silent weights.' The paper explicitly departs from ReCU, which only revives 'dead weights' at the distribution tails, by showing that silence is a universal phenomenon also present near zero, rooted in the theoretical independence of BNN gradients from latent weight magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that over 50% of BNN weights never flip signs, combined with a clean theoretical explanation (gradient–weight distribution independence), gives practitioners a concrete lever to improve BNN training beyond the usual gradient-estimation and representation-enhancement toolbox. The plug-and-play nature and negligible overhead make OvSW broadly applicable, and the convergence speedup (60 epochs matching 120-epoch vanilla) is practically valuable for edge-device training scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to multi-bit quantization, transformer-based BNNs, or non-convolutional architectures, and whether the theoretical independence result holds under more complex optimizers (Adam, LAMB) beyond vanilla SGD.
