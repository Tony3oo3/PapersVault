## Problem

Self-supervised 3D shape completion without any complete shape examples remains challenging: existing truly self-supervised methods rely on local patch distributions or adversarial discriminators that may be insufficient for accurate global completion. The paper asks whether the completion function itself can be constrained by its own definition (involution) and whether a shared canonical template space can serve as a supervisory signal for completion without ground-truth complete shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241547/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rigidly aligned partial point cloud (non-watertight, ~30K densely sampled surface points with precomputed UDF values) as input and produces a completed watertight 3D mesh via Marching Cubes on the learned UDF field, along with dense correspondences obtained by mapping points into the learned template space. Because partial shapes are non-watertight, the representation uses Unsigned Distance Fields rather than SDFs, and the pipeline operates on point sets throughout before extracting the final mesh at inference.

## Method

The architecture has two mutually supervising modules: a completion module (a Snowflakenet-style encoder-decoder G that predicts missing points, plus an upsampler U that refines them to 2048 points) and a template-based INR module (a deformation warp D mapping points to a canonical space, followed by a UDF decoder T). The key self-supervised constraint is that G is formulated as an exact involution—G(G(X'))=X'—yielding a strong Chamfer-based self-loss without external data. Completion is further supervised by template-space consistency (warped completed points should align with the learned template shape P), while the INR is supervised by partial-shape reconstruction plus identity and smoothness regularizers on the warp. Training uses a batch-wise alternate strategy (inspired by GANs) that freezes one module while optimizing the other, avoiding the ill-posedness of joint optimization.

## Results

On PartialUDF-Shapenet (5 categories), the method achieves an average F1 of 65.11 and CD of 6.61, the best among all self-supervised and unpaired baselines on F1. On the non-rigid DFaust dataset (~72% missing rate), it reaches F1 84.57 and CD 1.76, outperforming even the supervised SeedFormer on CD (1.76 vs. 2.82). On real-world KITTI scans, it achieves the lowest MMD of 0.08, substantially better than all baselines including supervised methods (next best: SInv at 1.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the supervised SeedFormer, the method trails on F1 (65.11 vs. 82.74) and CD (6.61 vs. 0.69) on rigid ShapeNet categories, but reverses the CD advantage on DFaust (1.76 vs. 2.82) and achieves a dramatically lower MMD on KITTI (0.08 vs. 1.22), indicating more category-consistent completions. Among unpaired methods, it clearly outperforms cGan on F1 (65.11 vs. 48.81) and ShapeInversion on F1 (65.11 vs. 62.89), though both unpaired baselines achieve lower CD (4.11 and 3.99 respectively), suggesting they produce tighter but less complete shapes. Against the closest self-supervised competitor P2C, the method wins on both F1 (65.11 vs. 50.50) and CD (6.61 vs. 7.50) across ShapeNet categories, and the gap widens substantially on DFaust (F1 84.57 vs. 42.27; CD 1.76 vs. 7.50). On KITTI, the method's MMD of 0.08 is an order of magnitude better than all baselines (range 1.11–1.22), at a small cost in Fidelity (4.61 vs. 1.88–2.11 for SInv/SeedFormer), attributed to its tendency to ignore outlier points in noisy scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Snowflakenet completion architecture (encoder-decoder-upsampler) and the template-based INR framework (deformation warp to a canonical space) into a self-supervised setting where neither complete shapes nor paired data are available. It departs from prior self-supervised approaches (P2C, Peters et al.) that rely on local patch distributions or adversarial discriminators by instead using global correspondence consistency in a shared template space as the supervisory signal. The key conceptual departure is the involution constraint (G∘G(X)=X), which is a stricter self-supervised loss than the cyclic constraints used in unpaired completion, combined with a GAN-inspired batch-wise alternate training strategy to resolve the ill-posed joint optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that self-supervised shape completion can approach supervised accuracy (F1 65 vs. 83 on ShapeNet; F1 85 vs. 81 on DFaust) without any complete shape examples, validating that global correspondences are a viable supervisory proxy. The mutual improvement between completion quality and correspondence quality (completion helps build the template; the template guides completion) suggests a broader principle for self-supervised geometry tasks. Limitations include over-completion on complex categories (chairs, tables), dependence on rigid alignment, and the need for multiple partial views per instance. Open questions include whether the involution constraint generalizes to non-rigid or articulated shapes beyond DFaust, and whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary or cross-category completion.
