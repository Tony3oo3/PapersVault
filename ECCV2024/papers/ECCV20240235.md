## Problem

Existing image-text alignment metrics like CLIPScore rely on contrastive VLMs whose text encoders act as a 'bag of words,' failing to distinguish compositional prompts involving multiple objects, attribute bindings, spatial relations, counting, and logical reasoning. This leaves the generative AI community without a reliable automated metric that can handle the compositional complexity of real-world user prompts, and without benchmarks that adequately stress-test such compositional structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240235/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single image (or a set of uniformly sampled video frames / 3D rendered views) and a free-form text prompt, the method wraps the text in a fixed yes/no question template ('Does this figure show "{text}"? Please answer yes or no.') and feeds it alongside the image to a VQA model. The output is a single scalar score equal to the model's generative probability of the token 'Yes', which for video and 3D is computed by averaging the per-frame or per-view scores. No reference images, captions, or additional prompts are required.

## Method

VQAScore computes alignment as P("Yes" | image, question) using an off-the-shelf VQA model, requiring no decomposition of the prompt into sub-questions. The key architectural contribution is CLIP-FlanT5, which replaces the unidirectional decoder-only LLM (Llama-2) in LLaVA-1.5 with the bidirectional encoder-decoder FlanT5, allowing image embeddings to be conditioned on the question and vice versa—mimicking top-down visual processing. The model pairs a frozen CLIP vision encoder with FlanT5-11B and is fine-tuned on the same 665K public VQA mixture and two-stage recipe as LLaVA-1.5, yet consistently outperforms it across all benchmarks. The paper also introduces GenAI-Bench, a 1,600-prompt benchmark with over 5,000 human-verified compositional skill tags and 15,810 human ratings across ten leading image and video generators, to evaluate both generative models and alignment metrics on realistic compositional prompts.

## Results

On the challenging Winoground benchmark, VQAScore with CLIP-FlanT5 achieves 60.0/57.5/46.0 (Text/Image/Group), surpassing the previous SOTA GPT4V-Eval (44.5/49.0/36.3) and VIEScore (40.8/39.3/34.5) by 15.5/8.5/9.7 points on Group score. On EqBen, CLIP-FlanT5-based VQAScore reaches 59.3/63.6/47.9, again exceeding all proprietary-model baselines. On human-judgment agreement benchmarks (Table 4), VQAScore (CLIP-FlanT5) achieves 85.3 AUROC on DrawBench, 85.0 on COCO-T2I, and 84.0 binary accuracy on Pick-a-Pic, outperforming all prior methods including those finetuned on human feedback (ImageReward: 70.4/77.0/75.0) and GPT4-Vision-based approaches (VIEScore: 63.9/78.0 on TIFA160/Pick-a-Pic).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQAScore (CLIP-FlanT5) decisively outperforms all 14 baselines across both Winoground and EqBen. Against the most widely-used metric CLIPScore, VQAScore achieves 5× higher Group scores on basic compositional skills and 10× on advanced skills (counting, negation, comparison). Among divide-and-conquer methods (TIFA, VQ2, Davidsonian), VQAScore wins by 20–40 Group points even when both use the same underlying VQA model (Table 2), demonstrating that the end-to-end 'Does this figure show…?' template is far more effective than LLM-decomposed QA pairs. VQAScore with open-source InstructBLIP (44.5/42.8/28.5 on Winoground) already matches GPT4V-Eval (44.5/49.0/36.3) on Text score and surpasses it on Image score, while the in-house CLIP-FlanT5 extends the lead to 60.0/57.5/46.0. On human-judgment agreement (Table 4), VQAScore (CLIP-FlanT5) beats ImageReward (70.4→85.3 on DrawBench), VIEScore (63.9→71.2 on TIFA160), and Davidsonian (78.8→85.3 on DrawBench). On video (EvalCrafter) and 3D (StanfordT23D) benchmarks, VQAScore surpasses T2VScore-A with GPT4-Vision (61.4→66.5 pairwise accuracy) and CLIPScore (61.0→68.6 on 3D), confirming generalization beyond images.

## Relation to Prior Work

VQAScore is positioned as a direct extension of VisualGPTScore [39], which computed generative likelihood P(text|image) via captioning models, but repurposes the idea for the reverse direction (image-to-text) using a VQA model. Architecturally, it builds on the LLaVA-1.5 training recipe [40] but makes a key departure by replacing the decoder-only Llama-2 backbone with the bidirectional encoder-decoder FlanT5, motivated by the observation that question tokens should influence image representations (and vice versa). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant divide-and-conquer paradigm (TIFA, VQ2, Davidsonian) that uses LLMs to decompose complex prompts into sub-questions, instead relying on a single end-to-end question template and the VQA model's own compositional reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VQAScore offers a simple, fully open-source, one-line Python alternative to CLIPScore that handles compositional prompts where CLIPScore fails, and GenAI-Bench (1,600 prompts, 15,000+ human ratings) fills a critical gap in benchmarks that test higher-order reasoning skills like counting, comparison, and logic. The finding that a trivially simple VQA template outperforms elaborate LLM-based decomposition pipelines suggests the field may have over-engineered evaluation. Open questions include whether VQAScore can be finetuned to assess non-alignment dimensions (aesthetics, toxicity, motion quality) and whether the bidirectional architecture advantage persists at larger scales or with newer VLMs.
