## Problem

DDIM inversion, widely used for prompt-driven image editing, suffers from inconsistency between forward (noising) and backward (denoising) intermediate states due to inherent first-order approximations, a problem exacerbated by classifier-free guidance. Existing fixes—EDICT (which doubles neural function evaluations) and Null-text inversion (which requires iterative optimization)—impose substantial computational overhead. The paper asks whether exact diffusion inversion can be achieved with negligible additional cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241509/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For round-trip image editing, the method takes a clean RGB image plus a text prompt to invert to noise, then a modified prompt to regenerate an edited image; for ControlNet-based editing, a scribble layout plus text prompt drives single-trip generation. Text-to-image and conventional sampling inputs are a text prompt (or none) combined with Gaussian noise at 512×512 resolution. A practical constraint is that BDIA-DDIM inversion requires two initial diffusion states (z_0 and z_1), where z_1 must be computed via a standard DDIM step, and the sampler must retain the previous state z_{i+1} in memory at each step.

## Method

BDIA (Bidirectional Integration Approximation) modifies the DDIM update by evaluating the DDIM step twice at each timestep: once forward over the next time-slot [t_i, t_{i-1}] and once backward over the previous time-slot [t_i, t_{i+1}], then combining them via a weighting parameter γ ∈ [0,1]. The resulting expression for z_{i-1} is a linear combination of (z_{i+1}, z_i, ε̂_θ(z_i, i)), which makes the update exactly invertible—z_{i+1} can be recovered as a linear function of (z_i, ε̂_θ(z_i, i), z_{i-1})—enabling exact diffusion inversion without auxiliary states. Crucially, only one neural network evaluation (NFE) is performed per timestep, halving the cost of EDICT while retaining exact reversibility. The paper provides a convergence analysis following linear multistep ODE solver theory, showing BDIA-DDIM converges as the step size h → 0, with DDIM recovered as the special case γ = 0. The γ parameter additionally offers a tunable degree of freedom for controlling how closely edited images adhere to the original during round-trip editing.

## Results

On COCO2014 text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion V2, BDIA-DDIM achieves the best FID across all NFE budgets: 12.62 at 10 NFEs, 13.44 at 20 NFEs, 13.87 at 40 NFEs, and 13.53 at 80 NFEs, outperforming DDIM (14.78/14.65/14.69/15.16) and EDICT (30.91/15.51/14.34) while using half the NFEs of EDICT. On conventional noise-to-image sampling, BDIA-DDIM reduces FID from 14.38 to 10.03 on CIFAR-10 and from 13.41 to 10.86 on CelebA at 10 NFEs compared to DDIM. For CLIP scores, EDICT edges out BDIA-DDIM at 40 NFEs (25.44 vs. 25.05), but BDIA-DDIM leads at 10 and 20 NFEs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDIA-DDIM consistently outperforms DDIM [35] on FID at every NFE budget in both text-to-image (Table 1) and conventional sampling (Table 2), with the largest gap at 10 NFEs (12.62 vs. 14.78 FID on COCO2014; 10.03 vs. 14.38 on CIFAR-10). Against EDICT [40], BDIA-DDIM achieves lower FID at all NFE settings (e.g., 13.44 vs. 30.91 at 20 NFEs; 13.53 vs. 14.34 at 80 NFEs) while requiring only half the neural function evaluations, though EDICT achieves a higher CLIP score at 40 NFEs (25.44 vs. 25.05). Compared to DPM-Solver++ [23], BDIA-DDIM wins on FID at all NFE levels (e.g., 12.62 vs. 15.82 at 10 NFEs) but trails slightly on CLIP at 80 NFEs (24.85 vs. 25.04). PLMS [21] is dominated by BDIA-DDIM on both FID and CLIP across all settings, with the FID gap reaching 11.8 at 10 NFEs (12.62 vs. 24.42). In round-trip image editing (Figs. 2, 3, 5), BDIA-DDIM produces reconstructions and edits visually on par with EDICT at half the NFE cost, while DDIM shows noticeable drift from the original image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions BDIA-DDIM as a second-order integration method that exploits time-reversibility of the DDIM ODE, drawing conceptual inspiration from linear multistep methods in numerical analysis and time-reversible solvers (Verlet, Leapfrog). It directly addresses the inconsistency problem in DDIM inversion for image editing, offering an alternative to null-text inversion (iterative optimization) and EDICT (auxiliary-state coupled transformations). The key departure from EDICT is that exact reversibility is achieved by averaging forward and backward DDIM steps within a single state trajectory, rather than introducing a paired auxiliary state that doubles the neural function evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BDIA-DDIM halves the NFEs of EDICT while preserving exact inversion, making round-trip image editing substantially cheaper, and the γ parameter introduces a practical degree of freedom for controlling edit strength. The convergence analysis (Theorem 1) provides theoretical grounding that subsumes DDIM as a special case. Limitations include the need to store an extra state z_{i+1}, the requirement that z_1 be computed via standard DDIM inversion, and the method's specificity to the DDIM update form—whether the bidirectional averaging principle extends to higher-order solvers (DPM-Solver++, DEIS) or stochastic (SDE-based) samplers remains open.
