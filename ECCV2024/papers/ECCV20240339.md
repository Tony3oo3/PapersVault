## Problem

Imitation learning-based planners for autonomous driving suffer from a fundamental mismatch: training supervision comes solely from human trajectory records (open-loop), yet deployment requires safe, closed-loop driving. Existing remedies—non-parameterized safety filters or handcrafted collision-avoidance losses—patch the symptom without teaching the network to understand the motivations behind human risk management or the consequences of its own planned actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240339/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a vectorized scene: H past steps of agent states (position, velocity, acceleration, dimensions), HD map elements (lanes, crosswalks, stop lines), traffic light signals, and route roadblocks, all encoded in local coordinate frames with global position embeddings. It outputs L ego-vehicle trajectory candidates and K context-agent trajectory candidates (each F steps of lateral/longitudinal position and heading), plus pair-wise future TTC predictions at P time points for a pruned set of agent pairs (those with TTC < 30 s in ground truth), along with confidence scores for each candidate.

## Method

RaSc extends a joint planning-and-prediction Transformer backbone with a pair-wise TTC decoder (cross-attention over agent token pairs) that predicts whether each agent pair's future TTC falls below a 10 s threshold and its numerical value. Two novel training objectives are added: (1) a risk-awareness imitation loss that matches the decoder's TTC predictions to TTCs computed from human trajectories in the dataset, and (2) a self-consistency constraint that requires TTCs recomputed from the model's own highest-confidence planned and predicted trajectories to agree with the decoder's output, teaching the model to interpret the consequences of its actions. The self-consistency weight follows a sine schedule (ramping up over training), and in later stages a self-consistency-induced OHEM strategy replaces trajectory-loss-based hard-sample selection: samples where the model's planned trajectory is close to ground truth but its self-consistency loss is high (indicating a hidden collision risk) are retained, while samples with high trajectory loss but low self-consistency loss (plausible alternative behaviors) are down-weighted.

## Results

On the nuPlan benchmark, RaSc achieves the best open-loop (OL) and non-reactive closed-loop (NR-CL) scores among pure learning-based planners across all three validation sets: 90/86/75 (Val14), 91/86/76 (Test14-random), and 86/75/63 (Test14-hard) for OL/NR-CL/R-CL respectively. With a lightweight post-processing safety check (RaSc*), NR-CL scores rise to 91/91/77, approaching the rule-based PDM-Closed (93/90/65) while maintaining a 75 ms inference time versus 127–139 ms for PDM methods. In reactive closed-loop (R-CL), RaSc slightly trails PlanTF (75 vs. 77 on Val14; 76 vs. 81 on Test14-random), which the authors attribute to distribution shift in IDM-driven reactive agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaSc outperforms all pure learning-based baselines (PlanCNN, UrbanDriver, GC-PGP, PDM-Open, PlanTF) in both OL and NR-CL across all three validation sets, with the largest margins over PDM-Open (e.g., +36 NR-CL on Val14) and GC-PGP (+27 NR-CL on Val14). Against the closest competitor PlanTF, RaSc gains +1 OL and +1 NR-CL on Val14, +4 OL on Test14-random, and +3 OL / +2 NR-CL on Test14-hard, while PlanTF retains a slight R-CL edge (77 vs. 75 on Val14; 81 vs. 76 on Test14-random). With post-processing, RaSc* reaches 91 NR-CL on both Val14 and Test14-random, matching or exceeding PDM-Closed/PDM-Hybrid (90–93) while running at 92 ms versus 127–139 ms; however, in R-CL, PDM-Closed/Hybrid still lead (92 vs. 86 on Val14, 92 vs. 82 on Test14-random). Against the rule-based IDM baseline, RaSc surpasses it by +52 OL and +10 NR-CL on Val14, though IDM retains a small R-CL advantage (77 vs. 75 on Val14). GameFormer, the only other learning-based method with post-processing, is outperformed by RaSc* in NR-CL (91 vs. 81 on Test14-random) and R-CL (82 vs. 79) while being roughly 4× faster (92 ms vs. 395 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

RaSc extends the joint trajectory planning and prediction paradigm (vectorized scene encoding with Transformer encoders, LSTM agent encoders, PointNet map encoders) by adding a pair-wise TTC decoder and two new training objectives. It departs from the two dominant safety-enhancement strategies in prior work—non-parameterized safety filters/optimizers (PlanCNN, GameFormer) and handcrafted collision-avoidance loss terms (ChauffeurNet, VAD)—by instead learning risk awareness directly from data via TTC supervision and enforcing self-consistency between planned trajectories and predicted TTCs. The authors explicitly position TTC as the first such safety metric integrated into a pure learning-based planner, repurposing a long-established transportation-engineering quantity as a differentiable training signal rather than a post-hoc constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized but persistent gap: open-loop imitation training does not reliably produce safe closed-loop behavior, and prior remedies (safety filters, handcrafted penalties) do not teach the network *why* a human driver acted as they did. By grounding training in TTC and self-consistency, RaSc offers a principled path toward planners that internalize risk reasoning rather than relying on external safety nets. Limitations include slightly weaker reactive closed-loop performance (the model's context-agent predictions diverge from IDM-driven sim agents) and hyperparameter sensitivity (TTC threshold, OHEM start time, easy-sample retention probability). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to datasets without dense TTC annotations, whether the sine-scheduled self-consistency weight is optimal, and whether the OHEM strategy transfers to other planning architectures.
