## Problem

Inverse rendering from posed images under unknown lighting is inherently ill-posed: diffuse shading and albedo are coupled (a white object under yellow light yields a yellow albedo), and self-occlusion shadows get baked into the recovered albedo. Prior methods that optimize materials via differentiable rendering alone get trapped in these ambiguities, producing inaccurate decompositions. The paper addresses this gap by learning data-driven material priors with generative models to regularize the optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images of a single object under one unknown static illumination, along with a 3D mesh reconstructed by MonoSDF. Materials are parameterized as UV-space texture maps at 2048×2048 (albedo and roughness), and lighting as a longitude-latitude environment map at 512×256. The output is a disentangled albedo map, roughness map, and environment map that support relighting and novel-view synthesis under arbitrary new lighting.

## Method

The key design choice is to model priors on albedo and specular shading (rather than roughness directly) by exploiting the fact that the Disney BRDF rendering equation decomposes into diffuse and specular terms, making both components observable and trainable from large-scale 3D data. Two conditional diffusion models, fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion, are trained on 350k Objaverse objects rendered under 180 HDR maps; conditioning is injected via CLIP image features processed through a cross-attention transformer into the UNet. A coarse-to-fine two-stage optimization then regularizes inverse rendering: the first stage supervises material and lighting parameters with diffusion-generated samples using a shift-and-scale-invariant loss, and the second stage applies Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) with the coarse materials as guiding signals to re-generate multi-view-consistent albedo and specular samples that stabilize the final optimization. Differentiable rendering uses NvDiffRast with Monte Carlo sampling and multiple importance sampling.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, IntrinsicAnything achieves 24.68 PSNR on albedo recovery, 26.88 PSNR on aligned albedo, 28.97 PSNR on relighting, and 0.0105 MSE on roughness, substantially outperforming all baselines on material and lighting decomposition. The method also achieves 30.24 PSNR on novel view synthesis under the original lighting. The largest gains are on relighting (28.97 vs. 24.36 PSNR for the best baseline InvRender) and roughness estimation (0.0105 vs. 0.0420 MSE), confirming that the diffusion priors effectively resolve the diffuse/specular and shading/albedo ambiguities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntrinsicAnything decisively outperforms all optimization-based baselines on material recovery: albedo PSNR improves by 4.4 dB over TensoIR (24.68 vs. 20.30) and 6.9 dB over InvRender (24.68 vs. 17.82), while roughness MSE drops to 0.0105 versus 0.0420–0.1046 for the baselines. On relighting, the method achieves 28.97 PSNR, a 4.6 dB gain over the best baseline InvRender (24.36), demonstrating that accurate material decomposition translates directly to better novel-lighting synthesis. Notably, TensoIR and NvDiffRec achieve higher view-synthesis PSNR (33.12 and 32.58 vs. 30.24) because rendering under the original lighting does not require correct material/lighting separation. Among data-driven prior baselines, USI3D and Yi et al. prevent shadow baking but still fail to separate diffuse shading from albedo under complex lighting, yielding albedo PSNR of only 11.67 and 13.20 respectively. The ablation further confirms that removing the specular prior drops albedo PSNR to 17.21 and roughness MSE to 0.0758, while removing guided sampling degrades aligned albedo PSNR from 26.88 to 24.61.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable physically-based rendering pipeline for inverse rendering (InvRender, TensoIR, NvDiffRec, NeILF++) by introducing conditional diffusion models as material priors, departing from the purely optimization-based approach that suffers from inherent ambiguity between albedo/shading and shadow/albedo. It builds directly on Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) for guided multi-view-consistent generation and on Stable Diffusion as the base architecture, but repurposes them from image generation to material decomposition. Unlike prior data-driven intrinsic decomposition methods (USI3D, Yi et al.) that use deterministic single-view predictors, this work models the full distribution of albedo and specular shading via generative models, and unlike SDS-based 3D generation (DreamFusion), it uses guided image samples rather than score distillation to avoid blurriness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that learning a material prior via diffusion models can substantially resolve the ill-posedness of inverse rendering, achieving large gains in albedo and roughness recovery over strong optimization-based baselines. The design choice to model specular shading (rather than roughness) as the prior target is practical and enables training on existing 3D datasets without requiring valid roughness annotations. Key limitations include dependence on MonoSDF geometry quality, exclusion of transparent objects, and evaluation on only 4 synthetic objects plus one real dataset (DTU), leaving open whether the approach scales to complex scenes or diverse material types. The coarse-to-fine guided sampling strategy is a reusable pattern that could transfer to other inverse problems requiring multi-view consistency.
