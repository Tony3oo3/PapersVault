## Problem

Magnifying imperceptible high-frequency, sub-pixel motions (micrometer-scale, Hz-to-MHz) has been limited to expensive high-speed cameras or active-light dual-shutter systems, both of which are costly, bulky, or spatially constrained. No prior work has combined the microsecond-level temporal density of event cameras with the spatial density of RGB images to perform motion magnification, and no dataset or network existed for this dual-modality task.

## Input / Output

The method takes two low-frame-rate RGB frames (30 Hz, e.g., 256×256 or 2048×1536) bracketing a time interval, the asynchronous event stream recorded between those timestamps (converted to a 2n×H×W voxel grid with separate positive/negative channels), and a user-specified magnification factor α (typically 30–80×). It outputs a sequence of motion-magnified RGB frames at a customizable high frame rate (up to ~100 interpolated frames), with the number of output frames adjustable at inference to match the target motion frequency. Because the network is fully convolutional, it generalizes across input resolutions despite being trained on 256×256 patches.

## Method

The core insight, derived from the physical event-camera model, is that the sub-pixel motion field is determined by the product of local RGB image gradients and event polarities (Eq. 5), meaning neither modality alone suffices to recover motion direction and magnitude. The network follows an Eulerian encoder–manipulator–decoder structure: the RGB branch extracts texture (V) and shape (M) features, while the event branch extracts an implicit motion representation ΔM via voxel-grid encoding, channel-attention fusion with RGB features, and a proposed Second-order Recurrent Propagation (SRP) module that aggregates information from two preceding temporal steps rather than one, enabling robust modeling of the long-range dependencies needed to interpolate up to 100 frames. The manipulator linearly scales ΔM by α and optionally applies a pixel-wise temporal band-pass filter in the frequency domain to suppress event-camera noise. Training is supervised with a Charbonnier reconstruction loss plus auxiliary texture-consistency and shape-consistency losses, and the first dedicated dataset SM-ERGB (10,000 synthetic + 7 real sequences with sub-pixel motion) is introduced to support training and benchmarking.

## Results

On the SM-ERGB-Synth test set, the proposed method achieves a PSNR of 22.32 dB and SSIM of 0.8753, matching the PSNR of LVMM on 900 Hz high-speed video (22.30 dB) while using only ~20% of the data volume (0.72M vs. 3.60M) and substantially improving SSIM (0.8753 vs. 0.7309). The method outperforms the strongest event-based baseline, retrained REFID, by approximately 2.5 dB in PSNR and 0.07 in SSIM. On the real-world SM-ERGB-Real dataset (256 Hz tuning fork, 110 Hz guitar string), the proposed method is the only approach that accurately recovers the inherent motion frequency, while baselines produce artifacts or fail to represent the correct frequency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against five baselines spanning RGB-only interpolation, event-based interpolation, and their combination with LVMM magnification. Against LVMM on 900 Hz high-speed video, the proposed method matches PSNR (22.32 vs. 22.30 dB) while improving SSIM by 0.14 (0.8753 vs. 0.7309) and reducing data volume from 3.60M to 0.72M (~80% reduction). Against the RGB interpolation + LVMM pipelines (SuperSloMo+LVMM at 14.32 dB, RIFE+LVMM at 17.09 dB), the proposed method gains 8.0 dB and 5.2 dB in PSNR respectively, attributed to the end-to-end design avoiding error accumulation from the two-stage interpolation-then-magnification process. Against the event-based interpolation + LVMM pipeline (TimeLens+LVMM at 17.60 dB), the gain is approximately 4.7 dB. Against the strongest baseline, retrained REFID (19.64 dB, 0.8029 SSIM), the proposed method improves by 2.5 dB in PSNR and 0.07 in SSIM, which the authors attribute to the Second-order Recurrent Propagation module for long-term temporal modeling and the manipulator's integration of the magnification factor. On real-world sequences, all baselines fail to recover the correct motion frequency, while the proposed method with its temporal filter accurately captures the 256 Hz and 110 Hz oscillations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research lines: Eulerian video motion magnification (LVMM, phase-based methods) that has relied on expensive high-speed RGB cameras, and event-based frame interpolation (TimeLens, REFID, TimeReplayer) that handles large-motion, few-frame interpolation. The key departure is formulating motion magnification as a dual-stream problem where temporally-dense events provide sub-frame motion cues and spatially-dense RGB provides texture/shape, rather than requiring 900 Hz+ RGB capture. The physics-based derivation (Eq. 5) showing that motion direction requires the product of image gradient and event polarity is a novel analytical contribution that motivates the network design, and the Second-order Recurrent Propagation module extends standard RNN/bidirectional architectures used in prior event interpolation to handle the much longer temporal spans (30–80+ frames) demanded by magnification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SM-ERGB dataset (10,000 synthetic + 7 real sequences) establishes the first benchmark for event-based motion magnification, a task that previously had no training data or evaluation protocol. The demonstrated ~20% data reduction relative to high-speed RGB while matching quality (PSNR 22.32 vs. 22.30) offers a practical, cost-effective path for industrial vibration analysis and medical vital-sign monitoring where high-speed cameras are prohibitive. However, the real-world evaluation is limited to 7 sequences, the method struggles with low-contrast and flickering scenes (acknowledged by the authors), and the synthetic-to-real generalization, while encouraging, has not been stress-tested across diverse motion types or lighting conditions.
