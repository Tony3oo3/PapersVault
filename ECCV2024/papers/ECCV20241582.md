## Problem

Plane adjustment—simultaneous optimization of LiDAR poses and 3D plane parameters—remains challenging at scale: existing methods achieve globally optimal convergence only with good initialization, and their high time complexity (often cubic) renders them impractical for large numbers of poses and planes. The paper addresses the gap of performing large-scale plane adjustment with linear time complexity, a larger convergence region, and robustness to poor initialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241582/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-segmented and plane-labeled LiDAR point clouds (where each point is assigned to a reconstructed plane), along with randomly initialized LiDAR poses (rotation + translation) and plane parameters (unit normal + arbitrary point on plane). Local point clouds are pre-accumulated into per-plane-per-frame matrices B(i,j) to avoid repeated point cloud operations during optimization. The output is the jointly optimized set of LiDAR poses and plane parameters, with rotations represented via unit quaternions internally.

## Method

The core contribution is Bi-Convex Relaxation, which decouples the non-convex plane adjustment into two sub-problems (pose-only and plane-only), reformulates each as a Semidefinite Programming (SDP) problem via convex relaxation of the underlying QCQP, and alternately solves them until convergence. This avoids solving a single intractable high-dimensional SDP, since fixing one parameter set makes the other a collection of independent low-dimensional SDPs that can be solved in parallel. Two variants are proposed: GlobalPointer uses point-to-plane error with SDP solvers (Mosek) for both sub-problems, while GlobalPointer++ uses plane-to-plane error with closed-form solvers (eigen decomposition for rotations/normals, linear least squares for translations/offsets) plus a one-time Rotation-Only SDP to resolve normal-direction ambiguity. The decoupling yields linear time complexity in the number of poses and planes for GlobalPointer, and a 5–10× speedup for GlobalPointer++ at moderate scales.

## Results

On synthetic data, GlobalPointer consistently converges to the global optimum (lowest total point-to-plane error $e_{total}$ on log10 scale) across all tested noise levels and pose initialization noise levels, while GlobalPointer++ is more sensitive to point cloud noise. GlobalPointer++ achieves a 5–10× speedup over GlobalPointer, and both exhibit linear time complexity with respect to the number of planes and poses (GlobalPointer++ becomes quadratic only when both are extremely large). On the Hilti real dataset, both variants demonstrate global convergence across all initialization noise settings, whereas all baselines require well-initialized values to reach the ground truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlobalPointer outperforms all four baseline families (BALM2, EF, ESO-Full/ESO-BFGS, PA-Full/PA-Decoupled) in convergence robustness: it reaches the global minimum from random initialization at noise levels where every baseline fails to converge. In terms of time complexity, BALM2, EF, and ESO-BFGS show less sensitivity to increasing plane counts, while ESO-Full and PA-Full exhibit near-cubic scaling; PA-Decoupled (which also uses alternating minimization) shows time complexity similar to GlobalPointer. ESO-Full additionally suffers from numerical instability in its implicit Hessian derivation, degrading its accuracy. GlobalPointer++ trades some robustness to point cloud noise for a 5–10× runtime speedup over GlobalPointer, making it the preferred choice when initialization is reasonable and scale is large. Overall, the paper's method wins on convergence region and robustness to poor initialization, ties or slightly trails on raw speed at small scale, and matches prior methods in final accuracy when all methods do converge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: nonlinear least-squares plane adjustment (Zhou et al. 2020 for point-to-plane, Kaess 2015 for plane-to-plane) and convex relaxation / SDP techniques borrowed from rotation averaging and point cloud registration (TE-ASER, Se-Sync). The key conceptual departure is the Bi-Convex Relaxation strategy, which decouples the joint pose-and-plane optimization into independent pose-only and plane-only sub-problems, each reformulated as a small SDP solvable in polynomial time. This avoids the intractability of a single high-dimensional SDP (as in prior convex relaxation approaches) while retaining the global-optimality benefits that purely nonlinear solvers (BALM2, Eigen-Factors, ESO) lack at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for LiDAR SLAM and large-scale 3D reconstruction: linear time complexity and robustness to random initialization make plane adjustment feasible for hundreds of frames and planes where prior methods either diverge or become prohibitively slow. However, the global optimality claim remains purely empirical with no theoretical guarantee, and GlobalPointer++ degrades to quadratic complexity at very large scale due to data-preparation overhead. Open questions include formal convergence/optimality proofs, handling of outliers and dynamic scenes, and whether the approach extends to other coupled non-convex problems beyond plane adjustment.
