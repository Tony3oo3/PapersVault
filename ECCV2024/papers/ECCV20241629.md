## Problem

Traditional 3D scene representations (meshes, voxels, point clouds, radiance fields) each trade off fidelity, memory, interpretability, and editability, and none offer a compact, semantically rich, and easily extensible format for full-scene reconstruction. No prior method represents an entire scene as a sequence of high-level, interpretable language commands that can be autoregressively predicted from visual input, and no large-scale dataset of scene walkthroughs paired with such structured descriptions existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241629/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes egocentric walkthrough data in three interchangeable forms: semi-dense point clouds from Project Aria's visual-inertial SLAM (discretized to 5 cm), posed RGB keyframes, or a combination of both. The output is a variable-length sequence of integer tokens encoding structured commands (make_wall, make_door, make_window, make_bbox, make_prim) at 5 cm spatial resolution; a custom interpreter parses these tokens into explicit 3D geometry (planes, box cutouts, oriented bounding boxes, volumetric primitives). Unlike conventional detectors, SceneScript emits no per-detection confidence scores, so evaluation uses F1-based metrics rather than mAP.

## Method

SceneScript is an encoder–decoder system where the encoder compresses the scene into a 1-D latent sequence and a causal-attention transformer decoder autoregressively generates the tokenized command sequence. Three encoder variants are explored: (1) sparse 3D convolutions over the SLAM point cloud, (2) a RayTran-style 2D↔3D bidirectional transformer that iteratively refines image-patch and voxel-grid features via ray-constrained attention, and (3) a lifted-feature point cloud that projects CNN image features onto 3D points before sparse convolution. Tokenization uses a custom [START, PART, CMD, PARAM₁…PARAM_N, PART, …, STOP] schema that packs commands of arbitrary length and count without fixed-size slots. The key design principle is extensibility: new scene entities (e.g., volumetric primitives for object parts, door states, curved walls) are added by introducing a single new command to the language while keeping the network architecture and visual input unchanged, and the model is trained end-to-end in a supervised manner on 100 k synthetic indoor scenes from the released Aria Synthetic Environments dataset.

## Results

On Aria Synthetic Environments for layout estimation, SceneScript with lifted features achieves a mean F1@5cm of 0.903 (wall 0.943, door 0.959, window 0.806) and mean Avg F1 of 0.801, substantially outperforming RoomFormer (0.139 F1@5cm) and SceneCAD (0.048 F1@5cm for walls). For 3D object detection on Aria Synthetic Environments, SceneScript achieves F1@0.25 IoU of 0.620 and F1@0.50 IoU of 0.577, surpassing ImVoxelNet (0.584/0.516) and Cube R-CNN (0.394/0.228). On ScanNet, SceneScript achieves F1@0.25 IoU of 0.506 and F1@0.50 IoU of 0.406, comparable to 3DETR-m (0.536/0.407) but below SoftGroup (0.622/0.573).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneScript dominates both layout estimation baselines by a wide margin: the lifted-features variant achieves 0.903 mean F1@5cm versus RoomFormer's 0.139 and SceneCAD's 0.048 (walls only), a gap of roughly 6–18×. For 3D object detection on Aria Synthetic Environments, SceneScript (F1@0.25 IoU = 0.620) outperforms ImVoxelNet (0.584), Cube R-CNN (0.394), and 3DETR (0.201), with the largest gap over 3DETR attributable to that method's encoder being ill-suited to the noisier semi-dense point clouds. On ScanNet, SceneScript (0.506/0.406 at 0.25/0.50 IoU) is competitive with 3DETR-m (0.536/0.407) but trails SoftGroup (0.622/0.573) by about 12 points at 0.25 IoU, suggesting the autoregressive token approach has room to close the gap on real-world data. Notably, SceneScript achieves these results without predicting per-detection confidence scores, so conventional mAP is not directly comparable; the authors report F1-based metrics to avoid order-dependent ranking artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneScript sits at the intersection of geometric sequence modeling (PolyGen, CAD-as-Language, DeepCAD, Pix2Seq, Point2Seq) and architectural layout estimation (SceneCAD, RoomNet, LayoutNet, RoomFormer). It departs from prior geometric-sequence work by using high-level, semantically meaningful commands (make_wall, make_door) rather than low-level primitives (vertices, faces), and from prior layout methods by requiring no hand-crafted heuristics or Manhattan-world priors. The autoregressive token-prediction framework is directly inspired by LLM next-token prediction, and the image-only encoder variant adopts the bidirectional 2D↔3D attention mechanism from RayTran.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of Aria Synthetic Environments (100k photorealistic indoor scenes with full structured-language ground truth) addresses a critical data bottleneck for scene understanding and will likely enable broader research beyond this paper. The extensibility property—adding a single new command to handle object parts, curved walls, or door states without architectural changes—lowers the barrier for incorporating new scene entities into reconstruction pipelines. Key open questions include whether the manually designed command vocabulary can be learned or discovered automatically, whether the representation can capture fine-grained (mm-level) geometry, and whether the approach scales to outdoor or large-scale environments. The absence of per-detection confidence scores also limits direct comparison with standard detection metrics.
