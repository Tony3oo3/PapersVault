## Problem

Neural image compression methods cannot be directly applied to graphics texture compression because they lack random-access decoding for parallel GPU rendering, do not support multi-resolution (mip-level) reconstruction, and cannot jointly compress variable-channel texture sets. The prior neural texture compression method (NTC) addresses these needs but suffers from substantial redundancy across its multi-resolution feature pyramid levels, which degrades compression efficiency as texture resolution increases.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240991/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a single texture set T of size c×h×w (c ranging from 5 to 12 channels, h×w up to 2048×2048) at mip level 0 only; all lower mip levels are derived by downscaling. The compressed representation is a pair of quantized grids (G0, G1) at h/8 × w/8 resolution plus the parameters of a per-texture-set MLP decoder. At decode time, given a texture coordinate (x,y) and mip level m, the system produces a single reconstructed texel T̂^m(x,y) of c channels, enabling independent random access by any rendering thread.

## Method

The method is an asymmetric autoencoder: a convolutional global transformer (based on the ELC encoder without attention, downsampling by 8×) maps the full-resolution texture set to a bottleneck latent, which is then projected and asymmetrically scalar-quantized (4-bit) into a single-resolution grid-pair (G0, G1). At decode, G0 is sampled with nearest-neighbor and G1 with bilinear interpolation at the query coordinate, using a stride of 2^(m−3) for mip levels m > 3 to handle lower resolutions without storing separate grids. The sampled features, a sinusoidal positional encoding of (x,y), and the normalized mip level are fed into a fully connected texture synthesizer (MLP with four residual blocks and skip connections) that outputs the reconstructed texel. This single-resolution grid-pair with stride-based sampling replaces NTC's multi-resolution feature pyramids, eliminating the inter-level redundancy identified via CKA analysis. Training is multi-stage (256→512 crops), using uniform-noise quantization early and straight-through-estimator quantization in the final stage, with MSE loss and mip-level sampling weighted by area.

## Results

On seven 2048×2048 texture sets (5–12 channels) from PolyHaven and ambientCG, the proposed CNTC method achieves a 40.8% Bjøntegaard-Delta (BD-rate) saving over the state-of-the-art neural texture compression (NTC) [45] in terms of PSNR, while also consistently outperforming NTC on SSIM across all tested bit rates. Rate is measured in bits-per-pixel-per-channel (BPPC) with a fixed 4-bit quantization per grid element, and quality is evaluated as PSNR and SSIM averaged over all mip levels. The method also substantially outperforms the conventional ASTC codec (12×12, 10×10, 8×8, 6×6 tile variants) across the entire rate-distortion curve.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NTC [45] (the prior state-of-the-art neural texture compression), CNTC achieves a 40.8% BD-rate reduction in PSNR and also wins on SSIM at every operating point; the gap is most pronounced at lower bit rates where NTC's multi-resolution feature pyramids introduce redundancy. Against the conventional ASTC codec [33] (tested with its four most aggressive tile sizes), CNTC dominates across the full rate-distortion range, confirming that learned compression can exploit cross-channel and cross-mip-level correlations that block-based vector quantization cannot capture. Against COIN [9] (implicit neural representation applied per-channel per-mip-level), CNTC achieves significantly higher PSNR because COIN lacks any learned spatial features and maps position directly to texel values. An ablation (Figure 8d) further shows that even when NTC is given the same decoder complexity as CNTC (NTC-dd, ~220 GFLOPs), CNTC still outperforms it, confirming that the global transformer encoder is the key differentiator rather than decoder capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural texture compression (NTC) framework of Vaidyanathan et al. by replacing its multi-resolution feature pyramids with a single-resolution grid-pair sampled at varying strides, motivated by a CKA analysis revealing substantial redundancy across pyramid levels. The asymmetric autoencoder design introduces a convolutional global transformer (adapted from ELC's image compression encoder) that was absent in NTC's pure autodecoder, enabling the grid-pair to capture high-frequency detail that SGD-trained grids cannot. The work also positions itself against conventional block-based texture compression (ASTC, ETC) limited to four channels and per-mip-level encoding, and against INR-based methods (COIN) that lack learned spatial features and multi-resolution support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40.8% BD-rate improvement over NTC and consistent superiority over ASTC across all bit rates make this a meaningful advance for real-time rendering pipelines where texture storage and bandwidth are critical. However, the per-texture-set training requirement (a unique model for each material) limits practical deployment, and the fixed 4-bit quantization without entropy coding leaves rate control coarse. Open questions include whether data-driven interpolation can resolve aliasing at intermediate mip levels, whether attention-based encoders can further exploit multi-dimensional redundancy, and whether a single model can generalize across diverse texture sets rather than requiring per-asset training.
