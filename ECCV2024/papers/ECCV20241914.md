## Problem

Existing diffusion-based virtual try-on methods achieve high visual quality but suffer from two critical limitations: excessively long inference times (over 17 seconds per image due to multi-step denoising) and poor preservation of identity-specific features such as tattoos, accessories, and garment texture details. No prior approach simultaneously addresses both the speed bottleneck and the identity-fidelity gap in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241914/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frontal-view person image and an in-shop garment image (RGB) as primary inputs, from which it derives auxiliary conditioning signals including human parsing maps, 2D pose keypoints, and DensePose representations. The garment type (upper-body, lower-body, or full dress) determines which body regions are masked out for warping versus preserved for identity retention. The output is a single try-on image at 512×384 or 1024×768 resolution, generated in approximately 1 second on an RTX 4090.

## Method

FIP-VITON combines a cascade flow-based warping module (using FPN multi-scale features and cross-attention replacing FlowNet2 correlation at fine resolutions) with a single-step diffusion try-on module built on DDIM but with a fixed noise level, eliminating the iterative denoising loop entirely. The try-on module conditions on a locally constructed composite image (warped garment + preserved body parts + background), DensePose, and a frozen CLIP image embedding of the garment as global conditioning. A two-stage mask-aware post-processing pipeline—unconditional (merging unchanged body regions into the condition before diffusion) and conditional (re-applying original body parts to the output when overlap ratio exceeds 0.8)—ensures identity features like tattoos and accessories are preserved without introducing artifacts. The warping module is trained with L1, perceptual, cross-entropy, adversarial, TV, and second-order smoothness losses, while the try-on module uses L1, VGG perceptual, and relativistic adversarial losses.

## Results

On VITON-HD at 512×384, FIP-VTON achieves the best SSIM (0.909) among all methods, with competitive FID (8.43) and KID (0.066) compared to the SOTA DCI-VTON (FID 8.09, KID 0.028). The headline advantage is inference speed: FIP-VTON completes generation in 1.01 s versus 17.60 s for DCI-VTON and 20.58 s for StableVITON, a ~17.4× speedup over the current SOTA. On DressCode (512×384), FIP-VTON attains the best SSIM on both Upper (0.9495) and Lower (0.9413) subsets, while remaining within ~0.005 LPIPS of DCI-VTON across all three subsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the current SOTA DCI-VTON [6], FIP-VTON trades a small quality gap (LPIPS 0.067 vs 0.043, FID 8.43 vs 8.09, KID 0.066 vs 0.028 on VITON-HD 512×384) for a 17.4× speedup (1.01 s vs 17.60 s) and a clear SSIM advantage (0.909 vs 0.886). On DressCode, DCI-VTON retains a slight edge in LPIPS and FID across all three subsets (e.g., Upper: 0.0301 vs 0.0357 LPIPS), while FIP-VTON wins on SSIM for Upper (0.9495) and Lower (0.9413). Compared to StableVITON [14], FIP-VTON is ~20× faster (1.01 s vs 20.58 s) and achieves better SSIM (0.909 vs 0.888) and marginally better FID (8.43 vs 8.58) on VITON-HD. Among GAN-based baselines, FIP-VTON substantially outperforms HR-VTON [15] (FID 8.43 vs 9.90, SSIM 0.909 vs 0.878) and GP-VTON [30] (FID 8.43 vs 9.20, SSIM 0.909 vs 0.894) on VITON-HD, while remaining competitive on DressCode where GP-VTON leads in LPIPS (0.0359 vs 0.0357 Upper). The single-step DDIM formulation is the key differentiator: all multi-step diffusion baselines (DCI-VTON, StableVITON, LaDI-VTON) require 8–21 s per image, whereas FIP-VTON completes inference in under 1.1 s on a single RTX 4090.

## Relation to Prior Work

FIP-VITON sits at the intersection of three research threads: flow-based warping pipelines from GAN-era try-on (VITON-HD, HR-VTON, GP-VTON), diffusion-based try-on generators (DCI-VTON, StableVITON, LaDI-VTON), and diffusion acceleration techniques (DDIM, consistency models, distillation). Its key departure is replacing the multi-step denoising loop of prior diffusion try-on methods with a single-step, fixed-noise diffusion conditioned on CLIP embeddings, dense pose, and a warped composite image, while adding a mask-aware post-processing stage for identity preservation. The warping module itself follows the cascade FPN architecture of GP-VTON but substitutes correlation with multi-head cross-attention at lower resolutions, and the overall design explicitly targets the two under-addressed failure modes of prior diffusion try-on: uncontrollable artifacts and loss of person-specific identity cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~17× inference speedup (1.01 s vs. 17.60 s on an RTX 4090) while maintaining competitive LPIPS/FID/KID on VITON-HD and DressCode makes high-fidelity virtual try-on viable for real-time e-commerce and metaverse applications where multi-second latencies are unacceptable. The mask-aware post-processing directly addresses a practical gap—preserving tattoos, accessories, and skin details—that prior SOTA methods (DCI-VTON, StableVITON) demonstrably fail on, as shown in the paper's qualitative comparisons. Open questions include whether the single-step diffusion generalizes to other conditional high-resolution generation tasks, whether the post-processing overhead can be absorbed into the network to reduce pipeline complexity, and how the approach scales to full-body multi-garment try-on beyond the upper-body focus of VITON-HD.
