## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) rely on text-only codebooks, which fundamentally restricts them to language generation and prevents them from producing images or other non-linguistic content. While one could simply expand the codebook to include visual codes, this introduces a large modality gap, inflates parameter counts, and risks codebook collapse. The paper asks whether a single unified codebook can be learned to tokenize both visual and textual signals, enabling multimodal generation without additional alignment modules.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (pretrained at 256×256 resolution) that are compressed via stacked quantization (e.g., 2-layer HQ-VAE) into a small set of discrete tokens (320 tokens per image), along with text prompts, questions, or class labels. Outputs include generated images (discrete visual tokens decoded by a VAE decoder), text responses for VQA, and reconstructed images. A distinctive input mode is the in-context image decompression task, where the LLM receives aggregated quantized embeddings (a flattened sequence of length ĥ×ŵ) and must autoregressively produce the full multi-layer code map (length ĥ×ŵ×D) in an instruction-following dialogue format.

## Method

UniCode integrates a VAE-style visual tokenizer (HQ-VAE) directly with a Vicuna-7B LLM so that both share a single codebook, eliminating any separate visual-text alignment module. The key training mechanism is language-driven iterative training: the visual tokenizer is trained on image reconstruction while the LLM is trained on instruction-following data, and at regular intervals the visual codebook is updated toward the LLM's codebook via exponential moving average (C' = λC + (1−λ)C_L), ensuring gradual convergence without disrupting LLM training. To handle the misalignment between compressed stacked-quantization embeddings and the LLM's word embeddings, the paper introduces an in-context image decompression pretraining task where the LLM learns to expand aggregated embeddings into the full multi-layer code map within a multi-turn dialogue. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I aligns the codebooks through alternating visual tokenizer and LLM updates, and Stage II freezes the visual encoder/decoder and fine-tunes only the LLM on a combined multimodal instruction dataset (Mixed-665K + CC3M text-to-image pairs + decompression data).

## Results

On VQA benchmarks, UniCode (104M visual encoder, 0 pretraining samples) achieves 53.1 on VQA-v2, 46.2 on VizWiz, 62.9 on ScienceQA-IMG, 42.5 on TextVQA, and 71.8 on POPE, outperforming the multimodal generation model Emu (52.0, 34.2) while using a 10x smaller visual encoder and no alignment pretraining data. For image generation, HQ-UniCode achieves FID 6.72 / IS 208.9 on ImageNet (class-conditioned), FID 11.54 / CLIP 0.30 on CC3M (text-conditioned), and FID 8.07/2.65/6.96 on LSUN Cat/Bedroom/Church (unconditioned), surpassing HQ-TVAE* (7.04, 12.13, 8.35/2.89/7.12) with the same 7B parameter budget. The enhanced UniCode+ variant (1B ViT encoder) improves to 56.2 on VQA-v2 and 77.6 on POPE, narrowing the gap to LLaVA-1.5 (79.1, 86.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On image generation, UniCode consistently outperforms all autoregressive VQ-based baselines: it beats HQ-TVAE* (7B) by 0.32 FID on ImageNet (6.72 vs 7.04), 0.59 FID on CC3M (11.54 vs 12.13), and 0.28 FID on LSUN-Cat (8.07 vs 8.35), while also surpassing RQ-Transformer (7.55, 12.33, 8.64) and VQ-GAN (15.78, 28.86, 17.31) by larger margins. Against diffusion-based methods, UniCode beats LDM (17.01 FID on CC3M) and VQ-Diffusion (11.89 on ImageNet), though ADM (10.94 on ImageNet) and StyleGAN2 (7.25/2.35/3.86 on LSUN) still lead in their respective settings. On VQA benchmarks, UniCode outperforms Emu (53.1 vs 52.0 on VQA-v2; 46.2 vs 34.2 on VizWiz) and BLIP-2 (53.1 vs 41.0 on VQA-v2; 46.2 vs 19.6 on VizWiz), but trails LLaVA-1.5 by 10–26 points across most benchmarks (e.g., 53.1 vs 79.1 on VQA-v2, 71.8 vs 86.4 on POPE) and Qwen-VL by similar margins, which the authors attribute to the lightweight 104M visual encoder trained on only 558K images. The UniCode+ variant (1B pretrained ViT) narrows the gap to LLaVA-1.5 to 4–9 points but still lags, particularly on POPE (77.6 vs 86.4) and MMBench (37.2 vs 64.3). In reconstruction, HQ-UniCode achieves rFID 2.83 on ImageNet, slightly worse than HQ-VAE (2.61) but better than RQ-UniCode (3.78) and SPAE (4.41), confirming the unified codebook paradigm introduces minimal quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCode departs from the two dominant MLLM paradigms: the 'visual encoder + text tokenizer' approach (LLaVA, BLIP-2) that maps visual signals into a frozen LLM's text space via a projector, and the 'separate visual + text codebooks' approach (Emu, Unified-IO 2) that concatenates distinct token vocabularies. Instead, it draws on VQ-VAE-style visual tokenization (Van Den Oord et al.) and hierarchical quantization (You et al.) to learn a single shared codebook, using the EMA update mechanism from RQ-VAE (Lee et al.) as the synchronization mechanism between the visual tokenizer and the LLM. The instruction-tuning framework follows LLaVA's multi-turn dialogue format, but extends it to non-linguistic outputs by reformulating image generation as an instruction-following task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a unified codebook can support both multimodal understanding and generation without any dedicated visual-text alignment module, reducing architectural complexity and parameter overhead (104M visual encoder vs. 303M in LLaVA, with zero alignment pretraining samples). However, the VQA gap versus LLaVA-1.5 (53.1 vs. 79.1 on VQA-v2) and the modest reconstruction quality relative to standalone HQ-VAE suggest the approach has not yet closed the performance gap that motivates larger, more data-hungry pipelines. Open questions include whether the unified codebook paradigm scales gracefully to higher resolutions, additional modalities (audio, video), and whether the in-context image decompression task generalizes beyond the specific stacked-quantization setup tested here.
