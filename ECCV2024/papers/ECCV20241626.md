## Problem

Diffusion models require many denoising time steps (typically T=1000) to converge because inaccurate early reconstructions accumulate error across subsequent steps, degrading both training speed and final image quality. Existing remedies—encoding VAE features, prioritizing noise levels, or adding conditional signals—introduce extra computation or are dataset-specific without addressing the root cause of error accumulation in the iterative denoising process itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241626/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes pure Gaussian noise as input and produces RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10), 64×64 (CelebA), or 256×256 (FFHQ) resolution. For face inpainting, the input is a masked face image paired with a binary mask (five mask types: Half, Completion, Expand, Thick Line, Medium Line), and the output is the completed face. For face de-occlusion, the input is a face image with semantically placed occlusion objects, and the output is the restored face. Notably, the compensation U-Net is only invoked during training; at inference the pipeline is identical to standard DDIM sampling with no additional network calls.

## Method

The core contribution is a compensation term w(t)(x̂₀ − x₀) added to the standard DDIM reverse step, where w(t) = g(t) − g(t−1) captures the difference in noise scales between consecutive steps. Mathematically, this term makes the inverse of the deterministic diffusion process exact (Eq. 13), so that each iterate x_{t−1} equals the ground-truth D(x₀, t−1) regardless of how imperfect the network's x̂₀ prediction is, thereby eliminating error accumulation. In practice, the compensation term is learned by a lightweight U-Net trained for a single epoch with L1 loss, adding negligible FLOPs (~0.1% increase). This allows reducing the training time steps from T=1000 to T=100 (a 10× reduction) while achieving equal or better FID scores. The approach is backbone-agnostic and is demonstrated on top of DDIM, DPM-Solver, PDM (patch-wise training), and Diffusion StyleGAN2, with the ADM U-Net as the primary denoising architecture.

## Results

On CelebA-64, DDIM+CS achieves FID-50k of 1.98 at NFE=1,000, outperforming DDIM (4.88) and Cold Diffusion (7.84) by 59% and 75% respectively. With patch-wise training, PDM+CS reaches FID-50k of 1.38 (CelebA-64) and 2.57 (FFHQ-256), setting new state-of-the-art among diffusion models. On CIFAR-10, PFGM+++CS achieves FID-50k of 1.50 at NFE=35, surpassing PFGM++ (1.91) and EDM (1.97). Training time is reduced by an order of magnitude: DDIM+CS (T=100) converges in 2 hours on CelebA-64 versus 16 hours for DDIM (T=1,000), while achieving a lower FID (2.11 vs. 4.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDIM+CS (T=100) outperforms all tested diffusion baselines at NFE=1,000, including DDIM (4.88→1.98 on CelebA-64, 8.41→4.02 on FFHQ-256), Cold Diffusion (7.84→1.98, 28.11→4.02), and F-PNDM (2.86→1.98, 4.79→4.02). Among the strongest baselines, DPM-Solver achieves FID 3.08 on CelebA-64 at NFE=1,000, while DDIM+CS reaches 1.98 with 10× fewer training steps; applying CS to DPM-Solver further improves it to 2.04. On CIFAR-10, PFGM+++CS (FID 1.50) beats PFGM++ (1.91) and EDM (1.97) at the same NFE=35, and DDIM+CS with T=100 (FID 2.01) matches EDM (1.97) while using 10× fewer time steps. For face inpainting on CelebA-HQ-256, DDIM+CS achieves the best FID across all five mask types (e.g., 15.33 vs. GLaMa's 18.61 for Half mask), though LPIPS is slightly worse than RePaint for thin-line masks. For face de-occlusion on FSG, DDIM+CS achieves PSNR 31.38 and SSIM 0.8699, surpassing the best GAN baseline SRNet (PSNR 27.00, SSIM 0.8493) by 16% in PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDIM framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and the DDPM training objective (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020), introducing a compensation term into the iterative denoising process to counteract error accumulation. It departs from prior approaches that address slow convergence by injecting additional conditionals (VAE features, prioritized noise schedules, semantic encoders) into the sampling loop, arguing that such conditions are dataset-specific and do not address the root cause of error accumulation. The closest prior work is Cold Diffusion (Bansal et al., NeurIPS 2024), which also adds a correction term during sampling, but the authors show Cold Diffusion violates the Gaussian noise assumption and underperforms DDIM, whereas their derivation mathematically realizes the perfect inverse of the degradation operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in training time steps (T=1000 → T=100) while simultaneously improving FID scores is a practically significant efficiency gain for diffusion model training, with negligible additional FLOPs from the lightweight compensation U-Net. The finding that the compensation term's value decays to near-zero after convergence—and that the module need only be trained for a single epoch—makes the approach cheap to adopt as a training-time modification to existing pipelines. Key limitations include restriction to score-based linear generative models and a deterministic reverse process, leaving open whether the approach extends to nonlinear or stochastic diffusion formulations. The method's generality across backbones (ADM, DPM-Solver, PDM, StyleGAN2) and tasks (unconditional generation, inpainting, de-occlusion) suggests broad applicability, but validation on larger-scale or non-face datasets remains to be seen.
