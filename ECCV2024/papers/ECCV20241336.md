## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for VLMs like CLIP employ a single phase of learnable prompts dedicated solely to adapting to downstream tasks, which easily leads to overfitting in the target domain. No prior work has explored assigning distinct roles to different prompt groups—capturing domain-general knowledge separately from task-specific expertise—within a unified training pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241336/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and class-name text prompts for inference, while the training pipeline additionally requires a large set of unlabeled domain images (the full training set without labels) for the first boosting phase and a small number of labeled few-shot images (1–16 per class) for the second adapting phase. The output is a standard classification probability over domain categories computed via cosine similarity between image and text features. The frozen boosting prompts add fewer than 0.1% parameters and negligible inference cost, making the approach suitable for deploying the smaller ViT-B/16 model in resource-constrained settings.

## Method

CasPL introduces a two-phase cascade of learnable prompts. In Phase 1 (boosting), vision and text prompt tokens are optimized via KL-divergence logit alignment against a larger ViT-L/14 CLIP teacher using extensive unlabeled domain images, distilling domain-general knowledge into the smaller ViT-B/16 student. In Phase 2 (adapting), the frozen boosting prompts are prepended to the learnable adapting prompts of any existing prompt learning framework (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC), which are then fine-tuned on few-shot labeled data without modifying the original loss function. This decouples domain-general priors from task-specific adaptation into explicitly separate prompt groups, alleviating overfitting while enabling the smaller model to match the larger teacher's performance. The framework is plug-and-play: the boosting prompt is trained once per domain and can be inserted into any prompt learning method as a frozen prefix, analogous to a change-of-variables on the base CLIP model.

## Results

On the base-to-novel generalization task averaged over 11 image classification datasets, CasPL (cascaded with PromptSRC) achieves 86.11% base accuracy, 79.54% novel accuracy, and 82.69% harmonic mean (HM), improving over PromptSRC alone by +1.85%, +3.44%, and +2.72% respectively. The largest gains appear on fine-grained and texture datasets: DTD improves by +19.38% HM over CoOp, UCF101 by +11.55% over CoOp, and FGVCAircraft by +12.38% over CoOp. In the few-shot setting (1–16 shots), CasPL consistently outperforms PromptSRC, with the largest margin of +3.59% HM at 1-shot. For domain generalization, CasPL adds +0.40% to +1.53% on the source ImageNet and +0.12% to +0.54% on target domains (ImageNet-V2, Sketch, R) depending on the base method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CasPL is evaluated as a plug-in on four prompt-learning baselines (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC) and compared against three pseudo-label-based CLIP adaptation methods (CLIP-PR, UPL, LaFTer) and three ENCLIP strategies (FPL, IFPL, GRIP). Against the strongest prior method PromptSRC, CasPL wins on all three metrics: +1.85% base, +3.44% novel, +2.72% HM on average over 11 datasets. The improvement is most dramatic when CasPL is added to CoOp (+7.64% HM), effectively closing the gap to PromptSRC's standalone performance. Against the pseudo-label methods, CasPL (82.73% HM on 8 datasets) outperforms the best IFPL strategy (79.88%) by +2.85% and surpasses UPL (72.15%) and LaFTer (72.82%) by over 9%. On domain generalization (Table 2), CasPL consistently improves every base method on both source and target domains, with PromptSRC+CasPL achieving the best average of 64.44% versus 63.90% for PromptSRC alone. The paper also shows that PromptSRC (ViT-B/16) + CasPL matches the performance of PromptSRC (ViT-L/14) without CasPL (82.69% vs 83.17% HM), demonstrating that the smaller model with boosting prompts approaches the larger model's capability at lower inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLIP architecture and the growing family of prompt learning methods for VLM adaptation (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, PromptSRC), all of which optimize learnable prompts in a single phase for task-specific fine-tuning. CasPL departs from this single-phase paradigm by introducing a two-stage cascade: first, a knowledge distillation phase where 'boosting prompts' are trained on unlabeled domain images to align a smaller student CLIP with a larger teacher CLIP; second, a standard adapting phase where the frozen boosting prompts are cascaded with task-specific adapting prompts. Conceptually, it repurposes knowledge distillation not as full model compression but as a domain-general knowledge injection mechanism through a small set of prompt tokens (<0.1% parameters), and positions the frozen boosting prompt as a 'change of variables' that redefines the base CLIP model for any downstream prompt learning method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means CasPL can be retrofitted onto any existing prompt learning framework without modifying loss functions, lowering the barrier to adoption for the CLIP adaptation community. The practical finding that a ViT-B/16 model with boosting prompts can match ViT-L/14 performance is relevant for resource-constrained deployment where only smaller models are feasible. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that boosting prompts must be trained per domain, introducing additional computation overhead; the open question is whether a single large-scale pre-training of boosting prompts could generalize across diverse domains, eliminating per-domain training. The approach also raises the broader question of whether multi-phase prompt optimization (decoupling general from specific knowledge) can be extended beyond two phases or to other modalities.
