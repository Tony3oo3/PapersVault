## Problem

Existing deblurring neural radiance field methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF) rely on volumetric rendering, which is not compatible with the rasterization-based 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline and renders at less than 1 FPS. No prior work addresses deblurring within the 3D-GS framework, leaving a gap between the real-time rendering speed of 3D-GS and the ability to reconstruct sharp scenes from blurry inputs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes blurry multi-view RGB images, their camera poses, and an SfM-derived point cloud, which is typically very sparse when images are blurred. At inference, the output is a sharp novel-view image rendered at >800 FPS using only the learned 3D Gaussian parameters (position, quaternion, scale, opacity, SH coefficients) — the deblurring MLP is entirely bypassed, making the inference pipeline identical to vanilla 3D-GS. During training, the method additionally produces M auxiliary rasterized images (for camera motion blur) or a single expanded-Gaussian rasterization (for defocus blur) to match the blurry ground truth.

## Method

A small 4-layer MLP (64 hidden units, ReLU, Xavier init) takes each 3D Gaussian's position, quaternion, scale, and viewing direction (with positional encoding) and outputs per-Gaussian deltas: for defocus blur, scaling factors δr and δs that expand the covariance to simulate pixel intermingling; for camera motion blur, M sets of position offsets δx plus geometry deltas that generate M shifted Gaussian sets whose rasterized images are averaged. The deltas are scaled by λ (1e-2), shifted toward 1.0, and clipped at a minimum of 1.0 to ensure the training-time Gaussians are always at least as large as the originals. At inference, the MLP is never invoked — the original (unmodified) 3D Gaussians are rendered directly, yielding sharp images at real-time speed. To counteract the sparse SfM point cloud caused by blur, the method injects up to 200,000 extra points via KNN color interpolation after 2,500 iterations and applies depth-dependent pruning thresholds that preserve more Gaussians on the far plane.

## Results

On the real defocus blur dataset (Deblur-NeRF benchmark), the method achieves an average PSNR of 23.71 and SSIM of 0.7471, matching or exceeding all NeRF-based deblurring baselines on SSIM while rendering at 804 FPS versus <1 FPS for volumetric methods. On the real camera motion blur dataset, it achieves state-of-the-art results on all metrics: average PSNR 26.61, SSIM 0.8224, and 961 FPS. The key advantage is that the MLP is only active during training, so inference is identical to vanilla 3D-GS, preserving real-time rendering speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRF [21] and vanilla 3D-GS [12], the proposed method clearly wins on both PSNR and SSIM across both blur types (e.g., 23.71 vs. 22.40 PSNR on defocus; 26.61 vs. 21.66 on motion blur), confirming that deblurring-aware training is essential. The Restormer + 3D-GS pipeline [41] (deblur images first, then train 3D-GS) is outperformed by 1.9–4.5 dB PSNR, showing that 2D deblurring before 3D reconstruction is inferior to joint 3D deblurring. Among NeRF-based deblurring methods, the proposed approach matches or slightly trails PDRF-10 [26] on PSNR for defocus blur (23.71 vs. 23.85) but surpasses it on SSIM (0.7471 vs. 0.7382) and by a large margin on motion blur PSNR (26.61 vs. 25.98). It outperforms Deblur-NeRF [20], DP-NeRF [17], and Sharp-NeRF [16] on SSIM for both blur types while being competitive on PSNR. The decisive advantage is rendering speed: all NeRF-based baselines render at <1 FPS, whereas the proposed method achieves 804–961 FPS, a >800× speedup, because the MLP is inactive at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) [12] as its rendering backbone, but departs from the volumetric-rendering deblurring NeRFs (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF, Sharp-NeRF) that model blur by predicting per-pixel PSF kernels in world space. Instead of learning a blur kernel, it manipulates the rotation and scaling components of each Gaussian's covariance matrix via a small MLP during training to simulate defocus and camera motion blur, then discards the MLP entirely at inference. It also addresses a practical gap that prior 3D-GS methods ignore: blurry inputs cause SfM to produce severely sparse point clouds, which the paper compensates for with KNN-based point insertion and depth-aware pruning of Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 800+ FPS rendering speed (versus <1 FPS for all deblurring NeRF baselines) makes real-time deblurring feasible for AR/VR and robotics pipelines where volumetric rendering is prohibitively slow, while matching or slightly exceeding SOTA quality on the Deblur-NeRF benchmark. However, the evaluation is confined to forward-facing scenes on a single benchmark, and the depth-based pruning heuristic relies on the z-axis being a valid depth proxy, limiting generalizability to unbounded or dynamic scenes. Open questions include whether the covariance-expansion trick transfers to more complex blur patterns (e.g., spatially varying motion blur), whether the approach scales to large outdoor scenes, and whether the sparse-point-cloud compensation is sufficient for heavily degraded inputs where SfM fails almost entirely.
