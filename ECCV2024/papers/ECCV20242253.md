## Problem

Traditional physics simulators (Bullet, MuJoCo) are slow, non-differentiable, and not natively parallelizable, making them impractical as drop-in components for differentiable computer vision pipelines such as physics-based 3D human pose reconstruction from video. The paper asks whether a neural network can learn to approximate articulated rigid-body dynamics with contact at comparable accuracy while being an order of magnitude faster under parallel execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242253/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the simulator consumes a maximal-coordinates scene state (world-frame positions, 9D flattened rotation matrices, and linear/angular velocities for every link of every articulated body) plus optional joint motor targets and external torques, and outputs the next-step velocities which are Euler-integrated into new positions and orientations. In the pose-reconstruction application the end-to-end system takes a monocular video and produces per-frame 3D joint positions via trajectory optimization over the learned dynamics. Rotation is encoded as a full 9D rotation matrix rather than quaternions or 6D representations, and the contact sub-network receives capsule geometry (length, radius, mass) alongside kinematic state to compute pairwise collision features.

## Method

LARP is a recurrent architecture with a per-object 12-layer MLP dynamics network (ELU activations) that maps concatenated link features to next-step velocities, and a 6-layer contact network that computes pairwise capsule-capsule interaction features (contact point, normal, penetration) via sum-pooling over all links of other bodies, analogous to message passing in a fully-connected GNN. The integrator applies simple Euler updates with quaternion derivatives for orientation. The key empirical finding is that performance depends critically on training details: unrolling over longer sequences (N_h=20 vs. 1), supervising with position/rotation/joint-displacement losses rather than velocity loss, applying gradient-norm clipping (threshold 0.3), augmenting with random z-axis rotations, and using stop-gradient on contact features before feeding them into the dynamics network. These choices reduce position error from ~1.1 m to ~11 cm on capsule-chain rollouts and enable LARP to match or exceed Bullet in MPJPE on Human3.6M and AIST while running 4096 parallel simulations in 1.3 ms versus ~20 ms for MuJoCo XLA.

## Results

On AIST-hard, LARP achieves 643 mm MPJPE-G, 73 mm MPJPE-PA, and 7.2 footskate, outperforming the Bullet-based baseline (654, 83, 8.5 respectively). On AIST-easy, LARP matches Bullet on MPJPE (113 mm) while improving MPJPE-G (150 vs. 154 mm) and velocity error (0.37 vs. 0.41). On Human3.6M, LARP ties Bullet at 143 mm MPJPE-G. In simulation speed benchmarks, LARP runs a single step in 0.07 ms (vs. 0.19 ms MuJoCo, 1.8 ms Bullet) and 4,096 parallel simulations in 1.3 ms (vs. ~20 ms for MJX/MuJoCo), an order-of-magnitude speedup in the parallel regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Bullet-based pipeline [13], LARP matches or slightly improves on most metrics: on AIST-hard it reduces MPJPE-G by 11 mm (643 vs. 654), MPJPE-PA by 10 mm (73 vs. 83), and footskate by 1.3 (7.2 vs. 8.5); on AIST-easy it improves MPJPE-G (150 vs. 154) and velocity error (0.37 vs. 0.41) while tying on MPJPE. Against DiffPhy [18], which uses a differentiable simulator with gradient-based optimization, LARP is slightly worse on H3.6M (143 vs. 139 MPJPE-G) and AIST-easy (150 vs. 150 MPJPE-G, 113 vs. 106 MPJPE), but DiffPhy is not evaluated on the harder AIST-hard subset. In simulation speed, LARP is ~2.7× faster than MuJoCo [37] and ~26× faster than Bullet [8] for a single simulation, and ~15× faster than MuJoCo XLA [29] and Brax [11] at 4,096 parallel simulations (1.3 ms vs. ~20 ms). For human-object collision accuracy, LARP reproduces Bullet trajectories within ~6 cm after 60 steps, confirming faithful contact modeling. Compared to SuperTrack [12], LARP maintains joint displacement error below 2 cm over 1,500 simulation steps without the progressive state inconsistency that degrades SuperTrack on long sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

LARP extends the neural physics simulation line of work, most directly generalizing SuperTrack [12] (a fully connected network updating human body-part dynamics) to support multiple interacting objects, capsule-capsule collisions, variable body-part dimensions, and longer-sequence consistency. It is methodologically related to GCN-based neural simulators [2,4,17,33] but deliberately uses per-object MLPs with a sum-pooled contact sub-network rather than a full graph convolution, positioning itself as complementary to those approaches that focus on many passive rigid bodies. The paper frames LARP as a drop-in replacement for the Bullet simulator within the trajectory-optimization pipeline of Gärtner et al. [13], rather than as a new reconstruction formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of LARP lies in its order-of-magnitude speedup for parallel simulations (1.3 ms for 4096 rollouts vs. ~20 ms for MJX/MuJoCo) and native differentiability, which opens the door to gradient-based optimization and integration with modern end-to-end learning pipelines that non-differentiable engines like Bullet cannot support. Key limitations include slow accumulation of joint displacement error over long sequences and instability during complex motions underrepresented in training data, suggesting that larger and more diverse training sets are needed. Open directions the authors identify include combining LARP with differentiable-physics frameworks like DiffPhy [18] and incorporating probabilistic motion control, which would make the simulator a more complete prior for motion synthesis.
