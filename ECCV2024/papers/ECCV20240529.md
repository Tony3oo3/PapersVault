## Problem

Existing text-to-motion diffusion models train exclusively on 3D motion data, causing them to underrepresent subtle body-part movements (e.g., hand gestures, foot articulations) that are visually small in 3D space. These fine-grained details are often magnified in 2D projections from different viewing angles, yet no prior method exploits this complementary information to improve full-body motion synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240529/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt (encoded by CLIP) and generates a 3D human motion sequence using a redundant pose representation (root position/velocity, local joint positions, velocities, rotations as sin/cos, and binary foot-contact labels) at 20 FPS on HumanML3D or 12.5 FPS on KIT-ML. During training, 3D poses are orthogonally projected into 2D planes in four directions (front, left, right, back) to create paired 2D motion sequences. Notably, the model can also accept in-the-wild 2D motion estimated from videos (via ViTPose) without any 3D ground truth, enabling zero-shot 3D motion generation for out-of-domain actions.

## Method

CrossDiff introduces a cross-diffusion mechanism that unifies 3D and 2D motion noise into a single feature space via a shared-weight transformer encoder, then cross-decodes features into either 3D or 2D clean motion using independent decoders that attend to same-level shared-layer outputs rather than the final layer. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I optimizes all four cross-domain denoising losses (3D→3D, 2D→3D, 3D→2D, 2D→2D) to learn motion characteristics in both domains and their inter-domain connections, while Stage II refines only the 3D→3D objective to eliminate mapping uncertainty. At inference, a novel mixture sampling strategy begins denoising in the 2D domain for the first α steps, then projects the partially denoised 2D noise into 3D and continues denoising in the 3D domain, leveraging the complementary detail captured in each representation. The architecture also supports fine-tuning on 2D-only data (e.g., UCF101) to generate 3D motion for actions absent from the original 3D training set.

## Results

On HumanML3D, CrossDiff achieves FID-L (lower-body FID) of 0.118, the best among all compared methods, and FID of 0.162 (third-best after ReMoDiffuse's 0.103 and T2M-GPT's 0.141). R-Precision (top-3) is 0.730 and MM Dist is 3.358, competitive with but slightly behind ReMoDiffuse (0.795, 2.974). On KIT-ML, CrossDiff achieves FID of 0.704 and FID-L of 0.434, though R-Precision drops to 0.281, which the authors attribute to the dataset's limited motion diversity (predominantly walking). The paper introduces FID-U and FID-L as new fine-grained metrics to separately evaluate upper- and lower-body motion quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossDiff achieves the best FID-L (lower-body FID) on HumanML3D at 0.118, outperforming ReMoDiffuse (0.125), T2M-GPT (0.145), and MLD (0.541), supporting the claim that 2D information helps capture subtle lower-body movements. However, on overall FID, ReMoDiffuse (0.103) and T2M-GPT (0.141) both outperform CrossDiff (0.162), and ReMoDiffuse also leads on R-Precision (0.795 vs. 0.730) and MM Dist (2.974 vs. 3.358). On KIT-ML, CrossDiff's R-Precision (0.281) is substantially lower than all other methods (MDM: 0.840, Fg-T2M: 0.745), and its FID-L (0.434) trails ReMoDiffuse (0.205), though the authors note KIT-ML is dominated by walking and does not stress the fine-grained details their method targets. Compared to the closest diffusion-based baselines (MDM, MLD, ReMoDiffuse), CrossDiff trades some overall distributional fidelity (FID, R-Precision) for superior lower-body motion quality, with the gap most pronounced on the more diverse HumanML3D dataset. The ablation in Table 2 confirms that adding 2D representations to 3D-only training improves FID from 0.224 to 0.162 and FID-L from 0.825 (MDM baseline) to 0.118.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based text-to-motion line initiated by MDM [40] and refined by MLD [4], MotionDiffuse [53], and ReMoDiffuse [54], all of which train exclusively on 3D motion data. The key departure is the cross-diffusion mechanism that injects 2D projected motion into the training loop via a shared-weight transformer encoder (inspired by BridgeTower [47]) and independent decoders, enabling the model to denoise in either 2D or 3D space. Unlike prior methods that treat 3D poses as the sole training signal, CrossDiff unifies both representations in a single feature space, arguing that 2D projections magnify subtle joint movements that 3D representations underrepresent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ability to fine-tune a 3D motion generator using only 2D pose data extracted from in-the-wild videos (e.g., UCF101), circumventing the cost and scarcity of 3D motion capture. However, the quantitative gains over the strongest baselines (ReMoDiffuse, T2M-GPT) are modest on standard metrics (FID, R-Precision), and the paper's 'competitive' framing somewhat overstates the SOTA position. Open questions include whether the benefit of 2D projections generalizes to truly diverse camera viewpoints (as opposed to four fixed orthogonal projections), whether the approach scales to longer sequences, and whether the computational overhead of dual-domain training is justified for real-time applications.
