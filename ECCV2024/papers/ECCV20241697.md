## Problem

Depth supervision in NeRF training typically enforces monocular depth priors as perfect pseudo-ground-truth via L2 loss, but these priors are inherently noisy and ambiguous due to the ill-posed nature of single-view depth estimation. Existing approaches either assume uniform pixel reliability, model uncertainty with restrictive Gaussian assumptions, or require expensive custom-trained multi-modal depth networks, none of which effectively capture the true uncertainty in depth priors for NeRF training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 18–20 sparse RGB images with SfM camera poses from indoor scenes (ScanNet) and a frozen pretrained diffusion model (DiffDP) that generates depth priors and pixel-wise uncertainty maps once before NeRF training begins. The NeRF is trained with 1024 rays per batch and 64/128 ray termination samples for coarse/fine networks, plus an additional 128 samples for the EMD computation. Output is a trained NeRF that renders photometrically consistent novel RGB views and geometrically accurate depth maps, evaluated with PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS and depth RMSE/AbsRel metrics.

## Method

The method uses DiffDP's denoising diffusion process to extract pixel-wise uncertainty by counting how often each pixel's depth estimate changes across denoising steps, augmented with a mirror-consistency term. Rather than supervising the NeRF-rendered depth directly with L2 loss, the paper normalizes NeRF ray weights into a piecewise-constant PDF, constructs its CDF, and samples new ray termination distances via inverse transform sampling. These samples are then supervised against the depth prior using Earth Mover's Distance (approximated by Sinkhorn Divergence for differentiability), which avoids assumptions of unimodality, normality, or support overlap that plague KL-divergence or L2 objectives. A focal-loss-inspired weighting scheme modulates the photometric and EMD depth losses per pixel based on the DiffDP-derived uncertainty, upweighting RGB loss where depth is unreliable and vice versa.

## Results

On the standard ScanNet benchmark (3 scenes, 18–20 training views), the proposed EMD-guided NeRF achieves the best depth metrics across all baselines: AbsRel 0.070, SqRel 0.024, RMSE 0.221, and RMSE log 0.105. It also attains the highest PSNR (21.69 dB) while maintaining competitive SSIM (0.737). On the held-out ScanNet+ dataset, the method reduces RMSE by at least 56% over baselines (0.456 vs. 1.032 for SCADE and 1.765 for DäRF) while achieving the best PSNR (22.29 dB). Notably, the NeRF's learned geometry (RMSE 0.221) surpasses the initial DiffDP depth prior itself (RMSE 0.261), demonstrating that EMD guidance allows the model to correct prior errors rather than blindly replicate them.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DäRF [25], the most closely related baseline (also using a frozen pretrained depth prior), the proposed method wins decisively on all four depth metrics: AbsRel 0.070 vs. 0.151 (54% reduction), SqRel 0.024 vs. 0.071 (66%), RMSE 0.221 vs. 0.356 (38%), and RMSE log 0.105 vs. 0.168 (38%), at the cost of a 3.7% lower SSIM (0.737 vs. 0.765). Against SCADE [26], the method achieves better depth accuracy (RMSE 0.221 vs. 0.252, AbsRel 0.070 vs. 0.086) and higher PSNR (21.69 vs. 21.54), though SCADE reports a lower LPIPS (0.292 vs. 0.373); the authors note SCADE's LPIPS is unstable across seeds. Against DDPrior [22] with in-domain depth maps, the method still wins on RMSE (0.221 vs. 0.236) despite using an out-of-domain prior, and substantially outperforms the out-of-domain DDPrior* variant (RMSE 0.221 vs. 0.474). Against DS-NeRF [7] and vanilla NeRF [15], the depth improvements are dramatic (RMSE 0.221 vs. 0.447 and 1.163 respectively). The method also surpasses the raw DiffDP [11] depth prior (RMSE 0.221 vs. 0.261), showing the NeRF corrects prior errors. In ablation Table 3(b), DiffDP is shown to be a stronger prior than DepthAnything [30] (RMSE 0.324 vs. 0.357 on training images), and the EMD objective improves upon both priors regardless of which is used.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends depth-supervised NeRF training by replacing the standard L2 loss on rendered depth with an Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) objective on the ray termination distance distribution, a framework originally introduced by SCADE. It departs from prior depth-supervised NeRFs (DS-NeRF, DDPrior, DäRF) that either assume depth priors are perfect pseudo-ground-truth or model uncertainty with restrictive Gaussian assumptions, by leveraging off-the-shelf diffusion models (DiffDP) to obtain both depth estimates and pixel-wise uncertainty for free from the denoising process. The Focal-Loss-inspired weighting of photometric and depth losses based on diffusion-derived uncertainty is a novel combination not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that photometric rendering quality and geometric accuracy are decoupled, and that EMD-based supervision allows a NeRF to learn depth *better* than the prior itself (31% RMSE reduction over the DiffDP prior), which is a practically important property for downstream applications like AR and mesh extraction. A key limitation is that the uncertainty measure is tied to diffusion-based depth networks, leaving model-agnostic uncertainty estimation as an open problem. The finding that SCADE's 20 multi-modal depth hypotheses introduce noise rather than resolving ambiguity challenges a working assumption in the field and suggests simpler single-prior approaches with proper loss design may suffice.
