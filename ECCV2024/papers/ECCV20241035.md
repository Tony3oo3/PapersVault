## Problem

Existing 3D semantic reconstruction systems produce uniform-resolution maps, incurring unnecessary computational and storage costs when only certain regions (e.g., small or geometrically intricate objects) require fine detail. The only prior semantic quality-adaptive method (Panoptic Multi-TSDFs) creates separate TSDF maps per object instance, which leads to overlapping duplicate reconstructions due to noisy segmentation and pose estimation, with no mechanism to disambiguate or merge them into a single coherent map.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241035/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGBD frames, from which camera pose is estimated via ORB-SLAM2 and per-pixel semantic segmentation is obtained from Lightweight RefineNet (up to four top-scoring labels per pixel with confidence > 0.1 are propagated). The output is a single unified multi-resolution TSDF voxel map with three quality levels (1 cm, 4 cm, 8 cm voxel sizes) where each region's resolution is determined by its semantic label and/or local geometric complexity, plus a multi-resolution 3D mesh extracted via a modified Marching Cubes procedure.

## Method

MAP-ADAPT builds on the Voxblox voxel-hashing TSDF framework and introduces a parent-child hierarchy where voxels are initialized at the coarsest resolution and subdivided (or merged back) based on whether the most likely semantic label or the incrementally updated geometric complexity (measured as change of curvature via 3D structure tensor eigenvalues) exceeds a threshold. Semantic probabilities are stored sparsely per voxel (only for labels actually observed) and updated via a depth-weighted Bayesian rule, reducing memory compared to fixed-size probability vectors. Adaptive raycasting uses three virtual subsampling grids (one per resolution level) to skip redundant points, and a modified Marching Cubes mesh generator substitutes missing fine-resolution neighbors with coarser counterparts while splitting neighboring voxels to the same resolution to suppress ghost meshes at resolution boundaries.

## Results

On HSSD, MAP-ADAPT-SG achieves completion errors of 2.53 cm (fine), 2.67 cm (middle), and 3.10 cm (coarse), matching or exceeding fixed-resolution Voxblox at the corresponding quality levels while using 3.5× less memory than Voxblox at 1 cm (469.85 MB vs. 1225.30 MB). Semantic accuracy reaches 13.12% (fine), 39.88% (middle), and 60.38% (coarse), substantially outperforming Multi-TSDFs [43] which scores only 8.58%, 10.57%, and 49.59% respectively. On ScanNet, MAP-ADAPT-SG similarly achieves the best completion error across all quality levels (3.27, 3.91, 4.27 cm) and matches Voxblox fixed on semantic accuracy. TSDF update runtime is 71.79 ms per frame for MAP-ADAPT-SG, compared to 201.45 ms for Multi-TSDFs and 89.61 ms for Voxblox (1 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fixed-resolution Voxblox [33], MAP-ADAPT-SG matches performance at the fine (1 cm) level (completion error 2.53 vs. 2.49 cm) and clearly wins at middle and coarse levels, reducing completion error from 3.06→2.67 cm and 3.59→3.10 cm respectively, while consuming 3.5× less memory than the 1 cm Voxblox map. Against Panoptic Multi-TSDFs [43], the advantage is most pronounced in semantic metrics: MAP-ADAPT-SG achieves 13.12% vs. 8.58% semantic accuracy and 6.74% vs. 4.86% mIoU at fine quality, and 60.38% vs. 49.59% accuracy at coarse quality, because Multi-TSDFs' per-instance maps produce overlapping reconstructions that corrupt semantic labels. In terms of runtime, MAP-ADAPT-SG updates TSDF in 71.79 ms per frame versus 201.45 ms for Multi-TSDFs, a 2.8× speedup, though mesh generation is slower (1252 ms vs. 204 ms) due to the multi-resolution boundary handling. On ScanNet, the same pattern holds: MAP-ADAPT-SG achieves the lowest completion error at all three quality levels (3.27, 3.91, 4.27 cm) and matches Voxblox fixed on semantic accuracy, while Multi-TSDFs lags significantly in both completion and semantic metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAP-ADAPT builds directly on the Voxblox voxel-hashing TSDF framework [33] for real-time geometric mapping and adapts the multi-resolution iso-surface extraction idea from [52] for mesh generation. It departs from the Panoptic Multi-TSDFs approach [43], which creates separate TSDF maps per object instance at different resolutions, by instead maintaining a single unified voxel grid where individual voxels are subdivided or merged based on semantic label and geometric complexity. The paper also extends the broader line of multi-resolution geometric mapping [5,6,15,45,46,50,57]—which typically adapts resolution by camera distance—by incorporating semantic category and surface curvature as additional criteria for resolution selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For autonomous agents operating under strict compute, power, and storage budgets, the ability to allocate reconstruction quality where it matters (fine for small/intricate objects, coarse for large flat surfaces) is practically significant, and the 3.5× memory reduction over full-resolution mapping while maintaining comparable accuracy makes this deployable. However, the method's performance is tightly coupled to the quality of the upstream SLAM (ORB-SLAM2) and segmentation (Lightweight RefineNet) modules, and the geometric complexity estimation via structure tensor eigenvalues is a relatively simple proxy that may not capture all relevant surface detail. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, whether the three-level resolution hierarchy is sufficient, and how well the Bayesian semantic update handles long-term label drift.
