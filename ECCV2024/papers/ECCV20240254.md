## Problem

Previous panoptic scene graph generation methods are limited to predicting predefined (closed-set) object and relation categories, making them inapplicable to open-world scenarios. While large multimodal models have enabled open-set object detection and segmentation, open-set relation prediction in PSG remains entirely unexplored, and the combinatorial explosion of object pairs makes this task particularly challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240254/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a complete panoptic scene graph: binary segmentation masks, open-set object category names (which may include novel categories not seen in training), and open-set relation labels between object pairs. Relations are output as subject-relation-object triplets where both the object categories and relation predicates can be arbitrary natural language terms, not restricted to a fixed vocabulary. The object segmentation is handled by a frozen pretrained model (OpenSeeD), so the trainable component focuses on relation prediction given the segmented objects.

## Method

OpenPSG comprises three components: a frozen open-set panoptic segmenter (OpenSeeD) that extracts object masks, categories, and whole-image visual features; a Relation Query Transformer (RelQ-Former) that uses two sets of learnable queries—pair feature extraction queries (with mask cross-attention to focus on interaction regions) and relation existence estimation queries (a binary classifier that filters irrelevant pairs, yielding a 20× inference speedup); and a multimodal relation decoder (BLIP-2's LLM decoder) that predicts relations autoregressively. The key design choice is the use of a judgement instruction ('Please judge between c_i and c_j whether there is a relation r_k') rather than a generation instruction, which leverages the LMM's analytical capability and caches the prefix computation across all candidate relations, maintaining the same prediction time while significantly outperforming generation, especially as the proportion of novel relations increases. Only the RelQ-Former is trained (with a binary cross-entropy loss for relation existence and a language-model cross-entropy loss for relation prediction), while the segmenter and LMM decoder remain frozen.

## Results

On the PSG dataset (closed-set), OpenPSG achieves R/mR@100 of 79.3/63.8 on Predicate Classification and 52.0/50.1 on Scene Graph Detection, surpassing the previous best (HiLo) by 9.0% in R@100 and 17.0% in mR@100 for SGDet. In the open-set setting (trained only on base relations), OpenPSG still outperforms methods trained on all relations, achieving a 39% improvement in R@100 over the previous best on PredCls. On the VG-150 dataset, OpenPSG improves over the best open-set baseline (Cacao+Epic) by 29.0% in R@100 and 9.5% in mR@100 in closed-set, and by 3.9% in R@100 and 5.4% in mR@100 in open-set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenPSG substantially outperforms all closed-set PSG baselines on the PSG dataset: on SGDet, it beats HiLo [47] (the strongest prior method) by 9.0% in R@100 (52.0 vs. 43.0) and 17.0% in mR@100 (50.1 vs. 33.1), and on PredCls it surpasses ADTrans [19] by 25.0% in mR@100 (63.8 vs. 38.8). Earlier SGG-based methods (IMP, Motifs, VCTree, GPSNet) are outperformed by even larger margins (e.g., 40+ points in R@100 on PredCls). On the VG-150 dataset, OpenPSG outperforms the two open-set SGG baselines in the open-set setting: it beats Cacao+Epic [38] by 8.8% in R@100 (30.6 vs. 21.8) and 5.4% in mR@100 (27.2 vs. 21.8), and surpasses OvSGTR [5] by 3.9% in R@100 (30.6 vs. 26.7). In the closed-set VG setting, OpenPSG slightly trails VCTree [30] on R@50 (60.2 vs. 66.4) but leads by a wide margin on mR@50 (45.8 vs. 16.8) and mR@100 (50.3 vs. 19.4), indicating superior per-relation recall. Notably, in the open-set PSG setting, OpenPSG (trained only on base relations) even exceeds PSGTR [36] and HiLo [47] which were trained on all relations, demonstrating the generalization advantage of the LMM-based autoregressive approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key components: OpenSeeD for open-set panoptic segmentation and BLIP-2's decoder for autoregressive language generation, combining them into a unified pipeline for open-set panoptic scene graph generation. It departs from prior closed-set PSG methods (PSGTR, HiLo, PairNet) that classify relations from a fixed vocabulary, and from prior open-set SGG works (Cacao+Epic, OvSGTR) that rely on CLIP-style feature matching or student-teacher alignment. The conceptual departure is the use of autoregressive LMM decoding with designed instructions (generation and judgement) for relation prediction, rather than classification or retrieval, enabled by the novel Relation Query Transformer that extracts interaction-focused pair features and filters irrelevant pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing open-set PSG as a task and demonstrating that autoregressive LMM decoding can predict truly open-vocabulary relations, this work opens a path toward scene understanding systems that are not limited to predefined taxonomies. The 20× inference speedup from the relation existence selector makes the approach more practical, and the finding that judgement-style instructions are more robust than generation-style instructions as novel-relation proportion increases offers a useful design principle for open-set prediction with LMMs. Key limitations include reliance on a frozen pretrained segmenter (only the RelQ-Former is trained), evaluation on relatively small datasets (PSG, VG-150), and the significant performance degradation when novel relations dominate (3:7 ratio), suggesting the approach still struggles in truly open-world settings with many unseen relations.
