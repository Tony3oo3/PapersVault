## Problem

Existing 3D single-object tracking methods assume smooth point cloud shape variations and small object motions between neighboring frames, causing dramatic performance degradation when frame intervals are large (high temporal variation). This paper identifies three compounding challenges in such scenarios—strong point cloud shape variations, similar-object distractions in enlarged search areas, and heavy background noise—and shows that state-of-the-art trackers fail to address any of them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240177/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a target point cloud with a 7-DoF 3D bounding box as the initial template and a sequence of search-area point clouds (N=128 points, C=128 channels) as input. Rather than re-extracting template features each frame, HVTrack maintains three memory banks (layer features, foreground masks, and 2D observation angles) of size K=6 at test time. The output per frame is the target's 3D center and rotation angle, a per-point foreground mask, and a 2D observation angle, all regressed by an X-RPN head.

## Method

HVTrack stacks L=2 transformer layers, each containing three novel modules. (1) The Relative-Pose-Aware Memory (RPM) module concatenates historical layer features, foreground masks, and observation angles into a unified template representation, allowing the network to implicitly learn how point cloud distributions shift with changing camera-relative pose. (2) The Base-Expansion Feature Cross-Attention (BEA) module splits the H=4 attention heads into two groups: half attend to local (base-scale) memory features directly, while the other half first apply EdgeConv to the memory to produce coarser expansion-scale features, thereby capturing spatial context across the enlarged search area. (3) The Contextual Point Guided Self-Attention (CPA) module computes a per-point importance score from both base and expansion attention maps, partitions points into G=3 importance groups, and aggregates each group into a variable number of contextual points (fewer for low-importance groups), then performs self-attention over these compressed tokens to suppress background noise while reducing computational cost. The backbone is DGCNN and the regression head is X-RPN, with a composite loss over center, mask, observation angle, targetness, and bounding box terms.

## Results

On the newly constructed KITTI-HV dataset with 5 frame intervals, HVTrack achieves 46.6% Success / 58.5% Precision (mean across Car, Pedestrian, Van, Cyclist), surpassing the previous SOTA CXTrack (35.3/42.8) by 11.3%/15.7%. At the most challenging 10-frame interval, HVTrack reaches 35.1/40.6 versus CXTrack's 23.8/26.2, a gap of 9.1%/10.4%. On the standard KITTI benchmark, HVTrack scores 65.5/83.1, ranking second to CXTrack (67.5/85.3) by 2.0% in Success. On Waymo, HVTrack achieves the best overall performance at 43.0/58.1, edging out CXTrack (42.2/56.7) by 0.8%/1.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On KITTI-HV, HVTrack outperforms all 12 baselines across every frame interval (2, 3, 5, 10). The gap widens dramatically with temporal variation: at 5 intervals, HVTrack beats CXTrack by 11.3%/15.7% (mean S/P), M2-Track by 6.0%/7.5%, and P2B by 13.6%/15.0%; at 10 intervals, the margin over CXTrack grows to 9.1%/10.4%. On standard KITTI, HVTrack is competitive but trails CXTrack by 2.0% in Success (65.5 vs 67.5) and 2.2% in Precision (83.1 vs 85.3), while slightly outperforming TAT (64.7/82.8) and M2-Track (62.9/83.4). HVTrack's weakness is the Van category on KITTI (54.8/63.8 vs CXTrack's 60.0/71.8), attributed to CPA's noise-suppression over-suppressing foreground points of large objects. On Waymo, HVTrack achieves the best mean Success (43.0) and Precision (58.1), surpassing CXTrack (42.2/56.7) and STNet (40.4/52.1), and leads all methods on the 'Hard' difficulty subset for both Vehicle and Pedestrian categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

HVTrack builds directly on CXTrack (CVPR 2023), reusing its DGCNN backbone and X-RPN head, and extends TAT's (ACCV 2022) temporal memory concept by augmenting the memory bank with foreground masks and, critically, observation angles—a signal all prior 3D SOT trackers had overlooked. The Base-Expansion Feature Cross-Attention and Contextual Point Guided Self-Attention modules are inspired by SGFormer's hybrid-scale token reallocation in 2D images, repurposed here for point cloud feature correlation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant Siamese-correlation paradigm that assumes smooth inter-frame variation, instead treating large frame intervals as a first-class design constraint that reshapes the search area, feature correlation, and noise suppression pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of KITTI-HV provides the community with a standardized benchmark for a practically important but previously unaddressed regime (skipped tracking, edge deployment, high-dynamic scenes), and the widening performance gap with frame interval (from ~1% at 2 intervals to ~9–10% at 10 intervals) quantifies how severely existing trackers degrade. The observation-angle-as-memory insight is a simple yet effective signal that could be adopted by other temporal tracking methods. A key open question is the CPA module's reliance on fixed manual hyperparameters for importance-based point grouping, which causes a measurable drop on large objects (Van); the authors flag replacing this with a learnable function as future work, and it remains unclear whether the approach generalizes beyond KITTI-scale scenes to truly unstructured or multi-sensor settings.
