## Problem

Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) is a transparent explanation method that backpropagates relevance scores through a network while guaranteeing a conservation property, but it was originally formulated only for two consecutive layers and does not account for skip connections. This makes its application to the widely used ResNet architecture unreliable, as residual connections create multiple non-linear relevance pathways that the standard LRP propagation rule cannot handle. The paper addresses how to extend LRP to models with residual connections while preserving the conservation property.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (e.g., 224×224) as input to a ResNet50 classifier and produces a 2D pixel-wise attribution map of the same spatial resolution as the input, where each pixel value indicates its contribution to the predicted class probability. The raw channel-wise sum of backpropagated relevance scores is post-processed with Heat Quantization (8 bins) to prevent overconcentration in irrelevant regions before being used as the final visual explanation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Relevance Splitting rule applied at each Bottleneck module where a skip connection output converges with a residual block output: the incoming relevance score R is divided into R_s (propagated through the skip connection) and R_m (propagated through the residual block) such that R_s + R_m = R, preserving conservation. The proposed Ratio-Based Splitting allocates each element's relevance proportionally to the absolute values of the skip connection output h_s and residual block output h_m, so that elements where the model's parameters contribute more (larger h_m) receive more relevance through the residual path. This splitting is applied uniformly to both identity-mapping (S-Bottleneck) and linear-projection (D-Bottleneck) skip connections, and within each path the standard z⁺-Rule propagates relevance through convolutions, pooling, and fully-connected layers. A Heat Quantization step discretizes the final attribution into 8 bins to mitigate overconcentration in irrelevant background regions.

## Results

On the CUB dataset, the proposed method achieves an Insertion-Deletion (ID) score of 58.2% ± 1.0, surpassing the best baseline (Score-CAM, 45.7%) by 12.5 points, while also attaining the best Insertion score (59.5%) and Deletion score (1.4%). On ImageNet, the method reaches an ID score of 54.5%, exceeding the strongest baseline (Grad-CAM, 37.1%) by 17.4 points, with a notably low Deletion score of 1.8% compared to 12.6–13.3% for Grad-CAM and Score-CAM. The method also strictly preserves the conservation property, verified empirically across 100 ImageNet samples at three network checkpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on both the Insertion-Deletion score and the Deletion score across both datasets. On CUB, it beats Score-CAM (the closest competitor) by 12.5 ID points (58.2 vs. 45.7) and reduces the Deletion score from 5.4% to 1.4%. On ImageNet, the margin over Grad-CAM is even larger: 54.5 vs. 37.1 ID points, with the Deletion score dropping from 12.6% to 1.8%. Gradient-based baselines (LRP, Integrated Gradients, Guided BackPropagation) perform poorly on ResNet, with ID scores below 6%, confirming that standard backpropagation-based attribution struggles with skip connections. Grad-CAM and Score-CAM, which are designed to handle skip connections, achieve competitive Insertion scores but suffer from high Deletion scores (5.4–13.3%), indicating their attributions spread over irrelevant background regions. The ablation study further shows that Ratio-Based Relevance Splitting combined with propagation through identity skip connections (Method iv) yields the best ID score (54.5) and lowest Deletion score (1.8%), outperforming Symmetric Splitting without identity propagation (Method i, ID 51.0, Del 3.3) by 3.5 ID points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), originally formulated for propagation between two consecutive layers, to architectures with residual/skip connections such as ResNet. Prior LRP adaptations to transformers and LSTMs have ignored the existence of residual connections in their relevance propagation, leaving a gap for the most widely used CNN family. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Relevance Splitting at skip-connection convergence points, with a Ratio-Based variant that allocates relevance proportionally to the absolute magnitudes of the skip and residual outputs, plus a Heat Quantization post-processing step to prevent overconcentration in irrelevant regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ResNet remains one of the most deployed architectures in practice, yet the standard LRP tool could not be rigorously applied to it without violating the conservation property; this work closes that gap with a transparent, provably conservative formulation. The ablation finding that propagating relevance through identity-mapping skip connections contributes more to explanation quality than the choice of splitting rule is a useful design insight for future extensions. However, the method is validated only on ResNet50 for image classification, and the authors themselves note that extension to transformers, other residual architectures, and non-vision modalities remains open.
