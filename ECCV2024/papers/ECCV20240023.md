## Problem

Existing methods for stylized human motion either generate diverse content (text-to-motion) or transfer style between two existing sequences (motion style transfer), but cannot rapidly produce motion across a broad range of content and diverse styles in a single model. Naively chaining a text-to-motion generator with a style transfer method suffers from error accumulation and is constrained by the limited motion content in specialized style datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form content text prompt (e.g., 'a person walks forward and then sits down') and a reference style motion sequence (N×263, using HumanML3D/SMPL-H skeleton representations) as input, and produces a stylized 3D human motion sequence of the same dimensionality. The style sequence is consumed as a conditioning signal through a dedicated style encoder rather than as a label; style labels from 100STYLE are never fed to the model. Optionally, a content motion sequence can replace the text prompt via DDIM-Inversion, making motion style transfer a downstream application.

## Method

SMooDi fine-tunes the pre-trained Motion Latent Diffusion (MLD) model by adding a ControlNet-inspired style adaptor: a trainable copy of MLD's Transformer Encoder that receives a style embedding from an independent encoder and injects residual corrections into each layer of the frozen backbone through zero-initialized linear connections. At inference, two complementary guidance mechanisms refine the output: (1) classifier-free style guidance, which decomposes the conditioned denoiser into separate content and style components with independent strength weights (w_c, w_s), and (2) classifier-based style guidance, which computes the L1 distance between style embeddings of the predicted clean motion and the reference style, then uses its gradient to steer the latent toward the target style. Training freezes MLD and optimizes the adaptor with three losses: a standard diffusion loss on 100STYLE, a content prior-preservation loss on HumanML3D samples to prevent content-forgetting, and a cycle prior-preservation loss that swaps content and style between the two datasets to enforce content invariance under style exchange.

## Results

On the stylized text-to-motion task (HumanML3D content + 100STYLE styles), SMooDi achieves a FID of 1.609, R-precision of 0.571, and Style Recognition Accuracy (SRA) of 72.4%, substantially outperforming MLD+Motion Puzzle (FID 6.127, R-precision 0.290, SRA 63.8%) and MLD+Aberman et al. (FID 3.309, R-precision 0.406, SRA 54.4%). On the motion style transfer task evaluated on the unseen Xia dataset, SMooDi achieves a CRA of 45.6%, a 78.3% relative improvement over Motion Puzzle (25.6%) and a 32.3% improvement over Aberman et al. (34.4%), while maintaining competitive SRA (61.1% vs. 67.8% for Motion Puzzle). The ChatGPT+MLD baseline, which merges style labels into text prompts, achieves a low FID (0.614) but a near-zero SRA of 4.8%, confirming that text-only prompting cannot effectively convey motion style.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMooDi outperforms both motion-style-transfer baselines (MLD+Motion Puzzle and MLD+Aberman et al.) across all metrics on the stylized text-to-motion task, with the largest gains in FID (1.609 vs. 6.127 and 3.309) and SRA (72.4% vs. 63.8% and 54.4%), indicating superior joint content preservation and style reflection. Against the ChatGPT+MLD baseline, SMooDi trades a slightly higher FID (1.609 vs. 0.614) and R-precision (0.571 vs. 0.605) for a dramatically higher SRA (72.4% vs. 4.8%), demonstrating that simply appending style adjectives to text prompts is insufficient for genuine motion stylization. On the motion style transfer task with the unseen Xia dataset, SMooDi achieves the best CRA (45.6%) by a wide margin over Motion Puzzle (25.6%) and Aberman et al. (34.4%), while its SRA (61.1%) is only 9.8% below Motion Puzzle (67.8%), showing a better style-content balance. The paper also reports that SMooDi achieves a 59.4% FID improvement over Aberman et al. on HumanML3D-based style transfer, and user studies with 22 participants confirm SMooDi is preferred over both baselines on realism, style reflection, and content preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMooDi builds directly on the pre-trained Motion Latent Diffusion model (MLD) [6] as its backbone, inheriting its text-to-motion generation capability, and adapts the ControlNet [61] architecture to design a content-aware style adaptor that injects style conditioning into each Transformer layer via zero-initialized linear connections. It departs from prior motion style transfer methods (Aberman et al. [1], Motion Puzzle [19]) that operate as a separate post-processing stage on existing motion sequences and rely on specialized style datasets with limited content diversity. The key conceptual shift is unifying content generation and style conditioning within a single diffusion model rather than chaining a text-to-motion generator with a style transfer module, thereby avoiding error accumulation across the pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a pre-trained text-to-motion diffusion model can be efficiently adapted (via a lightweight style adaptor and dual guidance) to produce stylized motions across diverse content and 100 locomotion styles without per-style fine-tuning, offering a practical single-model solution for animation pipelines. The content-forgetting phenomenon and its mitigation through prior-preservation and cycle-preservation losses provide a reusable recipe for fine-tuning generative models on narrow-domain data while retaining broad capabilities. A notable limitation is that the classifier-based style guidance relies on a style feature extractor trained solely on locomotion styles from 100STYLE, which the authors acknowledge degrades for non-locomotion content; extending the framework to expressive, non-locomotion styles (e.g., dance, acting) remains an open question.
