## Problem

Current VPR embeddings exhibit limited Geographic Distance Sensitivity (GDS): they produce high-variance descriptor distances at small geographic ranges, causing top-k retrievals to be incorrectly ordered relative to true geographic distance. This means correct matches often fall just outside the decision threshold, depressing recall. Existing hard-sample mining strategies are not specifically designed to address this ranking failure in densely sampled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241953/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB query images and a geotagged reference database, mapping each image to a fixed-dimensional embedding via a DINOv2-SALAD or MixVPR backbone. During training, the mining pipeline additionally requires UTM geographic coordinates for every frame to construct spatial proximity graphs, and operates on pre-extracted sequences from the MSLS training set. At inference, the output is a ranked list of top-k database images by embedding-space L2 distance, where the goal is that this ranking correlates monotonically with true geographic distance.

## Method

CliqueMining constructs a graph whose vertices are frames from S+1 visually similar sequences (sampled by central-frame descriptor similarity) and whose edges connect pairs within a geographic threshold τ (25 m). Training places are then sampled by extracting maximal cliques of size K from this graph, guaranteeing that every image in a place is within τ of every other, while successive cliques are separated by at least τ. Each batch mixes half of its places from CliqueMining with half from GSV-Cities to preserve coarse-grain invariance. The network is trained with the Multi-Similarity loss using its online pair-selection mechanism (Eqs. 3–4), which further weights the hardest positive and negative pairs within each clique-based batch. The entire graph and clique collection is computed once offline (4000 batches, ~20 min on 10 processes) and reused across all epochs, adding only ~40 min of GPU training time over the baseline.

## Results

DINOv2 SALAD with CliqueMining achieves 90.7% recall@1 on Nordland (+14.7% over the base model's 76.0%) and 82.7% recall@1 on MSLS Challenge (+7.7% over 75.0%), setting new state-of-the-art on both densely sampled benchmarks. On MSLS Val, recall@1 improves from 92.2% to 94.2%, while on Pittsburgh-250k the gain is marginal (95.1% → 95.2%), consistent with the paper's observation that GDS issues diminish as image density decreases. MixVPR with CliqueMining also improves (e.g., Nordland R@1 from 58.4% to 69.6%), though less dramatically than DINOv2 SALAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINOv2 SALAD + CliqueMining decisively outperforms all single-stage baselines (NetVLAD, GeM, CosPlace, MixVPR, EigenPlaces) and the two-stage SelaVPR (both global and re-ranking) on Nordland (90.7% vs. 60.0% R@1 for SelaVPR re-ranking) and MSLS Challenge (82.7% vs. 73.5%), where dense sampling makes GDS the dominant bottleneck. On MSLS Val, it also leads all methods (94.2% R@1 vs. 90.8% for SelaVPR re-ranking). On Pittsburgh-250k, however, SelaVPR with re-ranking edges out DINOv2 SALAD + CM by 0.5% R@1 (95.7% vs. 95.2%), likely because Pittsburgh's large viewpoint changes are less amenable to GDS-focused mining. Notably, MixVPR + CliqueMining underperforms the original MixVPR on Pittsburgh-250k (91.8% vs. 94.6% R@1), which the authors attribute to MSLS training data being predominantly forward-facing and thus mismatched to Pittsburgh's viewpoint diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GSV-Cities + Multi-Similarity loss training pipeline of Ali-Bey et al. and the DINOv2 SALAD architecture, positioning CliqueMining as a targeted improvement to the batch sampling stage rather than a change to the loss or network. It departs from prior hard-negative and proxy-based mining strategies by explicitly formulating Geographic Distance Sensitivity (GDS) as a diagnostic and designing clique-based graph sampling to produce batches of visually similar, spatially close places. The conceptual contribution is the GDS formulation itself, which reframes a known retrieval-ranking failure mode in terms of slope and dispersion of descriptor-vs-geographic distance distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GDS diagnostic gives practitioners a concrete, measurable criterion for identifying when a VPR model's bottleneck is fine-grained ranking rather than coarse invariance, and the paper clearly characterizes when CliqueMining helps (densely sampled data) versus when it does not (SF-XL, where viewpoint change dominates). The +14.7% R@1 on Nordland and +7.7% on MSLS Challenge are substantial for a training-only change with no inference overhead, making it practically attractive. However, the method is tightly coupled to VPR's geographic structure and to dense sampling, limiting its transfer to general image retrieval or sparsely sampled benchmarks, and the gains on MixVPR are notably smaller, suggesting sensitivity to the aggregation architecture.
