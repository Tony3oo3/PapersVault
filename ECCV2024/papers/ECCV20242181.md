## Problem

Zero-shot HOI detection requires recognizing interactions beyond a predefined category set, but existing methods struggle to balance seen and unseen class performance (yielding low harmonic means) and often cannot generalize to unseen verbs. A key gap is the lack of a mechanism to extract interactiveness-aware visual features for human-object pairs when confronted with previously unseen HOI concepts, while simultaneously preserving the semantic knowledge of large vision-language foundation models for accurate interaction classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242181/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and first runs a pre-trained DETR object detector to obtain all instance bounding boxes with confidence scores and CLIP text embeddings. It then exhaustively enumerates human-object pairs, extracts region features via ROI-align from the CLIP ViT feature map at three scales (human box, object box, and their union box), and outputs a set of <human_box, object_box, verb> triplets with a final interaction score computed as the sigmoid of the action logit multiplied by the object detector confidence scores raised to a suppression exponent λ.

## Method

CMMP decouples zero-shot HOI detection into two subtasks addressed by separate prompt streams to prevent error propagation. For interactiveness-aware visual feature extraction, conditional vision prompts are constructed by fusing an input-conditioned instance prior (concatenated bounding boxes, confidence scores, and CLIP semantic embeddings of all detected instances) with a global spatial pattern prior (K-means clustering centers of pairwise spatial features from training annotations) via a transformer decoder (SPI module); these prompts are injected into multiple blocks of the frozen CLIP ViT image encoder through cross-attention, where the feature map serves as query and the prompt as key/value. For generalizable interaction classification, a set of 16 learnable context words shared across all verb classes is prepended to human-designed prompt templates ('A photo of a person [verb-ing] an object') and encoded by the CLIP text encoder to produce class prototypes forming the interaction classifier. A consistency constraint loss (weighted by λ_cc) regularizes the learned prototypes to remain close to the embeddings of the human-designed prompts, preserving CLIP's original semantic space and improving unseen-class generalization. The full model is trained with focal loss on action classification plus the consistency loss, using only 2.3M trainable parameters out of 193.4M total.

## Results

On HICO-DET zero-shot HOI detection, CMMP (ViT-B/16) achieves the best unseen-class mAP across all five settings: 29.60 (UC), 29.45 (RF-UC), 32.09 (NF-UC), 33.76 (UO), and 26.23 (UV), with harmonic means of 30.93, 31.07, 30.85, 32.40, and 29.13 respectively. Scaling to ViT-L/14 (CMMP†) further boosts unseen mAP to 34.46, 35.98, 33.52, 39.67, and 30.84, with HM values up to 37.83 (UO). Under fully supervised evaluation, CMMP† reaches 38.14 mAP on HICO-DET Full and 64.0 AP on V-COCO, surpassing CLIP4HOI (35.33 and 66.3 respectively on V-COCO). The method uses only 2.3M trainable parameters (vs. 71.2M for CLIP4HOI) while achieving superior zero-shot generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the previous state-of-the-art CLIP4HOI [30], CMMP (ViT-B/16) achieves relative unseen-mAP gains of 6.82% (UC), 3.44% (RF-UC), 2.07% (NF-UC), 6.20% (UO), and 0.81% (UV), while using only 2.3M trainable parameters versus CLIP4HOI's 71.2M. The scaled-up CMMP† (ViT-L/14) widens these gaps substantially, e.g., 34.46 vs. 27.71 unseen mAP on UC and 39.67 vs. 31.79 on UO. Against HOICLIP [31], CMMP improves unseen mAP by 6.45 (UC), 3.92 (RF-UC), 5.70 (NF-UC), and 1.93 (UV) points. Compared to GEN-VLKT [23] and EoID [42], CMMP leads by 8.09 and 7.19 unseen mAP on RF-UC, and by 5.27 and 3.52 on UV, respectively. Notably, CMMP also narrows the seen–unseen performance gap (higher HM) relative to all baselines, indicating better balance rather than sacrificing seen-class accuracy for unseen gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage zero-shot HOI detection paradigm established by CLIP4HOI (Mao et al., NeurIPS 2023) by replacing its knowledge-distillation and classifier-initialization strategy with conditional multi-modal prompt learning that directly transforms CLIP's feature space. It departs from prior prompt-learning methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe) that target image classification by designing spatially-aware, input-conditioned vision prompts and decoupling them from language prompts to avoid mutual interference. Unlike one-stage zero-shot HOI detectors (HOICLIP, GEN-VLKT) that jointly localize and classify, CMMP retains the two-stage decomposition and injects instance-level and global spatial priors into the frozen image encoder via cross-attention, shifting it from image-level first-order semantics to region-level second-order pair-wise relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled conditional prompt design offers a practical recipe for adapting large frozen VLMs to fine-grained relational tasks without full fine-tuning, and the consistency constraint provides a simple mechanism to preserve pretrained semantic geometry for unseen categories. However, the method is evaluated only on HICO-DET and V-COCO, and the global spatial pattern prior (K-means clusters of training-set spatial features) is dataset-specific, raising questions about transfer to more diverse or open-world HOI distributions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger HOI taxonomies, whether the spatial prior can be learned in a dataset-agnostic manner, and how the method interacts with the quality of the upstream object detector.
