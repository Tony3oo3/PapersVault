## Problem

Unsupervised object detection methods that rely solely on self-supervised image features (e.g., DINO) produce irrelevant objects like buildings and roads, and over- or under-segment objects of interest. The paper argues that motion is the key missing cue: objects of practical interest (vehicles, pedestrians) are mobile, and their apparent motion in videos provides both instance separation and a relevance filter that static-image features cannot supply.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes uncurated unlabeled videos (with known camera intrinsics) from which motion masks and monocular depth are extracted via Dynamo-Depth. At inference, the trained detector takes a single static RGB image as input and outputs class-agnostic bounding boxes and segmentation masks for all mobile objects. No semantic class labels are used or produced; all instances are treated as a single 'mobile' foreground class.

## Method

MOD-UV initializes pseudo-labels by projecting motion-masked pixels into pseudo-3D point clouds (using monocular depth and inverse intrinsics) and clustering them with DBSCAN, yielding high-precision but low-recall instance labels that miss static and small objects. A three-stage self-training pipeline then progressively recovers these: (1) Moving2Mobile trains a Mask R-CNN (ResNet-50 backbone, MoCo v2 initialization) on single frames to reproduce the initial pseudo-labels, forcing it to detect static objects that share appearance with moving ones; (2) Large2Small trains a separate small-object detector on down-scaled images (scale 0.25) alongside a large-object detector at original scale, then aggregates their predictions; (3) a final round trains the detector from scratch on the aggregated pseudo-labels to convergence. The entire pipeline runs on unlabeled Waymo videos only, with no external data or supervised models.

## Results

On Waymo Open (in-domain), MOD-UV achieves Box AR of 19.3 and Box AR₀.₅ of 39.9, outperforming the nearest unsupervised baselines (CutLER: AR 11.7; HASSOD: AR 12.7) by 6.6 and 6.6 points respectively. The improvement is especially pronounced for small objects, where MOD-UV achieves Box AR_S of 8.6 versus 1.3 for CutLER (4.7× higher). On out-of-domain datasets, MOD-UV gains 4.9 Box AR on nuScenes and 6.2 on KITTI over the best prior method. Compared to a fully supervised Mask R-CNN trained on COCO (oracle: Box AR 31.9), MOD-UV closes the small-object recall gap from 11.6 to 4.8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOD-UV outperforms CutLER on every reported metric: Box AR improves from 11.7 to 19.3 (+7.6), Box AR₀.₅ from 20.9 to 39.9 (~2×), and small-object Box AR_S from 1.3 to 8.6 (4.7×). Against HASSOD, the gains are similar in magnitude (Box AR 12.7→19.3, AR_S 1.8→8.6), and notably HASSOD trained solely on Waymo (HASSOD†) performs even worse than the COCO-trained version, suggesting its hierarchical clustering struggles with uncurated driving scenes. Even when baselines are given an oracle ground-truth filter (removing proposals with <0.1 IoU to GT), MOD-UV still leads on Box AP by 4.1 points and on Mask AP_S by 4.5×. The only comparison where MOD-UV trails is against the fully supervised Mask R-CNN oracle (Box AR 31.9 vs 19.3), but the gap on small objects narrows substantially (13.4 vs 8.6, a 4.8-point gap vs the 11.6-point gap of prior unsupervised methods). On out-of-domain nuScenes and KITTI, MOD-UV maintains its lead over both CutLER and HASSOD across all AR and AP metrics except large-object AP, where it is comparable to the oracle-filtered baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOD-UV builds directly on Dynamo-Depth for unsupervised motion segmentation and monocular depth estimation, using their outputs to generate initial instance-level pseudo-labels via 3D DBSCAN clustering. It departs from the dominant line of unsupervised object detection (CutLER, HASSOD) that relies on DINO feature clustering over static images, arguing that these methods produce irrelevant or over/under-segmented objects because they lack a principled cue for what constitutes an object of interest. It also extends prior motion-based object discovery work (Pathak et al., Croitoru et al., Choudhury et al.) that could only detect a single salient moving object per frame, by formulating a full multi-instance detector that generalizes from video to static images through a progressive self-training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated answer to a fundamental question in unsupervised detection: without annotations, how do you know which pixel groups are objects and which are relevant? By grounding the definition of 'object of interest' in mobility, the approach aligns with the actual needs of embodied agents (self-driving, robotics) and achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised detection with no external data or supervised models. A key limitation is the assumption that mobile objects frequently move in the training videos, meaning rarely-moving objects (e.g., a parked truck that never moves in the dataset) remain undetectable. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended to non-mobile objects of interest, whether the motion cue generalizes beyond driving scenes, and whether the 12× recall gain over raw motion segmentation can be further improved by incorporating richer 3D or temporal cues.
