## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on pre-trained models either require the attacker to distribute a poisoned PTM or to fully control the victim's training procedure, and none exploit hardware-level weight bit flips during the victim's fine-tuning. WBP addresses the gap of inserting a task-agnostic backdoor at training time by flipping only a handful of weight bits via rowhammer, without knowledge of the downstream task, the PTM's integrity, or the victim's hyperparameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker consumes the publicly available clean PTM weights and 256 upstream (ImageNet) images offline to produce two artifacts: a trigger pattern (a small square patch covering 2.44% of the input area, placed at a fixed corner) and a minimal set of weight bit indices (as few as 11 for ResNet18) restricted to the 0th–2nd exponent bits of float32 parameters in the feature extractor layers. At runtime, these bits are flipped one at a time via rowhammer every 50 fine-tuning iterations, requiring no modification to the model file, dataset, or training script.

## Method

WBP replaces the fixed-OR alignment objective of prior PTM backdoors with a distance-aware formulation that directly maximizes the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between the output-representation distributions of clean and triggered inputs, making the attack task-agnostic. An iterative loop alternates between Optimized Trigger Updating (refining the trigger patch to maximize MMD under current weights) and Accumulative Bit Identification (selecting the single exponent bit whose flip most reduces the combined backdoor-plus-clean loss, constrained to bits that remain stable during fine-tuning). A Progressive Bit Reduction step then applies a Bit Set Influence score—balancing cumulative loss reduction against per-bit efficacy—to greedily evict non-critical bits, cutting the flip count by ~48% with <3.3% ASR loss. The resulting bit set is injected at runtime via double-sided rowhammer with memory massaging, requiring only unprivileged co-location on the victim's machine.

## Results

Across 24 scenarios (4 datasets × 6 architectures), WBP achieves a 99.6% average attack success rate (ASR) by flipping as few as 27 weight bits (CNNs) or 50 bits (ViT), with only a 0.2% drop in clean-input accuracy. With Progressive Bit Reduction (WBP-R), the required bits drop to an average of 20 (CNNs) and 38 (Transformers) while maintaining 99.3% average ASR; ResNet18 can be backdoored with as few as 11 bits. The attack is robust across learning rates (5e-4 to 5e-3 for SGD, 1e-5 to 5e-5 for Adam) and optimizers, sustaining >97% ASR in all configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Fixed OR baseline [Zhang et al., MIR 2023], which requires 500 bit flips and achieves only 23.4% average ASR, WBP-R uses just 26.2 bits on average yet reaches 98.0% average ASR — a 74.6-percentage-point improvement with roughly 19× fewer bit flips. WBP (without reduction) achieves near-perfect 100% ASR across all six model/dataset/optimizer configurations tested. Against the K-Arm detection defense [Shen et al., ICML 2021], WBP backdoors are detected in 3 of 4 CNN configurations but K-Arm fails to identify the actual misclassification target (e.g., detects 6→5 when the true backdoor maps All→38 for VGG16), and completely misses the backdoor in EfficientNet. NeuronCleanse [55] achieves only 7.1% detection rate on WBP-backdoored VGG16 models, and re-training for 10× the default duration (50,000 iterations) reduces ASR by only 2.4% (to 97.5%), demonstrating strong resistance to catastrophic-forgetting-based mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

WBP extends the line of hardware-based bit-flip attacks on DNNs (ProFlip, TBT, T-BFA) from inference-time, white-box settings to the training-time, task-agnostic fine-tuning scenario. It builds directly on DeepVenom [7], which demonstrated targeted backdoor insertion via weight bit flips during fine-tuning, by removing the requirement for downstream task knowledge and replacing the fixed-OR targeting mechanism with an MMD-based distribution distance optimization [19]. The paper explicitly departs from prior PTM backdoor attacks (BPM, NeuBA, BadPre) that require a poisoned pre-trained model or full control over training, positioning WBP as requiring only co-location and access to the public PTM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that as few as 11 weight bits can implant a persistent, task-agnostic backdoor in a fine-tuned model with ~99% ASR raises a practical security concern for the widely-adopted PTM fine-tuning paradigm, particularly in cloud environments where rowhammer is a realistic threat. The paper shows that existing defenses (NeuronCleanse, K-Arm) are largely ineffective and that the backdoor resists catastrophic forgetting even under 10× extended fine-tuning, leaving a gap in current protection strategies. Open questions include whether selective weight protection (the proposed mitigation) scales to larger models, whether the attack generalizes to NLP or multimodal PTMs, and how to detect training-time bit-level perturbations without full model access.
