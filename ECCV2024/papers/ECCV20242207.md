## Problem

Existing face personalization methods for text-to-image diffusion models achieve coarse text-based editing but cannot perform fine-grained, continuous control over facial attributes (e.g., smoothly varying smile intensity or age). StyleGANs offer a disentangled W+ latent space ideal for such control, but are restricted to cropped portraits. The paper asks how to combine the generalized scene-level knowledge of T2I models with the face-specific, attribute-disentangled prior of StyleGANs in a single generation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single portrait image as the identity source, which is inverted into a StyleGAN2 W+ latent code via the e4e encoder, and accepts free-form text prompts for coarse scene control. For fine-grained editing, a global linear attribute direction in W+ space is added to the latent code with a scalar strength parameter β, enabling continuous control (e.g., 0% to 100% smile). Outputs include personalized images in arbitrary novel contexts and styles, attribute-edited versions of the subject, and multi-person compositions where individual subjects can be edited independently while preserving others.

## Method

The core mechanism is a lightweight latent adaptor M (a shallow MLP with self-attention) that maps a W+ latent code w, conditioned on the diffusion timestep t, to a pair of time-dependent token embeddings (v_t^1, v_t^2) injected into the U-Net cross-attention of StableDiffusion-v2.1. Training is two-stage: first, M is pretrained on 70K (image, w) pairs from FFHQ and StyleGAN2 with a combined denoising loss, class-regularization loss, and face-recognition identity loss; second, subject-specific LoRA fine-tuning of the U-Net projection matrices plus M on the single input image improves identity without sacrificing text editability. At inference, token embeddings are injected only after a timestep threshold τ (delayed injection) to preserve layout, and a celebrity-name placeholder is used for early timesteps. Fine-grained attribute editing is achieved by adding a precomputed global edit direction d to w with scalar β, and multi-person composition uses chained per-subject diffusion processes fused via SAM instance segmentation masks at each denoising step to prevent attribute mixing.

## Results

For fine-grained attribute editing (Table 1), Ours+W+ achieves the lowest LPIPS across all five attributes (0.133–0.209), indicating the best preservation of image content during edits, while maintaining competitive ΔCLIP (1.104–4.917) and CS (0.625–0.834). In contrast, Imagic produces large edits (ΔCLIP up to 5.908) but at the cost of severe image distortion (LPIPS 0.499–0.585) and poor identity preservation (CS as low as 0.347). For single-subject personalization, the paper reports that their method achieves a balanced trade-off between identity similarity and prompt similarity, outperforming Custom Diffusion (good identity, poor text alignment) and Celeb Basis (good text alignment, cartoonish output) simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Imagic (text-based editing), Ours+W+ wins decisively on LPIPS (0.133–0.209 vs. 0.499–0.585) and CS (0.625–0.834 vs. 0.347–0.666), confirming that text-prompt-based edits distort the image and lose identity, while latent-space edits in W+ are far more disentangled. Against InterfaceGAN, Ours+W+ achieves comparable or slightly better LPIPS and CS on most attributes (e.g., Beard: LPIPS 0.185 vs. 0.221; Black: CS 0.834 vs. 0.825), but the key advantage is generality: InterfaceGAN is limited to cropped StyleGAN-generated faces, whereas Ours+W+ extends fine-grained editing to in-the-wild and stylized T2I-generated images. For single-subject personalization, Custom Diffusion preserves identity well but fails to follow text prompts (generates only close-up faces); Dreambooth and Dreambooth+LoRA improve text alignment but still struggle with faithful identity; Textual Inversion and Celeb Basis achieve good prompt similarity but poor identity preservation, with Celeb Basis additionally producing cartoonish, unrealistic faces. The proposed method is the only approach that simultaneously achieves high identity similarity and high prompt similarity while generating photorealistic output, using only a single input image (vs. 5 for Custom Diffusion and Dreambooth). For multi-subject composition, Textual Inversion fails to generate both subjects, while Custom Diffusion and Celeb Basis suffer from attribute mixing (e.g., transferring eyeglasses or hairstyle between subjects); the proposed chained-diffusion approach resolves this by running subject-specific fine-tuned models in parallel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate model families—StyleGAN2's disentangled W+ latent space and T2I diffusion models (StableDiffusion)—by training a lightweight MLP latent adaptor that projects W+ codes into the T2I model's token embedding space for cross-attention conditioning. It departs from prior face personalization methods (Celeb Basis, Dreambooth, Custom Diffusion, Textual Inversion) that rely solely on text prompts or token optimization and thus cannot offer continuous fine-grained attribute control. The multi-person composition strategy adapts MultiDiffusion's path-fusing idea, while the two-token embedding design and LoRA-based subject tuning are inherited from Celeb Basis and standard fine-tuning practice, respectively. The authors position this as the first work to combine StyleGANs and T2I diffusion models for controlled generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By injecting StyleGAN's disentangled attribute structure into a T2I model, the paper enables continuous, disentangled editing (smile, age, beard, race) on personalized subjects in in-the-wild and stylized contexts—something neither family could achieve alone. Key limitations include the need for test-time LoRA fine-tuning to recover identity (since e4e inversion is 'loose'), and the multi-person composition currently scales poorly beyond two subjects due to the multi-diffusion overhead. An open question is whether the W+ conditioning paradigm generalizes beyond faces to other object categories with similarly structured latent spaces, and whether the test-time tuning requirement can be eliminated with better encoders or larger pretraining.
