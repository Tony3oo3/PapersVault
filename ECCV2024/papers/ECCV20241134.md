## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation for 3D point cloud segmentation is difficult because LiDAR point clouds are sparse, unordered, and exhibit large distributional shifts across capture scenes, weather, and sensor types. Existing UDA methods attempt to align target-domain features directly to source-domain features, but the inherent irregularity of point clouds makes this alignment unreliable. The paper asks whether a pre-trained 2D vision foundation model (SAM) can serve as a universal feature space that both domains can independently align to, thereby bypassing the difficult direct 3D-to-3D alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud and its corresponding RGB image (with camera calibration parameters) as input; the image is processed offline by a frozen SAM ViT-H encoder to produce image embeddings and instance masks, while only the 3D network is trained online. For 360° point clouds where not all points are visible in any single image, alignment loss is computed only for the visible subset while segmentation loss covers the full point cloud. The output is a per-point semantic label (10 common classes), and the framework extends to panoptic segmentation with instance-level predictions (PQ, RQ, SQ).

## Method

The core mechanism is SAM-guided 3D feature alignment: each 3D point is projected into the image plane via camera extrinsics/intrinsics, and bilinear interpolation of the frozen SAM image embedding at the projected 2D position yields a 'SAM-guided point embedding.' A cosine-similarity alignment loss then pulls the trainable 3D point encoder's output (from both source and target domains) toward these SAM-guided embeddings, effectively mapping all domains into SAM's unified feature space. To enrich the 3D feature distribution beyond what source and target data alone provide, a Scene-Instance Hybrid Feature Augmentation strategy mixes point clouds at the scene level (polar, range, and laser-based splits) and at the instance level (concatenating complete instances selected via SAM-generated masks from the other domain), generating intermediate-domain samples that improve alignment coverage. Only the 3D backbone (SparseConvNet or MinkUNet) is trained; SAM remains frozen and is used entirely offline, making the pipeline lightweight compared to methods that co-train 2D and 3D networks.

## Results

On the 360° point cloud benchmark (Table 2), the method achieves 48.5 mIoU on nuScenes→SemanticKITTI, 42.9 on SemanticKITTI→nuScenes, 44.9 on nuScenes→Waymo, and 48.2 on Waymo→nuScenes, surpassing the best multi-modal baseline (MM2D3D*) by 9.2–15.6 mIoU points. On the xMUDA benchmark (Table 1), it reaches 73.6 mIoU (USA→Singapore), 70.5 (Day→Night), 64.9 (v.KITTI→Sem.KITTI), and 52.1 (A2D2→Sem.KITTI), outperforming the previous SOTA MM2D3D by up to +14.6 mIoU. The method also achieves comparable performance to the supervised oracle on Day→Night (70.5 vs. 69.2) and narrows the gap to oracle on USA→Singapore (73.6 vs. 76.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all five baselines across every domain shift scenario. Against MM2D3D [5] (the strongest multi-modal UDA baseline), it gains +6.8 to +14.6 mIoU on the xMUDA benchmark and +9.2 to +15.6 mIoU on the 360° benchmark; notably, even when SAM is plugged into MM2D3D's framework (MM2D3D*), the improvement is only +2.5 to +4.0 mIoU, confirming the gain comes from the novel alignment paradigm rather than SAM alone. Against CosMix [37], the best single-modal 3D UDA method, the margin is +17.9 to +18.2 mIoU on the 360° settings, highlighting the benefit of leveraging 2D foundation model knowledge. Against PL [30] (pseudo-label retraining), the improvement is +18.5 to +25.9 mIoU. Against xMUDA [20], the method gains +10.4 to +22.9 mIoU on the xMUDA benchmark. DsCML [32] is the weakest baseline, underperforming even the source-only model on three of four xMUDA scenarios, and is not included in the 360° comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard 3D UDA paradigm (exemplified by xMUDA, DsCML, MM2D3D) that directly aligns target-domain features to source-domain features, instead proposing to align both domains to the feature space of a 2D vision foundation model (SAM). It builds on the line of work transferring VFM knowledge to 3D (CLIP2Scene, OpenScene, SAM-based distillation) but repurposes SAM not as a 2D-3D knowledge distillation teacher for a single domain, but as a domain-agnostic 'bridge' that unifies disparate 3D feature spaces. The hybrid feature augmentation extends prior scene-level mixing strategies (PolarMix, LaserMix, CosMix) by adding an instance-level component that exploits SAM's instance masks to preserve geometric completeness during cross-domain point concatenation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm of using a pre-trained VFM's feature space as a universal alignment target—rather than forcing two 3D domains into each other's distribution—offers a potentially generalizable recipe for 3D UDA that avoids training a separate 2D network and processes images offline. The very large margins (14–26% mIoU) and the finding that an unsupervised method approaches supervised performance on city/light-change settings suggest the bottleneck in 3D UDA may be feature-space unification rather than label propagation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to settings without calibrated RGB images, whether other VFMs (the InternImage experiment shows smaller gains) can serve equally well, and whether the paradigm extends to 3D detection or other downstream tasks the authors mention as future work.
