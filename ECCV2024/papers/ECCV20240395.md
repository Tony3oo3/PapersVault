## Problem

Existing deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting methods model deformation as a coordinate-based function of (x,y,z,t), which is ill-suited to 3DGS's structure as a mixture of independent volumetric fields centered at each Gaussian. This design constrains the deformation field by grid resolution, model capacity, or input frequency, causing blurry reconstructions in complex dynamic scenes even when finer feature grids are added.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (300–1200 frames on Neural 3D Video, 50 frames on Technicolor at 2048×1088, all frames on HyperNeRF at 536×960) and a canonical 3D Gaussian representation. Each Gaussian carries a learnable 32-dim embedding, and each frame is associated with a 256-dim temporal embedding sampled from a 1D feature grid. The output is per-frame deformation offsets (Δx, Δr, Δs, Δσ, ΔY) added to canonical parameters, enabling real-time novel-view rendering at 74–139 FPS with model sizes as small as 33 MB.

## Method

The core contribution replaces coordinate-based deformation with a per-Gaussian embedding function F_θ(z_g, z_t) → (Δx, Δr, Δs, Δσ, ΔY), implemented as a shallow MLP with separate heads for each parameter, so each Gaussian's deformation is predicted from its own latent code rather than its spatial position. Temporal variation is decomposed into coarse and fine components: two identical decoders receive the same per-Gaussian embedding but different temporal embeddings, where the coarse path uses a 5× downsampled version of the temporal grid to suppress high-frequency motion, letting the fine path capture rapid or detailed changes. A local smoothness regularization (L_emb_reg) penalizes embedding differences between k=20 nearest-neighbor Gaussians with distance-weighted L2 terms, implicitly encouraging spatially coherent deformation without directly constraining physical quantities like rigidity. Training jointly optimizes canonical Gaussian parameters, per-Gaussian embeddings, and both deformation decoders under L1 + periodic DSSIM rendering loss.

## Results

On the Neural 3D Video Dataset (6 scenes), the method achieves PSNR 31.31, SSIM 0.945, and LPIPS 0.037, outperforming the strongest baseline 4DGS (PSNR 31.19, LPIPS 0.051) while using 248× less model storage (35 MB vs. 8700 MB) and rendering at 74.5 FPS vs. 33.7 FPS. On the Technicolor Light Field Dataset, it reaches PSNR 33.24 and LPIPS 0.100, surpassing HyperReel (PSNR 32.32, LPIPS 0.118) and 4DGaussians (PSNR 29.62, LPIPS 0.176). On the challenging HyperNeRF dataset with handheld cameras, it achieves PSNR 25.43 and LPIPS 0.231, beating 4DGaussians (PSNR 25.03, LPIPS 0.281) and D3DGS (PSNR 22.40, LPIPS 0.275) while rendering at 139.3 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 11 baselines across all three datasets on every reported metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). Against the closest Gaussian-based competitor 4DGaussians, it gains +0.60 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.056 to 0.037 on Neural 3D Video, while also achieving 44% higher FPS (74.5 vs. 51.9) and smaller model size (35 MB vs. 59 MB). Against 4DGS, which is the strongest in reconstruction quality among baselines, the method improves PSNR by +0.12 dB and LPIPS by 0.014, while reducing model size by 248× (35 MB vs. 8700 MB) and nearly doubling FPS. On the Technicolor dataset, the gap over 4DGaussians widens dramatically (+3.62 dB PSNR, LPIPS 0.100 vs. 0.176), indicating the per-Gaussian embedding approach is particularly effective for high-resolution, detail-rich scenes. On HyperNeRF with challenging handheld camera motion, the method beats D3DGS by +3.03 dB PSNR and 4DGaussians by +0.40 dB, while rendering at 139.3 FPS versus 96.3 FPS for 4DGaussians. NeRF-based baselines (DyNeRF, NeRFPlayer, MixVoxels, K-Planes, HyperReel) are consistently outperformed on both quality and speed, with the method achieving 1–3 dB higher PSNR and 100–1000× faster rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the coordinate-based deformation paradigm used by 4DGaussians and D3DGS, which predict Gaussian parameter changes as a function of 3D position and time. The authors argue this is architecturally mismatched with 3DGS, which is fundamentally a mixture of per-Gaussian volumetric fields rather than a single coordinate-based radiance field. Instead, they assign a learnable latent embedding to each Gaussian and combine it with temporal embeddings (coarse and fine) through separate MLP decoders to predict per-Gaussian deformations. The coarse-fine temporal decomposition draws on the SlowFast networks intuition, and the local smoothness regularization is inspired by Dynamic 3D Gaussians but operates on embeddings rather than physical properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-Gaussian embedding formulation offers a cleaner architectural alignment with the 3DGS representation and yields a smaller model (35 MB vs. 59 MB for 4DGaussians) with better reconstruction quality and faster rendering (74.5 FPS), making it practically attractive for real-time dynamic scene applications. The ablation showing that re-injecting Gaussian coordinates degrades performance provides evidence that the coordinate-free design is not merely a convenience but a meaningful inductive bias. However, the method still struggles with casually captured monocular videos, and it remains open whether per-Gaussian embeddings scale gracefully to very long sequences or highly deformable scenes where the canonical-to-target mapping becomes extreme.
