## Problem

Affine correspondences provide three linear constraints per match for relative pose estimation, but their extraction (e.g., ASIFT, AffNet) is 13.5×–180× slower than standard SIFT, making them impractical in real-time pipelines. SIFT features already encode scale and orientation that approximate affine information, yet no minimal solver existed that directly exploits this for the planar motion model common in autonomous driving. This paper closes that gap by deriving a planar-motion-compatible SIFT constraint and building a single-correspondence minimal solver.

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a single matched SIFT correspondence (2D point locations, scale, and orientation angle) from two calibrated images along with the shared intrinsic matrix K; point coordinates are normalized by K before solving. It outputs the two degrees of freedom of relative planar motion: the yaw angle θ and the translation direction φ (the translation magnitude is unrecoverable). In the full pipeline, the solver is called repeatedly inside GC-RANSAC over all putative SIFT matches (extracted via OpenCV with Lowe's ratio test at 0.8/0.9 thresholds) to produce a robust pose estimate.

## Method

The core contribution is a new linear constraint (Eq. 16) in the four essential-matrix elements under planar motion, obtained by computing the elimination ideal of the combined system of affine epipolar constraints (Eqs. 11–12) and the three SIFT-to-affine constraints (Eqs. 13–15), thereby eliminating the four affine matrix entries and the scale ratio. Together with the standard epipolar constraint (Eq. 8), a single SIFT match yields two linear equations in e₁…e₄, whose 2-D nullspace is parameterized as N = αn₁ + βn₂. Substituting into the trace constraint (Eq. 7) produces a quadratic in β with two analytic roots, giving a closed-form 1-SIFT minimal solver running in ~177 ns. When embedded in GC-RANSAC on KITTI and Málaga Urban, the solver matches the accuracy of the state-of-the-art 1-AC affine solver (Guan et al., CVPR 2020) while reducing total execution time by more than 25×, since it avoids the costly affine feature extraction and GPU-dependent matching entirely.

## Results

On the KITTI dataset (11 sequences, ~23,000 images), the proposed 1-SIFT solver achieves a mean translational angular error of 1.764° and mean rotational error of 0.290°, matching the best affine-based baseline (Guan 1AC: 1.746° / 0.332°) within 0.02°/0.04° while being 25.7× faster in total execution time (68.02 ms vs. 1746.39 ms). On the Malaga Urban dataset (~9,000 images), the proposed method reaches a solution with 80% probability in ~27.5 ms, an 8% speed improvement over competing solvers (~30 ms) with comparable accuracy. In synthetic experiments, the 1-SIFT solver runs in 177 ns per solve, compared to 12,427 ns for the least-squares 1-AC solver (70× faster) and 246 ns for the 2-point solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 1-SIFT solver is compared against three baselines: the 5-point Nistér solver (5PC), the 2-point Choi & Kim solver (2PC), and the single-affine-correspondance Guan et al. solver (1AC). On KITTI, the proposed method clearly outperforms both point-based solvers: it reduces mean translational error by 0.30° over 5PC (1.764° vs. 2.062°) and 0.35° over 2PC (1.764° vs. 2.118°), while also improving rotational error. Against the affine-based Guan 1AC baseline, the proposed method is essentially tied on accuracy (within 0.02° on translation and actually 0.04° better on rotation) but achieves a 25.7× speedup in total pipeline time, primarily because SIFT extraction on CPU is far cheaper than GPU-accelerated AffNet extraction. On Malaga Urban, all four methods show similar accuracy in the CDF plots, but the proposed method is the fastest in GC-RANSAC processing time. In synthetic noise experiments, the 1-SIFT solver matches or slightly exceeds the 2-point solver and is competitive with the 1-AC least-squares solver, while the linear 1-AC solver (which omits the trace constraint) degrades significantly under noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work by Barath and Kukelova on replacing affine correspondences with SIFT features for camera geometry estimation, specifically adapting their general SIFT-affine constraints to the planar motion setting via an elimination ideal. It directly builds on Guan et al.'s single-affine-correspondence minimal solver for planar motion, substituting the affine correspondence with a SIFT feature to obtain a 1-SIFT solver. The broader context is the shift from point-based solvers (5-point, 2-point) toward descriptor-rich solvers that exploit scale and orientation information already present in standard features, avoiding the need for expensive affine-invariant detectors like ASIFT or learned affine descriptors like AffNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving pipelines: by eliminating the GPU-dependent affine feature extraction step (which costs ~25× more than SIFT), the full GC-RANSAC pipeline accelerates by over 25× with negligible accuracy loss, making descriptor-rich solvers viable on CPU-only or embedded hardware. A key finding is that within a modern robust estimation framework with local optimization, the accuracy differences between point-based, SIFT-based, and affine-based solvers become small, shifting the dominant design criterion from solver precision to end-to-end latency. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other constrained motion models (e.g., pure rotation, known gravity), whether histogram voting can further reduce RANSAC iterations, and whether the SIFT approximation of affine structure degrades under extreme viewpoint changes or low-texture scenes.
