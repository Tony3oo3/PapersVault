## Problem

Existing 3D human-object interaction (HOI) datasets and models align only coarse global descriptions (e.g., 'a person picks up a backpack') with entire interaction sequences, leaving intermediate HOI states and the transitions between them semantically unexplored. This gap is compounded by the absence of fine-grained state-level annotations in current datasets and the lack of models capable of establishing alignments between detailed textual descriptions and 3D HOI states.

## Input / Output

F-HOI accepts a flexible combination of 2D RGB images, 3D object meshes, 3D HOI-Pose (SMPL body parameters θ,β with β fixed to zero, plus 6DoF object pose), and free-form text (task instructions, goal descriptions, or movement descriptions), with the specific subset determined by the task instruction. Outputs are either fine-grained part-level textual descriptions (whole-body, head, arms, hands, legs, feet, object state, interaction state) for understanding/reasoning tasks, or 3D HOI-Pose decoded from special <Human> and <Object> tokens for generation/reconstruction tasks. The reconstruction task is object-conditioned: it takes the object pose as input and predicts only the human pose, avoiding the difficulty of jointly regressing both.

## Method

F-HOI is a unified multimodal LLM framework built on Vicuna-7B with LoRA fine-tuning, equipped with modality-specific encoders (frozen CLIP for images, frozen Uni3D point encoder for 3D meshes, trainable projection layers for HOI-Pose, and SentencePiece for text) that project all inputs into the LLM's language embedding space. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) pretraining for cross-modal alignment using COCO (image-to-pose) and PoseScript (text-to-pose) to establish robust pose representations, then (2) multi-task instruction tuning on Semantic-HOI jointly optimizing understanding, reasoning, generation, and reconstruction tasks. A key design choice is offset regression for the generation task, where the model predicts pose deltas (Δθ, ΔO) from the current state rather than absolute poses, improving alignment with movement descriptions. Multi-task joint training yields consistent mutual benefits across all four tasks, and the unified formulation allows a single model to handle diverse input/output combinations via task-specific instructions.

## Results

On the Semantic-HOI test set, F-HOI outperforms the LLaVA-1.5V-7B baseline across all four state-level HOI tasks. For the Understanding task, F-HOI achieves BLEU-4 of 26.78 and ROUGE of 45.29 (vs. 20.09 and 35.20 for the baseline). For the Generation task, F-HOI reduces the averaged Chamfer distance from 44.4 to 22.9, with particularly large gains on hands (Left Hand: 35.2 vs. 55.1; Right Hand: 42.3 vs. 73.7) and legs (Left Leg: 10.6 vs. 23.4; Right Leg: 14.1 vs. 30.9). The Reconstruction task similarly improves from 48.6 to 24.7 averaged Chamfer distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares F-HOI against a single baseline: LLaVA-1.5V-7B finetuned on Semantic-HOI with an added 3D HOI-Pose embedding (but no 3D object mesh input and textual pose output via multi-turn conversation). F-HOI wins decisively on all four tasks. On Understanding, F-HOI improves BLEU-4 by 6.69 points (26.78 vs. 20.09) and ROUGE by 10.09 points (45.29 vs. 35.20). On Reasoning, the gains are BLEU-4 +6.05 (25.56 vs. 19.51) and ROUGE +6.15 (41.84 vs. 35.69). On Generation, F-HOI nearly halves the averaged Chamfer distance (22.9 vs. 44.4), with the largest relative improvement on the Right Hand (42.3 vs. 73.7, a 42.6% reduction). On Reconstruction, the averaged Chamfer distance drops from 48.6 to 24.7. The consistent superiority across all tasks and body parts indicates that the unified multimodal architecture (3D mesh encoder + HOI-Pose projection + offset regression) provides substantially better fine-grained alignment than the image-only baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLaVA/Visual Instruction Tuning architecture by adding 3D object mesh encoders (via Uni3D) and HOI-Pose projection layers, repurposing the MLLM paradigm for a domain (3D human-object interaction) previously handled by dedicated geometric or diffusion-based models (Phosa, GOAL, CHOIS, Humanise, HOI-Diff). Conceptually, it departs from prior HOI work that aligns only a single coarse goal description with an entire interaction sequence, instead introducing state-level fine-grained descriptions and inter-state transition descriptions as the unit of supervision. It also builds on ChatPose's use of 3D body pose as an LLM modality but extends the scope to full human-object interaction including object pose and contact semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing Semantic-HOI (20K+ annotated state pairs) and three state-level tasks (understanding, reasoning, generation), the paper opens a new research direction for fine-grained semantic alignment in 3D HOI that prior datasets and models have not addressed. However, significant limitations temper its immediate impact: the model requires 3D object meshes and 3D HOI-Pose as inputs (hurting practical usability), evaluation is restricted to closed-set scenarios, state-by-state generation accumulates errors without smoothness guarantees, and no physics or explicit contact constraints are enforced. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-set objects, whether input modality requirements can be reduced (e.g., via text-to-3D or monocular pose estimation), and whether the MLLM backbone is the right architecture as data scales.
