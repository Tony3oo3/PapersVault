## Problem

Existing 3D scene datasets either reconstruct real environments (noisy, incomplete) or use synthetic objects (artificially clean, unrealistic statistics). Prior efforts to align CAD models to real-world data (Scan2CAD, CAD-Estate, iGibson-DPC) produce sparse, partially-populated scenes that lack object support hierarchies and matching object sets, limiting their utility for panoramic scene understanding and cross-dataset generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241695/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Panoramic Scene Understanding task takes a single RGB panorama and produces a full 3D scene reconstruction: room layout (2D floorplan, 3D geometry, depth), 2D object detections, 3D oriented bounding boxes, 3D object meshes, and object-to-object/architecture attachment relations. R3DS provides three input variants—real Matterport3D panoramas, rendered panoramas from the synthetic scenes, and a mixed combination—enabling controlled study of the synthetic-to-real appearance gap. The dataset annotations uniquely include per-object support hierarchies and matching object instance sets (e.g., identical chairs around a dining table) not available in prior datasets.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction framework: a 3D annotation interface that overlays Matterport3D panoramas on manually refined architectural geometry (walls, doors, windows), allowing annotators to select and place CAD models from Wayfair and ShapeNet to match observed objects while enforcing physical support constraints and consistent asset selection for matching objects. This produces 19,050 object instances across 3,784 unique CAD models in 110 categories, with accurate support hierarchies by construction. For empirical evaluation, the paper benchmarks DeepPanoContext (DPC)—a relation-based graph convolutional network with differentiable relation optimization—across training configurations (iGibson, Structured3D, R3DS variants) and cross-dataset test sets, additionally augmenting DPC's Relation Scene-GCN with support relation prediction branches to quantify the impact of annotated versus heuristic support information on detection accuracy and collision rates.

## Results

For room layout estimation on R3DS-real, fine-tuning DPC on R3DS-mix achieves 59.6 2D IoU, 57.0 3D IoU, and 0.572 depth RMSE, outperforming the DPC baseline (53.4, 50.3, 0.682) by 6.2 and 6.7 points in IoU. For 3D object detection on the R3DS test set, R3DS-mix training yields 17.6 class-agnostic IoU and 15.8 mAP (at IoU 0.15), substantially exceeding the IG-trained baseline (15.6 IoU, 5.9 mAP). On the S3D test set, R3DS-mix achieves 7.5 mAP versus 3.5 for IG, demonstrating better generalization to photorealistic imagery. R3DS-mix also produces the fewest mesh collisions (0.171) and architecture collisions (1.007) on the R3DS test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses DeepPanoContext (DPC, Zhang et al. ICCV 2021) as the evaluation method, comparing training on iGibson-DPC (IG), Structured3D (S3D), and the proposed R3DS variants. On the R3DS test set, R3DS-mix training dramatically outperforms the IG baseline in 3D mAP (15.8 vs. 5.9, +9.9) and reduces mesh collisions by 70% (0.171 vs. 0.575), confirming that reality-linked scenes improve generalization to real panoramas. On the S3D test set, R3DS-mix also wins on mAP (7.5 vs. 3.5 for IG), while IG retains an advantage on its own domain (27.5 IoU vs. 21.6 for R3DS-mix on IG test), reflecting expected domain shift. For room layout estimation, even R3DS-syn alone surpasses the original DPC by 5.6% in 2D IoU and 5.8% in 3D IoU, attributed to more realistic object arrangements. The Structured3D-trained model performs best on the full R3DS-real test (10.0 mAP, 0.112 mesh collisions) when used purely as a test set, but R3DS-mix training still achieves competitive detection with far fewer collisions, validating R3DS as both a training resource and a challenging benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on aligning CAD models to real-world environments (Scan2CAD, CAD-Estate, iGibson, ReplicaCAD) by using RGB panoramas from Matterport3D as the reference for scene construction rather than perspective images or 3D scans. It builds directly on DeepPanoContext (DPC) as the evaluation method, augmenting its Relation Scene-GCN with support relation prediction branches. The key conceptual departure from prior CAD-alignment datasets is the use of panoramas to achieve more complete scene coverage, combined with explicit object support hierarchies and matching object set annotations that prior datasets lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

R3DS fills a practical gap between fully synthetic scene datasets and noisy 3D reconstructions by providing complete, densely populated 3D scene proxies linked to real panoramic observations, with physically consistent support relations. The experiments demonstrate that training on R3DS improves generalization to photorealistic panoramas and that annotated support relations outperform heuristic computation for scene relation prediction. However, the dataset's modest scale (842 panoramas across 20 houses) and the evaluation limited to a single method (DPC) constrain the breadth of conclusions; broader validation across architectures and tasks (e.g., scene generation, embodied AI) remains open. The finding that bounding-box collision optimization degrades performance on densely populated real scenes, while mesh-based collision is preferable, suggests that physical realism metrics need rethinking for cluttered environments.
