## Problem

Training high-quality generative models under differential privacy remains challenging: GAN-based DP methods are unstable and produce blurry outputs, while directly applying DPSGD to diffusion models causes excessive privacy consumption and requires large-scale pre-training. The core gap is that DP noise added to gradients degrades image quality without a principled way to dilute its effect while preserving the privacy guarantee.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240165/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model takes a random noise vector and an optional class label (assigned via MoCo feature extraction followed by k-means clustering for unlabeled or multi-label datasets) and produces synthetic RGB images at 32×32 or 64×64 resolution. The teacher model is trained on the private dataset without any protection and is never released; only the student model, trained with gradient clipping and Gaussian noise, is the released artifact. The discriminator receives concatenated teacher and student outputs at a given time step to classify their origin.

## Method

DP-SAD trains a teacher diffusion model without protection, then distills it into a student model under differential privacy by combining stochastic distillation with adversarial training. The key insight is that the diffusion time step T dilutes the DP noise: the Gaussian noise term is divided by B·T rather than B, so increasing T reduces the per-step noise impact without weakening the privacy bound. For efficiency, instead of backpropagating through all T steps, a single random time step r is sampled, and the chain rule of gradients combined with the post-processing property of DP allows noise to be injected only once at x_{θ,i,r-1} rather than at every intermediate gradient. A discriminator is trained to distinguish teacher outputs from student outputs, and the student is optimized with a combined MSE distillation loss and adversarial loss (weighted by λ=1), forming an end-to-end adversarial distillation loop that accelerates convergence and improves facial detail over pure distillation.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64, DP-SAD achieves the best IS of 2.37 and FID of 11.260 at ε=10, outperforming the closest pre-trained baseline DP-LDM (FID 14.300) and all non-pre-trained methods (best FID 55.910 by DPGEN). On downstream classification, DP-SAD reaches 96.21% on MNIST and 91.50% on CelebA-H at ε=1, and 97.61% on MNIST and 92.80% on CelebA-H at ε=10, consistently leading all 11 baselines. The largest margin appears on CelebA-H at ε=1, where DP-SAD (91.50%) exceeds the best non-pre-trained method (DataLens, 70.58%) by over 20 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-SAD outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and privacy budget where comparisons are available. Against the strongest non-pre-trained method DPGEN, DP-SAD gains 21.51 points on CelebA-H (ε=1: 91.50% vs 69.99%) and 16.49 points on CelebA-G (ε=1: 82.63% vs 66.14%), while also leading on MNIST (96.21% vs 90.46%) and FMNIST (84.37% vs 82.83%) at ε=1. Versus the pre-trained DP-LDM, DP-SAD achieves a lower FID (11.260 vs 14.300) on CelebA 64×64 and higher MNIST accuracy at ε=1 (96.21% vs 95.90%), though DP-LDM edges ahead on MNIST at ε=10 (97.40% vs 97.61% — actually DP-SAD still leads). Against DP-DM (pre-trained), DP-SAD wins on FMNIST by 4.97 points at ε=1 (84.37% vs 79.40%) and 3.40 points at ε=10 (89.60% vs 86.20%), while DP-DM slightly leads on MNIST at ε=10 (98.10% vs 97.61%). The paper notes that DP-SAD requires no pre-training, unlike DP-DM and DP-LDM, making its advantages more pronounced in resource-constrained settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DP-SAD sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPM), knowledge distillation, and differentially private training via DPSGD. Rather than applying DPSGD directly to a diffusion model (as in DP-DM and DP-LDM), it introduces a teacher-student distillation framework where the teacher is trained without privacy constraints and the student inherits DP through gradient clipping and noise addition. The key conceptual departure is using the diffusion time step T as a natural noise-dilution mechanism—dividing the Gaussian noise by B·T instead of B—and combining the chain rule with DP's post-processing property to apply noise only once per sample rather than at every intermediate step. The adversarial discriminator, borrowed from GANs, is repurposed not to distinguish real from fake but to distinguish teacher-generated from student-generated outputs, forming a novel adversarial distillation objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a diffusion-based DP generative model can match or exceed pre-trained baselines (e.g., DP-LDM) without requiring pre-training, which is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. However, a critical limitation is that the teacher model is trained on private data without any privacy protection, meaning the full pipeline is not end-to-end differentially private; only the student training step carries the DP guarantee. Evaluation is confined to small-scale datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CelebA at 32×32 and 64×64), leaving open whether the time-step dilution strategy and adversarial distillation scale to higher resolutions or more complex domains. Future work should address end-to-end privacy (e.g., a DP-trained teacher), scaling to ImageNet-level data, and the authors' own suggestion of time-step-varying λ for the adversarial loss.
