## Problem

Existing end-to-end affordance prediction methods are confined to in-domain object categories and viewpoints, failing to generalize to novel objects a robot encounters in open-world settings. Humans naturally transfer interaction experience across categories via semantic correspondence (e.g., grasping a badminton racket by recalling a tennis racket), but robots lack this ability. The paper asks whether foundation models can provide the cross-category semantic correspondence needed to bridge this gap without any task-specific training.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image of the target object (cropped via langSAM), a monocular point cloud from a depth camera, and a pre-built affordance memory of only 864 cropped object images with contact points extracted from EK100 videos. It outputs pixel-level contact points on the target object, which are then projected into 3D to select the nearest 7-DOF grasp candidate from AnyGrasp's pose set. Notably, no training data, annotations, or part segmentations are required at inference time, and the memory is orders of magnitude smaller than the 18K–54K training samples used by baselines.

## Method

Robo-ABC is a three-stage retrieval-and-correspondence pipeline. First, contact points are extracted from egocentric human videos using a hand-object interaction detector and skin segmentation, then mapped to unobstructed frames via homography and stored as a small affordance memory. Second, a CLIP-B32 encoder retrieves the most visually and semantically similar object from memory via cosine similarity. Third—and most critically—the paper exploits the emergent semantic correspondence of diffusion models (DIFT) to map the retrieved contact points onto the novel target object, applying 8 rotation/flipping augmentations to the source image and selecting the transformation with highest feature similarity to overcome orientation sensitivity. This diffusion-based correspondence naturally bridges category gaps (e.g., fork handle to flower stem) without any training, part segmentation, or pre-coded object knowledge, and the final contact point is used to select the nearest AnyGrasp grasp pose for real-robot execution.

## Results

On the AGD20K-based zero-shot affordance benchmark, Robo-ABC achieves a Success Rate (SR) of 60.7%, surpassing the second-best method LOCATE (32.0%) by a margin of 28.7 percentage points. It also leads on Normalized Scanpath Saliency (NSS = 0.516 vs. 0.283 for LOCATE) and Distance to Mask (DTM = 0.045 vs. 0.107 for LOCATE), indicating predicted contact points are both more accurate and closer to the ground-truth mask centers. In real-world cross-category grasping with a Franka arm, Robo-ABC reaches an 85.7% success rate over 7 object categories, compared to 68.6% for the VRB baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robo-ABC outperforms all five end-to-end baselines by a large margin on every metric. Against the strongest baseline LOCATE [29] (SR = 32.0%), Robo-ABC gains 28.7 percentage points in SR and nearly halves the DTM (0.045 vs. 0.107), showing its contact points land much closer to the ground-truth affordance regions. The video-based methods HOI [35] and VRB [4] both achieve SR = 26.5%, and Robo-ABC exceeds them by 34.2 points; VRB is also the real-world robot baseline, where Robo-ABC improves grasping success from 68.6% to 85.7%. The image-based methods HAP [20] (SR = 22.2%) and HOTSPOTS [44] (SR = 24.2%) trail further behind, with Robo-ABC winning by 38.5 and 36.5 points respectively. Notably, all baselines are constrained to in-domain categories and viewpoints, whereas Robo-ABC operates in a fully zero-shot, cross-category setting without any additional training or annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DIFT (Tang et al., 2023), which demonstrated that diffusion model features naturally encode dense semantic correspondence, and repurposes this capability for a robotics manipulation task. It departs from the end-to-end affordance learning line (VRB, HOI, HOTSPOTS, HAP, LOCATE), which trains models on in-domain categories and struggles with unseen objects, by instead constructing a small affordance memory from human videos and transferring contact points via retrieval plus diffusion-based correspondence. The key conceptual departure from prior semantic-correspondence-for-robotics work (which typically handles same-category instances or requires manual annotation) is the use of an off-the-shelf diffusion model to bridge arbitrary category gaps without any additional training or part segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Robo-ABC demonstrates that a small, untrained memory of 864 interaction samples combined with a frozen diffusion model can outperform end-to-end affordance models trained on 18K–54K samples by 28.7% in zero-shot cross-category settings, suggesting that foundation-model correspondence may be a more scalable path to open-world manipulation than task-specific training. The 85.7% real-world grasping success rate across 7 categories validates the pipeline end-to-end. However, the 3.41-second inference time per rotation limits real-time deployment, the approach requires manual verification of extracted affordances, and it remains unclear whether the framework extends beyond grasping to more complex manipulation (articulated objects, deformable objects) or scales to larger, more diverse memory banks without retrieval degradation.
