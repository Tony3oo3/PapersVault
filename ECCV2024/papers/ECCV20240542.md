## Problem

Existing radiology report generation models lack both interactivity (responding to user queries) and localized interpretability (visually grounding predictions with bounding boxes). Prior work either supports bounding box queries but not textual prompts (RGRG) or supports textual prompts without bounding box output (RaDialog, Med-PaLM M, OmniFM-DR), leaving no model that combines both capabilities for clinical use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240542/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ChEX takes a 224×224 chest X-ray image plus either a free-form textual prompt (e.g., 'pleural effusion in the right lower lung') or a user-drawn bounding box as the query. For textual prompts, it outputs a concise natural-language description of the queried region or pathology along with predicted bounding boxes localizing the relevant area; for bounding box queries, it outputs only the description. Full report generation is achieved by iterating over a predefined set of pathology and anatomy prompts and concatenating the per-region descriptions, with no task-specific fine-tuning required.

## Method

ChEX is a three-stage architecture: a CLIP ViT-B/32 image encoder extracts patch tokens, a frozen CLIP text encoder encodes textual prompts, and a DETR-style prompt detector (6-layer decoder) localizes each prompt by predicting M=3 bounding boxes per prompt via additive combination of prompt tokens with learned tokens, then computes ROI tokens through Gaussian ROI pooling weighted by box scores. A GPT-2-medium sentence generator, conditioned on each ROI token via P-tuning v2 and a 3-layer post-decoder attending to all patches, independently generates a description per region. Training is multitask across three token types (pathology, anatomy, sentence) with Hungarian-matched box losses, InfoNCE contrastive losses for pathology labels, and autoregressive language modeling for sentences; interactive capabilities emerge implicitly from this multitask paradigm without explicit question-answer pairs. The model is trained on MIMIC-CXR (227K images) and VinDr-CXR (15K images) with Chest ImaGenome providing additional bounding box and region-level supervision.

## Results

ChEX is competitive (within 1 standard deviation) or better than the best baseline on 8 of 9 evaluated tasks. On region classification, ChEX achieves AUROC 82.33 on MS-CXR and wAUROC 70.46 on CIG, outperforming all baselines by 5–21 points. On region explanation, ChEX reaches Mic-F1-14 of 53.34 on CIG, surpassing RGRG (45.26) by 18%. For full report generation on MIMIC-CXR, ChEX sets new SOTA on Ex-F1-14 (58.76 vs. 47.6 for MAIRA-1), Mic-F1-5+ (61.03 vs. 58.8), and Mac-F1-5+ (55.85 vs. 51.7), while trailing MAIRA-1 on Mic-F1-14 (52.32 vs. 55.7) and METEOR (13.26 vs. 33.3). The sole task where ChEX is outperformed is pathology detection on VinDr-CXR (mAP 14.12 vs. 18.21 for Faster R-CNN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TransVG [7] (SupVG), ChEX trails on mIoU for sentence grounding (47.52 vs. 53.51) but matches on mAP (44.47 vs. 44.05), despite TransVG being explicitly trained on this task. Against OmniFM-DR [72] (Generative), ChEX slightly edges out on mIoU (47.52 vs. 46.2) for sentence grounding. For pathology detection, ChEX loses to Faster R-CNN [54] on VinDr-CXR (14.12 vs. 18.21 mAP) but wins on NIH8 (11.14 vs. 6.69) and ties on MS-CXR (16.60 vs. 15.83); it consistently outperforms the weakly-supervised baselines ADPD [42] and CheXNet [51] across all three OD datasets. On region classification, ChEX dominates all baselines, beating Faster R-CNN by 6.2 AUROC on MS-CXR and 12.2 wAUROC on CIG. For region explanation, ChEX surpasses RGRG [59] by 1.0 Mic-F1-14 on MS-CXR and 8.1 on CIG, while contrastive models (BioVIL [3], CheXzero [60]) are far behind (e.g., 5.86 vs. 49.97 Mic-F1-14). On full report generation, ChEX outperforms MAIRA-1 [20] on Ex-F1-14 (+11.2), Mic-F1-5+ (+2.2), and Mac-F1-5+ (+4.2) but trails on Mic-F1-14 (−3.4), Mac-F1-14 (−7.3), and METEOR (−20.0), with the METEOR gap attributed to ChEX's query-based generation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChEX unifies three previously separate research threads: DETR-style prompt-based object detection (Carion et al., 2020), CLIP-based medical image-text pretraining (Tiu et al., 2022; Boecking et al., 2022), and GPT-2-based sentence generation conditioned via P-tuning v2 (Liu et al., 2021). The closest prior work, RGRG (Tanida et al., 2023), performs region-guided report generation but is restricted to anatomical regions and bounding-box queries only, while OmniFM-DR (Xu et al., 2023) supports textual prompts but does not describe the content within predicted boxes. ChEX departs from both by introducing a multitask training paradigm with three token types (pathology, anatomy, sentence) that jointly enables textual and bounding-box queries across pathologies, anatomical regions, and report sentences, producing both localized boxes and per-region textual descriptions in a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two barriers to clinical adoption of radiology report generation—lack of interpretability (visual grounding of predictions) and lack of interactivity (user steering of the generation process)—that most SOTA models (MAIRA-1, Med-PaLM M, RaDialog) do not jointly provide. The competitive performance with models up to 80× larger suggests that semantically grounded, region-level generation can substitute for brute-force LLM scale in this domain. Key limitations remain: training data lack question-answer pairs (limiting query diversity), answers are constrained to report-sentence style (no comparative or multi-image reasoning), and systematic radiologist evaluation of the interactive workflow is still needed. Future work on instruction tuning and clinical user-study validation would be essential before deployment.
