## Problem

Generating minority (low-density) samples from diffusion models is critical for applications like anomaly detection, fairness, and creative AI, but existing approaches rely on expensive external components such as separately trained classifiers to provide low-density guidance during inference. No self-contained method existed that could steer a pretrained diffusion model toward low-likelihood regions without additional costly training or annotation resources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241828/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The sampler starts from random Gaussian noise x_T ~ N(0, I) and optionally conditions on a class label for class-conditional generation, producing RGB images at 64×64 (CelebA, ImageNet-64) or 256×256 (LSUN-Bedrooms, ImageNet-256) that reside in low-density regions of the data manifold. The entire procedure requires only a pretrained diffusion model checkpoint—no external classifier, no additional training, and no access to real samples beyond what the model already encodes. The guidance is applied intermittently (every n steps) to keep inference overhead manageable.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-guided sampling technique that computes a low-density metric L̃(x_t; s) directly from a pretrained model: it obtains the posterior mean x̂₀ via Tweedie's formula, perturbs x̂₀ to x̂_s through the DDPM forward process, denoises x̂_s back to x̂x̂₀, and measures the reconstruction discrepancy between x̂₀ and x̂x̂₀. The gradient of this metric w.r.t. x_t serves as the guidance signal, with a stop-gradient applied on x̂₀ to reduce computation to a single backward pass per guidance step. Theoretically, the metric is shown to be equivalent to the negative ELBO (and thus a proxy for log-likelihood) when integrated over timesteps with squared-error loss. Two practical refinements are introduced: intermittent guidance (applying the update every n steps, e.g., n=5) to cut inference cost by ~38%, and a variance-based time schedule w_t = w·Σ_θ(x_t, t) that gradually reduces guidance strength in later timesteps to preserve fine-detail quality.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64, the proposed self-guided sampler with intermittent guidance achieves the best cFID of 18.57 and highest Recall of 0.48, substantially outperforming Um and Ye (cFID 27.32) and Sehwag et al. (cFID 28.25). On ImageNet-256, the method attains the best cFID of 9.93 and sFID of 4.19, surpassing Sehwag et al. (cFID 10.93) and Um and Ye (cFID 11.44). Across all four benchmarks (CelebA, LSUN-Bedrooms, ImageNet-64, ImageNet-256), the method consistently achieves the lowest cFID and sFID among all minority-generating baselines while also improving Recall, demonstrating superior proximity to real minority samples without requiring any external classifier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's self-guided sampler outperforms all minority-generating baselines on cFID and sFID across all four benchmarks. On CelebA, it beats Um and Ye [43] by 8.75 cFID (18.57 vs. 27.32) and Sehwag et al. [38] by 9.68 cFID (18.57 vs. 28.25), while also achieving the highest Recall (0.48 vs. 0.33 and 0.42). On ImageNet-256, it surpasses Sehwag et al. by 1.0 cFID (9.93 vs. 10.93) and Um and Ye by 1.51 cFID (9.93 vs. 11.44), with a notable sFID advantage (4.19 vs. 6.66 and 4.63). Against generic (non-minority) baselines like ADM, BigGAN, StyleGAN, EDM, DiT, and CADS, the method achieves dramatically lower cFID values (e.g., 9.93 vs. 13.22 for ADM on ImageNet-256), confirming that the gains come from targeted minority generation rather than general quality improvement. The key practical distinction is that all these improvements are achieved using only a pretrained diffusion model, whereas Um and Ye [43] requires a separately trained classifier (40 V100-days for ImageNet-64) and Sehwag et al. [38] requires an external class predictor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-guided minority sampling paradigm of Um and Ye [43] and Sehwag et al. [38] by eliminating the external classifier entirely. It builds on the energy-based guidance framework of Epstein et al. [12] and the DDPM forward/reverse process of Ho et al. [18], but the key conceptual departure is showing that a reconstruction loss on the Tweedie posterior mean of the pretrained model itself serves as a sufficient low-density signal. The theoretical connection to the ELBO (Proposition 1) grounds this self-guided metric in the same likelihood framework that prior methods approximated with separately trained classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: prior minority samplers required training expensive external classifiers (e.g., 40 V100-days for ImageNet-64 in [43]), making them infeasible in data-limited or resource-constrained settings. By making the guidance self-contained within any pretrained diffusion model, this work democratizes minority generation for practitioners who already have a checkpoint. Open questions include whether the approach transfers cleanly to text-to-image and medical imaging (explored only briefly in the supplement), and whether the additional inference cost (mitigated by intermittent guidance) becomes prohibitive at very high resolutions or for video generation.
