## Problem

Dehazing models trained on synthetic hazy-clean pairs suffer significant performance degradation on real-world hazy images due to the synthetic-to-real domain gap. Existing remedies (CycleGAN-based domain translation, prior-loss fine-tuning) require complex training procedures and still produce artifacts or are limited by physical prior assumptions. This paper asks whether the domain gap can be closed entirely at test-time, without any training or parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a real hazy RGB image x_r and a haze-free reference image y_s (randomly or optimally sampled from a synthetic clean-image pool) as inputs, and produces a single dehazed RGB image. The reference image is not a paired ground truth but a content source whose haze distribution is transferred onto x_r to form a visual prompt. The method is plug-and-play and model-agnostic: it can be attached to any encoder-decoder dehazing backbone (CNN, Transformer, or general restoration network) that was pre-trained on synthetic data, with no architectural modification beyond inserting the adaptation module into the encoder.

## Method

PTTD consists of two modules operating at inference time. The Prompt Generation Module (PGM) applies Color-Balanced Image-Level Normalization (CBILN) with a spatial partition strategy to transfer the haze distribution from the real hazy input onto a haze-free reference image, producing a visual prompt p that simultaneously resembles the synthetic domain (satisfying the pre-trained encoder's expectations) and carries the local haze density of x_r. The Feature Adaptation Module (FAM) is inserted after each encoder level of the pre-trained backbone; it applies Feature-Level Normalization (FLN) to the real-image features f_xr using the prompt features f_p as a statistical target, with two safety constraints: mean adaptation is aborted when the two means lie in different quadrants, and std adaptation is suppressed when the prompt std deviates more than α=2 standard deviations from the real-image mean std. The key empirical observation motivating the design is that even a tiny perturbation (Δ=0.005) to the channel-wise mean or standard deviation of encoder features produces a visible change in the reconstructed haze distribution, allowing the fixed decoder to recover a cleaner image without any weight updates.

## Results

On the four labeled real-world datasets, AECRNet-PTTD achieves state-of-the-art PSNR of 20.23 dB on O-HAZE, 18.47 dB on I-HAZE, 14.54 dB on NH-HAZE, and 13.67 dB on Dense-Haze, outperforming the strongest baseline DAD by 1.87 dB (O-HAZE) and 0.16 dB (Dense-Haze). The PTTD pipeline also boosts Dehazeformer by up to +2.74 dB (Dense-Haze) and NAFNet by up to +2.78 dB (Dense-Haze) over their unmodified versions. On the unlabeled RTTS dataset, AECRNet-PTTD achieves the best non-reference scores across all four metrics (FADE 0.7120, BRISQUE 16.63, PAQ2PIQ 72.04, MUSIQ 62.11). Inference time increases by ~60% over the backbone (38.03 ms vs 23.48 ms for AECRNet) but remains far faster than YOLY (21852 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AECRNet-PTTD outperforms all ten baselines across all four labeled datasets. Against the strongest real-image dehazing method DAD (CycleGAN-based, CVPR'20), AECRNet-PTTD gains +1.87 dB PSNR on O-HAZE (20.23 vs 18.36), +0.45 dB on I-HAZE, +0.20 dB on NH-HAZE, and +0.16 dB on Dense-Haze. The prior-loss-based PSD (CVPR'21) and the contrastive-regularization method RIDCP (CVPR'23) are substantially outperformed, with AECRNet-PTTD leading by 8.57 dB and 3.71 dB on O-HAZE respectively. Among zero-shot and unsupervised methods, AECRNet-PTTD surpasses Zero-Restore by 3.58 dB and DeepDCP by 3.31 dB on O-HAZE. The PTTD pipeline also consistently improves its own backbones: AECRNet gains +2.99 dB (O-HAZE) and +5.08 dB (Dense-Haze), Dehazeformer gains +2.61 dB (O-HAZE), and NAFNet gains +1.47 dB (O-HAZE), confirming the model-agnostic nature of the approach. On the unlabeled RTTS dataset, AECRNet-PTTD achieves the best non-reference scores (FADE 0.7120, BRISQUE 16.63, PAQ2PIQ 72.04, MUSIQ 62.11), beating RIDCP (the strongest non-reference baseline) by 0.206 in FADE and 2.73 in MUSIQ.

## Relation to Prior Work

PTTD repurposes adaptive instance normalization (adaIN) from the style-transfer literature [25] as a mechanism for domain adaptation in dehazing, but departs fundamentally from prior real-image dehazing methods (DAD, PSD, D4, RIDCP) that rely on complex training procedures such as CycleGAN cycles, physical prior losses, or test-time parameter updates. Instead of modifying model weights or requiring paired real data, PTTD operates entirely at inference time by adjusting channel-wise mean and standard deviation of encoder features, making it a plug-and-play module compatible with CNN, transformer, and general restoration backbones. The conceptual lineage traces from adaIN's content-style statistics alignment to a new interpretation: the 'style' is the haze distribution of the real input, and the 'content' is a synthetic clean image whose features define the target domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating all training (including test-time training) for real-image dehazing, PTTD removes the primary practical barrier—scarcity of real hazy-clean pairs—that has constrained the field. The model-agnostic design means any pre-trained dehazing backbone can be upgraded without retraining, which is immediately useful for practitioners. However, the paper acknowledges that dense-haze regions can still produce artifacts (color patches), and the ~60% inference-time overhead from PGM and FAM limits real-time deployment. An open question is whether the core insight—that small, structured perturbations to intermediate feature statistics can bridge a domain gap without parameter updates—generalizes to other low-level vision tasks beyond dehazing.
