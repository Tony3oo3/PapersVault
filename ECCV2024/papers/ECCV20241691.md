## Problem

The standard unsupervised MVS loss, unchanged since Khot et al. (2019), has fundamental flaws: its 1st-order depth-smoothness term encourages fronto-parallel planes (causing stair-stepping) and depth bleeding across object boundaries, while the min-K photometric heuristic inadequately handles view-dependent effects. A simple experiment—optimizing a ground-truth depth map under the standard loss—reveals large boundary errors and stair-step artifacts, demonstrating the loss is not a well-formulated objective.

## Input / Output

The method takes N RGB supervision images (5 on DTU at 1600×1184, 11 on T&T at 1920×1024, 11 on ScanNet++ at 1728×736) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a reference image, and produces a per-pixel depth map for the reference view. The DIV loss is a drop-in replacement that requires no architectural changes to the MVS network; the only additional network is a small CNN that takes the N warped supervision images concatenated along channels (3N×H×W) and outputs per-pixel weight maps at quarter resolution (N×H/4×W/4), upsampled to full resolution. Occlusion masks are rendered via shadow mapping from a meshed depth point cloud using PyTorch3D.

## Method

DIV loss comprises three modifications to the standard unsupervised MVS training objective. First, a clamped 2nd-order depth-smoothness loss penalizes min(|∇²D|, α) with α=4.0, enforcing piecewise-planar depth while truncating the penalty at boundaries to permit sharp discontinuities—unlike unclamped 2nd-order terms that bleed depth across objects, or 1st-order terms that produce stair-stepping. Second, a learned reference-synthesis network predicts per-pixel weights over warped supervision images, which are combined with shadow-mapped occlusion masks to produce a single synthesized reference image for photometric and SSIM loss, replacing the min-K outlier-rejection heuristic. Third, supervision views are sampled from a larger candidate set (M=10 on DTU) weighted by view score, so the network must predict depth consistent with views it did not see during the forward pass. The full loss retains the standard weighted combination L = λ₁L_photo + λ₂L_SSIM + λ₃L_smooth with unchanged hyperparameters, requiring less than 0.1 GB additional GPU memory and ~3 hours extra training time over ~30 hours total.

## Results

On DTU, DIV-CL achieves an Overall score of 0.321 mm, outperforming the best competing end-to-end unsupervised method (CL-MVSNet, 0.329) and all multi-stage self-supervised methods. DIV-MVS achieves 0.330 mm Overall, beating RC-MVSNet (0.345) and JDACS-MS (0.358). On generalization benchmarks, DIV-MVS (trained on DTU only, no fine-tuning) achieves F-scores of 60.36 (T&T intermediate), 38.35 (T&T advanced), and 41.64 (ScanNet++), surpassing all unsupervised baselines and even all DTU-only supervised and multi-stage self-supervised methods on T&T/ScanNet++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIV-CL (0.321 Ovr) beats the strongest end-to-end unsupervised baseline CL-MVSNet (0.329) by 0.008 mm and the multi-stage self-supervised KD-MVS (0.337) by 0.016 mm on DTU. DIV-MVS (0.330) outperforms RC-MVSNet (0.345) by 0.015 mm and JDACS-MS (0.358) by 0.028 mm. On T&T and ScanNet++ (zero-shot generalization from DTU training), DIV-MVS achieves F-scores of 60.36/38.35/41.64, beating CL-MVSNet (59.39/37.03/40.71) and ElasticMVS (57.88/37.81, which uses extra training data) on all three benchmarks. Among methods trained exclusively on DTU, DIV-MVS surpasses all fully-supervised methods (e.g., CasMVSNet 56.84, AttMVS 60.05 on T&T intermediate) and all multi-stage self-supervised methods (e.g., U-MVS 57.15, KD-MVS 64.14 on T&T intermediate). The paper does not report a direct head-to-head against MVSFormer-H or GeoMVSNet on T&T/ScanNet++ (marked as 'X' for DTU-only training), so the supervised comparison on generalization is limited to methods that also trained only on DTU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the core unsupervised MVS loss formulation established by Khot et al. [19]—the combination of 1st-order depth smoothness and min-K photometric consistency—that has become the de facto standard across nearly all subsequent unsupervised MVS methods (MVS², M³VSNet, RC-MVSNet, CL-MVSNet, etc.). It also adopts the mesh-based occlusion mask generation from Xu et al. [43] as a component of its improved image-synthesis loss. The key departure is identifying that the standard 1st-order smoothness enforces a fronto-parallel (locally-constant) depth prior rather than the more natural piecewise-planar one, and that the min-K heuristic for handling view-dependent effects is sub-optimal compared to a learned weighting scheme. The paper positions DIV loss as a drop-in replacement for the core loss in existing pipelines rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the standard depth-smoothness loss in unsupervised MVS is fundamentally mis-specified—and that a simple clamping operation on the 2nd-order gradient fixes it—provides a clear, low-cost improvement that practitioners can adopt immediately across multiple pipelines. The learned view-synthesis component replaces a hand-coded heuristic with a data-driven approach, and the additional supervision views come at zero computational cost. Open questions include whether the clamped smoothness insight transfers to monocular depth estimation and dense optical flow (the authors speculate it does but provide no evidence), how to handle the rare case where depth discontinuities coincide with low image gradients, and whether explicit camera-direction modeling in the weight network could better handle specularity.
