## Problem

Long-range point tracking (Tracking Any Point) requires estimating all past and future positions of any physical point in a video, but existing models are limited to training on small synthetic datasets due to the difficulty of acquiring labeled tracks. Self-supervised methods for space-time correspondence either produce only short-range motion fields (optical flow) or match semantically similar points rather than physically corresponding ones, failing to capture the precise, long-horizon tracking that TAP demands.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of (not necessarily temporally adjacent) 256×256 RGB video frames and query point coordinates as input, producing per-point trajectories across all timesteps, binary visibility/occlusion labels, and expected optical flow vectors. To achieve pixel-level precision, the model upsamples input images by a configurable stride (s ∈ {1, 2, 4}) before feature extraction, yielding a 64×64 grid of correspondences per frame pair. At test time, the two-frame model is applied either in a chained fashion (adjacent frames) or directly (query frame vs. all frames) to produce long-range tracks.

## Method

The paper adapts the GMFlow global matching transformer (six layers of self-attention, cross-attention, and feed-forward networks with Swin-style shifted windows) for self-supervised training via the contrastive random walk objective. The transformer's self-attention over correlation features F_t and F_{t+1} directly defines the transition matrix A = softmax(F_t F_{t+1}^T / τ) for a random walk on a space-time graph, enabling 'all pairs' matching at 64×64 resolution (vs. 7×7 in prior CRW). Training uses cycle consistency: the model is penalized when the chained forward-then-backward transition matrix deviates from the identity, supervised by cross-entropy loss. A key contribution is 'label warping'—applying different random resize-crop augmentations to the forward and backward cycles and warping the identity label accordingly—which prevents the transformer's positional encodings from providing a trivial shortcut solution that feature warping (as in Tang et al.) fails to eliminate. Unlike prior contrastive random walks that L2-normalize features with a small temperature, this method uses unnormalized features with τ = √d, following standard transformer practice.

## Results

On TapVid-DAVIS (real video), the chained variant (GMRW-C) achieves AJ=41.8 and <δ=60.9, outperforming all self-supervised baselines (best: Flow-Walk-C at AJ=35.2) and even exceeding the supervised TAP-Net (AJ=38.4, <δ=53.1). On TapVid-Kubric, GMRW-C reaches AJ=54.2 and <δ=72.4, again the best among self-supervised methods (vs. Flow-Walk-D at AJ=51.1). On TapVid-Kinetics and TapVid-RGB-Stacking, the method remains competitive but trails Flow-Walk-D (e.g., Kinetics AJ: 36.3 vs. 46.9; RGB-Stacking AJ: 56.4 vs. 66.3). Overall, the method is competitive with supervised TAP-Net on DAVIS but still well below the strongest supervised trackers (TAPIR: AJ=61.3 on DAVIS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against self-supervised baselines, GMRW-C wins on both TapVid-Kubric (AJ 54.2 vs. Flow-Walk-D 51.1) and TapVid-DAVIS (AJ 41.8 vs. Flow-Walk-C 35.2), with margins of 3.1 and 6.6 AJ points respectively. On DAVIS, the method also surpasses the supervised TAP-Net (AJ 41.8 vs. 38.4; <δ 60.9 vs. 53.1), making it the only self-supervised method to beat a supervised tracker on this real-video benchmark. However, on TapVid-Kinetics and TapVid-RGB-Stacking, Flow-Walk-D remains stronger (Kinetics AJ 46.9 vs. 36.3; RGB-Stacking AJ 66.3 vs. 56.4), suggesting the global matching approach is less effective on highly dynamic or highly synthetic content. DIFT, which relies on diffusion-model semantic features, underperforms all other self-supervised methods on DAVIS (AJ 29.7) and RGB-Stacking (AJ 24.4), confirming that semantic correspondences are insufficient for precise point tracking. Against the strongest supervised methods (TAPIR, CoTracker), the gap remains substantial (e.g., DAVIS AJ 41.8 vs. 61.3 for TAPIR), but the self-supervised approach narrows the gap considerably compared to prior self-supervised work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two previously separate lines of work: the global matching transformer architecture from GMFlow (Xu et al., CVPR 2022), which was designed for supervised optical flow, and the contrastive random walk objective from Jabri et al. (NeurIPS 2020), which was applied to CNN-based patch-level matching. The key departure is the identification that transformer positional encodings create shortcut solutions that invalidate the standard feature-warping augmentation proposed by Tang et al. (2023) for fully convolutional models, motivating the novel 'label warping' strategy. The paper also extends the contrastive random walk from short-range adjacent-frame matching to the long-range Tracking Any Point formulation, leveraging the all-pairs global matching to obtain a 64×64 spatial grid versus the 7×7 grid of prior CRW methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that self-supervised learning via cycle consistency can produce tracking performance competitive with supervised TAP-Net on several metrics, without any labeled trajectories, which is significant given the difficulty of acquiring TAP ground truth. The label warping technique is a broadly applicable fix for anyone training transformer-based models with cycle-consistency objectives, as positional encodings are ubiquitous in modern architectures. However, the method still trails the best supervised approaches (TAPIR, CoTracker) by a meaningful margin, does not explicitly model occlusions, and lacks multi-scale matching, leaving open whether combining these elements could close the gap to fully supervised performance.
