## Problem

Point cloud completion models trained on synthetic data (e.g., ShapeNet) degrade significantly on real-world scans due to domain gaps, and real-world datasets lack the complete point clouds needed for supervised training. Existing syn-to-real methods rely primarily on adversarial discriminators to bridge this gap, which proves insufficient. The paper identifies that part-level information (semantic and geometric subdivisions) is more domain-invariant than full-shape information, and leverages this to close the completion gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2048-point incomplete point clouds from both synthetic (ShapeNet) and real (USSPA) domains. In the synthetic domain, ground-truth part labels from PartNet are used for decomposition; in the real domain, a pre-trained part segmentation network (Dynamic Graph CNN) provides part predictions without any fine-tuning. A margin-based reliability filter removes noisy/ambiguous points before decomposition. The output is a single complete point cloud, assembled from individually completed part point clouds and then refined by an MLP-based module to smooth inter-part boundaries.

## Method

The framework consists of two modules: a Part-Based Decomposition (PBD) module that splits input point clouds into per-part subsets, and a Part-Aware Completion (PAC) module that processes these subsets in a part-wise manner. Within PAC, a shared encoder extracts per-part features, and a novel part-aware transformer—equipped with learnable part tokens—uses multi-head self-attention to model inter-part relationships, enabling inference of missing parts from observed ones. A shared decoder then generates complete point clouds for each part individually, which are integrated and passed through a refinement MLP to smooth boundaries. Training combines three losses: a Chamfer Distance completion loss on synthetic data (applied both per-part and on the full refined output), a one-sided Chamfer Distance self-supervised loss on real data (ensuring the output contains the input shape), and a per-part discriminator loss that pushes part-aware features toward domain invariance. The key architectural choice is that all PAC components (encoder, transformer, decoder) are shared across domains, and the part-wise processing itself—rather than the discriminator—serves as the primary mechanism for bridging the domain gap.

## Results

On the USSPA dataset, the proposed part-based framework achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 6.99×10⁻² and F1-score of 80.5 across chair, table, bed, and lamp categories, outperforming the previous SOTA (USSPA) by 2.02 in CD and 10.17 in F1-score. The method also surpasses supervised baselines (PoinTr: CD 7.96, F1 74.9; Snowflake: CD 8.09, F1 75.6) despite operating in a syn-to-real setting without access to real complete point clouds. On EMD and MMD metrics, the method achieves the best average scores (14.37 and 13.67 respectively) among all syn-to-real approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on both CD and F1-score across all four categories. Against the closest syn-to-real competitor USSPA [19], the improvement is 2.02 in average CD (6.99 vs. 9.01) and 10.17 in F1-score (80.5 vs. 70.3), with the largest gap on the lamp category (CD 6.80 vs. 11.80). The method also surpasses the two supervised baselines (PoinTr and Snowflake), which are trained exclusively on synthetic data and evaluated directly on real scans, indicating that the part-based domain adaptation strategy is more effective than simply training on synthetic data. ShapeInv. [48] and OptDE [8] lag substantially behind, with OptDE achieving the worst average F1-score of 42.6, suggesting that adversarial-only approaches struggle to bridge the synthetic-to-real gap. On EMD and MMD (Table 2), the method also achieves the best average scores (14.37 and 13.67), confirming consistent superiority across multiple evaluation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: synthetic-to-real point cloud completion (OptDE, UGAAN, SCoDA, USSPA) and part-based shape analysis (part retrieval, co-segmentation, part-aware generation). Prior syn-to-real methods rely primarily on adversarial discriminators to bridge the domain gap at the whole-shape level, while part-based shape analysis has shown that part representations are more domain-invariant but has not been applied to completion. The key departure is repurposing part-level domain invariance as the primary mechanism for domain adaptation in completion, rather than treating adversarial training as the sole bridge. The part-aware transformer with learnable part tokens draws on transformer-based point cloud completion (PoinTr, Snowflake) but introduces a novel inter-part attention mechanism specifically designed to infer missing parts from existing ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that part-level information has a substantially smaller domain gap than whole-shape information, and that this structural decomposition alone can bridge the gap without heavy reliance on adversarial training—offers a reusable principle for other domain adaptation tasks in 3D vision. The practical demonstration that the framework works even without ground-truth part labels (via co-segmentation) broadens its applicability beyond the four PartNet-annotated categories. However, the evaluation is limited to 10 object categories on a single real-world benchmark (USSPA), and the authors acknowledge dataset size and annotation errors as constraints. Open questions include whether the part-based decomposition strategy generalizes to objects with highly variable part counts or ambiguous part boundaries, and whether the approach extends to multi-object scenes or articulated objects.
