## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image generation systems fail on long, relation-rich prompts because existing datasets (e.g., Visual Genome, MSCOCO) contain predominantly single-relation triplets and standard metrics (FID, SSIM, LPIPS) cannot capture whether generated images respect the structural and relational content of the text. This paper introduces the Logic-Rich Text-to-Image Generation (LRT2I) task to explicitly target complex relational reasoning, and provides the first dedicated benchmark and evaluation protocol for it.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single long text prompt (average 89 words, up to 192 words) that encodes multiple spatial relations, cross-sentence entity references, and logical operations over a small vocabulary of 691 substantive words. It produces a 2D RGB image of synthetic scenes composed of three shape types and eight colors. Unlike conventional T2I settings where prompts average ~10 words, the TV-Logic prompts require the model to track entities across sentences and resolve ambiguous or sequential relational instructions, with ground-truth scene graphs provided for structural evaluation.

## Method

UnR-GAN is a GAN-based generator-discriminator framework with three specialized modules. The Relation Understanding Module uses a BERT encoder plus a Relation Enhancing Model to extract dependency-parsing information, then applies multi-head self-attention with a learnable soft mask (initialized from the dependency graph adjacency matrix) to amplify relation-related tokens before concatenating with noise-augmented sentence features. The Multimodality Fusion Module projects text and image features into shared spaces via n semantic self-encoders and m cross-modality towers (BridgeTower-style), then fuses them through a Text Image Residual Gating network that preserves image features when text is less informative. The Negative Pair Discriminator extends the standard GAN discriminator with three objectives: a U-Net local discriminator for pixel-level realism, a text-conditioned global discriminator that distinguishes paired from unpaired image-text sets, and an information-sensitive global discriminator that penalizes generated images for being too similar when informative tokens (adjectives, adpositions, verbs) are perturbed in the prompt.

## Results

On the TV-Logic dataset, the full UnR-GAN model (all three modules enabled) achieves the best scores across nearly all metrics: FID 40.60, SSIM 0.9303, PSNR 25.08, AP 73.62, AR 76.24, F1 74.13, and RSIM 72.78. The closest competitor, GPT+Blender, trails by 5.02 FID points and 2.87 RSIM points. The ablation confirms each module contributes: removing the Negative Pair Discriminator (Exp.7) drops RSIM from 72.78 to 66.12, while removing the Relation Understanding Module (Exp.8) increases FID from 40.60 to 39.41 but keeps relational metrics unchanged, and bypassing the Cross-Modality Tower (Exp.9) reduces RSIM to 68.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnR-GAN outperforms all six baselines on the relational similarity metric (RSIM), the paper's primary structural measure: 72.78 vs. GPT+Blender (69.91), GeNeVA-GAN (63.03), DALL-E (20.94), LatteGAN (14.29), Composable Diff (10.33), and U-ViT (7.47). Against the strongest baseline GPT+Blender, UnR-GAN gains +3.72 RSIM, +1.12 AP, +1.24 AR, and +0.41 F1, while also improving FID by 5.02 points (40.60 vs. 45.62). GeNeVA-GAN, the second-best relational baseline, is surpassed by UnR-GAN by +9.75 RSIM and +6.97 AP, indicating that explicit relation-guided attention and cross-modality fusion substantially improve spatial reasoning over prior GAN-based approaches. DALL-E and U-ViT, both transformer/diffusion-based, struggle severely with relational structure (RSIM 20.94 and 7.47 respectively), confirming that current large-scale generative models do not adequately handle logic-rich prompts. LatteGAN and Composable Diff, designed for multi-turn manipulation, also underperform on complex relational scenes, with RSIM scores of 14.29 and 10.33. The only metric where UnR-GAN does not lead is LPIPS, where GPT+Blender edges ahead by 0.0007 (0.1243 vs. 0.1250), a negligible difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-image generation lineage (GANs, diffusion models, VAEs) by reframing the input regime from short, simple prompts to long, relation-dense prompts requiring multi-step spatial and logical reasoning. Methodologically, it draws on scene-graph-based structural representations from visual question answering and image captioning, adapts the BridgeTower cross-modality tower architecture for feature fusion, and builds on the GAN-based image manipulation frameworks of GeNeVA-GAN and LatteGAN. The key departure is the introduction of a Relation Understanding Module that uses dependency-parsing-guided attention to highlight relational tokens, and a Negative Pair Discriminator that extends the standard GAN discriminator to enforce text-image pairing and sensitivity to informative-token perturbations—neither of which appears in prior T2I GANs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TV-Logic dataset is the first benchmark explicitly targeting logic-rich relational reasoning in T2I, filling a gap where existing datasets (MS-COCO, Visual Genome, SR_2D) either lack complex relational structure or omit logical operations. The paper demonstrates that standard perceptual metrics (FID, SSIM, LPIPS) can reward structurally incorrect images, motivating the relational-similarity (RSIM) evaluation. However, the dataset is entirely synthetic (three shapes, eight colors, clip-art style), which limits generalizability to real-world scenes, and the authors acknowledge open questions around handling inherent prompt ambiguity and scaling evaluation beyond controlled 3D-rendered settings. The modest performance gap between the proposed UnR-GAN and a simple GPT+Blender pipeline suggests that the core challenge may lie more in prompt parsing and scene-graph construction than in the generative model itself.
