## Problem

Existing point cloud registration methods rely on point-wise coordinate matching (e.g., Horn's solution) and degrade significantly under large rotations, partial overlap, and heterogeneous sampling patterns. The Universal Manifold Embedding (UME) framework offers a principled invariant/equivariant representation for transformation estimation, but was previously limited to synthetic closed objects because no practical coloring function existed that was simultaneously transformation-invariant and robust to real-world sampling variations in outdoor/indoor scans.

## Input / Output

The method takes two partially overlapping 3D LiDAR point clouds (64-beam KITTI or 32-beam nuScenes, or RGB-D 3DMatch scans) that may differ in density, sampling pattern, and viewpoint. Before feature extraction, each cloud is resampled onto a uniform voxel grid via the Sampling Equalizer Module, so the network always sees a regularized input regardless of the original sensor. The output is a single rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) selected from K per-pair hypotheses without any RANSAC or iterative refinement step.

## Method

UMERegRobust builds a UME-compatible coloring function from two cascaded blocks: a Sampling Equalizer Module (NKSR surface reconstruction followed by uniform grid resampling) and a fully convolutional sparse-3D-convolution UNet that assigns a dense feature vector to every point. The network is trained with a composite loss combining a point-wise supervised contrastive term, a novel UME contrastive loss that minimizes the Grassmann-manifold principal-angle distance d_pF between local UME descriptors of matching neighborhoods while maximizing it for non-matching ones, and an auxiliary Cube Reprojection Error registration loss. At inference, local UME descriptors are matched via a Matched Manifold Detector (Hungarian assignment on the d_pF distance matrix over Gr(M,4)), each matched pair yields one full transformation hypothesis through the RTUME estimator (exploiting the equivariant matrix form of the UME descriptor rather than just point coordinates), and the best hypothesis is selected by maximizing point-cloud feature correlation (PC-FCHT) instead of a consensus-size criterion.

## Results

On the KITTI benchmark, UMERegRobust achieves 94.3% Registration Recall at normal precision (1.5°, 0.6 m) and 87.8% at strict precision (1°, 0.1 m), outperforming the previous SOTA GCL by +9% under strict precision. On the newly proposed RotKITTI benchmark (large rotations of 30°–180°), UMERegRobust achieves 81.1% (normal) and 73.3% (strict), a +45% gain over GCL (40.1%/28.8%) and +23% over GeoTransformer under strict precision. On 3DMatch (indoor), the method achieves 93.4% RR and 79.7% Inlier Ratio, comparable to the best indoor-specific methods (PEAL: 94.6% RR, RoReg: 86.0% IR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMERegRobust outperforms all six outdoor baselines (FCGF, Predator, CoFiNet, GeoTransformer, GCL) on KITTI, RotKITTI, nuScenes, RotnuScenes, and LonuScenes under both normal and strict precision. The most dramatic gap is on RotKITTI, where UMERegRobust (81.1%/73.3%) nearly doubles GCL (40.1%/28.8%) and substantially exceeds GeoTransformer (78.5%/50.1%), confirming robustness to large rotations. On LoKITTI (low overlap, large translations), GCL retains the best normal-precision score (72.3% vs. 59.3%), but UMERegRobust wins under strict precision (30.2% vs. 26.9%), indicating its advantage is most pronounced when high accuracy is required. On the indoor 3DMatch benchmark, UMERegRobust (93.4% RR, 79.7% IR) is competitive with indoor-specific methods: it slightly trails PEAL in RR (93.4% vs. 94.6%) but exceeds RoReg in RR (93.4% vs. 93.2%) and matches or approaches the best IR (RoReg: 86.0%). The method is RANSAC-free, unlike FCGF, Predator, CoFiNet, and GeoTransformer which rely on RANSAC for hypothesis selection, yet still achieves superior or comparable registration recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Manifold Embedding (UME) framework [10, 14], originally developed for closed objects, to general outdoor/indoor scenes with partial overlap and heterogeneous sampling. It departs from the dominant registration paradigm (feature extraction → keypoint matching → Horn's closed-form estimator) by replacing the point-coordinate-only transformation estimation with a UME-based estimator (RTUME) that leverages both geometric and feature information from matched neighborhoods, and by using Grassmann manifold distances for matching rather than point-wise feature distances. The feature extractor architecture follows FCGF [6] but is trained with a novel UME contrastive loss and a Sampling Equalizer Module (NKSR-based resampling) to satisfy UME's invariance and sampling-robustness requirements. Hypothesis selection uses PC-FCHT [11] instead of RANSAC consensus, making the pipeline RANSAC-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of RotKITTI and RotnuScenes benchmarks targeting large-rotation registration (30°–180°) addresses a gap in existing evaluation suites that predominantly test small rotations, and is directly relevant to SLAM loop-closure applications. The +45% gain over GCL on RotKITTI and the ablation showing that UME-compatible coloring is the critical enabler (performance collapses from 81% to 17.5% without it on RotKITTI) validate the theoretical grounding of the approach. However, the authors acknowledge that strict-precision performance still falls short of what is needed for actual autonomous-robot deployment, and the method's dependence on a surface-reconstruction preprocessing step (NKSR) introduces a potential failure mode for very sparse or noisy scans. Open questions include whether the UME framework can be extended to non-rigid or scale-invariant registration, and whether the Grassmann-matching approach generalizes to higher-dimensional or non-Euclidean observation spaces.
