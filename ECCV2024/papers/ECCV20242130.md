## Problem

Existing video editing methods (e.g., Tune-A-Video, Video-P2P, Fate-Zero) fail to faithfully transfer motion to a new protagonist whose body structure differs substantially from the original. The paper identifies the root cause as a location bias in cross-attention maps of motion-related words, which attend to incorrect spatial regions and force the model to rely on temporal self-attention layers that cannot handle spatial rearrangements across dissimilar body structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242130/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source video (8–32 frames at 512×512) and a text prompt describing the desired new protagonist as input, and produces an edited video where the protagonist is replaced while preserving the original motion. During inference, a Prompt-to-Prompt technique from Video-P2P is applied to maintain the source video's background. The motion word S_mot is conditioned with N per-frame text embeddings (one per video frame) rather than a single shared embedding, enabling frame-specific spatial attention while keeping the number of learnable parameters fixed regardless of video length.

## Method

SAVE reformulates protagonist editing as a motion inversion problem by introducing a motion word S_mot with temporally expanded text embeddings v_mot^i = W_mot(v_b ⊕ γ(i)), where each frame receives its own embedding that combines the original motion word's prior with a positional encoding. A novel pseudo optical flow is computed from pre-calculated spatio-temporal attention maps (tracking semantically corresponding pixels across frames via MAX(k,i) locations) to generate motion masks M without requiring an external optical flow model. A cross-attention regularization loss L_attn constrains S_mot's attention to these motion masks, and a two-stage training strategy first pre-registers a protagonist pseudo-word S_pro to decouple appearance from motion before optimizing S_mot. This design shifts the burden of motion representation from temporal self-attention layers to cross-attention, allowing the new protagonist's spatial structure to be determined from the text encoder stage and enabling natural motion transfer across substantially different body shapes.

## Results

On the DAVIS/YouTube benchmark, SAVE achieves the highest CLIP-Text similarity (25.99) and CLIP-Image similarity (94.21) among all compared methods, indicating superior textual alignment and frame consistency. Flow similarity reaches 79.10, closely matching Fate-Zero (80.92) while substantially outperforming Tune-A-Video (55.61) and Video-P2P (64.59). In the user study, SAVE is preferred over all baselines across textual alignment, consistency, and overall editing preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVE outperforms Tune-A-Video on all three metrics, with the largest gain in Flow Similarity (+23.49), confirming that the motion word effectively transfers motion to structurally different protagonists where TAV's reliance on temporal self-attention fails. Against Video-P2P, SAVE leads on CLIP-Text (+0.72) and Flow Similarity (+14.51), showing better prompt adherence and motion preservation. Compared to Fate-Zero, SAVE wins on CLIP-Text (+1.85) and CLIP-Image (+0.16) but trails slightly on Flow Similarity (−1.82); the authors attribute Fate-Zero's higher flow score to its tendency to adhere closely to the source video structure rather than genuinely editing the protagonist, which simultaneously depresses its text-alignment score. In the user study, SAVE is preferred across all three criteria (textual alignment, consistency, overall preference), reinforcing that the quantitative trade-off with Fate-Zero does not reflect a perceptual quality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Tune-A-Video / Video-P2P / Fate-Zero lineage of one-shot video editing via fine-tuning inflated T2I diffusion models, and on image-level personalization techniques (textual inversion, DreamBooth) that register a new concept into a text encoder. Its key departure is a diagnostic: existing methods suffer a 'location bias' where motion-related words in cross-attention attend to spatially wrong regions, forcing the network to offload motion learning onto temporal self-attention layers that cannot handle structural changes. The proposed solution—temporally expanded motion-word embeddings, pseudo optical flow derived from pre-computed spatio-temporal attention maps, and a two-stage protagonist pre-registration—repurposes the textual-inversion paradigm for the video domain and reframes protagonist editing as a motion inversion problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously unarticulated failure mode (location bias of motion words) that explains why structure-agnostic video editing has been difficult, and offers a lightweight fix (pseudo optical flow from attention maps) that avoids the memory and compute cost of dedicated optical-flow models. This opens the door to editing protagonists with substantially different body plans (cat → Pikachu, man → raccoon), a regime where prior methods visibly degrade. Limitations remain: the method struggles with multiple simultaneous protagonists, and the P2P inference trick can introduce artifacts; scaling to longer videos or more complex multi-object motions is left open.
