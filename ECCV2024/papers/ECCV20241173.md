## Problem

COCO-2017's instance mask annotations contain systematic errors—coarse polygon boundaries, missing holes, inconsistent occlusion handling, non-exhaustive instance coverage, and near-duplicate masks—that increasingly mislead benchmarking as detector performance improves. The paper asks whether these annotation imperfections are now hindering COCO's ability to reliably rank and assess further progress in object detection and segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes COCO-2017 polygon masks (as prompts for SAM), the underlying RGB images, and LVIS instance annotations (for exhaustive coverage) to produce pixel-precise instance segmentation masks with proper holes, consistent occlusion handling, and complete instance enumeration. The validation set (~40.7K instances) undergoes full manual verification for a strong quality guarantee, while the training set (~1.09M instances) is processed through the automatic stages only. All masks are in the standard COCO RLE format, maintaining drop-in compatibility with existing evaluation APIs like Detectron2.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage semi-automatic annotation pipeline. Stage 1 refines mask boundaries by prompting SAM with point and box combinations sampled from COCO-2017 masks, yielding pixel-precise contours and naturally including holes that the original polygon interface could not represent. Stage 2 ensures exhaustive instance annotation by importing additional instances from the LVIS dataset (where LVIS has more instances for a given image-category pair) and discovering further missing instances via an ensemble of three LVIS-trained ViTDet models, with manual inspection of high-confidence predictions. Stage 3 manually resolves ~410 near-duplicate mask pairs and annotates remaining grouped instances as crowd. The validation set is fully hand-verified, and the pipeline is validated through ablations showing that boundary refinement alone (Stage 1) accounts for most of the AP improvement over COCO-2017.

## Results

All 50 evaluated object detectors achieve higher mask AP on COCO-ReM than on COCO-2017, with the current SOTA (OneFormer InternImage-H) gaining +7.7 AP points. The average AP across four representative models (Cascade ViTDet-B/L, Mask2Former Swin-B/L) rises from 49.6 on COCO-2017 to 55.5 on COCO-ReM. In training experiments, a ViTDet-B model trained on COCO-ReM converges faster and closely matches a 3× larger ViTDet-L trained on COCO-2017, demonstrating that data quality alone can close a significant performance gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central finding is a ranking reversal between COCO-2017 and COCO-ReM: on COCO-2017, region-based models (Cascade ViTDet) outperform query-based models (Mask2Former, OneFormer), but on COCO-ReM the query-based models score substantially higher, correctly reflecting their visually sharper mask predictions. Specifically, Mask2Former Swin-L surpasses Cascade ViTDet-L on COCO-ReM AP while ranking below it on COCO-2017 AP. OneFormer InternImage-H, the current SOTA, benefits the most from the refined annotations with a +7.7 AP gain over COCO-2017. In the training comparison, ViTDet-B trained on COCO-ReM matches the performance of a 3× larger ViTDet-L trained on COCO-2017, indicating that the noisy COCO-2017 masks were suppressing the effective capacity of smaller models. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that boundary refinement alone (Stage 1) accounts for most of the AP improvement (49.6 → 57.3), while exhaustive instance annotation and label correction contribute marginally.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the well-established instance segmentation task and the COCO benchmark, positioning itself as a targeted correction rather than a new dataset from scratch. It leverages SAM for pixel-precise mask boundary refinement (extending the model-assisted interactive segmentation paradigm from Benenson et al. and LVIS) and imports exhaustive instance annotations from the LVIS dataset to address non-exhaustive coverage. The work is concurrent with Zimmermann et al., who also re-assess COCO annotations but opt for full human re-annotation of polygon masks, whereas this paper uses a semi-automatic pipeline to maintain continuity with the existing COCO ecosystem and its supporting libraries (Detectron2, Torchvision).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COCO is the de-facto benchmark for object detection, and the finding that COCO-2017 AP systematically mis-ranks models—particularly penalizing query-based architectures that predict visually sharper masks—challenges a foundational assumption in the field. The practical impact is substantial: if the community adopts COCO-ReM, model rankings and research conclusions drawn over the past several years may need revision. Open questions include whether the test set can be similarly refined, how COCO-ReM interacts with other benchmarks (LVIS, SA-1B), and whether the annotation pipeline generalizes to other datasets with similar polygon-based annotation procedures.
