## Problem

Existing text segmentation methods achieve strong performance on regular scene text but fail on artistic text, whose strokes exhibit highly variable local shapes (slender tails, twisted ligatures) and complex global topology (holes, intricate connections). No dedicated dataset or specialized model has been proposed for this task, leaving a gap between the capabilities of current segmentation models and the demands of text-related generative applications on artistic text.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image containing artistic text (processed at 512×512 crop resolution during training) and produces a binary pixel-level segmentation mask distinguishing text foreground from background. During training, it additionally outputs a skeleton prediction (the central axis of the text strokes) as auxiliary supervision, derived from ground-truth masks via the Zhang-Suen thinning algorithm; at inference, only the mask is used. The architecture follows Mask2Former's mask-classification paradigm, predicting multiple binary masks with category labels rather than performing per-pixel classification.

## Method

WASNet builds on Mask2Former and introduces two mechanisms targeting the two core challenges of artistic text. First, a Layer-wise Momentum Query (LMQ) in the Transformer decoder computes the self-attention input as a momentum superposition (α=0.8) of masked queries from the current and all previous layers, preventing attention from rapidly collapsing to regular stroke regions and preserving sensitivity to special-shaped strokes captured by earlier layers. Second, a skeleton-assisted head jointly predicts the mask and the text skeleton, forcing the decoder to model global topological structure rather than merely local regions. Training uses binary cross-entropy plus dice loss for both mask and skeleton, combined with a classification loss. For data augmentation, a ControlNet is trained on WAS-R triplets (Monkey-generated captions, text masks, images) and then used with GPT-4-generated prompts and Mask Render–produced masks (250 artistic fonts, random rotation/skew) to synthesize 100k aligned image-mask pairs (WAS-S) for pre-training before fine-tuning on the real dataset.

## Results

On the introduced WAS-R dataset, WASNet achieves 82.11 fgIoU and 0.901 F-score, outperforming all compared baselines. With pre-training on the synthetic WAS-S dataset, WASNet reaches 84.18 fgIoU and 0.913 F-score, establishing a new SOTA for artistic text segmentation. On three public scene text segmentation benchmarks, WASNet(pre-train) achieves 73.28 fgIoU on COCO_TS, 79.30 fgIoU on Total-Text, and 87.42 fgIoU on TextSeg, matching or exceeding methods that rely on additional bounding-box or character-level annotations. Notably, a model trained solely on WAS (without fine-tuning) still achieves competitive results (e.g., 84.26 fgIoU on TextSeg), demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WASNet outperforms all ten baselines on the WAS-R benchmark. Against general-purpose segmentation models, it surpasses PSPNet by 11.0 fgIoU points (82.11 vs. 71.15), DeepLabV3+ by 2.5 points (82.11 vs. 79.65), OCRNet by 3.1 points, and SegFormer by 2.7 points. Among text-specific methods, WASNet beats TexRNet (which uses character-level recognition) by 4.9 fgIoU points and TextFormer (which requires extra bounding-box annotations) by 2.0 points. The closest competitor is DDP (81.07 fgIoU), which WASNet exceeds by 1.0 point; the Mask2Former baseline (80.21) is surpassed by 1.9 points. On the public TextSeg benchmark, WASNet(pre-train) achieves 87.42 fgIoU, edging out TextFormer (87.11) and TexRNet (86.84) despite using only binary mask labels without character-level or bounding-box supervision. On Total-Text, WASNet(pre-train) reaches 79.30 fgIoU, outperforming SMANet (74.44) and HRNetV2-W48 (74.44) by roughly 5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Mask2Former as its meta-architecture, adding a layer-wise momentum query mechanism to the Transformer decoder and a skeleton-assisted prediction head. It departs from prior text segmentation methods (TexRNet, TextFormer, PGTSNet) that rely on auxiliary text detection modules or character-level recognizers, instead using only binary mask supervision. The synthetic data pipeline repurposes ControlNet and GPT-4 from the broader diffusion-based dataset generation literature (DatasetGAN, Diffumask, SegGen), but inverts the typical approach by pre-rendering masks and generating images conditioned on them rather than generating both simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dedicated benchmark for artistic text segmentation, filling a clear gap where prior datasets (ICDAR13, Total-Text, TextSeg) contain insufficient or low-quality artistic text annotations. The proposed experimental paradigm—training on WAS and directly evaluating on other scene text datasets without fine-tuning—could simplify the field's evaluation workflow if it proves robust. However, the synthetic data improvement is modest and plateaus at 100k images, suggesting a bottleneck in the diversity and realism of diffusion-generated text images that remains an open problem for future work.
