## Problem

Existing adverse-condition driving datasets either provide multimodal sensor data with only coarse annotations (bounding boxes, road-only labels) or offer dense pixel-level semantic labels from camera-only inputs, leaving a gap for multimodal dense semantic perception under uncertainty. No prior dataset combines event cameras, MEMS lidars, and FMCW radars with fine-grained panoptic annotations that explicitly model both class-level and instance-level aleatoric uncertainty in degraded image regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241563/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MUSES sample pairs a 1920×1080 undistorted RGB frame with a 75K-point MEMS lidar scan (10 Hz), 3-second chunks of HD event data (1280×720, 15M events/s), a 4 Hz FMCW radar range-azimuth scan, and RTK-corrected IMU/GNSS pose, all synchronized and calibrated. The annotation output is a 19-class panoptic label map (Cityscapes taxonomy) plus a ternary difficulty map (not_difficult, difficult_instance, difficult_class) derived from a two-stage labeling protocol. For the novel uncertainty-aware panoptic segmentation task, models must additionally predict binary class-confidence and instance-confidence maps evaluated against the difficulty map via the UPQ metric.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage panoptic annotation protocol: Stage 1 labels the RGB image using a hierarchical decision tree (unknown_class → thing/stuff → instance/unknown_instance), while Stage 2 refines labels by consulting auxiliary data (enhanced images, event/lidar projections, temporally adjacent frames, and a corresponding normal-condition recording), yielding higher label coverage and a ternary difficulty map from the H1→H2 differences. The UPQ metric extends standard panoptic quality by converting correctly-uncertain pixels to ANY (wild cards in segment matching) and incorrectly-uncertain pixels to VOID (penalized as wrong predictions), with AUPQ averaging over a 16×16 grid of class/instance confidence thresholds for threshold-agnostic evaluation. For empirical evaluation, the authors train Mask2Former with Swin-T/Swin-L backbones, constructing multimodal variants by attaching separate Swin-T encoders per modality and fusing via a parallel cross-attention block, and compare against baselines (constant confidence, marginalization) and an oracle (ground-truth difficulty maps) for the uncertainty-aware task.

## Results

On MUSES, Mask2Former with a Swin-L backbone achieves the highest semantic segmentation mIoU of 77.1%, outperforming SegFormer (MiT-B5) at 74.7% and DeepLabv3+ (ResNet101-D8) at 70.5%. For panoptic segmentation, the quadrimodal Mask2Former (Swin-T) reaches 53.6 PQ overall, a 6.7-point improvement over the camera-only baseline (46.9 PQ), with the largest gains under nighttime (39.4→49.7) and snow (42.2→47.9). In cross-domain evaluation, the MUSES-trained model achieves the best mean mIoU of 74.1 across Cityscapes, ACDC, and MUSES test sets, despite having the smallest training set of the three datasets compared.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five architectures evaluated on MUSES, Mask2Former with Swin-L (77.1 mIoU) clearly leads, surpassing SegFormer MiT-B5 (74.7) by 2.4 points and DeepLabv3+ (70.5) by 6.6 points. The smaller Mask2Former variant (Swin-T, 70.7) underperforms even DeepLabv3+, suggesting that model capacity matters significantly on this challenging adverse-condition benchmark. In the multimodal ablation (Table 3), adding lidar to the camera input yields the largest PQ gain (+5.8, from 46.9 to 52.7), followed by radar (+4.4) and event camera (+2.6), while the full quadrimodal fusion reaches 53.6 PQ. In cross-domain generalization (Table 5), the MUSES-trained model achieves 74.1 mean mIoU, beating the ACDC-trained model (71.4) and Cityscapes-trained model (69.4), with the smallest domain-shift penalty on ACDC (−4.3 vs. −10.6 for Cityscapes-trained). For the novel uncertainty-aware panoptic segmentation task, the constant-confidence baseline (46.9 AUPQ) outperforms the marginalization baseline (44.3 AUPQ), while the oracle confidence achieves 75.2 AUPQ, indicating substantial room for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUSES extends the uncertainty-aware annotation protocol of ACDC (Sakaridis et al., CVPR 2021) from class-level to instance-level aleatoric uncertainty, and generalizes the panoptic quality metric (Kirillov et al., CVPR 2019) into the UPQ/AUPQ framework. It carves out a distinct niche in the dataset landscape: prior adverse-condition datasets (Foggy Zurich, Dark Zurich, Wilddash2, BDD100K, ACDC) are vision-only, while multimodal driving datasets (Zenseact, Waymo PVPS, DSEC-Semantic) either lack dense pixel-level panoptic annotations or do not model uncertainty in the ground truth. MUSES is the first to combine all four non-camera modalities (MEMS lidar, FMCW radar, HD event camera, IMU/GNSS) with two-stage panoptic annotations and ternary difficulty maps under diverse adverse conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MUSES fills a critical gap by being the first large-scale multimodal adverse-condition driving dataset with dense panoptic annotations and uncertainty-aware ground truth, enabling research in sensor fusion for pixel-level perception, event-camera utility in adverse weather, and the novel uncertainty-aware panoptic segmentation task. The large gap between baselines (46.9 AUPQ) and the oracle (75.2 AUPQ) signals substantial room for future work in confidence calibration for panoptic models. Open questions include whether more specialized fusion architectures can close the modest but consistent multimodal gains shown here, and whether the two-stage annotation protocol (4.6 hours/image) can be scaled or automated. The finding that a model trained on the smallest dataset (MUSES, 1500 samples) generalizes best across domains suggests annotation quality and condition diversity may matter more than raw data volume for robustness.
