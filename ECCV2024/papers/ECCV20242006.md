## Problem

Diffusion models can generate high-quality images, but controlling style characteristics beyond text descriptions or training-distribution consensus remains underexplored. Existing few-shot diffusion approaches extract a single consensus vector from a set of images, limiting diversity and offering no mechanism to inject styles from completely unseen or unannotated data. This gap is particularly acute in histopathology, where expert annotations are scarce and domain shifts between scanners and patients are substantial.

## Input / Output

The method takes a semantic layout image (downsampled to match the VQ-F4 latent resolution) as content conditioning and 1 to n style query images (512×512 RGB patches, potentially from unannotated whole slide images) as style conditioning. It produces a 512×512 synthetic RGB image that adheres to the specified spatial layout while adopting the staining, texture, and tissue-level style characteristics of the query images. No annotations are required for the style query images—only the layout must be known—enabling zero-shot style transfer from arbitrary unlabelled data.

## Method

STEDM builds on a Latent Diffusion Model with dual conditioning: the semantic layout is concatenated to the input latent, while a trainable Swin V2 Tiny style encoder extracts a style feature vector from each query image, and a two-layer MLP aggregation block combines vectors from multiple queries via a non-linear (rather than consensus/averaging) combination. The style encoder, aggregation block, and diffusion backbone are trained jointly under a standard denoising objective with classifier-free guidance (25% style dropout), allowing the model to learn both style extraction and the conditional generation process end-to-end. At inference, arbitrary unseen style query images can be paired with known layout queries to generate images in zero-shot, and two histopathology-specific sampling strategies—nearby-patch (spatially proximate) and multi-patch (randomly sampled across a patient's tissue)—address different levels of style granularity (local tissue vs. global patient characteristics).

## Results

On the HER2 dataset, the proposed Multi-patch STEDM achieves a mean IoU of 78.81% with only 6 training WSIs, closing 98% of the performance gap compared to using 24 WSIs (78.88%), while also reducing IoU variance from 1.84 to 0.24. On the CATCH dataset, Multi-patch STEDM reduces the performance gap between 42 and 7 training WSIs by 38%, reaching 85.09% mean IoU with 7 WSIs versus 85.71% without synthetic data. For generation quality (FID), the proposed method achieves the lowest scores on HER2 (45.22 at 24 WSI) and CATCH (84.09 at 42 WSI), outperforming the semantic-conditioned diffusion baseline and all style-transfer baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed STEDM (both Nearby and Multi-patch variants) outperforms all three baselines on the semi-supervised segmentation task across both histopathology datasets. Compared to the Gatys style transfer baseline [6], STEDM achieves substantially higher mean IoU (e.g., 78.81% vs. 74.60% at 6 WSI on HER2) and lower IoU variance (0.24 vs. 2.01), while the style transfer baseline provides no or minimal benefit. Against Swapping AE [21], STEDM Multi-patch improves mean IoU by 4.09 points at 6 WSI on HER2 (78.81% vs. 74.72%) and reduces variance by 67% (0.24 vs. 0.71); Swapping AE also produces the worst FID scores (82.76 on HER2 vs. 45.22 for STEDM). The sViT few-shot diffusion baseline [7] consistently underperforms STEDM in both FID and segmentation IoU across all data regimes, confirming that the non-linear aggregation block and trainable style encoder provide meaningful advantages over the consensus-based conditioning of sViT. On the CATCH dataset, improvements are more modest (e.g., 86.61% vs. 85.71% for Nearby at 7 WSI), which the authors attribute to subtler style differences and a more robust segmentation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

STEDM builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as its generative backbone and on the few-shot diffusion idea (Giannone et al., NeurIPS 2022 Workshop) of conditioning on features extracted from a set of images. The key departure is replacing the consensus-based conditioning of [7] with a trainable style encoder (Swin V2 Tiny) plus a non-linear aggregation block, enabling the model to compose style from multiple unannotated images rather than averaging them. This positions the work at the intersection of conditional diffusion generation and the style-transfer literature (Gatys et al., Swapping Autoencoder), but reframes the problem as generative synthesis with zero-shot style injection rather than transformation of an existing image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in histopathology: abundant unannotated whole-slide images but scarce expert annotations, compounded by scanner- and patient-specific style shifts. By showing that style-conditioned synthetic data from unannotated sources improves semi-supervised segmentation (closing 98% of the performance gap between 24 and 6 training WSIs on HER2), it offers a concrete pipeline for leveraging otherwise-unused data. However, the absolute IoU gains are modest, FID/IS metrics may not faithfully capture style fidelity, and the flower-dataset segmentation showed no benefit, suggesting the approach's value is domain- and task-dependent. Open questions include test-time adaptation, more rigorous style-quality quantification beyond FID, and whether the method generalizes to 3D or multi-modal histopathology.
