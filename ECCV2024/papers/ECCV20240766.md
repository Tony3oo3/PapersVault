## Problem

Existing dataset condensation methods generate synthetic images that are over-condensed on a specific model (typically a 3-layer ConvNet), causing severe performance degradation when those images are used to train other architectures like ResNet or Vision Transformers. This model dependency means that introducing a new model requires regenerating the entire condensed dataset, undermining the practical utility of condensed images. The paper asks whether it is possible to produce condensed images that are universally applicable across heterogeneous model families.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the full training dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB with class labels) and produces a small set of synthetic images (1, 10, or 50 per class) that can train any downstream model. During condensation, two heterogeneous models (ConvNet and ViT-tiny) are jointly optimized alongside the synthetic images, with learnable affine projections and a layer-matching matrix bridging their feature spaces. The output images are stored as floating-point tensors, occupying four times the storage of equivalent real images, and are evaluated by training seven different architectures (ConvNet through ViT-base) from scratch on the condensed set.

## Method

HMDC performs gradient matching simultaneously on two heterogeneous models (e.g., ConvNet and ViT-tiny) to extract common, model-agnostic features into the synthetic images. A Gradient Balance Module (GBM) accumulates the maximum gradient magnitude from each of the three optimization targets over training steps and rescales losses by the reciprocal of the normalized accumulator, preventing one model's gradient from dominating due to structural or depth differences. A Mutual Distillation (MD) loss with Spatial-Semantic Decomposition (SSD) aligns intermediate features between the two models by separating each model's features into a semantic component (CLS token for ViT, mean-pooled features for CNN) and a spatial component, then matching dimensions via bilinear interpolation, learnable affine transforms, and a softmax layer-matching matrix. The total condensation loss is the GBM-weighted sum of three terms: gradient matching for model 1, gradient matching for model 2, and gradient matching on the MD loss itself, ensuring that both the learning trajectories and the semantic representations of the two models are jointly tracked.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, HMDC achieves the best overall average accuracy at IPC 1 (48.87%) and IPC 10 (75.04%), substantially outperforming all baselines. At IPC 1, HMDC reaches 49.98% CNN Average and 47.39% ViT Average, while the next-best method (DREAM) scores only 22.16% and 12.46% respectively. At IPC 10, HMDC attains 69.34% CNN Average and 82.64% ViT Average, compared to DREAM's 47.07% and 49.64%. At IPC 50, HMDC (79.66%) is competitive with CAFE (80.86%) and IDM (80.32%) but trails DREAM (93.17%), though HMDC maintains the most balanced performance across both CNN and ViT model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HMDC decisively outperforms all four baselines at IPC 1 and IPC 10 on the overall average, with the largest margins over IDC (12.72% → 48.87% at IPC 1; 30.50% → 75.04% at IPC 10) and DREAM (18.00% → 48.87% at IPC 1; 48.17% → 75.04% at IPC 10). The critical advantage is model-agnosticism: while IDC and DREAM over-condense on ConvNet and collapse on ViT models (e.g., IDC scores only 12.72% ViT Average at IPC 1), HMDC maintains strong performance across both CNN and ViT families simultaneously. At IPC 50, DREAM achieves the highest overall average (93.17%) driven by very strong ViT-base performance (92.02%), but HMDC remains competitive (79.66%) and more balanced, with the best CNN Average (75.12%) and a ViT Average (85.71%) that is only 9 points below DREAM. CAFE and IDM, the distribution-matching baselines, are consistently outperformed by HMDC at IPC 1 and 10 but approach comparable levels at IPC 50, where more synthetic images reduce the benefit of gradient-based optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends gradient-matching dataset condensation (DC, IDC, DREAM) and distribution-matching approaches (CAFE, IDM) by replacing the single condensation model with a pair of heterogeneous architectures (e.g., ConvNet + ViT-tiny), motivated by the observation that prior methods produce images over-condensed on the training model. It repurposes knowledge distillation as a Mutual Distillation loss with Spatial-Semantic Decomposition to align features across structurally different models, and introduces a Gradient Balance Module to normalize the disparate gradient magnitudes that arise from architectural differences. The method thus sits at the intersection of dataset condensation and cross-architecture knowledge transfer, departing from the standard single-model optimization paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: every new evaluation model requires regenerating condensed images, undermining the efficiency gains that motivate dataset condensation. The IPC=1 results on CIFAR-10 (e.g., 52.45% on ResNet18 vs. ~18–22% for baselines) demonstrate that heterogeneous-model condensation can capture generalizable features even from a single synthetic image per class. However, experiments are confined to CIFAR-10, the method cannot surpass the capacity of the condensation models (notable ViT-Base gap), and floating-point storage still costs 4× real images, leaving quantization-aware condensation and scaling to ImageNet as open questions.
