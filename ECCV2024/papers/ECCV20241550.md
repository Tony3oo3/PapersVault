## Problem

No existing method for 3D human registration reliably generalizes across diverse real-world conditions such as varied poses, identities, noise, clutter, partial scans, and garments. Neural field approaches are purely data-driven and bounded by their training distribution, with no mechanism to pull predictions toward the target surface, often resulting in trivial misalignment. Classical ICP refinement is sensitive to initialization and Euclidean pairing, frequently converging to local minima.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unsorted 3D point cloud as input (ranging from ~5k to 200k vertices, potentially noisy, partial, or cluttered) with only a rough Y-axis orientation assumption. The neural field predicts 690×3 ordered offsets per query point (corresponding to a 690-vertex SMPL template subsample), which are used to deform the template. The final output is a fitted SMPL model (identity and pose parameters), optionally refined with SMPL+D displacements for high-frequency detail, along with the induced semantic correspondence between template and target vertices.

## Method

The core contribution is NICP, a self-supervised inference-time refinement that iterates two steps: (1) correspondence — querying the neural field on target surface points and pairing each with the template vertex whose predicted offset has minimum norm (replacing ICP's Euclidean nearest-neighbor with a network-suggested pairing), and (2) registration — minimizing the sum of these minimum-norm offsets via backpropagation to update the NF parameters. This is applied on top of LoVD, a localized variant of Learned Vertex Descent that uses 16 spectral-clustering segments of the SMPL template, each with a dedicated MLP head predicting offsets only for its local vertex region. The backbone is trained supervised on ~120k AMASS SMPL+H shapes, while NICP requires no additional data or supervision, taking only ~3 seconds (20 updates at lr 1e-5) to improve registration accuracy by up to 30% and enable robust out-of-distribution generalization.

## Results

On the SHREC19 challenge (44 diverse shapes with holes, clutter, and out-of-distribution identities), NSR achieves a normalized geodesic distance of 2.3, improving the previous state-of-the-art (ReduceBasis, 4.8) by 53%. On the FAUST leaderboard, NSR scores 1.06 cm (INTRA) and 2.26 cm (INTER), outperforming Loopreg (1.35 / 2.66) and DVM (1.19 / 2.37). On CAPE, NSR reaches 1.70 cm error versus PTF (2.05) and IP-Net (2.59), while processing each shape in ~1 minute versus ~3 minutes for competitors. NICP alone improves the backbone NF by 17.6% on noisy BEHAVE Kinect data and up to 30% on clean FAUST registrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSR outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every benchmark. On SHREC19, it reduces error from 4.8 (ReduceBasis [79]) to 2.3, a 53% gain, and from 6.1 (Transmatch [80]) to 2.3. On the FAUST leaderboard, NSR beats Loopreg [8] (1.35→1.06 INTRA, 2.66→2.26 INTER), DVM [39] (1.19→1.06 INTRA, 2.37→2.26 INTER), UD2E-Net [19] (1.51→1.06 INTRA), and Elementary [25] (1.74→1.06 INTRA). On CAPE, NSR (1.70 cm) surpasses PTF [83] (2.05), IP-Net [7] (2.59), and LVD [21] (1.98), all trained on the same split. The LIE/Uni baselines [54] (3.52/3.49 on FAUST_R/S) are outperformed by NSR (1.76/1.85) after Chamfer refinement. Notably, NSR achieves these gains with a single set of network weights trained on AMASS, whereas DVM requires 72 synthetic multi-view renders and a manually annotated training set, and PTF/IP-Net require ~200k training steps versus NSR's ~65k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Learned Vertex Descent (LVD, Corona et al., ECCV 2022), which trains a neural field to predict ordered offsets toward template vertices for 3D human registration. The two key departures are: (1) LoVD, which replaces the single global NF with multiple localized MLP heads segmented via spectral clustering of the SMPL Laplace-Beltrami operator, and (2) NICP, an inference-time self-supervised refinement that repurposes ICP's iterative correspondence-deformation loop by using the NF's own minimum-norm offset predictions as correspondences instead of Euclidean nearest-neighbor pairing. This positions the work at the intersection of classical iterative registration (ICP and its variants) and modern neural field representations, explicitly addressing the limitation that purely data-driven NFs are bounded by their training distribution and lack any geometric guidance toward the target surface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first 3D human registration pipeline demonstrated to generalize across 5K+ shapes from 10+ heterogeneous data sources (Kinect scans, NeRF reconstructions, multi-view captures, clothed humans, partial scans) using a single set of network weights, addressing a long-standing scalability gap in the field. NICP's backbone-agnostic, inference-time nature means it can be applied to any pre-trained NF without retraining, making it a broadly applicable technique beyond human registration. Open questions include failure modes under extreme partiality or severe self-contact (discussed in supplementary material), whether the spectral clustering localization strategy transfers to non-SMPL templates, and whether the approach extends to other articulated or non-articulated shape registration domains.
