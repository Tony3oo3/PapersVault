## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering and high visual quality on object-centric scenes but degrades significantly on unstructured in-the-wild photo collections due to photometric variations (weather, lighting, exposure) and transient occluders. Prior in-the-wild methods (NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF, CR-NeRF) address these challenges via implicit radiance fields but incur prohibitive volumetric rendering costs, making real-time interaction impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242038/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unconstrained collection of RGB photos with camera poses estimated via sparse Structure-from-Motion, where images may differ in exposure, weather, and contain moving people or vehicles. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that supports novel view synthesis at real-time frame rates, appearance transfer to arbitrary viewpoints via learned per-image embeddings, smooth appearance interpolation in embedding space, and occluders-free rendering by excluding Gaussians identified as transient (non-zero variance in their opacity variation across training images).

## Method

SWAG extends 3DGS with two coupled mechanisms driven by a shared 3-layer MLP (64 hidden units). For appearance, each image is assigned a 24-dim trainable embedding, and the MLP takes the concatenation of the Gaussian's SH color, a Hash Grid positional encoding of its 3D center (table size 2^19, 12 levels, resolutions 16–2048), and the image embedding to predict an image-conditioned color, capturing local photometric variations while keeping the parameter count tractable. For transient objects, the same MLP outputs an opacity-variation parameter Δα^I that is passed through a Binary Concrete distribution (fixed temperature T=0.1, no annealing) to yield a near-binary Δα̃^I; the final per-image opacity is max(α − Δα̃^I, 0), forcing each Gaussian to be either fully visible or fully masked per image. Transient Gaussians are then identified post-training by non-zero variance of Δα̃^I across the training set, enabling unsupervised occluder removal. Training minimizes L1 + D-SSIM loss with standard 3DGS adaptive densification and pruning, completing in ~0.83 h on a high-tier GPU versus 150–452 h for prior in-the-wild baselines.

## Results

On the Phototourism benchmark (Brandenburg Gate, Sacre Coeur, Trevi Fountain), SWAG achieves mean PSNR of 23.53 dB, SSIM of 0.868, and LPIPS of 0.177, improving 3DGS by an average of 5.01 dB in PSNR. Training time is 0.83 hours with 15.29 FPS rendering speed, compared to 150–452 hours and <1 FPS for prior in-the-wild NeRF-based methods. On the NeRF-OSR outdoor relighting benchmark, SWAG improves 3DGS by ~5 dB average PSNR and outperforms all baselines (NeRF, NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF, NeRF-MS) across all four evaluated sites.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWAG outperforms 3DGS by a large margin (+5.01 dB average PSNR) on all three Phototourism scenes, confirming that vanilla 3DGS fails on in-the-wild data due to lack of appearance conditioning. Against NeRF-W, SWAG wins on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all scenes while training ~480× faster (0.83 h vs. 400 h). Compared to CR-NeRF, SWAG achieves higher SSIM on all scenes (e.g., 0.929 vs. 0.900 on Brandenburg Gate) and higher PSNR on Trevi Fountain (23.10 vs. 21.48), though CR-NeRF retains a slight PSNR edge on Brandenburg Gate (26.53 vs. 26.33) and Sacre Coeur (22.07 vs. 21.16). RefinedFields shows marginally higher PSNR on Brandenburg Gate (26.64 vs. 26.33) and Sacre Coeur (22.26 vs. 21.16), but SWAG surpasses it on SSIM for Brandenburg Gate (0.929 vs. 0.886) and Trevi Fountain (0.815 vs. 0.737), and trains 180× faster. On the NeRF-OSR benchmark, SWAG dominates all baselines (NeRF, NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF, NeRF-MS, 3DGS) on both PSNR and SSIM across all four sites, with the largest gains over 3DGS on the lwp site (22.07 vs. 11.76 PSNR). The key efficiency advantage is that SWAG trains in under 1 hour and renders at 15 FPS, versus 150–452 hours and sub-1 FPS for all implicit NeRF-based in-the-wild methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWAG extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to the in-the-wild setting by transplanting the per-image appearance embedding concept from NeRF-W into the explicit Gaussian representation, augmented with a hash-grid positional encoding and an MLP to predict image-conditioned colors. For transient object handling, it departs from both NeRF-W's separate transient radiance field and Ha-NeRF/CR-NeRF's 2D visibility maps, instead learning an image-dependent opacity variation per Gaussian via a Binary Concrete distribution — a mechanism that naturally disentangles static and transient elements within the same explicit representation. The method thus occupies a distinct position: it retains 3DGS's real-time rasterization advantage while addressing the two core failure modes (appearance variation and occluders) that previously limited it to controlled, object-centric scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS rapidly becomes the dominant representation for real-time 3D rendering, demonstrating that it can handle unconstrained photo collections with SOTA quality and orders-of-magnitude faster training than NeRF-based in-the-wild methods is practically significant for applications like virtual tourism and interactive 3D. However, the approach still nearly doubles training time and increases per-frame inference cost by ~10× over vanilla 3DGS, and removing transient Gaussians can leave holes in regions with frequent occluders (e.g., clouds, crowds), requiring a manually tuned variance threshold. The paper explicitly identifies extension to truly dynamic scenes as future work, and the reliance on SfM poses for initialization remains a constraint for fully unconstrained capture.
