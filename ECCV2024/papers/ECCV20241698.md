## Problem

Prior weakly supervised affordance grounding methods require paired exocentric and egocentric images for training, a constraint that human observational learning does not impose. Additionally, these methods treat all other interaction classes as uniform negatives in contrastive objectives, ignoring the intricate many-to-many relationships between interactions (e.g., 'sip' entails 'hold', 'wash' and 'brush with' target the same object part), which causes the model to produce nearly identical affordance maps for different interactions on the same object.

## Input / Output

During training, INTRA consumes only exocentric images (showing a human interacting with an object) paired with interaction text labels, eliminating the need for egocentric object-only images. At inference, it takes an egocentric image and an arbitrary interaction text label (including verbs never seen in training) and produces a spatial affordance heatmap of the same resolution as the input feature map (336×336 after resizing/cropping). The text-conditioned design means the interaction label is not restricted to a fixed vocabulary, enabling zero-shot grounding for novel interactions.

## Method

INTRA recasts weakly supervised affordance grounding as representation learning via contrastive objectives on exocentric features only. A text-conditioned affordance map generation module fuses the ALBEF text encoder's class token with DINOv2 image features through a transformer encoder and a convolutional projection to produce a spatial heatmap, which is then used to extract weighted exocentric features. The key training signal is an interaction relationship-guided contrastive loss: GPT-4 uses Chain-of-Thought reasoning to classify every pair of interactions as positive (acting on the same object part) or negative, and this binary relationship map R modulates the supervised contrastive loss so that only semantically related interactions are pulled together. An auxiliary object-variance mitigation loss further enforces that the same interaction on different objects yields distinct features, and LLM-generated text synonym augmentation enriches the text conditioning for robustness to paraphrasing.

## Results

On AGD20K, INTRA achieves mKLD 1.199, mSIM 0.407, and mNSS 1.239 in the Seen setting, and mKLD 1.365, mSIM 0.375, and mNSS 1.209 in the Unseen setting, outperforming all seven baselines on every metric. Notably, INTRA is trained exclusively on exocentric images, whereas all competing methods use both exocentric and egocentric images. On the cross-dataset evaluation (IIT-AFF, CAD, UMD), INTRA also leads on all metrics, e.g., mKLD 2.663 vs. 3.315 (LOCATE) on IIT-AFF and mNSS 4.195 vs. 2.699 on UMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INTRA outperforms all seven baselines on every metric in both Seen and Unseen settings of AGD20K. Against the strongest prior method LOCATE [25], INTRA reduces mKLD from 1.226 to 1.199 (Seen) and 1.405 to 1.365 (Unseen), while improving mSIM by 0.006/0.003 and mNSS by 0.062/0.052 respectively. The cross-view methods (Cross-view-AG [32] and Cross-view-AG+ [31]) trail INTRA by a wider margin, with mKLD gaps of ~0.29–0.34 in the Seen setting. The weakly supervised object localization baselines (EIL [36], SPA [48], TS-CAM [15]) and the video-based Hotspots [41] are substantially weaker, with mKLD values 0.3–4.3 higher than INTRA. On the cross-dataset evaluation (IIT-AFF, CAD, UMD), INTRA extends its lead, e.g., achieving mKLD 2.663 vs. 3.315 for LOCATE on IIT-AFF and mNSS 4.195 vs. 2.699 on UMD, demonstrating superior generalizability across domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the weakly supervised affordance grounding line initiated by Cross-view-AG [32], Cross-view-AG+ [31], and LOCATE [25], which align object features from paired exocentric and egocentric images via supervised or pseudo-label-based losses. INTRA departs from this paradigm by recasting the problem as representation learning through contrastive learning on exocentric images alone, eliminating the paired-dataset requirement. It further introduces VLM text conditioning (ALBEF) for flexible, text-driven affordance map generation and leverages LLM chain-of-thought reasoning to construct an interaction-relationship map that guides contrastive learning, addressing the many-to-many interaction relationships that prior methods ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for paired exocentric/egocentric images and enabling inference on novel interactions outside a predefined set, INTRA lowers the practical barrier to deploying affordance grounding in open-world settings where such paired data is rarely available. The demonstrated generalization to domain-shifted images (pixel art, paintings) and unseen objects/interactions suggests the representation-learning formulation is more robust than feature-alignment approaches, though the evidence for these claims is primarily qualitative. Open questions include whether the LLM-derived interaction-relationship map scales to very large interaction vocabularies, whether the approach extends to video-based affordance grounding, and how sensitive performance is to the quality of the LLM's reasoning about object-part co-occurrence.
