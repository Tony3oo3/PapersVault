## Problem

Training object detectors with noisy crowdsourced annotations is challenging because existing methods either assume all annotators are equally reliable, require prior knowledge of annotator proficiency levels, or are tightly coupled to a specific detector architecture (e.g., Faster R-CNN). Additionally, prior works are evaluated on distinct, privately constructed synthetic datasets with different annotator counts and skill distributions, making direct comparison of results impossible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241688/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images paired with multiple per-image crowdsourced annotations, each consisting of a 4-element bounding box (centre x, y, width, height), a discrete class label, and an annotator ID. Rather than producing a single hard label per object, BDC outputs aggregated bounding boxes weighted by inferred annotator precision, soft class probability vectors (one per class), and per-annotator quality parameters (Gaussian-Gamma posteriors for box errors and Dirichlet posteriors for confusion matrices). The object detector is treated as a black box, so any architecture that emits predicted boxes and class probabilities can be plugged in without modification.

## Method

BDC comprises four components trained iteratively: an object detector (OD), an annotations-predictions matcher, a Bounding Box Aggregator (BBA), and a Class Label Aggregator (CLA). The matcher performs a one-to-many assignment (each prediction can match many annotations) by minimising a cost combining negative predicted class probability, IoU loss, and L1 box distance, avoiding the computationally expensive Hungarian algorithm. The BBA models each annotator's translation and scaling errors with a Gaussian-Gamma prior, updated from matched prediction-annotation residuals, and uses the posterior precision as weights for a weighted-average box fusion. The CLA extends BCCNet by placing Dirichlet priors on per-annotator confusion matrices and computing aggregated soft class probabilities via a multinomial posterior that fuses the OD's class predictions with annotator-specific likelihoods. Training alternates between optimising the OD loss (re-weighted by a loss scale factor reflecting annotator agreement) and updating the aggregator posteriors from the OD's current predictions, repeating until convergence.

## Results

On the real VinDr-CXR dataset, BDC achieves AP^4 of 19.2 (YOLOv7), 17.9 (FRCNN), and 8.9 (EVA), outperforming all baselines across all three detector backbones. On the synthetic VOC-MIX setting (25 annotators with mixed skill levels), BDC with YOLOv7 reaches 86.2 AP^5, approaching the ground-truth upper bound of 87.7, while the next-best method (MV) achieves only 77.1. On the large-scale COCO-MIX setting (1,000 annotators), BDC with YOLOv7 scores 64.8 AP^5 versus 60.9 for MV and 56.2 for WBF-EARL, with a GT upper bound of 67.5. BDC also achieves the highest train aggregation AP across all settings (e.g., 97.6 AP^5 on VOC-MIX vs. 75.7 for MV), confirming superior consensus annotation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BDC outperforms all four baselines (NA, MV, Crowd R-CNN, WBF-EARL) across every dataset and detector backbone evaluated. Against NA (no aggregation), BDC gains 12.1 AP^5 on VOC-MIX with YOLOv7 (86.2 vs. 46.1) and 11.4 AP^5 on COCO-MIX (64.8 vs. 53.4), showing that simply using all noisy annotations is far inferior to learned aggregation. Against MV, BDC improves by 9.1 AP^5 on VOC-MIX (86.2 vs. 77.1) and 3.9 AP^5 on COCO-MIX (64.8 vs. 60.9), with the gap widening as annotator heterogeneity increases. Against Crowd R-CNN (only comparable with FRCNN), BDC leads by 14.5 AP^5 on VOC-MIX (84.7 vs. 70.2) and 7.6 AP^5 on COCO-FULL (56.6 vs. 48.5), and Crowd R-CNN is infeasible at K=1,000 due to linearly scaling training time. Against WBF-EARL, BDC gains 25.2 AP^5 on VOC-MIX with YOLOv7 (86.2 vs. 61.0) and 2.9 AP^5 on COCO-MIX (64.8 vs. 56.2); WBF-EARL also requires a priori knowledge of annotator proficiency levels, which BDC does not. On the real VinDr-CXR dataset, BDC achieves 19.2 AP^4 with YOLOv7 versus 17.4 (NA), 13.9 (MV), 16.7 (Crowd R-CNN with FRCNN only), and 16.4 (WBF-EARL), confirming consistent gains in a real-world setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bayesian classifier combination (BCC) framework from Isupova et al. (BCCNet) from image classification to object detection, adding a Gaussian-Gamma bounding box aggregator alongside the Dirichlet-based class label aggregator. It departs from prior crowdsourced detection methods—Crowd R-CNN (FRCNN-specific, requires clustering pre-processing) and WBF-EARL (requires a priori annotator proficiency levels)—by being model-agnostic and automatically inferring annotator quality from the data. The one-to-many matching strategy draws inspiration from DETR's set prediction loss but uses a simpler heuristic rather than the Hungarian algorithm, positioning BDC as a lightweight, plug-and-play alternative to existing annotation aggregation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most object detectors assume clean annotations, yet real-world crowdsourcing inevitably produces noisy, disagreeing labels, and prior detection-specific methods were either architecture-locked or required unavailable annotator metadata. The introduction of four synthetic datasets (VOC-FULL, VOC-MIX, COCO-FULL, COCO-MIX) with controlled annotator skill distributions enables the first systematic, apples-to-apples comparison of crowdsourced detection aggregation methods, which was previously impossible due to incompatible synthetic setups. Limitations include reliance on simulated annotators (deep-learning models mimicking human error) rather than genuine human noise, and real-world quantitative validation is restricted to VinDr-CXR; the disaster response dataset yields only qualitative results. Open questions include whether the Bayesian priors generalize to domains with highly structured annotation errors (e.g., systematic bias in medical imaging) and how the framework behaves when annotator skill is non-stationary over time.
