## Problem

Despite the proliferation of spatio-temporal prediction (STP) methods, no standardized framework exists for holistic, fair comparison across diverse disciplines. Prior evaluations relied on limited datasets, inconsistent input-output settings between methods on the same dataset, and narrow metric suites, making cross-method and cross-domain comparisons unreliable and incomplete.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241551/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmarked model receives a historical spatio-temporal sequence of L_in frames (ranging from 2 to 13 depending on the dataset) and produces L_s predicted future frames (1–14 frames). Input modalities span single-channel 64×64 grayscale (Moving-MNIST), 3-channel 128×128 RGB video (CityScapes, KTH), 3-channel 120×160 robot video (RoboNet, BridgeData), 8-channel 128×112 traffic grids (Traffic4Cast2021), and 69-channel 128×256 atmospheric fields (WeatherBench). For long-term prediction, models iteratively feed their own outputs back as inputs to extrapolate up to L_l frames (e.g., 30 frames on BridgeData/nuScenes, 20 frames on WeatherBench).

## Method

PredBench is a benchmark framework rather than a new architecture; its core contribution is a standardized experimental protocol that fixes input/output lengths, train/val/test splits, and hyper-parameter tuning for 12 STP methods (7 recurrent: ConvLSTM, E3D-LSTM, MAU, PhyDNet, PredRNNv1/++/v2; 5 non-recurrent: SimVPv1/v2, TAU, Earthformer, MCVD) across 15 datasets in 5 domains. Evaluation proceeds along four dimensions: (1) short-term prediction on 14 datasets using domain-tailored metrics (SSIM/PSNR/LPIPS/FVD for visual tasks, MAE/RMSE/WMAPE for traffic, WRMSE/ACC/CSI for weather); (2) long-term prediction via autoregressive extrapolation beyond the trained output horizon; (3) generalization across unseen scenes, tasks, and cities (e.g., KITTI→Caltech, 9-city→Moscow traffic); and (4) temporal resolution robustness by evaluating at 2× and 3× the training frame interval. The framework fills 180+ previously unreported method–dataset–dimension combinations through large-scale experiments, enabling the first systematic comparison of STP architectures under uniform conditions.

## Results

Across 15 datasets and 5 application domains, no single method dominates all tasks. PredRNN++ achieves the best SSIM/PSNR on motion trajectory prediction (e.g., 0.9504 SSIM, 23.62 PSNR on Moving-MNIST) and BAIR, while MCVD leads on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.0433, FVD 3 on Moving-MNIST; FVD 107 on nuScenes). TAU is strongest on BridgeData (0.8671 SSIM, 22.79 PSNR), and Earthformer excels on weather forecasting (WRMSE 1.5875, ACC 0.9901 on WeatherBench t2m; CSI 0.4391 on SEVIR). For long-term extrapolation and temporal robustness, non-recurrent methods (Earthformer, SimVP, TAU) outperform recurrent ones, and MCVD shows the most stable performance under varying frame intervals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 12 methods (7 recurrent, 5 non-recurrent) across 15 datasets, revealing that no single architecture wins universally. PredRNN++ (ICML 2018) remains a strong baseline for short-term prediction on motion trajectory and BAIR, achieving top SSIM/PSNR, yet underperforms on KITTI (0.5184 SSIM) and driving scenes overall. MCVD (NeurIPS 2022) consistently leads on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FVD) across motion, robot, and driving domains—e.g., FVD 107 on nuScenes vs. 712 for SimVPv2—but lags on pixel-level metrics (SSIM 0.7197 on nuScenes). TAU (CVPR 2023) dominates BridgeData short-term prediction (0.8671 SSIM) and shows the best temporal robustness on BridgeData/nuScenes, while Earthformer (NeurIPS 2022) clearly outperforms all CNN/RNN baselines on weather tasks (WRMSE 1.5875 on WeatherBench t2m vs. 1.8240 for TAU) and generalization on Traffic4Cast2021 (MAE 4.372 vs. 5.189 for PhyDNet). Notably, recurrent methods (ConvLSTM, E3D-LSTM, MAU) degrade more severely under long-term extrapolation and temporal resolution changes than non-recurrent counterparts, and models trained on larger datasets (nuScenes) generalize better to unseen scenes (Caltech) than those trained on smaller ones (KITTI).

## Relation to Prior Work

PredBench extends the prior STP benchmark OpenSTL [36], which was limited to small-scale datasets and lacked comprehensive cross-domain analysis, by scaling to 12 methods across 15 datasets spanning five application domains (motion trajectory, robot action, driving, traffic, weather). Conceptually, it unifies the recurrent lineage (ConvLSTM → PredRNN family, E3D-LSTM, MAU, PhyDNet) and the non-recurrent lineage (SimVP, TAU, Earthformer, MCVD) under a single standardized experimental protocol, addressing the historical problem of inconsistent input/output settings that made cross-method comparisons unreliable. The multi-dimensional evaluation framework—particularly the long-term extrapolation and temporal robustness dimensions—represents a departure from prior benchmarks that assessed only short-term accuracy on a handful of metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PredBench provides the most comprehensive and standardized evaluation platform for STP networks to date, and its findings challenge widely-used evaluation practices: the paper demonstrates that the KITTI→Caltech generalization protocol is unreasonable because Caltech is too simplistic, and that performance on small datasets like MovingMNIST does not reliably predict performance on real-world datasets. The introduction of temporal robustness as an evaluation dimension opens a new research direction, with the finding that no model consistently handles frame-interval changes well suggesting concrete future work (dynamic frame-interval training, temporal modules). Open questions include whether transformer architectures will continue to dominate weather forecasting, how to bridge the short-term/long-term performance gap, and whether the benchmark's standardized settings can be extended to emerging generative approaches beyond MCVD.
