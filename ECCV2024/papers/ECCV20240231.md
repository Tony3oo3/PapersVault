## Problem

3D vision-language grounding suffers from two critical gaps: the scarcity of paired 3D scene-language data (existing datasets contain only thousands of scenes, orders of magnitude below 2D-VL corpora) and the absence of a unified pre-training framework that can distill knowledge from grounded 3D data without task-specific auxiliary losses. This paper addresses both by constructing the first million-scale 3D-VL dataset and demonstrating that data scaling enables a simple contrastive pre-training paradigm to achieve state-of-the-art grounding performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240231/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D point clouds (N×8 tensors with coordinates, RGB, instance ID, and semantic label) decomposed into object instances via an off-the-shelf 3D segmentation model, paired with text descriptions at three granularities: object captions (detailed visual/physical properties), object referrals (spatial relationship descriptions), and scene captions (global scene summaries). Outputs include per-object grounding scores, scene-level alignment scores, and referred-object identification; when fine-tuned, the model produces bounding box predictions (evaluated at IoU > 0.5) or exact-match answers for 3D QA.

## Method

GPS (Grounded Pre-training for Scenes) is a transformer-based model trained with multi-level contrastive alignment at three granularities: (1) object-level, where object point cloud features are aligned with object captions via symmetric CLIP-style contrastive loss; (2) scene-level, where object features are enriched with spatial locations through a spatial transformer, then max-pooled and aligned with scene captions; and (3) referral-object-level, where a self-attention reasoning transformer aligns referral text with the specific referred object using intra-scene contrast (positives/negatives drawn from objects within the same scene). The total loss combines all three contrastive objectives with a masked language modeling loss over referral texts. The key insight is that scaling to 2.5M scene-language pairs (from 68K scenes) allows this simple unified framework—requiring only a projection MLP at fine-tuning time—to outperform architectures with complex task-specific losses, and that the scaling benefit generalizes beyond GPS to other 3D models like RegionPLC for semantic segmentation.

## Results

On 3D visual grounding, GPS (fine-tuned) achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks: 64.9% on Nr3D, 77.5% on Sr3D, and 48.1% Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, surpassing the previous best (Vi3LDRel at 64.4% on Nr3D, 3D-VisTA at 76.4% on Sr3D, and 45.8% on ScanRefer). Notably, even without dataset-specific fine-tuning, GPS (pre-train) already reaches 47.1% on ScanRefer, exceeding all prior methods. On 3D question answering, GPS achieves 22.7 EM@1 on ScanQA val and 49.9 on SQA3D, outperforming 3D-VisTA (22.4 and 48.5 respectively). Pre-training RegionPLC on SCENEVERSE further improves open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation by +2.3% mIoU (SPUNet32 backbone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPS (fine-tuned) outperforms all 20 baselines on the three primary grounding benchmarks: it edges out Vi3LDRel (64.4%) by 0.5 points on Nr3D, surpasses 3D-VisTA (76.4%) by 1.1 points on Sr3D, and leads 3D-VisTA (45.8%) by 2.3 points on ScanRefer Acc@0.5. The pre-trained GPS model (without fine-tuning) already exceeds 3D-VisTA on ScanRefer (47.1 vs. 45.8), demonstrating that the SCENEVERSE data scale alone provides a strong initialization. On 3D question answering, GPS achieves 49.9 EM@1 on SQA3D, beating 3D-VisTA (48.5) and SQA3D's own baseline (47.2), while on ScanQA val it reaches 22.7 vs. 3D-VisTA's 22.4. For open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation, pre-training RegionPLC on SCENEVERSE yields consistent gains of +1.7 to +2.3 mIoU over the non-pre-trained version, confirming the dataset's utility beyond the GPS architecture. In zero-shot transfer, GPS consistently outperforms 3D-VisTA across Nr3D, Sr3D, ScanRefer, and the held-out SCENEVERSE-val, with the largest gap on SCENEVERSE-val (60.6 vs. 58.1 overall in the zero-shot text setting).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-VL data-scaling paradigm (CLIP, LAION-5B) to the 3D domain by constructing SCENEVERSE, a million-scale 3D scene-language dataset, and training a unified contrastive pre-training model (GPS) inspired by CLIP's symmetric contrastive loss and ULIP's object-level cross-modal alignment. For scene-level encoding, GPS adopts the spatial transformer formulation from 3D-ViSTA and Vi3LDRel to incorporate object spatial locations into features. The key departure from prior 3D-VL grounding work (3DVG-Transformer, BUTD-DETR, EDA, etc.) is the rejection of task-specific architectures and auxiliary losses in favor of a simple multi-level contrastive pre-training objective, with the hypothesis that data scale alone can drive strong grounding performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCENEVERSE (68K scenes, 2.5M pairs) is the first million-scale 3D-VL dataset and will likely become a standard pre-training resource for the 3D-VL community, much as LAION-5B did for 2D. The demonstration that a simple contrastive model pre-trained on this data outperforms complex task-specific architectures—and generalizes zero-shot to unseen scenes—suggests that the field's focus on elaborate model designs may be less important than data scale. A notable limitation is the significant real-to-synthetic domain gap (models trained on synthetic scenes score 4–7% on real test sets), indicating that the rate-determining bottleneck for further scaling is collecting diverse, photorealistic 3D scenes rather than generating more language. Open questions include whether incorporating richer language types (QA pairs, dialogues, multi-turn instructions) and whether the scaling trend continues beyond the current 2.5M pairs.
