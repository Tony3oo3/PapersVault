## Problem

Existing face obfuscation methods either retain facial appearance recognizable by humans (failing human indecipherability) or only preserve identity for machines while discarding other facial attributes. No prior method simultaneously encrypts both identity and facial attribute information so that machines can still decode them while humans cannot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241835/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB facial image (aligned to 112×112 for face verification tasks via MTCNN landmarks) and produces an obfuscated facial image of the same resolution. It operates entirely at inference time with no training; the facial region is partitioned into a 7×7 grid of blocks, and all transformations are applied blockwise. Two variants exist: Forbes-G uses five frozen pre-trained networks (AdaFace, GOL, SphereNet, MobileNetV2, ResNet-50) in parallel as the feature extractor, while Forbes-T uses a single task-specific network.

## Method

Forbes composes eight parameterized obfuscating transformations across four categories (averaging, warping, noising, scaling) into a single composite transformation, superposing outputs within each category via softmax-normalized blending parameters. It then optimizes all free parameters by minimizing a four-term energy function: two HI terms (E_U for uniform parameters, E_C for color scaling parameters) that push parameters toward their obfuscating extremes, and two MD terms (E_D for L2 feature distance, E_S for 1-minus-cosine-similarity) that keep the transformed image's feature vector close to the original. The MD energy derivatives are computed through the frozen feature extractor using the Backpropagation Refinement Scheme (BRS), and the full energy is minimized with L-BFGS. Fixed parameters (blending weights for averaging and checkerboard amplitude) are held at 1 to guarantee strong low-pass filtering, while uniform, color, and composing parameters are randomly initialized and refined.

## Results

Forbes-T achieves the best human indecipherability (ACR = 0.2422, near the random-guess floor of 0.2) while retaining strong machine decipherability: 98.72% face verification accuracy on LFW, 90.89% on IJB-C, 59.03% age estimation on Adience, 81.26% gender classification, and 76.20% facial attribute classification on CelebAMask-HQ. The general-purpose variant Forbes-G also outperforms all baselines on IJB-C verification (89.47%), gender (78.64%), and attributes (73.18%), with an ACR of 0.2578. Compared to the unobfuscated originals (99.82% LFW, 94.64% IJB-C, 65.24% age, 85.65% gender, 88.02% attribute), Forbes-T degrades machine accuracy only marginally on identity tasks while making faces effectively unrecognizable to humans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Forbes-T dominates all baselines on every metric simultaneously. On human indecipherability (ACR), Forbes-T scores 0.2422 versus 0.6844 for FALCO and 0.7800–0.8978 for the four PRO-Face variants, meaning humans can identify the correct identity in only ~24% of trials (near the 20% random-guess baseline) compared to 68–90% for prior methods. On machine decipherability, Forbes-T achieves 98.72% LFW verification accuracy, surpassing the closest PRO-Face (Blur) at 98.10% and FALCO at 78.68%; on IJB-C it reaches 90.89% versus 87.63% for PRO-Face (Blur). For attribute-preserving tasks, Forbes-T leads on age estimation (59.03% vs. 57.01% for PRO-Face Blur), gender (81.26% vs. 75.68% for PRO-Face FaceShifter), and facial attributes (76.20% vs. 75.47% for PRO-Face SimSwap). The key distinction is that PRO-Face variants retain high face-verification accuracy but fail on HI (ACR 0.78–0.90), while FALCO, an anonymization method, sacrifices identity entirely; Forbes is the only method that achieves both low ACR and high machine accuracy across all five tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Forbes repurposes the Backpropagation Refinement Scheme (BRS) from Jang & Kim [14], originally designed for interactive image segmentation, to optimize parameters of deterministic obfuscating transformations rather than a learned interaction map. It departs from the dominant generative-model paradigm in face anonymization (FALCO, CLEANIR, DeepPrivacy) by using fixed, interpretable transformations (mosaicking, warping, noise, color scaling) whose parameters are refined via gradient descent, avoiding the need for training a dedicated obfuscation network. Compared to prior obfuscation methods (PRO-Face, Li et al.), Forbes extends the preserved information from identity alone to include facial attributes (age, gender, 40 CelebA attributes), while simultaneously achieving substantially better human indecipherability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Forbes offers a training-free, inference-time obfuscation pipeline that practitioners can deploy without retraining any model, which is practically attractive for privacy-preserving data sharing. However, its machine decipherability is tied to specific pre-trained feature extractors (AdaFace, GOL, SphereNet, etc.), raising open questions about robustness if those models are updated or if an adversary uses a different network. The 7×7 block size represents a fixed HI/MD tradeoff that may not generalize across image resolutions or face sizes, and the method has not been evaluated on video or on adversarial robustness of the obfuscated output.
