## Problem

Weakly supervised semantic segmentation methods rely on class activation maps (CAMs) for initial localization, but CAMs often fail to activate complete object regions due to the limited supervision from image-level labels. Existing text-guided WSSS approaches (e.g., CLIMS, CLIP-ES) improve upon this by aligning visual and textual features, but they depend on implicit alignment over aggregated global features, neglecting spatial details, and typically require predefined background categories or external models to extract background text representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241846/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and image-level class labels only (no pixel-level annotations), along with simple text prompts of the form 'a photo of [CLS]' and 'a photo of background' fed into a frozen CLIP text encoder to produce foreground and background text embeddings. The ViT-B visual encoder processes images split into N×N patches, and the network produces both class activation maps (used as pseudo-labels) and a final pixel-level semantic segmentation mask via a segmentation head refined by a pixel-adaptive refinement (PAR) module. The entire pipeline is single-stage, sharing the ViT encoder between the classification and segmentation branches.

## Method

DALNet introduces a dual-level alignment strategy between ViT visual features and CLIP text embeddings in a unified vision-language space. Global Implicit Alignment (GIA) performs cross-contrastive learning between the class token and foreground/background text embeddings to capture global semantics, while Local Explicit Alignment (LEA) uses an object-aware mask (derived from intermediate ViT block features) to split patch tokens into foreground and background representations, then applies InfoNCE-based cross-contrastive learning between these spatially-aware tokens and text embeddings. The key distinction is a cross-contrastive learning scheme that simultaneously treats foreground visual features paired with target-class text as positives and background text as negatives, while also pairing background visual features with background text as positives and target-class text as negatives—thereby activating missing object regions and suppressing over-activation in irrelevant areas without requiring predefined background categories. The overall training combines classification loss, intermediate CAM loss, GIA loss, LEA loss, and a patch-token contrast loss to mitigate ViT over-smoothing, all in a single end-to-end stage.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, DALNet with ImageNet-21k pre-training (ViT-B†) achieves 74.5% mIoU on validation and 74.9% on test, surpassing the best single-stage method ToCo (71.1%/72.2%) by over 3 points. Without ImageNet-21k pre-training, the method still reaches 71.4%/71.4%, outperforming all other single-stage baselines. On MS COCO 2014 validation, DALNet† achieves 44.4% mIoU, exceeding ToCo (42.3%) and all other single-stage methods, while remaining competitive with multi-stage approaches that use additional saliency supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a single-stage method, DALNet† (74.5%/74.9%) decisively outperforms the strongest single-stage baseline ToCo (71.1%/72.2%) by 3.4 and 2.7 points on PASCAL VOC val/test, and also beats ViT-PCM (70.3%/70.9%) and SLRNet (67.2%/67.6%). Notably, DALNet† surpasses even multi-stage methods that rely on additional saliency map supervision, such as Du et al. (72.6%/73.6%) and RCA (72.2%/72.8%), using only image-level labels and text. Among methods sharing the I+L supervision setting, DALNet† exceeds MMCST (72.2%/72.2%) by 2.3 points and CLIP-ES (71.1%/71.4%) by 3.4 points on validation. On MS COCO, DALNet† (44.4%) outperforms ToCo (42.3%) by 2.1 points and MCTformer (42.0%) by 2.4 points, while remaining competitive with multi-stage methods like RIB (43.8%) that use saliency supervision. The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that combining GIA and LEA with cross-contrastive learning yields the best CAM quality (70.2% vs. 64.1% for GIA alone or 65.1% for LEA alone).

## Relation to Prior Work

DALNet extends the line of language-guided WSSS methods (CLIMS, CLIP-ES, MMCST) that use CLIP text embeddings to improve CAM quality, but departs from their reliance on implicit, single-granularity alignment by introducing a dual-level strategy: Global Implicit Alignment on class tokens and Local Explicit Alignment on patch tokens. It builds directly on AFA's pixel-adaptive refinement module and ToCo's patch-token contrast loss for the single-stage training pipeline, while its cross-contrastive learning objective generalizes prior contrastive WSSS work (C2AM, RCA) by explicitly pairing both foreground and background visual features with corresponding text embeddings as positive/negative pairs. The key conceptual departure is treating background as a first-class alignment target using only the generic 'background' prompt, rather than relying on predefined background category sets or external models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 74.9 mIoU on PASCAL VOC test as a single-stage method, DALNet narrows the gap to the best multi-stage approaches (73.6 mIoU) while offering a simpler, end-to-end training pipeline that avoids multi-stage pseudo-label refinement. The practical advantage of requiring only image-level class labels plus a single 'background' text prompt (no predefined background taxonomy) makes the approach broadly applicable without additional annotation or model dependencies. Open questions include whether the dual-level alignment strategy generalizes to open-vocabulary or referring segmentation settings, and whether the frozen CLIP text encoder limits performance on fine-grained or out-of-distribution categories. The reliance on an intermediate-layer object-aware mask for foreground/background separation also introduces a potential failure mode when the initial CAM is poor.
