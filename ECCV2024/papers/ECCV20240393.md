## Problem

Existing video Implicit Neural Representations (INRs) are designed exclusively for compression, producing encoded weights that carry no semantic meaning and thus cannot support downstream tasks like retrieval, video question answering, or interpolation. This limits the practical advantage of INR-based codecs over traditional codecs like HEVC, since they offer no functional edge beyond compression. The paper asks how to formulate video INRs whose internal representations are simultaneously compressive and semantically useful.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240393/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video of N frames at resolution H×W×3 and, at inference, takes spatial coordinate queries (x, y) to decode individual frames at arbitrary resolutions (enabling super-resolution without re-encoding). The primary stored artifact is a dictionary of N per-frame latent vectors z_t ∈ R^D plus quantized hypernetwork and base-network parameters (further compressed via Huffman encoding). Beyond reconstruction, the latents serve directly as semantic embeddings for text-to-video retrieval (aligned to CLIP) or as visual tokens for a VideoLlama LLM, producing text responses without ever decoding pixels.

## Method

Latent-INR decouples spatial and temporal modeling by learning a dictionary of per-frame latents (auto-decoder, no encoder) that condition a shared base INR through a set of hypernetworks. Each hypernetwork h_l takes a latent z_t and outputs low-rank matrices P^l ∈ R^{N×r}, Q^l ∈ R^{M×r} (rank r ≪ N,M) whose product, after activation, modulates the l-th layer weights of the base network via element-wise multiplication, effectively selecting a frame-specific subnetwork. The base network is a 6-layer MLP (width 512, ReLU) over spatial coordinates with hash-grid positional encoding, optionally augmented with a convolutional up-sample block for patch-based decoding. Training is end-to-end with MSE reconstruction loss plus an optional cosine-similarity alignment loss to CLIP image embeddings (λ=0.01) or VideoLlama features, and the final compressed representation applies uniform quantization followed by Huffman coding. Video interpolation is achieved by linearly interpolating latents between adjacent frames and passing the result through the hypernetwork, a capability that other coordinate-based or weight-based INR methods lack.

## Results

On video interpolation (the task where INR methods most struggle), Latent-INR achieves 33.17 PSNR at scale α=2 on Big Buck Bunny, dramatically outperforming NVP (20.10), NIRVANA (19.14), and NeRV (15.92); even at α=8 it reaches 25.88 PSNR while baselines drop below 19. For compression on UVG, the method achieves PSNR comparable to NVP (current SOTA) at similar BPP ranges while enabling any-resolution inference. On retrieval, aligning latents with CLIP yields 34.40 R@1 for class-level retrieval on COIN (vs. 31.60 for CLIP directly) and 23.24 R@1 for segment-level on HowTo100m (vs. 21.13 for CLIP). Whole-video retrieval on MSR-VTT matches CLIP closely (30.20 vs. 30.10 R@1 T2V).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRV [9], NVP [21], and NIRVANA [26] on video interpolation, Latent-INR wins by a wide margin: 33.17 vs. 20.10/19.14/15.92 PSNR at α=2 on Bunny, and 35.13 vs. 19.33/18.19/16.91 on TaiChi — a gap of 13–17 dB that persists even at α=8. On compression (rate-distortion curves, Fig. 3), the method is comparable to NVP at similar BPP, so it neither clearly wins nor loses on the primary compression axis. Against CLIP [33] as a retrieval baseline, Latent-INR's aligned latents match or slightly exceed CLIP: 34.40 vs. 31.60 R@1 on COIN class-level, 23.24 vs. 21.13 R@1 on HowTo100m segment-level, and 30.20 vs. 30.10 R@1 on MSR-VTT whole-video T2V. Against HEVC [47] on decoding speed, Latent-INR is competitive or faster at multiple resolutions, with the key advantage that a single model handles all resolutions whereas HEVC requires separate encodes per resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the video INR compression line (NeRV, NVP, NIRVANA) and the hypernetwork/auto-decoder paradigm (Trans-INR, IP-Composer, INR-V), but departs from them by explicitly decoupling spatial and temporal modeling: instead of a single network per video, it learns a dictionary of per-frame latents that condition shared hypernetworks via low-rank modulation. The key conceptual departure is treating the latent as a semantic proxy for the frame-specific INR weights, which prior video INRs never exploited. By adding an explicit alignment loss to CLIP or VideoLlama features, the paper repurposes the compression-trained latent space for retrieval and chat—something no prior video INR framework attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first video INR framework that simultaneously supports compression, interpolation, any-resolution decoding, and semantic downstream tasks (retrieval, VQA) without requiring a separate model, which could reduce the computational overhead of decoding before analysis. However, encoding time and storage remain well behind HEVC, and the chat/retrieval evaluations are qualitative or on small-scale benchmarks, so practical adoption is still distant. Open questions include whether the latent-semantic alignment scales to hours-long videos, whether more powerful VLMs (beyond VideoLlama) can be aligned without degrading reconstruction, and whether the low-rank modulation rank can be further reduced without quality loss.
