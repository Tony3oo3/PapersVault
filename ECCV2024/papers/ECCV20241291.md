## Problem

Existing segmentation models face a fundamental trade-off: SAM produces multi-granularity masks from a single click but provides no semantic labels, while object-level datasets (COCO, Objects365) offer rich semantics but only at a single granularity. No prior work has jointly trained a unified model on class-agnostic multi-granularity data (SA-1B) and semantic segmentation datasets, leaving both granularity-controllability and semantic-awareness unachieved in a single architecture.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 1024×1024 RGB image plus either a point click or a bounding box prompt. A point click is converted into a small anchor box and then duplicated into K=6 queries, each carrying a distinct learnable granularity-level embedding; a box prompt uses a single content embedding (K=1). The output is a set of up to 6 segmentation masks spanning different granularities (from fine parts to the whole object), each paired with both an object-level and a part-level semantic label predicted via a shared text encoder. For non-interactive use, the model also supports 300 latent queries for generic panoptic segmentation.

## Method

Built on MaskDINO's deformable transformer decoder with a Swin-T/L vision backbone, Semantic-SAM introduces three key mechanisms. First, multi-choice learning: each click generates K=6 queries whose content embeddings encode different granularity levels, and a many-to-many Hungarian matching scheme during training allows all six predictions to simultaneously match different ground-truth masks at different granularities (unlike SAM's many-to-one matching). Second, decoupled object and part classification: a shared UniCL text encoder produces separate embedding sets for object and part labels, with the loss function adapted per data type (e.g., SAM data omits classification loss, object-level data omits part loss). Third, multi-task training across seven datasets spanning three granularity types (class-agnostic SA-1B, object-level COCO/Objects365/ADE20K, and part-level Pascal Part/PACO/PartImageNet), where the part-level data bridges semantic transfer between object and part concepts while SA-1B bridges granularity coverage.

## Results

On COCO val2017 panoptic segmentation, Semantic-SAM(L) trained on COCO + SA-1B achieves a new SOTA of 60.2 PQ and 68.9 mIoU, surpassing OneFormer (60.0 PQ) and OpenSeed-L (59.5 PQ). Adding SA-1B data to COCO-only training yields +2.2 box AP and +1.3 mask AP, with the largest gains on small objects (+1.7 APs box). For interactive segmentation, Semantic-SAM(L) outperforms SAM(L) by +1.3 IoU under Point(Max) (57.0 vs 55.7) and +3.7 IoU under Point(Oracle) (74.2 vs 70.5) on COCO, and achieves 89.0 1-IoU@All Granularity on SA-1B versus SAM(H)'s 83.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against panoptic segmentation baselines, Semantic-SAM(L) achieves the highest PQ (60.2) and mIoU (68.9) on COCO val2017, narrowly beating OneFormer-H (60.0 PQ, 68.8 mIoU) and OpenSeed-L (59.5 PQ, 68.6 mIoU), while substantially outperforming Mask2Former-T (53.2 PQ) and X-Decoder-T (52.6 PQ). Notably, Semantic-SAM uses only COCO + SA-1B (1/10) for training, whereas OpenSeed and OneFormer use COCO + Objects365, suggesting SA-1B's multi-granularity masks are a more effective training signal than additional object-level data. On part segmentation (Pascal Part), Semantic-SAM(T) achieves 30.5 mask AP, surpassing VLPart(B)'s 27.4, though it trails VLPart on box AP (27.0 vs 29.2); adding SA-1B data improves both to 28.0/31.4. For interactive segmentation, Semantic-SAM(L) beats SAM(L) by +1.3 IoU (Point Max) and +3.7 IoU (Point Oracle) on COCO, and outperforms SAM(H) by +5.5 IoU on multi-granularity 1-IoU@All Granularity (89.0 vs 83.5) on SA-1B. On box-based interactive segmentation, Semantic-SAM(T) achieves 76.1 Box 1-IoU, exceeding SEEM(T) (73.7) and SAM(B) (50.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

Semantic-SAM builds directly on MaskDINO's query-based deformable decoder architecture and SAM's interactive segmentation paradigm, but departs from both in key ways. Unlike SAM, which uses many-to-one matching and produces at most three masks per click without semantic labels, Semantic-SAM introduces many-to-many Hungarian matching with six granularity-level content embeddings per click, enabling multi-granularity mask prediction. Unlike MaskDINO and other generic segmentation models that follow a single-input-single-output pipeline, Semantic-SAM incorporates spatial prompts (points and boxes) and a decoupled object/part classification head to handle heterogeneous training data. The paper positions itself as the first to jointly train on SA-1B (class-agnostic multi-granularity masks), instance-level datasets (COCO, Objects365, ADE20K), and part-level datasets (Pascal Part, PACO, PartImageNet) within a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that class-agnostic interactive segmentation data (SA-1B) transfers positively to semantic tasks, yielding +2.2 box AP and +1.3 mask AP on COCO panoptic segmentation without any additional labeled data. This suggests a path toward leveraging the massive SA-1B dataset to improve downstream semantic and part segmentation, reducing annotation costs. However, the evaluation is limited to COCO and Pascal Part, the six-level granularity is somewhat arbitrary, and the open-vocabulary capability relies on UniCL's fixed vocabulary rather than truly arbitrary text queries. Open questions include whether the multi-granularity framework scales beyond six levels, whether the decoupled classification generalizes to novel part concepts unseen during training, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation.
