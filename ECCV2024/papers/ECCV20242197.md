## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction datasets are limited in data cardinality, interaction scenario diversity (grasp types, object categories, hand identities), or annotation quality. No real dataset covers the full 33-grasp taxonomy with explicit grasp labels and wide intra-class variations: HO3D and DexYCB capture only 45% and 42% of grasp types, while YCB Affordance covers all but is entirely synthetic. This gap hinders learning of universal shape priors and foundation models for 3D hand-object interaction.

## Input / Output

For annotation, the pipeline consumes 4 temporally synchronized RGB-D views (1920×1080, 30 FPS, downsampled to 10 FPS) plus 8 IR cameras tracking 3–5 optical markers per object at 120 FPS; no markers are placed on hands. For benchmarking, a single RGB image is fed to HFL-Net to jointly regress a MANO hand mesh and 6D object pose, while grasp classification takes a hand mesh augmented with per-vertex contact values (10-bin discretization) as input. The dataset outputs 1.5M annotated frames with 21-joint 3D keypoints, MANO and HALO hand meshes, pre-scanned object meshes with textures, binary contact maps, and one of 28 grasp class labels per frame.

## Method

The annotation pipeline combines a novel bootstrapping procedure for robust 3D keypoint lifting (filtering outlier 2.5D estimates by cross-view MPJPE consistency) with a three-stage gradual MANO fitting scheme (global transform → partial wrist-extended pose → full hand+object pose) optimized via a multi-term loss including 2D reprojection, 3D marker alignment, segmentation/depth rendering, temporal regularization, and physics-based penetration/contact penalties. A key differentiator is the supplementary HALO implicit-surface annotation: lifted 3D keypoints are refined to the nearest valid MANO kinematic configuration via inverse kinematics, then fed to HALO's occupancy network, which requires no loss-weighting hyperparameters and yields resolution-independent hand shapes at comparable IoU (0.719 vs. 0.739 for MANO). For benchmarking, a modified CoMA convolutional mesh autoencoder with auxiliary contact-reconstruction and grasp-classification losses achieves 0.88 accuracy on 28-class grasp taxonomy, while HFL-Net is evaluated under five split protocols (unseen subjects, views, objects, taxonomies) to stress-test generalization across the interaction space.

## Results

On the HOGraspNet S0 split, HFL-Net achieves a mean PA-MPJPE of 5.67 mm for hand pose estimation and an average ADD-0.1D of 63.61 for 6D object pose estimation across 30 object classes. For grasp classification, the CoMA-based autoencoder reaches 0.88 accuracy on 28 grasp taxonomy classes and 0.95 F1 on contact map reconstruction. In the cross-benchmark experiment (Table 4), HFL-Net trained on HOGraspNet outperforms the same network trained on HO3D on the DexYCB test set, reducing MPJPE from 57.31 mm to 42.65 mm and PA-MPJPE from 10.31 mm to 9.36 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper uses HFL-Net [48] (CVPR 2023) as the state-of-the-art hand-object reconstruction baseline to benchmark the new dataset. HFL-Net achieves a mean PA-MPJPE of 5.67 mm on HOGraspNet, comparable to SOTA hand pose results on other widely-used hand-object datasets, validating annotation quality. The key comparison is cross-benchmark: HFL-Net trained on HOGraspNet reduces MPJPE by 14.66 mm (57.31 → 42.65) and PA-MPJPE by 0.95 mm (10.31 → 9.36) over the same network trained on HO3D [28] when evaluated on DexYCB, demonstrating that the broader interaction coverage of HOGraspNet transfers better to unseen objects. Per-object analysis (Table 3) reveals that object pose estimation (ADD-0.1D) varies widely (2.44 for dice to 99.42 for plate), with small, highly occluded objects like dice and foldable phones being the hardest cases. The authors note that three of the four highest-error grasp classes in hand pose estimation are absent from both DexYCB and HO3D, suggesting that HOGraspNet's newly captured real grasp poses present a genuine challenge for models trained on prior datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOGraspNet extends the line of hand-object interaction datasets (HO3D, DexYCB, OakInk, ARCTIC) by being the first real-capture dataset to cover the full grasp taxonomy of Feix et al. with explicit grasp labels, whereas prior datasets capture only 42–61% of the 33 grasp classes or lack grasp annotations entirely. For annotation, it builds on the MANO parametric model pipeline established by HO3D and DexYCB but introduces HALO (a skeleton-driven neural occupancy representation) as a complementary, lower-hyperparameter annotation path, and a bootstrapping procedure for robust multi-view 3D keypoint lifting. For benchmarking, it adopts HFL-Net (CVPR 2023) as the state-of-the-art reconstruction baseline and CoMA (ECCV 2018) as the backbone for grasp classification, positioning itself as a new evaluation ground rather than a new method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a concrete gap: no prior real dataset simultaneously provides full grasp-taxonomy coverage, 99 diverse hand identities, 1.5M multi-view RGB-D frames, and per-frame grasp labels, making it a natural training ground for universal shape priors or foundation models of hand-object interaction. The cross-benchmark result (HOGraspNet-trained HFL-Net outperforming HO3D-trained HFL-Net on DexYCB) and the observation that the highest-error grasp classes are absent from existing datasets suggest that current models are under-trained on a meaningful portion of the interaction space. Key limitations remain: articulated objects are treated as rigid, non-grasping actions (pushing, throwing, deforming) are excluded, and object textures/shapes beyond grasp areas are not synthetically augmented, leaving open whether the dataset's coverage generalizes to open-world manipulation.
