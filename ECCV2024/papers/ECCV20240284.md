## Problem

Existing unsupervised 3D object detection methods rely exclusively on LiDAR point clouds, whose inherent sparsity severely degrades detection of distant and small objects (e.g., pedestrians, cyclists beyond 30 m). Additionally, self-training pipelines suffer from a compounding bias toward easy, near-range, large objects, causing the model to progressively lose sensitivity to long-tail categories. No prior work has integrated 2D image cues to compensate for LiDAR sparsity in the unsupervised 3D detection setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds collected at multi-traversal locations (same scene visited ≥2 times, required for point-persistency scoring) together with synchronized RGB images from vehicle-mounted cameras. LiDAR points are projected into image space via calibrated intrinsics/extrinsics and matched against SAM-generated 2D masks to lift 2D detections into 3D frustums. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, yaw) evaluated with AP_BEV and AP_3D at IoU 0.25 across near (0–30 m), middle (30–50 m), and far (50–80 m) ranges on nuScenes and Lyft.

## Method

LiSe fuses two pseudo-label branches: a LiDAR branch that computes point-persistency scores via multi-traversal alignment, clusters points with a ppScore-aware DBSCAN variant, and fits 3D boxes; and an image branch that runs GroundingDINO for open-vocabulary 2D detection, refines masks with SAM, and lifts them to 3D via frustum construction and region growing. A distance-aware integration rule retains image-derived boxes only beyond 10 m to avoid conflicts with dense near-range LiDAR boxes. During self-paced training, an adaptive sampling strategy recomputes per-group sampling weights each round using a distance-volume metric (near/far × small/large), down-sampling groups the model already detects well and up-sampling neglected ones. Finally, weak model aggregation weight-averages the current round's detector with the previously aggregated model (coefficient λ ≈ 0.999, starting at round 6), merging complementary specializations across rounds into a single robust detector.

## Results

On nuScenes, LiSe (T=10) achieves 19.8% AP_BEV and 11.4% AP_3D over the full 0–80 m range, surpassing the state-of-the-art OYSTER (12.7% / 8.0%) by +7.1% and +3.4% respectively. On Lyft, LiSe reaches 65.6% AP_BEV and 62.5% AP_3D (0–80 m), outperforming MODEST (57.3% / 55.1%) by +8.3% and +7.4%. Notably, LiSe's AP_BEV in the long-range 50–80 m band on nuScenes (4.8) even exceeds the fully supervised upper bound (4.4), demonstrating that 2D scene integration can surpass label-driven performance for distant objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiSe is compared against two LiDAR-only unsupervised baselines: MODEST (You et al., CVPR 2022) and OYSTER (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023). On nuScenes, LiSe outperforms OYSTER by +7.1% AP_BEV and +3.4% AP_3D over the full range, and by a wider margin in the 30–50 m band (+7.0% AP_BEV). Against MODEST, the gap is even larger: +8.0% AP_BEV and +4.8% AP_3D (0–80 m). On Lyft, where OYSTER is not evaluated, LiSe surpasses MODEST by +8.3% AP_BEV and +7.4% AP_3D overall, with the most dramatic gain in the 50–80 m range (+19.6% AP_BEV, +18.9% AP_3D). A striking result is that LiSe's 50–80 m AP_BEV on nuScenes (4.8) exceeds the fully supervised model (4.4), indicating that 2D scene cues can compensate for LiDAR sparsity at long range beyond what labeled training achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LiDAR-only unsupervised 3D detection pipeline established by MODEST (multi-traversal pseudo-label generation) and OYSTER by integrating 2D scene understanding—specifically open-vocabulary detection (GroundingDINO) and segmentation (SAM)—to generate complementary 3D pseudo-boxes for distant and small objects. The self-paced learning framework draws on the classical curriculum idea of Kumar et al. but is tailored to 3D detection with distance- and volume-based object grouping, while the weak model aggregation mechanism is inspired by weight-averaging approaches (Tarvainen et al.) applied across iterative self-training rounds. Unlike prior image-LiDAR fusion work in closed-set detection, which fuses features or proposals at various pipeline stages, LiSe fuses at the pseudo-label level, making the 2D branch a seed generator rather than a runtime dependency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 2D texture and color information can meaningfully compensate for LiDAR sparsity in unsupervised 3D detection, with the notable result that unsupervised AP_BEV at 50–80 m on nuScenes (4.8) exceeds the fully supervised baseline (4.4). However, the approach is constrained by the multi-traversal requirement (same location visited multiple times) and depends on the quality of pretrained 2D detectors, limiting its applicability to single-pass or novel-scene settings. Open questions include whether the adaptive sampling and weak model aggregation strategies transfer to other sensor configurations (e.g., camera-only or radar), and whether the gains persist as 2D open-vocabulary detectors improve or degrade in distribution-shifted environments.
