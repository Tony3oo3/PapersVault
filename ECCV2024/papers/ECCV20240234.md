## Problem

Existing video object segmentation (VOS) datasets and methods are designed exclusively for conventional 2D planar images with limited field of view, leaving panoramic (360°×180°) videos entirely unaddressed. Panoramic videos introduce two domain-unique challenges—pixel-level content discontinuities where objects are split across the left and right image boundaries, and severe geometric distortions—that cause all 15 evaluated off-the-shelf VOS models to degrade substantially (by 10–38 J&F points). No dedicated dataset, benchmark, or architecture existed for this task.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a query frame x_t and a set of reference frames (first frame and previous frame for the Base variant; multiple historical frames at stride δ for the Large variant) from a 360°×180° panoramic video sampled at 6 fps, with resolutions up to 4K. The first-frame mask serves as the semi-supervised initialization. Output is a per-pixel instance segmentation mask y_t for the query frame, evaluated with standard J&F, J_s/F_s (seen categories), and J_u/F_u (unseen categories) metrics. The critical input property is that the leftmost and rightmost columns of each frame are spatially continuous in the underlying 3D scene, a relationship absent in planar video.

## Method

PSC-Former introduces a Panoramic Space Consistency (PSC) block that stacks self-attention, cross-attention (for reference-to-query matching), and a novel PSC-attention module. PSC-attention first applies a stitching operation that cyclically shifts a W/p-wide strip of the reference feature embedding from the right boundary to the left boundary, making the discontinuous seam locally contiguous. It then computes attention with a binary window mask R that restricts each query token to attend only to key tokens within a (2s+1)² spatial neighborhood, reducing complexity from O((HW)²) to O((2s+1)²) while preserving boundary-semantic correspondence. The full PSC block is repeated in a stack, followed by a decoder that produces the final mask. Two variants are provided: Ours-Base (R={1, t−1}) for speed and Ours-Large (R={1+2δ, 1+4δ, …}) for accuracy, with p=2 and s=7 as default hyperparameters.

## Results

On PanoVOS (retrained on PanoVOS train set), Ours-Large achieves J&F of 77.9 (validation) and 59.9 (test), outperforming the strongest baseline AOTB (67.6 / 55.4) by over 10 points on validation and 4.5 points on test. Ours-Base (using only first + previous frame as reference) reaches 74.0 / 56.8, still surpassing all baselines. On the domain-transfer setting (trained only on YouTube-VOS + COCO), XMem* achieves the best J&F of 63.4 (Val) / 61.0 (Test), but all 15 off-the-shelf models suffer 10–38 point drops relative to their YouTube-VOS scores, confirming the domain gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 15 off-the-shelf VOS baselines are outperformed by both Ours-Base and Ours-Large on every metric (J&F, Js, Fs, Ju, Fu) across both validation and test splits. Among matching-based methods retrained on PanoVOS, AOTB (67.6 Val / 55.4 Test J&F) and AOTL (66.6 / 53.8) are the strongest, yet Ours-Large exceeds them by 10.3 and 11.3 points on validation J&F respectively. XMem (55.7 / 53.5) and STCN (52.0 / 50.8) lag further behind, while AFB-URR (34.3 / 34.2) and CFBI variants (35.8–41.3) perform worst, indicating that propagation-based and early matching-based architectures struggle most with panoramic discontinuities. In the domain-transfer setting (Table 4), XMem* trained on YouTube-VOS + BL30K achieves the best transfer J&F of 63.4 (Val) / 61.0 (Test), but still trails Ours-Large by 14.5 points on validation. Foundation-model variants (PerSAM: 19.1 Val J&F; SAM-PT: 47.5 Val J&F) also fall well short of the proposed method, confirming that zero-shot or lightly-adapted SAM variants are not yet competitive for panoramic VOS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established video object segmentation (VOS) task—formulated by DAVIS and YouTube-VOS—to the panoramic (360°×180°) video setting, where equirectangular projection introduces pixel-level content discontinuities at the left-right boundaries and severe geometric distortions. Methodologically, PSC-Former builds on the transformer-based matching paradigm of AOT and XMem (cross-attention for reference-query correspondence) but introduces a windowed PSC-Attention that explicitly models the spatial continuity across the panoramic seam via a circular stitching operation. The paper also positions itself against prior panoramic datasets (SHD360, SOD360, Wild360), which target saliency detection rather than instance-level segmentation, and against visual foundation models (SAM, PerSAM, SAM-PT) whose zero-shot transfer to panoramic content is shown to be inadequate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic evaluation of 15 SOTA VOS models reveals a substantial and consistent domain gap (10–38 J&F point drops) between planar and panoramic videos, with all methods failing on content discontinuities—a finding that motivates dedicated panoramic VOS research. The PanoVOS dataset (150 videos, 19K instance masks, 20s average duration) is the first long-term, instance-level annotated panoramic VOS benchmark, opening the door to broader applications in VR/AR, autonomous driving, and embodied navigation. However, the acknowledged limitation that PSC-Former does not address severe distortion (no deformable convolution or geometric warping) leaves a significant open problem, and the relatively small dataset size (150 videos) limits generalization claims. The poor performance of SAM variants (19–47 J&F) also raises open questions about how to adapt large-scale foundation models to panoramic geometry.
