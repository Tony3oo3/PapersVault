## Problem

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) have been largely confined to simple image classification due to a significant performance gap with ANNs on complex tasks like object detection. Two specific obstacles limit SNN-based detection: (1) complex YOLO modules suffer spike degradation (deep layers stop firing) when naively converted to spiking versions, and (2) the binary quantization of membrane potentials into 0/1 spikes introduces large representation errors that are especially harmful for detection.

## Input / Output

The method accepts two input modalities unified into a tensor X ∈ R^{T×C×H×W}: static RGB images are repeated across T timesteps (direct input encoding), while neuromorphic event streams (x, y, t, p) from DVS sensors are aggregated into frames over a window of T×dt. The network outputs per-object bounding boxes containing confidence score, class label, center coordinates (x, y), and dimensions (w, h), using an anchor-free detection head inherited from YOLOv8.

## Method

SpikeYOLO simplifies YOLOv8 by replacing its complex feature-extraction modules (e.g., C2F) with meta SNN blocks: SNN-Block-1 uses standard convolutions (expansion ratio 4) for low-stage features, while SNN-Block-2 employs re-parameterization convolutions for high-stage features, both built on inverted residual structures with separable convolutions. The key neuron innovation is I-LIF (Integer Leaky Integrate-and-Fire), which replaces the Heaviside step function with a clipped rounding operation S[t] = Clip(round(U[t]), 0, D), emitting integer values 0–D during training to reduce quantization error. During inference, each integer value is decomposed into D binary spikes across extended virtual timesteps (T→T×D), so that all multiply-accumulate operations reduce to sparse additions, preserving the spike-driven energy efficiency of SNNs. The architecture retains YOLOv8's Backbone/Neck/Head macro structure and anchor-free head, while the micro design draws on Meta-SpikeFormer's token-mixer/channel-mixer block formulation.

## Results

On COCO 2017 val, SpikeYOLO (68.8M params, T×D=1×4) achieves 66.2% mAP@50 and 48.9% mAP@50:95, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art SNN (Meta-SpikeFormer YOLO, 45.0% mAP@50) by +15.0% and +18.7% respectively, while approaching YOLOv5 (64.1% mAP@50) at 3.3× lower energy cost. On the neuromorphic Gen1 dataset, SpikeYOLO (23.1M, T×D=4×2) reaches 67.2% mAP@50 and 40.4% mAP@50:95, outperforming the equivalent-architecture ANN (64.7% mAP@50) by +2.5% while delivering 5.7× energy efficiency. The 23.1M-parameter variant on COCO (T×D=1×4) achieves 62.3% mAP@50 and 45.5% mAP@50:95, already exceeding all prior directly-trained SNNs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeYOLO decisively outperforms all prior SNN baselines on COCO: it surpasses EMS-YOLO [50] (50.1% mAP@50) by +12.2% and Meta-SpikeFormer YOLO [58] (45.0% mAP@50) by +17.2% at the 23.1M scale, while using comparable or fewer parameters. Against ANN2SNN methods (Spiking-YOLO [29], Bayesian Optim [28], Spike Calib [32]), the gap is even larger, with SpikeYOLO exceeding the best ANN2SNN result (45.4% mAP@50) by +16.9%. On Gen1, SpikeYOLO (67.2% mAP@50) beats EMS-YOLO [50] (59.0%) by +8.2%, Tr-Spiking-YOLO [65] (45.3%) by +21.9%, and DenseNet121+SSD [6] (18.9% mAP@50:95) by a wide margin. Notably, SpikeYOLO also outperforms the equivalent-architecture ANN (64.7% mAP@50) by +2.5% on Gen1 while consuming only 12.9 mJ vs. 78.5 mJ (5.7× energy savings), and matches YOLOv5 [43] (64.1% mAP@50) on COCO at 3.3× lower power. The only comparison where SpikeYOLO trails is against the full YOLOv8 ANN (67.2% mAP@50, 50.2% mAP@50:95), but the SNN version achieves 95.5% of that accuracy at 2.2× lower energy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two main threads: the YOLOv8 single-stage detection architecture and the meta SNN block design from Meta-SpikeFormer (ICLR 2024). It departs from prior SNN object detection work (Spiking-YOLO, EMS-YOLO) by arguing that YOLO's complex modules (e.g., C2F) cause spike degradation when naively converted to spiking versions, and instead simplifies the architecture while incorporating inverted residual and re-parameterization convolution designs. The I-LIF neuron conceptually extends the ANN2SNN insight from Fast-SNN (quantized ANNs convert well to SNNs) into the direct-training regime, using integer-valued activations during training that are decomposed into binary spike sequences via virtual timestep extension at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper substantially narrows the SNN-ANN performance gap on object detection (66.2% mAP@50 vs. 67.2% for YOLOv8), making SNNs competitive for a task previously considered out of reach. The finding that SpikeYOLO outperforms its ANN counterpart by +2.5% mAP@50 with 5.7× energy efficiency on neuromorphic Gen1 data is practically significant for event-based vision systems. Open questions include whether the virtual timestep extension (T×D) is feasible on current neuromorphic hardware, whether I-LIF generalizes to other tasks beyond detection, and whether the architecture simplification principle (complex ANN modules are unsuitable for SNNs) holds more broadly.
