## Problem

Cross attention-based few-shot segmentation methods rely on pixel-by-pixel FG-FG matching between query and support features, but large receptive fields in deep backbone layers inevitably mingle foreground features with background features. This feature ambiguity causes query FG features to show reduced similarity to support FG features (because both are doped with different-class BG information), leading to ineffective support utilization. The paper is the first to explicitly identify and address this negative impact on cross attention matching.

## Input / Output

AENet is a plug-in inserted into existing cross attention-based FSS pipelines (CyCTR, SCCAN, HDMNet). It consumes mid-level features (from backbone blocks 2–3) for the ambiguity elimination step and high-level features (from block 4) for prior mask generation, alongside the binary support mask. The plug-in outputs refined query and support features that are forwarded to the original cross attention blocks, ultimately producing a binary query segmentation mask. No additional learnable parameters are introduced in the prior generator, and the only new supervision is an auxiliary BCE loss on the discriminative mask.

## Method

AENet comprises two modules. The Prior Generator (PG) computes FG and BG prior masks via cosine similarity between flattened query high-level features and GAP-pooled support FG/BG prototypes, then applies a clipped subtraction (ReLU) to obtain a discriminative prior mask that suppresses ambiguous regions similar to both support FG and BG. The Ambiguity Eliminator (AE) uses this discriminative mask to extract a query FG prototype via softmax-weighted aggregation of query value features, computes a cosine-similarity-based weight α to fuse the support FG prototype with the query FG prototype, and concatenates the fused prototype with the original query and support features for linear refinement. This increases the proportion of pure FG information in each pixel's mingled features, naturally enhancing FG-FG matching in the downstream cross attention blocks. The plug-in is inserted before each cross attention block in the baseline, with an auxiliary BCE loss on the discriminative mask providing class-agnostic guidance during training.

## Results

On PASCAL-5^i with ResNet50, AENet plugged into SCCAN achieves 69.8% mIoU (1-shot) and 74.1% (5-shot), improving over SCCAN alone by +3.0% and +3.8% respectively. The largest gains appear on CyCTR, where AENet boosts 1-shot mIoU from 64.2% to 69.0% (+4.8%) and 5-shot from 65.6% to 72.6% (+7.0%). On the more challenging COCO-20^i, improvements are even larger: CyCTR+AENet reaches 47.0% (1-shot) vs. 40.3% for CyCTR alone (+6.7%), and SCCAN+AENet reaches 49.4% vs. 46.3% (+3.1%). FB-IoU gains are consistent, e.g., SCCAN+AENet improves FB-IoU from 77.7% to 80.8% (1-shot, PASCAL-5^i).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AENet is a plug-in that consistently improves all three cross-attention baselines it is integrated into. The largest relative gains are on CyCTR: +4.8% mIoU (1-shot) and +7.0% (5-shot) on PASCAL-5^i, and +6.7%/+6.8% on COCO-20^i, suggesting CyCTR's cycle-consistent attention is most affected by feature ambiguity. SCCAN benefits by +3.0% (1-shot) and +3.8% (5-shot) on PASCAL-5^i, and +3.1%/+2.8% on COCO-20^i; notably, SCCAN+AENet (74.1% 5-shot) surpasses HDMNet (71.8%) by 2.3% on PASCAL-5^i. HDMNet, already the strongest baseline, sees smaller but still meaningful gains of +0.9% (1-shot) and +2.4% (5-shot) on PASCAL-5^i. On COCO-20^i, where small objects and complex backgrounds exacerbate feature ambiguity, AENet's improvements are uniformly larger than on PASCAL-5^i (e.g., +6.7% vs. +4.8% for CyCTR 1-shot), confirming the method's effectiveness in harder scenarios. Against non-plug-in baselines (PFENet, DACM, BAM, MIANet, ABCNet), SCCAN+AENet achieves the best 1-shot mIoU (69.8%) and 5-shot mIoU (74.1%) on PASCAL-5^i with ResNet50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-attention-based FSS line (SCCAN, CyCTR, HDMNet) by identifying a previously overlooked failure mode: deep backbone receptive fields cause FG features to be contaminated with BG information, degrading the FG-FG matching that cross attention relies on. Conceptually, it builds on PFENet's learning-agnostic prior masks but departs from them by introducing a BG-subtraction step to extract discriminative query regions, and further proposes an ambiguity eliminator (AE) that uses those regions to refine both query and support features before cross attention. The plug-in design explicitly positions AENet as an orthogonal add-on rather than a new architecture, distinguishing it from prior methods that redesign the entire matching pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of feature ambiguity as a systematic bottleneck in cross-attention FSS provides a clear diagnostic for why existing methods underutilize support information, and the plug-in design makes the fix broadly applicable without retraining baselines from scratch. The consistent 1–7% mIoU gains across three architectures and two datasets (with larger gains on the harder COCO-20^i) suggest the issue is real and not dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the same ambiguity problem affects other attention-based matching tasks (e.g., few-shot classification, retrieval), and whether the simple subtraction-based discriminative prior generalizes to settings with multiple support classes or open-vocabulary segmentation.
