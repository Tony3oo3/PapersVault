## Problem

Neural network weight matrices often exhibit large condition numbers, which propagates to the Hessian of the loss landscape and slows gradient-based convergence. Existing normalization techniques (batch normalization, weight standardization, weight normalization) stabilize activations or decouple magnitude from direction but do not directly reduce the condition number of the weight matrices themselves. This paper asks whether strategically preconditioning weight matrices can smooth the loss landscape and accelerate optimization without architectural changes.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in forward-pass modification on the weight matrices of standard architectures: for CNNs (Inception, DenseNet) it is applied to the first convolutional and final linear layers; for ViTs and NeRF MLPs it is applied to all linear layers. Inputs are standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 ImageNet-1k) or multi-view 2D images for NeRF; outputs are class labels or per-pixel radiance fields respectively. No changes to input resolution, batch size, or loss function are required.

## Method

Weight conditioning multiplies each weight matrix W_k by a diagonal equilibration preconditioner E_k whose entries are the inverse 2-norms of the rows of W_k, producing E_k W_k in the forward pass. The key theoretical result (Theorem 2) proves that this equilibration reduces the condition number of the loss Hessian at every full-rank point, leveraging the Van Der Sluis theorem that row equilibration is optimal among all diagonal preconditioners for minimizing κ. Because a lower Hessian condition number permits a larger stable learning rate (η < 2/σ_max), gradient descent converges faster along all eigendirections. The technique is parameter-free, adds no learnable variables, and composes additively with existing normalizations (BN for CNNs, LN for ViTs), yielding consistent accuracy gains across Inception, DenseNet, ViT-Base, and NeRF architectures.

## Results

On DenseNet trained on CIFAR-10, BN+E achieves 79.96% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming BN (70.5%), BN+W (76.2%), and BN+WS (75.9%) by 9.5, 3.8, and 4.1 percentage points respectively. On CIFAR-100, BN+E reaches 72.4% Top-1 vs. 64.6% for BN. On ViT-B (ImageNet-1k), LN+E achieves 80.2% Top-1 vs. 79.9% for LN alone. For NeRF on LLFF, E-NeRF improves average PSNR from 29.6 dB to 30.20 dB over vanilla NeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla Batch Normalization [8], BN+E consistently wins across all CNN experiments, with the largest gap on DenseNet CIFAR-10 (79.96% vs. 70.5%, a 9.5-point improvement) and CIFAR-100 (72.4% vs. 64.6%). Against Weight Standardization [14] (BN+WS), BN+E leads by 4.1 points on DenseNet CIFAR-10 and 0.7 points on CIFAR-100, while on Inception the margins are smaller (0.4 and 2.6 points respectively). Against Weight Normalization [17] (BN+W), BN+E achieves the largest advantage on DenseNet CIFAR-100 (72.4% vs. 68.8%, a 3.6-point gap) and a modest 0.4-point lead on Inception CIFAR-10. For Vision Transformers, LN+E [1] edges out plain LN by 0.3 points Top-1 on ViT-B/ImageNet-1k (80.2% vs. 79.9%) and outperforms LN+W by 0.2 points. In the NeRF setting, E-NeRF surpasses vanilla NeRF [12] by an average of 0.6 dB PSNR across all eight LLFF scenes, with the largest per-scene gain on Fern (+1.02 dB) and Room (+0.74 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct line from numerical linear algebra preconditioning (specifically row equilibration and the Van Der Sluis optimality theorem for diagonal preconditioners) to neural network training, proposing that the same diagonal scaling that reduces a matrix's condition number in iterative solvers can be applied to weight matrices to smooth the loss landscape. It positions itself alongside existing normalization techniques—batch normalization, weight normalization (Salimans & Kingma), and weight standardization (Qiao et al.)—but as a complementary, drop-in operation rather than a replacement, and provides a Hessian-conditioning theoretical justification that prior normalization methods lack. The conceptual bridge between weight matrix singular values and the Hessian's condition number via second-order Taylor approximation is the paper's central theoretical contribution, extending the well-known role of Hessian conditioning in optimization to a practically computable proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical result (Theorem 2: equilibrated weights yield a better-conditioned Hessian) gives practitioners a principled, low-complexity tool—multiplying by a diagonal matrix—that can be layered onto any existing normalization stack. However, the empirical gains are modest in most settings (0.2–0.5% on ImageNet ViT, ~0.6 dB on NeRF), and the method adds per-iteration computational cost that the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the conditioning benefit persists at very large scale, whether the per-iteration overhead can be amortized (e.g., by updating the equilibration matrix less frequently), and whether the theoretical Hessian-conditioning guarantee translates to consistent gains in stochastic, non-convex settings beyond the local quadratic regime the analysis assumes.
