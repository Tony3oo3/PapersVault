## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., ESD) can be circumvented by red teaming attacks that craft adversarial text prompts to reconstruct erased concepts. Traditional adversarial training is computationally impractical in this setting because identifying adversarial prompts requires optimization across all diffusion timesteps, making it infeasible to integrate into an erasure training loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model and requires only a short textual description of the target concept to erase (no additional image datasets). During training, CLIP-encoded text embeddings are perturbed with bounded adversarial offsets (ε=0.1) at a single diffusion timestep, and the model is fine-tuned to nullify the concept in that perturbed neighborhood. At inference, the model takes a standard text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image, with the erasure being intrinsic to the model weights rather than a post-hoc filter.

## Method

RACE introduces a single-timestep adversarial attack that, leveraging the Diffusion Classifier insight, identifies adversarial text embeddings capable of reconstructing an erased concept using PGD at only one timestep (t*=500) rather than across all 1000 steps. This perturbation δ is then injected into the ESD erasure loss, replacing the clean concept embedding c with c+δ in the trainable U-Net, so the model learns to map the entire ε-neighborhood of the target concept to its null representation. The training loop alternates between N steps of PGD to find δ and a gradient step on the RACE loss to update θ, making adversarial training computationally tractable. This design eliminates the need for external attack prompts or images during training, and the resulting model achieves up to 33% absolute ASR reduction against state-of-the-art white-box attacks like UnlearnDiff while preserving image quality on non-targeted concepts.

## Results

RACE achieves a 33% absolute reduction in Attack Success Rate (ASR) for the 'nudity' concept against UnlearnDiff (the SOTA white-box attack), dropping from 0.80 (ESD) to 0.47. Similar >30% ASR reductions are observed for 'Van Gogh' (0.36→0.04) and 'church' (0.68→0.38) under UnlearnDiff. Across all seven tested concepts and three attack methods (PEZ, P4D, UnlearnDiff), RACE consistently lowers ASR relative to ESD, with the largest gains on artistic styles and objects. The trade-off is a modest increase in FID (e.g., 18.88→25.16 for nudity) and slight CLIP-Score decrease, indicating some image quality degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is ESD (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023), the standard concept-erasure method that RACE augments with adversarial training. RACE wins on ASR across every concept and every attack method tested: the most dramatic gains are on artistic style (Van Gogh: 0.36→0.04, −0.32) and objects (church: 0.68→0.38, −0.30; parachute: 0.60→0.38, −0.22) under the strongest white-box attack UnlearnDiff. For explicit content, RACE reduces nudity ASR by 0.33 (0.80→0.47) and violence by 0.11 (0.79→0.68) under UnlearnDiff, though the gains on 'illegal acts' are smaller (−0.05). On black-box attacks (PEZ, I2P prompts), RACE also consistently improves, e.g., reducing I2P ASR for nudity from 0.14 to 0.05. The one area where RACE underperforms ESD is image quality: FID increases by roughly 4–7 points and CLIP-Score drops by 0.02–0.05 for non-artistic concepts, reflecting a robustness–quality trade-off that the authors partially mitigate with a weight-regularization variant (RACE+Reg.).

## Relation to Prior Work

RACE builds directly on the ESD concept erasure framework (Gandikota et al., ICCV 2023), which nullifies a target concept's text embedding by fine-tuning the U-Net. The key departure is the incorporation of adversarial training (PGD, Madry et al. 2017) into the erasure loss, motivated by the Diffusion Classifier insight (Li et al., ICCV 2023) that a single diffusion timestep suffices for image classification. This enables a computationally tractable single-timestep adversarial attack on the text embedding, which prior red-teaming methods (UnlearnDiff, P4D) could not achieve efficiently enough to plug into a training loop. Conceptually, RACE extends the erasure objective from nullifying a single embedding point to nullifying the entire ε-neighborhood around it, addressing the vulnerability that nearby embeddings can regenerate erased content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a critical practical gap: existing concept erasure methods are demonstrably bypassable by prompt-based red teaming, undermining their value as a safety mechanism for deployed T2I systems. The single-timestep adversarial attack is a practical enabler that makes adversarial training feasible in the diffusion setting, where full-timestep optimization is prohibitively expensive. However, the paper identifies an unresolved robustness-quality trade-off (FID degrades from ~19 to ~25 for nudity erasure), and the quality-preserving regularization only partially mitigates this. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), whether the trade-off can be fully resolved, and whether the defense holds against future, more sophisticated attack formulations.
