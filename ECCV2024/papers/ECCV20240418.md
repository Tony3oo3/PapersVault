## Problem

Existing prompt-based image restoration methods encode degradation information by mining spatial correlations, which neglects the fact that different degradations (rain streaks, raindrops, haze, etc.) affect distinct frequency bands. This spatial-only prompting leaves subtle or undetectable artifacts in the restored image because frequency-domain differences between the restored and ground-truth images are never explicitly addressed. The paper asks whether frequency-specific prompts can be extracted and used to guide a restoration transformer for finer detail and global structure recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240418/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image of size H×W×3 and outputs a restored RGB image of the same resolution, computed as the input plus a learned residual. Internally, a single 3×3 convolution extracts shallow features (H×W×48) that feed both the 3-level encoder-decoder restoration branch and the prompt branch; the prompt branch operates at the same spatial resolution as the shallow features before resizing to match each decoder level. Training uses 256×256 patches with 300K iterations, and evaluation is performed on full-resolution test sets across five degradation types.

## Method

FPro consists of a standard 3-level encoder-decoder restoration branch (with attention removed from the encoder to avoid diluting high-frequency details) and a parallel prompt branch. The prompt branch first applies a Gated Dynamic Decoupler (GDD) that predicts per-channel-group low-pass filters via global average pooling, 1×1 convolutions, a sigmoid gating, and softmax (ensuring the filter is low-pass); the high-pass filter is obtained by subtracting the low-pass filter from the identity kernel. The resulting low- and high-frequency maps are then processed by a Dual Prompt Block: the High-frequency Prompt Modulator (HPM) applies a depthwise-convolution gating, injects learnable prompt components, and performs local window cross-attention (query from decoder features, key/value from the prompted high-frequency map); the Low-frequency Prompt Modulator (LPM) projects into the Fourier domain via FFT, applies a 1×1-convolution gating and learnable prompt in the frequency domain, then performs global cross-attention with adaptive average pooling as a low-pass filter on the key/value side. The LPM's frequency-domain gating is shown to be equivalent to a dynamic large-kernel depthwise convolution in the spatial domain, providing efficient global interaction. Both modulators' outputs are injected into the decoder via 1×1 convolutions, and the whole network is trained end-to-end with a standard L1+LPIPS loss.

## Results

On the SPAD real deraining benchmark, FPro achieves 48.99 dB PSNR and 0.9936 SSIM, surpassing the previous best DRSformer (48.53 dB) by 0.46 dB and Restormer (47.98 dB) by 1.01 dB. Across five restoration tasks, FPro attains the best or near-best performance: 31.96 dB on AGAN-Data deraindrop, 29.25 dB on TIP-2018 demoiréing, 32.85 dB on SOTS dehazing (a 1.54 dB gain over PromptIR), and 33.05 dB on GoPro deblurring (matching PromptRestorer's 33.06 dB while using half the FLOPs). The model also achieves the lowest NIQE score (5.2999) on real-world deraining, indicating superior perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPro outperforms all 40 compared baselines on four of five tasks. On SPAD deraining, it beats the strongest prior method DRSformer [7] by 0.46 dB and the general-purpose Restormer [81] by 1.01 dB. On SOTS dehazing, FPro gains 1.54 dB over the prompt-based PromptIR [50] and 1.98 dB over Restormer, the largest margin among all tasks. On AGAN-Data deraindrop and TIP-2018 demoiréing, FPro leads Restormer by 0.28 dB and Wang et al. [66] by 0.38 dB respectively. On GoPro deblurring, FPro (33.05 dB) essentially ties PromptRestorer [68] (33.06 dB) while consuming only 82 GFLOPs versus 186 GFLOPs, and surpasses CODE [85] by 1.11 dB. The model also uses fewer parameters (22.3 M) and FLOPs (81.9 G) than Restormer (26.1 M / 174.7 G) and DRSformer (33.7 M / 242.9 G), confirming its efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPro builds directly on Restormer's encoder-decoder Transformer architecture (FFN + MSA blocks) for the restoration branch, while introducing a parallel prompt branch that departs from prior spatial prompt-learning methods (PromptIR, PromptRestorer) by operating in the frequency domain. The Gated Dynamic Decoupler repurposes the dynamic convolution idea (Chen et al., 2020) to generate learnable low-pass/high-pass filters per channel group, and the Dual Prompt Block draws on the observation that self-attention acts as a low-pass filter (Park & Kim, 2022) to motivate separate low- and high-frequency modulation pathways. Conceptually, the paper positions frequency-domain prompting as a complementary axis to the spatial prompting explored in recent work, arguing that degradation-specific cues are better captured by band-specific signals rather than raw spatial correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-prompting perspective offers a principled way to inject degradation-specific information into restoration networks without task-specific hand-crafted priors, and the demonstrated generality across five distinct restoration tasks (deraining, deraindrop, demoiréing, deblurring, dehazing) suggests the approach could serve as a reusable module in broader restoration pipelines. The theoretical equivalence between Fourier-domain gating and dynamic large-kernel depth-wise convolution (Eq. 8) provides an efficient implementation path that avoids explicit large convolutions. However, the paper acknowledges failure on heavy nighttime degradation, and it remains open whether frequency prompting generalizes to tasks with more complex or mixed degradation spectra, or whether the benefits persist at significantly higher resolutions where FFT costs grow.
