## Problem

Most 3D point cloud deep learning methods achieve translation and permutation invariance but lack rotation invariance, relying instead on rotation augmentation during training that fails to generalize to unseen orientations. Existing rotation-invariant approaches suffer from accuracy drops relative to non-rotation-invariant methods due to loss of global information, unstable local reference frames, and insufficiently expressive invariant descriptors. This paper aims to close and surpass the accuracy gap while guaranteeing strict rotation invariance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1024-point 3D point clouds with xyz coordinates; normals (nx, ny, nz) are optional and, when absent, are estimated via the weighted eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue of the local covariance. For classification, the output is a single class label; for segmentation, it is a per-point part label. Evaluation is conducted under three rotation protocols (z/z, SO3/SO3, z/SO3), and the method produces identical accuracy across all three, confirming true rotation invariance with zero standard deviation.

## Method

For each reference point, the method constructs two local triangle surfaces using the point, a neighbor x_i, and its two adjacent neighbors x_{i-1} and x_{i+1}, from which a 14-dimensional Rotation Invariant Surface Property (RISP) vector is extracted, comprising a distance, five Euclidean-space angles, and eight tangent-space angles involving estimated normals. These RISPs are embedded via shared MLPs, refined by a first self-attention layer over the K neighbors, concatenated with previous-layer features, passed through another MLP and max-pooling, and then refined by a second self-attention layer over the N representative points, forming the RISurConv operator. The full classification network stacks five RISurConv layers followed by an 8-head Transformer Encoder and fully connected layers, while segmentation uses an encoder-decoder with skip connections. Because all geometric descriptors are rotation-invariant by construction (distances and angles), the entire pipeline is invariant to arbitrary SO(3) rotations without requiring local reference frames or canonical alignment.

## Results

On ModelNet40, RISurConv achieves 96.0% overall accuracy (with normals) and 95.6% (without normals), with zero accuracy deviation (Std. = 0.0) across all three rotation evaluation protocols (z/z, SO3/SO3, z/SO3). On the harder real-world ScanObjectNN (PB_T50_RS), it reaches 93.1% accuracy, and on fine-grained FG3D it achieves 91.5%, 82.7%, and 78.5% class accuracy for Airplane, Chair, and Car respectively. For part segmentation on ShapeNet, it attains 81.5% mIoU (with normals) and 81.3% (without normals), all while maintaining perfect rotation invariance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RISurConv outperforms the strongest rotation-invariant baseline RIConv++ [42] by 4.7% on ModelNet40 (96.0% vs. 91.2%), 12.8% on ScanObjectNN (93.1% vs. 80.3%), and by 3.6–9.2% across the three FG3D fine-grained categories. It also surpasses the best non-rotation-invariant method, Point Transformer v2 [34], by 1.8% under the z/z protocol (96.0% vs. 94.2%), while maintaining zero accuracy deviation across all rotation scenarios where Pt Transformer v2 drops to 51.8% under z/SO3. On ShapeNet segmentation, it leads RIConv++ by 1.0% mIoU (81.5% vs. 80.5%) and RIF [16] by 2.1% (81.5% vs. 79.4%). The key differentiator is that all rotation-invariant baselines (RIConv, GCAConv, RIF, RI-GCN, RIConv++) show near-zero Std. but lower peak accuracy, whereas traditional methods (PointNet, DGCNN, Pt Transformer v2) achieve higher z/z accuracy but suffer catastrophic drops (20–40% Std.) under arbitrary SO3 rotations. RISurConv is the first method to simultaneously achieve the highest peak accuracy and perfect rotation invariance (Std. = 0.0) across all four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant rotation-invariant point cloud paradigm that relies on Local Reference Frames (LRF), Local Reference Axes (LRA), or spherical harmonic kernels (RIConv, RIConv++, GCAConv, SPHNet, SFCNN). Instead of transforming points into a canonical coordinate system, it constructs local triangle surfaces around each reference point and extracts 14-dimensional rotation invariant surface properties (RISP) directly from the geometry of those surfaces. The attention-augmented convolution (RISurConv) repurposes standard transformer self-attention modules as feature-refinement layers within a PointNet++-style hierarchical architecture, blending the CNN and transformer families in a way that prior rotation-invariant methods had not attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that rotation invariance need not come at an accuracy cost, closing and surpassing the long-standing gap between rotation-invariant and non-rotation-invariant methods (e.g., 96.0% vs. 94.2% on ModelNet40). However, the 14M parameter count (35× that of RIConv++) and longer training time (450 epochs) raise practical adoption questions, and the method's reliance on estimated normals for sparse or noisy scans remains an open concern. Whether the local triangle surface formulation generalizes beyond classification/segmentation to tasks like registration or generation, and whether the parameter overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the surface-based inductive bias, are natural next steps.
