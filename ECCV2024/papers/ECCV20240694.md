## Problem

DDIM inversion produces a latent noise code that is tightly coupled with the source prompt, because the practical inversion equation substitutes z_{t-1} (obtained via source-prompt-conditioned denoising) for the ideal input z_t. Previous inversion methods (NTI, NPI, DirectInv) focused on reducing the reconstruction gap D_Rec but ignored the noise gap D_Noi — the divergence between the inverted noise and the ideal prompt-independent noise — which causes artifacts and inconsistent details when editing with a target prompt.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (encoded into the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4), a source text prompt, and a target text prompt. It outputs a disentangled latent noise code z_T that is then fed into an editing engine (P2P, MasaCtrl, or PNP) together with the target prompt to produce the edited image. The inversion operates at 50 DDIM steps with CFG 7.5, and each timestep involves up to K=25 gradient-descent rounds (with early stopping at threshold δ=5e-6), making the total inversion time ~27 s on an RTX 3090.

## Method

SPDInv identifies that the ideal DDIM inversion equation z_t = C_{t,1}·z_{t-1} + C_{t,2}·ε_θ(z_t, t, c) is a fixed-point problem x = f_θ(x), and reformulates it into the loss L = ‖f_θ(z_t) − z_t‖₂. At each inversion step, it first obtains an initial z_t via the standard (prompt-coupled) DDIM inversion, then iteratively minimizes L by gradient descent on z_t alone while keeping the pre-trained diffusion model frozen, using the model's image-text comprehension to guide the search toward the prompt-independent fixed point. A threshold δ enables early termination once the fixed-point constraint is satisfied, and the number of optimization rounds K is capped to balance quality and speed. The entire procedure requires only ~10 lines of modification to existing inversion pipelines and is plug-and-play across P2P, MasaCtrl, PNP, and customized generation methods like ELITE.

## Results

On PIE-Bench with the P2P editing engine, SPDInv achieves the best scores across all six metrics: DINO 8.81×10³, PSNR 28.60, LPIPS 36.01×10³, MSE 24.54×10⁴, SSIM 86.23×10², and CLIP 25.26. Compared to the second-best methods (DirectINV and ProxEdit), SPDInv improves DINO by 24%, PSNR by 5%, LPIPS by 21%, and MSE by 13%, while incurring a moderate inversion time of 27.04 s (vs. 11.55 s for DDIM but far less than 137.54 s for NTI or 87.21 s for AIDI). Under the MasaCtrl and PNP engines, SPDInv similarly leads on every metric, e.g., DINO 20.48×10³ (MasaCtrl) and 15.58×10³ (PNP), confirming plug-and-play effectiveness across editing backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPDInv outperforms all seven inversion baselines (DDIM, NTI, NPI, AIDI, NMG, DirectINV, ProxEdit) on every metric under the P2P engine, with the largest gains over DirectINV (DINO 8.81 vs. 11.65, LPIPS 36.01 vs. 54.55) and ProxEdit (DINO 8.81 vs. 11.87, MSE 24.54 vs. 32.16). Under MasaCtrl, SPDInv (DINO 20.48, PSNR 24.12) clearly surpasses AIDI (DINO 55.93, PSNR 19.25) and NMG (DINO 40.54), which suffer severe degradation on this engine, and also beats DirectINV (DINO 24.70) and DDIM (DINO 28.38). Under PNP, SPDInv achieves DINO 15.58 and MSE 34.69, substantially better than AIDI (DINO 25.36, MSE 78.19) and DirectINV (DINO 24.29, MSE 80.52). In the customized-editing setting (Table 2), SPDInv-ELITE (DINO 21.23, PSNR 24.14, LPIPS 74.36) dramatically improves over vanilla ELITE (DINO 148.37, PSNR 14.83) and also beats the non-inversion methods BlendDM (DINO 59.21) and InstructP2P (DINO 155.49) on structure and background preservation, though SPDInv-P2P (DINO 14.77) remains the strongest overall. The main trade-off is inversion time: SPDInv at 27.04 s is roughly 2.3× slower than DDIM/NPI/ProxEdit (~11.7 s) but 5× faster than NTI (137.54 s) and 3.2× faster than AIDI (87.21 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPDInv builds directly on DDIM inversion (Song et al., ICLR 2020) and the fixed-point formulation identified by AIDI (Pan et al., ICCV 2023), but departs from both in a key conceptual way: whereas prior inversion methods (NTI, NPI, DirectInv, ProxEdit) optimize to minimize the reconstruction gap D_Rec, SPDInv instead minimizes the noise gap D_Noi by enforcing a fixed-point constraint at each inversion step. The practical departure from AIDI is replacing its unstable fixed-round iteration with a gradient-descent optimization of a reformulated loss, leveraging the pre-trained diffusion model as a fixed oracle. This positions SPDInv as a drop-in replacement for the inversion step in existing editing pipelines (P2P, MasaCtrl, PNP) rather than a new editing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of the inversion objective from reconstruction fidelity to noise-prompt independence offers a clearer diagnostic for why editing artifacts arise, and the 10-line plug-and-play integration makes it immediately adoptable across multiple editing engines. The extension to customized generation (SPDInv-ELITE) demonstrates that the same inversion improvement can unlock localized editing in methods previously limited to global generation. Open questions remain: the gap D_Noi is reduced but not eliminated, portrait editing still struggles, and the method inherits the limitations of its underlying editing engines (e.g., low success rates for adding/removing objects). Whether the fixed-point constraint can be further tightened or combined with learned inversion paths is a natural next direction.
