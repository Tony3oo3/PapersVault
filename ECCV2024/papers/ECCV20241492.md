## Problem

Large-scale instructional video-text datasets like HowTo100M rely on ASR transcripts that are noisy, non-descriptive, and poorly temporally aligned—only 30% of narrations are visually alignable and 15% have correct start/end timestamps. This limits the quality of video-text representation learning for fine-grained temporal grounding tasks. The paper addresses this by proposing an automatic denoising pipeline and a multi-sentence grounding model that simultaneously handles both narration alignment and procedural step grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241492/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed long-form instructional video (6.7 minutes on average, with visual features extracted per 1-second clip) and K associated text sentences—either ~100 ordered dense narrations or ~10 unordered sparse procedural steps—and outputs a K×T alignment score matrix in [0,1] plus a binary alignability label per sentence. A single unified model handles both task variants by toggling a positional-encoding indicator for ordered narrations versus shuffled steps, and the temporal resolution is 1 second per frame feature.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) Llama2-7B is prompted to transform noisy WhisperX ASR transcripts into concise descriptive procedural steps (~7M steps from 370K videos); (2) approximate timestamps are estimated by chaining a step-to-transcript text-similarity matrix with the transcript-to-video ASR timestamp matrix, then expanding from the peak score outward until scores drop below a threshold; (3) a lightweight Transformer model (NaSVA) with 3 encoder layers for temporal aggregation and 3 decoder layers for cross-attention grounding (visual features as key-value, text as queries) is trained with an InfoNCE variant, then used in a self-training round to refine the pseudo-labels. The key architectural insight is treating all text sentences as queries that iteratively attend to visual features, enabling simultaneous multi-sentence grounding rather than independent single-sentence localization.

## Results

On the three multi-sentence grounding benchmarks, the proposed NaSVA model achieves 46.4% R@1 on HT-Step, 71.6% R@1 on HTM-Align, and 46.7% Avg.R@1 (zero-shot) on CrossTask. These represent absolute improvements of 9.0%, 5.1%, and 1.9% over the previous state-of-the-art (VINA) on each benchmark respectively. The model is trained on a single unified architecture using both narrations and procedural steps, with InternVideo-MM-L14 as the default visual-textual backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four prior methods across three benchmarks. On HT-Step (grounding unordered Wikihow procedural steps), the proposed method achieves 46.4% R@1, surpassing VINA [28] (37.4%) by 9.0% and TAN [17] (31.2%) by 15.2%. On HTM-Align (aligning ASR narrations to video), it reaches 71.6% R@1, exceeding VINA (66.5%) by 5.1% and TAN (47.1%) by 24.5%. On CrossTask (zero-shot step localization without fine-tuning), it achieves 46.7% Avg.R@1, outperforming VINA (44.8%) by 1.9%, UniVL [27] (42.0%) by 4.7%, and Zhukov [50] (40.5%) by 6.2%. The method wins on every benchmark where a direct comparison is available, with the largest margin on HT-Step, which is the most challenging task due to unordered, externally-sourced steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads: large-scale video-text dataset construction (HowTo100M, YouTube-8M), multi-sentence temporal grounding in instructional videos (TAN for narration alignment, VINA for procedural step grounding), and LLM-based pseudo-label generation (VQA-T, LAVILA). It departs from prior work by unifying narration alignment and procedural step grounding—previously addressed separately—under a single Transformer-based model (NaSVA) with cross-attention, and by introducing an automatic LLM-driven pipeline to convert noisy ASR transcripts into temporally-aligned descriptive steps. The concurrent work [38] also uses LLMs on instructional video transcripts, but generates dense captions rather than the concise, grounded procedural steps targeted here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HowToStep dataset (≈7M steps from 340K videos) is roughly 70× larger than the Wikihow knowledge base used by prior step-grounding work, and the manual quality check shows alignability doubling from 30% to 61%, making it a substantially more reliable training resource for video-text alignment. The finding that direct video-step similarity for pseudo-labels causes the model to collapse to an identity mapping, while indirect chaining through ASR timestamps preserves useful alignment signal, offers a practical insight for self-training pipelines more broadly. Open questions include whether the single-round self-training can be iterated for further gains, whether the pipeline generalizes beyond instructional/HowTo-style videos, and how sensitive the results are to the specific LLM used for step summarization.
