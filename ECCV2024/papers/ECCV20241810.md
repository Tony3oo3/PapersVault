## Problem

Mainstream text-to-image diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion only accept English prompts, forcing non-English speakers to rely on translation that loses culture-specific concepts (e.g., a Chinese dish name becoming 'Braised lion head'). Training a new T2I model from scratch on a specific language is prohibitively expensive, and existing multilingual approaches either require full model retraining or fail to capture culturally relevant generation. The paper asks whether a low-cost, plug-and-play adapter can adapt a frozen English T2I model to non-English prompts while preserving general generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a non-English text prompt (tested in Chinese, Russian, Japanese, Korean, and Italian) which is encoded by a language-specific CLIP text encoder, then passed through a 6M-parameter MLP adapter before conditioning the frozen UNet denoiser. The output is a standard RGB image produced via the latent diffusion sampling process (512×512 for SD1.5, 1024×1024 for SDXL). Notably, the entire UNet, VAE, and CLIP encoder remain frozen; only the adapter's 6M parameters (~0.2% of SDXL's 3B total) are updated during training.

## Method

PEA-Diffusion inserts a lightweight MLP adapter between a language-specific CLIP text encoder and the frozen UNet of a pretrained Stable Diffusion model, using the English SD as a teacher for knowledge distillation. The KD loss replaces the standard KL divergence with L2-norm distance between teacher and student UNet outputs (since diffusion predicts pixel values, not categorical distributions), and additionally aligns intermediate UNet feature maps across a predefined set of layers. For culturally-specific data where English translation is unreliable, the method switches to standard diffusion denoising loss instead of KD, since the teacher cannot provide meaningful guidance. The key insight is that aligning UNet feature and logit spaces (rather than text encoder spaces as in GlueGen) is what enables the frozen UNet to 'awaken' its latent cultural generation ability. The resulting adapter is plug-and-play, directly compatible with community plugins like LoRA, ControlNet, Inpainting, LCM, and pruned/compressed SD models without retraining.

## Results

On SDXL, PEA-Diffusion achieves the highest language-specific CLIPScore for cultural prompts (MC) across 4 of 5 languages: Chinese (0.2610), Russian (0.5012), Italian (0.4191), and Korean (0.2194), outperforming translation, LoRA, and Wk/Wv fine-tuning. On general prompts (MG), PEA-Diffusion scores 1.0245 ImageReward and 0.2244 PickScore, slightly below the translation baseline (1.3647 / 0.2402) but far above LoRA (0.1217 / 0.1598) and Wk/Wv (0.1413 / 0.1621). On SD1.5, PEA-Diffusion leads all baselines on CLIPScore for Chinese cultural prompts (0.2524 vs. 0.2172 for AltDiffusion-m18 and 0.2153 for re-implemented GlueGen) while achieving positive ImageReward (0.0985) where GlueGen and AltDiffusion-m9 produce negative scores (−0.5187 and −0.6966).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEA-Diffusion clearly outperforms AltDiffusion (m9 and m18) and GlueGen (both original and re-implemented) on nearly every metric: on SD1.5 Chinese cultural CLIPScore, PEA-Diffusion reaches 0.2524 versus 0.2172 (AltDiffusion-m18) and 0.2153 (GlueGen re-implemented), while GlueGen produces negative ImageReward scores (−0.47 to −0.52) indicating poor image quality. Against LoRA and Wk/Wv fine-tuning on SDXL, PEA-Diffusion wins on cultural CLIPScore for all five languages (e.g., 0.2610 vs. 0.2366 for LoRA on Chinese) and on general ImageReward/PickScore by a wide margin (1.0245 vs. 0.1217 for LoRA), though it trails the simple translation baseline on general ImageReward (1.0245 vs. 1.3647) and PickScore (0.2244 vs. 0.2402). The one language where PEA-Diffusion underperforms translation is Japanese (0.2544 vs. 0.2958 CLIPScore), which the authors attribute to the Japanese CLIP encoder's limited capacity. Overall, PEA-Diffusion is the only method that simultaneously achieves top cultural relevance and competitive general image quality without requiring multi-stage training or full model fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion architecture and the classical knowledge distillation framework, but departs from prior multilingual T2I work in a key conceptual way: rather than aligning text encoder representations (as GlueGen does with its GlueNet module) or retraining the full UNet in multiple stages (as AltDiffusion does), PEA-Diffusion performs KD at the UNet level—aligning intermediate feature maps and final logits between a frozen English teacher and a student with a language-specific CLIP encoder. The only trainable component is a 6M-parameter MLP adapter bridging the new text encoder to the frozen UNet, which is a substantially lighter intervention than any prior multilingual T2I approach. The method also inherits the parameter-efficient fine-tuning lineage (LoRA, Wk/Wv tuning) but positions itself as superior because it avoids the high-quality paired-data dependency those methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play compatibility with the entire English SD ecosystem (LoRA, ControlNet, Inpainting, LCM, compressed models) is the most practically significant contribution, as it means non-English users can access the full breadth of community resources without retraining. The ~20× reduction in training cost over AltDiffusion (1600 vs. ~30000 GPU hours) makes multilingual T2I feasible for smaller research groups. However, the method's ceiling is bounded by the English base model's generative capacity, and performance is sensitive to the quality of the language-specific CLIP encoder (Japanese results lag, attributed to weaker CLIP encoding). An open question is whether this frozen-UNet + adapter paradigm generalizes to languages with fundamentally different writing systems or to tasks requiring the UNet to learn genuinely new visual concepts absent from English training data.
