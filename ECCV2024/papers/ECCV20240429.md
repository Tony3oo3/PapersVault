## Problem

Existing zero-shot skeleton-based action recognition methods directly align full skeleton features with text embeddings, implicitly assuming that skeleton sequences within a class are highly consistent. The authors observe that camera positions, actor body shapes, and movement ranges introduce substantial semantic-irrelevant noise into skeleton sequences, creating an inherent asymmetry between the variable visual modality and the constant text labels that degrades alignment quality and generalization to unseen classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240429/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D skeleton sequences (25 joints per frame) and text class descriptions. Skeleton features are extracted by a pre-trained Shift-GCN or ST-GCN, and text features by Sentence-BERT or CLIP. Under the ZSL protocol, the model outputs a probability distribution over unseen classes only; under GZSL, it produces a merged probability over all seen and unseen classes via a domain classifier that combines a seen-classifier output (operating on raw skeleton features f_x) and an unseen-classifier output (operating on the semantic-related latent z_x^r).

## Method

SA-DVAE employs a two-head skeleton encoder that disentangles the latent representation into a semantic-related vector z_x^r and a semantic-irrelevant vector z_x^v, each modeled as an independent multivariate Gaussian. Two modality-specific VAEs (skeleton and text) are trained with a cross-alignment loss that aligns only z_x^r with the text latent z_y, while z_x^v is concatenated with z_y to reconstruct the full skeleton feature, thereby preserving style information without polluting the semantic alignment. An adversarial total correlation penalty, implemented as a discriminator D_T trained on permuted pairs of (z_x^v, z_x^r), enforces statistical independence between the two disentangled factors. Training uses a cyclical annealing schedule to mitigate KL vanishing, and for GZSL a logistic-regression domain classifier merges temperature-tuned top-k seen probabilities with unseen probabilities to produce the final class prediction.

## Results

On the random-split ZSL protocol, SA-DVAE achieves 84.20% accuracy on NTU-60 (55/5), 50.67% on NTU-120 (110/10), and 66.54% on PKU-MMD (46/5), outperforming the strongest baseline SMIE by +19.12%, +4.27%, and +5.71% respectively. Under the GZSL protocol, SA-DVAE attains a harmonic mean of 75.27% on NTU-60, 47.54% on NTU-120, and 54.72% on PKU-MMD, with the largest gains on unseen-class accuracy (e.g., 72.60% Acc_u on NTU-60 vs. 60.77% for SynSE). On the predefined-split ZSL benchmark, SA-DVAE reaches 82.37% on NTU-60 55/5 and 68.77% on NTU-120 110/10, surpassing SMIE by +4.39% and +3.03%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-DVAE outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and protocol. The largest margin is against SMIE (the strongest prior method): +19.12% on NTU-60 ZSL, +4.27% on NTU-120 ZSL, and +5.71% on PKU-MMD ZSL. Against SynSE, which relies on part-of-speech decomposition, SA-DVAE gains +20.01% on NTU-60 and +12.69% on PKU-MMD, while using only simple class labels without PoS tagging. CADA-VAE and ReViSE, which perform direct cross-modal alignment without disentanglement, trail SA-DVAE by 22.36% and 23.26% on NTU-60 respectively. On the harder GZSL task, SA-DVAE's advantage widens: the harmonic mean improves by +7.80% over SynSE and +8.89% over CADA-VAE on NTU-60, driven primarily by a +11.83% gain in unseen-class accuracy. The ablation (FD vs. FD+TC) confirms that feature disentanglement is the dominant contributor (+12.95% over naive alignment on NTU-60), while the adversarial total correlation penalty adds a further +1.99% by reducing feature redundancy and improving generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SA-DVAE extends the CADA-VAE line of work (which uses modality-specific VAEs for cross-modal alignment in GZSL) by introducing feature disentanglement into the skeleton encoder, a concept previously applied only to image-based GZSL (SDGZSL, ICCV 2021). Unlike SynSE and JPoSE, which rely on part-of-speech tags to decompose text, SA-DVAE uses simple class labels and instead decomposes the skeleton representation into semantic-related and semantic-irrelevant components. The adversarial total correlation penalty for enforcing independence between the two latent factors is adapted from SDGZSL but is the first such mechanism applied to skeleton-based zero-shot recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete, previously unaddressed source of noise in skeleton ZSL—within-class variation due to camera angles and actor differences—and shows that explicitly separating this from semantic signal yields substantial gains (up to +12.95% ZSL accuracy from disentanglement alone). A limitation is that the semantic-irrelevant component is very small (8–32 dims vs. 160–256 for the semantic-related one), suggesting the disentanglement is heavily biased toward semantic content, and the method still depends on the quality of pre-trained feature extractors and text descriptions. Open questions include whether the adversarial TC penalty is strictly necessary over simpler independence regularizers, and whether the approach transfers to other skeleton-based tasks (e.g., few-shot or domain-adaptive recognition).
