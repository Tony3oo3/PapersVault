## Problem

The choice of activation function in deep networks lacks principled guidance: Sigmoid is standard for balanced classification but biases toward frequent classes under imbalance, while Gumbel helps imbalanced tasks but overfits balanced ones. No existing activation adapts its shape to the data distribution, forcing practitioners to select activations via cross-validation per task. The paper identifies that activation functions implicitly encode a prior on the error distribution and that this prior must shift with the degree of class imbalance.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in module within standard CNN backbones (SE-ResNet, MaskRCNN, FasterRCNN) consuming RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet, variable for LVIS/COCO). APA* replaces the Sigmoid inside channel-attention blocks while AGLU replaces ReLU in all intermediate convolutional layers, so the network's external I/O is unchanged: class labels for classification, bounding boxes and instance masks for detection/segmentation. The two learnable parameters (κ, λ) are per-layer scalars initialized and optimized jointly with the rest of the network.

## Method

The core contribution is the Adaptive Parametric Activation η_ad(z,κ,λ) = (λ·exp(−κz)+1)^(−1/λ), which is Richard's curve reparameterized with a gain κ controlling sensitivity and an asymmetry λ controlling the response rate to positive vs. negative inputs. This single formula unifies Sigmoid (κ=λ=1), Gumbel (κ=1, λ→0), ReLU (κ→∞), SiLU (κ=λ=1 in the linear form), and GELU (κ=1.702, λ=1). The linear variant AGLU = z·η_ad replaces ReLU in intermediate layers, while APA* (APA + LayerNorm + dropout) replaces Sigmoid in attention mechanisms, allowing the network to learn the optimal activation shape per layer during supervised training. Statistical analysis via Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests shows imbalanced training produces Gumbel-like logit distributions and reduced channel-attention entropy for rare classes, motivating the adaptive mechanism that shifts from Logistic-like (balanced) to Gumbel-like (imbalanced) behavior automatically.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, APA*+AGLU with SE-ResNeXt50 achieves 59.1% average top-1 accuracy (69.8% Many, 55.7% Medium, 41.1% Few), surpassing the SE-X50 baseline by 2.4pp and the prior SOTA RIDE(3E)+CMO+CR by 1.7pp using a single model. On iNaturalist2018, APA+AGLU reaches 74.8% top-1 accuracy, a 3.5pp gain over the SE baseline and 3.0pp above BCL. On LVIS instance segmentation, APA*+AGLU with SE-R101 achieves 30.7 AP^m and 31.1 AP^b, improving over the GOL+SE-R101 baseline by 1.0 and 1.1pp respectively. On balanced tasks, APA*+AGLU boosts COCO detection by 2.0 AP^b (41.2 vs 39.2), V3Det CascadeRCNN by 3.8 AP^b (35.4 vs 31.6), and ImageNet1K SE-R50 by 1.2pp top-1 (78.7 vs 77.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-LT with SE-X50, APA*+AGLU (59.1% avg) outperforms all single-model baselines including RIDE(3E)+CMO+CR (57.4%), BCL (57.1%), and RIDE(4E) (56.8%), with the largest margin on rare classes (+2.7pp over RIDE(3E)+CMO+CR). With SE-R50, APA*+AGLU (57.9%) beats RIDE(3E)+CMO (56.2%) by 1.7pp and CC-SAM (52.4%) by 5.5pp, while also improving both frequent and rare classes simultaneously—a property the paper highlights as unique. On iNaturalist2018, APA+AGLU (74.8%) surpasses BCL (71.8%) by 3.0pp, ResLT (70.5%) by 4.3pp, and LWS+ImbSAM (71.1%) by 3.7pp. On CIFAR100-LT (imbalance 100), APA+AGLU (52.3%) exceeds RIDE+CMO (50.0%) by 2.3pp and CC-SAM (50.8%) by 1.5pp. On LVIS, APA*+AGLU with SE-R101 (30.7 AP^m) outperforms GOL+SE-R101 (29.7), PCB+Seesaw (28.8), and ECM (28.7), with the rare-class AP^r improving from 23.0 to 23.6. On balanced ImageNet1K, APA*+AGLU with SE-R50 reaches 78.7% top-1, exceeding the SE-R50 baseline (77.5%) and CBAM-R50 (78.3%), confirming generalisability beyond imbalanced settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the activation-as-distributional-prior perspective introduced by the Gumbel activation [1] and Balanced Softmax [63] for imbalanced classification with the GELU/SiLU/PRELU line of work for balanced intermediate-layer activations, all under a single two-parameter formula derived from Richard's curve (1959). Prior work treated balanced and imbalanced settings as requiring different activation choices (Sigmoid vs. Gumbel at the classification head, ReLU vs. GELU in the body), and focused exclusively on either the classification layer or the intermediate layers. The key departure is making the activation itself learnable and parametric so that the network can adapt its response to the data distribution during optimisation, applied simultaneously to both the SE attention mechanism and the intermediate ReLU layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is a drop-in replacement (APA in attention, AGLU in place of ReLU) that consistently improves long-tailed and balanced benchmarks by 1–3 pp without architectural changes, which is attractive for practitioners already using SE-ResNet or CBAM backbones. However, the gains are modest and the paper's SOTA baselines rely heavily on 'bag of tricks' training recipes, making it difficult to isolate the activation's contribution from the training recipe. Open questions include whether the parametric advantage persists in Transformer-based architectures (only briefly mentioned in the appendix), whether the learned κ and λ parameters are stable across initialisations, and whether the distributional-prior framing extends to other imbalanced settings such as multi-label or open-world recognition.
