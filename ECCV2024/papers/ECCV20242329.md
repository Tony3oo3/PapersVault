## Problem

Prior video-to-audio generation systems either prioritize audio quality and semantic matching while ignoring temporal synchronicity, or enforce alignment at the cost of audio fidelity. Additionally, most approaches are restricted to wide-band audio (16–22.05 kHz), limiting the achievable quality of generated sound. This paper addresses the simultaneous challenge of producing full-band (44.1 kHz) audio that is both semantically faithful and temporally synchronized with visual events.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second silent video clip (RGB frames at ≥15 FPS) and produces a 44.1 kHz full-band audio waveform. Internally, the video is processed by two parallel front-ends: a per-frame CLIP image encoder for semantic content and a 3D-convolutional S3D encoder (fine-tuned for audio-visual offset detection) for alignment-sensitive temporal cues. The generated audio is represented as a 9-level RVQ codegram at 86 Hz framerate in DAC's latent space, which is decoded to waveform by the frozen DAC decoder.

## Method

MaskVAT is a masked generative Transformer that predicts the full DAC codegram (L×9 tokens) in parallel via iterative unmasking (MaskGIT-style with cosine scheduler), rather than autoregressively. Three architectural variants are explored: AdaLN (conditioning sequences concatenated channel-wise into AdaLN-modulated blocks), Seq2Seq (cross-attention decoder with auxiliary BEATs regression and contrastive losses at the encoder output), and Hybrid (combining both). The key design choices are: (1) operating in DAC's high-fidelity 9-level RVQ space to decouple audio quality from generation, (2) injecting alignment-sensitive S3D features to enforce temporal synchronicity, and (3) using BEATs-based auxiliary losses to pull encoder representations toward semantically meaningful audio features. At inference, classifier-free guidance and Gumbel-annealed diversity control the sampling, followed by a beam-selection step that picks the generated audio closest to the input video in SCAV sequential contrastive space.

## Results

On VGGSound-Test, MaskVAT variants dominate full-band quality metrics: MaskVAT_AdaLN-B achieves FDD=0.05 and FDM=0.88, while MaskVAT_Seq2Seq reaches FDM=0.60, far surpassing the best baseline (V2A-Mapper-BWE at FDD=0.24, FDM=2.72). For temporal alignment (SparseSync offset, lower is better), MaskVAT_Hybrid achieves SS=0.40 and MaskVAT_AdaLN-B achieves SS=0.43, substantially outperforming Diff-Foley (0.57) and V2A-Mapper (0.68). On semantic matching, MaskVAT_Seq2Seq and MaskVAT_Hybrid lead with WC=0.55 and CC=0.140/0.136, exceeding V2A-Mapper (WC=0.47, CC=0.124). However, on the low-band FAD metric, V2A-Mapper (0.89) still outperforms all MaskVAT variants (1.51–3.83), suggesting the lossy DAC codec imposes a quality ceiling in the 16 kHz band.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **V2A-Mapper** (the strongest quality competitor), MaskVAT wins decisively on full-band quality (FDD: 0.05–0.08 vs 0.24–0.45; FDM: 0.60–1.21 vs 2.72–14.1) and on semantic matching (WC: 0.55 vs 0.47; CC: 0.140 vs 0.124), but loses on the 16 kHz FAD metric (1.51–3.83 vs 0.89) and on subjective Fidelity (2.76 vs 3.00) and Relevance (3.33 vs 3.50). Against **Diff-Foley** (the strongest alignment competitor), MaskVAT_Hybrid achieves a substantially lower SparseSync offset (0.40 vs 0.57) and a much higher subjective Alignment score (3.81 vs 2.14), while also dominating on all quality and semantic metrics. Against **SpecVQGAN**, MaskVAT wins on every metric except FAD, where SpecVQGAN's 22.05 kHz native bandwidth gives it an edge (5.27 vs 1.51–3.83); after BWE, SpecVQGAN's advantage disappears. Against **Im2Wav**, MaskVAT dominates across all quality, semantic, and alignment metrics, with the largest gaps on FDM (0.60–1.21 vs 11.9) and SS (0.40–0.63 vs 0.68). Subjectively, MaskVAT_Hybrid is the clear winner on Alignment and Overall quality, while V2A-Mapper retains a narrow edge on Fidelity and Relevance among non-expert listeners that narrows among audio experts.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskVAT extends the masked generative token paradigm from MaskGIT (images) and VampNet (music) to the video-to-audio domain, operating in the 9-level RVQ codegram space of the DAC neural codec rather than in mel-spectrogram or waveform space. It departs from prior V2A systems (SpecVQGAN, Im2Wav, V2A-Mapper) that prioritized audio quality and semantic matching while treating video frames as independent images, and from alignment-focused systems (Diff-Foley, FoleyGen) that sacrificed full-band quality, by jointly addressing all three axes through a hybrid AdaLN + cross-attention Transformer conditioned on both CLIP (semantic) and S3D (alignment-sensitive) video features. The auxiliary BEATs regression/contrastive losses and the post-sampling SCAV-based beam selection are novel architectural and inference-time additions not present in prior masked audio generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first V2A system to simultaneously deliver full-band (44.1 kHz) audio quality, strong semantic matching, and explicit temporal synchronicity, closing a gap where prior work forced a trade-off between quality and alignment. The finding that the lossy DAC codec acts as a hard upper bound on low-band FAD quality (DAC reconstruction already scores worse than V2A-Mapper) is an important caveat: codec-based V2A may never match non-codec models in narrow-band fidelity, motivating future work on higher-fidelity codecs or hybrid codec/non-codec decoders. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos, multi-channel or spatial audio, and whether the SCAV-based post-sampling selection can be replaced by learned scoring to reduce inference cost.
