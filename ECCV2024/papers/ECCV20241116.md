## Problem

Integrating event camera data into video super-resolution typically requires redesigning network architectures from scratch and retraining them, which is laborious, resource-intensive, and discards the knowledge already encoded in pre-trained frame-only VSR models. The paper asks whether a parameter-efficient tuning paradigm—borrowed from NLP and vision-language tasks—can adapt existing VSR networks to exploit event streams without full retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241116/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes T low-resolution video frames (346×260 on the CED dataset) together with T−1 pairs of forward and backward event streams, which are first converted into voxel grids (B=5 time bins) via temporal bilinear interpolation before being processed by residual blocks into event features. It outputs T high-resolution frames at ×2 or ×4 upscaling, evaluated with PSNR and SSIM on the Y channel of YCbCr-transformed images.

## Method

EATER introduces two lightweight, plug-and-play units inserted into a frozen pre-trained VSR network: the Event-Adapted Alignment (EAA) unit and the Event-Adapted Fusion (EAF) unit. The EAA unit splits the aligned feature channel-wise, uses a spatial feature transform (SFT) conditioned on concatenated event features to generate affine parameters (α, β) that modulate one half of the aligned feature, then applies a second SFT stage for fine alignment, with the final output added residually to the original aligned feature. The EAF unit extracts multi-scale features (3 scales) from both the fused RGB feature and the event feature, processes them through a cascaded structure where each scale receives information from the previous one, and combines the upsampled multi-scale outputs via a residual addition. Only the EAA unit, EAF unit, and the event feature extractor are trained (with the original VSR backbone frozen), requiring roughly 15k–23k iterations versus ~100k for full fine-tuning while matching or exceeding its performance.

## Results

On the CED dataset for ×4 event-based VSR, EATER-tuned EDVR achieves 33.69 dB PSNR / 0.9113 SSIM, outperforming the EDVR baseline (33.37 dB) by +0.32 dB and even surpassing full fine-tuning (33.67 dB) while requiring only ~15k iterations versus ~100k. Similarly, EATER-tuned BasicVSR reaches 32.92 dB / 0.9032 SSIM, beating the BasicVSR baseline (32.75 dB) by +0.17 dB and full fine-tuning (32.91 dB) with ~23k iterations. The parameter-efficient tuning scheme thus matches or exceeds full fine-tuning performance with roughly 4–7× fewer training iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EATER consistently outperforms its corresponding frame-only baselines: EDVR improves from 33.37 to 33.69 dB (+0.32 dB) and BasicVSR from 32.75 to 32.92 dB (+0.17 dB) on the CED ×4 benchmark. Against full fine-tuning of the same architectures, EATER achieves marginally higher PSNR (33.69 vs. 33.67 for EDVR; 32.92 vs. 32.91 for BasicVSR) while requiring only 15k–23k iterations compared to ~100k, demonstrating clear parameter and training efficiency. The paper also compares against dedicated event-based VSR methods (E-VSR [24], EGVSR [42], EBVSR [27]), which require full network re-design and retraining from scratch; EATER's plug-and-play design avoids this overhead while leveraging pre-trained VSR weights. Among the sliding-window baselines (VSRNet, DUF, EDVR) and recurrent baselines (BasicVSR, IconVSR, BasicVSR++), the EATER-tuned variants uniformly surpass their unmodified counterparts, confirming the generality of the adapter approach across both architectural families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: event-based video super-resolution (E-VSR, EGVSR, EBVSR) and parameter-efficient tuning (PEFT) from NLP. Rather than following the dominant paradigm of redesigning network architectures from scratch to accommodate event streams, EATER repurposes the adapter/PEFT concept by inserting two lightweight modules (EAA and EAF) into existing pre-trained frame-only VSR backbones such as EDVR and BasicVSR. The EAA unit draws on spatial feature transform (SFT) for progressive alignment, while the EAF unit employs a cascaded multi-scale fusion design, both operating at the feature level so that the original VSR weights remain frozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: practitioners can adapt a pre-trained VSR model to event-based VSR without the cost of full retraining or architectural redesign, saving both compute and storage. However, the experimental validation is confined to a single real-world dataset (CED) and a handful of architectures, and the paper acknowledges that event noise in low-light conditions remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the PEFT paradigm generalizes across datasets (e.g., Vimeo90K, ALPIX-VSR), whether it extends to Transformer-based VSR backbones, and whether the added adapter overhead is acceptable in real-time deployment. The authors also note potential extension to related tasks like deblurring, frame interpolation, and HDR reconstruction.
