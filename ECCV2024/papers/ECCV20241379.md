## Problem

State-of-the-art deep learning-based video SCI reconstruction algorithms require large parameter counts and FLOPs, making deployment on resource-limited AI chips impractical. While network quantization is a natural compression strategy, directly quantizing these reconstruction networks to low-bit causes severe performance degradation (e.g., 4.11 dB PSNR drop for 4-bit), and the query/key distribution distortion in quantized Vision Transformers further compounds the problem. This paper presents the first low-bit quantization framework specifically designed for end-to-end video SCI reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The network takes a sequence of 2D snapshot measurements (compressed images captured by a low-speed CCD camera) and their corresponding binary modulation masks as input, and outputs a sequence of reconstructed high-speed video frames. Simulated experiments use 256×256 frames at compression ratio 8, while real-data experiments use 512×512 frames at compression ratio 10 from a custom DMD-based hardware system operating at 50 FPS. Training is performed on DAVIS2017 video sequences at 480×894 resolution with spatial crops of 128×128 and 256×256.

## Method

Q-SCI is a quantization-aware training framework built on the EfficientSCI-S backbone (a CNN-Transformer hybrid with dense connections and spacetime factorization). The key insight from empirical analysis is that the feature extraction module is the primary source of low-bit performance loss, so the framework introduces 8-bit 1×1×1 shortcut-connected convolution layers in both the feature extraction and video reconstruction modules to propagate high-quality features through the otherwise low-bit network. To address the query/key distribution distortion in the quantized Transformer branch (where the mean shifts significantly from the full-precision counterpart), a learnable shift bias is added to both query and key before the attention computation. The framework supports 8-bit, 4-bit, 3-bit, and 2-bit variants using asymmetric activation quantization and symmetric weight quantization with straight-through estimators, and is trained end-to-end with MSE loss initialized from the full-precision EfficientSCI-S weights.

## Results

On six simulated testing datasets (256×256, compression ratio 8), Q-SCI (4-bit) achieves an average PSNR of 34.69 dB and SSIM of 0.963 with only 0.48M parameters and 72.69G OPs, representing a 7.8× theoretical speedup over the full-precision EfficientSCI-S backbone (35.51 dB, 563.87G OPs) at a 2.3% relative PSNR gap. Q-SCI (8-bit) slightly exceeds EfficientSCI-S (35.57 vs. 35.51 dB) while reducing OPs by ~4×. On real testing datasets (512×512, compression ratio 10), Q-SCI (4-bit) produces clearer reconstructions than full-precision BIRNAT and PnP-FFDNet, and Q-SCI (8-bit) yields sharper details than full-precision STFormer-S and EfficientSCI-S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Q-SCI (4-bit) outperforms BIRNAT by 1.38 dB and RevSCI by 0.77 dB in average PSNR while reducing OPs by 5.4× and 10.6×, respectively. Against the full-precision backbone EfficientSCI-S, Q-SCI (8-bit) achieves a marginal 0.06 dB gain (35.57 vs. 35.51 dB) at 4× lower OPs, while Q-SCI (4-bit) trades 0.82 dB for a 7.8× OPs reduction. Q-SCI (8-bit) surpasses the heavy unfolding methods Dense3D-Unfolding and ELP-Unfolding by 0.31 dB and 0.16 dB, respectively, with 28.2× and 32.9× fewer OPs. Compared to the prior quantization method Q-ViT (8-bit), Q-SCI (8-bit) gains 0.4 dB at essentially the same computational cost (140.95G vs. 141.04G OPs). At the extreme low-bit end, Q-SCI (2-bit) matches MetaSCI (31.62 vs. 31.72 dB) with 2× fewer OPs, and Q-SCI (3-bit) beats BIRNAT by 0.31 dB with 10.4× fewer OPs. The framework also generalizes: when applied to STFormer-S, the high-quality feature extraction module alone adds 3.23 dB over the 4-bit baseline, confirming broad applicability beyond EfficientSCI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the network quantization paradigm—well established in high-level vision (Q-ViT, GPUSQ-ViT) and low-level vision (PAMS, CADyQ, FQSR)—to the video snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction task for the first time. It builds directly on EfficientSCI-S (CVPR 2023) as the full-precision backbone and adapts the query/key distribution shift idea from Q-ViT (NeurIPS 2022) to rectify Transformer distortion under low-bit quantization. The key methodological departure from prior quantization work is the targeted architectural modification: rather than uniformly quantizing all layers, the authors identify the feature extraction module as the primary degradation source and introduce 8-bit shortcut-connected convolution layers to preserve high-quality feature propagation, a design choice motivated by their empirical ablation rather than by prior quantization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.8× theoretical speedup at 4-bit with only a 2.3% PSNR gap is practically significant for deploying video SCI reconstruction on resource-limited AI chips, moving the field toward real end-to-end capture-and-reconstruct systems. However, the framework's reliance on 8-bit shortcut layers in the feature extraction and reconstruction modules partially undermines the 'low-bit' claim, and the paper does not demonstrate actual hardware deployment. Open questions include whether a fully 4-bit (or lower) design without 8-bit crutches is achievable, whether the framework generalizes beyond the two tested backbones (EfficientSCI-S, STFormer-S), and how algorithm-hardware co-design might further optimize the pipeline for specific target devices.
