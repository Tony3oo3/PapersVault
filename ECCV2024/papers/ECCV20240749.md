## Problem

Most multimodal Earth Observation datasets and models are restricted to a single data type—either mono-date images or time series—preventing them from jointly exploiting the spatial detail of VHR imagery, the temporal-spectral dynamics of optical series, and the weather resilience of radar. Existing fusion approaches are also typically tied to a fixed sensor set, limiting generalizability across the diverse and complementary EO sensors available.

## Input / Output

OmniSat consumes an arbitrary number of spatially aligned modalities per tile (e.g., a 0.2 m VHR RGB+NIR image, a 10 m Sentinel-2 optical time series, and a 10 m Sentinel-1 SAR time series), which are split into a consistent grid of non-overlapping patches (10×10 m or 40×40 m depending on the dataset). The model produces a single multimodal embedding per patch that fuses all available modalities; at inference time, any subset of modalities can be supplied, and the model still yields a useful representation. Downstream, these patch embeddings are classified into multi-label or multi-class targets (tree species, crop type, land cover).

## Method

OmniSat encodes each modality with a dedicated encoder—convolutional layers with SegNet-style max-pool index bypass for images, and a Lightweight Temporal Attention Encoder (LTAE) for time series—producing M×P token embeddings. A modality combining network then fuses these tokens via B residual self-attention blocks followed by cross-attention against a learned combining token, yielding one multimodal vector per patch. Training is fully self-supervised with two objectives: (i) a cross-modal contrastive loss (adapted InfoNCE) that pulls together tokens of the same patch across modalities while excluding spatially proximate negatives within the same tile, and (ii) a multimodal masked auto-encoding loss in which a random subset of tokens is replaced by a learned mask vector and the modality-specific decoders must reconstruct the original inputs, with cloud-affected optical dates filtered out via LTAE attention scores. This patch-level, georeferencing-aligned design lets the model handle an arbitrary number of heterogeneous modalities and improves even unimodal inference through cross-modal pre-training.

## Results

On TreeSatAI-TS (tree species, 20 classes), OmniSat with self-supervised pre-training achieves 74.2 weighted F1 using all three modalities at 100% labels, outperforming the best baseline PSE+LTAE (71.2) by 3.0 points and the DOFA foundation model (71.3) by 2.9 points. In the semi-supervised 10% label setting, OmniSat reaches 61.1 F1 versus 59.4 for PSE+LTAE and 41.8 for DOFA. On PASTIS-HD (crop type, 18 classes), OmniSat achieves 69.9 macro F1 at 100% labels, surpassing CROMA (60.1) by 9.8 points. On FLAIR (land cover), OmniSat reaches 73.4 F1, slightly below DOFA (74.9) but above ScaleMAE (70.0) and UT&T (48.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSat outperforms all 12 baselines on the TreeSatAI-TS and PASTIS-HD datasets in the multimodal setting. Against the strongest temporal baseline PSE+LTAE [35], OmniSat gains 3.0 F1 points at 100% labels and 1.7 at 10% on TreeSatAI-TS, and 23.0 points on PASTIS-HD (69.9 vs 46.9). The DOFA foundation model [96] (111M params, closed-source training) is the only method that beats OmniSat, achieving 74.9 F1 on FLAIR versus OmniSat's 73.4, but OmniSat surpasses it by 2.9 points on TreeSatAI-TS and 14.2 points on PASTIS-HD while using only ~10M parameters. Among self-supervised methods, OmniSat clearly dominates CROMA [29] (74.2 vs 61.0 on TreeSatAI-TS; 69.9 vs 60.1 on PASTIS-HD), ScaleMAE [74] (74.2 vs 60.4; 69.9 vs 42.2; 73.4 vs 70.0), and SatMAE [20] (70.4 vs 61.5 on TreeSatAI). Late-fusion baselines (UT&T [31], UTAE [34], ResNet50 [47]) are consistently outperformed, confirming the benefit of learned end-to-end multimodal fusion over simple concatenation. Notably, even without pre-training, OmniSat already matches or exceeds most baselines, and the cross-modal pre-training adds 8.9 F1 points in the 10% label regime on TreeSatAI-TS.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniSat sits at the intersection of self-supervised EO representation learning (SatMAE, ScaleMAE, CROMA) and multimodal contrastive pre-training from computer vision (ImageBind, Omnivore). It departs from prior multimodal EO models that either map all modalities to a single pivot or a shared latent space, instead merging patch-level tokens from heterogeneous sensors into a unified representation via a transformer combiner. The temporal encoder reuses LTAE, the contrastive objective is a geospatial adaptation of InfoNCE that exploits patch-level alignment from georeferencing, and the image encoder borrows SegNet's index-bypass trick to decouple spatial from spectral learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two new three-modality datasets (TreeSatAI-TS, PASTIS-HD) fill a clear gap in the EO benchmark landscape, where prior datasets contained at most one data type (mono-date or time series). The finding that cross-modal self-supervised pre-training substantially improves even unimodal inference (up to 17.5% F1 gain with 10% labels) suggests a practical path for regions where only one sensor is available at test time. Key limitations remain: all experiments are European, the method's gains shrink with fewer modalities (FLAIR is bimodal), and the approach requires aligned multi-sensor coverage that may be unavailable in developing regions—precisely where label scarcity is most acute.
