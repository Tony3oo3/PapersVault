## Problem

Existing referring expression segmentation (RES) methods are fully supervised, requiring expensive large-scale mask annotations, and fail to generalize to unseen or zero-shot scenarios. The only prior weakly-supervised approach (Partial-RES) still relies on 100% bounding box annotations and fully-supervised REC pretraining, making it unrealistic for practical low-annotation settings. SAFARI is the first to tackle a truly weakly-supervised RES task where both mask and box annotations are equally limited (box% = mask%), with no REC pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241184/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form referring expression (text sentence) as input and produces a segmentation mask via auto-regressive contour point prediction rather than independent per-pixel classification, preserving structural relationships between neighboring boundary points. A secondary output is a zero-shot bounding box generated by the SPARC module (CLIP-based proposal scoring with red-box visual prompts and rule-based spatial reasoning), which is used internally for mask validity filtering rather than as a final prediction. The contour-based formulation is specifically chosen because pixel-wise prediction lacks structural information and degrades sharply under limited annotations.

## Method

SAFARI introduces three key components. First, the X-FACT module injects lightweight normalized gated cross-attention directly into the layers of a Swin-B image encoder (no extra fusion-specific layers), with a learnable gating parameter β initialized to 0 to preserve unimodal representations; this is paired with an Attention Mask Consistency Regularization (AMCR) loss that has a localization term (constraining cross-attention mass within the GT mask) and a collapse-reduction term (KL divergence against a uniform distribution) to prevent attention from collapsing. Second, a bootstrapping pipeline with γ-scheduling iteratively retrains the model on GT masks plus validated pseudo-masks, where the pseudo-mask loss weight γ increases from 0.9 to 1.0 over retraining steps to progressively trust model predictions more. Third, Mask Validity Filtering (MVF) uses SPARC—a zero-shot REC module combining CLIP scoring with red-box visual prompts, Gaussian background blur, and a rule-based spatial reasoning component (parsing noun-chunk relations like left/right/above/below)—to generate bounding boxes and reject pseudo-masks whose DSC with the SPARC box falls below τ=0.1.

## Results

In the fully-supervised setting, SAFARI achieves 75.02 mIoU on RefCOCO@testA, 65.81 mIoU on RefCOCO+@testA, and 62.74 mIoU on RefCOCOg@test-u, outperforming the previous SOTA SeqTR by 5.23, 6.88, and 7.10 mIoU respectively. In the weakly-supervised setting with only 30% mask and box annotations, SAFARI reaches 59.31 mIoU on RefCOCO+@testA, surpassing the fully-supervised SeqTR (58.93) while using 70% fewer annotations. On zero-shot Referring Video Object Segmentation, SAFARI-30 achieves 55.3 J&F on RefDAVIS17 and 38.1 mIoU on JHMDB, outperforming Partial-RES-30 by 3.0 and 3.3 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the fully-supervised SOTA SeqTR [58], SAFARI wins by a large margin across all splits: +5.23 mIoU on RefCOCO@testA, +6.88 on RefCOCO+@testA, and +7.10 on RefCOCOg@test-u, attributable to the X-FACT cross-modal fusion and AMCR regularization. Even when compared to PolyFormer [30] (which is trained on additional combined RefCOCO data and pretrained on REC), SAFARI still leads by 0.74–2.50 mIoU on RefCOCO and 0.02–1.18 mIoU on RefCOCO+/g. In the weakly-supervised setting, SAFARI outperforms Partial-RES [41] at every label rate (10%, 20%, 30%) despite Partial-RES having access to 100% bounding boxes and a fully-supervised REC pretraining stage; at 30% labels, SAFARI gains +0.78 mIoU on RefCOCO@testA and +1.15 on RefCOCO+@testA over Partial-RES. On zero-shot Referring Video Object Segmentation, SAFARI-30 surpasses Partial-RES-30 by 3.0 J&F on RefDAVIS17 and 3.3 mIoU on JHMDB, and outperforms the fully-supervised SeqTR in zero-shot transfer by 7.8 J&F and 8.3 mIoU. The SPARC module also outperforms the prior SOTA RedCircle [46] on zero-shot REC by 39.3% on RefCOCO@testB and 16.2% on RefCOCO+@testB.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFARI builds directly on SeqTR's auto-regressive contour-prediction framework for RES, but departs from the closest prior work (Partial-RES) by rejecting its reliance on 100% bounding-box annotations and fully-supervised REC pretraining. Instead, it introduces a lightweight gated cross-attention fusion (X-FACT) that injects cross-modal interaction into existing Swin/RoBERTa backbones with minimal parameter overhead, replacing heavier fusion stacks like GLIP's. The Mask Validity Filtering stage replaces Partial-RES's GT-box-based filtering with SPARC, a zero-shot CLIP-based REC module augmented with rule-based spatial reasoning, enabling pseudo-label validation without any additional box annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first RES framework to operate under the realistic constraint that mask and box annotations are equally scarce (10–30%), making it directly applicable to settings where full annotation budgets are unavailable. The demonstrated zero-shot transfer to referring video object segmentation (RefDAVIS17, JHMDB) without any temporal modeling suggests the learned spatial-linguistic grounding is genuinely transferable. Open questions include whether the rule-based spatial reasoning in SPARC can be replaced by learned mechanisms for more complex relational expressions, and whether the bootstrapping pipeline extends naturally to multi-image or video-native settings, which the authors identify as future work.
