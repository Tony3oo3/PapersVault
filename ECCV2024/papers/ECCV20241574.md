## Problem

Existing methods for extracting multiple concepts from a single image (e.g., Break-A-Scene, MCPL) rely heavily on human-provided supervision such as object masks, concept-descriptive captions, carefully chosen initial words, and known instance counts. This paper introduces Unsupervised Concept Extraction (UCE), a strictly unsupervised setting where no prior knowledge about the image's contents is available, and asks whether a frozen pretrained diffusion model can automatically discover and recreate individual concepts from a compositional scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241574/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image containing multiple salient instances as input, with no masks, captions, concept names, or instance count provided. It outputs a set of N learnable embedding vectors (conceptual tokens) where N is automatically determined by the clustering procedure, along with corresponding latent masks and mean attention activation maps. The tokens are used with the prompt 'a photo of [V_i]' to regenerate individual concepts or compositional scenes via the frozen Stable Diffusion v2-1 model, and the self-attention for localization is computed at timestep 0 with an unconditional prompt to minimize textual intervention.

## Method

ConceptExpress has two core components. First, a three-phase concept localization pipeline aggregates U-Net self-attention maps across layers, applies FINCH hierarchical clustering with mean KL divergence distance (pre-clustering), filters non-salient backgrounds using the end-of-text cross-attention magnitude threshold, and performs post-clustering with two adaptive constraints (intra-cluster distance cutoff and spatial adjacency) to automatically determine the number of instance-level concepts. Second, a concept-wise masked denoising optimization trains each token via a lookup table associating it with its mask and attention map, using a split-and-merge strategy (g=5 randomly initialized tokens per concept with a contrastive loss pulling same-concept embeddings together, then averaging) to overcome the absence of initial words, plus an EMD regularization that aligns each token's cross-attention map with the corresponding self-attention centroid to prevent cross-concept activation leakage.

## Results

On D2 (7 Break-A-Scene images) with CLIP, ConceptExpress achieves SIM^I=0.715, SIM^C=0.737, ACC^1=0.566, and ACC^3=0.783, outperforming the adapted unsupervised baseline BaS† (0.613, 0.653, 0.368, 0.487) by a large margin on all four metrics. With DINO, the gap widens further: ConceptExpress reaches ACC^1=0.803 and ACC^3=0.934 on D2 versus BaS†'s 0.329 and 0.559. On the larger D1 dataset (96 Unsplash images), ConceptExpress also leads BaS† on every metric (e.g., SIM^I 0.689 vs. 0.627, ACC^1 0.263 vs. 0.174 with CLIP). For concept localization on CLEVR, the automatic method achieves IoU 57.3 and Precision 89.7, surpassing FINCH (best IoU 54.8 at 5 clusters) and k-means (best IoU 52.8 at 4 clusters) while automatically determining the concept count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary unsupervised baseline BaS† (Break-A-Scene adapted to remove all human annotations), ConceptExpress wins decisively on every metric across both datasets and both encoders (CLIP and DINO). The most striking gap is in classification accuracy on D2 with DINO: ConceptExpress achieves ACC^1=0.803 and ACC^3=0.934 versus BaS†'s 0.329 and 0.559, indicating far superior concept disentanglement. Notably, ConceptExpress (fully unsupervised) also outperforms the original supervised BaS f.t. on D2 with DINO (ACC^1 0.803 vs. 0.697, ACC^3 0.934 vs. 0.737), suggesting the unsupervised approach with self-attention-based localization is more effective than relying on ground-truth masks and hand-picked initializers. For concept localization on CLEVR, the automatic FINCH-based method (IoU 57.3, Precision 89.7) outperforms both k-means and fixed-cluster FINCH in IoU and Precision, though it trades off some Recall (89.1 vs. 97.9 for FINCH-7) due to occasional merging of two concepts into one. The training-free MaskCut (CutLER) achieves comparable IoU (58.1) but lower Precision (77.0), and requires a predefined cluster number unlike ConceptExpress's automatic determination.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConceptExpress extends the concept extraction line initiated by Break-A-Scene [2] by removing all supervision signals (object masks, initial words, concept count) and instead leveraging the spatial correspondence already encoded in Stable Diffusion's self-attention layers, following the aggregation strategy from DiffSeg [65]. For token learning, it builds on Textual Inversion [16]'s single-embedding optimization but generalizes to multiple simultaneously learned tokens via a split-and-merge initialization strategy and a masked denoising loss adapted from Break-A-Scene. The concept localization pipeline repurposes FINCH [57] hierarchical clustering with a novel three-phase design (pre-clustering, EOT cross-attention filtering, constrained post-clustering) that automatically determines the number of concepts without any user-specified count.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing UCE as a task requiring no human knowledge of the concepts, the paper opens a more practical and scalable path toward automatic concept extraction from arbitrary images, removing the costly annotation bottleneck that limits Break-A-Scene and MCPL. The finding that a fully unsupervised pipeline can match fully supervised Break-A-Scene (Fig. 8) suggests that pretrained diffusion models already encode sufficient spatial-semantic structure for concept disentanglement, potentially reducing the need for manual annotation in personalization pipelines. Key limitations include the small evaluation scale (96 images, single GPU), reliance on CLIP/DINO for similarity metrics that may not capture fine-grained identity, and the restriction to concrete object-level concepts rather than abstract or compositional ones. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly cluttered scenes with many overlapping concepts and whether the self-attention-based localization remains reliable for non-rigid or texture-poor objects.
