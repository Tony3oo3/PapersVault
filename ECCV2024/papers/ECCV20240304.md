## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods either treat a multi-object scene as a single undivided NeRF (preventing per-object editing) or generate independent objects that lack spatially plausible interactions because they require 3D bounding boxes as input conditions. This paper addresses the missing capability of disentangling an already-generated multi-object NeRF into independently editable meshes while preserving the complex interactions between objects (e.g., an astronaut riding a kangaroo).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240304/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a pre-generated multi-object text-to-3D NeRF (e.g., from DreamFusion) along with category-specific text prompts derived from the original scene prompt (e.g., 'a [v1] sitting on a [v2]' yields prompts 'a [v1]' and 'a [v2]'). It produces multiple independent textured surface meshes via DMTet isosurface extraction, where each mesh corresponds to one object category and the spatial interactions between objects are preserved. A single rendered view of the input NeRF is also used to generate segmentation masks (via Grounded-SAM) for the Deep Concept Mining stage.

## Method

The core representation is the Neural Category Field (NeCF), an MLP with softmax output that assigns each 3D point a probability distribution over object categories, effectively decomposing the frozen density field into per-category sub-densities without training additional density or color networks. Training uses Category Score Distillation Sampling (CSDS), a set of SDS losses each conditioned on a category-specific prompt, but this alone fails when the category prompt generates a different object variant than what appears in the scene (the 'concept gap'). To resolve this, Deep Concept Mining (DCM) personalizes the Stable Diffusion backbone and text embeddings via a masked diffusion loss on a single rendered view, aligning the diffusion model's output with the actual object appearance in the NeRF. After disentanglement, sub-NeRFs are converted to DMTets and fine-tuned with the DCM model for geometry refinement, followed by an optional color fine-tuning step with the original Stable Diffusion to correct over-saturation artifacts.

## Results

DreamDissector is evaluated using CLIP Score (cosine similarity between text and image embeddings) on both independent and composed objects. On CLIP-B-16, DreamDissector achieves 0.316, outperforming the Negative Prompting baseline (0.299) and the Composition baseline (0.281). On CLIP-L-14, the gap widens: DreamDissector reaches 0.270 versus 0.247 (Negative Prompting) and 0.234 (Composition). Qualitatively, DreamDissector successfully disentangles scenes with complex interactions (e.g., an octopus playing piano, an astronaut riding a kangaroo) and produces higher-quality textures after DMTet refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamDissector is compared against two quantitative baselines (Negative Prompting and Composition) and two qualitative baselines (vanilla CSDS without DCM, and SA3D). On all three CLIP Score metrics, DreamDissector outperforms both baselines, with the largest margin over Composition (+0.035 on CLIP-B-16, +0.036 on CLIP-L-14) and a smaller but consistent margin over Negative Prompting (+0.017 on CLIP-B-16, +0.023 on CLIP-L-14). In the qualitative disentanglement analysis (Figure 6), vanilla CSDS fails on scenes with concept gaps (e.g., a blue poison-dart frog on a water lily), while SA3D handles concept gaps but struggles with extensive occlusions (e.g., a beagle, an octopus). DreamDissector with DCM successfully disentangles both concept-gap and heavy-occlusion cases, demonstrating that the personalized diffusion model is critical for robust category separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamDissector builds directly on the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework from DreamFusion, extending it to a category-conditioned variant (CSDS) that supervises a new Neural Category Field (NeCF) representation for decomposing a unified NeRF into per-object sub-NeRFs. The Deep Concept Mining (DCM) module adapts the masked diffusion loss from Break-a-scene to personalize a Stable Diffusion backbone, addressing a newly identified 'concept gap' where category-specific prompts occupy different latent regions than the full scene prompt. For the refinement stage, the paper reuses Magic3D's DMTet conversion pipeline. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multi-object generation methods (CompoNeRF, Comp3D) that require explicit 3D bounding boxes as input, instead adopting a post-hoc disentanglement strategy that preserves the spatial interactions already learned in a unified NeRF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in text-to-3D workflows: enabling object-level editing (re-texturing, repositioning, replacement) of scenes with complex interactions like 'an astronaut riding a kangaroo,' which prior methods cannot produce without manual bounding-box specification. The identification of the concept gap in diffusion models is a useful diagnostic that may inform future work on compositional generation. However, the method is inherently two-stage (requires a pre-generated multi-object NeRF), relies on Grounded-SAM for mask generation, and the quantitative evaluation is limited to CLIP scores with only two weak baselines (negative prompting and a composition baseline), since the most relevant competitors (CompoNeRF, Comp3D) are not open-source. Open questions include whether the disentanglement approach scales to scenes with many objects, whether DCM's personalization generalizes beyond single-view masks, and whether the NeCF probability-simplex formulation handles overlapping or semi-transparent objects well.
