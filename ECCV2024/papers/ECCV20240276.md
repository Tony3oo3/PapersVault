## Problem

DETR-based 3D detectors for point clouds have not matched the performance of non-DETR SOTA methods, primarily due to two unresolved challenges: (1) selecting appropriate object queries from the high sparsity and uneven distribution of point clouds, and (2) performing effective query interaction that exploits the rich geometric structure of 3D objects. Existing approaches either select queries in a single step without considering localization quality or use attention mechanisms that do not leverage box geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240276/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes single-frame or multi-frame LiDAR point clouds (64-beam on Waymo, 32-beam on nuScenes) as input, which are quantized into voxels and processed by a sparse 3D convolution backbone to produce BEV feature maps. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with class labels, confidence scores, and heading angles, produced end-to-end without NMS. Three model sizes (SEED-S/B/L) vary the backbone depth, channel width, and voxel resolution, with SEED-L using a finer voxel size (0.08×0.08×0.15 m) to boost small-object detection.

## Method

SEED introduces two key modules. The Dual Query Selection (DQS) module operates in a coarse-to-fine manner: it first retains a large proportion (r=0.3) of BEV positions as coarse queries by confidence score to ensure high recall, passes them through one SEED decoder layer for feature enhancement, then selects the top-Nf=1000 queries by a combined quality score that weights classification confidence and 3D IoU localization. The Deformable Grid Attention (DGA) module divides each reference box into a k×k grid of sampling points, adds learned per-grid offsets to create a flexible receptive field, and aggregates BEV features at these deformed positions with predicted attention weights—combining the geometric structure of box attention with the adaptability of deformable attention. Training uses a Quality-aware Hungarian Matching that replaces classification scores with quality scores in the assignment cost, and the full decoder stacks six SEED decoder layers with self-attention and DGA cross-attention.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset validation split (100% training data), SEED-L achieves 75.5 mAP / 73.5 mAPH (L2), setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the previous best DSVT-Voxel (74.0/72.1) by 1.4 mAPH/L2. The smaller SEED-S variant already outperforms all prior DETR-based methods, beating ConQueR by 3.1 mAPH/L2 (70.8 vs. 67.7) and FocalFormer3D by 1.8 mAPH/L2 (70.8 vs. 69.0), while maintaining ~13.5 FPS on an RTX 3090. On nuScenes validation, SEED achieves 71.2 NDS and 66.6 mAP, exceeding TransFusion-L by 1.1 NDS and 1.5 mAP under the same 3D backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among DETR-based 3D detectors, SEED-S (70.8 mAPH/L2) decisively outperforms ConQueR (67.7) by 3.1 and FocalFormer3D (69.0) by 1.8, while also running faster (~13.5 FPS vs. slower multi-stage attention). Against non-DETR methods, SEED-B (72.8) surpasses the two-stage PV-RCNN++ (69.5) by 3.3 mAPH/L2, and SEED-L (73.5) exceeds the previous SOTA DSVT-Voxel (72.1) by 1.4 mAPH/L2 despite using a lower BEV resolution. On the Waymo test set with 3 frames, SEED-L achieves 77.3 mAPH/L2, beating PillarNeXt (74.1) by 3.2. On nuScenes, SEED (71.2 NDS / 66.6 mAP) outperforms TransFusion-L (70.1/65.1) by 1.1 NDS and 1.5 mAP, and Uni3DETR (68.5/61.7) by 2.7 NDS and 4.9 mAP. SEED does not directly compare against DSVT-Voxel on nuScenes, and the PillarNet entry in Table 1 (77.3/74.6) appears to use a different evaluation protocol or includes test-time augmentation, as its per-class numbers are inconsistent with its overall score.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEED extends the DETR paradigm (Carion et al., 2020) to 3D LiDAR point clouds by addressing two specific weaknesses in prior DETR-based 3D detectors: one-step Top-N query selection that ignores query quality, and cross-attention mechanisms that underutilize the geometric structure of 3D objects. The deformable grid attention (DGA) module synthesizes ideas from Deformable DETR (Zhu et al., 2021) — predicted offsets for flexible receptive fields — and BoxeR (Nguyen et al., 2022) — dividing reference boxes into uniform grids — into a single local attention operation. The dual query selection departs from the single-stage selection used by TransFusion, ConQueR, and FocalFormer3D by introducing a coarse-to-fine two-stage process that first ensures recall via confidence scores and then refines via quality scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEED establishes a new SOTA on both Waymo (75.5 mAPH/L2) and nuScenes (71.2 NDS) with a clean, modular architecture whose components (DQS, DGA, QHM) are individually ablated and could be adopted by other DETR-based 3D detectors. The finding that combining box geometry with deformable offsets outperforms either mechanism alone provides a practical design principle for query interaction in sparse 3D settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond LiDAR to camera or multi-sensor inputs, whether the voxel-based backbone dependency limits scalability, and whether the quality-aware Hungarian matching can be simplified without performance loss.
