## Problem

SOTA lidar 3D object detectors require expensive manual 3D bounding box annotations that become outdated with sensor changes. Existing self-supervised alternatives impose heavy requirements—full camera coverage with precise calibration, auxiliary optical flow engines, or high-precision GPS for cross-drive object matching—making them impractical for many datasets and robotics settings. This paper asks whether a SOTA lidar detector can be trained from scratch using only unlabeled lidar point cloud sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242303/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, unlabeled sequences of lidar point clouds (no cameras, GPS, or annotations required) and is agnostic to sensor model and mounting pose. The final trained detector operates on single-frame point clouds and outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized as (x, y, z, l, w, h, θ, c)—center, dimensions, heading, and confidence—with no class labels predicted. All animate object categories (cars, trucks, pedestrians, cyclists, etc.) are mapped to a single 'movable' class for evaluation.

## Method

LISO proceeds in three stages: (1) preprocessing each frame pair with JCP ground removal, KISS-ICP ego-motion estimation, and SLIM self-supervised lidar scene flow; (2) mining high-precision initial pseudo ground truth by clustering residual flow vectors in 6D (position + flow) with DBSCAN, fitting boxes oriented along motion, then running a flow-based tracker with a strict 1.5 m matching threshold and post-filtering tracks shorter than 4 steps; (3) trajectory-regularized self-training, where a single-frame detector (CenterPoint or Transfusion-L) is trained on the pseudo GT, then re-inferred over all sequences, re-tracked, and re-optimized (minimizing translational jerk) to regenerate improved pseudo GT, repeating every 30k steps with network weights dropped every 2nd iteration. The key insight is that motion cues enable very strict, high-precision initial proposals, while the single-frame detector—lacking any motion input—naturally generalizes from moving to movable objects across self-training iterations. The same hyperparameters work across four datasets and two detector architectures without modification.

## Results

On Argoverse 2, LISO-CP achieves BEV-IOU AP@0.3 of 0.448 and 3D-IOU AP@0.3 of 0.367, outperforming the best self-training baseline Oyster-CP (0.381 / 0.150) by 17% and 145% respectively, while supervised CenterPoint reaches 0.778 / 0.736. On Waymo Open Dataset, LISO-TF attains Movable BEV AP@0.4 of 0.327, surpassing Oyster-CP (0.292) and SeMoLi (0.195), though supervised CenterPoint reaches 0.765. On KITTI, LISO-CP scores BEV-IOU AP@0.3 of 0.446 versus Oyster-CP's 0.235 and supervised CP's 0.755. Across all four datasets, LISO consistently outperforms all self-supervised baselines on movable objects, with the largest gaps observed on nuScenes (sparse 32-beam lidar).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LISO outperforms Oyster (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023) across all datasets: on AV2, LISO-CP beats Oyster-CP by 6.7 AP points on BEV-IOU@0.3 (0.448 vs 0.381) and by 21.7 points on 3D-IOU@0.3 (0.367 vs 0.150); on KITTI, LISO-CP scores 0.446 vs Oyster-CP's 0.235 BEV AP@0.3. Against SeMoLi (Seidenschwarz et al., 2024), LISO-TF wins on Movable BEV AP@0.4 on WOD (0.327 vs 0.195) but SeMoLi retains an edge on Moving objects (0.575 vs 0.349), confirming LISO's key advantage in generalizing from moving to movable objects. LISO also substantially outperforms the motion-cue distillation baselines RSF (Deng et al., WACV 2023) and DBSCAN (Ester et al., KDD 1996) by large margins (e.g., 0.448 vs 0.074 BEV AP@0.3 on AV2 for LISO-CP vs RSF). The gap to fully supervised CenterPoint/Transfusion-L remains significant (roughly 40–55% of supervised AP), which the authors attribute to the difficulty of generating pseudo ground truth for very sparse, distant objects that are rarely in motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

LISO builds on the self-supervised lidar scene flow line of work (SLIM) for motion cue extraction and the pseudo-ground-truth mining paradigm (Najibi et al., SeMoLi) that distills moving objects from frame pairs. It departs most directly from Oyster, the closest prior self-training method, by replacing raw DBSCAN clustering with scene-flow-informed clustering, introducing a flow-based tracker that explicitly accounts for ego motion, and adding trajectory-regularized iterative retraining. Unlike prior approaches requiring calibrated cameras, high-precision GPS, or multi-drive recordings of the same location, LISO operates solely on unlabeled lidar sequences, making it applicable to datasets and sensor configurations where those auxiliary signals are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive 3D bounding box annotations while achieving consistent gains over all prior self-supervised baselines across four datasets and two architectures with a single hyperparameter set, LISO offers a practical path to training SOTA lidar detectors in settings where annotation is infeasible (robotics, new sensor deployments, domain shifts). The key open limitation is the inability to predict class labels, and a substantial performance gap to supervised training persists—particularly for sparse, distant objects where pseudo-ground-truth generation is hardest. Future work on class-agnostic-to-class-specific pseudo-label generation (e.g., via size or motion heuristics) is the natural next step to close this gap.
