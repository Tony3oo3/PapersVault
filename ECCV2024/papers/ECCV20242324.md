## Problem

Global optimization of continuous MRFs with variables on nonconvex manifolds (e.g., the unit sphere) and nonsmooth geodesic coupling terms is extremely challenging: the natural convex lifting via the local marginal polytope is an infinite-dimensional linear program, and existing polynomial-relaxation approaches either require polynomial cost functions or lack convergence guarantees. This paper closes that gap by providing an implementable semidefinite hierarchy with provable O(1/n) convergence for geodesic penalties on the sphere, yielding certified lower bounds on the optimal cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy signal whose values lie on a Riemannian manifold (S² for chromaticity, S¹ for angles, SE(2) for poses), a graph encoding pairwise couplings (pixel grid or pose graph), and cost functions consisting of a data-fidelity term and a geodesic-distance coupling. It produces a denoised manifold-valued signal obtained by projecting degree-1 moments back onto the manifold, together with a certified lower bound (dual value) that quantifies the duality gap. For pose graph optimization, the output is a set of estimated poses that can be further refined by a local Riemannian optimizer. The relaxation is implemented as a finite SDP via MOSEK, with problem size controlled by the polynomial degree n of the dual variables.

## Method

The paper lifts the nonconvex MRF to the local marginal polytope (an infinite-dimensional LP over product probability measures) and derives its dual using optimal transport duality. For geodesic coupling terms f_{(u,v)} = d, the Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality reduces the dual to maximizing over 1-Lipschitz continuous functions on the manifold, yielding the 'metric dual' (mR-D). The key implementation step restricts dual variables to polynomials of degree n on the ambient Euclidean space and enforces the Lipschitz constraint via a sum-of-squares certificate on the manifold gradient condition ‖∇_Ω φ(x)‖ ≤ 1, turning the problem into a tractable SDP. The central theoretical contribution is proving that every L-Lipschitz function on S^m can be uniformly approximated by L-Lipschitz polynomials at rate O(1/n), which implies the duality gap vanishes sublinearly. The degree-1 stage of the hierarchy is shown to coincide with a natural Fenchel-biconjugate convexification that preserves the cost on the manifold boundary, distinguishing it from naive Euclidean convexification.

## Results

On three chromaticity denoising experiments (Rooftop, Flowers, Butterfly) with a 1000-second runtime budget, the proposed SDP relaxation at degree 2 achieves primal costs of 3228.25, 5565.06, and 5288.99, with near-zero duality gaps of (2.479, 9.089, 0.959)×10⁻⁵ upon full convergence. These primal costs are consistently lower than all LP-discretization baselines (e.g., 3228.25 vs. 3288.05 for TRW-S[150] on Rooftop). For the S¹-valued toy problem, the method is competitive but faces a worst-case 180° jump scenario where higher-label LP relaxations eventually match or slightly exceed its cost. On pose graph optimization (INTEL, MIT, M3500b), the degree-2 relaxation followed by ManOpt local refinement recovers near ground-truth poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two LP-discretization baselines: TRW-S (tree-reweighted message passing, [19]) and LR-Solver (combinatorial Frank-Wolfe, [20]), each evaluated at 150 and 200 labels. The proposed SDP relaxation at degree 2 achieves strictly lower primal costs on all three imaging experiments (e.g., 3228.25 vs. 3288.05 for TRW-S[150] on Rooftop, a ~1.8% improvement) and provides near-zero duality gaps, whereas the LP baselines exhibit visible labeling bias and piecewise-constant artifacts. TRW-S[200] and LR-Solver[200] fail to close the duality gap within the 1000-second budget, with LR-Solver[200] producing dual bounds far below the primal (e.g., 2782.90 vs. 3402.78 on Rooftop), indicating poor convergence. On the adversarial S¹ toy problem with a 180° jump, the LP baselines at high label counts (≥100) can match or slightly beat the SDP relaxation at comparable memory, but the SDP method is superior at low degrees (2–4). For pose graph optimization, the method is compared to a local Riemannian optimizer (ManOpt); the SDP relaxation provides a better initialization that, after local refinement, recovers near ground-truth poses on INTEL, MIT, and M3500b datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the polynomial approximation hierarchy for continuous MRFs from the unit interval (Bauermeister et al., 2022; Fix & Agarwal, 2014) to higher-dimensional unit spheres, and introduces a 'metric relaxation' that exploits Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality to handle non-polynomial geodesic coupling terms. The SDP implementation follows the Moment-SOS framework of Henrion et al. (2020), while the theoretical analysis draws on optimal transport duality and Fenchel conjugation. The key departure from prior work is proving O(1/n) convergence for geodesic penalties on S^m by constructing Lipschitz-preserving polynomial approximants (Theorem 6), a result not available in the 1D literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first implementable convex relaxation with provable convergence guarantees for manifold-valued MRFs with geodesic costs, yielding near-zero duality gaps at degree 2 for practical chromaticity denoising—something LP-based discretizations fail to achieve even at 150+ labels. Limitations include reliance on an off-the-shelf SDP solver (MOSEK) without parallelization, restriction to polynomially-encodable manifolds (primarily spheres), and a demonstrated adversarial case on S^1 where 180° jumps degrade performance. Open questions include GPU-accelerated solvers for scaling to larger problems, extension to general Riemannian manifolds beyond spheres, and whether the hierarchy can be made tighter for non-tree graphs where no theoretical guarantee exists.
