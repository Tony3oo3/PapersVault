## Problem

Existing Transformer-based floorplan reconstruction methods suffer from missing or biased corners, self-intersecting or overlapping polygons, and disordered vertex sequences due to random query initialization. These issues stem from sparse corner-based representations that provide inadequate supervision and lack a direct semantic link between queries and room geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes an indoor 3D point cloud, projects it along the gravity axis into a 256×256 top-view density map, and produces a vectorized 2D floorplan as a set of room polygons. Each room is represented as a fixed-length sequence of 40 vertices (up to 20 rooms), where every vertex carries both a 2D position and a binary corner label, enabling dense supervision along all walls rather than only at corners.

## Method

PolyRoom frames reconstruction as progressive refinement of room queries in an encoder-decoder architecture. A pre-trained Mask2Former instance segmentation network predicts room masks, from which 40 uniformly sampled vertices initialize the room queries, establishing a direct geometric-semantic link absent in random initialization. The decoder (6 layers) employs room-aware self-attention that decomposes attention into intra-room (across vertices within one polygon) and inter-room (across polygons) components, reducing complexity from O((MN)²) to O(M²+N²) while improving accuracy over vanilla self-attention. Training uses a uniform sampling representation with dense supervision via L1 coordinate loss, rasterization loss, and an auxiliary angle loss, with bipartite matching computed purely on polygon geometry. Final floorplan extraction combines probability-thresholded corner detection, an angle-threshold filter, and Douglas-Peucker polygonization to yield minimal, non-redundant vertex sets.

## Results

On Structured3D, PolyRoom achieves Corner F1 of 90.2% and Angle F1 of 85.2%, surpassing the previous best (SLIBO-Net) by 4.8% and 0.8% respectively, while maintaining a comparable Room F1 of 98.3%. On SceneCAD, PolyRoom reaches Room IoU of 92.8%, Corner F1 of 91.2%, and Angle F1 of 78.0%, outperforming RoomFormer by 1.1%, 2.4%, and 2.2% on the respective metrics. In cross-dataset generalization (trained on Structured3D, tested on SceneCAD), PolyRoom achieves Room IoU of 85.2% and Corner F1 of 78.9%, substantially exceeding RoomFormer (74.0% / 60.3%) and HEAT (52.5% / 51.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyRoom outperforms all eight baselines on Corner and Angle metrics across both datasets. Against the strongest prior method SLIBO-Net (NeurIPS'23), PolyRoom gains +4.8% Corner F1 (90.2 vs 85.4) and +0.8% Angle F1 (85.2 vs 84.4) on Structured3D, while SLIBO-Net retains a marginal Room F1 edge (98.4 vs 98.3) due to its Manhattan assumption. Compared to RoomFormer (CVPR'23), PolyRoom improves Corner F1 by 3.0% and Angle F1 by 4.0% on Structured3D, and by 2.4% and 2.2% on SceneCAD. Against HEAT (CVPR'22), the gains are even larger: +7.7% Corner F1 and +6.9% Angle F1 on Structured3D. On SceneCAD, PolyRoom leads RoomFormer by +1.1% Room IoU (92.8 vs 91.7) and +2.4% Corner F1 (91.2 vs 88.8). In the cross-dataset generalization test, PolyRoom's advantage is most pronounced, achieving 85.2% Room IoU versus 74.0% for RoomFormer and 52.5% for HEAT, demonstrating the benefit of room-aware query initialization and dense supervision for out-of-distribution robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolyRoom extends the Transformer-based floorplan reconstruction line initiated by HEAT (corner/edge detection) and RoomFormer (two-level polygon queries), but departs from both in its representation and initialization strategy. Where RoomFormer uses sparse corner-based polygons with randomly initialized learnable queries, PolyRoom adopts a uniform sampling representation with fixed-length vertex sequences and initializes room queries via a pre-trained instance segmentation network (Mask2Former), establishing a direct semantic link between queries and room geometry. The room-aware self-attention mechanism, which decomposes attention into intra-room and inter-room components, draws inspiration from the self-attention variant in MapTRv2 for HD map construction, repurposing it for the two-level floorplan structure to reduce quadratic memory overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses persistent practical failures in floorplan reconstruction—missing corners, self-intersecting polygons, and implausible topology—that limit downstream applications in robotics, navigation, and VR/AR. The room-aware query initialization via instance segmentation is a straightforward yet effective design choice that practitioners can adopt in other polygon-sequence prediction tasks. However, the method remains dependent on segmentation quality (failing when room instances are missed) and struggles with complex structures like thin walls, leaving open the question of whether purely end-to-end approaches can eliminate the segmentation dependency. The cross-dataset generalization gap (85.2 vs. 74.0 IoU over RoomFormer) suggests the dense supervision and semantic initialization transfer better than purely learned query representations, an insight relevant to broader structured prediction problems.
