## Problem

Existing unsupervised part discovery methods for fine-grained classification impose restrictive geometric priors—parts must be small, compact, and appear only once per image—which fail on datasets with irregularly shaped or multiply-occurring parts (e.g., flower petals, snake bodies). The paper shows that self-supervised ViT backbones like DINOv2 carry sufficient inductive bias to support a far more flexible total variation prior that only requires parts to be piece-wise constant connected components, without constraining shape or size.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and produces K+1 spatial attention maps of size H×W (one per discovered foreground part plus one background map), each with values in [0,1] summing to 1 across channels at every pixel, along with a class probability vector over C categories. The attention maps are computed by applying Gumbel-Softmax over negative squared Euclidean distances between DINOv2 ViT-B patch tokens and K+1 learnable prototypes, so the output is a soft partition of the image into K+1 semantically consistent regions. Part embedding vectors are derived as attention-weighted averages of patch features, modulated by per-part LayerNorm, and fed through a shared linear classifier whose per-part scores are averaged before a final softmax.

## Method

PDiscoFormer builds on the PDiscoNet architecture but replaces the concentration (compactness) loss with a total variation loss that encourages piece-wise constant attention maps without constraining part size or shape, and adds a Gumbel-Softmax stochastic reparameterization to make part assignments effectively discrete. The training objective combines six losses: cross-entropy classification, orthogonality (decorrelating part embeddings), equivariance under random affine transforms, a two-part presence loss (foreground parts must appear in at least one batch image; background must appear in all images, weighted toward borders), an entropy loss enforcing unique part assignment per patch, and the TV loss. The backbone is a DINOv2 ViT-B with register tokens, fine-tuned end-to-end, and per-part LayerNorm modulation plus part dropout ensure each discovered part contributes distinct discriminative information. The key empirical finding is that this flexible prior, combined with the strong self-supervised representations, substantially outperforms the compactness prior across CUB, PartImageNet, and Oxford Flowers, especially on datasets where parts are large, irregular, or multiply instantiated.

## Results

On CUB (K=16), PDiscoFormer achieves 73.4% NMI, 55.8% ARI, and 88.7% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline PDiscoNet+ViT-B (68.6% NMI, 43.4% ARI, 84.0% top-1). On PartImageNet OOD (K=25), it reaches 44.7% NMI, 59.3% ARI, and 90.8% top-1 accuracy. On Oxford 102 Flowers (K=4), PDiscoFormer achieves 73.3% foreground mIoU and 99.5% top-1 accuracy, while the best competing method (PDiscoNet+ViT-B) scores only 13.2% mIoU and 97.4% top-1. The method consistently improves over all baselines across every dataset and every number of parts tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDiscoFormer outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor, PDiscoNet re-implemented with a ViT-B backbone (PDiscoNet+ViT-B), the gains are most dramatic on Flowers: 73.3% vs 13.2% foreground mIoU (a 60-point gap) and 99.5% vs 97.4% top-1 accuracy, indicating that the concentration prior in PDiscoNet fundamentally fails to segment irregular, multi-instance flower parts. On PartImageNet OOD (K=25), PDiscoFormer improves ARI from 50.4% to 59.3% and NMI from 28.2% to 44.7% over PDiscoNet+ViT-B, while maintaining comparable top-1 accuracy (90.8% vs 90.3%). On CUB (K=16), where compact parts are more natural, PDiscoFormer still leads with 73.4% NMI vs 68.6% and 55.8% ARI vs 43.4%, and notably maintains 88.7% top-1 accuracy where PDiscoNet+ViT-B degrades to 84.0%. Against the original PDiscoNet (CNN backbone), improvements are even larger: e.g., 59.3% vs 10.7% ARI on PartImageNet and 73.3% vs 34.8% mIoU on Flowers. The unsupervised Dino baseline (no class labels) is consistently the weakest, confirming that classification supervision remains essential even with a strong self-supervised ViT backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PDiscoNet (van der Klis et al., ICCV 2023), retaining most of its architecture (prototype-based attention maps, part dropout, equivariance loss) but replacing the concentration-based compactness prior with a total variation (TV) prior that only enforces piece-wise constant, spatially connected components. It departs from the broader part-discovery lineage (Huang et al. 2020, Hung et al. 2019, Marcos et al. 2020) that universally assumes parts are small, compact, and appear once per image—assumptions the authors argue are artifacts of CNN backbones rather than fundamental requirements. The key conceptual shift is that self-supervised DINOv2 ViT backbones carry sufficient inductive bias to make strong geometric priors unnecessary, and that TV regularization (borrowed from classical image segmentation) combined with Gumbel-Softmax and an entropy loss is sufficient to produce coherent part maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the geometric priors dominating part discovery (compactness, single-instance) are not intrinsic to the task but are compensations for weak CNN representations, opening the door to part discovery on objects with irregular shapes and multiple instances (flowers, snakes, plants) that prior methods simply cannot handle. Open questions include automatic selection of the number of parts K, handling multiple objects per image, and whether the TV-prior insight generalizes beyond the three tested datasets to larger, more diverse benchmarks. The finding that PDiscoNet re-implemented with the same ViT backbone still substantially underperforms PDiscoFormer (e.g., 13.2% vs 73.6% foreground mIoU on Flowers) underscores that the prior choice, not just the backbone, is the critical design decision.
