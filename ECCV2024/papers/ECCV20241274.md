## Problem

Supervised low-light image enhancement methods require large-scale paired datasets and suffer from poor generalization to unseen scenes, while zero-shot diffusion-based restoration is limited to known degradation modes and produces under-enhanced results. The paper addresses how to train a diffusion model in an unsupervised manner on unpaired data while incorporating physically interpretable Retinex decomposition to achieve both high visual quality and strong generalization across diverse real-world illumination conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241274/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-light RGB image as input and produces an enhanced normal-light RGB image at the same spatial resolution. During training, unpaired low-light and normal-light images are encoded into a shared latent space via a cascaded encoder that downsamples by a factor of 2^k (k=3, yielding 8× spatial reduction), where all decomposition and diffusion operations occur. At inference, only the low-light image is needed; the diffusion model is guided by its encoded latent feature to generate the restored result, which is decoded back to full resolution.

## Method

The framework consists of three key components: (1) a Content-Transfer Decomposition Network (CTDN) that performs Retinex decomposition in latent space rather than image space, using cross-attention to transfer illumination information into the reflectance branch and self-attention to extract residual content from the illumination branch, yielding content-rich reflectance and content-free illumination maps; (2) a Latent-Retinex Diffusion Model (LRDM) that takes the Hadamard product of the low-light reflectance and normal-light illumination as the diffusion input, with the low-light latent feature serving as conditional guidance during reverse denoising via a U-Net noise estimator; and (3) a self-constrained consistency loss that compares the diffusion output against a pseudo-label constructed from the low-light image's own reflectance and gamma-corrected illumination, preventing the normal-light content from leaking into the restored result. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the encoder, CTDN, and decoder are trained on paired low-light images from SICE with reconstruction and decomposition losses; second, the diffusion model is trained on ~180k unpaired low/normal-light image pairs with the combined diffusion and consistency objective.

## Results

On the paired LSRW dataset (unseen during training), LightenDiffusion achieves the best PSNR (18.555) and SSIM (0.539) among all methods, surpassing supervised baselines SMG (17.579 PSNR) and PyDiff (17.264 PSNR). On the three unpaired real-world benchmarks, LightenDiffusion attains the best NIQE and PI scores across all datasets: DICM (NIQE 3.724, PI 3.144), NPE (NIQE 3.618, PI 2.879), and VV (NIQE 2.941, PI 2.558), outperforming the strongest unsupervised competitor NeRCo by 0.38–0.82 in NIQE. On the paired LOL dataset (where supervised methods are trained), LightenDiffusion achieves 20.453 PSNR / 0.803 SSIM / 0.192 LPIPS, trailing supervised methods trained on LOL (e.g., SMG at 23.814 PSNR) but leading all unsupervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightenDiffusion outperforms all 10 unsupervised baselines on every dataset. On the unpaired benchmarks where generalization is the key challenge, it beats the strongest unsupervised competitor NeRCo by 0.38 NIQE on DICM (3.724 vs 4.107), 0.28 on NPE (3.618 vs 3.902), and 0.82 on VV (2.941 vs 3.765), with consistent PI improvements as well. On the paired LSRW dataset (unseen during training), LightenDiffusion surpasses all supervised methods including SMG (18.555 vs 17.579 PSNR) and PyDiff (18.555 vs 17.264 PSNR), demonstrating superior generalization. On the paired LOL dataset, where supervised methods are directly trained, LightenDiffusion (20.453 PSNR) trails SMG (23.814) and PyDiff (23.275) but still leads all unsupervised methods by a margin of 0.7–5.1 PSNR over the next-best NeRCo (19.738). The zero-shot diffusion-based GDP method (15.896 PSNR on LOL) is substantially outperformed, confirming that learnable unsupervised training with Retinex guidance is more effective than relying solely on pre-trained diffusion priors. In the downstream face detection task on DARK FACE, LightenDiffusion improves RetinaFace precision from 20.2% (raw) to 36.4%, outperforming all 10 competing LLIE methods in the high-recall region.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Retinex-based decomposition for LLIE (Land 1977, Wei 2018, Wu 2022) and diffusion models for image restoration (Ho 2020, Song 2021). The key departure is performing Retinex decomposition in latent space rather than image space, motivated by the observation that image-space decomposition leaks content into the illumination map. The training strategy borrows from PairLIE (Fu 2023) for the first stage and from conditional diffusion guidance (Chung 2022) for the second, while the CTDN's cross-attention/self-attention architecture and the self-constrained consistency loss are novel contributions specific to this framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a learnable unsupervised diffusion framework can match supervised methods on paired benchmarks and clearly outperform them on unseen scenes, addressing the generalization bottleneck that limits supervised LLIE in practice. The latent-space decomposition insight—that encoding content before separating reflectance and illumination yields cleaner factorization—is a transferable principle that could inform other decomposition-based restoration tasks. Limitations include the need for ~180k unpaired training images, inference speed that is slower than non-diffusion baselines (0.314s vs ~0.29s), and the framework being tailored specifically to the LLIE degradation model rather than generalizing to arbitrary degradations.
