## Problem

Data-driven traffic simulators can generate realistic multi-agent scenarios but lack controllability: they memorize training distributions and cannot produce unseen or condition-specific scenarios. Existing guidance approaches (constraint functions, language prompts) suffer from two limitations—desired scenarios may be absent from long-tail training data, and scalar or linguistic guidance is insufficiently expressive to specify intricate multi-agent interactions. The paper asks whether retrieval-augmented generation, proven effective in LLMs, can be adapted to traffic scenario synthesis to enable flexible, gradient-free controllable generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a small set of user-selected template scenarios (e.g., tagged left-turn or crash examples), the initial poses of up to 11 agents (x, y, v, cos θ, sin θ), and a vector map of 100 lane segments (start/end coordinates per segment). A KNN retriever then fetches K additional similar scenarios from a pre-encoded database. The output is a full 8-second, 2 Hz multi-agent trajectory tensor (M×T×5) that blends behaviors from all retrieved examples while respecting the specified initial configuration and road layout.

## Method

RealGen comprises three components: (1) a spatial-temporal transformer autoencoder trained with reconstruction loss plus an InfoNCE contrastive loss using the Wasserstein-2 distance (approximated via Sinkhorn) to produce behavior embeddings invariant to absolute position and agent ordering; (2) a lightweight Combiner of two multi-head attention layers that fuses K retrieved behavior embeddings with the initial-pose and map embeddings into a single RAG embedding; and (3) the frozen decoder that maps the RAG embedding back to trajectories. The combiner is trained in a KNN-based pipeline: for each query scenario, K nearest neighbors are retrieved, and the combiner is optimized (with encoder/decoder frozen) to reconstruct the query from those retrieved embeddings, effectively learning an 'inverse KNN' operation. At inference, the user supplies template scenarios, the retriever augments them with KNN matches from a large unlabeled database, and the combiner+decoder produce a novel scenario in a single gradient-free forward pass—enabling in-context editing, behavior composition, and safety-critical scenario generation without retraining.

## Results

In retrieval-based generation, RealGen achieves mADE 1.54±0.04 and mFDE 1.21±0.03, substantially outperforming LCTGen (mADE 4.76, mFDE 6.24) and KNN-based baselines (mADE 13–14). In human A/B evaluation, RealGen is preferred 81.8–100% of the time over LCTGen across five driving categories, with absolute scores of 3.94–4.27 versus LCTGen's 2.0–2.42. For safety-critical scenario generation, RealGen achieves a 59% collision rate using crash templates, compared to 83% for random retrieval. As a downstream task, augmenting the nuScenes training set with RealGen-generated scenarios reduces trajectory prediction mADE from 3.544 (original) to 2.309 and collision rate from 0.049 to 0.018.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary controllable-generation baseline is LCTGen [49], a language-conditioned traffic generation model. In retrieval-based generation, RealGen outperforms LCTGen by approximately 3× on mADE (1.54 vs. 4.76) and 5× on mFDE (1.21 vs. 6.24), while also achieving lower scene collision rate (0.05 vs. 0.07) and off-road rate (0.04 vs. 0.07). In human A/B evaluation, RealGen is preferred over LCTGen in 81.8–100% of trials across five driving categories, with absolute quality scores roughly 2× higher (e.g., 4.27 vs. 2.15 for left-turn). The KNN-only baselines (AE-KNN, RealGen-AE-KNN) demonstrate that simply retrieving and decoding a single nearest neighbor without the combiner module yields mADE of 13–14, confirming the combiner's critical role in fusing multiple retrieved behaviors. In the reconstruction-based setting, RealGen-AE (mADE 0.31) is slightly worse than plain AE (0.18) and Masked AE (0.16) due to the contrastive loss removing absolute position information, but this trade-off is by design since RealGen targets retrieval-based generation where the combiner recovers the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigm from large language models into the traffic scenario generation domain, where prior controllable methods (CTG, LCTGen) rely on gradient-based constraint functions or language-conditioned diffusion. Rather than memorizing a training distribution, RealGen learns a contrastive autoencoder to embed scenarios into a latent space and then trains a lightweight two-layer MHA combiner to fuse KNN-retrieved scenario embeddings, enabling in-context generation without retraining. The use of Wasserstein distance for permutation-invariant comparison of multi-agent embeddings is a creative adaptation of optimal transport to the traffic setting, and the overall architecture repurposes standard spatial-temporal transformer encoders in a retrieval-augmented pipeline that has no direct precedent in the autonomous driving literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RealGen opens a practically significant direction for AV development: the scenario database can be updated post-training with new tagged or critical scenarios, enabling continuous improvement without retraining the generative model. The demonstrated ability to compose behaviors from multiple retrieved examples and generate unseen crash scenarios (guided by NHTSA templates) addresses a key limitation of distribution-memorizing generators. However, the behavior encoder captures only agent trajectories and neglects explicit agent-map interactions, which constrains the complexity of generable scenarios. Open questions include whether the combiner generalizes to larger agent counts, longer horizons, or heterogeneous agent types (pedestrians, cyclists), and whether the approach scales beyond the single nuScenes dataset used for evaluation.
