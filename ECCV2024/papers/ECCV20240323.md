## Problem

NeRF-based novel view synthesis typically assumes dense, accurately posed input views, but real-world captures are sparse (3–9 images) with noisy poses from SfM pipelines. Prior methods that jointly optimize poses and radiance fields (BARF, SPARF, CorresNeRF) enforce only pairwise correspondence consistency between two views at a time, failing to exploit the global geometric constraint that all views share a single 3D scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240323/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3, 6, or 9 sparse RGB images (typically 300×400 on DTU) with initial camera poses corrupted by up to 15–35% Gaussian noise, plus feature tracks extracted offline via PDCNet++ dense correspondence. It produces a trained neural radiance field, refined 6-DoF camera extrinsics, and rendered novel views with associated depth maps. Training is two-stage: first jointly optimizing the radiance field and poses, then fine-tuning the radiance field alone with depth regularization enabled.

## Method

TrackNeRF replaces pairwise reprojection losses with a track-wise objective inspired by bundle adjustment in SfM. Feature tracks—connected pixel trajectories across all visible views corresponding to the same 3D landmark—are extracted via PDCNet++ and refined through a track keypoint adjustment step (Eq. 5) that minimizes pixel-wise feature differences along each track. The core track reprojection loss (Eq. 6) back-projects a pixel using NeRF-estimated depth, transforms it through the relative camera pose, and penalizes the reprojection error against the corresponding pixel in another view, summed over all pairs within a track with a Huber robust kernel. A depth regularization term (Eq. 7) aligns disparity gradients with image gradients to suppress floaters. The total loss combines photometric, track reprojection, and depth terms, and the track loss is the key differentiator: it enforces holistic multi-view consistency simultaneously rather than independent pairwise constraints, yielding ~8 PSNR improvement over BARF and ~1 over SPARF on DTU with noisy poses.

## Results

On DTU with 15% Gaussian pose noise, TrackNeRF achieves 18.53 PSNR (19.65 masked) with 3 views, 22.78 PSNR (23.66 masked) with 6 views, and 25.57 PSNR (26.03 masked) with 9 views, outperforming SPARF by ~0.8, ~1.4, and ~0.9 PSNR respectively. Camera pose errors are nearly halved compared to SPARF (e.g., 1.12° rotation and 2.48 translation with 3 views vs. 1.81° and 5.0 for SPARF). TrackNeRF also tolerates up to 35% Gaussian noise before degrading, whereas SPARF fails at 20%. On DTU with ground-truth poses (3 views), TrackNeRF achieves 18.78 PSNR (21.45 masked), surpassing diffusion-based methods ReconFusion (20.74 masked) and ZeroNVS (16.71 masked).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrackNeRF outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the closest competitor SPARF [70], TrackNeRF gains ~0.8–1.4 PSNR (masked) and reduces rotation error by 38–82% (e.g., 1.12° vs 1.81° at 3 views; 0.24° vs 1.31° at 6 views). Against BARF [43] and its variants (RegBARF [54], DistBARF [3]), the improvement is dramatic: ~8 PSNR on DTU 3-view and ~5–8 PSNR on 6/9-view settings, with pose errors reduced by 5–20×. SCNeRF [34] is the second-strongest baseline on pose recovery but still trails TrackNeRF by 3–4× in rotation error and 6–7 PSNR. On the GT-pose DTU 3-view setting (Table 2), TrackNeRF (18.78 PSNR, 21.45 masked) beats CorresNeRF [39] (18.23, 20.58), SPARF (18.30, 21.01), FreeNeRF [79] (20.46 masked), and diffusion-based ReconFusion [76] (20.74 masked) and ZeroNVS [60] (16.71 masked). On the simpler LLFF forward-facing dataset (Table 3), TrackNeRF (19.60 PSNR) marginally edges SPARF (19.58) but does not show the large gains seen on DTU, which the authors attribute to LLFF's limited camera motion saturating the benefit of track-based consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrackNeRF builds on the NeRF volumetric representation and BARF's coarse-to-fine frequency encoding for joint pose and scene optimization, while adopting PixSfM's track-wise keypoint refinement to improve correspondence quality. Its key conceptual departure is from SPARF and CorresNeRF, which enforce only pairwise (two-view) reprojection consistency; TrackNeRF instead extracts feature tracks spanning all visible views and enforces a global, track-wise reprojection loss that mirrors the bundle adjustment objective from classical SfM. This shifts the optimization from local, per-pair constraints to a holistic multiview geometric consistency that simultaneously constrains all cameras in a track.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrackNeRF demonstrates that a simple geometric principle—enforcing that all pixels in a feature track correspond to the same 3D landmark—yields substantial gains in both rendering quality and pose accuracy under sparse, noisy conditions, and can even outperform diffusion-based methods trained on large-scale scene data. The practical robustness to 35% Gaussian pose noise (where SPARF fails at 20%) and faster convergence make it a strong default for real-world sparse-view pipelines. Open questions include its behavior with only two views (where the track loss degenerates to pairwise), dependence on the quality of the dense correspondence network, and whether the track-based formulation transfers cleanly to Gaussian Splatting or other explicit representations.
