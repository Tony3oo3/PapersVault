## Problem

Category-level pose estimation methods trained on synthetic data typically rely on geometric features alone to avoid the RGB domain gap, but geometry cannot disambiguate poses for objects with similar shapes but different semantic parts (e.g., a laptop lid vs. keyboard). Conversely, RGB-based approaches suffer from texture and lighting differences between synthetic and real images. The paper addresses this by asking whether semantic information from a pre-trained foundation model can be lifted into 3D to complement geometry, enabling self-supervised training purely on synthetic CAD models without any real annotated data.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a 480×480 RGB-D crop (from a Mask R-CNN detection) of a novel object instance and a single reference CAD model from the same category. The depth image is back-projected into a partial point cloud (1000 points sampled), while the reference model is represented as a full semantic point cloud (3000 points sampled). The output is a 9D pose: rotation R∈SO(3), translation t∈R³, and per-axis scale s∈R³, recovered via Umeyama alignment and RANSAC on the predicted correspondences. Inference runs in ~51 ms on an RTX 3080.

## Method

GS-Pose constructs 3D semantic point clouds by projecting DINOv2 ViT-S features (384-dim) from multiple rendered views onto a CAD mesh and averaging per-point features across visible views, yielding a texture-invariant semantic representation. A LightGlue-based transformer with self- and cross-attention layers fuses these semantic features with geometric features (point coordinates + positional encoding) to predict correspondences between the full semantic point cloud and a partial input point cloud, along with per-point inlier probabilities that suppress outliers. Training is fully self-supervised: the network matches each synthetic model's semantic point cloud against its own rendered RGB-D partial observations, with ground-truth correspondences obtained from mutual nearest neighbors and symmetry disambiguation via xz-plane constraint. Only 10 synthetic CAD models per category (400 rendered views total) are needed, and the learned matching generalizes zero-shot to novel real objects without fine-tuning.

## Results

On NOCS REAL275, GS-Pose trained on only 10 synthetic CAD models per category achieves 3D₅₀* = 63.2%, 5°5cm = 28.8%, and 10°5cm = 60.1%, outperforming the geometric-only CPPF baseline (210 objects) by 36.8% on 3D₅₀* and 15.2% on 10°5cm. On Wild6D (162 novel objects), the method reaches 3D₂₅+ = 84.6% and 10°5cm = 51.0%, surpassing the semi-supervised RePoNet-semi by 8.5% on 10°5cm without any real training data. On SUN RGB-D (heavily occluded chairs), GS-Pose improves 3D₁₀+ by 20.8% and 3D₂₅+ by 18.4% over CPPF. Inference runs at 51 ms per object on an RTX 3080, compared to 930 ms for CPPF++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the geometric-only CPPF [63] (210 objects), GS-Pose (10 objects) wins decisively: 3D₅₀* improves from 26.4% to 63.2% (+36.8%), 5°5cm from 16.9% to 28.8% (+11.9%), and 15°5cm from 50.8% to 73.6% (+22.8%), despite using 21× fewer training objects. Against CPPF++ [62] with the same 10-object budget, GS-Pose leads on 3D₅₀* (63.2% vs 51.6%, +11.6%) and 5°5cm (28.8% vs 22.6%, +6.2%), while CPPF++ with 210 objects still edges ahead on 15°5cm (85.2% vs 73.6%) and 10°5cm (65.9% vs 60.1%). Against the zero-shot semantic-only ZSP [15], GS-Pose dominates on all rotation/translation metrics (e.g., 10°5cm: 60.1% vs 20.3%). Against real-data-trained methods on NOCS (Table 2), GS-Pose is competitive with DualPoseNet [31] (3D₇₅+: 37.0% vs 30.8%) and ACR-Pose [9] (10°5cm: 60.1% vs 54.8%) but trails the strongest real-data method VI-Net [32] (5°5cm: 28.8% vs 57.6%). On Wild6D, GS-Pose outperforms RePoNet-semi [65] on 10°5cm (51.0% vs 42.5%) while matching DualPoseNet [31] on 3D₂₅+ (84.6% vs 90.0%). On SUN RGB-D, GS-Pose substantially outperforms CPPF [63] across all metrics (e.g., 60°30cm: 69.7% vs 13.1%), demonstrating robustness to heavy occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-Pose builds on the transformer-based matching architecture of LightGlue (adapted from 2D keypoint matching to 3D point cloud correspondence) and the geometric feature embedding strategy of GeoTransformer (using point coordinates with positional encoding rather than hand-crafted PPF descriptors). It departs from the dominant synthetic-training paradigm (CPPF, PPF-Net) that relies solely on geometric features, and from real-data-trained methods (NOCS, VI-Net, DualPoseNet) that require expensive pose annotations, by introducing a self-supervised pipeline that lifts 2D DINOv2 semantic features onto 3D CAD point clouds and matches them against rendered partial observations. This positions the work at the intersection of foundation-model feature extraction, 3D point cloud registration, and category-level pose estimation, filling the gap between geometric-only and RGB-dependent approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is dramatic data efficiency: 10 synthetic CAD models per category suffice to match or exceed methods trained on 210+ objects, making category-level pose estimation feasible for long-tail object categories in robotics where real annotation is prohibitive. The technique of projecting pre-trained 2D foundation model features into 3D point clouds is a general recipe that could transfer to other 3D matching or registration tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to categories with high intra-class shape variability beyond the six evaluated, how it handles objects without available CAD models, and whether the reliance on a single reference shape prior limits accuracy for highly deformed instances. Failure modes on transparent objects and segmentation errors remain unresolved.
