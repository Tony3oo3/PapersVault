## Problem

Roof height maps from airborne LiDAR are frequently corrupted by extreme sparsity (up to 99% missing points), large regional incompleteness (up to 80% occluded area), and tree noise, yet existing DEM inpainting and unguided depth completion methods fail under these combined conditions. The 'long tail' of complex, severely corrupted roof geometry remains under-explored, and no prior method handles all three corruption types simultaneously while preserving sharp architectural details like dormers and gables.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel roof height map (pixel values in meters, where zero denotes missing measurement or ground level) and a binary building footprint mask as input; the No-FP variant omits the mask and jointly predicts both footprint and height. Before diffusion, heights are roof-focused normalized to [-1,1] by subtracting the lowest roof pixel and scaling by a global 10 m cutoff (the 99th percentile of per-building height ranges across 13k buildings), which accommodates the wide dynamic range of building heights. The output is a complete, noise-free height map in the same normalized space, later rescaled back to meters.

## Method

RoofDiffusion frames roof repair as a conditional denoising diffusion process: a forward chain injects Gaussian noise into ground-truth height maps, with a key trick of subtracting the complement mask so that non-footprint pixels are set to −1, letting the network infer the building boundary directly from the noise pattern without an extra channel. The reverse chain uses a learnable noise predictor ε_θ conditioned on the noisy intermediate x_t, the original corrupted input x, and the variance schedule α̅_t (following Palette's image-to-image conditioning), trained with an L1 loss masked to the footprint. This conditioning on the sparse input ensures the restored roof seamlessly integrates with pre-existing structures rather than hallucinating independent geometry. The approach is self-supervised, trained on intentionally corrupted versions of noise-free LoD 2.2 roof meshes from the newly introduced PoznanRD dataset (13k buildings), with corruption synthesized via random point removal, multi-Gaussian masking for incompleteness, and a novel 'tree planting' procedure for realistic canopy noise.

## Results

On the PoznanRD dataset under extreme corruption (99% sparsity, 80% incompleteness), footprint-guided RoofDiffusion achieves MAE 0.603 m and RMSE 0.916 m, outperforming the best baseline (IDW) by 27% in MAE and 22% in RMSE. On the more moderate setting (95% sparsity, 30% incompleteness), RoofDiffusion reaches MAE 0.162 m and RMSE 0.342 m. The No-FP variant, which simultaneously predicts footprints and heights, achieves MAE 0.319 m / RMSE 1.232 m at s95 i30 on PoznanRD, beating CU-Net (MAE 1.246) and pNCNN (MAE 1.635) by large margins. As a preprocessor for City3D 3D reconstruction, RoofDiffusion reduces point-to-plane RMSE from 0.352 (IDW) to 0.244 m at s99 i30, approaching the ground-truth baseline of 0.104 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoofDiffusion (FP) consistently outperforms all four DEM inpainting baselines across every sparsity/incompleteness combination on both PoznanRD and BuildingNet. The gap is most dramatic against Perona-Malik Diffusion [Biasutti, 2019], which degrades catastrophically at high corruption (MAE 3.085 m at s99 i80 vs. RoofDiffusion's 0.603 m), while IDW [Shepard, 1968] and Spline [Keys, 1981] remain competitive at moderate corruption but still trail RoofDiffusion by 20–35% in MAE. In the No-FP setting (Table 2), RoofDiffusion substantially outperforms pNCNN [Eldesokey, 2020] (MAE 0.319 vs. 1.635 at s95 i30) and CU-Net [Wang, 2022] (MAE 0.319 vs. 1.246), though CU-Net occasionally achieves lower RMSE due to over-smoothing that reduces large errors at the cost of sharp geometric detail. For 3D reconstruction (Table 3), using RoofDiffusion as a City3D preprocessor yields the lowest point-to-plane RMSE (0.244 m at s99 i30) and a face count (82.72) nearly identical to the ground-truth pipeline (82.68), whereas IDW and P.M. Diff. preprocessed inputs produce higher RMSE and more faces.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoofDiffusion adapts the conditional diffusion framework of DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) and the image-to-image conditioning mechanism of Palette (Saharia et al., 2022) to the specific problem of roof height map restoration, treating it as a denoising diffusion process conditioned on the corrupted input and building footprint. It departs from classical DEM inpainting (IDW, Spline, Perona-Malik Diffusion) and unguided depth completion (pNCNN, CU-Net) by explicitly targeting the 'long-tail' of extreme sparsity (up to 99%), large regional incompleteness (up to 80%), and tree-occlusion noise that those methods were not designed to handle. The paper also carves new ground in data synthesis by introducing a tree-planting simulation and multi-Gaussian masking for incompleteness, filling a gap left by existing roof datasets that provide clean ground truth but no corrupted variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With 500M buildings in OpenStreetMap and USGS 3DEP LiDAR showing 34–50% corruption rates in sampled regions, robust roof height map repair has direct practical impact on 3D city model quality. The PoznanRD dataset (13k LoD 2.2 roofs) and the corruption synthesis pipeline provide reusable infrastructure for future long-tail remote sensing research. Key open questions remain: ~36% of tree-noise cases are still misclassified as architectural elements, footprint misalignment degrades performance, and the model does not yet exploit auxiliary signals like floor count or ground elevation. The authors also flag hallucination risk when reconstructing from extremely few data points, a concern that will matter as the method is deployed at scale.
