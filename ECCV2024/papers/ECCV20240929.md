## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving frameworks (e.g., VAD, UniAD) treat prediction and planning as a single sequential pass: all agents' future trajectories are predicted first, then the ego plans its trajectory in one shot. This one-shot design cannot capture the timestep-wise, bidirectional interactions that characterize real driving, where agents continuously adjust their behavior in response to each other's evolving plans, often leading to invalid motion plans and collisions in interactive scenarios.

## Input / Output

The system consumes 6–7 synchronized camera images per frame plus 2 seconds of recurrently encoded historical BEV features, along with a high-level driving command (go straight, turn left, or turn right). Rather than outputting a single 3-second trajectory in one forward pass, PPAD produces the ego's 3-second future trajectory as six one-step waypoint offsets (every 0.5 s), each conditioned on the updated agent predictions from the preceding planning step. Side outputs include per-agent motion predictions across multiple modes and vectorized map elements (lane boundaries, road edges) decoded from learned queries.

## Method

PPAD interleaves a Prediction process and a Planning process N times (N=6 for a 3 s horizon) in an autoregressive loop: at each step, agents predict their next motion by cross-attending to the ego query from the previous planning step, map queries, and BEV features; the ego then plans its next waypoint by attending to the updated agent queries, map queries, and BEV features. A hierarchical dynamic key-objects attention mechanism applies multi-scale distance masks (+∞, 15 m, 7.5 m) to enforce coarse-to-fine spatial locality in both ego-to-agent and ego-to-map interactions, while a deformable-attention BEV query extracts fine-grained local geometry around the ego's current position. Training introduces two additional mechanisms: a confidence-aware collision loss that weights collision penalties by the predicted confidence of each agent mode, and a noisy-trajectory augmentation that perturbs the ground-truth ego path and trains the network to recover the correct next-step offset, improving robustness to accumulated planning errors.

## Results

On nuScenes (UniAD metrics), PPAD achieves 0.75 m average L2 displacement error and 0.33% average collision rate, outperforming VAD-Base (1.07 m / 0.42%) and UniAD (1.03 m / 0.31%). Under ST-P3 metrics, PPAD reaches 0.48 m average L2 and 0.19% collision, a ~33% L2 improvement over VAD-Base (0.72 m / 0.22%). On Argoverse 2, PPAD achieves 1.34 m average L2 and 0.89% collision, surpassing VAD (1.81 m / 1.37%) by 26% and 35% respectively. PPAD runs at 385 ms latency (2.6 FPS) on an RTX 3090, comparable to VAD-Base (224 ms / 4.5 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPAD consistently outperforms all compared baselines on nuScenes. Against VAD-Base (the closest competitor), PPAD reduces average L2 by 33% under ST-P3 metrics (0.48 m vs. 0.72 m) and 30% under UniAD metrics (0.75 m vs. 1.07 m), while also lowering collision rate from 0.22% to 0.19% (ST-P3) and from 0.42% to 0.33% (UniAD). Compared to UniAD, PPAD achieves a 27% L2 improvement (0.75 m vs. 1.03 m) under UniAD metrics, though UniAD reports a marginally lower 3s collision rate (0.31% vs. 0.33%). Against ST-P3, OccNet, and FusionAD, PPAD leads by large margins (e.g., 0.48 m vs. 2.11 m L2 over ST-P3). On Argoverse 2, PPAD outperforms VAD by 26% in L2 (1.34 m vs. 1.81 m) and 35% in collision rate (0.89% vs. 1.37%), confirming generalizability across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PPAD builds directly on the vectorized scene representation paradigm of VAD and the BEVFormer-style deformable-attention encoder for multi-camera feature fusion, inheriting their perception backbone and vectorized agent/map query design. Its key departure is from the one-shot 'predict-then-plan' pipeline used by VAD and UniAD: instead of a single sequential pass, PPAD interleaves agent motion prediction and ego planning autoregressively at every future timestep, framing the interaction as a multi-step game. The hierarchical key-objects attention and the noisy-trajectory training trick (borrowed from DN-DETR) are additional design choices layered on top of this iterative structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iterative prediction-planning formulation offers a more principled way to capture the bidirectional, timestep-wise coupling between ego and surrounding agents, and the ~20% L2 improvement over VAD on nuScenes validates the approach. However, the method incurs a meaningful latency cost (385 ms / 2.6 FPS vs. 224 ms / 4.5 FPS for VAD-Base), and all evaluations remain open-loop, leaving open whether the iterative gaming mechanism translates to closed-loop safety gains. A further open question is whether the fixed three-scale distance hierarchy (∞, 15 m, 7.5 m) generalizes across driving regimes (urban vs. highway) or whether it should be learned.
