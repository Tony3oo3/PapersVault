## Problem

Diffusion models have surpassed GANs in image synthesis quality, yet no existing diffusion framework is robust to outliers in the training data. Prior robust generation work (RobustGAN, UOTM) is confined to GAN architectures, leaving a critical gap for the now-dominant diffusion paradigm. This paper introduces the first diffusion framework that maintains high-fidelity generation even when training data is corrupted with outliers from unrelated distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242241/cropped_page10_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise vector z and a timestep t as input and produces a synthetic RGB image in just 4 function evaluations (NFEs), leveraging DDGAN-style large denoising steps. Experiments span resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (CelebA-HQ), with corrupted training sets mixing clean data with 3–10% outliers from visually distinct or similar distributions (e.g., MNIST digits in CIFAR-10, vertical-flip faces in CelebA-HQ). The generator G_θ additionally receives a latent style vector z ~ N(0, I) to inject stochasticity and style information into each denoising step.

## Method

RDUOT replaces the adversarial GAN discriminator in DDGAN with an OT-based generative model trained via the semi-dual UOT formulation, where a generator G_θ and potential network D_φ jointly optimize a UOT loss. The key insight is modeling the clean conditional distribution q(x_0|x_t) rather than the one-step backward q(x_{t-1}|x_t), because forward diffusion shrinks the Wasserstein distance between outlier and clean samples at large t (Proposition 1), making outlier filtering intractable at the noisy level but easy at the clean x_0 level. The potential network D_φ takes x_{t-1} (sampled from q(x_{t-1}|x_t, x̂_0)) rather than x_0 as input, explicitly encoding the sampling trajectory for better generation quality. For the Csiszár divergence Ψ, the authors use softplus as the convex conjugate Ψ* instead of KL or χ², exploiting its Lipschitz continuity to prevent loss explosion and stabilize training. The generator adopts a StyleGAN-style architecture with AdaIN and style modulation layers to inherit high-fidelity synthesis capabilities.

## Results

On clean CIFAR-10, RDUOT achieves FID 2.95 and Recall 0.58 with only 4 NFEs, outperforming DDGAN (FID 3.75) and matching DDPM (FID 3.21) while requiring 250× fewer sampling steps. On CelebA-HQ (256×256), RDUOT reaches FID 5.60 and Recall 0.38, surpassing DDGAN (FID 7.64). On STL-10, RDUOT achieves FID 11.50 and Recall 0.49, roughly 10 points lower FID than DDGAN (21.79). Under 5% MNIST corruption on CIFAR-10, RDUOT maintains FID 4.37 versus DDGAN's 8.81, and on CelebA-HQ corrupted with vertical-flip faces (CE+FCE), RDUOT achieves FID 5.99 versus DDGAN's 6.48.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDUOT consistently outperforms DDGAN across all three clean datasets: FID 2.95 vs 3.75 on CIFAR-10, 5.60 vs 7.64 on CelebA-HQ, and 11.50 vs 21.79 on STL-10, with matching or better Recall in every case. Against the robustness baselines, RDUOT dominates: on CIFAR-10+5%MNIST it achieves FID 4.37 versus UOTM's 7.89 and RobustGAN's 10.68, and on CE+FCE it reaches 5.99 versus UOTM's 6.72. On clean CIFAR-10, RDUOT (FID 2.95) marginally beats UOTM (2.97) and substantially outperforms OTM (21.78) and RobustGAN (21.57). RDUOT also surpasses WaveDiff on all three datasets (e.g., FID 2.95 vs 4.01 on CIFAR-10) and achieves comparable FID to DDPM (2.95 vs 3.21) with 250× fewer NFEs. The only baseline RDUOT does not beat is LFM on CelebA-HQ (FID 5.60 vs 5.26), though LFM does not report Recall. On STL-10, RDUOT (11.50) narrowly edges out Diffusion StyleGAN2 (11.53) and substantially outperforms StyleGAN2+Aug (12.97) and StyleFormer (15.17).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) robust generative modeling via unbalanced optimal transport (UOT), previously explored in GAN frameworks (RobustGAN, UOTM), and (2) fast diffusion-based generation via DDGAN, which replaces the GAN discriminator with an adversarial objective to handle large denoising steps. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that UOT cannot be naively plugged into DDGAN's adversarial loop because GANs minimize distribution-to-distribution OT distance while UOT minimizes source-to-target mapping cost. Instead, the authors replace the GAN process entirely with an OT-based generative model (following UOTM/OTM), and critically, model the clean-data posterior q(x₀|x_t) rather than the one-step denoising q(x_{t-1}|x_t), since the forward diffusion process erodes the distinguishability between outliers and clean samples at later timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion models increasingly displace GANs as the dominant generative paradigm, the absence of robust training methods for them is a practical gap this paper addresses. The finding that the UOT-based robustness mechanism also *improves* clean-dataset FID (e.g., 2.95 vs. 3.75 on CIFAR-10) suggests that outlier filtering at each diffusion step acts as a regularizer, which is a useful insight beyond the robustness setting. Limitations include evaluation only up to 256×256 resolution, corruption ratios capped at 10%, and a somewhat ad hoc choice of softplus as the Csiszár divergence (motivated by Lipschitz continuity but not deeply analyzed). Open questions include whether the approach scales to text-to-image or video generation, whether the x₀-vs-x_{t-1} modeling choice generalizes to other diffusion variants (score-based, flow-matching), and how the method behaves under more realistic, structured corruption patterns.
