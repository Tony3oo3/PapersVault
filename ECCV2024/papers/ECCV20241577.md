## Problem

Existing open-set domain generalization methods are limited to unimodal inputs, yet real-world applications (autonomous driving, action recognition) increasingly rely on multiple modalities. No prior work has addressed Multimodal Open-Set Domain Generalization (MM-OSDG) or Multimodal Open-Set Domain Adaptation (MM-OSDA), where a model must generalize to unseen target domains containing both known and previously unknown classes across multiple modalities simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241577/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes M modalities per sample (video clips, audio signals, and optical flow on the EPIC-Kitchens and HAC benchmarks), each processed by a dedicated feature extractor producing an embedding vector. At inference, the concatenated multi-modal embedding passes through a shared classifier producing a softmax prediction over known classes; a confidence threshold (max probability) then routes low-confidence samples to an 'unknown' label. Per-modality classifiers also produce individual predictions used for entropy-based weighting during training.

## Method

MOOSA introduces two complementary multimodal self-supervised pretext tasks alongside the supervised classification objective. The generative task, Masked Cross-modal Translation, randomly zeros a fraction β of one modality's embedding and trains an MLP to reconstruct another modality's full embedding from the masked source, forcing the network to internalize intrinsic cross-modal data distributions. The contrastive task, Multimodal Jigsaw Puzzles, splits all modalities' embeddings into P segments, shuffles the M·P segments across modalities, and trains a permutation classifier to recover the correct ordering, compelling the model to learn fine-grained cross-modal structure. An entropy weighting mechanism computes per-modality prediction entropies and uses them as softmax-normalized weights on each modality's cross-entropy loss, down-weighting less reliable modalities; an additional entropy minimization term encourages confident predictions. For MM-OSDA, unlabeled target samples are first filtered by a confidence threshold to separate known from unknown classes, and the self-supervised pretext losses are then applied to the retained known target samples to align source and target distributions.

## Results

On the EPIC-Kitchens dataset under the MM-OSDG setting with all three modalities (video, audio, optical flow), MOOSA achieves a mean HOS of 56.77%, outperforming the strongest baseline SimMMDG (53.99%) by 2.78% and the unimodal SOTA MEDIC (52.76%) by 4.01%. On the HAC dataset, MOOSA reaches 65.16% mean HOS versus 63.14% for SimMMDG. In the Multimodal Closed-Set DG setting, MOOSA achieves 67.29% (EPIC-Kitchens) and 72.19% (HAC), surpassing SimMMDG by up to 1.30%. Under MM-OSDA, MOOSA improves HOS by 4.15% over the best extended baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOOSA consistently outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against SimMMDG (the SOTA multimodal DG method), MOOSA gains +2.78% HOS on EPIC-Kitchens MM-OSDG (56.77 vs 53.99) and +2.02% on HAC (65.16 vs 63.14), with the gap widening to +10.09% over RNA-Net and +10.88% over MM-SADA on EPIC-Kitchens. The unimodal SOTA MEDIC, when extended to multimodal, trails MOOSA by 4.01% HOS on EPIC-Kitchens, confirming that simply combining unimodal open-set DG with multiple modalities is insufficient. In the MM-OSDA setting (Table 5), MOOSA achieves 51.94% mean HOS versus 48.92% for the best extended baseline (SimMMDG), a 4.15% improvement. In the closed-set DG ablation (Table 6), MOOSA edges out SimMMDG by 0.10% on EPIC-Kitchens (67.29 vs 67.19) and 1.09% on HAC (72.19 vs 71.10), indicating the self-supervised tasks also benefit standard multimodal DG.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOOSA sits at the intersection of three previously separate research threads: multimodal domain generalization (SimMMDG, RNA-Net), unimodal open-set DG (DAML, MEDIC, CrossMatch), and self-supervised pretext tasks for robust feature learning (Jigsaw puzzles, masked image modeling, rotation prediction). The paper departs from SimMMDG's cross-modal translation by introducing a masking mechanism inspired by BEiT and MOOD, and extends the unimodal Jigsaw puzzle task to a cross-modal embedding-space shuffling. The entropy weighting mechanism draws on test-time adaptation (TENT) but is applied during training to balance modality-specific losses, a use not seen in prior multimodal DG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing MM-OSDG and MM-OSDA, the paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world multimodal systems (autonomous driving, egocentric action recognition) routinely encounter novel object/action categories in new environments, and no prior method handled this in a multimodal setting. The consistent improvements across two datasets and multiple modality combinations validate the problem formulation, though the evaluation is limited to two datasets with three modalities and a single unknown class per domain. Open questions include whether the threshold-based open-class detection generalizes to more complex open-set scenarios, whether the self-supervised tasks scale to more than three modalities, and whether learned (rather than threshold-based) OOD scores from multimodal OOD detection methods could further improve detection.
