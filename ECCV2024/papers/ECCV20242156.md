## Problem

In popular LVLMs such as LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen-VL, visual tokens occupy the majority of input sequence length yet receive strikingly low attention in deep layers—only 0.21% of the attention allocated to system prompts after layer 2—making the self-attention and FFN computation over these tokens highly wasteful. While LLM inference acceleration has been studied, this specific visual-token redundancy in LVLMs was unexplored, and no training-free method existed to exploit it for practical speedup.

## Input / Output

FastV operates on standard LVLM inputs: images encoded by CLIP-ViT into 576 visual tokens (336×336) or video frames producing up to 2048 tokens (Video-LLaVA), concatenated with system prompt, user instruction, and autoregressively generated output tokens. The output is identical in format to the unmodified model—a generated text response—but the forward pass is shortened because a configurable fraction of visual tokens is removed mid-sequence, reducing both attention and FFN FLOPs in all subsequent layers without altering the input or output interface.

## Method

FastV is a plug-and-play, training-free pruning strategy that intervenes at a single configurable layer K of the LLM decoder. At layer K, every visual token is ranked by its average attention score received from all other tokens in the sequence (the φ_attn criterion), and the bottom R% of tokens are permanently discarded for all remaining layers. The key insight driving this design is that shallow layers aggregate image-specific, instruction-relevant features onto a small set of non-image 'anchor' tokens (primarily system-prompt tokens), so by layer 2 the visual tokens themselves carry little unique information and can be safely pruned. Because pruned tokens are removed entirely, FastV eliminates both the quadratic self-attention cost and the linear FFN cost for those tokens in deeper layers, achieving up to 45% FLOPs reduction (e.g., 84.6B vs. 154.6B for LLaVA-1.5-13B) while preserving or even slightly improving task performance across captioning, VQA, video QA, and fine-grained benchmarks.

## Results

FastV (K=2, R=50%) achieves approximately 45% FLOPs reduction across LLaVA-1.5-7B, LLaVA-1.5-13B, and QwenVL-Chat-7B while maintaining near-identical average performance (69.7 vs. 69.8 for 7B; 73.6 vs. 73.6 for 13B; 69.2 vs. 69.7 for QwenVL). On the latency test (A-OKVQA), LLaVA-1.5-13B with FastV (K=0, R=50%) achieves 0.341s/example versus 0.539s for the unpruned 13B model, matching the 7B model's 0.344s while scoring 80.5 vs. 76.7. For video understanding (Video-LLaVA), FastV (K=2, R=50%) reduces FLOPs to 52.3% while slightly improving average accuracy from 0.48 to 0.49 on TGIF/MSVD/MSRVTT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastV is evaluated against five baselines: (1) the same LVLM without pruning (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, QwenVL-Chat-7B, InstructBLIP-13B, Video-LLaVA), (2) retraining with 50% fewer image tokens via average pooling, (3) random token dropping, (4) StreamingLLM's attention-sink strategy, and (5) a cross-size latency comparison (13B+FastV vs. 7B). Against the unpruned baselines, FastV (K=2, R=50%) matches or marginally exceeds performance on all four main benchmarks (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-13B: 73.6 avg vs. 73.6 baseline) while cutting FLOPs by 45%. Against retraining with fewer tokens, FastV clearly wins: the retrained model drops to 69.2 avg (Nocaps 98.5, A-OKVQA 76.8) versus FastV's 70.4 avg (Nocaps 100.1, A-OKVQA 78.4). Random dropping (K=2, R=50%, random) underperforms attention-ranked pruning by ~1.2 avg points, confirming that attention-based selection is critical. StreamingLLM's sparse attention pattern, when directly applied to LVLMs, causes complete failure on A-OKVQA and MMMU, demonstrating that text-only acceleration strategies do not transfer to multimodal settings. In the latency comparison, LLaVA-1.5-13B with FastV (K=0, R=50%) achieves 0.341s/example—faster than the unpruned 7B model (0.344s)—while scoring 80.5 vs. 76.7 on A-OKVQA, showing that a pruned larger model can outperform a smaller one in both speed and accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LLM inference optimization (StreamingLLM, FastGen, sparse attention) to the multimodal setting, but departs from those methods by directly eliminating tokens rather than merely sparsifying attention computations. It also builds on the ViT token-reduction literature (EViT, SPViT, Pumer, PYRA) but is the first to apply such pruning within the LLM decoder of an LVLM, using the model's own attention scores as the ranking signal. Unlike LLaMA-VID, which compresses video frames via cross-attention but requires fine-tuning, FastV is plug-and-play and requires no retraining, positioning it as a lightweight post-hoc acceleration layer on top of any token-based LVLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that image tokens receive only 0.21% of system-prompt attention in deep layers (despite comprising 64% of input) provides a concrete diagnostic for why LVLM inference is wasteful and motivates a simple, broadly applicable fix. The 45% FLOPs reduction with negligible performance loss across captioning, VQA, video QA, and fine-grained benchmarks makes this immediately deployable for edge and commercial settings. Open questions include whether the 'anchor token' explanation generalizes to newer architectures (e.g., those with dynamic resolution or native multimodal pretraining), whether the optimal pruning layer K shifts with model scale or task difficulty, and whether the method interacts adversely with KV-cache-based serving systems like vLLM.
