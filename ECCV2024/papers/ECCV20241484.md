## Problem

It is widely assumed that scaling pretraining data and computation simultaneously drives SSL success, but it remains unclear how much of the benefit comes from data diversity (unique samples) versus raw compute. This paper isolates the effect of pretraining data diversity under a fixed computational budget and asks whether increasing diversity can compensate for distribution shift between pretraining and downstream data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Pretraining consumes unlabeled RGB images from large-scale datasets (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet and YFCC100M at 224×224), where the number of unique samples N and epochs E are jointly constrained by a fixed budget C = N·E. Evaluation uses linear probing on labeled downstream images (ImageNet, Stanford Cars, Flowers-102, Oxford-IIIT Pets, Places365, Food-101), where a frozen feature extractor (ResNet-18/50 or ViT-B/16) produces a representation vector that a single trainable linear head maps to a class label. The key controlled variable is pretraining diversity D = N/C = 1/E, which quantifies how many unique images the model sees per unit of compute.

## Method

The paper introduces a computationally-normalized evaluation protocol where all SSL methods process exactly C images during pretraining, decoupling the effect of data diversity (D = N/C) from total compute. Seven SSL methods (SimCLR, MoCo v3, VICReg, DINO, BYOL, Barlow Twins, SwAV) are pretrained from scratch on subsets of CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet, ImageNet, and YFCC100M, with diversity varied by adjusting dataset size N while holding C constant. To study distribution shift, the authors construct pretraining sets by augmenting a baseline with in-distribution or out-of-distribution samples sourced from the original dataset, web crawling (Flickr, Bing, DuckDuckGo), or Stable Diffusion V2.1 generation. Large-scale experiments (200+ GPU days on A100s) compare ImageNet (D ≈ 1.3×10⁻³) against YFCC100M (D = 1) under C = 98×10⁶, revealing that even a ~770× diversity increase cannot offset distribution shift, and that performance saturates rather than improving monotonically with more diverse out-of-distribution data.

## Results

Under normalized computation (C=98×10⁶), pretraining on ImageNet (D=13×10⁻³) with SimCLR+ResNet-50 achieves 64.5% on ImageNet, 46.4% on Cars, 84.6% on Flowers-102, 79.5% on Pets, 48.8% on Places365, and 68.0% on Food-101. In stark contrast, pretraining on YFCC100M (D=1000×10⁻³, ~77× more diverse) yields only 57.3%, 37.2%, 76.6%, 58.9%, 50.1%, and 62.1% respectively, underperforming on 5 of 6 downstream tasks. On in-distribution settings (CIFAR-100), increasing diversity from D=0.1×10⁻³ to D=1.0×10⁻³ consistently improves all 7 SSL methods by 10–20% (e.g., SimCLR: 38.0%→58.6%). The sole exception is Places365, where YFCC100M (50.1%) slightly outperforms ImageNet (48.8%), consistent with its lower FID/VisualDNA distance to that dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven SSL methods (SimCLR, MoCo v3, BYOL, Barlow Twins, VICReg, SwAV, DINO) exhibit the same qualitative trend: in-distribution diversity consistently improves performance, while out-of-distribution diversity degrades it. On CIFAR-100 (Table 1a), SimCLR leads at the highest diversity (58.6%) while DINO lags (40.9%), but all methods show monotonic improvement from D=0.1×10⁻³ to D=1.0×10⁻³. On the large-scale ImageNet vs. YFCC100M comparison (Table 2), both SimCLR and MoCo v3 with ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 confirm that YFCC100M-pretrained models (D=1000×10⁻³) underperform ImageNet-pretrained models (D=13×10⁻³) by 5–21 points on most downstream tasks, with MoCo v3+ViT-B/16 showing the largest gap (e.g., 68.8% vs. 57.2% on ImageNet, 86.5% vs. 70.3% on Flowers-102). The one consistent exception is Places365, where YFCC100M outperforms ImageNet across all methods and backbones, aligning with its lower distribution distance (FID 38.9 vs. 64.9; VisualDNA 2.74 vs. 3.52). No single SSL method is uniformly superior; performance rankings shift across diversity levels and datasets, but the diversity-vs.-distribution-shift interaction is method-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the extensive SSL pretraining literature (SimCLR, MoCo v3, BYOL, SwAV, DINO, VICReg, Barlow Twins) and prior observations that pretraining on distributions similar to downstream tasks improves transfer (Cole et al. 2022, Goyal et al. 2019, Van Gansbeke et al. 2021). However, it departs from prior work by introducing a computationally normalized evaluation protocol (C = N × E) that isolates the effect of data diversity (D = N/C) from raw compute, a confound that has plagued large-scale SSL comparisons like SEER and DINOv2 where data and computation were scaled simultaneously. The paper further extends the analysis by systematically comparing in-distribution vs. out-of-distribution data collection strategies (source, web-crawled, Stable Diffusion synthetic) under this controlled budget, a dimension not previously studied in a normalized setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even 98 million diverse images (YFCC100M) cannot compensate for distribution shift under normalized computation directly challenges the field's implicit assumption that 'more diverse data = better SSL representations,' with immediate practical implications for how practitioners allocate compute and data resources. The paper also exposes a methodological gap: without computational normalization, lower-diversity models with more epochs can outperform higher-diversity models, leading to incorrect conclusions about the role of data. Open questions include why language models benefit from diverse pretraining data while vision SSL models do not, and whether new SSL objectives can more effectively exploit large-scale diversity to improve out-of-distribution generalization.
