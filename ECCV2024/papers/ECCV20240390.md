## Problem

Transfer multi-modal federated learning, where unpaired uni-modal clients (image-only or text-only) collaborate with multi-modal clients to train a shared multi-modal model, remains largely unexplored—especially with transformer architectures. Existing multi-modal FL methods either require all clients to share identical modalities or rely on paired data from the same users, excluding the abundant unpaired uni-modal data. Two compounding gaps arise: a cross-modality gap (uni-modal clients never access the other modality) and an in-modality gap (same-modality models trained with different objectives diverge).

## Input / Output

Uni-modal clients consume 224×224 RGB images (patch-embedded at size 16) or BERT-tokenized text (max length 40) for local classification, while multi-modal clients consume paired image-text data for contrastive retrieval training. The server evaluates the final multi-modal model via Top-1 Recall on both image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval over 1k and 5k test images from Flickr30k or COCO Captions. Only model parameters are exchanged between clients and server—no raw data or public auxiliary datasets are shared.

## Method

FedCola splits each model into embedding layers, shared transformer blocks (ViT-Small), and task-specific heads, then addresses the two gaps with parameter-only mechanisms. For the cross-modality gap, uni-modal clients perform complementary local training: they download the other modality's transformer blocks and apply a Mixture-of-Experts-inspired learnable gate (initialized to 0) on every linear layer, blending local weights W_local with out-modality weights g·W_out; after training, the combined weights are compressed back to the original model size to avoid doubling upload cost. For the in-modality gap, the server applies collaborative aggregation that selectively averages self-attention layers across same-modality clients (capturing general inter-token knowledge) while disaggregating MLPs (preserving task-specific knowledge), and a compensation scheme rescales non-collaborated layers to fix the layer-level and modality-level misalignment introduced by differing aggregation coefficients.

## Results

Under the default FL setting (α=0.5, r=0.25), FedCola achieves a sum of top-1 recalls of 91.96 on Flickr30k and 105.10 on COCO Captions, outperforming the strongest baseline FedIoT (85.51 / 98.40) by 6.45 and 6.70 points respectively. The advantage is even more pronounced against FedAvg (81.08 / 95.42), with gains of +10.88 and +9.68. FedCola maintains its lead under more challenging settings: with higher heterogeneity (α=0.1) it scores 91.82 / 100.83, and with less participation (r=0.125) it scores 88.85 / 102.30, while baselines degrade substantially (e.g., FedAvg drops to 64.82 / 83.91 under less participation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCola consistently outperforms all four baselines across every FL setting and both evaluation datasets. Against the strongest baseline FedIoT, FedCola gains +6.45 on Flickr and +6.70 on COCO under the default setting, and the gap widens under less participation (FedIoT drops to 61.94/80.65 while FedCola holds at 88.85/102.30). Against FedAvg, the gains are +10.88 (Flickr) and +9.68 (COCO) by default, and FedAvg degrades far more severely under heterogeneity and low participation. FedProx and CreamFL are weaker baselines overall; CreamFL in particular underperforms FedAvg on Flickr (74.83 vs. 81.08) despite its knowledge-distillation mechanism, likely because it relies on a public dataset that is unavailable in the transfer MFL setting. Notably, FedCola also preserves uni-modal task performance (CIFAR-100: 89.23, AG NEWS: 87.07) while improving multi-modal retrieval, whereas other multi-modal methods sacrifice uni-modal accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-modal federated learning from the horizontal (identical modalities across clients) and vertical (same users, split modalities) settings to a new 'transfer' setting where unpaired uni-modal clients participate alongside multi-modal clients. It builds on FedAvg as the base aggregation and draws on Mixture-of-Experts-style gating for complementary local training, but departs from prior MFL methods (CreamFL, FedIoT) by eliminating the need for public datasets or paired data, instead exploiting the unified transformer architecture (ViT blocks shared across image and text encoders) as the sole knowledge carrier. The compensation scheme for layer-level and modality-level alignment during collaborative aggregation is a novel technical contribution not present in prior FL aggregation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The transfer MFL setting addresses a practically important gap: high-quality uni-modal data (e.g., medical images, news text) is often siloed and unpaired, yet can improve multi-modal models. The paper demonstrates that cross-modality knowledge transfer is achievable purely through model weights without any data sharing, which has direct implications for privacy-preserving multi-modal training at scale. However, the evaluation is limited to ViT-Small in the vision-language domain with relatively small datasets, and the authors acknowledge that large domain gaps still degrade performance. Open questions include whether the self-attention/MLP collaboration split generalizes to larger transformers, whether cross-modal (rather than in-modal) aggregation can further close gaps, and how the framework scales to more than two modalities.
