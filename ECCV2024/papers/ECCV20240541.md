## Problem

Existing CLIP-based video recognition methods fail to capture comprehensive spatio-temporal dynamics because their temporal interactions are either too limited (cross-frame [CLS] attention, local temporal windows) or too naive (frame-wise averaging). Naively extending to full joint space-time attention degrades performance due to CLIP's pretraining on short image-text pairs, creating an extrapolation problem where attention spreads over redundant background tokens rather than focusing on informative regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240541/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of T frames at spatial resolution H×W (processed as P×P patches per frame via ViT-B/16) and a text category name (e.g., 'skateboarding') as input. It produces a video-text similarity score per class, with the highest-scoring class as the predicted action label. The video is encoded frame-by-frame through the vision encoder with temporal context tokens injected at every layer, while the text side processes learnable prompt vectors conditioned on the same context tokens before the final text encoder layer.

## Method

TC-CLIP introduces Temporal Contextualization (TC), a layer-wise mechanism that (1) selects informative seed tokens per frame using [CLS] attention scores, (2) aggregates them across all frames into a small set of context tokens via bipartite soft matching, and (3) injects these context tokens as additional key-value pairs in self-attention at each subsequent layer, with a learnable bias distinguishing local frame-level from global video-level information. A Video-conditional Prompting (VP) module then uses cross-attention to inject context token representations into learnable text prompt vectors before the last text encoder layer, compensating for the lack of descriptive text in action recognition datasets. The model is trained end-to-end with a contrastive cross-entropy loss on video-text similarity, and a lightweight variant applies TC only to the 4th, 8th, and 12th vision encoder layers to reduce computational cost while maintaining performance.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting (trained on Kinetics-400, evaluated on HMDB-51, UCF-101, K-600), TC-CLIP with weight-space ensembling achieves 71.0% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming Open-VCLIP (70.1%) and ViFi-CLIP (69.0%). In the few-shot setting (K=2,4,8,16), TC-CLIP reaches 54.8% average top-1 accuracy versus 52.9% for ViFi-CLIP. Under fully-supervised training on Kinetics-400, TC-CLIP attains 85.2% top-1 accuracy, a 1.3%p improvement over the ViFi-CLIP baseline (83.9%). The lightweight variant (TC applied to only 3 of 12 layers) maintains 70.7% zero-shot accuracy while achieving 17.2% higher throughput than Open-VCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TC-CLIP consistently outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every evaluation protocol. Against the direct baseline ViFi-CLIP (frame-wise averaging), TC-CLIP gains +1.6%p (zero-shot without WE), +1.9%p (few-shot), +3.0%p (base-to-novel HM), and +1.3%p (fully-supervised top-1). Against the strongest prior method Open-VCLIP (temporal window expansion), TC-CLIP leads by +0.9%p in zero-shot (71.0 vs 70.1) and +2.0%p in base-to-novel (58.5 vs 56.5), while also achieving 17.2% higher throughput. Among LLM-augmented methods, TC-CLIP surpasses FROSTER by +1.7%p (73.2 vs 71.5) and OST by +3.0%p (73.2 vs 70.2) in zero-shot with rephrased prompts. The gap over cross-frame attention methods (X-CLIP, Vita-CLIP) is particularly large: +10.4%p and +7.3%p respectively in zero-shot All Top-1, confirming that limited temporal interaction ranges are a fundamental bottleneck. No baseline matches or exceeds TC-CLIP on any dataset under any protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021) to video action recognition by addressing a specific gap identified in prior CLIP-based video models: the inability to model global temporal interactions without incurring prohibitive computational cost or suffering from sequence-length extrapolation. It departs from cross-frame attention methods (X-CLIP, Vita-CLIP) that rely solely on [CLS] tokens, from local temporal window expansion (Open-VCLIP), and from naive joint space-time attention that the authors show degrades performance. The key architectural departure is Temporal Contextualization (TC), which uses parameter-free token pruning and bipartite soft matching to distill a small set of context tokens from the entire video, then injects them as additional key-value pairs in layer-wise self-attention. A complementary Video-conditional Prompting (VP) module bridges the vision and text encoders via cross-attention, injecting video-instance information into learnable text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TC-CLIP offers a practical, computationally efficient recipe for extending image-based VLMs to video without the quadratic cost of full space-time attention, and the lightweight variant (TC applied to only 3 of 12 layers) achieves near-full performance at 17% higher throughput than Open-VCLIP. The demonstration that naively extending CLIP's temporal sequence length degrades attention quality due to extrapolation is a useful caution for the broader community adapting image Transformers to longer sequences. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much longer videos (beyond 16 frames), whether it generalizes to other VLM backbones beyond ViT-B/16, and whether the token aggregation strategy (bipartite soft matching) remains optimal at larger context token counts or more diverse action vocabularies.
