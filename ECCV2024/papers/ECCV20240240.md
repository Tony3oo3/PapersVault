## Problem

Adapting large-scale vision transformers to downstream tasks requires balancing two conflicting goals: training efficiency (achieved by PEFT methods like LoRA that freeze the backbone) and inference efficiency (achieved by model compression like token merging). Simply combining PEFT with compression leads to performance drops at low compression rates and 'adverse compression' at high rates, where the compressed large model underperforms a smaller uncompressed model, because the limited tunable parameters cannot adequately compensate for information lost during token merging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240240/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (224×224, processed into 196 tokens for ViT/16) and produces class labels across 19 diverse tasks in the VTAB-1k benchmark, each with only 800 training samples. During inference, token merging reduces the token count by a configurable ratio (e.g., 50% for ~1.7× speedup or higher for >3× speedup), while the LoRA adapter is merged into the backbone so no extra FLOPs are incurred at test time. The only additional inference cost is 4rD FLOPs per merged token pair for the modulation computation.

## Method

PYRA inserts a lightweight token modulation module before the MHSA in each ViT block, operating on the token pairs selected by ToMe-style bipartite graph matching. Two learnable vectors per block—W_r ∈ R^{r×1} and W_D ∈ R^{1×D}—generate decoupled modulation weights δ_D (per-channel) and δ_r (per-token) in parallel by multiplying a LayerNorm'd sum of the token pair (M_info). A re-activation strategy then applies sigmoid-gated Hadamard products with residual connections to modulate the source tokens before average-pooling merge, constraining weight magnitudes and preserving gradient flow. The generators use random Gaussian (W_r) and zero (W_D) initialization so the module starts as an identity map. Trained end-to-end alongside LoRA, PYRA adds only D+r parameters per block (~0.4% total) while enabling the compressed model to adaptively calibrate feature distributions for downstream tasks, eliminating adverse compression at high rates.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 tasks), PYRA achieves 74.69% average accuracy on ViT-B/16 and 76.84% on ViT-L/16 under low compression (~1.7× speedup), matching or exceeding the uncompressed PEFT baselines (74.76% and 76.52%) while using only 0.35–0.40% trainable parameters. Under high compression (~3.2× speedup), PYRA reaches 72.06% on ViT-B/16 and 75.66% on ViT-L/16, surpassing the smaller-scale models with comparable throughput (ViT-S/16 PEFT: 71.85%; ViT-B/16 PEFT: 74.76%). PYRA outperforms the strongest baseline ToMe by 0.59–0.73% at low compression and 1.56–1.63% at high compression, while other baselines (DiffRate, SPViT, RaP) suffer severe degradation of 10–35 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PYRA consistently outperforms all four baselines across both compression rates and both backbone scales. Against ToMe (the strongest baseline), PYRA gains +0.59% on ViT-B/16 and +0.73% on ViT-L/16 at low compression, and +1.63% and +1.56% respectively at high compression, where ToMe still suffers from adverse compression. Against DiffRate, the gap is dramatic: PYRA leads by ~18.9% (ViT-B) and ~17.3% (ViT-L) at low compression, and by ~22.8% and ~35.2% at high compression, as DiffRate's searched compression schedule degrades severely under PEFT constraints. SPViT trails PYRA by ~10.5% (ViT-B) and ~6.6% (ViT-L) at low compression, and by ~19.6% and ~8.8% at high compression. RaP performs worst among all baselines, falling 19–30 points behind PYRA, indicating that channel pruning followed by re-training is incompatible with the parameter-efficient setting. Notably, at high compression PYRA even surpasses the smaller pre-trained model (ViT-S/16 or ViT-B/16 with PEFT) by 0.21% and 0.90% respectively, eliminating the adverse compression gap entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT, e.g., LoRA, adapters) and model compression (token merging via ToMe, DiffRate, SPViT, channel pruning via RaP). The authors identify that these threads have been studied in isolation—PEFT reduces training cost but preserves inference complexity, while compression reduces inference cost but demands expensive re-training—and that naively combining them (e.g., ToMe + LoRA) produces a previously uncharacterized 'Adverse Compression' failure at high compression rates. PYRA builds directly on ToMe's bipartite-graph token-merging mechanism as its compression backbone and on LoRA as its PEFT module, introducing a lightweight parallel-yielding re-activation module that adaptively modulates token features before merging, thereby bridging the gap between the two paradigms without heavy re-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: billion-parameter ViTs are difficult to adapt to downstream tasks without incurring prohibitive training costs or retaining full inference latency. By showing that a mere 8.64K additional parameters per model can eliminate the adverse-compression gap at >3× speedup, PYRA offers a drop-in solution for obtaining task-specific small models when no pre-trained smaller backbone is available. Key limitations include validation only on image classification (VTAB-1k) with no experiments on dense prediction tasks like detection or segmentation, and the small PYRA parameters remain in the deployed model (though the authors note post-hoc pruning as future work). Whether the parallel-yielding modulation strategy transfers to other compression paradigms (quantization, distillation) or to non-ViT architectures remains open.
