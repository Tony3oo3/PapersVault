## Problem

All existing binary image downsampling methods (bilinear/bicubic filtering-and-thresholding, pooling, adaptive crossing number) can alter the topology of the image, changing the number of connected components or holes (Betti numbers). No prior method provides a guarantee that the downsampled result preserves the original topology while also maintaining high pixel-wise similarity, making topology-preserving downsampling a previously unsolved problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240533/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary image of resolution W×H and integer downsampling factors A and B (where W is divisible by A and H by B), and produces a W/A × H/B binary image guaranteed to share the same region adjacency graph (and thus the same Betti numbers) as the original. Coverage of each component extends beyond its strict A×B block by a distance threshold of ⌊A/4⌋ and ⌊B/4⌋ pixels. The IP solver can also declare the problem infeasible when no topologically correct downsampling exists at the requested factor, which the method detects instantly.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating topology-preserving downsampling as an integer programming problem solved by Gurobi, where Boolean variables encode per-big-pixel coverage by each connected component (not merely black/white). Four constraint sets enforce correctness: full non-overlapping coverage, component non-emptiness, local neighborhood compatibility (8-neighborhood for black, 4-neighborhood for white), and a boundary-preservation constraint that encodes each component boundary as exactly one closed loop of 12 possible half-edge corner configurations. The key mechanism for the boundary constraint, inspired by 'no-island' constraints in network design, pairs each corner candidate with a distance integer variable and a 'last' Boolean flag, enforcing monotonically increasing distances along the loop with exactly one reset point, which rules out multiple closed loops. The objective function maximizes a distance-weighted similarity score between the downsampled result and the original, and the entire formulation is solved to optimality in under a second for downsampling factors ≥ 4 on 512×512 images.

## Results

On the 542-mask CNCB lung segmentation dataset (512×512), the proposed IP-based method achieves zero Betti number error at all downsampling factors (2, 4, 8, 16), guaranteeing exact topology preservation. At factor 4 (128×128), it reaches 85.12% IoU and 91.55% Dice, nearly matching non-topology-preserving baselines (Bicubic: 85.54% IoU; Pooling: 85.99% IoU) while the dilation baseline lags far behind at 61.69% IoU. The method also attains the lowest PH distance (0.012 at factor 4; 0.030 at factor 8) among all tested methods, confirming minimal topological distortion. Average solve time is 0.819 s at factor 4 and 0.612 s at factor 8, making it practical for interactive use.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bicubic and Pooling (OpenCV), the proposed method trades a marginal 0.4–0.9% IoU drop for a complete elimination of Betti number error (0 vs. 0.133–0.144 at factor 4) and a substantially lower PH distance (0.012 vs. 0.034–0.046), meaning the topology is exactly preserved while pixel-level similarity is nearly identical. Against the ACN method [6], the proposed approach wins decisively on all metrics: higher IoU (85.12% vs. 78.78% at factor 4), zero Betti error vs. 0.135, and lower PH distance (0.012 vs. 0.085). Against the dilation baseline, both methods achieve zero Betti error, but the proposed method dramatically outperforms it on pixel similarity (85.12% vs. 61.69% IoU at factor 4; 73.31% vs. 53.40% at factor 8), confirming that the IP formulation captures shape far better than greedy morphological growth. In the shortest-path application (Table 3), only the proposed method and dilation produce zero false positives/negatives, and the proposed method achieves the smallest average distance error (0.17 at factor 4 vs. 0.38 for dilation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from all existing binary image downsampling methods (bilinear/bicubic filtering, pooling, adaptive crossing number) by formulating the problem as an integer program with a hard topology-preservation constraint, rather than a soft or heuristic objective. The key technical idea is adapted from the 'no-island' constraint in computational network design [17], repurposed here to enforce that each component boundary remains a single closed loop of corners in the downsampled image. Prior topology-preserving transformation work [15,16,18] handles rigid or affine transforms but cannot reliably produce feasible downsampling solutions, and topology-aware segmentation losses [5,9,10] operate at training time rather than as a post-hoc image operation. The paper thus carves new ground by being the first to provide a guaranteed topology-preserving downsampling operator, with the IP formulation serving as the bridge between discrete image geometry and combinatorial optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: it enables topology-faithful visualization of medical segmentation masks at reduced resolutions (useful for human inspection of tiny components and holes), and it provides a principled speedup for expensive binary image operations like persistent homology computation and Dijkstra shortest path, where topological correctness is a hard prerequisite. A key limitation is that the objective function treats black and white components symmetrically, so thin structures (roads, blood vessels) are not well preserved, pointing to an open question about asymmetric or structure-aware cost functions. The reliance on a general-purpose IP solver (Gurobi) also raises scalability questions for very large images or 3D extensions, and the infeasibility cases at small downsampling factors (2–16) indicate that topology preservation is genuinely impossible for some inputs at certain resolutions.
