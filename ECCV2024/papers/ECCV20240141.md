## Problem

Existing image demoiréing methods operate exclusively in either the sRGB domain (where ISP nonlinear operations like demosaicing have already deteriorated moiré patterns) or the RAW domain (where the uncertainty of RAW-to-sRGB conversion causes color cast). No prior work jointly exploits both RAW and sRGB data to simultaneously achieve effective moiré removal and accurate color recovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240141/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a paired input: a RAW image packed into a 4-channel RGGB format at half spatial resolution (H/2 × W/2 × 4, typically 12- or 14-bit) and the corresponding ISP-generated sRGB image (H × W × 3, downsampled to match RAW resolution). The output is a single demoiréd sRGB image at full resolution. In deployment, the in-device ISP's sRGB output is not directly displayed but instead fed alongside the RAW capture into RRID, after which the RAW file can be discarded.

## Method

RRID employs a dual-branch architecture where a Skip-Connection-based Demoiréing Module (SCDM), built from Dilated Channel Attention Blocks (DCAB), processes RAW and sRGB features in parallel at multiple scales. Within SCDM's skip-connections, a Gated Feedback Module (GFM) adaptively separates texture from moiré in RAW features via point-wise/depth-wise convolutions and GELU-gated element-wise multiplication, while a Frequency Selection Module (FSM) applies a learnable band-stop filter through 8×8 Block DCT to suppress moiré frequencies in sRGB features. An RGB Guided ISP (RGISP) then performs RAW-to-sRGB conversion via a cross-attention mechanism: a learned C×C transformation matrix (from softmax(Q·K^T/λ)) maps demoiréd RAW features into the sRGB color space, guided by demoiréd sRGB features, effectively learning a device-dependent ISP. Final reconstruction uses four Residual Swin Transformer Blocks for global tone mapping and detail refinement, trained with a combined L1 loss in both RAW and sRGB domains (α=0.5).

## Results

On the TMM22 dataset, RRID achieves 27.88 dB PSNR, 0.938 SSIM, and 0.079 LPIPS, outperforming the second-best method RawVDmoiré by 0.62 dB in PSNR and 0.003 in SSIM while using fewer parameters (2.38M vs. 5.33M) and a faster inference time (0.089 s vs. 0.182 s). On the FHDMi dataset (sRGB-only generalization test), RRID achieves 24.39 dB PSNR and 0.830 SSIM, placing second behind ESDNet (24.50 dB / 0.835) despite having fewer than half the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RRID outperforms all sRGB-domain demoiréing baselines (DMCNN, WDNet, ESDNet) by 1.1–5.6 dB in PSNR on TMM22, confirming the advantage of incorporating RAW data. Against the RAW-only method RDNet, RRID gains 1.72 dB PSNR and 0.017 SSIM, attributed to the sRGB-guided color recovery via RGISP. The closest competitor is RawVDmoiré (also RAW-input), which RRID surpasses by 0.62 dB PSNR and 0.003 SSIM, though RawVDmoiré retains a slight LPIPS edge (0.075 vs. 0.079). RRID also substantially outperforms the cross-task baselines DNF (low-light, +4.33 dB) and CR3Net (de-reflection, +4.13 dB), validating the benefit of task-specific design. On the FHDMi generalization test, RRID trails ESDNet by 0.11 dB PSNR but beats MopNet (+1.63 dB), MBCNN (+2.08 dB), and FHDe2Net (+1.46 dB), demonstrating reasonable transferability to sRGB-only demoiréing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by combining two previously separate research threads: RAW-domain demoiréing (RDNet, RawVDemoiré), which showed that moiré patterns are less corrupted in RAW data, and sRGB-domain demoiréing (DMCNN, ESDNet, FHDe2Net), which dominates the literature but suffers from ISP-induced pattern degradation. The closest conceptual ancestor is CR3Net, which also uses paired RAW-sRGB inputs for a different restoration task (reflection removal) and includes a RAW-to-sRGB conversion module; RRID adapts this paired-input paradigm to demoiréing and replaces CR3Net's point-wise ISP emulation with a cross-attention-based device-dependent ISP (RGISP). Architecturally, the SCDM's placement of domain-specific modules (GFM for RAW, FSM for sRGB) in skip-connections rather than in the main encoder-decoder path is a deliberate departure from the brute-force multi-scale designs common in prior demoiréing networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is strong: paired RAW-sRGB data is natively available on modern smartphones (iPhone 15 Pro, HUAWEI P60 Pro), making the proposed pipeline directly deployable without requiring new hardware or capture protocols. The ~2 dB PSNR drop when removing the RAW branch (ablation B1) provides concrete evidence that RAW information is not merely redundant with sRGB but carries materially different signal for pattern removal. Open questions include whether the learned device-dependent ISP generalizes across camera models and lighting conditions, whether the approach extends naturally to video demoiréing with temporal consistency, and whether the 408-image TMM22 test set is sufficient to validate the claimed superiority over RAW-only methods.
