## Problem

Existing 3D-aware human image synthesis methods cannot achieve true semantic disentanglement—i.e., independently generating and editing individual body parts (tops, bottoms, shoes, etc.)—because prior approaches either sum signed distance fields before converting to density (entangling geometry) or use a single generator for all parts. Additionally, NeRF-based human synthesis is limited to 512² resolution due to the high computational cost of volume rendering at higher resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240780/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes random Gaussian noise z, SMPL pose parameters P, and a discrete semantic label L_s (indicating garment categories such as dress, skirt, or hat) as input. It produces a 1024² RGB image, depth map, semantic mask, and normal map through a two-stage pipeline: stage 1 renders all four outputs at 256², and stage 2 super-resolves the image and normal map to 1024² while using the stage-1 depth and semantic mask as rendering guidance. The latent code is decomposed into K=6 independent sub-codes (body, tops, outer, bottoms, shoes, accessories), each controlling one tri-plane representation.

## Method

The core architecture employs K=6 fully independent local generators, each mapping its own latent sub-code to a tri-plane representation for one semantic part. The key disentanglement mechanism differs from CNeRF: each generator's local SDF is first converted to a local density via sigmoid, and these local densities are then summed to obtain the final density, allowing per-part geometry to be independently controlled (whereas prior methods sum SDFs first, entangling geometry). A two-stage training procedure is used: stage 1 trains all K generators with a multi-discriminator GAN loss (image, semantic, normal, face) at 256²; stage 2 freezes the generators and trains a 3D-aware super-resolution module with a single high-resolution image discriminator plus an upsample consistency loss. The super-resolution module achieves a 432→11 sampling-point reduction per pixel via depth-guided sampling (aggregating a 3×3 neighborhood of depth values plus two offset values to handle discontinuities) and semantic-guided sampling (masking out semantic parts whose weight falls below a threshold for each pixel), preserving 3D consistency while enabling 1024² rendering.

## Results

On the DeepFashion dataset, SemanticHuman-HD achieves the best FID of 10.04 and 1000×KID of 5.02 at 512² resolution, outperforming the next-best method AG3D (FID 11.33, 1000×KID 5.75). At 1024² resolution, it further improves to FID 8.70 and 1000×KID 4.04, a resolution no other compared method can produce. The method also requires only 10 GB GPU memory at 512² (vs. 21 GB for AG3D and 34 GB for EVA3D), and 31 GB at 1024², thanks to its depth- and semantic-guided sampling that reduces per-pixel sampling points from 432 to 11.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemanticHuman-HD outperforms all five baselines on FID at 512² resolution, achieving 10.04 versus AG3D's 11.33 (the closest competitor), EVA3D's 15.89, GSM's 15.78, AttriHuman-3D's 16.85, and VeRi3D's 21.4. It is also the only method capable of synthesizing at 1024² resolution, where it reaches FID 8.70 and 1000×KID 4.04. In terms of functional capabilities, SemanticHuman-HD is the sole method supporting all four features—local editing, semantic-aware synthesis, semantic disentangled synthesis, and 3D garment generation—whereas AG3D and EVA3D support none, GSM and VeRi3D support only local editing, and AttriHuman-3D supports local editing and semantic-aware synthesis but lacks true disentanglement. AG3D, while second-best in image quality, relies on a 2D super-resolution module that compromises 3D consistency, and EVA3D suffers from circular artifacts due to its network design. GSM and VeRi3D enable local editing via SMPL vertex manipulation but produce artifacts due to lack of semantic awareness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional NeRF paradigm introduced by CNeRF, which uses K local generators for semantic parts, by making a critical architectural change: converting each local SDF to a local density *before* summing (rather than summing SDFs first), which enables true geometric disentanglement across semantic parts. It also builds directly on AG3D's deformer and loss formulation for articulated human rendering. The 3D-aware super-resolution module departs from prior 2D super-resolution approaches (used in AG3D, CNeRF) by leveraging depth maps and semantic masks to guide 3D sampling, reducing per-pixel samples from 432 to 11 while preserving volumetric consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving the first fully semantic-disentangled 3D human generation at 1024² resolution, this work unlocks practical applications like 3D garment generation, semantic-aware virtual try-on, and out-of-distribution synthesis that were previously infeasible. The 3D-aware super-resolution module is a general-purpose component applicable to other NeRF-based generative models, potentially lowering the compute barrier for high-resolution 3D synthesis broadly. Key open questions remain: the method is limited to the DeepFashion dataset (8K pairs), struggles with rare poses and hand generation, and relies entirely on 2D supervision, making accurate geometry a persistent challenge that may require 3D data augmentation.
