## Problem

Existing arbitrary-time video frame interpolation methods use a scalar timestep as the sole motion hint, forcing the network to implicitly learn a one-to-many time-to-location mapping. This velocity ambiguity—where objects could be accelerating, decelerating, or following curved paths—causes the model to average over plausible trajectories, producing blurry and imprecise intermediate frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240881/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB video frames (I_0, I_1) and a per-pixel distance ratio map D_t in [0,1] (replacing the scalar timestep), plus optionally a reference frame I_ref and its distance map D_ref for iterative estimation. At inference, a uniform map D_t(x,y)=t is used—identical in format to standard time indexing—so no additional optical flow computation is needed. The distance map can also be spatially varying (e.g., per-object via SAM segmentation), enabling independent temporal manipulation of individual objects. Training uses 448×256 septuplet sequences from Vimeo90K.

## Method

The paper proposes two plug-and-play strategies that require only modifying input channels of existing VFI networks. First, distance indexing replaces the ambiguous scalar timestep with a per-pixel distance ratio map D_t(x,y) = ‖V_{0→t}‖cosθ / ‖V_{0→1}‖, computed from RAFT optical flow during training, giving the network an explicit hint on how far each object has traveled and eliminating speed ambiguity. Second, iterative reference-based estimation addresses residual directional ambiguity (most severe at the midpoint) by breaking a long-range prediction into N shorter steps, where each step conditions on a previously estimated reference frame and its distance map, progressing from near to far. Both strategies are validated as drop-in improvements to RIFE, IFRNet, AMT, and EMA-VFI, yielding sharper outputs and higher perceptual quality (LPIPS, NIQE) without architectural changes.

## Results

On Vimeo90K septuplet with uniform distance maps (practical inference), the combined [D,R] strategy consistently improves perceptual quality across all four base models: RIFE (LPIPS 0.105→0.086, NIQE 6.663→6.220), IFRNet (LPIPS 0.088→0.078, NIQE 6.422→6.167), AMT-S (LPIPS 0.101→0.081, NIQE 6.866→6.326), and EMA-VFI (LPIPS 0.086→0.081, NIQE 6.736→6.227). Pixel-centric metrics (PSNR, SSIM) slightly decrease under uniform maps due to misalignment with ground truth, but the paper argues perceptual metrics are more relevant for VFI. On easier benchmarks (Adobe240, X4K1000FPS ×8), distance indexing [D]u even improves PSNR (e.g., 30.24→30.47 on Adobe240), confirming the constant-speed assumption holds well for smaller temporal gaps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper applies its plug-and-play strategies (distance indexing [D] and iterative reference-based estimation [R]) to four state-of-the-art VFI baselines: RIFE, IFRNet, AMT-S, and EMA-VFI. On perceptual metrics, [D]u outperforms [T] across all four models (e.g., RIFE NIQE 6.663→6.344; AMT-S NIQE 6.866→6.452), and the combined [D,R]u achieves the best perceptual scores in every case. The largest gains appear for AMT-S, where NIQE drops by 0.54 (6.866→6.326) and LPIPS by 0.020 (0.101→0.081), suggesting the base model suffers most from velocity ambiguity. On pixel-centric metrics (PSNR, SSIM), the uniform-map variants slightly underperform [T] due to spatial misalignment, but on easier benchmarks (Adobe240, X4K1000FPS ×8) [D]u actually improves PSNR (30.24→30.47, 36.36→36.80), indicating the constant-speed approximation is well-suited to smaller temporal gaps. A user study with 30 participants confirms [D,R] as the top-rated variant, aligning with the quantitative findings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the arbitrary-time VFI paradigm established by RIFE, IFRNet, AMT, and EMA-VFI, but departs from their shared reliance on a scalar timestep as the sole motion cue. It introduces 'distance indexing'—replacing the ambiguous scalar with a pixel-wise distance ratio map derived from optical flow—and an iterative reference-based estimation strategy that decomposes long-range interpolation into shorter steps, drawing conceptual inspiration from iterative solvers in optical flow and diffusion models. Unlike Zhou et al. [55], which implicitly addresses motion ambiguity via a texture consistency loss, this work explicitly reformulates the input to eliminate the one-to-many time-to-position mapping at the source, requiring only input-channel modifications to existing architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of velocity ambiguity as the root cause of blur in arbitrary-time VFI provides a clear diagnostic that prior work lacked, and the plug-and-play nature of the fix makes it immediately adoptable across the VFI ecosystem. The per-object manipulation capability (pairing distance curves with SAM masks) opens a new editing dimension—re-timing individual objects independently—that has no direct precedent in VFI. Open questions include whether accurate per-pixel distance maps computed from multiple intermediate frames can close the remaining gap between uniform-map inference and ground-truth-guided training, and whether the constant-speed assumption breaks down in highly non-linear motion regimes.
