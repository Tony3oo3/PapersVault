## Problem

In weakly supervised incremental semantic segmentation (WILSS), a pixel can be simultaneously predicted as an old class by the pre-trained model and as a new class by seed areas derived from image-level labels, creating an unresolvable conflict since no pixel-level annotations exist for new classes. This conflicting prediction issue is compounded by catastrophic forgetting, as the model must retain old-class knowledge while learning new classes with only coarse supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240918/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, its image-level label vector for the new classes, and the predictions of a pre-trained segmentation model (DeepLab V3 with ResNet-101 or Wide-ResNet-38) over the old classes. It also queries SAM (ViT-H backbone) to generate m binary masks used for binarizing old-class predictions and fusing new-class predictions. The output is a per-pixel pseudo-label tensor covering all classes (old, new, and background) plus updated model parameters, with the TME constraint enforced at every pixel location during training.

## Method

Teddy introduces a tendency-driven mutual exclusivity (TME) constraint that stipulates, for every pixel, the binarized old-class prediction and the new-class seed area cannot both be foreground simultaneously (Eq. 5). Old-class predictions are binarized by matching SAM-generated binary masks against one-hot model outputs using an IoU-like threshold α, while new-class seed areas are produced via CAM with a focal penalty and a localization loss that discourages overlap with old-class regions. Under the TME constraint, pseudo masks for new classes are formed by fusing SAM-based and CAM-based predictions with learned coefficients U and V, and the entire objective is cast as a bi-level optimization problem where the inner problem (solving for U and V) admits a closed-form indicator-function solution, enabling on-the-fly updates alongside the outer parameter optimization. This design simultaneously resolves prediction conflicts, preserves old-class accuracy against catastrophic forgetting, and produces high-quality pixel-level supervision for the new classes.

## Results

On the 15-5 VOC overlap setting, Teddy achieves 72.0 mIoU overall (77.6 old, 51.4 new), surpassing the best fully-supervised method PLOP (70.1) by 1.9 mIoU and the best weakly-supervised baseline WILSON (67.2) by 4.8 mIoU. On the more challenging 10-2 VOC multi-step setting, Teddy reaches 43.1 mIoU overall, exceeding WILSON (32.5) by 10.6 mIoU, with a particularly large gain on old classes (50.3 vs. 38.7). On COCO-to-VOC, Teddy attains 41.5 mIoU, outperforming all supervised and weakly-supervised baselines. Across all five settings, Teddy consistently achieves the best overall mIoU among image-level-supervised methods and matches or exceeds the strongest pixel-level-supervised incremental methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Teddy outperforms the direct weakly-supervised predecessor WILSON [7] across all five settings, with the largest margin on the multi-step 10-2 VOC protocol (+10.6 mIoU overall, +11.6 on old classes) and a notable +4.8 mIoU on 15-5 VOC. Against other image-level-supervised WSSS baselines (CAM, SEAM, SS, EPS), Teddy leads by 5–13 mIoU depending on the setting, confirming that the TME constraint and SAM-based binarization substantially improve pseudo-label quality. Among pixel-level-supervised incremental methods, Teddy (72.0 on 15-5 VOC) surpasses PLOP (70.1), SDR (69.9), and MiB (69.0), while on 10-10 VOC it is within 0.9 mIoU of the best supervised method SDR (67.4). On COCO-to-VOC, Teddy (41.5) exceeds all supervised baselines including LWF (40.3) and ILT (39.3). The gap to the Joint upper bound (75.4 on 15-5 VOC) remains 3.4 mIoU, indicating room for further improvement, but Teddy is the first image-level-supervised method to surpass all pixel-level-supervised incremental baselines on the 15-5 VOC setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on WILSON (Cermelli et al., CVPR 2022), which introduced the WILSS task and its seed-area-based pseudo-supervision pipeline, and on SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) for class-agnostic mask generation. The key departure is the Tendency-driven Mutual Exclusivity (TME) constraint, which formalizes a pixel-level non-overlap rule between binarized old-class predictions and new-class seed areas — a mechanism absent from prior WILSS or ILSS methods that typically handle old/new class interactions through regularization or replay. Methodologically, the paper introduces a bi-level optimization formulation that jointly solves for model parameters and fusion coefficients (U, V) between SAM-derived masks and CAM-derived pseudo-labels, going beyond the fixed-weight fusion used in prior weakly supervised segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TME constraint provides a principled, single-mechanism solution to two intertwined problems in WILSS — conflicting predictions and catastrophic forgetting — which prior methods addressed separately or not at all. The substantial multi-step gains (e.g., +32.6% mIoU over WILSON in 10-2 VOC) suggest the constraint becomes increasingly valuable as forgetting accumulates, though this remains to be validated on larger-scale or longer-horizon benchmarks. Limitations include reliance on the computationally heavy SAM ViT-H backbone, the NP-hard bi-level optimization solved only approximately, and evaluation restricted to Pascal VOC and MS COCO. Open questions include whether TME generalizes to video segmentation, 3D segmentation, or settings with more than two incremental steps of large class batches.
