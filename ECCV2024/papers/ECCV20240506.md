## Problem

Existing image matting models exhibit two systematic biases rooted in dataset composition: contrast bias (under-predicting alpha in low-contrast regions) and transparency bias (leaking background texture into mid-transparent alpha mattes). These biases arise because training data is dominated by high-contrast, extreme-opacity samples, causing models to learn spurious correlations between contrast/transparency and alpha values rather than true foreground structure. The paper frames these as confounder-induced backdoor paths in a causal graph and proposes an intervention to sever them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240506/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

COIN is a model-agnostic wrapper that accepts the same inputs as the underlying matting backbone (RGB image plus trimap, background image, or user interaction depending on the base method) and produces a standard alpha matte. Internally, it constructs multi-scale confounder spaces at n=5 resolution levels, discretizing transparency and contrast each into 25 categories (K_t=K_c=25) using pretrained Swin-B patch features. The framework adds roughly 10% parameters and FLOPs on top of the base model, operating at 1024×1024 resolution in experiments.

## Method

The core mechanism applies do-calculus backdoor adjustment to compute P(A|do(I,F,B)), which algebraically reduces to an expectation over a joint confounder space Z = T×C: E_z[P(A|f,z)]. This expectation is implemented via a sub-space-wise multi-head attention mechanism where the attention scores emulate the conditional distribution P(z|I) or P(z|F), and the weighted sum over confounder feature vectors yields the expected confounder representation. The framework is inserted in two stages: first, image features are attended against Z to produce an intervened foreground representation; second, foreground features are attended against Z to produce the final alpha matte. Training uses standard alpha matte supervision at each scale plus Gaussian label smoothing (N(0,1.2)) on the attention scores to supervise the learned confounder distribution, making the approach plug-and-play across trimap-based, interaction-based, and guidance-free matting methods.

## Results

The COIN framework is model-agnostic and consistently improves all four matting baselines across both Composition-1K and Distinctions-646. On Composition-1K, COIN(MatteFormer) reduces SAD from 23.8 to 21.9 (−1.9) and MSE from 4.0 to 3.3 (−0.7); on Distinctions-646, the same baseline drops SAD from 21.9 to 19.3 (−2.6) and MSE from 6.6 to 5.1 (−1.5). Improvements are largest for guidance-free and flexible-guidance methods: COIN(DIIM) cuts SAD by 3.7–3.8 and COIN(dugMatting 0-sel) by 3.3–3.5 across the two datasets. The additional computational cost is modest, adding roughly 10.5% parameters and 10.6% FLOPs over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COIN is a plug-and-play framework applied on top of each baseline rather than a competing architecture, so every comparison is a before/after on the same backbone. Against MatteFormer (the strongest trimap-based baseline), COIN reduces SAD by 1.9 on Composition-1K and 2.6 on Distinctions-646, with corresponding drops of 0.7 and 1.5 in MSE. Against DIIM (click-based interactive matting), the gains are larger: SAD improves by 3.7 and 3.8, and Conn by 2.5 and 3.3, reflecting DIIM's greater susceptibility to dataset bias without a trimap. Against dugMatting in its guidance-free (0-sel) and one-selection (1-sel) modes, COIN cuts SAD by 3.3/2.1 on Composition-1K and 3.5/2.9 on Distinctions-646. The paper notes that improvements are consistently larger on Distinctions-646 than on Composition-1K, attributed to more pronounced dataset bias in the former, and that flexible-guidance and guidance-free methods benefit more than trimap-based ones because they lack the strong spatial prior that a trimap provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies Pearl's do-calculus and backdoor adjustment—well-established tools in causal inference—to the image matting domain, where prior work has focused on architectural improvements (transformers, GANs, affinity-based methods) without explicitly addressing dataset-induced biases. It is model-agnostic, sitting atop existing matting backbones (MatteFormer, DIIM, dugMatting) rather than proposing a new backbone, and uses a Normalized Weighted Geometric Mean (NWGM) approximation to shift causal expectations to the feature level. The causal-inference-for-bias-mitigation paradigm has been explored in segmentation, VQA, and classification, but this is the first systematic application to matting with a concrete identification of contrast and transparency confounders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit identification of contrast bias and transparency bias as measurable, dataset-driven phenomena gives the matting community a diagnostic lens that was previously implicit. The framework's model-agnostic design and ~10% computational overhead make it a practical plug-in for existing pipelines, and the finding that gains are larger on Distinctions-646 (more biased data) validates the causal diagnosis. Open questions include whether additional confounders (e.g., edge sharpness, color saturation) exist beyond the two identified, whether the approach transfers to video matting or other decomposition tasks, and whether the simplified two-confounder causal graph captures the full bias structure in real-world (non-composited) images.
