## Problem

3D reconstruction from hand-drawn sketches remains largely under-explored because sketches are inherently abstract, diverse in style, and ambiguous compared to natural images, making standard image-based reconstruction pipelines inadequate. Existing approaches that treat sketches as natural images fail to capture the geometric cues embedded in contour lines, and no large-scale paired sketch-voxel dataset existed to train and evaluate deep reconstruction networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241969/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular hand-drawn sketch, which is cropped to its bounding box, resized to 224×224, and binarized (threshold 50) to produce two parallel representations: a binary image for pixel-wise processing and a 2D point cloud of 256 points sampled via farthest point sampling from the foreground pixels. The output is a 32×32×32 voxel occupancy probability volume, where each voxel encodes the likelihood of being occupied in the reconstructed 3D shape.

## Method

Sketch2Vox employs a dual-channel encoder: SktConv extracts hierarchical pixel-wise features using the first four VGG-16 convolution groups (ImageNet-pretrained for transfer learning) plus two custom convolution blocks, while SktMPFM treats the sketch as a 2D point cloud and captures local and global geometric context through multi-scale 1D convolutions (filter sizes 1×2, 3×2, 5×2) with max-pooling for global aggregation. A Cross-modal Fusion Attention Module (CFAM) then aligns and fuses the two feature maps via Recurrent Criss-Cross Attention and six stacked Multi-Head Attention blocks (h=4 heads), adaptively weighting complementary pixel and point cues. The fused features are decoded into a coarse 32×32×32 volume by five 3D transposed convolution layers, then refined by a U-Net-style 3D encoder-decoder that corrects surface imperfections and outliers. Training uses a Mean Squared False Cross-Entropy Loss (MSFCEL) that separately penalizes false positives on unoccupied voxels and false negatives on occupied voxels, squared and summed to balance the two error types.

## Results

On the Sketch3D dataset (9 categories, hand-drawn sketches), Sketch2Vox achieves an overall IoU of 0.4301, outperforming the strongest baseline 3D-RETR (0.3898) by 4.0 percentage points and the next-best 3D-R2N2 (0.3305) by 10.0 points. On the synthetic ModelNet-based dataset (10 categories), Sketch2Vox reaches an overall IoU of 0.3698, surpassing 3D-R2N2 (0.3611) by 0.9 points and Pix2Vox (0.2903) by 8.0 points. The largest per-category gains on Sketch3D appear on chair (+6.4% over 3D-RETR) and table (+4.6% over 3D-RETR), where long thin structures are common.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sketch2Vox outperforms all four baselines on the overall IoU metric on both datasets. On Sketch3D, it leads 3D-RETR (the strongest competitor) by 4.0 points (0.4301 vs. 0.3898) and the remaining three methods (3D-R2N2, Pix2Vox, 3D-VAE-GAN) by roughly 10 points. On the synthetic ModelNet dataset, the margin over 3D-R2N2 narrows to 0.9 points (0.3698 vs. 0.3611), and Sketch2Vox is slightly behind 3D-R2N2 on four of ten categories (desk, dresser, table, toilet), though it still wins on six categories including bathtub, bed, chair, monitor, night_stand, and sofa. Notably, 3D-RETR is not evaluated on the ModelNet dataset, so the comparison there is limited to three baselines. The user study further confirms the advantage: Sketch2Vox scores 4.70 ± 0.94 on faithfulness and 4.57 ± 0.87 on quality (7-point Likert), significantly higher than 3D-R2N2 (3.39/3.37), 3D-VAE-GAN (3.56/3.54), and Pix2Vox (3.49/3.45), with p < 0.001 for both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the common practice of treating hand-drawn sketches as natural images for feature extraction, arguing that the intrinsic differences (abstraction, lack of color/texture, stylistic diversity) make standard CNN pipelines suboptimal. It repurposes VGG-16 as a pixel-level encoder while introducing a parallel point-cloud branch (SktMPFM) that treats sketch contours as 2D point sets, a formulation borrowed from point-cloud processing but applied here to 2D sketch geometry. The cross-modal fusion via criss-cross and multi-head attention, along with a U-Net-inspired 3D refiner, are assembled into an end-to-end pipeline that positions itself between constructive sketch-to-3D methods and recent deep-learning-based sketch reconstruction networks (e.g., Zhang et al.'s view-aware Sketch2Model).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is the release of a 12,000+ pair hand-drawn sketch–voxel dataset spanning diverse drawing skill levels, which the authors claim is the largest in this niche and addresses the long-standing lack of paired training data for sketch-based 3D reconstruction. However, the method is limited to 32×32×32 voxel resolution and a single monocular view, and the synthetic ModelNet dataset relies on Canny edges that may not fully capture the variability of real hand-drawn sketches. Open questions include whether the dual-channel (pixel + point) formulation generalizes to higher-resolution outputs, more complex or open shapes, and whether the point-based branch's outsized ablation contribution (9.4% IoU drop vs. 2.6% for the pixel branch) holds across architectures and scales.
