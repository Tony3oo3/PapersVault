## Problem

Existing text-to-3D clothed human generation methods encode the body and all garments as a single holistic model optimized in one stage, making clothing editing (replacement, transfer, reordering) infeasible and eliminating fine-grained control over the generation process. The paper addresses this by formulating the task as progressive, layer-wise generation of disentangled components from text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240646/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single textual description of a clothed person (e.g., 'a man wearing jeans, a denim shirt, and a windbreaker') and programmatically decomposes it into sub-prompts: one for the minimal-clothed body ('a man only wearing underwear') and paired prompts for each garment (a compositional prompt like 'a man only wearing cloth A' and a garment-only prompt like 'a piece of cloth A'). The output is a set of independent NeRFs—one for the body and one per garment—each trained at 256×256 for the first 5k iterations and 512×512 for the remaining 5k, taking roughly 3 hours per component on a single A100 GPU. Because components are separate, they can be freely recombined, swapped, or transferred to other bodies.

## Method

TELA represents each component (minimal-clothed body, individual garments) as a separate NeRF with multiresolution hash encoding, arranged in a layer-wise hierarchy from innermost to outermost. A progressive optimization strategy generates the body first using a 2D-skeleton-conditioned ControlNet for SDS, then sequentially adds each garment while freezing previously trained layers. Two key mechanisms enable high-quality disentanglement: (1) a transparency-based stratified compositional rendering that computes accumulated opacity along each ray to partition sample points between layers, so only the assigned NeRF contributes density and color to its region, preventing interpenetration; and (2) dual SDS losses that simultaneously supervise the compositional rendering (person wearing the garment, with skeleton conditioning) and a cloth-only rendering (vanilla SDS), forcing the garment NeRF to contain only clothing geometry rather than absorbing body parts. An SMPL 'A-pose' mesh provides the human prior for 3D bounding boxes and 2D skeleton projection, and a binary-entropy plus L1 sparsity regularization suppresses floating radiance artifacts.

## Results

On the primary quantitative metric, TELA achieves a FID of 125.9 between rendered images and Stable Diffusion outputs, outperforming the holistic baseline DreamWaltz (FID 142.6) by approximately 11.7 points. In a user study with 26 volunteers evaluating 30 text prompts, TELA receives a strong preference over DreamWaltz across all three criteria: 71.46% for overall clothed human quality, 77.92% for cloth quality, and 73.18% for consistency with text inputs. For 3D garment generation, TELA qualitatively outperforms DreamFusion, Magic3D, and ProlificDreamer by alleviating the multi-face problem, producing disentangled clothing, and generating hollow structures (e.g., sleeveless dresses).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamWaltz [17], the strongest holistic-modeling baseline, TELA wins on both the FID metric (125.9 vs. 142.6) and all three user-study preference metrics (71–78% preference), with the largest margin on cloth quality (77.92%). The advantage is attributed to disentangled layer-wise modeling that preserves fine-grained cloth details and alleviates the multi-face problem. For 3D garment generation, TELA is compared qualitatively against three general text-to-3D methods: DreamFusion [37], Magic3D [24], and ProlificDreamer [52]. TELA outperforms all three on multi-view consistency (by leveraging a 2D skeleton-conditioned diffusion model), disentanglement (clothes do not include body parts), and structural fidelity (producing hollow garments like sleeveless dresses). No quantitative metrics are reported for the garment-generation comparison, so the advantage is assessed visually.

## Relation to Prior Work

TELA extends the DreamFusion-style Score Distillation Sampling pipeline and DreamWaltz's 2D-skeleton-conditioned ControlNet approach, but departs fundamentally from the holistic single-NeRF representation used by prior text-to-3D human methods (DreamWaltz, DreamHuman, DreamAvatar). Instead of encoding body and all garments in one radiance field, it decomposes the clothed human into a minimal-clothed body NeRF plus per-garment NeRFs, trained sequentially. The stratified compositional rendering (using per-point transparency to partition ray segments across layers) is a new mechanism distinct from the Max/Mean fusion used in prior multi-layer NeRF work (SSDNeRF, [53]), and the dual SDS losses (supervising both the compositional clothed-human image and a cloth-only image) are a novel training strategy with no direct precedent in the text-to-3D literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing genuinely disentangled garment NeRFs, TELA unlocks clothing editing applications—virtual try-on, free garment swapping, and per-garment transfer—that are structurally infeasible under holistic representations, and it simultaneously provides a practical route to high-quality 3D garment generation conditioned on an underlying body. Key limitations temper its impact: each component requires ~3 hours of per-scene optimization on an A100, the NeRF representation does not integrate directly into mesh-based CG pipelines, and animation still relies on a holistic linear-blend-skinning module rather than per-component dynamics. Open questions include whether the progressive layer-wise strategy can be accelerated with feed-forward or few-step generation, whether per-component animation (e.g., cloth simulation on individual garment NeRFs) is tractable, and whether the approach generalizes to loose garments (dresses, skirts) beyond what the current SMPL-box spatial priors support.
