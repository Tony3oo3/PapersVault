## Problem

Standard Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods for sparse-view CT rely on Gaussian denoising networks as implicit priors, which are ineffective at removing task-specific artifacts such as sparse-view streaks. Naively substituting a restoration network into the PnP loop fails because the network is not trained to behave as a proximal operator at every intermediate iterate. Moreover, end-to-end unrolled training, which would naturally tailor the prior to the inverse problem, is computationally prohibitive for large-scale 3D cone-beam CT due to the cost of backpropagating through the forward operator at each iteration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse-view cone-beam projections (30, 50, or 100 out of 1200 or 720 total views) and an initial FDK reconstruction x₀ as the starting point of the iterative loop. The restoration network D is implemented as a 2D axial-wise DRUNet operating on 256×256 patches, while the forward and adjoint operators A and Aᵀ remain fully 3D cone-beam operators. The output is a full 3D CT volume (501³ for Walnut-CBCT, 1024³ for Cork-CBCT) after up to 500 accelerated PGD iterations with a relative-change stopping criterion of 10⁻⁴.

## Method

The Learned Proximal Trajectory (LPT) approach replaces the Gaussian denoiser in PnP-PGD with a restoration network D trained to approximate the proximal operator of the reconstruction loss R*(x) = ½‖x − x*‖². During an offline pre-training phase, a target PGD trajectory is computed using the closed-form proximal operator (a simple interpolation toward x*) and intermediate iterates x_{k+½} are stored on disk; D is then trained to predict x* from any sampled intermediate point, conditioned on the iteration index k. At inference, the learned proximal step is x_{k+1} = (x_{k+½} + γ·D(x_{k+½}, k)) / (1+γ) with γ = τλ, embedded in an accelerated PGD loop with inertial momentum. Convergence to a fixed point is guaranteed via d-demicontractive operator analysis (Cohen et al.) without requiring a Lipschitz constraint on D, provided the regularization step size satisfies γβ < 1 where β is the Lipschitz constant of D.

## Results

On the Walnut-CBCT dataset, LPT achieves the best PSNR across all sparse-view settings: 25.26 dB (30/1200 views), 27.98 dB (50/1200), and 29.63 dB (100/1200), outperforming PnP-FBS by 2.3–1.5 dB and FDK-UNet by 3.0–4.4 dB. On the Cork-CBCT dataset, LPT similarly leads in PSNR (32.11, 33.60, 34.11 dB for 30/50/100 out of 720 views), edging out FDK-UNet by 0.2–0.4 dB. FDK-UNet retains the best SSIM on both datasets (e.g., 0.865 vs. 0.806 at 50 views on Walnut-CBCT), while LPT achieves the lowest data-fidelity error (ℓ/m), confirming superior measurement consistency over purely learned post-processing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPT outperforms all five baselines on PSNR across both datasets and all view counts. Against the closest PnP competitor, PnP-FBS [47], LPT gains 2.3 dB at 30 views and 1.5 dB at 100 views on Walnut-CBCT, and 0.1–0.4 dB on Cork-CBCT. Against the supervised post-processing baseline FDK-UNet [25], LPT leads by 3.0 dB (30 views) to 4.4 dB (100 views) on Walnut-CBCT PSNR, though FDK-UNet achieves higher SSIM (0.865 vs. 0.806 at 50 views), suggesting LPT trades some structural similarity for greater measurement fidelity. InDI [17] is consistently the weakest learned method, trailing LPT by 5.2 dB (30 views) on Walnut-CBCT. The TV-regularized iterative baseline [48] and the analytical FDK reconstruction [21] are substantially outperformed, with FDK achieving only 12.83 dB PSNR at 30 views versus LPT's 25.26 dB. Notably, LPT achieves the lowest normalized data-fidelity error (ℓ/m) among all methods, confirming that its reconstructions remain faithful to the actual measurements, unlike FDK-UNet and InDI which can hallucinate structures not supported by the data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a middle ground between standard Plug-and-Play (PnP) methods, which use pre-trained Gaussian denoisers as implicit priors, and deep unrolled approaches, which train the entire iterative procedure end-to-end. It builds on the PnP-PGD framework (Venkatakrishnan et al., Ryu et al.) and the d-demicontractive convergence analysis of Cohen et al., but departs from both by replacing the Gaussian denoiser with a task-specific restoration network trained offline along a pre-defined optimization trajectory. The key conceptual departure from unrolled methods is that the restoration network is trained independently of the forward operator at inference time, avoiding the linear scaling of training cost with iteration count and forward-operator size that makes unrolling prohibitive for 3D cone-beam CT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach addresses a practical scalability bottleneck: unrolled methods are prohibitively expensive for large-scale 3D cone-beam CT, while standard PnP with Gaussian denoisers underperforms on task-specific artifacts. The finding that a restoration prior trained along an optimization trajectory outperforms both naive PnP and one-pass post-processing (FDK-UNet) in data fidelity while maintaining competitive PSNR is practically relevant for applications where measurement consistency matters (medical imaging, NDT). Open questions include whether the pre-defined trajectory can be replaced by on-the-fly sampling to avoid disk storage, whether the 2D axial-wise operator can be extended to fully 3D without prohibitive memory cost, and whether the framework generalizes to nonlinear forward operators beyond the linear CT model considered here.
