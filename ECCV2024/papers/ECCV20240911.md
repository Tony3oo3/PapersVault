## Problem

Existing active domain adaptation (ADA) methods for semantic segmentation rely on pixel-level labeling, requiring 11,535–26,215 annotated pixels per image, which is impractical. Superpixel-level active learning has been shown to reduce annotation cost in standard segmentation, but direct application to domain adaptation fails because inter-class feature confusion (e.g., road vs. sidewalk) causes superpixel fusion errors that degrade annotation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes fully labeled source-domain images (GTA5 or SYNTHIA) and unlabeled target-domain images (Cityscapes, cropped to 1024×512), plus a small set of superpixel-level active labels obtained via a dominant single-click scheme (640 clicks per image covering ~0.12% of pixels). Superpixels (~4,985 per image) are generated by a Superpixel Sampling Network (SSN) pretrained on the source domain. The output is a standard pixel-level segmentation mask over 19 Cityscapes classes.

## Method

The core contribution is a low-uncertainty superpixel fusion module that partitions superpixels into low-uncertainty (LUSP) and high-uncertainty (HUSP) groups using entropy from a UDA model, then fuses only LUSPs via Jensen-Shannon divergence thresholding while leaving HUSPs untouched to avoid inter-class confusion errors. A two-stage acquisition strategy first labels all merged large superpixels (MLUSPs, ~23 per image covering 82% of pixels) by size, then selects the most domain-discriminative HUSPs using a domain-difference score computed as 1 minus cosine similarity between predictions from a source-domain UDA model and a target-domain model. Training employs cross-domain mixing (transplanting source pixels onto target images) to mitigate domain shift, combined with pseudo labels and data-augmentation-based consistency regularization to counteract label noise inherent in superpixel annotations. The approach is architecture-agnostic, validated on DeepLabv2, DeepLabv3+, and SegFormer backbones.

## Results

On GTA5→Cityscapes, the proposed superpixel-level method achieves 70.7% mIoU with SegFormer using only 640 labeling clicks (0.122% of pixels) per image, matching or exceeding pixel-level ADA methods that require 11,535–26,215 clicks (2.2%–5%). Specifically, it surpasses UBD (70.1%) and LabOR (68.7%) at 11,535 clicks by 0.6% and 2.0% mIoU respectively, while remaining within 0.2% of RIPU (70.9%) at 26,215 clicks — a ~40× reduction in annotation cost. At the same 640-click budget, the method outperforms ASAL (63.6%) by 7.1% mIoU, demonstrating the effectiveness of the low-uncertainty superpixel fusion module. The upper bound (labeling all 873 merged superpixels) reaches 72.3% mIoU, closely matching the fully supervised result (72.3% at 4,985 superpixels).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixel-level ADA methods, the proposed superpixel approach at 640 clicks (70.7% mIoU, SegFormer) outperforms UBD (70.1%) and LabOR (68.7%) at 11,535 clicks by 0.6% and 2.0% mIoU, and is within 0.2% of RIPU (70.9%) at 26,215 clicks — a 40× cost reduction. Compared to D²ADA (71.2%, DeepLabv2, 26,215 clicks), the method is 0.5% lower in mIoU but uses only 640 clicks. Against the superpixel-level ASAL baseline at the same 640-click budget, the method gains 7.1% mIoU (70.7% vs. 63.6%), attributed to the low-uncertainty fusion module that avoids inter-class fusion errors. Versus UDA-only baselines (DAFormer 68.3%, RIPU-UDA 65.5%), the method improves by 2.4% and 5.2% mIoU respectively with minimal active labels. The upper bound of labeling all 873 merged superpixels (72.3%) matches the fully supervised upper bound (72.3% at 4,985 superpixels), confirming that the fusion module preserves annotation quality while reducing the labeling unit count by ~5.7×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: superpixel-level active learning for semantic segmentation (RSAL, ASAL) and pixel-level active domain adaptation (LabOR, RIPU, UBD, D²ADA). While ASAL introduced superpixel merging via Jensen-Shannon divergence for standard active learning, it fails in domain adaptation due to inter-class feature confusion; this paper departs from ASAL by excluding high-uncertainty superpixels from fusion and introduces a two-stage acquisition strategy (size-based for merged superpixels, domain-difference-based for unmerged ones) that has no direct precedent in prior ADA literature. The training pipeline repurposes cross-domain mixing and consistency regularization from UDA literature to handle the label noise inherent in superpixel-level annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× reduction in annotation cost (640 clicks vs. 26,215 pixels) while matching or exceeding prior SOTA makes active domain adaptation practically feasible in real-world deployment where pixel-level annotation budgets are prohibitive. The finding that ASAL's naive superpixel fusion degrades significantly under domain shift (69.1% vs. 72.6% upper bound) highlights a previously unaddressed failure mode that could affect other superpixel-based methods in cross-domain settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video segmentation, 3D point clouds, or settings where a pre-trained UDA model for entropy extraction is unavailable, and whether the two-stage acquisition strategy transfers to other dense prediction tasks.
