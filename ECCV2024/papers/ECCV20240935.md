## Problem

Existing language-conditioned robotic manipulation methods—whether perceptive (relying on multi-view cameras for 3D understanding) or generative (reconstructing static 3D geometry via NeRF)—ignore the scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics that describe how objects physically interact during manipulation. This omission causes agents to fail on tasks requiring correct object interaction, such as stacking or sliding, because the predicted actions do not account for how the scene will change as a result of the robot's motion.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a single front-camera RGB-D observation at 128×128 resolution, a 4-dimensional gripper proprioception vector (end-effector position, openness, timestep), and a natural-language instruction. During training, 20 calibrated cameras provide multi-view supervision for the Gaussian Splatting reconstruction. The output is a four-part action: a 100³ voxel translation target, a 72×3 rotation distribution, a scalar gripper openness in [0,1], and a scalar collision-avoidance signal in [0,1], predicted by a PerceiverIO decoder conditioned on the propagated Gaussian parameters and the language instruction.

## Method

ManiGaussian formulates a dynamic Gaussian Splatting framework in which each Gaussian primitive carries geometric parameters (position, rotation) that propagate across timesteps, while color, scale, opacity, and a Stable-Diffusion-distilled semantic feature remain time-invariant. A Gaussian world model—comprising a representation network, a multi-head Gaussian regressor, a deformation predictor, and a differentiable Gaussian renderer—reconstructs the future scene from the current observation and the robot action, and a future-scene consistency loss (L2 between rendered and ground-truth next-step images) forces the representation to encode physical interaction dynamics. Training combines four objectives: current-scene reconstruction, semantic-feature distillation (cosine distance to pretrained features), action prediction (cross-entropy via PerceiverIO), and the future-scene consistency term, with a 3k-iteration warm-up that freezes the deformation predictor before joint training. The visual input is lifted and voxelized into a volumetric representation before the Gaussian regressor infers per-primitive parameters, and SE(3) augmentation over 20 expert demonstrations per task supports generalization across 166 RLBench variants.

## Results

On 10 RLBench tasks (166 variations, 25 episodes each), ManiGaussian achieves a 44.8% average success rate, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art GNFactor (31.7%) by 13.1 percentage points (41.3% relative improvement) and the perceptive baseline PerAct (20.4%) by 24.4 points. The method also trains 2.29× faster than GNFactor while achieving 1.18× better performance. The largest per-task gains appear in 'sweep to dustpan' (64.0% vs. 28.0% for GNFactor) and 'drag stick' (92.0% vs. 37.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ManiGaussian outperforms both baselines across nearly all 10 tasks. Against the perceptive method PerAct (20.4% avg), ManiGaussian gains 24.4 points, with particularly dramatic improvements on tasks requiring geometric reasoning such as 'sweep to dustpan' (64.0% vs. 0.0%) and 'drag stick' (92.0% vs. 5.3%). Against the generative method GNFactor (31.7% avg), which uses a generalizable NeRF for scene reconstruction, ManiGaussian achieves a 13.1-point absolute gain (41.3% relative), driven primarily by its ability to model scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics via future scene reconstruction. The only task where ManiGaussian does not achieve the best result is 'meat off grill' (60.0%), where PerAct with 4 cameras scores 65.3%; however, ManiGaussian still ranks second, ahead of GNFactor (57.3%). The ablation (Table 2) further confirms that the dynamic Gaussian Splatting component (deformation predictor + future scene consistency loss) contributes the largest single gain of 4.4 points over the geometry+semantics-only variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from static scene reconstruction to a dynamic manipulation setting, where Gaussian primitives propagate over time to encode object interactions. It departs from perceptive manipulation methods (PerAct, PolarNet, Act3D) that predict actions directly from 2D/3D visual features, and from generative NeRF-based methods (GNFactor) that reconstruct scenes but ignore spatiotemporal dynamics. The key conceptual departure is repurposing Gaussian Splatting not merely as a rendering tool but as the explicit state space of a world model, where a deformation field predicts future Gaussian parameters conditioned on robot actions, and future-scene reconstruction provides self-supervised dynamics learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that encoding scene-level spatiotemporal dynamics in an explicit 3D representation (Gaussian Splatting) yields both higher success rates (44.8% vs. 31.7% for GNFactor) and faster training (2.29×) compared to implicit NeRF-based approaches, suggesting explicit scene representations may be more sample-efficient for manipulation. However, the method still requires multi-view camera supervision with calibration, limiting deployment in truly unstructured single-camera settings, and all results are confined to RLBench simulation with modest absolute success rates. Open questions include whether the Gaussian world model generalizes to real-world environments, scales to longer-horizon or multi-object tasks, and whether the Stable Diffusion semantic distillation is necessary or if lighter-weight features suffice.
