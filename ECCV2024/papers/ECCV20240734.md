## Problem

Fast adversarial training (FAT) methods that use single-step FGSM attacks are highly susceptible to catastrophic overfitting, where adversarial accuracy suddenly collapses to 0% under multi-step attacks. Existing mitigations (FGSM-RS, FGSM-GA, FGSM-MEP) delay but cannot prevent this collapse, and none provide a mechanism to recover once the inner optimization problem has already failed. The paper identifies the root cause as the collapse of the inner optimization in the bi-level optimization framework and asks whether a FAT method can both prevent and rectify this collapse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) with class labels and a perturbation budget ε=8/255. During training, it generates adversarial examples by applying an amplified FGSM perturbation (γ·δ_t with γ=2) to the previous epoch's AEs rather than to clean inputs, then fuses the two. The output is a trained CNN (ResNet18, PreActResNet18, or WideResNet34-10) whose weights are evaluated under six attack types (FGSM, PGD-10/20/50, C&W, APGD, Square, AutoAttack) at both the best and last checkpoints.

## Method

FGSM-PCO generates current-stage adversarial examples by applying an amplified FGSM step (γ·δ_t) to the previous epoch's AEs, then fuses them with the historical AEs via a convex combination x_train = λ_t·x_{t-1}* + (1−λ_t)·x_am*. The fusion ratio λ_t is set adaptively to the model's classification confidence on the current AEs, so when overfitting begins and confidence drops, training automatically shifts toward the still-effective historical AEs. A tailored regularization loss L_PCO adds an L2-consistency term that penalizes divergence between the fused AE's prediction and the previous AE's prediction while rewarding closeness to the current AE's prediction, guiding the model away from the collapsed inner-optimum. This dual mechanism—adaptive fusion plus consistency regularization—enables FGSM-PCO to both prevent the onset of catastrophic overfitting and recover a model that has already entered the overfitting regime, a capability absent from prior FAT methods.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18, FGSM-PCO achieves 56.32% PGD-10 accuracy and 82.05% clean accuracy, outperforming the multi-step PGD-AT baseline (53.19% PGD-10) by 3.1% while training in 60 minutes vs. 199 minutes. On CIFAR-100 with WideResNet34-10, FGSM-PCO reaches 29.80% PGD-10 and 65.80% clean accuracy at the best checkpoint. On Tiny-ImageNet with PreActResNet18, FGSM-PCO obtains 18.17% PGD-10 and 34.96% clean accuracy. Notably, FGSM-PCO shows no catastrophic overfitting across 10 repeated runs on CIFAR-100 (0/10 overfitting events), whereas all other FAT methods overfit in 6–10 out of 10 runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGSM-PCO outperforms the multi-step PGD-AT baseline by 3.1% on PGD-10 (56.32% vs. 53.19%) on CIFAR-10 while training 3.3× faster (60 vs. 199 min). Against the strongest single-step baseline FGSM-MEP, FGSM-PCO gains 1.2% on PGD-10 (56.32% vs. 55.13%) with comparable training time (60 vs. 57 min). The advantage over earlier FAT methods is substantial: FGSM-PCO surpasses FGSM-RS by 14.9%, FGSM-GA by 10.8%, and Free-AT by 12.2% on PGD-10, and critically avoids the catastrophic overfitting that causes these methods to drop to 0% accuracy at the last checkpoint. On CIFAR-100 (WideResNet34-10), FGSM-PCO achieves 29.80% PGD-10 vs. 23.77% for FGSM-MEP and 32.36% for PGD-AT, while on Tiny-ImageNet it reaches 18.17% PGD-10 vs. 16.81% for FGSM-MEP and 15.35% for PGD-AT. The paper further demonstrates that FGSM-PCO can recover a model already in catastrophic overfitting (switching from FGSM-AT or FGSM-MEP mid-training), a capability no other compared method possesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fast adversarial training (FAT) lineage that began with FGSM-based single-step attacks (Goodfellow et al.) and was refined by methods like FGSM-RS (random initialization), FGSM-GA (regularization), and FGSM-MEP (momentum-based prior-guided initialization). Its key departure is reframing catastrophic overfitting as a collapse of the inner optimization in a bi-level formulation, and proposing an adaptive fusion of historical and current adversarial examples whose ratio is governed by the model's own classification confidence, rather than relying on accumulated gradient momentum or fixed randomization. A tailored L1-consistency regularization loss further distinguishes the method from prior FAT variants that only modify the attack or initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in FGSM-PCO's demonstrated ability to both prevent and recover from catastrophic overfitting—a capability no prior FAT method claims—evidenced by 0/10 overfitting occurrences versus 6–10/10 for competing methods on CIFAR100 with WideResNet34-10. However, the fusion coefficient's reliance on per-class confidence may degrade in open-world or very high-cardinality settings, and the method still requires storing previous-epoch AEs, introducing a memory overhead. An open question is whether the adaptive fusion principle generalizes beyond the single-step FGSM setting to multi-step or adaptive attack schedules, and how to more efficiently bridge information across training epochs without the current per-sample storage cost.
