## Problem

Reconstructing 3D point clouds from unseen classes using a single 2D image remains challenging because global priors learned in object-centered coordinates fail to generalize to new categories, while viewer-centered approaches that do generalize require additional information (camera poses, category shape priors) and suffer from lower accuracy due to large geometry variation across classes and poses. The paper addresses the gap of achieving both strong generalization to unseen classes and high reconstruction fidelity in the object-centered coordinate system without any auxiliary inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D RGB image as input and produces a 3D point cloud of 2048 points in the object-centered (canonical) coordinate system. The output is structured as M=8 spatial regions, each decomposed into N=8 learned patterns of P=256 points, with a final modularization shift applied per region. No camera parameters, segmentation labels, or category-specific shape priors are required at inference time.

## Method

The core contribution is learning a class-agnostic local prior through a three-stage pipeline: (1) an encoder-decoder produces an initial coarse shape S from the image using a weak Chamfer Distance loss (α=0.1) to avoid bias toward seen classes; (2) S is voxelized into M=8 regions, and N=8 region patterns are learned per region by MLPs that transform grid-sampled points in a local coordinate system, then a pattern modularizer re-expresses each region as a weighted combination of all patterns; (3) a modularization customizer (ResNet-style MLP) predicts a per-point shift t_m conditioned on the image latent code f_I to inject geometry details into the pattern-modularized regions. The key insight is that local geometric structures are shared across object categories, so a small set of learned patterns serves as a generalizable basis in object-centered coordinates, and the image-conditioned customization step recovers class-specific details without requiring large pattern sets or additional supervision.

## Results

On ShapeNet (1024 points, trained on only 3 classes: Chair, Plane, Car), the method achieves L1-CD of 0.0527 on seen classes and 0.0540 on unseen classes, outperforming the best object-centered baseline DRWR (0.0536 seen / 0.0715 unseen) and the best viewer-centered baseline GSIR (0.0680 seen / 0.0990 unseen). Across 10 unseen ShapeNet classes (Table 3), the method achieves a mean L1-CD of 0.064, substantially below DRWR (0.088), GenRe (0.106), and AtlasNet (0.127). On Pixel3D unseen classes (Table 5), the method achieves the best L1-CD on all five categories (e.g., Wardrobe 0.067 vs. GSIR 0.103, Sofa 0.074 vs. GSIR 0.083).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 20 baselines on unseen-class reconstruction. Against viewer-centered methods (DRC, MarrNet, GenRe, GSIR), it achieves 0.0540 vs. 0.0990–0.1270 L1-CD on unseen classes, a 45–58% relative improvement, while also matching or beating them on seen classes. Against object-centered point-cloud methods (AtlasNet, GraphX, DRWR), it reduces unseen L1-CD from 0.0715–0.1260 to 0.0540, with DRWR being the closest competitor (0.0715). Against implicit-function methods (3D43D, SDFNet, HPN, Point-e) at 2048 points, it achieves 0.058 mean L1-CD versus 0.111 (HPN) and 0.130 (Point-e), despite using far fewer training queries. On seen classes (Table 1, 13 ShapeNet categories, 2048 points), the method achieves 0.035 mean L1-CD and 0.776 IoU, beating SDT (0.039 / 0.750) and GraphX (0.041 / 0.750). Notably, the method requires no camera poses, no category shape priors, and no segmentation supervision, unlike GSIR (needs category prior) and GenRe (needs camera parameters), making it strictly more generalizable in setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes AtlasNet's pattern-generation mechanism (transforming 2D-sampled points into 3D patches) for a fundamentally different purpose: instead of generating a global shape from a set of patches, it learns a small set of class-agnostic local region patterns that serve as a shared basis for representing arbitrary local geometry across classes. It departs from the dominant paradigm for unseen-class reconstruction, which either learns a global prior in viewer-centered coordinates (GenRe, GSIR, MarrNet) for generalization at the cost of accuracy, or in object-centered coordinates (AtlasNet, DRWR, GraphX) for accuracy at the cost of generalization. The key conceptual move is showing that a *local* prior in object-centered coordinates can achieve both, by decomposing the shape into regions and representing each as a modularized combination of shared patterns plus an image-conditioned shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that interpretability and generalization need not be sacrificed for accuracy in unseen-class 3D reconstruction, offering a practical alternative to implicit-function approaches that require thousands of 3D queries per shape. The region-pattern decomposition provides a transparent, inspectable intermediate representation that could aid downstream tasks like part-level editing or shape analysis. Limitations include evaluation only on synthetic ShapeNet/Pixel3D with simple MLP backbones, a fixed number of regions and patterns, and reliance on ground-truth point clouds during training; whether the local-prior strategy transfers to real-world images, more complex geometries, or larger-scale datasets remains open.
