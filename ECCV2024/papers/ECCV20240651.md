## Problem

Object counting datasets rely on 2D point annotations whose positions vary due to annotator subjectivity, introducing ambiguity that degrades counting model training. Existing noise-resistance methods (BL, NoiseCC, RSI) make models tolerant to this inconsistency during training but do not fix the annotations themselves, and they are primarily designed for density-map-based approaches. This paper asks whether directly refining the point annotations to be more consistent before training can serve as a broadly applicable, model-agnostic solution.

## Input / Output

The SAE takes an RGB image (cropped to 512×512, or 128×128 for ADI) together with its 2D point annotations as input and produces a 2-channel restoration vector field F ∈ R^{2×H×W} (x and y displacement components at every pixel). During the restoration phase, the initial annotation coordinates are used to sample F, and the sampled vectors are added to the original coordinates to yield the refined point set. The refined annotations are then fed to any downstream counting model (density-map or localization-based) as the training supervision, replacing the original manual points.

## Method

SAE is a lightweight UNet with a VGG16 backbone trained in a denoising-autoencoder fashion analogous to MAE: random 2D shift vectors (angle uniform in (0,2π), magnitude uniform in (0, r_i)) are added to the initial point annotations to create shifted points, and the network is trained via MSE to predict a restoration vector field that maps each shifted point back to its original location. The shift radius r_i = α × min(d_i, d̄_i^{N_3}) is set from nearest-neighbor distances to approximate each object's spatial extent while avoiding overlap between sampling regions. The key insight is that, by training across many similar objects with diverse random shifts, the network is compelled to learn a general positional prior (the common distribution center of annotations for a given object type) and discard the specific per-annotator offset noise, exactly as MAE learns generic image structure while ignoring instance-specific details. At inference, the initial manual annotations are treated as 'shifted' points and restored by the learned vector field, yielding a more consistent annotation set that reduces training ambiguity for any downstream counting model.

## Results

On crowd counting, SAE-refined annotations yield average reductions of 3.0 MAE and 6.4 MSE across four datasets (SH PartA/B, UCF-QNRF, JHU-Crowd++) when applied to P2PNet, MAN, and STEERER. On remote sensing object counting (RSOC), SAE delivers average improvements of 9.2 MAE and 40.6 MSE over P2PNet, ASPDNet, and PSGCNet. On cell counting, SAE sets new state-of-the-art on all three datasets (MBM, ADI, DCC), e.g., reducing SAUNet's MAE from 5.7 to 4.2 on MBM and from 3.0 to 2.6 on DCC. In total, SAE establishes new SOTA records on nine of the eleven evaluated datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAE is not a standalone counting model but an annotation-refinement pre-processing step; it is evaluated by re-training existing counting methods with SAE-restored points versus original points. Across all six directly compared counting methods (BL, NoiseCC, RSI, P2PNet, MAN, STEERER) and all eleven datasets, SAE consistently improves MAE and MSE with no regressions observed. The largest single-dataset gains appear on JHU-Crowd++ (BL: MAE 74.4→60.8, MSE 290.0→230.7) and RSOC_Small-vehicle (ASPDNet: MAE 252.6→182.0, MSE 718.5→353.4), where annotation inconsistency is most pronounced. SAE also stacks additively with dedicated anti-noise methods: BL&SAE outperforms BL alone by an average of 4.1 MAE / 11.5 MSE on crowd counting, and NoiseCC&SAE outperforms NoiseCC by similar margins, confirming that annotation refinement and loss-level noise resistance are complementary. On the strongest baselines (MAN, STEERER, P2PNet), SAE still yields 2–5 MAE improvements, and MAN&SAE achieves the best overall crowd counting results (e.g., 49.7 MAE on JHU++, 5.4 MAE on SH PartB). On cell counting, SAE pushes SAUNet to new SOTA on all three benchmarks (MBM 4.2, ADI 11.2, DCC 2.6 MAE), surpassing the previous best by 1.1–3.1 MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the perturbation-then-restore paradigm of Masked Autoencoders (MAE) from image reconstruction to point annotation restoration, repurposing the core insight that reconstruction training captures general patterns while discarding specific noise. It departs from the dominant line of work on annotation inconsistency in object counting (ADSCNet, BL, RSI, NoiseCC), which modifies loss functions or network components to make counting models more tolerant to noisy labels during training. Instead, SAE operates as a dataset-level preprocessing step that refines annotations before any counting model is trained, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing anti-noise methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a lightweight UNet-based preprocessor that requires no modification to downstream counting models and consistently improves both density-map-based and localization-based methods across three distinct domains (crowd, remote sensing, cell counting). The radius estimation strategy (nearest-neighbor distance) is acknowledged as crude, and the authors note that depth estimation or other size cues could improve it, though at the cost of universality. An open question is whether this annotation-refinement philosophy generalizes to other point-supervised tasks beyond counting (e.g., keypoint detection, pose estimation), and whether the approach degrades gracefully when object sizes vary dramatically within a single image.
