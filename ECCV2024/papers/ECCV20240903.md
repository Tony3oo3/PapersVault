## Problem

Existing black-box substitute attack methods train the substitute model to mimic only the outputs or intra-class decision boundaries of the target model, ignoring the inter-class positional relationships in the decision space. This gap causes adversarial samples crafted on the substitute model to be misclassified into non-target classes by the target model, severely limiting targeted attack success rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240903/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes clean RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet), a target class label, and black-box outputs (probabilities or labels) from the target model. In the data-free setting, training data is synthesized via Stable Diffusion using the LCA strategy. The output is a trained substitute model from which adversarial samples are generated using standard white-box optimizers (FGSM, BIM, PGD) with ε=8/255 and α=2/255, evaluated by their success rate against the target model.

## Method

ICTA introduces a Position Exploration Sample (PES) that perturbs a clean image along the full gradient direction (not just the sign) of the substitute model toward an arbitrary class, with perturbation strength φ drawn from N(0,3) and no pixel clipping or iteration, yielding a much broader exploration of the decision space than traditional adversarial samples. The training loss combines a knowledge distillation term L_KD (aligning outputs at clean samples for intra-class consistency) with a relative position loss L_RP (aligning outputs at PES locations for inter-class consistency), both using the DIST metric, plus a cross-entropy term: L_all = λ₁L_KD + λ₂ΣL_RPⁱ + λ₃L_CE. By constraining the substitute model to match the target model's responses at multiple PES locations in different directions and distances, the inter-class topology of the two models is aligned, enabling the substitute-generated adversarial samples to successfully fool the target model into the intended target class.

## Results

ICTA achieves attack success rates of 99.19% (CIFAR-10/ResNet34), 99.25% (CIFAR-100/ResNet50), and 93.39% (Tiny-ImageNet/VGG19) for non-target PGD attacks, and 91.40%, 74.49%, and 33.41% for target PGD attacks, respectively. The most dramatic gains appear in target attacks, where ICTA surpasses the strongest baseline LCA by 11.42 pp on CIFAR-10, 22.99 pp on CIFAR-100, and 17.96 pp on Tiny-ImageNet. In the full-data scenario, ICTA's target attack success rate reaches 83%+ on CIFAR-10, 67% on CIFAR-100, and 44% on Tiny-ImageNet relative to the corresponding white-box attack (RP > 0.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICTA outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and target model in both non-target and target attacks. Against the GAN-based methods MAZE [9], DaST [37], and DST [27], ICTA achieves substantially higher ASR (e.g., 91.40% vs. 16.65%, 18.61%, and 19.33% on CIFAR-10/ResNet34 target PGD), confirming that output-mimicking alone is insufficient. Against Zhang et al. [35], which aligns intra-class decision boundaries, ICTA gains 27.35 pp on CIFAR-10 target attacks (91.40% vs. 64.05%) and 18.41 pp on Tiny-ImageNet (33.41% vs. 15.01%), demonstrating that inter-class topology alignment is critical for targeted misclassification. Against the strongest baseline LCA [21] (diffusion-based data generation with L2 loss), ICTA leads by 11.42 pp on CIFAR-10 target PGD (91.40% vs. 79.98%) and 22.99 pp on CIFAR-100 (74.49% vs. 51.50%), while also improving non-target ASR by 1.89 pp on CIFAR-10 (99.19% vs. 97.30%). The advantage is most pronounced on Tiny-ImageNet (200 classes), where the richer inter-class topology makes alignment more impactful: ICTA reaches 93.39% non-target and 33.41% target ASR versus LCA's 70.58% and 15.45%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the black-box substitute attack line of work that has progressed from simple output-matching (L2/KL-divergence in MAZE, DaST) to graph-structured multi-output alignment (DST) and intra-class decision boundary alignment (Zhang et al.). ICTA departs from this trajectory by arguing that intra-class consistency is insufficient and proposing to align the inter-class topology — the directional and distance relationships among all class centroids in the decision space. The Position Exploration Sample (PES), which uses the full gradient without iteration or pixel clipping, is a novel mechanism for probing this topology, and the method builds on LCA's diffusion-based data generation pipeline for the data-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that substitute model accuracy does not correlate with attack effectiveness challenges the implicit assumption that better output-mimicry yields better transfer, and the t-SNE visualizations provide intuitive confirmation that inter-class topology is the relevant geometric quantity. The particularly large gains in target attacks (e.g., 91.4% vs. 80% on CIFAR-10) address a long-standing weakness of substitute attacks, where targeted misclassification has been far harder than untargeted. Open questions include whether the inter-class topology alignment generalizes beyond CNN-based classifiers to transformer architectures, whether the PES mechanism can be adapted to query-based or transfer-based black-box settings, and whether the approach remains effective when the target model is significantly larger or architecturally different from the substitute.
