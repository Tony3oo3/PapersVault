## Problem

Existing click-based interactive segmentation methods that employ multiple click prompts to capture diverse user intentions suffer from homogeneous prompt-activated masks, because directly matching all prompts to the same visual features pushes them to converge to a single point. This lack of implicit diversity constraints limits the range of user intentions that can be captured, leading to suboptimal mask predictions especially when the user's intent is ambiguous.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 448×448 RGB image, positive and negative clicks (encoded as disk maps), and the previous round's segmentation mask as input. Only positive clicks are used to initialize N=5 click prompts (each of dimension d) via masked average pooling over positional encodings and visual features; negative clicks are conveyed solely through the disk map channel. The output is a single binary segmentation mask at the feature resolution, produced by a lightweight convolutional decoder that fuses visual features with the optimized multi-prompt score maps.

## Method

CPlot introduces two modules on top of a ViT-B + FPN backbone: (1) a Prompt-Pixel Alignment Module (PPAM) that uses L=2 plain transformer blocks (cross-attention, self-attention, FFN) to align click prompts with visual features in a shared embedding space, ensuring all prompts encode consistent region-of-interest information; and (2) a Click Prompt Optimal Transport Module (CPOT) that treats flattened visual features and aligned click prompts as two discrete distributions and computes an optimal transport plan via the Sinkhorn algorithm (cosine-distance cost), which assigns each pixel to a distinct prompt and thereby forces prompts to focus on different visual regions. Training uses a two-stage strategy: the inner loop freezes visual features and solves the OT problem, while the outer loop backpropagates through the differentiable Sinkhorn solution to update click prompts and all other parameters. The total loss combines focal loss on the final mask, focal loss on the mean of the N prompt-activated masks, and an argmin focal loss term, all weighted by hyperparameters α and β.

## Results

CPlot achieves state-of-the-art NoC85/NoC90 across all five natural image benchmarks under both SBD and COCO+LVIS training settings. With COCO+LVIS training, CPlot requires only 1.21 clicks for 85% IoU on GrabCut, 3.26 on DAVIS, and 1.98 on Pascal VOC, consistently outperforming the closest competitor SimpleClick (1.38, 3.66, 2.06 respectively). On medical benchmarks (mIoU@10), CPlot reaches 95.33 on ssTEM, 88.96 on BraTS, and 78.45 on OAIZIB without any medical fine-tuning, surpassing SimpleClick by 1.6–2.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPlot outperforms the closest ViT-based competitor SimpleClick on all five natural benchmarks under both training settings, with the largest gains on DAVIS (3.26 vs 3.66 NoC85 with C+L training) and GrabCut (1.21 vs 1.38 NoC85). Against the generalist model SAM, CPlot reduces SBD NoC85 from 6.50 to 3.37 (C+L), a 3.13-click improvement, confirming that task-specific click-prompt learning substantially outperforms general-purpose prompt handling. CPlot also beats PseudoClick on GrabCut, Berkeley, SBD, and DAVIS with C+L training, though PseudoClick retains a slight edge on Pascal VOC (1.94 vs 1.98 NoC85). On medical benchmarks, CPlot leads SimpleClick by 1.61 mIoU on ssTEM, 1.98 on BraTS, and 2.40 on OAIZIB without any domain-specific fine-tuning. Overall, CPlot is the consistent top performer across all eight evaluation benchmarks, with no clear loss to any single baseline on the primary NoC metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPlot builds directly on the SimpleClick architecture (ViT-B backbone with feature pyramid, click encoder, and mask decoder) and adapts SAM's multi-click-prompt paradigm, but departs from both by introducing optimal transport as a structural regularizer on the click prompts. The key conceptual departure is from prior multi-prompt approaches (SAM, SEEM, latent diversity methods) that generate multiple masks without explicit constraints on prompt diversity; CPlot identifies that unconstrained prompts collapse to a single point and uses the Sinkhorn algorithm to enforce a one-to-one mapping between prompts and distinct visual regions. The prompt-pixel alignment module (PPAM) draws on cross-attention transformer blocks for cross-modal alignment, while the CPOT module repurposes discrete optimal transport—previously applied to domain adaptation and vision-language matching—as a mechanism for prompt diversification in segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated failure mode in click-based interactive segmentation: homogeneous prompt-activated masks that fail to capture ambiguous user intent. The OT-based regularization is a lightweight, parameter-free mechanism (no extra network layers) that could be adapted to other multi-prompt or multi-query settings beyond segmentation. However, the improvements over SimpleClick are consistent but modest (e.g., 3.05 vs. 3.28 NoC85 on SBD), and the authors acknowledge limitations with thin structures and complex medical anatomy. Open questions include whether the OT formulation scales to larger prompt sets or higher-resolution features, and whether the two-stage optimization could be further simplified or made fully end-to-end without the Sinkhorn inner loop.
