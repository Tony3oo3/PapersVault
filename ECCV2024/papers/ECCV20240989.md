## Problem

Current video datasets contain egomotion and multi-agent interaction examples separately but rarely both simultaneously, and existing egocentric datasets focus on humans or vehicles rather than animals. This gap limits the ability to learn animal-like perception and action models from video, as there is no large-scale resource capturing the world from an animal's perspective with both self-motion and social interaction.

## Input / Output

The three tasks consume egocentric video at 30 fps and 480p (shortest side) with different temporal structures: VIP takes a video clip and outputs a binary interaction flag plus one of 17 object categories; LP conditions on m=16 past frames and predicts k=40 unit-normalized 3D trajectory points (4 seconds ahead); VPP takes a sequence of forward-facing camera frames from a quadruped robot and predicts a latent terrain representation z_t at offsets of ±0.8 s and ±1.5 s. All evaluation uses a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone with a single trainable linear head (linear probing), so the input is a fixed-length frame sequence (1, 4, or 8 frames at 4 Hz for video models; 16 frames at 30 Hz for LP).

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the EgoPet dataset (84 hours, 6,646 segments from 819 source videos of dogs, cats, eagles, turtles, and other animals wearing wearable cameras) together with three benchmark tasks and annotated evaluation splits. Pseudo ground-truth trajectories for LP are extracted via Deep Patch Visual Odometry (DPVO) with manual quality filtering, while VPP follows the latent-terrain formulation of Loquercio et al. [30], where a neural network trained in simulation encodes ground-truth terrain parameters into a latent vector that the model must predict from visual input. The empirical study pretrains multiple self-supervised ViT-B models (MAE, DINO, iBOT, VideoMAE, MVD, MVP) on ImageNet-1k, Kinetics-400, Ego4D, Ego Mix, or EgoPet, then evaluates via linear probing. The key finding is that pretraining on the much smaller EgoPet dataset (84 h) outperforms pretraining on Ego4D (3,670 h) and K400 for all three animal-specific tasks, indicating that domain-relevant egomotion and interaction content matters more than raw dataset scale for learning animal-like physical skills.

## Results

On the VIP task, MVD pretrained on EgoPet achieves the best interaction prediction accuracy (68.44%) and AUROC (74.31), outperforming MVD on K400 (65.63% / 70.35) and Ego4D (64.84% / 70.15). On the VPP task, MVD (EgoPet) with 8 frames achieves the lowest MSE across past (0.204), present (0.184), and future (0.253) predictions, beating MVD (Ego4D) at 0.208/0.192/0.249 and MVD (K400) at 0.217/0.196/0.252. On the LP task, EgoPet-pretrained models consistently outperform Ego4D and K400 in both ATE and RPE over 15 training epochs. Across all three tasks, the 84-hour EgoPet dataset yields better downstream performance than the 3,670-hour Ego4D and the K400 dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVD pretrained on EgoPet wins on VIP interaction accuracy (68.44%) over all six baselines, with the closest competitor being MVD on K400 (65.63%) and MVP on Ego Mix (65.47%). On VIP object prediction, iBOT (IN-1k) edges out MVD (EgoPet) on Top-1 accuracy (37.55% vs. 35.74%), likely due to the broader object diversity in ImageNet, but MVD (EgoPet) leads on Top-3 (64.62% vs. 58.12%). On VPP, MVD (EgoPet) with 8 frames achieves the lowest MSE in past (0.204) and present (0.184) prediction, narrowly beating MVD (Ego4D) (0.208/0.192) and MVD (K400) (0.217/0.196), while all video models substantially outperform single-frame image models (e.g., iBOT at 0.350/0.278). On LP, EgoPet-pretrained models achieve lower ATE and RPE than both Ego4D and K400 across all epochs, with Ego4D ranking second—possibly because it is also egocentric. The overall pattern shows that dataset relevance (animal egocentric content) matters more than dataset size, as 84 hours of EgoPet outperforms 3,670 hours of Ego4D on VPP and LP.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoPet carves out a new niche at the intersection of egocentric video datasets and animal behavior research. Prior egocentric datasets (Ego4D, Epic-Kitchens, Charades-Ego) focus on human activities, while animal datasets (Animal Kingdom, MacaquePose, DECADE) lack egocentric perspective or are extremely small (DECADE: 1.5 hours). The VPP task directly extends the latent terrain representation formulation from Loquercio et al. [30], while VIP and LP are novel formulations inspired by human-object interaction and SLAM trajectory prediction, respectively. Methodologically, the paper relies entirely on standard self-supervised pretraining (MAE, DINO, iBOT, VideoMAE, MVD) with linear probing, positioning EgoPet as a pretraining resource rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoPet provides the first large-scale (84 hours) egocentric animal video resource, expanding the prior DECADE dataset by over 56× in volume and diversity. The finding that this modest dataset outperforms Ego4D (3,670 hours) and K400 on animal-specific downstream tasks suggests that domain alignment matters more than raw scale for physical interaction skills, though the evidence is limited to linear probing on ViT-B. The VIP task remains far from solved (best accuracy ~68%), opening a clear research direction. Key limitations include the absence of non-visual sensory modalities (smell, touch) and the reliance on pseudo-ground-truth trajectories from DPVO, which may introduce systematic errors in the LP benchmark.
