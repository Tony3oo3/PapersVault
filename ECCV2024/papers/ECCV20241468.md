## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detection methods align object, image, and text sub-spaces using a discriminative, combinatorial paradigm that simultaneously minimizes object-image and object-text discrepancies. This approach ignores the underlying data distribution (causing class bias and distribution misalignment) and treats the image and text objectives as equivalent, despite their inherent disagreement. The paper asks whether a generative, distribution-aware formulation can resolve both limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241468/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and processes it through a two-stage detector (Faster RCNN C4, Mask RCNN FPN, or CenterNet v2) to produce N region proposals. For each proposal, it generates a text embedding via diffusion and computes per-class confidence as the cosine similarity between this generated embedding and pre-computed CLIP text embeddings (ViT-B/32, 224×224) for all classes in the vocabulary. The final output is a set of bounding boxes with class labels covering both base and novel classes, with the novel-class AP being the primary evaluation metric.

## Method

CLIFF replaces the standard random-noise initialization of DDPM with a Variational Latent Sampler (VLS) that learns a Gaussian posterior over the object embedding space via two FC layers on the shared region feature extractor, then samples object-centric noise through the reparameterization trick. A Continual Diffusion Module (CDM) then performs a two-stage reverse diffusion: first a short L=3-step object-to-image diffusion (conditioned on the object embedding) generates an intermediate CLIP image embedding, which is fused with the object embedding via an attentive gating mechanism to form a refined condition; then a longer T=10-step object-to-text diffusion (conditioned on this refined signal) generates the final text embedding for classification. The training objective combines standard RPN and box-regression losses with a KL divergence term (regularizing the VLS toward N(0,I)), an L1 loss on the generated image embedding, and a combined generative (noise-prediction) + discriminative (cross-entropy on cosine similarity) loss on the text embedding. This sequential object→image→text transfer avoids the combinatorial conflict of parallel alignment and introduces distribution-level perception absent in purely discriminative OVD pipelines.

## Results

On the COCO benchmark, CLIFF achieves 41.3% mAPn (novel-class AP) and 50.6% overall mAP with Faster RCNN C4, surpassing the strongest baseline BARON (41.0% mAPn) and outperforming OCD (36.6% mAPn) by 4.7 points. With a longer 8× training schedule, CLIFF reaches 43.2% mAPn. On LVIS, CLIFF attains 22.4% APrm with Mask RCNN and 24.5% APrm with CenterNet v2, setting new state-of-the-art in both detector families. Cross-dataset transfer to Pascal VOC and LVIS yields 66.1% and 16.7% AP50 respectively, exceeding GOAT by 2.5 and 2.7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIFF outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the COCO benchmark. The closest competitor is BARON (41.0% mAPn), which CLIFF surpasses by 0.3 points; the gap widens substantially against EdaDet (37.8%), GOAT (36.4%), and OCD (36.6%), with gains of 3.5, 4.9, and 4.7 mAPn respectively. On LVIS with Mask RCNN, CLIFF leads BARON-Ens (19.2% APrm) by 3.2 points and OCD (21.1%) by 1.3 points; with CenterNet v2, it beats CoDet (23.4%) and GOAT (23.3%) by 1.1 and 1.2 APrm. In cross-dataset evaluation (COCO→Pascal VOC / LVIS), CLIFF achieves 66.1% and 16.7% AP50, exceeding GOAT (63.6%, 14.0%) by 2.5 and 2.7 points, confirming superior generalization to unseen datasets. Notably, CLIFF uses a simpler sub-space alignment strategy (Obj→Img→Txt via continual diffusion) compared to the combinatorial Obj→Txt & Img approach used by most competitors, yet consistently achieves higher novel-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm in open-vocabulary object detection (ViLD, OCD, BARON, VLDet), which aligns object and text/image sub-spaces via combinatorial contrastive or distillation losses, by introducing a generative diffusion-based formulation. It builds on DDPM (Ho et al., 2020) for the denoising process and VAE (Kingma & Welling, 2013) for the variational posterior that replaces random noise with object-centric noise. The key conceptual departure is the 'continual' transfer (object → image → text) as a sequential Markov chain, rather than the parallel combinatorial objective (object → image AND object → text) used by prior CLIP-adaptation methods, motivated by the disagreement between the two alignment objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLIFF is the first to demonstrate that latent diffusion can serve as a distribution-transfer mechanism in a VL discriminative task, opening a new direction for incorporating generative modeling into detection pipelines. The practical finding that only 3 diffusion steps suffice for the object-to-image stage and 10 for object-to-text keeps inference overhead manageable, making the approach deployable. However, the method still depends on a frozen CLIP backbone and standard two-stage detectors, and the performance gains over strong baselines (e.g., +0.3 mAPn over BARON on COCO) are moderate, leaving open whether the probabilistic framing yields larger advantages at scale or in more challenging open-world settings.
