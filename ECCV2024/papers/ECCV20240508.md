## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking face generation suffers from two fundamental issues: (1) SyncNet-based lip-sync loss provides unstable and conflicting training signals that degrade both synchronization and visual quality, and (2) lip leaking from randomly selected identity references causes the model to replicate reference lip movements instead of generating audio-driven ones, leading to poor synchronization or non-converged training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240508/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three inputs: a pose reference (face with bottom half masked, 96×96), an identity reference (face image from the same video, first processed by the silent-lip generator to close the lips), and a mel-spectrogram audio snippet (16×80, 16 kHz, window 800, hop 200). Five consecutive frames are processed per step for temporal coherence. The output is a lip-synchronized talking face sequence, optionally enhanced to high resolution via VQFR post-processing.

## Method

The architecture is a U-Net-based GAN with separate CNN encoders for identity and pose references, and a frozen pretrained audio encoder (ResNetSE-34) jointly trained with a face encoder for synchronization. Three key components address the identified problems: (1) AVSyncNet, a shift-invariant synchronization evaluator using ResNet-50 (image) and ResNetSE-34 (audio) encoders, replaces the unstable SyncNet; (2) a stabilized synchronization loss (L_ss) computes the relative difference between GT-lip/audio and generated-lip/audio similarity scores rather than using absolute similarity, with an additional penalty term for identity-reference/audio similarity to counter residual lip leaking; (3) a silent-lip generator (G_S), trained as a GAN without synchronization loss, modifies the identity reference to have closed lips before it enters the main generator, eliminating the source of lip leaking. An adaptive triplet loss further prevents the model from over-copying visual details from the identity reference.

## Results

On the LRS2 test set, the proposed method (without face restoration) achieves the best SSIM (0.95), PSNR (32.64), FID (3.83), IFC (0.16), LMD (1.13), and LSE-D (6.03) among all compared methods, with only TalkLip slightly ahead on LSE-C (8.53 vs. 8.41). On the LRW test set, the method attains SOTA SSIM (0.92), PSNR (31.45), FID (4.46), IFC (0.18), LMD (1.22), LSE-C (7.86), and LSE-D (6.24), outperforming all baselines across every metric. With VQFR post-processing, LSE-C and LSE-D further improve to 8.52 and 5.83 on LRS2, and 7.92 and 6.00 on LRW, while FID degrades slightly due to the restoration step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of metrics across both LRS2 and LRW. On LRS2, it achieves the best SSIM (0.95 vs. 0.87 for IPLAP, the closest competitor), FID (3.83 vs. 4.10 for IPLAP), IFC (0.16 vs. 0.20 for IPLAP), and LMD (1.13 vs. 1.64 for IPLAP), with a marginal loss to TalkLip only on LSE-C (8.41 vs. 8.53). On LRW, the method dominates every metric, beating IPLAP (the strongest visual-quality competitor) by 0.01 SSIM, 1.0 PSNR, 3.94 FID, and 0.42 LMD, while also surpassing it on LSE-C (7.86 vs. 5.94) and LSE-D (6.24 vs. 7.76). Wav2Lip and TalkLip, which rely on the original SyncNet and lip-sync loss, show clear generalization gaps on LRW (FID 6.81 and 15.73 respectively) compared to the proposed method's 4.46, validating the paper's motivation for AVSyncNet and the stabilized synchronization loss. VideoReTalking achieves competitive lip sync but lags significantly in visual quality (FID 5.30 on LRW vs. 4.46), while DINet and EAMM underperform across nearly all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Wav2Lip/SyncNet-based training paradigm for talking face generation by diagnosing and fixing two specific failure modes: SyncNet's instability as a training signal and lip leaking from randomly selected identity references. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it retains the standard GAN/U-Net inpainting setup but introduces targeted modifications: a shift-invariant AVSyncNet (ResNet-50 + ResNetSE-34) to replace SyncNet, a stabilized synchronization loss that uses relative similarity differences instead of absolute scores, and a silent-lip generator that closes the lips on identity references before feeding them to the main generator. The separate identity and pose encoders and the adaptive triplet loss draw on SIDGAN's analysis of conflicting losses, while the overall pipeline remains within the conditional-inpainting framework established by Wav2Lip and subsequent works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic framework for practitioners: SyncNet's instability on ground-truth pairs and lip leaking from identity references are concrete, reproducible failure modes that explain why many talking face models exhibit training instability or out-of-sync outputs. The stabilized synchronization loss and silent-lip generator are simple, drop-in techniques likely to be adopted by subsequent work. However, the authors acknowledge that AVSyncNet's instability is not fully resolved, face restoration post-processing introduces temporal inconsistencies, and the silent-lip generator suppresses teeth in identity references—open issues that limit the approach's generality, particularly for high-resolution or diverse-expression scenarios.
