## Problem

Existing group activity detection (GAD) datasets are extensions of other benchmarks with mostly singleton groups rather than true multi-person groups, and lack practical multi-view, densely populated scenarios. Meanwhile, most GAD models depend on off-the-shelf spectral clustering for group localization, which is computationally expensive and not optimized for the task. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a purpose-built large-scale dataset (Café) and a Transformer-based model that localizes groups end-to-end without external clustering.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 6-second video clips (1920×1080) with ground-truth actor tracklets; T frames are sampled via segment-based sampling (T=5 for Café, T=1 for Social-CAD, T=2 for JRDB-Act), and actor features are extracted via RoIAlign (5×5 crop, 256 channels) from a ResNet-18 backbone. The model outputs K group candidates (K=12 for Café/JRDB-Act, K=10 for Social-CAD), each with an activity class prediction, plus a membership score per actor–group pair computed as a dot-product of projected embeddings; at inference each actor is assigned to the group with the highest score, and actors with no strong assignment are treated as outliers.

## Method

The core architecture is a Grouping Transformer that takes learnable group tokens, RoIAlign-extracted actor features (augmented with positional embeddings of box coordinates), and frame-level features, and produces group and actor embedding vectors through three attention types: multi-head self-attention (separately for actors and groups, with a distance mask that blocks attention between actors farther than threshold μ), multi-head grouping attention (group tokens as queries attend to actor features as keys/values to form group representations), and multi-head cross-attention (actors and groups attend to frame features for context). Group localization is performed by matching actor and group embeddings in a learned semantic space via dot-product, rather than 2D coordinate matching or spectral clustering. Training uses a DETR-style Hungarian-algorithm bipartite matching between K predicted groups and ground-truth groups (padded with empty groups), combined with a group consistency loss (a modified InfoNCE that pulls same-group actor embeddings together), a binary cross-entropy membership loss, and an individual action classification loss, all optimized end-to-end with Adam.

## Results

On the new Café dataset (split by view), the proposed model with 12 group tokens achieves Group mAP₁.₀ = 18.84, Group mAP₀.₅ = 37.53, and Outlier mIoU = 67.64, outperforming all baselines by substantial margins. On JRDB-Act, the model reaches 59.8 mAP with a lightweight ResNet-18 backbone, surpassing JRDB-base (59.2 mAP) which uses the much heavier I3D backbone. On Social-CAD, the model achieves 69.2 Social Accuracy using a single frame, matching Joint (69.0) which requires 17 frames and an I3D backbone, while inference time is 0.10 s per clip versus 0.23–0.32 s for clustering-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model outperforms all six baselines across all three datasets and both Café splits. Against the closest competitor JRDB-base [13] (spectral clustering + GNN), the model gains +5.58 Group mAP₁.₀ and +3.73 Outlier mIoU on Café (split by view), while also being ~2.5× faster (0.10 s vs 0.25 s per clip). Against HGC [43], the only other Transformer-based GAD method, the improvement is even larger (+13.66 Group mAP₁.₀), demonstrating the advantage of embedding-space matching over 2D coordinate-space point matching. On JRDB-Act, the model edges out JRDB-base (59.8 vs 59.2 mAP) despite using ResNet-18 instead of I3D, and substantially surpasses PAR [19] (47.1 mAP) and Joint [12] (41.4 mAP). On Social-CAD, the model matches Joint [12] (69.2 vs 69.0 Social Accuracy) while using a single frame and a far lighter backbone. Clustering-based methods (ARG [47], Joint [12], JRDB-base [13]) are highly sensitive to the number of clusters, with performance dropping sharply when the cluster count mismatches the true number of groups plus outliers, whereas the proposed model is relatively robust to the number of group tokens (12 tokens is optimal but 8 tokens still yields 18.10 Group mAP₁.₀).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the group activity detection (GAD) line of work initiated by Social-CAD [12], JRDB-Act [13], and HGC [43], which moved beyond the single-group, pre-identified-actor assumption of group activity recognition (GAR). It departs from prior GAD methods that rely on off-the-shelf spectral clustering for group localization by instead using learnable group tokens and embedding-space matching (inspired by DETR's Hungarian-algorithm matching), and from prior GAD datasets that are extensions of other benchmarks with mostly singleton 'groups' by introducing Café, a dataset purpose-built for multi-group, multi-view GAD with non-singleton groups. The Grouping Transformer architecture draws on Transformer attention mechanisms already used in GAR [16,31,37] but restructures them around a grouping-attention layer that explicitly forms group representations from actor features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Café dataset addresses a real gap in the GAD literature: prior benchmarks were not designed for the task, contained predominantly singleton groups, and lacked multi-view evaluation, making them poor proxies for practical deployment in surveillance or social scene understanding. The proposed model's ability to handle an unknown number of groups without clustering while being both faster (0.10 s vs. 0.22–0.32 s) and more accurate than prior methods makes it a practical baseline. Key open questions remain: the model does not exploit temporal dynamics or multi-view consistency despite the dataset supporting both, and whether the embedding-space matching strategy generalizes to even denser or more occluded scenes is untested.
