## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering quality but produces representations with massive storage overhead (59 float32 parameters per Gaussian), hindering practical deployment. Existing compression methods either minimize rate under a fixed distortion target rather than jointly optimizing rate and distortion, treat all Gaussians' view-dependent colors with the same spherical harmonic degree regardless of material complexity, or rely on multi-stage pipelines that prevent end-to-end learning from the data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241539/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS representation (15k iterations of standard 3DGS training) and the original training images as input, and produces a compressed 3D Gaussian representation whose size is continuously controllable via hyperparameters λ_GS_prune and λ_SH_prune. The final stored representation consists of arithmetic-coded codeword indexes, codebooks, entropy-model logits, and float16 positions, with a total size ranging from ~15 MB (40× compression) down to ~1.5 MB (300× compression) on the Truck scene. Novel views are rendered from the pruned and quantized Gaussians using standard tile-based differentiable rasterization.

## Method

RDO-Gaussian formulates 3DGS compression as an end-to-end joint optimization of rate and distortion (R + λD) with three coupled components. First, each Gaussian receives a learnable pruning mask (sigmoid + straight-through estimator) that zeros out its scale and opacity when below a threshold, dynamically removing redundant Gaussians. Second, each Gaussian is assigned three degree-adaptive SH masks (one per harmonic degree 1–3) that independently prune SH coefficients, enabling volumetric bit allocation where diffuse regions retain fewer SH parameters than view-dependent ones. Third, entropy-constrained vector quantization (ECVQ) discretizes scale, rotation, and color attributes by selecting codewords that minimize a combined rate-distortion loss (−log p_m / λ + L2 distance) rather than pure L2 distance, with a learned unconditional entropy model providing the rate estimate. All masks, codebooks, and logits are jointly optimized with the Gaussian parameters under a total loss combining pruning penalties, rate loss, VQ distortion, and rendering loss (L1 + SSIM). After training, Gaussians are sorted by their SH mask cluster to eliminate per-Gaussian mask storage, and the discrete indexes are compressed via arithmetic coding.

## Results

RDO-Gaussian achieves over 40× compression ratio on 3DGS with minor quality degradation, and can compress Gaussians to over 300× (approximately 1 MB) while retaining adequate scene information. At the high-rate operating point, the method maintains rendering quality comparable to uncompressed 3DGS (e.g., PSNR 25.04 vs. 25.36 on Truck, 30.16 vs. 29.95 on Playroom). Across all four datasets (Mip-NeRF360, Tanks and Temples, Deep Blending, NeRF-Synthetic), RDO-Gaussian achieves over 30% average bitrate savings compared to existing 3DGS compression methods at equivalent quality levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDO-Gaussian is compared against four baselines: 3DGS (uncompressed), CompGS, Compact-3DGS, and LightGaussian. Against uncompressed 3DGS, RDO-Gaussian reduces file size by over 40× while preserving rendering quality (PSNR drops of <0.3 dB on Truck and Playroom). Against the three compression baselines (CompGS, Compact-3DGS, LightGaussian), RDO-Gaussian achieves over 30% average bitrate savings at comparable quality, as shown in the RD curves (Fig. 3). A key differentiator is that RDO-Gaussian provides a continuous rate-distortion curve by tuning λ parameters, whereas the baselines only offer a single fixed compression ratio. On the Playroom scene, RDO-Gaussian's low-rate representation actually improves PSNR over 3DGS (30.16 vs. 29.95), attributed to pruning and quantization mitigating 3DGS overfitting. The main limitation noted is that each rate requires a separately trained representation, unlike the baselines which produce a single fixed-size output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3DGS framework (Kerbl et al. 2023) and repurposes the learned-mask pruning mechanism from Compact-3DGS (Lee et al. 2023), while adopting entropy-constrained vector quantization (Chou et al. 1989) as its core quantization strategy. It departs from prior 3DGS compression work (CompGS, LightGaussian, Compact-3DGS) by reframing the problem from minimizing rate under a fixed distortion budget to a joint rate-distortion optimization (R + λD), enabling flexible and continuous rate control. The adaptive SH pruning, which assigns different spherical-harmonics degrees per Gaussian, is a novel volumetric bit-allocation strategy not present in any prior 3DGS compression method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: compressing a 3DGS scene to ~1 MB (smaller than a single 3000×2000 JPEG) while retaining adequate rendering quality opens the door to deployment in bandwidth-constrained or storage-limited settings. The finding that 76% of per-Gaussian parameters are SHs contributing only marginally to quality challenges the default assumption of uniform SH allocation and motivates future work on learned, content-adaptive appearance representations. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that each bitrate requires a separately trained representation with no shared information, leaving open the important question of designing a single variable-rate representation that supports multiple bitrates simultaneously.
