## Problem

In Federated Learning, clients often deploy different CNN architectures (e.g., ResNet, EfficientNet, MobileNetV3) due to heterogeneous computational constraints, making standard weight-aggregation methods like FedAvg inapplicable. Existing approaches to handle architectural heterogeneity—distillation, feature fusion, or mapping into a shared latent space—require additional training of network components or structural modifications, reducing efficiency and scalability. FedHARM is, to the authors' knowledge, the first framework to directly harmonize diverse CNN architectures in FL without altering client model structures or aggregating weights.

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 for MNIST) and produces class labels. Unconventionally, each client's data is partitioned into a private set (fully supervised local training) and a shared set of 5,000 images common to all clients, on which a hybrid supervised + self-supervised representation-learning procedure operates. The intermediate output of the method is a 1920-dimensional per-image descriptor (128+256+512+1024 features extracted from four aligned blocks), which is uploaded to the server for cross-client averaging rather than raw model weights.

## Method

FedHARM replaces weight aggregation with a representation-centric, model-agnostic mechanism built around a Gradient-based Feature Extraction Module: for each CNN block, it computes ∂p_j^c / ∂F_j^b (gradient of the class prediction w.r.t. the block's feature map) and retains the top-k features with the largest absolute gradient values (k = 128, 256, 512, 1024 per block). To align blocks across architectures with differing depths (4 blocks in ResNet vs. 7 in EfficientNet/MobileNetV3), the method performs block-to-block mapping via representational similarity and angular divergence analysis. On the central server, per-image descriptors from all clients are averaged to form a global representation, and each client then refines its local model using a dual loss—cross-entropy on the private set plus cosine similarity between its local descriptor and the global one on the shared set—over iterative communication rounds (10 rounds × 25 epochs, Adam with One-Cycle LR). This avoids any weight averaging and instead aligns learned feature spaces, enabling heterogeneous architectures to contribute complementary representational knowledge to a unified global embedding.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IID, 5 clients), FedHARM_rem achieves 83.87% mean accuracy, outperforming all baselines; with 10 and 20 clients it reaches 82.03% and 79.76% respectively. On CIFAR-100 (IID, 5 clients), FedHARM_rem attains 53.9% accuracy versus 49.79% for the strongest baseline (FedSimSiam). On MNIST (IID, 10 clients), FedHARM_rem reaches 98.65% accuracy, surpassing FedCon's 98.08%. In the non-IID setting (α=0.1), FedHARM_rem maintains 82.91% on CIFAR-10 (5 clients) but drops to 38.98% on CIFAR-100 (10 clients), where it underperforms PerFCL (61.2%) and MOON (60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedHARM_rem decisively outperforms FedSimSiam [2] on CIFAR-10 (83.87% vs 76.27% at 5 clients) and CIFAR-100 (53.9% vs 49.79%), and similarly dominates FedCA [45] (83.87% vs 71.25% on CIFAR-10; 53.9% vs 43.30% on CIFAR-100). Against FedCon [24], FedHARM_rem edges ahead on CIFAR-10 (82.03% vs 81.47% at 10 clients IID; 81.97% vs 81.96% non-IID) and on MNIST IID (98.65% vs 98.08%), though FedCon slightly wins on MNIST non-IID (98.22% vs 97.98% at 10 clients). In the non-IID CIFAR-100 setting, however, FedHARM_rem underperforms both PerFCL [47] (38.98% vs 61.2% at 10 clients) and MOON [21] (38.98% vs 60%), indicating that the representation-centric aggregation struggles when per-client data is scarce across 100 classes. On CIFAR-10 non-IID, FedHARM_rem still leads all baselines (82.91% at 5 clients vs. FedCA's 68.01% and FedSimCLR's 64.06%). Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced on CIFAR-10 and MNIST, while CIFAR-100 non-IID with many clients remains a weakness relative to contrastive baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant federated averaging paradigm (FedAvg) and its contrastive-learning extensions (FedCon, MOON, FedSimCLR, FedCA, FedSimSiam) by replacing weight aggregation with a representation-centric mechanism that operates on gradient-based feature descriptors rather than model parameters. It repurposes gradient-based feature importance (inspired by Grad-CAM and personalized FL gradient analysis) as a cross-architecture alignment tool, a use not previously explored in the FL literature. The authors explicitly position this as the first work to systematically address heterogeneous CNN architectures (ResNet, EfficientNet, MobileNetV3) within a single FL system, carving new ground at the intersection of interpretability-driven feature selection and federated representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Handling architectural heterogeneity is a practical necessity in real-world FL deployments where edge devices impose different model choices, yet prior methods implicitly assumed uniform architectures. The shared-subset requirement (5000 images accessible to all clients) is a significant practical limitation that the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve, as privacy constraints in real deployments make such sampling difficult. Notably, the non-IID CIFAR-100 results (37–44%) substantially underperform baselines like PerFCL (58–61%), suggesting the method's representational alignment degrades under high class diversity and data scarcity per client. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-CNN architectures (transformers, vision transformers), whether the shared subset can be replaced by synthetic or federated pseudo-data, and whether the block-alignment strategy generalizes beyond the three families tested.
