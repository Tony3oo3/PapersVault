## Problem

Visual prompt learning requires access to real training data, making it inapplicable in privacy-sensitive or data-inaccessible scenarios, while Data-Free Knowledge Distillation (DFKD) suffers from catastrophic forgetting of the student model, high storage overhead (a separate student copy per teacher), and an inability to process multiple knowledge transfer requests in parallel. The paper asks whether a single visual prompt can encapsulate knowledge from multiple frozen source models without any real data, thereby combining the storage efficiency and model reusability of prompts with the data-free capability of DFKD.

## Input / Output

The method takes two (or more) frozen source models with different backbones (e.g., ResNet-18, ResNet-50, VGG-13) trained on distinct datasets, and produces a single learned padding-type visual prompt (default 128×128) that, when prepended to an input image and fed through any of the source models, reproduces that model's classification behavior. The prompt is internally partitioned into a shared Prompt Core (PC, default 36×36) and a model-specific Prompt Periphery (PP, default 128×128), and the entire trainable parameter count is roughly 50K, compared to over 11M for a full DFKD student model. No real training images are consumed at any stage; all training data is synthesized on-the-fly.

## Method

KiOP operates in two phases. In the Synthesize Period, a contrastive model inversion system (adapted from CMI) generates diverse synthetic samples for each model pair by optimizing a generator against an inversion loss (batch-norm, class-prior, and adversarial terms) plus a contrastive diversity loss, storing the best samples in per-model data banks across T iterations. In the Knowledge Storing Period, the VP is trained on the accumulated synthetic data: the PC is optimized so that SMA (Model A + PC) matches Model A's outputs via KL-divergence, while the PP is jointly optimized so that SMB (Model A + PC + PP, with a random label mapping on its output) matches Model B's outputs. All source models remain frozen throughout, and the two model pairs are trained simultaneously on their respective data banks, enabling parallel multi-model knowledge transfer. The partitioning into PC and PP allocates parameter capacity according to transfer difficulty—PC handles the simpler same-domain transfer for Model A, while PP absorbs the harder cross-domain transfer for Model B—and Grad-CAM visualizations confirm that the two regions attend to largely non-overlapping spatial regions, preventing interference.

## Results

On the (ResNet-18, ResNet-18) backbone with Dts.B=mnist and Dts.A=cifar100, KiOP-B achieves 44.49% Acc.B and 37.21% Acc.A, while KiOP-BF (fully data-free) reaches 43.67% and 36.17% respectively. For Dts.A=gtsrb, KiOP-B lifts Acc.A to 73.05% while retaining 46.48% Acc.B; for Dts.A=svhn, Acc.A reaches 86.65% with 42.77% Acc.B. In stark contrast, Vanilla-KD (DFKD) achieves the highest Acc.B (57.19% on mnist/cifar100) but catastrophically forgets Model A, dropping Acc.A to 0.15%. KiOP reduces trainable parameters from >11M (Vanilla-KD) to ~50K, cutting storage from >43 MB to ~400 KB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Vanilla-KD, which implements the DFKD framework from Fang et al. [16] (Contrastive Model Inversion) with the student model's parameters fully updated. Vanilla-KD consistently achieves the highest Acc.B across all dataset pairs (e.g., 57.19% vs. 44.49% on mnist/cifar100; 79.55% vs. 42.82% on cifar10/cifar100), but at the cost of catastrophic forgetting: Acc.A collapses to near-zero (0.15%–1.37%) in most settings, meaning the student model loses its original knowledge entirely. KiOP-B and KiOP-BF sacrifice 10–35 points of Acc.B but recover Acc.A to 37–87%, enabling simultaneous knowledge retention for both models. KiOP-BF (fully data-free for both models) matches KiOP-B within 1–3% on most configurations, confirming that the synthetic data pipeline is effective. The paper also includes internal ablations (KiOP-T for single-model transfer, and PC-size sweeps in Table 2) but no other external DFKD or prompt-learning baselines are directly compared.

## Relation to Prior Work

KiOP sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Visual Prompt Learning (Bahng et al., 2022), which injects learnable parameters into the input space to adapt frozen pre-trained models, and Data-Free Knowledge Distillation (specifically Contrastive Model Inversion, Fang et al., 2021), which synthesizes training data from a teacher model without access to real data. The paper's core conceptual move is to repurpose the VP mechanism as a *storage medium* for distilled knowledge rather than a task-adaptation tool, and to borrow the DFKD synthesis pipeline to eliminate VP's dependence on real training data. This departs from prior VP work (which always requires the downstream dataset) and from prior DFKD (which updates all student parameters, causing catastrophic forgetting and per-teacher storage overhead) by freezing both source models and training only a small prompt partition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm addresses a practical deployment gap: service providers who must offer knowledge-transfer-as-a-service without exposing training data or duplicating full model copies. The 50K-parameter prompt versus 11M+ parameter student model represents a ~200× storage reduction, and the frozen-model design enables true parallel multi-model service. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (e.g., ~40% on CIFAR-100, ~70–80% on GTSRB/SVHN), all experiments use small-scale datasets (≤100 classes, ≤32×32 or 28×28 images), and the PC/PP partitioning heuristic is dataset-dependent with no principled selection rule. Open questions include scalability to ImageNet-scale models, robustness to more than three concurrent models, and whether the approach transfers to non-CNN backbones (e.g., ViT) where input-space prompting behaves differently.
