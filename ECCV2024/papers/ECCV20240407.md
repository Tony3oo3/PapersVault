## Problem

Learning 3D-aware GANs from real images requires accurate camera poses, which are expensive to obtain. Existing methods that jointly learn pose and 3D content (e.g., 3DGP, PoF3D) entangle camera and object poses in 2D image space, causing them to collapse to a narrow pose distribution and produce incomplete geometry on datasets with complex, multi-peak pose distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unposed RGB images (256×256 for CompCars, SDIP Elephant, LSUN Plane; 128×128 for ShapeNet Cars) and offline-extracted DINO semantic features (64×64, reduced to 3 channels via PCA) with foreground masks applied. It produces a 3D radiance field and a 3D semantic feature field sharing the same density, per-image camera pose estimates parameterized by 4 DoF (azimuth, elevation, in-plane rotation, scale), and rendered images from arbitrary viewpoints. A separate StyleGAN2 background generator handles the background, composited via alpha blending.

## Method

The generator (built on EG3D) jointly produces a radiance field and a semantic feature field using two tri-planes that share density, enabling volume rendering of both color and features. A 3D template feature field is extracted by feeding the mean noise vector to the EMA generator, serving as a canonical object-space reference. Camera pose estimation is formulated as a 2D-3D matching problem: azimuth and elevation are discretized (36×3 or 36×18 bins), the template is rendered from each bin, and phase correlation in the frequency domain efficiently estimates the remaining scale and in-plane rotation. A softmax-weighted PDF over all discretized poses (with temperature annealing) is sampled via inverse sampling to assign poses during training. A dual-discriminator setup (image + feature) with R1 regularization trains the GAN, while the template is updated every 16 iterations early in training and then once per epoch.

## Results

On CompCars, TeFF achieves the best Depth_360 of 0.31 (vs. 0.95 for EG3D, 4.02 for 3DGP, 10.31 for PoF3D) and FID_360 of 27.71, indicating the most complete 360° geometry. On the multi-peak ShapeNet Cars dataset, TeFF attains the best FID_gt of 5.95 and Depth_gt of 0.53. The pose distribution accuracy is dramatically improved: the KL divergence to ground truth is 0.0555 (azimuth) and 0.0696 (elevation), compared to 4.48/0.55 for PoF3D and 40.46/39.36 for 3DGP. However, FID_est is higher than baselines (e.g., 20.60 vs. 6.62 for PoF3D on CompCars), reflecting that baselines collapse to narrow pose distributions where rendering is easier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EG3D (trained without pose conditioning), TeFF wins on geometry (Depth_360: 0.31 vs. 0.95 on CompCars, 0.60 vs. 1.10 on Elephant, 0.78 vs. 1.19 on Plane) and on FID_gt (5.95 vs. 7.25 on ShapeNet Cars), while EG3D's FID_est is lower only because it renders from a fixed narrow elevation range. Against 3DGP, TeFF achieves dramatically better Depth_360 (0.31 vs. 4.02 on CompCars; 0.60 vs. 3.29 on Elephant) and FID_360 (27.71 vs. 187.20), and a far more accurate pose distribution (KL 0.0555 vs. 40.46); 3DGP's lower Depth_est (0.37 vs. 0.29) is partly due to its use of depth-map supervision during training. Against PoF3D, TeFF wins on Depth_360 (0.31 vs. 10.31 on CompCars; 0.60 vs. 3.14 on Elephant) and FID_360 (27.71 vs. 44.52), as well as pose distribution accuracy (KL 0.0555 vs. 4.48); however, PoF3D achieves lower FID_est (6.62 vs. 20.60 on CompCars; 3.04 vs. 5.44 on Elephant) because its collapsed pose distribution makes rendering from the learned angles easier, at the cost of incomplete 3D geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on EG3D's triplane-based generative radiance field architecture and VoxGRAF's 2D background GAN, but departs fundamentally from prior unposed-image synthesis methods (CAMPARI, PoF3D, 3DGP) that jointly learn camera pose distributions and 3D content in an adversarial loop. The key conceptual shift is decoupling pose estimation from generation: instead of learning a pose distribution that can collapse on multi-peak data, the paper learns a 3D template feature field from DINO self-supervised features and performs explicit 2D-3D pose matching via grid search over discretized azimuth/elevation combined with phase correlation for scale and in-plane rotation. This repurposes the semantic alignment properties of DINO features—previously used for correspondence and segmentation—as a geometric anchor for camera pose recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling 3D-aware GAN training from unposed in-the-wild images with complex (multi-peak) pose distributions, this work removes a major practical bottleneck for 3D content generation from real data. The dramatic improvement in pose distribution accuracy (KL divergence ~0.06 vs. ~40 for 3DGP) directly translates to complete 360° geometry recovery where prior methods produce half-objects or collapsed views. Limitations include single-category applicability (one template per category), inability to handle significant perspective distortion, and no articulation modeling. Open questions include whether multiple templates can support multi-category generation and whether geometry can be disentangled from the feature-matching process to improve robustness.
