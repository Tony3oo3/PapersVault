## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding methods rely on frame-wise final-layer CLIP features supplemented by additional temporal backbones (e.g., SlowFast) and complex post-processing modules, which is both parameter-inefficient and granularity-inflexible. CLIP's multi-layer features encode distinct spatial-temporal information at different granularity levels, but this potential has not been exploited for fine-grained video understanding. The paper asks how to efficiently transfer an image-language foundation model to untrimmed video temporal grounding without extra backbones or large-scale pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241100/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an untrimmed video (sampled frames tokenized into patches by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 visual encoder) and a natural language query (tokenized into word tokens by the frozen CLIP text encoder). It produces frame-level predictions for three tasks simultaneously: start-end boundary displacements with confidence scores for moment retrieval, continuous saliency scores in [0,1] for highlight detection, and binary inclusion scores for video summarization. At inference, moment retrieval predictions are formed by combining the foreground confidence with boundary displacements from each frame, followed by NMS (IoU threshold 0.7) to suppress duplicates.

## Method

R²-Tuning learns a lightweight R² Block (only 1.5% of total parameters) that is recurrently attached to the last K=4 layers of a fully frozen CLIP encoder, processing them in reverse order (from the last layer toward earlier layers) to perform coarse-to-fine spatial-temporal modeling. Each recurrent step comprises two sub-operations: (1) query-modulated spatial pooling, which uses normalized Embedded Gaussian attention to adaptively pool patch-level visual features into a single token conditioned on the query, enabling granularity-flexible spatial focus; and (2) recurrent temporal refinement, which fuses the pooled features with the hidden state via a learnable gate and updates it through cross-attention (query as key/value), self-attention, and a feed-forward network. Two InfoNCE-based contrastive losses (video-level and layer-wise) calibrate the granularity alignment between CLIP's visual and text encoders across layers. The refined frame-level features are expanded into a temporal feature pyramid via strided 1D convolutions, then decoded by three heads (focal-loss classification, L1 boundary regression, and contrastive saliency prediction) that are jointly optimized while gradients never flow through the frozen CLIP encoders.

## Results

On QVHighlights test split, R²-Tuning achieves 69.04 mAP@0.5, 47.56 mAP@0.75, and 46.17 average mAP for moment retrieval, and 40.75 mAP / 64.20 HIT@1 for highlight detection, using only 2.7M learnable parameters with a frozen CLIP-B/32 backbone and no additional temporal backbone or post pre-training. On Ego4D-NLQ, Charades-STA, and TACoS, it achieves R@0.7 of 2.12, 37.02, and 25.12 respectively, outperforming UniVTG (which uses CLIP+SlowFast and 4.2M post pre-training corpus) on all three. For highlight detection on YouTube Highlights and video summarization on TVSum, R²-Tuning reaches 76.1 and 85.2 average mAP, setting new state-of-the-art without any video or audio features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²-Tuning outperforms all CLIP+SlowFast-based baselines on QVHighlights by a substantial margin: it gains +4.53 mAP@0.5 over CG-DETR (69.04 vs 64.51) and +5.11 over TR-DETR (69.04 vs 63.98), while using only 2.7M parameters versus 7.9–12.0M for those methods. Against UniVTG, which additionally leverages a 4.2M-sample post pre-training corpus and 41.3M parameters, R²-Tuning still leads by +4.98 mAP@0.5 and +2.54 average mAP for MR, and +0.21 mAP for HD. On Ego4D-NLQ, R²-Tuning achieves R@0.7 of 2.12 versus UniVTG's 1.32 (a 60% relative gain), and on TACoS it reaches R@0.7 of 25.12 versus 17.35 for UniVTG. For YouTube Highlights HD, R²-Tuning (76.1 avg mAP) surpasses UniVTG (75.2) and UMT (74.9) without any audio features. On TVSum VS, it achieves 85.2 Top-5 mAP, beating UMT (83.1) and UniVTG (81.0) by 2.1 and 4.2 points respectively. The only metric where R²-Tuning trails is HD HIT@1 on QVHighlights (64.20 vs 66.28 for UniVTG and 66.21 for CG-DETR), a narrow gap likely attributable to the absence of a dedicated temporal backbone for fine-grained saliency peak localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VTG paradigm of combining frozen CLIP final-layer features with an additional temporal backbone (e.g., SlowFast) and post-processing modules, arguing that this two-backbone design is both inefficient and granularity-inflexible. It builds on CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021) as a fully frozen backbone and borrows the three-head prediction design from UniVTG (Lin et al., CVPR 2023), but introduces a novel reversed recurrent side-tuning mechanism (R² Block) that exploits multi-layer CLIP features from coarse to fine. The approach also connects to the broader parameter-efficient transfer learning literature (prompt tuning, adapters, side-tuning like LST), positioning itself as a memory-efficient variant where gradients never flow through the main model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that VTG can be performed with only 2.7M learnable parameters and no additional video backbone, reducing inference cost and simplifying deployment compared to CLIP+SlowFast pipelines. The finding that CLIP's multi-layer features already encode sufficient spatial-temporal structure—without any video-specific pre-training—challenges the field's assumption that a dedicated temporal backbone is necessary for fine-grained video understanding. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/32 (no scaling study to larger CLIP variants), the modest gap between reversed and forward refinement directions, and the fact that the granularity-flexibility claim rests on a single query-length ablation. Open questions include whether the reversed recurrent strategy generalizes to other frozen encoders or to longer/untrimmed videos beyond the tested benchmarks.
