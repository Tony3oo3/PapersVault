## Problem

Cross-view geo-localization models achieve near-perfect accuracy on clean benchmarks like CVUSA and CVACT, but no prior work has systematically evaluated their robustness against real-world image corruptions such as adverse weather, sensor noise, and digital artifacts. This gap is critical for safety-critical deployments (autonomous driving, robot navigation) where GPS is unavailable and ground images are frequently degraded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242317/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a ground-level RGB query image (which may be corrupted by one of 16 corruption types at 5 severity levels) and matches it against a pre-cached database of geo-tagged aerial images. Only ground images are corrupted, reflecting the practical scenario where aerial references are stable and high-definition while ground captures are real-time and vulnerable. Evaluation uses R@K (K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 1%}) on both fine-grained subsets (one per corruption type and severity) and comprehensive subsets (all corruptions pooled), plus a Relative Corruption Error (RCE) metric quantifying percentage degradation from clean performance.

## Method

The paper synthesizes 16 corruption types (Weather: snow, frost, fog, brightness, spatter; Blur: defocus, glass, motion, zoom; Noise: Gaussian, shot, impulse, speckle; Digital: contrast, pixelation, JPEG) at 5 severity levels on CVUSA and CVACT ground images, yielding ~1.5 million corrupted images organized into two fine-grained benchmarks (CVUSA-C, CVACT_val-C, each with 80 independent evaluation subsets) and three comprehensive benchmarks (CVUSA-C-ALL, CVACT_val-C-ALL, CVACT_test-C-ALL, where all corruptions are pooled into a single test set). Eight state-of-the-art models (CVM-Net, OriCNN, SAFA, CVFT, DSM, L2LTR, TransGeo, GeoDTR) are benchmarked, revealing that clean accuracy does not guarantee robustness, that snow/spatter/zoom blur cause the most damage (>28% RCE), and that models trained on urban scenes (CVACT) are inherently more robust than those trained on suburban scenes (CVUSA). For robustness enhancement, the paper applies stylization (style transfer) and CLAHE to training data under three strategies—clean-only, augmented-only, and equal-mix—and shows that the equal-mix strategy preserves clean performance while significantly improving corruption robustness without any architectural changes.

## Results

On the CVUSA-C fine-grained benchmark, L2LTR [42] achieves the highest corruption-robust R@1_cor of 82.88 (vs. 94.05 clean), while CVM-Net [17] drops from 22.47 clean to just 4.35 R@1_cor. The average performance degradation across all 8 models is 24.24% on CVUSA-C and 20.14% on CVACT_val-C, with snow, spatter, and zoom blur causing the largest drops (RCE > 28% for all models). On the comprehensive CVUSA-C-ALL benchmark, L2LTR attains R@1_all of 87.93, R@5_all of 95.45, and R@10_all of 97.01, substantially outperforming all other methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2LTR [42] is the most robust model across all benchmarks, achieving R@1_cor of 82.88 on CVUSA-C and 79.15 on CVACT_val-C, outperforming GeoDTR [43] (75.48 / 70.66) and TransGeo [45] (70.12 / 67.58) despite having comparable or slightly lower clean accuracy. Notably, TransGeo and GeoDTR have higher clean R@1 (94.08 and 95.43 on CVUSA) than L2LTR (94.05), yet L2LTR's deeper architecture makes it more resistant to corruption, demonstrating that clean performance does not guarantee robustness. CVM-Net [17] and OriCNN [21] are the least robust, with R@1_cor of only 4.35 and 16.88 on CVUSA-C respectively. Among corruption types, snow, spatter, and zoom blur degrade all models most severely (RCE > 28%), while glass blur, JPEG, and pixelation have minimal impact. Models evaluated on CVACT_val-C (urban scenes) show ~4% less average degradation than on CVUSA-C (suburban scenes), suggesting richer training scenes improve robustness. Stylization-based data augmentation with the proposed mixed training strategy further improves R@1_cor for SAFA, L2LTR, and TransGeo, particularly at high corruption severities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the ImageNet-C corruption taxonomy (16 types × 5 severity levels) and the Relative Corruption Error metric from classification and detection robustness benchmarks (PASCAL-C, COCO-C, KITTI-C) and applies them for the first time to the cross-view geo-localization task. It benchmarks eight established cross-view matching methods (CVM-Net through GeoDTR) that were previously evaluated only on clean CVUSA and CVACT test sets. For robustness enhancement, it draws on stylization (Geirhos et al.) and CLAHE as data augmentation strategies, extending their use from classification/detection to cross-view retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmarks (~1.5M corrupted images) fill a clear gap: prior cross-view geo-localization work reported near-perfect clean accuracy while ignoring the domain shift that occurs under real-world weather and sensor degradation, a critical concern for safety-critical deployments in autonomous driving and robot navigation. The finding that clean accuracy does not reliably predict robustness (L2LTR outperforms higher-accuracy TransGeo and GeoDTR under corruption) challenges the implicit assumption that better clean models are automatically more deployable. The problem of robustness enhancement remains open — stylization and CLAHE provide modest gains but no method achieves clean-level performance under severe corruption, and the paper explicitly calls for further research.
