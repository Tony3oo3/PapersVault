## Problem

Existing RGB-T semantic segmentation methods suffer from two key limitations: they lack open-vocabulary recognition ability, restricting them to a fixed set of training categories, and they rely on complex fusion network architectures that introduce substantial trainable parameters and training overhead. No prior method has combined open-vocabulary segmentation with efficient RGB-thermal fusion in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241983/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a spatially aligned RGB-thermal image pair (resolutions range from 480×640 to 1024×1224 across datasets) and a free-form text prompt describing target categories. It outputs a pixel-level segmentation map at one-quarter spatial resolution (H/4 × W/4 × N classes), where N is determined by the number of category names in the text prompt. During inference, any natural-language sentence can substitute for the training-time prompts, enabling truly open-vocabulary prediction without retraining.

## Method

OpenRSS freezes SAM's entire ViT image encoder and injects thermal information through lightweight TIP modules placed before each Transformer layer. Each TIP module projects both RGB and thermal token sequences to a reduced dimension (α=8) via 1×1 convolutions, applies a correlative self-attention block to enhance RGB embeddings, fuses the result with thermal embeddings via element-wise addition, and projects back to the original dimension; the resulting prompt tokens are added residually to the RGB stream. A Dynamic LoRA (DLoRA) strategy further adapts the frozen encoder by inserting four parallel low-rank bypasses (ranks 1, 2, 4, 8 with weights 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.3) into the Q and V projections of every Transformer layer, eliminating the need for manual rank search. The mask decoder is modified to align its output dimension with CLIP's text embedding space, and a dense points-grid prompt replaces SAM's sparse point prompts to ensure full-image coverage. Pixel-level classification is achieved by computing the inner product between the decoder's feature map and CLIP text embeddings, yielding the final segmentation map with only ~1% trainable parameters.

## Results

OpenRSS (SAM-H backbone) achieves 22.81% mIoU on MF, 28.58% mIoU on MCubeS, and 27.55% mIoU on Freiburg Thermal, outperforming the best RGB-only open-vocabulary baselines by +12.1%, +18.4%, and +21.4% mIoU respectively. Even the smallest variant (SAM-B) reaches 18.96% mIoU on MF and 20.27% on MCubeS, surpassing all baselines. The method uses only ~1% trainable parameters (TIP modules + DLoRA bypasses) while freezing the entire SAM image encoder and CLIP text encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenRSS is compared against three RGB-only open-vocabulary segmentation methods (ZegFormer, OVSeg, SAN) that are forced to use only the RGB channel of each RGB-T pair. On MF, OpenRSS (SAM-H) achieves 22.81% mIoU versus the best baseline ZegFormer-R101 at 10.71% (+12.1%). On MCubeS, OpenRSS reaches 28.58% mIoU versus SAN-ViT-B at 10.10% (+18.4%). On Freiburg Thermal, the gap is largest: OpenRSS scores 27.55% mIoU versus SAN-ViT-L at 6.10% (+21.4%). Notably, even OpenRSS with the smallest SAM-B backbone (18.96% mIoU on MF) exceeds the best baseline by roughly 8 mIoU points, confirming that the thermal prompt mechanism provides substantial gains independent of model scale. The baselines struggle particularly on nighttime-heavy datasets (FT), where thermal information is most complementary, while OpenRSS maintains strong performance across both day and night conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: RGB-T semantic segmentation (which typically relies on complex cross-modal fusion architectures like RTFNet, MFNet, or CMX) and open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (which leverages vision-language models like CLIP via two-stage pipelines or side networks). OpenRSS departs from both: it replaces heavy fusion branches with lightweight Thermal Information Prompt (TIP) modules inserted into a frozen SAM encoder, and it replaces two-stage mask-then-classify pipelines with an end-to-end pixel-level alignment between SAM's mask decoder and CLIP's text embeddings. The DLoRA strategy further distinguishes it from standard LoRA by using multiple parallel bypasses with heterogeneous ranks, eliminating the need for manual rank search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first open-vocabulary RGB-T semantic segmentation framework, OpenRSS establishes a baseline and a practical recipe (prompt learning + DLoRA on frozen SAM + CLIP alignment) that the community can build upon for multi-modal open-vocabulary tasks. However, absolute mIoU scores remain modest (22–28%), the model is trained on a single dataset (KP) with limited labeled images (~950), and the primary comparisons are against RGB-only open-vocabulary methods rather than dedicated RGB-T segmentation baselines, which limits the strength of the performance claims. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., RGB-NIR, RGB-LiDAR), whether scaling training data substantially improves absolute accuracy, and whether the correlative self-attention in TIP is truly necessary or whether simpler fusion suffices at scale.
