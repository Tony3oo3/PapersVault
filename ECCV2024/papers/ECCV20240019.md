## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) overfits to training views when only sparse views are available, producing unrealistic novel view synthesis due to ambiguities in 3D-to-2D projection that prevent optimization from correcting misplaced Gaussians. Existing remedies rely on external depth supervision from pre-trained estimators, which introduces additional noise. This paper asks whether the inherent randomness in 3DGS densification can be exploited unsupervisedly to identify and suppress inaccurate reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240019/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse RGB training views (3–24 views depending on dataset), known camera poses, and a stereo-fusion point cloud for initialization (following FSGS). It simultaneously trains two 3D Gaussian radiance fields and retains one for inference, producing a compact set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians (reducing Gaussian count by ~33% over vanilla 3DGS) that renders novel-view images and depth maps in real time (~349 FPS on RTX 3090 Ti). The key structural difference from standard 3DGS is that the second field serves purely as a co-regularization partner during training and is discarded at inference.

## Method

CoR-GS trains two 3D Gaussian radiance fields on the same sparse views and suppresses their disagreement through two complementary mechanisms. First, co-pruning treats the two fields as point clouds, computes KNN correspondences between them, and prunes any Gaussian whose nearest counterpart in the other field exceeds a distance threshold τ=5, removing outliers that optimization cannot correct under sparse supervision (applied every 5 optimization/density-control cycles). Second, pseudo-view co-regularization samples an online pseudo view by interpolating between two nearest training cameras, renders both fields at that view, and adds a color co-regularization loss (weighted L1 + D-SSIM between the two rendered images) to the standard ground-truth supervised loss. The paper's empirical foundation shows that both point disagreement (measured via Fitness/RMSE on Gaussian positions) and rendering disagreement (PSNR on images, absErrorRel on depth) are negatively correlated with reconstruction quality, enabling unsupervised identification of inaccurate regions without ground-truth access.

## Results

On LLFF with 3 training views, CoR-GS achieves PSNR 20.45, SSIM 0.712, LPIPS 0.196, and AVGE 0.101, outperforming all baselines across all four metrics. With 9 views, it reaches PSNR 26.06 and LPIPS 0.089, maintaining the lead over 3DGS (25.44) and FSGS (25.31). On Mip-NeRF360 (12-view), CoR-GS improves PSNR by ~1 dB over vanilla 3DGS (19.52 vs. 18.52). On DTU (9-view), it achieves the best SSIM (0.947), LPIPS (0.045), and AVGE (0.029), while on Blender (8-view) it attains the best SSIM (0.896) and LPIPS (0.084).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoR-GS outperforms all seven baselines on LLFF across every metric and view count (3/6/9), with the largest gains over NeRF-based methods (e.g., +4.3 dB PSNR over Mip-NeRF at 3 views) and consistent 0.3–0.7 dB PSNR improvements over the closest 3DGS-based competitors (3DGS, FSGS). On Mip-NeRF360, CoR-GS leads 3DGS by ~1 dB PSNR at 12 views and ~0.6 dB at 24 views, while FSGS trails by a smaller margin. On DTU, CoR-GS wins on SSIM, LPIPS, and AVGE at all view counts, but FreeNeRF edges it out on PSNR at 3 views (19.92 vs. 19.21) due to NeRF's interpolation advantage in large invisible regions. On Blender (8 views), FSGS slightly leads on PSNR (24.64 vs. 24.43), but CoR-GS achieves the best SSIM (0.896) and LPIPS (0.084), indicating superior perceptual quality. Overall, CoR-GS is the only method to achieve the best score on all four metrics simultaneously on LLFF and DTU (6/9-view), demonstrating consistent superiority in both geometric and photometric fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoR-GS builds directly on 3DGS's optimization/density-control pipeline and adopts FSGS's stereo-fusion initialization and pseudo-view sampling strategy. It departs from the dominant sparse-view 3DGS paradigm (FSGS, SparseGS, DNGaussian) that relies on external depth-estimator supervision, instead drawing on the co-training/co-regularization literature (Blum & Mitchell, Co-teaching) to exploit the disagreement between two independently trained 3D Gaussian fields as an unsupervised quality signal. The key conceptual shift is treating the randomness inherent in 3DGS densification not merely as a nuisance but as a diagnostic: Gaussians that diverge between two fields are flagged as geometrically unreliable and pruned or regularized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for external depth priors, CoR-GS avoids the noise injection problem that plagues depth-supervised sparse-view methods, offering a self-contained regularization that is easy to plug into any 3DGS pipeline. The empirical demonstration that densification randomness is a primary source of geometric error in sparse-view 3DGS provides a useful diagnostic for the community. Limitations include the 2× training cost of maintaining two fields, a fixed co-pruning threshold (τ=5), and the method's dependence on 3DGS-specific densification mechanics, which limits immediate transfer to other radiance field representations. Open questions include whether the co-regularization principle generalizes to dynamic scenes, 4D Gaussian splatting, or other unstructured representations, and whether the two-field overhead can be reduced through shared parameters or asynchronous training.
