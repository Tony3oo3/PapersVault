## Problem

Image-based pose estimation for flexible (continuum) robots is difficult because their textureless, continuously deformable bodies lack distinguishable joints, exhibit large shape variations, and suffer from self-occlusion and camera-proximity dominance in endoscopic views. Existing keypoint- and skeleton-based methods fail on such robots, and direct regression approaches lack an effective mechanism to capture part-level shape information from a single monocular image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240578/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB endoscopic image, a 3D shape prior point cloud constructed from the robot's piecewise constant curvature configuration (part lengths, control point counts, radius), and the robot's binary mask. It outputs four pose parameters—two internal bending angles (α, β) and two global orientation angles (γ, δ)—each represented as a matrix Fisher distribution, from which both the pose value and a per-parameter uncertainty (via singular values of the distribution matrix) are recovered.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. In the pose estimation stage, the 3D shape prior is parsed into part-level geometry tokens via Farthest Point Sampling, KNN patch extraction, and PointNet encoding; these part features then serve as keys and values in a multi-head cross-attention module that queries the image encoder's features, producing a part-aware descriptor fused with the original image features for regression. In the pose refinement stage, the initial pose prediction is used to dynamically deform the static shape prior through skeleton curve parameterization (piecewise constant curvature) and cylinder instantiation, yielding a pose-aligned point cloud that is re-processed through the same shape-guided pipeline for a second, more accurate regression. Pose regression in both stages uses independent matrix Fisher distributions per degree of freedom, trained with negative log-likelihood loss, which provides a continuous parameterization and recovers prediction uncertainty.

## Results

On the most challenging evaluation group G3 (all four pose parameters α, β, γ, δ vary simultaneously), the proposed PoseRefine. achieves mean angular errors of 7.08° (α), 4.66° (β), 1.62° (γ), and 11.68° (δ), with median errors of 4.16°, 3.28°, 1.19°, and 4.66° respectively. For the roll angle δ, PoseRefine. reaches 53.0% Acc5 and 71.2% Acc10, substantially outperforming the best baseline SimPS (27.3% Acc5, 48.2% Acc10). The pose estimation stage (PoseEst.) already surpasses all baselines, and the shape-deformation refinement stage provides further consistent gains across every parameter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every pose parameter and every evaluation group. The keypoint-based KP [18] and skeleton-based SKL [36] baselines perform poorly (δ mean errors of 45.39° and 56.60° respectively on G3), confirming that explicit shape parsing is unreliable for textureless, continuously deformable flexible robots. Among regression-based methods, DR [42] achieves moderate accuracy (δ mean 27.49°) but lacks shape-aware feature modeling, while SimPS [35] is the strongest baseline (δ mean 17.96°, Acc10 48.2%). PoseRefine. reduces the δ mean error to 11.68° (a 35% improvement over SimPS) and nearly doubles its Acc10 to 71.2%. The gap is most pronounced on the roll angle δ, where camera-parallel viewing makes distant robot parts hard to distinguish, and the 3D shape prior provides critical geometric context that purely image-based methods cannot recover.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of 2D-3D joint representation learning and flexible robot kinematics. It builds on the piecewise constant curvature model for continuum robot shape parameterization and repurposes cross-attention mechanisms (commonly used in 2D-3D retrieval and multimodal fusion) to transfer part-level geometry from a 3D shape prior into 2D image features. It departs from the dominant keypoint- and skeleton-based paradigms for robot pose estimation, arguing that these explicit shape representations are ill-suited to textureless, continuously deformable bodies, and instead adopts a direct regression approach augmented by shape-guided feature enhancement. The probabilistic regression head using the matrix Fisher distribution follows the rotation regression literature but is applied here to multi-DOF flexible robot configuration parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in minimally invasive surgery where sensor-based shape sensing is costly, occlusion-prone, and incompatible with disposable instruments, offering a purely vision-based alternative. However, the evaluation is limited to a single proprietary robot prototype in ex vivo conditions with no public benchmark, which constrains the generalizability claims and reproducibility. Open questions include whether the shape-prior-guided cross-attention transfers to other deformable objects (e.g., surgical drapes, catheters), whether the dynamic deformation mechanism scales to robots with more segments or non-circular cross-sections, and whether the probabilistic uncertainty estimates can be meaningfully exploited in closed-loop control. The authors note future work on synthetic data generation and integration into real-time control loops.
