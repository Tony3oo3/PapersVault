## Problem

Pretrained text-to-image diffusion models struggle to generate high-resolution human-centric scenes with multiple people due to three compounding limitations: the training image size cap introduces artifacts when scaling up, the text encoder's 77-token budget cannot accommodate detailed per-instance descriptions, and existing joint-diffusion or dilation-based upscaling methods produce anatomical distortions, duplicated objects, and poor text-image correspondence in multi-person scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241704/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt containing a global scene description plus per-instance descriptions (which can collectively exceed the 77-token encoder limit) and per-instance human pose maps as input, and produces a single high-resolution RGB image up to 8192×8192 (64× beyond SDXL's 1024×1024 training resolution). Because each human instance is described and posed independently, the effective text budget is decoupled from the encoder's token cap, and the pose maps provide spatial anchoring for each individual in the final composition.

## Method

BeyondScene is a training-free, two-stage framework. Stage 1 (detailed base image generation) creates each human instance separately at training resolution using pose-guided T2I diffusion, then segments, crops, and composites them onto a diffusion-inpainted background with CLAHE tone normalization to bypass the text-encoder token limit. Stage 2 (instance-aware hierarchical enlargement) upsamples the base image and refines it beyond training resolution via two novel mechanisms: (a) high-frequency-injected forward diffusion, which adaptively perturbs pixels near Canny-detected edges to counteract the blurriness of naive upsample-and-diffuse while avoiding border flickering, and (b) adaptive joint diffusion, which applies view-wise conditioning (injecting only the pose and text of instances present in each overlapping view) and an adaptive stride (finer in human regions, coarser in background) to prevent object duplication and preserve per-instance identity during the multi-view denoising process.

## Results

On the CrowdCaption test set, BeyondScene achieves the highest MLLM (GPT-4)-based scores across all three resolutions for text-image correspondence, global naturalness, and human naturalness. At 4096×2048 (8× training resolution), BeyondScene scores 7.118 (text-image correspondence), 6.612 (global naturalness), and 6.375 (human naturalness), nearly doubling the best baseline ScaleCrafter (3.552, 3.569, 3.359). The method also demonstrates 8192×8192 generation (64× SDXL training resolution), exceeding the 8K standard. Global CLIP scores remain relatively flat across methods (0.24–0.35), confirming its limited discriminative power for complex multi-person scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeyondScene outperforms all seven baselines on every MLLM-based metric (text-image correspondence, global naturalness, human naturalness) across all three tested resolutions. The closest competitor is ScaleCrafter (dilation-based T2I-Large), which leads among baselines but still trails BeyondScene by roughly 2× on text-image correspondence at 4096×2048 (3.552 vs. 7.118) and by ~2.5× on human naturalness (3.359 vs. 6.375). Joint-diffusion methods (MultiDiffusion, SyncDiffusion) and visual-prior methods (ControlNet, T2IAdapter, R-MultiDiffusion) degrade sharply at higher resolutions, with scores often dropping below 2. Direct high-resolution inference (SDXL, RPG) is worst at larger scales, with RPG running out of memory beyond 2048×2048. Notably, global CLIP scores do not reflect the large MLLM gaps (all methods cluster between 0.24–0.35), and a user study (12,120 responses, 101 participants) confirms that MLLM scores align with human preference while CLIP does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two lines of work: (1) large-scene generation via joint diffusion (MultiDiffusion, SyncDiffusion) and dilation-based upscaling (ScaleCrafter), which address the training-resolution bottleneck but produce severe artifacts in human-centric scenes; and (2) controllable human generation via pose-conditioned diffusion (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, HumanSD), which handles individual humans well but cannot scale to multi-person scenes with per-instance descriptions. BeyondScene departs from both by introducing a staged 'artist workflow'—first generating detailed per-instance base images to bypass text-encoder token limits, then hierarchically enlarging via a novel high-frequency-injected forward diffusion (Canny-guided adaptive pixel perturbation) and adaptive joint diffusion (view-wise pose/text conditioning with variable stride)—rather than attempting to fill the entire canvas in a single joint-diffusion pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BeyondScene demonstrates that 8K+ (8192×8192, 64× training resolution) human-centric scenes with per-instance text control are achievable without any retraining of the base diffusion model, opening practical avenues for high-fidelity scene creation in film, gaming, and design. A notable finding is that global CLIP scores are misleadingly close across methods while GPT-4-based MLLM metrics reveal large quality gaps, suggesting the field needs better evaluation protocols for complex multi-instance scenes. Open questions include whether the hierarchical approach generalizes beyond SDXL-based architectures, the computational cost at 8K resolution, and whether the staged pipeline can be further accelerated or made end-to-end differentiable.
