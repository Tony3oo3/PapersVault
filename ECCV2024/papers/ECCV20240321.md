## Problem

Low-light video capture inherently requires long exposure, producing images degraded by both reduced illumination and motion blur simultaneously. While frame-based low-light enhancement and motion deblurring have been studied separately, and event cameras have been applied to each task individually, no prior work addresses the joint problem of event-guided low-light enhancement and deblurring in a unified framework. Moreover, existing datasets rely on synthetic low-light or blur generation, limiting generalizability to real-world conditions.

## Input / Output

The network consumes three sequential low-light blurry RGB frames (B^{t-1}, B^t, B^{t+1}) and three corresponding event voxel grids (E^{t-1}, E^t, E^{t+1}) at 1024×768 resolution, where the event stream is binned into voxel grids with bin size B for spatiotemporal preservation. The output is a single enhanced, deblurred normal-light sharp image at the same resolution. All data is captured synchronously via a custom triple-axis beam-splitter camera system with hardware-level microcontroller synchronization, ensuring pixel-accurate alignment between the low-light blur, normal-light sharp, and event modalities.

## Method

The framework employs two key modules within a UNet-based architecture. The ED-TFA module performs event-guided deformable temporal feature alignment: a transposed-attention transformer encoder extracts multi-scale feature pyramids from both blur and event inputs, then a coarse-to-fine deformable convolution (DCN) pipeline uses event features to estimate offsets and modulation masks for aligning adjacent frames to the current frame, overcoming the ill-posed correspondence problem under low light and blur. The SFCM-FE module enhances cross-modal features via spectral filtering: it applies FFT to a branch of the fused feature, multiplies by a learnable Gaussian low-pass filter to isolate low-frequency structural information, passes through a Fast Fourier Convolution block for additional spectral selection, and applies pixel-wise dynamic filters to adaptively enhance spatially variant structures while suppressing noise. The two branches (low-pass-filtered and unfiltered) are fused with spatial attention and channel attention blocks before feeding into the decoder. The entire network is trained end-to-end with supervised learning on the RELED dataset for 200 epochs.

## Results

On the RELED dataset, the full model achieves a PSNR of 31.30 dB and SSIM of 0.925, outperforming all 16 baselines. The lightweight variant (Ours-s) reaches 30.98 dB PSNR with only 5.3 MB parameters, surpassing even the best event-guided baseline REFID (30.10 dB) while using fewer than half the parameters. Compared to the only prior joint LLE+deblurring method LEDNet (26.47 dB), the proposed method gains 4.83 dB in PSNR. The best frame-based deblurring method (DSTNet, 29.59 dB) and best event-guided deblurring method (UEVD, 29.93 dB) are both outperformed by 1.71 dB and 1.37 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins against all 16 baselines on both PSNR and SSIM. Among frame-based LLE methods, the best performer SDSDNet (28.47 dB) is outperformed by 2.83 dB, while image-based LLE methods (SNRNet, LLFormer, RetinexFormer) all fall in the 26.47–26.66 dB range, trailing by 4.64–4.83 dB. Frame-based deblurring methods (MPRNet, MIMOUNet+, NAFNet) cluster around 26.5–26.9 dB, and video-based deblurring methods (RNN-MBP, DSTNet) reach 29.52–29.59 dB, still 1.7–1.8 dB below the proposed method. The only prior joint LLE+deblurring method LEDNet (26.47 dB) is surpassed by a substantial 4.83 dB margin. Among event-guided baselines, REFID (30.10 dB) is the closest competitor, with the proposed method gaining 1.20 dB; UEVD (29.93 dB) and EFNet (29.85 dB) trail by 1.37 dB and 1.45 dB respectively. Notably, the lightweight Ours-s variant (5.3 MB) already exceeds REFID (15.9 MB) by 0.88 dB, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two well-studied but previously separate tasks: low-light enhancement (LLE) and motion deblurring. While LEDNet [58] first addressed joint LLE and deblurring, it relied on synthetic degradation and frame-based inputs only; event-guided deblurring (EFNet, REFID, UEVD) and event-guided LLE (Liu et al., Liang et al.) have each been explored independently but never jointly. The authors repurpose deformable convolution for event-guided temporal alignment (ED-TFA) and introduce a frequency-domain cross-modal fusion module (SFCM-FE) that combines Gaussian low-pass filtering, Fast Fourier Convolution, and pixel-wise dynamic filters—techniques drawn from video deblurring and image restoration literature but assembled here for the first time in a joint low-light + blur + event setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RELED dataset is the first real-world benchmark providing synchronized low-light blur frames, normal-light sharp ground truth, and event streams at 1024×768, filling a critical gap where prior work relied on synthetic degradation or low-resolution DAVIS cameras. The hybrid beam-splitter camera system is a practical engineering contribution that the community can replicate. However, the dataset is small (42 scenes, 13 test), limiting statistical confidence, and the method's generalizability beyond the specific degradation profile (1/8 radiant exposure, 16 ms exposure) remains untested. Open questions include whether the joint formulation scales to longer video sequences, whether the SFCM-FE frequency-domain approach transfers to other cross-modal restoration tasks, and whether the performance gap over cascaded LLE-then-deblur pipelines holds under different noise levels.
