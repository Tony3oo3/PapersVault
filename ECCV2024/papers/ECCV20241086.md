## Problem

Machine unlearning in multimodal settings is largely unexplored, as existing unimodal approaches (weight scrubbing, certified removal, optimization-based methods) fail to handle the complex inter-modality dependencies inherent in multimodal data. Weight-scrubbing methods disrupt shared parameters across modalities, certified methods assume convexity that does not hold in multimodal architectures, and unimodal optimization methods cannot remove cross-modality associations beyond the last layer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained multimodal model decomposed into a vision/graph encoder f_I, a text encoder f_T, and a fusion module f_F, along with a deletion subset D_f of paired samples (e.g., image-text or graph-text pairs) and the remaining data D_r. Crucially, only the inter-modality associations in D_f are targeted for removal while individual unimodal elements are preserved. The output is an unlearned model f' evaluated on retrieval rankings (recall@1/3/10), classification accuracy, and membership inference vulnerability.

## Method

MULTIDELETE optimizes an aggregate loss L = α·L_MD + β·L_MKR + γ·L_UKR via SGD, where each term enforces a distinct property. Modality decoupling (L_MD) minimizes the distance between multimodal representations of deleted pairs and randomly sampled unrelated pairs from D_r, making deleted associations indistinguishable from noise. Multimodal knowledge retention (L_MKR) constrains f' to match f on the remaining data D_r, preserving overall task capability. Unimodal knowledge retention (L_UKR) preserves the individual encoder outputs f_I(I_i) and f_T(T_i) for deleted samples, preventing unnecessary loss of single-modality features. The approach is architecture-agnostic (applied to ALBEF, BLIP, and GCN+BERT), does not require strongly convex loss, and can be restricted to updating only the fusion module parameters for parameter-efficient unlearning.

## Results

On average across four image-text and one graph-text dataset, MULTIDELETE achieves 84.2 on D_Test (retention) and 28.7 on D_f (forgetting), outperforming the best baseline (FineTune: 83.5 / 50.2) by +0.7 and +21.5 points respectively. The paper reports an average improvement of 17.6 points over the best-performing baseline on the deletion set D_f, while maintaining a 0.3-point gain on D_Test. On the PGR graph-text dataset, MULTIDELETE achieves 18.6 on D_f versus 48.8 for the next-best method (L-CODEC), a gap of 30.2 points. MULTIDELETE also achieves a lower prior-to-post MI ratio (1.27) than non-scrubbing baselines, indicating better protection against membership inference attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MULTIDELETE outperforms all five baselines on both retention (D_Test) and forgetting (D_f) metrics across all datasets. Against the modality-agnostic FineTune [Golatkar et al., CVPR 2020], MULTIDELETE gains +0.7 on D_Test and +21.5 on D_f (28.7 vs. 50.2), showing that simple continued fine-tuning fails to remove inter-modality associations. Against NegGrad [Golatkar et al., CVPR 2020], the gap is +3.7 on D_Test and +1.3 on D_f. The weight-scrubbing methods DtD [Neel et al., ALT 2021] and L-CODEC [Mehta et al., CVPR 2022] perform worst on retention (33.0 and 66.3 on D_Test respectively) while still failing to forget (D_f ≈ 50), confirming that noise-based scrubbing disrupts shared parameters without targeted unlearning. The unimodal baselines ERM-KTP [Lin et al., CVPR 2023] and UL [Jang et al., ACL 2023] also fail to achieve meaningful forgetting (D_f ≈ 49–50), demonstrating that unlearning a single modality is insufficient for multimodal data. On the graph-text PGR dataset, MULTIDELETE's advantage is most pronounced: 18.6 on D_f versus 48.8 for L-CODEC and 49.7 for UL, a gap exceeding 30 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating multimodal machine unlearning as a distinct problem, arguing that existing unimodal unlearning methods (weight scrubbing, certified removal, negative gradient, retraining) fail to address cross-modality dependencies inherent in vision-language or graph-text models. Conceptually, it repurposes the teacher-student unlearning paradigm—keeping the unlearned model's outputs close to the original on retained data—but decomposes this into three targeted objectives: a contrastive-style modality decoupling loss that pushes deleted pairs toward random-pair representations, plus separate multimodal and unimodal knowledge retention losses. The approach is architecture-agnostic and does not require strongly convex losses, departing from certified removal methods that assume convexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multimodal models (CLIP, BLIP, ALBEF) become ubiquitous in deployed systems, the need to selectively remove data associations without full retraining is practically urgent (GDPR, copyright, consent revocation). This paper provides the first formal problem definition and a simple, efficient solution, but several open questions remain: whether the approach scales to more than two modalities or very large deletion sets, whether certified guarantees can be established in the multimodal setting, and how it handles sequential or overlapping deletion requests. The finding that weight-scrubbing methods with theoretical guarantees perform catastrophically in multimodal settings (33–66 average vs. 84 for MultiDelete) suggests that unimodal unlearning theory does not transfer, motivating further theoretical work.
