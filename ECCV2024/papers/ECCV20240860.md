## Problem

Existing robustness evaluations for Text-to-Image (T2I) diffusion models frame the problem as binary (does an AE exist?) or worst-case (what is the closest AE?), which cannot answer how robust the model is in general whenever an adversarial example can be found. No probabilistic robustness verification framework with statistical guarantees exists for T2I diffusion models, and the generative nature of these models introduces two unique challenges: the high computational cost of image generation and the need to compare two output distributions (rather than a single label) to identify adversarial examples.

## Input / Output

ProTIP takes a text prompt, a T2I diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion V1.4/V1.5/SDXL-Turbo), a verification target (b_l, σ) specifying a minimum robustness threshold and confidence level, and a perturbation rate (e.g., 10% or 20%). Perturbations are character-level (insert, substitute, swap, delete, keyboard error) and are filtered through a CLIP text-encoder similarity threshold γ to ensure semantic preservation. The output is a pass/fail verification decision, an estimated probabilistic robustness R_M(x), and a statistically guaranteed lower bound on R_M(x), along with the adaptively determined number of perturbations actually needed.

## Method

ProTIP employs a two-loop sequential framework. The inner loop performs two-sample hypothesis testing on CLIP scores (measuring image-text alignment) to classify each perturbation as an AE or Non-AE, using sequential analysis with both efficacy (reject H_0 early) and futility (accept H_0 early) stopping rules governed by Pocock-type alpha/beta spending functions via the RPACT package, reducing image generation cost by up to 97% for Non-AEs. The outer loop applies an adaptive Hoeffding's inequality (Theorem 1) where the sample size J is itself a stopping variable, dynamically increasing perturbations until the empirically estimated lower bound μ̂_I^(J) − ε(σ,J) meets the verification target b_l, at which point ProTIP asserts 'pass'; if the maximum budget j_max is exhausted without meeting the target, it asserts 'fail'. This adaptive mechanism avoids the need to pre-specify a fixed sample size, saving up to 122 perturbations compared to a conservative fixed-budget Hoeffding approach while preserving the same statistical guarantee.

## Results

For a 10% perturbation rate on SD V1.5, ProTIP requires only 70–100 perturbations to meet the verification target (b_l = 0.8), and its estimates converge to the approximated ground truth across confidence levels of 90%, 95%, and 99%. In the inner loop (sequential hypothesis testing), 97% of Non-AEs and 68% of AEs are identified before the final interim stage, with 246 out of 347 Non-AEs resolved at stage 1 alone (a 4× image-generation cost saving). In the outer loop, ProTIP's adaptive sample size of 78 correctly verifies the target where fixed-sample Hoeffding's inequality with n=50 incorrectly asserts 'fail', and saves 122 perturbations compared to n=200 while reaching the same correct decision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark ProTIP against competing probabilistic robustness verification frameworks; instead, it compares its adaptive Hoeffding's inequality (outer loop) against the original fixed-sample Hoeffding's inequality at n = 50, 100, and 200. At n = 50, fixed Hoeffding's incorrectly asserts 'fail' while ProTIP (n = 78) correctly passes; at n = 200 both are correct but ProTIP saves 122 perturbations; at n = 100 the two are comparable, though the fixed version is slightly tighter (Corollary 1). For the defence-method ranking application, Autocorrect 0.3.0 consistently outperforms Pyspellchecker and Gramformer in restoring probabilistic robustness after character-level perturbations, as confirmed by box-plot statistics over 36 prompts (Fig. 7). The sequential inner-loop analysis (Pocock spending, 5 interim stages) is compared implicitly against a fixed-sample two-sample test, showing that early stopping eliminates the need to generate the full maximum sample for the majority of perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the probabilistic robustness verification framework established for DL classifiers (e.g., [55,64]) to the fundamentally different setting of T2I diffusion models, where adversarial examples are identified by comparing output image distributions rather than prediction labels. It draws on the adaptive Hoeffding's inequality from [64,65] for the outer-loop sample-size determination and adapts the CLIP Score-based distributional comparison from [11,18] for the inner-loop AE detection. The sequential analysis with efficacy/futility stopping rules (borrowed from clinical trial methodology via RPACT) is a novel application to the T2I robustness setting, addressing the high computational cost of iterative image generation that makes fixed-sample approaches impractical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProTIP provides the first statistically principled, black-box verification framework for T2I DM robustness with guaranteed confidence bounds, filling a gap where only deterministic (binary/worst-case) evaluations existed. Its practical demonstration—ranking spell-checking defences—shows immediate applicability for practitioners deploying T2I systems. However, the evaluation is limited to character-level perturbations on Stable Diffusion variants, and the reliance on CLIP Score as a proxy for distributional difference (rather than direct image-space comparison) raises open questions about sensitivity to semantic shifts that CLIP may not capture. Extending to word/sentence-level perturbations, other DM architectures, and more nuanced output-distribution metrics remain important next steps.
