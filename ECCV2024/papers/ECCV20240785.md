## Problem

Generalizable NeRF methods for street scenes rely on feature matching to recover geometry, which overfits to specific reference camera pose configurations and degrades severely under sparse views with small parallax angles typical of forward-moving vehicles. The paper asks whether explicit geometric priors (e.g., noisy monocular/stereo depth) can be leveraged in a 3D-space formulation to achieve robust, generalizable urban view synthesis across diverse sparsity levels without per-scene optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240785/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse RGB reference images (typically 3 frames) with known camera poses and intrinsics, plus predicted metric depth maps from off-the-shelf estimators (MobileStereoNet for KITTI-360, Metric3D for Waymo). Depth maps are unprojected into a world-coordinate point cloud, filtered by a depth-consistency check (0.2 m threshold), and discretized into a 128×64×256 voxel grid at 0.2 m resolution covering a 25.2×12.8×51.2 m foreground volume. The output is a rendered novel-view color image (full resolution 376×1408) and an implicit depth map, produced either by feed-forward inference or after ~5 minutes of per-scene fine-tuning that updates only the global feature volume.

## Method

EDUS decomposes the unbounded street scene into three generalizable modules: a depth-guided foreground field, an image-based background field, and a view-dependent sky field. The foreground module lifts noisy accumulated depth point clouds into a 3D volume and processes them with a SPADE CNN (3 spatially-adaptive normalization residual blocks with multi-resolution modulation) to produce a feature volume, which is combined with 2D bilinearly-interpolated colors from the K=3 nearest reference views via a 6-layer MLP to predict per-point density and color. The background and sky are modeled purely through image-based rendering with lightweight MLPs conditioned on 2D features and view direction. Training uses photometric L2 loss, an optional softened LiDAR line-of-sight loss (with exponentially decaying bound width from 0.5 m to 0.1 m), a sky-segmentation BCE loss, and an entropy regularization term to penalize semi-transparent reconstructions. For per-scene fine-tuning, the SPADE CNN is frozen and only the global feature volume is updated, enabling convergence in under 5 minutes with 6 GB memory at full resolution.

## Results

On KITTI-360 with per-scene fine-tuning at 50% drop rate, EDUS achieves 24.43 dB PSNR, 0.793 SSIM, and 0.136 LPIPS, outperforming the closest generalizable baseline MuRF (23.71 / 0.762 / 0.233). At the more challenging 80% drop rate, EDUS reaches 20.91 dB PSNR and 0.220 LPIPS, maintaining a clear advantage over all baselines. On the out-of-domain Waymo dataset (50% drop, per-scene opt.), EDUS scores 28.45 dB PSNR and 0.132 LPIPS. Fine-tuning converges in under 5 minutes, roughly 5–10× faster than per-scene optimization baselines like DS-NeRF, SparseNeRF, and MixNeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generalizable NeRF baselines, EDUS consistently outperforms IBRNet, MVSNeRF, and NeO 360 across all metrics and sparsity levels; the gap widens substantially at 80% drop (e.g., EDUS 19.63 vs. IBRNet 15.96 PSNR on KITTI-360). The closest competitor is MuRF, which matches EDUS on PSNR at 50% drop (22.19 vs. 21.93 feed-forward) but EDUS achieves markedly better LPIPS (0.178 vs. 0.264) and pulls ahead at 80% drop (19.63 vs. 18.69 PSNR). After per-scene fine-tuning, EDUS surpasses MuRF on PSNR and LPIPS at every setting (e.g., 24.43 vs. 23.71 PSNR, 0.136 vs. 0.233 LPIPS on KITTI-360 50%). Against test-time optimization methods, EDUS dominates at 80% and 90% drop rates (20.91 and 19.16 dB PSNR vs. 19.80 and 17.46 for 3DGS, the strongest baseline), while 3DGS edges out EDUS on SSIM and LPIPS at 50% drop (0.804/0.109 vs. 0.793/0.136). EDUS also converges in ~5 minutes versus 10–51 minutes for 3DGS, MixNeRF, SparseNeRF, and DS-NeRF, making it the fastest method to reach competitive or superior quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDUS departs from the dominant paradigm in generalizable NeRF (IBRNet, MVSNeRF, MuRF) that recovers geometry through feature matching between reference and target views, which is sensitive to camera pose configurations and degrades under sparse street-view baselines. Instead, it lifts noisy monocular/stereo depth predictions into a world-coordinate point cloud and processes them with a 3D SPADE CNN (borrowed from semantic image synthesis), making the geometry representation invariant to reference pose arrangements. The scene decomposition into foreground, background, and sky modules draws on unbounded-scene methods (Mip-NeRF 360, Zip-NeRF), while the LiDAR line-of-sight loss is adapted from Urban Radiance Fields with a softened formulation. The image-based 2D feature retrieval for high-frequency appearance follows IBRNet's projection-and-interpolation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving: EDUS achieves feed-forward novel view synthesis from as few as 3 reference images and fine-tunes in under 5 minutes (vs. 30–50 minutes for per-scene baselines), making it viable for real-time simulation and data augmentation pipelines. Cross-dataset generalization (KITTI-360 → Waymo) without retraining demonstrates that geometric-prior-based generalization transfers across sensor suites and driving environments. Key limitations include reliance on the quality of off-the-shelf depth estimators, inability to handle dynamic objects (explicitly deferred to future work), and the fixed foreground volume that may not adapt to all scene scales. An open question is whether the SPADE-CNN-on-point-cloud formulation can be extended to longer sequences or multi-day captures where illumination and scene content change substantially.
