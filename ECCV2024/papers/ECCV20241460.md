## Problem

Computing the 6DOF relative pose of multi-camera systems from the minimal six point correspondences has been limited to a single solver (Stewenius et al.) with 64 solutions that does not exploit structural redundancies in the correspondence configuration. When subsets of PCs are observed by the same cameras across both views, additional algebraic constraints exist that can reduce the solution count and improve numerical stability, but these had not been formally identified or leveraged. Furthermore, the full combinatorial space of six-point configurations for multi-camera systems had never been enumerated.

## Input / Output

Each of the six point correspondences carries normalized image-plane coordinates plus camera indices (i_k, i_k') specifying which camera in view 1 and view 2 observes the point, so the essential matrix differs per PC. The calibrated intrinsic and extrinsic parameters of all cameras in the rig are assumed known. The solver selection depends on the correspondence pattern: the generic solver handles arbitrary camera assignments, while dedicated inter-camera and intra-camera solvers activate when all six PCs cross between different cameras or stay within the same camera pair, respectively, yielding 48 solutions instead of 64.

## Method

The method decouples rotation and translation in the epipolar constraint by parametrizing rotation with Cayley (or quaternion) variables and treating translation as a hidden variable, producing a 6×4 matrix M whose 4×4 submatrix determinants yield degree-6 polynomial equations in the three rotation unknowns. The key theoretical contribution is the ray bundle constraint (Theorem 1): when three or more PCs are observed by the same camera pair, the corresponding 3×3 submatrix of M has rank 2, generating additional degree-4 equations that eliminate extraneous roots and reduce the solution count from 64 to 48. The polynomial system is solved via Gröbner basis computation (Macaulay 2) with elimination templates precomputed over a finite prime field for numerical stability. Three solvers are generated: a generic solver for arbitrary generalized cameras (64 solutions), and inter-camera and intra-camera solvers for two-camera rigs (48 solutions each). All 5953 distinct six-point configurations for multi-camera systems are enumerated using the Pólya enumeration theorem and a recursive graph enumeration algorithm.

## Results

On the KITTI dataset (11 sequences, ~23,000 image pairs), the proposed 6pt-Our-intra solver achieves a median rotation error of 0.101°–0.175° and translation direction error of 1.677°–3.323°, outperforming all baselines on the majority of sequences. On EuRoC MAV (5 sequences), it achieves median rotation error of 0.127°–0.193° and translation direction error of 2.376°–3.105°. In terms of runtime, 6pt-Our-intra runs in 1.546 s on average, which is 2.0× faster than 17pt-Li (3.157 s) and 2.7× faster than 6pt-Stewenius (4.161 s), though slower than 8pt-Kneip (0.648 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 6pt-Our-intra solver consistently outperforms 6pt-Stewenius [46] on both rotation and translation direction errors across all 11 KITTI sequences and all 5 EuRoC sequences, with translation direction errors roughly 1.5°–4° lower. Against 17pt-Li [35], 6pt-Our-intra wins on 9/11 KITTI sequences for rotation error and 10/11 for translation direction error, while also being 2.0× faster in RANSAC runtime (1.546 s vs. 3.157 s). Compared to 8pt-Kneip [23], 6pt-Our-intra achieves lower translation direction error on 10/11 KITTI sequences but is slightly behind on rotation error for a few sequences (e.g., Seq 03: 0.143° vs. 0.139°); however, 8pt-Kneip is the fastest at 0.648 s. Notably, 17pt-Li fails entirely on the generic (non-overlapping) case due to rank deficiency, and 6pt-Stewenius exhibits large error variance on inter-camera and intra-camera configurations, confirming that the ray bundle constraints exploited by the proposed method are critical for practical multi-camera rigs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the six-point minimal solver for multi-camera relative pose introduced by Stewenius et al. (2005), which established the 64-solution baseline. The key methodological departure is the identification and proof of ray bundle constraints—algebraic properties that arise when a subset of point correspondences link the same cameras across views—which reduce the solution count from 64 to 48 and yield numerically more stable elimination templates. The solver construction follows the established Gröbner-basis pipeline (Cayley/quaternion parametrization, Macaulay 2, syzygy-based reduction) used in prior minimal-problem work, but applies it to a decoupled rotation-translation formulation specific to generalized cameras. The paper also completes the combinatorial picture by enumerating all 5953 minimal six-point configurations via the Pólya enumeration theorem, a step not attempted in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduced solution space (48 vs. 64) and smaller elimination templates translate directly into faster RANSAC iterations and lower outlier sensitivity, which matters for real-time multi-camera systems in autonomous driving and UAV navigation. The ray bundle constraint provides a general theoretical tool that can be applied to other multi-camera configurations, including partially uncalibrated cameras and known-rotation-angle scenarios. Limitations include the Cayley parametrization's inability to represent 180° rotations and the algebraic solvers' potential numerical fragility for very large camera rigs; the paper does not address learning-based or hybrid approaches that might complement these algebraic solvers at scale.
