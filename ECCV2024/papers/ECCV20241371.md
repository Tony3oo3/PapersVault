## Problem

Stylized text-to-image generation struggles to capture fine-grained style details from a single reference image, as existing personalized generation methods require multiple references (where subtle inter-image variations confuse the model) and textual prompts are inherently ambiguous for precise style description. The paper identifies that the inversion noise produced by DDIM inversion of a stylized image retains a non-zero signal-to-noise ratio, meaning it secretly encodes style information that prior methods discard.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241371/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single reference image (any resolution, encoded into latent space by a pretrained autoencoder) and a text prompt specifying the target object. The reference image is consumed indirectly: it is first inverted to a noise vector via DDIM inversion conditioned on a learned style token, and this noise vector—rather than the image itself—serves as the initial state for generation. The output is a new RGB image in the reference style depicting the text-described object, generated at the standard Stable Diffusion resolution. For style combination, two reference images and two style tokens are jointly used with tunable noise-mix and prompt-mix ratios.

## Method

INSTASTYLE operates in two stages. First, DDIM inversion transforms the reference image into a terminal noise z_T whose non-zero SNR (0.015 at T=1000 in Stable Diffusion) certifies retained style signal; new images are then sampled from this noise via standard DDIM denoising. Second, Prompt Refinement learns a dedicated style token (e.g., '<style1>') by fine-tuning its embedding and the key/value projection matrices in the U-Net cross-attention layers using LoRA, trained on 5 human-selected high-quality images from stage 1. The learned style token is injected into both the inversion prompt and the generation prompt, and classifier-free guidance (w=2.5) drives the final sampling. For style combination, two inversion noises are blended element-wise via a random binary mask (ratio α) and two style-conditioned guidance terms are weighted by a prompt mix ratio β, enabling continuous interpolation between styles.

## Results

On 100 CIFAR-100 objects, INSTASTYLE achieves the highest style consistency (CLIP score 0.655) and content fidelity (CLIP score 0.294) among all 10 compared methods. The closest competitor on style is Custom Diffusion (0.611), while the closest on content is QuantArt (0.289). In a 6,000-vote user study with 20 participants, INSTASTYLE is preferred over Textual Inversion 78% of the time and over Custom Diffusion 76% of the time, though it is less dominant against CAST (43% preference) and CCPL/StyleDrop (47% preference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INSTASTYLE outperforms all four personalized generation baselines on both metrics: it beats Custom Diffusion (0.611/0.280) by +0.044 style and +0.014 content, StyleDrop (0.602/0.248) by +0.053 style and +0.046 content, Textual Inversion (0.562/0.278) by +0.093 style and +0.016 content, and DreamBooth (0.561/0.244) by +0.094 style and +0.050 content. Against the six style transfer methods, INSTASTYLE also leads on both scores despite the unfair disadvantage of using only text prompts for content (style transfer methods receive a content image), with the largest style margin over CCPL (+0.113) and the largest content margin over StyTr2 (+0.012). In the user study, INSTASTYLE is strongly preferred over Textual Inversion (78%) and Custom Diffusion (76%), moderately preferred over QuantArt (65%) and AdaAttN (61%), but shows a narrower or even reversed margin against CAST (43% preference for ours), CCPL (47%), and StyleDrop (47%), suggesting these methods produce visually competitive results in subjective evaluation even when their CLIP-based scores are lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes DDIM inversion (Song et al., ICLR 2021) within the Stable Diffusion framework, but with a key conceptual departure: rather than treating the non-zero signal-to-noise ratio of inversion noise as a training artifact to be eliminated (as Lin et al. do), the authors exploit it as a carrier of fine-grained style information. It positions itself against personalized generation methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, StyleDrop) that require multiple reference images and fine-tune the full model, and against stylized generation approaches (ZDAIS, Diffusion in Style) that treat style as domain adaptation. The prompt refinement stage borrows the learnable-token idea from Textual Inversion and the LoRA fine-tuning strategy, but applies them during the inversion (noising) process rather than the denoising process, which is a distinct architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-reference-image requirement is a practical advantage over multi-image personalization methods, and the style combination capability (via noise masking and composed guidance) opens a creative generation direction not well addressed by prior stylized generation work. However, the method still depends on human feedback to select training images for prompt refinement, limiting full automation. Open questions include whether the style signal in inversion noise generalizes across different diffusion architectures and noise schedules (the SNR analysis is specific to Stable Diffusion's linear schedule), and whether the human-in-the-loop selection can be replaced by automated quality scoring. The moderate quantitative gains over strong baselines (e.g., 0.655 vs. 0.611 style score) suggest the approach is effective but not transformative.
