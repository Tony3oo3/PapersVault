## Problem

Existing CLIP-based compositional zero-shot learning methods rely on deterministic hard or soft prompts that lack the diversity to capture intra-class visual variance and the linguistic informativeness needed for fine-grained compositional categories. Additionally, the entanglement between visual primitives (state and object) in an image is not properly addressed, limiting the model's ability to decompose and re-compose for zero-shot generalization to unseen compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240357/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, which is augmented with N=8 random views to enrich the visual representation before encoding by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14. On the text side, M=64 LLM-generated sentence-level descriptions per compositional class serve as distribution support points. The output is a compositional class label (a state-object pair), evaluated in both closed-world (seen + feasible unseen compositions) and open-world (all compositions, with GloVe-based feasibility calibration) settings.

## Method

PLID prompts language-informed Gaussian distributions by using an LLM (OPT-1.3B) to generate diverse, informative sentence descriptions for each compositional class, which are encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder to form distribution support points (DSP) around a learnable class mean produced by a single shared soft prompt and learnable state/object embeddings. A joint Gaussian is built across all seen classes, and minimizing the cross-entropy loss induces intra-class variance reduction and inter-class separability through the covariance structure. A visual-language primitive decomposition (VLPD) module uses two parallel networks to split the enhanced visual feature into state and object sub-features, each supervised against grouped text embeddings with their own distributional losses. Finally, a stochastic logit mixup with a Beta(1,9) prior fuses the directly predicted compositional logit with the recomposed logit (Cartesian sum of state and object logits), providing regularization during training and a fixed weighted average at test time.

## Results

On the three CZSL benchmarks, PLID achieves state-of-the-art performance in most metrics under both closed-world and open-world settings. On MIT-States (closed-world), PLID reaches H=39.0 and AUC=22.1, surpassing the strongest baseline DFSP (H=37.3, AUC=20.6). On UT-Zappos (closed-world), PLID attains H=52.4 and AUC=38.7, a substantial margin over DFSP (H=47.2, AUC=36.0), though it trails DFSP on unseen accuracy (68.8 vs. 71.7). On C-GQA (closed-world), PLID achieves H=27.9 and AUC=11.0, outperforming DFSP (H=27.1, AUC=10.5) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLID outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of metrics across all three datasets in both closed- and open-world settings. Against the strongest competitor DFSP [21], PLID gains +1.7 H and +1.5 AUC on MIT-States (closed), +5.2 H and +2.7 AUC on UT-Zappos (closed), and +0.8 H and +0.5 AUC on C-GQA (closed); however, PLID trails DFSP on UT-Zappos unseen accuracy (68.8 vs. 71.7) and open-world UT-Zappos unseen accuracy (55.5 vs. 60.0). Against CSP [30], the original CLIP-based CZSL baseline, PLID improves H by +2.7 (MIT-States CW), +5.8 (UT-Zappos CW), and +7.4 (C-GQA CW), demonstrating the benefit of distributional prompting over deterministic hard prompts. ProDA [22], which also models class-wise Gaussian distributions but without LLM-informed descriptions, is substantially outperformed (e.g., AUC 22.1 vs. 16.1 on MIT-States CW), confirming that informativeness of the distribution support is critical beyond mere diversity. HPL [40] and PCVL [42], which use prompt learning at both compositional and primitive levels, remain well below PLID, indicating that traditional prompt learning alone is insufficient for CZSL generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLID sits at the intersection of three research threads in CLIP-based CZSL: (1) prompt learning for compositional recognition (CSP's hard prompt, CoOp's soft prompt, DFSP's decomposed soft prompts), (2) distributional prompt modeling (ProDA's class-specific Gaussian distributions, PPL, PBPrompt), and (3) LLM-generated text for class representation (Menon & Vondrick's classification-by-description). The paper's core positioning is that prior CLIP-CZSL methods address either diversity (distributional prompts) or informativeness (hard/soft prompts) but not both simultaneously, and that LLM sentence-level descriptions can serve as the support points for class-specific Gaussian distributions. The VLPD module with stochastic logit mixup extends the decompose-recompose paradigm from traditional vision-based CZSL into the CLIP prompt-learning framework, going beyond DFSP's text-only decomposition by also decomposing visual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that context informativeness (LLM descriptions) is more critical than context diversity alone for fine-grained compositional recognition provides a useful design principle for CLIP-based zero-shot methods beyond CZSL. The observation that a small OPT-1.3B outperforms GPT-3.5 and Mistral-7B for generating class descriptions is practically important (reduces LLM dependency) but is demonstrated only on MIT-States with small margins, leaving open whether this holds across domains. The method remains limited by the inherent entanglement of visual primitives—case studies show persistent errors on state and object predictions—and the open-world performance on C-GQA (H=10.6) remains far from practical utility. Whether the distributional prompting + LLM description recipe transfers to other fine-grained recognition tasks (e.g., medical imaging, industrial inspection) is an open question.
