## Problem

Online clustering-based self-supervised methods require large batch sizes to maintain balanced cluster assignments and prevent collapse, while offline re-clustering methods do not scale to large datasets. Existing balancing constraints rely on in-batch statistics or proxy metrics (e.g., aggregate assignment confidence), making them unreliable with small batches and introducing high resource demands. The paper asks whether cluster balancing can be done explicitly, online, and effectively at small batch sizes without solving optimization problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240859/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and produces multiple augmented views per sample: two global 224×224 crops (scale range [0.14, 1]) and, for multi-crop training, six local 96×96 crops (scale range [0.05, 0.2]). The output is a 256-dimensional visual representation from the backbone plus a soft assignment vector over K=65,536 learned cluster centroids, computed via cosine similarity. Notably, the balancing operator operates on the teacher's hard cluster assignments tracked across batches via an exponential moving average, rather than on per-batch soft statistics.

## Method

ExCB is a DINO-style teacher-student framework where the student matches the teacher's cluster assignments across augmented views, with the teacher updated via EMA. The key novelty is the balancing operator B, which has two components: (1) it maintains a relative cluster size vector s by tracking hard (argmax) assignments across multiple batches with a momentum of 0.999, yielding an accurate dataset-wide size estimate independent of batch size; and (2) it adjusts sample-cluster cosine similarities proportionally to each cluster's deviation from the ideal 1/K size—increasing similarities for undersized clusters and decreasing them for oversized ones via a piecewise linear transformation. This explicit, optimization-free balancing is applied only to the teacher's global-view assignments, and cluster centroids are updated exclusively from global views (stop-gradient on local crops). The result is a soft balancing constraint that prevents collapse while remaining fully online, requiring no large batch size, and incurring negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, ExCB achieves 76.5% linear classification accuracy and 71.0% k-NN accuracy, setting a new state of the art while using only a batch size of 1,024 and 400 pretraining epochs (vs. 4,096 batch size and 800–1,000 epochs for most competitors). With a ViT-S/16 backbone, ExCB reaches 77.1% linear accuracy at 800 epochs, marginally surpassing DINO (77.0%). In semi-supervised fine-tuning with 10% labels, ExCB attains 71.5% top-1 accuracy, and on MS COCO object detection it achieves 39.2 AP^b and 34.3 AP^m, competitive with methods trained for twice as many epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExCB outperforms all 15 compared baselines on ImageNet linear classification. Against the closest competitors, it edges out CoKe (76.4%) and SMoG (76.4%) by 0.1%, and surpasses MAST (75.8%) and MIRA (75.7%) by 0.7–0.8%. The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: ExCB beats DINO (75.3%) and SwAV (75.3%) by 1.2%, BYOL (74.4%) by 2.1%, and SimCLR (71.7%) by 4.8%. Critically, ExCB achieves these gains with a batch size of 1,024 (4× smaller than DINO, SwAV, MIRA, and SMoG which use 4,096) and only 400 epochs (half of the 800 used by DINO/SwAV/MIRA, and a quarter of BYOL/MoCo-v3's 1,000). On k-NN evaluation, ExCB's 71.0% substantially exceeds MIRA (68.8%), DINO (67.5%), and Barlow Twins (66.0%). In semi-supervised fine-tuning (1% labels), ExCB (57.8% top-1) is within 0.2% of SMoG (58.0%) while using a 4× smaller batch size; at 10% labels it leads all methods at 71.5%. On MS COCO detection, ExCB (39.2 AP^b) ties SimSiam and Barlow Twins and trails MoCo-v2 (39.3) by 0.1, while matching or exceeding all other baselines on segmentation (34.3 AP^m vs. 34.4 for MoCo-v2/SimSiam).

## Relation to Prior Work

ExCB builds directly on the DINO teacher-student framework with learned cluster centroids and the SwAV online clustering paradigm, adopting their architecture, multi-crop augmentation, and EMA-based teacher updates. The key conceptual departure is in the balancing mechanism: rather than relying on in-batch statistics (SwAV's Sinkhorn-Knopp, DINO's centering) or solving per-batch optimization problems (MIRA, TWIST), ExCB tracks hard cluster assignments across multiple batches via an exponential moving average and adjusts sample-cluster similarities proportionally to each cluster's measured size. This shifts the balancing constraint from a per-batch, batch-size-dependent operation to a dataset-level, batch-size-agnostic one, while remaining fully online.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance of ExCB lies in substantially lowering the resource barrier for self-supervised pretraining: achieving state-of-the-art linear accuracy (76.5%) with a batch size of 1,024 and 400 epochs, compared to 4,096 and 800–1,000 for most competing methods, makes high-quality pretraining accessible on far fewer GPUs. The demonstrated stability at batch size 256 (70.5% vs. 70.7% at 1,024) suggests the approach could extend to even more constrained settings. Open questions include whether the EMA-based cluster size tracking remains reliable at web-scale dataset sizes, how the method behaves with a much larger number of clusters, and whether the efficiency gains transfer to other modalities or dense-prediction-focused pretraining objectives.
