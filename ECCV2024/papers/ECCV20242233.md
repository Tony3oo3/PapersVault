## Problem

Diffusion models for inverse problems require hundreds to thousands of sampling steps for convergence, and while irregular noise schedules (denser steps at low noise levels) dramatically reduce steps for unconditional generation, they are impractical for inverse problems because each irregular timestep demands a distinct log-likelihood weight that must be hand-tuned. This paper addresses the gap by automatically learning these per-timestep weights in a zero-shot, test-time manner without retraining the pre-trained score model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242233/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy linear measurement y = Ax_0 + n, the known forward operator A, a frozen pre-trained unconditional diffusion score model (trained on FFHQ or ImageNet at 256×256×3), and the measurement noise level σ_y. It outputs a single reconstructed image x_0 after a fixed number of fine-tuning epochs (e.g., 10) over a fixed number of sampling steps (e.g., 30) within a total NFE budget (e.g., 300). The pre-trained score network is never updated; only scalar log-likelihood weights and diagonal Hessian entries are optimized per image.

## Method

ZAPS unrolls the DPS posterior sampling loop for S steps on an irregular noise schedule, alternating between score-model denoising and a likelihood-guidance correction weighted by a learnable scalar ζ_t at each step. To avoid the memory explosion of backpropagating through the score network's Jacobian at every unrolled step, the Hessian of the log prior is approximated as W D_t W^T using a fixed orthogonal Daubechies-4 wavelet transform, where only the diagonal entries of D_t are learned. Both {ζ_t} and {D_t} are optimized end-to-end over a small number of epochs using a measurement-consistency loss ‖y − Ax_0‖², with the score model held frozen. This wavelet-diagonalization trick eliminates the need to store or backpropagate through the score network's computational graph, reducing both memory and wall-clock time while enabling the use of irregular schedules that were previously infeasible for inverse problems.

## Results

On FFHQ (256×256×3, σ=0.05), ZAPS achieves the best LPIPS, SSIM, and PSNR across all four inverse problem tasks using only 300 NFEs (30 steps × 10 epochs). For Gaussian deblurring, ZAPS reaches PSNR 26.06 dB and LPIPS 0.121, outperforming DPS (25.20 dB, 0.128) by ~0.86 dB. For super-resolution (×4), ZAPS achieves PSNR 26.63 dB and LPIPS 0.104, surpassing DPS (23.86 dB, 0.168) by 2.77 dB. Wall-clock time is 14.71 s for ZAPS versus 47.25 s for DPS, a ~3× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZAPS outperforms DPS (the primary baseline) on all three metrics across all four tasks while using only 300 NFEs versus DPS's 1000 NFEs; the largest gap is in super-resolution where ZAPS gains 2.77 dB PSNR (26.63 vs 23.86). Against ΠGDM, ZAPS wins on LPIPS and PSNR for inpainting (0.078 vs 0.086 LPIPS; 27.79 vs 26.62 PSNR) and super-resolution (0.104 vs 0.131 LPIPS; 26.63 vs 24.48 PSNR), though ΠGDM retains a slight SSIM edge on inpainting (0.842 vs 0.813); ΠGDM could not be reliably implemented for deblurring tasks. ZAPS dominates DDRM by a wide margin (e.g., 26.06 vs 24.42 dB PSNR on Gaussian deblurring) and MCG/Score-SDE by an even larger margin (~10 dB PSNR gap), confirming that projection-based and unguided methods are substantially inferior. The key practical advantage is that ZAPS achieves these gains with irregular noise schedules and zero-shot learned weights, eliminating the manual hyperparameter tuning required by DPS and ΠGDM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework of Chung et al. (ICLR 2023), adopting its log-likelihood guidance term as the core update, and on the algorithm unrolling paradigm from physics-driven deep learning (Gregor & LeCun, 2010) to structure the sampling process as a trainable network. It departs from prior inverse-problem diffusion methods (DPS, IGDM, DDRM, MCG) by making the per-timestep log-likelihood weights learnable via zero-shot fine-tuning rather than hand-tuned, and by introducing a wavelet-based diagonal Hessian approximation to avoid backpropagating through the score model's Jacobian. The zero-shot/test-time adaptation idea is borrowed from self-supervised MRI reconstruction literature, repurposed here for hyperparameter tuning at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZAPS makes irregular (non-uniform) noise schedules practical for inverse problems for the first time, reducing NFEs from ~1000 to ~300 while matching or exceeding DPS quality, which is a meaningful practical speedup for deployment. The wavelet Hessian approximation is a clever and lightweight trick that avoids the memory explosion of storing full computational graphs across unrolled steps. However, the method is limited to linear forward operators, tested on only two datasets (FFHQ, ImageNet) at a single noise level, and the wavelet decorrelation assumption may not hold for all image statistics. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-linear operators, whether richer Hessian approximations (e.g., low-rank plus diagonal) would help, and whether the learned weights transfer across noise levels or require re-fine-tuning.
