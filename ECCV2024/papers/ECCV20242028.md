## Problem

Current diffusion-based text-to-image models generate single-layer, monolithic images, which limits their utility in professional graphic design and digital artistry where images are composed and manipulated across multiple layers for iterative refinement and object-specific control. Existing layered approaches like Text2Layer are restricted to two layers, and post-processing pipelines (segmentation + inpainting) introduce computational overhead and error accumulation. The paper addresses the gap of a unified, end-to-end diffusion framework for multi-layered (two or more) composable image synthesis with per-layer content control.

## Input / Output

The model takes a global text prompt describing the overall scene plus one layer-specific prompt per foreground element (and the fixed string 'the background' for the background layer), each encoded separately by a frozen CLIP text encoder. It simultaneously produces a background layer image, k foreground layer images, and k non-overlapping binary masks at 256×256 resolution; masks are generated in RGB space (single channel replicated to three) and binarized by argmax across layers. The layers are composited into a final image via I = m_b * B + Σ(m_i * F_i), enabling direct layer-wise editing without re-generating the full scene.

## Method

LayerDiff extends Stable Diffusion's latent UNet by stacking all layer latents (image + mask concatenated along channels) along a new layer dimension and replacing standard attention blocks with Layer-Collaborative Attention Blocks. Each block contains an inter-layer attention module that attends across the layer dimension to capture cross-layer spatial dependencies, and a text-guided intra-layer attention module that cross-attends each layer's features to its own layer-specific prompt for content-specific generation. A Layer-Specific Prompt Enhancer (self-attention for inter-layer distinctiveness + cross-attention with the global prompt as keys/values) enriches each layer prompt before injection. During sampling, a Self-Mask Guidance (SMG) strategy predicts the clean layer image at each DDIM step, applies Gaussian blur and re-noising within the predicted mask region, and amplifies the noise prediction difference to focus generation inside each layer's boundary. The model is initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5 weights and trained on the MLCID dataset (~2M multi-layered image-text pairs) with 50% probability of shared vs. independent timesteps across layers.

## Results

On the MLCID test set (27k samples), LayerDiff+SMG achieves a FID of 21.3 and CLIP-Score of 35.5 for two-layer synthesis, outperforming Stable Diffusion V1.5 (FID 27.0, CS 33.5). For three-layer synthesis, LayerDiff+SMG reaches FID 42.8 and CS 33.7, while Stable Diffusion achieves FID 25.9 and CS 33.9. For four-layer synthesis, the gap widens: LayerDiff+SMG scores FID 58.7 and CS 32.3 versus Stable Diffusion's FID 32.8 and CS 33.8. The authors attribute the three- and four-layer FID gap to limited training data (only ~15% and ~3% of MLCID, respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Stable Diffusion V1.5, LayerDiff+SMG wins on both FID (21.3 vs 27.0) and CLIP-Score (35.5 vs 33.5) for two-layer synthesis, demonstrating that the layered approach can match or exceed whole-image generation quality when the layer count is small. However, for three- and four-layer settings, LayerDiff+SMG trails Stable Diffusion on FID (42.8 vs 25.9 and 58.7 vs 32.8), though CLIP-Score remains competitive (33.7 vs 33.9 and 32.3 vs 33.8), suggesting the text-image alignment is preserved even as pixel-level fidelity degrades. The authors note this FID gap is likely due to the scarcity of three- and four-layer training samples in MLCID (~15% and ~3% of the dataset). Text2Layer is compared only qualitatively in Figures 5–6; no quantitative metrics are reported for it in the main results table, but the paper states LayerDiff's composite images are visually competitive with both Stable Diffusion and Text2Layer. The self-mask guidance (SMG) ablation shows a substantial FID improvement over vanilla LayerDiff across all layer counts (e.g., 35.1→21.3 for two layers), confirming its critical role in closing the gap with whole-image baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerDiff builds directly on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework (UNet architecture, CLIP text encoder, DDIM sampling) and extends it from single-image generation to multi-layer composable synthesis. The closest prior work, Text2Layer, performs two-layer generation (foreground + background) via a latent diffusion model, but LayerDiff generalizes to an arbitrary number of foreground layers and introduces per-layer text prompts for fine-grained content control. The layer-collaborative attention block (inter-layer attention for cross-layer coherence, text-guided intra-layer attention for per-layer content) and the self-mask guidance sampling strategy are novel architectural and algorithmic contributions not present in prior layered or whole-image diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LayerDiff opens a practical path toward layer-wise controllability in text-to-image generation, enabling applications like per-layer inpainting and style transfer without fine-tuning—capabilities directly relevant to professional graphic design and digital art workflows. However, the performance gap widens substantially beyond two layers (FID degrades from ~21 to ~59 for four layers with SMG), and the authors attribute this to insufficient training data for 3- and 4-layer compositions, leaving the scalability of the approach to more complex scenes an open question. The data construction pipeline, while functional, is acknowledged as inefficient, suggesting that a more scalable annotation strategy would be needed to close the quality gap and make the method broadly competitive.
