## Problem

Existing MLLMs can recognize individual objects or ground them to regions, but they lack the ability to comprehend relations between objects, leading to hallucinations on relation questions and over-reliance on language priors. This gap stems from the absence of both an appropriate modeling formulation that explicitly requires predicate grounding and high-quality training data containing relation annotations in a conversational format.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240888/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image plus a text prompt that may include bounding boxes as region pointers (normalized to integers in [0, 1000)). The response is a natural-language sentence in which every mentioned object is wrapped in <ref></ref> tags followed by its bounding box, and every predicate is wrapped in <pred></pred> tags followed by two bounding boxes identifying its subject and object. This structured output can be directly parsed into a scene graph (objects as nodes, predicates as directed edges), and the same model also produces standard captions, VQA answers, and multiple-choice selections without the grounding markup.

## Method

The core contribution is the Relation Conversation (ReC) task, which unifies text generation, object localization, and relation comprehension into a single autoregressive generation objective: the model must simultaneously produce a coherent sentence and ground every object and every predicate's subject/object to image regions. ASMv2 follows the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP-ViT-L-336px vision encoder, linear projector, Vicuna-13B language model) and is trained in two stages—first for image-level understanding (595K CC3M pre-training + 665K LLaVA-1.5 instruction tuning), then for region-level understanding (30M pre-training samples from CC12M, AS-1B, and GRiT + 4M instruction-tuning samples including the newly constructed AS-V2 dataset of 127K relation-conversation samples). AS-V2 is generated by prompting GPT-4V with COCO caption, location, and relation annotations plus manually crafted seed examples, producing three data types (detailed descriptions, region captioning, and multi-turn conversations) that include negative instructions for robustness. Because the ReC response format maps directly to scene-graph triplets, the model naturally supports open-ended scene graph generation, predicate classification, and all standard MLLM tasks without architectural modification.

## Results

On the proposed CRPE benchmark, ASMv2 achieves an overall relation comprehension accuracy of 64.50, surpassing LLaVA-1.5 (55.63) by 8.87 points and Qwen-VL (38.48) by 26.02 points. On general multimodal benchmarks, ASMv2 scores 74.4 on MMBench and 1621.0 on MME, outperforming LLaVA-1.5 by 6.7 and 89.7 points respectively. On region-level tasks, ASMv2 achieves 87.42 average accuracy on RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg (outperforming Qwen-VL by 1.69) and 114.7 CIDEr on RefCOCOg region captioning. On Open-ended Scene Graph Generation (PSG), ASMv2 achieves 14.2 recall with only 9.2 average tuples, substantially outperforming TextPSG (5.5 recall with 100 tuples).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA-1.5-13B, ASMv2 wins on MMBench (74.4 vs 67.7, +6.7), MME (1621.0 vs 1531.3, +89.7), CRPE overall (64.50 vs 55.63, +8.87), and RefCOCO average (87.42 vs not directly listed but surpassing Qwen-VL's 85.73). Against VILA-13B, ASMv2 leads on MMBench (+4.1), MME (+50.9), and CRPE (+8.87), though VILA retains an edge on TextVQA (66.6 vs 60.2) and VizWiz (60.6 vs 58.1). On the CRPE benchmark specifically, ASMv2 dominates all four splits: Existence (92.14 vs 88.69), Subject (69.21 vs 57.44), Predicate (58.95 vs 54.24), and Object (65.34 vs 55.21) over LLaVA-1.5. On Open-ended Scene Graph Generation, ASMv2 achieves 14.2 recall with only 9.2 tuples versus TextPSG's 5.5 recall with 100 tuples, and outperforms close-set SGG models (PSGFormer: 18.6 recall at 20 tuples) while generating far fewer predictions. On Referring Expression Comprehension, ASMv2 (87.42 avg) surpasses Qwen-VL (85.73), Ferret (85.57), and Shikra-13B (84.21). On Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR), ASMv2 in multi-task setting (87.8 Q→A) is competitive with single-task-finetuned GPT4RoI (87.4), and after single-task finetuning ASMv2 edges ahead (88.4 vs 87.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the All-Seeing Project V1 (ASM) and the LLaVA-1.5 architecture by introducing a new task formulation—Relation Conversation (ReC)—that unifies text generation, object grounding, and predicate grounding into a single conversational output. It departs from traditional Scene Graph Generation, which relies on closed-set label vocabularies and specialized architectures, by embedding scene graph construction within an open-ended, autoregressive MLLM framework where predicates are explicitly grounded to their subject-object bounding boxes. The work also builds on the broader line of Grounded MLLMs (Kosmos-2, Shikra, Qwen-VL) that introduced bounding-box outputs, but identifies and addresses their critical blind spot: the inability to model inter-object relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CRPE benchmark and AS-V2 dataset fill a genuine gap: prior evaluation of relation comprehension in MLLMs was either absent or limited to binary classification (e.g., VSR), and no high-quality training data existed for predicate-level grounding. The paper's finding that even strong MLLMs show a 20–30 point drop from object recognition (Existence) to relation comprehension (Subject/Predicate/Object) quantifies a weakness the field has informally acknowledged but lacked tools to measure. Open questions include whether the exact-match evaluation for open-ended SGG unfairly penalizes semantically equivalent triplets, whether the approach generalizes to long-range or multi-hop relations, and whether the relatively small abnormal-data subset in CRPE is sufficient to probe language-prior dependence.
