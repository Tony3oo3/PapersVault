## Problem

Existing human trajectory prediction methods apply a single, uniform training paradigm to the entire future trajectory, treating short-term and long-term prediction identically. This neglects the fundamental distinction between local dynamic patterns (short-term) and global movement tendencies (long-term), forcing the model into a suboptimal compromise between the two temporal regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240791/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes the past 8 timesteps (3.2 s) of 2D (x, y) positions for each agent, augmented with temporal position embeddings, and produces K=20 candidate future trajectories spanning 12 timesteps (4.8 s). For the unobserved future positions, the model appends learnable prompt embeddings in place of ground-truth coordinates, enabling non-autoregressive parallel generation of all intermediate waypoints in a single forward pass rather than step-by-step decoding.

## Method

PPT trains a 3-layer Transformer encoder (dim 128, 8 heads) through three progressive stages: Task-I pretrains on stepwise next-position prediction over randomly sampled subsequences to capture short-term dynamics; Task-II continues training on leapfrog destination prediction (K destinations with precision and diversity losses) to capture long-term dependencies; Task-III replicates the model into a destination predictor and a trajectory predictor, jointly fine-tuning them for full trajectory regression. A cross-task knowledge distillation loss penalizes feature divergence between the frozen Task-I/Task-II models and their Task-III counterparts to prevent catastrophic forgetting. At inference, the destination predictor generates K candidate endpoints, and the trajectory predictor—conditioned on the closest destination and a series of learnable prompt embeddings for each future timestep—produces all intermediate waypoints in one parallel step, yielding a two-step non-autoregressive pipeline.

## Results

On the SDD dataset, PPT achieves minADE₂₀ of 7.03 px and minFDE₂₀ of 10.65 px, outperforming the previous best (MID: 7.61/14.30) by 0.58 ADE and 3.65 FDE. On ETH/UCY, PPT attains average minADE₂₀ of 0.20 m and minFDE₂₀ of 0.31 m, surpassing the prior best (SocialVAE/LED: 0.21/0.33). On GCS, PPT reaches 6.20 px ADE and 9.34 px FDE, improving over EigenTrajectory (7.42/12.49) by 16.4% and 25.2% respectively. Inference speed is 5.28 ms per trajectory, comparable to the one-step TUTR model (4.06 ms) while significantly outperforming all autoregressive Transformer baselines in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPT outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all three datasets. On SDD, the closest competitors are MID (7.61 ADE) and LED (11.66 FDE); PPT reduces ADE by 0.58 and FDE by 1.01 over the best single metric from each. On ETH/UCY, PPT edges out SocialVAE and LED (both 0.21/0.33) by 0.01 ADE and 0.02 FDE on average, with the largest per-scene gain on ZARA2 (0.12/0.21 vs. 0.13/0.22). On GCS, the margin is most dramatic: PPT beats EigenTrajectory (7.42/12.49) by 1.22 ADE and 3.15 FDE, and NPSN (7.66/13.41) by 1.46 ADE and 4.07 FDE. Among Transformer-based methods specifically, PPT surpasses TUTR (7.76/12.69 on SDD), AgentFormer (10.18/16.91 on GCS), and STAR (0.26/0.53 avg on ETH/UCY) while matching or exceeding their inference speed. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that removing both pretext tasks degrades SDD performance to 10.40/18.64, validating that the progressive learning strategy is the primary source of the gains over direct end-to-end training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the progressive pretraining paradigm—well established in image generation (Progressive GAN), image enhancement, and object detection—into human trajectory prediction for the first time. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two existing threads: destination-conditioned trajectory prediction (PECNet, MemoNet) and non-autoregressive Transformer predictors (TUTR, MID), but departs from both by introducing two structured pretext tasks (stepwise next-position and leapfrog destination prediction) that incrementally build short-term and long-term understanding before the final trajectory task. The cross-task knowledge distillation mechanism, borrowed from progressive learning in other domains, is repurposed here to prevent catastrophic forgetting across the three training stages, and the learnable prompt embeddings for parallel waypoint generation extend TUTR's non-autoregressive idea with explicit temporal position signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive pretext task framework offers a principled alternative to the dominant single-stage training paradigm in trajectory prediction, and the two-step destination-driven inference (5.28 ms) provides a practical efficiency gain over autoregressive Transformers without sacrificing accuracy. However, the paper is limited to single-agent, scene-free prediction; it does not model multi-agent interactions or environmental context, which are central to real-world deployment in autonomous driving or robotics. Open questions include whether the progressive paradigm transfers to multi-agent or scene-aware settings, and whether the gains over strong baselines (modest: ~8% ADE on SDD, ~5% on ETH/UCY) persist under more challenging evaluation protocols or larger model scales.
