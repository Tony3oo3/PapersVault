## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video editing methods rely on ancestral sampling from Gaussian noise or inverted latents, which fails to reproduce complex, real-world motion even when augmented with structural cues or fine-tuned temporal priors. Publicly available text-to-video models lack sufficiently rich temporal priors to accurately depict natural motion in generated videos, making zero-shot editing that preserves both structure and motion an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240800/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input video (8–16 frames), a target text prompt describing the desired appearance change, the original reference text prompt, and per-frame binary masks (from off-the-shelf detectors) delineating regions to edit. It produces an edited video of the same length and resolution. In the cascaded setting (Show-1), optimization operates only on low-resolution keyframes at 80×128 pixels before temporal interpolation and spatial super-resolution are applied; in the non-cascaded setting (ZeroScope), the full latent-space video is optimized directly.

## Method

DreamMotion bypasses the standard reverse diffusion process entirely, instead initializing the target video from the input and iteratively modifying its appearance via Video Delta Denoising Score (V-DDS) gradients from a pre-trained T2V diffusion model, with gradients masked to editing regions to prevent blurriness and over-saturation. To counteract the structural and motion drift that accumulates during V-DDS optimization, the method introduces two self-similarity regularizations computed from attention key features of the diffusion U-Net: Spatial Self-Similarity Matching (S-SSM) aligns per-frame spatial self-similarity maps between the original and edited videos to preserve object layout, while Temporal Self-Similarity Matching (T-SSM) aligns frame-axis self-similarity of spatially-averaged key features to suppress flickering and localized distortions. All three losses (V-DDS, S-SSM, T-SSM) share the same noise sample and timestep, enabling a single forward–backward pass per optimization step. The framework is model-agnostic, applying identically to both non-cascaded latent diffusion (ZeroScope) and cascaded pixel-space pipelines (Show-1), where optimization is confined to the keyframe generation stage.

## Results

On the non-cascaded ZeroScope setup (26 text-video pairs from DAVIS and WebVid), DreamMotion achieves the best score across all seven evaluated metrics: Text-Align 0.8209, Frame-Consistency 0.9726, Motion-Fidelity 0.9259, Frame-LPIPS 0.3042 (lower is better), and human ratings of 4.14 (Edit Accuracy), 4.21 (Frame Consistency), and 4.33 (Structure & Motion Preservation) on a 5-point scale. In the cascaded Show-1 setup, DreamMotion similarly leads on all five metrics, with a particularly large margin in Structure & Motion Preservation (4.30 vs. 3.35 for VMC and 2.21 for Inversion+Word Swap). Optimization takes approximately 2 minutes for 8-frame and 4 minutes for 16-frame videos on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamMotion outperforms all five non-cascaded baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor TokenFlow [8], it gains +0.0396 in Text-Align (0.8209 vs. 0.7813), +0.015 in Motion-Fidelity (0.9259 vs. 0.9184), and a substantial −0.0385 improvement in Frame-LPIPS (0.3042 vs. 0.3427), while also leading in human-rated Edit Accuracy (4.14 vs. 3.63) and Structure & Motion Preservation (4.33 vs. 3.92). Gen-1 [7] achieves the second-best Text-Align (0.8192) and Frame-Consistency (0.9704) but lacks motion-fidelity and LPIPS scores, and trails DreamMotion in all human evaluations. Tune-A-Video [54] and Control-A-Video [5] show the weakest motion preservation (0.6947 and 0.8453 respectively), confirming that attention-inflation and depth-guided approaches struggle with real-world motion. In the cascaded Show-1 setting, DreamMotion beats VMC [17] by +0.0184 in Text-Align and +0.95 in SM-Preserve (4.30 vs. 3.35), and substantially outperforms the Inversion+Word Swap baseline across all metrics, particularly in SM-Preserve (4.30 vs. 2.21).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamMotion extends the score distillation lineage (SDS from DreamFusion, DDS from Hertz et al.) from 3D/image generation into the video editing domain, departing fundamentally from the ancestral-sampling paradigm used by prior video editing methods (Tune-A-Video, ControlVideo, Control-A-Video, TokenFlow). Rather than denoising from Gaussian noise or inverted latents, it optimizes directly from the input video using video delta denoising score gradients, and introduces a novel structural regularizer based on self-similarity of diffusion U-Net key features—a concept borrowed from visual pattern matching and DINO-based editing but applied here for the first time to diffusion features in a video context. The approach is explicitly model-agnostic, plugging into both non-cascaded (ZeroScope) and cascaded (Show-1) video diffusion pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a training-free, model-agnostic recipe for zero-shot video editing that preserves motion without requiring depth maps, fine-tuning, or feature-uniformity constraints, which broadens the set of usable video diffusion backbones. The self-similarity regularization using diffusion features is a reusable technique that could transfer to other score-distillation-based editing or generation tasks. A clear limitation is that the method cannot handle edits requiring substantial structural changes (e.g., adding or removing large objects), since the self-similarity constraint enforces layout preservation. Open questions include scalability to longer video sequences, handling of more complex multi-object edits, and whether the approach generalizes to video-to-video translation beyond text-driven appearance changes.
