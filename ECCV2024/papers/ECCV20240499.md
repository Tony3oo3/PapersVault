## Problem

Existing image restoration methods are designed for isolated degradation types (low light, haze, rain, snow), yet real-world images frequently suffer from composite degradations where multiple factors coexist. One-to-One models cannot generalize across combinations, and One-to-Many models either scale in parameter count with degradation diversity or fail to handle simultaneous multi-factor corruption. The paper addresses the absence of a single, controllable framework that can adaptively restore images under arbitrary combinations of degradations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a degraded RGB image (1080×720 in the CDD-11 dataset; 256×256 patches during training) plus a scene descriptor that specifies the degradation type. The descriptor is either a manual text embedding (one of 12 predefined scene descriptions: 1 clear, 4 single-degradation, 7 composite) or an automatic visual embedding extracted via a ResNet-18 backbone and matched to the nearest text embedding via cosine similarity. The output is a single restored clear image at the same resolution as the input.

## Method

OneRestore is an encoder-decoder network whose basic unit is a Scene Descriptor-guided Transformer Block (SDTB) containing three components: a Scene Descriptor-guided Cross-Attention (SDCA) module, a Self-Attention module, and a Feed-Forward Network. The key architectural insight is that SDCA reverses the standard cross-attention role—the scene descriptor embedding generates the Query matrix while image features generate the Key and Value matrices—so the degradation description directly steers which features are attended to. A composite degradation imaging model (low-light → rain/snow → haze) is used to synthesize the CDD-11 dataset with 11 degradation categories. Training employs a Composite Degradation Restoration Loss (CDRL) that, in addition to the standard input-as-negative contrastive term, introduces 10 additional degraded images as extra negatives in a VGG-16 feature-space ratio loss, forcing the restored output to approach the clear image while maintaining separation from all degradation variants. The full model has only 5.98M parameters.

## Results

On the CDD-11 composite degradation dataset, OneRestore with text embedding (OneRestore†) achieves 28.72 PSNR and 0.8821 SSIM, while the visual-embedding variant reaches 28.47 PSNR and 0.8784 SSIM, both with only 5.98M parameters. These results surpass the best One-to-One method (SRUDC: 27.64 PSNR, 0.8600 SSIM) by over 1 dB in PSNR and the best One-to-Many method (WGWSNet: 26.96 PSNR, 0.8626 SSIM) by nearly 1.8 dB. The model also outperforms all baselines across individual degradation scenarios (low-light, haze, rain, snow, and their composites) as shown in radar-chart comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneRestore† (28.72 PSNR / 0.8821 SSIM) outperforms all 14 baselines on CDD-11. Against the strongest One-to-One methods, it leads SRUDC [40] by +1.08 dB PSNR and +0.022 SSIM, and Restormer [52] by +1.73 dB PSNR and +0.018 SSIM, while using fewer parameters (5.98M vs. 6.80M and 26.13M respectively). Among One-to-Many methods, the gap is even larger: OneRestore† surpasses WGWSNet [62] (the best One-to-Many) by +1.76 dB PSNR and +0.020 SSIM, PromptIR [37] by +2.82 dB, and AirNet [26] by +4.97 dB. The diffusion-based WeatherDiff [35] and TransWeather [42] trail by over 5 dB in PSNR, and WeatherDiff also has the largest parameter count (82.96M) among all compared methods. Notably, OneRestore achieves these gains with a parameter budget comparable to the lightest One-to-One models (MIRNetv2: 5.86M, OKNet: 4.72M), demonstrating that the composite-degradation formulation and scene-descriptor-guided cross-attention yield substantial quality improvements without architectural bloat.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoder-decoder transformer paradigm established by Restormer [52] by replacing standard self-attention with a Scene Descriptor-guided Cross-Attention (SDCA) module that injects degradation-specific conditioning into every transformer block. Conceptually, it departs from both One-to-One methods (single degradation, single model) and One-to-Many methods (multiple individual degradations, shared or partial weights) by introducing a 'One-to-Composite' formulation where arbitrary combinations of low-light, haze, rain, and snow co-occur in a single image. The visual attribute control mechanism draws on Saini et al. [39] for embedding-based scene classification, while the composite degradation restoration loss generalizes contrastive regularization [47,57] by treating multiple degraded variants as simultaneous negative samples rather than a single input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CDD-11 dataset and the One-to-Composite formulation address a gap that prior One-to-Many methods explicitly cannot fill: real-world scenes rarely exhibit a single isolated degradation. The controllability via scene descriptors (manual text or automatic visual extraction) offers a practical interface for downstream applications like autonomous driving where the operator or system knows which impairments are present. However, the framework is limited to four degradation types and their combinations, and the authors acknowledge failure on extremely high-density or unconsidered corruptions. Open questions include whether the scene descriptor mechanism scales gracefully to additional degradation modalities (e.g., fog, motion blur, sensor noise) and whether the CDRL loss generalizes beyond the VGG-16 feature space used here.
