## Problem

Pre-training for image recognition is conventionally performed on large-scale real-world datasets (e.g., 1M+ ImageNet images), and is typically interpreted as discovering universal visual structures. This paper challenges that interpretation by asking whether a minimal purely synthetic dataset—potentially a single image—can achieve comparable downstream performance, and if so, what the minimal requirements for successful pre-training actually are.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240386/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single fractal image rendered from an iterated function system (IFS) and a perturbation degree Δ that defines a hypercube R_Δ = [−Δ/2, Δ/2]^{6j} over the 6 affine parameters of each IFS map. Perturbations ε are sampled uniformly from this hypercube (L=1,000 samples by default), producing perturbed images whose 'labels' are the perturbation vectors themselves rather than semantic categories. The output is a pre-trained ViT (Tiny or Base) whose weights encode visual representations transferable to downstream classification via standard fine-tuning.

## Method

The core contribution is the 1p-frac dataset combined with the Locally Perturbed Cross Entropy (LPCE) loss defined over the Locally Integrated Empirical (LIEP) distribution p_Δ. The LIEP distribution places probability mass over perturbed versions of a single fractal image, with support controlled by Δ; as Δ→0 it converges to a Dirac delta at the original image, enabling systematic analysis of minimal pre-training support. The LPCE loss treats the perturbation parameters as classification targets, forcing the network to learn fine-grained geometric distinctions that are imperceptible to humans. The σ-factor of the IFS controls fractal complexity, and experiments reveal that Δ≈0.1 and σ≈3.5 (moderately complex shapes) yield optimal pre-training, while Δ=0.001 causes complete collapse. For real images, the analogous recipe is Canny edge extraction followed by affine transformation perturbations, confirming that structured geometric regularity—not semantic content—is the essential ingredient.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-100 fine-tuning (ViT-Tiny), 1p-frac (1 fractal image) achieves 84.2% and 89.0% top-1 accuracy, surpassing FractalDB (1M images: 81.6%/88.5%) and OFDB (1k images: 84.0%/88.6%). On ImageNet-1k fine-tuning (ViT-Base), 1p-frac reaches 82.1%, outperforming ImageNet-21k supervised pre-training (14M images: 81.8%) and matching FractalDB-21k (21M: 81.8%). Across seven diverse fine-tuning datasets (Table 13), 1p-frac achieves a mean accuracy of 87.5% (88.2% with random patch augmentation), approaching MAE self-supervised pre-training on 1.28M ImageNet images (88.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

1p-frac (1 image) outperforms FractalDB (1M images) by +2.6% on CIFAR-100 (84.2 vs 81.6) and +0.5% on ImageNet-100 (89.0 vs 88.5), and beats OFDB (1k images) by +0.2% and +0.4% respectively. On ImageNet-1k fine-tuning with ViT-Base, 1p-frac (82.1%) surpasses ImageNet-21k supervised pre-training (81.8%, 14M images) and matches FractalDB-21k (81.8%, 21M images). Against MAE self-supervised pre-training on 1.28M ImageNet images, 1p-frac with augmentation achieves a near-tie in mean accuracy (88.2% vs 88.5%) across seven datasets, while without augmentation it trails by 1.0%. Compared to 1-image SSL (DINO/MAE) [3], 1p-frac improves mean accuracy by +0.9% to +4.7% (87.5% vs 86.6%/82.8%). On VTAB specialized/structured datasets, 1p-frac (1 image) matches or exceeds MAE on 3 of 5 benchmarks (Retinopathy 77.9 vs 76.7, Resisc45 95.8 vs 95.6, sNORB-Azim 28.9 vs 26.3) while trailing on Camelyon (82.7 vs 83.9) and CLEVR-Count (86.2 vs 89.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the OFDB line of work (Nakamura et al., ICCV 2023), which pre-trained ViTs with 1,000 synthetic fractal images generated via iterated function systems (IFS), by reducing the dataset to a single fractal. It builds on the broader FDSL (Formula-Driven Supervised Learning) framework introduced by FractalDB (Kataoka et al., IJCV 2022) and its successors (RCDB, Visual Atoms), but departs from the prevailing trend of scaling synthetic datasets toward millions of images. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of the LPCE loss and LIEP distribution, which reframe pre-training as classification of small perturbations around a single image rather than learning across diverse categories, enabling a 'scaling backwards' investigation of minimal pre-training requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single fractal with perturbations matches or exceeds 1M-image pre-training on multiple downstream benchmarks challenges the widely held assumption that pre-training discovers universal visual structures from large diverse datasets, suggesting it may function more as a structured weight initialization. This has practical implications: it eliminates licensing and ethical concerns of large real-image datasets and reduces data generation to seconds. However, the real-image extension (Canny + affine) remains preliminary, and the paper acknowledges the need for a similarly minimal real-image pre-training set for interpretability and calibration purposes. Open questions include whether the result generalizes beyond IFS-generated fractals to other structured synthetic patterns, and whether the 'scaling backwards' effect persists at much larger model scales.
