## Problem

Existing pose estimation benchmarks such as NOCS-REAL275 are too small (6 categories, 18 videos) to expose the true limitations of SOTA methods, which achieve near-perfect scores on these datasets yet fail catastrophically when transferred to unseen real-world data. PACE addresses this gap by providing a large-scale benchmark with 238 objects across 43 categories in cluttered, occluded scenes that reflect real-world complexity, including moving and articulated objects absent from prior datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241372/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides RGB-D video sequences at 1280×720 resolution captured at 0.5–1.5 m distance, with per-frame 6D pose annotations (rotation + translation) for both rigid and articulated objects across 55K frames and 258K annotations. For instance-level evaluation, methods consume RGB-D images, 3D CAD models, and ground-truth object masks; for category-level, they additionally predict 3D bounding box dimensions. The companion PACE-Sim synthetic set (100K frames, 2.4M annotations, 931 objects) is rendered with BlenderProc2 to support supervised training under both instance-level and category-level protocols.

## Method

The core technical contribution is a scalable annotation pipeline built around a calibrated 3-camera system (three Intel RealSense D415 cameras on a metal frame) that triples data yield and enforces cross-view consistency. Multi-camera extrinsics are calibrated via trifocal tensor estimation with SuperPoint+SuperGlue feature matching, resolving the inherent scale ambiguity by least-squares fitting against ArUco marker measurements. Still objects are annotated through ArUco-marker-based PnP tracking, with markers subsequently removed via Poisson image inpainting using reference frames captured before marker placement. Moving objects are annotated using BundleTrack's feature-correspondence tracking with manual pose correction every 10 frames, verified through 3D point-cloud alignment and 2D multi-view agreement. Occlusion-aware segmentation masks are generated by depth rendering, with SAM used to subtract hand regions where human interaction occurs.

## Results

On the PACE benchmark, SOTA deep learning pose estimation methods perform dramatically worse than on prior benchmarks: on instance-level estimation, the classical PPF method achieves AR=52.2%, while the best deep learning method (SurfEmb) reaches only AR=9.0%. On category-level estimation, the best method (CPPF++) achieves AP@0:60° of 74.0% (rigid) and 66.7% (articulated), but all methods remain far below the near-perfect scores (99.8%) reported on NOCS-REAL275. For pose tracking, the best model-based tracker (ICG) reaches only 38.1% ADD(-S) AUC on rigid objects and 10.1% on articulated objects, while the best model-free tracker (CAPTRA) achieves just 12.9% 5°5cm accuracy on rigid objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On instance-level pose estimation, the non-learning PPF method (AR=52.2%) dramatically outperforms all deep learning baselines: SurfEmb (AR=9.0%), GDRNPP (AR=7.0%), and CosyPose (AR=4.4%), suggesting that current learned methods fail to generalize to the scale and diversity of PACE. On category-level estimation, CPPF++ leads with AP@0:60° of 74.0% (rigid) and 66.7% (articulated), outperforming HS-Pose (81.0%/75.6% on translation-only AP@0:15cm but only 8.6%/0.3% on rotation AP@0:20°) and SAR-Net (79.8%/69.7% on AP@0:15cm), indicating CPPF++ is more balanced across rotation and translation. ANCSH, designed specifically for articulated objects, underperforms severely (AP@0:60° = 50.4% articulated), revealing a large sim-to-real gap. For model-based tracking, ICG (ADD(-S) AUC = 38.1% rigid / 10.1% articulated) substantially outperforms RBOT (7.4%/0.5%), while for model-free tracking, CAPTRA (5°5cm = 12.9% rigid) edges out 6-PACK (9.2%) and BundleTrack (6.4%), though all remain below 13% accuracy. The ablation study further shows that even under single-instance real-to-real fitting, depth-based methods like HS-Pose (81.2%→1.1%) and SGPA (86.7%→0.3%) collapse under sim-to-real transfer, while PPF-like geometric methods retain more robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in pose estimation benchmarking by unifying multiple challenging dimensions—articulated objects, moving objects, piled objects, and 43 diverse categories—into a single large-scale real-world dataset, whereas prior benchmarks (YCB-Video, NOCS-REAL275, LINEMOD) each addressed only a subset of these challenges. The annotation pipeline repurposes multi-view geometry (trifocal tensor calibration with SuperPoint/SuperGlue) and marker-based PnP tracking in a novel 3-camera setup, combined with Poisson inpainting to remove calibration artifacts, creating a scalable annotation framework not seen in prior pose datasets. For evaluation, it adopts the BOP challenge metric suite for instance-level tasks and NOCS-style AP metrics for category-level tasks, positioning itself as a more rigorous successor to both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical gap: methods achieving near-perfect scores (99.8%) on NOCS-REAL275 collapse to under 1% on PACE, revealing that the field's progress has been substantially overestimated by existing evaluation protocols. The ablation study further isolates two distinct failure modes—sim-to-real degradation (especially for depth-dependent methods like HS-Pose dropping from 81% to 1%) and scalability collapse (single-instance fitting at 88% AR vs. all-instances at 8% AR)—pointing to concrete research directions. Open questions include whether larger foundation models or improved sim-to-real transfer can close these gaps, and how to handle articulated objects in cluttered, dynamic scenes. The dataset's acknowledged limitation (absence of large objects like tables and laptops) leaves room for future expansion.
