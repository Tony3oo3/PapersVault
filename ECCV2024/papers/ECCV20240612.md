## Problem

Traditional data augmentations (flips, crops, colour shifts) only apply simple pixel-level transformations that marginally expand a dataset's visual diversity without introducing new information. Generating whole synthetic images with diffusion models avoids this limitation but requires producing accompanying annotations, which is challenging and error-prone. The paper asks whether one can leverage the rich priors of large pre-trained diffusion models to repaint individual object instances in-place, thereby preserving original annotations while substantially increasing visual variety.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image with per-object binary segmentation masks and class labels (obtainable via off-the-shelf instance segmentation if not provided). It computes a relative depth map (DepthAnything) and an edge map (HED) at the image level, and constructs text prompts from class names enriched with WordNet synsets, random colour/lighting descriptors, and for people, BLIP-VQA action predictions. The output is a new image where each object has been independently redrawn while retaining its original spatial extent; original annotations remain valid. Objects smaller than 32×32 px are cropped and upsampled to 512 px to match the generator's minimum resolution, and each object can be redrawn up to three times, yielding up to 2^N real/generated combinations per image.

## Method

The pipeline uses a pre-trained conditional latent diffusion model (LDM) in inpainting mode, augmented with ControlNet to inject depth and edge map conditioning into the decoder, ensuring generated objects adhere to the original shape and scene geometry. Objects are sorted back-to-front by their mean depth to handle occlusions, and each is inpainted independently from the original image (rather than sequentially) to avoid the noise accumulation that degrades quality under repeated latent-space encode/decode cycles; results are composited via depth-sorted alpha-blending. Prompt engineering extends class labels with WordNet synset lemmas for stability, randomly sampled colour and lighting qualifiers for diversity, and BLIP-VQA-predicted actions for people. A mask refinement stage using TRACER-7 re-segments each generated object crop to eliminate boundary mismatches with the original annotations. No fine-tuning on the target dataset is required; the method directly employs off-the-shelf pre-trained models, and an NSFW safety filter rejects and regenerates any unsafe outputs.

## Results

On MS-COCO object detection, instance-level augmentation consistently improves all three tested detectors: Deformable-DETR gains +1.2 AP (39.3→40.5), RT-DETR gains +1.0 AP (51.4→52.4), and YOLOv5m gains +1.6 AP (44.1→45.7). On Pascal VOC semantic segmentation, the method achieves 61.5 mIoU (with finetuning on original data) versus 27.8 mIoU for Diffumask, a +15.5 mIoU improvement. On salient object detection across four benchmarks (ECSSD, DUTS-TE, DUT-OMRON, HKU-IS) and three architectures (U2Net, F3-Net, TRACER-4), the method improves 34 out of 36 metrics, with up to 8% error reduction. For data anonymization on COCO, replacing all people and cars with synthetic versions costs only 1 AP overall while reducing face re-identification to 0.14%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior synthetic-data-generation methods. Against Diffumask [52] on Pascal VOC semantic segmentation, the proposed method achieves 43.5 mIoU (synthetic-only training) versus Diffumask's 27.8 mIoU, and 61.5 mIoU after finetuning on a subset of original data, a margin of +15.7 mIoU over the best Diffumask result. The largest per-class gap is on 'chair' (42.4 vs 14.7 mIoU), where Diffumask's full-image generation struggles with complex multi-object scenes. Against Wang et al. [53] on salient object detection, the proposed synthetic data outperforms their diffusion+BigGAN pipeline across all four benchmarks: e.g., F3-Net reaches 0.959 F_max on ECSSD and 0.903 on DUTS-TE with the proposed data, versus 0.938 and 0.856 with [53]'s data. The advantage is most pronounced on DUT-OMRON (0.841 vs 0.793 F_max), suggesting the instance-level approach better preserves scene structure than whole-image generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent diffusion models (LDM) for image inpainting and ControlNet for spatial conditioning (depth, edges), but departs from prior synthetic-data-generation work (Diffumask, DatasetDM) in a key way: rather than generating entire images and their annotations from scratch (which requires dataset-specific finetuning), it repaints individual object instances in-place, preserving original labels and enabling seamless mixing of real and synthetic content within a single image. The iterative, depth-sorted redrawing with alpha-blending and the 'always start from the original image' formulation to avoid latent-space noise accumulation are practical engineering contributions not present in prior diffusion-based augmentation pipelines. The method also repurposes off-the-shelf depth estimation (DepthAnything), edge detection (HED), and VQA (BLIP-VQA) as conditioning signals without any task-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released augmented and anonymized versions of COCO, Pascal VOC, and DUTS provide immediately usable resources for the community, and the finding that replacing 30.5% of all instances (people) with synthetic ones costs only ~1 AP makes retrospective privacy remediation of scraped datasets practically viable. The 0.14% face re-identification rate (vs. 2.8% for LDFA) demonstrates that full-instance replacement is a stronger privacy guarantee than face-only anonymization. Open questions include scalability to video, 3D, or very large scenes where depth/edge estimation errors compound, and whether the approach transfers to tasks requiring temporal consistency or fine-grained geometric fidelity beyond what 2D inpainting can provide.
