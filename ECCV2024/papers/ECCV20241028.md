## Problem

Domain generalization for semantic segmentation in autonomous driving has focused on model architectures and training strategies, while the potential of pretrained latent diffusion models as large-scale data generators remains underexplored. Two fundamental obstacles prevent off-the-shelf LDMs from serving this role: fine-tuning for semantic mask alignment causes the model to overfit to the source domain's visual style, and the low-resolution latent space fails to generate small objects faithfully aligned with masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241028/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes one-hot encoded semantic segmentation masks (cropped to 512×512) and text prompts combining class names with randomized style qualifiers (e.g., 'foggy', 'night') as conditioning signals. It produces 1024×1024 generated street scene images (via 2× upscaling in latent space) with pixel-aligned segmentation masks inherited from the source domain. The generated image-mask pairs are combined with the original source dataset (6000 generated + 6000 source images) to train downstream segmentation networks.

## Method

DGInStyle builds on Stable Diffusion 1.5 with three key innovations. First, Style Swap decouples semantic control from style: the LDM U-Net is fine-tuned via DreamBooth on the source domain to absorb its style (yielding U^S), ControlNet is then trained on top of U^S to learn mask-to-image layout, and at inference the base generator is swapped back to the original prior-domain U-Net, preserving diverse style while retaining learned semantic control. Second, Multi-Resolution Latent Fusion (MRLF) performs a two-stage generation: a low-resolution ControlNet pass provides a reference, followed by a high-resolution pass that tiles the upsampled latent canvas (Controlled Tiled MultiDiffusion) for small-object fidelity and blends large objects from the first pass via Latent Inpainting Diffusion to avoid artifacts. Third, Rare Class Generation adjusts sampling probabilities during both ControlNet training and dataset generation to counteract class imbalance. The resulting pipeline generates 6000 diverse, style-varied image-mask pairs that, when added to the GTA source domain, boost mIoU by 2.5–7.2 points across five real-world driving benchmarks.

## Results

Using MiT-B5 backbone, HRDA + DGInStyle achieves the best overall performance of 48.99% average mIoU across five autonomous driving datasets (Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary Vistas, ACDC, Dark Zurich), improving over HRDA alone (46.54%) by +2.5 mIoU. DAFormer + DGInStyle reaches 46.47% Avg5 mIoU (+4.3 over DAFormer alone). With ResNet-101, the gains are even larger: HRDA + DGInStyle jumps from 30.88% to 38.05% Avg5 (+7.2 mIoU). Improvements are consistent across all tested DG methods, ranging from +2.5 to +7.2 mIoU on the 5-dataset average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGInStyle is a data generation pipeline that augments existing DG methods rather than replacing them, so all comparisons are framed as 'method ± DGInStyle.' With MiT-B5, HRDA + DGInStyle (48.99% Avg5) outperforms DAFormer + DGInStyle (46.47%) and Color-Aug + DGInStyle (42.03%), confirming that the strongest base architecture benefits most. With ResNet-101, HRDA + DGInStyle (38.05%) surpasses SHADE (34.58%), FSDR (32.77%), and RobustNet + DGInStyle (34.11%) by 3.5–6.1 mIoU. The largest absolute gain comes from HRDA on ResNet-101 (+7.2 mIoU), suggesting that weaker backbones benefit most from the diverse generated data. On the more challenging adverse-weather datasets (ACDC, Dark Zurich), gains are particularly pronounced: DAFormer improves from 38.25→44.04 on ACDC and 17.45→25.58 on Dark Zurich. When Cityscapes is used as source (Table 2), improvements shrink to +0.7–2.1 mIoU, as expected given the smaller synthetic-to-real domain gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGInStyle builds on the Stable Diffusion LDM architecture, ControlNet for mask-conditioned generation, and DreamBooth for domain fine-tuning, but departs from prior diffusion-based segmentation data generators (DatasetDM, DiffuMask) that rely on a separate segmentation decoder to produce masks (grounded generation). Instead, it follows the semantic-guidance route where existing source-domain masks directly condition the diffusion process, and introduces the key conceptual departure of Style Swap: training ControlNet on a domain-fine-tuned LDM to absorb style, then swapping back to the original LDM at inference to decouple semantic layout control from domain-specific appearance. The Multi-Resolution Latent Fusion module extends MultiDiffusion's tiled generation with a latent inpainting step to preserve large objects, addressing a resolution limitation that prior LDM-based generators have not tackled for dense segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single consumer GPU and ~6,000 generated images can consistently boost multiple state-of-the-art DG methods by 2.5–7.2 mIoU across five autonomous driving benchmarks, positioning generative data augmentation as a practical, complementary lever alongside model-centric DG techniques. The Style Swap finding—that semantic control learned on a domain-specific LDM transfers cleanly to the original LDM—suggests a general principle for decoupling task structure from style in conditional diffusion, potentially applicable beyond segmentation. Limitations include dependence on labeled source-domain data, evaluation restricted to autonomous driving, and the generated dataset serving as augmentation rather than a standalone training set; open questions include whether the approach scales to other dense prediction tasks or domains with fewer source labels, and whether the 512×512 base resolution remains a bottleneck for very fine-grained classes.
