## Problem

Existing compressed image restoration (CIR) methods either target a single codec (e.g., JPEG only) or, when universal, lack the generative priors needed for realistic texture recovery at low bitrates. No prior work simultaneously handles diverse compression artifacts from both traditional and learned codecs while leveraging diffusion-based generation for perceptual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB compressed image from any of 7 codecs (JPEG, VVC, HEVC, WebP, C_PSNR, C_SSIM, HIFIC) at one of three quality levels as input. The LQ image is encoded into latent space via a frozen VAE encoder, processed through the fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 2.1-base UNet, and decoded by a fine-tuned VAE decoder to produce a restored high-quality image. A quality-enhancement module (transformer blocks) preprocesses the LQ image before CLIP visual feature extraction for the text-conditioning pathway.

## Method

MoE-DiffIR introduces a Mixture-of-Experts Prompt module where N=7 learnable prompts act as degradation experts, and a degradation-aware router (using noisy Top-K selection with K=3) dynamically composes prompt combinations per compression task. The router is conditioned on degradation priors extracted by a pre-trained CLIP encoder (from DACLIP) applied to the LQ image, enabling task-specific prompt scheduling without per-task parameters. A Visual-to-Text adapter (MLP layers) translates CLIP visual embeddings of the LQ image into text embeddings that condition Stable Diffusion, activating its cross-modality text-to-image generation prior for more consistent texture synthesis. The MoE-Prompt modulates multi-scale LQ features onto the UNet's outputs via SPADE layers. Training proceeds in two stages: first, only the MoE-Prompt module is trained (0.4M steps, Adam, lr=5e-5) with VAE and UNet frozen; second, only the VAE decoder is fine-tuned (0.1M steps, lr=1e-4) with LPIPS loss to correct structural fidelity.

## Results

On JPEG (QF=5/10/15) averaged over three distortion levels, MoE-DiffIR achieves the best perceptual quality across all test sets: LPIPS of 0.0964 and FID of 62.72 on LIVE1, outperforming StableSR (LPIPS 0.1069, FID 68.19) and PASD (LPIPS 0.1055, FID 70.46). The method reports a 10.9% LPIPS reduction and 5.4-point FID decrease over SUPIR, while maintaining competitive PSNR (30.50 dB on LIVE1, within 0.9 dB of PromptIR's 31.42 dB). Across all 7 codecs (JPEG, VVC, HEVC, WebP, C_PSNR, C_SSIM, HIFIC), MoE-DiffIR consistently achieves the lowest LPIPS and FID, demonstrating strong perceptual restoration at low bitrates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE-DiffIR achieves the best LPIPS and FID across nearly all codecs and test sets, with the most notable margins over SUPIR (10.9% lower LPIPS, 5.4 lower FID) and StableSR (0.41 dB higher PSNR, lower LPIPS/FID). Against PromptIR, which leads on PSNR (31.42 vs. 30.50 dB on JPEG/LIVE1), MoE-DiffIR trades ~0.9 dB in fidelity for substantially better perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.0964 vs. 0.1231, FID 62.72 vs. 111.15). DiffBIR achieves competitive LPIPS on JPEG (0.0995) but produces erroneous texture details at low bitrates, while MoE-DiffIR's VAE decoder fine-tuning yields more faithful reconstructions. On learned codecs (C_PSNR, C_SSIM, HIFIC), MoE-DiffIR maintains its perceptual advantage over all baselines, confirming the universality of the MoE-Prompt routing. On VVC and HEVC, the PSNR gap to PromptIR widens (e.g., 27.46 vs. 30.00 on VVC/LIVE1), but MoE-DiffIR still leads in LPIPS (0.1468 vs. 0.3053) and FID (94.73 vs. 168.36), indicating that the diffusion prior excels at perceptual restoration even when fidelity metrics lag.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage Stable Diffusion fine-tuning paradigm established by StableSR and AutoDIR, but replaces their fixed modulation modules (ControlNet, feature adapters) with a Mixture-of-Experts prompt routing mechanism inspired by Sparsely Gated MoE. It departs from prior prompt-based IR methods (PromptIR, ProRes, PIP, DACLIP) that use single prompts or soft-weighted prompt pools, arguing that such approaches suffer from 'mean feature' collapse; instead, MoE-Prompt treats each prompt as a degradation expert and uses a degradation-aware router (fed by DACLIP's CLIP encoder) to dynamically select top-K prompt combinations per task. The visual-to-text adapter is a novel addition that activates SD's text-to-image priors—overlooked by StableSR and DiffBIR, which use empty text prompts—by translating CLIP visual embeddings into the text embedding space via MLP layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first comprehensive universal CIR benchmark (7 codecs, 21 degradation types, including both traditional and learned codecs) fills a clear gap in the field and provides a standardized evaluation protocol that prior single-codec studies lacked. The MoE-Prompt mechanism offers a principled way to share a small set of prompt parameters across many degradation tasks while preserving task-specificity, which could generalize to other multi-task restoration settings. However, the paper acknowledges a notable quality gap at extremely low bitrates, and the perceptual gains over strong diffusion baselines (SUPIR, PASD) are modest (≈5% LPIPS, ≈4 FID), leaving open whether the added complexity of MoE routing and the V2T adapter is justified over simpler multi-prompt weighting at scale.
