## Problem

Existing LiDAR point cloud generation methods face a quality-speed tradeoff: score-based models like LiDARGen sample extremely slowly (0.02 samples/s) and degrade at far range, while voxel-based approaches like UltraLiDAR waste computation on empty voxels. Additionally, range-view-based generative models suffer from blurriness caused by inaccurate spherical projection when multiple Velodyne lasers do not share a common origin, leading to incorrect range-view data distributions that fundamentally limit generation quality.

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes random Gaussian noise in a compressed latent space (16×256×c, downsampled by factor 4 from the 64×1024×2 range image) and produces a full range-view LiDAR point cloud with range and intensity channels. For conditional tasks, upsampling takes a sparse 16-beam range image and outputs a dense 64-beam counterpart, while inpainting takes a masked point cloud plus a binary mask and reconstructs the occluded region. A simple h×w direction matrix (first column = 1, rest = 0) is optionally concatenated to control the generated scene's orientation, compensating for the rotation invariance introduced by circular convolution.

## Method

RangeLDM operates in two stages. First, a VAE compresses 64×1024×2 range images into a low-dimensional latent space, where the decoder is guided by a range-guided discriminator that replaces standard convolutions with Meta-Kernel layers computing relative spherical coordinates between neighboring pixels, making the adversarial signal geometry-sensitive to 3D structure. Second, a latent diffusion model (UNet with transformer layers) learns the distribution over these latent features, enabling fast 50-step DDIM sampling. A critical preprocessing step uses Hough Voting to estimate per-laser heights and pitch angles for Velodyne sensors, correcting the spherical projection so that the range-view data distribution is accurate—ablation shows this alone improves MMD by over 6×. Circular convolution is applied throughout both VAE and LDM to respect the topological wrap-around of range images, and the entire pipeline achieves 4.86 samples/s on a single RTX 3090, roughly 200× faster than LiDARGen.

## Results

On KITTI-360, RangeLDM achieves MMD_BEV of 3.07×10⁻⁵, FRD of 1074.9, and JSD_BEV of 0.045, outperforming all baselines by a large margin — MMD is roughly 6× lower than the next-best (UltraLiDAR, 1.96×10⁻⁴) and over 100× lower than LiDAR GAN. In terms of generation speed, RangeLDM produces 4.86 samples/s on a single RTX 3090, which is 200× faster than LiDARGen (0.02 samples/s) and 30× faster than UltraLiDAR (0.16 samples/s). On the upsampling task, RangeLDM achieves MAE 0.89, segmentation accuracy 0.722, and IoU 0.566, surpassing LiDARGen (MAE 1.23, accuracy 0.608, IoU 0.449).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RangeLDM dominates all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against LiDARGen (ECCV 2022), the closest prior method in quality, RangeLDM reduces MMD_BEV from 3.87×10⁻⁴ to 3.07×10⁻⁵ (≈12.6× improvement) and FRD from 2040.1 to 1074.9 (≈47% reduction), while being 200× faster in inference. Against UltraLiDAR (CVPR 2023), which previously held the best MMD (1.96×10⁻⁴), RangeLDM improves MMD by 6.4× and JSD from 0.071 to 0.045, and is 30× faster. Against the older GAN/VAE baselines (LiDAR GAN, LiDAR VAE, Projected GAN), the gap is even wider — MMD improves by 100×+ over LiDAR GAN. On the upsampling task, RangeLDM beats LiDARGen by 27% in MAE (0.89 vs 1.23) and by 11.8 points in segmentation IoU (0.566 vs 0.449), and outperforms interpolation methods (bicubic, NN) by a large margin on both accuracy and IoU. In the human study, participants preferred RangeLDM over LiDARGen 90.2% of the time and over UltraLiDAR 86.5% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

RangeLDM sits at the intersection of two research threads: LiDAR point cloud generation (LiDAR GAN/VAE → LiDARGen → UltraLiDAR) and latent diffusion models (LDMs from Rombach et al.). It adopts the range-view representation from LiDARGen but replaces the slow score-based sampling with a VAE-compressed latent diffusion pipeline, and borrows the Meta-Kernel from RangeDet to build a geometry-aware discriminator. The key conceptual departure is the identification of Hough Voting as a critical data-preprocessing step that corrects the range-view distribution for multi-origin Velodyne sensors, a factor prior range-based generators had overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 200× speedup over LiDARGen and 30× over UltraLiDAR make large-scale LiDAR data synthesis practically feasible for autonomous driving training pipelines, where prior methods were prohibitively slow. The finding that a simple geometric correction (Hough Voting) yields order-of-magnitude metric improvements reveals that data-distribution fidelity in the range view is a previously underappreciated bottleneck for range-based generative models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full 3D multi-viewpoint scenes, labeled point cloud generation, and corner-case synthesis (accidents, extreme weather), which the authors flag as future work.
