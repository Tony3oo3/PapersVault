## Problem

Existing MIL aggregation strategies for whole slide image analysis rely on first-order statistics (e.g., mean difference) to pool patch features, ignoring the covariance structure of the feature distribution and producing biased slide-level representations. Additionally, the limited number of WSI observations causes deterministic models to overfit, and frequentist frameworks cannot quantify the epistemic uncertainty inherent in the fitted model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241440/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each WSI is segmented via Otsu's thresholding and cropped into non-trivial patches (~2,231 per TCGA slide, ~24,401 per Camelyon 16 slide), whose features are extracted by a frozen SimCLR-pretrained KimiaNet encoder. The method outputs a slide-level class label (binary tumor/normal or multi-class subtype), per-patch log-likelihood scores that enable tumor region localization, and a predictive uncertainty estimate derived from the slide-level DP posterior mixture density.

## Method

cDP-MIL cascades two Dirichlet process modules: a patch-level DP aggregation module that clusters instance features via a stick-breaking construction with Normal-Inverse-Wishart priors on full (non-diagonal) covariance matrices, and a slide-level DP prediction module that classifies the resulting cluster centroids into K task-relevant classes. Both modules parameterize their Gaussian component means and covariances with deep neural networks, enabling the bag-level supervision signal to be backpropagated through variational inference to shape the clustering. Training optimizes the ELBO via stochastic variational inference with a mean-field factorization, replacing intractable MCMC with gradient-based updates. The richer-gets-richer property of the DP automatically determines the effective number of clusters, and the posterior log-responsibility at each level provides interpretable patch scores and calibrated uncertainty for OOD detection.

## Results

On the cancer binary classification task (Table 4), cDP-MIL achieves 98.2% AUROC on Camelyon 16, 99.7% AUROC on TCGA-COAD, and 99.8% AUROC on TCGA-BRCA, consistently outperforming all 8 competing baselines. On cancer subtyping (Table 3), cDP-MIL reaches 94.0% AUROC and 93.3% Macro-F1 on TCGA-ESCA, and 83.6% AUROC and 92.3% Macro-F1 on TCGA-BRCA, again leading all methods. On TCGA-NSCLC subtyping (Table 2), cDP-MIL attains 93.5% accuracy and 97.4% AUROC, surpassing the next-best TransMIL (88.4% / 96.0%). Notably, cDP-MIL also exhibits the lowest standard deviations across folds (e.g., 0.01 on Camelyon 16 AUROC), indicating substantially more stable performance than deterministic baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

cDP-MIL wins on every dataset and metric in the main comparison. On Camelyon 16, the gap is largest: cDP-MIL's 98.2% AUROC exceeds HEAT (78.3%) by ~20 points, PatchGCN (69.9%) by ~28 points, and GTNMIL (57.9%) by ~40 points, while also beating the stronger ABMIL (95.9%) and BayesMIL (94.6%) by 2.3 and 3.6 points respectively. On TCGA-COAD, cDP-MIL (99.7% AUROC) ties with H2-MIL and HEAT at the top but achieves the highest F1 (98.9%) and lowest variance (0.1 vs. 0.4–1.7). On TCGA-BRCA, cDP-MIL (99.8% AUROC, 99.2% F1) edges out BayesMIL (99.5% / 98.3%) and HEAT (98.8% / 98.7%). For subtyping on TCGA-ESCA, cDP-MIL leads by 1.2–11.6 AUROC points over the next best (HEAT 92.8%, ABMIL/DSMIL/Remix 92.5%), and on TCGA-BRCA subtyping it achieves 83.6% AUROC vs. BayesMIL's 82.8% and DSMIL's 81.5%. The only near-tie is TCGA-COAD AUROC where H2-MIL and HEAT also report 99.7%, but cDP-MIL still wins on F1 and stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Dirichlet Process from Bayesian nonparametric statistics into the MIL pipeline for whole slide image analysis, departing from the dominant deterministic aggregation strategies (attention weights, graph convolutions, mean/max pooling) that operate on first-order distances between patch features. It extends the Bayesian MIL line initiated by BayesMIL [32] by replacing its simpler log-normal mixture with a full-covariance Normal-Inverse-Wishart prior under a cascaded DP structure, and by substituting intractable MCMC with a neural-network-parameterized stochastic variational inference scheme. The stick-breaking formulation and ELBO-based training follow the variational DP literature (Blei & Jordan 2006; Echraibi et al. 2020), but the two-stage cascade—patch-level DP for clustering followed by slide-level DP for classification—is a novel architectural composition not seen in prior WSI or general MIL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cascaded DP framework addresses two persistent pain points in WSI-MIL: overfitting from limited bag-level supervision (mitigated by the natural Bayesian regularization of the slide-level DP) and biased feature aggregation from ignoring covariance structure (addressed by the full-covariance patch-level DP). The built-in posterior uncertainty enables practical OOD detection and tumor localization without auxiliary modules, which is valuable for clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the full covariance is essential versus a diagonal approximation at scale, how the method behaves when the pretrained encoder is weaker or domain-shifted, and whether the variational inference cost remains tractable for the largest WSIs (24k+ patches). The reliance on a fixed pretrained KimiaNet encoder also limits the method's end-to-end adaptability to new tissue types or staining protocols.
