## Problem

Existing 3D head generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based approaches suffer from feature entanglement in compact latent spaces that prevents fine-grained region-based editing, while recent diffusion-based methods using global tri-plane representations cannot support animation or local editing. No prior method simultaneously achieves high-quality 3D head synthesis, 3DMM-driven expression animation, and flexible global or fine-grained region-based editing of geometry and appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241727/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, the method consumes 50 multi-view RGB images and depth maps (64×64 for tri-plane fitting, 512×512 for super-resolution) per identity from Panohead-generated 3D heads. The diffusion model operates on a 256×256×33 UV-space tensor (32×32 texels unfolded with 8×8 tri-plane payloads and replicated 9-DOF Gaussian parameters), producing a complete 3D volumetric head. Editing inputs include 3DMM expression/shape codes that drive Gaussian positions, and binary UV-space masks that select semantic regions (e.g., nose, mouth) for localized diffusion inpainting or cross-identity transfer.

## Method

The core representation encodes a 3D head as 1024 local tri-planes (32×32 UV grid) anchored on a FLAME 3DMM surface, where each tri-plane (8×8×8 feature volume) is modulated by a 9-DOF 3D Gaussian controlling its position, orientation, and spatial influence via a radial basis function. Training is two-stage: first, a shared convolutional auto-decoder (StyleGAN-style) learns a 512-dim latent space that decodes to UV-space tri-planes from multi-view supervision, ensuring semantic alignment of local features across identities; second, a 2D U-Net diffusion model (Imagen architecture) is trained on the exported UV maps to generate new heads. Rendering queries K=3 nearest tri-planes per ray sample, weights their contributions by normalized Gaussian RBFs, and passes features through a shared tiny MLP for color and opacity. The key enabling insight is that UV-space parameterization via 3DMM gives each texel a consistent semantic meaning across identities, so region-based editing reduces to swapping or interpolating Gaussian-modulated tri-planes at corresponding UV locations, and geometry-texture disentanglement follows naturally since Gaussians encode shape while tri-plane payloads encode appearance.

## Results

Loc3DIFF achieves competitive identity consistency (ID 0.78) and depth accuracy (2.58) on Panohead-generated 3D portraits, while dramatically outperforming the only other animation-capable baseline IDE-3D on expression editing: PCK@2.5 of 0.783 vs. 0.16, and PCK@5 of 0.99 vs. 0.33. It also renders at 64 FPS (37 FPS with super-resolution) and requires only 8.25 MB of storage per identity, compared to 48 MB for IDE-3D and 72 MB for Panohead. The paper notes that ID and Depth metrics are comparable to Panohead since Loc3DIFF is trained on Panohead-generated data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FENeRF, Loc3DIFF wins on ID (0.78 vs. 0.61) and Depth (2.58 vs. 2.71), and uniquely supports 3DMM-driven expression animation (PCK@2.5 = 0.783) while FENeRF does not. Against Panohead, Loc3DIFF is slightly lower on ID (0.78 vs. 0.80) and Depth (2.58 vs. 2.32), but Panohead does not support animation at all; Loc3DIFF also renders 3.4× faster (64 vs. 19 FPS) and uses 8.7× less storage (8.25 vs. 72 MB). Against IDE-3D, the most direct competitor, Loc3DIFF dominates on expression editing (PCK@2.5: 0.783 vs. 0.16; PCK@5: 0.99 vs. 0.33) and on rendering speed (64 vs. 25.1 FPS) and storage (8.25 vs. 48 MB), while IDE-3D retains an edge on Depth (1.71 vs. 2.58) and is marginally lower on ID (0.76 vs. 0.78). The paper attributes IDE-3D's poor PCK to its segmentation-based animation paradigm, which cannot preserve identity when the reference expression differs in facial layout, whereas Loc3DIFF's 3DMM-anchored tri-planes enable direct expression code manipulation with full identity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: 3D-aware GANs for head synthesis (Panohead, EG3D), tri-plane-based neural rendering (IDE-3D, FENeRF), and diffusion models for 3D generation (Shue et al., Gu et al.). The key departure is replacing the global tri-plane with a set of local tri-planes, each modulated by a 3D Gaussian and anchored to a 3DMM (FLAME) UV map, which simultaneously enables 2D diffusion training and semantically consistent region-based editing. The auto-decoding strategy (shared latent space across identities) is borrowed from Bojanowski et al. and adapted here to produce semantically aligned tri-plane features that make cross-identity interpolation and diffusion training feasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UV-space parameterization of local tri-planes is a practical and elegant bridge between 3D volumetric representation and the mature 2D diffusion ecosystem, potentially making it a reusable design pattern for other 3D asset generation tasks. The demonstrated capabilities—3DMM-driven expression animation, region-based geometry/texture transfer, and diffusion-based 3D inpainting—all from a single unified representation—address a real gap left by both GAN-based (rigid editing) and prior diffusion-based (no editing) approaches. Open questions include extending to full-body avatars, adding text or segmentation conditioning, and improving the memory-inefficient volumetric rendering; the authors also note the method currently lacks fine-grained control and is limited to heads.
