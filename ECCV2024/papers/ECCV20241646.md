## Problem

The field of pathology lacks a specialized, high-quality benchmark for evaluating Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) on image understanding and reasoning. Existing datasets like PathVQA rely on heuristic question generation from textbook captions without expert validation, leading to unsolvable questions, text-only-solvable items, and substantial noise that obscures true multimodal capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241646/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a pathology image (average resolution ~900×700 pixels) with a text-based multiple-choice question (4 options) requiring visual analysis to answer. The expected output is the correct option letter accompanied by a detailed explanation of the reasoning. Questions are specifically designed and filtered so that they cannot be answered by reading the text alone—multi-LLM guessing filters remove any item that three or more text-only LLMs can correctly guess without seeing the image.

## Method

PathMMU is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) collecting ~30,000 image-caption pairs from five diverse sources (PubMed papers, YouTube educational videos, pathology atlas textbooks, Twitter posts by pathologists, and standard classification datasets), with automated filtering via ConvNeXt and YOLOv6 to isolate pathological content; (2) prompting GPT-4V to generate detailed morphological descriptions of each image, using original captions as reference to ensure coherence; (3) prompting GPT-4V to produce three multiple-choice questions per image with explanations, followed by multi-LLM filtering (GPT-3.5 Turbo, GPT-4 Turbo, Gemini Pro, ERNIE-Bot-4) to eliminate text-guessable questions, and final manual review by seven pathologists applying strict validity criteria. The evaluation protocol includes zero-shot testing of 18 LMMs, text-only LLM baselines to quantify shortcut reliance, robustness testing under eight types of image corruption at five severity levels, and fine-tuning of InstructBLIP variants on the training split to assess domain adaptability.

## Results

On the PathMMU test set (9,677 Q&As), GPT-4V-1106 achieves the highest LMM accuracy at 49.8%, while the best open-source model, LLaVA-1.5-13B, reaches only 37.6%. Human pathologists score 71.8%, revealing a ~22% gap between the best LMM and expert performance. Notably, the text-only GPT-4 Turbo achieves 38.1% accuracy without any image input, surpassing the best open-source LMM and indicating that many questions can be answered via educated guessing. After fine-tuning, InstructBLIP FLAN-T5-XXL improves from 33.9% to 55.2% on the test set, surpassing GPT-4V's zero-shot score but still falling short of the 71.8% expert benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V-1106 leads all 18 evaluated LMMs at 49.8% on the full test set, outperforming Gemini Pro Vision (42.7%) and Qwen-VL-PLUS (45.9%) by 7.1 and 3.9 points respectively, yet still trails human pathologists (71.8%) by 22 points. Among open-source LMMs, LLaVA-1.5-13B (37.6%) edges out InstructBLIP FLAN-T5-XXL (33.9%) and BLIP-2 FLAN-T5-XXL (33.5%), with the 13B variant beating its 7B counterpart by 2.2 points, confirming a scaling trend. Strikingly, the text-only GPT-4 Turbo (38.1%) surpasses every open-source LMM, including LLaVA-1.5-13B, suggesting that visual information contributes less than expected to current LMM performance on pathology. After fine-tuning on PathMMU's training set, InstructBLIP FLAN-T5-XXL jumps from 33.9% to 55.2% (+21.3%), surpassing GPT-4V's zero-shot score by 5.4 points, while InstructBLIP FLAN-T5-XL improves from 31.8% to 53.3% (+21.5%). However, even fine-tuned models remain 16–18 points below the expert benchmark, and adding explanation generation during fine-tuning slightly degrades performance (−0.3% to −2.3%) compared to direct answer prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

PathMMU extends the general multimodal VQA benchmark paradigm (MMMU, MMBench) into the specialized pathology domain, departing from the closest prior effort, PathVQA, which relied on heuristic question generation from textbook captions without expert validation. The construction pipeline repurposes GPT-4V for caption enrichment and question generation, then applies a multi-LLM filtering stage and seven-pathologist manual review—procedures absent from all prior pathology benchmarks. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of LMM evaluation methodology and clinical pathology, filling a gap where no large-scale, expert-validated, multimodal pathology benchmark existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22-percentage-point gap between GPT-4V (49.8%) and human pathologists (71.8%) quantifies how far current LMMs are from clinical utility in pathology, while the finding that text-only GPT-4 Turbo (38.1%) outperforms the best open-source LMM (LLaVA-1.5-13B at 37.6%) raises serious concerns about whether these models genuinely leverage visual information. The robustness and Gaussian-noise experiments further suggest LMMs rely on textual shortcuts rather than morphological image analysis, pointing to a fundamental architectural limitation. Open questions include how to force genuine visual grounding in pathology, whether multi-image reasoning (mimicking real pathologist workflows at varying magnifications) can close the remaining gap, and how to exploit PathMMU's explanation annotations for training without the observed performance degradation.
