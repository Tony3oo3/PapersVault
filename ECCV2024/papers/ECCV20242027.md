## Problem

Neural scene representations (NeRF, LFN) are highly sensitive to inconsistencies in input observations—occlusions, noisy camera poses, blur, lens flare—producing ghosting and floating artifacts in novel views. Existing robust-loss approaches (e.g., RobustNeRF) down-weight outliers but also degrade clean high-frequency details and are restricted to unconditioned, single-scene models. No prior work provides a reliable outlier-removal mechanism for conditioned neural fields or for single-shot reconstruction from occluded views.

## Input / Output

For the LFN variant, a single RGB image with known intrinsics/extrinsics is consumed as a set of pixel-color/ray pairs (rays in Plücker coordinates); the output is a latent code plus the consensus set of inlier samples, from which arbitrary novel views can be rendered. For the NeRF variant, multiple RGB images with estimated camera poses (via COLMAP) and foreground masks (via Segment Anything) are consumed as whole-image observations; the output is a fitted NeRF scene representation. In both cases the algorithm explicitly partitions input samples into inliers and outliers, and only the inlier subset drives the final model fit.

## Method

RANRAC adapts the RANSAC paradigm to large-scale, data-driven neural fields by treating the initial sample size as a tunable hyperparameter rather than a fixed minimal set, since neural fields require many samples for a meaningful fit. For LFNs, N random subsets of M pixel-color/ray pairs are inferred in parallel through the pretrained LFN autoencoder to obtain candidate latent codes; each hypothesis renders the full input image, and the hypothesis explaining the most pixels within a color-space margin ε is selected, after which the model is re-inferred on the union of the winning subset and all its inliers. For NeRFs, M observations are randomly sampled per iteration, a NeRF is fit to each subset, and a two-step validation (pixel-level inlier counting followed by observation-level inlier classification) identifies the best hypothesis; the final NeRF is re-fit on the consensus set. The key distinction from robust-loss methods is that inconsistent perspectives are hard-excluded rather than soft-down-weighted, preserving all clean data at full weight and avoiding the loss of view-dependent and high-frequency details.

## Results

For single-shot LFN reconstruction on ShapeNet, RANRAC achieves up to 8 dB PSNR improvement over vanilla LFNs under occlusion (e.g., Cabin: 22.92 vs. 19.20 PSNR; Bench: 19.21 vs. 17.89 PSNR at ~25% object occlusion). For multi-view NeRF reconstruction, RANRAC consistently outperforms both RobustNeRF and vanilla NeRF across all tested inconsistency types: on blurred perspectives (Lego: 34.79 vs. 29.14 vs. 31.15 PSNR) and noisy camera parameters (Lego: 34.95 vs. 29.83 vs. 24.67 PSNR). The 5th-percentile PSNR (P5) confirms that RANRAC also improves the worst-case views, not just the average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla LFNs [40], RANRAC achieves substantial gains in occluded single-shot reconstruction (up to 8 dB PSNR), with the improvement growing as occlusion increases; in clean (unoccluded) scenarios a slight penalty is observed due to wrongful exclusion of some high-frequency inliers. Against RobustNeRF [38], RANRAC wins on every tested inconsistency type (occlusions, blur, noisy camera parameters) and every dataset, with the largest margins on blurred perspectives (e.g., Ship: 29.76 vs. 23.31 PSNR) and noisy camera parameters (e.g., Lego: 34.95 vs. 29.83 PSNR); RobustNeRF specifically struggles with view-dependent appearance and concavities, while RANRAC preserves these details. Against vanilla NeRF [29], RANRAC improves both average and worst-case (P5) PSNR in all inconsistent settings, confirming that the RANSAC-based outlier removal is more effective than no robustness at all. The only scenario where RANRAC does not clearly dominate is the Ship scene under blur, where strong view-dependent effects are partially misclassified as blur, causing some clean perspectives to be excluded (lower P5), though the overall reconstruction still improves.

## Relation to Prior Work

RANRAC adapts the classical RANSAC paradigm (Fischler & Bolles, 1981) from small-parameter analytical models to large-scale, data-driven neural scene representations, introducing a 'fuzzy' consensus mechanism that trades guaranteed convergence for a tunable sample-size hyperparameter. It departs from RobustNeRF's approach of down-weighting outliers via robust loss functions by instead explicitly identifying and excluding inconsistent perspectives, preserving view-dependent details that robust losses tend to blur. The method is demonstrated on two distinct neural field architectures—unconditioned NeRF (built on iNGP) and conditioned Light Field Networks—showing generality across the supervised multi-view and single-shot reconstruction settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a general, architecture-agnostic robustness framework that handles diverse inconsistencies (occlusions, noisy poses, blur) without requiring semantic priors or assumptions about distractor type, which is practically valuable for real-world captures. Key limitations include the need for fast NeRF variants (ruling out MipNeRF360 for unbounded scenes), mandatory foreground separation, and inheritance of RANSAC's requirement for a sufficient fraction of clean samples. Open questions include extending the approach to other conditioned neural fields, using importance sampling or learned priors for hypothesis generation, and adapting to unbounded scene representations.
