## Problem

Existing scene text segmentation methods largely ignore the importance of text edges, which are critical for downstream tasks such as text erasing where accurate edge boundaries determine how much background information is preserved for inpainting. Additionally, widely-used benchmarks like COCO_TS and MLT_S contain coarse bounding-box-derived annotations that are insufficient for evaluating pixel-level segmentation quality, particularly at text boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240668/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene text image (3×H×W) as input and produces a pixel-level binary text segmentation mask (text vs. background). Internally, the Canny edge detector is applied to the raw image to obtain whole-image edges, which are then filtered by a lightweight text detection head to retain only text-region edges. The final output is a 2-class mask predicted by an MLP decoder, and the method requires only semantic-level (bounding-box) annotations for training, not pixel-level edge labels.

## Method

EAFormer comprises three modules: a text edge extractor that applies Canny edge detection to the input image and uses a lightweight ResNet-based text detector to filter out non-text edges via pixel-wise multiplication with a soft-argmax text mask; an edge-guided encoder built on SegFormer's four-stage hierarchical Transformer architecture, where a symmetric cross-attention sub-module at the first stage bidirectionally exchanges information between the stage-1 features and the filtered text edges (edges as Query attending to features, and features as Query attending to edges); and an MLP-based decoder that unifies channel dimensions, upsamples, and fuses multi-scale features to predict the final mask. Training is fully supervised with only two cross-entropy losses (text detection and text segmentation) and all modules are jointly optimized without requiring any pre-trained external models or additional datasets.

## Results

EAFormer achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across six scene text segmentation benchmarks. On TextSeg, it reaches 88.06% fgIoU and 0.939 F-score, outperforming the previous best (TextFormer) by 0.64% fgIoU. On COCO_TS, EAFormer achieves 81.03% fgIoU, a 7.63% improvement over the prior best (TFT at 73.40%). On the bilingual BTS dataset, EAFormer attains 88.08% fgIoU and 0.937 F-score, surpassing TFT (87.84/0.935) and PGTSNet (86.48/0.909). The model has 92M parameters and runs at 0.47s per image, slightly heavier than TextFormer (85M, 0.42s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAFormer outperforms the previous SOTA TextFormer [41] on TextSeg by 0.64% fgIoU (88.06 vs 87.42) and 0.6% F-score, and on COCO_TS by a large margin of 7.83% fgIoU (81.03 vs 73.20). Against TFT [48], EAFormer wins on COCO_TS (+7.63% fgIoU), MLT_S (+1.22%), Total-Text (+0.63%), and BTS (+0.24% fgIoU, 0.937 vs 0.935 F-score). On ICDAR13 FST, EAFormer (72.63% fgIoU) slightly trails TexRNet [44] (73.38%) and ARM-Net [32] (0.851 F-score), making it the one dataset where EAFormer does not achieve the top result. Compared to its direct baseline SegFormer [43], EAFormer gains 12.19% fgIoU on TextSeg (88.06 vs 84.59) and 3.09% on BTS (88.08 vs 84.99), confirming the contribution of the edge-guided encoder. On BTS, EAFormer also surpasses PGTSNet [45] by 1.6% fgIoU despite PGTSNet using a pretrained text detector, and beats TexRNet [44] by 4.27% fgIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAFormer builds directly on SegFormer's hierarchical Transformer encoder with MLP decoder, repurposing it for scene text segmentation by injecting edge information via a symmetric cross-attention module at the first stage. It departs from the dominant trend in scene text segmentation (TexRNet, PGTSNet, TextFormer) of introducing text-specific supervisions like character recognition or perceptual discriminators, instead leveraging the traditional Canny edge detector as a free, annotation-free source of edge guidance. The edge-filtering step (multiplying Canny edges by a predicted text-area mask) distinguishes it from general edge-guided segmentation methods (BCANet, BSNet, EGCAN) that either require edge annotations or fuse edges across all layers without domain-specific filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Accurate text-edge segmentation is a practical bottleneck for downstream applications like text erasing and inpainting, where even a few-pixel boundary error degrades results; EAFormer addresses this with a simple, interpretable mechanism. The re-annotation of COCO_TS and MLT_S is a valuable community contribution that makes benchmark comparisons more meaningful. Open questions include whether replacing Canny with a learned edge detector would yield further gains, whether the first-stage-only edge injection strategy transfers to other fine-grained segmentation tasks, and whether the added text-detection parameters can be reduced without sacrificing edge filtering quality.
