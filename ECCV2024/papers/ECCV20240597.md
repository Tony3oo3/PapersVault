## Problem

Existing patch pruning methods for efficient inference in ViTs and hybrid transformers require training additional mask-prediction modules, are tied to specific architectural features (e.g., class tokens), and perform gradual multi-step pruning across intermediate layers. This makes them impractical for frequent model updates and limits their applicability across diverse architectures. The paper seeks a training-free, architecture-agnostic, single-step patch pruning approach that can immediately accelerate off-the-shelf models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1k) or video clips (16 frames of 224×224 for Kinetics-400) and produces a binary patch pruning mask via a Patch Significance Map (PSM). The PSM is generated by a lightweight ConvNet (e.g., MobileOne-S0 at 0.27 GFLOPs) whose FC layer is suppressed, yielding a spatial map whose values rank patch importance. The mask is applied immediately after patch extraction in ViTs or before pixel operators in hierarchical models, and the pruned model outputs the final class or action label.

## Method

PaPr exploits the observation that lightweight ConvNets localize discriminative image regions competitively with much larger models (as measured by top-k accuracy), even when their top-1 classification accuracy is lower. The key mechanism replaces the FC layer weights with a uniform value 1/(KC), reducing the class-weighted activation to a simple pixel-wise channel mean that produces a discriminative region proposal independent of classification confidence. This proposal is upsampled to the target feature map resolution to form the PSM, from which a top-z% pruning mask is extracted via sorting. For ViTs, patches are pruned in one step immediately after the patch extractor, so all subsequent transformer blocks operate only on retained tokens; for hierarchical models (Swin, ConvNeXt), pixel operators (1×1 convs/linear layers, which account for >60% of FLOPs) are applied only to foreground patches while background patches are zero-padded. The entire procedure is training-free, batch-compatible, and composable with existing token-merging methods like ToMe.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with AugReg-pretrained ViT-B-16, PaPr (z=0.5) achieves 82.11% top-1 accuracy at 8.98 GFLOPs, outperforming ToMe (80.38%), TokenFusion (80.7%), and GTP-ViT (80.98%) at comparable FLOPs. For MAE-pretrained ViT-B-16, the gap widens to 4.5% over ToMe (82.4% vs. 78.82%), as PaPr's masked inference aligns naturally with MAE's training paradigm. On Kinetics-400 video recognition, PaPr achieves up to 3.7× FLOPs reduction (598→160 GFLOPs for ViT-L-MAE) with only 0.8% accuracy drop. Combining PaPr with ToMe yields an additional 22.3% FLOP reduction while maintaining higher accuracy than either method alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaPr consistently outperforms all three training-free token-merging baselines (ToMe, TokenFusion, GTP-ViT) by 1.1–4.5% top-1 accuracy at similar or lower FLOPs across ViT-S/B/L sizes, with the largest margin (4.5%) on MAE-pretrained models where masked inference is most natural. Against training-based pruning methods (DynamicViT, A-ViT, SP-ViT) on DeiT-S, PaPr achieves comparable accuracy (79.2% vs. 79.3–79.5%) while uniquely supporting batch processing and 1.6× training speedup, whereas DynamicViT and A-ViT cannot batch-process. On hierarchical models, PaPr matches DynCNN within 0.33% accuracy on ConvNeXt-Base (82.75% vs. 83.08%) with 7.1% higher throughput, and achieves 98% of DynSwin accuracy on Swin-B without any re-training. For video recognition on Kinetics-400, PaPr substantially outperforms ATS (85.12% vs. 80.0% for XViT at similar FLOPs) and ToMe (85.12% vs. 84.5% for ViT-L-MAE), while also enabling 3× more spatial views (3×5 vs. 1×10) for the same computational budget. PaPr's key differentiator is its training-free, architecture-agnostic design: it works identically across ViTs, ConvNeXt, and Swin without per-model adapter training, and can be stacked on top of existing methods like ToMe for Pareto-optimal accuracy–FLOP trade-offs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of incremental, multi-step token pruning in ViTs (DynamicViT, A-ViT, SP-ViT) and architecture-specific methods relying on class tokens or attention maps (ATS, ToMe) by proposing a single-step, early-network pruning strategy that is architecture-agnostic. Conceptually, it repurposes the region-localization capability of ConvNets—previously studied in the CAM/explainability literature—but decouples localization from classification accuracy through a simple uniform weight recalibration of the FC layer, yielding a Patch Significance Map. Unlike prior patch reduction methods that require training dedicated mask predictors or adapters for each target model, PaPr leverages off-the-shelf lightweight ConvNets as frozen proposal networks, making it training-free and immediately applicable to any model update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, one-step nature of PaPr addresses a practical pain point: as foundation models are frequently updated with new pre-training data or architectures, retraining patch selectors becomes infeasible, whereas PaPr simply swaps in a new ConvNet proposal. The finding that ultra-lightweight ConvNets (<0.3 GFLOPs) localize discriminative regions nearly as well as models 42× larger suggests that spatial understanding and fine-grained classification are separable capabilities, opening questions about whether other tasks (detection, segmentation) could similarly benefit from small-model-guided pruning. Limitations include the additional forward pass cost of the proposal ConvNet, the somewhat ad-hoc zero-padding strategy for hierarchical models, and the fact that accuracy still drops (albeit minimally) at aggressive pruning ratios. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative or multi-modal models and whether the proposal model itself could be distilled or learned end-to-end without the FC recalibration hack.
