## Problem

Existing video deblurring methods based on CNNs and Transformers struggle to recover high-frequency details because regression losses are inherently conservative with high-frequency information. While diffusion models can generate rich high-frequency content, directly applying them to video deblurring is impractical: they require many denoising iterations and are easily misled by motion blur artifacts, producing irrational content and distortion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241208/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurry video clip of T frames (3×H×W) and produces a deblurred video of the same dimensions. During training, 256×256 patches with a sequence length of 15 are used; at inference, full-resolution 1280×720 frames with a sequence length of 16 are processed. The diffusion model operates in a highly compressed latent space (256 channels) requiring only 4 denoising steps, and the entire pipeline runs on a single GPU with ~12M parameters and 0.31s inference time per frame.

## Method

VD-Diff integrates a Wavelet-Aware Dynamic Transformer (WADT) with a latent diffusion model. Wavelet Transform decomposes input features into low-frequency approximation and high-frequency detail coefficients; a diffusion model in a compact latent space generates prior features containing high-frequency information conforming to the GT distribution using only 4 denoising steps conditioned on the blurry video. WADT layers fuse these prior features with approximation coefficients via element-wise multiplication and dynamic multi-head self-attention, while a Wavelet-based Bidirectional Propagation Fuse (WBPF) module propagates spatio-temporal information across frames in both forward and backward directions. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) train the latent encoder and WADT with L1 and multi-scale frequency reconstruction losses, (2) train the diffusion model to generate prior features from the condition encoder output, and (3) jointly fine-tune DM and WADT to improve their collaborative deblurring performance.

## Results

VD-Diff achieves SOTA performance on all three benchmark datasets. On GoPro, it reaches 35.55 dB PSNR and 0.9752 SSIM, outperforming the previous best (DSTNet-L) by 0.50 dB. On DVD, it achieves 34.56 dB PSNR and 0.9684 SSIM, surpassing RVRT by 0.26 dB. On BSD, it attains 35.61 dB PSNR and 0.9758 SSIM, exceeding DSTNet by 1.16 dB. The model uses 11.93M parameters and 0.31 s inference time per frame at 1280×720, with only 4 diffusion iteration steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VD-Diff outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every benchmark dataset. On GoPro, it beats the closest competitors DSTNet-L (35.05 dB) and RVRT (34.92 dB) by 0.50 and 0.63 dB respectively, and surpasses the Transformer-based FGST by 2.65 dB. On DVD, it leads RVRT (34.30 dB) by 0.26 dB and DSTNet (33.79 dB) by 0.77 dB. On BSD, the margin over DSTNet (34.45 dB) is the largest at 1.16 dB. In terms of efficiency, VD-Diff (11.93M params, 0.31 s) is comparable to DSTNet (7.45M, 0.08 s) and BasicVSR++ (9.46M, 0.10 s) in parameter count and inference speed, while delivering at least 1.3 dB higher PSNR than both. The paper also shows that directly applying a pretrained LDM [31] to video deblurring produces significant artifacts and content inconsistencies, validating the need for the proposed WADT-guided integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Transformer-based video deblurring (RVRT, FGST, DSTNet), which the authors argue is limited in recovering high-frequency details; (2) latent diffusion models (LDM, DDPM), which have been applied to image restoration (DiffIR, Hi-diff, DiffMSR) but not yet to video deblurring; and (3) wavelet-based restoration methods (WavTrans, DSTNet), which separate frequency bands for targeted processing. VD-Diff departs from prior video deblurring by introducing a diffusion model as a prior-feature generator operating in a highly compressed latent space, and from prior diffusion-based restoration by coupling the DM with a wavelet-decomposed dynamic transformer that explicitly assigns low-frequency recovery to the transformer and high-frequency synthesis to the DM. The Wavelet-based Bidirectional Propagation Fuse (WBPF) is a novel spatio-temporal propagation module that replaces the simple frame-stacking or optical-flow alignment used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first application of diffusion models to video deblurring, establishing a new paradigm where a DM generates high-frequency prior features in a compact latent space (requiring only 4 denoising steps) while a wavelet-aware transformer handles structural/low-frequency recovery. The practical implication is significant: the 4-step latent-space diffusion makes DM-based video restoration computationally feasible (0.31s inference, 11.93M params), whereas direct application of LDM to video deblurring produces severe artifacts as shown in Fig. 8. Open questions include whether the wavelet+DM frequency-splitting strategy generalizes to other video restoration tasks (denoising, super-resolution), whether the 4-step budget can be further reduced, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions without the error accumulation the authors note at 16+ frames.
