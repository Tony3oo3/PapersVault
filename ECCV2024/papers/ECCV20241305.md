## Problem

Neural implicit representations (NIRs) such as NeRF and MonoSDF require all scene images to be available simultaneously for one-time training, which is impractical for large-scale or streaming scenarios. When trained sequentially on new data without access to previous images, these networks suffer severe catastrophic forgetting, losing previously reconstructed geometry or rendered views. This paper addresses the under-explored task of incremental learning for general NIRs, aiming to preserve old knowledge while learning from newly arriving data without storing any previous training samples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming batches of RGB images with corresponding camera poses arriving in 10 sequential time steps, where camera poses generally do not overlap between steps. For the SDF-based 3D reconstruction backbone (MonoSDF), monocular depth and normal priors from a pre-trained model are additionally used as geometric supervision. The outputs are a full 3D surface reconstruction (SDF field) or novel-view rendered images, plus per-ray uncertainty estimates that serve as an internal confidence signal for selecting reliable teacher knowledge. Crucially, no previous images or poses are stored across time steps—only the range of camera matrix parameters is retained.

## Method

UNIKD proposes a student-teacher pipeline where the student network is initialized from the teacher and trained on current data while being distilled from the teacher's outputs; at the end of each time step, the student becomes the next teacher. A random inquirer generates camera views by sampling within the stored ranges of the six camera-matrix parameters from a randomly chosen previous time step, probing the teacher's knowledge space without needing the original images. Because the teacher was trained only on old data and may produce unreliable outputs for arbitrary queries, a self-supervised uncertainty branch (following Kendall & Gal's formulation, rendered via volume integration) predicts per-ray confidence, and only camera views whose mean uncertainty falls below a threshold are retained for the distillation loss. Training alternates between the supervised rendering loss on current data and the uncertainty-weighted distillation loss on filtered teacher outputs, with the SDF Eikonal and geometry-prior terms applied in both phases.

## Results

On the ICL-NUIM 3D reconstruction benchmark, UNIKD achieves an F1 score of 90.32 and Chamfer Distance of 2.60, improving 39.6% over the MonoSDF baseline (F1 64.71) and slightly surpassing the batch-training upper bound MonoSDF* (F1 89.68). On the Replica dataset, it reaches F1 86.52 (61.3% improvement over MonoSDF's 53.63). For novel view synthesis, UNIKD achieves 21.21 PSNR on 360Capture (36.3% over NeRF's 15.56) and 22.59 PSNR on ScanNet (63.9% over NeRF's 13.78), while using only ~3 MB of additional memory compared to 300 MB for batch training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIKD outperforms all incremental learning baselines (MAS, PackNet, KR, POD, AFC) on both 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis tasks. The closest competitor is keyframe replay (KR), which achieves F1 86.78 on ICL-NUIM and 19.68 PSNR on 360Capture, but UNIKD surpasses it by 3.5 F1 points and 1.5 PSNR respectively while using far less memory (3 MB vs 30 MB). For SLAM-based methods, UNIKD outperforms iMAP by a large margin (F1 86.52 vs CD 4.99 on Replica) but is slightly behind NICE-SLAM on Chamfer Distance (3.11 vs 2.93), which the authors attribute to NICE-SLAM's more powerful scene representation. The method also generalizes to newer NeRF backbones, improving Tri-MipRF from 20.34 to 27.02 PSNR and ZipNeRF from 21.78 to 27.85 PSNR on ScanNet. Notably, UNIKD matches or exceeds the batch-training upper bound (MonoSDF*, NeRF*) on most metrics without storing any previous data, whereas all replay-based baselines require additional memory for keyframes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the student-teacher knowledge distillation framework (Hinton et al., 2015) from model compression into a continual learning mechanism for neural implicit representations, where the teacher encodes previously learned scene knowledge and the student absorbs new views. It departs from CNM (the first incremental NIR work) and SLAM-based approaches (iMAP, NICE-SLAM) by eliminating the need to store any previous data or keyframes, instead relying on a self-supervised uncertainty branch (Kendall & Gal, 2017) to filter unreliable teacher outputs generated by a random inquirer. The method is positioned as a general pipeline applicable across NIR backbones (NeRF, MonoSDF, Tri-MipRF, ZipNeRF), in contrast to prior incremental NIR work that was tied to a single representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling NIR-based 3D reconstruction and view synthesis in streaming or memory-constrained settings (e.g., robotics, AR) where storing all training images is infeasible, achieving near-batch-training quality with only ~3 MB of additional memory versus ~300 MB for full-data training. A key limitation is that the random inquirer relies on stored camera pose ranges and is heuristic; its effectiveness may degrade in highly non-uniform or dynamic scenes. Open questions include how the approach scales to very long sequences (beyond 10 steps), whether the uncertainty threshold requires per-scene tuning, and whether the framework extends to dynamic or multi-scene settings where the teacher's knowledge becomes partially invalid.
