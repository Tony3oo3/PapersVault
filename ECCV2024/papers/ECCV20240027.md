## Problem

Existing portrait animation methods struggle to transfer facial expressions across identities without leaking appearance information: 2D warping-based approaches entangle expression with local motion, and 3D NeRF-based methods either fail to faithfully reconstruct identity or introduce video-level artifacts via point-wise deformation fields. A key underlying issue is that 3DMM expression parameters remain entangled with identity-appearance, causing undesirable appearance swap (e.g., eye shape, facial contour) during cross-identity expression transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240027/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 source portrait image and a driving image from which 3DMM expression parameters (β ∈ R^64) and camera parameters (p ∈ R^25, combining intrinsics and inverse extrinsics) are extracted via a 3DMM extractor. It produces a 3D-aware animated portrait at the driving camera view with the driving expression, rendered at 1/4 resolution via differentiable volume rendering and then super-resolved to full 256×256 resolution. The method is one-shot (no per-identity optimization) and supports both same-identity and cross-identity transfer as well as novel-view synthesis.

## Method

Export3D introduces a hybrid tri-plane generator combining a ViT backbone with convolutional blocks that directly generates a tri-plane from the source image and driving expression, bypassing deformation-field prediction. The key architectural mechanism is Expression Adaptive Layer Normalization (EAdaLN), which modulates visual tokens in each ViT block (before MSA and Mix-FFN) using scale and shift factors computed from the refined expression, effectively injecting expression semantics into the tri-plane generation. To address appearance-expression entanglement in 3DMM parameters, the paper proposes CLeBS (Contrastive Learned Basis Scaling): a contrastive pre-training framework that learns an appearance-free expression encoder (LeBS) mapping β ∈ R^64 to a low-dimensional coefficient vector λ ∈ R^n (n≪64) that scales a learned orthonormal basis V ∈ R^{d×n} (obtained via QR-decomposition), producing an orthogonal expression sub-space. CLeBS is pre-trained on video data using aligned image-expression pairs as positives and non-aligned pairs from the same video as negatives, then frozen during generator training. The generated tri-plane is decoded into RGB via differentiable volume rendering with the driving camera rays, followed by a plane-convolutional super-resolution block.

## Results

On VFHQ (same-identity), Export3D achieves the best PSNR (23.555), AKD (3.453), AED (0.082), and APD (0.030), while DPE leads on SSIM (0.750 vs. 0.704) and HiDe-NeRF leads on CSIM (0.867 vs. 0.811). In the cross-identity setting, Export3D achieves the lowest AED (0.208), indicating the most faithful expression transfer without appearance swap, and ranks second on CSIM (0.694) behind HiDe-NeRF (0.707). On TalkingHead-1KH, Export3D similarly leads on PSNR (23.239), SSIM (0.797), AKD (3.581), and all three cross-identity metrics (CSIM 0.772, AED 0.204, APD 0.076).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Export3D outperforms all five baselines on expression fidelity (AED) and pose matching (APD) in the same-identity setting, with the closest competitor being DPE (AED 0.083 vs. 0.082; APD 0.032 vs. 0.030). In the cross-identity setting, Export3D achieves the lowest AED (0.208) by a clear margin over the next-best ROME (0.224), confirming that the CLeBS contrastive pre-training effectively removes appearance information from the expression parameter. HiDe-NeRF slightly edges out Export3D on cross-identity CSIM (0.707 vs. 0.694) and APD (0.065 vs. 0.080), but suffers from unpredictable lighting changes due to its point-wise deformation field approach. DPE, while competitive on same-identity metrics, exhibits visible appearance swap (eye shape, facial contour changes) in cross-identity transfer due to insufficient pose-expression disentanglement in its motion latent space. OTAvatar and StyleHEAT are consistently the weakest baselines across all metrics, with OTAvatar showing the largest AED (0.290) and StyleHEAT the worst PSNR (14.233) and AKD (30.406).

## Relation to Prior Work

Export3D builds on EG3D's tri-plane representation and differentiable volume rendering as its 3D backbone, and on Live3DPortrait's ViT+CNN hybrid architecture for tri-plane generation. It departs from two dominant paradigms in portrait animation: (1) 2D warping-based methods (FOMM, LIA, DPE) that encode expression in a motion latent space where it becomes entangled with head pose, and (2) 3D deformation-field methods (HiDe-NeRF, NOFA) that warp a canonical tri-plane, which introduce flicker artifacts. The key conceptual shift is treating the 3DMM expression parameter as a multi-dimensional label injected via EAdaLN rather than as a signal to estimate motion or deformation, combined with a contrastive pre-training step (CLeBS) that strips appearance information from the expression before it enters the generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in cross-identity expression transfer—undesirable appearance swap (e.g., eye shape, facial contour changing)—by showing that 3DMM expression parameters carry hidden identity information and that a lightweight contrastive pre-training step can remove it. The learned orthonormal basis (LeBS) also provides interpretable, orthogonal expression directions, which is useful for fine-grained control. Limitations remain: the tri-plane representation cannot separate foreground from background, and 3DMM parameters do not cover non-facial body parts or eye gaze, restricting the method's applicability to full-body or gaze-controllable scenarios. The approach is a clean, modular solution that could be integrated into other tri-plane-based pipelines.
