## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on SNNs processing event data target grid-based representations (e.g., event-count frames or binary voxel grids), which are intermediate formats often hidden within the vision pipeline and inaccessible to an external attacker. Directly attacking raw event data in COO format remains unexplored due to the three-valued discrete optimization, the vast temporal optimization space (often exceeding 10,000 timestamps per sample), and the need to preserve the inherent sparsity of event streams.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw event data in sparse COO format—indices (x, y, t) with polarity values in {-1, 1}—along with a target class label, and produces an adversarial event stream in the same COO format. The output is constrained so that event count remains within [θ_l, θ_h] to preserve sparsity, and the dense-representation L1 distance between adversarial and original events is bounded by ε. Unlike grid-based attacks, no intermediate aggregation step (Dirac pulse, exponential kernel, etc.) is required, making the attack directly deployable against models that only expose a raw-event interface.

## Method

The attack formulates a continuous optimization over discrete event polarities by treating each value as a sample from a three-way categorical distribution (polarity +1, polarity −1, or removal) and applying Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization with a straight-through estimator for gradient backpropagation. To make the optimization tractable, the method restricts the search to a curated subset of positions: the union of original event indices and indices from a target-labeled sample, with additional positions initialized to zero. A sparsity penalty term (λ‖ΔE*‖₁) is added to the classification loss to keep the adversarial event count close to the original. The algorithm iterates over mini-batches of M samples, updating log-probabilities α via the averaged straight-through gradient, and terminates early when the target label is achieved.

## Results

On raw event data, the proposed Gumbel-Softmax attack achieves ASR of 72.23% (CIFAR10-DVS), 86.67% (DVS Gesture), and 53.06% (NMNIST) on SEW ResNet-34, and 64.79%, 62.22%, and 78.57% on Spiking ResNet-18, respectively — 50–70 percentage points higher than the FGSM baseline. The method also maintains lower MSE (0.67–0.77 vs. 0.82–0.98 for FGSM) and a smaller event-ratio perturbation Δ (15–19% vs. 34–66%), indicating more subtle and sparsity-preserving adversarial samples. On binary grid representations, the method achieves 86.87–98.10% ASR, competitive with Spike-Compatible (92.86–99.84%) while operating directly on raw events where grid-based methods are inapplicable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FGSM (Kurakin et al. [23]), the proposed method dominates on raw event data across all six dataset–model combinations, with ASR gains of 40–65 percentage points (e.g., 86.67% vs. 15.56% on DVS Gesture/SEW ResNet-34) while simultaneously reducing the perturbation magnitude (MSE) and event-ratio change (Δ). Against SpikeFool (Büchel et al. [6]) on binary grid representations, the proposed method achieves substantially higher ASR (e.g., 97.85% vs. 37.62% on DVS Gesture/SEW ResNet-34), as SpikeFool suffers from frequent vanishing gradients. Against Spike-Compatible (Liang et al. [26]) on binary grid representations, the proposed method is slightly lower on some settings (e.g., 95.45% vs. 96.98% on CIFAR10-DVS/SEW ResNet-34) but competitive overall (86.87–98.10% vs. 92.86–99.84%). Critically, Spike-Compatible and SpikeFool operate only on grid representations and cannot be applied when the model exposes only a raw-event interface, making the proposed method the only viable option in that deployment scenario.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial attack literature on Spiking Neural Networks from grid-based representations (continuous or binary) to raw event data in COO format. Prior methods such as SpikeFool, Spike-Compatible, and DVS-attacks all operate on intermediate grid representations, which the authors argue are often encapsulated within the vision pipeline and inaccessible to an external attacker. The key technical departure is the use of Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization with a straight-through estimator to convert the inherently discrete {-1, 1} event-value optimization into a continuous one, combined with a position-selection strategy that merges original and target-labeled event indices to shrink the optimization space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies a practical security gap: if a deployed SNN system only exposes a raw-event interface (as is common in modularized pipelines), all existing grid-based attack methods become inapplicable, leaving the system's vulnerability uncharacterized. The finding that soft (continuous) values yield 100% ASR while hard (binary) values drop to 53–87% (Table 5) highlights a significant discrete-to-continuous vulnerability gap in SNNs that has not been previously quantified. Limitations include evaluation on only 100 samples per dataset, two architectures, and three relatively small-scale datasets; open questions include transferability across event-camera resolutions, defense mechanisms specific to raw-event sparsity, and whether the position-selection strategy generalizes to higher-dimensional event streams.
