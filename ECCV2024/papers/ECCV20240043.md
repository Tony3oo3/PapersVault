## Problem

Existing egocentric full-body pose estimation methods from headset tracking suffer from three critical limitations: they use global world-space positions as network input, causing severe overfitting to the limited indoor motion-capture spaces where training data was recorded; they assume hands are always visible to the headset cameras, ignoring the intermittent tracking that occurs when hands leave the field of view; and they assume a single mean body shape, producing ground penetration and floating artifacts for users with different body dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240043/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 80 frames of 3-point tracking data (headset and two hands, each with 3D position and 6D orientation) from an HMD, with hand features zeroed out whenever the hand falls outside the headset's actual field of view based on relative hand-head geometry. After SlowFast fusion, the effective input sequence is 40 frames of 59 features each (decomposed positions, orientations, linear and angular velocities). The network outputs SMPL-H parameters—global root orientation, 22 local joint rotations, and 16 shape parameters β—which are then passed through differentiable forward kinematics to produce full-body joint positions anchored to the tracked head position.

## Method

EgoPoser combines four key components within a Transformer encoder–decoder architecture. First, a global motion decomposition applies temporal normalization (subtracting each joint's first-frame position over the window) and spatial normalization (subtracting only the head's horizontal translation while retaining vertical position) to make predictions invariant to the user's absolute location. Second, a realistic FoV model zeros hand input features based on the actual angular relationship between hand and headset rather than random masking. Third, a SlowFast module concatenates the last τ/2 frames at full rate with τ/2 frames sampled at stride 2, doubling the temporal context without increasing the quadratic self-attention cost. Fourth, shape-aware pose optimization implicitly regresses SMPL shape parameters β through joint-position errors from a differentiable SMPL forward-kinematics pass, with L1 regularization to encourage sparsity, eliminating the need for a separate calibration step. The full system is trained end-to-end with a composite L1 loss on orientation, rotation, position, and shape terms, achieving over 600 fps inference on a single GPU.

## Results

On the HPS dataset (large-scale real-world scenes), EgoPoser achieves the best MPJPE across all five test sequences, ranging from 6.90 cm (Go_Around) to 11.05 cm (MPI_EG), and the best MPJVE from 35.60 cm/s to 49.39 cm/s. The method uses only 4.12M parameters and 0.33G FLOPs, achieving over 600 fps inference. On the AMASS test set with position offsets up to 50 m, EgoPoser maintains a stable MPJPE of 4.14 cm and MPJVE of 25.95 cm/s, while global-input baselines degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoPoser outperforms all three primary baselines (AvatarPoser, AGRoL, AvatarJLM) on every HPS sequence for both MPJPE and MPJVE, even when those baselines are augmented with local normalization (the '-Improved' variants). The closest competitor is AvatarJLM-Improved, which on UG_Computers achieves 10.28 cm MPJPE versus EgoPoser's 10.25 cm, but EgoPoser pulls ahead by 4.3 cm on Go_Around (6.90 vs. 11.19) and by 4.9 cm on BIB_EG_Tour (9.55 vs. 14.80). In the position-offset robustness experiment (Fig. 8), all global-input methods (AvatarPoser, AGRoL, AvatarJLM) degrade sharply as the offset increases from 0 to 50 m, while EgoPoser maintains a near-constant 4.14 cm MPJPE, matching the stability of local-representation methods like CoolMoves and FinalIK. For the FoV robustness evaluation (Table 3), EgoPoser's realistic field-of-view modeling beats the random-masking strategy from FLAG [7] by 1.2–2.2 cm MPJPE across 180°, 120°, and 90° FoV settings, and also achieves lower MPJVE, indicating smoother predictions during hand visibility transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoPoser builds directly on the authors' own AvatarPoser (ECCV'22) and the broader line of sparse-sensor full-body pose estimation methods (AGRoL, AvatarJLM, MANIKIN), all of which map head-and-hand tracking to SMPL-H parameters. The key conceptual departure is from the universal practice in these prior methods of feeding global world-space positions into the network; EgoPoser identifies this as a critical generalization failure and replaces it with a temporal-spatial normalization decomposition. The SlowFast module repurposes the dual-rate sampling idea from video recognition (Feichtenhofer et al., 2019) for pose estimation, and the realistic FoV modeling supersedes FLAG's random-masking strategy by encoding actual geometric visibility constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoPoser makes full-body avatar estimation practical on commodity MR headsets (600+ fps, 4.12M parameters) without external mocap infrastructure, addressing three real deployment gaps: position invariance, intermittent hand tracking, and body-shape variation. The position-offset experiment reveals that prior methods silently overfit to the training location, a finding that should influence how future sparse-sensor pose models are designed and evaluated. Remaining limitations include the single-floor assumption (vertical position is retained as a feature), the absence of post-processing for physical plausibility, and the use of a straightforward Transformer backbone that leaves room for more expressive architectures.
