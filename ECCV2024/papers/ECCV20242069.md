## Problem

Existing sign language video generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: pose transfer approaches (e.g., DynaST) produce fine hand and facial details but suffer from poor temporal consistency (flickering, clothing changes between frames), while image animation approaches (e.g., MRAA, TPSMM) maintain temporal coherence but generate blurred, distorted, or structurally incorrect fine details in complex regions like hands and faces. No prior method simultaneously achieves both high-fidelity detail generation and frame-to-frame temporal consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source image (defining the person's appearance) and a driving video (defining the target motion) as input, both cropped and resized to 128×128. During training, five random source–driving frame pairs are sampled per video. A pre-trained HRNet-W32 pose estimator (36 COCO-WholeBody heatmaps) and a TPSMM-style keypoint detector (100 keypoints in 20 groups) extract pose and motion conditions respectively. The output is a sequence of reconstructed driving frames that preserve the source person's identity while performing the driving video's sign language motions.

## Method

PGMM decouples a sign language image into appearance, coarse-grained motion, and fine-grained detail, then reconstructs the driving frame via a generator that alternates two novel modules. The Coarse Motion Module (CMM) computes optical flow from TPSMM-style motion features, warps the input features to transfer large-scale structural motion, and concatenates the original features with the warped (occlusion-masked) features to preserve appearance while moving the body. The Pose Fusion Module (PFM) performs cross-attention with pose features as Query and image features as Key/Value, injecting fine-grained pose guidance (fingers, facial expressions) at each decoder stage. Training is supervised reconstruction with a composite loss: the TPSMM base loss (perceptual, equivariance, background, warp), a Pose Distance Loss that penalizes deviation between the generated frame's estimated keypoints and the target pose, and a Feature Alignment Loss that aligns intermediate decoder features from the source and driving paths to better separate appearance from motion in latent space.

## Results

On PHOENIX-2014T (test), PGMM achieves L1=0.01380, SSIM=0.9377, LPIPS=0.01883, FVD=136.472, TCD=0.113, and WER=39.7%, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On CSL-Daily (test), it reaches FVD=82.974 and WER=57.1%, compared to TPSMM's FVD=98.778 and WER=59.7%. On LSA64, PGMM attains the best L1 (0.01144), SSIM (0.9395), LPIPS (0.01638), FVD (154.726), and TCD (0.125). The most dramatic gains are in WER (39.7% vs. 55.2% for TPSMM) and FVD (136.472 vs. 209.297 for TPSMM on PHOENIX-2014T test), indicating substantially better semantic fidelity and video quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGMM outperforms all three baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Against TPSMM (the closest competitor and the motion-generation backbone), PGMM reduces FVD by 35% (136.472 vs. 209.297 on PHOENIX-2014T test) and WER by 15.5 percentage points (39.7% vs. 55.2%), while also improving L1, SSIM, LPIPS, and TCD. Against MRAA, the gap is even larger: FVD drops from 303.350 to 136.472 and WER from 70.7% to 39.7% on PHOENIX-2014T test, reflecting MRAA's severe blurriness in finger and facial details. Against DynaST (a pose-transfer GAN), PGMM achieves roughly 4.5× lower FVD (136.472 vs. 626.207) and 25.1 percentage points lower WER, confirming that the optical-flow-based motion decoupling in CMM resolves DynaST's temporal inconsistency (e.g., clothing changes between frames). The only noted weakness is that PGMM struggles to preserve hairstyle semantics in cross-person generation when source and driving images have very different hairstyles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established human image synthesis families—Pose Transfer (good details, poor temporal consistency) and Image Animation (good temporal consistency, poor details)—and explicitly builds on TPSMM's thin-plate-spline motion model as its optical flow backbone. The key architectural departure is the decoupling of generation into a Coarse Motion Module (optical flow warping for structural motion) and a Pose Fusion Module (cross-attention between pose and RGB features for fine-grained detail), which no prior SLVG method had combined in this manner. The paper also introduces the TCD metric as a new evaluation tool, extending the standard FVD/LPIPS/SSIM suite with a frame-level temporal consistency measure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-motivated gap in sign language video generation: the trade-off between hand/facial detail fidelity and inter-frame temporal coherence, both of which are critical for sign language comprehension. The TCD metric provides a simple, interpretable tool for assessing temporal consistency that could be adopted beyond sign language. However, the method operates at 128×128 resolution, cross-person generation still fails on large appearance differences (e.g., hairstyle), and the authors themselves note the need to extend this to full Sign Language Production (text-to-video) tasks, which remains an open and more ambitious direction.
