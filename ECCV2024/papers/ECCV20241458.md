## Problem

3D scene-level data is expensive and time-consuming to collect, creating a data desert that limits self-supervised pre-training for scene understanding. Existing shape-level SSL methods produce low-resolution global representations that transfer poorly to scene-level tasks requiring high-resolution point-level features, due to the large disparity in point quantities between shapes (~1K) and scenes (~100K–1M). The paper tackles how to learn scene-level representations from readily available shape data while preserving the high-resolution capacity needed for segmentation and detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241458/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, M individual shapes (each resampled to 2,048 points) are aggregated into a pseudo scene of M×2,048 points, then two rigid-transformed views are produced for contrastive learning. For downstream tasks, MH-P handles shape-level inputs (~1K–2K points) while MH-V handles scene-level inputs ranging from ~40K points (ScanNet v2) to ~956K points (S3DIS). The backbone outputs multi-scale high-resolution point-level semantic features (one per scale) that are concatenated and fed to a task-specific head for classification, per-point segmentation, or 3D bounding box prediction.

## Method

The core architecture is a stack of Multi-scale High-resolution (MH) Modules, each comprising subsampling (set abstraction or voxel averaging), local aggregation (shared MLPs or submanifold convolutions), and high-resolution mapping (nearest-neighbor interpolation or inverse-index assignment) that projects deep low-resolution semantics back to point-level resolution. This creates direct pathways from multi-scale deep features to high-resolution representations, unlike encoder-decoder or hourglass designs. The Shape-to-Scene Strategy (S2SS) bridges the shape-scene gap by randomly aggregating M shapes (rescaled to unit spheres, positioned with barycenter distance >2) into pseudo scenes, eliminating the need for real scene data. The Point-Point Contrastive Loss (PPC) exploits the natural correspondences inherent in S2SS—rigid transformations preserve point order and points from the same shape form positive pairs—removing the need for expensive FCGF-based point pairing. MH-V further replaces trilinear upsampling with index-based assignment to avoid blurring, and uses submanifold convolutions for local feature extraction at the voxel level.

## Results

On shape-level tasks, MH-PS (pre-trained on ShapeNet) achieves 93.8% OA on ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS and 94.1% on ModelNet40, while MH-PH (pre-trained on UHD+LHD) reaches 93.8% and 94.6% respectively. For part segmentation, MH-PH attains 87.6% instance mIoU on ShapeNetPart. On scene-level tasks, MH-VH achieves 74.1% mIoU on S3DIS Area5, 75.8% on ScanNet v2, 71.5% on SemanticKITTI, 84.2% on Synthia4D, and 43.9% mAP@0.5 on 3D object detection (ScanNet v2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ScanObjectNN PB_T50_RS, MH-PS (87.8%) outperforms all ShapeNet-pretrained baselines: PointGPT-S (86.9%), Point-M2AE (86.4%), Point-MAE (85.2%), MaskPoint (84.3%), and Point-BERT (83.1%), with gains of 0.9–4.7%. On ShapeNetPart, MH-PS achieves 87.1% instance mIoU, surpassing PointGPT-S (86.2%) and Point-M2AE (86.5%) despite using a simpler non-Transformer backbone. For scene-level semantic segmentation, MH-VH (74.1% on S3DIS, 75.8% on ScanNet v2) outperforms PointContrast (70.9%/74.1%) and DepthContrast (71.5%/71.2%) pre-trained on ScanNet, but falls slightly short of PointClustering with SR-UNet (73.2%/75.5%). On 3D object detection, MH-VH achieves 43.9% mAP@0.5, beating DepthContrast (42.9%), MaskPoint (42.1%), PointDif (43.7%), and PointContrast (38.0%). On outdoor segmentation, MH-VH leads on both SemanticKITTI (71.5% vs. PointDif's 71.3%) and Synthia4D (84.2% vs. PointContrast's 83.1%), confirming that shape-pretrained representations transfer effectively to large-scale scene understanding without requiring scene-level pre-training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PointContrast's point-level contrastive framework (PointInfoNCE loss) but departs from it in three fundamental ways: (1) replacing hourglass/encoder-only backbones (SR-UNet, MinkUNet) with the proposed MH-P/MH-V architectures that map multi-scale deep semantics to high-resolution point-level features; (2) eliminating the need for scene-level pre-training data by introducing the Shape-to-Scene (S2SS) strategy that aggregates individual shapes into pseudo-scenes; and (3) deriving positive/negative point pairs naturally from shape correspondences rather than relying on expensive FCGF-based pairing. It positions itself against both shape-level generative SSL methods (Point-BERT, Point-MAE, PointGPT) that ignore high-resolution features, and scene-level contrastive methods (PointContrast, DepthContrast, PointClustering) that require costly scene data for pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D scene understanding: the 'data desert' of large-scale annotated scene data. By demonstrating that pseudo-scenes assembled from readily available shape data can yield competitive (and sometimes superior) scene-level performance compared to pre-training on actual scene datasets like ScanNet, it opens a path toward scalable 3D SSL without expensive scanning infrastructure. The S2SS strategy is simple and could be adopted broadly. However, the authors acknowledge that extending S2S to open-world scene understanding and more complex 3D contexts remains an open challenge, and the current pseudo-scene construction (non-overlapping shapes on a unit sphere) is a significant simplification of real scene geometry.
