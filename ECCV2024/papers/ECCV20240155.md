## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods rely on agent tracks and static HD maps, missing dynamic visual cues such as pedestrian gazes, gestures, vehicle turn signals, and road conditions that significantly influence agent behavior. Prior vision-based approaches either crop only agent-local regions or process full images without explicit guidance on which semantic details matter, leading to sub-optimal exploitation of visual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes ego-centric surround-view camera images, per-agent past trajectories (as relative position deltas), agent type labels, and rasterized HD map segments. It outputs a Gaussian Mixture Model over M modes and T_f future time steps for every agent in the scene simultaneously (scene-centric, not per-agent). Notably, textual descriptions generated by a VLM+LLM pipeline are used exclusively as training-time supervision and are absent at inference, keeping the 53 ms latency unchanged.

## Method

VisionTrap encodes multi-view images into a composite BEV feature via a BEVDepth-style vision encoder, concatenates it with a CNN/FPN map feature, and then uses a deformable cross-attention module to selectively sample scene regions around each agent's predicted future positions (obtained from a recurrent auxiliary trajectory predictor) to augment per-agent state embeddings. A Text-driven Guidance Module applies an asymmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss that aligns each agent's scene-augmented embedding with its BERT-encoded textual description, while debiasing negative pairs by filtering out semantically similar sentences (cosine similarity > 0.8) and capping the number of negatives via top-k selection to stabilize optimization across variable scene densities. A Transformation Module standardizes agent orientation before the contrastive step to make embeddings of similarly-situated agents comparable. The final GMM trajectory decoder is trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of the main trajectory NLL, an auxiliary trajectory loss for the recurrent predictor, and the contrastive loss.

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset, VisionTrap (full model) achieves ADE₁₀=1.17, MR₁₀=0.32, and FDE₁=8.72 at 53 ms inference time for multi-agent prediction. This represents a 27.56% average improvement over its own baseline (ADE₁₀=1.48, MR₁₀=0.56, FDE₁=10.75 at 13 ms), with the Visual Semantic Encoder contributing 21.97% of the gain and the Text-driven Guidance Module adding a further 5.59%. The method matches or exceeds the miss-rate performance of all single-agent baselines (e.g., MR₁₀=0.32 vs. PGP's 0.37 and LAformer's 0.33) while running 2–4× faster than those single-agent methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionTrap outperforms all multi-agent baselines across every reported metric: it reduces ADE₁₀ from 1.51 (Trajectron++) and 1.45 (AgentFormer) to 1.17, and MR₁₀ from 0.57 to 0.32. Against single-agent methods, VisionTrap achieves a lower MR₁₀ (0.32) than PGP (0.37), P2T (0.46), MHA-JAM (0.46), and Multipath (0.74), and is competitive with LAformer (0.33). However, it trails the strongest single-agent methods on ADE₁₀ (1.17 vs. LAformer's 0.93 and PGP's 1.00) and FDE₁ (8.72 vs. PGP's 7.17). The critical advantage is speed: at 53 ms for all agents in the scene, VisionTrap is 2.2× faster than P2T (116 ms), 4× faster than PGP (215 ms), and 2.2× faster than LAformer (115 ms), making it the only method that combines multi-agent prediction with real-time operation while matching single-agent miss-rate performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisionTrap extends the trajectory prediction literature by incorporating surround-view camera images and VLM/LLM-generated textual descriptions as additional inputs, departing from the dominant paradigm that relies solely on agent trajectories and HD maps. Architecturally, it builds on BEVDepth for multi-view-to-BEV encoding and Deformable DETR's deformable cross-attention for scene-agent interaction, while introducing a novel text-driven guidance module that uses debiased multimodal contrastive learning (InfoNCE with false-negative filtering) to align visual features with textual semantics during training. The scene-centric design, combined with a Transformation Module for orientation standardization, positions it as an efficient alternative to agent-centric methods that scale poorly in dense scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides compelling qualitative and quantitative evidence that visual cues (gaze, turn signals, road conditions, pedestrian gestures) carry predictive information fundamentally inaccessible to trajectory-and-map-only models, and that textual supervision from VLMs can further guide the model toward exploiting these cues. The released nuScenes-Text dataset (1.2M descriptions for 391K objects) is a practical resource for the community. However, the authors explicitly frame the work as demonstrating the value of visual information rather than achieving SOTA, and open questions remain about generalization beyond nuScenes, the cost of the VLM+LLM annotation pipeline at scale, and whether the contrastive text guidance transfers to other driving datasets or sensor configurations.
