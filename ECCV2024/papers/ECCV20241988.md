## Problem

Current text-to-image generative models are tuned to appeal to a broad audience and produce unpersonalized outputs, forcing users into inefficient iterative prompt engineering to achieve their preferred results. Existing personalization methods rely on sparse feedback signals—binary likes/dislikes, image rankings, or representative image sets—that fail to capture the full complexity of an individual's visual taste, especially when multiple attributes (e.g., cool colors but pixel style) conflict within a single image.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt plus a one-time set of free-form user comments written on 8–50 diverse images (no fixed structure required). These comments are processed by a fine-tuned IDEFICS2-8b Visual Preference Extractor (VPE) into structured liked (VP+) and disliked (VP−) attributes spanning ~20 categories and ~500 predefined attributes, though the VPE can also extract novel attributes mentioned in comments. The output is a personalized image from Stable Diffusion, with a tunable β parameter (≤1) controlling personalization strength; the preference extraction is a one-time process requiring no further user feedback during generation.

## Method

ViPer operates in two stages. First, a Visual Preference Extractor (VPE) is fine-tuned from IDEFICS2-8b on a synthetic dataset of 5,000 simulated agents, each assigned random liked/disliked attribute sets with ≥85% Jaccard distance for diversity, and paired with GPT-4-generated free-form comments on images prompted by those attributes. Second, at generation time, VP+ and VP− are encoded with Stable Diffusion's text encoder and injected into the pipeline in two ways: (1) added to the prompt embedding as p = E(p) + β(E(VP+) − E(VP−)), and (2) a VP-specific classifier-free guidance term ε_vp = ε_θ(x_t, t, E(VP+)) − ε_θ(x_t, t, E(VP−)) is incorporated into the denoising update (Eq. 3), steering each diffusion step toward the user's preferences. No fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion itself is required, making the approach training-free at the generation stage and directly applicable to off-the-shelf models.

## Results

In user studies with 20 participants across 15 prompts, ViPer achieves 86.1% top-1 accuracy when users choose between their personalized image and 7 non-personalized images (vs. 12.5% random chance, a 6.89× improvement). When comparing against images personalized for other users, top-1 accuracy is 65.4% (5.23× random). The proposed proxy metric closely tracks actual user preferences, scoring 86.7% and 71.6% on the same two tests, validating it as a scalable evaluation tool.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViPer outperforms all five baselines on the proxy metric and user preference rankings. ZO-RankSGD [31] (iterative ranking) produces results nearly indistinguishable from unpersonalized Stable Diffusion, failing to capture individual taste. FABRIC [26] (binary like/dislike feedback) consistently converges toward a realistic, vibrant style that contradicts the test user's stated dislikes of 'Realism' and 'Vibrant Colors.' Textual Inversion [5] (user-provided reference images) struggles with diverse image sets and produces overly extreme textures and palettes. Fine-tuning Stable Diffusion yields some personalization but at the cost of noticeably degraded image quality. Prompt Personalization (LLM-rewritten prompts) is the closest competitor, achieving the second-highest proxy score, but it frequently drops key user attributes (e.g., 'Retro,' 'Creepy') and deviates from the original prompt's content. ViPer's advantage is that it requires no per-prompt user interaction, no fine-tuning, and no reference images—only a one-time comment capture—while maintaining prompt fidelity through the β-scaled embedding injection in Eq. (1) and the modified guidance in Eq. (3).

## Relation to Prior Work

ViPer builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and its classifier-free guidance mechanism, extending the standard CFG formula to incorporate a user-specific visual preference signal at both the embedding level (Eq. 1) and the denoising step (Eq. 3). It departs from prior personalization methods—Textual Inversion (reference-image-based), FABRIC (iterative binary feedback), and ZO-RankSGD (ranking oracles)—by using free-form user comments as the preference signal, processed through a fine-tuned VLM (IDEFICS2-8b) into structured visual attributes. This positions ViPer as a training-free, one-time-capture alternative to methods requiring iterative user interaction or per-user fine-tuning, while also distinguishing itself from RLHF-based alignment that targets general rather than individual preferences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free personalization via embedding manipulation and modified CFG is practically attractive: users need only comment on ~8 images once, and no per-user model weights are stored or fine-tuned. The introduction of a proxy metric (fine-tuned IDEFICS2-8b) that closely tracks human preference judgments (86.7% vs. 86.1% top-1) offers a scalable evaluation tool for the personalization literature. Key limitations remain: the 77-token text encoder bottleneck can drop preferred attributes, the ~500-attribute taxonomy may not cover all visual dimensions, and the method inherits Stable Diffusion's inability to render attributes outside its training distribution. Open questions include whether richer encoders or direct brain-signal proxies could further improve preference capture, and whether the approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion to other diffusion architectures.
