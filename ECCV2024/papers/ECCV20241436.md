## Problem

Existing collaborative perception methods assume all agents share the same perception model architecture, so intermediate features have identical sizes, channel counts, and semantic spaces. In practice, agents use different encoders (e.g., PointPillar vs. SECOND vs. VoxelNet), producing heterogeneous features that prior methods align via interpolation or convolution before fusion, causing irreversible information loss. The paper asks how to fuse such heterogeneous intermediate features without approximating or resampling them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241436/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent's LiDAR point cloud is voxelized and encoded into a BEV feature tensor of shape C×H×W, where C, H, and W differ across architectures (e.g., 256×50×176 for PointPillar at 0.4 m voxels vs. 256×66×234 at 0.3 m voxels). A binary spatial confidence map (shared parameters with the detection head) gates which feature points are transmitted to neighbors, and the relative ego–neb pose is used to warp neighbor features into the ego-centric frame. The final output is a per-agent detection tensor of shape H×W×7 encoding class, 3D center (x, y, z), length, and orientation (cos θ, sin θ).

## Method

Hetecooper constructs a feature collaboration graph whose nodes are the raw BEV feature points of ego and neighbors (no resampling), with edges linking ego–neb pairs whose corresponding real-world pillar regions overlap or whose center-point distance falls below a threshold. Each edge carries a three-component weight: the spatial-correlation ratio (overlap area / ego pillar area) and the two axis-aligned distance components (dx, dy). A graph-transformer fusion module then (i) maps neighbor nodes into the ego's channel and semantic space via a per-architecture semantic mapper with a channel aligner, (ii) adds sinusoidal positional encodings based on each node's distance from the ego center, and (iii) computes attention as the sum of a standard QK dot-product term and a correction term derived from the edge weights, so that nodes with greater spatial overlap receive higher attention. Multi-scale collaboration is achieved by building graphs at several scales and combining them with Split-attention, followed by cross-attention across all neighbors and a feed-forward layer. Training is two-stage: first homogeneous (all parameters learned jointly), then heterogeneous (encoders frozen; fusion module and detection head adapted per architecture), supervised by standard 3D detection loss.

## Results

On OPV2V, Hetecooper achieves the best AP@0.50 across all seven encoder configurations (both homogeneous and heterogeneous). In the most challenging heterogeneous setting (PointPillar ego / VoxelNet neb), Hetecooper reaches 0.822/0.724 (AP@0.50/AP@0.70), outperforming the next-best method MDPA (0.675/0.535) by 14.7 points in AP@0.50. Under homogeneous SECOND encoders, Hetecooper scores 0.863/0.759, leading Where2comm (0.838/0.783) by 2.5 points in AP@0.50 while trailing slightly in AP@0.70. The extensibility experiments (Table 4) further show that a model trained with one parameter setting generalizes to unseen feature sizes without retraining, maintaining AP@0.50 above 0.83 across all tested configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hetecooper outperforms all four baselines across every encoder configuration in Table 1. Against HM-ViT [31], Hetecooper gains 4.3–18.0 points in AP@0.50, with the largest margin (18.0 pts) in the VoxelNet homogeneous setting where HM-ViT drops to 0.632. Against MDPA [34], Hetecooper leads by 6.3–14.7 points in AP@0.50 under heterogeneous settings, confirming that the graph-based fusion avoids the information loss from interpolation/convolution used by MDPA's feature resizer. Against Where2comm [8], Hetecooper gains 4.3–14.3 points in AP@0.50 in heterogeneous scenarios, while in homogeneous settings the gap narrows to 2.5–4.5 points (Where2comm's spatial confidence map is shared by both methods). Against F-cooper [4], Hetecooper leads by 4.3–20.9 points in AP@0.50, with the largest gap in the VoxelNet/PointPillar heterogeneous case (0.816 vs 0.607). Notably, in the homogeneous SECOND setting, MDPA and Where2comm slightly exceed Hetecooper in AP@0.70 (0.783 vs 0.759), suggesting the graph structure's advantage is most pronounced under heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the intermediate-fusion collaborative perception line (Where2comm, V2VNet, F-cooper) to the heterogeneous-model setting, where prior work either assumed isomorphic encoders or resorted to interpolation/convolution-based feature alignment (MDPA, HEAL) that approximates and loses spatial information. The key architectural departure is replacing feature resizing with a feature collaboration graph whose nodes are the original feature points and whose edges encode spatial overlap and proximity, then performing fusion via a graph transformer with edge-weight-guided attention. The graph transformer design draws on Rampasek et al.'s recipe for positional encoding and efficient message passing, adapted here to use pillar-region geometry as the positional signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In real V2X deployments, vehicles will inevitably run different perception backbones, so a method that fuses heterogeneous BEV features without information loss and without retraining when minor parameter changes occur has direct practical value. The extensibility result (a model trained with one voxel-grid setting generalizing to others without adaptation) is a useful property for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two agents with very different architectures, how it behaves under significant (not just minor) parameter shifts, and whether the distance-threshold hyperparameter D can be learned rather than fixed. The evaluation is primarily on the simulated OPV2V dataset, with V2V4Real results relegated to the appendix, leaving the real-world robustness claim somewhat under-supported.
