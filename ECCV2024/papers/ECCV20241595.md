## Problem

Learning-based methods for garment reconstruction, virtual fitting, and generation are hindered by the absence of large-scale, open datasets of 3D garments paired with ground-truth sewing patterns. Existing synthetic datasets either lack sewing pattern labels, cover only a narrow set of garment styles, do not vary body shapes, or rely on proprietary simulators that limit reproducibility and scalability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes body shape meshes sampled from a PCA-based statistical model (47.5k triangles), parametric design parameters drawn from a probability-weighted GarmentCode design space, and one of three manually chosen fabric stiffness/softness profiles. Each of the 115,000 output samples is draped on both a random body and a canonical neutral body, yielding a 3D garment mesh (~30k triangles), a JSON-structured sewing pattern, panel and edge segmentation labels, a UV map aligned to initial panel shapes, 25 body measurements, the full design parameter vector, and basic visualization renders.

## Method

The generation pipeline has three stages: (1) automatic body measurement on 5,000 PCA-sampled body shapes using edge-loop initialization, ground-parallel measurement planes, convex-hull circumference extraction, and local-extremum plane shifting; (2) sewing pattern sampling from a curated GarmentCode design space where each parameter has a manually set default-value probability to enforce realistic style conventions, supplemented by quality-control filters that reject self-intersecting panels, over-length garments, and high-risk sliding designs; (3) GPU-parallel draping via an extended XPBD (Warp) simulator that introduces several novel mechanisms: global ray-intersect collision detection that excludes intersecting edges from self-collision prevention, semantic body-part-aware pull constraints that resolve inter-panel overlaps by pulling vertices toward their corresponding limb, a body-cloth constraint projecting interior garment vertices onto the nearest body surface, half-space attachment constraints (e.g., waist vertices must lie above the waistline plane) that are released after a fixed number of frames, and segmentation-based collision filters that discard physically implausible arm-skirt or internal-geometry contacts. The overall simulation success rate is approximately 72%, with batches of 5,000 designs processed in parallel on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

GarmentCodeData contains 115,000 made-to-measure garment samples across tops, shirts, dresses, jumpsuits, skirts, and pants, each fitted to a random body shape and a canonical neutral body, with ground-truth sewing patterns, 3D draped meshes, segmentation labels, UV maps, and body measurements. Automatic body measurements show mean overestimation of 2.81 cm (bust), 1.99 cm (hip), and 3.49 cm (leg circumference), and underestimation of 4.57 cm (shoulder width) relative to CAESAR ground truth. The draping pipeline achieves a ~72% simulation success rate, with generation of 5,000 designs taking ~7 hours on CPU and ~44 hours for 5,000 drapes on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a dataset paper, GarmentCodeData does not benchmark a method against competing algorithms on a standard task. Instead, it positions itself against prior 3D garment datasets: it provides 115,000 samples versus 23,500 in Korosteleva & Lee (NeurIPS 2021) and 563 in Deep Fashion3D, while uniquely supplying ground-truth sewing patterns alongside 3D meshes for every sample. Body measurement accuracy is validated against manually taken CAESAR anthropometric data, revealing that circumference overestimations (2–3.5 cm) do not negatively impact garment fitting, as demonstrated by successful retargeting of a fitted jumpsuit across extreme PCA body shapes. The ablation in Table 1 shows that the proposed pull-based collision resolution constraints reduce 95th-percentile self-collisions from 747 to 423 (neutral) and 535 to 346 (random bodies), and body-cloth 95th-percentile collisions from 82.8 to 52.2 (neutral) and 141 to 71.2 (random), confirming their net benefit despite occasional erroneous vertex-to-body-surface matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parametric sewing pattern framework of GarmentCode [14] and the garment dataset generation paradigm of Korosteleva & Lee [12] by introducing made-to-measure fitting to diverse body shapes, replacing the commercial CPU simulator with the open-source GPU-based Warp/XPBD engine [22], and adding a suite of collision-resolution and attachment-constraint mechanisms. The design space is refined with sampling probabilities and quality-control filters to avoid non-representative or physically implausible garments, while a new automatic body-measurement module (based on a CAESAR-derived statistical body model) replaces the single-body assumption of prior work. The result is a 5× larger dataset (115k vs. 23.5k) with body-shape variation, pose variation, and three material types that were absent in the closest prior dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GarmentCodeData fills a critical gap: no prior open dataset provided large-scale, made-to-measure 3D garments paired with ground-truth sewing patterns, which is the bottleneck for learning-based sewing-pattern reconstruction, virtual fitting, and garment generation. The open-source draping pipeline (Warp-based, with collision filters, half-space attachment constraints, and body-cloth pull constraints) is itself a reusable contribution for anyone building garment simulation at scale. Key limitations remain: the body model is biased toward European/North-American adults, the design space lacks fabric layers, sharp pleats, and hardware (zippers, buttons), and ~28% of simulation attempts fail, pointing to unresolved challenges in robust body-cloth collision resolution and initial pattern placement.
