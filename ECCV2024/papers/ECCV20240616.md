## Problem

Existing unsupervised homography estimation methods rely solely on cross-modal intensity-based learning, which fails to converge under large modality gaps (e.g., satellite vs. map images) combined with large geometric offsets. No prior approach could achieve effective unsupervised homography estimation on the GoogleMap dataset under [-32,+32] pixel offsets on 128×128 images, a regime where even supervised methods struggle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240616/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SCPNet takes a pair of 128×128 cross-modal or cross-spectral images (satellite/map, flash/no-flash, RGB/NIR, multispectral bands) and outputs a homography matrix parameterized as four corner-point offsets in a 2×2×2 tensor. During training, each image is additionally warped by a simulated homography to create intra-modal self-supervised pairs, and a consistent feature map projector maps each image to a 1-channel intensity-invariant latent space before cross-modal supervision is applied. At inference, only the cross-modal prediction branch is active.

## Method

SCPNet introduces intra-modal self-supervised learning, which applies simulated homography deformations within each modality separately to provide direct supervision on a weight-shared network, enabling homography knowledge to generalize from intra-modal to cross-modal settings. The architecture replaces simple channel concatenation with a correlation-based design: a weight-shared feature extractor produces F_A and F_B, their local inner-product correlation C(x,r)=ReLU(F_A(x)ᵀF_B(x+r)) is computed over a radius-R neighborhood, and this correlation volume is decoded by a homography estimator. A consistent feature map projector (conv + two residual blocks + 1×1 conv) maps images to a 1-channel intensity-invariant latent space, enabling a normalized L1 cross-modal loss that simultaneously supervises homography estimation and enforces latent-space consistency. Training combines three branches—two intra-modal self-supervised branches (L1 on corner offsets) and one cross-modal branch (normalized L1 on projected feature maps)—all applied simultaneously to the shared parameters with λ=0.1 weighting, using AdamW at lr=4×10⁻⁴ for 120k iterations.

## Results

On the most challenging cross-modal dataset GoogleMap (satellite-map pairs, [-32,+32] offset on 128×128 images), SCPNet achieves a mean MACE of 4.35, outperforming the supervised MHN by 14.0% (5.06) and DHN by 37.2% (6.93), while all other unsupervised methods either fail to converge or score above 21. On Flash/no-flash, SCPNet reaches 2.67 MACE, beating MHN (5.24) by 49.0%. On cross-spectral datasets, SCPNet achieves 4.00 MACE on Harvard (beating MHN by 25.2%) and 4.78 on RGB/NIR (beating MHN by 36.4%). On PDS-COCO, SCPNet leads biHomE by 58.2% under maximum distortion and 59.5% under minimum distortion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCPNet decisively outperforms all unsupervised baselines (UDHN, CA-UDHN, biHomE, BasesHomo, UMF-CMGR) on every dataset, with the gap being most dramatic on GoogleMap where competing unsupervised methods score 21–25 MACE versus SCPNet's 4.35. Against the supervised MHN, SCPNet achieves lower MACE on all five datasets (14.0% on GoogleMap, 49.0% on Flash/no-flash, 25.2% on Harvard, 36.4% on RGB/NIR, and 10.7% on PDS-COCO), and similarly beats DHN by 37–60% across datasets. Handcrafted methods (SIFT, ORB, DASC, RIFT) are consistently 3–6× worse than SCPNet in MACE. The only baselines that beat SCPNet are the supervised LocalTrans, IHN, and RHWF on Flash/no-flash and PDS-COCO, which benefit from iterative and multi-scale refinement architectures that SCPNet does not employ. Notably, biHomE fails to converge entirely on GoogleMap and Harvard, while SCPNet maintains stable performance under the same conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCPNet extends the unsupervised deep homography estimation line initiated by UDHN (photometric loss) and CA-UDHN (feature-space similarity) by introducing intra-modal self-supervised learning as an additional supervision signal: simulated homography is applied within each modality to provide direct, ground-truth-based training for a weight-shared network. Architecturally, it departs from prior concatenation-based designs by explicitly decomposing the network into a weight-shared feature extractor, a correlation (inner-product) module, and a homography estimator, and adds a consistent feature map projector that maps images into an intensity-invariant latent space before cross-modal supervision. The combination of correlation and consistent feature map projection as a unified unsupervised architecture had not been previously investigated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCPNet is the first method to achieve effective unsupervised homography estimation on satellite–map pairs (GoogleMap) under large offsets, outperforming the supervised MHN by 14% in MACE, which is practically significant for GPS-denied navigation and multi-spectral fusion where ground-truth correspondences are unavailable. The core finding—that intra-modal self-supervised learning alone can drive convergence while cross-modal intensity-based learning alone cannot—suggests a generalizable training strategy for other cross-modal registration problems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-scale or iterative refinement architectures (which supervised methods like IHN and RHWF exploit to close the remaining gap), and whether the technique transfers to non-homography geometric tasks.
