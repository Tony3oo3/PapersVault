## Problem

In few-shot class-incremental learning (FSCIL), existing methods that improve transferability to new classes via feature spreading (e.g., self-supervised contrastive loss, low-temperature cross-entropy) simultaneously degrade base-class discriminability, particularly when classification relies on feature-average class prototypes. Prior work assumed maximizing inter-class distance was beneficial for reserving space for new classes, but this paper identifies that this assumption is actually counterproductive for the discriminability–transferability trade-off specific to FSCIL.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 84×84 for miniImageNet, 224×224 for CUB200) and outputs a class label via cosine similarity between the extracted feature and a class prototype. The feature extractor is trained exclusively on base classes and then frozen; at inference, each class is represented by the mean feature of its (few) training samples, and prediction is the nearest prototype. No gradient updates occur during incremental sessions, so the entire burden of handling new classes falls on the quality of the base-trained representation.

## Method

CLOSER trains a ResNet feature extractor on base classes using a three-term objective: (1) softmax cross-entropy with a low temperature (τ=1/32) to encourage feature spreading, (2) a self-supervised contrastive (infoNCE) loss on augmented views to further promote intra-class spread and shareable low/mid-level features, and (3) a novel inter-class distance minimization loss that maximizes the average cosine similarity between features of different classes, effectively compressing the overall representation space. The key counterintuitive insight is that reducing inter-class distance—rather than the conventional practice of maximizing it—regularizes intra-class spread (preserving prototype-based discriminability) while promoting feature sharing across classes (improving transferability). The paper provides theoretical justification via information bottleneck theory, showing that the joint objective increases the lower bound of I(Y;Z)/I(X;Z) by raising intra-class covariance determinants while suppressing the overall covariance determinant. After base-session training, the feature extractor is frozen and classification uses cosine similarity to feature-average class prototypes, with new-class prototypes computed from just K samples per class in each incremental session.

## Results

On CUB200 (10-way 5-shot), CLOSER achieves 63.58% whole-class accuracy (A_W) with a performance drop (PD) of 15.82%, outperforming the best prior method SAVC (62.50%, PD 19.35%) by 1.08% in A_W and 3.53% in PD. On CIFAR100 (5-way 5-shot), CLOSER reaches 53.32% A_W with 22.40% PD, the best on both metrics among all compared methods. On miniImageNet (5-way 5-shot), CLOSER attains 53.33% A_W (best) with 22.69% PD, achieving a better balance than FACT which has the lowest PD (22.07%) but only 50.49% A_W.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLOSER achieves state-of-the-art performance on both CUB200 and CIFAR100, surpassing all 16 baselines on both A_W and PD simultaneously. On CUB200, the closest competitor SAVC [40] reaches 62.50% A_W with 19.35% PD, while CLOSER improves A_W by 1.08% and reduces PD by 3.53%; ALICE [33] and CABD [49] are next closest on PD (17.30% and 18.19%) but trail CLOSER by 3.48% and 2.65% in A_W respectively. On CIFAR100, CLOSER (53.32%, 22.40%) edges out FACT [51] (52.10%, 22.50%) and GKEAL [54] (51.40%, 22.61%) on both metrics. On miniImageNet, CLOSER achieves the highest A_W (53.33%) but does not match the lowest PD of FACT [51] (22.07% vs. 22.69%), indicating a slightly different trade-off point on this dataset. Notably, CLOSER achieves these gains without relying on sample storage (unlike F2M, ERDIL, IDLVQ-C, CABD), additional computational modules (unlike CEC, CLOM, NC-FSCIL, SAVC, MetaFSCIL), or test-time data augmentation (unlike S3C, SAVC), attributing its advantage purely to representation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLOSER operates within the standard FSCIL formulation established by Tao et al. and Zhang et al. (CEC), where the feature extractor is frozen after the base session and a non-parametric class-prototype classifier is used. It builds on the representation-spreading literature (Chen et al.'s contrastive learning, Kornblith et al.'s temperature-transferability link) to improve transferability, but departs sharply from the prevailing FSCIL practice of maximizing inter-class distance (Hersche et al., Zhou et al., Yang et al.) by instead minimizing it. The key conceptual departure is the claim that compressing the inter-class space, when combined with intra-class spreading, yields a better discriminability-transferability Pareto front than the widely adopted strategy of pushing classes apart to 'reserve space' for future classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By challenging the near-universal assumption in FSCIL that larger inter-class separation is beneficial, this work reframes how representation learning should be approached for incremental settings with fixed extractors. The information bottleneck analysis provides a principled justification (increase intra-class variability while suppressing overall feature-space spread) that could inform representation design beyond FSCIL. Limitations include the restriction to classification tasks and the fixed-extractor formulation, which precludes continual representation updates; the stability-plasticity trade-off when allowing updates remains open. Whether the 'closer is better' principle generalizes to other incremental or few-shot settings with different classifier architectures is an interesting open question.
