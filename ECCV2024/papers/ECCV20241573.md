## Problem

Existing 3D feature distillation methods (LERF, LangSplat) inherit CLIP's compositional generalization weakness, failing to correctly localize compound queries like 'bag of cookies' or 'paper napkin' because they treat each word as an independent concept. Additionally, these methods require expensive multi-scale evaluation at inference (30 scales for LERF, 3 for LangSplat), creating a linear computational overhead per query. No prior approach encodes multi-scale scene semantics within a single unified feature field with a mechanism to aggregate across scales without explicit scale selection.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses, SAM class-agnostic segments, and CLIP vision-encoder embeddings of each segment as teacher signals. At inference, given a single text query and a camera viewpoint, the method renders one 512-dimensional composite feature map (e.g., 730×987×512) from the 3DGS model and computes a single relevance map via dot-product with the CLIP text embedding—no iterative scale search is needed. The 3D variant queries point features directly at Gaussian centers rather than rendering a 2D map.

## Method

N2F2 augments a 3DGS scene representation with a single 512-dimensional feature field (TriPlane + 3-layer MLP) whose dimensions are hierarchically organized: lower dimensions encode coarse-scale scene structure while higher dimensions encode fine-grained details. Training uses scale-aware hierarchical supervision—SAM segments are lifted to 3D via 3DGS depth, their physical scales quantized into D bins, and each feature dimension is supervised by the CLIP embedding of segments at the corresponding scale through a learnable projection matrix that maps variable-length feature subsets to the full D dimensions. At inference, a composite embedding is computed as a weighted sum of all scale-aware features, where the weights are pre-computed once from canonical phrases ('object', 'stuff', 'thing', 'part', 'texture') via a softmax over scale-specific feature similarities, yielding a single relevance map per query that implicitly handles compound expressions by aggregating responses across relevant scales. Deferred volumetric rendering (rendering TriPlane features first, then applying the MLP) is used during training for memory efficiency without sacrificing performance.

## Results

On the expanded LERF dataset, N2F2 achieves 54.4% mIoU for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation, outperforming LangSplat (51.4%) and LERF (37.4%) by 3.0 and 17.0 points respectively. For 3D localization, N2F2 reaches 88.6% overall accuracy versus 84.3% for LangSplat and 73.6% for LERF. On the 3D-OVS dataset, N2F2 achieves 93.9% mIoU, marginally surpassing LangSplat (93.4%) in a highly saturated setting. The composite embedding strategy also yields a 1.7× speedup over LangSplat and 26× over LERF during inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

N2F2 consistently outperforms all baselines on the expanded LERF dataset, where compound queries (e.g., 'bag of cookies', 'paper napkin') are the primary challenge. Against LERF [22], N2F2 gains 17.0 mIoU points (54.4 vs. 37.4) on segmentation and 15.0 points on localization (88.6% vs. 73.6%), largely because LERF's global CLIP features lack precise localization and it does not use mask supervision. Against LangSplat [43], N2F2 improves segmentation by 3.0 mIoU (54.4 vs. 51.4) and localization by 4.3 points (88.6% vs. 84.3%), with the largest gains on compound queries where LangSplat's 3-scale hierarchy is insufficient. On the 3D-OVS dataset, where scenes contain objects of similar scales and results are highly saturated, N2F2 edges out LangSplat by 0.5 mIoU (93.9 vs. 93.4), winning on 4 of 6 scenes. The LSeg baseline [28] is substantially outperformed across all metrics, confirming the advantage of CLIP-based language-aligned features over pixel-aligned segmentation features. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that N2F2's composite embedding outperforms explicit scale selection (54.4 vs. 53.7 mIoU) while being ~5× faster, and even the k=170 setting (3 scales, closest to LangSplat) still beats LangSplat by 1.3 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

N2F2 sits at the intersection of 3D feature field distillation (LERF, LangSplat, FFD) and hierarchical representation learning (Matryoshka Representation Learning). It departs from LERF's scale-conditioned NeRF and LangSplat's fixed 3-scale 3DGS by encoding all scales within a single feature field via nested dimension subsets, supervised by SAM segments lifted to 3D and CLIP embeddings. The composite embedding for inference is a novel departure from the explicit multi-scale search used by both LERF and LangSplat, drawing instead on query-agnostic weighting over canonical phrases to aggregate relevance across scales in one pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hierarchical supervision within a unified feature field can simultaneously improve accuracy on compound queries (e.g., 'bag of cookies') and reduce inference cost, addressing two practical pain points in open-vocabulary 3D understanding. However, the authors acknowledge a clear limitation: the method struggles with global scene-context queries (e.g., 'wooden desk in the corner of the room'), leaving open the question of how to integrate broad spatial/relational reasoning with the fine-grained object-level hierarchy it captures. The nested-dimension design is a reusable inductive bias that could transfer to other multi-scale 3D representation tasks beyond language-conditioned segmentation.
