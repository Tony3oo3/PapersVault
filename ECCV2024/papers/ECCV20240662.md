## Problem

Existing image-to-3D methods that leverage view-conditioned diffusion models produce plausible geometry but suffer from texture deficiencies in novel views, including multi-view inconsistency and quality degradation, largely because these models are trained on small-scale synthetic 3D datasets. The core challenge is simultaneously enhancing texture quality and maintaining cross-view consistency without the slow, over-smoothing SDS optimization that dominates current pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240662/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image with an alpha mask as input and produces a UV-textured 3D mesh (extracted from 3D Gaussian Splatting) in approximately 2 minutes on a single A100 GPU. The fine stage progressively samples 8 camera views at 40-degree angular intervals, bidirectionally from the reference view, and optimizes a texture map in UV space rather than directly optimizing volumetric or Gaussian parameters for texture.

## Method

Repaint123 uses a two-stage framework: a coarse stage that optimizes 3D Gaussian Splatting with SDS loss (view-conditioned diffusion) plus reference reconstruction loss in ~1 minute, followed by a fine stage that progressively repaints invisible content in novel views using a view-independent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) with DDIM inversion. The key mechanism is a visibility-based adaptive repainting mask that binarizes a per-pixel visibility map (computed from depth alignment and normal-camera-ray dot products) at each denoising timestep, so visible regions are refined with strength proportional to the change in viewing obliqueness while invisible regions receive full repainting. Multiple control techniques mitigate accumulated bias: ControlNet conditions on coarse depth maps for geometry, attention injection replaces novel-view Key/Value features with reference-view features for texture transfer, and IP-Adapter provides CLIP-encoded image prompts for classifier-free guidance. After obtaining high-quality repainted images, the method uses simple MSE loss (instead of SDS) to efficiently optimize the UV texture map, achieving a >10× speedup over NeRF-based baselines while producing more detailed and consistent textures.

## Results

On both RealFusion15 and test-alpha datasets, Repaint123 achieves the best multi-view consistency scores: CLIP-Similarity of 0.85/0.88 and Contextual Distance of 1.55/1.50, outperforming all NeRF-based and Gaussian-Splatting-based baselines. It also attains the lowest LPIPS (0.101/0.048), indicating superior perceptual texture quality. On reference-view PSNR, Repaint123 (19.00/22.38) trails Magic123 (19.68/24.69) and Zero-123-XL* (19.56/24.39), which the authors attribute to the immaturity of Gaussian Splatting for reference-view reconstruction. The entire pipeline completes in ~2 minutes on a single A100 GPU, matching DreamGaussian's speed while substantially improving texture quality and consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the NeRF-based baselines, Repaint123 wins decisively on multi-view consistency: it surpasses Magic123 (the strongest NeRF baseline) by 0.03 CLIP-Similarity and 0.07 Contextual Distance on test-alpha, and by 0.03/0.04 on RealFusion15. It also outperforms Zero-123-XL* on CLIP-Similarity (0.88 vs 0.84) and Contextual Distance (1.50 vs 1.52) on test-alpha, while matching or exceeding it on LPIPS. On reference-view PSNR, Repaint123 (22.38) trails Magic123 (24.69) and Zero-123-XL* (24.39) on test-alpha, a gap the authors attribute to Gaussian Splatting's weaker reference-view reconstruction compared to NeRF. Against the only other Gaussian-Splatting baseline, DreamGaussian, Repaint123 wins on all four quality metrics (e.g., CLIP-Sim 0.88 vs 0.79, LPIPS 0.048 vs 0.057 on test-alpha) while matching its 2-minute runtime. RealFusion and Make-it-3D are clearly outperformed across all metrics, with Make-it-3D showing particularly poor LPIPS (0.237 on test-alpha) and long optimization time (1 hour).

## Relation to Prior Work

Repaint123 sits at the intersection of two lines of work: SDS-based 3D generation (DreamFusion, RealFusion, Magic123) and fast 3D representations (3D Gaussian Splatting, DreamGaussian). It departs from the dominant SDS paradigm by replacing it in the fine stage with a progressive repainting strategy that uses a view-independent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) to synthesize invisible content, guided by depth (ControlNet), reference-view attention features (inspired by HiFi-123), and CLIP-based image prompts (IP-Adapter). The key conceptual departure is that after progressively repainting novel views with multi-view consistency, a simple MSE loss suffices for texture optimization, eliminating the need for expensive SDS in the refinement stage. The visibility-based adaptive refinement, which modulates repainting strength by the cosine of the viewing angle, is a novel contribution not present in prior repainting or super-resolution work applied to 3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-minute generation time (roughly 10× faster than NeRF-based baselines) makes high-quality image-to-3D generation practical for interactive applications, and the progressive repainting strategy offers a general recipe for balancing texture quality against multi-view consistency that could transfer to other 3D generation pipelines. However, the method inherits limitations from 3D Gaussian Splatting in the coarse stage—mesh extraction artifacts (holes) and inferior reference-view reconstruction compared to NeRF-based methods—which the authors acknowledge as an open problem tied to the maturity of Gaussian Splatting for generation tasks. Whether the progressive repainting approach scales to more complex multi-object scenes or requires the current 8-view, 40-degree sampling schedule remains unexplored.
