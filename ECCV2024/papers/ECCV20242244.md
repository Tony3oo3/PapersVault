## Problem

Fusing event camera and frame-based camera data for object detection remains an open challenge because existing fusion strategies (concatenation, single-modality attention, post-processing) fail to fully exploit the complementary strengths of the two modalities. In particular, a feature-modality imbalance arises: in daytime scenes event features struggle with remote small targets, while in nighttime scenes RGB features degrade due to poor lighting, and prior methods do not explicitly address this misalignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242244/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes two inputs: an event stream encoded as a voxel grid (bilinear interpolation over B time bins, treated as channels for 2D convolution) and a synchronized RGB frame. On DSEC the event camera is 640×480 and the RGB camera is a separate sensor aligned via homographic transformation; on PKU-DDD17-Car both come from a single 346×260 DAVIS sensor. The output is a set of bounding boxes with class labels (cars, pedestrians, large vehicles on DSEC; vehicles on PKU-DDD17-Car) produced by a RetinaNet head with 9 anchors per spatial location.

## Method

The architecture is a dual-stream ResNet-50 backbone (one branch per modality) with Cross-Modality Adaptive Feature Refinement (CAFR) modules inserted between each pair of residual blocks, followed by an FPN and RetinaNet detection head. Each CAFR module operates in two stages: (1) a Bidirectional Cross-Modality Interaction (BCI) stage that applies 1×1 convolutions, pixel-wise multiplication for coarse spatial-channel attention, and then a cross-self-attention where queries and keys are drawn from one modality while values come from the other, enabling bidirectional information bridging; (2) a Two-Fold Adaptive Feature Refinement (TAFR) stage that aligns the channel-level mean and variance of the two refined feature maps using an AdaIN-style normalization (borrowed from style transfer) before concatenation, with all affine parameters computed adaptively from the inputs rather than learned. This coarse-to-fine design—first bridging information across modalities, then harmonizing their statistical distributions—directly targets the feature imbalance that degrades detection in challenging lighting conditions.

## Results

On the high-resolution DSEC dataset, CAFR achieves 38.0% average mAP, surpassing the second-best method EFNet (30.0%) by 8.0% and the best RGB-D method SPNet (27.7%) by 10.3%. On the low-resolution PKU-DDD17-Car dataset, CAFR reaches 86.7% mAP50 and 46.0% mAP, outperforming all fusion baselines. Under 15 corruption types at 5 severity levels, CAFR achieves 69.5% mPC50, substantially exceeding the frames-only baseline (38.7%) and the next-best fusion method ECANet (67.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAFR outperforms all 20 baselines across both datasets. On DSEC, the largest gap is against EFNet (30.0% mAP), the closest RGB-E competitor, with CAFR leading by 8.0%; against the best attention module (SENet, 26.2%) and best RGB-D method (SPNet, 27.7%), the margins are 11.8% and 10.3% respectively. On PKU-DDD17-Car, CAFR (86.7% mAP50, 46.0% mAP) beats the strongest fusion baselines FAGC (84.8%, 42.4%) and SPNet (84.7%, 43.3%) by 1.9–3.6% mAP50 and 2.7–3.6% mAP. Single-modal baselines lag far behind: events-only achieves 12.0% mAP on DSEC and 46.5% mAP50 on PKU-DDD17-Car, while frames-only reaches 25.0% and 82.7% respectively. Under corruption, CAFR (69.5% mPC50) exceeds the next-best fusion method ECANet (67.1%) by 2.4% and the frames-only RetinaNet (38.7%) by 30.8%, with the largest per-category advantage in the weather group (70.6% vs. 28.5% for frames-only). The ablation confirms that the dual-branch design with both CrossAtt and TAFR is necessary: removing either component drops mAP by 0.7–2.4% on DSEC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-frame fusion literature for object detection by introducing a dual-branch coarse-to-fine architecture that combines cross-self-attention with AdaIN-inspired feature statistics alignment. It departs from prior fusion approaches—simple concatenation (FPN-Fusion), single-modal spatial attention (FAGC), and cross-attention without statistics normalization (RENet, EFNet)—by explicitly addressing the 'feature-modality imbalance' problem through a two-fold refinement: bidirectional cross-modality interaction for information bridging, followed by channel-level mean/variance alignment borrowed from the style transfer domain (Huang & Belongie, ICCV 2017). The detection backbone (RetinaNet with FPN) and event representation (voxel grid) follow standard practice, so the novelty is concentrated in the CAFR fusion module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.0% mAP improvement on DSEC and the large robustness gap (69.5% vs 38.7% mPC under 15 corruption types) demonstrate that principled feature-level fusion with statistics alignment can substantially outperform both single-modal detection and prior fusion strategies, particularly under degraded conditions. The repurposing of AdaIN-style normalization as a modality-balancing mechanism is a creative and potentially transferable idea for other multi-modal fusion tasks beyond event-frame detection. Limitations include evaluation restricted to driving scenarios on two datasets, synthetic corruption data, and the absence of real-time inference benchmarks; whether the statistics alignment component generalizes to other sensor pairs (e.g., LiDAR-camera, thermal-RGB) remains an open question.
