## Problem

Large-scale 3D mapping with implicit neural representations requires storing local features in a feature volume, but existing discrete representation methods (e.g., VQ-AD) must explore the entire 2^B embedding space during training, causing prohibitive memory usage that prevents practical use at scale. The paper addresses how to represent local features in a storage- and memory-efficient manner while preserving mapping quality in large-scale outdoor environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241932/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential LiDAR point clouds with known poses and produces a learned signed distance function (SDF) over 3D space, extractable as an explicit mesh via marching cubes at 10 cm voxel resolution. Internally, the feature volume stores B-bit binary composition indicators (rather than full D-dimensional continuous vectors) at octree voxel corners, plus a small set of low-resolution continuous embeddings at the 0-level octree for global location context. The spatial structure is an octree of height L=3 with 0.2 m smallest voxel size, with voxel locations encoded as Morton codes.

## Method

DNMap replaces the full discrete embedding space with a decomposition into B+1 shared component vectors (one bias and B offsets), where each voxel corner stores only a B-bit binary composition indicator specifying which offsets to include. A key reformulation splits each offset into two sub-vectors (Δe_j^0 and Δe_j^1) so that gradients flow to all component vectors regardless of the indicator value, avoiding the dead-gradient problem of naive composition. Binary composition indicators are made differentiable via a straight-through estimator using a sigmoid relaxation. An efficient implementation interpolates the binary indicator vectors at query points before applying the linear composition (Eq. 15), reducing redundant computation since query points per batch are far fewer than total voxels. Low-resolution continuous embeddings at the 0-level octree (only ~4% of total voxels) supplement the discrete features with global positional information, and all parameters are jointly optimized with a binary cross-entropy SDF loss plus Eikonal regularization.

## Results

On MaiCity, DNMap (8-bit) achieves an F-score of 89.56 with 9.80 cm Chamfer-L1 distance, outperforming SHINE-Mapping (F-score 89.23, 10.47 cm) while using only 8.5% of its feature storage (1,337 kB vs. 15,775 kB). On Newer College, DNMap (8-bit) reaches F-score 93.60 and 6.68 cm accuracy, surpassing SHINE-Mapping (92.96, 7.42 cm) in all metrics except completeness. Notably, VQ-SHINE-Mapping fails to train at 8-bit on Newer College due to out-of-memory, while DNMap-discrete (8-bit) completes training in 37 min using only 3% of SHINE-Mapping's feature storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SHINE-Mapping (continuous embeddings), DNMap (8-bit) wins on accuracy (3.14 vs. 3.19 cm), completeness (16.46 vs. 17.75 cm), Chamfer-L1 (9.80 vs. 10.47 cm), and F-score (89.56 vs. 89.23) on MaiCity, while reducing feature storage from 15,775 kB to 1,337 kB (8.5%). On Newer College, DNMap again beats SHINE-Mapping in accuracy (6.68 vs. 7.42 cm) and Chamfer-L1 (8.67 vs. 8.94 cm) with a higher F-score (93.60 vs. 92.96), though completeness is slightly worse (10.64 vs. 10.47 cm). Against VQ-SHINE-Mapping (discrete, indexing-based), DNMap-discrete (8-bit) achieves substantially better completeness (12.42 vs. 15.38 cm on MaiCity) and Chamfer-L1 (8.25 vs. 9.79 cm) with comparable F-score (87.63 vs. 87.90), and trains approximately 1.4× faster (30 min vs. 43 min). Critically, VQ-SHINE-Mapping cannot be trained at 8-bit on Newer College due to out-of-memory, whereas DNMap-discrete (8-bit) completes training successfully, demonstrating the practical advantage of the decomposition strategy over full embedding-space indexing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SHINE-Mapping [34], which established the octree-based sparse feature volume framework for large-scale 3D mapping with implicit neural representations. It departs from VQ-AD [27] (Variable Bitrate Neural Fields), which compresses feature volumes via indexing into a full 2^B discrete embedding space, by replacing indexing with a decomposition strategy: each embedding is composed from a small set of B+1 shared component vectors selected by binary indicators. This shift from indexing to composition is the core conceptual departure, motivated by the prohibitive training memory cost of exploring the entire embedding space in VQ-AD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition strategy offers a practical path to storage-efficient large-scale 3D mapping: DNMap achieves comparable or better mapping quality than SHINE-Mapping using only ~8.5% of the feature storage, and avoids the out-of-memory failures that plague VQ-SHINE-Mapping at higher bitwidths. The paper acknowledges a key limitation—difficulty representing thin surfaces—which remains an open problem for implicit representations in general. Future work will need to balance local structural detail with the memory constraints of large-scale environments, and it is unclear whether the decomposition approach generalizes beyond LiDAR-based outdoor mapping to other sensor modalities or indoor settings.
