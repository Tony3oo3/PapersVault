## Problem

Cross-domain few-shot learning (CD-FSL) for egocentric action recognition faces two unresolved challenges: (1) the extreme domain gap between source (e.g., daily life) and target (e.g., industrial, outdoor sports) domains, where RGB-only representations are highly susceptible to background and appearance shifts, and (2) the high computational cost of processing densely-sampled video tokens with Transformer attention, which precludes deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. Prior CD-FSL methods with unlabeled target data neither exploit complementary modalities to bridge the domain gap nor reduce inference cost.

## Input / Output

During meta-training, the method consumes multimodal video inputs—RGB frames, optical flow, and 2D hand pose heatmaps—each as T frames of size H_m × W_m × C_m, tokenized and processed by a ViT-S encoder. During meta-testing (few-shot training and inference), only RGB video clips are used, making the approach deployable without auxiliary sensor hardware. The output is a single action class label per query clip, produced by averaging Softmax probabilities over P independently masked forward passes of the same input.

## Method

MM-CDFSL operates in four stages. First, a VideoMAE encoder-decoder is trained independently per modality with a joint objective combining source and target reconstruction losses plus a source-only cross-entropy classification loss, using Tube Masking to learn domain-adapted and class-discriminative features. Second, multimodal distillation transfers knowledge from frozen per-modality teacher encoders to a single student RGB encoder using L2 feature distillation with per-modality projection layers, trained exclusively on unlabeled target data so the student inherits target-domain characteristics. Third, a lightweight classifier head is trained on N-way K-shot support data with Tube Masking at the inference mask ratio to prevent distribution drift. Fourth, ensemble masked inference applies P different random Tube Masking patterns (mask ratio ρ_infer) to the input and averages the resulting predictions, reducing per-pass token count to (1−ρ_infer)·I and total complexity to O(P((1−ρ_infer)I)²), achieving 2.2× faster inference while maintaining or exceeding full-token accuracy.

## Results

On 5-way 1-shot and 5-shot top-1 action recognition across three egocentric target datasets (EPIC-Kitchens, MECCANO, WEAR) with Ego4D as source, MM-CDFSL achieves average accuracies of 40.52% (1-shot) and 55.36% (5-shot), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CDFSL-V by 6.12 and 6.10 points respectively. The largest gain appears on WEAR (outdoor sports), where 5-shot accuracy jumps from 58.27% to 69.57%. In addition to accuracy, the method achieves 2.2× faster inference (9.64 ms vs. 22.1 ms), 46% fewer GFLOPs (37.0 vs. 68.5), and 34% less memory (968 vs. 2782 MiB) compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-CDFSL outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and both shot settings. Against the strongest prior method CDFSL-V [44], the gains are 3.80/4.98/12.14 points (1-shot) and 4.98/2.16/11.30 points (5-shot) on EPIC/MEC/WEAR respectively, with the largest margin on WEAR where multimodal distillation most effectively bridges the domain gap. Dynamic Distillation++ [19] is the second-strongest baseline, trailing MM-CDFSL by 5.01/0.47/15.41 points (1-shot) and 4.92/0.07/13.34 points (5-shot); notably, Dynamic Distillation++ actually underperforms CDFSL-V on WEAR 5-shot (56.23 vs. 58.27), highlighting its weaker domain adaptability in the outdoor domain. STARTUP++ [34] and the plain VideoMAE [52] baseline lag further behind, with VideoMAE achieving only 44.65% on WEAR 1-shot versus MM-CDFSL's 51.25%. In terms of inference cost, all four baselines share identical runtime (22.1 ms, 68.5 GFLOPs, 2782 MiB) since they process all input tokens, whereas MM-CDFSL with ρ_infer=0.75 and P=2 reduces these to 9.64 ms, 37.0 GFLOPs, and 968 MiB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CD-FSL video framework of CDFSL-V (Samarasinghe et al., ICCV 2023) by introducing multimodal distillation and ensemble masked inference, while building directly on VideoMAE's (Tong et al., NeurIPS 2022) masked autoencoder architecture and Tube Masking operation. Prior CD-FSL methods (STARTUP, Dynamic Distillation, CDFSL-V) rely solely on RGB modality, and prior multimodal egocentric action recognition works (Radevski et al., Tan et al.) require processing all modalities at inference time, increasing computational cost. The key departure is using multimodal information exclusively during meta-training via teacher-student distillation, so that the student RGB model inherits domain-adaptive, class-discriminative features without any multimodal overhead at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous achievement of ~6-point accuracy gains and 2.2× faster inference (with 46% fewer GFLOPs and 34% memory) addresses a practical bottleneck for deploying egocentric action recognition on edge devices, where both domain shift and compute constraints are critical. Limitations include reliance on off-the-shelf optical flow and hand-pose estimators whose errors propagate into the training signal, and the use of fixed distillation loss weights that do not adapt to modality relevance varying across target domains (e.g., motion may matter more than hand pose in outdoor settings). Open questions include whether dynamic modality weighting, additional modalities (IMU, audio), or end-to-end joint training of the auxiliary modality estimators could further close the gap.
