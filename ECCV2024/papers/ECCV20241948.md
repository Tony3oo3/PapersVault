## Problem

Existing NeRF-based methods for dynamic urban street scenes (e.g., NSG, MARS, EmerNeRF) achieve reasonable view synthesis but suffer from prohibitively slow training (hours) and rendering speeds (0.21–0.68 FPS), making them impractical for real-time autonomous driving simulation. The core gap is the lack of an explicit, efficient scene representation that can faithfully model both static backgrounds and moving vehicles while supporting real-time rendering and scene editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241948/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB images (1066×1600 on Waymo, 375×1242 on KITTI at 10 Hz), an aggregated LiDAR point cloud from the ego vehicle for initialization, tracked vehicle poses from an off-the-shelf 3D tracker, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics; 2D semantic segmentation from Video-K-Net is optionally provided for semantic supervision. It produces photorealistic novel-view renderings at 135 FPS, a decomposed 3D Gaussian scene (separate background and per-vehicle point clouds), per-pixel semantic maps, depth maps, and cleanly extracted foreground objects suitable for editing.

## Method

Street Gaussians decomposes the scene into a static background and per-vehicle foreground models, each represented as an explicit set of 3D Gaussians with position, anisotropic covariance (scale + quaternion rotation), opacity, and spherical-harmonic appearance coefficients. For moving vehicles, Gaussians live in a local coordinate system and are transformed to world space via tracked poses that are made optimizable through learnable rotation (yaw offset) and translation deltas, correcting tracker noise without implicit differentiation. Time-varying vehicle appearance is captured by a 4D spherical harmonics model that replaces each SH coefficient with a small set of Fourier coefficients, recovering the coefficient at any timestep via a real-valued inverse DFT, avoiding per-frame storage. Rendering concatenates all point clouds, projects 2D Gaussians via the camera Jacobian, and performs standard alpha-blending, with a high-resolution cubemap handling sky regions. Training runs 30K Adam iterations with a composite loss (color L1+LPIPS, LiDAR-projected depth L1, sky BCE, optional semantic CE, and a regularization term for floater removal and decomposition), and LiDAR points (rather than SfM) initialize both background and object Gaussians to handle textureless urban regions.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset (1066×1600), Street Gaussians achieves PSNR 34.61, SSIM 0.938, LPIPS 0.079, and PSNR* (moving objects) 30.23, outperforming all baselines by 12.1% in PSNR and 13.9% in PSNR* over the strongest dynamic-scene competitor (MARS). Rendering speed reaches 135 FPS, which is over 100× faster than NeRF-based methods (0.21–0.68 FPS for EmerNeRF, NSG, and MARS). On KITTI (375×1242), the method achieves PSNR 25.79 / 25.52 / 24.53 and LPIPS 0.081 / 0.084 / 0.090 across the 75%/50%/25% train splits, consistently beating MARS (the best NeRF-based baseline) by 1.5–1.3 dB in PSNR and roughly halving LPIPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Street Gaussians decisively outperforms all four baselines on every quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, PSNR*) on both Waymo and KITTI. Against the closest dynamic-scene competitor MARS [60], it gains +4.86 dB PSNR and +3.69 dB PSNR* on Waymo while rendering ~200× faster (135 vs 0.68 FPS). EmerNeRF [65] achieves the second-best overall PSNR (30.87) but produces severe ghosting in dynamic regions, yielding the worst PSNR* (21.67) and the slowest rendering (0.21 FPS). NSG [37] is the weakest baseline overall (PSNR 28.31, LPIPS 0.346) due to its limited model capacity for complex scenes. 3D GS [19] is the only method faster than Street Gaussians (205 FPS), but it cannot model moving objects at all, causing PSNR* to collapse to 21.25 and producing visible ghosting artifacts in dynamic regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Street Gaussians extends 3D Gaussian Splatting [19] from static to dynamic urban scenes by introducing per-object point clouds with optimizable tracked poses and a 4D spherical harmonics appearance model. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based compositional approaches (NSG, MARS, EmerNeRF) that represent foreground vehicles and background with implicit neural fields, replacing them with explicit point-based Gaussians that enable direct rasterization rather than ray marching. The compositional decomposition of scene into static background plus moving vehicles follows prior work, but the explicit representation unlocks real-time rendering and straightforward scene editing that neural-field methods cannot easily support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 135 FPS rendering at 1066×1600 within half an hour of training makes photorealistic dynamic street simulation practically viable for autonomous driving testing, a regime where NeRF-based methods are orders of magnitude too slow. The explicit per-object representation enables editing operations (rotation, translation, swapping) that are difficult or impossible with entangled neural fields, opening the door to data augmentation for self-driving systems. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences or dense traffic, handling of non-vehicle dynamics (pedestrians, cyclists), and whether the reliance on LiDAR for initialization limits applicability in sensor-poor settings.
