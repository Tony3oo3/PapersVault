## Problem

Existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods learn representations invariant to object pose, mapping all views of the same object to a single feature for semantic categorization. This pose invariance makes SSL representations poor at downstream geometric tasks like pose estimation, and no standardized benchmark exists to evaluate SSL's geometric representation quality without using pose or semantic labels during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240538/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled image triplets {X_L, X_C, X_R} of the same object captured at adjacent viewpoints with subtle pose changes (no semantic or pose labels, no additional augmentations on triplet images). It produces a shared visual representation z that supports both semantic classification (via linear probe on 512-d pooled features) and pose estimation (via k-NN retrieval for absolute pose or a 2-layer MLP probe on concatenated 1024-d features for relative pose). A key finding is that mid-layer 'conv3' representations (16,384-d) outperform the final pooled layer by 10–20% on pose estimation, and can be compressed to 512-d with only ~1% absolute pose accuracy loss.

## Method

The core contribution is a viewpoint trajectory regularization loss added to standard invariant SSL (VICReg, SimCLR, SimSiam). Given a triplet of encoded representations {z_L, z_C, z_R} on the unit hypersphere, the loss projects the difference vectors v₁ = z_C − z_L and v₂ = z_R − z_C onto the tangent plane at z_C, then maximizes their cosine similarity to enforce a locally linear (geodesic) trajectory, yielding L_traj = −(u₁·u₂)/(‖u₁‖‖u₂‖). The total training objective is L = L_sem(z_T1, z_T2) + λ·L_traj(z_L, z_C, z_R), where L_sem is the standard SSL semantic loss and the right image X_R is generated via slerp from the left and center poses. The trajectory loss is applied at the pooled feature layer, and the paper further shows that evaluating pose with mid-layer 'conv3' features (rather than the final layer) yields an additional 9–20% gain, since mid-level features retain local geometric cues that the final invariant layer discards.

## Results

On the ShapeNet benchmark, adding the trajectory regularization loss to VICReg yields a 3.5% gain in in-domain absolute pose estimation (87.8% vs. 84.3%) and a 4.4% gain on out-of-domain unseen poses (77.5% vs. 73.1%), while semantic classification accuracy remains unchanged at 85.6%. On the real-world Carvana dataset, VICReg+traj achieves 91.7% relative pose accuracy, outperforming the fully-supervised baseline (88.5%) by 3.2%. Using mid-layer 'conv3' representations instead of the final feature layer provides an additional 9–20% gain in relative pose estimation across in-domain and out-of-domain settings (Table 3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three SSL baselines (VICReg, SimCLR, SimSiam) against their trajectory-regularized variants, plus fully-supervised and geometry-supervised references. VICReg+traj consistently outperforms plain VICReg by 3.5–4.4% across all pose estimation tasks (in-domain absolute, in-domain relative, unseen semantics, unseen poses, and Carvana), with no loss in semantic classification. SimCLR+traj and SimSiam+traj show smaller but consistent gains of 1.6–3.2% over their respective baselines. Critically, on out-of-domain pose estimation (unseen poses and unseen semantic categories), the trajectory-regularized SSL methods match or slightly exceed the fully-supervised and geometry-supervised baselines (e.g., VICReg+traj 77.5% vs. Fully-Sup. 77.4% on unseen poses; 91.7% vs. 88.5% on Carvana), demonstrating that the unsupervised trajectory prior enables competitive generalization without any pose labels during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends invariant self-supervised learning methods (VICReg, SimCLR, SimSiam) by adding a trajectory regularization loss inspired by slow feature analysis, shifting the learned representation from pose-invariant to pose-aware. It departs from geometry-supervised approaches like SIE [26] by strictly prohibiting pose labels during training, and from prior SSL benchmarks by introducing a standardized evaluation protocol that includes both in-domain and out-of-domain pose estimation. The benchmark itself builds on ShapeNet rendering pipelines but introduces Fibonacci-sphere pose sampling and relative pose estimation to enable class-agnostic out-of-domain evaluation, filling a gap left by prior datasets like 3DIEBench and 3DIdent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap: SSL has been evaluated almost exclusively on semantic tasks, yet pose awareness is critical for robotics, video understanding, and world models. The practical finding that mid-layer features (conv3) yield 10–20% pose estimation gains over the final feature layer is immediately actionable for practitioners building pose-aware SSL pipelines. However, the benchmark is limited to synthetic ShapeNet data with 2D spherical pose (azimuth, elevation) rather than full 6-DoF, and the trajectory regularization gain (~4%) is modest. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to real-world video sequences with occlusions and whether it generalizes to more complex geometric tasks like depth prediction.
