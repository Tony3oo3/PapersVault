## Problem

State-of-the-art open-vocabulary MOT methods rely on pure appearance matching, while motion and semantic cues are either ignored or applied through unreliable heuristics (e.g., Kalman Filter linear-motion assumptions, hard class grouping) at late association stages. These approaches fail in large-vocabulary settings where object motion is highly non-linear across diverse classes and classification of novel objects is unstable, leading to poor generalization on unseen categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of adjacent video frames (within a 3-second interval) and, from a frozen pre-trained open-vocabulary detector, extracts per-object bounding boxes, CLIP-aligned semantic embeddings, normalized location coordinates (scaled relative to image center with 70% of max dimension), and RoI-based appearance features. It outputs a single differentiable association matrix computed via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, which directly yields object-to-object correspondences across frames. A notable design choice is Detection Aware Training: during training, all predicted detection boxes (not just ground-truth-annotated ones) are fed into the association module, closing the train-test gap caused by TAO's sparse annotations.

## Method

SLAck fuses semantic, location, and appearance embeddings per object via simple summation, then processes them through a Spatial-Temporal Object Graph (STOG) comprising intra-frame self-attention (spatial object graph) and inter-frame cross-attention (temporal object graph), following the SuperGlue architecture. This attention-based graph enables the model to learn implicit motion priors and spatial object relationships directly from data, replacing explicit Kalman Filter assumptions and late-stage heuristic cue fusion with a single end-to-end trainable matching matrix. Training uses a differentiable Sinkhorn-Knopp loss with a dustbin class for unmatched objects, and the detector weights remain frozen throughout to preserve open-vocabulary detection capability. The key architectural insight is that early integration of all three cues into one fused embedding, refined by spatial and temporal attention, yields substantially better novel-class association than any combination of late-stage heuristic fusion strategies.

## Results

On the open-vocabulary MOT benchmark (TAO validation, novel classes), SLAck-OV achieves 31.1 TETA, 54.3 LocA, 37.8 AssocA, and 1.3 ClsA, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art OVTrack by +4.2 in AssocA and +3.3 in TETA. On the closed-set TAO TETA benchmark, SLAck-L (Swin-Large backbone) reaches 41.1 TETA and 41.8 AssocA, surpassing TETer-L by +1.9 in AssocA and outperforming GLEE-Plus (Swin-L) by +0.9 in AssocA despite GLEE being trained on 10 million images. On the test set, SLAck-OV achieves 27.1 TETA and 30.0 AssocA for novel classes, a +1.3 TETA improvement over OVTrack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLAck-OV outperforms all 12 baselines on the open-vocabulary MOT benchmark. Against the previous SOTA OVTrack [19], SLAck-OV gains +4.2 in AssocA (37.8 vs 33.6) and +3.3 in TETA (31.1 vs 27.8) on novel classes, confirming that early semantic-location fusion surpasses pure appearance matching. MASA [20], the strongest appearance-based competitor, is beaten by +3.2 in AssocA (37.8 vs 34.6). Motion-based trackers (OC-SORT [5], ByteTrack [43], DeepSORT [37], Tracktor++ [1]) are substantially outperformed, with AssocA gaps of 17–23 points, highlighting the inadequacy of Kalman-filter-based linear motion assumptions in open-vocabulary settings. On the closed-set TAO TETA benchmark, SLAck-T surpasses TETer-T [18] by +2.2 in AssocA (38.9 vs 36.7) and SLAck-L surpasses TETer-L by +1.9 (41.8 vs 39.9). SLAck-L also edges out GLEE-Plus [38] (a foundation model trained on 10M images) by +0.9 in AssocA (41.8 vs 40.9), though GLEE-Plus retains a slight TETA advantage (41.5 vs 41.1) and higher ClsA (30.8 vs 25.1). UNINEXT [40] and AOA [9], which use additional tracking annotations, are outperformed by +6.3 and +11.2 in AssocA respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLAck adapts the SuperGlue [30] spatial-temporal graph architecture (intra-frame self-attention, inter-frame cross-attention, differentiable Sinkhorn-Knopp assignment) from point matching to multi-object tracking, and builds its detection backbone on OVTrack's [19] open-vocabulary Faster R-CNN and TETer's [18] class-exemplar detector. The key conceptual departure from prior MOT is the early, learned fusion of semantic, location, and appearance cues into a single association matrix, replacing the heuristic late-stage fusion (IoU + cosine similarity + Hungarian) used by DeepSORT, ByteTrack, and OC-SORT. The paper also introduces Detection Aware Training (DAT), a practical strategy for handling TAO's incomplete annotations that has no direct analogue in prior MOT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a semantic-plus-motion tracker (SLck) can outperform the previous appearance-based SOTA (OVTrack) on novel-class association is a useful signal that the field's heavy reliance on appearance embeddings may be suboptimal for open-vocabulary settings, where semantic transfer across related classes (e.g., horse → zebra) provides a generalization advantage that pure appearance features lack. However, the overall SOTA margin is modest (+4.2 AssocA on novel classes), the method is evaluated only on TAO, and it still depends on a frozen pre-trained detector, leaving open questions about scalability to even larger vocabularies, real-time deployment, and whether the implicit motion modeling generalizes beyond the TAO domain. The DAT strategy, while yielding a large +13.7 AssocA gain, is specific to the incomplete-annotation regime and may not transfer to fully annotated tracking benchmarks.
