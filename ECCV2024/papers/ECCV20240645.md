## Problem

Transformer-based video models suffer from quadratic computational complexity in sequence length, making them impractical for long or high-resolution video in resource-constrained settings. While Mamba's selective state space model offers linear complexity for 1D sequences, adapting it to video is non-trivial because video combines non-sequential spatial information with sequential temporal information in an interlinked manner. Prior SSM-based video approaches (e.g., S4ND) lacked input-dependent selectivity, and the appropriate backward scanning strategy for spatio-temporal token sequences remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip of T×H×W×C, sampled at 16 or 32 frames with temporal stride 2 and resized to 224×224, which is divided into non-overlapping tubelets of size 2×16×16 and projected to d-dimensional tokens via a 3D convolution. A class token is prepended, and at inference the final prediction is obtained by averaging scores across multiple spatial crops and temporal clips (e.g., 5×3 views). The output is a single action class label produced by a one-layer MLP on the normalized class token.

## Method

VideoMamba processes video tokens through L=24 encoder blocks, each containing layer normalization, a 1D convolution, and a proposed Spatio-Temporal Forward and Backward SSM. The forward scan flattens n_t·n_h·n_w tokens in natural order, while the backward scan applies a full spatio-temporal reversal (reversing the entire flattened sequence), which ablations show outperforms spatial-only or temporal-only reversal. The selective SSM mechanism makes Δ, B, and C input-dependent, enabling context-aware gating that emphasizes dynamic elements over static background. Positional embeddings are learned and initialized by temporally expanding the ImageNet-pretrained image model's positional embeddings, and the 3D video tokenizer is inflated from 2D convolutions by averaging weights along the temporal axis. This transfer-learning strategy from large-scale image pretraining compensates for the limited size of video datasets.

## Results

On HMDB51, VideoMamba with Kinetics-400 pretraining achieves 75.7% Top-1 accuracy (32 frames), outperforming VideoSwin-T (69.9%) by 5.8 points and Mamba-ND (59.0%) by 16.7 points, while using fewer parameters (26.8M vs. 27.9M and 36.0M respectively). On Something-Something V2, VideoMamba reaches 64.2% Top-1 with only 68 GFLOPs and 26.8M parameters, surpassing VideoSwin-T (57.2% at 88 GFLOPs) by 7 points. On Kinetics-400, VideoMamba achieves 77.7% Top-1 (32 frames) with 68 GFLOPs, competitive with VideoSwin-T (78.8% at 88 GFLOPs) and VideoMAE (79.0% at 57 GFLOPs) while using fewer FLOPs than VideoSwin-T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoMamba clearly outperforms all SSM-based baselines: it surpasses Mamba-ND [29] by 16.7 points (75.7% vs. 59.0%) and S4ND-ConvNeXt-3D [35] by 20.5 points (75.7% vs. 55.2%) on HMDB51 with K400 pretraining, while using fewer parameters (26.8M vs. 36.0M and 29.0M). Against the strongest transformer baseline VideoSwin-T [33], VideoMamba wins by 5.8 points on HMDB51 (75.7% vs. 69.9%) and by 7.0 points on SSV2 (64.2% vs. 57.2%), all with fewer GFLOPs (68 vs. 88) and fewer parameters (26.8M vs. 27.9M). On Kinetics-400, VideoMamba (77.7%) is slightly behind VideoSwin-T (78.8%) and VideoMAE (79.0%) by 1.1 and 1.3 points respectively, but uses fewer GFLOPs (68 vs. 88 and 57). Compared to VideoMAE [40], VideoMamba uses fewer parameters (26.8M vs. 87.0M on HMDB51) and fewer GFLOPs while achieving higher accuracy. On SSV2, VideoMamba also outperforms TimeSformer [4] (ViT-L, 62.4%) by 1.8 points while using dramatically fewer GFLOPs (68 vs. 5549) and parameters (26.8M vs. 430M). Overall, VideoMamba wins or ties on all three benchmarks while consistently offering 20–60% lower computational cost than comparable transformer baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMamba extends the selective state space model introduced by Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023) from 1D sequences to video, building directly on the bidirectional scanning strategies developed for image classification in Vision Mamba (Zhu et al., 2024) and VMamba (Liu et al., 2024). It departs from earlier SSM-based video approaches like S4ND (Nguyen et al., 2022), which used non-selective SSMs and struggled with input-dependent information, and from Mamba-ND (Li et al., 2024), which generalized Mamba to multi-dimensional data without a video-specific design. The key conceptual departure is the Spatio-Temporal Forward and Backward SSM, where the authors systematically explore how to define the backward scanning direction when spatial information is non-sequential but temporal information is inherently ordered—a design question absent from the image-domain bidirectional SSM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VideoMamba provides the first comprehensive empirical validation that a pure Mamba architecture with linear complexity can match or exceed transformer-based video models (e.g., VideoSwin-T) while using roughly half the FLOPs, making it a practical option for resource-constrained video understanding. The ablation on backward scanning direction (spatio-temporal reversal outperforming spatial or temporal-only reversal) and the Delta visualization offering mechanistic insight into how the model allocates attention to dynamic vs. static regions are useful design guidelines for future SSM-based video architectures. Open questions include whether these efficiency gains and design choices hold at significantly larger scales (longer videos, higher resolutions, larger model sizes), and whether the model's reliance on temporal ordering (demonstrated via frame-reordering experiments) translates to robustness in real-world streaming or variable-frame-rate settings.
