## Problem

Adapting a large pre-trained RGB object detector to a new modality such as infrared (IR) typically requires fine-tuning, which causes catastrophic forgetting of the original RGB knowledge. Existing image-translation methods (CycleGAN, FastCUT, etc.) optimize for pixel-level reconstruction quality rather than downstream detection accuracy, and many require paired or unpaired source-modality data during training. The paper asks whether one can adapt the input signal to a frozen detector using only target-modality data, while preserving the detector's full prior knowledge for other modalities and tasks.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single-channel IR image (C=1) as input and produces a 3-channel RGB-like translated image via a U-Net, which is then fed to a frozen RGB detector (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, or FCOS) that outputs bounding boxes with class labels. The translation network's output is fused with the original IR input via a Hadamard (element-wise) product before entering the detector, so the detector always receives a 3-channel spatially-aligned image at the original resolution. Training requires only IR images with ground-truth bounding boxes—no RGB source images are needed.

## Method

ModTr is a lightweight U-Net (default ResNet-34 backbone, reducible to MobileNet-v2/v3s) that learns an IR-to-RGB-like mapping by directly minimizing the detection loss of a frozen pre-trained detector, rather than any reconstruction or adversarial objective. A residual-learning formulation lets the network focus on small corrective variations, and a non-parametric Hadamard-product fusion between the translated output and the original input acts as a learned attention/gating map that highlights or suppresses pixels relevant to detection. Because the detector weights θ remain fixed throughout training, the original RGB knowledge is fully preserved, enabling a single server-side detector to serve multiple modalities through interchangeable ModTr translators. The approach is modality-agnostic in principle (any single- or multi-channel input can be mapped to 3 channels) and detector-agnostic (works with one-stage and two-stage architectures), and it can optionally be combined with light detector fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA) for further gains without sacrificing the zero-shot RGB capability.

## Results

On LLVIP, ModTr⊙ achieves 57.63 AP (FCOS), 54.83 AP (RetinaNet), and 57.97 AP (Faster R-CNN), surpassing the second-best method (HalluciDet) by over 29 AP on FCOS and RetinaNet. On FLIR, ModTr⊙ reaches 35.49, 34.27, and 37.21 AP respectively, improving over HalluciDet by more than 11 AP on FCOS/RetinaNet and 7 AP on Faster R-CNN. Against full fine-tuning, ModTr⊙ matches or exceeds FT on LLVIP (57.63 vs 57.37 for FCOS) and clearly outperforms it on FLIR (35.49 vs 27.97 for FCOS, 37.21 vs 30.93 for Faster R-CNN) while preserving the original detector's COCO knowledge (38.41 AP retained vs 0.33 AP for the 1-detector joint-training baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ModTr⊙ decisively outperforms all reconstruction-based translation baselines: Histogram Equalization [14] (31.69/38.33 AP on LLVIP FCOS/Faster R-CNN), CycleGAN [51] (23.85/26.54), CUT [37] (14.30/14.78), and FastCUT [37] (19.39/22.91), with margins of 19–43 AP depending on detector and dataset. Against the closest competitor HalluciDet [29], ModTr⊙ gains over 29 AP on FCOS and RetinaNet for LLVIP (57.63 vs 28.00; 54.83 vs 19.95) while matching it on Faster R-CNN (57.97 vs 57.78); on FLIR the gap widens to 11.75 AP (FCOS) and 7.30 AP (Faster R-CNN). Compared to LoRA fine-tuning [18], ModTr⊙ substantially outperforms on FLIR (35.49 vs diverged for FCOS; 37.21 vs 29.44 for Faster R-CNN) and is competitive on LLVIP (57.63 vs 47.72 for FCOS). Against full fine-tuning, ModTr⊙ is within 1.65 AP on LLVIP Faster R-CNN (57.97 vs 59.62) but clearly superior on FLIR across all three detectors (e.g., 37.21 vs 30.93 for Faster R-CNN), while critically preserving the original COCO knowledge that fine-tuning destroys.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own prior work HalluciDet (WACV 2024), which used image translation for object detection but required access to source RGB data for pre-fine-tuning. ModTr removes that dependency by training a small U-Net translation network directly against the frozen detector's detection loss, so no source-modality data is needed. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant fine-tuning paradigm (which causes catastrophic forgetting) and from reconstruction-focused image translation methods (CycleGAN, CUT, FastCUT) by optimizing the translation for the downstream detection task rather than pixel-level fidelity. The Hadamard-product fusion, borrowed from attention mechanisms, serves as a lightweight gating strategy that lets the translation network act as an attention map over the input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a flexible, memory-efficient deployment architecture: a single frozen RGB detector on a server can be queried by multiple modalities (IR, depth, etc.) through small per-modality translators, avoiding the need to store or retrain a separate detector per modality. This is particularly relevant for surveillance, autonomous driving, and robotics where illumination conditions change continuously. Limitations include evaluation on only two IR datasets (LLVIP, FLIR) and a single target modality; the authors note that for information-poor modalities like Canny edges, the deterministic translator falls short of full fine-tuning, suggesting generative translators may be needed. An open question is whether this translation-for-detection paradigm generalizes to larger modality gaps or to open-vocabulary detection settings.
