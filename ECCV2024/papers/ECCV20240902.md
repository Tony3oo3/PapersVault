## Problem

Existing unified face perception approaches either require separate finetuning per task (unified representation) or use fixed output structures that cannot accommodate new tasks (unified training). No prior work addresses the unified model structure, which is the key to achieving both task extensibility and application efficiency (storage, inference, and training overhead) in a single face generalist model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240902/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB face images at two resolutions: 512×512 for dense prediction tasks (landmarks, parsing) and 112×112 for attribute and identity prediction tasks, with a shared learnable positional embedding interpolated to match the input spatial size. A single forward pass simultaneously produces per-pixel dense maps (landmark heatmaps, parsing masks), scalar/class predictions via task-specific query tokens (age from 101 queries, expression from 7 queries, binary attributes from one query each), and a 512-dimensional identity embedding vector from the top encoder layer.

## Method

Faceptor adopts a single-encoder dual-decoder architecture: a 12-layer ViT-B encoder (pre-trained with FaRL) extracts multi-scale features, a 9-layer transformer decoder processes task-specific queries against those features, and a lightweight pixel decoder (two 2×2 deconv layers) restores spatial resolution for dense outputs. The key mechanism is task-specific queries that encode different semantics (each landmark, parsing class, attribute, age bin, or expression is one query), minimizing non-shared parameters compared to per-task output heads. A Layer-Attention mechanism introduces learnable layer-aware embeddings into the cross-attention keys, enabling the decoder to adaptively weight features from six uniformly sampled encoder layers. A two-stage training process first trains queries using only top-layer features to establish semantic representations, then unfreezes only the layer-aware embeddings to learn layer preferences, preventing the random initialization of queries from destabilizing the multi-layer attention.

## Results

Faceptor-Full achieves state-of-the-art results across most face analysis tasks: 4.03 NME on WFLW and 2.52 on 300W Comm for landmark localization, 88.2 F1-mean on CelebAMask-HQ for face parsing, 1.96 MAE on MORPH II and 4.10 MAE on UTKFace for age estimation, and 91.26% accuracy on RAF-DB for expression recognition. For binary attribute classification, it achieves 91.39% mAcc on CelebA, competitive with the best specialized method (91.70%). Face recognition slightly lags behind the strongest baseline (95.28% mean vs. 96.46% for ViT+CosFace trained from scratch), attributed to task conflicts in multi-task training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Faceptor-Full outperforms all specialized baselines on facial landmark localization across every benchmark (e.g., 4.03 NME on WFLW vs. 4.05 for DTLD+; 0.95 NME_diag on AFLW-19 vs. 1.37 for DTLD+). For face parsing, it achieves 88.2 F1-mean on CelebAMask-HQ, surpassing DML-CSR (86.1) and AGRNet (85.5), though it trails DML-CSR on LaPa (91.5 vs. 92.4) due to the absence of Tanh-warping. In age estimation, Faceptor beats DLDL-v2 (1.96 vs. 1.97 MAE on MORPH II) and MWR (4.10 vs. 4.37 on UTKFace). For expression recognition, it surpasses SwinFace (91.26% vs. 90.97% on RAF-DB) and EAC (88.99%). On binary attribute classification, Faceptor (91.39%) is slightly below DMM-CNN (91.70%) but competitive. The only clear loss is in face recognition, where Faceptor (95.28% mean) trails ViT+CosFace trained from scratch (96.46%) by 1.18%, which the authors attribute to conflicting multi-task objectives and the FaRL initialization not being tailored for identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a third axis of unification for face perception, complementing prior work on unified representation (FRL, FaRL) and unified training (HyperFace, AIO). Architecturally, it adapts the single-encoder dual-decoder paradigm from MaskFormer and the task-specific query mechanism from DETR/SLPT/RLPFER to the face domain, while leveraging FaRL's visual-linguistic pre-training as the task-agnostic backbone and SwinFace's standardized subnets as the starting point for output heads. The key conceptual departure is from fixed-task-set multi-task models: by categorizing tasks into dense/attribute/identity prediction and using shared structural designs (Naive Faceptor) or shared parameters via queries (Faceptor), the model becomes extensible to new tasks without architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single 103M-parameter model replaces six specialized models while matching or exceeding their performance on most tasks, reducing storage overhead by 73% compared to the naive unified alternative. The Layer-Attention mechanism reveals that different face tasks genuinely prefer features from different encoder depths, a finding that could inform future multi-task architectures beyond face perception. However, the face recognition degradation (95.28 vs. 96.46 mean accuracy) highlights an unresolved tension between identity-invariant and texture-sensitive objectives in unified training, and the paper does not explore whether this gap can be closed with better task weighting or gradient surgery. Open questions include scalability to even more tasks, whether the two-stage Layer-Attention training generalizes to new tasks added post-hoc, and whether the prompt-tuning transfer results (Table 9) hold for substantially different semantic spaces.
