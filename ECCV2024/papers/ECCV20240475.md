## Problem

Body-worn IMUs capture accurate limb motion robust to lighting and occlusion, yet their potential for egocentric action recognition remains under-explored due to two barriers: (1) severe scarcity of synchronized video–limb-IMU data with action labels, and (2) the difficulty of modeling the collaborative dynamics among multiple IMU devices placed across different body parts. Existing multimodal approaches either rely on camera-mounted IMUs that add no information beyond the video, or treat each IMU independently without capturing inter-device motion relationships.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2-second synchronized egocentric video clips (downsampled to 16 frames at 224×224, split into 16×16 patches) and acceleration signals from 4 limb-mounted IMUs (left/right wrists and ankles), which are resampled, STFT-transformed into 160×128 spectrograms, and similarly patchified. During pretraining, 90% of video patches and 75% of IMU spectrogram patches are masked; during finetuning, all patches are fed through the encoders, whose outputs (unified encoder + graph encoder) are concatenated, globally pooled, and passed to a linear classifier producing an action label (32 classes on CMU-MMAC, 18 on WEAR).

## Method

EVI-MAE performs self-supervised pretraining with two parallel reconstruction branches. The multimodal pixel reconstruction branch encodes visible video patches (ViT with joint space-time attention) and visible IMU spectrogram patches (ImageMAE-style encoder), merges them via modality-type and positional embeddings into a unified single-layer Transformer encoder, then decodes to reconstruct both masked video pixels and masked IMU spectrogram pixels. The IMU feature reconstruction branch encodes per-device IMU features as nodes in a fully-connected graph (GIN encoder), masks a fraction of node features, and reconstructs the original graph via a GIN decoder, thereby learning inter-device motion relationships. The total loss combines MSE pixel reconstruction, cosine-similarity graph reconstruction, and an InfoNCE contrastive term aligning video and IMU encoder outputs across the batch. At finetuning, both decoders are discarded and the frozen-then-tuned encoder features are classified with a linear head.

## Results

On the multimodal (IMU-Video) setting, EVI-MAE achieves 87.96 mAP on CMU-MMAC and 92.78% accuracy on WEAR, outperforming the strongest baseline AV-MAE by 3.21 mAP and 1.18% respectively. In the IMU-only setting, the method reaches 31.68 mAP on CMU-MMAC, more than doubling the prior best LIMU-BERT (15.30 mAP). For video-only recognition, EVI-MAE attains 85.07 mAP on CMU-MMAC and 89.78% on WEAR, marginally surpassing VideoMAE (84.63 / 88.47).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVI-MAE outperforms all nine baselines across every modality configuration and both datasets. In the IMU-only setting, the method more than doubles LIMU-BERT (31.68 vs. 15.30 mAP on CMU-MMAC) and substantially surpasses the LSTM-based DCL and ADCL (7.46 and 8.59 mAP), attributing the gain to MAE-based pretraining and graph-based multi-IMU modeling. In the video-only setting, EVI-MAE edges out VideoMAE by 0.44 mAP on CMU-MMAC and 1.31% on WEAR, with the advantage coming from the added contrastive pretraining objective. In the full multimodal setting, EVI-MAE leads AV-MAE (the closest competitor) by 3.21 mAP on CMU-MMAC and 1.18% on WEAR, and outperforms the non-pretrained WEAR+ by 4.60 mAP and 2.64%. CAV-MAE, adapted from audio-visual to IMU-image, lags significantly on CMU-MMAC (74.69 vs. 87.96 mAP) due to its lack of graph-based IMU modeling and video-specific temporal modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE, VideoMAE, GraphMAE) to a novel multimodal setting: jointly pretraining on egocentric video and body-worn IMU acceleration signals. It departs from prior IMU-based action recognition (DCL, ADCL, LIMU-BERT) by introducing self-supervised MAE pretraining and a graph-based multi-IMU modeling approach inspired by skeleton-based recognition, and from prior egocentric multimodal work (CAV-MAE, AV-MAE, MMG) by specifically targeting limb-mounted IMUs rather than head-mounted sensors. The contrastive alignment objective is adapted from CAV-MAE, while the graph reconstruction branch follows GraphMAE's masked-node reconstruction strategy applied to a fully connected IMU feature graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most egocentric multimodal datasets use only head-mounted sensors, leaving body-worn limb IMUs underexplored despite their complementary motion information. The demonstrated robustness to partially missing IMU devices (only 3% accuracy drop with video+IMU) and to low-light video degradation has direct implications for real-world wearable deployments where sensor availability and environmental conditions are unpredictable. Limitations include reliance on small datasets (9–13 hours), a fully connected graph with only four IMU nodes, and still-modest absolute IMU-only accuracy (31.68% mAP on CMU-MMAC). Open questions include whether more sophisticated graph topologies (e.g., kinematic-chain structures) would help when more IMUs are available, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more diverse action vocabularies.
