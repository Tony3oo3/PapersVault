## Problem

Parametric HPSE methods produce realistic but shape-biased results and struggle with detailed geometry, while non-parametric methods that directly regress 3D vertex coordinates achieve higher precision but generate non-anthropomorphic meshes—especially under limited training data—and are sensitive to distribution shift. No prior work had reframed the HPSE problem as a classification task over a discrete mesh representation, leaving the discriminative power of classification networks untapped for this problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241399/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a full 3D human mesh of 6890 vertices following SMPL topology, a 3×3 rotation matrix, and a weak-perspective camera (scale + 2D translation). Internally, the mesh is first predicted in a canonical (non-oriented, origin-centered) form as N discrete indices drawn from a codebook of S latent codes, then decoded by the frozen Mesh-VQ-VAE decoder and rotated into the camera frame. The CNN backbone (ResNet-50) extracts spatially structured image features that feed the Transformer encoder.

## Method

VQ-HPS introduces a two-stage pipeline. First, a Mesh-VQ-VAE—built on the fully convolutional mesh autoencoder with a vector-quantization step in the latent space—is pre-trained on AMASS to map canonical meshes to N discrete indices from a learned codebook of size S, achieving a 4.7 mm reconstruction error. Second, a Transformer encoder-decoder (VQ-HPS) predicts those N indices from image features: the encoder applies self-attention over CNN-extracted image patch tokens, while the decoder uses N learned mesh tokens with cross-attention to image features, outputting per-index logits over S classes. Training uses cross-entropy on the discrete indices (the key departure from prior regression-based losses), MSE on the rotation matrix, and a 2D reprojection loss for camera alignment. Because the Mesh-VQ-VAE decoder is frozen during VQ-HPS training, predictions are inherently constrained to the manifold of realistic human meshes learned during pre-training, which is the primary mechanism enabling strong performance with scarce labeled data.

## Results

On 3DPW (ResNet-50 backbone, large-scale training), VQ-HPS achieves 93.6 mm PVE, 79.1 mm MPJPE, and 50.4 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming all compared methods. On EMDB, VQ-HPS reaches 112.9 mm PVE, 99.9 mm MPJPE, and 65.2 mm PA-MPJPE, tying PARE and beating all other baselines. In the limited-data setting (trained only on 3DPW), VQ-HPS achieves 163.9 mm PVE and 139.8 mm MPJPE, surpassing FastMETRO-S (176.3 / 157.0), CLIFF (223.9 / 188.8), and HMR (209.3 / 177.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQ-HPS outperforms all 11 compared baselines on 3DPW across all three metrics (PVE, MPJPE, PA-MPJPE). The largest margins are against HMR (−36.4 mm MPJPE) and FastMETRO-S (−33.5 mm MPJPE), while the closest competitor is PARE (99.7 vs. 93.6 mm PVE, 82.9 vs. 79.1 mm MPJPE). On EMDB, VQ-HPS ties PARE exactly (112.9 / 99.9 / 65.2) and beats ROMP by 10 mm PVE and DSR by 11.5 mm PVE. In the limited-data setting (Table 1), VQ-HPS leads FastMETRO-S by 12.5 mm PVE and 17.2 mm MPJPE on 3DPW, and by 12.5 mm PVE and 15.8 mm MPJPE on EMDB, while CLIFF and HMR trail by over 50 mm in PVE. Against TokenHMR (HRNet backbone, additional 2D data), VQ-HPS still wins on both 3DPW and EMDB, though TokenHMR takes the lead when additionally trained on BEDLAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines the vector-quantized variational autoencoder (VQ-VAE) framework with the fully convolutional mesh autoencoder to create a discrete latent representation of 3D human meshes, then reframes human pose and shape estimation as a multi-class classification problem over these discrete indices. It departs from both parametric methods (which regress SMPL parameters) and non-parametric methods (which regress 3D vertex coordinates) by predicting a sequence of codebook indices instead, drawing an analogy to how VQ-VAE-based motion generation works but encoding a single pose with multiple indices rather than a sequence with one. The Transformer encoder-decoder architecture for the classification head is inspired by FastMETRO's disentangled modality design, but the training objective shifts from 3D regression losses to cross-entropy, which the authors argue better exploits the discriminative power of attention mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that the pre-trained Mesh-VQ-VAE decoder acts as a strong regularizer, confining predictions to anthropomorphic meshes even with very limited image data — a known failure mode of non-parametric regression methods. The ablation showing that cross-entropy on discrete indices substantially outperforms direct 3D vertex regression (PVE 176.6 vs. 220.3) challenges the assumption that 3D losses are necessary for mesh recovery. Open questions include whether the 4.7 mm reconstruction floor of the Mesh-VQ-VAE limits ultimate accuracy, whether the approach extends to other registered meshes (hands, faces), and whether the discrete representation can serve as a 'language' for multimodal text-to-3D applications as the authors speculate.
