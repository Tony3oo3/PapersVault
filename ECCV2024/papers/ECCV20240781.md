## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) severely overfits when trained on extremely sparse inputs (e.g., 3 images), producing a jumble of semi-random anisotropic blobs from novel views. Existing NeRF-based sparse-view regularizations rely on the implicit coherency of MLPs and do not transfer to the discrete, unstructured 3DGS representation, leaving a gap in how to constrain individual Gaussians during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240781/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–4 posed RGB images as primary input, supplemented by monocular depth estimates (Depth Anything) and dense optical flow correspondences (FlowFormer++) computed between all image pairs. It produces a structured 3D Gaussian representation with exactly one Gaussian per pixel per input view, constrained to move along the camera-to-pixel ray, enabling real-time novel view rendering at ~278 fps on a V100. Regions occluded in all input views are intentionally left unreconstructed, allowing downstream inpainting with diffusion models.

## Method

The core idea is to assign a single Gaussian to every pixel of each input image and restrict its movement to the ray from the camera center to that pixel, so position is controlled by a scalar depth value. A single-view constraint uses an implicit convolutional decoder (conditioned on view index) to predict smooth residual depth, with binary segmentation masks preserving sharp depth discontinuities; a multi-view constraint applies a total variation loss on rendered disparity with a curriculum from global TV to region-masked TV. A flow-based regularization then forces Gaussians at corresponding pixels in different views to occupy similar 3D positions. Initialization uses monocular depth with per-view scale and offset aligned via a flow-based loss, and Gaussians are given isometric scale proportional to depth. Multi-sampling within each pixel during rendering prevents semi-transparent surfaces from novel views.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset with 3 input views, CoherentGS achieves PSNR 20.33, SSIM 0.725, and LPIPS 0.180, outperforming the best NeRF-based baseline (SparseNeRF: PSNR 20.33, SSIM 0.657, LPIPS 0.302) by a large margin on perceptual quality (LPIPS reduced by ~40%) and structural similarity. With only 2 input views, the advantage is even more pronounced: CoherentGS reaches PSNR 18.32 and LPIPS 0.220 versus SparseNeRF's 17.74 and 0.386. On NVS-RGBD (ZED2, 2 views), the method achieves PSNR 23.05, SSIM 0.774, and LPIPS 0.164, again leading on SSIM and LPIPS while matching or exceeding baselines on PSNR. Inference speed is 278 fps on a V100 GPU, roughly 3,500× faster than the NeRF-based alternatives (~0.08 fps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoherentGS is compared against vanilla 3DGS and four sparse-view NeRF methods: RegNeRF, FlipNeRF, FreeNeRF, and SparseNeRF. Against the strongest baseline (SparseNeRF), CoherentGS wins decisively on SSIM and LPIPS across all view counts on LLFF (e.g., SSIM 0.725 vs 0.657 and LPIPS 0.180 vs 0.302 at 3 views), while matching PSNR at 3 views (20.33) and slightly trailing at 4 views (21.58 vs 21.90). On NVS-RGBD, the method leads on SSIM and LPIPS in most settings but occasionally trails SparseNeRF on PSNR (e.g., 24.93 vs 26.56 on ZED2 with 3 views). Compared to vanilla 3DGS, the improvement is dramatic across all metrics (e.g., PSNR jumps from 14.99 to 20.33 on LLFF with 3 views). The paper also notes that concurrent 3DGS-based sparse-view methods (FSGS, SparseGS, DNGaussian) produce floaters and blurry occluded-region fills, whereas CoherentGS's structured representation enables clean identification and inpainting of occluded areas.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to the extremely sparse-view setting (2–4 images), a regime where vanilla 3DGS overfits into unstructured anisotropic blobs. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based sparse-view line (FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF, RegNeRF, FlipNeRF), whose regularizations exploit the implicit coherency of MLPs and are not directly transferable to the discrete, unstructured Gaussian representation. The key conceptual move is to re-impose coherency on 3DGS by assigning one Gaussian per input pixel and constraining its motion along a camera ray, then regularizing the residual depth through an implicit convolutional decoder (single-view) and a total-variation loss on rendered disparity (multi-view), complemented by a flow-based correspondence loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Making 3DGS viable for sparse inputs is practically important because it inherits 3DGS's real-time rendering (278 fps vs. 0.08 fps for NeRF baselines) while closing the perceptual-quality gap (LPIPS 0.220 vs. 0.376–0.417 at 2 views). A unique byproduct is that the constrained representation leaves occluded regions explicitly empty, enabling targeted diffusion-based inpainting rather than the blurry fill-in produced by competing methods. Limitations include difficulty with transparent objects (single Gaussian per pixel) and dependence on monocular depth accuracy; open questions include whether the per-pixel Gaussian constraint can be relaxed for complex geometry and whether the inpainting pipeline can be made end-to-end differentiable.
