## Problem

Subject-driven generation requires inverting a specific subject's visual identity into the text embedding space of a diffusion model. Optimization-based methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) achieve high fidelity but demand multiple images and thousands of iterations, while direct-regression methods (ELITE, FastComposer) are fast but suffer from detail loss and poor generalization to new prompts. The paper addresses the gap between speed and fidelity by asking whether a hybrid of both paradigms can achieve high-quality subject generation from a single image in just a few optimization steps.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB subject image and an arbitrary text prompt as input, and produces a generated image of that subject in the prompted context. The key intermediate output is a set of n=5 learned word embeddings in the CLIP text encoder space, which can be freely composed with new prompts (e.g., 'S* in a snow forest'). During training, a segmentation mask from InSPyReNet is also required to compute the mask regularization loss, but at inference only the subject image and prompt are needed.

## Method

HybridBooth is a two-stage prompt inversion framework built on Stable Diffusion v1.5. Stage 1 (Word Embedding Probe) trains a PromptNet regressor—initialized from the SD UNet—on 70k FFHQ images to map multi-grained image features (CLIP global semantics concatenated with DINOv2 pixel-level details via an MLP) to multiple word embeddings. Stage 2 (Word Embedding Refinement) adapts the regressor to a specific subject by fine-tuning only the cross-attention parameters (key, query, value matrices) for 3–5 iterations using a residual update rule W' = W + λΔW, which anchors the learned prior and prevents overfitting to the single image. A mask regularization loss constrains cross-attention maps to the subject region, preventing attention leakage into the background. The cross-attention layers were identified as most important via a layer-importance analysis (cosine distance of 56.3 vs. 43.9 for self-attention and 12.4 for others), justifying the selective parameter update.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, HybridBooth achieves CLIP-I 0.865 and DINO-I 0.644 with only 5 refinement iterations, outperforming the best direct-regression baseline FastComposer (CLIP-I 0.782, DINO-I 0.581) and all optimization-based methods (DreamBooth: CLIP-I 0.564, DINO-I 0.376; Custom Diffusion: CLIP-I 0.675, DINO-I 0.398). On the DreamBooth dataset, HybridBooth leads on all three metrics: CLIP-T 0.261, CLIP-I 0.865, and DINO-I 0.755, surpassing Custom Diffusion (0.245, 0.801, 0.695) and ELITE (0.255, 0.762, 0.652). The DINO-I improvement over FastComposer exceeds 10% on CelebA-HQ, a key metric in the DreamBooth evaluation framework. Overall, HybridBooth requires only 5 optimization steps compared to 200–5000 steps for optimization-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HybridBooth outperforms all six baselines on subject fidelity (CLIP-I and DINO-I) across both datasets. Against optimization-based methods, HybridBooth achieves substantially higher subject fidelity (e.g., DINO-I 0.644 vs. DreamBooth's 0.376 on CelebA-HQ) while requiring only 5 iterations versus 1000–5000. Against direct-regression baselines, HybridBooth improves CLIP-I by 8.3 points over FastComposer (0.865 vs. 0.782) and DINO-I by 6.3 points (0.644 vs. 0.581) on CelebA-HQ, and leads on all three metrics on the DreamBooth dataset. On prompt fidelity (CLIP-T), HybridBooth (0.246) is competitive with DreamBooth (0.251) and Custom Diffusion (0.237) on CelebA-HQ, and achieves the best score (0.261) on the DreamBooth dataset. FastComposer, the strongest direct-regression baseline, is not evaluated on the DreamBooth dataset, limiting a full head-to-head comparison there. Qualitatively, the paper also shows HybridBooth surpassing HyperDreamBooth [37] in subject feature preservation, attributing the gap to HyperDreamBooth's low-rank weight updates limiting personalized representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HybridBooth sits at the intersection of two established paradigms for subject-driven generation: optimization-based methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion) that iteratively align a model or embedding to subject images, and direct-regression-based methods (ELITE, FastComposer) that map images to word embeddings in a single forward pass. The paper builds directly on PromptNet as its regressor backbone and on Stable Diffusion's UNet for initialization, but departs from both lines by introducing a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline: a domain-agnostic probe stage trained on FFHQ provides a robust initial embedding, followed by a residual refinement stage that updates only cross-attention parameters in 3–5 steps. The multi-grained feature merging (CLIP + DINOv2) and mask regularization loss are novel architectural additions not present in either prior paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving DreamBooth-level subject fidelity from a single image in 5 iterations (versus 1000–5000 for optimization-based baselines) makes personalized generation feasible in interactive or resource-constrained settings. The residual refinement strategy—treating the probe-stage weights as an anchor and learning only a small residual on cross-attention—is a lightweight technique likely to be adopted in other personalization pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges that HybridBooth cannot perform fine-grained semantic edits (e.g., changing expressions or age) and inherits Stable Diffusion's weakness on fine details like fingers, leaving open whether incorporating vision-language models or 3D structural priors can close these gaps.
