## Problem

Existing non-exemplar Domain Incremental Learning (NEDIL) methods extend classifiers incrementally for each new domain within a shared frozen feature space, which causes inter-class confusion as the number of domains grows. These methods also treat each domain as an independent task, overlooking concept consistency across domains (e.g., treating a car in 'desert' and a car in 'forest' as distinct classes), and their domain selectors rely on features that conflate class and domain cues, degrading domain identification accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241300/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of unknown domain as input and produces a class label as the primary output. During inference, the image is first processed without shuffling to extract a feature vector, which is compared via cosine similarity against pre-computed domain prototypes (built from patch-shuffled training images in a 3×2 grid) to predict the domain label. The predicted domain label then selects the corresponding prompt token and alignment parameters from stored pools, which are injected into the frozen ViT-B/16 or CLIP backbone for final classification. No exemplars or stored samples from previous domains are required at any stage.

## Method

PINA replaces the per-domain extended classifier paradigm with a single Unified Classifier (UC) trained on the base domain and frozen thereafter, serving as a shared concept container for all domains. For each incremental domain, a Domain Specific Alignment (DSA) module—comprising a domain-specific prompt token and a lightweight encoder-decoder alignment branch (two linear layers + ReLU, hidden dimension d≪D)—is trained to align the domain's feature distribution to the base domain's concept space via residual connections into the frozen transformer layers. Two variants exist: PINA (shallow, alignment only in the final layer) and PINA-D (deep, alignment in every layer). For domain identification at test time, a Patch Shuffle Selector (PSS) computes domain prototypes by averaging features of patch-shuffled training images, which disrupts class-dependent information and yields cleaner domain signatures than K-NN or Nearest Mean Classifier baselines. Training uses only cross-entropy loss with no auxiliary regularization, and the total additional parameters are as low as 0.1%–1.2% of the backbone.

## Results

On the challenging DomainNet dataset (ViT-B/16 backbone), PINA-D achieves 62.23% average accuracy (A_T) with only 1.2% additional parameters, outperforming the SOTA non-exemplar method S-iPrompts (50.62%) by +11.61%. On CORe50, PINA-D reaches 90.01% A_T (vs. CODA-P's 85.68%), and on CDDB-Hard it achieves 78.20% A_T with a forgetting degree of only −0.73%. With the CLIP backbone, PINA-D attains 73.27% on DomainNet and 89.03% on CDDB, surpassing MoP-CLIP and S-liPrompts respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PINA-D decisively outperforms the primary SOTA baseline S-iPrompts [68] by +11.61% on DomainNet (ViT: 62.23 vs. 50.62) and by +3.69% on CDDB-Hard (ViT: 78.20 vs. 74.51), while using fewer additional parameters (1.2M vs. 1.8M). Against CODA-P [62] on CORe50, PINA-D gains +4.33% (90.01 vs. 85.68) with only 0.3M extra parameters versus CODA-P's 2.0M. On the CLIP backbone, PINA-D surpasses MoP-CLIP [46] by +3.57% on DomainNet (73.27 vs. 69.70) and by +0.38% on CDDB (89.03 vs. 88.65). The lighter PINA variant (shallow DSA) still beats S-iPrompts by +4.24% on DomainNet while using only 34% of its trainable parameters (0.6M vs. 1.8M). On CORe50 with CLIP, MoP-CLIP (92.29%) retains a slight edge over PINA-D (89.23%), the one comparison where the proposed method does not lead.

## Relation to Prior Work

PINA departs from the dominant 'extended classifier' paradigm in non-exemplar DIL (S-Prompts, CODA-Prompt, L2P, DualPrompt), where each new domain gets its own classifier head or prompt in the same feature space, causing inter-class confusion. Instead, it introduces a Unified Classifier trained once on the base domain and frozen thereafter, with a Domain Specific Alignment module that warps new-domain features into the base-domain concept space via lightweight encoder-decoder branches. The Patch Shuffle Selector for domain identification is a novel mechanism that repurposes the patch-level structure of ViTs to strip class-dependent cues, contrasting with K-NN or Nearest Mean Classifier approaches used by prior prompt-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified-classifier paradigm offers a cleaner conceptual framework for DIL—treating each domain as a partial view of the same object concept rather than an independent task—which could generalize to other continual learning settings beyond domain shifts. A notable limitation is the sensitivity to base-domain quality (66.67% vs. 56.52% on DomainNet depending on whether 'real' or 'quickdraw' is first), suggesting the frozen UC may not be robust to poor initialization. The authors note potential extension to detection and segmentation, but the current evaluation is limited to classification on three datasets, and the method's advantage narrows on smaller-class datasets like CDDB where parameter-count benefits diminish.
