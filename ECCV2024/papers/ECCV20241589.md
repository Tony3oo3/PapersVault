## Problem

Explicit mesh representations enable efficient, pipeline-compatible rendering but are difficult to optimize for complex topologies, while implicit field representations support smooth continuous optimization but suffer from low volume-rendering efficiency and imprecise sharp-surface recovery. Existing hybrid methods (e.g., DMTet, FlexiCubes) discretize the implicit function on a fixed tetrahedral grid, forfeiting the continuous-space optimization advantage, and cannot directly render from the implicit function to receive image supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images (e.g., 49 views per DTU scan) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It outputs a 3D surface mesh extracted via marching cubes at 512³ resolution during inference, together with a learned neural shader (MLP with multi-resolution hash encoding and 4th-degree sphere harmonics) that enables real-time, photorealistic rendering of the extracted mesh through standard graphics pipelines. Training operates on batches of 14,800 rays per iteration on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

Sur²f learns two parallel streams: a continuous implicit SDF (MLP with multi-resolution hash encoding) and an explicit surrogate surface mesh, whose vertices are deformed each iteration by a single-step SDF-driven rule (v ← v − f(v)·∇f(v)/‖∇f(v)‖), enforcing synchronization without per-iteration isosurface extraction. A shared neural shader c(x, n, F, ω; κ) unifies SDF-induced volume rendering (with NeuS-style sigmoid opacity) and differentiable surface rendering of the surrogate mesh, so both streams receive image supervision and are driven to converge on the same underlying surface. The synchronized surrogate mesh further enables surface-guided volume sampling: ray-surface intersections are obtained via ray casting, and sample points are drawn from a Gaussian N(t_S, σ²) centered at the intersection, with σ annealed from 0.5 to 0.01, concentrating computation near the surface and reducing training time by ~27× compared to hierarchical sampling. Training minimizes a combined loss of L1 (surface), Smooth L1 (volume), Eikonal regularization, and optional binary cross-entropy mask loss, with periodic marching-cubes re-booting every 500 iterations to handle topology changes.

## Results

On the DTU benchmark, Sur²f achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.66 (lower is better), outperforming NeuS2 (0.70), NeuS (1.07), UNISURF (1.02), and NDS (1.95), while matching NeuS2's training time of ~5 minutes. For rendering quality, Sur²f attains a mean PSNR of 33.95 dB, surpassing NeuS2 (33.29), UNISURF (30.93), NeuS (29.79), and NDS (25.27), with a rendering time of only 5 ms. On the NeRF synthetic dataset for inverse rendering, Sur²f achieves the best PSNR and MAE across all 8 objects compared to DMTet and FlexiCubes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sur²f outperforms all six baselines on the primary DTU benchmark. Against NeuS2 (the closest competitor), Sur²f improves mean CD from 0.70 to 0.66 and mean PSNR from 33.29 to 33.95, while matching its ~5-minute training time and reducing rendering time from 1 s to 5 ms. Against NeuS and UNISURF, the gains are substantial: CD drops by ~0.4 and PSNR rises by ~3–4 dB, with training time reduced from hours to minutes. Against NDS (explicit mesh optimization), Sur²f achieves a 3× lower CD and 8.7 dB higher PSNR. On the NeRF synthetic inverse-rendering task, Sur²f beats both DMTet and FlexiCubes on every object for both PSNR and MAE; for example, on the Ship object it improves PSNR from 26.0 (DMTet) / 25.9 (FlexiCubes) to 27.1 and reduces MAE from 34.2 / 39.7 to 22.7, indicating markedly better normal-map recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sur²f sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines of work: SDF-based implicit surface reconstruction via volume rendering (NeuS, VolSDF) and explicit mesh reconstruction via differentiable surface rendering (NDS, NVDIFFREC). It departs from prior hybrid representations like DMTet and FlexiCubes, which discretize the SDF on a tetrahedral grid and cannot directly render from the implicit function, by using a continuously-defined MLP-based SDF with multi-resolution hash encoding that supports both direct volume rendering and efficient surface rendering of a synchronized surrogate mesh. The shared neural shader unifying both rendering paths, and the surface-guided volume sampling strategy, are the paper's key technical departures from prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by maintaining a synchronized surrogate mesh, Sur²f achieves a ~27× speedup in volume rendering sampling (348s vs. 9538s for hierarchical sampling) while matching or exceeding reconstruction quality, and enables real-time (5 ms) rendering at inference via standard graphics pipelines. This makes high-quality neural surface reconstruction accessible in settings where NeuS-style volume rendering is too slow. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale scene reconstruction (the paper shows preliminary results on ScanNet and Tanks & Temples in the appendix) and whether the periodic re-booting of the surrogate mesh via marching cubes can be made fully differentiable to handle topology changes more gracefully.
