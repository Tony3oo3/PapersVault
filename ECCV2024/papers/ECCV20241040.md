## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic radiance fields from monocular videos is ill-posed due to sparse viewpoint information and scale ambiguity. Existing methods regularize motion using displacement or scene-flow fields but do not exploit the fact that real-world motion is governed by kinematic laws (velocity, acceleration, jerk relationships). This paper bridges the gap between sparse monocular observations and physical motion constraints by introducing a kinematic field that encodes higher-order motion quantities and is regularized with physics-driven losses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence with precomputed camera poses (SE(3)) and intrinsics, along with auxiliary signals (monocular depth, optical flow, dynamic masks) extracted by prior models. It produces rendered images at arbitrary novel times and viewpoints, and additionally outputs a spatio-temporal kinematic field that estimates point-wise velocity, acceleration, and jerk throughout the scene. The kinematic field is a tensor-decomposed representation queried at (x, y, z, t) coordinates, and displacement for any time offset Δt is recovered via a third-order Taylor approximation of these quantities.

## Method

The core contribution is a kinematic field that generalizes scene-flow to higher-order kinematic quantities (velocity, acceleration, jerk), represented via tensor decomposition (HexPlane-style) and jointly learned with dynamic (HexPlane) and static (TensoRF) radiance fields. Displacement for photometric consistency is computed through a Taylor series expansion of the kinematic quantities rather than a learned displacement network. Three physics-driven regularizers enforce physical plausibility: a kinematic integrity loss (a = ∂v/∂t + v·∇v, j = ∂a/∂t + v·∇a), a rigidity loss based on the first and second invariants of the strain rate tensor, and a transport loss derived from the simplified Navier-Stokes equation (∂σ/∂t + v·∇σ = 0). Training is self-supervised with no motion ground truth, combining photometric consistency on Taylor-deformed rays, cycle consistency over four timestamps, and the physics-based losses, all optimized jointly over 70k steps.

## Results

On the NDVS 24-frames protocol, the proposed method achieves 28.78 PSNR, 0.936 SSIM, and 0.042 LPIPS on full frames, and 22.13 PSNR, 0.774 SSIM, and 0.094 LPIPS on dynamic-only pixels, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On the 12-frames protocol, it reaches 26.33 PSNR and 0.071 LPIPS, surpassing the previous best (DynamicNeRF at 26.10 PSNR). The method also trains in approximately 7 GPU-hours per scene, roughly 12× faster than NSFF which requires ~96 GPU-hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the 24-frames protocol, the proposed method outperforms NSFF (the closest competitor) by 0.59 dB PSNR (28.78 vs. 28.19) and 0.22 dB on dynamic-only PSNR (22.13 vs. 21.91), while also improving SSIM and LPIPS across all settings. Against earlier baselines, the gains are substantial: NeRF is surpassed by 3.88 dB PSNR, Luo et al. by 7.41 dB, and Yoon et al. by 7.00 dB on full-frame PSNR. On the 12-frames protocol, the method edges out DynamicNeRF (26.33 vs. 26.10 PSNR) and RoDynRF (26.33 vs. 25.89 PSNR), while maintaining a lower LPIPS than RoDynRF (0.071 vs. 0.065 is a slight loss on LPIPS but a PSNR win). Notably, the method achieves these gains while training ~12× faster than NSFF (7 GPU-hours vs. ~96 GPU-hours), making it both more accurate and significantly more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic radiance field line of work (NeRF, D-NeRF, NSFF, DynamicNeRF) by replacing the conventional displacement/scene-flow field with a higher-order kinematic field that explicitly models velocity, acceleration, and jerk via Taylor approximation. It draws on physics-informed neural network techniques from fluid reconstruction (transport equations, Navier-Stokes simplifications) and continuum mechanics (strain rate tensor invariants) to impose physically grounded regularizers on the learned motion. The dynamic/static decomposition follows D²-NeRF, and the tensor-decomposition backbone (HexPlane, TensoRF) is adopted from recent efficient radiance field representations, positioning the contribution as a new motion representation and regularization strategy rather than a new rendering architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The kinematic field offers a principled way to inject physical plausibility into monocular dynamic scene reconstruction, which is inherently ill-posed due to scale ambiguity and sparse observations. The finding that jerk (3rd-order) is the sweet spot while higher orders (snap, crackle) degrade accuracy suggests a practical ceiling on how much kinematic detail monocular video can support, an important constraint for future work. However, evaluation is limited to the 8-scene NDVS benchmark, and the modest PSNR gains over NSFF (~0.6 dB) leave open whether the physics priors generalize to more complex, non-rigid, or long-duration scenes. The 12× training speedup over NSFF is a practical advantage that may encourage adoption of tensor-decomposition backbones in this setting.
