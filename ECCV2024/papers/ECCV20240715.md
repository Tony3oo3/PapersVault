## Problem

Current nucleus instance segmentation methods rely on regressing nuclear proxy maps (distance, contour, or offset maps) followed by carefully tuned, error-prone post-processing to recover individual instances. While SAM has shown strong generalization in medical imaging, its potential for automatic nucleus instance segmentation remains largely unexplored—existing SAM-based approaches either require impractical manual prompts or only produce semantic (non-instance) segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240715/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single H&E-stained histology image patch (256×256 for PanNuke, 500–600 px for CPM-17, 1000×1000 for Kumar) and directly outputs per-nucleus binary instance masks along with a class label per nucleus (five classes in PanNuke: Neoplastic, Epithelial, Inflammatory, Connective, Dead). Unlike proxy-map methods, no watershed, connected-component, or other post-processing step is needed: the prompter emits exactly one point prompt per detected nucleus, and the segmentor produces one mask per prompt in a one-to-one fashion.

## Method

PromptNucSeg couples a lightweight anchor-point prompter with a fine-tuned SAM segmentor. The prompter places a grid of anchor points on the image, extracts multi-scale features via a feature pyramid, and refines/classifies each anchor through MLP heads; a Hungarian-algorithm matching assigns anchors to ground-truth nuclear centroids, and an auxiliary nuclear-region segmentation head (trained with focal loss) guides attention to foreground and later filters false-positive prompts at inference. SAM is fine-tuned in a one-prompt-one-nucleus regime: for each nucleus, a positive point is sampled from its mask and the frozen prompt encoder plus updated image encoder and mask decoder predict the corresponding instance mask. A key design choice is treating the K nearest neighboring nuclei as negative prompts during both fine-tuning and inference, which suppresses over-segmentation of overlapping nuclei without requiring explicit overlap detection at test time.

## Results

On the PanNuke dataset (19 tissue types, 3-fold CV), PromptNucSeg-H achieves an average bPQ of 0.6924 and mPQ of 0.5123, outperforming the previous best CellViT-H (0.6793 bPQ, 0.4980 mPQ) by 1.3 bPQ and 1.4 mPQ. On Kumar, PromptNucSeg-H reaches AJI 0.622 and PQ 0.627, and on CPM-17 it achieves AJI 0.740 and PQ 0.733, exceeding the second-best by 1.9 and 2.7 points respectively. In terms of efficiency, PromptNucSeg-B requires only 59.0G MACs and runs at 27 FPS, substantially fewer computations and faster inference than U-Net-based baselines (e.g., PointNu-Net: 335.1G MACs, 11 FPS) while delivering higher mPQ (0.5095 vs. 0.4957).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptNucSeg-H outperforms all 15 baselines on the PanNuke dataset in both bPQ and mPQ, with the closest competitor being CellViT-H (0.6793 bPQ / 0.4980 mPQ) and PointNu-Net (0.6808 bPQ / 0.4957 mPQ), trailing by 1.3 and 1.4 points respectively. On Kumar, PromptNucSeg-H (AJI 0.622, PQ 0.627) edges out CIA-Net (AJI 0.620) and Full-Net (PQ 0.620), while on CPM-17 it leads PointNu-Net (AJI 0.712, PQ 0.706) by a substantial margin of 2.8 AJI and 2.7 PQ points. For per-class nucleus detection (Table 2), PromptNucSeg achieves the highest F1 across all five nuclear categories (Neoplastic 0.84, Epithelial 0.71, Inflammatory 0.76, Connective 0.59, Dead 0.46), surpassing CellViT-H and CPP-Net. In segmentation quality (Table 3), PromptNucSeg-H attains the best PQ for Neoplastic (0.598) and ties with CellViT-H for Epithelial (0.582), Inflammatory (0.441), Connective (0.433), and Dead (0.161). Notably, PromptNucSeg-B achieves a higher mPQ (0.5095) than CellViT-B (0.4923) and PointNu-Net (0.4957) while using 4–7× fewer MACs (59.0G vs. 232.0G/335.1G) and running at 27 FPS versus 11–20 FPS, highlighting its computational efficiency advantage over U-Net-based proxy-map regression methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant bottom-up paradigm in nucleus instance segmentation (regressing proxy maps like distance or contour fields followed by parameter-sensitive post-processing) by repurposing SAM's promptable architecture in a one-prompt-one-nucleus regime. The prompter component adapts the anchor-point refinement and Hungarian matching mechanism from point-based crowd counting [32] to automatically generate per-nucleus point prompts, while the segmentor is a fine-tuned SAM that predicts a mask per prompt. This positions the work as a new top-down approach that avoids bounding boxes entirely, using center points as a more precise localization cue for touching nuclei, and contrasts with prior SAM-for-medical-image efforts (CellViT, SPPNet) that either repurposed only SAM's encoder or restricted it to semantic segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of complex post-processing is a practical advantage for clinical deployment, and the efficiency gain (59G MACs, 27 FPS vs. 150–335G MACs, 7–20 FPS for U-Net-based competitors) while matching or exceeding accuracy makes the approach attractive for whole-slide image analysis. The finding that negative prompts must be incorporated during fine-tuning (not merely at inference) to avoid catastrophic forgetting is a useful practical insight for prompt-based segmentation more broadly. Open questions include whether prompt quality (the identified bottleneck, as shown by the practical-vs-oracle gap) can be improved with cheaper point annotations, and whether the approach scales to the tens of thousands of nuclei in a full WSI without prohibitive per-prompt inference cost.
