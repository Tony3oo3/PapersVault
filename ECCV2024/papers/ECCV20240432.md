## Problem

Tiny object detection suffers from severe information loss during backbone downsampling, causing weak signals of very tiny objects (2–8 pixels) to be nearly wiped out in feature maps. Existing generative approaches (super-resolution, GANs) attempt to restore these features but introduce spurious textures and artifacts that degrade detection. No prior method directly exploits the information loss itself as a detectable signal to guide feature enhancement for tiny objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240432/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and outputs bounding boxes with class labels, following the standard object detection paradigm. Internally, the FPN P2 feature map (at 1/4 spatial resolution) is fed into a reconstruction head that upsamples it back to full input resolution via two transpose-convolution stages, producing a reconstructed image. The element-wise absolute difference between this reconstruction and the original input, averaged over the three color channels, yields a single-channel difference map at full resolution that is then resized to P2 dimensions for feature enhancement.

## Method

SR-TOD introduces a self-reconstruction mechanism into the detector's FPN neck: a lightweight reconstruction head (two upsampling blocks with transpose convolutions) reconstructs the input image from the P2 feature map, trained with MSE loss. The difference map between the reconstructed and original images reveals regions of significant information loss, which strongly correlate with tiny object locations. A Difference Map Guided Feature Enhancement (DGFE) module then converts this spatial prior into an element-wise attention matrix by combining a learnable-threshold-filtered binary difference map (spatial dimension) with SE-style channel reweighting from P2, and applies it to enhance P2 before it enters the detection head. The framework is plug-and-play, requiring only modification of the FPN top-down path, and is compatible with both CNN-based and transformer-based detectors.

## Results

On the newly proposed DroneSwarms dataset (average object size ~7.9 px), RFLA w/ SR-TOD achieves 39.0 AP, surpassing the strongest baseline DetectoRS (37.9 AP) by 1.1 AP and the Cascade R-CNN baseline by 2.1 AP. On VisDrone2019, RFLA w/ SR-TOD reaches 27.8 AP (+0.6 over RFLA), and on AI-TOD, DetectoRS w/ SR-TOD attains 24.0 AP (+2.3 over RFLA). The most consistent gains appear in the very-tiny (APvt) and tiny (APt) sub-metrics, e.g., +2.3 APvt and +1.9 APt on DroneSwarms, confirming the method's focus on the hardest scale range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DroneSwarms, SR-TOD consistently improves every backbone it is plugged into: Cascade R-CNN gains +1.9 AP (36.4→38.3), DetectoRS gains +0.9 AP (37.9→38.8), and RFLA gains +2.1 AP (36.9→39.0), with the largest sub-metric gains in APvt (+2.3) and APt (+1.9). The method also outperforms transformer-based detectors (DINO 35.4, Deformable-DETR 32.6) by 3.6 and 6.4 AP respectively, and even DINO w/ SR-TOD improves by 0.2 AP, confirming plug-and-play compatibility. On VisDrone2019, RFLA w/ SR-TOD leads with 27.8 AP (+0.6 over RFLA, +1.5 over DetectoRS), while Cascade R-CNN w/ SR-TOD achieves the best APs (24.7) among all entries. On AI-TOD, DetectoRS w/ SR-TOD reaches 24.0 AP, surpassing RFLA (21.7) by 2.3 AP and HANet (22.1) by 1.9 AP, with a notable +10.1 APvt gain over the DetectoRS baseline. The method does not conflict with orthogonal improvements (e.g., RFLA label assignment, DetectoRS recursive FPN), as it targets information loss rather than label assignment or feature hierarchy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant generative/super-resolution paradigm for tiny object detection (GAN-based feature imitation, texture transfer) by instead repurposing the detector's own feature maps for image self-reconstruction. The reconstruction head is architecturally inspired by U-Net and inserted into the FPN top-down path, while the Difference Map Guided Feature Enhancement module draws on channel-attention ideas (SE-style reweighting) but applies them to a reconstruction-error signal rather than learned statistics. The framework is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play add-on to existing FPN-based detectors (Cascade R-CNN, DetectoRS, RFLA, RetinaNet, DINO), and the authors position it as complementary to label-assignment strategies like RFLA rather than a replacement for any specific backbone or neck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that pixel-level reconstruction error from the detector's own features acts as a spatial prior for where tiny-object information has been lost—offers a lightweight, training-free-at-inference alternative to GAN-based enhancement, avoiding spurious texture fabrication. The DroneSwarms dataset (avg. object size ~7.9 px) fills a notable gap in anti-UAV benchmarks, which previously lacked very-tiny-scale targets. However, the practical gains are moderate (1–2 AP over strong baselines), the high-frequency difference map variant yields only a marginal 0.1 AP edge over the pixel version, and the reconstruction head alone contributes just 0.1 AP, suggesting the approach's ceiling may be limited. Open questions include whether the difference-map prior remains informative at even smaller scales (sub-4 px), whether the added reconstruction head's inference cost is acceptable for real-time anti-UAV deployment, and whether the mechanism transfers to video or multi-modal settings.
