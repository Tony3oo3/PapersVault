## Problem

Fully supervised referring image segmentation (RIS) methods require expensive pixel-level annotations (instance masks paired with referring expressions), while existing weakly supervised and zero-shot alternatives lack precise localization because their training data lacks grounding annotations and uses ambiguous scene-level captions. This paper addresses the gap by automatically generating high-quality pseudo-supervisions—precise masks with distinctive, target-specific referring expressions—without any human labeling cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241806/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes unlabeled RGB images (16,992 from the RefCOCO+ training set) and produces paired pseudo-supervisions: class-agnostic instance segmentation masks (reduced from ~120 SAM masks to ~5 per image via CutLER overlay) and distinctive referring expressions (44 caption candidates generated per mask, filtered to those with distinctiveness score above τ=1.3). The generated (mask, caption) pairs are then used to train any supervised RIS model (CRIS or ETRIS) at scale, with the trained model producing a segmentation mask given an image and a natural language query at inference time.

## Method

Pseudo-RIS is a three-stage pipeline: (1) SAM extracts class-agnostic instance masks, overlaid with CutLER to reduce mask count; (2) a frozen CoCa captioning model generates 44 candidate captions per mask using a novel 'distinctive caption sampling' decoding strategy that calibrates the target's next-word distribution by subtracting a similarity-weighted average of other patches' word distributions (weighted by cosine similarity of visual embeddings), thereby suppressing generic words shared across objects and amplifying target-specific descriptors; (3) a 'distinctiveness-based text filtering' step scores each candidate using a DoS metric derived from CLIP, combining uniqueness (target CLIP score divided by max other-mask CLIP score) and correctness (CLIP score between the masked region and the extracted target noun phrase), discarding captions below threshold τ=1.3. The key insight is that both generation-time calibration and post-hoc filtering are necessary: sampling alone cannot prevent the captioning model from describing non-targeted objects visible in the cropped patch, and filtering alone cannot recover captions that were never generated with sufficient target specificity.

## Results

On RefCOCO val, the best pseudo-supervised configuration (Ours + ETRIS) achieves 37.33 oIoU and 41.05 mIoU, surpassing the strongest zero-shot method Ref-Diff (35.16 oIoU / 37.21 mIoU) and the best weakly-supervised method TRIS (28.12 oIoU / 31.17 mIoU) by large margins. On RefCOCO+ testA, Ours + ETRIS reaches 46.43 oIoU and 51.42 mIoU, outperforming Ref-Diff (38.66 / 40.51) by ~8 oIoU points. In the open-world PhraseCut evaluation, the pseudo-supervised model (33.70 mIoU) substantially outperforms fully supervised models trained on RefCOCO variants (13.03–16.28 mIoU) and even approaches the upper bound of a fully supervised model trained on PhraseCut itself (38.10 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two zero-shot baselines, Ours + ETRIS consistently wins: it beats Ref-Diff [53] by 2.17 oIoU on RefCOCO val (37.33 vs 35.16) and by 7.77 oIoU on RefCOCO+ testA (46.43 vs 38.66), while also surpassing Global-Local [91] by 12.78 oIoU on RefCOCO val (37.33 vs 24.55). Against weakly-supervised methods, the margin is even larger: Ours + ETRIS outperforms TRIS [40] by 9.21 oIoU on RefCOCO val (37.33 vs 28.12) and SaG [27] by 6.93 oIoU (37.33 vs 30.40); Lee et al. [34] is only reported in the mIoU table where Ours + ETRIS leads by 9.99 mIoU on RefCOCO val (41.05 vs 31.06). Against the fully supervised upper bounds (CRIS [78] and ETRIS [85]), the pseudo-supervised model is substantially lower (e.g., 37.33 vs 69.36 oIoU on RefCOCO val), which is expected since no human labels are used. However, in the cross-domain and open-world PhraseCut evaluation, the pseudo-supervised model (33.70 mIoU) dramatically outperforms fully supervised models trained on RefCOCO variants (13.03–16.28 mIoU) and even exceeds the zero-shot Global-Local [91] (29.73 mIoU), demonstrating superior generalization to unseen object categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-supervision generation paradigm from video localization and referring expression comprehension (e.g., Pseudo-Q's heuristic template-based queries) to referring image segmentation, replacing hand-crafted heuristics with a captioning foundation model (CoCa) and a novel decoding strategy. It departs from prior zero-shot RIS methods (Global-Local, Ref-Diff) that directly leverage vision-language models at inference time, instead using foundation models (SAM, CoCa, CLIP) offline to produce training data that can train any standard supervised RIS architecture. The distinctive caption sampling mechanism, which calibrates word probabilities by contrasting the target patch against other patches in the same image, is a new decoding strategy that builds on contrastive decoding ideas but applies them to spatially-grounded caption generation rather than open-ended text generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is eliminating the need for expensive pixel-level human annotation in RIS while simultaneously addressing the open-world generalization bottleneck: pseudo-supervised models maintain consistent performance across domains (RefCOCO vs. PhraseCut) where fully supervised models degrade sharply due to domain gaps in textual style and object categories. The finding that no-human-label training outperforms fully supervised training on unseen object categories challenges the assumption that more human data always yields better generalization. Open questions include whether the method can explicitly generate positional and relational descriptions (acknowledged as a limitation), how it scales to video or 3D segmentation, and whether the inherited biases from web-crawled captioning data can be systematically mitigated.
