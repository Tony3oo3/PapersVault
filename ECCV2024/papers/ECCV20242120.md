## Problem

Most 3D-aware generative models produce single objects or faces from an outside-in viewpoint, while methods that generate full interior scenes typically require depth maps or annotated camera poses during training, or lack explicit camera positioning control. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of synthesizing omnidirectional interior scenes from unposed, unlabeled RGB images while still enabling free camera movement within the generated environment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242120/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a latent vector z ~ N(0,I) and a 3D camera position p (sampled from a truncated Gaussian within a unit sphere, |p| ≤ 0.75) and produces a 512×256 equirectangular omnidirectional image. The camera position is not a conditioning signal in the usual sense; instead, it geometrically reprojects the input Fourier features by shifting the ray-sphere intersection, so the same latent with different p values yields the same scene from different viewpoints. Training uses only unposed RGB images from Pano3D with no depth or pose annotations.

## Method

EpipolarGAN builds on StyleGAN3-T, replacing its 2D planar input frequencies with volumetric 3D Fourier features sampled on a unit sphere and projected to an equirectangular plane, enabling translational reprojection: given a camera position, rays through each pixel intersect the sphere at shifted points, simulating a perspective change without any explicit scene geometry. To enforce multi-view consistency, an epipolar loss performs cross-correlation feature matching along epipolar arcs in the intermediate synthesis layers, penalizing both the mean correlation (encouraging a single strong peak) and misalignment of peaks across layers. A spatially varying polar filter blends between equatorial and polar bandlimiting kernels weighted by cos(φ) to suppress the angular-frequency distortion near the poles. Spherical augmentations apply 3D rotations to pixel direction vectors rather than 2D image transforms, and border fixing uses horizontal cyclic and vertical reflective (with 180° shift) pixel blitting to maintain seamless equirectangular topology.

## Results

On the VLN-CE/Matterport3D evaluation set, EpipolarGAN achieves an FID of 10.78, substantially outperforming GSN (43.32) and GAUDI (18.52). For multi-view consistency, EpipolarGAN attains a SfM RMSE of 6.64×10⁻³ (lower is better), beating GSN's 7.80×10⁻³, though GSN recovers a slightly higher fraction of poses (SfM NP 0.77 vs. 0.716). In the ablation at 2000 kimgs, the full model (with epipolar loss and polar filtering) achieves FID 15.0 and FID-Poles 58.6, compared to StyleGAN3-T's FID 16.7 and FID-Poles 73.0, while also enabling explicit camera translation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GSN [11], EpipolarGAN achieves a dramatically lower FID (10.78 vs. 43.32) and a lower SfM RMSE (6.64 vs. 7.80×10⁻³), indicating both higher image quality and better multi-view geometric coherence; however, GSN recovers a slightly higher fraction of camera poses (SfM NP 0.77 vs. 0.716). Against GAUDI [4], EpipolarGAN improves FID by roughly 42% (10.78 vs. 18.52), though GAUDI does not report SfM metrics and requires annotated camera poses and per-frame depth at training time. Compared to StyleGAN3-T [19] at the 2000-kimg ablation stage, EpipolarGAN achieves a marginally lower FID (15.0 vs. 16.7) and a substantially lower FID-Poles (58.6 vs. 73.0), reflecting reduced polar distortion from the polar filtering component. Against CubeGAN [27], both methods produce seamless, distortion-free omnidirectional images, but CubeGAN does not support explicit camera position control, which is the key differentiator of EpipolarGAN. The authors note that FID values across methods are not directly comparable due to differences in evaluation datasets (Pano3D vs. VLN-CE/Matterport3D).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends StyleGAN3's alias-free synthesis architecture (specifically the StyleGAN3-T translational equivariance configuration) to the equirectangular domain, and generalizes CubeGAN's volumetric Fourier feature scheme from a cube-map to a spherical manifold. It departs from prior scene-generation methods (BIPS, GSN, GAUDI) by eliminating the need for depth or camera pose annotations, and from omnidirectional generators (CubeGAN, DiffCollage) by introducing explicit camera position control via translational reprojection of input features. The epipolar loss, which enforces multi-view consistency through feature matching along epipolar arcs in intermediate synthesis layers, is a novel geometric constraint not previously applied in GAN training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously achieve omnidirectional output, explicit camera position control, and training without depth or pose annotations, filling a gap identified in Table 1 where no prior method satisfies all three criteria. The SfM-based reconstruction metric offers a practical way to evaluate multi-view coherence in generative settings where ground-truth geometry is unavailable. Key limitations remain: camera motion is bounded by the sampling sphere radius, occlusions conflict with the epipolar loss, and the approach does not model explicit 3D geometry, limiting representational capacity for complex environments. Stabilizing unbounded translation via phase offsetting is identified as a promising but unresolved direction.
