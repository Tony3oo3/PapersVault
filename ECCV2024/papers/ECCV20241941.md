## Problem

Existing object placement methods rely primarily on visual feature extraction and GAN-based or reinforcement learning frameworks, producing placements that are visually plausible but semantically unrealistic (e.g., objects floating in mid-air). The paper identifies that these methods lack the common sense reasoning humans naturally apply before placing an object—considering physical constraints and contextual appropriateness—leading to a gap between visual coherence and contextual correctness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241941/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a foreground image (3×H×W), its binary mask (1×H×W), a background image (3×H×W), and a fixed text prompt ('Where should the foreground object be appropriately placed in the background?') as input. It first generates a natural-language caption describing the appropriate placement location, then outputs multiple candidate placement tuples (r, x, y) via multi-head regression, from which a scoring network selects the final placement. The foreground is transformed by the chosen (r, x, y) and composited onto the background to produce the final image.

## Method

CSENet builds on LLaVA (7B parameters) as a backbone to exploit implicit common sense from pre-training, and introduces a 'Think Before Placement' pipeline where the model first generates a position-descriptive caption before regressing coordinates. Explicit common sense is injected through position-relative captions (Simple: 'on the table'; Detailed: 'on the table, rather than on the floor') auto-generated by Qwen-VL-Chat and used as training targets. A regression token [REG] is appended to the language output, followed by multiple fully-connected heads that each predict a distinct (r, x, y) tuple, encouraging diverse candidate placements. Training combines three losses: cross-entropy captioning loss, a ground-truth loss (MSE to positive samples plus a margin-based penalty away from negative samples on x,y only), and a pairwise diversity loss that penalizes heads for producing near-identical predictions. A single-layer linear scoring network, following IOPRE, ranks the multi-head candidates and selects the highest-scoring placement.

## Results

On the OPA dataset, CSENet achieves 94.0% accuracy, 0.266 user study score, and 17.51 FID, outperforming the strongest baseline IOPRE (89.5% Acc., 0.234 U.S., 21.59 FID) by 4.5 percentage points in accuracy and 0.032 in user preference. On the zero-shot OPAZ dataset, CSENet reaches 61.8% accuracy and 0.251 user study score, surpassing IOPRE (58.6% Acc., 0.221 U.S.) while IOPRE retains a lower FID (27.8 vs. 42.1). The model also achieves the best Mean IoU (0.321) among all methods on OPA, though GracoNet edges it slightly (0.336).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSENet outperforms all five baselines on the OPA dataset in user study score, accuracy, and FID. Against the strongest baseline IOPRE (reinforcement learning), CSENet gains 4.5% in accuracy (94.0 vs. 89.5) and 0.032 in user preference, while reducing FID from 21.59 to 17.51. GracoNet (graph-based) is the second-best in accuracy (84.7%) but trails CSENet by nearly 10 points; it does achieve a slightly higher Mean IoU (0.336 vs. 0.321). PlaceNet and TERSE, both GAN-based, lag substantially in accuracy (68.3% and 67.9% respectively) and FID. On the zero-shot OPAZ benchmark, CSENet again leads in accuracy (61.8%) and user study (0.251) over all baselines, though IOPRE achieves a notably lower FID (27.8 vs. 42.1), suggesting CSENet's composite images are slightly less photorealistic in unseen scenarios despite better placement decisions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant GAN-based and reinforcement-learning paradigms in object placement (TERSE, PlaceNet, GracoNet, IOPRE) and the single-path transformer approach of TopNet by repurposing a large vision-language model (LLaVA) as the placement backbone. The key conceptual shift is the 'Think Before Placement' pipeline: a VLM first generates a semantic caption describing where the object should go, and only then regresses concrete (r, x, y) parameters via a multi-head mechanism. The confidence-estimation scoring head is borrowed directly from IOPRE, and the explicit common-sense annotations are generated by Qwen-VL-Chat, making the method a hybrid of implicit (pre-trained VLM knowledge) and explicit (caption-guided) reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that a large multimodal model's built-in common sense can meaningfully improve object placement, and the OPAZ zero-shot benchmark provides a useful stress test for generalization beyond the OPA training distribution. However, the 7B-parameter LLaVA backbone makes the approach computationally expensive (4× A40 GPUs for training), and the reliance on externally generated captions adds a non-trivial annotation pipeline. Open questions include whether the captioning step can be distilled into a lighter module, whether the approach extends to multi-object or 3D placement, and whether the zero-shot FID gap versus IOPRE (42.1 vs 27.8) reflects a fundamental limitation of VLM-based regression for photometric quality.
