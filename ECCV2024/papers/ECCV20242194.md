## Problem

Video-based seizure detection remains impractical for continuous residential monitoring because existing RGB-based methods are computationally heavy and struggle with short detection latency, while skeleton-based GCN approaches that use raw joint coordinates fail to capture the subtle behavioral changes at seizure onset. Additionally, no public seizure video dataset exists, and pre-trained pose estimators cannot reliably track epileptic patients' joints in clinical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5-second RGB video clips (1920×1080 surveillance footage) and a fine-tuned lightweight-openpose model that produces per-joint heatmaps. From these, 32×32 patches are extracted around each of 15 joints via Gaussian filtering (σ=0.3), yielding a tensor of shape N×T×H×W×3 that is embedded into 1D feature vectors. The model outputs a continuous probability in [0,1] per clip (not a binary label), which is then fed into an accumulative decision rule (τ=3s window, DT=0.3 threshold) to determine the precise seizure onset timestamp.

## Method

VSViG replaces traditional skeleton coordinate inputs with skeleton-based patch embeddings: each joint's local RGB appearance is captured by filtering the raw frame with a 2D Gaussian centered at the joint, then embedded via a trainable patch embedding layer. Spatial modeling uses a partition strategy where 15 joints are grouped into 5 partitions of 3, and max-relative graph convolution is applied first across partitions (inter) then within partitions (intra), following a coarse-to-fine hierarchy. Temporal modeling treats the partition-ordered joint sequence as a 3D volume processed by residual 3D-CNN blocks (K_S=3, K_T=3), with dynamic partition shuffling to break the 1D adjacency limitation. The task is framed as regression with exponentially increasing transition labels rather than binary classification, enabling the model to output graded probabilities that support early detection before full convulsion onset.

## Results

On the Hospital EMU Seizure Video Dataset (14 patients, 33 seizures), VSViG achieves 5.9% RMSE, outperforming the best prior skeleton-based method HDGCN (7.9%) by 2.0 percentage points and the best RGB-based seizure detection method R3D+ViT (14.2%) by 8.3 points. The lightweight variant VSViG-Light reaches 6.1% RMSE with only 0.44 GFLOPs and 1.4M parameters, making it the most efficient model in the comparison. With an accumulative decision rule (DT=0.3), the system achieves a 5.1 s detection latency after EEG onset, a 13.1 s advance before clinical onset, and zero false detections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSViG outperforms all 12 baselines on RMSE. Against the four RGB-based seizure detection methods (I3D+LSTM 18.7%, CNN+LSTM 17.7%, R3D+LSTM 16.5%, R3D+ViT 14.2%), VSViG reduces error by 8.3–12.8 points while also being far more computationally efficient (1.76 vs. 5–163 GFLOPs). Among skeleton-based action recognition models, VSViG beats the strongest prior method HDGCN (7.9%) by 2.0 points and CTRGCN (8.3%) by 2.4 points. The gap narrows for the most recent GCN baselines (AAGCN 9.5%, CTRGCN 8.3%, HDGCN 7.9%) but remains consistent. Notably, VSViG-Light (6.1%, 0.44 GFLOPs, 1.4M params) achieves near-parity with the full VSViG while being 4× fewer FLOPs than HDGCN and matching the parameter count of DGSTGCN and CTRGCN, positioning it as the most deployable option for real-time residential monitoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Vision GNN (ViG) from Han et al. (NeurIPS 2022) from image recognition to skeleton-based spatiotemporal action recognition, introducing a partition strategy (inter-partition, intra-partition, dynamic partition) for graph construction that has no direct precedent in prior skeleton-based GCN work. It departs from both RGB-based video seizure detection methods (I3D, R3D, etc.) and coordinate-based skeleton GCNs (STGCN, CTRGCN, HDGCN) by using skeleton-based patch embeddings—small RGB patches filtered by Gaussian heatmaps around each joint—as input, rather than raw frames or coordinate triplets. The regression-based formulation (outputting continuous probabilities rather than binary ictal/interictal labels) is a deliberate departure from prior seizure detection work that treated the problem as classification or action anticipation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving a deployable real-time seizure detector (0.44G FLOPs, 1.4M parameters for the light version) with 5.1s post-EEG-onset latency and 13.1s pre-clinical-onset advance at zero false detection rate, which could enable residential monitoring without EEG hardware. However, the evaluation is conducted on a very small dataset (14 patients, 33 seizures), making generalizability to diverse seizure types, patient populations, and camera setups a major open question. The authors themselves note the need for more data and suggest extension to other movement-based disorders (Parkinson's, fall detection), but the current evidence base is too narrow to confirm broad applicability.
