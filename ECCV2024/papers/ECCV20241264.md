## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary semantic image segmentation (OVSIS) methods rely on public datasets not designed for open-vocabulary settings and largely ignore camouflaged objects in complex natural scenes, where objects share color, texture, and size with their background. Pre-trained VLMs like CLIP carry an inherent object-concept bias that limits their ability to segment such imperceptible targets, and no dedicated benchmark or method exists for this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241264/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image (resized to 384×384) and a set of class name strings as text input; it produces a per-pixel segmentation mask and a class label for the detected object via masked average pooling and cosine similarity in the CLIP embedding space. During training only, auxiliary depth maps (from monocular depth estimation) and edge maps (from morphological dilation/erosion) are used as supervision signals for the structure enhancement branches, but they are not required at test time, avoiding any information leakage.

## Method

OVCoser is a single-stage encoder-decoder built on a frozen CLIP-ConvNeXt-L backbone. The decoder performs iterative refinement (T=2) where each stage combines three mechanisms: (1) Semantic Guidance (SG), which injects normalized class text embeddings into multi-head self-attention as a class guidance vector that modulates the value stream, highlighting semantically relevant spatial cues; (2) Structure Enhancement (SE), which fuses edge and depth features extracted by lightweight convolutional branches into the low-level image features via a dedicated structure enhancement attention with learnable weighting; and (3) top-down feedback, where the coarse segmentation from the previous iteration is used to compute an object-aware pooled representation and a visual-textual correlation matrix that re-modulate the spatial activation map in the next iteration. Task-specific CamoPrompts (e.g., 'A photo of the <class> camouflaged to blend in with its surroundings') are ensembled to produce the final text embedding. Training uses a weighted segmentation loss plus auxiliary dice loss for edges and L1+SSIM loss for depth, with only ~7M trainable parameters (<2% of total) and 0.2T FLOPs.

## Results

On the proposed OVCamo dataset (trained on 14 seen classes, tested on 61 unseen classes), OVCoser achieves cSm=0.579, cFβω=0.490, cMAE=0.336, cFβ=0.520, cEm=0.616, and cIoU=0.443, substantially outperforming all existing OVSIS methods re-trained on the same training split. The best competing method, SAN, reaches only cSm=0.275 and cIoU=0.189, meaning OVCoser more than doubles the segmentation quality. Under zero-shot transfer (S.I, weights trained on COCO-Stuff without OVCamo fine-tuning), the strongest baseline CAT-Seg achieves cSm=0.430, highlighting that existing methods partially generalize but still fall well short of the task-tailored OVCoser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVCoser decisively outperforms all six baselines under the fair same-training-set comparison (S.III). Against the strongest competitor SAN (cSm=0.275, cIoU=0.189), OVCoser more than doubles cSm to 0.579 and cIoU to 0.443, a gap of over 110% relative improvement. ODISE (cSm=0.187) and CAT-Seg (cSm=0.181) are mid-tier, while SimSeg (cSm=0.053), OVSeg (cSm=0.024), and FC-CLIP (cSm=0.080) perform very poorly, suggesting their architectures are poorly suited to the camouflaged-object setting. Notably, under zero-shot transfer from COCO-Stuff (S.I), CAT-Seg achieves cSm=0.430 and SAN achieves 0.414, showing that pre-training on large-scale data provides some generalization, but direct fine-tuning on OVCamo (S.II) actually degrades most methods (e.g., CAT-Seg drops to 0.185), indicating catastrophic forgetting. OVCoser's advantage is attributed to its task-specific iterative refinement with multi-source cues (depth, edge, semantic guidance) rather than relying solely on the CLIP alignment space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on frozen CLIP as the open-vocabulary backbone, following the single-stage paradigm established by SAN, CAT-Seg, and FC-CLIP, but departs from them by integrating task-specific multi-source cues (depth, edge, class semantics) that standard OVSIS methods ignore. It also extends the class-agnostic camouflaged scene understanding (CSU) literature by adding open-vocabulary class recognition, and introduces iterative top-down refinement inspired by cognitive attention models. The CamoPrompts template set represents a task-specific departure from generic CLIP prompt engineering, explicitly encoding camouflage context into the textual embedding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OVCamo benchmark (11,483 images, 75 classes) fills a clear gap: no prior open-vocabulary segmentation dataset targets camouflaged objects, and existing OVSIS benchmarks suffer from semantic overlap and lack of concealment complexity. The finding that existing OVSIS methods degrade when fine-tuned or retrained on OVCamo (S.II/S.III) compared to using COCO-trained weights (S.I) suggests that general scene knowledge is fragile under domain shift to camouflage, an important caveat for practitioners. However, the ideal performance ceiling (cSm 0.703) versus the achieved 0.579 indicates substantial room for improvement, and the authors acknowledge that more powerful paradigms may be needed. Open questions include whether the depth/edge auxiliary supervision generalizes to other occluded-object tasks and whether the 14-class training split is sufficient for robust open-vocabulary generalization.
