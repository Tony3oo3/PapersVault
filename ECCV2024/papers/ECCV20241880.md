## Problem

Existing shadow removal methods oversimplify the task by restricting to a single light source, flat surfaces, and excluding self-shadows from complex geometries, while also relying heavily on shadow mask priors that are impractical to obtain in real-world scenarios. This paper introduces Ambient Lighting Normalization (ALN) as a broader, more realistic restoration task that unifies image restoration and shadow removal under complex multi-source lighting with self-shadows, and provides the first large-scale high-resolution dataset (Ambient6K) to support it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241880/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image captured under non-optimal multi-directional lighting (no shadow mask or auxiliary input required) and produces a fully lighting-normalized RGB image at the same resolution. The Ambient6K dataset provides 2560×1920 paired samples (input with complex shadows from 3 directional lights, ground truth under 5 softbox lights), with 5000/500/500 train/val/test splits. On conventional benchmarks (ISTD, ISTD+, SRD), the model operates at 640×480 or 512×512 resolution, still without mask inputs.

## Method

IFBlend is a CNN-based encoder-decoder that decomposes input features into low-frequency (via average pooling) and high-frequency (via max pooling) domains at each stage, then applies a secondary decomposition using both learned frequency blocks (LFB/HFB) and explicit Haar DWT. The decoder fuses domain-specific features through a cross-attention module (WA-SAM) that computes an affinity matrix between image and frequency features, enabling selective modification of local regions based on their illumination characteristics. The key design principle is 'shrinkage multi-band fusion': by separately combining low- and high-frequency components with their image counterparts before merging, the model maximizes joint entropy and pulls domain-specific features in opposing directions, achieving region-aware normalization without any shadow localization prior. Training uses L1 loss plus SSIM loss, with the Global Context Branch based on ConvNeXt.

## Results

On the newly introduced Ambient6K benchmark (1280×960 px), IFBlend achieves 21.443 dB PSNR, 0.819 SSIM, and 0.128 LPIPS, setting new SOTA across all three metrics. It outperforms the second-best method HINet by +0.59 dB PSNR while using ~40% fewer MACs (26.01G vs. 42.68G). On conventional shadow removal benchmarks, IFBlend (mask-free) achieves 3.52 RMSE on ISTD and 2.40 RMSE on ISTD+, and 31.95 dB PSNR on SRD, remaining competitive with mask-based SOTA while requiring no shadow mask input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Ambient6K, IFBlend outperforms all 11 compared methods on PSNR, with the largest margins over shadow-specific models DCShadowNet (+3.71 dB) and SpA-Former (+1.59 dB), and a +0.30 dB gain over the next-best restoration model Restormer. Among frequency-based methods (SFNet, DW-NET, SpA-Former), IFBlend leads by +0.7 to +1.6 dB PSNR. On ISTD/ISTD+, IFBlend (mask-free) trails the best mask-based methods—ShadowFormer (2.43/1.93 RMSE) and DNSR (2.84/2.24)—by 0.09–0.47 RMSE, but decisively beats mask-free baselines DCShadowNet (4.06/4.60) and DeS3 (n/a/2.80), as well as retrained SwinIR, Uformer, and Restormer. On SRD, IFBlend achieves 31.95 dB PSNR, surpassing DCShadowNet (30.85) and DeS3 (30.38) by over 1 dB, and narrowing the gap to mask-based ShadowFormer (32.38) to just 0.43 dB while matching its SSIM (0.950 vs. 0.955). Overall, IFBlend is the strongest mask-free method across all three benchmarks and the top performer on the new Ambient6K dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing shadow removal as a special case of a broader Ambient Lighting Normalization (ALN) task, departing from the single-light-source, flat-surface, external-occluder assumptions that dominate existing benchmarks (ISTD, SRD, WSRD). Conceptually, it repurposes general image restoration architectures (SwinIR, Restormer, Uformer, NAFNet) and frequency-domain tools (Haar DWT from dehazing/denoising literature) for the shadow domain, demonstrating that mask-free restoration models are competitive with mask-prior shadow removal methods. The IFBlend architecture introduces a locality-aware shrinkage fusion of image and frequency features, extending global frequency-branch designs (SFNet, DW-NET, SpA-Former) with region-selective low/high-frequency decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ambient6K dataset (6,000 samples at 2560×1920, multi-source lighting, self-shadows, 32 surface types) is the first of its kind and addresses a real gap: existing benchmarks exclude the most common shadow type (self-shadow) and restrict surfaces to smooth ones. The observation that no method exceeds 22 dB PSNR on ALN—compared to 30+ dB on standard restoration tasks—quantifies how much harder realistic lighting normalization is and signals substantial room for future research. A key open question is whether the mask-free paradigm can close the remaining gap to mask-based methods as scene complexity increases, and whether the ALN formulation generalizes to in-the-wild captures beyond the controlled studio setup.
