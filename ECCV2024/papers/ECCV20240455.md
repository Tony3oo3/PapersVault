## Problem

Joint optimization of camera poses and NeRF is notoriously ill-posed: the standard compact SE(3) pose parameterization suffers from poor convergence basins when solved simultaneously with the neural field. While overparameterizing poses with an MLP can improve convergence, the paper shows that naive MLP-based warp learning fails catastrophically without an explicit invertibility constraint, and that this constraint is tightly coupled to the choice of overparameterization strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB images with known intrinsics but unknown extrinsic poses, treating each pixel as a 3D ray in camera coordinates (pixel location plus camera center). The INN outputs per-pixel world-coordinate locations from which a global rigid SE(3) pose is recovered via a closed-form Umeyama registration (or DLT for 2D experiments). The final outputs are the estimated camera poses, a trained NeRF for novel view synthesis, and optionally a neural surface reconstruction via Voxurf for geometry evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is a single globally shared Invertible Neural Network (NDR-INN architecture) that maps camera-coordinate rays to world-coordinate rays, conditioned on a per-frame learnable latent code, replacing the explicit SE(3) pose parameterization. Because the INN is bijective by construction (affine coupling blocks), it enforces the one-to-one correspondence between camera and world space without requiring a separate backward network, halving the computational overhead of implicit-invertible MLP baselines. A rigidity prior (Eq. 5) is added to the photometric loss to recover a global rigid transformation from the INN's per-pixel correspondences. The key empirical finding is that the INN's homeomorphic property—predicting general continuous invertible transformations rather than rigid motions during optimization—provides a more flexible trajectory through the loss landscape, avoiding the suboptimal local minima that trap SE(3)-constrained methods like L2G.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset, the INN-based approach achieves 0.31° mean rotation error and 0.24×100 translation error, reducing rotation error by 70% over BARF (0.90°) and 35% over L2G (0.48°). For novel view synthesis before test-time optimization, the method reaches 19.31 PSNR versus 17.00 (BARF) and 17.99 (L2G). On the DTU 360° benchmark with noisy initial poses (15° rotation, 70×100 translation), the approach yields approximately 50% improvement in rotation and 60% in translation over BARF, and 70% improvement over L2G. In the 2D planar alignment experiment, the INN achieves a 75% success rate (5-pixel threshold) compared to 30% for BARF and 0% for a naive MLP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: BARF (standard SE(3) pose parameterization) and L2G (MLP predicting per-ray SE(3) transformations). On LLFF, the INN approach dominates both baselines on pose accuracy, achieving 0.31° rotation error versus 0.90° (BARF) and 0.48° (L2G), and 0.24 translation error versus 0.40 and 0.30 respectively. The pre-refinement PSNR gap (19.31 vs. 17.00/17.99) confirms the pose improvement translates directly to rendering quality; after test-time photometric optimization, all methods converge to similar PSNR (~24.3), indicating the refinement step masks the initial pose differences. On DTU with 15°/70×100 noisy initialization, the INN method outperforms BARF by ~50% in rotation and ~60% in translation, and outperforms L2G by ~70% on both metrics, with the largest per-scan gains on challenging sequences (e.g., scan 55: 1.64° vs. 10.81° for L2G). The 2D planar ablation further isolates the role of invertibility: a naive MLP fails completely (0% success), an implicit-invertible MLP recovers to 65%, and the explicit INN reaches 75%, confirming that architectural invertibility is the critical factor rather than mere overparameterization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint pose-and-NeRF optimization line initiated by BARF (which parameterizes each camera as a 6-DoF SE(3) vector) and L2G (which overparameterizes pose by having an MLP predict per-ray SE(3) transformations). The key departure is architectural: rather than predicting rigid transformation parameters, the authors model the camera-to-world warp function directly as an Invertible Neural Network (borrowing the NDR-INN architecture from dynamic NeRF work), coupled with a per-frame latent code. This reframes the overparameterization question from 'how many parameters to predict SE(3)' to 'what structural constraints on the warp function enable convergence,' with invertibility emerging as the critical property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a naive MLP warp representation fails catastrophically (0% success) while an INN achieves 75% success provides a clear, actionable design principle: overparameterization for pose estimation is not merely about capacity but about enforcing bijectivity. The homeomorphism perspective—that the INN traverses non-rigid intermediate states during optimization before converging to a rigid solution—offers a useful lens for understanding why overparameterized pose representations converge better. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale benchmarks (8 LLFF scenes, 14 DTU scans), the need for a post-hoc rigidity prior to extract actual SE(3) poses, and the computational overhead of the INN relative to a simple 6-DoF parameter. Open questions include scalability to large-scale scenes and whether the shared-INN design generalizes to very large frame counts.
