## Problem

Deep functional map methods for non-rigid 3D shape matching operate in the low-frequency spectral domain, producing locally inconsistent pointwise correspondences that lack spatial smoothness. Existing smoothness regularisations either require rigid alignment, are sensitive to topological noise, or rely on computationally expensive iterative optimisation that cannot be integrated into end-to-end learning frameworks. This paper seeks a universal, efficient regularisation that encourages spatially smooth matchings and is directly compatible with unsupervised deep functional map training.

## Input / Output

The method takes two 3D meshes with their precomputed Laplacian eigenfunctions (first k eigenvectors) and vertex positions as input, and produces a soft pointwise correspondence matrix Π ∈ R^{n_N × n_M} that encodes spatially smooth correspondences. The regularisation operates on randomly sampled initial functions with unit norm along a feature dimension h, and randomly sampled diffusion times t_i ~ Uniform(0, T) per function column, enabling multiscale neighbourhood enforcement without requiring ground-truth correspondences or rigid alignment between shapes.

## Method

The core contribution is a synchronous diffusion regularisation L_diff that penalises correspondences failing to preserve local neighbourhoods. Given a random function F_M on shape M, it is transferred to shape N via the predicted correspondence, heat-diffused synchronously on both shapes (using spectral approximation h_t(u) = Φ exp(−tΛ) Φᵀ u), and the diffused result on N is transferred back to M; the Frobenius-norm difference between the directly diffused and round-tripped functions forms the loss. Randomly sampling different diffusion times per function column yields multiscale regularisation, while the random initial distribution acts as a matrix sketching of the full quadratic assignment problem, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(nh). Theoretically, the loss is shown to be a continuous analogue of the Weisfeiler-Leman graph isomorphism test (replacing discrete message passing with heat diffusion) and a special case of heat kernel matching. The regularisation is integrated into the URSSM unsupervised functional map framework, improving state-of-the-art results across near-isometric, topologically noisy, non-isometric, and partial shape matching benchmarks.

## Results

On near-isometric shape matching, the method achieves mean geodesic error (×100) of 1.5 on FAUST, 1.8 on SCAPE, and 3.4 on SHREC'19 (trained on FAUST+SCAPE), outperforming the base method URSSM (1.6/1.9/4.6) and all prior unsupervised approaches. On the challenging TOPKIDS dataset with topological noise, the method reduces geodesic error from 9.2 (URSSM) to 5.4, also beating methods that rely on extrinsic rigid alignment (Deep Shells: 13.7, NeuroMorph: 13.8). For non-isometric matching, it achieves 3.6 on SMAL and 3.9 on DT4D-H inter-class, surpassing URSSM (3.9/4.1) and AttnFMaps (5.4/11.6). On partial shape matching (SHREC'16), it obtains 2.9 (CUTS) and 8.1 (HOLES) geodesic error, improving over URSSM (3.2/8.2) and supervised DPFM (3.2/13.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms its base framework URSSM [16] across all four evaluation settings: near-isometric (1.5 vs 1.6 on FAUST, 3.4 vs 4.6 on SHREC'19), topological noise (5.4 vs 9.2 on TOPKIDS), non-isometric (3.6 vs 3.9 on SMAL, 3.9 vs 4.1 on DT4D-H inter-class), and partial matching (2.9 vs 3.2 on CUTS, 8.1 vs 8.2 on HOLES). Against other unsupervised deep functional map methods, it beats SSSDFM [84] (3.4 vs 3.8 on SHREC'19), AttnFMaps [53] (3.4 vs 5.8 on SHREC'19; 3.6 vs 5.4 on SMAL), and DUO-FMNet [24] (3.4 vs 6.4 on SHREC'19). On TOPKIDS, the improvement is most dramatic: the method (5.4) nearly halves the error of URSSM (9.2) and substantially outperforms methods requiring rigid alignment such as Deep Shells [30] (13.7) and NeuroMorph [29] (13.8), as well as fully intrinsic methods like AttnFMaps [53] (23.4) and WSupFMNet [79] (47.9). On non-isometric DT4D-H inter-class, it surpasses even the supervised GeomFMaps [25] (3.9 vs 4.1) and AttnFMaps [53] (3.9 vs 11.6). For partial matching, it outperforms the supervised DPFM [5] on HOLES (8.1 vs 13.1) and matches or beats it on CUTS (2.9 vs 3.2), while also improving over ConsistFMaps [13] (4.9 vs 15.7 on HOLES-trained). The only setting where gains are modest is DT4D-H intra-class (0.9 vs 0.9, tied with URSSM), which the authors attribute to near-isometric conditions where diffusion consistency is less critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on URSSM [16], a state-of-the-art unsupervised deep functional map framework, by adding a synchronous diffusion regularisation that encourages spatially smooth correspondences. Conceptually, the regularisation draws from two distinct lineages: (1) heat kernel matching [91], which the authors show is a special case of their formulation, and (2) message-passing-based graph isomorphism testing (Weisfeiler-Leman) and deep graph matching [34], where the diffusion process replaces discrete neighbourhood aggregation with continuous heat propagation. The key departure from prior smoothness regularisations (Dirichlet energy, spatial registration) is that synchronous diffusion operates purely in the intrinsic spectral domain, requiring no rigid alignment and remaining robust to topological noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The regularisation is a simple, efficient drop-in component that substantially improves matching quality—most dramatically on topologically noisy shapes (41% relative error reduction on TOPKIDS)—without requiring ground-truth correspondences or extrinsic alignment. The theoretical connections to the Weisfeiler-Leman test and quadratic assignment problems provide a principled justification that may inform future work on matching regularisation more broadly. Open questions include extending the approach to partial-to-partial matching (currently inherited as a limitation from the functional map framework) and understanding the optimal choice of maximum diffusion time T under severe non-isometry, where the diffusion processes on two shapes become theoretically inconsistent.
