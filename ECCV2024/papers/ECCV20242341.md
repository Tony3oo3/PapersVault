## Problem

Regression-based 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., pixelSplat, pixelNeRF) are fast and scalable but collapse to blurry outputs in regions of high uncertainty, while generative approaches (e.g., GeNVS) handle uncertainty well but are orders of magnitude slower and fail to scale to large scenes or high resolutions. No prior method combines the real-time efficiency of Gaussian splatting with the generative quality needed for unobserved regions, while remaining trainable purely on readily available real video data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242341/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two reference video frames with known camera poses and produces novel view images at arbitrary target poses, both for interpolation between and extrapolation beyond the reference views. The intermediate 3D representation is a set of variational Gaussians (position, scale, rotation, opacity, RGB spherical harmonics, and per-Gaussian mean/variance of feature spherical harmonics). Downstream, the 3D-consistent synthesized views can be fed into surface reconstruction (2D Gaussian Splatting) to obtain textured meshes. The method handles both object-centric 360° settings (CO3D) and large-scale general scenes (RealEstate10k) at high resolution.

## Method

The core contribution is variational 3D Gaussians: each Gaussian stores not just explicit features but parameters (μ, σ) of a normal distribution over spherical harmonic feature coefficients, enabling per-location uncertainty modeling in 3D space. An encoder (pre-trained DINO ViT-B/8 + epipolar cross-attention + per-ray depth distribution sampling, adapted from pixelSplat) predicts these variational Gaussians from two reference views. A specific instance is sampled via the reparameterization trick, rendered to pixel-space features via an extended 3D Gaussian splatting rasterizer, and decoded by a lightweight pre-trained LDM VAE-GAN decoder (four convolutional upsample blocks) that receives multi-scale rendered features plus an RGB skip connection. Training is end-to-end on video triplets using L1+LPIPS reconstruction loss, an auxiliary RGB rendering loss for structural Gaussian parameters, and a GAN adversarial loss to generate plausible details in unobserved regions. This design achieves real-time rendering (~3 ms) while outperforming both regression-based and diffusion-based baselines in perceptual and generative quality metrics.

## Results

On CO3D (object-centric 360° NVS), latentSplat achieves FID 40.93 and KID 0.005 on hydrant interpolation, and FID 48.03 / KID 0.008 on extrapolation, substantially outperforming pixelSplat (FID 58.13 / 92.61) and pixelNeRF (FID 183.24 / 238.41). On RealEstate10k (scene-level), latentSplat reaches FID 2.22 and LPIPS 0.164 for interpolation and FID 2.79 / LPIPS 0.196 for extrapolation, beating pixelSplat (FID 4.41 / 5.78) and Du et al. (FID 9.73 / 11.34). In rendering efficiency, latentSplat renders in 3 ms per view (vs. 2 ms for pixelSplat and 6423 ms for GeNVS), maintaining real-time performance while training in 192 GPU-hours (vs. 2112 for GeNVS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pixelNeRF [76], latentSplat achieves roughly 4–5× lower FID (e.g., 40.93 vs. 183.24 on hydrant interpolation) and 0.05–0.11 lower LPIPS, while PSNR is within ~0.4 dB. Against Du et al. [17] (light-field rendering), latentSplat reduces FID by 3–4× (40.93 vs. 154.20) and LPIPS by ~0.11, though Du et al. retains a slight PSNR edge on hydrants (18.78 vs. 18.01). Against the closest regression-based competitor pixelSplat [10], latentSplat improves FID by ~30% (40.93 vs. 58.13) and LPIPS by ~0.045 on hydrants, and by ~28% FID (53.47 vs. 74.82) on teddybears, while matching or exceeding PSNR (20.83 vs. 20.73 on teddybears). The generative baseline GeNVS [9] is only compared on efficiency: latentSplat renders ~2000× faster (3 ms vs. 6423 ms) and trains in 192 vs. 2112 GPU-hours. Notably, latentSplat's advantage grows for extrapolation (views outside the reference frustums), where regression-based methods degrade most severely, confirming the benefit of explicit uncertainty modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

latentSplat extends the pixelSplat encoder architecture by replacing deterministic feature predictions with variational (mean + variance) parameters over spherical harmonic coefficients, introducing spatially-varying uncertainty into the 3D Gaussian representation. It departs from the purely regression-based lineage (pixelNeRF, pixelSplat) that predicts a single mean solution, and from the diffusion-based generative lineage (GeNVS, Zero-1-to-3) that is too slow for large scenes, by coupling the efficient Gaussian splatting renderer with a lightweight LDM VAE-GAN decoder. The conceptual bridge is the reparameterization trick applied per-Gaussian in 3D space, allowing the model to acknowledge high uncertainty in unobserved regions while remaining deterministic where observations are strong.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that a lightweight generative decoder (not a diffusion model) is sufficient to fill uncertain regions in 3D reconstruction, achieving near-diffusion quality at ~2000× lower inference cost, which makes generalizable 3D reconstruction practical for large, high-resolution scenes. A key open limitation is that Gaussians are confined to input camera frustums, so extrapolated views rely on 2D inpainting by the decoder, introducing 3D inconsistencies; the paper also notes that depth and appearance are sampled independently, leaving the full conditional distribution uncaptured. The spatially-varying uncertainty formulation and the validation that classical metrics (PSNR/SSIM) systematically favor blur over generated detail are useful reference points for the field's evaluation practices.
