## Problem

Existing CLIP-based semantic segmentation methods rely on pixel-text alignment, which suffers from two fundamental issues: low-resolution image features blur class boundaries causing misalignment, and CLIP's globally-oriented text embeddings poorly match localized pixel features. This paper asks whether shifting from per-pixel to per-mask vision-language alignment can resolve these ambiguities while better matching CLIP's holistic scene understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241429/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces N=100 binary segmentation masks, each assigned a class label from a closed set (150 classes on ADE20k, 19 on Cityscapes). During training, the frozen CLIP text encoder processes K×C prompted class tokens (K=3 prompts × C classes) alongside the mask tokens; during inference, text tokens are discarded and only the text-aligned mask tokens from the final decoder layer are used for mask and class prediction via dot-product with pixel-decoder features and a classification MLP.

## Method

MTA-CLIP introduces a Mask-Text Decoder that concatenates N mask queries with dimensionally-reduced CLIP text embeddings (1024→256 via MLP) into a joint query set, then processes them through L=9 layers of cross-attention with multi-scale pixel-decoder features and self-attention where mask and text queries interact bidirectionally. After each layer, both mask and text queries are projected back into the CLIP feature space (with residual connection to the original text embedding) and a contrastive loss (Mask-to-Text Contrastive Learning) enforces class-level alignment between each mask and its ground-truth text query. A second component, Mask-Text Prompt Learning, learns K=3 context-specific prompts shared across all C classes, expanding the text query set to K×C and selecting the highest-similarity prompt instance as the positive for each mask (MixNeg strategy). The overall loss combines cross-entropy mask classification, binary cross-entropy mask segmentation, and the contrastive similarity loss, trained end-to-end with a frozen CLIP text encoder and CLIP-pretrained image backbone.

## Results

On ADE20k, MTA-CLIP achieves 49.1% mIoU (single-scale) with ResNet-50, 49.8% with ResNet-101, and 52.3% with ViT-B, surpassing the prior CLIP-based SOTA (PPL) by 4.4%, 3.4%, and 0.7% respectively. On Cityscapes, it reaches 81.7% and 82.1% mIoU (SS) for ResNet-50 and ResNet-101, outperforming DenseCLIP by 2.5% and 1.1%. Overall, MTA-CLIP improves over prior state-of-the-art by an average of 2.8% on ADE20k and 1.3% on Cityscapes across backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTA-CLIP consistently outperforms all compared baselines across both datasets and all three backbones. Against the strongest CLIP-based pixel-text alignment methods, it beats PPL [20] by 4.4% (ResNet-50) and 3.4% (ResNet-101) on ADE20k SS, and DenseCLIP [31] by 5.6% and 4.7% respectively, demonstrating the advantage of mask-level over pixel-level alignment. Over the CLIP-pretrained Mask2Former* baseline (no alignment), gains are 1.7%, 1.4%, and 1.3% for ResNet-50, ResNet-101, and ViT-B on ADE20k, confirming the contribution of the Mask-Text Decoder and Prompt Learning. On Cityscapes, MTA-CLIP surpasses DenseCLIP by 2.5% (ResNet-50) and 1.1% (ResNet-101), and the CLIP-pretrained Mask2Former* by 0.7% and 1.0%. Against classical ImageNet-pretrained methods (PSPNet, DeepLabv3+, UperNet), the margins are substantially larger (6–11%), underscoring the benefit of VLM pretraining. On alternative backbones, MTA-CLIP also beats SegFormer [39] by 1.4% (MiT-B4) and Segmenter [33] by 5.2% (ViT-S), showing generality beyond CLIP-pretrained encoders.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTA-CLIP builds directly on Mask2Former's mask-based segmentation architecture, repurposing its transformer decoder into a Mask-Text Decoder that jointly processes mask tokens and CLIP text embeddings. It departs from the dominant pixel-text alignment paradigm established by DenseCLIP and PPL, arguing that per-pixel alignment at low resolution introduces boundary ambiguities that are better resolved by aligning whole mask regions with text. The paper also distinguishes itself from open-vocabulary mask-text matching methods (e.g., MaskCLIP, OSAM) by updating both mask and text representations through contrastive learning rather than treating text embeddings as fixed, and extends the prompt learning lineage (CoOp, CoCoOp, ProDA) from image-level to mask-level contextual prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from pixel-level to mask-level vision-language alignment offers a principled resolution to the boundary ambiguity problem that has limited CLIP-based segmentation, and the consistent 2–4% gains across multiple backbones and datasets suggest the approach is robust rather than architecture-specific. However, the method remains confined to closed-set segmentation and relies entirely on CLIP's pre-trained text encoder, leaving open whether mask-text alignment transfers to open-vocabulary or other VLM backbones. The finding that joint self-attention between mask and text queries (vs. separate attention) contributes a meaningful 0.5% also suggests that the interaction mechanism between modalities at the mask level is a design choice worth further exploration.
