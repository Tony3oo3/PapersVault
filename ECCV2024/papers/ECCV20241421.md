## Problem

In long-range or wide-area scenarios (e.g., wind turbine monitoring, long-distance depth estimation), traditional planar-target calibration becomes impractical due to site constraints, large target requirements, and unstable lighting. Existing collimator-based methods rely on expensive goniometers or theodolites to measure collimated ray directions, making them inflexible and sensitive to measurement error. This paper asks whether a collimator system can provide a self-contained, direction-free calibration environment that matches or exceeds the accuracy of standard target-based methods.

## Input / Output

The method requires at least two (typically 5–20) RGB images of a star-based calibration pattern mounted on the reticle of a portable collimator (~200×170×300 mm), captured from different orientations. Unlike standard calibration where the camera optical center is free per view, the spherical motion constraint fixes it to a single 3D position t_cp shared across all views, reducing per-image motion parameters from 6 to 3. The output is the full intrinsic matrix (f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y, γ), radial distortion coefficients, the fixed optical center, and per-image rotation matrices.

## Method

The core insight is that the collimator's optical geometry enforces angle invariance between any point pair on the reticle, which the authors prove implies the target-camera relative motion is pure rotation about the camera optical center (spherical motion), reducing the problem from 6DOF to 3DOF. This constraint, combined with the planar homography relation, yields 5 independent equations per image for 8 unknowns (5 intrinsic + 3 for t_cp), making two views theoretically sufficient. For N>2 views, a closed-form linear solver stacks the per-image constraints and solves via least squares; for the minimal two-view case, a hidden-variable technique reduces the problem to a degree-2 polynomial in one scalar. A final bundle adjustment with Cauchy robust cost and Levenberg-Marquardt refines intrinsics, distortion, rotations, and the fixed optical center, with the total parameter count reduced from 6N to 3N+3.

## Results

On real collimator images with 20 calibration views, the proposed method achieves the lowest evaluation re-projection error (ε_eval = 0.1207 px) among all methods, outperforming Bouguet (0.1221 px), Zhang (0.1225 px), and Hartley (0.1871 px). The calibration re-projection error (ε_calib = 0.1778 px) is slightly higher than Bouguet (0.1722 px) due to the reduced degrees of freedom from the spherical motion constraint. On epipolar rectification error (20 images), the method achieves the best mean (0.0756 px) and lowest standard deviation (0.0208 px). Across focal lengths from 12 mm to 35 mm, the proposed method consistently yields the lowest evaluation error (e.g., 0.2174 px at 12 mm vs. 0.2225 px for Bouguet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Zhang's plane-based calibration [46], Bouguet's MATLAB toolbox [3], and Hartley's self-calibration for stationary cameras [17]. On evaluation re-projection error (the key accuracy metric), the proposed method wins across all image counts (2–20), achieving 0.1207 px at 20 images versus 0.1221 px (Bouguet), 0.1225 px (Zhang), and 0.1871 px (Hartley). The calibration re-projection error is slightly higher for the proposed method (0.1778 vs. 0.1722 px for Bouguet at 20 images), which the authors attribute to the reduced degrees of freedom imposed by the spherical motion constraint. On epipolar rectification error, the proposed method achieves the best mean (0.0756 px) and lowest standard deviation (0.0208 px) at 20 images, outperforming Bouguet (0.0768, 0.0253) and Zhang (0.0772, 0.0252). Hartley's method, while theoretically suited to the 3DOF spherical motion, performs worst on evaluation error (0.1871 px) because it relies solely on 2D point correspondences without leveraging the known planar pattern geometry. In the multi-focal-length experiment (12–35 mm), the proposed method consistently achieves the lowest evaluation error, with the gap over Bouguet widening at longer focal lengths (e.g., 0.3118 vs. 0.3348 px at 35 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical plane-based calibration framework (Zhang, Bouguet) and Hartley's self-calibration from a rotating camera, but departs from both by exploiting the specific optical geometry of a collimator to prove that the target-camera relative motion is constrained to 3DOF pure rotation (spherical motion) rather than general 6DOF. It also extends the collimator-based calibration lineage (which traditionally requires a goniometer or theodolite to measure ray directions) by eliminating that measurement entirely, instead using a structured star pattern on the reticle and the angle-invariance property inherent to collimated light. The two-view minimal solver borrows the hidden-variable technique from Hartley and Li [14] to handle the rank-deficient linear system that arises in the minimum configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical gap: long-range cameras in outdoor or constrained environments (wind-turbine monitoring, long-distance depth estimation) where traditional planar-target calibration is infeasible due to site limitations, and where expensive goniometers make prior collimator-based methods impractical. The portable ~200×170×300 mm collimator system with a star pattern offers a flexible, repeatable calibration environment. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (Zhang, Bouguet) are modest (sub-0.01 pixel improvements in evaluation re-projection error), and the authors acknowledge that significant lens distortion (e.g., fisheye cameras) remains an open problem, as the current pipeline initializes distortion to zero and relies on bundle adjustment to recover it.
