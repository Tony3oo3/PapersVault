## Problem

Exemplar-free class incremental learning (CIL) synthesizes previous-task data to combat catastrophic forgetting, but a significant domain gap between synthetic and real images causes the model to classify by domain (real vs. synthetic) rather than by true class, severely degrading accuracy on previously learned classes—especially in long-term CIL with many incremental tasks. Existing methods using knowledge distillation or model inversion fail to close this gap, leaving a critical stability-plasticity imbalance unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, variable resolution for ImageNet-100) and outputs a class label over all classes seen so far. During training, each incremental task's dataset is a mixture of real current-task images, synthetic previous-task images generated by fine-tuned diffusion models, and selectively augmented synthetic current-task images. The network produces two outputs per image: a class prediction from the main classifier and a binary domain prediction (real vs. synthetic) from an adversarial branch, with the latter used only during training via gradient reversal.

## Method

DiffClass reformulates exemplar-free CIL as a task-agnostic multi-domain adaptation problem with three integrated components. First, a Multi-Distribution Matching (MDM) loss fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion model via LoRA to simultaneously align the synthetic distribution with the current real-task distribution and with all prior synthetic distributions, ensuring uniform generation quality across tasks. Second, Selective Synthetic Image Augmentation (SSIA) generates twice the real-data count of synthetic images per class, then filters them by sampling from a Gaussian fitted to each class's mean and covariance, retaining only images within a computed threshold to expand the training distribution. Third, the CNN backbone feeds two branches: a standard linear classifier for class labels and a gradient-reversal-layer (GRL) + binary classifier for domain prediction, so the backbone learns domain-invariant features while the classifier retains class-discriminative knowledge. The combined training loss is the sum of cross-entropy classification loss and adversarial domain loss, applied jointly over both real and synthetic data in each incremental phase.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with N=20 tasks, DiffClass achieves 67.10% average incremental accuracy (Acc_avg) and 57.11% final accuracy (Acc_L), surpassing the previous SOTA method SEED (57.42% / 42.87%) by 9.68 and 14.24 percentage points respectively. On ImageNet-100 with N=20 tasks, the method reaches 72.51% Acc_avg and 68.68% Acc_L, outperforming SEED (62.26% / 45.77%) by 10.25 and 22.91 percentage points. The method maintains near-consistent accuracy as the number of incremental tasks grows (e.g., CIFAR-100 Acc_L drops only from 62.21% at N=5 to 57.11% at N=20), whereas baselines suffer severe degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffClass outperforms all seven exemplar-free CIL baselines across every dataset and task-split setting. Against the strongest prior method SEED (ICLR 2024), the gains are most pronounced in long-term settings: on ImageNet-100 with N=20, DiffClass improves Acc_L by 22.91 points (68.68% vs. 45.77%) and Acc_avg by 10.25 points (72.51% vs. 62.26%). On CIFAR-100 with N=20, the improvement over SEED is 14.24 points in Acc_L (57.11% vs. 42.87%). Earlier baselines such as R-DFCIL (ECCV 2022) and ABD (ICCV 2021) trail by 15–35 points in Acc_L under N=20, while methods like PASS, IL2A, SSRE, and FeTril degrade to below 30% final accuracy on ImageNet-100 with 20 tasks. The consistent advantage across all settings indicates that the domain-gap mitigation strategy (MDM + MDA + SSIA) is effective regardless of dataset difficulty or incremental horizon.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the exemplar-free CIL line of work that synthesizes previous-task data via generative models (GANs, diffusion, model inversion) to combat catastrophic forgetting. It departs from prior methods like R-DFCIL and SEED, which rely on knowledge distillation to constrain domain gaps, by arguing that distillation misattributes performance loss to model capacity rather than data distribution mismatch. The method combines three relatively established ideas—LoRA-based diffusion fine-tuning (from text-to-image personalization), domain-adversarial training with gradient reversal (Ganin et al.), and distribution-matching loss (Real-Fake)—into a unified framework that explicitly targets the multi-task, multi-domain structure of CIL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnosis that models trained on mixed real/synthetic data develop a domain-over-class labeling bias (Sec. 3) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why prior exemplar-free CIL methods fail in long-horizon settings, and the MDA reformulation offers a simple, computationally lightweight fix. The 14–22% accuracy gains over SEED on ImageNet-100 with N=20 tasks are substantial and suggest that domain alignment, not just data quantity, is the bottleneck. A key limitation is the per-task LoRA fine-tuning cost, which scales with the number of new classes and may be prohibitive in very long incremental sequences; whether the approach transfers to other generative backbones (e.g., autoregressive or flow-based models) or to non-image modalities remains open.
