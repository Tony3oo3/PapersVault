## Problem

Standard generative model evaluation metrics such as FID, KID, and FD_DINO-V2 are sensitive to visual artifacts (noise, blurriness) yet tolerant to structural/semantic errors like missing components or malformed shapes. For generative design tasks, human designers prioritize structural plausibility over pixel-level fidelity, so these metrics produce rankings that contradict expert judgment. The paper asks whether an evaluation metric can be made robust to visual corruption while remaining sensitive to structural implausibility.

## Input / Output

The metric takes two sets of images (real reference and generated samples), rescales each to 299×299×3 (or 256×256×1 for the BIKED-specific variant), and encodes them through the DAE encoder into 2048-dimensional (or 64-dimensional) latent vectors. From these vectors it fits multivariate Gaussians and computes the Fréchet Distance, yielding a single scalar score where lower values indicate greater similarity to the reference set. The same pipeline supports alternative distance critics (MMD for KDD, Topology Distance for TDD) while keeping the DAE encoder fixed.

## Method

FDD replaces the Inception-V3 backbone in the FID framework with the encoder of a Denoising Autoencoder trained unsupervised on ImageNet. The DAE architecture uses 5 convolutional layers in both encoder and decoder with channel dimensions [32, 64, 128, 256, 512], 3×3 kernels, stride 2, and ReLU activations; during training, inputs are corrupted with Gaussian noise (σ = 0.1) and the network is optimized with MSE reconstruction loss via Adam (lr = 1e-3, batch 128, 1000 epochs with early stopping). The key insight is that the denoising objective forces the latent representation to encode structural information invariant to pixel-level noise, so the resulting 2048-dim features are robust to visual artifacts yet sensitive to shape-level changes. At evaluation time, real and generated images are encoded by the frozen DAE encoder, fitted to multivariate Gaussians, and the Fréchet Distance between them is reported as the FDD score. Grad-CAM visualization confirms that the DAE encoder attends to object boundaries and structural details, whereas Inception-V3 focuses on the central object region regardless of shape integrity.

## Results

In the model ranking experiment on BIKED (256×256 bicycle designs), FDD correctly ranks DDPM (FDD: 48.08) > DDIM (FDD: 60.66) > EDM (FDD: 111.25), aligning with human expert judgment that DDPM and DDIM produce structurally more plausible designs. In contrast, FID assigns EDM the best score (7.84) despite its high rate of implausible structures, while DDPM (FID: 11.77) and DDIM (FID: 31.35) are unfairly penalized. In the sensitivity test across BIKED, Seeing3DChairs, FFHQ, and ImageNet, FDD consistently penalizes structural failures (patch mask/swap) more heavily than visual artifacts (Gaussian/salt-and-pepper noise), whereas FID and FD_DINO-V2 show the opposite bias. FDD also demonstrates the most stable performance (lowest variance across 10 repeated runs) among all tested metrics, particularly as sample size decreases from 300 to 100 per group.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FID (Heusel et al., NeurIPS 2017), FDD decisively outperforms in structural plausibility assessment: FID ranks EDM best (7.84) despite its high implausibility rate, while FDD correctly assigns EDM the worst score (111.25 vs. DDPM's 48.08). Against FD_DINO-V2 (Stein et al., 2023), which was previously identified as the most reliable general-purpose metric, FDD again shows superior alignment with human judgment on design images—FD_DINO-V2 scores (342.82, 571.21, 435.25) do not clearly separate the models in a way matching human evaluation. Against KID (Binkowski et al., ICLR 2018), which shares the same Inception-V3 backbone as FID, FDD similarly avoids the visual-artifact bias. Against TD (Horak et al., AAAI 2021), the most competitive shape-based baseline, FDD achieves comparable structural sensitivity but with significantly better stability: TD exhibits high standard deviation at smaller sample sizes (K=100) and poor performance under pepper noise, while FDD maintains consistent scores across all disturbance types and sample sizes. Pearson correlation analysis confirms FDD clusters with TD (topology-sensitive metrics) rather than with FID/FD_DINO-V2, validating that the DAE latent space preserves topological structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FID framework (Heusel et al., 2017) by replacing the Inception-V3 backbone with a Denoising Autoencoder (Vincent et al., 2008), arguing that the DAE's denoising training objective naturally extracts structural representations robust to visual corruption. It also adapts the distance measures from KID (Binkowski et al., 2018) and Topology Distance (Horak et al., 2021) to the DAE latent space, yielding KDD and TDD variants. Unlike prior backbone-swapping efforts (CLIP, DINOv2, VQ-VAE) that target general perceptual alignment, this work specifically targets the structural-plausibility-vs-visual-artifact axis that matters to human designers in generative design tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where the dominant evaluation metric (FID) systematically misranks generative design models by over-penalizing visual noise and under-penalizing structural failures, as confirmed by human expert judgment. The limitation is that FDD's low priority on visual artifacts could be problematic in contexts where pixel-level fidelity is critical, and the model ranking experiment uses only five models on a single design dataset (BIKED). The authors note potential extension to 3D generative models via autoencoder-based 3D processing, and the structural-bias insight could inform training objectives for DGMs to produce more plausible designs.
