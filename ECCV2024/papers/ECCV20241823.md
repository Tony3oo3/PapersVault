## Problem

Whole-image global descriptors in visual place recognition fail under significant viewpoint change because the dissimilarity of non-overlapping regions dominates the similarity of overlapping regions. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating place recognition as recognition of specific 'things' (segments) within their spatial context, enabling matching of partially-overlapping images that global descriptors cannot handle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241823/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, segments it with SAM into ~105 binary masks, and extracts DINOv2 pixel-level descriptors at the encoder's native low resolution (masks are downsampled to match). Rather than producing a single global vector per image, it outputs multiple overlapping SuperSegment descriptors (each a 1024-d PCA-reduced VLAD vector), which are matched against a flat index of all SuperSegments from the entire reference database. Image-level retrieval is then obtained by converting top segment matches into image indices via a similarity-weighted frequency count (top K'=50 segments per query segment → top K=5 images).

## Method

SegVLAD constructs a Delaunay Triangulation graph over segment pixel centers to define a binary adjacency matrix, then generates overlapping SuperSegments by raising the adjacency matrix to a power o (neighborhood expansion) and multiplying with the original masks. Feature aggregation uses a factorized formulation (Eq. 2) that unifies segment-level, neighborhood-level, and whole-image aggregation under a single matrix product, with hard-assignment VLAD as the aggregation kernel over DINOv2 residual features. Retrieval is performed at the segment level against a flat index of all database SuperSegments, and image-level ranking is achieved by accumulating similarity-weighted frequency across all query segments' top matches. The approach is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated with both off-the-shelf DINOv2 (ViT-G, AnyLoc-style) and VPR-finetuned DINOv2 (ViT-B, NetVLAD-style) encoders, and outperforms whole-image baselines particularly under large viewpoint shifts and in highly aliased indoor environments.

## Results

SegVLAD achieves state-of-the-art Recall@1/5 on the majority of evaluated VPR benchmarks. On the hardest outdoor subset (RO5k Hard), SegVLAD-PreT reaches 60.0/81.4, substantially outperforming the best baseline EigenPlaces (42.8/57.1) by 17.2 points in R@1. On out-of-distribution datasets, SegVLAD-PreT leads on Baidu Mall (78.5/93.8 vs. AnyLoc's 75.2/87.6), VPAir aerial imagery (69.8/83.7 vs. AnyLoc's 66.7/79.2), and InsideOut (4.2/9.4 vs. AnyLoc's 2.4/8.0), where all other baselines fail near zero. SegVLAD-FineT matches or exceeds the best whole-image methods on standard street-view benchmarks (e.g., 93.2/96.8 on Pitts-30K vs. EigenPlaces' 92.6/96.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CosPlace [6], MixVPR [2], and EigenPlaces [8] (all trained on large-scale urban data), SegVLAD-FineT matches or exceeds them on standard street-view benchmarks (e.g., 93.2/96.8 on Pitts-30K vs. EigenPlaces' 92.6/96.7) and dramatically outperforms them on viewpoint-challenging subsets: RO5k Hard (60.0 vs. 42.8 R@1 for EigenPlaces) and RP6k Hard (48.6 vs. 11.4 R@5 for AnyLoc). Against AnyLoc [32] (the closest architectural sibling, differing only in segment vs. whole-image aggregation), SegVLAD-PreT wins on RO5k Med (91.4 vs. 88.6), RO5k Hard (60.0 vs. 40.0), Baidu Mall (78.5 vs. 75.2), VPAir (69.8 vs. 66.7), and InsideOut (4.2 vs. 2.4), while AnyLoc retains a small edge on MSLS SF (83.4 vs. 88.4) and RP6k Med (97.1 vs. 94.3). Against SALAD [29] (finetuned DINOv2 with optimal-transport aggregation), SegVLAD-PreT dominates on out-of-distribution data: VPAir (69.8 vs. 25.8 R@1), Baidu (78.5 vs. 74.8), and RO5k Hard (60.0 vs. 37.1), while SALAD is competitive on MSLS CPH (92.3 vs. 81.7). The consistent pattern across all five baselines is that segment-level retrieval provides the largest gains precisely where viewpoint variation is most severe, confirming the paper's central hypothesis that whole-image descriptors are dominated by non-overlapping dissimilarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant whole-image global descriptor paradigm in VPR (CosPlace, MixVPR, EigenPlaces, AnyLoc, SALAD) by performing retrieval at the image-segment level rather than the image level. It builds on SAM for open-set segmentation, DINOv2 for pixel-level feature extraction, and hard-assignment VLAD (from AnyLoc/NetVLAD) for feature aggregation, but reorganizes these components around Delaunay-triangulated segment neighborhoods to form overlapping 'SuperSegments.' The approach is positioned as 'semi-global'—between point-level local descriptors used for geometric reranking and whole-image global descriptors used for coarse retrieval—and explicitly contrasts with patch-based methods (Patch-NetVLAD, MultiVLAD) by using semantically meaningful segments rather than arbitrary grid regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even with powerful pretrained encoders like DINOv2, whole-image aggregation fundamentally struggles under large viewpoint shifts because dissimilarity in non-overlapping regions dominates similarity in overlapping ones—a problem that segment-level partial matching directly addresses. The object instance retrieval evaluation (OOI task) opens a bridge between VPR and object-goal navigation, suggesting segment-based maps could serve dual purposes for localization and goal recognition. Key limitations include the large storage footprint of segment-level descriptors (mitigated partially by IOU-based filtering) and the reliance on SAM's segmentation quality. Future work points toward hierarchical pipelines combining segment-based coarse retrieval with segment-based reranking (e.g., MESA) and text-based interfaces via CLIP/LLMs.
