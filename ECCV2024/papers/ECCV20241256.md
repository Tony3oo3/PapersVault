## Problem

Existing vision foundation models are split: SAM segments arbitrary regions but lacks semantic understanding, while CLIP recognizes objects but cannot localize them. No unified promptable framework, web-scale dataset with aligned masks-classes-captions, or training method exists to integrate both capabilities into a single model for simultaneous segmentation, recognition, and captioning of arbitrary regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241256/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image and a flexible visual prompt (single point, bounding box, or sketch) and jointly produces a segmentation mask, a category label over a 2560-concept vocabulary, and a free-text caption of up to 40 words. The image decoder outputs 4 candidate masks with 4 associated semantic tokens and 1 IoU token; a routing heuristic selects the final mask-semantic-token pair, which then drives both classification (via a 3-layer MLP) and autoregressive caption generation (via a 38M-parameter causal text decoder).

## Method

TAP upgrades SAM's mask decoder into a generic image decoder by appending a semantic token to each predicted mask token. The semantic token is trained to match a target concept distribution computed by EVA-CLIP (5B parameters) over a curated 2560-category vocabulary, using KL divergence loss rather than direct feature alignment, which the ablations show is critical for transferring CLIP's open-world knowledge. The pre-training dataset SemanticSA-1B is constructed by injecting these CLIP priors into SA-1B's 1.1B masks, eliminating the need for hand-collected region-text pairs. A lightweight 38M-parameter causal text decoder with rotary position embeddings is then fine-tuned on Visual Genome, prompted solely by the semantic token, to generate region captions end-to-end without requiring an LLM.

## Results

On zero-shot instance classification (LVIS, GT-box prompted), TAP-L achieves 59.1 AP, surpassing RegionSpot-BL (56.6) and RegionCLIP-R50x4 (50.7) while using only 0.3B parameters versus EVA-CLIP-E's 5B (64.3 AP). For region captioning on Visual Genome, TAP with full fine-tuning sets a new record of 164.7 CIDEr using only a 38M-parameter text decoder, outperforming AlphaCLIP (160.3) and ASM (145.1). On zero-shot instance segmentation, TAP-L matches SAM-L closely (43.0 vs. 43.8 AP on LVIS), confirming that the added semantic tasks do not degrade SAM's core segmentation capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAP-L (59.1 AP) substantially outperforms both region-level CLIP baselines: RegionCLIP-R50x4 (50.7 AP, +8.4) and RegionSpot-BL (56.6 AP, +2.5), demonstrating that joint segmentation+concept pre-training on SA-1B masks is more effective than region-specific CLIP training on smaller datasets. Against image-level CLIP, TAP-L surpasses CLIP-L (48.8 AP) by 10.3 points but trails the much larger EVA-CLIP-E (64.3 AP) by 5.2 points, though TAP operates at 1/16th the parameter count (0.3B vs. 5B). On segmentation, TAP-L (43.0 AP on LVIS) is within 0.8 AP of SAM-L (43.8), confirming no degradation from the added semantic objectives. For region captioning, TAP full-FT (164.7 CIDEr on VG) beats all prior methods including AlphaCLIP (160.3), SCA (149.8), ASM (145.1), GPT4ROI (145.2), and GRiT (142.0), using a 38M text decoder versus 7B LLMs in most competitors. Notably, even with a frozen image encoder (partial-FT), TAP-L reaches 154.7 CIDEr, already exceeding GRiT and GPT4ROI, indicating the semantic tokens encode rich region-level information during pre-training alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAP builds directly on SAM's promptable segmentation architecture and EVA-CLIP's contrastive vision-language pretraining, unifying them through a semantic token added to each mask token in the decoder. It departs from prior merging strategies (SAM-CLIP's distillation, RegionSpot's adapter, ASM's handcrafted multimodal dataset) by instead injecting CLIP's concept distributions into SA-1B masks to form the SemanticSA-1B dataset, and by using KL-divergence concept prediction rather than feature-level alignment. The promptable captioning component extends the tokenizer with a lightweight causal text decoder, contrasting with concurrent works that rely on 7B-parameter LLMs for region description.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAP demonstrates that a compact 0.1–0.3B promptable model can simultaneously segment, classify, and caption arbitrary regions, offering a practical region-level image tokenizer for downstream perception tasks without requiring a 5B CLIP at inference. The SemanticSA-1B construction (injecting CLIP priors into existing segmentation masks) provides a reusable recipe for aligning spatial and semantic supervision without new annotation. Open questions include whether the fixed 2560-category concept space limits open-world generalization, whether the approach scales to longer-form or multi-region captions, and how well the semantic tokens transfer to tasks beyond the three demonstrated (e.g., detection, grounding, or video).
