## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods rely on a proxy ID classification task to learn representations, but the information-theoretic relationship between classification and detection has not been formally characterized. This paper shows that optimizing the classification objective inevitably causes over-confidence on known classes and undesired compression of detection-relevant information, degrading OOD detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241477/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and produces a non-parametric KNN distance score on penultimate-layer features for OOD detection, a binary in/out-of-distribution label, and a class label. The architecture includes a ResNet encoder, an MLP projection head mapping to a hyperspherical embedding, and a reconstruction network that operates on intermediate block features, making the output a multi-level representation rather than a single scalar score.

## Method

OER (OOD Entropy Regularization) regularizes classification-oriented representation learning through three complementary mechanisms grounded in Information Bottleneck analysis. First, it tunes the temperature coefficient τ in the vMF-based classification loss to increase the conditional entropy of training IDs, creating a larger uncertainty gap between IDs and OODs (Proposition 1 shows maximizing I(Z;Y) causes over-confidence). Second, it applies kernel density estimation—vMF for final hyperspherical features and RBF for intermediate block features—to maximize I(Z;X) and preserve detection-relevant information that would otherwise be compressed (Proposition 2). Third, it uses a variational reconstruction network to provide a lower bound on I(Z;X), compensating for noise introduced by the density estimator. The overall loss combines the classification loss with these three regularization terms, and OOD scoring is performed via KNN distance on the penultimate-layer features.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34), OER achieves an average FPR95 of 28.4% and AUROC of 91.2%, outperforming the closest competitor CIDER (FPR 39.4%, AUROC 90.0%) by 11.0 percentage points in FPR. On ImageNet-100, OER reduces average FPR by 7.6% relative to CIDER. OER also surpasses all score-based baselines (MSP, ODIN, Mahalanobis, Energy, DICE) and synthesis-based methods (VOS, NPOS) across all five OOD test sets, while maintaining competitive ID classification accuracy (74.6% vs. 75.1% for CIDER).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OER decisively outperforms the most closely related method CIDER [34], reducing average FPR95 from 39.4% to 28.4% (−11.0 pp) and improving AUROC from 90.0 to 91.2 on CIFAR-100, with the largest gains on iSUN (FPR 49.5→23.2) and Textures (FPR 36.4→17.8). Against other representation-learning baselines, OER cuts FPR by over 30 pp relative to SSD+ (61.4→28.4) and KNN+ (60.9→28.4), and by 17.7 pp relative to NPOS (46.1→28.4). All score-based methods (MSP, ODIN, Mahalanobis, Energy, DICE) are substantially outperformed, with the best score-based method (ODIN, FPR 56.6) still trailing OER by 28.2 pp in FPR. OER also beats the virtual-outlier-synthesis method VOS (FPR 72.8) by 44.4 pp without requiring any pseudo-OOD generation. The only trade-off is a slight drop in ID classification accuracy (74.6% vs. 75.1% for CIDER and 75.9% for SSD+/KNN+), which the authors attribute to irrelevant information introduced alongside detection-relevant information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CIDER's hyperspherical embedding framework with von Mises-Fisher classification, extending it with an information-theoretic analysis rooted in the Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al.). It departs from prior OOD detection work by formally identifying two specific failure modes of proxy classification—over-confidence on known classes and compression of detection-relevant information (U_d)—rather than heuristically adding regularization. The regularization components draw on kernel density estimation (Ahmad & Lin) and variational information maximization (Barber & Agakov), repurposing these tools to preserve mutual information I(Z;X) that the classification objective would otherwise discard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information-theoretic decomposition (U_c vs. U_d) provides a principled explanation for why classification-oriented representation learning is suboptimal for OOD detection, giving the field a clearer theoretical target for future methods. The temperature-tuning mechanism is simple and broadly applicable to any softmax-based classifier, potentially benefiting methods beyond OER. A key open question acknowledged by the authors is that maximizing I(Z;X) without direct OOD supervision inevitably introduces irrelevant noise alongside detection-relevant information, and how to selectively suppress such noise remains unresolved. The method's modest drop in classification accuracy (74.6% vs. CIDER's 75.1%) also raises the practical trade-off question of how much ID performance is acceptable to sacrifice for detection gains.
