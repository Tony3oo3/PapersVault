## Problem

Most existing document tampering localization (DTL) models suffer catastrophic performance drops under adversarial attacks, yet adversarial robustness has not been explored in the DTL community. Unlike natural image tampering localization, DTL faces two compounding challenges—extreme class imbalance (far more authentic than tampered pixels) and weak discriminative forensic features—that make standard label-guided adversarial training insufficient, as perturbations collapse the tampered-pixel manifold while leaving the authentic-pixel manifold nearly unchanged.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241740/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB tampered document image (contracts, invoices, bills in Chinese or English) and produces a pixel-level binary segmentation map where 0 denotes authentic pixels and 1 denotes tampered pixels. The DTL backbone (VF-DTL or PS-DTL) first extracts forensic features (e.g., DCT-domain frequency cues or multi-view progressive supervision) before feeding them into a shared feature extractor and segmentation head. Evaluation uses F-score under clean conditions and under white-box (FGSM, BIM, PGD, APGD with ε=8/255, T=4) and black-box transfer attacks.

## Method

The paper proposes a latent manifold adversarial training (LM-AT) method that generates adversarial examples by maximizing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the latent manifolds of adversarial and clean pixels, rather than relying on label-guided loss gradients. A WideResNet-based discriminator D_W is trained to distinguish adversarial from clean latent features z_{i,j}=ε_θ(x)_{i,j} at each spatial position, with its parameters updated by a combined manifold loss L_M (BCE on manifold labels) and an auxiliary tampering-localization loss L_aux. The perturbation δ is then crafted by following the gradient of L_M over the input image, preserving the original data manifold structure that label-guided PGD perturbations tend to corrupt. This GAN-inspired f-divergence formulation avoids label leaking and captures global distributional relationships between adversarial and clean manifolds, yielding more diverse and universally effective adversarial examples for the outer defensive optimization of the DTL segmentation network.

## Results

On T-Sroie (white-box, ε=8/255, T=4), the proposed manifold-based adversarial training (LM-AT) achieves the best F-score under all four iterative attacks for both baseline DTL models: 81.53% PGD and 51.08% APGD on VF-DTL, and 61.15% PGD and 60.96% APGD on PS-DTL. On DocTamper, LM-AT similarly leads under FGSM (42.23% on VF-DTL, 37.78% on PS-DTL) and APGD (19.23% on VF-DTL, 19.72% on PS-DTL). Under black-box transfer attacks on T-Sroie, LM-AT achieves 77.81% F-score (BIM, source PS-DTL → target VF-DTL) and 67.58% (BIM, source VF-DTL → target PS-DTL), substantially outperforming all other AT methods. Clean F-scores are sacrificed relative to the untrained baseline (e.g., 85.95% vs. 95.23% for VF-DTL on T-Sroie), a known trade-off in adversarial training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LM-AT against four adversarial training baselines (PGD-AT, DDC-AT, SegPGD-AT, Trades-AT) on two DTL backbones (VF-DTL, PS-DTL). On T-Sroie white-box attacks, LM-AT wins on every adversarial metric for both models: e.g., 81.53% PGD F-score vs. 77.78% (PGD-AT) and 75.22% (SegPGD-AT) on VF-DTL, and 60.96% APGD vs. 45.55% (PGD-AT) and 24.39% (SegPGD-AT) on PS-DTL. The gap is most dramatic under APGD, where LM-AT outperforms the second-best by 1.93% (VF-DTL) and 15.41% (PS-DTL). On DocTamper, LM-AT leads under FGSM by 3.74% over PGD-AT (VF-DTL) and 6.81% over Trades-AT (PS-DTL), and under APGD by 29.50% over the second-best (VF-DTL). The only metric where LM-AT trails is clean F-score on VF-DTL (85.95% vs. 92.90% for Trades-AT and 90.96% for DDC-AT), reflecting the standard accuracy–robustness trade-off; however, for PS-DTL, LM-AT also achieves the best clean score (78.25%). Under black-box transfer attacks, LM-AT consistently outperforms all baselines, with the largest margin over SegPGD-AT (e.g., 77.81% vs. 47.76% BIM, source PS-DTL → target VF-DTL).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training literature from image classification (PGD-AT, Trades-AT) and semantic segmentation (SegPGD-AT, CosPGD) to the domain of document tampering localization, a task with distinctive challenges of extreme class imbalance and weak forensic features. It departs from label-guided adversarial example generation by introducing a GAN-based discriminator that measures Jensen-Shannon divergence between latent manifolds of adversarial and clean pixels, drawing on f-divergence variational bounds and manifold adversarial training ideas from Zhang et al. (2021). The baseline DTL models (VF-DTL, PS-DTL) are treated as fixed architectures onto which the adversarial training framework is grafted, rather than being redesigned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that state-of-the-art DTL models are catastrophically vulnerable to adversarial perturbations (F1 dropping from ~95% to near 0% under APGD), which is a critical gap for any real-world forensic deployment. The manifold-based approach offers a principled alternative to label-guided attacks that avoids label leaking and better handles the class-imbalance pathology of DTL, though the clean-accuracy trade-off remains unresolved. An open question is whether the clean/robust accuracy trade-off can be mitigated, and whether the manifold divergence formulation generalizes beyond the two DTL architectures and two datasets tested here.
