## Problem

Existing text-to-image personalization methods (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, Break-a-scene) learn entire objects as monolithic concepts, making it impossible to selectively compose specific parts (e.g., head of one species, wings of another) into a coherent new object. Text prompts are imprecise for visual details, sketches require drawing ability, and spatial controls (bounding boxes, masks) add cumbersome manual intervention. PartCraft addresses this by enabling fine-grained part-level selection as the sole control mechanism for creative object composition.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes unlabeled RGB images of a single object category (e.g., 5,994 bird images from CUB-200-2011) and discovers parts via unsupervised DINOv2 feature clustering in a three-tier hierarchy (foreground/background → M common parts → K species-specific variants per part). At inference, the user provides a discrete selection of part cluster indices (e.g., head from species A, wings from species B, body from species C, background from species D), which are formatted as pseudo-word tokens in a prompt like 'a photo of a [Head,42] [Wing,87]...'. The output is a single composed RGB image where all selected parts are faithfully integrated into a holistically coherent object, generated at the standard Stable Diffusion v1.5 resolution.

## Method

PartCraft first discovers object parts unsupervised by applying three-level k-means clustering on DINOv2 patch features: separating foreground from background, identifying M common semantic parts, and splitting each part into K species-specific variants. Each part is then encoded as a learnable pseudo-word token, but rather than feeding raw embeddings to the text encoder, a two-layer MLP bottleneck projects them into a shared part-level embedding space first, enabling information exchange among instances of the same part and accelerating convergence. The key training innovation is an entropy-based normalized attention loss: cross-attention maps from the denoiser's cross-attention blocks are averaged over selected layers, normalized across all M parts at each spatial location (analogous to softmax), and then minimized via binary cross-entropy against the part segmentation masks, enforcing that each image region is attended to by at most one part. This loss, combined with LoRA fine-tuning on the cross-attention blocks of Stable Diffusion v1.5 and the standard diffusion reconstruction loss, enables strong part disentanglement while learning generic prior topology knowledge about how parts compose into a faithful whole object.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011 (birds), PartCraft achieves the best DINO (0.618), EMR (0.460), and CoSim (0.882) scores, with a competitive FID of 12.86 (vs. 10.10 for Textual Inversion). On Stanford Dogs, PartCraft leads on all five metrics: FID 16.92, CLIP 0.669, DINO 0.573, EMR 0.358, and CoSim 0.796. The EMR advantage over the strongest baseline (Break-a-Scene) is 7 percentage points on birds (0.460 vs. 0.390) and 5.8 points on dogs (0.358 vs. 0.300), demonstrating substantially better part-level disentanglement and compositional fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartCraft outperforms all four baselines on the part-specific metrics (EMR and CoSim) across both datasets, with the largest gap over Break-a-Scene (the only other method using an attention loss): +7.0 EMR on birds and +5.8 EMR on dogs. On image quality (FID), PartCraft is competitive with Textual Inversion on birds (12.86 vs. 10.10) and clearly superior on dogs (16.92 vs. 23.36), while Custom Diffusion and Break-a-Scene degrade substantially (FID 37.61 and 20.05 on birds). PartCraft also leads on DINO similarity (0.618 birds, 0.573 dogs), indicating better structural fidelity to the target parts. The only metric where PartCraft trails is CLIP on birds (0.783 vs. 0.784 for Textual Inversion), a negligible 0.001 difference. Overall, PartCraft dominates the part-composition task while maintaining image quality on par with the simplest baseline (Textual Inversion) that does not fine-tune the diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartCraft extends the textual inversion paradigm (Gal et al., ICLR 2023) from learning whole-object pseudo-words to learning individual object *parts* as separate text tokens, using a pre-trained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5) as the generative backbone. It departs from prior personalization methods (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, Break-a-scene) in three key ways: (1) parts are discovered unsupervised via DINOv2 feature maps and hierarchical k-means clustering rather than relying on segmentation models or manual annotations; (2) an entropy-based normalized attention loss replaces the MSE-based attention loss of Break-a-scene to enforce that each spatial location is attended by at most one part; and (3) a two-layer MLP bottleneck encoder projects part tokens through a shared part-embedding space before fine-grained adaptation, a design absent from all compared baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PartCraft introduces a genuinely new interaction paradigm for generative AI—selecting visual parts from existing objects rather than writing prompts or drawing sketches—lowering the barrier to fine-grained creative control. The demonstrated transferability (e.g., a cat with a dog's ear, a bird-shaped robot) suggests the learned part tokens are semantically meaningful and composable across domains, opening avenues for design and artistic tooling. However, the authors acknowledge that part discovery quality is bounded by the self-supervised feature extractor, small parts (tails, legs) remain difficult to compose faithfully, and cross-domain part merging (e.g., rigid + non-rigid parts) is left as future work, so the approach's generality beyond fine-grained animal datasets is still unproven.
