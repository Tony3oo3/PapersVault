## Problem

In semi-supervised domain generalization for medical image segmentation, domain shifts between institutions cause disparate feature statistics in batch normalization layers, which degrades pseudo-label quality when a single normalization is applied across all domains. Existing SSL methods ignore multi-domain distribution shifts, while DG methods underutilize abundant unlabeled data. The paper identifies this statistics mismatch as both a source of pseudo-label corruption and an exploitable signal for improving generalization to unseen domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241431/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D medical image slices (288×288 for Prostate and M&Ms MRI, 256×256 for Fundus retinal images) from K source domains, each with a small labeled subset (2–30%) and a large unlabeled pool, and produces pixel-wise binary or multi-class segmentation masks. During training, weakly augmented unlabeled images are forwarded through domain-specific SIBs while strongly augmented and histogram-matched variants are forwarded through the shared SAB; at inference, only the SAB is retained, yielding a standard U-Net parameter count (~1.81M).

## Method

The core architecture pairs per-domain statistics-individual branches (SIBs), each with dedicated BN layers that normalize features using domain-specific batch statistics, with a single statistics-aggregated branch (SAB) that uses a learnable per-channel mixture of batch and instance normalization to produce domain-invariant predictions. Pseudo-labels are generated by ensembling teacher-model outputs from both SIB and SAB forward passes on weakly augmented unlabeled images. Training employs a mean-teacher framework with three consistency streams: weak-to-strong (color jittering), weak-to-style (histogram matching to align grayscale distributions across domains), and random-forward (replacing the correct SIB's BN affine parameters with those randomly drawn from another domain's SIB to simulate feature-level domain shift). The SIBs are discarded at test time, so the deployed model has the same parameter size as a vanilla U-Net.

## Results

On the Prostate dataset (30% labeled), the proposed method achieves 88.73% average Dice and 1.93 average ASD, outperforming the previous SSDG SOTA CACPS by 2.26% Dice and 0.19 ASD. On Fundus (30% labeled), it reaches 85.60% average Dice, surpassing CACPS (84.12%) by 1.48%. On M&Ms, the method leads with 85.68% Dice and 0.59 ASD at 2% labeled data, and 86.68% Dice and 0.53 ASD at 5% labeled, improving over CACPS by 1.16% Dice and 0.59 ASD at the 2% setting. Overall, the method improves average Dice by 8.00% over the best SSL method (SS-Net) and 9.61% over the best DG method (RAM-DSIR) on Prostate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on average Dice across all three datasets. Against the previous SSDG SOTA CACPS [40], it gains 2.26% Dice on Prostate, 1.48% on Fundus, and 1.16% on M&Ms (2% labeled), while also achieving lower ASD in every setting. Among SSL baselines, SS-Net [37] is the strongest on Prostate (80.73% Dice) and BCP [2] on Fundus (81.94% Dice), both trailing the proposed method by 8+ points on Prostate and 3.7 points on Fundus. Among DG baselines, RAM-DSIR [44] leads on Prostate (79.12% Dice) and FedDG [16] on Fundus (83.62% Dice), yet both remain 9.6% and 2.0% below the proposed method respectively. The weakest baselines are MT [32] and UA-MT [41], which struggle severely on challenging domains (e.g., MT scores only 12.81% Dice on Prostate domain E), highlighting the difficulty of domain shift without explicit statistics handling. CauSSL [22] also underperforms, scoring 63.46% average Dice on Prostate, suggesting that causality-inspired SSL alone is insufficient under multi-domain shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-teacher / weak-to-strong consistency framework (Tarvainen 2017; Sohn 2020; Yang 2023) into the semi-supervised domain generalization (SSDG) setting, where prior work like CACPS [40] addressed pseudo-label quality through post-hoc Fourier-based confidence filtering. The key conceptual departure is identifying batch normalization statistics as the primary mechanism through which domain shift degrades pseudo-labels, and designing a multi-branch architecture (statistics-individual branches with per-domain BNs, plus a statistics-aggregated branch using a learnable batch-instance mixture from Nam 2018 / Seo 2020) to address this at the normalization level rather than at the output level. The random BN selection strategy for feature-level perturbation is a novel repurposing of the affine parameters already present in the SIBs, turning a domain-specific adaptation mechanism into a domain-shift simulator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why multi-domain semi-supervised segmentation degrades (BN statistics mismatch) and a targeted architectural fix, which is more principled than the post-processing approaches in prior SSDG work. However, the SIBs are discarded at inference, meaning the pseudo-label quality benefit is confined to training, and the method is only validated on 2D U-Net across three datasets with 4–6 domains. Open questions include whether the random BN selection strategy scales to many more domains or to transformer-based backbones where normalization plays a different role, and whether the histogram-matching augmentation generalizes beyond grayscale medical images to multi-channel or volumetric data.
