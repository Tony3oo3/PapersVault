## Problem

3D hand mesh reconstruction in the wild is severely limited by the scarcity of in-the-wild hand images with accurate 3D annotations. Existing alternatives—pseudo-annotated in-the-wild data and synthetic relit datasets—suffer from either poor label precision or unnatural hand-background combinations that fail to close the indoor-outdoor domain gap. This paper addresses the data bottleneck by generating diverse, accurately labeled in-the-wild hand images on demand.

## Input / Output

The method consumes four modalities: a 512×512 global RGB image, a 512×512 global hand mesh image, a 1×4 bounding box, and a free-form text prompt. The bounding box is used to crop and resize both the RGB and mesh images into 512×512 local versions, so the network simultaneously conditions on global scene context and hand-focused local detail. The output is a single 512×512 RGB hand image that is spatially aligned with the input mesh geometry and semantically consistent with the text prompt.

## Method

AttentionHand is a Stable Diffusion-based framework whose conditioning phase has two stages. The Text Attention Stage (TAS) identifies hand-related tokens in the prompt via part-of-speech tagging, extracts their cross-attention maps, refines them with softmax and a fixed Gaussian filter, and updates the noisy latent embedding through a novel max-over-tokens loss (L^TAS) that prevents any single token from dominating. The Visual Attention Stage (VAS) follows a ControlNet-style design: a frozen diffusion U-Net (θ_d) and a learnable guidance U-Net (θ_g) initialized from the same pretrained weights, where θ_g contains zero convolutions in its last 12 blocks and is conditioned on concatenated global and local mesh images; the guidance feature is added to the diffusion feature. Training minimizes a weighted sum of global and local denoising losses (L^G + L^L) in the standard diffusion forward/reverse process, with the global and local branches sharing weights to reduce parameters by ~20%.

## Results

On MSCOCO for text-to-hand image generation, AttentionHand (with TAS) achieves the best FID of 20.71, FID-Hand of 27.09, hand confidence of 0.965, MSE-2D of 1.026, MSE-3D of 1.986, and user preference of 36.9%, outperforming all baselines across every metric. For 3D hand mesh reconstruction, adding AttentionHand-generated data to InterWild reduces MPVPE by 3.66% on HIC and 7.81% on ReIH, and reduces MRRPE by 6.40% on HIC, demonstrating significant gains on in-the-wild benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttentionHand outperforms all four text-to-image baselines on every metric. Compared to the closest competitor T2I-Adapter (FID 22.00, FID-H 32.08, Hand Conf. 0.914), AttentionHand improves FID by 1.29, FID-H by 4.99, and hand confidence by 0.051, while nearly doubling user preference (36.9% vs. 19.7%). ControlNet achieves a competitive FID (21.67) but has a much worse FID-H (40.32 vs. 27.09) and lower hand confidence (0.810), indicating it struggles with hand-specific quality. Uni-ControlNet and vanilla Stable Diffusion lag substantially on all metrics (FID 30.34 and 40.52 respectively). For 3D hand mesh reconstruction, adding AttentionHand-generated data consistently improves all five baselines (IHMR, InterShape, IntagHead, DIR, InterWild) across HIC, ReIH, and IH2.6M; the largest relative gains appear on in-the-wild datasets (e.g., InterWild MRRPE drops from 31.26 to 29.26 on HIC, a 6.4% reduction), confirming that the generated data most effectively bridges the indoor-to-outdoor domain gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

AttentionHand extends the Stable Diffusion framework by incorporating ControlNet-style zero-convolution conditioning on hand mesh images, while departing from prior text-to-image models (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, Uni-ControlNet) that treat hands as just another object within the image. It also diverges from prior hand-specific generative models—GAN-based (GestureGAN, GANerated Hands) and diffusion-based (DiffHand, HandDiffuse, HandRefiner)—by being explicitly text-driven and targeting in-the-wild scene generation rather than gesture-to-gesture translation or inpainting. The key architectural departure is the dual-stage conditioning: a text attention stage that selectively amplifies hand-related tokens via part-of-speech tagging and Gaussian-refined attention maps, combined with a visual attention stage that jointly conditions on global and local mesh images to preserve both background harmony and hand fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent bottleneck in 3D hand reconstruction: the severe scarcity of accurately annotated in-the-wild hand data, where existing solutions (pseudo-annotation on MSCOCO, relit synthetic datasets) introduce domain gaps or label noise. By showing that generated data from AttentionHand improves MRRPE by 20–30% across multiple reconstruction baselines on in-the-wild benchmarks, it validates a practical data-augmentation pipeline that could be adopted by the broader 3D hand community. However, the authors acknowledge that diversity may degrade as the model over-optimizes for mesh alignment, and the approach remains dependent on the quality of the base Stable Diffusion model—future work on more diverse diffusion backbones or on extending the conditioning to object interactions would be natural next steps.
