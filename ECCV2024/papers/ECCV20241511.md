## Problem

Prior 3D talking head methods suffer from poor multi-view consistency, inaccurate lip synchronization, and a lack of emotional expressiveness, while no existing dataset provides both emotion annotations and per-frame 3D facial geometry needed to train emotion-controllable models. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the EMOTALK3D dataset and a synthesis framework that explicitly controls emotion in generated talking heads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241511/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a speech audio signal (encoded by a pre-trained HuBERT model) and manually set emotion labels (8 emotions × 2 intensities plus neutral, changeable per time step) as input, and produces a free-view 3D talking head video renderable across −90° to +90° viewing angles. The internal representation is a 4D Gaussian model where the 3D mesh predicted by S2GNet is quadrupled-upsampled before feeding the appearance module, and the final output is rendered via differentiable 3D Gaussian splatting with spherical-harmonic appearance.

## Method

The core contribution is a 'Speech-to-Geometry-to-Appearance' pipeline that decomposes the complex audio-to-appearance mapping into two simpler stages. S2GNet (a GRU-based network initialized from FaceXHubert) regresses vertex displacements on a mean template mesh conditioned on HuBERT audio features and a transformer-based emotion extractor's output, producing a 4D point cloud. G2ANet then synthesizes appearance by disentangling it into Canonical Gaussians (static head learned from a neutral first frame) and Dynamic Gaussians, where a 4-layer MLP (FeatureNet) predicts per-Gaussian spherical-harmonic coefficients and a 2-layer MLP (RotationNet) predicts rotations, both conditioned on the canonical parameters, emotion vector, UV position, and local displacement δμ. Non-facial regions (hair, neck, shoulders) are completed from randomly initialized points with distance-decayed motion coupling to the face, and a smooth weight mask fuses the dynamic facial Gaussians with the static canonical Gaussians to avoid blur in the non-facial regions.

## Results

On the EMOTALK3D test set, EmoTalk3D achieves PSNR 21.22, SSIM 0.83, LPIPS 0.12, LMD 3.62, and CPBD 0.30, outperforming all 2D and 3D baselines on most metrics. The method leads in PSNR (21.22 vs. 20.61 for MakeItTalk), LMD (3.62 vs. 3.76 for DreamTalk), and CPBD (0.30 vs. 0.38 for DreamTalk), while tying AD-NeRF on SSIM (0.83) and MakeItTalk on LPIPS (0.12). In the user study, EmoTalk3D scores highest on Video Fidelity (3.96/5) and ties for best Image Quality (4.25/5), with competitive Speech-Visual Sync (3.54/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoTalk3D outperforms all 2D image-based methods (MakeItTalk, EAMM, SadTalker, DreamTalk) by a wide margin on every metric, with the closest competitor MakeItTalk trailing by 0.61 dB in PSNR and 0.83 in LMD. Among 3D methods, it surpasses AD-NeRF in PSNR (21.22 vs. 20.39), LPIPS (0.12 vs. 0.22), and LMD (3.62 vs. 5.10), while tying on SSIM (0.83); AD-NeRF retains a large advantage in CPBD (0.04 vs. 0.30), suggesting EmoTalk3D's renders are slightly less sharp in a no-reference sharpness metric. Real3D-Portrait and DreamTalk are clearly outperformed across all five metrics. In the user study, EmoTalk3D achieves the highest Video Fidelity score (3.96) and ties SadTalker for best Image Quality (4.25), while its Speech-Visual Sync score (3.54) is marginally below MeshTalk (3.60) and VOCA (3.54). Notably, EmoTalk3D is the only method that supports both wide-range free-view rendering (−90° to +90°) and explicit emotion control, capabilities absent from all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of audio-driven talking head synthesis, 3D Gaussian Splatting, and emotional expression generation. It departs from the NeRF-based talking head line (AD-NeRF, SSP-NeRF, ER-NeRF) by adopting 3DGS for real-time rendering, and from prior emotional talking head work (EVP, EAMM) by operating in explicit 3D geometry rather than 2D pixel space. The 'Speech-to-Geometry-to-Appearance' decomposition builds on FaceXHubert's audio-to-mesh prediction but introduces a GRU-based S2GNet conditioned on emotion labels, followed by a G2ANet that disentangles canonical and dynamic 3D Gaussians — a design not present in prior 3DGS or NeRF talking head methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EMOTALK3D dataset is the first to combine emotion annotations with per-frame 3D facial geometry and calibrated multi-view capture, filling a critical gap that has hindered research on emotional 3D talking heads. The canonical/dynamic Gaussian decomposition offers a practical recipe for capturing motion-induced wrinkles and shading that prior methods miss. However, the method is strictly person-specific and requires a 11-camera dome setup, limiting immediate applicability; open questions include generalization to unseen identities, single-image input, and modeling dynamic hair — all acknowledged by the authors as future directions.
