## Problem

Existing adversarially robust knowledge distillation methods align teacher and student at the sample-to-sample level (individual features or predictions), overlooking the statistical alignment of feature distributions. The paper shows that robust accuracy correlates strongly and negatively with the variance gap between adversarial and clean feature representations, suggesting that reducing this gap—rather than merely matching individual samples—is the key mechanism for transferring robustness to lightweight students.

## Input / Output

The method consumes mini-batches of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) along with their adversarial counterparts generated via multi-step PGD (ε=8/255). Rather than processing samples independently, it computes second-order statistics—covariance matrices over concatenated clean and adversarial feature vectors, and gram matrices over prediction score vectors—across the entire batch. The output is a standard class label from the distilled student, but the training signal is driven by batch-level statistical alignment rather than per-sample matching.

## Method

STARSHIP augments standard adversarial robust knowledge distillation (ARKD) with three statistics-level losses: (1) Feature Covariance Alignment (FCA) aligns the student's 2d×2d feature covariance matrix (built from concatenated clean and adversarial projected features) toward the teacher's using a non-Euclidean distance on SPD matrices (MaxExp, KL divergence, or Frobenius norm); (2) Prediction-score Gram Matching (PGM) aligns the student's 2B×2B gram matrix of prediction scores toward the teacher's; and (3) Statistical Sub-matrices Alignment (Ω) enforces the student's adversarial-adversarial and clean-adversarial sub-matrices to match its clean-clean sub-matrix, acting as an intrinsic self-distillation. A parameter-level perturbation (inspired by AWP) is applied to the projection head parameters via a min-max game to make the covariance alignment robust to parameter shifts. The key distinction from prior work is that covariance is invariant to sample order while gram matrices are invariant to class order, so FCA and PGM capture complementary structural information about the teacher's robust decision boundary.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (WRN-28 → ResNet-18, ε=8/255), STARSHIP achieves 86.47% clean accuracy and 53.78% Auto-Attack robust accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline AdaAD (86.38% clean, 52.36% AA) by +0.09% and +1.42% respectively. The adaptive variant Ada-STARSHIP further improves to 87.04% clean and 54.47% AA. On CIFAR-100, STARSHIP reaches 61.54% clean and 29.30% AA, surpassing AdaAD (61.26% clean, 27.46% AA) by +0.28% and +1.84%. The method also generalizes to MNV2 students and ImageNet-100, where it achieves 71.10% clean and 32.74% AA (ResNet-18) and 68.20% clean and 32.87% AA (MNV2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STARSHIP consistently outperforms all five baselines on robust accuracy across both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18), STARSHIP surpasses the closest competitor AdaAD by +1.42% AA robustness (53.78 vs. 52.36) and +1.24% PGD (57.45 vs. 56.23), while also achieving the highest clean accuracy (86.47%). The gap over earlier methods is larger: RSLAD is beaten by +2.42% AA, AKD by +3.67% AA, and ARD by +4.38% AA on CIFAR-10. On CIFAR-100, STARSHIP leads AdaAD by +1.84% AA (29.30 vs. 27.46) and +2.21% PGD (34.45 vs. 32.24), with the advantage over RSLAD reaching +1.78% AA. Notably, Ada-STARSHIP achieves the best clean accuracy (87.04% on CIFAR-10, 62.19% on CIFAR-100) but shows a slight robustness drop on CIFAR-100 compared to vanilla STARSHIP, suggesting the adaptive adversary generation trades some robustness for natural accuracy on the harder dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

STARSHIP departs from the dominant sample-to-sample alignment paradigm in adversarially robust knowledge distillation (ARD, IAD, RSLAD, AKD, AdaAD) by introducing second-order statistics alignment—specifically, feature covariance alignment (FCA) and prediction-score gram matrix matching (PGM)—between teacher and student. It builds directly on RSLAD's adversarial sample generation and ARKD baseline loss, incorporates AdaAD's adaptive adversary generation, and adapts AWP's parameter-level perturbation to the projection head to enforce robust covariance alignment. The key conceptual shift is treating adversarial samples as specialized augmentations whose set-level statistical properties (covariance, gram structure) should be matched, rather than aligning individual sample representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that robust accuracy correlates negatively with the feature variance gap between adversarial and clean samples provides a useful diagnostic for understanding why lightweight students fail to inherit teacher robustness, and motivates a principled alignment objective. The method achieves consistent SOTA across CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet-100 with multiple student architectures and teacher types (CNN and ViT), and the self-distillation component (L_Ω) enables the student to surpass the teacher in same-architecture settings. Open questions include whether the variance-gap correlation holds beyond image classification, whether the MaxExp distance metric advantage generalizes to higher-dimensional feature spaces, and whether the added computational cost of parameter-level perturbations and projection heads limits practical deployment in extremely resource-constrained settings.
