## Problem

SAM's billion-scale pretraining produces highly asymmetric activation distributions with extreme outliers concentrated in specific channels, causing severe performance degradation under low-bit post-training quantization. Existing PTQ methods designed for conventional ViT classifiers do not address these distributional pathologies, making 4-bit and 2-bit quantization of SAM impractical for edge deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240266/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantized SAM accepts an RGB image combined with a point prompt, bounding box prompt, or an automatic grid of foreground points, and outputs a binary segmentation mask. Calibration requires only 100 unlabeled images from SA1B over 100 epochs with batch size 1. Critically, the learned shifting and scaling parameters are reparameterized into the preceding linear layer's weights and bias, so the deployed quantized model introduces zero additional computation or parameters at inference time.

## Method

PQ-SAM inserts a Grouped Activation Distribution Transformation (GADT) into each transformer block, applying learnable per-group shifting and scaling to reshape activations toward a quantization-friendly tensor-wise distribution. A two-stage Outlier Hierarchical Clustering (OHC) scheme governs the grouping: first, tensor-level IQR-based truncation removes extreme outliers to reduce inter-channel scale variance; second, an iterative channel-wise procedure allocates each pair of (shift, scale) parameters to a group of channels whose values fall within a quantile range of the remaining unallocated channels, progressively refining group granularity. The shifting/scaling sizes and the per-layer quantization step sizes (Δ_W, Δ_X) are jointly optimized end-to-end via MSE loss against the full-precision SAM output, using Adam with cosine annealing. This holistic optimization preserves the hardware-friendly per-tensor quantization format while effectively performing channel-wise distribution adjustment, and the final parameters fold into the linear layer for deployment.

## Results

On the point prompt mode (the primary evaluation), PQ-SAM at 6-bit quantization maintains less than 0.04 IoU/Dice gap to the full-precision SAM across most of the six zero-shot datasets for all three ViT sizes. At the more challenging 4-bit setting, PQ-SAM achieves 0.7080 IoU on BBBC038V1 (ViT-B), 0.7818 IoU on NDD20, and 0.7975 IoU on DOORS, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline RepQ-ViT (0.4738, 0.6479, 0.5878 IoU respectively). On the automatic mode (BSDS500 edge detection), PQ-SAM at 4-bit reaches 0.706 ODS / 0.642 AP for ViT-B, closing the gap to FP (0.745 / 0.690) to within ~5%. In the ultra-low 2-bit setting, PQ-SAM improves Dice by over 35% relative to RepQ-ViT on NDD20 (0.4480 vs 0.2941).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PQ-SAM outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and both 4-bit and 6-bit settings for all three SAM sizes. The strongest baseline, RepQ-ViT [23], is the only method that remains competitive at 4-bit; however, PQ-SAM still leads by a wide margin — e.g., on BBBC038V1 (ViT-B, 4-bit) PQ-SAM achieves 0.7080 IoU versus RepQ-ViT's 0.4738, a relative improvement of ~49%. FQ-ViT [25] performs poorly, often dropping below 0.20 IoU at 4-bit (e.g., 0.1669 on NDD20, 0.1134 on iShape for ViT-B), and is entirely non-functional at 2-bit. The classical MinMax [16] and MSE [5] baselines collapse at 4-bit (IoU < 0.40 on most datasets), while Percentile [20] sits in between but still trails PQ-SAM by 0.20–0.30 IoU on the harder datasets. At 6-bit, the gap narrows (PQ-SAM leads RepQ-ViT by ~0.02–0.06 IoU), but PQ-SAM remains consistently on top. In the ultra-low 2-bit ablation, PQ-SAM improves Dice by 35–40% over RepQ-ViT, confirming its advantage grows as bit-width decreases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of ViT post-training quantization work (RepQ-ViT, FQ-ViT) by identifying that SAM's billion-scale pretraining produces a qualitatively different activation distribution—highly asymmetric with extreme outliers concentrated in specific channels—that breaks assumptions underlying existing per-channel scaling methods. Rather than applying channel-level reparameterization directly, PQ-SAM introduces a grouped activation distribution transformation (GADT) that shares learnable shifting and scaling parameters across clusters of similar channels, guided by a two-stage outlier hierarchical clustering scheme. The joint end-to-end optimization of these transformation parameters with quantization step sizes (as opposed to per-layer independent calibration in prior work) represents a departure from the standard PTQ pipeline, drawing conceptually from OmniQuant-style shifting/scaling but adapting it to the grouped, outlier-aware setting specific to SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PQ-SAM is the first PTQ method that makes 4-bit SAM practically usable across all three prompt modes, requiring only 100 unlabeled calibration images and no retraining—critical given that SAM's SA1B training set contains 11 million images and full retraining is infeasible for most practitioners. The grouped outlier clustering approach is a reusable design pattern that could transfer to other large vision foundation models with similar activation pathologies. However, the method is validated exclusively on SAM and its specific ViT variants; whether the GADT+OHC scheme generalizes to other foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, CLIP) or to different quantization granularities (per-channel vs. per-tensor) remains open. The 2-bit results, while impressive relative to baselines, still show substantial degradation from FP, suggesting a fundamental precision floor for SAM's segmentation task.
