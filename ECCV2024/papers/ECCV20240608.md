## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detectors that leverage CLIP suffer from two intertwined issues: (1) category name embeddings from CLIP's text encoder lack the textual depth and visual semantic information needed to discriminate visually confusable categories, and (2) training solely on base-category annotations causes the detector to overfit, misclassifying novel objects as background or base classes. The paper identifies that inter-category visual relationships—explored via large language models—remain largely untapped for addressing both challenges simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a set of bounding boxes with class labels drawn from a large open vocabulary (up to 26,410 concepts compiled from LVIS, Object365, VisualGenome, Open Images, and ImageNet-21K). Detection scores are calibrated by a VLM score computed via a single pooling operation over the predicted box region, following the F-VLM inference pipeline. The detector uses 900 object queries and a frozen ConvNeXt-L CLIP backbone, requiring no RoI-Align or NMS post-processing at inference.

## Method

LaMI-DETR is a DETR-based detector (built on DINO with 900 queries) that uses a frozen CLIP ConvNeXt-L image encoder as backbone and CLIP text embeddings as classification weights. The core contribution is the Language Model Instruction (LaMI) strategy: GPT-3.5 generates fine-grained visual descriptions (shape, color, size) for each category, and the Instructor Embedding (T5) model encodes these descriptions to cluster visually similar categories into K groups. During training, Visual Concept Sampling leverages these clusters to zero out the sampling probability of categories visually similar to the ground truth in each iteration's federated loss, forcing the detector to learn generalized foreground features rather than overfitting to base classes. At inference, a Confusing Category mechanism identifies the most visually similar category for each class and regenerates a discriminative visual description to refine the VLM score, improving separation between confusable categories.

## Results

On OV-LVIS, LaMI-DETR achieves 43.4 rare box AP (APr) and 41.3 overall box AP, surpassing the previous best method CFM-ViT (ViT-L/16) by +7.8 APr and +2.8 AP, while using fewer parameters (196M vs. 303M) and no additional image-level training data. In the zero-shot LVIS setting, LaMI-DETR reaches 37.8 APr, outperforming OWL-ST (34.9) and DetCLIPv2 (33.3). For cross-dataset transfer, the model achieves 42.8 AP on COCO and 21.9 AP on Object365, exceeding CoDet by +3.7 AP and CFM by +3.2 AP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaMI-DETR outperforms all 22 compared baselines on the primary OV-LVIS benchmark. The most significant margin is over CFM-ViT (ViT-L/16), the previous state-of-the-art, by +7.8 APr (43.4 vs. 35.6) and +2.8 AP (41.3 vs. 38.5), despite using 107M fewer parameters and no external image-level dataset (CFM-ViT relies on ALIGN). Against other frozen-CLIP-based DETR methods like CORA (22.2 APr) and F-VLM (32.8 APr), LaMI-DETR gains +21.2 and +10.6 APr respectively, demonstrating the benefit of language-model-instructed concept representation and visual concept sampling. In the zero-shot LVIS setting (Table 2), LaMI-DETR (37.8 APr) beats OWL-ST (34.9) by +2.9 APr and DetCLIPv2 (33.3) by +4.5 APr under identical training data. For cross-dataset transfer (Table 3), LaMI-DETR leads CoDet by +3.7 AP on COCO (42.8 vs. 39.1) and CFM by +3.2 AP on Object365 (21.9 vs. 18.7), confirming strong generalizability without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaMI-DETR builds on the frozen-CLIP DETR paradigm established by F-VLM (for VLM score calibration at inference) and DINO (for the 900-query transformer decoder), while incorporating Federated Loss for handling incomplete annotations in large-vocabulary detection. Its key departure from prior OVOD methods (pseudo-labeling, distillation, parameter transfer) is the explicit modeling of inter-category visual relationships extracted via LLMs—GPT-3.5 generates visual descriptions and T5/Instructor Embedding clusters them—rather than relying solely on CLIP's text-space name embeddings. Compared to other frozen-CLIP DETR variants like CORA and EdaDet, LaMI-DETR maintains a single-backbone, end-to-end architecture without RoI-Align or decoupled classification/regression, requiring only one pooling operation at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +7.8 AP_r gain on OV-LVIS with a simpler architecture (196M params vs. 303M for CFM-ViT) and no additional image-level training data demonstrates that inter-category relationship modeling via LLMs is a powerful and underexplored axis for OVOD improvement. The Visual Concept Sampling mechanism—using LLM-clustered visual descriptions to exclude visually similar categories from negative sampling—is orthogonal to existing approaches and could potentially be combined with distillation or pseudo-labeling methods. A notable limitation is that the method is validated only with a ConvNeXt backbone; the authors acknowledge that exploring ViT-based VLMs remains open, and the reliance on GPT-3.5 for description generation introduces a dependency on a proprietary model.
