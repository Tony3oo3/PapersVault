## Problem

Existing model-free layer-wise vectorization methods like LIVE produce topology-preserving SVGs but cannot represent gradient fills, forcing them to emit excessive solid-color primitives to approximate smooth color transitions. Simply replacing RGBA colors with gradient parameters is non-trivial because the optimization must determine which pixels contribute to a path's gradient, a problem closely related to segmentation that prior methods do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240252/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB raster image as input and produces an SVG file consisting of an ordered list of cubic Bezier paths, each filled with a two-stop radial gradient (center, radius, and two color stops). The user specifies the number of paths to add per epoch and the total number of epochs; no training data or model weights are required. The output preserves layer-wise hierarchy, where the order of primitives encodes the drawing sequence and occlusion structure of the input.

## Method

The framework operates progressively on a DiffVG differentiable renderer: at each epoch it computes the residual between the target and current output, applies a gradient-aware segmentation (Laplacian filtering → Otsu binarization → morphological closing → watershed) to identify regions where gradient boundaries exist, and initializes new circle paths with radial gradients at the region with the largest accumulated squared error. A novel segmentation-guided loss reweights pixel-wise color error by intersecting each path's coverage with its originating segment, giving high weight to unoccluded interior pixels while retaining UDF-based contour emphasis, thereby fitting gradient centers that UDF alone ignores. An additional Xing loss penalizes Bezier self-intersection. The entire pipeline is model-free, requiring no training or user-provided segmentation.

## Results

On the user study (223 participants, 4,460 votes), the proposed method is preferred over LIVE across all three datasets: 59.6% vs. 40.4% on Noto Emoji, 65.3% vs. 34.7% on Fluent Emoji, and 57.9% vs. 42.1% on Iconfont. In terms of PSNR, the method achieves notably higher reconstruction quality at low path budgets (e.g., 26.73 vs. 25.19 dB at 8 paths on Fluent Emoji; 25.69 vs. 24.92 dB at 8 paths on Noto Emoji), with the gap narrowing as more paths are added. On the complex Iconfont dataset (no gradients), the method slightly underperforms LIVE at 512 paths (31.74 vs. 32.36 dB) but leads at lower budgets (20.31 vs. 19.22 dB at 16 paths).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is LIVE (Ma et al., CVPR 2022), a model-free progressive vectorization framework that uses DiffVG but lacks gradient support. The proposed method consistently outperforms LIVE in the user study across all three datasets, with the largest margin on Fluent Emoji (65.3% vs. 34.7%), where rich gradient effects are most prominent. In PSNR, the advantage is most pronounced at low path counts (e.g., +1.54 dB at 8 paths on Fluent Emoji, +0.77 dB at 8 paths on Noto Emoji), reflecting the method's ability to capture large-scale gradient features with fewer primitives. On the gradient-free Iconfont dataset, the method leads at low budgets (20.31 vs. 19.22 dB at 16 paths) but falls slightly behind LIVE at 512 paths (31.74 vs. 32.36 dB), suggesting that for highly detailed solid-color inputs, many small precise paths eventually outperform fewer gradient-filled ones. The ablation confirms that both gradients and segmentation guidance contribute independently, with their combination yielding the best results on gradient-rich inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LIVE's progressive, layer-wise vectorization framework by adding radial gradient fill support, which the authors argue is non-trivial because gradient parameters (center, radius, color stops) cannot be optimized effectively with LIVE's UDF loss alone. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a gradient-aware segmentation subroutine (Laplacian filtering + Otsu thresholding + watershed) that guides both path initialization and a novel segmentation-guided loss weight, drawing on classical image segmentation rather than learned models. The approach remains model-free and built entirely on DiffVG's differentiable renderer, positioning it alongside LIVE as a training-free alternative to learning-based vectorizers like Im2Vec and DeepSVG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in model-free vectorization: gradient-filled primitives are ubiquitous in real-world SVGs (emojis, icons, illustrations), yet prior progressive methods like LIVE required excessive paths to approximate them. The segmentation-guided loss is a broadly applicable idea for differentiable rendering optimization—weighting pixels by their estimated contribution to a specific path rather than uniformly or only at contours. Limitations include the fixed 4-Bezier initial shape struggling with intricate contours, the restriction to two-stop radial gradients, and evaluation against only a single baseline (LIVE), leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex gradient types or larger, more diverse image collections.
