## Problem

Existing point cloud denoising methods are trained under the assumption of synthetic isotropic Gaussian noise, which fails to capture the complex noise patterns of real-world scanners (outlier clusters, ghost points, edge flares). Additionally, prior approaches predict pointwise displacements or model noise distributions in isolation, rather than learning a joint transport plan between paired noisy and clean point clouds, and their distance-based losses scale poorly with input size.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240038/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy point cloud as XYZ coordinates and optionally augments each point with RGB values and DINOv2 features projected from raw RGB images via camera poses and intrinsics. For large scene-level inputs, processing is done in dataset-dependent radius-sphere patches to ensure spatial correspondence between noisy and clean points. The output is a denoised point cloud of the same cardinality, produced via iterative DDPM sampling (as few as 3–10 steps suffice).

## Method

P2P-Bridge formulates denoising as a Schrödinger bridge (data-to-data diffusion) problem, using the distribution of noisy point clouds as the prior rather than a Gaussian, so the model learns data-dependent noise characteristics. The bridge path between unordered point sets is defined by shortest-path interpolation (from PointMixup), which corresponds to an optimal transport plan; the optimal assignment is pre-computed once per data pair to align clean points with their noisy counterparts, enabling fast training without repeated assignment solving. The network is a PVCNN-based encoder with squeeze-excitation blocks, multi-headed global attention, and a feature-embedding layer, conditioned on timestep via sinusoidal embeddings and on global features via adaptive group normalization. Training uses a standard noise-prediction loss, and inference follows the OT-ODE limit (zero stochasticity) of the bridge SDE, which ablations show outperforms the stochastic SDE variant for this task.

## Results

On the PU-Net dataset with 3% isotropic Gaussian noise (sparse, 10k points), P2P-Bridge achieves a Chamfer Distance of 3.99×10⁻⁴ and P2M of 1.42×10⁻⁴, outperforming the best baseline PD-Flow (CD 5.19, P2M 2.52) by ~23% in CD. On the PC-Net evaluation set under the same conditions, P2P-Bridge reaches CD 5.58 and P2M 1.29, again beating PD-Flow (CD 6.61, P2M 1.62). On real-world ScanNet++ indoor scenes, P2P-Bridge with XYZ+RGB+DINO features achieves CD 2.65×10⁻⁴, substantially outperforming the next-best method ScoreDenoise (CD 3.72×10⁻⁴). All results are obtained with only 3 DDPM denoising steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2P-Bridge outperforms all 8 baselines on the majority of noise settings, with the largest margins at 2% and 3% Gaussian noise where methods trained under synthetic noise assumptions degrade most. At the lowest noise level (1%, sparse PU-Net), PD-Flow slightly edges out P2P-Bridge (CD 2.13 vs. 2.28), but P2P-Bridge recovers the lead at all higher noise levels. On the PC-Net generalization set, P2P-Bridge achieves the best CD and P2M across all noise levels, indicating stronger generalization to unseen objects. On real-world ScanNet++ scenes, P2P-Bridge (XYZ+RGB+DINO) reduces CD from 3.72 (ScoreDenoise) to 2.65 (×10⁻⁴), a ~29% improvement, and similarly dominates on ARKitScenes. ScoreDenoise and MAG exhibit visible patch-border artifacts and point collapse due to their Langevin sampling under Gaussian noise assumptions, while PD-Flow shows circular patch artifacts but avoids point collapse. I-PFN is competitive at low noise but degrades faster at 3% noise (CD 5.49 vs. 3.99 for P2P-Bridge on PU-Net sparse).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Schrödinger bridge framework—previously applied to image-to-image translation (I2SB, De Bortoli et al.) and text-to-speech (Chen et al.)—to the domain of 3D point cloud denoising, which is the first such application. It departs from the dominant data-to-noise paradigm in prior denoising work (ScoreDenoise, MAG, PD-Flow) by formulating denoising as a data-to-data diffusion between paired noisy and clean point clouds, leveraging the analytic Gaussian posterior and OT-ODE connection from recent SB literature. For handling the unordered nature of point sets, it borrows shortest-path interpolation from PointMixup to define the bridge path, and adopts the PVCNN architecture with squeeze-excitation blocks from LION as its backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most prior denoising methods are trained under synthetic Gaussian noise assumptions that fail on real-world LIDAR and ARKit scans with outlier clusters, ghost points, and edge flares. By learning data-specific noise characteristics through the bridge formulation, P2P-Bridge generalizes better to real sensor data, as demonstrated on ScanNet++ and ARKitScenes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to point cloud completion (the paper acknowledges incomplete objects remain unaddressed), whether the strong alignment requirement limits applicability to unpaired data, and whether the DINOv2 feature integration can be made more efficient for large-scale deployment.
