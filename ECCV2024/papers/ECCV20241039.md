## Problem

Noisy few-shot learning faces a unique challenge where data cleansing to remove noisy labels further diminishes the already scarce per-task training data, causing severe information loss that degrades few-shot generalization. Existing remedies—robust loss functions, data augmentation, and standard cleansing—either ignore this information loss or cannot cover the full task manifold from limited samples. The paper asks how to recover the corrupted task manifold without incurring additional annotation costs or introducing further label noise.

## Input / Output

During meta-training, the method consumes two streams: (1) primary tasks sampled from a noisy-labeled distribution in N-way K-shot format (e.g., 5-way 1-shot or 5-way 5-shot), where each task contains a small support set and query set with potentially corrupted labels; and (2) auxiliary tasks constructed by randomly sampling N unlabeled images from a separate (possibly cross-domain) dataset and generating Q augmented views of each image. At meta-test time, the model receives a few labeled support examples and unlabeled query images from an unseen target domain and outputs class predictions. The shared feature extractor (Conv4 backbone) produces domain-invariant embeddings that feed both the task-specific classifier and the domain critic.

## Method

DUNT trains a shared Conv4 feature extractor via three coupled losses under a bi-level (MAML-style) optimization. First, a cross-entropy loss on primary tasks is regularized by episode cleansing: high-loss query and support examples are down-weighted using a self-paced threshold to suppress noisy-label memorization. Second, a contrastive loss is applied to carefully selected unsupervised auxiliary tasks, where a novel diversity metric (the average reciprocal cosine similarity of softmax class-probability vectors across all image pairs in a task) filters out 'confusing' tasks whose samples likely share the same true class, preventing the model from learning spurious sample-specific features. Third, an adversarial domain-critic loss with a gradient reversal layer enforces domain invariance between primary and auxiliary feature distributions; the paper proves this regularizer is equivalent to a W₁ Wasserstein distance term, yielding tight generalization bounds that decompose target risk into primary and auxiliary risks plus transport costs. In practice, six auxiliary tasks are upsampled per iteration and only the single most diverse task is retained for training.

## Results

On the non-transductive setting (Table 1), DUNT achieves the best accuracy across all five datasets in both 5-way 1-shot and 5-way 5-shot settings. Headline numbers include 56.94% on CUB 1-shot (vs. 55.39% for the best baseline RNNP), 52.22% on miniImageNet 1-shot (vs. 50.18% for RNNP), and 62.80% on CIFAR-FS 1-shot (vs. 60.86% for TraNFS). In the 5-shot setting, DUNT reaches 72.17% on CUB, 65.10% on miniImageNet, and 74.44% on CIFAR-FS, consistently outperforming the strongest baselines (DCML, RNNP) by 0.5–2.5 percentage points. In the transductive setting (Table 2), DUNT similarly leads all methods, e.g., 55.01% on CUB 1-shot vs. 52.55% for DDNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUNT outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and both shot settings in the non-transductive evaluation. The closest competitors are RNNP and DCML (dedicated noisy-FSL methods), which DUNT surpasses by 1.5–2.5 points in 1-shot (e.g., 56.94 vs. 55.39 on CUB; 62.80 vs. 60.86 on CIFAR-FS) and by 0.5–2.0 points in 5-shot. General data-cleansing baselines (PN+SPL, PN+FSR) perform substantially worse, confirming the paper's claim that removing noisy samples causes harmful information loss in the few-shot regime. In the transductive setting, DUNT beats the strongest transductive baselines (DDNet, STARTUP) by 2–5 points (e.g., 55.01 vs. 52.55 on CUB 1-shot; 49.29 vs. 45.11 on tiered 1-shot). On cross-domain benchmarks (Table 3), DUNT achieves the highest accuracy on all four target domains (EuroSAT, CropDisease, ChestX, ISIC), with the largest margin on ChestX 5-shot (40.31% vs. 37.84% for RNNP), demonstrating the benefit of the Wasserstein-based domain-invariant regularization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ProtoNet's bi-level meta-optimization framework for few-shot classification and repurposes the Wasserstein distance regularizer from WGAN-style domain adaptation to align a primary (noisy, labeled) distribution with an auxiliary (clean, unlabeled) distribution. It departs from standard noisy-label learning (data cleansing, robust loss, noise transition estimation) by arguing these techniques are inadequate in the few-shot regime because removing noisy examples causes irrecoverable information loss when only a handful of samples per class are available. The novel contribution is a diversity-based auxiliary task selection criterion that filters out 'confusing' contrastive tasks where randomly sampled same-class images are mistakenly treated as negatives, combined with episode cleansing on the primary task to mitigate label noise without discarding all potentially noisy data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: standard noisy-label defenses degrade sharply in few-shot settings where each sample is precious, and the proposed auxiliary-task compensation strategy offers a principled way to recover the task manifold without additional annotation. The theoretical generalization bounds (Lemma 1–2) provide a clean justification for the Wasserstein-regularized objective, linking the adversarial critic to optimal transport cost. Limitations include the requirement of an external auxiliary dataset from a related domain, the circularity of using the model's own softmax predictions to compute the diversity metric, and evaluation restricted to image classification benchmarks; open questions include whether the approach extends to non-visual modalities or settings where no suitable auxiliary domain is available.
