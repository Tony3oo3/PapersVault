## Problem

Patch-wise higher-resolution image generation with pre-trained diffusion models suffers from small object repetition because an identical text prompt is applied to every patch, encouraging the model to regenerate the same objects in each region. While DemoFusion partially mitigates this via residual connections and dilated sampling, the adversarial tension between the prompt (encouraging repetition) and global structure (suppressing it) still produces repeated small objects, especially at ultra-high resolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240137/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image (up to 4096×4096, i.e., 16× the 1024×1024 training resolution of SDXL) without any additional training. Internally, it first generates a low-resolution image to extract cross-attention maps, which are then used to construct per-patch prompt substrings; the final output is a single seamless high-resolution image decoded from the latent representation.

## Method

AccDiffusion introduces two training-free modules on top of the DemoFusion pipeline. First, it decouples the global text prompt into patch-content-aware substrings by thresholding cross-attention maps from the low-resolution pass (using the per-word mean as threshold), applying morphological opening to remove spurious small regions, and including a word token in a patch's prompt only if its highly-responsive region covers more than a fraction c of that patch. Second, it replaces DemoFusion's independent dilated-sample denoising with a position-wise bijective permutation within each window, enabling noise interaction between dilation samples before and after each denoising step, which yields smoother global semantic information and further suppresses repetition.

## Results

At the highest tested resolution of 4096×4096 (16×), AccDiffusion achieves FIDr of 63.89, FIDc of 58.51, and CLIP Score of 33.79, outperforming the closest baseline DemoFusion (FIDr 65.97, FIDc 59.94, CLIP 33.21) and dramatically surpassing direct-generation methods like SDXL-DI (FIDr 202.93) and Attn-SF (FIDr 203.08). At 2048×2048 (4×), AccDiffusion records FIDr 59.63 and CLIP 32.79, maintaining a consistent lead over all baselines. Inference time scales from 3 min at 4× to 26 min at 16×, comparable to DemoFusion's 25 min at 16×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AccDiffusion outperforms all five baselines on every metric (FIDr, ISr, FIDc, ISc, CLIP) at all three extrapolated resolutions. The closest competitor is DemoFusion, which AccDiffusion edges out by small margins (e.g., FIDr 63.89 vs. 65.97 at 16×; CLIP 33.79 vs. 33.21), attributable to patch-content-aware prompts and window-interaction dilated sampling. MultiDiffusion, while avoiding seams, suffers severe object repetition and trails AccDiffusion by ~67 FIDr points at 16× (131.39 vs. 63.89). ScaleCrafter avoids large-object repetition but introduces structural distortions, yielding FIDr 139.18 at 16×. Direct-generation methods SDXL-DI and Attn-SF perform nearly identically to each other (FIDr ≈ 203 at 16×) and are the weakest baselines, as they cannot alleviate repetition and incur prohibitive GPU memory at high resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

AccDiffusion extends the DemoFusion pipeline (progressive upscaling, residual connection, dilated sampling) and the MultiDiffusion patch-wise denoising framework, both of which use a single identical text prompt across all patches. The key conceptual departure is decoupling that global prompt into patch-content-aware substrings derived from cross-attention maps of the low-resolution generation, so each patch only receives word tokens relevant to its content. Additionally, the paper replaces DemoFusion's independent dilated-sample denoising with a position-wise bijective window interaction to produce smoother global semantic information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying small-object repetition as an adversarial tension between identical prompts (encouraging repetition) and residual/dilated sampling (suppressing it), the paper offers a clean diagnostic that guides the fix. The training-free, plug-and-play nature makes it immediately applicable to any SDXL-based pipeline without retraining. However, it inherits DemoFusion's inference latency from progressive upscaling and overlapped patches, and its reliance on LDMs' prior knowledge of cropped images can produce locally irrational content in close-up generation. Open questions include whether non-overlapped patch-wise denoising can achieve comparable quality with lower compute, and whether the cross-attention-based prompt decoupling generalizes to video or 3D generation.
