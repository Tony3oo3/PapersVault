## Problem

Existing LiDAR segmentation methods rely heavily on raw 3D coordinates and intensity, resulting in poor isometric invariance under rotation, translation, occlusion, and range-dependent sparsity. While Pointwise Distance Distribution (PDD) features offer isometry invariance, they have never been adapted to outdoor LiDAR point clouds, where their high dimensionality and sensitivity to distant points make direct application impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240174/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single LiDAR scan (LiDAR-only, no RGB or range images) with per-point 3D coordinates, intensity, and reflectivity. RAPiD features are computed using range-specific neighbor counts (k_close, k_mid, k_far) within ring- or class-bounded Regions of Interest, producing a 4D distance matrix per point that fuses Euclidean geometry with normalized reflectivity. The network outputs per-point semantic class labels (19 classes on SemanticKITTI, 16 on nuScenes) at an inference time of ~105 ms/frame.

## Method

RAPiD-Seg introduces three coupled components: (1) RAPiD features, a range-aware adaptation of PDD that computes sorted 4D distances (3D position + reflectivity) within LiDAR-native RoIs (intra-ring R-RAPiD or intra-class C-RAPiD) using range-dependent k values to handle sparsity; (2) a class-aware double-nested autoencoder (RAPiD AE) that compresses high-dimensional pointwise RAPiD into compact voxel-wise embeddings via an outer Voxel Set Attention encoder and an inner convolutional AE, trained with a novel class-aware contrastive loss alongside MSE reconstruction; (3) a channel-wise attention fusion module (FuAtten) that adaptively weights coordinate, intensity, and RAPiD voxel embeddings before feeding a Minkowski-UNet34 backbone. Training follows a two-stage strategy: R-RAPiD-Seg is trained first, then C-RAPiD-Seg leverages pseudo-labels from the first stage to compute class-bounded RAPiD features, yielding a further +0.7 mIoU gain.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI test set, RAPiD-Seg achieves an mIoU of 76.1%, surpassing the best single-modal baseline RangeFormer (73.3%) by 2.8 points and the best multi-modal method UniSeg (75.2%) by 0.9 points. On the nuScenes test set, RAPiD-Seg reaches 83.6% mIoU, edging out UniSeg (83.5%) and outperforming the next-best single-modal method 2DPASS (80.8%) by 2.8 points. Notably, RAPiD-Seg is a single-modal (LiDAR-only) method that outperforms all multi-modal approaches on both benchmarks, with inference time of 105 ms per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPiD-Seg outperforms all 15 compared baselines on both SemanticKITTI and nuScenes. On SemanticKITTI, it leads the best single-modal method RangeFormer [22] by 2.8 mIoU (76.1 vs 73.3) and the best multi-modal method UniSeg [37] by 0.9 mIoU (76.1 vs 75.2); gains are especially pronounced on rigid-object classes (truck: 72.5 vs 63.6 for UniSeg; o.veh: 80.7 vs 74.1). On nuScenes, RAPiD-Seg edges out UniSeg [37] by 0.1 mIoU (83.6 vs 83.5) and surpasses the strongest single-modal 2DPASS [58] by 2.8 mIoU (83.6 vs 80.8), with notable advantages on truck (88.5 vs 75.5) and car (95.0 vs 92.0). Earlier single-modal baselines such as Cylinder3D [64] (68.9/77.2), GASN [61] (70.7/80.4), and PVKD [17] (71.2/—) trail by 5–7 mIoU on SemanticKITTI. The paper highlights that its single-modal LiDAR-only design surpasses multi-modal approaches (UniSeg, LidarMultiNet [60], 2D3DNet [12], AMVNet [34], PMF [65]) that additionally exploit RGB or range imagery, suggesting the RAPiD features capture complementary geometric and material information not provided by those extra modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Pointwise Distance Distribution (PDD) invariant, originally developed for periodic crystal structures by Widdowson & Kurlin [52], to the LiDAR segmentation domain by introducing range-aware Regions of Interest (intra-ring and intra-class) and a 4D distance metric incorporating reflectivity. It departs from the dominant paradigm of coordinate-and-intensity-based LiDAR segmentation (SPVCNN, Cylinder3D, LiM3D) by replacing raw spatial features with isometry-invariant local distance distributions, addressing the poor translational and rotational invariance of prior methods. The embedding pipeline builds on Voxel Set Attention [15] and SE-Net-style channel-wise attention [18], while the backbone leverages Minkowski-UNet [8], positioning the contribution as a feature-engineering and embedding innovation atop an established sparse-convolutional architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a single-modal LiDAR method can match or slightly exceed multi-modal approaches (e.g., UniSeg) suggests that well-designed invariant geometric features may reduce reliance on additional sensor modalities, which is practically significant for cost-constrained autonomous driving deployments. The range-aware k-parameter design (different neighbor counts for near/mid/far ranges) offers a principled way to handle LiDAR sparsity that could transfer to other sparse-sensor tasks. Open questions include whether the two-stage training (pre-train AE, then fine-tune full network) is strictly necessary, how the method scales to very dense or very sparse point clouds beyond current LiDAR configurations, and whether the pseudo-label generation for C-RAPiD at test time introduces systematic biases in long-tail classes.
