## Problem

Existing distillation-based open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods simply imitate 2D teacher models (e.g., LSeg, OpenSeg), inheriting their noise from occlusion, lighting variations, and viewing angles, which caps the 3D student's representational capacity at the 2D teacher's level. These approaches fail to exploit the geometric priors inherent in 3D point clouds or the representational advantages of 3D data (robustness to viewpoint and illumination changes) that could allow the student to surpass the teacher.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes only a sparse 3D point cloud (voxelized at 2 cm for indoor scenes, 5 cm for outdoor) and a set of text prompts (e.g., 'a chair in a scene') as input, producing per-point semantic labels via cosine similarity between 3D point features and CLIP text embeddings. During training, multi-view RGB images are additionally consumed to extract 2D pixel features via a pre-trained open-vocabulary segmentor (LSeg for indoor, OpenSeg for outdoor), which are back-projected onto the point cloud to form pixel-point feature pairs for distillation. Notably, the method avoids the 2D-3D ensemble strategy used by OpenScene, relying purely on 3D point clouds at test time.

## Method

GGSD introduces a two-stage training procedure on a MinkowskiNet18A backbone. Stage 1 (Geometry Guided Distillation) augments standard pixel-point cosine distillation with a superpoint-level consistency loss: VCCS superpoints partition the point cloud into geometrically homogeneous neighborhoods, and mean 2D/3D features within each superpoint are aligned, enforcing semantic coherence that filters out 2D noise. Stage 2 (Geometry Guided Self-Distillation) exploits the 3D model's superior representation by generating pseudo-labels from an EMA model's predictions on 3D points, then applying a majority-voting mechanism within each superpoint to correct noisy assignments before computing a contrastive point-text loss. The key insight is that 3D geometric structure provides a natural denoising prior, and the 3D model's own predictions—refined by superpoint voting—yield more reliable supervision than direct 2D projection, enabling the student to significantly outperform the 2D teacher.

## Results

On ScanNet, GGSD achieves 56.5% mIoU and 68.6% mAcc, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art OpenScene (2D-3D ensemble) by 2.3% mIoU and 2.0% mAcc, and outperforming the pure-3D OpenScene baseline by 3.6% mIoU. On nuScenes, GGSD reaches 46.1% mIoU and 59.2% mAcc, improving over OpenScene (ensemble) by 4.0% mIoU. In open-vocabulary scenarios with expanded NYU-40 labels, GGSD gains 2.4% mIoU on Head categories and 2.3% on Common categories over OpenScene. In cross-domain transfer (trained on ScanNet, tested on Matterport3D), GGSD achieves 40.1% mIoU on Matterport21, outperforming OpenScene by 4.1% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGSD outperforms all open-vocabulary baselines by a large margin on both indoor and outdoor benchmarks. On ScanNet, GGSD (56.5% mIoU) beats the closest competitor OpenScene with 2D-3D ensemble (54.2%) by 2.3% mIoU, and the pure-3D OpenScene (52.9%) by 3.6% mIoU; it surpasses CLIP-FO3D (30.2%), CNS (26.8%), CLIP2Scene (25.1%), and PLA (17.7%) by 26.3%, 29.7%, 31.4%, and 38.8% mIoU respectively. On nuScenes, GGSD (46.1% mIoU) leads OpenScene ensemble (42.1%) by 4.0% and OpenScene pure-3D (42.9%) by 3.2%, while CNS (33.5%) and CLIP2Scene (20.8%) trail by 12.6% and 25.3%. Notably, GGSD uses only pure 3D point clouds at inference (no 2D-3D ensemble), yet still exceeds OpenScene's ensemble results, demonstrating that the geometry-guided self-distillation effectively compensates for the absence of 2D features at test time. In cross-domain transfer to Matterport3D, GGSD consistently outperforms OpenScene across all vocabulary sizes (21/40/80/160 classes), with the largest gap of 4.1% mIoU on Matterport21.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OpenScene's pixel-point distillation framework, which established the paradigm of transferring open-vocabulary capability from 2D vision-language models (LSeg, OpenSeg) to 3D point cloud networks. The key conceptual departure is the argument that prior methods merely imitate 2D teachers and inherit their noise (occlusion, lighting, viewpoint artifacts), thereby capping the 3D student's representational ceiling. GGSD introduces two departures: (1) geometry-guided distillation using VCCS superpoints to enforce intra-superpoint semantic consistency, and (2) a self-distillation stage where the 3D network learns from its own EMA predictions with superpoint-level voting, drawing on the MoCo-style EMA trick from self-supervised learning. The method also positions itself against optimization-based approaches (LERF, 3DGS-based) by emphasizing generalization to unseen scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 3D student can substantially surpass its 2D teacher (up to +7.8% mIoU on Matterport3D) challenges the common assumption that distillation performance is bounded by teacher quality, suggesting that modality-specific geometric structure is a first-class signal worth exploiting. The superpoint-based voting mechanism is a simple, lightweight noise-mitigation strategy that outperforms applying a powerful 2D foundation model (SAM) for the same purpose, which is practically useful. However, the authors acknowledge that geometric priors provide limited benefit for objects lacking strong geometric structure, and language ambiguity (e.g., sofa vs. chair) remains an unresolved issue. Open questions include whether the self-distillation paradigm extends to other 3D tasks (detection, instance segmentation) and whether the approach scales to very large outdoor scenes where superpoint construction may become less meaningful.
