## Problem

Diffusion models learn denoising tasks across many timesteps, and conflicting tasks cause negative transfer that slows convergence. Prior architectural fixes either hard-partition parameters by timestep clusters (losing detailed inter-task relationships) or apply channel masks (risking semantic information loss). The paper asks how to exploit inter-task relationships between conflicting denoising tasks without sacrificing semantic information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241426/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates on 32×32×4 latent representations (from 256×256×3 RGB images via a Stable Diffusion VAE encoder), patchified with patch size 2. In unconditional mode the input is a noisy latent plus a timestep embedding; in class-conditional mode a class label embedding is added via adaLN-Zero. The output is a predicted noise vector that, after iterative denoising (250 DDPM steps), is decoded by the VAE into a 256×256 image.

## Method

Switch-DiT inserts a sparse mixture-of-experts (SMoE) layer into every DiT transformer block, using a timestep-based gating network (rather than token-based) to select k=2 out of M=3 experts per block, ensuring at least one shared expert across all timesteps. The SMoE output is integrated via a channel-mask + skip-connection design (z·m(z) feeds the block, z·(1−m(z)) is skip-connected) with identity initialization so the layer starts as a no-op. A novel diffusion prior loss regularizes the gating probabilities toward DTR's predefined channel mask using Jensen-Shannon Divergence, with bipartite matching to resolve channel-permutation ambiguity across blocks. This replaces the standard load-balancing loss, which fails to converge the EMA model during diffusion training, and instead provides strong supervision that encodes inter-task relationships (adjacent timesteps share experts at low noise, diverge at high noise) while preserving full semantic information in the shared path.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, Switch-DiT achieves FID-50K of 33.99 (S), 16.21 (B), and 8.76 (XL), consistently outperforming both DiT and DTR at every model size. On FFHQ unconditional generation, Switch-DiT-B reaches FID-10K of 8.53, surpassing the best loss-design baseline (ANT-UW at 9.30) and DTR-B (8.82). Switch-DiT also achieves the highest IS (169.17 at XL) and Precision (0.79 at XL) among all compared methods, and demonstrates the fastest convergence rate across all model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiT (the base architecture), Switch-DiT achieves substantial FID improvements at all sizes: 33.99 vs. 44.28 (S), 16.21 vs. 27.96 (B), and 8.76 vs. 9.40 (XL). Against DTR (the closest architectural competitor using channel masking), Switch-DiT wins at every size (33.99 vs. 37.43, 16.21 vs. 16.58, 8.76 vs. 10.85), and notably DTR-XL actually underperforms DiT-XL (10.85 vs. 9.40) due to semantic information loss from masking 20% of channels, whereas Switch-DiT-XL beats both. On FFHQ, Switch-DiT-B (FID-10K 8.53) outperforms all loss-design baselines—P2 (10.08), Min-SNR (9.73), and ANT-UW (9.30)—as well as DTR-B (8.82), confirming that explicit parameter isolation via SMoE is more effective than reweighting or channel masking. The paper further argues the gains are not merely from extra parameters by showing Switch-DiT-S (6.74 GFLOPs) achieves FID 33.99 versus the 42.79 predicted by the DiT GFLOPs–FID correlation curve.

## Relation to Prior Work

Switch-DiT builds directly on the DiT architecture and the MTL reinterpretation of diffusion training, where each timestep's denoising is a separate task. It departs from DTR's channel-masking strategy—which routes tasks through fixed channel subsets—by replacing it with a sparse mixture-of-experts layer gated on timestep embeddings, thereby achieving parameter isolation without discarding semantic information. The diffusion prior loss, which uses DTR's channel mask as a supervision target via bipartite matching and Jensen-Shannon divergence, is a novel auxiliary objective that replaces the standard load-balancing loss (shown to be incompatible with EMA convergence in diffusion training). Conceptually, the paper unifies the 'shared expert' and 'task-specific expert' paradigms from multi-expert diffusion models (e-DiffI, ERNIE-ViLG 2.0, MEME) into a single unified network with soft routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that parameter isolation via SMoE consistently outperforms channel masking across model scales, and notably reveals that DTR-XL underperforms DiT-XL—suggesting that the semantic information lost to channel masking becomes a dominant cost at scale, a finding that challenges the assumption that task routing always helps. The diffusion prior loss and its bipartite-matching alignment mechanism offer a reusable recipe for incorporating structured priors into MoE gating in diffusion models. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond M=3/k=2, whether the DTR-derived prior is truly necessary or the model could learn its own routing, and whether the technique transfers to video or 3D generation where the timestep-task structure may differ.
