## Problem

Gaze following models require large quantities of human-annotated gaze target coordinates, a process that is both laborious and inherently ambiguous due to occlusions, cluttered scenes, and multiple plausible targets. No semi-supervised method existed for gaze following, and standard semi-supervised techniques from recognition or segmentation are not directly applicable because gaze following predicts a spatial heatmap from an intact scene image rather than a class distribution. The paper asks how to generate high-quality pseudo-annotations from unlabeled data to reduce annotation effort by 50% while matching or exceeding fully supervised performance.

## Input / Output

The gaze following model takes an input triplet: a scene image (3×H×W), a cropped head image (3×H×W), and a binary head location mask (H×W), and outputs a gaze target heatmap of size 64×64. The pseudo-annotation pipeline additionally consumes a text prompt ('What is the person in the green bounding box looking at?') fed to the OFA VQA model, whose decoder cross-attention weights are converted into Grad-CAM heatmaps. The diffusion refinement step takes these noisy Grad-CAM heatmaps, injects Gaussian noise at a chosen timestep, and produces a refined 64×64 heatmap that serves as the pseudo-label for training.

## Method

The method introduces two complementary priors for pseudo-annotation generation. First, a pretrained OFA vision-language model is prompted with a gaze-following question, and Grad-CAM heatmaps are extracted from the decoder cross-attention weights to localize the predicted gaze target, providing a strong but noisy spatial prior. Second, a DDPM diffusion model with a U-Net denoiser is trained on the small labeled set to learn the annotation distribution p(h|X), conditioned on gaze features extracted via the VAT dual-pathway architecture. During refinement, the Grad-CAM heatmap is perturbed with Gaussian noise at an intermediate timestep (t=250 out of 500), then the reverse diffusion process (DDIM) generates a clean heatmap that retains VQA spatial information while conforming to the geometry and context of human-labeled annotations; the noise magnitude acts as a tunable trade-off between the two priors, allowing the model to ignore unreliable Grad-CAM signals. The final gaze following model (VAT) is trained with MSE loss on both ground-truth and refined pseudo-annotations, and the diffusion model itself can be further improved via Mean Teacher consistency regularization on unlabeled data.

## Results

On GazeFollow with 10% labels, GCDR-MT achieves an average distance of 0.172 and AUC of 0.892, outperforming the supervised VAT baseline (0.202 / 0.869) and the best semi-supervised baseline VAT-MT (0.189 / 0.882). At 5% labels, GCDR-MT (0.194 / 0.870) surpasses the fully supervised VAT trained with 10% labels (0.202 / 0.869), effectively halving the annotation requirement. On the VideoAttentionTarget dataset, GCDR achieves the best average distance of 0.142 and AUC of 0.843 across 10 videos using only ~100 annotated frames per video, compared to 0.166 / 0.773 for the VAT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: supervised VAT (trained only on labeled data), Semi-VAT (self-training with VAT-generated pseudo-labels), Semi-VAT-GC (self-training with a VAT variant that takes Grad-CAM as input), and VAT-MT (Mean Teacher consistency regularization on VAT). GCDR-MT consistently outperforms all four baselines across every labeled-data ratio (5%, 10%, 20%) on both distance and AUC metrics. The most striking result is that GCDR-MT with only 5% labels (Avg. Dist. 0.194) beats supervised VAT trained with 10% labels (0.202), and with 10% labels (0.172) beats supervised VAT with 20% labels (0.182), demonstrating a 50% annotation reduction. The improvement over the strongest baseline VAT-MT is 0.017 in Avg. Dist. at 5% and 0.017 at 10%, with AUC gains of 0.020 and 0.010 respectively. On the video adaptation task, GCDR leads all baselines by 2–5% in both metrics, with the largest gaps appearing on videos where baselines struggle most.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to apply semi-supervised learning to gaze following, a task previously addressed exclusively with fully supervised methods (VAT, GazeFollow, and subsequent depth/pose-augmented variants). It repurposes two established ideas in novel combination: (1) Grad-CAM heatmaps from a pretrained VQA model (OFA) as a source of pseudo-annotations, extending the 'prompting' paradigm from classification to spatial prediction, and (2) diffusion models as annotation priors, adapting the inverse-problem/editing formulation (SD-Edit, adversarial purification) to refine noisy pseudo-labels rather than images. The semi-supervised training framework borrows from Mean Teacher consistency regularization, but the core contribution is the two-stage VQA-to-diffusion pseudo-annotation pipeline rather than the training procedure itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment scenarios where annotating gaze targets is expensive and ambiguous (psychology labs, retail analytics): reducing annotation needs by 50% while matching or exceeding fully supervised performance is a concrete efficiency gain. The conceptual contribution—using a diffusion model trained on the target annotation distribution as a 'refinement prior' that can selectively accept or reject noisy external signals—generalizes beyond gaze following to any task where large-model outputs are spatially informative but noisy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D gaze following, whether the VQA prior quality (currently OFA) is a bottleneck, and whether the diffusion refinement step is truly necessary given that a simpler direct-mapping U-Net achieves close performance in ablations.
