## Problem

Existing brain-to-video reconstruction methods are subject-specific, training one model per participant and evaluating on a single dataset, which limits generalization and scalability. Cross-subject reconstruction and the impact of multi-dataset training on video reconstruction fidelity have not been systematically studied, leaving open whether a subject-agnostic reconstruction technology is feasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240697/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes fMRI beta values (estimated via GLM) from 41 regions of interest on the cortical surface in Cifti fsLR32k space, corresponding to a subject's response to a 2- or 3-second video clip. It produces a reconstructed video at 224×224 resolution and 15 FPS by regressing a latent vector (shape 4×15×33×33) and a BLIP text embedding (shape 226×768) from the fMRI signal, which condition a frozen Zeroscope V2 video generation model.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) a Masked Brain Modeling encoder-decoder is pre-trained on HCP resting-state and HAD task data to learn fMRI spatial structure, then fine-tuned with a CLIP contrastive alignment objective against video caption embeddings; (2) two MLP regressors map the aligned fMRI embedding to a video latent vector z and a BLIP embedding b, where regressing BLIP embeddings (rather than raw CLIP-text conditioning vectors) is shown to improve generation quality by letting BLIP's decoder produce clean text tokens; (3) z is re-noised (strength 0.8) and denoised over 40 steps by a frozen Zeroscope V2 U-Net conditioned on the BLIP-decoded caption. The key architectural insight is that multi-subject and multi-dataset training at both the alignment and regression stages substantially improves reconstruction quality and enables zero-shot generalization to unseen subjects.

## Results

On CC2017, Brainflix (multi-subject) achieves SSIM 0.195, 2-way top-1 0.888, and 50-way top-1 0.221, outperforming Mind-Video (0.186 / 0.853 / 0.202) and Kupershmidt (0.128 SSIM). On BMD, the multi-subject model reaches SSIM 0.190, 2-way 0.816, and 50-way 0.165, substantially exceeding Mind-Video (0.176 / 0.711 / 0.101) and Kupershmidt (0.031 / 0.514 / 0.004). In crowd-sourced human evaluation (6-way retrieval), Brainflix scores 86.1% on BMD and 84.7% on CC2017, surpassing Mind-Video (80.3% / 83.9%) and approaching the oracle upper bound of 94.1% / 92.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Brainflix (multi-subject) outperforms both baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. Against Mind-Video (Chen et al., 2023), the closest prior method, Brainflix gains +0.009 SSIM and +0.035 on 2-way classification on CC2017, and +0.014 SSIM, +0.105 on 2-way, and +0.064 on 50-way on BMD. Against Kupershmidt et al. (2022), the gap is far larger: on BMD, Brainflix multi-subject achieves 0.190 SSIM versus 0.031, and 0.816 2-way versus 0.514. In the human evaluation (6-way retrieval), Brainflix scores 86.1% on BMD and 84.7% on CC2017, beating Mind-Video by 5.8 and 0.8 percentage points respectively, while remaining below the oracle ceiling (94.1% / 92.8%) set by feeding ground-truth conditioning vectors to the same Zeroscope V2 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the brain-to-video reconstruction line initiated by Nishimoto et al. (2011) and most directly by Chen et al.'s 'Cinematic Mindscapes' (2023), which first combined Masked Brain Modeling with a diffusion model for video decoding. The key departures are (1) a three-stage pipeline that inserts a CLIP-based contrastive alignment step between the MBM encoder and the regression stage, (2) multi-subject and multi-dataset training across four fMRI corpora (HCP, BMD, HAD, CC2017) rather than the single-subject paradigm of prior work, and (3) a re-noising/denoising video-to-video procedure borrowed from Takagi & Nishimoto (2023) applied to a text-to-video backbone (Zeroscope V2). The use of BLIP embeddings as an intermediate regression target—rather than regressing the raw CLIP-text conditioning vector directly—is a practical design choice not explored in prior brain-decoding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-subject training paradigm and the preliminary zero-shot results (where adding more subjects' data improves held-out subject performance without ever seeing that subject's training data) point toward a subject-agnostic brain-to-video decoder, which would be a major practical advance for neurotechnology applications such as sensory restoration or brain-computer interfaces. However, the temporal resolution of fMRI (~2 s hemodynamic lag), the vulnerability to subject inattention, and the reliance on a frozen 3-second video generator all cap the fidelity and duration of reconstructions. Open questions include whether the zero-shot trend holds at larger scale, whether the between-subject fMRI correlations observed in visual cortex generalize to other cortical regions, and whether longer or higher-frame-rate video generators can close the gap to oracle performance (94% semantic fidelity vs. 86% achieved).
