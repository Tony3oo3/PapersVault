## Problem

Strand-based hair reconstruction from multi-view images is heavily underconstrained because a substantial portion of hair geometry is occluded, and classical orientation maps extracted via Gabor filters are inherently noisy. Existing strand-based methods lack a differentiable rendering pathway to propagate photometric supervision into strand geometry, while unstructured 3D Gaussian Splatting approaches produce representations incompatible with physics-based hair simulation and editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240427/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (or monocular video) captured under unconstrained lighting as input, with initial camera estimates from COLMAP and hair segmentation masks as preprocessing. It produces a hair map parameterized on the scalp region of a fitted FLAME head model, where each texel stores a 3D polyline strand, plus a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians attached to each line segment for rendering. The resulting strand-aligned Gaussians and explicit polylines are directly importable into engines like Unreal Engine for simulation and editing without further conversion.

## Method

Gaussian Haircut operates in two stages. First, a modified 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline with BARF camera refinement lifts 2D Gabor-filter orientation maps into a 3D orientation field encoded in the covariance matrices of unstructured Gaussians, producing denoised multi-view orientation renders. Second, a coarse-to-fine strand fitting stage optimizes a latent hair map (coarse) and then an explicit hair map (fine), using a dual representation where each strand line segment carries an anisotropic Gaussian whose scale is proportional to segment length and whose rotation aligns with the strand direction. Differentiable rasterization of these strand-aligned Gaussians propagates photometric, segmentation, and orientation losses directly into strand coordinates, while a pre-trained diffusion-based hairstyle prior regularizes the internal geometry via score distillation sampling. A key practical detail is that during coarse fitting, only 1,000 guiding strands are decoded per batch and interpolated to 10,000 for rendering, since full hairstyle decoding is too memory-intensive.

## Results

The paper is primarily evaluated qualitatively, with the main quantitative metric being the average angular error of re-projected hair orientation maps: Gaussian Haircut achieves 7° versus 8° for Gabor filters (the standard 2D orientation map baseline), averaged over 70 test views on two synthetic hairstyles. In terms of reconstruction speed, the method achieves more than a 10× speedup over Neural Haircut while producing higher-quality strand geometry. Full reconstruction of a hairstyle takes up to 6 hours on a single RTX 4090 GPU (30,000 steps for 3D lifting + 20,000 coarse + 10,000 fine optimization steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Neural Haircut (Sklyarova et al., ICCV 2023), which reconstructs strand-based hair geometry from the same multi-view/monocular video input. Gaussian Haircut outperforms it on both quality and speed: the qualitative comparison in Fig. 3 shows visibly more accurate strand geometry (particularly in dense, poorly-illuminated regions), and the optimization is more than 10× faster. The key architectural difference is that Neural Haircut relies on a layered neural SDF for coarse geometry plus Chamfer distance for strand fitting, whereas Gaussian Haircut uses differentiable rasterization of strand-aligned Gaussians for photometric supervision. For the orientation map lifting sub-task, the paper compares against Gabor filter banks (Paris et al., SIGGRAPH 2004), the classical approach for extracting 2D hair directions. The 3D line lifting via unstructured Gaussians reduces the average angular error from 8° (Gabor) to 7°, and the denoised orientation maps lead to measurably better strand geometry in real-world examples, especially in regions with poor illumination and highly entangled strands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting in a fundamentally different way from its original radiance-field formulation: the covariance matrices of unstructured Gaussians are used to encode a 3D strand-orientation field, effectively turning 3DGS into a differentiable 3D line-lifting and denoising tool for Gabor-filter orientation maps. For the strand-fitting stage, it builds directly on the latent hair map, strand decoder/encoder, and diffusion-based prior from Neural Haircut (Sklyarova et al.) and Neural Strands (Rosu et al.), but introduces a dual representation—classical 3D polylines with anisotropic Gaussians attached to each segment—that enables differentiable rasterization of strands and photometric supervision, something the prior strand-based methods could not achieve. Camera refinement via BARF-style learnable residuals addresses a practical gap in SfM accuracy for hair-centric scenes, and the coarse-to-fine latent-to-explicit optimization strategy is a new combination of these components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the output is a strand-based hairstyle directly importable into production graphics engines (e.g., Unreal Engine) for physics simulation and editing, bridging the gap between neural reconstruction and classical CG pipelines that prior 3DGS-based avatar methods left open. The 10× speedup over Neural Haircut with improved quality makes strand-based hair reconstruction more accessible for telepresence, VFX, and gaming workflows. However, the method inherits limitations from the strand prior: it struggles with curly hairstyles and complex internal structures like braids, and the six-hour reconstruction time on a single GPU, while faster than the baseline, is still far from real-time. Open questions include whether the dual representation can be extended to other thin-structure reconstructions (fur, fibers) and whether the diffusion prior can be made more general to handle diverse hair types without per-style fine-tuning.
