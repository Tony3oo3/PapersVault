## Problem

In Single Positive Multi-Label Learning, the Assumed Negative (AN) assumption inevitably treats truly positive unannotated labels as negatives, degrading model performance. Previous false-negative identification methods rely on model outputs (confidence or loss) of assumed negative labels, but these signals become unreliable as the model learns from noisy labels, making it increasingly difficult to distinguish false negatives from true negatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240915/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 448×448 RGB image annotated with exactly one positive label (all other labels unannotated) and produces a multi-label prediction vector over C classes. During training, it additionally constructs a masked image by preserving only the CAM-derived discriminative region of the single positive label, and applies strong augmentation (RandAugmentation) to generate a second view for consistency regularization. The model is trained with a warm-up phase using plain AN loss before the rejection mechanism activates.

## Method

IGNORE identifies false negatives via an information gap: it extracts the CAM for the single positive label, generates a masked image retaining only that object's discriminative region, and computes the per-class logit gap between the original and masked image. Two adaptively updated (EMA) thresholds gate the rejection: a logit gap threshold (average gap of single positives) and a per-class logit average threshold (average logit of assumed negatives for that class). A label is rejected from the AN loss only if it exceeds both thresholds, preventing the model from receiving incorrect negative supervision on truly positive labels. The full training objective combines the rejection-modified AN loss (IGNORE), a single-positive binary classification loss to maintain reliable CAM extraction, and a consistency regularization term aligning logits between original and strongly augmented views.

## Results

Using the Q2L transformer model, IGNORE achieves the best mAP on three of four datasets: 90.1 on PASCAL VOC 2012, 76.3 on MS COCO, and 52.8 on NUSWIDE, while attaining 23.4 on CUB (second-best, trailing EM+APL by 0.2). With ResNet-50, the method achieves the highest mAP on NUSWIDE (50.08) and CUB (21.90), and is competitive on VOC (89.19) and COCO (72.00), where it trails the strongest baseline (+BoostLU) by 0.1 and 0.89 respectively. The largest margin over the previous best is +1.7 mAP on NUSWIDE (Q2L) and +0.49 mAP on NUSWIDE (ResNet-50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Cole et al. [5] variants (WAN, EPR, AN-LS, ROLE), IGNORE outperforms all of them on every dataset with Q2L, with the largest gaps on NUSWIDE (+4.3 mAP over EPR) and CUB (+0.3 over EPR). Compared to Zhou et al.'s EM and EM+APL [33], IGNORE wins on VOC (+0.9), COCO (+3.2), and NUSWIDE (+4.2) with Q2L, but loses by 0.2 on CUB (23.4 vs 23.6). Against Xie et al.'s PLC+LAC [29], IGNORE leads on all four datasets with Q2L (e.g., +0.5 on VOC, +0.7 on COCO, +1.7 on NUSWIDE, +0.1 on CUB). With ResNet-50, IGNORE surpasses LL-R [12] on all datasets and outperforms the combined LL-R+BoostLU [12,13] on NUSWIDE (+0.49) and CUB (+2.10), while trailing it slightly on VOC (−0.10) and COCO (−0.89). Label smoothing [19] and asymmetric loss [21] are consistently outperformed across all datasets with both backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the well-established SPML framework where the Assumed Negative (AN) loss provides supervision to unannotated labels, but departs from prior false-negative identification strategies that rely on model confidence or loss values of assumed negatives (PLC, SCL, LL-R, BoostLU). The key conceptual departure is using the single positive label's discriminative area (via CAM) to construct a masked image, then exploiting the logit gap between masked and original images as a noise-robust criterion for false negative detection. This repurposes the most reliable signal in SPML (the single positive label) rather than the least reliable (assumed negative outputs corrupted by noisy training), and combines it with a per-class logit average threshold to avoid rejecting true negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and fundamental problem in SPML—false negative labels from the AN assumption that systematically bias model training—by proposing a criterion that is structurally independent of the noisy assumed-negative outputs. The ablation revealing that consistency regularization alone degrades performance (especially on CUB, dropping from 19.7 to 11.1 mAP) highlights that the components are mutually dependent, suggesting the information-gap signal and regularization serve complementary roles. Limitations include reliance on CAM quality for masked image generation, modest gains on CUB where the method does not achieve best performance, and evaluation limited to four datasets. The authors note future directions involving more precise CAM extraction or foundation models for masked image generation, which could broaden applicability.
