## Problem

Existing video object segmentation methods rely on explicit queries—pre-defined categories, pixel masks, or short descriptive phrases—and cannot interpret implicit, complex text instructions that require world knowledge reasoning and long-term video understanding. This limits their utility in embodied AI scenarios where users naturally issue high-level, inferential commands such as 'the car powered by electricity' or 'Which person will take the baton?'
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240383/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video of T frames (each H×W×3) and a complex text instruction that may involve world knowledge, temporal inference, or prediction of upcoming events. It outputs a full binary mask sequence of T frames (T×H×W) tracking the described object throughout the video. Notably, the text queries are not limited to explicit appearance/action/localization phrases but include implicit descriptions requiring multi-step reasoning, and the benchmark also includes negative samples (nonexistent objects) to evaluate hallucination resistance.

## Method

VISA decouples long-video processing from high-resolution segmentation via a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Text-guided Frame Sampler (TFS) built on LLaMA-VID (2 tokens/frame) selects the most relevant target frame and a small set of reference frames based on the text query, reducing the token burden; (2) the selected frames are encoded at full spatial resolution (112–256 tokens/frame via ViT + Spatial Merging) and fed to a multi-modal LLM (Chat-UniVi or LLaVA) that generates a text response containing a special <SEG> token, whose last-layer embedding is projected by an MLP into a SAM prompt to decode the target-frame mask; (3) an XMem tracker propagates that single mask bidirectionally across all T frames. Training is end-to-end with a weighted sum of autoregressive cross-entropy loss on the text output and a BCE+DICE mask loss on the target frame, combined with instruction tuning on the newly collected ReVOS dataset (35,074 instruction-mask pairs from 1,042 videos) alongside standard Referring VOS, video VQA, and image segmentation data.

## Results

On the newly introduced ReVOS benchmark, VISA(IT) with Chat-UniVi-13B achieves 50.9 J&F overall (57.4 referring, 44.3 reasoning), outperforming the strongest prior method TrackGPT(IT)-13B (45.0 J&F) by 5.9 points and LISA-13B (41.6 J&F) by 9.3 points. On reasoning-specific queries, VISA(IT)-13B reaches 44.3 J&F versus 40.5 for TrackGPT and 36.7 for LISA. VISA also achieves state-of-the-art on vanilla Referring VOS (e.g., 70.4 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17) and competitive image segmentation (57.8 cIoU on ReasonSeg), while attaining the highest robustness score (R=15.5 for the 7B variant) on hallucination evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISA(IT)-13B outperforms all 16 baselines on the ReVOS benchmark. Against the closest competitor TrackGPT(IT)-13B, VISA gains +5.9 J&F overall and +3.8 J&F on reasoning queries, attributed to VISA's multi-frame temporal processing versus TrackGPT's single-frame-at-a-time approach. Compared to LISA-13B (image-only reasoning segmentation), VISA achieves +9.3 J&F overall, demonstrating the benefit of video-level context. Traditional Referring VOS methods (LMPM, ReferFormer, MTTR) score 20–25 J&F lower on reasoning queries, confirming they cannot handle implicit world-knowledge instructions. On vanilla Referring VOS (Table 2), VISA also sets new SOTA: 44.5 J&F on MeViS (+3.3 over TrackGPT), 63.0 on Ref-YT-VOS (+3.5), and 70.4 on Ref-DAVIS17 (+3.9). On image segmentation (Table 3), VISA matches LISA on refCOCO variants and surpasses it by 3.8 cIoU on ReasonSeg (57.8 vs 54.0), while traditional methods (CRIS, LAVT, ReLA) trail by over 20 points on ReasonSeg.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISA extends LISA's image-domain reasoning segmentation paradigm (LLM + <SEG> token + SAM decoder) to the video domain, addressing the computational intractability of processing all frames with full spatial detail. It departs from TrackGPT, which processes single frames without temporal correspondence, by introducing a Text-guided Frame Sampler (built on LLaMA-VID) to select relevant frames and a multi-frame LLM input for long-term video understanding. The task itself generalizes Referring VOS from explicit short phrases to complex, reasoning-requiring text queries, bridging the gap between structured video segmentation and open-ended language understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ReVOS benchmark (35,074 instruction-mask pairs from 1,042 videos) provides the first systematic evaluation of reasoning-based video segmentation, filling a gap between explicit referring VOS and the complex queries needed for embodied AI. Key limitations remain: small objects are poorly captured due to limited visual tokens per frame, and objects appearing in only a few frames are difficult to locate via the TFS mechanism. Open questions include how to gather extremely long temporal dependencies while preserving spatial detail, and whether the TFS approach generalizes to queries requiring understanding of the entire video rather than a few key moments.
