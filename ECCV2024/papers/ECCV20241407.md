## Problem

In MIL-based WSI classification, attention mechanisms over-concentrate on a small subset of discriminative instances, which the paper demonstrates is tightly correlated with overfitting (lower attention entropy coincides with higher validation loss). While prior work has tackled overfitting via better representations, spatial modeling, or data augmentation, the direct role of attention value concentration as a driver of poor generalization remains under-explored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted patch-level features (from ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 or DINO-pretrained ViT-S/16) for each WSI, treating the N patches as a bag. It outputs a bag-level class label (binary for CAMELYON16, 3-class for BRACS, 4-class for LBC) and an attention heatmap of dimension N that assigns a weight to every patch. The heatmap is used both for the weighted aggregation that produces the bag embedding and for downstream localization evaluation (FROC, visual inspection).

## Method

ACMIL extends ABMIL with two components targeting different facets of attention concentration. Multiple Branch Attention (MBA) runs M parallel gated-attention branches, each encouraged to capture a distinct pattern among discriminative instances via a per-branch cross-entropy (semantic) loss and a pairwise cosine-similarity (diversity) loss on the branch heatmaps; the final bag embedding is the mean of the M branch embeddings. Stochastic Top-K Instance Masking (STKIM), applied only during training, sorts attention values and randomly zeros out the Top-K entries with probability p, then renormalizes the remaining values so that suppressed salient instances redistribute their attention to the rest of the bag. The total training loss is the sum of the bag-level prediction loss, the M-branch semantic loss, and the diversity loss, while STKIM is removed at inference.

## Results

ACMIL achieves the best performance in 10 out of 12 evaluation metrics (F1-score and AUC across three datasets with two backbones). With ViT-S/16 SSL pre-trained features, ACMIL reaches 0.954 F1 / 0.974 AUC on CAMELYON16, 0.722 F1 / 0.888 AUC on BRACS, and 0.662 F1 / 0.901 AUC on LBC. With ResNet-18 ImageNet pre-trained features, ACMIL scores 0.798 F1 / 0.841 AUC on CAMELYON16, 0.552 F1 / 0.754 AUC on BRACS, and 0.546 F1 / 0.821 AUC on LBC. The FROC score for tumor localization also improves from 0.3987 (ABMIL) to 0.4233 (ACMIL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACMIL outperforms all seven baselines (ABMIL, DSMIL, TransMIL, Clam-SB, DTFD-MIL, MHIM-MIL, IBMIL) on 10 of 12 metrics, securing second place only on CAMELYON16 AUC with ViT-S/16 (0.974 vs. DTFD-MIL's 0.980) and BRACS AUC with ResNet-18 (0.754 vs. MHIM-MIL's 0.774). The largest margins appear on BRACS with ViT-S/16, where ACMIL surpasses IBMIL by 4.2% F1 (0.722 vs. 0.645) and 1.7% AUC (0.888 vs. 0.871), and on CAMELYON16 with ResNet-18, where it beats DTFD-MIL by 2.1% F1 and 2.6% AUC. On the in-house LBC dataset, ACMIL dominates all baselines across both backbones, with the most notable gap being 2.9% AUC over MHIM-MIL (0.901 vs. 0.872) under ViT-S/16. Against the direct baseline ABMIL, ACMIL improves F1 by 2.1–4.2% and AUC by 0.4–7.0% depending on dataset and backbone, confirming that both MBA and STKIM contribute meaningfully to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACMIL extends the ABMIL framework by introducing two targeted interventions against attention value concentration: Multiple Branch Attention (MBA), which draws conceptual inspiration from multi-head attention but distinguishes itself through an explicit diversity regularization loss and independence from any specific attention formulation, and Stochastic Top-K Instance Masking (STKIM), which adapts the stochastic masking philosophy of dropout and cutout to the attention-value space. The paper positions itself against prior overfitting-mitigation strategies in WSI classification (representation enhancement, spatial correlation modeling, data augmentation) by arguing that attention concentration is an under-explored direct cause, and differentiates from related masking methods (MHIM-MIL, WENO) by requiring no teacher-student framework and masking far fewer instances stochastically rather than deterministically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-motivated diagnosis (attention entropy correlates negatively with validation loss) and two lightweight, plug-and-play remedies that improve SOTA across three datasets and two backbones without architectural overhead or pre-training stages. This is practically valuable for computational pathology groups working with limited WSI data where overfitting is a persistent bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-class or survival-prediction WSI tasks, whether the specific hyperparameters (M=5, K=10, p=0.8) transfer across domains, and whether the attention-concentration-overfitting link holds for non-attention-based MIL aggregators. The private LBC dataset limits full reproducibility of one of the three benchmarks.
