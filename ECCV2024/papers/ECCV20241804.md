## Problem

Diffusion models amplify biases in face generation, producing uneven distributions of sensitive attributes such as age, gender, and race. While GAN-based bias mitigation techniques manipulate latent space semantics, these approaches do not transfer to the stochastic, multi-step Gaussian latent space of diffusion models. No prior work has addressed bias mitigation in unconditional face diffusion models without retraining the generative model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241804/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained unconditional DDPM and extracts latent codes at an intermediate time step of the reverse diffusion process (not the final image), fitting a GMM over these high-dimensional vectors. It produces debiased face images by sampling uniformly from each GMM component and passing them through the reverse diffusion chain, along with pseudo-labels from pre-trained ResNet-101 classifiers. The final output is an augmented training set combining original image-label pairs with generated images and their pseudo-labels, used to retrain downstream attribute classifiers (smile, glasses, hair color, age, gender, race).

## Method

GAMMA-FACE exploits the inherent Gaussian structure of diffusion model latent codes at an intermediate reverse-diffusion step by fitting a Gaussian Mixture Model (K=4, determined via BIC grid search over 0–11 components, diagonal covariance, k-means++ initialization) to disentangle the latent space into attribute-correlated clusters. Instead of sampling from the full latent distribution, the method draws an equal number of samples from each GMM component, ensuring balanced representation of visually distinct attribute groups. Generated images are pseudo-labeled by off-the-shelf ResNet-101 classifiers for both protected and target attributes, and the original training set is augmented with these labeled synthetic samples to retrain the downstream classifier. The entire debiasing pipeline operates exclusively in the sampling/evaluation phase of the diffusion model, requiring no retraining of the generative model and a single generation pass that serves all downstream tasks.

## Results

On the FairFace dataset, GAMMA-FACE achieves the lowest Bias score across all three target attributes (0.088 for gender, 0.102 for race, 0.128 for age) while simultaneously attaining the highest accuracy (86.5%, 80.23%, 81.00% respectively). On FFHQ, the method reduces Bias to 0.0056 for smile classification (a 65% decrease over the baseline) while boosting accuracy from 93.08% to 94.84%, and achieves the best Bias (0.0208) and accuracy (98.70%) for glasses classification. The Bias Performance Coefficient (BPC) reaches 0.673 for smile and 0.454 for gender, confirming simultaneous bias reduction and TPR improvement over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAMMA-FACE outperforms all three baselines on the Bias metric in 5 out of 6 task configurations, with the largest margins on FairFace gender classification (0.088 vs. 0.142–0.187) and FFHQ smile classification (0.0056 vs. 0.0064–0.019). It also achieves the highest accuracy in 5 of 6 settings, most notably 86.5% on FairFace gender (vs. 81.67–84.14%) and 98.70% on FFHQ glasses (vs. 96.10–97.29%). The one exception is FFHQ hair-color classification, where [50] (Zhang et al., CVPR 2024) achieves higher accuracy (88.87% vs. 82.81%) though with a slightly higher Bias (0.153 vs. 0.146). On Bias Amplification, GAMMA-FACE is competitive but not uniformly best: [34] (Ramaswamy et al.) achieves lower BA on FairFace race (1.27 vs. 1.36) and FFHQ smile (1.48 vs. 1.52), while GAMMA-FACE wins on the remaining four configurations. Overall, the method's key advantage is the simultaneous reduction of Bias and increase in accuracy, a trade-off that the GAN-based baselines [34, 8] and the distributional augmentation baseline [50] do not consistently achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of bias mitigation in face generative models from the GAN era (Ramaswamy et al. [34], Dhar et al. [8], Zhang et al. [50]) to the diffusion model setting, where the stochastic latent space precludes direct application of GAN-style semantic latent manipulation. The core conceptual departure is exploiting the inherent Gaussian structure of DDPM intermediate timesteps (Ho et al. [15]) by fitting a GMM to the latent codes and sampling uniformly from components, rather than retraining the generator or applying importance weighting as in prior GAN debiasing work (Grover et al. [13], Choi et al. [7]). The method also builds on the data-augmentation-for-fairness paradigm but operates entirely at the inference/evaluation phase of a pre-trained diffusion model, requiring no retraining of the generative model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first exploration of using the Gaussian structure of diffusion model latent codes for fair generation without retraining, offering a lightweight post-hoc debiasing tool that practitioners can apply to existing unconditional face diffusion models. However, the scope is narrow: only two face datasets, only unconditional generation, and the GMM clustering is unsupervised (the authors cannot verify which component corresponds to which attribute), leaving open whether the disentanglement is truly capturing the intended protected attributes or merely correlating visual features. Key open questions include generalization to conditional diffusion models, non-face domains, and whether the fixed K=4 (from BIC) is robust across architectures and dataset scales.
