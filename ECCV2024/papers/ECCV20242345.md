## Problem

Existing text-to-image (TTI) benchmarks cover at most seven languages and rely on simplistic single-concept prompts, leaving the linguistic diversity of 30+ Indic languages spoken by over 1.4 billion people entirely unmeasured. Because TTI models are trained predominantly on English-language data, their performance and cultural representation for Indic scripts remain unknown, with no systematic evaluation of correctness degradation or script-driven cultural bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1000 diverse image-caption pairs from COCO-NLLB, translated into 31 languages (30 Indic across 10 scripts plus English), with four images generated per prompt per model. Evaluation produces six scalar metrics per language: three correctness scores (CLGC in text-text space, IGC in image-image space, LGC in image-text space) and three representation scores (SCAL across language pairs, SCWL within a language, DWL across prompts). Feature extraction uses a CLIP-style image encoder f, a text encoder t, and a caption generator c, with a shared similarity function φ for all comparisons.

## Method

The IndicTTI benchmark introduces two novel metrics: Cyclic Language-Grounded Correctness (CLGC), which generates captions from each produced image via a caption generator and measures text-text similarity against the original prompt, and Self-Consistency Across Languages (SCAL), which computes pairwise image-image similarity for the same prompt rendered in two different languages, averaged over all language pairs. The evaluation pipeline applies these metrics to four TTI systems—Stable Diffusion and AltDiffusion (open-source, 124K images each) and Midjourney and DALL-E 3 (API-based, 24.8K and 6.2K images respectively)—across all 30 Indic languages. A common 40-prompt subset enables direct cross-model comparison, while the full 1000-prompt set is used for per-model analysis. Qualitative analysis further examines script-to-culture correlations, revealing that Devanagari prompts evoke Indian religious figures in Midjourney, Arabic scripts trigger Muslim cultural elements, and DALL-E 3 uniquely handles unsupported scripts by identifying the language and generating culturally themed imagery.

## Results

On the proposed CLGC metric (text-text faithfulness), DALL-E 3 maintains 53–64% across Indic languages (only a 13.62% average drop from its 65.70% English score), while Stable Diffusion drops 56.64% (64.53%→~7.3%), AltDiffusion drops 42.16% (58.16%→~15.9%), and Midjourney drops 52.16% (64.31%→~12.1%). On IGC (image-image similarity to ground truth), DALL-E 3 shows only an 8.70% average drop versus 27.74% (SD), 21.53% (AltDiffusion), and 26.32% (Midjourney). On LGC (image-text alignment), DALL-E 3 achieves 25.09% average across Indic languages, far exceeding Stable Diffusion (4.01%), AltDiffusion (7.08%), and Midjourney (3.12%). For cross-language consistency (SCAL), DALL-E 3 scores highest at 29.90%, followed by Midjourney (26.75%), Stable Diffusion (25.44%), and AltDiffusion (23.73%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DALL-E 3 dominates all three correctness metrics across Indic languages: it achieves 25.09% average LGC versus 4.01% (Stable Diffusion), 7.08% (AltDiffusion), and 3.12% (Midjourney), and only an 8.70% IGC drop from English versus 21–28% for the other three models. Among the non-DALL-E models, AltDiffusion consistently outperforms Stable Diffusion and Midjourney on CLGC and LGC for Indic languages (e.g., 16.79% vs 7.35% vs 11.31% CLGC on Hindi), likely due to its multilingual training, though it underperforms on English (58.16% vs 64.53% CLGC), suggesting catastrophic forgetting. Midjourney shows the lowest LGC (3.12% avg) and lowest distinctiveness (DWL), indicating it generates highly similar, culturally stereotyped content regardless of prompt. All four models struggle with Santali, Manipuri (Meitei script), and Bodo, with DALL-E 3 dropping to 10.93% CLGC on Santali and 2.09% LGC on Manipuri (Meitei), the weakest scores in the entire benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multilingual TTI evaluation line initiated by CocoCrola [13], which assessed DALL-E and Stable Diffusion across seven languages using simple prompts, by scaling to 30 Indic languages with complex, multi-element prompts drawn from COCO-NLLB. It builds on AltDiffusion [4] as the primary open-source multilingual baseline and on Struppek et al. [15]'s observation that script-specific characters influence cultural content in generated images, but formalizes this into a systematic benchmark with new metrics (CLGC, SCAL) that go beyond CocoCrola's distinctiveness and self-consistency measures. The use of IndicTrans2 [6] for translating prompts into four underrepresented scheduled languages (Bodo, Dogri, Konkani, Santali) and two additional scripts (Meitei, Sindhi-Devanagari) extends the linguistic coverage well beyond what prior multilingual TTI benchmarks have addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark 56% performance drop for Stable Diffusion on Indic languages versus English, and a dramatic gap where DALL-E 3 achieves ~25% LGC while all other models fall below 7%, highlighting that current open-source and API-based TTI systems are effectively unusable for the 1.4 billion Indic-language speakers. The systematic correlation between writing script and culturally stereotyped imagery (Devanagari → Hindu deities, Arabic → Muslim dress, Gurmukhi → turbans) raises important questions about whether these associations are learned from training data or are artifacts of the text encoder, and whether they constitute harmful reinforcement of cultural stereotypes. Key limitations include the indirect dependence on translation quality (COCO-NLLB or IndicTrans2) and the use of potentially biased pre-trained models for evaluation, leaving open whether the observed gaps reflect true model failure or measurement artifacts. Future work could explore whether script-agnostic representations or targeted fine-tuning can close the performance gap without sacrificing cultural specificity.
