## Problem

Deep learning-based keypoint descriptors have lost much of the affine robustness that handcrafted methods like ASIFT provided, which is critical for reliable matching across wide baselines. The steerers framework for structured equivariant keypoint description was previously limited to the rotation group SO(2), leaving the much larger class of local affine transformations unaddressed. Hard-coding affine equivariance into network architectures has only been feasible with small networks and group sampling, precluding use with state-of-the-art descriptor backbones.

## Input / Output

Given a pair of RGB images, the method detects K keypoints per image using the DeDoDe detector and evaluates a feature extractor (VGG-19 or DINOv2 ViT backbone) at those locations to produce 256-dimensional descriptors. The descriptor space is structured as a direct sum of irreducible GL(2) representations (degrees 0–4) with learned determinant scalings and a learned change-of-basis matrix. Matching is performed via mutual nearest neighbours using a max-similarity score that searches over a small set of affine transformations, with a dual-softmax threshold to filter spurious matches.

## Method

The core contribution is a steerer ρ: GL(2) → GL(256) constructed from irreducible representations of GL(2) on spaces of homogeneous polynomials (degrees 0–4), combined with learned determinant-power scalings and a learned orthogonal change of basis, so that f(W_φ[I]) ≈ ρ(M(x))·f(I)(x) for any differentiable warp φ with local affine map M(x). Training proceeds in two stages: first, joint training of the descriptor and steerer on MegaDepth with heavy affine augmentations, where ground-truth local affine maps (estimated by warping small octagons around matched keypoints) steer descriptions to align across views; second, fine-tuning on upright image pairs through a max-similarity loss with two learnable affine prototypes, which sacrifices strict equivariance for upright-image performance. The similarity metric uses Euclidean distance rather than cosine similarity, since the steerer can change descriptor norms under non-orthogonal affine maps.

## Results

On IMC2022, AffSteer-B achieves 77.3 mAA@10, a substantial improvement over DeDoDe-B (72.9) and Steerers-B-C4-Perm (73.4), and even outperforms all G-size variants. On MegaDepth-1500, AffSteer-B reaches 52.7 AUC@5° (vs. 49.4 for DeDoDe-B), and on HPatches it scores 70.1 AUC@3px (vs. 68.2 for DeDoDe-B). The equivariant variant AffEqui-B achieves competitive results on the rotation-heavy AIMS benchmark (60 AP with 12-rotation max similarity), matching the dedicated rotation steerer approach.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffSteer-B outperforms DeDoDe-B [22] on every benchmark: IMC2022 (77.3 vs. 72.9 mAA@10), MegaDepth-1500 (52.7 vs. 49.4 AUC@5°), HPatches (70.1 vs. 68.2 AUC@3px), and WxBS (50.0 vs. 45.9 mAA@10px). Against the rotation-steerer baseline Steerers-B-C4-Perm [9], AffSteer-B wins on IMC2022 (77.3 vs. 73.4), MegaDepth (52.7 vs. 51.0), and HPatches (70.1 vs. 69.5), but trails slightly on WxBS (50.0 vs. 51.0). The older detector-descriptor methods SuperPoint [18], DISK [84], ALIKED [91], and SiLK [29] are all surpassed by large margins (e.g., 77.3 vs. 64.8–68.5 on IMC2022). Notably, the B-size AffSteer-B outperforms all G-size variants including DeDoDe-G (75.8) and Steerers-G-C4-Perm (75.5) on IMC2022, suggesting the affine steering framework is particularly effective at the smaller model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes the steerers framework of Bokman et al. (CVPR 2024) from the rotation group SO(2) to the full affine group GL(2), using irreducible representations of GL(2) on spaces of homogeneous polynomials as the mathematical backbone. It builds on DeDoDe as the baseline detector-descriptor architecture and departs from prior equivariant CNN work (which hard-codes equivariance via group-convolutional layers) by instead learning a linear steerer that maps features under affine warps, making the approach compatible with large pretrained backbones like VGG-19 and DINOv2. The training recipe—homography pretraining followed by upright fine-tuning through a max-similarity loss with learnable affine prototypes—is a new combination not present in either the rotation-steerer or DeDoDe literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that affine-equivariant keypoint descriptors can achieve state-of-the-art upright matching (77.3 mAA@10 on IMC22, beating the previous best detector-descriptor by ~4.4 points) while retaining a structured, interpretable response to affine warps. A key open question is the tension between equivariance and upright specialization: after fine-tuning, the steerer's scale response is degraded (especially for the B-model), and the oracle-steering results (comparable to dense SOTA RoMa) suggest substantial unrealized potential if local affine estimation could be integrated end-to-end. The work also highlights that the B-size model can outperform larger G-size variants on some benchmarks, raising questions about the compute-efficiency frontier for keypoint descriptors.
