## Problem

Generalizable neural surface reconstruction from sparse views is hindered by a fundamental trade-off: existing methods either use non-end-to-end multi-stage pipelines that accumulate errors, require ground-truth depth supervision, or build dense (or per-view) cost volumes that consume prohibitive memory. Crucially, unsupervised end-to-end sparsification has not been achieved because determining which voxels to retain requires evaluating SDF predictions for every voxel via MLPs, making joint coarse-to-fine optimization infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240844/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N calibrated RGB images (2–9 views at test time, 4 at training) with known intrinsics and extrinsics, resized to 640×480 (training) or 800×576 (testing). It outputs a 3D surface mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from the SDF zero-level set, along with the underlying multi-scale surface-centric feature volumes. A key practical property is that memory consumption remains nearly constant (~2.6–3.1 GB) regardless of the number of input views, unlike per-view cost-volume methods that scale linearly.

## Method

SuRF introduces surface-centric modeling, which concentrates computation on narrow bands around the surface rather than the full scene volume. A novel Matching Field—a single-channel volume trained unsupervised via image warping loss (SSIM + L1)—encodes rough geometry as a view-dependent weight distribution along rays; the surface position is retrieved by softmax-weighted interpolation, making sparsification a cheap trilinear lookup rather than an expensive per-voxel MLP evaluation. Region Sparsification then prunes voxels by projecting them onto per-view surface maps and retaining only those within the surface region visible from at least two views, providing occlusion-aware, end-to-end differentiable sparsification. Across 4 scales with progressively narrowing surface bands (ε ratios 1:0.3:0.1:0.01), this yields multi-scale surface-centric feature volumes that enable higher-resolution geometry with ~80% less memory than dense-volume baselines. Appearance is recovered via color blending of projected source-view features and standard SDF-based volume rendering with an eikonal regularizer.

## Results

On the DTU benchmark (3 input views, 800×576 resolution), SuRF achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 1.05, outperforming the previous best generalizable method C2F2NeuS (1.11) and the baseline SparseNeuS (1.96) by 5.4% and 46.4% respectively. In terms of efficiency, SuRF requires only 2.6–3.1 GB of memory for 3–9 input views at 256³ volume resolution, compared to 10–28 GB for C2F2NeuS, representing over 80% memory reduction. The method also generalizes without finetuning to large-scale scenes on BlendedMVS, Tanks and Temples, and ETH3D, where volumetric baselines struggle due to empty voxels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuRF achieves the best mean Chamfer distance (1.05) across all 15 DTU test scenes, winning on 13 out of 15 individual scenes. Against the closest competitor C2F2NeuS (1.11), SuRF improves by 5.4% on average and wins on 12 of 15 scenes, with the largest margin on scene 63 (1.17 vs. 1.41). Compared to the direct baseline SparseNeuS (1.96), SuRF delivers a 46.4% improvement, primarily by enabling end-to-end multi-scale sparsification rather than a non-differentiable multi-stage pipeline. Against depth-fusion methods VolRecon (1.48) and ReTR (1.25), SuRF wins by 29% and 16% respectively while not requiring ground-truth depth supervision. For classical MVS baselines, SuRF outperforms RC-MVSNet (1.22) by 14% and COLMAP (1.52) by 31%. In memory efficiency, SuRF uses only 2.6–3.1 GB for 3–9 views versus 10–28 GB for C2F2NeuS, and its runtime is nearly insensitive to the number of input views, unlike per-view cost-volume methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuRF extends the generalizable neural surface reconstruction line initiated by SparseNeuS [24] and GenS [34], which introduced multi-stage and multi-scale volume strategies respectively, but both are hampered by non-end-to-end training or dense volume memory costs. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'matching field'—a single-channel weight distribution along rays—that replaces SDF-threshold-based voxel selection with a view-consistency-based region sparsification, enabling the first unsupervised end-to-end sparsification in this setting. The SDF-based volume rendering formulation is inherited from NeuS [42], and the color blending strategy follows IBRNet [43], but the surface-centric volume construction and the warping-loss-driven matching field are novel architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80% memory reduction (3G vs 20G+ at 256³ resolution) and near-flat memory scaling with input views make high-resolution surface reconstruction practical on consumer hardware, which is a significant step toward real-time and large-scale applications. However, quantitative evaluation is limited to the DTU dataset (15 scenes), and generalization to BlendedMVS, Tanks and Temples, and ETH3D is demonstrated only qualitatively, leaving open questions about robustness on truly large-scale or texture-poor scenes. The matching field's reliance on a single-channel volume and trilinear interpolation is elegant but may struggle with highly ambiguous or occluded regions where two-view consistency is insufficient. Future work could explore whether the surface-centric sparsification extends to dynamic scenes or single-image settings.
