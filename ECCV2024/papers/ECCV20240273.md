## Problem

Existing NeRF watermarking methods like CopyRNeRF embed ownership messages only after NeRF creation via fine-tuning, leaving a vulnerability window for malicious exploitation, and require jointly training a message extractor, making the process complex and time-consuming (≈30 h). This complexity discourages NeRF creators from adopting watermarking at all, rendering even well-designed protection schemes ineffective in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240273/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard NeRF training images (8 views per scene from Blender or LLFF), a fixed 48-bit binary copyright message chosen by the creator, and a pre-trained watermarking base model (a frozen CNN extractor from HiDDeN or MBRS, obtainable from third parties or trained separately). It produces a watermarked NeRF model with the same architecture as the underlying variant (Instant-NGP, TensorRF, or Plenoxels) and enables message extraction from any rendered view by passing the image through the same base model. No architectural modifications to the NeRF are required; the only change is to the rendering schedule during optimization.

## Method

NeRFProtector distills watermark knowledge from a frozen pre-trained extractor into the NeRF during its creation, eliminating the post-creation embedding delay. The key mechanism is Progressive Global Rendering (PGR): instead of the standard local rendering that samples a random subset of 4096 pixels, PGR renders all pixels at three cascade resolutions (W/2^n × H/2^n for n=1,2,3), ensuring message patterns are embedded globally across the scene representation. The training loss combines the standard NeRF local reconstruction loss, an L2 invisibility loss at the highest-resolution cascade, and a weighted BCE distillation loss that pushes the base model's extracted message toward the target binary message. Because the base model is frozen and only NeRF parameters are optimized, the approach is truly plug-and-play: it adapts to different NeRF backbones (Instant-NGP, TensorRF, Plenoxels) and different extractors (HiDDeN, MBRS) without architectural changes, and reduces watermarking time from ~30 h (CopyRNeRF) to ~50 min.

## Results

On the Blender dataset, NeRFProtector achieves 92.69% bit accuracy (no distortion) while maintaining strong reconstruction quality (PSNR 29.26, SSIM 0.9393, LPIPS 0.0483), closely approaching the non-watermarked NeRF baseline (PSNR 30.62). On the LLFF dataset, NeRFProtector reaches 96.99% bit accuracy and even surpasses the non-watermarked NeRF in PSNR (26.82 vs. 26.37) and SSIM (0.8569 vs. 0.8352). Under common distortions, bit accuracy remains above 76% (JPEG) and 80% (Resize) on LLFF, and above 78% (JPEG) on Blender, demonstrating robust extraction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRFProtector decisively outperforms all watermarking baselines on both bit accuracy and reconstruction quality. Compared to CopyRNeRF (the strongest prior NeRF watermarking method), NeRFProtector gains ~30 percentage points in bit accuracy on Blender (92.69% vs. 62.15%) and ~33 points on LLFF (96.99% vs. 63.72%), while also improving PSNR by 3.76 dB (Blender) and 1.02 dB (LLFF). The 2D-watermarking-then-train baselines (HiDDeN+NeRF and MBRS+NeRF) essentially fail at watermarking, with bit accuracy hovering around 50% (chance level), and MBRS+NeRF severely degrades reconstruction (LPIPS 0.2967 on Blender). Against the non-watermarked Instant-NGP baseline, NeRFProtector incurs a modest PSNR drop of 1.36 dB on Blender but actually improves PSNR by 0.45 dB on LLFF, indicating the watermarking overhead is negligible or even beneficial on real scenes. The plug-and-play property is confirmed by Table 3: combining the same HiDDeN extractor with TensorRF yields 89.35% bit accuracy (PSNR 32.73 vs. 34.96 unwatermarked) and with Plenoxels yields 97.92% bit accuracy (PSNR 34.19 vs. 35.80 unwatermarked), demonstrating consistent performance across NeRF variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the extractor from HiDDeN, a 2D image watermarking framework, as a plug-and-play 'teacher' model whose knowledge is distilled into NeRF during its creation, rather than treating watermarking as a post-hoc fine-tuning step as in CopyRNeRF. It departs from CLIP-NeRF-style approaches that modify NeRF's internal representation by instead altering only the rendering schedule (replacing local random-pixel rendering with progressive global rendering across multiple scales), preserving the original NeRF architecture. The conceptual lineage is: 2D DNN watermarking (HiDDeN, MBRS) provides the base model; NeRF/Instant-NGP provides the 3D representation; and knowledge distillation bridges the two without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, during-creation embedding closes a practical security gap identified in CopyRNeRF (a window between NeRF creation and watermark insertion where the model is unprotected) and reduces training overhead from ~30 hours to ~50 minutes, making watermarking feasible for everyday NeRF creators. However, the paper itself acknowledges that white-box PGD attacks on the base model can remove watermarks with minimal distortion, and that copyright enforcement ultimately requires legislative frameworks beyond technical solutions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic or 4D NeRFs, how it scales to longer messages or more complex multi-object scenes, and whether keeping the base model secret is a realistic long-term assumption.
