## Problem

In federated domain generalization, each client holds data from a single domain, causing domain shift across clients and overfitting to local distributions. Existing federated DG methods either leak privacy by sharing client-specific style distributions or classifiers, prioritize aggregation over local training, or require access to target-domain data for adaptation. This paper addresses how to improve generalization to unseen test domains in a federated setting without additional privacy leaks or target data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241915/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and outputs class labels, operating within a standard image classification pipeline. During training, each client processes single-domain images through a ResNet-18 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet) with batch normalization layers, while the instance adapter consumes the channel-wise difference between per-instance and global BN statistics as its input. At test time, the adapter requires only a single forward pass per image to compute interpolated normalization statistics, adding minimal inference overhead (1.37 ms vs. 1.34 ms baseline).

## Method

FedFD-A comprises two components. First, federated feature diversification normalizes local inputs using randomly interpolated statistics between local (instance-level) and global (server-aggregated) BN statistics across all layers, generating augmented features that span multiple domain characteristics. This is paired with client-agnostic learning objectives: a feature loss (CAFL) that minimizes the L2 distance between original and diversified features, and a classification loss (CACL) that trains the classifier on diversified features, jointly encouraging client-invariant representations. Second, an instance feature adapter—a small two-layer network per BN layer—takes the concatenated difference between instance and global statistics, generates interpolation parameters α via a reparameterization trick (Gaussian sampling clamped to [0,1]), and produces interpolated statistics for test-time normalization. The adapter is trained with alternating updates alongside the main network, enabling zero-shot adaptation to unseen domains at inference without any additional forward passes or hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On PACS, FedFD-A achieves 85.53% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest privacy-preserving baseline SiloBN (80.79%) by 4.74% and the closest competitor COPA (85.37%, which has privacy concerns) by 0.16%. On VLCS and OfficeHome, FedFD-A reaches 76.68% and 64.71% respectively, leading all compared methods. Across the three datasets, FedFD-A attains a 75.64% average, surpassing the next-best privacy-preserving method (L2D at 73.87%) by 1.77%. The feature diversification component alone (FedFD) already yields 84.07% on PACS, and the instance adapter adds a further 1.46% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedFD-A (85.53% PACS avg) outperforms all privacy-preserving baselines: SiloBN (80.79%), L2D (82.96%), Mixstyle (82.43%), RSC (81.60%), and CSAC (82.58%), with the largest margin over SiloBN at +4.74%. Among federated DG methods, COPA (85.37%) is the closest on PACS but suffers privacy leaks and underperforms FedFD-A on VLCS (74.51 vs 76.68) and OfficeHome (62.42 vs 64.71). FedDG (82.52%) and CCST (83.56%) also have privacy concerns and trail FedFD-A by 3.01% and 1.97% on PACS respectively. On VLCS, FedFD-A leads all methods by a clear margin (76.68% vs next-best RSC at 75.90%), and on OfficeHome it achieves the best result (64.71%) ahead of FedDG (64.90% is actually slightly higher but has privacy issues; among privacy-preserving methods, CSAC at 64.35% is closest). The feature diversification component alone (FedFD, 84.07%) already surpasses all single-domain DG methods, and the instance adapter adds a consistent 1.46% gain, confirming both components contribute meaningfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SiloBN and FedBN, which preserve local batch normalization statistics to handle domain shift in federated learning, but departs from them by actively leveraging the aggregated global statistics as a data augmentation signal during local training. It positions itself against prior federated DG methods (FedDG, COPA, CCST) that achieve multi-source diversification by sharing client-specific information (style distributions, classifiers), and against MixStyle, which can only mix statistics within a single-domain batch. The key conceptual departure is treating the server's aggregated BN statistics as a privacy-safe proxy for multi-source domain diversity, combined with a learned instance adapter for zero-shot test-time adaptation that replaces rule-based calibration methods like IABN and MixNorm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, privacy-preserving recipe for federated DG that requires no additional data sharing beyond standard FedAvg parameter exchange, making it deployable in real-world federated settings where client data is strictly siloed. The instance adapter's minimal overhead (0.22M extra parameters, 0.03ms extra inference) makes it attractive for edge deployment. Limitations include the reliance on BN layers (though a DoPrompt/Transformer extension is shown), evaluation limited to image classification with at most four clients per domain, and the assumption that global statistics adequately represent the full domain diversity. Open questions include scalability to many more clients, non-iid label distributions (briefly addressed in supplementary), and whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities or tasks beyond classification.
