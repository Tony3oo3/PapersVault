## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting and related novel view synthesis methods assume steady, high-quality still photographs, making them impractical for handheld video captures that suffer from motion blur and rolling shutter distortion. Existing deblurring approaches either operate on 2D images without leveraging 3D scene structure or learn blur kernels from data rather than modelling the physical image formation process. This paper addresses the gap by directly incorporating camera motion effects into the 3DGS rendering pipeline, enabling high-quality reconstruction from naturally captured handheld video.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse keyframes (selected at ≥10 cm intervals to minimize blur) from handheld video, synchronized IMU data for VIO-based velocity estimation, COLMAP-estimated camera poses and intrinsics, and device-specific rolling shutter readout times. It produces a 3D Gaussian splatting model whose rendering equation explicitly accounts for per-pixel exposure timing, enabling novel view synthesis from the same blurry inputs. Camera poses are treated as non-static during each frame's exposure, and linear/angular velocities are added as optimizable parameters initialized from VIO estimates.

## Method

The core contribution is a screen-space approximation that models motion blur and rolling shutter by computing per-Gaussian pixel velocities during the exposure interval, avoiding repeated projective transforms for each blur sample. The rendering equation integrates the 3DGS alpha-blending over N_blur uniform time samples, where each sample shifts Gaussian means in pixel space by Δt·v′_ij (Eq. 5–6), with the rolling shutter offset encoded as a per-row time shift. Camera poses and velocities are jointly optimized with the Gaussians using approximated pose gradients (Eq. 10) and l₂ regularization to prevent drift, while VIO provides initial velocity estimates to resolve the ill-posed deblurring problem. This design keeps the expensive 3D-to-2D projection computed once per frame, making the multi-sample blur integration efficient relative to world-space velocity formulations.

## Results

On the BAD-NeRF synthetic benchmark (Table 2), the proposed method achieves the best PSNR across 4 of 5 scenes, reaching 31.80 on Cozyroom, 32.08 on Pool, and 30.16 on Trolley, outperforming BAD-NeRF (30.97, 31.72, 28.25 respectively) and the Splatfacto baseline (24.93, 27.88, 19.47). On the re-rendered synthetic variants (Table 1), rolling shutter compensation yields the largest gains, e.g. 19.21→35.84 PSNR on Cozyroom and 15.29→35.27 on Factory. On 11 real smartphone sequences (Table 3), the full method achieves 28.59 average PSNR versus 26.91 for the Splatfacto baseline, with the largest per-scene improvement on s20-BIKERACK (25.98→29.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Splatfacto (Nerfstudio/gsplat) baseline, the proposed method wins on every scene and every metric in both synthetic and real-data experiments, with PSNR gains ranging from ~1 dB (mild blur) to ~16 dB (rolling shutter on Factory). Against BAD-NeRF, the strongest prior NeRF-based deblurring method, Ours wins on 4 of 5 synthetic scenes (e.g. Cozyroom 31.80 vs 30.97, Trolley 30.16 vs 28.25) but loses on Factory (30.54 vs 31.65), where the authors note the pose optimization converged to a local minimum. Against Deblur-NeRF, Ours consistently outperforms by 1–4 dB PSNR across all scenes. Against the MPR+Splatfacto pre-processing pipeline, Ours wins on all five scenes, confirming that modelling blur in 3D is more effective than 2D deblurring as a pre-processing step. Against CVR (rolling-shutter de-rolling pre-processing, Table 3), results are mixed: CVR wins on the two Pixel 5 sequences (e.g. PIXEL5-TABLE 32.42 vs 31.86) but Ours is clearly better on iPhone (low readout time) and competitive on S20, with the authors attributing CVR's inferiority to artefacts in its output that degrade 3DGS training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rendering pipeline to explicitly model motion blur and rolling shutter as physical camera-motion effects during exposure, departing from prior NeRF-based deblurring methods (Deblur-NeRF, BAD-NeRF) that learn blur kernels or add MLPs to the rendering path. The key conceptual departure is a screen-space approximation that computes per-Gaussian pixel velocities from VIO-estimated linear and angular velocities, avoiding repeated projective transforms for each temporal sample. It also integrates pose optimization with l2 regularization, building on BARF-style approaches but combining them with rolling shutter compensation for the first time in the 3DGS context, and uses VIO velocity estimates (from a HybVIO-based system) as initializations rather than learning them from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes 3DGS practically applicable to the dominant real-world capture modality—handheld smartphone video—by handling the two most common degradation sources (motion blur and rolling shutter) within the 3D reconstruction rather than as a 2D preprocessing step. The finding that their integrated 3D-aware approach outperforms state-of-the-art 2D de-rolling (CVR) and deblurring (MPR) preprocessing suggests that modeling image formation in 3D is more robust than learning-based image manipulation. Limitations include a ~5× training time overhead, dependence on IMU data (unavailable for many cameras), and the screen-space approximation that neglects motion effects on Gaussian covariances and view-dependent colors. Open questions include whether spline-based trajectory models would further improve accuracy, whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, and whether the training overhead can be reduced through better CUDA optimization.
