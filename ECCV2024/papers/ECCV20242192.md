## Problem

Localizing procedure steps in instructional videos via narrated supervision is hindered by two sources of alignment noise: narrations often contain task-irrelevant content (e.g., background sounds, personal asides), and ASR timestamps are unreliable because demonstrators may describe steps before or after performing them. Existing remedies—wikiHow knowledge-base steps and self-training filters—either mismatch the actual video content or inherit noise from the very models they try to correct.

## Input / Output

The model takes a narrated instructional video (split into 1-second segments at 16 FPS, encoded by a frozen S3D backbone) and a sequence of LLM-summarized procedure-step sentences (encoded by a BoW/Word2Vec text backbone) as input, and outputs a cosine-similarity matrix of shape L×T between each step and each video segment. Unlike prior work that feeds raw narrations or wikiHow articles, the text input here is a compact, video-specific set of steps produced by Llama2-7B, and the supervision signal is a pseudo-matching matrix generated offline by the MPTVA strategy rather than derived directly from ASR timestamps.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, Llama2-7B is prompted to filter colloquial/task-irrelevant sentences from ASR narrations and summarize the remaining content into concise LLM-steps that align more closely with the actual video than generic wikiHow procedures. Second, the Multi-Pathway Text-Video Alignment (MPTVA) strategy generates pseudo step–video matching by fusing three complementary alignment scores: (1) S-N-V, which maps steps to narrations via semantic similarity and then to video segments via narration timestamps; (2) S-V-long, which directly scores step–segment similarity using a long-term instructional-video alignment model (TAN*); and (3) S-V-short, which uses a general-domain video foundation model (InternVideo-MM-L14) for fine-grained short-term matching. The three score matrices are mean-pooled, and pseudo-labels are extracted by selecting the top-scoring segment per step within a temporal window (W=2) above a confidence threshold (γ=0.65). The downstream model Φ—comprising frozen S3D and BoW backbones, unimodal Transformers (positional embeddings for video only), and a joint cross-modal Transformer—is trained with the MIL-NCE loss against these pseudo-labels, leveraging mutual agreement across pathways to suppress noise that any single pathway would propagate.

## Results

On the HTM-Align narration grounding task, the method achieves 69.3% R@1, surpassing the previous best (VINA, 66.5%) by 2.8%. On HT-Step procedure step grounding, it reaches 43.3% R@1, a 5.9% improvement over VINA (37.4%). On CrossTask zero-shot action step localization, the method attains 47.9% Avg.R@1, outperforming VINA (44.8%) by 3.1%. In a fair comparison without test-time narrations, the method beats VINA (w/o nar) by 7.7% on step grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 compared baselines across all three downstream tasks. Against the closest competitor VINA [30], it gains +2.8% on narration grounding (69.3 vs 66.5), +5.9% on step grounding (43.3 vs 37.4), and +3.1% on action step localization (47.9 vs 44.8). TAN [18] achieves 49.4% on narration grounding but does not report step grounding; the method exceeds it by 19.9% on that task. MIL-NCE [31] and HowTo100M [32] serve as early contrastive-learning baselines, scoring 34.2%/30.7% and 33.6% Avg.R@1 respectively, well below the proposed method. General video-text models (CLIP [34] at 23.4%, InternVideo [45] at 40.1%/37.3%, VideoCLIP [46] at 33.9%) are substantially outperformed, confirming the benefit of task-specific multi-pathway pseudo-labeling. Among sequence-alignment methods (DWSA [37] at 35.3%, VT-TWINS* [21] at 40.7%, UniVL [29] at 42.0% on CrossTask), the method leads by 5.9–12.6% Avg.R@1. MCN [8] (35.1%) and the Zhukov/Gan survey baseline [16] (40.5%) are also clearly surpassed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on training step-localization models from narrated instructional videos, building most directly on TAN (Han et al., CVPR 2022) and VINA (Mavroudi et al., 2023). TAN introduced mutual agreement between two differently-architected models to filter noisy pseudo-labels, while VINA replaced raw narrations with task-specific steps from the WikiHow knowledge base and explored pseudo-alignments via pre-trained long-term video-text models. The key departures here are (1) replacing the generic, human-curated wikiHow-steps with video-specific LLM-summarized steps that better match actual video content, and (2) replacing single-pathway or dual-model agreement with a three-pathway fusion (timestamp-based, long-term instructional-domain, and short-term general-domain alignment) to generate more reliable pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in instructional video understanding: the cost of temporal annotations and the noise inherent in ASR-derived narrations. The finding that LLM-steps outperform both wikiHow-steps (by 10.7%) and the combination of narrations plus wikiHow-steps (by 6%) suggests that video-specific, LLM-generated text is a more effective supervision signal than human-curated but task-generic procedures. An open question is whether the multi-pathway fusion strategy generalizes beyond the instructional video domain, and whether the specific choice of pre-trained models (TAN*, InternVideo) is critical or whether any diverse set of alignment models would suffice. The approach also raises the broader question of how much of the remaining gap to fully supervised methods is attributable to the irreducible noise in pseudo-labels versus model capacity.
