## Problem

Existing image restoration networks exhibit strong task-specific performance but poor task generality: a network that excels at super-resolution often underperforms on deblurring or dehazing, and vice versa. The paper identifies that this disparity stems from mismatched functional requirements across degradation types (local detail reconstruction vs. global information aggregation) and proposes that a general backbone must simultaneously satisfy all such requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241890/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network accepts a single degraded RGB image at 256×256 patch size and outputs a restored RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. For super-resolution, the low-resolution input is first upsampled to full resolution via bilinear interpolation before entering the U-shape encoder-decoder. In the all-in-one restoration setting, the same model handles multiple degradation types (×2/×4 SR, Gaussian noise at σ∈(0,50), motion blur, rain, haze) with equal sampling probability during training.

## Method

X-Restormer builds on Restormer's 4-level U-shape encoder-decoder (channels [48, 96, 192, 384], 26.06M parameters) by alternating transposed self-attention blocks (TSAB) with spatial self-attention blocks (SSAB) at every level. The TSAB retains Restormer's Multi-Dconv Transpose Attention (MDTA), which computes channel-wise attention over C×C tokens to capture global information, while the SSAB introduces Overlapping Cross-Attention (OCA) from HAT with window size 8 and 0.5 overlap ratio to provide strong local spatial fitting. Both blocks share a Gated-Dconv Feed-forward Network (GDFN) with expansion factor 2.66. The key design principle is that combining global channel-wise interaction (TSA) with local spatial interaction (SSA) in a single U-shape architecture satisfies the diverse functional demands of all five restoration tasks without adding parameters, and the paper validates this through a systematic five-network, five-task benchmark that motivates the design.

## Results

X-Restormer achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across all five image restoration tasks. In the all-in-one restoration setting (Table 8), it leads on every task: 34.72 dB (SR×2), 28.81 dB (SR×4), 29.12 dB (denoising σ=50), 30.85 dB (deblurring), 32.27 dB (deraining), and 38.24 dB (dehazing), outperforming Restormer by 0.21–1.29 dB depending on the task. On individual task benchmarks, X-Restormer reaches 29.17 dB on Set14 (SR), 30.24 dB on Urban100 (denoising), 33.44 dB on GoPro (deblurring), 32.21 dB on Rain100H (deraining), and 42.90 dB on SOTS Indoor (dehazing), with the largest single-task gain being +2.85 dB over DehazeFormer on dehazing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Restormer outperforms all five benchmark networks (MPRNet, SwinIR, Uformer, Restormer, NAFNet) on every task in the all-in-one setting, with the largest margins on dehazing (+1.29 dB over Restormer, +3.18 dB over NAFNet) and deraining (+0.36 dB over Restormer). Against task-specific SOTA, X-Restormer surpasses DehazeFormer by 2.85 dB on SOTS Indoor dehazing, EDT by 0.20 dB on Urban100 SR, and NAFNet by 0.36 dB on GoPro deblurring. The most notable win is over SwinIR on dehazing (42.90 vs. 29.14 dB), where SwinIR's plain architecture and local spatial attention are fundamentally mismatched to the global depth-dependent degradation. X-Restormer slightly trails Restormer on Test1200/Test2800 deraining (32.31 vs. 33.22 dB), attributed to variations in synthetic rain models, but leads on Test100 and Rain100H. Overall, X-Restormer achieves the best or second-best result on all 18 evaluation datasets, confirming its superior task generality without any additional parameters (26.06M vs. Restormer's 26.13M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Restormer (Zamir et al., CVPR 2022) as its architectural baseline, retaining its U-shape encoder-decoder structure, MDTA transposed self-attention, and GDFN feed-forward network, while incorporating the Overlapping Cross-Attention (OCA) module from HAT (Chen et al., CVPR 2023) as the spatial self-attention component. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of designing the strongest possible network for a single task by instead framing the problem as one of functional completeness: a general backbone must simultaneously provide large-receptive-field processing (U-shape), global channel interaction (transposed self-attention), and strong local spatial fitting (spatial self-attention). The systematic five-network-by-five-task benchmark is a new empirical contribution that had not been conducted under unified training and evaluation protocols before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, parameter-matched general-purpose backbone (X-Restormer) that achieves competitive or SOTA results across five diverse restoration tasks without task-specific tuning, which is valuable for practitioners building multi-task restoration systems. The all-in-one restoration experiment demonstrates that a more functionally complete backbone mitigates optimization conflicts across tasks, suggesting a path toward unified restoration models. However, the paper acknowledges that X-Restormer still cannot match specialized SR architectures (e.g., HAT) on pure SR, and deraining performance is inconsistent across rain models, leaving open the question of whether a single architecture can truly dominate all degradation types or whether task-specific inductive biases remain necessary.
