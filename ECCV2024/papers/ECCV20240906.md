## Problem

Existing video-text retrieval methods rely on rote memorization of all training pairs through cross-modal interaction, diverging from human learning where knowledge is sought and associated from an open set. This 'closed-book' paradigm suffers from dense memorization overhead and difficulty with hard samples, while prior open-world knowledge injection methods lack a unified interface for diverse knowledge sources. The paper asks: can knowledge be decoupled from the retrieval model into external stores to balance memorization with optimization and enable plug-and-play open-world knowledge injection?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240906/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes uniformly sampled video frames (12 for MSR-VTT/LSMDC, 64 for ActivityNet/DiDeMo) and text captions (up to 32 or 64 tokens) encoded by CLIP ViT-B/32, producing bidirectional retrieval rankings (text-to-video and video-to-text) evaluated by R@K, MdR, and MnR. Beyond the standard query-candidate pair, the method additionally queries multi-grained knowledge stores (fine-grained top-K nearest neighbors and coarse-grained K-Means clusters of size 256) at inference and training time, with the stores themselves constructed from closed-set training features or open-world sources (CLIP-ViP pretraining, scene-graph triplets, or GPT-3.5 CoT captions).

## Method

KDProR decouples knowledge into fine-grained (individual text-video feature pairs) and coarse-grained (K-Means cluster centroids with max-pooling) stores, then injects this knowledge via a novel Expectation-Knowledge-Maximization (EKM) algorithm that extends classical EM. In the E-step, top-K knowledge is interpolated into query features (controlled by ratio ρ) and batch-level mismatches are corrected via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport to produce a realigned similarity matrix. In the K-step, a KNN distribution is computed from the retrieved knowledge and used as a calibration factor (negative log-likelihood) that reweights the InfoNCE contrastive loss in the M-step, increasing penalty for pairs that KNN identifies as mismatched. The framework is agnostic to the interaction backbone (DRL, CLIP4Clip, EMCL) and knowledge source, and a theoretical analysis proves the EKM iterations yield monotonically increasing likelihood and convergence.

## Results

On MSR-VTT (text-to-video), KDProR with CoT knowledge (KDProR‡) achieves R@1=49.6, R@5=75.1, R@10=84.4, and MnR=11.6, outperforming the strongest baseline DRL (R@1=47.4) by 2.2% in R@1. On DiDeMo, KDProR‡ reaches R@1=53.2 and MdR=1.0, surpassing DRL (R@1=47.9) by 5.3%. On LSMDC, KDProR‡ achieves R@1=28.2 and MdR=5.0 versus DRL's R@1=24.9 and MdR=7.0. Even in closed-set settings without open-world knowledge, KDProR+ gains 1.0% R@1 over DRL on MSR-VTT, confirming the benefit of knowledge decoupling alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KDProR outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. Among CLIP-based methods on MSR-VTT, KDProR‡ (R@1=49.6) surpasses DRL (47.4) by 2.2%, UATVR (47.5) by 2.1%, PromptSwitch (47.8) by 1.8%, and CLIP4clip (44.5) by 5.1%. On DiDeMo, the gap widens: KDProR‡ (R@1=53.2) beats DiffusionRet (46.7) by 6.5%, X-CLIP (45.2) by 8.0%, and DRL (47.9) by 5.3%. Against open-world baselines specifically, KDProR‡ exceeds Cap4Video (R@1=52.0 on DiDeMo) by 1.2% and TEFAL (R@1=49.4 on MSR-VTT) by 0.2%, while also achieving lower MnR (11.6 vs. 12.0 on MSR-VTT). Non-CLIP baselines (CE, MMT, Support, Frozen) trail by 18–29% in R@1, reflecting the advantage of CLIP-based pre-training. KDProR also demonstrates plug-and-play compatibility, improving CLIP4clip and EMCL base models by 3.7–3.7% R@1 when attached (Table 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

KDProR extends the classical EM algorithm (Dempster et al., 1977) into an Expectation-Knowledge-Maximization (EKM) framework, repurposing the E-step to inject knowledge from external stores rather than merely estimate latent variables. It builds on DRL's weighted token-wise interaction module as its base cross-modal architecture, but deliberately decouples knowledge from model parameters into multi-grained external stores (fine-grained nearest-neighbor caches and coarse-grained K-Means clusters), positioning VTR as an 'open-book examination' rather than the standard 'closed-book memorization' paradigm. The method also draws on optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp) for batch-level alignment correction and KNN distribution calibration, both borrowed from other subfields and unified under a single probabilistic objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The knowledge-decoupling paradigm offers a practical, unified interface for injecting heterogeneous open-world knowledge (pre-trained video-text features, scene-graph structured knowledge, LLM chain-of-thought captions) into any CLIP-based VTR model without architectural modification, which is a meaningful step toward modular, updatable retrieval systems. The EKM algorithm's monotonic-increase and convergence guarantees provide theoretical grounding that prior heuristic knowledge-injection methods (e.g., Cap4Video, TEFAL) lack. Open questions include how knowledge stores scale to very large or dynamically changing corpora, whether the approach transfers to other cross-modal tasks (the authors mention VQA but do not test it), and whether the added retrieval and OT computation overhead remains acceptable in latency-sensitive deployment settings.
