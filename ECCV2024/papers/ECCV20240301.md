## Problem

Phone-captured avatars suffer from baked-in capture lighting, missing regions (e.g., back of ears), and insufficient facial detail compared to studio-captured avatars, yet creating large-scale paired phone-studio datasets for supervised translation is impractical and existing unsupervised image-to-image translation methods fail to preserve the fine facial details critical for photorealistic avatars.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240301/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular phone video, from which a neutral texture map and expression texture maps are extracted via AVA's mesh fitting procedure at 1024×1024 resolution. It produces a high-resolution, studio-lit, inpainted neutral texture map that is fed into AVA's Universal Prior Model (UPM) to render a photorealistic animatable 3D avatar. Additionally, a lightweight 32×32 gain-and-bias color transform is estimated to transfer studio-like illumination to expression texture maps without applying inpainting or super-resolution to them.

## Method

The core pipeline has two stages. First, a StyleGAN2 is pretrained on 12,543 phone textures (G_phone), then finetuned into G_studio by sampling from the inverted W+ vectors of the phone dataset (rather than the Z space) and adversarially training against a studio discriminator, with identity-preservation losses (FaceID, LPIPS) and a small perceptual reconstruction loss on 83 paired samples; only generator parameters above 8×8 resolution are updated to lock in identity. Second, a Reshift-style latent diffusion model, conditioned on the image gradient of the phone texture map during both training and inference, super-resolves the low-resolution G_studio output to recover accurate facial details (moles, pores) while discarding phone-specific shading. The key insight is that W+ space inversion vectors are, by construction, as diverse as the training data, avoiding the mode collapse of Z-space sampling and yielding a generator that generalizes to unseen identities with far fewer artifacts.

## Results

On 10 paired phone-cum-studio texture maps, the proposed method achieves PSNR 22.76, SSIM 0.726, LPIPS 0.364, and DISTS 0.163, outperforming all three baselines across every metric. On 31 unpaired phone captures, the method attains the lowest FaceID distance (4.31e-4), indicating the best identity preservation compared to AgileGAN (1.36e-3), CUT (6.89e-4), and CycleGAN (5.19e-4). The largest margin is over AgileGAN, which lags by ~4.7 dB in PSNR and ~3.1× in FaceID distance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three unpaired image-to-image translation baselines: AgileGAN, CUT, and CycleGAN. The proposed method wins on all four reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS) and on FaceID identity preservation across every baseline. AgileGAN is the weakest competitor, trailing by 4.71 dB in PSNR (18.05 vs 22.76) and showing a 3.1× larger FaceID distance (1.36e-3 vs 4.31e-4), which the authors attribute to its use of the less flexible Z+ space and lack of identity-preserving constraints. CUT and CycleGAN are closer in PSNR (21.44 and 21.09 respectively) but still fall short by 1.3–1.7 dB, and both introduce visible artifacts and fail to inpaint missing regions around the ears. On identity preservation (FaceID), the proposed method leads CUT by 37% (4.31e-4 vs 6.89e-4) and CycleGAN by 17% (4.31e-4 vs 5.19e-4), confirming that the W+-space finetuning strategy better preserves facial identity than contrastive or cycle-consistency objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the AVA pipeline (Cao et al.) for phone-captured avatar creation and the StyleGAN2 W+ latent space for inversion, but departs from standard unsupervised image-to-image translation (CycleGAN, CUT, AgileGAN) by reframing the phone-to-studio conversion as a fine-tuning problem in inverted W+ space rather than a direct domain-translation task. It further extends the latent diffusion super-resolution line (ReShift) by conditioning on phone-captured texture gradients to recover identity-specific facial details lost during GAN-based illumination transfer. The key conceptual departure is using W+ space samples (near-perfect inversions of real data) instead of Z-space samples for adversarial fine-tuning, motivated by the observation that W+ samples are inherently more diverse and avoid mode collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck in consumer avatar creation: phone captures bake in arbitrary lighting, lack fine facial details, and have missing regions (ears, back of head), making them visibly inferior to studio captures. By enabling studio-quality texture generation from a single phone scan, the method could scale photorealistic avatar creation to millions of users without expensive LightStage infrastructure. However, limitations remain: the method struggles with extreme non-uniform lighting, does not personalize the UPM for expression-specific details, and produces head-only avatars. Open questions include whether the W+ fine-tuning strategy generalizes beyond face textures to other domains, and whether the gradient-conditioned diffusion approach can be extended to handle more complex geometric deformations.
