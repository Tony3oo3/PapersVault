## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient image-to-video adaptation methods (e.g., ST-Adapter, EVL, AIM) inevitably introduce extra parameters and computational overhead during inference to handle temporal modeling and the image-to-video domain gap. This paper asks whether it is possible to adapt pre-trained image transformers to video recognition with zero additional inference cost while maintaining competitive accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip as a sequence of T frames (typically 8, 16, or 32) fed through a standard ViT backbone (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 pre-trained with CLIP or IN21K), producing action class labels for recognition or per-frame action detection scores for AVA. Critically, at inference time the model architecture is identical to the original image ViT—no extra temporal modules, adapters, or parameters remain—so the input is processed with exactly the same FLOPs and parameter count as the frozen image model.

## Method

ZeroI2V combines two zero-cost mechanisms. First, Spatial-Temporal Dual-Headed Attention (STDHA) reassigns a subset of the existing multi-head attention heads to query key-value pairs from offset time steps (Δt ≠ 0) for temporal modeling, while the remaining heads continue spatial attention; this requires no new parameters or FLOPs. Second, a serial Linear Adapter (W_new = (I + W_up·W_down)·W_old) is densely placed on every linear projection in both MHSA and MLP, and after training the adapters are merged into the frozen weights via structural reparameterization, eliminating all extra inference cost. The full-adaptation strategy of wrapping all six linear projections (Q, K, V, O, MLP-up, MLP-down) compensates for the linearity constraint, matching or exceeding non-linear adapter baselines.

## Results

On Kinetics-400, ZeroI2V ViT-L/14 (CLIP, 32 frames) achieves 87.2% Top-1 accuracy with zero extra GFLOPs and zero new parameters, matching AIM (87.5%) while using ~30% fewer GFLOPs (7,783 vs 11,208) and 38M fewer parameters. On Something-Something V2, ZeroI2V ViT-L/14 (16 frames) reaches 71.4% Top-1, surpassing EVL (66.7%) and AIM (70.6%) with substantially lower computation (3,892 vs 9,641 and 11,508 GFLOPs). On smaller benchmarks, ZeroI2V ViT-L/14 (CLIP+K400) achieves 98.6% on UCF101 and 83.4% on HMDB51, outperforming ST-Adapter (98.1% and 81.7%), and 91.4% on Diving48, beating AIM (90.6%). On AVA action detection, ZeroI2V ViT-B/16 achieves 26.4 mAP versus 18.3 for the frozen CLIP baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PETL baselines, ZeroI2V consistently wins or ties while eliminating all extra inference cost. On K400, ZeroI2V ViT-L/14 (87.2%) nearly matches AIM (87.5%) with 3,425 fewer extra GFLOPs and 38M fewer new parameters; it matches EVL (86.3%) at 8 frames with zero extra parameters versus EVL's 58M. On SSv2, ZeroI2V ViT-L/14 (71.4% at 16 frames) clearly surpasses both EVL (66.7%) and AIM (70.6%) with 2–3× lower GFLOPs. On UCF101 and HMDB51, ZeroI2V ViT-L/14 (CLIP+K400) beats ST-Adapter by 0.5 and 1.7 points respectively (98.6 vs 98.1; 83.4 vs 81.7), and on Diving48 it edges out AIM (91.4 vs 90.6). Against full fine-tuning methods, ZeroI2V ViT-L/14 at 8 frames (86.3%) outperforms MViTv2-L (86.1%) with over 20× fewer GFLOPs (1,946 vs 42,420), and on SSv2 it improves over directly fine-tuned CLIP ViT-L/14 by 21.4 points (70.1 vs 48.7) at identical parameter and computation cost. The only comparison where ZeroI2V trails slightly is on VidTAB few-shot, where it scores 46.5 average vs ST-Adapter's 46.9, though it leads on the Emotion sub-task (30.6 vs 29.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) paradigm from NLP and image understanding to the image-to-video domain, building directly on LoRA's low-rank decomposition but converting its parallel structure into a serial Linear Adapter that can be merged into the original weights via structural reparameterization. It departs from prior video PETL methods (ST-Adapter, EVL, AIM) that all introduce extra inference-time computation or parameters for temporal modeling, by instead repurposing a subset of existing attention heads for temporal modeling (STDHA) at zero additional cost. The head-reassignment strategy draws on the head-redundancy findings of Michel et al. [44], while the zero-inference-cost merging is inspired by the reparameterization literature in efficient inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-extra-inference-cost property makes this approach particularly attractive for real-world deployment where latency and memory are constrained, and the finding that a purely linear adapter (no nonlinearity) can match non-linear adapters when applied densely challenges the assumption that nonlinearity is essential for cross-domain transfer. Limitations include that training cost remains comparable to other PETL methods (the savings are at inference, not training), and the approach is specific to ViT architectures with standard MHSA/MLP blocks. Open questions include whether the zero-cost constraint limits expressiveness for more complex video understanding tasks (e.g., dense prediction, long-form video) and whether the technique generalizes to non-Transformer backbones.
