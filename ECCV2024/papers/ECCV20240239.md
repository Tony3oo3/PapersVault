## Problem

Existing NeRF watermarking methods like CopyRNeRF and StegaNeRF embed binary messages in color representations, making them unrecoverable when the NeRF is recolorized—a common and increasingly accessible operation. This leaves original creators unable to claim ownership over recolorized versions of their NeRF models, creating a critical gap in digital asset protection for 3D scene representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240239/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an already-trained NeRF model (vanilla NeRF, InstantNGP, or TensoRF) and a 48-bit binary message as input, producing a watermarked NeRF where the message is embedded in geometry density values at surface-adjacent 3D points. For extraction, a VGG16-based CNN message extractor takes 400×400 patches of rendered 2D images and outputs the recovered binary message. The watermark is designed to survive both model-level recolorization (CLIP-based, palette-based) and image-level color modifications (hue jittering) without degrading recolorization quality.

## Method

GeometrySticker embeds binary messages into the geometry (density σ) component of NeRF rather than color, exploiting the fact that recolorization only modifies color representations. A lightweight MLP 'message sticker' converts the binary message into a one-dimensional embedding compatible with the target NeRF architecture's geometry representation. A learnable Laplace CDF adaptively selects 3D points near object surfaces (high geometry values) as cover media, ensuring subtlety by limiting modifications to a small fraction of the total representation. Messages are attached via simple addition (σ̃ = σ + ψ·m), making the approach architecture-agnostic and scalable across NeRF variants. Training jointly optimizes the message sticker, the CDF threshold parameter, and a VGG16-based message extractor using BCE message loss, MSE content loss, a watermark-detection classification loss, and a sparsity loss that pushes importance values toward binary extremes.

## Results

On Blender, GeometrySticker achieves 99.33–99.50% bit accuracy under color-jitter, CLIP-based, and palette-based recolorization, compared to ~50% (random chance) for all baselines. On LLFF, bit accuracy remains high at 96.50–97.12% across the same recolorization types. In terms of visual quality, GeometrySticker attains the best PSNR/SSIM (32.13/0.9999 on Blender, 30.82/0.9999 on LLFF) and lowest LPIPS (0.0136, 0.2165) among all methods, indicating minimal visual degradation from watermarking. Under standard 2D distortions (Table 2), GeometrySticker maintains ≥98.55% bit accuracy on both datasets, far exceeding CopyRNeRF (~63–66%) and StegaNeRF (57–93%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeometrySticker decisively outperforms all three baselines on bit accuracy under recolorization. HiDDeN+NeRF completely fails, with bit accuracy hovering around 50% (random chance) on both datasets, because image-level watermarks are destroyed by NeRF retraining. CopyRNeRF also drops to ~50% under CLIP-based recolorization (49.96% on Blender) since it embeds messages in the color representation, which is directly altered by recolorization; it is entirely incompatible with palette-based recolorization (N.A.). StegaNeRF performs slightly better than the other two (52–54% on Blender) but still fails under recolorization and is also incompatible with palette-based editing. GeometrySticker, by embedding messages in geometry rather than color, achieves 96–100% bit accuracy across all recolorization types and both datasets, while simultaneously delivering the best visual quality (highest PSNR/SSIM, lowest LPIPS). The method is also the only one compatible with all three recolorization schemes (color-jitter, CLIP-based, palette-based), underscoring its scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior NeRF watermarking methods (CopyRNeRF, StegaNeRF) that embed messages in color representations or require architecture-specific structural modifications, by instead attaching a lightweight MLP 'sticker' to geometry components via simple addition. The key conceptual shift is recognizing that since recolorization operates on color, the geometry channel is a natural and robust cover medium, and that a learnable Laplace CDF can select only surface-proximate 3D points to minimize visual distortion. The method repurposes standard volume rendering and ray marching infrastructure without altering the underlying NeRF architecture, positioning itself as a plug-and-play add-on rather than a retraining procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As NeRF models become shareable digital assets via platforms like NeRFStudio, the ability to verify ownership after color modification is a practical and timely need that prior watermarking methods cannot address. The demonstrated scalability across NeRF, InstantNGP, and TensoRF architectures, combined with compatibility with existing recolorization pipelines (CLIP-based, palette-based, image-level), makes this a deployable solution. However, the paper acknowledges vulnerabilities to adversarial attacks (PGD) and model purification, and future work is needed to extend the approach to geometry editing and emerging representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting, which use explicit point clouds rather than implicit neural fields.
