## Problem

Single-view 3D human reconstruction methods produce overly smooth surfaces in occluded body regions because no shape information is available for those areas, while traditional multi-view reconstruction requires calibrated, registered images from specialized capture systems inaccessible to general users. Existing approaches that leverage unconstrained images of the same person rely on parametric body models (e.g., SMPL) for cross-view correspondence, which introduces fitting noise and prevents the use of images with different clothing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target RGB image and a collection of monocular unconstrained images (uncalibrated, unregistered, with arbitrary pose and clothing variations) of the same individual. Rather than fusing RGB features directly, COSMU generates 2D multi-view normal maps registered to the target pose as intermediate representations, which avoids per-point color estimation and permits clothing differences among references. The final output is a complete 3D clothed human shape mesh extracted via marching cubes from the learned implicit function.

## Method

COSMU simulates a multi-view scenario through a two-stage module: (1) body part-based reference selection, which partitions unconstrained images into N_r orientation groups and selects N_p references per group by minimizing an energy combining orientation angle, body part visibility, and 3D bone pose similarity; and (2) body part-based registration, which classifies 3D points of coarse point clouds into N_p body parts via a pixel-aligned network and rigidly registers each reference body part to its target counterpart. The registered point clouds are rendered as 2D normal maps (one per simulated view plus the target), which are processed by a multi-view attention-based implicit model that computes per-view attention scores to fuse features without imposing a fixed view order. The final pixel-aligned implicit function regresses occupancy values from the fused multi-view features, trained with MSE loss on THuman2.0. No parametric body model is used at any stage, distinguishing COSMU from prior unconstrained-image methods.

## Results

On THuman3.0, COSMU achieves the best scores across all three metrics: Chamfer Distance (CD) 1.304 cm, Normal reprojection error 0.1475, and Point-to-Surface (P2S) distance 1.279 cm. On X-Humans, COSMU similarly leads with CD 2.006 cm, Normal 0.2146, and P2S 1.929 cm. The closest competitor, MVP-Human (4 views, SMPL-based), scores CD 1.348 / 2.063 and Normal 0.1558 / 0.2204 on the two datasets respectively, while single-view methods like PiFU lag substantially (CD 1.698 / 2.516). COSMU-2 (using only 2 views) already outperforms all 2-view baselines, confirming the benefit of leveraging real multi-view normals over estimated ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COSMU outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both THuman3.0 and X-Humans. Against the strongest competitor MVP-Human [58] (4 views, SMPL-based), COSMU reduces CD by 0.044 cm (1.304 vs 1.348) and Normal error by 0.0083 (0.1475 vs 0.1558) on THuman3.0, and by 0.057 cm / 0.0058 on X-Humans. Compared to the 2-view SMPL-based methods ECON [49] and Multi-PaMIR [56], COSMU achieves substantially lower CD (1.304 vs 1.368 / 1.401) and Normal error (0.1475 vs 0.1632 / 0.1650) on THuman3.0. Against single-view methods, the gap is largest: PiFU [40] scores CD 1.698 and Normal 0.1785, while PiFu-HD [41] and SuRS [37] improve to CD 1.501/1.573 but still trail COSMU by 0.2+ cm in CD. Notably, COSMU-2 (only 2 views) already beats all 2-view baselines, demonstrating that real multi-view normals from unconstrained images are more informative than normals estimated from a single input image.

## Relation to Prior Work

COSMU builds on the pixel-aligned implicit function paradigm introduced by PiFU and the U-Net-based super-resolution coarse shape estimation from SuRS, but departs from both single-view methods (which cannot recover occluded regions) and prior unconstrained-image methods (PaMIR, MVP-Human, CrossHuman, UnconFuse) that rely on SMPL parametric models for cross-image correspondence. The key conceptual departure is replacing parametric-model-guided feature fusion with a body-part-based rigid registration pipeline that simulates a calibrated multi-view scenario from uncalibrated, unregistered, pose- and clothing-varying images, using 2D normal maps rather than RGB to decouple geometry from appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for calibrated multi-view capture systems and parametric body models, COSMU makes high-quality complete 3D avatar reconstruction accessible from casual monocular image collections, a practical step toward democratizing avatar creation for metaverse and virtual try-on applications. However, the approach is contingent on having a sufficient set of reference images of the same individual, and it struggles with small body regions (face, hands) where registration and multi-view fusion are less reliable. Open questions include whether the method can be extended to temporally coherent dynamic reconstruction and whether the body-part registration can be made more robust to extreme pose or clothing differences.
