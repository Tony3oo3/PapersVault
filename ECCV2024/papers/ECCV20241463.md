## Problem

Compact face recognition models (e.g., MobileFaceNet) suffer from significantly lower verification accuracy than large SOTA models, and existing knowledge distillation methods either fix teacher class centers (suboptimal across training stages), require multi-phase student-teacher training, or force the student to imitate the exact teacher embedding space—ignoring the student's limited capacity to learn complex class-center relationships early in training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241463/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model (MobileFaceNet, 1.19M parameters) takes aligned 112×112 RGB face images normalized to [-1,1] and produces 512-dimensional L2-normalized face embeddings. The teacher (ResNet50, 43.59M parameters) is pre-trained and frozen; only its last-layer class center weights and per-sample feature representations are accessed during distillation—training samples are not passed through the teacher at inference time. Verification is performed via cosine similarity between embeddings, and identification via Rank-1 retrieval.

## Method

AdaDistill embeds knowledge distillation directly into the margin penalty softmax loss (ArcFace or CosFace) by replacing the student's learned class centers with adaptively estimated teacher class centers. The class center for each identity is updated every training iteration via an exponential moving average whose momentum α is set to the clipped positive cosine similarity between the student and teacher feature representations of the current sample. At early training (α≈0), the class center approximates the individual teacher sample embedding, so the student learns simpler sample-to-sample alignment; as training progresses (α→1), the center converges to the average of all teacher embeddings for that class, teaching more complex sample-to-class-center discrimination. A hard-sample weighting term further modulates α by the cosine similarity between the teacher feature and the current class center, pulling the center toward hard samples. This adaptive schedule requires no additional hyperparameters beyond the margin value and uses only the distillation loss (no separate classification loss or loss-weighting terms).

## Results

On the five small verification benchmarks (LFW, CFP-FP, AgeDB-30, CA-LFW, CP-LFW), AdaArcDistill achieves the best average accuracy of 95.43%, surpassing EKD (95.03%) and ReFO+ (not reported as average). On the large-scale IJB-C benchmark, AdaArcDistill reaches 93.27% TAR@FAR=1e-4, outperforming ReFO+ (92.41%), SH-KD (91.75%), and EKD (90.48%). On ICCV21-MFR, AdaArcDistill leads on Children (37.21%) and Mask (35.36%) test sets, while AdaCosDistill achieves the best MF-all score (60.56%). On MegaFace (R), AdaArcDistill attains 93.32% verification accuracy, competitive with ReFO+ (93.75%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaArcDistill outperforms all 13 baselines on IJB-C (93.27% vs. best baseline ReFO+ at 92.41%) and IJB-B (91.21% vs. best baseline EKD at 88.35%), with margins of +0.86% and +2.86% respectively. On ICCV21-MFR, AdaArcDistill leads on Children (37.21% vs. ReFO's 33.36%) and Mask (35.36% vs. ReFO's 31.88%), while AdaCosDistill achieves the best MF-all score (60.56% vs. ReFO+'s 59.17%). On the five small benchmarks, AdaArcDistill attains the highest average (95.43%), beating EKD (95.03%) and all other methods. On MegaFace (R) verification, AdaArcDistill (93.32%) is slightly behind ReFO+ (93.75%) and ReFO (93.80%), representing the one benchmark where the proposed method does not achieve the top score. Compared to the closest FR-specific KD baselines (ReFO/ReFO+), AdaDistill requires no student proxy, no reverse distillation, and no multi-phase training, while also not needing to tune loss-weighting hyperparameters beyond the standard margin value.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaDistill extends the margin-penalty softmax distillation line initiated by MarginDistillation [39], which fixed teacher-learned class centers and trained the student with a margin-based softmax loss. The key departure is replacing those fixed centers with an exponential moving average (EMA) that adaptively shifts from sample-to-sample distillation (early training) to sample-to-class-center distillation (late training), controlled by the student's own cosine similarity to the teacher. Unlike ReFO [27], which requires a student proxy and multi-phase reverse distillation, AdaDistill uses only the distillation loss as the training objective, eliminating the need for loss balancing or recursive training. The hard-sample weighting (Eq. 8) further refines the EMA by pulling the class center toward difficult samples identified by the teacher, a mechanism absent in prior margin-based distillation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdaDistill offers a practical, single-phase distillation recipe for compact face recognition models (e.g., MobileFaceNet) that avoids hyperparameter tuning beyond the standard margin value, making it straightforward to deploy on resource-constrained devices. The paper acknowledges that compact students still trail full-size SOTA models (e.g., ResNet50 teacher vs. MFN student on IJB-C: 96.05% vs. 93.27% TAR@FAR 1e-4), leaving open the question of whether adaptive distillation alone can close this capacity gap or whether architectural innovations are also needed. The observation that a mid-size teacher (ResNet50) yields better distillation than larger ones (ResNet100, TransFace-B) reinforces the 'teacher-student gap' hypothesis and suggests that future work should focus on matching teacher complexity to student capacity rather than simply scaling up the teacher.
