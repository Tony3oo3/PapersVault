## Problem

Existing sequential disentanglement methods (DSVAE and its extensions) assume independence between static and dynamic factors, but in practice the static content of a scene often causally influences the dynamics (e.g., object shape constraining motion). When this dependency exists, prior methods produce entangled codes and fail to recover a clean factorization. Additionally, the definition of static/dynamic factors lacked formal grounding, and no prior approach provided provable identifiability guarantees for the learned representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video sequence of T frames, first embedding each frame independently through a frozen pretrained convolutional autoencoder into a low-dimensional feature vector. From these frame features, it produces a single static code f (shared across all frames, extracted via an aggregated posterior that is inherently permutation-invariant) and per-frame dynamic codes λ_{1:T} (obtained as the output of a conditional normalizing flow). Reconstructed frames are generated by inverting the frozen autoencoder decoder, so no fine-tuning of the pixel-space decoder is needed.

## Method

The core architectural change replaces the standard VAE decoder with a Conditional Normalizing Flow (cNF) that models p(x_{1:T}|f), where the flow is conditioned on the static code and uses affine coupling with LSTM layers to capture temporal dynamics. This explicitly encodes the causal dependency between static and dynamic factors rather than assuming independence. A key training innovation is the shuffle constraint: during training, the cNF is conditioned on randomly permuted per-frame static codes f_{π(T)} rather than the aggregated code, which enforces time-invariance of the static code without any additional loss term. The resulting ELBO (Eq. 12) is shown via Propositions 1–3 to provably recover the ground-truth static and dynamic factors under mild assumptions, making the representation identifiably disentangled. The entire pipeline is self-supervised and modality-free, requiring no data augmentation, contrastive losses, adversarial training, or external models.

## Results

On datasets with dependent static-dynamic factors (c-dSprites, MPI3D, LPCSprites), the proposed method achieves near-perfect disentanglement accuracy (98.6–99.97%) across all factors, while the strongest baselines (CDSVAE, SPYL) drop to 33–74% on the most affected factors. On independent-factor datasets (MUG, s-dSprites, MHAD), the method performs on par with the best baselines (e.g., 84.32% action accuracy on MUG vs. SPYL's 85.71%; 98.91/98.98/98.76% on s-dSprites vs. SPYL's 97.76/98.72/98.12%). The intra-entropy H(y|x) is consistently the lowest among all methods (e.g., 0.008 on c-dSprites vs. 0.027–0.046 for baselines), indicating the most confident and consistent factor preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On independent-factor datasets (MUG, s-dSprites, MHAD), the proposed method is competitive but not dominant: it trails SPYL by ~1.4% on MUG action accuracy (84.32 vs. 85.71) and by ~1% on MHAD (67.65 vs. 68.63), while slightly leading on s-dSprites (98.91/98.98/98.76 vs. 97.76/98.72/98.12). It clearly outperforms CDSVAE, SKD, R-WAE, S3VAE, DSVAE, and MoCoGAN across all independent-factor benchmarks. The decisive advantage appears on dependent-factor datasets: on c-dSprites, shape accuracy jumps from 74.32% (CDSVAE) and 33.28% (SPYL) to 98.98%; on MPI3D, shape accuracy rises from 43.81% (CDSVAE) and 16.78% (SPYL) to 90.15%; on LPCSprites, body accuracy surges from 54.94% (CDSVAE) and 70.89% (SPYL) to 99.97%. SPYL, the strongest baseline, suffers the largest degradation under dependency (e.g., 33.32% on MPI3D camera and LPCSprites orientation), confirming that its contrastive MI estimation conflicts with the causal structure. The paper attributes these failures to baselines enforcing independence between codes, which contradicts the ground-truth causal mechanism and causes static information to leak into dynamic codes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DSVAE framework (Li & Mandt, 2018) for sequential static/dynamic disentanglement by replacing the VAE decoder with a Conditional Normalizing Flow (Winkler et al., 2019) and drawing identifiability conditions from the style/content disentanglement formalism of Yin et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure from prior methods (CDSVAE, SPYL, R-WAE, S3VAE) is rejecting the independence assumption between static and dynamic factors: instead of ablating the conditional link and adding complex mutual-information losses, the paper models the causal dependency explicitly through the cNF and enforces disentanglement via a simple shuffle of the aggregated posterior. The aggregated posterior (Bouchacourt et al., 2018) and β-VAE weighting (Higgins et al., 2016) are retained as architectural components, but the shuffle constraint subsumes the role of the elaborate self-supervised losses used by competing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that static and dynamic factors are frequently dependent in practice, and that enforcing independence is the root cause of failure in prior SOTA methods—reframes how the field should approach sequential disentanglement. The shuffle constraint's simplicity (no additional losses, no data augmentation, no external models) combined with provable identifiability makes the method broadly applicable and modality-free, potentially extending to audio or biological sequences as the authors note. However, the formalism's definition of 'static' as strictly time-invariant across all sequences may not align with human semantics (e.g., character orientation in LPCSprites), and the method cannot handle factors that are static in some sequences but dynamic in others. Open questions include whether per-sequence adaptive static codes are feasible and whether the identifiability guarantees hold under weaker assumptions or with finite data.
