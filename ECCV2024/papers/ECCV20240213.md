## Problem

Scaling up vision model size has become the default strategy for obtaining stronger visual representations, yet it remains unclear whether this is always necessary. This paper investigates whether scaling image resolution (multi-scale processing) on a smaller frozen model can match or exceed the performance of larger models, and under what conditions larger models retain an advantage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240213/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at a base resolution (e.g., 224×224), interpolates it to multiple scales (up to 1008×1008), and splits each larger scale into sub-images of the original training size. The output is a concatenated multi-scale feature map that preserves the original spatial dimensions (e.g., 16×16) while increasing the channel dimension (e.g., 1536 vs. 768), ensuring the token count fed to downstream decoders or LLMs remains unchanged regardless of how many scales are used.

## Method

S²-Wrapper is a parameter-free mechanism that extends any pre-trained frozen vision model to multiple image scales by interpolating the input to several resolutions, splitting each into sub-images of the original training size, processing all sub-images through the same model, merging sub-image features back into full-scale feature maps, average-pooling to the base spatial size, and concatenating across scales. Three key design choices make this efficient: splitting avoids quadratic self-attention cost and position-embedding interpolation artifacts; independent sub-image processing eliminates the need for window attention or new relative position embeddings; and pooling to the original spatial size keeps the output token count constant for downstream use. The paper further shows that pre-training (not just post-hoc application) with S² on ImageNet-21k closes the generalization gap on rare/hard examples, and that a single linear transform can reconstruct large-model features from multi-scale small-model features with near-100% information recovery, indicating comparable learning capacity.

## Results

On the V* benchmark for visual detail understanding, LLaVA-1.5-7B with S² scaling at 1008² resolution achieves 51.3 (Att) and 61.8 (Spa), matching GPT-4V on Att and surpassing it on Spa (60.5), while using only 576 image tokens. On ImageNet classification, pre-training a ViT-B with S² scaling yields 82.4% top-1 accuracy, exceeding the ViT-L baseline (81.6%). On semantic segmentation (ADE20K) and depth estimation (NYUv2), S² scaling from base-size models (ViT-B, DINOv2-B, OpenCLIP-B) consistently outperforms large or giant models with comparable GFLOPs. On robotic manipulation (cube picking), S² on MVP-B improves success rate by ~19% versus ~6% for scaling from base to large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4V, LLaVA-1.5-7B with S² scaling matches the V* Att score (51.3 vs. 51.3) and surpasses it on V* Spa (61.8 vs. 60.5), while also improving VQAv2 (80.0 vs. 77.2) and TextVQA (61.0 vs. 78.0 is lower, but the 13B+S² variant reaches 63.1). Against Gemini Pro, S² LLaVA-1.5-7B leads by 10.4 points on V* Att (51.3 vs. 40.9) and 2.6 on V* Spa. Against the LLaVA-1.5-7B baseline without S², scaling to 1008² improves V* Att by 7.8 points (51.3 vs. 43.5) and V* Spa by 5.2 points (61.8 vs. 56.6), while keeping the same 576 image tokens. On dense prediction tasks (ADE20K segmentation, NYUv2 depth), S² on ViT-B, DINOv2-B, and OpenCLIP-B outperforms their large or giant counterparts in 6 of 9 model-task combinations at matched GFLOPs; the sole failure is OpenCLIP classification where the giant model still wins. On feature reconstruction (Table 2), multi-scale ViT-B reconstructs 100.5% of ViT-L's test-set information (vs. 87.6% for single-scale ViT-B), and multi-scale OpenCLIP-B reconstructs 99.9% of OpenCLIP-L's training-set information (vs. 92.7% for single-scale). On robotic manipulation, S² on MVP-B yields a ~19% success-rate gain over single-scale MVP-B, compared to only ~6% from scaling MVP-B to MVP-L.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the long-standing multi-scale representation idea (from FPNs, U-Nets, and multi-scale ViTs) as a general, parameter-free scaling strategy applicable to any frozen pre-trained vision model, rather than designing a new architecture. It departs from the dominant 'scale up model size' paradigm in vision pre-training (ViT, DINOv2, OpenCLIP scaling laws) by showing that scaling input resolution on a smaller model is often more effective than scaling model width/depth. The closest prior work is Hu et al.'s long-sequence MAE, which scales image resolution only during pre-training; here S² is applied at inference time and also during pre-training, and is evaluated as a drop-in replacement for model-size scaling across diverse downstream tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen ViT-B with multi-scale input can match or exceed ViT-H/ViT-G across classification, segmentation, depth, MLLM, and robotics — with 3–14× fewer parameters — directly challenges the field's default assumption that larger models are necessary for stronger visual representations. The linear-reconstruction analysis (showing ~100% of a large model's feature information is recoverable from a multi-scale small model) provides a principled explanation for why this works and suggests the 'extra capacity' of large models is largely redundant. Open questions include whether S² pre-training generalizes beyond ImageNet-21k, how to efficiently select which scales to process per region (scale-selective attention), and whether the one failure case (OpenCLIP classification) reflects a fundamental limitation or a fixable pre-training recipe issue.
