## Problem

Modern detectors still miss a significant fraction of objects, particularly under heavy occlusion (17% of MOT17 detections are missed by YOLOX), causing online trackers to prematurely terminate valid trajectories. Existing trajectory-repair methods are offline—they modify past predictions or access future frames—making them incompatible with real-time deployment. The paper asks whether a fully online, plug-and-play module can persistently track undetected objects without altering prior tracking decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241965/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BUSCA receives only the unmatched tracks (those with no detection match in the current frame) along with their last Z=11 observations, a Kalman-filter predicted position, the Q=4 closest neighboring observations, and image crops for appearance features. It outputs a single assignment decision per track: either the track is matched to one of the generated proposals (updating its coordinates) or it is paused via the [Miss.] or [Halluc.] learned tokens. The entire module runs in 45 ms per frame with 8.7 M parameters, adding roughly 13 fps overhead when integrated with ByteTrack.

## Method

BUSCA frames the track-proposal association as a multi-choice question-answering task solved by an L-layer encoder Transformer. The proposal set combines three sources: a Kalman-filter candidate (motion-based), contextual proposals from the Q nearest neighboring observations, and two learned tokens ([Halluc.] for spurious tracks, [Miss.] for scene departure) that let the network decide when to pause. A novel Spatiotemporal Encoding (STE) replaces standard positional encoding by computing relative time, size, and distance embeddings against the track's last known observation, then projecting them into a 3-dimensional sinusoidal space to fuse with convolutional appearance features. The network is trained end-to-end on synthetic MOTSynth data only—no real MOT sequences or per-tracker fine-tuning—using a shared-weight MLP head that scores each proposal independently, making it agnostic to the number of proposals and the underlying tracker.

## Results

BUSCA consistently improves all five integrated trackers across MOT16, MOT17, and MOT20 test sets. The most dramatic gains appear with weaker baselines: CenterTrack+BUSCA achieves +12 HOTA and +21 IDF1 on MOT20, while TransCenter+BUSCA gains +5.1/+8.6 HOTA on MOT17/MOT20. With the strongest tracker (ByteTrack), BUSCA sets a new online SOTA of 79.3 MOTA and 63.1 HOTA on MOT17, outperforming all prior online methods. The system runs at ~13 fps with only 8.7M additional parameters, trained exclusively on synthetic MOTSynth data without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BUSCA+ByteTrack achieves the best online MOT performance on MOT17 (79.3 MOTA, 63.1 HOTA, 77.7 IDF1), surpassing CountingMOT (78.0/61.7/74.8), OC-SORT (76.0/61.7/76.2), and MO3TR-YOLOX (77.6/60.3/72.9). The most striking comparison is against end-to-end tracking-by-attention methods: TransCenter+BUSCA outperforms MO3TR-YOLOX by +1.4 HOTA and +1.2 IDF1 on MOT17, and by +1.5 HOTA and +3.4 IDF1 on MOT20, despite using a simpler TbD pipeline. CenterTrack+BUSCA shows the largest relative improvement (+12 HOTA, +21 IDF1 on MOT20), demonstrating that BUSCA is most impactful when the underlying tracker is weaker. Even with already-competitive trackers like GHOST and StrongSORT, BUSCA provides consistent small gains (+0.1 to +0.5 HOTA) while reducing IDSW. The paper also shows that naive alternatives (lowering detection threshold, pure IoU matching, mixed cues) all underperform BUSCA, with lower-score detections increasing false positives by +786 and pure IoU associations degrading MOTA by -1.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

BUSCA extends the tracking-by-detection (TbD) paradigm by introducing a Decision Transformer that formulates the association of unmatched tracks to proposals as a multi-choice question-answering task, drawing on the Transformer architecture from Vaswani et al. [52] and adapting its positional encoding into a novel 3D spatiotemporal encoding. It departs from prior trajectory-continuity methods (e.g., [11, 35, 44]) that repair fragmented tracks offline by altering past predictions or using future frames, positioning itself as the first fully online solution that neither modifies prior outputs nor accesses future information. The proposal generation leverages the Kalman filter standard in SORT-family trackers [3, 67] alongside contextual information from neighboring tracks and learned [Halluc.]/[Miss.] tokens, while the overall design is explicitly tracker-agnostic, building on the modular TbD pipeline rather than proposing an end-to-end alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BUSCA offers a practical, plug-and-play module that any online TbD tracker can adopt to handle detector failures without retraining or fine-tuning, which is significant for real-time deployment scenarios (autonomous driving, surveillance) where offline post-processing is infeasible. The fact that it is trained exclusively on synthetic data (MOTSynth) and transfers across five heterogeneous trackers without dataset-specific tuning suggests a degree of generalizability that reduces the engineering burden for practitioners. However, the gains on already-strong trackers (ByteTrack, StrongSORT, GHOST) are modest (+0.1–0.5 MOTA), and the method still assumes a Kalman-filter motion model that may struggle with extreme non-linear motions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D tracking, whether it can retroactively correct incorrect associations, and how it performs under camera motion or very long occlusion periods beyond the Z=11 frame context window.
