## Problem

Large-scale Transformer trackers (e.g., ViT-g backbones) demand multiple data-center GPUs and weeks of training, making them inaccessible to most research groups. While Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) via LoRA has proven effective for LLMs, its transfer to visual tracking is non-trivial: unshared positional embeddings for template and search tokens disrupt the pre-trained ViT structure that LoRA relies on, and convolutional heads introduce inductive biases that prevent convergence under low-rank adaptation.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a small template crop (112×112 or 196×196) and a larger search region crop (224×224 or 378×378) per frame, both projected into patch tokens and processed jointly through a single ViT encoder. The MLP-only head produces an anchor-free classification confidence map (per-patch IoU estimate) and a 4-channel bounding box offset map (left, top, right, bottom distances from each patch center); the final box is taken from the argmax patch of the confidence map after a Hanning window penalty.

## Method

LoRAT applies LoRA (rank 64) to all linear layers of a frozen DINOv2-pretrained ViT within the one-stream OSTrack architecture, but requires two architectural modifications for LoRA to work. First, positional embeddings are decoupled into a shared spatial component (inherited directly from the pre-trained ViT, adapted to the smaller template resolution via interpolation or top-left slicing) and a learned token-type component (three embeddings for template-foreground, template-background, and search tokens, inspired by BERT segment embeddings), preserving the pre-trained backbone's original structure. Second, the convolutional head is replaced with a three-layer MLP anchor-free head for both classification and box regression, eliminating the locality inductive bias that blocks LoRA convergence. The result is that ViT-g tracking becomes trainable on 25.8 GB of GPU memory (batch size 16), L-224 training drops from 35.0 to 10.8 GPU hours, and LaSOT SUC improves from 0.703 to 0.742 (L-224) or 0.762 (g-378) over the full-fine-tuned baseline.

## Results

LoRAT-g-378 sets a new state-of-the-art on LaSOT with 76.2 SUC, surpassing the previous best (ARTrack, 73.1) by 3.1%. On LaSOT-ext, LoRAT-L-378 achieves 56.6 SUC (vs. ARTrack's 52.8), and on GOT-10k, LoRAT-g-378 attains 78.9 AO. The lightest variant LoRAT-B-224 still reaches 71.7 SUC on LaSOT at 209 FPS, while the full ViT-g backbone can be trained on 8×V100 GPUs in 60 hours with only 25.8 GB peak memory (batch size 16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoRAT-g-378 outperforms the previous SOTA ARTrack [56] on all five benchmarks: +3.1 SUC on LaSOT (76.2 vs 73.1), +3.7 on LaSOT-ext (56.5 vs 52.8), +0.4 on TrackingNet (86.0 vs 85.6), +0.4 AO on GOT-10k (78.9 vs 78.5), and +2.4 on TNL2K (62.7 vs 60.3). Even the smallest variant LoRAT-B-224 (71.7 SUC) surpasses OSTrack [60] (71.1) and SwinTrack [38] (71.3) on LaSOT while running at 209 FPS versus their 130 and 45 FPS respectively. LoRAT-L-224 (74.2 SUC) beats DropTrack [57] (71.8) and SeqTrack [6] (72.5) on LaSOT by 2.4 and 1.7 points. On GOT-10k, LoRAT-g-378 (78.9 AO) leads all prior methods including ARTrack (78.5) and MixViT [9] (75.7). The aligned backbone comparison (Table 6) shows that simply swapping in DINOv2 weights into OSTrack [60] yields only 67.1 SUC (L-224), confirming that the decoupled embedding and MLP-head designs are essential for unlocking pre-trained ViT potential under LoRA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants LoRA, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique developed for large language models, into the visual tracking domain for the first time, building on the one-stream OSTrack architecture as its baseline. It departs from prior PEFT work by identifying two domain-specific obstacles that make naive LoRA transfer to tracking ineffective: the unshared positional encoding design in Siamese trackers disrupts the pre-trained ViT structure that LoRA relies on, and the inductive biases in convolutional heads prevent convergence under low-rank adaptation. The solution draws on BERT's token type embedding to decouple spatial position from token identity, and replaces the convolutional head with an MLP-only anchor-free head, both motivated by preserving the pre-trained backbone's original structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: LoRAT makes the largest ViT backbone (ViT-g) trainable for tracking on a single consumer GPU (25.8 GB, 11 hours on an RTX 4090 for the B-224 variant), democratizing access to large-scale pre-trained models that previously required multi-GPU data-center resources. The finding that convolutional heads catastrophically fail under LoRA (SUC dropping from 74.2 to 4.7) reveals a fundamental incompatibility between strong inductive biases and low-rank adaptation that may generalize beyond tracking. Open questions include whether the decoupled positional embedding strategy and MLP head design transfer to other tracking paradigms (two-stream, autoregressive) or to other PEFT methods, and whether the modest performance gains of LoRA over full fine-tuning (attributed to reduced catastrophic forgetting) persist with larger training data or longer training schedules.
