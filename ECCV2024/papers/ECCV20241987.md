## Problem

Prevailing 3D keypoint detection methods produce a single shared heatmap and rely on post-processing steps such as clustering or non-maximum suppression to isolate keypoints from high-confidence regions. These post-processing stages introduce parameter sensitivity, computational overhead from distance-matrix computations, and persistent issues of redundant or omitted detections that degrade both saliency and correspondence estimation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241987/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2,048 sampled 3D point coordinates as input and produces M=50 parallel predictions, each consisting of a per-point heatmap (R^{N_p}) and a probability vector. For keypoint saliency, the probability is binary (C=2); for correspondence estimation, it encodes semantic class membership (C=N_c+1). At inference, keypoints are extracted by selecting the argmax point from each heatmap whose probability exceeds a threshold, requiring no clustering or NMS.

## Method

KeypointDETR employs a transformer encoder-decoder trained with a bipartite matching loss derived from the Hungarian algorithm. The point-wise encoder stacks dynamic graph transformer modules that perform local self-attention over k-nearest neighbors in the learned feature space (not spatial space), progressively expanding the receptive field and producing heatmap features as intrinsic geometric descriptors. The query-wise decoder transposes these features into M query embeddings, applies multi-head self-attention across queries to enable global inter-keypoint interaction, and uses cross-attention between heatmap and probability branches to capture latent position-semantic relationships. The loss computes a cost matrix combining L1 heatmap dissimilarity and negative log-probability, resolves optimal assignment via the Hungarian algorithm, then applies Focal Loss on probabilities and MSE on matched heatmaps, with unmatched queries penalized only on probability.

## Results

On the KeypointNet dataset (four categories: airplane, chair, guitar, table; 2048 points per model), KeypointDETR achieves an average Hungarian mIoU of 93.81% and Chamfer Distance of 0.043 for keypoint saliency estimation at threshold ε=0.1, with per-category mIoU of 91.37% (airplane), 91.89% (chair), 93.96% (guitar), and 98.05% (table). For keypoint correspondence estimation, the method similarly leads all baselines in both Hungarian mIoU and Dual Alignment Score (DAS). The paper reports that KeypointDETR consistently outperforms all compared methods across the full range of distance thresholds (0–0.1) in both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For keypoint saliency estimation, KeypointDETR outperforms all six baselines—Skeleton Merger [3], UKPGAN [46], PointNet++ [28], PRA-Net [5], DGCNN [40], and Wei et al. [42]—in Hungarian mIoU across all four categories at ε=0.1. The advantage is attributed to the end-to-end set-prediction formulation that eliminates NMS/clustering post-processing, which the paper shows still produces omissions and redundant detections even with optimal NMS radius (0.1). For keypoint correspondence estimation, KeypointDETR beats PointConv [43], PointNet++ [28], DGCNN [40], PRA-Net [5], and Wei et al. [42] in both Hungarian mIoU and DAS, with the paper noting that no redundant same-label points appear near predicted keypoints. The ablation further confirms that the DGCNN encoder backbone (89.81% avg mIoU) is the strongest alternative, while PointNet++ (73.28%) and Point Transformer (80.45%) fall substantially behind, and replacing the transformer decoder with MLPs drops average mIoU from 93.81% to 91.67%.

## Relation to Prior Work

KeypointDETR adapts the DETR paradigm (bipartite matching loss, set prediction, transformer decoder) from 2D object detection to 3D point cloud keypoint detection, replacing the shared-heatmap-plus-NMS/clustering pipeline that dominates prior 3D keypoint methods (e.g., Wei et al., PRA-Net). The encoder draws on DGCNN's dynamic graph construction and Point Transformer's local self-attention, while the decoder introduces a query-wise self-attention mechanism over keypoint queries that has no direct analogue in prior 3D keypoint work. The shift from a single shared heatmap to per-keypoint multi-heatmaps is borrowed from 2D landmark detection but is the first application in the 3D point cloud setting with a DETR-style training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating post-processing (NMS, clustering) and its associated hyperparameter sensitivity, KeypointDETR offers a cleaner, more reproducible inference pipeline for 3D keypoint detection, which is practically important for downstream tasks like shape matching and registration. However, evaluation is confined to the KeypointNet dataset (four categories, 2048 points), leaving open questions about scalability to larger point clouds, more diverse categories, and real-time deployment. The ablation showing that direct coordinate regression (even with cascade offsets) substantially underperforms the heatmap-based formulation is a useful negative result that reinforces the value of the heatmap representation for 3D shapes.
