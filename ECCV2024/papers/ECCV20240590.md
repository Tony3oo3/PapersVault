## Problem

Existing learning-based ToF denoising methods process only a single frame, ignoring the temporal correlation between neighboring frames. Because MPI noise is regionally distributed and its spatial pattern shifts with camera perspective, the standard multi-frame pipeline of explicit alignment followed by fusion fails to capture useful geometric information from adjacent frames, leaving a clear gap in leveraging multi-frame data for ToF denoising.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240590/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes two temporally separated frames, each providing a ToF depth image and a corresponding amplitude (intensity) image, cropped to 384×512. The temporal gap between the two frames is dataset-dependent (3 intermediate frames for TFT3D and Cornell Box, 7 for HAMMER), simulating different camera motion speeds. The output is a single denoised ToF depth image at the same resolution as the input depth frames.

## Method

MTDNet replaces explicit alignment and fusion with a correlation-guided strategy built on two key modules. The Dual-Correlation Estimation Module (DCEM) constructs a cost volume from multi-scale features to compute intra-correlation (spatial relationships among geometric objects within a frame, aiding MPI residual initialization) and inter-correlation (variations in noise spatial distribution across the two frames, localizing strong ToF noise regions). The Confidence-guided Residual Regression Module (CRRM) is deployed at each of 6 pyramid scales in a coarse-to-fine manner, using the dual-correlation embeddings to jointly regress a depth residual map and a confidence map that prioritizes high-noise regions. A residual channel attention block fuses the multi-scale confidence-guided residuals into a coarse depth residual, and a kernel-prediction depth refinement module removes residual shot noise. Training is supervised with an L1 loss plus a Sobel-gradient term (λ=10) against ground-truth depth.

## Results

MTDNet achieves the lowest overall MAE across all three datasets: 0.82 cm on TFT3D (vs. 1.02 cm for SHARP-Net), 0.49 cm on Cornell Box (vs. 0.59 cm for SHARP-Net), and 0.097 cm on HAMMER (vs. 0.116 cm for SHARP-Net). The gains are most pronounced at the 4th quantile (highest-error regions where MPI is strongest), e.g., 2.03 cm vs. 2.55 cm on TFT3D and 0.68 cm vs. 0.96 cm on Cornell Box. On the realistic HAMMER dataset, MTDNet reduces the original MAE of 3.662 cm to 0.097 cm, outperforming all baselines at every error quantile.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTDNet outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and every error quantile. Against the strongest single-frame baseline SHARP-Net [7], MTDNet reduces overall MAE by 19.6% on TFT3D (0.82 vs. 1.02 cm), 16.9% on Cornell Box (0.49 vs. 0.59 cm), and 16.4% on HAMMER (0.097 vs. 0.116 cm). The advantage is largest at the 4th quantile (strongest MPI regions), where MTDNet achieves 2.03 cm vs. SHARP-Net's 2.55 cm on TFT3D and 0.68 cm vs. 0.96 cm on Cornell Box. RADU [30] is competitive at low-error quantiles (1st–2nd) but degrades significantly at the 4th quantile (3.27 cm on TFT3D, 1.51 cm on Cornell Box), indicating it struggles with complex geometric details. DeepToF [21] and ToF-KPN [27] are consistently the weakest baselines, with DeepToF performing particularly poorly on the realistic HAMMER dataset (1.211 cm overall MAE), likely due to its inability to handle transparent and reflective objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SHARP-Net's single-frame confidence-guided residual pyramid to a multi-frame setting by introducing a Dual-Correlation Estimation Module that repurposes the cost-volume mechanism from optical flow (PWC-Net) to estimate intra- and inter-frame correlations for ToF noise localization. It deliberately departs from the standard alignment-fusion-reconstruction pipeline used in multi-frame depth processing (e.g., EDVR, scene-flow alignment), arguing that explicit alignment fails for ToF because MPI noise is regionally distributed and its spatial pattern shifts with camera perspective. The depth refinement stage reuses the kernel prediction network from prior shot-noise removal work, and the residual channel attention block follows the SHARP-Net lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first learning-based multi-frame ToF denoising framework, this work opens the door to exploiting temporal redundancy in iToF sequences, which is particularly relevant for video-rate depth sensing in robotics and AR. However, the practical gains over the best single-frame baseline (SHARP-Net) are modest (~16–20% MAE reduction), and the method is limited to exactly two frames with a fixed intermediate-frame gap, raising open questions about scaling to longer sequences, real-time deployment, and generalization beyond iToF to dToF or hybrid sensors. The finding that alignment-based multi-frame strategies are fundamentally ill-suited for ToF denoising due to the regional, perspective-dependent nature of MPI is a useful negative result that could redirect future multi-frame depth work.
