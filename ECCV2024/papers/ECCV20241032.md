## Problem

State-of-the-art open-vocabulary segmentation models suffer over 12% mIoU degradation when moving from pinhole to 360° panoramic images, while existing panoramic segmentation is restricted to a small set of predefined classes and requires expensive dense panoramic annotations. This paper defines the Open Panoramic Segmentation (OPS) task, which simultaneously opens the field of view (360° panoramas), the vocabulary (unlimited classes), and the domain (trained on pinhole, evaluated on panoramas), and proposes a model and augmentation strategy to address all three challenges at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241032/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes pinhole RGB images from COCOStuff-164k (171 classes, 118k training images) with 640×640 crops and random short-side resizing in [320, 1024]. At inference, it takes full 360° equirectangular panoramas (e.g., 400×2048 for WildPASS, 512×1024 for Stanford2D3D and Matterport3D) and produces pixel-wise open-vocabulary semantic segmentation masks by multiplying mask proposals from the adapter with proposal logits from the frozen CLIP text encoder, enabling recognition of arbitrary class names not present in the training set.

## Method

OOOPS pairs a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with a Deformable Adapter Network (DAN) of Transformer layers that fuses intermediate CLIP features and outputs both mask proposals and proposal logits. The key component, the Deformable Adapter Operator (DAO), extends DCNv3's grouped deformable convolution with a per-pixel salient scalar s(p) computed as the standard deviation of softmax-normalized cosine similarities between a center pixel and its kernel neighbors, thereby amplifying edge pixels where panoramic distortion is strongest. A novel data augmentation, Random Equirectangular Projection (RERP), divides each pinhole training image into four patches, randomly shuffles them, and applies equirectangular projection to inject panorama-like geometric distortion into the source domain, teaching the model to handle spherical warping before ever seeing a true panorama. Training uses cross-entropy for mask recognition plus dice and binary cross-entropy losses for mask proposal generation, with 60k iterations at batch size 32 on 4×A40 GPUs.

## Results

On WildPASS, OOOPS with RERP achieves 58.0% mIoU and 59.7% open mIoU, surpassing the strongest open-vocabulary baselines SAN (55.6%), CAT-seg (55.7%), and OpenSeeD (55.8%) by +2.2–2.4% mIoU. On Stanford2D3D, OOOPS with RERP reaches 41.1% mIoU (+2.4% over SAN's 38.5%), and on Matterport3D it achieves 31.2% mIoU (+0.6% over OpenSeeD's 30.6%). The model uses only 8.7M learnable parameters (vs. 59.5M for CAT-seg and 65.4M for OpenSeeD) while being trained exclusively on pinhole COCOStuff-164k images and evaluated zero-shot on 360° panoramas.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-vocabulary methods, OOOPS with RERP consistently outperforms all three baselines across all three datasets: +2.4% mIoU over SAN, +2.3% over CAT-seg, and +2.2% over OpenSeeD on WildPASS; +2.4–2.6% on Stanford2D3D; and +0.6–1.0% on Matterport3D. The gains are most pronounced on challenging small-object categories: crosswalk improves by +9.3% over SAN (19.4% vs 10.1%) and curb by +5.5% (8.3% vs 2.8%). OOOPS also uses far fewer parameters (8.7M) than CAT-seg (59.5M) and OpenSeeD (65.4M) while achieving higher accuracy. Against close-vocabulary panoramic methods (ECANet at 69.0%, PASS at 64.7%), OOOPS still trails in overall mIoU, but it surpasses them on individual categories like sidewalk, person, and bus, and uniquely handles unlimited class labels. The DA subtask comparison (Table 4) shows that when target-domain data is additionally provided, OOOPS gains over +14% mIoU, confirming OPS is a strictly harder setting than standard domain adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OOOPS is architecturally built on SAN's side-adapter design for open-vocabulary segmentation, with a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone providing zero-shot class recognition. The key methodological departure is the Deformable Adapter Operator (DAO), which extends DCNv3's grouped deformable convolution with a pixel-wise salient scalar derived from patch-level cosine-similarity statistics, specifically targeting the edge distortion characteristic of equirectangular panoramas. The paper also introduces RERP, a data augmentation that randomly shuffles four pinhole image patches before applying equirectangular projection, a technique with no direct precedent in either the deformable-convolution or panoramic-segmentation literatures. Conceptually, the work sits at the intersection of three previously separate threads: open-vocabulary segmentation (pinhole, close-domain), panoramic segmentation (close-vocabulary, panoramic-domain), and unsupervised domain adaptation (pinhole-to-panoramic), and explicitly distinguishes OPS from DA by forbidding any target-domain data during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OPS task formulation addresses a practical bottleneck: densely annotated panoramic labels are expensive and close-vocabulary settings cannot cover the unbounded class space of real-world scenes. By training only on pinhole COCOStuff-164k and evaluating zero-shot on panoramas, the paper demonstrates that a ~10% mIoU degradation (vs. ~12% for baselines) is achievable, narrowing but not closing the gap to supervised close-vocabulary panoramic methods (58.0% vs. 69.0% mIoU on WildPASS). Open questions include whether the domain gap can be further reduced with more aggressive distortion-aware pre-training, how the approach extends to video panoramas or 3D scene understanding, and whether the RERP augmentation generalizes beyond equirectangular projections to other panoramic representations (cubemap, fisheye). The modest parameter overhead (8.7M vs. SAN's 8.4M) makes the approach practical for deployment.
