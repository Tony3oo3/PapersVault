## Problem

Online perception models for autonomous vehicles degrade significantly in unfamiliar urban areas, under occlusion, or in extreme lighting, yet existing solutions either depend on expensive manual HD-map annotations or on LiDAR point clouds from past traversals that lack semantic richness. No prior framework automatically generates semantically and geometrically rich static priors from camera-only historical data that can be seamlessly plugged into diverse online perception models without architectural modifications.

## Input / Output

The offline NeRF construction consumes 12 Hz RGB video sequences from six synchronized cameras with associated poses (no LiDAR), augmented with DINO pixel features (compressed from 764 to 64 dims) and SegFormer segmentation masks for dynamic-object exclusion. At deployment, the online perception model receives real-time camera images plus pre-built featurized prior voxels—compact 3D voxel grids carrying per-voxel semantic features (only 3.5 GB for Boston Seaport, versus 38 GB for Neural Map Prior's rasterized BEV features). The integrated models output vectorized or rasterized local HD maps (100×50 m range) or per-voxel 3D semantic occupancy predictions.

## Method

PreSight partitions the city into ~1 km² tiles, each containing multiple Instant-NGP sub-fields (multi-resolution hash grids + small MLP) optimized in parallel with per-video embeddings to handle lighting variation and a position-independent sky head. Semantic knowledge is distilled from DINO features via a direction-invariant feature head, while dynamic objects are masked using SegFormer during training; the NeRF is optimized self-supervisedly with L2 color/feature losses, BCE sky loss, and Mip-NeRF 360-style interlevel and distortion losses. A one-time ray-marching extraction identifies occupied surface voxels (first point where cumulative transmittance × opacity exceeds 0.5) and aggregates their semantic features via voxel-based downsampling, producing compact featurized prior voxels. At inference, a lightweight CNN integration module encodes these priors into BEV or 3D features and concatenates them with the online model's existing features, requiring no changes to the base architecture and adding negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset with a geographically disjoint train/test split, PreSight priors yield substantial gains across all evaluated models. For HD map construction, StreamMapNet's mAP jumps from 11.10 to 25.72 (+14.62), MapTR from 7.67 to 23.12 (+15.45), and BEVFormer's mIoU from 25.84 to 34.28 (+8.44). For occupancy prediction, BEVDet's overall mIoU improves from 29.3 to 33.7 and FB-OCC from 30.0 to 34.3, with static-category mIoU gains of +12.3 and +11.5 respectively, while dynamic-category performance remains essentially unchanged. Runtime overhead is minimal, with FPS dropping by less than 2 across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreSight is compared against three categories of baselines: no prior, LiDAR point-cloud priors (HindSight [49]), and Neural Map Prior (NMP [45]). In HD map construction, PreSight priors substantially outperform LiDAR priors (e.g., StreamMapNet: 25.72 vs. 16.87 AP; MapTR: 23.12 vs. 15.93 AP) and Neural Map Prior (BEVFormer: 34.42 vs. 26.50 IoU), while also being far more compact (3.5 GB vs. 38 GB for NMP). In occupancy prediction, PreSight matches LiDAR priors on overall mIoU (BEVDet: 33.7 vs. 33.1; FB-OCC: 34.3 vs. 34.3) while preserving dynamic-object accuracy (dynamic mIoU 24.4 vs. 21.7 for BEVDet), which LiDAR priors degrade due to their inability to distinguish static from dynamic content. All baseline models (StreamMapNet, MapTR, BEVFormer, BEVDet, FB-OCC) show consistent improvements when augmented with PreSight priors, confirming the framework's model-agnostic compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

PreSight sits at the intersection of city-scale NeRF reconstruction (building on Instant-NGP's hash-grid sub-fields and EmerNeRF's self-supervised dynamic/static separation with DINO feature distillation) and multi-traversal prior generation for autonomous driving. It departs from prior NeRF-based driving work (StreetSurf, UniSim, OccNeRF) that either requires LiDAR, targets novel view synthesis, or uses NeRF as a ground-truth oracle, by instead treating NeRF as an offline prior *generator* whose extracted voxel features are injected into existing online perception models. Compared to HindSight (LiDAR point clouds) and Neural Map Prior (neural map representations requiring 38 GB), PreSight produces camera-only, semantically rich priors at a fraction of the memory cost (3.5 GB) while remaining model-agnostic across vectorized and rasterized HD-map and occupancy architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: extending AV perception to new cities without the costly manual annotation pipeline that underpins traditional HD maps. The consistent gains across four different perception models and two tasks, with static categories improving dramatically while dynamic categories remain unaffected, validate the prior-as-supplement paradigm. Key limitations include the dependence on accurate GPS/IMU poses (problematic for crowdsourced data) and the requirement of prior traversals, which constrains truly first-visit scenarios. Open questions include whether 3D Gaussian Splatting or other implicit representations can reduce the 20-hour-per-tile training cost, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the single nuScenes benchmark to more diverse urban environments.
