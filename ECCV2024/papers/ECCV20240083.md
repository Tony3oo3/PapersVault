## Problem

Pre-trained visual models are overwhelmingly trained on sRGB images, creating a significant domain gap when applied to camera RAW data. Existing approaches either treat the ISP pipeline and downstream network as independent modules with no interaction, or replace the ISP entirely with heavy neural networks (e.g., stacked residual UNets), incurring substantial computational overhead. The paper asks whether a lightweight, adapter-based strategy can effectively bridge RAW inputs to sRGB pre-trained models while preserving ISP prior knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-camera RAW image (Bayer-pattern sensor data) as input and produces a machine-vision-oriented sRGB image I_5 that feeds into a standard detection or segmentation backbone. Uniquely, intermediate ISP-stage images (I_1 through I_4, spanning gain/denoise, white balance, and CCM stages) are also extracted as feature maps and injected into the backbone via model-level adapters. The final outputs are object detection bounding boxes (RetinaNet/Sparse-RCNN) or per-pixel semantic segmentation masks (SegFormer), with the total adapter overhead being only 0.2M–0.8M parameters versus ~25M–197M for the backbone.

## Method

RAW-Adapter introduces two complementary adapter types. Input-level adapters preserve the traditional ISP pipeline structure (demosaicing, gain & anisotropic Gaussian denoising, adaptive Minkowski-distance white balance, learnable 3×3 CCM, and NILUT-based 3D LUT color mapping) but replace fixed parameters with Query Adaptive Learning (QAL) blocks—small attention modules that predict ISP hyperparameters (gain ratio, kernel axes r₁/r₂, Minkowski exponent ρ, CCM offsets) from down-scaled image features via self-attention with learnable queries. Model-level adapters extract convolutional features from each intermediate ISP stage (I₁–I₄), concatenate them, pass through residual blocks, and merge the resulting adapter vectors into backbone stages 1–3 via concatenate-and-residual merge blocks, allowing the network to incorporate ISP prior knowledge at multiple depths. The entire adapter stack is trained end-to-end with ImageNet pre-trained backbone weights, using standard task losses (focal loss for detection, cross-entropy for segmentation), requiring no human-vision-oriented auxiliary losses.

## Results

On PASCAL RAW with ResNet-50, RAW-Adapter achieves 89.6 mAP (normal), 89.4 mAP (dark), and 86.6 mAP (over-exposure), outperforming the strongest joint-training baseline Dirty-Pixel (89.0/89.0/83.6) by up to 3.0 mAP in over-exposure. On ADE20K RAW with MIT-B5, RAW-Adapter reaches 48.38 mIOU (normal), 47.06 mIOU (over-exp), and 41.82 mIOU (dark), surpassing Dirty-Pixel (47.76/46.80/40.02) and all ISP-only baselines. On the real-world low-light LOD dataset, RAW-Adapter achieves 62.2 mAP with RetinaNet and 59.2 mAP with Sparse-RCNN, beating Dirty-Pixel by 0.6 and 0.4 mAP respectively. The method adds only ~0.2–0.8M parameters, far fewer than Dirty-Pixel's 4.28M or SID's 11.99M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAW-Adapter consistently outperforms all six baselines across every lighting condition and both tasks. Against the strongest joint-training competitor Dirty-Pixel [18], RAW-Adapter gains +0.6 mAP (normal) and +3.0 mAP (over-exp) on PASCAL RAW ResNet-50, and +0.6/+0.4 mAP on LOD with RetinaNet/Sparse-RCNN. Traditional ISP baselines (Karaimer [35], Lite-ISP [79]) degrade severely under over-exposure (73.5–74.7 mAP vs. 86.6 for RAW-Adapter), while low-light-specific ISPs (SID [6], DNF [34]) are only evaluated on the dark/over-exp split and trail RAW-Adapter by 4–5 mAP. InvISP [71] performs well on normal-light segmentation (47.53 mIOU) but collapses to 4.87 mIOU under dark conditions, highlighting its poor generalization. Notably, RAW-Adapter with a smaller MIT-B3 backbone (45.16M params) achieves 40.52 mIOU in dark conditions, surpassing Dirty-Pixel with the larger MIT-B5 backbone (40.02 mIOU at 86.29M params), demonstrating superior parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAW-Adapter sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the line of work on making ISP pipelines differentiable or replacing them with neural networks for downstream vision tasks (VisionISP, Hardware-in-the-loop, Dirty-Pixel, SID), and (2) the adapter/prompt-tuning paradigm from NLP and CV (Houlsby et al., Visual Prompt Tuning, ViT-Adapter). It departs from prior ISP-integration methods by preserving the traditional ISP structure while making it lightweight and differentiable via Query Adaptive Learning and Implicit Neural Representations, and by introducing model-level adapters that inject ISP intermediate-stage features into the backbone — a connectivity mechanism absent from all prior joint-training approaches. The paper also explicitly builds on the Shades of Gray white-balance formulation and NILUT for color manipulation, repurposing them within a task-oriented rather than human-vision-oriented pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: sRGB pre-trained models (ImageNet, COCO) perform significantly worse when applied to RAW data, and prior solutions either discard pre-trained weights or add heavy neural-network ISP replacements (Dirty-Pixel adds ~4.3M params). RAW-Adapter's 0.2–0.8M parameter overhead and consistent gains across detection and segmentation under normal, dark, and over-exposed conditions make it a lightweight, broadly applicable adapter for the growing ecosystem of RAW-based vision tasks. Open questions include whether a single unified model can handle all lighting conditions without per-scenario retraining, and whether multi-task decoders can further exploit the shared ISP-adapter representations. The approach is also limited to specific camera sensors in its experiments, and generalization to unseen sensor characteristics remains untested.
