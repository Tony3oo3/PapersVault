## Problem

Existing ViT-based diffusion models (DiT, MDT) condition on the denoising time step via AdaLN, which predicts channel-wise shift, scale, and gate parameters for both self-attention and MLP layers. This approach inflates parameter count and fails to capture the joint spatial-temporal dynamics of denoising, where the network must shift focus from low-frequency structure early on to high-frequency details later. The paper asks whether a more principled, parameter-efficient mechanism can integrate temporal information directly into the attention computation itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes image-shaped Gaussian noise (or VAE-encoded latent noise for the latent variant) plus a class label (for conditional generation) and a continuous time step, and produces a denoised image at the target resolution. Two variants are provided: a latent-space model operating on 32×32 VAE latents (yielding 256×256 or 512×512 images) and an image-space model operating directly on 32×32 or 64×64 RGB pixels. The time step is encoded via positional embeddings passed through a small MLP with Swish activation to produce a single time token that is broadcast to every transformer block.

## Method

The core contribution is Time-dependent Multihead Self-Attention (TMSA), which replaces AdaLN conditioning by projecting both spatial tokens and the time token into the query, key, and value spaces: q_s = x_s·W_qs + x_t·W_qt (analogously for k and v). This makes Q, K, V linear functions of both spatial and temporal information, allowing attention to dynamically reweight spatial relationships at each denoising step while requiring only three temporal projection matrices per block (vs. six AdaLN parameters). The full DiffiT architecture is a U-shaped encoder-decoder for image-space generation (with 3×3 conv + GroupNorm + Swish preceding each transformer block) or a plain ViT for latent-space generation, with optional window-based local attention to reduce quadratic cost. The model achieves FID 1.73 on ImageNet-256 with 561M parameters—19.85% fewer than MDT and 16.88% fewer than DiT—while also setting SOTA on CIFAR-10 (FID 1.95) and FFHQ-64 (FID 2.22) in image space.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, latent DiffiT achieves a new SOTA FID of 1.73, outperforming MDT-G (1.79), SiT-XL (2.06), and DiT-XL/2-G (2.27) while using 19.85% fewer parameters than MDT and 16.88% fewer than DiT. On ImageNet-512, DiffiT achieves FID 2.67, improving over DiT-XL/2-G (3.04) but trailing StyleGAN-XL (2.41). In image space, DiffiT achieves FID 1.95 on CIFAR-10 and 2.22 on FFHQ-64, surpassing EDM (1.99/2.39) and DDPM++ (3.01/3.39) under VP settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-256, DiffiT achieves the best FID (1.73) among all 20 compared methods, beating the closest Transformer-based diffusion baselines MDT-G (1.79) and SiT-XL (2.06) by 0.06 and 0.33 FID respectively, and DiT-XL/2-G (2.27) by 0.54 FID. On ImageNet-512, DiffiT (FID 2.67) outperforms DiT-XL/2-G (3.04) and ADM-G-U (3.85) but is outperformed by StyleGAN-XL (2.41), though the paper notes GANs suffer from low diversity not captured by FID. In image space on CIFAR-10, DiffiT (FID 1.95) surpasses EDM-VP (1.99), EDM-VE (2.01), LSGM (2.01), DDPM++-VP (3.01), U-ViT (3.11), and GenViT (20.20). On FFHQ-64, DiffiT (FID 2.22) outperforms EDM-VP (2.39), EDM-VE (2.53), and DDPM++-VP (3.39). DiffiT also achieves the highest Recall (0.62) on ImageNet-256, indicating better sample diversity than MDT-G (0.61) and DiT-XL/2-G (0.57).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffiT extends the Transformer-based diffusion model lineage initiated by DiT (which replaced the U-Net backbone with a ViT) and the latent diffusion framework of LDM. Its key conceptual departure is from the AdaLN conditioning mechanism used in DiT, MDT, and SiT, which modulates features via channel-wise shift, scale, and gate parameters predicted from time embeddings. Instead, DiffiT introduces TMSA, which injects temporal information directly into the query, key, and value projections of self-attention, enabling the attention mechanism itself to adapt across denoising steps. The training objective and sampling procedure remain standard DDPM/SDE formulations, so the contribution is architectural rather than algorithmic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TMSA mechanism offers a drop-in, parameter-efficient alternative to AdaLN that practitioners can adopt in existing Transformer-based diffusion pipelines, as demonstrated by the ablation on DDPM++. The ~20% parameter reduction while achieving a new SOTA FID of 1.73 on ImageNet-256 is practically significant for deployment and training cost. Open questions include whether TMSA's advantage persists at substantially larger model scales, whether it transfers to video or 3D generation, and whether the windowed-attention variant can be further optimized for very high resolutions without the U-Net skip-connection crutch.
