## Problem

Deep learning-based multi-exposure HDR imaging methods have achieved strong accuracy through increasingly complex attention and transformer mechanisms, but their large computation cost and inference delay prevent deployment on resource-constrained mobile devices. Meanwhile, traditional alignment-then-fusion pipelines remain error-prone under large motion and severe saturation. The paper addresses the gap between SOTA reconstruction quality and practical efficiency by asking whether a selective, region-aware approach can match or exceed transformer-based accuracy at a fraction of the cost.

## Input / Output

The network takes three LDR images at different exposures, converts each to the linear HDR domain via gamma correction (γ=2.2) and exposure-time division, then concatenates the gamma and linear versions into 6-channel tensors. Optical flow and selection masks are predicted at half input resolution and upsampled; the final HDR output is produced in the linear domain. During training, the two-stage pipeline uses 512×512 patches for the fusion stage and 128×128 patches for the refinement stage, unified via a window partition cropping scheme.

## Method

SAFNet's core insight is that not all regions in non-reference LDR frames require precise alignment: over/under-exposed areas or regions already well-captured by the reference frame can be discarded, while only regions containing unique valuable texture need flow estimation. A shared pyramid encoder extracts 4-level features (40 channels), and a parameter-shared coarse-to-fine decoder jointly refines one-channel selection probability masks and cross-exposure optical flow at each scale, so that masks guide where to estimate flow and better flow in turn improves mask prediction. The fused HDR image is produced explicitly by backward-warping the linear-domain LDR inputs and combining them with mask-reweighted fusion coefficients (unselected weight is transferred to the reference frame). A lightweight refine network with dilated residual blocks at full resolution then compensates missing high-frequency details, leveraging the flow, masks, and initial HDR prediction as additional inputs. Training uses a μ-law tonemapped weighted L1 + VGG perceptual loss on the refined output, plus an auxiliary L1 + census loss on the merged image, with a window partition cropping strategy that assigns larger patches to the fusion stage and smaller patches to the refinement stage.

## Results

On the Kalantari 17 test set, SAFNet achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five metrics: PSNR-μ 44.66 dB, PSNR-l 43.18 dB, SSIM-μ 0.9932, SSIM-l 0.9917, and HDR-VDP2 66.93. On the more challenging Challenge123 dataset (with ~7× larger motion magnitude and ~3× higher saturation ratio), SAFNet trained at 512×512 reaches PSNR-μ 41.88 dB and PSNR-l 29.73 dB, outperforming the next best method (AHDRNet) by 1.27 dB in PSNR-μ. In terms of efficiency, SAFNet runs in 0.151 s at 1500×1000 resolution with only 1.12M parameters and 0.976T FLOPs, making it approximately 10×, 18×, and 23× faster than FlexHDR, HDR-Transformer, and SCTNet respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFNet outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric in both Kalantari 17 and Challenge123. Against the strongest transformer-based competitors, SAFNet surpasses SCTNet [40] by 0.17 dB PSNR-μ and 0.89 dB PSNR-l while running 23× faster (0.151 s vs 3.466 s), and beats HDR-Transformer [24] by 0.34 dB PSNR-μ and 1.00 dB PSNR-l at 18× lower latency. Compared to the alignment-based FlexHDR [2], SAFNet gains 0.31 dB PSNR-μ and 0.58 dB PSNR-l with 10× speedup. On the more challenging Challenge123 dataset, the gap widens: SAFNet exceeds AHDRNet [44] by 1.27 dB PSNR-μ and NHDRRNet [45] by 4.44 dB PSNR-μ, confirming that the selective alignment strategy is particularly effective under large motion and heavy saturation. The lightweight SAFNet-S variant matches NHDRRNet [45] in speed (0.049 s vs 0.045 s) yet achieves 1.71 dB higher PSNR-μ, demonstrating a superior speed-accuracy trade-off across the entire efficiency spectrum.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFNet sits at the intersection of two established HDR paradigms: the alignment-then-fusion pipeline (Kalantari 2017, FlexHDR, HyHDRNet) and attention-based end-to-end fusion (AHDRNet, HDR-Transformer, SCTNet). It departs from the first by introducing jointly refined selection probability masks that gate where optical flow is estimated, avoiding the error-prone flow computation in saturated or redundant regions. It departs from the second by rejecting complex attention mechanisms entirely in favor of a lightweight pyramid encoder-decoder with explicit linear-domain fusion, drawing architectural inspiration from optical flow networks (PWC-Net, FastFlowNet) rather than vision transformers. The window partition cropping method is a novel training strategy that unifies two different patch sizes for the two-stage network, addressing the long-range vs. local-detail tension in large-motion HDR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–23× speedup over transformer-based SOTA while matching or exceeding accuracy makes multi-exposure HDR practically deployable on mobile devices, which is the paper's stated motivation from vivo. The Challenge123 dataset (7× larger motion, 3× larger saturation than prior benchmarks) is a useful contribution that exposes the limitations of patch-based transformer methods under extreme displacement. However, the explicit fusion in Eq. 5 fundamentally cannot synthesize content occluded in all input frames, and the lightweight refine module compensates only partially; whether the selective-alignment idea scales beyond three frames or to video HDR remains open. The approach also assumes a fixed reference frame, limiting its applicability to unordered exposure sets.
