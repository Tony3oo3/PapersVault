## Problem

Open-source vision-language models (VLMs) fail to follow visual prompts such as bounding boxes or segmentation masks, producing answers that are nearly identical whether or not the visual markers are present. Existing solutions to this grounding gap either rely on proprietary models like GPT-4V or require expensive fine-tuning on curated visual-prompt data, making them impractical for the broader open-source VLM ecosystem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242112/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a text prompt or question, and bounding box proposals (either provided as visual prompts or generated by GroundingDINO-B from noun phrases extracted via spaCy, filtered at 0.3 confidence). At decoding time, it produces a modified token probability distribution that amplifies the contribution of the target region, yielding generated text answers, region-text alignment scores for re-ranking, or image-text alignment scores for generated-image evaluation. The key operational detail is that the model is run twice per token—once on the original image and once on a version with the target region blacked out—making the method training-free but computationally 2× the base inference cost.

## Method

CRG extends classifier-free guidance (CFG) from diffusion models to autoregressive VLM decoding by contrasting the model's output distribution on the original image against its distribution on a version where the target region is blacked out. Concretely, at each decoding step the sampling distribution is softmax[(1+α)·logit(y_t|I,X,y_<t) − α·logit(y_t|mask(I,b),X,y_<t)] with α=1, which factors out the model's prior bias (its answer in the absence of the region's visual evidence) and retains only the answer that depends on that region. The masking strategy of blacking out each object's bounding box separately outperforms alternatives including segmentation masks, combined multi-object masks, and full-image blackout, as validated on What'sUp. When no explicit region is given, CRG uses GroundingDINO to propose boxes for all noun phrases and either guides generation (VQA) or scores/forces-decodes candidate texts (alignment, re-ranking), selecting the region that produces the largest contrast when removed.

## Results

On ViP-Bench (visual prompt following), LLaVA-1.6-34B + CRG achieves 56.1% average accuracy across six task categories, surpassing GPT-4V (55.9%) and improving over the base model by 11.1 points. On What'sUp's hardest 'Set of 4' spatial reasoning setting, CRG boosts LLaVA-1.6-34B from 54.0% to 64.0% (+10 points). On SugarCrepe's compositional splits, CRG improves LLaVA-1.6-34B by 11.5% (SWAP-OBJ: 87.8%) and 7.5% (SWAP-ATT: 93.7%), exceeding GPT-4V by 4.7 and 3.6 points respectively. On SeeTRUE text-to-image evaluation, CRG increases AUROC by 8.4 and F1 by 6.7 points for LLaVA-1.6-34B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRG (LLaVA-1.6-34B) achieves 56.1% on ViP-Bench, narrowly surpassing GPT-4V (55.9%) and substantially outperforming all fine-tuned visual prompting baselines: Shikra 7B (33.7%), GPT4ROI 7B (35.1%), Qwen-VL-Chat (39.2%), and InstructBLIP-13B (35.8%). Set-of-Mark (SoM) prompting, which works on GPT-4V, consistently degrades LLaVA-based models (e.g., 42.0% vs. 45.0% base on LLaVA-1.6-34B), while CRG improves the same model by 11.1 points. CRG is also complementary to the fine-tuned ViP-LLaVA-13B, adding 0.8% average improvement (48.6→49.4%), and on LLaVA-1.6-34B+CRG surpasses ViP-LLaVA in all categories except LANG. On What'sUp, CRG with LLaVA-1.5-13B (37.2% Set-of-4) outperforms ViP-LLaVA-13B (21.8%) and FLAVA (0.0%), and on SugarCrepe SWAP-OBJ, LLaVA-1.5-13B+CRG (84.5%) exceeds GPT-4V (83.1%). On SeeTRUE, CRG with LLaVA-1.6-34B (74.1 AUROC) beats VQ2 (66.3) and all CLIP variants. On RefCOCO/+/g and Flickr30K, CRG re-ranking improves over GroundingDINO-B by 2.73% on average, with gains up to 5.8% on Flickr30K in the multiple-proposal subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRG extends classifier-free guidance (CFG), originally developed for diffusion models, to autoregressive VLMs by contrasting token distributions produced with and without a masked image region. It departs from Set-of-Mark prompting, which overlays numbered segmentation markers but only works on proprietary models like GPT-4V, and from visual-prompt fine-tuning approaches (e.g., ViP-LLaVA) that require costly curated training data. The method also builds on contrastive decoding and context-guided sampling for text generation, but operates at the sub-image level with hard region masking rather than soft input perturbations, enabling fine-grained region grounding without any parameter updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CRG provides a broadly applicable, training-free mechanism that unlocks visual prompt following in open-source VLMs and is complementary to fine-tuned models, making it a practical tool for practitioners who cannot afford proprietary APIs or large-scale fine-tuning. A key limitation is the 2× inference cost from running the model on both the original and masked image, and the reliance on an external object detector when no visual prompts are available. Open questions include whether better visual encoders could eliminate the need for separate detectors, and how CRG might combine with concurrent textual-context guidance approaches for a unified multimodal prompting strategy.
