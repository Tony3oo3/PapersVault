## Problem

Joint part and object segmentation requires simultaneously capturing fine-grained local details for parts and broad global context for whole objects, yet existing methods either focus primarily on part segmentation without effective object-level output or employ separate specialized architectures for each task, inflating computational cost. The conflicting spatial demands—local precision versus global coherence—remain inadequately reconciled within a single efficient framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image and produces two pixel-level segmentation maps: a part mask and an object mask. Internally, the image is processed through a lightweight convolutional stem that downsamples to pixel features, then to superpixels at 1/4 spatial resolution, and finally to a small set of group tokens (64 by default) at 1/16 of the superpixel count. Predictions are generated at the coarsest group level and progressively restored to full resolution via association-aware upsampling rather than standard bilinear interpolation.

## Method

LGFormer introduces a three-level hierarchical representation (pixels → superpixels → groups) with two complementary aggregation mechanisms: Superpixel Context Aggregation (SCA) uses local cross-attention to iteratively refine superpixels from adjacent pixels, reducing local redundancy, while Group Context Aggregation (GCA) performs global S2G and G2S cross-attention between superpixels and a small set of group tokens, reducing global redundancy, with a ViT self-attention block inserted between the two phases for inter-group interaction. A dual-branch architecture handles the two tasks: the part branch applies additional ViT blocks directly on superpixels, while the object branch further abstracts superpixels into groups via GCA before classification. Both branches use association-aware upsampling, which leverages the attention score matrices from SCA and GCA to progressively upscale predictions from groups to superpixels to pixels, preserving boundary sharpness that bilinear upsampling loses at 32× downsampling. The model is trained end-to-end with an ImageNet-pretrained ViT-S backbone, using LayerScale for stable convergence and a single unified loss for both segmentation heads.

## Results

On PartImageNet (joint training), LGFormer achieves 67.4% part mIoU and 79.8% object mIoU, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Compositor (Swin-T) by 2.8% and 0.8% respectively. On Pascal-Part, LGFormer records 57.4% part mIoU and 85.1% object mIoU, surpassing Compositor by 1.5% and 2.0%. Notably, LGFormer achieves these gains with fewer parameters (38M vs. 51M) and lower FLOPs (50G vs. 57G) than the strongest baseline. Under separate (task-specific) training, LGFormer further improves to 69.4% part mIoU and 80.0% object mIoU on PartImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGFormer outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both PartImageNet and Pascal-Part in the joint training setting. Against the closest competitor, Compositor with Swin-T (51M params, 57G FLOPs), LGFormer gains +2.8% part mIoU (67.4 vs. 64.6) and +0.8% object mIoU (79.8 vs. 79.0) on PartImageNet, and +1.5% part mIoU (57.4 vs. 55.9) and +2.0% object mIoU (85.1 vs. 83.1) on Pascal-Part, while using 13M fewer parameters and 7G fewer FLOPs. Compared to MaskFormer with Swin-T, LGFormer improves part mIoU by 5.7% and object mIoU by 2.6% on PartImageNet. The gap over the ResNet-50-based baselines (MaskFormer and Compositor) is even larger, with part mIoU improvements of 5.0–9.4% and object mIoU improvements of 8.0–13.7%. LGFormer also demonstrates superior occlusion robustness, with only an 8.1% part mIoU drop under 20–40% occlusion versus 10.0% for Compositor and 13.7% for MaskFormer.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGFormer builds directly on SPFormer's Superpixel Context Aggregation (SCA) mechanism for pixel-to-superpixel refinement, extending it with a new Group Context Aggregation (GCA) stage that organizes superpixels into object-level group tokens. It positions itself as a departure from Compositor, the closest prior work, by replacing Compositor's purely global-scale pixel-part-object interactions with a hierarchical pixel→superpixel→group structure that introduces local inductive biases and reduces computational cost. The association-aware upsampling strategy, which leverages cross-scale attention scores for progressive reconstruction, is a novel contribution not present in prior hierarchical segmentation models that rely on standard bilinear upsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that superpixels spontaneously acquire part-level semantics under object-only supervision (and groups acquire object-level semantics under part-only supervision) suggests the hierarchical structure itself encodes meaningful semantic stratification, potentially reducing annotation burden for fine-grained segmentation. The model achieves state-of-the-art results with fewer parameters (38M vs. 50–51M for Compositor) and lower FLOPs, making it a practical option for joint part-object segmentation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes, whether the fixed 64-group token count scales appropriately with scene complexity, and whether the semantic emergence phenomenon holds under more diverse or noisy supervision regimes.
