## Problem

Existing text-to-4D generation methods confine motion to a fixed bounding box, producing only small local deformations (swaying, gesticulating) and unable to synthesize global motion where objects traverse a scene. This limitation creates a stark realism gap compared to video generation models that naturally produce both global and local motion at scale. The paper addresses this by introducing trajectory conditioning that explicitly decomposes scene motion into a global rigid component and a local deformation component.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241215/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a user-specified 3D cubic spline trajectory (defined by control points, parameterized over t∈[0,1] starting at the origin) as input. It produces an animated 4D scene represented as a deformable NeRF with a learned local deformation field, where the bounding box is rigidly transformed along the trajectory and the deformation field is conditioned on trajectory position. A key design constraint is that the trajectory-aware VSDS decouples the temporal duration of the generated 4D scene from the finite frame count (16 frames) of the VideoCrafter2 video diffusion model used for supervision.

## Method

TC4D factors motion into a global rigid transformation R(x,t) = R(n̂, T′)x + T(t) along a user-defined spline trajectory and a local deformation field D optimized via trajectory-aware VSDS. The trajectory-aware VSDS divides the trajectory into segments of length Δt matched to the video model's intrinsic motion scale, then stochastically samples segments to render video clips for computing VSDS gradients, ensuring all trajectory regions receive adequate supervision. A timestep annealing schedule linearly decays the diffusion timestep sampling interval from [0.02, 0.98] to [0.02, 0.5] during training to suppress high-frequency jitter in the deformation field. The initial static 3D scene is generated with hybrid SDS (MVDream + ProlificDreamer), then frozen while the deformation field (parameterized with hash-encoded feature grids) is optimized for 10,000 Adam iterations with a smoothness penalty on spatial and temporal derivatives of the displacement.

## Results

Evaluation is conducted via a user study with 20 human evaluators across 33 text-prompt-generated scenes. TC4D is preferred over the 4D-fy baseline (rigidly transformed along the same trajectory) in 85% of overall comparisons, 89% for motion quality, 92% for motion amount, and 81% for text alignment (all p < 0.05). Appearance quality (55%) and 3D structure quality (52%) show no statistically significant difference, as expected since both methods share the same underlying static 3D representation. The ablation study confirms that removing local deformation, trajectory conditioning, or the smoothness penalty each degrade motion quality and amount substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is 4D-fy [4] (CVPR 2024), whose static output is rigidly transformed along the same spline trajectory to isolate the effect of learned local deformation. TC4D wins decisively on all motion-related metrics: 89% preference for motion quality, 92% for motion amount, and 81% for text alignment, with 85% overall preference. Appearance quality (55%) and structure quality (52%) show no significant difference, confirming that the gains come from motion rather than geometry or appearance. The concurrent work DreamGaussian4D [66] is compared in the appendix but is not the focus of the main experimental section. The ablation results further show that the smoothness penalty is the most impactful single component to remove (dropping overall preference to 74%), while removing local deformation entirely (pure rigid transform) yields the largest gap (97% preference for TC4D), underscoring the value of learned deformation on top of trajectory conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-4D generation line (4D-fy, DreamGaussian4D) by introducing a trajectory-conditioned motion decomposition: global motion is handled by rigidly transforming the scene bounding box along a user-specified spline, while local motion is learned via a deformation field supervised by trajectory-aware video score distillation sampling (VSDS). It builds on MVDream and ProlificDreamer for initial static 3D scene generation and VideoCrafter2 for video-level supervision, but departs from prior 4D methods by explicitly decoupling scene-scale translation/rotation from in-place deformation—a decomposition that prior deformation-only or time-dependent NeRF approaches cannot express. The trajectory-aware VSDS (segmented sampling along the spline) and diffusion-timestep annealing are novel training procedures not present in earlier VSDS-based 4D work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work closes a visible realism gap between 2D video generation (which naturally exhibits global+local motion) and 4D generation (which was confined to small in-place deformations), and enables compositional multi-object scenes where each object follows its own trajectory. Key limitations remain: motion quality is bottlenecked by the 16-frame output of VideoCrafter2, leg/foot articulation is imperfect, and trajectories must be user-specified (though LLM-assisted generation is shown). Open questions include extending to multi-object interaction, unbounded scene-scale motion, and developing automated 4D motion metrics that go beyond user studies.
