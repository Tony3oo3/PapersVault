## Problem

SPAD-based single-photon cameras consume non-negligible energy per photon detection due to avalanche multiplication, and this flux-dependent power scales with scene brightness, severely limiting achievable sensor resolution and hindering widespread adoption. The paper asks 'which photons should be detected?' and proposes a computational-imaging framework to selectively disable SPAD pixels in real time, reducing avalanche power while preserving downstream vision task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242025/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary-valued photon frames (one bit per pixel per frame) from SPAD sensors operating at up to ~100 kHz frame rates (e.g., SwissSPAD2 at 512×256, 97,700 FPS). Inhibition policies operate on small local spatio-temporal neighborhoods (≤3×3×4) using only arithmetic and Boolean operations, producing a binary enable/disable pattern per pixel per frame. Downstream, the inhibited photon stream feeds into burst reconstruction (Quanta Burst Photography), HED edge detection, or YOLOv8 object detection, yielding reconstructed images, edge maps, or detection boxes.

## Method

Photon inhibition adaptively disables individual SPAD pixels based on their spatio-temporal photon history, preventing avalanches and their associated energy cost. Two policy families are proposed: (1) calculation-based inhibition, where a spatio-temporal filter kernel K (separable into spatial and temporal components) computes a score S from ternary-encoded binary frames and the inhibition pattern; pixels exceeding a threshold η are disabled for τ_H subsequent frames, with kernel design (e.g., Laplacian for edge enhancement, center-weighted averaging for reconstruction) tuned to the target task; and (2) saturation look-ahead, which uses a Fibonacci exposure bracketing sequence where early short-exposure measurements predict near-saturation at longer exposures, disabling the pixel in subsequent brackets. Both policies are designed for in-pixel implementation with power-of-two multiplications (bit shifts) and small kernels. Energy-aware metrics—detection efficiency (SNR²_H/D) and measurement efficiency (SNR²_H/W)—guide the allocation of measurements across pixels and exposure levels, and the approach achieves 42% fewer detections at equal SSIM for reconstruction, 30% reduction for edge detection, and 95% photon inhibition for object detection under camera motion.

## Results

The proposed photon inhibition policies achieve substantial energy savings across multiple vision tasks while maintaining performance. For image reconstruction with exposure bracketing, the calculation-based policy reduces photon detections by 42% at equal SSIM (and 14% at a single 1.0 ppp exposure at SSIM=0.7). For edge detection on BSDS500, the edge-enhancing policy reduces detections by 30% at equal F-score. In dynamic scene experiments with the SwissSPAD2 sensor, the saturation look-ahead policy inhibits over 90% of photons under bright light while preserving burst-reconstruction quality, and YOLOv8 object detection remains successful with ~95% of photons inhibited under camera motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed adaptive inhibition policies against two baselines: (1) conventional clocked recharge with no additional inhibition, and (2) static sub-sampling (dropping 9 of every 10 frames, a fixed 90% reduction). Against conventional capture, the proposed policies consistently reduce detections by 14–42% for imaging tasks and 30% for edge detection while maintaining equal task performance (SSIM or F-score). Against static sub-sampling, the adaptive saturation look-ahead policy is superior in mixed-light dynamic scenes: sub-sampling loses detail in low-light regions (furniture, person outlines become invisible), whereas the adaptive policy allocates more detections to dim regions and fewer to bright ones, yielding cumulatively fewer total detections over the full sequence while preserving quality across all illumination levels. In bright light alone, both methods perform comparably, but the adaptive method decouples detection energy from flux, a property static sub-sampling cannot achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on SPAD sensor power management, building directly on clocked recharge (Morimoto et al., Optica 2020) and related global exposure-control strategies, which the authors reframe as special cases of a broader adaptive inhibition framework. The key departure is from global, content-agnostic methods to lightweight, spatio-temporal, data-dependent policies inspired by retinal lateral inhibition in biological vision. The paper also introduces new energy-aware performance metrics (SNR_H/D and SNR_H/W) that explicitly incorporate avalanche detection cost, going beyond the standard exposure-referred SNR used in prior single-photon imaging literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As SPAD-based cameras move from research prototypes to commercial products (Canon MS-500, smartphone LiDAR), flux-dependent avalanche power is a fundamental bottleneck limiting resolution and battery life; demonstrating 90%+ photon reduction while preserving task performance directly addresses this. The paper's main limitations are that inhibition policies are handcrafted rather than learned, the noise model excludes practical effects (crosstalk, afterpulsing, pixel sensitivity variation), and the on-sensor computational cost is estimated but not implemented in silicon. Open questions include whether learned or task-specific policies can further close the gap to oracle-optimal photon allocation, and how these ideas extend to active (LiDAR) imaging where laser power adds another energy dimension.
