## Problem

Class imbalance in semi-supervised medical image segmentation causes poor tail-class performance, slow convergence, and training instability. Existing class-rebalancing methods (CLD, DHC) weight classes based on dataset-level statistics, ignoring the distribution mismatch between the full dataset and randomly sampled patches where classes may be entirely absent. The paper identifies, through theoretical gradient analysis, three intrinsic gradient-bias issues stemming from this extrinsic data-bias: inconsistent non-target-class gradients in Dice loss, near-zero gradients in Cross Entropy due to ~1M voxels per patch, and mismatched CE weights relative to the actual batch distribution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 3D CT/MRI volumes cropped to 96×96×96 patches (Synapse, AMOS) or 2D MRI slices (ACDC), with a batch of 4 patches (2 labeled + 2 unlabeled). It produces a per-voxel/per-pixel multi-class probability map (C+1 classes including background) from which the segmentation mask is obtained via argmax. The GA loss is plug-and-play: it simply replaces the standard Dice loss and Cross Entropy in any existing SSL framework (MT, CPS, MagicNet, etc.) without modifying the backbone architecture or sampling strategy.

## Method

The GA loss has two components. GADice modifies Dice loss for the class-absence case: when no voxels of class c appear in the batch (all y_{i,c}=0), it backpropagates a probability-aware gradient of ⟨p_{i,c}⟩/N (with stop-gradient on p) instead of the standard zero gradient, so the network still receives a small corrective signal proportional to its current (incorrect) confidence. GACE addresses the diminutive-gradient and weight-mismatch problems in CE by first mining the k% worst voxels (default k=10) to partially balance classes within the batch, then applying a dynamic weight equilibrium: each mined voxel receives a combined weight of (1/N_K)^γ · (1/N_c)^(1−γ), where N_K is the number of mined voxels, N_c is the per-class count among mined voxels, and γ=0.5 balances volume weight against class weight. Crucially, the class weight is computed from the current batch rather than pre-computed dataset statistics, making it dynamically matched to the actual patch distribution at every iteration.

## Results

On 20% labeled Synapse (13 organs, 3D CT), GA-MagicNet achieves 68.43% average DSC and 3.11 ASD, the best result among all 15 compared methods. The plug-and-play GA loss yields large gains over the base SSL frameworks: GA-CPS reaches 66.39% DSC (+17.90% over CPS at 48.49%), GA-UAMT reaches 60.93% (+19.56% over UA-MT at 41.37%), and GA-RDrop reaches 60.97% (+21.07% over RDrop at 39.90%). On 5% labeled AMOS (15 organs), GA-MagicNet achieves 63.51% DSC, and on 5% labeled ACDC (cardiac MRI), GA-BCP reaches 88.24% DSC and 1.11 ASD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The GA loss outperforms all 15 baselines on 20% labeled Synapse. Among general SSL methods, the largest gains are over RDrop (+21.07%), UA-MT (+19.56%), and CPS (+17.90%); even the strongest baseline MagicNet is surpassed by +7.86% (68.43% vs 60.57%). Among class-imbalanced designs, GA-CPS beats the best CPS-based method CReST by +6.41% (66.39% vs 59.98%), and GA-MagicNet exceeds the overall best imbalance method A&D by +7.55% (68.43% vs 60.88%). The advantage is most pronounced on tail classes: UA-MT scores 0% DSC on gallbladder, esophagus, and both adrenal glands, while GA-UAMT recovers 27.0%, 38.5%, 43.8%, and 51.6% respectively. On 5% labeled AMOS, GA-MagicNet (63.51%) again leads all methods, surpassing the next-best GA-DePL (57.05%) and the best imbalance baseline DHC (49.53%). On ACDC, GA-BCP (88.24% DSC at 5% labeled) marginally trails the fully-supervised U-Net (91.44%) while outperforming all semi-supervised baselines, with the closest competitor BCP at 87.59%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Dice loss from V-Net and the hard example mining concept from Wu et al., but repositions them within a unified gradient-bias analysis for class-imbalanced semi-supervised medical image segmentation. It departs from prior class-imbalanced SSLMIS methods (CLD, DHC, Basak et al.) that rely on dataset-level class distribution statistics, arguing these are mismatched with the actual distribution of randomly cropped patches. The key conceptual departure is a three-step paradigm—identify extrinsic data-bias, analyze intrinsic gradient-bias theoretically, then design targeted loss corrections—rather than ad-hoc reweighting based on global dataset counts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play GA loss (GADice + GACE) offers a simple drop-in replacement for Dice+CE in any SSL segmentation framework, with large and consistent gains across three datasets and eight SSL backbones. The ablation revealing that dynamic weight equilibrium alone (reweighting by batch-level class counts) accounts for most of the improvement suggests that prior methods' reliance on static dataset-level weights was a significant source of suboptimality. Open questions include whether the weight equilibrium factor γ can be adapted dynamically during training, and whether the theoretical gradient-bias analysis extends to other loss families (e.g., boundary-based or Tversky losses).
