## Problem

Learning accurate SDF-based implicit neural representations from only a few images (3–9 views) is severely underconstrained, and existing solutions either depend on expensive pre-trained data priors across many scenes or suffer from noisy supervision signals (imperfect calibration, MVS noise, volumetric rendering bias). The paper addresses how to robustly regularize SDF learning in this sparse-view regime without any pre-learned priors while maintaining fast training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241968/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of calibrated RGB images (3–9 for DTU, 24 for Tanks & Temples) together with the dense fused MVS point cloud—including per-point normals and averaged color labels—produced as a byproduct of the COLMAP calibration step, so no additional capture overhead is incurred. It outputs a converged SDF whose zero level set yields a watertight 3D mesh, and a view-dependent radiance field that enables photo-realistic novel view synthesis via ray marching. Training completes in under 10 minutes on a single GPU.

## Method

SparseCraft jointly learns an SDF and a decomposed radiance field (diffuse + specular MLPs) through differentiable volumetric rendering, using a multi-resolution hash encoding whose resolutions are progressively activated throughout training to act as an implicit regularizer against overfitting. The key novelty is a Taylor-expansion-based geometric loss: by sampling query points near MVS surface points and enforcing that the SDF approximates its own first-order Taylor polynomial at the nearest MVS point, the method simultaneously supervises the zero level set, the surface normal, and—crucially—penalizes curvature near the surface, making the SDF robust to noisy MVS and rendering supervision. A complementary color loss regularizes the diffuse radiance component against MVS color labels. This combination of progressive hash encoding, Taylor linearization losses, and MVS color/normal/point supervision achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction and rendering from sparse views without any pre-trained priors.

## Results

On the DTU reconstruction benchmark (15 scenes, 3 views), SparseCraft achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 1.04, outperforming the best generalizable method ReTR (1.17) and VolRecon (1.38), as well as the fine-tuned SparseNeuS (1.27) and the COLMAP MVS baseline (1.52). For novel view synthesis on DTU, SparseCraft attains 20.55 PSNR / 0.832 SSIM / 0.116 LPIPS with only 3 input views, surpassing the previous best FreeNeRF (19.92 PSNR) by over 0.6 dB, and the gap widens to 26.03 PSNR at 9 views. Training completes in under 10 minutes on a single GPU, faster than SparseNeuS-ft (20 min), NeuralAngelo (15 min), and S-VolSDF (18 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseCraft outperforms all 20 compared baselines on the DTU reconstruction mean Chamfer distance (1.04 vs. 1.17 for ReTR, 1.38 for VolRecon, 1.27 for SparseNeuS-ft, and 1.52 for COLMAP), with particularly large margins on challenging reflective scenes (e.g., scan 37: 1.74 vs. 2.31 for ReTR and 2.59 for VolRecon). In novel view synthesis, SparseCraft leads all methods across every input-view setting (3/6/9 views) on all four metrics; the largest gap is at 3 views where it beats FreeNeRF by 0.63 dB PSNR and 0.066 LPIPS, and RegNeRF by 1.66 dB PSNR. Generalizable methods that require pre-training on large multi-view datasets (MVSNeRF, PixelNeRF, SRF) are consistently outperformed, confirming that no pre-learned prior is needed. Per-scene optimization baselines (NeuS, VolSRF, IDR, UNISURF) show substantially worse Chamfer distances (3.4–6.3) and are also slower to train. On the large-scale Tanks & Temples dataset (24 views), SparseCraft produces more faithful reconstructions than VolRecon, which fails to generate reasonable outputs, and outperforms NeuralAngelo and S-VolSDF qualitatively.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseCraft sits at the intersection of SDF-based neural surface reconstruction (NeuS, Hf-NeuS) and efficient hash-encoded implicit representations (Instant-NGP, Neuralangelo), but departs from both the generalizable-prior line (SparseNeuS, VolRecon, ReTR) and the dense-image per-scene optimization line (Neuralangelo, MonoSDF). Its core conceptual departure is a learning-free regularization strategy: rather than conditioning on spatially local features from a pre-trained encoder, it leverages the MVS point cloud, normals, and colors already produced by COLMAP calibration, and encodes them through a novel Taylor-expansion-based geometric linearization loss that enforces local linearity of the SDF near the surface. The progressive multi-resolution hash encoding is adapted from Neuralangelo but applied throughout training as a regularizer rather than a warm-up, and the SDF-to-transparency mapping follows Hf-NeuS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving state-of-the-art reconstruction and novel-view synthesis simultaneously without any pre-trained data priors and in under 10 minutes, SparseCraft makes high-quality 3D capture accessible in constrained, low-budget settings where large multi-view training datasets or expensive pre-training are infeasible. The Taylor linearization principle—enforcing first-order approximation of the SDF near MVS samples as a noise-robustness mechanism—is a generalizable insight that could transfer to other implicit-representation learning tasks. Key limitations remain: the method inherits COLMAP's sensitivity to non-Lambertian surfaces and requires sufficient inter-view overlap, and the MVS point cloud must be dense enough for reliable normal estimation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the MVS dependency can be replaced by learned depth/normal priors without sacrificing the learning-free guarantee, and whether the linearization principle generalizes beyond SDFs to other implicit representations.
