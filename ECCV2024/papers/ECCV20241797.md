## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods are largely designed for ResNet-like backbones, leaving the adaptation of Vision Transformers underexplored. For ViTs, domain shifts severely corrupt self-attention maps, and the key challenge is to separate domain-shift noise from class-discriminative information in the attention features during online inference with only one-time access to unlabeled target data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241797/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential mini-batches (size 64) of unlabeled RGB images from the target domain, processing each batch exactly once in an online fashion. At each ViT layer, a single domain shift token is prepended to the class token, and the model outputs standard classification logits over the source-trained class set. The only additional parameters are one lightweight MLP prediction network per layer (~1.2M total) and the domain shift tokens themselves, keeping the overhead minimal relative to the frozen ViT-B/16 backbone.

## Method

Inspired by the wavelet lifting scheme (split–predict–update), the method introduces a domain shift token at every ViT layer and performs dual-path lifting: a lightweight MLP prediction network extracts a domain-shift feature from the token, and an update network subtracts this feature from the class token to restore discriminative information. The two networks are trained adversarially with a combined loss of reliable entropy (for the update path) and a cosine-similarity loss that encourages the predicted domain-shift features to be class-invariant within each batch. Crucially, the update network is optimized with Sharpness-Aware Minimization (smooth optimization) while the prediction network uses standard gradient descent (non-smooth optimization); a theoretical analysis based on L-smooth function bounds shows that smoothing the prediction network's similarity objective leads to sub-optimal domain-shift estimation when only a single gradient step is available per batch.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (Level 5 severity), the proposed Dual-Path Adversarial Lifting method achieves 67.0% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the second-best method EATA (64.8%) by 2.2% and the direct baseline SAR (63.6%) by 3.4%. On ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, and VisDA-2021, the method achieves 64.8%, 49.9%, and 64.1% respectively, surpassing SAR by 2.8%, 4.6%, and 4.0%. On Office-Home, the method reaches 79.4% average accuracy across all 12 transfer tasks, exceeding SAR (77.3%) and TENT (78.9%). The method runs in approximately 8 minutes on a single RTX 3090 GPU, only ~13% slower than SAR (~7 min) but dramatically faster than MEMO (~14 hours) and DDA (~5 days).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 baselines on ImageNet-C average accuracy. The closest competitor is EATA (64.8%), which the method surpasses by 2.2%; SAR (63.6%) and DePT-G (63.6%) trail by 3.4%. On ImageNet-R, ImageNet-A, and VisDA-2021, the method beats SAR by 2.8%, 4.6%, and 4.0% respectively, and TENT by 3.5%, 5.4%, and 4.0%. On Office-Home, the method achieves 79.4% average, exceeding TENT (78.9%) by 0.5% and SAR (77.3%) by 2.1%. Notably, the method wins on nearly all 15 individual ImageNet-C corruptions, with the largest margins on pixelation (+2.5% over EATA), JPEG (+2.8% over EATA), and elastic transform (+2.5% over EATA). The method also matches or exceeds the source model on all corruptions, whereas several baselines (e.g., DDA on fog at 5.0%, CoTTA on snow at 2.9%) suffer severe degradation on specific corruptions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAR framework (Niu et al., ICLR 2023), which stabilizes test-time adaptation via Sharpness-Aware Minimization on entropy loss, by introducing a dual-path adversarial lifting mechanism inspired by wavelet lifting in signal processing. The key architectural departure is a single prependitive domain shift token per ViT layer (versus 10–100 tokens in visual prompt tuning methods like VPT or VPA), paired with a lightweight MLP prediction network and a feature-subtraction update network. The adversarial optimization strategy—smooth (SAM-based) for the update network but non-smooth for the prediction network—draws on domain adversarial learning principles (Rangwani et al., ICML 2022) and is supported by a theoretical analysis showing that smooth optimization of the similarity loss leads to sub-optimal domain shift estimation under single-step online updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, low-overhead (~1.2M parameters, ~13% slower than SAR) approach to layer-by-layer domain shift removal in ViTs, with consistent 2–5% gains over strong baselines across five benchmark datasets. The asymmetric smooth/non-smooth optimization insight, backed by the upper-bound analysis in Eq. 14, provides a principled justification for why the prediction and update networks should be optimized differently, which could inform future adversarial TTA designs. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/16, reliance on a single-step gradient assumption in the theory, and the fact that the domain shift token's representational capacity (a single vector per layer) may be insufficient for highly complex or multi-modal domain shifts. Open questions include whether the lifting scheme generalizes to larger ViT variants, video, or multi-modal settings, and whether the adversarial formulation can be extended to handle compound or sequential domain shifts.
