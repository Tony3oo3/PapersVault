## Problem

Existing 3D avatar reconstruction methods rely on linear blend skinning or pose-conditioned deformations that produce rigid, unconvincing motion for loose-fitting garments, and they bake lighting into appearance, preventing relighting. The only prior work incorporating cloth physics (Xiang et al.) requires tedious manual parameter search rather than principled estimation. PhysAvatar addresses this gap by jointly learning garment physical parameters and relightable appearance from multi-view video in a unified inverse rendering + inverse physics framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240983/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video (160 cameras in experiments), a re-meshed initial 3D mesh with manually annotated garment segmentation and boundary vertices, and SMPL-X body poses fitted via DWPose keypoint matching. It outputs three scalar garment physical parameters (density, membrane stiffness, bending stiffness), a time-invariant diffuse texture map, and an ambient light vector, which together enable rendering the avatar under arbitrary novel motions, viewpoints, and lighting conditions. The physics simulation uses the SMPL-X body mesh as a collider, and garment boundary vertices (e.g., shoulders) are driven by LBS from the pose sequence.

## Method

PhysAvatar is a three-stage pipeline: (1) mesh-aligned 4D Gaussian tracking, where one Gaussian is attached per face barycenter with rotation derived from triangle edges and a small normal-direction scale, optimized with photometric, isometry, and normal losses to produce dense temporal correspondences; (2) inverse physics estimation using the C-IPC cloth simulator with StVK elastic potential and discrete shell hinge bending energy, where the three scalar parameters (ρ, κs, κb) are optimized via forward finite-difference gradients and Adam, exploiting the low dimensionality of the problem to avoid needing an analytically differentiable simulator; (3) physics-based appearance refinement with the Mitsuba3 differentiable Monte Carlo renderer, which optimizes a single diffuse texture map and ambient light to remove baked-in shadows inherited from the Gaussian tracking stage. The key insight is that the physics simulation provides geometrically accurate, self-occlusion-aware geometry that the inverse renderer can then use to cleanly separate material from lighting, enabling relighting and novel-motion synthesis that LBS-based or purely learned methods cannot achieve.

## Results

On the ActorHQ dataset, PhysAvatar achieves the best geometry accuracy with a Chamfer Distance of 0.55×10³ (vs. 0.66 for TAVA, 0.91 for GS-Avatar, and 1.12 for ARAH) and the highest F-Score of 92.9. For appearance, PhysAvatar attains the best LPIPS of 0.035, substantially outperforming all baselines (0.044–0.055), while achieving competitive PSNR (30.2 dB) and SSIM (0.957). The method also generalizes to unseen motions from AMASS and Mixamo, producing realistic garment deformations where baselines fail on large loose-garment motions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysAvatar outperforms all three baselines on both geometry metrics: it reduces Chamfer Distance by 17% over TAVA (0.55 vs. 0.66) and by 51% over ARAH (0.55 vs. 1.12), while achieving the highest F-Score (92.9 vs. 92.3 for TAVA). On perceptual appearance (LPIPS), PhysAvatar leads by a clear margin—0.035 versus 0.044 for GS-Avatar, 0.051 for TAVA, and 0.055 for ARAH—indicating substantially better visual fidelity. GS-Avatar edges out PhysAvatar on PSNR (30.6 vs. 30.2) and SSIM (0.962 vs. 0.957), though the authors note these metrics are sensitive to minor pixel misalignments and that LPIPS better reflects human perception. Qualitatively, PhysAvatar captures fine wrinkle details and large garment deformations that all three baselines (which rely on LBS or pose-conditioned skinning without explicit physics) fail to reproduce, particularly on AMASS and Mixamo motions involving loose garments.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysAvatar sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) animatable avatar reconstruction via LBS-based skinning (TAVA, ARAH, GS-Avatar), which the paper identifies as fundamentally inadequate for loose garments; (2) physics-based cloth simulation (C-IPC, XPBD), which prior avatar work either omits or applies with manually tuned parameters (Xiang et al. 2022); and (3) differentiable/inverse rendering for appearance estimation (Mitsuba3). The key departure is the principled, fully automated coupling of inverse physics (finite-difference gradient estimation of density, membrane stiffness, and bending stiffness) with inverse rendering, using mesh-aligned 4D Gaussians as the tracking backbone that bridges the two. Unlike AniDress, which uses simulation only for rigging, PhysAvatar optimizes physical parameters directly against tracked geometry, and unlike CaPhy, which estimates StVK parameters from 4D scans in a quasi-static setting, it handles full dynamic simulation with body-cloth collision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first end-to-end pipeline that automatically recovers both the physical parameters and the relightable appearance of loose garments from multi-view video, removing the manual parameter search that limited prior physics-based avatar work. The practical impact is significant: the resulting avatars can be animated with arbitrary motions (including non-SMPL sources like Mixamo), relit, and exported to standard CG pipelines (Blender), making them immediately useful for virtual production and digital fashion. Key limitations remain—manual garment segmentation and UV unwrapping, reliance on SMPL-X as an imperfect body collider, and the dense-view (160-camera) capture requirement—each of which constrains real-world deployment. Open questions include adapting the pipeline to sparse views, automating segmentation, and extending to multi-layer or structured garments.
