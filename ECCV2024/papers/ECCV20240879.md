## Problem

State-of-the-art face recognition models suffer severe performance drops (over 50% in TAR@FAR=1e-5) on low-quality images, yet existing LQ FR methods (Facial Image Enhancement and Common Space Mapping) address only general degradation factors like resolution and blur while ignoring Face Alignment Error (FAE). The paper identifies FAE as a critical, FR-specific degradation component that has been overlooked: even small alignment perturbations cause dramatic accuracy losses on LQ faces, and current training pipelines never expose models to such misalignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240879/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes aligned 112×112 RGB face images during training and produces a d-dimensional ℓ₂-normalized face embedding via a ResNet-100 backbone with a hyperspherical classifier. During training, each input is additionally transformed by a 4-parameter affine spatial transformation (rotation angle φ, horizontal shift Δu, vertical shift Δv, scale factor λ) to generate an adversarial alignment-perturbed counterpart. The constraint on the transformation is defined via per-landmark flow vectors bounded by a template-based upper bound, rather than a standard ℓ_p-norm ball, ensuring the perturbed face remains on the manifold of valid training instances.

## Method

ARoFace formulates LQ FR improvement as a min-max optimization: an inner loop uses k-step PGD with a randomly sampled Gaussian step size α to find the spatial transformation θ* that maximizes the FR loss (crafting hard, FAE-expressing samples), while an outer loop minimizes the FR loss over network parameters ψ using a batch of both original and adversarial samples. The key design choices are (1) using a differentiable spatial transformer (composition of scaling, rotation, translation) rather than GAN-based image degradation, adding only 4 trainable parameters; (2) constraining the perturbation via per-landmark flow vectors tied to the standard 5-landmark FR alignment template, avoiding the need for a separate landmark detector; and (3) randomizing the PGD step size to increase diversity of crafted transformations. The method is plug-and-play, orthogonal to the choice of angular margin loss (ArcFace, AdaFace, CosFace), and requires no face detection or landmark estimation module at inference time.

## Results

On IJB-S (the most challenging LQ benchmark), AdaFace+ARoFace (WebFace12M) achieves 40.51% Rank1 on Surveillance-to-Surveillance, a +4.3% improvement over AdaFace (36.71%). On TinyFace, ArcFace+ARoFace (MS1MV2) reaches 67.32% Rank1, outperforming ArcFace+CFSM (64.69%) by +2.63%. On IJB-B and IJB-C (mixed HQ/LQ), ARoFace consistently improves TAR@FAR=1e-5 by 0.76% and 0.38% respectively over ArcFace (MS1MV3), while AdaFace+ARoFace (WebFace12M) achieves 93.14% and 96.20% TAR@FAR=1e-5, surpassing AdaFace by 0.01% and 0.26%. The method also improves robustness to alignment perturbation dramatically: on IJB-B, TAR@FAR=1e-5 for perturbed inputs jumps from 45.74% (ArcFace) to 61.09% (ArcFace+ARoFace), a +15.35% gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARoFace consistently outperforms all baselines across every training dataset and evaluation benchmark. Against ArcFace+CFSM [41] (the closest LQ-specific competitor using GAN-based degradation), ArcFace+ARoFace wins by +2.63% Rank1 and +3.65% Rank5 on TinyFace (MS1MV2), while also matching or exceeding CFSM on IJB-B and IJB-C verification. Against the ArcFace [11] baseline, ARoFace improves IJB-B TAR@FAR=1e-5 by +0.76% and IJB-C by +0.38% (MS1MV3), and TinyFace Rank1 by +3.73%. Against AdaFace [36], ARoFace delivers larger gains on LQ-heavy benchmarks: +1.31% TAR@FAR=1e-5 on IJB-B and +0.64% on IJB-C (WebFace4M), and +1.96% Rank1 on TinyFace (WebFace12M). On IJB-S, AdaFace+ARoFace surpasses the previous best (AdaFace) by +4.3% Rank1 on Surveillance-to-Surveillance and +0.98% on Surveillance-to-Single. Notably, ARoFace does not sacrifice HQ performance: on IJB-B/IJB-C (which contain both HQ and LQ faces), gains are positive or near-neutral, confirming the method's orthogonality to the underlying FR objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the differentiable spatial transformer framework of Jaderberg et al. [28] and the per-landmark flow vector constraint from Xiao et al. [71], repurposing them for a face-recognition-specific adversarial augmentation. It departs from the dominant CSM line of work (e.g., CFSM [41]) that synthesizes LQ faces via GAN-based degradation, arguing instead that Face Alignment Error (FAE) is an overlooked, FR-specific degradation factor that prior LQ-FR studies have ignored. Unlike alignment-free FR approaches (REST [70], rectification networks [89]) that learn alignment jointly with recognition, ARoFace keeps alignment as a fixed preprocessing step and trains the recognition model to be robust against its inevitable errors in LQ inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of FAE as a critical, previously neglected degradation factor reframes how the community thinks about LQ FR failure: it is not solely about pixel-level quality but about geometric misalignment that compounds with low resolution. The method's plug-and-play nature (4–9 extra parameters, no GAN, no target dataset) makes it broadly adoptable, and the consistent gains across IJB-B, IJB-C, IJB-S, and TinyFace with multiple backbones and training sets suggest a generalizable robustness mechanism. Open questions include whether the per-landmark flow constraint is the optimal way to bound spatial perturbations, whether the approach extends to 3D or multi-view alignment errors, and whether the adversarial budget should be quality-adaptive (larger for lower-quality inputs) rather than fixed.
