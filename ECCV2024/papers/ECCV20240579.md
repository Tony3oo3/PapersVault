## Problem

Existing AI research examines nonverbal signals (gaze, facial expression, gesture, posture, touch) in isolation using specialized datasets, failing to capture the concurrent, multifaceted nature of real social interactions. No large-scale dataset or unified detection task exists that jointly models all major nonverbal interaction types across varying numbers of participants.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240579/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and outputs triplets of the form <individual bounding box, social group bounding box, interaction category label>, where the interaction category is one of 22 atomic-level behaviors spanning five broad types (gaze, touch, facial expression, gesture, posture). For individual-level interactions (e.g., facial expression, posture), the social group bounding box is identical to the individual's box; for group-level interactions (e.g., mutual gaze, handshake), the group box encompasses all participating individuals. Evaluation uses mean Recall@K (mR@25/50/100) rather than mAP to handle the long-tailed category distribution and incomplete annotations.

## Method

NVI-DEHR is built on a DETR-style encoder-decoder with ResNet-50 backbone and a Transformer encoder. A query-based instance decoder localizes individuals and social groups using paired learnable queries with position-guided embeddings. The core novelty is a dual multi-scale hypergraph: one hypergraph uses human individuals as vertices with hyperedges formed by clustering s most-affine vertices (distance-based construction at S=5 scales), modeling intra-group human-human relations; the other uses social groups as vertices, modeling inter-group relations. L=2 hyperedge convolutional layers perform message passing on each scale, and final vertex embeddings are aggregated across scales via an MLP. An independent interaction decoder then predicts interaction categories using dynamically constructed queries (the element-wise average of position-aligned individual and group features) rather than random initialization, enabling the model to exploit high-order relational context for interaction classification.

## Results

On the primary NVI-DET task, NVI-DEHR achieves 71.20 AR on NVI val and 74.67 AR on NVI test, outperforming the strongest baseline m-GEN-VLKT (67.18/71.72 AR) by 4.02 and 2.95 points respectively. On the secondary HOI-DET task, NVI-DEHR reaches 35.30 Full AP on HICO-DET and 64.1/65.3 AP_role^{S1}/S2 on V-COCO, surpassing the previous best HOICLIP (34.54 Full AP; 63.5/64.8 AP_role) by 0.76 and 0.6/0.5 points. The model also shows consistent gains on individual-wise interactions (67.74 AR vs. 65.21 for m-CDN) and group-wise interactions (80.42 AR vs. 78.14 for m-GEN-VLKT) on NVI val.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NVI-DET, NVI-DEHR wins against all three adapted HOI-DET baselines: m-QPIC (68.26 AR val), m-CDN (68.48 AR val), and m-GEN-VLKT (67.18 AR val), with the largest margin over m-GEN-VLKT (+4.02 AR). Notably, the CLIP-based m-GEN-VLKT underperforms despite its strong HOI-DET results, suggesting that visual-language knowledge transfer is less effective for subtle nonverbal signals. On HOI-DET, NVI-DEHR outperforms all 12 compared methods including HOICLIP (34.54 Full AP on HICO-DET, 63.5/64.8 AP_role on V-COCO), CDN (31.78/62.3/64.4), DOQ (33.28/63.5/-), and GEN-VLKT (33.75/62.4/64.4), confirming that the hypergraph-based relational modeling generalizes well beyond nonverbal interactions to standard human-object interaction detection. The gains are most pronounced on rare interaction categories in HICO-DET (31.43 vs. 30.71 for HOICLIP), suggesting the multi-scale hypergraph is particularly effective for underrepresented relational patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in nonverbal interaction research, which has examined individual signal types (facial expressions, gestures, gaze, postures, touch) in isolation on specialized datasets. It builds on the DETR encoder-decoder detection framework for instance localization, incorporates hypergraph neural network convolutions (Feng et al., 2019) and distance-based hyperedge construction (Gao et al., 2020) to model high-order relational structure, and adopts the position-guided query pairing strategy from GEN-VLKT (Liao et al., 2022) for individual-group association. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between HOI-DET (which handles explicit human-object pairs) and social signal processing (which has lacked a unified detection formulation), introducing hypergraphs as the natural structure for modeling multi-person nonverbal interactions that standard pairwise graphs cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NVI dataset and NVI-DET task provide the first unified benchmark for generic nonverbal interaction understanding, addressing a long-standing fragmentation in the field where no single dataset or task encompassed multiple signal types simultaneously. The finding that CLIP-based transfer (m-GEN-VLKT) underperforms simpler baselines on NVI-DET despite succeeding on HOI-DET suggests that nonverbal signals are fundamentally harder to capture with visual-language pretraining, pointing to a gap in current foundation models. The authors explicitly acknowledge that NVI-DET remains far from solved, citing ambiguity, subtlety, and occlusion as persistent challenges, leaving open questions about whether richer temporal modeling (video), explicit gaze geometry, or larger-scale data are needed to close the performance gap.
