## Problem

Existing skeleton-based temporal action segmentation methods model spatial joint dependencies via GCN or attention and temporal frame relationships via TCN or attention, but they overlook semantic relationships among joints and actions at a linguistic level. Graph-based joint connections cannot explicitly encode which joints are semantically related (e.g., left hand vs. right hand vs. head), and one-hot cross-entropy supervision fails to capture fine-grained similarities and dissimilarities between action classes in the category space.

## Input / Output

The method takes skeleton sequences X ∈ R^{C×T×V} (C channels, T frames, V joints) as input and produces frame-level action class predictions Y_cls ∈ R^{Q×T} along with boundary probability predictions Y_b ∈ R^{1×T}. During training, GPT-4-generated text descriptions for each joint, each action class, and multi-action clips are encoded by the CLIP text encoder into 512-dimensional embeddings that serve as auxiliary supervision signals; these text features are entirely discarded at inference, so the deployed model incurs no additional computational cost beyond the backbone.

## Method

LaSA builds on the DeST backbone (multi-scale GCN for spatial modeling + linear transformer for temporal modeling) and introduces two language-assisted modules. The Joint Relationships Establishment (JRE) module uses CLIP-encoded joint text embeddings to generate an attention matrix that fuses spatial features at a semantic level, and simultaneously injects those same embeddings as positional encodings to differentiate joints during spatio-temporal attention fusion. The Action Relationships Supervision (ARS) module applies contrastive learning with KL-divergence loss in two forms: (1) single-class action-text pairs that pull same-class action segment features toward their text embeddings and push apart different classes, and (2) mixed-class clip-text pairs where multi-action clips are contrasted against sequential textual descriptions to model semantic correlations between adjacent actions. Both contrastive losses are applied only during training across the spatial-temporal backbone and the multi-stage action segmentation branch, while the boundary regression branch uses standard binary logistic loss; the overall objective combines classification cross-entropy, smoothness, boundary regression, and the two contrastive terms with fixed weights (λ₁=0.1, λ₂=0.8, λ₃=0.5).

## Results

LaSA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five benchmark datasets. On MCFS-22, it reaches 76.3% F1@50 (vs. 75.0% for DeST-Former) and 86.7% Edit score; on MCFS-130, it attains 66.6% F1@50 (vs. 63.0%) and 79.3% Edit. On PKU-MMD, LaSA improves F1@50 by 4.9% (X-sub) and 5.0% (X-view) over the previous SOTA, while on LARa it gains 0.2% F1@50. The paper notes a positive correlation between performance gains and the number of action classes, with the largest improvements on the 130-class MCFS-130 and 52-class PKU-MMD datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaSA outperforms the previous skeleton-based SOTA, DeST-Former [24], on every dataset and metric: on MCFS-22 it gains +1.3% F1@50 (76.3 vs. 75.0) and +1.5% Edit (86.7 vs. 85.2); on MCFS-130 it gains +3.6% F1@50 (66.6 vs. 63.0) and +3.5% Edit (79.3 vs. 75.8). Against the earlier GCN-based baselines (MS-GCN, IDT-GCN, ID-GCN+ASRF), LaSA leads by 1.4–18.4% in F1@50 depending on the dataset, with the largest margin on MCFS-130 (66.6 vs. 58.6 for IDT-GCN). Compared to video-based methods (MS-TCN, ETSN, ASRF, ASFormer), LaSA achieves substantially higher segment-level scores, e.g., 76.3% F1@50 on MCFS-22 versus 66.9% for the best video method ASFormer. On PKU-MMD (X-sub), LaSA surpasses DeST-Former by 4.9% F1@50 and 9.0% Edit, and on LARa it edges out DeST-Former by 0.2% F1@50, confirming consistent gains across datasets with varying class counts (8–130 classes).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaSA builds directly on the DeST [24] backbone (multi-scale GCN for spatial modeling and linear transformer for temporal modeling) and extends it with two language-assisted modules. The Joint Relationships Establishment module repurposes CLIP text embeddings of joint descriptions as both attention queries/keys (to establish inter-joint correlations) and positional embeddings (to differentiate joints), drawing on the attention-as-positional-encoding idea from Transformers [44]. The Action Relationships Supervision module adapts the KL-divergence-based contrastive learning framework from Bridge-Prompt [20] and GAP [49] to skeleton-based segmentation, applying it at two granularities: single-action segments vs. action text, and multi-action clips vs. sequential clip text. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior STAS work that relies solely on graph/attention spatial modeling and one-hot classification supervision by injecting linguistic priors at both the joint and action levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that performance gains scale with the number of action classes (0.2% on 8-class LARa to 4.9% on 52-class PKU-MMD) suggests language priors are most valuable when the category space is large and discrimination is harder, offering a principled argument for when to adopt such approaches. The ARS module being training-only (no inference overhead) makes the method practically deployable. However, the paper acknowledges residual category prediction errors and boundary offsets, and future work points toward finer-grained temporal-spatial motion descriptions as a path forward. An open question is whether the GPT-4-generated text prompts are optimal or whether task-specific fine-tuning of text descriptions could yield further gains.
