## Problem

Existing crowd counting methods require extensive manual annotation of every person in dense images, which is prohibitively time-consuming. While unsupervised approaches exist, they perform poorly in high-density scenes, and the semantic segmentation model SEEM (a SAM variant) misses many small, occluded individuals in crowded regions due to limited dense-crowd coverage in its training data. This paper addresses the gap of achieving accurate, fully unsupervised crowd counting and localization that rivals supervised methods even in complex, high-density environments.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB crowd images as input and produces a predicted density map from which both the total person count (by summation) and individual head locations (by local maxima extraction) are derived. During pseudo-label generation, images are processed at 512×512 resolution, with adaptive zooming into smaller patches (down to 64×64) in high-uncertainty regions. The counting network (VGG-based, batch size 1, 100 epochs) outputs a per-pixel density map, and the iterative refinement loop additionally outputs updated person segmentation masks and GMM-fitted head point coordinates.

## Method

The core pipeline has three technical components. First, AdaSEEM adaptively zooms into image patches where the uncertain-region ratio exceeds a threshold τ, doubling resolution iteratively and merging segments via NMS to recover small, occluded persons that standard SEEM misses. Second, head localization fits a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model to a soft mask distribution (averaged over K SEEM re-segmentations prompted by random points within each mask), selecting the component with the smaller vertical mean as the head position. Third, a robust loss combines a background term (driving density to zero in non-person regions) and an individual term (enforcing unit density per mask plus exponential-distance attraction toward the head point), while explicitly excluding uncertain regions from the gradient. An iterative refinement loop then uses the trained counter's local maxima as point prompts to SEEM, discovering previously missed individuals in dense areas and retraining on the expanded pseudo-label set for two iterations.

## Results

On the five standard crowd counting benchmarks, the proposed method (Iter. 1) achieves MAE of 181.2 on UCF-QNRF, 105.1 on JHU-CROWD, 122.8 on ShanghaiTech A, 33.3 on ShanghaiTech B, and 382.7 on UCF-CC-50, all without any crowd annotations. This represents a substantial improvement over the previous best unsupervised method CrowdCLIP (MAE 283.3 on UCF-QNRF, 213.7 on JHU, 146.1 on ShTech A, 69.3 on ShTech B, 438.3 on UCF-CC-50). The method also outperforms cross-domain supervised methods like SFCN and RCC on most datasets, and approaches the performance of classic supervised methods such as MCNN and Switch CNN, though it remains behind SOTA supervised methods like STEERER (MAE 74.3 on UCF-QNRF) and MAN (MAE 77.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unsupervised baseline CrowdCLIP [23], the proposed method wins decisively on all five datasets, reducing MAE by 102.1 on UCF-QNRF (181.2 vs 283.3), 108.6 on JHU (105.1 vs 213.7), 23.3 on ShTech A (122.8 vs 146.1), 36.0 on ShTech B (33.3 vs 69.3), and 55.6 on UCF-CC-50 (382.7 vs 438.3). Compared to the other unsupervised method CSS-CNN [1], the improvement is even more dramatic (e.g., MAE 181.2 vs 437.0 on UCF-QNRF). Against cross-domain supervised methods, the proposed method outperforms SFCN [46] on ShTech A (122.8 vs 160.0) and ShTech B (33.3 vs 22.8 is a loss), RCC [13] on ShTech A (122.8 vs 240.1), and CLIP-Count [18] on ShTech A (122.8 vs 192.6) and ShTech B (33.3 vs 45.7). However, the method still trails SOTA supervised methods like STEERER [11] (MAE 74.3 on UCF-QNRF) and MAN [25] (MAE 77.3) by a wide margin, and even classic supervised MCNN [53] (MAE 277.0 on UCF-QNRF) is beaten but LSC-CNN [33] (MAE 120.5) remains ahead. The iterative refinement (Iter. 1 vs Iter. 0) consistently improves results across all datasets, confirming the value of the iterative pseudo-label generation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Segment-Everything-Everywhere Model (SEEM), a semantic adaptation of SAM, and the generalized loss function for crowd counting and localization from Wan et al. (CVPR 2021), repurposing both for an unsupervised setting. It departs from prior unsupervised crowd counting methods (CSS-CNN's distribution matching, CrowdCLIP's vision-language prompting) by leveraging a segmentation foundation model to produce both mask and point pseudo-labels, and from supervised density-map regression by eliminating the need for any manual annotations. The adaptive resolution strategy (AdaSEEM) and GMM-based head localization are novel contributions that address specific failure modes of SEEM in dense scenes, while the iterative pseudo-label refinement loop connects the counting network's predictions back into the segmentation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a foundation-model-driven pseudo-labeling pipeline can close much of the gap between unsupervised and supervised crowd counting, which is practically significant given the extreme cost of annotating dense crowd images. The adaptive resolution idea—iteratively zooming into high-uncertainty patches—is a generalizable strategy that could benefit other dense-scene segmentation tasks beyond counting. However, the method's recall remains substantially below supervised baselines (0.263 vs. 0.748 on UCF-QNRF), and the iterative process is computationally expensive, scaling poorly with crowd density. Open questions include whether the approach can be made more efficient (e.g., learned patch selection instead of exhaustive zooming), whether it extends to video or 3D crowd scenes, and whether the GMM localization can be replaced by a learned head-position predictor to further improve recall.
