## Problem

Existing deep unfolding methods for CASSI reconstruction face two intrinsic bottlenecks: the denoiser must operate on heavily degraded compressed measurements (an ill-posed problem), and regression-based losses are conservative with high-frequency spectral details. No prior work has integrated a generative model to supply degradation-free knowledge into a physics-driven unfolding framework for spectral compressive imaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240871/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D CASSI compressed measurement (W×H̃, where H̃ = H + d_λ accounts for spectral shifting) and the corresponding coded aperture sensing matrix A (a concatenation of diagonal matrices encoding mask and shift per wavelength) as input, and produces a 3D hyperspectral data cube of size W×H×N_λ (28 bands, 450–650 nm). The measurement is first Euclidean-projected into 3D space and normalized by A before being fed to the latent encoder, balancing the scale difference between the compressed input and the clean HSI ground truth used during training.

## Method

The proposed LADE-DUN employs a two-phase training strategy. In Phase I, a latent encoder (built from MobileNetV3 blocks and an MLP-Mixer) compresses the concatenated normalized measurement and clean HSI into a compact latent prior z_GT, which is injected into a U-shaped denoiser within a GC-GAP (Gradient Correction Generalized Alternating Projection) unfolding framework. In Phase II, a DDPM-based latent diffusion model (16 steps, latent size 16×256) is trained to generate this prior from Gaussian noise conditioned on the measurement, while jointly updating the DUN, a conditional encoder, and the diffusion denoiser under a combined reconstruction + prior-matching loss. The denoiser's core building block is the Trident Transformer, which fuses three parallel branches—spatial flow (MBlocks), cross-spectral flow (asymmetric cross-scale multi-head self-attention with 3×5 dilated convolution inspired by pansharpening), and cross-prior flow (shared cross-attention with multi-scale prior features)—to compensate spatial and spectral deficiencies from the degraded measurement.

## Results

On the 10-scene KAIST synthetic benchmark, the proposed method (Ours-10stg) achieves an average PSNR of 40.16 dB and SSIM of 0.980, surpassing the previous SOTA RDLUF-MixS2-9stg (39.57 dB, SSIM 0.974) by +0.59 dB and +0.06 SSIM. With only 3 unfolding stages, the method already reaches 38.31 dB / 0.972 SSIM, matching or exceeding most 9–12-stage baselines. In terms of efficiency (Table 2), Ours-9stg uses 2.78 M parameters and 88.68 G FLOPs—substantially fewer FLOPs than RDLUF-9stg (231.09 G) while delivering higher PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on average PSNR and SSIM across the 10 KAIST test scenes. Against the closest deep-unfolding competitor RDLUF-MixS2-9stg (CVPR'23), Ours-9stg gains +0.52 dB PSNR (40.09 vs 39.57) and +0.005 SSIM while using 61% fewer FLOPs (88.68 G vs 231.09 G). Compared to PADUT-12stg (ICCV'23), the method achieves +1.20 dB PSNR with fewer parameters (2.78 M vs 5.38 M). End-to-end baselines (MST++, BIRNAT, DADF-Plus-3) trail by 1.5–4.2 dB, and the self-supervised LRSDN lags by ~4 dB. Even the 3-stage variant (38.31 dB) surpasses DAUHST-9stg (38.36 dB) and DADF-Plus-3 (38.29 dB) at comparable or lower stage counts, demonstrating that the LDM prior and Trident Transformer yield strong per-stage efficiency. On real SD-CASSI data, the method visually shows fewer noise artifacts and sharper edges than competing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network (DUN) lineage for CASSI reconstruction by introducing a latent diffusion model (LDM) as a generative prior that feeds into the unfolding denoiser, building on the GAP optimization framework of Liao et al. and the DDPM/LDM formulations of Ho et al. and Rombach et al. It departs from prior DUN methods (PADUT, RDLUF, DAUHST) that rely solely on regression-based denoisers operating directly on heavily degraded measurements, instead generating a degradation-free prior from clean HSI statistics and conditioning it on the compressed measurement. The Trident Transformer with its pansharpening-inspired asymmetric cross-scale attention is a novel architectural contribution that unifies spatial, spectral, and prior information flows in a single module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized bottleneck in DUN-based CASSI reconstruction—the difficulty of learning intrinsic HSI properties from severely degraded compressed measurements—by injecting external generative knowledge, and achieves SOTA PSNR (40.16 dB) with substantially lower FLOPs (88.68G) than the prior best (RDLUF at 231.09G). However, the approach still requires clean HSIs for Phase I prior learning, limiting its applicability to settings where such data is unavailable, and the inference time (~1.1s per scene) remains non-trivial for real-time applications. Open questions include whether the LDM prior can be learned in a self-supervised or few-shot manner, whether the two-phase training can be unified, and whether the framework generalizes to other compressive spectral imaging architectures (e.g., dual-disperser CASSI).
