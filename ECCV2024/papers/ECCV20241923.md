## Problem

Existing adversarial distillation methods (e.g., AdaAD) apply a static loss weight across all samples and directly transfer teacher predictions without correcting misclassifications, causing teacher errors to propagate to the student and producing a suboptimal balance between clean accuracy and adversarial robustness. The paper identifies that samples misclassified by the teacher on clean inputs should be treated differently from correctly classified ones, and that adversarial examples generated from misclassified inputs degrade student learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241923/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) in two forms: clean inputs and L∞-perturbed adversarial examples (bound 8/255, generated via 10-step PGD with step size 2/255). The student model outputs a class label, while the teacher's softmax predictions serve as soft supervision signals that are dynamically corrected before distillation. Each training batch is partitioned on-the-fly into a Standard Training subset (teacher misclassified on clean input) and an Adversarial Training subset (teacher correctly classified), so the same image may receive different treatment depending on the teacher's current prediction.

## Method

DGAD introduces three coupled mechanisms. (1) Misclassification-Aware Partitioning (MAP) splits each batch into x_ST (teacher's argmax ≠ ground truth on clean input) and x_AT (teacher's argmax = ground truth), routing x_ST through a standard KL-distillation path and x_AT through an adversarial KL-distillation path where adversarial examples are generated only from correctly classified samples. (2) Error-corrective Label Swapping (ELS) replaces the teacher's incorrect top-1 probability with the correct-class probability for all x_ST samples unconditionally, and for x_AT adversarial examples only when the margin M = P_T(y|x′) − P_T(ŷ|x′) is negative, preventing unreliable late-stage teacher signals from contaminating training. (3) Predictive Consistency Regularization (PCR) adds an L2 penalty ‖S_θ(x) − S_θ(x′)‖₂ weighted by β to enforce that the student's predictions remain consistent between clean and adversarial views, counteracting the imbalance introduced by the separate ST/AT learning paths. The total loss is L_ST + L_AT + β·L_PCR, where both distillation terms use the corrected teacher predictions T̂(·) rather than raw T(·).

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18 student and WideResNet-34-10 teacher, DGAD achieves 87.58% clean accuracy and 55.29% PGD-10 robust accuracy, surpassing the closest baseline AdaAD (86.75% / 54.13%) by +0.83% and +1.16% respectively. With MobileNetV2 student and WideResNet-34-20 teacher, gains are larger: 85.30% clean and 56.77% PGD accuracy (+1.51% and +3.73% over AdaAD). On CIFAR-100 (ResNet18 + WRN-34-10), DGAD reaches 63.24% clean and 33.68% PGD accuracy, and on Tiny ImageNet it attains 47.92% clean and 23.05% PGD accuracy, both outperforming all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGAD outperforms all seven baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES, ARD, IAD, RSLAD, AKD, AdaAD) on every metric across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet. The closest competitor is AdaAD [16], the most recent AD method; DGAD improves over it by +0.83% clean / +1.16% PGD on CIFAR-10 (ResNet18 + WRN-34-10) and by a wider margin of +1.51% clean / +3.73% PGD / +3.44% AA on CIFAR-10 (MobileNetV2 + WRN-34-20), indicating larger gains for smaller student models. Against direct adversarial training baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES), DGAD gains 4.6–4.7% clean accuracy and 2.4–2.5% PGD robustness on CIFAR-10, confirming that distillation from a larger robust teacher substantially narrows the robustness gap. On CIFAR-100, DGAD leads AdaAD by +1.05% clean and +1.16% PGD (ResNet18), and by +0.81% clean and +0.67% PGD (MobileNetV2). On Tiny ImageNet, gains over AdaAD are smaller (+0.38% clean, +0.23% PGD), likely due to the more challenging dataset and smaller teacher (PreActResNet18). In transfer-attack evaluations (Table 7), DGAD also leads AdaAD by 0.96–2.39% across FGSM, PGD, and JSMA from both ResNet34 and VGG16 surrogates.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGAD extends the adversarial distillation line initiated by ARD and refined by AdaAD, which uses a static weighting parameter α to balance clean and adversarial losses. The key conceptual departure is importing the misclassification-aware philosophy from adversarial training (MMA, MART) into the distillation setting: rather than treating all samples uniformly, DGAD partitions them by teacher correctness and corrects teacher errors via label swapping. The Predictive Consistency Regularization component adapts the clean-vs-adversarial consistency principle from TRADES to the distillation context, bridging the gap between the two separately trained subsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously underexplored failure mode in adversarial distillation: teacher misclassifications silently degrading student robustness. The modular design (MAP, ELS, PCR) makes it easy to adopt on top of existing AD pipelines. However, improvements over the strong AdaAD baseline are modest (0.5–1.5% on most metrics), evaluations are limited to CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny ImageNet without ImageNet-scale validation, and it remains open whether the dynamic partitioning benefit persists when the teacher is already highly accurate or when the student-teacher capacity gap is small.
