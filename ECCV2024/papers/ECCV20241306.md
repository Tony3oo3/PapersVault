## Problem

In point cloud semantic segmentation, minority categories suffer from obscured high-dimensional features that are dominated by majority categories, leading to poor discrimination and misclassification. Existing remedies such as oversampling/undersampling compromise scene integrity, while loss-reweighting strategies (Focal Loss, Weighted CE) yield only marginal or even negative gains on point cloud tasks. The paper asks how to obtain high-quality, category-specific feature prototypes that are not contaminated by heterogeneous contextual information, and how to use them to constrain the segmentation network's feature space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241306/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw 3D point cloud (XYZ coordinates, optionally with color) and, during training only, per-point ground-truth category labels. At inference, only the main segmentation branch (e.g., PointNet++, PTv1, PTv2) is active, producing a per-point semantic label with zero additional latency over the base network. During training, the auxiliary branch additionally requires the point cloud to be pre-grouped by category label so that each category's points are processed in isolation, preventing cross-category feature interference in the encoder's neighborhood aggregation.

## Method

SPG introduces a plug-and-play auxiliary branch (the base network's encoder plus a small MLP projection head) that maps category-grouped point subsets into separate feature subspaces, where a supervised contrastive loss (L_con) sharpens intra-class cohesion and inter-class separation. Category prototypes are computed as the mean of L2-normalized features in each subspace and updated via exponential moving average to accumulate cross-scene semantic information. These prototypes then supervise the main branch's feature space through a smooth-L1 loss that pulls each point's feature toward its category prototype, reducing intra-class variance. A reverse supervision path (L_l1) feeds correctly classified features from the main branch back into the auxiliary branch, creating a mutual-constraint loop that keeps both branches converging in the same direction. The total training loss is a simple weighted sum of four terms (L_con, L_l1, L_l1', L_ce) with default weights of 1, and the auxiliary branch is discarded at inference, so no architectural or computational changes are needed at test time.

## Results

SPG consistently improves the base segmentation networks across all four benchmarks. On S3DIS Area5, SPG(PTv2) achieves 91.9% OA, 79.5% mAcc, and 73.3% mIoU, surpassing the previous SOTA (Stratified Transformer at 72.0% mIoU) and improving over the PTv2 baseline by 1.7% mIoU. On Toronto-3D, SPG(PTv2) reaches 83.1% mIoU, a 1.5-point gain over PTv2 and substantially ahead of the next-best method (MS-TGNet at 70.5%). On ScanNet v2 and ScanNet200, SPG(PTv2) achieves 31.5% and 97.6% mIoU respectively, both setting new state-of-the-art results. Notably, SPG introduces zero additional inference cost since the auxiliary branch is used only during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPG(PTv2) achieves the best mIoU on all four benchmarks, outperforming the strongest prior methods: Stratified Transformer (72.0% mIoU on S3DIS), PointNeXt (70.5%), CBL (69.4%), and KPConv (67.1%) on S3DIS; MS-TGNet (70.5%) on Toronto-3D; and MinkowskiNet (25.0%) on ScanNet v2. Compared to its own base networks, SPG yields consistent gains: +11.5% mIoU for PointNet++ (53.5→64.0), +1.1% for PTv1 (70.4→71.5), and +1.7% for PTv2 (71.6→73.3) on S3DIS. Against dedicated class-imbalance strategies (Table 5), SPG(PTv2) at 73.3% mIoU clearly surpasses Focal Loss (70.7%), Weighted CE (70.9%), BBN (69.3%), Meta-Softmax (69.8%), and NORCAL (69.9%), all of which actually degrade PTv2's baseline performance. On per-category analysis (Tables 3–4), the gains are most pronounced for minority classes: e.g., 'chair' improves by +8.6% (PTv1) and 'sofa' by +7.3% (PTv2) on S3DIS, while 'Fence' jumps +7.8% on Toronto-3D, confirming that SPG specifically targets the class-imbalance problem rather than uniformly boosting all categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of prototype-based learning (borrowed from few-shot and contrastive learning literature) and point cloud semantic segmentation, repurposing category prototypes not as classification references but as convergence targets that constrain feature-space geometry. It explicitly departs from the dominant class-imbalance toolkit—resampling, focal loss, weighted cross-entropy, and re-calibration—arguing that these methods either destroy spatial context in point clouds or fail to address the root cause of feature-space overlap. The dual-branch design with mutual supervision draws conceptually from bilateral-branch networks (BBN) and momentum-based prototype updating (MoCo), but the specific mechanism of projecting category-grouped point sets into separate subspaces via a lightweight auxiliary encoder is novel in the 3D segmentation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SPG (no inference overhead, compatible with any base segmentation network) makes it a practical drop-in for practitioners facing imbalanced 3D scenes, and the consistent gains across four datasets and three architectures demonstrate reasonable generalization. However, the improvements on strong baselines (PTv1, PTv2) are modest at 1–2% mIoU, and the method adds training-time complexity with an auxiliary branch and four loss terms. Open questions include whether the separate-subspace extraction remains effective at very high category counts (e.g., ScanNet200's 200 classes), whether the EMA-based prototype memory can be replaced by lighter alternatives, and whether the approach transfers to other 3D tasks such as instance or part segmentation where class imbalance is equally prevalent.
