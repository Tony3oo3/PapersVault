## Problem

Existing 3D shape detailization methods (DECOR-GAN, ShaDDR) only support global style control from a single exemplar, making it impossible to assign different geometric styles to different regions of a coarse shape. When naively extending these methods to per-region style mixing, they produce disconnected structures, floating pieces, and incoherent transitions, especially when styles come from different shape categories. The paper tackles the challenge of enabling interactive, localized, and structure-preserving 3D detailization with controllable style mixing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241029/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a coarse 2^4 voxel grid where each voxel carries binary occupancy, a semantic part label, and a style index pointing to one of N exemplar shapes (each a 2^8 voxel grid with co-segmentation). Users interactively 'paint' style labels over regions of the coarse shape, enabling per-region style assignment. The output is a 2^8 high-resolution occupancy voxel grid whose local geometry in each region follows the assigned exemplar's style while preserving the input's global structure; a mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes.

## Method

The architecture is a 4-level Pyramid GAN of 3D CNNs that progressively upsamples from 2^4 to 2^8 resolution, with each level supervised by a PatchGAN discriminator having N+1 channels (N style-specific plus one global plausibility channel). A key innovation is adaptive α weighting of the style-specific versus global adversarial loss: voxels within 2 units of a style-transition boundary receive a small α (0.1) to prioritize structural coherence, while interior voxels receive a larger α (0.5) for style fidelity. Two novel structure-preserving losses enforce multi-scale consistency: a downsampling loss (max-pool the level j+1 output and match level j) and an upsampling loss (nearest-neighbor upsample level j and match level j+1), both using stop-gradient to prevent cross-level interference. Training data is augmented by randomly scaling, rotating, and combining the limited set of style shapes to synthesize diverse coarse inputs with varied part segmentations, enabling the network to generalize to out-of-distribution structures.

## Results

On single-category chair detailization (Table 1), DECOLLAGE achieves Strict-IOU of 0.748 and Loose-IOU of 0.908, substantially outperforming DECOR-GAN (0.581 / 0.753) and ShaDDR (0.596 / 0.760) in structure preservation. Local detail quality (LP-IOU) also improves from 0.517–0.563 to 0.591, while the overall plausibility classifier score (Cls-score, lower is better) drops from 0.527–0.533 to 0.506. In the multi-category style mixing setting (Table 2), the method maintains its lead with Strict-IOU 0.750 and Loose-IOU 0.906 versus 0.609–0.611 and 0.839–0.854 for the modified baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECOLLAGE outperforms both DECOR-GAN [6] and ShaDDR [5] across all five metrics in the single-category chair setting, with the largest gains in structure preservation: Strict-IOU improves by ~0.16 over DECOR-GAN and ~0.15 over ShaDDR, and Loose-IOU by ~0.15–0.16. In the multi-category style mixing setting (where baselines are modified to accept per-region style labels, denoted DECOR-GAN* and ShaDDR*), the advantage persists: Strict-IOU 0.750 vs. 0.609/0.611, and Cls-score 0.518 vs. 0.567/0.549, indicating the generated shapes are both more structurally faithful and more globally plausible. Qualitatively, the baselines produce disconnected parts and floating pieces at joint regions (e.g., armrest-to-seat connections), while DECOLLAGE maintains coherent connectivity thanks to its structure-preserving losses and adaptive α weighting. The method also wins on local detail metrics (LP-IOU, LP-F-score) in both settings, confirming that the pyramid architecture with style-conditioned PatchGAN discriminators captures finer geometric details than the single-level approaches used by the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DECOR-GAN's global 3D shape detailization framework by introducing localized, per-region style control via a masking-aware Pyramid GAN architecture. The hierarchical pyramid structure draws from Denton et al.'s Laplacian pyramid GAN, repurposed here so that coarse levels enforce global structural coherence while fine levels generate style-specific geometric details. The key departures from prior detailization work (DECOR-GAN, ShaDDR) are the novel structure-preserving losses (downsampling and upsampling consistency) and the spatially adaptive α weighting that reduces style-discriminator influence near style-transition boundaries, addressing the connectivity failures that plague global-only approaches when mixing diverse exemplars.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work enables a new interactive 3D modeling paradigm where users can paint style labels onto coarse shapes and obtain structure-preserving, high-resolution details in under a second, opening creative workflows for games, AR/VR, and e-commerce content pipelines. However, the method is constrained by its voxel representation (max 256³ resolution), requires co-segmentation of style shapes, and struggles with semantically incompatible style mixing (e.g., chair back → plant). Open questions include whether diffusion-based upsampling or integration with vision-language models could overcome the resolution ceiling and the semantic gap that causes failure cases with drastically different geometries.
