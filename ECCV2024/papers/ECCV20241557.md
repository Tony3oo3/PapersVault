## Problem

Standard surface extraction algorithms like Marching Cubes and Dual Contouring rely on sign flips across a surface, making them inapplicable to Unsigned Distance Fields (UDFs), which are the natural representation for open, non-watertight surfaces. Existing UDF meshing methods either depend on hand-crafted rules that produce artifacts, holes, and spurious surfaces, or use neural networks that ignore gradient information and are accuracy-bounded by their training data. This paper introduces a data-driven, rule-free approach to surface detection in UDFs that generalizes to unseen shapes and integrates with both primal and dual triangulation algorithms.

## Input / Output

The network consumes the 8 UDF values and their 8 corresponding 3D gradients at the corners of a single grid cell (a 32-dimensional vector), with UDF values normalized by cell size for resolution invariance. It produces a one-hot 128-dimensional output representing the pseudo-sign configuration of the 7 non-anchor corners (up to global sign symmetry), which is then fed to a standard triangulation algorithm (Marching Cubes or Dual Contouring) to produce the final 3D mesh. Training uses 128³ voxel grids sampled from watertight ABC shapes, yielding approximately 5.5 million cell-level examples.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight MLP (two 1024-dim hidden layers with leaky ReLU, followed by a 128-dim output layer) that performs per-cell classification of pseudo-signs, locally converting a UDF into a pseudo-SDF without any hand-crafted heuristics or global consistency constraints. The 128-way one-hot output (rather than a 7-output multi-label classification) is shown to improve accuracy, and the anchor-vertex convention (fixing one corner's sign) resolves the ill-posedness of global orientation for non-watertight surfaces. Training is supervised on 80 watertight ABC shapes using cross-entropy loss with Gaussian noise augmentation on both UDF values and gradients, taking only ~1 minute on an A100 GPU. The pseudo-SDF can be triangulated directly with Marching Cubes, or integrated with DualMeshUDF by placing vertices at cell centers wherever the network detects a sign flip but DMUDF's thresholding discards the cell, effectively closing holes without parameter tuning.

## Results

On Marching Cubes-based triangulation (Table 1), the method achieves median CD of 4.46×10⁻⁵ and IC of 95.4 on ABC at resolution 64, outperforming MeshUDF (6.76/94.7), CAP-UDF (239/73.4), and DCUDF (291/86.5). The advantage is most pronounced on neural UDFs: on the MGN autodecoder at resolution 128, Ours+MC achieves CD 14.5 vs. MeshUDF's 16.7 and DCUDF's 355. When coupled with DualMeshUDF (Table 2), the method eliminates the need for manual parameter tuning and reduces CD from 216 to 0.803 on the MGN autodecoder at resolution 64, matching the tuned DMUDF-T (0.805) while strictly outperforming untuned DMUDF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CAP-UDF [20], the method wins decisively across all datasets and resolutions, with CD improvements of 1–2 orders of magnitude at low resolutions (e.g., 4.46 vs. 239 on ABC at res. 64). Against MeshUDF [10], the closest MC-based competitor, the method consistently achieves lower CD and higher IC, with the gap most visible on neural UDFs (e.g., CD 10.0 vs. 14.9 on Cars autodecoder at res. 64) and on complex shapes like cars. Against DCUDF [11], the method is substantially more accurate (CD 4.47 vs. 169 on Cars at res. 64) and faster, though DCUDF produces smoother surfaces. In the Dual Contouring setting, the method (Ours+DMUDF) matches or slightly beats untuned DMUDF [19] on ground-truth UDFs (CD 0.574 vs. 0.579 on ABC at res. 64) but dramatically outperforms it on neural UDFs where DMUDF's default thresholds filter out too many vertices (CD 0.803 vs. 216 on MGN autodecoder at res. 64). Against UNDC [6], the method achieves lower CD in nearly all settings (e.g., 0.574 vs. 0.783 on ABC at res. 64) while requiring far less training data (80 shapes, ~1 min vs. 4280 shapes, ~12 hrs) and no data augmentations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two families of UDF meshing methods: rule-based primal approaches (MeshUDF, CAP-UDF) that use hand-crafted heuristics to assign pseudo-signs, and dual methods (UNDC, DualMeshUDF) that predict vertex locations or use threshold-based filtering. It departs from both by formulating surface detection as a simple per-cell classification problem solved by a small MLP, then delegating triangulation to standard SDF algorithms (Marching Cubes or Dual Contouring). The closest conceptual ancestor is DualMeshUDF [19], which the paper extends by using its pseudo-SDF sign-flip predictions to close holes and eliminate the need for manual threshold tuning. Unlike UNDC [6], the method leverages gradient information, predicts vertex-level pseudo-signs rather than edge sign flips, and requires no data augmentations beyond noise injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a ~1-minute training on 80 shapes yields a method that generalizes across datasets (ABC, MGN garments, ShapeNet-Cars) and works with both primal and dual meshing algorithms without parameter tuning, removing a major usability barrier in UDF-based reconstruction pipelines. Key limitations include jagged edges when coupled with Marching Cubes (inherent to MC's edge-based vertex placement), a tendency to produce small cracks compared to surface-following heuristics, and sensitivity to gradient noise near surfaces. Open questions include whether a tighter integration with Dual Contouring's quadratic optimization (beyond the current simple cell-center fallback) could further improve accuracy, and whether the approach can be extended to handle the inherent ambiguity of open surface orientation more explicitly.
