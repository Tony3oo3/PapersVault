## Problem

Prevailing MLLMs rely on CLIP-ViT as the vision encoder, which captures global, low-frequency representations but struggles with fine-grained local details such as object counting, spatial relationships, and structural intricacies. While masked image modeling (MIM) excels at capturing these local patterns, no prior work has effectively unified CL and MIM representations within an MLLM framework without depending on instruction-tuning datasets or curated visual grounding annotations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (processed by two frozen encoders: CLIP-ViT for global features and MAE-ViT with random patch masking for local features) alongside a text input (question or caption). It produces a fixed set of learnable query embeddings that are projected into a frozen LLM's embedding space for text generation (answers, captions), while simultaneously feeding enriched MAE features to a frozen MAE decoder for reconstructing the masked image patches. The dual output path—text generation and image reconstruction—is a distinguishing structural choice that forces the queries to encode both semantic and pixel-level information.

## Method

X-Former extends Q-Former with a dual cross-attention module that dynamically fuses CLIP-ViT and MAE-ViT features. In the first cross-attention block, MAE features (M) serve as queries attending to Q-Former's global output (Z_q) as keys/values, producing enriched MAE representations (M') that carry both local detail and global semantics; M' is then passed to a frozen MAE decoder for masked-patch reconstruction. In the second cross-attention block, M' enhances Z_q into the final query set Z' by injecting local information, and Z' is optimized with standard vision-language objectives (ITC, ITM, ITG). Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 jointly optimizes reconstruction, contrastive, matching, and generation losses on 14M image-text pairs with both encoders frozen; Stage 2 aligns the pre-trained queries to a frozen OPT LLM via a single fully-connected layer trained with language-modeling loss. This design avoids the need for instruction-tuning data and achieves fine-grained gains (e.g., +13% on COCO object counting over BLIP-2) using roughly one-tenth the pre-training data.

## Results

On VQAv2 (zero-shot), X-Former with OPT-6.7B achieves 55.0 overall accuracy (45.6 Other, 73.3 Yes/No, 37.8 Number), surpassing BLIP-2 trained on the same 14M pairs (52.4) and approaching the official BLIP-2 checkpoint trained on 129M pairs (55.1). On GQA, X-Former (OPT-6.7B) reaches 34.9 vs. 33.1 for BLIP-2 (14M), and on OKVQA it achieves 34.2 vs. 31.5. On fine-grained object counting (COCO), X-Former scores 39.64 vs. 25.88 for BLIP-2 (14M) and 34.3 for the official BLIP-2 (129M), a 13% relative improvement over the same-data baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BLIP-2 trained on the same 14M image-text pairs, X-Former consistently wins: +1.4% on VQAv2 (51.3 vs 49.9, OPT-2.7B) and +2.6% (55.0 vs 52.4, OPT-6.7B), +1.8% on GQA (34.9 vs 33.1), and +2.7% on OKVQA (34.2 vs 31.5). Remarkably, X-Former trained on only 14M pairs matches or exceeds the official BLIP-2 checkpoint trained on 129M pairs (10× more data) on VQAv2 (55.0 vs 55.1) and GQA (34.9 vs 34.2), though it trails slightly on OKVQA (34.2 vs 35.3). On fine-grained object counting, X-Former outperforms the 129M BLIP-2 by 5.3 points on COCO (39.64 vs 34.3) and 8.3 points on VCR (27.24 vs 18.9). Against older baselines (FewVLM, VLKD, Frozen), X-Former achieves substantially higher scores across all VQA benchmarks. On image captioning (COCO/NoCaps), X-Former is essentially on par with BLIP-2 (14M), confirming that the gains in fine-grained reasoning come without sacrificing global semantic understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-Former extends BLIP-2's Q-Former by introducing a dual cross-attention module that fuses features from two frozen vision encoders—CLIP-ViT (contrastive learning, capturing global/low-frequency semantics) and MAE-ViT (masked image modeling, capturing local/high-frequency details). This departs from the prevailing MLLM paradigm of relying solely on CLIP-ViT and from prior attempts to combine CL and MIM by linearly blending training objectives in a single encoder (e.g., Weers et al.), instead leveraging off-the-shelf pre-trained encoders with a lightweight interaction module. The paper also positions itself against methods requiring curated instruction-tuning data (Shikra, GVT, MMVP), demonstrating that the approach works with standard image-text pairs alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that fine-grained visual understanding in MLLMs can be substantially improved (13% on object counting) using only 1/10 the pre-training data of BLIP-2 and without instruction tuning, suggesting a more data-efficient path to detailed visual perception. The ablation showing that naive feature concatenation fails while structured dual cross-attention succeeds highlights that the interaction mechanism is critical, not merely the addition of another encoder. Open questions include whether this approach scales to larger LLMs and more diverse tasks, whether the frozen-encoder assumption limits ceiling performance, and whether the modest gains on standard VQA (1.4–2.6%) will hold as MLLM architectures evolve beyond the Q-Former paradigm.
