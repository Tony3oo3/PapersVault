## Problem

Large image-to-video (I2V) diffusion models can hallucinate complex dynamics from a single start frame but lack the ability to steer generation toward a specified end frame, making them incapable of bounded generation — the task of synthesizing in-between frames given arbitrary start and end constraints. This limitation prevents a unified approach to frame interpolation, novel view synthesis from wide-baseline pairs, and video looping, which existing methods address separately with domain-specific inductive biases.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images (a start frame and an end frame) as input and produces a video sequence that smoothly connects them, operating entirely within the latent space of Stable Video Diffusion. The three evaluation scenarios impose different constraints: dynamic bounds require generating subject/object motion between temporally distant snapshots, view bounds require synthesizing a camera trajectory between two viewpoints of a static scene, and identical bounds (same image for both) require a seamless looping video. The curated evaluation dataset contains 426 high-resolution image pairs spanning human kinematics, camera motion, and stochastic phenomena like fire and water.

## Method

Time Reversal Fusion (TRF) is a training-free sampling strategy that runs two parallel denoising paths from identical initial noise — a forward path conditioned on the start frame and a backward path conditioned on the end frame — through the frozen SVD 3D-UNet. At each denoising step, the backward path's temporal order is reversed and the two paths are fused via a per-frame weighted average (the closed-form solution of a joint least-squares optimization), where the weight α is adjusted linearly or exponentially based on proximity to each bounding frame. To prevent blending artifacts and abrupt transitions caused by dynamic mismatches between the two paths, TRF introduces noise re-injection: at early denoising steps, supplementary Gaussian noise is added to the fused latent followed by additional denoising iterations, allowing the sampling trajectory to realign with SVD's learned dynamics. The approach requires no fine-tuning, pixel correspondence, or motion assumptions, and the only tuning parameters are the motion bucket ID and frames-per-second setting of the underlying SVD model.

## Results

On the Bounded Generation Dataset, Time Reversal Fusion (TRF) applied to frozen Stable Video Diffusion achieves FVD25 of 431.16 on Dynamics Bound (vs. 656.88 for FILM), FVD25 of 458.91 on Identical Bound (vs. 911.67 for T2C), and FID192 of 10.31 / FID64 of 3.43 on View Bound (vs. 28.70 / 8.67 for Du et al.). On View Bound, TRF also substantially improves 3D consistency, recovering 884.08 COLMAP correspondences compared to 379.61 for the baseline. Overall, TRF outperforms the closest baselines by approximately 30–60% across all three subtasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Dynamics Bound, TRF reduces FVD25 from 656.88 (FILM) to 431.16, a 30% improvement, because FILM relies on flow-based correspondence and fails on large or semantically complex motions (e.g., articulated human kinematics). On Identical Bound, TRF nearly halves the FVD25 score from 911.67 (T2C) to 458.91 (~50% reduction), producing more natural looping motion (e.g., wave dynamics) without requiring scene segmentation or Eulerian motion priors. On View Bound, TRF achieves FID192 of 10.31 versus 28.70 for Du et al. (a 60% improvement) and FID64 of 3.43 versus 8.67, while also recovering 884.08 COLMAP 3D correspondences compared to 379.61, indicating substantially better 3D consistency across wide-baseline views. TRF wins on every metric across all three subtasks, and the comparisons are against the closest existing method for each specific scenario rather than a single unified baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as a frozen I2V backbone and introduces Time Reversal Fusion (TRF), a training-free sampling strategy that runs two parallel denoising paths—one conditioned on the start frame and one on the end frame (time-reversed)—then fuses them via a per-frame weighted average. Conceptually, TRF extends the path-fusion ideas from MultiDiffusion and DiffCollage (which merge spatially parallel diffusion paths for large images) to the temporal axis of video generation. The paper explicitly departs from two intuitive alternatives: condition manipulation (linearly interpolating the conditioning image over time) and temporal inpainting (replacing the last frame at each denoising step), both of which fail due to SVD's forward arrow-of-time bias in its training data and positional encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying frame interpolation, wide-baseline novel view synthesis, and video looping under a single 'bounded generation' formulation, the paper provides a practical, training-free control mechanism for any I2V model, preserving the base model's generalization without fine-tuning. The ability to probe an I2V model's implicit dynamics understanding by comparing generated in-betweening to ground-truth trajectories opens a new diagnostic avenue. However, the approach requires careful per-input tuning of motion bucket ID and fps, is validated only on SVD, and the evaluation dataset (426 pairs) is relatively small; whether TRF transfers cleanly to other I2V architectures or longer sequences remains open.
