## Problem

Existing self-supervised point cloud sequence representation learning methods rely on coarse temporal signals (e.g., frame order prediction, temporal cardinality difference) or frame-level contrastive learning, which fail to precisely capture the motion dynamics and global semantic structure inherent in point cloud sequences. This insufficient exploitation of temporal and semantic information limits the quality of learned representations for downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242043/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud sequence of L frames with N points per frame (24×1024 during pre-training, 24×2048 during fine-tuning) and partitions it into K point tubes via farthest point sampling with spatial radius r and temporal window l=3. During pre-training, the encoder outputs feature representations of visible point tubes; during downstream evaluation, a classification head is attached to produce action or gesture class labels. Motion trajectory supervision signals (displacement vectors between adjacent frames, dimension 3×(L−1) per point) are extracted offline via CorrNet3D and are only used during pre-training.

## Method

M2PSC integrates three pretext tasks into a single masked autoencoder framework (P4Transformer or PST-Transformer encoder, 75% masking ratio). First, a lightweight 4-layer transformer motion trajectory decoder predicts per-point displacement vectors between adjacent frames from visible point tube features plus learnable point tokens, providing precise temporal supervision that replaces the coarse temporal cardinality difference used by MaST-Pre. Second, a global semantic contrast task treats visible and masked point tubes as natural positive pairs: a 2-layer MLP with global pooling maps each side to a semantic embedding, and a symmetric InfoNCE loss (τ=0.01) pulls them together while pushing apart other samples in the batch. Third, a standard appearance reconstruction head predicts masked point coordinates with Chamfer distance loss. The total training objective is the sum of all three losses, and the pre-trained encoder is transferred to downstream action/gesture recognition via end-to-end fine-tuning, semi-supervised learning, or cross-dataset transfer.

## Results

On MSRAction-3D, M2PSC achieves 94.84% top-1 accuracy with PST-Transformer backbone and 93.03% with P4Transformer, surpassing the supervised PST-Transformer (93.73%) and Kinet (93.27%). On NTU-RGBD, it reaches 91.3% under end-to-end fine-tuning and 88.7% with only 50% labeled data (semi-supervised). In the transfer learning setting on NvGesture and SHREC'17, M2PSC attains 89.6% and 92.8% accuracy at 50 epochs, outperforming the supervised P4Transformer (87.7% / 91.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

M2PSC consistently outperforms its closest self-supervised competitor MaST-Pre across all four datasets: +1.74% on MSRAction-3D (P4Transformer), +0.76% on MSRAction-3D (PST-Transformer), +0.5% on NTU-RGBD, and +0.3%/+0.4% on NvGesture/SHREC'17. Against supervised baselines, M2PSC with PST-Transformer (94.84%) exceeds the best supervised method Kinet (93.27%) by 1.57% on MSRAction-3D, and with P4Transformer (93.03%) it surpasses supervised P4Transformer (90.94%) by 2.09%. On NTU-RGBD, M2PSC (91.3%) edges out supervised PST-Transformer (91.0%) and P4Transformer (90.2%). In the semi-supervised setting (50% labels), M2PSC (88.7%) outperforms MaST-Pre (87.8%) and PointCMP (88.5%), though it remains below fully supervised methods. The only comparison where M2PSC does not lead is against PointCMP (93.27%) and CPR (93.03%) on MSRAction-3D with P4Transformer backbone, where it ties or trails by a narrow margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder framework for point cloud sequences introduced by MaST-Pre [27], which used point reconstruction and a coarse temporal cardinality difference as pretext tasks. The key departure is replacing that crude temporal signal with explicit motion trajectory prediction, adapted from video self-supervised learning (MME [31], dense trajectory clustering [32]) via the pre-trained CorrNet3D correspondence model. A second novelty is the addition of a global semantic contrast loss that treats visible and masked point tubes as natural positive pairs, building on the contrastive learning line (CPR [29], PointCMP [28], PointCPSC [30]) but operating at the tube level within the MAE framework rather than at the frame or clip level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap—prior self-supervised point cloud sequence methods underutilize temporal structure—and the ablation confirms that motion trajectory prediction is the dominant driver of performance gains. However, the approach's reliance on CorrNet3D (a pre-trained model trained specifically for human body point clouds) limits generality and partially undermines the self-supervised framing, since the supervision signal depends on an external model. Open questions include whether the method transfers to non-human or object-centric point cloud sequences, whether learned (rather than pre-trained) correspondence could close the loop, and whether the modest 1–2% gains over MaST-Pre persist on larger, more diverse benchmarks.
