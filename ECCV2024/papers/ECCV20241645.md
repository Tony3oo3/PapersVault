## Problem

Existing MLLMs either handle coarse-grained tasks (captioning, VQA) or fine-grained recognition (grounding, detection) but struggle to scale up for both simultaneously. A key challenge is how to efficiently integrate knowledge from diverse data domains, multiple visual encoder architectures, and high-resolution image processing without modifying the frozen vision encoders or incurring prohibitive training costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241645/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SPHINX takes an RGB image (up to 448×448 or higher) and a text instruction as input. For high-resolution processing, the input image is spatially decomposed into five 224×224 views: one globally downsampled view plus four corner sub-images cropped from a 448×448 intermediate, all encoded independently by the frozen vision encoders. The model produces free-form text responses that can embed structured outputs such as bounding box coordinates, region-level descriptions, object detections with spatial relations, and human pose keypoints, all in a multi-turn conversation format.

## Method

SPHINX introduces a three-fold mixing strategy around a frozen LLaMA-2-13B backbone. First, weight mixing linearly interpolates LLM parameters from a real-data pre-trained checkpoint (LAION-400M) and a synthetic-data fine-tuned checkpoint (LAION-COCO) via θ_mix = β·θ_real + (1−β)·θ_syn, avoiding domain-contradictory joint training. Second, visual embedding mixing ensembles four vision encoders spanning CNN (CLIP-ConvNeXt), ViT (CLIP-ViT, DINOv2-ViT), and global summarization (Q-Former), channel-wise concatenating patch-level tokens and spatial-wise concatenating with Q-Former global features before projecting into the LLM input space. Third, scale mixing feeds the five 224×224 views (one global + four high-res corners) through the frozen encoders and concatenates all token groups as a long sequence, forcing the LLM to perform cross-patch interaction as an implicit vision decoder. Training is two-stage: pre-training with unfrozen LLM for vision-language alignment (jointly with RefinedWeb text to prevent catastrophic forgetting), followed by multi-task instruction fine-tuning on detection, grounding, OCR, VQA, and pose estimation datasets.

## Results

SPHINX-1k achieves state-of-the-art results on 7 out of 11 MLLM benchmarks, including POPE (90.8 vs. 85.9 for LLaVA1.5-13B), MME-Perception (1560.2 vs. 1531.3), SEED-Bench (71.6 vs. 61.6), and CC-Bench (27.9 vs. 26.5). SPHINX-2k further leads on MM-Vet (40.2), LLaVA-Wild (76.9), MathVista (27.8), and TouchStone (659.5). On visual grounding (RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg), SPHINX-1k reaches 86.64/91.08/80.35 (val/test-A/test-B on RefCOCO+), surpassing all generalist baselines and approaching the specialist model UNINEXT (85.24/89.63/79.79). The introduction of high-resolution sub-images (SPHINX-1k vs. SPHINX) yields a notable POPE improvement from 80.7 to 90.8, indicating reduced visual hallucination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPHINX-1k outperforms LLaVA1.5-13B on POPE (90.8 vs. 85.9), MME-Perception (1560.2 vs. 1531.3), SEED-Bench (71.6 vs. 61.6), and LLaVA-Wild (74.3 vs. 70.7), while SPHINX-2k additionally leads on MM-Vet (40.2 vs. 35.4) and LLaVA-Wild (76.9 vs. 70.7). Against Qwen-VL-7B-Chat, SPHINX-1k wins on MME-Perception (1560.2 vs. 1487.6), SEED (71.6 vs. 58.2), and MMBench (67.1 vs. 60.6), but loses on CC-Bench (27.9 vs. 39.3), which the authors attribute to insufficient text-related pre-training data. On visual grounding (Table 3), SPHINX-1k surpasses all generalist baselines including Qwen-VL-7B-Chat (86.64 vs. 82.82 on RefCOCO+ val) and Shikra-13B (86.64 vs. 82.89), and approaches the specialist UNINEXT (86.64 vs. 85.24). SPHINX-2k achieves the best TouchStone score (659.5) and MM-Vet score (40.2) among all compared models. The base SPHINX (without high-resolution sub-images) trails LLaVA1.5-13B on POPE (80.7 vs. 85.9) but matches or exceeds it on MMBench (66.9 vs. 67.7) and SEED (69.1 vs. 61.6), confirming that the scale-mixing strategy is critical for hallucination-sensitive tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPHINX builds on the LLaMA-2 backbone and the LLaMA-Adapter instruction-tuning framework, but departs from prior MLLMs (LLaVA, BLIP-2, Qwen-VL, Shikra) by introducing a three-fold mixing strategy: linearly combining LLM weights trained on real-world vs. synthetic data, ensembling visual embeddings from heterogeneous backbones (CLIP-ConvNeXt, CLIP-ViT, DINOv2, Q-Former), and concatenating multi-scale sub-image tokens. The weight-mixing idea draws from the model-soups/weight-interpolation literature but is novel in applying it to domain-specific MLLM pre-training. The multi-scale approach explicitly contrasts with the common practice of fine-tuning vision encoders at higher resolution (as in Qwen-VL), instead keeping all encoders frozen and delegating cross-scale fusion to the LLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-fold mixing provides a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe for integrating diverse data domains and visual encoders without modifying the vision backbones, which is valuable as MLLM training scales to more heterogeneous sources. The finding that high-resolution sub-images substantially reduce hallucination (POPE 80.7→90.8) and that a global low-resolution view is essential for grounding (sub-images alone degrade performance below baseline) offers actionable guidance for multi-scale MLLM design. Key limitations include the LLaMA context-length constraint preventing multi-image, video, or very large image inputs, and the lack of text-specific pre-training data that limits OCR performance relative to Qwen-VL. Open questions include whether the mixing strategy generalizes to long-context LLMs and whether additional encoder or domain combinations yield further gains.
