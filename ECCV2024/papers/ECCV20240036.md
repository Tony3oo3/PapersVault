## Problem

Existing unsupervised knowledge distillation (UKD) methods rely on L2-normalized embedding features to preserve pairwise sample relationships, but L2 normalization is a non-invertible, non-bijective mapping that destroys the original manifold structure. This means prior UKD approaches cannot faithfully replicate the teacher's embedding manifold, overlooking spatial relationships that are critical for downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240036/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and produces student embedding feature vectors (2048-dim, matching the teacher's penultimate-layer output via a small projection head). Before distillation, an offline step computes k-nearest-neighbour sets for every training sample using the frozen teacher's L2-normalized features; during training, each mini-batch is augmented with these pre-computed neighbours to capture local manifold structure. The distilled student's embeddings are then used for linear probing, kNN classification, dense prediction (detection boxes and segmentation masks), or image retrieval.

## Method

CoSS introduces a space similarity loss (L_ss) that transposes the teacher and student feature matrices and computes cosine similarity along the spatial (dimension) axis, motivating each student feature dimension to align with its teacher counterpart up to a scale factor. This is combined with the standard cosine similarity loss (L_co) on L2-normalized sample features, yielding the total objective L_CoSS = L_co + λ·L_ss. The key theoretical insight is that normalizing along the spatial dimension scales all data points identically, making the induced mapping continuous, bijective, and invertible (i.e., a homeomorphism), whereas normalizing along the sample axis collapses all points on a ray to a single hypersphere point. Training uses offline kNN batch augmentation (k=15 neighbours appended per sample) to ensure local neighbourhood information is available, and the method requires no feature queues, contrastive objectives, or heavy augmentations.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with a MoCo-v2 ResNet-50 teacher, CoSS achieves 62.35% top-1 accuracy for ResNet-18, 64.01% for ResNet-34, and 67.36% for EfficientNet-B0, outperforming the strongest baselines (BINGO and DisCo) by 0.95–3.62 points across architectures. For kNN-10 classification, CoSS reaches 58.33% on EfficientNet-B0, a 3.55-point gain over DisCo (54.78%). In dense prediction on MS-COCO, the CoSS-distilled ResNet-18 student achieves 33.9 AP^bbox and 30.2 AP^mask, surpassing the purpose-built PCD method (32.2 / 29.0). On image retrieval (Oxford-5k), CoSS attains 20.59 mAP (medium) and 5.50 mAP (hard), leading all baselines on the medium split.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSS consistently outperforms all compared UKD methods on top-1 ImageNet accuracy: it beats BINGO by 0.95 (R-18), 0.51 (R-34), and 3.62 (Eff-B0) points, and DisCo by 1.75, 1.51, and 0.86 points respectively. Against SEED, the gains are larger (4.75–6.06 points), reflecting SEED's reliance on a feature queue and contrastive objective. On kNN-10, CoSS leads DisCo and SMD on ResNet-34 (54.80 vs. 53.65 / 52.61) and EfficientNet-B0 (58.33 vs. 54.78), though BINGO edges it slightly on ResNet-18 (54.16 vs. 53.78). In dense prediction (Table 3), CoSS surpasses PCD on MS-COCO (33.9 vs. 32.2 AP^bbox) and matches or exceeds it on VOC, despite PCD being specifically designed for dense tasks. On transfer learning (Table 2), CoSS achieves the best top-1 on 6/7 datasets for ResNet-18 and 7/7 for EfficientNet-B0, with the largest margin on CIFAR-100 (70.11 vs. DisCo's 67.50 for R-18). For robustness (Table 5), CoSS leads all baselines across every ImageNet-C corruption type and ImageNet-v2/Sketch, outperforming BINGO (which uses CutMix augmentation) by 1.9–4.4 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoSS extends the unsupervised knowledge distillation (UKD) framework initiated by SEED [18] and builds directly on the pairwise cosine-similarity feature matching objective from SimReg [42], which it retains as its L_co component. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a 'space similarity' loss (L_ss) that operates on the transposed feature matrix, motivated by a topological argument that L2 normalization is not a homeomorphism and thus irreversibly destroys the teacher's embedding manifold structure. Unlike SEED, BINGO, and DisCo, CoSS requires no feature queues, no contrastive objectives, and no heavy augmentations, positioning itself as a simpler alternative that directly models the teacher's latent geometry rather than handcrafting preservation-worthy sample relationships.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a single matrix transpose plus cosine similarity along spatial dimensions can consistently improve UKD performance across classification, transfer, dense prediction, retrieval, and robustness benchmarks without additional infrastructure. However, the gains over strong baselines (BINGO, DisCo) are modest (1–2% top-1), and the homeomorphism guarantee only ensures alignment up to a per-dimension scale, leaving room for stronger topological constraints. The paper explicitly leaves open the extension to NLP and to settings where the per-dimension scaling is not sufficient, and notes that integrating CoSS into dense-prediction-specific frameworks like PCD could yield further gains. For practitioners, the main value is a drop-in loss component that simplifies the UKD pipeline while matching or slightly exceeding more complex methods.
