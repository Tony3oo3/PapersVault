## Problem

Few-shot novel view synthesis faces a persistent trade-off: NeRF-based methods produce high-quality renders but require hours of training and cannot achieve real-time inference, while 3D Gaussian Splatting renders at real-time speeds but degrades severely under sparse views due to inadequate scene coverage from sparse COLMAP initialization, oversmoothed textures, and overfitting to the limited training views. No existing method simultaneously delivers photo-realistic quality and real-time rendering from as few as 3 input images.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–24 sparse RGB images (504×378 to 1008×756 depending on dataset), camera poses, and a sparse point cloud all extracted from COLMAP patch-match stereo using only the training views. During optimization, a pre-trained DPT model provides monocular depth maps as auxiliary geometric supervision, and virtual pseudo-views are rendered online from interpolated camera poses with 3DoF Gaussian noise. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation (typically ~57K Gaussians on LLFF, fewer than 3D-GS's ~63K) that renders novel views at 200–460 FPS on an A6000 GPU, with training completing in ~9.5 minutes (LLFF) or ~24 minutes (Mip-NeRF360).

## Method

FSGS builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting but replaces its gradient-based densification with Proximity-guided Gaussian Unpooling: a proximity graph connects each Gaussian to its K nearest neighbors, and when a Gaussian's average neighbor distance exceeds a threshold, new Gaussians are inserted at edge midpoints with scale/opacity inherited from the destination and rotation/SH coefficients zeroed, progressively filling observation gaps. To counter overfitting from sparse parallax, the method synthesizes pseudo-views by interpolating camera poses between the two closest training views (with random 3DoF location noise) and applies a Pearson-correlation depth loss between differentiable alpha-blended rendered depth and DPT monocular depth estimates on both real and pseudo views. The total loss combines L1, D-SSIM photometric terms with the depth correlation regularization (λ = 0.8, 0.2, 0.05), with pseudo-view sampling activated after 2,000 iterations and unpooling triggered every 100 iterations over 10,000 total optimization steps.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset (3 training views, 1/8 resolution), FSGS achieves 20.31 PSNR, 0.652 SSIM, and 0.288 LPIPS at 458 FPS, outperforming the previous best few-shot method SparseNeRF (19.86 PSNR, 0.624 SSIM) by 0.45 dB in PSNR while running 2,180× faster. On Mip-NeRF360 (24 training views, 1/8 resolution), FSGS reaches 23.70 PSNR and 0.745 SSIM at 290 FPS, surpassing SparseNeRF by 0.85 dB in PSNR and 0.052 in SSIM while being 4,142× faster. FSGS also outperforms the dense-view 3D-GS baseline on both quality and speed (458 vs. 385 FPS on LLFF), using fewer Gaussians (57,513 vs. 63,219).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSGS outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FPS) across both LLFF and Mip-NeRF360. Against the closest few-shot competitor SparseNeRF, FSGS gains 0.45 dB PSNR and 0.028 SSIM on LLFF while accelerating inference from 0.21 to 458 FPS (2,180× speedup); on Mip-NeRF360 the PSNR gap widens to 0.85 dB with a 4,142× speedup. Compared to the dense-view 3D-GS baseline, FSGS improves PSNR by 2.88 dB on LLFF (3 views) and 2.81 dB on Mip-NeRF360 (24 views), while also rendering faster (458 vs. 385 FPS) due to using fewer Gaussians. Among the NeRF-based few-shot methods, FreeNeRF (19.63 PSNR) and RegNeRF (19.08 PSNR) are the strongest quality baselines on LLFF but remain 2,000+× slower than FSGS. DietNeRF and Mip-NeRF/Mip-NeRF360 are substantially weaker on both quality and speed, confirming that the Proximity-guided Gaussian Unpooling and pseudo-view depth regularization are critical for sparse-view performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSGS builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework of Kerbl et al., adapting its explicit point-based representation to the few-shot novel view synthesis setting previously dominated by NeRF-based methods (SparseNeRF, FreeNeRF, RegNeRF, DietNeRF). The key conceptual departure is replacing 3D-GS's gradient-based densification with a Proximity-guided Gaussian Unpooling strategy inspired by mesh subdivision, and augmenting training with synthesized pseudo-views regularized by monocular depth priors (DPT). This positions FSGS at the intersection of two research threads: the efficiency gains of explicit Gaussian representations and the geometric regularization techniques developed for sparse-view NeRF, but reframed within a rasterization-based pipeline rather than a volume-rendering one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSGS demonstrates that real-time (200+ FPS) few-shot view synthesis is achievable with as few as three input views while simultaneously outperforming both NeRF-based few-shot methods and standard 3D-GS in rendering quality, making it a practical option for VR/AR and interactive applications where capture is expensive. The finding that fewer, better-placed Gaussians (57K vs 63K) yield both faster rendering and higher quality than 3D-GS's denser representation is a useful insight for the broader 3D-GS community. However, the method cannot generalize to occluded regions unobserved during training, and its reliance on COLMAP for initialization and DPT for depth priors introduces dependencies that may limit robustness in challenging real-world capture scenarios. Open questions include whether the proximity-based densification strategy transfers to dynamic scenes or to settings with even fewer (1–2) views.
