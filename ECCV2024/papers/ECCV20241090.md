## Problem

Current Transformer-based deraining methods achieve global modeling through spatial-domain self-attention, incurring high computational cost with diminishing performance gains. Additionally, most supervised deraining approaches either discard negative (rainy) samples entirely or apply generic perceptual contrastive regularization that fails to exploit the distinctive frequency-domain signatures of rain streak patterns.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single rainy RGB image (H×W×3) and outputs a clean RGB image via a residual formulation (Y = I + R), where R is the predicted rain layer. Training operates on 256×256 patches with a hierarchical encoder-decoder of depth {4,8,10,8,4} and initial channel width 32, using pixel-shuffle/unshuffle for resolution transitions. The model contains 6.958M parameters and 48.51G FLOPs at 256×256 resolution, substantially lighter than competing Transformer derainers.

## Method

FADformer replaces self-attention with a Fused Fourier Convolution Mixer (FFCM) that extracts multi-scale local features via depth-wise convolutions, then applies DFT to blend them in the frequency domain with learnable 1×1 convolutions before inverse DFT, achieving global receptive fields at convolution-level cost. A Prior-Gated Feed-forward Network (PGFN) then refines local details by gating a DConv-FFN with Residue Channel Prior features (max−min across RGB channels) propagated through a lightweight iterative branch. For training, a Frequency-domain Contrastive Regularization (FCR) term computes the ratio of L1 distances in DFT space between the prediction and the ground truth (positive) versus randomly sampled other rainy images (negatives), pulling the output toward clean frequency patterns while pushing it away from diverse rain streak signatures. The total loss is L1 pixel loss plus 0.1×FCR, and FCR is shown to be model-agnostic, improving other deraining and dehazing networks when added to their training.

## Results

FADformer achieves state-of-the-art deraining performance across all four synthetic benchmarks and the real-world SPA-Data, reaching 41.80 dB PSNR / 0.9906 SSIM on Rain200L, 32.48 dB / 0.9359 on Rain200H, 34.42 dB / 0.9602 on DDN-Data, 35.48 dB / 0.9657 on DID-Data, and 49.21 dB / 0.9934 on SPA-Data (first method to exceed 49 dB). The model uses only 6.96M parameters and 48.51G FLOPs (256×256 patch), substantially lighter than the closest Transformer-based competitors. On the unpaired Internet-Data, FADformer attains the lowest BRISQUE (25.96) and SSEQ (26.67) among all compared methods, confirming superior generalization to real-world scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FADformer outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest concurrent Transformer-based method DRSformer (CVPR'23), FADformer gains +0.57 dB PSNR on Rain200L, +0.31 dB on Rain200H, +0.67 dB on SPA-Data, while using only 20.65% of the parameters (6.96M vs. 33.70M) and 19.97% of the FLOPs (48.51G vs. 242.9G). Compared to the efficient CNN-based SPDNet (ICCV'21), FADformer improves by +1.30 dB on Rain200L and +6.01 dB on SPA-Data with comparable parameter count (6.96M vs. 2.98M). Against the large Restormer (CVPR'22, 26.10M params), FADformer leads by +0.81 dB on Rain200L and +1.23 dB on SPA-Data while being 3.7× smaller. On the unpaired Internet-Data, FADformer achieves the best no-reference scores (BRISQUE 25.96, SSEQ 26.67), surpassing DRSformer (26.08, 27.95) and SPDNet (26.02, 27.51). The FCR component is further validated as model-agnostic: when added to RCDNet and MPRNet it yields +0.25 and +0.30 dB respectively, and when applied to dehazing (FFA-Net, Dehazeformer-B) it improves PSNR by +0.90 and +0.56 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

FADformer builds on Fast Fourier Convolution (FFC) for its global modeling component, extending it with a spatial-frequency concatenation design that blends multi-scale depth-wise convolutions before the Fourier transform, unlike FFC's pure frequency-domain operation or Fourmer's frequency-only approach. The Prior-Gated Feed-forward Network repurposes the Residue Channel Prior (originally from optical flow in rainy scenes) as a learnable gating signal for a DConv-based FFN, a novel integration not seen in prior deraining architectures. For training, the paper departs from VGG-based contrastive perceptual learning (CPL) by using the DFT itself as the contrastive encoder, arguing that frequency-domain representations naturally separate rain streak patterns from content in a way semantic feature extractors do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5× reduction in parameters and FLOPs relative to the prior SOTA (DRSformer) while simultaneously improving PSNR makes FADformer practically relevant for deployment-constrained deraining pipelines. FCR's demonstrated model-agnosticism (improving RCDNet, MPRNet, and even dehazing methods like FFA-Net) suggests it could be adopted as a plug-in regularization across low-level restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain contrastive space generalizes to other degradation types beyond rain (e.g., motion blur, sensor noise) and whether the efficiency advantage persists at higher resolutions where self-attention's quadratic cost becomes more prohibitive.
