## Problem

Existing methods for synthesizing time-varying BRDFs rely on parametric BRDF models (e.g., Torrance-Sparrow) and polynomial temporal models to represent material appearance changes over time. Both the parametric representation and the polynomial temporal model have limited expressiveness, restricting the fidelity of synthesized temporal appearance, especially when transferring changes between materials with large differences in reflectance properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241892/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source time-varying BTF (e.g., 36 frames at 300×300 from the STAF database) and a target static BRDF or BTF as input, and produces a synthesized time-varying BTF. The BRDF encoder consumes m=16,200 sets of half-vector parameterized angles and RGB reflectances per pixel, while the NTM operates on per-pixel latent vectors (3-dim color + 7-dim permutation-invariant) sequentially across timesteps. Synthesis is conducted per-pixel, with individual BRDF estimates arranged into a full BTF.

## Method

The approach disentangles static BRDF representation from temporal modeling in a learned latent space. A branched encoder-decoder (trained on MERL and Disney principled BRDFs) embeds each BRDF into a 3-dim color latent vector (extracted via channel-wise median) and a 7-dim permutation-invariant latent vector (extracted via self-attention and class-attention over concatenated angle-reflectance samples), avoiding the need for color-channel permutation data augmentation. A lightweight Neural Temporal Model (NTM) with two parallel LSTM blocks—one for color and one for permutation-invariant vectors—learns to predict the sequence of latent vectors from the initial timestep, trained on source time-varying BTFs. At synthesis time, the NTM is conditioned on the target BRDF's latent vector at t=0 and generates the full temporal sequence, which is decoded back to reflectances. This latent-space formulation replaces both the rigid parametric BRDF model and the polynomial temporal model, enabling faithful transfer of temporal changes even across materials with large specularity gaps.

## Results

On the synthetic BTF synthesis task, the proposed method achieves a mean final-frame PSNR of 39.2 dB for both temporal models (S1 and S2), substantially outperforming STT which scores 30.9 dB (S1) and 36.2 dB (S2). The gap is most pronounced under S1 with α=1.0, where the proposed method reaches 38.0 dB versus STT's 29.6 dB. For BRDF reconstruction, the proposed encoder-decoder matches BRDFNPs on MERL BRDFs but surpasses both BRDFNPs and NBRDF on Disney-principled synthetic BRDFs, particularly for rough materials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against STT [17], the primary synthesis baseline, the proposed method wins decisively on final-frame PSNR: 39.2 vs 30.9 dB under S1 (an 8.3 dB gap) and 39.2 vs 36.2 dB under S2. The advantage grows with increasing temporal parameter α and roughness offset β, where STT's polynomial model struggles to transfer specular changes across materials with large reflectance gaps. For BRDF reconstruction (Figure 5), the proposed encoder-decoder matches BRDFNPs [27] on average for MERL BRDFs but clearly outperforms it on Disney-principled synthetic BRDFs, especially for rough materials, thanks to the permutation-invariant architecture. Both the proposed method and BRDFNPs outperform NBRDF [21] on reconstruction PSNR across all tested BRDFs. On the real-world STAF dataset (qualitative, Figure 10), the proposed method preserves target texture and specularity where STT fails due to patch-matching errors and inability to adapt specular parameters to the target material.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural BRDF representation line initiated by BRDFNPs (Zheng et al., 2021) by introducing a branched, permutation-invariant encoder architecture that separates color and structural reflectance properties. It departs fundamentally from prior time-varying BRDF synthesis methods (STAF by Gu et al., STT by Meister et al.), which relied on parametric Torrance-Sparrow models and polynomial temporal functions, by replacing both with a learned latent-space embedding and an LSTM-based neural temporal model. The concept of operating in a shared latent space for material composition is also inspired by neural layered BRDFs (Fan et al., 2022), but here applied to temporal rather than spatial layering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a more flexible and faithful alternative to polynomial-based temporal models for synthesizing time-varying material appearances, particularly when source and target materials differ substantially in reflectance properties. However, a key limitation is that the NTM must be trained separately for each type of temporal change (burning, rusting, drying, etc.), requiring users to select the appropriate model for their target. The authors identify adaptive synthesis through material classification and user instruction as a promising future direction, and the small scale of available real-world time-varying BTF data (26 samples in STAF) remains a bottleneck for broader generalization.
