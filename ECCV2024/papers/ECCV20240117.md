## Problem

Existing Large Video-Language Models align static visual features with language through conventional question-answering instruction tuning, which captures spatial understanding but fails to model fine-grained temporal dynamics and spatial consistency. No ready-made video-language dataset provides pixel-level object trajectories that could serve as a cross-modal alignment signal across both spatial and temporal dimensions simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240117/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 100 uniformly sampled video frames at 224×224 resolution encoded by a frozen ViT-L/14 CLIP encoder, concatenated with tokenized text instructions. During training, the model additionally predicts a trajectory matrix of P=3 key points across N frames (2D coordinates per point per frame) for each object noun phrase in the caption, with absent objects marked as (-1,-1). At inference, the model produces free-form text answers, temporal segment boundaries expressed as 'from s to e' frame indices, and dense event captions with start/end timestamps.

## Method

PiTe introduces a trajectory projector (an MLP) parallel to the vocabulary mapping layer that maps LLM hidden states to P×N 2D coordinates, enabling pixel-level alignment across spatial and temporal dimensions. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) spatial localization on Localized Narratives to train the visual adapter and a localization projector, (2) pixel-temporal alignment on the newly curated PiTe-143k dataset (1.02M object trajectories from 143.6k videos) where the trajectory projector is initialized by replicating the localization projector weights P·N times, and (3) standard video QA instruction tuning on Valley and Video-ChatGPT data. The trajectory supervision uses L1 regression loss combined with cross-entropy for text generation, and trajectories are condensed to 3 key points via k-means++ to balance geometric fidelity with computational cost. The PiTe-143k dataset is built through an automated pipeline: SuPar constituency parsing for noun-phrase extraction, GLaMM for referring-expression segmentation in the first frame, and DOT dense optical tracking to recover per-object trajectories across frames.

## Results

On zero-shot video question answering, PiTe (7B) achieves 68.4% accuracy on MSVD-QA, 56.4% on MSRVTT-QA, and 42.0% on ActivityNet-QA, outperforming the strongest baseline Video-ChatGPT (64.9%, 49.3%, 35.2%) by 3.5–6.8 points. On temporal grounding (ActivityNet), PiTe (7B) reaches 22.0 mIoU and 30.4 R@0.3, surpassing Video-ChatGPT (18.9 mIoU, 26.4 R@0.3). For dense captioning, PiTe (7B) scores 21.7 CIDEr and 5.8 METEOR, a 15.9-point CIDEr gain over Video-ChatGPT (5.8 CIDEr). The 13B variant further improves to 71.6% MSVD-QA accuracy and 26.0 mIoU temporal grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiTe outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across all three QA benchmarks. The closest competitor on QA is Video-ChatGPT [26], which PiTe (7B) surpasses by 3.5 points on MSVD-QA (68.4 vs. 64.9), 7.1 points on MSRVTT-QA (56.4 vs. 49.3), and 6.8 points on ActivityNet-QA (42.0 vs. 35.2). PG-Video-LLaVA [27] is competitive on GPT-scored metrics (3.7 on MSVD-QA) but still trails PiTe by 4.3 accuracy points on MSVD-QA and 4.8 on MSRVTT-QA. On temporal grounding and dense captioning (Table 4), PiTe (7B) leads Video-ChatGPT by 3.1 mIoU (22.0 vs. 18.9) and 15.9 CIDEr (21.7 vs. 5.8), with the gap widening further for the 13B model (26.0 mIoU, 26.5 CIDEr). The 1B FrozenBiLM [44] and 7B Video-LLaMA [46] are substantially weaker, confirming that trajectory-guided alignment provides a clear advantage over vanilla instruction-tuning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

PiTe extends the LLaVA-style architecture (ViT encoder + linear projection + LLM) by introducing a trajectory-guided alignment stage that bridges the gap between static image-level pixel grounding (as in PixelLLM) and video understanding. Unlike prior LVidLMs (VideoChat, Video-LLaMA, Video-ChatGPT) that rely solely on instruction tuning with QA pairs to align visual features into language space, PiTe explicitly models fine-grained spatial-temporal correspondence by requiring the model to predict object trajectories at the pixel level. The automated annotation pipeline combining GLaMM (referring expression segmentation) and DOT (dense optical tracking) to generate trajectory labels is a practical contribution that no prior video-language dataset provided.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The trajectory-guided alignment strategy offers a principled mechanism for injecting spatial-temporal structure into LVidLMs beyond what vanilla instruction tuning provides, and the PiTe-143k dataset (1.02M object trajectories) fills a concrete gap in available resources. However, the approach is constrained by the quality of the automated annotation pipeline (small objects may be missed, GLaMM segmentation errors propagate), the 100-frame sampling limit, and the k-means++ compression of trajectories to 3 points, which may lose information for complex motions. Open questions include whether trajectory alignment generalizes to longer or higher-resolution videos, whether the method scales to multi-object interactions, and whether the gains persist when evaluated on more challenging or diverse video benchmarks beyond ActivityNet.
