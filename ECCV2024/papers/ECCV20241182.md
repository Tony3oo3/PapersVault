## Problem

State-of-the-art VLAD-based VPR methods rely solely on feature-to-cluster soft assignment weighting, which fails to discount over-represented repetitive structures (shadows, window panes, road surfaces) within a cluster — the 'burstiness' problem. Additionally, the cluster-wise residual computation becomes a significant compute bottleneck when paired with high-dimensional local features from modern encoders like DINOv2, limiting practical deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 224×224 (training) or 322×322 (evaluation), extracts N local descriptors of dimension D=768 from the backbone's final layer, and produces a compact global descriptor of 4096–8192 dimensions (after PCA whitening) suitable for cosine-similarity retrieval. The pre-pool projection can reduce local feature dimensions from 768 to as low as 64 (12× reduction) while preserving recall, directly shrinking both aggregation compute time and final descriptor size.

## Method

VLAD-BuFF extends NetVLAD with two mechanisms: (1) a learnable soft-counting layer that computes N×N pairwise dot-product similarities among L2-normalized local features, passes them through a sigmoid with learnable slope and offset to produce per-feature burstiness weights w_i, and divides the standard soft-assignment weights α_ik by w_i^p (with learnable exponent p) before residual aggregation; (2) a PCA-initialized learnable linear projection that maps local features from D to D′ dimensions before aggregation, avoiding the initialization collapse that random projections cause in cluster-center and soft-assignment learning. Both components are trained end-to-end with standard VPR losses (multi-similarity, triplet, or classification) on top of frozen or fine-tuned backbones (DINOv2 ViT-B, ResNet, VGG), with cluster centers initialized via k-means on sampled backbone features.

## Results

VLAD-BuFF (with PCAW, 8K-dim descriptor) achieves the best Recall@1 across all 9 benchmark datasets, with headline numbers of 92.4% on MSLS, 78.0% on Nordland, 95.6% on Pitts250k, 96.5% on Tokyo247, and 100% on St Lucia. The fast variant (PrePool + PCAW, 4K-dim) reduces aggregation time from 44.8 ms to 14.2 ms while retaining near-identical recall (e.g., 91.9% vs 92.4% on MSLS R@1). On the out-of-distribution Baidu Mall (indoor) dataset, VLAD-BuFF reaches 77.5% R@1, substantially ahead of the next-best method SALAD at 69.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLAD-BuFF outperforms all five baselines on every dataset for both R@1 and R@5. Against the closest competitor SALAD (same GSV/MultiSim/DINOv2 training setup, differing only in aggregation), VLAD-BuFF gains +0.5 R@1 on MSLS, +7.2 on Nordland, +1.5 on SFSM, and a striking +8.4 on Baidu Mall, confirming that burstiness-aware weighting and PCA-initialized pre-pool projection are the key differentiators. Against AnyLoc (frozen DINOv2 with hard VLAD assignment), VLAD-BuFF leads by large margins (e.g., +33.2 on MSLS, +68.4 on Nordland), underscoring the benefit of end-to-end fine-tuning. Against MixVPR, CosPlace, and EigenPlaces, VLAD-BuFF consistently leads by 4–13 R@1 points across datasets, with the largest gaps on appearance-challenging benchmarks like Nordland (+13.0 over MixVPR) and AmsterTime (+11.2 over CosPlace). The fast PrePool variant (14.2 ms) matches SALAD's inference speed while still exceeding it by 1.6–8.4 R@1 on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NetVLAD's soft-assignment VLAD aggregation by incorporating the classical burstiness-discounting idea from Jégou et al. (2009) as a learnable, differentiable soft-count layer within end-to-end VPR training — a departure from prior work that treated burstiness as a post-hoc, non-learnable correction on hand-crafted descriptors. It also introduces a PCA-initialized learnable pre-pool projection to reduce local feature dimensions before aggregation, which is orthogonal to and complementary with recent VLAD variants like SALAD (Sinkhorn-based optimal transport) and AnyLoc (frozen DINOv2 with hard assignment). The method is explicitly positioned as a drop-in improvement to the pooling layer, independent of backbone architecture, loss function, or training dataset choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PCA-initialized pre-pool projection enabling 12× local feature dimension reduction (768→64) with minimal recall loss is a practically important efficiency gain for resource-constrained embodied agents performing topological navigation. However, the paper acknowledges that absolute recall still depends heavily on large pretrained backbones (DINOv2), and generalization to indoor or out-of-distribution environments (Baidu Mall, AmsterTime) remains weak. Open questions include whether burstiness-aware weighting transfers to other aggregation paradigms (GeM, attention-based pooling), whether the PCA initialization trick generalizes beyond VLAD-style residual aggregation, and how to reduce reliance on compute-intensive backbones without sacrificing the recall gains that currently dominate SOTA performance.
