## Problem

Current state-of-the-art discrete diffusion models (DDMs) for layout generation suffer from a 'layout sticking' phenomenon: once tokens are generated, the model cannot update inharmonious elements even in later denoising steps. While rule-based post-processing can fix low-level issues like alignment, it fails to capture higher-order compositional harmony. The paper identifies that DDMs can effectively correct tokens reset to [MASK] but struggle to fix tokens replaced with other regular values, motivating a correction mechanism that selectively re-masks erroneous tokens during generation.

## Input / Output

The Layout-Corrector receives a [MASK]-free intermediate token sequence of length 5N (category, x, y, w, h for N variable-length elements, each quantized into discrete bins) along with the current timestep t, and outputs a correctness score in [0,1] per token. The full pipeline takes either all-[MASK] tokens (unconditional) or partially specified elements with known attributes held fixed (conditional, e.g., category→size+position) and produces a complete layout of 1–25 elements. The corrector is applied only at selected timesteps (default t={10,20,30}) rather than every step, and a threshold with Gumbel noise determines which tokens to reset to [MASK] for regeneration.

## Method

Layout-Corrector is a Transformer encoder (without positional encoding, since layout elements are order-agnostic) that fuses the five attribute tokens per element via an MLP, processes them through self-attention to capture global layout harmony, and outputs per-token correctness scores. It is trained as a binary classifier in the ELECTRA style: given a forward-corrupted state z_t and the DDM's predicted [MASK]-free sequence ẑ_{t-1}, the corrector learns to predict whether each token matches the original ground-truth token z_0 (rather than whether it was originally masked, as in Token-Critic). During generation, tokens scoring below a threshold (perturbed by Gumbel noise) are reset to [MASK], and the DDM regenerates them using the remaining high-scored tokens as contextual clues. The selective scheduling—applying the corrector at only a few timesteps rather than every step—provides a direct knob for the fidelity-diversity trade-off: more frequent correction increases fidelity by removing more inharmonious tokens but reduces the stochastic diversity of the DDM.

## Results

On unconditional generation, LayoutDM+Corrector achieves FID 4.79 (Rico), 4.36 (Crello), and 11.85 (PubLayNet), improving over LayoutDM alone (6.37, 5.28, 13.72). The enlarged LayoutDM*+Corrector further reduces FID to 4.23, 4.11, and 9.85 respectively, achieving the best FID on Crello among all compared methods including large models. On conditional generation (C+S→P), LayoutDM*+Corrector reaches FID 1.22 (Rico), 2.69 (Crello), and 2.05 (PubLayNet), outperforming LayoutDiffusion on Crello and PubLayNet. Layout-Corrector also mitigates fast-sampling degradation, attaining FID competitive with T'=100 using only T'=20 steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Layout-Corrector consistently improves FID when applied to MaskGIT (70.37→14.40), VQDiffusion (7.83→5.29), and LayoutDM (6.37→4.79) on Rico, whereas Token-Critic degrades VQDiffusion (7.83→15.22) and LayoutDM (6.37→17.97), demonstrating that the corrector's ELECTRA-style training objective and threshold-based masking are critical. Against the large-model LayoutDiffusion [47], LayoutDM*+Corrector wins on Crello (FID 4.11 vs. 6.61) but loses on Rico (4.23 vs. 3.84) and PubLayNet (9.85 vs. 7.57). On conditional C+S→P tasks, LayoutDM*+Corrector achieves the best FID on Crello (2.69) and PubLayNet (2.05), outperforming LayoutDiffusion (4.68, 3.09) and LayoutNUWA (which uses Code Llama 7B), though LayoutDiffusion retains a slight edge on Rico (1.13 vs. 1.22). Against LayoutNUWA [42], the corrector achieves superior FID across all three datasets despite using a substantially smaller backbone, while LayoutNUWA achieves higher Max-IoU due to its LLM-based architecture. The ablation confirms that removing self-attention, switching to mask-estimation objective, using lowest-K masking, or applying the corrector at every timestep all degrade performance, validating each design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the external-critic paradigm from non-autoregressive image generation (Token-Critic, DPC) to the layout domain, where it departs in three key ways: a binary-classifier objective that predicts alignment with the original token rather than mask status, a threshold-based masking strategy instead of fixed lowest-K selection, and a selective application schedule rather than per-timestep correction. It builds directly on LayoutDM's discrete diffusion formulation and its transition-matrix design, identifying that the standard β̄t,K=ε schedule causes token sticking, and on MaskGIT's parallel decoding as a special case of DDMs. Unlike prior layout-correction work (optimization-based alignment, RUITE, LayoutFormer++) that operates post-hoc, Layout-Corrector intervenes during generation, and unlike Token-Critic it is trained against the ground-truth layout rather than the intermediate mask state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design means any existing DDM for layout generation can be improved without retraining the base model, and the scheduling mechanism gives practitioners a simple knob to trade fidelity against diversity or speed against quality. The finding that the image-domain Token-Critic actually degrades VQDiffusion and LayoutDM performance underscores that layout tokens (few, multi-attribute, variable-length) require domain-specific correction strategies. Limitations include added memory and parameters, and the corrector lacks layout-specific relational embeddings; future work could incorporate element-relation encodings or extend the approach to continuous-attribute diffusion models.
