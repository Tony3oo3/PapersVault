## Problem

Existing text-to-texture methods for 3D meshes suffer from three co-occurring limitations: slow generation (tens of minutes per texture), visual artifacts such as seams and blurriness, and baked-in lighting that prevents proper relighting in new environments. No prior method adequately addresses all three issues simultaneously, limiting commercial adoption in gaming, film, and AR/VR pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240704/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh and a free-form text prompt as input. The mesh is rendered into a 3-channel conditioning image using three pre-defined PBR materials (non-metal/not-smooth, half-metal/half-smooth, pure metal/smooth) under a specified lighting environment, which is fed to LightControlNet alongside the text prompt. The output is a relightable PBR texture represented as a multi-resolution hash-grid with a 2-layer MLP producing per-point base color, metallicness, roughness, and bump vector, which can be exported as standard 2D UV texture maps for downstream rendering engines.

## Method

The core contribution is LightControlNet, a ControlNet variant that conditions a text-to-image diffusion model on a lighting-aware rendering (three material-based renders stacked into one image) rather than geometric cues like depth or edges, enabling explicit control over illumination in generated images. The two-stage pipeline first uses multi-view visual prompting—concatenating four canonical views into a 2×2 grid—to produce visually consistent reference images under fixed lighting, exploiting the diffusion model's learned prior for grid-organized multi-view data. In the second stage, a texture optimization alternates between a reconstruction loss on the canonical reference views and an illumination-aware SDS loss computed with LightControlNet on randomly sampled views and lighting, using a multi-resolution hash-grid as the 3D texture representation. This combined supervision, together with a distilled SD encoder (5× faster) and a material smoothness regularizer, converges in only 400 iterations—over 10× faster than Fantasia3D's 5000—while successfully disentangling lighting from surface reflectance.

## Results

On the Objaverse test set (70 meshes), FlashTex with LightControlNet achieves a KID of 2.69×10⁻³, the best among all methods, while the depth-ControlNet variant achieves the best FID of 60.49. On the 22 game-asset meshes, the LightControlNet variant achieves KID 3.34×10⁻³ and FID 83.32, again leading all baselines. In terms of speed, the depth variant runs in 2 minutes and the LightControlNet variant in 4 minutes, making them 3× and 7.5× faster than the fastest and slowest baselines respectively (TEXTure at 6 min, Fantasia3D at 30 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashTex outperforms all four baselines on both FID and KID across both the Objaverse and game-asset test sets. Against Fantasia3D [8], the closest relightable-texture competitor, FlashTex (LightControlNet) reduces KID from 8.34 to 2.69 on Objaverse and from 9.34 to 3.34 on game assets, while also being 7.5× faster (4 min vs. 30 min). Against TEXTure [42], the fastest baseline at 6 minutes, FlashTex's depth variant is 3× faster (2 min) and achieves substantially lower FID (60.49 vs. 71.64) and KID (3.96 vs. 5.43) on Objaverse. Compared to Latent-Paint [28] and Text2tex [7], FlashTex leads on both metrics on both datasets with margins of 10–60 FID points and 1–4 KID points. The LightControlNet variant trades a small FID increase (~2 points) over the depth variant for a meaningful KID improvement, reflecting better lighting disentanglement, which is also confirmed by the user study where participants prefer FlashTex over every baseline on realism, text consistency, and relightability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) by introducing LightControlNet, which conditions a text-to-image diffusion model on a lighting-specific rendering rather than geometric cues like depth or edges. The optimization stage builds directly on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling (Poole et al., ICLR 2023), but augments it with a reconstruction loss from multi-view reference images and an illumination-aware SDS term that uses LightControlNet as the score function. The material smoothness regularizer is borrowed from nvdiffrec (Munkberg et al., CVPR 2022). The key departure from prior text-to-texture methods (LatentPaint, Text2tex, TEXTure, Fantasia3D) is the explicit treatment of lighting as a controllable input throughout both generation stages, rather than allowing it to be implicitly baked into the texture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup over Fantasia3D (400 vs. 5000 SDS iterations) makes automated PBR texturing practical for interactive content pipelines in gaming and AR/VR, where per-asset generation time is a real bottleneck. The multi-view visual prompting trick—concatenating canonical views into a single grid image to enforce cross-view consistency—is a simple but effective workaround for a known failure mode of independent diffusion sampling. Remaining limitations include residual baked-in lighting for out-of-distribution meshes and material maps that are not always cleanly interpretable as metallicness/roughness, suggesting the disentanglement problem is not fully solved. An open question is whether the LightControlNet conditioning strategy generalizes to more complex or dynamic lighting scenarios beyond static environment maps.
