## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models require many neural function evaluations (NFEs) for high-quality generation, making inference slow. Existing training-free retrieval-based acceleration methods (text-based and noise-based) retrieve a single full-prompt trajectory, which introduces extraneous elements and lacks diversity because truly analogous prompts are scarce in practice. No prior method decomposes prompts into visual concepts to construct flexible, composable trajectories for acceleration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a pre-built concept knowledge base (1.2M visual concepts with their intermediate noise trajectories, derived from 1.6M unique prompts and stored in a FAISS vector database). It produces a 1024×1024 RGB image via SDXL with reduced NFEs (e.g., 35 instead of 50 for vanilla generation). The concept retrieval step itself consumes only 1.15% of total inference time on an A10G GPU, and the framework is sampler-agnostic, compatible with Euler, DDIM, DPM-Solver, and PNDM.

## Method

RECON decomposes each input prompt into visual concepts using RoBERTa for noun-phrase extraction followed by a fine-tuned CLIP model for visualness filtering (scoring each phrase against a random-noise image to exclude non-visual terms). At inference, each extracted concept is embedded and matched against the concept KB to retrieve the closest stored concept and its intermediate noise vector at step k. These noise vectors are then aggregated via a cosine-similarity-weighted sum (grounded in energy-based model compositionality), forming a 'flexible trajectory' that incorporates details from multiple related concepts while leaving room for input-specific elements. Denoising resumes from step k to T guided by the original full prompt, so non-noun details (e.g., adjectives like 'tall') still influence the final image. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no additional diffusion steps beyond the standard solver, unlike noise-based retrieval (REDI) which must run h initial steps to obtain a query noise.

## Results

With NFE fixed at 35, RECON achieves CLIP Scores of 30.19 (MS-COCO), 29.93 (Pick-a-Pic), and 31.46 (DiffusionDB), outperforming both Baseline (text-based retrieval) and REDI (noise-based retrieval) on all three datasets across CLIP Score, PickScore, and Aesthetics Score. RECON generates high-fidelity images in 6–7 seconds on an A10G GPU, compared to over 10 seconds for both REDI and Baseline, while reducing required NFEs by up to 40% relative to vanilla 50-step generation. In a user study with 200 participants, 75.5% of RECON-generated images were ranked as highest fidelity (p < 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECON consistently outperforms both baselines on every metric and dataset. Against Baseline (text-based retrieval, Agarwal et al., NSDI 2024), RECON gains +1.04 CLIP on COCO (30.19 vs. 29.15), +2.26 on Pick-a-Pic (29.93 vs. 27.67), and +1.78 on DiffusionDB (31.46 vs. 29.68), with similar margins on PickScore (+0.57 to +0.27). Against REDI (noise-based retrieval, Zhang et al., ICML 2023), the advantage is substantially larger: RECON leads by +12.02 CLIP on COCO (30.19 vs. 18.17), +8.13 on Pick-a-Pic (29.93 vs. 21.80), and +11.03 on DiffusionDB (31.46 vs. 20.43). The paper also notes a surprising finding that the simple text-based Baseline is more competitive than REDI when paired with the high-fidelity SDXL model, particularly on T2I alignment scores, yet RECON still surpasses both. RECON additionally matches or slightly exceeds Baseline on Aesthetics Score while delivering significantly better semantic alignment, and it is faster (6–7 s vs. >10 s on A10G) because it avoids REDI's extra initial denoising steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECON extends the retrieval-based diffusion acceleration paradigm introduced by Agarwal et al. (text-based caching, NSDI 2024) and Zhang et al. (noise-based retrieval via REDi, ICML 2023) by replacing whole-prompt or whole-noise trajectory lookup with concept-level decomposition and multi-trajectory aggregation. The concept extraction pipeline draws on NLP tools (RoBERTa for noun-phrase parsing) and a CLIP-based visualness filter (Verma et al., EMNLP 2023) to ensure only visually representable phrases enter the knowledge base. The weighted noise aggregation is grounded in the energy-based model compositionality framework of Du et al. (NeurIPS 2020) and Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2021), which justifies summing noise vectors from semantically related concepts. Unlike prior retrieval methods that lock the generation to a single stored trajectory, RECON constructs a 'flexible trajectory' that preserves room for input-specific details during the remaining denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RECON offers a practical, training-free acceleration path for large T2I models (demonstrated on SDXL) that reduces NFEs by up to 40% while maintaining fidelity, and the released 1.2M-concept knowledge base is a useful community resource for the nascent retrieval-based acceleration field. A key open question is how well the concept KB generalizes to out-of-distribution or rapidly evolving prompt distributions, since retrieval quality depends on coverage. The method is also orthogonal to numerical-solver optimizations, suggesting composability with DPM-Solver or UniPC for further speedups, but the paper does not explore such combinations. Limitations include reliance on a fixed visualness threshold, the assumption that noun phrases capture the salient visual content, and evaluation restricted to image generation (no video or 3D extension).
