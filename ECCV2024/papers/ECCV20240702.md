## Problem

Satellite metadata (GSD, timestamp, geolocation) encodes rich semantic cues about scene content, yet existing remote sensing SSL methods incorporate different metadata fields through specialized architectures or pretext tasks (e.g., positional encodings in MAEs, location classification heads). No unified, architecture-agnostic framework exists for jointly leveraging heterogeneous metadata fields as a flexible supervision signal during pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240702/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB satellite patches (cropped from fMoW MAXAR imagery) alongside metadata fields—GSD, UTC timestamp, and lat/lon centroid—which are either serialized into text tokens (BERT-style encoder) or fed as numerical/categorical columns (FT-Transformer encoder). The image encoder (ViT-S/16, MoCoV3, 21.7M params) outputs a visual embedding usable for kNN or linear-probing classification; the metadata encoder is discarded for image-only transfer but retained for bimodal (image + metadata) classification where concatenated features feed a linear head. No augmentation is applied to metadata, while images use SimCLR-style strong augmentations plus vertical flips and rotations.

## Method

SatMIP aligns image and metadata embeddings in a shared space via a symmetrized InfoNCE contrastive loss (CLIP-style), treating matching pairs as positives and cross-batch pairs as negatives, with a ViT image backbone and a 3-layer Transformer metadata encoder (8 heads, width 512) each followed by a linear projection head. SatMIPS extends this by co-optimizing the SatMIP metadata-image loss with a SimCLR image-image contrastive loss (equal weighting, λ=1) in a multi-task framework that shares the visual backbone across both branches. A key efficiency design couples one augmented image view between the SimCLR and SatMIP objectives—its backbone output feeds both projection heads—reducing training time and memory by ~30% relative to a decoupled SLIP-style architecture while preserving downstream accuracy. The approach is agnostic to the specific SSL method paired with the metadata objective and to the metadata encoder type (textual vs. tabular), enabling flexible deployment across sensors and downstream tasks.

## Results

On kNN classification across 7 downstream remote sensing datasets, SatMIPS (ViT-S, 200 epochs on fMoW) achieves 62.3 F1 on fMoW, 89.7% on RESISC45, 87.9% on Optimal31, 94.9% on UC Merced, 60.8% on FGSC23, 95.1% on EuroSAT, and 57.1% on So2Sat. SatMIPS outperforms its SimCLR baseline on all datasets except UC Merced and So2Sat where they tie, and converges faster: performance at 100 epochs matches or exceeds SimCLR at 200 epochs. SatMIP alone (metadata-only supervision) already surpasses all three MAE-based methods (SatMAE, Scale-MAE, SatMAE++) on 6 of 7 datasets, validating metadata as a strong pretext signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SimCLR (the natural image-only baseline), SatMIPS improves fMoW F1 from 61.1 to 62.3, RESISC45 from 88.5 to 89.7, and FGSC23 from 57.3 to 60.8, while tying on UC Merced (94.9 vs 95.0) and So2Sat (57.1 vs 56.9). Against the metadata-augmented contrastive baselines from [2], SatMIPS beats SimCLR-Geo on fMoW (62.3 vs 59.0), RESISC45 (89.7 vs 88.9), and FGSC23 (60.8 vs 60.4), but is outperformed by SimCLR-TP and SimCLR-Geo-TP on most datasets (e.g., fMoW 62.3 vs 65.2/65.8; FGSC23 60.8 vs 61.2/63.0), indicating that temporal-positive sampling remains a stronger extension than metadata alignment. Against MAE-based methods, SatMIPS dominates: it exceeds Scale-MAE (the strongest MAE baseline) by 10.9 F1 on fMoW (62.3 vs 51.4), 3.8% on RESISC45 (89.7 vs 85.9), and 12.6% on FGSC23 (60.8 vs 48.2), losing only on EuroSAT (95.1 vs 96.1). SatMIP alone, without any image-image contrastive objective, already surpasses all MAE methods on 6 of 7 datasets, confirming that metadata-image alignment is a competitive standalone pretext task.

## Relation to Prior Work

SatMIP directly adapts the CLIP/SLIP language-image contrastive pretraining paradigm to the satellite domain, replacing natural language captions with sensor metadata (GSD, timestamp, location) as the second modality. It departs from prior remote sensing SSL methods that either predict metadata as a pretext task (Geo, TP) or bake metadata into ViT positional encodings for MAE-based reconstruction (SatMAE, Scale-MAE), instead treating metadata as a free-standing modality aligned via a symmetric contrastive objective in a shared embedding space. SatMIPS further extends SLIP's multi-task framework by coupling one image view between the SimCLR and SatMIP branches, reducing the ~3× activation overhead of SLIP to ~5% over SimCLR while preserving performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that metadata supervision is a strong, architecture-agnostic competitor to image-only SSL objectives, and that the two are synergistic when combined. This provides a practical, unified recipe for incorporating heterogeneous sensor metadata into pretraining without task-specific architectural modifications. However, SatMIPS still trails SimCLR-TP on most datasets, suggesting that temporal-positive contrastive learning and metadata supervision are complementary rather than substitutive signals that could be jointly exploited. Open questions include extension to multispectral/radar modalities, dense prediction tasks, and finetuning behavior, all of which the authors note as future work.
