## Problem

Monocular depth estimation has been predominantly formulated as per-pixel regression or bin-based classification, both of which suffer from overfitting, poor object-level detail, and visible discontinuities in the output depth maps. Applying generative models to this task is further complicated by the sparse ground-truth depth problem in outdoor datasets (e.g., KITTI has only 3.75–5% valid pixels), which causes mode collapse in standard generative training. This paper is the first to introduce a diffusion-denoising framework into monocular depth estimation, addressing both the quality gap and the sparse-GT challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a dense pixel-wise depth map at full input resolution (e.g., 1216×352 for KITTI, 640×480 for NYU-Depth-V2). Internally, the diffusion-denoising process operates in a compressed latent depth space of shape H/2 × W/2 × 16, which is decoded to the final H × W × 1 depth map via a lightweight convolutional decoder with a Sigmoid activation and a max-depth clamp of 1e6. Inference uses DDIM sampling with 20 deterministic steps, achieving 14 FPS (ResNet) or 5 FPS (Swin) on an RTX 3090.

## Method

DiffusionDepth reformulates depth estimation as a visual-conditioned iterative denoising process: starting from a random depth latent x_T, a Monocular Conditioned Denoising Block (MCDB) progressively refines it to x_0 over 20 DDIM steps, guided by multi-scale visual features aggregated through an FPN/HAHI neck. The MCDB fuses the upsampled visual condition with the depth latent via element-wise summation, self-attention, a bottleneck CNN, and channel-wise attention, keeping the denoising head lightweight. A key design choice is self-diffusion: during training, noise is added to the model's own refined depth output rather than to the sparse GT depth, which prevents mode collapse in outdoor scenarios where GT coverage is under 5%. The full loss combines a DDIM denoising loss, a pixel-wise depth loss on valid pixels, and an L2 latent-space loss between predicted and encoded GT latents, all trained end-to-end with a Swin Transformer or ResNet backbone.

## Results

On the KITTI official offline split (0–50 m), DiffusionDepth achieves 0.041 Abs Rel and 1.452 RMSE, outperforming the previous best URCDC-Depth (0.049 Abs Rel, 1.528 RMSE) by a clear margin. On the KITTI Eigen split (0–80 m), it reaches 0.050 Abs Rel and 2.016 RMSE, surpassing URCDC-Depth (2.032) and VA-Depth (2.090). On NYU-Depth-V2, DiffusionDepth attains 0.085 Rel and 0.295 RMSE, edging out VA-Depth (0.086 Rel, 0.304 RMSE) and URCDC-Depth (0.088 Rel, 0.316 RMSE). Inference speed is 14 FPS (ResNet backbone) and 5 FPS (Swin backbone) with 20 DDIM steps on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the KITTI official offline split, DiffusionDepth (1.452 RMSE) decisively outperforms URCDC-Depth (1.528), P3Depth (1.651), PWA (1.872), BTS (1.995), TransDepth (1.992), and VPD (3.262), with the largest gap over VPD exceeding 1.8 m in RMSE. On the KITTI Eigen split, it edges out URCDC-Depth (2.032) and VA-Depth (2.090) by small margins while substantially surpassing earlier methods like AdaBins (2.360), DepthFormer (2.143), and DORN (2.727). On NYU-Depth-V2, DiffusionDepth (0.295 RMSE) beats VA-Depth (0.304), URCDC-Depth (0.316), PixelFormer (0.322), and BinsFormer (0.330), but is outperformed by VPD (0.254 RMSE), which leverages a text-to-image diffusion prior. On the KITTI online benchmark, DiffusionDepth (10.58 iRMSE) trails VA-Depth (10.44) and ND-Depth (10.62) slightly but surpasses URCDC-Depth (10.71), BinsFormer (10.90), and PixelFormer (10.84); the authors note their visual condition is simpler (no long-range attention or constraint priors) and that incorporating such features could close the gap. Overall, the method achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA results across all four evaluation settings, with the most dramatic gains on the outdoor KITTI official split where sparse GT depth makes the self-diffusion strategy particularly effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the diffusion-denoising framework from image generation (DDPM, DDIM, Latent Diffusion) for the discriminative task of monocular depth estimation, which has been dominated by per-pixel regression (BTS, DAV), ordinal classification (AdaBins, BinsFormer), and constraint-based refinement (UCRDepth, P3Depth, VA-Depth). The key conceptual departure is the self-diffusion formulation: rather than diffusing sparse ground-truth depth (which causes catastrophic mode collapse in outdoor settings), the model iteratively denoises its own refined output, effectively turning the generative process into a progressive refinement loop guided by monocular visual features. This positions the work at the intersection of generative modeling and 3D perception, following the broader trend of applying diffusion to discriminative tasks (DiffusionDet for detection, SegDiff for segmentation) but being the first to do so for depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-diffusion mechanism provides a principled solution to the sparse-GT problem that plagues outdoor depth estimation (only ~4% pixel coverage in KITTI), and the ablation showing RMSE 12.38 vs 1.45 for GT-diffusion vs self-diffusion underscores how critical this design choice is. However, the practical impact is tempered: online KITTI results trail the current SOTA (VA-Depth, URCDC-Depth), inference speed is limited to 5–14 FPS depending on backbone, and the offline gains, while consistent, are modest (e.g., 1.452 vs 1.528 RMSE on KITTI official). Open questions include whether the diffusion head can be combined with stronger long-range attention or geometric priors to close the online gap, whether the approach transfers to stereo matching or depth completion, and whether the sensitivity to inference step count (severe degradation when changing steps without retraining) can be mitigated for practical deployment.
