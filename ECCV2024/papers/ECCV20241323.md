## Problem

Existing CISS strategies suffer from a two-sided 'background shift': pseudo-labeling and knowledge distillation misclassify old-class pixels as background (shift towards old classes), while background weight transfer initializes new class tokens identically to the background token, making them hard to separate (shift towards new classes). No prior method simultaneously addresses both directions of this shift, leading to compounded error propagation across incremental steps.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 512×512 RGB images and outputs a pixel-wise segmentation mask over all classes seen so far. At each incremental step, ground-truth labels are provided only for the newly introduced classes; all other pixels (old classes, future classes, and true background) are annotated as a single background class, which is the core constraint that drives the background shift problem. The architecture is a ViT-B/16 encoder with two transformer decoder layers (Segmenter), processing patches of size 16.

## Method

The paper proposes a background-class separation framework built on three mechanisms. First, selective pseudo-labeling introduces an object identifier that compares the old model's background confidence against the sum of all object-class confidences per pixel; pixels where object confidence exceeds background confidence are excluded from training rather than labeled as background, preventing old-class pixels from being absorbed into the background. Second, adaptive feature distillation constructs a patch-wise reliability map (1 for confidently labeled old-class patches, 0 for ignored/new-class patches, and the background confidence score for background-labeled patches) that weights the L2 feature distillation loss, so only trustworthy features are distilled. Third, to separate new classes from the background after background weight transfer, the method combines label-guided output distillation (transferring the old model's background logit to the new class logit for correctly labeled new-class pixels) with a novel orthogonal loss that penalizes the dot product between new class tokens and all old/background tokens (with stop-gradient on old weights), directly pushing the new class embeddings away from the background embedding space.

## Results

On Pascal VOC (Transformer backbone), the proposed method achieves 82.8% overall mIoU in the 19-1 disjoint setting and 83.3% in the overlapped setting, surpassing the previous SOTA (INC) by 0.6%p and 1.2%p respectively. In the more challenging 15-1 (6-step) scenario, the method reaches 78.1% overall mIoU (disjoint) and 80.6% (overlapped), exceeding INC by 1.9%p and 5.0%p. On ADE20k (overlapped setting), the method achieves 45.7% overall mIoU in the 100-50 scenario and 42.8% in the 100-5 (11-step) scenario, yielding an average improvement of 1.0%p over the state of the art across all four ADE20k protocols.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 11 baselines spanning regularization (EWC), replay (RECALL, REMIND), and distillation-based methods (MiB, PLOP, SDR, RCIL, SPPA, RBC, INC). The closest competitor is INC (Incrementer, CVPR 2023), the previous Transformer-based SOTA; the proposed method outperforms INC in every scenario, with the largest gap in the 15-1 multi-step overlapped setting (+5.0%p overall mIoU: 80.6 vs 76.2) and the 15-5 overlapped setting (+2.7%p: 82.6 vs 79.9). Against RBC (ECCV 2022), the gains are even more pronounced in multi-step scenarios (e.g., 15-1 disjoint: 78.1 vs 60.5, a +17.6%p margin), reflecting RBC's vulnerability to error propagation across steps. MiB (CVPR 2020), the original background-weight-transfer method, is outperformed by 10–20%p in multi-step settings, confirming that the background shift problem it introduces is severe without the proposed separation mechanisms. On ADE20k, the method leads INC by 0.9%p (100-50), 1.5%p (50-50), 0.6%p (100-10), and 1.1%p (100-5) in overall mIoU, with the advantage growing as the number of steps increases, validating the cumulative benefit of the selective distillation strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the regularization-based branch of class-incremental semantic segmentation (CISS), building directly on MiB's background weight transfer (Cermelli et al., CVPR 2020), PLOP's pseudo-labeling and knowledge distillation (Douillard et al., CVPR 2021), and LGKD's label-guided output distillation (Yang et al., ICCV 2023). Its key conceptual departure is identifying 'background shift' as a unifying failure mode shared by both dominant strategies: pseudo-labeling/KD causes shift toward old classes by mislabeling ambiguous pixels as background, while background weight transfer causes shift toward new classes by initializing them identically to the background token. The paper addresses this with a background-class separation framework combining selective pseudo-labeling, adaptive feature distillation, and a novel orthogonal objective on classifier weights, all atop the Segmenter ViT backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By diagnosing background shift as the common root cause of degradation in two otherwise independent strategies, the paper provides a clearer conceptual framework for understanding why CISS methods underperform relative to joint training, particularly in multi-step and overlapped settings where errors compound. The practical techniques (selective pseudo-labeling filtering ~37% of mispredicted pixels, patch-wise reliability-weighted distillation, and the orthogonal classifier constraint) are modular and could be adopted by other CISS methods. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with a single ViT backbone, and the added complexity of multiple loss terms with hand-tuned coefficients; whether the background-shift framing generalizes to other incremental tasks (e.g., instance segmentation, 3D) remains open.
