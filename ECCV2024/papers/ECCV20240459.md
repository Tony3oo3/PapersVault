## Problem

Automatic animal face identification is critical for non-invasive monitoring, surveys, and locating lost animals, yet progress has been severely limited by the absence of large-scale datasets and benchmarks. Prior animal face datasets contain fewer than 100 individuals (at most ~1,393 for DogFaceNet), making it impossible to train generalizable models or properly evaluate recognition of unseen individuals—a key requirement for real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240459/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 224×224 pixel RGB face crops aligned via species-specific facial landmarks (detected with AnyFace trained on AnimalWeb), where different reference points are used per species to handle anatomical variation. For re-identification, the model outputs a discrete identity label over all individuals in the training set; for verification, it produces a cosine similarity score between two face embeddings to judge whether they belong to the same individual. The dataset additionally provides fine-grained per-individual annotations (sex, breed, color, pattern) enabling attribute-conditioned evaluation.

## Method

PetFace is assembled by crawling pet shop and animal adoption websites (one per region to minimize duplicates), yielding 1.44M images from 325K individuals, then filtered through a two-stage process: automatic removal of multi-face images followed by ~100 man-hours of manual curation, retaining 1.01M images from 257K individuals. For benchmarking, the authors train ResNet-50 backbones under four loss functions (Softmax, Center, Triplet, ArcFace) in both per-family and joint cross-family configurations, evaluating via two protocols: closed-set re-identification (classification over seen identities) and open-set verification (cosine similarity on unseen identities with a 7:1:2 train/val/test split that separates sources to prevent background leakage). ArcFace with joint training achieves the best average AUC of 92.17% on unseen verification, outperforming ImageNet, CLIP, and MegaDescriptor baselines, while cross-dataset experiments on CTai/CZoo (chimpanzee) and DogFaceNet/Flickr-dog (dog) confirm the dataset's superior generalization.

## Results

On the unseen-individual verification benchmark (AUC), the jointly trained ArcFace model on PetFace achieves 92.17% average AUC across 13 animal families, outperforming the per-family ArcFace model (91.30%), Softmax (90.38%), Triplet (83.48%), and Center (80.81%) loss variants. On the seen-individual re-identification benchmark (Top-1 accuracy), the jointly trained ArcFace model reaches 53.80% average, compared to 51.23% for per-family ArcFace, 41.88% for Softmax, and 9.81% for Center loss. The best external baseline, MegaDescriptor (Swin-B trained on WildlifeDatasets), achieves only 83.70% average AUC on verification, a gap of 8.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The jointly trained ArcFace model on PetFace (92.17% avg AUC) decisively outperforms all three external baselines: ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 (79.60%), CLIP ResNet-50 (78.63%), and MegaDescriptor Swin-B trained on WildlifeDatasets (83.70%), with margins of 12.6, 13.5, and 8.5 points respectively. The gap is most pronounced on smaller families like Chinchilla (89.63% vs. 70.80% for ImageNet) and Ferret (88.44% vs. 67.95% for ImageNet), while the advantage narrows on Dog (99.01% vs. 97.18% for ImageNet) where ImageNet already captures strong dog features. In cross-dataset verification on chimpanzees, the PetFace-trained model beats both CTai-trained (67.33% vs. 66.76% on CTai test) and CZoo-trained (71.27% vs. 69.31% on CZoo test) models. For dogs, the PetFace-trained model (46,755 individuals) surpasses not only cross-dataset baselines but also the in-dataset DogFaceNet model (99.65% vs. 98.72% on DogFaceNet test), demonstrating that scale and diversity drive performance. On fine-grained same-breed dog verification, the PetFace model maintains 98.30% average AUC while MegaDescriptor degrades from 93.75% to 86.25%, confirming that the larger individual count in PetFace overcomes the breed-specific limitations of prior dog datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the mature human face recognition toolkit (ArcFace, triplet loss, center loss, ResNet-50) for the animal domain, where prior datasets (CTai, CZoo, DogFaceNet, etc.) contained at most ~1,400 individuals and focused on single species. It departs from the WildlifeDatasets/MegaDescriptor aggregation approach by constructing a unified, multi-family dataset from scratch with fine-grained annotations (sex, breed, color, pattern) rather than combining heterogeneous existing collections. The key conceptual departure is introducing an unseen-individual verification benchmark across 13 animal families, moving beyond the closed-set re-identification paradigm that dominated prior animal face work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

At 257,484 individuals across 13 families, PetFace is over 110× larger than the previous largest animal face dataset, enabling the first meaningful evaluation of open-set verification and fine-grained breed-level identification that were infeasible at prior scales. The finding that joint training across families improves some species (Cat: 54→70%) but degrades others (Chimp: 43→34%) highlights an unresolved imbalance problem in multi-family animal identification. Open questions include how to handle the wide performance gap across families, whether the ResNet-50-over-Transformer result generalizes beyond this dataset, and whether the internet-sourced data distribution adequately represents wild (vs. captive/pet) animal appearance variation.
