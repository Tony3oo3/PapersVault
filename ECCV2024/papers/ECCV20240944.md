## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models (e.g., SD 2.1 trained at 512²) produce severely degraded outputs—object repetition, desaturation, and broken composition—when directly applied to higher resolutions. Full fine-tuning to a new scale requires over 1000k steps and 860M trainable parameters, while existing tuning-free methods (ScaleCrafter, Attn-SF) lack semantic constraints and still produce artifacts. The paper asks: can a well-trained low-resolution diffusion model be rapidly adapted to higher-resolution generation with minimal new parameters and training steps?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240944/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a frozen pre-trained diffusion model (SD 2.1 at 512², SD XL at 1024², or LVDM at 16×256² video) and produces higher-resolution outputs at arbitrary scales (1024², 2048², 3072×1536, 1536×3072 for images; 16×512² for video). The generation is inherently cascaded: a low-resolution pivot is first synthesized, then progressively upsampled through multiple 4× scale stages, with the final output decoded from the highest-resolution latent. Inference time is nearly identical to direct single-stage sampling (1.04×–1.06× overhead).

## Method

The self-cascade diffusion model decomposes the full scale adaptation (e.g., 512²→2048²) into multiple moderate 4× stages, cyclically re-utilizing the same frozen low-resolution U-Net at each stage. A pivot replacement strategy initializes each stage by upsampling the previous stage's clean latent, adding noise to an intermediate step K (700 of 1000), and denoising from K→0, thereby injecting reliable semantic guidance without any new parameters (tuning-free version). For the tuning version, N=4 lightweight time-aware feature upsampler modules (each: bilinear upsampling + 2 residual blocks, totaling only 0.002M parameters) are plugged into the U-Net's skip connections to upsample and transform pivot features from the previous stage, adding them to the current stage's features in a time-step-adaptive manner. These modules are trained for just 10k steps on a small set of high-resolution images, achieving a 5× training speed-up over full fine-tuning while preserving the base model's composition and temporal consistency capabilities.

## Results

On the 4× scale adaptation task (512²→1024²) using Laion-5B, the tuning-free version (Ours-TF) achieves FID_r of 12.25 and FID_b of 6.09, substantially outperforming Direct Inference (FID_r 29.89) and ScaleCrafter (FID_r 20.88). The tuning version (Ours-T) with only 0.002M parameters and 4k steps further improves to FID_b of 3.15 and pFID_r of 15.35, surpassing Full Fine-tuning (860M params, 18k steps; FID_b 17.14) and LoRA-R4 (1.9M params, 18k steps; FID_b 9.47). On the more challenging 16× adaptation (512²→2048²), Ours-T (10k steps) achieves FID_r of 18.46 versus 43.55 for Full Fine-tuning (20k steps) and 50.72 for LoRA-R4 (20k steps). For video generation (16×512²), Ours-T (10k steps) attains FVD of 494.19 and KVD of 31.55, outperforming Full Fine-tuning at 50k steps (FVD 531.57) and LoRA-R4 at 50k steps (FVD 623.72).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the tuning-free baseline Attn-SF [17], Ours-TF achieves a 2.4× lower FID_r (12.25 vs. 29.95) and a 3.7× lower FID_b (6.09 vs. 22.75), indicating far superior preservation of the base model's composition ability. Against ScaleCrafter [10], Ours-TF reduces FID_r by 41% (12.25 vs. 20.88) and FID_b by 63% (6.09 vs. 16.67), while the tuning version Ours-T further narrows the gap to FID_b of 3.15. Against LoRA fine-tuning [16], Ours-T uses 950× fewer trainable parameters (0.002M vs. 1.9M for R4) and 4.5× fewer training steps (4k vs. 18k) while achieving lower FID_b (3.15 vs. 9.47) and pFID_r (15.35 vs. 18.06); the advantage widens dramatically at 16× scale where Ours-T (10k steps) reaches FID_r 18.46 versus LoRA-R4 (20k steps) at 50.72. On video generation, Ours-T (10k steps) outperforms LoRA-R4 (50k steps) by 21% in FVD (494.19 vs. 623.72) and 57% in KVD (31.55 vs. 74.13), while also avoiding the motion inconsistency artifacts observed in both Full Fine-tuning and LoRA approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model / Stable Diffusion architecture and the multi-stage cascaded diffusion framework of Ho et al. (JMLR 2022), but departs fundamentally by cyclically re-using the same frozen low-resolution denoiser at every cascade stage rather than training separate super-resolution models. The pivot replacement strategy—injecting the model's own low-resolution output as a noise-schedule starting point for the next resolution stage—is a novel mechanism not present in prior cascade or tuning-free scale-adaptation work (ScaleCrafter, Attn-SF). The tuning version further distinguishes itself from LoRA-based fine-tuning by inserting only 0.002M parameters of time-aware feature upsamplers into skip connections, targeting the specific bottleneck of scale adaptation rather than general domain shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 5× training speedup with 0.002M trainable parameters (vs. 860M for full fine-tuning) and virtually no extra inference time makes higher-resolution adaptation accessible to practitioners without large compute budgets. A notable finding is that full fine-tuning at 18k steps actually underperforms the proposed method (FID 21.88 vs. 12.40 at 4×), suggesting that indiscriminate parameter updates destroy the composition priors that make scale adaptation difficult. The authors acknowledge an upper bound on performance due to the tiny upsampler capacity, particularly for very large scale gaps (>4k), and leave open the trade-off between adaptation efficiency and generalization ability as future work.
