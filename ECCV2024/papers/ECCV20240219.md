## Problem

Web-scale data grows exponentially, yet existing data cleaning and selection methods are offline, address only noise or redundancy in isolation, and incur O(n) per-sample costs that become prohibitive at billion scale. There is no practical online algorithm that simultaneously detects and corrects noisy samples while suppressing redundant ones as data arrives in a stream.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240219/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of data points (image-text pairs for multimodal tasks, or images with class labels for single-modal tasks), processing each point independently against the already-collected dataset. For each incoming sample, it produces a binary keep/discard decision, a relabeled caption if the sample is noisy (via MiniGPT-4 recaptioning), a scalar information gain score computed from k-nearest-neighbor cosine distances in BLIP embedding space, and a sampling probability used during training. The final output is a curated dataset where training cost is reduced to 50–55% of full-data training while matching or exceeding full-data accuracy.

## Method

InfoGrowth frames dataset growth as an online process where each new sample is classified as noisy, redundant, or informative by its neighborhood in a multimodal embedding space (BLIP). A cleaner module filters samples below a cosine-similarity threshold δ and recaptions them with MiniGPT-4; a gain calculator then queries an HNSW approximate nearest-neighbor index (O(log n) per sample) to compute a submodular-like information gain as the mean cosine distance to k nearest neighbors, replacing the O(n) full-set submodular evaluation. For classification tasks, an additional entropy gain term (1 − p, where p is the probability that a neighbor's label correctly predicts the sample's label) up-weights boundary points. Two sampling strategies exploit the gain scores: a static proportional sampler for fixed-budget diversity, and a dynamic two-phase sampler that alternates between global diversity (gain-proportional) and local generalization (inverse-gain) phases each epoch, reducing training cost to ~50% of full-data training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50 at 50% prune ratio, InfoGrowth achieves 75.8% top-1 accuracy, surpassing GC (72.8%), EL2N (74.6%), and UCB (75.3%) while incurring only 0.8h overhead (vs >24h for GC and >17.5h for EL2N). On multimodal CC3M→BLIP, InfoGrowth with only 1.35M selected data (out of 2.7M seen) achieves 47.7% TR@1 and 38.1% IR@1 zero-shot on COCO, beating full CC3M (34.7% / 28.0%) by a wide margin. With dynamic two-phase sampling, training cost drops to ~8% of full CC3M while still improving zero-shot text retrieval by 3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet-1K, InfoGrowth (75.8%) outperforms all three baselines: GC (72.8%, +3.0 pts), EL2N (74.6%, +1.2 pts), and UCB (75.3%, +0.5 pts), while matching UCB's total compute cost (70.8 vs 70 node-hours) and drastically reducing overhead compared to GC (>24h) and EL2N (>17.5h). On multimodal CC3M, InfoGrowth with 1.35M data surpasses full CC3M (2.7M) on COCO zero-shot retrieval (TR@1: 47.7 vs 34.7; IR@1: 38.1 vs 28.0) and on downstream VQA, NLVR2, and captioning tasks. Even at 0.4M data, InfoGrowth matches or exceeds full CC3M on COCO retrieval, demonstrating 2×–4× data efficiency. The paper also notes that InfoGrowth's O(log n) per-sample cost scales far better than the O(n) cost of GC/EL2N on exponentially growing datasets, and unlike InfoBatch [22], it generalizes to multimodal contrastive-learning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfoGrowth builds directly on BLIP's bootstrapping paradigm (filter + recaption) for multimodal data cleaning, but reframes the entire curation pipeline as an online streaming algorithm rather than the offline batch process used by BLIP, ALBEF, or DivideMix. It also repurposes submodular information-gain theory—previously applied in offline data pruning (GC, EL2N)—by replacing the O(n) set-operation cost with an O(log n) HNSW nearest-neighbor query, enabling incremental dataset growth. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the unified, single-pass treatment of both noise and redundancy in a streaming setting, whereas existing methods address one problem at a time and must be re-run as data accumulates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The online formulation is practically important for real-world data engines (e.g., Common Crawl, LAION-scale pipelines) where data arrives continuously and re-running expensive offline curation is infeasible. The finding that 0.4M InfoGrowth-curated samples match or exceed 2.7M raw CC3M for retrieval, and that the marginal benefit of seeing more candidate data plateaus (Table 10), suggests a practical ceiling on curation quality that could guide budget allocation. However, validation is limited to million-scale data; the paper's billion-scale claims rely on architectural arguments (distributed ANN) rather than empirical evidence, and the dependence on a strong pretrained encoder (BLIP/MiniGPT-4) for the embedding space remains an open question for domains where such encoders are weak or unavailable.
