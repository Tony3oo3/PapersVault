## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLMs such as GPT-4V and Gemini exhibit poor zero-shot object detection, frequently missing targets or producing inaccurate bounding boxes, especially for occluded, small, or rotated objects. Existing remedies either require instruction fine-tuning (infeasible for closed-source models) or rely on simple textual/visual prompts that have not been systematically designed for detection. The paper asks whether a structured prompting paradigm can unlock the latent detection capability of frozen MLLMs without any parameter updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240843/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a natural-language detection query as input and iteratively feeds progressively modified versions of the image (with overlaid rulers, compasses, zoomed crops, convex hulls, or scene-graph edges) back into a frozen MLLM. The MLLM outputs intermediate reasoning text at each round and ultimately returns axis-aligned or oriented bounding boxes in normalized coordinates, which are converted back to absolute pixel coordinates for standard evaluation. The entire pipeline runs with a fixed 1024-token budget per call and no gradient updates.

## Method

DetToolChain couples a Detection Prompting Toolkit with a multimodal Detection Chain-of-Thought (Det-CoT). The toolkit comprises three categories of visual processing prompts—Regional Amplifier (image splitting and zoom-in), Spatial Measurement Standard (linear rulers for translational reference and graduated compasses for rotational reference), and Scene Image Parser (centroids, convex hulls, labeled bounding boxes, and scene-graph edges drawn on the image)—plus four detection reasoning prompts (Problem Insight Guider, Spatial Relationship Explorer, Contextual Object Predictor, Self-Verification Promoter) that diagnose predictions and enforce commonsense consistency. Det-CoT orchestrates these tools in an iterative loop: the MLLM proposes a thought, selects and executes a visual prompt, verifies the result, and either refines the box in the next round or returns the final detection. The key design insight is that directly drawing spatial references (rulers, compasses, scene graphs) onto the image is far more effective than textual coordinate descriptions, and that progressive box refinement across multiple CoT steps mirrors the iterative refinement in DETR/SparseRCNN, enabling a frozen MLLM to act as a competitive zero-shot detector.

## Results

On the COCO OVD benchmark, GPT-4V + DetToolChain achieves 65.8 AP₅₀ on Novel classes, 65.0 on Base, and 65.2 on All, surpassing the prior best (CORA) by 22.7 AP₅₀ on Novel. On COCO val2017, the zero-shot GPT-4V + DetToolChain reaches 34.5 AP / 64.8 AP₅₀ / 31.5 AP₇₅, exceeding Faster R-CNN (58.4 AP₅₀) and DETR-R50 (62.4 AP₅₀) on AP₅₀ without any training. On D-cube described object detection, it attains 37.2 AP in the FULL setting (+14.5 over FIBER-B), and on RefCOCO val it reaches 70.01% Top-1 Acc@0.5 (+44.53 over the GPT-4V baseline). On HRSC2016 oriented detection, it achieves 0.63 mIoU_p and 0.59 mIoU_r, a +0.50/+0.51 gain over the GPT-4V baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On open-vocabulary detection (COCO OVD), GPT-4V + DetToolChain (65.8 Novel AP₅₀) decisively outperforms all nine supervised OVD baselines, with the largest margin over CORA (+22.7) and BARON (+23.1). Against supervised general detectors on COCO val2017, the zero-shot method beats Faster R-CNN (+6.4 AP₅₀), Mask R-CNN (+5.8), DETR-R50 (+2.4), and VisionLLM-R50 (+0.8) on AP₅₀, but trails UNINEXT-R50 (68.4) and Co-DETR-L (78.5) on both AP₅₀ and AP, reflecting the gap in precise boundary regression. On D-cube described object detection, GPT-4V + DetToolChain (37.2 FULL AP) surpasses the previous best FIBER-B (22.7) by 14.5 AP and G-DINO-B (20.7) by 16.5 AP. On RefCOCO referring expression comprehension, the method (70.01% val Acc) is the strongest zero-shot approach, beating StructureSimi (49.37%), ReCLIP (45.78%), and Grad-CAM (42.85%) by 20+ points, yet remains well below instruction-tuned MLLMs such as UNINEXT (92.64%) and Qwen-VL (89.36%). On HRSC2016 oriented detection, DetToolChain improves GPT-4V by +0.50 mIoU_p and +0.51 mIoU_r, with the Compass Marker contributing the single largest tool-level gain (+0.34 mIoU_p over baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Chain-of-Thought prompting paradigm (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022) from text-only reasoning to a multimodal detection setting, introducing a detection-specific CoT that orchestrates visual tools. It departs from the dominant finetuning-based approach for MLLM detection (BuboGPT, Kosmos-2, Shikra, Qwen-VL, SPHINX) by requiring no parameter updates, and from prior visual prompting work (RedCircle, SOM, SCAFFOLD) by designing prompts inspired by detection priors—rulers, compasses, scene graphs, and zooming—rather than generic spatial markers. Unlike zero-shot referring expression methods (CPT, SOM) that select from pre-extracted proposals of external detectors, DetToolChain instructs the MLLM to predict and progressively refine bounding boxes using its own spatial reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that any API-accessible MLLM (GPT-4V, Gemini) can be converted into a competitive zero-shot detector without training data, compute for finetuning, or access to model weights—addressing a key bottleneck for closed-source models. However, the AP75 gap (31.5 vs. 44.2 for DETR-R50) reveals that box boundaries remain less precise than supervised regression, and the multi-round prompting incurs substantial API cost and latency. Open questions include whether the toolkit generalizes to video detection, whether the gains persist as MLLMs natively improve their spatial grounding, and whether the approach can be distilled into a single forward pass.
