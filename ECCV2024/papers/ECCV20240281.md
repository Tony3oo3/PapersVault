## Problem

Few-shot weakly supervised WSI classification (FSWC) requires diagnosing from only 2–32 labeled slides per class, a regime where standard MIL methods degrade sharply. Existing prompt-learning approaches for pathology either operate only at the patch level or rely exclusively on language prompts, leaving task-specific visual pathology patterns (e.g., vascular invasion, tumor boundary morphology) unexploited. This paper asks whether injecting both visual and textual pathology prior knowledge into prompts at both patch and slide levels can bridge the gap between limited training data and accurate slide-level diagnosis.

## Input / Output

The method consumes non-overlapping patches extracted from gigapixel H&E WSIs, a small set of curated visual prompt examples (e.g., 6 slides + 6 patches for the prognosis task) selected by pathology experts, and hand-written plain-language descriptions of slide- and patch-level pathology features. A frozen CLIP image encoder extracts patch features; the only trainable parameters are the prompt vectors, a lightweight self-attention Messenger Layer, an attention-pooling Summary Layer, and a one-layer projector. Output is a slide-level class probability vector (e.g., good vs. poor prognosis, metastasis vs. non-metastasis, or one of four round-cell subtypes) obtained by cosine-similarity matching between the aggregated slide visual feature and class-specific textual features, followed by a temperature-scaled softmax.

## Method

PEMP introduces a two-level (patch + slide) prompt architecture on a frozen CLIP backbone. On the visual side, fixed pathology image examples are encoded once and, for each training patch, the most cosine-similar example feature is concatenated to the patch feature; a Messenger Layer (single-head self-attention) then models inter-patch relationships, and a Summary Layer (learnable attention pooling) aggregates patches into a slide feature, to which a learnable slide-level prompt vector is appended. On the textual side, three token groups—Slide task [Token], Slide-level descriptive [Token], and Patch-level descriptive [Token]—each carry their own learnable context vectors and are fed through the frozen CLIP text encoder. Training minimizes three alignment contrastive losses: L_t aligns slide visual features with task-class text features (driving classification), L_s aligns slide-level visual example features with their corresponding slide-level text descriptions, and L_p aligns patch-level visual example features with patch-level text descriptions, thereby binding visual pathology patterns to their linguistic counterparts at both granularities.

## Results

On the Survival Prognosis Prediction task (C-index), PEMP achieves 0.667 (32-shot) and 0.562 (2-shot) on the in-house dataset, and 0.637 (32-shot) and 0.551 (2-shot) on TCGA-CESC, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline TOP by 1.5–5.4 points. On Lymph Node Metastasis Prediction (AUC), PEMP reaches 0.849 (32-shot) and 0.783 (2-shot) in-house, and 0.818 (32-shot) and 0.704 (2-shot) on TCGA. On the Round Cell Subtype Diagnosis task (AUC), PEMP achieves 0.751 (32-shot) and 0.625 (2-shot), surpassing TOP by 6.9 and 6.5 points respectively, with an average margin of 6.2% over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEMP outperforms all five baselines across every task, dataset, and shot setting. LinearProbe (CLIP with a linear head) and VPT (visual prompt tuning) are the weakest, as they rely solely on the frozen CLIP image encoder without incorporating language-based prior knowledge; PEMP exceeds them by 4–12 points in C-index/AUC. CoOp and KgCoOp introduce learnable text prompts but struggle to comprehend complex pathology-specific medical terms, yielding intermediate results that PEMP surpasses by 2–7 points. TOP (NeurIPS'23), the closest competitor and the only prior FSWC-specific prompt method, provides language descriptions at both patch and slide levels but lacks task-specific visual examples; PEMP consistently beats TOP by 1.5–6.9 points, with the largest gap on the Round Cell Subtype task (6.2% average AUC improvement) where visual pathology patterns are most critical. The advantage of PEMP is most pronounced in the extreme few-shot regime (2-shot, 4-shot), where prior visual and textual knowledge compensates for the scarcity of training slides.

## Relation to Prior Work

PEMP extends the prompt learning paradigm for few-shot WSI classification established by TOP [27], which introduced two-level (patch and slide) language-based prompts, by adding task-specific visual examples as fixed prompts on the vision side at both levels. It also builds on CoOp's learnable text prompt mechanism [42], which the ablation confirms as the base when all visual and textual examples are removed. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that pathology-specific visual patterns (e.g., vascular invasion, tumor boundary morphology) cannot be adequately conveyed through language descriptions alone, motivating the injection of curated image examples directly into the visual encoder's input space alongside learnable context vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PEMP addresses a practically critical gap: clinical pathology tasks involving rare diseases, prognosis prediction, and metastasis detection inherently suffer from extreme label scarcity, making standard MIL infeasible. The consistent 4–6% improvement over the strongest baseline (TOP) across all shot settings and three distinct clinical tasks suggests that domain-expert-curated visual priors are a viable and interpretable alternative to collecting large annotated datasets. Limitations include dependence on expert curation of visual examples (limiting automation), evaluation on only five datasets across three tasks, and the use of a single VLM backbone (CLIP). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other staining modalities (IHC, FISH), whether the number of visual examples can be reduced or auto-selected, and whether the Messenger/Summary architecture transfers to other MIL aggregation schemes.
