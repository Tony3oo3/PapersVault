## Problem

Existing diffusion model-based unsupervised anomaly detection methods apply a fixed denoising step to all test samples and treat all image regions uniformly during reconstruction. This is suboptimal because different anomalies (e.g., missing elements vs. scratches) require different reconstruction effort, and abnormal regions demand non-Gaussian noise prediction while normal regions only need standard Gaussian noise removal. The paper addresses both the global (per-sample step selection) and local (per-region reconstruction) adaptivity gaps.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB industrial product images resized to 512×512 (or 256×256) as input and produces three outputs: an image-level anomaly score (mean of top-K values from the anomaly map), a pixel-level anomaly map constructed via multi-layer DINO ViT-B/8 cosine similarity between test and reconstructed features, and a reconstructed normal image. A key operational detail is that the number of denoising steps is not fixed but determined adaptively per sample during inference by monitoring the divergence between the noise-free reconstruction and the noise-contaminated input at each step.

## Method

GLAD builds on a pre-trained latent diffusion model (LDM) with a fine-tuned UNet and introduces three components. First, Adaptive Denoising Steps (ADS) performs a global per-sample step selection: starting from a fully noised input, it iteratively denoises and compares the noise-free versions of the current reconstruction against the directly-noised input; when the difference exceeds a threshold δ, it identifies the appropriate starting step and continues denoising from there, preserving normal-region detail while still reconstructing anomalies. Second, Spatial-Adaptive Feature Fusion (SAFF) provides local adaptivity by deriving a spatial mask from the anomaly map at the identified step and fusing the test sample's features in normal regions with the reconstructed features in abnormal regions (Eq. 6–7), then re-adding consistent noise for continued denoising. Third, the Anomaly-oriented Training Paradigm (ATP) synthesizes abnormal samples from normal ones (following MemSeg) and trains with a generalized loss (Eq. 10) that targets ε − (√ᾱₜ/√(1−ᾱₜ))·n rather than pure Gaussian ε, enabling the UNet to predict the non-Gaussian noise required in abnormal regions. Anomaly scoring uses multi-layer DINO features with per-pixel cosine similarity, summed across layers.

## Results

On the average of four datasets (MVTec-AD, MPDD, VisA, PCB-Bank), GLAD (512×512) achieves 98.8/99.2/97.5 (I-AUROC/I-AP/I-F1-max) for image-level detection and 98.8/51.1/54.0/95.3 (P-AUROC/P-AP/P-F1-max/PRO) for pixel-level localization. On MVTec-AD specifically, GLAD reaches 99.3/99.7/98.4 for detection and 98.6/70.9/69.0/95.3 for localization, surpassing the strongest diffusion-based baseline DDAD by 11.9/9.6/3.0 points in P-AP/P-F1-max/PRO. GLAD also achieves the best average P-AP (51.1) and P-F1-max (54.0) across all four datasets, outperforming the next-best method (DDAD) by 13.6 and 11.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLAD is compared against 8 baselines spanning embedding-based (PatchCore, RD4AD, SimpleNet) and reconstruction-based (DRAEM, OCR-GAN, Lu et al., DiffAD, DDAD) methods. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline DDAD, GLAD wins on all localization metrics across all four datasets: on MVTec-AD it exceeds DDAD by 11.9/9.6/3.0 in P-AP/P-F1-max/PRO, and on MPDD by 1.8/10.5/5.7/5.7 in P-AUROC/P-AP/P-F1-max/PRO. For image-level detection, GLAD slightly trails DDAD on I-AUROC for MVTec-AD (99.3 vs 99.8) but surpasses it on I-AP and I-F1-max (99.7/98.4 vs 99.5/97.9). Against embedding-based methods, GLAD dominates on localization: PatchCore achieves only 55.9 P-AP on MVTec-AD versus GLAD's 70.9, and RD4AD reaches 59.0 P-AP versus GLAD's 70.9. Among reconstruction-based non-diffusion methods, DRAEM and OCR-GAN are substantially weaker on localization (P-AP of 68.4 and N/A respectively on MVTec-AD), while Lu et al. and DiffAD report only partial results. Overall, GLAD achieves the best average scores on all seven metrics across the four datasets, with the largest margins over DDAD in P-AP (+13.6) and P-F1-max (+11.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-model-based unsupervised anomaly detection (DiffAD, DDAD, Lu et al.) by identifying and addressing two limitations of the fixed-step reconstruction pipeline: (1) a single denoising step is suboptimal across diverse anomaly severities, and (2) the standard Gaussian noise assumption breaks down in abnormal regions. The global Adaptive Denoising Step (ADP) departs from prior methods that uniformly apply a large fixed step, while the Anomaly-Oriented Training Paradigm (ATP) generalizes the diffusion loss to accommodate non-Gaussian noise targets, building on the synthetic-anomaly idea from MemSeg. The anomaly scoring follows the DINO-based multi-layer cosine similarity approach from AprilGAN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean theoretical derivation showing that the noise to be predicted in abnormal regions necessarily deviates from N(0,I) provides a principled justification for why standard diffusion training is insufficient for anomaly reconstruction, and the ATP loss offers a practical remedy. The ~12-point P-AUROC gap over DDAD on MVTec-AD is substantial for localization, and the new PCB-Bank dataset broadens evaluation beyond the three standard benchmarks. However, the adaptive step determination requires step-by-step comparison during inference, so the method does not actually reduce denoising computation—a limitation the authors acknowledge and flag for future work. Open questions include whether the step-by-step evaluation can be replaced by a learned predictor and whether ATP generalizes to anomaly types not covered by the synthetic augmentation.
