## Problem

Grid-type parametric encoding (e.g., NVP) accelerates INR video training but treats video as static 3D data, ignoring temporal dynamics and resulting in redundant parameters and lower reconstruction quality. NeRV-style methods model temporal structure but suffer from very slow encoding speeds since every pixel requires a full network update. No prior work combines the computational efficiency of parametric encoding with explicit temporal redundancy removal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video sequences (600 frames at HD resolution for UVG, 100 frames for MCL-JCV) and pre-computed optical flow from RAFT (used only as an auxiliary training signal, not at inference). The base network receives normalized 3D coordinates (x, y, t) ∈ [0,1]³ and a modulation latent z_xyt derived from 2D grid lookups, producing (r,g,b) output. Because the representation is continuous in all three dimensions, decoding at doubled spatial or temporal coordinates enables super-resolution and frame interpolation without retraining, and the 2D grid parameters can be further compressed via HEVC for a video compression pipeline.

## Method

NVTM segments the video into GOPs of size n (default 10) and, within each GOP, warps 3D coordinates (x,y,t) to 2D keyframe-aligned coordinates (x_k, y_k) using a hyper-SIREN flow network F_H(t)(x,y) scaled by log(t−t_k). An adaptive normalization step identifies the high-density bounding region of aligned coordinates and maps them into [0,1] for 2D latent grid lookup, avoiding the information loss of clipping or the parameter waste of global re-normalization. The resulting latent z_xyt is concatenated across neighboring GOP grids (index set P) and used to modulate a modulated-SIREN base network via frequency/phase control, so that temporally corresponding pixels share the same modulation and are learned jointly. Training minimizes MSE reconstruction loss plus an auxiliary MSE loss between the learned alignment flow and RAFT optical flow (weight 0.5), and a single static 2D grid is added to capture frame-invariant content.

## Results

On UVG (Dynamic), NVTM achieves 40.54 dB PSNR and 0.071 LPIPS with 122M parameters (10% fewer than competing grid-type methods), outperforming NVP (39.00 dB / 0.090) by 1.54 dB / 0.019. On MCL-JCV (Dynamic), NVTM reaches 41.39 dB PSNR and 0.080 LPIPS, a 1.84 dB / 0.013 improvement over NVP. In encoding speed, NVTM reaches 26.52 dB in ~1 minute of training versus 18.10 dB for NeRV and 23.37 dB for NVP, a >3× speedup over NeRV-style methods at comparable quality. For downstream tasks, NVTM achieves 35.82 dB PSNR on video super-resolution and 30.49 dB on frame interpolation, substantially outperforming NVP (31.87 / 23.88 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRV-style methods (NeRV, E-NeRV, HNeRV), NVTM achieves a >3× encoding speed advantage: at ~1 minute it reaches 26.52 dB versus 18.10–20.73 dB for those methods, and at ~60 minutes it matches or exceeds their final quality (31.85 dB vs. 30.88–32.51 dB on UVG). Against the closest grid-type competitor NVP [25], NVTM wins on both quality (1.54 dB on UVG, 1.84 dB on MCL-JCV) and parameter count (10% fewer), while also encoding faster. Against Instant-NGP [35] and 3D ModSIREN [31], NVTM leads by 3.46 dB (UVG) and 1.84 dB (MCL-JCV) over Instant-NGP, and by 3.30 dB (UVG) and 4.43 dB (MCL-JCV) over 3D ModSIREN. In video compression (BPP-PSNR), NVTM is comparable to or slightly better than NeRV [6] and E-NeRV [27], and notably outperforms NVP [25] at the same bitrate, while remaining competitive with H.264 and HEVC standards. On downstream tasks, NVTM dominates NVP by 3.95 dB (super-resolution) and 6.61 dB (frame interpolation), and also surpasses 3D ModSIREN on both tasks despite not using a 3D-shaped grid.

## Relation to Prior Work

NVTM extends NVP's grid-type parametric encoding for video INR by introducing temporal coherence: instead of treating video as a static 3D volume decomposed into three 2D grids, it uses optical-flow-guided alignment to warp 3D coordinates onto 2D keyframe planes within GOP segments, then applies modulated-SIREN [31] with per-pixel latents from 2D grids. The flow network itself is a hyper-SIREN [17] conditioned on time, and the overall framework departs from NeRV-style frame-wise INR (NeRV, E-NeRV, HNeRV) which sacrifice spatial resolution flexibility for parameter efficiency. The key conceptual departure from NVP is that temporally corresponding pixels share the same modulation latent, explicitly modeling video dynamics rather than encoding them implicitly in a 3D grid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3× encoding speedup over NeRV-style methods while simultaneously achieving 1.5–1.8 dB PSNR gains over the prior grid-based SOTA (NVP) with 10% fewer parameters addresses the two main practical barriers to deploying video INR: training time and model size. The demonstrated versatility across compression, super-resolution, frame interpolation, and inpainting suggests the temporally coherent modulation principle could transfer to other sequential-signal INR settings. Open questions include whether variable GOP sizing (identified as beneficial but not implemented) could close remaining gaps, whether the training-time dependency on RAFT optical flow limits applicability to scenarios where flow is unreliable, and how the approach scales to very long videos beyond the 2400-frame experiments.
