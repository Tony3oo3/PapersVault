## Problem

Despite rapid progress in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), the research community lacks a systematic understanding of which architectural and data decisions actually drive multimodal pre-training performance. Most prior works—both open and closed—release little to no information about the ablation process behind their design choices, making it difficult to distill transferable principles for building performant MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (pre-trained at 378×378, fine-tuned up to 1792×1792 via positional embedding interpolation and sub-image decomposition into 672×672 patches) alongside text prompts, supporting up to 16 images per sequence with 4096-token context. Each image is encoded into 144 visual tokens by default (2,304 tokens at 672×672 resolution). Output is free-form text: image captions, VQA answers, chain-of-thought reasoning, and multi-image comparisons, evaluated in both zero-shot and few-shot (4/8/16-shot) settings.

## Method

The paper conducts comprehensive small-scale ablations across three axes—image encoder (resolution, pre-training objective, model size), vision-language connector (average pooling, attention pooling, C-Abstractor), and pre-training data mixture (caption/interleaved/text-only ratios)—using a 1.2B–2.9B LLM base to identify which choices matter. Key findings: image resolution has the largest impact (~3% boost from 224→336), the VL connector architecture is comparatively negligible, and a 45% interleaved / 45% caption / 10% text-only data mix is critical for balancing few-shot, zero-shot, and text-only performance. The winning recipe (ViT-H at 378×378, C-Abstractor with 144 tokens, the above data mix) is then scaled to 3B, 7B, and 30B dense models plus 64B and 47B MoE variants, trained for 200k steps (~400B tokens) with a learning rate extrapolated via log-space linear regression over model size.

## Results

After supervised fine-tuning, MM1-3B-Chat and MM1-7B-Chat set new state-of-the-art results for their respective model sizes across 12 multimodal benchmarks. MM1-7B-Chat achieves 82.8 on VQAv2, 72.8 on TextVQA, 72.6 on ScienceQA-IMG, and 72.3 on MMBench, outperforming all listed 7B baselines. At 30B scale, MM1-30B-Chat reaches 83.7 on VQAv2, 73.5 on TextVQA, 81.0 on ScienceQA-IMG, and 1637.6 on MME-Perception, surpassing Emu2-Chat-37B and CogVLM-30B on most benchmarks. MoE variants (3B-MoE with 64 experts, 7B-MoE with 32 experts) achieve uniformly better performance than their dense counterparts on nearly every benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM1-3B-Chat outperforms all 3B baselines on average, with particularly large margins on TextVQA (71.9 vs. 59.4 for Imp-v1 and 59.1 for TinyLLaVA) and VQAv2 (82.0 vs. 79.9 for TinyLLaVA). At 7B scale, MM1-7B-Chat leads all competitors on TextVQA (72.8 vs. 64.9 for LLaVA-NeXT-7B), MMBench (72.3 vs. 67.4 for LLaVA-NeXT-7B), and ScienceQA (72.6 vs. 70.1 for LLaVA-NeXT-7B), while matching or exceeding LLaVA-NeXT on VQAv2 (82.8 vs. 81.8). At 30B scale, MM1-30B-Chat surpasses Emu2-Chat-37B on TextVQA (73.5 vs. 66.6), SEED-Bench (65.9 vs. 62.8), and MMMU (44.7 vs. 36.3), and beats CogVLM-30B on TextVQA (73.5 vs. 68.1) and MMMU (44.7 vs. 32.1). However, LLaVA-NeXT-34B retains an edge on MMMU (51.1 vs. 44.7) and MathVista (46.5 vs. 39.4), though MM1 uses only 720 image tokens per image versus LLaVA-NeXT's 2,880. The MoE variants consistently outperform their dense counterparts: MM1-7B-MoE-Chat improves over MM1-7B-Chat on every reported benchmark (e.g., TextVQA 73.8 vs. 72.8, MMMU 40.9 vs. 37.0, MME-P 1597.4 vs. 1529.3). In pre-training evaluations (Table 3), MM1-30B achieves SOTA few-shot performance, scoring 123.1 on COCO Captioning (8-shot) and 70.9 on VQAv2, surpassing Flamingo-80B (108.8, 65.6) and IDEFICS-80B (114.3, 64.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the decoder-only multimodal architecture lineage initiated by Kosmos-1 and the MoE scaling techniques from GShard/ST-MoE, but its primary contribution is a systematic empirical ablation study of MLLM pre-training components. It departs from prior works like VILA, Emu2, and IDEFICS, which each provide partial details on pre-training, by offering comprehensive ablations across image encoders, vision-language connectors, and data mixtures under a unified protocol. A key conceptual departure is the finding that the vision-language connector architecture (average pooling vs. attention pooling vs. C-Abstractor) has comparatively negligible impact, contradicting claims made by Honeybee and others that connector design is critical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-documented recipe for building competitive MLLMs, with actionable findings on data mixture (45% interleaved / 45% caption / 10% text) and the dominance of image resolution over encoder type. The demonstration that large-scale multimodal pre-training enables few-shot chain-of-thought and multi-image reasoning that persists after SFT opens a path toward more flexible, prompt-driven multimodal systems rather than task-specific fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the MoE scaling results (3B-MoE with 64 experts, 7B-MoE with 32 experts) can be pushed further, whether the VL connector finding holds at much larger scales, and how the data mixture conclusions generalize to new data sources as they emerge.
