## Problem

Current evaluation methodologies for adversarial robustness in semantic segmentation rely on a limited set of attacks and class-wise aggregated mIoU, which overestimate the robustness of adversarially trained models. The paper argues that a reliable assessment of segmentation model sensitivity to adversarial perturbations requires significantly more effort than what is currently considered satisfactory, and that the apparent lack of a robustness-accuracy tradeoff in the literature is an artifact of insufficient evaluation.

## Input / Output

The evaluation framework takes a pre-trained segmentation model (PSPNet, DeepLabv3, or UPerNet with ResNet-50 or ConvNeXt backbones) and RGB images normalized to [0,1] in H×W×3 format, producing adversarial perturbations bounded by ε=8/255 in the L∞ norm. Evaluation is performed on PASCAL VOC 2012 validation (longer dimension scaled to 512px) and Cityscapes validation (1024×512), reporting pixel accuracy, class-wise mIoU (CmIoU), and image-wise mIoU (NmIoU) both on clean and adversarially perturbed inputs, with and without background pixels.

## Method

The paper constructs a battery of ten attacks—two novel PAdam variants (using AMSGrad with 200 iterations and step size 2/255, optimizing cross-entropy or cosine similarity), four SEA attacks (BCE, MCE, JSD, MSL), ALMAProx, DAG (two step sizes), and PDPGD—and applies them in an ensemble fashion, selecting the most successful attack per input according to the target metric. A key finding is that no single attack dominates across all models, making the diverse ensemble essential for reliable upper bounds on vulnerability. The paper also introduces NmIoU (image-wise average mIoU) as a complementary metric to the standard CmIoU, revealing a size-bias where robust models protect large objects while sacrificing small ones—a phenomenon invisible to class-wise aggregation. Across 22 models and 280 model-attack-dataset combinations, the aggregated attacks reduce robust metrics to near zero for 50%-adversarial-batch models and expose severe NmIoU degradation even in the best-performing SEA-AT models.

## Results

Using an aggregated set of 10 attacks (including the novel PAdam-CE and PAdam-Cos), all models trained with 50% adversarial batches (DDC-AT, PGD-AT, SegPGD-AT) drop to near-zero robustness on every metric (e.g., DDC-AT PSPNet+PASCAL: 91.42% clean accuracy → 0.00% robust accuracy; 68.87% clean CmIoU → 0.00% robust CmIoU). Models trained with 100% adversarial batches (PGD-AT-100, SegPGD-AT-100) retain some pixel-accuracy robustness (39–48%) but collapse in CmIoU (23–25%) and NmIoU (33–37%). The best-performing SEA-AT-Small model maintains 93.10% clean accuracy and 70.57% robust accuracy, yet its NmIoU plummets from 77.08% (clean) to 12.89% (robust), revealing a severe size-bias where small objects are sacrificed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDC-AT [42] and its PGD-AT baseline, both trained with 50% adversarial batches, show essentially zero robustness under the aggregated attack set: robust accuracy, CmIoU, and NmIoU all drop to 0.00–0.51% across all four architecture/dataset combinations, contradicting the non-trivial robustness reported in the original paper. SegPGD-AT [22], also using 50% adversarial batches, similarly collapses to near-zero on all three metrics (e.g., 51.59% clean CmIoU → 0.02% robust CmIoU for PSPNet+PASCAL). The 100%-adversarial variants (PGD-AT-100, SegPGD-AT-100) trained by the authors retain moderate pixel-accuracy robustness (39–48%) but remain highly vulnerable in IoU-based metrics (CmIoU 5–6%, NmIoU 11–13%), and their clean performance degrades substantially (e.g., 79% vs. 92% accuracy). SEA-AT [17] is the strongest baseline: SEA-AT-Small achieves 93.10% clean accuracy and 70.57% robust accuracy, the best robust accuracy among all models tested. However, its NmIoU drops from 77.08% to 12.89%, revealing a size-bias where small objects are systematically removed by attacks while large objects are preserved—a vulnerability invisible to the CmIoU metric (73.53% → 32.66%) or pixel accuracy used in the original evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a methodological critique of the adversarial robustness evaluation pipeline for semantic segmentation, building directly on the attack and training frameworks of DDC-AT (Xu et al.), SegPGD-AT (Gu et al.), and SEA-AT (Croce et al.). It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it introduces PAdam, a projected Adam-based attack that complements the existing PGD/SegPGD/SEA family, and (2) it argues that the standard class-wise mIoU aggregation (CmIoU) masks a critical size-bias, proposing image-wise mIoU (NmIoU) as a necessary additional metric. Conceptually, it extends the 'ensemble of diverse attacks' evaluation philosophy from image classification (Croce & Hein's APGD ensemble) to segmentation, but with the added insight that no single attack dominates across all model types.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fundamentally challenges the reported robustness of all state-of-the-art adversarially trained segmentation models, showing that the 50% adversarial training recipe used by DDC-AT and SegPGD-AT yields essentially zero robustness under a stronger evaluation. The revealed size-bias—where models protect large objects while sacrificing small ones—has practical implications for safety-critical applications (e.g., autonomous driving) where small objects matter. Open questions include: what fraction of adversarial training samples is actually needed, whether the size-bias can be mitigated through loss design, and whether the evaluation methodology generalizes to other pixel-wise prediction tasks like depth estimation or object detection. The authors acknowledge the limited exploration of training variants due to computational cost, leaving the design space of robust segmentation training largely unexplored.
