## Problem

Previous face reconstruction attacks produce images that bypass only the targeted (seen) encoder but fail catastrophically when tested against unseen face recognition systems, limiting their practical threat. This paper identifies that the transfer attack problem has been underexplored and shows it can be rigorously formulated as an out-of-distribution generalization problem over encoder parameters, enabling principled OOD techniques to be applied.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single white-box encoder and its corresponding biometric template (a feature vector embedded through that encoder) as input, and produces a 256×256 RGB face image as output. Optimization is performed in the W+ latent space of a frozen StyleGAN2 generator trained on FFHQ-256, so the attacker never directly manipulates pixels. A surrogate validation encoder (e.g., Swin-T) is also used during the search phase but is not part of the attack-time input.

## Method

The paper proves that FRTA is equivalent to OOD generalization via a data-parameter duality argument for MLPs, then proposes ALSUV (Averaged Latent Search with Unsupervised Validation) to exploit this equivalence. ALSUV has three components: (1) parallel optimization of n=100 latents to avoid underfitting and escape poor local minima; (2) trajectory averaging over the last t_avg=70 optimization steps to produce flatter minima in the reconstruction loss landscape, directly analogous to weight averaging in OOD generalization; and (3) unsupervised validation where a pseudo target is constructed by averaging the top-k=10 reconstructed features in a surrogate validation encoder's space, and the final latent is selected by minimizing distance to this pseudo target. This three-stage pipeline simultaneously prevents underfitting to the seen encoder (via multi-latent search) and overfitting to it (via averaging and validation), yielding reconstructed faces that generalize across diverse unseen encoders.

## Results

On LFW, ALSUV achieves 99.83% SAR on the seen encoder (FaceNet) and maintains 59–97% SAR across unseen encoders (ResNet50, ResNet100, Swin-S, VGGNet), closely matching real-face performance (99.8–99.9%). On CFP-FP, it reaches 94.62% SAR on the seen encoder and 58–84% on unseen encoders. The best prior method (QEZOGE) achieves only 19.55% SAR on the seen FaceNet encoder and drops to 0.57–93.29% on unseen encoders, showing high variance. In the ablation (Table 3), the full ALSUV pipeline (n=100, latent averaging, unsupervised validation) improves average SAR by 46.14% on LFW, 12.23% on CFP-FP, and 22.62% on AgeDB-30 over single-latent optimization without ALSUV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALSUV outperforms all seven baselines on unseen encoders across LFW, CFP-FP, and AgeDB-30. NBNet [21] and LatentMap [7] achieve reasonable SAR on the seen encoder (82.91%) but collapse to 0.57–36% on unseen encoders, indicating severe overfitting. GaussBlob [26] is the weakest baseline, scoring below 22% on nearly all unseen encoders. EigenFace [27] shows moderate transfer (17–84%) but with high variance depending on the encoder pair. QEZOGE [22], the strongest prior transfer method, reaches 93.29% on one unseen encoder (Net Encoder) but drops to 0.57% on ResNet100, demonstrating inconsistent generalization. FaceTI [32] and Genetic [8] show intermediate performance but remain well below ALSUV on most unseen configurations. On seen encoders, ALSUV matches or exceeds real-face SAR (99.83% vs. 99.87%), while DiBiGAN [9] (Table 5) achieves 99.18% on LFW seen, slightly below ALSUV's 99.85%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the face reconstruction attack literature (NBNet, LatentMap, DiBiGAN, GaussBlob, EigenFace, FaceTI, QEZOGE) by explicitly targeting transfer to unseen encoders rather than the seen system, and by providing a rigorous OOD generalization formulation of the problem. Conceptually, it adapts flat-minima-seeking and weight-averaging techniques from the OOD generalization literature (Petzka et al. 2021, Cha et al. 2021, Rame et al. 2022) to the latent space of a frozen StyleGAN2 generator, and borrows pseudo-label validation ideas from domain adaptation to construct a surrogate target for unseen-encoder selection. The key departure from prior face reconstruction work is the formal theorem establishing duality between the FRTA optimization and standard OOD generalization, which justifies the averaging and validation components as principled rather than heuristic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The transfer attack scenario is arguably more dangerous than single-system attacks because a single reconstructed image can potentially bypass multiple deployed face recognition systems, yet prior work has underexplored this setting. The OOD generalization framing provides a unifying lens that connects biometric security to a well-studied ML generalization problem, and the Hessian-based validation that latent averaging produces flatter minima gives mechanistic evidence for why the approach works. Limitations include the white-box assumption (full access to one encoder), the pseudo-target being an approximation of the true unseen-encoder target, and evaluation on relatively small-scale verification datasets (200 identities). Open questions include whether the OOD framing extends to black-box or multi-encoder settings, whether defenses can be designed by explicitly flattening the encoder's decision boundary, and whether the approach generalizes to other biometric modalities.
