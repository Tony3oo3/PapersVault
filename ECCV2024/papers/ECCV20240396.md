## Problem

Existing image interpolation methods—video frame interpolation and image morphing—either assume small motion between frames or restrict to topologically similar objects, producing severe artifacts and semantic fragmentation when the input pair exhibits large motion. No prior method explicitly models semantic consistency of intermediate frames, where the relevant semantic content may be absent from both input images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240396/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images with large motion as input and produces a sequence of 32 intermediate images forming a short video. All operations occur in the 4×-downsampled latent space of Stable Diffusion 1.5; the LoRA fine-tuning step requires only ~40 seconds on a single RTX 3090 GPU. The pipeline uses 50 DDIM steps, performs fusion at the 30th noisy step, and extracts flow-estimation features at the 14th noisy step from the second U-Net up-block.

## Method

DreamMover leverages a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) as a semantic prior through three key mechanisms. First, it estimates bidirectional optical flow without an external flow network by extracting feature maps from U-Net up-blocks during DDIM inversion and computing pixel correspondences via cosine similarity. Second, it performs a two-level fusion in latent space: the high-level component (z_{T→0}) is warped with softmax splatting and time-weighted interpolation to preserve spatial layout, while the low-level component (ε_θ) is fused with a Winner-Takes-All operation to retain high-frequency details that averaging would destroy. Third, during denoising, it replaces the intermediate image's self-attention keys and values with the concatenation of those from both input images, and fine-tunes a single rank-16 LoRA on the image pair to lock in semantic identity.

## Results

On InterpBench, DreamMover achieves the best FID (43.18), LPIPS (0.2227), and WE_mid (0.3687) among all methods, outperforming the closest competitor LDMVFI (FID 48.35, LPIPS 0.2347, WE_mid 0.4373). However, DreamMover trails Film (WE 0.1244) and LDMVFI (WE 0.1453) on the Warping Error metric, which measures temporal coherency. In a user study with 139 participants, DreamMover is preferred over all baselines by a wide margin, with the closest comparison being Film at 75.23% vs 24.77%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamMover outperforms all four baselines on FID, LPIPS, and WE_mid, the metrics most directly tied to image fidelity and semantic consistency with the input pair. Against DiffInterp (FID 185.78) and Diffmorpher (FID 68.23), the gains are dramatic, reflecting that these methods struggle with large motion and produce fragmented or distorted results. Against the stronger video-interpolation baselines Film and LDMVFI, DreamMover wins on FID by 11.1 and 5.2 points respectively, and on WE_mid by 0.049 and 0.069, confirming better preservation of input identity. The one metric where DreamMover underperforms is WE (temporal warping error): Film achieves 0.1244 and LDMVFI 0.1453 versus DreamMover's 0.2069, indicating slightly less frame-to-frame smoothness. The authors note that this trade-off is acceptable because Film and LDMVFI's smoother motion comes at the cost of semantically inconsistent content (e.g., object fragmentation), which is the primary failure mode the paper targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a pre-trained text-to-image latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5) for large-motion image interpolation, departing from both video frame interpolation methods (which assume small inter-frame motion and lack semantic reasoning) and image morphing methods (which require topologically similar objects). The key conceptual departure is using the U-Net's up-block features during DDIM inversion as a semantic correspondence signal for optical flow estimation—eliminating the need for a dedicated flow network—while simultaneously leveraging the diffusion prior to hallucinate intermediate semantics absent from both input images. The two-level fusion strategy (softmax splatting for layout, Winner-Takes-All for high-frequency details) and self-attention concatenation/replacement are novel mechanisms layered on top of the standard DDIM denoising pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing large-motion image interpolation as a semantic consistency problem and introducing InterpBench, the paper provides the first dedicated evaluation protocol for this setting, which prior video frame interpolation and morphing benchmarks cannot address. The practical limitation of operating in low-resolution latent space (texture sticking, difficulty with subtle motion) and the reliance on a single pre-trained model (SD 1.5) leave open questions about scalability to higher resolutions and more diverse scene types. The insight that a text-to-image model's implicit semantic features can substitute for explicit optical flow estimation without any additional training is a broadly applicable idea that could inform other correspondence-based tasks.
