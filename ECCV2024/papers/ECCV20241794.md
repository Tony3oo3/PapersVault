## Problem

Event-based networks trained on daytime events suffer significant performance degradation when applied to nighttime events due to distributional shifts (excessive single-polarity activation, inherent noise, edge blurring from artificial lighting). This gap is compounded by the scarcity of annotated night event data, making it impractical to simply augment training sets. The paper addresses this by learning an unpaired day-to-night event translation that preserves the unique spatio-temporal structure of events, which image-based generation methods fail to capture.

## Input / Output

The method takes day event histograms of shape (2×B)×H×W (two polarity channels × B temporal bins × spatial dimensions), cropped and resized to 256×256, and produces night event histograms in the identical format. Experiments use bin sizes B ∈ {1, 3, 8}, corresponding to 2, 6, and 16 channels respectively. The translation is strictly unpaired—no day-night correspondence exists—so the generator must learn the mapping from marginal distributions alone. For evaluation, translated events are also converted to images via E2VID to compute FID/KID scores.

## Method

The framework builds on UNSB (a Diffusion GAN using Schrödinger Bridge) to define a stochastic path from the day event distribution to the night event distribution, with a U-Net generator stepping along this path. Three key modules address event-specific challenges: (1) a Temporally Disentangled Encoder that processes each temporal bin through separate convolutional blocks before merging, preventing standard convolutions from collapsing temporal order; (2) a Wavelet-based Decomposed Bottleneck that splits features into low- and high-frequency subbands, applying residual processing to the HH (high-high) component to capture the characteristic noise style of night events while preserving structure via the LL residual; and (3) a Temporally Shuffling Contrastive Regularization that creates negative samples by permuting the temporal bin order of encoded features, pulling the correctly-ordered representation closer to the day-event reference while pushing shuffled versions away. The total loss combines the adversarial/Schrödinger Bridge objective with spatial and temporal contrastive terms, all applied in an unsupervised manner.

## Results

On the DSEC dataset, the proposed method achieves the best E2I-FID (23.20) and E2I-KID (3.74) for Bin=1, and a dominant FVD of 7.19 with E2I-KID of 1.24 for Bin=3, substantially outperforming all baselines. For the 16-channel (Bin=8) setting, it attains FVD 10.14 and E2I-KID 1.77, again leading on temporal-consistency metrics. In downstream tasks, training with translated night events yields mIoU of 39.58 (Bin=8) for semantic segmentation and AP of 13.55 (Bin=3) for object detection, compared to day-event-only baselines of 36.65 and 8.87 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on the E2I-FID and E2I-KID metrics across all three bin configurations, confirming superior fidelity to real night event characteristics. Against the closest event-based competitor UNSB, the gap is most dramatic for multi-bin settings: FVD drops from 74.25 to 7.19 (Bin=3) and from 34.38 to 10.14 (Bin=8), while E2I-KID improves from 4.48 to 1.24 and from 3.31 to 1.77 respectively. CUT achieves a marginally lower FVD for Bin=8 (9.72 vs. 10.14) but is far behind on E2I-KID (3.43 vs. 1.77), indicating CUT preserves temporal structure less faithfully. Frame-based methods (EventGAN, CMDA) and simulators (v2e, DVS-Voltmeter) are consistently worst, with FVD values 5–40× higher, reflecting their inability to model event temporal continuity. On the unseen MVSEC dataset, the method trained on DSEC achieves FVD 130.47, beating CycleGAN (294.94), CUT (158.39), and UNSB (322.60), demonstrating generalizability. In downstream tasks, the method is the only one that improves over the day-event-only baseline for Bin=8 in both segmentation (39.58 vs. 36.65 mIoU) and detection (13.55 vs. 8.87 AP), where all other baselines degrade performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Schrödinger Bridge-based Diffusion GAN framework of UNSB (Kim et al., 2023) from the image modality to event camera data, where temporal continuity across histogram bins introduces constraints absent in standard unpaired image translation. It departs from prior event generation approaches (EventGAN, CMDA) that synthesize events from sequential images and from simulators (v2e, DVS-Voltmeter) that cannot perform day-to-night domain transfer, instead operating directly in the event-to-event space. The key architectural departures are a temporally disentangled encoder that prevents cross-bin information leakage during convolution, a wavelet-decomposed bottleneck that isolates high-frequency night-event noise from low-frequency structure, and a temporally shuffling contrastive loss that enforces temporal ordering without requiring paired data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in event-based perception: the scarcity of annotated night events causes significant performance degradation for models trained on day data, and this work provides a task-agnostic pipeline to synthesize night events from abundant day annotations. However, the framework is limited to frame-wise (histogram) event representations and is validated primarily on autonomous driving scenes, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to raw event streams, SNN-based architectures, or non-driving domains. The reliance on convolutional U-Net generators and the need to convert events to grid-based formats remain constraints that future work with graph-based or spiking networks could address.
