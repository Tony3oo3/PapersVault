## Problem

Graphics pipelines require physically-based rendering (PBR) materials, yet existing 3D content generation approaches operate in the RGB domain, either generating RGB images and extracting PBR via ambiguous inverse rendering, or fine-tuning RGB diffusion models on the limited PBR data available (Objaverse, ~800K objects), which causes catastrophic forgetting. The paper addresses this by proposing a paradigm that directly models the PBR image distribution while retaining the expressiveness and generalizability of a pre-trained RGB model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and screen-space geometry normals (concatenated to the PBR branch input) and produces a full PBR material stack — albedo (3ch), metallic (1ch), roughness (1ch), and a bump map (3ch) defined in a geometry-based tangent space rather than an arbitrary UV tangent space — at 512×512 resolution. The PBR images are encoded in a dedicated 14-channel PBR VAE latent space, and the frozen RGB model simultaneously produces a rendered RGB image aligned to a fixed camera and environment map, ensuring the two outputs are pixel-aligned.

## Method

Collaborative Control trains a parallel PBR diffusion model alongside a fully frozen RGB diffusion model (ZeroDiffusion/SD 1.5), linked by bidirectional cross-network communication layers placed after every self-attention module. Each communication layer concatenates the hidden states of both models, processes them through a small per-pixel linear (or MLP) layer, and residually distributes the result back to both branches — allowing the PBR model to extract semantic/structural information from the RGB model while simultaneously guiding the RGB model's output toward render-like images (black background, consistent lighting). A dedicated PBR VAE (14 latent channels) encodes the multi-channel PBR stack, and text cross-attention is deliberately disabled in the PBR model so that prompt conditioning flows exclusively through the frozen RGB branch, preventing overfitting on the restricted Objaverse training set. Training jointly optimizes the PBR model weights and communication layers against both denoising losses while keeping the RGB model frozen, requiring only ~2 days on a single A100 at 512×512.

## Results

On the 2% held-out Objaverse evaluation, the full Collaborative Control configuration (bi-directional pixel-wise zero-conv, PBR VAE, 512×512, 98% data) achieves CMMD 6.30 (PBR) / 1.79 (relit), FID 11.65 / 10.64, QAlign aesthetics 2.11 / quality 2.03, and CLIPScore 26.76 / 25.41. The method is remarkably data-efficient: training on only 1% of the dataset (~60K images) yields CMMD 6.25, nearly matching the 98% configuration (6.30). Scaling to 8 A100s (8× batch size) improves CMMD to 2.96 and FID to 9.55, while the PBR-specific VAE is critical—replacing it with the RGB VAE on channel triplets degrades CMMD from 6.30 to 84.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly notes that no published PBR generation models exist for entire objects, so all comparisons are internal ablations rather than external baselines. Among communication paradigms, bi-directional control (CMMD 6.30) outperforms clockwise (6.78) and dramatically outperforms one-way (16.44), confirming that the PBR branch must both read from and write to the frozen RGB model. The PBR-specific VAE is the single most critical design choice: replacing it with the RGB VAE on channel triplets causes a catastrophic CMMD degradation from 6.30 to 84.66. End-to-end fine-tuning of the base model matches Collaborative Control on distribution match (CMMD 5.25 without RGB output) but collapses on OOD CLIPScore (23.31 vs. 25.41), confirming the frozen-model approach prevents catastrophic forgetting. The method is highly data-efficient: 1% of training data achieves CMMD 6.25, essentially matching 98% (6.30), though enabling PBR prompt cross-attention at 1% data causes severe overfitting (CMMD 20.61). A per-pixel MLP communication layer marginally improves CMMD (5.43 vs. 6.30) and OOD CLIPScore (27.15 vs. 26.76) over the simpler zero-conv layer, but the authors prefer the latter for computational efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet/ControlNet-XS paradigm of parallel-model conditioning for diffusion models, but departs fundamentally by making the communication bidirectional and by keeping the base RGB model fully frozen while training a new PBR branch. Where prior cross-modal fine-tuning work (depth, semantics, intrinsic properties) edits the base model's weights, Collaborative Control instead trains a dedicated PBR VAE and a parallel PBR diffusion model linked via lightweight cross-network layers, avoiding catastrophic forgetting on the restricted Objaverse dataset. The approach also builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the geometry-conditioning strategy of Ke et al., but introduces a geometry-tangent-space bump map representation to disentangle surface detail from arbitrary UV mappings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By directly modeling the PBR image distribution rather than generating RGB and performing inverse rendering, this work eliminates the photometric inaccuracies and material ambiguities that plague current Text-to-Texture pipelines, making generated content immediately usable in game engines and other PBR-based graphics workflows. The demonstrated data efficiency (strong performance at 1% of training data) and compatibility with existing adapters like IP-Adapter make the approach practical and extensible. Key open questions include whether the two-model inference cost can be distilled into a single PBR model, how to resolve the persistent failure mode of constant roughness/metallic/bump maps (likely a data bias from Objaverse), and whether the bidirectional communication paradigm generalizes to other high-dimensional multi-channel output modalities beyond PBR.
