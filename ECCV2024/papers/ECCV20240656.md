## Problem

Blind Spot Network (BSN) methods for self-supervised denoising prevent identity mapping by restricting receptive fields through blind-spot convolutions and dilated convolutions, which severely limits the choice of denoiser architecture and degrades performance on detailed textures. This paper asks whether masking pixels at the input level can achieve the same identity-mapping prevention without imposing any structural constraints on the denoiser.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (no clean reference) and produces a fully denoised RGB image. Before processing, the image is pixel-downsampled with stride s (P_s) to generate s² sub-samples that break spatial noise correlation; during training, approximately 50% of pixels in each sub-sample are randomly zeroed out. The framework is denoiser-agnostic and has been validated with Restormer, DnCNN, NAFNet, UNet, and DeamNet, operating on images from 256×256 (SIDD) to 512×512 (DND, PolyU) resolutions.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces BSN's receptive-field restriction with input-level masking: a binary mask M zeros out ~50% of pixels, the denoiser D_E processes the masked image, and the loss is computed only on masked positions where reconstruction must come from unmasked neighbors, thereby preventing identity mapping without constraining the network. Training uses a single mask branch for efficiency, while inference employs a Multi-branch Mask complementary Denoising Block (MMDB) with k≥2 branches whose complementary masks partition all pixels, and outputs are summed to reconstruct the full image. A pixel-downsampling strategy (inspired by AP-BSN) is applied to both training and inference to satisfy the zero-mean, pixel-independent noise assumption. To mitigate the checkerboard artifact from interlaced pixel reconstruction, a prior smoothness loss is introduced during fine-tuning and a random replacement refinement is applied at inference. The asymmetric design—single branch for training, multi-branch for inference—enables fast optimization while guaranteeing complete image coverage.

## Results

On SIDD Validation, AMSNet-P-E achieves 37.93 dB PSNR / 0.8948 SSIM, outperforming the best self-supervised baseline BNN-LAN (37.39/0.8830) by 0.54 dB. On SIDD Benchmark, it reaches 37.87/0.941, and on DND Benchmark, 38.70/0.9471, setting new state-of-the-art among self-supervised methods. On PolyU, AMSNet-P-E scores 37.92/0.9645, surpassing the next-best BNN-LAN (37.13/0.9541) by 0.79 dB PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMSNet-P-E outperforms all self-supervised baselines across SIDD, DND, and PolyU. On SIDD Validation it leads BNN-LAN by 0.54 dB and LG-BPN+R³ by 0.62 dB in PSNR; on DND it surpasses LG-BPN+R³ by 0.27 dB and BNN-LAN by 0.52 dB. On PolyU, the margin over BNN-LAN widens to 0.79 dB (37.92 vs. 37.13). A critical ablation (Table 3) shows that when the denoiser's receptive field is unrestricted (D_AS), AP-BSN collapses to 20.91 dB PSNR due to identity mapping, while AMSNet retains 37.11 dB—demonstrating that the mask scheme fundamentally removes the receptive-field constraint that plagues BSN-based methods. Among supervised baselines, Restormer (40.02 dB on SIDD Bench) still leads, but AMSNet-P-E narrows the gap to under 2.2 dB without requiring any clean reference images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant blind spot network (BSN) lineage in self-supervised denoising (Noise2Void, AP-BSN, LG-BPN, BNN-LAN, SCPGabN) by replacing the blind-spot/dilated-convolution mechanism with a mask-based strategy inspired by MAE. It retains the pixel downsampling (PD) strategy from AP-BSN to handle spatially correlated real-world noise, but the core architectural constraint—limiting receptive fields to prevent identity mapping—is eliminated. The asymmetric design (single mask for training, multi-mask for inference) is the paper's central conceptual contribution, positioning it as a drop-in replacement framework that accepts any modern denoiser (Restormer, NAFNet, DnCNN, etc.) without structural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the receptive-field constraint, this work unblocks the use of state-of-the-art denoisers (e.g., Restormer, NAFNet) in self-supervised real-image denoising, a practical limitation that has persisted since Noise2Void. The remaining open issues are the checkerboard artifacts introduced by pixel downsampling and the residual information loss from sub-sampling, which the authors mitigate but do not fully resolve. A key open question is whether the mask-based paradigm can be extended to handle structured or non-i.i.d. noise without the PD step, and whether the multi-branch inference cost (k× forward passes) can be reduced for real-time applications.
