## Problem

Existing vision transformers inject spatial information only through local positional embeddings attached to individual image tokens, which cannot model the global spatial structure of the underlying scene. This design constrains generalization: adapting to higher resolutions requires lossy interpolation of positional embeddings, and transferring to 3D tasks leaves the encoder without any 3D scene awareness, forcing task-specific decoders to compensate for the missing spatial prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (or multi-view images with camera intrinsics/extrinsics for 3D tasks) and produces two streams of output tokens: updated image tokens encoding spatial-aware context features, and updated spatial tokens that can be directly fed as initial queries into task-specific decoders (DETR-style detection, Mask2Former segmentation, BEVFormer 3D detection). The number of spatial tokens (default 8×8 = 64) is decoupled from input resolution, so the same backbone handles 224×224, 384×384, or arbitrary-resolution inputs without re-interpolating positional embeddings. For 3D adaptation, spatial tokens are initialized from 3D world coordinates obtained by lifting 2D pixels through discretized depth and camera transforms.

## Method

SpatialFormer introduces a set of adaptive spatial tokens initialized with Fourier positional encoding of a regular spatial grid (2D pixel coordinates or 3D world coordinates) plus learnable embeddings, providing both a general spatial prior and capacity for task-specific adaptation. The core building block is a Bilateral Cross-Attention (BCA) layer that, in parallel, performs (i) image-to-spatial cross-attention so spatial tokens absorb context, (ii) spatial self-attention to model inter-token spatial relations, and (iii) spatial-to-image cross-attention so image tokens receive spatial guidance. The overall architecture is a decoder-only four-stage pyramid: stages 1–2 use BCA blocks, while stages 3–4 switch to a Self-Attention block that concatenates spatial and image tokens, trading some spatial modeling for lower FLOPs at reduced token counts. Because the spatial tokens carry explicit scene geometry, they can be directly reused as initial queries for downstream decoders, transferring spatial perception learned during pre-training without architectural modification.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, SpatialFormer-T achieves 81.5% top-1 accuracy (11.6M params, 2.4G FLOPs), SpatialFormer-S achieves 83.8% (25M, 4.8G), and SpatialFormer-B achieves 84.5% (50M, 9.8G). At 384×384 fine-tuning, SpatialFormer-S and SpatialFormer-B reach 84.7% and 85.3% respectively. On COCO detection (Mask-RCNN 1x), SpatialFormer-S and SpatialFormer-B achieve 47.8 and 49.2 AP^b; on ADE20K segmentation (UpperNet), they achieve 48.1 and 50.3 mIoU; and on nuScenes 3D detection (BEVFormer), SpatialFormer(3D) reaches 0.392 NDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialFormer consistently outperforms same-size baselines across all four tasks. On ImageNet, SpatialFormer-T (81.5%) surpasses PVTv2-b1 (78.7%) by 2.8% and Shunted-T (79.8%) by 1.7%; SpatialFormer-S (83.8%) beats UniFormer-S (82.9%) by 0.9% and CMT-S (83.5%) by 0.3%; SpatialFormer-B (84.5%) exceeds Swin-B (83.5%) by 1.0%, ConvNeXt-B (83.8%) by 0.7%, and CSWin-B (84.2%) by 0.3% while using roughly half the FLOPs (9.8G vs. 15.0–15.8G). On COCO detection, SpatialFormer-S achieves 47.8 AP^b, outperforming UniFormer-S (45.6) by 2.2 and CrossFormer-S (45.4) by 2.4; SpatialFormer-B reaches 49.2 AP^b, beating UniFormer-B (47.4) by 1.8. On ADE20K segmentation, SpatialFormer-B (50.3 mIoU) surpasses Swin-B (48.1) by 2.2 mIoU with fewer FLOPs (1028G vs. 1188G). On nuScenes 3D detection, SpatialFormer(2D) improves NDS from 0.369 (BEVFormer+Swin-T) to 0.389, and SpatialFormer(3D) further reaches 0.392, demonstrating the benefit of 3D positional encoding for spatial scene understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialFormer extends the vision transformer lineage (ViT, Swin, PVT) by introducing explicit spatial tokens initialized with Fourier positional encodings (following Tancik et al.) alongside standard image tokens, departing from the conventional approach of injecting spatial information solely through per-token positional embeddings. The bilateral cross-attention block repurposes the decoder cross-attention mechanism to enable bidirectional information flow between context and spatial tokens within a decoder-only backbone, a design distinct from encoder-only ViTs or encoder-decoder frameworks like DETR. The concept of using learned queries to encode scene structure echoes DETR's object queries and BEVFormer's spatial queries, but SpatialFormer elevates this to the backbone level so that spatial awareness is baked into the pre-trained representation rather than introduced only at the task-specific decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding spatial understanding directly in the backbone, SpatialFormer addresses a practical pain point: conventional ViTs require bicubic interpolation of positional embeddings for resolution changes and lack 3D perceptual capacity when transferred to multi-view tasks. The spatial tokens' dual role—as internal scene representations during pre-training and as enhanced initial queries for downstream decoders—offers a clean mechanism for knowledge transfer that could simplify the pre-training-to-fine-tuning pipeline for dense prediction and 3D perception. Open questions include whether the fixed grid of spatial tokens scales gracefully to very high-resolution or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the Fourier initialization remains optimal as model capacity grows. The 3D generalization results, while promising, are evaluated only within the BEVFormer framework on nuScenes, leaving broader 3D task validation (e.g., occupancy prediction, depth estimation) to future work.
