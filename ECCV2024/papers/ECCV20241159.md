## Problem

Correspondence-based point cloud registration struggles in low-overlap scenarios because local geometric similarities (e.g., repeated right angles) cause both inter-class mismatches (matching keypoints from different semantic categories) and intra-class mismatches (matching keypoints from the same category but different scene contexts). Existing semantic-assisted methods only filter by label identity, which is brittle to segmentation noise and cannot address intra-class mismatches at all.

## Input / Output

The framework takes two point clouds with per-point semantic labels (generated by an off-the-shelf model like PTv2) and randomly sampled keypoints from each cloud. It outputs a set of correspondences with a high inlier ratio, which are then fed into any rigid transformation estimator (RANSAC, TEASER, SC2PCR, MAC, PointDSC) to produce the final 4×4 transformation matrix. The method is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing local descriptor (FPFH, GEDI, FCGF) and matcher (NN, OT, MNN) without requiring retraining.

## Method

ML-SemReg is a training-free, two-stage matching framework that enforces multi-level semantic consistency. The Group Matching module first partitions keypoints into per-category matching groups using Local Semantic Consistency (LS-Consistency): two keypoints are eligible to match only if their local semantic signatures (the set of labels within a fixed search radius) share at least one common label, thereby eliminating inter-class mismatches while tolerating segmentation noise. The Mask Matching module then constructs a Binary Multi-Ring Semantic Signature (BMR-SS) for each keypoint by dividing the surrounding scene into N concentric rings and recording which semantic instance centroids (from Euclidean clustering) fall in each ring, yielding a rotation-invariant |S|×N binary vector. Scene semantic similarity between two keypoints is the element-wise product of their BMR-SS vectors; a top-K row-wise thresholding produces a binary scene consistency mask that reweights the local-descriptor match score matrix, suppressing intra-class mismatches. The final correspondence set is the union of masked matches across all groups, combining both local and scene-level semantic constraints.

## Results

On the hard KITTI dataset (30 m translation, lowest overlap), ML-SemReg boosts Registration Recall (RR) of MAC from 1.43% to 56.33% with FPFH (+54.9 pp) and from 40.53% to 74.65% with GEDI (+34.1 pp). With the GEDI descriptor, SC2PCR+Ours achieves the best hard-case RR of 82.01% (vs. 55.86% for SC2PCR alone), and PointDSC+Ours reaches 82.01% RR. On the medium KITTI dataset, SC2PCR+Ours with FPFH attains 91.01% RR, surpassing SC2PCR's easy-dataset RR of 90.69%, demonstrating strong low-overlap robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ML-SemReg is a plug-and-play module that improves every baseline estimator it is paired with, with the largest gains on the hardest (lowest-overlap) cases. RANSAC (1M and 4M iterations) benefits most dramatically: on the hard KITTI dataset with FPFH, RR jumps from 0.00%/0.20% to 6.93%/11.10%, because the higher inlier ratio reduces the exponential iteration count RANSAC needs. TEASER+ (TeaserPlus) improves from 2.85% to 55.99% RR (FPFH, hard) and from 51.71% to 78.85% (GEDI, hard). MAC shows the paper's headline improvement: +54.9 pp (FPFH, hard) and +34.1 pp (GEDI, hard). SC2PCR gains +59.3 pp with FPFH on the hard set (8.56%→67.82%) and +25.3 pp with GEDI (55.86%→81.20%). PointDSC, already the strongest baseline with GEDI, still improves from 50.50% to 82.01% RR on the hard set, achieving the best absolute registration recall in the paper. Across all five baselines and both descriptors, ML-SemReg never degrades performance, confirming its plug-and-play nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior semantic-assisted matching methods (SARNet, Segregator, entity-level approaches) that simply restrict matching to same-label keypoints, by introducing a two-level semantic consistency framework: Local Semantic Consistency (via Group Matching) to handle inter-class mismatches robustly to segmentation noise, and Scene Semantic Consistency (via Mask Matching with the novel BMR-SS descriptor) to handle intra-class mismatches that prior methods ignore entirely. Rather than proposing a new end-to-end network, ML-SemReg is positioned as a plug-and-play module that wraps around any existing descriptor (FPFH, GEDI), matcher (NN, OT, MNN), and estimator (RANSAC, TEASER, MAC, SC2PCR, PointDSC), repurposing the idea of semantic information through a consistency-based lens rather than a hard-filtering one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can immediately boost any existing PCR pipeline without retraining, and the ~34-point RR gain on hard KITTI cases (e.g., MAC from ~22% to ~56%) addresses a critical failure mode in low-overlap registration that limits real-world SLAM and autonomous driving. The finding that handcrafted FPFH + ML-SemReg matches or exceeds learned GEDI in medium-overlap settings suggests semantic consistency may be a more underexploited axis than descriptor quality. Open questions include whether the BMR-SS construction (which requires Euclidean clustering of semantic instances) scales to very large or unstructured scenes, how the method performs in non-rigid or dynamic scenarios, and whether the approach can be integrated into learned end-to-end registration networks rather than remaining a post-hoc filtering stage.
