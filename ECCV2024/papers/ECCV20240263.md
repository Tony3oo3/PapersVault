## Problem

Existing NeRF-based talking head methods either require per-identity training on long video sequences or rely on intermediate representations (3DMM coefficients, expression codes) that introduce information loss. Directly driving a face with raw audio in a single-shot setting is non-trivial because audio correlates strongly only with the lower face, making it difficult to model global facial motion while maintaining lip-area temporal consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240263/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single face-cropped, aligned source image at 256×256 resolution and a driven audio sequence (16 kHz, features extracted via Wav2Vec2.0) as input, with the target head pose available at both training and inference time. It produces a high-fidelity talking head video with free-view capability, where the inner face is rendered by the NeRF and the outer face/background is refined by a super-resolution module in a coarse-to-fine pipeline.

## Method

S³D-NeRF constructs a multi-scale tri-plane representation from a Hierarchical Facial Appearance Encoder (feature pyramid network) that captures identity-specific appearance from a single image. A Cross-modal Facial Deformation Field aggregates multi-scale visual features via slot attention, then computes audio-visual correlation scores through multi-head cross-attention (visual embedding as query, audio features as key/value); these region-aware scores serve as a prior for a U-Net-like deformation predictor that warps 3D points before tri-plane feature retrieval. A frozen lip-sync discriminator, trained separately on HDTF+LRS2 with contrastive triplet loss, penalizes out-of-sync audio-visual pairs during generation. Training is end-to-end on HDTF (~2 days on 4 A100 GPUs) with pixel, perceptual, StyleGAN-adversarial, lip-sync, and deformation-regularization losses applied to both coarse and fine outputs.

## Results

On HDTF, S³D-NeRF achieves the best SSIM (0.819), LPIPS (0.258), F-LMD (2.799), M-LMD (2.929), and CPBD (0.263) among all single-shot methods, while Wav2Lip retains a higher SyncNet score (8.365 vs. 6.514). On the unseen-identity MEAD dataset, S³D-NeRF again leads on LPIPS (0.161), F-LMD (2.014), M-LMD (2.921), CPBD (0.152), and Sync (6.768), with SSIM (0.845) slightly below StyleTalk (0.850). Against identity-specific NeRF methods, S³D-NeRF achieves the best M-LMD (1.493), CPBD (0.244), and Sync (7.118), while requiring <0.01 h fit time versus 4.5–42 h for baselines, and renders at 9.5 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-shot methods, S³D-NeRF dominates on image quality and structural fidelity: it outperforms MakeItTalk and PC-AVS on every metric by large margins (e.g., SSIM 0.819 vs. 0.559/0.582 on HDTF), and beats SadTalker on all six metrics. Compared to StyleTalk, S³D-NeRF wins on SSIM (+0.043), LPIPS (−0.031), F-LMD (−0.824), M-LMD (−0.344), and CPBD (+0.029) on HDTF, but trails on SyncNet (6.514 vs. 6.188 is actually a win; the paper notes Wav2Lip's Sync of 8.365 exceeds even ground truth due to its simpler architecture and larger batch). Wav2Lip remains the only method with a higher Sync score (8.365 vs. 6.514 on HDTF), at the cost of noticeably blurrier faces (SSIM 0.749, CPBD 0.195). Against identity-specific NeRF baselines (AD-NeRF, ER-NeRF, GeneFace), S³D-NeRF achieves the best M-LMD (1.493 vs. 1.659–2.436), CPBD (0.244 vs. 0.207–0.239), and Sync (7.118 vs. 5.366–6.781), while ER-NeRF retains advantages in SSIM (0.884 vs. 0.852), LPIPS (0.068 vs. 0.104), F-LMD (1.158 vs. 2.019), and rendering speed (29 vs. 9.5 FPS). The critical practical advantage is fit time: S³D-NeRF requires <0.01 h for a new identity versus 4.5 h (ER-NeRF), 36 h (AD-NeRF), and 42 h (GeneFace), making it the only method truly zero-shot for unseen speakers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NeRF-based talking head synthesis line (AD-NeRF, ER-NeRF, GeneFace) from identity-specific per-person training to a single-shot setting that generalizes to arbitrary unseen speakers, while simultaneously moving away from intermediate representations (3DMM coefficients, depth maps, PNCC) toward direct audio-to-deformation mapping. The 3D representation builds on multi-scale tri-planes from EG3D/Instant-NGP, and the cross-modal deformation field introduces slot-attention-based feature aggregation combined with multi-head cross-attention between audio and visual features—a combination not previously applied to speech-driven face animation. The lip-sync discriminator extends the Wav2Lip formulation with a larger mixed-dataset training protocol and contrastive triplet loss for more distinctive audio-visual embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for per-identity retraining (Fit Time <0.01h vs. 4–42h for prior NeRF methods), this work makes high-fidelity 3D-consistent talking head synthesis practically deployable for arbitrary speakers from a single image, which is critical for digital humans, virtual avatars, and content creation at scale. The cross-modal deformation field's region-aware motion prediction (lower face strongly coupled to audio, upper face driven by global structure) offers a principled alternative to global driving signals. Open questions include handling large head pose changes (where contours blur), background variation, and whether the coarse-to-fine super-resolution strategy can be replaced by a fully end-to-end high-resolution NeRF without the segmentation mask dependency.
