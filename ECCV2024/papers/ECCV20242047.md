## Problem

Single-stage text-to-image diffusion models perform every denoising step at high resolution, making inference expensive, and struggle to refine fine image details. Existing remedies like diffusion distillation degrade generation quality unless a GAN loss is introduced, which complicates and destabilizes training. The paper asks whether a cascaded relay formulation can simultaneously reduce cost and improve detail without these trade-offs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242047/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a short user text prompt, which is first expanded into a detailed description by a language model, then encoded by a frozen T5-XXL encoder (225-token window). The base diffusion stage produces a 512×512 latent image, and one or more relay super-resolution stages upscale it to 1024×1024 or 2048×2048 in the 8×-compressed VAE latent space. The distilled variant completes the full pipeline in as few as 4+1 sampling steps (≈1.47 s on a batch of 4).

## Method

CogView3 is a 2-stage relay diffusion system built on a 3B-parameter UNet. The key mechanism is latent relay super-resolution: instead of conditioning every SR step on the low-resolution image (as in cascaded diffusion), the SR stage starts from the base-stage latent corrupted by Gaussian noise at an intermediate timestep T_r, using a simple linear blurring schedule z_0^t = ((T_r−t)/T_r)·z_0 + (t/T_r)·z^L. This lets the SR stage denoise and deblur jointly, rectifying artifacts from the base stage without augmentation tricks. The backward sampler is a DDIM-style ODE that interleaves denoising with the linear blurring transition. For distillation, the blurring schedule is merged into progressive distillation so the student matches two teacher relay steps in one, yielding a 4+1-step model that retains most of the full 50+10-step quality.

## Results

On DrawBench, CogView3 (50+10 steps, 10.33s) achieves Aes 5.97, HPS v2 0.290, and ImageReward 0.847, outperforming SDXL (5.54, 0.288, 0.676) and Stable Cascade (5.88, 0.285, 0.677) while using roughly half the inference time of SDXL. On PartiPrompts, CogView3 reaches Aes 6.15, HPS v2 0.290, and ImageReward 1.025. The distilled variant (4+1 steps, 1.47s) retains most of this performance with Aes 5.87 and ImageReward 0.731 on DrawBench, compared to LCM-SDXL's 5.45 and 0.394. In human evaluation on DrawBench, CogView3 achieves a 77.0% win rate over SDXL on prompt alignment and 78.1% on aesthetic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL, CogView3 wins on every metric across both DrawBench and PartiPrompts (e.g., ImageReward 0.847 vs 0.676 on DrawBench; Aes 6.15 vs 5.78 on PartiPrompts) while requiring only ~10.33s vs 19.67s inference time. Against Stable Cascade, CogView3 leads on Aes, HPS v2, and ImageReward on DrawBench, and on Aes and HPS v2 on PartiPrompts, but trails slightly on PartiPrompts ImageReward (1.025 vs 1.029). The distilled CogView3 (4+1 steps, 1.47s) substantially outperforms LCM-SDXL (4 steps, 2.06s) on all metrics — for instance, ImageReward 0.731 vs 0.394 on DrawBench and 0.968 vs 0.689 on PartiPrompts — while also being faster. In human evaluation, CogView3 achieves 77.0% and 78.1% win rates over SDXL on prompt alignment and aesthetic quality respectively, and 74.8% and 82.1% over Stable Cascade with expanded prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogView3 is the first application of relay diffusion (Teng et al., 2023) to text-to-image generation, adapting the pixel-space relay framework to the latent space with a simplified linear blurring schedule in place of the original patch-wise blurring. It departs from standard cascaded diffusion (Ho et al., 2022; Ramesh et al., 2022; Saharia et al., 2022), which conditions every super-resolution step on the low-resolution output, by instead starting the super-resolution diffusion from a noised version of the base-stage generation, allowing artifact correction. The system builds on the latent diffusion paradigm of SDXL (Podell et al., 2023) and incorporates DALL-E-3's (Betker et al., 2023) recaptioning strategy, while its distillation pipeline extends progressive distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022; Meng et al., 2023) to the relay setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The relay framework's ability to make diffusion distillation substantially easier—achieving comparable quality at 1/10 of SDXL's inference time versus the significant quality degradation seen with LCM-SDXL—offers a practical path to fast, high-quality text-to-image deployment. The iterative super-resolution design naturally extends to 2048×2048 and potentially 4096×4096 (via tiled inference), addressing a key limitation of single-stage models. Open questions include whether the linear latent-space blurring is optimal compared to more complex schedules, how the approach generalizes to other modalities or conditional generation tasks, and whether the 3B parameter scale is necessary or if smaller relay models can match the quality.
