## Problem

Existing certified training methods focus on single-input adversarial perturbations, which is an overly conservative threat model for practical scenarios where universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs) — a single perturbation affecting many inputs — are more realistic. These methods achieve suboptimal clean accuracy due to excessive regularization and provide no certified guarantees against UAP attacks, limiting their applicability in safety-critical deployments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241737/cropped_page7_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard image classification inputs (MNIST 28×28 grayscale, CIFAR-10 32×32 RGB, TinyImageNet 64×64 RGB) and produces class labels. Unusually, the training process consumes inputs in batches and computes cross-input adversarial perturbations: for each input x_i, the loss is evaluated using adversarial perturbations v_j derived from *other* inputs x_j in the same batch. The certified UAP accuracy is measured by running the RACoon verifier on sets of 5 test images simultaneously, reporting the average worst-case accuracy under a shared perturbation.

## Method

CITRUS introduces a theoretical framework of k-common perturbations (k-cps) that upper-bounds the UAP robustness objective by maximizing loss over perturbations adversarial to at least k inputs, yielding a decreasing sequence of upper bounds. The practical algorithm uses the 2-cp loss, which is further upper-bounded by maximizing over the 1-cp (single-input adversarial) perturbation sets of *other* inputs in the batch, excluding the same-input adversarial set to reduce over-regularization. Concretely, for each input x_i, the method computes small l∞ boxes of radius τ centered at x_i + v_j (where v_j is a precomputed adversarial perturbation for a different input x_j), propagates these cross-input boxes through the network via IBP/Box propagation, and sums the resulting IBP losses. This design captures only the overlapping adversarial regions between inputs rather than the full single-input adversarial region, inducing significantly less regularization than SABR or standard IBP training while still providing certified UAP robustness guarantees.

## Results

CITRUS achieves the best standard accuracy across all datasets and perturbation magnitudes, with gains of up to 10.3% over prior certified training methods (63.12% vs. 52.82% for TAPS on CIFAR-10 at ε=8/255). On certified average UAP accuracy, CITRUS obtains SOTA performance in 3 out of 5 settings, including 26.27% on TinyImageNet (vs. 24.71% for TAPS) and 95.61% on MNIST at ε=0.3 (vs. 95.37% for SABR). Against universal adversarial training methods (UAT, CW-UAT), CITRUS achieves 39.88% certified UAP accuracy on CIFAR-10 ε=8/255 compared to at most 1.51% for CW-UAT, though at the cost of lower standard accuracy (63.12% vs. 94.91%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three single-input certified training baselines (IBP, SABR, TAPS), CITRUS consistently wins on standard accuracy across all five dataset/ε settings, with the largest margin being +10.3% on CIFAR-10 ε=8/255 (63.12% vs. 52.82% for TAPS). On certified UAP accuracy, CITRUS wins in 3 of 5 settings (MNIST ε=0.3, CIFAR-10 ε=2/255, TinyImageNet ε=1/255) but trails SABR by 1.69% and TAPS by 1.02% on CIFAR-10 ε=8/255, and trails TAPS by 0.24% on MNIST ε=0.1. Against universal adversarial training methods (PGD, UAT, CW-UAT), CITRUS dramatically outperforms on certified UAP accuracy (39.88% vs. 0.0–1.51%) but sacrifices standard accuracy (63.12% vs. 87.25–94.91%), confirming the fundamental trade-off between empirical and certified robustness. The ablation in Table 3 shows that adding same-input adversarial boxes to CITRUS (CITRUS+SI) drops standard accuracy to 50.24% while barely improving UCert to 40.06%, validating the cross-input design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

CITRUS extends the certified training lineage (IBP, SABR, TAPS) from single-input adversarial robustness to the universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) threat model. It repurposes SABR's small-box approximation strategy but applies it to cross-input adversarial perturbations rather than same-input ones, grounded in a new theoretical framework of k-common perturbations (Theorem 1) that upper-bounds the UAP loss. On the empirical UAP training side (UAT, CW-UAT), CITRUS departs by providing formal certification guarantees via interval bound propagation, whereas those methods offer only empirical robustness with near-zero certified UAP accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CITRUS fills a clear gap: prior certified training methods target the single-input threat model (overly conservative for many practical scenarios), while prior UAP training methods lack formal guarantees. The 35.62% vs. 1.51% certified UAP accuracy gap over CW-UAT demonstrates that certification and UAP robustness are not mutually exclusive. Key limitations include the reliance on the weaker UAP threat model (which may give false security if single-input attacks are feasible), the use of 2-cp as a fixed approximation (tighter bounds for k>2 remain unexplored), and the quadratic scaling of training time with batch size. Open questions include whether the cross-input box strategy generalizes to larger datasets (ImageNet), deeper architectures, or other norm balls.
