## Problem

Existing video quality assessment methods trained on small-annotator MOS scores show very poor correlation with actual short-video engagement, and no large-scale dataset exists for predicting engagement of UGC short videos in a cold-start setting. The paper tackles this by introducing a 90,000-video dataset with real user interaction metrics and demonstrating that multi-modal content features can predict engagement purely from video content without user or creator context.

## Input / Output

The network consumes a short video (10–60 s) split into clips of 16 frames, from which per-frame semantic (EfficientNet) and distortion features, per-clip action (ResNet-3D) and aesthetic (DOVER) features, and mPLUG-2 captioning mid-layer features are extracted. Textual inputs—YAMNet top-5 sound labels, creator title/description, and generated captions—are encoded with T5 and shared across all clips. The model outputs two regression values: NAWP (a duration-normalized watch percentage in [0,1] derived by linearly fitting the top-3% AWT boundary) and ECR (the probability of watch time exceeding 5 s, computed only from frames in the first 5 seconds).

## Method

Following MD-VQA's clip-based design, the architecture processes each clip through learnable MLPs, then uses a cross-modal attention mechanism to fuse per-clip visual action features with the shared text embeddings. Eight MLP layers produce per-clip fused representations, which are combined across clips by an 8-layer self-attention module. Two separate MLP heads then predict NAWP (averaged over all clips) and ECR (averaged over only the first 5 s of clips), trained jointly with an L2 loss. The key design insight is that NAWP removes duration bias via a linear normalization of AWT, while ECR captures initial-hook engagement; their strong empirical correlation (0.926) makes joint training mutually beneficial, improving both metrics over separate training. Incremental ablation shows that adding background sound, title/description, captioning mid-layer features, and aesthetic scores progressively lifts SRCC from 0.625 (VQA features alone) to 0.696.

## Results

On the proposed SnapUGC dataset, the full multimodal method achieves SRCC of 0.696 for NAWP and 0.675 for ECR, outperforming the strongest baseline DOVER (0.635 / 0.619) by 0.061 and 0.056 respectively. The method also attains the lowest RMSE (0.172 for NAWP, 0.174 for ECR) and the best RMSE on the top-10% most popular videos (0.181 for NAWP, 0.152 for ECR), indicating superior accuracy precisely where recommendation systems care most. Using only VQA features (Ours-VQA) yields 0.625 NAWP SRCC, confirming that the multimodal features (sound, text, captions, aesthetics) contribute an additional ~0.07 SRCC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five VQA baselines (VSFA, PVQ, MD-VQA, FastVQA, DOVER) retrained on the SnapUGC engagement dataset. The full multimodal method (Ours) wins on every metric for both NAWP and ECR, with the largest margins over DOVER (the strongest baseline): +0.061 SRCC on NAWP (0.696 vs 0.635) and +0.056 SRCC on ECR (0.675 vs 0.619). DOVER leads the baselines on SRCC but has notably worse RMSE (0.206 vs 0.172 for NAWP), suggesting its predictions are less calibrated in absolute terms. FastVQA is the weakest baseline across all metrics (SRCC 0.587/0.581, RMSE 0.218/0.223), likely due to its frame-sampling strategy. The ablation 'Ours-VQA' (same architecture, VQA features only) scores 0.625 NAWP SRCC, showing that the multimodal features (background sound, title/description, video captions, aesthetic scores) collectively add ~0.07 SRCC over visual-only features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQA feature-extraction pipeline (per-frame semantic features from EfficientNet, distortion features from UVQ, action features from ResNet-3D) but deliberately departs from the VQA objective of predicting human-annotated MOS scores, showing empirically that those scores have near-zero correlation with real engagement. It extends the engagement-prediction line of work (Wu et al. for YouTube, video-lecture datasets) to short-form UGC by introducing duration-normalized metrics (NAWP, ECR) and a 90K-video dataset grounded in thousands of real user interactions rather than ≤40 annotators. Methodologically, it moves beyond the visual-only paradigm of prior VQA by fusing audio classification (YAMNet), text (T5), and video captioning (mPLUG-2) through cross-modal attention, positioning itself at the intersection of VQA and multimodal video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that state-of-the-art VQA models (UVQ, DOVER) fail to predict short-video engagement challenges a common assumption in the field that perceived quality is a reasonable proxy for viewer retention, and motivates treating engagement prediction as a distinct task. The SnapUGC dataset and the NAWP/ECR metrics provide a practical, duration-independent benchmark that the community can adopt for cold-start recommendation research. Open questions include whether the linear NAWP normalization generalizes beyond 60-second videos and other platforms, whether the 5-second ECR threshold is optimal, and whether the multi-modal gains (primarily from mPLUG-2 intermediate features) transfer to other short-video ecosystems. The paper is limited to a single platform (Snapchat Spotlight) and does not explore how recommendation-system feedback loops interact with the predicted engagement.
