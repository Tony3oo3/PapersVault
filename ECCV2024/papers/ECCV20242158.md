## Problem

Existing continuous category discovery methods assume offline continual learning over static data streams, which fails to capture the dynamic nature of real-world data where samples are created and deleted in real time. The paper identifies that prior methods (e.g., CGCD, FRoST) suffer severe performance degradation when forced into an online, batch-wise setting due to catastrophic forgetting and inability to cluster novel data without access to the full training set. This motivates the introduction of OCGCD, a new online scenario, and a method (DEAN) specifically designed to handle its challenges.

## Input / Output

The method first consumes a labeled image batch (base session, trained offline with cross-entropy) to initialize a frozen ViT-B-16/DINO backbone, then processes unlabeled image batches of size 64 sequentially in the incremental session, erasing each batch after training (no replay). For each online batch, the model outputs pseudo-labels for all samples, discovers the number of novel clusters via affinity propagation on augmented features, and extends the classifier head accordingly. The final output is a class prediction over both known and discovered novel categories, evaluated via Hungarian-alignment clustering accuracy.

## Method

DEAN employs a two-stage energy-guided discovery: the frozen offline model's logits are converted to energy scores and clustered with a 2-component GMM to separate known from unknown samples, then the online model's energy scores further split unknowns into seen (previously discovered) and unseen (truly novel) categories. For unseen data, Variance-based Feature Augmentation (VFA) samples K additional feature vectors from a Gaussian centered at each original feature with the batch's standard deviation, temporally expanding the feature set to improve affinity-propagation clustering and pseudo-label quality. An energy-based contrastive loss (L_ec) is applied only to novel data, amplifying the energy ratio between the novel and known classifier heads to enforce discriminative learning. LoRA adapters are attached to the last 5 ViT layers for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, preserving pre-trained weights and mitigating catastrophic forgetting while the total incremental loss combines cross-entropy on pseudo-labels with L_ec.

## Results

On CUB200, DEAN achieves 59.56% overall clustering accuracy (M_all), 66.33% on known classes (M_old), and 33.11% on novel classes (M_new), while maintaining a low forgetting metric of 16.26. On FGVC-Aircraft, DEAN reaches 46.44% M_all with 17.99% M_new and 20.97 forgetting. On CIFAR-100, DEAN attains 62.34% M_all, 70.34% M_old, and 30.35% M_new with 19.17 forgetting, substantially outperforming both baselines across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEAN decisively outperforms both baselines across all three datasets and all four metrics. On CIFAR-100, the gap is most dramatic: DEAN surpasses CGCD by 59.7 percentage points in M_all (62.34 vs. 2.64), 67.58 in M_old (70.34 vs. 2.76), 28.2 in M_new (30.35 vs. 2.15), and reduces forgetting by 67.57 points (19.17 vs. 86.74). On CUB200, DEAN improves M_all by 19.16 points over CGCD (59.56 vs. 40.40) and 35.58 over FRoST (59.56 vs. 23.98), while cutting the forgetting metric from 35.61 to 16.26. On FGVC-Aircraft, DEAN achieves 46.44% M_all versus 14.08% (CGCD) and 14.44% (FRoST), with M_new of 17.99% compared to 7.75% and 9.60% respectively. Both baselines suffer severe degradation in the online setting, with CGCD and FRoST dropping to near-chance accuracy on CIFAR-100, confirming that offline continual learning methods cannot handle the dynamic, batch-wise nature of OCGCD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) line of work (CGCD, FRoST, MetaGCD) from offline continual learning to a genuinely online, batch-wise setting where data is created and deleted in real time. Conceptually, it repurposes the energy score mechanism from OOD detection (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2020) as the core discovery signal for novel categories, replacing the additional split network that CGCD requires. For pseudo-labeling, it introduces Variance-based Feature Augmentation (VFA) as a lightweight alternative to the extra modules used by prior continuous GCD methods, and employs LoRA-based parameter-efficient tuning to combat catastrophic forgetting in the online regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing OCGCD, the paper highlights a significant and previously unquantified gap: existing continuous GCD methods degrade catastrophically when forced into an online, batch-wise protocol (e.g., CGCD drops to 40.4% M_all on CUB200 vs. 72.5% supervised upper bound). The energy-guided discovery and VFA offer practical, end-to-end trainable components that avoid the extra parameters and learning stages of prior split networks. However, the evaluation is limited to two baselines that were not designed for online learning, and the authors acknowledge that DEAN cannot guarantee stable inference performance mid-training, leaving open the question of anytime-inference guarantees in online category discovery.
