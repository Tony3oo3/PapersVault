## Problem

Existing multimodal adversarial attacks on VLP models, particularly SGA, enhance transferability by augmenting diversity only around online adversarial examples during optimization, neglecting diversity toward the clean input. This over-reliance on local adversarial examples risks overfitting the surrogate model, resulting in substantially lower attack success rates when transferring to other VLP models. The paper asks how to judiciously broaden adversarial diversity across the full optimization trajectory to improve cross-model and cross-task transferability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean image-text pair (e.g., from Flickr30K, MSCOCO, or RefCOCO+) and produces a paired adversarial image and adversarial text that induce a modality mismatch. Image perturbations are bounded at ξ_v = 8/255 via PGD (T=10 steps, α=2/255), while text perturbations use BERT-Attack with a word-level budget of ξ_t = 1 and a candidate word list of W=10. At each iteration, N=5 samples are drawn from the intersection region, and the final adversarial text is generated by jointly deviating from three reference images with weights λ=0.6, μ=0.2, ν=0.2.

## Method

The core mechanism is diversification along the intersection region of adversarial trajectory: at iteration i, a triangle is formed by the clean image v, the previous adversarial image v'_{i-1}, and the current adversarial image v'_i, and N convex-combination samples are drawn within it. For each sample, a normalized gradient perturbation is computed, and a text-guided selection picks the perturbation that maximally increases the image-text mismatch loss J, leveraging cross-modal interaction to choose the most effective direction. The selected sample is then resized (following SGA's augmentation) and used to compute the final perturbation for the next adversarial image. For the text modality, the adversarial text is generated to simultaneously deviate from the last intersection region (a weighted combination of v, v'_{T-1}, and v'_T) rather than solely from the final adversarial image v'_T, reducing surrogate-model overfitting. This dual diversification—spatial sampling in the image trajectory and multi-reference deviation in the text—consistently outperforms SGA by 2.7–11.6% in cross-model ASR across ALBEF, TCL, CLIP_ViT, and CLIP_CNN.

## Results

On the Flickr30K image-text retrieval task, the proposed method achieves ~99.9% white-box ASR (R@1) on the source model, matching SGA. For black-box transferability, the method consistently outperforms SGA: when transferring from ALBEF to CLIP_CNN, IR R@1 improves from 49.78% to 59.01% (+9.23%), and TR R@1 from 39.59% to 49.55% (+9.96%). Cross-architecture transfers (e.g., CLIP_ViT → CLIP_CNN) see the largest gains, with IR R@1 rising from 61.1% to 69.5% (+8.4%). Same-architecture transfers (ALBEF → TCL) show smaller but consistent improvements of 2.7–3.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PGD (image-only) and BERT-Attack (text-only), the proposed method achieves dramatically higher cross-model ASR—e.g., 59.01% vs. 19.11% IR R@1 on CLIP_CNN from ALBEF—confirming that joint multimodal perturbation is essential. Compared to Sep-Attack and Co-Attack (which perturb modalities separately or with limited cross-modal guidance), the method gains 10–20+ percentage points on cross-architecture transfers, demonstrating the benefit of set-level diversification. The most direct comparison is against SGA, the paper's baseline: the proposed method wins on every black-box transfer setting, with improvements of 2.7–3.7% for same-architecture pairs (ALBEF↔TCL) and 7.7–11.6% for cross-architecture pairs (e.g., ALBEF→CLIP_ViT, CLIP_ViT→CLIP_CNN). On white-box attacks, both methods saturate at ~99.9% R@1, leaving no meaningful gap. The ablation (Fig. 4) further shows that the intersection-region diversification contributes most for same-architecture targets, while the text-deviation-from-interaction-region component is more critical for cross-architecture transfers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SGA (Lu et al., 2023), which introduced set-level augmentation around online adversarial examples to improve transferability of multimodal attacks on VLP models. The key conceptual departure is shifting the diversity sampling from the neighborhood of the current adversarial image to the geometric intersection region (triangle) formed by the clean input, the previous-step adversarial image, and the current adversarial image, thereby incorporating diversity toward the clean input rather than solely around AEs. In the text modality, the paper extends SGA's strategy of deviating adversarial text from only the final adversarial image to simultaneously deviating from the entire last intersection region, reducing surrogate-model overfitting. The broader lineage traces back to Zhang et al. (2022) for white-box VLP attacks and to data-augmentation-based transferability methods in image-only adversarial examples (DIM, TIM, SIM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical overfitting risk in black-box VLP attacks: by anchoring diversity to the clean input as well as the optimization trajectory, the method reduces dependence on surrogate-model-specific features, yielding consistent 3–12% ASR gains over SGA across four VLP models and three downstream tasks. However, absolute cross-architecture transferability remains modest (e.g., ~50–60% ASR from fused to aligned models), and the approach is evaluated only on four relatively older VLP architectures (ALBEF, TCL, CLIP-ViT, CLIP-CNN), leaving open whether the intersection-region strategy generalizes to newer, larger VLP models such as BLIP-2 or LLaVA. The cross-task transferability demonstration (ITR → VG, IC) is useful but does not fundamentally alter the threat model. Open questions include whether the geometric sampling can be adapted to continuous (non-PGD) optimization or to multi-step trajectory regions beyond a single triangle.
