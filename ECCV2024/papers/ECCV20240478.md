## Problem

3D dense captioning has seen significant progress in indoor scenes, but outdoor scenes present fundamental domain gaps—dynamic object states, spatially varying LiDAR sparsity, fixed 6-camera rigs with high self-occlusion, and much larger scene areas—that render existing indoor architectures sub-optimal. Compounding this, no large-scale dataset with aligned box-caption annotations exists for outdoor 3D dense captioning, leaving the task largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-frame LiDAR point cloud (N_p × 3) and 6 synchronized RGB images (H_c × W_c × 3) from a panoramic camera rig as input, and produces a set of K 3D bounding boxes each paired with a natural language caption. Captions are structured to cover four dimensions—appearance, motion, environment, and relationship—following a compositional template for spatial relations (target-object, spatial-relation, anchor-object). The introduced TOD3Cap dataset provides 2.3M such box-caption pairs across 850 nuScenes scenes (34.1K frames), covering over 500 object types with a ~2k-word vocabulary.

## Method

TOD3Cap is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a BEV-based detector lifts multi-view image features via BEVFormer-style spatial cross-attention and temporal self-attention, fuses them with voxelized LiDAR features through BEVFusion, and generates 3D object proposals with a DETR-style query head; (2) a Relation Q-Former encodes proposals via MLP, concatenates them with BEV features, and applies self-attention layers so each object query absorbs context from surrounding objects and the scene; (3) a frozen LLaMA-7B generates captions, with an LLaMA-Adapter projecting the context-aware object queries into the LLM's hidden space to bridge the modality gap without retraining the language model. Training follows a three-stage curriculum—pre-train the detector on nuScenes detection, freeze it and train the captioner, then jointly fine-tune at a reduced learning rate—and uses a 3D Hungarian assigner to filter matched proposals before sampling a subset for caption loss computation, mitigating the memory burden of generating hundreds of sentences per frame.

## Results

On the TOD3Cap dataset (val split, 150 scenes), the proposed TOD3Cap network with 2D+3D input achieves 108.0 CIDEr@0.5IoU, 50.2 BLEU-4@0.5IoU, 48.9 METEOR@0.5IoU, and 69.2 ROUGE@0.5IoU. With LiDAR-only (3D) input, it reaches 74.4 CIDEr@0.5IoU; with camera-only (2D) input, 94.1 CIDEr@0.5IoU. The headline improvement over the strongest baseline (Vote2Cap-DETR) is +9.6 CIDEr@0.5IoU and +10.2 CIDEr@0.25IoU under the 2D+3D setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOD3Cap outperforms all four baselines across every metric and input modality. Against the strongest baseline Vote2Cap-DETR (adapted with the same BEV detector), TOD3Cap gains +10.2 CIDEr@0.25 and +9.6 CIDEr@0.5 under 2D+3D input, and +12.5 / +11.8 under 3D-only input. X-Trans2Cap, the second-strongest baseline, trails by +20.5 CIDEr@0.25 and +15.8 CIDEr@0.5 in the 2D+3D setting. Scan2Cap and SpaCap3D, both originally designed for indoor RGB-D or point-cloud scenes, show the largest gaps (CIDEr@0.5 of 62.5 and 51.2 respectively vs. 108.0 for TOD3Cap), confirming that indoor architectures are poorly suited to outdoor scenes even after detector adaptation. The consistent advantage across CIDEr, BLEU-4, METEOR, and ROUGE at both IoU thresholds indicates the gain is not limited to a single metric but reflects broadly better caption quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D dense captioning line of work (Scan2Cap, Vote2Cap-DETR, SpaCap3D, X-Trans2Cap) from indoor RGB-D settings to outdoor scenes with LiDAR point clouds and fixed multi-view cameras. Architecturally, it composes well-established BEV perception components (BEVFormer's spatiotemporal attention for camera features, BEVFusion for LiDAR-camera fusion) with a novel Relation Q-Former that replaces the graph-based or transformer-decoder relation modules used in prior indoor methods. The captioning stage leverages LLaMA-Adapter to interface frozen LLM weights with 3D object features, avoiding costly LLM retraining while preserving commonsense language generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TOD3Cap dataset (2.3M captions, 850 scenes) is the first million-scale outdoor 3D dense captioning resource, filling a critical gap that has limited progress in driving-oriented scene understanding. The paper clearly articulates why indoor methods fail outdoors (sparse LiDAR, fixed cameras, dynamics, scale), providing a useful diagnostic framework for future work. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes), acknowledged failures on small/remote objects, and reliance on GPT-4-assisted annotation that may introduce systematic biases. Open questions include whether the Relation Q-Former's context-awareness generalizes to more complex multi-agent interactions and whether the three-stage training protocol is necessary at larger scale.
