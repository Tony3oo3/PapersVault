## Problem

Existing event-based synthetic aperture imaging (E-SAI) de-occlusion methods require prior camera information (focal length, speed, depth of field) and are restricted to uniform linear motion acquisition, making them fragile to real-world lens shakes, non-uniform trajectories, and focus changes. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a refocus-free, end-to-end approach that performs automatic event registration and reconstruction without any prior knowledge of acquisition parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242151/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-timestamp event streams (30 timestamps per group) captured by a DAVIS346 event camera during synthetic aperture motion as input, with no requirement for calibrated camera intrinsics or motion parameters. It produces a single de-occlusion reconstructed image of the target scene. The V-ESAI dataset extends the standard E-SAI data by applying random affine transformations (rotation, scaling, translation) to simulate non-uniform motion, lens rotation, and focal length changes, expanding evaluation from 588 to 2352 scene groups.

## Method

REDIR is a three-stage end-to-end pipeline combining a UNet-based registration module with Spatial Transformer Networks (STN) that predict per-timestamp affine matrices (encoding rotation, scaling, and translation) to align global and local event features into a unified coordinate system, a Temporal-Spatial Attention Spiking Neural Network (TSA-SNN) filter using Leaky Integrate-and-Fire neurons to suppress discontinuous occlusion noise while retaining persistent target events, and a Perceptual Mask Connection Module (PMCM) that creates a shortcut pathway fusing registered multi-scale features into the reconstruction decoder. Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training the SNN filter/reconstruction (200 epochs), training the STN registration module (500 epochs), and fine-tuning the full network with PMCM (300 epochs), optimized with a combined perceptual, pixel, and total-variation loss. The key insight is that predicting full affine transformations per timestamp via STN at multiple UNet scales enables adaptive registration that generalizes beyond uniform linear motion without manual focusing or prior camera calibration.

## Results

On the E-SAI dataset (without prior information), REDIR achieves a total PSNR of 25.74 dB, SSIM of 0.7395, and LPIPS of 0.1139, outperforming the prior-information-free baselines Hybrid (22.32 dB PSNR) and FSAI+CNN (23.67 dB PSNR). For indoor scenes, REDIR reaches 27.28 dB PSNR and 0.7664 SSIM; for outdoor scenes, 18.53 dB PSNR and 0.6130 SSIM. The method also demonstrates comparable reconstruction quality on the newly introduced V-ESAI dataset under rotation, scaling, and non-uniform translation transformations, where existing methods fail entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among methods that do not require prior information, REDIR clearly outperforms all baselines: it surpasses Hybrid [31] by 3.42 dB in PSNR (25.74 vs. 22.32), 7.46% in SSIM (0.7395 vs. 0.6649), and reduces LPIPS by 5.39% (0.1139 vs. 0.1678) on the total metric. REDIR also beats FSAI+CNN [25] by 2.07 dB PSNR and 1.24% SSIM on the total metric. The strongest baseline overall is Refocus+Hybrid [30], which achieves 27.94 dB PSNR but requires manual focusing and prior camera parameters (focal length, speed, depth of field); REDIR narrows this gap to 2.2 dB while operating entirely without such information. FSAI+ACC [23] performs the worst (13.81 dB PSNR) and also requires prior information. On the V-ESAI dataset with variable transformations (rotation, scaling, non-uniform translation), only REDIR produces coherent reconstructions, whereas Refocus+Hybrid and Hybrid fail to handle these acquisition conditions at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the E-SAI de-occlusion reconstruction line initiated by Zhang et al. (CVPR 2021) and Yu et al. (TPAMI 2023), which rely on uniform linear camera motion and prior information (focal length, speed, manual focusing) for event alignment. REDIR departs from this by replacing fixed linear-offset registration with per-timestamp STN-predicted affine transformations, enabling adaptive alignment without any acquisition metadata. The filtering stage adapts the TSA-SNN architecture from Yao et al. for occlusion event suppression, and a novel perceptual mask-gated connection module bridges the registration and reconstruction UNet branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the uniform-linear-motion and prior-information constraints makes event-camera SAI de-occlusion practically deployable in uncontrolled environments (search-and-rescue, tracking through foliage), where precise motion control and manual focusing are infeasible. However, the V-ESAI dataset is constructed via synthetic affine augmentation of the original E-SAI data rather than real variable-motion captures, so generalization to genuinely non-uniform real-world trajectories remains unverified. Open questions include whether the STN-based registration degrades under large inter-frame displacements, whether the approach extends to 3D reconstruction, and how performance holds when occlusion density or scene complexity increases beyond the current benchmark.
