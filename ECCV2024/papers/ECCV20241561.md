## Problem

Existing multimodal sentiment analysis (MSA) models invariably capture two types of harmful dataset biases—utterance-level label bias from imbalanced category distributions and word-level context bias from spurious correlations between context words and sentiment categories—causing models to rely on statistical shortcuts rather than true causal effects. Prior debiasing efforts in MSA have either ignored label bias entirely or failed to disentangle main content from context at the word level, leaving both confounders unaddressed simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241561/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

MCIS is a parameter-free, training-free inference-time framework that wraps around any already-trained MSA model. At inference, it consumes the same multimodal inputs (BERT-based language embeddings, audio features, visual features) as the host model, but additionally requires pre-computed average feature vectors over the entire training set and a word-level content/context split (via NLTK tokenization, masking ~69% of words as main content). Two adaptive bias-elimination weights (λ̂, λ̃) are determined once per dataset via coarse-to-fine grid search on the validation set, then applied to all test samples.

## Method

MCIS formulates a structural causal graph for MSA where multimodal inputs (L,A,V) produce a fused representation M that feeds prediction Y, while a confounder node Z (encoding label and context biases) creates a backdoor path M←Z→Y. To block this path, the framework applies the do-operator via two counterfactual interventions: (1) a no-treatment scenario replacing all modalities with training-set average embeddings to isolate pure label bias, and (2) a context-only scenario masking main content words and zeroing audio/visual features to isolate pure context bias. The debiased prediction is computed as the factual output minus a weighted sum of the two counterfactual outputs, with weights tuned by grid search to maximize weighted F1 on validation. This subtraction-based bias elimination strategy is model-agnostic, requiring no architectural modifications or retraining of the host MSA model.

## Results

On MOSI, MMIM+MCIS achieves new SOTA with 47.9% Acc-7, 86.6% Acc-2, and 86.5% F1, while DMD+MCIS reaches 46.5%/86.3%/86.3%. On MOSEI, DMD+MCIS attains the best overall performance with 55.2% Acc-7, 87.3% Acc-2, and 87.1% F1, improving over its vanilla counterpart by +1.3/+1.7/+1.6. Across all five host models, MCIS yields average gains of approximately 0.94%–1.76% per metric on MOSI and ~1.63% on MOSEI, all achieved in a parameter-free, training-free manner at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCIS consistently outperforms all seven external baselines (TFN, LMF, MFM, ICCN, MAG-BERT, FDMER, Self-MM) on both MOSI and MOSEI across all three metrics. On MOSI, the best MCIS model (MMIM+MCIS, 47.9/86.6/86.5) surpasses the strongest external baseline Self-MM (45.8/84.8/84.9) by +2.1/+1.8/+1.6; on MOSEI, DMD+MCIS (55.2/87.3/87.1) beats FDMER (54.1/86.1/85.8) by +1.1/+1.2/+1.3. Against their own vanilla counterparts, MCIS improves MulT by +0.9/+1.4/+1.3 (MOSI) and +1.8/+1.6/+1.5 (MOSEI), MISA by −0.1/+1.4/+1.5 and +1.5/+1.4/+1.5, MMIM by +1.5/+1.1/+1.1 and +1.4/+1.6/+1.6, CubeMLP by +1.2/+1.2/+1.2 and +1.5/+2.0/+2.2, and DMD by +1.2/+1.2/+1.2 and +1.3/+1.7/+1.6. The only exception is MISA+MCIS on MOSI Acc-7, which drops marginally (42.0 vs. 42.1) while still gaining substantially on Acc-2 and F1. The paper notes that MCIS achieves these gains with zero additional parameters and ~26× less time overhead than training a new SOTA model from scratch, making it a cost-effective plug-in for any existing MSA architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the causal-inference debiasing paradigm from unimodal text classification (Qian et al., ACL 2021) to multimodal sentiment analysis, and is the first to jointly identify and disentangle both utterance-level label bias and word-level context bias in MSA from a causal perspective. Unlike prior MSA work that pursues performance gains through increasingly complex representation learning and fusion architectures (MulT, MISA, CubeMLP, MMIM, DMD), MCIS operates entirely at inference time on already-trained models via a parameter-free subtraction of counterfactual outcomes, drawing on backdoor adjustment and the do-operator. It also distinguishes itself from Sun et al. (ACM MM 2022), which applied counterfactual reasoning to out-of-distribution MSA, by targeting in-distribution biases and providing a unified causal graph that captures both bias types simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's model-agnostic, training-free design means practitioners can retrofit debiasing onto any existing MSA pipeline without retraining, reducing time overhead by roughly 26× compared to training a new SOTA model. However, the method depends on a validation set for grid-searching the two bias-magnitude parameters (λ̂, λ̃), and the context-word masking relies on a heuristic NLTK-based content-word extraction that may not generalize to all languages or domains. Open questions include whether the approach extends to modality-missing scenarios (acknowledged as future work), whether the linear subtraction is optimal versus learned debiasing, and whether the gains hold on datasets with less pronounced label imbalance.
