## Problem

Existing music-to-dance generation methods lack explicit beat synchronization, producing motions that drift from musical beats, and cannot map user-specified key poses to particular beat frames for practical choreography. Prior approaches that do incorporate keyframes (e.g., EDGE) fuse them with music via simple concatenation or temporal blending, which disproportionately emphasizes dense music features over sparse keyframe constraints, causing misalignment with the specified poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes three inputs: a 5-second music clip at 30 fps, a sparse set of keyframe poses specified by a binary temporal mask (1–30% of frames during training, 10% during testing), and an independent beat condition encoded as a nearest-beat-distance integer vector (each entry gives the frame distance to the closest beat, extracted via Librosa). The output is a full 3D dance motion sequence in 6-DOF rotation representation, where poses at keyframe positions match the reference and motion beats align with the designated beat frames.

## Method

Beat-It is a DDPM-based diffusion framework with a transformer denoiser that introduces three key mechanisms. First, beats are explicitly disentangled from music and represented as a nearest-beat-distance vector rather than a binary mask, providing dense local temporal context that the model can exploit for rhythmic structure. Second, a hierarchical multi-condition fusion module first injects the sparse keyframe condition into dense music and beat embeddings via a beat-aware dilated cross-attention (dilation step scales inversely with distance to the nearest beat, so keyframes near beats propagate influence more broadly), then fuses the refined dense conditions through a second-stage transformer. Third, a beat alignment loss uses a pre-trained beat-distance estimator to supervise the generated motion's own beat structure, with adaptive shrinkage weighting that amplifies penalties where alignment is imprecise and exponential decay that focuses supervision near beat frames.

## Results

On AIST++, Beat-It achieves the best generation quality with PFC of 0.966 (vs. EDGE 1.084, Bailando 1.578, FACT 2.698) and a Beat Alignment Score (BAS) of 0.661, surpassing the previous SOTA EDGE by 0.426. In terms of controllability, Beat-It reduces Key Pose Distance (KPD) to 0.306 (vs. EDGE 0.859) and achieves a Beat Assignment Precision (BAP) of 0.793, a metric only Beat-It reports. Diversity metrics (Div_k, Div_g) remain comparable to baselines, confirming that the quality gains do not come at the cost of motion variety.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Beat-It outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against EDGE (the closest competitor, also supporting keyframe input), Beat-It improves BAS from 0.235 to 0.661 (+0.426), KPD from 0.859 to 0.306 (−0.553), and PFC from 1.084 to 0.966, while being the only method to report BAP (0.793). Against Bailando, Beat-It improves BAS by 0.446 and PFC by 0.612; against FACT, the gains are even larger (BAS +0.459, PFC +1.732). In the user study, Beat-It wins 92.2% of comparisons against FACT, 78.8% against Bailando, and 60.3% on quality / 86.9% on keyframe controllability against EDGE. The paper notes that FACT and Bailando do not support keyframe or beat conditions, so KPD and BAP are not applicable to them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EDGE [42], a diffusion-based dance generation framework that already supports keyframe conditioning, and extends it by introducing an explicit, disentangled beat condition alongside the key pose condition. The hierarchical multi-condition fusion mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from multi-condition image generation (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) and from DiffKFC's masked attention for sparse keyframe control, but adapts both to the specific sparsity mismatch between sparse keyframes and dense music/beat signals in dance. The beat-aware mask dilation and the nearest-beat-distance representation are novel formulations that depart from prior work's reliance on implicit beat information embedded in music features or simple binary beat masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making beat alignment an explicit, supervised objective rather than an emergent property of music conditioning, this work addresses a fundamental gap in practical choreography tools where dancers need to assign specific poses to specific beats. The ablation showing that naive condition concatenation causes PFC to explode from ~1 to ~26 highlights that multi-condition fusion in sparse-dense settings is far more fragile than previously assumed, a finding relevant beyond dance generation. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (AIST++) with 5-second clips at 30 fps, and the method's reliance on pre-extracted musical beats via Librosa, which may not generalize to genres with complex or polyrhythmic structures. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, real-time inference, and whether the hierarchical fusion strategy transfers to other sparse-dense multi-condition generation tasks.
