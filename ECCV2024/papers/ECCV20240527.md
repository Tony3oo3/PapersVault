## Problem

LiDAR semantic segmentation models degrade significantly when deployed on unseen sensors or environments, primarily due to point cloud density variations. Existing domain generalization methods treat sensor density as a single global value (e.g., 64-beam vs. 32-beam), overlooking that a single source LiDAR point cloud inherently contains a wide spectrum of local densities depending on object distance. This paper exploits the observation that objects at different ranges across different sensors share overlapping density regions, reframing a single source domain as multiple density sub-domains.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw LiDAR point clouds (3D coordinates only, with intensity deliberately omitted to reduce sensor-specific variance) along with the LiDAR beam configuration parameters (horizontal/vertical beam counts and FOV limits from Tab. 1). Points are voxelized into 20 cm cubic voxels (default) or 5 cm voxels (LiDomAug setting). The output is per-point semantic class labels, produced jointly through a point-wise head and a voxel-wise head whose losses are combined with Lovász-Softmax and Weighted Cross-Entropy.

## Method

The proposed DDFE module has four components. First, a point-voxel feature encoder uses PointNet-style extraction with direct voxel-wise encoding of spherical coordinates (cos θ, sin θ, φ, r) via an MLP, bypassing intra-voxel noise. Second, a beam density estimation module projects the LiDAR's horizontal and vertical beam inclinations onto a 512×5120 spherical image, convolves the resulting binary vectors with four 1-D Gaussian kernels (σ ∈ {10,30,50,70}), and computes per-point density as √(B̂_h · B̂_v / r²), yielding a 4-channel density embedding. Third, density soft clipping constrains this embedding via a tanh function parameterized by the 10th and 90th channel-wise percentiles of the training distribution (computed online via reservoir sampling), preventing the model from extrapolating to unseen density extremes. Fourth, a density-aware embedding module uses the clipped density as an attention signal—two 1-D convolutions followed by sigmoid—to modulate both point-wise and voxel-wise features before feeding them into a 3D backbone (MinkNet42 or C&L). A density augmentation strategy (enhanced-Mix3D with ego-direction translations plus beam sampling) is applied at 0.5 probability during training to broaden the source density spectrum.

## Results

In the LiDomAug experimental setting (MinkNet42 backbone, 20 cm voxel), the proposed DDFE achieves 50.1% mIoU on K→N and 46.3% mIoU on N→K, outperforming the strongest baseline LiDomAug (45.9% / 48.3%) by +5.9% and −2.0% respectively. With the C&L backbone, DDFE reaches 47.1% (K→N) and 40.3% (N→K), surpassing LiDomAug (39.2% / 37.9%) by +7.9% and +2.4%. In the DGLSS setting with MinkowskiNet, the method yields average gains of +12.9% (Waymo source), +7.8% (SemanticKITTI source), and +15.8% (nuScenes source) over DGLSS on unseen target domains. The DDFE module adds only 8 ms inference overhead and 23.8k parameters (+0.06%) to the MinkNet42 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against 13 baselines spanning domain generalization, domain adaptation, and data augmentation. Against the closest DG competitor DGLSS [16], DDFE wins by an average of +12.9% (W→K, W→N), +7.8% (K→W, K→N), and +15.8% (N→W, N→K) mIoU, while also dominating source-to-source settings. LiDomAug [29], which relies on ego-motion and multi-frame aggregation, is outperformed by +5.9% (K→N) and −2.0% (N→K) with MinkNet42, and by +7.9% / +2.4% with C&L. Among augmentation-only baselines, DDFE surpasses PolarMix (45.8/39.1), Mix3D (43.1/44.7), Copy-Paste (38.5/41.1), and CutMix (37.1/37.6) on both transfer directions with MinkNet42. For domain adaptation methods under the C&L backbone, DDFE beats LiDAR-UDA (41.8/34.0) by +5.3% (K→N) and +6.3% (N→K), and substantially outperforms SWD (27.7/24.5) and 3DGCA (27.4/23.9). The method also surpasses generic DG baselines IBN-Net (62.50/42.73/49.43) and MLDG (68.16/45.98/46.52) in the DGLSS setting, as well as COSMIX variants trained on each source domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior LiDAR domain generalization work (DGLSS, LiDomAug) that treats sensor density as a single global property (e.g., 64-beam vs. 32-beam) by reframing a single source scan as containing a spectrum of local density domains across distance. Methodologically, it builds on PointNet-style feature extraction and adapts Mix3D augmentation, but its core contribution is a lightweight density-aware attention module (DDFE) that operates on analytically computed beam densities rather than learned representations. Unlike LiDomAug and C&L, which require ego-motion and multi-frame aggregation, DDFE is a single-frame plug-in that can be attached to existing sparse-convolution backbones (MinkNet42, C&L) with minimal overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a single-frame, ego-motion-free domain generalization solution that outperforms multi-frame methods requiring sequential data and motion estimation—important for real-time autonomous driving deployment. The lightweight nature (+8 ms, +23.8k parameters) makes it broadly applicable across 3D backbones. However, the work is limited to semantic segmentation, and the authors acknowledge the need to extend to 3D object detection. An open question is whether the density-space alignment generalizes to other sensor-induced domain shifts (e.g., different FOV, mounting height) beyond beam count differences.
