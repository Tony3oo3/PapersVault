## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models used to generate synthetic training data for image classification often produce out-of-distribution images that misinterpret ambiguous class names or omit fine-grained discriminative features, limiting downstream classifier performance. Prior few-shot-guided methods (IsSynth, DISEF) condition generation on noised real samples or diverse captions but do not adapt the generative model itself to the target data distribution, leaving a gap in distributional fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241900/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K few-shot real images per class (K ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}) along with a simple text prompt template ('a photo of a [CLS]') to fine-tune the diffusion model, then generates 500 synthetic 512×512 images per class with the adapted model. The synthetic images (optionally combined with the original few-shot real images) are fed into a CLIP ViT-B/16 classifier adapted via LoRA, which outputs class labels. The classifier training uses a weighted cross-entropy loss with λ=0.8 assigned to real-data terms when both sources are present.

## Method

DataDream fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v2.1 by inserting LoRA adapters (rank 16) into the Q, K, V, and O projection matrices of every attention layer in both the text encoder and the diffusion U-Net, trained for 200 epochs on the few-shot real images with the standard diffusion denoising objective. Two variants are offered: DataDream_dset trains a single shared LoRA across all classes to capture dataset-level commonalities, while DataDream_cls trains one LoRA per class for finer per-class alignment. Unlike DreamBooth, no preservation loss is used and only LoRA weights are updated, avoiding over-regularization that would impede distributional alignment. After adaptation, 500 images per class are generated using the same class-name prompt, and a CLIP ViT-B/16 classifier is fine-tuned with LoRA on the synthetic data (alone or jointly with real few-shots), yielding state-of-the-art few-shot classification on 7 of 10 benchmarks.

## Results

In the synthetic-only setting (16 real shots used to generate 500 synthetic images per class), DataDream_dset achieves 83.4% average top-1 accuracy across 10 datasets, substantially outperforming IsSynth (74.5%) and DISEF (72.1%). In the real+synthetic setting, DataDream_dset reaches 87.0% average accuracy, surpassing IsSynth (85.5%) and DISEF (85.4%). The largest gains appear on fine-grained datasets: FGVC Aircraft improves from 64.8% (IsSynth) to 72.3%, and Stanford Cars from 88.5% to 92.4%. Against the best non-generative SOTA (CoOp at 79.2%), DataDream_dset improves by 7.8% on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP zero-shot (64.1% avg), DataDream_dset improves by 22.9 points in the real+synthetic setting, with the largest single-dataset gain on FGVC Aircraft (23.8% → 72.3%). Compared to IsSynth [14], DataDream_dset wins on 7 of 10 datasets in the real+synthetic setting (e.g., +7.5 pts on Aircraft, +3.9 pts on Cars) and dominates all 10 in the synthetic-only setting (83.4% vs 74.5% avg). Against DISEF [9], the advantage is similar: 7/10 dataset wins in real+synthetic and 10/10 in synthetic-only, with DISEF's LLM-diversified prompts introducing out-of-distribution artifacts that hurt fine-grained classes. Against the PEFT baselines VPT (76.9%) and CoOp (79.2%), DataDream_dset leads by 10.1 and 7.8 points respectively, confirming that adapted synthetic data generation provides substantially more training signal than prompt-tuning alone. The only datasets where DataDream_dset does not clearly lead are Caltech 101 and Food101, where DataDream_cls or IsSynth are marginally competitive, suggesting that per-class LoRA (DataDream_cls) is preferable when classes span a wide visual range.

## Relation to Prior Work

DataDream sits at the intersection of personalized generative modeling (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) and few-shot synthetic data generation for classification (IsSynth, DISEF, FakeIt). Prior few-shot generation methods condition a frozen diffusion model on real images via partial noising or LLM-generated prompts, while personalization methods fine-tune the generator on a single object. DataDream departs from both by fine-tuning the diffusion model's attention layers via LoRA on multi-class few-shot data, explicitly positioning itself as the first to adapt the generator rather than merely condition it. For the downstream classifier, it follows the CLIP + LoRA paradigm established by DISEF, making the generator adaptation the primary methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that synthetic-only DataDream images can match or exceed real few-shot performance on fine-grained datasets (e.g., Stanford Cars: 92.2% synthetic vs. 92.4% real+synthetic) suggests that a well-adapted generator can extract more class-discriminative signal from a handful of examples than the examples themselves provide to a classifier. This reframes the value of few-shot data: it is not just training signal for the classifier but a conditioning signal for the generator. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion v2.1 to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), whether per-class LoRA training scales to thousands of classes, and whether the compute cost of fine-tuning the generator is justified relative to simply generating more images with a frozen model.
