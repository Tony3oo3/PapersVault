## Problem

In multi-task partially supervised learning (MTPSL) for dense prediction, not all task labels are available for every training image, making cross-task knowledge transfer essential. Existing approaches align tasks via global vector representations, which discard fine-grained spatial structure, while local matching is hindered by the absence of precise region supervision. This paper addresses both gaps by leveraging SAM for free region detection and modeling region features as Gaussian distributions for flexible cross-task alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 288×384 for NYU-V2 or 128×256 for Cityscapes) and simultaneously produces a semantic segmentation mask, a depth map, and a surface normal map from a shared multi-task CNN backbone (SegNet or HRNet18). During training, the image is also fed to a frozen pre-trained SAM to obtain region masks; at inference, only the MTL network is used. The framework operates under one-label or random-label settings where each image carries labels for at least one but not all K tasks.

## Method

The core contribution is a region-aware distribution contrast loss that enforces cross-task consistency at the object level. An auxiliary network maps both the labeled task's ground truth and the unlabeled task's prediction into a shared high-dimensional feature map; SAM-generated region masks then partition these maps into object-level regions. Each region's feature set is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian (mean and covariance), and the Wasserstein distance between Gaussians serves as the similarity metric in a noise-contrastive-estimation (NCE) loss that pulls same-region distributions across tasks together while pushing different-region distributions apart. The total training objective is the standard supervised loss on labeled tasks plus this region contrastive loss, and the approach generalizes across both discrete (segmentation) and continuous (depth, normals) prediction tasks without task-specific modifications.

## Results

On NYU-V2 (one-label, HRNet18), the proposed region-aware Gaussian contrast achieves 42.28% mIoU, 0.4641 aErr, and 4.86 mErr, improving over the prior SOTA DejaVu by +2.48%, +2.25%, and +5.51% respectively. In the random-label setting, gains are even larger: 46.21% mIoU, 0.4482 aErr, and 4.49 mErr (+11.75%, +7.49%, +68.69% over DejaVu). On Cityscapes (one-label, HRNet18), the method reaches 83.92% mIoU and 0.0121 aErr, a +22.73% improvement in depth over Li et al. Notably, when combined with TaskExpert (ViT-Large), the method surpasses fully supervised TaskExpert on surface normals in the random-label setting (4.78 vs. 6.47 mErr).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Li et al. [25] (the primary MTPSL baseline using vector-based cross-task alignment), the proposed method wins decisively on all three tasks: in the one-label setting, it improves mIoU from 39.42 to 42.28, aErr from 0.5071 to 0.4641, and mErr from 14.79 to 4.86; in the random-label setting, mErr drops from 14.34 to 4.49 (a 68.7% relative improvement). Against DejaVu [1] (the prior partially-supervised SOTA), the method outperforms on every task in both label settings, with the largest margin on surface normals (+5.51% one-label, +2.85% random-label). Against TaskExpert [44] (fully supervised SOTA), the paper demonstrates that adding the proposed contrastive loss to TaskExpert yields substantial gains in partially supervised scenarios — in the random-label setting, TaskExpert+Ours even exceeds fully supervised TaskExpert on surface normals (4.78 vs. 6.47 mErr) and depth (0.3977 vs. 0.4282 aErr), while slightly trailing on semantic segmentation (57.14 vs. 56.32 mIoU, a +1.46% gain). On Cityscapes, the method improves depth aErr by 22.73% over Li et al. and mIoU by 2.68% over the same baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MTPSL framework introduced by Li et al. (CVPR 2022), which aligned cross-task predictions via global vector representations in a shared semantic space. The key conceptual departure is shifting from global vector alignment to local region-level alignment, where SAM-generated regions are modeled as Gaussian distributions and contrasted across tasks using Wasserstein distance. It also draws on the contrastive learning lineage (CMC, DenseCL, RegionContrast) and prior use of Gaussian distributions in CV (GMMSeg, DUL), but is the first to combine SAM-based region extraction with distributional contrast for partially supervised multi-task dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical recipe for alleviating the high cost of pixel-level annotation across multiple dense tasks by leveraging SAM as a free, high-quality region prior, and the Gaussian distribution formulation provides a unified alignment mechanism that handles both classification (segmentation) and regression (depth, normals) tasks. Limitations include dependence on SAM's segmentation quality, evaluation on only two relatively small benchmarks (NYU-V2, Cityscapes), and the added training-time computational cost of running SAM alongside the MTL network. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three tasks or to larger outdoor datasets, whether SAM can be replaced by lighter region detectors without significant performance loss, and whether the distributional contrast idea transfers to non-dense prediction multi-task settings.
