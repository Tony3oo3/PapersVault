## Problem

Existing navigation instruction generation models produce instructions in a single fixed style with no controllability over linguistic style or landmark content, and they largely disregard the spatial topology of the navigation environment. This limits their practical utility in human-robot collaboration, where the recipient's familiarity with the environment and focus on specific landmarks should dictate the instruction's abstraction level and emphasis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240755/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of panoramic observations (each partitioned into 36 subview images at 224×224×3) paired with discrete navigation actions, along with a style prompt and a landmark prompt. Inference is two-stage: the model first autoregressively predicts a set of key landmarks from the trajectory, then generates the full instruction conditioned on those landmarks. The style of the output instruction (e.g., step-by-step vs. high-level) is switched by changing the prompt within the same model instance, and the landmark list can be manually edited to redirect the model's spatial focus.

## Method

C-INSTRUCTOR builds on LLaMA-Adapter v2 (7B, 32 layers) with a Trajectory Encoder that extracts per-subview features via CLIP, adds spatial/temporal/action positional encodings, and aggregates them through ViT blocks into per-step trajectory tokens. These tokens are injected into the LLM at every layer via zero-initialized cross-attention adapters, preserving the LLM's language priors. Three training components differentiate the approach: (1) CoTL, a chain-of-thought mechanism where the model first identifies landmarks scored by temporal scene-change importance and spatial distinctiveness, then generates instructions conditioned on those landmarks; (2) STMT, an auxiliary task predicting the backtracking action from each viewpoint to its predecessor, which forces the model to learn spatial connectivity; (3) SMT, which jointly trains on multiple instruction styles distinguished by prompts, enabling a single model to switch output style at inference time. Only the last 2 LLM layers and the adapter/encoder parameters are fine-tuned, with pre-training on PREVALENT followed by multi-dataset fine-tuning.

## Results

C-INSTRUCTOR achieves state-of-the-art instruction generation across all four benchmarks. On R2R val unseen, it reaches SPICE 0.212 and CIDEr 0.447, surpassing the previous best (Lana) by 21.8% and 51.2% in relative terms, respectively. On REVERIE val unseen, SPICE improves from 0.107 (Lana) to 0.141 (+31.8% relative), and on UrbanWalk, SPICE jumps from 0.566 (Kefa-speaker) to 0.645 (+14.0% relative). In downstream navigation, instructions from C-INSTRUCTOR yield SR 34.25 and SPL 31.25 with HAMT on REVERIE val unseen, exceeding human-annotated instructions (SR 32.95, SPL 30.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-INSTRUCTOR outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor is Lana [63], which achieves the best prior SPICE on R2R val unseen (0.174) and REVERIE val unseen (0.107); C-INSTRUCTOR surpasses it by 0.038 and 0.034 in absolute SPICE, respectively. On REVERIE, the gap widens substantially: C-INSTRUCTOR's CIDEr of 0.545 (val unseen) exceeds Lana's 0.327 by 66.7% in relative terms, reflecting stronger high-level description generation. On RxR, C-INSTRUCTOR leads BT-speaker [16] (the best prior method there) by 0.112 in BLEU-1 (0.678 vs 0.566) and 0.053 in BLEU-4 (0.233 vs 0.211) on val unseen. On the outdoor UrbanWalk benchmark, C-INSTRUCTOR beats Kefa-speaker [68] by 0.079 in SPICE (0.645 vs 0.566) and 0.126 in Rouge (0.781 vs 0.655), while ASSISTER [26] trails by a wider margin (SPICE 0.451). In the downstream navigation evaluation, C-INSTRUCTOR-generated instructions yield the highest SR (34.25) and SPL (31.25) with HAMT, exceeding even human annotations, whereas all other baselines produce instructions that degrade navigator performance below the human-annotated baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-INSTRUCTOR builds directly on the LLaMA-Adapter architecture (Gao et al., 2023) for parameter-efficient multimodal integration, extending it with a trajectory encoder that encodes panoramic observations and actions into the LLM via layer-wise adapters with zero-initialized attention. It departs from prior instruction generation methods (BT-speaker, EDrop-speaker, CCC-speaker, Lana), which relied on LSTM or smaller transformer encoders and could only produce single-style, uncontrollable instructions, by leveraging a 7B LLM with chain-of-thought prompting to achieve both style and content controllability. The paper also introduces two task-specific mechanisms absent from prior work: a Spatial Topology Modeling Task to compensate for the LLM's limited embodied spatial cognition, and a Chain-of-Thought with Landmarks (CoTL) mechanism inspired by cognitive psychology findings on how humans structure route descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Controllable instruction generation is practically critical for human-robot collaboration, assistive navigation for the visually impaired, and hazardous-environment guidance, where the recipient's familiarity with the environment or focus on specific landmarks dictates the appropriate instruction style and content. The finding that C-INSTRUCTOR's generated instructions improve navigator performance via data augmentation while prior methods' instructions degrade it highlights a quality threshold that smaller models cannot cross. Open questions include how the landmark selection threshold and style prompts generalize to novel or highly cluttered environments, whether the controllability degrades with longer trajectories, and whether the approach extends to continuous (non-discrete) navigation spaces.
