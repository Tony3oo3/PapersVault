## Problem

Conventional blind deconvolution methods rely on alternating minimization over the joint (x, k) posterior, a framework shown by Levin et al. to favor degenerate no-blur solutions because the number of unknowns far exceeds the number of observations. Although Levin et al. proposed a kernel-first marginalization strategy (MAP_k) that avoids this pathology, it was never adopted in practice due to the intractable image-space integration required to evaluate p(k|y). This paper bridges that gap by making the kernel-first approach computationally feasible within a diffusion model framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB patch with spatially invariant motion blur as input and produces both a 256×256 deblurred image and a 64×64 estimated blur kernel. The diffusion U-Net consumes both the current noisy kernel estimate and the blurred image as dual-branch inputs at every reverse-diffusion step, while a separate differentiable non-blind solver (DWDN) is invoked at each step to compute the reblurring loss for guidance. On the RealBlur dataset, the method uses a sparser kernel prior (γ = 5×10⁻⁴) and a diffusion model trained exclusively on realistic motion kernels to accommodate the less structured real-world blur.

## Method

Kernel-Diff replaces the joint (x,k) alternating minimization with a two-stage kernel-first pipeline: (1) a kernel-only conditional diffusion model samples from p(k|y) using a modified U-Net with separate image and kernel encoders whose features are concatenated before a shared decoder, and (2) a pre-trained differentiable non-blind solver (DWDN) estimates the image given the sampled kernel. The key approximation p(k|y) ≈ (1/Z(y)) p(y|F(y,k), k) makes the intractable image-space marginalization tractable by substituting the MAP image estimate F(y,k) for the full integral. At each reverse-diffusion step, a temporary kernel estimate k̂₀ is computed, passed through the non-blind solver to obtain x̂₀, and the reblurring loss ‖y − k̂₀⊛x̂₀‖² is minimized via gradient descent on k_t, effectively guiding the diffusion toward high-likelihood kernels. A Stage II refinement applies 20 additional gradient steps with an L1 sparsity penalty on the kernel to fine-tune the final estimate.

## Results

On BSD100 with synthetic motion blur, Kernel-Diff achieves 19.07 PSNR, 0.500 SSIM, 0.355 LPIPS, and 172.33 FID, outperforming the closest blind baseline DMPHN (18.73 PSNR, 0.427 SSIM) and the diffusion-based Blind-DPS (17.56 PSNR, 0.387 SSIM) across all metrics. The estimated kernel quality (MNC) of 0.663 also substantially exceeds Blind-DPS (0.283) and Self-Deblur (0.206). On the real-world RealBlur-50 dataset, Kernel-Diff reaches 25.57 PSNR and 0.798 SSIM, surpassing the next-best methods DMPHN (24.37 PSNR) and LMG (24.20 PSNR) by over 1 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Kernel-Diff outperforms all seven blind baselines on every metric in both Table 1 (BSD100) and Table 2 (RealBlur-50). Against the most direct competitor Blind-DPS [15] (also a diffusion-based blind deconvolution method), Kernel-Diff gains +1.51 dB PSNR and +0.113 SSIM on BSD100, and +5.75 dB PSNR and +0.191 SSIM on RealBlur-50, while also reducing FID from 280.53 to 172.33 (BSD100) and from 175.95 to 98.89 (RealBlur). Among end-to-end CNN baselines, DMPHN [85] is the strongest competitor (18.73 PSNR on BSD100, 24.37 on RealBlur), but Kernel-Diff still leads by 0.34 dB and 1.20 dB respectively. Classical methods Fast-Deblur [13] and Self-Deblur [54] trail significantly (14.02 and 13.53 PSNR on BSD100), and DeblurGAN [39] is the weakest overall. On kernel estimation quality (MNC), Kernel-Diff (0.663) nearly doubles Blind-DPS (0.283) and triples Self-Deblur (0.206), confirming that the kernel-first marginalization strategy yields substantially better blur kernel recovery. The gap to the non-blind upper bound DWDN [24] (22.95 PSNR) narrows to ~3.9 dB on BSD100, indicating the method recovers most of the information available when the kernel is known.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the kernel-first estimation strategy proposed by Levin et al. (2011), which argued that the conventional alternating minimization (MAP_{x,k}) framework is ill-posed and prone to degenerate no-blur solutions, but which was never practically adopted due to the intractable image-space marginalization. The key methodological departure from prior diffusion-based blind deconvolution (notably Blind-DPS, which runs parallel diffusion in both image and kernel spaces) is to sample only from p(k|y) using a kernel-conditioned U-Net, and to replace the intractable marginalization with a differentiable non-blind solver (DWDN) that provides a tractable likelihood approximation. This positions the work at the intersection of classical blind deconvolution theory and modern guided diffusion, effectively using the non-blind solver as a bridge between the kernel prior and the data fidelity term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first practical implementation of a 12-year-old theoretical recommendation, demonstrating that the kernel-first approach not only avoids the degenerate solution but also yields measurably better reconstructions with fewer parameters (43M vs. 110M for Blind-DPS). The residual convergence analysis (Figure 5) offers a clear empirical confirmation that alternating minimization gets trapped in worse local minima, lending credibility to the kernel-first framing. However, the method assumes spatially invariant blur and relies on a differentiable non-blind solver, limiting its applicability to real-world scenarios with spatially varying or non-convolutional degradations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-spatially-invariant blur, whether the non-blind solver can be made robust to kernel inaccuracies, and whether faster sampling (DDIM, consistency models) can reduce the ~300s inference time.
