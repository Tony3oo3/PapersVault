## Problem

When an object detector is deployed in a novel environment, its performance drops significantly, yet no fully self-supervised method exists for an embodied agent to autonomously fine-tune its detector while exploring without human annotation. Prior approaches either relied on ground-truth labels, used naive pseudo-labeling vulnerable to confirmation bias, or optimized exploration for map coverage rather than specifically mining the hard, inconsistent samples that most improve detection.

## Input / Output

The policy network consumes a 2-channel top-down grid map (disagreement scores and explored-area mask, each K×K) plus the agent's global orientation, and outputs a 2D long-term goal position fed to an A* path planner. The perception module takes the accumulated 3D semantic voxel map and projects it back onto each collected RGB-D frame to produce per-instance pseudo-labels (consistent bounding boxes, masks, class labels, and aggregated softmax vectors), which are then used to fine-tune a Mask R-CNN with ResNet-50 backbone. No raw images are fed to the policy; the abstraction to top-down maps makes the approach agnostic to camera placement and robot dynamics.

## Method

The method has two self-supervised stages. In the action stage (Look Around), an RL policy is trained to maximize the entropy-based disagreement score of the detector: 2D detections are projected into a 3D semantic voxel map (0.05 m voxels), connected components form distinct object instances, and the entropy of each instance's averaged logits yields a per-object disagreement score that is reprojected to a 2D top-down map guiding exploration toward uncertain regions. In the perception stage (Disagreement Reconciliation), the voxel map is reprojected onto every collected frame to generate view-consistent pseudo-labels, and the detector is fine-tuned with a combined loss: a contrastive triplet loss (instance-matching) that pulls feature vectors of the same 3D object together and pushes different objects apart across views, a soft-distillation cross-entropy loss against the aggregated softmax vector per instance, and standard Mask R-CNN head losses on the consistent pseudo-labels. The key insight is that the exploration policy deliberately seeks conflicting observations, and the reconciliation mechanism converts those inconsistencies into a structured training signal rather than discarding them.

## Results

On the Gibson test set (4,000 samples), Look Around + Disagreement Reconciliation achieves mAP@50 of 46.60, a 6.27% absolute improvement over the off-the-shelf Mask R-CNN baseline (40.33) and 2.90% over the strongest baseline (Inform + SEAL perception, 43.70). The ground-truth upper bound with the same policy reaches 48.01 mAP@50. On a real R1 humanoid robot, the same pipeline improves mAP@50 from 55.83 to 65.62 (+9.97%) without any human annotation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed Look Around + Disagreement Reconciliation (46.60 mAP@50) outperforms all six baselines under self-supervised settings. Against the strongest learned-policy baseline, Informative Trajectories + SEAL perception (43.70), the gain is 2.90 points; against SEAL + SEAL perception (42.38), it is 4.22 points. Notably, the method also beats the classical Frontier Exploration + SEAL perception (43.09) by 3.51 points, showing that uncertainty-driven exploration surpasses area-coverage strategies. The one case where the proposed policy underperforms is when paired with naive self-training (38.66 vs. 44.15 for Inform), which is expected because the policy deliberately seeks conflicting views that degrade raw pseudo-labels. On the real robot, Look Around + Disagreement Reconciliation (65.62) beats both random goals (56.65) and human-guided exploration (56.00) by roughly 9–10 points, confirming that the learned policy identifies harder samples than human intuition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised embodied active learning line initiated by SEAL [6], which used confident-prediction rewards and voxel-map consistency for pseudo-labeling, by replacing the confidence signal with an entropy-based disagreement score that simultaneously drives exploration and pseudo-label refinement. It also departs from Semantic Curiosity [9] (which operates on 2D top-view cells assuming one object per cell) and Informative Trajectories [18] (which uses KL divergence) by computing per-instance 3D disagreement and feeding it into both the RL policy and the perception loss. The perception stage introduces a contrastive triplet loss for cross-view instance matching and a soft-distillation loss on aggregated softmax vectors, drawing on consistency-training and knowledge-distillation literature but applying them in the embodied exploration context for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first real-robot validation of self-training object detection by active exploration (9.97% mAP gain on an R1 humanoid without any human annotation), demonstrating that simulator-trained policies transfer to physical platforms. The publicly released benchmark unifying multiple exploration policies, disagreement metrics, and perception modules under a common protocol is a useful resource for the embodied-AI community. Key limitations include the assumption of a static environment, reliance on a single 2D detector (Mask R-CNN), and evaluation on only two real-world scenes; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, 3D detectors, and larger-scale multi-robot settings.
