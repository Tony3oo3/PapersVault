## Problem

Unsupervised medical image registration assumes uniform intensity constancy and covisibility across the entire image domain, but nuisance variables such as sensor noise, non-Lambertian reflection in ultrasound, and inconsistent acquisition violate these assumptions. This produces spurious error residuals in regions lacking true correspondence, which inject noisy gradients during training and cause the displacement estimator to drift toward undesirable minima, degrading registration accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241000/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source and target medical image pair (2D cardiac MRI at 256×256, 2D echocardiography, or 3D echocardiography volumes) and produces a dense displacement vector field mapping source to target. Additionally, a U-Net-based scoring estimator takes only the target image as input and outputs a pixel-wise correspondence scoring map in [0,1], which is used exclusively during training to re-weight the data-fidelity loss and is discarded at inference, incurring no extra memory or runtime cost.

## Method

The proposed AdaCS framework introduces a learnable correspondence scoring map that adaptively re-weights the reconstruction error in the unsupervised registration loss, down-weighting pixels where nuisance variables prevent reliable correspondence. The scoring estimator is trained with an unsupervised objective that minimizes the weighted reconstruction error (with the displacement detached via stop-gradient), a regularizer penalizing scores toward zero to prevent the trivial all-zeros solution, and a momentum-guided adaptive total variation term whose strength is modulated by an exponential moving average of cosine-activated mean residuals, allowing weaker smoothness early in training and stronger smoothness near convergence. Training follows a warm-up phase where the displacement and scoring estimators are trained individually, followed by alternating optimization. The framework is architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play, demonstrated on Voxelmorph (CNN), Transmorph (Swin Transformer), and Diffusemorph (diffusion-based) across three cardiac datasets.

## Results

On ACDC (2D cardiac MRI), AdaCS improves Dice score from 79.48% (Voxelmorph) to 80.50%, from 76.94% (Transmorph) to 78.39%, and from 67.38% (Diffusemorph) to 72.09%. On CAMUS (2D cardiac ultrasound), improvements are 81.50%→81.74% (Voxelmorph), 79.24%→79.64% (Transmorph), and 75.23%→77.65% (Diffusemorph). Paired t-tests confirm statistical significance (p < 0.05) for all architecture-dataset combinations. Notably, all alternative baselines (NLL, β-NLL, AdaFrame, AdaReg) degrade performance relative to their respective base models, while AdaCS consistently improves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaCS outperforms all base architectures (Voxelmorph, Transmorph, Diffusemorph) on both ACDC and CAMUS, with the largest gain on Diffusemorph (e.g., +4.71 DSC on ACDC, +2.42 DSC on CAMUS). In stark contrast, all four alternative baselines—NLL, β-NLL, AdaFrame, and AdaReg—consistently degrade performance below their respective base models across nearly every architecture-dataset combination (e.g., NLL drops Voxelmorph ACDC DSC from 79.48% to 76.49%; AdaFrame drops it to 66.38%). The classical elastix baseline achieves 77.26% DSC on ACDC and 80.18% on CAMUS, which AdaCS-on-Voxelmorph surpasses on both. Paired t-tests confirm the AdaCS improvements are statistically significant (p = 5.85×10⁻¹⁶ for Voxelmorph on ACDC, p = 0.01 for Transmorph on both datasets, p = 4.56×10⁻⁴¹ for Diffusemorph on CAMUS). The authors attribute the baseline failures to their reliance on statistical assumptions about error residuals rather than learned correspondence scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised deformable registration lineage (Voxelmorph, Transmorph, Diffusemorph) by introducing a learned, pixel-wise correspondence scoring map that adaptively re-weights reconstruction residuals during training. It departs from prior adaptive weighting schemes (AdaReg, AdaFrame) that rely on fixed statistical assumptions about residual distributions, and from aleatoric uncertainty formulations (NLL, β-NLL) that model heteroscedastic noise variance, by instead learning a soft confidence score for whether correspondence can be established at each pixel. The momentum-guided adaptive smoothness regularizer, which modulates the degree of total-variation penalization on the scoring map based on training progress, is a novel component not present in prior registration or uncertainty-estimation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical and underappreciated failure mode in unsupervised registration: nuisance variables (noise, covisibility, non-Lambertian reflection) create irreconcilable residuals that push the displacement estimator toward undesirable minima. The finding that standard adaptive-weighting and uncertainty-estimation baselines consistently degrade performance relative to their base architectures is a useful caution for the community. Limitations include the need to tune scoring-loss hyperparameters per architecture and dataset, evaluation primarily on cardiac imaging, and the fact that the scoring network adds training-time cost (though no inference overhead). Open questions include whether amortized hyperparameter learning can remove the per-dataset tuning burden and whether the approach generalizes to non-cardiac or non-rigid-body registration tasks.
