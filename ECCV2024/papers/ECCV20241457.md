## Problem

Converting sparse DVS events into dense grayscale images in static scenes with a stationary camera has been an unsolved problem. Existing event-to-video methods (E2VID and variants) rely on camera or scene motion to generate events and suffer from grayscale distortion, missing texture detail, and complete failure on static scenes. This paper is the first to achieve faithful event-to-image conversion without any motion, using a controlled transmittance ramp instead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241457/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a Temporal Matrix of size W×H (1280×720 for the Prophesee EVK4), where each entry stores the timestamp (in μs, 1 μs resolution) of the pixel's initial positive event during a controlled transmittance ramp from 0 to 1. The output is a single grayscale intensity image with adaptive dynamic range (up to 114 dB demonstrated), optionally super-resolved by 4×. Unlike conventional cameras with a fixed ~70 dB dynamic range, the effective grayscale resolution scales with the temporal span of IPE timestamps (e.g., 292,111 levels vs. 4,096 for a 12-bit sensor).

## Method

The key insight is to use time as an intensity proxy: by ramping transmittance from 0 via an aperture shutter (AT-DVS), each pixel's IPE timestamp is negatively correlated with scene light intensity, forming a Temporal Matrix. A closed-form mapping (Eq. 4) converts timestamps to relative intensities via the integral of the transmittance function, but real-world degradations (spatially variant contrast thresholds, Poisson timestamp errors, cold/hot pixels) prevent direct use. A SwinIR-based temporal-mapping network is trained under a custom two-stage degradation model that first applies temporal-domain perturbations (random threshold C, Poisson timestamp noise, cold-pixel masking) to a synthetic Temporal Matrix, then applies spatial-domain degradations (Gaussian noise, blur, 4× downsampling) after converting back to the intensity domain. Training is supervised with L1 + perceptual + PatchGAN losses on DIV2K, Flick2K, and WED, requiring no synthetic event simulation and thus avoiding the sim-to-real domain gap.

## Results

On the TemMat dataset, EvTemMap achieves a PSNR of 15.75 dB and SSIM of 0.69, substantially outperforming all event-to-video baselines. The closest competitor, E2VID+ [39], reaches only 11.21 dB PSNR and 0.55 SSIM, a gap of over 4.5 dB in PSNR. The method also demonstrates adaptive dynamic range up to 114 dB and a theoretical grayscale resolution of 292,111 levels (71.3× a 12-bit raw file), along with a 94% reduction in capture time compared to conventional low-light photography.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvTemMap decisively outperforms all three baselines on both PSNR and SSIM. Against E2VID [35] (CVPR 2019), the proposed method gains +5.27 dB PSNR and +0.11 SSIM; against E2VID+ [39] (ECCV 2020), the margins are +4.54 dB and +0.14; and against SPADE-E2VID [6] (IEEE TIP 2021), the gains are +5.55 dB and +0.17. Qualitatively, the E2VID-family methods produce severely distorted grayscale (even inverted in night scenes) and lose high-frequency textures, while EvTemMap faithfully recovers both shadow and highlight details in 114 dB HDR scenes. The fundamental reason for the gap is that E2VID methods rely on motion events and recover intensity only up to an unknown initial frame, whereas EvTemMap directly measures per-pixel brightness via temporal mapping from a stationary camera, eliminating the ghosting and grayscale-ambiguity issues inherent to event integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the event-to-video reconstruction lineage (E2VID, E2VID+, SPADE-E2VID), which relies on motion-induced events in dynamic scenes or with a moving camera. Rather than treating the temporal dimension of events as a byproduct of motion, EvTemMap repurposes it as the primary grayscale encoding: by gradually increasing transmittance with an aperture shutter (AT-DVS), the timestamp of each pixel's first positive event directly encodes scene intensity. The reconstruction network borrows the SwinIR backbone and BSRGAN-style degradation framework from the blind image restoration literature, but applies them to a novel temporal-domain degradation model (spatially variant contrast threshold, Poisson timestamp noise, cold/hot pixels) that has no analogue in prior event-processing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a stationary event camera in a static scene can produce a faithful grayscale image, opening a new imaging modality that combines the event camera's 1 μs temporal resolution with adaptive dynamic range (114 dB demonstrated) and ~292k effective grayscale levels. Key limitations include the mandatory transmittance adjustment hardware, non-negligible capture time (78 ms even in low light), and the fact that the method is inherently single-channel. Open questions include whether the temporal-mapping principle can be extended to color imaging, whether faster transmittance modulation (e.g., liquid crystal shutters) can reduce motion blur, and whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution DVS sensors as bandwidth limitations are overcome.
