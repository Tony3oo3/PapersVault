## Problem

Existing audio-to-video generation methods either reduce audio to a single global semantic embedding (losing temporal structure) or are restricted to narrow scenarios like talking faces. No prior work achieves high-quality video generation with fine-grained, frame-level temporal synchronization between audio and object motions, largely due to the absence of high-quality training data with strong audio-visual synchronization cues and the lack of architectures designed to exploit audio's temporal structure for motion control.

## Input / Output

The model takes a static RGB image (256×256 or 128×256) as the first frame, a 2-second audio clip (16 kHz, converted to 128-d mel spectrograms), and a text label for the audio category. It produces a 12-frame video at 6 fps by iteratively denoising noisy latents of frames 2 through 12 while conditioning on the clean first-frame latent at every diffusion step. A classifier-free guidance factor η (≥1) scales the audio conditioning strength at inference, allowing users to trade off synchronization fidelity against visual diversity.

## Method

AVSyncD extends a frozen Stable Diffusion V1.5 UNet with three key mechanisms for audio-synchronized animation. First, it repurposes ImageBind's local patch tokens (originally discarded) as time-aware audio embeddings, splitting them into per-frame segments that condition each frame's latent via cross-attention, enabling frame-level synchronization rather than global semantic control. Second, it adds temporal attention layers (with sinusoidal positional embeddings) and first-frame-lookup temporal convolutions (kernel size 3, receptive field adjusted to include frame 1) to capture long-range video dynamics while anchoring generation to the input image. Third, it modifies the frozen spatial self-attention layers to cross-attend to the first frame rather than self-attend, ensuring faithful animation of the input. The model is trained with a standard LDM denoising objective on the remaining frames, with 20% of training steps using a null audio embedding to support classifier-free audio guidance at inference.

## Results

On the proposed AVSync15 benchmark, AVSyncD (η=4) achieves the best FVD of 349.1, AlignSync of 22.62, and RelSync of 45.52, while maintaining a competitive FID of 11.7. In the user study (300 votes per metric), AVSyncD (η=4) receives the most votes for image quality (163/300), frame consistency (186/300), and synchronization (192/300). On Landscapes, AVSyncD also achieves the best FVD (415.2) and FID (16.2), with synchronization scores close to ground truth (AlignSync 24.82 vs. 25.01). On TheGreatestHits, AVSyncD (η=4) achieves the best FVD (249.3) and FID (8.7) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVSyncD (η=4) outperforms all five baselines on the primary AVSync15 benchmark across nearly all metrics. Against TPoS, AVSyncD reduces FVD from 1227.8 to 349.1 (3.5× improvement) and improves AlignSync from 19.67 to 22.62, while TPoS retains a marginal edge on IT (30.73 vs. 30.45). Against TempoToken, AVSyncD dominates on every metric, with FVD dropping from 4466.4 to 349.1 and IA jumping from 18.84 to 38.53, reflecting TempoToken's lack of image input. Against VideoCrafter (no audio), AVSyncD improves FVD from 840.7 to 349.1 and RelSync from 43.16 to 45.52, confirming the value of audio conditioning. Against AADiff, AVSyncD achieves a substantially lower FID (11.7 vs. 18.8) and FVD (349.1 vs. 978.0), while matching its RelSync (45.52 vs. 45.48); AADiff's poor image quality stems from its reliance on raw audio amplitude. CoDi is the weakest baseline, trailing AVSyncD on all metrics (FVD 1522.6 vs. 349.1, AlignSync 19.54 vs. 22.62), consistent with its purely semantic (non-temporal) audio encoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) framework for video generation by adding temporal attention layers, first-frame lookup convolutions, and audio cross-attention conditioned on ImageBind's local patch tokens. It departs from prior audio-to-video generation work (TPoS, AADiff, TempoToken, CoDi) that either encodes audio into a single global semantic feature or relies on crude amplitude signals, by explicitly modeling per-frame temporal synchronization. The dataset curation pipeline builds on VGGSound and VGGSoundSync's synchronization classifier but adds multi-stage filtering (pixel/CLIP differences, waveform amplitude, semantic alignment, av-sync scoring) followed by manual verification to produce a much tighter benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AVSync15 benchmark and the finding that static videos score competitively on synchronization metrics for ambient-sound datasets (Landscapes, TheGreatestHits) expose a fundamental weakness in prior audio-video generation evaluations: without strong per-frame motion cues, synchronization metrics become uninformative. The classifier-free audio guidance mechanism offers a practical, tunable knob for controlling synchronization strength at inference time. However, the 15-category, 1,350-video scale is far too small to generalize broadly, and the authors themselves note that covering all audio classes would require orders of magnitude more data. Open questions include whether the temporal attention + first-frame lookup architecture scales to longer videos or more diverse motion types, and whether the ImageBind local-token approach can be replaced by lighter-weight encoders.
