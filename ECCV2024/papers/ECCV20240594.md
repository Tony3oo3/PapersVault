## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods produce depth only up to an unknown scale, making them unusable for metric tasks like 3D object detection or motion planning. Existing scale-recovery approaches require auxiliary sensors (GPS, IMU, velocity, or measured camera height), which prevents training on arbitrary in-the-wild driving videos. The paper asks whether metric scale can be learned entirely from monocular driving video by exploiting the fact that cars on the road have known, invariant physical sizes.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB driving video sequences and camera intrinsics as input, and additionally consumes pre-computed road region masks and vehicle instance segmentation masks (from OneFormer) during training only. It outputs a metric depth map in absolute meters. The framework is architecture-agnostic: it wraps any existing self-supervised MDE network (e.g., Monodepth2, Lite-Mono, HR-Depth, VADepth) and at inference the computational cost is identical to the original model, with no camera-height invariance assumption required.

## Method

FUMET derives a per-frame camera height estimate from the depth map by computing surface normals in the road region and taking the negated inner product with reprojected 3D points. A Learned Size Prior (LSP), trained on a large web-scraped vehicle dataset with occlusion/truncation augmentations, estimates each car's physical height from its masked appearance. The Silhouette Projector orthographically projects the car's 3D point cloud onto a plane perpendicular to the ground, measures the silhouette height, and compares it to the LSP estimate to obtain a per-frame scale factor; outlier cars are filtered by geometric plausibility against the horizon. The scaled camera heights across all frames in a sequence are consolidated via median and optimized across training epochs with a weighted moving average, producing a stable pseudo-supervision. The network is trained with a camera-height L1 loss (L_cam) and an auxiliary rough geometric loss (L_aux) that anchors object depths to their approximate metric values, with logarithmically scheduled weights that shift emphasis from L_aux to L_cam as training progresses.

## Results

On the KITTI Eigen test split (640×192), FUMET with Monodepth2 achieves AbsRel 0.108, RMSE 4.736, and δ<1.25 of 0.871 without any post-hoc median scaling, matching or surpassing all weakly-supervised baselines that rely on GPS, velocity, IMU, or ground-truth camera height. On Cityscapes, FUMET achieves AbsRel 0.125 and δ<1.25 of 0.858, dramatically outperforming the best weakly-supervised baseline (VADepth: AbsRel 0.363, δ<1.25 0.295) by a wide margin. When median scaling is applied, FUMET+VADepth reaches the best overall AbsRel of 0.102 and RMSE of 4.473 on KITTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUMET (Monodepth2) outperforms all five weakly-supervised baselines on KITTI without scaling: it beats DynaDepth (IMU+V+G) by 0.001 in AbsRel (0.108 vs 0.109) and 0.002 in δ<1.25 (0.871 vs 0.869), and surpasses G2S (GPS), PackNet-SfM (velocity), VADepth (CamH), and Wagstaff (CamH) by 0.001–0.015 in AbsRel. On Cityscapes, FUMET dominates all weakly-supervised methods by large margins—achieving AbsRel 0.125 vs. VADepth's 0.363 and PackNet-SfM's 0.504, with δ<1.25 of 0.858 vs. 0.295 and 0.029 respectively. Against the unsupervised baselines (Monodepth2, HR-Depth, Lite-Mono), FUMET consistently improves every metric after median scaling (e.g., Monodepth2 AbsRel drops from 0.115 to 0.106; HR-Depth from 0.109 to 0.108; Lite-Mono from 0.110 to 0.107). For the LSP component, the learned size prior outperforms both the fixed 1.59 m height prior [20] and MonoCInIS [19] on KITTI (AbsRel 0.062 vs. 0.065 and 0.159 for height) and generalizes far better to Cityscapes (0.080 vs. 0.093 and 0.851), where MonoCInIS overfits to KITTI and degrades severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUMET extends the self-supervised monocular depth estimation framework established by Zhou et al. (CVPR 2017) and Godard et al. (ICCV 2019), which produce scale-ambiguous depth from video reconstruction losses. It departs from the line of weakly-supervised scale-recovery methods (GPS, IMU, velocity, ground-truth camera height) by replacing auxiliary sensor measurements with a learned car-size prior (LSP) aggregated into a camera-height invariance constraint. The Silhouette Projector mechanism and the cross-frame/epoch camera-height optimization are novel contributions that distinguish FUMET from both fixed-prior approaches (Hoiem et al., Zhu et al.) and sensor-dependent baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUMET provides a drop-in training scheme that converts any scale-oblivious monocular depth network into a metric estimator without requiring auxiliary sensors, thereby unlocking the vast pool of in-the-wild driving videos (YouTube, dashcams) for training. The demonstrated ability to train on mixed datasets with different camera heights and still generalize well (Tab. 3) suggests a path toward large-scale, domain-diverse metric depth models. Limitations include reliance on cars as scale anchors (road-scene specific), dependence on off-the-shelf segmentation quality, and the fact that the camera-height invariance assumption only holds within a single sequence. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-road scenes or to scale objects other than vehicles, and whether the learned size prior can be further generalized across vehicle types and cultures.
