## Problem

Previous segmentation data synthesis methods rely on a 'segmentation labeler module'—itself a segmentation model—to produce masks for synthetic images, creating a chicken-or-egg dilemma where downstream model performance is bounded by the labeler's capacity. This bottleneck prevents synthetic data from surpassing existing segmentation models on fully-supervised benchmarks like ADE20K and COCO.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240208/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes text prompts (generated by BLIP-2-FlanT5xxl captions of real training images) and segmentation masks as inputs. The Text2Mask model produces new segmentation masks by generating RGB color maps (one unique color per category) at 768 resolution using 200 EDM sampling steps, which are then decoded to class-ID masks via nearest-color lookup. The Mask2Img model takes a mask (human-annotated or synthetic) plus text prompt and produces a photorealistic image at 768 resolution in 40 steps; for panoptic/instance tasks, instance boundaries are outlined with a special edge color on the input color map. The final output is large-scale image-mask training pairs (e.g., ~1.2M samples for ADE20K, ~1.2M for COCO).

## Method

SegGen reverses the traditional data generation pipeline: instead of generating images first and then labeling them with a segmentation model, it first synthesizes diverse segmentation masks from text prompts (Text2Mask) and then generates realistic images conditioned on those masks (Mask2Img), entirely eliminating the segmentation labeler bottleneck. Text2Mask is a fine-tuned SDXL-base model trained on [text, segmentation color map] pairs, where masks are encoded as RGB maps with one color per category; Mask2Img adopts the ControlNet architecture, freezing SDXL-base weights and training a compact side network on [text, color map, image] triplets for mask-conditioned generation. Two complementary data generation strategies are proposed: MaskSyn creates entirely new mask-image pairs from text prompts (diversifying both masks and images), while ImgSyn generates new images conditioned on existing human-annotated masks (diversifying images only). The synthetic data is integrated into segmentation model training via either a data augmentation strategy (replacing real samples with synthetic ones at probability p_aug=60%, best for ADE20K) or a pre-training strategy (pre-train on synthetic data, then fine-tune on real data, best for COCO).

## Results

On ADE20K semantic segmentation, SegGen boosts Mask2Former R50 from 47.2 to 49.9 mIoU (+2.7) and Swin-L from 56.1 to 57.4 mIoU (+1.3), establishing new SOTA without extra real data. On COCO panoptic segmentation, Mask DINO with Swin-L improves PQ from 58.6 to 59.3 (+0.7) and mIoU_pan from 67.0 to 68.1 (+1.1). On COCO instance segmentation, Mask2Former Swin-L gains +0.8 AP (49.5→50.3). Most strikingly, when trained purely on synthetic data, SegGen achieves 82.2 mIoU* on ADE20K, surpassing DiffuMask (57.0) by +25.2 mIoU and approaching pure real-data performance (83.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegGen consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every benchmark. On ADE20K, Mask2Former+SegGen (R50, 49.9 mIoU) surpasses the newer Mask DINO (48.7) and OneFormer (47.3), and the Swin-L variant (57.4) exceeds OneFormer (57.0) and Mask DINO (56.6). On COCO panoptic segmentation, SegGen pre-training yields gains of +0.6 to +0.7 PQ and +1.0 to +1.6 mIoU_pan over real-data pre-training baselines for both Mask2Former and Mask DINO across R50 and Swin-L. On COCO instance segmentation, improvements of +0.5 AP (R50) and +0.8 AP (Swin-L) are observed, with an additional +1.2 AP on the higher-quality LVIS annotations. The most dramatic comparison is against DiffuMask (ICCV 2023) under pure synthetic-data training: SegGen achieves 82.2 mIoU* versus DiffuMask's 57.0, a +25.2 mIoU margin, while approaching the 83.3 mIoU* of pure real-data training. The paper also demonstrates superior domain generalization on PASCAL and AI-generated images (Kandinsky-2) compared to models trained without synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegGen builds on SDXL as the generative backbone and ControlNet's architecture for conditional image synthesis, but departs fundamentally from prior segmentation data generation methods (DatasetGAN, Grounded Diffusion, DiffuMask) that rely on a 'segmentation labeler module' to produce masks for synthetic images. The key conceptual departure is reversing the traditional pipeline: instead of generating images first and then labeling them with a segmentation model (which bounds quality), SegGen first generates diverse masks from text prompts via a Text2Mask model, then synthesizes images conditioned on those masks via a Mask2Img model. This eliminates the 'chicken or egg dilemma' where the labeler's capacity constrains the downstream segmentation model's performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a fundamental bottleneck in synthetic data generation for segmentation and demonstrates that high-quality synthetic data can push SOTA on competitive benchmarks (ADE20K, COCO) without additional human annotations, while also improving robustness to unseen domains including AI-generated images. The finding that synthetic images often exhibit better mask-image alignment than real images (due to annotation imperfections) suggests a broader implication for data quality in pixel-level tasks. Open questions include scalability to open-vocabulary or video segmentation, the computational cost of generating ~1M synthetic samples, and whether the approach transfers to datasets with very different category structures or annotation conventions.
