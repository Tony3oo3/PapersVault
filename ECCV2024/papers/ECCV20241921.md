## Problem

Domain adaptive panoptic segmentation via Mean Teacher consistency learning suffers from noise amplification: the teacher produces false-positive masks and uncertain pseudo-labels that, when fed back to the student, create a positive feedback loop that degrades performance. Prior methods address this with image-wide confidence scaling (EDAPS) or handcrafted teacher prediction calibration (UniDAformer), but neither exploits the fine-grained, per-mask uncertainty that mask transformers can naturally estimate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241921/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a labeled source domain (e.g., Synthia at 1280×760, Cityscapes at 1024×2048) and unlabeled target domain images, producing panoptic segmentation outputs: per-pixel class labels, instance assignment maps, and dense sigmoidal mask scores for each of N mask queries. During training, the teacher additionally emits per-mask confidence scores λ_i and dense pixel-level confidence maps ρ that modulate the student's loss, and the student operates on strongly perturbed versions of target images (color augmentation, Gaussian smoothing, SegMix pasting of source segments).

## Method

MC-PanDA builds on Mask2Former with a Swin-B backbone under a Mean Teacher consistency framework, contributing two confidence-aware loss modifications. First, Mask-wide Loss Scaling (MLS) multiplies each mask's localization loss by λ_i, the fraction of that mask's foreground pixels whose teacher pixel-level confidence ρ exceeds a threshold τ₁, thereby suppressing gradients from uncertain masks while preserving learning from confident ones. Second, Confidence-based Point Filtering (CBPF) replaces PointRend's uncertainty-driven sparse point sampling with a teacher-aware affinity A_i(r,c) that sets the sampling score to −∞ where teacher confidence falls below τ₂, and otherwise to −|s_{i,r,c}| (negative student pre-activation magnitude), so that sampled points are simultaneously teacher-confident and student-uncertain. The teacher confidence Φ is computed as the max over all masks of ρ_{i,r,c}, making the filtering conservative by preventing any mask from training on pixels the teacher is unsure about. Together, these two mechanisms break the positive feedback loop by selectively withholding back-propagation at both the mask level and the pixel level.

## Results

On the primary Synthia → Cityscapes benchmark, MC-PanDA achieves 47.4 PQ₁₆ (76.7 SQ₁₆, 59.3 RQ₁₆), surpassing the previous state of the art EDAPS (41.2 PQ₁₆) by 6.2 percentage points. On Synthia → Vistas, the method reaches 38.7 PQ₁₆, improving over EDAPS (36.6) by 2.1 points. In the real-to-real setting (Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes), MC-PanDA attains 63.7 PQ₁₆ / 62.0 PQ₁₉, a 7.0-point gain over EDAPS (56.7 PQ₁₆). All results are averaged over three random seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MC-PanDA outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated domain adaptation setup. Against CVRN [24], the improvement is the largest: +15.3 PQ₁₆ on Synthia → Cityscapes (47.4 vs. 32.1) and +17.4 PQ₁₆ on Synthia → Vistas (38.7 vs. 21.3), reflecting CVRN's reliance on cross-task consistency without uncertainty-aware loss modulation. Against UniDAformer [61], MC-PanDA gains +13.2 PQ₁₆ on Synthia → Cityscapes (47.4 vs. 34.2 for the PSN variant), showing that fine-grained mask confidence surpasses UniDAformer's handcrafted hierarchical mask calibration. Against the closest competitor EDAPS [43], MC-PanDA improves by 6.2 PQ₁₆ on Synthia → Cityscapes (47.4 vs. 41.2) and by 7.0 PQ₁₆ on Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes (63.7 vs. 56.7); the gap narrows to 2.1 PQ₁₆ on Synthia → Vistas (38.7 vs. 36.6), which the authors attribute to the greater intra-class variation in Vistas requiring dataset-specific hyperparameters. A backbone-matched comparison (Table 7) confirms the advantage is not due to architecture: with the same Swin-B backbone, MC-PanDA beats retrained EDAPS by 8.1 PQ₁₆ (47.4 vs. 39.3).

## Relation to Prior Work

MC-PanDA extends the Mean Teacher consistency learning framework for domain-adaptive panoptic segmentation by replacing EDAPS's image-wide confidence scaling with two finer-grained mechanisms: mask-wide loss scaling (MLS) and confidence-based point filtering (CBPF). It builds directly on Mask2Former's mask transformer architecture and PointRend's sparse point sampling, but departs from prior work by jointly exploiting both teacher confidence and student uncertainty in the sampling affinity, rather than relying on handcrafted teacher prediction calibration (UniDAformer) or uniform image-level downscaling (EDAPS). The paper positions itself as the first panoptic adaptation method grounded in region-wide and fine-grained uncertainty quantification from mask transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.2 PQ improvement over EDAPS on Synthia→Cityscapes, backed by thorough ablations isolating each component's contribution, provides a clear recipe for reducing noise amplification in self-training loops. The finding that mask-wide scaling consistently outperforms image-wide scaling (Table 5) offers a practical design principle for any confidence-modulated self-supervised loss. However, the method still requires dataset-specific tuning of thresholds τ₁ and τ₂ (notably on Synthia→Vistas), and the small batch size (2+2) limits scalability; automatic per-class hyperparameter selection and validation at larger batch sizes remain open.
