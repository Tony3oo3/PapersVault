## Problem

Vehicle trajectory prediction models are heavily domain-specific, yet their generalization across datasets and cities has been underexplored due to incompatible data formats, map resolutions, annotation taxonomies, and evaluation metrics across existing benchmarks (nuScenes, Argoverse 2, WOMD). Additionally, the impact of scaling dataset size and diversity on prediction performance remains largely unexplored, as prior work has been confined to single-dataset training and evaluation.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes agent-centric vectorized inputs: 2D historical trajectories (2 s at 10 Hz), vectorized HD map lanes (0.5 m point spacing, 100 m radius, with direction and one-hot lane-type encoding), and surrounding agent features (velocity, heading, acceleration, one-hot type). Outputs are 6-mode multi-modal future trajectories over a 6 s horizon. All four source datasets (nuScenes, Argoverse 2, WOMD, nuPlan) are harmonized into this single format via ScenarioNet, with user-configurable parameters for past/future length, map resolution, and agent attributes.

## Method

UniTraj is a unified open-source framework rather than a new architecture: it repurposes ScenarioNet as a common scenario-description format, applies harmonization steps (agent-centric coordinate transform, 8 s temporal truncation, 0.5 m map interpolation, unified agent feature set), and integrates three transformer-based predictors (AutoBot 1.5 M, MTR 60.1 M, Wayformer 16.5 M) with a shared evaluation suite (brier-minFDE, minADE/minFDE, miss rate, plus fine-grained stratification by trajectory type and Kalman difficulty). The key empirical contributions are: (1) cross-dataset and cross-city experiments revealing significant performance drops and a consistent generalization hierarchy (WOMD > Argoverse 2 > nuScenes) driven by dataset diversity rather than size alone; and (2) multi-dataset training on 2 M+ trajectories across 15 cities, which yields a new state-of-the-art on nuScenes (rank 1, minADE5 = 0.96) without any architectural modification, demonstrating that data scale and diversity are the primary levers for improved generalization.

## Results

Training MTR on the combined UniTraj dataset (2.01M trajectories from nuScenes, Argoverse 2, and WOMD) achieves brier-minFDE of 2.27 on nuScenes, 1.99 on Argoverse 2, and 2.13 on WOMD, substantially outperforming single-dataset training (e.g., 2.86 on nuScenes when trained only on nuScenes). This multi-dataset MTR ranks 1st on the nuScenes public leaderboard with minADE5 of 0.96. Cross-dataset transfer without retraining causes severe degradation: MTR trained on nuScenes jumps from 2.86 to 7.38 brier-minFDE when evaluated on WOMD. Scaling training data from 20% to 100% of the combined set yields a consistent monotonic reduction in average brier-minFDE for AutoBot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the nuScenes public leaderboard, MTR-UniTraj (trained on all three datasets) achieves minADE5 of 0.96, beating Goal-LBP [48] (1.02, rank 2), CASPNet++ [39] (1.16, rank 3), and Q-EANet [12] (1.18, rank 4) by margins of 0.06, 0.20, and 0.22 respectively. The same multi-dataset training strategy also improves AutoBot from rank 19 (minADE5 1.37, single-dataset) to rank 11 (minADE5 1.26), a 0.11 absolute gain. In cross-dataset generalization (Table 2), the multi-dataset MTR outperforms every single-dataset variant on all three evaluation sets, with the largest gains on nuScenes (2.27 vs. 2.86) and Argoverse 2 (1.99 vs. 2.08). Notably, WOMD-trained models generalize best across datasets (e.g., 3.10 on nuScenes vs. 3.72 for Argoverse2-trained), attributed to WOMD's greater trajectory-type diversity and more balanced Kalman-difficulty distribution rather than sheer size alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes ScenarioNet, originally designed for traffic scenario simulation, as the backbone for a unified vehicle trajectory prediction framework, extending its data conversion pipeline to Argoverse 2. It draws on the multi-dataset benchmarking tradition established in human trajectory prediction (Trajnet++, trajdata) and the Kalman difficulty evaluation from Makansi et al., but is the first to provide an open-source, end-to-end framework unifying datasets, models, and evaluation for vehicle trajectory prediction. The three integrated models (AutoBot, MTR, Wayformer) represent the current state-of-the-art in transformer-based motion forecasting, and the paper positions itself as an empirical study on generalization and data scaling rather than proposing a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniTraj provides the largest publicly available vehicle trajectory prediction dataset (2M+ samples, 1337 hours, 15 cities) and a practical platform that lowers the barrier to multi-dataset research, which was previously hampered by format incompatibilities. The finding that cross-dataset performance drops are substantial and driven by diversity (not just size) has direct implications for data collection strategies in autonomous driving. Open questions include whether the framework's harmonization choices (e.g., 2D coordinates, 0.5m map resolution) mask important 3D or high-resolution information, whether the results extend to pedestrian or cyclist prediction, and whether the generalization gaps can be closed with domain-adaptive training rather than simply more data.
