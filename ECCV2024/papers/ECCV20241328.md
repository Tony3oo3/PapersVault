## Problem

Multi-view 3D reconstruction via SfM and MVS inherently recovers scene geometry only up to an unknown scale factor, even with known camera intrinsics. Existing solutions to resolve this ambiguity require reference objects of known size, pre-calibrated camera poses, or external sensors (LiDAR, IMU), which are not always available. This paper shows that the defocus blur information encoded in dual-pixel (DP) camera images can resolve the scale ambiguity without any of these external aids.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241328/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view DP images (left and right sub-aperture views extracted per pixel), known per-view camera intrinsics (focal length and f-number), and depth maps up to scale produced by an off-the-shelf SfM+MVS pipeline (Metashape). All images are undistorted before blur estimation. The output is a single global scene scale factor and per-view focus distances. The minimum theoretical requirement is just two pixels with distinct depths in a single view, though in practice hundreds of pixels across multiple views are used for robustness.

## Method

The core insight is coupling two independent ambiguities: the linear scale ambiguity in SfM/MVS depths (z = s·z′) and the focus-distance ambiguity in the DP thin-lens blur model. Substituting the scale-ambiguous depth into the paraxial thin-lens equation yields a relationship that is linear in the reciprocal focus distance 1/g_i and reciprocal scale 1/s, producing an overdetermined linear system across all views and selected pixels. This system is solved via ℓ₁-norm minimization using Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS) to handle outliers. A confidence-based pixel selection (combining blur-estimation residuals with local depth variance) and a median-based view selection improve robustness. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no reference objects, pre-calibration, or learned blur estimators.

## Results

The proposed method achieves an overall average scale error e_s of 0.219 across six scenes and multiple camera/lens/aperture settings, where the scale ratio r_s ranges from 0 to ∞ and values closer to 1 indicate better accuracy. Performance varies by lens: the 50mm f/1.8 setting yields the best average error (e_s = 0.061), while the 35mm f/8.0 setting is the worst (e_s = 0.396) due to smaller defocus blur. The smartphone (Google Pixel 4, f/1.73) achieves e_s = 0.224, comparable to the DSLR overall. In the most favorable settings, the method estimates scene scale with less than 0.2 error ratio, enabling centimeter-level measurements in wide outdoor scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against ZoeDepth [6], a zero-shot monocular metric depth estimation method, as the sole quantitative baseline. On the same six scenes and lens settings, ZoeDepth achieves an average scale error e_s of 0.679, which is more than 3× worse than the proposed method's 0.219. The proposed method consistently outperforms ZoeDepth across all scenes and camera configurations, with the gap being particularly large in scenes with complex geometry. The paper also demonstrates stability through repeated experiments (200–300 images per scene), showing that the estimated scale ratio remains tightly bounded across 10 trials. A key limitation noted is that the method fails on textureless or fine-texture scenes where defocus blur estimation becomes unreliable, whereas ZoeDepth, being purely learned, does not suffer from this specific failure mode but is far less accurate overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: defocus-based depth/scale estimation and dual-pixel (DP) imaging. It builds directly on the thin-lens model relating signed defocus blur to scene depth (Garg et al. [10], Wadhwa et al. [36]) and the unsupervised DP blur size estimation of Punnappurath et al. [25]. The key departure from prior defocus-scale methods (Shiozaki & Dissanayake [32], Wöhler et al. [37]) is treating the focus distance as unknown rather than fixed, and from the closest prior work (Mishima et al. [22]) is replacing a learned blur estimator with the hardware-native DP disparity, yielding a fully uncalibrated, training-free linear formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, calibration-free path to absolute scale in photogrammetry and SfM pipelines, removing the need for reference objects, LiDAR, or IMU sensors—valuable for field surveys and consumer-grade capture. However, performance is bottlenecked by the accuracy of DP blur size estimation, which degrades on textureless or repetitive-texture surfaces (failure case in Fig. 10), and the ~22% average scale error, while reasonable, may be insufficient for high-precision metrology. An open question is whether improved DP blur estimation (e.g., learned or multi-frame) can push the error below 10% without introducing the training/calibration overhead the paper explicitly avoids.
