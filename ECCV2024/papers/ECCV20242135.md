## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) offer interpretable mitigation of spurious correlations, but constructing them requires costly human concept annotation. Existing CLIP-based CBMs that bypass annotation are themselves vulnerable to spurious correlations due to biases inherited from CLIP's pre-training, and prior weight-pruning fixes still demand human expertise to select which concepts to remove.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242135/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes standard RGB images from classification benchmarks (Waterbirds, ImageNet-Opener, Metashifts) and produces two outputs: a final class label from the classifier and per-image binary concept annotations (hard CBM) indicating presence/absence of each curated visual attribute. The concept annotations are generated as binary values by querying LLaVA, distinguishing this from soft CBMs that output continuous relevance scores from CLIP projections. An intermediate output is a filtered concept pool of visual attributes deemed free of spurious-correlation associations.

## Method

The framework constructs Annotation-based CBMs in three stages, all driven by foundation models. First, GPT-3 generates a diverse concept pool per class, LLaVA produces image descriptions, and point biserial correlation between extracted keywords and class labels identifies spurious concepts that are then removed via CLIP/sentence-embedding similarity filtering. Second, LLaVA-v1.5-13B is queried per image with 'Does the object have {attribute}?' to produce binary concept annotations, replacing human labeling. Third (optional), a LangChain-orchestrated chain of BLIP-2 → Grounding DINO → SAM segments out the main object to remove confusing backgrounds before re-querying LLaVA, correcting a common MLLM error of attributing background properties to the object. The final CBM uses a ResNet-50 concept model and classifier trained independently, and the key design choice of using hard binary annotations from an MLLM rather than soft CLIP projections is what makes the model immune to CLIP's inherent spurious-correlation biases.

## Results

On Waterbirds, the proposed LLaVA-based CBM achieves 83.2% worst-group accuracy and 94.2% average accuracy, substantially outperforming ERM (72.6%/97.3%), Label-free CBM (55.0%/82.8%), and Post-hoc CBM (31.6%/91.7%). On ImageNet-Opener, it reaches 90.0% worst-group and 86.1% average accuracy, compared to 68.0%/80.1% for ERM and 45.0%/48.0% for Post-hoc CBM. On Metashifts, it attains 78.0% worst-group and 80.0% average accuracy, surpassing ERM (62.1%/72.9%) and Post-hoc CBM (71.4%/80.4%). The method approaches the human-annotation CBM (85.0% worst-group on Waterbirds) and the Group DRO oracle (91.4%) while requiring no human annotation effort.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The LLaVA-based CBM decisively outperforms both CLIP-based CBM baselines across all three datasets: on Waterbirds it improves worst-group accuracy from 55.0% (Label-free CBM) and 31.6% (Post-hoc CBM) to 83.2%, and on ImageNet-Opener from 82.5% and 45.0% to 90.0%. Against the non-interpretability baselines, it beats DISC [38] on Metashifts (78.0% vs. 73.5% worst-group) and [40] on ImageNet-Opener (90.0% vs. 68.0%). Compared to the Group DRO oracle [31], the method wins on ImageNet-Opener (90.0% vs. 76.0%) and Metashifts (78.0% vs. 66.0%) but trails on Waterbirds (83.2% vs. 91.4%), where Group DRO benefits from explicit group annotations. Against the human-annotation CBM [18], the gap is small (83.2% vs. 85.0% worst-group on Waterbirds) while eliminating the need for costly manual labeling. The paper attributes the large margin over CLIP-based CBMs to inherent biases in CLIP's pre-training that leak spurious information into soft concept representations, a problem avoided by the hard binary annotations produced by the MLLM pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Annotation-based CBM framework of Koh et al. [18], which uses a two-stage architecture (concept model + classifier) with binary concept annotations, and departs from the more recent CLIP-based CBMs (Label-free CBM [27], Post-hoc CBM [46]) that replace trained concept models with CLIP text-encoder projections. The key conceptual departure is the identification that CLIP-based CBMs inherit spurious-correlation biases from CLIP's pre-training data, even when concept sets are carefully curated to exclude spurious attributes, motivating a return to hard (binary) CBMs. The paper's practical novelty lies in using MLLMs (LLaVA) and LLMs (GPT-3) to automate the concept-pool curation, spurious-correlation detection, and binary annotation steps that previously required substantial human labor, and in introducing an optional refinement chain of vision foundation models (BLIP-2, Grounding DINO, SAM) to correct MLLM annotation errors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CLIP-based CBMs leak spurious-correlation information through their soft concept representations—even with spurious-free concept sets—challenges a common assumption in the CBM literature and suggests that the choice of concept-model architecture (hard vs. soft) matters as much as concept selection for robustness. The practical pipeline reduces CBM construction cost to near-zero human effort, making interpretable, bias-aware classification more accessible. However, the LLaVA annotation quality remains notably below human level (F1 ≈ 0.31 on Waterbirds), the refinement step is demonstrated on only two attributes in one dataset, and all experiments are on small-scale benchmarks (≤200 classes), leaving open whether the approach scales to large, diverse datasets or whether the MLLM annotation gap can be closed without human intervention.
