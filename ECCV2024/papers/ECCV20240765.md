## Problem

Generative 3D models still lack the geometric and textural fidelity of state-of-the-art image and video generative models, partly because large-scale high-quality 3D training data is scarce and grid-based 3D representations cap achievable detail. Prior 3D super-resolution work relies on image-based upsampling priors applied frame-by-frame, which introduces temporal inconsistencies that blur the final 3D reconstruction. The paper asks whether pre-trained video upsampling models, which inherently enforce temporal coherence, can be directly repurposed for 3D super-resolution without requiring large 3D-specific training sets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240765/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any coarse 3D representation of a static scene—Gaussian Splats, NeRF, low-poly meshes, text-to-3D generated objects, or low-res posed RGB images—and renders it into a video from N smooth camera trajectories with known poses (no SfM needed). The video is upsampled 4× (e.g., 64×64 → 256×256 per frame), and the final output is a high-resolution 3D Gaussian Splat (or NeRF/TensoRF on the Blender synthetic benchmark). Camera trajectories are sampled as B-Spline curves with sinusoidal perturbation, and the initial rendering resolution must be high enough that fidelity is bottlenecked by the coarse 3D input rather than pixel count.

## Method

SUPERGAUSSIAN is a two-stage pipeline: (1) the coarse 3D input is rendered into a low-resolution video along sampled trajectories, then upsampled by a pre-trained generative video upsampler (VideoGigaGAN, built on GigaGAN with BasicVSR++ temporal propagation layers) that is optionally fine-tuned on domain-specific degradation pairs (low-res Gaussian Splat renderings vs. original MVImgNet videos) using Charbonnier, LPIPS, and GAN losses; (2) the upsampled video is consolidated into a 3D-consistent representation via standard 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization (2k steps, L1 + SSIM loss) with known camera poses. The key differentiator over image-based 3D SR is that the video upsampler's temporal feature extraction and propagation enforce cross-frame consistency, avoiding the per-frame hallucination mismatches that cause blurriness in iterative image-upsample-then-reconstruct baselines. The framework is fully modular—any video upsampler or 3D representation can be swapped in—and the fine-tuning step specifically models the stripy/blob-like artifacts characteristic of low-res Gaussian Splat renderings that standard video degradation models do not capture.

## Results

On MVImgNet (×4 upsampling of low-res Gaussian Splats), SUPERGAUSSIAN achieves LPIPS 0.1290, NIQE 6.80, FID 24.32, and IS 11.69, outperforming all baselines across every perceptual metric. On the Blender-synthetic dataset (×4 upsampling from 200×200 to 800×800), the method achieves the best LPIPS (0.067) while matching NeRF-SR on PSNR (28.44 vs 28.46) and SSIM (0.923 vs 0.921), though trailing FastSR-NeRF on PSNR (28.44 vs 30.47). The full pipeline (video upsampling + Gaussian Splat optimization) completes in ~141 seconds on average, making it the most efficient method compared to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUPERGAUSSIAN consistently outperforms all four baselines on MVImgNet. Against Instruct-G2G (image-based iterative upsampling with Gaussian Splats), it reduces LPIPS from 0.1867 to 0.1290 (−31%) and FID from 32.56 to 24.32 (−25%). Against Super-NeRF (image upsampling with consistency enforcement), the gap is even larger: LPIPS drops from 0.2204 to 0.1290 and FID from 37.54 to 24.32. The closest competitor is the Pre-hoc image baseline (upsample all frames independently, then optimize 3D), which SUPERGAUSSIAN still beats on every metric (LPIPS 0.1290 vs 0.1524, FID 24.32 vs 27.04), confirming that video priors yield better temporal consistency than per-frame image upsampling. On Blender-synthetic, SUPERGAUSSIAN achieves the best LPIPS (0.067) but trails FastSR-NeRF on PSNR (28.44 vs 30.47) and SSIM (0.923 vs 0.944); the authors attribute this to their method generating novel high-frequency details that reference-based metrics penalize, while visually the results show richer generative detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D super-resolution line of work (Super-NeRF, NeRF-SR, FastSR-NeRF, Instruct-NeRF2NeRF) by replacing the image-based upsampling prior with a video-based one (VideoGigaGAN), arguing that temporal consistency in the upsampling step translates directly to 3D consistency after reconstruction. It also departs from the NeRF-centric focus of prior methods by being representation-agnostic, operating on any coarse 3D input (Gaussian Splats, NeRFs, meshes, text-to-3D outputs) through a common intermediate video representation. The 3D consolidation step leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting as the output format, chosen for its object-centric nature and rendering efficiency, though the framework is modular and compatible with other representations like TensoRF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is sidestepping the chronic shortage of large, high-quality 3D training datasets by repurposing video models already trained on billions of video samples, making 3D super-resolution accessible without category-specific training. The modular design means the video upsampler can be swapped for future, stronger video models (e.g., diffusion-based upsamplers) without redesigning the pipeline. However, the method cannot recover missing or occluded geometry from insufficient viewpoint coverage, and the authors note that combining generative completion with super-resolution would sacrifice the universality that makes the approach attractive. An open question is whether the video-prior advantage persists as video models become more 3D-aware natively, potentially making the explicit 3D consolidation step redundant.
