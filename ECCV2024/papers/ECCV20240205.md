## Problem

Existing text-to-image generation methods either require per-object fine-tuning (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) or support only a single object with a fixed text-and-image modality combination in a zero-shot setting. The challenge of generating images from multi-object, multi-modal prompts—combining text, coordinates, and object-level images in arbitrary combinations—without any model tuning remains unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240205/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

MultiGen accepts any flexible combination of three modalities per object: a text description, 4-dimensional bounding-box coordinates, and a cropped object-level RGB image, supporting up to 15 objects per scene. At inference, missing modalities are automatically filled in by auxiliary generative models, so the user can provide as little as a plain text prompt or as much as full multi-object multi-modal specifications. The output is a single generated image produced by a latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion 1.5 backbone) at standard resolutions.

## Method

The core mechanism is the 'augmented token': for each detected object, the paper fuses an object-level text embedding (CLIP ViT-L/14), a global image feature (SigLIP ViT-SO400M-384, mapped through a 2-layer MLP), and 4D coordinates (mapped through a separate 2-layer MLP) into a single vector that is appended to the standard text embedding sequence as extra conditioning for the diffusion model—requiring no architectural modification to the pre-trained U-Net. Training uses ~100M image-text pairs with Grounding DINO for offline object detection (NMS threshold 0.7, max 15 objects). To handle missing modalities at inference, two separate ~1.2B-parameter Transformer-based diffusion models are trained: a coordinate model that generates bounding boxes conditioned on global text and object-level text, and a feature model that generates image embeddings conditioned on text and coordinates, both using learnable position embeddings to enforce one-to-one object correspondence.

## Results

On the DreamBooth benchmark (30 subjects, 25 prompt templates, 3000 generated images), MultiGen achieves the best zero-shot performance with DINO 0.615, CLIP-I 0.780, and CLIP-T 0.308, surpassing all other zero-shot methods. On MS-COCO 2014 validation (30K captions), MultiGen achieves a zero-shot FID of 9.84, slightly improving over the base Stable Diffusion 1.5 model (9.93), though remaining well behind the strongest text-to-image systems like Imagen (7.27) and Parti (7.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot methods, MultiGen outperforms BLIP-Diffusion (0.594/0.779/0.300) and Re-Imagen (0.600/0.740/0.270) on all three DreamBooth metrics, with the largest margin over Re-Imagen on CLIP-I (+0.040) and CLIP-T (+0.038). MultiGen also surpasses the tuning-based Textual Inversion (0.569/0.780/0.255) without any per-object optimization, gaining +0.046 DINO and +0.053 CLIP-T. However, MultiGen still trails the tuning-based DreamBooth (0.668/0.803/0.305) and BLIP-Diffusion (0.670/0.805/0.302) on subject fidelity (DINO and CLIP-I) by roughly 0.05, while matching or slightly exceeding them on prompt fidelity (CLIP-T: 0.308 vs. 0.305/0.302). On MS-COCO text-to-image, MultiGen (FID 9.84) marginally beats the SD 1.5 base model (9.93) but is substantially outperformed by Imagen (7.27) and Parti (7.23). Qualitatively, MultiGen outperforms InstructPix2Pix, Kosmos-G, and Emu2 on multi-object generation, preserving all prompted objects and blending backgrounds more coherently.

## Relation to Prior Work

MultiGen builds directly on Stable Diffusion 1.5's latent diffusion architecture, appending per-object 'augmented tokens' (fusing text, coordinate, and image embeddings) to the standard text conditioning without modifying the diffusion backbone. It departs from prior zero-shot multi-modal generation methods (BLIP-Diffusion, Re-Imagen, KNN-Diffusion) that are limited to single-object constraints and fixed text+image modality pairs, and from tuning-based personalization (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) that require per-object optimization. The key conceptual departure is the use of two auxiliary Transformer-based generative models (coordinate and feature models) to synthesize missing modalities from text alone, enabling arbitrary modality combinations at inference time—a flexibility absent in prior approaches like Kosmos-G or Emu2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MultiGen provides a practical, training-free pipeline for multi-object image generation where users can mix text, bounding-box coordinates, and reference images per object, addressing a real gap in current text-to-image tools. The approach's reliance on ~100M images processed offline with Grounding DINO and two 1.2B-parameter auxiliary models raises questions about scalability to very large object counts and whether the generative fillers degrade gracefully as prompt complexity grows. Open questions include whether the augmented-token formulation extends to video or 3D generation, whether the coordinate/feature models can be distilled to smaller sizes, and how the method performs when detection quality (used for training data) is poor.
