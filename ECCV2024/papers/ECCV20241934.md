## Problem

State-of-the-art synthetic image detection methods that leverage foundation model features (e.g., CLIP) extract representations only from the final layer, which primarily encodes high-level visual semantics. However, fine-grained low-level details are more discriminative for distinguishing synthetic from real images, and intermediate Transformer layers that carry such information have not been explored for this task. This paper asks whether leveraging representations from intermediate encoder-blocks can substantially improve detection generalization across diverse generative models.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images that are center-cropped to 224×224 without resizing (to preserve synthetic traces) and outputs a single real/fake probability score. During training, images undergo Gaussian blurring and JPEG compression (each with 0.5 probability), random cropping, and random horizontal flip; at test time only center-cropping is applied. The frozen CLIP ViT encoder processes each image into a sequence of patches, and the method extracts the CLS token from every one of the n Transformer blocks rather than just the final output.

## Method

RINE concatenates the CLS tokens from all n intermediate Transformer blocks of a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder into a representation matrix K ∈ R^{b×n×d}, then projects it through a lightweight MLP (q layers, d′ dimensions, with dropout 0.5). A Trainable Importance Estimator (TIE) module maintains a learnable n×d′ score matrix A, applies softmax across blocks for each feature dimension, and computes a weighted sum across the block axis to produce one d′-dimensional feature vector per image. A second projection network and a two-layer classification head map this to the final logit. Training combines binary cross-entropy with a supervised contrastive loss (SupContrast) weighted by a tunable factor ξ, and the entire trainable portion (6.3M parameters for the 4-class model) converges in a single epoch (~8 minutes on one RTX 3090 Ti) using only ProGAN-generated training data.

## Results

RINE (4-class, ViT-L/14) achieves 91.5% average ACC and 98.8% average AP across 20 test datasets, surpassing the state-of-the-art (UFD) by +10.6% ACC and +4.5% AP. The 1-class model (trained on a single ProGAN object class) still outperforms all baselines by +9.4% ACC and +4.3% AP, while requiring only 10.52M parameters and ~2 minutes of training. The best model (4-class) uses 6.32M learnable parameters and trains for just 1 epoch (~8 minutes) on a single RTX 3090 Ti. RINE obtains the best ACC on 14/20 datasets and best AP on 15/20, with the largest single-dataset gain of +16.9% ACC on SAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RINE outperforms all six baselines on average across both ACC and AP. Against the closest competitor UFD [41] (linear probing on CLIP last-layer features), RINE (4-class) gains +10.6% ACC (91.5% vs 80.9%) and +4.5% AP (98.8% vs 94.3%), with the largest per-dataset ACC gap on SAN (+16.9%). Against LGrad [49] (gradient-based detection), RINE improves by +11.8% ACC and +13.3% AP, showing that intermediate CLIP representations capture forgery cues that gradient features miss. Against DMID [12] (ResNet without first-layer downsampling), RINE leads by +13.9% ACC and +4.9% AP, particularly on Diffusion and Glide datasets where DMID drops to near-chance. Against Wang [52] (ResNet-50 with augmentation), the gap is +21.3% ACC, and against Patch-Forensics [6] (patch-level Xception), it is +35.7% ACC. FrePGAN [26] is only comparable on GAN datasets (where it reaches 99.0% on ProGAN) but does not report scores on Diffusion, Glide, or DALL-E datasets, making a full comparison impossible.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends UFD [41], which demonstrated that linear probing on CLIP's final-layer features yields strong synthetic image detection with minimal training. The key departure is replacing the single last-layer representation with concatenated CLS tokens from all intermediate Transformer blocks, motivated by the observation that low-level visual information (encoded in shallow layers) is more discriminative for forgery than high-level semantics. The Trainable Importance Estimator (TIE) module, which learns per-block weighting, is a novel architectural addition not present in prior deep-layer aggregation approaches [53,27], which concatenate outputs during CNN training rather than processing frozen foundation-model features. The method also builds on the broader SID literature (Wang et al. [52], LGrad [49], DMID [12]) by showing that foundation-model intermediate representations outperform hand-crafted frequency or gradient-based features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a +10.6% average accuracy improvement over SOTA across 20 diverse test datasets, achieved with only 6.3M trainable parameters and ~8 minutes of training, makes high-quality synthetic image detection accessible without large compute budgets. However, the task remains far from solved — DALL-E 3 images are still nearly undetectable (21.1% ACC with ProGAN training), and combined social-media perturbations degrade performance substantially. Open questions include whether the intermediate-representation advantage persists as generative models evolve, and whether the approach can be extended to video or multimodal synthetic content detection.
