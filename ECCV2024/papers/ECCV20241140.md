## Problem

Radiology images share highly similar anatomical content with abnormalities occupying only small regions, causing report generation models to learn spurious and entangled visual representations that produce misdiagnostic reports. Existing approaches relying on pseudo-labeled lesion detectors introduce weakly supervised signals that can misalign, and prior counterfactual methods only integrate CEs into the decision process without conveying factual or counterfactual information to the generation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241140/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single front-view chest X-ray image as input, processed by a ViT-S encoder into 16×16 patches, and produces a free-text radiology diagnostic report via autoregressive GPT-2 Medium generation. During training, each image is paired with its ground-truth report and a diagnosis label (one of 14 CheXPert terminologies or 'No Finding'), and a counterfactual image is constructed by swapping a single patch with a semantically similar negative image of a different diagnosis. At inference, the counterfactual module is discarded and only the visual embeddings concatenated with a refined prompt are fed to the LLM.

## Method

CoFE introduces a counterfactual generation module that iteratively swaps patches between a factual image and a negative image (selected via maximum BLEU score among candidates with a different diagnosis label) until a frozen discriminator's predicted diagnosis shifts, thereby identifying the critical region. A novel counterfactual contrastive loss (L_CF) then pushes the factual image embedding away from the counterfactual image embedding relative to the paired text embedding, forcing the ViT-S encoder to learn non-spurious visual representations. A learnable prompt encoding the factual label, counterfactual label, critical patch index, and a list of diagnostic entities is prepended to the visual tokens to efficiently fine-tune GPT-2 Medium, eliciting its embedded medical knowledge. The full framework is trained end-to-end with joint optimization of image-report contrastive loss, report generation cross-entropy, and the counterfactual loss.

## Results

On IU-Xray, CoFE achieves a CIDEr score of 0.731, substantially outperforming the next-best method DCL (0.586) by 24.7%, and also leads on ROUGE-L (0.438) and METEOR (0.202). On MIMIC-CXR, CoFE attains the highest ROUGE-L (0.304) and METEOR (0.176) among all compared methods, with a CIDEr of 0.453. For clinical correctness on MIMIC-CXR, CoFE achieves the best F1-score of 0.405 (Precision 0.489, Recall 0.370), surpassing DCL (F1 0.373) and MKSG (F1 0.371).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoFE outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of NLG metrics across both datasets. On IU-Xray, the largest gap is against DCL [23] (CIDEr 0.586 → 0.731, +24.7%) and R2Gen [4] (CIDEr 0.398 → 0.731, +83.7%); CoFE also beats PPKED [27], MGSK [48], KERP [20], MKG [52], and CMCL [26] on CIDEr, ROUGE-L, and METEOR, though MGSK [48] narrowly edges CoFE on BLEU-4 (0.178 vs. 0.175). On MIMIC-CXR, CoFE leads on ROUGE-L (0.304 vs. 0.284 for DCL/PPKED/MGSK) and METEOR (0.176 vs. 0.150 for DCL), while its CIDEr (0.453) and BLEU-4 (0.125) trail RGRG (not in the formal comparison set) but exceed all listed baselines. For clinical efficacy on MIMIC-CXR, CoFE achieves F1 0.405, beating DCL [23] (0.373), R2Gen [4] (0.276), and MKSG [48] (0.371) by 3.2, 12.9, and 3.4 F1 points respectively. The contrastive-learning baselines DCL [23] and CMCL [26] are the closest competitors, but CoFE's counterfactual contrastive loss and learnable prompt provide consistent gains of 5–15% in CIDEr and 2–5% in ROUGE-L over these methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoFE repurposes counterfactual explanations—previously employed in medical imaging primarily for post-hoc interpretability (Tanyel et al.) or as a data augmentation signal (CPL, CGN)—as an explicit training mechanism for learning non-spurious visual representations in radiology report generation. It extends the contrastive learning paradigm of DCL [23] by introducing a counterfactual contrastive loss that contrasts factual and patch-swapped images, and it departs from Tanida et al. [36], who rely on a separately pre-trained lesion detector with pseudo labels, by deriving critical regions directly through the iterative patch-exchange CE process without external detectors. The learnable prompt, which encodes both factual and counterfactual diagnostic content, extends standard prompt tuning (CoOp, PTuning v2) by embedding structured counterfactual reasoning into the conditioning signal for a GPT-2 decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized failure mode in RRG—models latching onto spurious anatomical context rather than diagnostic abnormalities—by providing a self-supervised, annotation-free mechanism to identify and emphasize critical regions. The counterfactual-as-training-signal framing could generalize beyond chest X-rays to other modalities where abnormalities occupy small image regions, though the patch-swapping procedure is inherently 2D and anatomy-specific. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LLMs (the paper uses GPT-2 Medium at 355M parameters), whether the CE process remains effective for 3D volumes or multi-organ imaging, and whether the gains over strong contrastive baselines like DCL persist as model scale increases.
