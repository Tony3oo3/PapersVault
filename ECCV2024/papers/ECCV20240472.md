## Problem

Mainstream unsupervised outlier detection methods focus on learning discriminative score functions but produce no predicted labels, preventing them from leveraging the target dataset's self-supervision (e.g., uncontaminated inlier manifold learning or outlier-based feature refinement). The paper argues that thresholding is actually more critical than scoring, since finding a single optimal threshold on a non-separable score distribution is ill-posed, and proposes that generating two separate thresholds (for inliers and outliers) can unlock significant improvements to existing detectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240472/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabelled target dataset of images (no train/test split required) with features extracted via a pre-trained network (ResNet-50 or CLIP for RGB datasets, raw pixels for grayscale like MNIST) and produces two thresholds—an inlier threshold φ_in and an outlier threshold φ_out—along with predicted inlier/outlier labels and an enhanced outlier score function. The thresholding module itself is training-free and runs in under 1 second on CPU for 10,000 samples, while the integrated scoring step (e.g., DeepSVDD fitting) uses only the predicted inliers, reducing fitting data size and improving both accuracy and speed.

## Method

Multi-T operates in three stages on an initial outlier score function (LVAD-S: Ergodic-set-normalized distance to the dataset mean). Stage 1 identifies uncontaminated inliers via an iterative loop that sort-transforms the score distribution, fits a linear regressor to the sorted scores, and applies the 3-sigma rule to progressively remove outliers until convergence, yielding the inlier threshold. Stage 2 estimates the outlier ratio by computing the Pearson correlation between rank orderings from Shell Normalization (outlier-dependent) and Ergodic-set Normalization (outlier-ratio-invariant), then selects among three candidate outlier thresholds based on the similarity value. Stage 3 integrates the results: predicted inliers are used to fit the inlier manifold (e.g., DeepSVDD hypersphere) while predicted outliers provide the reference vector for Shell Normalization, producing a refined distance-based score that is applied to the full dataset.

## Results

Multi-T achieves SOTA AUC across all evaluated benchmarks: 0.968 (STL-10/ResNet), 0.989 (STL-10/CLIP), 0.895 (CIFAR-10/ResNet), 0.957 (CIFAR-10/CLIP), 0.938 (CIFAR-100/ResNet), 0.974 (MIT-Places/CLIP), and 0.897 (MNIST). When integrated with DeepSVDD, Multi-T yields a 48.7% AUC improvement on STL-10 (0.622 → 0.925) and 60.9% on CIFAR-10 (0.509 → 0.819). The method is also extremely efficient, processing 10,000 ResNet-50 samples in 1.2 seconds on CPU, compared to 645.2 s for LVAD and 121.6 s for RSRAE on GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-T (standalone) outperforms all 21 compared baselines on every dataset and feature extractor in Table 2. On STL-10 with ResNet, it leads LVAD (0.968 vs 0.954), RSRAE (0.968 vs 0.962), and ECOD (0.968 vs 0.907); with CLIP it reaches 0.989, surpassing ECOD (0.981) and LVAD (0.968). On the more challenging CIFAR-10/100, Multi-T beats LVAD by 3.5 pp (0.895 vs 0.860) and 1.7 pp (0.938 vs 0.921) respectively, and outperforms Shell-Re. by 13.5 pp on CIFAR-10/ResNet. The most dramatic gains come from integrating Multi-T with weaker detectors: DeepSVDD improves by 48.7% AUC on STL-10 (0.622→0.925) and 60.9% on CIFAR-10 (0.509→0.819), while OCSVM gains 3.24% on STL-10 and 7.76% on CIFAR-10. In thresholding-specific evaluation (Table 1), Multi-T achieves balanced F₀.₁=0.917 and F₁₀=0.980, whereas single-threshold methods that maximize one metric sacrifice the other (e.g., best F₀.₁=0.976 but F₁₀=0.452). In efficiency, Multi-T runs in 1.2 s on CPU for 10,000 samples, roughly 540× faster than LVAD (645.2 s) and 100× faster than RSRAE (121.6 s on GPU).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LVAD (Lin et al., ECCV 2022) as its initial outlier score function and on Shell Theory (Lin et al., IEEE TPAMI 2021) for the Shell Normalization step that refines feature representations using predicted outliers. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant UOD paradigm of learning increasingly complex discriminative score functions by arguing that the thresholding step—typically treated as a trivial post-hoc decision boundary—is actually the more critical and underexplored component. The Multi-T module unifies two previously separate paradigms (inlier-manifold learning and outlier exposure) by generating two thresholds simultaneously, bridging a gap that single-thresholding methods (statistical, kernel-based, curve-based) leave open due to the inevitable overlap in score distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a training-free, 1.2-second module can boost weak detectors like DeepSVDD by ~49% AUC on STL-10 and push a naive distance-to-mean baseline to SOTA, offering a low-cost plug-in for existing pipelines. The conceptual reframing—that self-supervision via thresholding matters more than score-function complexity—opens a path for iterative refinement of UOD methods that were previously stuck in single-pass, non-iterative designs. However, the method's reliance on the initial score function's ranking quality, its assumption of a single normal class, and its reduced effectiveness on very small datasets or multi-modal inlier distributions remain open limitations that constrain broader applicability.
