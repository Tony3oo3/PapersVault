## Problem

Existing multi-view crowd localization methods are trained with Gaussian density map MSE supervision, which fails in dense crowd regions where overlapping kernels smooth away local peaks and make individual person locations unrecoverable. Although optimal transport (OT) point supervision has proven effective for single-image crowd localization, it has not been adapted to the multi-view setting, where projection artifacts along view rays and varying object-to-camera distances introduce direction-dependent and distance-dependent errors that a standard Euclidean OT cost cannot address.

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized multi-camera RGB images (native 1920×1080, resized to 1280×720) along with camera calibration parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics) that define each camera's view ray and object-to-camera distance on the ground plane. It outputs a ground-plane crowd density map at 0.1 m per pixel resolution (e.g., 120×360 for Wildtrack, 160×250 for MultiviewX), from which individual person locations are extracted via local non-maximum suppression. On CVCS, 5 views are randomly sampled per iteration during training and 21 random 5-view subsets are averaged during evaluation.

## Method

The proposed M-MVOT loss replaces the Euclidean distance in the unbalanced optimal transport cost matrix with a Mahalanobis distance whose covariance matrix is manually designed from camera geometry: the ellipse's short axis aligns with the view-ray direction (σ₁² < σ₂²) to penalize deviations along the ray more heavily, counteracting streaking artifacts from the 3D-to-2D projection step. The object-to-camera distance further modulates the ellipse extents via σ₂² = exp(α · MinMaxNorm(d_cam)), so that farther points receive stronger penalties in all directions while retaining the anisotropic view-ray weighting. For the multi-view extension, each ground-truth point's transport cost is computed exclusively under its closest camera (a binary indicator selects the minimum-distance view), avoiding the complexity of soft-weighted multi-camera fusion. The full loss is an entropic-regularized unbalanced OT objective solved via Sinkhorn iteration, and it is applied on top of the MVDet or MVDeTr architecture (replacing their original density-map MSE loss) while retaining auxiliary 2D per-view density losses.

## Results

On the challenging cross-scene CVCS dataset, M-MVOT achieves 43.5% MODA and 64.9% F1, substantially outperforming the previous best method SHOT (31.7% MODA, 49.6% F1) by 11.8 and 15.3 points respectively, driven primarily by a much higher recall (52.3% vs. 33.6%). On the single-scene MultiviewX dataset, M-MVOT reaches 96.7% MODA and 98.3% F1, surpassing 3DROM (95.0% MODA, 97.5% F1) and MVDeTr (93.7% MODA, 97.8% F1). On Wildtrack, M-MVOT achieves 92.1% MODA and 96.1% F1, slightly behind 3DROM (93.5% MODA, 96.7% F1) but with a notably higher MODP (81.3 vs. 75.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CVCS, M-MVOT decisively outperforms all density-map-based baselines: it gains +11.8 MODA and +15.3 F1 over SHOT, +18.6 MODA and +24.5 F1 over MVDeTr, and +23.4 MODA and +27.9 F1 over MVDet, primarily through a large recall improvement (52.3% vs. 25–34%). MVDeTr retains the highest MODP (79.6) and Precision (98.1) on CVCS, reflecting its conservative detection strategy, but its low recall (25.4%) limits overall F1. On MultiviewX, M-MVOT achieves the best MODA (96.7) and F1 (98.3), edging out 3DROM (95.0, 97.5) and MVDeTr (93.7, 97.8). On Wildtrack, 3DROM leads in MODA (93.5 vs. 92.1) and F1 (96.7 vs. 96.1), likely benefiting from its 3D occlusion data augmentation on this small single-scene dataset, while M-MVOT compensates with a higher MODP (81.3 vs. 75.9). The Euclidean-distance variant E-MVOT already surpasses all prior methods on CVCS and MultiviewX, confirming that the OT formulation itself is the key gain, with the Mahalanobis distance providing a further small but consistent improvement (+0.4 MODA, +0.4 F1 on CVCS; +0.4 MODA, +0.2 F1 on MultiviewX).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unbalanced optimal transport (UOT) loss for crowd localization, established in single-image settings by Wan et al. (GL, CVPR 2021) and Ma et al. (AAAI 2021), to the multi-view crowd localization task for the first time. All prior multi-view crowd localization methods (MVDet, SHOT, MVDeTr, 3DROM) rely on Gaussian density map MSE supervision, which the authors identify as fundamentally limited in dense regions where local peaks are smoothed away. The key methodological departure is replacing the Euclidean transport cost with a Mahalanobis distance whose elliptical iso-contours are oriented by the view-ray direction and scaled by object-to-camera distance, directly addressing projection streaking artifacts unique to the multi-view ground-plane setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that point-supervision via optimal transport is a viable and superior training paradigm for multi-view crowd localization, closing a gap that existed between single-view and multi-view methods. However, the practical gains from the Mahalanobis-specific design (M-MVOT over E-MVOT) are modest (e.g., F1 64.9 vs 64.5 on CVCS), suggesting the bulk of the improvement comes from switching to OT rather than the geometric cost shaping. Open questions include whether the manually designed covariance matrix can be replaced by a learnable one with appropriate priors, how the method scales to very large cross-scene deployments, and whether the binary closest-camera selection strategy is optimal compared to learned soft fusion.
