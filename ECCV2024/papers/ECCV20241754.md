## Problem

Existing 3D semi-supervised learning methods, including the SOTA ConFid, fail to fully utilize all unlabelled point cloud samples: FixMatch uses only ~25% of unlabelled data, ConFid ~76%, and high-confidence pseudo-labels contribute minimally to learning due to their already-low loss. No prior 3D SSL framework addresses the combined issue of under-utilized easy samples, discarded low-confidence samples, and the remaining middle-confidence samples that fall outside pseudo-label-based learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds of shape m×3, split into a small labelled set (n samples with class labels) and a large unlabelled set (N samples, n≪N). Each sample is processed through both weak augmentations (rotation, random scale) and strong augmentations (jitter, translation, rotation, random scale), constrained to preserve geometric invariance of point clouds. The model outputs a class prediction per point cloud, evaluated by overall accuracy and mean per-class accuracy on ModelNet40 (40 classes) and ScanObjectNN (15 classes).

## Method

AllMatch builds on FixMatch's entropy-minimization pseudo-labeling framework (using PointTransformer as encoder) and adds three modules to ensure every unlabelled sample contributes to learning. (1) Adaptive Hard Augmentation (AHA) identifies 'easy' samples via an exponential moving average of per-sample loss thresholded by OTSU, then applies additional hard augmentations (extra scaling, rotation, translation, jitter) to these high-confidence, low-loss samples so they continue to provide learning signal. (2) Inverse Learning exploits the observation that correct classes fall within the top-k predictions; it assigns inverse pseudo-labels to all classes ranked below k, penalizing the model for predicting those classes on strongly augmented samples. (3) A contrastive learning module applies an unsupervised NT-Xent loss on weak/strong augmented pairs of all unlabelled samples (reusing the same augmented pairs as the pseudo-label loss, adding no extra compute) and a supervised contrastive loss on the labelled set. The total loss is a weighted sum of supervised cross-entropy, unsupervised pseudo-label cross-entropy, supervised contrastive, unsupervised contrastive, and inverse losses, with AHA activated only after a warm-up period to avoid destabilizing early training.

## Results

On ScanObjectNN with only 1% labelled data, AllMatch achieves 49.4% overall accuracy and 47.4% mean accuracy, surpassing the previous SOTA (ConFid-Match) by 11.2% and 14.67% respectively. On ModelNet40 with 2% labelled data, AllMatch reaches 84.3% overall accuracy, outperforming ConFid-Match (73.8%) by 10.48%. Remarkably, with just 10% labelled data on ModelNet40, AllMatch attains 90.2% overall accuracy, nearly matching the fully supervised performance of 90.75% achieved with 100% labelled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AllMatch outperforms all six baselines across both datasets and all labelled set sizes, with the largest margins observed at the smallest labelled sets. Against the previous SOTA ConFid-Match [8], AllMatch gains 11.2% overall accuracy on ScanObjectNN (1% labels: 49.4% vs 38.2%) and 10.48% on ModelNet40 (2% labels: 84.3% vs 73.8%). The image-domain methods FixMatch [21] and FlexMatch [28], when adapted to point clouds, trail AllMatch by 15.9% and 15.2% overall accuracy on ScanObjectNN at 1% labels, respectively. Dash [27] and the basic pseudo-label method PL [14] are further behind, achieving only 35.1% and 31.2% on ScanObjectNN (1%), while the PCT encoder baseline [10] without any SSL reaches 32.1%. The consistent pattern across all comparisons is that AllMatch's advantage grows as the labelled set shrinks, confirming its superior utilization of unlabelled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

AllMatch is built directly on the FixMatch pseudo-labeling framework (weak/strong augmentation with confidence thresholding) and extends it with three modules drawn from the image-domain SSL literature: adaptive hard augmentation for easy samples (from Gui et al., CVPR 2023), inverse learning via top-k exclusion (from Chen et al., CVPR 2023), and contrastive learning. The key departure from prior 3D SSL work (ConFid, FixMatch-adapted-to-3D) is the explicit goal of utilizing *all* unlabelled samples rather than a confidence-filtered subset, which prior methods achieve only partially (25–76% utilization). The contribution is thus primarily one of systematic combination and 3D-specific adaptation of known image-domain techniques, rather than a fundamentally new learning principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that 10% labelled data achieves near-fully-supervised performance (90.19% vs 90.75%) is directly relevant to practitioners facing expensive 3D annotation pipelines, and the halving of training epochs (250 vs 500) adds a meaningful efficiency gain. However, the approach is validated on only two classification datasets with a single encoder (PointTransformer), and the authors acknowledge that the augmentation space and the choice of contrastive loss as the self-supervised backbone remain under-explored. Open questions include whether the three-module combination generalizes to 3D segmentation or detection tasks, whether the OTSU-based easy/hard split is robust across architectures, and whether the inverse learning signal degrades when class imbalance is severe.
