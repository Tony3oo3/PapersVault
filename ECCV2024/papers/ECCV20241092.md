## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods that incorporate Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) scene information fail to significantly outperform single-source strategies because the visual and trajectory representations are not effectively aligned. Pre-training a BEV encoder from scratch is infeasible due to limited scene data in trajectory datasets, and without a principled cross-modal alignment mechanism, the scene context contributes little to prediction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241092/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 200×200 BEV scene crop centered on the agent's current position, historical trajectory coordinates, and text prompts describing surrounding types (pavement, meadow, building, road). The trajectory is rendered as a colored Gaussian-shaped line directly on the BEV image, and the text input follows a Table-to-Text format combining attribute words with trajectory tokens and learnable soft prompts. Output is a sequence of future pixel coordinates retrieved via top-k nearest-neighbor search from a pre-defined 2D pixel dictionary, supporting both deterministic (k=1) and stochastic (k=5) decoding.

## Method

TrajPrompt freezes a pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone and introduces only lightweight learnable soft prompts (0.17M parameters) to bridge trajectory and vision-language representations. The key mechanism draws a colored line (blue, Gaussian-shaped) on the BEV scene as a hard visual prompt, paired with text prompts describing local surroundings, so that CLIP's attention localizes to the trajectory region. A CoCoOp-inspired visual-prompt generator produces a conditional image prompt from the raw scene features. Training jointly optimizes three objectives: an image-text contrastive loss aligning the colored-line image with the T2T sequence embedding, a KL-divergence alignment loss matching surrounding-type similarity distributions, and a trajectory modeling loss regressing future pixel embeddings. Decoding uses autoregressive retrieval from a 2D positional-embedding pixel dictionary rather than direct coordinate regression, ensuring each step's input remains a recognizable representation.

## Results

On the Stanford Drone Dataset (SDD), TrajPrompt achieves ADE 3.78 and FDE 6.81 (in pixels, 20 samples), outperforming the best prior method with image information (TDOR: ADE 6.77, FDE 10.46) by 42% and 35% respectively. On DroneCrowd, TrajPrompt reaches ADE 3.72 and FDE 7.04, improving over TDOR (ADE 6.92, FDE 11.23) by 46% and 37%. The method uses only 0.17M learnable parameters (frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone), which is 4× fewer than TDOR (0.85M) and 96× fewer than SOPHIE (26.23M), while also achieving the lowest inference cost (1.87 GFLOPs, 15.24 ms) among compared generative models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajPrompt outperforms all ten baselines on both SDD and DroneCrowd. Against the strongest image-based competitor TDOR [14], it reduces ADE from 6.77 to 3.78 (−42%) and FDE from 10.46 to 6.81 (−35%) on SDD, and from 6.92 to 3.72 (−46%) and 11.23 to 7.04 (−37%) on DroneCrowd. Compared to the best trajectory-only method NSP-SFM [46] (ADE 6.52, FDE 10.61 on SDD), the gain is 42% on ADE. Against PreTraM [43], which uses a similar contrastive pre-training strategy but trains the BEV encoder from scratch, TrajPrompt improves ADE from 7.27 to 3.72 on DroneCrowd. In terms of efficiency, TrajPrompt uses 0.17M learnable parameters versus 0.85M for TDOR, 1.64M for Y-net [26], and 26.23M for SOPHIE [32], while also running 3× faster than FlowChain [25] (15.24 ms vs. 47.25 ms) and 30× faster than Y-net (15.24 ms vs. 453.38 ms). The only method with fewer parameters is TUTR [35] (0.11M), but TUTR does not use image information and achieves substantially worse accuracy (ADE 7.76 vs. 3.78 on SDD).

## Relation to Prior Work

TrajPrompt sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) trajectory prediction with BEV scene context, where prior work (PreTraM, TDOR, Y-net) either trains scene encoders from scratch or uses CNN-based feature extraction, and (2) prompt-based adaptation of frozen vision-language models (CoOp, CoCoOp, RedCircle, CPT). The key departure is repurposing a frozen CLIP backbone with a combination of hard visual prompts (colored trajectory lines drawn on the BEV image) and soft learnable prompts (trajectory, surrounding, and conditional image prompts) to align trajectory representations into the pre-trained image-text space, rather than learning a dedicated BEV encoder. The Table-to-Text input format, borrowed from prefix-tuning, structures the sequence so that text attributes (surrounding types) and trajectory tokens are interleaved in a way that the frozen text encoder can process autoregressively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen, general-purpose VLM can be adapted to a highly specialized spatial-reasoning task (BEV trajectory prediction) with only 0.17M learnable parameters, suggesting that prompt-based VLM adaptation may be a viable paradigm for other spatial prediction tasks where training dedicated encoders is data-hungry. However, the authors themselves note a significant limitation: CLIP struggles with synthetic or fully-labeled BEV scenes (e.g., nuScenes semantic maps, Argoverse vector graphs), which are the dominant formats in autonomous driving, limiting immediate transfer to the most widely used driving benchmarks. Open questions include whether the colored-line prompting strategy generalizes to 3D scenes or multi-agent interactions, and whether the pixel-dictionary retrieval approach scales to higher-resolution or larger spatial extents.
