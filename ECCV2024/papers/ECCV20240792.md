## Problem

Continuous-Time SLAM (CTSLAM) methods, while enabling fusion of asynchronous multi-modal sensors, remain computationally demanding and are conventionally posed as centralized Non-Linear Least Squares optimizations, strictly limiting them to single-agent setups. Additionally, existing spline-based motion parametrizations lack fully analytic, high-performance implementations. This paper addresses both gaps by providing the fastest SymForce-based B- and Z-Spline implementations and introducing a distributed GBP framework for continuous-time state estimation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes absolute pose measurements (e.g., from MoCap or AprilTag detections at 40 Hz) and visual landmark pixel reprojections (20 Hz, 50 landmarks at 2–6 m range), each accompanied by a covariance. Unlike standard NLLS solvers that require only a point estimate, Hyperion demands an initial mean *and* covariance for every optimizable parameter to bootstrap the GBP message-passing. Outputs include the continuous-time B- or Z-Spline trajectory (pose, velocity, acceleration at arbitrary query times), landmark 3D positions, and per-parameter covariance estimates that can drive adaptive, selective vertex updates.

## Method

Hyperion formulates CTSLAM as a factor graph over continuous-time motion bases and solves it via iterative Gaussian Belief Propagation: node updates sum incoming factor-to-node messages in a Mixed Gaussian Representation (MGR) on Lie groups, requiring warping of precision matrices to a privileged frame via the ⊖ operator; factor updates compute factor-to-node messages by marginalizing neighboring nodes through Schur complements. Spline-based cost factors (pose, velocity, acceleration, and their Jacobians with respect to bases) are fully auto-generated via SymForce, yielding 2.43×–110.31× speedups over Sommer et al.'s hand-crafted recursive formulation. The framework supports two update strategies—synchronous (mimicking NLLS) and dropout (probabilistic vertex skipping that stabilizes loopy graphs and mimics imperfect communication)—alongside robust loss functions and the classification of non-variable parameters as pure constants to reduce marginalization dimensionality. Multithreading exploits the inherent parallelism of message passing across independent nodes and factors.

## Results

Hyperion (GBP) and Ceres (NLLS) converge to identical motion estimates under substantial initial perturbation (±1.00 m/rad) and measurement noise (±0.05 m/rad), with RMSEs closely aligned across all tested levels. In terms of per-iteration speed, Ceres averages 0.70 ms (absolute setup, 400 pose measurements) vs. Hyperion's 4.18 ms (~6× slower), and 15 ms vs. 112 ms in the localization setup (10,000 landmark reprojections, ~7.5× slower). The SymForce-generated B-Spline implementations achieve speedups of 2.43×–110.31× over Sommer et al.'s hand-crafted recursive formulation, with the largest gains in Jacobian evaluation. GBP requires 2–4 additional iterations to converge compared to the centralized NLLS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Ceres (the standard centralized NLLS solver), Hyperion matches convergence quality exactly—both solvers reach identical solutions under the same initialization and noise conditions—but is 6–7.5× slower per iteration, a gap the authors attribute to Ceres excluding implicit covariance estimation and benefiting from years of optimization. Against Sommer et al.'s hand-crafted cumulative B-Spline implementation, the SymForce-generated code dominates across all evaluation types (pose, velocity, acceleration, and Jacobians), with speedups ranging from 2.43× for simple pose queries to 110.31× for full Jacobian computation, where Sommer et al. rely on automatic differentiation. The paper does not report a scenario where Hyperion outperforms Ceres in raw speed; the trade-off is that GBP provides explicit covariance estimates, distributed/asynchronous operation, and selective-update capability that Ceres lacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: continuous-time SLAM (CTSLAM), which uses spline-based motion parametrizations to natively fuse asynchronous sensors, and Gaussian Belief Propagation (GBP), a distributed message-passing algorithm previously applied to discrete-time SLAM by Ortiz et al. and Murai et al. The authors extend the Mixed Gaussian Representation from Murai et al. [28] to handle Lie-group-valued nodes in a continuous-time setting, and combine it with Sommer et al.'s [40] cumulative B-Spline formulation, supercharged by SymForce [22] symbolic code generation to produce the fastest analytic spline implementations to date. The key departure from prior work is the first integration of GBP's distributed, probabilistic inference paradigm with continuous-time motion parametrization, addressing what the authors identify as the absence of a decentralized CTSLAM framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework opens a path toward multi-agent, distributed continuous-time SLAM by providing an open-source GBP solver that explicitly models parameter covariances, enabling adaptive resource allocation toward uncertain estimates. However, the practical impact is currently limited: the solver remains 6–7.5× slower per iteration than Ceres, and the authors acknowledge that numerical instabilities in loopy factor graphs (particularly in visual/localization setups) prevent deployment as a full SLAM system. Open questions include whether adaptive vertex updates leveraging covariance estimates can close the performance gap, whether the dropout strategy generalizes to truly asynchronous multi-agent communication, and whether the framework scales to long trajectories with dense landmark constraints.
