## Problem

Existing vectorized HD map construction methods (e.g., MapTR, MapTRv2) produce jittery or jagged point sequences due to incomplete inter-/intra-instance interactions, neglect of multi-scale BEV features, and reliance on simple L1 or cosine-embedding losses that fail to exploit geometric topology. These distortions degrade the quality of online maps and adversely affect downstream planning and control tasks in autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240232/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes multiview surround-view images (6 cameras on nuScenes, 7+2 on Argoverse2) and a lidar point cloud, converting them into a shared BEV representation via LSS view transformation and SECOND voxelization respectively. The output is a set of ordered point sequences for three map element categories (lane lines, road boundaries, pedestrian crossings), where each instance is represented by a fixed number of points predicted in a specific topological order rather than as an unordered set.

## Method

ADMap introduces three modules on top of a Deformable DETR-style decoder. (1) Multi-scale Perception Neck (MPN) downsamples fusion BEV features at multiple levels, concatenates each downsampled level back to its upsampled counterpart, and merges them into a single multi-scale feature; during inference only the merged feature is used so no extra latency is incurred. (2) Instance Interactive Attention (IIA) cascades Instance self-attention (merging point and channel dimensions, compressing via MLPs, then multi-head self-attention over instances) with Point self-attention (adding instance embeddings back and attending within each instance), enabling hierarchical inter-then-intra-instance interaction that differs from MapTRv2's parallel decoupled self-attention. (3) Vector Direction Difference Loss (VDDL) computes the cosine of angular differences between consecutive predicted and ground-truth vector segments, applies adaptive weights that increase with the ground-truth angular change (so topology-critical points receive stronger supervision), and sums contributions from both neighboring segments at each point to capture full local geometry.

## Results

On nuScenes, ADMap (camera-only, 24 epochs) achieves 54.5% mAP, improving 4.2% over MapTR (50.3%), while the multimodal ADMap reaches 68.0% mAP at 24 epochs and 71.5% at 110 epochs. ADMapv2 (built on MapTRv2) achieves 62.9% mAP camera-only and 72.7% mAP multimodal at 110 epochs; with additional training tricks (EA-LSS, CBGS, multi-task, detection pre-training), ADMapv2† reaches 82.8% mAP at 24 epochs. On Argoverse2, ADMapv2 (C&L, 6 epochs) achieves 76.9% mAP, outperforming MapTRv2 (67.4% camera-only) by 9.5%. Inference speed is maintained at 14.8 FPS (camera-only) and 5.8 FPS (multimodal) on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADMap consistently outperforms its direct baseline MapTR across all settings: +4.2% mAP in camera-only (54.5 vs. 50.3) and +5.5% in multimodal (68.0 vs. 62.5) at 24 epochs. ADMapv2 similarly beats MapTRv2 by +1.4% (camera-only, 62.9 vs. 61.5) and +1.8% (multimodal, 70.8 vs. 69.0) at 24 epochs, while also improving inference speed from 14.1 to 14.8 FPS in the camera-only setting. Against other SOTA methods, ADMapv2 (72.7% at 110 epochs) surpasses BeMapNet (59.8%), StreamMapNet (58.1%), and PivotNet (57.6%) by a wide margin, though those methods use different training budgets (30 epochs). On Argoverse2, ADMapv2 (C&L) achieves 76.9% mAP, beating MapTRv2 (C) by 9.5% and MapTR* (C) by 15.6%, while ADMap (C) improves over MapTR* by 3.4% (64.7 vs. 61.3). The anti-jitter metrics (Table 8) further confirm ADMapv2 reduces average chamfer distance from 0.560 to 0.422 and average jitter points from 4.53 to 2.44 compared to MapTRv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADMap is a direct extension of the MapTR/MapTRv2 lineage for end-to-end vectorized HD map construction, adopting their Deformable DETR-based decoder and instance-point query formulation. It departs from MapTRv2's parallel decoupled self-attention (DSA) by proposing a cascading Instance Interactive Attention (IIA) that first encodes inter-instance topology before refining intra-instance point relations, and introduces a Multi-scale Perception Neck (MPN) that feeds all scale levels to the decoder during training while outputting only the fused feature at inference. The Vector Direction Difference Loss (VDDL) is a new geometric supervision signal that goes beyond the L1 and cosine-embedding losses used in prior work, weighting points adaptively based on ground-truth topological curvature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The anti-jitter framing reframes HD map construction quality in terms of point-sequence smoothness (ACD, ARD, AJP metrics), which is practically critical for downstream planning and control in autonomous driving where jagged lane lines degrade trajectory quality. The modest but consistent gains over MapTRv2 (1.4–1.8% mAP) and the speed improvement of ADMapv2 over its baseline suggest the cascading attention and direction-difference loss are drop-in improvements for existing pipelines. However, the headline 82.8% mAP result relies on additional tricks (EA-LSS, CBGS, multi-task training, detection pre-training), and the paper does not explore whether the anti-jitter metrics transfer to other structured-prediction tasks or whether the approach generalizes beyond the two benchmark datasets.
