## Problem

Fast adversarial training (FAT) suffers from catastrophic overfitting (CO), where adversarial accuracy suddenly collapses, and existing mitigation strategies (e.g., ConvergeSmooth, FGSM-RS) restore robustness at the cost of a significant drop in clean accuracy. This paper asks whether CO is truly a defect to be eliminated or a property that can be exploited, and identifies the specific feature channels whose activation differences drive CO.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 32×32 RGB images from CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, producing a single class label. During training, adversarial examples are generated via one-step FGSM with a perturbation budget ξ_T (8–16/255). The distinctive evaluation-time input is a uniform random noise δ_R ~ U(−16/255, 16/255) added to every image before inference; this noise is applied identically to clean and adversarial inputs and is the key mechanism that unlocks the CO-affected model's full capacity.

## Method

The paper first diagnoses CO by measuring channel-wise feature activation differences V_act between clean and adversarial examples at five ReLU nodes in ResNet18, showing that only a few specific channels exhibit large differences when CO occurs. To mitigate CO, it introduces a regularization term L_stable that penalizes the L2 distance between features of the adversarial example and a random-noise-perturbed example at a chosen node, effectively suppressing the attack-specific channel activations; to induce CO, it introduces L_co that maximizes the classification loss on a masked model while amplifying differences in the top-p% most sensitive channels. The central insight is 'attack obfuscation': because CO-affected models route attack information through a small set of channels, adding uniform random noise at evaluation time disrupts those channels' attack signal while leaving the broader data-extraction pathways intact, yielding near-optimal accuracy on both clean and adversarial examples without any retraining.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet18 (ξ_T=16/255, ξ_E=16/255, with δ_R~U(-16/255,16/255)), the CO-leveraging model (Ours-CO) achieves 88.45% clean accuracy and 58.48% PGD-50 robust accuracy, dramatically outperforming C.Smooth (62.88% clean, 34.29% PGD-50) and ATAS (79.91% clean, 25.16% PGD-50). The CO-mitigating variant (Ours-FD) also surpasses all baselines, reaching 64.08% clean and 36.83% PGD-50 accuracy. On CIFAR-100 (ξ_T=12/255), Ours-FD achieves 50.44% clean and 14.49% PGD-50, comparable to C.Smooth (50.61% clean, 14.70% PGD-50) while using fewer training minutes (93 vs. 104).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The CO-leveraging model (Ours-CO) dominates all five baselines across every metric and perturbation budget. At ξ_T=16/255, Ours-CO achieves 88.45% clean accuracy, exceeding ATAS (79.91%) by 8.5 points and C.Smooth (62.88%) by 25.6 points, while its PGD-50 robustness of 58.48% surpasses C.Smooth (34.29%) by 24.2 points and ATAS (25.16%) by 33.3 points. The CO-mitigating variant (Ours-FD) also consistently outperforms ATAS and ZeroGrad on adversarial metrics (e.g., 36.83% vs. 25.16% PGD-50 at ξ_T=16/255) while matching or slightly exceeding C.Smooth on clean accuracy. On CIFAR-100, Ours-FD is roughly on par with C.Smooth (50.44% vs. 50.61% clean), confirming comparable performance on a harder dataset. FGSM-RS [39] is tested separately in Table 10, where adding δ_R similarly boosts its PGD-50 accuracy from 0.16% to 46.55% at ξ_T=16/255, demonstrating the generality of the attack-obfuscation strategy across FAT baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FAT literature (FGSM-MEP, ConvergeSmooth, ZeroGrad, ATAS) and the CO analysis of He et al. [16], who attributed CO to high-variance channels and proposed variance-based masking. The key departure is replacing variance-based channel selection with feature activation differences between clean and adversarial examples, which the authors argue more precisely identifies the pathways responsible for CO. More fundamentally, the paper reframes CO from a purely negative training instability into a structural property (separation of data and attack pathways) that can be exploited at inference time via random noise, a perspective absent from prior CO-mitigation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'attack obfuscation' framing challenges the field's default assumption that CO must be eliminated, suggesting that the clean-accuracy drop in stably trained FAT models may be an unnecessary cost. However, the practical applicability is constrained: the inference-time random noise requirement is a specific deployment assumption, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10/100 with ResNet18/WideResNet34, and the AA scores for CO-affected models remain notably lower than C&W scores, indicating the robustness gain is not uniform across attack families. Open questions include whether this phenomenon persists at ImageNet scale, with transformer architectures, or under adaptive attacks that account for the inference-time noise.
