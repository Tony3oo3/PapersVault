## Problem

Traditional two-view relative pose estimation pipelines rely on computationally expensive robust estimators (e.g., RANSAC) to recover poses from matched keypoints, making real-time deployment difficult. Meanwhile, deep learning-based pose regressors lack generalizability across varying image sizes and camera intrinsics, and existing methods only address camera-to-world estimation, leaving the object-to-camera scenario (e.g., 6D object pose tracking) without a unified, efficient solution.

## Input / Output

SRPose takes two RGB images (resized to 640×480 for batched keypoint detection, then rescaled to original coordinates) along with their respective camera intrinsics. For object-to-camera estimation, a single bounding-box prompt in the reference image identifies the target object, eliminating the need for segmentations in both views. The output is a relative rotation matrix R ∈ SO(3) (parameterized as a 6D vector and converted via partial Gram-Schmidt) and a translation vector t ∈ R³, which together define the 6D pose transformation between the two views.

## Method

SRPose replaces explicit keypoint matching and robust estimation with a direct regression pipeline built on sparse keypoints. A frozen SuperPoint detector extracts 1024–2048 keypoints and descriptors per image; an intrinsic-calibration position encoder normalizes keypoint coordinates by K⁻¹ and maps them to high-dimensional position embeddings via a single FC layer, enabling adaptation to arbitrary image sizes and intrinsics. Six layers of self-attention and prior-knowledge-guided cross-attention then establish implicit cross-view correspondences: the cross-attention scores are element-wise multiplied by a similarity matrix S (cosine similarity of keypoint descriptors) to bias attention toward semantically correlated keypoint pairs. For object-to-camera estimation, the bounding-box prompt simply filters reference-view keypoints before the attention layers, and the guided cross-attention learns to select corresponding object keypoints in the query view. A two-branch 3-layer MLP regresses rotation and translation, trained with Huber losses on rotation angle, translation magnitude, normalized translation direction, and translation angle; the network is pretrained on ScanNet and fine-tuned per dataset.

## Results

On Matterport (camera-to-world), SRPose achieves 2.65° median rotation error, 91.6% rotation accuracy at ≤30°, and 0.27 m median translation error with 83.7% accuracy at ≤1 m, outperforming the best regressor NOPE-SAC (2.77°/89.0%/0.52 m/73.2%) and all matcher-based methods that cannot estimate scaled translation without depth. On HO3D (object-to-camera), SRPose achieves 8.9° median rotation error and 95.4% accuracy at ≤30°, more than doubling the ADD-S score (56.8 vs. 33.3 for the best matcher DKM) using only RGB and a single bounding-box prompt. On ScanNet, SRPose runs in 28.5 ms total, saving at least 200 ms compared to all matcher-based pipelines that require RANSAC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRPose outperforms all four regressor baselines on Matterport: it beats NOPE-SAC (the strongest regressor) by 2.2° in average rotation error (11.12° vs. 14.37°), 10.5 points in ≤30° rotation accuracy (91.6% vs. 89.0%), and 10.5 points in ≤1 m translation accuracy (83.7% vs. 73.2%). Against sparse matchers (SuperGlue, SGMNet, LightGlue) and dense matchers (LoFTR, ASpanFormer, DKM), SRPose is the only method that estimates scaled translation from RGB alone; while dense matchers like DKM achieve lower median rotation error (0.46° vs. 2.65°), they cannot report translation metrics without external depth. On ScanNet, SRPose (28.5 ms total) is roughly 9–17× faster than all matchers (269–492 ms) while matching or exceeding sparse matcher accuracy (AUC@10°: 34.3 vs. LightGlue 33.6, SuperGlue 33.8), though dense matchers like DKM (AUC@10°: 50.7) remain more accurate at the cost of 17× slower inference. On HO3D, SRPose dramatically outperforms all four matcher baselines, achieving 95.4% rotation accuracy at ≤30° versus 61.7–64.8% for the best matchers, and 56.8% ADD-S versus 30.9–33.3%, using only a single bounding-box prompt rather than full segmentations in both views. On Niantic for map-free relocalization, SRPose achieves the best VCRE precision (46.4) among all methods without an auxiliary depth model, slightly exceeding RoMa+DPT (45.6), though RoMa+DPT attains higher relocalization precision (22.8 vs. 16.9) by leveraging DPT-estimated depth for scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRPose carves new ground by repurposing the sparse keypoint detection pipeline from matcher-based methods (SuperPoint, SuperGlue, LightGlue) and replacing the downstream RANSAC essential-matrix solve with a neural network regressor, thereby inheriting the geometric grounding of classical two-view geometry while gaining the speed of end-to-end regression. It departs from prior deep regressors (Map-free, SparsePlanes, 8point, NOPE-SAC) by introducing an intrinsic-calibration position encoder that makes the model agnostic to image size and camera intrinsics, and by adding a promptable object-prompt mechanism that extends the framework from camera-to-world to object-to-camera estimation without requiring full segmentations. The prior-knowledge-guided cross-attention, which modulates attention scores with a cosine-similarity matrix of keypoint descriptors, is a novel architectural choice not present in either the matcher or regressor literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the RANSAC step, SRPose achieves a practical speedup of over 200 ms per pair while remaining competitive in accuracy, making real-time map-free relocalization and 6D object pose tracking more feasible on modest hardware. The intrinsic-calibration design addresses a genuine gap in prior regressors, which silently assume fixed intrinsics and fail on datasets like MegaDepth with heterogeneous cameras. Open questions include whether the frozen SuperPoint detector is a bottleneck for accuracy (dense matchers still lead on ScanNet), whether the single-view object prompt generalizes to multi-object or occluded scenes, and whether the approach extends to more than two views without re-deriving correspondences.
