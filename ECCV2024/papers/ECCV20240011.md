## Problem

Event cameras suffer from motion blur due to the limited bandwidth of their sensor pixel analog circuit, which is proportional to incident light intensity, yet no prior NeRF-from-events method or event simulator has modeled this effect. This omission limits reconstruction quality precisely under high-speed or low-light conditions where event cameras are expected to outperform standard cameras.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a motion-blurred event stream from a calibrated event camera with known trajectory, plus a set of reference RGB images for post-reconstruction correction. It produces a blur-minimal NeRF whose volume renders represent predicted effective radiance (L_sig + L_dark) rather than pure incident radiance, since the unknown black level cannot be disentangled from events alone. The NeRF is then used for novel view synthesis, with a translated-gamma correction applied to resolve per-channel offset and potential gamma inaccuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is a physically-accurate pixel bandwidth model: a unity-gain 4th-order NLTI low-pass filter in state-space form that cascades a 2nd-order NLTI filter (logarithmic photoreceptor, with input-dependent damping ratio and natural frequency), a 1st-order LTI filter (source follower), and another 1st-order LTI filter (differencing amplifier). A discrete-time LTV variant is derived by linearizing at per-interval operating points and applying first-order-hold discretization; the transient response is solved numerically as a weighted sum of past inputs, and a transformed exponential importance sampling strategy concentrates NeRF render samples where the bandwidth kernel has the largest weight. Training follows an Analysis-by-Synthesis loop that synthesizes motion-blurred effective log-radiance from NeRF renders and optimizes a Huber-norm threshold-normalized difference loss plus a novel threshold-normalized total variation loss for regularizing textureless patches, with optional joint optimization of the pixel bandwidth parameters. Post-reconstruction, a Levenberg-Marquardt translated-gamma correction (scale + per-channel offset) eliminates the black level and resolves residual gamma inaccuracy.

## Results

On synthetic sequences with 4× camera speed (the most challenging motion-blur setting), Deblur e-NeRF achieves PSNR 27.48, SSIM 0.939, and LPIPS 0.061, substantially outperforming Robust e-NeRF (PSNR 22.18) and E2VID+NeRF (PSNR 17.82). Under low-light conditions (E_sc = 10 lux), Deblur e-NeRF maintains PSNR 28.62 while Robust e-NeRF drops to 22.72, demonstrating robustness to severe blur-induced event loss (only 17.22% of blur-free events remain). On real EDS sequences, Deblur e-NeRF achieves PSNR 18.27 and 16.53 on the two sequences, consistently exceeding Robust e-NeRF by 0.27 and 0.62 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deblur e-NeRF consistently outperforms both baselines across all tested conditions. Against Robust e-NeRF (the state-of-the-art event-based NeRF method), the advantage grows with motion blur severity: at 4× camera speed, Deblur e-NeRF gains 5.3 dB PSNR (27.48 vs 22.18) and reduces LPIPS by 0.061 (0.061 vs 0.122); at 10 lux illuminance, the PSNR gap widens to 5.9 dB (28.62 vs 22.72). Against the E2VID+NeRF pipeline baseline, Deblur e-NeRF leads by 9.7 dB PSNR at 4× speed (27.48 vs 17.82) and by 11.4 dB at 10 lux (28.62 vs 17.24). On real EDS sequences, Deblur e-NeRF edges out Robust e-NeRF by 0.27–0.62 dB PSNR and improves SSIM by 0.018–0.033, while maintaining a large margin over E2VID+NeRF (3.4–3.5 dB PSNR). Notably, Deblur e-NeRF achieves these results while being trained with only 1/8× the batch size of the baselines, and its performance remains close to the blur-free upper bound (PSNR 29.43) even under the hardest combined condition (4× speed + 10 lux, PSNR 26.15).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Robust e-NeRF (same authors, ICCV 2023) by replacing its idealized event generation model with a physically-accurate 4th-order NLTI pixel bandwidth model derived from the analog circuit of DVS sensors. It departs from all prior event-based NeRF methods (Ev-NeRF, E-NeRF, EventNeRF) and existing event simulators (ESIM, v2e), which at best model first-order bandwidth behavior under extreme low light, by capturing the full non-linear, light-dependent transient response of the pixel. The Analysis-by-Synthesis training framework is adapted from the image deblurring NeRF literature (Deblur-NeRF, ExBlURF, BAD-NeRF), but applied to events for the first time, with a novel threshold-normalized total variation loss replacing the gradient loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work is the first to make event-based NeRF reconstruction robust to the very conditions (high speed, low light) where event cameras are supposed to excel over standard cameras, closing a gap that had been overlooked because event motion blur was not widely documented in the CV community. The open-sourced physically-accurate event simulator and synthetic dataset provide a practical tool for the broader event-based vision community. Key limitations include the inherited assumption of known camera trajectory, higher computational/memory cost from multi-sample log-radiance synthesis, and occasional instability in joint optimization of bandwidth parameters. An open question is whether the pixel bandwidth model generalizes across different DVS architectures and manufacturing processes without re-derivation.
