## Problem

Monocular depth estimation trained on synthetic data (e.g., vKITTI) suffers significant performance degradation when deployed on real-world images due to the domain gap. Existing domain adaptation strategies—style transfer or dual-domain projection—attempt to close this gap but lack a principled mechanism to disentangle domain-specific style components from the structural content that actually determines depth. SEDiff is the first to leverage a diffusion-based generative model to explicitly extract domain-invariant structural information for depth estimation.

## Input / Output

At inference, SEDiff takes a single real-world RGB image and produces a dense depth map (H×W×1) without any iterative denoising steps. During training, it consumes synthetic images with paired dense depth labels (e.g., vKITTI) and independently sampled real-world images (e.g., KITTI), with inputs preprocessed to grayscale via the ITU-R 601-2 luma transform before feature extraction. The intermediate structural content S and attention map A are both H×W×1 tensors, combined via Hadamard product before the final depth regression head.

## Method

SEDiff trains a geometry-domain-conditioned latent diffusion model where a U-Net denoising network receives noisy latents, geometric features from a dedicated feature network G (operating on grayscale inputs), and domain-specific style embeddings w_d. The key architectural innovation is an alternating arrangement of time AdaIN and domain AdaIN layers: the domain AdaIN layer, conditioned on learnable per-domain parameters processed through a shared MLP, strips residual domain-specific style from the geometric features while enabling re-stylization toward either the synthetic or real domain. An auxiliary depth estimation network supervised on synthetic depth labels ensures the extracted structural content S retains depth-relevant information. During training only, the trained LDM performs iterative denoising to generate depth-consistent style-transferred images (synthetic→real), which feed an attention network to produce a domain-gap-reduced attention map A. At inference, all iterative denoising is bypassed: the geometric feature network and structural decoder directly produce S_r from the real image, and a separate attention network produces A from the RGB image, yielding the final depth prediction as f_depth(A ⊙ S_r).

## Results

On KITTI (80m cap, Eigen test split), SEDiff achieves δ<1.25 = 0.773, Abs Rel = 0.165, RMSE = 5.686, and RMSElog = 0.237 using only synthetic (vKITTI) and monocular real (KITTI) data, outperforming the prior SOTA 3D-PL on all metrics except Sq Rel. With stereo pairs added during training (Table 2), SEDiff further improves to δ<1.25 = 0.871 and RMSElog = 0.195, surpassing all competing methods. On NYU Depth v2 (indoor), SEDiff achieves Abs Rel = 0.221 and RMSElog = 0.096, outperforming all domain-adaptive baselines. On unseen datasets (DrivingStereo, DDAD), SEDiff demonstrates superior domain generalization with Abs Rel of 0.193 and 0.216 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEDiff outperforms 3D-PL (the prior SOTA using the same V+K(M) data) on δ<1.25 (0.773 vs 0.753), Abs Rel (0.165 vs 0.169), RMSE (5.686 vs 6.034), and RMSElog (0.237 vs 0.249), though 3D-PL retains a slight edge on Sq Rel (1.262 vs 1.301). Against T²Net, SEDiff improves δ<1.25 by 1.6 points and RMSE by 0.258. SEDiff is marginally behind S2R-DepthNet on δ<1.25 (0.773 vs 0.781) and DESC on δ<1.25 (0.773 vs 0.787) and Abs Rel (0.165 vs 0.156), but both of those methods leverage additional data sources (WikiArt paintings and KITTI semantic labels, respectively) that SEDiff does not use. In the stereo-pair setting (Table 2), SEDiff achieves the best results across all metrics, beating 3D-PL (δ<1.25: 0.871 vs 0.859, RMSElog: 0.195 vs 0.201), SharinGAN, and GASDA. On NYU Depth v2, SEDiff surpasses both supervised baselines (Eigen et al., Li et al.) and domain-adaptive methods (T²Net, SharinGAN) on all metrics. On unseen datasets (DrivingStereo, DDAD), SEDiff outperforms T²Net and 3D-PL by substantial margins (e.g., Abs Rel 0.193 vs 0.419/0.243 on DrivingStereo), confirming stronger domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEDiff builds on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) for its generative backbone and adapts the attention-map mechanism from S2R-DepthNet (Chen et al., CVPR 2021) for structure-guided depth prediction. It departs from prior domain-adaptive depth estimation methods (T2Net, GASDA, SharinGAN, 3D-PL) that rely on GAN-based image translation or dual-domain projection by instead using a diffusion-based denoising network with alternating time-AdaIN and domain-AdaIN layers to disentangle domain-specific style from domain-invariant geometric structure. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the iterative denoising process not for image generation but as a structure-extraction mechanism that inherently strips domain-specific components, with the diffusion model used exclusively during training to avoid inference-time cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEDiff demonstrates that diffusion models can serve as structure extractors rather than generators in domain-adaptive pipelines, offering a principled alternative to GAN-based style transfer that avoids mode collapse and training instability. The practical advantage of confining the iterative denoising to training (with a single forward pass at inference) makes the approach deployable in real-time applications like autonomous driving. However, the grayscale preprocessing as a proxy for domain invariance is a coarse approximation, and the method still requires paired synthetic depth labels, limiting its applicability where such data is unavailable. Open questions include whether the denoising-based structure extraction generalizes to other domain-adaptive tasks beyond depth estimation and whether simpler alternatives (e.g., contrastive learning on grayscale features) could achieve comparable disentanglement without the training overhead of diffusion.
