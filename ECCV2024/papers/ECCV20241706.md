## Problem

Self-supervised representation learning for Earth observation has largely exploited geolocation and time as alignment signals, but the opportunity to leverage automatically paired multi-modal sensor data (SAR, elevation, canopy height, climate, landcover) as pretext tasks for learning better optical image representations remains underexplored. Additionally, simply replacing ImageNet pretraining data with domain-specific satellite images does not improve downstream performance without architectural adaptations to medium-resolution imagery.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes 112×112 crops (randomly cropped from 128×128) of 12-band Sentinel-2 multi-spectral imagery at 10 m ground resolution, split into 7×7 patches of 16×16 pixels. Pretext targets include five pixel-level rasters (Sentinel-1 SAR, Aster DEM, ETH-GCHM canopy height, Dynamic World, ESA World Cover) at the same 128×128 spatial extent and six image-level scalars (biome, ecoregion, ERA5 temperature/precipitation, geolocation, observation month). The output is a frozen or fine-tunable 3.7 M-parameter ConvNeXt V2-Atto encoder whose features feed a linear probe or U-Net decoder for downstream classification and segmentation.

## Method

MP-MAE extends the ConvNeXt V2 fully convolutional masked autoencoder by attaching a separate one-block convolutional decoder for each of T pretext tasks, all sharing a single encoder. A random mask is applied simultaneously to the Sentinel-2 input and all pixel-level targets; the encoder processes only visible patches via sparse convolutions, and a learnable mask token fills masked positions before dense convolutional decoders reconstruct each modality. For image-level modalities, global average pooling over decoded non-visible patches precedes a linear prediction head. Two key architectural modifications adapt ConvNeXt V2 to medium-resolution satellite imagery: the patch size is halved from 32 to 16 (preserving the 7×7 patch grid and optimal masking ratio), and the first layer's learned 4× downsampling is replaced by a stride-1 convolution followed by a depth-wise downsampling layer to retain full-resolution features for pixel-level downstream tasks. The multi-task loss combines per-task losses (MSE for regression, cross-entropy for classification) with learnable task-uncertainty weights to balance noisy and clean targets.

## Results

On the GEO-Bench downstream tasks, MP-MAE pretrained on MMEarth (1.24M locations, 3.7M-parameter ConvNeXtV2-Atto encoder) achieves 67.1 F1 (FT) / 43.3 F1 (LP) on BigEarth20k, 54.6% Acc (FT) / 43.8% Acc (LP) on So2Sat20k, 79.8 IoU on Cashew1k, and 38.2 IoU on SAcrop3k. Multi-modal pretext tasks improve linear probing by ~5 pp on BigEarth20k and ~8 pp on So2Sat20k over optical-only pretraining, and in the extreme few-shot setting (1% of EuroSat2k training data) lift LP accuracy from 16.5% to 34%. The encoder uses only 3.7M parameters, roughly 7× fewer than the ResNet-50 (25M) backbones used by prior baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MP-MAE outperforms all three baselines on 5 of 6 metrics. On BigEarth20k fine-tuning, MP-MAE achieves 67.1 F1, surpassing GASSL (53.6), SeCo (59.6), and SatlasNet (55.5) by 11.5, 7.5, and 11.6 pp respectively. On So2Sat20k, MP-MAE leads in both FT (54.6 vs. 42.4/45.2/37.1) and LP (43.8 vs. 35.4/39.9/22.1), with the largest margin over SatlasNet LP (+21.7 pp). On the segmentation tasks, MP-MAE achieves 38.2 IoU on SAcrop3k, nearly doubling the best baseline (SeCo at 22.7), and 79.8 IoU on Cashew1k, a modest +0.8 pp over GASSL (79.0). The sole exception is BigEarth20k linear probing, where SeCo edges out MP-MAE by 1.2 pp (44.5 vs. 43.3). Notably, MP-MAE achieves these results with a 3.7M-parameter encoder versus 25M for all baselines, and with a larger pretraining corpus (1.24M vs. 0.20–0.86M locations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ConvNeXt V2-MAE (FCMAE) architecture by adding multiple reconstruction decoders for aligned multi-modal targets, building directly on the masked autoencoder framework of He et al. and the fully convolutional design of Woo et al. In the EO representation learning literature, prior work has exploited geolocation (GASSL, SatCLIP) and temporal structure (SeCo, SatMAE, Presto) as separate self-supervision axes, while multi-modal MAE approaches (MultiMAE, 4M) focused on pixel-level modalities like depth and semantic masks. MMEarth departs from both lines by unifying geolocation, time, climate, and sensor data as 'modalities' within a single multi-pretext reconstruction framework, and by being fully automatic (no human annotation) in contrast to SatlasPretrain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMEarth dataset (1.2M locations, 12 modalities, global coverage across 14 biomes) provides a practical resource for the EO community that was previously unavailable at this scale and modality diversity. The finding that multi-modal pretext tasks substantially close the fine-tuning/linear-probing gap and improve few-shot label efficiency is practically important for real-world applications where labelled data is scarce. However, limitations remain: pretraining was limited to 200 epochs with a small (3.7M parameter) encoder, evaluation was restricted to five GEO-Bench datasets, and the model is not robust to varying input channel counts. Open questions include whether the multi-pretext benefit persists at larger scale, whether the specific modality set is optimal, and how the approach generalizes beyond the evaluated European/African/global benchmarks.
