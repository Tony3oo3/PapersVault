## Problem

Learning-based holographic reconstruction methods trained on synthetic data fail to generalize to real holographic measurements due to the significant domain gap, and no existing approach simultaneously unifies in-line holographic reconstruction, spatial super-resolution, and extensive refocusing in a single interpretable framework. The scarcity of real holographic datasets with ground-truth complex fields further limits supervised training, leaving a critical gap between synthetic-domain performance and real-world utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241893/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stack of N low-resolution noisy holograms (e.g., 128×128 pixels, downsampled by ×4 or ×8) captured at different object-detector distances (0.5–1.0 mm range, 10 μm step) with known sub-pixel spatial shifts and arbitrary illumination wavelengths (440–638 nm). It outputs a high-resolution complex transmission field (amplitude and phase) at the object plane (e.g., 512×512), effectively performing joint phase retrieval and spatial super-resolution. The model is wavelength- and distance-agnostic at inference, requiring no retraining when these physical parameters change.

## Method

HoloADMM unrolls the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers into a fixed number (n=5) of differentiable blocks, each containing: (i) L=3 gradient descent steps on the latent field x using Wirtinger derivatives through the full holographic forward model (Fresnel propagation, sub-pixel warping, downsampling, Poisson-Gaussian noise), (ii) a shared-weight ResUnet denoiser that acts as a learned image prior on the auxiliary variable v, and (iii) a Lagrange multiplier update. A shallow complex-valued residual CNN refines the initial back-propagated estimate before the ADMM loop, and FFT-based sub-pixel image registration on back-propagated fields enables super-resolution by exploiting aliasing across the hologram stack. The entire architecture is trained end-to-end with MSE loss on 100k+ synthetic samples (generated via Stable Diffusion and random-shape generators) with randomly sampled wavelengths and refocusing distances, and the interpretability of the unrolled optimization—rather than a black-box mapping—is what enables robust generalization to real DIHM holograms without any domain adaptation.

## Results

On out-of-domain synthetic test data (D_o), HoloADMM achieves a phase PSNR of 28.092 dB and SSIM of 0.877, outperforming the second-best method (Ren et al., 23.822 dB PSNR) by over 4 dB on phase. On in-domain data (D_i), it reaches 29.373 dB PSNR (amplitude) and 26.410 dB PSNR (phase) with SSIM of 0.923 and 0.902 respectively. With ×4 spatial super-resolution from 128×128 inputs, HoloADMM attains 27.626 dB phase PSNR on D_o, surpassing full-resolution baselines. Inference time is under 1 second per image versus 60–990 seconds for iterative methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoloADMM outperforms all nine baselines across every reported metric and dataset. Against the strongest learning-based competitor, Chen et al.'s FIN [3], HoloADMM gains +2.06 dB PSNR on phase (D_i) and +6.69 dB on phase (D_o), with a particularly large out-of-domain margin (28.092 vs. 21.402 dB). The self-supervised approach of Huang et al. [19] trails by ~12 dB on phase PSNR (D_i), and the end-to-end framework of Ren et al. [30] lags by ~5 dB. Among iterative methods (Table 3), HoloADMM with a single hologram achieves 29.178 dB PSNR on the Airplane amplitude target versus 16.307 dB for SH-PR [10] and 18.297 dB for DH-GAN [7], while running in 0.29 s compared to 60–990 s. With 10 holograms, HoloADMM reaches 32.223 dB (Airplane A) versus 19.465 dB for MH-PR [10]. The untrained decoder of Niknam et al. [27] and the reverse-attention method of Xiwen et al. [38] both fall 8–12 dB behind HoloADMM on phase PSNR. Notably, all competing models trained solely on synthetic data produce poor or meaningless reconstructions on real DIHM holograms, whereas HoloADMM generalizes without any domain adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloADMM unrolls the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) into a deep network, where each iteration's data-fidelity step is solved via gradient descent on the physical forward model (Fresnel propagation) and the prior step is a learned ResUnet denoiser. This places it in the model-based deep learning lineage, distinct from black-box supervised networks (FIN, Rivenson et al.) that learn a direct hologram-to-field mapping, and from untrained-network approaches (Niknam et al., DH-GAN) that use random weights as priors. The key conceptual departure is the explicit use of interpretability—each unrolled block corresponds to a physically meaningful subproblem—as the mechanism enabling robust synthetic-to-real domain transfer, combined with FFT-based sub-pixel registration to exploit aliasing for spatial super-resolution, which prior holographic reconstruction methods do not jointly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a model trained exclusively on 100k+ synthetic holograms (generated via Stable Diffusion and random-shape software) generalizes to real DIHM captures without any domain adaptation, while competing methods trained on real data (e.g., FIN) produce poor or meaningless reconstructions on the same real samples. This challenges the prevailing assumption that learning-based holographic reconstruction requires real-data training and opens the door to scalable, domain-agnostic QPI pipelines. Open questions include extending the framework to joint computational refocusing, handling more complex 3D specimens, and validating generalization across a broader range of biological samples and instrument configurations beyond the two real test cases presented.
