## Problem

State-of-the-art unsupervised instance segmentation methods (e.g., MaskCut, TokenCut) rely on repeatedly solving generalized eigenvalue systems for normalized-cut, incurring high computational cost and slow per-image inference. Additionally, these approaches impose a fixed number of segmentations per image (e.g., three for MaskCut), which fails to capture the full object taxonomy in dense, multi-object scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240351/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 480×480 pixels and produces a variable number of binary instance masks (no fixed upper bound per image). Internally, the image is encoded by a DINO ViT-B/8 encoder yielding 60×60 patch tokens of 768-dim features; a 2D grid of K prompt tokens (default stride=4, giving K=900) is sampled from this grid. Output masks undergo CRF post-processing and connected-component splitting before final prediction. For the ProMerge+ variant, a Cascade Mask R-CNN with ResNet-50 backbone (initialized with DINO weights) is trained on pseudo-labels and outputs standard detection boxes and masks.

## Method

ProMerge operates in three stages: (1) Point-prompting — K equally spaced DINO patch tokens serve as seeds; each seed's cosine similarity to all patch tokens produces an affinity matrix that is binarized at threshold τ_b=0.2 to yield initial mask proposals. (2) Background-based mask pruning — masks touching two or more image edges are classified as background candidates, aggregated via pixel-wise majority voting into a single background mask, and then foreground proposals are filtered using a Cascade strategy that evaluates only the novel (previously uncovered) pixels of each mask against the background via IoA and feature-similarity thresholds. (3) Iterative merging — remaining masks are sorted by area in descending order and sequentially merged with previously processed larger masks if either their Intersection-over-Area exceeds τ_IoA^merge or their mean-embedding cosine similarity exceeds τ_f^merge=0.1. This avoids the eigenvalue decomposition entirely, yielding ~3.6× faster inference than MaskCut while achieving higher AP and AR across six benchmarks.

## Results

In the training-free setting, ProMerge outperforms both TokenCut and MaskCut across all six benchmarks in average precision and recall, with the overall advantage attributed to its flexibility in not fixing the number of masks per image. In the pseudo-label trained setting, ProMerge+ (Cascade Mask R-CNN trained on ProMerge pseudo-labels from ImageNet) achieves 7.9 AP and 24.3 AR₁₀₀ on average across six benchmarks, surpassing CutLER (7.0 AP, 22.9 AR₁₀₀) by 0.9 AP and 1.4 AR₁₀₀. ProMerge also runs at 0.54 FPS, approximately 3.6× faster than MaskCut (0.15 FPS) and 1.6× faster than TokenCut (0.34 FPS), by avoiding the computationally intensive normalized-cut eigenvalue decomposition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TokenCut [34], ProMerge wins on both AP and AR₁₀₀ across all six benchmarks in the training-free setting, with the largest gains on COCO2017 (AR₁₀₀ 7.5 vs 6.9) and SA-1B. Against MaskCut [33], ProMerge matches or exceeds AP on most datasets while achieving higher recall overall, and is 3.6× faster in inference (0.54 vs 0.15 FPS) by replacing repeated normalized-cut eigenvalue solves with lightweight cosine-similarity prompting and iterative merging. In the pseudo-label trained comparison, ProMerge+ outperforms CutLER [33] on every benchmark: the average improvement is +0.9 AP and +1.4 AR₁₀₀, with the largest per-dataset gain on SA-1B (+2.3 AP, +2.8 AR₁₀₀) and KITTI (+1.5 AP, +2.4 AR₁₀₀). Both ProMerge and ProMerge+ use the same Cascade Mask R-CNN architecture and training recipe as their respective baselines, isolating the contribution to the quality of the generated pseudo-labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProMerge builds directly on the normalized-cut lineage of unsupervised instance segmentation (TokenCut, MaskCut/CutLER), which uses self-supervised DINO features to construct a graph and solve a generalized eigenvalue system for segmentation. It departs from this by replacing the eigenvalue decomposition with a much simpler point-prompting step (cosine similarity between a grid of seed tokens and all patch tokens) followed by iterative IoA/feature-similarity-based merging. The background-based mask pruning with pixel-wise voting and Cascade filtering is a novel component absent from prior spectral methods, and the pseudo-label training stage (ProMerge+) follows the CutLER recipe but substitutes ProMerge's masks for MaskCut's.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is a ~3.6× inference speedup over MaskCut at comparable accuracy, making unsupervised instance segmentation more deployable in latency-sensitive settings. The ablation revealing that naive prompting+merging is actually worse than prompting alone (0.4 vs 0.5 AP) underscores that the background pruning is the critical enabler, not the merging itself. The authors acknowledge a substantial gap remains versus fully-supervised methods like SAM, attributing it to DINO features not being trained with segmentation-specific pretext tasks; whether purpose-built self-supervised features can close this gap is an open question.
