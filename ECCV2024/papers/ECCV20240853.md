## Problem

Few-Shot Object Detection suffers from two intertwined failures: class confusion, where novel-class instances are mis-predicted as base classes due to overlapping feature clusters, and catastrophic forgetting, where base-class performance degrades during few-shot adaptation. Existing contrastive or meta-learning approaches target only one of these issues and rely on pairwise similarity metrics that struggle to simultaneously reduce inter-class bias and intra-class variance, especially under extreme class imbalance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240853/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on RGB images at 800×600 (VOC) or 764×1333 (COCO) resolution, consuming K-shot support samples (K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 30}) from novel classes alongside abundant base-class samples during the few-shot adaptation stage. A dedicated combinatorial head projects RoI-pooled features into 128-dimensional vectors on which the submodular objectives are computed, while the standard classification and box-regression heads remain unchanged. The final model outputs bounding boxes and class labels for both base and novel classes at test time.

## Method

SMILE introduces a joint combinatorial loss L_comb = (1−η)·L_comb^intra + η·L_comb^inter applied to RoI features during few-shot fine-tuning. L_comb^inter uses Submodular Mutual Information (SMI) to minimize the common information between base and novel class sets, enforcing inter-cluster separation; L_comb^intra minimizes Total Submodular Information within each class set, promoting intra-class compactness and mitigating forgetting. The best-performing instantiation, SMILE-FLMI, derives both terms from the Facility-Location submodular function, where L_comb^inter penalizes the maximum (hardest-negative) similarity between class sets and L_comb^intra minimizes the sum of maximum similarities from all samples to each class. The approach is architecture-agnostic—plugging into Faster-RCNN/FPN or ViT backbones without modifying their RoI heads—and is trained in two stages: base training on abundant data followed by few-shot adaptation with the combined classification, box-regression, and combinatorial losses.

## Results

On PASCAL-VOC (10-shot, split 3), AGCM+SMILE achieves 61.4 mAP_novel, outperforming the AGCM baseline (58.2) by 3.2 mAP points (5.7% relative improvement). On MS-COCO (30-shot), PDC+SMILE reaches 49.9 mAP50, surpassing imTED-B+PDC (47.3) by 2.6 mAP points (5.4% relative improvement). Across all settings, SMILE also demonstrates up to 2× faster convergence and up to 11% reduction in class confusion compared to SoTA baselines, while retaining base-class performance (e.g., DiGeo+SMILE achieves 92.6 mAP_base vs. 90.5 for DiGeo alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMILE is applied as a plug-and-play combinatorial loss on top of four SoTA FSOD backbones (FSCE, AGCM, DiGeo, and imTED+PDC), consistently improving novel-class mAP across all splits and shot settings. On PASCAL-VOC 10-shot, AGCM+SMILE (61.4 mAP, split 3) outperforms all meta-learning baselines (Meta-RCNN 48.1, Meta-Reweight 45.9, MetaDet 44.1, Add-Info 49.6, CME 48.8) by 13+ mAP points, and also surpasses metric-learning baselines FsDet w/ cos (49.8) and Retentive-RCNN (50.1) by 11+ points. On MS-COCO 30-shot, PDC+SMILE (49.9 mAP50) beats FCT (35.5), FADI (29.1), CME (–), and imTED-B+PDC (47.3) by 2.6 mAP points, establishing a new SoTA. The improvement is most pronounced in low-shot regimes: on VOC split 2, 1-shot, AGCM+SMILE (31.9) outperforms AGCM (27.5) by 4.4 points, and PDC+SMILE (27.1) outperforms imTED+PDC (15.5) by 11.6 points. SMILE also improves base-class retention (e.g., DiGeo+SMILE 92.6 vs. DiGeo 90.5 mAP_base) and reduces class confusion by up to 11% (AGCM+SMILE vs. AGCM) and 4% (DiGeo+SMILE vs. DiGeo), confirming that the combinatorial objectives address both forgetting and confusion simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMILE extends the submodular combinatorial representation learning framework introduced in SCoRe [30] (which applied submodular total information to long-tail recognition) into the few-shot object detection setting, and draws on the Submodular Mutual Information function family defined in PRISM [22] to model inter-class overlap. It departs from the dominant contrastive/pairwise-similarity paradigm in FSOD (FSCE, AGCM, DiGeo, imTED+PDC) by treating each object class as a set and applying set-level combinatorial objectives that simultaneously penalize inter-class bias and intra-class variance. The framework is explicitly architecture-agnostic, plugging a combinatorial head onto existing RoI-based detectors (Faster-RCNN, ViT) rather than proposing a new backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified combinatorial objective addressing both class confusion and catastrophic forgetting in a single loss is a clean conceptual contribution, and the architecture-agnostic plug-in design (validated on both CNN and ViT backbones) makes it broadly adoptable for FSOD practitioners. The reported ~2× faster convergence is practically relevant for deployment scenarios requiring rapid adaptation to new object categories. However, the evaluation is limited to two benchmarks with relatively few novel classes (5 on VOC, 20 on COCO), and the paper does not explore how the combinatorial formulation behaves in extreme low-shot (1-shot) or very large novel-class settings, leaving open whether the set-based objectives scale gracefully as the number of novel classes grows.
