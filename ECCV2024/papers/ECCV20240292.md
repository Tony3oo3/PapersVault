## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models struggle with open-domain visual entity recognition over a label space of over 6 million Wikipedia entities, suffering from hallucinations, incorrect granularity, and poor generalization to unseen entities. Prior approaches either use classifier-based retrieval that cannot scale to such a vast ontology or generative models whose outputs are not guaranteed to be grounded in the knowledge base, and none effectively leverage entity-side visual information for disambiguation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a query image (resized to 336×336 with aspect-ratio padding) and a short text question as input, and produces a specific Wikipedia entity identifier (e.g., 'ATR 42') as output. At inference, the model first retrieves top-300 candidate entities from a pre-cached vector database, then dynamically constructs a prefix tree over those candidates to constrain autoregressive token generation, ensuring the output is always a valid entity from the retrieved set. Entity descriptions are truncated to 77 tokens to fit the CLIP text encoder context window.

## Method

AUTOVER extends an autoregressive MLLM (Vicuna-7B/13B) by inserting a special <ret> token whose last-layer hidden state serves as the query representation, and a lightweight two-layer Transformer encoder that fuses CLIP visual and text features of entity images and descriptions into an entity representation. Training jointly optimizes an InfoNCE contrastive loss (query-to-entity retrieval) and a next-token prediction loss, with hard negative mining via two strategies: VISION-HARD (grouping visually similar entities via a pre-trained ViT classifier) and KB-HARD (grouping knowledge-similar entities via Wikidata category hierarchy). At inference, the query representation retrieves top-k candidates from a pre-built entity vector database, a prefix tree (trie) is dynamically constructed over those candidates, and constrained decoding eliminates invalid token paths, guaranteeing the generated text is always grounded to a retrieved entity. This retrieval-augmented constrained decoding framework eliminates hallucinations and enables recognition of entities never seen during training.

## Results

On the OVEN-Wiki validation set, AutoVER-7B achieves 61.5% accuracy on ENTITY SEEN (nearly doubling PaLI-17B's 30.6%) and 21.7% on ENTITY UNSEEN (vs. 12.4% for PaLI-17B), with an overall harmonic mean of 36.8%. On the QUERY split, AutoVER-7B reaches 69.0% SEEN and 31.4% UNSEEN accuracy. On the A-OKVQA-ENT zero-shot generalization benchmark, AutoVER-7B attains 62.8% overall multi-choice accuracy and 55.0% entity-match accuracy, outperforming all zero-shot baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoVER-7B substantially outperforms all discriminative CLIP-based baselines (CLIP, CLIP Fusion, CLIP2CLIP from [28]) by 29–56 points on ENTITY SEEN, confirming that retrieval-augmented generation far exceeds shallow dot-product matching for fine-grained entity recognition. Against the strongest generative baseline PaLI-17B [12], AutoVER-7B nearly doubles ENTITY SEEN accuracy (61.5% vs. 30.6%) and improves ENTITY UNSEEN by 9.3 points, while also leading on QUERY SEEN by 24.8 points. GPT-4V [51] is the only baseline that surpasses AutoVER-7B on QUERY UNSEEN (52.7% vs. 31.4%), yet AutoVER-7B still leads on ENTITY SEEN by 31.7 points and overall harmonic mean by 3.9 points. On the A-OKVQA-ENT zero-shot benchmark, AutoVER-7B (62.8% multi-choice, 55.0% entity-match) outperforms InstructBLIP [14] (52.4%, 37.2%), LLaVA-v1 [43] (12.1%, 42.7%), and OpenFlamingo [4] (6.9%, 36.8%) by large margins. The sole exception is fine-tuned LLaVA-v1.5 [42], which edges out AutoVER-7B on A-OKVQA-ENT multi-choice (72.8% vs. 62.8%) due to in-domain instruction tuning, but still trails on entity-match (46.2% vs. 55.0%). GER-400M [8] achieves 31.5% ENTITY SEEN on the test set, well below AutoVER-7B's 62.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AUTOVER builds on the LLaVA paradigm of treating images as a foreign language within an autoregressive MLLM, and on the generative entity-linking line of work (Cao et al., ICLR 2021) that reframes knowledge grounding as sequence-to-sequence generation. It departs from prior VER approaches in two key ways: (1) instead of using CLIP-based discriminative retrieval or sparse BM25 matching of generated text to entities, it integrates contrastive query-to-entity retrieval directly into the MLLM via a special <ret> token, and (2) instead of standard RAG that appends retrieved items as context, it dynamically constructs a prefix tree (trie) from retrieved candidates to constrain autoregressive decoding, guaranteeing the output is always a valid knowledge-base entity. The hard-negative mining strategies (visual similarity via ViT, knowledge-graph hierarchy via Wikidata) extend contrastive learning beyond the standard in-batch negatives used in CLIP-style training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AUTOVER demonstrates that a 7B MLLM with retrieval-augmented constrained decoding can double the accuracy of a 17B generative model (PaLI-17B) on open-domain visual entity recognition, suggesting that architectural design (constrained generation + integrated retrieval) matters more than raw parameter scale for knowledge-grounded tasks. The ablation showing UNSEEN accuracy collapsing from 19.0% to 0.6% without retrieval, and the [CLS] classifier variant dropping to 0.1%, provides strong evidence that autoregressive generation with external grounding is fundamentally superior to classification for massive-label-space recognition. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the full 5M training pairs (only 10–50% were used due to compute), whether the 35% gap to Human+Search on UNSEEN can be closed, and whether the constrained-decoding paradigm generalizes to other knowledge-grounded generation tasks beyond Wikipedia entities.
