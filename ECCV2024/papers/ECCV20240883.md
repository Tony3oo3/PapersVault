## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) inherit a strong bias from their text pretraining stage, causing them to generate responses that reflect common textual patterns rather than faithfully grounding in the visual input, leading to hallucinated objects, incorrect attributes, and wrong counts. Preference learning for MLLMs remains largely under-explored, and existing approaches either require costly human annotation for preference labels or rely on unstable PPO-based RLHF pipelines that are sample-inefficient and GPU-intensive.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image and a text query and produces a free-form text response. During training, the preference dataset consists of tuples (I, q, r_w, r_l, p) where the image I is encoded via a CLIP vision transformer into token embeddings concatenated with text token embeddings; negative responses are generated either by injecting Gaussian noise into the CLIP image embeddings (weakening the visual signal) or by prompting the LLM component to alter ground-truth captions with plausible but incorrect details. The DPO loss operates on the log-likelihood ratio of winning versus losing responses relative to a frozen reference model.

## Method

BPO (Bootstrapped Preference Optimization) is a DPO variant that automatically constructs preference pairs at scale by bootstrapping negative responses from the model itself. Two complementary strategies generate negatives: (1) image-weakened prompting applies Gaussian masks to CLIP image embeddings, progressively weakening the visual signal so the MLLM's output is dominated by pretraining distribution and exposes biased tokens; (2) LLM bias injection uses carefully designed prompts and few-shot examples to have the LLM component modify ground-truth responses by swapping object existence, attributes, or counts with common but incorrect alternatives. These bootstrapped negatives are paired with high-quality annotations from ShareGPT4V, LLaVAR, and LLaVA-Instruct as positives, and the model is fine-tuned with the DPO objective (Eq. 4) using LoRA (rank 64) on LLaVA1.5 checkpoints. The key insight is reframing pretraining bias as an 'old preference' and visual grounding as a 'new preference,' so that the DPO margin directly suppresses the former while reinforcing the latter without requiring an external reward model.

## Results

On MM-Vet, LLaVA1.5-7B-BPO achieves a total score of 36.8, matching the larger LLaVA1.5-13B baseline (36.8) and surpassing the 7B baseline (31.7) by 5.1 points. LLaVA1.5-13B-BPO further improves to 41.4 total, a 4.6-point gain over its baseline. On Object-HalBench, BPO substantially reduces hallucination: object-level hallucination drops from 23.7% to 15.1% (7B) and from 21.8% to 12.9% (13B). On LLaVA-Wild, the 13B-BPO model reaches 74.4, outperforming the 13B baseline (71.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLaVA1.5-7B, BPO improves the MM-Vet total from 31.7 to 36.8 (+5.1) and reduces object-level hallucination from 23.7% to 15.1% (−8.6 pp). Against LLaVA1.5-13B, the 7B-BPO model matches its MM-Vet total (36.8 vs. 36.8) while the 13B-BPO model pushes it to 41.4 (+4.6), with hallucination dropping from 21.8% to 12.9%. Compared to MiniGPT-4-8B (22.1) and BLIP-2-12B (22.4), the 7B-BPO model leads by over 14 points on MM-Vet total. InstructBLIP-14B (25.6) is also clearly outperformed. On LLaVA-Wild, LLaVA1.5-13B-BPO (74.4) surpasses InstructBLIP-14B (58.2) and LLaVA-13B (67.3) by substantial margins, confirming that preference learning yields gains beyond what larger model scale alone provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) [46] from text-only LLMs to multimodal settings, proposing BPO as a practical variant that avoids the instability and GPU overhead of PPO-based RLHF. It draws on visual contrastive decoding [28] for the image-weakened prompting strategy and on the broader RLHF/alignment literature [2, 42, 57] for the preference-learning framework. The key departure from prior MLLM alignment work (e.g., LLaVA-RLHF [55], Silkie [32]) is the fully automated construction of negative responses by bootstrapping from the model itself, eliminating the need for human-annotated preference pairs or external reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a scalable, low-cost recipe for constructing multimodal preference datasets that directly targets the pretraining-bias problem, which is a persistent and practically important limitation of current MLLMs. The ablation showing that SFT on the same positive data yields only marginal gains while BPO delivers substantial improvements validates that the preference-learning formulation (not just more data) is what drives the benefit. Open questions include whether the bootstrapping strategies generalize beyond LLaVA1.5 architectures, whether the approach transfers to other modalities (video, audio), and whether the LoRA-based training limits the magnitude of achievable gains.
