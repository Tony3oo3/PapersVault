## Problem

Existing semi-supervised segmentation methods for histopathology images optimize pixel-wise accuracy but frequently produce topological errors—incorrectly merged or separated glands, false-positive islands, and missing holes—that significantly alter morphological interpretation. Prior topology-aware losses require clean ground-truth annotations, making them inapplicable to unlabeled data where only noisy predictions are available. This paper addresses the unique challenge of enforcing topological consistency between two noisy prediction maps in a teacher-student semi-supervised setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D RGB histopathology images as input, applying strong augmentations to the student branch and weak augmentations to the teacher branch. It produces a pixel-wise likelihood map (segmentation mask) from which 0-dimensional persistence diagrams are extracted for topological loss computation. The method is evaluated on gland segmentation (CRAG, GlaS) and multi-organ nuclei segmentation (MoNuSeg) at 10% and 20% labeled data ratios.

## Method

TopoSemiSeg builds on the Mean Teacher framework and introduces a noise-aware topological consistency loss that decomposes each model's persistence diagram into signal dots (persistence > threshold φ) and noise dots (persistence ≤ φ) using the stability theorem as justification. The signal topology consistency loss enforces matching between teacher and student signal dots via the squared Wasserstein distance, while the noisy topology removal loss minimizes the total persistence of the student's noise diagram to eliminate spurious structures. Both losses are differentiable through the birth/death critical points of the student likelihood map, allowing gradient-based optimization. The teacher is updated via exponential moving average of student parameters, and the overall objective combines supervised cross-entropy/Dice loss on labeled data with pixel-wise and topological consistency losses on unlabeled data.

## Results

On the 20% labeled data setting, TopoSemiSeg achieves the best Betti Error across all three datasets: 0.227 on CRAG (vs. 0.422 for XNet, the next best), 0.551 on GlaS (vs. 0.735 for XNet), and 6.661 on MoNuSeg. On pixel-wise metrics, TopoSemiSeg is competitive, achieving Dice Obj of 0.884 (CRAG), 0.878 (GlaS), and 0.783 (MoNuSeg), within 0.5–1% of the best pixel-wise baselines. The method also attains the lowest Betti Matching Error on CRAG (10.475) and GlaS (8.300), and the lowest VOI on CRAG (0.758) and GlaS (0.811), confirming consistent topological superiority without sacrificing pixel-level accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoSemiSeg clearly outperforms all baselines on Betti Error for CRAG (0.227 vs. 0.422 for XNet, the closest competitor) and GlaS (0.551 vs. 0.735 for XNet), representing roughly a 46% and 25% reduction respectively. On MoNuSeg, URPC achieves a marginally lower Betti Error (5.325 vs. 6.661) and Betti Matching Error (193.429 vs. 196.357), but TopoSemiSeg matches or exceeds it on VOI (0.789 vs. 0.788) and Dice Obj (0.783 vs. 0.779). Against the strongest pixel-wise baseline HCE*, TopoSemiSeg trades a small accuracy gap (0.905 vs. 0.910 on CRAG) for a substantially lower Betti Error (0.227 vs. 0.875). Compared to the recent wavelet-based XNet, TopoSemiSeg achieves comparable pixel-wise scores (within 0.1–0.5% on Dice Obj) while halving the Betti Error on CRAG and GlaS. The older baselines (MT, EM, UA-MT, URPC) are consistently outperformed on both pixel-wise and topology-wise metrics across all datasets, with Betti Error gaps of 1–10×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) by adding a topological consistency objective, but departs fundamentally from prior topology-aware segmentation losses (TopoLoss [18], Clough et al. [6]) that assume clean ground-truth topology. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that in the semi-supervised setting, both teacher and student predictions carry noisy persistence diagrams, making direct diagram matching counterproductive. The authors introduce a signal-noise decomposition of persistence diagrams (using a persistence threshold) and pair it with a total-persistence-based noise removal loss, creating the first topology-aware semi-supervised loss for histopathology segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topological errors (merged or split glands/nuclei) have direct clinical consequences for morphological assessment in pathology, yet prior semi-supervised methods optimized only pixel-wise accuracy. The practical finding that naive topological consistency (direct PD matching without decomposition) actually degrades performance below no topology loss at all highlights a non-obvious pitfall for anyone attempting to add topological objectives to semi-supervised pipelines. Open questions include: making the persistence threshold φ adaptive rather than manually tuned, extending to 3D volumes, and addressing the acknowledged limitation that the teacher's own topological predictions can be unreliable. The method is currently 2D-only and the failure mode on extremely dense objects suggests the decomposition threshold may need to be object-density-aware.
