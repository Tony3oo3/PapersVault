## Problem

Vision-language models for language-based object detection behave like bags-of-words, failing to parse complex compositional queries involving attributes, shapes, and spatial relations. Prior work addressed this by augmenting only the text domain with hard negatives, but the authors show this is insufficient because the model never learns dense image-text alignment. The paper asks how to automatically generate the dense, multi-domain training triplets needed to teach true compositional understanding without expensive human annotation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the detector receives an RGB image and a free-form language query (e.g., 'an avocado spread on a toasted bagel lying on a cutting board') and outputs bounding boxes for objects matching the query in an open-set, multi-label setting where a description may match zero, one, or multiple instances. During training, the method consumes synthetic triplets of the form <generated image, object description, pseudo bounding box>, where descriptions are 6–12 words long and images are produced by a diffusion model conditioned on those descriptions. The training also incorporates standard detection data (Objects365) to preserve localization precision and prevent domain shift.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, a 'weak-to-strong' synthetic triplet generator uses an LLM to produce diverse object descriptions per category, a text-to-image diffusion model (Pixart) to render corresponding images, and a task-decomposition labeling strategy that breaks a complex phrase-grounding problem into multiple simpler noun-phrase detection sub-tasks, combining the resulting boxes into accurate pseudo-labels. Second, a compositional contrastive learning objective trains the detector on these triplets with two synergistic signals: (a) intra-class negative contrast, where the query is augmented with descriptions of the same object category from other images, forcing the model to attend to distinguishing attributes; and (b) textural-structural contrast, where an NLP parser identifies subject vs. non-subject noun phrases, the model is trained to suppress detections for non-subject entities (structural negatives) while positively aligning the full sentence with the subject's box (sentence-level positive) and also detecting non-subject objects when queried directly (structural positives). The visual backbone is frozen and real detection data is co-trained to prevent overfitting to synthetic artifacts.

## Results

On the OmniLabel benchmark, the method improves GLIP-T from 19.3 to 24.3 AP (+5.0) and FIBER-B from 25.7 to 30.5 AP (+4.8). On the D³ benchmark, GLIP-T improves from 19.1 to 26.0 Full AP (+6.9) and FIBER-B from 22.7 to 26.5 (+3.8). The gains are especially pronounced for long queries, where GLIP-T's AP-dL doubles from 8.2 to 16.4. Combined with DesCo, the best configuration (DesCo-FIBER + Ours) achieves 32.0 AP on OmniLabel and 30.8 on D³ Full, establishing a new state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method substantially outperforms all seven baselines. Against the weakest baselines, RegionCLIP (2.7 AP) and Detic (8.0 AP) on OmniLabel, the proposed method achieves 24.3–32.0 AP, a gap of over 16 AP. On D³, Grounding-DINO (20.7 Full) and OFA-DOD (21.6 Full) are surpassed by 5.3–9.1 AP. The direct architectural baselines GLIP-T (19.3→24.3, +5.0 AP) and FIBER-B (25.7→30.5, +4.8 AP) show the most consistent gains across all sub-metrics, with the largest relative improvement on long descriptions (AP-dL: 8.2→16.4 for GLIP-T). The method also complements DesCo, the prior text-augmentation approach: DesCo-GLIP improves from 23.8 to 26.5 AP and DesCo-FIBER from 29.3 to 32.0 AP on OmniLabel, demonstrating that synthetic triplet generation in both image and text domains provides gains beyond text-only augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GLIP and FIBER as base language-based object detectors, extending them with a synthetic data generation pipeline and a new compositional contrastive learning objective. It departs from prior text-augmentation approaches (DesCo, DAC) that only enrich the language domain with hard negatives, instead generating dense triplets in both image and text domains using LLMs and text-to-image diffusion models. The weak-to-strong labeling strategy—decomposing complex phrase grounding into simpler detection sub-tasks to obtain accurate pseudo-bounding boxes—is a novel data construction idea not present in prior grounding or detection work. The compositional contrastive learning formulation (intra-class description negatives plus structural subject/non-subject positives and negatives) is a new loss design that goes beyond standard region-word alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented weakness of VL models (bag-of-words behavior on complex queries) with a scalable, model-agnostic pipeline that could be applied to any existing language-based detector. The finding that naively adding synthetic triplets degrades performance, and that the specific contrastive formulation is essential, provides useful guidance for future synthetic-data training. Limitations include reliance on diffusion model fidelity (artifacts in complex scenes), the domain gap between synthetic and real images, and the dependence on a weak detector for pseudo-labeling, which may bottleneck quality for very novel compositions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D detection, and whether the domain gap can be further closed as diffusion models improve.
