## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting couples scene appearance and geometry within per-Gaussian color attributes, making appearance editing operations like texture swapping infeasible compared to mesh-based representations where texture maps are naturally separable. Existing 3D-GS editing methods still follow this entangled representation, and NeRF-based texture mapping approaches (e.g., NeuTex) are too slow for real-time rendering due to per-ray MLP evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240647/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images and SfM camera poses as input, and produces a disentangled representation consisting of 3D Gaussians (geometry), a learnable 2D texture map defined on a unit-sphere UV domain (appearance), and a UV mapping MLP. For editing, a user-supplied texture map replaces the learned one, and the system renders novel views in real time (~58 FPS on RTX 2080 Ti). Training requires ~90 minutes per scene on a single GPU, and the texture resolution is decoupled from the number of Gaussians, allowing high-resolution textures with relatively few geometric primitives.

## Method

Texture-GS uses a two-stage training procedure: first, a UV mapping MLP is learned via cycle-consistency and symmetric Chamfer distance constraints applied to 3D points back-projected from a pre-trained 3D-GS depth map; second, the 2D texture is optimized through differentiable rendering. The key technical contribution is an efficient UV mapping at render time: rather than evaluating the MLP at every ray-Gaussian intersection, the method computes a first-order Taylor expansion of the MLP at each Gaussian center, reducing the per-pixel UV lookup to a small matrix product. To enable ray-Gaussian intersection computation, Gaussians are flattened along their surface normal (smallest eigenvector of the covariance matrix) via a zero-one opacity regularization, and a normal loss supervised by SfM pseudo-ground-truth enforces correct orientation. The final color is a sum of a bilinear texture lookup (view-independent diffuse) and per-Gaussian spherical harmonic coefficients (view-dependent residual), with a no-SH rendering loss encouraging most appearance to reside in the texture.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, Texture-GS achieves a PSNR of 30.03, L1 of 0.0135, and LPIPS of 0.1440 at 58 FPS on a single RTX 2080 Ti GPU. This is approximately 2000× faster than NeRF-based baselines (NeuTex and NGF at 0.025 FPS) while maintaining competitive rendering quality. Training takes 90 minutes per scene, compared to 30 hours for NeuTex. Pruning 80% of Gaussians still yields 28.75 PSNR at 82 FPS, demonstrating extensibility to lower-end hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeuTex (PSNR 30.39, LPIPS 0.1613, 0.025 FPS), Texture-GS trades a small PSNR drop (30.03 vs 30.39) for a 2320× speedup in rendering (58 vs 0.025 FPS) and a 20× reduction in training time (90 min vs 30 hours), while also improving LPIPS (0.1440 vs 0.1613). Against NGF (PSNR 29.44, LPIPS 0.1506, 0.025 FPS), Texture-GS wins on all three quality metrics (PSNR +0.59, L1 −0.0031, LPIPS −0.0066) and is orders of magnitude faster. Against vanilla 3D-GS (PSNR 30.99, L1 0.0121, LPIPS 0.1079, 198 FPS), Texture-GS is slightly worse on every quality metric and 3.4× slower in rendering, but this is the necessary cost for the disentangled representation that enables real-time texture swapping and painting—capabilities that 3D-GS does not support. The paper positions Texture-GS as the best trade-off: near-3D-GS quality with the editing flexibility of a mesh-based pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the UV mapping concept from NeuTex (which operates within NeRF's ray-marching paradigm) to the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline, where per-ray evaluation of an MLP is prohibitively expensive. It departs from existing 3D-GS editing methods (GaussianEditor, Point'n Move, Gaussian Grouping) that treat per-Gaussian color attributes as the sole appearance representation, by introducing a learnable 2D texture queried at per-pixel UV coordinates. The key technical departure is the use of a first-order Taylor expansion of the UV mapping MLP at each Gaussian center to approximate per-pixel UV coordinates during splatting, reconciling the per-Gaussian efficiency of 3D-GS with the per-pixel texture variation needed for smooth, editable textures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Texture-GS provides a practical bridge between 3D-GS's real-time rendering speed and the editing flexibility of mesh-based pipelines, enabling operations like global texture swapping and fine-grained texture painting at 58 FPS on consumer hardware. The limitation to a single unit-sphere texture space restricts applicability to single objects and excludes outdoor or multi-object scenes; extending to multi-chart UV mapping (à la Nuvo) is identified as future work. Open questions include handling thin structures and cavities where the UV mapping degenerates, and whether the disentanglement framework extends to dynamic or 4D Gaussian representations.
