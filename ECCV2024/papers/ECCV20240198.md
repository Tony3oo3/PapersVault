## Problem

Existing visual math benchmarks contain excessive textual redundancy that duplicates diagram content, making it unclear whether MLLMs truly interpret diagrams or simply read text to solve problems. Additionally, binary correct/incorrect evaluation cannot distinguish between models that arrive at wrong answers through different levels of reasoning quality, and prior benchmarks either focus too narrowly on plane geometry or dilute math evaluation with peripheral tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240198/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each of the 2,612 problems is transformed into six versions that systematically vary the text-vision information split (from full text + diagram to diagram-only), yielding 15,672 test samples. MLLMs receive either text, a diagram, or both depending on the version, and produce free-form or multiple-choice answers. Evaluation produces two outputs: a naive accuracy score and a CoT score computed as 0.7×(mean per-step correctness) + 0.3×(final-answer correctness), accompanied by GPT-4V-generated error analysis for each reasoning step.

## Method

The benchmark's core mechanism is a three-category text decomposition (Descriptive Information, Implicit Property, Essential Condition) that expert annotators use to progressively strip textual content while transferring critical elements into the diagram, creating six problem versions that form a gradient from text-dominant to vision-only. For fine-grained assessment, a two-phase CoT Evaluation strategy first uses GPT-4 (language-only) to extract N pivotal reasoning steps from the MLLM output without access to the question or answer, mitigating the judge's own problem-solving bias. Then GPT-4V scores each extracted step as correct or incorrect against the original question, diagram, and ground truth, aggregating into a weighted final score (α=0.7 for intermediate steps). This design avoids pre-defined key-step templates, accommodating diverse solution pathways and variable reasoning lengths across models.

## Results

On MATHVERSE's testmini subset (788 problems, 4,728 instances), GPT-4V achieves the best closed-source MLLM accuracy of 39.4% (All) and 54.7% (Text Dominant), while MAVIS-7B leads open-source models at 27.5% (All) and 41.4% (Text Dominant). Human performance reaches 67.7% (All), revealing a substantial gap of ~28 points over the best model. A striking finding is that several MLLMs (e.g., Gemini-Pro at 27.3% vs. 26.3%, Math-LLaVA at 35.7% vs. 21.2%) score higher on the Text-Only version than on the Text-Dominant version with the diagram, indicating the visual input actively harms their reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V (39.4% All) outperforms all other closed-source MLLMs, leading Qwen-VL-Max (25.3%) by 14.1 points and Gemini-Pro (23.5%) by 15.9 points on the overall accuracy. Among open-source models, MAVIS-7B (27.5% All) surpasses LLaVA-NeXT (23.4%) by 4.1 points and Math-LLaVA (19.0%) by 8.5 points, though its Vision-Only score (14.6%) drops sharply relative to its Text-Dominant score (41.4%), exposing a heavy reliance on textual cues. A critical finding is that Math-LLaVA (35.7% Text-Only vs. 21.2% Text-Dominant) and Gemini-Pro (27.3% vs. 26.3%) achieve higher accuracy *without* the diagram than with it, demonstrating that the visual input acts as a distraction rather than a signal. Under the CoT evaluation (Table 3), GPT-4V scores 53.6% (All) versus GPT-4's 52.1%, a marginal 1.5-point gain that suggests the visual modality contributes little to intermediate reasoning quality. The best open-source CoT score is InternLM-XC2 at 27.4% (All), still trailing GPT-4V by over 26 points, highlighting a large gap in multi-modal mathematical reasoning between closed and open models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a corrective to existing visual math benchmarks (GeoQA, UniGeo, MathVista, MMMU), identifying three specific deficiencies: textual redundancy that lets MLLMs bypass diagram interpretation, binary correct/incorrect scoring that obscures reasoning quality, and either too-narrow (plane geometry only) or too-broad (peripheral tasks, college-level) scope. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, it repurposes the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (GPT-4/GPT-4V) into a structured two-phase CoT evaluation and introduces a systematic six-version ablation over text-visual information content. The six-version formulation—progressively shifting information from text to diagram—is the paper's core methodological departure from prior benchmarks, which typically present a single fixed text+image pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark's central finding—that most MLLMs rely on textual redundancy rather than genuinely interpreting diagrams, and that some models perform better without visual input—challenges a core assumption in the MLLM evaluation community and points toward the need for math-specific vision encoders. The CoT evaluation strategy offers a reusable methodology for fine-grained reasoning assessment beyond binary scoring. Open questions include whether the six-version framework generalizes to other diagram-heavy domains (chemistry, physics), whether the visual-encoding bottleneck can be addressed through targeted pretraining, and whether the GPT-4V-based CoT scoring introduces its own biases that limit cross-model comparability.
