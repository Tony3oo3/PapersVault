## Problem

NeRF-based animatable head models achieve photorealistic quality but are limited to interactive frame rates (10–15 fps), while 3D Gaussian Splatting offers real-time rendering but lacks a natural mechanism for expression-driven animation. The paper addresses this gap by asking how to make 3DGS natively animatable for heads without sacrificing either rendering speed or visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a monocular RGB video of a single subject (~1500–2500 frames) along with pre-computed per-frame head poses and expression weights from a 3DMM tracker (FLAME with 52 expression coefficients, or FaceWarehouse). At inference, given a sequence of expression vectors and camera poses, it renders photorealistic head frames at 512² resolution in ~4 ms per frame (~250 fps), supporting novel-view synthesis and cross-subject expression transfer while preserving the target subject's identity.

## Method

HeadGaS extends each 3D Gaussian with a learnable latent feature basis F ∈ R^{B×f_dim} (f_dim=32) that is linearly blended with the current frame's expression vector to produce a frame-specific feature, which a two-layer MLP (64 hidden channels) maps to expression-dependent color and opacity. The key design choice is to model facial dynamics through opacity and color modulation (over-representation) rather than geometric deformation: multiple Gaussians occupy the same facial region and become visible or transparent depending on the expression, avoiding the optimization instability of moving Gaussian centers. Rendering uses the standard 3DGS tile-based differentiable rasterizer with spherical harmonics (degree 3) for view-dependent color, and training combines L1, SSIM, and VGG perceptual losses with adaptive densification and pruning inherited from 3DGS.

## Results

On the INSTA dataset, HeadGaS achieves PSNR 32.50, SSIM 0.971, and LPIPS 0.033, outperforming the strongest baseline NeRFBlendShape (PSNR 30.52) by ~2 dB. On the NBS dataset, HeadGaS reaches PSNR 36.66 vs. NeRFBlendShape's 34.34. Rendering time is 0.004 s per 512² frame, a >25× speedup over NeRFBlendShape (0.10 s) and >12× over INSTA (0.05 s), placing HeadGaS at ~250 fps in real-time territory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HeadGaS outperforms all eight baselines on the INSTA and NBS datasets across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), with a consistent ~2 dB PSNR advantage over the closest competitors NeRFBlendShape and PointAvatar. On the PointAvatar dataset, HeadGaS matches PointAvatar's PSNR (25.99 vs. 26.04) while improving SSIM (0.897 vs. 0.885) and LPIPS (0.108 vs. 0.147), suggesting better structural fidelity despite a negligible PSNR gap. The rendering speed advantage is the most dramatic: HeadGaS renders in 0.004 s per frame, which is 25× faster than NeRFBlendShape (0.10 s), 12.5× faster than INSTA (0.05 s), and over 2000× faster than IMAvatar (12.34 s) and NeRFACE (9.68 s). Among the faster baselines, FLARE (0.11 s) and AvatarMAV (0.85 s) are still an order of magnitude slower than HeadGaS. The paper attributes the quality gap to HeadGaS's opacity-and-color modulation strategy, which avoids the geometric inconsistencies and floater artifacts that arise when deforming Gaussian positions directly (as INSTA does with mesh-based deformation).

## Relation to Prior Work

HeadGaS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [20] with per-Gaussian latent feature bases that are linearly blended with expression weights from parametric head models (FLAME, FaceWarehouse), drawing conceptual inspiration from blendshape 3DMMs but operating in a learned feature space rather than mesh or PCA space. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based head avatar line (NeRFBlendShape, INSTA, NeRFACE) by replacing implicit volumetric representations with explicit Gaussian primitives for real-time rendering, and from concurrent 3DGS head avatar works (FlashAvatar, GaussianAvatars) by avoiding explicit geometric deformation in favor of opacity and color modulation. The feature-blending mechanism is analogous to NeRFBlendShape's hash-grid basis blending but applied per-Gaussian, and the opacity-driven dynamics represent a deliberate departure from the intuitive approach of moving Gaussian centers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10×+ speedup to ~250 fps at 512² resolution makes high-fidelity animatable head avatars practical for real-time applications (AR/VR, teleconferencing, gaming) that were previously limited to interactive rates (10–15 fps) with NeRF-based methods. The ablation finding that opacity/color modulation outperforms geometric deformation (shifting μ and R) challenges the natural assumption that 3D animation requires spatial motion, suggesting that over-representation with visibility control is a more stable optimization strategy within the 3DGS framework. Open questions include the memory overhead of per-Gaussian feature bases (B × f_dim floats each), generalization to under-observed expressions from non-frontal views, and whether the opacity-based dynamics approach transfers to full-body avatars or other deformable objects.
