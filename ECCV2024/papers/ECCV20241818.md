## Problem

Existing multi-sensor fusion methods for 3D perception either rely on computationally heavy transformer cross-attention (hard to adapt to BEV segmentation) or produce noisy unified BEV features due to inherent misalignment between LiDAR and camera modalities. The potential of diffusion models for multi-modal feature fusion and denoising in 3D perception remains unexplored, and no prior approach leverages generative synthesis to compensate for sensor failures in a unified detection-and-segmentation pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241818/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 32-beam LiDAR point clouds (voxelized at 0.075 m for detection, 0.1 m for segmentation) and 6-camera RGB images at 448×800 resolution, processed by VoxelNet and Swin-T backbones respectively. The fused BEV feature is generated through a multi-step diffusion denoising process and then fed to a TransFusion-L detection head (producing 3D bounding boxes for 10 nuScenes classes) and a segmentation head (producing a 7-class semantic map over a 100 m × 100 m BEV grid). Notably, during inference the model can synthesize features for a missing modality, effectively operating with degraded sensor input while maintaining or exceeding single-sensor performance.

## Method

DifFUSER replaces the standard fusion module with a conditional latent diffusion process built on a cMini-BiFPN architecture: three encoder blocks (with GSM modulation) process a noisy BEV feature x_t^F conditioned on a partially masked clean feature x̃_0^F, and three decoder blocks perform bidirectional top-down/bottom-up fusion to produce the final BEV representation. The GSM module applies FiLM-style scale, shift, and sigmoid-gated modulation derived from the condition, providing stronger trajectory shaping than time-step-only conditioning. Training uses x_0-prediction with MSE diffusion loss combined with focal segmentation loss and Hungarian-matched detection loss, under a Progressive Sensor Dropout Training (PSDT) schedule that linearly ramps camera or LiDAR feature dropout from 0% to 25% over training epochs. At inference, DDIM sampling with 8 steps (or 4 for efficiency) denoises the fused features, and the generative process naturally synthesizes missing modality features when one sensor is dropped.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, DifFUSER achieves a state-of-the-art 70.04% mIOU for BEV map segmentation, surpassing the BEVFusion baseline (62.7%) by 7.3 percentage points and outperforming all camera-only and LiDAR-only methods by a wide margin. For 3D object detection on the nuScenes test set, DifFUSER reaches NDS 73.8 and mAP 71.3, improving over BEVFusion (NDS 72.9, mAP 70.2) by +0.9 and +1.1 respectively, and competing closely with the best transformer-based fusion method CMT (NDS 74.1, mAP 72.0). The model also demonstrates robustness to sensor dropout, maintaining or exceeding single-sensor performance when either LiDAR or camera input is removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For BEV map segmentation, DifFUSER decisively outperforms all 20 compared methods: it beats the strongest multi-modal baseline BEVFusion [22] by 7.3 mIOU points (70.0 vs. 62.7), surpasses camera-only methods like LSS [30] (44.4) and BEVFusion-C (56.6) by 25.6 and 13.4 points respectively, and exceeds LiDAR-only methods CenterPoint [49] (48.6) and PointPillars [12] (43.8) by over 20 points. For 3D object detection on the nuScenes test set, DifFUSER (NDS 73.8, mAP 71.3) improves over BEVFusion [22] (72.9/70.2) and DeepInteraction [48] (73.4/70.8), LinK [26] (73.4/69.8), MSMDFusion [11] (73.0/71.0), and TransFusion [1] (71.7/68.9), but trails the top transformer-based methods CMT [46] (74.1/72.0), LargeKernel-F [7] (74.1/71.1), and FocalFormer3D [5] (73.9/71.6) by 0.2–0.3 NDS. On the validation set (Table 3), DifFUSER (NDS 72.6, mAP 70.0) is within 0.3 NDS of CMT [46] (72.9/70.3) and DeepInteraction [48] (72.6/69.9), confirming competitive performance. The robustness experiments show that under sensor dropout, DifFUSER exceeds single-sensor baselines (e.g., TransFusion-L at NDS 70.1) thanks to its generative feature synthesis, whereas CMT [46] can only match single-sensor performance under the same conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the denoising diffusion framework (DDPM) from image generation into a multi-sensor feature fusion module for 3D perception, a setting where diffusion had not previously been applied. Architecturally, it adapts the BiFPN weighted-fusion design from EfficientDet into a compact cMini-BiFPN structure that serves as the latent diffusion backbone, and introduces a FiLM-style GSM conditioning module that goes beyond the standard time-step-only conditioning of prior diffusion models. The overall pipeline builds directly on BEVFusion's unified BEV representation (Swin-T + VoxelNet backbones, LSS lifting), replacing its simple concatenation-based fusion with the diffusion-based cMini-BiFPN. Unlike concurrent diffusion-for-detection works (DiffusionDet, DiffBEV) that operate on a single modality, DifFUSER is the first to use diffusion explicitly as a cross-modal fusion and denoising mechanism between LiDAR and camera features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.3% mIOU gain in BEV map segmentation and the demonstrated ability to synthesize missing sensor features (exceeding single-sensor baselines under dropout) address two practically critical needs in autonomous driving: higher-quality fused representations and operational resilience to sensor failure. However, the method is validated only on nuScenes, multi-task joint training exhibits negative transfer (both tasks degrade), and the 8-step DDIM inference at 123 ms may be too slow for real-time deployment despite the 4-step approximation. Open questions include whether the diffusion-fusion paradigm generalizes to other sensor combinations or datasets, whether the negative transfer in joint training can be mitigated, and whether the PSDT dropout schedule can be made adaptive rather than linear.
