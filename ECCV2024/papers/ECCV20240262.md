## Problem

Existing embodied instruction-following benchmarks face a fundamental trade-off: synthetic environments (e.g., ALFRED) support rich object interaction but suffer from visual domain gaps and single-room constraints, while 3D-captured environments (e.g., HM3D, Gibson) offer photorealism and multi-room scale but lack object interactivity. This gap prevents agents trained in simulation from being readily deployable in real-world household settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240262/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives an egocentric RGB frame and a free-form natural language instruction (a high-level goal plus step-by-step sub-instructions, 30,696 total from 93 AMT annotators) at each time step, and outputs a discrete action (navigation or interaction) along with an object interaction mask when applicable. The environment comprises 150 3D-captured residential scenes with 112 object classes (86 pickupable, 26 receptacle), spanning multi-room layouts with an average navigable area more than three times larger than ALFRED's single rooms.

## Method

REALFRED is constructed by 3D-scanning 150 real residential properties at 2.5-meter intervals using a Matterport-style scanner, then manually separating objects from the background mesh and adding state-relevant textures (e.g., stain for dirty state) before reconstructing the scene in Unity/AI2-THOR. Expert demonstrations are generated via a PDDL-based planner over seven task types inherited from ALFRED, and free-form language instructions are collected and validated through AMT with a voting survey. The empirical study evaluates seven SOTA baselines (ABP, MOCA, Seq2Seq, HLSM, FILM, LLM-Planner, CAPEAM) and shows all consistently underperform their ALFRED results, with imitation-learning methods outperforming spatial-map-reconstruction methods due to limited field-of-view causing single-room-sized map failures. A sim-to-real transfer experiment using CycleGAN and UVCGAN-v2 as 'goggles' confirms that domain adaptation alone cannot close the performance gap, necessitating training directly in scanned environments.

## Results

On the REALFRED benchmark, the best-performing method ABP achieves a test seen success rate of 27.44% and test unseen success rate of 3.54%, with goal-condition success rates of 35.81% and 10.57% respectively. All baselines show substantially lower performance compared to their reported results on the original ALFRED benchmark, confirming that the larger multi-room 3D-captured environments pose significantly greater challenges. Human evaluators achieve 85.00% SR and 91.30% GC on the test unseen split, highlighting a large gap between current methods and human-level performance. In sim-to-real transfer experiments, the real-to-real agent (2.41% SR on multi-room tasks) outperforms the sim-to-real agent (0.115% SR) by over 20×, and GAN-based domain adaptation (CycleGAN, UVCGAN) provides only marginal PLWSR improvements without improving SR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABP [33] (Imitation Learning) is the strongest baseline, achieving 27.44% test seen SR and 3.54% test unseen SR, outperforming the best Spatial Map Reconst. method CAPEAM [34] (15.61% / 2.87%) by roughly 12 and 0.7 percentage points respectively. Notably, the performance ordering reverses relative to ALFRED: on ALFRED, Spatial Map Reconst. methods outperformed Imitation Learning methods, but on REALFRED the Imitation Learning group (ABP, MOCA) consistently surpasses the Spatial Map Reconst. group (HLSM, FILM, LLM-Planner, CAPEAM), which the authors attribute to limited field-of-view causing incomplete map reconstruction in multi-room settings. Seq2Seq [61] and MOCA [62] (the original ALFRED baselines) degrade severely, with Seq2Seq achieving near-zero success on unseen splits. In the sim-to-real transfer study (Table 4), the sim-to-real agent trained on ALFRED synthetic data achieves only 0.115% SR on multi-room tasks versus 2.41% for the real-to-real agent trained on REALFRED scans; CycleGAN [79] and UVCGAN [70] goggle methods improve PLWSR from 0.012 to 0.016 and 0.046 respectively but leave SR unchanged at 0.115%, indicating that visual domain adaptation alone cannot bridge the sim-to-real gap. Overall, all methods fall far short of the 85% human SR, underscoring the difficulty of the REALFRED benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

REALFRED directly extends the ALFRED benchmark (Shridhar et al., 2020) by replacing its synthetic single-room AI2-THOR scenes with 3D-captured multi-room environments, while preserving the same task taxonomy, PDDL-based expert demonstration generation, and evaluation protocol. It bridges two previously separate lines of work: photorealistic scanned environments (Matterport3D, HM3D, Gibson) that lack object interactivity, and synthetic interactive simulators (AI2-THOR, RoboTHOR, Habitat) that suffer from visual domain gaps. The sim-to-real experiments additionally draw on GAN-based domain adaptation (CycleGAN, UVCGAN) from the robotics literature to quantify the visual gap between synthetic and scanned environments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REALFRED exposes a critical deployment gap: state-of-the-art embodied instruction-following agents trained in synthetic environments consistently degrade in photorealistic multi-room settings, and GAN-based domain adaptation only partially closes the sim-to-real gap (e.g., 0.33% vs. 2.61% SR on single-room tasks). This motivates the community to develop agents that are robust to visual domain shift and capable of navigating larger, more cluttered spaces. Open questions include whether the task set (limited to seven ALFRED task types, English-only, no bimanual manipulation) is sufficient for real-world household assistance, and whether the manual object-separation pipeline from scans generalizes to arbitrary real-world clutter.
