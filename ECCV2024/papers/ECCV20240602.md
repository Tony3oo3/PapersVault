## Problem

No prior method simultaneously achieves multi-person processing, whole-body mesh recovery (including hands and facial expressions), 3D location estimation in camera space, and optional camera-awareness in a single model. Multi-person approaches like ROMP and PSVT are limited to body-only meshes, while whole-body methods like OSX and SMPLer-X require per-person crops and cannot handle variable numbers of people in one forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240602/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (448×448 to 896×896, aspect-ratio preserved with zero-padding) and optionally a pinhole camera intrinsic matrix (focal length and principal point). It outputs, for each detected person, the full SMPL-X parameter set (53×3 axis-angle pose, 10-dim shape, 10-dim expression), a 3D root location in camera coordinates, and a scalar depth — all in one forward pass with a variable number of people. The number of outputs is determined by a patch-level detection threshold, so the model naturally handles zero to many people without a separate detector.

## Method

A DINOv2-initialized ViT backbone encodes the image into patch-level tokens, from which a lightweight MLP predicts a per-patch probability of containing a head keypoint plus a sub-patch 2D offset. Detected tokens serve as queries in the Human Perception Head (HPH), a 2-block cross-attention module where each query attends to all image tokens as keys/values, followed by self-attention and MLP layers, enabling each person's prediction to leverage global context. Depth is regressed in log-space (nearness) and back-projected through the camera model to yield 3D location; optionally, camera intrinsics are injected as Fourier-encoded ray-direction embeddings concatenated to each patch feature. To address the difficulty of regressing fine-grained hand poses from a single low-resolution view, the authors introduce CUFFS, a 60K-image synthetic dataset of close-up full-body renderings with diverse hand poses sampled from BEDLAM, AGORA, UBody, and InterHand2.6M, which substantially improves hand accuracy without degrading other metrics.

## Results

On whole-body benchmarks, Multi-HMR (ViT-L/14, 896×896) achieves 42.0 mm PVE-All on EHF (vs. 65.4 for SMPLer-X and 70.8 for OSX), 95.9 mm PVE-All on AGORA-SMPLX, and 51.2 mm PVE-All on UBody-intra, while also being the only method that is both multi-person and single-shot. On body-only benchmarks, the same model reaches 41.7 mm PA-MPJPE on 3DPW (vs. 45.7 for PSVT, 46.9 for BEV, 47.3 for ROMP) and 77.3 mm MPJPE on CMU (vs. 97.7 for PSVT, 105.3 for BEV). With camera intrinsics, depth estimation (MRPE) drops to 514 mm on MuPoTs and 110 mm on CMU, substantially outperforming ROMP (1688/679) and BEV (1884/673). A ViT-S model at 448×448 runs at 30 fps on a V100 GPU while remaining competitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-HMR outperforms all multi-person body-only baselines (ROMP, BEV, PSVT, 3DCrowdNet) by large margins on 3DPW (41.7 vs. 45.7–47.3 PA-MPJPE) and CMU (77.3 vs. 97.7–128.2 MPJPE), while simultaneously predicting hands and face. Against single-person whole-body methods, Multi-HMR (ViT-L) beats SMPLer-X (ViT-L/16) on EHF PVE-All (42.0 vs. 65.4 mm) and UBody PVE-All (51.2 vs. 57.4 mm), and surpasses OSX on EHF (42.0 vs. 70.8) and AGORA (95.9 vs. 122.8). On hand-specific metrics, Multi-HMR achieves 28.9 mm PVE-Hands on EHF, outperforming SMPLer-X (49.4) and OSX (53.7), though PyMAF-X (29.7) and Hand4Whole (39.8) are competitive on this single metric. For depth estimation, the camera-aware variant of Multi-HMR reduces MRPE to 514 mm on MuPoTs and 110 mm on CMU, roughly 3× better than ROMP (1688/679) and BEV (1884/673). The key differentiator is that Multi-HMR is the only method combining multi-person detection, whole-body (hands + face) regression, 3D scene placement, and optional camera awareness in a single forward pass, whereas all baselines require either a separate detector, per-person crops, or assume a fixed camera.

## Relation to Prior Work

Multi-HMR unifies two previously separate research threads: single-shot multi-person body-only HMR (ROMP, BEV, PSVT) and single-person whole-body expressive mesh recovery (ExPose, PIXIE, OSX, SMPLer-X). It departs from the multi-crop paradigm dominant in whole-body methods by using a single ViT backbone with a cross-attention Human Perception Head, and from the HRNet-based architectures of prior multi-person methods by leveraging DINOv2-pretrained Vision Transformers. The patch-level detection mechanism follows the 'Objects as Points' formulation, while the depth parametrization and optional camera-ray Fourier encoding draw on monocular depth and NeRF literature respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously handle multi-person detection, whole-body (hands + face) mesh regression, 3D camera-space localization, and optional camera-awareness in a single forward pass, making it directly applicable to AR/VR, human-robot interaction, and real-time scene understanding. The CUFFS dataset addresses a genuine gap in training data for fine-grained body parts at close range, and the finding that synthetic data alone outperforms real data with pseudo-GT fits reinforces the viability of synthetic-only training pipelines. Open questions include extension to video (temporal consistency), handling of severe occlusion and truncation in crowded scenes, and whether the single-shot approach can match dedicated hand/face specialists at extreme close-up.
