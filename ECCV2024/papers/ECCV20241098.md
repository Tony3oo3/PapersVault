## Problem

Naively using pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models to synthesize training data for few-shot segmentation suffers from two critical misalignments: instance-level misalignment, where text-conditioned generation produces degenerate or out-of-distribution samples for rare categories (actually diminishing segmentation performance by over 10%), and annotation-level misalignment, where diffusion models cannot produce the pixel-level masks required for segmentation training. No prior method addresses both issues simultaneously while operating under the few-shot constraint of only a handful of real examples per novel class.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AlignDiff takes as few as a single real image-mask pair per novel category along with the class name text, and a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. It outputs synthetic RGB images (generated in latent space) paired with accurate pixel-level segmentation masks. The synthetic samples are injected into the support set during few-shot testing to augment the small number of real examples, rather than being used for base-model training. The mask generation operates on coarse cross-attention maps extracted from the diffusion U-Net and refines them into high-quality binary masks.

## Method

AlignDiff addresses the two misalignments with two complementary mechanisms. For instance-level alignment, it proposes Normalized Masked Textual Inversion: it learns an instance-specific embedding v* placed as an adjective token (rather than a noun, as in standard textual inversion) from a single image-mask pair, optimizing a loss that is masked and separately normalized over foreground and background pixels with a balancing hyperparameter λ, ensuring the generated samples preserve both object identity and scene context. For annotation-level alignment, it formulates mask refinement as a semi-supervised problem: coarse masks from diffusion cross-attention are scored against predictions from a few-shot segmentation model conditioned on the few real samples, and samples exceeding an IoU threshold are promoted to a growing reference set; a final re-conditioning pass generates pseudo-labels for remaining samples. This two-stage scoring-and-re-estimation pipeline requires no per-category model training, reducing mask generation from ~40 seconds per image (DiffuMask) to ~0.5 seconds with comparable accuracy.

## Results

On FSS-1000 (1-shot), AlignDiff improves HSNet from 86.5 to 88.3 overall IoU and VAT from 90.0 to 90.8, while naive text-conditioned Grounded Diffusion (GD) degrades performance to 81.4 and 84.7 respectively. On Pascal-5i 1-shot FSS, SCCAN+AlignDiff achieves 69.0 mean IoU, surpassing the previous best (SCCAN at 67.7 and VAT at 67.0). In the more challenging GFSS setting, GAPS+AlignDiff boosts novel-class IoU by ~80% over GAPS alone on Pascal-5i 1-shot (43.3 vs 23.6) and by ~10% on COCO-20i 1-shot (23.1 vs 12.7). For mask generation efficiency, AlignDiff requires only 15 GPU hours vs 130 for DiffuMask while achieving comparable novel IoU (41.5 vs 40.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AlignDiff consistently outperforms Grounded Diffusion (GD) [14] across all settings: on FSS-1000, GD degrades HSNet from 86.5 to 81.4 IoU, whereas AlignDiff lifts it to 88.3; in GFSS, GAPS+AlignDiff achieves 52.7 HM vs 51.0 for GAPS+GD on Pascal-5i 1-shot. Against DiffuMask [31], AlignDiff achieves on-par mask quality (41.5 vs 40.7 novel IoU) but is 8.7× faster (15 vs 130 GPU hours for 10,000 images). As a data augmentation plug-in, SCCAN+AlignDiff surpasses all prior FSS methods on Pascal-5i 1-shot (69.0 vs 67.7 for SCCAN, 67.0 for VAT, 63.5 for DCAMA) and COCO-20i 5-shot (55.4 vs 55.1 for SCCAN, 51.9 for DCAMA). In GFSS, GAPS+AlignDiff dramatically outperforms PIFS [1] (52.7 vs 26.7 HM) and GFS [28] (52.7 vs 24.6 HM) on Pascal-5i 1-shot, and improves over GAPS [20] by +17.8 HM. For mask quality specifically, AlignDiff (92.9 IoU) outperforms both coarse cross-attention [8,31] (78.6) and FSS-only [5] (88.9) on Pascal-5i 2-shot categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

AlignDiff builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and adapts textual inversion (Gal et al.) with a mask-weighted, normalized loss to learn instance-specific adjective embeddings from as few as one image-mask pair, drawing on DreamBooth's token placement strategy. For mask generation, it extends the cross-attention extraction approach of Hertz et al. by reframing mask refinement as a semi-supervised problem that bootstraps from a few real samples, replacing DiffuMask's expensive per-category noise-learning pipeline. The key departure from prior text-to-image-mask synthesis work (GD, DiffuMask) is conditioning the generative process on real novel samples rather than relying solely on text prompts, which the paper shows is critical for out-of-distribution rare categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that naively using text-conditioned diffusion to augment few-shot segmentation actively degrades performance by over 10% on rare categories (FSS-1000), a warning that prior work overlooked. AlignDiff's plug-and-play, model-agnostic design and 80× speedup over DiffuMask for mask generation make it a practical tool for practitioners augmenting any FSS/GFSS backbone. However, the authors note it may fail under drastic domain gaps (e.g., medical imaging), and the approach's reliance on at least one real sample per novel class limits its applicability to truly zero-shot scenarios. Future work combining AlignDiff with copy-paste and grounded conditioning (e.g., GLIGEN) for general segmentation is identified as a promising direction.
