## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to semantically align generated images with prompts, manifesting as incorrect attribute binding (e.g., 'purple' applied to the wrong object) and catastrophic object neglect (objects simply absent from the output). Prior methods address only one failure mode: AnE maximizes object attention intensity but ignores attribute alignment, while SynGen aligns attribute-object pairs but neglects object presence, degrading to vanilla Stable Diffusion when no explicit attributes exist. No unified framework simultaneously enforces both object presence and correct attribute-object binding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a pretrained Stable Diffusion model as input, parsing the prompt with Spacy's dependency parser to extract object tokens (NOUN/PROPN tags) and their associated modifier sets. It operates on 16×16-resolution cross-attention maps within the latent diffusion pipeline, updating the latent z_t via gradient descent during the first 25 of 50 DDIM denoising steps (step size α=20). The output is a generated RGB image whose cross-attention maps exhibit both high object intensity and aligned attribute-object spatial distributions.

## Method

EBAMA formulates a z-parameterized conditional energy-based model over attention maps, where the probability of a modifier token l given object token s is proportional to exp(cosine_similarity(A_l, A_s)), with the latent z serving as the model parameter. Maximizing the log-likelihood of this EBM via negative sampling (uniformly sampling unrelated tokens to approximate the expectation term) yields an object-centric attribute binding loss that simultaneously attracts syntactically related modifiers toward their object and repels unrelated tokens in the feature space. An object-centric intensity regularizer, computed as the negative L∞ norm of a 3×3 Gaussian-kernel-smoothed attention score map, prevents the binding loss from excessively shifting object attention toward modifiers and causing object disappearance. The combined loss L = Σ_s (L_b^(s) + λL_n^(s)) is backpropagated to update z_t at each of the first 25 denoising steps, with λ balancing the trade-off between attribute binding fidelity and object presence.

## Results

On the AnE dataset, EBAMA (with λ>0) achieves the best Full Sim. across all three benchmarks: 0.340 (Animal-Animal), 0.362 (Animal-Object), and 0.366 (Object-Object), outperforming the strongest baselines AnE (0.332/0.353/0.360) and SG (0.311/0.355/0.355). The T-C Sim. gains are more pronounced, reaching 0.817, 0.851, and 0.836 respectively, versus 0.806/0.830/0.811 for AnE and 0.767/0.830/0.811 for SG. Notably, EBAMA with λ=0 (binding loss only) already surpasses both AnE and SG on every metric, and the advantage of the full method (with intensity regularizer) grows with prompt complexity, reaching +0.025 T-C Sim. over AnE on Object-Object.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBAMA outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three AnE benchmarks. Against the two strongest competitors, AnE and SG, the gains are consistent: Full Sim. improves by +0.006 to +0.029 and T-C Sim. by +0.011 to +0.050 depending on the benchmark. SG degrades to SD on Animal-Animal (identical scores of 0.311/0.213/0.767) because its pair-centric objective is inapplicable when no attribute-object pairs exist, whereas EBAMA's object-centric formulation handles this case naturally. EBCA, CD, and StrD are uniformly weaker, with EBCA trailing EBAMA by up to 0.051 in Full Sim. (Animal-Object) and 0.125 in T-C Sim. (Object-Object). The advantage of the full EBAMA (with intensity regularizer) over its λ=0 variant is modest on Animal-Animal (+0.003 T-C Sim.) but grows to +0.013 on Object-Object, indicating the regularizer matters most for complex multi-object prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: inference-time attention guidance for diffusion models (AnE, StrD, SG, EBCA) and energy-based modeling of attention mechanisms (Modern Hopfield Networks, Energy Transformer, EBCA). It departs from AnE by adding attribute-aware alignment beyond pure intensity maximization, and from SG by shifting from a pair-centric to an object-centric formulation that naturally handles prompts without explicit attributes. The key conceptual departure is framing the attention map alignment problem as log-likelihood maximization of a z-parameterized EBM conditioned on object tokens, with negative sampling borrowed from NLP to approximate the intractable expectation term, and cosine similarity as a deliberately 'non-crafted' energy function in place of SG's KL-divergence-based multinomial assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying object presence and attribute binding in a single training-free objective, EBAMA addresses two failure modes that prior methods handle in isolation, and its object-centric design generalizes gracefully to prompts lacking explicit attribute-object pairs (where SG degrades to vanilla SD). The practical impact is notable: no fine-tuning, no additional training data, and a single hyperparameter (λ) to balance the two concerns. Open questions include whether the Spacy-based parsing step is robust enough for highly complex or non-English prompts, whether the approach transfers to other diffusion architectures beyond Stable Diffusion, and whether the cosine-similarity energy function's advantage over distributional assumptions (KL divergence) persists at larger scales or with more diverse token vocabularies.
