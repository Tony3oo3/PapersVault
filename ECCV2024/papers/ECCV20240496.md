## Problem

Temporal HD-map construction requires propagating curve-shaped map elements across frames, but unlike bounding boxes, curves undergo non-uniform deformations where new segments appear, old segments detach, and points at different positions on the same curve require different offsets. This makes direct temporal propagation of queries unstable and the learning process counter to conventional training, as the network must simultaneously handle diverse, position-dependent biases within a single curve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240496/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras at 480×800 for nuScenes, 7 ring cameras at 608×608 for Argoverse2) along with the ego-motion transformation matrix between consecutive frames. During training, it additionally ingests the ground-truth curves of the previous frame warped into the current frame's coordinate system. The output is a set of vectorized map elements—lane dividers, pedestrian crossings, and road boundaries—each represented as a fixed-length point sequence with a semantic class, evaluated at 30 m and 50 m perceptual ranges.

## Method

Stream Query Denoising (SQD) reframes temporal curve propagation as a denoising task: the ego-warped previous-frame ground truth serves as a noisy query that the network must reconstruct into the current-frame ground truth. Two components enable this. First, Adaptive Temporal Matching (ATM) establishes one-to-one correspondences between previous and current curves using bidirectional Chamfer distance with a per-instance threshold proportional to each curve's spatial scale, avoiding the instability of bipartite graph matching. Second, Dynamic Curve Noising (DCN) adds random perturbations to the warped curves but dynamically decays the noise magnitude inversely with the matching distance, so curves already well-aligned receive less extra noise while poorly aligned ones receive more, balancing diversity against overfitting. The noised queries are concatenated with standard map queries and processed jointly through a deformable-attention transformer decoder; the noised-query branch is supervised with the same focal + line losses as the main branch. SQD is purely an auxiliary training signal and is entirely removed at inference, introducing zero additional computation.

## Results

On nuScenes at 30 m range with ResNet-50 and 24 epochs, SQD-MapNet achieves 63.9 mAP, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art StreamMapNet (60.4 mAP) by 3.5 mAP. With a stronger V2-99 backbone pretrained on DD3D at 900×1600 resolution and 110 epochs, SQD-MapNet reaches 74.0 mAP, setting a new record on the nuScenes benchmark. On Argoverse2, SQD-MapNet consistently outperforms StreamMapNet by approximately 1.8 mAP at 30 m (63.3 vs. 61.5) and 1.6 mAP at 50 m (59.3 vs. 57.7). The SQD strategy also improves static baselines: MapTR gains 1.9 mAP and BeMapNet gains 2.1 mAP when SQD is added.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SQD-MapNet outperforms all six baselines across both datasets. Against the closest temporal competitor StreamMapNet [37], SQD-MapNet gains 3.5 mAP on nuScenes 30 m (63.9 vs. 60.4) and 1.8 mAP on Argoverse2 30 m (63.3 vs. 61.5), confirming that the denoising strategy specifically benefits temporal propagation. Against the strongest static method BeMapNet [28], SQD-MapNet leads by 4.1 mAP on nuScenes (63.9 vs. 59.8), while also improving BeMapNet itself by 2.1 mAP when SQD is added as an auxiliary loss. The gap over MapTR [19] is 13.6 mAP (63.9 vs. 50.3) and over VectorMapNet [22] is 23.0 mAP (63.9 vs. 40.9), reflecting the advantage of the query-based vectorized paradigm over earlier segmentation or auto-regressive approaches. On Argoverse2, SQD-MapNet also surpasses PivotNet [8] by 22.6 mAP (63.3 vs. 40.7) and HDMapNet [17] by 44.5 mAP (63.3 vs. 18.8), demonstrating consistent superiority across datasets and perception ranges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the query denoising paradigm from DN-DETR (object detection) to the temporal HD-map construction setting, building directly on StreamMapNet's streaming query propagation and MapTR's deformable cross-attention decoder. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that temporal propagation of curves is fundamentally different from that of bounding boxes—curves gain and lose segments over time, making the 'noise' structured and instance-dependent rather than uniform. The paper introduces two mechanisms specific to this geometry: Adaptive Temporal Matching (per-instance Chamfer-distance thresholds) and Dynamic Curve Noising (noise decay proportional to actual stream noise), which together form a training-only auxiliary supervision with zero inference overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that SQD is a drop-in training strategy requiring no architectural changes and no additional inference computation, yet it yields 1.9–3.5 mAP gains across static and temporal baselines—making it immediately adoptable by the HD-map construction community. The larger gain on temporal methods (3.5 mAP) versus static ones (1.9–2.1 mAP) confirms that the core benefit is learning temporal consistency, which is the central open challenge in streaming map construction. Open questions include whether the fixed-point curve representation and Chamfer-distance matching generalize to topological map elements (intersections, connectivity), and whether the approach extends to longer temporal horizons or higher-frequency sensing beyond 2 Hz.
