## Problem

Conventional 3D human pose estimation methods rely on absolute depth supervision (e.g., MPJPE), which becomes unreliable when body joints are occluded, producing ambiguous and inconsistent learning signals. No existing loss function explicitly enforces the correct relative depth ordering among joints in both spatial and temporal dimensions, leaving models vulnerable to spatially incoherent reconstructions under self-occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240502/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 2D joint positions with confidence scores (X ∈ R^{T×J×3}) extracted by Stacked Hourglass, using 243-frame sequences on Human3.6M and 81-frame sequences on MPI-INF-3DHP. It outputs a full 3D pose sequence (Ŷ ∈ R^{T×J×3}) via a regression head, where the third coordinate is treated as the depth channel for computing the relational consistency losses during training.

## Method

RePOSE employs a bifurcated spatio-temporal transformer backbone (spatial stream models inter-joint relations within a frame; temporal stream models cross-frame relations) and introduces two novel ranking-based losses on the depth channel. The spatial relational depth consistency loss (L_S) penalizes any pair of joints whose predicted depth ordering contradicts the ground-truth ordering within the same frame, while the temporal loss (L_T) enforces consistent depth ordering for the same joint across all frame pairs. Both losses are implemented as a simple max(0, d̂_i − d̂_j)·1(d_i < d_j) term over all relevant pairs, requiring only a few lines of PyTorch code. The final objective combines standard MPJPE with weighted spatial and temporal consistency terms (λ_S = λ_T = 20), shifting supervision from absolute depth values to relative depth relationships that remain informative even under occlusion.

## Results

On Human3.6M, RePOSE achieves an average MPJPE of 36.5 mm and PA-MPJPE of 30.7 mm, outperforming the previous best (MotionBERT: 37.5 / 31.7) by 1.0 mm on both metrics. On MPI-INF-3DHP, RePOSE reaches 15.5 mm MPJPE and 86.7% AUC, surpassing MotionAGFormer (16.2 mm, 85.3%) by 0.7 mm and 1.4% respectively. In terms of efficiency, RePOSE uses 16.0M parameters and 43.7G MACs while achieving 15.7 mm MPJPE with ground-truth 2D input, beating MotionBERT (42.5M params, 174.7G MACs, 17.8 mm) on both accuracy and computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePOSE outperforms all 10 compared baselines on Human3.6M in both MPJPE and PA-MPJPE. The closest competitor is MotionBERT (37.5 / 31.7), which RePOSE beats by 1.0 mm on both metrics; the gap widens substantially against earlier methods like PoseFormerV2 (45.2 / 35.6, a 8.7 mm MPJPE gap) and VideoPose3D (46.8 / 36.5, a 10.3 mm gap). On MPI-INF-3DHP, RePOSE achieves the best MPJPE (15.5 mm) and AUC (86.7%), beating MotionAGFormer by 0.7 mm and 1.4% respectively, though it trails STCFormer by 0.4% on PCK (98.3% vs 98.7%). The generalization study (Table 5) confirms that adding RePOSE's losses to MotionBERT, STCFormer, and P-STMO-S consistently improves their MPJPE by 0.8–1.4 mm without any hyperparameter tuning, demonstrating that the relational depth consistency losses are architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

RePOSE sits within the 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm for 3D HPE, building on spatio-temporal transformer backbones (PoseFormer, MotionBERT, STCFormer, P-STMO) that have become the dominant architecture family. Its conceptual departure is in the supervision signal: rather than the standard MPJPE loss that treats each joint's absolute 3D position independently, RePOSE introduces spatio-temporal relational depth consistency losses that enforce correct relative depth ordering among joints within a frame and across frames. This contrasts with MHFormer's multi-hypothesis approach to resolving 2D-to-3D ambiguity and with Pavlakos et al.'s ordinal depth loss, which relies on predicted (unreliable) depth rather than ground-truth depth rankings. The method also implicitly challenges the assumption that absolute depth supervision is necessary, showing that relative ordering suffices and is more robust under occlusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme simplicity of the contribution: a few lines of PyTorch code that can be dropped into any existing 2D-to-3D lifting pipeline, with the generalization study (Table 5) confirming consistent gains across MotionBERT, STCFormer, and P-STMO without hyperparameter tuning. This makes it a low-cost, high-utility addition for practitioners working on occlusion-heavy scenarios. Open questions include whether the fixed λ_S and λ_T = 20 remain optimal across architectures and datasets, whether the approach extends to GNN/CNN backbones, and how it performs under severe occlusion (e.g., object interactions, low-light) where even relative depth cues degrade. The dependence on 2D pose quality remains an inherent limitation of the lifting paradigm that RePOSE does not address.
