## Problem

Infrared-visible object detection faces two intertwined challenges: the complementary characteristics between modalities vary dynamically per object and scene (e.g., an object may be fully visible in IR but absent in RGB, or partially visible in both), and the two modalities are often spatially misaligned. Existing fusion methods perform global or one-step region-based reweighting, which cannot adapt to fine-grained, object-specific complementarity, and alignment is typically handled separately or requires extra paired annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a registered (or slightly misaligned) pair of infrared and visible images, resized to 640×640 (M³FD, FLIR) or 1024×1024 (LLVIP, VEDAI) for training and inference. Each modality is processed by its own ResNet50 backbone and a single-layer Efficient Encoder, producing three semantic-level feature maps per modality. The network outputs 300 object queries mapped to bounding boxes (x, y, w, h) and per-class confidence scores via a standard DETR-style detection head, with no NMS post-processing.

## Method

DAMSDet introduces two key mechanisms within a DETR-style cascade decoder. First, Modality Competitive Query Selection (MCQS) concatenates the flattened encoded IR and visible feature sequences, scores them with a linear projection, and selects the Top-300 features as initial object queries—each query is inherently tied to the modality from which it was drawn, providing a salient, interference-free prior. Second, the Multispectral Deformable Cross-attention (MDCA) module extends deformable cross-attention to the multi-modal setting: for each query, it independently predicts sampling offsets and aggregation weights on the multi-semantic feature maps of both modalities, so misaligned objects are still captured (offsets are modality-specific) and complementary information is adaptively weighted per semantic level. The six-layer cascade decoder with 4D anchor boxes (from DAB-DETR) iteratively refines queries, progressively shifting focus from low-level IR features (contours, shapes) to high-level visible features (context, category) as layers deepen. Training uses IoU-aware classification loss, Noise Query Learning, and COCO-pretrained ResNet50 weights.

## Results

On the M³FD dataset, DAMSDet achieves 52.9 mAP (80.2 mAP50, 56.0 mAP75), outperforming the best fusion baseline CFT by 10.4% in mAP. On FLIR, it reaches 49.3 mAP (86.6 mAP50), surpassing LRAF-Net by 6.5% in mAP. On LLVIP, it attains 69.6 mAP (97.9 mAP50, 79.1 mAP75), leading LRAF-Net by 3.3% in mAP. On VEDAI, it achieves 91.5 mAP50 but 55.3 mAP, where it trails the CNN-based LRAF-Net (59.1 mAP) due to the latter's advantage in precise small-object bounding box localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAMSDet consistently outperforms all transformer-based fusion baselines (CFT, ICAFusion, MS-DETR, MFPT) across all four datasets, with the largest margin on M³FD where it exceeds CFT by 12% in mAP50 and 10.4% in mAP. Against the strongest CNN-based fusion method LRAF-Net, DAMSDet wins on M³FD (+10.1 mAP), FLIR (+6.5 mAP), and LLVIP (+3.3 mAP), but loses on VEDAI mAP (55.3 vs. 59.1) because small-object detection favors CNNs' local feature precision over Transformer's long-range modeling. Compared to single-modality DINO baselines, DAMSDet gains 6.6–10.4% mAP over the best single modality on each dataset, confirming the value of adaptive multispectral fusion. On LLVIP, where mAP50 is near-saturated (~97–98%), DAMSDet still leads MS-DETR by 2.8% in mAP75 and LRAF-Net by 3.3% in mAP, indicating finer-grained complementary information mining. The method also surpasses image-level fusion approaches (TarDAL_CT + DINO/YoloV7) by 9.6–12.9% mAP on M³FD, demonstrating that feature-level adaptive fusion is superior to pre-fusing images before detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAMSDet extends the DETR family (DETR, Deformable DETR, DAB-DETR, DINO, RT-DETR) to the multispectral infrared-visible detection setting by adapting deformable cross-attention to jointly sample from two modality-specific feature pyramids. It departs from prior multispectral fusion methods (CFT, ICAFusion, LRAF-Net, AR-CNN) that handle modality interference and misalignment as separate problems, instead unifying both within a single multispectral deformable cross-attention module that predicts independent sampling offsets per modality. The competitive query selection strategy is a novel initialization mechanism that dynamically assigns each object query to its dominant modality, contrasting with prior work that uses learnable embeddings or global/region-level fusion weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By linking misalignment adaptation with fine-grained complementary feature fusion in one module, DAMSDet offers a more efficient and principled alternative to the multi-stage pipelines (explicit alignment + separate fusion) used by prior methods. The observation that complementary information shifts across semantic levels (infrared dominates at low levels, visible at high levels) provides a useful inductive bias for multispectral fusion design. However, the method's reliance on a Transformer decoder limits its advantage for small-object detection (VEDAI mAP), where CNN-based detectors still hold an edge in bounding-box precision, suggesting that hybrid or CNN-centric approaches may remain competitive in aerial/surveillance scenarios. Open questions include whether the competitive query selection generalizes to more than two modalities and whether the cascade depth can be reduced without sacrificing the multi-level fusion benefit.
