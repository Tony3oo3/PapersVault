## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization models are trained and evaluated under specific orientation or FoV settings, requiring separate models for each ground view variation and relying heavily on North-aligned spatial correspondence as a shortcut. This limits generalization to real-world scenarios where query image orientation and field of view are unknown, and prevents the model from learning robust, view-invariant features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240363/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground-level query image that may be North-aligned, cyclically shifted by an arbitrary angle, or further cropped to a limited FoV (70°, 90°, 180°, or 360°), alongside a gallery of geo-referenced aerial images. During training, each ground panorama is transformed by a random cyclic shift (0°–360°) and FoV cropping (180°) to generate its variation; during inference, the query is used as-is. Output is a ranked list of aerial images scored by cosine similarity between query and reference embeddings, with retrieval evaluated via Top-k Recall and Average Precision.

## Method

ConGeo is a model-agnostic contrastive learning pipeline that augments a base geo-localization model's vanilla cross-view loss with three additional objectives: a single-view contrastive loss aligning transformed ground views with their original North-aligned full-panorama counterparts, a single-view contrastive loss aligning augmented aerial views with originals, and a cross-view contrastive loss aligning transformed ground views directly with aerial references. The total loss is a weighted sum (L = L_vanilla + 0.5·L_single-q + 0.5·L_single-r + 0.25·L_cross) using learnable temperature parameters. The key insight is that by disrupting the initial geometric correspondence and enforcing proximity between view variants of the same location, the model is compelled to learn semantically consistent features rather than exploiting spatial correspondence shortcuts, yielding a single model robust to arbitrary orientations and FoVs without FoV-specific training.

## Results

On CVUSA, ConGeo (single model, no FoV-specific training) achieves R@1 of 85.2% (FoV=360°), 92.3% (FoV=180°), 55.9% (FoV=90°), and 37.1% (FoV=70°), improving the base Sample4Geo by 68.9% and 53.4% R@1 on FoV=360° and FoV=90° respectively. On CVACT validation, ConGeo reaches R@1 of 70.3% (FoV=180°) and 34.8% (FoV=90°), outperforming the best prior method (SAIG-D) by 11.4% and 12.7% R@1. On the North-aligned setting (Table 2), ConGeo remains competitive at 98.3% R@1 on CVUSA and 90.1% on CVACT, within 0.4–0.7% of the best baseline. On VIGOR cross-area, ConGeo achieves 16.2% R@1 (FoV=360°) and 3.9% R@1 (FoV=90°), roughly doubling the base model's performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConGeo dramatically outperforms the un-augmented Sample4Geo baseline (16.3% → 85.2% R@1 on CVUSA FoV=360°; 2.5% → 55.9% on FoV=90°), confirming that the contrastive objectives are far more effective than simply training on North-aligned data. Against the strongest FoV-specific competitor SAIG-D, ConGeo wins on every FoV setting on CVUSA (e.g., 92.3% vs 52.5% R@1 at FoV=180°; 55.9% vs 26.7% at FoV=90°) while using a single model trained once. On CVACT, ConGeo surpasses SAIG-D and DSM by 11.4% and 12.7% R@1 at FoV=180° and 90° respectively. On the North-aligned setting (Table 2), ConGeo is within 0.4% of Sample4Geo on CVUSA (98.3% vs 98.7%) and 0.7% on CVACT (90.1% vs 90.8%), showing minimal cost for the robustness gain. ConGeo also outperforms TransGeo and SAIG-D when plugged into their respective architectures (Table 5), with R@1% gains of 37.8% (TransGeo, FoV=360°) and 69.9% (TransGeo, FoV=90°) over the un-augmented base models.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConGeo builds on the siamese-network paradigm for cross-view geo-localization (Sample4Geo, TransGeo, SAIG-D) and departs from prior work that trains separate models for each orientation or FoV setting (DSM, SAIG-D). Its core contribution is a model-agnostic contrastive learning framework—combining single-view and cross-view InfoNCE-style objectives—that enforces feature consistency between ground view variants and their original representations, rather than relying on targeted data augmentation or FoV-specific cropping. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-in loss that can be layered onto existing CVGL pipelines without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single ConGeo-trained model replaces the need for multiple FoV-specific or orientation-specific models, simplifying deployment in real-world settings where query orientation and FoV are unknown. The paper's analysis (Grad-CAM, orientation invariance curves) reveals that prior models exploit spatial-correspondence shortcuts (e.g., road direction) that collapse under view shifts, while ConGeo shifts focus toward semantically consistent cues—a useful diagnostic for the broader CVGL community. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other ground view degradations (seasonal change, weather, zoom) and whether non-contrastive alignment strategies (e.g., redundancy reduction) could complement or replace the proposed objectives.
