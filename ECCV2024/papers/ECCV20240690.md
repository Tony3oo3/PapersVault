## Problem

Existing temporal depth completion methods fuse feature maps across frames using ConvLSTM or spatio-temporal convolution, which requires warping previous features to align with the current view. This warping depends on potentially erroneous depth predictions from prior frames, causing misalignment and inferior accuracy compared to single-view baselines. The paper asks whether temporal information can be fused in a way that is robust to such depth prediction errors while remaining computationally tractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240690/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a calibrated RGB-D video sequence, taking per-frame RGB images, sparse depth maps (as low as 0.05% density), SE(3) camera poses, and camera intrinsics. It processes frames incrementally: each frame's cost volume is fused with the cost volume accumulated from all prior frames, producing a completed depth map per frame. Images are downscaled by a factor of 4 to manage the memory cost of 4D cost volumes (D×C×H×W), and the number of hypothesis depth planes is fixed at 16.

## Method

RayFusion constructs a cost volume per frame via a modified CostDCNet (3D U-Net over concatenated occupancy, residual, and multi-scale RGB feature volumes), then fuses temporally adjacent cost volumes in 3D space using a ray-based attention scheme. For each pixel, the D×C ray-wise feature slice is treated as D tokens; self-attention refines the current frame's depth hypothesis distribution, followed by cross-attention to fuse with the previous frame's aligned cost volume (aligned via inverse mapping using relative camera poses). This reduces attention computation from D²H²W² (full volume) to D²HW (per-ray) entries. Depth is regressed by softmax over the fused probability volume, refined by NLSPN, and trained end-to-end with L1 depth loss plus a cross-entropy loss using soft labels derived from nearest-plane interpolation. The entire framework uses only 1.15M parameters, roughly 94.5% fewer than the closest video-based competitor (LRRU, 21M).

## Results

On ScanNetV2, RayFusion achieves MAE 0.0160 m and RMSE 0.0554 m, outperforming CostDCNet (MAE 0.0244 m, RMSE 0.0759 m) and NLSPN (MAE 0.0266 m, RMSE 0.0859 m) with only 1.15M parameters (94.5% fewer than LRRU's 21M). On KITTI, it reaches MAE 176.23 mm and RMSE 720.63 mm, beating LRRU (188.80 mm / 729.50 mm) and NLSPN (198.64 mm / 771.80 mm). On VOID, it achieves MAE 24.51 mm and RMSE 65.46 mm, surpassing CostDCNet (25.84 mm / 76.28 mm) and NLSPN (26.74 mm / 79.12 mm). Notably, even in single-view mode (self-attention only), the method remains competitive with SOTA on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayFusion outperforms all single-view baselines across all three datasets. On ScanNetV2, it beats CostDCNet by 34% in MAE (0.0160 vs. 0.0244 m) and NLSPN by 40% in MAE, while using fewer parameters than both. On KITTI, it surpasses LRRU (the strongest video-based baseline) by 6.7% in MAE (176.23 vs. 188.80 mm) and 11.2% in RMSE (720.63 vs. 771.80 mm vs. NLSPN), and outperforms CompletionFormer by 11.5% in MAE. On VOID, it leads CostDCNet by 5.1% in MAE and 14.2% in RMSE. The single-view variant (self-attention only, no cross-attention) still achieves MAE 0.0208 m on ScanNetV2 and 193.05 mm on KITTI, remaining competitive with NLSPN and CompletionFormer. In cross-dataset generalization (trained on ScanNetV2, tested on VOID), RayFusion achieves MAE 29.08 mm versus 65.90 mm for CompletionFormer and 158.60 mm for NLSPN, demonstrating substantially better domain transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cost volume formulation from CostDCNet (single-view depth completion) to the video setting by introducing a ray-based attention fusion mechanism for temporally adjacent cost volumes. It departs from prior video depth completion methods (DeepSmooth, Patil et al.) that rely on feature map warping via ConvLSTM or spatio-temporal convolution, which are error-prone due to their dependence on previous depth predictions. Instead, fusion is performed directly in 3D viewing frustum space via volume resampling, and the ray-wise self-attention + cross-attention design borrows the attention paradigm from multi-view stereo Transformers while avoiding the prohibitive memory cost of global volume attention. The depth refinement stage adapts NLSPN, and the soft cross-entropy supervision follows Nuanes et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework achieves state-of-the-art or competitive depth completion across three diverse datasets (indoor ScanNetV2, mixed VOID, outdoor KITTI) with only 1.15M parameters — 94.5% fewer than LRRU — making it attractive for deployment on resource-constrained devices. The finding that the self-attention stage alone (single-view mode) already rivals SOTA suggests that cost volume refinement via intra-ray attention is a powerful inductive bias independent of temporal information. Open questions include whether the approach can be made more memory-efficient by eliminating 3D convolutions (as the authors note), how it behaves under persistent depth errors over long sequences, and whether the ray-based fusion generalizes to other 3D volume-based tasks beyond depth completion.
