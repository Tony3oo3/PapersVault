## Problem

Existing 3D neural style transfer methods target object- or room-scale scenes, and their view planning and explicit texture optimization strategies are neither scalable nor memory-feasible for city-scale meshes spanning hundreds of meters. Moreover, these methods largely ignore local semantic consistency, which is critical for photorealistic stylization of complex urban environments with diverse material classes (buildings, roads, water, vegetation, etc.).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241584/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a UV-textured 3D mesh (approximately 0.5 km × 0.5 km city tiles from Google 3D Tiles API), a 2D style reference image, and a free-form text prompt as input. It produces a stylized UV texture map that can be baked at arbitrary resolution (the neural texture field achieves ~90% compression over the original texture) and a style-aligned 360° omnidirectional sky panorama. Optimization runs at 512×512 resolution, and the final baked texture integrates directly into conventional real-time rendering pipelines without requiring custom materials or lighting setups.

## Method

StyleCity represents scene appearance as a UV-based neural texture field (an MLP with multi-resolution hash-grid encoding over normalized UV coordinates), initialized by distilling the original texture via MSE loss. The core optimization employs a multi-scale progressive strategy: over 5 levels, the field-of-view shrinks from 90° to 20° while novel views are sampled along Bézier curves with random horizontal/vertical translation, progressively transferring fine-grained style details. A scale-adaptive style matching mechanism randomly crops the reference image into multi-scale patches and selects the best-matching patch per training view via self-similarity structure descriptors (cosine similarity of flattened VGG-19 layer-3 features), ensuring style patterns are transferred at the appropriate spatial frequency. A semantics-aware local style loss computes class-wise ROI feature statistics (mean and std of VGG features) using Mask2Former segmentation masks, with a hierarchical re-matching graph to handle missing semantics in the style reference. Text-driven style is enforced through global and directional CLIP losses, and photorealism is preserved via a matting-Laplacian regularizer. A Stable Diffusion-based omnidirectional sky synthesizer extends multi-window joint diffusion with 90° bounding-FOV latent feature sampling to produce a seamless 360° panorama supervised by LPIPS against the style reference.

## Results

Across 5 city models (London, Sydney, New York, Hong Kong, Singapore; ~0.5 km × 0.5 km each) and 7 styles (35 outputs per method), StyleCity achieves the best scores on all metrics: eSSIM 0.623, LPIPS 0.342, CLIP score 0.244, and user-study ratings of 4.586/5 (content preservation) and 4.572/5 (style match). The closest baseline, InstructN2N, reaches eSSIM 0.503 and LPIPS 0.370 but trails significantly on CLIP (0.186) and user preference (3.156 / 2.024). Optimization runs for 20 epochs (~10 hours) on a single RTX 3090 at 512×512 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleCity outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against InstructN2N (the strongest baseline), it improves eSSIM by 0.120 (0.623 vs 0.503), reduces LPIPS by 0.028 (0.342 vs 0.370), and raises the CLIP score by 0.058 (0.244 vs 0.186), while user-study content-preservation and style-match scores jump from 3.156/2.024 to 4.586/4.572. Against StyleMesh, the gains are even larger on style fidelity (CLIP 0.244 vs 0.188; style-match 4.572 vs 2.402), reflecting StyleMesh's tendency to transfer mismatched styles (e.g., painting buildings with sky colors). Against ARF, the improvement is most dramatic on LPIPS (0.342 vs 0.812) and user style-match (4.572 vs 1.430), as ARF's volume-rendering NeRF representation produces hazy, blurry outputs. Qualitatively, the NeRF-based baselines (ARF, InstructN2N) suffer from hazy artifacts inherent to volume rendering, while StyleMesh produces non-photorealistic distortions; StyleCity preserves sharp logos, lit windows, and semantically consistent style patterns across the urban scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D neural style transfer line of work from object- and room-scale meshes (StyleMesh, ARF, InstructN2N) to city-scale textured meshes by replacing explicit texture or NeRF volume rendering with a UV-based neural texture field built on multiresolution hash encoding [39]. It departs from prior mesh stylization in three key ways: a multi-scale progressive view optimization strategy with scale-adaptive style reference matching (using self-similarity descriptors), a semantics-aware local style loss that operates on per-class ROI features, and a directional CLIP loss adapted from StyleGAN-NADA [13] for text-driven holistic style. For the sky component, it extends SyncDiffusion's multi-window joint diffusion [30] with omnidirectional latent feature sampling to produce artifact-free 360° panoramas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to demonstrate controllable, vision-and-text-driven stylization at urban scale (~0.5 km × 0.5 km), filling a gap between object-level stylization and the labor-intensive manual material/lighting workflows used in virtual production. The baked UV texture output integrates directly into conventional real-time rendering pipelines, making it practically deployable for applications like personalized Google Earth views. Limitations include the 10-hour per-scene optimization cost, the lack of illumination coupling between the synthesized sky and foreground geometry, and dependence on accurate semantic segmentation. Open questions include whether the progressive multi-scale strategy can be accelerated for near-real-time use, whether illumination-aware stylization is feasible, and how the approach scales to gigapixel textures spanning multiple city blocks.
