## Problem

VLMs like CLIP, when applied to cropped image regions for open-vocabulary detection, produce highly noisy pseudo-labels (76.6% error on noisy boxes) due to two root causes: lack of surrounding image context and absence of a 'background' concept in their classification. Additionally, conventional detector training equally weights all proposals matched to a pseudo-label, which is harmful when pseudo-labels are mislocalized relative to the true object.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240438/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (short side 640–800, long side <1333) and category-name text embeddings produced by a CLIP text encoder with template prompts. A class-agnostic proposal generator first yields candidate regions per image; CLIP scores are pre-computed offline on all candidates, while the detector's predictions are computed online at each training iteration. The output is standard bounding boxes with category labels for both base and novel classes, evaluated via AP at IoU 0.5 (COCO) or AP averaged over IoU 0.5–0.95 (LVIS).

## Method

MarvelOVD integrates the detector's contextual and background-aware capabilities into pseudo-label generation and training. After a short burn-in phase (0.5k iterations) using high-confidence CLIP pseudo-labels, the detector computes a novelty score for each candidate box (ratio of summed novel-category logits to all logits, max-normalized across candidates) and combines it with the CLIP top-1 score via a weighted sum (λ=0.5) to dynamically select precise pseudo-labels at every iteration. Stratified label assignment first matches proposals to base annotations by IoU, then assigns remaining boxes to pseudo-labels, preventing base-novel conflicts. Finally, adaptive proposal reweighting assigns each novel training box an independent loss weight w_i = λ′·s_i + (1−λ′)·(1−b_i), where s_i is the pseudo-label confidence and b_i is the detector's background score, so that boxes with poor overlap to the true object receive smaller weights.

## Results

On COCO-2017 (48 base / 17 novel categories), MarvelOVD achieves AP50^Novel of 38.9, AP50^Base of 56.4, and AP50^All of 51.8, outperforming the direct baseline VL-PLM by +6.6 in novel AP and +3.5 in overall AP. On LVIS-v1 (337 rare categories as novel), MarvelOVD reaches APr 26.0, APc 34.2, APf 36.9, and overall AP 34.2, surpassing Detic by +1.4 in APr and +1.8 in overall AP. The method requires no extra data or supervision beyond the standard training set and CLIP pseudo-labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MarvelOVD outperforms all seven compared methods on COCO-2017 in AP50^Novel, with the largest margin over VL-PLM (+6.6) and PromptDet (+12.3). On AP50^All, MarvelOVD (51.8) leads all baselines, including RegionCLIP (50.4) and OADP (50.5), which rely on additional internet-sourced image-text pairs or knowledge distillation. The only metric where MarvelOVD is not the best is AP50^Base, where RegionCLIP edges ahead by 0.7 (57.1 vs. 56.4), though MarvelOVD still exceeds all other methods. On LVIS-v1, MarvelOVD dominates across all four metrics (APr, APc, APf, AP), beating Detic by +1.4/+1.7/+1.3/+1.8 respectively and Rasheed et al. by +0.8/+0.8/+1.1/+1.3, without using any extra image-level classification data. Notably, MarvelOVD matches VL-PLM's training data (only base annotations + CLIP pseudo-labels) yet achieves substantially higher novel detection accuracy, demonstrating that the gains come from the online mining and adaptive reweighting rather than additional supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling paradigm for open-vocabulary detection established by VL-PLM [38] and related works (PromptDet, Gao et al.), which use CLIP to generate offline pseudo-labels for novel categories. The key departure is a diagnostic insight: CLIP's primary failure mode on cropped regions is not misclassification (3.3% error) but an inability to reject non-object 'noisy' boxes (76.6% error), stemming from its lack of contextual awareness and absence of a 'background' concept. MarvelOVD addresses this by dynamically incorporating the detector's RoI Align contextual features and background classification into both pseudo-label selection (online mining) and loss computation (adaptive reweighting), rather than treating VLM outputs as fixed targets. It also introduces stratified label assignment to resolve a previously overlooked 'base-novel-conflict' in IoU-based matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that strong OVD performance can be achieved without auxiliary data sources (internet image-text pairs, image-level labels, or pre-training on grounding data) that most competing methods rely on, making the approach more practical and data-efficient. The stratified label assignment and adaptive proposal reweighting are general-purpose techniques applicable to any pseudo-label-based detection training, not limited to OVD. Open questions include whether the online mining strategy scales to very large label spaces beyond LVIS's 1200+ categories, whether the approach transfers to transformer-based detectors (e.g., DETR variants) where RoI Align is absent, and how sensitive the method is to the quality of the initial proposal generator.
