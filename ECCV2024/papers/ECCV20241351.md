## Problem

Existing snapshot spectral imaging (SSI) systems require complex multi-stage calibration (focal-plane mask positioning, aperture-plane dispersion, and cross-branch registration) that is highly sensitive to physical vibrations and temperature drift, making them impractical for portable deployment. While parallel architectures improve reconstruction accuracy, they exacerbate calibration demands between optical branches. The paper asks whether a calibration-free parallel SSI framework can simultaneously achieve high spectral fidelity and minimal system complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241351/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two uncalibrated 2D measurements captured in a single exposure: a monochromatic aperture-diffraction image (from a monochrome camera behind an orthogonal-mask diffractive lens) and an RGB image (from a standard Bayer-filter camera), along with analytically computed PSFs derived from the mask period and focal length. In uncalibrated mode, the RGB prior may be misaligned by up to 40 pixels (simulation) or 300 pixels (real experiments) relative to the diffraction image. The output is a 28-band hyperspectral image spanning 450–650 nm; the real prototype recovers 1824×1824×28 HSI from a single snapshot.

## Method

PCCADIS parallelizes the serial spatial-spectral coding of ADIS into two independent optical branches (monochromatic aperture diffraction + RGB prior), eliminating the need for beam splitters, focal-plane calibration, and dispersion calibration since the diffraction PSFs are computed directly from the mask geometry (period d, duty ratio ε) and focal length via closed-form equations. For reconstruction, SSFCT augments the CSST decoder with a Self-aware Feature Encoding Module (SFEM) that extracts detail features via Restormer blocks, base features via Lite Transformer blocks, and structural features via INN blocks from each branch independently. A correlation-based alignment loss (L_align) guides the encoder to produce spatially consistent features even when inputs are misregistered, and the fused features are concatenated with the raw inputs before the CSST decoder. In well-calibrated mode, SFEM degenerates and SSFCT reduces to the CSST decoder; in uncalibrated mode, the SFEM pathway is essential, yielding 41.33 dB PSNR versus 39.48 dB for Restormer on the same uncalibrated inputs.

## Results

On 10 KAIST test scenes with uncalibrated RGB guidance (perspective, scaling, and calibrated variants), SSFCT-9stg achieves 41.33 dB PSNR and 0.982 SSIM on average, outperforming the strongest baseline Restormer (39.48 dB / 0.974) by 1.85 dB and MST++ (38.34 dB / 0.966) by 2.99 dB. The method uses only 6.62M parameters and 73.50 GFLOPS, fewer parameters than TSA-Net (44.25M) and Restormer (15.11M). Compared to the serially-encoded ADIS predecessor, PCCADIS with SSFCT yields a 7.25 dB PSNR improvement (41.33 vs. 34.08 dB), and matches parallel DOE-based methods (41.79 dB) while eliminating the need for per-DOE calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSFCT outperforms all five baselines on both PSNR and SSIM across the 10 uncalibrated-input test scenes. Against the strongest spectral reconstruction baselines, SSFCT beats Restormer [52] by 1.85 dB (41.33 vs. 39.48) and MST++ [9] by 2.99 dB (41.33 vs. 38.34), while using fewer parameters than Restormer (6.62M vs. 15.11M). Against compressive spectral imaging baselines, SSFCT surpasses TSA-Net [33] by 3.05 dB and Lambda-Net [34] by 6.29 dB, with a substantial SSIM gap (0.982 vs. 0.962 and 0.918 respectively). UNet [37], a general-purpose segmentation architecture, lags by 4.87 dB, confirming that task-specific spectral priors are critical. Additionally, the paper demonstrates that SSFCT transfers to parallel DOE-based systems (achieving 41.79 dB), matching PCCADIS accuracy while highlighting that PCCADIS's calibration-free design removes the per-DOE calibration burden that DOE methods still require.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Aperture Diffraction Imaging Spectrometer (ADIS, Lv et al. ICCV 2023) by converting its serial coding (orthogonal mask + mosaic filter in one optical path) into a parallel two-branch architecture (PCCADIS), and by replacing the need for empirical PSF calibration with analytical computation from hardware parameters. The reconstruction algorithm SSFCT builds on the CSST decoder from the same ADIS line and borrows its self-aware feature encoding module (SFEM) from the CDDFuse multi-modality fusion framework (Zhao et al. CVPR 2023), adapting it to handle uncalibrated cross-branch inputs. Conceptually, the paper departs from the broader SSI trend toward increasingly complex optical encoders (DOEs, metasurfaces, prisms) by arguing that a simple orthogonal-mask diffraction pattern, paired with a fusion-aware network, can match DOE-based reconstruction quality while eliminating the calibration burden entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibration fragility is arguably the single biggest barrier to deploying snapshot spectral imagers outside controlled labs; by making the system calibration-free and the most compact parallel design (2f+2f optical path, no beam splitter), PCCADIS opens a realistic path toward mobile and edge spectral sensing. The acknowledged limitations—PSF approximation error at longer wavelengths, filter boundary effects, and the computational cost of SSFCT for true edge deployment—leave open whether the calibration-free advantage survives in production hardware with manufacturing tolerances. The finding that a trivially simple mask pattern can rival carefully fabricated DOEs in reconstruction fidelity (41.33 vs 41.79 dB) suggests the field may be over-investing in optical complexity at the expense of algorithmic fusion, a trade-off worth revisiting.
