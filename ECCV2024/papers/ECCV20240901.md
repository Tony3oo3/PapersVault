## Problem

Group portrait editing—inserting, removing, or manipulating persons in group photos—remains unsolved because no large-scale paired training data exists, appearance preservation across multiple persons is difficult, and natural human interactions (handshakes, hugs) must be synthesized. Prior single-person editing and general inpainting methods do not account for the complex compositions and inter-person dynamics inherent to group photos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240901/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 512×512 masked group photo (masked region filled with gray), a binary mask, a skeleton map (concatenated as 3 extra input channels to the standard 9-channel SD Inpainting input, yielding 12 channels), and one or more reference person images. Reference images are encoded by DINOv2 into 256×768 feature embeddings rather than fed as raw pixels. The output is a full-resolution edited group photo where the masked interaction regions are filled with natural human interactions while preserving the identities of all referenced persons.

## Method

GroupDiff builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 Inpainting and introduces two key components. First, a hierarchical data generation engine synthesizes paired training data from LV-MHP-v2 by masking at two scales: coarse (bounding-box boundary regions simulating person insertion/removal) and fine (skeleton-guided arm/hand regions with random rotation augmentation to prevent skeleton leakage through mask shape). Second, a person-aware cross-attention module preserves appearance via two guidance mechanisms: intra-person guidance concatenates DINOv2 image features with separately encoded skeleton pose features as keys (pose is excluded from values to avoid fusing structural and appearance information), and inter-person guidance adds a bounding-box-derived indicator matrix to the attention scores before softmax, with a noise-level-dependent scale factor w′ = w·log(1+σ)·max(M_attn) to route each spatial location to the correct reference person's features.

## Results

On the LV-MHP-v2 validation set, GroupDiff achieves PSNR 19.46, SSIM 0.8033, FID 18.10, CLIP-I 86.90, and DINO 95.03, outperforming all baselines across every metric. The strongest baseline (Finetuned SD Inpainting with Skeleton) reaches PSNR 18.57, SSIM 0.7868, FID 20.34, CLIP-I 83.32, and DINO 93.52, leaving a clear gap of ~0.89 PSNR and ~2.24 FID in favor of GroupDiff. The pretrained SD Inpainting model without fine-tuning scores PSNR 17.41 and FID 24.13, while Paint by Example is the weakest at PSNR 14.48 and FID 34.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroupDiff wins on all five metrics against every baseline. Against the pretrained SD Inpainting model, GroupDiff improves PSNR by 2.04 dB, SSIM by 0.047, FID by 6.02, CLIP-I by 7.35, and DINO by 3.81, demonstrating that the person-aware attention and data engine are essential beyond generic inpainting. Against Paint by Example, the gains are even larger (PSNR +4.97, FID −16.80), confirming that exemplar-based editing without human-specific priors struggles with group interactions. The two adapted baselines (Finetuned SD Inpainting and Finetuned SD Inpainting with Skeleton) narrow the gap but still trail GroupDiff by 0.89–1.14 PSNR and 2.24–2.38 FID, indicating that skeleton control alone is insufficient without the intra-person and inter-person appearance preservation modules. The ablation in Table 2 further confirms that removing either the inter-person or intra-person guidance degrades performance, validating the necessity of both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

GroupDiff builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and extends the inpainting paradigm to the multi-person group setting, where prior work (single-person pose transfer, virtual try-on, exemplar-based editing like Paint by Example) does not address the synthesis of natural human interactions among multiple individuals. The person-aware cross-attention module departs from standard exemplar injection (as in AnyDoor) by introducing both intra-person skeleton conditioning on keys and an inter-person indicator matrix derived from bounding boxes, solving the identity-mismatch problem when multiple reference persons are provided simultaneously. The hierarchical data synthesis engine (coarse bounding-box masking + fine skeleton-guided masking) is a novel contribution, as no prior work has addressed the paired-data generation problem specific to group portrait editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified framework handling person insertion, removal, and manipulation in group photos, addressing a practical need that existing single-person editing tools cannot fulfill. The person-aware attention with indicator matrices offers a reusable technique for any multi-reference editing task where identity disambiguation is critical. Key limitations remain: the method assumes correct facial expressions and facing orientations in reference images, lighting harmonization is a separate preprocessing step, and the approach has only been validated on 512×512 images with relatively small groups. Open questions include scaling to larger crowds, handling more complex multi-person interactions (e.g., three-way handshakes), and whether the skeleton-based control generalizes to non-rigid deformations.
