## Problem

The linking step in cell tracking pipelines typically relies on discrete optimization (ILP, LAP) with hand-tuned cost functions and hyperparameters that must be reconfigured for each dataset, making it cumbersome in practice. Existing transformer-based multi-object tracking methods do not account for dividing objects, which is central to cell tracking. This paper asks whether a transformer can directly learn biologically valid pairwise associations—including mother-daughter links—well enough to replace or simplify the optimization step.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-frame object detections (center coordinates, segmentation masks, and shallow shape descriptors: mean intensity, area, inertia tensor) grouped into overlapping temporal windows of s=6 frames, producing a pairwise association probability matrix over all detection pairs within each window. Crucially, it operates on object-level tokens rather than dense image patches, so it avoids processing raw pixel data and scales to ~2000 objects per frame at ~1 FPS on a single GPU. The association matrix supports links across non-adjacent timepoints and appearance/disappearance events, and is averaged over overlapping windows before being fed into a candidate graph for final track extraction.

## Method

TRACKASTRA uses a 6-layer encoder-decoder transformer (d=256, 4 heads) where each detection is encoded as a token via learned Fourier positional encodings of its center point concatenated with shallow shape features, projected to the embedding dimension. The encoder applies self-attention with a distance-based mask (disabling attention beyond a threshold) and rotary positional embeddings for relative spatial-temporal information; the decoder applies cross-attention, and the final association logits are computed as the outer product of two MLP heads applied to encoder and decoder outputs. The key training innovation is a parental softmax normalization that enforces at-most-one-parent per detection while permitting one-to-many (division) links and unassigned objects (appearance/disappearance) via a quiet-softmax denominator, combined with a binary cross-entropy loss that up-weights division and continuing-track associations. At inference, a sliding window produces per-pair association scores that are averaged across overlapping windows, thresholded, and pruned into a biologically valid track graph using either a greedy algorithm (enforcing ≤2 children, ≤1 parent per node) or an ILP solver.

## Results

On the BACTERIA dataset (6 test videos, ~2912 edges, 236 divisions), TRACKASTRA with ILP linking achieves AOGM=23 and TRA=0.999, reducing total errors by ~5x compared to the state-of-the-art DELTA 2.0 (AOGM=118). Even with simple greedy linking, TRACKASTRA reaches AOGM=36, already a 3x improvement over DELTA 2.0. On DEEPCELL (12 test videos), TRACKASTRA with ILP achieves AOGM=7.9 vs. Caliban's 18.1, and on the out-of-domain HELA dataset, the general model achieves AOGM=96 vs. Caliban's 931 (~10x improvement). On the VESICLE particle tracking benchmark, TRACKASTRA outperforms the transformer-based MoTT at high density (JSC_θ: 0.806 vs. 0.748).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRACKASTRA decisively outperforms DELTA 2.0 (the state-of-the-art deep-learning bacteria tracker) on BACTERIA: AOGM drops from 118 to 23 with ILP linking (5x reduction), and even greedy linking alone (AOGM=36) surpasses DELTA 2.0. On DEEPCELL, TRACKASTRA with ILP halves Caliban's error (AOGM 7.9 vs. 18.1), though Caliban retains a slight edge in Division F1 (0.97 vs. 0.94), likely due to its explicit division modeling. The out-of-domain HELA experiment is particularly striking: the general TRACKASTRA model (trained on diverse data, never seen HELA) achieves AOGM=96 vs. Caliban's 931, a ~10x improvement, suggesting shallow features prevent overfitting. On VESICLE, TRACKASTRA beats the transformer-based MoTT at high particle density (JSC_θ 0.806 vs. 0.748) and also outperforms classical LAP and Kalman filter baselines. Simple distance-based tracking and TrackMate perform poorly across all datasets, confirming that learned associations are essential for dense, dividing populations.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRACKASTRA repurposes the encoder-decoder transformer architecture (originally for language translation) for cell tracking, departing from prior transformer-based MOT methods (TrackFormer, Global Tracking Transformers) that cannot handle dividing objects and rely heavily on CNN-extracted appearance features. It also moves away from the dominant tracking-by-detection paradigm where linking is solved via hand-tuned discrete optimization (ILP, LAP), instead learning pairwise associations directly from data. The parental softmax normalization is a novel contribution that enforces biological constraints (one parent, multiple children) at the logit level, and the use of rotary positional embeddings for spatio-temporal context distinguishes it from prior graph-based association learners like CellTrackerGNN that restrict interactions to a predefined hypothesis graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRACKASTRA demonstrates that a single transformer model trained on diverse data can outperform specialized, hand-tuned trackers across bacteria, cell cultures, and particle tracking, reducing the practical burden of per-dataset hyperparameter tuning that has long plagued cell tracking pipelines. The finding that simple positional and shape features suffice (no pretrained CNN needed) makes the method broadly applicable whenever a domain-specific detector exists. Key limitations remain: the method does not correct faulty detections, does not yet exploit non-adjacent frame associations for gap closing, and is validated only on 2D data, though the architecture is expected to extend to 3D. An important open question is whether end-to-end joint training of detection and association prediction can close the remaining gap to fully automated pipelines.
