## Problem

Existing incremental semantic segmentation methods either rely solely on class-shared knowledge (old model weights), which provides only limited protection against average forgetting without adapting to class distribution discrepancies, or integrate old and new weights without discrimination, biasing the model toward old classes. No prior work thoroughly combines class-specific knowledge (old class prototypes) with class-shared knowledge to simultaneously address overrepresentation of new classes and underfitting of new classes.

## Input / Output

The method operates in an exemplar-free incremental setting where at each step t, only the current dataset D^t is available (no stored old samples), and the background label encompasses future classes, previous classes, and true background. The model uses a DeepLab-v3 architecture with a ResNet-101 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet, processing standard RGB images and producing pixel-wise segmentation masks over all classes encountered so far. The method is plug-and-play and can be applied on top of existing ISS baselines such as MiB and PLOP.

## Method

Cs²K introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, prototype-guided pseudo labeling computes similarity weights κ based on feature proximity to old class prototypes (via a softmax over negative distances with temperature τ=1) to reweight the previous model's softmax probabilities, correcting misclassified background pixels near decision boundaries. Second, prototype-guided class adaptation augments old prototypes through self-prototype augmentation (adding Gaussian noise scaled by a dynamic factor s^t that adapts to class distribution) and inter-prototype augmentation (randomly mixing two old prototypes with a uniform λ), then trains the classifier on these augmented prototypes to maintain discriminability. Third, weight-guided selective consolidation uses Fisher information to rank old model weights by importance for old classes, then selectively integrates only the top-β fraction of important weights into the new model via a weighted blend (controlled by dynamic factor ω), while leaving less important weights free to learn new classes. The overall loss combines cross-entropy on corrected pseudo labels and cross-entropy on augmented prototypes, with weight consolidation applied after each step's training.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, PLOP+Cs²K achieves 70.4 mIoU (all classes) in the 15-1 scenario, 61.5 in 10-1, and 54.8 in 5-3, while MiB+Cs²K reaches 68.0, 39.3, and 56.2 respectively. On ADE20K, MiB+Cs²K attains 34.1 mIoU (all) in the 100-10 scenario and 34.2 in 100-5, surpassing the strongest baseline MiB+EWF by 0.9 and 2.1 mIoU. The method is plug-and-play, yielding 35.8% and 31.0% mIoU gains over MiB and PLOP respectively in the 15-1 and 10-1 scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLOP+Cs²K outperforms PLOP+EWF by 3.4, 9.6, and 7.1 mIoU (all) in the 15-1, 10-1, and 5-3 scenarios respectively, with the largest gap on new classes (16.9 mIoU in 10-1). MiB+Cs²K surpasses MiB+EWF by 2.5, 2.0, and 4.4 mIoU (all) across the three scenarios, showing consistent gains particularly on new classes. Against standalone baselines, Cs²K achieves dramatic improvements: 35.8 mIoU over MiB and 31.0 over PLOP in the 15-1 scenario, and 25.0 over RCIL in 15-1. On ADE20K, MiB+Cs²K leads all methods by 0.9 mIoU (100-10) and 2.1 mIoU (100-5) over MiB+EWF, the strongest competitor. The method consistently wins on new-class mIoU across all scenarios, confirming that the weight-guided selective consolidation effectively prevents underfitting of new classes while the prototype-guided components preserve old-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cs²K positions itself at the intersection of two underexploited knowledge sources in exemplar-free incremental semantic segmentation: class-specific knowledge (old class prototypes) and class-shared knowledge (old model weights). It builds directly on MiB and PLOP as base segmentation frameworks and modifies EWF's uniform weight integration by introducing Fisher-information-guided selective consolidation, which only merges weights deemed important for old classes. The prototype-guided pseudo labeling and class adaptation components depart from prior replay-based or distillation-based approaches by using compact prototype representations rather than stored exemplars, addressing both data privacy and representativeness concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design makes Cs²K broadly applicable to existing ISS pipelines, and the selective weight consolidation offers a practical alternative to uniform EWF that practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural changes. The paper acknowledges a persistent performance gap relative to joint training in long-sequence scenarios (e.g., 10-1 on VOC), leaving open the question of whether prototype-based guidance can close this gap as the number of incremental steps grows. A key open direction is whether the dynamic β and ω scheduling rules generalize beyond the tested scenarios or require per-dataset tuning.
