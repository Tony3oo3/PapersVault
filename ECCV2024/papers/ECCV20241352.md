## Problem

Cross-modal recipe retrieval suffers from a fundamental data misalignment: recipes contain redundant cooking instructions irrelevant to visual appearance (e.g., oven temperatures, marinating times), while food images include irrelevant background and container information. Prior work focused on designing better encoders but largely ignored this misalignment, which hinders effective cross-modal alignment in a shared embedding space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB food images and recipe documents (each with title, ingredients, and instructions sections) as input. Recipes are split into three parts encoded by separate CLIP text encoders, with ingredients and instructions further decomposed into individual sentences (max 15 sentences, 20 tokens each). The visual imagination description generated by Llama2 is constrained to ~30 words to fit CLIP's token limit, and SAM segments are filtered by area threshold and CLIP-based semantic consistency before use. Output is a ranked list of recipes or images via cosine similarity in a shared 512-d embedding space, evaluated under three protocols (DAR, DAR+, DAR++) that progressively incorporate augmented signals at test time.

## Method

DAR leverages two foundation models for data augmentation: Llama2-13b-chat generates a ~30-word 'visual imagination description' from each recipe to capture visual cues, and SAM's 'everything' mode segments food images into key ingredient regions (top-4 after filtering). Rather than fully fine-tuning CLIP ViT-B/16, the framework injects lightweight bottleneck adapter layers (downsampled dim 64) into both image and text encoders, reducing trainable parameters to ~8% of full fine-tuning. A multi-level circle loss jointly optimizes four alignment objectives: image–recipe, segment–recipe, image–description, and a self-supervised recipe loss aligning title/ingredients/instructions embeddings. The augmented data (descriptions and segments) can be pre-computed and used not only during training but also at inference time, where DAR++ multiplies distance scores from all three channels for final ranking.

## Results

On Recipe1M (10k test setup), DAR++ achieves R@1 of 47.8% (image-to-recipe) and 47.4% (recipe-to-image), outperforming the strongest baseline CIP (44.9% / 45.2%) by 2.9 and 2.2 points respectively. In the 1k setup, DAR++ reaches R@1 of 77.3% (I2R) and 77.1% (R2I), surpassing CIP (77.1% / 77.3%) and MALM (74.0% / 73.0%). The method uses only 8% trainable parameters in the image encoder compared to full fine-tuning approaches like T-Food and MALM, while achieving superior retrieval quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAR++ outperforms all 13 baselines on the majority of metrics in both 1k and 10k setups. Against the strongest CLIP-based baseline CIP [9], DAR++ gains +2.9 R@1 in 10k image-to-recipe (47.8 vs 44.9) and +2.2 R@1 in 10k recipe-to-image (47.4 vs 45.2); in 1k, DAR++ edges out CIP by +0.2 R@1 (I2R) but trails by −0.2 R@1 (R2I). Compared to MALM [34], which also uses mask-based augmentation, DAR++ improves 10k I2R R@1 by +1.9 (47.8 vs 45.9) and R2I R@1 by +3.2 (47.4 vs 44.2). Even without test-time augmentation, DAR alone surpasses T-Food [27] by +0.8 R@1 (10k I2R) and VLPCook [28] by −1.1 R@1 (10k I2R), while achieving comparable or better R@5/R@10 across all directions. Notably, DAR uses only 8% trainable parameters in the image encoder (adapter layers) versus full fine-tuning in T-Food, MALM, and CIP, making it substantially more parameter-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the line of CLIP-based recipe retrieval methods (T-Food, VLPCook, MALM, CIP) that fully fine-tune the CLIP image encoder, instead adopting parameter-efficient adapter layers (Houlsby et al., 2019) to keep computation low. Its core departure is reframing the problem as one of data misalignment between recipes and food images, and addressing it through foundation-model-based data augmentation (Llama2 for visual imagination descriptions, SAM for ingredient segmentation) rather than through architectural changes to the encoders. The multi-level circle loss extends the circle loss formulation (Sun et al., 2020) to simultaneously align original and augmented data pairs across multiple embedding levels, going beyond the single-pair contrastive or triplet losses used in prior recipe retrieval work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that foundation models can serve as a data augmentation engine for cross-modal retrieval, a perspective that could transfer to other modality-pairing tasks beyond recipes. The finding that augmented data (visual imagination descriptions and image segments) improves retrieval even at test time, not just during training, offers a practical deployment advantage. However, the SAM-based segmentation remains a clear bottleneck: segments can miss small objects, over-segment large ones, and injecting adapters into the segment encoder actually degrades performance, suggesting the noise in SAM outputs limits how much the framework can exploit fine-grained visual structure. Open questions include whether more targeted or learned segmentation could close this gap, and whether the visual imagination description approach generalizes to domains where the 'visual appearance' is harder to infer from text alone.
