## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image models, when applied to garment generation, suffer from fine-grained semantic misalignment in the quantity, spatial position, and interrelations of garment components. Existing garment generation methods focus primarily on visual semantics but neglect component-level positioning and quantity, making it difficult to produce garments that match the precise details specified in captions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240653/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a garment caption (text prompt) as input and produces a 512×512 RGB garment image. During training, captions are enriched via an automatic component extraction pipeline (GroundingDINO for detection, SAM for segmentation, and a garment parsing model) that appends per-component spatial position and quantity information to the original textual descriptions, enabling the model to learn fine-grained component-level alignment.

## Method

GarmentAligner fine-tunes a pre-trained SD v2.1 latent diffusion model with a composite training objective combining retrieval-augmented contrastive learning and three component-level correction losses. For contrastive learning, each sample's retrieval subset is constructed via a multi-level semantic similarity ranking that scores component pairs using Jaro string distance and quantity differences; positive and negative pairs are then sampled (filtered by aesthetic and human-preference scores) to expand the effective training set. The three corrections target: (1) visual alignment via CLIP Score on masked component regions, (2) spatial alignment by matching text-conditioned cross-attention maps to ground-truth component masks, and (3) quantitative alignment by comparing detected component counts in the generated image against ground-truth quantities. All losses are weighted and summed into a single training objective, with the contrastive loss minimizing MSE to positive samples while maximizing MSE from negative samples.

## Results

On the CM-Fashion dataset, GarmentAligner achieves the best performance across all four evaluation metrics: FID of 8.735 (vs. 9.091 for SDXL), CLIPScore of 0.9245 (vs. 0.8974 for DiffCloth), AestheticScore of 5.8776 (vs. 5.4299 for SDXL), and HPSv2 of 0.2648 (vs. 0.2450 for SDXL). In component-level accuracy evaluation on 1000 captions, GarmentAligner outperforms all baselines by 20–45% in both quantity and spatial accuracy. A user study with 110 participants confirms that GarmentAligner is the most preferred method, with selections exceeding 28%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GarmentAligner outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest general-purpose text-to-image models (SDXL, SD v2.1), it improves FID by 0.36 and 0.42 respectively, and CLIPScore by 0.049 and 0.043, indicating substantially better text-image alignment. Against the closest garment-specific baselines, DiffCloth (FID 9.201, CLIPScore 0.8974) and ARMANI (FID 12.336, CLIPScore 0.6988), GarmentAligner achieves a 0.47 and 3.60 FID reduction and a 0.027 and 0.226 CLIPScore gain, respectively. In the component-level accuracy evaluation (Fig. 8), GarmentAligner surpasses all baselines including PixArt-α, RAPHAEL, and Attend-and-Excite by 20–45% in both quantity and spatial accuracy. The user study further confirms that GarmentAligner is preferred over SDXL, DiffCloth, PixArt-α, and RAPHAEL by a majority of 110 participants, with over 28% selecting its outputs as most authentic and consistent.

## Relation to Prior Work

GarmentAligner extends the text-to-garment generation line initiated by ARMANI and DiffCloth, which established component-level semantic alignment via divided captions and garment parsing. It departs from these methods by explicitly targeting quantitative (how many components) and spatial (where components are positioned) alignment, which prior work largely neglected in favor of visual-semantic matching. The retrieval-augmented contrastive learning strategy is borrowed from NLP and general vision retrieval-augmentation (Re-Imagen, RDMs) but repurposed here to expand a small garment dataset into contrastive training pairs, while the multi-level correction losses (CLIP-based visual, cross-attention spatial, detection-based quantitative) represent a novel composite training objective for diffusion fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automatic component extraction pipeline (GroundingDINO + SAM + garment parsing) is a reusable tool that can enrich any garment dataset with spatial and quantitative annotations, lowering the barrier for future fine-grained garment generation research. The paper's main limitation is its heavy dependence on the accuracy of upstream detection and segmentation models, which introduces error propagation at scale, and the inherited biases of the pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone. An open question is whether the retrieval-augmented contrastive learning and multi-level correction framework generalizes beyond garments to other structured visual domains (e.g., furniture, vehicles) where component quantity and spatial arrangement matter.
