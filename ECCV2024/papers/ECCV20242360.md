## Problem

Object detection and trajectory forecasting in autonomous driving are typically executed in a cascading pipeline (detection → tracking → forecasting), where the narrow tracking interface creates a lossy information bottleneck and compounding errors propagate downstream. Even recent end-to-end methods that share scene features still perform cascading inference, so detection errors inevitably degrade forecasting. No prior work unifies both tasks into a single, jointly refined output.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 consecutive LiDAR sweeps (0.5 s of history, ~107K–177K points per frame) voxelized at 10 cm in BEV, plus an HD map encoded as a lane graph. It outputs a set of BEV pose trajectories for up to 400–600 objects, each with 6 future hypotheses over 10 time steps (5 s at 2 Hz); the t=0 pose constitutes the detection (centroid, heading, length, width, presence confidence) while t>0 poses are the forecasted waypoints, each parameterized by an isotropic Laplacian with an associated mode probability. The region of interest is 80×80 m on Argoverse 2 and 150×150 m on Waymo Open.

## Method

DETRA reformulates detection and forecasting as a single trajectory refinement problem: a 4-D query volume (N objects × F modes × T timesteps × d dims) is iteratively refined over B=3 transformer blocks, with each block performing deformable cross-attention to local LiDAR tokens around the current pose, k-nearest-neighbor cross-attention to map tokens, and factorized self-attention along the time, mode, and object axes. Poses are initialized from a lightweight high-recall detector head and updated at the end of each block via an MLP (for detection parameters) and a bidirectional GRU (for future waypoints as Laplacian means/scales), with gradients stopped between blocks to stabilize learning. Training is fully end-to-end with a multi-task objective combining focal + gIoU detection losses (via bipartite matching) and a winner-takes-all Laplacian NLL + cross-entropy forecasting loss, applied at every refinement block.

## Results

On Argoverse 2 Sensor, DETRA achieves 73.0 OccAP and 50.0 TrajAP (joint metrics), 80.2 AP@IoU 0.7 (detection), and 15.4% Miss Rate with K=6 (forecasting), outperforming all baselines across every metric. On Waymo Open, it reaches 70.4 OccAP, 37.1 TrajAP, 74.9 AP@IoU 0.7, and 18.0% MR (K=6). The paper reports relative improvements of 9.6% (OccAP) and 13.4% (TrajAP) over the best baseline on AV2, and 8.5% and 13.5% on WOD, with a 27% relative reduction in Miss Rate (K=6) on AV2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DETRA outperforms all six baseline families (MultiPath, LaneGCN, SceneTF, GoRela, FaF, IntentNet) in both their modular and end-to-end variants across every metric on both AV2 and WOD. The largest gains appear in joint metrics: DETRA's 73.0 OccAP on AV2 exceeds the best baseline (IntentNet, 66.6) by 6.4 points, and its 50.0 TrajAP surpasses MultiPathE2E (44.1) by 5.9 points. On detection, DETRA achieves 80.2 AP@IoU 0.7 on AV2, a 3.9-point improvement over the best baseline (76.3, shared by MultiPath/LaneGCN/SceneTF), while maintaining comparable AP@0.3. On forecasting, DETRA's 15.4% MR (K=6) on AV2 is a 27% relative reduction over GoRela (21.3%), and its 0.55 ADE (K=6) beats IntentNet (0.80) by 31%. On WOD, the pattern holds: DETRA leads by 5.5 OccAP points over SceneTF (64.9) and by 4.4 AP@0.7 points over GoRela (72.9), with a 18.0% MR (K=6) vs. GoRela's 19.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

DETRA builds on the DETR/Deformable-DETR lineage for set-based detection with deformable attention, the mode-query forecasting paradigm from AutoBots and QCNet, and LiDAR/map encoders from VoxelNet and LaneGCN. It departs from prior end-to-end detection-forecasting methods (FaF, IntentNet, SPAGNN, PnPNet) that still cascade detection into forecasting internally, by reformulating both tasks as a single trajectory refinement problem where detection is the t=0 pose and forecasting is t>0. The factorized self-attention over object, mode, and time dimensions generalizes QCNet's single refinement step into an iterative multi-block process, while the k-nearest-neighbor map attention and deformable LiDAR attention provide the geometric inductive biases that make the 3D query volume tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified trajectory refinement formulation eliminates the tracking bottleneck and compounding errors that plague modular pipelines, and the anytime-predictor property (early exits at each refinement block) offers a practical latency-quality tradeoff for real-time deployment. The ablations confirm that local deformable attention is critical—global attention to LiDAR tokens fails catastrophically—providing a clear design principle for future work on high-resolution feature maps. Open questions include whether the approach extends to camera-based inputs, whether HD map dependency can be relaxed, and whether the refinement mechanism scales to larger query volumes in denser urban scenes.
