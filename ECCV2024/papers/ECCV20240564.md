## Problem

Existing transformer-based Large Reconstruction Models (LRMs) rely on triplane NeRF representations that impose a fixed spatial resolution, require expensive volume rendering, and are limited to object-centric reconstruction. This makes them unable to scale to large, complex scenes with varying depth ranges and high-frequency details, and their architectures demand careful design of additional 3D tokens beyond the input images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240564/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 2–4 posed RGB images (512×512 for objects, up to 512×904 for scenes) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, which are converted to 6-channel Plücker ray coordinates and concatenated with the 3-channel RGB to form 9-channel per-view feature maps. It outputs N×H×W per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives (12 parameters each: RGB, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, and ray distance), where the total Gaussian count scales with input resolution rather than being fixed. Inference completes in ~0.23 s on a single A100 GPU, and the model accepts a variable number of input views at test time despite being trained on a fixed count.

## Method

GS-LRM uses a straightforward transformer that patchifies (8×8) the concatenated multi-view feature maps into tokens, processes them through 24 self-attention + MLP blocks (1024 hidden dim, 16 heads, 4096 MLP width), and decodes per-pixel Gaussian parameters via a single linear layer. The key design choice is pixel-aligned prediction: each output token corresponds to exactly one 2D pixel, and the associated Gaussian is placed along that pixel's ray at a predicted distance, eliminating the need for triplane tokens, epipolar sampling, or other 3D inductive biases. Plücker coordinates naturally serve as spatial embeddings, removing the need for learned positional or view embeddings. Training is supervised with MSE plus a VGG-19 perceptual loss on rendered supervision views, using a 256→512 resolution curriculum; two models (object on Objaverse, scene on RealEstate10K) share the same 300M-parameter architecture with minimal domain-specific changes.

## Results

On object-level reconstruction, GS-LRM (Res-512) achieves 30.52 dB PSNR on GSO and 29.09 dB PSNR on ABO, outperforming Triplane-LRM by 3.98 dB and 1.59 dB respectively. On scene-level reconstruction (RealEstate10K), GS-LRM achieves 28.10 dB PSNR, 0.892 SSIM, and 0.114 LPIPS, surpassing the best baseline pixelSplat by 2.21 dB in PSNR. Inference takes approximately 0.23 seconds on a single A100 GPU for 2–4 input views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-LRM outperforms all six baselines across both object and scene settings. Against Triplane-LRM (Instant3D) [32], the closest object-level competitor, GS-LRM gains 3.98 dB PSNR on GSO and 1.59 dB on ABO, with visibly sharper high-frequency details (e.g., readable text) that the triplane representation cannot capture. Against the concurrent Gaussian-based LGM [59], GS-LRM achieves an 8 dB PSNR advantage on both GSO and ABO, with LGM showing distorted textures and broken geometries. On scene-level RealEstate10K, GS-LRM surpasses pixelSplat [8] (the strongest scene baseline) by 2.21 dB PSNR, 0.034 SSIM, and 0.028 LPIPS, producing fewer floaters and sharper results. The older NeRF-based baselines—pixelNeRF [70], GPNR [55], and Du et al. [22]—are all outperformed by 3.3–7.7 dB in PSNR, confirming that the pixel-aligned Gaussian prediction with a clean transformer architecture substantially outperforms both epipolar-line-based and volume-rendering approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-LRM extends the transformer-based Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) line initiated by Instant3D and DMV3D, which rely on triplane NeRF representations and 3D-to-2D geometric projection. The key departure is replacing triplane tokens with per-pixel 3D Gaussian Splatting parameters decoded directly from multi-view image tokens, eliminating the need for explicit 3D inductive biases such as epipolar lines or cost volumes. Among concurrent works, PixelSplat uses epipolar-line-based feature aggregation for scenes and LGM employs a U-Net for objects; GS-LRM unifies both settings under a single clean transformer architecture with dense self-attention over all patches, and uses Plücker ray coordinates as the sole pose conditioning mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a deliberately simple transformer (no 3D geometric structure, no U-Net, no epipolar sampling) can outperform more architecturally complex feed-forward reconstruction methods by large margins on both object and scene benchmarks, suggesting that scale and pixel-aligned prediction may be more important than hand-crafted 3D inductive bias. Practical impact is significant: ~0.23 s inference on a single A100, the same architecture handling both objects and scenes, and straightforward chaining with text/image-to-multi-view generators for 3D content creation. Open questions include extending to higher resolutions (beyond ~512×904), removing the known-pose requirement, and improving hallucination of unseen regions, which the pixel-aligned representation inherently cannot model.
