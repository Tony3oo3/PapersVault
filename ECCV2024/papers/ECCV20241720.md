## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models are trained exclusively on 2D image-text pairs, yet they appear to produce 3D-consistent outputs (shadows, reflections, geometry). It remains unclear whether these models encode an internal 3D scene representation, and if so, where in the model's architecture that representation lives. Prior interpretability work used linear probing on UNet feature maps, but no one had demonstrated a constructive mechanism for 3D control in the text conditioning space.

## Input / Output

The method takes continuous camera extrinsics/intrinsics (as a normalized projection matrix in [-1,1]) and a text prompt as input; for the NVS application it additionally takes a single RGB image of a target scene. The camera parameters are passed through a Fourier feature encoder (bandwidth σ=0.5) concatenated with the diffusion timestep and UNet layer index, producing a 64-dim conditioning vector. The output is a 768-dim word embedding (the 'view token') that is spliced into the CLIP text-encoder sequence and conditions the frozen Stable Diffusion UNet via cross-attention, yielding a 512×512 image rendered from the specified 3D viewpoint after 50 DPMSolver steps.

## Method

ViewNeTI learns a '3D view token' by training a tiny 2-layer MLP mapper (140k parameters, LayerNorm, LeakyReLU) that maps Fourier-encoded camera parameters, timestep, and UNet layer index to a 768-dim word embedding, using the standard latent diffusion loss while keeping all diffusion model weights frozen (textual inversion). In single-scene mode, the mapper is trained on as few as 3–9 posed images of one object, and the resulting continuous mapping from camera pose to word embedding reveals a pre-existing view-control manifold in Stable Diffusion's text space. In multi-scene mode, a single shared view-mapper is jointly optimized with per-scene mappers (each predicting a scene-specific semantic token) over <100 DTU scenes, producing a semantically disentangled view token that generalizes to unseen object classes. The key architectural choice is conditioning the mapper on both the diffusion timestep and UNet layer (following NeTI), since different denoising stages control different image features; the predicted embedding is L2-norm-scaled to match a reference word embedding before being injected into the prompt.

## Results

On the DTU single-image novel view synthesis benchmark, ViewNeTI achieves state-of-the-art LPIPS of 0.378, outperforming all baselines (best prior: NerDi at 0.421). It also achieves near-SOTA SSIM of 0.516 (second only to pixelNeRF at 0.564), reflecting strong perceptual photorealism. However, ViewNeTI trails all baselines on PSNR (10.947 vs. 14.47–16.52 for the best NeRF-based methods), indicating errors in precise geometric alignment and fine object details.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewNeTI achieves the best LPIPS (0.378) across all five baselines, beating the closest competitor NerDi (0.421) by 0.043 and the NeRF-based methods (pixelNeRF 0.515, SinNeRF 0.525, NeRF 0.703) by a wide margin, confirming superior perceptual photorealism. On SSIM, ViewNeTI (0.516) is second-best, trailing pixelNeRF (0.564) and SinNeRF (0.560) by roughly 0.04–0.05, but clearly ahead of NerDi (0.465) and NeRF (0.286). On PSNR, ViewNeTI (10.947) is the weakest, losing to NerDi (14.472), pixelNeRF (16.048), and SinNeRF (16.520), which the authors attribute to errors in precise object pose and small texture details. Zero-1-to-3 [33], a diffusion-based method without explicit geometry, is excluded from the table because it produces unreasonable predictions on DTU due to distribution mismatch with its training data. Overall, ViewNeTI trades geometric precision (PSNR) for perceptual quality (LPIPS), a profile consistent with leveraging a frozen 2D diffusion prior rather than an explicit 3D representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViewNeTI extends the NeTI textual inversion architecture (Alaluf et al., 2023) by conditioning the per-layer, per-timestep embedding mapper on continuous camera parameters, repurposing the TI framework to learn a 3D viewpoint control token rather than a concept token. It departs from prior 3D-representation studies in diffusion models (Chen et al., 2023; Zhan et al., 2023; El Banani et al., 2024), which use linear probing on UNet feature maps, by instead providing a 'proof by construction' in the CLIP word embedding space via cross-attention. For novel view synthesis, the method contrasts with NeRF-based approaches (pixelNeRF, SinNeRF, NerDi) by requiring no explicit 3D representation, no geometric regularizers, and no large multi-view pretraining datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first constructive evidence that a frozen 2D diffusion model's text embedding space contains a continuous 3D view-control manifold, with the striking implication that a single 768-dim word token can steer rendering viewpoint without any model modification. However, the evidence remains qualitative (no quantitative view-control metric), limited to DTU-scale objects with a restricted camera sphere, and exhibits residual style entanglement with the pretraining distribution. Open questions include whether the manifold generalizes to outdoor scenes and video models, whether larger pretraining corpora can achieve full semantic disentanglement, and whether the PSNR gap versus NeRF baselines can be closed through better reconstruction fidelity in the TI framework.
