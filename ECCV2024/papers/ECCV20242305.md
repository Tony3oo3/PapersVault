## Problem

Cross-modal steganography—hiding secret data of one modality within a cover of another—remains largely unexplored due to incompatibility between modalities that complicates embedding and increases detectability. Existing deep-learning steganography methods are restricted to fixed modality pairs, suffer from a capacity-security trade-off, and require expensive training of GANs or diffusion models. No prior work has enabled arbitrary combinations of modalities for both the secret and cover data while maintaining high capacity and undetectability.

## Input / Output

The method accepts cover data and one or more secret data items, each independently from the set {image, audio, video, 3D shape}, along with a 128-bit private key. All inputs are first converted into SIREN-based INRs with modality-specific I/O dimensions: (1,1) for audio, (2,3) for image, (3,1) for 3D shape, and (3,3) for video. The output is a single stego INR (an MLP whose weights encode both the cover reconstruction and all embedded secrets) plus, upon decoding with the key, the original secret INRs recovered losslessly.

## Method

INRSteg transforms every data item into a SIREN MLP, then allocates the secret INR weight matrices into diagonal blocks of a larger stego INR while the off-diagonal blocks are fitted to the cover data via a masked parameter update that freezes the secret weights. This block-allocation scheme generalizes to any number of secrets by partitioning the hidden-layer dimension D into sub-blocks, and allows secrets to be placed at arbitrary positions within the stego network. For security, a 128-bit key is expanded through a KDF into per-layer permutation keys that shuffle hidden-layer nodes, distributing secret parameters uniformly across the stego INR without altering its function (exploiting MLP permutation invariance). Because the entire pipeline requires only fitting small MLPs to data—no GAN, diffusion model, or adversarial training—the method eliminates training-data bias, domain-shift issues, and reduces model size to ~0.4M parameters versus 4M–1066M for prior approaches.

## Results

On image-to-image steganography, INRSteg achieves a cover PSNR of 62.34 dB and secret PSNR of 45.84 dB, substantially outperforming DeepSteg (37.48/33.34 dB) and DeepMIH (36.05/32.97 dB). For security, INRSteg achieves 50% detection accuracy on both SiaStegNet and XuNet steganalysis tools (i.e., random-chance level), whereas DeepSteg is detected at 92.87%/75.83% and DeepMIH at 90.70%/90.82%. The model requires only 0.40M parameters, making it 10–2690× smaller than compared baselines. In cross-modal settings (hiding audio+video in an image), INRSteg recovers audio at SNR 37.56 dB and video at PSNR 41.45 dB with cover PSNR of 39.26 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

INRSteg decisively outperforms both DeepSteg [1] and DeepMIH [4] on every distortion metric in the image-to-image setting: cover PSNR improves by +24.9 dB over DeepSteg and +26.3 dB over DeepMIH, while secret PSNR improves by +12.5 dB and +12.9 dB respectively. On steganalysis, INRSteg achieves 50% detection accuracy (indistinguishable from random) on both SiaStegNet and XuNet, compared to 92.87%/75.83% for DeepSteg and 90.70%/90.82% for DeepMIH, indicating the stego images are effectively undetectable. Against HiNet [8], CRoSS [27], and LF-VSN [14], the comparison is limited to model size: INRSteg's 0.40M parameters are 10.22×, 2690.54×, and 18.68× smaller than those baselines (4.05M, 1066.24M, and 7.40M respectively), enabling deployment on resource-constrained devices. The paper does not report head-to-head distortion or security comparisons against HiNet, CRoSS, or LF-VSN, as those methods target different modalities or architectures (wavelet-domain INN, diffusion-based, video INN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the INR literature (SIREN, NeRF, and general INR frameworks) by repurposing the weight space of small MLPs as a steganographic container, and on prior INR-based steganography (StegaNeRF, Han et al. 2023) which first demonstrated hiding data in neural representations but restricted the cover to images. It departs from the dominant deep-learning steganography paradigm (DeepSteg, DeepMIH, CRoSS, LF-VSN) that relies on training large encoder-decoder or generative networks, replacing them with a simple masked-parameter-update fitting procedure and a layer-wise permutation encoding for security. The key conceptual leap is that all modalities—image, audio, video, 3D shape—can be uniformly represented as SIREN networks, enabling arbitrary cross-modal hiding without modality-specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

INRSteg establishes the first framework where both secret and cover data can be any of four modalities, opening steganography to previously infeasible combinations (e.g., hiding a video and audio simultaneously in a single image). The 0.4M-parameter footprint (100×–2700× smaller than prior methods) and the 50% steganalysis detection accuracy make it practically deployable on resource-constrained devices and resistant to existing forensic tools. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large payloads (the fitting time grows with network size), how it performs under adversarial steganalysis specifically designed for INR-based containers, and whether the quantization robustness (PSNR drops ~6 dB even as SSIM holds) is sufficient for lossless-grade applications. The ethical implications of undetectable multi-modal hiding are acknowledged but not deeply addressed.
