## Problem

Self-supervised contrastive learning demands large datasets and very long training schedules (e.g., ~800 epochs), making compute costs prohibitive. Prior data-selection methods for SSL (e.g., SAS) pre-select a fixed subset before training using a small amount of labeled data, ignoring the model's evolving state and thus missing opportunities to reduce data as the model learns. This paper asks whether an unsupervised, dynamically adapting selection strategy can match or beat static selection while substantially cutting total FLOPs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 96×96 for STL-10, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) together with a small pretrained proxy encoder (ResNet-10 or ResNet-18) that is trained once via contrastive learning and then frozen. At each selection step, the target model (ResNet-50, or ResNet-18 for Tiny ImageNet) processes augmented views of a minibatch; the scoring function operates on the cosine similarities between the target model's representations and the proxy model's representations. The outputs are (a) a dynamically shrinking subset of training indices used for the next interval of gradient steps, and (b) the final learned representations, evaluated by linear-probing accuracy on a held-out test set.

## Method

DySCoF combines three mechanisms. First, an unsupervised importance score is computed per sample within each minibatch: the cosine similarity between the two augmented views of the sample minus the log-sum-exp of its similarities to all negative-view representations in the batch (Eq. 2); the importance is the gap between this score and an 'optimal' score approximated by the frozen proxy model, so samples the target model has not yet learned receive higher scores. Second, a coarse-to-fine cosine schedule (Eq. 4) governs the selection ratio r, starting at a high fraction (e.g., 80%) and decaying to a low fraction (e.g., 20%) with a tunable power β; data re-selection is triggered at variable intervals that become more frequent as the subset shrinks. Third, a Proxy-Guided Training Regularizer (PGTR) adds an L2 alignment term between the proxy and target encoders' outputs to the InfoNCE loss, jump-starting early convergence. The target model is trained with SimCLR (InfoNCE, Adam, lr 0.001, batch 512) and evaluated via the standard linear-probing protocol.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DySCoF (L) achieves approximately 31.6% reduction in total FLOPs (target model training + data selection) compared to SAS (20%) while maintaining similar linear probing accuracy. On Tiny ImageNet, DySCoF (L) uses 9.05% fewer FLOPs than SAS (60%) while achieving 29.04% accuracy versus 28.11% for SAS (60%). On CIFAR-100, DySCoF (L) reaches 68.44% linear probing accuracy with fewer FLOPs than SAS (80%) at 68.38%. In terms of convergence speed, DySCoF (M) requires approximately 45.6% fewer mini-batches than SAS (15%) on CIFAR-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is SAS (Joshi et al., ICML 2023), a static data selection method for contrastive SSL that pre-selects a fixed-size subset before training. DySCoF consistently outperforms SAS across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, Tiny ImageNet) in the compute-accuracy tradeoff: it either achieves lower FLOPs at comparable accuracy or higher accuracy at comparable FLOPs. Specifically, on CIFAR-10, DySCoF (L) is ~31.6% more efficient than SAS (20%), and DySCoF (M) saves 28.2% compute versus SAS (15%) at similar accuracy. On Tiny ImageNet, DySCoF (L) simultaneously reduces FLOPs by 9.05% and improves accuracy by 0.93 points over SAS (60%). DySCoF also demonstrates greater robustness: when the proxy model is only 40% trained, SAS (80%) drops from 68.38% to 65.9% on CIFAR-100, while DySCoF (L) drops only from 68.44% to 67.0%. Finally, DySCoF extends beyond contrastive learning, outperforming SAS when applied to BYOL with a SimCLR-pretrained proxy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the static data selection paradigm for contrastive SSL introduced by Joshi et al. (SAS, ICML 2023) by making subset selection dynamic and context-dependent on the model's evolving state. It departs from SAS in two key ways: replacing the labeled-data-dependent proxy scoring with an unsupervised proxy model that compares target-model scores against proxy-model scores, and introducing a coarse-to-fine cosine schedule that progressively shrinks the selected subset. The scoring function is structurally derived from the InfoNCE loss of SimCLR (Chen et al., ICML 2020), and the PGTR regularizer adds a novel alignment loss between proxy and target representations to accelerate early training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Efficient SSL training is increasingly critical as dataset sizes and training schedules grow, and this paper provides a practical, reusable framework (the proxy model can serve multiple target models and SSL paradigms, as shown with BYOL) that reduces compute by ~30% on small benchmarks while preserving accuracy. However, all experiments are confined to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, STL-10, Tiny ImageNet) with ResNet-50 or smaller, leaving open whether the dynamic scheduling and proxy-based scoring generalize to ImageNet-scale training or transformer-based encoders. The modest PGTR ablation gain (~0.2% accuracy) and the reliance on a separately trained proxy model (whose cost is excluded from the headline FLOPs) are limitations that temper the practical impact.
