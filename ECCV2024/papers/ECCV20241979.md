## Problem

Multi-modal crowd counting from RGB and thermal/depth images is hindered by a large spectral gap between modalities and spatial misalignment between sensors, which causes ghosting artifacts when images are directly fused. Existing methods perform cross-modal feature interactions directly between the two original modalities, but this struggles to bridge the modality discrepancy effectively. The paper asks whether introducing an intermediate auxiliary modality can smooth the fusion process and improve counting accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241979/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB and thermal (or depth) images that are not strictly spatially aligned due to different sensor angles. A lightweight Broker Modality Generator (BMG, ~4M parameters) first produces a 3-channel intermediate fused image at the same resolution as the inputs, which is then fed alongside the two source images into a shared feature extractor. The final output is a single-channel crowd density map at reduced spatial resolution (h×w), trained with Bayesian Loss against point annotations. Crop sizes are 224×224 for RGBT-CC/DroneRGBT and 1024×1024 for ShanghaiTechRGBD.

## Method

The core idea is to reframe dual-modal counting as a triple-modal problem by generating a 'broker' image via a lightweight non-diffusion network (BMG) that bridges RGB and thermal features. BMG comprises a U-Net encoder-decoder (cross-modal contracting + modal fused decoding modules) and a 1-layer cross-attention Transformer that uses thermal features as Query and RGB features as Key/Value to enhance modality alignment. Training follows a distillation-then-finetuning scheme: BMG is first pre-trained with MSE loss to mimic the 552M-parameter DDFM diffusion fusion model, then jointly fine-tuned with the counting backbone (shared VGG-19 + 2-layer Transformer encoder) using Bayesian Loss. This two-stage process both transfers strong fusion capability at a fraction of the cost and, surprisingly, alleviates the ghosting effect caused by spatial misalignment, since the counting loss penalizes the overestimation that ghosting induces in the density map.

## Results

On RGBT-CC, the proposed method achieves GAME(0)=10.19, GAME(1)=13.61, GAME(2)=17.65, GAME(3)=23.64, and RMSE=17.32, setting new state-of-the-art across all five metrics. On DroneRGBT, it reaches GAME(0)=6.20 and RMSE=10.40, and on ShanghaiTechRGBD it achieves GAME(0)=3.72 and RMSE=5.28. The Broker Modality Generator (BMG) adds only ~4M parameters (total model: 40.55M) and generates the broker modality in 0.022s per image, compared to 59s for the DDFM teacher it distills from.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 32 compared baselines across all three datasets. On RGBT-CC, it improves over the previous best MSDTrans [29] by 0.71 on GAME(0), 1.20 on GAME(1), 1.37 on GAME(2), 2.50 on GAME(3), and 1.47 on RMSE; over CAGNet [52] (RMSE 17.83) and MJPNet-T [64] (RMSE 17.83) it achieves a lower RMSE of 17.32. On DroneRGBT, it beats I-MMCCN [55] by 0.71 on GAME(0) (6.20 vs 6.91) and 0.86 on RMSE (10.40 vs 11.26). On ShanghaiTechRGBD, it achieves the best GAME(0) of 3.72 and RMSE of 5.28, surpassing CCANet [28] (3.78 / 5.56) and AGCCM [34] (3.90 / 5.86). Notably, the method achieves these gains with only 40.55M parameters, well below MC3Net [65] (113.08M) and BBSNet [3] (49.78M), while the BMG module itself adds just 3.89M parameters and runs 2680× faster than the DDFM [62] teacher (0.022s vs 59s per image) while also producing better counting accuracy (RMSE 17.32 vs 18.32).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on two distinct research threads: (1) intermediate modality generation in cross-modal person re-identification (Li et al. 2020, Zhang et al. 2021), where contrastive learning bridges visible and infrared gaps, and (2) denoising diffusion-based multi-modality image fusion (DDFM, Zhao et al. ICCV 2023). The key departure is repurposing the intermediate-modality idea for a dense regression task (crowd counting), where contrastive learning is inapplicable, by instead constructing the broker modality through image fusion. The BMG is a lightweight non-diffusion U-Net distilled from DDFM, effectively transferring the teacher's fusion capability into a 3.89M-parameter module that can be jointly trained with the counting backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification and analysis of the ghosting effect in direct cross-modal fusion is a useful empirical contribution for the multi-modal counting community, as spatial misalignment between RGB and thermal sensors is a practical concern that prior work largely overlooked. The two-stage distill-then-fine-tune strategy provides a practical recipe for incorporating heavy generative models (diffusion-based fusion) into lightweight end-to-end pipelines. Open questions include whether the broker modality concept generalizes beyond crowd counting to other multi-modal regression tasks, whether the ghosting alleviation is an artifact of the specific Bayesian loss or would hold with other supervision signals, and whether the approach remains effective when the spatial misalignment is extreme or when a third modality (e.g., LiDAR) is added.
