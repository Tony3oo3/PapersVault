## Problem

Existing BRDF reconstruction methods either overfit neural fields to individual materials (requiring dense sampling and per-material training) or rely on oversimplified analytic models that fail on complex real-world reflectance. No prior approach can simultaneously generalize to unseen materials from highly sparse, unstructured samples and compress dense BRDFs into very compact latent embeddings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary number of BRDF samples (as few as 40) specified as Rusinkiewicz-parameterized H and D direction vectors paired with reflectance values, with no requirement on ordering or regularity. For compression, it ingests the full dense MERL grid (90×90×180 per channel, ~1.46M measurements). Output is a continuous, queryable BRDF function via the hyponet MLP, a low-dimensional latent embedding (down to 7D), and a renderable material appearance; the hyponet's nonlinear structure provides built-in interpolation between sampled directions.

## Method

The architecture comprises three components: a permutation-invariant set encoder (4 FC layers, 128 hidden units, producing a 40D per-sample embedding averaged into a single latent vector), a hypernetwork decoder (10 blocks of 3-layer FC networks that output the weights and biases of the hyponet), and the hyponet itself (5 FC layers: 6→60→60→60→3 with ReLU activation, following NBRDF's structure but replacing the exponential output). The key insight is that conditioning the neural field via hypernetwork-generated weights—rather than per-material training—enables generalization to unseen materials from sparse samples while the set encoder's symmetry operation handles arbitrary input sizes. Training is supervised with a cosine-weighted MSE reconstruction loss plus L2 regularization on both hyponet weights and latent embeddings, over 80 epochs on 80 MERL materials (optionally augmented with 51 RGL materials). Preprocessing applies a Log Relative Mapping to handle HDR reflectance values before feeding the network.

## Results

For sparse BRDF reconstruction on 20 unseen MERL materials, the hypernetwork achieves PSNR of 29.6 dB, Delta E of 3.19, and SSIM of 0.968 with only 40 samples, improving to 33.2 dB / 2.14 / 0.977 at 4000 samples — outperforming both NBRDF and IPCA by at least 2 dB in PSNR and 1 in Delta E across all tested sample sizes. For BRDF compression, a 7-dimensional latent embedding yields PSNR 47.49, Delta E 0.57, and SSIM 0.994 on the full MERL dataset, substantially exceeding Neural Processes (46.13 / 2.42 / 0.935) and IPCA (29.89 / 3.32 / 0.979).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Neural Processes (NPs) [55], the prior state-of-the-art BRDF compression method, the proposed hypernetwork wins on all three metrics at the same 7D latent dimension: PSNR 47.49 vs 46.13 (+1.4 dB), Delta E 0.57 vs 2.42 (4× lower), and SSIM 0.994 vs 0.935. NPs also produces invalid (negative) sample values that black out parts of renderings for certain MERL materials, whereas the hypernetwork consistently decompresses without artifacts. Against IPCA (PCA with Log Relative Mapping) [36], the gap is even larger in compression (PSNR 47.49 vs 29.89, a 17.6 dB difference), and in sparse reconstruction the hypernetwork outperforms IPCA by at least 2 dB PSNR and 1 Delta E at every sample size from 40 to 4000. Against NBRDF [46], which requires per-material fitting and struggles with sparse samples (producing completely wrong diffuse colors for materials like natural-209 and teflon), the hypernetwork preserves diffuse colors more faithfully thanks to its learned prior over material appearance, while NBRDF only matches or exceeds the hypernetwork at very high sample counts (SSIM ≈ 0.995 with dense data).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines two established ideas—hypernetworks (Ha et al., ICLR 2017) for weight generation and set encoders from point-set networks—into a unified architecture for BRDF representation, with the hyponet structure directly inherited from NBRDF (Sztrajman et al., CGF 2021). It departs from prior neural BRDF methods (NBRDF, CNF) that overfit to individual materials requiring dense sampling, and from compression-only approaches (Neural Processes, DeepBRDF) that overfit to a fixed training dataset, by introducing a generalizable framework that handles arbitrary sample counts and unstructured sampling directions. The positioning within the broader Generalizable Neural Fields (GNF) literature is explicit, but the application to BRDFs with the specific set-encoder + hypernetwork combination is novel for this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling BRDF acquisition from sparse, unstructured point-based setups (as few as 40 samples) without per-material optimization, which could significantly reduce capture time and equipment requirements. The 7D compression outperforming the purpose-built Neural Processes method suggests hypernetworks are a more efficient latent space for BRDFs. Key limitations remain: the model struggles with specular components due to the extreme dynamic range gap, editing via latent interpolation is non-intuitive, and the work is restricted to spatially uniform BRDFs. Extending to SVBRDFs and mapping the latent space to interpretable material parameters are natural next steps.
