## Problem

Existing 3D indoor object detectors aggressively downsample point cloud features, causing severe accuracy degradation on small objects (e.g., cups, keyboards) while large furniture is detected well. Increasing spatial resolution to preserve fine-grained geometry is a natural fix, but the computational cost of generative upsampling in the decoder grows prohibitively, making high-resolution inference infeasible for real-time applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240746/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes colorless 3D point clouds voxelized at 4 cm resolution (finest level) and outputs 3D axis-aligned bounding boxes with semantic category labels, filtered by 3D NMS. A single inference-time threshold τ controls the pruning aggressiveness, providing a continuous accuracy–speed tradeoff without retraining: at τ=0 the model runs at ~4 FPS with full accuracy, while at τ=0.5 it reaches ~14 FPS with only marginal accuracy loss. The architecture scales to building-scale scenes (4500k+ points) in under 2 seconds on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

DSPDet3D builds on TR3D's multi-level sparse-convolutional architecture but replaces the decoder with four stacked Dynamic Spatial Pruning (DSP) modules that cascade from coarse to fine. After detecting objects at each level, a lightweight MLP predicts a per-voxel retention probability, which is thresholded to produce a binary pruning mask that removes voxels in regions unlikely to contain smaller objects before the next generative upsampling. The pruning mask is theoretically derived to guarantee that voxel features within a P×P×P neighborhood of every object center remain unaffected, with the radius r computed from the kernel sizes of intervening sparse convolutions and the transposed convolution. A novel 'partial addition' operator fuses the pruned upsampled features with backbone features only at retained voxels, preserving sparsity. During training, a weak pruning mode (top-N score selection after upsampling) stabilizes convergence, and positive proposals are assigned within a fixed-size cube around each object center rather than inside the bounding box, ensuring sufficient supervision for small objects.

## Results

On ScanNet-md40, DSPDet3D (τ=0) achieves 65.39 mAP@0.25 and 44.79 mAP_S@0.25, surpassing the strongest baseline TR3D-higher (65.18/41.70) by 0.21 and 3.09 points respectively. On TO-SCENE-down, DSPDet3D (τ=0) reaches 66.81 mAP@0.25 and 66.53 mAP_S@0.25, improving over TR3D-higher (63.96/62.84) by 2.85 and 3.69 points. With dynamic spatial pruning enabled (τ=0.3 on ScanNet, τ=0.5 on TO-SCENE), inference speed jumps to 12.5 and 13.9 FPS respectively while memory drops to 700 MB and 800 MB, with only a marginal accuracy loss (~0.3–0.7 mAP points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Point-based methods (VoteNet, H3DNet, RBGNet) and transformer-based methods (GroupFree3D) nearly fail on small objects, achieving mAP_S below 30 on TO-SCENE-down and below 12 on ScanNet-md40, due to aggressive downsampling in their PointNet++ backbones. The two-stage CAGroup3D achieves competitive overall mAP (54.28 on TO-SCENE) but is the slowest (2.2 FPS) and most memory-hungry (3500 MB) among efficient baselines. Among multi-level sparse-convolution detectors, FCAF3D (45.13 mAP) and TR3D (55.58 mAP) are outperformed by their higher-resolution variants (FCAF3D-higher: 57.23, TR3D-higher: 63.96), confirming that resolution is the key bottleneck. DSPDet3D outperforms TR3D-higher by 2.85 mAP points on TO-SCENE and 0.21 on ScanNet while using less memory (5300 vs 4600 MB on TO-SCENE; 4200 vs 4450 MB on ScanNet). With pruning enabled, DSPDet3D achieves the best speed-accuracy tradeoff: 13.9 FPS at 66.12 mAP on TO-SCENE, surpassing all baselines in both speed and accuracy simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSPDet3D builds directly on the multi-level voxel-based detector TR3D, replacing its decoder with stacked Dynamic Spatial Pruning (DSP) modules that prune redundant voxel features after each detection level. Conceptually, it bridges the 2D small-object detection literature (which addresses low-resolution features via FPNs and high-resolution backbones) with the 3D network pruning literature, but departs from both: unlike 2D methods that simply increase resolution at full cost, and unlike prior 3D spatial pruning (SPS-Conv, Ada3D) that prunes within a single layer, DSPDet3D prunes across decoder levels in a cascade guided by a theoretically derived affecting-field analysis. The generative upsampling operation in sparse-convolution decoders is identified as the primary computational bottleneck, a specific architectural insight that motivates the level-by-level pruning design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck for indoor 3D perception in robotics and AR/VR: small objects (cups, keyboards) are nearly undetectable by mainstream 3D detectors due to aggressive downsampling, yet increasing resolution is computationally prohibitive. The flexible accuracy-speed tradeoff via a single pruning threshold (no retraining) and the ability to process a 4.5M-point building in under 2 seconds on one GPU make this immediately deployable. Open questions include whether the cube-shaped affecting-field assumption generalizes to non-cubic kernel configurations or outdoor scenes, whether the method extends to other backbone architectures beyond sparse convolution, and whether the category-agnostic transfer to building-scale scenes holds for more diverse object distributions.
