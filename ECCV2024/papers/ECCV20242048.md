## Problem

Standard diffusion models like DiT tie together the forward noising process, the learning objective, and the sampling procedure in a rigid way, making it unclear which specific design choices drive performance. The paper asks: by systematically decoupling and varying the time discretization, model prediction (score vs. velocity), interpolant schedule, and sampler (ODE vs. SDE with tunable diffusion coefficient), can we identify which choices yield the best generative quality under identical architecture and compute?

## Input / Output

SiT operates in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE, consuming a 32×32×4 Gaussian noise tensor and a class label (with optional null token for classifier-free guidance) to produce a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image after VAE decoding. The model uses a patch size of 2, yielding 256 tokens for 256×256 images, and all sampling is performed in latent space before the final decode step.

## Method

SiT retains the exact DiT ViT backbone (with AdaLN-Zero conditioning) but replaces the discrete-time VP-SDE score-matching objective with a continuous-time velocity-prediction loss under a Linear interpolant (α_t = 1−t, σ_t = t), which makes the data-to-noise interpolation exact on [0,1] and eliminates the singularity at t=0 present in VP. The key architectural insight is that the stochastic interpolant framework decouples the forward process from sampling: the diffusion coefficient w_t in the reverse-time SDE can be chosen post-training without retraining, and the paper shows that a KL-optimized coefficient w_t^KL (or its cost-regularized variant w_t^{KL,η} for Linear/GVP interpolants) tightens the gap between the model density and the true data distribution. At inference, SiT samples via an Euler-Maruyama SDE integrator (250 NFE) with classifier-free guidance, achieving FID-50K of 2.06 (256×256) and 2.62 (512×512) with the same parameter count and GFLOPs as DiT-XL.

## Results

SiT-XL with classifier-free guidance (cfg=1.5) and SDE sampling achieves FID-50K of 2.06 on ImageNet 256×256 and 2.62 on ImageNet 512×512, surpassing DiT-XL (2.27 and 3.04 respectively) with identical model architecture, parameter count, and GFLOPs. Across all model sizes (S, B, L, XL), SiT consistently outperforms DiT by 1.1–10.8 FID points at 400K training steps. The best SiT-XL configuration also improves SIFD to 4.49 (256) and 4.18 (512) while maintaining competitive IS and Recall scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiT-XL (SDE, cfg=1.5) achieves FID 2.06 on ImageNet 256×256, outperforming the primary baseline DiT-XL (2.27) by 0.21 FID, VDM++ (2.12) by 0.06, Simple Diffusion U-ViT L (2.77) by 0.71, and StyleGAN-XL (2.30) by 0.24. On 512×512, SiT-XL (2.62) beats DiT-XL (3.04) by 0.42 FID and VDM++ (2.65) by 0.03, though StyleGAN-XL (2.41) retains a slight FID advantage. The advantage over DiT is consistent across all model scales: SiT-S improves over DiT-S by 10.8 FID (57.6 vs 68.4), SiT-B by 10.5 (33.0 vs 43.5), SiT-L by 4.5 (18.8 vs 23.3), and SiT-XL by 1.3 (17.2 vs 19.5) at 400K steps. SiT also matches or exceeds DiT-XL on SIFD (4.49 vs 4.60 at 256; 4.18 vs 5.02 at 512) and Recall (0.59 vs 0.57 at 256; 0.57 vs 0.54 at 512), while IS is comparable (277.50 vs 278.24 at 256). The gains are attributed to the combination of continuous-time training, velocity prediction, Linear interpolant, and tunable SDE diffusion coefficient rather than any architectural change.

## Relation to Prior Work

SiT builds directly on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture [45] and the stochastic interpolant framework [2], which generalizes the noise-data connection beyond the equilibrium-distribution constraint of standard score-based diffusion. It also draws on flow matching [38,40] for velocity prediction with the Linear interpolant, and on latent diffusion via the Stable Diffusion VAE [48]. The key conceptual departure is the decoupling of the interpolant (α_t, σ_t), the model prediction (velocity vs. score), and the sampling diffusion coefficient (w_t) into independently tunable design choices, whereas prior diffusion models tightly couple these to the forward SDE. The paper positions itself as a systematic ablation study over this design space rather than a single architectural innovation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear, modular recipe—velocity prediction, Linear interpolant, continuous-time training, and SDE sampling with a cost-regularized KL-optimal diffusion coefficient—that practitioners can adopt without modifying any architecture or hyperparameter, making it a strong new baseline for class-conditional image generation. The post-training tunability of w_t is a practically important insight: it allows tightening the KL bound between model and target without retraining, a degree of control absent in standard diffusion pipelines. Open questions include whether these gains transfer to unconditional generation, video, 3D, or other modalities, and whether the optimal w_t choice remains stable across different data distributions and model scales beyond ImageNet.
