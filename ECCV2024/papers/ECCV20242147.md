## Problem

Few-shot image classifiers are susceptible to spurious bias—relying on non-essential attributes (e.g., background, color) as prediction shortcuts—but no dedicated benchmarking framework exists to systematically evaluate and quantify this vulnerability. Standard FSC evaluation randomly samples support and query samples, which inadvertently preserves spurious correlations in the majority of test data and gives unfair advantages to classifiers that exploit them, masking true robustness differences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FewSTAB consumes existing FSC test sets (miniImageNet, tieredImageNet, CUB-200; images resized to 84×84) and a pre-trained VLM (ViT-GPT2 or BLIP) that generates free-text descriptions from which nouns and adjectives are extracted via Spacy as candidate attributes. It produces 3000 constructed C-way N-shot FSC tasks where support samples are curated to embed specific class-attribute spurious correlations and query samples are selected to lack those attributes, yielding a worst-class accuracy metric (wAcc-A) that ranks classifiers by their degree of spurious bias vulnerability.

## Method

FewSTAB combines two key mechanisms: (1) automated text-based attribute detection using a pre-trained VLM to generate image descriptions, from which an automatic word selector (Spacy) extracts nouns and adjectives as the attribute set, eliminating manual labeling; and (2) attribute-based sample selection strategies that construct FSC tasks with controlled spurious correlations. The inter-class selection strategy (the default) selects query samples that lack their own class's spurious attribute but contain another class's spurious attribute, maximizing the chance of misclassification for biased classifiers. A further query sample selection step computes likelihood scores based on the frequency of non-selected attributes in each candidate image and picks those with the lowest scores to minimize confounding shortcuts. This design allows FewSTAB to benchmark spurious bias at varied degrees (by adjusting shot number) and to reveal new robustness rankings that differ from standard accuracy-based rankings, particularly in the 5-shot setting.

## Results

On 5-way 5-shot tasks, wAcc-A (FewSTAB-constructed) is consistently 10–20 percentage points lower than wAcc-R (randomly sampled) across all three datasets, confirming that FewSTAB effectively exposes spurious bias in few-shot classifiers. The accuracy gap widens substantially from 1-shot to 5-shot (e.g., ProtoNet on miniImageNet: 7.93 gap at 1-shot vs. 19.88 at 5-shot), demonstrating FewSTAB's ability to benchmark spurious bias in varied degrees. Spearman rank correlations between wAcc-A and wAcc-R drop from 0.96–1.00 (1-shot) to 0.90–0.95 (5-shot), revealing that standard evaluation masks important differences in robustness. On 5-shot miniImageNet, the most robust methods by wAcc-A are RFS (36.85), CAN (36.44), and RENet (36.19), while BOIL is consistently the least robust (13.09).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FewSTAB benchmarks ten FSC methods (ANIL, LEO, BOIL, ProtoNet, DN4, R2D2, CAN, RENet, Baseline++, RFS) and reveals that standard evaluation (wAcc-R) masks significant differences in spurious-bias robustness. On 5-shot miniImageNet, RFS (36.85) and CAN (36.44) achieve the highest wAcc-A, while BOIL (13.09) is the least robust; under random evaluation, RENet (56.52) and CAN (54.23) lead, showing a different ranking. On CUB-200 5-shot, ProtoNet (60.06) and DN4 (59.25) are most robust, whereas Baseline++ (16.86) and BOIL (19.17) are severely vulnerable to spurious bias. The Spearman correlation between wAcc-A and wAcc-R rankings drops from 0.96–1.00 (1-shot) to 0.90–0.95 (5-shot), confirming that FewSTAB surfaces new robustness information invisible to standard evaluation. Notably, increasing training shots from 1 to 5 does not uniformly improve robustness: for example, CAN benefits most on 5-shot FewSTAB tasks but not on 10-shot, indicating that design choices for robustness are degree-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

FewSTAB extends the spurious-correlation robustness literature (Sagawa et al., Geirhos et al., Xiao et al.) from the traditional full-supervision setting into the few-shot regime, where the paper argues bias is more acute due to limited data. It departs from existing spurious-bias benchmarks (WILDS, CelebA) that lack sufficient class diversity for FSC training, and from MetaCOCO [67], which requires curating a new dataset, by instead reusing any existing FSC test set and automating attribute detection via a pre-trained VLM. The inter-class attribute-based sample selection strategy is a novel methodological contribution that goes beyond prior intra-class-only approaches to multi-class spurious correlation testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FewSTAB fills a clear gap: prior FSC evaluation via randomly sampled tasks inadvertently rewards classifiers that exploit spurious shortcuts, masking their fragility. The framework's practicality (works on any existing dataset, no manual annotation) makes it broadly adoptable, and the finding that a common robustness strategy (increasing training shots) fails inconsistently across test-shot settings provides a concrete design guideline. Limitations include dependence on VLM attribute-detection accuracy (~60–70% cross-VLM agreement), evaluation on only three datasets, and the assumption that text-level attributes captured by a VLM adequately represent the visual spurious correlations a classifier exploits. Open questions include whether the framework can capture more subtle or compositional spurious correlations and whether it can directly guide the training of provably robust FSC methods.
