## Problem

Existing multimodal 3D object detection methods for autonomous driving are validated primarily under favorable weather and rely heavily on LiDAR for depth estimation and query generation, causing significant performance degradation in adverse conditions such as dense fog, heavy snow, and low light. Furthermore, most fusion stacks are limited to RGB camera and LiDAR, overlooking the complementary robustness that active gated imaging and radar provide in precisely these degraded scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method consumes four sensor streams: stereo RGB camera images (scaled to 800×400 with center cropping), a mono gated NIR camera image (same resolution), a LiDAR point cloud restricted to 0–100 m range, ±40 m width, and −3 to 1 m height, and a radar point cloud with the same horizontal extent but −0.2 to 0.4 m height. Depth for the RGB branch is predicted via a cascaded recurrent stereo network, while gated-camera depth is obtained from a fine-tuned mono-RGB estimator. The network outputs 3D bounding boxes with class labels and orientations, evaluated in both full 3D and BEV formats using IoU thresholds of 0.2 (cars) and 0.1 (pedestrians) across distance bins of 0–30 m, 30–50 m, and 50–80 m.

## Method

SAMFusion is a transformer-based architecture with three core stages. First, a Cross-Modal Adaptive Blending encoder performs bidirectional attention: camera features (RGB and gated) are projected into LiDAR BEV space via pixel-wise depth lifting and scored against sampled LiDAR context, while LiDAR features are projected into camera space and scored against blended camera context; radar features are similarly refined against RGB context, with intra-modal attention run in parallel and combined via learned per-modality weights. Second, a Multi-Modal Decoder Proposal module fuses enriched LiDAR and radar BEV features through a learned Gaussian distance mask (Γ_MLP) that amplifies LiDAR at close range and radar at long range, then additively incorporates gated-camera BEV features (encoded via pillar-based pooling) to produce 200 initial proposals from all modalities rather than LiDAR alone. Third, a four-layer transformer decoder with Multi-Modal-Predictive-Interaction layers refines these proposals into final detections. The entire network is trained end-to-end for 12 epochs with Hungarian-matching loss (cross-entropy + regression + IoU), initialized from a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained with Cascade Mask R-CNN weights.

## Results

On the SeeingThroughFog dataset, SAMFusion achieves 34.31 AP for pedestrian detection at 50–80 m in dense fog, surpassing the next-best method (SparseFusion, 17.05 AP) by 17.2 AP. In heavy snow at the same range, SAMFusion reaches 41.45 AP versus MVXNet's 25.83 AP (+15.62 AP). For the car class in fog, SAMFusion attains 92.41 AP at 30–50 m (+4.6 AP over SparseFusion). In day/night settings (Table 1), SAMFusion leads all multi-modal baselines on pedestrian 3D-AP at 50–80 m by up to 11.6 AP (day) and 6.6 AP (night), while car-class gains are modest due to labeling bias favoring precision over completeness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMFusion outperforms all nine baselines on pedestrian 3D-AP in adverse weather, with the largest margins over BEVFusion (+25.7 AP in fog at 50–80 m) and MVXNet (+15.6 AP in snow at 50–80 m). Among the strongest LiDAR–camera fusion baselines, DeepInteraction and SparseFusion are the closest competitors in clear day/night conditions, but both degrade sharply in fog and snow; SAMFusion's additional gated-camera and radar modalities plus distance-weighted proposal generation close this gap. For the car class, SAMFusion matches or slightly trails DeepInteraction at 50–80 m in clear night conditions (44.40 vs 46.83 AP), a gap the authors attribute to the car annotation protocol marking objects with fewer than five LiDAR points as 'don't care.' Monomodal baselines (M3D-RPN, Gated3D, SECOND) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ AP on pedestrian detection at long range, confirming the benefit of multi-sensor fusion. The ablation (Table 2b) further shows that the multi-modal proposal strategy alone contributes +15.2 AP over LiDAR-only proposals at night, and the learned Γ_MLP distance weighting adds up to +20.7 AP, indicating that architecture design matters as much as modality count.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMFusion extends the camera-LiDAR BEV fusion paradigm (BEVFusion, MVXNet, DeepInteraction, SparseFusion) by incorporating two additional sensing modalities—gated NIR camera and radar—into a unified transformer-based architecture. It builds directly on DeepInteraction's Multi-Modal-Predictive-Interaction decoder layers and TransFusion's transformer fusion methodology, but departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a depth-guided cross-modal blending encoder that projects camera features into LiDAR space (and vice versa) using per-pixel depth, rather than relying solely on BEV concatenation or virtual-point projection; and (2) a multi-modal, distance-weighted query generation scheme that avoids the sole reliance on LiDAR for proposal initialization, which is a known failure mode under sensor degradation. The gated camera integration connects to prior active-imaging detection work (Gated3D, PatchNet) but repurposes it as one branch within a multi-sensor fusion stack rather than a standalone detector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical safety gap: most multi-modal fusion methods are validated only under clear-weather conditions, yet autonomous vehicles must operate in fog, snow, and rain where LiDAR point clouds degrade asymmetrically. The 17.2 AP gain for pedestrians in dense fog at 50–80 m is safety-relevant for vulnerable road users at the distances where reaction time is most constrained. Limitations include evaluation on a single adverse-weather dataset (SeeingThroughFog, ~13k samples), the requirement for four synchronized sensors (increasing hardware cost and calibration complexity), and modest car-class improvements partly attributable to labeling bias. Open questions include whether the distance-weighted fusion generalizes to unseen weather types, how the method degrades gracefully when individual sensors fail entirely, and whether the approach extends to downstream tasks like planning under uncertainty, which the authors flag as future work.
