## Problem

Spoken2Sign translation (spoken language to sign language) has been largely under-explored compared to the reverse Sign2Spoken direction, and existing approaches produce keypoints or 2D videos that are difficult for signers to interpret or suffer from blurriness and visual distortions. No prior work has presented Spoken2Sign results in a 3D format that enables multi-view visualization and natural co-articulation between adjacent signs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes spoken language text as input and produces a temporally coherent 3D sign sequence parameterized by SMPL-X (global orientation, body shape, facial expression, and 54-joint pose parameters), which is rendered frame-by-frame in Blender as an animated 3D avatar. The intermediate representation is a gloss sequence, and the 3D signs are estimated from monocular sign videos at 118 joints per frame. For fair comparison with prior keypoint-based works, the 3D keypoints can be re-projected to 2D frontal view for back-translation evaluation.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) a gloss-video dictionary is built by segmenting continuous sign videos into isolated signs using a CTC-based CSLR model (TwoStream-SLR); (2) each sign video is converted to a 3D sign via SMPLSign-X, an optimization-based extension of SMPLify-X that adds three sign-specific regularizers—pushing unseen joints toward rest pose, enforcing upright upper-body depth consistency, and penalizing frame-to-frame pose changes for temporal smoothness; (3) Spoken2Sign translation uses mBART as a Text2Gloss translator, retrieves the best-matching 3D sign per gloss via an ISLR confidence score, and stitches adjacent signs with a 4-layer MLP sign connector that predicts co-articulation duration and interpolates in 3D joint space (hands, wrists, elbows). The approach also yields two by-products: 3D keypoint augmentation via random rotation of the global orientation parameter, and multi-view understanding by feeding both frontal and 60°-rotated side-view keypoint sequences into a TwoStream network.

## Results

On the P-2014T benchmark, the proposed Spoken2Sign system achieves a back-translation BLEU-4 of 24.16 (dev) and 25.46 (test), with ROUGE-L of 49.12 and 49.68 respectively, setting a new state-of-the-art. On CSL-Daily, the system reaches BLEU-4 of 21.66 (dev) and 21.44 (test) with ROUGE-L of 49.69 and 49.80. In a user study with four deaf signers, the method scores 3.58–4.14 out of 5 on naturalness, smoothness, and similarity, substantially outperforming the SMPLify-X baseline (1.27–2.41).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against prior Spoken2Sign systems, the gap is substantial: Ours surpasses SignDiff [16] by 5.9 BLEU-4 on P-2014T dev (24.16 vs. 18.26) and FS-Net [58] by 7.2 BLEU-4 (24.16 vs. 16.92), while the earlier keypoint-based methods (Progressive Transformer [54], Adversarial Training [53], Mixture Density Networks [56], Mixture of Motion Primitives [57]) trail by 10–13 BLEU-4 points. Even when evaluated with SignDiff's own (weaker) back-translator, Ours still leads by 4.0 BLEU-4, confirming the advantage is not an artifact of the evaluation pipeline. For 3D sign estimation, SMPLSign-X beats the optimization-based SMPLify-X [49] by 4.95 BLEU-4 and the regression-based SMPLer-X [3] and OSX [41] by 4.67 and 1.85 BLEU-4 respectively, while also achieving the lowest 2D keypoint loss (22.09 vs. 26.87–31.56) and highest temporal consistency (0.982 vs. 0.945–0.969). The method also edges out the strong HRNet [65] 2D-keypoint baseline by 1.22 BLEU-4 on P-2014T dev, demonstrating that the 3D re-projection with temporal smoothing is preferable to direct 2D keypoint estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spoken2Sign (sign language production) line of work, which has predominantly produced keypoint sequences or 2D videos via generative models (FS-Net, SignDiff, Progressive Transformer). It departs from this by introducing a 3D avatar output format, building on the SMPL-X parametric model and the optimization-based fitting framework of SMPLify-X, while adding sign-language-specific regularization terms (unseen-joint, upright-posture, temporal-smoothness losses). For the translation backbone, it repurposes the mBART-based Sign2Spoken architecture (TwoStream-SLR) by simply reversing input/output for Text2Gloss, and leverages CTC-based CSLR models for dictionary construction rather than relying on external sign dictionaries as in FS-Net.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical Spoken2Sign system that outputs 3D signs rendered through an avatar, enabling multi-view visualization and more natural co-articulation stitching in 3D space. The two by-products—3D keypoint augmentation and multi-view understanding—demonstrate that the 3D sign representations have value beyond the primary task, improving keypoint-based SLR and SLT models. Key limitations include reliance on back-translation as the sole quantitative metric (no direct 3D sign quality evaluation), the optimization-based fitting being slow (300 L-BFGS epochs per frame), and the system's dependence on a fixed gloss vocabulary. Open questions include real-time inference, generalization to unseen signers or signing styles, and whether the 3D avatar format is genuinely more interpretable to deaf users at scale.
