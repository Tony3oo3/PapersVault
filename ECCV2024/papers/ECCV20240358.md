## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods struggle with complex real-world scenarios (multi-garment, model-to-model, intricate poses) and fail to preserve fine garment details like patterns and logos. More critically, no prior method allows users to control the wearing style—such as rolling sleeves, opening a coat, or adjusting a tuck—leaving the generation rigid and static despite the importance of styling in fashion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240358/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The main U-Net receives a 9-channel tensor (4 latent noise + 4 inpainting background + 1 binary mask) at 768×576 or 512×384 resolution, while the reference U-Net takes the garment image as a standard 4-channel latent input. For dual-garment try-on, two garment images are concatenated horizontally (H×W×3 → H×2W×3), and a randomly zeroed garment preserves single-garment capability. Control point pairs are encoded as disk maps with random integer labels (1 to K=24) to make the representation permutable, and a DW-Pose map provides additional structural guidance.

## Method

Wear-Any-Way builds a dual-branch diffusion pipeline where an inpainting Stable Diffusion U-Net generates the try-on result and a reference U-Net extracts fine-grained garment features, injected via concatenated key/value in self-attention; a CLIP image embedding replaces text conditioning for global color/texture guidance. The core novelty is sparse correspondence alignment: a point embedding network projects labeled disk maps into high-dimensional embeddings that are added to the query and key vectors of the attention layers, forcing garment features at marked points to align with target positions on the person. Three training strategies—condition dropping (randomly removing pose and degrading the mask to a bounding box), zero-initialization of the point embedding output (ControlNet-inspired), and point-weighted MSE loss—stabilize optimization and sharpen controllability. Training point pairs are collected automatically by extracting features from a siamese pre-trained diffusion model and matching via cosine similarity, avoiding the need for dense manual annotation.

## Results

On VITON-HD, Wear-Any-Way achieves the best FID (8.155) and KID (0.78), along with the best LPIPS (0.078), outperforming the previous best StableVITON (FID 8.698, KID 0.88). On DressCode Upper, it attains the best LPIPS (0.0409) and KID (0.33), with a competitive FID of 11.72 (vs. StableVITON's 11.266) and SSIM of 0.934 (vs. DCI-VTON's 0.937). The method also demonstrates superior point-control accuracy, reducing landmark distance from 35.65 (no control) to 17.65 (full sparse correspondence alignment) for upper-body garments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wear-Any-Way outperforms all eight quantitative baselines on VITON-HD in FID and KID, with the closest competitor being StableVITON (FID 8.698 vs. 8.155; KID 0.88 vs. 0.78). On DressCode Upper, it achieves the best KID (0.33 vs. StableVITON's 0.72) and LPIPS (0.0409 vs. DCI-VTON's 0.0421), though StableVITON retains a slight FID edge (11.266 vs. 11.72) and DCI-VTON a marginal SSIM advantage (0.937 vs. 0.934). GP-VTON achieves the highest SSIM on VITON-HD (0.884) but degrades severely on DressCode (SSIM 0.769, FID 20.110), indicating poor generalization. For the interactive editing comparisons, Wear-Any-Way surpasses both DragDiffusion [53] and DragonDiffusion [41]: DragDiffusion fails to precisely follow drag instructions, while DragonDiffusion tends to destroy the structural integrity of humans and garments. KGI [32] is included only in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6), where Wear-Any-Way shows clearer detail preservation. Overall, the method establishes new state-of-the-art on the primary distribution metrics (FID, KID) while remaining competitive on perceptual metrics (SSIM, LPIPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based virtual try-on lineage (TryOnDiffusion, StableVITON, DCI-VTON) by adopting the reference-only dual U-Net architecture for garment feature extraction and the zero-initialization trick from ControlNet for stable training. Its key departure is the introduction of sparse correspondence alignment, which injects point-pair embeddings into the self-attention layers of both U-Nets to enable user-controlled wearing style — a capability absent from all prior try-on methods. The training data pipeline also repurposes a recent finding that pre-trained diffusion models serve as natural image matchers, using a siamese U-Net to automatically collect dense garment-to-person point correspondences without manual annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Wear-Any-Way is the first try-on framework to support interactive, point-based manipulation of wearing style (rolling sleeves, opening coats, tucking), which has direct practical value for e-commerce and fashion design where styling variations of the same garment matter. The condition-dropping ablation reveals an important training insight: without it, the model shortcuts via pose/mask cues rather than truly learning from point correspondences, a pitfall likely relevant to other conditional diffusion tasks. Limitations include artifacts on small regions (hands) at current resolution, and the approach is confined to 2D image generation; extending to video try-on or 3D garment simulation remains open.
