## Problem

Deploying NeRFs in real-world 3D applications requires distinct models and pipelines for each task (instant reconstruction, real-time rendering, scene understanding, scene editing), leading to tedious per-task training and cumbersome experimentation. Existing generalizable NeRFs suffer from high computational complexity that precludes real-time rendering, and their potential for zero-shot scene understanding and scene editing remains unexplored. The paper asks whether a single image-based rendering NeRF backbone can simultaneously support generalizable reconstruction, zero-shot multitask understanding, real-time rendering, and text-guided editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes N=4 source RGB views at 640×512 resolution with known camera poses from a target scene, and optionally a text instruction for editing. It outputs a 3D mesh (via TSDF fusion of predicted depths), novel-view RGB images, per-point SDF values, and blending weights. Notably, the same blending weights predicted for radiance are reused to lift 2D monocular predictions (segmentation logits, edge maps, keypoints) into 3D novel views without any task-specific training, and the geometry branch can be 'baked' into a textured mesh where the lightweight appearance branch acts as a GPU shader for real-time rendering at 71.3 FPS.

## Method

Omni-Recon uses an image-based rendering backbone with two decoupled branches: a complex transformer-based geometry branch that progressively fuses geometry features (from a 3D U-Net-enhanced feature volume) and appearance features (projected source-view features) through B=2 blocks, each containing a geometry transformer (cross-attention over sampled points), an appearance transformer (subtraction attention over source views), and an occlusion transformer (self-attention along the ray), ultimately predicting a per-point SDF; and a lightweight 3-layer MLP appearance branch that predicts blending weights for composing source-view colors. The key insight is that a properly trained image-based rendering pipeline can lift 2D monocular priors to 3D novel views by reusing the RGB blending weights (predict-then-blend strategy), enabling zero-shot multitask scene understanding. For real-time rendering, the geometry branch is baked into a mesh via TSDF fusion and the appearance branch serves as a differentiable shader, jointly finetunable in under 5 minutes via Nvdiffrast. Text-guided editing is achieved by iteratively applying InstructPix2Pix to source views and reconstructing them with the NeRF backbone to enforce 3D consistency.

## Results

On the DTU dataset (15 test scenes, 3 source views), Omni-Recon achieves a mean Chamfer distance of 1.13, setting a new SOTA for generalizable sparse-view surface reconstruction, outperforming ReTR (1.17) and VolRecon (1.38). For novel view rendering on DTU, it reaches 26.32 mean PSNR, a +0.73 dB improvement over ReTR and +1.74 dB over VolRecon. The real-time rendering pipeline achieves 71.3 FPS (a >2458× speedup over baselines at 0.024–0.029 FPS), and after just 10 seconds of finetuning already matches ReTR's rendering quality, surpassing it by +2.75 PSNR after 1 minute. For zero-shot scene understanding on ScanNet, the predict-then-blend strategy yields 61.11 mIoU for semantic segmentation, a 19.79-point gain over the render-then-predict baseline (41.32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-Recon outperforms all 13 baselines on mean Chamfer distance for sparse-view reconstruction on DTU, winning on 10 of 15 individual scenes. The closest competitor, ReTR (NeurIPS 2024), trails by 0.04 in mean Chamfer distance (1.17 vs. 1.13), while VolRecon (CVPR 2023) is further behind at 1.38. For novel view rendering PSNR, Omni-Recon leads ReTR by +0.73 dB and VolRecon by +1.74 dB on average. In the real-time rendering setting, Omni-Recon's Nvdiffrast-based pipeline achieves 71.3 FPS versus 0.024–0.029 FPS for ReTR and VolRecon, and after only 10 seconds of mesh+shader finetuning it already matches ReTR's quality (25.68 vs. 25.59 mean PSNR), surpassing it by +2.75 PSNR after 1 minute. For zero-shot scene understanding, the predict-then-blend strategy beats the render-then-predict baseline by +19.79 mIoU on ScanNet (61.11 vs. 41.32) and +16.47 mIoU on Replica (32.11 vs. 15.64). After parameter-efficient tuning (LoRA), Omni-Recon surpasses SRay (CVPR 2023) on generalizable semantic segmentation by +5.20 mIoU (62.35 vs. 57.15) and +7.34% total accuracy on ScanNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

Omni-Recon builds directly on the image-based rendering (IBR) lineage (IBRNet, MVSNeRF, Neural Rays) for its backbone, adopting their paradigm of lifting 2D source-view features into 3D via blending, and on NeuS for SDF-based volumetric rendering. Its key architectural departure is the explicit decoupling of a complex transformer-based geometry branch (using cross-attention, subtraction attention, and self-attention to model occlusion) from a lightweight MLP appearance branch, a separation that prior generalizable NeRFs did not exploit for downstream efficiency. The predict-then-blend strategy for zero-shot scene understanding repurposes the RGB blending weights for arbitrary 2D monocular priors, a conceptual reuse not explored in prior IBR or generalizable NeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single image-based rendering backbone can serve as a practical substrate for reconstruction, zero-shot multitask scene understanding, real-time rendering (71.3 FPS via Nvdiffrast), and text-guided editing, reducing the need for task-specific NeRF pipelines. The predict-then-blend insight is practically valuable: it avoids feeding rendering-noisy images to 2D models and leverages multiview information, yielding a ~20 mIoU gain over render-then-predict on ScanNet. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale outdoor scenes, whether the zero-shot understanding gains hold for more diverse or fine-grained tasks, and whether the per-scene finetuning for real-time rendering can be eliminated entirely.
