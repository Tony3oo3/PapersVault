## Problem

Text-driven image editing with diffusion models suffers from a fundamental misalignment: the classifier-free guidance intended to modify a specific target region simultaneously alters non-target areas, producing unwanted global changes. Existing attention-manipulation methods (P2P, PNP, MasaCtrl) address this but are task-specific, require complex architectural modifications, and cannot be unified into a single framework for diverse editing types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240119/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a target text prompt (e.g., 'a hat') as input and produces a single edited image. It operates entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4/v1.5 (64×64 latent, 4 channels), using 50-step DDIM deterministic sampling with a guidance scale of 7.5. The source image is first inverted to a noisy latent via fixed-point DDIM inversion (N=5 iterations per step), and the editing is performed by modifying the guidance signal during the subsequent denoising pass without any network weight changes.

## Method

FreeDiff identifies that the denoising network's learned prior, combined with the decaying noise schedule, causes it to recover low-frequency components first, injecting excessive low-frequency energy into the guidance that disrupts non-target regions. The method applies progressive frequency truncation to the classifier-free guidance g_t in the 2D Fourier domain: at each timestep within a user-defined 'response period,' the guidance is filtered by a band-pass mask (high-pass radius r_t^H and low-pass radius r_t^L) that progressively widens across timesteps, retaining only the spatial frequency bands relevant to the specific edit type. Two additional zero-out operations (a relative-change threshold κ=0.6 and an 80th-percentile η-truncation) further suppress residual low-frequency artifacts. The approach is tuning-free, requires no attention map access, and uses empirically defined hyperparameter sets per editing category (identity, pose, color, environment) to select the response period and filter radii.

## Results

On a ~200-image subset of the PIE benchmark, FreeDiff achieves the best CLIP score (25.51) and the lowest background LPIPS (11.14) among all compared methods. The CLIP score margin over the closest competitor PNP (25.47) is modest, but the background preservation advantage is substantial: FreeDiff's LPIPS of 11.14 is 25% lower than PNP (15.01) and 20% lower than MasaCtrl (13.97). Per-category results (Table 2) show FreeDiff leading on CLIP score in 3 of 5 categories and on LPIPS in 4 of 5, with the notable exception of Cat:5 where P2P achieves a lower LPIPS (0.0835 vs. 0.1341).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeDiff outperforms all three attention-based baselines on both headline metrics in the overall comparison (Table 1). Against PNP, FreeDiff edges out on CLIP score (25.51 vs. 25.47) but achieves a dramatically lower background LPIPS (11.14 vs. 15.01), indicating far better preservation of non-target regions. Against P2P, FreeDiff leads on CLIP score (25.51 vs. 24.75) and LPIPS (11.14 vs. 11.83), with the LPIPS gap being the more practically meaningful difference. Against MasaCtrl, FreeDiff wins on both metrics (25.51 vs. 24.66 CLIP; 11.14 vs. 13.97 LPIPS). Qualitatively, FreeDiff handles both rigid and non-rigid edits (e.g., pose changes) within a single framework, whereas P2P and PNP fail on non-rigid tasks and MasaCtrl struggles with object replacement or addition. The per-category breakdown reveals that PNP still leads on CLIP score for Cat:1 and Cat:3, and P2P achieves the best LPIPS on Cat:5, suggesting FreeDiff's advantage is most pronounced in texture/identity editing (Cat:2) and general object editing (Cat:7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020) as its core inversion mechanism but departs fundamentally from the dominant attention-manipulation paradigm (P2P, PNP, MasaCtrl) for guidance refinement in text-driven image editing. Rather than swapping or re-weighting cross-attention or self-attention maps at specific layers and timesteps, FreeDiff operates solely on the denoising network's output in the spatial frequency domain, applying progressive band-pass filtering to the classifier-free guidance signal. The key conceptual departure is reframing the well-known guidance misalignment problem through the lens of the power law of natural images and the decaying noise schedule, providing a frequency-domain explanation for why the network's learned prior introduces excessive low-frequency alterations during editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeDiff offers a unified, tuning-free framework that handles both rigid and non-rigid editing within a single mechanism, avoiding the task-specific attention manipulations that limit the versatility of P2P, PNP, and MasaCtrl. The frequency-space analysis provides theoretical grounding for empirical timestep-dependent practices previously observed but not formally explained in the editing literature. However, the method remains sensitive to prompt wording, requires per-editing-type hyperparameter sets, and is constrained by the base SD model's prior (e.g., failing on precise pose changes or multi-object disambiguation). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), whether it can be productively combined with attention manipulation for finer control, and whether the two-step color-editing strategy extends to other low-frequency-dominant editing types.
