## Problem

Transferring a temporal action segmentation model from exocentric (fixed-camera) to egocentric (wearable-camera) views typically requires expensive egocentric labeling, while standard unsupervised domain adaptation leaves a residual domain shift. The paper asks whether unlabeled synchronized exocentric-egocentric video pairs—which are inexpensive to collect in real deployment scenarios (e.g., factory workers wearing headsets alongside existing fixed cameras)—can serve as a sufficient self-supervision signal to close the exo-ego gap without any egocentric annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241926/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time the method consumes labeled exocentric videos (for teacher pre-training) and unlabeled time-synchronized exo-ego video pairs (for distillation); at test time it operates on egocentric video alone with no exocentric input. The feature extractor (TSM or DINOv2) produces dense frame-wise features from each video, which are fed to a C2F-TCN head that outputs per-frame coarse-grained action class predictions. Assembly101 egocentric frames are 640×480 grayscale, while exocentric frames are 1920×1080 RGB; EgoExo4D adds non-standardized camera placement across videos.

## Method

The model is factorized as Ψ = γ ∘ φ (feature extractor followed by C2F-TCN TAS head). A teacher (φ^T, γ^T) is first trained on labeled exocentric data and then frozen. A student with identical architecture is adapted on unlabeled synchronized exo-ego pairs via two complementary distillation losses: (1) feature distillation, an MSE between the student's egocentric features and the teacher's exocentric features, and (2) TAS model distillation, an MSE between intermediate-layer activations of the student's C2F-TCN on egocentric features and the teacher's C2F-TCN on exocentric features. The key insight is that temporal synchronization of the video pairs provides a strong frame-aligned supervision signal that is far more informative than unpaired domain adaptation losses (MMD, CORAL, GRL) or temporal alignment objectives (TCC, SDTW), enabling the adapted model to match fully supervised egocentric baselines without ever seeing an egocentric label.

## Results

On Assembly101 (v3→e4 setting), the proposed synchronized knowledge distillation method achieves 28.59 Edit score, 29.58 F1_10, 24.84 F1_25, and 31.36 MoF, improving by +15.99 Edit over the no-adaptation baseline (12.60) and by +10.75 Edit over the strongest baseline (TCC* at 17.84). On the more challenging EgoExo4D dataset, the method recovers the exo-ego domain gap with a +3.32 Edit improvement (23.88 vs 20.56) for EXO→EGO transfer and +4.44 Edit (20.49 vs 16.05) for the reverse EGO→EXO direction. Notably, the best adapted model (28.59 Edit) closely matches the ego-oracle supervised baseline (29.25 Edit) without using any egocentric labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Among domain adaptation losses, DCORAL [46] is the strongest (17.13 Edit), followed by MMD [17] (16.98) and GRL [14] (16.57), all of which remain far below the proposed 28.59 Edit. Among temporal alignment losses, TCC [11] with same-procedure pairs (TCC*, 17.84 Edit) is the best baseline, while SDTW [18] with same-procedure pairs (SDTW*, 15.27) and random pairs (13.82) perform worse. The gap between the proposed method and the best baseline (TCC*) is +10.75 Edit and +13.64 MoF, demonstrating that synchronized video pairs provide substantially stronger supervision than unsupervised domain alignment or temporal cycle-consistency objectives. A random-pair MSE baseline (13.27 Edit) confirms that the gains are not merely from any pairwise loss but specifically from the temporal synchronization signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes knowledge distillation — a technique originally designed for model compression — as a domain adaptation mechanism, leveraging unlabeled synchronized exo-ego video pairs as the distillation signal. Prior exo-to-ego transfer work (Charades-Ego, EgoExo, view-invariant representation learning) either targeted different tasks (action recognition, affordance grounding) or operated without synchronized pairs, while standard unsupervised domain adaptation methods (MMD, DCORAL, GRL) discard the temporal correspondence that synchronized pairs provide. The paper carves new ground by showing that the temporal alignment inherent in synchronized pairs, combined with a simple MSE distillation loss at the feature and model levels, is sufficient to close the exo-ego domain gap for temporal action segmentation without any target-domain labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: in real deployment (factories, assistive robotics), collecting synchronized exo-ego pairs is inexpensive compared to labeling egocentric videos, and the method requires as little as ~5 hours of such pairs to double baseline performance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to scenarios where no exocentric camera is available at test time (the paper assumes exocentric access during adaptation only), how it scales to more diverse or uncontrolled camera placements beyond the curated EgoExo4D cooking subset, and whether the distillation signal degrades when synchronization errors exceed the tested 3% drop rate. The finding that domain-agnostic DINOv2 features outperform domain-specific TSM features in the adaptation setting also raises questions about the role of pretraining in cross-view transfer.
