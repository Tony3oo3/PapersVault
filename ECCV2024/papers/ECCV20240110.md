## Problem

Training-based Generative Architecture Search (GAS) methods require hundreds to thousands of GPU-days due to the instability and complexity of GAN training, while existing training-free zero-cost proxies designed for classification tasks show poor or even negative correlation with generative model performance. The paper asks how to automatically discover customized training-free proxies that accurately rank generator architectures for generation tasks without any parameter training.

## Input / Output

The proxy search consumes pre-computed architecture-performance pairs (7k U-Net generators with SSIM scores) from TransNAS-Bench-101, requiring only a single forward pass of 16 randomly generated Gaussian noise images through each candidate to extract per-layer latent features. The architecture search stage takes randomly initialized generator candidates from a macro-level search space (e.g., EAGAN's space) and outputs the highest-scoring architecture, which is then fully trained for 500 epochs. For image-to-image translation, the proxy operates on 512×512 source images to compress Pix2Pix or GauGAN architectures by varying channel and layer configurations.

## Method

Auto-GAS grounds its proxy design in information bottleneck theory, arguing that mutual information between input data and latent representations (approximated as feature entropy for deterministic networks) is the key signal for generative quality. It defines a proxy search space as a computation graph with four sequential stages—transform (swish, relu, norm, etc.), encoding (gram-matrix, sign, hamming, etc.), reduction (slogdet, trace, eigenvalue, etc.), and augment (abs, pow, log, etc.)—applied to per-layer latent features. An evolutionary algorithm with crossover and mutation searches this space using Kendall's Tau correlation against benchmark performance as the fitness objective, converging in ~1.5 hours. The discovered proxy, (slogdet[[F′, F′ᵀ]])² where F′ = above-mean(swish(F)), achieves 61.14% Kendall correlation, substantially outperforming handcrafted proxies like NWOT (51.28%) and parameter-based methods (negative correlation). The evolved proxy is then directly applied to rank untrained generator candidates, enabling 110× faster search than GAN Compression while matching or exceeding state-of-the-art FID and IS scores.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Auto-GAS achieves IS 8.44 and FID 12.21 with only 0.09 GPU-days of search cost, outperforming the best training-based evolutionary method EAGAN (IS 8.41, FID 12.83) and all other training-free proxies (best: NWOT at FID 16.32). On STL-10, Auto-GAS reaches IS 9.72 and FID 23.19, surpassing EAGAN (IS 9.69, FID 23.82) and AutoGAN (FID 31.01). For image-to-image translation, Auto-GAS achieves FID 63.66 on horse→zebra (29.3× compression) and FID 56.04 / mIoU 61.21 on Cityscapes (4.5× compression), while being 110× faster in search than GAN Compression. The auto-discovered proxy also achieves the highest ranking correlation on TransNAS-Bench-101 (Kendall 61.14%, Spearman 79.32%, Pearson 81.25%), exceeding the best prior proxy NWOT (Kendall 51.28%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Auto-GAS outperforms all training-free zero-cost proxies (Fisher, Zen, SNIP, NWOT) on both CIFAR-10 and STL-10, achieving the best FID (12.21 vs. 15.99–17.66) and IS (8.44 vs. 7.96–8.05) while matching their 0.09 GPU-day search cost. Against training-based NAS methods, Auto-GAS surpasses EAGAN (the strongest EA baseline) on both datasets in FID (12.21 vs. 12.83 on CIFAR-10; 23.19 vs. 23.82 on STL-10) and IS, while requiring 9× less search time (0.09 vs. 0.8 GPU-days). Compared to AutoGAN (2 GPU-days), Auto-GAS achieves a lower FID on CIFAR-10 (12.21 vs. 12.42) and a substantially lower FID on STL-10 (23.19 vs. 31.01). For image-to-image translation, Auto-GAS achieves the best FID among compression methods on horse→zebra (63.66 vs. 65.19 for Fast GAN Comp.) with a 105× search speedup, and competitive mIoU on Cityscapes (61.21 vs. 61.22 for GAN Comp.) with a 110× speedup. The auto-discovered proxy also dominates all prior proxies in ranking correlation on TransNAS-Bench-101, achieving Kendall 61.14% vs. NWOT's 51.28% and EZNAS's 23.30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

Auto-GAS extends the evolutionary proxy search paradigm introduced by EZNAS (Akhauri et al., NeurIPS 2022) from classification to generative tasks, but departs in several key ways: it uses latent feature statistics (rather than parameters or gradients) as proxy inputs, motivated by Information Bottleneck theory, and designs a richer search space with transform, encoding, reduction, and augment operations tailored to encoder-decoder generator architectures. It also contrasts with existing training-free NAS proxies (Synflow, SNIP, NWOT, Zen) that were hand-crafted for encoder-only classification models and, as the paper demonstrates, exhibit negative or weak correlation on generation benchmarks. On the GAS side, it positions itself against training-based methods (EAGAN, AutoGAN, AdversarialNAS) that require hundreds to thousands of GPU-days, offering a training-free alternative that only needs a single forward pass per candidate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 110× search speedup over GAN Compression (0.09 vs. 11.6 GPU-days) makes GAN architecture search practically accessible for resource-constrained settings, and the discovered proxy transfers across different search spaces (EAGAN for image generation, GAN Compression for image translation) without re-searching. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, STL-10 at 48×48) and relatively modest search spaces; whether the proxy generalizes to large-scale generation (e.g., ImageNet-resolution GANs or diffusion models) remains open. The paper also does not explore whether the evolutionary search is strictly necessary—simpler grid search over the same operation space might capture most of the gain—and the proxy was searched on an autoencoding benchmark (TransNAS-Bench-101) before being transferred to adversarial generation, a gap that could limit robustness.
