## Problem

Existing 3D weakly supervised semantic segmentation methods treat scene-level or subcloud-level labels as mere one-hot digits, ignoring the rich textual semantics carried by category names like 'chair' or 'table'. While 2D-3D fusion has been explored, prior approaches either require additional 2D image-level annotations or fail to deliver competitive performance, leaving open the question of whether pretrained vision-language models can bridge 3D point clouds and text without any per-point or per-pixel supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241944/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes an RGB point cloud (N points, each with 6-dim RGBXYZ), a set of T multi-view RGB images at H×W resolution with known camera calibration, scene-level labels, and text category labels. During inference, only the 3D point cloud is required; the 2D images and their projections are entirely removed, and segmentation is produced by comparing the learned 3D embeddings against frozen text embeddings via matrix multiplication and argmax.

## Method

3DSS-VLG uses 2D images as a semantic bridge: a frozen OpenSeg image encoder produces per-pixel 2D embeddings that are back-projected onto 3D points via geometric camera calibration, yielding 2D-projected embeddings per point. A three-stage training procedure then refines these into a reliable 3D embedding space: (1) pseudo labels are generated by multiplying 2D-projected embeddings with frozen text embeddings and filtering unreliable predictions using scene-level label masks; (2) the Embeddings Specialization Stage trains a two-layer MLP adapter (with residual connection) to transform 2D-projected embeddings into adapted 3D embeddings, supervised by cross-entropy on the pseudo labels; (3) the Embeddings Soft-Guidance Stage freezes the adapter and aligns MinkowskiNet18A UNet 3D embeddings to the adapted 3D embeddings via cosine similarity loss. At inference, only the MinkowskiNet 3D backbone and the frozen text encoder remain, making the pipeline image-free at test time.

## Results

On S3DIS, 3DSS-VLG achieves 45.3 mIoU using only scene-level labels, surpassing the previous best weakly supervised method MIT (27.7 mIoU) by 17.6 points. On ScanNet, it reaches 48.9 mIoU (test) and 49.7 mIoU (val), outperforming Kweon et al. (47.4/49.6) which additionally requires image-level annotations, and MPRM (41.1/43.2) which uses subcloud-level labels. The method also exceeds several fully supervised baselines such as PointNet (41.1 on S3DIS) and PointNet++ (33.9 on ScanNet test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among scene-level weakly supervised methods, 3DSS-VLG dominates: it outperforms MIT [44] by 17.2 mIoU on ScanNet test (48.9 vs 31.7) and 13.9 on val (49.7 vs 35.8), WYPR [31] by 24.9 on test (48.9 vs 24.0), and MIL-Trans [45] by 23.5 on val (49.7 vs 26.2). It also beats MPRM [36], which uses the more expensive subcloud-level annotations, by 7.8 mIoU on test (48.9 vs 41.1) and 6.5 on val (49.7 vs 43.2). Notably, 3DSS-VLG surpasses Kweon et al. [18] by 1.5 mIoU on the ScanNet test set (48.9 vs 47.4) despite requiring no image-level annotations, and matches it on val (49.7 vs 49.6). On S3DIS, the gap over MIT is even larger at 17.6 mIoU (45.3 vs 27.7). The method does not close the gap to strong fully supervised baselines like MinkowskiNet (73.6) or SemAffiNet (74.9) on ScanNet, but it outperforms PointNet++ (33.9) and TangentConv (43.8) on the test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the conventional 3D WSSS paradigm (MPRM, MIL-Trans, WYPR, MIT) that treats scene-level labels as mere one-hot identifiers and relies on CAM-based back-derivation for per-point predictions. Instead, it repurposes the pretrained 2D open-vocabulary segmentation model OpenSeg (Ghiasi et al., ECCV 2022) as a frozen semantic bridge: 2D images connect to 3D points via geometric projection, and 2D images connect to category text via CLIP-style alignment, implicitly co-embedding 3D point features with text embeddings. The three-stage training (pseudo-label filtering, adapter-based specialization, cosine-similarity soft guidance) is a novel architectural contribution that sits between the 2D OVSS literature and the 3D sparse-convolution backbone (MinkowskiNet), but the core insight is that the textual content of category names—previously ignored—carries exploitable semantic signal for 3D segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a scene-level-label-only method can outperform Kweon et al. (which additionally requires 2D image-level annotations) by 1.5% on ScanNet and surpass the previous scene-level SOTA (MIT) by 17.6% on S3DIS is practically significant for annotation-cost-sensitive indoor scene understanding. The demonstrated cross-dataset generalization (ScanNet→S3DIS: 33.3 mIoU without fine-tuning) suggests the text-anchored embedding space is more transferable than CAM-based representations, though the reverse direction (S3DIS→ScanNet: 13.4 mIoU) is weak, likely due to the smaller training set. A key open question is how the method handles abstract or ambiguous categories (e.g., 'other' in S3DIS), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; more broadly, whether this text-bridging strategy extends to outdoor or large-scale 3D scenes with different category taxonomies remains unexplored.
