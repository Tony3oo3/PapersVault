## Problem

Standard semantic segmentation models use one-hot category-level label spaces that ignore inter-category semantic correlations, resulting in poor out-of-domain generalization and limited interpretability. Prior attempts to fix this—manual label merging, hierarchical label spaces, or CLIP-based embeddings—either degrade performance, lack scalability, or remain opaque because CLIP's image-text pre-training does not encode rich common-sense knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241009/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images at 512×512 (natural scenes) or 768×768 (driving) and produces pixel-wise embeddings of dimension 384 or 768. These embeddings are compared via cosine similarity against a fixed property embedding bank (≈256 vectors) to yield multi-hot property-level logits per pixel. At inference, a category label is assigned by selecting the category whose multi-hot property descriptor has the highest cosine similarity with the pixel's property logits, while the raw property activations also serve as interpretable segmentation maps (e.g., 'covered in fur', 'electronic device').

## Method

ProLab replaces the conventional one-hot category label space with a multi-hot property-level label space constructed entirely from LLM common-sense knowledge. First, GPT-3.5 is prompted with a structured template to generate uniform descriptions (shape, texture, material, color, typical contexts) for every category in the training set. Second, these descriptions are encoded by a sentence embedding model (BGE) and clustered via K-Means into approximately 256 interpretable properties. The segmentation backbone (ViT-Adapter + UperNet) is then trained with cosine-similarity loss against the multi-hot property labels, using a sigmoid-rescaled logit temperature of 0.04. At test time, category assignment is a nearest-neighbor lookup in property space rather than a learned classifier head, which is what enables zero-shot generalization to unseen categories by matching their properties.

## Results

On the primary comparison (Table 1), ProLab with ViT-Adapter (DeiT-B) consistently outperforms the category-level baseline across all five benchmarks, with the largest gains on Pascal Context (58.2 vs. 53.3 mIoU, +4.9) and BDD (65.7 vs. 60.7 mIoU, +5.0). On ADE20K with a larger backbone (BEiT-L/BEiTv2-L + UperNet), ProLab achieves 58.2 and 58.7 mIoU respectively, setting a new state-of-the-art among models of comparable scale. ProLab also demonstrates better scalability, continuing to improve with extended training steps where the category-level baseline saturates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the category-level ViT-Adapter baseline, ProLab wins on all five datasets, with the most substantial margins on Pascal Context (+4.9 mIoU) and BDD (+5.0 mIoU), and smaller but consistent gains on ADE20K, COCO-Stuff, and Cityscapes. ProLab is also plug-and-play: when applied to DeepLabv3+ (ResNet50) it improves ADE20K from 42.7→43.6, Cityscapes from 79.5→79.9, and BDD from 63.6→64.7; on SegFormer (MIT-B1) it lifts ADE20K from 41.5→42.3, Cityscapes from 78.8→79.9, and BDD from 61.7→62.1. On the ADE20K SOTA table, ProLab (58.2 mIoU with BEiT-L) surpasses Swin-L (56.1), FaPN (56.4), SeMask (57.0), HorNet (57.5), and BEiT-L (56.7), trailing only the much larger 3B-parameter SwinV2-G (59.3). In the open-vocabulary setting (Table 5), ProLab with linear probing (ProLab†) achieves 57.7 mIoU on Pascal Context and 92.5 on Pascal VOC, outperforming CLIP-based methods like ODISE (57.3 / –) and MaskCLIP (45.9 / –) without requiring large-scale image-text pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProLab carves new ground by repurposing LLM common-sense knowledge as a supervision signal for segmentation, departing from the dominant CLIP-based label-space construction (LSeg, LSeg+, LMSeg) that relies on image-text paired pre-training. Conceptually, it revives the older attribute-learning and semantic-hierarchy literature (Farhadi et al., Dekel et al., Lambert et al.) but automates the property extraction via GPT-3.5 prompts and sentence-embedding clustering rather than manual annotation. The segmentation backbone (ViT-Adapter + UperNet) is standard; the novelty lies entirely in replacing the one-hot category label space with a multi-hot property-level space derived from pure language models, sidestepping CLIP's long-tail and interpretability limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent ~5 mIoU gains across five diverse benchmarks and the demonstrated scalability (no performance saturation with extended training) suggest that richer inter-class semantic structure in the label space is an underexploited lever for segmentation. The emergent ability to segment out-of-domain or entirely unknown categories via in-domain properties opens a practical path toward open-vocabulary segmentation without requiring large-scale image-text alignment. Open questions include how the method scales to very large category spaces (thousands of classes), whether the K-Means clustering step can be replaced by learned property discovery, and whether the approach transfers to other dense prediction tasks like panoptic or instance segmentation.
