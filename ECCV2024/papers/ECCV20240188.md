## Problem

In exemplar-free class-incremental learning under the Cold Start setting (training from scratch with a small first task), semantic drift of the feature backbone progressively invalidates stored class prototypes, causing severe forgetting. Prior drift compensation methods like SDC assume drift is locally a translation, which fails under more complex transformations (rotation, scaling). The paper demonstrates that most of this forgetting is correctable by prototype compensation rather than being due to loss of discriminative power in the backbone.

## Input / Output

At each task boundary, LDC consumes the frozen old feature extractor, the frozen new feature extractor, the stored old class prototypes (one vector per class, no images or features), and the current task's unlabeled data to train the projector. The final output is a class label predicted by a Nearest Class Mean classifier that measures Euclidean distance between the test image's feature and all accumulated (drift-corrected) prototypes. No exemplars, old images, or old features are ever stored, and the projector training requires no labels.

## Method

LDC learns a forward projector p_F^t (typically a single linear layer) at the end of each task by minimizing MSE between p_F^t(f_{θ}^{t-1}(x)) and f_{θ}^{t}(x) over current task data, with both feature extractors frozen. The learned projector is then applied to all previously stored class prototypes to update their positions in the new feature space. Unlike SDC, which assumes a local translation, LDC makes no parametric assumption on the drift and can capture arbitrary nonlinear transformations. The method is label-agnostic for projector training, enabling the first exemplar-free semi-supervised continual learning setup where labels are used only for prototype computation while all available data (labeled and unlabeled) trains the projector. LDC is modular and can be plugged on top of any CL strategy (LwF, CaSSLe, PFR, POCON) without modifying the backbone training procedure.

## Results

In the supervised Cold Start setting (10 tasks, ResNet-18), LwF+LDC achieves 45.4% last-task accuracy on CIFAR-100, 34.2% on Tiny-ImageNet, and 51.4% on ImageNet100, outperforming the strongest baseline EFC by 1.8% and 4.1% respectively. In the semi-supervised exemplar-free setting (first of its kind), CaSSLe+LDC reaches 35.0% on CIFAR-100 and 35.8% on ImageNet100 with 25% labels, surpassing SDC by over 8% and matching the exemplar-based NNCSL method. On Stanford Cars with a pre-trained ViT-B/16, LDC improves last-task accuracy from 53.3% (SDC) to 62.9% after 10 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDC consistently outperforms all supervised baselines across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor EFC (also designed for Cold Start), LDC wins by 1.8% on CIFAR-100 (45.4 vs 43.6) and 4.1% on ImageNet100 (51.4 vs 47.3), while essentially tying on Tiny-ImageNet (34.2 vs 34.1). The translation-based SDC method, which was effective in Warm Start settings, fails to improve over naive NCM in the higher-drift Cold Start regime and is outperformed by LDC by 4.8% on CIFAR-100 and 8.8% on ImageNet100. Methods that freeze the backbone after the first task (FeTrIL, FeCAM) or use prototype augmentation (PASS) are all surpassed by 5–13% on last-task accuracy. In the semi-supervised setting, LDC surpasses SDC by at least 7% across all SSL backbones (PFR, CaSSLe, POCON) and reaches parity with the exemplar-based NNCSL at 5% and 25% label budgets, while NNCSL degrades sharply when exemplars are removed (dropping to 8.7–9.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within exemplar-free class-incremental learning, departing from the dominant Warm Start paradigm by targeting the harder Cold Start setting where the backbone is trained from scratch on a small first task. It builds directly on regularization-based CL (LwF) and self-supervised CL strategies (CaSSLe, PFR, POCON) as the backbone training methods, but introduces a novel prototype correction layer (LDC) that is orthogonal to these strategies. Conceptually, it departs from prior drift compensation work (SDC, EFC, FeTrIL) by replacing the assumption of local translational drift with a learnable, assumption-free projector, and by being label-agnostic in its projector training, which enables the first exemplar-free semi-supervised CL formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that most forgetting in class-prototype methods stems from feature drift rather than genuine loss of discriminative power reframes how the community should think about exemplar-free CL: the features are still there, they just need repositioning. LDC's plug-and-play nature (a single linear layer trained per task) makes it immediately adoptable on top of any existing CL pipeline, and the semi-supervised extension opens a practically important regime where only a small fraction of labels is available. The acknowledged gap between corrected and oracle prototypes, driven by biases in current-task data, remains an open problem for further refinement.
