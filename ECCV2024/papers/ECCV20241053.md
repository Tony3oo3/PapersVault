## Problem

Global SfM pipelines are orders of magnitude faster and more scalable than incremental systems like COLMAP, but have historically suffered from inferior accuracy and robustness. The root cause lies in the translation averaging step, which is ill-posed due to scale ambiguity, highly sensitive to noisy observations and inaccurate camera intrinsics, and degenerates under co-linear camera motion. This gap has limited the adoption of global SfM for real-world applications involving uncalibrated images or sequential data.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a collection of 2D RGB images (unordered or sequential, calibrated or uncalibrated) and optionally pre-computed feature matches. It handles large-scale collections (tens of thousands of images in LaMAR) and internet photos with unknown or inaccurate intrinsics. The output is a 3D point cloud, per-camera extrinsics (rotation and translation), and refined camera intrinsics, with optional camera clustering to separate non-overlapping reconstructions.

## Method

GLOMAP's key innovation is a single 'global positioning' step that jointly estimates camera positions and 3D point positions, replacing the traditional two-stage pipeline of translation averaging followed by triangulation. The optimization minimizes normalized direction differences between observed camera rays and rays from estimated 3D points, using a Huber robustifier and Levenberg-Marquardt solver, with all variables initialized randomly. By discarding relative translation constraints entirely, the method becomes robust to unknown or inaccurate camera intrinsics and handles co-linear motion, since feature tracks constrain multiple overlapping cameras simultaneously. The full pipeline proceeds as: correspondence search with RootSIFT and view graph calibration, rotation averaging via Chatterjee et al., global positioning, iterative global bundle adjustment with track filtering, and optional structure refinement and camera clustering.

## Results

On ETH3D SLAM (sequential, calibrated), GLOMAP achieves 66.4 Recall@0.1m and 57.0 AUC@0.1m, outperforming COLMAP (57.9 / 47.6) by ~8 and ~9 points while running ~8× faster (133.5s vs 1115.4s). On IMC 2023 (unordered, uncalibrated), GLOMAP scores 69.6 AUC@3° vs COLMAP's 65.3, again ~8× faster (497s vs 4051s). On MIP360, GLOMAP reaches 97.5 AUC@3° (vs COLMAP 96.5), and on LaMAR it achieves 49.1 Recall@1m vs COLMAP's 32.0, demonstrating consistent superiority across calibrated, uncalibrated, sequential, and unordered settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLOMAP consistently outperforms both global SfM baselines (OpenMVG and Theia) across all six datasets by large margins—e.g., on ETH3D SLAM it gains ~18 points in Recall@0.1m over Theia and ~14 over OpenMVG. Against COLMAP (the dominant incremental SfM system), GLOMAP achieves higher accuracy on ETH3D SLAM (+8.5 Recall@0.1m, +9.4 AUC@0.1m), IMC 2023 (+4.3 AUC@3°), and LaMAR (+17.1 Recall@1m), while matching or slightly exceeding COLMAP on ETH3D MVS (DSLR) (80.8 vs 79.2 AUC@1°) and MIP360 (97.5 vs 96.5 AUC@3°). The runtime advantage is dramatic: GLOMAP is ~8× faster than COLMAP on ETH3D SLAM, ~8× faster on IMC 2023, and ~1.5× faster on MIP360, while remaining comparable to other global SfM pipelines. The only notable loss to COLMAP is on ETH3D MVS (rig) where COLMAP achieves slightly higher AUC@1° on some scenes (e.g., forest: 74.7 vs 74.1), though GLOMAP still wins on average (57.6 vs 45.5). The ablation (Table 7) confirms that using only point-ray constraints (without relative translation terms) yields the best accuracy, validating the core design choice over BATA and LUD-based translation averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLOMAP departs from the standard global SfM pipeline (rotation averaging → translation averaging → triangulation → bundle adjustment) used by OpenMVG and Theia by merging the translation averaging and triangulation steps into a single joint 'global positioning' optimization. The objective function is adapted from Zhuang et al.'s BATA formulation [81], but the paper deliberately discards the relative translation constraints that prior works (Wilson et al. [75], Cui et al. [18]) incorporated, relying instead solely on camera-ray-to-point direction differences. For rotation averaging and view graph calibration, the system reuses established components (Chatterjee et al. [14], Sweeney et al. [68]), positioning the contribution squarely in the global positioning step rather than in upstream or downstream stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving COLMAP-level accuracy at orders-of-magnitude lower runtime, GLOMAP makes high-quality SfM practical for large-scale and sequential datasets (handheld video, self-driving, AR) where incremental methods are prohibitively slow. The ablation revealing that relative translation constraints actively degrade performance challenges the field's assumption that more geometric constraints improve translation estimation, suggesting that the ill-posedness of scale-ambiguous translations is better handled by point-ray constraints alone. Remaining limitations include vulnerability to rotation averaging failures on symmetric scenes and dependence on traditional feature matching, which constrains performance under drastic appearance changes. The open-source release within the COLMAP ecosystem positions it as a likely default for downstream 3D reconstruction pipelines.
