## Problem

Existing diffusion-based text-to-motion methods require dozens to hundreds of sampling steps at inference (MDM ~12s, MLD ~0.2s, OmniControl ~81s per sequence), making real-time controllable motion generation infeasible. The paper addresses this by introducing latent consistency distillation into the motion generation domain for the first time, enabling one-step (or few-step) inference while preserving generation quality and spatial-temporal controllability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240426/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and an autoregressive control signal consisting of the last τ fraction (default 0.25) of frames from the previous motion, specified as global absolute 3D positions of K=6 control joints (Pelvis, Left/Right foot, Head, Left/Right wrist) in R^{K×3}. The output is a full 3D human motion sequence in the redundant representation (root velocity, root height, local joint positions/velocities/rotations in root space, and foot contact labels). At one-step inference, each motion sequence is generated in approximately 30 ms on a Tesla V100 GPU.

## Method

MotionLCM is distilled from the pre-trained motion latent diffusion model (MLD) via latent consistency distillation: a VAE encoder compresses raw motion into a latent vector, and a consistency function f_Θ is trained to map any noisy latent z_{n+k} directly to the clean latent z_0, with classifier-free guidance integrated into the distillation objective (DDIM solver, skipping interval k=20, Huber loss, EMA rate 0.95). For controllability, a motion ControlNet (initialized as a zero-initialized copy of MotionLCM) and a Trajectory Encoder (stacked transformer layers with a [CLS] token) are added; the encoded trajectory signal is injected into the noisy latent before the ControlNet processes it. A key design choice is decoding the predicted latent through the frozen VAE decoder back into motion space during training, providing an explicit joint-position control loss (L_control) that the latent-space reconstruction loss alone cannot supply. Training is two-stage: 96K iterations for consistency distillation, then 192K iterations for the ControlNet with a combined latent reconstruction loss and motion-space control loss (λ=1.0).

## Results

On HumanML3D, MotionLCM achieves real-time inference at ~30 ms per motion sequence (1-step), which is ~7.5× faster than MLD (225 ms) and ~825× faster than MDM (24.74 s), while matching or exceeding MLD's generation quality. With 2-step inference, MotionLCM attains the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.505) and MM Dist (2.986); with 4-step inference it achieves the best FID (0.304) among all compared methods. In the controllable motion task (Table 2), MotionLCM (2-step, LC&MC) reduces trajectory error to 0.196 and location error to 0.0143, outperforming OmniControl (0.3362 / 0.0322) by a large margin while being 1929× faster (47 ms vs. 81 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline MLD* (reproduced, 50-step DDIM), MotionLCM (1-step) is 7.5× faster (30 ms vs. 225 ms) with a slightly higher FID (0.467 vs. 0.450) but better R-Precision Top-3 (0.803 vs. 0.796); at 2-step, MotionLCM surpasses MLD* on FID (0.368 vs. 0.450) and R-Precision Top-1 (0.505 vs. 0.504) while remaining 6.4× faster. Compared to MDM and MotionDiffuse, MotionLCM is 2–3 orders of magnitude faster (30 ms vs. 24.74 s / 14.74 s) and achieves substantially better FID (0.467 vs. 0.544 / 0.630). In the controllable motion task, MotionLCM (2-step, LC&MC) outperforms OmniControl on every metric: FID 0.397 vs. 2.328, R-Precision Top-3 0.759 vs. 0.557, trajectory error 0.196 vs. 0.336, and location error 0.0143 vs. 0.0322, while being 1929× faster (47 ms vs. 81 s). Against MLD with the same control setup (LC&MC), MotionLCM achieves lower trajectory error (0.196 vs. 0.272) and location error (0.0143 vs. 0.0215) at 11.7× lower inference time, confirming that one-step latent generation provides a stronger training signal for the ControlNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionLCM directly extends the latent consistency model (LCM) framework from image generation [Luo et al., 2023] and the foundational consistency model [Song et al., ICML 2023] into the human motion generation domain, distilling from the motion latent diffusion model MLD [Chen et al., CVPR 2023]. For controllability, it adapts the ControlNet architecture [Zhang et al., ICCV 2023] to operate in the motion latent space, a non-trivial transfer since latent representations lack explicit motion semantics. The paper departs from prior controllable motion methods (e.g., OmniControl, MDM) that operate in the vanilla motion space by introducing a VAE-decoded control loss to provide explicit spatial-temporal supervision in the latent domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving ~30ms per-sequence inference (1929× faster than OmniControl) while matching or exceeding 50-step diffusion quality opens the door to real-time interactive applications such as live character animation, VR, and robotics. The key open question is whether the VAE-based latent space, which lacks explicit temporal modeling, can support more complex or longer-horizon control tasks; the authors acknowledge this limitation and point toward more explainable compression architectures. The work is also limited to a single dataset (HumanML3D) and a single base architecture (MLD), so generalizability to other motion representations or longer sequences remains untested.
