## Problem

CNNs apply uniform convolution kernels across all spatial regions, limiting their ability to handle spatially varying degradations, while transformers address local adaptiveness through self-attention at quadratic computational cost without explicitly modeling multiscale feature processing. The paper tackles the open question of how to efficiently achieve both local adaptiveness and multiscale feature processing in image restoration without the computational overhead of attention mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240490/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network accepts a single degraded RGB image (motion-blurred, noisy, or low-resolution) as input and produces a restored high-quality RGB image of the same spatial dimensions, or a 4× upscaled image for super-resolution. Training uses 256×256 patches with a combined L1 pixel-space and L1 frequency-space loss; evaluation is performed at resolutions up to 1280×720. The U-Net encoder-decoder structure processes features at four spatial scales, halving spatial size and doubling channels in the encoder, then reversing in the decoder with skip-connection fusion.

## Method

ECMA introduces a Local Adaptive Module (LAM) that performs local adaptive convolution: a standard convolution on a spatial context feature I′ produces a modulation scalar σ_{s,t} that scales a small (3×3) convolution kernel W applied to the input I, making the effective kernel spatially dependent without self-attention's quadratic cost. Three LAMs are cascaded in a Cascaded Multiscale Learning (CML) block with kernel sizes decreasing from large to small, where each subsequent LAM receives the output of the preceding one as additional spatial context, enabling coarse-to-fine multiscale feature refinement. Large-kernel convolutions in the first LAM are computed via FFT to reduce complexity from O(n²) to O(n log n). ECMA blocks are stacked in a U-Net architecture with fusion blocks that concatenate upsampled decoder features with encoder skip connections, and the entire network is trained end-to-end with AdamW and cosine annealing over 400K iterations.

## Results

On the GoPro deblurring benchmark, ECMA-B achieves 34.14 dB PSNR and 0.969 SSIM with only 63.0 GFLOPs, outperforming GRL-B (33.93 dB, 281 GFLOPs) by 0.21 dB while using 4.5× fewer FLOPs. The lightweight ECMA-T variant reaches 33.19 dB PSNR at just 15.9 GFLOPs, a 9.7× reduction over Uformer-B (89.5 GFLOPs). For 4× super-resolution, ECMA-SR attains 28.89 dB on Set14 and 26.82 dB on U100 with 50.5 GFLOPs, surpassing SRFormer (62.8 GFLOPs) on all five test sets while using 1.2× fewer FLOPs. On SIDD denoising, ECMA-S achieves 40.28 dB PSNR with 48.4 GFLOPs, matching NAFNet (40.30 dB) at 1.3× lower cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECMA-B outperforms the strongest transformer-based baseline GRL-B by 0.21 dB PSNR on GoPro while requiring 4.5× fewer FLOPs (63.0 vs. 281), and surpasses the CNN-based NAFNet by 0.45 dB at roughly 30% of its model size. The lightweight ECMA-T beats both Uformer-B and Stripformer in PSNR (33.19 vs. 33.06/33.08) at 9.7× lower computational cost. On HIDE, ECMA-B edges out Restormer-local by 0.10 dB PSNR with 2.2× fewer FLOPs; on RealBlur-J it leads Stripformer by 0.24 dB at 2.5× lower cost. For super-resolution, ECMA-SR surpasses SRFormer on all five benchmarks (e.g., +0.07 dB on Set14, +0.15 dB on U100) while using 1.2× fewer FLOPs. On SIDD denoising, ECMA-S is within 0.02 dB of NAFNet (40.28 vs. 40.30) at 1.3× lower cost, and ECMA-T matches UFormer's PSNR (39.86 vs. 39.85) at 5.6× fewer FLOPs. The only marginal loss is ECMA-S on SIDD trailing NAFNet by 0.02 dB, which is within noise and offset by the efficiency gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECMA carves new ground at the intersection of CNN efficiency and transformer-style local adaptiveness. It departs from the transformer line (Restormer, GRL, Uformer) by replacing quadratic-complexity self-attention with a lightweight local adaptive convolution that modulates a 3×3 kernel via a context map, and from the pure-CNN line (NAFNet, MPRNet) by introducing spatially varying kernels and explicit cascaded multiscale processing. The U-Net backbone and FFT-based large-kernel convolution are borrowed from prior restoration and signal-processing work, but the specific LAM formulation—equivalent to an element-wise product of two convolutions—and the large-to-small cascading order are the paper's architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.2×–9.7× FLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA PSNR/SSIM across deblurring, denoising, and super-resolution makes ECMA a practical option for deployment-constrained settings where transformer-based restorers are too expensive. The ablation confirming that large-to-small cascading outperforms both reversed order and independent multiscale branches by 0.3–0.4 dB provides a clear design principle for future multiscale CNN blocks. Open questions include whether the local adaptive convolution can replace attention in video or 3D restoration, whether the FFT trick generalizes to irregular kernel sizes, and whether the U-Net backbone is truly optimal versus other topologies. The method remains fundamentally CNN-based, so it may still lag transformers on tasks requiring very long-range global reasoning.
