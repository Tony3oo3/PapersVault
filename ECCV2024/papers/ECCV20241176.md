## Problem

Existing online lane graph construction methods model lanes at the pixel level (segmentation-then-vectorization) or the piece level (splitting at junctions with separate connectivity prediction), both of which fragment lane continuity and struggle at complex intersections. The paper argues that the continuous directed path — the actual driving trajectory — is the true primitive of the lane graph, and that modeling at this level naturally preserves continuity and encodes traffic flow for downstream planning.

## Input / Output

The method takes 6 (nuScenes) or 7 (Argoverse2) surrounding-camera RGB images covering 360° horizontal FOV, which are encoded into BEV features at 200×100 resolution (0.3 m grid) via a shared ResNet50 + GKT encoder. The network outputs a set of directed paths, each parameterized as a 30-point polyline or 5-point Bezier curve in normalized (0,1) coordinates; these paths are then discretized at 0.15 m intervals and merged via a Path2Graph algorithm into a directed lane graph whose vertices encode junction topology and whose edges encode direction and connectivity.

## Method

LaneGAP introduces a path-wise modeling paradigm with three components: (1) a Graph2Path algorithm that converts the ground-truth directed lane graph into root-to-leaf paths via DFS, providing training targets; (2) a single-stage query-based Transformer decoder (6 deformable attention layers) that simultaneously predicts all paths using learnable path queries, with deformable attention aggregating local BEV features along each path's spatial extent; and (3) a Path2Graph algorithm that discretizes predicted paths at 0.15 m, creates directed edges between successive points, and merges spatially overlapping vertices across paths to recover the full lane graph. Training uses a modified bipartite matching loss (Hungarian assignment) combining Focal classification loss, L1 path regression loss, and an auxiliary BEV segmentation branch. The key architectural advantage is that connectivity between lane segments is implicitly encoded in the continuous path representation, eliminating the need for a separate piece-association branch and avoiding the fragmentation that degrades pixel-wise and piece-wise methods at junctions.

## Results

On nuScenes, LaneGAP achieves the best directed TOPO F1 of 0.529 and Junction TOPO F1 of 0.564, outperforming pixel-wise HDMapNet (0.513 / 0.507) and piece-wise MapTR (0.465 / 0.496) while running at 15.6 FPS (2× faster than HDMapNet's 6.8 FPS). On Argoverse2, LaneGAP reaches TOPO F1 of 0.565 and Junction TOPO F1 of 0.616, again leading all baselines. On the OpenLane-V2 benchmark, a LaneGAP variant adapted from TopoNet achieves 40.6 mIoU, surpassing the state-of-the-art piece-wise TopoNet (39.0 mIoU) by 1.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaneGAP outperforms all four in-dataset baselines (HDMapNet, STSU, MapTR) on every TOPO and Junction TOPO F1 metric on both nuScenes and Argoverse2, with the largest margins over piece-wise STSU (e.g., +0.145 TOPO F1 on nuScenes) and consistent gains over the stronger piece-wise MapTR (+0.064 TOPO F1 on nuScenes, +0.065 on Argoverse2). Against pixel-wise HDMapNet, LaneGAP improves TOPO F1 by 0.016 on nuScenes and 0.030 on Argoverse2 while being roughly 2× faster in inference (15.6 vs. 6.8 FPS on nuScenes). On the OpenLane-V2 benchmark, the adapted LaneGAP† variant beats the state-of-the-art piece-wise TopoNet by 1.6 mIoU (40.6 vs. 39.0) and MapTR by 4.9 mIoU (40.6 vs. 35.7). In the downstream VAD planner experiment, LaneGAP reduces average L2 error to 0.70 m and collision rate to 0.29%, outperforming MapTR (0.74 m / 0.34%) and the VAD-Tiny baseline (0.78 m / 0.38%). The paper also shows that given ground-truth inputs, the Path2Graph post-processing achieves perfect (1.000) TOPO F1, whereas Pixel2Graph drops to 0.788, confirming that the path representation is inherently more robust to topology recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pixel-wise (HDMapNet) and piece-wise (STSU, MapTR, TopoNet) paradigms for online lane graph construction by proposing path-wise modeling, where continuous directed paths are treated as the primitive unit rather than pixels or junction-split fragments. It builds directly on DETR's bipartite matching for set prediction and Deformable DETR's deformable attention for feature aggregation along path geometry, while adapting the TOPO metric from aerial lane graph extraction to the ego-centric online setting. The key conceptual shift is that connectivity information is encoded implicitly within each continuous path rather than predicted as a separate inter-piece association matrix, eliminating the need for a dedicated connectivity branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Path-wise modeling addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving: trajectory planners need continuous, direction-aware paths rather than disconnected lane pieces, and the paper demonstrates this through improved VAD planner performance (lower L2 error and collision rate). The introduction of the Junction TOPO metric fills an evaluation gap by specifically measuring local topology quality around merging/fork points, where prior metrics were insensitive. Open questions include whether path-wise modeling extends naturally to 3D lane graphs (the paper omits height for fair comparison), how it scales to very dense urban intersections with many overlapping paths, and whether the Graph2Path decomposition is optimal for all topologies or whether alternative path decompositions could further improve performance.
