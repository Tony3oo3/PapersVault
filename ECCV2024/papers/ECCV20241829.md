## Problem

Video action recognition models learn scene-biased representations due to spurious action-scene correlations in training data, causing failures on unseen action-scene combinations. Existing scene-debiasing methods mitigate this by discarding scene information entirely, but this overlooks potentially valuable scene context. No prior method learns both high-quality action and scene representations in a disentangled manner from a single model.

## Input / Output

The model takes 16-frame video clips resized to 224×224 pixels per frame, encoded by a ViT backbone pretrained with VideoMAE on the target dataset. During training, it additionally consumes pseudo-human masks (from a non-learning foreground extractor) and pseudo scene labels (from a frozen Places365 ViT when ground-truth scene labels are unavailable). At inference, only the disentangling encoder runs, producing two disentangled slots (action and scene) that feed a shared linear classifier to yield action and scene class predictions.

## Method

DEVIAS is an encoder-decoder architecture comprising a Disentangling Encoder (DE), an Action Mask Decoder (AMD), and a shared prediction head. The DE employs slot attention with K learnable slots whose attention coefficients are softmax-normalized along the slot axis (rather than the conventional key axis), fostering competitive learning so slots progressively encode distinct information. A Hungarian-algorithm-based K-to-2 matching assigns slots to action and scene roles, supervised by respective labels. The AMD is a lightweight 3-layer MLP that reconstructs action masks from the action slot plus a pseudo-human mask; because slots are complementary in slot attention, forcing one slot to encode action via the AMD implicitly forces the other to encode scene. The total training loss combines the classification loss, the mask reconstruction loss, an attention-guidance L² loss between the action slot's attention map and pseudo-human masks, and a cosine-similarity loss to diversify slots.

## Results

On UCF-101, DEVIAS achieves a harmonic mean (H.M.) of 61.1% across seen/unseen action and scene recognition, surpassing the second-best method (Two-Token w/FAME, 44.7%) by 16.4 points. On Kinetics-400, DEVIAS reaches 70.8% H.M. (vs. 69.2% for Two-Token w/FAME), and on the more realistic HVU dataset it achieves 57.2% H.M. (vs. 54.8%). In downstream transfer learning (pre-trained on Kinetics-400, fine-tuned on Diving48, SSV2, UCF-101, ActivityNet), DEVIAS attains 84.8% H.M., outperforming all single-task and multi-task baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEVIAS is compared against two scene-debiasing methods (BE [58] and FAME [14]) and multi-task baselines (One-Token, Two-Token, and their debiased variants). On UCF-101, DEVIAS dramatically outperforms Two-Token w/FAME (61.1% vs. 44.7% H.M., +16.4 pts) and Two-Token w/BE (61.1% vs. 40.1%, +21.0 pts), primarily due to a large gap in unseen action recognition (39.4% vs. 20.3% and 16.8% respectively) while maintaining comparable scene accuracy. Against the single-task debiasing baselines (BE, FAME), DEVIAS achieves higher unseen action accuracy (39.4% vs. 25.4% and 20.2%) while also providing scene predictions they cannot. On Kinetics-400 and HVU, the margins narrow to 1.6 and 2.4 H.M. points over Two-Token w/FAME, respectively. In downstream transfer, DEVIAS edges out BE (84.8% vs. 84.0% H.M.) and FAME (84.8% vs. 83.4%), with the largest gains on the temporal-biased Diving48 dataset (84.4% vs. 81.9% for BE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the scene-debiasing line of work (BE, FAME) that removes scene information entirely to improve action recognition, arguing instead that discarding scene context loses valuable information. It repurposes slot attention—originally developed for object-centric learning in image generation—to learn disentangled action and scene representations in video, a novel application of the mechanism. The architecture also draws on reconstruction-based representation learning (masked autoencoders) through the action mask decoder, and on Hungarian matching for slot-to-role assignment. Unlike prior disentangled video representation works that focus on motion/appearance or foreground/background, DEVIAS is the first to jointly learn high-quality representations for both action and scene in a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEVIAS provides a practical, flexible framework where practitioners can adjust the emphasis on action versus scene depending on the downstream task (temporal-biased vs. scene-biased datasets), which prior scene-debiasing methods cannot offer. The k-NN sanity check providing clean evidence of true disentanglement (near-random reverse-feature performance) is methodologically valuable for the broader disentangled representation learning community. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than two attributes (e.g., action, scene, object, temporal context), whether the reliance on pseudo-human masks and frozen scene models limits performance in domains where these priors are unreliable, and whether the slot attention mechanism can handle more complex, multi-object scenes.
