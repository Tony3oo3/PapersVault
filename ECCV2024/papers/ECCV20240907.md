## Problem

Monocular 6D pose estimation suffers from inherent scale ambiguity, while RGB-D methods fail on specular and transparent objects where depth sensors produce missing or inaccurate measurements. Existing stereo-based category-level methods rely on two-stage pipelines (detection followed by pose estimation) that accumulate errors and discard image context outside bounding boxes, and none jointly address shape reconstruction. CODERS targets the gap of a single end-to-end stereo framework that concurrently estimates detection, 6D pose, and 3D shape for everyday objects with diverse material properties.

## Input / Output

The method takes a calibrated stereo image pair (left and right views) as input and processes the full image without requiring pre-cropped object regions, unlike two-stage methods that rely on 2D bounding boxes. Outputs include per-object category labels, 6D poses (rotation encoded as a 6D vector to avoid discontinuity, plus 3D translation and size), and implicit shape embeddings (SDF codes) that can be queried at arbitrary 3D coordinates to reconstruct a mesh. The shape output is category-level: a single learned SDF decoder maps per-object embeddings to continuous distance fields, enabling zero-shot reconstruction of unseen instances within a trained category.

## Method

The core contribution is an Implicit Stereo Matching module that discretizes the camera frustum into 3D meshgrids, back-projects them to global 3D coordinates using calibrated stereo parameters, and fuses 2D ConvNeXt-FPN features with 3D position encodings via a coordinate transformer (MLP), producing stereo-aware 3D features that implicitly encode depth without explicit disparity computation. A DETR-style transformer decoder with L=6 layers and learned object queries then cross-attends to these stereo-aware features to generate object embeddings. Three parallel heads (MLP-based) decode category (focal loss), 6D pose (L1 loss with 6D rotation representation from GDR-Net), and shape embedding (L1 loss) from the same object embeddings, enabling end-to-end multi-task training. The shape encoder is trained with a supervised contrastive loss that maximizes dissimilarity between shape embeddings of different categories in the SDF latent space, improving category-level generalization. The entire pipeline is single-stage and end-to-end, avoiding error accumulation between detection and pose/shape estimation stages.

## Results

On the TOD dataset, CODERS achieves 99.5% 3D₅₀ and 100% 3D₂₅ on the Bottle category, a dramatic improvement over the previous SOTA stereo method StereoPose (22.2% 3D₅₀). On the Mug category, CODERS reaches 100% 3D₅₀ and 92.8% 3D₇₅, compared to StereoPose's 77.4% and no reported 3D₇₅. For shape reconstruction, CODERS achieves Chamfer distances of 0.542 (bottle), 0.352 (bowl), 0.593 (cup), 0.211 (knife), and 0.458 (soup), outperforming zero-shot methods Zero123 and TripoSR on all five objects and matching or exceeding the instance-level StereoPoints on three of five.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODERS decisively outperforms all pose estimation baselines on the TOD dataset. Against the strongest stereo competitor StereoPose [4], CODERS improves Bottle 3D₅₀ from 22.2% to 99.5% and Mug 3D₅₀ from 77.4% to 100%, while also reporting metrics (3D₇₅, 5°2cm) that StereoPose does not. RGB-D methods SPD [41] and SGPA [3] are far behind, with Bottle 3D₅₀ scores of only 7.4% and 9.6% respectively, confirming the depth-sensor limitations on transparent and specular objects. KeyPose [27], a keypoint-based stereo method, reports only 10°5cm/10°10cm metrics (52.7%/62.3% on Bottle), which CODERS exceeds at 99.8%. For shape reconstruction, CODERS beats zero-shot methods Zero123 [26] and TripoSR [42] on all five test objects (e.g., bottle CD 0.542 vs 1.266 and 1.478). Against the instance-level StereoPoints [48], CODERS wins on bottle (0.542 vs 0.700), bowl (0.352 vs 0.509), and soup (0.458 vs 0.695) but trails on cup (0.593 vs 0.563) and knife (0.211 vs 0.140), which is expected since StereoPoints is fine-tuned on the same objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

CODERS unifies three previously separate research threads—DETR-style transformer detection, PETR-inspired 3D position encoding, and DeepSDF-based implicit shape representation—into a single end-to-end pipeline driven by stereo image pairs. It departs from prior stereo category-level pose methods (StereoPose, KeyPose) that rely on two-stage detect-then-estimate pipelines and only output poses, by jointly predicting detections, 6D poses, and 3D shapes in one forward pass. The key architectural departure is the Implicit Stereo Matching module, which encodes triangulated 3D coordinates into image features via a coordinate transformer rather than computing explicit disparity, and the use of a contrastive SDF shape encoder for category-level reconstruction without shape priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in robot manipulation: RGB-D sensors fail on specular and transparent surfaces, while monocular methods suffer from scale ambiguity, leaving stereo as the most robust sensing modality for everyday objects. The 99.5% 3D50 on bottles (vs. 22.2% for the prior SOTA) demonstrates that implicit stereo feature alignment can resolve the depth ambiguity that has limited prior approaches, though the result is validated on a small 12-instance benchmark. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger category sets with high intra-class size variation (the SS3D results show degradation on large boxes), whether the 3 Hz inference speed can be improved for real-time manipulation, and whether the implicit stereo matching strategy transfers to multi-view or dynamic settings.
