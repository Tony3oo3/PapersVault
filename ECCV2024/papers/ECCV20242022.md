## Problem

Zero-shot text-to-video editing methods rely on DDIM inversion to obtain temporally coherent initial latents, but the naive inversion process accumulates errors across timesteps, causing unfaithful spatial textures and significant flickering in the edited output. Existing remedies such as attention fusion or shared unconditional embeddings reduce semantic drift but do not directly address the per-timestep error accumulation that propagates through the inversion trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242022/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (384×672, 24 frames) and a free-form text prompt as input, and produces a temporally consistent edited video of the same length. Keyframes are sampled at a fixed interval (every 4 or 8 frames) for joint editing, and the full video is reconstructed by propagating edited keyframes to intermediate frames via Ebsynth patch-matching. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, requiring no training or fine-tuning on video data, and runs on a single A100 GPU using a pretrained Stable Diffusion backbone with PnP-Diffusion for frame-level editing and ControlNet (edge condition) for structural guidance.

## Method

WAVE introduces a flow-guided feature warping strategy that uses optical flow extracted from the source video to propagate latent features of a reference keyframe to subsequent frames within the self-attention and residual blocks of the diffusion U-Net, constraining Q, K, and V tokens to maintain temporal coherence. To prevent error accumulation from fixed-order warping, the DDIM inversion stage randomly shuffles keyframes at each timestep before applying the warping, effectively averaging out per-frame errors. During the joint editing stage, an extended-attention mechanism concatenates the keys and values of the first keyframe with those of each frame, so every keyframe queries both itself and the reference frame for a unified global appearance. An occlusion mask derived from forward-backward flow consistency and a residual map determines which regions are warped versus regenerated, and the strategy is applied symmetrically in both inversion and denoising for a unified framework.

## Results

On 42 DAVIS videos (84 text-video pairs), WAVE achieves the lowest warp error (8.94×10⁻³) among all compared methods, indicating the best temporal consistency. It also attains a CLIP score of 24.44, second only to Tune-A-Video (25.36) but with a warp error 3.6× lower. Compared to the closest zero-shot baselines, WAVE improves warp error by 1.83×10⁻³ over TokenFlow and 0.38×10⁻³ over FateZero, while boosting CLIP score by 1.26 and 1.47 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WAVE outperforms both FateZero and TokenFlow on both metrics: it achieves a higher CLIP score (24.44 vs. 22.97 and 23.18) and a lower warp error (8.94 vs. 9.32 and 10.77), confirming superior temporal consistency and prompt alignment. Against Tune-A-Video, WAVE trades a 0.92-point CLIP score deficit for a dramatic 23.39×10⁻³ reduction in warp error (8.94 vs. 32.33); the authors note that Tune-A-Video's higher CLIP score comes at the cost of generating frames unfaithful to the original video content. The ablation rows further show that the flow-guided warping strategy contributes 0.11×10⁻³ warp-error reduction and 0.84 CLIP points over the no-warping variant, while the randomly shuffled DDIM inversion adds another 0.11 warp-error improvement over naive DDIM inversion within the same framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

WAVE builds directly on the zero-shot text-to-video editing pipeline established by Tune-A-Video, FateZero, and TokenFlow, which all rely on DDIM inversion to obtain structural guidance and cross-frame attention to enforce temporal consistency. The key departure is the introduction of a flow-guided feature warping strategy applied during the DDIM inversion itself (not just during denoising), motivated by the observation that naive inversion accumulates per-timestep errors that manifest as flickering. The random shuffle of keyframes at each timestep is a practical trick to prevent the warping operation from introducing its own directional bias, and the extended-attention mechanism (concatenating keys/values with a reference frame) extends the TokenFlow-style cross-frame attention with an explicit anchor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underappreciated bottleneck in zero-shot video editing—error accumulation during DDIM inversion—by showing that inter-frame feature propagation via optical flow can substantially reduce warp error (from 10.77 to 8.94 vs. TokenFlow) without any training. However, the authors acknowledge that the method's strong structural preservation may impede large structural changes, and that the pipeline's quality is ultimately bounded by the underlying image editing model (PnP-Diffusion) and the LDM decoder's high-frequency artifacts. Open questions include whether the warping strategy generalizes to longer videos or more dramatic edits, and whether the random shuffle can be replaced by a principled ordering that avoids the heuristic nature of the current approach.
