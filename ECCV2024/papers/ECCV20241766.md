## Problem

Existing physical adversarial attacks on object detectors (patches, textures, optical manipulations) are applied directly on the object surface, making them conspicuous and impractical for covert real-world deployment. This paper asks whether an adversarial trigger placed entirely outside the target object's bounding box can still reliably cause the detector to miss that object, a setting that is substantially harder because the trigger must exploit only peripheral, low-information image regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241766/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a white-box object detector (e.g., YOLOv3/YOLOv5) and a dataset of images containing the target class (stop signs), and produces a single universal adversarial trigger of fixed spatial dimensions U×V×3 with pixel values in [0,1]. The trigger is rendered onto each image via a differentiable affine transform (scale, rotate, translate) into a region C(B) that is guaranteed not to overlap the object's bounding box, and its placement shifts with the object's position. A total-variation regularizer enforces spatial smoothness so the trigger can be physically printed and deployed.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part optimization framework. First, a Feature Guidance (FG) loss term minimizes the L2 distance between the Neck-module feature map of the triggered image and that of a masked image (object region replaced by a gray rectangle), steering intermediate representations toward the 'object-absent' state before the Head makes predictions. Second, a Universal Auto-PGD (UAPGD) optimizer monitors the loss trajectory over sliding observation windows and halves the PGD step size when both the running minimum loss and the loss variance fail to decrease over a comparison window, preventing violent oscillation around local optima during multi-term universal optimization. The full objective combines a detection-confidence loss (L_det), the FG loss, and a TV smoothness loss, optimized with EOT-style random augmentations (noise, contrast, brightness, rotation) for physical robustness.

## Results

On the COCO dataset (1100 stop-sign images), the best ensemble configuration (UAPGD + Feature Guidance, step size 8/255) achieves an Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 69.33% on the training set and 66.60% on the test set for YOLOv3, and 62.67%/58.60% for YOLOv5. In the CARLA simulator, the ensemble method causes the detector to miss the stop sign for 58.89% of the 9-second driving video, compared to 36.67% for vanilla PGD. Real-world experiments confirm that the printed trigger maintains high attack effectiveness as the vehicle approaches the stop sign, with the detector confidence dropping to near zero at close range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Dpatch2 (Lee & Kolter, 2019), the closest prior work that also places adversarial perturbations outside the object bounding box. The proposed Ensemble method (UAPGD + Feature Guidance) outperforms Dpatch2 by a large margin: on YOLOv3 test, ASR rises from 24.00% (Dpatch2) to 66.60% (Ensemble), a 42.6-point improvement; on YOLOv5 test, the gap is even wider, from 6.67% to 58.60%. Even the modified Dpatch2(L_det), which adopts the paper's detection-confidence loss instead of the original model-loss maximization, only reaches 35.80% (YOLOv3) and 32.00% (YOLOv5), still well below the Ensemble result. Within the paper's own ablations, UAPGD consistently surpasses vanilla PGD by 1–3 ASR points at the same step size, and adding Feature Guidance on top of UAPGD yields a further 1–4 point gain, confirming that both components contribute meaningfully over the Dpatch2 baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physical adversarial attack literature on object detectors by shifting the perturbation location from the object surface (as in adversarial patches, camouflage textures, and optical attacks) to an inconspicuous region outside the bounding box. Methodologically, it builds directly on Auto-PGD (Croce & Hein, 2020) by adapting its step-size decay logic to the universal-attack setting (UAPGD), and introduces a Feature Guidance loss that aligns intermediate feature maps with those of an object-removed image. The closest prior work is Dpatch/Dpatch2, which also targets regions outside the object, but the paper positions its contribution as independent, emphasizing the FG mechanism and the UAPGD optimization strategy as distinct technical advances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stealth property of out-of-box triggers has direct safety implications for autonomous driving: a trigger placed below a stop sign is far less likely to be noticed by human observers or flagged by anomaly detection than a patch covering the sign itself. However, the attack success rate plateaus around 65–67% on COCO and degrades sharply beyond ±50° viewing angles in the real world, leaving open questions about scalability to diverse object classes, robustness against detector retraining or feature-level defenses, and whether the FG mechanism generalizes to transformers-based detectors. The paper also acknowledges that the approach is limited to white-box trigger generation (with only brief black-box results in the appendix), and defense strategies against this specific spatial attack pattern remain unexplored.
