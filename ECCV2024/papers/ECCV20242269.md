## Problem

Semi-supervised learning methods degrade significantly when only 1–2 labeled samples per class are available, because the model's own pseudo-labels become unreliable and confirmation bias causes the model to repeatedly learn from incorrect labels. Existing approaches that attempt to improve pseudo-label accuracy still rely heavily on the model's own predictions, which are insufficiently grounded when labeled data cannot represent the data distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242269/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a very small set of labeled RGB images (as few as 1 per class), a large pool of unlabeled images, and a separately pre-trained SimCLR model (ResNet-50) that has been trained on the unlabeled data. The self-supervised model is not fine-tuned on labeled data; instead, self-pseudo-labels are generated by computing cosine similarity between its feature representations of unlabeled and labeled samples and assigning each unlabeled sample the label of its nearest labeled neighbor. The semi-supervised classifier (Wide ResNet-28-2 for CIFAR/SVHN/Tiny-ImageNet, ResNet-50 for ImageNet) outputs standard class probabilities.

## Method

ExMatch operates in two decoupled phases. In Phase 1, a SimCLR contrastive model is trained on unlabeled data, and self-pseudo-labels are obtained by nearest-neighbor assignment in the self-supervised feature space (cosine similarity to the few labeled samples). In Phase 2, the semi-supervised model is trained with three losses: a standard FixMatch supervised loss, a FixMatch unsupervised loss (strong augmentation, confidence-thresholded pseudo-labels), and a novel self-guided exploitation loss. The exploitation loss selects unlabeled samples whose semi-supervised pseudo-label agrees with the self-pseudo-label AND whose confidence exceeds a threshold τ_ex, then retrains the model on weakly-augmented versions of those samples. This retraining mechanism anchors the model to the distribution captured by the self-supervised features, resisting confirmation bias without directly using the (often noisy) self-pseudo-labels as supervision. The decoupling of the two models ensures the self-supervised signal provides an independent perspective rather than collapsing into the semi-supervised model's own (unreliable) predictions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 10 labels per class, ExMatch achieves 72.97% accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (FlatMatch, 69.28%) by 3.7%. On CIFAR-100 with 100 labels, ExMatch reaches 32.49%, a 21.1% improvement over the best baseline (PR, 26.83%). On high-resolution datasets, ExMatch improves over FixMatch by 41.8% on STL-10 (54.01% vs 38.09%), 22.9% on Tiny-ImageNet (33.77% vs 27.48%), and 20.8% on ImageNet with 10k labels (25.94% vs 21.47% Top-1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExMatch achieves the best accuracy on CIFAR-10 with 10 labels (72.97% vs. FlatMatch 69.28%), CIFAR-100 with 100/200/400 labels (32.49%/43.71%/51.13% vs. PR 26.83%/36.22%/47.75%), and SVHN with 10/20/40 labels (45.25%/82.07%/92.97% vs. SelfMatch 43.07%/73.79%/90.52%). However, on CIFAR-10 with 20 and 40 labels, ExMatch (84.66%, 89.78%) underperforms FlexMatch (94.62%, 94.86%) and FreeMatch (91.66%, 94.88%), suggesting the self-guided exploitation is most beneficial in the most label-scarce regime. Self-supervised baselines (SelfMatch, CoMatch, DoubleMatch) show similar or lower performance than FixMatch on CIFAR-10/100, indicating that simply combining self-supervised and semi-supervised learning is insufficient without the proposed exploitation mechanism. On high-resolution datasets (STL-10, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet), ExMatch outperforms FixMatch by 41.8%, 22.9%, and 20.8% respectively, confirming scalability. The paper notes that all baselines were re-evaluated on identical labeled sample folds to ensure fair comparison, as performance varies significantly with label selection in scarce-label settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExMatch builds directly on FixMatch's confidence-thresholded pseudo-labeling framework and introduces a third loss term (self-guided exploitation) that leverages a separately trained SimCLR model to generate distribution-aware self-pseudo-labels. It departs from prior joint self-supervised/semi-supervised approaches (SelfMatch, CoMatch, DoubleMatch) by fully decoupling the two models, using the self-supervised model only as a source of distribution information rather than a shared backbone or pre-training stage. The method also extends the line of work addressing confirmation bias in label-scarce settings (Propagation Regularizer, FlexMatch, FreeMatch, FlatMatch), but instead of refining pseudo-label quality through entropy regularization or curriculum scheduling, it uses cross-model consistency as a selection criterion for additional retraining on weakly-augmented samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper targets a practically important regime—1 to 10 labels per class—where most semi-supervised methods degrade sharply, and demonstrates that a simple decoupling of self-supervised feature learning from semi-supervised classification can yield large gains (up to 21% on CIFAR-100). The decoupled design also offers practical flexibility: the self-supervised model can be pre-trained on a different dataset (ImageNet-32) or use a different architecture, reducing training cost. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-contrastive self-supervised objectives, whether the exploitation mechanism generalizes to other modalities (text, audio), and whether the additional self-supervised training cost is justified in deployment settings where compute is constrained.
