## Problem

Existing image watermarking methods, even those trained with adversarial robustness, remain vulnerable to strong removal and forgery attacks that add small perturbations to images. No prior method provides a certified guarantee that no attack within a bounded ℓ2 perturbation radius can remove or forge a watermark, leaving a critical gap between empirical robustness and provable security.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242071/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (standardized to 128×128) and a ground-truth watermark bitstring of m=30 bits, and produces a smoothed bitwise accuracy (BA) score along with certified lower and upper bounds on BA under any ℓ2-bounded perturbation of radius R. The smoothed decoder consumes N=10,000 Gaussian-noised copies of the input image at inference time, making it a black-box wrapper around any existing decoder. Detection is binary: the image is flagged as watermarked if the smoothed BA meets or exceeds a threshold τ (default 0.83).

## Method

The core contribution is extending randomized smoothing to watermarking by treating the base decoder D as a model to be smoothed with isotropic Gaussian noise, yielding three variants: multi-class smoothing (per-bit binary classification), multi-label smoothing (watermark as a multi-label set), and regression smoothing (BA as a continuous regression target). For each variant, the paper derives closed-form lower and upper bounds on BA under any ℓ2-bounded perturbation, providing certified robustness against all removal and forgery attacks simultaneously. Regression smoothing outperforms the other two because it aggregates across all bits and thus captures inter-bit correlations that per-bit or per-label approaches miss. Certified bounds are estimated via order statistics of N noisy BA samples (for regression) or Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals with Bonferroni correction (for multi-class/multi-label), yielding probabilistic guarantees at confidence level 1−α. The framework is model-agnostic: it wraps any existing encoder-decoder pair (demonstrated on HiDDeN), and optionally improves certified robustness by inserting a Gaussian noise layer between encoder and decoder during adversarial training.

## Results

On the Stable Diffusion dataset (m=30 bits, τ=0.83, σ=0.1, N=10,000), regression smoothing based watermarking achieves the lowest certified false negative rate (CFNR) and certified false positive rate (CFPR) among the three smoothing variants, outperforming multi-class and multi-label smoothing. Under JPEG compression as a removal attack (quality factor Q from 99 to 20), the smoothed method maintains FNR close to 0 while the base HiDDeN method's FNR increases to 0.25. Adversarial training of the base encoder/decoder further reduces CFNR substantially and slightly reduces CFPR compared to standard training, while maintaining comparable visual quality (SSIM 0.941 vs. 0.943). Under black-box attacks, the smoothed method requires larger perturbation norms to achieve the same FNR/FPR, indicating greater empirical robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is unsmoothed HiDDeN [41] (ECCV 2018), evaluated under four removal/forgery attacks: JPEG compression, black-box, white-box, and adaptive white-box. The smoothed HiDDeN (regression smoothing + adversarial training) consistently outperforms the base method across all four attacks. Under JPEG compression, the smoothed method achieves FNR near 0 across all quality factors while the base method degrades to FNR=0.25 at Q=20. Under black-box attacks, the smoothed method requires a larger query budget (i.e., larger perturbation) to reach FNR=1, demonstrating greater resistance. Under white-box and adaptive white-box attacks, the smoothed method achieves smaller FNR and FPR than the base method once the perturbation size R exceeds a threshold, while both methods perform similarly (FNR/FPR near 0) at small R. Among the three smoothing variants, regression smoothing dominates multi-class and multi-label smoothing on both CFNR and CFPR, attributed to its ability to capture inter-bit correlations through the aggregated bitwise accuracy regression target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends randomized smoothing—a technique well-established for certifiably robust classification (Cohen et al. 2019) and regression (Chiang et al. 2020)—to the fundamentally different problem of watermark detection, where the output is a bitstring rather than a class label or scalar. It builds directly on HiDDeN (Zhu et al. 2018) as the base encoder-decoder pair and adapts three distinct smoothing formulations (multi-class, multi-label, regression) to the watermarking setting, each requiring new theoretical guarantees because bitwise accuracy couples all bits in a way that standard per-class or per-label smoothing does not. The work departs from prior robust watermarking efforts that relied solely on adversarial training (which provides no worst-case guarantees) by showing that distributional smoothing of the decoder yields provable ℓ₂-bounded robustness against arbitrary removal and forgery attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As watermarking is now deployed by major AI companies and mandated by the White House Executive Order, the first certified robustness guarantees for image watermarks provide a principled, attack-agnostic safety net that adversarial training alone cannot offer. The finding that regression smoothing (treating bitwise accuracy as a single regression target) outperforms the more natural per-bit multi-class approach is a useful design insight: it shows that inter-bit correlations matter and that the detection metric should be smoothed holistically. Limitations include the relatively small certified ℓ₂ radius, evaluation only at 128×128 resolution, and reliance on a single base method (HiDDeN); extending the framework to higher resolutions, other base watermarks (e.g., Stable Signature), and to audio/text/video modalities remains open.
