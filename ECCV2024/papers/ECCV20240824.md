## Problem

Industrial defect inspection systems must handle diverse and evolving product portfolios, yet existing approaches either train separate models per object (costly and inflexible), use unified models that cannot adapt to new objects, or rely on memory banks that suffer from capacity limits and cross-object distribution conflicts. No prior method integrates object-incremental learning into a unified reconstruction-based detector while avoiding explicit feature storage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240824/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images of industrial products (MVTec-AD: 15 categories; VisA: 12 categories) and is trained exclusively on normal (defect-free) samples in an unsupervised manner. Objects are introduced incrementally in steps (e.g., 10 base objects followed by 1 new object per step), with no access to prior-step training data during updates. The output is a reconstruction-error map that serves as a pixel-level defect heatmap, from which an image-level anomaly score is also derived, enabling both localization and classification of defects.

## Method

IUF builds on a transformer-based autoencoder (UniAD) and introduces three mechanisms to combat catastrophic forgetting during object-incremental learning. First, Object-Aware Self-Attention (OASA) injects a category-conditioned semantic vector—produced by a discriminator trained with cross-entropy loss—into the self-attention query of the reconstruction network, explicitly delineating per-object semantic boundaries. Second, Semantic Compression Loss (SCL) performs SVD on spatially-averaged latent features and penalizes the sum of the smallest singular values, compacting non-primary channels to free capacity for new objects. Third, a constrained update strategy projects gradients into the channel eigenspace of previously learned objects (via V_old^T), applies a log-based mask that zeros out updates in the most semantically important channels, projects back to the original space, and adds a β-weighted copy of old parameters to reinforce retention.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, IUF achieves the best image-level ACC of 96.0% and pixel-level ACC of 96.3% in the 14-1 (1-step) setting, with the lowest forgetting measure (FM) of 1.0/0.6 (image/pixel). In the most challenging 10-1 (5-step) incremental protocol, IUF reaches 94.2/95.1 ACC with FM of 3.2/1.0, substantially outperforming the next-best UniAD+EWC (89.6/93.8, FM 5.4/3.6). On VisA, IUF attains 87.3/97.6 ACC (1-step) and 79.8/95.0 ACC (4-step), again leading all baselines on both image and pixel metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IUF outperforms all 11 baselines on both image-level and pixel-level ACC across every task protocol on both MVTec-AD and VisA. The closest competitor is UniAD+EWC, which reaches 92.8/95.4 ACC in the 14-1 setting versus IUF's 96.0/96.3, and 89.6/93.8 in the 5-step setting versus IUF's 94.2/95.1 — a gap of roughly 3–5 points that widens with more incremental steps. CAD-based methods (CAD+DNE, CAD+CutPaste, CAD+PANDA) report only image-level scores (pixel-level marked NA) and trail IUF by 8–15 ACC points in multi-step protocols, e.g., CAD+DNE scores 77.7 image-ACC in the 10-1 (5-step) setting versus IUF's 94.2. Standalone anomaly detectors (PaDiM, PatchCore, DRAEM) suffer severe forgetting (FM > 15 in most settings) and are 10–30 points below IUF on ACC. Notably, IUF also achieves the lowest forgetting measure in the 14-1 and 10-1 (5-step) settings (FM 1.0/0.6 and 3.2/1.0), confirming that the OASA + SCL + updating strategy effectively suppresses catastrophic forgetting without sacrificing new-object performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified reconstruction-based anomaly detection paradigm (UniAD) by integrating object-incremental learning, addressing a gap where prior unified models could not adapt to new object categories without retraining. It departs from memory-bank-based incremental approaches (CAD, UCAD) that store reference features and suffer from capacity limits and cross-object distribution conflicts, instead eliminating explicit feature storage entirely. The three proposed mechanisms—Object-Aware Self-Attention for semantic boundary delineation, SVD-based Semantic Compression Loss for feature space compaction, and a log-gated weight projection for protecting prior semantics—collectively replace the memory bank with implicit, in-network regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete industrial pain point: production lines that rotate through diverse product variants require inspection systems that can absorb new objects without retraining from scratch or deploying new hardware. A key practical advantage is that, unlike CAD-based methods, IUF retains pixel-level localization capability, which is essential for downstream quality control. Open questions include scalability to dozens or hundreds of incremental objects, robustness when new objects share strong visual similarity with prior ones, and whether the SVD-based channel importance heuristic generalizes beyond the two benchmark datasets tested. The task protocol (one object per step) is also relatively simple compared to real-world scenarios where multiple objects may arrive simultaneously.
