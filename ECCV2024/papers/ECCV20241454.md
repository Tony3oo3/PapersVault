## Problem

Urban scene reconstruction methods trained on forward-facing driving cameras produce high-quality renderings on interpolative test views but degrade significantly when asked to synthesize views outside the training camera distribution (e.g., looking left, right, or downward). No prior work has systematically identified or addressed this Extrapolated View Synthesis (EVS) gap in urban scene reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241454/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of forward-facing RGB images, a corresponding LiDAR point-cloud sequence, and 3D bounding box detections from an off-the-shelf estimator, plus a text prompt for the diffusion model. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation comprising a static model and per-instance dynamic object models in canonical bounding-box coordinates, renderable in real-time at ~108 FPS. EVS test cameras are constructed by rotating conventional test cameras ±60° around the world Z-axis (left/right) or 10° around the camera x-axis with a 1.0-unit upward translation (downward), and the left/right views are cropped to the half-plane closer to the training trajectory to avoid unobserved regions.

## Method

VEGS builds a dynamic scene in 3D Gaussian Splatting initialized from a dense multi-frame LiDAR map (rather than sparse per-frame scans), with dynamic objects parameterized in canonical bounding-box space and jointly optimized via learnable per-frame box transformations regularized toward identity. To counter the 'lazy covariance optimization' problem—where Gaussians over-fit their shape to the training view frustum—the paper introduces a covariance rendering technique using slerp-based quaternion blending to produce orientation and scale maps, which are supervised by off-the-shelf surface normal estimates via axis-alignment and normal-aligned scale-minimization losses. Finally, a Stable Diffusion model fine-tuned with LoRA on the training images provides scene-specific yet generalizable visual priors, distilled into EVS renderings through denoising score matching, directly guiding the model on unobserved views without ground-truth supervision.

## Results

On KITTI-360, VEGS achieves the best EVS quality with FID 124.4 and KID 0.0561, outperforming MARS (FID 131.1, KID 0.0617) and all other baselines. On conventional test cameras, VEGS is competitive with MARS (PSNR 23.71 vs. 23.13, LPIPS 0.106 vs. 0.125) while rendering at 108 FPS, orders of magnitude faster than NeRF-based methods (0.08–0.17 FPS). For dynamic object reconstruction (PSNR*), VEGS scores 22.44, slightly ahead of MARS (21.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MARS [41], the strongest NeRF-based baseline, VEGS wins on all EVS metrics (FID 124.4 vs. 131.1; KID 0.0561 vs. 0.0617) and on PSNR (23.71 vs. 23.13) and LPIPS (0.106 vs. 0.125) for conventional test cameras, while MARS edges out slightly on SSIM (0.814 vs. 0.812). VEGS also surpasses MARS on dynamic object PSNR* (22.44 vs. 21.98). Compared to vanilla 3DGS [18], VEGS improves FID by 41% (124.4 vs. 211.8) and KID by 60% (0.0561 vs. 0.1382), with a 2 dB PSNR gain, while maintaining comparable real-time speed (108 vs. 121 FPS). Against BlockNeRF++ [8,36,47], VEGS dominates across every metric, with FID 124.4 vs. 245.1 and PSNR 23.71 vs. 21.03. The 3DGS+ ablation (adding LiDAR initialization and dynamic modeling to 3DGS without the covariance or diffusion losses) already narrows the gap to MARS on EVS (FID 126.3), confirming that the remaining gains come from the surface-normal covariance guidance and diffusion score distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its core rendering framework and departs from S-NeRF's per-frame sparse LiDAR supervision by constructing a dense multi-frame LiDAR map for initialization. It extends the extrapolated view synthesis line (RapNeRF for altitude changes, NeRFVS for indoor scenes) to the specific and practically important setting of urban driving scenes with forward-facing cameras, where prior urban reconstruction methods (NSG, MARS, BlockNeRF) have only evaluated on interpolative test cameras. The diffusion-based knowledge distillation draws on SJC and DiffusioNeRF but introduces LoRA fine-tuning of a large pretrained model to balance scene-specificity with generalization, a departure from the patch-wise or GAN-based approaches used previously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically critical gap: urban scene reconstructions from driving vehicles cannot reliably render views outside the forward-facing camera distribution, yet such views are essential for autonomous driving simulation and scene understanding. The conceptual finding that 3DGS covariances 'lazily' overfit to the training view frustum rather than representing true surface geometry is a useful diagnostic for the broader 3DGS community. However, the quantitative gains over strong baselines (3DGS+, MARS) are modest, evaluation is limited to KITTI/KITTI-360 driving sequences, and the multi-component pipeline (LiDAR + normal estimation + diffusion distillation) adds substantial complexity. Open questions include whether the covariance guidance generalizes to non-driving scenes and whether the diffusion distillation can be replaced by lighter-weight priors.
