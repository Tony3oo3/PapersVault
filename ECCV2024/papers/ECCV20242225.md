## Problem

Self-attention in Vision Transformers has quadratic complexity with token length, making high-resolution inputs computationally prohibitive. Existing sparse attention methods (spatial reduction, window attention) reduce cost but still treat all tokens equally and produce highly similar attention patterns across queries, incurring significant computation redundancy. This paper identifies that most queries generate nearly identical attention maps, meaning attending every query to all spatial locations is suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242225/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet-1K classification, 512×512 for ADE20K segmentation and MS-COCO detection) and produces class logits, bounding boxes with class scores, or pixel-level segmentation masks depending on the downstream head. The hierarchical backbone uses convolution-based patch embeddings with spatial downsampling rates of {4, 8, 16, 32} across four stages, with channel widths scaled as {C, 2C, 3C, 4C}. Pre-trained ImageNet-1K backbones are transferred to detection and segmentation tasks with standard heads (RetinaNet, Mask R-CNN, Semantic FPN, UperNet).

## Method

PartialFormer introduces partial attention that splits image tokens into a small foreground set and a large background set by sorting context scores (channel-wise mean) in descending order. Foreground tokens are processed by Mixed Multi-Head Self-Attention (MMSA), which applies standard multi-head attention plus a HeadMLP (two FC layers with Softmax between them) across the head dimension to increase attention head diversity. Background tokens are processed by Single-Query Attention (SQA), where one learnable abstract token Q_A attends to all background keys/values, yielding linear complexity in the background token count. Q_A is added to the foreground set before MMSA so it abstracts a weighted sum of foreground features, then serves as the sole query in SQA to exchange information between the two sets at negligible cost. Q/K/V linear projections are shared between the MMSA and SQA branches to further reduce parameters, and the N/N_F ratio is set adaptively per stage (e.g., [64, 16, 4, 1]) to balance foreground coverage against computational savings.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, PartialFormer-B3 achieves 83.0% Top-1 accuracy with only 3.4 GFLOPs and 36M parameters, surpassing Swin-T (81.3%) by 1.7% while saving 25% GFLOPs, and DAT-T (82.0%) by 1.0% while saving 25% GFLOPs. PartialFormer-B4 reaches 83.9% Top-1 at 6.8 GFLOPs, outperforming PVTv2-B4 (83.6%) by 0.3% while saving 33% GFLOPs. On ADE20K segmentation with UperNet, PartialFormer-B3 achieves 47.0 mIoU, exceeding Swin-T (44.5) by 2.5% and DAT-T (45.5) by 1.5%. On MS-COCO detection with RetinaNet, PartialFormer-B3 attains 44.1 AP^bb, beating Swin-T (41.5) by 2.6% and DAT-T (42.8) by 1.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartialFormer-B3 (83.0%, 3.4 GFLOPs) outperforms all same-compute-class baselines: Swin-T (81.3%, 4.5 GFLOPs) by 1.7% Top-1 at 25% lower cost, DAT-T (82.0%, 4.5 GFLOPs) by 1.0% at 25% lower cost, Focal-T (82.2%, 4.9 GFLOPs) by 0.8% at 31% lower cost, and ConvNeXtV2-T (82.5%, 4.5 GFLOPs) by 0.5% at 25% lower cost. Against the strongest competitor CSWin-T (82.7%, 4.5 GFLOPs), PartialFormer-B3 gains 0.3% accuracy while using 24% fewer GFLOPs. At the larger scale, PartialFormer-B4 (83.9%, 6.8 GFLOPs) beats PVTv2-B4 (83.6%, 10.1 GFLOPs) by 0.3% at 33% lower cost and CSWin-S (83.6%, 6.9 GFLOPs) by 0.3% at similar cost. For lighter models, PartialFormer-B1 (79.3%, 0.7 GFLOPs) surpasses MixFormer-B1 (78.9%, 0.7 GFLOPs) by 0.4% and EMO-5M (78.4%, 0.9 GFLOPs) by 0.9% at 22% lower cost. On dense prediction, PartialFormer-B3 with UperNet on ADE20K (47.0 mIoU) beats Swin-T (44.5) by 2.5% and DAT-T (45.5) by 1.5%, and with RetinaNet on COCO (44.1 AP^bb) beats Swin-T (41.5) by 2.6% and DAT-T (42.8) by 1.3%, confirming consistent gains across tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the hierarchical Vision Transformer architecture (ViT) and positions itself within the efficient ViT literature that addresses quadratic self-attention complexity. It departs from spatial reduction attention (PVT, DAT) and window attention (Swin) by observing that attention maps across queries exhibit high cosine similarity, indicating computation redundancy. Rather than uniformly downsampling keys/values or restricting attention to fixed windows, PartialFormer introduces a data-dependent foreground/background token split (via simple mean-pooled context scores) and applies distinct attention mechanisms to each: Mixed Multi-Head Self-Attention for the small foreground set and Single-Query Attention for the large background set. Unlike token pruning methods (ToMe, DynamicViT) that discard unimportant tokens entirely, PartialFormer retains background information through SQA, and unlike prior sparse attention, it adds cross-head mixing via HeadMLP to improve feature diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The partial attention mechanism offers a simple, parameter-light (no learned selection policy) way to reduce attention redundancy that can be plugged into existing ViT backbones (DeiT, PVT, Swin) with consistent accuracy gains and FLOPs reductions, making it practically useful for resource-constrained deployment. An open question is whether the crude mean-pooling context score could be replaced by more informative importance estimators, and whether the fixed per-stage N/N_F ratio could be made input-adaptive. The method's reliance on a single learnable abstract token as the sole bridge between foreground and background sets is elegant but raises questions about information bottleneck in very high-resolution settings where background regions may contain diverse semantic content.
