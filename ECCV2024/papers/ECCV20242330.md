## Problem

Experience replay-based continual learning suffers from two under-addressed issues: (1) the small buffer causes new-task data to dominate training, disrupting old-task knowledge, and (2) repeated replaying of a fixed buffer subset while discarding unsampled old-task data introduces distributional bias and overfitting. Existing replay methods focus on sample selection strategies but neglect how unsampled data can still contribute to new-task learning.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100 and miniImageNet) in a class-incremental setting, producing a single class label per image. Beyond the standard new-task batch, the method consumes two additional signals: buffered old-task samples (100–5000 depending on dataset) and pre-computed unsampled-data features z_u extracted after each old-task training phase. The unsampled features act as a surrogate regularization term rather than direct training inputs, and their influence is dynamically annealed via a cosine schedule over epochs.

## Method

IBCL comprises two modules derived from information bottleneck theory. IBTA constrains buffer-data features during new-task training by minimizing I(z_m; x_m) (approximated via a Gaussian variational posterior with reparameterization, reducing to L2-norm regularization on embeddings) while maximizing I(z_m; y_old) (cross-entropy), encouraging task-agnostic representations that resist new-task interference. IBDS, applied after old-task training, decouples sampled and unsampled features by minimizing I(z_m; z_u) through a chain-rule decomposition, yielding a surrogate loss H(y_old | z_u) that compensates for the missing unsampled data during new-task training. The total loss is α‖e(x_m)‖² + β·η·H(y_old|z_u) + γ·H(y_old|z_m), where η is a dynamic cosine-annealing factor that imposes stricter constraints early in training and relaxes them later. The method is plug-and-play, combining additively with any existing replay strategy (ER, DER++, X-DER, ER-ACE).

## Results

The paper reports average accuracy (ACC) across four class-incremental benchmarks (Split CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, miniImageNet) at multiple buffer sizes. IBCL consistently improves all four replay baselines: e.g., on Split CIFAR-10 (buffer 500), DER+++IBCL reaches 76.61% vs. DER++'s 72.70%, and X-DER+IBCL reaches 74.14% vs. X-DER's 68.10%. On the harder Split CIFAR-100 (buffer 200), ER+IBCL jumps from 14.35% to 23.98%, and DER+++IBCL reaches 33.82% vs. DER++'s 25.11%. The largest relative gains appear at small buffer sizes, where the unsampled-data correction has the most to compensate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBCL is evaluated as an add-on to four replay baselines (ER, ER-ACE, DER++, X-DER) and also compared against six regularization methods (sSGD, oEWC, oLAP, OCM, LiDER, DualHSIC). IBCL wins across every dataset and buffer size for all four replay baselines, with the largest absolute gains on ER (e.g., +9.04 pts on CIFAR-10 buffer 100, +9.63 pts on CIFAR-100 buffer 200) and moderate but consistent gains on the stronger baselines (e.g., +3.91 pts for DER++ on CIFAR-10 buffer 500, +4.04 pts for X-DER on CIFAR-100 buffer 200). Against regularization methods (Table 2), DER+++IBCL (76.61 on CIFAR-10/500) outperforms the best regularization baseline DualHSIC+DER++ (75.94) and LiDER+DER++ (73.39), while ER-ACE+IBCL (74.82) surpasses DualHSIC+ER-ACE (73.78) and OCM+ER-ACE (70.86). The advantage is most pronounced at small buffer sizes (100–200), where the unsampled-data surrogate (IBDS) compensates for the largest information gap; at larger buffers the gap narrows but IBCL still maintains a 1–3 point lead. No comparison shows IBCL underperforming its respective base method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Information Bottleneck (IB) framework of Tishby et al. and the variational IB approximation of Alemi et al., repurposing them for continual learning in a way that prior IB-in-CL work (e.g., DualHSIC, OCM) has not. Within the experience replay CL literature, it departs from the dominant focus on *which* samples to store (herding, diversity sampling, gradient-based selection) by instead asking how to recover the information lost to *unsampled* data. The resulting IBCL algorithm is positioned as a modular regularization layer that can be stacked on top of existing replay strategies (ER, DER++, X-DER, ER-ACE) rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-addressed source of bias in replay-based CL: the progressive discarding of unsampled old-task data creates a skewed loss estimate that compounds over tasks. By showing that a decoupled surrogate for unsampled features can be extracted once (after old-task training) and then used as a regularizer during new-task learning, the work opens a path toward 'replaying' information without storing data. Limitations include the approximate nature of the feature decoupling, the acknowledged semantic drift that degrades fixed unsampled features over time, and the reliance on a cosine-annealing schedule that may not generalize to non-epoch-based or online settings. Open questions include whether alternative estimation strategies (e.g., generative surrogates, kernel methods) could replace the IB-based decoupling, and whether the approach extends to task-incremental or domain-incremental scenarios.
