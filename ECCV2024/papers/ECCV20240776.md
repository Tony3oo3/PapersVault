## Problem

Transformer-based video super-resolution models that excel on synthetic degradations degrade significantly on real-world inputs, yet the root cause in the attention mechanism itself had not been investigated. This paper identifies that spatial attention is highly sensitive to artifacts in its queries (cosine similarity drops to 0.75–0.92 under degradation), while channel attention is far more robust (0.98–0.99), and further reveals that naive channel attention inflates inter-channel covariance, introducing feature redundancy that hinders reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240776/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 15-frame low-resolution video sequence (64×64 spatial crops during training) bearing complex in-the-wild degradations (blur, noise, JPEG and video compression). Optical flow between consecutive frames is estimated internally via a frozen SPyNet module and used to warp the previous hidden state before fusion. The output is a 4× upscaled high-resolution frame per input frame, produced by a unidirectional recurrent pipeline that propagates a hidden state forward in time.

## Method

RealViformer is a unidirectional recurrent Transformer that replaces the standard concatenation-based temporal fusion with a Channel Attention Fusion (CAF) module, where the current frame's shallow feature serves as query and the warped hidden state provides key and value, thereby selecting informative temporal cues while suppressing artifact propagation. The reconstruction backbone uses an Improved Channel Attention (ICA) module that augments Restormer-style channel attention with two anti-redundancy mechanisms: a squeeze-and-excite pathway that compresses channels by a factor of 4, applies self-attention, and expands back to generate new discriminative features, and a covariance-based rescaling step that predicts per-channel scalar weights from the attention map's row statistics to decorrelate outputs. Training follows a two-stage protocol on REDS with Real-ESRGAN-style random degradations: 300K iterations with Charbonnier and SSIM losses, then 130K iterations adding perceptual and GAN losses. The model uses only 6.3M parameters and achieves 49 ms/frame runtime, outperforming RealBasicVSR (16.7M params, 73 ms) on both real-world and synthetic benchmarks.

## Results

RealViformer achieves the best no-reference scores on both real-world datasets: NRQM 6.338 and ILNIQE 25.94 on VideoLQ, and NRQM 6.588 and ILNIQE 28.61 on RealVSR. On synthetic datasets with ground truth, it attains the best full-reference scores: PSNR 23.34 / SSIM 0.6079 / LPIPS 0.2877 on REDS4 and PSNR 26.42 / SSIM 0.7609 / LPIPS 0.3063 on UDM10. Notably, RealViformer uses only 5.3M parameters and runs at 49 ms/frame, compared to 16.7M / 73 ms for the closest competitor RealBasicVSR and 25.5M / 180 ms for BSRGAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealViformer outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor is RealBasicVSR [5], which RealViformer edges out on VideoLQ ILNIQE (25.94 vs 25.98) and RealVSR NRQM (6.588 vs 6.582), while leading more clearly on REDS4 (PSNR 23.34 vs 23.09, LPIPS 0.2877 vs 0.2991) and UDM10 (PSNR 26.42 vs 25.96, LPIPS 0.3063 vs 0.3209); RealViformer also uses 3.2× fewer parameters (5.3M vs 16.7M) and is 1.5× faster (49 ms vs 73 ms). BSRGAN [40] is the strongest image-based baseline, achieving competitive NRQM on VideoLQ (6.156) and PSNR on UDM10 (25.97), but RealViformer surpasses it on all metrics with roughly half the parameters. Real-ESRGAN [34] trails on full-reference metrics (e.g., PSNR 21.56 on REDS4) and is slower (196 ms). DBVSR [28] has the fewest parameters (2.7M) but is the slowest (772 ms) and weakest on full-reference scores (PSNR 18.30 on REDS4). DAN [14] and RealSR [17] are consistently outperformed by 1–4 dB in PSNR and by large margins in LPIPS across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BasicVSR's unidirectional recurrent propagation framework and RealBasicVSR's real-world VSR formulation, but departs from the dominant use of spatial (Swin-style) attention in video super-resolution transformers by investigating channel attention as an alternative. It draws on Restormer's channel attention for image restoration and SE Network's squeeze-and-excite mechanism, but repurposes both for a video setting: channel attention becomes the temporal fusion mechanism (CAF) and the squeeze-and-excite is adapted to generate new channel information rather than merely predict scalar weights. The key conceptual departure is a systematic comparison of spatial versus channel attention sensitivity to real-world degradations, a question not previously addressed in the VSR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that channel attention is substantially less sensitive to query artifacts than spatial attention provides a principled reason to prefer channel-based designs in real-world restoration, and the discovery that naive channel attention inflates inter-channel covariance (a redundancy signal) is an overlooked issue with broad implications for low-level vision transformers. The practical impact is significant: RealViformer achieves SOTA on real-world VSR with roughly one-third the parameters and half the runtime of RealBasicVSR, making it a more deployable option. Open questions include whether the covariance-reducing techniques generalize to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring) and whether the redundancy issue can be addressed at the architectural level rather than through post-hoc rescaling.
