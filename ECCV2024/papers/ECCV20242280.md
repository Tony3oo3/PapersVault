## Problem

VAEs produce blurry generated samples because the generative direction (prior-to-data) is never explicitly trained, while EBMs achieve high sample quality but require expensive MCMC sampling at test time. No existing non-adversarial method achieves both sharp single-step generation and the training stability of likelihood-based models without incurring MCMC costs during inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242280/cropped_page8_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model takes a 128-dimensional Gaussian latent vector and produces a single RGB image (32×32, 64×64, or 256×256) in one forward pass through the decoder—no MCMC steps at test time. For zero-shot image restoration, it takes a degraded image y and a known linear degradation operator A (e.g., downsampling, color channel removal, or masking), and produces a restored image by running MCMC in latent space against a neural transport prior constructed from the EBM, with no task-specific retraining.

## Method

EC-VAE introduces a conditional EBM p_ω(x̃|x) that models the density of 'calibrated' samples x̃ near VAE-generated samples x, combining an unconditional energy E_ω(x̃) with a Gaussian distance term that localizes high-density regions around x. During training, short-run MCMC (30 Langevin steps) starting from x yields calibrated x̃, and the VAE decoder is updated to minimize the distance between x and x̃, effectively teaching the decoder to produce samples that already look like real data. The joint training is formulated as a constrained optimization solved via a primal-dual algorithm: the EBM maximizes conditional likelihood while the VAE minimizes ELBO plus an adaptively weighted calibration loss, with the dual variable λ dynamically balancing the two objectives. At test time, the EBM is entirely discarded—generation is a single decoder pass from the prior, yielding the 'free lunch' of EBM-quality samples without MCMC. The calibration idea extends to the variational posterior (calibrating the encoder via MCMC on a conditional posterior in latent space) and to normalizing flows (EC-Flow), and is applied to zero-shot restoration by constructing a neural transport prior p_{θ,ω}(z) ∝ exp(−E_ω(g_β(z)))p_θ(z) combined with range-null space decomposition.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, EC-VAE achieves FID 5.20 with a single forward pass (NFE=1, 0.03s for 50 images), outperforming all VAE-family methods (NVAE: 50.97, VAEBM: 12.19), all flow-based methods (Glow: 48.9), and most EBM baselines (CLEL-Large: 8.61 with 1200 NFE, Dual MCMC: 9.26 with 31 NFE). On ImageNet 32, EC-VAE reaches FID 5.76, surpassing DDPM (6.99) and CLEL-Large (15.47). On CelebA 64, EC-VAE achieves FID 2.71, beating COCO-GAN (4.0) and Dual MCMC (5.15). The method requires only 3 GPU days on a single RTX-3090, compared to 18.3+ GPU days for NVAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EC-VAE (FID 5.20, 1 NFE) decisively outperforms all VAE-family baselines: NVAE (50.97), NCP-VAE (24.08), and VAEBM (12.19) on CIFAR-10, and similarly dominates on Church 64 (4.28 vs. 13.51 for VAEBM) and CelebA 64 (2.71 vs. 5.31 for VAEBM). Among EBM methods, EC-VAE beats CLEL-Large (8.61, 1200 NFE), Dual MCMC (9.26, 31 NFE), and Diffusion EBM (9.58, 180 NFE) while requiring zero MCMC steps at test time. Against GANs, EC-VAE surpasses StyleGAN2 (9.9), AutoGAN (12.4), and SN-GAN (21.7) on CIFAR-10, and COCO-GAN (4.0) on CelebA 64. The only methods that beat EC-VAE are multi-step score/diffusion models: DDPM (3.17, 1000 NFE) on CIFAR-10 and Flow Matching (5.02) on ImageNet 32, as well as PGGAN (8.03) on CelebA-HQ-256 where EC-VAE scores 12.35. Notably, EC-VAE's single-step generation (0.03s for 50 images) is roughly 3,600× faster than NCSN (107.9s) and 293× faster than VAEBM (8.79s), while training cost is 3 GPU days vs. 18.3+ for NVAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

EC-VAE sits at the intersection of VAEs and EBMs, building directly on the conditional EBM formulation from Gao et al. (2020) and the standard VAE architecture of Kingma & Welling (2014). It departs from prior VAE+EBM combinations (VAEBM, Divergence Triangle, cooperative learning) in two key ways: (1) it jointly trains both components without adversarial dynamics, using a primal-dual constrained optimization with adaptive weighting between data likelihood and calibrated-sample likelihood, and (2) it discards MCMC entirely at test time, treating the EBM purely as a training-time teacher. The extension to normalizing flows (EC-Flow) and variational posterior calibration follows the same conditional-density-plus-MCMC-then-minimize-distance template, and the zero-shot restoration application repurposes the trained EBM as a neural transport prior within the range-null space framework of DDNM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single-step, non-adversarial generator that matches or exceeds GANs and diffusion models on FID across six datasets while requiring only 3 GPU days on a single RTX-3090, addressing the compute bottleneck of NVAE-family models (18+ GPU days, 153600-dim latent). The finding that removing MCMC at test time *improves* FID challenges the assumption that iterative refinement always helps, suggesting the calibrated decoder internalizes a denoising function. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions (beyond 256×256), whether the EBM can be made lighter without sacrificing calibration quality, and whether the EC-Flow variant (which still benefits from MCMC at test time) can be made MCMC-free through architectural changes to the flow.
