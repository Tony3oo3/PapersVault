## Problem

Multi-target transferable adversarial attacks train a single class-conditional generator to produce perturbations for many target classes, but existing methods condition the generator only on class indices or one-hot vectors, ignoring the rich semantic information inherent to each target category. This limits the generator's ability to model the target class's feature distribution, yielding modest black-box fooling rates. Meanwhile, single-target generative attacks that train a dedicated generator per class achieve higher performance but become computationally infeasible for the hundreds or thousands of classes in real-world classification systems.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a clean RGB image (224×224, 3-channel) and a target class label, which is converted into a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') and encoded by CLIP's frozen text encoder into a 512-dimensional embedding. The output is an l-infinity-constrained adversarial perturbation (budget ε = 16/255) that, when added to the clean image, causes an unseen black-box classifier to predict the specified target class. The generator is trained on unlabeled images from ImageNet and can produce perturbations for any target class at inference time without retraining.

## Method

CGNC replaces the simple class-label conditioning of prior multi-target generators with CLIP-encoded text embeddings, processed through three modules: a VL-Purifier (FC + spectral normalization layers) that compresses 512-dim CLIP embeddings to 16-dim task-specific representations; an F-Encoder that fuses these purified embeddings with multi-scale visual features via channel-wise concatenation; and a CA-Decoder that injects the original 512-dim CLIP embedding into the decoder backbone through cross-attention layers (decoder features as queries, CLIP embedding as keys/values). Training minimizes cross-entropy loss on a surrogate model (Inc-v3 or Res-152) over unlabeled images with randomly sampled target labels. For single-target scenarios, a masked fine-tuning (MFT) step fixes the text condition to one class and fine-tunes the generator with patch-wise random masking (ratio 0.2) to prevent overfitting to specific image regions, enabling CGNC to outperform dedicated single-target generators at a fraction of the training cost.

## Results

On the ImageNet-NeurIPS validation set with 8 target classes, CGNC achieves an average black-box ASR of 52.96% (Inc-v3 surrogate) and 58.48% (Res-152 surrogate) across six victim models, outperforming the SOTA multi-target method C-GSP by 10.08% and 17.88% respectively. The largest single-model gain is 21.46% ASR from Res-152 to DenseNet-121 (85.66% vs. C-GSP's 64.20%). Under robust training defenses (Table 2), CGNC with Res-152 surrogate reaches 84.05% on Res50^fine and 79.80% on Res50^IN, versus C-GSP's 65.51% and 60.30%. The single-target variant CGNC† (via masked fine-tuning) achieves 74.76% on Inc-v4 and 78.94% on DN-121 with Inc-v3 surrogate, surpassing the best single-target baseline DGTA-PI by 6.81% and 31.56% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGNC decisively outperforms all iterative instance-specific baselines (MIM, TIM, SIM, DIM, Logit, SU), which achieve near-zero black-box ASR (typically <16%) due to overfitting to the surrogate's decision boundary. Against the closest multi-target competitor C-GSP [67], CGNC wins on every black-box model in both surrogate settings, with average gains of 10.08% (Inc-v3 surrogate) and 17.88% (Res-152 surrogate); the largest single gap is +21.46% on DN-121 (85.66% vs. 64.20%). In the single-target comparison (Table 5), CGNC† surpasses all four SOTA single-target methods (GAP, CD-AP, TTP, DGTA-PI) on most victim models, with the most dramatic margin being +31.56% over TTP on DN-121 (78.94% vs. 47.38%) and +6.81% over DGTA-PI on Inc-v4 (74.76% vs. 67.95%) with Inc-v3 surrogate. The only notable exception is Inc-Res-v2 with Res-152 surrogate, where CGNC† (45.71%) trails DGTA-PI (47.61%) by 1.9%. Overall, CGNC wins or ties on 15 of 16 black-box model comparisons in the multi-target setting and 13 of 16 in the single-target setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-target transferable adversarial attack line initiated by MAN (Han et al., ICCV 2019) and C-GSP (Yang et al., ECCV 2022), which train a single class-conditional generator to produce perturbations for many target classes simultaneously. The key departure is replacing the simple one-hot or class-index conditioning used by C-GSP with CLIP text embeddings, and augmenting the decoder (derived from TTP, Naseer et al., ICCV 2021) with cross-attention layers to better fuse visual and textual modalities. The masked fine-tuning mechanism further bridges the gap between multi-target efficiency and single-target performance, positioning CGNC as a unified framework that subsumes both attack regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making targeted attacks scalable to real-world classification systems with hundreds or thousands of classes, where single-target methods become computationally prohibitive. The finding that a single CLIP-conditioned generator with brief fine-tuning can outperform dedicated single-target generators (trained from scratch per class) suggests a more efficient paradigm for adversarial evaluation. Open questions include whether the CLIP dependency limits applicability to domains where text descriptions are less informative, whether the approach transfers to non-ImageNet taxonomies, and whether the gains persist against stronger adaptive defenses beyond the standard robust-training and preprocessing baselines tested here.
