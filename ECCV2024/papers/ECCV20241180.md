## Problem

Existing human motion prediction methods treat motion as discrete consecutive frames, ignoring the continuous temporal dynamics inherent in human movement and suffering significant degradation when historical observations contain missing joint values. No prior approach provides a unified framework that simultaneously handles incomplete observations and predicts future poses at arbitrary continuous time locations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a past motion sequence of J joints × 3D coordinates over T_h timesteps (normalized to [0,1]) along with a binary mask M ∈ {0,1}^{J×T_h} indicating which joint positions are observed versus missing. It outputs a continuous function that can be evaluated at any temporal coordinate to produce 3D skeleton positions, enabling both repair of missing historical values and prediction of future poses at arbitrary timestamps rather than only at pre-defined discrete frames.

## Method

NeRMo represents motion as a continuous neural function f_θ(t, z) → x_t that explicitly disentangles temporal coordinates (encoded via Fourier features) from spatial joint indices (encoded as learnable latent codes), then enriches both through a Codebook-Coordinate Attention module that retrieves motion-relevant prototypes from a pre-trained codebook. The network backbone combines MLP blocks with a mask-aware spatial attention module that uses a binary mask to restrict attention between observed joints while allowing full attention over all joints, enabling inference from incomplete observations. Training employs a bi-level meta-optimization: the inner loop solves a closed-form ridge regression to obtain instance-specific personalized modulation parameters that reconstruct the historical motion, while the outer loop updates instance-agnostic generic rule parameters to learn a shared inductive bias for extrapolating to future time steps, all in a single feed-forward pass at inference.

## Results

On Human3.6M (average over 15 actions), NeRMo achieves MPJPE of 9.9 mm at 80 ms and 21.8 mm at 160 ms, the best among all compared methods, and ties with SPGSN at 320 ms (47.1 mm) while being slightly behind at 400 ms (59.1 vs 58.3 mm). In the incomplete-observation setting (20% missing values), NeRMo reduces MPJPE by 10.7%, 7.9%, 6.0%, and 4.1% at 80, 160, 320, and 400 ms respectively compared to the best baseline. On CMU-MoCap and 3DPW, NeRMo also achieves the lowest average prediction errors at most timestamps, confirming consistent performance across datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeRMo outperforms all seven historical-value baselines (Res-sup, DMGNN, LTD, MSR-GCN, PGBIG, SPGSN, DeFeeNet) on the average 80 ms and 160 ms MPJPE on Human3.6M, achieving 9.9 mm and 21.8 mm respectively versus 10.3/22.7 (PGBIG) and 10.4/22.3 (SPGSN) for the closest competitors. At 320 ms, NeRMo ties SPGSN (47.1 mm) and at 400 ms it is marginally behind SPGSN (59.1 vs 58.3 mm), which the authors attribute to the efficient last-layer meta-optimization sacrificing some long-horizon extrapolation. In the incomplete-observation setting (20% missing), NeRMo clearly dominates: it beats the two-stage repair-then-predict baselines (R+LTD, R+DMGNN), the multi-task MT-GCN [9], and the diffusion-based TCD [37] by 10.7% at 80 ms and 4.1% at 400 ms. NeRMo also surpasses MT-GCN and TCD in the pure repairing task (L2 distance to ground truth), especially at higher missing ratios. Notably, MT-GCN cannot handle complete observations at all, whereas NeRMo unifies both repairing and forecasting in a single model.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeRMo transplants the implicit neural representation (INR) paradigm—established in 3D scene synthesis (NeRF), image/video compression, and time-series forecasting—into human motion prediction, a domain previously dominated by discrete historical-value models (RNNs, GCNs, Transformers). It specifically adapts Fourier feature encoding from NeRF for temporal coordinates and borrows codebook-coordinate attention from 3D scene INRs, while departing from video INRs by explicitly disentangling spatial (joint) and temporal axes rather than encoding a continuous space-time grid. The meta-optimization framework draws on differentiable closed-form solvers (ridge regression) to make the bi-level training tractable, distinguishing it from gradient-based MAML-style approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By representing motion as a continuous neural function, NeRMo offers a unified single-model solution for both repairing missing values and forecasting future poses, eliminating the need for separate repair-then-predict pipelines or multi-task frameworks. This is practically valuable given that incomplete MoCap data is common in real deployments. However, the paper acknowledges that performance at distant prediction horizons is sacrificed due to the efficient last-layer meta-optimization, and the gains over strong discrete baselines (SPGSN, PGBIG, DeFeeNet) are modest—often within 1–3 mm MPJPE. Open questions include whether the INR formulation scales to longer prediction windows, more diverse motion vocabularies, or real-time inference settings where the per-instance ridge regression step must be extremely fast.
