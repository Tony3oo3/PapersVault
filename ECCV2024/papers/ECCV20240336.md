## Problem

Low-bit quantization of neural networks typically requires access to the original training data for calibration or fine-tuning, but this data is often unavailable due to privacy or IP restrictions. Existing data-free quantization methods rely on image inversion or GAN-based synthesis, which produce low-fidelity images that fail to capture complex objects in large-scale datasets like ImageNet, and are either computationally expensive or not reusable across model architectures.

## Input / Output

In the data-free setting, GenQ takes only class label names (e.g., 1000 ImageNet labels) and a pre-trained full-precision model as input; in the data-scarce setting, it additionally accepts one real image per class to guide generation. The output is a set of filtered synthetic images (1K for PTQ, 1.2M for QAT) that are used to produce a low-bit quantized model (W4A4, W2A4, or W3A3). Unlike inversion-based methods that produce one image per class, GenQ generates multiple candidates per class and retains only a fraction after filtering, making the synthetic data reusable across different target architectures.

## Method

GenQ leverages Stable Diffusion to generate photorealistic images from text prompts constructed from class labels (e.g., 'A photo of a small hamster'), then applies a two-stage filtering pipeline to select in-distribution samples: first, energy score filtering (treating selection as the inverse of OOD detection) removes images with high energy scores under the pre-trained model; second, architecture-specific filtering uses BatchNorm sensitivity for CNNs or patch similarity entropy for ViTs. In the data-scarce regime, a learnable token embedding is optimized by inverting the diffusion denoising loss on the limited real images, producing a style prompt that aligns generated images with the original dataset's characteristics. For QAT, GenQ initializes from a PTQ model with learned rounding variables frozen, introducing an additional learnable offset updated via STE to stabilize fine-tuning on synthetic data.

## Results

On ImageNet data-free QAT (W4A4), GenQ achieves 76.10% top-1 accuracy on ResNet-50, outperforming the best prior method TexQ (70.72%) by 5.4% and approaching the real-data LSQ upper bound (76.70%). Under W3A3 QAT, GenQ reaches 73.99% on ResNet-50 versus TexQ's 25.27%, a 48.7-point margin. For data-free PTQ (W4A4) on CNNs, GenQ with GENIE-M achieves 75.50% on ResNet-50 and 68.96% on MobileNetV2, matching or exceeding real-data baselines. On ViTs (W4A4 PTQ), GenQ with RepQ-ViT reaches 76.10% on DeiT-B, surpassing PSAQ-ViT (74.69%) by 1.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenQ outperforms all 13 data-free baselines across PTQ and QAT settings. In data-free QAT (W4A4), GenQ beats the strongest baseline TexQ by 2.3% (Res18), 2.6% (MBV2), and 5.4% (Res50); the gap widens dramatically at W3A3 where GenQ exceeds TexQ by 17.9% on Res18 and 48.7% on Res50. In data-free PTQ (W4A4), GenQ with GENIE-M matches or slightly exceeds GENIE-D on ResNet (75.50 vs. 75.61 on Res50) but pulls ahead on lightweight architectures: +0.34% on MBV2, +1.43% on MobileNet-b, and +1.03% on MnasNet; at W2A4 the advantage grows to +3.35% on MBV2 and +4.38% on MnasNet. For ViT PTQ (W4A4), GenQ surpasses PSAQ-ViT by 2.9% on ViT-B and 1.4% on DeiT-B with RepQ-ViT. GenQ approaches but does not fully match the real-data LSQ upper bound (76.10% vs. 76.70% on Res50 W4A4 QAT), closing 99.2% of the gap with zero real training images. In cross-model transferability, GenQ data filtered by one model retains 99.5–99.8% of its accuracy when applied to a different architecture, whereas GENIE-D/PSAQ-ViT data drops up to 33% when transferred from ViT to MobileNetV2.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenQ carves out a third category of data-free quantization data generation, distinct from the two established lines: (1) gradient-based image inversion (ZeroQ, KW, GENIE, MixMix) and (2) GAN-based synthesis (GDFQ, ZAQ, Qimera, TexQ). It repurposes a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as the synthesis engine, replacing the need to invert activations or co-train a GAN with the quantized model. The filtering mechanisms are adapted from OOD detection (energy scoring from Liu et al.), BN-distance calibration from ZeroQ, and patch-similarity entropy from PSAQ-ViT, while the QAT initialization builds directly on GENIE-M's learned rounding variables. The data-scarce variant introduces a learnable token embedding optimized via diffusion loss to inject dataset-level style information from a handful of real images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenQ provides a practical, reusable pipeline for quantizing models when training data is inaccessible or severely limited, with a 15× speedup over inversion-based methods and strong cross-architecture transferability. The data-scarce setting (1-shot per class matching 300-shot performance) addresses a more realistic deployment scenario than fully data-free quantization. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ImageNet, dependence on Stable Diffusion's class coverage and quality, and the somewhat ad-hoc choice of filtering thresholds. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-ImageNet domains, whether newer diffusion models (SDXL, Flux) further close the gap to real-data quantization, and whether the prompt-tuning mechanism can be extended to multi-domain or multi-task settings.
