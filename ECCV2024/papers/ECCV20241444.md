## Problem

Long-tailed semi-supervised learning (LTSSL) suffers from biased pseudo-labels and poor calibration when the unlabeled class distribution differs from the labeled one (label shift). Most existing distribution alignment methods assume labeled and unlabeled data share the same marginal distribution or require access to the ground-truth prior, conditions rarely met in practice. This paper tackles the case where the unlabeled distribution is unknown and potentially distinct from the labeled prior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100-LT), 96×96 (STL-10-LT), or 32×32/64×64 (ImageNet-127), split into a small labeled set with a long-tailed class distribution and a larger unlabeled set with an unknown distribution. It produces a balanced classifier that outputs class labels and calibrated probabilities, evaluated via balanced accuracy and expected calibration error. Both weak and strong data augmentations are applied to unlabeled samples during training, and an EMA network tracks the evolving pseudo-label distribution.

## Method

ADELLO extends FixMatch with two key components. First, Flexible Distribution Alignment (FlexDA) introduces logit-adjusted supervised and consistency losses that add log-ratio terms (log P_L/Q̂_αt for supervised, log Q̂/Q̂_αt for unsupervised) to the network logits, where Q̂ is an EMA estimate of the unlabeled class distribution and Q̂_αt is a progressively smoothed target prior controlled by a decaying exponent α_t. This first aligns the model with the true unlabeled distribution (enabling accurate pseudo-labeling) and then gradually debiases toward a uniform prior for balanced inference. Second, Complementary Consistency Regularization (CCR) applies temperature-scaled distillation to the subset of unlabeled samples that fall below the confidence threshold, using a temperature T = exp(KL(P_bal || Q̂)) that adapts to the degree of class imbalance, thereby exploiting underconfident samples that standard thresholding discards.

## Results

ADELLO achieves the top Friedman rank (1.50 for CIFAR10-LT, 1 for CIFAR100-LT) across all label-shift settings, with balanced accuracy of 57.5% on CIFAR100-LT (γ_l=50, γ_u=1) and 53.0% under reversed long-tail (γ_u=0.02), outperforming the previous best by +1.0 and +2.2 points respectively. On the large-scale ImageNet127 benchmark (γ≈286), ADELLO reaches 47.5% balanced accuracy at 32×32 and 58.0% at 64×64, surpassing CoSSL by +3.8 and +4.2 points. On STL10-LT with only 150 head-class labels, ADELLO achieves 75.7% (γ_l=10) and 74.6% (γ_l=20), exceeding CoSSL by +5.1 and +8.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADELLO wins or ties on 5 of 6 CIFAR100-LT label-shift settings, with the largest margins over CReST+ (+1.0 at γ_u=1, +12.5 at γ_u=0.02) and CoSSL (+7.1 at γ_u=1, +6.2 at γ_u=0.02). On CIFAR10-LT, ADELLO is competitive but slightly trails CoSSL (84.6 vs 83.8) and CReST+ (92.6 vs 91.9) in the forward and balanced settings, yet clearly leads under reversed long-tail (86.1 vs 87.0 for ABC, 84.2 for CoSSL). Against the strongest SSL baseline SoftMatch, ADELLO gains +2.8 (γ_u=50), +0.0 (γ_u=1, tie), and +1.8 (γ_u=0.02) on CIFAR100-LT. On ImageNet127, ADELLO surpasses all baselines by a wide margin: +3.8 over CoSSL and +3.4 over UDAL at 32×32, and +4.2 over CoSSL and +5.7 over UDAL at 64×64. On STL10-LT with OOD unlabeled data, ADELLO outperforms CoSSL by +5.1 (γ_l=10) and +8.6 (γ_l=20), and beats SoftMatch by +3.1 and +4.0 respectively, demonstrating robustness to distribution mismatch that other methods lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADELLO extends the FixMatch pseudo-labeling and consistency framework by replacing its static cross-entropy losses with logit-adjusted supervised and unsupervised losses (FlexDA), directly building on the logit adjustment mechanism of Menon et al. and the progressive distribution-alignment scheduling of UDAL. The key departure from prior DA methods (ReMixMatch, CReST+, DebiasPL, UDAL) is that FlexDA does not assume labeled and unlabeled distributions match or require a known target prior; instead it dynamically estimates the unlabeled marginal via EMA and progressively smooths the target toward a balanced prior. The complementary consistency regularization (CCR) further distinguishes ADELLO by distilling temperature-scaled soft pseudo-labels from below-threshold samples, a use of low-confidence data that contrasts with both hard pseudo-labeling and indiscriminate knowledge distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in LTSSL: most prior distribution-alignment methods either assume labeled and unlabeled priors coincide or require the target prior to be known in advance, conditions rarely met in real deployments. By showing that a dynamically estimated prior combined with progressive logit adjustment yields a balanced, well-calibrated classifier without auxiliary networks or resampling, ADELLO offers a drop-in improvement for practitioners facing unknown label shift. Open questions remain around open-set scenarios (the paper acknowledges the closed-world limitation), extension to dense prediction tasks, and whether the EMA-based prior estimation remains reliable under extreme distribution shifts or very small unlabeled pools.
