## Problem

Existing image-based generative novel view synthesis (GNVS) methods rely on first-order autoregressive sampling, generating one view at a time conditioned on a single prior frame. This causes error accumulation over long trajectories, loop inconsistencies when the camera returns to a previously seen viewpoint, and an inability to handle view-sets that lack a natural sequential ordering (e.g., binocular stereo pairs or cyclic trajectories). No prior method could simultaneously generate multiple self-consistent views in a permutation-invariant manner.

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-sized set of posed conditioning images (0 to n_c views, each 256×256 center crops) along with camera poses for the target views to synthesize, and outputs a set of novel view images generated simultaneously. Conditioning views are injected with a fixed diffusion time embedding (t≡0, i.e., no noise), while generated views undergo the full denoising process. The model is permutation-invariant over both the conditioning and generated view sets, and invariant to global rigid transforms of the camera poses.

## Method

PolyOculus is a set-to-set latent diffusion model built on a VQ-GAN autoencoder, where each view in the set is processed by a shared-weight U-Net stream that communicates with other streams exclusively through cross-attention layers. Camera geometry is injected via Fourier-encoded per-pixel ray directions that are canonicalized per stream (each stream treats its own camera as the origin), making the attention mechanism invariant to global rigid transforms while preserving relative pose information. Cross-attention uses canonicalized rays as queries and keys and image features as values, enabling order-independent inter-view constraints. For practical sampling of large view-sets, the paper introduces a hybrid keyframed strategy: first generate a sparse set of keyframes simultaneously, then fill in intermediate views conditioned on their neighboring keyframes, reducing maximum sampling depth from O(N) to O(1) and mitigating both autoregressive error accumulation and generalization errors from oversized sets. Training uses standard DDPM with a masked denoising score matching loss that excludes conditioning views from the noise prediction target.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, Ours-KF achieves the best LPIPS of 0.290 (short-term) and 0.564 (long-term), outperforming PhotoCon (0.333 / 0.588) by a clear margin. On Matterport3D, Ours-KF similarly leads with LPIPS 0.473 (short-term) and 0.598 (long-term), versus PhotoCon's 0.504 / 0.674. Ours-1step attains the highest PSNR (16.42 short-term, 12.88 long-term on RealEstate10K) but produces over-smoothed images with fewer feature matches (60.0 vs. 148.8 for Ours-KF), reducing TSED consistency. In the loop-closure spin trajectory, set-based sampling methods achieve >90% TSED consistency between first and last frames, versus <40% for the autoregressive (Ours-Markov) variant.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PhotoCon [58] (the primary image-based baseline), Ours-KF wins on LPIPS across all four dataset/setting combinations (e.g., 0.290 vs. 0.333 on RealEstate10K short-term; 0.598 vs. 0.674 on Matterport3D long-term), while PhotoCon retains a slight PSNR edge in some long-term settings. Lookout [36] and GeoGPT [38] are clearly outperformed by all Ours variants on both LPIPS and PSNR, with GeoGPT not evaluated on Matterport3D at all. Against the rendering-based DFM [51] (compared at 128×128), Ours-KF achieves lower LPIPS (0.210 vs. 0.255 short-term; 0.471 vs. 0.484 long-term) and better FID, while DFM retains an advantage in TSED consistency due to its explicit NeRF geometry; however, Ours-KF runs ~3× faster (11 min vs. 29 min) with ~5 GB vs. 24 GB peak memory. On the spin (loop-closure) trajectory, Ours-KF and Ours-1step dramatically outperform Ours-Markov in TSED (>90% vs. <40% first-to-last frame consistency), and on binocular stereo pairs, grouped set-based sampling beats the zigzag autoregressive baseline on both LPIPS (0.349 vs. 0.550 short-term) and cross-frame TSED.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-based generative NVS line initiated by PhotoCon (Yu et al. [58]) and video diffusion models (Ho et al. [21]), which use separate U-Net streams with shared weights communicating via cross-attention. The key conceptual departure is reformulating NVS from ordered, first-order autoregressive generation (as in GeoGPT, Lookout, PhotoCon, and Tseng et al.) to permutation-invariant set-to-set generation, where both conditioning and output are unordered sets of views. Camera canonicalization via per-stream rigid-transform invariance and Fourier-encoded ray representations in attention keys/queries enable this set-based formulation without a natural ordering. A hybrid keyframing sampling strategy bridges the gap between full simultaneous generation (computationally intractable for large sets) and pure autoregression (error-accumulating).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The set-based formulation addresses a structural limitation of all prior image-based GNVS methods: the arbitrary ordering assumption that causes error accumulation and loop inconsistencies. The keyframing strategy provides a practical, general-purpose sampling hierarchy that practitioners can adapt to diverse trajectory types (linear, cyclic, binocular, multi-camera). Limitations include the absence of explicit 3D geometry (relying purely on learned multi-view consistency), the 256×256 resolution ceiling, and the quadratic attention cost that still constrains set size. Open questions include whether set-based generation can be combined with 3D representations (e.g., NeRFs) to get both ordering flexibility and geometric grounding, and how the approach scales to very large view sets or higher resolutions.
