## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods either require a pre-defined 3D layout as input (limiting usability for non-experts), produce multi-view inconsistent results via render-refine-repeat paradigms, or lack fine-grained control over individual object placement and appearance. No prior framework allows novice users to generate globally consistent, high-quality 3D rooms from natural language alone while also supporting both scene-level and object-level style editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242282/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language prompt describing each object's spatial position and orientation within a canonical 2D coordinate system (partitioned into a 3×3 grid), optionally followed by a style edit instruction (e.g., 'Make it Halloween themed' or 'Turn the double bed into golden'). The output is a 3D scene represented as anisotropic 3D Gaussians, initialized from mesh vertices of retrieved CAD models and trained on 250 rendered images at 512×512 resolution, with camera positions sampled on an upper hemisphere at 1.5r distance. Object-level edits are constrained via binary masks derived from 2D segmentation renders, ensuring gradient propagation only to target objects.

## Method

SceneTeller is a three-stage pipeline: (1) an LLM (GPT-4) generates 3D bounding boxes in CSS format via in-context learning, conditioned on retrieval-based exemplars selected by scene dimension similarity, with rule-based spatial descriptions paraphrased by GPT for linguistic variation; (2) the predicted layout is populated by retrieving the nearest CAD model from 3D-FUTURE for each bounding box based on Euclidean distance of dimensions, then placing it at the predicted center and orientation; (3) the assembled scene is fitted with a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and stylized using Instruct-GS2GS, where InstructPix2Pix edits training images every 2.5k iterations, and binary segmentation masks are applied both to the edited images (via element-wise compositing) and to the L1/SSIM losses to restrict edits to specified objects. The key architectural insight is decoupling layout reasoning (LLM), geometry (CAD retrieval), and appearance (diffusion-based 3DGS editing) into independent, composable stages.

## Results

In the user study (30 participants, 1–5 scale), SceneTeller achieves 4.154 (Realism), 4.189 (Text Alignment), 4.479 (Geometric Fidelity), and 4.323 (Compositional Plausibility), substantially outperforming the best baseline LucidDreamer (3.543, 2.751, 3.050, 3.116). On FID against ScanNet, SceneTeller scores 167.27, well below LucidDreamer (238.75), GSGEN (297.10), and Set-the-scene (297.57). For stylization consistency, SceneTeller attains a CLIP Score of 30.923, marginally exceeding LucidDreamer (30.715) and clearly surpassing GSGEN (26.478) and Set-the-scene (27.188). In the layout generation task, the LLM-based module achieves 44.60% mIoU, outperforming human participants (28.70%) while matching their 100% recall/precision on object counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneTeller outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against GSGEN [9] (a two-stage 3DGS pipeline for geometry and appearance), the gap is the largest: user-study scores are roughly 3× higher (e.g., 4.479 vs. 1.439 on Geometric Fidelity) and FID is nearly halved (167.27 vs. 297.10). Against LucidDreamer [10] (render-refine-repeat + 3DGS), SceneTeller leads by 0.6–1.4 points on user-study criteria and by 71.5 FID points, while the CLIP Score advantage is modest (30.923 vs. 30.715). Against Set-the-scene [11] (compositional NeRF requiring a 3D layout as input), SceneTeller wins by 2.1–2.4 user-study points and 130 FID points, and notably operates with text-only input (T) whereas Set-the-scene needs both a layout and text (L+T). The layout generation module also surpasses human annotators in mIoU (44.60% vs. 28.70%), though humans achieve a lower out-of-bound rate (12.09% vs. 56.78%), reflecting the LLM's tendency to place objects near walls.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneTeller sits at the intersection of three research threads: LLM-based spatial reasoning (extending LayoutGPT's in-context learning for 3D layouts by adding per-object position and orientation control), 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time scene representation and editing (building on Instruct-GS2GS for instruction-guided stylization), and object retrieval from CAD databases for scene assembly. It departs from prior text-to-3D scene generation methods in two key ways: unlike layout-conditioned approaches (Set-the-scene, DiscoScene) that require a 3D layout as input, SceneTeller generates the layout itself from natural language; unlike render-refine-repeat methods (SceneScape, Text2Room, LucidDreamer) that produce multi-view-inconsistent results without object-level control, SceneTeller maintains compositional plausibility through explicit 3D bounding boxes and supports fine-grained per-object editing via segmentation-masked diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pipeline offers a practical, turnkey path toward democratizing 3D room design for non-experts, and the large user-study margins (4.15–4.48 vs. 1.31–3.54 for baselines) suggest a meaningful quality gap over prior methods. However, the 56.78% out-of-bound rate—nearly five times the human rate of 12.09%—reveals that LLM spatial reasoning, while strong on object sizing (mIoU 44.60 vs. 28.70 for humans), still struggles with global boundary constraints, limiting direct deployment. Open questions include scalability beyond rectangular rooms and fixed furniture databases, reducing dependence on proprietary LLM APIs, and whether the 3DGS stylization stage can handle more complex structural edits (adding/removing objects) rather than appearance-only changes.
