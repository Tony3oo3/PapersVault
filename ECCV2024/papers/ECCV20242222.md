## Problem

Existing talking head generation methods rely on strong intermediate representations such as 3D morphable models, blendshapes, or predefined pose sequences to guide facial animation, which constrains the range of natural expressions and head movements. The ambiguous one-to-many mapping between audio cues and facial dynamics means these rigid priors fail to capture spontaneous gestures, emotional nuance, and individual facial styles, resulting in less lifelike videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242222/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image (realistic, anime, or 3D-styled) and a vocal audio clip of arbitrary length as input, producing a 512×512 expressive talking head video with synchronized lip motion, facial expressions, and head poses. Video is generated in 12-frame clips with 4 overlapping motion frames for seamless concatenation, enabling arbitrarily long output videos. A constant speed value is assigned per clip to control head rotation frequency, with typical ranges of 0.1–1.0 for speech and 1.0–1.3 for singing.

## Method

EMO extends the Stable Diffusion 1.5 UNet into a video backbone by replacing text cross-attention with reference-attention (driven by a parallel ReferenceNet that extracts identity features from the input image) and inserting wav2vec-based audio-attention layers to modulate facial motion from vocal features. Temporal self-attention modules (initialized from AnimateDiff) operate across the frame dimension for inter-frame coherence, while motion frames from the preceding clip are fed through the ReferenceNet to ensure cross-clip continuity. Instead of strong geometric priors, the method uses two weak conditions: a Face Locator that encodes a facial bounding-box mask to loosely constrain face position, and Speed Layers that discretize head rotation velocity into buckets and inject it via cross-attention in the temporal modules. Training proceeds in three stages—image pretraining, video training with temporal and audio modules, and speed-layer training (deliberately excluding audio layers to prevent the speed signal from overriding audio-driven motion)—on a 250-hour multi-language dataset of talking and singing videos.

## Results

On HDTF, EMO achieves the best FVD (67.66) and E-FID (0.116), substantially outperforming all baselines, while also attaining the lowest FID (8.76) among diffusion-based methods. On the more diverse internet dataset, EMO's advantage widens: FVD drops to 192.77 (vs. 340–585 for baselines) and E-FID to 0.187 (vs. 0.54–3.55). Wav2Lip retains the highest SyncNet lip-sync score (5.79/4.14), but EMO's F-SIM (78.96/77.16) closely tracks the ground-truth values (77.44/72.64), confirming strong identity preservation without the static-head artifact that inflates Wav2Lip's sync score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMO decisively outperforms all four quantitative baselines on FVD and E-FID, the two metrics most sensitive to video-level motion quality and facial expressiveness. Against Wav2Lip, EMO reduces FVD by ~6× (67.66 vs. 407.93 on HDTF) and E-FID by ~6× (0.116 vs. 0.693), but concedes the SyncNet lip-sync score (3.89 vs. 5.79), which Wav2Lip optimizes directly via a sync discriminator. Against SadTalker, EMO achieves a 3.2× lower FVD (67.66 vs. 214.98) and 4.3× lower E-FID (0.116 vs. 0.503), while SadTalker retains a slight SyncNet edge (4.82 vs. 3.89). DreamTalk is the weakest baseline across every metric (FID 58.80, FVD 619.05, E-FID 2.257 on HDTF), confirming the limitations of 3DMM-coefficient diffusion. MakeItTalk sits in the middle, with EMO improving FVD by 5.2× and E-FID by 9.3×. Diffused Heads [33] is compared only qualitatively (Figure 6); EMO produces higher-resolution, temporally coherent clips without the error accumulation that degrades Diffused Heads' sequential generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMO builds directly on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework and AnimateDiff's temporal attention modules, extending them from text-to-video to audio-driven portrait animation. It departs from the dominant prior approach of using 3D intermediate representations (3DMM in SadTalker, blendshapes in VividTalk, 3DMM coefficients in DreamTalk) by adopting a fully generative audio-to-video pipeline with no explicit motion parameters. The identity-preservation mechanism borrows the dual-UNet ReferenceNet design from Animate Anyone, while the audio conditioning leverages wav2vec features injected via cross-attention. The key conceptual departure is replacing strong structural priors with 'weak conditions' (a loose face-region mask and discrete speed buckets), arguing that these are sufficient for stability without constraining expressiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper validates that expressive, lifelike talking head generation is achievable without 3D face models or explicit motion controls, opening a simpler and more flexible design space for future audio-driven animation work. The introduction of E-FID as an expressiveness-specific metric addresses a gap in evaluation where prior methods optimized lip-sync at the cost of facial dynamism. Limitations include the 512×512 resolution, 12-frame clip length requiring cascaded generation, and dependence on a large 250-hour proprietary dataset; generalization to non-portrait subjects, real-time inference, and longer coherent sequences remain open. The finding that co-training speed and audio layers degrades audio-driven motion suggests a non-trivial tension between explicit control signals and learned audio-visual correlations that warrants further investigation.
