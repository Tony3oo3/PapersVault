## Problem

Existing continual learning methods for large pre-trained models focus on reducing forgetting during training via regularization or replay, while model merging approaches have only been evaluated on fixed sets of heterogeneous tasks rather than realistic sequential learning scenarios. Moreover, prior merging methods fine-tune each task independently from the pre-trained model, forgoing the knowledge transfer that sequential adaptation could provide, and have not been systematically compared across varying task similarity, number of tasks, and granularity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of disjoint image classification tasks (class-incremental or domain-incremental splits of CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, CUB-200, Cars, DomainNet, and an 8-dataset benchmark) as RGB images, using a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 text encoder for open-vocabulary classification. It produces a single consolidated multi-task model that classifies images across all previously seen tasks without storing any exemplars from prior tasks. The scaling factor λ is fixed at 0.5 (tuned once on CIFAR-100/5) since no validation data from previous tasks is available in the exemplar-free setting.

## Method

MAGMAX combines sequential fine-tuning with a maximum magnitude selection merging strategy. During sequential fine-tuning, each new task D_t is learned by fine-tuning the model starting from the previous task's weights θ_{t-1} rather than the original pre-trained weights θ_0, which empirically reduces sign conflicts between task vectors. For knowledge consolidation, task vectors τ_i = θ_i − θ_0 are computed for all tasks seen so far, and for each parameter position the value with the highest absolute magnitude across all task vectors is selected to form the merged vector τ_MAGMAX. The final model is θ_MAGMAX = θ_0 + λ·τ_MAGMAX. The approach is grounded in two validated hypotheses: (H1) only a small fraction of highest-magnitude parameters in task vectors drives performance, and (H2) sequential fine-tuning significantly reduces sign conflicts compared to independent fine-tuning, making magnitude-based selection a reliable proxy for selecting updates that multiple tasks agree on.

## Results

On class-incremental learning benchmarks, MAGMAX achieves an average task-agnostic accuracy of 74.50% across CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, CUB-200, and Stanford Cars, outperforming the second-best method (TIES-Merging at 71.84%) by 2.1% on average. In domain-incremental settings, MAGMAX reaches 85.40% on ImageNet-R and 69.00% on DomainNet, matching or exceeding regularization-based CL methods (LwF, EWC). The method also shows strong robustness: sequential fine-tuning alone exhibits 25.7% forgetting on the first task, while MAGMAX reduces this to 9.8%, and the scaling factor λ=0.5 stays within 0.5% of optimal in 11 out of 14 scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGMAX outperforms all four baselines across every class-incremental and domain-incremental scenario tested. Against TIES-Merging [47], the closest competitor, MAGMAX gains 2.66% on average (74.50% vs. 71.84%), with the largest margins on fine-grained datasets (Cars: +6.89% at /20 tasks). Against Model Soup / Task Arithmetic (Avg) [44], MAGMAX leads by 2.75% on average, and notably the simple averaging baseline already matches or beats the regularization-based CL methods LwF [23] (72.36%) and EWC [18] (70.79%), suggesting that the merging paradigm itself is the key driver rather than the specific selection strategy. MAGMAX's advantage over LwF is 2.14% on average and over EWC is 3.71%, with particularly large gains on CUB-200 (+6.12% over EWC at /10) and Cars (+8.58% over EWC at /20). The paper further shows that combining model merging with LwF or EWC (Table 3) improves those CL methods but still does not surpass MAGMAX, indicating that sequential fine-tuning plus maximum magnitude selection is more effective than regularization-based forgetting mitigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: continual learning (CL) of large pre-trained models and model merging. Traditional CL methods (EWC, LwF, replay-based approaches) focus on mitigating forgetting during training via regularization or memory, while model merging techniques (Task Arithmetic, TIES-Merging, Model Soup) consolidate knowledge from independently fine-tuned models post-hoc. MAGMAX departs from both by combining sequential fine-tuning (which the authors show reduces sign conflicts between task vectors) with a maximum-magnitude parameter selection rule, and it explicitly positions itself as filling an evaluation gap: prior merging work was tested only on fixed sets of heterogeneous tasks, not on realistic CL scenarios with varying task similarity, granularity, and number of tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a simple, constant-memory procedure (sequential fine-tuning + max-magnitude merge) achieves state-of-the-art CL results without validation data, replay buffers, or architectural modifications, making it attractive for deployment. The broader finding that sequential fine-tuning improves all merging strategies (up to 12 p.p. on the 8-dataset benchmark) suggests a general principle for how to structure fine-tuning when merging is the end goal. Open questions include sensitivity to the scaling factor λ (which cannot be tuned in exemplar-free settings), whether the approach transfers to non-vision modalities or non-CLIP architectures, and whether the max-magnitude rule generalizes to parameter regimes where sign conflicts are more prevalent.
