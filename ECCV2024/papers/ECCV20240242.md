## Problem

Novel view synthesis (NVS) evaluation traditionally relies on full-reference metrics like SSIM, which require pixel-aligned ground truth images unavailable for true novel views. Existing no-reference, general-reference, and multi-modal-reference metrics lack the pixel-level, scene-specific detail needed for NVS assessment. This paper introduces cross-reference (CR) IQA, a new evaluation regime that substitutes a single aligned ground truth with multiple unregistered views of the same scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240242/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image (typically a rendered image with artifacts) and a set of N_ref=5 unregistered real captured images from the same scene but different viewpoints. During training, 518×518 crops are used; at inference, arbitrary resolutions are supported. The output is a per-pixel quality score map S_cross ∈ R^{H×W} that approximates the SSIM score map, predicted at 14×14 patch granularity by the DINOv2 encoder and upsampled to full pixel resolution by the regression head.

## Method

The architecture comprises three components: a pre-trained DINOv2-small encoder (14×14 patches, 384 channels) applied independently to the query and each reference image; a 2-layer Transformer Decoder cross-reference module where query features serve as the attention query and concatenated reference features serve as key and value; and a 2-layer MLP head that maps each latent patch-level score to a 196-dim vector reshaped into a 14×14 patch. Training is self-supervised: NVS systems (Gaussian Splatting, Nerfacto, TensoRF) are optimized on the MFR dataset, and rendered images at multiple training checkpoints provide query images with SSIM maps against ground truth as supervision via L1 loss. The key insight is that cross-attention over unregistered multi-view features can recover the scene-specific, pixel-level quality information that a single aligned ground truth would provide, eliminating the need for registered references.

## Results

CrossScore achieves Pearson correlation of 0.99 with SSIM on RE10K, 0.95 on Mip360, and 0.83 on MFR, while being trained exclusively on the MFR dataset. This substantially outperforms all no-reference baselines (BRISQUE, NIQE, PIQE), whose correlations range from −0.30 to 0.69 across the three datasets. On the novel-trajectory evaluation (Table 3), CrossScore correlates 0.84 (Pearson) with SSIM scores and 0.85 (Spearman) with SSIM-based quality rankings across 14 MFR scenes, confirming its utility for ground-truth-free NVS evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossScore is compared against two full-reference metrics (SSIM, PSNR) and three no-reference metrics (BRISQUE, NIQE, PIQE). Against the NR baselines, CrossScore dominates by a wide margin: on MFR it reaches 0.83 correlation versus −0.30 (NIQE), −0.11 (PIQE), and 0.23 (BRISQUE); on Mip360 it scores 0.95 versus 0.61, 0.69, and 0.19 respectively. Against the FR baseline PSNR (which also requires ground truth), CrossScore is slightly lower on MFR (0.83 vs. 0.92) but higher on RE10K (0.99 vs. 0.92) and Mip360 (0.95 vs. 0.91), all without needing an aligned reference image. SSIM itself serves as the target (correlation = 1.00 by construction), so CrossScore's 0.99 on RE10K and 0.95 on Mip360 indicate near-equivalent discriminative power to the oracle metric. In the few-shot NeRF comparison (Table 2), CrossScore correctly ranks IBRNet above PixelNeRF, consistent with both SSIM and PSNR, whereas NIQE and PIQE also agree but BRISQUE gives a less separated gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossScore carves out a new evaluation paradigm (cross-reference IQA) that sits conceptually between full-reference metrics (which require pixel-aligned ground truth) and no-reference/general-reference metrics (which lack scene-specific detail). The architecture repurposes the Transformer Decoder's cross-attention mechanism—originally designed for object detection and segmentation—to associate a query image's features with a set of unregistered multi-view reference features, and leverages DINOv2 as a frozen visual encoder. The self-supervised training pipeline is a notable departure from prior IQA work: rather than relying on curated distortion datasets, it harvests intermediate renderings from NVS optimisation (Gaussian Splatting, Nerfacto, TensoRF) to generate diverse distortion samples with pixel-level SSIM supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method directly addresses a practical bottleneck in NVS research: the conventional subsample-then-compare evaluation strategy wastes training data and precludes scoring true novel views where no ground truth exists. By achieving near-SSIM correlation (0.95–0.99 Pearson) without aligned references, CrossScore enables the full captured image set to be used for training while still permitting quality assessment from arbitrary novel trajectories. Open questions include whether the patch-wise DINOv2 encoding limits score-map sharpness, how the method generalises to unconventional optics (e.g., fish-eye), and whether the approach extends beyond NVS to other multi-view settings such as video quality or 3D reconstruction fidelity.
