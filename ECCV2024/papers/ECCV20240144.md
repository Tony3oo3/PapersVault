## Problem

Existing unsupervised video anomaly detection (UVAD) methods are purely data-driven, relying on feature representation and data distribution to identify abnormal patterns. This makes them prone to missing less salient anomalies that are not substantially different from normal events, as there is no common definition of 'what an anomaly is' and data alone cannot resolve ambiguities between normal and abnormal events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes video sequences divided into non-overlapping snippets, with features extracted by a fixed-weight backbone (ResNext3d or I3D) producing D-dimensional vectors per snippet. During training, each video is sampled into 32 snippets and mini-batches contain 30 randomly selected videos. The output is a snippet-level anomaly score (higher indicates more likely abnormal) produced by a lightweight classifier (temporal convolution + two linear layers), along with pseudo-labels used for self-training.

## Method

The method introduces a data-irrelevant normality prior (start and end of a video are predominantly normal, inspired by the boundary prior in saliency detection) to seed label propagation. Normality propagation then estimates normal magnitudes for all snippets by iteratively spreading normal knowledge through a temporally-modulated feature-based similarity matrix that combines a Gaussian feature-space kernel with exponential temporal proximity, solved in closed form as z* = (I − αS)⁻¹y. Pseudo-labels are generated by selecting videos with low standard deviation of normal magnitudes as normal, then flagging the lowest-scoring snippets in remaining videos as abnormal. A loss re-weighting strategy computes additional global abnormal scores via cosine distance to mean features of high-confidence normal videos, and down-weights training samples whose pseudo-labels disagree with these global scores, mitigating the impact of incorrectly propagated labels.

## Results

On ShanghaiTech, the proposed method achieves 86.46% frame-level AUC with ResNext3d and 88.32% with I3D, establishing a new state-of-the-art among unsupervised methods. On UCF-Crime, it reaches 76.64% AUC (ResNext) and 80.02% (I3D), outperforming all prior unsupervised baselines. The method also surpasses one-class methods (e.g., CLAWS Net+ at 91.46%/84.16%) on ShanghaiTech/UCF-Crime respectively, while remaining below the best weakly-supervised approaches (e.g., RTFM at 97.21%/84.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised methods, the proposed approach achieves the highest AUC on both datasets with both backbones. With ResNext, it outperforms the strongest unsupervised baselines by a large margin: 86.46% vs. GCL's 78.93% (+7.53) and LBR-SPR's 77.12% (+9.34) on ShanghaiTech, and 76.64% vs. C2FPL's 74.71% (+1.93) and GCL's 71.04% (+5.60) on UCF-Crime. With I3D, it reaches 88.32% on ShanghaiTech, surpassing C2FPL (80.65%) by 7.67 points. The method also exceeds one-class baselines such as CLAWS Net+ (91.46% on ShanghaiTech) and GODS (70.46% on UCF-Crime), confirming the viability of the unsupervised setting. However, it remains below the best weakly-supervised methods (RTFM: 97.21%/84.30%), which is expected given the absence of any annotations. The diffusion-based methods (Tur et al. [37,38]) perform notably worse, with AUC scores of 62–69%, highlighting the limitations of reconstruction-based approaches for less salient anomalies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant data-driven paradigm in unsupervised video anomaly detection (reconstruction-based and self-training methods) by introducing a data-irrelevant normality prior, drawing an analogy to the boundary prior in saliency detection. The core propagation mechanism builds directly on the iterative label propagation framework of Zhou et al. (NeurIPS 2003), adapted with a temporally-modulated feature-based similarity matrix and applied per-video rather than over the full dataset. The loss re-weighting strategy complements the propagation by incorporating global normal statistics, distinguishing it from prior self-training approaches that rely solely on local contrast or global clustering for pseudo-label generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental ill-posedness in UVAD by showing that a simple, data-irrelevant prior (video boundaries are predominantly normal) combined with propagation can outperform complex data-driven pseudo-labeling strategies, offering a principled alternative when anomalies are rare and poorly defined. A clear limitation is that the method assumes temporal semantic consistency within a video, causing failures on multi-scene or multi-angle normal videos. Open questions include whether the normality prior generalizes to streaming/online settings, whether it can be combined with reconstruction-based methods for complementary strengths, and how to handle videos where the boundary assumption breaks down.
