## Problem

Existing ophthalmic surgical video datasets are small-scale (≤100 videos), restricted to cataract surgery with few phase categories, lack fine-grained time-localized annotations, and exhibit uniform domain characteristics that impede model generalization. OphNet addresses these gaps by providing a large-scale benchmark spanning 66 surgery types, 102 phases, and 150 operations with hierarchical, expert-annotated temporal boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240107/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For surgery presence recognition, untrimmed videos (284.8 h total, ≥1280×720) are classified into multi-label surgery types (one primary, multiple secondary per video). For phase and operation recognition, trimmed segments averaging 32 seconds are classified into 102 or 150 categories respectively. For phase localization, RGB frames at 25 fps and TV-L1 optical flow are encoded by an ImageNet-pretrained I3D into 2048-dim embeddings uniformly interpolated to 100 timesteps, from which start/end boundaries are predicted; phase anticipation uses the same feature pipeline with progressively masked observation ratios.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction and annotation pipeline: text-based YouTube search retrieves cataract, glaucoma, and corneal surgery videos, which are quality-filtered and then hierarchically annotated by 15 ophthalmologists at three granularity levels (surgery, phase, operation). A key design choice is multi-label surgery classification reflecting real clinical practice where multiple conditions are treated in a single procedure, with complete linkage clustering applied to merge temporal boundaries across annotators into stable segments. For empirical evaluation, the paper establishes baselines across four tasks using CNN (I3D, SlowFast), Transformer (X3D, MViT V2), vision-language (X-CLIP, ViFi-CLIP), and temporal detection (ActionFormer, TriDet) models under both random initialization and Kinetics-400 transfer learning. Results show CLIP-based models dominate classification (ViFi-CLIP reaches 68.4% Top-1 on phase recognition) while TriDet with a SwinV2 backbone leads in localization (50.4% average mAP at IoU 0.1-0.9).

## Results

On the OphNet benchmark, CLIP-based models substantially outperform traditional video architectures across all four tasks. For phase recognition on trimmed segments, ViFi-CLIP with 16 input frames achieves the best overall Top-1 accuracy of 66.1% (75.9% cataract, 40.4% glaucoma, 66.6% cornea), while for operation recognition it reaches 65.0% Top-1 (85.0% cornea). For phase localization, TriDet with a SwinV2 backbone attains the highest average mAP of 50.4% (61.0% at IoU=0.1, 33.1% at IoU=0.7). For phase anticipation, MViT V2 pretrained on Kinetics 400 achieves the best average Top-1 accuracy of 48.2% across observation ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates eight model families as baselines on the OphNet dataset. CLIP-based models (X-CLIP, ViFi-CLIP) dominate over all traditional video architectures (I3D, SlowFast, X3D, MViT V2) by a wide margin: ViFi-CLIP achieves 66.1% Top-1 on phase recognition versus 33.5% for the best traditional model (X3D*), a gap of over 32 points. Among CLIP variants, ViFi-CLIP with 16 frames edges out X-CLIP (66.1% vs. 63.4% phase Top-1) and also leads on operation recognition (65.0% vs. 62.5%). For phase localization, TriDet with SwinV2 backbone outperforms ActionFormer with the same backbone (50.4% vs. 46.4% avg mAP), and TriDet+SlowFast achieves the highest single-threshold mAP at IoU=0.1 (61.3%). For phase anticipation, Kinetics-400-pretrained MViT V2* leads at 48.2% average Top-1, marginally ahead of SlowFast* (47.8%) and I3D* (47.6%), with all pretrained models consistently outperforming their random-initialization counterparts by 0.5–1.0 points. Overall, the results confirm that vision-language pretraining is the strongest paradigm for fine-grained surgical video understanding, while traditional spatiotemporal CNNs and ViTs remain competitive but substantially weaker on this domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in ophthalmic surgical video benchmarks by addressing five specific gaps identified in prior work: small scale, limited surgery/phase categories, coarse-grained annotations, single time-boundary labeling, and uniform domain. Rather than proposing a new method, it repurposes a diverse set of existing video understanding models (I3D, SlowFast, X3D, MViT, X-CLIP, ViFi-CLIP, ActionFormer, TriDet) as baselines to establish reference performance on the new benchmark. Conceptually, it extends the surgical workflow understanding task—previously dominated by laparoscopic/endoscopic datasets (CholecT50, HeiCo) and small cataract-only ophthalmic datasets (CATARACTS, Cataract-1K)—to a much broader and hierarchically structured setting spanning 66 surgery types with multi-label, time-localized annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OphNet's 20× scale advantage over prior ophthalmic benchmarks and its hierarchical surgery-phase-operation annotation scheme open the door to studying multi-surgery co-occurrence, fine-grained temporal localization, and cross-surgery generalization in ways previously infeasible. Key limitations include YouTube-sourced video quality variability (subtitles, watermarks, split screens) and potential annotation bias from a single institutional/clinical tradition, which may limit domain generalization claims. Open questions include whether the scale advantage translates into meaningful performance gains over smaller curated datasets, how well models transfer to real operating-room footage, and whether the 48% phase anticipation ceiling reflects a fundamental difficulty or simply insufficient model capacity.
