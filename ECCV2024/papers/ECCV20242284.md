## Problem

Existing video foundation models (e.g., InternVideo, UMT, VideoPrism) rely on two-stage training combining masked reconstruction and video-text contrastive learning, but lack a unified framework that also incorporates next-token prediction for open-ended video dialogue, and do not leverage audio and speech modalities to enrich video semantics. Additionally, prior approaches have not scaled the video encoder to 6B parameters while simultaneously improving data quality through temporally consistent segmentation and multimodal caption fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The video encoder consumes 8 uniformly sampled frames with 14×14 spatial downsampling (yielding spatiotemporal tokens concatenated with a class token and 3D position embeddings), while the audio encoder takes 64-dimensional log Mel filterbank spectrograms computed with a 25 ms Hamming window over 10-second clips. Outputs are flexible: the model produces a video representation embedding usable as a frozen or fine-tunable backbone for classification, retrieval, temporal grounding, and segmentation, or—when connected to an LLM via QFormer—generates next-token predictions for video-centric dialogue and question answering.

## Method

InternVideo2 is trained in three progressive stages on a ViT-based encoder (1B or 6B parameters with attention pooling). Stage 1 performs unmasked video token reconstruction by distilling from two frozen teachers—InternVL-6B (semantic) and VideoMAEv2-g (motion-aware)—aligning the student's unmasked tokens to both via MSE over 80% masked positions. Stage 2 expands the architecture with a BEATs-based audio encoder (90M) and a BERT-Large text/speech encoder, then trains crossmodal contrastive, matching, and masked-language-modeling losses across video, audio, speech, image, and text modalities in a two-step masked-then-unmasked schedule. Stage 3 connects the video encoder to an LLM (Mistral-7B) through a QFormer and fine-tunes with next-token prediction on video-centric instruction data, updating the video encoder for open-ended dialogue. The data pipeline introduces K-Mash (2M unlabeled videos for Stage 1) and InternVid2 (50M clips with fused video-audio-speech captions generated by the VidCap system, which segments videos using AutoShot for temporal semantic consistency and fuses unimodal captions via an LLM).

## Results

InternVideo2-6B achieves state-of-the-art end-to-end finetuning action recognition with 92.1%/91.9%/85.9% top-1 accuracy on Kinetics-400/600/700, 77.4% on Something-Something V2, and 51.2% on Moments in Time (surpassing CoCa-g by 2.2%), all using only 16 frames at 224 resolution. On untrimmed video analysis, it reaches 95.9% on ActivityNet and 97.0% on HACS. For zero-shot video retrieval, InternVideo2-6B attains 55.9% R@1 on MSR-VTT (T2V) and 63.2% on ActivityNet (T2V), outperforming VideoPrism-g and UMT-L across all six retrieval benchmarks. In video-centric dialogue, VideoChat2 with InternVideo2-1B scores 60.3 on MVBench and 55.8 on EgoSchema, leading all open-source models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternVideo2-6B sets new SOTA on Kinetics-400/600/700 (92.1/91.9/85.9%), surpassing VideoMAEv2-g (90.0/89.9), UniFormerV2-L (90.0/90.1/82.7), and the previous best InternVideo ensemble (91.1/91.3/84.0) while using only 16 frames at 224 resolution versus 576 for CoCa-g or 64 frames for VideoMAEv2-g. On temporal-dynamics benchmarks, it edges out MVD-H on SthSthv2 (77.4 vs. 77.3) and beats CoCa-g on MiT by a substantial 2.2% margin (51.2 vs. 49.0). In zero-shot video retrieval, InternVideo2-6B outperforms VideoPrism-g on MSR-VTT T2V (55.9 vs. 39.7), DiDeMo T2V (57.9 vs. –), and VATEX T2V (71.5 vs. 62.5), though VideoPrism-g retains a lead on MSR-VTT V2T (71.0 vs. 53.7). For video-centric dialogue, VideoChat2-InternVideo2-1B leads all open-source systems on MVBench (60.3 vs. 54.6 for VideoLLaMA2) and EgoSchema (55.8 vs. 53.3), though it trails Gemini 1.5 Pro (72.2) on EgoSchema. The one notable loss is zero-shot Kinetics-400, where VideoPrism-g (76.4%) exceeds InternVideo2-6B (72.7%), which the authors attribute to VideoPrism's larger pretraining corpus (311M videos with 36.1M manually labeled).

## Relation to Prior Work

InternVideo2 extends the progressive training paradigm established by InternVideo and UMT, which combined masked video reconstruction with crossmodal contrastive learning in a two-stage pipeline. The key methodological departure is the addition of a third stage—next-token prediction via a QFormer-connected LLM (following VideoChat2/BLIP-2)—and the scaling of the video encoder to 6B parameters using InternViT and CoCa architectures. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper's core contribution is a unified three-stage recipe (unmasked token reconstruction guided by InternVL and VideoMAEv2 teachers, video-audio-speech-text contrastive alignment, and video-centric dialogue tuning) combined with a substantially improved data pipeline (VidCap multimodal captioning, AutoShot temporal segmentation, K-Mash unlabeled video corpus).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InternVideo2 provides a strong open-source 6B video foundation model that sets new SOTA across 60+ video and audio benchmarks, making it a likely default backbone for downstream video understanding research. The paper's practical insight—that data engineering (semantic temporal segmentation, fused video-audio-speech captions) contributes as much to performance as model scale—offers actionable guidance for future video pretraining efforts. However, the authors acknowledge that fixed input resolutions, sparse frame sampling (8 frames), and highly compressed tokens limit fine-grained spatiotemporal perception, and that scaling model capacity without corresponding data refinement yields diminishing returns on fine-grained tasks like FineAction. Open questions include whether the progressive three-stage recipe generalizes to longer video contexts and whether the audio/speech modalities can be leveraged more deeply for procedural reasoning.
