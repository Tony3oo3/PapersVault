## Problem

Existing 3D scene graph prediction methods model the scene as a fully-connected homogeneous graph, performing indiscriminate message passing that treats all relationship types equally. This causes semantic confusion and redundancy accumulation, particularly problematic given the three fundamentally different predicate super-categories (support, proximity, comparative) and the long-tail distribution across 26 predicate classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240687/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D point clouds (N×3) with class-agnostic instance masks as input, producing a directed scene graph with 160 object categories and 27 predicate classes (including 'none'). Predicates are organized hierarchically into three super-categories: support (14 classes, one-way), proximity (6 classes, bilateral multi-label), and comparative (6 classes, bilateral). Evaluation follows two standard tasks: PredCls (given ground-truth objects) and SGCls (joint object and predicate prediction), measured by R@k, ngcR@k, and mR@k on the 3DSSG dataset.

## Method

The paper formulates the 3D scene graph as a directed heterogeneous graph with three edge types and proposes a two-stage framework. In the Heterogeneous Graph Structure Learning (HGSL) stage, a GNN with GRU-based message passing on an initial fully-connected graph predicts type confidence scores for each edge, partitioning it into three type-specific subgraphs. In the Heterogeneous Graph Reasoning (HGR) stage, type-weighted message passing is performed independently on each subgraph, with spatial features (offset, distance, direction) fused into proximity edges and geometric features (log-ratios of length, width, height, volume) fused into comparative edges. A graph structure updating step refines the type edges using final predicate confidence scores before retraining. Hierarchical classifiers first assign predicate type, then classify the specific sub-category within each type, reducing classification difficulty.

## Results

On the 3DSSG dataset, 3D-HetSGP achieves 0.618 R@20 and 0.703 ngcR@20 on the PredCls task, outperforming the strongest baseline KISGP (0.599 R@20, 0.631 ngcR@20) by 1.9% and 7.2% respectively. On the SGCls task, the method reaches 0.312 ngcR@20 and 0.273 mR@20, surpassing KISGP (0.303 ngcR@20, 0.251 mR@20). The mean recall improvements are particularly notable: 0.681 mR@50 on PredCls vs. 0.619 for KISGP, indicating stronger handling of long-tail predicate classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 3D-HetSGP outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric in both PredCls and SGCls tasks. Against the strongest baseline KISGP (the model it builds upon), 3D-HetSGP gains 7.2% on ngcR@20 and 10.1% on ngcR@50 in PredCls, while in SGCls it improves ngcR@20 by 0.9% and mR@20 by 2.2%, though it marginally trails KISGP on R@20 (0.285 vs. 0.288). Compared to SGFN, the improvement is even larger: 8.9% on ngcR@20 and 33.7% on mR@50 in PredCls, reflecting SGFN's weakness on long-tail predicates. Against SGPN, Schemata, KERN, and Co-Occurrence, the gains are substantial across all metrics (e.g., 15.8% on ngcR@20 over SGPN). The per-type breakdown (Table 4) reveals that the largest gains come from Proximity (+9.96% R@20) and Comparative (+5.51% R@20) predicates, while Support performance is nearly matched (−0.12% R@20), confirming that heterogeneous reasoning primarily benefits the more complex bilateral relation types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on KISGP's GNN architecture (PointNet initialization, GRU-based message passing, focal loss) but departs from the fully-connected homogeneous graph framework shared by all prior 3D SGP methods (SGPN, SGFN, SGGpoint, SGFormer, VL-SAT). The key conceptual shift is formulating the 3D scene graph as a heterogeneous graph with three edge-type subgraphs (support, proximity, comparative), drawing on heterogeneous graph learning literature (HetsGG for 2D SGG, SCENE for traffic) that had not been applied to 3D point cloud scene graphs. The two-stage pipeline—heterogeneous graph structure learning followed by type-conditioned message passing with iterative structure updating—repurposes the standard GNN message-passing mechanism to operate on type-specific subgraphs rather than a single undifferentiated graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that the indiscriminate message passing on fully-connected homogeneous graphs, a default assumption in 3D SGP, is a meaningful bottleneck: type-separated reasoning yields the largest gains on proximity and comparative predicates (the multi-class, spatially-grounded relations) while barely affecting the already-well-predicted support type. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (3DSSG), the method inherits KISGP's reliance on pre-trained PointNet and class-agnostic instance masks, and the large gap between ground-truth and learned graph structure in the ablation (ngcR@20: 0.810 vs. 0.703) suggests the structure-learning stage remains a significant accuracy ceiling. Open questions include whether the heterogeneous formulation generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, whether the iterative structure updating converges reliably, and whether the gains persist when the base GNN is replaced by a Transformer backbone.
