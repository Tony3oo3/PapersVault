## Problem

Pose-conditioned diffusion models for novel view synthesis have achieved strong results at the object level but fail to generalize to realistic, in-the-wild scenes due to the absence of large-scale, diverse scene-level training data. Existing scene datasets are either limited in scale (MegaDepth: 196 landmarks) or restricted to narrow categories (WikiScenes: cathedrals; DTU/CO3D: object-centric), leaving a critical gap for training models that handle arbitrary real-world scenes with varied lighting, poses, and geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (resized to 256×256 with aspect-ratio-preserving padding), a target camera pose encoded as a flattened extrinsic matrix plus field of view fed into cross-attention, and a warped image created by aligning monocular depth (Depth Anything) with COLMAP sparse points via RANSAC, unprojecting to a mesh, and rendering from the target pose. The reference image's CLIP embedding is also concatenated for consistency. The output is a single RGB image at the target pose; translation scale is set by the 20th quantile of the reference depth to handle arbitrary scene scales without per-scene grid search.

## Method

The paper introduces MegaScenes, a dataset of ~430K scenes (100K+ SfM reconstructions, 2M+ registered images, 400M 3D points) curated from Wikimedia Commons using COLMAP and the Doppelgangers pipeline for ambiguous reconstructions. For NVS, they mine 2M+ image pairs filtered for lighting consistency (≤3-hour gap), visual overlap (≥50 shared SfM points), and matching aspect ratio, then finetune Zero-1-to-3 and ZeroNVS on these pairs. The key methodological contribution is adding a warped reference-to-target image as an additional conditioning input alongside the extrinsic matrix: the warp encodes pose through visible pixel displacement and is inherently scale-aligned with the scene, addressing the ambiguity of translation scale in uncanonicalized scenes. Conditioning on both the warp and the extrinsic matrix (rather than either alone) yields the best pose accuracy and 3D consistency, as the extrinsic matrix provides a geometric prior that compensates for depth estimation errors in noisy in-the-wild scenes.

## Results

On the MegaScenes test set (41,240 in-the-wild image pairs), the proposed warp+pose conditioned method achieves the best LPIPS (0.3444), PSNR (13.397), SSIM (0.4446), Masked LPIPS (0.1256), FID (11.580), and KID (0.0040). Finetuning on MegaScenes alone (ZeroNVS-MS) already reduces FID from 69.1 (released) to 9.84, a ~7× improvement, validating the dataset's value. The full method further improves LPIPS by 11% and FID by 17% over ZeroNVS-MS by adding warped-image conditioning. On out-of-domain datasets (DTU, Mip-NeRF 360, Re10K), the same trend holds: Ours achieves the best LPIPS and FID across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (warp+pose) outperforms all pose-conditioned baselines on perceptual and generative quality: it achieves LPIPS 0.3444 vs. 0.3857 for ZeroNVS-MS and 0.5476 for Zero-1-to-3 (released), and FID 11.58 vs. 9.84 for ZeroNVS-MS (slightly higher due to warping constraints) but far below 86.89 for Zero-1-to-3 (released). Against SD-inpainting (the warp-conditioned baseline using a frozen Stable Diffusion inpainter), Ours wins decisively on FID (11.58 vs. 38.48) and KID (0.0040 vs. 0.0242), indicating far more realistic and 3D-consistent outputs, though SD-inpainting retains an edge on Masked PSNR (24.38 vs. 22.48) and Masked SSIM (0.8005 vs. 0.7842) because it faithfully copies warped pixels. Finetuning on MegaScenes alone (ZeroNVS-MS) already surpasses the released ZeroNVS on every metric (e.g., FID 9.84 vs. 69.10), confirming the dataset's contribution. On out-of-domain benchmarks (DTU, Mip-NeRF 360, Re10K), the same ordering holds: Ours achieves the best LPIPS and FID, while SD-inpainting again leads only on masked reconstruction metrics but with substantially worse generative quality (e.g., FID 214 on DTU vs. 86 for Ours).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pose-conditioned diffusion model lineage (Zero-1-to-3, ZeroNVS) by addressing the data bottleneck that limits scene-level generalization: prior models were trained on object meshes (Objaverse) or object-centric scenes (CO3D, DTU), which lack the pose diversity and visual variety of real-world scenes. The method additionally adopts the warping-based conditioning first proposed by Liu et al. (Infinite Nature, ICCV 2021) and combines it with the extrinsic-matrix conditioning from ZeroNVS, creating a hybrid pose signal. The dataset itself departs from prior scene-level collections (MegaDepth's 196 landmarks, WikiScenes' cathedral focus) by leveraging the full breadth of Wikimedia Commons categories and COLMAP SfM at scale, yielding 430K scenes and 100K+ reconstructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MegaScenes fills a critical gap: no prior dataset offered both the scale (100K+ SfM reconstructions, 2M+ registered images) and the diversity (indoor, outdoor, natural, object-like scenes with varied lighting, weather, and camera intrinsics) needed to train scene-level NVS models that generalize to in-the-wild photos. The demonstrated ~10× FID improvement from finetuning on MegaScenes validates that data diversity, not model architecture, was the primary bottleneck. Open questions remain: the method cannot handle large camera motions (e.g., behind a scene), relies on potentially erroneous monocular depth for warping, bypasses lighting variation via metadata filtering, and leaves text captions and the full 475K-image pool unused—suggesting substantial headroom for future work.
