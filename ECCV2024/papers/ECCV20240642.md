## Problem

Large-scale human motion datasets collected in the wild remain scarce due to three compounding challenges: obtaining long-term ground-truth motion outside constrained mocap volumes, achieving accurate multi-device temporal and spatial alignment with off-the-shelf hardware, and producing rich natural-language annotations at scale. Existing datasets are limited in duration (typically under 30 minutes per sequence), lack synchronized multimodal egocentric data from multiple devices, or provide only sparse action labels rather than contextual language descriptions.

## Input / Output

The dataset captures 300 hours of activity from 264 participants across 50 locations using XSens MVN Link (17 IMUs + magnetometer at 240 Hz), Project Aria glasses (1 RGB + 2 grayscale cameras, 2 eye-tracking cameras, 2 IMUs, barometer, magnetometer, 7 microphones, GNSS), and two custom miniAria wristbands matching Aria's sensor suite minus microphones and ET cameras. All devices are synchronized to sub-millisecond accuracy via a custom hardware time server and localized into a single metric 3D world via Project Aria MPS, yielding 260M parametric body poses, 201M egocentric images, 11.7B IMU samples, 10.8M gaze points, and 399 Km of accumulated head trajectories. Language annotations are structured hierarchically: fine-grained motion narration (<5 s clips), atomic action labels (<5 s clips), and one-sentence activity summaries (30 s clips), totaling 310.5 K sentences and 8.64 M words from a 6545-word vocabulary.

## Method

The pipeline combines dense inertial mocap with visual-inertial SLAM and a custom HandEye calibration to produce globally consistent full-body motion. XSens skeleton output (23 segments, 79 anatomical landmarks) is retargeted onto a parametric human model via per-frame inverse-kinematics optimization (Eq. 1) that minimizes landmark reprojection error while estimating shape parameters. Because XSens dead-reckoning accumulates drift, the body trajectory is aligned to the MPS SLAM world frame by solving a HandEye calibration (Eq. 2) over 4.2 ms segments, comparing local velocities between the drifting XSens odometry and the drift-free Aria trajectory. For language annotation, annotators view synchronized egocentric video, third-person observer video, and 3D motion rendering to write descriptions at three granularities. Baseline evaluations adapt AvatarPoser (3-point regression), BoDiffusion and EgoEgo (diffusion-based synthesis), VQ-VAE (motion tokenization), and MotionGPT/TM2T (motion-to-text) to the dataset, demonstrating that real sensor noise degrades tracking modestly and that Nymeria's motion distribution is well captured by product-quantized VQ-VAE tokenizers.

## Results

For motion tracking, AvatarPoser trained on Nymeria with real device poses achieves 7.97 cm mean MPJPE (16.74 cm lower body, 3.13 cm upper body), compared to 4.20 cm on AMASS, with the gap attributed to harder in-the-wild motions (hiking, stairs, sports). For motion synthesis, 3-point BoDiffusion achieves 7.98 cm MPJPE and 2.32 FID, while 1-point EgoEgo achieves 13.22 cm MPJPE and 5.14 FID on Nymeria. For motion-to-text, MotionGPT outperforms TM2T across all metrics (BERT 14.09 vs 11.08, CIDEr 37.27 vs 20.85, ROUGE-L 32.33 vs 30.70) when trained on 30 hours of Nymeria narrations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AvatarPoser trained on Nymeria with real SLAM poses (7.97 cm MPJPE) performs worse than the original AMASS-trained model (4.20 cm), but the gap is modest and the synthetic-input variant (7.31 cm) confirms the dataset's tracking quality is close to ideal. BoDiffusion (3-point) trained on Nymeria achieves 7.98 cm MPJPE and 2.32 FID, which the authors note is comparable to the original work trained on a subset of AMASS (3.63 cm MPJPE), validating the dataset's suitability for diffusion-based synthesis. EgoEgo (1-point) underperforms BoDiffusion (13.22 vs 7.98 cm MPJPE; 5.14 vs 2.32 FID), consistent with the expected advantage of additional wristband observations. For motion-to-text, MotionGPT dominates TM2T on every metric (BERT 14.09 vs 11.08, BLEU@4 10.31 vs 8.99, CIDEr 37.27 vs 20.85), attributed to TM2T's weaker T5 language backbone. The authors note that all Nymeria-trained results are somewhat worse than their AMASS-trained counterparts, which they attribute to the greater complexity and diversity of in-the-wild daily activities versus curated studio motions.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nymeria extends the motion dataset tradition (AMASS, EgoExo4D, HPS, EgoBody) by being the first to combine full-body mocap ground truth with multiple synchronized multimodal egocentric devices (Project Aria glasses, XSens suit, miniAria wristbands) in a single metric 3D world, using hardware-based sub-millisecond synchronization rather than the visual or audio cues employed by prior work. For baselines, it adapts AvatarPoser for sparse-input body tracking, VQ-VAE (following MotionGPT) for motion tokenization, and diffusion models (BoDiffusion, EgoEgo) for motion synthesis, while introducing a novel coarse-to-fine hierarchical narration schema (motion narration → atomic action → activity summarization) that departs from the sparse action labels or single-level descriptions in prior motion-language datasets like HumanML3D and KIT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a critical bottleneck in egocentric motion research: prior datasets are either lab-constrained, lack multimodal egocentric sensing, lack parametric full-body ground truth, or lack in-context language at scale. With 300 hours of unscripted daily activity, 310.5K sentences, and precise multi-device alignment, it enables research on gaze-conditioned prediction, wristband-based tracking, and language-grounded motion generation that was previously infeasible. Key limitations include the mocap suit restricting natural appearance and motion range, coverage of only a subset of daily scenarios (no public transit, crowded spaces), and the fact that baseline evaluations use only small subsets (30h for motion-to-text), leaving open whether full-data training closes the gap with prior results on simpler narrations.
