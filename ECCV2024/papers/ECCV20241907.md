## Problem

Prior audio-visual generalized zero-shot learning (AVGZSL) methods rely on complex auto-encoder architectures with cross-attention transformers and projected text embeddings to reconstruct cross-modal features, making it difficult to capture the interplay between audio-visual features and class-label embeddings. A fundamental issue is that class name embeddings from pretrained language models, while semantically rich, lack sufficient class separability to be directly useful for zero-shot classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241907/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted unimodal features: visual frames encoded by C3D (multiple frames spanning a short time window), audio encoded by VGGish, and class names encoded by word2vec. These are fused via a cross-attention transformer into a joint audio-visual representation, which is then matched against optimized text embeddings to produce a class label prediction over both seen and unseen categories. At inference, the cross-attention similarity layer outputs C scores (one per class), and the highest-scoring class is selected.

## Method

EZ-AVGZL replaces complex reconstruction objectives with two simple components. First, Class Embedding Optimization (CEO) uses differentiable optimization to transform frozen text embeddings into a more discriminative space, balancing a separability loss (maximizing minimum nearest-neighbor distance) with a margin ranking loss that preserves the relative similarity ordering of the original embeddings, optimized via gradient descent with α=0.5. Second, Supervised Audio-Visual Language Alignment (AVLA) applies a single cross-entropy contrastive loss to align the fused audio-visual representation with the corresponding optimized class embedding, using a non-linear multi-head cross-attention similarity function (text embeddings as queries, AV features as key-value pairs) rather than cosine similarity. The key insight is that a simple contrastive objective suffices for state-of-the-art performance once class embeddings are made sufficiently separable while retaining semantic structure, eliminating the need for elaborate reconstruction decoders.

## Results

On VGGSound-GZSL (276 classes), EZ-AVGZL achieves 17.26% Seen, 8.68% Unseen, 11.55% Harmonic Mean, and 8.92% ZSL accuracy, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art Hyper-multiple [16] by 2.88 points in Harmonic Mean. On UCF-GZSL (51 classes), the method reaches 58.24% Harmonic Mean (78.32% Seen, 46.35% Unseen), a 7.46-point gain over TCaF. On ActivityNet-GZSL (200 classes), EZ-AVGZL achieves 19.27% Harmonic Mean (38.52% Seen, 12.85% Unseen), surpassing TCaF by 7.07 points in Harmonic Mean.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EZ-AVGZL outperforms all seven baselines across all three benchmarks and all four metrics (Seen, Unseen, Harmonic Mean, ZSL). Against the strongest prior method Hyper-multiple [16] (hyperbolic alignment), the gains are 1.64@Seen, 2.68@Unseen, 2.88@HM, and 1.61@ZSL on VGGSound-GZSL; on UCF-GZSL the gap widens to 4.06@Seen, 10.56@Unseen, and 9.94@HM. Against TCaF [27], the previous strong baseline using temporal contrastive feature alignment with reconstruction losses, EZ-AVGZL improves by 4.63@Seen, 1.96@Unseen, 2.78@HM, and 1.51@ZSL on VGGSound, and by 11.18@Seen, 5.52@Unseen, 7.46@HM, and 3.63@ZSL on UCF-GZSL. The earlier cross-modal attention baseline AVCA [28] is surpassed by 4.63@Seen and 2.49@Unseen on VGGSound. The reconstruction-based AVGZSLNet [26] and the joint multimodal embedding CJME [48] are both significantly outperformed (e.g., 11.55 vs. 5.26 HM on VGGSound). The adapted general-purpose models AttentionFusion [9] and Perceiver [19] perform worst, with Harmonic Means below 5% on VGGSound, highlighting the difficulty of AVGZSL without task-specific design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the reconstruction-based paradigm established by AVCA and TCaF, which used auto-decoders to reconstruct audio-visual attributes and projected word2vec embeddings via cross-attention transformers. Instead, EZ-AVGZL replaces the entire reconstruction pipeline with a single supervised text-to-audio-visual contrastive loss, drawing on the CLIP-style alignment philosophy. The key methodological novelty is the differential optimization of class embeddings (balancing a Tammes-style separability objective with a margin-ranking semantic preservation loss), which the authors claim is the first explicit class embedding optimization mechanism applied to AVGZSL. The cross-attention transformer for fusion is inherited from TCaF, and the non-linear similarity function (multi-head cross-attention as a scoring operator) is a modest architectural addition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a deliberately simple contrastive objective, combined with optimized class embeddings, can outperform substantially more complex multi-objective reconstruction frameworks by large margins across three benchmarks and multiple foundation-model backbones (unimodal, paired, and unified). The class embedding optimization procedure—maximizing nearest-neighbor distance while preserving pairwise similarity rankings via a margin ranking loss—is a general-purpose technique that could transfer to other zero-shot or few-shot classification settings beyond audio-visual learning. Limitations include the cubic complexity of the ranking loss (mitigated only by sampling), reliance on pre-extracted unimodal features rather than end-to-end training, and evaluation on class sets of at most 276, leaving open whether the approach scales to very large open-vocabulary settings. The paper does not explore whether the gains persist under distribution shift or with truly open-world class sets at test time.
