## Problem

Unsupervised bias discovery in visual recognition systems relies on VLMs to generate textual descriptions of underperforming subgroups, but these methods do not exploit the fact that visual explanations (e.g., GradCAM heatmaps) from biased models systematically highlight spurious features rather than core object features. This paper asks whether incorporating such visual explanations can direct the attention of existing bias discovery and mitigation frameworks toward the actual spurious correlations causing model failures.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and a pretrained image classifier (trained with standard ERM) as input. The classifier produces GradCAM heatmaps that are thresholded at τ (typically 0.5–0.7) to create binary spatial masks, which are then element-wise multiplied with the original images to yield masked versions. These masked images replace the originals when fed into CLIP for cross-modal embeddings or ClipCap for captioning. Outputs include discovered bias-conflicting slices (groups of images sharing a spurious attribute), ranked spurious-attribute keywords, and debiased classifiers trained via GroupDRO or zero-shot prompting with the discovered keywords.

## Method

ViG-Bias introduces a simple mapping h(x) = 1{x ⊙ GradCAM(x) ≥ τ} that masks an image to retain only regions where a biased classifier focuses its attention, which the paper quantitatively confirms (via IoU with LangSAM segmentation masks) to overlap significantly more with spurious features than core features in bias-conflicted cases. This masked image is then substituted for the original in three existing frameworks: ViG-DOMINO feeds masked images into CLIP embeddings before error-aware mixture-model clustering; ViG-FACTS applies the same masking after a bias-amplification regularization stage; and ViG-B2T feeds masked images into ClipCap for captioning before keyword extraction and CLIP-score ranking. For mitigation, the discovered keywords from ViG-B2T are used to construct group annotations for GroupDRO training or to augment zero-shot CLIP prompts, improving worst-group accuracy and reducing the worst-average accuracy gap across CelebA, Waterbirds, and NICO++.

## Results

On bias discovery (Precision@k, k=10), ViG-B2T achieves 97.0% on Waterbirds and 70.0% on CelebA, outperforming B2T (92.0% and 64.0% respectively). ViG-FACTS reaches 66.7% on NICO++90 versus 60.8% for FACTS, and ViG-DOMINO improves DOMINO from 87.0% to 90.0% on CelebA. For bias mitigation via GroupDRO, ViG-B2T groups achieve 90.2% worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (vs. 88.0% for ground-truth GroupDRO) and 91.0% on CelebA (vs. 88.4%), while also yielding the lowest worst-average gap (3.2% and 2.9% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViG-Bias consistently improves upon all three discovery baselines: ViG-DOMINO beats DOMINO by 2–3 points on Waterbirds and CelebA, ViG-B2T outperforms B2T by 5 points on Waterbirds (97% vs 92%) and 6 points on CelebA (70% vs 64%), and ViG-FACTS surpasses FACTS by 5–6 points on the NICO++ variants while matching it on Waterbirds and CelebA (both at 100%). For zero-shot mitigation, ViG-B2T groups achieve 63.1% worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds, a 12.1-point gain over the CLIP base prompt (51.0%) and 8.1 points over B2T groups (55.0%), while also outperforming the group-label prompting of Zhang et al. [24] (50.3%). In GroupDRO-based mitigation, ViG-B2T groups (90.2% WGA on Waterbirds, 91.0% on CelebA) surpass both ground-truth GroupDRO (88.0%, 88.4%) and B2T-inferred GroupDRO (88.3%, 87.8%), and substantially outperform JTT (86.3%, 82.0%) which does not use group information. The method also achieves the lowest worst-average gap across all mitigation settings (2.9–3.2%), indicating the most balanced performance across subgroups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a plug-and-play enhancement to three existing unsupervised bias discovery frameworks—DOMINO (cross-modal embedding clustering), FACTS (bias amplification followed by slicing), and Bias-to-Text (VLM-based captioning for keyword extraction)—by inserting a visual explanation preprocessing step (GradCAM or ScoreCAM heatmaps) before cross-modal embedding or captioning. It builds on Krishnakumar et al.'s qualitative observation that biased models' GradCAM heatmaps focus on spurious features and Singla et al.'s demonstration that such heatmaps can distinguish spurious from core features, but provides the first systematic quantitative confirmation and the first integration of this signal into automated bias discovery and mitigation pipelines. The mitigation side extends GroupDRO and zero-shot prompting by using visually grounded keywords to define groups, rather than relying on ground-truth annotations or ungrounded B2T keywords.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the simplicity and generality of the approach: a single thresholded heatmap multiplication can be inserted into any existing CLIP-based or VLM-based bias discovery pipeline without architectural changes, yielding consistent (if modest) gains across three datasets. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the method only captures spatially localized spurious correlations (e.g., a face region vs. hair region) and cannot detect global distributional shifts like illumination or noise differences. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, 3D, or non-visual modalities, and how to handle spurious correlations that are not spatially separable in the input image.
