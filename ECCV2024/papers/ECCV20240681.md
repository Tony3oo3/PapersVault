## Problem

Model-based 6D pose estimation of unseen objects from a single RGB image has traditionally required large-scale, task-specific training to generalize to new objects and bridge the synthetic-to-real domain gap. Existing training-free approaches either rely on RGB-D inputs or achieve significantly lower accuracy. This paper asks whether a frozen, self-supervised vision foundation model can replace the need for any object- or task-specific training while still achieving state-of-the-art accuracy.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image, per-instance segmentation masks (produced by CNOS from the same 3D models), and known camera intrinsics, and outputs the 6D pose (3D rotation and 3D translation) for every visible instance of the target objects. The offline onboarding stage is constrained to 5 minutes on 1 GPU, during which 800 RGB-D templates of size 420×420 px are rendered from the texture-mapped 3D mesh. The method is designed to handle symmetric and texture-less objects, which are notoriously ambiguous for pose estimation.

## Method

FoundPose is a training-free pipeline that integrates frozen DINOv2 ViT-L patch descriptors (from layer 18, which balances positional and semantic information) into classical computer vision techniques. During onboarding, 800 rendered templates have their 14×14 px patch descriptors extracted, projected to 256 PCA components, and registered in 3D via rendered depth; a bag-of-words index (2048 k-means visual words with soft assignment) is built for efficient template retrieval. At inference, the query crop is matched against retrieved templates via one-way kNN search of patch descriptors to establish 2D-3D correspondences, from which a coarse pose is solved by EPnP-RANSAC. A featuremetric refinement stage then optimizes the pose via Levenberg-Marquardt, minimizing a robust cost between DINOv2 patch descriptors sampled from the query feature map and those of the template, compensating for the coarse 14×14 px patch granularity. The key insight is that intermediate-layer DINOv2 descriptors carry sufficient 2D positional information to produce geometrically consistent correspondences even on symmetric, texture-less objects where purely semantic features fail.

## Results

On the seven core BOP datasets, FoundPose (coarse, no refinement, training-free) achieves 37.2 average AR, outperforming GigaPose (27.6), GenFlow (23.5), and MegaPose (20.8) by +10, +14, and +16 AR respectively, while running 1.7 s per image. Adding the featuremetric refinement raises this to 42.6 AR, and combining it with the MegaPose render-and-compare refiner on 5 pose hypotheses yields the best RGB-only result of 59.6 AR (20.5 s). The method requires no object- or task-specific training, using only frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 features from layer 18.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundPose (coarse, training-free) outperforms all three main RGB baselines by large margins: +10 AR over GigaPose (37.2 vs 27.6), +14 AR over GenFlow (37.2 vs 23.5), and +16 AR over MegaPose (37.2 vs 20.8), while also being 9× faster than MegaPose's coarse stage (1.7 s vs 15.5 s). In the best single-hypothesis refinement setting, FoundPose + Feat.+MegaPose reaches 55.0 AR, edging out GigaPose + MegaPose (54.7) and MegaPose + MegaPose (50.9). In the 5-hypothesis setting, FoundPose achieves the top RGB-only score of 59.6 AR, beating GigaPose + MegaPose (57.9), GenFlow (57.1), and MegaPose (54.7). Against the two other training-free methods, FoundPose dominates: it beats OSOP by +8.4, +1.6, and +12.0 AR on LM-O, HB, and YCB-V respectively, and beats ZS6D by +9.8, +12.8, and +12.8 AR on LM-O, T-LESS, and YCB-V. Notably, FoundPose is the only method in the table that requires no task-specific training at all, yet matches or exceeds methods trained on millions of synthetic images.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoundPose repurposes two classical computer vision techniques—the 2003 bag-of-words image retrieval framework and the Lucas-Kanade-style photometric refinement—by replacing their hand-crafted features with frozen DINOv2 patch descriptors. This departs sharply from the dominant paradigm in unseen-object pose estimation, where methods like MegaPose, GigaPose, and GenFlow train on millions of synthetic images to bridge the synthetic-to-real gap. The paper also extends the line of work on DINOv2-based semantic correspondences (Goodwin et al., 2024) by demonstrating that intermediate-layer descriptors, rather than last-layer features, are the key to establishing geometrically consistent synthetic-to-real matches, particularly for symmetric and texture-less objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a training-free pipeline combining a frozen foundation model with classical CV achieves state-of-the-art RGB-only pose estimation challenges the field's working assumption that large-scale task-specific training is necessary to close the domain gap. Practically, the 5-minute onboarding, 25× lower memory footprint, and 1.7 s inference time make the method immediately deployable in robotics and mixed-reality settings where rapid object onboarding is critical. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view or video settings, how it behaves when 3D models are imperfect or untextured, and whether the intermediate-layer finding generalizes to other foundation models beyond DINOv2.
