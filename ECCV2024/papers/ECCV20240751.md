## Problem

Existing action anticipation methods rely on sequence models (LSTMs, causal transformers) to extrapolate visual and temporal information but cannot explicitly model the semantic connectivity between actions beyond the immediate video context. This limits their ability to reason about co-occurring action sequences and draw on structured knowledge of how actions relate to each other. The paper asks whether meaningful semanticity can be encoded between action representations in a purely visual model.

## Input / Output

S-GEAR consumes a video frame sequence (384×384 for Epic-Kitchens, 224×224 for EGTEA/50 Salads) sampled at 1 fps over a 10–15 s observation window, plus verb+noun action labels that are encoded by a frozen Sentence Transformer to produce fixed language prototypes. The output is a class probability distribution over action classes, obtained by computing cosine similarities between the decoder's predicted future features and learnable visual prototypes, applying softmax, and aggregating prototype vectors through a learnable weighted sum before a linear classification head.

## Method

S-GEAR uses a ViT-B encoder, a Temporal Context Aggregator (TCA) that propagates full patch-level causal context across frames, a Prototype Attention (PA) block that attends over learnable visual prototypes using the class token as query, and a Causal Transformer decoder that produces future action features. The core novelty is a semantic guiding policy: a set of fixed language prototypes (from Sentence Transformer) and a set of learnable visual prototypes are linked through a common communication space where each action encoding is represented by its vector of cosine similarities to all prototypes, and a semantic loss (L_Sem) minimizes the L1 distance between the visual and language relative-position vectors without any direct cross-modal alignment. A L2 regularization term pulls each predicted feature toward its ground-truth visual prototype, while the classification head uses cosine attention (softmax over prototype similarities) to aggregate prototypes into a single representation for the final prediction. Training jointly optimizes L_Sem, cross-entropy on future and past labels, and a feature-matching loss, with the visual prototypes initialized from an action-recognition-trained encoder.

## Results

On Epic-Kitchens 55 (RGB+Obj+Flow, τa=1s), S-GEAR achieves 22.7% Top-1 and 43.2% Top-5 Accuracy, surpassing the previous best (DCR) by 3.5 and 2.0 points respectively. On Epic-Kitchens 100 (RGB+Obj, τa=1s), S-GEAR-4B reaches 19.9% action Top-5 Recall on validation, a +1.6 gain over DCR (18.3%). On EGTEA Gaze+ (RGB, τa=0.5s), S-GEAR attains 45.7% Top-1 Accuracy (+2.7 over AVT), and on 50 Salads it achieves up to 41.0% Top-1 Accuracy, outperforming SRL by 3.1 points at the shortest anticipation horizon.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-GEAR consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across the four benchmarks. On EK55 multimodal, it beats DCR (the strongest prior method) by 3.5 Top-1 and 2.0 Top-5, and surpasses AVT+ by 6.1 Top-1. On EK100, S-GEAR-4B (19.9% action Top-5 Recall) edges out DCR (18.3%) and AFFT (18.5%), though the margin is smaller than on EK55. On EGTEA Gaze+, S-GEAR using only RGB beats AVT by 2.7 Top-1 points and HRO by 0.4 Top-5 points despite HRO using RGB+Flow+Obj. On 50 Salads, S-GEAR wins 5 out of 8 anticipation scenarios, with the largest gain (+3.1) over SRL at τa=10%, but slightly trails Time-Cond. [19] at τa=50%, τo=20% (15.3 vs. 16.0), acknowledging that Time-Cond. is specifically designed for long-term anticipation.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-GEAR builds directly on the Anticipative Video Transformer (AVT) [15] architecture (ViT encoder + causal transformer decoder) and extends it with two novel modules—Temporal Context Aggregator and Prototype Attention—that inject semantic structure into the anticipation pipeline. The key conceptual departure from prior anticipation work (RU-LSTM, SRL, DCR, AFFT) is the explicit modeling of semantic interconnectivity between actions, inspired by cognitive science, rather than relying solely on visual-temporal extrapolation. The paper also distinguishes itself from vision-language alignment methods (CLIP, LiT) by transferring geometric associations between action prototypes from language to vision without performing direct cross-modal alignment, instead using a common communication space based on relative cosine-similarity positions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that encoding semantic relationships between actions—transferred from language models—consistently improves anticipation across egocentric and exocentric, short-term and long-term settings, suggesting that anticipation benefits from structured knowledge about how actions co-occur beyond what is visible in the immediate video context. Open questions remain: the method lacks an in-built multimodal fusion mechanism, does not account for the temporal order of co-occurrences (only their existence), and the reliance on a frozen Sentence Transformer for language prototypes limits adaptability. The finding that visual prototypes outperform direct use of language prototypes highlights the tension between semantic structure and visual grounding, a trade-off likely relevant to broader vision-language anticipation research.
