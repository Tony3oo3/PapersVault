## Problem

Existing normalization-based DG-ReID methods combine IN and BN to balance generalization and discrimination, but the normalization layers still overfit to source domains: at test time, BN population statistics (from source) and IN statistics (from target) create a distribution mismatch, and learnable affine parameters further entrench source-specific information. The paper identifies that the normalization layers, not the weight matrix, are the root bottleneck for domain generalization, and that current approaches lack a mechanism to actively train the model to handle this source-to-target statistical gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB person images resized to 256×128, processed in mini-batches of 64 (16 identities × 4 images each), and produces a person feature embedding used for re-identification ranking via CMC and mAP. During training, each mini-batch is fed through the same weight matrix twice with different normalization strategies; during testing, each target image passes through both networks K times (once per source domain's BN buffer), and the 2K resulting feature vectors are averaged to form the final embedding.

## Method

ReNorm is a purely normalization-based framework that performs two forward propagations per iteration on a shared ResNet50 weight matrix. In the first pass, Remix Normalization (RN) standardizes features by averaging the IN and BN mean/variance computed from the current mini-batch, while storing per-domain BN population statistics via EMA. In the second pass, Emulation Normalization (EN) treats the current source domain as a simulated target: it combines IN statistics from the current input with BN population statistics drawn from a randomly selected *different* source domain, using learnable balancing weights, thereby forcing the weight matrix to learn how to transfer feature extraction across distribution shifts. Domain Frozen (DF) complements both passes by freezing the affine parameters (γ, β) in every normalization layer—set to identity for EN—preventing these small, fragile parameters from overfitting to source-specific channel statistics. Training uses standard triplet and cross-entropy losses summed across both networks, with no additional loss terms or architectural modifications beyond the normalization design.

## Results

Under Protocol-1 (training on M+C2+C3+CS, testing on four small-scale datasets), ReNorm achieves an average mAP of 78.2% and Rank-1 of 71.2%, outperforming the previous best (CINorm and PAOA at 76.2% mAP / 67.9% Rank-1) by 2.0% mAP and 3.3% Rank-1. Under Protocol-2 (leave-one-out, training sets only), ReNorm reaches 47.3% mAP and 63.1% Rank-1 on average, surpassing ACL (45.3% / 59.0%) by 2.0% mAP and 4.1% Rank-1. Under Protocol-3 (all images used), ReNorm attains 52.4% mAP and 67.2% Rank-1, exceeding PAOA (50.7% / 64.3%) by 1.7% mAP and 2.9% Rank-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReNorm outperforms all ten compared baselines on the average mAP and Rank-1 across all three protocols. Under Protocol-1, it surpasses the strongest prior methods CINorm (76.2% mAP) and PAOA (76.2% mAP) by 2.0% mAP and 3.3% Rank-1, with the largest individual gains on GRID (+7.6% mAP over CINorm) and VIPeR (+0.4% mAP over PAOA). Under Protocol-2, ReNorm leads ACL (45.3% mAP) by 2.0% mAP and 4.1% Rank-1, with a particularly large margin of 3.1% mAP and 5.7% Rank-1 when MSMT17 is the target domain. Under Protocol-3, it exceeds PAOA (50.7% mAP) by 1.7% mAP and 2.9% Rank-1. Notably, ReNorm achieves the best per-dataset mAP on PRID (81.3%), GRID (67.5%), and VIPeR (77.0%) under Protocol-1, while CINorm retains a slight edge on iLIDs (88.0% vs. 87.0%). The consistent wins across all target domains and protocols confirm that the normalization-only design generalizes robustly without requiring additional loss functions or architectural modifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the normalization-based line of work in DG-ReID (SNR, MetaBIN, META, CINorm, ACL, PAOA), which all combine Batch Normalization and Instance Normalization in a single forward pass to balance discrimination and generalization. The key departure is the two-forward-pass design: Remix Normalization (RN) sums IN and BN statistics within the same domain (similar in spirit to prior IN+BN hybrids), while Emulation Normalization (EN) deliberately mixes IN statistics from the current domain with BN population statistics from a *different* source domain, effectively simulating the train-test distribution mismatch during training. The Domain Frozen strategy (freezing affine parameters) is a simpler but effective complement that prior normalization-based DG-ReID methods did not explicitly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that careful normalization-layer design alone—without additional loss functions, meta-learning, or ensemble mechanisms—can match or exceed more complex DG-ReID pipelines, offering a lightweight and easily integrable alternative for practitioners. The two-forward-pass training overhead and the 2K forward passes at test time are practical costs, and the method is validated only on ResNet50 CNN backbones, leaving open whether the approach transfers to transformer-based architectures or single-source-domain settings. A broader open question is whether the 'emulate the inference mismatch during training' principle generalizes beyond ReID to other domain-generalization tasks where normalization layers encode domain-specific statistics.
