## Problem

Text-based person search requires aligning fine-grained human part features between free-form text queries and images, but existing methods either rely on heuristic horizontal cropping (fragile to occlusion and pose) or demand external part-level supervision (attribute segmentation, pose estimation). The core gap is discovering distinctive body parts and establishing their cross-modal correspondences without any part-level annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240563/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a free-form text query (up to 77 BPE tokens) and a gallery of person images resized to 384×128, processed through a pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. It produces a ranked list of gallery images scored by the sum of global cosine similarity and a query-weighted part similarity, and additionally exposes 8 interpretable part-level correspondences between the query and each retrieved image, making retrieval decisions auditable at the body-part level.

## Method

PLOT introduces a Part Slot Attention (PSA) block that iteratively refines 8 shared learnable part slots over 5 rounds: each slot computes softmax-normalized attention over patch or text tokens (normalizing across slots to enforce competition), aggregates the weighted tokens, and updates its state through a GRU followed by an MLP residual. Because the same initial slots are shared between the visual and textual discovery modules, part embeddings from slot k in both modalities are trained to represent the same body part, yielding unsupervised cross-modal correspondence. A Text-based Dynamic Part Attention (TDPA) module maps the textual global embedding through an MLP+softmax to produce per-slot weights that re-weight the K part cosine similarities, so each query adaptively emphasizes the parts it actually describes. Training combines a global InfoNCE + identity + CMPM loss, a part-level InfoNCE (PartNCE) that uses the TDPA-weighted similarity as its score function, a part identity loss on concatenated part embeddings, and an auxiliary cross-modal masked language modeling loss, all optimized end-to-end with a 5e-6 learning rate on the CLIP encoders.

## Results

PLOT achieves R@1 of 75.28%, 65.76%, and 61.80% on CUHK-PEDES, ICFG-PEDES, and RSTPReid respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. On CUHK-PEDES, the full metrics are R@1=75.28%, R@5=90.42%, R@10=94.12%. The method improves over the previous best (IRRA) by 1.9, 1.7, and 1.6 percentage points on R@1 across the three datasets, and by a larger margin over earlier part-based methods such as CFine (+5.77%p on CUHK-PEDES R@1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLOT outperforms all 20 compared baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the previous state-of-the-art IRRA [7] (CVPR 2023), PLOT gains +1.9%p on CUHK-PEDES R@1 (75.28 vs. 73.38), +1.7%p on ICFG-PEDES (65.76 vs. 64.06), and +1.6%p on RSTPReid (61.80 vs. 60.20). The margin over the next-strongest CLIP-based method CFine [29] is substantially larger at +5.77%p on CUHK-PEDES R@1. Part-based methods that rely on heuristic horizontal cropping (TIPCB [2], SRCF [22]) or external pose/attribute tools (ViTAA [24], PMA [8]) trail by 10–20%p, confirming that learned part discovery via slot attention captures more discriminative features. In the ablation (Table 3), PLOT's slot-attention part discovery also beats the querying-transformer approach PAT [14] by +2.52%p on R@1, showing that the competitive slot mechanism yields better spatially disentangled parts than conventional cross-attention queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLOT repurposes slot attention from object-centric learning [15] for cross-modal part discovery in person search, adapting the iterative slot-competition and GRU-update mechanism to aggregate patch/text tokens into part embeddings. It departs from the dominant heuristic part paradigm (equi-horizontal cropping as in TIPCB, SRCF) and from learning-based approaches that rely on external tools like pose estimation or attribute segmentation (PMA, ViTAA), instead discovering parts entirely through learned slot competition. The CLIP backbone follows the recent IRRA [7] and CFine [29] line of work, but PLOT extends these global-only methods by adding a part-level discovery and alignment layer, with the key conceptual novelty of sharing part slots across modalities to establish correspondence without any part-level supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shared-slot mechanism provides a clean, supervision-free way to obtain interpretable part-level correspondences between text queries and retrieved images, which is valuable for both retrieval accuracy and explainability in person search. The acknowledged limitation—that some slots may capture irrelevant regions and TDPA only partially mitigates this—points to an open question about how to explicitly prune or regularize uninformative slots. More broadly, the idea of using a shared set of learnable slots as a cross-modal correspondence bridge could generalize to other fine-grained retrieval tasks beyond person search, though the paper does not explore this.
