## Problem

The state-of-the-art self-supervised temporal video alignment method, CASA, operates directly on 3D skeleton coordinates and performs self-attention only in the temporal domain, making it highly sensitive to noisy and missing keypoints from pose estimation. No prior work has explored 2D skeleton heatmap inputs or multi-modality fusion for temporal video alignment, leaving open the question of whether 2D representations with spatial self-attention can match or exceed 3D-coordinate-based methods while being more robust.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sequences of 2D skeleton heatmaps (K×H×W per frame, combining joint and limb Gaussian maps, cropped to 30×30 spatial dimensions) as primary input, and optionally RGB video frames for the multi-modality variant. It produces frame-level latent embeddings that are matched via contrastive regression to predict dense frame-to-frame correspondences between an original and an augmented sequence. These embeddings are then frozen and used with a linear probe (SVM or linear regressor) for downstream tasks: phase classification, phase progression scoring, Kendall's Tau alignment, and fine-grained frame retrieval (AP@K).

## Method

The core contribution is a ViViT-based video transformer that performs self-attention in both the spatial domain (across h×w non-overlapping patches within each heatmap frame) and the temporal domain (across frame-level tokens via global average pooling), followed by a cross-attention module to capture interactions between the original and augmented sequences. Self-supervised training is driven by simple 2D skeleton heatmap augmentations—temporal subsampling, Gaussian position noise, limb rotation, and horizontal flipping—which generate correspondence labels without requiring 3D pose data. The matching objective uses contrastive regression with a temperature parameter to encourage temporally close frames to map to nearby latent points, minimizing the squared error between predicted and ground-truth frame indices. For multi-modality fusion, separate encoders process 2D skeleton heatmaps and RGB video frames independently, and their features are combined via concatenation and a linear projection (late fusion), trained in two stages: first each modality is trained with its own alignment loss, then the fused model is trained jointly.

## Results

On phase classification with 100% labels, LA2DS achieves 92.63% on Penn Action, 34.73% on IKEA ASM, and 70.12% on H2O, outperforming the strongest skeleton-based baseline CASA (92.20%, 31.06%, 68.78%) and all RGB-based baselines. For fine-grained frame retrieval (AP@15), LA2DS reaches 91.35% on Penn Action, 31.56% on IKEA ASM, and 61.75% on H2O, again leading all competitors. The multi-modality fusion variant (2D heatmaps + RGB) further improves to 93.57% classification and 92.15% retrieval on Penn Action, establishing state-of-the-art across all metrics and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LA2DS outperforms all six temporal video alignment baselines (SAL, TCN, TCC, LAV, VAVA, CASA) on phase classification across all three datasets. The margin over CASA is narrow on Penn Action (92.63% vs 92.20%) but substantial on IKEA ASM (34.73% vs 31.06%, +3.67%) and H2O (70.12% vs 68.78%, +1.34%). Against RGB-based methods (LAV, VAVA), LA2DS leads by 8+ points on Penn Action and H2O, though on IKEA ASM with limited labels, VAVA (33.79% at 50% labels) slightly edges LA2DS (32.56%), which the authors attribute to similar skeleton shapes across IKEA actions requiring RGB context. For frame retrieval (AP@15), LA2DS beats CASA by 2.28 points on Penn Action (91.35 vs 89.07) and 2.95 points on H2O (61.75 vs 59.01). Against recent RGB-based methods on IKEA ASM (Table 9), LA2DS loses to VSP on classification (34.73% vs 47.52% Acc@1.0) but wins on retrieval (31.56% vs 30.23% AP@5), and beats STEPs on Acc@0.1 (26.43% vs 28.59%—actually loses here) while the authors note their compact skeleton input lacks context details that full RGB provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CASA (Kwon et al., CVPR 2022), the state-of-the-art self-supervised temporal video alignment method that operates on 3D skeleton coordinates with temporal-only self-attention. It departs from CASA in three key ways: replacing 3D coordinates with 2D skeleton heatmaps (inspired by Duan et al.'s heatmap representation for action recognition), adding spatial self-attention via a ViViT video transformer, and introducing a multi-modality fusion branch with RGB video. The paper positions itself as the first to use 2D skeleton heatmap inputs and the first to explore multi-modality fusion specifically for temporal video alignment, while the broader task lineage traces back to TCN, TCC, LAV, and VAVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the robustness gap: 2D Gaussian heatmap representations degrade far less than 3D coordinate representations under missing or noisy keypoints (~1% vs ~8% drop at 100% keypoint removal), making the approach more deployable in real-world settings where pose estimation is imperfect. The multi-modality fusion (2D heatmaps + RGB) achieves SOTA across all metrics and datasets, and the privacy-preserving nature of skeleton-based inputs is a meaningful advantage for human activity understanding applications. An acknowledged limitation is that on IKEA ASM with limited labels, RGB-based methods can outperform skeleton-based methods when different actions share similar skeleton patterns, suggesting that context-rich modalities remain important for highly repetitive procedural tasks.
