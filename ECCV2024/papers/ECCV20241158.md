## Problem

Existing T2I personalization methods that finetune diffusion models (e.g., DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) constrain instance images and scene text prompts independently, causing the model to lose pretrained knowledge of both subject classes and scene contexts. This leads to a persistent trade-off: generated images either preserve the subject's appearance but ignore the requested scene, or render the scene but fail to maintain the specific instance's identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241158/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ComFusion operates in a one-shot setting (single instance image, though up to five are supported) on top of Stable Diffusion 1.5. The text prompt follows the format 'a [identifier] [class noun] [scene]' (e.g., 'a sks dog in the rain'), where the identifier is a unique token learned during finetuning. The output is a 512×512 RGB image of the specific subject rendered in the described scene, generated via standard DDIM sampling at inference time.

## Method

ComFusion introduces a two-stream training objective. The composite stream combines an instance finetune loss with a novel class-scene prior loss that extends DreamBooth's class prior by generating prior images from composite texts like 'a dog in the rain,' thereby preserving the pretrained model's scene knowledge alongside class knowledge. The fusion stream generates coarse images by partially denoising class-scene prior latents (with noise level t sampled from [0.2T, 0.8T]) using the instance-scene text over τ=3 DDIM steps, then applies a dual visual-textual matching loss: DINO cosine similarity between the coarse image and the instance image (enforcing instance fidelity) and CLIP image-text cosine similarity between the coarse image and the class-scene text (enforcing scene fidelity). This coarse-denoising-and-matching mechanism explicitly fuses the subject's visual identity with the scene's textual description, mitigating the catastrophic neglecting problem where one modality dominates the other.

## Results

On the combined TI + DreamBooth dataset (25 subjects × 15 scenes, 3750 generated images per method), ComFusion achieves the best scores on all three metrics: DINO 0.658, CLIP-I 0.814 (instance fidelity), and CLIP-T 0.321 (scene fidelity). The closest baseline, Break-A-Scene, scores 0.632 / 0.771 / 0.294 respectively, while DreamBooth reaches 0.619 / 0.752 / 0.229. In a 12-user preference study (90,000 votes), ComFusion is preferred for instance fidelity 55.6% of the time and for scene fidelity 62.1% of the time, substantially ahead of the next-best method Break-A-Scene (34.5% / 20.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComFusion outperforms all six baselines on every metric. Against the strongest competitor Break-A-Scene [2], ComFusion gains +0.026 DINO, +0.043 CLIP-I, and +0.027 CLIP-T, with the largest relative improvement in scene fidelity (CLIP-T: 0.321 vs. 0.294, ~9% relative gain). DreamBooth [39] and CD [25] are competitive on instance fidelity (DINO 0.619 and 0.615) but lag significantly on scene fidelity (CLIP-T 0.229 and 0.205), confirming that class-only prior loss is insufficient for scene-specific generation. TI [12] and XTI [51] perform worst overall (DINO 0.465 and 0.435), as their prompt-embedding-only finetuning fails to preserve both subject identity and scene context. ELITE [53] achieves the highest CLIP-T among non-finetuning methods (0.249) but has the lowest DINO (0.405), indicating it captures scene text at the cost of instance appearance. In the user study, ComFusion's 55.6% instance-fidelity preference rate is 1.6× that of Break-A-Scene (34.5%), and its 62.1% scene-fidelity rate is 3.1× that of Break-A-Scene (20.2%), underscoring a clear perceptual advantage in both dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ComFusion extends the finetuning-based T2I personalization lineage initiated by DreamBooth and Textual Inversion, which map a subject to a unique identifier token while regularizing against language drift. Its key conceptual departure is replacing DreamBooth's class-only prior loss with a class-scene prior loss that explicitly preserves the pretrained model's knowledge of specific scene contexts, and adding a fusion stream that uses coarse DDIM denoising to generate intermediate images supervised by DINO (instance) and CLIP (scene) matching losses. This directly addresses the 'catastrophic neglecting' problem identified in Attend-and-Excite, where few-shot finetuning causes the model to drop scene or subject information that the large-scale pretrained model originally handled well.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The class-scene prior loss is a simple but effective regularization that practitioners can adopt in any DreamBooth-style pipeline to improve scene fidelity without sacrificing instance fidelity, and the visual-textual matching loss via coarse denoising offers a practical mechanism for balancing the two. However, the evaluation is limited to Stable Diffusion 1.5, 25 subjects, and 15 scenes in a primarily one-shot setting, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, Flux), more complex multi-subject compositions, or video generation. The paper also does not explore how the method interacts with other recent personalization techniques like LoRA-based approaches or multi-concept customization.
