## Problem

Existing unsupervised co-salient object detection (CoSOD) methods either mine only local patch-level correspondences (e.g., clustering ViT patch descriptors) or rely on computationally heavy off-the-shelf components (SAM, STEGO, Stable Diffusion), resulting in poor performance or impractical inference speeds. The core gap is the inefficient utilization of unlabeled data at multiple scales—both patch-level and region-level feature correspondences remain underexploited for self-supervised CoSOD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240228/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a group of N RGB images (each resized to 224×224) containing co-occurring salient objects of a specific class and produces a binary co-salient segmentation mask per image. At inference, all images in the group are processed simultaneously through a frozen DINO ViT-B backbone (patch size 8, 768-dim descriptors), and the network requires only 1.77M trainable parameters. Inference runs at 20.5 FPS without DenseCRF post-processing and 4.1 FPS with it, on a single GPU.

## Method

SCoSPARC is a two-stage self-supervised pipeline. In Stage 1, a lightweight network computes cross-attention maps by extracting DINO ViT patch features, passing them through a residual block, and computing a feature similarity matrix via learned key/query projections; the network is trained with a co-occurrence loss (enforcing foreground feature similarity across images and foreground-background dissimilarity within each image) and a saliency loss (aligning predictions with DINO's class-token self-attention maps), followed by a confidence-based adaptive thresholding step that replaces the conventional fixed 0.5 threshold. In Stage 2, connected-component labeling identifies sub-regions in the intermediate masks, and regions whose averaged patch embeddings are dissimilar (cosine distance below a threshold) to the group-level average foreground token are eliminated, enforcing region-level semantic consistency. A final DenseCRF pass enforces spatial coherence and sharpens boundaries.

## Results

On the CoCA dataset, SCoSPARC (trained on COCO9213 + DUTS-Class) achieves an Fβmax of 0.614 and MAE of 0.092, yielding a 13.7% F-measure gain over the SOTA unsupervised model US-CoSOD (0.546) and a 4.6% F-measure gain over the supervised DCFM (0.598). On Cosal2015, it reaches Fβmax 0.869 and MAE 0.062, and on CoSOD3k, Fβmax 0.827 and MAE 0.064. The model uses only 1.77M trainable parameters (vs. 542.9M for DCFM) and runs at 20.5 FPS without DenseCRF, making it substantially lighter than competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCoSPARC outperforms all unsupervised CoSOD baselines by a wide margin: it gains 13.7% Fβmax over US-CoSOD (0.614 vs. 0.546) on CoCA, 16.5% over ZS-CoSOD (0.614 vs. 0.549), and 19.2% over TokenCut (0.614 vs. 0.467). Among supervised methods, SCoSPARC beats DCFM (0.614 vs. 0.598 Fβmax on CoCA), CoRP (0.614 vs. 0.564), UFO (0.614 vs. 0.571), MCLC (0.614 vs. 0.590), and GEM (0.614 vs. 0.599) on CoCA, despite requiring no labeled training data. However, it trails the strongest supervised models GCoNet+ (0.637) and DMT (0.619) on CoCA Fβmax, and falls behind GCoNet+, DMT, MCLC, and GEM on Cosal2015 and CoSOD3k Fβmax. The paper also shows that with only 50% or 75% of training labels, GCoNet+ degrades significantly (Fβmax drops to 0.534 and 0.547 on CoCA respectively), while SCoSPARC at 0% labels still achieves 0.614, highlighting its robustness to label scarcity. In terms of efficiency, SCoSPARC uses only 1.77M trainable parameters and 158 GFLOPs, compared to 542.9M/63.4 GFLOPs for DCFM and 629M/354 GFLOPs for US-CoSOD, and runs at 20.5 FPS (without DenseCRF) versus 0.50 FPS for SegSwap and 0.23 FPS for DVFDVD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DINO's self-supervised ViT features and self-attention maps as the backbone for co-salient object detection, extending the line of work showing that DINO's attention heads localize salient regions. It departs from prior unsupervised CoSOD methods (DVFDVD, US-CoSOD, ZS-CoSOD) by replacing heavy off-the-shelf components (SAM, STEGO, Stable Diffusion) with a lightweight trained network that explicitly models cross-image patch-level feature correspondences via a co-occurrence and saliency loss. The two-stage design—patch-level correspondences followed by region-level filtering via connected-component analysis—is a conceptual departure from single-scale approaches, and the confidence-based adaptive thresholding is novel for the CoSOD setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or superior CoSOD performance without any segmentation annotations, using only 1.77M trainable parameters versus 542.9M for the prior SOTA unsupervised method, making real-time deployment feasible. The finding that a self-supervised model can outperform several fully supervised CoSOD methods (DCFM, CoRP, UFO) on standard benchmarks challenges the assumption that labeled data is necessary for strong group-based segmentation. Open questions include whether the multi-scale correspondence strategy generalizes to other group-based tasks (co-segmentation, video saliency), how performance scales with group size, and whether the DenseCRF post-processing can be replaced by a learned module for further speedup.
