## Problem

Adapting pre-trained image-language models to video QA requires both efficient frame sampling and effective cross-modal alignment, but prior approaches treat these separately: uniform sampling misses question-critical frames, while dedicated keyframe localizers (e.g., SeViLA) add substantial parameters and inference latency. No existing method jointly learns question-guided frame selection and cross-modal distillation in a unified, lightweight framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241653/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes uniformly sampled video frames (up to 32 frames encoded by a frozen ViT-G 1B visual encoder) and a text question as input. The Frame-Prompter reduces this to 1–8 selected frames before cross-modal fusion, and the frozen Flan-T5 XL (3B) LLM produces a multi-choice answer. Inference is evaluated on a single A100 GPU with batch size 4, achieving 64–227 ms per video depending on frame count, compared to 301–306 ms for SeViLA.

## Method

ViLA introduces two jointly trained modules: (1) a text-guided Frame-Prompter that performs differentiable frame selection via Gumbel-Softmax over segmented uniform sampling, where cross-attention with the question text and backpropagated VQA loss guide the selector toward question-relevant frames; and (2) a QFormer-Distiller that applies teacher-student knowledge distillation on top of the BLIP-2 QFormer, where a teacher QFormer processes all 32 frames and a student QFormer processes only the Frame-Prompter-selected frames, with an MSE distillation loss through a simple FC+LN decoder. The entire pipeline (Frame-Prompter + QFormer-Distiller) is trained end-to-end with a combined VQA cross-entropy loss and distillation loss, while the visual encoder and LLM remain frozen (or optionally LoRA-fine-tuned). This design keeps the introduced parameters at 188M (vs. 376M for SeViLA) while improving accuracy on temporal and causal question types.

## Results

On NExT-QA, ViLA with 4 frames (8-to-4 distillation) achieves 74.3% average accuracy (71.0% Temporal, 72.9% Causal, 82.7% Description), outperforming SeViLA (73.4%) by 0.9% while running 3.04× faster (99 ms vs 301 ms per video). With LoRA fine-tuning of the LLM, ViLA reaches 75.1% average on NExT-QA with only 4 frames. On STAR, ViLA (4 frames) achieves 67.1% average, beating SeViLA (64.9%) by 2.2% with 3.04× speedup, and on TVQA it improves over SeViLA by 1.8% (63.4% vs 61.6%). The model also outperforms BLIP-2 (4 frames) by 1.3% on NExT-QA and 5.1% on STAR while using the same frame budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViLA outperforms all nine baselines across every benchmark. Against the closest competitor SeViLA [65] (NeurIPS 2023), ViLA (4 frames) improves NExT-QA average by 0.9% (74.3% vs 73.3%), STAR average by 2.2% (67.1% vs 64.9%), TVQA by 1.8% (63.4% vs 61.6%), How2QA by 0.3% (83.9% vs 83.6%), and VLEP by 0.7% (69.6% vs 68.9%), while using half the parameters (188M vs 376M) and achieving 1.35–4.2× inference speedup. Against BLIP-2 [27] (ICML 2023) with the same 4-frame budget, ViLA gains 2.8% on NExT-QA (74.3% vs 71.5%), 5.1% on STAR (67.1% vs 62.0%), 1.7% on How2QA, 2.6% on VLEP, and 8.9% on TVQA. Earlier methods (Just Ask [58], All-in-One [47], MIST [16], HiTeA [62], InternVideo [52]) are surpassed by 11–24% on NExT-QA average, reflecting the advantage of the QFormer-based cross-modal alignment over prior video-language architectures. On STAR, ViLA also beats Flamingo-9B 4-shot [1] (67.1% vs 42.8%) and FrozenBiLM [59] on TVQA (63.4% vs 57.5%). Notably, ViLA with only 2 frames already exceeds BLIP-2 with 4 frames on NExT-QA (72.8% vs 71.5%) and outperforms SeViLA 4-frames on VLEP (69.2% vs 68.9%) with 4.2× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViLA extends the BLIP-2/BLIP QFormer architecture for cross-modal alignment by adding a teacher-student distillation layer (QFormer-Distiller) and a text-guided Frame-Prompter that uses Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable frame selection. It departs from SeViLA's approach of training a separate keyframe localizer by integrating frame selection directly into the end-to-end QFormer pipeline, making it lighter and more inference-friendly. The Frame-Prompter is supervised by the VQA loss through backpropagation, so question-relevant gradients flow directly to the frame selector without an auxiliary localization head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that efficient frame selection and cross-modal distillation can be jointly optimized to achieve 3× inference speedup while improving accuracy over the prior SOTA (SeViLA) across five video QA benchmarks, which is practically significant for real-time video understanding. The finding that 2 ViLA frames outperform 4 SeViLA frames on VLEP suggests the quality of selected frames matters more than quantity. Limitations include evaluation only on LLMs up to 13B parameters and a 32-frame teacher ceiling, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer videos or larger language models.
