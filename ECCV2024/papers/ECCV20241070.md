## Problem

Point cloud completion requires inferring unknown missing shapes from partial observations, but existing MLP-based methods produce ambiguous seeds from incomplete global features, and Transformer-based set-to-set translation methods are computationally heavy (e.g., PoinTr uses 28.9M parameters). The paper identifies that partial and missing shapes are inherently correlated and proposes that this correlation can be exploited through simple linear operations in a suitable feature space, rather than requiring complex attention-based set-to-set prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241070/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an incomplete point cloud resampled to 2,048 points and produces a dense complete point cloud at target resolution (16,384 points on PCN, 8,192 on ShapeNet). The pipeline is coarse-to-fine: a feature extractor subsamples to 512 and 128 points via Set Abstraction and Point Transformer layers, the extrapolation module generates 512 missing points merged with partial seeds to form a 1,024-point seed cloud, and stacked interpolation modules progressively upsample to the final resolution. Feature channels are fixed at 128 throughout all modules.

## Method

EINet reframes completion as feature-space extrapolation and upsampling as feature-space interpolation, enabled by Mapping Constraints: paired encoder-decoder MLPs (EN/DE) that transform shape features into a linear-addable space where addition and subtraction are meaningful. The Extrapolation Module predicts scale and center parameters from a global shape feature, then computes new features via F_new' = F_c' + w_scale(F_c' − W_center) − Avg(F_c'), followed by deconvolution and coordinate prediction. The Interpolation Module uses a Seed Feature Propagation step (combining coordinate and feature differences with channel-wise cross attention) to build local guidance, then adaptively predicts interpolation coefficients via cross attention over mapped features and point proxy embeddings to generate new point features. Training uses Chamfer Distance for seed and upsampling stages plus a Mapping Constraint loss (‖F_p − DE(EN(F_p))‖) to enforce the linear-addability assumption, with only 2.80M parameters and 21 pc/s inference speed.

## Results

On the PCN dataset, EINet achieves an average CD-L1 of 6.63×10⁻³, outperforming SeedFormer (6.74), ProxyFormer (6.77), FBNet (6.94), PoinTr (7.26), and SnowFlake (7.21), though slightly behind AdaPoinTr (6.53). On ShapeNet-55, EINet reports a CD-Avg of 0.90×10⁻³ and F-Score of 0.432, competitive with ProxyFormer (0.93) and SeedFormer (0.92) but trailing AdaPoinTr (0.81). The model is notably lightweight at 2.80M parameters (roughly 10× smaller than PoinTr's 28.9M) while maintaining a testing speed of 21 pc/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EINet (6.63 avg CD-L1 on PCN) beats all MLP-based and folding-based baselines (FoldingNet 14.31, TopNet 12.15, PCN 9.64, GR-Net 8.83, PMP 8.73, NSFA 8.06) by large margins, and also outperforms the transformer-based PoinTr (7.26), SnowFlake (7.21), FBNet (6.94), ProxyFormer (6.77), and SeedFormer (6.74). It trails only AdaPoinTr (6.53) by 0.10×10⁻³ on PCN average, though EINet achieves the best per-category scores on Cabinet (8.81) and Watercraft (5.57). On ShapeNet-55, EINet (CD-Avg 0.90) is competitive with ProxyFormer (0.93) and SeedFormer (0.92) but behind AdaPoinTr (0.81); on ShapeNet-34 Seen it achieves CD-Avg 0.79, slightly ahead of ProxyFormer (0.81) and SeedFormer (0.83) but behind AdaPoinTr (0.73). On the real-world KITTI dataset, EINet reports MMD of 0.512, comparable to SeedFormer (0.516) and ProxyFormer (0.508), while producing visually cleaner outputs with fewer outliers than PoinTr. In terms of efficiency, EINet uses only 2.80M parameters versus 28.9M for PoinTr and 19.30M for SnowFlake, while running at 21 pc/s (slower than PCN's 241 pc/s but faster than SeedFormer's 18 pc/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

EINet departs from the dominant Transformer-based set-to-set translation paradigm (PoinTr, ProxyFormer, SeedFormer) by reframing missing-shape prediction as a linear extrapolation in a learned feature space, rather than a sequence-to-sequence generation problem. It builds on PoinTr's proxy embedding mechanism for the interpolation/upsampling stage and adopts Point Transformer and Set Abstraction layers for feature extraction, but replaces the transformer decoder with a simple predictive linear extrapolation guided by a global shape code. The Mapping Constraints loss, which enforces invertibility of the feature-space mapping, is a new architectural requirement that distinguishes this approach from prior MLP-based or transformer-based completion networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 2.8M-parameter network can match or slightly exceed 28.9M-parameter transformer-based methods on standard benchmarks, suggesting that the set-to-set translation framing may be over-parameterized for point cloud completion. However, the performance margins are modest (e.g., 6.63 vs 6.74 CD-L1 on PCN), and the method's limitations—object-only scope, assumption of clean inputs, and reliance on transfer from synthetic to real data (KITTI)—leave open the question of whether the extrapolation paradigm scales to large, noisy, multi-object scenes. The efficiency gain is practically relevant for deployment-constrained settings, but the conceptual contribution (linear extrapolation suffices where transformers were assumed necessary) would benefit from stronger evidence across more diverse and challenging scenarios.
