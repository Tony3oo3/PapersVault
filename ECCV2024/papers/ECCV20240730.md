## Problem

Existing image geo-localization methods predict GPS coordinates and rely on reverse geocoding to produce readable addresses, but this two-stage pipeline requires large pre-collected geo-tagged databases, introduces ambiguity at street intersections, and is not end-to-end. This paper formulates Image Address Localization (IAL) as a new problem that directly predicts human-readable textual addresses from images without a retrieval gallery, and introduces three city-scale datasets to support this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB street-view image (resized to 224×224) and outputs a structured textual address following a semantic partition hierarchy: main street name plus one or two intersecting street names and a neighborhood label. During training, BLIP-generated scene captions are appended to the address text to enrich the textual signal, but at inference only the address text is used. The model selects the candidate address whose text embedding has the highest cosine similarity to the query image embedding, requiring no retrieval database or reranking step.

## Method

AddressCLIP fine-tunes a pre-trained CLIP (ViT/B-16) with no additional parameters using a composite loss of three terms: an image-address contrastive loss, an image-caption contrastive loss (using BLIP-generated scene descriptions appended to the address), and an image-geography matching loss. The geography matching loss, inspired by manifold learning, computes a Manhattan-distance matrix over UTM coordinates within each batch and minimizes the squared difference between this spatial distance matrix and the cosine-similarity matrix of image embeddings, thereby pulling geographically proximate images together and pushing distant ones apart in feature space. A semantic address partition strategy segments streets at intersections to eliminate ambiguity and balance address granularity. All three losses are optimized end-to-end, and at inference the model simply retrieves the address text with the highest embedding similarity to the query image.

## Results

AddressCLIP achieves SSA-1 (sub-street-level Top-1 accuracy) of 80.39%, 86.32%, and 85.92% on Pitts-IAL, SF-IAL-Base, and SF-IAL-Large respectively, with corresponding SA-1 (street-level Top-1) of 82.62%, 87.44%, and 88.10%. On the largest dataset (SF-IAL-Large, 1.96M training images covering all of San Francisco), the method reaches 97.28% SSA-5 and 98.33% SA-5. The method outperforms the strongest prompt-learning baseline (CLIP+MaPLe) by 7.41%, 4.86%, and 6.29% in SSA-1 on the three datasets, and surpasses the fine-tuned CLIP+address baseline by 2.73%, 2.66%, and 4.08%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AddressCLIP outperforms all 11 baselines across all three datasets. Against zero-shot CLIP, the gap is dramatic (e.g., 85.92% vs. 0.26% SSA-1 on SF-IAL-Large), confirming that CLIP's pre-training contains no address-specific knowledge. The fine-tuned CLIP+address baseline is the strongest single-component competitor, but AddressCLIP's image-caption alignment and image-geography matching add 2.7–4.1% SSA-1 improvement. Among prompt-learning methods, MaPLe is the best (72.98/81.46/79.63 SSA-1), yet AddressCLIP still leads by 7.41/4.86/6.29 points, showing that task-specific alignment mechanisms outperform generic prompt tuning when the domain gap is large. Against retrieval-based geo-localization methods (CosPlace, MixVPR, EigenPlaces, AnyLoc, SALAD) evaluated via the two-stage Image-GPS-Address pipeline on Pitts-IAL, AddressCLIP achieves 77.01% SSA-1 versus 73.04–75.17% for the retrieval methods, while also eliminating the need for a 2+ GB retrieval database and reranking step (0.34 GB model, 3.46 ms inference, 0.64 MB memory vs. 1.7–61 GB memory for VPR methods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant retrieval-based image geo-localization (VPR) paradigm, which requires a pre-built geo-tagged database and outputs GPS coordinates, by reformulating the problem as an end-to-end image-to-text-address alignment task using CLIP. It builds directly on CLIP's contrastive image-text pre-training and extends it with two task-specific mechanisms: auxiliary scene captions (generated by BLIP) to enrich sparse address text, and a manifold-learning-based image-geography matching loss that constrains feature-space distances to mirror UTM spatial distances. Compared to generic CLIP transfer approaches (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe), AddressCLIP introduces structured supervision from both the address hierarchy and geographic coordinates, positioning itself between pure prompt-learning and full fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three introduced datasets (up to 1.96M images with administrative addresses) fill a clear gap: existing geo-localization benchmarks only provide GPS coordinates, and image-text datasets lack geographic labels. The end-to-end formulation eliminates the storage and retrieval overhead of VPR databases, making address prediction feasible in resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings. However, the method is inherently discriminative—limited to a fixed candidate address set at inference—so it cannot generalize to unseen streets or cities without retraining. The paper's exploration of MLLMs (LLaVA-IAL vs. GPT-4V, Gemini) highlights an open question: whether generative models can eventually replace discriminative address classifiers with more flexible, open-vocabulary geographic reasoning.
