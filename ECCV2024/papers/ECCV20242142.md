## Problem

Traditional Poisson Image Editing relies on discrete pixel grids and Dirichlet boundary conditions to solve the variational blending problem, introducing color shifts, artifacts, and sensitivity to mask shape. Moreover, existing solvers are restricted to 2D images and lose accuracy at higher signal dimensions (e.g., 5D radiance fields), with no prior work addressing natural blending of NeRF scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source INR (S), a target INR (T), a blending mask or 3D region of interest (Ω), and a hyperparameter λ, all encoded as continuous INRs using a DINER backbone (hash-table encoding + MLP). For 2D blending, inputs are standard RGB images; for 5D blending, inputs are two trained NeRFs plus a 3D ROI, requiring three NeRF models of identical size for ray tracing. The output is a blended INR (G) that seamlessly integrates the target region into the source, rendered at the native resolution of the input signals (256×256 for 5D due to GPU memory constraints).

## Method

The Neural Poisson Solver replaces the classical finite-difference or Fourier solver by using the Poisson PDE directly as a differentiable loss over the continuous INR, eliminating the need for Dirichlet boundary conditions. Two loss terms drive optimization: L_grad aligns the gradient of the blending INR G with a guiding vector field v (constructed from source and target gradients) within the ROI Ω, while L_color enforces G ≈ S over the entire background (S−Ω) rather than just the boundary ∂Ω, broadening the receptive field and preventing color distortion. A hyperparameter λ balances these terms, trading off gradient fidelity inside Ω against smooth color transitions at the boundary. For 5D radiance fields, the solver operates through volumetric rendering with modified alpha compositing restricted to rays intersecting Ω, blending density and color via weighted combinations of source and target NeRF outputs, and employs Pose Sampling for efficient multi-view training.

## Results

On 2D image blending, the Neural Poisson Solver reduces the Poisson PDE gradient error (L/N)_grad by 10×–300× and the color error (L/N)_color by 200×–2600× compared to the classical PIE method across five scenes and two guiding vector field operators. For example, on the Board/Slogan scene, the gradient error drops from 32.50 (PIE) to 0.17 (Ours) and the color error from 6.68 to 0.003 under the argmax operator. The method also extends to 5D radiance field blending, producing view-consistent results that the authors describe as the first such application of Poisson blending to NeRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline is the classical Poisson Image Editing (PIE) method [26], which uses a finite-difference solver with Dirichlet boundary conditions. The Neural Poisson Solver outperforms PIE on every scene and both operator settings, with gradient error reduced by roughly 10×–300× and color error reduced by roughly 200×–2600×, confirming that the continuous INR-based solver resolves the Poisson PDE far more accurately than the discrete approach. Qualitative comparisons in Fig. 8 place the method alongside three additional baselines: Deep Image Blending [41] (style/content loss optimization), Blended Diffusion [1] (text-driven diffusion editing), and TF-ICON [21] (diffusion-based cross-domain composition). The authors argue their method offers greater controllability and dimension-agnostic blending without requiring text prompts or generative priors, though no quantitative metrics are reported for these three. The ablation in Fig. 7 further shows that replacing the global L_color over (S−Ω) with a local boundary-only L_color over ∂Ω (effectively recovering the PIE formulation) reintroduces background color distortion, validating the boundary-condition-free design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical Poisson Image Editing (PIE) framework of Pérez et al. by replacing the discrete finite-difference or Fourier solver with a continuous INR-based neural solver, eliminating the need for Dirichlet boundary conditions that plague traditional approaches. For the 5D radiance field blending task, it builds on the NeRF architecture and borrows the Pose Sampling strategy from DreamFields and the alpha-compositing formulation from Blended-NeRF. The key conceptual departure is reframing the Poisson PDE as a training loss over a continuous INR rather than a discrete linear system, which allows the blending to operate over the entire background rather than being constrained to mask boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first plug-and-play INR blending tool and the first application of Poisson blending to 5D radiance fields, opening a new direction for continuous signal editing. However, practical limitations temper its impact: the 5D task requires three full NeRF models simultaneously (heavy GPU memory), rendered resolution is capped at 256×256, and sharp-edged masks still produce visible artifacts. Open questions include whether the boundary-condition-free formulation generalizes to occluded or multi-object 3D scenes, and whether the training cost (50–100 s for 2D, hours for 5D) can be reduced for interactive use.
