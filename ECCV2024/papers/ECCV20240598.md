## Problem

While MLLMs excel at solving multimodal problems, their capacity to generate visual instruction tuning data remains unexplored. Existing data creation relies on either reformulating fixed VL datasets (constraining image diversity) or prompting GPT-4 (incurring high costs and failing on complex grounding tasks like REC). This paper asks whether MLLMs themselves can be trained as autonomous, cost-free data generators for arbitrary unlabeled images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240598/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 1.4M unlabeled RGB images (from LAION, CC3M, SBU) paired with a two-level instruction template: a generic instruction (randomly sampled from 58 handwritten prompts) and an optional specific instruction that pins the output to a particular task type (e.g., 'This is a Common VQA task'). Output is an autoregressively generated 'Question: <q> Answer: <a>' sequence; for grounding tasks the answer additionally encodes normalized bounding-box coordinates [x1, y1, x2, y2]. A controlling constant τ during training balances task-agnostic versus task-specific samples, enabling the same model to switch generation modes at inference time.

## Method

GENIXER is a four-stage pipeline: (i) collecting nine representative VL tasks split into generic (VQA, MC-VQA, multi-turn dialogue) and grounding (REC, REG, PointQA, referential dialogue) categories; (ii) designing two-level instruction templates with a ratio constant τ to balance task-agnostic and task-specific training samples; (iii) fine-tuning LLaVA1.5 into GENIXER_L for generic tasks and Shikra into GENIXER_S for grounding tasks via standard autoregressive next-token prediction over the question-answer sequence; (iv) generating raw data from 1.4M images and filtering it with two dedicated pipelines — a Fuyu-8B-driven verifier that computes the probability of a 'Yes' verdict (threshold λ=0.7) for VQA data, and a CLIP-driven coarse-to-fine filter (format check, minimum box size, text-region similarity ≥ 0.6) for REC data. The key distinction from prior work is that the generator is a trained open-source MLLM rather than a prompted commercial model, and the two-phase training of GENIXER_S (first REC/REG, then adding PointQA/RD while down-weighting REC/REG) addresses data imbalance across grounding subtasks.

## Results

Training LLaVA1.5 with the synthetic Genixer-915K VQA dataset yields improvements on 10 out of 12 multimodal benchmarks, with notable gains of +3.8% on VizWiz, +2.9% on ScienceQA, and +37.7 points on MME (1502.7 vs. 1465.0). For grounding tasks, training Shikra with the synthetic Genixer-350K REC dataset achieves an average score of 83.49 across RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg, surpassing the Shikra baseline (82.92) on 7 out of 8 test splits. The Fuyu-8B filtering pipeline retains 915K high-quality samples from 1.4M raw generations (λ=0.7), and human evaluation confirms >80% accuracy across Common VQA, MC VQA, and Multi-turn Dialogue categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct LLaVA-1.5 baseline, the proposed Genixer-915K synthetic data yields consistent gains on 10 of 12 benchmarks, with the largest improvements on VizWiz (+3.8%), ScienceQA (+2.9%), and MME (+37.7 points), while showing slight declines on LLaVA-Bench (−1.4) and MM-Vet (−1.0). Compared to InstructBLIP (7B/13B), the method substantially outperforms on VQAv2, GQA, VizWiz, and MME, though InstructBLIP-13B remains competitive on ScienceQA. Against Qwen-VL-Chat, the method wins on VQAv2, GQA, VizWiz, ScienceQA, POPE, MME, MMB, and SEED, but trails on TextVQA (59.0 vs. 61.5) and LLaVA-Bench (66.6 vs. 65.4). For the REC task, Shikra + Genixer-350K surpasses the Shikra baseline on 7 of 8 splits (e.g., RefCOCO test-B: 81.77 vs. 80.24; RefCOCOg test: 83.15 vs. 82.19) and outperforms prior grounding methods including MDETR (avg 81.81), UniTAB (80.89), and VILLA (77.77), though it slightly underperforms on RefCOCOg val (81.99 vs. 82.27). The paper also demonstrates that GENIXER_L itself, when retrained with mixed data (MixT setting), improves over vanilla LLaVA1.5 on all six tested benchmarks, confirming the data generator's own multimodal competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLaVA1.5 and Shikra as backbone MLLMs, extending the visual instruction tuning paradigm established by these models. It departs from the dominant data-generation approach of prompting GPT-4/GPT-4V (used by LLaVA, ShareGPT4V, and Shikra) by instead training MLLMs themselves as autonomous data generators, introducing a two-level instruction template for controllable task-specific and task-agnostic generation. The data filtering frameworks (Fuyu-driven for VQA, CLIP-driven for grounding) draw on existing verification models but are novel in their application to synthetic data quality control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: organizations can generate large-scale visual instruction data without recurring GPT-4V API costs, democratizing MLLM training. The two released datasets (Genixer-915K, Genixer-350K) provide immediately usable resources that improve LLaVA1.5 on 10/12 benchmarks and Shikra on 7/8 REC datasets. However, limitations remain: the authors did not test larger LLM backbones (13B+), the image corpus is capped at 1.4M images, and evaluation of complex open-ended tasks like Referential Dialogue lacks automated filtering. An open question is whether this self-generation paradigm scales to truly novel task types beyond those seen in training data.
