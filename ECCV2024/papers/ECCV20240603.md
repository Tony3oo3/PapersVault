## Problem

CNN-based gaze estimation methods suffer severe performance degradation in cross-domain settings because they capture spurious correlations between gaze-irrelevant factors (identity, illumination, complexion) and gaze direction during source-domain training. These spurious correlations are domain-specific and do not hold in unseen target domains, causing systematic estimation errors. The paper frames this as a causal confounding problem and proposes to eliminate the influence of gaze-irrelevant factors via causal intervention, without requiring any target-domain data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB face image (pixel values normalized to [0,1]) as input and regresses a 3D gaze direction vector as output. During training, the model also consumes head pose labels (generated via MediaPipe for Gaze360, which lacks native annotations) and gaze labels. The framework is trained exclusively on source domains (ETH-XGaze and Gaze360) and evaluated zero-shot on target domains (MPIIFaceGaze and EyeDiap), with no target-domain data accessed at any stage.

## Method

The FSCI framework consists of two modules. The Feature Separation Module (FSM) uses a cross-attention layer on ResNet features to decompose the representation into three distinct vectors—head pose (H), gaze (G), and gaze-irrelevant (I)—enforced by a classification loss and regression losses on H and G. The Causal Intervention Module (CIM) implements front-door adjustment via do-calculus: it computes the expected gaze-irrelevant feature E[I] using a Dynamic Confounder Bank (an online moving average over training batches, frozen at inference) and estimates gaze as g ≈ φ(W₁E[I] + G + W₂H), where W₁ maps the averaged confounder to its causal effect on gaze, W₂ projects head pose into a gaze-relevant subspace, and φ is a two-layer MLP. This formulation, derived from the Normalized Weighted Geometric Mean approximation of the front-door formula, simultaneously severs the spurious path I→G and the confounded path I→H→G, forcing the model to rely on true gaze and head-pose cues rather than domain-specific confounders.

## Results

The FSCI framework reports angular error (in degrees, lower is better) across four cross-domain gaze estimation tasks. It achieves 5.79° on D_E→D_M, 6.96° on D_E→D_D, 7.06° on D_G→D_M, and 7.99° on D_G→D_D. This represents up to a 36.2% reduction in angular error over the ResNet18 baseline (e.g., 9.07°→5.79° on D_E→D_M) and up to an 11.5% improvement over the strongest SOTA method (Xu et al., 9.03°→7.99° on D_G→D_D), all without accessing any target-domain data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSCI outperforms all six baselines on every cross-domain task where a comparison is available. Against the ResNet18 baseline, FSCI reduces angular error by 36.2% (D_E→D_M), 31.4% (D_E→D_D), 27.6% (D_G→D_M), and 30.0% (D_G→D_D). Against the two strongest SOTA DG methods, PureGaze [7] and Xu et al. [39], FSCI achieves consistent gains of 1.29°–2.22° per task, with the largest margin of 1.04° on D_G→D_D (7.99° vs. 9.03° for Xu et al.). The older appearance-based methods (Full-Face, Dilated-Net, CA-NET) perform substantially worse in cross-domain settings, with CA-NET reaching 31.41° on D_G→D_D compared to FSCI's 7.99°, highlighting the difficulty of domain shift for non-DG approaches. Notably, FSCI achieves these gains without any target-domain data, distinguishing it from UDA methods that require unlabeled target samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the domain generalization line for gaze estimation (PureGaze, Xu et al., Dilated-Net) by reframing the cross-domain failure mode through Pearl's causal inference lens: gaze-irrelevant factors (identity, illumination) act as confounders that create spurious correlations with gaze during source-domain training. It builds on causal-intervention methods in computer vision (R-CNN for VQA, causal VQA models) but is the first to apply front-door adjustment with do-calculus specifically to appearance-based gaze estimation. The Dynamic Confounder Bank departs from the discrete confounder-dictionary strategy used in prior causal CV work by computing a continuous, online moving average of the confounder, making the intervention tractable for regression tasks without requiring manual confounder labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled causal explanation for why cross-domain gaze estimation degrades (spurious I→G and I→H→G paths) and validates this explanation empirically through noise-robustness and head-pose-cluster analyses, which is more diagnostic than typical SOTA-bumping papers. The Dynamic Confounder Bank is a lightweight, label-free mechanism that could transfer to other regression tasks where a continuous confounder must be marginalized. The acknowledged limitation—that CIM performance is bounded by FSM separation quality—leaves open the question of whether stronger disentanglement (e.g., contrastive or adversarial separation) would yield further gains, and whether the approach extends to tasks with more complex or higher-dimensional confounders.
