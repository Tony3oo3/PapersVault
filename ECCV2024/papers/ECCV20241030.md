## Problem

Existing scene-aware human motion prediction methods constrain only partial aspects of human-scene interaction—global position, a few contacting joints, or gaze direction—leaving the rest of the body unconstrained and prone to 'ghost motion' artifacts. No prior representation simultaneously constrains both the detailed local pose and the global trajectory of the entire body.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene represented as a signed distance volume (cropped to 2 m around the root joint of the last observed pose), a sequence of past SMPL-X poses, and pre-computed past mutual distances (67 per-vertex signed distances and 150 per-basis point distances sampled on a fixed-radius Fibonacci sphere). It outputs future SMPL-X poses autoregressively via an RNN, conditioned on predicted future mutual distances. The SDF volume representation enables fast trilinear interpolation to query signed distances of arbitrary predicted mesh vertices during training, which is not as straightforward with point-cloud scene encodings.

## Method

The core contribution is a mutual distance representation combining per-vertex signed distance (constraining local pose and penetration) with per-basis point distance (constraining global position), which together provide explicit whole-body constraints. A two-step pipeline first predicts future mutual distances using DCT for temporal encoding and GCN for spatial encoding (following the trajectory-dependency strategy of Mao et al. 2019), with the scene encoded by a 3D CNN over the SDF volume; then an RNN forecasts future poses autoregressively conditioned on the predicted distances. During motion training, two additional consistency losses (per-vertex and per-basis point) enforce that the predicted poses' actual distances match the ground-truth mutual distances, computed via SDF interpolation and nearest-surface queries. The authors adopt a stage-wise training strategy—training the distance predictor and motion forecaster separately before end-to-end fine-tuning—to avoid convergence to local minima caused by early inaccurate distance predictions.

## Results

On GTA-IM, the proposed method achieves a mean path error of 72.0 mm and mean pose error of 44.6 mm, outperforming ContAware (87.1/51.8), GIMO (110.3/59.9), and STAG (83.4/47.0). On the real-world PROX dataset, it reduces mean path error to 162.6 mm (vs. 192.2 for ContAware, 240.4 for GIMO, 189.6 for STAG) and mean pose error to 110.7 mm. On HUMANISE, the method achieves 45.6 mm mean path error on seen scenes and 50.1 mm on unseen scenes, consistently leading all baselines. When provided with ground-truth mutual distance, the method's path error drops to 22.4 mm on GTA-IM and 52.6 mm on PROX, far below the 40–42 mm achieved by baselines with ground-truth contact maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines—ContAware, GIMO, and STAG—on every dataset (GTA-IM, PROX, HUMANISE) and every prediction horizon for both path error and pose error. The largest margins appear on the real-world PROX dataset, where the method reduces mean path error by ~30 mm over ContAware (162.6 vs. 192.2) and ~78 mm over GIMO (162.6 vs. 240.4). On HUMANISE unseen scenes, the advantage is more pronounced for pose error: 80.6 mm vs. 90.3 (ContAware), 146.5 (GIMO), and 85.6 (STAG). A particularly telling comparison is the ground-truth oracle setting: with GT mutual distance, the method achieves 22.4 mm mean path error on GTA-IM, nearly halving the 40.0–41.7 mm achieved by STAG and ContAware with GT contact maps, indicating that the mutual distance representation is a fundamentally stronger constraint than the contact map. GIMO consistently performs worst across all settings, likely because it relies on gaze information unavailable in most datasets and does not explicitly constrain local pose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage pipeline of ContactAware (Mao et al., NeurIPS 2022), which predicts a human-scene interaction representation before forecasting motion, by replacing the contact map with a mutual distance representation that constrains the entire body rather than only contacting joints. The temporal-spatial encoding of the distance sequences reuses the DCT + GCN strategy from Mao et al. (ICCV 2019), while the per-basis point distance adapts the BPS concept from PLACE (Hassan et al., CVPR 2021) with scene-independent, fixed-radius sampling. The key conceptual departure is the combination of per-vertex signed distance (local pose constraint) with per-basis point distance (global position constraint), which together resolve the ambiguity that either component alone would leave, and the use of an SDF volume rather than a point cloud for global scene encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual distance representation offers a more complete and principled constraint on human-scene interaction than contact maps, which only constrain joints in contact and leave unsupported joints (e.g., elbows) unconstrained. However, the large performance gap between the w/GT and predicted mutual distance results (e.g., path error dropping from 72.0 to 22.4 mm on GTA-IM) reveals that the mutual distance prediction itself is the primary bottleneck, leaving a clear direction for future work. The paper also notes the absence of explicit physics modeling as a risk for real-world deployment in safety-critical settings like human-robot interaction, where predicted motions could involve imbalanced forces.
