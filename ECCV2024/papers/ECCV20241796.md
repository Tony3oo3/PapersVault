## Problem

Existing 4D shape analysis methods either handle objects that preserve topology over time or perform only spatial registration of plant sequences, leaving a gap for 4D tree-shaped structures that simultaneously bend, stretch, and change their branching topology. No prior framework provides a unified mathematical space, metric, and computational toolkit for spatiotemporal registration, geodesic computation, and statistical modeling of such topologically variable 4D shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sequences of 3D plant point clouds (e.g., from Pheno4D) that are first skeletonized and partitioned into hierarchical branch layers; trees with differing branch counts are padded with null branches to share a common pre-tree-shape space. Outputs include spatiotemporally registered 4D tree trajectories, geodesic deformation paths between pairs, a Karcher-mean 4D atlas, principal directions of spatiotemporal variation, and novel 4D tree shapes synthesized by sampling coefficients along those directions. All results are mapped back from the SRVF/SRVFT representation to the original 3D tree geometry for visualization.

## Method

The core representation encodes each tree branch as a Square Root Velocity Function (SRVF), so that the full elastic metric (bending + stretching + topological sliding) reduces to an L2 norm in the SRVFT space, with invariance to rotation, reparameterization, and subtree permutation handled by an infimum over those transformations. A 4D tree then becomes a trajectory in this SRVFT space, and temporal registration is solved by applying a second SRVF to the trajectory itself, converting time-warping (diffeomorphism) into another L2 minimization. To keep computation tractable, a PCA subspace of the SRVFT space is learned via Karcher-mean and covariance eigendecomposition, reducing each 4D tree to a curve in R^k. Geodesics are straight-line interpolations in the SRVF-of-trajectory space, and the generative model samples random coefficients along the leading principal directions to synthesize new 4D tree shapes, all without any learned neural network.

## Results

On the Pheno4D dataset, the proposed spatiotemporal registration reduces the mean geodesic distance between 4D tomato plants from 325 to 136 (median 136, std 27) and between 4D maize plants from 35 to 10 (median 9, std 2.5), substantially outperforming both baselines. Cycle consistency error at ε=0.1 is 0.94% (mean) for the proposed method versus 10.33% for Pan et al. [15]. Temporal registration reduces the geodesic distance between a 4D tree and its randomly warped version from 356 to 26 (tomato) and 71 to 15 (maize). Spatial registration of two 3D trees takes 2.3 s on average, compared to 30 s for [3] and 20.1 s for [15].

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior 4D plant registration methods: Chebrolu et al. [3] (spatio-temporal point cloud registration) and Pan et al. [15] (multi-scale space-time registration). On spatial registration quality (geodesic distance), the proposed method achieves a mean of 136 for tomato and 10 for maize, beating [3] (416 and 554) by a wide margin and [15] (261 and 19) by roughly 2× on tomato and ~2× on maize. On cycle consistency error at ε=0.1, the proposed method reports 0.94% mean error versus 10.33% for [15], an order-of-magnitude improvement; [3] is not evaluated on this metric. In terms of speed, the proposed method registers two 3D trees in 2.3 s on average, compared to 30 s for [3] and 20.1 s for [15], making it roughly 13× faster than [3] and 9× faster than [15]. The proposed method wins on every reported metric against both baselines, with the largest gains over [3] on maize (554→10) and over [15] on cycle consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SRVFT representation of Wang et al. [21] (spatial registration of tree-like 3D shapes) to the 4D setting by treating each 4D tree as a trajectory in the SRVFT space, and further extends the 4D-as-trajectory paradigm of Laga et al. [12] from topology-preserving manifold shapes to topology-changing tree structures. The sliding-branch distance formulation of Duncan et al. [4] and the SRVF encoding of Srivastava et al. [17] provide the mathematical backbone, while prior 4D plant registration work (Chebrolu et al. [3], Pan et al. [15]) addressed only spatial alignment within sequences, not joint spatiotemporal registration. The key conceptual departure is that topology changes (branching/merging) are handled natively through the hierarchical SRVFT structure rather than requiring graph edit distance or edge collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework that unifies spatial and temporal registration, geodesic computation, statistical summarization, and generative modeling for 4D tree-shaped objects that undergo topological change, with direct applications to plant phenotyping, vascular/neuronal morphology, and 4D content generation. The reduction of the full elastic metric to an L2 metric in SRVF space yields dramatic computational gains (2.3s vs 30s for spatial registration; 6–37ms for temporal registration) and enables closed-form geodesics. A key limitation is that the framework operates on skeleton structure only; extending it to full 3D branch geometry (thickness, surface) remains open, as does scaling to very large collections or continuous-time formulations.
