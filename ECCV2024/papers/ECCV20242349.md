## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS), the dominant loss for text-to-3D generation, suffers from over-saturation and over-smoothing that degrade generated 3D assets. The paper identifies the root causes: over-saturation stems from the large classifier-free guidance scale (100.0) required to compensate for the reconstruction term, while over-smoothing arises from the reconstruction term's single-step denoising error and its tendency to preserve noise in the rendered image.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and optimizes a 3D representation (NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting) from scratch in a single-stage procedure. Rendered images are produced at 512×512 resolution from random camera poses (distance 1.5–2.0 for NeRF, 3.5–5.0 for 3DGS; fovy 40–70°) and fed through Stable Diffusion 2-1-base for score estimation. Optimization runs for 25,000 iterations (NeRF) or 5,000 iterations (3DGS) on a single A6000 GPU, with an annealed time-step schedule that narrows the sampling range from U(0.02, 0.98) to U(0.02, 0.50) after 5,000 steps.

## Method

ISD decomposes the SDS gradient into a reconstruction term ε_φ(z_t;y,t)−ε and a classifier-free guidance term ε_φ(z_t;y,t)−ε_φ(z_t;∅,t), then replaces the reconstruction term with an invariant score term δ_inv = ε_φ(z_{t−c};y,t−c) − ε_φ(z_t;y,t) derived from the DDIM sampling recurrence. Rather than attempting a single-step reconstruction of the clean image (which introduces error at high noise levels and preserves existing noise), δ_inv aligns the noisy latent to its previous time-step state over an interval c=20, continuously refining detail while removing accumulated noise. This substitution eliminates the need for the extreme guidance scale, allowing a conventional w=7.5 that avoids over-saturation. The final gradient is ∇_θ L_ISD = E[w(t)(λ(t)δ_inv + w·δ_cls) ∂x/∂θ], where λ(t)=√((β_{t−c}/α_{t−c})/(β_t/α_t)) balances the two terms across timesteps.

## Results

On 50 text prompts evaluated with CLIP ViT-B/32, ISD achieves a CLIP Score of 81.06 and R-Precision of 82.30% (R@1), 95.08% (R@5), and 97.93% (R@10), outperforming all baselines. In a user study with 47 participants, ISD receives 40.92% preference for prompt alignment and 61.84% for image quality, roughly doubling the next-best method (ProlificDreamer at 20.37% and 22.37% respectively). The method operates in a single-stage optimization with a standard guidance scale of 7.5, avoiding the over-saturation and over-smoothing artifacts of SDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ISD outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against DreamFusion (SDS), the largest gap is 13.84 CLIP Score points (81.06 vs. 67.22) and 6.51 R@1 points, reflecting the fundamental over-smoothing and over-saturation issues of the original SDS loss. Compared to ProlificDreamer (VSD), the closest competitor, ISD leads by 2.16 CLIP Score points and 3.66 R@1 points while requiring only single-stage optimization versus ProlificDreamer's additional LoRA training. Against Magic3D, ISD gains 5.33 CLIP Score points, and against NFSD, 5.79 points. In the user study, ISD's 40.92% prompt-alignment preference is roughly double that of ProlificDreamer (20.37%) and NFSD (17.53%), and its 61.84% image-quality preference is nearly triple ProlificDreamer's 22.37%, indicating a clear perceptual advantage in both semantic alignment and visual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) [40], the foundational 2D-lifting approach for text-to-3D generation, and positions itself within the line of work that diagnoses and fixes SDS's known pathologies (over-saturation, over-smoothing). It shares conceptual ground with VSD/ProlificDreamer [53], NFSD [21], and CSD [59], which also decompose or modify the SDS loss, but departs by providing a clean two-term decomposition (reconstruction + classifier-free guidance) and replacing the reconstruction term with a DDIM-derived invariant score term rather than learning an auxiliary model (VSD) or simply dropping the denoising term (NFSD). The method is implemented within the threestudio framework and is compatible with both NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean decomposition of SDS into a reconstruction term and a classifier-free guidance term, with a clear causal attribution of over-smoothing to the former and over-saturation to the latter, provides a diagnostic framework that the broader text-to-3D community can build upon. The practical advantage is that ISD achieves competitive or superior quality in a single-stage optimization without requiring additional model training (unlike VSD's LoRA), making it a low-friction drop-in replacement for SDS in existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the invariant score term's benefits persist at higher resolutions or with newer diffusion backbones, and whether the single-stage formulation can fully address multi-view consistency issues (the 'Janus' problem) that multi-stage methods like Magic3D handle more explicitly.
