## Problem

Instant-NGP achieves high-quality novel view synthesis with multi-level hash features but requires a per-point shallow MLP to aggregate them, limiting rendering to ~10 fps at 1080p. Deferred NeRF methods (SNeRG, MERF) remove the per-point MLP by storing explicit colors and densities, but their feature construction strategies (sparse grids, simple summation) cannot adequately represent the multi-level hash features of Instant-NGP without significant quality degradation, especially in handling hash collisions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images with known camera poses of a static scene and produces rendered novel view images at 1080×1920 resolution. During training, an auxiliary NGP model optimizes explicit deferred features (density, diffuse color, view-dependent feature vector) and spatially-varying attention parameters, which are then baked into sparse voxel grids and high-resolution hash tables. At inference, the model renders at 108 fps on a single RTX 3090 GPU, with the only per-ray computation being a tiny view-dependent MLP applied once after volumetric accumulation.

## Method

NGP-RT decomposes the deferred NeRF feature into a coarse-grained part (baked into a sparse voxel grid at resolution L_C) and a fine-grained part aggregated from L high-resolution hash levels via a lightweight attention mechanism. The attention uses two spatially-varying, modality-specific parameters per level (one for density, one for color features) that are learned to adaptively weight features and mitigate hash collisions, reducing over 90% of MAC operations compared to Instant-NGP's per-point MLP. During training, an auxiliary NGP model decodes the attention parameters and coarse features at grid corners, which are then baked into sparse grids for direct tri-linear interpolation at render time. Additionally, a pre-computed occupancy distance grid (256³, uint8) informs the ray marching strategy of the distance to the nearest occupied voxel, reducing redundant marching points by over 40% and cutting global memory access to occupancy grids.

## Results

On the Mip-NeRF 360 dataset at 1080p resolution, NGP-RT achieves a mean PSNR of 25.64, SSIM of 0.737, and LPIPS of 0.299 across all 9 scenes, while rendering at 108 fps on a single RTX 3090 GPU. On indoor scenes, NGP-RT reaches PSNR 29.25 and LPIPS 0.195, outperforming both Instant-NGP (PSNR 29.15) and MERF (PSNR 27.80). The method achieves a 10× speedup over Instant-NGP (108 fps vs. 10.4 fps) with comparable rendering quality, and matches 3D Gaussian Splatting (7K) in speed (108 vs. 107 fps) with slightly lower PSNR (25.64 vs. 25.91).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Instant-NGP, NGP-RT achieves nearly identical overall quality (PSNR 25.64 vs. 25.62) while rendering 10× faster (108 fps vs. 10.4 fps), validating that the lightweight attention mechanism can replace the per-point MLP without quality loss. Compared to MERF, the closest deferred-NeRF competitor, NGP-RT wins on all quality metrics (PSNR 25.64 vs. 25.24, SSIM 0.737 vs. 0.722, LPIPS 0.299 vs. 0.311) at a comparable speed (108 vs. 119 fps), with the gap most pronounced on indoor scenes (PSNR 29.25 vs. 27.80). Against 3D Gaussian Splatting at 7K iterations, NGP-RT matches the rendering speed (108 vs. 107 fps) but trails slightly in quality (PSNR 25.64 vs. 25.91, LPIPS 0.299 vs. 0.288), though both use similar model sizes. NGP-RT clearly outperforms BakedSDF (PSNR 25.64 vs. 24.51) and MobileNeRF (PSNR 25.64 vs. 21.95) in both quality and speed. On outdoor scenes, NGP-RT is slightly behind MERF (PSNR 22.76 vs. 23.19), which the authors attribute to high-frequency structures that the model struggles to capture. Against offline methods like Zip-NeRF and Mip-NeRF 360, NGP-RT sacrifices some quality (PSNR 25.64 vs. 28.54 and 27.69) but renders 200–1800× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

NGP-RT sits at the intersection of two lines of work: Instant-NGP's multi-level hash encoding (which stores implicit features and decodes them with a per-point shallow MLP) and the deferred NeRF architecture introduced by SNeRG and refined by MERF (which bakes explicit colors and densities into grids to eliminate per-point MLPs). The paper's key departure is replacing the per-point MLP with a lightweight spatially-varying attention mechanism to aggregate multi-level hash features, addressing the hash-collision problem that makes naive sum aggregation (as in MERF) insufficient for high-resolution NGP features. It also introduces an occupancy distance grid to reduce global memory access during ray marching, a practical optimization orthogonal to the feature representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NGP-RT demonstrates that NeRF-based volumetric rendering can reach 1080p at over 100 fps while maintaining quality comparable to Instant-NGP, making it competitive with 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time applications. The lightweight attention mechanism is a general-purpose technique that could benefit other methods relying on MLP-based feature aggregation (e.g., MLP-enhanced 3D Gaussians). However, the method still requires an offline training phase, performs slightly worse than MERF on outdoor scenes with high-frequency structures, and the authors note that extending to dynamic scenes remains open. The practical impact is somewhat tempered by 3D Gaussian Splatting already occupying the real-time rendering niche with simpler pipelines.
