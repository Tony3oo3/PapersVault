## Problem

Zero-shot visual attribute recognition remains challenging because CLIP's contrastively-learned representations treat text as an unstructured whole, failing to capture the sequential dependency between objects and attributes in a sentence. This leads to counterfactual pairings (e.g., 'bell-shaped sky') and an inability to distinguish fine-grained attributes when the object alone is already distinguishable in the image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240706/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image cropped to the bounding box of a target object, along with the object's name, and produces a ranked list of candidate attributes (or objects for the reverse task) scored by cross-entropy of sentence generation. On VAW, each instance is scored against 620 attribute choices; on VGARank, against 50 choices where false pairings are sampled from the dataset's conditional co-occurrence distribution to make the task harder.

## Method

The paper proposes 'generative retrieval' on top of CoCa's image-conditioned prefix language model, replacing contrastive image-text similarity with the cross-entropy of generating a short sentence that encodes the object-attribute relation. By engineering different sentence templates—'{A}', '{O} is {A}', '{A}{O}', and '{A}{O} is {A}'—the method constructs distinct conditional probability graphs at inference time, each modeling a different dependency structure (pure classification, attribute given object, object given attribute, or joint co-dependency). The hybrid template '{A}{O} is {A}' jointly captures p(A|v), p(O|v,A), and p(A|v,O), which no single contrastive or masked-LM formulation can express simultaneously. For few-shot adaptation, learnable per-class bias and scaling parameters rescale the generative retrieval scores, optimized with cross-entropy loss while keeping the backbone frozen.

## Results

On the VAW dataset (finetuned setting), the best generative retrieval template "{O} is {A}" achieves 72.0 mAP and 74.7 mA, outperforming all baselines without in-domain pretraining and trailing only TAP with in-domain pretraining (73.4 mAP). The method's advantage is most pronounced on long-tail attributes: 60.6 mAP on Tail classes vs. 58.8 for TAP (in-domain) and 48.0 for SCoNE. In the zero-shot setting, generative retrieval achieves an average rank of 56.0 (best template "{A}{O} is {A}"), nearly halving the error of the best contrastive retrieval (95.1). On the open-vocabulary VGARank-Attribute benchmark, generative retrieval reaches a rank of 12.0 vs. 16.1 for contrastive retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed generative retrieval method (best template "{O} is {A}", 72.0 mAP) outperforms all seven baselines that do not use in-domain pretraining on VAW, including SCoNE (68.3 mAP), ML-GCN (63.0 mAP), and TAP without in-domain pretraining (65.4 mAP). Against TAP with in-domain pretraining (73.4 mAP), the proposed method trails by 1.4 mAP overall but surpasses it on Tail attributes (60.6 vs. 58.8) and matches it on Medium (72.0 vs. 72.9), indicating stronger generalization to rare attribute classes. The gap to TAP (in-domain) narrows further on the open-vocabulary VGARank-Attribute benchmark, where generative retrieval (rank 12.0) beats contrastive retrieval (rank 16.1) by a wider margin than on VAW. Among the four sentence templates tested, the hybrid "{A}{O} is {A}" consistently achieves the best zero-shot rank (56.0 on VAW, 12.0 on VGARank-A), while the simpler "{O} is {A}" template yields the best finetuned mAP (72.0), suggesting that the optimal template depends on the evaluation protocol. The method's key advantage over contrastive retrieval baselines is its ability to reject counterfactual attribute-object pairings (e.g., "bell-shaped sky"), which contrastive retrieval erroneously ranks highly due to image-level co-occurrence learned during pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CoCa, which already unifies contrastive learning and image-conditioned prefix language modeling in a single architecture, and repurposes the prefixLM component for a task it was not originally designed for: fine-grained attribute recognition. It departs from the dominant CLIP-style contrastive retrieval paradigm (used in works like SCoNE, TAP, and various CLIP-prompting methods) by reframing attribute recognition as a sentence-generation probability problem rather than an embedding-similarity problem. The generative retrieval formulation also connects to the broader NLP tradition of prefixLM (Bengio et al., GPT) and masked language modeling, but applies it in a vision-conditioned setting where the sentence structure itself encodes the probabilistic dependency graph between objects and attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that contrastive retrieval is fundamentally ill-suited for attribute recognition because it cannot model object-attribute co-dependency, and that a simple reformulation via prefixLM generative retrieval closes much of the gap to task-specific SOTA methods without in-domain pretraining or custom architectures. The strong long-tail performance (Medium/Tail mAP) suggests the method inherits better priors from large-scale pretraining rather than overfitting to dataset distributions. Limitations include evaluation on a single foundation model (CoCa Base), minimal finetuning (only per-class bias/scale), and the VGARank benchmark being constructed from Visual Genome annotations, which may carry its own biases. Open questions include whether the generative retrieval formulation scales to larger VLMs, handles multi-object scenes with complex relational structure, and generalizes beyond the attribute-recognition setting to broader visual reasoning tasks.
