## Problem

Existing unrestricted adversarial attacks based on GANs directly inject PGD gradients into low-dimensional GAN latents without theoretical justification, producing flipped-label or distorted examples that are visually distinguishable from clean generations. Diffusion-based attacks (DiffAttack, AdvDiffuser) similarly apply PGD perturbations at each reverse step, degrading generation quality. The paper addresses the need for a theoretically grounded, interpretable adversarial sampling procedure that preserves the high-fidelity generation of diffusion models while producing effective unrestricted adversarial examples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a random Gaussian noise vector x_T ~ N(0, I), a ground-truth class label y (defining the visual content), and a target attack label y_a (defining the misclassification objective). It outputs a full-resolution unrestricted adversarial image that visually resembles a sample from p(x|y) but is classified as y_a by the target model. The procedure is entirely training-free, operating on a pre-trained classifier-free conditional diffusion model (DDPM for MNIST at 28×28, LDM with DDIM sampler for ImageNet at 256×256), and requires no modification to the diffusion model's weights.

## Method

AdvDiff introduces two adversarial guidance techniques injected into the reverse diffusion sampling loop. First, adversarial guidance (Eq. 9) modifies each denoised sample x_{t-1} by adding σ_t² · s · ∇_{x_{t-1}} log p_f(y_a | x_{t-1}), gradually steering the trajectory toward the target attack label's decision region at every timestep. Second, noise sampling guidance (Eq. 11) shifts the initial noise x_T by σ̄_T² · a · ∇_{x_0} log p_f(y_a | x_0) over N outer iterations, embedding a strong adversarial prior derived from the final classification gradient into the generation starting point. Because perturbations are applied to high-dimensional intermediate samples rather than low-dimensional GAN latents, each step's modification is small and the overall generation quality is preserved. The theoretical analysis shows these steps correspond to sampling from an adversarial conditional distribution p(x_{t-1}* | x_t, f(x)≠y), providing a principled justification absent in prior PGD-injection approaches.

## Results

On ImageNet, AdvDiff achieves 99.8% ASR against clean ResNet50 and 99.9% against clean WideResNet50-2, outperforming U-BigGAN (96.8%/96.5%) and DiffAttack (92.8%/90.6%). Against the DiffPure defense, AdvDiff-Untargeted reaches 75.2% ASR (ResNet50) and 70.5% (WRN50-2), far exceeding U-BigGAN (40.1%/35.5%) and DiffAttack (30.6%/27.6%). In generation quality, AdvDiff attains FID 16.2 and IS 343.8 on ImageNet, substantially better than U-BigGAN (FID 25.4, IS 129.4) and AdvDiffuser (FID 26.8, IS 206.8), while maintaining the highest Precision (0.90) among all adversarial methods. AdvDiff also runs fastest at 9.2 s per sample versus 28.2 s for DiffAttack and 38.6 s for AdvDiffuser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against perturbation-based attacks (PGD, BIM, C&W, AutoAttack), AdvDiff-Untargeted achieves dramatically higher ASR against adversarial defenses: 75.2% vs. 22.2% (AutoAttack) against DiffPure on ResNet50, and 94.5% vs. 56.2% against PGD-AT. Among GAN-based unrestricted attacks, AdvDiff outperforms U-BigGAN by 3.0 pp on clean ResNet50 ASR (99.8% vs. 96.8%) and by 35.6 pp against DiffPure (75.2% vs. 40.1% for the untargeted variant), while also producing substantially better generation quality (FID 16.2 vs. 25.4, IS 343.8 vs. 129.4). Against the two prior diffusion-based attacks, AdvDiff surpasses DiffAttack by 7.0 pp on clean ResNet50 (99.8% vs. 92.8%) and by 11.0 pp against DiffPure (41.6% vs. 30.6%), and beats AdvDiffuser by 4.4 pp on clean ResNet50 (99.8% vs. 95.4%) and by 12.7 pp against DiffPure (41.6% vs. 28.9%). In generation quality (Table 3), AdvDiff achieves the best FID (16.2) and IS (343.8) among all adversarial methods, with Precision 0.90 versus 0.81 for U-BigGAN and 0.70 for AdvDiffuser, confirming fewer flipped-label artifacts. The untargeted variant of AdvDiff also dominates in transfer attacks (Table 5), reaching 52.0% ASR on ResNet-152 and 81.5% on BEiT, compared to 30.8%/69.4% for U-BigGAN and 21.1%/78.0% for DiffAttack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unrestricted adversarial attack paradigm introduced by Song et al. (2018) from GANs to diffusion models, building directly on DDPM's reverse generation process and classifier-free guidance as the conditional sampling mechanism. The key conceptual departure is from both GAN-based UAEs (which inject PGD gradients into low-dimensional latents without theoretical justification) and prior diffusion-based attacks (DiffAttack, AdvDiffuser) that similarly apply PGD/I-FGSM perturbations to the denoising steps. Instead, AdvDiff reframes adversarial generation as a conditional sampling problem—guiding the reverse process toward the target attack label via a theoretically derived adversarial gradient (Eq. 9) and a noise-level prior (Eq. 11)—analogous to how classifier guidance steers conditional generation, but with the target classifier's attack label replacing the desired class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion models' multi-step, high-dimensional generation process inherently preserves sample quality under adversarial guidance (Precision 0.90 vs. 0.81 for BigGAN; FID 16.2 vs. 25.4), making unrestricted attacks both more effective and more stealthy than GAN-based alternatives. This has direct security implications: as diffusion models become the dominant image generation paradigm, the threat of imperceptible, unlimited adversarial examples becomes more realistic. Open questions include the trade-off between the targeted version's generation quality and the untargeted version's superior transferability/defense evasion, the ineffectiveness of adversarial guidance at early (noisy) timesteps, and whether the theoretical framework extends to video or 3D generation.
