## Problem

Existing Video LLMs cannot accurately answer 'When?' questions, lacking temporal localization capabilities. The authors identify three root causes: (i) plain-text timestamps are ill-defined without frame rate, (ii) sparse frame sampling provides insufficient temporal resolution, and (iii) temporal localization data is largely absent from instruction-tuning corpora.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241708/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 100 uniformly sampled video frames (encoded by CLIP ViT-L-14 into 256 tokens each) and a text prompt, reducing the 25,600 visual tokens to 356 via SlowFast pooling before feeding them alongside text tokens into the LLM. Outputs are structured as relative time tokens (<1> to <100>) that map to fractions of video length, paired with natural-language explanations. For the Reasoning Temporal Localization task, the answer format is '[start end] Explanation', requiring both localized timestamps and a reasoning chain.

## Method

LITA introduces three coupled design choices on top of the LLaVA architecture (CLIP-L-14 + Vicuna). First, T=100 specialized time tokens replace absolute timestamps, encoding time as a fraction of video length so the model never needs to know the frame rate. Second, a SlowFast token pathway reduces 25,600 frame tokens to 356: 100 fast tokens (one per frame via spatial averaging) preserve temporal resolution, while 256 slow tokens (4 sparsely sampled frames × 64 tokens each via 2×2 pooling) retain spatial detail. Third, training emphasizes temporal localization through five tasks—dense video captioning, event localization, video QA, NLVQA, and the newly proposed Reasoning Temporal Localization (RTL)—where RTL questions require multi-step world-knowledge reasoning before localizing an event, and the ActivityNet-RTL dataset (33.5K training, 229 evaluation pairs) is generated via GPT-4 from ActivityNet-Captions annotations.

## Results

On the proposed ActivityNet-RTL benchmark, LITA-13B achieves 28.6 mIOU and 25.9 Precision@0.5, nearly doubling the naive 'Slow Tokens Only' baseline (14.6 mIOU, 11.8 P@0.5) and substantially outperforming Video-LLaMA-v2 and Video-ChatGPT on explanation quality (GPT-Score 46.3 vs. 32.1 and 38.8). On the Video-based Text Generation benchmark, LITA achieves an average score of 3.04, surpassing the best prior method Video-ChatGPT (2.38) by 28%, with a 36% relative improvement in Temporal Understanding (2.68 vs. 1.98) and a 22% improvement in Correctness of Information (2.94 vs. 2.40).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LITA-13B outperforms all external baselines across every available metric. On ActivityNet-RTL, Video-LLaMA-v2 and Video-ChatGPT cannot produce timestamps, so only the GPT-Score (explanation quality) is comparable: LITA-13B scores 46.3 versus 32.1 (Video-LLaMA-v2) and 38.8 (Video-ChatGPT), a 44% and 19% relative improvement respectively. On the Video-based Text Generation benchmark (Table 2), LITA dominates all five evaluation axes: Correctness (2.94 vs. 2.40 for Video-ChatGPT), Detail (2.98 vs. 2.52), Context (3.43 vs. 2.62), Temporal Understanding (2.68 vs. 1.98), and Consistency (3.19 vs. 2.37). The internal ablation 'Slow Tokens Only' (14.6 mIOU) serves as the closest architectural baseline to prior Video LLMs; LITA-13B nearly doubles its mIOU (28.6) and P@0.5 (25.9), demonstrating the critical contribution of both time tokens and fast tokens. Scaling from 7B to 13B also yields consistent gains (24.1→28.6 mIOU), indicating that temporal reasoning benefits from model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

LITA builds directly on LLaVA's architecture (CLIP visual encoder + linear projection + Vicuna LLM) and extends it to video by introducing SlowFast-inspired token pooling and relative time tokens. It departs from prior Video LLMs (Video-LLaMA, Video-ChatGPT, VideoChat) that treat temporal localization as an afterthought, by making it a first-class training objective with dedicated data and architectural support. The proposed Reasoning Temporal Localization task draws conceptual inspiration from LISA's reasoning segmentation, transplanting the 'reason before localize' paradigm from spatial grounding to the temporal domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal localization is a fundamental capability that distinguishes video from images, yet existing Video LLMs largely ignore it; LITA provides a practical recipe (time tokens + SlowFast tokens + temporal data emphasis) that closes this gap and, as a side benefit, improves general video understanding metrics. The ActivityNet-RTL dataset and task formulation open a new evaluation axis that jointly tests reasoning and temporal grounding, which prior benchmarks do not capture. Limitations include the relatively small evaluation set (229 pairs), reliance on GPT-4 for explanation scoring (somewhat circular), and the fact that baseline comparisons on temporal metrics are weakened because competing models simply do not output timestamps. Open questions include how the approach scales to much longer videos, whether the discretization into T=100 chunks is sufficient for fine-grained tasks, and whether the time-token representation generalizes beyond the ActivityNet domain.
