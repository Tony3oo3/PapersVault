## Problem

Existing point-based dragging methods (DragGAN, DragDiffusion) suffer from two key flaws: (1) feature-difference-based point tracking is easily misled by visually similar distractor points, a problem exacerbated in diffusion models where intermediate features carry substantial noise, and (2) motion supervision is incomplete at certain optimization steps, causing latent quality to degrade and tracking to drift over long-range manipulations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241554/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image (either a GAN-generated image or a real image reconstructed via DDIM inversion into Stable Diffusion latent space), user-specified handle points and target points, and an optional mask delineating the flexible editing region. It outputs a single edited image in which the semantic content at each handle point has been driven to its corresponding target point while preserving the rest of the image. For the diffusion variant, a per-image LoRA is first fine-tuned to anchor identity, and MasaCtrl self-attention control is applied during final denoising to maintain consistency.

## Method

StableDrag introduces two modules that replace the vanilla feature-difference tracking and fixed motion supervision of DragGAN/DragDiffusion. First, a Discriminative Point Tracking (DPT) module learns a 1×C×1×1 convolutional filter per handle point via a Gaussian-target regression loss on the initial feature map; this filter is trained once (~1 s) before manipulation and then frozen, producing a confidence score map that is fused with the original feature-difference score via a weighted sum (λ=0.3) to robustly localize the updated handle point. Second, a Confident Motion Supervision (CMS) module monitors the peak tracking confidence s_i at each step: when s_i exceeds τ·s₁ (τ=0.4), the standard dynamic loss L₁ (pulling current patch features toward the target offset) is used; when it falls below the threshold, a template-anchored loss L₂ (pulling toward the initial handle-point features) is substituted to prevent content degradation. The framework is instantiated on both StyleGAN2 (StableDrag-GAN) and Stable Diffusion V1.5 (StableDrag-Diff), with the tracking filter operating on intermediate features of whichever backbone is used.

## Results

On DragBench (205 samples), StableDrag-Diff consistently outperforms DragDiffusion across all optimization step settings. At 60 steps, StableDrag-Diff achieves a Mean Distance of 36.36 (vs. 39.58 for DragDiffusion) and Image Fidelity of 0.893 (vs. 0.876), representing improvements of 3.22 in MD and 0.017 in IF. At 100 steps, the gap widens further with MD of 35.92 vs. 38.86 and IF of 0.869 vs. 0.863. The tracker training adds only ~1.1 s overhead to the overall drag process (~29 s for 60 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DragDiffusion, StableDrag-Diff wins on both Mean Distance and Image Fidelity at every optimization step setting (60, 80, 100), with the largest MD gain of 3.22 at 60 steps. Against DragGAN, the comparison is qualitative only (different input domains: StyleGAN2-generated vs. real images), but StableDrag-GAN demonstrates more precise point-level manipulation and better preservation of local details (e.g., bag, glasses, horse). Against FreeDrag, StableDrag-Diff produces more accurate dragging outcomes and better detail retention; notably, in a lion-editing example, FreeDrag requires 320 optimization steps with oscillating handle points along a linear path, while StableDrag-GAN reaches the target in only 46 steps along a more natural trajectory. FreeDrag also struggles with creative out-of-distribution content generation, whereas StableDrag-GAN handles such cases more robustly.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableDrag directly extends the point-based dragging paradigm established by DragGAN (GAN latent optimization with iterative motion supervision and feature-difference tracking) and DragDiffusion (adapting that pipeline to Stable Diffusion via LoRA fine-tuning and single-step latent optimization). Its key departure is borrowing discriminative tracking ideas from the visual object tracking literature (correlation filters, Siamese networks) to replace the naive nearest-neighbor feature search, and introducing a confidence-gated switch between dynamic and template-based motion supervision. The paper also positions itself against FreeDrag, which eliminates point tracking entirely via a linear search path, arguing that discriminative tracking plus confident supervision offers both greater flexibility and fewer optimization steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two identified failure modes—tracking drift under noisy diffusion features and incomplete per-step latent optimization—are practical bottlenecks that limit the usability of drag-based editing in real applications, and the proposed fixes are lightweight (a single 1×1 convolution filter trained in ~1 second, plus a threshold-based loss switch). However, the quantitative gains on DragBench are modest (≈3 MD, ≈0.02 IF over DragDiffusion), the evaluation is limited to 205 samples and a single diffusion backbone, and the local search strategy explicitly cannot disambiguate two nearly identical objects in the global image. Open questions include whether the discriminative tracking generalizes to video or 3D manipulation, whether the confidence threshold τ can be learned rather than hand-tuned, and whether the approach remains stable under very long-range or multi-object drags beyond the tested scenarios.
