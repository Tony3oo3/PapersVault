## Problem

Existing all-in-one adverse weather restoration methods are trained exclusively on synthetic paired data, creating a significant domain gap when deployed on real-world images. Additionally, these methods focus narrowly on visual clarity while neglecting the semantic context of scenes, limiting their utility for downstream vision tasks. The paper addresses both limitations by leveraging vision-language models to provide clearness and semantics supervision signals on unlabeled real-world data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240466/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes two data streams: paired synthetic images (Outdoor-Rain, RainDrop, SPA, OTS, Snow100K) for supervised training, and unlabeled real-world adverse weather images (URHI haze, manually collected rain and snow) for semi-supervised learning. The VLMs generate intermediate text artifacts—five-scale quality ratings, weather-condition prompt embeddings, and scene descriptions (negative weather → positive clear)—that serve as soft supervision. The final output is a restored RGB image from the MSBDN backbone, evaluated via no-reference metrics since no ground-truth clear images exist for real data.

## Method

WResVLM is a semi-supervised framework that integrates VLMs at two levels. For clearness, it first uses zero-shot VLM image assessment (converting token-level probabilities over five MOS ratings into a scalar score via softmax) to select and iteratively update pseudo-labels from a mean-teacher model, then performs weather prompt learning by optimizing four learnable CLIP text embeddings (clear/rain/haze/snow) to maximize cosine similarity between restored images and the 'clear' prompt. For semantics, it generates per-image negative descriptions (e.g., 'heavy rain') via LLaVA, converts them to positive descriptions (e.g., 'good weather') via Llama with in-context examples, and applies a contrastive CLIP-based loss pulling restored images toward the positive description. The overall loss combines supervised appearance, pseudo-label, weather prompt, semantic contrastive, and feature-similarity (Depth Anything encoder) terms, trained over four iterative rounds with progressive pseudo-label, prompt, and description updates.

## Results

On real-world adverse weather images (no ground truth available), the proposed method achieves the best scores across all six no-reference quality metrics on average: NIMA 5.084, MUSIQ 59.34, CLIP-IQA 0.456, LIQE 2.640, Q-Align 3.574, and the proposed VLM-Vis 0.387. The largest gains over the strongest baselines (PromptIR and DA-CLIP) appear in MUSIQ (+3.27 and +3.75) and VLM-Vis (+0.044 and +0.041), indicating substantially better perceived visibility. The method also leads in every individual weather condition (rain, haze, snow) for all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every metric and in every weather condition. The closest competitors are PromptIR (MUSIQ 56.07, CLIP-IQA 0.443) and DA-CLIP (MUSIQ 55.59, LIQE 2.438), which are the only baselines that also leverage vision-language models; the proposed method surpasses them by +3.27 and +3.75 MUSIQ respectively, and by +0.044 and +0.041 VLM-Vis. Among the all-in-one weather restoration baselines, TransWeather, TKL, WeatherDiff, and WGWS-Net all score 5–7 points lower in MUSIQ, reflecting their reliance on synthetic training data. MWDT achieves the second-best VLM-Vis (0.370) but introduces severe color distortion noted in the qualitative comparison. The general-purpose Restormer backbone, when fine-tuned, reaches MUSIQ 56.38 but still trails the proposed method by nearly 3 points, confirming that the VLM-guided semi-supervised strategy provides a meaningful advantage over simply scaling a strong restoration architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the all-in-one adverse weather removal line (TransWeather, TKL, WeatherDiff, WGWS-Net, MWDT, PromptIR, DA-CLIP) by addressing their shared limitation: training and evaluation on synthetic data with poor real-world generalization. It repurposes vision-language models—previously applied to image quality assessment (CLIP-IQA, LIQE, Q-Align) and prompt-based enhancement (Liang et al.)—as supervision signals for semi-supervised restoration on unlabeled real images. The framework combines mean-teacher pseudo-labeling with two VLM-derived losses (weather prompt learning for clearness, description-assisted semantics for content preservation), building on the prompt-learning paradigm from CLIP-based backlit enhancement and the in-context learning approach for LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practically important gap: weather restoration models that perform well on synthetic benchmarks degrade significantly on real-world images, and prior work has largely ignored semantic preservation alongside visual clarity. The VLM-as-supervisor paradigm for low-level vision tasks is a direction likely to be adopted more broadly, though the heavy computational cost of querying multiple large VLMs during training limits immediate scalability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other degradation types (fog, blur, low-light), whether smaller or distilled VLMs can replace the large models, and whether the iterative multi-round training can be compressed without sacrificing quality.
