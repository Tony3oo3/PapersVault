## Problem

Existing prompt-based continual learning methods optimize prompts without explicitly modeling inter-session class relationships, so they fail to construct clear margins between old and new class spaces. Additionally, when old samples inadvertently select new prompts during inference, a 'prompt-ambiguity overlap space' emerges that further entangles old and new class regions, making separation even harder.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241238/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and processes them through a frozen pre-trained ViT (Sup-21K, DINO-1K, or MoCo-1K) with session-specific learnable prompts inserted via cross-attention. During inference, session IDs are unavailable; a lightweight session indicator (trained on PTM feature prototypes) predicts the session for each sample, which then selects the appropriate prompt set. The output is a class label over all seen classes, with the method operating in a rehearsal-free setting where no old data is stored or replayed.

## Method

RCS-Prompt introduces an Inter-session Bidirectional Prompt Loss (L_dis) that splits new-session samples into two groups using a session indicator based on cosine similarity to stored PTM feature prototypes. 'ID-clean' samples (predicted to belong to the new session) are trained with standard cross-entropy over all classes, explicitly pushing their logits down on old classes to create discriminative regions. 'ID-noisy' samples (predicted to belong to old sessions) are relabeled to pseudo old classes and supervised with reverse cross-entropy loss, which adaptively down-weights gradients when the sample's score on the pseudo label is low, preventing excessive drift toward old class spaces. A Prompt Magnitude Normalization step rescales prompts to match feature magnitudes before cross-attention, ensuring prompts retain effective influence. The total training objective is L_ce + α·L_dis (α=0.4), and the method serves as a plug-in that can be added to existing prompt-based continual learning frameworks such as HiDe-Prompt or DualPrompt.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-100 with ViT-B/16 Sup-21K, HiDe-Prompt + RCS-Prompt achieves 93.34% FAA and 95.34% CAA, outperforming all baselines. On the more challenging Split ImageNet-R, the same configuration reaches 74.85% FAA and 77.52% CAA. With self-supervised pre-trained models (DINO-1K), the gains are particularly large: +2.05% FAA on CIFAR-100 and +5.20% FAA on ImageNet-R over HiDe-Prompt alone. The method also serves as a plug-in for DualPrompt, improving its DINO-1K CIFAR-100 FAA from 74.90% to 81.44%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCS-Prompt consistently outperforms all five baselines across every PTM and dataset combination. Against the strongest baseline HiDe-Prompt, RCS-Prompt gains +0.42% FAA on CIFAR-100 and +1.30% on ImageNet-R with Sup-21K, but the advantage widens dramatically with self-supervised models: +2.05% (CIFAR-100) and +5.20% (ImageNet-R) with DINO-1K, and +1.30% (CIFAR-100) and +5.19% (ImageNet-R) with MoCo-1K. Against DualPrompt, the plug-in yields even larger jumps, e.g., +6.54% FAA on CIFAR-100 with DINO-1K (81.44% vs. 74.90%). L2P, S-Prompt++, and CODA-Prompt are all substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10% FAA on CIFAR-100 under DINO-1K. The FFM (forgetting) metric also improves or matches across all settings, confirming that the gains come from better class-space separation rather than overfitting to new sessions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-based continual learning line (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, HiDe-Prompt) by identifying a specific failure mode—prompt-ambiguity overlap space—where old samples inadvertently attend to new prompts during inference, exacerbating class-space overlap. It builds directly on HiDe-Prompt as its primary baseline, adding an Inter-session Bidirectional Prompt Loss that repurposes the symmetric cross-entropy loss (originally designed for noisy labels) to handle ID-noisy samples whose features resemble old classes. The Prompt Magnitude Normalization is a practical, orthogonal addition addressing a previously underexplored issue of prompt-feature scale mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of prompt-ambiguity overlap space as a distinct and previously unaddressed failure mode provides a clearer diagnostic for why existing prompt-based methods struggle with inter-session separation, and the plug-in design (validated on both HiDe-Prompt and DualPrompt) makes the technique broadly applicable. However, the method's reliance on a session indicator trained on frozen PTM features introduces a potential bottleneck in very long sequences where class boundaries blur, and the evaluation is limited to image classification with ViT backbones. Open questions include whether the bidirectional relabeling strategy generalizes to other modalities (e.g., language, video) and whether the approach remains effective when the number of sessions grows substantially beyond 10.
