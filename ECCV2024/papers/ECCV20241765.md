## Problem

Arbitrary neural style transfer suffers from over-rendering artefacts caused by feature redundancy in the latent space and under-stylisation due to heterogeneous style statistics, yet existing methods lack a principled mechanism to learn compact, decoupled representations that simultaneously preserve content structure and transfer style faithfully. The paper addresses this by framing representation learning for style transfer through an information-theoretic lens, targeting both inter-module redundancy and intra-module channel coupling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241765/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images—a content image and a style reference image—and produces a single stylized image. Experiments use 512×512 resolution for standard evaluation and 768×768 for higher-resolution tests, with content images drawn from MS-COCO and style images from WikiArt. The style transfer mechanism itself is the AdaIN adaptive instance normalization module, applied to features extracted by the reversible flow encoder.

## Method

The architecture is a normalizing flow with 20 InfoMinRev modules and 2 squeeze layers, where each InfoMinRev block uses a parallel attention-convolution (PAC) coupling layer with a learnable mixing parameter α to combine convolutional and attentional transformations in a bijective manner. Three information-theoretic losses drive training: (1) a mutual information minimization loss (estimated via Gaussian kernel density estimation) between consecutive module outputs to compress representations, (2) a Barlow Twins cross-correlation loss that maximizes diagonal and minimizes off-diagonal entries of the channel correlation matrix between content and stylized features to decouple channels, and (3) a Jensen-Shannon divergence style loss that aligns the full feature distributions (mean and variance) of style and stylized images rather than merely matching their statistics, with a warm-up phase using standard L2 style loss to mitigate vanishing gradients in non-overlapping regions.

## Results

On the MS-COCO/WikiArt test set (1200 stylised images at 512×512), the proposed method achieves the best SSIM (0.496) and lowest content loss (0.491) among all eight compared methods, outperforming the next-best IEContraAST (SSIM 0.445, content loss 0.605). The style loss is 0.652, competitive with SANet (0.587) and CAP-VSTNet (0.608) while far surpassing IEContraAST (1.853). Inference time is 0.081 s per image, faster than ArtFlow (0.134 s) and CAP-VSTNet (0.096 s) but slower than AdaIN (0.003 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best content preservation (SSIM 0.496, content loss 0.491) across all seven baselines, with the largest margins over ArtFlow (+0.131 SSIM) and AdaIN (+0.145 SSIM). Against the closest competitor IEContraAST, it improves SSIM by 0.051 and content loss by 0.114, while also dramatically reducing style loss from 1.853 to 0.652. On style loss, the method is competitive but not the best: SANet (0.587) and CAP-VSTNet (0.608) achieve slightly lower values, though both sacrifice content preservation (SSIM 0.407 and 0.408 respectively). Compared to the other flow-based methods ArtFlow and CAP-VSTNet, the proposed approach is faster (0.081 s vs. 0.134 s and 0.096 s) while improving all quality metrics. In the user study (20 participants, 25 image pairs), the method ranks first on all three criteria (content preservation 1.43, style consistency 1.63, overall preference 1.50), ahead of IEContraAST (2.14/3.25/2.38) and CAP-VSTNet (2.88/4.63/2.50).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-based style transfer lineage (ArtFlow → CAP-VSTNet) by introducing an information-theoretic compression objective between consecutive reversible modules (InfoMinRev), departing from prior work that relied solely on architectural design to manage feature redundancy. It repurposes the Barlow Twins loss from self-supervised learning as a soft channel-independence constraint, relaxing the strict independence enforced in classical flow models like NICE and Glow to preserve content-style interactions. The Jensen-Shannon divergence style loss replaces the standard mean-variance L2 objective, drawing on distributional alignment ideas from information theory to address the under/over-stylisation imbalance that statistical style losses exhibit across heterogeneous reference images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, unified information-theoretic framework for the long-standing tension between content preservation and style transferability in arbitrary NST, showing that feature redundancy (not just architectural choices) is the root cause of artefacts. The reversible architecture ensures that compressive representations do not sacrifice content fidelity, and the JSD style loss offers a more balanced alternative to mean-variance matching that practitioners can adopt independently. Limitations include reliance on KDE-based mutual information estimation (potentially costly at scale), evaluation limited to 512×512 and 768×768 resolutions, and the use of a fixed VGG19 backbone for both content and style losses. Open questions include whether the information-theoretic compression strategy generalises to video style transfer, 3D content, or other generative tasks where feature redundancy is a bottleneck.
