## Problem

In incremental object detection, images simultaneously contain objects from past, present, and future stages, but only current-stage annotations are available. This information asymmetry causes inconsistent optimization objectives across stages: old objects are mislabeled as background (aggravating catastrophic forgetting) and future objects are also treated as background (hindering subsequent learning). Previous methods focused on regularizing against forgetting of old classes but ignored the impact of future classes on current training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240430/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images and outputs bounding boxes with class labels, built on a Faster R-CNN (R50) two-stage detector. Training is strictly rehearsal-free: no stored exemplars or replay memory are used. During training, the current model additionally consumes pseudo-labels generated by the previous-stage model and attention maps derived from backbone feature activations to identify regions to exclude from negative sampling.

## Method

BPF consists of three components that align optimization objectives across all incremental stages. (1) Bridge the Past: high-confidence predictions from the old model on current images are filtered by confidence threshold and NMS, then combined with current annotations as pseudo-labels to restore supervision for old classes. (2) Bridge the Future: an attention map computed from backbone feature activations (softmax of summed absolute feature magnitudes) is used to score each RoI; regions with high attention and objectness scores are excluded from negative samples, preventing future objects from being learned as background. (3) Distillation with Future (DwF): two teacher models are used class-by-class — the old model for old classes and an intermediate model (trained fully supervised on current data) for current classes — with each teacher's background probability expanded by the other teacher's class probabilities, enabling fine-grained KL-divergence distillation that preserves both old and new class discrimination.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2007 single-step settings, BPF achieves 74.1, 72.7, 72.9, and 73.0 mAP@0.5 (all 20 classes) under 19-1, 15-5, 10-10, and 5-15 splits respectively, outperforming the strongest memory-free baseline MMA by 3.4%, 2.8%, 6.3%, and 13.4%. On MS COCO 2017, BPF reaches 36.2 AP under the 70-10 setting, surpassing MMA by 6.0% and ABR by 5.1%. In multi-step settings (e.g., 10-1 with 10 tasks), BPF improves over MMA by 23.6% on overall mAP@0.5 while remaining rehearsal-free.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPF consistently outperforms MMA [5] (the primary memory-free distillation baseline) across all four single-step VOC settings by 3.4% (19-1), 2.8% (15-5), 6.3% (10-10), and 13.4% (5-15) on overall mAP@0.5, with the largest gap in the 5-15 setting where old-class forgetting is most severe. Against the strongest rehearsal-based method ABR [41], BPF wins by 3.2%, 1.7%, 0.9%, and 3.6% on the same settings while requiring no stored exemplars. On COCO 2017, BPF surpasses MMA by 1.4% AP (40-40) and 6.0% AP (70-10), and outperforms ABR by 5.1% AP on 70-10, though it is marginally below ABR (34.4 vs. 34.5) on 40-40. In multi-step settings, BPF improves over MMA by up to 23.6% (10-1, 10 tasks) and beats ABR by 0.8–4.1% on overall mAP@0.5 in 10-5 and 5-5, though it trails ABR slightly in the extreme 10-2 and 10-1 settings due to very limited per-stage data. BPF also outperforms other distillation-based methods (MVC, PPAS, PROB, PseudoRM) by 1–6% on the 1-20 metric across settings, confirming that the Bridge Past/Future and DwF components address limitations in prior distillation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge-distillation-based line of work on incremental object detection, building most directly on MMA (Cermelli et al., CVPR 2022), which introduced Unbiased Knowledge Distillation to handle missing old-class annotations. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that information asymmetry is bidirectional: not only do past classes lack supervision in the current stage, but future classes are also erroneously treated as background, creating inconsistent optimization objectives in both directions. BPF addresses this with pseudo-labeling from the old model (bridging the past) and attention-based exclusion of salient regions from negative samples (bridging the future), combined with a dual-teacher Distillation with Future loss that replaces MMA's unified background+old-class treatment with class-by-class distillation from two complementary teachers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a previously underappreciated source of degradation in IOD: the misclassification of future-stage objects as background in the current stage, which poisons future learning. This reframing opens a new design axis for incremental detectors beyond the standard 'preserve old knowledge' paradigm. A noted limitation is that when only one class is added per stage, old-class objects may rarely co-occur in current training images, weakening the pseudo-labeling signal; the authors suggest copy-paste augmentation as a partial remedy. Open questions include how the approach scales to very long incremental sequences (beyond 10 stages) and whether the attention-based future-object identification generalizes to open-vocabulary or few-shot detection settings.
