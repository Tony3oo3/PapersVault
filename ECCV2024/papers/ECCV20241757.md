## Problem

Traditional scene graph generation is confined to a closed set of predefined object and relation categories, limiting real-world applicability. While object-centric open-vocabulary SGG has received recent attention, the more challenging problem of recognizing unseen *relations* (and the combination of unseen objects and relations) remains largely unexplored, with no unified framework addressing all four node/edge open-vocabulary settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241757/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a scene graph consisting of up to 100 object detections (each with a 4-d bounding box and an open-vocabulary category label) plus pairwise relationship labels between detected object pairs. Category prediction is not done via a fixed classifier but by computing dot-product similarity between visual features and text-encoder embeddings of all candidate noun phrases or relation terms, making the vocabulary set flexible at inference time. The text prompt is constructed by concatenating sampled noun phrases and relation categories (up to M=80 positive and negative words each), similar to Grounding DINO's prompt design.

## Method

OvSGTR is a DETR-like end-to-end transformer with a frozen Swin image backbone, a frozen BERT text encoder, and a deformable-attention transformer decoder that jointly decodes object queries and relation queries. The key architectural choice is replacing fixed classification heads with a visual-concept alignment: node categories are predicted by sigmoid dot-product similarity between hidden features and word embeddings, and edge categories are predicted by a lightweight two-layer MLP relation head (concatenating subject, object, and a single learned relation query) whose output is scored against relation text features via binary cross-entropy. To address the absence of large-scale relation-annotated data, the model is first pre-trained on image-caption pairs where an off-the-shelf language parser extracts (subject, relation, object) triplets as weak pseudo-labels. During fine-tuning on manually annotated SGG data, a knowledge-distillation loss (L1 distance between student and teacher edge features on negative samples) is added to prevent catastrophic forgetting of the rich relation semantic space learned during pre-training, with the pre-trained model serving as a frozen teacher.

## Results

On the VG150 benchmark, OvSGTR (Swin-B) achieves R@20/50/100 of 27.8/36.4/42.4 and mR@20/50/100 of 5.2/7.4/9.0 under Closed-set SGG, outperforming the strongest prior method VS3 (Swin-L) by 1.4% on R@100 while using fewer trainable parameters (41M vs. 124M) and lower inference latency (0.19s vs. 0.24s). Under the most challenging OvD+R-SGG setting, OvSGTR (Swin-B) reaches R@50/100 of 17.11/21.02 on joint base+novel categories, surpassing VS3 (Swin-T) by 11.23/13.82 points. On novel relation recognition (OvR-SGG), OvSGTR (Swin-B) achieves R@50/100 of 16.39/19.72, whereas all prior methods score 0.00, demonstrating the first non-trivial open-vocabulary relation prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OvSGTR outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the most direct open-vocabulary competitor VS3 [48], OvSGTR (Swin-T) gains +3.8% R@50 and +5.4% R@100 on Closed-set SGG, +19.6%/+20.8% R@50/R@100 on novel objects (OvD-SGG), and achieves 13.45 vs. 0.00 R@50 on novel relations (OvR-SGG) — a setting where VS3 and all other methods completely fail. On the fully open-vocabulary OvD+R-SGG setting, OvSGTR (Swin-B) scores 17.11/21.02 R@50/100 on joint categories versus VS3's 5.88/7.20, a 2.9× improvement. Among closed-set baselines, OvSGTR surpasses HL-Net [21] (26.0/33.7/38.1) and MOTIFS [46] (25.5/32.8/37.2) on R@20/50/100, and notably achieves better mR@K (5.2/7.4/9.0) than MOTIFS (5.0/6.8/7.9), indicating superior long-tail handling. For language-supervised SGG baselines (LSWS [44], UNITER [4]), OvSGTR trained on COCO captions alone achieves 6.61/8.92/10.90 R@20/50/100, exceeding UNITER+Li et al. grounding (5.42/6.74/7.62) and VS3+GLIP-L (5.59/7.30/8.62) with a lighter backbone. The knowledge distillation component is critical: without it (λ=0), novel relation R@50 collapses from 10.78 to 0.32, confirming that all prior methods' zero scores on novel relations stem from catastrophic forgetting rather than architectural limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary SGG line initiated by VS3 (Zhang et al., CVPR 2023) and SVRP (He et al., ECCV 2022), which handled only open-vocabulary objects while keeping relations in a closed set. Architecturally, it inherits the DETR-like end-to-end design from SGTR (Li et al., CVPR 2022) and the frozen visual/text encoder paradigm from Grounding DINO (Liu et al., 2023), but departs by introducing a visual-concept alignment for edges (relations) in addition to nodes, and by using image-caption triplets as weak supervision for relation-aware pre-training. The key conceptual departure is the four-setting taxonomy (Closed, OvD, OvR, OvD+R) that makes explicit what prior work treated as a single 'open-vocabulary' problem, and the observation that relation open-vocabulary learning suffers catastrophic forgetting that object open-vocabulary learning does not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that fine-tuning a caption-pretrained model on SGG annotations causes near-total collapse of novel-relation recognition (R@50 dropping from ~10.8 to 0.32 without distillation) is practically important: it warns that the standard pretrain-then-finetune recipe for open-vocabulary tasks does not transfer straightforwardly to relations, and motivates the distillation-based retention strategy. The unified four-setting framework provides a clearer evaluation protocol for the community, and the open-source code lowers the barrier for follow-up work. However, evaluation is confined to VG150 (150 objects, 50 relations), the triplet extraction relies on a brittle off-the-shelf parser, and the authors themselves flag the open question of whether LLMs can replace the parser or whether scene graphs can in turn reduce LLM hallucination—both of which could substantially reshape the pipeline.
