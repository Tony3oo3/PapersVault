## Problem

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has been proposed as a defense against availability poisons, but the paper demonstrates that SSL still learns poison features alongside genuine ones, particularly on high-resolution, many-class datasets like ImageNet-100 where existing defenses (augmentation-based, adversarial training on SL) degrade significantly. The key unexplored question is how to leverage the known vulnerability of supervised learning to poisons to actively improve SSL's robustness rather than treating the two paradigms independently.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) that may carry imperceptible availability-poison perturbations bounded by a small ε. During training, each image is augmented into two views, and one view is further perturbed by an L∞-bounded adversarial offset (ε_AT = 4/255) generated via 10-step PGD. The output is a class label from a single linear head on a 512-dim ResNet-18 encoder, but the primary learning objective is instance-discriminative representation learning via a 3-layer MoCo-style MLP projector.

## Method

VESPR is a multi-task framework that jointly trains a ResNet-18 encoder with a contrastive (InfoNCE) loss and a supervised cross-entropy loss. The critical mechanism is that the CE loss gradient is used to generate adversarial perturbations via projected gradient descent on one of the two augmented views; this adversarially perturbed view replaces the original in the contrastive loss computation, effectively creating poison-obfuscating augmentations. The insight is that a poisoned SL classifier amplifies shortcut signals, so adversarial examples crafted against it maximally muddle those signals, steering the SSL encoder toward robust, clean features. The combined loss L_VESPR = α·L_CL + β·L_CE balances instance discrimination with class-specific learning, while the adversarial augmentation ensures the contrastive objective focuses on genuine image content rather than poison shortcuts.

## Results

On ImageNet-100, VESPR achieves a minimum poison accuracy (Psn Min) of 45.20% and average poison accuracy (Psn Avg) of 55.37% across seven availability poisons, surpassing the best prior defense (SSL/SimCLR) by 16% and 9% respectively. On CIFAR-10, VESPR reaches 80.31% Psn Min and 86.74% Psn Avg, outperforming the next-best method (SSL) by 13% and 5%. VESPR also maintains a competitive clean-data accuracy of 74.84% on ImageNet-100 and 89.35% on CIFAR-10, close to the best clean baselines (CutMix at 81.08% and 95.78% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VESPR outperforms all six baselines on both Psn Min and Psn Avg across ImageNet-100 and CIFAR-10. Against the strongest prior defense, SSL/SimCLR [12], VESPR improves Psn Min from 14.80% to 45.20% (+30.4 pts) and Psn Avg from 46.05% to 55.37% (+9.3 pts) on ImageNet-100; the gap is smaller on CIFAR-10 (80.31% vs 66.58% Psn Min). SL+AT [57] is the second-best baseline (Psn Min 28.92%, Psn Avg 44.29% on ImageNet-100), but VESPR still leads by 16.3 and 11.1 points respectively. Augmentation-based defenses (Cutout [21], Mixup [87], CutMix [85]) are consistently weak, with Psn Min values below 10% on ImageNet-100, though CutMix achieves the highest clean accuracy (81.08%). ISS [53] performs well on specific poisons (e.g., 67.52% on RUE8) but collapses to 3.58% on CUDA, yielding the worst Psn Min among all methods. SSL+AT [42] (adversarial contrastive learning) achieves Psn Min of 31.78% and Psn Avg of 44.81%, still trailing VESPR by 13.4 and 10.6 points on ImageNet-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

VESPR sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: contrastive SSL (SimCLR/InfoNCE), PGD-based adversarial training (Madry et al.), and multi-task learning that combines supervised and self-supervised objectives. Prior poison-defense literature treated SL-based defenses (AT, augmentations) and SSL as separate, competing strategies, with SSL positioned as a robust alternative that bypasses label-dependent shortcut learning. This paper departs from that framing by showing SSL is not inherently robust on larger datasets and instead *exploits* SL's known vulnerability to poisons—using the poisoned classifier's gradients to generate adversarial perturbations that obfuscate poison features for the SSL objective. The multi-task SSL+SL combination with SL-guided AT is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such architecture applied to availability poison defense.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16% improvement in minimum poison accuracy on ImageNet-100 is practically significant for deployment scenarios where an adversary selects the most effective attack. The finding that SSL's robustness degrades substantially on high-resolution, many-class datasets (e.g., CP8 drops SSL to 14.80%) challenges the common assumption that contrastive learning is a broadly effective poison defense. Limitations include the clean-accuracy trade-off (VESPR's 74.84% clean accuracy trails CutMix's 81.08%), evaluation restricted to ResNet-18 and a 100-class ImageNet subset, and no consideration of dirty-label or federated settings. Open questions include whether the SL-guided AT mechanism generalizes to vision transformers, full ImageNet, or non-additive poisons with larger perturbation budgets.
