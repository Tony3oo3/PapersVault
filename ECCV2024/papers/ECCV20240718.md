## Problem

Current facial makeup transfer methods rely on unpaired learning, which necessitates complex face-prompt pipelines (landmarks, parsing maps) and large model sizes (>1M parameters) to compensate for inaccurate supervision signals like color histogram matching. These requirements make mobile deployment impractical, as phones demand models under 100K parameters and inference under 30 ms. The paper asks whether a tiny, prompt-free makeup model can be trained if the fundamental data bottleneck—lack of paired makeup annotations—is solved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240718/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TinyBeauty takes a single RGB face image (256×256 at inference) with no auxiliary inputs—no landmarks, no parsing maps, no style codes—and outputs a makeup residual that is added element-wise to the input to yield the final makeup-applied image. The DDA, used only at training-data generation time, takes a non-makeup face image plus a style reference (a cropped, mask-isolated makeup image encoded by OpenCLIP ViT-H/14) and an identity embedding, and produces a paired makeup image. Because the output is a residual rather than a full image, the model can be applied to inputs of arbitrary resolution without degrading original texture.

## Method

Data Amplify Learning (DAL) replaces unpaired training with a two-stage pipeline. First, a Diffusion-based Data Amplifier (DDA) fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v1.5 via LoRA on just 5 manually annotated makeup pairs, using a Residual Diffusion Model (two parallel conditioned/unconditioned denoising branches whose outputs are combined as y = F(x) + λ_m·R_m + λ_d·R_d) to preserve high-frequency facial texture, and a Fine-Grained Makeup Module that couples an Identity Preservation Block, a Style Preservation Block (visual style tokens from a pre-trained image encoder), and per-region facial masks (face, lips, eyes) to enforce precise, identity-preserving makeup placement. Second, the DDA generates ~4000 paired images that train TinyBeauty—a 14-convolution-layer UNet with only 80K parameters—under a simple L1 reconstruction loss plus a Sobel-edge loss dedicated to eyeliner delineation, eliminating the need for adversarial-only or histogram-matching objectives. The residual-output design and the absence of any face-prompt pre-processing yield a 2.18 ms iPhone 13 inference time (460 fps) while surpassing EleGANt by 5.21 dB PSNR on FFHQ.

## Results

On FFHQ, TinyBeauty achieves PSNR 35.39 dB, FID 8.03, and LPIPS 0.0146, outperforming the strongest prior method EleGANt (PSNR 30.18, FID 25.47, LPIPS 0.0396) by +5.21 dB in PSNR (17.3% improvement). On the MT dataset, TinyBeauty reaches PSNR 34.26 dB and FID 9.33, surpassing EleGANt by +1.49 dB. In the user study, TinyBeauty receives 86.56% Rank-1 votes versus 10.46% for EleGANt. TinyBeauty achieves all this with only 0.08M parameters and 2.18 ms inference latency on iPhone 13, compared to 10.27M parameters and N/A (cannot deploy) for EleGANt.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TinyBeauty dominates all five prior makeup-transfer baselines (BeautyGAN, PSGAN, SCGAN, EleGANt, BeautyREC) on both FFHQ and MT across PSNR, FID, and LPIPS, with the largest gap over EleGANt (+5.21 dB PSNR on FFHQ, +1.49 dB on MT). When the two strongest baselines (EleGANt and BeautyREC) are retrained under the same DAL protocol, TinyBeauty still matches or exceeds them: EleGANt* reaches PSNR 35.45 on FFHQ vs. TinyBeauty's 35.39, but TinyBeauty achieves a substantially lower FID (8.03 vs. 10.78) and LPIPS (0.0146 vs. 0.0148), indicating better perceptual quality. On MT, TinyBeauty (PSNR 34.26) slightly trails EleGANt* (34.65) but leads in FID (9.33 vs. 11.57) and LPIPS (0.0181 vs. 0.0164). For the DDA component, the paper compares against ControlNet, IP-Adapter, and InstantID (Fig. 5); DDA outperforms all three in preserving facial identity and maintaining consistent makeup color/style, whereas IP-Adapter and InstantID fail to retain fine facial details like wrinkles. In the user study, TinyBeauty earns 86.56% first-place votes, far ahead of EleGANt (10.46%) and all other methods, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived makeup quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant unpaired-learning paradigm in facial makeup transfer (BeautyGAN, PSGAN, SCGAN, EleGANt, BeautyREC), which relies on adversarial training, face-prompt pipelines (landmarks, parsing), and approximate supervision (histogram matching, EMD). Instead, it introduces Data Amplify Learning (DAL), which uses a LoRA-fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model as a training-time data generator to produce paired makeup images from just 5 annotated seeds, enabling a tiny 14-layer CNN (TinyBeauty) to be trained with straightforward L1 supervision. The Diffusion-based Data Amplifier itself builds on IP-Adapter-style image prompting and OpenCLIP encoding for style/identity conditioning, while the Residual Diffusion Model and Fine-Grained Makeup Module are novel components designed to address detail vanishing and identity drift in diffusion-based portrait editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for mobile deployment: an 80K-parameter model achieving 2.18 ms inference on iPhone 13 (460 fps) while outperforming 10M+ parameter GAN baselines by over 5 dB PSNR demonstrates that training-time compute (diffusion data generation) can be traded for extreme inference-time efficiency. The broader implication is that the DAL paradigm—using a heavy generative model to synthesize paired training data for a lightweight inference model—could generalize to other image editing tasks where paired annotations are expensive. Limitations include the two-stage training pipeline (DDA must be trained first), the restriction to 5 predefined makeup styles, and the fact that the DDA itself is a large diffusion model, so the total training cost is non-trivial even if inference is tiny.
