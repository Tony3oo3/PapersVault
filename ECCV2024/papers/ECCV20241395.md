## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors learn specific fake patterns (e.g., swap boundaries, frequency anomalies) that generalize poorly to unseen deepfake algorithms. The paper argues that detectors should instead learn consistent real face appearances, so that any face exhibiting anomalies not found in real faces is classified as fake, rather than matching against a limited set of known forgery artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241395/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB face crops (extracted from video frames via RetinaFace with 15% margin) along with 81 pre-extracted Dlib landmarks used to construct the Face Disturbance masks. During training, inputs include real faces, texture-anomalous pseudo-fakes (I_ta), and structure-anomalous pseudo-fakes (I_sa); during inference, only a single face image is input. The model produces a binary real/fake probability and, for the RAM branch, a pixel-level recovered face image where only the disturbed patches (defined by a binary mask) are predicted, leaving undisturbed regions unchanged.

## Method

The core contribution is the Real Appearance Modeling (RAM) task, which trains the detector to recover original faces from controlled disturbances, thereby learning real face appearance rather than fake patterns. Face Disturbance generates pseudo-fakes via two mechanisms: Texture Disturbance (TD) augments inner or outer face regions in color/brightness/clarity and blends them with a soft, elastically-deformed landmark mask to create texture inconsistency; Structure Disturbance (SD) covers random landmark areas with tiled surrounding textures using the Fast Marching Method, preserving original texture while destroying local structure. The Recovery Autoencoder (RAE) uses a ViT-Base encoder with a Multi-layer Token Fusion (MTF) block that fuses shallow, mid, and deep encoder features via cross-layer attention, followed by an 8-layer transformer decoder. To prevent trivial identity mapping, tokens in disturbed regions are replaced with a learnable mask token before decoding, forcing the model to reconstruct those regions from context. Training is multi-task: cross-entropy classification loss plus L1 reconstruction loss (weighted α=0.1) over masked patches only, with the recovery target fixed to the undisturbed reference (I_outer for TD, I_ori for SD), making the problem well-posed unlike masked autoencoding.

## Results

Trained on FF++(HQ) real faces only, RAE achieves video-level AUC of 95.5% on Celeb-DF v2, 99.0% on DFD, 80.2% on DFDC, 89.5% on DFDC preview, and 88.4% on WildDeepfake, outperforming the previous best methods by 2.6%, 3.4%, and 3.2% on CDF, DFDC, and DFDCp respectively. On diffusion-generated deepfakes (DFF), RAE attains 70.8% average frame-level AUC, surpassing the prior best (ENB4+SBI at 68.6%) by 2.2%. RAE also achieves the lowest false-positive rates on real faces: 36.9% on FFHQ and 6.9% on VoxCeleb, compared to 57.7%/59.2% for ENB4+SBI and 62.3%/41.7% for RECCE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAE outperforms all 10 compared methods across every reported test set. Against the strongest pseudo-fake-trained baselines (ENB4+SBI, SeeABLE, AltFreezing), RAE gains 2.6–8.2 points on CDF, 4.3–13.2 points on DFDC, and 3.2–15.5 points on DFDCp, with the largest margins on DFDC where reenactment deepfakes dominate. Against fake-face-trained methods (TALL, IDDDM, Mover, RFFR, FTCN, RealForensics), RAE leads by 4.7–8.6 points on CDF and 3.4–12.4 points on DFDC, confirming that Face Disturbance pseudo-fakes plus RAM generalize better than direct deepfake supervision. On WildDeepfake (the only dataset with online-collected fakes), RAE achieves 88.4% vs. Mover's 82.0%. On diffusion deepfakes (DFF), RAE beats ENB4+SBI by 2.2% average AUC and dramatically outperforms RECCE (49.9%) and UIA-ViT (58.3%), whose T2I scores fall below 50% (worse than random). On real-face false-positive rates, RAE reduces FP to 6.9% on VoxCeleb versus 18.9% (UIA-ViT) and 59.2% (ENB4+SBI), validating that RAM teaches the model a faithful real-face prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in deepfake detection of learning specific fake patterns (boundaries, frequency anomalies, motion inconsistencies) by instead proposing to learn consistent real face appearances through a reconstruction objective. It extends the pseudo-fake construction line (SBI, AltFreezing, PCL+I2G) by adding a Structure Disturbance component that goes beyond the texture inconsistencies those methods introduce, and it differentiates itself from MAE-based detectors (Mover, RFFR) by arguing that its Face Disturbance produces a well-posed recovery problem with a unique target rather than the multiple feasible solutions inherent in masked reconstruction. The Recovery Autoencoder architecture repurposes a standard ViT-Base encoder with a lightweight decoder and a Multi-layer Token Fusion block, making the contribution primarily in the training objective and data construction rather than in architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from 'learn what fakes look like' to 'learn what real faces look like' addresses a fundamental generalization bottleneck: detectors trained on specific deepfake types fail when confronted with novel generation algorithms, which is the realistic deployment scenario. The dramatically lower false-positive rates on real faces (6.9% on VoxCeleb vs. 41–59% for baselines) suggest the approach is more practically deployable in authentication settings where misclassifying real users is costly. However, performance on diffusion-generated deepfakes remains modest (70.8% average AUC), and the method has only been validated at ViT-Base scale, leaving open whether the RAM objective scales effectively with larger backbones or truly diverse real-face distributions. The reliance on controlled Face Disturbance as a proxy for real deepfake artifacts also raises the question of how well the learned 'real appearance' transfers to fundamentally different forgery modalities (e.g., 3D morphable models, full-body synthesis).
