## Problem

Existing text-to-3D methods either require expensive per-prompt optimization (up to an hour) or use amortized optimization (e.g., ATT3D) that is limited to small, curated prompt sets, cannot capture high-frequency geometry and texture details, and generalizes poorly to diverse prompts. The key gap is scaling amortized text-to-3D synthesis to orders of magnitude larger and more diverse prompt sets while maintaining high visual quality and robustness.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a text prompt (encoded via CLIP) and a fixed dummy sphere point cloud, producing a textured 3D mesh with a UV-mapped albedo texture image in ~400 ms on a single A6000 GPU. During training, real point clouds from 3D assets are gradually annealed to the dummy input over the last 5000 iterations of stage-1. The geometry network outputs a triplane-based density field (rendered at 256 px via VolSDF in stage-1, extracted as a mesh via Marching Cubes and rasterized at 1024 px in stage-2), while the texture network predicts per-texel RGB in UV space.

## Method

LATTE3D uses two networks—a geometry network G and a texture network T—sharing a U-Net encoder/decoder over a triplane representation, with cross-attention to CLIP text embeddings at every residual block. Training proceeds in two amortized stages: stage-1 optimizes volumetric geometry using a 3D-aware SDS loss from MVDream blended with a mask-based regularization loss against retrieved 3D shapes, while stage-2 freezes G and refines T with depth-conditional ControlNet SDS at 512 resolution and an upsampled triplane (128→480) for high-frequency texture. Three 3D-data mechanisms enable scaling to ~100k prompts: reconstruction pretraining on Objaverse shapes to stabilize early training, the multiview-consistent MVDream prior for stronger supervision, and explicit shape regularization to prevent geometric drift. A point-cloud annealing schedule bridges the gap between real 3D inputs during training and the dummy input at inference, and an optional test-time optimization (freezing geometry, fine-tuning texture) further boosts quality in under 10 minutes.

## Results

LATTE3D generates textured 3D meshes in 400 ms on a single A6000 GPU, achieving a Render-FID of 180.58 (seen) and 190.00 (unseen DreamFusion prompts) with a CLIP score of 0.2608/0.2605. With optional 10-minute test-time optimization, Render-FID improves to 171.69/178.37 and CLIP to 0.2800/0.3000. In user studies, LATTE3D at 400 ms is preferred over MVDream at 36 min optimization on seen prompts (51.5% vs 48.5%), representing a ~10× reduction in total GPU cost (215 s vs 2160 s per prompt).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MVDream (the strongest per-prompt optimization baseline), LATTE3D at 400 ms is preferred by users over MVDream at 36 min on seen prompts (51.5% vs 48.5%) while being ~10× faster in total GPU cost; on unseen prompts MVDream at 36 min still wins (89.3% preference), but LATTE3D-opt at 10 min narrows the gap substantially. Against LGM (5 s inference), LATTE3D is preferred in 73.8% of seen and 63.6% of unseen user comparisons, and achieves a lower Render-FID on seen prompts (180.58 vs 178.67 is close, but LATTE3D wins on unseen: 190.00 vs 166.40 — actually LGM wins on unseen FID). Against 3DTopia/Instant3D (20 s), LATTE3D is clearly superior on all metrics: lower Render-FID (180.58 vs 217.29 seen), higher CLIP (0.2608 vs 0.1837), and preferred in 79.2% of seen user comparisons. The key trade-off is that per-prompt optimization methods (MVDream) still achieve better absolute FID and CLIP scores given sufficient compute, but LATTE3D delivers competitive quality at an order-of-magnitude lower latency, making it suitable for interactive use.

## Relation to Prior Work

LATTE3D extends the amortized text-to-3D paradigm introduced by ATT3D, which first demonstrated that a single shared model could generalize across a set of prompts at inference time. The key departures are threefold: (1) it amortizes both the volumetric geometry stage and the surface-based texture refinement stage, whereas ATT3D only amortized stage-1; (2) it scales from ~2,400 prompts to over 100,000 by incorporating 3D-aware diffusion priors (MVDream), 3D shape regularization, and reconstruction pretraining; and (3) it replaces ATT3D's hypernetwork with a triplane U-Net architecture that supports higher-resolution texture generation. The two-stage SDS pipeline itself is inherited from DreamFusion and Magic3D, and the use of MVDream as a multiview-consistent prior follows the broader trend of making 2D diffusion models 3D-aware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400ms inference time and 10× reduction in total optimization cost make interactive text-to-3D creation practical for content pipelines, and the 100k-prompt training set (gpt-101k) is a useful resource for the community. However, the method inherits SDS's reliance on 2D diffusion model semantics, limiting fine-grained part-level control, and the geometry is frozen during stage-2 and test-time optimization, so stage-1 geometric errors cannot be corrected. An open question the authors flag is whether amortized training can be made stable enough to allow geometry refinement in stage-2, which would close the quality gap with per-prompt optimization methods like MVDream at 36 minutes.
