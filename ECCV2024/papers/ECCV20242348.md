## Problem

Compressing unconditional GANs for edge deployment remains difficult because, unlike conditional GANs, there is no per-instance correspondence between teacher and student outputs, requiring distribution-level matching rather than instance-level matching. Existing methods either suffer severe performance degradation at high compression rates, demand costly auxiliary procedures (manual labeling, MCMC sampling), or fail to maintain the Nash equilibrium between the pruned generator and discriminator, leading to training instability.

## Input / Output

The compressed student generator takes the same latent code z as the full teacher StyleGAN2 and produces RGB images at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 resolution, but with up to 99.69% fewer parameters (e.g., 2.35M vs. 24.77M) and correspondingly fewer FLOPs. The method is architecture-agnostic within the GAN family, demonstrated on StyleGAN2, BigGAN, and SNGAN across face, church, and cat image datasets. Evaluation uses FID, Precision, and Recall computed via NVIDIA's official FID code for fair comparison.

## Method

DiME performs distribution matching between the teacher generator G^T and student generator G^S by computing Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) in the embedding space of pretrained foundation models (DINO and CLIP), treating these networks as characteristic kernels in RKHS. To reduce batch-level sampling error, DiME precomputes global statistics from 20,000 teacher inferences, effectively making the teacher-side sampling error negligible. NICKEL additionally distills semantic knowledge from G^T into the student discriminator D^S by matching intermediate feature maps (at 1/4 resolution, on Haar wavelet LH/HL/HH components) via a wavelet-based loss, so D^S provides richer adversarial feedback to G^S from the start of training. The full training objective combines the adversarial loss (using both D^T and D^S in a minmax game), the DiME MMD losses weighted by λ_dino and λ_clip, and the NICKEL loss weighted by λ_NICKEL, yielding stable convergence even at extreme 321-fold compression where prior methods collapse.

## Results

On StyleGAN2 for FFHQ 256×256 (baseline FID 4.02), NICKEL & DiME achieves FID 4.42 at 74.96% compression, FID 7.43 at 90.73%, FID 15.93 at 98.92%, and FID 29.38 at the extreme 99.69% compression rate (321× smaller). At 99.69% compression, all prior methods collapse (FID 164.92–186.61), while NICKEL & DiME maintains a reasonable FID of 29.38. On LSUN-Church 256×256, NICKEL & DiME achieves FID 3.94 at 90.87% compression, outperforming the full model's FID of 3.97, and also surpasses the diffusion-model pruning method SPDM (FID 13.9 at 44.19% compression).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NICKEL & DiME outperforms all seven baselines across every compression rate and dataset tested. Against the strongest prior GAN compression method ITGC [21], NICKEL & DiME reduces FID from 22.23 to 15.93 at 98.92% compression on FFHQ, and from 164.92 to 29.38 at 99.69%—a gap of over 135 FID points where ITGC essentially fails. CAGC [39] and GS [57] show similar degradation patterns, with FID scores of 23.05 and 184.33 respectively at 98.92% and 99.69% compression, all far behind NICKEL & DiME. GCC [36] (FID 11.19 at 90.73%) and DCP-GAN [5] (FID 8.93 at 90.87%) are also consistently outperformed. On LSUN-Church, NICKEL & DiME (FID 3.94) even surpasses the uncompressed full model (FID 3.97) and dramatically outperforms the diffusion-model pruning method SPDM [7] (FID 13.9 at only 44.19% compression). StyleKD [59] is limited to StyleGAN architectures and achieves FID 10.41 at 97.60% on CelebA, compared to NICKEL & DiME's 7.56 at the same rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAN compression literature by combining two previously separate ideas: MMD-based distribution matching (Gretton et al.) and generator-guided discriminator regularization (GGDR, Lee et al.). DiME repurposes pretrained foundation models (DINO, CLIP) as characteristic kernels in an RKHS, replacing the perceptual or frequency-space embeddings used by prior methods like CAGC and ITGC. NICKEL adapts GGDR's insight that discriminators learn semantic structure from generators, but redirects the knowledge source from the (weak) student generator to the (strong) teacher generator, which is a novel twist for the compression setting. The paper also repositions the field empirically by reimplementing baselines with official NVIDIA code, revealing that several previously reported FID scores were inflated by custom feature-embedding pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: (1) the method enables deployment of high-quality GANs on resource-constrained devices, with the 99.69% compression result (FID 29.38 vs. >164 for all baselines) demonstrating that extreme compression is feasible where prior methods collapse entirely; (2) the benchmarking correction (e.g., CAGC FID 7.9→10.8) is important for the field's integrity and should be factored into future comparisons. Open questions remain: recall degrades substantially even at moderate compression rates, the generator-discriminator imbalance persists at extreme compression, and the reliance on large pretrained foundation models as fixed kernels raises questions about domain transfer and whether the MMD matching truly captures population-level distributional fidelity beyond the sampled statistics.
