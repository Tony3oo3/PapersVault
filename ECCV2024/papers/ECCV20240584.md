## Problem

Existing navigation instruction generation methods rely exclusively on 2D perspective features, ignoring the 3D scene geometry and object semantics essential for accurate route descriptions. Meanwhile, general-purpose MLLMs pre-trained on third-person image-text pairs cannot adequately interpret sequences of egocentric observations from embodied agents, making them insufficient for navigation instruction generation in a zero-shot manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240584/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T navigation steps, each containing K multi-view RGB images (encoded to 14×14 spatial features), orientation angles (heading and elevation), and a navigation action. The BEV encoder operates on a 15×15 grid covering a 10m×10m perception range with 4 depth reference points per query. Output is an autoregressive navigation instruction, with the visual representation compressed to 10 tokens per step via learnable queries to keep MLLM input length manageable. A two-stage output structure first produces landmark tokens, then refines them into the full instruction.

## Method

BEVINSTRUCTOR introduces a Perspective-BEV Visual Encoder that fuses 2D perspective features with 3D BEV features (generated via deformable attention with depth-consistency weighting, trained under 3D detection supervision) through transformer cross-attention, then compresses the result to 10 tokens per step using a lightweight Q-Former-style module. For language generation, it employs perspective-BEV prompt tuning on LLaMA-7B, inserting learnable visual prompts and using zero-initialized attention plus learnable scale vectors at the top 31 layers, with only 7.2% of parameters trainable. The key differentiator is an instance-guided iterative refinement pipeline: the model first identifies key landmark objects from the perspective-BEV embeddings, then conditions the full instruction generation on these landmark drafts, progressively enriching the output with scene-specific details across multiple refinement turns.

## Results

On REVERIE val seen, BEVINSTRUCTOR achieves SPICE 0.208 and CIDEr 0.745, surpassing the previous best (Lana) by 7.0% on SPICE and 12.6% on CIDEr. On REVERIE val unseen, it reaches SPICE 0.159 and CIDEr 0.489, improving by 4.2% and 8.3% respectively. On R2R val unseen, BEVINSTRUCTOR attains SPICE 0.208 and CIDEr 0.449, outperforming Lana by 1.4% and 3.0%. On the outdoor UrbanWalk test set, it achieves SPICE 0.679 and Rouge 0.786, exceeding the prior best (Kefa-speaker) by 11.3% on SPICE and 13.1% on Rouge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEVINSTRUCTOR outperforms all nine baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Against the strongest prior method Lana [91] (CVPR 2023), it gains 7.0% SPICE and 12.6% CIDEr on REVERIE val seen, and 4.2% SPICE and 8.3% CIDEr on REVERIE val unseen; on R2R val unseen the margins are smaller (1.4% SPICE, 3.0% CIDEr). The earlier LSTM-based speakers (BT-speaker, EDrop-speaker) and the Transformer-based CCC-speaker are consistently outperformed by 10–50+ points on CIDEr, reflecting the gap between sequence models and the MLLM backbone. On the outdoor UrbanWalk benchmark, BEVINSTRUCTOR surpasses Kefa-speaker [100] by 11.3% SPICE and 13.1% Rouge, and substantially outperforms ASSISTER [37] (11.3% SPICE gap). Zero-shot GPT-4V [64] and fine-tuned InstructBLIP [20] / PASTS [87] on R2R val unseen confirm that general-purpose MLLMs without BEV perception and task-specific prompt tuning remain 10–20 points behind on CIDEr, validating the need for the proposed Perspective-BEV encoder and instance-guided refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the navigation instruction generation lineage from LSTM/Transformer-based speakers (BT-speaker, EDrop-speaker, CCC-speaker, Lana) that rely exclusively on 2D perspective features, by importing Bird's Eye View perception—previously developed for autonomous driving (BEVFormer, FB-BEV, BEVDepth)—into the embodied instruction generation pipeline. It further departs from prior work by leveraging a frozen LLaMA-7B backbone with parameter-efficient prompt tuning (7.2% trainable parameters) rather than training a dedicated language decoder from scratch, and by introducing an instance-guided iterative refinement stage inspired by cognitive science findings on landmark-based route description. The perspective-BEV fusion via transformer cross-attention is a novel architectural choice that had not been applied to instruction generation before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding MLLM-based instruction generation in explicit 3D geometric and object-semantic representations, this work addresses a fundamental limitation of prior egocentric-only approaches and demonstrates that spatial scene understanding measurably improves the fidelity of generated navigation language. The parameter-efficient design makes the approach practical for resource-constrained embodied agents, and the instance-guided refinement offers a template for progressive generation in other embodied tasks. Open questions include whether BEV is the optimal 3D representation (vs. point clouds, NeRFs, or scene graphs), how the method generalizes to continuous (non-discrete) environments, and whether the gains persist as MLLMs themselves improve in spatial reasoning.
