## Problem

Existing interaction intention anticipation methods either treat hand trajectory forecasting and interaction hotspot prediction as independent tasks or model only the unidirectional influence of trajectories on contact points. This one-way causation causes prediction errors to accumulate progressively over time steps, degrading accuracy especially at the end of trajectories. The paper identifies an underexploited bidirectional spatial correlation between hand trajectories and interaction hotspots that can enable continuous mutual correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241565/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2-second egocentric video clip (resized to 456×256) and its optical flow as temporal input, with the last observation frame serving as the spatial reference. It produces 4 future hand trajectory points and 1 contact point (T=5 horizon), all projected onto the last observation frame for spatial consistency, along with an interaction hotspot heatmap and geometrically constrained elliptical uncertain regions for each trajectory point. Prediction horizons differ by dataset: 1 second on Epic-Kitchens-100 and EGO4D, 0.5 seconds on EGTEA Gaze+.

## Method

BOT employs a Spatial-Temporal Reconstruction Module that fuses optical flow features (via a frozen TSN) and last-frame spatial features (via Segformer-b2) through a Cross-Transformer to produce per-timestep spatial features, mitigating view-change conflicts in first-person video. Two independent prediction branches then process these features: the trajectory branch uses Cross-Transformers with temporal ordering, while the hotspot branch applies overlap patch embedding to emphasize the central field of view. The core novelty is the Bi-Progressive Enhancement Module, which uses cross-attention blocks to alternately refine trajectory and hotspot features in chronological order—each corrected trajectory feature conditions the next hotspot refinement and vice versa—iteratively reducing error accumulation. For decoding, a C-VAE generates the interaction hotspot heatmap (selecting the argmax location to bound uncertainty), while a Trajectory Stochastic Unit computes elliptical uncertain regions whose size grows with time step (controlled by parameters α and β) and whose orientation follows local trajectory segment geometry.

## Results

On EGO4D (the most diverse dataset), BOT achieves ADE 0.09 and FDE 0.08 with 20 stochastic samplings, outperforming the best non-joint method Segformer-b2 (ADE 0.16, FDE 0.16) by 31.3% and 18.8% respectively, and the best joint method OCT* (ADE 0.12, FDE 0.12) by 25% and 33.3%. For interaction hotspots, BOT reaches SIM 0.32, AUC-J 0.77, and NSS 1.70 on EGO4D, surpassing OCT* by 45.5% in SIM, 5.5% in AUC-J, and 93.2% in NSS. On Epic-Kitchens-100 and EGTEA Gaze+, BOT similarly attains the best ADE/FDE (0.10/0.08 and 0.09/0.08) and the highest hotspot scores (SIM 0.34/0.32, NSS 2.07/2.03) across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOT matches OCT* exactly on single-sampling trajectory metrics (ADE/FDE) for EK100 and EGO4D, but with 20 stochastic samplings it pulls ahead substantially: on EGO4D, ADE drops from 0.12 to 0.09 (25% improvement) and FDE from 0.12 to 0.08 (33.3% improvement). Against the strongest non-joint baseline Segformer-b2, BOT (single sampling) leads by 31.3% in ADE and 18.8% in FDE on EGO4D, and by 35.3% in ADE on EK100. For interaction hotspots, BOT (20 samplings) surpasses OCT* by 45.5% in SIM, 5.5% in AUC-J, and 93.2% in NSS on EGO4D, with similar margins on EK100 (SIM 0.34 vs 0.19) and EGTEA (NSS 2.03 vs 1.01). The earlier joint method FHOI [37] is far behind on trajectory (ADE 0.36 vs 0.12 on EK100) and only reports partial hotspot results. Unilateral baselines (KF, Seq2Seq, USST, Hotspots) are limited to single-task evaluation and are consistently outperformed on their respective metrics across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint hand-trajectory and interaction-hotspot prediction framework established by OCT (Liu et al., CVPR 2022) and FHOI (Liu et al., ECCV 2020), which treated the two subtasks with only unidirectional influence (trajectories informing hotspots). The key departure is the Bidirectional Progressive mechanism, which introduces iterative mutual correction between the two prediction branches at each time step via cross-attention, directly addressing the error accumulation problem inherent in sequential one-way conditioning. The C-VAE decoder (Sohn et al., NeurIPS 2015) is repurposed for interaction hotspot decoding, while a novel Trajectory Stochastic Unit handles trajectory uncertainty through geometrically-constrained elliptical regions rather than learned distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bi-Progressive mechanism offers a generalizable principle for any sequential multi-task prediction where subtasks are mutually informative, potentially applicable beyond egocentric interaction anticipation. However, the current formulation is limited to 2D predictions projected onto the last observation frame, and the authors explicitly acknowledge that 3D geometric reasoning is needed for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the progressive correction scales to longer prediction horizons (beyond T=5), whether the mechanism generalizes to multi-object or multi-agent interaction scenarios, and how the approach performs under domain shift (e.g., from kitchen to industrial settings).
