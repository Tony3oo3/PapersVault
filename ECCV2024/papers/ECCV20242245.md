## Problem

Prior Event-VFI methods estimate optical flow using either a two-segment formulation (independently estimating f_{τ→0} and f_{τ→1}) or parametric trajectory models (e.g., Bezier splines), both of which cannot correctly recover large, nonlinear, non-rigid, or occluded motions between frames, leading to accumulated flow errors and ghosting artifacts. Additionally, existing real-world RGB+Event datasets suffer from severe noise, RGB-event misalignment, and demosaicing artifacts that compromise reliable evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive RGB frames (I_0, I_1) and the temporally dense event stream E_{0→1} (represented as a voxel grid with P polarity channels) captured between them. It produces an interpolated frame at any arbitrary timestamp 0 < τ < 1, along with the corresponding bi-directional optical flow fields f_{τ→0} and f_{τ→1}. Unlike prior methods that can only generate the midpoint frame, the complete motion trajectory is stored as a dense field, enabling flexible sampling at any desired output frame rate without re-running the network.

## Method

The core insight is to decompose the large inter-frame motion into N small steps and iteratively estimate the full per-pixel motion trajectory using a lightweight Flow Updater module that, at each step, updates the intermediate frame features with the local event stream and accumulates the optical flow (f_{t_{k+1}→0} = f_{t_{k+1}→t_k} + f_{t_k→0}). The full TLXNet architecture has three parts: a feature encoder (convolutions + ResBlocks at quarter resolution), the iterative TimeLens-XL module (the same small Flow Updater network unrolled over N steps for both forward and backward trajectories), and a frame blending module inspired by RIFE's IFBlock but augmented with an explicit timestamp input to enable arbitrary-τ sampling. Because each small-step motion is nearly linear, the per-step estimation is accurate and cheap, and the final frame is synthesized with simple warping and blending rather than a heavy generative network. The entire pipeline is end-to-end trainable with a Charbonnier + LPIPS loss, uses only 4–8M parameters, and achieves 26–28 FPS at 1280×720 on a single RTX 3090, making it the first real-time Event-VFI method.

## Results

On the GOPRO dataset at ×16 interpolation, TLXNet+ achieves 36.52 PSNR / 0.967 SSIM / 0.022 LPIPS / 0.023 DISTS, outperforming the best prior Event-VFI method CBMNet (35.44 PSNR) by over 1 dB. At the more challenging ×32 setting, TLXNet+ reaches 35.35 PSNR, maintaining a ~1.4 dB lead over CBMNet (33.92). On the real-captured HQ-EVFI dataset at ×16, TLXNet+ achieves 33.02 PSNR / 0.919 SSIM / 0.022 LPIPS, again surpassing all baselines. Computationally, TLXNet requires only 271.45G MACs and 4M parameters versus CBMNet's 2549.18G MACs and 14M parameters (~10.6% of the compute), enabling real-time 26 FPS inference at 1280×720 on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLXNet/TLXNet+ consistently outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and interpolation factor. Against the strongest Event-VFI baseline CBMNet, TLXNet+ gains +1.08 dB PSNR on GOPRO ×16 (36.52 vs 35.44) and +1.43 dB on ×32 (35.35 vs 33.92), with even larger LPIPS improvements (0.022 vs 0.055). On the real-captured BS-ERGB dataset at ×2, TLXNet+ leads CBMNet by +0.36 dB PSNR (29.46 vs 29.10) and by a wide margin on LPIPS (0.047 vs 0.111). On the new HQ-EVFI dataset at ×16, TLXNet+ achieves 33.02 PSNR versus CBMNet's 32.62 and TimeLens's 32.38. RGB-only methods (RIFE, Super Slomo) are substantially worse, trailing by ~10 dB PSNR at ×16, confirming the critical role of event streams for large-motion interpolation. Notably, TLXNet achieves these gains with only ~10.6% of CBMNet's MACs (271G vs 2549G) and 4M vs 14M parameters, running at 26 FPS in real time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Event-VFI line initiated by TimeLens (CVPR 2021) and TimeLens++ (CVPR 2022), which established the use of event streams to aid frame interpolation. It departs from the two-segment flow formulation (TimeLens, CBMNet) and the parametric Bezier spline trajectory model (TimeLens++) by instead decomposing large inter-frame motion into a sequence of small iterative steps via a recurrent Flow Updater module. The frame blending component is adapted from RIFE's IFBlock, augmented with a timestamp input to enable arbitrary-rate sampling. The paper also introduces HQ-EVFI, a new real-captured RGB+Event dataset designed to address synchronization and quality limitations of prior benchmarks like BS-ERGB and ERF-X170FPS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TimeLens-XL is the first real-time Event-VFI method at 1280×720 (27 FPS on a single RTX 3090), making event-assisted interpolation practically deployable for the first time, and the HQ-EVFI dataset provides a much-needed high-quality benchmark free from the noise, misalignment, and color bias that plagued prior real-captured datasets. Key limitations remain: the method requires well-aligned RGB and event streams (dual-camera systems introduce residual misalignment), event noise degrades performance in low light, and single-sensor RGB-EVS chips are still too immature for consumer deployment. Open questions include whether the iterative decomposition strategy generalizes to even larger motion gaps (e.g., ×64 interpolation) and whether the approach can be adapted to the emerging single-sensor RGB-EVS hardware.
