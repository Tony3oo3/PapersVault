## Problem

Training-agnostic Trojan attacks in SSL encoders (e.g., SSL-Backdoor, CTRL) embed triggers in unlabeled pre-training data, causing downstream classifiers to inherit backdoor behavior. Existing detection tools (NC, ABS) fail on SSL encoders because downstream tasks, dataset labels, and the original training set are typically unavailable, and recent encoder scanners like DECREE achieve only ~50% detection on stealthy training-agnostic attacks. This paper addresses the gap of detecting and mitigating such backdoors in SSL encoders without any labeled data or downstream task knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pre-trained SSL encoder (ResNet-18 trained via BYOL, SimCLR, or MoCo V2) and a small unlabeled image subset (5–10% of the training set, e.g., ~12,700 images from ImageNet-100), with no access to labels, the poisoned training set, or downstream task specifications. Detection produces a binary Trojan/benign verdict plus the identified trigger pattern (either a spatial patch mask or a frequency-domain perturbation), and mitigation outputs a cleansed encoder. The full detection pipeline runs in ~20 minutes and mitigation in ~7 minutes on a single RTX-3090.

## Method

SSL-Cleanse comprises a Detector and a Mitigator. The Detector first uses the Sliding Window Kneedle (SWK) algorithm to auto-estimate the number of representation clusters by averaging adjacent Kneedle silhouette scores within a sliding window, mitigating noise that causes standard Kneedle to under-estimate (e.g., 20 vs. 100 on ImageNet-100). It then applies K-Means to form K clusters and runs Representation-Oriented Trigger Reverse (ROTR), which for each cluster optimizes two trigger sets (a patch mask and a global perturbation) to minimize cosine distance to the target cluster's representation while penalizing trigger size or L1-norm magnitude. A Size-Norm Trigger Outlier Detector (STOD) flags any trigger whose size or norm is an anomaly (Anomaly Index > 2) among the K candidates, identifying the Trojaned cluster. The Mitigator (Self-supervised Clustering Unlearning, SCU) then distills representations from the Trojaned encoder into a copy by training on augmented image pairs where one view is randomly (50%) attached with a trigger from a different cluster, effectively unlearning the trigger-to-representation association while preserving clean accuracy.

## Results

Across 1200 encoders (600 Trojaned, 600 benign) on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-100, SSL-Cleanse achieves an average detection accuracy (DACC) of 82.2% on ImageNet-100 and 80.5% on CIFAR-10, with peak DACC of 88% (SimCLR + CTRL on ImageNet-100). After mitigation, the attack success rate (ASR) drops to an average of 0.3% on ImageNet-100 and 1.2% on CIFAR-10, while clean accuracy (ACC) decreases by only ~1% (e.g., 60.57% → 60.24% for BYOL on ImageNet-100 under SSL-Backdoor). The method generalizes across downstream tasks (CIFAR-10, STL-10, GTSRB), maintaining ASR below 2% in all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not run a direct head-to-head quantitative comparison against other detection methods in its main experiments. Instead, it qualitatively demonstrates the limitations of existing scanners: NC [30] and ABS [21] fail to detect the CTRL attack even when the encoder and downstream classifier share the same dataset (Anomaly Index 1.52 < 2.0 threshold; REASR 0.52 < 0.88 threshold), and their detection degrades further on non-overlapping datasets (STL-10, GTSRB). The recent encoder-scanning method DECREE [8] is reported to achieve only ~50% detection accuracy on training-agnostic attacks like SSL-Backdoor and CTRL. SSL-Cleanse's 82.2% average DACC on ImageNet-100 thus represents a substantial improvement over these prior approaches for the training-agnostic threat model, though a controlled side-by-side benchmark is left for future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor detection literature from supervised classifiers to the SSL encoder setting, where downstream labels and tasks are unavailable. It builds directly on the Kneedle algorithm for automatic cluster number estimation, augmenting it with a sliding-window smoothing step (SWK) to handle noisy SSL representations. The detection pipeline departs from prior scanners (NC, ABS, DECREE) that rely on downstream classifier behavior or training-phase control, instead operating purely on unlabeled representations and reverse-engineering triggers via the ROTR optimization. The mitigation component (SCU) is a new unlearning strategy that has no direct analogue in prior SSL backdoor defenses like SSL-ABD, which require knowledge of the target class.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSL encoders are widely distributed as pre-trained artifacts, making pre-dissemination Trojan detection a practically critical but previously underserved problem. The 82% average detection rate, while useful, leaves a non-trivial miss rate that could allow Trojaned encoders to propagate in real-world pipelines. Open questions include generalization beyond ResNet-18 and the two evaluated attack families (patch-based SSL-Backdoor and frequency-domain CTRL), scalability to larger encoder architectures (ViT, DINOv2), and whether the representation-clustering assumption holds for more diverse or domain-specific SSL pre-training. The paper also does not address adaptive attackers who could design triggers that evade the size/norm outlier criterion.
