## Problem

Existing video diffusion models rely solely on text prompts, which cannot precisely control visual details or subject identity, while image-driven methods (e.g., VideoCrafter1) assign equal weights to image and text conditions, failing to account for the different controllability of homogeneous vs. heterogeneous modalities. No unified, tuning-free diffusion framework exists that simultaneously supports high-fidelity video generation and editing while properly aligning subject appearance with textual instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240469/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a segmented subject image (extracted via a segmentation model from a reference image) and a text prompt; for editing, it additionally takes the full source video sequence. The subject image is consumed at three resolutions (384×384, 320×320, 256×256) through a VAE-based pyramid encoder, and the output is a temporally consistent video at the target resolution. The method is tuning-free at inference, requiring no per-sample fine-tuning unlike methods such as Tune-A-Video.

## Method

MagDiff introduces three alignment modules on top of a VidRD-based U-Net. Subject-Driven Alignment (SDA) replaces the full reference image with a segmented subject image as the conditioning signal, unifying generation and editing in one framework by treating non-editable regions as the 'subject.' Adaptive Prompts Alignment (APA) modifies the cross-attention block to share a single query between text and image prompts while applying learnable scalar weights α₁ and α₂ to each modality's attention output, allowing the model to dynamically balance homogeneous (image) and heterogeneous (text) controllability. High-Fidelity Alignment (HFA) encodes the subject image at three resolutions via VAE, aligns the multi-scale latent features with convolutional layers, and concatenates the resulting feature with the noise latent before denoising, injecting pixel-level detail that CLIP-based semantic encoding alone cannot preserve.

## Results

On UCF-101, MagDiff achieves an Inception Score of 48.57 and FVD of 339.62, outperforming the best text-to-video baseline PyoCo (IS 47.76, FVD 355.19) and the best text&image-to-video baseline VideoCrafter1 (IS 44.53, FVD 415.87). On MSR-VTT, MagDiff reduces FVD to 245, a 47% improvement over VideoCrafter1 (465) and a 58% improvement over I2VGen-XL (597.49). For video editing on DAVIS, MagDiff (tuning-free) achieves a textual-alignment score of 27.65 and frame-consistency of 90.86, surpassing the tuning-free baseline Framewise IP2P by +2.54 and +4.10 respectively, while remaining competitive with fine-tuning methods Tune-A-Video (28.33) and FateZero (92.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagDiff outperforms all five text-to-video baselines (LVDM, ModelScope, Make-A-Video, VideoFactory, PyoCo) on UCF-101 FVD, with the closest competitor PyoCo trailing by 15.57 FVD points. Among text&image-to-video methods, MagDiff surpasses VideoCrafter1 by 76.25 FVD on UCF-101 and by 220 FVD on MSR-VTT, and beats I2VGen-XL by 257.87 FVD on UCF-101, despite using far fewer training videos (5.3M+76K vs. 10–10.3M). On the additional fidelity metrics (Table 2), MagDiff leads AnimateDiff(V3) and I2VGen-XL on DINO score across all three benchmarks (e.g., 50.8 vs. 46.2 vs. 44.1 on UCF-101) and on textual alignment (25.4 vs. 24.1 vs. 21.3). For video editing on DAVIS, MagDiff (tuning-free) beats the tuning-free baseline Framewise IP2P by +2.54 textual-alignment and +4.10 frame-consistency, but slightly trails Tune-A-Video (fine-tuning) by 0.68 on textual-alignment and FateZero (fine-tuning) by 2.06 on frame-consistency, demonstrating that a tuning-free approach can match fine-tuning methods. In human evaluation (Table 4), MagDiff scores 4.4/5 on image-prompt alignment and 4.1/5 on text-prompt alignment, substantially ahead of VideoCrafter1 (3.2/2.8) and AnimateDiff(V3) (3.5/3.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

MagDiff builds directly on VidRD's U-Net architecture and extends the image-conditioned video diffusion line (VideoCrafter1, I2VGen-XL, VideoComposer) by replacing the full reference image with a subject-segmented image prompt, thereby decoupling identity preservation from background control. Its key conceptual departure from VideoCrafter1 is the introduction of learnable cross-attention weights (α₁, α₂) to differentially balance the homogeneous (image) and heterogeneous (text) conditioning signals, rather than the equal-weight fusion used in prior work. The High-Fidelity Alignment module, which injects multi-scale VAE latents via a pyramid encoder, is a novel addition not present in prior image-to-video pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying video generation and editing in a single tuning-free framework, MagDiff removes the need for task-specific fine-tuning (as in Tune-A-Video) or separate model architectures, which is practically valuable for deployment. However, the approach depends on an external segmentation step for subject extraction, which introduces a failure mode for ambiguous or multi-subject scenes, and the training set (76K videos) is relatively small compared to competitors using 10M+ videos. Open questions include whether the subject-driven formulation generalizes to multi-subject or scene-level editing, and whether the pyramid VAE encoder scales efficiently to higher resolutions or longer video sequences.
