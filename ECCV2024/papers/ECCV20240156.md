## Problem

Extensive occlusions in real-world gait recognition (carrying, crowds, static obstacles, partial camera views) cause missing information, noisy features, and body misalignment that severely degrade identification, yet most existing methods largely ignore these scenarios. Additionally, no unified benchmark with explicit occlusion type annotations exists to systematically evaluate occlusion-robust gait recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240156/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes gait silhouette sequences (extracted from RGB video via MaskFormer) resized to 64×44 pixels, with spatial alignment applied for OccGait and OccCASIA-B. During training, 30 frames are sampled per sequence; at inference, all frames are used. The network outputs part-level identity feature embeddings (via Separate FCs and BNNeck) that are compared against gallery embeddings for Rank-1/mAP retrieval, trained end-to-end with only ID labels.

## Method

GaitMoE reframes occluded gait recognition as an action detection problem where a gait sequence is a composition of actions, leveraging two dynamic contextual properties: gait continuity (adjacent holistic frames infer occluded regions) and gait cycles (similar actions across cycles are integrated). Mixture of Temporal Experts (MTE) uses dilated 3D convolutions with K=4 different dilation ratios applied to S=8 channel segments to construct multi-scale action anchors, each capturing a body region at a specific temporal granularity. Mixture of Action Experts (MAE) then employs M=16 learnable action prototypes that attend over the full temporal dimension via softmax-weighted aggregation to select and integrate similar action anchors into discriminative action proposals, effectively filtering occluded noise. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a joint loss of triplet loss plus a weighted cross-entropy term, requiring only ID labels without explicit action annotations.

## Results

On Gait3D, GaitMoE-B (10 residual blocks) achieves 73.7% Rank-1 and 66.2% mAP, surpassing the previous best DyGait (66.3% Rank-1, 56.4% mAP) by 7.4% and 9.8% respectively. On GREW, GaitMoE-B reaches 79.6% Rank-1 and 89.1% Rank-5, outperforming DyGait (71.4%, 83.2%) and GaitGCI (68.5%, 80.8%). On the authors' own OccGait benchmark, GaitMoE achieves the highest average Rank-1 across all four occlusion types (NM: 91.4%, CA: 82.1%, CR: 79.9%, ST: 84.7%), with the largest margin under Carrying Occlusion (+5.8% over STOR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaitMoE-B outperforms all ten baselines on both Gait3D and GREW. The closest competitor, DyGait (ICCV'23), is surpassed by 7.4% Rank-1 on Gait3D and 8.2% Rank-1 on GREW, with a particularly large mAP gap of 9.8% on Gait3D. GaitBase (CVPR'23), the strongest 2D-convolution baseline, trails by 9.1% Rank-1 on Gait3D and 19.5% on GREW. On the occlusion-specific benchmarks, GaitMoE dominates: on OccGait it leads STOR (IJCNN'23) by 3.1–5.8% average Rank-1 across occlusion types, and on OccCASIA-B it exceeds GaitBase by up to 16.8% under Detect Occlusion with cloth-changing. The lighter GaitMoE-T variant (4 residual blocks) still beats DyGait on Gait3D (71.3% vs. 66.3% Rank-1) while using fewer parameters, confirming the efficiency-accuracy trade-off highlighted in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the action detection paradigm (action anchors, action proposals, actionness scoring) from temporal video understanding into gait recognition, framing a gait sequence as a composition of actions rather than a flat feature sequence. The Mixture of Experts architecture draws on the divide-and-conquer philosophy from NLP/ViT MoE literature but instantiates it with dilated convolutions as temporal experts and attention-like soft selection as action experts, a combination not previously explored in gait recognition. It departs from prior occluded gait approaches (STOR's registration, reconstruction-based methods) by avoiding explicit reconstruction and instead leveraging dynamic contextual information—gait continuity and gait cycles—to implicitly infer occluded regions through multi-scale temporal modeling and cross-cycle action integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OccGait benchmark fills a critical gap as the first real-world occluded gait database with explicit occlusion type annotations (carrying, crowd, static), enabling quantitative occlusion analysis that prior synthetic databases could not support. The action detection perspective offers a principled alternative to reconstruction-based occlusion handling, potentially generalizable to other sequence recognition tasks with partial observations. However, the authors acknowledge expert redundancy in the selection maps, and the benchmark is limited to indoor collection with 101 subjects, leaving open whether the approach scales to truly wild, large-scale occlusion scenarios. The reliance on silhouette extraction via MaskFormer also introduces a dependency on upstream segmentation quality under heavy occlusion.
