## Problem

Existing subcellular structure prediction (SSP) methods require dense Z-axis imaging (up to 2.5 hours per structure) and rely on pure 3D convolutions, incurring high computational and GPU memory costs. No prior work has explored predicting 3D fluorescent volumes from sparse-view transmitted light images, which would enable faster, less phototoxic imaging of live cells and visualization of rapid biological dynamics on low-cost devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240741/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse-view TL image stack of size (D_n/r) × H_n × W_n (where r ∈ {2, 4, 8} is the sparsity ratio, reducing imaging frequency by up to 87.5%) at voxel resolution (0.29r) × 0.29 × 0.29 μm³, and produces a full 3D fluorescent intensity volume of size D_n × H_n × W_n at 0.29 μm³ isotropic resolution. Each sample corresponds to one of 12 subcellular structures (e.g., DNA, Mitochondria, ActinFilament), and the output is a per-voxel scalar regression of fluorescence intensity. Inference uses a Gaussian sliding window of 32×128×128 voxels.

## Method

SparseSSP introduces two key mechanisms: (1) a one-to-many voxel mapping via prefix interpolation (nearest-neighbor) that expands the sparse TL stack into a pseudo voxel grid before network input, implicitly teaching the model to restore missing Z-axis context; and (2) a hybrid dimensions topology (3-to-2D) that uses a 5-layer 3D UNet encoder (channels 32→256) followed by a depth-to-channel transformation (1×1×1 3D convolution projecting to U/D_k channels, then reshaping Z into the channel dimension) and a 2D UNet decoder whose final head outputs D_n channels corresponding to the full Z-axis length. The 3D-space projection preserves spatial context before the channel fold, and the 2D decoder refines the sparse Z-axis information during layer-by-layer upsampling. Task embedding is plug-and-play (DoDNet, RepMode, Tgnet, TSNs, CondNet), and the framework is trained with MSELoss under supervised learning. Extensive ablations show the 3-to-2D topology with prefix interpolation achieves the best performance across all sparsity ratios, with slower R² decay as sparsity increases compared to pure 3D topologies, while reducing MACs by ~35% versus pure 3D.

## Results

On the AllenCell benchmark (12 subcellular structures), the best SparseSSP configuration (Tgnet-3-to-2D with prefix interpolation) achieves MSE 0.4995, MAE 0.4113, and R² 0.4693 at sparsity ratio r=2, outperforming the dense-view SOTA RepMode 3D (MSE 0.5210, R² 0.4453). At the extreme sparsity ratio r=8 (reducing imaging frequency by 87.5%), Tgnet-3-to-2D retains MSE 0.5386, MAE 0.4231, and R² 0.4292, showing only a modest degradation. The 3-to-2D hybrid topology consistently outperforms pure 3D topologies across all task-embedding methods and sparsity ratios, with the R² decay from r=2 to r=8 being noticeably slower for 3-to-2D than for pure 3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense-view FNet/Multi-Net baseline [20] (r=1, MSE 0.5341, R² 0.4337), Tgnet-3-to-2D at r=2 improves all three metrics (MSE 0.4995, MAE 0.4113, R² 0.4693), and even at r=8 the gap narrows to near-parity (MSE 0.5386 vs 0.5341, R² 0.4292 vs 0.4337), meaning an 87.5% reduction in imaging frequency costs less than 1% R². RepMode [30], the prior SOTA for dense-view SSP, is outperformed by Tgnet-3-to-2D at every sparsity ratio (e.g., r=2: R² 0.4693 vs 0.4453; r=8: R² 0.4292 vs 0.3984), confirming that the hybrid topology advantage is independent of the task-embedding choice. Among task-embedding methods paired with the 3-to-2D topology, Tgnet [26] edges out RepMode [30] by ~0.8% R² at r=2, which in turn beats DoDNet [28] (~1.7% R²), CondNet [6] (~1.9% R²), and TSNs [23] (~1.7% R²). The consistent ordering Tgnet > RepMode > DoDNet > CondNet ≈ TSNs holds across all sparsity ratios, indicating that task-guided functional blocks (Tgnet) are most compatible with the sparse-view 3-to-2D decoding. Pure 3D topologies of any method are uniformly worse than their 3-to-2D counterparts (e.g., Tgnet 3D R² 0.4459 vs 3-to-2D R² 0.4693 at r=2), validating the core hybrid-dimensions hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Subcellular Structure Prediction (SSP) line of work initiated by FNet [20] and advanced by RepMode [30], which both rely on pure 3D topologies and dense Z-axis imaging. The key conceptual departure is the hybrid dimensions topology, which borrows the depth-to-channel folding idea from FlashOcc [27] (3D occupancy prediction) and channel rearrangement from super-resolution [21], repurposing them to let 2D decoding layers handle 3D voxel prediction. The one-to-many voxel mapping paradigm (sparse input → dense output) is new to SSP; prior methods assumed a one-to-one voxel correspondence. The framework is explicitly plug-and-play, integrating existing multi-task embedding methods (DoDNet, Tgnet, TSNs, CondNet, RepMode) without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for live-cell imaging: reducing Z-axis imaging frequency by up to 87.5% (r=8) with only modest quality degradation directly addresses phototoxicity, motion artifacts, and the inability to capture rapid biological dynamics on low-cost devices. The finding that 2D decoding layers outperform pure 3D convolutions for sparse-view prediction challenges the default assumption that 3D operations are necessary for 3D prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the AllenCell benchmark, whether more powerful 2D backbones (the Swin/ViT ablations show promise but DINOv2 underperforms) can close the remaining gap to dense-view, and how the method behaves on structures with high Z-axis anisotropy.
