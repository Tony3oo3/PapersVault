## Problem

Existing CNN-based deep watermarking frameworks suffer from a desynchronization problem under geometric distortions: their fixed convolutional sampling positions cannot flexibly track watermark-embedded locations after rotation, shear, or affine transformations, leading to significant extraction accuracy drops even when trained with such distortions. Additionally, conventional combined noise layers for non-geometric distortions provide a narrow distribution representation, limiting the model's generalizability to unseen distortion types.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 3×224×224 RGB cover image and a 196-bit binary watermark as input and produces a watermarked image at the same resolution. The decoder takes a potentially distorted watermarked image and outputs the 196 extracted watermark bits. During training, the style transfer network is initialized on DIV2K (original/distorted image pairs), while the watermark model itself is trained on 5000 COCO images with 500 held out for testing. The distortion-style-ensembled noise layer replaces direct distortion application with learned style transfer, so the decoder sees simulated distortion styles rather than ground-truth distorted pixels during training.

## Method

The framework uses a two-step training procedure. In Step 1, a style transfer network (image encoder E_I, image decoder D_I, and per-distortion style layers T^i) is trained to decouple image content from distortion style, then styles within each of three classes (Noise, Blur, Compression) are linearly fused to produce class-level style operators T^N, T^B, T^C. In Step 2, the watermark model is trained end-to-end with these fixed style operators as the noise layer, randomly selecting one class style per mini-batch. The model backbone replaces standard CNNs with Swin Transformer Blocks (for global, adaptive attention scheduling) and Deformable Convolutional Networks (for free-form sampling grid offsets), enabling the decoder to flexibly track watermark-embedded regions under geometric distortions. The encoder integrates ConvSNPRelu layers, Squeeze-and-Excitation blocks, and Swin Transformer Blocks to couple cover image and watermark features, while the decoder pre-processes with DCN layers before deep feature extraction via STB and a final linear layer for bit output.

## Results

The proposed method achieves 100% bit correct rate (BCR) under Rotation at all tested angles (60°–180°) and under most Affine Transformations, demonstrating near-complete immunization against geometric distortions. Under the most extreme geometric cases (Shear s=50, Affine s=50/r=180), BCR drops to 93.60% and 93.37% respectively, still far exceeding baselines. For non-geometric distortions, the method reaches 100% BCR on Gaussian Blur, Median Blur, Box Blur, and Salt-and-Pepper Noise, 99.93% on JPEG Q=40, and 95.44% on Dropout p=0.7. Visual quality is also superior: PSNR of 35.973 dB and SSIM of 0.992 without geometric distortion, and 33.720 dB / 0.986 with geometric distortion, at the same information density (0.003906 bits/pixel) as baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three SOTA deep watermarking methods: SSL [21], MBRS [31], and CIN [40], all evaluated at the same information density (0.003906 bits/pixel). Under geometric distortions, the proposed method achieves 100% BCR in nearly all cases, improving the average BCR of baselines (~80–88%) to almost 100%; for example, at Affine s=50/r=180, Ours reaches 93.37% versus 69.59% (SSL), 85.19% (MBRS), and 75.06% (CIN). Under non-geometric distortions, Ours also leads consistently: 99.93% on JPEG Q=40 vs. 74.83% (SSL), 96.48% (MBRS), 71.54% (CIN); and 95.44% on Dropout p=0.7 vs. 80.99% (SSL), 79.57% (MBRS), 84.58% (CIN). Visual quality is also best: PSNR 35.973 dB and SSIM 0.992 (no geometric) vs. 25.562/0.765 (SSL), 35.382/0.924 (MBRS), 33.506/0.949 (CIN). The ablation in Table 5 further confirms that the distortion-style-ensembled noise layer adds ~2.5–3.7% average BCR over the traditional combined noise layer, and the Swin Transformer + DCN backbone adds ~2.15% over MBRS with the same noise layer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CNN-based deep watermarking lineage (HiDDeN, MBRS, CIN) by replacing the fixed-positional convolutional backbone with a Swin Transformer and Deformable Convolutional Network, directly targeting the desynchronization problem that geometric distortions impose on CNN decoders. For the noise layer, it repurposes the StyleBank [9] framework—originally designed for artistic style transfer—to simulate and fuse distortion styles (Noise, Blur, Compression), offering a richer distributional representation than the direct-implementation combined noise layers used in prior work. The two-step training (style network initialization, then end-to-end watermark training) is a novel orchestration of these components within the standard watermarking pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving near-100% extraction accuracy under geometric distortions (rotation, shear, affine) addresses a long-standing practical weakness of deep watermarking, making such systems viable for real-world pipelines where geometric warping is common. The style-transfer-based noise layer provides a principled path toward generalization across unseen non-geometric distortions, though the gains over direct distortion simulation are moderate (2–4% in ablation). Open questions include scalability to higher-resolution images and longer watermarks, robustness under compound or adversarial distortion sequences, and whether the 100% geometric result holds under more extreme or combined geometric attacks beyond the tested parameter ranges.
