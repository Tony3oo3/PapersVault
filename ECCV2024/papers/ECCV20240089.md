## Problem

Existing point cloud registration methods, both traditional and learning-based, have insufficiently exploited the intrinsic SE(3) rotation equivariance symmetry of point cloud data, forcing models to rely on more training data and greater complexity to learn rotation-invariant representations. Additionally, geometric feature descriptors derived from local neighborhoods overlook the global point connectivity topology, producing features that lack SE(3) equivariance and impeding robust registration under rotation.

## Input / Output

The model takes two sparse point clouds of N=1024 points each (downsampled via voxel filter from raw scans), with points rearranged in descending order of ray length from the sensor center for consistent similarity search. The source scan is normalized to a canonical frame and the target is expressed relative to it, so the network predicts the relative rigid transform. Output is a 3D translation vector and a 4D quaternion encoding the source-to-target rotation, trained with arccos-based rotation loss and L2 translation loss.

## Method

Equi-GSPR embeds SE(3) equivariance into a graph neural network via E(n)-equivariant message passing (Satorras et al.), where edge messages are projected onto locally constructed equivariant frames (ClofNet-style) to preserve SO(3) invariance of scalar coefficients while maintaining vector equivariance of aggregated features. A PointNet++-inspired descriptor module (or pre-trained FCGF/FPHF) initializes node features, which are then propagated through 4 equivariant graph layers with ball-query or kNN neighborhood construction. A novel Low-Rank Factor Transformation (LRFT) module, inspired by LoRA but trained from scratch, compresses the N×35 feature matrices to N'×35 via two stacked linear layers with rank r≪N, enabling efficient dot-product similarity matching without explicit correspondence supervision. A trace-based rank regularizer and per-element sub-matrix determinant verification (5×5 at borders, 7×7 interior) enforce full-rank similarity structure and mask outlier correspondences, after which weighted features are pooled and mapped through fully-connected layers to the final transform.

## Results

On 3DMatch (indoor), Equi-GSPR achieves the best registration recall of 94.60% and F1 of 94.35%, with the lowest rotation error (1.67°) and translation error (5.68 cm) among all compared methods, while maintaining a fast inference time of 0.12 s. On KITTI (outdoor), the model attains the lowest RE (0.92°) and TE (8.74 cm), the highest RR (83.83%) and F1 (85.09%), again with sub-0.2 s latency. The model outperforms the second-best method (SpinNet) by roughly 0.9° in RE and 0.8–2 cm in TE on both benchmarks, and is over 18,000× faster than RoReg (0.12 s vs. 2226 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Equi-GSPR outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric (RE, TE, RR, F1) on both 3DMatch and KITTI. Against the closest learning-based competitor SpinNet (SO(2)-equivariant), it improves RE by 0.26° and TE by 0.56 cm on 3DMatch, and by 0.16° and 2.01 cm on KITTI, while being ~24× faster (0.12 s vs. 2.84 s). Compared to RoReg, which achieves comparable accuracy (RE 1.84° vs. 1.67°), Equi-GSPR is roughly 18,000× faster (0.12 s vs. 2226 s), making it practical for real-time use. Against non-learning methods (RANSAC variants, Go-ICP, Super4PCS), the gap is substantial: RANSAC-100k+refine achieves only 92.30% RR vs. 94.60% for Equi-GSPR, and Go-ICP/Super4PCS fall below 23% RR. Among learning-based baselines, PointDSC (93.28% RR) and DGR (91.30% RR) are both clearly surpassed, confirming that the SE(3) equivariant graph architecture and LRFT matching strategy provide meaningful gains over spectral-consistency or classification-based correspondence approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the E(n) equivariant graph neural network architecture of Satorras et al. [38] for SE(3)-equivariant message passing, and adopts the complete local frame construction from ClofNet [14] for SO(3)-invariant projection of edge messages. It departs from prior equivariant registration work (SpinNet's SO(2) cylindrical convolution, RoReg's rotation-guided descriptors) by embedding full SE(3) equivariance into a sparse graph structure rather than a dense volumetric or cylindrical representation. The Low-Rank Feature Transformation module is conceptually inspired by LoRA [24] but repurposed for feature aggregation and similarity matching rather than parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and the rank-based regularizer for outlier suppression is a novel addition not present in prior registration pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that SE(3) equivariance in a graph-based sparse registration pipeline yields consistent accuracy gains across indoor (3DMatch) and outdoor (KITTI) settings while maintaining sub-150ms inference, making it practically viable for real-time applications like visual odometry. The modular design—allowing interchangeable descriptors (FCGF, FPHF, or learned PointNet++-style)—lowers the barrier to adoption and enables practitioners to plug in their preferred front-end. Open questions include whether the equivariant advantage persists under extreme sparsity or dynamic scenes, whether the rank regularizer generalizes beyond the 35-dimensional feature space, and whether permutation invariance (noted as future work) is needed for robustness to input ordering.
