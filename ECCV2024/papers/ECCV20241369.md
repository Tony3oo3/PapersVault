## Problem

MIL-based WSI classification methods treat patch instances as unordered, losing both local spatial context and global slide-level context that are critical for locating small tumor regions. While a few methods preserve local ordering, region-level features remain unordered, so the full sequential structure of the slide is never exploited. This paper reframes WSI classification as a long-sequence problem to recover both local and global context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241369/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a gigapixel WSI (100,000+ × 100,000 px) and a single slide-level label. The tissue area is segmented via Otsu thresholding and scanned in a zigzag pattern to produce an ordered sequence of ~10,000+ non-overlapping patches, each encoded by an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50. The sequence is split into segments of length 1024 for sequential processing. The output is a slide-level class prediction obtained by averaging segment-level predictions across two cyclic inference passes, along with per-segment tumor probabilities that enable spatial localization.

## Method

Norma serializes each WSI into an ordered sequence and processes it with a Memory-Augmented ViT (MA ViT) that combines a global memory sink (caching segment representations in FIFO order to accumulate global context) with a Rolled Positional Encoding Generator (RPEG) that encodes local context via a dual-branch 1D-convolution scheme with a roll-and-restore mechanism to fix boundary-token information loss. Because slide-level labels are inherited by every segment, normal-tissue segments in tumor slides introduce label noise; Norma addresses this with a two-cycle Cyclic Training procedure. In Cycle One, a differentiable Noisy Label Detection module computes cosine similarity between each segment embedding and class prototypes to produce soft pseudo-labels, and noisy segments are cached into a noise prototype sink. In Cycle Two, the global memory sink already contains the full sequence context (so effective context length equals the entire slide), and supervised contrastive learning pushes features from different classes—including the noise class—apart, yielding a noise-free training setting. The final prediction averages all segment scores across both cycles.

## Results

Norma achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks. On CAMELYON-16, it reaches 91.6% ACC, 95.1% AUC, and 90.8% F1, improving over the second-best method (LA-MIL) by 4.1%, 4.7%, and 3.8% respectively. On TCGA-LUNG, Norma attains 92.8% ACC, 96.9% AUC, and 92.7% F1, surpassing the runner-up (IBMIL-MIL) by 2.1% on AUC. On TCGA-BRAC, it achieves 91.3% ACC, 95.5% AUC, and 89.2% F1, with a 4.6% AUC gain over the next best method (IBMIL-MIL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Norma outperforms all 12 baselines on ACC and AUC across all three datasets. The closest competitors are LA-MIL [29] (best on CAMELYON-16 with 89.5% ACC / 92.1% AUC) and IBMIL-MIL [22] (best on TCGA-LUNG with 91.4% ACC / 94.7% AUC and TCGA-BRAC with 90.9% AUC). The largest margins appear on CAMELYON-16, where Norma's 95.1% AUC exceeds LA-MIL by 3.0 points and TransMIL [31] by 5.0 points, which the authors attribute to the small tumor regions (<10% of tissue area) benefiting from global context. On TCGA-BRAC F1, Norma (89.2±3.6) is marginally below LA-MIL (89.7±1.2), though the large standard deviation suggests this is not statistically significant. GNN-based methods (GTP [52], HEAT [4]) and hierarchical approaches (HIPT [5]) consistently underperform the top MIL-based baselines, with HIPT showing the weakest CAMELYON-16 results (80.8% ACC) despite using ViT-extracted features. The attention-based AB-MIL [15] and dual-stream DS-MIL [18] baselines trail Norma by 8+ points in AUC on CAMELYON-16, highlighting the advantage of sequential context modeling over unordered aggregation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Norma departs from the dominant MIL paradigm for WSI classification, which treats patches as an unordered bag, by reframing the problem as long-sequence classification with a Memory-Augmented ViT. The sequential processing strategy is directly inspired by NLP long-sequence models (Transformer-XL, Compressive Transformer) and video recognition (MeMViT), adapted here with a novel Cyclic Training procedure to handle the label noise that arises when segment-level supervision is inherited from slide-level labels. The RPEG positional encoding extends Conditional Positional Encoding (CPE) with a dual-branch rolling mechanism to address boundary-token information loss in 1D sequences, and the Noisy Label Detection module introduces a differentiable soft-label approach that contrasts with prior non-differentiable noise detection methods (KNN, GMM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the unordered-bag assumption in MIL discards spatial context that is critical for localizing small tumor regions, and that a sequential memory-augmented approach can recover this information with substantial gains (up to ~5% AUC over strong baselines). The Cyclic Training framework provides a principled way to handle the inherent noise in weakly-supervised sequential training, and the finding that MIL is a special case of Norma (global memory = 0) unifies the two paradigms. Open questions include whether the sequential approach scales to even larger WSIs or multi-class settings with many more classes, and whether the Cyclic Training idea generalizes to other weakly-supervised tasks beyond WSI classification. The authors note future directions toward survival prediction and WSI segmentation.
