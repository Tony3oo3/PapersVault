## Problem

Existing biomedical multimodal methods struggle with two compounding challenges: (1) the high-order semantic heterogeneity across diverse clinical modalities, where cross-modal relations carry distinct meanings (e.g., genomics 'express' images, images 'depict' texts) that flat attention mechanisms fail to capture, and (2) the practical reality of modality missing in clinical data, which breaks the completeness assumption underlying most fusion pipelines. No prior work unifies heterogeneous clinical modalities (genomics, pathology, cell graphs, text) into a single representation while explicitly modeling relation-level heterogeneity and handling missingness.

## Input / Output

Each patient subject is represented as a 4-tuple of modality-specific instance sets: genomic groups (CNV+mutations and RNA-Seq, each ~80–240 features) encoded by SNN, WSI patches (512×512, ~1–2 per patient) encoded by ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50, curated cell spatial graphs encoded by GCN, and LLM-generated (MiniGPT-4) diagnostic sentences encoded by MedBERT, all projected to a shared dimension d=512. The method natively handles 40% missing RNA-Seq in GBMLGG without data modification. Outputs are task-specific: WHO/Fuhrman grade labels (classification) or survival time (regression), produced by a task head on the graph-level pooled representation.

## Method

GTP-4o embeds all modalities into a heterogeneous graph where node attributes encode modality identity and edge attributes encode semantic relations ({express, depict, atomize, intra-modal}), making cross-modal relation heterogeneity explicit rather than learned implicitly. For missing modalities, a graph prompting module inserts hallucination nodes initialized by Gaussian sampling from the modality prior of complete subjects, refined by an entity-dependent prompt bank (N_B=5 components weighted per-node via softmax), effectively reconstructing the deficient subgraph. Aggregation proceeds hierarchically: a global meta-path neighbouring step (2-hop paths like G→express→I→atomize→C derived from domain knowledge) identifies valid heterogeneous neighbors, followed by a local multi-relation aggregation using relation-specific multi-head attention where edge features modulate the attention score (SHA = v_s^K · e^K · v_t^Q / √d). The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with a smaller learning rate (2×10⁻⁴) for prompt parameters versus the aggregation and task head (1×10⁻³), over 150 epochs with early stopping.

## Results

On the full omni-modal setting (Genomics + Images + Cell graphs + Texts), GTP-4o achieves AUC 0.9389 / ACC 0.8126 on TCGA-GBMLGG glioma grading and AUC 0.8416 / ACC 0.8068 on TCGA-KIRC, with survival prediction C-Index of 0.8351 (GBMLGG) and 0.7336 (KIRC). In the more standard Genomics + Images setting, GTP-4o reaches AUC 0.9256 / ACC 0.8036 (GBMLGG) and C-Index 0.8296 (GBMLGG), 0.7273 (KIRC). All experiments are conducted under a 40% RNA-Seq missing rate with 5-fold cross-validation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the standard Genomics+Images setting, GTP-4o outperforms the strongest multimodal baseline MCAT on GBMLGG accuracy (0.8036 vs. 0.7929) and both survival C-Index metrics (0.8296 vs. 0.8274 on GBMLGG; 0.7273 vs. 0.7235 on KIRC), though MCAT retains a marginal AUC edge on GBMLGG (0.9288 vs. 0.9256) and KIRC (0.8352 vs. 0.8349). Against the best unimodal image baseline HEAT, GTP-4o (G+I) surpasses it on GBMLGG accuracy (0.8036 vs. 0.8057 is actually a slight loss) but wins on KIRC accuracy (0.7985 vs. 0.7961) and both C-Index scores (0.8296 vs. 0.8223; 0.7273 vs. 0.7059). When extended to three modalities (G+I+C), GTP-4o clearly dominates TransFusion across all six metrics, e.g., GBMLGG AUC 0.9336 vs. 0.9225 and KIRC C-Index 0.7315 vs. 0.7260. In the full four-modality setting (G+I+C+T), GTP-4o achieves the best scores on every metric, with the largest gains on KIRC AUC (0.8416 vs. 0.8325) and GBMLGG C-Index (0.8351 vs. 0.8296) over TransFusion. Overall, GTP-4o wins or ties on the majority of metrics across all modality combinations, with the most consistent advantage in survival prediction C-Index and accuracy metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends heterogeneous graph neural networks (Zhang et al., KDD 2019) and relation-specific attention mechanisms (Hu et al., WWW 2020) from single-modality pathology graphs to a unified multi-modality setting spanning genomics, histopathology, cell graphs, and text. It departs from prior biomedical multimodal fusion methods (PathomicFusion, MCAT, TransFusion) that rely on cross-modal attention or optimal transport between only two modalities, instead encoding all cross-modal relations as typed edges in a heterogeneous graph with domain-knowledge-derived meta-paths. The graph prompting mechanism for missing modality completion is a novel repurposing of prompt-learning ideas from NLP into the graph domain, addressing a gap that prior multimodal methods largely ignore by assuming data completeness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTP-4o provides the first unified framework for handling four or more clinical modalities with explicit missing-modality robustness, which is a practical necessity in real clinical pipelines where data collection is incomplete. However, the evaluation is limited to two TCGA cancer types, the text modality is synthetically generated by MiniGPT-4 rather than real clinical reports (introducing noise), and the performance gains over strong baselines like MCAT and TransFusion are modest (1–2 AUC points). Open questions include whether the graph prompting generalizes to truly heterogeneous missing patterns, whether the framework scales to additional modalities (e.g., tabular EHR data, imaging beyond H&E), and whether the domain-knowledge meta-paths transfer across cancer types without re-derivation.
