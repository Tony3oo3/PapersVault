## Problem

Existing 3D/4D head avatar synthesis methods rely heavily on 3DMM for geometry reconstruction, expression control, and pose estimation, but monocular 3DMM reconstructors are inaccurate and the 3DMM parameter space has limited expressiveness, capping the fidelity and vividness of synthesized avatars. The paper asks whether a 4D head synthesizer can be learned in a fully data-driven manner from monocular videos without 3DMM inductive bias, by first converting them into pseudo multi-view videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240450/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source image (for identity/appearance) and a driving image (for expression and pose) as input, both at 512×512 resolution. It outputs an animatable tri-plane NeRF head that supports free-view rendering from arbitrary camera angles, with the motion embedding extracted from the driving image via a pre-trained 2D motion encoder rather than 3DMM parameters. Inference runs at 10 FPS (15 FPS with source-feature caching) on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

The core idea is a two-stage training pipeline that creates pseudo multi-view supervision from monocular videos. In stage 1, a static 3D head synthesizer Ψ₃d (the Portrait4D ViT backbone with all motion cross-attention layers disabled) is trained on synthetic multi-view data from the GenHead 4D GAN to predict tri-plane NeRF from a single image. In stage 2, the full 4D synthesizer Ψ is initialized from Ψ₃d with motion cross-attention layers randomly initialized, then trained via cross-view self-reenactment: for each real video frame, Ψ₃d generates novel views, and Ψ must take a source frame plus a driving frame at viewpoint θ_p and reconstruct the same motion at a different viewpoint θ_q. This distills geometry priors from Ψ₃d into Ψ while learning expressive motion control from real data. Asymmetric substitution rates (10% for the driving input, 80% for the target) ensure the model prioritizes reconstructing real frames while treating synthetic views as geometry regularization, and the use of a non-3D-aware 2D motion embedding at an arbitrary viewpoint squeezes out pose information to achieve strong 3D consistency.

## Results

On VFHQ self-reenactment, Portrait4D-v2 achieves LPIPS_h of 0.139, FID of 26.3, ID similarity of 0.873, AED of 0.019, and APD of 0.580, outperforming all 10 baselines on every metric. On cross-ID reenactment, it reaches FID 48.5, ID 0.736, AED 0.161, and APD 0.858, again leading all competitors. In the user study, 80.7% of participants rated its image quality as best and 71.6% rated its expression accuracy as best, compared to 11.6% and 6.28% for the next-best method (Portrait4D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Portrait4D-v2 outperforms all 10 baselines on every reported metric in both self- and cross-ID reenactment. The closest competitor is Portrait4D [9], which shares the same transformer backbone but relies on 3DMM-conditioned 4D GAN synthetic data; Portrait4D-v2 improves FID from 43.0→26.3 (self) and 54.8→48.5 (cross-ID), ID from 0.773→0.873 (self) and 0.620→0.736 (cross-ID), and APD from 0.612→0.580 (self) and 1.020→0.858 (cross-ID). Among 2D-based methods, Face-vid2vid [41] and PIRenderer [36] are the strongest, but they trail by large margins (e.g., FID 52.0 vs 26.3 on self-reenactment) and cannot support free-view rendering. Among 3D-aware methods that use 3DMM supervision (ROME, OTAvatar, HideNeRF, Real3DPortrait, GPAvatar, GOHA), the gap is even wider, with FID values ranging from 44.6 to 110 versus 26.3 for the proposed method. On cross-ID expression accuracy (AED_f) and lip-sync (SyncNet), Portrait4D-v2 also leads (0.185 AED_f, 3.66 SyncNet vs. 0.200/2.95 for GPAvatar and 0.205/3.23 for Portrait4D), confirming that the pseudo multi-view learning paradigm yields more faithful and identity-preserving expression transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to Portrait4D (same first author), which trained a 4D GAN to generate synthetic multi-view data for learning a feedforward 4D head synthesizer. The key departure is replacing the 4D GAN with a static 3D head synthesizer that converts real monocular videos into pseudo multi-view videos, thereby avoiding the 3DMM-conditioned expression space that limits the 4D GAN's data. It also departs from the broader family of 3D-aware head synthesis methods (ROME, HideNeRF, Real3DPortrait, GPAvatar) that rely on monocular 3DMM reconstructors for geometry and motion, instead learning geometry purely from cross-view self-reenactment on pseudo multi-view data. The ViT-based triplane reconstructor backbone is inherited from Portrait4D, but the training paradigm—frozen 3D synthesizer as a data augmentation tool for 4D learning—is the core conceptual contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a static 3D synthesizer can serve as a practical bridge between monocular video data and 4D synthesis, eliminating the need for either large-scale multi-view captures or 3DMM-based inductive bias. This opens a path to training 4D head synthesizers on arbitrarily large monocular video datasets while preserving geometry consistency, and enables the use of expressive 2D motion embeddings that go beyond 3DMM's limited description space. Limitations include dependence on a pre-trained 3D synthesizer (GenHead) whose imperfections propagate into the pseudo multi-view data, and the method is restricted to head synthesis. An open question is whether this pseudo multi-view distillation paradigm generalizes to full-body or scene-level 4D synthesis, and whether the 3D synthesizer itself could be learned end-to-end without synthetic data.
