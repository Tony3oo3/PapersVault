## Problem

The standard evaluation protocol for panoptic scene graph generation (MultiMPO) allows models to output duplicate masks and multiple predicate distributions per subject-object pair, inflating mR@k scores by up to 19.3 points for one-stage methods. This makes fair comparison across methods impossible and obscures the true relative performance of two-stage versus one-stage approaches. The paper corrects the protocol (SingleMPO) and introduces a new model that exploits the corrected evaluation to set a new SOTA.

## Input / Output

DSFormer receives a 640×640 RGB image, subject and object panoptic segmentation masks, and their class labels as input. The masks and labels come from a decoupled first-stage segmentation model (e.g., MaskDINO) at inference time, or from ground truth during training. The model outputs a per-relation predicate distribution over all predicate classes plus a virtual no-relation class, from which top-k relations are selected for mR@k or mNgR@k evaluation.

## Method

DSFormer is a two-stage architecture where a SOTA segmentation model (MaskDINO) provides masks and a lightweight ResNet-50 + FPN backbone extracts 160×160×256 features split into 8×8 patch tokens. The key encoding mechanism adds a weighted sum of three learnable tokens (subject, object, background) to each patch token, weighted by the mask overlap ratio, so the transformer retains global context while knowing where subject and object reside. A 6-layer ViT-inspired transformer processes these patch tokens along with optional semantic tokens (a unique learned vector per subject-object class pair) and location tokens (normalized bounding box coordinates projected via MLP). Training uses a weighted binary cross-entropy relation loss that down-weights false positives to handle noisy negative ground truth, plus an auxiliary node classification loss on subject/object identity. The decoupled design means only the scene graph module is trained, enabling easy swapping of segmentation backbones without retraining.

## Results

On the PSG dataset under the corrected SingleMPO protocol, DSFormer achieves 30.67 mR@50 and 50.08 mNgR@50 on Scene Graph Generation (SGGen), surpassing the previous SOTA (HiLo at 18.33 mR@50) by +12.34 mR@50 and +9.60 mNgR@50. On Predicate Classification (PredCls), DSFormer reaches 40.06 mR@50 and 64.05 mNgR@50, outperforming the best two-stage baseline VCTree (23.07 mR@50) by +17 mR@50. The paper further shows that under SingleMPO, all one-stage methods suffer score drops of up to 19.3 mR@50 compared to the previously reported MultiMPO scores, while two-stage methods remain largely unaffected.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSFormer decisively outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest one-stage method Pair-Net (19.64 mR@50 on SGGen), DSFormer gains +11.03 mR@50; against the previous SOTA HiLo (18.33 mR@50), the margin is +12.34 mR@50. Among two-stage baselines, VCTree is the strongest at 17.58 mR@50 on SGGen, and DSFormer exceeds it by +13.09 mR@50. The paper's key finding is that under the corrected SingleMPO protocol, one-stage methods (PSGTR, PSGFormer, Pair-Net, HiLo) lose up to 19.3 mR@50 relative to their originally published MultiMPO scores due to duplicate mask and relation exploitation, while two-stage methods (IMP, MOTIFS, GPS-Net, VCTree) are essentially unchanged since they inherently produce single masks per object. This re-ranking reveals that two-stage methods with a strong segmentation backbone (e.g., MaskDINO) are competitive with or superior to one-stage methods, a conclusion that was obscured under the flawed MultiMPO evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the recent one-stage paradigm in panoptic scene graph generation (PSGTR, PSGFormer, Pair-Net, HiLo) by arguing that a decoupled two-stage approach—where a SOTA segmentation model provides masks and a lightweight transformer infers only the graph—can outperform end-to-end methods. Architecturally, DSFormer replaces the RoIAlign-based feature cropping used by prior two-stage methods (IMP, MOTIFS, GPS-Net, VCTree) with a mask-aware token encoding that injects subject/object/background ratios directly into ViT patch tokens, preserving global context. The paper also repositions the evaluation landscape by identifying a systematic flaw in the MultiMPO protocol introduced with the PSG dataset, which allowed one-stage models to inflate scores through duplicate masks and relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SingleMPO correction is a fundamental fix that invalidates prior SOTA rankings in PSGG: one-stage methods lose up to 19.3 mR@50 while two-stage methods are unaffected, reversing the perceived superiority of one-stage approaches. This shifts the research agenda toward leveraging ever-improving segmentation foundation models rather than jointly learning masks and relations. Limitations include dependence on a single dataset (PSG), single-label metrics that underrepresent multi-predicate relations, and the fact that DSFormer's performance is tightly coupled to first-stage segmentation quality. Open questions include extending to multi-label PSGG (partially addressed by the Haystack dataset), exploiting inter-relation dependencies, and enabling zero-shot predicate generalization.
