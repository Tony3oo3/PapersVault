## Problem

Imitation-based trajectory predictors for traffic simulation struggle to capture safety-critical, long-tail events that involve multiple interacting drivers, because they model each vehicle independently and cannot represent the competitive, interdependent decision-making that characterizes real driving. Existing refinement strategies (safety mapping, compliance constraints) focus on a single target agent and ignore how surrounding vehicles react, creating a persistent sim-to-real gap. This paper reframes traffic simulation as a general-sum Markov game and targets the Coarse Correlated Equilibrium (CCE) to model these multi-agent competitive interactions.

## Input / Output

The world model consumes a static traffic map, spatial-temporal agent trajectories over a sliding window, and neighboring vehicle features, producing a constrained action space parameterized as a mixture of K Laplace distributions over (Δx, Δy) position increments per timestep. The RL agent outputs a correlated policy for each vehicle conditioned on its local observation and the full decision history of all agents, meaning each agent's action distribution explicitly depends on others' past behaviors. During evaluation, trajectories are sampled as a weighted product of per-timestep Laplace mixtures, yielding diverse multi-agent driving trajectories that reflect competitive equilibria rather than independent predictions.

## Method

The approach has two stages: (1) a generative world model built from cross-attention layers (map encoder → agent-to-map attention for transitions; agent-to-temporal and agent-to-agent attention feeding a mixture-of-Laplace policy network for constrained action prediction; agent-specific MLP for observations) is pretrained via supervised best-mode and classification losses on offline multi-agent trajectories; (2) CCE-MASAC, a multi-agent soft actor-critic under CTDE, learns the CCE on top of this world model by optimizing an objective that combines an optimistic Bellman value (adding an exploration bonus α_t(s)=c/m(s) for less-visited states, extending CCE-V-Learning to continuous actions) with magnetic mirror descent (KL divergence to the imitation policy for fidelity and to the previous-iteration policy for stable updates). The resulting per-agent objective reduces to a maximum-entropy soft actor-critic form where the Boltzmann policy is proportional to exp(Q/η)·(π^o)^{η₁}·(π^{j-1})^{1/η₂}, and actions are sampled via a Laplace reparameterization trick to enable gradient-based policy improvement.

## Results

The primary metric is the CCE-gap (exploitability), where lower is better. CCE-MASAC consistently achieves the smallest CCE-gap across all four Argoverse 2 scenarios (Wrong-way driving, Four-lane intersection, T-junction merging, Dense-lane intersection) and all agents, outperforming MASAC, MAPPO, QCNet, QCNext, and GameFormer. In terms of discounted cumulative rewards (Figure 4), single-agent SAC achieves the highest rewards, while CCE-MASAC yields lower rewards than SAC but higher than MASAC, reflecting the trade-off between individual reward maximization and equilibrium fidelity under competitive interactions. The detailed per-agent CCE-gap values are reported in Table 2 (Appendix F); the main body presents training curves (Figure 3) showing CCE-MASAC converging to the lowest gap most rapidly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCE-MASAC outperforms all five baselines on the CCE-gap metric across all four scenarios. QCNet and QCNext (supervised trajectory predictors) produce the largest CCE-gaps because they imitate ground-truth trajectories without modeling inter-agent competitiveness. GameFormer, which applies hierarchical game theory, also yields a larger CCE-gap than CCE-MASAC, suggesting its game-theoretic modeling is insufficient for capturing coarse correlated equilibria in continuous multi-agent driving. MAPPO is inherently unstable and struggles to converge to any meaningful equilibrium. MASAC (multi-agent SAC without optimistic Bellman update or magnetic mirror descent) performs better than the supervised baselines but still falls short of CCE-MASAC, confirming that the optimistic value function and MMD regularization are critical for closing the CCE-gap. In the single-agent reward comparison (Figure 4), SAC outperforms CCE-MASAC, indicating that the competitive equilibrium constraint intentionally sacrifices some individual reward to achieve a more realistic and safety-critical multi-agent behavior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CCE-V-Learning algorithms for general-sum Markov games (Bai et al. 2020; Song et al. 2022), which are extended here from discrete to continuous action spaces via a soft actor-critic formulation; (2) the Soft Actor-Critic framework (Haarnoja et al. 2018), adapted to a multi-agent CTDE setting with agent-specific critics and actors; and (3) the QCNet mixture-of-Laplace trajectory prediction architecture (Zhou et al. 2023), repurposed as a generative world model that provides the transition, observation, and constrained action-space dynamics for offline MARL. The key conceptual departure from prior traffic simulation work (which relies on imitation or single-agent safety mapping) is the explicit game-theoretic formulation: rather than imitating human trajectories, the agents learn a Coarse Correlated Equilibrium that captures interdependent competitive decisions, with Magnetic Mirror Descent (Sokota et al. 2023) and optimistic Bellman updates providing the convergence machinery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap in traffic simulation: imitation-based predictors fail to generate safety-critical long-tail events that involve multi-agent competitive interactions, creating a persistent sim-to-real gap. By framing driving as a general-sum Dec-POMDP and targeting CCE rather than Nash Equilibrium, the approach is both more realistic (drivers condition on others' behavior) and more tractable (CCEs are less restrictive and more prevalent than NEs). However, the evaluation is limited to four hand-picked Argoverse 2 scenarios with a small number of controlled agents, and the paper acknowledges two significant limitations: no modeling of physical constraints (velocity, collision avoidance) and no treatment of cooperative behaviors (yielding, coordinated merging), both of which are essential for realistic traffic. Open questions include whether the CCE formulation scales to dense multi-agent scenes, how to incorporate hard safety constraints into the equilibrium search, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the Argoverse 2 map topology.
