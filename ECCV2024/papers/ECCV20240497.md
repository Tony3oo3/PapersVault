## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods optimize each Gaussian independently without leveraging the scene's underlying geometry, causing points to drift far from their initial positions, producing floating artifacts, and degrading depth accuracy. This structure-agnostic optimization limits both rendering fidelity and the geometric integrity needed for VR/AR applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240497/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse COLMAP point cloud (M×3) and a set of sparse RGB views with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs a set of 3D Gaussians (position, color, opacity, covariance) that are splatted via the standard 3D-GS rasterizer to produce novel-view renderings at >100 FPS. The SAGS-Lite variant additionally generates mid-point Gaussians on-the-fly from interpolated key-point features, reducing stored Gaussians by up to 24× without any compression or quantization scheme.

## Method

SAGS builds a k-NN graph over the COLMAP point cloud and applies a curvature-aware densification step (inspired by Grad-PU) to populate low-curvature, under-sampled regions with mid-points. A graph neural network encoder aggregates local neighborhood features using inverse-distance-weighted message passing with relative feature differences, augmented by a global max-pooled feature and multi-resolution hash positional encodings. Four small MLPs (hidden size 32) decode the structural encoding into Gaussian attributes: color, opacity, scale/rotation, and a displacement vector Δp added to the initial COLMAP position, enforcing camera-agnostic geometry. Training uses a standard L1 + SSIM loss over 30,000 iterations with progressive growing/pruning. The SAGS-Lite variant stores only the initial key-point features and generates mid-point Gaussians at inference by linearly interpolating both positions and structural features between neighboring key points.

## Results

SAGS achieves state-of-the-art rendering quality across all three benchmark datasets, reaching 29.65 PSNR / 0.874 SSIM / 0.179 LPIPS on Mip-NeRF360, 24.88 PSNR / 0.866 SSIM / 0.166 LPIPS on Tanks&Temples, and 30.47 PSNR / 0.913 SSIM / 0.241 LPIPS on Deep Blending. In terms of efficiency, SAGS reduces storage by 5.1×–11.7× compared to 3D-GS while maintaining real-time rendering speeds of 108–138 FPS. The lightweight SAGS-Lite variant achieves up to 24× storage reduction (28 MB on Deep Blending) without any compression scheme, at a modest quality cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGS outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor Scaffold-GS, SAGS gains +0.81 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 (29.65 vs 28.84), +0.92 PSNR on Tanks&Temples (24.88 vs 23.96), and +0.26 PSNR on Deep Blending (30.47 vs 30.21), while also reducing storage by 2×–2.1× (135 MB vs 252 MB on Mip-NeRF360; 58 MB vs 66 MB on Deep Blending). Compared to the original 3D-GS, SAGS improves PSNR by +0.96 (Mip-NeRF360), +1.74 (Tanks&Temples), and +1.06 (Deep Blending) while cutting memory by 5.1×–11.7×. The NeRF-based baselines (Mip-NeRF360, iNGP, Plenoxels) are substantially outperformed, with SAGS exceeding Mip-NeRF360 by +0.42 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and by +2.66 PSNR on Tanks&Temples. SAGS-Lite trades a small quality drop (e.g., −0.11 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 vs full SAGS) for up to 24× storage reduction, still matching or exceeding 3D-GS on Tanks&Temples PSNR (24.16 vs 23.14).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) by replacing its structure-agnostic, per-Gaussian optimization with a graph neural network encoder that aggregates local and global scene structure from a k-NN graph over the COLMAP point cloud. It departs from Scaffold-GS, which introduced a hierarchical scaffold but still optimizes Gaussians independently, by explicitly modeling inter-point connectivity to predict Gaussian attributes as displacements from initial positions. The method also draws on point cloud upsampling (Grad-PU) for curvature-aware densification and on multi-resolution hash encodings (iNGP) for positional encoding, effectively bridging the point cloud analysis and 3D Gaussian splatting literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enforcing structure-aware point displacements, SAGS mitigates the floater artifacts and depth inconsistencies that plague structure-agnostic 3D-GS methods, which is critical for downstream VR/AR applications requiring geometrically faithful reconstructions. The SAGS-Lite variant's 24× storage reduction via simple mid-point interpolation—without any compression or quantization—offers a practical path to compact scene representations. Open questions include whether the GNN-based approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, whether the k-NN graph construction remains effective under extreme sparsity, and whether the structure-aware inductive bias generalizes beyond static outdoor/indoor scenes to more complex or unbounded environments.
