## Problem

In class incremental semantic segmentation, initializing new classifiers is critical for preventing catastrophic forgetting and background shift, yet existing methods (background-classifier copying, auxiliary classifiers, gradient-based attribution) treat all new classes uniformly and lack a data-driven alignment step with the current feature extractor. This misalignment causes drastic parameter updates in the backbone when new data arrives, undermining previously learned knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240682/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from Pascal VOC 2012 (20 classes) or ADE20K (150 classes) under overlapped incremental settings (e.g., 10-1, 15-1, 100-5), where pixels of future classes are labeled as background in the current step. It outputs per-pixel class predictions over all classes learned so far. During the pre-tuning phase, the same current-step training images are used, but only lightweight transformation matrices are updated while the feature extractor remains frozen, adding negligible FLOPs (~15.6K) and parameters (~5.4K).

## Method

NeST (New claSifier pre-Tuning) learns a per-class linear transformation from all old classifiers to generate new classifier weights before formal training begins. For each new class c, an importance matrix M_c (Hadamard product with old classifier channels) and a projection matrix P_c (linear combination across old classes) are jointly optimized with unbiased cross-entropy loss on current-step data, while the backbone stays frozen. A cross-task class similarity strategy initializes these matrices by decomposing the old model's forward pass into per-channel Hadamard products, masking positive contributions, and averaging over ground-truth new-class pixels to identify which old classes and channels are most relevant. This yields class-specific, data-adapted initializations that bridge the stability-plasticity gap, and the extra parameters are discarded after pre-tuning so the formal training proceeds identically to the baseline.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012 (15-1, ResNet-101), NeST improves MiB from 38.2% to 51.8% mIoU (+13.6%), PLOP from 56.2% to 63.1% (+6.9%), and RCIL from 58.9% to 61.4% (+2.5%). On the harder 10-1 setting, MiB+NeST achieves 37.4% mIoU versus 10.2% for MiB alone (+27.2%). On ADE20K (100-5, ResNet-101), NeST boosts MiB from 25.9% to 32.7% (+6.8%), PLOP from 29.2% to 32.0% (+2.8%), and RCIL from 30.2% to 31.5% (+1.3%). With Swin-B backbone on Pascal VOC 15-1, MiB+NeST reaches 71.4% mIoU versus 36.9% for MiB alone (+34.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeST is a plug-and-play module applied to three base methods (MiB, PLOP, RCIL), and in every setting it outperforms the corresponding base method. On Pascal VOC 15-1 (Res101), PLOP+NeST (63.1% all mIoU) surpasses GSC (60.8%), ALIFE (57.4%), SPPA (56.0%), MiB+AWT (49.1%), and the original PLOP (56.2%). RCIL+NeST (61.4%) also exceeds GSC and ALIFE. On the harder 10-1 setting, MiB+NeST (37.4%) dramatically outperforms MiB (10.2%), SDR (25.1%), and ILT (5.5%), though it remains below the Joint Training upper bound (78.3%). On ADE20K 100-5, PLOP+NeST (32.0%) edges out MiB+AWT (31.1%) and GSC (30.2%), while RCIL+NeST (31.5%) slightly trails ALIFE (35.0%) on the 100-10 setting but leads on 100-5. The largest gains appear when the base method is weakest (MiB), confirming that NeST's classifier pre-tuning is most impactful when the original initialization is poor.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeST builds directly on the CISS framework established by MiB [4], which introduced the background shift problem and unbiased cross entropy loss, and extends the line of classifier initialization strategies (MiB's background-copy, AWT's gradient-based attribution, SSUL/DKD's auxiliary classifiers). The key conceptual departure is shifting from directly initializing or tuning new classifier weights to learning a *transformation* (importance matrix × projection matrix) from all old classifiers in a dedicated pre-tuning phase, thereby decoupling the generation of new classifiers from the formal training process. The cross-task class similarity-based initialization of these matrices further distinguishes NeST from prior methods that treat all new classes identically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeST offers a lightweight, plug-and-drop module (two small matrices per new class, ~5K extra parameters, ~2.7% extra training time) that can be bolted onto existing CISS methods like MiB, PLOP, and RCIL without architectural changes, making it immediately practical for practitioners. The finding that the *initialization* of new classifiers—not the training dynamics—is the primary bottleneck for stability in CISS reframes where methodological effort should be directed. Limitations include the additional pre-tuning overhead (which grows with the number of old classes) and the fact that gains over strong baselines (PLOP, RCIL) are more modest (2–7%) than over weaker ones (MiB, 13–36%). Open questions include whether the transformation-based initialization generalizes to other continual learning settings beyond segmentation, as the authors themselves note.
