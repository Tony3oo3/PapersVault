## Problem

Existing multimodal information retrieval systems are task-specific and homogeneous, each fine-tuned for a single retrieval format (e.g., text-to-image on MSCOCO or image-to-text on EDIS) within a narrow domain. This limits their ability to serve diverse user needs that span multiple modalities, domains, and retrieval intentions simultaneously. The paper asks whether a single instruction-guided retriever can handle all such heterogeneous retrieval tasks from a unified candidate pool.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts any combination of text query, image query, or compositional image+text query, paired with a natural language instruction (e.g., 'Find an image that is identical to the given image') that specifies the retrieval intent, target modality, and domain. Instructions are prepended to the text query as a prefix; padding tokens fill in missing modalities. The model retrieves from a heterogeneous pool of 5.6M candidates spanning text, images, and image-text pairs across four domains, using dot-product similarity in a shared embedding space with 224×224 image resolution.

## Method

UniIR builds on pretrained CLIP (ViT-L/14) and BLIP backbones and introduces two multimodal fusion mechanisms to extend them beyond cross-modal retrieval: score-level fusion computes a weighted sum of modality-specific encoder outputs with learnable importance weights (w₁–w₄), while feature-level fusion integrates modalities via a 2-layer mixed-modality transformer (CLIP_FF) or cross-attention layers in BLIP's image-grounded text encoder (BLIP_FF). The key training innovation is instruction tuning: human-authored natural language instructions describing retrieval intent, domain, and target modality are prepended to text queries, and the model is trained with a query-target contrastive objective across 1.1M instances from 10 datasets covering 8 retrieval tasks. Ablations show that multi-task training alone yields +9.7 R@5 over dataset-specific training, while adding instruction tuning provides an additional +10 R@5 and enables zero-shot generalization to held-out datasets, primarily by teaching the model to select the correct candidate modality rather than defaulting to same-modality retrieval.

## Results

On the M-BEIR heterogeneous candidate pool (5.6M candidates), the best UniIR model (CLIP_SF) achieves an average Recall@5 of 48.9, substantially outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline GPT-4V (16.7) and all cross-modal embedding models (SigLIP 8.2, CLIP 8.0, BLIP2 6.1, BLIP 5.8). The largest gains appear on cross-modal retrieval tasks (e.g., text→image on MSCOCO: 77.9 vs. 0.0 for CLIP; text→image-text on EDIS: 59.4 vs. 6.7 for CLIP), where zero-shot models fail to identify the correct modality. On the dataset-specific pool (M-BEIR_local), UniIR (CLIP_SF) reaches 50.6 avg R@5 and UniIR (BLIP_FF) reaches 46.8, both surpassing SigLIP's 37.2 zero-shot average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniIR (CLIP_SF) achieves 48.9 average Recall@5 on the 5.6M heterogeneous pool, outperforming GPT-4V (16.7) by 32.2 points, SigLIP (8.2) by 40.7 points, CLIP (8.0) by 40.9 points, and BLIP2 (6.1) by 42.8 points. The gap is most dramatic on cross-modal tasks: on text→image (Task 1) and image→text (Task 4), all cross-modal embedding baselines (CLIP, SigLIP, BLIP, BLIP2) score 0.0 because they retrieve candidates from the wrong modality, while UniIR scores 42–92. GPT-4V is the strongest zero-shot baseline, reaching 72.5 on image→text MSCOCO and 56.1 on text→text WebQA, but still trails UniIR by 19.8 and 28.6 points respectively. On within-modality tasks (text→text WebQA, image→image NIGHTS), the margin narrows (e.g., NIGHTS: UniIR 32.0 vs. GPT-4V 30.1), confirming that instruction-tuning primarily helps the model disambiguate retrieval intent across modalities. On the dataset-specific pool (M-BEIR_local), UniIR (CLIP_SF) averages 50.6 R@5 vs. SigLIP's 37.2 zero-shot, and UniIR (BLIP_FF) at 46.8 surpasses SigLIP by 9.6 points, with the largest win on CIRR (52.2 vs. 22.7 for SigLIP).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniIR extends the instruction-tuning paradigm established for text retrieval (TART, OneEmbedder) and LLMs (FLAN) into the multimodal domain, where prior work has been limited to task-specific fine-tuning of CLIP or BLIP on individual datasets. The model architecture builds directly on CLIP and BLIP vision-language encoders, with two fusion mechanisms (score-level and feature-level) adapted from prior multimodal retrieval systems like OVEN and EDIS. The M-BEIR benchmark is the multimodal analogue of BEIR, unifying 10 heterogeneous datasets under a single instruction-guided retrieval formulation rather than treating each task in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

M-BEIR provides the first standardized benchmark for evaluating universal multimodal retrievers across 8 task types and 4 domains with a 5.6M candidate pool, filling a gap that has hindered fair comparison in this area. The clean ablation showing that instruction tuning (not just multi-task training) is the critical component for cross-modality retrieval and zero-shot generalization offers a practical recipe for building more flexible retrieval systems. However, performance remains far from saturated (best average Recall@5 around 50%), the system is limited to image+text modalities, and the paper acknowledges that stronger vision-language backbones and large-scale pre-training are needed to close the gap—leaving open whether the instruction-guided formulation scales to truly open-ended, multi-modal search.
