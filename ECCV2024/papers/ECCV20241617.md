## Problem

Feature-based CAD reverse engineering from point clouds has been largely addressed by adapting generative models (e.g., DeepCAD) that rely on a fixed latent space, limiting generalization to noisy or irregular real-world scans. Existing approaches also produce implicit representations (CSG, B-Rep) that require post-processing and do not integrate seamlessly into CAD software. Additionally, the standard evaluation metrics (command accuracy, parameter accuracy, Chamfer Distance) fail to penalize over-prediction and cannot distinguish error magnitudes in the parametric space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4096-point 3D point cloud with estimated surface normals as input and produces a full parametric CAD sequence: a variable-length list of loops (each a sequence of primitives described by 6 coordinates: start, mid, end 2D points) and extrusion operations (11 quantized parameters encoding plane, scale, type, and distances). Primitives are represented in a type-agnostic format where the geometric type (line, arc, circle) is inferred from coordinate configurations rather than predicted as a separate categorical label. Loop parameters are first predicted in an 8-bit quantized space and then refined to unquantized values by a dedicated refiner network.

## Method

TransCAD is a single-stage, end-to-end, non-autoregressive hierarchical transformer. A 4-layer PointNet++ encoder produces per-point features, which are cross-attended by a loop-extrusion decoder (multi-head transformer blocks with learned constant embeddings) to yield a high-level sequence of loop/extrusion embeddings along with predicted step types. These embeddings are then routed to specialized decoders: a 4-layer transformer loop decoder (exploiting sequential structure) and a 3-layer MLP extrusion decoder, each predicting quantized parameters via cross-entropy. A loop refiner (4-layer MLP) takes the concatenation of loop embeddings and predicted parameter probabilities to regress the offset between quantized predictions and unquantized ground truth, correcting accumulated quantization errors. The total loss is the sum of type classification, loop parameter, extrusion parameter, and refiner MSE losses, all trained jointly.

## Results

On the DeepCAD dataset, TransCAD achieves an APCS of 0.732, substantially outperforming DeepCAD (0.604) and the retrieval baseline (0.629), while also reducing the invalidity ratio to 0.011 (vs. 0.038 for DeepCAD and 0.115 for MultiCAD). On the cross-dataset Fusion360 evaluation, TransCAD reaches an APCS of 0.365 and a median CD of 33.3, compared to DeepCAD's 0.360 APCS and 104.2 CD. The retrieval baseline achieves the lowest CD on DeepCAD (2.8 vs. TransCAD's 4.5) due to dataset duplicates, but TransCAD dominates on APCS and IR across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransCAD is compared against two learned baselines (DeepCAD and MultiCAD) and a retrieval baseline. On the DeepCAD dataset, TransCAD wins on APCS (0.732 vs. 0.604 for DeepCAD) and IR (0.011 vs. 0.038), but the retrieval baseline achieves a lower CD (2.8 vs. 4.5) due to the presence of duplicate models in the training set. On the cross-dataset Fusion360 evaluation, TransCAD clearly outperforms DeepCAD on CD (33.3 vs. 104.2) while matching it on APCS (0.365 vs. 0.360), and also beats the retrieval baseline on CD (33.3 vs. 60.0). MultiCAD does not report APCS values, but TransCAD achieves a lower CD (4.5 vs. 8.1 on DeepCAD; 33.3 vs. 42.2 on Fusion360) and a substantially lower IR (0.011 vs. 0.115; 0.024 vs. 0.165). Under perturbed inputs (noise and holes), TransCAD maintains its APCS advantage over DeepCAD while achieving a much lower CD (18.1 vs. 31.1 under noise; 4.4 vs. 26.7 under holes).

## Relation to Prior Work

TransCAD builds directly on the DeepCAD [42] and MultiCAD [27] line of work for feature-based CAD sequence prediction, but departs from their two-stage (separate point-cloud and CAD-sequence training) paradigm by proposing the first non-autoregressive, single-stage, end-to-end trainable architecture for this task. It introduces a hierarchical two-tiered decoding strategy (a loop-extrusion decoder followed by specialized loop and extrusion decoders) and a type-agnostic primitive representation that eliminates explicit categorical type prediction, narrowing the search space compared to DeepCAD's formulation. The proposed APCS evaluation metric also represents a conceptual departure from the accuracy-based framework in [42,27], addressing over-prediction and error-magnitude blind spots.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating robustness to realistic point-cloud perturbations (Perlin noise, missing geometry) and achieving state-of-the-art CAD sequence recovery, TransCAD moves feature-based reverse engineering closer to practical deployment with commercial 3D scanners. The APCS metric is likely to become a standard for fair comparison in this emerging area, as it penalizes over-prediction and distinguishes error magnitudes in unquantized parameter space. Key limitations remain: the model handles only extrusion operations, struggles with models containing many small features, and still produces a small fraction of invalid sequences. Extending to full CAD operation sets (revolution, fillet, boolean) and handling higher-complexity industrial models are natural next steps.
