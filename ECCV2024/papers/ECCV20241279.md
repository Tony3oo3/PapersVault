## Problem

Existing VQA datasets are predominantly artificially constructed to probe model performance, and the only fully authentic one (VizWiz-VQA) is limited to blind users with answers never validated by questioners. Moreover, standard VQA evaluation metrics assume brief answers (≤11 words), rendering them ineffective for authentic community question-answering where responses average 173 words in paragraph form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241279/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each VQA example takes an RGB image, a natural language question (mean 9.3 words), and a natural language context (mean 127 words, median 94 words) that provides supplementary information needed to answer the question. The expected output is a long-form text answer averaging 173 words (median 120), typically structured as 3-sentence paragraphs, spanning 105 diverse topics from Stack Exchange. This answer length is orders of magnitude longer than the 1–11 word answers typical of prior VQA benchmarks, necessitating entirely different evaluation approaches.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by scraping Stack Exchange (Aug 2010–Nov 2022), retaining only examples with an accepted answer, positive community scores, a single non-GIF image, and no visual answers, yielding 64,696 VQAs across 105 topics in 5 super-categories. For evaluation, the authors adopt five long-form text metrics—ROUGE-L, METEOR, and BERTscore for answer similarity, and CLIP-S and RefCLIP-S for image-answer compatibility—since the standard VQA accuracy metric is inapplicable to paragraph-length responses. They benchmark six VLMs (mPLUG-Owl, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP, BLIP-2, GPT-4V) in zero-shot and one-shot settings, ablate input types (question, context, image) to assess each component's contribution, and conduct an IRB-approved human study with 10 domain experts rating 1,200 model-generated answers to quantify how well each automated metric aligns with human judgment.

## Results

On VQAonline (64,696 VQAs with mean answer length of 173 words), mPLUG-Owl achieves the best zero-shot performance on 4 of 5 metrics: ROUGE-L 0.16, METEOR 0.12, BERTscore 0.76, and RefCLIP-S 0.75. LLaVA edges out mPLUG-Owl on CLIP-S (0.73 vs. 0.72), suggesting it generates more image-compatible but not necessarily more accurate answers. All models show compressed score ranges (e.g., BERTscore spans only 0.67–0.76), and one-shot in-context examples fail to improve mPLUG-Owl's performance. Human evaluation (10 domain experts, 200 VQAs) confirms the ranking: GPT-4V (0.76) > mPLUG-Owl (0.57) > LLaVA (0.50) > MiniGPT-4 (0.37) > InstructBLIP (0.12) > BLIP-2 (0.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six VLMs on the new VQAonline dataset rather than proposing a new model. mPLUG-Owl leads on ROUGE-L (0.16 vs. 0.15 for LLaVA/MiniGPT-4), METEOR (0.12 vs. 0.08/0.09), and BERTscore (0.76 vs. 0.75), but LLaVA surpasses it on CLIP-S (0.73 vs. 0.72), indicating LLaVA's answers are more visually grounded even if less textually aligned with references. MiniGPT-4 consistently outperforms InstructBLIP and BLIP-2 across all five metrics (e.g., BERTscore 0.75 vs. 0.67/0.69; METEOR 0.09 vs. 0.05/0.04), while InstructBLIP and BLIP-2 are closely matched at the bottom. The authors note that mPLUG-Owl, LLaVA, and MiniGPT-4 likely had access to Stack Exchange data during training, giving them an unfair advantage, yet their still-poor scores (e.g., METEOR ≤ 0.12) underscore the dataset's difficulty. Human evaluation with domain experts confirms the quantitative ranking and reveals that even the best model (GPT-4V at 0.76) leaves substantial room for improvement, while the weakest (BLIP-2 at 0.10) is nearly useless on authentic long-form VQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in VQA by introducing the first dataset where every component—question, context, image, and answer—originates from an authentic community use case (Stack Exchange), departing from the artificially-constructed paradigm of VQAv2, OK-VQA, and DocVQA. The closest prior work, VizWiz-VQA, also sources authentic questions but from a single population (blind users) with crowdsourced, unvalidated answers; VQAonline extends authenticity to the full pipeline including asker-validated answers and 105 diverse topics. The dramatically longer answers (mean 173 words vs. ≤11 in prior datasets) force a departure from standard VQA accuracy metrics toward long-form text evaluation (ROUGE-L, METEOR, BERTscore, CLIP-S, RefCLIP-S), a methodological shift not addressed in prior VQA benchmarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset exposes a significant gap between VQA model performance on synthetic benchmarks and authentic community questions: even GPT-4V achieves only 0.76 human-rated correctness, and the best open model (mPLUG-Owl) scores 0.57. The finding that removing the image or the question barely degrades performance—despite 65.7% of images being deemed necessary—suggests current VLMs rely heavily on language priors rather than genuine visual grounding, a concern with broad implications for the field. Open questions include developing evaluation metrics that better capture factual accuracy in long-form answers (the LLaMA2-based metric shows promise but is computationally expensive), improving multilingual VQA where performance drops sharply for non-Germanic scripts, and determining whether one-shot or few-shot learning can be made effective for the high diversity of authentic community questions.
