## Problem

Existing concept-erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models fail to simultaneously satisfy robustness (resisting paraphrased or learned attack prompts) and locality (preserving generation of non-target concepts). Full-model fine-tuning approaches (ESD, Ablating) compromise locality by updating many parameters, while cross-attention-only methods (UCE, FMN) are vulnerable to paraphrased prompts because they cannot recognize semantic similarity between rephrased queries and the erased concept.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (simple, paraphrased, or adversarially learned) as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image via DDIM sampling (50 steps, guidance scale 7.5) from a Stable Diffusion v1.4 backbone. The lightweight eraser (0.37% of U-Net parameters) is inserted after each cross-attention layer and operates on the visual feature outputs at multiple resolutions, with its predictions directly added to the cross-attention outputs before further U-Net processing.

## Method

Receler introduces a lightweight adapter-based eraser placed after every cross-attention layer in the diffusion U-Net, trained to predict negatively guided noise that steers generation away from the target concept. Two key training components ensure the desired properties: (1) concept-localized regularization, which thresholds cross-attention maps of the target concept's text tokens to produce a binary spatial mask, then penalizes the eraser's output outside that mask to preserve non-target content; and (2) adversarial prompt learning, where continuous soft prompts are iteratively optimized to regenerate the erased concept from the modified model, while the eraser is simultaneously trained to suppress those regenerated images. The eraser and adversarial prompts are trained in an alternating fashion (each fixed while the other updates), and the original U-Net weights remain frozen throughout, ensuring parameter efficiency and minimal interference with pre-trained capabilities.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 object erasure, Receler achieves a harmonic mean (H) of 83.2, exceeding the second-best method UCE (72.0) by 11.2 points. Specifically, Receler attains 14.9% efficacy (Acc_E, lower is better), 17.6% robustness (Acc_R), and 82.1% locality (Acc_L) on average across 10 erased classes. On the I2P inappropriate-content benchmark, Receler reduces the overall inappropriate proportion to 27.0% (vs. 31.3% for UCE and 32.8% for ESD) and erases 84.5% of nudity content. Against learned attack prompts (P4D, Ring-A-Bell), Receler achieves failure rates of 13.7% (CIFAR-10) and 1.1% (nudity via Ring-A-Bell), substantially lower than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Receler outperforms all five erasure baselines on the primary CIFAR-10 benchmark. Against ESD [15], the closest competitor on efficacy, Receler reduces Acc_R from 39.9% to 17.6% (a 22.3-point improvement in robustness) while also improving Acc_E (14.9% vs. 17.1%) and Acc_L (82.1% vs. 75.9%). Against UCE [16], which achieves the best locality among baselines (81.6%), Receler matches locality (82.1%) while dramatically improving robustness (17.6% vs. 42.4% Acc_R) and efficacy (14.9% vs. 17.1%). FMN [52] and Ablating [23] preserve locality well (87.4% and 86.0% Acc_L) but fail to meaningfully erase the target concept (Acc_E drops of only 3.2% and 7.6% from SD, versus Receler's 81.2% drop). On the I2P inappropriate-content benchmark, Receler achieves the lowest overall inappropriate proportion (27.0% vs. UCE 31.3%, ESD 32.8%) and the highest nudity erasure ratio (84.5% vs. ESD 81.3%). Against learned attack prompts, Receler's failure rate drops to 13.7% (P4D, CIFAR-10) and 1.1% (Ring-A-Bell, nudity), compared to 48.1% and 55.8% for ESD and 53.8% and 49.5% for UCE.

## Relation to Prior Work

Receler sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) concept erasure in diffusion models, where prior methods like ESD fine-tune the full U-Net with negative guidance, UCE and FMN edit only cross-attention projection matrices, and Ablating maps target concepts to anchor concepts; (2) parameter-efficient adapters for diffusion models (T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter), which Receler repurposes as a lightweight 'eraser' inserted after each cross-attention layer; and (3) adversarial training, where Receler borrows the min-max loop from adversarial robustness literature but applies it to prompt embeddings rather than input perturbations. The key departure from prior erasure work is the explicit joint optimization of a spatially-regularized adapter (for locality) and adversarial soft prompts (for robustness), whereas previous methods addressed at most one of these properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent safety concern—preventing diffusion models from generating NSFW or copyrighted content—while showing that a 0.37%-parameter adapter can outperform full fine-tuning methods on both robustness and locality, which is encouraging for deployment where retraining is infeasible. The adversarial robustness gap (13.7% vs. 48.1% failure rate against P4D prompts) is substantial and suggests that paraphrase-resilient erasure requires visual-level understanding rather than purely textual dissociation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), how compositional erasure via output averaging degrades with many simultaneous concepts, and whether the adversarial training loop converges stably for more semantically complex or abstract target concepts beyond CIFAR-10 objects and nudity.
