## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inpainting methods fall into two camps with complementary weaknesses: sampling-strategy modifications (e.g., Blended Latent Diffusion) lack perceptual knowledge of mask boundaries and unmasked context, while dedicated inpainting models (e.g., Stable Diffusion Inpainting, HD-Painter) fuse noisy latent, masked image, mask, and text at the first UNet layer, causing text embeddings to contaminate masked-image features and requiring full fine-tuning of the backbone. No prior method is simultaneously plug-and-play with arbitrary pre-trained diffusion models, content-aware, shape-aware, and flexible in unmasked-region preservation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a masked RGB image, a binary mask, and a text prompt, and outputs a fully inpainted image at the same resolution. The masked image is first encoded by the VAE encoder to align with the pre-trained UNet's latent distribution, and the mask is downsampled via cubic interpolation to match the latent spatial dimensions (e.g., 64×64 for 512×512 input). A pixel-space blurred blending step is applied post-decoding to guarantee exact preservation of the unmasked region, with the blur radius and blending on/off serving as additional control knobs.

## Method

BrushNet introduces a trainable dual-branch architecture where a clone of the pre-trained UNet (with all cross-attention layers removed to preserve pure image features) processes the concatenated noisy latent, VAE-encoded masked image latent, and downsampled mask. The extracted features are injected layer-by-layer into the frozen pre-trained UNet through zero-convolution blocks, enabling dense per-pixel hierarchical control rather than the sparse residual injection used by ControlNet. A scalar preservation weight w scales the BrushNet contribution, allowing continuous adjustment from precise to rough unmasked-region control. A blurred-mask copy-and-paste in pixel space (rather than latent-space blending) ensures near-perfect unmasked region fidelity. The model is trained with standard diffusion denoising loss on BrushData (segmentation masks auto-annotated via Grounded-SAM on Laion-Aesthetic) or LAION-5B with random masks, requiring only the BrushNet branch to be learned while the base UNet remains frozen.

## Results

On BrushBench (the paper's proposed segmentation-based benchmark), BrushNet achieves the best image quality scores across both inside-inpainting (IR 12.36, HPS 27.40, AS 6.53) and outside-inpainting (IR 10.82, HPS 28.02, AS 6.64) settings, while also attaining the lowest MSE (9.31×10⁻³ inside, 22.86×10⁻³ outside) and best LPIPS (15.08×10⁻³ outside) for masked region preservation. On EditBench (random brush masks), BrushNet leads in IR (4.40 vs. next-best 1.86), HPS (25.10 vs. 24.50), and CLIP similarity (28.67 vs. 28.62). With the optional blending operation (Ours*), masked region preservation improves dramatically (e.g., PSNR jumps from 21.65 to 31.94 on inside-inpainting, LPIPS drops from 48.28 to 18.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrushNet outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across both benchmarks. Against Blended Latent Diffusion (BLD), the simplest baseline, BrushNet gains substantial margins in image quality (IR 12.36 vs. 9.78 inside; 10.82 vs. 7.81 outside) and text alignment, reflecting BLD's inability to perceive mask boundaries. Against the dedicated inpainting models (SDI, HD-Painter, PowerPaint), BrushNet leads in image quality (IR, HPS, AS) on both inside and outside tasks, while SDI remains competitive on masked region preservation (PSNR 21.52 vs. 21.65 inside). Notably, HD-Painter and PowerPaint degrade significantly on outside-inpainting (IR drops to 9.66 and 7.45 respectively) due to their exclusive focus on inside-inpainting training. ControlNet-Inpainting (CNI) performs worst on masked region preservation (PSNR 12.39 inside, 11.91 outside; LPIPS 243.62 inside), and even with BLD blending (CNI*) it still trails BrushNet on image quality and text alignment. On EditBench with random masks, BrushNet's IR advantage is particularly large (4.40 vs. 1.86 for SDI), confirming generalizability beyond segmentation masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrushNet builds directly on ControlNet's dual-branch architecture with zero-convolution connections, but departs from it in three key ways: it replaces ControlNet's randomly initialized lightweight encoder with a VAE encoder to align masked-image features with the pre-trained UNet's latent distribution, it injects full UNet features layer-by-layer (hierarchical dense control) rather than ControlNet's sparse residual additions, and it removes text cross-attention from the additional branch to preserve pure image information. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against two existing paradigms—sampling-strategy modification (BLD, RePaint) and dedicated single-branch inpainting models (SDI, HD-Painter, PowerPaint)—arguing that the single-branch design conflates condition processing with generation and allows text embeddings to corrupt masked-image features at early UNet layers. The blurred pixel-space blending operation further distinguishes it from latent-space blending used in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (frozen base model + trainable BrushNet branch) is practically significant because it allows the community to attach inpainting capability to any fine-tuned Stable Diffusion variant without retraining the base model, lowering the barrier to adoption. The introduction of BrushBench and BrushData fills a gap in segmentation-based inpainting evaluation that EditBench's random masks do not cover. However, the paper acknowledges that generation quality remains heavily dependent on the chosen base model, and performance degrades with irregularly shaped masks or text-image misalignment—open challenges for future work. The flexible preservation scale (weight w) and blurring parameters offer practical controllability but add a hyperparameter tuning burden for end users.
