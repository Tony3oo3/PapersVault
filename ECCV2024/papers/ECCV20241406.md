## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-motion synthesis methods lack meaningful control over the target sequence length: GPT-based approaches cannot constrain output duration, and diffusion-based methods like MLD produce motions of a given length without adapting dynamics or style to that length. The paper argues that a short kick is not a fast-forwarded long kick—it requires faster dynamics and different motion style—yet no prior method treats target length as a stylizing attribute that should reshape the generated motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-text prompt (encoded via CLIP-ViT-L-14 into a fixed-length vector) and an integer target frame count f* as joint inputs. It outputs a 3D human motion sequence X ∈ R^{F×V} in SMPL parameterization (pose, velocity, rotation; 22 joints on HumanML3D, 21 on KIT-ML) with exactly F = f* frames. Unusually, the target length f* directly determines the dimensionality of the latent code used during generation: the model activates k = ⌈f*/r⌉ subspaces of D dimensions each, so shorter sequences are decoded from lower-dimensional latents, reducing both representational capacity and inference-time attention cost.

## Method

LADiff decomposes the VAE latent space into K subspaces of dimension D and progressively activates them via the rate k = ⌈f/r⌉, so the shortest motions live on a 1-D line while longer motions occupy higher-dimensional subspaces, each subspace specializing on r contiguous frames. A length-adaptive latent DDPM then denoises a noise vector z_T ∈ R^{k×D} (dimensionality set by the target length) using self-attention over the latent and cross-attention with the CLIP text embedding, with masked attention columns for inactive subspaces. To bridge the distributional gap between the clean-input VAE decoder and the residual-noise output of the DDPM, the authors train a Denoising VAE that reconstructs sequences perturbed with controlled Gaussian noise on a random subset of frames. Training proceeds in two stages: first the encoder-decoder VAE is trained with the standard ELBO objective (batch 64, 3k epochs), then the encoder and decoder are frozen while the 9M-parameter denoiser is trained with the conditional diffusion loss (batch 128, 1000 diffusion steps at training, 20 at inference).

## Results

On HumanML3D, LADiff (r=48) achieves the best R-Precision among non-RAG methods: Top-1 0.503, Top-2 0.696, Top-3 0.792, surpassing the previous SOTA AttT2M (0.499/0.690/0.786). Its FID is 0.182, slightly behind AttT2M (0.112) and T2M-GPT (0.116), while the r=32 variant reaches FID 0.110. On KIT-ML, LADiff (r=48) leads in R-Precision Top-1 (0.429) and Top-3 (0.773) over ReMoDiffuse (0.427/0.765). With retrieval augmentation, LADiff RAG achieves FID 0.054 on HumanML3D, a 47% improvement over ReMoDiffuse (0.103), while also improving Diversity (9.517 vs 9.081) and MModality (2.453 vs 1.795).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LADiff (r=48) outperforms all non-RAG baselines on HumanML3D R-Precision (Top-1/2/3: 0.503/0.696/0.792 vs. AttT2M's 0.499/0.690/0.786), but trails AttT2M and T2M-GPT on FID (0.182 vs. 0.112 and 0.116 respectively); the r=32 variant closes this gap with FID 0.110. On KIT-ML, LADiff (r=48) edges out ReMoDiffuse on R-Precision Top-1 (0.429 vs. 0.427) and Top-3 (0.773 vs. 0.765) while ReMoDiffuse retains a lower FID (0.155 vs. 0.470). In the RAG setting, LADiff RAG dramatically improves FID over ReMoDiffuse (0.054 vs. 0.103, a 47% reduction) and also surpasses it on Diversity (9.517 vs. 9.081) and MModality (2.453 vs. 1.795), though it sacrifices some R-Precision. Against DDPM-based baselines (MotionDiffuse, MDM) and GPT-based baselines (T2M-GPT, MotionGPT), LADiff consistently leads on R-Precision and is competitive or superior on FID, confirming that the length-aware latent structure provides a meaningful advantage over both autoregressive and fixed-length diffusion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

LADiff builds directly on MLD's two-stage VAE + latent diffusion pipeline for text-to-motion generation, but reorganizes the latent space into progressively activated subspaces whose dimensionality scales with target sequence length. It also incorporates the Denoising VAE (DVAE) framework to bridge the distributional gap between clean VAE reconstructions and noisy DDPM outputs, a problem not addressed in prior latent-diffusion motion work. The key conceptual departure from all prior methods (MLD, MotionDiffuse, T2M-GPT, etc.) is treating target length not merely as a sampling constraint but as a first-class stylizing attribute that should alter motion dynamics and style, rather than simply subsampling or stretching a fixed-length generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Length control is a practical necessity for interactive applications (gaming, robotics, VR) where the user specifies how long an action should take, and this paper demonstrates that respecting that constraint requires fundamentally different dynamics (faster, more compressed) rather than temporal resampling. The DVAE bridging technique and the progressive subspace activation design are modular ideas that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks with variable-length outputs. The main limitation acknowledged is the assumed linear relationship between sequence length and the number of activated subspaces, and whether this scaling law holds for very long or very short sequences remains open. Broader implications include whether length-awareness can be extended to other motion attributes (intensity, complexity) in a unified controllable framework.
