## Problem

Existing DETR-based multi-view 3D detectors rely on pixel-wise depth supervision from projected LiDAR points, but this depth is distributed on object surfaces rather than at object centers, making it poorly aligned with center-based object queries. Additionally, fine-grained depth estimation across an entire object is significantly harder for distant objects than predicting a single center depth. No prior method effectively injects object-wise (center) depth into the detection pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240680/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 surrounding camera images (at 256×704, 512×1408, or 640×1600 resolution) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and uses projected LiDAR points as pixel-wise depth supervision during training. It operates in a streaming video setting, leveraging temporal information from previous frames. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with class labels, velocity, and attributes, evaluated via nuScenes metrics (NDS, mAP, mATE, mASE, mAOE, mAVE, mAAE) over 10 foreground classes.

## Method

OPEN introduces an object-wise position embedding (OPE) that encodes predicted object-center depth into the transformer decoder to produce 3D object-aware features. The pipeline has three stages: (1) a Pixel-wise Depth Encoder (PDE) using DepthNet with deformable convolutions predicts a pixel-wise depth map supervised by projected LiDAR points; (2) an Object-wise Depth Encoder (ODE) performs attention-based temporal depth aggregation—converting pixel coordinates to camera-space reference points via the predicted depth, sampling features at 3D offsets across current and previous frames, and feeding the resulting depth embedding through an FFN to predict object-wise depth and 2D object center, supervised by projected 3D GT box centers; (3) the OPE back-projects the predicted (x, y, d) to a 3D LiDAR-coordinate center, applies cosine 3D position embedding, and adds the result to image features for interaction with learnable object queries. A depth-aware focal loss (DFL) further encourages attention to the 3D center by using an exponential L2-based depth score as a soft classification label.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set with a V2-99 backbone (640×1600 input), OPEN achieves a new state-of-the-art 64.4% NDS and 56.7% mAP, outperforming the previous best (Sparse4Dv2 at 63.8% NDS / 55.6% mAP) by 0.6% NDS and 1.1% mAP. On the nuScenes val set with ResNet101 (512×1408), OPEN reaches 60.6% NDS and 51.6% mAP, surpassing Far3D (59.4% NDS / 51.0% mAP) by 1.2% NDS. With the lighter ResNet50 backbone (256×704), OPEN still leads with 56.4% NDS and 46.5% mAP, exceeding SparseBEV by 0.6% NDS and 0.7% mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OPEN consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines across both nuScenes val and test sets. On the val set (ResNet50, 256×704), OPEN (56.4% NDS / 46.5% mAP) surpasses the closest competitor SparseBEV (55.8 / 44.8) by 0.6% NDS and 0.7% mAP, and its direct baseline StreamPETR (55.0 / 45.0) by 1.4% NDS and 1.5% mAP. At the higher resolution (ResNet101, 512×1408), OPEN (60.6 / 51.6) leads Far3D (59.4 / 51.0) by 1.2% NDS and 0.6% mAP, and Sparse4Dv2 (59.4 / 50.5) by 1.2% NDS and 1.1% mAP. On the test set (V2-99, 640×1600), OPEN (64.4 / 56.7) beats Sparse4Dv2 (63.8 / 55.6) by 0.6% NDS and 1.1% mAP, and SparseBEV (63.6 / 55.6) by 0.8% NDS and 1.1% mAP. The gains are particularly pronounced for distant objects: at a 40 m threshold, OPE improves NDS by 2.3% over ray-aware PE and by 1.2% over point-aware PE, confirming that object-wise depth is most beneficial where pixel-wise depth is least reliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

OPEN is built directly on the StreamPETR architecture (transformer-based multi-view 3D detection with temporal modeling) and adopts the DepthNet from 3DPPE for pixel-wise depth prediction. Its key conceptual departure is shifting from pixel-wise depth (distributed on object surfaces via projected LiDAR) to object-wise depth (the 3D center of objects), motivated by the observation that DETR-based detectors use center-based object queries. The proposed object-wise position embedding (OPE) replaces both ray-aware (StreamPETR) and point-aware (3DPPE) position embeddings, and a depth-aware focal loss further aligns classification with 3D center accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete misalignment between how depth supervision is provided (surface-level from LiDAR) and how DETR-based detectors consume geometric information (center-based queries), and shows that resolving this gap yields consistent gains, especially for distant objects where fine-grained surface depth is hardest to estimate. The distance-stratified ablation (Table 6) suggesting OPE's advantage grows with range is practically relevant for autonomous driving. However, the method still depends on LiDAR for training supervision and is validated only on nuScenes, leaving open whether object-wise depth can be learned without LiDAR or whether the approach transfers to other sensor configurations or datasets.
