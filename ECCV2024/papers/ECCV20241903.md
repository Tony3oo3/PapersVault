## Problem

Sequential fine-tuning of pre-trained models (PTMs) for continual learning causes catastrophic forgetting, as the backbone representation deteriorates and previously learned tasks degrade. While weight-averaging techniques have shown promise for robust fine-tuning and model merging, they had not been adapted to the sequential, memory-constrained setting of class-incremental learning. This paper bridges that gap by casting PTM-based CL as a robust fine-tuning problem and showing that iterative weight-ensembling between successive task models effectively resolves the stability-plasticity trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for CUB-200, Cars-196, and ImageNet-R) and outputs a class label from the cumulative set of all classes observed across tasks. Evaluation is performed in the class-incremental setting where the task identity is unknown at inference time, and the model must classify over all previously seen classes simultaneously. The approach is memory-free: it requires no replay buffer or storage of past task data, only the previous task's averaged weights and its diagonal Fisher information matrix.

## Method

CoFiMA performs full fine-tuning on each new task to ensure plasticity, then computes a Fisher-weighted average between the current task's weights and the previous task's averaged weights to preserve stability. Specifically, each parameter is weighted by its diagonal Fisher information value (estimated via a single forward-backward pass on the current task data), so parameters critical to the previous task receive stronger retention while less important parameters are allowed to shift toward the new task. A constant mixing coefficient λ (0.4 for supervised, 0.2 for self-supervised PTMs) biases the average toward the most recent task, reflecting that later models have already encoded knowledge from all prior tasks through sequential fine-tuning. The resulting averaged model serves as the initialization for the next task, and only the previous task's Fisher values need to be stored between tasks, keeping memory overhead to a single model's size.

## Results

Using a ViT-B/16 backbone supervisedly pre-trained on ImageNet-21K, CoFiMA achieves state-of-the-art class-incremental learning performance across all four benchmarks: 91.87% Inc-Acc on CUB-200, 82.65% Inc-Acc on Cars-196, 94.89% Inc-Acc on CIFAR-100, and 81.48% Inc-Acc on ImageNet-R. On CIFAR-100, CoFiMA's Last-Acc of 92.77% is within only 0.45% of the joint-training upper bound (93.22%). The method is memory-free, requiring storage of only a single model and one Fisher vector between tasks, while consistently outperforming the strongest baselines SLCA and RanPAC by 1–9% in Last-Acc depending on the dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoFiMA outperforms the strongest PTM-based CL baselines SLCA and RanPAC across all four datasets and both metrics. The largest gains are on Cars-196, where CoFiMA improves Last-Acc by +9.23% over SLCA (76.96% vs 67.73%) and +23.12% over RanPAC (76.96% vs 53.84%), and Inc-Acc by +5.72% over SLCA. On CUB-200, CoFiMA leads SLCA by +2.4% Last-Acc and +0.93% Inc-Acc, and on CIFAR-100 it surpasses SLCA by +1.24% Last-Acc and +4.02% Inc-Acc. Classical CL methods (EWC, LwF, DER++, BiC, GDumb) and prompt-based approaches (L2P, DualPrompt) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10–20% on most datasets. CoFiMA also outperforms the simple sequential fine-tuning baseline (Seq FT) by 19.04% Last-Acc on Cars-196 and 4.03% on CUB-200, confirming that weight averaging is critical for preventing forgetting. The method remains within 0.45% of the joint-training upper bound on CIFAR-100 Last-Acc, demonstrating near-optimal knowledge retention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct conceptual bridge between robust fine-tuning (WiSE-FT, Model Soups) and continual learning, extending weight-averaging from single-task or parallel multi-model settings to sequential CL. It departs from the dominant PTM-based CL paradigm of prompts (L2P, DualPrompt), replay buffers (DER++, BiC), or constrained fine-tuning (SLCA, RanPAC) by instead performing full fine-tuning followed by Fisher-weighted averaging with the previous task's checkpoint. The Fisher-weighted averaging mechanism is adapted from Matena et al.'s model merging, but repurposed: rather than merging independently trained models, it selectively weights parameters in a sequential CL recursion, and unlike EWC it uses Fisher information as an averaging coefficient rather than a regularization term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoFiMA offers a memory-free, prompt-free, replay-free CL method that is conceptually simple (one extra forward-backward pass for Fisher estimation per task) yet achieves SOTA on four standard CIL benchmarks, narrowing the gap to joint training to under 1% on CIFAR-100. The practical appeal is high: no architectural modifications, no additional inference cost, and compatibility with any PTM backbone. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer task sequences (beyond 10), whether it transfers to domain-incremental or task-incremental settings, and whether the Fisher diagonal approximation remains sufficient for very large models where parameter importance may be more heterogeneous.
