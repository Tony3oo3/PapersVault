## Problem

Camera-based gait recognition fails in low-light and long-distance scenarios where LiDAR excels, yet LiDAR's high deployment cost limits its standalone use. No prior work has attempted to match gait features across these two fundamentally different modalities—2D camera silhouettes and 3D LiDAR point clouds—making cross-modality gait recognition an entirely unexplored task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240913/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes camera silhouette sequences (binary masks) and LiDAR point cloud sequences as input. Point clouds are projected and interpolated into depth images at 64×64 resolution to match silhouette dimensions, ensuring data-processing consistency across streams. The output is a sequence-level gait feature embedding in a shared metric space, where cross-modality identity matching is performed via Euclidean distance between probe and gallery embeddings from different modalities.

## Method

CL-Gait employs a two-stream modified ResNet-18 backbone with modality-specific first convolution layers (Conv-S for silhouettes, Conv-P for projected depth images) followed by shared deeper layers, and uses horizontal pooling to produce local feature vectors aligned across modalities. To bridge the 2D–3D modality gap, the paper introduces Contrastive Silhouette-Point Pre-training (CSPP), a CLIP-style symmetric cross-entropy loss on paired single-frame embeddings that aligns feature spaces without requiring identity labels. Because real paired camera-LiDAR data is scarce, a large-scale pseudo-data generation pipeline uses Depth Anything monocular depth estimation on RGB images, back-projects depth to pseudo point clouds via virtual camera intrinsics, and voxel-downsamples them—enabling pre-training on 770K HITSZ-VCM and 50K LIP images. Fine-tuning uses a cross-modality triplet loss (alternating anchor modality) combined with cross-entropy loss, and the pre-trained backbone initializes the two-stream network.

## Results

On SUSTech1K, CL-Gait achieves an average rank-1 accuracy of 54.21% (53.29% LiDAR→Camera, 55.12% Camera→LiDAR), representing at least a 22.90% improvement over the best baseline structures. The two-stream architecture with horizontal pooling (HP) and contrastive silhouette-point pre-training (CSPP) is critical: adding HP to the base two-stream ResNet improves average rank-1 by ~11%, and CSPP adds another ~10%. The best image-point baseline (Point-Transformer) reaches only 13.25% L→C and 15.42% C→L rank-1, far below CL-Gait.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-Gait decisively outperforms all three image-point baselines that use dedicated point cloud encoders: GCN [9] (12.20% L→C, 14.30% C→L rank-1), PointNet [26] (7.11%, 8.40%), and Point-Transformer [43] (13.25%, 15.42%). The gap is largest for PointNet, where CL-Gait exceeds it by over 46 points in L→C rank-1. Even the simplest two-stream ResNet without any pre-training (30.39% L→C, 35.26% C→L) already surpasses all image-point methods, indicating that projecting point clouds into depth images for data consistency is more effective than encoding raw point clouds. Among the structural baselines, the one-stream (2.60%/14.94%) and asymmetric FC (3.08%/16.43%) configurations perform poorly, confirming that modality-specific adaptation must occur in shallow layers rather than at the final FC layer. The progressive gains from adding HP (+~11% avg rank-1) and CSPP (+~10% avg rank-1) on top of the two-stream backbone demonstrate that both spatial feature alignment and contrastive pre-training are necessary to close the 2D–3D modality gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out an entirely new task—cross-modality gait recognition between camera silhouettes and LiDAR point clouds—by combining two previously separate lines of work: camera-based gait recognition (GaitSet, OpenGait) and the nascent LiDAR-based gait recognition (LidarGait). Architecturally, it adapts the two-stream cross-modality design from RGB-IR person ReID [36] and the contrastive pre-training paradigm from CLIP [27], but repurposes both for the gait domain where the modality gap is between 2D contours and 3D geometry rather than visible and thermal appearance. A key methodological departure is the use of monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything) to synthesize pseudo point clouds from single RGB images, enabling large-scale contrastive pre-training without requiring physically paired camera-LiDAR captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first cross-modality gait recognition framework, this work opens a practically important direction: cameras handle normal-lighting scenes cheaply while LiDAR covers low-light and long-distance scenarios, so a unified cross-modality system broadens deployment scope significantly. The 54.21% average rank-1 accuracy, while still modest, demonstrates feasibility and provides a baseline for future work. Key open questions include whether the modality gap can be further closed (e.g., via denser point clouds or dedicated cross-modality encoders), whether the synthetic-data pre-training strategy generalizes beyond the SUSTech1K evaluation, and how performance scales with more diverse sensor configurations. The finding that projected depth images consistently outperform dedicated point cloud encoders (GCN, PointNet, Point-Transformer) suggests the field may need to rethink how 3D gait data is represented for cross-modality matching.
