## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for image super-resolution rely on a single teacher model and simple spatial-domain loss functions (e.g., L1), which fail to exploit the complementary strengths of multiple well-performing ISR architectures and do not explicitly account for the frequency-domain nature of super-resolution—recovering lost high-frequency spatial detail. This paper addresses both limitations by introducing a multi-teacher framework with a frequency-aware distillation objective.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a low-resolution RGB image (H×W×C) and produces a high-resolution reconstruction at scale factors ×2, ×3, or ×4. During training, the LR image is first processed by N pre-trained teacher models (EDSR, RCAN, SwinIR) whose HR outputs are concatenated along a new channel dimension and fed into the Knowledge Aggregation network; the student model then maps the original LR image directly to an HR image. Training uses 64×64 LR patches from DIV2K with random rotation and horizontal flip augmentation.

## Method

MTKD operates in two stages. In Stage 1 (Knowledge Aggregation), a DCTSwin-based network—where each Swin Transformer layer is modified to insert DCT before and IDCT after the shifted-window multi-head self-attention—fuses the concatenated outputs of N teacher models into a single enhanced HR representation, trained with L1 loss against ground truth. In Stage 2 (Model Distillation), the student network is trained with a combined objective: a weighted L1 loss (α=0.1) against the ground-truth HR image plus a discrete wavelet transform (DWT) distillation loss that computes L1 differences across all four subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH) between the student output and the aggregated teacher representation. The wavelet loss explicitly encourages the student to learn high-frequency structure from the multi-teacher signal, while the DCTSwin aggregation network allows heterogeneous teacher architectures to contribute complementary information without requiring the student to mirror any single teacher's design.

## Results

MTKD consistently achieves the best PSNR/SSIM across all three student models (RCAN_lightweight, EDSR_baseline, SwinIR_lightweight), four test sets (Set5, Set14, BSD100, Urban100), and three scale factors (×2, ×3, ×4). The headline gain is up to 0.46 dB PSNR over the strongest baseline (DUKD) on RCAN_lightweight ×4 Urban100 (27.08 vs 26.62). Notably, MTKD-distilled RCAN_lightweight outperforms the full RCAN teacher by 0.26 dB on Urban100 ×4 while using only one-third of the parameters. The multi-teacher aggregated output (MT) also surpasses every individual full teacher model across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTKD outperforms all five baselines (basic KD, AT, FAKD, DUKD, CrossKD) on every student model, dataset, and scale factor tested. The largest margin is 0.46 dB PSNR on RCAN_lightweight ×4 Urban100, where MTKD (27.08) beats DUKD (26.62), the strongest single-teacher baseline. DUKD is consistently the second-best method for RCAN, while FAKD edges out the others for SwinIR and EDSR students, yet both trail MTKD by 0.1–0.46 dB depending on the setting. MTKD's advantage grows when the teacher–student capacity gap is larger, consistent with prior observations in the KD literature. In the most striking case, MTKD-distilled RCAN_lightweight (5.2 M params) surpasses the full RCAN teacher (15.6 M params) by 0.26 dB on Urban100 ×4, demonstrating that multi-teacher aggregation plus wavelet-based distillation can extract more knowledge than the original teacher alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-teacher knowledge distillation paradigm for image super-resolution (established by SRKD, FAKD, DUKD, and CrossKD) to a multi-teacher setting, drawing on multi-teacher selection strategies previously explored only in NLP and multi-task learning. The DCTSwin aggregation network repurposes the Swin Transformer's shifted-window attention by inserting DCT/IDCT modules, a design inspired by recent token-mixing models that replace self-attention with spectral transforms. The wavelet-based distillation loss adapts a technique originally proposed for image-to-image translation [76] to the ISR domain, where frequency-domain supervision is particularly relevant for recovering high-frequency detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MTKD provides a practical recipe for combining heterogeneous teacher architectures (CNN and Transformer) into a single distillation signal, which is useful for practitioners who have access to multiple pre-trained ISR models but need a compact deployment model. The wavelet-based loss offers a principled alternative to spatial L1 losses that better targets the high-frequency reconstruction objective central to ISR. However, the evaluation is limited to three architectures, four standard test sets, and scale factors 2–4; the authors themselves note that extension to diffusion-based SR models and other low-level tasks remains open. The modest but consistent gains (≤0.46 dB) suggest the approach is reliable but unlikely to replace single-teacher KD in settings where only one strong teacher is available.
