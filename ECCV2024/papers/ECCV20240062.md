## Problem

Real-world AI products require a suite of models at different sizes to accommodate varying compute, storage, and power constraints, yet existing self-supervised pre-training methods train only a single model size per session. Adapting a large pre-trained model to smaller sizes typically demands additional pruning, distillation, or re-training from scratch, incurring substantial development effort. POA asks whether a single self-supervised pre-training session can simultaneously produce multiple well-trained models of diverse sizes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images from ImageNet-1K, generating two global augmented views and v lower-resolution local crops per image at each training step. The output is a single pre-trained teacher backbone (e.g., ViT-L/16, Swin-B/W7, or ResNet-152) from which a family of sub-networks—up to 143 ViT variants, 39 Swin variants, or 465 ResNet variants—can be directly extracted by selecting elastic width and depth configurations, all ready for downstream tasks without any additional pre-training.

## Method

POA introduces an elastic student branch into a three-branch self-distillation framework (teacher, intact student, elastic student), where the elastic student is a randomly sampled sub-network of the intact student with fully shared parameters. Elastic operators reduce width by truncating attention heads (ViT) or channels (CNN) with a scaling factor, and reduce depth by selecting blocks at equal intervals, yielding a compact set of (N+1)×(M+1) sub-networks. Training employs dual distillation: cross-view distillation from the teacher to both students, plus same-view distillation from the intact student to the elastic student, which the ablations show is critical for sub-network quality. Multiple projection heads with varying prototype counts ensure each randomly sampled sub-network receives adequate optimization across different semantic spaces. The teacher is updated via EMA over both the intact and elastic students, effectively ensembling sub-networks to improve representation quality and stabilize training.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, POA achieves SOTA k-NN accuracy of 82.3% and linear probing (LP) accuracy of 83.6% with ViT-L/16, outperforming DINOv2 (82.0% k-NN, 83.3% LP) at the same 1200 effective epochs. Critically, the ViT-S/16 and ViT-B/16 sub-networks are extracted directly from the pre-trained ViT-L/16 teacher with zero additional training epochs, yet still achieve SOTA k-NN of 76.8% and 80.9% respectively, surpassing iBOT (75.2%, 77.1%) and DINO (74.5%, 76.1%) trained for 3200 and 1600 epochs. On Swin Transformer backbones, POA reaches 79.6% k-NN and 82.0% LP for Swin-B/W7, and on ResNet-152 achieves 76.4% k-NN and 79.9% LP, all setting new benchmarks across model sizes from a single pre-training session.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POA outperforms all compared baselines across ViT, Swin, and ResNet backbones. Against DINOv2 (the strongest prior method), POA gains +0.3% k-NN and +0.3% LP on ViT-L/16 at equal 1200 epochs, and +2.7% k-NN and +1.8% LP on ViT-B/16 (80.9% vs. 78.2% for DINOv2's ViT-B). Against iBOT, POA achieves +1.6% k-NN on ViT-S/16 (76.8% vs. 75.2%) and +3.8% k-NN on ViT-B/16 (80.9% vs. 77.1%), despite requiring zero additional training epochs for the smaller models versus iBOT's 3200/1600 epochs. Against DINO, the margin is even larger: +2.3% k-NN on ViT-S/16 and +4.8% k-NN on ViT-B/16. In the knowledge distillation comparison (Table 4), POA's ViT-S/16 (76.8% k-NN) surpasses DINOv2+SEED (74.0% k-NN) by 2.8% with half the total training epochs, and POA's ViT-B/16 (80.9%) beats DINOv2+SEED (78.8%) by 2.1%. The only near-tie is ResNet-50 LP, where POA (76.9%) is competitive with ReLICv2 (77.1%) trained for 4000 epochs versus POA's 2400.

## Relation to Prior Work

POA extends the self-distillation paradigm established by DINO, DINOv2, and iBOT by introducing an elastic student branch that samples sub-networks from the intact student at each training step. The elastic design draws conceptual inspiration from weight-sharing strategies in neural architecture search (Once-for-All, AutoFormer, MaskTAS), but departs fundamentally from NAS: rather than searching a vast architecture space and then re-training, POA defines a compact set of sub-networks and trains them all simultaneously within the SSL loop. The same-view distillation between the intact and elastic students is a novel component not present in prior self-distillation or knowledge distillation methods like SEED, which require a separately pre-trained teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: real-world AI products (e.g., Gemini Nano/Pro/Ultra) require suites of models at different scales, and POA eliminates the need for separate pre-training runs per size, producing ~100 candidate models from a single session. The finding that the elastic student stabilizes training (preventing NaN divergence in ResNet) and acts as an implicit ensemble of sub-networks suggests a broader principle that multi-scale parameter sharing can regularize SSL. Open questions include whether this approach extends to multimodal LLMs (noted as future work), whether the sub-network constraint (must be a strict sub-network of the largest model) limits architectural flexibility, and how well the extracted models perform under aggressive fine-tuning regimes beyond linear probing.
