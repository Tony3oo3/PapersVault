## Problem

Camouflaged object detection (COD) methods depend on large-scale pixel-level annotations that are extremely costly to produce due to the subtle boundaries and low contrast of camouflaged objects. While semi-supervised learning offers a natural remedy, its application to COD is hindered by substantial pseudo-label noise at both the pixel level (inconsistent labeling within a single mask) and the instance level (varying quality across different masks), and no prior end-to-end semi-supervised COD framework addresses this dual noise problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images resized to 352×352 pixels and outputs a binary foreground-background segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution. In the semi-supervised setting, only a small fraction (1%–30%) of the training set carries pixel-level ground-truth masks; the remaining unlabeled images are processed through a teacher model that generates soft pseudo-labels (probability maps in [0,1]) used to supervise the student. Each unlabeled image is additionally fed through two randomly rotated views (after flip and random resize) to produce the consistency signals that drive the weighting mechanism.

## Method

CamoTeacher builds on the Mean Teacher architecture (teacher generates pseudo-labels, student is trained on labeled + pseudo-labeled data, teacher updated via EMA) and introduces Dual-Rotation Consistency Learning (DRCL) to dynamically reweight pseudo-label supervision. For each unlabeled image, two independent random rotations produce two views; the teacher predicts on both, and predictions are rotated back to a common horizontal orientation. Pixel-wise Consistency Learning (PCL) computes the absolute difference between the two horizontal predictions (pixel-wise inconsistency) and multiplies it by the squared L2 distance of the averaged prediction from 0.5, yielding a per-pixel weight that suppresses uncertain and inconsistent regions in a weighted BCE loss. Instance-wise Consistency Learning (ICL) computes the SSIM between the two horizontal predictions to obtain a per-image weight that downweights globally noisy pseudo-labels in a weighted IoU loss. The total training objective combines the standard supervised BCE loss on labeled data with the PCL-weighted BCE loss and ICL-weighted IoU loss on unlabeled data, and the framework is demonstrated to be model-agnostic across CNN-based (SINet, SINet-v2, SegMaR) and Transformer-based (DTINet, FSPNet) COD backbones.

## Results

On COD10K with 10% labeled data, CamoTeacher achieves S_α=0.759, F_β^w=0.594, and M=0.049, surpassing the fully supervised SINet baseline (S_α=0.675, F_β^w=0.336, M=0.095) by 12.4% in S_α and 76.8% in F_β^w. Under the extreme 1% labeled setting on NC4K, CamoTeacher reaches S_α=0.718 and F_β^w=0.599, a 28.6% gain in F_β^w over supervised SINet (0.291) and a 38.6% gain over Mean Teacher (0.213). With only 5% labeled data on COD10K, CamoTeacher already exceeds 100% of fully supervised performance on F_β^w, F_β^m, E_φ^m, and M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CamoTeacher decisively outperforms the supervised SINet baseline across all four datasets and all labeled-data ratios; on COD10K at 10% labeled data, F_β^w improves from 0.336 to 0.594 (+76.8%). Against Mean Teacher, the gains are even more pronounced at low label budgets: on NC4K at 1% labeled data, F_β^w jumps from 0.213 to 0.599 (+181%), confirming that DRCL's noise-aware weighting is critical when pseudo-labels are most unreliable. When applied to stronger backbones (Table 2), CamoTeacher consistently improves over each model's supervised counterpart: +2.8% S_α and +6.1% F_β^w on SINet-v2, +2.0% S_α and +7.9% F_β^w on SegMaR, +6.0% S_α and +9.1% F_β^w on DTINet, and +3.6% S_α and +10.0% F_β^w on FSPNet. In the 100%-labeled + additional-unlabeled setting (Table 3), CamoTeacher (S_α=0.787, M=0.048) surpasses all unsupervised methods (SelfMask, FOUND, UCOS-DA) and weakly-supervised SCOD and SCWSSOD, though WS-SAM reports a slightly higher S_α (0.803) and lower M (0.038); the paper notes WS-SAM requires scribble annotations on the unlabeled data, whereas CamoTeacher needs none. Overall, CamoTeacher wins or ties on every reported metric against all 12 baselines, with the sole exception being WS-SAM's S_α and M in the 100%-labeled setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mean Teacher framework (Tarvainen et al., NeurIPS 2017), adopting its teacher-student architecture, EMA parameter updates, and weak/strong augmentation pipeline as the training backbone. It departs from standard pseudo-labeling by introducing Dual-Rotation Consistency Learning (DRCL), which uses two randomly rotated views to estimate pseudo-label noise at both pixel and instance levels—a mechanism absent from prior semi-supervised segmentation methods. The COD task itself is well-studied (SINet, SegMaR, DTINet, FSPNet), but the paper positions semi-supervised COD as a previously unexplored formulation, filling a gap between fully-supervised COD and the weaker unsupervised/weakly-supervised alternatives (UCOS-DA, SCOD, WS-SAM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COD datasets require laborious pixel-level annotations due to subtle object boundaries, so a method that achieves near-fully-supervised performance with only 20% labeled data (and surpasses it with 5%) has direct practical value for reducing annotation costs. The dual-rotation consistency mechanism is a lightweight, model-agnostic plug-in that could potentially transfer to other segmentation tasks with noisy pseudo-labels, though the paper does not explore this. Open questions include whether the rotation-based noise estimation generalizes to 3D or video COD, whether the approach benefits from stronger base models beyond FSPNet, and how it scales to even lower label ratios (e.g., 0.1%).
