## Problem

Vision Transformers are significantly less resource-efficient than CNNs in memory, computation, and power consumption, yet existing pruning methods for ViTs either use unstructured sparsity (poor hardware acceleration) or channel-wise structured sparsity (severe accuracy loss), and none explicitly optimize for inference power consumption. Block-structured (semi-structured) pruning, which aligns with the block-wise GEMM execution on modern hardware, remains largely unexplored for ViTs, and no prior work formulates a hardware-aware pruning objective that directly targets power reduction.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained ViT weight matrices (qkv, feedforward, and projection linear layers) and produces block-sparse pruned weights followed by a short fine-tuning phase. At inference, the pruned model still takes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet classification, 384×384 for Cityscapes segmentation via SETR) and outputs class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks. The block-sparsity pattern (e.g., 32×32, 64×32) is chosen to match the GEMM tile size of the target hardware (RISC-V ViTA, TPU-V3, or A100 GPU), so the sparsity structure directly determines achievable speedup.

## Method

LPViT groups neurons in each linear layer into fixed 2D blocks and prunes entire blocks using a first-order Taylor score averaged per block. The key novelty is a hardware-aware pruning objective that jointly minimizes (i) the second-order Taylor-approximated output distortion and (ii) an analytical power-consumption term derived from the block-sparse GEMM model (proportional to the number of surviving blocks times per-block power cost), subject to a FLOPs reduction constraint. This objective is solved via a Lagrangian formulation: empirical δ-vs-sparsity curves are collected per layer using an incremental update trick (complexity O(½ΣDᵢ²) rather than O(NKDᵢ⁴)), and the per-layer pruning ratios are found by matching a target slope λᵢ that encodes both the accuracy penalty and the power-saving incentive. The power term requires no empirical look-up tables—only hardware parameters (per-block power cost, GEMM tile size)—and the i.i.d. weight-perturbation assumption eliminates cross-layer terms, reducing the problem to independent per-layer optimizations.

## Results

On ImageNet classification, LPViT achieves 80.69% top-1 accuracy on DeiT-Small at 50% FLOPs (2.3G), surpassing the dense model (79.8%) by 0.89 points. On DeiT-Base at 50% FLOPs (8.8G), it reaches 80.81% top-1, and when combined with attention and token pruning at 29% FLOPs (5.1G), it attains 81.03%. On hardware, LPViT achieves up to 3.93× speedup on the ViTA RISC-V platform and 1.79× on NVIDIA A-100 for DeiT-Base, with a 71.8% inference power reduction (167W → 120W) measured on A-100. On Cityscapes segmentation, the pruned DeiT-Base/384 backbone retains 77.39 mIoU (vs. 78.66 dense) at 50% FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DeiT-Small, LPViT (block-sparse only) decisively outperforms all baselines: it beats SCOP by 3.19 points (80.69 vs. 77.5) at comparable FLOPs, S²ViTE by 1.47 points at lower FLOPs (2.3G vs. 3.14G), and UVC by 1.87 points at similar FLOPs. On DeiT-Base, LPViT at 50% FLOPs (80.81%) edges out VTP (80.7% at 56.8% FLOPs) and UVC (80.57% at 45.5% FLOPs), though S²ViTE achieves a slightly higher 82.22% at a much higher FLOPs budget (66.9% vs. 50%). On Swin-Tiny, LPViT surpasses X-Pruner by 1.45 points (80.0 vs. 78.55) at similar FLOPs (3.47G vs. 3.19G), and when combined with attention pruning (B+A) it reaches 80.0% at only 60.5% FLOPs. The key differentiator is that LPViT uses pure block-structured weight pruning without token gating, attention modification, or skip-connection manipulation, yet matches or exceeds hybrid methods that combine multiple compression strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts block-structured (semi-structured) pruning—previously validated on NLP transformers like BERT and MobileBERT [30, 37]—to vision transformers, combining it with a second-order Taylor/Hessian-based importance criterion [38, 29] and a rate-distortion-style layer-wise sparsity allocation [48, 50]. It departs from prior ViT compression work (S²ViTE, UVC, VTP, X-Pruner) by focusing exclusively on weight-level block sparsity rather than token pruning, attention head removal, or channel-wise structured pruning, and introduces a hardware-aware power consumption term directly into the pruning objective. The I.i.d. weight perturbation assumption [58] enables the per-layer decomposition that makes the optimization tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first post-training pruning method for ViTs that explicitly optimizes inference power consumption alongside accuracy, eliminating the need for empirical look-up tables and offering a principled tradeoff knob (β) between speedup and energy. The practical hardware results (3.93× speedup on VITA, 1.4× power reduction on A100) make it relevant for edge and datacenter deployment of ViTs. Limitations include the requirement for finetuning after pruning, the dependence of speedup on hardware GEMM block-size alignment, and modest accuracy retention on larger models (DeiT-B loses ~1% at 50% FLOPs). Open questions include whether the power model generalizes across diverse accelerators and whether the approach extends to generative or multi-modal transformer architectures.
