## Problem

Existing single-view 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: 2D novel-view-synthesis approaches lack 3D geometric consistency, while explicit 3D representations (voxels, point clouds, meshes) suffer from coarse geometry and suboptimal rendering quality. No prior work has leveraged the emerging Gaussian Splatting representation—which simultaneously provides explicit geometry and photorealistic rendering—as the native space for a generative diffusion model with view-conditioned reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB image with an object mask at arbitrary resolution along with relative camera parameters in world space, and outputs a 3D object explicitly represented as 1024 Gaussian Splatting ellipsoids (each parameterized by 3D position, 6D covariance, 3D color, and 1D opacity). Unlike implicit representations requiring marching cubes for geometry extraction, the GS output is directly renderable in real-time from any viewpoint, and the fixed 1024-point count is enforced during training via constrained densification to enable feed-forward transformer processing.

## Method

GSD trains an unconditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) over 1024 GS ellipsoids (16-dim feature vectors) to learn category-specific 3D generative priors, using a vanilla transformer backbone that treats GS as a point cloud with implicit attention edges and no positional encoding. At inference, view-guided sampling replaces classifier conditioning: at each DDIM denoising step, the current GS estimate is rendered to the input view via differentiable splatting, and the gradient of an image-space loss (LPIPS or L1) is backpropagated through the splatting function to bias the score prediction, following the loss-guided diffusion / DPS framework. An auxiliary 2D diffusion model (UNet, 256×256) then polishes the rendered views, and these refined images are re-used as new conditioning targets in an alternating iterative loop (3 iterations for single-view) that progressively improves both 2D fidelity and 3D geometry. The transformer backbone outperforms PVCNN because GS attributes (especially covariance) encode spatial information beyond position, making the implicit edge structure of attention better suited than explicit k-NN connectivity.

## Results

On the CO3Dv2 single-view reconstruction benchmark (10 categories), GSD achieves 18.49 PSNR, 0.288 LPIPS, and 0.696 SSIM averaged across all categories, outperforming ViewFormer (16.87 PSNR, 0.321 LPIPS) and NerFormer (16.67 PSNR, 0.404 LPIPS) by a substantial margin. For 3D geometry accuracy on the hydrant category, GSD attains F-score@0.01 of 0.191 and Chamfer Distance of 0.068, surpassing PC² (0.185, 0.073). In the 2-view setting, GSD matches or slightly exceeds SparseFusion (e.g., 22.6 vs 22.3 PSNR on hydrant) while being significantly faster at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSD outperforms both NerFormer and ViewFormer across all 10 CO3D categories and all view-synthesis metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, SSIM), with the largest gains on structurally complex objects like teddy bear (+3.7 PSNR over NerFormer) and hydrant (+3.1 PSNR over NerFormer). Against SparseFusion in the 2-view setting, GSD is competitive, achieving comparable or slightly better PSNR on most categories (e.g., 22.6 vs 22.3 on hydrant, 23.0 vs 22.8 on apple) while avoiding the expensive SDS-based optimization that SparseFusion requires. For 3D geometry, GSD surpasses PC² on both F-score@0.01 (0.191 vs 0.185) and Chamfer Distance (0.068 vs 0.073) on hydrant, with the paper attributing PC²'s weaker performance to its tendency to reconstruct category-mean shapes rather than instance-specific geometry. The iterative polishing and re-using strategy contributes an additional ~0.4 PSNR and ~0.015 LPIPS improvement over the non-iterative variant, confirming the benefit of the auxiliary 2D diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSD sits at the intersection of three research threads: diffusion-based 3D generation (DDPM, DiT), the Gaussian Splatting representation for explicit 3D geometry and rendering, and loss-guided / posterior-sampling conditioning for diffusion models. It departs from prior diffusion-based 3D reconstruction methods (PC², LION, Shape-E) that condition on 2D features via image encoders or point-projection modules, instead exploiting the differentiable splatting function itself as a pixel-level gradient bridge between the input image and the GS ellipsoids. The iterative 2D-diffusion polishing step is a practical addition not seen in prior GS reconstruction work, addressing a domain gap between GS renderings and real photographs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first diffusion model trained directly on raw GS parameters, GSD demonstrates that GS is a viable generative 3D representation that simultaneously yields explicit geometry and photorealistic rendering without the geometry-extraction overhead of implicit methods. The splatting-based conditioning mechanism is a reusable design pattern for any future GS-based generative model. However, the current formulation requires GS ground truth (via constrained densification) and is category-specific with a fixed 1024-point budget, limiting immediate applicability to open-vocabulary or generic object reconstruction. Whether the approach scales to large, diverse object categories without per-category training remains the key open question the authors flag.
