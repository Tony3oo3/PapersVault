## Problem

Gloss-free sign language translation methods underperform gloss-based ones because the flexible, many-to-many correspondence between visual frames and text tokens fails to provide valid frame-wise supervision for the visual encoder. The paper attributes this gap to the absence of a monotonic, token-level constraint analogous to gloss annotations, and asks how to construct such a constraint directly from text sentences without any gloss labeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241123/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes skeleton sequences extracted from sign videos (processed by a CoSign-1s encoder producing T frame-level features) and the corresponding text sentence (U tokens encoded by a frozen mBART word-embedding layer). It produces a text sentence via an autoregressive mBART translation module, and as an intermediate artifact a pseudo-gloss sequence obtained by merging consecutive repeated tokens from the greedy visual-to-text alignment. The alignment matrix A^{v2s} ∈ R^{T×U} is one-hot per row, assigning each visual frame to its single most similar text token.

## Method

VAP constructs a gloss-like constraint by greedily aligning each visual feature to its most similar textual feature via cosine similarity in a joint space (projected by two linear layers), then optimizes a bidirectional contrastive loss L_Align (video-to-text and text-to-video) with a temperature-scaled softmax to pull matched pairs together while pushing apart non-matches. This is combined with a shallow translation loss L_SLT to enforce temporal consistency of the alignment. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) pre-train the visual encoder with L_Align + L_SLT, (2) fine-tune mBART on a pseudo-gloss-to-text (P2T) task using the generated pseudo glosses, and (3) end-to-end fine-tune the full model while retaining L_Align. A key design choice is using max pooling (rather than attention or average pooling) to aggregate token-wise similarities into the video-text score, and using a frozen word-embedding layer rather than a full transformer encoder for the text side, which the ablations show transfers richer semantic knowledge to the visual encoder.

## Results

On PHOENIX14T (Test), VAP achieves BLEU-4 of 26.16 and ROUGE of 51.28, surpassing the previous gloss-free SOTA GFSLT-VLP by +4.72 BLEU-4 and Sign2GPT by +3.64 BLEU-4. On CSL-Daily (Test), VAP★ achieves BLEU-4 of 23.65, improving over GFSLT-VLP by +12.65 BLEU-4. The performance gap to the gloss-based SOTA (2S-SLT★) narrows to 2.79 BLEU-4 on PHOENIX14T and 5.90 BLEU-4 on CSL-Daily. On the large-scale OpenASL dataset, VAP improves BLEU-4 by +10.71 over the baseline (21.23 vs. 10.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAP substantially outperforms all prior gloss-free methods: it improves BLEU-4 by +4.72 over GFSLT-VLP (26.16 vs. 21.44) and by +3.64 over the concurrent Sign2GPT (26.16 vs. 22.52) on PHOENIX14T Test, and by +12.65 over GFSLT-VLP (23.65 vs. 11.00) on CSL-Daily Test. Against the gloss-based SOTA 2S-SLT★, VAP★ narrows the BLEU-4 gap to 2.79 on PHOENIX14T (26.16 vs. 28.95) and 5.90 on CSL-Daily (23.65 vs. 29.55), approaching but not matching gloss-based performance. On How2Sign, VAP achieves BLEU-4 of 12.87, edging out YT-ASL+YT→H2S (12.39) by +0.48 without requiring extra pre-training data. On OpenASL, VAP improves BLEU-4 by +10.71 over the skeleton-based baseline (21.23 vs. 10.52) and by +14.51 over the OpenASL [40] baseline (21.23 vs. 6.72). The ablation in Table 3 further shows that simply re-initializing the translation module (without VAP) already yields +2.17 BLEU-4, confirming that the translation loss provides useful alignment supervision, but VAP's contrastive alignment loss adds another +1.58 BLEU-4 on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gloss-free SLT line initiated by GFSLT-VLP [59], which performed only sentence-level vision-language alignment, by introducing token-level greedy alignment that approximates the role of gloss annotations. It also builds on the transfer-learning strategy from MMTLB [8] of plugging in large-scale pre-trained translation modules (mBART), adding a Pseudo-gloss2Text fine-tuning stage to bridge the sign-to-spoken language gap. Conceptually, VAP draws from FILIP's fine-grained patch-word alignment and CLIP's contrastive framework, but repurposes them to construct a 'gloss-like constraint' from text sentences rather than requiring explicit gloss labels, directly addressing the flexible visual-textual correspondence problem identified as the bottleneck for gloss-free methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VAP demonstrates that a gloss-free pipeline can narrow the BLEU-4 gap with gloss-based SOTA to roughly 2–6 points across four benchmarks, including large-scale datasets (How2Sign, OpenASL) where gloss annotation is infeasible, making it a practical path toward scaling SLT to web-scale sign video. The generated pseudo-glosses serve as a useful intermediate representation, and the finding that a frozen mBART word-embedding layer outperforms a full transformer encoder as the text-side signal suggests that rich contextual information can actually hinder visual encoder learning in this setting. Open questions include whether the greedy alignment can be made monotonic or differentiable to close the remaining gap, and whether the approach generalizes to full-video (non-skeleton) inputs and other sign languages.
