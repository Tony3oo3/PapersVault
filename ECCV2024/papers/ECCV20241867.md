## Problem

Existing Driver Intention Prediction (DIP) methods and datasets only capture maneuvers from their onset to execution, providing no pre-maneuver context for early anticipation. This limits ADAS systems to reactive rather than preemptive responses, as the brief window between detecting a risky maneuver and an impending collision is often insufficient. No prior dataset or method addresses the task of anticipating a driver's action before the maneuver begins.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241867/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six synchronized video streams at 30 FPS (1080p, resized to 224×224): four out-cabin views (front, rear, left and right side mirrors), one in-cabin driver-facing view, and one ego-centric RGB view from Aria glasses, plus gaze tracking data (320×240) derived from Aria eye-tracking cameras. Videos span 5–35 seconds, sampled as T′×τ frame clips (16×4 or 32×3). The output is a single driving maneuver class label from seven categories (go straight, left/right turn, left/right lane change, slow/stop, U-turn).

## Method

M²MVT is a multi-view multi-modal vision transformer that applies early fusion across the five in-/out-cabin views (each projected via separate linear layers into a shared embedding space with a joint CLS token) and late fusion with the ego-view/gaze stream (separate CLS token, concatenated before classification). Twelve learnable episodic memory tokens (10 for multi-view, 2 for ego-view) are prepended to the input to retain prior temporal context, and the encoder uses spatio-temporal tube patchification with progressive multi-scale pooling and decomposed relative position embeddings inherited from MViTv2. The model is pre-trained on Kinetics-600 and fine-tuned with AdamW (lr 1e-4) over 80 epochs on 4 GPUs, using the context-aware loss from CEMFormer. This hybrid early/late fusion strategy, combined with the memory-augmented temporal modeling, outperforms CEMFormer by 4.85% accuracy with 61.22% fewer parameters.

## Results

On the proposed DAAD dataset with in- and out-cabin views, M²MVT achieves 61.74% accuracy and 63.82% F1, outperforming the strongest baseline CEMFormer (58.33% Acc, 61.68% F1) by 3.41% accuracy. With all views and gaze (Table 4), M²MVT reaches 65.59% accuracy and 66.72% F1, surpassing the ViT-based CEMFormer by 4.85% accuracy and 3.63% F1 while using 61.22% fewer parameters. On the standard Brain4Cars benchmark (in- and out-cabin), M²MVT achieves 83.18% accuracy and 84.11% F1, comparable to CEMFormer (83.37% Acc) and Rong et al. (81.87% Acc). The model also demonstrates early anticipation capability, achieving 37.66% accuracy at the onset of maneuver (t=0), which is 9.12% above ViT and 4.95% above MViTv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M²MVT consistently outperforms all three baselines on the DAAD dataset. Against Gebert et al. [14] (3D ResNet + LSTM), M²MVT gains +8.95% accuracy on DAAD (In+Out-Cabin: 61.74% vs. 52.79%), reflecting the advantage of transformer-based temporal modeling over recurrent architectures for longer videos. Against Rong et al. [37] (FlowNet + ConvLSTM), the improvement is +8.27% accuracy (61.74% vs. 53.47%), again highlighting the difficulty LSTM-based methods face with DAAD's longer sequences. Against CEMFormer [30] (ViT with learnable memory), M²MVT achieves +3.41% accuracy on DAAD (In+Out-Cabin) and +4.85% accuracy with all views and gaze (65.59% vs. 60.74%), while using 61.22% fewer parameters. On Brain4Cars (In+Out-Cabin), M²MVT is essentially tied with CEMFormer (83.18% vs. 83.37% accuracy) but surpasses both LSTM-based baselines by 1.3–6.0% accuracy, confirming that the performance gap widens as video length and scene complexity increase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established Driver Intention Prediction (DIP) paradigm, which predicts maneuvers only during their execution using short clips (<6 s), by introducing Anticipating Driving Maneuvers (ADM), a task that requires prediction before the maneuver onset. Architecturally, M2MVT inherits episodic memory tokens from CEMFormer [30] and Sandler et al. [38], and adopts MViTv2's [26] decomposed relative position embeddings and spatio-temporal pooling, but reorganizes them into a hybrid-fusion scheme that early-fuses five camera views and late-fuses the ego-view/gaze modality. The key conceptual departure is the DAAD dataset itself, which captures 'Before the Maneuver' (BM) sequences absent from all prior driving datasets (Brain4Cars, VIENA², HDD, AIDE), enabling the anticipation framing that prior DIP work could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing ADM as distinct from DIP and providing the first multi-view, multi-modal dataset with pre-maneuver context, this work opens a practically important direction for ADAS: alerting drivers before a hazardous maneuver begins rather than after it has started. However, the best reported accuracy (~65.6%) remains well below the levels achieved on shorter DIP benchmarks, and performance degrades sharply at night (56.5%) and in rain (59.2%), leaving open whether the task is solvable at the reliability levels required for production safety systems. The finding that out-cabin views outweigh in-cabin views for longer videos—reversing the Brain4Cars trend—suggests that as anticipation horizons grow, scene context becomes more discriminative than explicit driver head movements, a shift that future methods should account for.
