## Problem

Existing black-box model inversion attacks rely on iterative latent-space optimization, requiring tens of thousands of queries per identity (e.g., RLB-MI needs ~120k queries), which is impractical under real-world query limits. Training-based alternatives like LB-MI avoid this cost but use shallow decoders (3–4 convolutional layers) that cannot learn high-level mappings from prediction vectors to images, resulting in poor reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At attack time, the method consumes only the target model's output prediction vector (a C-dimensional confidence score vector, log-transformed to mitigate dominance effects) and the top-n public images classified as the target identity for ensemble. It produces a single 64×64 reconstructed face image via one forward pass through the PAE encoder and fixed StyleGAN generator, requiring only 0.13 million total queries for 300 identities—roughly 0.36% of RLB-MI's budget.

## Method

The P2I model consists of a Prediction Alignment Encoder (PAE) that maps the prediction vector into StyleGAN's disentangled W+ space (1×18×512) via a linear layer, deconvolution layers, a ResNet backbone, and 18 parallel linear layers, followed by a fixed StyleGAN generator to produce the image. Training is supervised on top-n public images per identity (no overlap with private data) using a composite loss of MSE, LPIPS, multi-layer ArcFace identity loss, face parsing loss, and an alignment regularization term that constrains the intermediate feature to the input image. At attack time, the Aligned Ensemble Attack computes a confidence-weighted average of W+ latent codes from multiple public images and enhances the target-dimension prediction scores by a hyper-parameter m, integrating complementary facial attributes to improve reconstruction fidelity over single-vector input.

## Results

On the standard CelebA setting (Table 1), P2I achieves the best black-box attack accuracy across all three target models: 0.715 (VGG16), 0.865 (ResNet-152), and 0.830 (Face.evoLVe), surpassing the strongest black-box baseline RLB-MI by 8.5%, 7.6%, and 4.7% respectively. In the distribution-shift setting (FFHQ→PubFig83), P2I reaches 0.82 attack accuracy, exceeding even the white-box KED-MI (0.63). Query cost is reduced by ~99%: P2I requires only 0.13 million queries to attack 300 identities, compared to 36 million for RLB-MI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among black-box baselines, P2I decisively outperforms RLB-MI (the strongest) by 8.5% relative attack accuracy on VGG16 (0.715 vs 0.659) and by 7.6% on ResNet-152 (0.865 vs 0.804), while also achieving substantially lower KNN and Feature distances. Against MIRROR, P2I gains 30+ percentage points in attack accuracy (e.g., 0.715 vs 0.413 on VGG16), and against the training-based LB-MI the gap is even larger (0.715 vs 0.075), confirming that shallow inversion networks fail to capture high-level facial semantics. In the label-only category, P2I surpasses BREP-MI (0.715 vs 0.581 on VGG16) and also outperforms LOKT when its query budget is constrained to match practical settings (0.715 vs 0.450 for LOKT*). Against white-box upper bounds, P2I narrows the gap considerably: it reaches 0.865 on ResNet-152 versus PLG-MI's 1.000, and in the distribution-shift setting (FFHQ→PubFig83) it even exceeds KED-MI (0.82 vs 0.63). The query cost advantage is dramatic: P2I uses 0.13M queries versus 36M for RLB-MI, 1.85M for MIRROR, and 17.98M for BREP-MI, making it the only method practical under real-world API query limits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-based model inversion paradigm introduced by LB-MI (Yang et al.) by replacing its shallow convolutional decoder with a Prediction Alignment Encoder that maps prediction vectors into StyleGAN's disentangled W+ space, followed by a fixed StyleGAN generator. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: GAN inversion (which encodes real images into W+ space) and black-box MI (which searches latent space iteratively), by training a direct prediction-to-W+ mapping that eliminates the need for iterative optimization. The method departs from optimization-based MI (RLB-MI, MIRROR, GMI) that require tens of thousands of queries per identity, and from the label-only setting (BREP-MI, LOKT) that only exploits hard labels, by leveraging full soft prediction vectors as a richer signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99% reduction in query count (0.13M vs. 36M for RLB-MI) makes black-box MI attacks feasible in realistic query-limited settings such as cloud vision APIs, shifting the threat model from theoretical to practical. The strong distribution-shift results (e.g., 82% attack accuracy on PubFig83 when trained on FFHQ) suggest the W+ alignment captures generalizable facial semantics rather than dataset-specific artifacts. However, the authors acknowledge they have not fully explored the latent space's potential, and the method has not been extended to the label-only scenario where only hard labels are available. Open questions include whether the prediction-W+ alignment generalizes beyond face recognition to other sensitive modalities (e.g., medical imaging, text) and whether effective defenses exist against this single-forward-pass attack paradigm.
