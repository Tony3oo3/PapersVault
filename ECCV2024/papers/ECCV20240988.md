## Problem

Existing fairness-aware debiasing algorithms are designed for CNNs and do not transfer effectively to Vision Transformers due to fundamental architectural differences in how features are extracted (self-attention over global patch relationships vs. local receptive fields). The paper establishes that CNN-based debiasing methods (MMD, MFD, DANN) fail to outperform vanilla ViT on key fairness metrics, and the only prior ViT-specific fairness method (TADeT) requires restrictive parameter sharing and still underperforms CNN baselines. This leaves a gap in how to effectively mitigate spurious-feature-driven bias in ViT while preserving prediction accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images partitioned into non-overlapping fixed-size patches (16×16 or 32×32, yielding 196 patches for 224×224 inputs) and produces a single binary class score via a sigmoid output. The sensitive label (e.g., gender, background type) is accessible only during training for fairness enforcement and is not required at inference. During the adversarial attack step, the input patch sequence is selectively perturbed at the top-k patches identified as carrying spurious features, producing an augmented adversarial example that is paired with the original for joint training.

## Method

DSA is a two-step hierarchical framework. In Step 1, a bias-only ViT is trained with an adversarial objective (minimizing sensitive-label CE loss while maximizing target-label CE loss via gradient reversal) so that its feature extractor learns exclusively spurious features correlated with the sensitive label. An attention-aware adversarial attack (inspired by Patch-Fool) then selects the top-k most influential patches for sensitive-label prediction and perturbs them using PGD with PCGrad to resolve gradient conflicts between the classification loss and a layer-wise attention-attracting loss. In Step 2, the debiased ViT is trained on both original and adversarial examples with a composite objective: standard CE losses on both examples plus an attention weights alignment regularizer (using Attention Transfer, MSE, or KL-Div) that minimizes the discrepancy between attention maps of the original and adversarial examples, thereby encouraging the self-attention mechanism to shift focus from spurious to real features.

## Results

DSA achieves the best fairness metrics (lowest EO, DP, and DBA) across all three datasets while simultaneously maintaining or improving prediction accuracy. On CelebA (Hair Color / Gender), DSA reaches EO=0.2558, DP=0.2337, DBA=0.0031, BA=82.92%, and Acc=90.95%. On Waterbirds (Bird Type / Background), DSA achieves EO=0.0185, DP=0.0113, DBA=0.0709, BA=63.87%, and Acc=69.58%. On bFFHQ (Age / Gender), DSA attains EO=0.2651, DP=0.2856, DBA=0.0879, BA=71.37%, and Acc=78.82%, outperforming the vanilla ViT by 5.6 percentage points in accuracy while reducing EO by 5.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSA outperforms all seven baselines on EO, DP, and DBA across all three datasets, and also achieves the highest accuracy on Waterbirds (69.58% vs. 62.36% Vanilla) and CelebA (90.95% vs. 90.25% Vanilla). The only ViT-specific fairness method, TADeT, is consistently outperformed by DSA on fairness metrics (e.g., EO 0.2558 vs. 0.2850 on CelebA; EO 0.0185 vs. 0.0424 on Waterbirds) and on accuracy, though TADeT edges out DSA on Waterbirds BA (64.14% vs. 63.87%). CNN-designed baselines (MMD, MFD, DANN) fail to transfer to ViT: on Waterbirds, none of them beat the vanilla model on EO, and DANN actually degrades accuracy to 60.04%. On bFFHQ, MMD achieves the best DP (0.2774) and DBA (0.0847), but DSA still leads on EO (0.2651 vs. 0.3041), BA (71.37% vs. 70.59%), and Acc (78.82% vs. 76.59%). The in-house AM baseline, which masks top-k patches by attention score alone, underperforms DSA on all fairness metrics, confirming that adversarial perturbation plus attention alignment is more effective than simple attention-based masking.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSA carves new ground at the intersection of adversarial robustness and fairness by repurposing adversarial attacks (inspired by Patch-Fool) not for robustness but for localizing and masking spurious features in ViT inputs. It departs from the dominant CNN-oriented fairness literature (MMD, MFD, DANN, LAFTR) by designing an attention-weights alignment regularizer specific to the self-attention mechanism, and from the sole prior ViT fairness method TADeT by avoiding its parameter-sharing constraint and complex query-matrix alignment. The 'bias-only model' that is deliberately trained to exploit only spurious features serves as an oracle for adversarial patch selection, a concept not present in prior debiasing work. The framework thus unifies pre-processing (adversarial data augmentation) and in-processing (attention alignment) strategies in a way that is architecturally native to ViT rather than ported from CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that mainstream CNN fairness methods (MMD, MFD, DANN) fail to transfer to ViT, a practically important finding as ViT adoption accelerates. The adversarial-attack-based spurious-feature localization offers a scalable alternative to manual curation or inpainting, though it still requires sensitive labels during training and is validated only on binary classification across three datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class or multi-sensitive-attribute settings, whether it works with large pre-trained ViTs (e.g., ViT-L/16 on ImageNet-scale data), and whether the bias-only model's quality as a spurious-feature oracle degrades under more complex or overlapping biases.
