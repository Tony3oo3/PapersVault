## Problem

Existing unsupervised domain adaptation methods for 3D object detection require lengthy offline training on the target domain and cannot respond to rapid, unpredictable environmental changes (e.g., weather shifts) in an online manner. Prior test-time adaptation techniques designed for 2D classification (TENT, SHOT) fail on 3D detection because they only update batch normalization layers and optimize for classification semantics rather than the regression accuracy that actually limits cross-domain detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241142/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained source-domain detector (SECOND-IoU) and streams unlabeled target-domain LiDAR point clouds in batches at test time, requiring no source data access. It outputs 3D bounding boxes (center coordinates, dimensions, rotation angle) with class labels for car, pedestrian, and cyclist. Adaptation is performed online per batch, updating only 2.73K regression-layer parameters (<0.1% of the full 12.12M model) in approximately 4.5 minutes on a single GPU, compared to hours on 4 GPUs for UDA baselines.

## Method

Reg-TTA3D adapts only the regression layers of a SECOND-IoU detector, motivated by the finding that the source model already achieves high classification accuracy while inaccurate box regression is the primary cross-domain bottleneck. Three modules drive the adaptation: (1) Noise-consistency pseudo-label generation (NPG) injects random perturbations into box size and angle, computes an inconsistency rate between perturbed and original predictions, and filters unstable boxes to yield reliable pseudo-labels; (2) Confidence-guided regression refinement (CRR) computes the mean size of high-confidence predicted boxes per class and uses it as a soft supervision signal to pull low-confidence box sizes toward the target-domain distribution via a hinge-style loss; (3) Class-balanced EMA updating (CBU) modulates the teacher-student EMA momentum based on the variance of class proportions in pseudo-labels, reducing updates when a single class dominates to prevent multi-class performance degradation. The student model is trained on augmented views of the same batch using the combined detection and refinement losses, while the teacher is updated via the class-balanced EMA.

## Results

On the Waymo→KITTI cross-location task, Reg-TTA3D achieves 61.78% AP_BEV and 49.96% AP_3D, surpassing the best UDA method ReDB (61.14%/50.10%) on AP_BEV while matching it on AP_3D, and significantly outperforming all TTA baselines (TENT: 31.34%/19.47%, SHOT: 51.27%/35.38%, MATE: 52.94%/35.92%). On nuScenes→KITTI, Reg-TTA3D reaches 33.90%/23.13%, outperforming ST3D (34.00%/23.64%) and ST3D++ (33.75%/23.07%) while using only target data and one epoch of adaptation. On the cross-weather task (nuScenes Normal→Fog/Snow), Reg-TTA3D improves mAP from 48.40→49.67 and 45.86→47.09 respectively, while all other TTA methods degrade performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UDA methods, Reg-TTA3D matches or slightly exceeds ReDB (best UDA) on Waymo→KITTI (61.78 vs 61.14 AP_BEV; 49.96 vs 50.10 AP_3D) while requiring only target data and 4.5 min on 1 GPU versus 3.2 h on 4 GPUs. It clearly outperforms ST3D and ST3D++ on all three cross-domain settings (e.g., 61.78 vs 56.60/59.42 AP_BEV on Waymo→KITTI). Against TTA baselines, Reg-TTA3D dominates: TENT collapses to 31.34% AP_BEV (below Source Only's 46.64%), SHOT reaches 51.27%, and MATE reaches 52.94% on Waymo→KITTI, all far below Reg-TTA3D's 61.78%. On the cross-weather task, Reg-TTA3D is the only method that improves over Source Only (49.67 vs 48.40 mAP on Fog; 47.09 vs 45.86 on Snow), while TENT, SHOT, and MATE all degrade performance. The paper also validates generalizability on PointRCNN, where Reg-TTA3D (50.87% AP_3D moderate) outperforms SF-UDA3D (49.80%) despite the latter being trained on a single class.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of test-time adaptation (TTA) and 3D object detection, building on the SECOND-IoU detector architecture and the pseudo-label self-training paradigm established by ST3D/ST3D++/ReDB for offline UDA. It departs from prior TTA work (TENT, SHOT, MATE) in two key ways: (1) it identifies that updating only regression layers—rather than BN layers or the full model—is the critical adaptation target for 3D detection, and (2) it introduces regression-specific mechanisms (noise-consistency pseudo-label generation, confidence-guided regression refinement) that have no direct analogue in classification-oriented TTA. The class-balanced EMA updating strategy extends standard EMA teacher-student schemes with a variance-driven momentum to address multi-class imbalance during online adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for autonomous driving, where environmental changes (weather, geography) demand rapid, low-cost online adaptation without retraining or source data access. The finding that <0.1% of parameters updated in <1% of UDA training time can match or exceed full UDA performance offers a compelling efficiency story for deployment. However, the paper acknowledges it cannot handle corner cases or emergencies, and the approach is validated primarily on LiDAR-based detectors in driving scenarios—whether the regression-bottleneck insight generalizes to other detection modalities (e.g., camera-based 3D detection) or non-driving 3D tasks remains open.
