## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for remote sensing images focus on pixel reconstruction but overlook the diverse angles that RS objects exhibit due to overhead imaging. Unlike natural images where gravity fixes object orientations, RS objects appear at arbitrary angles, and prior MIM-based approaches only implicitly encounter angle variation during reconstruction without explicitly learning rotation-invariant representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RS image and constructs a composite input by inserting a 96×96 rotated crop (36 patches of 16×16) at a random position, rotated at a random angle in [-45°, +45°], replacing the original scene at that location. The encoder processes only visible patches (75% masked separately for the rotated crop and background), and the decoder outputs pixel values for all masked patches. The encoder features are then transferred to downstream tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) while the decoder is discarded.

## Method

MA3E uses an asymmetric ViT-B encoder with an 8-block 512-D decoder. A scaling center crop operation extracts the largest inscribed square (side a = √2/2 · h) from a randomly positioned square region, preserving scene content while introducing explicit angle variation; a learnable angle embedding is added to all patches within the rotated crop. The rotated crop and background are masked independently at 75% to avoid losing all crop patches. For the rotated crop, reconstruction is formulated as an optimal transport problem: the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm solves for a transportation plan that automatically assigns similar original image patches as reconstruction targets for each predicted crop patch, avoiding the positional bias of direct MSE. The total loss combines MSE for background patches and the OT loss for rotated crop patches, jointly driving pixel reconstruction and angle restoration.

## Results

MA3E pre-trained for 1600 epochs achieves the best performance across all downstream tasks: 79.47 mAP on DOTA1.0, 71.82 mAP on DIOR-R, 64.06 mIoU on iSAID, and 91.50 mF1 on Potsdam. For scene classification (fine-tuning), MA3E (1600 ep.) reaches 96.23% on NU45, 99.04% on AID, and 99.81% on UC Merced. Even with only 300 pre-training epochs, MA3E already outperforms most baselines, achieving 77.93 mAP on DOTA1.0 and 95.77% on NU45, with only ~0.2 GPU-hours extra per epoch over standard MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MA3E (1600 ep.) outperforms the strongest RS-specific baseline, MAE with ViTAE-B+RVSA [45], by 0.51 mAP on DOTA1.0, 0.87 mAP on DIOR-R, 0.58 mIoU on iSAID, and 0.28 mF1 on Potsdam, while using a simpler ViT-B backbone without the rotated varied-size window attention module. Against GFM [35] (Swin-B, 100 ep.), MA3E leads by 1.66 mAP on DOTA1.0 and 4.15 mAP on DIOR-R. Among natural-image pre-training methods, MA3E (300 ep.) already surpasses MAE† (1600 ep.) on DOTA1.0 (77.93 vs. 77.53) and DIOR-R (68.41 vs. 67.72), and MA3E (1600 ep.) extends this lead to 79.47 and 71.82. On scene classification (Table 1), MA3E (1600 ep.) achieves 96.23% on NU45, beating MAE[45] ViTAE-B+RVSA (95.69%) and GFM★ (96.06%), while matching or exceeding all other baselines. The only case where MA3E trails is UCM fine-tuning at 300 epochs (99.05% vs. 99.70% for MAE[45] ViT-B+RVSA), though MA3E requires only 52% of the FLOPs; at 1600 epochs MA3E recovers the lead (99.81% vs. 99.56%). SatMAE [9] and ScaleMAE [38] perform substantially worse across all tasks, confirming that temporal/multi-spectral or scale-aware objectives do not substitute for explicit angle awareness in RS pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

MA3E builds directly on the asymmetric encoder-decoder architecture and masking principle of MAE [20], extending it with three RS-specific modifications: a scaling center crop operation to introduce explicit angle variation, learnable angle embeddings for the rotated crop region, and an Optimal Transport loss (inspired by OT assignment in object detection [17]) to handle the spatial offset between rotated crop patches and their original positions. It departs from prior RS-specific MIM methods (Wang et al. [45], SatMAE [9], ScaleMAE [38], CMID [36], GFM [35]) that address resolution, spectral, or contrastive aspects but treat angle as an implicit byproduct of pixel reconstruction rather than an explicit learning signal. The paper positions angle awareness as a missing ingredient in RS pretraining, motivated by the observation that overhead-captured objects exhibit a wide range of orientations unlike gravity-fixed natural image objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The angle-aware pretraining approach addresses a genuine gap in RS representation learning: objects in aerial/satellite imagery appear at arbitrary orientations, and the paper's angle-stratified detection analysis (Fig. 1) provides concrete evidence that standard MAE underperforms specifically on non-axis-aligned objects. The OT loss is a creative repurposing of optimal transport from detection assignment to reconstruction target matching, offering a generalizable technique for any reconstruction task involving spatially shifted content. The authors acknowledge a key limitation: angle information is most relevant for man-made objects, while extensive land cover benefits more from scale awareness, leaving open the question of how to jointly model angle and scale in a unified pretraining objective.
