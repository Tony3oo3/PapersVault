## Problem

Existing one-stage weakly supervised REC methods like RefCLIP rely on anchor features from pre-trained detectors, which are noisy, fragmented, and lack fine-grained visual semantics such as color and precise spatial information. This ambiguity causes anchor-text matching to select incorrect anchors, particularly for expressions requiring attribute-level or relational distinctions, limiting the effectiveness of weakly supervised training on image-text pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 416×416 RGB image and a referring expression (text length 15–20 tokens depending on dataset) as input. The image is processed by a DarkNet-53 backbone to produce multi-scale anchor features, which are filtered by detection confidence before prompt fusion; the text is encoded by a bi-directional GRU followed by self-attention. At inference, APL outputs the referent's bounding box via a predictive branch (a SimREC-style REC decoder with GARAN fusion) rather than the anchor-text ranking path, and when extended to RES, it produces a segmentation mask using YOLOv5-Seg as the detection backbone.

## Method

APL introduces an Anchor-based Prompt Encoder (APE) that generates three discriminative prompts per anchor: a spatial prompt (positional embedding of the detected box center), a color prompt (RGB values from the anchor region converted to color words via a pre-defined table), and a category prompt (class name from the detection head). The color and category prompts are combined into a text prompt encoded via word embeddings, and all prompts are fused into anchor features through a dynamic weighted sum with learned attention weights. Training combines three objectives: the original anchor-text contrastive loss, a text reconstruction loss that projects the best-matched anchor-prompt feature through an MLP to reconstruct the expression embedding, and a visual alignment loss that uses a SimREC-style REC decoder to regress the pseudo-box in a detection-loss manner (applied after 9,000 warm-up steps). This design enriches the otherwise ambiguous anchor representations with explicit position, color, and category semantics, enabling finer-grained vision-language alignment under weak supervision.

## Results

APL achieves state-of-the-art weakly supervised REC performance on all four benchmarks: 64.51% IoU on RefCOCO val, 42.70% on RefCOCO+ val, 50.22% on RefCOCOg val-g, and 41.80% on ReferIt test, outperforming the prior best one-stage method RefCLIP by +4.15%, +2.31%, +2.35%, and +2.22% respectively. On the most challenging RefCOCO testB split, APL reaches 63.57%, a +6.44% gain over RefCLIP. APL maintains real-time inference at 26.7 fps, slightly slower than RefCLIP (31.3 fps) but far faster than two-stage methods (5.7–5.9 fps). Extended to weakly supervised RES, APL achieves 55.92% mIoU on RefCOCO val, surpassing the previous SOTA (TRIS, 31.17%) by 24.75%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APL consistently outperforms all 23 compared baselines across the four REC benchmarks. Against the closest one-stage competitor RefCLIP (CVPR'23), APL gains +4.15% on RefCOCO val, +6.44% on RefCOCO testB, +2.31% on RefCOCO+ val, +2.35% on RefCOCOg val-g, and +2.22% on ReferIt, while maintaining comparable inference speed (26.7 vs. 31.3 fps). Two-stage weakly supervised methods (ARN, DTWREG, IGN, etc.) trail APL by 20–30+ points on RefCOCO and 4–10 points on RefCOCO+, and are 4–5× slower. Among prompt-learning baselines (Table 3), APL surpasses VPT by +9.12% on RefCOCO val and CoOp by +4.18%, attributed to its anchor-conditioned multimodal prompts (position, color, category) versus generic learnable vectors. In the pseudo-label setting, APL as teacher improves student SimREC by up to +4.04% over RefCLIP-as-teacher on RefCOCO+ testA. For RES (Table 5), APL beats the prior SOTA TRIS by +24.75% mIoU on RefCOCO val and +4.13% on RefCOCOg val-g, and also outperforms PKS (which uses additional click annotations) on RefCOCO and RefCOCOg.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RefCLIP (Jin et al., CVPR 2023), which reformulated weakly supervised REC as an anchor-text matching problem using one-stage YOLOv3 detectors, eliminating the expensive region proposal stage of two-stage methods like MAttNet. The key departure is the identification that anchor features are inherently ambiguous and semantically insufficient for fine-grained REC, motivating the introduction of an anchor-based prompt encoder (APE) that injects structured position, color, and category information into anchor representations. Conceptually, APL bridges the prompt learning literature (VPT, CoOp) with one-stage detection, but unlike prior prompt methods that use learnable vectors, APL dynamically constructs multimodal prompts conditioned on each anchor's detected box and category, making the prompts instance-specific and interpretable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the visual bottleneck in one-stage weakly supervised REC is not merely a matter of model capacity but of semantic richness in anchor representations, and that structured, interpretable prompts can close much of the gap to fully supervised methods while preserving real-time inference (26.7 fps). The successful transfer to weakly supervised RES (outperforming TRIS by up to 26 mIoU) suggests the prompt-enriched anchor paradigm is broadly applicable beyond bounding box prediction. However, the authors acknowledge that APL still struggles with long expressions requiring multi-step reasoning and occluded objects, leaving open the question of whether prompt-based enrichment can scale to compositional and relational reasoning without explicit language modeling.
