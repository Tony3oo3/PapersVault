## Problem

Existing radar-based 3D human pose estimation methods rely on CFAR-extracted point clouds that are hardware-sensitive and lose spatial-temporal information, or on decomposed 4D tensors that discard Doppler velocity data. No benchmark dataset or single-stage architecture existed for HPE using raw 4D radar tensors, and prior radar HPE datasets were limited in detection range, action complexity, and environmental diversity, hindering practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241676/cropped_page4_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single 4D radar tensor of shape 64×32×128×256 (Doppler × Z × Y × X in Cartesian coordinates) at 10 FPS, where the 64-channel Doppler axis encodes velocity information at 3.9 cm/s resolution and the spatial axes span a 2–8 m detection volume with 4.5 cm range resolution. It outputs, per detected person, a 3D body center location (pelvis) and 15 3D joint keypoints, decoded by adding per-joint offsets to the predicted center; multi-person inference uses range NMS to resolve overlapping detections.

## Method

HRRadarPose is a single-stage, fully 3D-convolutional network adapted from HRNet that treats the Doppler axis as input channels and extracts volumetric spatial features along Z, Y, and X. The backbone uses multi-stage strided convolutions with parallel branches for cross-resolution information exchange, and a key architectural modification replaces batch normalization with group normalization in each HRNet block so that features within each velocity group are normalized independently (Radar-Doppler Convolutional Block). The pose estimation head comprises two 3D-convolutional branches producing a body-center confidence map and a 15-keypoint offset map; training uses pixel-wise focal loss for center classification and L1 regression on keypoint offsets. This single-stage design avoids region-proposal complexity and inherently associates joints to individuals without an explicit matching step. Ground-truth annotations are generated by a semi-automatic pipeline combining HRNet 2D poses, ZeDO 3D pose estimation initialized with LiDAR-derived pelvis depth, and manual per-frame correction.

## Results

On the RT-Pose benchmark, HRRadarPose achieves 9.91 cm MRPE, 9.93 cm MPJPE, and 14.73 cm Abs-MPJPE, outperforming all three baselines across every metric. The radar point cloud-based methods (mm-Pose, mmMesh) show substantially higher errors (21.26 and 13.78 cm MPJPE respectively), while the 4D radar tensor-based RF-Pose3D achieves 18.43 cm MPJPE. Per-action analysis reveals that simple standing actions (waving, lifting legs) yield ~7 cm MPJPE, while complex actions like walking-and-waving reach 15.28 cm MPJPE, highlighting the persistent challenge of accurate HPE in diverse real-world scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRRadarPose decisively outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against the radar point cloud methods, the advantage is most pronounced: mm-Pose [37] (simple CNN on point clouds) achieves 102.28 cm MRPE and 21.26 cm MPJPE, while mmMesh [48] (RNN + CNN with global localization) improves to 62.69 cm MRPE and 13.78 cm MPJPE — yet both remain 6–10× worse than HRRadarPose in MRPE. Against the closest competitor, RF-Pose3D [55] (4D radar tensor with a region-proposal network), HRRadarPose reduces MRPE from 29.17 to 9.91 cm (3× improvement) and MPJPE from 18.43 to 9.93 cm (~46% reduction). The authors attribute the gap between tensor-based and point cloud methods to the difficulty of CFAR-extracted point clouds in discriminating individual keypoints under complex poses and cluttered environments, since the raw 4D tensor preserves full spatio-temporal and Doppler information that point cloud generation discards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HRNet architecture (Wang et al., TPAMI 2020) by converting its 2D convolutional blocks to fully 3D convolutions and replacing batch normalization with group normalization to handle the Doppler axis as a channel dimension. It departs from prior radar-based HPE methods (mm-Pose, mmMesh) that rely on CFAR-extracted point clouds, arguing that raw 4D radar tensors preserve richer spatio-temporal information. It also contrasts with RF-Pose 3D, which used 4D RF tensors but required a two-stage region-proposal pipeline; HRRadarPose is explicitly single-stage. The annotation workflow leverages ZeDO (a diffusion-based zero-shot 3D HPE method) combined with LiDAR depth to produce ground truth, a multi-sensor fusion approach not seen in prior radar HPE datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RT-Pose is the first benchmark providing calibrated 4D radar tensors alongside LiDAR and RGB for 3D HPE, filling a gap where prior radar HPE datasets used only point clouds or decomposed tensors in limited indoor settings. The ~10 cm MPJPE achieved by the baseline underscores that accurate radar-based HPE in cluttered, multi-person, and occluded real-world scenarios remains an open challenge. Key limitations include the 100 MB per-frame tensor size constraining training throughput, the 2–8 m detection range, and degraded performance on complex actions like walking-and-waving. Future work must address scalability to longer sequences, larger detection volumes, and more diverse action vocabularies before radar-based HPE can replace camera systems in privacy-sensitive deployments.
