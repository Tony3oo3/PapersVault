## Problem

Facial expression analysis tasks (AU detection, AU intensity estimation, FER) are degraded by task-irrelevant noise such as identity, head pose, and background, causing models to overfit seen identities and fail to generalize. Existing remedies—identity-based expression pairs, synthetic neutral/average faces, or task-specific generative disentanglement—either still suffer from residual identity bias, rely on controlled lab data, or cannot be unified across all three tasks. The paper asks whether a single normalization step can strip these confounders from arbitrary in-the-wild and in-the-lab images while preserving expression fidelity.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a 256×256 aligned-and-cropped RGB facial image (I_o) together with a fixed target face image (I_t^id) selected from BP4D/BP4D+ for consistent identity, pose, and background. The normalization stage outputs a synthetic face I_n that matches the target's identity/pose/background while retaining the original's expression; both I_n and I_o are then jointly fed into the classification network, which produces per-AU binary labels (detection), continuous intensity scores (estimation), or a single emotion class (FER) depending on the task.

## Method

Norface is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (normalization network F^nor) uses a pre-trained Masked AutoEncoder as a shared face encoder to project both original and target faces into patch embeddings; an Expression Merging Module (EMM) with multi-head cross-attention adaptively transfers expression information from the original into the target's latent space, and a convolutional decoder renders the normalized face, supervised by seven losses (adversarial, reconstruction, perceptual, identity via ArcFace, landmark, fine-grained expression, and 3DMM-based eyebrow). Stage 2 (classification network F^cla) encodes both the normalized and original images with a second MAE-based encoder, then applies an Input MoE (two identity-specific Mixture-of-Experts blocks with Top-2 noisy routing) to refine each identity's representation and an Output MoE to fuse them into a task-specific latent vector for the final AU/emotion prediction. The MoE blocks are regularized with an importance loss (squared coefficient of variation of gating weights) and global/local entropy losses to encourage balanced yet specialized expert usage.

## Results

On AU detection, Norface achieves 69.3% average F1 on BP4D (+4.7% over GLEE-Net), 66.7% on BP4D+ (+3.0% over GLEE-Net), and 72.7% on DISFA (+5.4% over LGRNet). For FER, Norface reaches 92.97% accuracy on RAF-DB (+1.41% over LA-Net) and 68.69% on AffectNet (+1.09% over LA-Net). On cross-dataset tasks, Norface surpasses the best baselines by 5.1% (AU detection BP4D→BP4D+), 6.09% (FER R→A), and 11.2% (FER A→R), demonstrating strong domain generalization benefits from identity normalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Norface outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every task and dataset. On AU detection, it exceeds the strongest baseline GLEE-Net [94] by 4.7% on BP4D (69.3 vs 64.6 avg F1) and 3.0% on BP4D+ (66.7 vs 63.7), and surpasses LGRNet [17] by 5.4% on DISFA (72.7 vs 67.3). For FER, Norface beats the prior SOTA LA-Net [74] by 1.41% on RAF-DB (92.97 vs 91.56) and 1.09% on AffectNet (68.69 vs 67.60). On cross-dataset AU detection (BP4D→BP4D+), Norface leads GLEE-Net by 5.1% (62.6 vs 57.5), and on cross-dataset FER it outperforms RANDA [24] by 6.09% (R→A: 58.43 vs 52.34) and 11.2% (A→R: 73.68 vs 62.48). For AU intensity estimation on DISFA, Norface achieves the best ICC (0.67 vs 0.58 for APs [56]), lowest MSE (0.22 vs 0.30), and lowest MAE (0.17 vs 0.20). The consistent margins across in-domain and cross-dataset settings indicate that identity normalization effectively reduces domain-specific noise, yielding the largest gains in cross-dataset transfer where identity and pose variation are most pronounced.

## Relation to Prior Work

Norface departs from the dominant identity-invariant FEA paradigm (DLN, GLEE-Net, IdenNet) that learns disentangled representations or constructs identity-based expression pairs, and from generative approaches (DeRL, Huang et al.) that synthesize neutral or average identities. Instead, it repurposes a pre-trained Masked AutoEncoder and a cross-attention-based Expression Merging Module to directly render every face onto a fixed target identity with consistent pose and background, then feeds both original and normalized images into a Mixture-of-Experts classifier. This 'normalize-then-classify' pipeline unifies AU detection, AU intensity estimation, and FER without requiring co-annotated labels, distinguishing it from multi-task methods like JPML and MT-VGG that suffer from task imbalance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The released normalized dataset and the unified framework give practitioners a practical tool to reduce identity, pose, and background confounds across three FEA tasks simultaneously, with particularly notable cross-dataset gains (up to 11.2% on A→R FER) suggesting that domain barriers shrink when task-irrelevant noise is removed. However, the approach depends on a carefully selected fixed target identity (certain identities degrade performance due to expression confusion), adds a full image-generation stage at inference, and is limited to single-frame inputs, leaving open whether the strategy extends to video-based or micro-expression settings. A key open question is whether the normalization can be made identity-agnostic or adaptive per input rather than relying on a single fixed target.
