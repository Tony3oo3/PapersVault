## Problem

Existing ICL-based segmentation frameworks select visual prompts either randomly or via simple similarity sorting, yet the paper demonstrates that model performance is highly sensitive to example choice (gaps exceeding 5 mIoU points) and that the most dissimilar contexts outperform the most similar ones on ~40% of test instances. The critical factor of diversity in contextual prompts remains unexplored, and no efficient strategy exists to balance diversity with query-specific relevance while keeping annotation costs low.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image (resized to 224×224) and a small pre-annotated candidate pool of 2M example images with corresponding segmentation masks (where M is the number of clusters, e.g., 20 images for M=10). Unlike prior approaches that require annotating the entire training set, SCS only needs labels for the compact candidate pool. The output is a pixel-level segmentation mask produced by the underlying ICL model (e.g., SegGPT or MAE-VQGAN) after the SCS module selects which 1 or 5 examples to feed as in-context demonstrations.

## Method

SCS operates in two stages. First, it constructs a diverse candidate pool by extracting CLIP features from unlabeled data, applying k-means clustering, and selecting both the nearest and farthest member from each cluster centroid (rather than just the centroid-proximal sample), yielding 2M representative examples that span the feature space. Second, an Adaptive Search Module (ASM) — a lightweight MLP — concatenates the query's CLIP feature with each candidate's feature and outputs selection probabilities via softmax. The ASM is trained with a REINFORCE-style policy gradient where per-example IoU scores (computed by running the ICL model with each candidate) serve as rewards, encouraging the agent to up-weight examples that yield higher segmentation quality. At inference, the top-k candidates by probability are selected as the final in-context demonstrations, making SCS a plug-and-play module compatible with any ICL-based segmentation backbone.

## Results

On COCO-20i, the proposed SCS method applied to SegGPT achieves 57.2 mIoU (1-shot) and 59.5 mIoU (5-shot), outperforming the similarity-sorting baseline [56] by 3.9 and 3.4 points respectively. On PASCAL-5i, SCS reaches 76.6 mIoU (1-shot) and 78.2 mIoU (5-shot) with SegGPT. When applied to the weaker MAE-VQGAN backbone, SCS yields larger absolute gains: +7.0 mIoU on COCO-20i 1-shot (12.1→19.1) and +7.5 mIoU on PASCAL-5i 1-shot (27.5→35.0), demonstrating its plug-and-play effectiveness across ICL segmentation frameworks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCS consistently outperforms the similarity-sorting baseline [56] across all settings: +3.9 mIoU on COCO-20i 1-shot (57.2 vs 53.3), +3.4 on COCO-20i 5-shot (59.5 vs 56.1), +2.2 on PASCAL-5i 1-shot (76.6 vs 74.4), and +1.3 on PASCAL-5i 5-shot (78.2 vs 76.9). Compared to random sampling (SegGPT w/R [45]), the gains are larger: +8.6 mIoU on COCO-20i 1-shot and +6.8 on 5-shot. For the weaker MAE-VQGAN backbone, SCS improves 1-shot mIoU from 12.1 to 19.1 on COCO-20i and from 27.5 to 35.0 on PASCAL-5i, surpassing the similarity baseline [56] by 1.1 and 1.4 points respectively. In the alternative-mechanism comparison (Table 3), SCS beats Entropy sorting [11] (+4.1 mIoU 1-shot) and Foreground sorting [53] (+3.7 mIoU 1-shot) on COCO-20i, while Density-sort [2] achieves a marginally higher 1-shot mIoU (57.6 vs 57.2) but lower 5-shot mIoU (59.1 vs 59.5) and requires O(MN) per-iteration computation. Painter [44] serves as a standalone ICL baseline without any prompt selection strategy and is substantially outperformed by SegGPT+SCS (e.g., 57.2 vs 32.8 mIoU on COCO-20i 1-shot), though this reflects the backbone difference rather than the selection method alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ICL-based segmentation paradigm established by MAE-VQGAN and SegGPT, which reformulate segmentation as a context-conditioned inpainting or coloring task. It departs from the prevailing practice of selecting visual prompts via random sampling or cosine-similarity sorting (as in [56, 37]) by identifying diversity—not similarity—as the critical factor for effective prompt selection. The method draws on NLP's in-context example selection literature (KATE, EPR, UDR) but adapts the problem to the vision domain, introducing a clustering-based candidate pool construction and a reinforcement-learning search agent that has no direct analogue in prior visual ICL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that dissimilar examples outperform similar ones in ~40% of test instances challenges the default assumption of similarity-based retrieval and provides a principled justification for diversity-aware prompt design. The plug-and-play nature of SCS (reducing annotation cost while boosting mIoU by 3–7 points across SegGPT and MAE-VQGAN) makes it immediately applicable to any ICL segmentation pipeline. Open questions include whether the diversity principle generalizes beyond segmentation to other ICL vision tasks (detection, depth estimation), whether the simple k-means + nearest-farthest heuristic scales to very large or highly heterogeneous databases, and whether the RL-based search agent can be replaced by a learned end-to-end retriever without the expensive per-example IoU computation.
