## Problem

Vision-language models typically rely on a single vision encoder (e.g., CLIP), which exhibits 'blindness' to certain visual differences and is prone to hallucinations. The paper shows through systematic benchmarking that no single encoder consistently achieves top performance across captioning and VQA tasks, yet no existing method efficiently consolidates features from multiple encoders with different inductive biases into a unified VLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240411/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (224×224 for pre-training, 336×336 for fine-tuning) and a text prompt (e.g., 'A photo of' for captioning or the question for VQA) as input, producing a free-form caption or short text answer. The text prompt is not only prepended to the LM input but also fed into the MEQ-Former alongside learnable queries to extract task-aligned visual features. The visual representation is compressed to a fixed 160×768 token sequence (a 14× reduction from the raw 1223×1408 concatenated encoder features) before being projected into the LM's embedding space.

## Method

BRAVE introduces the MEQ-Former, a multi-encoder generalization of BLIP-2's Q-Former that cross-attends 160 learnable queries (32 per encoder) to sequence-wise concatenated features from five frozen vision encoders (EVA-CLIP-g, CLIP-L/14, SILC-G/16, ViT-e, DINOv2-L/14). Each encoder's output is linearly projected to a shared 1408-dim space and concatenated, then jointly cross-attended without any encoder-specific positional or identity markers, allowing the model to adaptively weight encoders per task. Training is single-stage on 100M WebLI image-text pairs with a captioning objective, using 20% encoder dropout as a regularizer to prevent the MEQ-Former from collapsing onto a single encoder. Only 116M parameters (~1% of the 10.3B total) are trainable during pre-training, with optional LoRA fine-tuning of the frozen FlanT5-XL LM for VQA tasks. The key insight is that diverse visual biases are complementary rather than redundant, and a lightweight resampling bottleneck can consolidate them more effectively than naive ensembling while maintaining parameter efficiency.

## Results

BRAVE achieves state-of-the-art results on 6 out of 7 VQA benchmarks: 66.0% on OKVQA, 66.3% on GQA (fine-tuned), 54.2% on VizWiz-QA, 42.0% on MMVP, and 87.6% on POPE, while remaining competitive on VQAv2 (82.5% vs. PaLI-17B's 84.3%). For captioning, BRAVE attains 148.0 CIDEr on COCO (second only to PaLI-17B's 149.1) and the best NoCaps scores (127.6 overall val, 133.3 out-domain val) using only 116M trainable parameters (~1% of the 10.3B total). The most striking gain is on MMVP, where BRAVE reaches 42.0% average pair accuracy versus 31.3% for the best single-encoder baseline (LLaVA 1.5 with I-MoF), demonstrating that multi-encoder fusion substantially reduces CLIP-blindness failure modes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline PaLI-17B [19], BRAVE wins on OKVQA (66.0 vs. 64.5), GQA fine-tuned (66.3 vs. not reported), VizWiz-QA (54.2 vs. not reported), MMVP (42.0 vs. not reported), and POPE (87.6 vs. not reported), while trailing by 1.8 points on VQAv2 (82.5 vs. 84.3) and 1.1 CIDEr on COCO (148.0 vs. 149.1); however, BRAVE uses 150× fewer trainable parameters (116M vs. 16.9B) and 16× less pre-training data. Compared to LLaVA 1.5 [64], BRAVE improves MMVP by 17.3 points (42.0 vs. 24.7), POPE by 1.7 points (87.6 vs. 85.9), GQA by 3.0 points (66.3 vs. 63.3), and VizWiz-QA by 0.6 points (54.2 vs. 53.6). Against InstructBLIP [25], the gains are even larger: +10.5 on OKVQA, +13.2 on VizWiz-QA, +25.3 on MMVP, and +8.7 on POPE. Versus the concurrent multi-encoder method LLaVA 1.5 (I-MoF) [84], BRAVE leads MMVP by 10.7 points (42.0 vs. 31.3) and POPE by 0.9 points, while also reporting results on all other benchmarks. On NoCaps captioning, BRAVE outperforms PaLI-17B by 0.6 CIDEr overall (127.6 vs. 127.0) and 6.7 CIDEr on out-domain samples (133.3 vs. 126.7), confirming that multi-encoder diversity aids out-of-distribution generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRAVE generalizes the Q-Former bridge from BLIP-2 [56] from a single vision encoder to an arbitrary set of frozen encoders, introducing the MEQ-Former that cross-attends over concatenated multi-encoder features via learnable queries. It departs from the dominant single-encoder paradigm (CLIP, EVA-CLIP, etc.) used by InstructBLIP, LLaVA, and PaLI, and from concurrent multi-encoder attempts like LLaVA-MoF [84] and SPHINX [61] that simply concatenate encoder outputs to the LM without a resampling mechanism. The paper also contributes a systematic empirical study of eight vision encoders under a unified training/evaluation protocol, which to the authors' knowledge is the first such comprehensive comparison for VLM tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no single vision encoder consistently dominates across captioning and VQA tasks, and that diverse encoders can be consolidated with only 116M trainable parameters (≈1% of total) to match or exceed 10B+ parameter methods, suggests that scaling along the vision axis is a viable and efficient alternative to the field's focus on larger language models. The near-1.5× improvement on MMVP (42% vs 27.3%) directly addresses the well-documented 'CLIP blindness' problem. Key open questions include the inference cost of running all encoders simultaneously (no adaptive selection yet), whether the approach generalizes to encoder sets beyond the five tested, and whether the combined features can be distilled back into a single encoder for deployment.
