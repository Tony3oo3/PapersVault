## Problem

Deterministic data-driven weather forecasting models achieve high accuracy but cannot represent the spectrum of possible futures, while probabilistic diffusion-based models generate diverse samples that often deviate significantly from ground truth. This creates a fundamental trade-off: accuracy without uncertainty, or uncertainty without accuracy. The paper asks how to obtain both high-precision forecasts and controlled probabilistic diversity in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240786/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an observed weather video sequence x ∈ R^{C×L×H×W} (e.g., 3 satellite channels at 128×128 over 10 hourly frames for PNW-Typhoon, or a single variable at 32×64 over 12 frames for WeatherBench) and produces a future sequence y ∈ R^{C×L̂×H×W} as multiple probabilistic samples from the diffusion process plus a single deterministic forecast. Notably, only the last frame of the input is replicated to match the output length for the Brownian bridge endpoint, and the deterministic branch output is fed back into the reverse diffusion to truncate it, so the number of generated samples and their spread are directly controllable at inference time.

## Method

DGDM couples a non-autoregressive deterministic branch (encoder–translator–decoder, following SimVP/TAU) with a probabilistic branch built on a Brownian bridge diffusion process conditioned via cross-attention on the deterministic branch's latent features. A Sequential Variance Schedule (SVS) assigns fewer diffusion steps to near-future frames and more to distant frames, so near-futures are generated first and serve as conditions for later frames, improving temporal coherence. At inference, the deterministic branch's prediction ŷ is substituted into the forward process to compute a pseudo intermediate state x̂_t, from which the reverse diffusion is truncated—reducing denoising steps while simultaneously narrowing the diversity of generated samples around the deterministic estimate. Both branches are trained jointly end-to-end with a combined MSE loss, and the Brownian bridge formulation (rather than standard DDPM) ensures the diffusion trajectory is anchored between the observed last frame and the target, making the probabilistic output inherently guided toward the correct future.

## Results

On Moving MNIST, DGDM-Best achieves MAE 47.31, MSE 19.14, PSNR 25.59, SSIM 0.966, and FVD 7.427, outperforming the best deterministic baseline TAU (MAE 51.46, FVD 28.169) and the best probabilistic baseline PreDiff (MAE 81.16, MSE 42.20, FVD 7.889). On the novel PNW-Typhoon dataset (IR channel), DGDM-Average achieves MSE 456.11 and FVD 733.03, surpassing TAU (MSE 565.47, FVD 997.47) and PreDiff (MSE 568.93, FVD 760.73). On WeatherBench, DGDM-Best attains the lowest MSE across all three variables: temperature (1.025), humidity (28.572), and wind (1.591), beating the best deterministic model TAU (1.162, 31.831, 1.593).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGDM dominates across all three datasets by combining the accuracy of deterministic models with the diversity of probabilistic ones. On Moving MNIST, DGDM-Best beats the best deterministic model TAU on MAE (47.31 vs 51.46) and FVD (7.427 vs 28.169), while also surpassing the best probabilistic model PreDiff on MAE (47.31 vs 81.16), MSE (19.14 vs 42.20), PSNR (25.59 vs 19.05), and SSIM (0.966 vs 0.931). On PNW-Typhoon, DGDM-Average outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three satellite channels (IR, SW, WV); for example, on IR it achieves MSE 456.11 vs TAU's 565.47 and PreDiff's 568.93, and FVD 733.03 vs TAU's 997.47. On WeatherBench, DGDM-Best achieves the lowest MSE for temperature (1.025 vs TAU's 1.162), humidity (28.572 vs TAU's 31.831), and wind (1.591 vs TAU's 1.593), while DGDM-Average is second-best on humidity. The key finding is that deterministic models (TAU, SimVP) win on MSE/PSNR but lose badly on FVD, while pure probabilistic models (VDM, MCVD, RaMViD) achieve low FVD but have 2–4× worse MSE/MAE; DGDM closes this gap by using deterministic guidance to truncate the diffusion reverse process, yielding both high accuracy and controlled diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGDM sits at the intersection of two lines of work: non-autoregressive video prediction architectures (SimVP, TAU) that provide high-accuracy deterministic forecasting, and Brownian bridge diffusion models (BBDM) that enable image-to-image translation via stochastic processes. The closest prior work, PreDiff, applies latent diffusion to precipitation nowcasting but suffers from high sample diversity that makes ensemble selection difficult. DGDM departs from both by coupling a deterministic branch to a probabilistic diffusion branch and using the deterministic output to truncate the reverse diffusion process, a mechanism not present in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The truncated diffusion mechanism offers a practical way to control the spread of probabilistic forecasts while simultaneously reducing inference cost (halving denoising steps), which is significant for operational weather forecasting where both speed and uncertainty quantification matter. The release of the PNW-Typhoon dataset fills a gap in regional extreme-weather benchmarks at 2 km resolution. However, the paper acknowledges that selecting the best sample from probabilistic outputs remains an open problem, and the method has not been extended to multi-modal inputs, limiting its immediate applicability to full NWP-style multi-variable forecasting.
