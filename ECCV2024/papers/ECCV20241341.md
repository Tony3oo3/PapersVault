## Problem

Existing source-free domain adaptation methods predominantly rely on self-training with individual pseudo labels, which ignore the intrinsic data structure and inter-sample relationships. This causes the optimization to degrade toward entropy minimization, where the model produces high-confidence but incorrect predictions due to pseudo-label noise from domain shift. The paper addresses this by exploiting hierarchical structural relations across samples as a more robust form of supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241341/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images from the target domain (no source data access) through a pre-trained ResNet-50 or ResNet-101 backbone. The student model receives weakly augmented inputs (resized crops and horizontal flip) while the teacher processes original images, and a memory bank stores features and predicted logits of all target samples for relation computation. The output is a C-dimensional class probability vector per image, evaluated as Top-1 accuracy across multiple domain-shift benchmarks.

## Method

HRD employs a teacher-student framework where the teacher (EMA of student) maintains a memory bank of target features and logits. Target samples are divided into a source-like set and target-specific set via a class-balanced confidence threshold. Two relation branches are then established: (1) a local clustering relation that aggregates the logits of top-K nearest source-like neighbors weighted by cosine affinity, and (2) a global semantic relation that computes class-wise posterior probabilities using a dynamically built Gaussian Mixture Model with source-like samples as centroids. An adaptive ensemble weights each branch per-sample using the product of max prediction score and exp(−entropy) of the relation distribution, and the student is trained via temperature-scaled KL divergence to these relation vectors plus a diversity loss.

## Results

On Office-Home, HRD achieves 75.2% average top-1 accuracy and HRD++ (combined with AaD's dispersing loss) reaches 76.5%, outperforming the SHOT baseline by 3.4% and the previous best SFDA method CREL (75.5%) by 1.0%. On VisDA, HRD attains 89.8% and HRD++ reaches 90.2%, surpassing AaD (88.0%) by 1.8–2.2%. On Office-31, HRD and HRD++ achieve 90.6% and 90.8% respectively, matching the best prior method CREL (90.8%). On DomainNet, HRD reaches 69.9% and HRD++ 70.3%, outperforming GPUE (69.6%) and DaC (68.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRD outperforms the SHOT baseline [18] by 3.4% on Office-Home (75.2 vs 71.8) and 2.0% on Office-31 (90.6 vs 88.6), confirming that relation-based supervision is more effective than individual pseudo-label self-training. Against DaC [45], which also uses data division but ignores inter-set relations, HRD gains 2.9% on VisDA (89.8 vs 87.3) and 2.0% on DomainNet (69.9 vs 68.3). HRD surpasses AaD [38] by 2.5% on Office-Home (75.2 vs 72.7) and 1.8% on VisDA (89.8 vs 88.0), while HRD++ (HRD + AaD's dispersing loss) further extends the lead to 3.8% and 2.2% respectively. On Office-Home, HRD slightly trails CREL [43] (75.2 vs 75.5) but HRD++ surpasses it by 1.0% (76.5 vs 75.5). On DomainNet, HRD edges out GPUE [20] (69.9 vs 69.6) and C-SFDA [12] (69.9 vs 69.0), with HRD++ reaching 70.3%. The ablation in Table 6 shows that relation distillation outperforms traditional logit-based knowledge distillation by 4.6% on VisDA (89.8 vs 85.2), validating the core hypothesis that structural relations provide more reliable supervision than single-sample predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) framework established by SHOT (Liang et al., TPAMI 2021), which introduced the teacher-student paradigm with diversity loss for adapting without source data. It departs from the dominant self-training paradigm in SFDA—where individual pseudo labels supervise the student—by reframing supervision as hierarchical relation distillation, drawing on knowledge distillation literature but replacing single-sample logit transfer with structural relations (local KNN affinity and global GMM-based semantic similarity). The class-balanced sample division echoes DaC (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2022), but unlike DaC's contrastive approach, HRD explicitly models inter-set relations to propagate reliable knowledge from source-like to target-specific samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that exploiting inter-sample structure (local clustering + global semantic distribution) provides substantially more robust supervision than individual pseudo labels, with a clean 4.6% gap over logit distillation on VisDA. This reframing—treating the target dataset's geometry as the supervision signal rather than per-sample predictions—could inform other self-training and test-time adaptation settings where label noise is a bottleneck. Limitations include reliance on ResNet backbones for image classification only, the memory bank storing all target features (potentially costly for very large target sets), and the assumption that prediction confidence reliably indicates sample quality, which may break down under severe domain shift.
