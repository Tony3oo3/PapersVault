## Problem

Estimating human and camera trajectories with accurate absolute scale in the world coordinate system from a single monocular video is ill-posed because 3D-to-2D projection discards depth information, rendering camera trajectories scaleless and camera-frame human depth estimates invalid. Existing EHPS methods operate entirely in camera coordinates and fail when the camera and subject move concurrently, while prior world-grounded approaches rely on expensive optimization or multi-sensor setups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240890/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video as input and requires only the camera focal length (in pixels) as an additional parameter, which can be read from device metadata or approximated via the diagonal pixel length. It produces full SMPL-X parameters (global orientation, root translation, body/hand/jaw poses, shape, and expression) in the world coordinate system, along with a scaled camera trajectory (rotation and translation per frame), all with absolute metric scale. The pipeline is regression-based and optimization-free, operating in real time rather than requiring iterative fitting.

## Method

WHAC exploits two key observations: (1) camera-frame SMPL-X estimators implicitly learn absolute human root depth when trained with a correct focal length, and (2) human motion sequences encode absolute spatial velocity cues. The pipeline first estimates camera-frame SMPL-X via a finetuned SMPLer-X (with added GRUs for temporal modeling) and a scaleless camera trajectory via DPVO visual odometry. A MotionVelocimeter network then predicts per-frame velocity from canonicalized 3D joint sequences, recovering the human trajectory with absolute scale. Finally, the scale is transferred to the camera trajectory by deriving a camera path from the scaled human trajectory and applying Umeyama alignment to combine VO's robust shape with the human-derived scale, yielding jointly consistent human and camera trajectories in the world frame.

## Results

On the world-grounded EMDB2 benchmark, WHAC achieves 39.4 mm PA-MPJPE, 389.4 mm W-MPJPE, and 142.2 mm WA-MPJPE, outperforming all baselines including WHAM (GT Gyro) at 41.9/436.4/165.9 and SLAHMR at 61.5/807.4/336.9. The human trajectory error (H-ATE) drops to 76.7 mm with an alignment scale of 1.1, compared to 83.2 mm (AS 1.5) for WHAM and 207.8 mm (AS 1.9) for SLAHMR. On camera-frame benchmarks, WHAC achieves a dramatic T-PVE improvement: 140.2 mm on EMDB1 versus 41,298 mm for SMPLer-X-B, confirming that WHAC recovers meaningful absolute human depth. On EgoBody, WHAC attains 46.9 mm PA-MPJPE and 11.6 m/s² acceleration error, surpassing SMPLer-X-B (47.1 mm, 18.9 m/s²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WHAC outperforms all six baselines across both world-grounded and camera-frame settings. Against the closest world-grounded competitor WHAM (GT Gyro) [35], WHAC reduces WA-MPJPE from 165.9 to 142.2 mm and H-ATE from 83.2 to 76.7 mm, while achieving a much better scale alignment (H-AS 1.1 vs. 1.5). Compared to SLAHMR [42], WHAC halves the WA-MPJPE (142.2 vs. 336.9 mm) and H-ATE (76.7 vs. 207.8 mm), and does so without expensive optimization. Against the adapted camera-frame baselines OSX-L + DPVO [22] and SMPLer-X-B + DPVO [5], WHAC reduces WA-MPJPE by 69% and 62% respectively, and brings the camera alignment scale from 5.1 down to 1.4, indicating near-perfect scale recovery. On camera-frame EgoBody, WHAC edges out SMPLer-X-B [5] on PA-MPJPE (46.9 vs. 47.1) and substantially improves acceleration error (11.6 vs. 18.9 m/s²) and face PVE (26.3 vs. 32.4 mm). The most striking gap is in T-PVE on EMDB1/3DPW, where WHAC achieves 140.2/260.8 mm versus 41,298/32,532 mm for SMPLer-X-B, confirming that WHAC's formulation of subject-to-camera distance yields physically meaningful depth estimates that prior methods completely lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

WHAC builds directly on SMPLer-X as its camera-frame EHPS backbone, adding GRUs for temporal modeling, and on DPVO for scaleless camera trajectory estimation. It departs from prior world-grounded methods (SLAHMR, PACE, WHAM) that rely on iterative optimization or recover only human trajectories, by formulating a fully regression-based pipeline that jointly recovers both human and camera trajectories with absolute scale. The key conceptual departure is the observation that camera-frame SMPL-X estimators implicitly learn absolute depth through their scale parameter, and that human motion velocity (via the proposed MotionVelocimeter) can serve as an independent scale anchor, eliminating the need for optimization-based alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WHAC provides the first optimization-free, real-time-capable framework for jointly recovering world-grounded human and camera trajectories, which is critical for applications like AR/VR, robotics, and video editing where both actors and cameras move. The WHAC-A-Mole dataset (1.46M instances with interactive motions and cinematic camera trajectories) fills a significant gap in benchmarks for world-grounded EHPS, as prior synthetic datasets (BEDLAM, SynBody) lack moving cameras and human-human interaction. Open questions include multi-person handling with occlusions, generalization beyond synthetic training data, and whether the implicit depth learning in camera-frame estimators is robust across diverse focal lengths and capture conditions.
