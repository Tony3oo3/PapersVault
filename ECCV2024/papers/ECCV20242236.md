## Problem

Few-shot image generation (FSIG) with only ~10 target-domain samples is dominated by GAN fine-tuning methods that suffer from overfitting, mode collapse, and catastrophic forgetting. Diffusion models, despite their superior generation quality, have not been successfully adapted to FSIG because they lack explicit latent codes and their larger parameter count makes fine-tuning even more prone to overfitting. The paper asks whether a training-free diffusion-based approach can match or exceed GAN-based FSIG while avoiding these failure modes.

## Input / Output

The method takes 10 (or as few as 1) 256×256 RGB target-domain images and a frozen DDPM noise network pre-trained on FFHQ, and produces a per-image Sample-wise Guidance Embedding (SGE) tensor that guides the reverse diffusion process. Generation uses DDIM with 25 inference steps, and the SGE can be time-dependent (active over η timesteps, η ∈ [1, T]) or time-independent (η = 1), with η controlling the trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and output diversity. The SGE is the sole learned artifact; the diffusion model weights remain frozen throughout.

## Method

CRDI decomposes FSIG into two training-free stages: (1) per-instance reconstruction, where a Sample-wise Guidance Embedding (SGE) is optimized against a combined x₀ and x_{t−1} reconstruction loss using the frozen noise network, with the forward noising step relaxed to allow stochastic intermediate states; and (2) diversity enhancement, where an annealing noise-perturbation schedule (adapted from Sadat et al.) progressively corrupts the SGE during generation to expand the output distribution. A mean-SGE penalty across all target samples regularizes individual embeddings toward a shared 'set-wise' direction, preventing implausible diversion. The degree of rigidity η determines how many timesteps the SGE actively constrains the reverse process, shifting its role from dictating pixel values (large η) to guiding pixel evolution (small η), thereby providing a tunable quality–diversity knob without any model fine-tuning.

## Results

On the primary FSIG benchmark (Table 2), CRDI achieves FID scores of 48.52 (Babies), 24.62 (Sunglasses), and 94.86 (MetFaces), with the best MC-SSIM of 0.78 on MetFaces, indicating superior mode coverage. CRDI outperforms the SOTA fine-tuning method RICK on Sunglasses (24.62 vs. 25.22 FID) and all representation-learning baselines (e.g., GenDA: 63.31/35.64/104.48 FID) across all three domains. For reconstruction (Table 1), CRDI with η=15 achieves SSIM scores of 0.74–0.84 across six target domains, consistently surpassing Image2StyleGAN (0.43–0.76). In the one-shot setting (Table 3), CRDI reduces FID from 105.13 to 100.85 (Babies) and from 83.70 to 74.60 (Sunglasses) compared to GenDA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA fine-tuning method RICK [62], CRDI wins on Sunglasses (24.62 vs. 25.22 FID) but trails on Babies (48.52 vs. 39.39) and MetFaces (94.86 vs. 48.53), where RICK exploits dominant sub-domains to lower FID at the cost of coverage. CRDI achieves the best MC-SSIM (0.78 vs. RICK's 0.69), confirming broader sub-domain coverage on MetFaces. Against the SOTA representation-learning method GenDA [30], CRDI dominates on all three domains (e.g., 48.52 vs. 63.31 FID on Babies; 24.62 vs. 35.64 on Sunglasses) and in the one-shot setting (100.85 vs. 105.13 FID on Babies). Compared to other fine-tuning baselines (FreezeD, EWC, CDC, RSSA, AdAM), CRDI achieves the best or near-best FID on Sunglasses and competitive FID on Babies, while avoiding the overfitting artifacts visible in those methods. Against foundation-model adaptation methods (LoRA, DreamBooth, Textual-Inversion in Table 4), CRDI achieves dramatically lower FID (e.g., 48.52 vs. 143.78 for LoRA on Babies), as those methods are designed for subject-level editing rather than domain-level generation. For reconstruction, CRDI (η=15) outperforms Image2StyleGAN [1] on all six evaluated domains (SSIM 0.71–0.84 vs. 0.43–0.76), with the largest gap on Bedrooms (0.84 vs. 0.52) and Sketches (0.71 vs. 0.43).

## Relation to Prior Work

CRDI departs from the dominant GAN-based FSIG paradigm (fine-tuning methods like RICK, AdAM, EWC, and representation learning like GenDA) by introducing the first training-free diffusion model approach to few-shot image generation. It builds on the score-based SDE framework (Song et al.) and DDPM/DDIM for the generative backbone, while borrowing the condition-annealed sampling schedule from CADS (Sadat et al.) for diversity enhancement. The key conceptual departure is the Sample-wise Guidance Embedding (SGE), which the authors show functions analogously to explicit GAN latent codes but operates within the implicit, iterative diffusion process—eliminating the need for any weight updates or latent manifold construction that prior methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that diffusion models can handle FSIG without fine-tuning, CRDI sidesteps the overfitting and catastrophic forgetting that plague weight-adaptation approaches, and the degree-of-rigidity parameter η provides a practical, interpretable knob for trading off reconstruction fidelity against generation diversity. However, the method's FID is not uniformly best (RICK still wins on Babies and MetFaces), and the approach struggles with structurally dissimilar domains like sketches, suggesting the SGE's capacity is bounded by source-model prior compatibility. Open questions include whether SGE-based guidance scales to larger foundation diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and whether the training-free paradigm can be extended to non-face domains without the source-target similarity constraint that currently limits applicability.
