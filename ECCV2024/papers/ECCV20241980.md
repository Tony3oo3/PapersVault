## Problem

Point cloud frame interpolation requires accurate scene flow estimation while preserving 3D geometric structure, but existing methods face a fundamental trade-off: PointINet relies on a fixed pre-trained motion estimator and ignores structure consistency, producing noisy results, while NeuralPCI achieves higher accuracy through per-scene neural radiance field optimization at the cost of ~60 seconds per frame. No prior feedforward method simultaneously enforces structure-aware motion, cycle consistency, and fast inference.

## Input / Output

FastPCI takes two consecutive LiDAR point cloud frames of 8192 points each (at 2 Hz temporal resolution) and produces an interpolated point cloud at any arbitrary time t ∈ (0,1) between them. In practice, three uniformly spaced intermediate frames are estimated per input pair. The entire pipeline is a single feedforward pass with no per-scene optimization, completing in ~109 ms per frame on an RTX 3090.

## Method

FastPCI employs a three-stage Pyramid Convolution-Transformer network where each stage contains a Motion-Structure Transformer block that jointly estimates motion and structure features via a Dual-Direction Cross-Attention: forward features serve as queries while backward features serve as keys and values, making the estimated motion explicitly structure-aware. A Motion Compensation Block then refines the motion by fusing enhanced motion and structure features through 1D convolutions and MLPs, and a 3-stage U-Net RefineNet (with Point Transformer blocks) polishes the forward-warp estimate before fusion with the backward estimate. Training uses a composite loss combining Chamfer Distance on the final output, half-way cycle-consistency losses on both forward and backward intermediate estimates, and a multiscale pyramid reconstruction loss that provides hierarchical supervision at each resolution level. This design achieves 26.6% and 18.3% CD reduction over NeuralPCI and PointINet on KITTI while running 600× and 10× faster respectively.

## Results

On KITTI odometry, FastPCI achieves an average CD of 0.58 and EMD of 53.78, reducing CD by 26.6% over PointINet (0.79) and 18.3% over NeuralPCI (0.71). On Argoverse 2 and NuScenes, FastPCI similarly leads with average CD/EMD of 0.73/57.95 and 1.11/173.64 respectively. In terms of speed, FastPCI interpolates a frame in 109 ms, making it ~13× faster than PointINet (1458 ms) and ~558× faster than NeuralPCI (60822 ms) on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastPCI outperforms all four baselines on average CD and EMD across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor NeuralPCI (which uses per-scene optimization), FastPCI reduces average CD by 0.13 on KITTI (0.58 vs 0.71), 0.02 on Argoverse 2 (0.73 vs 0.75), and 0.04 on NuScenes (1.11 vs 1.15), while also being ~558× faster (109 ms vs 60822 ms per frame). Against PointINet, the gap is larger: FastPCI cuts average CD by 0.21 on KITTI and 0.44 on NuScenes, and is ~13× faster. The two earlier baselines, PointPWC-Net and NSFP, are substantially outperformed on every metric and dataset (e.g., NSFP's average EMD on KITTI is 90.24 vs FastPCI's 53.78). The only minor losses are on NuScenes Frame-1 CD (1.02 vs NeuralPCI's 1.00) and Frame-2 EMD (205.75 vs 205.24), which do not affect the overall ranking.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastPCI positions itself against two dominant paradigms in point cloud frame interpolation: PointINet's reliance on a fixed pre-trained scene flow estimator followed by fusion, and NeuralPCI's per-scene optimization of a dynamic neural radiance field. It departs from both by learning motion and structure jointly in a single feedforward Pyramid Convolution-Transformer, drawing architectural inspiration from PointNet++'s hierarchical downsampling and Point Transformer's attention mechanism, while adapting the dual-direction cross-attention concept from video frame interpolation (EMA-VFI, IFRNet) to the unordered point cloud setting. The key conceptual departure is treating motion estimation as structure-aware—jointly predicting displacement and geometric structure via bidirectional feature interaction—rather than estimating motion in isolation as prior methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 600× speedup over NeuralPCI and 10× over PointINet (109 ms per frame) makes high-quality point cloud interpolation viable for real-time autonomous driving pipelines (tracking, navigation) where per-scene optimization is prohibitive. The pyramid Convolution-Transformer architecture with dual-direction cross-attention offers a reusable pattern for other point cloud tasks requiring both local detail and global reasoning. However, the authors acknowledge that robustness to outliers, occlusion, and varying environmental conditions remains untested, and real-world deployment adaptability is left open. Whether the structure-aware motion formulation generalizes beyond the 8192-point, 2 Hz regime used in experiments is an open question.
