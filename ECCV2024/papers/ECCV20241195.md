## Problem

Multimodal LLMs remain heavily concentrated on vision-language tasks, with limited exploration of audio, video, and 3D modalities and a critical scarcity of instruction-tuning data—especially for joint multi-modality inputs. It is unclear whether cross-modal reasoning (integrating or discriminating across 2+ non-linguistic modalities) can emerge from independently aligning each modality to a frozen LLM, or whether expensive joint modality training is required.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241195/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts arbitrary combinations of RGB images, point clouds, audio waveforms, and video clips alongside a text instruction. For sequential modalities (video, audio), each frame is encoded and processed separately by the projection module, yielding N×k query tokens (k=32 per frame, hidden dim 768). Outputs are free-form text: descriptive captions, open-ended QA responses, closed/open-vocabulary class labels, or in the DisCRn task, a positional selection (first/second) identifying which of two modality inputs satisfies a queried property.

## Method

X-InstructBLIP independently fine-tunes a modality-specific projection module (either an instruction-aware Q-Former initialized from BLIP-2 stage-1 weights, or a single linear projection layer) for each modality while keeping the LLM (Vicuna 7b/13b) frozen. Each modality uses its own pre-trained encoder—EVA-CLIP-ViT-G/14 for image/video, BEATs for audio, ULIP-2 (PointBERT backbone) for 3D—and the Q-Former variant inserts cross-attention layers every other transformer block to fuse modality embeddings with instruction text and learnable query tokens. A key enabler is a three-stage data augmentation pipeline that leverages flan-t5-xxl to automatically generate ~250K 3D QA and ~24K audio QA instruction-tuning pairs from existing captioning datasets (Cap3D, AudioCaps). The paper's central finding is that discriminative and joint cross-modal reasoning emerges without any joint modality training: the Q-Former projection shows superior single-modality performance and the ability to switch between joint and discriminative reasoning across modalities, while the linear projection generalizes better under limited task-modality data.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D classification (ModelNet40), X-Instruct Proj. (7b) achieves 62.8% closed-vocabulary and 49.4% open-generation accuracy, substantially outperforming PointBind-LLM (47.3%/36.3%) and InstructBLIP (31.4%/23.7%). On audio, X-Instruct Proj. (7b) reaches 75.9% ESC50 closed accuracy and 38.2% open accuracy, far exceeding ImageBind-LLM (40.1%/27.4%). For image tasks, X-Instruct Proj. (13b) attains 1174.0 MME and 35.1 MMVet, while the 7b variant scores 82.1 CIDEr on Flickr30k and 48.1 GQA. In emergent cross-modal joint reasoning (MusicAVQA, VATEX), X-Instruct Proj. (7b) shows positive synergy (Δ=+1.3, +1.7) over single-modality baselines, whereas X-LLaVA-style projection shows no gain or degradation (Δ=0.0, −10.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3D (ModelNet40), X-Instruct Proj. (7b) outperforms PointBind-LLM by 15.5 points (closed) and 13.1 points (open), and InstructBLIP by 31.4 points (closed), demonstrating that dedicated 3D encoders with modality-specific Q-Formers substantially exceed unified-embedding approaches. On audio, X-Instruct Proj. (7b) beats ImageBind-LLM by 35.8 points on ESC50 closed accuracy (75.9 vs. 40.1) and 3.5 points on open accuracy, but trails Pengi on ClothoAQA (21.4 vs. 64.5) and Clotho captioning CIDEr (29.4 vs. 39.6), indicating that audio-specific instruction data remains a bottleneck. On image tasks, X-Instruct Proj. (7b) is slightly below InstructBLIP on Flickr30k (82.1 vs. 84.5) and NoCaps (117.7 vs. 123.1) due to the absence of BLIP-2 Stage-2 finetuning, but surpasses it on MMVet (29.0 vs. 26.2); the 13b variant leads on VizWiz (36.0 vs. 34.5) and MMVet (35.1 vs. 26.2) while remaining below on MME (1174.0 vs. 1212.8). On silent video, X-Instruct Proj. (7b) matches or exceeds VideoLLaMA on MSVDQA (51.7 vs. 51.6) and VATEX (59.2 vs. 57.6) without any video pretraining stage. In cross-modal discriminative reasoning (DisCRn), both X-InstructBLIP variants outperform the captioning baseline (30.8/41.8) by 13–7 points, with X-Instruct Proj. (13b) achieving the best image-3D score (48.8) and X-LLaVA-style leading on audio-video (47.1), confirming that individual modality strength transfers to cross-modal performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the InstructBLIP/BLIP-2 Q-Former paradigm and the LLaVA linear projection paradigm from image-language alignment to an arbitrary set of modalities (3D, audio, video) by independently fine-tuning modality-specific projections onto a frozen LLM. It deliberately departs from joint-modality training approaches (X-LLM, ChatBridge, Audio-Visual LLM) and unified-representation methods (ImageBind-LLM, PandaGPT) by maintaining separate encoders and per-modality projections throughout, arguing this avoids training instability from disparately aligned projections. The key conceptual departure is demonstrating that cross-modal reasoning can emerge from purely unimodal instruction tuning without any joint modality data, a claim not made by prior projection-based or unified-pretraining frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, scalable recipe for adding new modalities to a frozen LLM without modality-specific pretraining, and the contributed 250K 3D QA and 24K audio QA datasets address a real data scarcity gap. However, the emergent cross-modal reasoning demonstrated on DisCRn remains far from human performance (45–48% vs. 90%), and no conclusive winner emerges between Q-Former and linear projection for discriminative cross-modal tasks, leaving open which architectural choice best supports multi-modal reasoning. The finding that Q-Formers can flexibly switch between joint and discriminative reasoning modes while linear projections cannot is a useful design insight, but the practical utility of the emergent ability at current accuracy levels is limited.
