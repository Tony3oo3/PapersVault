## Problem

Existing neural surface reconstruction methods rely on photometric consistency computed directly in RGB image space, which is fragile under occlusions and non-Lambertian surfaces. It remains unclear which pretext visual tasks yield the most effective feature priors for NSR, and whether patch-wise photometric consistency can be meaningfully extended beyond raw pixels to learned feature representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241560/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images (downsampled to 512×768 for cross-model comparison) along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a continuous signed distance function (SDF) parameterized by an MLP geometry network. Surface points are extracted via differentiable zero-crossing of the SDF along rays, and the SDF is regularized with an eikonal loss. The approach is also extended to grid-based representations (Neuralangelo), showing the feature-prior loss is representation-agnostic.

## Method

Building on NeuS, the paper introduces a feature-level photometric consistency loss that warps surface points (located at SDF zero-crossings) from the reference view to source views via homography and compares pre-trained features at the projected locations. The authors systematically evaluate 13 pre-trained models spanning 7 pretext tasks (masked image modeling, image classification, semantic segmentation, monocular depth estimation, stereo matching, image matching, and MVS) using both pixel-wise cosine similarity and patch-wise NCC/SSIM losses. Key findings: MVS and image matching features dominate because their training provides explicit feature-similarity supervision and produces high-resolution (1/2 to 1/1) feature maps; 3D cost-volume regularization in MVS/stereo models degrades 2D feature quality; and patch-wise feature consistency outperforms pixel-wise due to larger receptive fields that handle occlusions. A Top-k view selection strategy (k=4) adaptively picks the best source views for patch-wise loss. The resulting MVS-NeuS (MVSFormer features) and Match-NeuS (QuadTree features) achieve state-of-the-art Chamfer Distance on DTU and EPFL.

## Results

On the DTU dataset (15 scenes), MVS-NeuS achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.548 mm and Match-NeuS achieves 0.565 mm, both outperforming the closest competitor GeoNeuS* (0.59 mm) and the baseline NeuS (0.87 mm) by a substantial margin. On the EPFL dataset, Match-NeuS with patch NCC achieves a mean CD of 1.414 mm, compared to 1.97 mm for NeuralWarp and 3.677 mm for NeuS. The paper also demonstrates that patch-wise feature-level consistency (NCC/SSIM) outperforms pixel-wise cosine similarity for both MVS-NeuS and Match-NeuS on EPFL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVS-NeuS (0.548 mm) and Match-NeuS (0.565 mm) achieve the best mean Chamfer Distance on DTU, outperforming the strongest prior method GeoNeuS* (0.59 mm) by ~7% and D-NeuS (0.61 mm) by ~10%. Both methods also surpass NeuralWarp (0.68 mm), PET-NeuS (0.71 mm), and the NeuS baseline (0.87 mm) by large margins. On EPFL, Match-NeuS with patch NCC (1.414 mm) beats MVS-NeuS (1.444 mm), NeuralWarp (1.97 mm), and NeuS (3.677 mm), with the gap over NeuS being particularly dramatic (~2.6x). The paper notes that MVS-NeuS benefits from potential data leakage since MVSFormer is pre-trained on DTU, while Match-NeuS (pre-trained on MegaDepth) generalizes better to the unseen EPFL scenes. COLMAP (0.65 mm) and MVSDF (0.88 mm) represent traditional pipeline baselines that are also outperformed, confirming the advantage of the feature-prior-enhanced neural approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuS [42] as its geometric backbone and extends the photometric-consistency line of work (NeuralWarp [14], GeoNeuS [9], D-NeuS [6]) by replacing raw RGB or single-model MVS features with a systematic comparison of 13 pre-trained feature extractors spanning 7 pretext tasks (MIM, classification, segmentation, depth, stereo, matching, MVS). The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) treating feature-level consistency as a design space to be explored empirically rather than assuming a single feature source suffices, and (2) extending patch-wise photometric consistency (previously applied only to RGB) to the feature level, which the authors show yields substantial gains. The method is orthogonal to the choice of neural representation (MLP-based NeuS vs. grid-based Neuralangelo), positioning it as a plug-in loss rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive empirical map of which pretext-task features are useful for NSR, giving practitioners concrete guidance (MVS and image-matching features with high resolution; avoid 3D cost-volume regularization) rather than leaving the choice ad hoc. The finding that 3D-CNN cost-volume denoising actively degrades 2D feature quality is a useful caution for anyone repurposing MVS/stereo encoders. Limitations include potential data leakage on DTU (MVS models pre-trained on the same dataset), evaluation restricted to two relatively small benchmarks, and a heuristic Top-k view-selection strategy whose sensitivity is not fully characterized. Open questions include whether the findings transfer to dynamic or unposed settings, whether the benefit persists at larger scene scales, and whether learned (rather than frozen) feature adapters could close the remaining gap to COLMAP-level accuracy.
