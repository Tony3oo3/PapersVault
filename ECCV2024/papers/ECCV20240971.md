## Problem

Existing controllable human motion synthesis methods either support only high-level language conditioning or require complete end-effector trajectories for inverse kinematics, leaving a gap for precise multi-joint trajectory control combined with semantic language guidance. The closest prior work, OmniControl, struggles to accurately align generated motion with specified trajectories—especially when controlling multiple joints simultaneously—and suffers from slow multi-step diffusion sampling that limits real-time interactivity.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a natural language description (encoded via frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32) and partial 3D joint trajectories specifying positions for a user-chosen subset of joints at each of T=196 frames. Trajectories can be incomplete in both time (masked segments) and joints (only some of the six control groups specified), supporting inputs from analytical shape parameters, 3D hand drawings, or environment settings. The output is a full-body 3D motion sequence of T frames represented as joint rotations and positions in the HumanML3D/KIT-ML format.

## Method

TLControl operates in a part-structured discrete latent space learned by a VQ-VAE that disaggregates the skeleton into six body-part groups (Head, Left/Right arm, Left/Right leg, Root), each with a dedicated encoder and codebook, yielding a compact and topology-aware representation. A Masked Trajectory Transformer (MTT) then performs feed-forward coarse prediction of quantized code indices conditioned on CLIP text embeddings and masked partial trajectories, trained with continuous trajectory masking and joint-level masking to simulate incomplete user specifications, and uses Gumbel-Softmax to enable diverse sampling. At test time, an L-BFGS optimization refines the MTT's initial code prediction in the latent space to minimize the distance between decoded joint positions and the user-specified control trajectories, achieving sub-centimeter accuracy in ~0.015 s/frame—roughly 40× faster than OmniControl—while preserving language semantics.

## Results

On HumanML3D, TLControl achieves 0.00% trajectory error and 0.00% location error (50 cm threshold) when controlling the pelvis, with an average joint error of only 1.08 cm, while OmniControl reports 3.87% and 0.96% respectively. For the challenging all-six-joints control setting, TLControl maintains 0.00% trajectory error and 1.57 cm average error, whereas OmniControl degrades to 75.59% trajectory error and 23.67 cm average error. TLControl also attains the highest R-precision (0.794) in the all-joints setting, indicating strong language alignment. In terms of runtime, TLControl processes at 0.015 s/frame on an RTX 3080 Ti, roughly 40× faster than OmniControl (0.606 s/frame) and 13× faster than MDM (0.192 s/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLControl decisively outperforms all four baselines on trajectory accuracy: it achieves 0.00% trajectory and location error (50 cm threshold) on HumanML3D for both single-joint (pelvis) and all-six-joints control, compared to OmniControl's 3.87%/0.96% (pelvis) and 75.59%/12.30% (all joints), GMD's 9.31%/3.21%, PriorMDM's 34.57%/21.32%, and MDM's 40.22%/30.76%. On the KIT-ML dataset, TLControl similarly dominates with 0.28% trajectory error vs. OmniControl's 11.05% and GMD's 54.43% for pelvis control. TLControl also achieves the highest R-precision (0.779 pelvis, 0.794 all-joints on HumanML3D; 0.757 pelvis, 0.751 average on KIT-ML), indicating superior language-motion alignment, while OmniControl trails at 0.687 and 0.606 respectively. The one metric where OmniControl edges ahead is FID in the pelvis-only setting (0.218 vs. 0.271), though TLControl's FID is still well below all other baselines. In runtime, TLControl is an order of magnitude faster than every baseline (0.015 s/frame vs. 0.192–0.606 s/frame), making it the only method practical for interactive use.

## Relation to Prior Work

TLControl builds on the VQ-VAE discretization framework (Van Den Oord et al.) and the T2M-GPT encoder/decoder architecture, but departs from prior whole-body tokenization by introducing a part-based VQ-VAE with separate codebooks per body region (head, arms, legs, root). It positions itself against the diffusion-model line of work (MDM, GMD, OmniControl) that attempts to combine language and spatial control through multi-step denoising with guidance, instead adopting a two-stage pipeline: a Masked Trajectory Transformer for coarse feed-forward prediction in a compact discrete latent space, followed by a lightweight L-BFGS optimization for precise trajectory alignment. The key conceptual departure is treating trajectory control as a post-hoc optimization problem in a structured latent space rather than a conditioning signal embedded in a generative diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× runtime advantage over OmniControl (0.015 vs. 0.606 s/frame) makes precise multi-joint trajectory control practical for interactive animation, gaming, and AR/VR pipelines where diffusion-based methods are too slow. The part-based latent structure is a reusable architectural idea that could transfer to other motion or character animation tasks requiring per-body-part controllability. A key open question the authors acknowledge is handling conflicts between language semantics and user-specified trajectories, which they explicitly exclude from scope; resolving this would be necessary for truly open-ended interactive use. The assumption that input trajectories are physically feasible also limits generality to scenarios where users provide anatomically valid paths.
