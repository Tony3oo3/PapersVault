## Problem

Prior work on mitigating spurious correlations by augmenting biased datasets with synthetic data (e.g., USB, ASB) trains on a mixed real-synthetic distribution, which introduces a new, unaddressed bias toward the pair (B, G) — the original bias variable and the real/synthetic data source. The paper proves that no augmentation strategy can eliminate this bias, meaning models can exploit generative artifacts to make predictions rather than learning task-relevant features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241548/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images in two separate training stages: balanced synthetic images generated by Stable Diffusion V1.4 (via text prompts like 'A photo of {bias} {class}') in Stage 1, and real images from CelebA-HQ, UTK-Face, or SpuCo Animals in Stage 2. The ResNet-50 model outputs a class label for the target attribute (e.g., Smiling, Gender, animal type), evaluated via worst-group accuracy and balanced accuracy across bias subgroups at multiple bias ratios (90%–99.9%).

## Method

From Fake to Real (FFR) is a two-stage training pipeline that separates synthetic and real data into distinct phases to prevent the model from exploiting distributional differences between them. In Stage 1, a ResNet-50 is pretrained with ERM on a balanced synthetic dataset where each bias subgroup (intersection of class Y and bias group B) has equal representation, learning robust, unbiased initial features. In Stage 2, the model is fine-tuned on the biased real dataset using ERM or loss-based bias mitigation methods (GroupDRO, DFR, Resampling), which now only need to address the original bias toward B rather than the doubled (B, G) bias. The key mechanism is that because the model never sees real and synthetic data in the same training step, it cannot learn to use generative artifacts or other source-discriminative signals, effectively decoupling the two sources of bias.

## Results

FFR improves worst-group accuracy over prior synthetic augmentation methods (USB and ASB) by up to 20% across three datasets (CelebA-HQ, UTK-Face, SpuCo Animals) and five bias ratios (90%–99.9%). On SpuCo Animals, FFR achieves 72.6% worst accuracy on real data and 89.2% on synthetic data, compared to 59.5%/68.7% for USB and 48.9%/80.7% for ASB. When combined with synthetic-data-free methods, FFR boosts Resampling and GroupDRO worst accuracy by 13% each and DFR by 4%, while FFR alone (70.3% avg. worst accuracy) already exceeds ASB+GroupDRO (69.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFR consistently outperforms both USB (Mondal et al., AISTATS 2023) and ASB (Ramaswamy et al., CVPR 2021) on worst-group accuracy across all three datasets and all bias ratios, with the largest gap on UTK-Face at 95% bias where FFR exceeds both by over 10%. On SpuCo Animals, FFR achieves 72.6% real-data worst accuracy versus 59.5% for USB and 48.9% for ASB, and also shows superior synthetic-data performance (89.2% vs. 68.7% and 80.7%), indicating FFR avoids the (B,G) bias that causes prior methods to underperform on both data sources. Against GroupDRO (Sagawa et al., ICLR 2020), FFR alone (70.3% avg. worst accuracy) already surpasses ASB+GroupDRO (69.0%), and combining FFR with GroupDRO yields a further ~3-point improvement. Against DFR (Kirichenko et al., ICLR 2023), FFR improves worst accuracy by 4%, with the largest gains (13%) seen when FFR is paired with Resampling or GroupDRO. The paper attributes FFR's advantage to its two-stage design, which eliminates the need to optimize over doubled bias groups (B,G) that USB and ASB introduce when combined with loss-based mitigation methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the joint-training paradigm of prior synthetic augmentation methods (USB, ASB) that mix real and synthetic data in a single training loop. It repurposes the idea of balanced synthetic data generation but restructures the training into a two-stage pipeline (pretrain on synthetic, fine-tune on real), thereby avoiding the model's exposure to real-synthetic distributional differences. Theoretically, it builds on the spurious correlation literature (Sagawa et al., Kirichenko et al.) by proving that any joint augmentation strategy necessarily introduces a new bias toward the (bias, data-source) pair, and practically it complements loss-based bias mitigation methods (GroupDRO, DFR, Resampling) by providing a stronger initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Theorem 1 provides a clean, general argument that no amount of synthetic data balancing in a joint training setup can eliminate bias toward the real/synthetic data-source variable, which reframes how practitioners should think about synthetic augmentation for fairness. The practical impact is significant (up to 20% worst-group accuracy improvement across three datasets and five bias levels), and the method is simple enough to adopt broadly. Key open questions include whether the approach degrades with weaker or less controllable generative models, how biases inherent to the generative model itself (e.g., Stable Diffusion's known demographic biases) interact with the pipeline, and whether the two-stage separation generalizes to other distributional shifts beyond real-vs-synthetic.
