## Problem

Audio-Visual Semantic Segmentation (AVSS) requires a model to simultaneously learn audio-visual alignment and semantic understanding, but previous end-to-end methods suffer from ambiguous supervisory signals (e.g., a background pixel may be truly background or a silent semantic object) and insufficient optimization of both subtasks. This leads to sub-optimal performance where models trained under the AVSS setting exhibit weaker sound source localization than those trained under the simpler AVS setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241957/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (T frames) and a corresponding audio signal as input. Video frames are encoded by a frozen Swin-B backbone with FPN pixel decoder producing multi-scale features at 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, and 1/16 resolutions; audio is encoded by a VGGish model pre-trained on AudioSet into a T×256 feature vector. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation map at original resolution (T×H×W) over 71 object classes plus a null class, produced via per-mask classification where N predicted masks are each assigned a semantic label through Hungarian matching. In the two-stage setting, Stage 1 additionally outputs a binary sound-source localization mask that serves as prior knowledge for Stage 2.

## Method

The AAVS framework builds on Mask2Former with two key architectural innovations: (1) an Adaptive Audio Query Generator that uses N_q learnable audio prototypes to dynamically weight the shared audio feature via cosine similarity before adding it to object queries, introducing no extra parameters while improving audio discriminability; and (2) masked cross-attention in the transformer decoder that lets each query adaptively restrict its attention region over visual features. The core training contribution is the Stepping Stones two-stage strategy: Stage 1 trains a standard AVS model with binary labels to learn fine-grained audio-visual alignment; Stage 2 trains the AVSS model with semantic labels while injecting Stage 1 localization results as prior knowledge in three ways — fusing the binary mask with the mask feature in the prediction head (a shortcut for recognizing sounding regions), initializing the masked-attention mask with the Stage 1 result, and a Robust Audio-aware Key Generator that thresholds the Stage 1 mask into silent/uncertain/sounding categories to modulate cross-attention keys, thereby compensating for localization errors and allowing the model to focus exclusively on semantic discrimination.

## Results

On AVSBench-Object, AAVS achieves 83.2 mIoU and 91.3 F-score on S4, and 67.3 mIoU and 77.6 F-score on MS3, outperforming all prior methods. On the more challenging AVSBench-Semantic (AVSS) subtask, AAVS with the Stepping Stones training strategy reaches 48.5 mIoU and 53.2 F-score, surpassing the previous best (AVSegFormer at 36.7 mIoU / 42.0 F-score) by 11.8 and 11.2 points respectively. The ablation with oracle first-stage labels pushes AVSS mIoU to 64.9, indicating substantial headroom as localization accuracy improves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AAVS outperforms all 12 baselines on every subtask where they are compared. On S4, AAVS leads the previous best AVSegFormer [6] (82.1 mIoU) by 1.1 mIoU and CATR [15] (81.4) by 1.8 mIoU. On MS3, the gap widens: AAVS (67.3 mIoU) surpasses MUTR [35] (65.0) by 2.3 mIoU and GAVS [31] (63.7) by 3.6 mIoU, with the largest margin over AVSBench [38] (54.0) at 13.3 mIoU. On AVSS, the improvement is most dramatic—AAVS with Stepping Stones (48.5 mIoU) beats AVSegFormer [6] (36.7) by 11.8 mIoU and CATR [15] (32.8) by 15.7 mIoU. The generalization experiments (Table 2) further show that applying Stepping Stones to AVSBench [38] and AVSegFormer [6] yields consistent gains (e.g., AVSegFormer improves from 36.7 to 46.3 mIoU with oracle first-stage labels), confirming the strategy's transferability. No baseline matches or exceeds AAVS on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mask2Former's masked-attention transformer decoder and per-mask classification framework, adapting them to the audio-visual domain with an adaptive audio query generator that replaces the simple audio-feature repetition used in prior AVS methods (AQFormer, CATR, AuTR). The task formulation and feature extraction pipeline follow AVSBench, while the progressive two-stage training strategy draws conceptual inspiration from the coarse-to-fine multi-source localization framework of Qian et al. (ECCV 2020). The key departure from prior AVSS work is the explicit decomposition of the joint alignment-plus-semantics objective into sequential subtasks, motivated by the observation that end-to-end training under mixed supervision leads to sub-optimal audio-visual alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Stepping Stones strategy offers a practical, generalizable recipe for improving AVSS performance across existing architectures without architectural redesign, which is valuable for the small AVS research community. However, the substantial gap between pseudo-label (48.5 mIoU) and oracle (64.9 mIoU) results on AVSS reveals that the strategy's ceiling is tightly coupled to first-stage localization accuracy, leaving significant room for improvement as better AVS models emerge. The finding that low-quality first-stage results can actually degrade second-stage performance (27.3 vs. 29.8 mIoU for AVSBench) highlights a robustness concern that remains open. Broader implications include whether this progressive decomposition principle generalizes to other multimodal tasks where alignment and higher-level understanding must be learned jointly.
