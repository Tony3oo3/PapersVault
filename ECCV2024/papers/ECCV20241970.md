## Problem

Existing text-guided 3D editing methods rely on iterative mechanisms (score distillation sampling or iterative dataset updates) to reconcile inconsistent per-view edits from monocular 2D editors, making each edit take minutes to hours. The root cause is that 2D diffusion editors like InstructPix2Pix produce independent, statistically inconsistent edits across views, so the 3D model must be repeatedly re-fitted to gradually converge on a multi-view consensus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241970/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting model and a free-form text editing prompt, optionally augmented with a 2D segmentation mask (unprojected to 3D) for partial editing of specific scene regions. It renders 20–30 views from the model, edits them jointly, and outputs a directly re-fitted 3D Gaussian Splatting model in approximately 4 minutes. The 3D fitting uses LPIPS and L1 losses over 500–1500 optimization iterations, with classifier-free guidance (7.5 for text, 1.5 for image) controlling edit strength.

## Method

DGE decomposes 3D editing into two stages: multi-view consistent image editing followed by direct 3D fitting. For consistent editing, it treats the rendered view sequence as an orbital video and modifies IP2P with a spatio-temporal self-attention block applied to a random subset of key views at each denoising step, so that key views attend to each other jointly. Features from key views are then propagated to remaining views via correspondence matching constrained to epipolar lines (computed from known camera poses), reducing the search space from a plane to a line and leveraging 3D geometry where appearance cues are ambiguous. For 3D fitting, the edited views are used as a multi-view reconstruction target: the Gaussian parameters are optimized directly against the edited images using LPIPS loss, eliminating the need for SDS or iterative dataset replacement. An optional iterative refinement loop re-renders, re-edits, and re-fits to recover fine textures, and partial editing is achieved by masking Gaussians so only selected primitives are updated.

## Results

On 3 scenes × 10 prompts, DGE achieves the best CLIP similarity (0.226) and CLIP directional similarity (0.67) among all compared methods, while also being the fastest at ~4 min per edit. This represents a ~13× speedup over InstructNeRF2NeRF (~51 min) and nearly 2× speedup over the next-fastest GS-based method, GaussianEditor (~7 min). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that multi-view consistent editing contributes the largest gain (CLIP sim 0.203→0.226), with epipolar constraints providing an additional small but visually meaningful improvement in fine texture fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGE outperforms all four baselines on both CLIP similarity and CLIP directional similarity. Against InstructNeRF2NeRF [25] (NeRF-based, iterative dataset updates), DGE improves CLIP directional similarity from 0.64 to 0.67 and reduces editing time from ~51 min to ~4 min (~13×). Against ViCA-NeRF [16], which also targets multi-view consistency but via depth-based feature projection, DGE achieves substantially higher CLIP directional similarity (0.67 vs 0.44) and is ~7× faster, while also handling geometry-changing edits that ViCA-NeRF cannot. Against GaussianEditor [13] (the closest GS-based competitor using iterative dataset updates), DGE gains +0.025 CLIP similarity and +0.07 CLIP directional similarity while cutting time from ~7 min to ~4 min; qualitatively, DGE produces sharper textures (e.g., Venetian mask) where GaussianEditor's iterative averaging leads to blur. Against the IP2P+SDS baseline [94,69], which uses the same GS representation but score distillation sampling, DGE improves both metrics (0.226 vs 0.206; 0.67 vs 0.61) and is ~1.5× faster, confirming that direct fitting to multi-view consistent edits is superior to SDS-based optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGE builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as an efficient explicit 3D representation and InstructPix2Pix as a text-guided 2D editor, but departs fundamentally from the iterative paradigms (iterative dataset updates à la InstructNeRF2NeRF/GaussianEditor, or score distillation sampling à la DreamFusion/Vox-E) that dominate prior text-guided 3D editing. Its key conceptual move is to borrow spatio-temporal attention from the training-free video-editing literature (TokenFlow, Pix2Video) and augment it with epipolar constraints derived from known camera geometry, thereby producing multi-view consistent edits in a single pass. This positions DGE as a direct-fitting alternative to the incremental consensus-building that prior methods require, and it extends ViCA-NeRF's multi-view consistency idea by replacing depth-based feature projection with epipolar-line-constrained correspondence matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing a single 3D edit to roughly 4 minutes (vs. ~7 min for GaussianEditor and ~51 min for InstructNeRF2NeRF) while improving CLIP fidelity, DGE makes instruction-based 3D editing practically usable for interactive workflows. The explicit GS representation also enables natural partial editing of scene subsets, a capability that implicit NeRF-based methods struggle with. Limitations include residual inconsistencies in fine textures that require iterative refinement passes, dependence on the quality of the underlying IP2P editor, and evaluation limited to a small number of scenes and prompts. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or unbounded scenes, whether it can handle edits that substantially alter geometry, and whether the epipolar constraint generalizes when camera poses are only approximately known.
