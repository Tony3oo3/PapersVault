## Problem

Point-based neural re-rendering methods like NPBG were designed for ideal conditions (synthetic scenes, well-captured human heads) and degrade severely on generic in-the-wild data due to patchy triangulation, incorrect point visibility, and unbounded backgrounds. No unified point-based pipeline handles diverse scene types—free-trajectory, inside-out, large-scale, and sparse-view—without case-by-case parameterization, unlike RF-based methods that require different strategies per scene type.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240399/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed RGB images with calibrated intrinsics and extrinsics, from which a 3D point cloud is triangulated via COLMAP (sparse) and a trained MVS network (dense). During training, images are randomly cropped to 256×256 patches; the point cloud is rasterized non-differentiably via hard z-buffering into a neural buffer, which the CNN renderer then maps to RGB. A single learnable 1×8 feature vector serves as the default value for vacant (background) pixels in the fragment, replacing any explicit environment map.

## Method

RPBG replaces NPBG's vanilla U-Net renderer with a multi-scale fusion architecture augmented by a Downgrade-aware Convolution (DAC) module that runs Fast Fourier Convolution (global receptive field) in parallel with local convolutions, then applies gated convolution to adaptively suppress erroneously visible back-face points and inpaint sparse rasterizations. Environment modeling is reduced from a dense H×W×C sphere to a single learnable 1×8 vector, and an optional point cloud augmentation step uses pseudo-density (absolute activation of the neural texture) to verify and densify poorly triangulated regions. All parameters—the per-point neural texture (dim 8), the environment vector, and the renderer weights—are optimized jointly end-to-end with a composite loss of Huber, VGG perceptual, and FFT terms, eliminating the phased training of prior work. The entire pipeline uses a single unified parameterization across all scene types with no per-scene hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

RPBG achieves the best SSIM and LPIPS across all four scene categories in Table 1. On the Free dataset (7 free-trajectory scenes), it reaches 26.33 PSNR, 0.832 SSIM, and 0.177 LPIPS; on GigaMVS (8 sparse-view scenes), 20.54 PSNR, 0.686 SSIM, and 0.317 LPIPS; on Mill19 (2 large-scale aerial scenes), 22.62 PSNR, 0.596 SSIM, and 0.368 LPIPS. Notably, RPBG trains in 29 GPU hours on Mill19 versus 240 GPU hours for Mega-NeRF, and is the only method that can handle both F²-NeRF and Gaussian Splatting's memory constraints on large-scale scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against F²-NeRF (the strongest RF-based baseline for free-trajectory scenes), RPBG matches PSNR on the Free dataset (26.33 vs 26.32) but substantially outperforms it on SSIM (0.832 vs 0.779) and LPIPS (0.177 vs 0.276), and dominates on GigaMVS sparse-view scenes (20.54 vs 17.44 PSNR, 0.686 vs 0.540 SSIM). Against Gaussian Splatting, RPBG wins on SSIM and LPIPS across all four categories (e.g., 0.832 vs 0.740 SSIM and 0.177 vs 0.290 LPIPS on Free), while Gaussian Splatting edges ahead on PSNR for T&T unbounded (23.51 vs 22.50) and inside-out (23.46 vs 23.29). Against the direct point-based baselines NPBG and NPBG++, RPBG improves PSNR by 4.9–6.4 dB and LPIPS by 0.09–0.27 across categories, confirming the gains from the enhanced renderer and collaborative optimization. On the large-scale Mill19 dataset, RPBG outperforms Mega-NeRF on all three metrics (22.62 vs 22.49 PSNR, 0.596 vs 0.550 SSIM, 0.368 vs 0.510 LPIPS) while training in 29 GPU hours versus 240, and is the only method that completes training under the 256 GB RAM / RTX 3090 constraint where both F²-NeRF and Gaussian Splatting fail. Overall, RPBG achieves the best perceptual quality (SSIM, LPIPS) in every category, with PSNR competitive or superior except for a narrow gap to Gaussian Splatting on bounded indoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

RPBG is a direct reform of NPBG/NPBG++, retaining the core pipeline of non-differentiable point rasterization, neural texture indexing, and CNN-based re-rendering, but reinterpreting the renderer as an image restoration problem rather than a simple feature-to-RGB mapping. The key architectural departure borrows from low-level vision: Fast Fourier Convolution provides a global receptive field for adaptive point-visibility correction (the DAC module), and gated convolutions from inpainting literature help locate downgraded regions. On the environment modeling side, the paper departs from ADOP's dense environment map by showing a single learnable 1×C feature vector suffices when paired with a stronger renderer. The point cloud augmentation via pseudo-density from neural textures draws an analogy to density-based surface extraction in NeRF implementations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single, unified point-based parameterization can handle free-trajectory, inside-out, large-scale, and sparse-view scenes without per-scene hyperparameter tuning, which is a practical advantage over RF-based methods that require case-by-case design (e.g., mip-NeRF 360's contraction, Mega-NeRF's partitioning). The memory efficiency of non-differentiable rasterization (vs. Gaussian Splatting's differentiable splatting) makes RPBG viable on large-scale scenes where competitors fail on standard hardware. Open limitations include temporal consistency flicker under patchy triangulation, linear parameter growth with point cloud scale, and reduced editability due to the non-local renderer—issues the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
