## Problem

Stain shifts between hospitals or staining protocols cause severe performance degradation in histopathology classifiers, yet existing solutions are either training-time methods (augmentation, normalization) that cannot generalize to unseen stains, or test-time diffusion methods (DiffPure, DDA) that do not preserve image structure well enough for diagnostic classification. To our knowledge, formulating stain adaptation as a nonlinear inverse problem solvable by a diffusion model at test time is unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 H&E-stained histopathology patch from an unseen target domain, a pre-trained DDIM diffusion model trained solely on source-domain patches, and a source-domain stain matrix (either the domain overall center or the domain tumor center inferred from tumor-class images). It produces a stain-adapted patch whose chromatic appearance matches the source domain while preserving structural content, which is then passed to a ResNet-50 classifier to yield a tumor/normal label. The per-input stain shift matrix A is computed on the fly from the input's own stain matrix and the fixed source stain matrix.

## Method

TT-SaD formulates stain adaptation as a nonlinear inverse problem: find an image x̂ with the desired source stain such that applying the stain shift matrix A to x̂ recovers the input. During the diffusion reverse process, after N<T forward noising steps, each denoising step computes a predicted x₀ and then projects it in Beer-Lambert optical density space using the pseudo-inverse A† and the null-space projector (I − A†A), ensuring the output's stain is corrected to the source domain while the structural (null-space) component is preserved. This consistency constraint is inspired by DDNM's null-space projection but adapted to the nonlinear, input-dependent stain shift operator. A key design choice is selecting the domain tumor center (stain matrix from tumor-class images only) rather than the domain overall center, avoiding bias from the class-imbalanced nature of tumor classification. The entire pipeline requires only a single source domain for training and adapts to any number of unseen target domains at test time without retraining.

## Results

On CAMELYON17 tumor classification (Hospital 1 as source), TT-SaD with domain tumor center achieves 85.87% average ACC, 90.73% average AUROC, and 35.71% average AUPRC across four target hospitals, substantially outperforming DiffPure (62.46% ACC, 79.36% AUROC) and DDA (59.34% ACC, 78.66% AUROC) at T=10. For stain adaptation quality on MitosAtypia14, TT-SaD achieves SSIM of 0.92–0.96 versus 0.15–0.60 for DiffPure and 0.06–0.59 for DDA, with FID reduced to 21–44 (vs. 48–280 for baselines). Against training-time methods, TT-SaD (trained on Hospital 1 only) reaches 81.54% ACC and 89.89% AUROC, beating Stain Mixup (73.47% ACC, 77.87% AUROC) and RandStainNA (75.23% ACC, 15.86% AUROC) which used all five hospitals for training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TT-SaD decisively outperforms both diffusion-based test-time baselines: on average ACC it gains +23.4 points over DiffPure (85.87 vs. 62.46) and +26.5 points over DDA (85.87 vs. 59.34), with similar margins on AUROC (+11.4 and +12.1) and AUPRC (+6.3 and +14.3). The advantage is most dramatic on Hospital 5, where DiffPure and DDA fail almost entirely (ACC ≈ 7%) while TT-SaD recovers to 87.16% ACC. Against training-time augmentation methods, TT-SaD (using only Hospital 1 for training) beats Stain Mixup by +8.1 ACC and +12.0 AUROC, and RandStainNA by +6.3 ACC and +74.0 AUROC, despite having access to fewer training hospitals. On stain adaptation quality (SSIM), TT-SaD achieves 0.92–0.96 versus 0.15–0.60 for DiffPure and 0.06–0.59 for DDA, confirming that the inverse-problem-based consistency constraint is critical for structure preservation. In the leave-one-hospital-out protocol (Table 6), TT-SaD still leads DiffPure by +6.5 ACC and DDA by +7.3 ACC on average, confirming generalizability beyond a single source domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

TT-SaD builds on the stain separation framework of Vahadane et al. (Beer-Lambert decomposition into stain matrix and density map) and the null-space consistency constraint of DDNM, but repurposes both for a new setting: solving a nonlinear inverse problem for stain shift at test time using a diffusion model's reverse process. It departs from DiffPure and DDA, which apply diffusion-based domain adaptation to natural images without exploiting the low-dimensional stain structure of histopathology, and from GAN-based stain transfer methods (CAGAN, CycleGAN, DRIT++) that suffer from mode collapse. Unlike training-time stain augmentation (Stain Mixup, RandStainNA) or linear normalization (Macenko, Vahadane), TT-SaD requires only a single source domain and handles unseen target domains without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: hospitals deploying pathology classifiers face stain shifts whenever a new scanner or staining protocol is introduced, and retraining is often infeasible. TT-SaD's single-domain training requirement and test-time adaptation directly address this deployment bottleneck. Key limitations include slow inference (iterative diffusion denoising), restriction to H&E staining (r=2), and dependence on diffusion model quality. Open questions include whether the inverse-problem formulation extends to multi-stain protocols (e.g., immunohistochemistry with 3+ dyes), whether it generalizes beyond patch-level to whole-slide classification, and how it interacts with foundation models for pathology.
