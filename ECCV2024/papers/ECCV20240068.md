## Problem

Existing efficient SR methods (ClassSR, ARM) allocate computational resources at the patch level, but a single patch can contain pixels with vastly different restoration difficulties, leading to wasted computation on easy pixels or under-computation on hard ones. This inefficiency worsens with larger patch sizes, which are desirable for reducing overlap redundancy and capturing more context. The paper asks whether moving the allocation granularity from patch-level to pixel-level can further reduce FLOPs while maintaining PSNR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (32×32 patches during training on DIV2K; full 2K–8K images decomposed into overlapping patches at inference) and produces a high-resolution super-resolved RGB image. Because the upsamplers are LIIF-based, the model natively supports arbitrary scale factors including non-integer scales, and the per-pixel assignment mechanism means the output is assembled by querying each HR pixel's RGB value independently through its assigned upsampler branch.

## Method

PCSR consists of three components: a standard SR backbone (FSRCNN, CARN, or SRResNet) that extracts LR features, a lightweight MLP-based pixel-level classifier that predicts per-pixel class probabilities from the LR feature map and the query pixel's relative coordinate, and M parallel LIIF-based upsamplers with decreasing capacity. During training, each pixel's output is a soft-weighted sum across all M upsamplers (enabling gradient flow), with a reconstruction loss on high-frequency residuals and an average loss encouraging uniform class assignment; training proceeds in multi-stage fashion, freezing previously trained upsamplers. At inference, each pixel is hard-assigned to a single upsampler via argmax of probability divided by cost^k, where the tunable hyperparameter k lets users trade PSNR for FLOPs without retraining. A K-means-based Adaptive Decision Making module can automate the assignment, and a pixel-wise refinement step replaces RGB values at heavy/light upsampler boundaries with local averages to suppress artifacts.

## Results

On Test2K (×4 SR), PCSR matches the PSNR of the original models (e.g., 26.01 dB for CARN-PCSR vs. 26.03 dB for CARN) while reducing FLOPs to 57% of the original, compared to 91% (ClassSR) and 89% (ARM). For FSRCNN, the reduction is even more dramatic: PCSR uses only 19% of the original FLOPs (8.5 vs. 45.3 GFLOPs) at the same 25.61 dB PSNR, versus 85% and 79% for ClassSR and ARM. On Test8K, SRResNet-PCSR achieves 33.52 dB at 5093.7 GFLOPs (43% of original), outperforming ClassSR (67%) and ARM (66%) in efficiency while matching or exceeding their PSNR. The same pattern holds on Urban100, where PCSR consistently achieves the lowest FLOPs at matched PSNR across all three backbone sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCSR consistently outperforms both patch-distributing baselines (ClassSR and ARM) in FLOPs at matched PSNR across all three backbone sizes and all four benchmarks. On Test2K, FSRCNN-PCSR uses only 19% of original FLOPs versus 85% (ClassSR) and 79% (ARM); CARN-PCSR uses 57% versus 91% and 89%; SRResNet-PCSR uses 49% versus 89% and 85%. Against the per-image processing method MGA (Table 2), PCSR also wins on FLOPs: FSRCNN-PCSR achieves 28% vs. MGA's 64%, and SRResNet-PCSR achieves 38% vs. MGA's 50% on Test2K, while matching PSNR. PCSR also uses fewer parameters than ClassSR (e.g., 25K vs. 113K for FSRCNN, 1.1M vs. 3.1M for SRResNet) because it shares the backbone across upsampler branches rather than duplicating subnets. The PSNR gap to the original (non-efficient) model is at most 0.04 dB, confirming that the pixel-level distribution introduces negligible quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCSR extends the patch-distributing line of work initiated by ClassSR and ARM, which allocate computational resources at the patch granularity based on a difficulty classifier, by moving the allocation decision to the pixel level. The upsampler design borrows from LIIF's local implicit image function, which naturally supports pixel-wise querying, while the backbone can be any existing CNN-based SR network (FSRCNN, CARN, SRResNet). The paper also contrasts with per-image processing methods like MGA, which perform global-then-local refinement, positioning pixel-level allocation as a complementary and more fine-grained strategy for large-image SR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method delivers substantial FLOP reductions (up to ~81% for FSRCNN, ~51% for SRResNet on Test2K) with only marginal PSNR drops, making large-image SR (2K–8K) more practical on resource-constrained hardware. A key limitation is that the classifier operates on backbone LR features, so the FLOP floor is set by the backbone size, which is wasteful for predominantly flat images; the authors suggest earlier-layer classification or lookup-table shortcuts as remedies. Open directions include extending PCSR to generative SR models and combining it with model compression, pruning, or quantization for further efficiency gains.
