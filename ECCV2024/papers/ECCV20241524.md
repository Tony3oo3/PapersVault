## Problem

Transformer decoders for image generation have relied on discrete tokens from finite VQ-VAE vocabularies, introducing non-differentiable quantization, low codebook utilization, large embedding matrices, and a loss of low-level detail. This paper asks whether a decoder-only transformer can be made to generate sequences of continuous real-valued vectors directly, completely removing the quantization step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241524/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional image generation, a learned class embedding is prepended to a sequence of 16×16 spatial positions (for 256×256 images), each a 16-dimensional real-valued vector produced by a β-VAE encoder; the model outputs a 16-component GMM per position, from which a latent sequence is sampled and decoded to an image. For dense prediction (panoptic segmentation, depth estimation), an RGB image at 512px is encoded by a ViT-L/16 encoder and cross-attended into the GIVT decoder, which predicts continuous latent codes decoded back to label maps. The key distinction from VQ-based methods is that every 'token' is a d-dimensional continuous vector rather than an integer codebook index.

## Method

GIVT makes two minimal changes to a standard decoder-only transformer: at the input, the finite-vocabulary embedding lookup is replaced by a single linear projection of real-valued vectors; at the output, the categorical logit head is replaced by a head predicting 2kd+k parameters (means, variances, and mixture weights of a k-component GMM with diagonal covariance). The latent space is learned by a standard β-VAE (Gaussian encoder and prior, no VQ layer), trained with MSE + perceptual + GAN losses. Two training variants are explored: GIVT-Causal (autoregressive with teacher forcing and causal masking) and GIVT-MaskGIT (progressive masked bidirectional modeling). A small invertible iRevNet adapter is optionally trained jointly with GIVT to warp the VAE latent space into a distribution easier for the transformer to model. For inference, the paper derives continuous analogs of temperature sampling (variance scaling), beam search, and classifier-free guidance (DB-CFG), the latter implemented via rejection sampling from the predicted GMM components.

## Results

On class-conditional 256×256 ImageNet, GIVT-Causal-L+A achieves FID 2.59 (with DB-CFG=0.4, t=0.95), outperforming VQGAN (FID 5.20) with a 4.5× smaller model and ViT-VQGAN-L (FID 3.04) with a 4× shorter sequence. GIVT-MaskGIT achieves FID 4.53 in just 16 inference steps, beating MaskGIT (FID 4.92) at comparable cost. On 512×512 ImageNet, GIVT-Causal-L+A reaches FID 2.92, surpassing DiT-XL/2-G (FID 3.04) with guidance. For dense prediction, GIVT-based UViM achieves PQ 40.2 on COCO Panoptic (vs. 39.0 for VQ-based UViM) and RMSE 0.474 on NYU Depth v2 (vs. 0.459).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIVT-Causal (0.3B params) outperforms VQGAN (1.4B params) by a large margin: FID 3.35 vs. 5.20 with guidance, and also beats LDM-4-G (FID 3.60) and ADM-G (FID 4.59). The larger GIVT-Causal-L+A (FID 2.59) surpasses ViT-VQGAN-L (FID 3.04) despite using a 4× shorter sequence and 10× smaller VAE, and slightly trails DiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.27) on 256×256 but beats it on 512×512 (FID 2.92 vs. 3.04). GIVT-MaskGIT consistently outperforms MaskGIT at comparable model size and 16-step inference: FID 4.53 vs. 4.92 (256×256) and 4.86 vs. 7.80 (512×512). Against GANs, GIVT-Causal-L+A (FID 2.59) approaches StyleGAN-XL (FID 2.30) while offering class-conditional control without adversarial training. For dense prediction, GIVT-based UViM improves panoptic segmentation PQ from 39.0 to 40.2 over VQ-based UViM, though depth estimation RMSE is slightly worse (0.474 vs. 0.459). In representation learning (linear probing), GIVT-Causal matches ViT-VQGAN (65.1% vs. 65.1%) with fewer than half the parameters and 4× shorter sequences, but trails MAGE (78.9%) which uses an encoder-decoder architecture better suited for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIVT directly extends the VQ-GAN/MaskGIT paradigm of modeling VAE latent sequences with decoder-only transformers, but removes the vector quantization step entirely. Where prior work (VQ-GAN, MaskGIT, ViT-VQGAN) requires a discrete codebook and associated training tricks (entropy losses, codebook splitting, reinitialization), GIVT replaces the embedding lookup with a linear projection and the categorical output head with a Gaussian Mixture Model, enabling the transformer to consume and predict real-valued 'soft tokens' from a standard β-VAE. The inference techniques—variance scaling, beam search, and a novel density-based classifier-free guidance (DB-CFG)—are continuous analogues of temperature sampling and CFG from the discrete and diffusion literatures, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating quantization, GIVT sidesteps a cluster of practical pain points in VQ-VAE training (low codebook utilization, non-differentiable updates, large embedding matrices) while matching or exceeding the generation quality of both VQ-based transformers and latent diffusion models, particularly at 512×512. The approach is also directly transferable to multimodal interleaved generation without the memory overhead of per-modality codebooks, and the same continuous-token formulation extends naturally to dense prediction (panoptic segmentation, depth estimation) via UViM. Open questions include whether end-to-end joint training of the β-VAE and GIVT (left to future work) can further close the gap to the best diffusion models, and whether the method generalizes to other modalities such as audio and time-series.
