## Problem

Dexterous grasping methods face a fundamental trade-off: regression-based approaches (e.g., DDG) achieve high success rates but suffer from mode collapse or averaging that limits posture diversity, while generation-based methods (e.g., GraspTTA) produce diverse grasps but lack discriminative signal, yielding low success rates. Additionally, conventional contact representations such as contact maps are incompatible with joint generative modeling over unordered point clouds, preventing unified treatment of object, hand, and contact in a single generative framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes an object point cloud (N×3) and optionally a hand posture (22-DOF ShadowHand joint angles, SO(3) rotation, R³ translation) as input, and produces diverse hand postures, contact anchors (Nc×3 points on the object surface within threshold η of the hand mesh), and optionally generated object point clouds. Three generation modes are supported by fixing or freeing the diffusion timestep per modality: hand-only generation (object fixed), object-only generation (hand fixed), and joint generation (both free). At inference, M candidate grasps are generated, filtered by a physics discriminator, and the top-N are refined via test-time optimization.

## Method

UGG unifies object, hand, and contact into a single diffusion process using a U-ViT transformer backbone (from UniDiffuser) with asynchronized timesteps per modality, enabling a single model to express all conditional, marginal, and joint distributions. Objects are encoded by a hierarchical VAE (LION-style) producing global and local latent codes; hands are encoded by a separate VAE on joint angles plus explicit rotation/translation tokens. The key novelty is the Contact Anchor representation—Nc points randomly sampled from the object point cloud whose distance to the hand mesh falls below threshold η—which replaces contact maps and naturally aligns with the unordered point-cloud structure for seamless joint diffusion. A lightweight physics discriminator, trained on Isaac Gym simulation labels (success/failure), is applied post-generation to filter candidates, exploiting the generative model's diversity to balance success rate against diversity. Test-time optimization further refines selected grasps using contact, penetration, self-penetration, and joint-angle losses.

## Results

On the DexGraspNet benchmark, UGG achieves a 72.7% grasping success rate, the highest Q1 score of 0.063, and the lowest penetration of 0.14 mm among all compared methods. In terms of diversity, UGG attains a mean joint-angle entropy of 7.17 with a standard deviation of 0.07, outperforming all baselines. For seen categories the success rate reaches 73.8%, while for novel categories it remains strong at 68.1%, demonstrating good generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UGG outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the regression-based DDG [33], UGG improves success rate by 5.2 points (72.7% vs. 67.5%), Q₁ by 0.005, and reduces penetration from 0.17 to 0.14 mm, while also achieving substantially higher diversity (H mean 7.17 vs. 5.68) and far lower variance (H std 0.07 vs. 1.99). Compared to the generation-based GraspTTA [20], UGG more than triples the success rate (72.7% vs. 24.5%) and reduces penetration by nearly 5× (0.14 vs. 0.68 mm). Against UniDexGrasp's generation module (UDG [76]), UGG achieves a 3× higher success rate (72.7% vs. 23.3%) and a higher Q₁ (0.063 vs. 0.056), while maintaining comparable penetration (0.14 vs. 0.15). Notably, even without the physics discriminator, UGG reaches 64.1% success—close to DDG—indicating that the unified diffusion backbone itself is competitive, and the discriminator provides the final quality boost.

## Relation to Prior Work

UGG extends the UniDiffuser framework (asynchronized timesteps over multiple modalities with a U-ViT backbone) to the dexterous grasping domain, unifying object, hand, and contact distributions in a single diffusion model. For shape encoding, it adopts LION's hierarchical VAE for point clouds and a similar VAE for hand joint parameters, while departing from prior contact representations (contact maps, contact parts) by introducing the simpler Contact Anchor — a set of points near the hand mesh. The physics discriminator is a novel addition that leverages the generative model's own diversity to create adversarial training samples, bridging the gap between generative diversity and discriminative quality that prior methods (DDG's regression, GraspTTA's two-stage generation) could not simultaneously address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UGG demonstrates that a single unified diffusion model can match or exceed regression-based success rates while substantially outperforming all baselines in diversity, and the physics discriminator enables explicit control over the success-rate/diversity trade-off — a capability prior generative grasping methods lacked. The reverse capability of generating objects from hand poses opens a path toward human-centric object design and provides interpretability into how the model perceives graspable shapes. Open questions include real-world deployment (the pipeline relies on Isaac Gym validation and test-time optimization), whether the discriminator generalizes beyond the ShadowHand model, and whether the Contact Anchor representation scales to more complex multi-contact or articulated-object scenarios.
