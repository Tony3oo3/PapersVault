## Problem

Existing GAN-based model inversion attacks optimize only in the latent space of the GAN prior, which limits semantic extraction and transferability, particularly under out-of-distribution (OOD) settings where the public GAN training data differs substantially from the private target dataset. The rich semantic information encoded in intermediate features of GAN generators has remained unexploited for inversion attacks, leaving a gap in recovering high-quality private data under rigorous distributional shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes white-box access to a target image classifier (e.g., ResNet-18, ResNet-152, DenseNet-169, ResNeSt-101), a target class label, and a pre-trained StyleGAN2 generator (trained on public datasets such as FFHQ or MetFaces) as inputs, and produces reconstructed privacy-sensitive RGB images at 224×224 resolution. The identity loss uses a Poincaré distance rather than standard cross-entropy to avoid gradient vanishing when prediction confidence approaches the one-hot target, and the one-hot vector's 1 is replaced with 0.9999 to prevent division by zero.

## Method

IF-GMI disassembles the StyleGAN2 synthesis network into L+1 sequential blocks and extends the optimization space from the latent code to intermediate features between blocks. The pipeline first performs initial selection on mapped W-space vectors (choosing those with highest augmented prediction confidence), then optimizes the latent code w, and finally successively optimizes intermediate features layer-by-layer from front to back, using the remaining downstream blocks to generate images and compute the Poincaré identity loss against the target classifier. An l1 ball constraint with per-layer radius r[i] restricts each intermediate feature's deviation from its initial value, preventing collapse into unrealistic images. The number of optimized layers L is tuned per dataset pair to balance underfitting (too few layers) against overfitting to local details (too many layers), and the final reconstruction is selected from the outputs collected after each layer's optimization.

## Results

On ResNet-18 trained on FaceScrub, IF-GMI achieves 97.9% top-1 attack accuracy (Acc@1) with the FFHQ-trained StyleGAN2 and 94.9% with the MetFaces-trained StyleGAN2, substantially outperforming all baselines. The most dramatic gain appears in the out-of-distribution (OOD) setting with MetFaces, where IF-GMI improves Acc@1 by 32.1 percentage points over PPA (0.949 vs. 0.628) and by over 90 points over GMI (0.949 vs. 0.038). FID scores are also competitive or best: 40.6 (FFHQ) and 68.1 (MetFaces), confirming high reconstruction fidelity alongside accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IF-GMI outperforms all five baselines on every metric in both the in-distribution (FFHQ) and OOD (MetFaces) settings. Against the strongest baseline PPA [38], IF-GMI gains 1.7 points in Acc@1 on FFHQ (0.979 vs. 0.962) but a massive 32.1 points on MetFaces (0.949 vs. 0.628), demonstrating that intermediate-feature optimization is critical when the GAN prior's training distribution diverges from the target. Against GMI [51] and KEDMI [5], which rely on 64×64 GANs and optimize only the latent code, IF-GMI achieves roughly 90+ point Acc@1 improvements in the OOD setting (e.g., 0.949 vs. 0.038 for GMI on MetFaces). LOMMA [31], a plug-and-play model-augmentation technique, also degrades severely under OOD (LOMMA+GMI drops to 0.160 Acc@1 on MetFaces), while IF-GMI remains robust. PLGMI [47] achieves 0.438 Acc@1 on MetFaces, still 51 points below IF-GMI. On CelebA with ResNet-152 (Table 2), IF-GMI extends the OOD advantage to 38.8 points over PPA (0.784 vs. 0.396 Acc@1), and across ResNet-152, ResNeSt-101, and DenseNet-169 on FaceScrub (Table 3), it consistently improves Acc@1 by 13–17 points over PPA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAN-based model inversion attack paradigm established by GMI (Zhang et al., CVPR 2020) and refined by PPA (Struppek et al., ICML 2022), which introduced pre-trained StyleGAN2 as a plug-and-play prior and W-space optimization. The key departure is moving beyond latent-code-only optimization: the authors decompose the StyleGAN2 synthesis network into sequential blocks and successively optimize intermediate feature maps between them, drawing on the GAN dissection literature that documents rich semantic structure in these layers. The l1 ball constraint around each intermediate feature's initial value is a novel regularization that prevents the expanded optimization space from producing unrealistic images, a problem that does not arise when only the compact latent code is optimized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OOD robustness gain is the most practically relevant contribution: when the public GAN training data (e.g., MetFaces artworks) has a large distributional shift from the private target (real faces), prior methods degrade sharply while IF-GMI maintains strong accuracy, suggesting the intermediate feature space provides a more flexible and semantically grounded search space. Limitations include evaluation restricted to face and dog-breed classification, white-box access only, and dependence on pre-trained StyleGAN2 checkpoints; whether the intermediate-feature optimization strategy transfers to other GAN architectures (e.g., diffusion models) or to non-image modalities remains open. The work also raises the question of whether defenders can exploit the same intermediate-feature structure to detect or mitigate inversion attacks.
