## Problem

Existing cross-modality person ReID methods such as CAJ and DEEN are tailored to a specific modality pair (e.g., visible-infrared) and exhibit substantial performance degradation when the same architecture is applied to a different pair (e.g., visible-sketch). No prior work provides a single generalized framework that can adaptively learn consistent relationships across arbitrary heterogeneous modality combinations for person re-identification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241928/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pedestrian images resized to 288×144 from any two of three modalities (visible RGB, infrared, or artist-drawn sketch), processed through a ResNet-50 backbone whose first two ResBlocks are modality-specific and remaining layers are shared. It outputs a cross-modality retrieval ranking (Rank-1/5/10 and mAP) by computing identity similarity in a common embedding space, with the multi-semantic representation set doubling the effective sample count per batch to improve shared-relationship learning.

## Method

DNS introduces a distribution-shifting mechanism in two complementary spaces: Heterogeneous Space Shifting (HSS) applies directional global max pooling and softmax to build a modality-specific distribution mask, shifts the feature distribution via residual addition to amplify modality-specific knowledge, and then uses coordinate attention to enforce cross-modality consistency; Common Space Shifting (CSS) projects the shifted features into a shared embedding via GAP, applies a second softmax-based distribution shift to amplify modality-shared patterns, and refines them through layer-normalized FC layers. A Domain Distillation-Alignment (DDA) strategy complements the architecture: the domain alignment loss uses MMD to align inter-class distributions across modalities and intra-class distributions across pooling strategies, while the domain distillation loss employs a two-branch temporal accumulation scheme that iteratively updates a shared classifier and computes KL-divergence plus cross-entropy losses to distill identity-invariant patterns. The total objective combines identity loss, circle loss, and the DDA losses, and the entire framework is modality-agnostic, requiring no task-specific architectural changes to switch between visible-infrared and visible-sketch ReID.

## Results

On the VI-ReID task, DNS achieves 77.27% Rank-1 and 74.35% mAP on SYSU-MM01 (All-Search), 84.21% Rank-1 and 86.83% mAP (Indoor-Search), 93.48% Rank-1 and 88.10% mAP on RegDB (IR→VIS), and 57.45% Rank-1 and 64.11% mAP on LLCM (IR→VIS), outperforming all SOTA methods on SYSU-MM01 and LLCM. On the VS-ReID task, DNS achieves 87.6% Rank-1 and 81.5% mAP on PKU-Sketch, surpassing the previous best (SketchTrans, 84.6% Rank-1) by 3.0% Rank-1 and introducing a substantial mAP improvement over all prior methods. The method demonstrates consistent superiority across both cross-modality settings, validating its generalization capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SYSU-MM01 (All-Search), DNS leads all 13 baselines by at least 1.03% Rank-1 over MUN (76.24%) and 2.57% over DEEN (74.70%), with a 2.54% mAP margin over MUN. On the more challenging LLCM low-light dataset, DNS outperforms the next-best DEEN by 2.55% Rank-1 (57.45 vs. 54.90) and 1.21% mAP in IR→VIS, and by 3.52% Rank-1 (66.02 vs. 62.50) in VIS→IR. On RegDB, DNS is competitive but slightly trails CAL (93.64%) and MUN (95.19%) on Rank-1 in the two directions, though it matches or exceeds them on mAP (88.10 vs. 87.61; 88.56 vs. 87.15), which the authors attribute to RegDB's smaller intra-class variation limiting DDA's benefit. On PKU-Sketch (VS-ReID), DNS achieves 87.6% Rank-1 and 81.5% mAP, surpassing SketchTrans (84.6% Rank-1, no mAP reported) by 3.0% Rank-1 and dramatically outperforming CAJ (59.2%/58.1%) and DEEN (61.2%/57.3%) by over 26% Rank-1, demonstrating the method's cross-modality generalization from VI-ReID to VS-ReID without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in cross-modality person ReID, where methods like CAJ, DEEN, and MUN are explicitly designed for a single modality pair (visible-infrared) and degrade sharply when transferred to another (visible-sketch). Rather than building on one specific prior architecture, it synthesizes ideas from both VI-ReID (two-stream feature alignment, channel augmentation) and VS-ReID (cross-domain adversarial learning) literature, introducing a distribution-shifting mechanism (HSS/CSS) and a domain distillation-alignment loss (DDA) as a modality-agnostic framework. The shifting concept repurposes coordinate attention and softmax-based distribution manipulation in a novel way, while the DDA strategy combines MMD-based alignment with a temporal-accumulation distillation scheme borrowed from knowledge distillation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to achieve competitive SOTA on both visible-infrared and visible-sketch ReID with a single architecture, addressing a practical gap where real-world surveillance systems must handle multiple modality pairs. The ablation showing that the shifting mechanism improves CAJ and DEEN by ~9% on PKU-Sketch suggests it is a broadly applicable component beyond this specific architecture. However, the generalization claim is limited to exactly two modality pairs with a single ResNet-50 backbone, and the PKU-Sketch dataset is small (200 identities, frontal sketches only), leaving open whether the approach scales to additional modalities (e.g., low-light, thermal at different temperatures) or more complex sketch styles. The authors themselves note future work on broadening application to other cross-modality recognition tasks.
