## Problem

Accurate cephalometric landmark detection on high-resolution X-ray images currently requires either heatmap-based methods with non-differentiable post-processing and high memory costs, or regression-based methods that cascade multiple models to achieve precision. No existing regression approach performs all 19 landmark detections in a single end-to-end model on high-resolution inputs without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240758/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single lateral cephalometric X-ray image scaled to 1024×1024 pixels and directly regresses the (x, y) coordinates of 19 cephalometric landmarks along with per-landmark uncertainty distributions (σ). Unlike heatmap methods that require argmax post-processing, the output is a continuous coordinate vector of shape K×2 plus a K×1 distribution vector, making the entire pipeline differentiable from input to output.

## Method

The architecture is a dual-encoder design using only the transformer encoder (no decoder), comprising a ResNet-34 feature extractor that fuses multi-scale features (S2–S5) into a unified map F_u, a reference encoder that takes the deepest feature map S5 with randomly initialized landmark queries and BERT-style positional encodings to predict coarse coordinates via N layers of MHSA+FFN, and a finetune encoder that iteratively refines positions over M layers by bilinearly sampling F_u at the current landmark locations, computing (Δx, Δy) offsets, and adding them back to the coordinates. Training is fully supervised and end-to-end, using Dice+MSE loss on an auxiliary heatmap from the feature extractor and Residual Log-likelihood Estimation (RLE) loss on both encoder outputs, with the finetune encoder supervised at every intermediate layer. The key distinction from prior regression methods is that a single model handles all 19 landmarks on the full high-resolution image without region cropping or separate per-landmark models, reducing GFLOPs by 132% compared to the prior best regression approach.

## Results

On the ISBI 2015 test1 dataset (1024×1024 input), the proposed dual-encoder method achieves an MRE of 1.01 mm and a 2 mm SDR of 89.51%, outperforming the best heatmap-based method (Ao et al., 1.12 mm / 88.03%) and the best regression-based method (Gilmour et al., 1.01 mm / 88.32%) while using only 95.0 GFLOPs versus 220.2 GFLOPs for Gilmour et al. On ISBI 2015 test2, the method records 1.27 mm MRE and 79.53% 2 mm SDR. On the more challenging ISBI 2023 dataset (multi-device, 10-fold CV), it achieves 0.9372 mm MRE and 90.68% 2 mm SDR, surpassing the previous best (Gilmour et al., 0.9793 mm / 89.37%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest heatmap-based baseline (Ao et al. [2], DenseNet121), the proposed method improves MRE from 1.12 mm to 1.01 mm and 2 mm SDR from 88.03% to 89.51% on ISBI 2015 test1, while also reducing GFLOPs from 157.2 to 95.0. Against the best regression-based baseline (Gilmour et al. [11], 19 separate models at 2432×1920), the method matches MRE (1.01 mm) but improves 2 mm SDR from 88.32% to 89.51% while cutting GFLOPs by more than half (220.2 → 95.0). On ISBI 2023, the method beats Gilmour et al. by 0.042 mm in MRE (0.9372 vs. 0.9793) and 1.31% in 2 mm SDR (90.68% vs. 89.37%), and outperforms the transformer-based Poseur [24] by 0.061 mm in MRE and 2.17% in 2 mm SDR. The method also surpasses the multi-stage cascaded approaches (Song et al. [29,30], Zeng et al. [36]) by 0.07–0.33 mm in MRE and 2.6–9.3% in 2 mm SDR, demonstrating that a single end-to-end model can match or exceed cascaded multi-model pipelines. At the lower 512×512 resolution, the method already achieves 88.07% 2 mm SDR with only 23.0 GFLOPs, outperforming all other low-resolution baselines in the table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying the coarse-to-fine refinement concept from cascaded regression methods (Gilmour et al., Song et al.) into a single end-to-end model, while drawing on the transformer encoder architecture and BERT-style positional encoding. It departs from heatmap-based methods (which require non-differentiable post-processing and U-Net structures) and from prior regression approaches (which rely on multiple cascaded models or per-landmark networks) by using only the encoder module of a transformer in a dual-encoder design. The conceptual lineage connects to deformable transformer keypoint regression (Poseur, DTLD) but simplifies the architecture to encoder-only, avoiding the deployment complexity of deformable decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving state-of-the-art accuracy (MRE 0.9372mm on ISBI 2023) with a single end-to-end model that reduces computational cost by 132% in GFLOPs compared to the previous best regression method, making clinical deployment substantially more feasible. The elimination of cascaded multi-model pipelines simplifies both training and inference workflows. Open questions include whether the encoder-only design generalizes to 3D cephalometric analysis, whether the approach extends to the full 29-landmark set on ISBI 2023, and how performance holds under domain shift across imaging devices beyond the seven tested.
