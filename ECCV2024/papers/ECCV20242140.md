## Problem

Dense visual SLAM systems based on neural implicit scene representations face a fundamental trade-off between memory efficiency and reconstruction quality: voxel-grid methods (NICE-SLAM) grow cubically O(L³), while the more recent plane-based ESLAM still grows quadratically O(L²), both limiting scalability to large-scale real-time scenes. The paper asks whether a linear-memory O(L) scene representation can match or exceed the localization and reconstruction accuracy of these existing approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242140/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a sequence of RGB-D frames (RGB images paired with per-pixel depth maps) and jointly optimizes camera poses, a truncated signed distance field (TSDF) for geometry, and a color field for appearance. The scene is encoded at two resolutions — coarse (24 cm) and fine (6 cm for geometry, 3 cm for appearance) — with 32-channel axis-aligned feature tensors. Rendering is performed via SDF-based volume rendering (stratified + importance sampling along rays), producing rendered depth and color that are compared against sensor measurements in the loss.

## Method

LRSLAM replaces ESLAM's tri-plane scene representation with a hybrid of two low-rank tensor decompositions, both achieving O(n) memory: CP decomposition for geometry (factorizing the 4D tensor into k_g=2 outer products of three axis-aligned rank-one vectors) and a novel Six-axis (SA) decomposition for appearance (factorizing each of the three tri-plane feature planes into k_a=16 outer products of two axis-aligned vectors, yielding six total axis-aligned tensors). The hybrid design is motivated by two observations: geometry contains lower-frequency content that CP can capture compactly, and CP's faster early convergence stabilizes the geometric dependency in volume rendering, which in turn accelerates appearance optimization. The overall SLAM pipeline (SDF-based volume rendering, depth/color/free-space/TSDF losses, and pose optimization) is inherited from ESLAM, with only the scene representation swapped.

## Results

On ScanNet (6 large real scenes), LRSLAM achieves an average ATE Mean of 6.1±0.1 cm and ATE RMSE of 7.0±0.2 cm, outperforming ESLAM (6.5±0.2 / 7.4±0.2) by 6.2% and 5.4% respectively, and NICE-SLAM (9.3±0.7 / 10.7±0.7) by 34% and 35%. In terms of efficiency, LRSLAM uses 87.3%–90.1% fewer parameters than ESLAM (e.g., 0.86M vs 6.79M on Replica) and is 4.3%–73.2% faster in frame processing time. On Replica with added depth noise N(0, 0.05²), LRSLAM reduces reconstruction Depth L1 error by 62.7% and ATE RMSE by 2.7% compared to ESLAM, demonstrating superior robustness to sensor noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ESLAM (the primary baseline), LRSLAM achieves better localization on ScanNet with 6.2% lower ATE Mean and 5.4% lower ATE RMSE, while using 87–90% fewer parameters and faster processing. On TUM RGB-D, LRSLAM wins on 2 of 3 scenes (fr1/desk: 2.45 vs 2.47; fr2/xyz: 0.96 vs 1.11) but loses on fr3/office (2.79 vs 2.42). On clean Replica, LRSLAM is slightly worse than ESLAM on some reconstruction metrics (Depth L1: 1.58 vs 1.18), but with added depth noise it clearly outperforms ESLAM across all metrics (e.g., Depth L1: 1.20 vs 3.22, a 62.7% reduction), indicating the low-rank representation acts as an implicit noise filter. Against NICE-SLAM, LRSLAM dominates on every metric: 34% lower ATE Mean, 35% lower ATE RMSE on ScanNet, and uses O(L) vs O(L³) memory with roughly 1/12th the parameters on TUM RGB-D (0.84M vs 44.64M). The key trade-off is that on perfectly clean synthetic data (Replica without noise), ESLAM's more expressive tri-plane representation can slightly overfit and achieve marginally better reconstruction, but this advantage disappears under realistic noisy conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LRSLAM sits at the end of a clear lineage in dense RGB-D SLAM: iMAP introduced neural implicit scene representations for SLAM, NICE-SLAM added voxel-grid features (cubic memory), and ESLAM reduced this to tri-plane tensor decomposition (quadratic memory). This paper's core contribution is the Six-axis decomposition, which factorizes the three tri-plane feature planes into six axis-aligned low-rank tensors, achieving linear memory complexity, combined with CP decomposition for geometry. The hybrid design (CP for geometry, SA for appearance) is a deliberate departure from ESLAM's uniform tri-plane approach, motivated by the observation that geometry is lower-frequency and benefits from faster CP convergence, while appearance requires the higher expressiveness of SA decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction from O(n²) to O(n) memory growth is practically significant for real-time SLAM on resource-constrained hardware, and the 87–90% parameter reduction with matched or improved localization accuracy makes the approach attractive for deployment. A notable practical finding is that the low-rank representation acts as an implicit regularizer against sensor noise, with LRSLAM outperforming ESLAM by 40–63% on reconstruction metrics under added Gaussian depth noise. However, evaluation is limited to indoor scenes (ScanNet, TUM, Replica) and the method's scalability to very large outdoor environments or long sequences remains untested. Open questions include whether the linear memory advantage persists at extreme scene scales and whether the hybrid CP/SA split generalizes to other SLAM variants (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting-based systems).
