## Problem

Existing spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) methods depend on fine-grained per-frame bounding box and temporal boundary annotations, severely limiting scalability to large-scale unlabeled data. While weakly-supervised grounding has been studied for images, the video setting remains underexplored, and existing weakly-supervised STVG approaches fail to model fine-grained entity interactions needed to resolve semantic ambiguities when multiple visually similar objects co-occur in a scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241288/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (sampled at 5 fps, capped at 200 frames) and a natural language query sentence. Region proposals (10 per frame) are extracted via a pre-trained Faster R-CNN detector, and the sentence is parsed into entity noun phrases and relationship phrases using Stanford CoreNLP. The output is a spatio-temporal tube—a sequence of per-frame bounding boxes plus temporal start/end boundaries—produced by a weighted combination of predictions from two separate networks (q_φ and p_θ) at inference.

## Method

The paper reformulates STVG as two cross-modal alignment sub-problems—region-phrase (spatial) and frame-sentence (temporal)—and treats the true assignments as latent variables optimized via a variational EM algorithm. In the E-step, a mean-field variational posterior approximates the spatial assignment via softmax over region-phrase similarity scores and the temporal assignment via a Gaussian over normalized frame positions, supervised by a contrastive video-sentence alignment loss. In the M-step, a separate network reconstructs the visual relationships between entities (e.g., the verb 'push') using a transformer decoder conditioned on the estimated assignments, so that correct relationship recovery forces correct entity-region alignment. Spatial and temporal context are encoded via alternating multi-head self-attention within frames and across object tubes (linked by a Viterbi algorithm), enabling the model to capture both local interactions and temporal dynamics.

## Results

On the VidSTG benchmark (declarative sentences), the proposed method achieves 14.45 m_VIoU, 18.57 VIoU@0.3, and 8.76 VIoU@0.5, surpassing the strongest baseline WINNER by +2.83 m_VIoU and +4.45 VIoU@0.3. On interrogative sentences, it reaches 13.25 m_VIoU and 16.74 VIoU@0.5, again leading all competitors. On HC-STVG, the method attains 14.64 m_VIoU and 18.60 VIoU@0.3, outperforming WINNER (14.20 / 17.24) and Vis-Ctx (9.76 / 6.81) by clear margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on both benchmarks across every metric. The closest competitor is WINNER [19], which uses hierarchical decomposition and alignment; the gap is +2.83 m_VIoU and +4.45 VIoU@0.3 on VidSTG declarative sentences, narrowing to +0.44 m_VIoU on HC-STVG where WINNER is more competitive. Among the dedicated weakly-supervised video grounding methods, Vis-Ctx [32] (9.34 m_VIoU on VidSTG) and ADWS [8] (8.96) are surpassed by 5+ m_VIoU points, while WSSTG [9] (8.85) trails by 5.6 m_VIoU. The factorized grounding baselines combining image grounding (GroundeR [31], MATN [44], RAIR [21]) with temporal grounding (LCNet [39], CPL [45]) all fall in the 7.85–8.67 m_VIoU range on VidSTG, roughly 6 points below the proposed method, confirming that decoupling spatial and temporal grounding loses critical cross-modal interaction cues. The object-similarity baseline (3.41 m_VIoU) demonstrates that category-level matching alone is insufficient when multiple same-category objects coexist in a scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of weakly-supervised video grounding and variational inference. While fully-supervised STVG methods (Zhang et al. [43], TubeDETR [38]) require per-frame bounding boxes and temporal boundaries, and prior weakly-supervised approaches (WSSTG [9], ADWS [8], WINNER [19]) rely on multiple instance learning or hierarchical decomposition without explicitly modeling entity interactions, this work repurposes the variational EM framework from probabilistic logic networks [28, 30] to treat region-phrase and frame-sentence alignments as latent variables. The key conceptual departure is using autoregressive relationship reconstruction as the M-step training signal, so the model learns spatial and temporal grounding as a byproduct of recovering inter-entity interactions rather than through direct supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The weakly-supervised formulation addresses a practical scalability bottleneck: per-frame bounding box annotation is prohibitively expensive for large-scale video corpora, and this paper demonstrates that video-sentence correspondence alone can drive meaningful spatio-temporal grounding. However, absolute performance remains low (14.45 m_VIoU on VidSTG), and the method depends on a pre-trained Faster R-CNN detector for region proposals, which constrains grounding to detectable objects. Open questions include whether the variational EM framework extends to longer videos or more complex multi-entity scenes, and how the approach compares to emerging end-to-end transformer-based grounding methods that may bypass the need for explicit region proposals.
