## Problem

Existing point cloud registration methods rely on rotation-sensitive networks trained with rotation augmentation to approximate invariance, making them fragile to unseen rotations, parameter-heavy, and unable to focus on learning structurally distinctive descriptors. Patch-wise alternatives avoid this but are computationally expensive and suffer from information loss or sensitivity to occlusion. The paper addresses how to achieve truly rotation-equivariant feature extraction that is simultaneously lightweight, robust to pose variation, and discriminative.

## Input / Output

The method takes two partially overlapped 3D point clouds (source P and target Q) as input, with no requirement for overlap prior or pre-aligned frames. It produces a rigid transformation (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) aligning source to target, derived from point-level correspondences. Internally, the backbone operates at 1/8 resolution (three downsampling stages) for superpoint-level features and at 1/4 resolution for point-level features, with correspondences established in a coarse-to-fine hierarchy before the final pose is solved.

## Method

PARE-Net builds a hierarchical backbone on PARE-Conv, a rotation-equivariant convolution that replaces KPConv's fixed linear kernel-to-point distance with a learned correlation network: a VN-MLP extracts equivariant spatial features from relative coordinates and their cross products, an L2-norm layer converts them to rotation-invariant scalars, and an MLP predicts per-kernel correlation scores that dynamically assemble shadow kernel weights. A Geometric Transformer module performs superpoint matching on rotation-invariant features, followed by LightGlue-style point matching within matched patches. The key architectural insight is a feature-based hypothesis proposer: each matched correspondence's rotation-equivariant features (which encode local structure orientation) directly yield one pose hypothesis via closed-form SVD, eliminating the multi-sample requirement of RANSAC and resolving orientation ambiguity. Training uses a contrastive rotation loss that pulls equivariant features of positive correspondences closer under the ground-truth rotation while pushing negative pairs apart, making the features robust to occlusion, noise, and downsampling artifacts.

## Results

On 3DMatch, PARE-Net achieves 95.0% Registration Recall (RR) and 76.9% Inlier Ratio (IR), outperforming the previous best PEAL (94.2% RR) by 0.8% while being 7× faster (0.17s vs. 1.46s) and 2.6× smaller (3.84 MB vs. 9.83 MB). On the more challenging 3DLoMatch (10–30% overlap), PARE-Net reaches 80.5% RR, surpassing PEAL (78.8%), GeoTrans (74.3%), and RoITR (74.1%) by 1.7%, 6.2%, and 6.4% respectively. On KITTI Odometry, it achieves the lowest translation error (4.9 cm) and ties for the best TR (99.8%) with the smallest model size (2.08 MB) and fastest inference (0.21 s). On Rotated 3DLoMatch, PARE-Net's TR actually increases by 0.5% (81.3→81.8%), whereas non-equivariant methods like FCGF drop by 15.1% and PEAL by 2.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PARE-Net outperforms all 12 baselines on Registration Recall across every dataset. Against the strongest competitor PEAL [36], it gains +0.8% RR on 3DMatch (95.0 vs. 94.2) and +1.7% on 3DLoMatch (80.5 vs. 78.8) while running 7× faster (0.17 s vs. 1.46 s). Versus the coarse-to-fine methods GeoTrans [25] and RoITR [35], the gains are larger on low-overlap data: +6.2% and +6.4% RR on 3DLoMatch respectively, attributed to the position-aware equivariant convolution extracting more discriminative descriptors. Among equivariant-based methods, PARE-Net beats RoReg [28] by +22.4% RR on 3DLoMatch (80.5 vs. 70.1) and YOHO [27] by +14.5% (80.5 vs. 66.0), while also being 3–4× smaller and 10–16× faster. BUFFER [1] is the lightest baseline (0.92 MB) but trails PARE-Net by +10.2% RR on 3DLoMatch. On KITTI Odometry, PARE-Net ties for the best TR (99.8%) with GeoTrans, CoFiNet, Predator, and BUFFER, but achieves the lowest translation error (4.9 cm vs. 6.2 cm for GeoTrans) at a fraction of the model size (2.08 MB vs. 25.50 MB). On Rotated 3DLoMatch, PARE-Net is the only method whose TR increases under rotation (+0.5%), while FCGF [7] drops by 15.1%, GeoTrans [25] by 2.8%, and PEAL [36] by 2.5%, confirming the robustness advantage of the equivariant design.

## Relation to Prior Work

PARE-Net sits at the intersection of three prior threads: (1) the Vector Neuron (VN) framework for SO(3)-equivariant point cloud processing, which the authors extend with position-aware shadow kernel points and a learned correlation network to overcome VN's neglect of local spatial structure; (2) the KPConv kernel-point mechanism, repurposed here with rotation-invariant correlation scores instead of distance-based ones; and (3) the coarse-to-fine matching pipeline of GeoTrans and the matchability/saliency disentanglement of LightGlue, which provide the matching scaffold. The key conceptual departure from prior equivariant methods (YOHO, RoReg, BUFFER) is the use of equivariant features not merely for orientation prediction but as the direct basis for a per-correspondence pose hypothesis, replacing RANSAC-style multi-point sampling with a single-point SVD solve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 3.84 MB equivariant backbone can match or exceed methods an order of magnitude larger (YOHO, RoReg at ~12 MB) while running 7× faster than PEAL, suggesting that careful equivariant inductive bias can substitute for brute-force augmentation and heavy group convolutions. The feature-based hypothesis proposer offers a clean, generalizable alternative to RANSAC that any equivariant-feature pipeline could adopt. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-rigid or very large-scale outdoor scenes, how it behaves under severe noise beyond the tested degradation, and whether the contrastive rotation loss generalizes to other equivariant architectures beyond VN-based convolutions.
