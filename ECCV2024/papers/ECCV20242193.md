## Problem

Hyperbolic neural networks learn embeddings that capture hierarchical structure but often distort the geometric relationships present in the original Euclidean feature space. Comparing distributions before and after embedding is non-trivial because Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces differ fundamentally in curvature and dimensionality, and no prior work had formalized such a comparison for HNNs. This paper asks: can we regularize HNN training to explicitly preserve the pairwise geometric structure of the input distribution in the hyperbolic embedding?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For few-shot image classification, the method consumes RGB images (MiniImageNet 84×84, CUB 224×224) processed through a CNN backbone (Conv4, ResNet10/12/18) to produce Euclidean feature vectors, which are then mapped into a Poincaré ball (curvature −0.08 for 1-shot, −0.01 for 5-shot) and classified by distance-to-origin. For graph tasks, raw node feature vectors (11–2,325 dimensions) are embedded into a Poincaré ball or Lorentz model (curvature −1) and used for node classification (projected back to Euclidean space for a linear classifier) or link prediction (hyperbolic distances converted to edge probabilities via the Fermi-Dirac algorithm).

## Method

The core contribution is a Gromov-Monge (GM) distance regularization added to the HNN training loss. The HNN layers are treated as an explicit transport map T from the Euclidean feature space to the hyperbolic space, and the GM penalty computes the expected squared mismatch between pairwise Euclidean costs c_X(x,x′) and pairwise hyperbolic costs c_H(T(x),T(x′)) over all pairs in a mini-batch. This avoids the intractable pushforward constraint of the full GM problem by simply minimizing the cost over the hypothesis class of HNNs. A generalization theorem (Theorem 1) provides an upper bound on the gap between the empirical GM and the distributional GM under a bi-Lipschitz condition on T, ensuring the training-set regularization transfers to the underlying data distribution. The regularization is applied as β·GM(T;{x_i}) added to the standard task loss (cross-entropy or negative log-likelihood), with β tuned on validation data, and is validated across hyperbolic ProtoNet, Vanilla HNN, HGCN, and HyboNet on both image and graph benchmarks.

## Results

On few-shot image classification, GW regularization consistently improves accuracy across all CNN backbones and both datasets. On MiniImageNet 1-Shot 5-Way, ResNet18+GW reaches 58.51% vs. 57.04% for the baseline (+1.47 pp), while on CUB 1-Shot 5-Way, Conv4+GW achieves 68.67% vs. 64.66% (+4.01 pp). For 5-Shot 5-Way, the largest gains appear on CUB (ResNet12+GW: 85.66% vs. 82.90%, +2.76 pp) and MiniImageNet (ResNet12+GW: 73.04% vs. 70.32%, +2.72 pp). On graph tasks, GW regularization lifts AUC by up to 1.63 pp (HNN+GW on Disease link prediction: 94.45% vs. 92.83%) and F1 by up to 7.22 pp (HNN+GW on Disease node classification: 65.62% vs. 58.40%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Hyperbolic ProtoNet of Khrulkov et al. [14], GW regularization yields consistent gains of 1.5–4.0 pp in accuracy across all four CNN backbones on both MiniImageNet and CUB, with the largest improvement on CUB 1-Shot (Conv4: +4.01 pp). For graph tasks, the Vanilla HNN of Ganea et al. [10] is outperformed by HNN+GW on every dataset: AUC improves by 0.96–1.63 pp on link prediction (Disease, Airport, Cora) and F1 improves by 1.6–4.9 pp on node classification. HGCN [5] is similarly surpassed, with AUC gains of 0.11–2.39 pp on link prediction and F1 gains of 0.91–5.31 pp on node classification (e.g., Cornell: 88.64% vs. 83.33%). HyboNet [6], the strongest baseline on most graph datasets, also benefits from GW regularization, achieving AUC improvements of 0.29–1.49 pp on link prediction and F1 gains of 1.96–6.80 pp on node classification (e.g., Wisconsin: 80.87% vs. 74.07%). In all 24 model–dataset combinations tested, the GW-regularized variant matches or exceeds the unregularized baseline, with no observed regressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: hyperbolic neural networks (HNNs) that learn embeddings in Poincaré or Lorentz spaces, and the Gromov-Wasserstein/Gromov-Monge (GW/GM) distance for comparing distributions across heterogeneous metric spaces. Prior HNN work (Ganea et al., HGCN, HyboNet, hyperbolic ProtoNet) transitions Euclidean features into hyperbolic space via simple exponential maps without explicitly preserving the pairwise geometric structure of the input. The paper extends the GM-based geometry-preserving embedding of Lee et al. [16]—originally developed in a generative modeling context—into an end-to-end HNN training loop, treating the HNN layers themselves as the transport map and adding the empirical GM distance as a regularization penalty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contribution provides a principled, distributional criterion for ensuring that hyperbolic embeddings faithfully reflect the geometry of the original feature space, backed by a generalization bound (Theorem 1) under a bi-Lipschitz condition. However, the bi-Lipschitz assumption is strong and may not hold for arbitrary HNN layers, and the O(nm²) pairwise computation becomes a bottleneck when the number of samples far exceeds the feature dimension—a regime the authors explicitly flag as future work. The consistent but modest gains (typically 1–3 percentage points) across many architectures and datasets suggest the regularization is a useful but not transformative addition; its broader impact will depend on whether it composes well with more complex HNN architectures and scales to large-scale settings.
