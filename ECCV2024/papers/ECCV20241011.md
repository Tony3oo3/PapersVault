## Problem

Novel view synthesis from casual monocular videos is difficult because existing NeRF-based dynamic methods require days of per-scene training and cannot render in real time, while explicit representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting fail on the weak-parallax scenes typical of casual videos, producing floaters and artifacts. No prior method simultaneously handles scene dynamics, weak parallax, and the need for fast training and real-time rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a T-frame monocular RGB video and preprocesses it with off-the-shelf video depth (RCVD or CasualSAM), camera pose estimation, and dynamic mask extraction (semantic segmentation + optical-flow thresholding + Segment Anything refinement). It produces novel view images and depth maps at arbitrary camera poses and timestamps, rendered at 27 FPS on a single GPU after a 15-minute per-video optimization (2000 iterations on an A100). The approach is per-scene rather than generalizable, and the output composites a global static layer with a per-frame dynamic layer.

## Method

The core contribution is a hybrid explicit representation that separates static and dynamic content: static scenes are modeled as a soup of oriented 3D planes (inspired by Piecewise Planar Stereo), each carrying RGBA textures augmented with spherical-harmonic coefficients for view-dependent appearance and view-dependent displacement maps to approximate non-planar geometry. Dynamic content is represented as per-frame point clouds warped via differentiable depth-ordered softmax splatting, with temporal neighbor blending through optical flow to reduce inconsistencies. Static planes are rendered by planar homography warping followed by pixel-wise depth-sorted back-to-front alpha compositing, while the final image blends static and dynamic layers using a soft depth-aware mask. The entire hybrid representation is optimized self-supervisedly with a recipe of photometric (L2 + DSSIM + VGG perceptual), depth-alignment, mask sparsity/smoothness, and plane-transparency total-variation losses, achieving comparable LPIPS to NeRF-based SOTA at over 100× faster training and real-time rendering.

## Results

On the NVIDIA Dynamic Scene dataset, the method achieves LPIPS 0.081 (DynNeRF protocol) and 0.050 (DynIBaR protocol), ranking second-best in perceptual quality behind only DynIBaR (0.027) and RoDynRF (0.065). Training requires just 0.25 GPU-hours (15 minutes on a single A100), which is over 100× faster than NeRF-based methods (74–320 GPU-hours) and 5× faster than 4D-GS (1.2 GPU-hours). Rendering reaches 26.7 FPS at 860×480 resolution, enabling real-time novel view synthesis, compared to 0.04–0.42 FPS for NeRF-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest NeRF-based baseline DynIBaR [35], the method trades a small quality gap (LPIPS 0.050 vs. 0.027 on the [35] protocol) for a 1280× speedup in training (0.25 vs. 320 GPU-hours) and a 590× speedup in rendering (26.7 vs. 0.045 FPS). Compared to DynNeRF [13] and NSFF [34], the method achieves better LPIPS (0.081 vs. 0.082 and 0.199 on [13] protocol; 0.050 vs. 0.070 and 0.062 on [35] protocol) while training 296× and 892× faster respectively. Against RoDynRF [43], which reports the best LPIPS on the [13] protocol (0.065), the method is slightly worse in quality (0.081) but trains 112× faster (0.25 vs. 28 GPU-hours) and renders 202× faster (26.7 vs. 0.132 FPS). Versus the closest explicit-representation competitor 4D-GS [80], the method achieves substantially better LPIPS (0.081 vs. 0.199) at a comparable rendering speed (26.7 vs. 28.6 FPS) and 5× faster training. On the DAVIS dataset with weak-parallax casual videos, the method produces coherent results while DynIBaR introduces noticeable artifacts, and 3D Gaussian Splatting fails entirely due to insufficient parallax cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits explicit plane-based scene representations (multi-plane images, piecewise planar stereo) that predate the NeRF era, augmenting them with spherical harmonics and displacement maps to capture view-dependent effects and non-planar geometry. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based dynamic view synthesis line (DynNeRF, NSFF, RoDynRF, DynIBaR) by replacing implicit neural radiance fields with a hybrid of a global soup-of-planes for static content and per-frame point clouds for dynamics, leveraging the non-neural RGBA compositing formulation from Zhou et al. [91] and depth-ordered softmax splatting from Niklaus et al. [51]. The work also positions itself against 3D Gaussian Splatting, demonstrating that 3DGS's reliance on strong multi-view parallax cues causes it to fail on casual monocular videos with primarily rotational camera motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× speedup in both training (15 min vs. days) and rendering (27 FPS real-time vs. sub-1 FPS) while achieving comparable LPIPS to SOTA NeRF-based methods makes high-quality dynamic novel view synthesis practical for casual video applications that were previously infeasible. Key limitations remain: the method depends heavily on preprocessed depth and pose quality, cannot separate objects with subtle motion from static backgrounds, and the per-frame dynamic representation cannot synthesize sub-timesteps for slow-motion. Open directions include incorporating temporal frame interpolation, handling volumetric 360° scenes, and reducing sensitivity to imperfect monocular depth estimates.
