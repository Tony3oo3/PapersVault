## Problem

Existing multi-person pose forecasting methods treat all individuals equally within a global interaction, failing to capture that each person plays a different role and exerts varying influence in a scene. Additionally, the accumulation of prior knowledge about high-frequency social interactions (e.g., reciprocal gestures) remains unexplored in human pose forecasting, limiting the model's ability to perform semantically grounded predictions rather than mere trajectory extrapolation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a historical 3D skeleton sequence of N people (50 frames for multi-person datasets, 10 frames for Human3.6M) where each frame contains J joint positions in 3D space. The last historical pose is repeated T−t times to pad the input to the full prediction horizon. Poses are mapped to a feature space via Discrete Cosine Transform before processing, and predictions are returned to 3D spatial space via inverse DCT. The framework handles variable numbers of people (1, 3, 6, or 10) without architectural changes.

## Method

IAFormer combines a GCN-based Multi-Pose Encoder/Decoder with two novel modules. The Interaction Perceptron Module (IPM) quantifies each person's influence by computing an Amplitude Weight (from frame-to-frame pose differences mapped through self-attention) and a Trajectory Weight (from the log-normalized MSE between each person's historical root trajectory and the interaction center), then fuses individual features weighted by the product of these two scores. The Interaction Prior Learning Module (IPLM) maintains a fixed-size Interaction Knowledge Space of embedding vectors that are updated online during training via nearest-neighbor matching; at inference it returns a confidence-weighted interaction feature based on the ratio of nearest to second-nearest distances. The main transformer block uses a two-branch attention structure where one branch cross-attends individual features against IPM output and the other against IPLM output, then averages the two branches before a feed-forward residual connection. Training optimizes a combined loss of spatial JPE, a CNN-based temporal consistency term, and the knowledge-space update loss.

## Results

On CMU-Mocap(UMPM) (3 persons), IAFormer achieves 96 mm average JPE and 66 mm average APE, outperforming the previous best multi-person method JRFormer (99 mm JPE, 71 mm APE) by 3 mm and 5 mm respectively. On the 10-person Mix2 dataset, IAFormer reaches 108 mm JPE and 69 mm APE, surpassing JRFormer by 16 mm JPE and 6 mm APE. On CHI3D (2-person close interaction), IAFormer achieves 129 mm average JPE, beating TBIFormer by 13 mm. On the single-person Human3.6M benchmark, IAFormer attains 39.8 mm JPE at 320 ms, outperforming AuxFormer (43.4 mm) by 3.6 mm in short-term forecasting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAFormer outperforms all five multi-person baselines (MSR-GCN, HRI, MRT, TBIFormer, JRFormer) on CMU-Mocap(UMPM) in both JPE and APE, with the largest margin over TBIFormer (11 mm JPE, 10 mm APE). On Mix2 (10 persons), IAFormer achieves the best JPE (108 mm) and APE (69 mm), beating JRFormer by 16 mm JPE and 6 mm APE. On Mix1 (6 persons), IAFormer achieves the best APE (65 mm) but trails JRFormer by 6 mm in JPE (114 vs. 108 mm), suggesting the interaction prior is most beneficial when many individuals are minimally involved. On CHI3D, IAFormer (129 mm avg JPE) surpasses TBIFormer (142 mm) by 13 mm and PGBIG (172 mm) by 43 mm, confirming the advantage of interaction-aware modeling in close two-person scenarios. On single-person Human3.6M, IAFormer leads all baselines at short horizons (≤400 ms), beating AuxFormer by 3.6 mm at 320 ms, but degrades at 1000 ms (130.4 mm vs. AuxFormer's 107.0 mm), indicating the interaction prior provides less benefit for long-horizon single-person prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

IAFormer builds directly on the DCT/iDCT pose representation and multi-GCN encoder/decoder architecture from Mao et al. (ICCV 2019) and Ma et al. (CVPR 2022), repurposing them as the backbone for a transformer-based multi-person forecasting pipeline. It departs from prior multi-person methods (TBIFormer, JRFormer, MRT) that model interactions globally across all participants by introducing per-individual interaction weighting via the Interaction Perceptron Module, and by borrowing the discrete embedding space mechanism from VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., NeurIPS 2017) to construct an Interaction Knowledge Space for prior learning. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first to incorporate accumulated interaction prior knowledge into human pose forecasting, framing this as a conceptual gap rather than a methodological one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a reasonable gap—existing multi-person pose forecasters treat all scene participants symmetrically, ignoring that only a subset actively drives an interaction—and the IPM weighting mechanism is a clean, interpretable way to inject that asymmetry. However, the IPLM's prior learning is implemented as a simple nearest-neighbor update in a fixed-size embedding space, raising open questions about whether it captures genuinely reusable interaction semantics or merely memorizes frequent training patterns. Notably, IAFormer's 1000 ms JPE on Human3.6M (130.4 mm) is substantially worse than AuxFormer (107.0 mm) and PGBIG (110.3 mm), suggesting the interaction-aware machinery may not transfer well to long-horizon single-person prediction. Broader impact is limited by the reliance on motion-capture datasets with small, fixed group sizes; generalization to truly open-world, variable-count scenes remains untested.
