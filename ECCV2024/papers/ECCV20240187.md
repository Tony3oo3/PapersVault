## Problem

Current text-to-image diffusion models can only specify object colors through linguistic color names (e.g., 'red', 'beige'), which map to broad, imprecise color ranges and cannot reproduce a user's exact chosen color. Existing T2I personalization methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion) fail at this task because they entangle color with shape in the training images, producing inconsistent or inter-mixed colors. This paper formalizes the 'color prompt learning' problem and proposes a solution that achieves precise, user-specified color generation.

## Input / Output

The user provides an RGB triplet or color coordinates, and the system auto-generates a small set of 2D (circle, square, hexagon, triangle) or 3D (sphere, cylinder, hexagon, cube, cone) geometric shapes rendered in that exact color as training instances. After training, the output is a pair of learned modifier tokens (color token c* and shape token s*) that can be inserted into arbitrary text prompts to generate objects in the target color. The learned tokens also support downstream tasks: linear interpolation between color tokens for intermediate hues, and P2P-style cross-attention swapping for recoloring objects in existing images.

## Method

ColorPeel generates multiple basic geometric shapes (at least two) in the same target color and trains a Stable Diffusion v1.4 model to learn separate color and shape tokens, so the model must analogize the shared color attribute across different shapes to achieve disentanglement. Following Custom Diffusion, the key and value projection matrices in the UNet cross-attention layers are jointly optimized with the new tokens. The key novelty is a Cross-Attention Alignment (CAA) loss (Eq. 4) that maximizes cosine similarity between the color-token and shape-token attention maps, forcing both to concentrate on the same object region rather than leaking into the background. This addresses the attention leakage problem that causes color inter-mixing in baselines. The final objective (Eq. 5) combines the standard LDM noise-reconstruction loss with the CAA loss weighted by λ (optimal at 0.2). The same framework extends to learning texture and material tokens by swapping the training shapes for textured/material-rendered geometries.

## Results

On the coarse-grained color prompt learning task, ColorPeel (2D) achieves the lowest ΔE of 20.45 and ΔE_ch of 15.29 in CIE Lab space, while ColorPeel (3D) achieves the lowest MAE (Hue) of 21.35° at 100% mask coverage. These represent roughly 44–58% reductions over the best baselines (Rich-Text at ΔE=36.62; Custom Diffusion at ΔE=48.47). ColorPeel also trains in 19 minutes, comparable to Custom Diffusion (24 min) and substantially faster than Textual Inversion (118 min) or DreamBooth (56 min). A 15-participant user study using Thurstone Case V confirms ColorPeel is statistically significantly preferred over all four competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColorPeel outperforms all five baselines by a wide margin on every color-fidelity metric. Against the strongest non-personalization baseline, Rich-Text [14] (ΔE=36.62), ColorPeel (2D) reduces ΔE by 44% (20.45) and MAE (Hue) by 77% (21.46° vs. 93.51°). Among personalization methods, Custom Diffusion [27] is the closest competitor (ΔE=48.47, MAE Hue 100%=78.43°), yet ColorPeel still halves ΔE and reduces hue error by ~73%. DreamBooth [43] and Textual Inversion [12] perform worst on ΔE (50.71 and 48.98 respectively) and also show the largest hue deviations, confirming the color–shape entanglement problem the paper targets. In the user study, ColorPeel is statistically significantly preferred over all four competing methods (TI, Rich-Text, DB, CD) under the Thurstone Case V model. Notably, integrating ColorPeel's CAA loss into DreamBooth improves DB's ΔE from 50.71 to 23.53, suggesting the cross-attention alignment mechanism is broadly applicable beyond the Custom Diffusion backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

ColorPeel builds directly on the Custom Diffusion training paradigm (optimizing key/value projection matrices alongside new tokens) and borrows the P2P cross-attention swapping mechanism for its image-editing extension. Conceptually, it departs from the T2I personalization literature (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion), which has focused on binding tokens to concrete visual concepts, by reframing the problem as learning abstract color attributes through shape-color disentanglement on synthetic geometric objects. The cross-attention alignment loss is inspired by DPL but inverted in purpose—maximizing overlap between color and shape attention maps rather than minimizing it—making it a targeted adaptation rather than a novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in creative and design workflows where users need pixel-precise color control rather than approximate linguistic color names, and demonstrates that a surprisingly simple training recipe (a handful of colored geometric shapes plus a cosine-similarity attention loss) can close a large color-fidelity gap. Limitations include evaluation only on Stable Diffusion v1.4, reliance on synthetic training shapes that may not capture real-world color interactions (lighting, material reflectance), and the fact that the large quantitative margins are partly attributable to baselines not designed for this task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-object scenes with competing color constraints, to newer diffusion architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), and to more complex attribute combinations beyond single-hue color.
