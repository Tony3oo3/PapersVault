## Problem

Pixel-based Masked Image Modeling (MIM) learns representations that fail to capture high-level semantics without supervised fine-tuning, because the pixel reconstruction objective forces the model to retain low-level details. The natural remedy—reconstructing learned latent representations instead of raw pixels (Latent MIM)—is unstable in practice due to trivial solutions and high inter-patch semantic correlation, yet prior work has not systematically identified or addressed these optimization challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB images, splits them into a 14×14 patch grid, and partitions patches into a 10% visible / 90% target split using non-contiguous stochastic grids (patches separated by a random gap of ~4 pixels to reduce spatial redundancy). The output is a set of patch-level latent representations from a ViT-B encoder, which are evaluated via nearest-neighbor search, linear probing, fine-tuning, and unsupervised clustering for segmentation.

## Method

Latent MIM uses an online ViT-B encoder, a momentum-updated target encoder, and a shallow 3-layer cross-attention decoder to predict target-patch latents from visible-patch latents. Four key design choices address the identified challenges: (1) a momentum target encoder with stop-gradient prevents representation collapse; (2) a patch-discrimination InfoNCE loss replaces direct MSE reconstruction, encouraging spatially diverse representations; (3) a 90% mask ratio combined with non-contiguous stochastic grids and an explicit inter-patch similarity regularizer reduces the triviality of predicting nearby patches; (4) the cross-attention decoder initializes mask tokens with a softmax-weighted blend of neighboring visible-patch latents and applies an MLP projector to visible features, providing direct spatial conditioning at every layer while remaining shallow enough to avoid absorbing the encoder's semantic role.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (ViT-B/16, 800 epochs), Latent MIM achieves 50.1% top-1 nearest-neighbor accuracy and 72.0% top-1 linear probing accuracy, surpassing MAE (12.2% NN, 67.8% LP) by +37.9% and +4.2% respectively. On DAVIS-2017 video object segmentation, Latent MIM reaches 65.5 J&Fm, outperforming MAE (57.5) and data2vec (28.5). In 16-shot transfer learning across seven datasets, Latent MIM consistently leads, e.g., 89.2% on Caltech101 vs. 80.5% (MAE) and 76.6% (data2vec), and 79.8% on OxfordPets vs. 60.2% (MAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent MIM decisively outperforms all four baselines on ImageNet-1k. Against MAE (the strongest pixel-level MIM baseline), it gains +37.9% in NN accuracy (50.1% vs. 12.2%) and +4.2% in LP accuracy (72.0% vs. 67.8%), despite using half the training epochs (800 vs. 1600). Against SimMIM, the LP gap is +15.3% (72.0% vs. 56.7%). Among prior latent MIM methods, Latent MIM surpasses data2vec by +24.4% NN (50.1% vs. 25.7%) and +11.7% LP (72.0% vs. 60.3%), and ConMIM by +32.7% LP (72.0% vs. 39.3%). The advantage extends to downstream tasks: on DAVIS-2017 video object segmentation, Latent MIM (65.5 J&Fm) beats MAE (57.5) by +8.0 and data2vec (28.5) by +37.0; in 16-shot transfer, it leads on all seven datasets, with the largest margins on OxfordPets (+19.6 over MAE) and SUN397 (+26.5 over MAE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Masked Image Modeling paradigm (MAE, SimMIM) by replacing pixel-level reconstruction targets with learned latent representations, drawing on BYOL's momentum encoder and stop-gradient mechanisms to prevent representation collapse, and InfoNCE-based contrastive losses to enforce patch diversity. Prior latent MIM attempts (data2vec, ConMIM, I-JEPA) either used mask tokens instead of patch removal, relied on fixed pre-trained features, or treated latent reconstruction as a supplementary objective; this paper is the first to systematically identify and address the four core optimization challenges (joint target optimization, reconstruction objective, inter-patch correlation, decoder design) that make pure latent MIM unstable. The framework is positioned as a principled middle ground between pixel-based MIM (local but low-level) and contrastive learning (semantic but batch-dependent).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that latent MIM can simultaneously achieve high-level semantics (50.1% NN, 72.0% LP on ImageNet-1k) and the spatially diverse, localizable patch representations characteristic of MIM, without requiring supervised fine-tuning for strong performance. The unsupervised segmentation and video object segmentation results suggest the learned representations encode both semantic and spatial structure in a way that is directly exploitable. Open questions include whether the specific design choices (90% mask ratio, non-contiguous grids, 3-layer cross-attention decoder) generalize to larger backbones (ViT-L/H) and different architectures, and whether the framework can be extended to video or multi-modal settings. The reliance on ImageNet-100 for ablations and a single ViT-B/16 configuration for the full-scale results limits confidence in the generality of individual design decisions.
