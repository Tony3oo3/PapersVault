## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models tightly couple a specific language model with a specific vision model during joint training, making it nontrivial to swap in more advanced components as they emerge in their respective fields. The problem of integrating any two unrelated, independently pre-trained language and generative vision models for text-to-image generation remains unexplored, leaving a gap between the modules inside diffusion pipelines and the state-of-the-art models available in NLP and CV.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242159/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts a free-form text prompt (short, long at 20–70 words, or compositional) and produces a 512×512 RGB image via a 50-step DDIM sampling process with classifier-free guidance at scale 7.5. The framework is plug-and-play: any combination of a language model (CLIP, T5-Small/Base/Large, Llama-2-7B) and a generative vision model (U-Net from LDM or SD 1.4, ViT from PixArt) can be paired without architectural redesign, as the adapter automatically handles dimension mismatches between the text embedding space and the vision model's cross-attention input space.

## Method

LaVi-Bridge freezes the original weights of both the chosen language model f and vision model g, then injects LoRA (rank 32) into all linear layers of their attention blocks (and into convolutional layers of ResBlocks for U-Net backbones) to introduce small trainable parameter deltas Δθ₁ and Δθ₂. A two-layer feedforward adapter h is inserted between the language model's output embeddings and the vision model's cross-attention keys/values, projecting from the language model's embedding dimension to the vision model's expected dimension. Only Δθ₁, Δθ₂, and h are trained on ~1M text-image pairs (COCO 2017 + internal data) with AdamW at lr 1e-4 for 50k steps, while all original model parameters remain untouched. This design is structure-agnostic, accommodating encoder-only, encoder-decoder, and decoder-only language models as well as U-Net and Transformer vision models, and requires far fewer trainable parameters than retraining a full diffusion pipeline.

## Results

With a fixed U-Net (Stable Diffusion V1.4), Llama-2 achieves the best text alignment across all prompt types: CLIP score 0.3172 (short), 0.3231 (long), and the highest scores on all six T2I-CompBench compositional metrics (e.g., Color 0.4859, Spatial 0.1914). Llama-2 also attains the best FID of 21.80 on short prompts. With a fixed T5-Large language model, the PixArt Transformer vision model yields the best image quality (FID 23.02, aesthetics 6.145 short / 6.406 long) and the best CLIP scores (0.3172 short, 0.3210 long), outperforming both U-Net variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original Stable Diffusion V1.4 (CLIP text encoder + U-Net, FID 20.32, aesthetics 5.899, CLIP score 0.3132 on short prompts). When LaVi-Bridge is applied to the identical CLIP+U-Net pair, it shows a modest degradation in image quality (FID 23.57 vs. 20.32) and text alignment (CLIP 0.3102 vs. 0.3132), confirming that the bridge introduces a small overhead when no model upgrade is made. However, the key advantage emerges when upgrading components: replacing CLIP with Llama-2 improves FID to 21.80 and CLIP score to 0.3172, surpassing the original SD on text alignment while approaching its image quality. Replacing the U-Net with PixArt's Transformer (while keeping T5-Large) boosts aesthetics from 5.881 to 6.145 and CLIP score from 0.3156 to 0.3172, demonstrating that a stronger vision model yields both better image quality and slightly better text alignment. Overall, LaVi-Bridge does not aim to beat the original SD with the same components but to enable plug-and-play upgrades that consistently improve the corresponding modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaVi-Bridge builds directly on LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) as the parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism and on the standard text-to-image diffusion architecture (cross-attention between text embeddings and image features) as the integration point. It departs from prior text-to-image diffusion models—Stable Diffusion, PixArt, ParaDiffusion—where the language and vision components are jointly trained and tightly coupled, by treating them as independently swappable modules connected through a lightweight feedforward adapter. The adapter concept is borrowed from NLP adapter tuning but repurposed here to bridge the representational gap between unrelated pre-trained encoders, a problem the authors explicitly identify as unexplored in the text-to-image literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As language models (e.g., Llama-2, future LLMs) and generative vision models (e.g., DiT, PixArt) advance rapidly in their respective domains, LaVi-Bridge offers a practical, low-cost pathway to upgrade one component without retraining the entire diffusion pipeline, which is valuable for practitioners who cannot afford full-scale retraining. However, the evaluation is limited to 512×512 resolution, 1M training pairs, and 50k steps, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, larger model pairs, or more diverse architectures. An interesting open question is whether the adapter can be replaced by learned cross-modal projections or whether the current two-layer feedforward design is sufficient for increasingly large representational gaps between state-of-the-art language and vision models.
