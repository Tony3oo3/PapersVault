## Problem

Existing inherently explainable attribution methods suffer from two coupled failures: an incompleteness problem where the selector highlights only a subset of discriminative features (optimizing sufficiency but not completeness), and an interlocking problem where the jointly trained selector and predictor degenerate into selecting and fitting on noise, creating a self-reinforcing cycle that is hard to escape. These issues limit the faithfulness and practical utility of attribution maps, especially in high-stakes domains like medical imaging where all relevant regions must be surfaced.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image (H×W×C) and jointly produces a class label (K-way classification) and a per-pixel attribution map S(x) ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} at the same spatial resolution as the input. The attribution map is used as a soft mask: masked-in features x_s (selected pixels blended with dataset-mean fill elsewhere) are fed to the predictor, while masked-out features x_{1-s} are fed to a separate detector. The selector is a lightweight Lite R-ASPP network with a MobileNetV3 backbone, while the predictor and detector are both ResNet-18.

## Method

COMET introduces a three-module framework (selector, predictor, pre-trained detector) with a novel adversarial-style objective: the selector is trained to minimize the predictor's cross-entropy on masked-in features while simultaneously maximizing the detector's cross-entropy on masked-out features (L_S = L_P(x_s) − a·L_D(x_{1-s}) + b·R(S(x))). The detector is pre-trained on full images and kept frozen during joint training, so it retains the ability to recognize discriminative features regardless of what the selector masks out. This design addresses incompleteness by penalizing any residual label information in masked-out regions, and breaks the interlocking cycle because the independent detector can still identify overlooked discriminative features even when the predictor has already fit on noise. A threshold-based sparsity regularizer R(S(x)) = max(1/(HW)·ΣS(x) − t, 0) prevents the trivial solution of selecting all pixels.

## Results

COMET achieves 94.33% accuracy on ImageNet-9 and 56.47% on NICO++, surpassing the black-box ResNet18 (91.16% / 40.20%) and all inherently explainable baselines. On noisy variants (10% pixels removed), COMET retains 74.81% on ImageNet-9 and 33.12% on NICO++, again the highest among all methods. For attribution quality, COMET scores 72.5 on area-under-IOU (an8Flower) and 76.38 PxAP (localization), substantially outperforming the next-best baselines (B-cos: 39.2 IOU, 60.36 PxAP). In the fidelity test, removing the top-5% attributed pixels drops COMET's accuracy to 10.64% on ImageNet-9, indicating highly faithful attribution maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMET outperforms all seven baselines on classification accuracy across both clean and noisy settings. Against the black-box ResNet18, COMET gains +3.17 pp on ImageNet-9 and +16.27 pp on NICO++, attributed to the detector ensuring all discriminative features are retained while noise is removed. Among inherently explainable methods, COMET leads B-cos (75.21%) by 19.12 pp on ImageNet-9 and DiET (73.41%) by 20.92 pp; REAL-X and RB-AEM trail further behind (66.13% and 78.40%) due to distribution shift from training the predictor on randomly masked images. For attribution quality, COMET's 72.5 IOU on an8Flower nearly doubles B-cos (39.2) and is 2.9× RT-Saliency (25.3), while its 76.38 PxAP exceeds the best post-hoc method RT-Saliency (60.67) by 15.71 pp. In the fidelity test, COMET's perturbed accuracy (10.64% at top-5% on ImageNet-9) is lower than all baselines, confirming its attribution maps identify the most critical pixels. On robustness (PxAP under 20% pixel removal and background change), COMET scores 73.04 and 74.22 respectively, beating B-cos (57.28 / 57.85) and all other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMET extends the inherently explainable selector-predictor framework (B-cos, REAL-X, RB-AEM, DiET) by addressing two specific failure modes: the incompleteness problem (only sufficient, not complete, feature selection) and the interlocking problem (selector-predictor co-adapting to noise). Rather than isolating the predictor from the selector via random or RBF-style masking as REAL-X and RB-AEM do, COMET introduces a pre-trained feature detector that remains fixed during joint training, providing an independent signal to penalize the selector when discriminative features are masked out. This departs from the adversarial three-module frameworks in domain adaptation and rationalization literature by keeping the detector frozen and pre-trained on full images, avoiding the distribution shift that plagues random-masking approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pre-trained detector offers a clean, distribution-shift-free solution to the interlocking problem that prior methods only partially mitigated, and the completeness objective directly addresses the practical concern that attribution maps may miss critical regions (e.g., tumors in medical imaging). Limitations include the difficulty of setting a uniform sparsity threshold across varying target sizes and longer training time due to the three-module architecture. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full ImageNet or larger datasets, whether it generalizes to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), and whether the fixed detector remains effective as the selector improves over training.
