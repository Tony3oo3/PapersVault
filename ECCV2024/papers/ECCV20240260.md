## Problem

Existing point tracking methods rely on local 2D correlation maps (point-to-region correspondence) that struggle with matching ambiguities in homogeneous regions, repetitive patterns, and co-occurring objects. Dense correspondence methods using 4D correlation are robust to these ambiguities but are computationally intractable for the point tracking setting. The paper bridges this gap by formulating point tracking as a local all-pair correspondence problem, retaining the disambiguation power of 4D correlation while constraining the search space for efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model takes a query point (x_q, y_q, t_q) at an arbitrary time step and a video of T frames (H×W×3), producing a 2D position track and a per-frame occlusion probability in [0,1]. Unlike prior methods that require the query at the first frame or use fixed sliding windows, LocoTrack handles variable-length sequences at test time without hand-designed chaining. Training uses 256×256 resolution on Panning MOVi-E (Kubric), while evaluation spans 256×256 and 384×512 resolutions on TAP-Vid and RoboTAP benchmarks.

## Method

LocoTrack operates in two stages: a global 2D correlation initialization (softargmax over multi-level ResNet18 features) followed by K=4 iterative refinement steps using a local 4D correlation of size 7×7×7×7 around the query and predicted target positions. A lightweight correlation encoder decomposes the 4D volume into two symmetrical 2D-convolution branches (one flattening target dims as channels, the other flattening query dims), producing a compact embedding that captures bidirectional matching smoothness. A 3-layer Transformer with a novel split relative position bias (separate left/right attention heads with linear distance decay) models temporal context and generalizes to arbitrary sequence lengths without sliding-window inference. The refinement model predicts position residuals and occlusion updates using sinusoidal-encoded inter-frame position differences, achieving SOTA accuracy with only 8.2M parameters and ~6× faster throughput than CoTracker.

## Results

On TAP-Vid DAVIS (strided query, 256×256), LocoTrack-S achieves 66.9 AJ, surpassing TAPIR (61.3 AJ) by +5.6 and CoTracker (65.9 AJ at 384×512) by +2.5, while running 3.5× faster than TAPIR (7,244 vs 2,097 points/sec) and 6× faster than CoTracker (6,820 vs 1,146 points/sec at 384×512). LocoTrack-B further improves to 67.8 AJ at 256×256 and 69.4 AJ at 384×512. The model uses only 8.2M parameters (small) / 11.5M (base) with 1.08G FLOPs per point, compared to 5.12G for TAPIR and 4.65G for CoTracker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocoTrack-S outperforms TAPIR by +5.6 AJ on DAVIS (66.9 vs 61.3) and +17.7 AJ on RGB-Stacking (77.4 vs 62.7) at 256×256, while being 3.5× faster in throughput. Against CoTracker at 384×512, LocoTrack-S gains +2.5 AJ on DAVIS (68.4 vs 65.9) with 6× higher throughput (6,821 vs 1,147 pts/s), and LocoTrack-B extends this to +3.5 AJ (69.4 vs 65.9). Both LocoTrack variants substantially surpass TAP-Net (+28.5 AJ on DAVIS) and RAFT (+36.9 AJ on DAVIS), confirming that the local 4D correlation formulation is far more effective than prior 2D-correlation-based or optical-flow-based approaches. LocoTrack-B also edges out FlowTrack (+3.4 AJ on DAVIS at 384×512). The only notable trade-off is a slight degradation at 384×512 relative to 256×256, which the authors attribute to the diminished effective receptive field of the local correlation window at higher resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

LocoTrack departs from the dominant point-to-region formulation in recent point tracking methods (TAPIR, CoTracker, PIPs), which construct local 2D correlation maps between a single query point and a target region. Instead, it repurposes the 4D correlation volume from dense correspondence literature (RAFT, GoCor, NCNet) by constraining it to local neighborhoods around both query and target, yielding a 'local all-pair' correspondence that provides bidirectional matching and smoothness priors. For temporal modeling, it adopts a compact Transformer with relative position bias (borrowed from NLP length-extrapolation work) to replace the sliding-window or 1D-convolution strategies used by prior trackers, enabling variable-length sequence processing without hand-designed chaining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LocoTrack processes 10⁴ query points in roughly one second on a single GPU with only 8.2M parameters, opening the door to real-time near-dense tracking in applications like autonomous driving and robotics. A notable limitation is the performance degradation at higher input resolutions (384×512), attributed to the fixed local correlation window having a diminished effective receptive field; adapting window size to resolution remains an open question. The finding that just 3 Transformer layers with relative position bias suffice to outperform deeper or convolutional alternatives suggests that the bottleneck in prior trackers was the correlation formulation rather than temporal model capacity.
