## Problem

Pre-trained latent diffusion models can generate realistic details for image restoration, but their inherent sampling randomness causes severe temporal flickering when applied frame-by-frame to video super-resolution. Existing real-world VSR methods, which avoid diffusion, are limited by insufficient training data and model capacity for handling complex, unknown degradations. The paper addresses how to exploit the strong generative prior of LDMs for real-world VSR while enforcing temporal consistency across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241170/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video sequence with complex real-world degradations (blur, noise, compression, downsampling) and produces a ×4 high-resolution video sequence. Processing operates in the 8×-downsampled latent space of a VAE, with sequences of 5–6 frames processed jointly; inference uses 50 DDPM sampling steps per sequence and a progressive patch aggregation scheme to handle arbitrary output resolutions. Optical flow (RAFT) is estimated between adjacent LR frames and downsampled to latent resolution to drive the motion-guidance computation.

## Method

MGLD-VSR is a two-stage pipeline built on Stable Diffusion V2.1. In the first stage, a motion-guided diffusion sampling process computes forward and backward optical flow on the LR sequence, warps latent features to neighboring frames, and uses the occlusion-masked L1 warping error as a guidance signal whose gradient is injected into each DDPM reverse step via Langevin dynamics (weight η=10), steering the latent trajectory toward temporally consistent features. In the second stage, a temporal-aware sequence decoder is constructed by inserting 1D temporal convolutions and a Controllable Feature Warping (CFW) module into the frozen VAE decoder, then fine-tuned on HR ground-truth sequences with a composite loss combining per-frame reconstruction, a frame-difference loss, a structure-weighted consistency loss (Sobel-edge-weighted warping error), and a GAN loss. The key insight is that motion dynamics from the LR input can be injected at the latent level to constrain the stochastic diffusion path, while the temporal decoder resolves residual fine-grained discontinuities in pixel space.

## Results

On synthetic VSR benchmarks (REDS4, UDM10, SPMCS), MGLD-VSR achieves the best perceptual quality across all three datasets with LPIPS of 0.3776, 0.3491, and 0.3990, and DISTS of 0.1151, 0.1369, and 0.1934, respectively. On the real-world VideoLQ benchmark, it attains the best no-reference scores of NIQE 3.5346, BRISQUE 21.9839, and DOVER 0.7481, while ranking second on MUSIQ (52.78 vs. RealBasicVSR's 55.97). The method also achieves the best or second-best VMAF on all synthetic datasets (e.g., 39.39 on UDM10, 28.70 on SPMCS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest real-world VSR competitor RealBasicVSR [11], MGLD-VSR wins decisively on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.3776 vs. 0.4190 on REDS4; DISTS 0.1151 vs. 0.1581) and on VideoLQ no-reference scores (NIQE 3.53 vs. 3.69, BRISQUE 21.98 vs. 24.55, DOVER 0.748 vs. 0.740), but trails on MUSIQ (52.78 vs. 55.97) and VMAF on REDS4 (34.32 vs. 34.99). Against the diffusion-based image SR method StableSR [48], MGLD-VSR dominates on every metric across all four datasets, confirming that the motion-guided sampling and temporal decoder effectively resolve the flickering that plagues frame-independent diffusion SR. Against non-diffusion VSR baselines (RVRT [31], DBVSR [39], RealVSR [55]), MGLD-VSR consistently achieves substantially lower LPIPS/DISTS (e.g., 0.3776 vs. 0.6221 for RVRT on REDS4) and higher VMAF, at the cost of slightly lower PSNR/SSIM on some datasets, reflecting the well-known fidelity–perceptual-quality trade-off. Against image-only SR methods (BSRGAN [57], RealESRGAN [51]), MGLD-VSR matches or exceeds their perceptual quality while adding temporal consistency, and clearly outperforms them on VMAF (e.g., 39.39 vs. 36.92 for RealESRGAN on UDM10). In the user study, MGLD-VSR receives more preference votes than both RealBasicVSR and StableSR across 20 VideoLQ sequences evaluated by 10 subjects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based image restoration (StableSR, DiffBIR) to the video domain by introducing a motion-guided sampling mechanism that incorporates optical flow from the LR input into the latent diffusion reverse process. It builds directly on the Stable Diffusion VAE and U-Net architecture, the DDPM reverse scheduler, and the score-based guidance formulation, while adapting the Controllable Feature Warping module from StableSR for the decoder. The key departure from prior real-world VSR methods (RealBasicVSR, RealVSR, DBVSR) is the use of a pre-trained generative prior rather than task-specific training, and the key departure from prior video diffusion models (VideoLDM, SVD) is the conditioning on a specific LR input sequence rather than text prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically address the temporal consistency problem when applying latent diffusion priors to video super-resolution, and the motion-guided sampling strategy is a generalizable technique that could transfer to other video restoration tasks. The paper honestly acknowledges that the method is computationally expensive (50 sampling steps on a large SD model) and that the WE metric actually worsens relative to non-diffusion baselines because the generated details are more aggressive — a trade-off that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether model distillation or faster ODE solvers can make this practical, and whether emerging video diffusion models (e.g., Stable Video Diffusion) could subsume the need for the separate motion-guided mechanism.
