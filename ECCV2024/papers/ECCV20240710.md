## Problem

Existing event denoising methods either rely on hand-crafted thresholds that cannot adapt to varying noise rates, sacrifice temporal granularity by converting events to images or time surfaces, or suffer from unstable performance due to the scarcity of real-world annotated denoising datasets. The paper addresses both the dataset gap and the architectural limitation by introducing the first real-world annotated event denoising dataset (ED24, 21 noise levels) and a transformer model that denoises directly at the event level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240710/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw asynchronous event sequences from a DAVIS346 sensor (346×260), where each event is a (x, y, polarity, timestamp) tuple. During training, segments of N=4096 events are randomly sampled; at inference, the full sequence is processed. Output is a per-event binary score (valid vs. background-activity noise) that, when thresholded, yields a denoised event sequence preserving the original temporal ordering and granularity without any intermediate image or time-surface representation.

## Method

EDformer segments the event stream and applies two parallel branches (large-scale and small-scale) that each perform spatiotemporal embedding—sparse convolution over pixel-stacked event images for spatial features and polarity-specific MLPs over timestamps for temporal features—followed by three transformer attention layers: LXformer (KNN-based local spatiotemporal attention), SCformer (ball-query-based local spatial attention), and GXformer (subsampled KNN-based global spatiotemporal attention). An attention feature fusion module then combines the two branches' outputs via a softmax-weighted Hadamard product to produce the final per-event classification. The key design insight is that the three attention mechanisms implicitly capture the same spatiotemporal cues exploited by density-based, image-filtering, and global-motion denoising paradigms, but in a learned, threshold-free manner that generalizes across 21 noise levels. Training uses cross-entropy loss with AdamW (lr=0.001, 60 epochs) on the ED24 dataset, and the sparse attention design reduces complexity from O(N²) to O(Nk).

## Results

On the DND21 dataset, EDformer achieves the highest AUC across all five shot noise rates (1–10 Hz/pixel) on both Hotel-bar and Driving scenes, reaching 0.9928/0.9541 at 1 Hz/pixel and 0.9699/0.9264 at 10 Hz/pixel. On the MESR metric, EDformer attains the best score on RGBDAVIS (Indoor 1.051, Outdoor 1.17) and on E-MLB Night ND64 (1.099), while ranking second on E-MLB Daylight and DND21. The advantage over the strongest baseline (TS) grows with noise level: at 10 Hz/pixel, EDformer leads by 0.008 AUC on Hotel-bar and 0.006 on Driving, whereas at 1 Hz/pixel the margin is 0.021 and 0.023 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDformer outperforms all 10 baselines on AUC across every noise rate and scene in Table 2. The closest competitor is TS (time-surface based, [20]), which trails EDformer by 0.021 AUC on Hotel-bar at 1 Hz/pixel and by 0.008 at 10 Hz/pixel; the gap narrows at higher noise but EDformer still leads. Learning-based baselines EDnCNN [3] and MLPF [15] show moderate performance (AUC ≈ 0.90–0.97) but degrade more sharply at high noise rates compared to EDformer. Traditional methods (BAF, KNoise, DWF) fall well behind, with KNoise [19] achieving only 0.64–0.68 AUC, confirming that hand-crafted thresholds cannot adapt to varying noise levels. On the MESR metric (Table 3), EDformer achieves the best score on RGBDAVIS (1.051/1.17) and E-MLB Night ND64 (1.099), but is slightly behind EventZoom [10] on E-MLB Daylight ND1 (0.952 vs. 0.996) and on DND21 (1.041 vs. 1.059), indicating that EDformer's advantage is most pronounced under low-light, high-noise conditions rather than in well-lit settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event denoising literature by departing from the dominant learning-based paradigm (EDnCNN, EventZoom) that converts event sequences into time surfaces or event images, instead operating directly on individual events to preserve full spatiotemporal granularity. The architectural design draws on the Point Transformer's vector attention formulation for irregular metric spaces and adapts sparse convolution and ball-query neighborhood retrieval from 3D point cloud processing to the event domain. The three-way attention decomposition (local spatiotemporal via KNN, local spatial via ball query, global spatiotemporal via farthest-point subsampling) and the large/small-scale dual-branch design are novel combinations not seen in prior event denoising work. The ED24 dataset also departs from the simulated DND21 by capturing real BA noise under 21 controlled illumination levels, addressing a gap the authors identify in prior training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ED24 dataset is the first real-world annotated event denoising benchmark with 21 noise levels, filling a critical gap that has limited generalization of prior denoising models. The event-by-event formulation and the demonstrated zebrafish microscopy application open a practical pathway for event cameras in low-light biomedical imaging where RGB sensors fail. However, the authors acknowledge two limitations: merging well-lit noise-free events with low-light BA noise may not fully reflect real-world sparsity patterns, and noise characteristics may differ across sensor types. Open questions include whether the spatiotemporal correlation features transfer across event camera hardware and how valid-event density changes with illumination affect denoising performance.
