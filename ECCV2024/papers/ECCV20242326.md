## Problem

Continual learning methods face a stability-plasticity dilemma: approaches that prevent catastrophic forgetting (e.g., regularization-based methods like EWC) inevitably constrain the model's capacity to learn new tasks, while methods that maximize plasticity suffer severe forgetting. The paper asks whether stability and plasticity can be decoupled into two independent sub-problems solved by separate architectural components rather than forced into a single optimization trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242326/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, processed by a frozen ViT-B-16 backbone, and outputs a class label via a weighted combination of logits from two parallel prompt-tuning modules. In class-incremental settings, each task introduces new classes from the same distribution; in domain-incremental settings, the same classes appear under shifted distributions. A learnable weight mask is applied to the final logits to rebalance old and new class scores, and in the memory-free variant, synthetic features are sampled from stored Gaussian class statistics to simulate rehearsal.

## Method

PromptFusion decouples stability and plasticity into two modules: a Stabilizer instantiated with CoOp (class-specific text prompts where past-task prompts are frozen and only new task prompts are trained, preserving historical knowledge) and a Booster instantiated with shallow VPT (a single shared set of visual prompt tokens continuously updated across all tasks, enabling full plasticity). Their output logits are fused via a dataset-conditioned learnable scalar λ, and a learnable weight mask W (split into α for new classes and β for old classes) corrects for class imbalance. PromptFusion-Lite further reduces compute by running the Stabilizer by default and using a Gumbel-Softmax decision module (a two-layer MLP on CLIP image features) to adaptively gate the Booster per input, with a usage penalty and knowledge distillation loss to prevent the decision module from degenerating.

## Results

PromptFusion achieves state-of-the-art Average Accuracy across all four benchmarks: 88.5% on Split-Cifar100, 85.1% on Split-CUB200, 82.2% on Split-Imagenet-R, and 93.7% on Core50. In the memory-free setting on Split-Imagenet-R, PromptFusion reaches 78.7% average accuracy, surpassing the best prompt-based method CODA-Prompt by 5.3%. PromptFusion-Lite achieves competitive performance (81.5% on Split-Imagenet-R) while reducing GFLOPs from 42.6 to 34.4 (19.2% fewer) compared to PromptFusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptFusion outperforms all baselines on every dataset. On Split-Cifar100, it beats the strongest prompt-based baseline DualPrompt (87.5%) by 1.0% and L2P (85.9%) by 2.6%. The gap is most dramatic on Split-Imagenet-R, where PromptFusion (82.2%) surpasses L2P (70.8%) by 11.4% and DualPrompt (65.2%) by 17.0%, attributed to the Stabilizer's robustness to intra-class variations. On Core50 (domain-incremental), PromptFusion (93.7%) exceeds S-Prompt (92.1%), the only method specifically designed for this setting, by 1.6%. In the memory-free setting on Split-Imagenet-R, PromptFusion outperforms CODA-Prompt by 5.3%. PromptFusion-Lite trades a small accuracy drop (0.7–2.4% depending on dataset) for 14.8–19.2% fewer GFLOPs, making it competitive with or even below L2P and DualPrompt in computational cost while maintaining higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the prompt-tuning paradigm for continual learning (L2P, DualPrompt, S-Prompt, CODA-Prompt), which typically trains a single prompt pool with a selection mechanism per task. It departs from this by instantiating two architecturally distinct prompt modules—CoOp (class-dependent text prompts, frozen per task) and VPT (shared visual prompts, continuously updated)—and combining them to explicitly decouple stability from plasticity, drawing on the Complementary Learning Systems theory from neuroscience. The adaptive selection in PromptFusion-Lite further builds on Gumbel-Softmax differentiable discrete decisions, applied here to per-input module routing rather than prompt selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled decomposition of the stability-plasticity dilemma into two independently solvable sub-problems, offering a design template that could extend to other prompt-based or parameter-efficient continual learning settings. The empirical finding that CoOp and VPT exhibit complementary dataset sensitivities (robustness to intra-class variation vs. plasticity on simple distributions) is practically useful for practitioners choosing architectures. Limitations include reliance on a single ViT-B-16 backbone, a dataset-specific ensemble weight λ, and evaluation on only four datasets; whether the dual-module principle generalizes to other prompt families or non-vision modalities remains open.
