## Problem

RGB-based category-level object pose estimation faces two fundamental challenges absent in RGBD settings: (1) the lack of depth exacerbates intra-class shape variation, making NOCS coordinate predictions uncertain in occluded or ambiguous regions, and (2) single-image inputs introduce inherent scale ambiguity, rendering metric translation and size estimation ill-posed. Prior methods treat all pixel correspondences equally and rely on RANSAC for outlier filtering, while either ignoring scale ambiguity or coupling scale prediction with NOCS estimation in a way that destabilizes training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and camera intrinsics as input; the object of interest is first cropped and resized using MaskRCNN detection results. The output is a scale-agnostic 9DoF pose where translation and size are normalized by the tight bounding-box diagonal (set to 1), plus a separate MobileNet prediction of the absolute diagonal length to recover metric scale at inference. Internally, the NOCS coordinate map is represented not as deterministic points but as per-pixel Laplacian distributions (mean and variance), which feed into the PnP solver.

## Method

LaPose models object shape as a Laplacian Mixture Model (LMM) by running two parallel feature streams—pretrained DINOv2 for category-agnostic 3D geometry and a trained ConvNeXt for category-specific features—each independently predicting a Laplacian distribution (μ, σ²) over the NOCS map via aleatoric uncertainty loss. The two distributions are combined into an LMM that explicitly quantifies per-point shape uncertainty, with the Laplacian chosen over Gaussian for its superior outlier robustness. A convolutional PnP module (inspired by GDR-Net) consumes the LMM parameters and 2D pixel coordinates to predict scale-agnostic rotation and translation end-to-end, dynamically weighting correspondences by their variance to filter erroneous matches. A separate size head predicts the normalized size delta from concatenated global features, and a scale-agnostic pose representation (SAP) decouples metric scale from pose estimation to improve training stability and provide more informative evaluation metrics.

## Results

On NOCS-REAL275 (real-world), LaPose achieves 70.7% NIoU₂₅, 47.9% NIoU₅₀, and 60.7% 10° under scale-agnostic evaluation, surpassing the previous best DMSR by 13.5% on NIoU₂₅ and 23.8% on 10°. On NOCS-CAMERA25 (synthetic), LaPose reaches 75.3% NIoU₂₅, 49.4% NIoU₅₀, and 80.0% 10°, outperforming DMSR by 3.0% on NIoU₇₅ and 5.6% on 10°. Under absolute-scale metrics on REAL275, the multi-model variant Ours(M) leads all baselines, achieving 18.3% IoU₅₀ and 4.1% IoU₇₅.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaPose decisively outperforms all three RGB-based baselines (MSOS, OLD-Net, DMSR) across every metric on both NOCS-REAL275 and NOCS-CAMERA25. Against the strongest competitor DMSR, LaPose gains 13.5% on NIoU₂₅ (70.7 vs. 57.2) and 12.5% on 10°0.5d (57.4 vs. 44.9) on REAL275, with the multi-model variant further extending the lead to 10.8% on NIoU₇₅ and 13.7% on 10°0.2d. The gap over DMSR narrows on the synthetic CAMERA25 dataset (3.0% on NIoU₇₅, 5.6% on 10°), suggesting the real-world domain gap is where the Laplacian Mixture Model's uncertainty modeling provides the largest benefit. MSOS and OLD-Net, which rely on the Umeyama algorithm and 3D-3D correspondences, are substantially outperformed—LaPose achieves 37.4% on 10°0.2d versus only 3.3% (MSOS) and 2.8% (OLD-Net), a roughly 10× improvement. Under absolute-scale metrics (Table 2), LaPose still leads but by smaller margins (e.g., 3.2% over DMSR on IoU₂₅), confirming that the scale-agnostic representation is a key contributor to the larger gains seen in scale-agnostic evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaPose extends the RGB-based category-level pose estimation line (MSOS, OLD-Net, DMSR) by replacing deterministic NOCS regression with a probabilistic Laplacian Mixture Model, drawing on aleatoric uncertainty losses from Kendall & Gal and MonoPair. The PnP module is adapted from GDR-Net's end-to-end convolutional pose solver, while the generalized 3D stream repurposes DINOv2's SE(3)-consistent features—previously shown to support zero-shot semantic correspondence—for NOCS prediction. The key departure from prior work is the dual-stream design (DINOv2 + ConvNeXt) combined with a scale-agnostic pose representation that decouples metric scale from rotation/translation estimation, addressing two limitations the authors identify in all prior RGB-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scale-agnostic pose representation is a practical contribution that makes evaluation metrics more informative and, more importantly, stabilizes training by preventing scale-ambiguity errors from propagating into pose estimation—a 22.5-point NIoU25 drop when reverting to absolute scale underscores how critical this decoupling is. The LMM approach offers a principled, interpretable mechanism for the PnP module to down-weight unreliable correspondences, which is particularly valuable for categories with high intra-class shape variation (e.g., cameras). Open questions include whether the separate MobileNet for absolute scale can be unified into the main pipeline, whether the approach generalizes beyond the six NOCS categories, and how it performs in the robotic manipulation setting the authors mention as future work.
