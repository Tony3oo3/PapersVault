## Problem

Single-image reflection removal is highly ill-posed, and existing deep methods struggle with complex or low-transmitted reflections due to insufficient scene knowledge. While language-based diffusion models (e.g., ControlNet) offer generative priors and semantic guidance, directly applying them to reflection removal fails because the contaminated mixture image provides inaccurate control conditions and the generative nature of diffusion models leads to color shifts and structural distortions in the recovered output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB mixture image M (H×W×3) and two natural-language descriptions: a positive prompt describing the transmission layer content and a negative prompt describing the reflection layer (which can be set to an empty string when reflections are not recognizable). The mixture image is consumed in two forms—its pixel intensity as the initial color condition and its gradient as the initial structure condition—while the language prompts are encoded via a CLIP text encoder. The output is a single recovered transmission layer T̃ (H×W×3) in pixel space, decoded from the latent representation after 50 DDIM sampling steps.

## Method

L-DiffER builds on Stable Diffusion with a ControlNet-style parallel branch that injects color and structure latents at each denoising step, guided by classifier-free guidance with positive and negative language prompts. The key innovation is an iterative condition refinement strategy: at each reverse-diffusion timestep, the model decodes a pseudo transmission layer from the current noisy latent and blends it with the original mixture image (and its gradient) using time-variant coefficients β_t and γ_t that shift weight from the contaminated mixture toward the progressively cleaner estimate. A multi-condition constraint mechanism then enforces faithfulness: a Maximum Value Constraint (MVC) clamps the refined color condition so it never exceeds the mixture image (exploiting M = T + R), and a Saturation-Aware Structure Constraint (SSC) applies a binary mask derived from a saturation threshold (τ=0.95) to preserve generative freedom in low-transmitted regions while constraining structure in well-exposed areas. Training is supervised with a combined objective of the standard LDM noise-prediction loss plus pixel-space RGB, gradient, and statistical (mean/std) losses, using ground-truth transmission layers to refine conditions during training in place of the pseudo estimates used at inference.

## Results

L-DiffER achieves the best performance across all five metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, NIQE, FID) on all three real datasets. On the average of 540 images, it reaches PSNR 25.08, SSIM 0.905, LPIPS 0.123, NIQE 4.322, and FID 46.58, outperforming the strongest baseline DSRNet by +0.29 dB PSNR, +0.005 SSIM, −0.004 LPIPS, −0.272 NIQE, and −2.23 FID. The gains are most pronounced on the Nature dataset (PSNR 23.95 vs. 23.69 for IBCLN) and on perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, NIQE, FID), confirming the benefit of language-guided generative priors for low-transmitted reflection regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L-DiffER outperforms all seven single-image reflection removal baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is DSRNet [22], which trails by 0.29 dB PSNR and 2.23 FID on average; IBCLN [31] is the second-closest on PSNR (23.31 vs. 25.08) but lags significantly on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.156 vs. 0.123, FID 63.67 vs. 46.58). On the Nature dataset, L-DiffER gains the largest margin over DSRNet (+2.25 dB PSNR), suggesting the language-guided generative prior is most beneficial for challenging real-world scenes. Qualitatively, ControlNet [74] adapted for reflection removal produces noticeable color shifts and structural distortions, whereas L-DiffER's iterative condition refinement and multi-condition constraints preserve fidelity while still recovering content in low-transmitted regions. The older baselines (Zhang et al. [76], CoRRN [61], ERRNet [65]) are consistently 2–3 dB lower in PSNR and 10–20 points higher in FID, confirming that the diffusion-based approach with language guidance represents a clear step forward over prior CNN-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) and Stable Diffusion latent framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) to the reflection removal task, which prior single-image methods (IBCLN, DSRNet, YTMT) addressed purely through learned deep priors without auxiliary semantic input. It extends the initial language-guided reflection separation work of Zhong et al. (CVPR 2024), which lacked generative capability for low-transmitted regions, by coupling language prompts with a diffusion-based generative prior. The key methodological departure is the iterative condition refinement strategy and multi-condition constraint mechanism, which address the fundamental mismatch between the contaminated mixture image and the desired clean transmission layer that makes standard ControlNet-style conditioning inadequate for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that language-guided diffusion models can handle the ill-posed single-image reflection removal task, opening a path for generative priors to assist other layer-separation and image-restoration problems where initial conditions are inherently inaccurate. The iterative condition refinement strategy—progressively replacing coarse mixture-image conditions with self-generated pseudo-transmission estimates—is a generalizable technique applicable to any diffusion-based restoration task where the input is a corrupted superposition. The authors note a key limitation: when transmission and reflection layers share similar content, the language descriptions may misidentify layers, suggesting that spatial guidance (masks, edges) would be a natural complement. Open questions include whether language descriptions can be automatically generated to remove the user-input dependency, and whether the approach extends to other multi-layer decomposition tasks.
