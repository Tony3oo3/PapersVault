## Problem

Existing online HD map construction methods either rely on rasterized segmentation (losing precise vector contours), key-point detection (failing to capture diverse instance shapes), or scene-agnostic learnable queries (limiting simultaneous semantic and geometric capture). Additionally, query-based two-stage architectures suffer from inter-network inconsistency when bipartite matching assigns different GT instances to the same query across sub-networks, causing slower convergence and degraded performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241833/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images (6 views at 1600×900 for nuScenes, 7 views at up to 2048×2048 for Argoverse2) and optionally 32-beam LiDAR point clouds (voxelized at 0.1×0.1×0.2 m), processed within a 30 m × 60 m perception range. It outputs vectorized map instances—each annotated with a class label (Pedestrian Crossing, Lane Divider, or Road Boundary) and an ordered set of 20 normalized BEV coordinates—along with per-instance BEV segmentation masks at 50 query slots. The ordered point structure encodes sequential geometry, enabling direct use in downstream planning without heuristic post-processing.

## Method

Mask2Map employs a two-network hierarchy: IMPNet (a Mask2Former-style Mask Transformer over multi-scale BEV features from LSS/SECOND encoders) generates Mask-Aware Queries and BEV Segmentation Masks capturing global instance semantics, while MMPNet refines these into vectorized geometry via two submodules—PQG injects 2D positional embeddings into sparsified masks to produce instance-level positional queries, and GFE samples key pixels via Max Survival + Farthest Point Sampling to extract point-level geometric features from BEV features. A Mask-Guided Map Decoder then performs cross-attention between PQG queries and GFE features, followed by Deformable Transformer decoding over multi-scale BEV features to predict class scores and ordered point coordinates. The key training innovation is Inter-network Denoising Training: noisy GT queries (with class-flipping noise at probability 0.2) and perturbed GT Segmentation Masks (with Map Noise) are injected into IMPNet and matched directly to their GTs without bipartite matching, raising the query-to-GT matching consistency ratio between networks from 24.7% to 74.7% and yielding a 6.5% mAP gain.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set (Chamfer distance metric, camera-only, 24 epochs), Mask2Map achieves 71.6% mAP, surpassing the previous SOTA MapTRv2 (61.5%) by 10.1% mAP. With camera+LiDAR fusion at 24 epochs, it reaches 78.4% mAP (+9.4% over MapTRv2). On the rasterization-based metric, Mask2Map achieves 54.7% mAP†, exceeding MapTRv2 by 18.0% mAP. On Argoverse2, Mask2Map attains 71.5% mAP, outperforming MapTRv2 by 4.1% mAP with the same ResNet-50 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mask2Map outperforms all seven compared baselines across both benchmarks. Against the closest competitor MapTRv2 [21], it gains 10.1% mAP on nuScenes (camera, 24 epochs) and 4.1% mAP on Argoverse2, with the largest per-class improvement on pedestrian crossings (+10.8% AP on nuScenes). Earlier query-based methods MapTR [20] and MapVR [40] trail by roughly 20% mAP on nuScenes, while the detection-based VectorMapNet [24] lags by over 30% mAP even at 110 epochs. PivotNet [9] and BeMapNet [30] are intermediate, achieving 57.6% and 59.8% mAP respectively, still 12–14% below Mask2Map. On the rasterization metric (Table 2), the gap widens further: Mask2Map's 54.7% mAP† exceeds MapTRv2's 36.7% by 18.0 points, indicating superior geometric fidelity of predicted contours. The only trade-off is inference speed: Mask2Map runs at 10.1 FPS (camera) vs. 14.1 FPS for MapTRv2, a ~28% slowdown attributed to the additional IMPNet and GFE modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mask2Map extends the MapTR/MapTRv2 line of work on query-based vectorized HD map construction by inserting an instance-segmentation stage (adapted from Mask2Former) between BEV feature extraction and vectorized point prediction. Rather than decoding learnable queries directly from BEV features, it first generates Mask-Aware Queries and BEV Segmentation Masks to capture global semantic context, then refines them with positional and geometric information via PQG and GFE. The Inter-network Denoising Training strategy is a targeted adaptation of DN-DETR and MP-Former denoising ideas, repurposed to resolve bipartite-matching inconsistencies between two coupled networks rather than within a single decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.1% mAP gain on nuScenes and 4.1% on Argoverse2 over MapTRv2, supported by thorough ablations and two evaluation metrics, makes Mask2Map a strong new reference for online vectorized HD map construction. The finding that inter-network matching consistency (Util) was only 24.7% without denoising training reveals a previously underappreciated bottleneck in multi-stage query-based architectures. However, the FPS drops from 14.1 to 10.1 (camera-only) and the model relies solely on single-frame input without temporal fusion, limiting immediate real-time deployment. Open questions include whether temporal query/BEV fusion can close the occlusion gap and whether model compression can recover the lost throughput without sacrificing the mask-guided gains.
