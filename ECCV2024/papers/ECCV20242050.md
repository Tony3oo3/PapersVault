## Problem

Existing VEST methods (e.g., VEST-MPI) rely on entangled layered MPI representations that simultaneously model scene geometry, future motion, and disoccluded content synthesis under simplified assumptions of affine motion and homography-based warping. This entanglement causes blurred and inaccurate extrapolation in complex urban scenes, and the coupling of ego-motion with dynamic object motion introduces compounding errors in 3D motion prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242050/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive RGB frames (I_{t-1}, I_t) at resolutions up to 1024×2048 (Cityscapes) or 375×1242 (KITTI) along with a target novel camera pose K, and produces a single future frame I'_{t+1} rendered from that viewpoint. Depth is estimated per-frame via ZoeDepth in metric units, semantic segmentation via DeepLabV3 identifies foreground/background regions for disocclusion handling, and the pipeline can be rolled out recursively to forecast up to 10 frames ahead.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully disentangled 3D pipeline: (1) each input frame is lifted into a continuous 3D point cloud (3D location + learned appearance feature) via metric depth, with semantic-segmentation-guided inpainting filling disocclusion holes behind foreground objects; (2) future 3D motion is forecast in two decoupled stages — an ego-motion forecasting (EMF) module uses self-attention over background-only inpainted features to predict relative camera pose transforms, and a multi-scale object motion forecasting (OMF) module with L=9 multi-scale motion flow blocks (MMFBs) at decreasing scale factors [4,4,4,2,2,2,1,1,1] refines the residual 3D motion of dynamic objects; (3) motion-adjusted point clouds are rendered via differentiable point-based splatting (PyTorch3D) to the target viewpoint, and a 2D U-Net refinement network decodes the concatenated feature maps into the final RGB frame. Training is end-to-end supervised with equal-weight L1 and VGG perceptual losses, using pre-trained ZoeDepth, DeepLabV3, and 3D Photo as frozen front-ends.

## Results

On Cityscapes (512×1024), the method achieves SSIM 96.4 / LPIPS 4.6 at t+1, SSIM 86.2 / LPIPS 9.8 at t+5, and SSIM 78.0 / LPIPS 14.9 at t+10, outperforming the strongest baselines WALDO (95.7/4.9) and DMVFN (95.7/5.6) at t+1. On KITTI (256×832), it reaches SSIM 87.7 / LPIPS 10.1 at t+1 and SSIM 71.3 / LPIPS 19.8 at t+5, surpassing DMVFN (70.5/26.0) and WALDO (70.2/20.6) at t+5. For the concurrent VEST-[S+T] task on KITTI, the method achieves SSIM 69.3 / LPIPS 21.4 at t+5, compared to WALDO→3D Photo (68.3/24.9) and DMVFN→3D Photo (62.6/29.2). For independent novel view synthesis on KITTI, it attains SSIM 94.6 / LPIPS 5.2, substantially exceeding VEST-MPI (82.5/8.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 15 baselines on the majority of metrics. On Cityscapes, it beats the closest competitors WALDO (95.7/4.9) and DMVFN (95.7/5.6) at t+1, with the gap widening substantially at longer horizons (t+5: 86.2/9.8 vs. 85.4/10.5 and 83.5/14.9; t+10: 78.0/14.9 vs. 77.1/15.8). On KITTI, DMVFN achieves a marginally higher SSIM at t+1 (88.5 vs. 87.7), but the proposed method wins on LPIPS (10.1 vs. 10.7) and clearly dominates at t+3 and t+5 (77.6/15.4 and 71.3/19.8 vs. 78.0/19.3 and 70.5/26.0 for DMVFN). For the VEST task specifically, the method surpasses VEST-MPI by a large margin on both novel view synthesis (SSIM 94.6 vs. 82.5, LPIPS 5.2 vs. 8.5) and video prediction (LPIPS 8.1 vs. 11.5 at t+1; 20.4 vs. 39.1 at t+5). Older video prediction baselines (PredNet, MCNet, VFlow, OMP, VPVFI, CorrWise, SADM, PredRNNV2) are all substantially outperformed across both datasets, with gaps of 10–30+ points in LPIPS. The concurrent VEST-[S+T] comparison shows consistent wins over the DMVFN→3D Photo and WALDO→3D Photo pipelines, with the advantage growing over longer forecasts (t+5: SSIM 69.3 vs. 68.3 and 62.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the VEST-MPI formulation [55], which models video extrapolation in space and time via an entangled multi-plane image (MPI) representation with simplified affine motion and homography-based warping. Instead, it repurposes ideas from 3D photography (depth-based scene lifting, as in 3D Photo [35] and SLIDE [15]) to construct explicit 3D point clouds, and adapts the multi-scale flow prediction philosophy of DMVFN [13] into a 3D motion forecasting setting. The two-stage ego-motion-then-object-motion decomposition is a conceptual departure from prior VEST methods that jointly estimate all motion components, drawing on the observation that static background provides a cleaner signal for camera trajectory prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentangled 3D point-cloud representation offers a cleaner inductive bias for VEST than layered MPIs, and the two-stage motion forecasting provides a practical recipe that practitioners can adopt for any setting where camera and object motion must be separated. However, the method's reliance on monocular depth estimation (ZoeDepth) introduces a fundamental accuracy ceiling, particularly for thin structures and cross-frame depth inconsistencies, which the authors acknowledge as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons without recursive error accumulation, whether RGB-D inputs would close the remaining gap, and whether the point-cloud formulation can be extended to handle more complex dynamic scenes beyond urban driving.
