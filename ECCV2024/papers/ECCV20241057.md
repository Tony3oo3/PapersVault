## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D object detection is limited to a small fixed vocabulary (typically 3–4 classes), and open-vocabulary learning in 3D point clouds remains largely unexplored due to the scarcity of VLMs pre-trained on point cloud data. Existing top-down approaches overfit to base classes and miss novel objects with diverse shapes, while bottom-up weakly-supervised methods rely on rigid geometric priors (e.g., rectangular objects on a ground plane) that fail for non-rectangular or elevated classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-resolution LiDAR point clouds and multi-view camera images (six 1600×900 views on nuScenes) together with text prompts specifying novel class names. A 2D region VLM (GLIP or OWL-ViT) first produces 2D bounding boxes across all camera views, which are then lifted into 3D frustums for proposal search. The final output is a set of 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) with open-vocabulary class labels, evaluated via mAP, NDS, and novel-class AP/AR on nuScenes and KITTI.

## Method

FIND N' PROPAGATE is a two-stage pipeline that first maximises recall of novel objects and then propagates detection capability to distant regions. The Greedy Box Seeker lifts each 2D VLM prediction into a 3D frustum and exhaustively searches over depth, scale, and orientation intervals (k_d × k_s × k_o candidates) to find the best-fitting 3D proposal. The Greedy Box Oracle then ranks candidates using a composite score combining point-density ranking (fraction of LiDAR points inside the box) and multi-view alignment (IoU between the re-projected 3D box and the original 2D prediction). To counteract the inherent bias toward camera-proximal, dense objects, the Remote Propagator introduces a geometry simulator (copy-paste with Gaussian perturbations on position and orientation) and a density simulator (random point dropout) that augment the memory bank with sparser, farther instances, enabling iterative self-training that progressively captures more novel objects across depth.

## Results

On nuScenes SETTING 1 (6 base / 4 novel classes), FIND N' PROPAGATE with OWL-ViT + Transfusion achieves 33.65% AP_N and 68.10% AR_N for novel classes, compared to 6.47% AP_N and 39.40% AR_N for the best TOP-DOWN SELF-TRAIN baseline — a 420% AP_N improvement and 20.7% absolute recall gain. Under the more challenging SETTING 2 (3 base / 7 novel classes), FIND N' PROPAGATE with GLIP + CenterPoint reaches 18.46% AP_N versus a maximum of 2.54% for any TOP-DOWN variant. On KITTI, the Greedy Box Seeker outperforms FGR by at least 73% in AP3D for both Cyclist and Pedestrian across all difficulty levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FGR [40] (BOTTOM-UP WEAKLY-SUPERVISED) on KITTI, the Greedy Box Seeker substantially outperforms FGR on non-rectangular classes: Cyclist AP3D improves from 4.48/3.28/3.15 (Easy/Mod/Hard) to 7.73/8.02/7.50, and Pedestrian AP3D from 3.97/4.51/4.21 to 12.20/14.29/12.30 — gains of at least 73% across all difficulties, attributed to FGR's rectangular-shape prior failing on non-vehicle objects. Against OpenSight [52] on nuScenes SETTING 3 (all 10 classes novel, no base annotations), the Greedy Box Seeker upper bound achieves 10.65–23.10% AP_N versus OpenSight's 5.40–5.80%, a 97% relative improvement in AP_N, demonstrating superior proposal quality without any 3D supervision. Within the paper's own baselines, FIND N' PROPAGATE dominates all four TOP-DOWN and BOTTOM-UP variants: it surpasses TOP-DOWN SELF-TRAIN by 420% in AP_N (33.65% vs. 6.47%) and gains 20.7% absolute AR_N, while TOP-CLUSTERING (HDBScan + CLIP2Scene) yields less than 1% mAP, confirming that density-based clustering is ill-suited for diverse urban object geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes 2D open-vocabulary detection tools (GLIP, OWL-ViT, CLIP) and weakly-supervised 3D detection ideas (FGR's frustum lifting) into a unified pipeline for LiDAR-based 3D OV detection in urban scenes, a setting the authors explicitly identify as unexplored. It departs from TOP-DOWN approaches (which regress class-agnostic boxes from base annotations and project to 2D) by arguing these overfit to known geometries, and from BOTTOM-UP weakly-supervised methods (which assume rectangular, ground-plane objects) by introducing a geometry-agnostic greedy search over orientation and depth within camera frustums. The Remote Propagator concept—simulating distant, sparse point-cloud structures via copy-and-paste with dropout—draws on data augmentation intuition but applies it specifically to counteract the camera-proximity bias inherent in 2D-derived pseudo-labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark of OV-3D in urban LiDAR settings, providing four baselines and three evaluation protocols that the field lacked; the 3.97× AP gain for novel classes and 53% recall improvement, while measured against deliberately weak TOP-DOWN baselines, establish a concrete starting point for future work. Key limitations remain: the approach still relies on 2D VLM predictions that are biased toward near-camera objects, velocity attributes are unsupervised in mined proposals, and multi-view consistency is acknowledged as suboptimal. Open questions include whether temporal fusion and stronger 3D-native VLMs (currently scarce) can reduce dependence on 2D frustum lifting, and whether the greedy search scales to denser urban scenes with hundreds of novel instances per frame.
