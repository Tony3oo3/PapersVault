## Problem

Point-supervised temporal action localization (PTAL) provides only a single annotated frame per action instance, creating semantic ambiguity in determining action boundaries due to the lack of temporal continuity. Existing PTAL methods either rely on iterative pseudo-label refinement with empirical thresholds or treat point labels as video-level categorical labels, both of which fail to capture the full semantics of actions and ignore snippets that differ from the point label.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed videos encoded into 2048-dimensional snippet-level features via a two-stream I3D network (16-frame non-overlapping snippets with TV-L1 optical flow, pre-trained on Kinetic-400), along with point supervision consisting of one timestamp and action category per action instance. At inference, it outputs per-instance predictions of (start, end, category, confidence), where proposals are scored by outer-inner contrast and consolidated via NMS with multiple thresholds.

## Method

SMBD introduces a Background Anchor Generator (BAG) that uses N parallel classification heads to compute a stability metric (mean background probability divided by standard deviation) and selects the most reliable background snippet between each pair of adjacent action labels. A Dual Boundary Detector (DBD) then searches for boundaries within each background-anchor-to-action-label interval from two complementary perspectives: a fine-grained branch minimizes the intra-subinterval uncertainty of classification scores to detect action changes, while a coarse-grained branch maximizes the foreground-background score difference to detect scene changes. The two boundary estimates are fused via a weighted combination, and pseudo labels are assigned to all video snippets based on the detected boundaries. Training employs focal loss for multi-class classification, binary entropy for foreground/background discrimination, and a KL-divergence loss to penalize score-label inconsistencies near boundaries, with boundaries continuously updated through backpropagation across epochs.

## Results

On THUMOS'14, LACP+SMBD achieves 64.2% mAP@[0.1:0.5] and 45.7% mAP@[0.3:0.7], improving over the LACP baseline by +2.6% and +2.4% respectively. On BEOID, LACP+SMBD reaches 57.4% AVG mAP@[0.1:0.7], a +5.5% gain over the reproduced LACP* (51.9%). On GTEA, LACP+SMBD attains 47.4% AVG mAP@[0.1:0.7], outperforming LACP* (46.0%) and all other point-supervised baselines. The method also improves SF-Net by +4.3% on mAP@[0.1:0.5] on THUMOS'14, demonstrating versatility across backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among point-supervised methods on THUMOS'14, LACP+SMBD outperforms LACP [11] by +2.7% at IoU 0.3, +3.1% at IoU 0.5, and +1.2% at IoU 0.7, with consistent gains across all thresholds. It surpasses SF-Net [22] by +5.9% at IoU 0.3 and +3.8% on AVG [0.3:0.7], and exceeds Ju et al. [9] by +9.8% at IoU 0.3. The method also outperforms the strongest weakly-supervised baseline DDG-Net [36] (57.8% AVG [0.1:0.5]) by +6.4%, demonstrating that explicit boundary detection with point supervision can surpass video-level supervision. On BEOID, LACP+SMBD achieves 57.4% AVG [0.1:0.7], beating LACP* [11] by +5.5% and SF-Net [22] by +26.5%. On GTEA, the improvement over LACP* is more modest (+1.4%), while the gap over SF-Net [22] remains large (+16.4%). The method narrows the gap to fully-supervised ASL [32] (67.9% AVG [0.3:0.7]) to within 2.2% on THUMOS'14, though it still trails by a notable margin at high IoU thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the point-supervised temporal action localization (PTAL) paradigm introduced by Moltisanti et al. and advanced by SF-Net and LACP, which expand pseudo labels from single-frame annotations without explicitly localizing action boundaries. SMBD departs from this lineage by introducing a two-stage boundary detection pipeline: a Background Anchor Generator that uses Monte Carlo-style multi-head voting to identify reliable background snippets, and a Dual Boundary Detector that searches for boundaries from complementary perspectives (action-change and scene-change). The method is designed as a plug-in module that can be attached to existing PTAL backbones (SF-Net, LACP) rather than replacing them, positioning it as an orthogonal improvement to the pseudo-label expansion strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation of sparse point supervision—semantic ambiguity at action boundaries—by explicitly detecting boundaries to assign pseudo labels to more snippets, which is a practical concern for any PTAL system. However, the improvements are modest (2.4–2.7% mAP over LACP on THUMOS'14), and the approach is tightly coupled to the I3D feature extraction pipeline and the specific evaluation protocol of untrimmed video TAL. Open questions include whether the boundary-detection formulation generalizes to other supervision regimes (e.g., few-shot or semi-supervised TAL), whether the multi-head background voting is the most sample-efficient strategy, and whether the method's gains persist under different feature backbones or longer video durations.
