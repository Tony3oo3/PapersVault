## Problem

Online action detection requires timely predictions from incomplete streaming observations, but existing methods struggle to simultaneously quantify epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty efficiently. Evidential deep learning (EDL) enables single-pass uncertainty estimation but cannot accurately separate the two uncertainty types, while Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) model both precisely at the cost of expensive multi-sample inference. Additionally, OAD inputs contain redundant background and irrelevant actions that degrade detection accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240420/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step t, the model consumes only the past and current frames (V_t = {I_1, ..., I_t}), with features extracted via TSN using a multi-scale Vision Transformer for RGB and BN-Inception for optical flow (24 fps, chunk size 6), or raw multi-sensor data for the HDD driving dataset. The student model outputs a single action class prediction, closed-form epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty values derived from Dirichlet parameters, and a spatial-temporal attention mask of shape t×J that gates the input features element-wise. During inference, only the lightweight student model (19.4M parameters, 0.48 GFLOPs) runs in a single forward pass at 163 FPS, with no sampling or ensemble required.

## Method

BEDL uses a teacher-student architecture where a last-layer Bayesian neural network (teacher, trained with Laplace approximation on the final layer) computes Bayesian mutual information (BMI) between each past feature and the ongoing action, and provides a posterior distribution over class probabilities. The evidential student model (Dirichlet-parameterized) is trained via two distillation channels: (1) distribution distillation minimizing KL-divergence between the teacher's integrated posterior and the student's Dirichlet, and (2) attention distillation where a fully-connected network generates a spatial-temporal mask supervised by MSE against sigmoid-transformed BMI values. Training follows a two-stage strategy—first the attention module is trained in isolation, then the full student is jointly optimized with cross-entropy, attention, and distribution distillation losses. At test time, the student alone performs inference, yielding accurate predictions and both uncertainty types in one forward pass, while the attention mask actively suppresses low-MI features to improve detection under limited data and distribution shift.

## Results

On THUMOS'14, BEDL achieves 70.6% mAP (ActivityNet features) and 72.7% mAP (Kinetics features), outperforming the previous best Transformer-based methods (MAT: 71.6%, MiniROAD: 71.8%). On TVSeries, BEDL reaches 88.9% mcAP (ActivityNet) and 90.1% mcAP (Kinetics), slightly trailing E2E-LOAD (90.3%) but surpassing MAT (89.7%) and LSTR (89.1%). On HDD (sensor data only), BEDL achieves 33.0% mAP, exceeding MAT (32.7%). BEDL also achieves the highest model inference speed (163.2 FPS) with the fewest parameters (19.4M) and lowest FLOPs (0.48 GFLOPs) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On THUMOS'14 with Kinetics features, BEDL (72.7% mAP) outperforms all prior methods including E2E-LOAD (72.4%), MiniROAD (71.8%), MAT (71.6%), and GateHUB (70.7%). On TVSeries with Kinetics features, E2E-LOAD (90.3% mcAP) narrowly edges out BEDL (90.1%), while BEDL surpasses MAT (89.7%), LSTR (89.1%), and Uncertainty-OAD (89.3%). On HDD with sensor data, BEDL (33.0% mAP) beats MAT (32.7%) and GateHUB (32.1%); E2E-LOAD reports 48.1% but uses RGB input rather than sensor data, making it not directly comparable. In terms of efficiency, BEDL uses only 19.4M parameters and 0.48 GFLOPs versus 45–95M parameters for competing methods, and achieves 163.2 FPS model inference speed compared to 71–123 FPS for others. The paper also shows BEDL generalizes better under cross-view (74.3% vs. 70.2% for TeSTra) and occlusion (91.8% vs. 89.9% for TeSTra) conditions on TVSeries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established uncertainty quantification paradigms: Bayesian neural networks (which accurately model both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty but require expensive sampling) and evidential deep learning (which enables single-pass uncertainty estimation but struggles with epistemic accuracy). The key architectural contribution is a teacher-student framework where a last-layer Bayesian teacher (using Laplace approximation, building on Kristiadi et al.) distills its posterior distribution and Bayesian mutual information into an evidential student (building on Sensoy et al.'s Dirichlet-based EDL). This departs from prior knowledge distillation for OAD (e.g., PKD [72]) that transfers prediction scores or features, and from prior MI-based attention (e.g., Uncertainty-OAD [27]) that relies on point-estimation MI rather than Bayesian MI.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of BNN accuracy and EDL efficiency in a single forward pass is practically important for safety-critical streaming applications (autonomous driving, surveillance) where both reliable uncertainty and real-time inference are required. The 19.4M parameter model achieving 163 FPS while matching or exceeding larger competitors (40–95M params, 71–123 FPS) makes the approach deployable. However, the last-layer Bayesian approximation is a pragmatic compromise (full Bayesian showed only ~0.5% improvement), and the teacher-student pipeline adds training complexity. Open questions include whether the BMI-based attention generalizes to other streaming tasks, whether the Laplace approximation suffices for highly non-Gaussian posteriors, and whether the distillation gap between teacher and student can be further closed without sacrificing efficiency.
