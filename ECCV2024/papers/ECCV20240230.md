## Problem

Learning object-centric representations in 3D scenes remains a critical challenge: existing 3D object-centric methods either demand prohibitive computational resources (OSRT requires 64 TPUv2 chips for 7 days on 1M scenes), rely on auxiliary losses (adversarial, LPIPS) with prolonged training, or fail to generalize to complex real-world scenes. The core difficulty is effectively aligning calibrated multi-view information and reconstructing 3D scenes from compressed slot representations without excessive sampling overhead.

## Input / Output

SLOTLIFTER accepts L calibrated source views (L=1 for single-view synthetic benchmarks, L=4 nearby views for ScanNet and DTU) along with their camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It outputs rendered novel-view images, per-slot 2D segmentation masks (rendered via volume rendering of the point-slot mapping weights W_p), and K learned slot vectors encoding object-centric scene abstractions. The model requires no auxiliary supervision—only pixel-level reconstruction loss on the rendered views.

## Method

SLOTLIFTER introduces slot-guided feature lifting: 2D feature maps from source views are projected onto 3D sample points to form lifted point features, which are then pooled (mean and variance for multi-view) and combined with positional embeddings. A cross-attention allocation transformer uses these point features as queries and slot representations (plus a learnable empty slot) as keys/values to produce a point-slot mapping W_p and slot-aggregated point features F_s. Density σ is predicted directly from the attention weights via a ReLU-gated Hadamard product, tightly coupling slot interactions with volume rendering. A cosine-annealed random masking schedule (0.99→0 over 30K steps) prevents the model from degenerating to rely solely on lifted features, forcing slots to carry meaningful object information. Training uses only MSE reconstruction loss with the Lion optimizer, requiring ~5× less compute than uORF by needing just 1024 rays and a single radiance field rather than K per-slot fields.

## Results

On the Room-Diverse synthetic benchmark, SLOTLIFTER achieves 77.5 NV-ARI and 90.0 ARI for scene decomposition, outperforming the previous best (BO-uORF) by 15+ ARI points. On the complex Room-Texture dataset, it reaches 79.3 NV-ARI, 86.0 ARI, and 30.68 PSNR for novel-view synthesis. On real-world ScanNet, SLOTLIFTER achieves 28.36 PSNR and 31.1 NV-FG-ARI, surpassing both 3D object-centric methods (OSRT: 13.34 PSNR) and generalizable NeRF baselines (GNT: 27.76 PSNR). On Kitchen-Shiny, it improves PSNR by ~4 dB over the prior best (uOCF-P: 28.58 → 32.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLOTLIFTER outperforms all 3D object-centric baselines across every dataset. On Room-Diverse, it beats BO-uORF (62.5→78.1 NV-ARI), uORF (56.9→78.1), COLF (52.5→78.1), and sVORF (75.6→78.1) by 2.5–25.6 ARI points. On Room-Texture, it surpasses uOCF-P (70.4→79.3 NV-ARI) and uOCF-N (72.2→79.3) while also improving PSNR from 28.85 to 30.68. On Kitchen-Shiny/Matte, it gains ~4 PSNR over uOCF-P (28.58→32.02 / 29.40→32.92) and ~12 PSNR over uORF (19.23→32.02). On ScanNet, SLOTLIFTER (28.36 PSNR, 31.1 NV-FG-ARI) decisively outperforms OSRT (13.34 PSNR, 29.7 NV-FG-ARI) and BO-uORF (12.72 PSNR, 0.0 NV-FG-ARI), and also edges out the generalizable NeRF method GNT (27.76 PSNR). On DTU, it achieves 26.75 PSNR, slightly beating MVSNeRF (26.63) and NeRFusion (26.19) while trailing MVSNeRF on SSIM (0.896 vs 0.931). Slot-Attention (2D-only) is far behind on 3D metrics, confirming the necessity of 3D-aware representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLOTLIFTER sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Slot-Attention-based object-centric learning (Locatello et al., 2020) and image-based rendering / generalizable NeRF methods (PixelNeRF, IBRNet, MVSNeRF, GNT). It directly extends the uORF paradigm of combining slots with volume rendering for unsupervised 3D scene decomposition, but departs from prior 3D object-centric methods (uORF, OSRT, COLF, sVORF) by introducing a slot-guided feature lifting mechanism that projects 2D input-view features into 3D point features and uses them as queries in a cross-attention transformer with slots. This design borrows the multi-view feature aggregation principle from image-based rendering while retaining the object-centric slot structure, eliminating the need for auxiliary losses (adversarial, LPIPS) or depth supervision that prior methods require.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLOTLIFTER demonstrates that a single reconstruction loss, combined with feature lifting and random masking, is sufficient to achieve strong 3D object decomposition and novel-view synthesis on complex real-world scenes (ScanNet, DTU) where prior 3D object-centric methods (OSRT, BO-uORF) produce near-uniform masks and PSNR below 14. The 5× training speedup over uORF and the ability to outperform dedicated generalizable NeRF methods (GNT, SurfelNeRF) on rendering while simultaneously performing object decomposition make this a practically relevant unification. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger scenes with more objects (sensitivity to slot count), whether the ARI metric's coverage bias (noted for OSRT) affects fair comparison, and how the method handles dynamic or partially observed scenes.
