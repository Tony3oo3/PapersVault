## Problem

In single-image Human Mesh Recovery, the 3D mesh and its projecting camera are estimated jointly, so an incorrect mesh-camera pair can still produce a low 2D reprojection loss, misleading the network toward wrong global orientations and joint rotations. Existing remedies either change the projection model or require ground-truth camera supervision, but none provide a self-supervised geometric constraint that directly enforces camera accuracy across multiple views of the same person.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241262/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full RGB image, detects a human bounding box, and generates M=5 RoIs by applying fixed translations (±0.1b along x and y) and resizing factors {1.5, 1.25, 0.8, 0.65} to the original box. It outputs a single shared SMPL mesh (24×3 pose + 10 shape parameters) plus M local weak-perspective cameras (one scale and 2D translation per RoI), from which a full-image camera can be derived via closed-form geometric conversion.

## Method

A shared CNN backbone (ResNet-50 or HRNet-W48) encodes each RoI independently, and a RoI-aware fusion module then computes pairwise relative-position relations between bounding boxes using sinusoidal positional encoding (L=32). These relations are concatenated with the corresponding features to produce softmax attention weights, yielding RoI-specific fusion features (decoded to local cameras) and a RoI-shared feature (decoded to the mesh). Two novel losses exploit the multi-RoI setting: a camera consistency loss that converts each local camera to the full-image coordinate system and enforces pairwise agreement across all RoI pairs, and a contrastive loss (SimCLR-style, τ=0.5) that pulls latent features of the same person's RoIs together while pushing different persons apart.

## Results

On 3DPW, Ours^H48 achieves 64.1 mm MPJPE, 40.4 mm PA-MPJPE, and 78.6 mm PVE, outperforming the strongest baseline CLIFF^H48 (69.0 / 43.0 / 81.2) by 5 mm in MPJPE. On Human3.6M, Ours^H48 reaches 42.2 mm MPJPE and 30.7 mm PA-MPJPE, the best among all compared methods. With the lighter ResNet-50 backbone, Ours^R50 still achieves 68.2 mm MPJPE on 3DPW and 45.4 mm on Human3.6M, surpassing all ResNet-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper is built directly on CLIFF [28], and Ours^H48 improves upon it by 5 mm in MPJPE (64.1 vs 69.0) and 2.6 mm in PA-MPJPE (40.4 vs 43.0) on 3DPW, with a 4.9 mm MPJPE gain on Human3.6M (42.2 vs 47.1). Against the Transformer-based MPT^H48 [31], the closest competitor on 3DPW, Ours^H48 leads by 1.8 mm MPJPE and 2.4 mm PA-MPJPE. On Human3.6M, Ours^H48 achieves the best MPJPE (42.2) and PA-MPJPE (30.7), beating Deformer^H48 [57] by 2.6 mm MPJPE and 0.9 mm PA-MPJPE. Zolly^H48 [53] is competitive on PA-MPJPE (40.9 vs 40.4) and PVE (78.4 vs 78.6, marginally better), but Ours^H48 leads by 3.1 mm in MPJPE on 3DPW. NIKI^H48 [26] achieves a slightly lower PA-MPJPE (40.6) but trails by 7.2 mm in MPJPE and 8.0 mm in PVE. Overall, Ours^H48 achieves the best or near-best result across all five reported metrics on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-RoI HMR paradigm (established by HMR [19] and refined by CLIFF [28]) by introducing multiple RoIs of the same person as simultaneous inputs. It builds directly on CLIFF's full-image reprojection framework and the local-to-full camera conversion theory from [22,28], repurposing these geometric relations as a self-supervised pairwise consistency constraint rather than a supervised target. The RoI-aware fusion module draws on positional encoding techniques from vision transformers [39,50], and the contrastive loss adapts the SimCLR [4] framework to the multi-RoI setting. The key conceptual departure from prior work is treating the mesh-camera entanglement problem not by improving the camera model or requiring GT cameras, but by exploiting the geometric redundancy inherent in multiple views of the same person.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The camera consistency loss offers a practical, self-supervised remedy for the well-known mesh-camera entanglement problem that plagues reprojection-based HMR training, without requiring expensive GT camera annotations. The multi-RoI formulation is lightweight (5 RoIs at 55.6 fps) and could be adopted as a plug-in regularization in existing HMR pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges that the fundamental ill-posedness of single-image 3D recovery persists—failure cases show that 2D-perfect reprojections can still correspond to incorrect 3D configurations. Whether these strategies transfer to multi-view or video-based HMR, where temporal and multi-view constraints already partially resolve ambiguity, remains an open question the authors flag for future work.
