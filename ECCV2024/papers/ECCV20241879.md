## Problem

Existing parametric CAD generative models (Vitruvion, DeepCAD, SketchGraphs) rely exclusively on text or graph representations of sketches, lacking both commonsense geometric reasoning and the complementary geometric patterns available in rendered sketch images. No prior CAD generative model incorporates the image modality, and the field lacks practical, CAD-specific evaluation metrics for assessing generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241879/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For CAD autocompletion, the model receives a partial sketch (20%–80% of primitive entities) as both a tokenized text sequence (coordinates quantized to [1,64]; lines encoded by 2 points, arcs by 3, circles by 4) and a 224×224×3 rendered image, and autoregressively generates the remaining primitive tokens plus a pixel-level reconstructed image. For autoconstraint, the input is the complete primitive sequence and full sketch image, producing a constraint token sequence. For image-conditional generation, only the sketch image is provided and the model outputs the full primitive sequence.

## Method

CadVLM is an asymmetric two-stream encoder-decoder that pairs a fine-tuned ViT-MAE-base image encoder (75% masking, one-epoch reconstruction on SketchGraphs) with a CodeT5+ 770M text encoder, feeding fused embeddings into both a CodeT5+ text decoder (autoregressive primitive/constraint generation) and a lightweight ViT-MAE image decoder (pixel-level sketch reconstruction). Training jointly optimizes three objectives: image-text contrastive loss (ITC) to align the two modalities, image decoding loss (IDL, MSE on pixel patches) to ground the text representation in visual space, and language modeling loss (LM, cross-entropy) for autoregressive token generation. The key architectural insight is that sketch images provide geometric pattern information (symmetry, curvature) that is complementary to the sequential token representation, and that pre-trained code LLMs (CodeT5+) transfer effectively to the code-like structure of quantized sketch primitives. Ablations confirm that removing the image modality or the contrastive alignment consistently degrades performance across all three evaluation metrics.

## Results

On the CAD autocompletion task (SketchGraphs dataset), CadVLM achieves 23.8% Sketch Accuracy, 68.3% Entity Accuracy, and 45.2% CAD-F1, substantially outperforming Vitruvion (3.0%, 40.7%, 11.3%) and DeepCAD (1.2%, 23.1%, 8.9%). On the CAD autoconstraint task, CadVLM reaches 11.3% Sketch Accuracy, 94.9% Entity Accuracy, and 73.8% CAD-F1, surpassing Vitruvion (10.7%, 94.6%, 71.6%) and SketchGraphs (0.48%, 93.0%, 42.0%). For image-conditional generation, CadVLM achieves 98.0% Entity Accuracy and 62.7% CAD-F1 on precise renderings, compared to Vitruvion's 83.8% and 35.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CadVLM decisively outperforms all baselines on the primary CAD autocompletion task. Against Vitruvion, CadVLM improves Sketch Accuracy by 20.8 percentage points (23.8% vs 3.0%), Entity Accuracy by 27.6 points (68.3% vs 40.7%), and CAD-F1 by 33.9 points (45.2% vs 11.3%). DeepCAD, with only 4 encoder-decoder layers, performs worst at 1.2% Sketch Accuracy and 8.9% CAD-F1. Fine-tuned ChatGPT (GPT-3.5) on 1–15% of SketchGraphs reaches at most 7.6% Sketch Accuracy and 22.5% CAD-F1, roughly half of CadVLM's performance, suggesting that general LLMs struggle with CAD-specific geometric reasoning without domain-adapted multimodal architecture. On the autoconstraint task, CadVLM edges out Vitruvion (73.8% vs 71.6% CAD-F1) and dramatically surpasses SketchGraphs (73.8% vs 42.0% CAD-F1), confirming that modeling primitive sequences is essential beyond constraint graphs alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

CadVLM extends the BLIP two-stream encoder-decoder framework (image-text contrastive alignment, image decoder, text decoder) to the parametric CAD domain, where prior generative models (Vitruvion, DeepCAD, SketchGraphs) model sketches exclusively as token sequences or constraint graphs without any visual modality. The key conceptual departure is treating rendered sketch images as a complementary input alongside primitive sequences, leveraging pre-trained ViT-MAE for vision and CodeT5+ for text rather than training CAD-specific architectures from scratch. This positions the work at the intersection of multimodal vision-language pre-training and engineering sketch generation, a combination absent from the CAD literature at the time of writing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that pre-trained vision-language foundation models can be effectively adapted to parametric CAD sketch generation, opening a path toward more general-purpose CAD assistants that combine geometric reasoning with visual understanding. The introduction of Sketch Accuracy, Entity Accuracy, and CAD F1 as standardized metrics addresses a noted gap in the field's evaluation practices. However, the work is limited to 2D sketches on a single dataset (SketchGraphs), the image decoder output is discarded at inference, and the autoconstraint results show the image modality can actually hurt performance, suggesting the modality fusion strategy is not yet robust across all CAD subtasks. Open questions include scaling to 3D construction sequences, handling more diverse constraint types, and whether the gains persist with stronger or more recent foundation models.
