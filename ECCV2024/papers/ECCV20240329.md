## Problem

Existing long-video understanding methods rely on uniform frame sampling that is agnostic to human activity context (location, hand use, interaction timing), require semantically labelled training data, and produce implicit representations that are difficult to interpret. This paper addresses these gaps by proposing an explicit, structured, and semantic-free memory that captures object interactions and location visits in egocentric videos, enabling multiple queries without reprocessing the full video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240329/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long untrimmed egocentric video (ranging from under 3 minutes to over 30 minutes) and processes it in a single online pass, producing a structured memory of HOI tracklets (each with spatio-temporal bounding boxes, hand side, temporal interval, and an object instance ID) and location segments (temporal intervals of activity-centric zones). At query time, a single visual crop of an object or location (no text) is matched against stored instances via feature similarity, returning temporal intervals, sequences, or sets of concurrent interactions. The entire pipeline is training-free and zero-shot, with no fixed object or location vocabulary.

## Method

AMEGO builds its memory through a three-step online pipeline for object interactions: (1) Initialisation uses a class-agnostic hand-object interaction detector, requiring a spatio-temporal tubelet of at least s_o=20 detections with IoU > θ=0.6 over w_s=30 frames to suppress noise; (2) Updating employs the EgoSTARK single-object tracker to follow the object even when hands exit the egocentric field of view, terminating the tracklet after e_o=20 consecutive frames with a visible free hand; (3) Assignment matches the terminated tracklet to existing object instances via DINOv2 visual features and online cosine-similarity clustering. Location segments are identified by combining FlowFormer optical flow (low-norm threshold) with hand detection to detect activity pauses, then assigned to location instances using SWAG scene features. Querying is purely visual: a crop's DINOv2 or SWAG features are matched against stored instances to retrieve temporal intervals, sequences, or concurrent sets, enabling sequencing, concurrency, and temporal-grounding questions without any language model or reprocessing of the video.

## Results

On the Active Memories Benchmark (AMB, 20.5K visual queries), AMEGO-L achieves 36.3% total accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (S-QA with BLIP-2 at 23.6%) by 12.7 percentage points. AMEGO-S (using ViT-S/B feature extractors) reaches 33.8%. The largest gains appear on temporal grounding queries (Q8: 48.9% for AMEGO-L vs. 24.1% best baseline) and sequencing queries (Q4: 38.3% vs. 24.6%), while concurrency Q5 remains the weakest point (27.6%) due to limitations in detecting concurrent hand-object interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMEGO outperforms all baselines by a substantial margin across every query type. The semantic-free QA baseline using CLIP [37] performs worst overall (22.0%), confirming that raw visual features without structured representation are insufficient for long-video reasoning. Among semantic QA baselines, BLIP-2 [20] as an image captioner paired with LLaMA-2 [49] achieves the best baseline score of 23.6%, while the egocentric-specific LaViLa [68] captioner yields 22.4%. Multi-round LLM pipelines (LLoVi) [68] paradoxically underperform single-round QA (20.8–22.6% vs. 22.4–23.6%), likely because the intermediate summarisation step discards fine-grained temporal detail. AMEGO-L (36.3%) exceeds the best baseline by 12.7 points, with the largest absolute gains on temporal grounding Q8 (+24.8 pts) and sequencing Q4 (+13.7 pts), while the smallest gap appears on concurrency Q5 (+0.1 pt over BLIP-2), reflecting the difficulty of detecting simultaneous hand-object interactions.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMEGO departs from the dominant paradigm of long video understanding that relies on uniform frame/clip sampling and semantic training, instead repurposing off-the-shelf perception models (HOI detectors, single-object trackers, optical flow, DINOv2/SWAG features) into an online, activity-aware, training-free pipeline. Conceptually it is closest to VideoAgent [9] in proposing a structured video representation, but narrows the focus to hand-object interactions and activity-centric locations rather than indiscriminate object memorisation. It also extends the video summarisation and storyline literature [60,62] by producing a semantic-free, queryable memory that supports multi-dimensional (object × location × temporal) retrieval without a fixed vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Active Memories Benchmark (20.5K visual queries) is the first to jointly evaluate object interactions, location visits, and their interplay in long egocentric video, filling a gap left by EgoSchema and ReST which target only one dimension. The finding that a training-free structured memory outperforms both CLIP-based and LLM-based QA pipelines by ~12.7% suggests that explicit activity-aware structure, rather than scale or language, is the key to fine-grained temporal reasoning. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (33–36%), concurrent same-hand interactions (Q5) remain a clear weakness, and the approach inherits errors from its off-the-shelf detectors, leaving open whether the pipeline can scale to hour-long videos or more cluttered, non-kitchen environments.
