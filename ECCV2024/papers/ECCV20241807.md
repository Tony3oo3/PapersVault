## Problem

Existing training-free layout-to-image methods handle only individual token cross-attentions, overlooking two critical failure modes in multi-object scenes: adversarial semantic intersections where similar concepts (e.g., bear and tiger) produce overlapping cross-attentions and irregular textures, and self-attention misalignment in later diffusion stages that disrupts pixel-level coherence. Training-based alternatives avoid these issues but require expensive image-text-layout triplets that are scarce and costly to compile.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language prompt (e.g., 'a tiger and a bear') plus per-object bounding boxes specifying spatial placement, and outputs a 512×512 RGB composite scene image. Bounding boxes are converted to binary masks at the 16×16 upscaling block resolution of the U-Net, and the entire pipeline runs on a frozen Stable Diffusion 1.4 model with 50 denoising steps and a fixed guidance scale of 7.5, requiring no additional training or fine-tuning.

## Method

The core contribution is a set of three gradient-based loss constraints applied to the latent during backward diffusion: (1) an intra-token loss that pushes each token's cross-attention mass inside its bounding box above the outside mass, (2) an inter-token loss that penalizes any location where a competing token's attention exceeds the target token's by a margin g, and (3) a self-attention loss that reinforces pixel-to-pixel connections within each object region while diverting irrelevant attention outward. A selective sampling strategy (retain top-K=80% of attention values, then randomly keep M=50%) prevents gradient updates from over-concentrating on a few extreme values and preserves the latent's Gaussian structure. Between refinement stages, an attention redistribution step reassigns each token's cross-attention to the aggregated attention of all tokens within its bounding box followed by max-normalization, correcting misaligned attentions accumulated during forward diffusion. The latent is updated per refinement step via z' = z_t − η_t ∇_{z_t}(L_intra + L_inter + L_self) with a linearly decaying step size.

## Results

On the COCO 2014 dataset with 2, 3, and 2–4 objects, CSG achieves AP50 of 50.3, 42.1, and 48.0 respectively, and AP of 20.4, 14.5, and 19.1, outperforming all training-free baselines on every metric. CLIP scores reach 0.3201, 0.3246, and 0.3209, representing 6.9–7.3% relative improvements over the strongest baseline (MultiDiffusion). The method also leads on the 3-object setting where baselines degrade most sharply (e.g., Layout-control drops to AP50 = 17.5 vs. CSG's 42.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSG outperforms all three baselines on every metric (AP50, AP, CLIP) across all three dataset splits. Against MultiDiffusion [3], which is the strongest baseline on localization (AP50), CSG gains 5.8–7.9 AP50 and 0.2–1.9 AP, while also improving CLIP score by 6.9–7.3% (e.g., 0.3201 vs. 0.2983 on 2 objects), indicating MultiDiffusion sacrifices semantic coherence for placement accuracy. Against BoxDiff [42], CSG leads by 5.0–6.1 AP and 3.2–5.6 AP50, with comparable CLIP scores, showing BoxDiff's single-token cross-attention guidance is insufficient for multi-object scenes. Against Layout-control [8], the gap is largest (up to 24.6 AP50 on 3 objects), reflecting Layout-control's difficulty with complex compositions. The 3-object setting is where CSG's advantage is most pronounced, as baselines suffer from semantic intersection and object merging that the inter-token and self-attention constraints specifically target.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free backward guidance paradigm established by BoxDiff and Layout-control, which optimize latents by manipulating individual token cross-attentions at specific upscaling blocks (a concept inherited from Attend-and-excite). The key departure is a shift from single-token to multi-token reasoning: the authors introduce an inter-token constraint that resolves semantic intersections between conceptually similar objects (e.g., bear vs. tiger) and a self-attention constraint that enforces pixel-level coherence within target regions during later diffusion stages. Additionally, selective sampling and attention redistribution are proposed as practical mechanisms to prevent gradient-based latent updates from disrupting the natural Gaussian noise distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the approach removes the need for expensive image-text-layout triplets, making controllable multi-object generation accessible without retraining. The finding that self-attention misalignment in early diffusion stages measurably degrades object positioning (10.1 AP50 gain from the self-attention constraint) is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the method is evaluated only on CompVis 1.4 with COCO 2014 bounding boxes and 61 filtered object classes, leaving open questions about scalability to more complex layout representations (segmentation masks, keypoints), newer diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and larger, more diverse object vocabularies where semantic intersections may be more severe.
