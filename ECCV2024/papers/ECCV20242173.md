## Problem

Existing MLLM-based segmentation methods (LISA, PerceptionGPT, GLaMM, GSVA) require architectural modifications such as special segmentation tokens, additional decoder modules, or heterogeneous loss functions to produce pixel-level masks, increasing model complexity and hindering extension to other tasks. This paper asks whether MLLMs can be aligned to pixel-level object localization without any such modifications, using only standard text auto-regression training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242173/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a natural-language referring expression as input. The MLLM outputs a bounding box and a set of discrete point prompts (x, y, inside/outside label) encoded as plain text tokens; these prompts are then fed to an off-the-shelf SAM model to produce the final pixel-level segmentation mask. Notably, the MLLM never directly outputs a mask or uses any segmentation-specific token—its output is entirely text, and the mask is generated post-hoc by SAM. For the PQPP variant, the MLLM is queried in a second dialog turn to classify a 5×5 grid of sampled points within the predicted bounding box.

## Method

SAM4MLLM encodes arbitrary-shaped segmentation masks as discrete SAM prompts (a bounding box plus K point groups, each containing n₁ positive and n₂ negative points with coordinates), allowing the MLLM to learn pixel-level localization through standard text cross-entropy loss with no architectural changes. Two prompting strategies are proposed: PPG, where the MLLM directly generates the bounding box and point prompts in a single turn, and PQPP, where the MLLM first predicts a bounding box and then, in a second dialog turn, is queried point-by-point (on a 5×5 grid) whether each point lies inside the object, with low-confidence responses filtered by a 0.9 token-probability threshold. Training uses LoRA (rank 256) on Qwen-VL-7B-Chat with only the visual resampler and LoRA adapter updated, trained on refCOCO/refCOCO+/refCOCOg/gRefCOCO plus VQAv2 for ~100K images and 82K masks—roughly 100× fewer images and 10,000× fewer masks than GLaMM. EfficientViT-XL1-SAM serves as the segmentation engine, converting the MLLM's text prompts into high-quality masks, and its lightweight decoder is fine-tuned on COCO for one epoch to mitigate annotation bias.

## Results

On the refCOCOg validation set (complex narrative queries), SAM4MLLM-7B-PQPP achieves 74.5 cIoU with Qwen-VL and 74.5 with LLaVA-1.6, outperforming GLaMM-7B (74.2) and GSVA-7B (72.7). On refCOCO validation, the LLaVA-1.6 variant reaches 79.6 cIoU, marginally exceeding GLaMM-7B (79.5), while the Qwen-VL PQPP variant scores 77.1. On gRefCOCO (GRES), SAM4MLLM-7B-PQPP achieves 68.96 gIoU on validation, surpassing GSVA-7B (66.47) and LISA-7B (61.63). On ReasonSeg (zero-shot), SAM4MLLM-7B-PQPP attains 46.7 gIoU and 48.1 cIoU, outperforming LISA-7B (44.4/46.0) and all traditional methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GLaMM-7B (the strongest 7B baseline trained on 11M images / 810M masks), SAM4MLLM-PQPP-LLaVA1.6 wins on refCOCO val (79.6 vs 79.5), refCOCO+ val (73.5 vs 72.6), refCOCO+ testB (65.8 vs 64.6), refCOCOg val (74.5 vs 74.2), and refCOCOg test (75.6 vs 74.9), while trailing slightly on refCOCO testA (82.8 vs 83.2) and refCOCO+ testA (77.8 vs 78.7). The paper attributes the refCOCOg advantage to the streamlined architecture preserving the LLM's reasoning capability for complex narrative queries. Against GSVA-7B, SAM4MLLM-PQPP (Qwen-VL) leads on refCOCO val (77.1 vs 77.2, near-tie), refCOCO+ val (71.5 vs 65.9), refCOCO+ testA (76.8 vs 69.6), and refCOCOg test (75.2 vs 73.3). Against LISA-7B and PerceptionGPT-7B, SAM4MLLM consistently outperforms by 2–7 cIoU points across all three refCOCO splits. On gRefCOCO, SAM4MLLM-7B-PQPP achieves 68.96 gIoU (val), beating GSVA-7B (66.47) by 2.5 points and LISA-7B (61.63) by 7.3 points, though it lags GSVA-7B on Test Set A gIoU (70.54 vs 71.08). Notably, SAM4MLLM uses only 100K images and 82K masks for training—roughly 100× fewer images and 10,000× fewer masks than GLaMM—while achieving comparable or superior segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Segment Anything Model (SAM) as a promptable segmentation engine and on MLLMs (Qwen-VL-7B-Chat) for semantic understanding, but departs sharply from prior MLLM-based segmentation methods (LISA, PerceptionGPT, GLaMM, GSVA) that require architectural modifications, segmentation-specific tokens, or heterogeneous loss functions. Instead, SAM4MLLM encodes masks as discrete text prompts (bounding box plus positive/negative points) that SAM consumes, preserving the original MLLM's text cross-entropy training paradigm. The Proactive Query of Prompt-Points (PQPP) strategy further leverages the dialog capability of LLMs, treating point classification as a multi-turn query-response interaction rather than a single-shot generation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that competitive RES performance can be achieved with roughly 100× fewer training images and 10,000× fewer mask annotations than GLaMM, by offloading pixel-level precision to a pre-trained segmentation model. This suggests a general recipe—pair a semantically strong MLLM with a geometrically strong segmentation model via text prompts—that could extend to other pixel-aware tasks without architectural surgery. Open questions include whether the two-model pipeline's inference latency and the reliance on SAM's prompt sensitivity limit real-world deployment, and whether the approach generalizes beyond COCO-style object segmentation to more complex scene understanding.
