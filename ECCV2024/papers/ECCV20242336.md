## Problem

Large-scale road surface reconstruction for autonomous driving faces two key challenges: (1) explicit mesh methods like RoMe struggle with steep elevation changes because a plain MLP must predict drastic height variations from scratch, and (2) surround-view cameras with different models produce inconsistent luminance, causing optimization conflicts when directly optimizing explicit RGB values per vertex. NeRF-based alternatives avoid the second issue but cannot efficiently produce explicit geometric information at scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 7 surround-view cameras (two front-facing at 3840×2160, five others at 1920×1080), a 128-line Lidar at 10 Hz, and high-precision vehicle trajectory from IMU/GPS/wheel encoders, though only three front-facing cameras are used for reconstruction. It produces a triangular mesh (0.1 m edge length) whose vertices carry per-camera decoded RGB values, a shared semantic label, and a final elevation, yielding separate RGB maps for each camera plus a unified semantic and elevation map. The per-camera RGB outputs are decoded from a single shared implicit color feature, so the same scene representation naturally renders differently for cameras with different exposure characteristics.

## Method

EMIE-MAP represents the road as an explicit mesh of equilateral triangles where each vertex stores position, a semantic label, and a 16-dimensional implicit color encoding. Elevation is initialized by interpolating the vehicle trajectory's height (exploiting the prior that the car follows the road) and refined by an 8-layer MLP (width 128) that predicts only low-frequency residuals, making steep slopes tractable. Color is handled by a shared implicit encoding decoded through separate 2-layer per-camera RGB MLPs, which disentangles scene-intrinsic appearance from camera-specific luminance/saturation shifts. Rendering uses direct pinhole projection of the mesh rather than NeRF-style ray sampling, and training is supervised by L1 RGB loss, cross-entropy semantic loss (ground truth from a pre-trained Mask2Former), L1 elevation loss against Lidar points, and a Laplacian smoothness regularizer, all applied within a trajectory-centered 80 m observation window for computational efficiency.

## Results

On the custom dataset across three challenging scenes (city street, night, ramp), EMIE-MAP with Lidar supervision achieves PSNR of 26.75, 24.53, and 20.74 respectively, with mIoU of 95.27%, 96.02%, and 88.89%, and elevation errors of 2.35 cm, 3.57 cm, and 4.33 cm. On the KITTI-odometry sequence-09 (curved uphill road, monocular, no Lidar), EMIE-MAP achieves PSNR 21.93, mIoU 94.76%, and Elev-error 28.12 cm, compared to RoMe's 20.87, 94.57%, and 107.98 cm. Even without Lidar supervision, EMIE-MAP substantially outperforms RoMe in all three custom scenes, with the gap being most dramatic in night and ramp scenarios where RoMe essentially fails.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RoMe [23], a mesh-based road reconstruction method that stores explicit per-vertex color and semantics and uses an MLP to predict elevation. EMIE-MAP wins decisively on all metrics across all three custom scenes. In the city street scene, EMIE-MAP (with Lidar) improves PSNR from 17.31 to 26.75 and reduces elevation error from 45.08 cm to 2.35 cm. In the night scene, RoMe completely collapses (PSNR 0.99, mIoU 4.91%, Elev-error 331 cm) while EMIE-MAP maintains PSNR 24.53 and mIoU 96.02%. In the ramp scene, RoMe again fails (PSNR 3.93, Elev-error 496 cm) whereas EMIE-MAP achieves PSNR 20.74 and Elev-error 4.33 cm. On KITTI-odometry (monocular, no Lidar), EMIE-MAP improves PSNR by ~1 dB and reduces elevation error from 108 cm to 28 cm, at the cost of roughly 2.3× longer runtime (14 min 20 s vs 6 min 20 s), which the authors note is acceptable for offline reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMIE-MAP directly extends RoMe, the closest prior work, which also represents road geometry as an explicit triangular mesh with per-vertex attributes and an MLP for elevation. The key departures are twofold: (1) replacing RoMe's direct MLP elevation prediction with a trajectory-based initialization plus a low-frequency residual MLP, and (2) replacing RoMe's explicit per-vertex RGB with a shared implicit color encoding decoded by camera-specific MLPs. Conceptually, the paper selectively borrows the implicit-encoding philosophy from NeRF (applied only to color, not full scene representation) while retaining the explicit mesh structure that makes geometric extraction and direct projection rendering tractable for large-scale road scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in autonomous-driving mapping: surround-view cameras of different models produce inconsistent luminance, which breaks explicit color optimization and makes large-scale road reconstruction unreliable in real deployments. By decoupling scene physical properties from camera characteristics, EMIE-MAP enables a single reconstruction to serve multiple camera types, which is essential for production systems. Limitations include the requirement for high-precision trajectory (IMU/GPS/wheel-encoder), the offline-only runtime (~14 min per scene), use of only 3 of 7 available cameras, and evaluation against a single baseline (RoMe). Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-kilometer highway networks, whether the implicit color encoding generalizes to unseen camera models without retraining, and whether the method can be made real-time for online mapping.
