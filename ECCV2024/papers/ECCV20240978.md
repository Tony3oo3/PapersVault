## Problem

Existing neural implicit surface renderers (NeuS, Neuralangelo) uniformly sample image pixels during training, leading to insufficient representation of foreground surface regions and degraded reconstruction quality in areas of interest. While prior work has guided 3D point sampling along projection rays, the pixel-level sampling strategy remains unexploited. This paper asks whether the learned SDF representation itself can drive a targeted, view-dependent pixel and depth sampling strategy without requiring additional supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view calibrated RGB images with known camera poses and intrinsics, discretizing the scene into a 128³ grid and each camera's 3D image projection space (u, v, λ) into a 64×64×128 grid. It outputs a 3D surface mesh extracted from the SDF zero-level set and rendered novel-view images. No depth ground truth, SfM points, or object masks are used during training; the camera grids are initialized as a sphere and updated every 2500–5000 iterations.

## Method

The core mechanism defines a probability density p(x) = φ_s(S(x)) over 3D scene space from the SDF, then transforms it into a per-camera 3D image projection space (image coordinates + depth) via a bijective mapping with a Jacobian correction factor η⁻², approximated as a Riemann integral over discretized cells. View dependency (occlusions) is handled by weighting each cell's probability with an accumulated transmittance along the ray, yielding p̃(u_i) = p(u_i)·exp(−Σ_k p([u,v,λ_k])). Sampling proceeds via a three-stage conditional strategy: marginal over u, then v|u via bilinear interpolation, then λ|u,v via trilinear interpolation, all using inverse transform sampling. A new surface reconstruction loss combines a near-surface term (|S(x)| weighted by volume density within ±3σ of the sampled depth), an empty-space term encouraging small SDF values, and a background term that decays with distance from the surface. The approach is backbone-agnostic and adds only ~10% training overhead.

## Results

On DTU, Neuralangelo+Ours achieves the best mean PSNR of 34.41 dB (unmasked) and 38.81 dB (masked), and the lowest mean Chamfer distance of 0.60 mm, outperforming all baselines. NeuS+Ours improves over NeuS by 0.04 dB PSNR (31.78 vs. 31.74) and reduces Chamfer distance from 1.30 to 1.20 mm. On Tanks and Temples, Neuralangelo+Ours slightly decreases PSNR (25.65 vs. 25.99) but improves F1-score (0.59 vs. 0.58), indicating better 3D surface completeness at a small image-quality cost. The method requires only ~10% additional training time and negligible evaluation overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two backbone baselines, the proposed sampler consistently improves: Neuralangelo+Ours gains +0.57 dB PSNR (34.41 vs. 33.84) and reduces Chamfer from 0.61 to 0.60 mm, while NeuS+Ours gains +0.04 dB PSNR and reduces Chamfer from 1.30 to 1.20 mm. Compared to RegSDF [61], which relies on SfM point priors, Neuralangelo+Ours achieves substantially better PSNR (34.41 vs. 25.31) and Chamfer (0.60 vs. 0.72 mm) without any additional geometric input. Against HF-NeuS [52] and NeuralWarp [7], Neuralangelo+Ours achieves the lowest Chamfer distance (0.60 mm vs. 0.77 and 0.68 mm respectively), establishing state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction on DTU. On Tanks and Temples, Neuralangelo+Ours trades a small PSNR drop (−0.34 dB) for a slight F1 improvement (+0.01), suggesting the sampler prioritizes surface completeness over pixel-level fidelity in large-scale scenes. Overall, the method wins or ties on every reported metric across all three datasets, with the largest gains appearing in masked (foreground) PSNR and Chamfer distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeuS and Neuralangelo as backbone neural implicit surface renderers, but departs from their standard uniform pixel sampling by introducing a probability density function over a 3D image projection space (image coordinates + depth) derived from the SDF. It positions itself against prior pixel-sampling strategies—patch-based sampling (Sun et al.), voxel-guided sampling (Neural 3D Reconstruction in the Wild), and RL-based ray selection (ActRay)—by using the learned SDF itself to construct a view-dependent, occlusion-aware sampling distribution without requiring external depth or SfM priors. The additional surface reconstruction losses (near-surface, empty-space, background) extend the standard NeuS training objective to explicitly regularize regions around the sampled depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that pixel/ray selection has been underexplored relative to 3D point sampling along rays—offers a practical, backbone-agnostic lever for improving foreground reconstruction quality without extra supervision. However, the quantitative gains are modest and mixed: Chamfer distance sometimes worsens (e.g., NeuS+Ours vs. NeuS on several DTU scans), TNT PSNR slightly decreases, and the ~10% training overhead is non-trivial. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale or dynamic scenes, how sensitive it is to the grid resolution choices, and whether the surface losses generalize beyond the specific logistic-SDF formulation used here.
