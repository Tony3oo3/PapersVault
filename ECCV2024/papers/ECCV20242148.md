## Problem

Few-step distilled diffusion models (1–4 NFEs) enable real-time image generation, but existing inversion and disentangled-editing techniques designed for multi-step models (DDIM/DDPM inversion requiring 50+ steps, attention-freezing) either produce severe artifacts or over-constrain generation when applied to few-step settings. This makes interactive, high-quality text-guided editing on fast diffusion models an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242148/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 real image and an automatically generated detailed text caption (via LLaVA) as input. Inversion produces a set of 4 frozen noise maps (ε_T…ε_1) in 8 NFEs (one-time cost); each subsequent edit requires only 4 NFEs by re-running the frozen SDXL-Turbo generator with a modified prompt and the same noise maps. An optional binary mask (derived from a rough 16×16 attention map or user-provided) restricts edits to a local region. The output is a photorealistic edited image at the same resolution, with only the specified attribute changed.

## Method

TurboEdit introduces the first encoder-based iterative inversion network for diffusion models, inspired by GAN inversion (ReStyle). The network F, initialized from SDXL-Turbo, predicts injected noise ε_t conditioned on the input image x_0, the previous-step reconstruction x_{0,t+1}, the text prompt c, and timestep t; it is trained with an MSE reconstruction loss plus a small KL term (λ=10⁻⁶) that constrains predicted noise to a near-Gaussian distribution via the reparameterization trick, preventing overfitting and preserving editability. Disentangled editing exploits an emergent property of adversarially distilled models: conditioning on a long, detailed text prompt makes the model adhere to individual attributes independently, so changing a single word in the prompt alters only that attribute in the image. Editing strength is controlled by linearly interpolating source and target text embeddings, and an LLM (LLaVA) converts short user instructions into the required detailed target prompt. The full pipeline requires 8 NFEs for inversion and 4 NFEs per edit, running in under 0.5 seconds on a single H100 GPU.

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 images × 10 edit types), TurboEdit achieves the best background preservation across all metrics: PSNR 29.52, LPIPS 44.74×10⁻³, MSE 26.08×10⁻⁴, and SSIM 91.59×10⁻², outperforming the next-best method (Inversion-Free) by 1.01 dB in PSNR and 6.06 in SSIM. It also attains the highest CLIP similarity for both whole-image (25.05) and edited-region (22.34) alignment. In terms of speed, TurboEdit requires only 0.668 s for inversion and 0.508 s per edit (4 NFEs), compared to 56.98 s inversion and 3.66 s per edit for Null-Text Inversion, making it roughly 85× faster in the inversion stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TurboEdit outperforms all quantitative baselines in Table 1 on every background-preservation and CLIP-similarity metric. Against the closest competitor Inversion-Free (12 steps), it gains +1.01 dB PSNR, +6.06 SSIM, and +0.12 CLIP-Edited while being nearly 2× faster in forward generation (0.508 s vs 0.975 s). Compared to multi-step methods (Null-Text Inv, Direct Inv, P2P-Zero, MasaCtrl), TurboEdit improves PSNR by 2.3–9.1 dB and reduces inversion time by 15–85×. The 4-step DDIM baseline is the only method with a faster inversion (0.344 s), but it lags TurboEdit by 10.93 dB in PSNR and 24.73 in SSIM, indicating severe reconstruction degradation. Qualitatively, the paper shows that Ledits/Ledits++ and InfEdit/Pix2PixZero fail on large structural edits (e.g., adding a hat, man-to-woman), while InstructPix2Pix and its variants underperform on identity preservation (cat-to-dog) and prompt alignment (sweater-to-T-shirt). Against the concurrent ReNoise method (also built on SDXL-Turbo), TurboEdit requires 8 NFEs per inversion versus 36 NFEs and produces fewer artifacts with better face identity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: GAN encoder-based inversion (ReStyle, StyleGAN encoders) and diffusion model image editing. It departs from the dominant multi-step diffusion editing paradigm (DDIM/DDPM inversion + attention freezing) by showing these techniques are fundamentally incompatible with few-step distilled models like SDXL-Turbo, and instead proposes an iterative encoder network for inversion and an emergent property of distillation—disentangled adherence to detailed text prompts—as a replacement for attention-based structural control. The instruction-based editing component repurposes LLaVA/LLMs for prompt transformation, extending the InstructPix2Pix paradigm without requiring supervised training on paired edit datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing image editing from >3 seconds to <0.5 seconds enables truly interactive, iterative editing workflows that were previously impractical. The discovery that adversarial distillation naturally induces disentangled text-prompt adherence is a useful empirical finding that may generalize to other distilled models. However, open questions remain: the method cannot handle large pose changes, relies on the computationally heavy LLaVA for caption generation (a bottleneck for real-time use), and the attention-mask-based localization is imprecise near faces. Whether encoder-based inversion can be made fully real-time (eliminating the 8-NFE one-time cost) and whether the disentanglement property holds across different distillation methods are important follow-up directions.
