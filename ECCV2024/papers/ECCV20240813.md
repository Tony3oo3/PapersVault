## Problem

Skeleton-based pose gait recognition suffers from two fundamental drawbacks: (1) the discrete keypoint representation inevitably loses human shape information, leading to inferior performance compared to silhouette-based methods, and (2) the methods are highly sensitive to keypoint prediction errors, limiting cross-dataset generalization. The paper argues that the skeleton is an unnecessary intermediate representation and that the heatmaps produced by upstream pose estimation can be used directly for gait recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240813/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB video frames as input, which are cropped via human detection, resized to a fixed dimension in RGB space (with ×1.25 padding), and passed through a top-down pose estimator (HRNet or ViTPose) to produce multi-channel heatmaps of fixed size 64×48 per frame (V joint channels × T frames × H×W). During training, 30 frames are randomly sampled per sequence; during testing, all available frames are used. The output is a compact gait identity embedding produced by a multi-stage fusion branch, designed for efficient storage and retrieval.

## Method

GaitHeat bypasses the heatmap-to-keypoint decoding step and feeds the raw multi-channel heatmaps directly into a parameter-sharing global-local CNN (ResNet9 backbone). A parameter-free Pose-Guided Heatmap Alignment (PGHA) module, inspired by spatial transformer networks, corrects body tilt via rotation around the neck-hip midpoint and positional bias via translation to a fixed anchor, using bilinear grid sampling. The global branch aggregates all V joint channels via max-pooling, while K local branches apply different body-part partitioning strategies (concatenated in the batch dimension for parameter sharing); a Max Response operation merges local features into a global-like representation. A multi-stage fusion branch applies MR at multiple network depths followed by a parameter-independent F_fusion network to produce a compact embedding. Training jointly optimizes triplet and cross-entropy losses across all three branches (global, local, fusion), with only the fusion embedding used at inference.

## Results

On SUSTech1K, GaitHeat achieves 76.94% rank-1 accuracy (91.63% rank-5), surpassing the best prior pose-based method GPGait (42.45%) by 34.49% and matching the silhouette-based GaitBase (76.12%). With ViTPose heatmaps, GaitHeat++ reaches 90.00% rank-1 (97.37% rank-5), exceeding GaitBase by 13.88% and even outperforming the LiDAR-based LidarGait (86.77%) by 3.23%. On CASIA-B, GaitHeat++ achieves 95.94% mean rank-1 (99.60% NM, 97.88% BG, 90.35% CL), while on CCPG it reaches 83.04% rank-1 / 54.74% mAP in the clothing-changing setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaitHeat dramatically outperforms all prior skeleton-based pose methods: on SUSTech1K it beats GPGait (42.45%) by 34.49%, GaitTR (31.71%) by 45.23%, GaitGraph2 (22.11%) by 54.83%, and GaitGraph (19.96%) by 56.98% in rank-1 accuracy. Against silhouette-based methods, GaitHeat (76.94%) is comparable to GaitBase (76.12%) and GaitGL (63.14%), while GaitHeat++ (90.00%) surpasses GaitBase by 13.88% and GaitGL by 26.86%. Notably, GaitHeat++ also exceeds the LiDAR-based LidarGait (86.77%) by 3.23%, demonstrating that RGB-derived heatmaps can rival a dedicated 3D sensor modality. On CASIA-B, GaitHeat++ (95.94% mean) edges out GaitBase (89.67%) by 6.27%, and on CCPG the CL setting shows GaitHeat++ (83.04% R-1) trailing GaitBase (87.72%) by 4.68%, indicating that clothing variation remains a harder challenge for the heatmap representation. In cross-domain evaluation, GaitHeat and GaitHeat++ consistently lead over GPGait and other skeleton methods on CASIA-B and SUSTech1K targets, though GPGait retains a slight edge in the most challenging CCPG clothing conditions (CL, UP, DN).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant skeleton-based pose gait recognition paradigm (GaitGraph, GPGait, GaitTR) by arguing that the intermediate keypoint decoding step is both lossy and error-sensitive, and instead feeds raw multi-channel heatmaps from upstream pose estimators (HRNet, ViTPose) directly into a recognition network. Architecturally, it borrows set pooling and horizontal pyramid mapping from GaitSet, the ResNet9 backbone from GaitBase, and the grid-sampling mechanism from Spatial Transformer Networks for its Pose-Guided Heatmap Alignment module. The conceptual lineage also connects to heatmap-based action recognition (PoTion, PoseConv3D), but this is the first work to apply that representation specifically to gait identity recognition with a dedicated global-local fusion design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the long-standing performance gap between pose-based and silhouette-based gait recognition (GaitHeat++ reaches 95.94% mean on CASIA-B vs. 91.83% for GaitGL), this work makes pose-based gait recognition a practically viable alternative that is inherently more robust to clothing changes, occlusions, and low-light conditions. The key open questions the authors identify are: (1) whether end-to-end joint training of pose estimation and gait recognition can further align upstream objectives with downstream identity discrimination, (2) whether the semantic information in heatmaps can be more deeply exploited beyond the current max-pooling fusion, and (3) the higher computational and storage cost of heatmaps versus sparse coordinates in deployment. Cross-domain generalization remains imperfect, particularly on CCPG's clothing-variation conditions, suggesting that distribution shift in appearance covariates is not fully resolved by alignment alone.
