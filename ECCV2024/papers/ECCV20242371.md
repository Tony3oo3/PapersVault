## Problem

Whether CLIP models exhibit genuine Compositional Out-of-Distribution (C-OoD) generalization — the ability to classify unseen combinations of known attributes and objects — remains unexplored, largely because prior compositional benchmarks for VLMs fail to verify that test compositions are truly absent from CLIP training data. The paper asks two questions: do CLIPs possess nontrivial C-OoD generalization in the single-object setting, and what internal representation property is responsible for it?

## Input / Output

The primary evaluation takes RGB images from the ImageNet-AO dataset (~60,000 images spanning 21,000 novel attribute-object pairs) and produces zero-shot class predictions over ~21,000 combinations, computed via cosine similarity between image embeddings and class-averaged caption embeddings built from 80 template captions per class. A secondary retrieval task takes an image embedding plus a text adjective embedding (summed as a query) and outputs the nearest image in the dataset. Disentanglement analysis additionally yields quantitative scores (Z-Diff Score, DCI, Explicitness, normalized soft rank) for both text and image encoders across multiple datasets.

## Method

The paper introduces ImageNet-AO, a benchmark of ~60,000 images generated by SD-XL Turbo from 140 VAW adjectives × 1000 ImageNet nouns, filtered through three stages (human validation, exclusion of combinations present in LAION/CommonPool/YFCC/CC captions, and Faiss kNN image deduplication) to guarantee novelty relative to CLIP training data. The core empirical finding is that C-OoD accuracy strongly correlates with disentanglement metrics of the text encoder (and, to a lesser degree, the image encoder), measured via Z-Diff Score, DCI, Explicitness, and normalized soft rank of composition embeddings. Theoretically, the paper shows that CLIP's contrastive loss implicitly maximizes mutual information between text and image embeddings; when text embeddings are decomposable (attribute ⊥ object), a KL-divergence decomposition proves this mutual information maximization induces corresponding decomposability in image embeddings. Additional experiments on Shapes3D and Sprites confirm that models with higher C-OoD accuracy exhibit fewer shared dimensions across latent factors and require fewer dimension switches for zero-shot color manipulation.

## Results

On the ImageNet-AO benchmark (~21,000 unique attribute-object combinations, ~60,000 images), LAION-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 achieves the highest C-OoD accuracy at 64.61%, followed by LAION ViT-B/16 (61.55%) and LAION ViT-B/32 (61.05%). OpenAI CLIPs range from 47.07% (ViT-B/32) to 52.28% (ViT-L/14), while CC RN50 and YFCC RN50 drop to 26.64% and 12.23% respectively. Disentanglement metrics (Z-Diff Score, DCI, Explicitness) and intrinsic dimensionality (soft rank) of both text and image embeddings are strongly correlated with C-OoD accuracy across all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks over a dozen CLIP variants trained on different datasets (LAION, OpenAI, CC12m, YFCC15m, DataComp, CommonPool, WebLI, DFN-5B) with various backbones (ResNet-50/101, ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, ViT-H/14, ViT-g/14, ViT-BigG-14). LAION-trained models consistently achieve the highest C-OoD accuracy, outperforming OpenAI CLIPs by roughly 10–15 percentage points and CC/YFCC models by 35–50 points. Notably, dataset quality matters more than raw size: CommonPool-XL filtered with ClipScore outperforms the unfiltered CommonPool-XL (which contains 7 billion additional images). SigLIP (WebLI) shows a negative slope, where larger backbones improve in-distribution accuracy but degrade C-OoD performance. In the image+text retrieval task, LAION and DataComp CLIPs also achieve the best Recall@1, consistent with their C-OoD rankings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional generalization literature from NLP and VLM evaluation (ARO, Winoground, VL-Checklist) by introducing a stricter Compositional Out-of-Distribution (C-OoD) formulation that guarantees test compositions are truly absent from CLIP training data. It builds on the CLIP contrastive learning framework (Radford et al.) and its zero-shot evaluation protocol (Ilharco et al.), but departs from prior compositional understanding studies by shifting focus from 'can the model encode composition?' to 'can the model generalize to unseen compositions?' The paper further bridges the disentanglement representation learning literature (Z-Diff, DCI, Explicitness) with CLIP's compositional generalization, proposing that text-space disentanglement is transferred to image space through contrastive alignment—a connection not previously formalized for CLIPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ImageNet-AO benchmark (~60K images, 21K novel attribute-object pairs) fills a critical gap: prior compositional VLM evaluations could not guarantee test novelty relative to training data, making C-OoD claims unreliable. The consistent finding across multiple disentanglement metrics, intrinsic dimensionality, and retrieval experiments that text representation decomposability drives C-OoD performance offers a concrete diagnostic for practitioners selecting or designing CLIP variants. Key limitations include the single-object setting (no multi-object or relational compositions), reliance on synthetic SD-XL Turbo images, and a theoretical argument that, while suggestive, lacks formal guarantees. Open questions include whether explicit disentanglement regularization during CLIP training can further improve C-OoD, and whether these findings extend to multi-object and relational compositions.
