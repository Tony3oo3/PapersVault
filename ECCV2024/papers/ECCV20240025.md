## Problem

Existing novel view synthesis methods built on 2D image diffusion models suffer from limited multi-view consistency, while prior video-diffusion-based approaches (SVD-MV, IM-3D) generate orbital videos only at fixed elevation without explicit camera pose control. This paper addresses both gaps by adapting a high-resolution latent video diffusion model for pose-controllable, multi-view-consistent NVS at 576×576, and then leveraging the resulting views for high-quality 3D mesh generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240025/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of an object and produces a 21-frame orbital video at 576×576 resolution following an arbitrary camera trajectory parameterized by per-frame elevation and azimuth angles. For 3D generation, the SV3D-generated multi-view images are used as reconstruction targets to optimize a textured mesh via a two-stage pipeline (Instant-NGP NeRF at low resolution, then DMTet at full resolution), with the final mesh exported via xatlas UV unwrapping. The entire mesh extraction takes approximately 8 minutes without SDS loss and ~20 minutes with it.

## Method

SV3D finetunes the SVD-xt latent video diffusion model by (i) concatenating the conditioning image's VAE latent to the noisy state, (ii) feeding its CLIP embedding into cross-attention layers, and (iii) adding sinusoidal embeddings of camera elevation and azimuth angles to the noise-timestep embedding in every residual block, enabling explicit pose control. Training uses both static orbits (fixed elevation, regular azimuth) and dynamic orbits (irregular azimuth with sinusoidal elevation variation), with a progressive curriculum (SV3D^p) that first learns static orbits unconditionally before adding pose conditioning on dynamic orbits. For 3D generation, the paper introduces a soft-masked SDS loss that applies diffusion guidance only to surfaces not visible in the reference orbit (using a smoothstep function on the dot product of view direction and surface normal), a disentangled illumination model with 24 Spherical Gaussians to avoid baked-in lighting, and a triangular CFG scaling schedule that avoids over-sharpening at the loop-back frame.

## Results

On GSO static orbits, SV3D^p achieves LPIPS 0.08, PSNR 21.26 dB, SSIM 0.88, and CLIP-S 0.89, substantially outperforming the best prior method (Stable Zero123: LPIPS 0.13, PSNR 18.34 dB, SSIM 0.78). Even the pose-unconditioned SV3D^u (LPIPS 0.09, PSNR 21.14 dB) surpasses all baselines. For 3D generation on GSO, SV3D^p with dynamic orbit and masked SDS achieves Chamfer Distance 0.024 and 3D IoU 0.614, approaching the GT-render upper bound (CD 0.021, 3D IoU 0.689) while far exceeding the best baseline (Stable Zero123: CD 0.039, 3D IoU 0.550).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SV3D wins decisively across all metrics and all baselines on both NVS and 3D generation. On NVS (GSO static orbits), SV3D^p reduces LPIPS by 38–53% relative to baselines (0.08 vs. 0.13–0.17) and gains 2.9–5.5 dB in PSNR over the strongest competitor Stable Zero123 (21.26 vs. 18.34). The gap is even larger on dynamic orbits, where pose-controllable SV3D^p (LPIPS 0.09, PSNR 20.38) outperforms the best baseline Stable Zero123 (LPIPS 0.13, PSNR 18.04) by a wide margin. For 3D generation, SV3D^p achieves CD 0.024 and 3D IoU 0.614, beating Stable Zero123 (CD 0.039, IoU 0.550) by 38% in Chamfer distance and 11.6% in IoU, and outperforming geometry-focused methods like SyncDreamer (CD 0.053, IoU 0.451) and One-2-3-45++ (CD 0.054, IoU 0.406) by roughly 2×. Notably, even SV3D^u without explicit camera conditioning outperforms all image-diffusion-based NVS baselines, suggesting the video-diffusion prior itself provides substantial multi-view consistency. The paper also reports a user study where 30 participants preferred SV3D orbital videos over Zero123XL, Stable Zero123, EscherNet, and Free3D 96–99% of the time on 22 real-world images.

## Relation to Prior Work

SV3D repurposes Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) for novel view synthesis by adding explicit camera pose conditioning (elevation and azimuth) to the UNet's residual blocks, departing from prior NVS methods (Zero123, SyncDreamer, MVDream) that finetune image diffusion models and impose multi-view consistency through cross-attention or 3D-aware losses. The key conceptual move is treating temporal coherence in video generation as a proxy for spatial 3D consistency, an idea shared in spirit with SVD-MV and IM-3D but extended here with full pose controllability and dynamic orbits. For 3D generation, the paper builds on DreamFusion's SDS loss but introduces a masked variant that restricts guidance to unseen regions, and adds a disentangled spherical-Gaussian illumination model to reduce baked-in lighting artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SV3D provides a practical, publicly-released foundation model for single-image-to-3D generation that outperforms prior methods by a substantial margin on both NVS and 3D reconstruction metrics, with the best configuration approaching ground-truth-render reconstruction quality (CD 0.024 vs. 0.021). The demonstration that video diffusion priors inherently encode stronger multi-view consistency than image diffusion priors offers a clear design principle for future 3D generation systems. Open limitations include the restriction to 2-DOF camera control (elevation/azimuth only), failure on mirror-like reflective surfaces, and the Lambertian shading assumption, all of which constrain applicability to more complex real-world scenes.
