## Problem

Existing text-driven NeRF editing methods, particularly InstructNeRF2NeRF (IN2N), suffer from over-editing: they modify background and irrelevant regions alongside the intended target, because they lack a mechanism to localize edits within the 3D scene. This paper addresses the gap by enabling precise, locally controlled editing of neural radiance fields using only text prompts, without requiring bounding boxes, pre-computed segmentation masks, or other external guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241717/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images of a 3D scene (encoded via Stable Diffusion's VAE into 4-channel latent feature maps at reduced spatial resolution W'×H'×4) and a free-form text editing prompt as input. It produces an edited 3D NeRF scene parameterized in latent space (decodable to RGB via the SD decoder), a per-pixel binary editing mask in latent space (W'×H'×4), and for multi-attribute prompts, multiple masks generated by an LLM-decomposed set of sub-prompts. Cross-view mask consistency is enforced via a zero-shot point tracker with K-Medoids query point selection.

## Method

LatentEditor trains NeRF directly in Stable Diffusion's latent space, redefining volume rendering to predict 4-channel latent features and density rather than RGB color, which reduces computational cost and enables native latent-space editing. The key innovation is a delta module that computes pixel-level scores by contrasting IP2P's conditional noise prediction (given the editing prompt) against its unconditional prediction in the latent space; thresholding these delta scores yields a binary mask that constrains edits to the target region while preserving the background. A refinement adapter with residual connections and self-attention corrects misalignment between rendered latent features and the original scene's latents, and camera parameters are preconditioned via Cholesky decomposition of the Jacobian covariance to balance their influence during latent-space optimization. Editing proceeds through an iterative dataset-update strategy where DDIM denoising (guided by the mask and prompt) generates edited latents that progressively replace originals, achieving 5–8× fewer iterations than IN2N. For multi-attribute editing, Llama 2 decomposes compound prompts into sub-instructions, each independently generating its own mask through the delta module.

## Results

Across 200 edits on 20 scenes from four datasets (LLFF, IN2N, NeRFStudio, NeRF-Art), LatentEditor achieves the best CLIP Text-Image Direction Similarity (0.2661), CLIP Direction Consistency (0.9387), and Edit PSNR (25.15) among all compared methods. The method requires only ~2,000 editing iterations to converge, compared to 10,000+ for IN2N, yielding a 5–8× speedup. A user study with 500 votes confirms LatentEditor is preferred over baselines on 3D consistency, content preservation, and text adherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LatentEditor outperforms all four quantitative baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor IN2N [8], it gains +0.0012 CLIP text-image similarity, +0.0029 direction consistency, and +1.08 dB Edit PSNR, while also being 5–8× faster (≈2,000 vs. >10,000 iterations). The gap over NeRF-Art [47] is larger: +0.0044 CLIP similarity and +4.11 dB PSNR, reflecting NeRF-Art's weaker local-editing capability. Control4D [41] and DreamEditor [55] trail by 0.018–0.028 in CLIP similarity and 4.3–5.3 dB in PSNR, largely because they rely on pre-computed masks or GAN guidance rather than text-driven localization. In the object-removal task (Figure 6), LatentEditor surpasses Gaussian Grouping [52] in spatial accuracy and view consistency, though this comparison is qualitative only.

## Relation to Prior Work

LatentEditor extends the InstructNeRF2NeRF (IN2N) paradigm of iterative dataset-update editing by shifting the entire NeRF training and editing pipeline from RGB space into the Stable Diffusion latent space, following the latent-NeRF training principle from Metzer et al. The key conceptual departure is the delta module, which repurposes the conditional-vs-unconditional noise prediction difference from InstructPix2Pix's classifier-free guidance as a pixel-level editing mask in latent space, eliminating the need for pre-computed segmentation masks, bounding boxes, or GAN guidance that prior methods (DreamEditor, Blended-NeRF, Control-4D) require for localization. Camera parameter optimization is adapted from CAMP's Cholesky-based preconditioning, and multi-attribute editing leverages LLaMA 2 for prompt decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–8× reduction in editing iterations over IN2N makes text-driven NeRF editing substantially more practical, and the guidance-free local editing capability removes a significant usability barrier for non-expert users. However, the method's ceiling is bounded by InstructPix2Pix's instruction-following quality (e.g., failing on 'turn the bear orange'), so gains from future instruction-conditioned diffusion models will directly translate to improved editing fidelity. An open question is whether the latent-space NeRF formulation generalizes beyond the four relatively small-scale benchmark scenes tested, and whether the delta module's threshold-based masking remains robust for complex multi-object scenes with overlapping semantic regions.
