## Problem

Existing vision-language models for driving are confined to 2D scene description, lacking the spatial localization (pinpointing objects in 3D space), temporal memorization (recalling past events), and forecasting (predicting future states) capabilities that embodied agents require for complete 4D scene understanding. This paper identifies these four missing competencies and formulates a comprehensive benchmark and model to address them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241650/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-frame video sequences (up to 60 seconds for Ego4D tasks, 3.5 seconds for nuScenes tasks), a natural-language text prompt, a timestamp, and optionally a 2D pixel query point. For localization tasks, the 3D space is discretized into 1-meter resolution grids and the model outputs the grid index as a rewritten vocabulary token alongside a category label; for language tasks it generates free-form text descriptions, driving decisions, or event summaries. The output format is unified as text generation, with spatial coordinates encoded as special tokens rather than regression heads.

## Method

ELM builds on the BLIP-2 architecture (EVA visual encoder → Slot Attention → Q-former → FlanT5 with LoRA) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, space-aware pre-training rewrites the least frequent FlanT5 vocabulary entries to represent 1-meter 3D grid indices (an RT-2-style tokenizer), then pre-trains on over 9 million auto-labeled image-text pairs from nuScenes, Waymo, YouTube, and Ego4D to instill spatial localization while preserving description ability. Second, time-aware token selection encodes timestamps as text (via the FlanT5 encoder rather than sinusoidal positional codes) and uses a token bank where learnable queries first attend to the text prompt, then cross-attend to the concatenated sequence of per-frame video tokens and timestamp embeddings, performing soft weighted aggregation to extract moment-specific and content-specific visual features from long videos. The final selected tokens are passed through Q-former and fed to the language model for generation.

## Results

On the nuScenes benchmark, ELM achieves 51.6% Pr@1 on Box Detection (vs. 41.8% for Otter, the strongest prior method), 14.0% Pr@1 on Tracking, and 15.1% Pr@1 on Box Prediction. On language tasks, ELM scores 76.5 CIDEr on Traffic Sign Inquiry, 73.2 on Surrounding Narration, and 74.4 on Action & Decision. On Ego4D, ELM reaches 22.6 CIDEr on Moment Recap and 18.1 on Activity Prediction, substantially outperforming all baselines. ELM uses only 2.7B parameters (FlanT5 backbone), matching the parameter count of BLIP-2 while surpassing 7B-parameter models like LLaVA and Otter on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELM outperforms all seven VLM baselines (BLIP2-opt, BLIP2-flant5, LLaMA-Adapter V2, LLaVA, Otter, VideoChat, Vid-ChatGPT) on 9 out of 10 benchmark tasks. The most notable margin is on Box Detection, where ELM (51.6% Pr@1) surpasses Otter (41.8%) by +9.8% and LLaVA (28.5%) by +23.1%, validating the space-aware pre-training. On temporal retrieval tasks, ELM leads by +13.4% CIDEr on Traffic Sign Inquiry over LLaMA-Adapter V2 and +9.4% on Moment Recap over VideoChat, attributed to the time-aware token selection. The sole exception is Event Query on Ego4D, where LLaVA (39.8 CIDEr) narrowly edges out ELM (38.0), which the authors attribute to FlanT5's limited capacity for long-text comprehension. In the out-of-distribution planning evaluation on Waymo, ELM achieves 2.28m ADE and 4.27m FDE, beating both UniAD-single (4.16m/9.31m) and Flamingo (2.78m/5.31m). Against traditional 3D detectors, ELM (51.6% Pr@1) is competitive with BEVFormer (47.4%) and DETR3D (43.6%) but slightly trails VCD (53.4%) overall, though it leads on car (64.9 vs. 50.3), barrier (70.4 vs. 68.1), and trailer (26.4 vs. 20.6) categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELM builds directly on the BLIP-2 architecture (Q-former + frozen visual encoder + LLM) and extends it with two key modules: a space-aware pre-training stage inspired by RT-2's vocabulary rewriting to enable 3D coordinate prediction as a language modeling task, and a time-aware token selection module (token bank with cross-attention) for long-horizon temporal retrieval. It departs from prior driving VLMs (DriveLM, DriveGPT4, LLM-driver, nuScenes-QA) that are confined to scene description, by formulating localization, memorization, and forecasting as first-class tasks. The pre-training data pipeline draws on LLaMA-Adapter V2 for auto-captioning and GPT-4 for template generation, while the fine-tuning stage uses LoRA on FlanT5, keeping the overall parameter count at 2.7B comparable to BLIP-2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified benchmark (10 tasks across nuScenes and Ego4D) that systematically evaluates VLMs on four embodied capabilities—description, 3D localization, temporal memorization, and forecasting—rather than the description-only framing of prior driving VLM work. The finding that a 2.7B VLM achieves 3D detection performance (51.6% Pr@1) competitive with dedicated perception models (DETR3D: 43.6%, BEVFormer: 47.4%) suggests language-model-based perception may be a viable alternative to purpose-built detectors. Key limitations remain: ELM does not generate control signals, is evaluated primarily on nuScenes and Ego4D, and the FlanT5 backbone constrains long-context reasoning (evidenced by underperformance on Event Query vs. LLaVA). Open questions include whether this framework can close the loop for autonomous driving and whether common-sense reasoning from LLMs genuinely improves decision-making in novel scenarios.
