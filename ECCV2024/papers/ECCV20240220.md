## Problem

Rendered novel views from 3D reconstructions (meshes, point clouds, NeRFs, Gaussian splats) suffer from geometric and appearance artifacts that degrade downstream tasks such as localization validation, synthetic data generation, and autonomous agent training. Existing artifact-removal methods are tied to specific reconstruction pipelines, and no general post-processing step leverages nearby reference images captured during the reconstruction to enhance arbitrary renderings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images at 160×160 resolution: a rendered novel view (from any 3D reconstruction at a given camera pose) and a nearby reference image captured at a different pose during the original reconstruction. The output is an enhanced rendering at the same resolution. The pipeline is applied iteratively—the enhanced output of one pass becomes the rendering input of the next—so the number of iterations is a user-controlled inference-time parameter. The method is device-agnostic and generalizes to grayscale inputs and unseen scenes without retraining.

## Method

MaRINeR adapts the MASA RefSR pipeline for rendering enhancement by (1) extracting multi-level features from both inputs with a shared 3-level convolutional encoder, (2) matching the deepest (level-3) features via MASA's coarse-to-fine MEM module using cosine similarity, and (3) warping and fusing reference features into the rendering through Spatial Adaptation Modules, Dual Residual Aggregation Modules, and residual blocks in a decoder. A key architectural departure from RefSR is that matching operates on the deepest features and the decoder merges rendering and warped-reference features at the same spatial resolution (rather than fusing across resolutions), enabling the model to transfer structural information from the reference to remove artifacts and fill gaps. Training uses a combined L1 reconstruction loss, a perceptual loss on shallow VGG16 layers (relu1_1, relu2_2, relu3_3) to avoid deep-feature domain mismatch, and a Relativistic GAN adversarial loss. Two task-specific data augmentations—rendering from meshes down-sampled to 10% of original triangles and random reference selection within a 5-second temporal window—improve robustness to mesh quality variation and reference distance.

## Results

On the LaMAR dataset, MaRINeR achieves 20.03 PSNR and 0.180 LPIPS on CAB, 21.73 PSNR and 0.155 LPIPS on LIN, and 20.96 PSNR and 0.176 LPIPS on the unseen HGE scene, substantially outperforming all baselines. With the GT image as reference (upper bound), MaRINeR reaches 23.89 PSNR and 0.089 LPIPS on CAB. On the 12 Scenes dataset (different reconstruction pipeline), MaRINeR improves PSNR from 20.59 to 22.99 and LPIPS from 0.164 to 0.071. The method also improves homography estimation for pseudo-GT validation, increasing inlier ratio from 61.24% to 78.16% and reducing homography error from 4.86 to 1.88.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaRINeR outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all four test sets, with the largest margins in LPIPS (perceptual quality): 0.180 vs. 0.364–0.393 on CAB, a roughly 2× improvement. Among the RefSR baselines, DATSR (15.63 PSNR on CAB) slightly edges out MASA (15.47), but both remain close to the unenhanced rendering baseline (15.60), indicating that methods designed for resolution-gap super-resolution struggle with the domain gap between renderings and real images. Among style-transfer baselines, NNST (semantic ST) is the strongest, reaching 18.53 PSNR on LIN and 18.48 on HGE, yet still trails MaRINeR by over 3 PSNR. WCT2 (photorealistic ST) achieves 17.44 PSNR on LIN but preserves rendering artifacts since it only adapts color statistics. ArtFlow (universal ST) introduces unrealistic distortions because it transfers both content and style indiscriminately. MaRINeR also generalizes to the unseen HGE scene with performance comparable to the in-distribution CAB and LIN scenes, and to the 12 Scenes dataset (different reconstruction pipeline: RGB-D SLAM + BundleFusion vs. LiDAR + Advancing Front), confirming robustness across reconstruction methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaRINeR repurposes the feature-matching-and-transfer paradigm from reference-based super-resolution (specifically MASA-SR) for a fundamentally different domain gap: rather than bridging a resolution difference between low-res and high-res images, it bridges the render-to-real gap between a noisy 3D reconstruction rendering and a nearby real photograph. The MEM, SAM, and DRAM modules are directly inherited from MASA, but the authors modify the architecture to fuse features at matching spatial resolutions (rather than fusing across resolutions as in RefSR) so that structural information from the reference can correct geometric artifacts, not just add texture. The paper also positions itself against style transfer methods, arguing that neither universal ST (which corrupts content) nor semantic ST (which introduces distortions) can simultaneously remove artifacts and preserve rendering structure the way their approach does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in AR/mixed-reality pipelines: the render-to-real gap that currently forces manual sanity checks in pseudo-ground-truth validation and limits the utility of synthetic training data for reinforcement learning agents. By enabling automated homography-based validation (reducing error from 4.86 to 1.88) and enhancing synthetic trajectories, it has direct operational impact on datasets like LaMAR and 12 Scenes. Key limitations remain: the model operates best at ~160px resolution, may erroneously remove real content it mistakes for artifacts, can introduce temporal flickering between frames, and matches at the texture level rather than semantic level, restricting its applicability when rendering and reference have very different appearances.
