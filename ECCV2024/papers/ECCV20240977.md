## Problem

Completing a room-scale 3D scene from sparse RGBD observations requires generating missing geometry and appearance with cross-view consistency, yet existing methods like RGBD2 depend on human-designed text prompts, predefined camera trajectories, and dataset-specific training (e.g., ScanNet), limiting both automation and cross-domain generalizability. No prior approach can automatically produce a complete, textured room mesh from a handful of views without these manual inputs or domain-specific fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse RGB images (240×180) with corresponding depth maps and camera poses as input, where N can be as low as 5% of a scene's frames. It outputs a complete room-scale 3D mesh represented as vertices, vertex colors, and faces, generated at 512×512 resolution via Stable Diffusion. The pipeline is entirely training-free: it leverages a pre-trained Stable Diffusion inpainting model and IronDepth for monocular depth estimation, requiring no fine-tuning on the target dataset. A Gaussian blur (kernel size 5) is applied to rendered outputs before metric computation to account for the resolution mismatch with 240×180 ground truth.

## Method

GenRC's core insight is to generate a single cross-view consistent panoramic RGBD image (from a computed room center) that completes the main portion of the scene in one shot, rather than iteratively inpainting along a camera trajectory. The key architectural contribution is E-Diffusion, a modified MultiDiffusion that decomposes the equirectangular panorama into M overlapping perspective views (64×64 latents) and enforces equirectangular geometry by warping and averaging predicted clean latents across all views at each denoising step. In the final 20 of 50 denoising steps, the equirectangular constraint is dropped and standard MultiDiffusion is used for Texture Refinement to recover high-frequency details lost during warping. Textual inversion extracts a single style token from the input images, replacing human-written prompts with a fixed template ('a simple and clean room in the style of S*'). An active sampling strategy generates A=3 panorama candidates and selects the one minimizing MSE between rendered and input depth maps, preventing generated geometry from occluding the original camera views. Depth is estimated via IronDepth with scale alignment to the rendered mesh and iterative refinement using rendered depth as ground truth.

## Results

On ScanNet, GenRC achieves the best PSNR (14.4 dB at 5% sparse, 17.6 dB at 20%) and SSIM (0.524 at 5%, 0.628 at 20%), outperforming RGBD2 (13.7/17.5 PSNR) and T2R+RGBD (12.9/16.6 PSNR). For geometry, GenRC attains the lowest depth MSE (0.27 at 5%, 0.09 at 20%) versus RGBD2 (0.38/0.08) and T2R+RGBD (0.57/0.12). On the cross-domain ARKitScenes dataset, GenRC maintains its lead with 13.2 PSNR at 5% sparse input, compared to 12.2 for RGBD2 and 12.0 for T2R+RGBD, while also achieving the best CLIP Score (0.84) across all sparsity levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenRC is compared against two baselines: RGBD2 [18] (a four-channel RGBD diffusion model trained on ScanNet, using iterative inpainting along a fixed camera trajectory) and T2R+RGBD [15] (Text2Room modified for sparse RGBD completion, also using iterative inpainting). On ScanNet, GenRC clearly outperforms both baselines under sparse observations (5% and 10%): it gains +0.7 dB PSNR over RGBD2 and +1.5 dB over T2R+RGBD at 5%, and reduces depth MSE by 29% (0.27 vs. 0.38) over RGBD2. At denser settings (20% and 50%), RGBD2 trained on ScanNet becomes competitive on depth MSE (0.08 vs. 0.09 at 20%; 0.06 vs. 0.09 at 50%) and PSNR at 50% (18.6 vs. 18.2), but GenRC still leads on SSIM, LPIPS, and CLIP Score. On the cross-domain ARKitScenes dataset, GenRC wins on every metric at every sparsity level, with RGBD2 degrading significantly (e.g., 12.2 vs. 13.2 PSNR at 5%), confirming that GenRC's reliance on large-scale pre-trained Stable Diffusion generalizes better than RGBD2's ScanNet-specific training. T2R+RGBD consistently lags on geometric metrics (depth MSE 0.57 at 5% vs. 0.27 for GenRC) despite competitive CLIP Scores, indicating it prioritizes texture fidelity over geometric correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenRC builds directly on MultiDiffusion [3] by modifying its multi-view denoising loop to enforce equirectangular projection geometry, enabling panorama inpainting with a standard perspective-image diffusion model. It departs from the iterative view-inpainting paradigm of RGBD2 [18] and Text2Room [15], which require predefined camera trajectories and (in RGBD2's case) a domain-specific RGBD diffusion model trained on ScanNet. Instead, GenRC repurposes a large-scale pretrained text-to-image model (Stable Diffusion) with textual inversion for style matching and a single-shot panoramic inpainting step, eliminating both the need for human-authored prompts and hand-designed camera paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, cross-domain pipeline lowers the barrier to 3D room completion: practitioners no longer need to train a scene-specific diffusion model or engineer camera trajectories, and the method generalizes from ScanNet to ARKitScenes without fine-tuning. However, the approach still depends on accurate input camera poses, monocular depth estimation quality (IronDepth), and a heuristic active-sampling step that may struggle with highly occluded or non-convex room layouts. Open questions include scalability to multi-room or outdoor scenes, handling of dynamic objects, and whether the panorama-centric formulation can be extended to arbitrary camera configurations beyond a single room center.
