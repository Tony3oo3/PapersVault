## Problem

Existing Vision Prompt Generators (VPGs) for Multimodal LLMs inevitably lose visual information when compressing image features into a fixed set of visual tokens: caption-prior VPGs capture only main objects while ignoring fine details, and instruction-prior VPGs overfit to the specific task templates in their training data. This information loss degrades downstream understanding, yet recovering the missing semantics has traditionally required additional high-quality image-text pairs or instruction data, which are costly to curate.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a frozen ViT-G encoder into patch embeddings) and a text instruction as input, and produces a set of complementary visual tokens (32 additional tokens per iteration) that are concatenated with the original VPG tokens before being prepended to the LLM's text embedding. The VTC module itself is trained in a fully unsupervised manner using only the input image and a frozen Stable Diffusion decoder—no image-text pairs or instruction data are required. At inference, the VTC can be iteratively reapplied (typically twice) to progressively squeeze out additional visual concepts on demand.

## Method

VTC introduces a trainable self-attention layer that takes the concatenation of multi-level image patch embeddings (layers 12, 24, 36 of ViT-G) and the existing visual tokens as input, outputting a complementary feature vector that encodes the visual regions the VPG missed. This feature is fed back through the frozen VPG (e.g., Q-Former) to generate new visual tokens, which are concatenated with the originals to form a completed visual prompt. Training uses a reconstruction prior: the completed tokens are linearly projected into CLIP embedding space (via an orthogonal Procrustes mapping) and serve as text conditioning for a frozen Stable Diffusion model to denoise and reconstruct the input image, with the diffusion noise-prediction loss as the sole training signal. This makes the entire pipeline instruction-tuning-free and enables vision-language disentanglement, where the LLM can later be fine-tuned on instruction data without retraining the visual pathway.

## Results

On LVLM-eHub (Table 1), VTC consistently outperforms its base models across 12 VQA and reasoning datasets. The most striking gain is in visual dialogue (Visdial), where VTC(BLIP2) scores 77.20 vs. BLIP2's 10.73, and VTC(InstructBLIP) scores 65.78 vs. InstructBLIP's 45.20 — a ~45% relative improvement. On MME (Table 2), VTC variants achieve 3/14 best sub-scores each, with VTC(InstructBLIP) leading on Existence (188.00), Count (144.01), and Color (154.51). On DEMON, VTC(InstructBLIP) surpasses all baselines across all 7 task categories, including a 20% improvement over InstructBLIP in visual relation inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its three base models, VTC delivers consistent and often dramatic gains: VTC(BLIP2) improves Visdial from 10.73 to 77.20 and GQA from 45.53 to 48.97; VTC(MiniGPT-4) lifts Visdial from 10.31 to 76.62 and GQA from 30.82 to 35.11; VTC(InstructBLIP) raises Visdial from 45.20 to 65.78 (~45% relative gain) and WHOOPS from 30.13 to 33.74. Compared to LLaVA, VTC(BLIP2) substantially outperforms on Visdial (77.20 vs. 14.66), IconQA (62.31 vs. 42.95), and VSR (63.77 vs. 51.24), while LLaVA retains an edge on DocVQA (6.26 vs. 5.32) and TextVQA (38.92 vs. 32.00). Against Otter, which achieves 5/14 best sub-scores on MME, VTC(InstructBLIP) still wins on DEMON across all task categories, indicating superior performance in complex multi-modal dialogue scenarios. VTC variants also outperform mPLUG-Owl and VPGTrans on nearly all LVLM-eHub sub-tasks, with the largest margins in visual dialogue and spatial reasoning. The only notable loss is VTC(InstructBLIP) scoring 46.21 vs. InstructBLIP's 46.26 on ScienceQA, a negligible 0.05-point difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VPG-based MLLM architecture (Q-Former in BLIP-2/MiniGPT-4, instruction-tuned Q-Former in InstructBLIP, linear projection in LLaVA) but departs from the standard training paradigm of aligning visual tokens via autoregressive image-to-text losses (caption or instruction prior). The key conceptual shift is replacing the text-generation objective with a text-to-image reconstruction prior: a frozen Stable Diffusion model serves as the decoder, and a small trainable module (VTC) is optimized to recover visual features that the VPG's tokens fail to encode. This draws on the autoencoder principle of reconstruction-based representation learning, repurposed here to identify and complement missing visual semantics in a frozen multimodal pipeline without any image-text supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The instruction tuning-free property addresses a practical bottleneck: curating diverse, high-quality vision-language instruction pairs is expensive and limits the diversity of visual concepts a VPG can capture. The demonstrated disentanglement workflow (freeze VTC+VPG, fine-tune only the LLM for instructions) offers a cleaner training pipeline that may generalize better. However, the approach is validated only on sub-10B models with a single frozen SD checkpoint, and its dependence on the SD model's own biases in what constitutes 'complete' image content remains an open question. Scaling to larger models, video/audio modalities, and alternative generative decoders are natural next steps.
