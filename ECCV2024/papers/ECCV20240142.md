## Problem

Event cameras only trigger on brightness changes, so in the absence of motion or over large untextured regions the event stream becomes sparse and uninformative, causing stereo matching to fail catastrophically. While fusing sparse LiDAR depth with RGB stereo is well-studied, this fusion has remained unexplored for event stereo, and the fixed acquisition rate of LiDARs (e.g., 10 Hz) is in direct tension with the asynchronous microsecond resolution that makes event cameras attractive.

## Input / Output

The method consumes stereo event camera streams (asynchronous pixel-level brightness-change events) and sparse LiDAR depth measurements, which may be time-misaligned by up to 100 ms relative to the target disparity timestamp. Events are first converted into one of eight structured W×H×C representations (histogram, voxel grid, MDES, TORE, time surface, ERGO-12, Tencode, or concentration), and the output is a dense disparity map estimated by a CNN stereo backbone (AANet-derived). VSH operates on the stacked representation (gray-box access), while BTH operates on the raw event stream before stacking (black-box compatible).

## Method

The paper proposes two hallucination strategies that inject fictitious, distinctive patterns at LiDAR-measured depth locations to ease cross-view matching without modifying the stereo backbone. Virtual Stack Hallucination (VSH) augments each channel of the left and right event stacks with a random pattern sampled uniformly between the stack's min and max values, placed at pixel coordinates related by the triangulated disparity; it supports alpha-blending and patch-based injection. Back-in-Time Hallucination (BTH) instead inserts pairs of fictitious events (matching polarity and timestamp) directly into the raw left/right event histories, respecting time-ordering and epipolar geometry constraints; a repeated-injection variant distributes K fictitious events across B temporal bins using an MDES-inspired schedule, improving robustness to LiDAR misalignment. Both strategies generalize the virtual pattern projection operator from RGB stereo [4] to the event domain, and exhaustive ablations across eight stacked representations on DSEC and M3ED show they outperform cost-volume modulation (Guided) and input concatenation (LidarStereoNet) baselines by 2–25 percentage points on 1PE error.

## Results

On DSEC (retrained), VSH achieves the best 1PE of 9.25% and MAE of 0.50 (with ERGO-12), while BTH achieves the best 2PE of 2.87% (also ERGO-12). Both methods consistently outperform all RGB-stereo-derived baselines by 2–6 percentage points on 1PE across all eight event representations. On M3ED (pre-trained, no fine-tuning), BTH nearly halves the 1PE error (e.g., 20.41% vs. 36.33% baseline with ERGO-12), demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization. Average ranks confirm VSH (1.38/1.88/1.13) and BTH (1.63/1.38/1.88) dominate over Guided (5.00/5.00/5.00), Concat (3.38/3.00/3.13), and Guided+Concat (3.63/3.50/3.38) on DSEC retrained.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Guided Stereo Matching [49] (cost-volume modulation) is consistently the weakest baseline, achieving 1PE of 13.85–16.00% on DSEC retrained and 35.61–45.06% on M3ED pre-trained, because cost-volume modulation is ineffective where events are absent. LidarStereoNet-style concatenation [12] (1PE 11.95–12.99% on DSEC retrained) and Guided+Concat [69] (1PE 11.75–12.82%) perform better than Guided alone but are still outperformed by both VSH and BTH by 2–3 percentage points on 1PE. VSH wins on 1PE and MAE in most DSEC retrained settings (avg. rank 1.38/1.13), while BTH wins on 2PE (avg. rank 1.38) and is the clear winner on M3ED pre-trained (avg. rank 1.25 across all three metrics), nearly halving the 1PE error relative to the event-only baseline. On M3ED retrained, BTH is the most robust across domains and LiDAR densities (64-line vs. 16-line), while Guided+Concat occasionally fails to improve over the baseline (e.g., with Histogram and ERGO-12 representations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Virtual Pattern Projection (VPP) idea from Bartolomei et al. [4] (ICCV 2023), which projected distinctive color patterns onto RGB images guided by LiDAR depth, to the event-camera domain by hallucinating fictitious events instead of modifying pixel values. It departs from the standard RGB LiDAR-stereo fusion strategies (cost-volume modulation à la Guided Stereo Matching [49], input concatenation à la LidarStereoNet [12]) by arguing these are ill-suited to the sparse, asynchronous nature of event streams, and instead inserts synthetic events directly into the stream (BTH) or the stacked representation (VSH). The approach is built on the SE-CFF/AANet stereo backbone and is evaluated across eight event stacking representations, positioning it as a general, representation-agnostic fusion layer rather than a new stereo architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of LiDAR–event stereo fusion, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify as unexplored. The practical significance is that it preserves the microsecond temporal resolution of event cameras while still benefiting from active depth cues, and it tolerates up to 100 ms of LiDAR staleness with only marginal accuracy loss—removing the need for synchronous processing. A key open question is whether the residual accuracy drop from temporal misalignment can be eliminated entirely, and whether the hallucination paradigm extends to other sparse-sensor fusion problems (e.g., ToF, structured light) beyond LiDAR.
