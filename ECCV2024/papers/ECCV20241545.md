## Problem

Combining full 8-bit quantization with large-tile Winograd convolutions (F(4,3) and F(6,3)) causes severe numerical instability that degrades network accuracy, because quantization errors are amplified by the large matrix-element magnitudes in standard Winograd transformations. While the SOTA trainable-Winograd pipeline of Fernandez-Marques et al. reduces this gap, significant accuracy losses remain for F4 and F6, hindering their practical deployment on resource-constrained devices.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard CNN inputs (32×32 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet, low-resolution images for ×3 super-resolution, and RGB images for CamVid segmentation) and produces the corresponding task outputs (class labels, high-resolution images, or segmentation masks). The key internal modification is that all stride-1, 3×3 convolution layers (except first and last) are replaced by 8-bit quantized F4 or F6 Winograd convolutions, where every step of the Winograd pipeline—including the transformation matrices—is quantized to 8-bit signed symmetric uniform scalar precision.

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage approach to derive quantization-friendly Winograd transformation matrices Θ = {A, B, G}. First, a model- and data-free global particle swarm optimization (PSO) search over polynomial points in the extended Vandermonde parameterization minimizes the combined Frobenius norm of A, B, and G, yielding transformations with smaller element magnitudes that are inherently more robust to 8-bit quantization rounding. Second, these PSO-derived matrices replace the standard Winograd matrices as the initialization point in the SOTA trainable-Winograd pipeline of Fernandez-Marques et al., where Θ is treated as learnable parameters during supervised network training. The critical insight is that the starting point of the trainable optimization matters: evolving from Θ^pso rather than Θ^std leads to substantially lower quantization error and higher final accuracy, closing or nearly closing the gap to full-precision direct convolutions across classification, super-resolution, and segmentation tasks.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, the proposed method achieves 93.26% top-1 accuracy with F4 Winograd convolutions (vs. 92.50% for the SOTA trainable-Θ pipeline and 93.17% for FP direct convolutions), effectively closing the quantization gap. For F6, accuracy reaches 89.57% (vs. 87.74% SOTA), a +1.8 pp improvement. On ImageNet (ResNet-18), F4 reaches 69.15% (vs. 67.05% SOTA, 69.79% FP), while F6 jumps from 53.90% to 62.87%. For super-resolution (ESPCN, ×3, Set5), F4 achieves 32.11 dB PSNR (vs. 30.86 dB SOTA, 32.53 dB FP), and for segmentation (ENet, CamVid), F4 reaches 60.00 mIoU (vs. 56.12 SOTA, 60.61 FP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated task. Against the SOTA Winograd-aware training pipeline of Fernandez-Marques et al. [17], the improvement is consistent: +0.8 pp on CIFAR-10 F4 (93.26 vs. 92.50), +2.1 pp on ImageNet F4 (69.15 vs. 67.05), and a dramatic +9.0 pp on ImageNet F6 (62.87 vs. 53.90). Against the Legendre-polynomial base change of Barabasz [6], the method gains +0.73 pp (92.53 vs. 91.8) on CIFAR-10 F4 ResNet-18 (mult. 0.5) and +0.59 pp (90.29 vs. 89.7) at mult. 0.25. Against WinoTrain [35], the advantage is substantial: +2.61 pp on CIFAR-10 F4 ResNet-20 (92.31 vs. 89.7) and +7.87 pp on ImageNet F4 ResNet-18 (69.15 vs. 61.28). Against WinoVidiVici [34], the method leads by +1.42 pp on CIFAR-10 F4 ResNet-20 (92.31 vs. 90.89) and is essentially tied on ImageNet F4 ResNet-18 (69.15 vs. 69.14). The largest absolute gains appear for F6 convolutions, where the standard Winograd matrices suffer severe numerical instability and the PSO-derived initialization provides a critical starting point for trainable optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Winograd-aware training pipeline of Fernandez-Marques et al. [17], which first demonstrated that treating Winograd transformation matrices Θ as learnable parameters during quantized network training dramatically reduces accuracy loss. The key departure is a two-stage strategy: first, a model- and data-free PSO search over the extended Vandermonde parameterization of [44] minimizes the Frobenius norms of {A, B, G} to produce quantization-friendly matrices Θ^pso; second, these matrices serve as the initialization for the trainable-Θ pipeline of [17] rather than the standard Winograd matrices. This positions the work as an initialization improvement to an existing training framework, grounded in the observation that smaller-magnitude transformation elements reduce quantization error amplification at each step of the Winograd computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: F(6,3) Winograd convolutions, previously unusable at full 8-bit precision due to severe numerical instability, become viable with accuracy gaps to FP direct convolutions reduced to 1–2% on classification tasks. The approach preserves the same inference speedup and training cost as the SOTA pipeline, making it a drop-in improvement. Open questions remain: F6 training is still unstable for some architectures (e.g., RFDN), non-unit-stride convolutions are excluded, and the paper acknowledges that more sophisticated quantization schemes (tap-wise, mixed-precision) could yield further gains. Selective replacement of error-tolerant layers with F6 convolutions via NAS-style search is identified as a promising direction.
