## Problem

CLIP, pre-trained on 400M natural image-text pairs, suffers severe catastrophic forgetting and overfitting when directly fine-tuned on small pathology datasets, often performing worse than its own zero-shot baseline. The large domain gap between natural images and histopathology images, combined with the scarcity of labeled pathology data, makes standard fine-tuning or prompt-tuning approaches (e.g., CoOp, CLIP-Adapter) insufficient for efficient and robust pathology image analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB pathology image patches and a text prompt template (e.g., 'this is a pathological breast photo of []' with the class name inserted) as input. The frozen CLIP vision encoder (ResNet-50) and text encoder (BERT) produce image and text embeddings, which are then refined by the lightweight RFR module. For classification, the output is a K-dimensional probability vector over pathology classes; for the GM/WM segmentation task, the framework is adapted to produce pixel-level segmentation masks. All experiments run on a single GPU (RTX 2080Ti) with batch size 32.

## Method

Path-CLIP freezes both CLIP encoders and trains only a shallow Residual Feature Refinement (RFR) module with a 'down-and-up' fully-connected architecture (1024→256→64→256→1024) that blends fine-tuned features with original CLIP features via a self-adaptive residual ratio α. To prevent overfitting, Hidden Representation Perturbation (HRP) injects Gaussian noise into the visual features before the RFR layers, and Dual-view Vision Contrastive (DVC) measures representation consistency across weak and strong augmentations to dynamically compute α = min(max(τ, T), 1−τ), where τ is the DVC cosine similarity and T is training accuracy. The training objective is the Doublet Multimodal Contrastive Loss (DMCL), which combines CLIP's symmetric image-text InfoNCE loss with a novel image-image contrastive loss (SWAPP) that pairs weakly and strongly augmented views of the same image as positives. This design requires only ~10 minutes of fine-tuning on a single GPU to achieve over 19% accuracy improvement over zero-shot CLIP on PCam with just 0.1% labeled data.

## Results

On PCam with only 0.5% labeled data, Path-CLIP achieves 81.9% accuracy and 0.89 AUC, surpassing zero-shot CLIP (56.5%) by +25.4% and the best CLIP-adapter baseline TIP-Adapter (73.9%) by +8.0%. With 1.5% data, Path-CLIP reaches 83.1% accuracy, still ahead of TIP-Adapter (75.2%) and FlexMatch (84.1% is the closest semi-supervised competitor). On MHIST with 10% labeled data, Path-CLIP attains 74.8% accuracy, outperforming all CLIP-based fine-tuning methods (CoOp 68.9%, CLIP-Adapter 65.9%, TIP-Adapter 63.1%). For GM/WM segmentation with 0.1% data, Path-CLIP achieves 78.05 GM-IoU and 54.50 WM-IoU, substantially exceeding U-Net trained from scratch (46.13 GM-IoU, 5.75 WM-IoU) and TIP-Adapter (69.92 GM-IoU, 46.23 WM-IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Path-CLIP consistently outperforms all three CLIP fine-tuning baselines across every dataset and data regime tested. On PCam (0.5% data), it beats TIP-Adapter by +8.0% accuracy, CLIP-Adapter by +9.6%, and CoOp by +18.3%; on MHIST (10% data), the margins are +11.7% over CoOp, +8.9% over CLIP-Adapter, and +11.7% over TIP-Adapter. Against semi-supervised methods that exploit the full unlabeled training set, Path-CLIP (which uses no unlabeled data) still surpasses FixMatch (+8.7%) and FlexMatch (+7.9%) on PCam at 0.5% labeled data, and edges out FlexMatch (+1.7%) on MHIST at 10%. For GM/WM segmentation at 0.1% data, Path-CLIP improves GM-IoU by +8.1 over TIP-Adapter and +31.9 over U-Net trained from scratch, while WM-IoU gains are even larger (+8.3 over TIP-Adapter, +48.8 over U-Net). The only setting where a baseline is competitive is PCam at 1.5% data, where FlexMatch (84.1%) slightly exceeds Path-CLIP (83.1%) by 1.0%, though Path-CLIP does not require the large unlabeled pool that semi-supervised methods depend on.

## Relation to Prior Work

Path-CLIP extends the CLIP adaptation lineage initiated by CoOp (prompt tuning), CLIP-Adapter, and TIP-Adapter (additive feature modules), but departs from them in two key ways: it focuses on visual feature refinement rather than prompt optimization, and it replaces the fixed blending hyperparameter used by CLIP-Adapter/TIP-Adapter with a self-adaptive residual ratio driven by dual-view contrastive consistency and training accuracy. The Hidden Representation Perturbation module borrows the noise-injection strategy from NLP fine-tuning (NoisyTune, HYPE), and the SWAPP contrastive loss adapts SimCLR-style image-image contrastive learning with a structured weak/strong augmentation pairing rather than random augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that zero-shot CLIP outperforms fine-tuned CLIP on pathology data (56.5% vs. 35.9–45.6%) highlights a practical pitfall for practitioners who assume fine-tuning always helps, and motivates the residual-preservation design. The 10-minute, single-GPU fine-tuning with 0.1% labeled data is practically attractive for digital pathology workflows where annotation is expensive. However, the evaluation is limited to two classification benchmarks and one segmentation task, the self-adaptive ratio mechanism (Eq. 7) is somewhat heuristic, and it remains open whether the approach generalizes to other foundation models (e.g., ViT-based CLIP, pathology-specific PLIP) or to more complex multi-task pathology pipelines.
