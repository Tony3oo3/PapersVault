## Problem

Single-image HDR reconstruction requires recovering both lost luminance details and desaturated colors in clipped regions, but existing methods formulate this in standard color spaces (RGB, Luv), forcing networks to simultaneously model the complex entangled relationship between luminance, surface reflectance, and geometry. This unified formulation limits the ability of data-driven approaches to generate accurate high-resolution reconstructions, particularly in complex in-the-wild scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240279/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single 8-bit LDR photograph as input, which is first dequantized and linearized using pre-trained networks from Liu et al. before intrinsic decomposition. The three internal networks consume concatenated multi-channel tensors (h×w×4 for shading, h×w×7 for albedo, h×w×10 for refinement) and produce outputs in bounded inverse representations (D = 1/(S+1), J = 1/(I+1)) to handle the long-tailed HDR distribution. The final output is a full HDR image in linear radiance space, evaluated against ground truth after scale alignment and per-channel CRF correction.

## Method

The core contribution is a physically-motivated three-stage pipeline that decomposes HDR reconstruction into the intrinsic domain: (1) an HDR shading reconstruction network extends the dynamic range by predicting HDR inverse shading from LDR image + LDR inverse shading, exploiting the strong correlation between shading and scene geometry; (2) an HDR albedo reconstruction network recovers lost color in saturated regions using a soft attention mask to guide inpainting-like color recovery, leveraging the sparsity of reflectance values; (3) a refinement network combines the separately estimated intrinsic components into the final HDR image. All three networks share an EfficientNet backbone with a decoder and sigmoid output, trained with RAdam and supervised by MSE plus multi-scale gradient losses applied to both the primary output and the implied complementary intrinsic component (e.g., the shading network is also penalized on the implied albedo computed via the intrinsic model), promoting consistency within the physical image formation equation.

## Results

The method achieves state-of-the-art HDR-VDP3 quality scores on the high-resolution SI-HDR benchmark (8.96 JOD), outperforming the closest competitor Single-HDR [24] (8.79) by 0.17 JOD. It ties for best on HDR-Eye (8.96, matching Single-HDR) and HDR-Real (7.57, matching DrTMO), while remaining competitive on HDR-Synth (8.21 vs. 8.51 for Single-HDR) and RAISE (8.89 vs. 8.95 for Single-HDR). Across all five datasets, the method is either best or within 0.3 JOD of the best score, with the largest margin over prior work appearing on the challenging SI-HDR benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against eight baselines spanning direct LDR-to-HDR conversion (Single-HDR, DrTMO, HDR-CNN), masked reconstruction (Mask-HDR), denoising-based approaches (HDRUNet), multi-exposure generation (Multi-Exp Gen.), lightweight models (Lightweight), and color-focused expansion (ExpandNet). The proposed method achieves the best score on the high-resolution SI-HDR benchmark (8.96 vs. 8.82 for HDRUNet, the next best), and ties for best on HDR-Eye (8.96, matching Single-HDR) and HDR-Real (7.57, matching DrTMO). On HDR-Synth and RAISE, Single-HDR [24] retains a narrow lead (8.51 vs. 8.21 and 8.95 vs. 8.89 respectively), suggesting the intrinsic formulation's advantage is most pronounced on high-resolution, in-the-wild content. The largest absolute gains over all baselines appear on HDR-Real, where the method scores 7.57 versus 5.71 for HDRUNet and 6.70 for HDR-CNN, indicating the intrinsic decomposition is particularly effective at recovering color in real-world saturated regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the modular inverse pipeline of Liu et al. [24], which decomposes single-image HDR reconstruction into dequantization, linearization, and saturation reconstruction stages, and on the intrinsic image decomposition method of Careaga and Aksoy [3]. The key departure is reformulating the final 'hallucination' (dynamic range extension) stage in the intrinsic domain: rather than mapping LDR RGB directly to HDR RGB as prior data-driven methods (Eilertsen, Endo, Santos, Chen) do, the authors split the task into a shading reconstruction network (extending luminance range, exploiting geometry correlation) and an albedo reconstruction network (recovering lost color, exploiting reflectance sparsity). This positions the work at the intersection of intrinsic imaging and HDR reconstruction, drawing on the physical image formation model I = A·S to motivate the decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The intrinsic formulation provides a physically grounded rationale for why separating luminance range extension from color recovery improves reconstruction quality, and the results confirm this with SOTA performance on the challenging SI-HDR benchmark. A practical limitation is the dependence on an off-the-shelf intrinsic decomposition step, whose errors propagate into both reconstruction networks and necessitate the refinement stage. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to non-Lambertian materials (specular highlights, subsurface scattering) where the single-channel shading model breaks down, and whether the pipeline can be extended to video or to scenes with moving objects where intrinsic decomposition is less reliable.
