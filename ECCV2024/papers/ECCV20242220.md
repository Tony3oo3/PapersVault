## Problem

Industrial anomaly detection is hampered by extreme scarcity of real anomaly samples, and existing synthetic anomaly methods (DRAEM's texture blending, CutPaste, noise perturbation) produce anomalies with a large semantic gap from real-world defects, limiting segmentation performance. The paper asks whether a pre-trained diffusion model can be guided by only 1–3 real anomaly images to generate realistic, diverse, and spatially controllable anomalies that are semantically consistent with actual defect concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242220/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–3 real anomaly images with pixel-level ground-truth masks (support set), a normal training image, and a user-specified bounding box mask as input, and produces a generated anomalous image with the anomaly confined to the box region. The bounding box serves a dual role: it controls the spatial location and size of the generated anomaly during the denoising process, and it provides the only supervision signal (weak labels) for training the downstream discriminative detector. The final detector outputs an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level segmentation map, evaluated on MVTec's 10 objects and 5 textures.

## Method

AnoGen operates in three stages. In Stage 1, a 768-dimensional embedding (initialized from the CLIP text encoder applied to the word 'defect') is optimized against a frozen pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model using a mask-weighted noise-prediction loss (Eq. 6), so the embedding captures the local anomaly distribution rather than the whole object. In Stage 2, the learned embedding is injected via cross-attention into the frozen LDM, and an inpainting-style denoising loop (Eq. 7) retains generated content inside the bounding box while replacing the exterior with the noisy normal image at every step, yielding spatially controlled anomalies. In Stage 3, DRAEM or DeSTSeg is trained with a weakly-supervised segmentation loss (Eq. 9) that zeros out the Focal Loss for high-confidence normal pixels (predicted normal probability ≥ τ = 0.9) inside the box, preventing the model from learning false positives on non-anomalous pixels within the bounding box.

## Results

On MVTec, AnoGen-enhanced DRAEM achieves 73.2% pixel-level AU-PR (a +5.8% improvement over original DRAEM's 67.4%) and 98.7% image-level AU-ROC. AnoGen-enhanced DeSTSeg reaches 78.1% pixel-level AU-PR (+1.5% over DeSTSeg's 76.6%) and 98.8% image-level AU-ROC. Both enhanced models substantially outperform all unsupervised baselines on the pixel-level AU-PR metric, with the largest gap being over 16 points versus CS-Flow (59.6%) and PaDiM (48.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnoGen-enhanced DRAEM (73.2% pixel AU-PR) outperforms all four unsupervised baselines by a wide margin: CS-Flow (59.6%), PaDiM (48.5%), PatchCore (61.7%), and RD4AD (55.4%), with the largest gap of +16.5 points over CS-Flow. AnoGen-enhanced DeSTSeg (78.1% pixel AU-PR) extends this lead, surpassing PatchCore by +16.4 points and PaDiM by +29.6 points. Against their own base models, AnoGen delivers the most substantial gain on DRAEM (+5.8% pixel AU-PR) compared to DeSTSeg (+1.5%), suggesting DRAEM benefits more from semantically realistic anomalies. On image-level classification, improvements are more modest (DRAEM +1.6% AU-ROC, DeSTSeg +0.5%), indicating the primary value of generated anomalies lies in pixel-level segmentation rather than image-level detection. Both AnoGen-enhanced models also lead on pixel-level AU-ROC (98.1% and 98.8%), confirming consistent gains across segmentation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of few-shot embedding learning (textual inversion / DreamBooth-style optimization of a small condition vector) and conditional diffusion inpainting, repurposing both for the specific goal of generating industrial anomaly images. It departs from prior synthetic-anomaly methods (CutPaste, DRAEM's DTD blending, SimpleNet's feature noise) by grounding generation in a few real anomaly examples rather than external textures or random perturbations, and from concurrent AnomalyDiffusion by avoiding a separate mask-generation network and instead using a 768-parameter embedding with a mask-guided loss that focuses on local anomaly regions rather than whole objects. The weakly-supervised segmentation loss (thresholding high-confidence normal pixels within bounding boxes) is a practical addition not present in prior discriminative anomaly detectors, which assume pixel-accurate masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a handful of real anomaly images (as few as 3) can meaningfully close the semantic gap between synthetic and real anomalies, yielding a 5.8% pixel AU-PR gain for DRAEM on MVTec — a practically significant improvement in a domain where collecting anomaly samples is expensive. The main limitation is that generated anomalies carry only bounding-box annotations, forcing the use of a weakly-supervised training procedure with an additional threshold hyperparameter; obtaining pixel-accurate annotations during generation remains an open question. The finding that combining DRAEM's original DTD-based anomalies with the generated ones outperforms either alone suggests the two sources of diversity are complementary, pointing toward hybrid data-augmentation pipelines as a promising direction.
