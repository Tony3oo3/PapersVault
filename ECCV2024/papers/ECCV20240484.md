## Problem

Existing image-to-video and text-to-video methods use text only to control spatial content while exerting limited influence over temporal motion. The paper identifies two root causes: text inherently lacks speed/magnitude information (making the same prompt map to many possible motions), and when content descriptions in the text conflict with the reference image, the model suppresses the entire text signal, including motion descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240484/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a real reference image (encoded to a 4-channel VAE latent), a free-form text prompt (encoded via CLIP), and a discrete motion intensity level from 1 to 10 (default 5) as a 1-channel embedding. These are concatenated with a 1-channel frame embedding to form a 10-channel tensor fed into the UNet. The output is a 16-frame video at 256×256, optionally upscaled to 1024×1024 via a 4× spatial upscaler. During inference, a noise inversion of the reference latent replaces pure Gaussian noise at the initial denoising step to inject content priors.

## Method

LivePhoto builds on frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 with AnimateDiff-style trainable motion modules for inter-frame modeling. Image content guidance is achieved through three complementary mechanisms: pixel-level reference latent concatenation at the UNet input, global identity tokens from a frozen DINOv2 encoder injected via cross-attention, and prior inversion of the reference latent into the initial noise. To resolve the text-motion ambiguity, a motion intensity coefficient (derived from average SSIM between adjacent frames, binned into 10 levels) is concatenated as an additional conditioning channel, decoupling motion magnitude from textual semantics. A prompt adapter—three trainable transformer layers plus a linear projection applied to CLIP text embeddings—learns per-token re-weighting (via sigmoid-normalized weights) that suppresses content descriptions and accentuates motion-related tokens, enabling the text to guide motion without conflicting with the image-provided content.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, LivePhoto achieves an FVD of 289, substantially outperforming AnimateDiff-I2V (687) and VideoComposer (356). It also leads in ID-preservation (80.2 vs. 65.4 and 73.2) and frame consistency (93.1 vs. 81.6 and 90.5), while maintaining a comparable motion intensity (26.7) to VideoComposer (25.8). In the user study, LivePhoto scores 3.9/5 on text consistency and 3.5/5 on motion quality, surpassing all commercial baselines (GEN-2, Pikalabs) and VideoComposer on these two axes, though trailing GEN-2 (4.6) and Pikalabs (4.3) on content quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LivePhoto wins decisively over AnimateDiff-I2V on every metric: FVD is 2.4× lower (289 vs. 687), ID-preservation is 14.8 points higher (80.2 vs. 65.4), and frame consistency is 11.5 points higher (93.1 vs. 81.6), though AnimateDiff-I2V shows a much higher raw motion intensity (55.4 vs. 26.7) that the authors attribute to instability and blur. Against VideoComposer, LivePhoto improves FVD by 19% (289 vs. 356), ID-preservation by 7 points, and frame consistency by 2.6 points, while matching motion intensity (26.7 vs. 25.8). In the user study, LivePhoto (full model) beats VideoComposer on all four subjective scores (text consistency 3.9 vs. 2.9, motion quality 3.5 vs. 3.1, image consistency 3.3 vs. 2.5, content quality 3.8 vs. 3.1). Compared to the commercial products GEN-2 and Pikalabs, LivePhoto achieves significantly better text consistency (3.9 vs. 2.4/2.5) and motion quality (3.5 vs. 3.0/2.9), but concedes ground on image consistency (3.3 vs. 3.6/3.8) and content quality (3.8 vs. 4.6/4.3), which the authors attribute to GEN-2 and Pikalabs using larger models and higher-resolution training data. Concurrent open-source projects (I2VGEN-XL, PIA, DynamiCrafter) are shown qualitatively to struggle with ID-preservation, while AnimateDiff-I2V preserves identity but produces nearly static videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

LivePhoto builds directly on the AnimateDiff paradigm of freezing Stable Diffusion and inserting learnable motion modules for temporal modeling, and on content-guidance techniques from AnyDoor (DINOv2 patch tokens via cross-attention) and noise inversion from Text2Video-Zero. It departs from prior image-to-video systems (VideoComposer, GEN-2, Pikalabs, AnimateDiff-I2V) by identifying that text in those pipelines controls spatial content but not temporal motion, and addresses this gap with two novel components: a motion intensity parameter (SSIM-based) as a supplementary conditioning signal, and a prompt adapter that re-weights CLIP text embeddings to suppress content descriptions and accentuate motion descriptions. The overall architecture is a composition of existing building blocks (frozen SD, motion modules, cross-attention injection) arranged in a new pipeline specifically designed to decouple text's role in content vs. motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LivePhoto is among the first practical systems where text genuinely steers the motion (actions, camera moves, conjured effects) of an animated real image rather than merely describing its content, which is a meaningful step toward usable image-animation tools for creative applications. The motion intensity slider offers a simple, interpretable control that users can exploit without retraining. However, the system is constrained to SD-1.5, 256×256 resolution, 16 frames, and the WebVID dataset, so scaling to SD-XL, longer/higher-resolution videos, and richer training data remains an open question. The prompt adapter's success with only three transformer layers over frozen CLIP embeddings suggests that lightweight token re-weighting could be a broadly applicable trick for separating semantic roles in text conditioning, but its generalizability beyond this specific image-to-video setting is untested.
