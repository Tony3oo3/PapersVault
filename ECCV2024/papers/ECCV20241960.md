## Problem

Zero-shot OOD detection with CLIP requires only ID class labels, but existing approaches either demand a manually or automatically designed OOD label set that may not reflect real OOD data, or require fine-tuning CLIP per ID dataset. The paper seeks a simple, outlier-free augmentation strategy that amplifies the ID/OOD separation without external LLM knowledge, prompt ensembling, or additional training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image and the list of class-name strings for the ID dataset (e.g., 100 names for CIFAR-100, 1000 for ImageNet-1k); no actual training images or OOD samples are needed. For each image, M randomly shuffled text prompts per class are tokenized and encoded, and the final output is a scalar OOD score (averaged over M augmentations) that can be thresholded to yield an in-distribution or out-of-distribution decision. The temperature τ and augmentation count M are the only hyperparameters.

## Method

TAG exploits the observation that CLIP's text encoder is largely invariant to word order: shuffling the words in the default prompt 'a photo of a <class_name>' still yields correct classifications, and the non-uniform cosine-similarity distribution across classes is preserved under shuffling. Concretely, M augmented prompt sets are generated by randomly permuting the four template words (keeping multi-word class names intact), and for each set the standard OOD score (MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, or GEN) is computed from the cosine similarities between the image embedding and the M×K text embeddings. The final OOD score is the arithmetic mean of the per-augmentation scores. This averaging amplifies the separation between ID and OOD samples because ID images consistently align with their class prototype across shuffles, while OOD images do not, making the mean score more discriminative than any single prompt.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (ID) with 5 OOD datasets, TAG consistently improves all five baseline score functions across four CLIP architectures, yielding average AUROC gains of 6.35% and FPR95 reductions of 10.67%. The largest single improvement is MaxLogit on CIFAR-100 (average AUROC 69.67→82.07, FPR95 81.37→68.58 across ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ResNet-50). On ImageNet-1k (ID) with 4 OOD datasets, improvements are more modest but still consistent for MSP, MaxLogit, and Energy (e.g., MSP average AUROC 80.62→82.75, FPR95 63.92→62.57), while GEN and MCM show slight degradation in FPR95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAG is applied on top of five OOD score functions (MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, GEN, MCM) and two prompt strategies (DCLIP, WaffleCLIP). On CIFAR-100, TAG wins across the board: MaxLogit* achieves the best average AUROC (82.07 vs. 69.67 baseline, +12.40), and GEN* achieves the best average FPR95 (64.80 vs. 77.80 baseline, −13.00). On ImageNet-1k, TAG improves MSP (+2.13 AUROC, −1.35 FPR95), MaxLogit (+1.63 AUROC, −2.23 FPR95), and Energy (+1.10 AUROC, −0.88 FPR95), but loses to the un-augmented GEN (85.98 vs. 87.12 AUROC) and MCM (86.52 vs. 86.86 AUROC), with FPR95 degrading by ~5–6 points for both. When combined with external prompt strategies, TAG still improves DCLIP (+4.02 AUROC, −10.25 FPR95) and WaffleCLIP (+5.02 AUROC, −7.73 FPR95) on ImageNet-1k, confirming its compatibility with richer text prompts. The authors attribute the weaker ImageNet-1k gains to the low rank of CLIP's text embeddings for 1000 classes (rank < 710) and the limited informativeness of the default 'a photo of a ⟨y_k⟩' prompt.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAG positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: (1) OOD score functions originally designed for discriminative classifiers (MSP, MaxLogit, Energy, GEN) and (2) CLIP-based zero-shot OOD detection pioneered by MCM. It departs from prior CLIP-based OOD methods (Fort et al., Esmaeilpourcharandabi et al., CLIPN) that either require manually designed OOD label sets, fine-tuning CLIP per ID dataset, or external LLM-generated descriptors. The core conceptual departure is the observation that grammatically incorrect shufflings of the default text prompt (e.g., '<y_k> of a a photo') preserve classification accuracy while altering the cosine-similarity landscape in a way that amplifies ID/OOD separation — a property the authors exploit as a free, training-free augmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAG offers a drop-in, training-free, outlier-free enhancement that consistently improves AUROC and FPR95 across five CLIP architectures and five score functions, making it practically attractive for deployment scenarios where ID training data is unavailable and OOD labels are unknown. The less pronounced gains on ImageNet-1k (vs. CIFAR-100) point to an open question about whether the simple 'a photo of <y_k>' prompt exhausts CLIP's text-embedding space, and whether richer prompts or learned prompt ensembles could close this gap. The mechanism — why word-order shuffling helps — remains only partially explained (linked to non-uniform cosine similarity and text-embedding rank), leaving room for deeper theoretical understanding.
