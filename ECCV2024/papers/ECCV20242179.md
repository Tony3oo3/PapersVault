## Problem

Data-driven clothing deformation methods struggle to generalize to unseen body poses because they rely on global representations (normal maps, vertex positions) that are inherently coupled to the underlying body pose, and high-fidelity 3D capture data is expensive and limited in diversity. This paper addresses the gap by identifying a local, rotation-invariant conditioning signal that decouples wrinkle generation from body pose, enabling generalization from limited real captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242179/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes SMPL body pose and shape parameters as input, which drive a CVAE to produce virtual skeleton transformations (64 joints × 6 DoF) for coarse LBS deformation of a clothing mesh. The wrinkle diffusion model then consumes a 256×256 UV image combining the Green-Lagrange strain field (1 channel, Frobenius norm) with the noisy wrinkle displacement field (3 channels in the base mesh local frame). The output is a 3D wrinkled clothing mesh, where the high-frequency wrinkle is a per-vertex displacement in the base surface's local tangent/normal frame, stored as a 256×256×3 UV image.

## Method

The core contribution is a strain-conditioned denoising diffusion model (U-Net, T=1000 training steps, DDIM S=50 inference) that generates high-frequency wrinkle displacement fields in UV space, conditioned on the Frobenius norm of the Green-Lagrange strain tensor computed on the coarsely deformed base mesh. The key insight is that strain is a local, rotation-invariant measure of stretching relative to the rest state, making it independent of body pose and clothing topology—unlike normal maps or global vertex positions. The coarse deformation component is a CVAE trained on synthetic AMASS simulation data to map SMPL parameters to virtual skeleton poses, decoupling the data-hungry coarse component from the quality-critical wrinkle component trained on ~3K real captures. Wrinkles are represented as 3D displacement in the base mesh's local coordinate frame (tangent + normal), and the diffusion model learns the conditional distribution p(wrinkle | strain) from paired base/wrinkled mesh data obtained via curved Hessian smoothing.

## Results

On the in-distribution test set, the strain-conditioned diffusion model achieves the lowest L2 vertex distance (E_v) and Chamfer distance (E_cd) on both garments: 0.347/0.205 (×10⁻² m / ×10⁻⁴ m²) for the T-shirt and 0.526/0.666 for the skirt, outperforming the closest baseline LFGD (0.586/0.391 and 0.941/1.225). On the out-of-distribution test set, the full pipeline achieves FID scores of 75.05 (T-shirt) and 45.09 (skirt) on UV-space normal maps, substantially below the best physics-inspired baselines HOOD (163.22/79.79) and NCS (142.87/90.50). Ablation confirms that using the Frobenius norm of Green-Lagrange strain as conditioning outperforms both surface-normal conditioning (FID 129.19/60.24) and full strain-tensor conditioning (134.52/116.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the wrinkle-generation baselines (DeepWrinkles, DDE, LFGD), the strain-conditioned diffusion model reduces vertex distance by 41–77% and Chamfer distance by 48–94% on the in-distribution test set; DDE in particular fails to produce meaningful wrinkle patterns from a smooth base mesh. On the out-of-distribution test set (FID on UV normal maps), the full pipeline (FID 75.05 T-shirt, 45.09 skirt) beats the strongest physics-inspired body-dependent methods HOOD (163.22/79.79), NCS (142.87/90.50), and PBNS (143.58/129.87) by 2.2–4× in FID, and also surpasses the wrinkle-only baselines DeepWrinkles (231.40/92.46), DDE (218.29/126.77), and LFGD (305.89/104.63) when applied to the same base mesh. The ablation further shows that the choice of Green-Lagrange strain norm over surface normals or the full strain tensor is critical: the norm variant achieves FID 75.05/45.09 versus 129.19/60.24 (normal) and 134.52/116.05 (strain tensor), confirming that the rotation-invariant scalar stretching measure provides the best generalization with limited training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the denoising diffusion framework (DDPM/DDIM) and CVAE architectures, but its key conceptual departure is conditioning wrinkle generation on the Frobenius norm of Green-Lagrange strain rather than on surface normals (DeepWrinkles, DDE) or global vertex positions (LFGD). This choice draws from physics-based cloth simulation literature where strain has been studied as a local deformation measure, but repurposes it as a data-driven conditioning signal that is rotation-invariant and independent of body pose. The coarse-to-fine decomposition (base surface + wrinkles) echoes physics-based methods like Wrinkle Meshes and Chen et al., but here the fine component is learned generatively from real captures rather than simulated, and the coarse component is learned from synthetic data via a CVAE over a virtual skeleton.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a local, rotation-invariant scalar (strain magnitude) can serve as a sufficient conditioning signal for high-fidelity wrinkle generation, decoupling the wrinkle model from body pose and enabling generalization to unseen poses with only ~3K real frames. This is practically important for virtual avatar pipelines where real capture data is expensive and limited. Key limitations remain: the method models only static deformation (no dynamics), does not enforce physical constraints like collision, and inherits LBS artifacts in the coarse component. Open questions include whether the strain-conditioned formulation extends to dynamic wrinkles, whether physics heuristics can be integrated into the diffusion process, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two clothing items evaluated.
