## Problem

Quadruped robot learning has traditionally treated visual perception (QUAR-VA) and language instruction (QUAR-LA) as separate task specifications, preventing the robot from autonomously combining fine-grained verbal commands with real-time visual observations. Additionally, no large-scale multi-task dataset existed for quadruped robots that jointly captures vision, language, and robot command data, and the action space for agile quadruped locomotion is harder to define than for fixed-base manipulators or 2-D mobile bases.

## Input / Output

QUART takes a single RGB image from the robot's front-mounted first-person camera and a free-form natural language instruction as input. The output is a 12-dimensional action vector — 11 continuous high-level commands (base velocities v_x, v_y, ω_z; gait pattern θ_1–θ_3; gait frequency f; body height h_z; pitch φ; foot width s_y; foot height h_z^f) plus a binary termination signal — all discretized into 256 uniform bins for tokenization. These commands are issued at 2 Hz to a pre-trained low-level command-tracking controller that resolves them into joint-level motor actions, deliberately avoiding the high-frequency demands of direct motor control.

## Method

QUART is a decoder-only transformer built on the 8B-parameter Fuyu-8B vision-language model, fine-tuned via symbol tuning so that the model's existing integer tokens (0–999) are repurposed to represent the 256 discrete action bins, eliminating the need for new embedding vectors. Training uses standard next-token prediction (equivalent to behavior cloning) with categorical cross-entropy and causal masking over the action token sequence, at learning rate 2e-5 and batch size 256 for 100K steps. The model is trained on the newly introduced QUARD dataset (249K episodes: ~246K from Isaac Gym simulation and ~3K from real WR-2 robot teleoperation) spanning seven tasks across perception, navigation, and whole-body manipulation. A co-training pipeline jointly optimizes on simulation and real data to mitigate the sim-to-real gap in visual appearance, sensor noise, and system dynamics. At inference, the model generates the full 12-token action sequence in a single forward pass, which is then detokenized back to continuous command values for deployment.

## Results

On the QUARD multi-task benchmark (249K episodes across 7 tasks), QUART achieves the highest success rate on all six seen tasks, ranging from 0.66 on the easy Distinguish task to 0.12 on the hardest Unload task. QUART is the only method that achieves non-zero success on the two most challenging tasks (Crawl under Bar: 0.32, Unload Object: 0.12), where all three baselines record 0. On generalization, QUART reaches 0.35 on unseen objects and 0.33 on unseen verbal instructions, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline (VC-1 at 0.29 and 0.19 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUART outperforms all three baselines on every evaluated task. Against CLIP, QUART gains +0.22 on Distinguish (0.66 vs 0.44), +0.17 on Go to (0.60 vs 0.43), and +0.22 on Go Through (0.41 vs 0.19), while being the sole method to succeed on Crawl and Unload. Against VC-1, QUART leads by +0.20 on Go Through (0.41 vs 0.31) and +0.14 on unseen verbal instructions (0.33 vs 0.19), again being the only method with non-zero Crawl/Unload scores. R3M, lacking language alignment, fails entirely on all tasks except Distinguish (0.58), where it narrowly trails QUART (0.66) but still cannot execute any navigation or manipulation command. The most striking gap is on unseen verbal instructions, where QUART's 0.33 success rate is nearly double that of VC-1 (0.19) and more than double CLIP (0.14), demonstrating the advantage of the pre-trained MLLM's language generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA paradigm established by RT-1/RT-2 for manipulation to the quadruped locomotion domain, where the action space is fundamentally richer (gait pattern, frequency, body height, pitch, foot width) rather than end-effector trajectories. Architecturally, QUART fine-tunes the Fuyu-8B multimodal LLM using the 256-bin action discretization from RT-1, treating action generation as next-token prediction via symbol tuning. Conceptually, it departs from prior quadruped work that treated vision (QUAR-VA) and language (QUAR-LA) as separate pipelines, unifying them into a single conditional policy that maps (image, instruction) → 12-dim action.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QUARD (249K episodes across 7 tasks) is the first large-scale quadruped dataset pairing vision, language instructions, and robot commands, filling a clear gap that has limited progress in this area. The practical deployment at 2 Hz on a physical WR-2 robot demonstrates feasibility, but the sim-to-real gap remains addressed only through co-training rather than principled domain adaptation, and absolute success rates on hard tasks (12% crawl, 32% tunnel) leave substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the 8B VLM backbone is necessary or whether smaller models suffice, how the approach scales to more complex whole-body manipulation, and whether the 2 Hz control rate is adequate for dynamic terrain.
