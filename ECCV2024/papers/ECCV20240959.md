## Problem

SDEdit-based video enhancement with pre-trained diffusion models often disrupts the spatial structure and content of the original video when the initial denoising step is set large enough for meaningful quality improvement. Existing solutions require expensive retraining of expert refinement models, yet still fail to reliably preserve well-established structures. The paper asks whether content-preserving enhancement can be achieved in a training-free, plug-and-play manner using only a pre-trained T2V model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality reference video (evaluated at 320×512 and 640×1024 resolutions) and a frozen pre-trained video diffusion model (e.g., VideoCrafter 576×1024) as inputs, and produces a single enhanced video clip as output. All operations occur in the latent space of the diffusion model, and the only tunable hyperparameters are the initial denoising step t₀ (default 600), the number of calibration iterations N (default 3), and a threshold frequency ν (default 1.0) that controls the low/high-frequency split. The method adds less than 10% inference time over vanilla SDEdit and requires no gradient backpropagation through the diffusion model.

## Method

The key insight is that the dominant content loss in SDEdit occurs at the initial denoising step t₀, so optimizing only the initial noise ε_{t₀} suffices to preserve structure. The authors formulate a constrained optimization that minimizes the L2 distance between the low-frequency components of the initial estimation x̂₀^{t₀} and the reference video, subject to x̂₀^{t₀} being produced by the frozen diffusion model. By algebraically decomposing x_{t₀} into the model's noise prediction and the input noise, they derive a fixed-point iteration (Eq. 14) that updates ε_{t₀} using the model's predicted noise plus a high-frequency correction term, analogous to ILVR's low-frequency replacement but applied to the noise variable. After 1–3 iterations of this calibration, the refined noise is re-injected into the reference video and standard SDEdit denoising proceeds unchanged. The entire procedure is training-free and plug-and-play, and can be dropped into existing refinement pipelines (MS-Vid2Vid-XL, SDXL-1.0-refiner) without modification.

## Results

On the EvalCrafter-based reference set (700 videos at 640×1024, enhanced via VideoCrafter), NC-SDEdit with N=2 iterations achieves the best overall balance: MSE₁ of 2.80 (vs. 4.34 for SDEdit), SSIM of 0.704 (vs. 0.646), DOVER of 62.71 (vs. 60.17), and D_SF of 0.0554 (vs. 0.0527), while CLIP-IQA drops marginally from 0.4482 to 0.4400. The method requires only 1–3 additional forward passes (less than 10% extra inference time) and is entirely training-free. When plugged into existing refinement models (MS-Vid2Vid-XL, SDXL-1.0-refiner), Noise Calibration improves all reported metrics, e.g., raising DOVER from 52.89 to 57.61 on MS-Vid2Vid-XL and SSIM from 0.7344 to 0.7625 on SDXL-1.0-refiner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDEdit (the direct baseline using random initial noise), NC-SDEdit with N=2 reduces MSE₁ by 35% (2.80 vs. 4.34), improves SSIM by 8.4% (0.704 vs. 0.646), and raises DOVER by 2.5 points (62.71 vs. 60.17), while CLIP-IQA drops only 0.008 (0.440 vs. 0.448) — a negligible trade-off. In the user study, 86.11% of raters preferred NC-SDEdit over SDEdit for content consistency, versus 13.89%. When applied as a plug-in to MS-Vid2Vid-XL, Noise Calibration improves every metric: MSE₁ drops from 3.22 to 2.68, SSIM rises from 0.708 to 0.725, and DOVER jumps from 52.89 to 57.61. Similarly, on SDXL-1.0-refiner, MSE₁ decreases from 1.28 to 0.88, SSIM increases from 0.734 to 0.763, and CLIP-IQA rises from 0.859 to 0.873. The method thus consistently outperforms all three baselines on content-fidelity metrics while maintaining or slightly improving visual quality scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SDEdit (Meng et al., 2021) for the noising-denoising enhancement pipeline and adapts ILVR's (Choi et al., 2021) low-frequency replacement mechanism into a fixed-point iteration on the initial noise. It departs from the dominant cascade video generation paradigm (Show-1, Modelscope, Lavie) where an expert refinement model is retrained on low noise scales; instead, Noise Calibration is training-free and operates entirely on the initial noise of a pretrained T2V model. The paper also positions itself against SR3-based refinement (Lavie) that conditions on the low-resolution input, arguing that SDEdit-based approaches offer stronger generative detail but at the cost of content disruption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a plug-and-play, training-free technique adding less than 10% inference time, Noise Calibration is immediately applicable to any SDEdit-based pipeline (video refinement, image editing, stroke-to-image), making it practically valuable for production video generation systems. The main limitation is that enhancement quality remains bounded by the base model, and excessive iterations cause color oversaturation due to the low-frequency-only consistency objective. Open questions include designing a more balanced consistency loss that avoids color artifacts, and whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or higher noise scales where content drift is more severe.
