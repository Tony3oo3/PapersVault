## Problem

Creating animatable 3D digital humans from monocular videos remains slow with NeRF-based methods (hours of training) or incomplete with mesh-only approaches that lack realistic re-rendering under pose change. Recent Gaussian Splatting methods are faster but produce unstructured Gaussians whose geometry is too inaccurate for faithful animation. The paper identifies that accurate mesh-type surface modelling is essential for animatable digitization via Gaussian splats, and that naive photometric optimization alone cannot recover the surface details needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242010/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of a single subject in front of a fixed camera (known intrinsics and pose) along with per-frame SMPL body pose parameters, and produces a personalized, watertight, fully rigged 3D mesh sharing SMPL topology (165K faces) together with renderable surface-aligned Gaussian splats. The output mesh includes body shape, hair, and clothing geometry with an underlying skeleton, enabling forward linear blend skinning to novel poses. Training completes in approximately 15 seconds on a single RTX 4090, and inference runs at over 100 fps.

## Method

iHuman initializes 3D Gaussians on an upsampled SMPL canonical mesh and binds each Gaussian to a specific triangular face, aligning one covariance axis to the face normal with near-zero scale to produce 2D surface-aligned splats. Surface displacements from the canonical shape to the clothed subject are modeled implicitly via a hash encoder feeding a 3-layer MLP, while appearance is encoded with spherical harmonics also driven by hash-encoded vertex positions. A key novelty is a second spherical harmonics channel that encodes per-Gaussian surface normals, rasterized through the Gaussian splatting pipeline to produce a normal map supervised by pix2pixHD-predicted ground-truth normals, providing geometric detail beyond photometric loss alone. Deformation to posed space uses standard forward linear blend skinning with SMPL weights. Training optimizes a combined L1 + D-SSIM loss on both RGB and normal maps plus a 3D regularization term, achieving convergence in seconds at 20 iterations per second.

## Results

On the Multi-Garment (MGN) dataset, iHuman achieves a mean vertex-to-vertex (v2v) error of ~12.2 mm and normal consistency (NC) of 0.031, dramatically outperforming Anim-NeRF (v2v ~74 mm, NC ~0.34) and GART (v2v ~143 mm, NC ~0.041) for 3D mesh reconstruction. For novel view synthesis on PeopleSnapshot, iHuman achieves the best LPIPS (0.0196–0.0332) across all view counts (6/12/20 views) while reporting second-best PSNR (25.08–27.48), trailing GART by ~2–3 dB in PSNR. Critically, iHuman achieves these results in 6–20 seconds of training time versus 100+ seconds for GART and 5 minutes for Anim-NeRF and Instant-Avatar — an order-of-magnitude speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Anim-NeRF, iHuman wins decisively on 3D mesh reconstruction: v2v error drops from ~74 mm to ~12 mm (a 6× improvement) and normal consistency improves from ~0.34 to ~0.031, while also achieving higher PSNR (~30 vs ~24) and lower LPIPS (~0.018 vs ~0.071) on MGN. Against GART, iHuman achieves substantially better geometry (v2v ~12 mm vs ~143 mm, a 12× improvement) and comparable normal consistency, though GART retains a ~5 dB PSNR advantage on MGN rendering (35–36 vs 29–32) and slightly better LPIPS. On PeopleSnapshot novel view synthesis, iHuman matches or beats GART on LPIPS at all view counts (e.g., 0.0196 vs 0.0327 at 20 views) while GART leads on PSNR by ~2–3 dB; both methods far outperform Anim-NeRF and Instant-Avatar on all rendering metrics. The most striking comparison is training time: iHuman completes in 6–20 seconds versus 100–110 s for GART and 300 s for Anim-NeRF/Instant-Avatar, making it an order of magnitude faster than the closest competitor while delivering superior 3D geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering paradigm (Kerbl et al.) and the SMPL parametric body model, combining them with the surface-alignment idea from SuGaR to produce animatable human representations. It departs from prior Gaussian-based human methods (GART, HUGS, SplatArmor) by explicitly binding each Gaussian to a specific triangular face of the SMPL template and introducing a novel normal-map supervision signal derived from a pre-trained pix2pixHD network, rather than relying solely on photometric loss. The hash-encoded spherical harmonics for both appearance and normals draw on the Instant-NGP encoding strategy, but applied here to per-vertex parameters rather than scene-level radiance fields. Compared to NeRF-based animatable methods (Anim-NeRF, Instant-Avatar), the shift to explicit Gaussian splatting is what enables the order-of-magnitude speedup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15-second training time and simultaneous output of both an accurate 3D mesh and a renderable Gaussian representation make this method practically relevant for scalable digital avatar creation in VR/AR/gaming pipelines. The ablation results underscore a key insight for the field: photometric loss alone on unstructured Gaussians is insufficient for geometric fidelity, and explicit mesh binding plus normal supervision are both necessary for accurate surface reconstruction. Limitations remain significant—accurate input poses are required, the SMPL template cannot model loose clothing deformations, and pose/view-dependent appearance is not handled—leaving open the question of whether per-frame residual deformations or more flexible template models can close these gaps without sacrificing speed.
