## Problem

Existing 3D-aware portrait editing methods face a fundamental trade-off between editing quality and speed: 3D GAN inversion approaches are constrained by limited latent attributes and slow optimization, while diffusion-based editing requires step-by-step inference that is incompatible with real-time applications. The paper asks how to achieve high-fidelity, 3D-consistent portrait editing driven by flexible prompts (images or text) at real-time speed (~40 ms per image).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241365/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 portrait image, its camera pose (estimated via a face pose estimator), and a user-specified prompt that can be either a reference image (encoded by MAE) or a text description (encoded by CLIP). It outputs an edited triplane-based 3D NeRF representation from which photorealistic novel-view renderings can be synthesized. Inference runs in approximately 40 ms on a single consumer GPU, and the system also supports frame-by-frame video editing and fast adaptation to user-defined styles with only 10 image pairs in ~5 minutes.

## Method

3DPE fine-tunes Live3D (a two-branch triplane 3D GAN built on EG3D) by exploiting the natural frequency disentanglement of its encoders: the frozen low-resolution branch E_low preserves coarse geometry, while the high-resolution branch E_high is augmented with a cross-attention layer that injects prompt embeddings from a lightweight prompt encoder E_p (MAE for images, CLIP for text). Editing knowledge is distilled from InstructPix2Pix via a 2D reconstruction loss (L_2D) on pseudo-label pairs, while 3D consistency is enforced by a 3D distillation loss (L_3D) that matches multi-view images, triplane values, and depth maps predicted by the pretrained Live3D on the pseudo-labels. Only E_p and the ViT decoder E_t are trainable during pretraining, keeping the model lightweight and enabling 40 ms feedforward inference. For customized styles, the method further fine-tunes only E_p and the normalization layers of E_t on 10 image pairs in ~5 minutes, achieving rapid adaptation without retraining the full network.

## Results

On 100 FFHQ test images, 3DPE achieves the best 3D consistency score (0.76) and the highest reference alignment (CLIP_r = 0.73) among all methods that report this metric. The headline advantage is inference speed: 3DPE processes each image in 0.04 s on a single A6000 GPU, a 250× speedup over the fastest baseline (IP2P+Live3D at 10 s) and a 9000× speedup over PTI+StyleCLIP (360 s). Identity preservation (ID_t = 0.47) is competitive with IP2P+Live3D (0.44) but lower than Live3D+StyleCLIP (0.63), which the authors attribute to that baseline producing nearly unedited outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PTI+StyleCLIP, 3DPE wins decisively on every comparable metric: ID_t (0.47 vs 0.11), 3D consistency (0.76 vs 0.73), and inference time (0.04 s vs 360 s), while PTI+StyleCLIP produces low-fidelity textures and geometric artifacts. Against Live3D+StyleCLIP, 3DPE achieves better 3D consistency (0.76 vs 0.72) and is 750× faster (0.04 s vs 30 s), but reports a lower ID_t (0.47 vs 0.63); the authors note that Live3D+StyleCLIP outputs are 'basically unchanged' from the input, making its high identity score misleading. Against IP2P+Live3D (the 2D-edit-then-reconstruct pipeline), 3DPE wins on all four metrics—ID_t (0.47 vs 0.44), CLIP_r (0.73 vs 0.62), 3D (0.76 vs 0.75), and time (0.04 s vs 10 s)—and produces textures more consistent with the prompt while better preserving input structure. SPI+StyleGAN-NADA, the third 3D-reconstruction-plus-editing baseline, is the slowest at ~18 minutes per instance and, while producing realistic textures, fails to reconstruct fine details or follow prompts accurately.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Live3D's two-branch triplane-based 3D portrait reconstruction and InstructPix2Pix's text-guided diffusion editing, but departs from both by distilling their respective priors into a single lightweight feedforward module rather than invoking either at inference time. It positions itself against two families of prior work: (1) 3D GAN inversion + latent-space editing methods (PTI+StyleCLIP, Live3D+StyleCLIP, SPI+StyleGAN-NADA) that are constrained by limited latent attributes and slow iterative optimization, and (2) 2D-then-3D pipelines (IP2P+Live3D) that lack 3D consistency guarantees. The key architectural insight is exploiting Live3D's natural low/high-frequency disentanglement in its two encoder branches—freezing the low-frequency branch for geometry preservation while injecting prompt-conditioned high-frequency features via cross-attention—enabling a single model to handle diverse editing prompts in real time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 3D-aware portrait editing at 40 ms per image (250× faster than the next-best baseline) makes interactive 3D editing feasible for AR/VR, telepresence, and video conferencing applications that previously required per-instance optimization. The fast adaptation mechanism (10 image pairs, ~5 minutes) lowers the barrier for users to customize editing styles without retraining the full model. Limitations include flickering artifacts in video editing (the model is frame-by-frame) and detail inconsistencies in novel views inherited from EG3D's super-resolution module. Open questions include whether the distillation framework generalizes beyond faces to full-body or scene-level 3D editing, and whether the 2D pseudo-label strategy can be replaced with true multi-view 3D supervision as such data becomes available.
