## Problem

Existing video-based energy expenditure (EE) estimation datasets suffer from either limited activity intensity in restricted home settings or inaccurate EE labels derived from MET lookup tables that ignore individual differences. No prior dataset provides continuous, authentic calorie readings from a gold-standard indirect calorimeter across diverse, high-intensity sports, leaving the field without a reliable benchmark for evaluating EE estimation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240939/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 300-frame human skeleton sequence (T×J×3: x-coordinate, y-coordinate, and visibility confidence per joint, extracted by AlphaPose from 856×480, 30 fps video clips of 10 seconds each), augmented with per-subject heart rate (Polar H10 chest belt) and physical attributes (gender, age, height, weight). It produces two outputs simultaneously: a discrete action category label (14 classes spanning 6 exercise types at 3 speed levels) and a continuous EE value in kcal/min. Evaluation uses a cross-subject split (22 train / 7 val / 7 test subjects) to test generalization to unseen individuals.

## Method

E3SFormer uses a Dual-stream Spatio-temporal Transformer (DSTformer) backbone, initialized from MotionBERT pretrained weights, to extract a motion representation F ∈ R^{T×J×C} from the skeleton sequence. Two parallel Transformer branches then operate on F: a spatial branch (Self-Attention Pooling + Spatial Transformer) for action recognition, and a temporal branch (Temporal Transformer + SAP) for EE regression. The key architectural insight is transferring the CLS token's multi-head attention over joints from the action recognition branch as a category-related joint-specific attention vector A_c ∈ R^J, which weights the joint-dimension aggregation in the regression branch—encoding the intuition that different joints contribute differently to EE depending on the action (e.g., legs dominate for running). Multi-modal data (HR, physical attributes) is encoded by a 3-layer MLP and concatenated with both branch outputs before a final Transformer layer. Training uses a combined loss L = L_r + α·L_c (L1 regression + cross-entropy) with the Lion optimizer, cosine annealing, and 50 epochs.

## Results

On the E3V-K5 test set with multi-modal input (skeleton + heart rate + physical attributes), E3SFormer achieves 18.01% MRE, 1.2490 MAE, 0.9157 PCC, and 0.7953 R², ranking first on all four metrics. Without multi-modal data (skeleton only), E3SFormer still leads on MRE (28.81%), MAE (2.0304), and PCC (0.7528), though PoseConv3D edges it out on R² (0.5175 vs. 0.5118). The multi-modal augmentation yields substantial gains across all methods, with E3SFormer's MRE dropping from 28.81% to 18.01%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E3SFormer outperforms all seven baselines on MRE, MAE, and PCC in both the skeleton-only and multi-modal settings. In the skeleton-only setting, the closest competitor is PoseConv3D (MRE 33.03%, R² 0.5175), which narrowly beats E3SFormer on R² (0.5118) but trails on MRE by 4.22 percentage points and on PCC by 0.0296. With multi-modal input, E3SFormer achieves a clear margin over the next-best method CTR-GCN (MRE 18.01% vs. 20.11%, MAE 1.2490 vs. 1.3016, PCC 0.9157 vs. 0.8967, R² 0.7953 vs. 0.7909). Among GCN-based baselines, DG-STGCN and CTR-GCN are the strongest, while MS-G3D performs worst (MRE 47.67% skeleton-only). RGBConv3D, the video-based CNN variant, consistently underperforms all skeleton-based methods, confirming that raw RGB input introduces distracting background noise that degrades EE estimation. The ablation further shows that removing the joint-specific attention transfer from the action recognition branch degrades MRE from 18.01% to 24.88% (w/ MM), validating the cross-branch attention mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-based energy expenditure estimation (E3V) line of work initiated by SPHERE-calorie, Stanford-ECM, and Vid2Burn, but departs fundamentally from their label construction: instead of MET lookup tables or older calorimeters, E3V-K5 uses continuous readings from the COSMED K5 indirect calorimeter (the field's gold standard) and introduces a cross-subject train/test split to probe generalization. Architecturally, E3SFormer builds on the MotionBERT pretraining and DSTformer spatio-temporal backbone, but introduces a novel cross-branch mechanism where category-related joint-specific attention from an action recognition head is transferred to an EE regression head, a design not present in prior skeleton-based action recognition or EE estimation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E3V-K5 is the first large-scale (16,526 clips, 36 subjects, 14 classes) video-EE benchmark with authentic calorimetric ground truth, heart rate, and physical attributes, and its cross-subject split directly addresses the generalization gap that prior datasets ignored. The ablation showing that the ACSM formula yields R² = −0.15 while HR+attributes alone reach R² = 0.73 underscores that motion information is essential and that no closed-form physiological equation suffices. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the six aerobic gym activities covered, whether the joint-specific attention transfer scales to more diverse or non-aerobic movements, and whether the strong dependence on multi-modal inputs (HR, body composition) limits deployment in settings where such signals are unavailable.
