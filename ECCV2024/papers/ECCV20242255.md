## Problem

Voxel-based diffusion transformers for 3D point cloud generation suffer from prohibitively high training costs due to the cubic complexity of self-attention over the volumetric token space (e.g., 128×128×128 voxels require 1,668 A100 GPU hours). Despite the high inherent redundancy in 3D voxels—where only ~2.34% of voxels are occupied—prior methods like DiT-3D process all patchified tokens uniformly, leaving a large efficiency gap unexploited.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample x_T ∈ R^{N×3} and a class label as input, and outputs a 3D point cloud of 2,048 points (x,y,z coordinates). During training, point clouds are voxelized into V×V×V×3 tensors (V=32 or 128) and patchified with patch size p=4 into L=(V/p)³ tokens; the encoder processes only the ~1% unmasked tokens while the decoder reconstructs all L tokens before devoxelizing back to point cloud space. The output noise prediction ε_θ(x_t, t) is in the original N×3 point cloud coordinate space.

## Method

FastDiT-3D introduces a foreground-background aware masking strategy that masks 99% of non-occupied (background) voxels and 95% of occupied (foreground) voxels, reducing the encoder input from 32,768 tokens to just 327 for 128³ resolution. The architecture uses an encoder with 3D global self-attention over unmasked tokens (reducing complexity from O(L²) to O(L_u²)) and a decoder with 3D window attention over all tokens to predict noise. Training employs a dual objective: a standard denoising loss on unmasked patches plus a masked point cloud prediction loss (weighted by λ=0.1) on masked patches to capture global shape structure. Additionally, Mixture-of-Experts FFN layers replace standard FFNs in encoder blocks, allowing each category to learn a distinct diffusion path through different experts across layers, alleviating gradient conflicts in multi-category training.

## Results

On ShapeNet (Chair, Airplane, Car), FastDiT-3D-S achieves state-of-the-art 1-NNA and COV across nearly all categories, outperforming the larger DiT-3D-XL model. For example, on Chair it reaches 58.53% COV@CD and 60.79% COV@EMD (vs. 52.45% and 54.32% for DiT-3D-XL), and on Car it attains 47.81% 1-NNA@CD and 53.86% COV@CD. Crucially, these gains are achieved at only 6.5% of the original DiT-3D training cost (8 GPU-hours vs. ~123 hours for 128³ voxels), thanks to the extreme 99% foreground-background-aware masking ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastDiT-3D-S (a small model) outperforms the much larger DiT-3D-XL on 11 out of 12 metrics across the three categories, with the sole exception being Chair 1-NNA@CD (50.35 vs. 49.11, a 1.24-point gap). The most substantial gains are in COV: +6.08 on Chair COV@CD, +5.05 on Airplane COV@CD, and +3.86 on Car COV@CD, indicating significantly better generation diversity. Against the previous best UNet-based method LION, FastDiT-3D-S improves 1-NNA@CD by 3.35 points on Chair and 5.60 points on Car, while also surpassing it on all COV metrics. Compared to flow-based methods (PointFlow, SoftFlow, DPF-Net) and VAE-based methods (SetVAE), the advantage is even more pronounced, with 1-NNA@CD improvements of 12–15 points. The paper also demonstrates that these results are achieved at only 8 GPU-hours of training (vs. 91 hours for the unmasked baseline), a 6.5% training cost relative to the original DiT-3D pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DiT-3D (Mo et al., NeurIPS 2023), which established plain diffusion transformers for voxelized 3D point cloud generation, by introducing an encoder-decoder architecture that operates on masked voxel inputs. It adapts the masked diffusion training paradigm from MaskDiT (Zheng et al., 2023) to the 3D setting, but departs critically from MaskDiT's random masking by proposing a foreground-background aware strategy that exploits the extreme occupancy imbalance in 3D voxels (97.66% background on average). The addition of Mixture-of-Experts FFN layers for multi-category generation represents a further architectural departure from the dense transformer blocks in both DiT-3D and MaskDiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× reduction in training cost (6.5% of original) makes high-resolution 3D diffusion transformer training substantially more accessible, addressing a key bottleneck that has limited scaling of voxel-based 3D generative models. The finding that 99% masking is viable in 3D (versus 50% in 2D) reveals a structural property of volumetric data that could inform future efficient 3D architectures. However, the evaluation is limited to three ShapeNet categories at 32×32×32 resolution, and the paper does not explore text-conditioned generation or larger-scale datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex or open-vocabulary 3D generation settings.
