## Problem

NeRF and 3DGS require hundreds of input views to produce artifact-free novel views, yet real-world capture (e.g., mobile phones) typically yields only a handful of images. Existing diffusion-based approaches like DiffusioNeRF and ReconFusion use the diffusion model as a per-step regularizer (score distillation), incurring prohibitive inference cost at every training iteration. The paper asks whether diffusion priors can be leveraged more efficiently—by generating pseudo-observations once and treating them as real training data—without sacrificing reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240423/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes W sparse RGB images (5–20 views) with calibrated camera poses as input; in super-resolution experiments, inputs are downsampled by a factor of 4. It outputs a trained NeRF or 3DGS representation capable of rendering novel views at the original resolution, effectively super-resolving beyond the input quality. A key operational detail is that the diffusion model is invoked only once per densification iteration (not at every gradient step), yielding roughly 10× faster training than score-distillation baselines. The training set is progressively densified by 5–10× with generated pseudo-observations, and 20% of the least perceptually similar pseudo-observations (by LPIPS) are discarded to prevent drift from the original scene.

## Method

The core mechanism is a 'deceptive diffusion model'—a Stable Diffusion latent diffusion architecture fine-tuned with ControlNet (5 control channels: 3 for coarse RGB, 1 for depth, 1 for uncertainty) that transforms coarse NeRF/3DGS renderings into photorealistic pseudo-observations. A reconstruction uncertainty map is computed by warping the nearest real input view to the novel viewpoint via rendered depth and taking the squared pixel difference, providing explicit cross-view consistency guidance to the diffusion model. Text conditioning combines an image-captioning prompt with a shared textual-inversion embedding to preserve scene semantics and stylistic congruence. Training alternates between two steps: (1) rendering RGB, depth, and uncertainty at randomly sampled novel viewpoints and passing them through the diffusion model to produce pseudo-observations, and (2) re-optimizing the NeRF/3DGS on the union of real inputs and accumulated pseudo-observations. The deceptive diffusion model itself is trained on quartets (fine image, coarse image, depth, uncertainty) constructed from coarse-fine NeRF duos, augmented with noisy-RGB-plus-depth pairs to reduce data preparation cost.

## Results

On Hypersim with 10 input views, Deceptive-3DGS achieves the best PSNR of 21.45 dB, SSIM of 0.774, and LPIPS of 0.241, outperforming the strongest baseline FreeNeRF (18.06 dB PSNR, 0.671 SSIM, 0.286 LPIPS) by 3.4 dB in PSNR. On LLFF with 6 input views, Deceptive-3DGS reaches 24.15 dB PSNR and 0.177 LPIPS, again surpassing FreeNeRF (23.73 dB, 0.195 LPIPS). The method also demonstrates super-resolution capability, recovering fine details from inputs downsampled by a factor of 4, a setting where both FreeNeRF and NeRF-SR produce visible artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Deceptive-NeRF/3DGS outperforms all ten baselines across every metric on both Hypersim and LLFF. The closest competitor is FreeNeRF [71], which uses frequency regularization; on Hypersim 10-view, Deceptive-3DGS leads by 3.4 dB PSNR (21.45 vs. 18.06) and 0.103 SSIM, while on LLFF 6-view the gap narrows to 0.42 dB PSNR (24.15 vs. 23.73). Among diffusion-based methods, DiffusioNeRF [69] (which uses diffusion as a regularizer/scorer) trails Deceptive-NeRF by 2.66 dB PSNR on Hypersim 10-view, confirming that generating pseudo-observations is more effective than score-based regularization. Pretrained methods (PixelNeRF [73], MVSNeRF [9], SRF [12]) and depth-supervised approaches (DS-NeRF [15]) show the largest gaps, with PixelNeRF achieving only 8.31 dB PSNR on Hypersim 10-view versus 21.45 for Deceptive-3DGS. RegNeRF [42] and FlipNeRF [51] are competitive on PSNR but lag significantly on SSIM and LPIPS, indicating their reconstructions lack structural fidelity. For the super-resolution evaluation, NeRF-SR [64] degrades under sparse observations and produces artifacts, while FreeNeRF fails to restore complex details due to the absence of 2D diffusion priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the NeRF and 3DGS representation frameworks and the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) architecture, but departs sharply from prior diffusion-based few-view methods like DiffusioNeRF and ReconFusion, which use a diffusion model as a per-step 'scorer' to regularize radiance field optimization. Instead, the authors repurpose the diffusion model as a one-time pseudo-observation generator: coarse NeRF/3DGS renderings are fed through a ControlNet-conditioned diffusion model (with depth and uncertainty maps) to produce photorealistic images that are then added to the training set as if they were real views. The uncertainty measure, computed via nearest-input-view warping, is a lightweight alternative to ensemble or variational uncertainty approaches, and the textual inversion component borrows from personalization literature to maintain stylistic consistency across generated views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~10× training speedup over diffusion-as-regularizer methods, making diffusion-guided few-view reconstruction far more accessible for consumer-level applications where users capture only a handful of images. The super-resolution capability emerging as a byproduct of pseudo-observation densification is a useful bonus for low-resolution capture scenarios. However, the approach still requires calibrated camera poses, the uncertainty measure relies on a single nearest-view correspondence (which may degrade under extreme sparsity or large baseline gaps), and the one-shot pseudo-observation generation does not adapt to progressively improving reconstructions within a single diffusion call. Open questions include whether the method extends to dynamic scenes, whether the 5–10× densification factor is optimal across scene complexities, and whether the LPIPS-based filtering of low-similarity pseudo-observations can be made more principled.
