## Problem

Standard discriminative classifiers learn entangled, image-level representations that ignore the compositional and 3D structure of objects, leading to significant performance degradation under out-of-distribution shifts such as occlusion, corruption, and novel contexts. This paper asks whether explicitly embedding a 3D compositional object representation into the network architecture can close this OOD generalization gap while maintaining real-time inference speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240095/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 640×800 RGB image as input and produces two outputs: an object class label (from 10–12 categories) and a 3D object pose (azimuth, elevation, in-plane rotation). Classification inference operates on a 1/8-resolution feature map (D=128) via a simple convolutional matching step (~0.01s), while pose estimation additionally requires inverse rendering of the neural object volume (~0.21s). During training, annotated 3D object poses are required to establish correspondences between Gaussians and image features.

## Method

NOVUM pairs a shared ResNet50 backbone with one neural object volume per class, where each volume is a spatial composition of ~1100 3D Gaussians arranged on a cuboid, each emitting a 128-dim feature vector. Training uses a three-level vMF-based contrastive loss that pushes each Gaussian's feature to be distinct from (i) non-neighboring Gaussians of the same object, (ii) Gaussians of other categories, and (iii) a shared background feature set, while pulling it toward the corresponding image feature across instances. Classification at test time reduces to a geometry-free convolution: each pixel's feature is matched against all Gaussian and background features, and the class with the highest aggregate likelihood wins. Pose estimation is obtained by inverse rendering the predicted class's volume via gradient-based optimization of the reconstruction loss, initialized from a coarse 144-pose grid search. The compositional design means each Gaussian independently contributes evidence about a local object part, so occlusion or corruption of some parts is compensated by the remaining visible Gaussians.

## Results

On in-distribution PASCAL3D+ (L0), NOVUM achieves 99.5% classification accuracy, on par with all baselines (99.3–99.4%). Under out-of-distribution shifts, NOVUM substantially outperforms all baselines: 81.6% mean accuracy on occluded-P3D+ (L1–L3) vs. 75.8% for the best baseline (ConvNeXt), 85.2% on OOD-CV vs. 64.2% (Swin-T), and 91.3% on corrupted-P3D+ vs. 87.6% (ViT-b-16). For 3D pose estimation at the π/6 threshold, NOVUM reaches 88.2% on P3D+ and 52.9% on OOD-CV, surpassing the dedicated pose-estimation method NeMo (86.1% and 51.6% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOVUM matches all four baselines (ResNet50, Swin-T, ConvNeXt, ViT-B-16) on in-distribution PASCAL3D+ (99.5% vs. 99.3–99.4%) but consistently outperforms every baseline across all OOD scenarios. On occluded-P3D+, NOVUM leads by 5.8 points over ConvNeXt (81.6% vs. 75.8% mean L1–L3) and by 13.9 points over ResNet50 (81.6% vs. 72.3%). The gap widens dramatically on OOD-CV, where NOVUM achieves 85.2% versus 64.2% for Swin-T (the strongest baseline), a 21-point margin. On corrupted-P3D+, NOVUM (91.3%) edges out ViT-B-16 (87.6%) by 3.7 points. For 3D pose estimation, NOVUM also surpasses the purpose-built NeMo method by 2.1 points on P3D+ (88.2% vs. 86.1% at π/6) and by 1.3 points on OOD-CV (52.9% vs. 51.6%), while using 12× fewer parameters (83M vs. 996M) and converging 10× faster (30 vs. 300 optimization steps).

## Relation to Prior Work

NOVUM extends the line of category-level 3D representation work (NeMo, VOGE) that previously served only 3D pose estimation, repurposing 3D Gaussian splatting for object classification. The key architectural departure is a shared class-agnostic backbone combined with per-class neural object volumes, enabling a vMF-based three-level contrastive loss that makes Gaussian features discriminative across categories, spatially within objects, and against background. Unlike prior render-and-compare methods that require full geometry at inference, NOVUM performs classification via simple feature matching without 3D structure, reserving volume rendering only for the secondary pose estimation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly embedding 3D compositional structure into a classification network yields substantial and consistent OOD robustness gains (up to 33% on OOD-CV) while maintaining real-time inference, suggesting that 3D-awareness is a viable inductive bias for generalization beyond data augmentation. The interpretability of visualizing which Gaussian parts matched provides a qualitatively different window into model decisions compared to attention maps or Grad-CAM. However, the approach is currently limited to small category sets (10–12 classes), requires 3D pose annotations during training, and uses a fixed cuboid geometry that cannot handle intra-class shape variability—addressing these constraints (e.g., via deformable volumes or pose-free training) would be necessary for broader adoption.
