## Problem

Existing image composition methods require task-specific training on foreground-background-composite triplets, limiting generalization to open-world scenarios. Image harmonization and semantic image composition are typically addressed by separate models, and no unified zero-shot framework exists that handles both appearance-level and structure-level composition without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240436/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image with its object mask and a background image with a designated placement region mask, all aligned to 512×512 resolution. Optionally, text prompts or sketch images (processed via T2I-Adapter) can guide semantic composition. The pipeline produces three intermediate outputs in sequence: a background with the original object removed, a harmonized composite, and a final semantically edited image, with the user selecting which phase to terminate at.

## Method

FreeCompose exploits the observation that pre-trained diffusion models (Stable Diffusion V2.1) automatically identify copy-paste boundaries as low-density regions during denoising, and uses the Delta Denoising Score (DDS) loss to optimize the composite toward high-density, natural regions in a zero-shot, training-free manner. Three phase-specific modifications extend the vanilla DDS loss: (1) object removal discards K,V values in self-attention layers corresponding to the masked region, forcing the model to inpaint from surrounding context; (2) image harmonization applies separate VGG-16 perceptual losses on foreground and background masks to balance identity preservation against blending; (3) semantic composition replaces the optimized image's K,V values with those of the input image in deeper self-attention layers (layer > 10, after 400 optimization steps) to lock object identity while allowing structural changes guided by condition differences. The entire pipeline uses fixed predefined prompts and requires no gradient updates to the diffusion model weights.

## Results

The paper evaluates via user studies (1–5 scale, >20 raters) rather than standard quantitative metrics. On object removal, FreeCompose achieves 3.85±1.01 (Image Harmony) and 4.47±0.73 (Object Removal), outperforming all three baselines. On image harmonization, FreeCompose scores 3.69±1.07 (Image Harmony) and 4.11±0.92 (Object Identity Preserving), leading on harmony but trailing DucoNet by 0.05 on identity preservation. Semantic image composition results are presented qualitatively only, with no numerical comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On object removal, FreeCompose outperforms all three baselines on both criteria: it beats Repaint (3.85 vs 3.24 harmony; 4.47 vs 3.82 removal), SD Inpainting (3.85 vs 2.99; 4.47 vs 3.55), and Lama (3.85 vs 3.47; 4.47 vs 4.14), with the largest margin over SD Inpainting. On image harmonization, FreeCompose clearly surpasses Diff Harmonization on both metrics (3.69 vs 3.11 harmony; 4.11 vs 3.83 identity), confirming that a training-free diffusion-prior approach can match or exceed a dedicated inversion-and-redenoising pipeline. Against the training-based DucoNet, FreeCompose wins decisively on image harmony (3.69 vs 3.14) but falls slightly short on object identity preservation (4.11 vs 4.16), which the authors attribute to their lower foreground perceptual-loss weight (λ_for = 0.1) prioritizing blending over strict identity retention. No numerical comparison is provided for semantic image composition or multi-concept customization; those results are qualitative only.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeCompose extends the Delta Denoising Score (DDS) loss [11]—originally designed for text-guided image editing—into a generic composition framework by introducing a mask-guided variant and K-V self-attention manipulation (inspired by KV inversion [15]). It departs from the dominant learning-based pipeline for image harmonization (DoveNet, DocuNet) and semantic composition (Paint-by-Example, AnyDoor), which each require task-specific training on curated triplets, by instead exploiting the natural-image prior of a frozen pre-trained diffusion model in a fully zero-shot, test-time-optimization setting. The key conceptual departure is the observation that the diffusion prior inherently localizes copy-paste boundaries as low-density regions, enabling a single loss formulation to handle both appearance-level harmonization and structure-level semantic composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying image harmonization, semantic composition, and object removal under one training-free pipeline, FreeCompose removes the need for task-specific datasets and separate model training, lowering the barrier to open-world composition. However, the evaluation relies solely on small-scale user studies (5 cases per task, ~20 volunteers) without standard quantitative benchmarks, and the resolution is capped at 512×512, leaving open questions about scalability to higher resolutions and more complex multi-object scenes. The authors note future directions toward video composition and broader composition tasks, but the current method's dependence on predefined prompts and the K-V replacement heuristic (with fixed T and L thresholds) limits its flexibility for arbitrary editing intents.
