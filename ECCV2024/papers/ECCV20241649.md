## Problem

Recovering camera poses from sparse-view images (2–5 views) is challenging because direct pose regression assumes a single mode and fails under symmetry, while probabilistic methods like RelPose must brute-force sample the entire SO(3) space (up to 500K rotations) to achieve high precision, creating an unavoidable trade-off between accuracy and runtime. The paper identifies that real-world pose distributions are highly skewed with only a few isolated modes, making uniform grid sampling fundamentally inefficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241649/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–10 sparse-view RGB images of a single object with no known poses and outputs 6DoF camera extrinsics (rotation matrix + 3D translation) for each image relative to a canonical first frame. The first frame is designated as the reference (identity rotation, translation normalized to [0,0,1]), and all translations are scaled by the distance from the canonical camera to the object's center of mass. At inference, the model generates 500 pose hypotheses and the discriminator selects the single highest-probability one as the final prediction.

## Method

ADen uses a shared ResNet-50 backbone to extract per-image features, followed by a 6-layer transformer to fuse multi-view information. A pose generator head (MLP with M learnable queries) produces M=500 pose hypotheses (quaternion + translation) from the fused features; during training, only the hypothesis closest to ground truth receives a geodesic/L2 regression loss, allowing other hypotheses to explore alternative modes without mode collapse. A pose discriminator head (MLP) scores each hypothesis via a contrastive negative log-likelihood loss that rewards the ground-truth pose and penalizes the generated samples. Crucially, the generator adaptively concentrates samples near the true distribution modes rather than uniformly covering SO(3), eliminating the grid-resolution lower bound on accuracy. Training stability is maintained by injecting Gaussian noise into the query embeddings to create harder negatives for the discriminator, avoiding the adversarial instability typical of GANs.

## Results

On CO3D (5 input images, seen categories), ADen achieves 86.5% pairwise relative rotation accuracy at 15°, outperforming RelPose++ (84.7%) and PoseDiff (77.7%). At tighter thresholds, the gap widens: ADen reaches 56.0% Acc@5° and 79.9% Acc@10° versus 44.7% and 74.2% for RelPose++. ADen also achieves 88.1% translation accuracy (@0.2 scene scale) on seen categories and runs at 20 FPS with 9 input images, compared to 0.020 FPS for RelPose++ and 0.015 FPS for PoseDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADen outperforms all seven baselines across every image count and both seen/unseen splits. Against the strongest prior method RelPose++ (84.7% @15° seen, 5 images), ADen gains +1.8 points, and the advantage grows to +12.1 points at the tighter 5° threshold (56.0% vs. 44.7%). PoseDiff, the second-strongest baseline, trails ADen by 8.8 points at 15° and by 14.3 points at 5° on seen categories. On unseen categories, ADen leads RelPose++ by 8.1 points (80.9% vs. 72.8%) and SparsePose by 10.9 points. In inference speed, ADen (0.05 s / 20 FPS for 9 images) is roughly 960× faster than RelPose++ (48 s) and 1276× faster than PoseDiff (63.8 s), while COLMAP (SP+SG) is 360× slower. On zero-shot Objectron, ADen achieves 85.8% rotation accuracy at 5 images versus 76.6% for RelPose++ and 68.0% for PoseDiff.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADen carves new ground by repurposing the generator-discriminator paradigm (from GANs and DETR-style learnable queries) for pose distribution modeling, departing from both the direct-regression line (PoseNet, SparsePose) and the uniform-sampling energy-based line (Implicit-PDF, RelPose, RelPose++). Conceptually, it sits between these two: the generator produces a small set of adaptive pose hypotheses (like regression, but multi-modal), while the discriminator scores them via contrastive loss (like the energy-based methods, but without exhaustive grid evaluation). The multi-view feature extraction (ResNet + Transformer) follows RelPose++ closely, and the learnable query mechanism borrows from DETR, but the joint generator-discriminator training with a shared backbone and non-adversarial contrastive objective is the paper's distinctive architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1000× sample-efficiency gain (500 adaptive samples vs. 500K uniform samples) with simultaneously better accuracy and real-time inference (20 FPS vs. 0.02 FPS) makes sparse-view pose estimation practically deployable in AR and casual-capture pipelines where latency matters. The method also sidesteps the curse of dimensionality that limits uniform-sampling approaches to SO(3), naturally extending to joint 6DoF (rotation + translation) without combinatorial blowup. Open questions include how the generator's coverage degrades in genuinely high-dimensional pose spaces, whether the approach transfers to non-object-centric scenes with more complex multi-modal structure, and whether the contrastive discriminator can be replaced by a simpler scoring head without the observed training instability.
