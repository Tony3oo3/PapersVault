## Problem

Existing sensor noise models are either parametric (e.g., heteroscedastic Gaussian) and make distributional assumptions that poorly fit real sensor noise, or DNN-based and require extensive multi-scene captures. Two practical gaps remain unaddressed: (1) calibrating accurate noise models for every ISO level is prohibitively expensive, and (2) standard noise synthesis assumes a noise-free input image, which is unavailable when ground truth comes from black-box rendering pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240622/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes clean raw images (or noisy raw images for the noise-on-noise variant) and calibrated noise PMFs indexed by sensor intensity level (binned every 4 levels from black to white), CFA channel (4 Bayer channels), and ISO level. It outputs synthesized noisy raw images at a target ISO, interpolated PMFs for uncalibrated ISOs, and—via the noise-on-noise path—a noisy image at a second ISO given only a noisy image at a first ISO plus a denoised intensity estimate. The PMF footprint is ~240 distributions per ISO per channel for a 10-bit sensor.

## Method

The core contribution is a non-parametric noise model that builds empirical probability mass functions (PMFs) directly from observed noise samples at each intensity level, bypassing all parametric distributional assumptions. Calibration captures bursts of a uniform color checker at multiple exposure values; low-ISO bursts are averaged to a clean reference, and per-pixel differences at each intensity bin form the noise sample set whose histogram defines the PMF. Noise synthesis uses inversion sampling from these PMFs. For uncalibrated ISOs, the method assumes normalized PMFs (zero mean, unit variance) share shape across ISOs, so it interpolates only the mean and variance curves to rescale a reference PMF. For noise-on-noise synthesis, a trained denoiser estimates the clean intensity, and the method samples from the conditional difference distribution p(n₂−n₁) rather than naively adding p(n₂) on top of an already-noisy image.

## Results

On the raw denoising-demosaicking task, the proposed non-parametric noise model achieves the best PSNR and SSIM on both cameras: 39.74 dB / 0.954 on S22+ and 38.49 dB / 0.950 on Pixel 6, outperforming all four baselines. The interpolated noise models (ISO 400 and ISO 1600) match the calibrated models within 0.02 dB PSNR, confirming that interpolation preserves accuracy. For the noise-on-noise synthesis experiment on S20FE, the proposed method reaches 39.05 dB PSNR / 0.939 SSIM (JSD 0.008), closing most of the gap to the real-capture upper bound of 39.38 dB / 0.948, while naive noise-on-noise synthesis drops to 38.03 dB / 0.929 (JSD 0.049).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed non-parametric model outperforms all four baselines on PSNR for both cameras: on S22+ it leads LRD (39.63) by 0.11 dB and HG (39.62) by 0.12 dB; on Pixel 6 it leads LRD (38.11) by 0.38 dB and HG (38.00) by 0.49 dB. On SSIM, the advantage is smaller but consistent—0.954 vs. 0.951 (Noise Flow) on S22+ and 0.950 vs. 0.949 (HG) on Pixel 6. Noise Flow [1] is the weakest baseline overall, particularly on Pixel 6 where it trails the proposed method by 1.32 dB in PSNR, suggesting DNN-based generative noise synthesis struggles to match the statistical fidelity of direct PMF sampling. HG [34] is the closest competitor on Pixel 6 SSIM (0.949 vs. 0.950) but lags by nearly 0.5 dB in PSNR, reflecting its parametric assumptions about noise component distributions. The interpolated models (ISO 400, ISO 1600) achieve PSNR within 0.02 dB of the fully calibrated model, demonstrating that the interpolation procedure preserves the accuracy advantage over all baselines without additional captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of modeling sensor noise through parametric assumptions about individual components (Poisson shot noise, Gaussian read noise, etc.) or through learned DNN-based generators (Noise Flow, LRD). Instead, it treats the joint signal-dependent noise as a black-box empirical distribution, constructing per-intensity-level PMFs directly from burst captures and synthesizing noise via inversion sampling. The noise-on-noise synthesis (Sec. 4.2) extends the standard clean-image-only assumption in prior augmentation pipelines, and the ISO interpolation scheme replaces the parametric linear-system fitting used in HG-model calibration [34] with a simpler curve-fit over measured variances and means.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in reducing calibration burden (fewer ISOs needed via interpolation) and enabling noise augmentation when clean raw data is unavailable—a common situation in rendering and restoration pipelines where ground truth comes from black-box ISP chains. The paper acknowledges that fixed-pattern and banding noise are not explicitly modeled, and the per-intensity PMF storage is inherently larger than a few parametric coefficients. An open direction the authors note is a hybrid parametric/non-parametric scheme over the intensity range to reduce memory while retaining accuracy, and whether the non-parametric advantage persists at extreme low-light levels where sample counts per intensity bin become sparse.
