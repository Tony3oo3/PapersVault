## Problem

Existing multi-weather restoration methods struggle to dynamically handle the unpredictable and varied combinations of degradations encountered in the real world, and diffusion-based approaches like WeatherDiffusion rely on the degraded image alone as a condition, which inadequately guides reconstruction and necessitates many sampling steps. The paper asks how to design an information-rich, adaptive condition for the diffusion process that can flexibly accommodate arbitrary weather combinations while also modeling the underlying scene background.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240221/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single adverse weather RGB image (degraded image y) at 256×256 resolution and produces a restored clean image. Unusually, the diffusion model does not directly reconstruct the clean image; instead it predicts the degradation residual (x − y) and the final output is obtained by adding this residual back to the input. The condition guiding denoising is not the degraded image itself but a pair of learned prompt vectors (weather-prompts and general prompts) injected via cross-attention into the latent diffusion layers, requiring only 2 DDIM sampling steps at inference.

## Method

T³-DiffWeather introduces a 'Teaching Tailored to Talent' paradigm built on two complementary prompt mechanisms. First, a prompt pool of 20 learnable sub-prompts (64 tokens each) is queried against the degradation-residual embedding via cosine similarity, and the top-5 most relevant sub-prompts are concatenated to form an instance-specific weather-prompt that adaptively captures the particular combination of degradations present. Second, a set of 256-token general prompts is constrained by cross-attention with intermediate features from a frozen Depth-Anything ViT-S model, anchoring them to degradation-robust scene structure. A compact contrastive prompt loss then pushes the two prompt types apart (serving as natural negatives) while pulling each toward its respective feature constraint. These prompts are embedded into the latent diffusion backbone (Refusion-style) via cross-attention, and the overall training combines a noise-prediction loss on the residual, a PSNR reconstruction loss on sampled outputs, and the contrastive prompt loss, all with equal weighting.

## Results

T3-DiffWeather achieves state-of-the-art multi-weather restoration across all three benchmarks: 37.51 PSNR / 0.9664 SSIM on Snow100K-S, 31.99 PSNR / 0.9365 SSIM on Outdoor-Rain, and 32.66 PSNR / 0.9411 SSIM on RainDrop. On Snow100K-S, it surpasses the previous best diffusion method WeatherDiffusion by 1.68 dB in PSNR. In terms of efficiency, the method requires only 2 DDIM sampling steps (vs. 25 for WeatherDiffusion), reducing computational complexity to 1/52 of the prior SOTA at 256×256 resolution (59.82 GFLOPs vs. 248.4 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T3-DiffWeather outperforms all five multi-weather baselines (All-in-One, TransWeather, TKL&M, WeatherDiffusion, AWRCP) on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is AWRCP [88], which T3-DiffWeather surpasses by 0.59 dB PSNR on Snow100K-S, 0.60 dB on Outdoor-Rain, and 0.73 dB on RainDrop. Against the prior diffusion-based SOTA WeatherDiffusion [58], the gains are larger: +1.68 dB on Snow100K-S and +2.35 dB on Outdoor-Rain, while also reducing sampling steps from 25 to 2. On RainDrop, the single-task UDR-S²Former [13] (marked with △, trained only on RainDrop) achieves marginally higher SSIM (0.9427 vs. 0.9411) but T3-DiffWeather edges it out on PSNR (32.66 vs. 32.64) while handling all three weather types simultaneously. In the computational comparison (Table 4), T3-DiffWeather uses 69.38M parameters and 59.82 GFLOPs, compared to 113.68M / 248.4 GFLOPs for WeatherDiffusion and 131.4M / 63.4G×50 for Refusion [54], making it roughly 52× cheaper in total FLOPs than the prior SOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based adverse weather restoration line initiated by WeatherDiffusion [58], which used the degraded image as the sole conditioning signal, by replacing it with a dual-prompt condition: instance-wise weather-prompts assembled from a learnable prompt pool and scene-level general prompts constrained by Depth-Anything features. The prompt pool design departs from PromptIR [59], which relies on a fixed set of shared prompts, by allowing the network to autonomously select and concatenate sub-prompts via a query-key matching mechanism, thereby explicitly modeling both shared and distinct attributes across weather types. The architectural backbone follows Refusion [54] (latent-space diffusion), and the shift from reconstructing the full clean image to reconstructing the degradation residual is a key conceptual departure that reframes the diffusion target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that only 2 DDIM sampling steps suffice to match or exceed 25-step WeatherDiffusion, reducing inference FLOPs by roughly 52×—a substantial efficiency gain that makes diffusion-based weather restoration viable for near-real-time deployment. The prompt pool mechanism offers a generalizable recipe for handling unpredictable degradation combinations without task-specific architectural modifications, though its current validation is limited to three weather types (snow, rain, raindrop) on synthetic and semi-real datasets. Open questions include whether the prompt pool scales gracefully to additional degradation types (fog, glare, mixed multi-weather), whether the Depth-Anything constraint remains effective on truly unseen real-world scenes, and whether the residual-reconstruction objective transfers to other restoration tasks beyond adverse weather.
