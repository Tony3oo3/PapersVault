## Problem

In multi-object indoor scenes, SDF-based volume density produces spurious local maxima along camera rays because the SDF at a sampled point is influenced by nearby surfaces that do not intersect the ray. This causes high rendering weights at points far from the actual surface, introducing noise into 2D observations and degrading 3D geometry reconstruction. The paper asks whether a ray-conditioned distance measure can yield a density function more consistent with true occupancy along each viewing direction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images and optionally 2.5D depth/normal maps (following the MonoSDF pipeline) as geometric cues during training. It outputs a watertight 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from the predicted SDF, as well as rendered 2D RGB views for view synthesis evaluation. Spatial encoding is provided either by a multi-resolution hash grid (Instant-NGP style) or by Fourier features fed into an MLP, and the network is trained with 2D photometric supervision plus a self-supervised visibility signal that requires no additional annotations.

## Method

The core contribution replaces the SDF with the Signed Ray Distance Function (SRDF) for volume density: a geometry MLP predicts the SDF and shared geometry features, while a separate SRDF MLP (conditioned on viewing direction, geometry features, and point position) predicts the ray-specific distance, which is then passed through the same Laplace CDF used in VolSDF to produce a ray-conditioned density. Two auxiliary losses enforce geometric consistency: (1) an SRDF-SDF consistency loss that penalizes sign disagreement between the two distance fields using a sigmoid-approximated sign function, and (2) a self-supervised visibility task where pseudo-labels are generated by requiring agreement between SRDF and SDF sign changes along each ray, and a binary visibility probability is predicted from the SRDF branch with a cross-entropy loss. The SDF is retained solely for final mesh extraction via marching cubes, while all volume rendering (RGB, depth, normal) uses the SRDF-derived density, ensuring that rendering weights peak only at the true ray-surface intersection.

## Results

On ScanNet (MLP representation), RS-Recon achieves an F-score of 0.794, precision of 0.809, and recall of 0.779, outperforming the MonoSDF baseline (F-score 0.733) by 6.1% and Occ_SDF_Hybrid (F-score 0.765) by 2.9%. On Replica, the method reaches a Chamfer-L1 distance of 2.60 and F-score of 91.72 (MLP), improving over MonoSDF by 9.91% in Chamfer distance. On the large-scale Tanks and Temples dataset, the mean F-score is 6.41 (MLP) and 7.73 (Grid), surpassing MonoSDF by 1.24% and 1.15% respectively. For view synthesis, PSNR improves to 30.09 (Grid) and 27.77 (MLP) on ScanNet, 42.04 and 36.06 on Replica, and 29.52 and 25.47 on Tanks and Temples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-Recon (MLP) achieves the best F-score of 0.794 on ScanNet, outperforming the primary baseline MonoSDF_MLP (0.733) by 6.1% in F-score, 9.8% in precision, and 1.0% in recall. Against the strongest competing method Occ_SDF_Hybrid (F-score 0.765), the improvement is 2.9% in F-score with higher precision (0.809 vs. 0.783) and recall (0.779 vs. 0.748). On Replica, the method surpasses MonoSDF by 9.91% in Chamfer-L1 distance (2.60 vs. 2.94) and 5.54% in F-score (91.72 vs. 86.18), while matching Occ_SDF_Hybrid (91.72 vs. 91.54). On Tanks and Temples, RS-Recon leads MonoSDF by 1.24% mean F-score (6.41 vs. 5.17) and Occ_SDF_Hybrid by 1.03% (6.41 vs. 5.38). The method also outperforms earlier neural implicit methods (NeuS, VolSDF, UNISURF) by large margins (F-score 0.794 vs. 0.291, 0.346, 0.267 respectively) and the classic MVS approach COLMAP (0.794 vs. 0.537). For view synthesis, PSNR gains of 1.13–1.61 dB over MonoSDF are observed across all three datasets, confirming that the SRDF-based density also improves 2D rendering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDF-to-density pipeline established by VolSDF [42] and adopted by MonoSDF [46], NeuS [38], and others, by replacing the global SDF with a ray-specific Signed Ray Distance Function (SRDF) for volume density parameterization. The SRDF concept itself originates from VolRecon [26] and Zins et al. [48], which introduced it for generalizable multi-view reconstruction; here it is repurposed for per-scene indoor reconstruction with additional architectural components (a separate SRDF MLP branch, a sign-consistency loss, and a self-supervised visibility task). The method departs from prior SDF-based approaches by arguing that in multi-object indoor scenes, the global SDF introduces spurious local density maxima from off-ray surfaces, whereas the ray-local SRDF produces a unimodal weight distribution aligned with actual 2D observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode of SDF-based density in cluttered indoor scenes, and the self-supervised visibility task offers a lightweight alternative to multi-view-geometry-based visibility priors (e.g., VIP-NeRF) without requiring additional annotations. However, the improvements over the strong MonoSDF baseline are modest (1–6% F-score gains), and the method adds considerable architectural complexity (two distance-field branches, a consistency loss, and a visibility head) for these gains. Open questions include whether the SRDF-based density advantage persists in outdoor or sparsely-occupied scenes, whether the approach generalizes to sparse-view settings, and whether the consistency loss and visibility task remain beneficial when the scene contains fewer objects.
