key_contribution: A training-free model merging technique for multi-target domain adaptation that combines linear mid-point parameter averaging (justified by empirical mode connectivity analysis showing pretraining enables linear connectivity) with Gaussian-prior-based batch-normalization buffer merging, achieving performance on par with data-combination baselines (56.3% harmonic mean mIoU with ResNet101) while outperforming prior MTDA methods by +8.1% and requiring no access to any target-domain training data.
limitations: The method requires all models to be initialized from identical pretrained backbone weights and use the same network architecture, which limits applicability when models are trained with different initializations or architectures. surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that Git Re-Basin, a sophisticated permutation-based merging algorithm, degenerates exactly to simple mid-point averaging in this domain-adaptation setting — a conceptual surprise that challenges the assumption that hidden-layer permutation symmetries matter when models share a pretrained backbone. ## Problem

Existing multi-target domain adaptation (MTDA) methods for semantic segmentation require simultaneous access to unlabeled data from all target domains during training, an assumption that is impractical due to data transfer bandwidth constraints and privacy policies. This paper asks whether models independently adapted to distinct target domains can be merged into a single robust model without any access to the training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The two-stage pipeline first trains standard STDA segmentation models (e.g., HRDA) on labeled synthetic source images (GTA at 1914×1052 or SYNTHIA at 1280×760) plus unlabeled target-domain images, producing pixel-level 19-class segmentation masks. The merging phase then operates entirely on model checkpoint state dictionaries—no images are needed—combining learnable parameters and batch-normalization running statistics from the separately trained models to yield a single merged model that accepts RGB images and outputs segmentation masks across all target domains.

## Method

The merging has two components. For parameters, the authors show that linear mid-point weight averaging suffices because pretraining (not merely identical initialization) places domain-adapted models within a shared linearly connected loss basin; they demonstrate that permutation-based methods like Git Re-Basin degenerate to simple mid-point merging in this setting. For buffers (BN running means, variances, and batch counts), they model the real-world input distribution with a Gaussian prior and compute merged statistics via weighted formulas that account for the number of tracked batches from each source model, correctly combining both within-domain variance and between-domain mean shift. The approach is training-free, requires no access to any target-domain images during merging, and scales to m≥2 domains with O(n) complexity versus O(n²) for inter-domain consistency methods.

## Results

On the GTA→{Cityscapes, IDD} multi-target domain adaptation task, the proposed merging method achieves a harmonic mean mIoU of 56.3% with ResNet101 (CS: 58.8%, IDD: 54.0%) and 60.8% with MiT-B5 (CS: 67.0%, IDD: 55.6%), without requiring access to any target-domain training data during the merging phase. This matches the Data Combination baseline (56.2% harmonic mean with R101) that trains on combined target data, while outperforming the best prior MTDA method (Coast, 48.2% harmonic mean) by +8.1%. When only the backbone is merged (keeping separate decode heads), the improvement over the best single-target STDA model reaches +5.6% (R101) and +2.5% (MiT-B5). Extending to four target domains (Cityscapes, IDD, ACDC, DarkZurich), merging yields gains up to +5.8% over the best single-domain model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed merging method substantially outperforms all prior MTDA baselines: it exceeds Coast [88] (48.2% H.Mean) by +8.1%, CCL [34] (45.5%) by +10.8%, MT-MTDA [55] (43.6%) by +12.7%, and MTDA-ITA [20] (40.8%) by +15.5% on harmonic mean mIoU with ResNet101. Against the strongest single-target STDA methods (HRDA [29] at 61.9% CS, DDB [5] at 62.7% CS), the merged model achieves 58.8% on Cityscapes and 54.0% on IDD simultaneously, yielding a 56.3% harmonic mean that surpasses the best single-domain STDA harmonic mean (52.1% for HRDA→IDD) by +4.2%. The method also outperforms domain generalization approaches (Yue et al. [84] at 42.4%, Kundu et al. [39] at 53.4% CS-only) without requiring any target data at inference. Notably, the merged model's 56.3% harmonic mean is on par with the Data Combination baseline (56.2%) that trains on combined target data, demonstrating that model merging recovers the benefit of multi-domain training without data access. With the MiT-B5 backbone, the method achieves 60.8% harmonic mean, exceeding the best STDA (HRDA at 72.6% CS / 55.0% H.Mean) and all MTDA baselines by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HRDA (Hoyer et al., ECCV 2022) as the STDA method whose independently trained models are merged, and draws on the mode connectivity literature (Garipov et al., Frankle et al., Entezari et al.) to justify why simple linear parameter averaging suffices. It departs from prior MTDA methods (Gholami et al., Isobe et al., Nguyen-Meidine et al., Zhang et al.) that require simultaneous access to all target-domain data for inter-domain consistency losses or knowledge distillation, instead operating entirely in model space post-training. The buffer merging component extends the Gaussian-prior estimation framework from batch normalization theory to the multi-domain setting, where prior model-averaging work (Izmailov et al., Huang et al.) assumed same-domain data subsets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving deployments where data privacy regulations and bandwidth constraints prevent pooling target-domain images from multiple cities or weather conditions; this paper shows that independently trained models can be merged to match data-combination baselines without ever accessing the training data. The O(n) scaling (vs. O(n²) for inter-domain consistency) makes adding new target domains incrementally cheap. Open questions include whether the Gaussian prior assumption for buffer merging holds under highly non-Gaussian feature distributions, whether the approach extends to tasks beyond semantic segmentation (the paper briefly tests classification), and whether the requirement of identical pretrained backbones limits real-world applicability where models may have been trained with different initializations.
