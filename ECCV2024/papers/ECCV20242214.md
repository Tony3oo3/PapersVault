## Problem

Fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models with differentiable rewards faces a depth-efficiency dilemma: backpropagating through all 20–50 sampling steps is prohibitively expensive, so prior methods (ReFL, DRaFT) train only the last few steps. However, this shallow supervision fails for low-level rewards such as symmetry that depend on global layout established in early denoising steps. The paper identifies that training early steps also triggers gradient explosion, and proposes a mechanism to resolve both issues simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a generated RGB image (512×512 for SD 1.5 experiments, 1024×1024 for SDXL). A differentiable reward model scores the final output image (downsampled to 224×224 for SD 1.5, or randomly shifted before downscaling for SDXL to handle resolution mismatch). Training modifies only LoRA weights (rank 128) in the U-Net module, leaving the VAE and text encoder frozen.

## Method

DRTune introduces two key modifications to the standard iterative denoising loop. First, it applies a stop-gradient operation to the denoising network input (sg(x_t)), so gradients flow only through the linear combination branch, reducing inter-step gradients to simple scalar multiplications and eliminating gradient explosion when training early timesteps. Second, instead of training only the last K steps, it samples K equally spaced steps uniformly across all T sampling steps, enabling deep supervision of early denoising steps at a fraction of the memory cost. The equally spaced selection is shown to converge faster than random step sampling, and an early-stop mechanism (inherited from ReFL) further accelerates each iteration by terminating the denoising loop at the earliest trained step.

## Results

On the HPS v2 benchmark (400 prompts), DRTune achieves the best score across all 7 reward metrics simultaneously: HPS v2.1 = 40.63, PickScore = 0.2492, Aesthetic Score = 10.360, Symmetry = 0.0551, CLIPScore = 0.3856, Compression error = 0.0038, and Objectness = 0.0001. The most striking result is on the symmetry reward, where DRTune reduces the score to 0.0551 while all baselines remain above 0.72, indicating complete failure of shallow-supervision methods on this low-level control signal. In the user study on FDXL 1.0 (tuned SDXL 1.0), the model achieves a 69% winning rate over base SDXL 1.0 and a 37% winning rate against Midjourney v5.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRTune outperforms all four baseline families (ReFL, DRaFT, AlignProp) on every one of the 7 reward metrics under an equal 2-hour / 4×A100 compute budget. The gap is most dramatic on the symmetry reward: DRTune achieves 0.0551 while the best baseline (DRaFT-10) reaches only 0.7250, a >13× difference, confirming that early-step supervision is essential for low-level spatial properties. On high-level preference metrics (HPS v2.1, Aesthetic Score), DRTune leads the best baseline (ReFL-20) by 1.2 points and 0.45 points respectively, while PickScore is essentially tied (0.2492 vs 0.2491). AlignProp, which trains the most steps per iteration, is the weakest baseline overall (HPS 34.04, Aesthetic 6.627) due to its high per-step memory cost limiting the number of updates within the fixed budget. In the separate FDXL 1.0 user study, the DRTune-tuned SDXL 1.0 wins 69% of head-to-head comparisons against base SDXL 1.0 but trails Midjourney v5.2 (37% win rate), with the largest gap in the 'Photo' category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward-based fine-tuning line for diffusion models initiated by ReFL, DRaFT, and AlignProp, all of which back-propagate a differentiable reward through the iterative denoising process. The key departure is identifying the 'depth-efficiency dilemma': prior methods either ignore early sampling steps (ReFL, DRaFT) or train them without addressing gradient explosion (AlignProp), and the authors show this is not merely a performance gap but a fundamental failure for low-level rewards like symmetry. The solution borrows the stop-gradient-on-input trick from iterative refinement literature (Faster R-CNN, Deformable DETR, RAFT) and applies it to the denoising network, combined with equally spaced step sampling rather than the last-K or random-subset strategies used by prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that early denoising steps are essential for global structural properties (symmetry) and that a simple stop-gradient operation resolves the associated gradient explosion provides a practical recipe for reward tuning that prior methods could not achieve. The resulting FDXL 1.0 model, reaching near-parity with Midjourney v5.2 on user studies, demonstrates the practical ceiling of preference-based fine-tuning for open-source models. However, the paper acknowledges that reward hacking remains a serious limitation—over-optimized models can score highly on the reward while visually degrading—and that bias in the preference model (HPS v2.1) limits realism gains, leaving open how to leverage preference signals while mitigating their biases.
