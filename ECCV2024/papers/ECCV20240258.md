## Problem

Causal domain generalization methods lack consensus on which latent causal variables to select for prediction: most focus exclusively on parent (causal) variables, while others argue for child (anti-causal) variables, yet both approaches exclude other invariant, discriminative features. The Markov Blanket—the minimal set containing maximum mutual information with the target—is theoretically optimal for prediction but has not been effectively discovered in the latent space of high-dimensional data for domain generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from an unseen test domain (e.g., 28×28 CMNIST digits, 160×160 CelebA face crops, 224×224 PACS/VLCS natural images) and outputs a discrete class label. At inference, latent variables are not read from the encoder but recovered by solving a regularized optimization over the decoder (Eq. 7), with separate L2 penalties for CMB and spurious components, before the invariant predictor maps the CMB subset to a label.

## Method

CMBRL is a three-phase framework. Phase I learns component-wise identifiable latent variables Z via an identifiable VAE (NF-iVAE) whose exponential-family prior p(Z|Y,U) is consistent with a proposed SCM that partitions Z into parent, child, spouse, and spurious variables relative to target Y. Phase II discovers the Causal Markov Blanket (CMB) set—parents, children, and spouses of Y—using a fast-search step that tests I(Z_i; Y | Z\i) for each latent variable to separate spurious from CMB variables, followed by a forward-backward verification loop that removes false positives and recovers missed variables; mutual information is estimated via a trained predictor's conditional entropy (Eq. 3–5). Phase III trains a standard invariant predictor p(Y|Z_cmb) on the selected CMB representations. The key theoretical guarantee is that conditioning on the CMB blocks all paths from the domain variable U to Y, rendering p(Y|Z_cmb) domain-invariant while remaining the minimal sufficient set for prediction.

## Results

On CMNIST, CMBRL achieves 74.1% OOD accuracy, outperforming the strongest causal baseline iCaRL (68.8%) by 5.3 points and ERM (10.3%) by over 63 points. On CelebA, CMBRL reaches 76.2% average OOD accuracy, surpassing the second-best method iCaRL (71.5%) by 4.7 points. On the standard DG benchmarks, CMBRL attains 82.3% average on VLCS and 89.1% on PACS, setting new state-of-the-art results that exceed iCaRL (81.8% / 88.7%) and all non-causal baselines by 2–6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMBRL outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset. Against the closest causal competitor iCaRL [32], CMBRL gains 5.3 points on CMNIST (74.1% vs 68.8%), 4.7 points on CelebA (76.2% vs 71.5%), 0.5 points on VLCS (82.3% vs 81.8%), and 0.4 points on PACS (89.1% vs 88.7%). Causal transportability (CTrans [34]) achieves 72.5% on CMNIST and 66.3% on CelebA, trailing CMBRL by 1.6 and 9.9 points respectively. Among invariant-risk-minimization variants, IRM [6] reaches 62.8% on CMNIST, 78.5% on VLCS, and 85.5% on PACS, while F-IRM GAME [2] scores only 60.0% on CMNIST OOD. Non-causal DG methods (GroupDRO [41], MLDG [25], CORAL [44], RSC [15], Mixup [54], SagNet [36]) cluster between 76.6–78.7% on VLCS and 84.4–86.3% on PACS, all below CMBRL by at least 3.6 points. Causalrep [53] performs weakest among causal methods at 68.6% (CMNIST) and 60.4% (CelebA), confirming that restricting to parent variables alone is insufficient when child and spouse variables also carry causal signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identifiable VAE framework of iCaRL (Lu et al., ICLR 2021) by adding a latent-space Markov Blanket discovery phase, building on the theoretical result from Gao et al. (ICCV 2015) that the MB is the minimal sufficient set for prediction. It departs from prior causal DG methods that select only parent variables (IRM, iCaRL) or only anti-causal features (Lopez-Paz et al.) by arguing that the full CMB set—parents, children, and spouses—is theoretically optimal and empirically superior. The work also extends the authors' own earlier NeurIPS 2023 workshop paper [55] with a more complete SCM, an efficient fast-search plus forward-backward CMB discovery algorithm, and broader empirical validation across four benchmark datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded answer to the open question of which causal features to select for OOD prediction, showing that restricting to parent variables (as in IRM or iCaRL) leaves performance on the table. Key limitations include the need for identifiability assumptions on the VAE, the three-phase training pipeline, and inference-time optimization, which may hinder practical adoption. The method also explicitly does not handle latent confounders between input and target, a common real-world scenario that remains an open extension. Future work on improving independence test accuracy under limited data and scaling CMB discovery to very high-dimensional latent spaces would strengthen the approach.
