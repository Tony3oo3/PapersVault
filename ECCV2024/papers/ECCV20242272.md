## Problem

Existing monocular head avatar methods either rely on 3D neural rendering that limits photorealistic detail, or use 2D generative models that lack fine-grained 3D control over pose and expression. A key unresolved challenge is how to leverage the high-fidelity image prior of large text-to-image diffusion models while maintaining temporal coherence across video frames and enabling precise 3DMM-based animation control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For training, the method requires a short monocular talking-head video (8+ minutes in their dataset) from which per-frame 3DMM parameters are extracted via Spectre, face parsing maps via BiSeNet, and iris locations via MediaPipe. At test time, the user supplies a temporal sequence of 3DMM parameters (head pose + expression) rendered as 5 consecutive grayscale mesh images (5-channel temporal conditioning), plus an optional text prompt for celebrity morphing. The output is a photorealistic video where each frame is a full RGB image of the person's head and shoulders, with the 3DMM controlling pose/expression and the text prompt optionally shifting identity toward a named celebrity.

## Method

The method fine-tunes Stable Diffusion via a two-stage ControlNet pipeline: Stage I generates temporally smooth binary face parsing masks (capturing hair and shoulders not modeled by the 3DMM) conditioned on 5-frame grayscale mesh sequences, while Stage II synthesizes the final detailed image conditioned on the parsing mask, the current mesh rendering with iris overlay, and a motion map computed from accumulated renderings of frames n±2. The key architectural contribution is a spatio-temporal denoising procedure: at a specific DDIM step (τ=23 of S=30), the latent prediction is replaced by a weighted sum of the current frame's latent, the previous frame's final latent, and a noise term (x̂_τ^n = w_c·x_τ^n + w_p·x_0^{n-1} + w_n·ε), with higher w_p in Stage I for smooth outlines and higher w_c in Stage II for detail. Both the LDM and ControlNet are unlocked during person-specific training to capture idiosyncratic appearance, while for celebrity morphing the LDM is kept locked and the ControlNet strength parameter c (0–1) plus classifier-free guidance scale (default 7.5) control the blend between the original identity and the text-defined target.

## Results

On 8 monocular sequences (from INSTA, NeRFace, NHA, and IMAvatar), Stable Video Portraits achieves PSNR 31.4 dB, SSIM 0.9445, MSE 0.0010, LPIPS 0.026, FID 11, and KID 0.005. The method ties with PECHead on PSNR (31.4 dB) and is competitive on SSIM and MSE, while substantially outperforming all baselines on perceptual and distributional metrics: LPIPS is 24% lower than the next best (PECHead, 0.034), FID is more than 2× lower (11 vs. 23 for PECHead), and KID is 5× lower (0.005 vs. 0.025 for PECHead). A data-reduction study (Table 1) shows graceful degradation, with LPIPS rising from 0.016 at 100% data to 0.132 at 6.25% data on the Obama sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable Video Portraits is compared against seven monocular head avatar baselines: I'M Avatar, MegaPortraits, NHA, PointAvatar, NeRFace, INSTA, and PECHead. On pixel-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM, MSE), the method ties with PECHead (31.4 dB PSNR) and is within 0.001–0.002 of it on SSIM and MSE, making it competitive but not clearly superior in these traditional reconstruction metrics. However, on perceptual and distributional metrics the advantage is decisive: LPIPS of 0.026 beats PECHead (0.034) by 24%, FID of 11 is more than 2× better than PECHead (23) and 2.3× better than NHA (25), and KID of 0.005 is 5× lower than PECHead (0.025) and 4.6× lower than NHA (0.023). All other baselines (I'M Avatar, MegaPortraits, PointAvatar, NeRFace, INSTA) are clearly outperformed across every metric. The qualitative analysis further shows SVP excels at rendering fine details such as wrinkles, teeth, mouth interior, hair/beard, and eyeglasses where 3DMM-based methods struggle.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of work: 3DMM-based head avatar reconstruction (NeRFace, INSTA, NHA, PointAvatar) and large-scale 2D text-to-image diffusion models (Stable Diffusion). Rather than learning a 3D representation from scratch, it repurposes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion prior as the rendering engine, conditioned on 2D projections of a FLAME 3DMM via a modified ControlNet that ingests temporal mesh sequences. The key departure from prior 3D avatar methods is that reconstruction fidelity no longer depends on the accuracy of the 3D model—the diffusion prior hallucinates fine details (teeth, wrinkles, hair) that the 3DMM cannot represent. The face morphing capability, which leverages the text conditioning of the diffusion prior to shift identity at test time without fine-tuning, has no direct analogue in prior monocular avatar literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that a large 2D generative prior can serve as a high-fidelity renderer for 3D-controlled avatars, potentially reducing the need for expensive 3D reconstruction pipelines in telepresence and content-creation applications. The text-based face morphing without test-time fine-tuning opens a practical path for identity editing in avatar systems. However, the approach remains person-specific (requiring per-subject fine-tuning), the 3DMM cannot model tongue or hair dynamics, and 3D reconstruction errors still propagate into the output. Open questions include whether the temporal denoising trick generalizes to longer sequences or more complex motions, and whether the person-specific requirement can be relaxed toward a single-shot or few-shot regime.
