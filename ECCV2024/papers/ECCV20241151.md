## Problem

Existing Explicit Caption Editing (ECE) models are trained on carefully curated Ref-GT caption pairs with narrow edit distributions, causing them to fail on out-of-domain inputs such as captions with varying error severities, model-generated captions, or purely random word sequences. Additionally, prevailing ECE architectures rely on multi-stage sequential modules (e.g., first predict an operation, then predict the word), which slows inference and limits flexibility.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (encoded via CLIP into visual tokens) and a reference caption of arbitrary length as input, where the reference can range from a near-correct sentence to a fully random word sequence. It outputs, for each word position, a predicted edit operation (KEEP, DELETE, INSERT, or REPLACE) and a content word simultaneously via two parallel fully-connected heads; only content words corresponding to INSERT or REPLACE operations are applied to produce the next denoised caption. The time step t is injected as a sinusoidal embedding added to word tokens, and the process iterates over T denoising steps starting from an all-random-word sequence.

## Method

DECap reformulates ECE as a discrete diffusion process where the noising step applies Levenshtein edit operations (KEEP, DELETE, INSERT, REPLACE) to GT captions to synthesize noisy reference captions, parameterized by per-step probabilities α, β, γ over REPLACE, DELETE, and INSERT that also depend on GT caption length k. This edit-based noising eliminates the need for hand-curated Ref-GT pairs and exposes the model to a broad spectrum of editing scenarios during training. The denoising step uses a Transformer encoder with self-attention and co-attention layers over concatenated visual and word tokens, followed by two parallel FC heads that simultaneously predict the edit operation (4-way classification) and the content word (vocabulary softmax) for each position. Training minimizes a combined cross-entropy loss over both edit operations and content words (the latter only for INSERT/REPLACE positions), and inference iterates T denoising steps from a random-word sequence to produce the final caption. This parallel prediction design replaces the multi-stage sequential pipelines of prior ECE models, yielding roughly 2× faster inference while achieving stronger out-of-distribution generalization.

## Results

On the in-domain COCO-EE test set, DECap (4 steps) achieves BLEU-1 of 55.5, BLEU-4 of 23.3, CIDEr-D of 173.7, and SPICE of 29.8, outperforming TIger (4 steps) on all BLEU metrics while matching on CIDEr-D and SPICE, and running 2.2× faster (277 ms vs. 614 ms). With 6 editing steps, DECap surpasses TIger on every metric (CIDEr-D 177.0, SPICE 32.2, CLIP-Score 0.7522). As a captioning model on COCO, DECap (10 steps) reaches CIDEr-D 119.0 and SPICE 21.9, exceeding DDCap (20 steps: CIDEr-D 117.8, SPICE 21.7) with 5× fewer steps and 4.9× faster inference (676 ms vs. 3283 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TIger (the SOTA ECE model trained with paired Ref-GT data), DECap wins on all BLEU metrics at 4 steps (e.g., BLEU-1: 55.5 vs. 50.3) but trails slightly on CIDEr-D (173.7 vs. 176.7) and SPICE (29.8 vs. 31.4); with 6 steps DECap surpasses TIger on every metric while remaining 1.5× faster. Against TIger-N (TIger retrained on unpaired noised data), DECap dominates across all quality metrics (e.g., CIDEr-D 173.7 vs. 145.0, SPICE 29.8 vs. 21.8) and is 2.2× faster. In the captioning comparison (Table 4), DECap (10 steps) outperforms the discrete diffusion model DDCap (20 steps) on CIDEr-D (119.0 vs. 117.8) and SPICE (21.9 vs. 21.7) with 5× fewer steps and 4.9× faster inference. Against the continuous diffusion model SCD-Net (50 steps), DECap achieves comparable CIDEr-D (119.0 vs. 118.0) and slightly better SPICE (21.9 vs. 21.6) with 5× fewer steps, though SCD-Net retains a small BLEU-N advantage (BLEU-1: 79.0 vs. 78.0). Overall, DECap is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier editing quality, strong generalization to out-of-domain and random captions, and the fastest inference among all compared ECE and diffusion-based captioning models.

## Relation to Prior Work

DECap builds directly on the Explicit Caption Editing (ECE) task introduced by Wang et al. (TIger, ECCV 2022), which frames caption refinement as a sequence of Levenshtein edit operations (KEEP, DELETE, INSERT, REPLACE). It departs from TIger's multi-stage sequential architecture (predict operation → predict word in separate modules) by adopting a parallel Transformer encoder that generates both edit operations and content words simultaneously. The method also draws on discrete diffusion models for text (Austin et al., DDCap), but replaces the standard [MASK]-based noising with an edit-based noising process that parameterizes word-level corruption by edit-type distributions, eliminating the need for carefully curated reference–ground-truth caption pairs. This positions DECap at the intersection of structured text editing and diffusion-based generation, unifying both under a single iterative denoising framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and practically important limitation: state-of-the-art ECE models like TIger degrade caption quality on out-of-domain reference captions (e.g., model-generated or heavily corrupted inputs), restricting their real-world utility. DECap's diffusion-based reformulation addresses this by learning a broader distribution over input captions during training, and the resulting model doubles as a competitive captioning system (CIDEr-D 119.0 vs. DDCap's 117.8) and a word-level controllable captioning tool. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or longer-form text, whether the controllable captioning capability (currently preliminary) can be made more systematic, and whether the edit-type distribution hyperparameters generalize across domains without re-tuning.
