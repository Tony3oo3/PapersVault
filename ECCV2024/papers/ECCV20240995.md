## Problem

Existing listener motion generation methods treat the listener as a purely reactive agent responding to the speaker's voice and facial motions, failing to capture the bidirectional, concurrent nature of real dyadic interactions. Prior approaches like L2L are unidirectional and do not model the mutual influence between speaker and listener behaviors, while regression-based methods like RLHG produce overly smooth and deterministic responses that lack the non-deterministic diversity of natural human reactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240995/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes speaker EMOCA 3DMM parameters (expression and pose codes) and HuBERT audio features as input, with the audio features fed directly into the decoder rather than the encoder since HuBERT already provides robust pre-trained speech representations. Output is structured as discrete VQ-VAE latent codes that are decoded into continuous EMOCA expression and pose parameters, which can then drive PIRenderer to produce photorealistic listener video from a single reference portrait image. The same framework, when fine-tuned for speaker generation (DIM-SPEAKER), takes only speaker speech and produces speaker facial motion in the same EMOCA parameter space.

## Method

DIM introduces a self-supervised pre-training strategy on the large-scale CANDOR dyadic conversation corpus that jointly models speaker and listener motions through masked reconstruction and contrastive learning. Two separate VQ-VAEs encode speaker and listener motions into discrete latent codes; during pre-training, p% of frames are randomly masked for both roles, and role-specific Transformer encoders process the masked inputs. A contrastive loss aligns temporally mean-pooled speaker and listener representations, while a joint Transformer encoder fuses the concatenated features and a joint decoder predicts the masked discrete codes conditioned on the other role's features plus audio. Unlike L2L, the VQ-Decoder is integrated into the prediction pipeline and jointly optimized rather than frozen, enabling end-to-end refinement of both discrete and continuous motion. For listener generation, the model is fine-tuned with speaker input unmasked and listener input fully masked, training only the listener reconstruction loss while keeping the listener VQ-Encoder frozen.

## Results

On the LM_Listener dataset, DIM-LISTENER achieves a Fréchet Distance (FD) of 12.49 for expressions, roughly 2× better than L2L (23.56) and 3× better than RLHG (30.50), while also attaining the lowest MSE (0.29) and highest diversity scores (SID 4.53, Var 0.94). On ViCo, DIM-LISTENER reduces FD to 23.88 versus 33.93 for L2L and 39.02 for RLHG. For speech-driven speaker generation on BiWi, DIM-SPEAKER achieves LVE of 3.86×10⁻⁴ and FDD of 0.98×10⁻⁴, outperforming FaceFormer (5.40/1.37), CodeTalker (5.93/2.42), and MeshTalk (6.14/2.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIM-LISTENER decisively outperforms all listener-generation baselines on both ViCo and LM_Listener in terms of distributional realism (FD, P-FD) and accuracy (MSE). On LM_Listener, the FD gap is particularly large: 12.49 vs. 23.56 for L2L (≈47% reduction) and 30.50 for RLHG (≈59% reduction). Pre-training L2L on CANDOR (L2L†) narrows the gap somewhat (21.63) but still trails DIM-LISTENER by nearly half, confirming that the gains stem from the dyadic modeling architecture rather than additional data alone. On diversity metrics (SID, Var), DIM-LISTENER matches or exceeds ground-truth levels while maintaining low FD, whereas ELP achieves high SID (5.07) at the cost of higher MSE (0.45) and lower Var (0.49). For speaker generation on BiWi, DIM-SPEAKER beats FaceFormer by 28% in LVE (3.86 vs. 5.40) and by 28% in FDD (0.98 vs. 1.37), and outperforms MeshTalk and CodeTalker by wider margins on both metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the listener motion generation line of work (L2L, ELP, RLHG) by introducing a dyadic pre-training strategy that jointly models speaker and listener behaviors rather than treating the listener as a purely reactive agent. It builds on the VQ-VAE discrete motion representation from L2L and ELP, but departs by integrating the VQ-Decoder into the prediction pipeline (rather than freezing it) and by adding a joint Transformer encoder with contrastive learning to capture bidirectional speaker-listener context. The pre-training on the large-scale CANDOR corpus (850+ hours of dyadic conversation) followed by task-specific fine-tuning draws on the masked-modeling paradigm from NLP and self-supervised speech (HuBERT), applied here to 3DMM facial motion sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dyadic pre-training paradigm offers a principled way to move beyond unidirectional reactive models toward more naturalistic social behavior generation, with the added benefit of enabling speaker motion generation from the same framework. However, the ablation revealing that the contrastive loss (the component most directly tied to the 'dyadic' narrative) is the least important component somewhat weakens the conceptual story, suggesting that the gains come primarily from the joint architecture and VQ-VAE integration rather than explicit cross-modal alignment. Open questions include generalization to multi-party interactions, real-time inference, full-body motion, and whether the person-specific rendering pipeline (PIRenderer per speaker-listener pair) limits practical deployment.
