## Problem

Long-range atmospheric turbulence (1–13 km) produces severe geometric distortions that existing registration-based or decomposition-based methods cannot handle effectively, as they rely on either a zero-mean averaging assumption or direct matrix decomposition that breaks down under large pixel displacements. Additionally, no large-scale real-world benchmark exists for long-range turbulence, with prior datasets limited to hot-air turbulence or at most 4 km sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241121/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static-scene turbulence-distorted video sequence (approximately 800 frames at 1920×1080 resolution, captured at 30 fps) and produces a single turbulence-mitigated, deblurred restored frame. The pipeline is inherently multi-frame: it first registers all frames to a constructed reference, then refines the aligned sequence via tensor decomposition, and finally applies a lightweight CNN to remove residual blur. The method assumes static scenes and does not model object or camera motion.

## Method

CDSP is a coarse-to-fine framework that cooperates a dynamic turbulence prior with a static background prior. In the coarse stage, a Frequency-aware Reference Frame (FRF) is constructed by exploiting the discovered pixel-motion statistical prior (displacements follow a zero-mean Gaussian), weighting each pixel intensity at a position by an exponential function of its temporal frequency to produce a sharper reference than naive temporal averaging; distorted frames are then aligned to this reference via optical-flow-based registration. In the fine stage, a Subspace-based Low-Rank Tensor Refinement (SLRTR) model decomposes the registered 3-D tensor into a low-rank background (exploiting joint global temporal subspace and non-local spatial self-similarity via tensor nuclear norm), a sparse registration-error term (L1), and noise, solved by alternating minimization. A final supervised CNN removes residual blur using paired data generated by the distortion-correction pipeline itself. The two stages are complementary: FRF reduces the magnitude of residual misalignment, while SLRTR corrects the remaining errors without losing detail.

## Results

On the synthetic turbulence benchmark (ADE20K + P2S simulator), CDSP achieves the best PSNR and SSIM across all tested distances (2–8 Km), outperforming the second-best method by 0.67 dB (PSNR) at 2 Km and 0.89 dB at 8 Km, with the gap widening as distortion severity increases. At 8 Km, CDSP reaches 24.84 dB PSNR and 0.8319 SSIM, surpassing the best static-scene-independent method (TurbRecon, 23.95 dB) and the best static-scene-dependent method (NDL, 22.76 dB). On the proposed RLR-AT real-world dataset (1–13 Km), qualitative comparisons confirm CDSP consistently produces sharper, more aligned results than all nine baselines, particularly at longer distances where other methods exhibit persistent misalignments or detail loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDSP outperforms all nine baselines across every distance (2–8 Km) on both PSNR and SSIM. Among static-scene-independent methods, TurbRecon [37] is the strongest competitor (e.g., 23.95 dB at 8 Km), but CDSP still leads by 0.89 dB, with the margin growing from 0.67 dB at 2 Km to 0.89 dB at 8 Km as distortions become more severe. Among static-scene-dependent methods, NDL [58] is the best (22.76 dB at 8 Km), trailing CDSP by 2.08 dB. Single-frame methods (TurbNet, PiRN) lag the most, losing 4–7 dB to CDSP at 8 Km due to their inability to exploit temporal information. Supervised deep-learning methods (TSR-WGAN, TMT) suffer from domain gap between synthetic training and real turbulence, producing visible residual distortions. CLEAR, SG, and NDIR all produce artifacts or misalignments at longer distances, confirming that neither pure registration nor pure decomposition alone suffices for severe long-range turbulence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physics-based multi-stage restoration framework of Zhu et al. [58] (distortion correction followed by deblurring) by replacing the standard temporal-averaging reference frame with a frequency-aware one, and by upgrading the refinement step from 2-D matrix decomposition to a 3-D subspace-based low-rank tensor model that preserves spatio-temporal structure. The tensor formulation draws directly on the non-local sub-cubic clustering and tensor nuclear norm machinery of Chang et al. [9] (CVPR 2017), originally developed for multispectral denoising. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work by unifying the dynamic-turbulence prior (pixel-motion statistics) and the static-background prior (low-rank structure) within a single coarse-to-fine pipeline, whereas earlier methods relied on only one of the two.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RLR-AT benchmark (1500 sequences, 1–13 km, 1920×1080) fills a critical gap: all prior real-world turbulence datasets are dominated by hot-air or sub-kilometer captures, leaving the long-range, severe-distortion regime essentially unbenchmarked. The finding that the performance gap over SOTA widens monotonically with distance (0.67 dB at 2 km → 0.89 dB at 8 km) validates that existing methods fundamentally break down under long-range conditions rather than merely underperforming. A key open question is dynamic-scene turbulence, where object motion couples with atmospheric distortion; the authors acknowledge CDSP fails on moving regions and leave this for future work. The frequency-aware reference frame is a simple, training-free component that could be dropped into other registration-based pipelines, making it broadly reusable.
