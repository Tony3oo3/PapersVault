## Problem

While large language models excel at multi-turn dialogue with contextual memory, this conversational capability remains unexplored in human motion generative models. Existing text-to-motion and motion-language methods treat all tasks as one-turn conditioned generation, lacking the ability to maintain context across turns or produce temporally continuous motion sequences that resemble natural human movement. This limits their applicability to embodied systems like humanoid robotics and game agents that require step-by-step, context-aware motion control.

## Input / Output

MotionChain accepts multi-turn conversations (up to 10 turns) where each turn can include text instructions, RGB images (as first-frame or last-frame pose conditions), and/or motion sequences. All modalities are encoded into a single unified token vocabulary: motion is discretized via VQ-VAE into a 512-entry codebook with temporal downsampling rate 4, images are projected through a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder plus a linear layer into the language embedding space, and text uses SentencePiece subword tokens. The model autoregressively generates output tokens that can be either motion tokens (decoded back to SMPL joint velocities, positions, and rotations) or text tokens, with a special end-of-sentence flag controlling when generation stops.

## Method

MotionChain integrates three components: a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer (512×1024 codebook, 1D convolutions with l=4 downsampling) that builds a discrete motion vocabulary, a vision tokenizer (frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 + trainable linear projection) that maps images into the language token embedding space, and a Flan-T5-base language model operating over the merged text-motion vocabulary. Training follows a 3-stage strategy: (1) pre-train the motion tokenizer, (2) freeze it and pre-train the language model on single-turn modality translation tasks (text-to-motion, motion-to-text, image-conditioned motion generation), and (3) instruction-tune on a constructed multi-turn conversation dataset built with ChatGPT and TMR for motion reasoning and editing. The key mechanism for temporal continuity is the 'tokens-joint' composition strategy, where motion tokens from consecutive turns are concatenated before being decoded by the motion decoder, ensuring smooth transitions between actions without explicit boundary conditioning.

## Results

On the motion reasoning task (Table 1), MotionChain (573M params) achieves Bleu@1 of 37.92, Rouge of 38.05, Cider of 24.53, and BertScore of 32.24, substantially outperforming all text-only LLM baselines including Vicuna-1.5-13b (BertScore 18.00) and Flan-T5-xl (BertScore 18.34). On temporal motion composition (Table 2), MotionChain reduces MPJPE to 276.05 mm and PA-MPJPE to 53.72 mm, compared to TEACH's 979.21 mm and 933.32 mm respectively, while also achieving lower acceleration error (ACCL 7.11 vs. 23.02). The ablation on motion composition variants (Table 3) confirms that the tokens-joint decoding strategy yields the best reconstruction quality (MPJPE 108.77, PA-MPJPE 18.85, ACCL 2.26) over independent and past-conditioned alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionChain (573M) decisively outperforms all text-only LLM baselines on motion reasoning. Against Flan-T5 variants [14], MotionChain achieves roughly 3× higher Bleu@1 (37.92 vs. 4.64–12.18) and 2× higher BertScore (32.24 vs. 3.45–18.34), despite being smaller than Flan-T5-xl (3B). Against Llama-2-7b [94], MotionChain surpasses it on every metric (BertScore 32.24 vs. 6.81, Cider 24.53 vs. 1.04), while also producing far more concise answers (22 words vs. 131 words). Against Vicuna-1.5-13b [122], the strongest LLM baseline, MotionChain still leads by a wide margin on all metrics (BertScore 32.24 vs. 18.00, Bleu@1 37.92 vs. 17.20), demonstrating that motion perception provides a substantial advantage over pure language modeling. On temporal composition [3], MotionChain reduces MPJPE by 72% (276.05 vs. 979.21 mm) and PA-MPJPE by 94% (53.72 vs. 933.32 mm) relative to TEACH, with ACCL also improved from 23.02 to 7.11, though MotionChain generates more diverse (and thus less conservative) outputs (Diversity 43.25 vs. 27.11).

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionChain extends the unified motion-language vocabulary paradigm introduced by MotionGPT, which first treated human motion as a 'foreign language' with discrete VQ-VAE tokens alongside text tokens. It departs from prior text-to-motion and motion-composition methods (TEMOS, MDM, T2M-GPT, TEACH) that handle only single-turn or fixed-sequence generation, by introducing multi-turn conversational context and continuous cross-turn motion generation. The vision integration follows the visual instruction-tuning recipe from BLIP-2/LLaVA, connecting a frozen CLIP ViT encoder to a language model via a lightweight projection, while the motion tokenizer architecture is inherited from the VQ-VAE lineage of TM2T and T2M-GPT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to unify multi-turn conversational control with continuous 3D human motion generation, enabling step-by-step task execution relevant to humanoid robotics and interactive game agents. Key limitations include restriction to articulated bodies (no face or hands), absence of collision or human-object interaction signals, and reliance on stochastic generation rather than deterministic control. Open questions include whether the token-level composition strategy scales to very long multi-turn sequences, how to incorporate physical constraints, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively small HumanML3D/BABEL datasets used here.
