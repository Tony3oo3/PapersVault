## Problem

Existing HSI denoising methods face a dilemma between effectiveness and flexibility in capturing long-range spectral dependencies: CNNs are limited by short spectral receptive fields, while transformers require fixed spectral band counts and dataset-specific training. Additionally, mainstream convolutional approaches apply shared filters across the entire spatial-spectral domain without region discrimination, introducing negative interference from semantically unrelated regions during feature extraction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241601/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy hyperspectral image of shape H×W×B with an arbitrary number of spectral bands B and produces the corresponding denoised HSI. A key distinction is that RAS2S is trained once on ICVL (31 bands) and directly generalizes to test datasets with different spectral counts (e.g., WDC Mall with 200+ bands) without any fine-tuning or architectural modification. The decoder generates bands autoregressively, conditioning each output band on previously reconstructed bands and context features from the encoder.

## Method

RAS2S reformulates HSI denoising as a sequence-to-sequence translation problem, treating the noisy spectral band sequence as input and the clean sequence as output. The encoder uses a bidirectional ConvGRU to propagate hidden states across all spectral bands, with a Hidden State Compensation (HFComp) module that applies cross-attention between the current band's features and the previous hidden state to bridge the information gap. A Spatial-Spectral Region-Aware (SSRA) module generates spatial masks via SpecDynConv (spectrally-calibrated dynamic convolution) and spectral masks via SpecClassifier (linear layer), then assigns dedicated learnable filters to each predicted semantic region to eliminate cross-region interference. The decoder employs an Adjacent Attention Mechanism (AAM) using 3D convolution to compute attention weights over 2n+1 neighboring encoded hidden states, and generates each denoised band autoregressively with residual learning. The entire model contains only 0.08M parameters, substantially smaller than competing transformer-based methods.

## Results

Under mixture noise, RAS2S achieves 41.33 dB PSNR on ICVL, 39.03 dB on CAVE, 36.84 dB on Pavia Centre, and 40.92 dB on WDC Mall, outperforming the strongest baseline HSDT-S by 0.57, 0.51, 2.59, and 3.13 dB respectively. On the KAIST dataset under the same noise, RAS2S reaches 39.48 dB PSNR, surpassing HSDT-S (38.31 dB) and SERT (35.71 dB). On the real-world RealHSI dataset, RAS2S attains 27.39 dB PSNR with only 0.08M parameters (4.18% of SERT's 1.91M), while also achieving the lowest SAM (0.0535). The model is trained solely on ICVL and directly generalizes to datasets with different spectral band counts without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAS2S outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among the closest competitors, HSDT-S (ICCV 2023) is the strongest baseline, trailing RAS2S by 0.57 dB on ICVL, 0.51 dB on CAVE, 2.59 dB on Pavia Centre, and 3.13 dB on WDC under mixture noise; the gap widens substantially on remote-sensing datasets with more spectral bands. SERT (CVPR 2023) and SST (AAAI 2023), the other transformer-based methods, lag further behind by 0.89–9.59 dB depending on dataset. Traditional low-rank/TV methods (LLRT, LRTDTV, LLRGTV, NGMeet) are outperformed by 6–25 dB, confirming the advantage of learned approaches. CNN-based methods (QRNN3D, GRNet, MAC-Net) and the RNN-based GRUNet are also consistently surpassed, with QRNN3D being the strongest CNN baseline but still 2.11 dB below RAS2S on ICVL. Notably, RAS2S achieves these gains with only 0.08M parameters—roughly 4% of SERT's 1.91M and 1.6× smaller than HSDT-S's 0.13M—while also running faster on CPU (8.16 s vs. 17.49 s for HSDT-S on CAVE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Seq2Seq paradigm (Sutskever et al., NeurIPS 2014) from NLP to hyperspectral denoising, treating spectral bands as a sequence to be translated from noisy to clean in an autoregressive fashion. It adapts dynamic region-aware convolution (DRConv, Chen et al., CVPR 2021) by replacing its local spatial semantics with HSI-specific spatial-spectral mask prediction (SpecDynConv + SpecClassifier) to allocate dedicated filters to semantically distinct regions. Conceptually, it departs from both CNN-based methods (limited spectral receptive field) and transformer-based methods (fixed band count, dataset-specific training) by offering a flexible, parameter-efficient serial architecture that handles arbitrary spectral lengths without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.08M parameter count (roughly 50× smaller than SST, 24× smaller than SERT) while achieving SOTA across seven datasets and five noise types makes this practically relevant for resource-constrained HSI deployment. The flexibility to handle arbitrary spectral band numbers without dataset-specific training addresses a real limitation of current transformer-based denoisers. Open questions include whether the autoregressive generation bottleneck becomes prohibitive at very high spatial resolutions, whether the region-aware module generalizes beyond the tested r=2–3 region count, and whether the Seq2Seq framing extends to other sequential spectral tasks (e.g., compression, super-resolution).
