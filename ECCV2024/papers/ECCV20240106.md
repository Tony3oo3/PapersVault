## Problem

Camera trajectory generation for cinematography has been limited by reliance on synthetic data with small vocabularies, character-centric coordinate systems that constrain generation diversity, and the absence of proper evaluation metrics. No existing dataset provides both camera and character trajectories from real movies paired with rich textual descriptions, making it difficult to train models that capture the correlation between camera movement and character motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a character trajectory (3D hip-center positions over up to 300 frames, with masking for shorter sequences) and a free-form textual caption describing the desired camera motion relative to the character. It outputs a camera trajectory as a sequence of 6D poses, where each pose combines a 6D continuous rotation representation with a 3D translation, all expressed in a global coordinate system rather than a character-relative frame. This global representation is a key distinction from prior work like CCD, which constrains the camera to character-centric coordinates.

## Method

DIRECTOR is a diffusion-based camera trajectory generator built on the EDM framework with a pre-norm Transformer backbone (8 layers, 512 hidden dim, 16 heads). It conditions on both the character trajectory and a textual caption through three interchangeable architectures inspired by DiT variants: in-context token concatenation (A), AdaLN modulation of layer norms (B), and cross-attention over the full conditioning sequence (C). The model is trained with denoising score matching loss and sampled using 2nd-order deterministic sampling with classifier-free guidance. For evaluation, the authors introduce CLaTr, a contrastive language-trajectory embedding trained in a CLIP-like VAE framework on the E.T. dataset, enabling FID-style trajectory quality metrics (FD_CLaTr) and text-trajectory coherence scores (CLaTr-Score) that replace the oversimplified six-class classifier used by prior work.

## Results

On the E.T. mixed trajectory subset (the harder compositional setting), DIRECTOR C achieves the best FD_CLaTr of 3.76, outperforming MDM (6.79) by 3.0 points and CCD (35.81) by 32.1 points. In text-camera coherence, DIRECTOR C scores 21.95 CLaTr-Score, exceeding MDM (18.32) by +3.6 and CCD (6.26) by +15.7. On the pure trajectory subset, DIRECTOR C attains FD_CLaTr of 4.57 and C-F1 of 0.80, again leading both baselines across all quality and coherence metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIRECTOR (all three variants) consistently outperforms both baselines across every metric on both the pure and mixed trajectory subsets. Against CCD [21] (the prior state-of-the-art camera diffusion model), DIRECTOR C achieves a 32.1-point lower FD_CLaTr (3.76 vs. 35.81) and a 15.7-point higher CLaTr-Score (21.95 vs. 6.26) on the mixed subset, reflecting CCD's reliance on a character-centric coordinate system and a 48-word vocabulary that severely limits its generative capacity. Against MDM [39] (a strong human-motion diffusion baseline), DIRECTOR C improves FD_CLaTr by 3.0 points (3.76 vs. 6.79) and CLaTr-Score by 3.6 points (21.95 vs. 18.32) on the mixed subset, demonstrating that character-aware conditioning in a global coordinate system yields meaningfully better camera trajectories. Notably, DIRECTOR B achieves the highest CLaTr-Score (23.10) on the pure subset but degrades on the mixed subset, while DIRECTOR C's cross-attention mechanism provides the most robust performance across both settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIRECTOR builds on the EDM diffusion framework and the DiT transformer architecture, adapting three conditioning strategies (in-context, AdaLN, cross-attention) from image generation to the camera trajectory domain. It departs from the concurrent CCD work [21] by replacing the character-centric relative coordinate system with a global one and by conditioning on both character trajectories and rich textual captions rather than a 48-word synthetic vocabulary. The CLaTr evaluation embedding extends the contrastive VAE methodology from human motion (TMR [32]) and the CLIP paradigm [35] to the text-to-trajectory setting, filling a gap where no standard metric existed for camera trajectory generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The E.T. dataset is the first to pair real-movie camera trajectories with character trajectories and natural-language captions, opening the door to character-aware cinematography tools that were previously infeasible with synthetic or real-estate-only data. CLaTr provides the community with a principled FID-like metric for trajectory generation, addressing a long-standing evaluation gap. Limitations include auto-generated captions (via Mistral-7B) that may not capture all cinematic nuance, a 300-frame cap, and single-character focus; the authors note future work on richer modifiers and on-screen positioning. For practitioners, the dataset and metric are likely to become reference resources even if the DiT-based generator itself is superseded.
