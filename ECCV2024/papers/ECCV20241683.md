## Problem

Thermal cameras enable robust perception in low-light and adverse weather conditions critical for field robotics, yet no publicly available dataset captures synchronized RGB-thermal-GPS-IMU data from aerial platforms in natural environments such as rivers, forests, and coastlines. Existing thermal benchmarks target urban autonomous driving, leaving a domain gap that prevents field robots from reliably operating at night or in challenging terrain without extensive, location-specific data collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241683/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 16-bit thermal frames (640×512, 75° FoV, 60 Hz) paired with RGB images (960×600, 30 Hz), 200 Hz IMU, and 5 Hz GPS, captured at 20°–45° oblique angles from <120 m altitude or at ground level. Thermal images are normalized via 1st–99th percentile rescaling followed by CLAHE, and RGB-T pairs are stereo-rectified and projected to RGB resolution. Outputs include per-pixel semantic masks over 10 natural-scene classes, RGB-to-thermal translated images, and absolute trajectory estimates (ATE in meters) for VIO/SLAM evaluation.

## Method

The paper introduces a custom sensor stack (FLIR ADK thermal, dual FLIR Blackfly RGB, VectorNav VN100 IMU, u-blox M8N GPS) rigidly mounted on an Aurelia X6 hexacopter, used to capture 37 aerial and ground trajectories across Southern California, Kentucky, and North Carolina. Four benchmarks are defined with distinct splits: a general random split (75:12.5:12.5), a temporal split isolating daytime training from twilight/nighttime testing to probe thermal inversion, and two geographical splits (terrain-based and region-based) to stress intra-class domain shift. Baselines span 20+ segmentation architectures (CNNs, ViTs, foundation models like DINOv2 and CLIP-pretrained ConvNext), seven RGB-T image translation methods (paired/unpaired GANs and diffusion), two VIO/SLAM systems (VINS-Fusion, OpenVINS), and zero-shot vision-language models (SAM, Grounded SAM, ODISE, OV-Seg). Key findings include that lightweight models like EfficientViT-B0 achieve near-best mIoU at 50 Hz on Jetson AGX Orin, that geographic and temporal domain shifts cause severe performance drops (mIoU falling below 0.25), and that thermal VIO/SLAM suffers from feature sparsity in water-dominated scenes.

## Results

On the thermal semantic segmentation benchmark (general split), EfficientViT-B0 achieves the best mIoU of 0.725 at 51.2 FPS on Jetson AGX Orin, outperforming the fastest baseline FastSCNN (0.690 mIoU, 124.2 FPS) by only 0.027 mIoU at 130× fewer FLOPs. For RGB-T semantic segmentation, CMNeXt achieves the highest mIoU of 0.740, surpassing the thermal-only baseline (0.661) by 0.079. For RGB-to-thermal image translation, VQGAN achieves the best thermal mIoU of 0.388, while Pix2Pix leads in PSNR (20.06 dB). For VIO/SLAM, VINS-Fusion in SLAM mode outperforms Open-VINS across most sequences, with RGB consistently outperforming thermal except in specific high-contrast thermal scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 20 thermal segmentation baselines, EfficientViT-B0 (0.725 mIoU) wins overall, beating the next-best ResNet18/ResNet50/ResNetXi50 (all 0.713) by 0.012 mIoU while running 2–3× faster on GPU. Foundation models (frozen DINOv2 at 0.686–0.704, frozen ConvNext-B CLIP at 0.717) do not surpass end-to-end trained lightweight networks, and the thermal-specific FTNet (0.613) underperforms all general-purpose models, suggesting its edge priors are ineffective in unstructured natural scenes. For RGB-T segmentation, CMNeXt (0.740) beats CRM (0.714) and EAEFNet (0.692), and all RGB-T methods outperform the thermal-only baseline (0.661) by 0.03–0.08 mIoU, confirming RGB's value for distinguishing land-based stuff classes. In image translation, paired GANs (VQGAN mIoU 0.388, Pix2PixHD 0.379, Pix2Pix 0.354) substantially outperform unpaired GANs (UNIT 0.189, MUNIT 0.173) and the diffusion-based Palette (0.194), indicating that paired supervision remains critical for thermal translation quality. For VIO/SLAM, VINS-Fusion in SLAM mode achieves lower ATE than Open-VINS on most sequences (e.g., 2.88 m vs. 22.8 m on North Field N1), and RGB consistently outperforms thermal except on the Castaic Lake C2 sequence where thermal contrast of stone grains gives VINS-Fusion thermal (1.513 m) an edge over RGB (—, failed).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the aerial RGB-T dataset from Lee et al. (IROS 2023), which provided only 1,272 single-class (water) annotations, to a 10-class, 4,195-sample dataset spanning rivers, lakes, coastlines, deserts, and forests. It deliberately departs from the dominant thermal/RGB-T dataset literature, which targets urban autonomous driving (KAIST, FLIR ADAS, HIT-UAV) or fixed-camera surveillance, by focusing on natural littoral and wilderness environments typical of field robotics. The multi-task benchmark design (segmentation, image translation, VIO/SLAM) also broadens the scope beyond prior thermal datasets that typically support only one or two tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a critical gap for field robotics operating at night or in adverse weather, where thermal is the primary viable sensor. The geographic and temporal split experiments reveal that current segmentation models suffer near-total failure (mIoU dropping from ~0.65 to ~0.08) when transferred across regions or from day to night, highlighting that thermal domain adaptation remains largely unsolved for natural environments. However, the dataset's modest size (4,195 annotated frames) and the limited number of geographic regions (three US states) constrain the generalizability of these findings, and the authors themselves note that a much larger, more diverse collection would be needed to draw firmer conclusions. Open questions include whether unsupervised domain adaptation or synthetic thermal generation (via image translation) can bridge these gaps without requiring new labeled data at every deployment site.
