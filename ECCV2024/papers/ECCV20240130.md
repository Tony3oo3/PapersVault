## Problem

Existing diffusion-based text rendering methods suffer from three interrelated limitations: they require users to manually specify keywords, glyph images, or character-level masks (reducing automation and flexibility); they produce poor multi-line layouts due to weak layout prediction; and character-level positional guidance constrains the diversity of generated text styles. TextDiffuser-2 addresses all three by leveraging language models for both layout planning and layout encoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240130/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language prompt (optionally with keywords marked in quotation marks) and produces a 512×512 RGB image with rendered text. For text inpainting, it additionally accepts a source image and only the text position/content from the inpaint region as conditions. The layout planner outputs text lines as coordinate quadruples (top-left and bottom-right corners, normalized to 0–128), which are then encoded as discrete tokens alongside the prompt for the diffusion model.

## Method

TextDiffuser-2 employs two language models in a two-stage pipeline. In stage 1, a Vicuna-7B model (M1) is fine-tuned on 5k caption-OCR pairs from MARIO-10M with cross-entropy loss to act as a layout planner: it infers keywords from the prompt (or uses user-specified ones) and predicts bounding box coordinates for each text line in a language format. In stage 2, the CLIP text encoder (M2) within SD 1.5 is repurposed as a layout encoder that consumes a hybrid-granularity token sequence—BPE tokens for the prompt followed by character-level tokens and 256 new coordinate tokens for each keyword—trained end-to-end with L2 denoising loss. The key design choice is using line-level bounding boxes rather than character-level segmentation masks, which preserves style diversity while maintaining rendering accuracy. The layout planner is also shown to be a drop-in replacement for the Layout Transformer in TextDiffuser or a plug-in for methods like GlyphControl and Brush Your Text, boosting their F-measure by up to 6.51%.

## Results

On the MARIO-Eval benchmark for text-to-image generation, TextDiffuser-2 achieves the best FID (33.66) and OCR accuracy (57.58%) among all methods requiring no additional conditions, while maintaining a competitive OCR F-measure of 75.06. When the proposed layout planner (LP) and layout encoding (LE) components are applied to existing methods, TextDiffuser's OCR F-measure improves from 78.24 to 87.30 (+1.60% with LE alone), and GlyphControl's OCR accuracy jumps by 12.65% (32.56→45.21). For text inpainting, TextDiffuser-2 achieves 76.42% OCR accuracy, outperforming DiffSTE (72.25%) on 10,000 test cases from MARIO-10M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextDiffuser-2 outperforms all general-purpose diffusion baselines (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, SD-XL, StableCascade, Deepfloyd, PixArt-α) by a wide margin on OCR accuracy (57.58% vs. ≤2.62%) and FID (33.66 vs. ≥34.90), confirming that dedicated text-rendering architectures are still necessary. Against the closest competitor TextDiffuser, TextDiffuser-2 achieves a lower FID (33.66 vs. 38.76) and higher OCR accuracy (57.58% vs. 56.09%), but a slightly lower OCR F-measure (75.06 vs. 78.24), which the authors attribute to TextDiffuser's use of a single standard font that reduces rendering complexity at the cost of style diversity. The most striking result is that the proposed layout planner alone boosts TextDiffuser's OCR F-measure from 78.24 to 85.70, and adding layout encoding pushes it to 87.30, demonstrating that the components are broadly applicable. For GlyphControl, the LP+LE combination improves OCR accuracy by 12.65 percentage points (32.56→45.21) and reduces FID by 7.60, while AnyText+LP, ControlNet+LP, and Brush Your Text+LP all show substantial gains over their unassisted versions. In text inpainting, TextDiffuser-2 surpasses DiffSTE by 4.17 percentage points in OCR accuracy (76.42% vs. 72.25%) while also producing more visually coherent results without requiring per-character segmentation masks.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextDiffuser-2 builds directly on TextDiffuser (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2023), inheriting its MARIO-10M training data and overall diffusion-based text rendering framework, while replacing the Layout Transformer with a fine-tuned Vicuna-7B language model for layout planning. It also draws on the broader line of LLM-based layout generation (LayoutGPT, LayoutPrompter, Pix2Seq) and the character-aware tokenization insight from ByT5-based work. The key conceptual departure is from character-level segmentation masks (TextDiffuser) and glyph-image conditions (GlyphControl): TextDiffuser-2 encodes text position and content at the line level through the diffusion model's own CLIP text encoder using hybrid-granularity tokens, trading tight positional control for greater stylistic diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses three practical pain points in visual text rendering—manual keyword specification, poor multi-line layout quality, and constrained font diversity—by making the pipeline fully prompt-driven and compatible as a plug-in for existing methods (GlyphControl, Brush Your Text, ControlNet). The 6.51% F-measure boost for GlyphControl and the elimination of user-specified glyph images have direct practical value for production text-to-image systems. However, the OCR F-measure (75.06) is slightly below TextDiffuser's (78.24), reflecting a deliberate accuracy-for-diversity trade-off, and the method currently lacks text layering, a common requirement in graphic design. Open questions include scaling beyond 512×512, multilingual rendering, and whether the line-level encoding approach generalizes to more complex typographic compositions.
