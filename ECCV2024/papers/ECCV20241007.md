## Problem

In-context segmentation aims to segment novel images from a few labeled examples, but prior approaches either rely on implicit modeling with ad-hoc mosaic designs (Painter, SegGPT) or heavy two-stage pipelines that depend on SAM for prompt generation (PerSAM, Matcher). No existing method provides a simple, end-to-end framework that explicitly exploits the dense correspondences emerging in vision foundation models to transfer segmentation knowledge from in-context samples to the target.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image (cropped/padded to 896×896), K reference images each paired with a binary mask, and optional meta text (class name or task description). The frozen VFM processes both target and reference images to produce patch-level features; the reference masks are consumed only through correspondence-based propagation and mask pooling rather than being fed directly into the network. The output is a single binary segmentation mask for the target image, produced by a lightweight query-based decoder with one learnable query.

## Method

SEGIC builds on a single frozen vision foundation model (DINOv2 by default) followed by a lightweight query-based mask decoder, with only the decoder parameters trained. The core mechanism computes dense pixel-level correspondences between target and reference images via cosine distance on patch features, upsampled to full resolution. From these correspondences and the reference mask, three explicit in-context instructions are extracted: a geometric instruction (propagated coarse mask plus top-k points with cosine positional encoding), a visual instruction (mask-pooled reference features capturing appearance and semantics), and a meta instruction (CLIP text encoding of class/task descriptions). Spatial instructions are added to the target image features while non-spatial instructions are concatenated with the decoder query, enabling the decoder to segment conditioned on all three instruction types simultaneously. Training uses a frozen backbone with dice + BCE loss on importance-sampled points, augmented by context reversion (swapping target/reference) and negative entity augmentation for robustness to noisy in-context samples.

## Results

SEGIC (DINOv2-G backbone) achieves 74.5 mean mIoU on COCO-20^i, 88.4 mIoU on FSS-1000, and 47.8 mean mIoU on LVIS-92^i, setting new state-of-the-art on all three one-shot segmentation benchmarks. On zero-shot video object segmentation, SEGIC reaches 73.7 J&F on DAVIS-17 and 65.4 G on YouTube-VOS-18 without any VOS training data. For generic semantic segmentation, SEGIC attains 74.0 mIoU on COCO and 59.0 mIoU on ADE20k, surpassing specialist models like Mask2Former (67.4/56.1) and MaskDINO (56.6). The method uses only 5M trainable parameters with a frozen backbone, requiring less than 10 GB of GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEGIC dominates all in-context generalist baselines on one-shot segmentation: it outperforms SegGPT (56.1) by 20 mIoU on COCO-20^i, Matcher (33.0) by 14.8 mIoU on LVIS-92^i, and Matcher (87.0) by 1.4 mIoU on FSS-1000. On zero-shot VOS, SEGIC (73.7 J&F) trails Matcher (79.5) and SegGPT (75.6) on DAVIS-17, and falls short of SegGPT (74.7) on YouTube-VOS-18, though it still surpasses Painter (34.6/24.1) and PerSAM (60.3) by large margins. For generic semantic segmentation, SEGIC (74.0/59.0 mIoU) exceeds specialist models Mask2Former (67.4/56.1), MaskDINO (56.6), and OneFormer (67.4/57.7) on COCO and ADE20k respectively. On open-vocabulary segmentation, SEGIC (34.9/20.1 mIoU) surpasses X-decoder (29.6/9.2) on PC-459 and ADE-847. Notably, SEGIC achieves these results with only 5M trainable parameters and a fully frozen backbone, whereas competitors like SegGPT and Painter require 354M trainable parameters, and Matcher/PerSAM depend on an external SAM model.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEGIC positions itself against two prior lines of in-context segmentation: inpainting-based approaches (Painter, SegGPT) that concatenate images into mosaics and recover masked regions, and two-stage SAM-based pipelines (PerSAM, Matcher) that use external correspondence models to generate prompts. It departs from both by building an end-to-end framework on a single frozen vision foundation model, extracting three explicit instruction types (geometric, visual, meta) from emergent dense correspondences rather than relying on implicit feature interactions or external prompt generators. The conceptual bridge from NLP in-context learning (where label propagation flows through word relationships) to vision (where it flows through pixel-level correspondences) is the paper's core framing, drawing on recent evidence that VFMs like DINOv2 encode high-quality correspondence as an emergent property.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a frozen backbone plus only ~5M trainable parameters achieves SOTA across one-shot, video, and open-vocabulary segmentation, making in-context segmentation accessible without large-scale fine-tuning. The empirical finding that zero-shot correspondence quality strongly predicts in-context segmentation performance provides a useful diagnostic for selecting or designing VFMs for this purpose, and the observation that model/data scale does not directly help challenges the default assumption that bigger is better. Open questions include whether multiple in-context examples per entity can further improve performance, whether the framework extends to instance-level (panoptic) segmentation, and whether pre-training objectives explicitly emphasizing inter-image correspondence could yield even stronger in-context segmentation capabilities.
