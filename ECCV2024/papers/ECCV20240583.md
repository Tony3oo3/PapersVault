## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud segmentation methods are each tailored to a single task (semantic, referring, or open-vocabulary) and rely on explicit target names or predefined categories, making them unable to interpret implicit, reasoning-heavy instructions like 'Where to sit in the room?' The paper asks whether a single unified model can handle all these segmentation tasks by leveraging an LLM's reasoning capabilities to understand diverse human-like instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240583/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud (N points, each with 3D coordinates and auxiliary features such as color) and a free-form text instruction as input. The LLM produces a text response that may contain one or more <SEG> tokens, each preceded by a predicted category name; the embedding at each <SEG> position is projected to a mask embedding that, when dot-producted with per-point embeddings, yields a binary segmentation mask of shape N. Multi-target queries produce multiple <SEG> tokens and thus multiple masks, while zero-target queries produce the text 'There is no mask.' with no segmentation output.

## Method

SegPoint couples LLaMA2-7B with a Uni3D point encoder, augmented by two key modules. The Geometric Enhancer Module (GEM) extracts local geometric features from the full N-point cloud via stacked KPConv layers and injects them into the point encoder's intermediate features through cross-attention with learnable gating factors initialized to zero, preserving pretrained scene-level representations while adding the local detail needed for dense prediction. The Geometric-guided Feature Propagation (GFP) replaces naive PointNet++ upsampling from N₁ to N points by using the geometric features as a 'gold message': higher-layer features are upsampled, geometric features are downsampled via FPS, the two are fused, and attentive cross-attention propagates information across different point densities to produce high-quality per-point embeddings. Training is end-to-end with a combined autoregressive text loss, binary cross-entropy, and DICE loss, using task-specific prompt templates to jointly train on semantic, referring, and instruction segmentation data within one model.

## Results

On the novel Instruct3D benchmark, SegPoint achieves 31.6% Acc and 27.5% mIoU, surpassing the best prior method (M3DRef-CLIP, 12.8% mIoU) by over 14 percentage points. On established referring segmentation benchmarks, SegPoint scores 41.7 mIoU on ScanRefer, 32.2 on Nr3D, and 36.1 on Multi3DRefer. For semantic segmentation, SegPoint reaches 35.3 mIoU on ScanNet200 (outperforming the previous best OctFormer by 2.7 points), 74.1 on ScanNet, and 72.4 on S3DIS. On open-vocabulary segmentation (ScanNet++), SegPoint attains 19.3 mIoU, exceeding the best open-vocabulary baseline RegionPLC (14.9) by 4.4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Instruct3D, SegPoint (27.5 mIoU) dominates all baselines by a wide margin; the strongest competitor M3DRef-CLIP [79] reaches only 12.8 mIoU, and even the vanilla SegPoint† baseline (16.1) already exceeds all prior methods. On ScanRefer, SegPoint (41.7 mIoU) trails RefMask3D [18] (44.8) by 3.1 points but outperforms 3D-STMN [63] (39.5), EDA [67] (36.2), BUTD-DETR [23] (35.4), and TGNN [22] (27.8). On Nr3D and Multi3DRefer, SegPoint achieves the best results (32.2 and 36.1 mIoU respectively), beating EDA [67] by 2.9 and 7.2 points. For semantic segmentation, SegPoint leads on ScanNet200 (35.3 vs. OctFormer [60] at 32.6) but is slightly behind PTv2 [66] (75.4) and OctFormer (75.7) on ScanNet, while roughly matching Swin3D [72] (72.5) on S3DIS. On open-vocabulary ScanNet++ [74], SegPoint (19.3 mIoU) surpasses all open-vocabulary methods—RegionPLC [71] (14.9), PLA [12] (14.2), and OpenScene [42] (12.8)—though it remains below supervised baselines like KPConv [58] (30.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

SegPoint directly extends LISA's (CVPR 2024) <SEG>-token paradigm for LLM-based reasoning segmentation from 2D images to 3D point clouds, while departing from prior 3D segmentation methods (TGNN, BUTD-DETR, EDA, M3DRef-CLIP) that each address a single task with explicit object descriptions. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a Geometric Enhancer Module (KPConv-based local feature extraction) and Geometric-guided Feature Propagation (attentive cross-density upsampling), which address the specific challenge of adapting a scene-level pre-trained point encoder (Uni3D) to dense per-point prediction without full fine-tuning. Unlike 2D counterparts that rely on SAM or other pre-trained segmentors, SegPoint generates masks purely from LLM hidden states and geometric priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework demonstrating that a single LLM-based model can handle instruction, referring, semantic, and open-vocabulary 3D segmentation is a meaningful step toward general-purpose 3D perception, and the Instruct3D benchmark fills a gap by evaluating implicit-instruction reasoning that prior referring segmentation benchmarks do not capture. However, the current framework is limited to text-only prompts (no box or point prompts), the Instruct3D baselines are relatively weak (12–18% Acc), and the approach depends on a 7B LLM with ~3-day training on 4 A100s, raising questions about scalability and real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the geometric modules generalize to outdoor/large-scale scenes, whether smaller LLMs retain the reasoning advantage, and how to incorporate non-textual interaction modalities.
