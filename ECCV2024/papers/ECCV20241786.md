## Problem

Existing explainable VQA methods (Explanation Generation, Prototype Networks, LIME) map directly from <Question, Image, Answer> to a single explanation, which produces large semantic deviations in complex scenarios where the question and answer are far apart in meaning. This paper argues that explanations should be generated step-by-step along the reasoning chain rather than as a single jump, and that the explanation task must cover intermediate reasoning steps, not just the final answer.

## Input / Output

The model takes a question string (including prompt text and multiple-choice option text) and an RGB image as input, embedding them via T5 and DETR respectively, with a projection matrix aligning their vector lengths. It outputs a chain of n reasoning explanations R1 → R2 → … → Rn where the final explanation Rn contains the answer. Training uses 256×256 and 512×512 image resolutions with a patch size of 2 to determine token granularity of the fused semantic vector.

## Method

VQA-TD (583M parameters) comprises three modules: a semantic embedding module (T5 for text, DETR for image), a Co-Attention alignment module (self-attention + guided-attention units), and a transformer-based reasoning module that implements two coupled diffusion processes. The external diffusion models the Markov-chain transition probability P(Ri→Ri+1) between consecutive reasoning steps, while the internal diffusion models the conditional probability P(<Q,I>→Ri) of the question migrating to each explanation, using a total-probability noise model derived from classifier-free guidance (Theorems 1–2). The reasoning module is a 4-block transformer decoder (18 attention heads, 26 total layers) with noised-latent, embed, patchify, and linear layers, trained with an L2 noise-prediction loss (Eq. 16) over 450K steps in two resolution stages, accelerated by DPM-Solver every 50 steps.

## Results

On ScienceQA, VQA-TD (583M parameters, ~1% of GPT-3.5's 175B) achieves 79.7% average accuracy across eight sub-tasks, surpassing GPT-3.5 (74.0%), GPT-3.5 w/CoT (75.2%), and UnifiedQA_Base w/CoT (74.1%). On explanation quality, VQA-TD scores BLEU-1 0.421, BLEU-4 0.407, and ROUGE-L 0.820, outperforming MC_Base (0.406/0.384/0.769) and GPT-3.5 w/CoT (0.192/0.052/0.323) while approaching GPT-4 w/CoT (ROUGE-L 0.839) and LLaMA-Adapter (ROUGE-L 0.868). The model performs best on the natural science sub-task (83.2%) and worst on social science (71.8%), reflecting its dependence on both text and image prompt information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VQA-TD (79.7% avg) clearly outperforms all smaller and mid-size baselines: VisualBERT (61.9%, +17.8%), UnifiedQA_Base w/CoT (74.1%, +5.6%), GPT-3.5 (74.0%, +5.7%), and GPT-3.5 w/CoT (75.2%, +4.5%), despite using only 1% of GPT-3.5's parameters. Against the large multimodal models, VQA-TD trails GPT-4 w/CoT (84.0%, −4.3%), LLaMA-Adapter (85.2%, −5.5%), and LLaMA-SciTune (90.0%, −10.3%) on accuracy. However, on explanation quality VQA-TD beats MC_Base (223M) on all three metrics—BLEU-1 (0.421 vs 0.406), BLEU-4 (0.407 vs 0.384), and ROUGE-L (0.820 vs 0.769)—despite MC_Base's 5.2-point accuracy advantage, demonstrating that the diffusion chain-of-thought produces more faithful step-by-step explanations. VQA-TD's ROUGE-L (0.820) is within 1.9 points of GPT-4 w/CoT (0.839) and 4.8 points of LLaMA-Adapter (0.868), while its BLEU scores remain substantially lower than those LMMs (e.g., BLEU-1: 0.421 vs 0.927/0.944), which the authors attribute to the use of Co-Attention rather than CLIP for cross-modal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the denoising diffusion probabilistic model (Ho et al., 2020) and classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) — originally designed for image generation — as a step-by-step reasoning and explanation framework for VQA. It departs from prior explainable VQA methods (Explanation Generation, Prototype Networks, LIME), which perform a direct mapping from <Q, I, A> to a single explanation, by instead modeling the reasoning chain as a Markov diffusion process with two coupled diffusion streams (external for inter-step transitions, internal for question-to-explanation alignment). Architecturally, it borrows the transformer-decoder backbone from U-ViT and DiT for the diffusion denoising network, and uses T5/DETR/Co-Attention for multi-modal embedding and alignment, positioning itself as a compact, prompt-free alternative to LLM-based chain-of-thought approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 583M-parameter task-specific model can match or exceed GPT-3.5 (175B) on both answer accuracy and explanation quality on ScienceQA, suggesting that diffusion-based reasoning may be a viable path for efficient, interpretable VQA without relying on massive LLMs. However, VQA-TD remains substantially below GPT-4, LLaMA-Adapter, and LLaMA-SciTune on both accuracy and explanation recall, and the authors acknowledge persistent reasoning hallucinations and weak cross-modal alignment (Co-Attention vs. CLIP). Open questions include whether the dual-diffusion formulation generalizes beyond ScienceQA, whether replacing Co-Attention with a stronger aligner closes the gap to LMMs, and whether the diffusion sampling cost (mitigated here by DPM-Solver) remains practical at scale.
