## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models inadvertently generate unwanted 'implicit concepts' (watermarks, QR codes, text, unsafe content) that are never mentioned in the text prompt, yet existing erasure methods fail because they assume the model can controllably generate or recognize the target concept. This paper identifies that implicit concepts are neither controllably generable nor recognizable by the model, making standard paired-image fine-tuning and attention-based navigation inapplicable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240562/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method takes an image-text pair and augments the text condition with the concept name plus discretized location tokens (e.g., a 32×32 grid of special vocabulary entries) derived from an external detector's bounding box output; the detector's parameters are never accessed, only its predicted coordinates. At inference, the learned concept and geometry tokens are fed as negative prompts alongside the user's text prompt, and the model produces a 256×256 or 512×512 RGB image free from the targeted implicit concept. The geometric information is encoded purely as text tokens rather than pixel-level masks, keeping the interface compatible with standard diffusion sampling.

## Method

GEOM-ERASING exploits the observation that implicit concepts are spatially localized (e.g., watermarks in corners, unsafe content in specific body regions) and converts this geometric information into the text domain. An external classifier/detector provides bounding boxes, which are discretized into a grid of bins; the corresponding location tokens are appended to the text condition alongside the concept name, teaching the model to associate the concept with specific spatial regions. A loss re-weighting scheme (Eq. 5) down-weights the denoising loss in bins covering the concept, so the model learns high-quality generation in non-concept regions while effectively ignoring concept regions. In the Model Refinement setting only the new tokens are optimized; in Data Refinement the UNet parameters are also fine-tuned. At generation time, the learned concept and geometry tokens serve as negative prompts, steering the diffusion process away from the implicit concept without requiring the model to have previously recognized it.

## Results

Under the Data Refinement setting, GEOM-ERASING achieves FID/ICR/F*R of 41.41/5.38/2.23 on ICD-QR, 6.99/5.02/0.35 on ICD-Watermark, and 38.74/13.48/5.22 on ICD-Text, substantially outperforming all baselines on every metric. Under the Model Refinement setting, it reduces the watermark ICR from 11.13% (SD) to 7.31% and achieves the lowest overall I2P toxicity score of 0.09 (vs. 0.39 for SD and 0.13 for SLD-Strong). The method also improves FID over the unmodified SD on ICD-Watermark (6.99 vs. 7.59), indicating that erasing implicit concepts simultaneously enhances generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEOM-ERASING decisively outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. On ICD-QR, it reduces ICR from 74.59% (SD) to 5.38% and F*R from 49.10 to 2.23, while ESD achieves the second-best ICR (17.64%) but at a severe FID cost (90.97). On ICD-Watermark, GEOM-ERASING cuts ICR from 30.40% (SD) to 5.02% and F*R from 2.31 to 0.35; NP is the closest competitor (F*R=2.09) but still 6× worse. On ICD-Text, where 100% of training images contain text, GEOM-ERASING reduces ICR to 13.48% versus 38.08% (ESD, the next best) and 71.84% (SD). On the I2P benchmark (Model Refinement), GEOM-ERASING achieves the lowest overall toxicity score of 0.09, beating SLD-Strong (0.13), NP (0.16), and ESD (0.19), while also attaining the best FID (8.34) and ICR (7.31) for watermark erasure. The paper attributes the baselines' failure to their reliance on the diffusion model's ability to recognize or controllably generate the target concept, which is infeasible for implicit concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-erasure line of work in diffusion models (ESD, FMN, NP, SLD) by identifying a fundamental assumption failure: prior methods require the model to either controllably generate or recognize the target concept, which is infeasible for 'implicit concepts' that were never explicitly conditioned during training. Building on Stable Diffusion's latent-space fine-tuning framework and the classifier-free guidance negative-prompt mechanism, GEOM-ERASING departs from all prior erasure methods by injecting external geometric information (bounding-box coordinates discretized into location tokens) into the text condition, rather than relying on the model's internal concept representation. The loss re-weighting over spatial bins is a new component not present in any prior erasure approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic formulation and benchmark (ICD) for implicit concept removal, a practically important problem given that web-crawled training data inevitably contains watermarks, QR codes, and text that no text filter can remove. The key open question is whether the geometric-token approach generalizes beyond the three tested concepts and beyond the SD architecture, and whether the unexplained FID increase when using geometry tokens as negative prompts can be resolved. The method's reliance on an external detector also raises questions about robustness in deployment settings where such detectors may not be available or may have limited coverage.
