## Problem

ANN-SNN conversion introduces an unevenness error caused by temporal variability in input spike sequences, which existing methods fail to resolve: SNN-Calibration uses average firing rates that ignore spike timing, while methods like QCFS and RTS require modifying ReLU activations and retraining ANNs from scratch, potentially degrading ANN accuracy. No prior approach performs per-timestep bias correction in the forward pass alone to eliminate this temporal mismatch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241841/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ANN (ResNet-20/34, VGG-16 with standard ReLU or ReLU-like activations) and a sampled training dataset, performing only forward passes to compute time-dependent, channel-wise bias terms for each layer and timestep. The calibrated SNN then accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) with constant input encoding across T timesteps and outputs class labels. Notably, no backpropagation or gradient computation is required during calibration, and the original ANN weights and architecture remain unmodified.

## Method

FTBC introduces a time-dependent channel-wise bias b^(ℓ)[t] added to the IF neuron membrane potential equation at each timestep, grounded in a theoretical result (Proposition 1) proving that for any continuous membrane-potential distribution, a unique bias exists that makes the expected SNN output equal the corresponding ANN output, thereby reducing expected conversion error to zero. Practically, a heuristic forward-pass algorithm (Algorithm 1) iteratively updates biases by computing the channel-wise mean difference between SNN spike outputs and ANN activations at each layer and timestep, scaled by a learning-rate hyperparameter α. The calibration proceeds layer-by-layer and timestep-by-timestep (t=1 to T), updating membrane potentials immediately after each bias adjustment, requiring no gradient computation. FTBC is composable with existing conversion techniques (e.g., QCFS, RTS), yielding additive accuracy gains at low latency without altering the source ANN.

## Results

On ImageNet with VGG-16, FTBC achieves 71.19% top-1 accuracy at T=16, surpassing all baselines (QCFS: 50.97%, RTS: 55.80%, SlipReLU: 51.54%) and reaching near-ANN performance (75.29% vs. 75.36% ANN) at T=128. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-20, FTBC reaches 81.94% at T=128, exceeding the ANN accuracy of 81.89%. When combined with QCFS, FTBC yields dramatic gains at low latency: 71.66% vs. 59.35% (ResNet-34, T=16) and 72.98% vs. 50.97% (VGG-16, T=16) on ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FTBC as a standalone method (no ReLU modification) outperforms all ReLU-based conversion baselines—SNNC-AP, QCFS, RTS, SlipReLU, OPI, and SNM—across all timesteps on both ImageNet and CIFAR-100. On ImageNet VGG-16 at T=16, FTBC achieves 71.19%, exceeding QCFS (50.97%) by 20.22 points and RTS (55.80%) by 15.39 points. When combined with QCFS, FTBC further amplifies gains: ResNet-34 at T=16 jumps from 59.35% to 71.66% (+12.31%), and VGG-16 at T=16 from 50.97% to 72.98% (+22.01%). Against direct-training and hybrid-training methods (BPTT, Dual-Phase, Diet-SNN, HC-STDB), FTBC+QCFS achieves 94.67% on CIFAR-10 and 71.50% on CIFAR-100 at T=4 (VGG-16), surpassing all listed BPTT/hybrid baselines at comparable or lower latency. The sole exception is SlipReLU, which marginally outperforms FTBC at T=1–2 on CIFAR-100, though SlipReLU requires extensive hyperparameter search and degrades the underlying ANN accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ANN-SNN conversion framework initiated by Cao et al. [7] and the post-conversion calibration approach of SNN Calibration [43], but departs from the latter's key design choice of averaging firing rates over all timesteps. Instead, FTBC introduces per-timestep, channel-wise bias correction grounded in a theoretical result (Proposition 1) showing that a properly chosen temporal bias can drive the expected conversion error to zero at each step. The method is explicitly complementary to existing conversion techniques like QCFS and RTS, which modify the ANN's activation function, whereas FTBC operates purely in the forward pass on the SNN side without any retraining or architectural changes to the source ANN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FTBC offers a theoretically grounded, computationally cheap (forward-pass-only) calibration that can be applied as a drop-in post-processing step to any existing ANN-SNN conversion pipeline, potentially making it widely adoptable without altering upstream training. The near-lossless conversion at high latency (within 0.4% of ANN accuracy on ImageNet at T=128) and the large low-latency gains when combined with QCFS (e.g., +22% at T=16 on VGG-16) suggest a practical path to energy-efficient SNN deployment. Open questions include whether the iterative calibration converges reliably for very deep or wide networks, how the method behaves under non-constant input encoding, and whether the continuous-distribution assumption in the theory holds for highly quantized or sparse spike patterns in practice.
