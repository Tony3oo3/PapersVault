## Problem

LLM-based planners for autonomous driving offer superior interpretability and controllability but suffer from high computational cost and slow inference, making real-time deployment impractical. Existing approaches either encode complex scene information in language (constrained by token limits) or use the LLM as the sole decision-making entity at every planning step, creating a serial bottleneck. The paper asks how to leverage LLM scene-reasoning capabilities to enhance a real-time planner without requiring the LLM to run at the same frequency as the planner.

## Input / Output

The system consumes vectorized scene data (ego state, 20 surrounding agents over 20 historical frames, and global map, all ego-relative) alongside a series of routing instructions (velocity commands like stop/accelerate/decelerate and routing commands like turn left/right/go straight, each augmented with distance information). The LLM processes these as multimodal tokens (map embeddings via a Vector Map Encoder and Map Adapter, plus language embeddings) through Llama2-13B, producing a hidden feature vector rather than a linguistic output. The final output is an 8-second ego trajectory (waypoints) plus neighbor-agent trajectory predictions parameterized as a Gaussian mixture model, generated at 10 Hz in closed-loop simulation.

## Method

AsyncDriver decouples LLM and real-time planner inference via two components. The Scene-Associated Instruction Feature Extraction Module feeds vectorized scene embeddings and routing instructions into Llama2-13B (with LoRA, R=8, α=32) to extract a high-level instruction feature from the last token's hidden state; an Alignment Assistance Module (five 2-layer MLP heads predicting ego velocity/acceleration, velocity decision, traffic light state, adjacent lane presence, and lane change) enforces multimodal alignment during training only. The Adaptive Injection Block then projects this feature and injects it into every decoder layer of a GameFormer-based real-time planner via an additional cross-attention path modulated by a learnable gate initialized to zero, preserving the original decoder's scene-attention while adding instruction guidance. Crucially, the LLM need only run every N frames (e.g., every 3 frames yields ~40% inference-time reduction with ~1% accuracy loss), since the cached instruction features remain valid across short intervals due to the long-term nature of routing instructions. Training proceeds in two stages: LoRA pre-training on Planning-QA and Reasoning1K for instruction semantics, followed by joint fine-tuning with alignment loss (L1 + cross-entropy + BCE) and planning loss (GMM NLL for agents + L1 for ego trajectory).

## Results

On the nuPlan Hard20 closed-loop reactive benchmark (279 scenarios across 14 challenging types), AsyncDriver achieves a final score of 65.00, outperforming the strongest learning-based baseline GameFormer (62.05) by 2.95 points (4.6%) and the rule-based PDM-Closed (64.18) by 0.82 points. With the PDM scorer adapted for trajectory refinement (AsyncDriver*), the score rises to 67.48, surpassing PDM-Hybrid by 3.43 points and PDM-Closed by 3.30 points. AsyncDriver also leads on Drivable Area (94.62 vs. 93.54 for GameFormer) and Time-to-Collision (73.48 vs. 74.55 for GameFormer, though PDM-Closed edges ahead at 73.47). Asynchronous inference at a 3-frame interval reduces LLM inference time by ~40% with only ~1% accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AsyncDriver outperforms all five baselines on the Hard20 final score. Against the learning-based GameFormer (62.05), it gains 2.95 points (4.6%), with notable improvements in Drivable Area (+1.08) and Progress (+0.86), while slightly trailing on Collision (85.13 vs. 86.02). Against the rule-based PDM-Closed (64.18) and PDM-Hybrid (64.05), AsyncDriver leads by 0.82 and 0.95 points respectively; with the PDM scorer (AsyncDriver*), the margin widens to 3.30 and 3.43 points, and AsyncDriver* also surpasses PDM on TTC (76.70 vs. 73.47/72.75). The older learning-based baselines UrbanDriver (35.35) and GCPGP (36.85) are far behind, with AsyncDriver exceeding them by 29.65 and 28.15 points. IDM (53.07), a classic rule-based car-following model, is outperformed by 11.93 points. Per-scenario analysis (Table 2) shows AsyncDriver leading on 8 of 14 scenario types, with particularly strong gains on type3 (+12.94 over GameFormer) and type8 (+10.93 over GameFormer), while PDM-Closed retains advantages on type12 and type13.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GameFormer's transformer-based real-time planner architecture and departs from the dominant paradigm in LLM-for-driving research (DriveGPT4, LMDrive, LanguageMPC) where the LLM serves as the core decision-making entity outputting language or control signals. Instead, AsyncDriver repurposes the LLM as a feature extractor that produces scene-associated instruction features, which are injected into a conventional real-time planner via a lightweight Adaptive Injection Block. The asynchronous inference design—decoupling LLM and planner inference frequencies—is a novel architectural choice not present in prior LLM-augmented driving frameworks, which typically require full LLM inference at every planning step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that LLM guidance for motion planning need not be synchronous, enabling a ~40% inference speedup with only ~1% accuracy loss, which is a meaningful step toward real-world deployment of LLM-enhanced driving systems. The model-agnostic Adaptive Injection Block could be adopted by other transformer-based planners beyond GameFormer. However, the paper acknowledges it does not substantiate the LLM's generalization properties for planning, and the modest performance gap over GameFormer (4.6%) raises open questions about whether the gains stem from pretrained world knowledge or simply additional model capacity. Broader evaluation across diverse driving conditions and vehicle platforms remains to be done.
