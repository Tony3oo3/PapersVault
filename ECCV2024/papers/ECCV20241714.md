## Problem

Hyperspectral SCI reconstruction models trained on limited datasets (KAIST, CAVE) suffer from poor generalization to unseen scenes, yet existing self-supervised fine-tuning strategies fail for this task: full fine-tuning causes model instability and degraded reconstruction, while linear probing cannot accommodate deep unfolding architectures. The paper addresses the absence of a universal, lightweight self-supervised adaptation method that works across diverse SCI model families without requiring paired ground-truth HSIs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241714/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D CASSI measurement y ∈ R^{H×(W+d(N_λ−1))} and a pre-designed sampling mask Φ as input. A frozen pre-trained model first produces an initial reconstruction X_pre, which the SAH adapter (adding only 0.29M parameters and 5.18G FLOPs) refines into the final H×W×N_λ hyperspectral cube. Crucially, no ground-truth HSI is needed during adapter training—the measurement y itself serves as the self-supervised label, making the approach applicable to real hardware captures where GT is unavailable.

## Method

SAH-SCI freezes the pre-trained SCI model and trains a lightweight U-shaped adapter (SAH) whose basic module is a spatial-spectral convolution: a 3×3 group convolution extracts spatial features while a 1×1 convolution captures spectral features, fused via channel shuffle and channel-weighted gating. A frequency adaption module modulates skip connections in the Fourier domain with learnable weights to balance backbone denoising against high-frequency detail recovery. Training uses a four-component self-supervised loss: measurement consistency (ΦF(y)=y), an equivariance imaging loss that enforces invariance under group transformations to access virtual masks with different nullspaces, an image uncertainty loss that models nullspace ambiguity via random sign-flipped residuals, and a total variation regularizer. The equivariance term is the key mechanism for reducing solution ambiguity—by requiring the network to produce consistent reconstructions under shifted/rotated inputs, it effectively enriches the single incomplete CASSI mask with multiple virtual measurements, all without any ground-truth supervision.

## Results

On the ICVL dataset (5 scenes), SAH-SCI improves all 7 pre-trained models by an average of over 2 dB in PSNR and 0.04 in SSIM. The largest gain is on HDNet, where SAH adds 5.87 dB PSNR and 0.086 SSIM (28.91→34.78 / 0.851→0.937), while the smallest gain is on the already-strong DAUHST (+0.66 dB / +0.006). The adapter adds only 0.29 M parameters and 5.18 G FLOPs, confirming its lightweight nature. Results on Harvard and NTIRE2022 (Table 2) show consistent improvements of 2–4 dB across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAH-SCI is evaluated as a plug-in adapter on 7 pre-trained reconstruction models spanning CNN (λ-Net, TSA-Net), deep unfolding (DGSMP, GAP-Net, DAUHST), and Transformer (HDNet, MST++) architectures. The adapter consistently outperforms every base model on all three evaluation datasets (ICVL, Harvard, NTIRE2022). The most dramatic gains occur on models that generalize poorly from their KAIST/CAVE training: HDNet improves by 5.87 dB (28.91→34.78 PSNR) and TSA-Net by 3.56 dB (26.85→30.41), while DGSMP gains 4.64 dB. Already-strong models like DAUHST see a more modest +0.66 dB improvement, suggesting the adapter is most beneficial when the base model's training distribution diverges from the test data. On Harvard, HDNet-SAH reaches 34.04/0.882 versus 30.68/0.827 for HDNet alone, and on NTIRE2022, MST++-SAH achieves 37.45/0.947 versus 35.86/0.938. The adapter adds only ~0.29 M parameters and ~5 G FLOPs regardless of the base model, making it a near-zero-cost enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the adapter-tuning paradigm from NLP and vision (Houlsby et al. 2019; He et al. 2021) into the hyperspectral snapshot compressive imaging domain, where it had not previously been applied. The self-supervised loss design draws directly on the equivariant imaging framework of Chen et al. (ICCV 2021, CVPR 2022), extending group-invariance-based learning from general compressive sensing to the CASSI-specific setting with its coded-aperture and dispersive-shift structure. Architecturally, the spatial-spectral convolution and frequency-adaptive fusion module are novel components tailored to exploit the joint spatial-spectral structure of HSIs, departing from standard U-Net skip connections that the authors show can introduce aberrant high-frequency details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in hyperspectral imaging: the near-impossibility of collecting large-scale paired ground-truth datasets, which forces all existing reconstruction networks to train on small, biased collections (KAIST, CAVE) and limits real-world generalization. By showing that a 0.29M-parameter adapter trained purely self-supervised can improve seven architecturally diverse pre-trained models by 2–6 dB without touching their weights, the work opens a practical path toward leveraging large image foundation models for hyperspectral tasks. Open questions include whether the group-invariance assumption (shifts, rotations) holds for all CASSI hardware configurations, how the approach scales to higher spectral resolutions or video, and whether the self-supervised loss can be further enriched with physics-based priors beyond the current consistency/equivariance/uncertainty triad.
