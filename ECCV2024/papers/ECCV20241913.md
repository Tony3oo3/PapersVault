## Problem

Existing text-to-image models, including DALL-E3, GPT-4-Vision, and SDXL, produce high-quality single-human images but fail in complex multi-human scenes, generating incorrect quantities, confused identities, wrong spatial layouts, and deformed limbs or faces. No prior method simultaneously enforces accurate human count, identity binding, spatial arrangement, and pose fidelity across multiple interacting individuals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241913/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a final multi-human RGB image, but the chain also emits structured intermediate outputs at each stage: a scalar quantity, an N×4 bounding-box tensor, an N×17×3 COCO-Pose keypoint tensor, an N×182 SMPL-X parameter tensor, and an N×1280 CLIP semantic-embedding tensor. Because every node is conditioned on all predecessors, the pipeline can be halted at any intermediate stage to yield condition-to-condition or multi-condition-to-image generation, and users can inspect or edit intermediates before committing to the final render.

## Method

Chains decomposes text-to-image generation into a sequential condition chain (text → quantity → layout → skeleton → 3D mesh → semantic embedding → image), where each node acts as a structural prior constraining the next. Quantity is predicted by a transformer-based softmax classifier over text embeddings; layout, skeleton, 3D mesh, and semantic embedding are each generated by an independent ~1B-parameter transformer (24 layers, 2048 dim, 32 heads) performing v-prediction denoising diffusion with a cosine schedule, with inputs formed by point-wise addition of clean predecessor embeddings and noisy current-stage embeddings. The final image is synthesized by a modified ControlNet that consumes three composable conditions—rendered SMPL meshes fed through stacked convolutional hint blocks, SMPL-X parameters and semantic embeddings mapped via MLPs and concatenated with text embeddings for cross-attention. Trained on 200M filtered text-image pairs with balanced per-count sampling (τ=0.5), each 20-step inference stage completes in under 0.45 s, enabling interactive control over intermediate structural outputs.

## Results

On the 86-prompt multi-human scene benchmark, Chains achieves an average human rating of 0.77, outperforming Midjourney (0.73), ControlNet with SDXL variant (0.69), SDXL variant (0.66), and SDXL (0.60). The largest gains are on Identity (0.75 vs. 0.69 for Midjourney) and Layout (0.78 vs. 0.71 for Midjourney). In terms of image quality, Chains achieves a FID of 5.31 on the COCO validation subset, comparable to the SDXL variant baseline (5.00). Each intermediate stage completes a 20-step inference in under 0.45 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chains outperforms all four baselines on the overall average human rating. Against SDXL (0.60) and SDXL variant (0.66), Chains leads by 17% and 11% respectively, with the gap widening on Identity and Layout where end-to-end models struggle most with multi-human structural accuracy. Compared to ControlNet with SDXL variant (0.69), Chains gains 8% on average, with particularly strong improvements of 10% on Identity (0.75 vs. 0.65) and 11% on Layout (0.78 vs. 0.67), confirming that the chained condition pipeline adds meaningful structural control beyond a single ControlNet hint. Against Midjourney (0.73), the closest competitor, Chains leads by 4% on average, winning on Quantity (0.83 vs. 0.80), Identity (0.75 vs. 0.69), and Layout (0.78 vs. 0.71), while tying on Pose (0.73 vs. 0.73). On image quality (FID), Chains (5.31) is slightly behind the SDXL variant (5.00), indicating a small quality trade-off for the substantial structural accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-image diffusion paradigm by introducing a sequential 'condition chains' architecture, where each structural condition (quantity, layout, skeleton, 3D mesh, semantic embeddings) is generated by an independent transformer-based diffusion process conditioned on all preceding nodes. It departs from layout-to-image methods (GLIGEN, InteractDiffusion, BoxDiff) that apply a single spatial constraint, and from multi-condition approaches (ControlNet, InstructImagen) that treat conditions as parallel inputs with limited inter-dependency. The design is conceptually inspired by how human designers build complex scenes step-by-step, and it builds on SMPL-X body modeling, CLIP text encoding, and the ControlNet conditioning mechanism as individual components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practically important gap: existing text-to-image models (including DALL-E3, Midjourney, SDXL) fail at structurally accurate multi-human scenes, producing wrong quantities, confused identities, and broken spatial relationships. The chain framework offers a composable, inspectable generation pipeline where users can intervene at any intermediate stage, and the 0.45s-per-stage inference speed makes this practical. Key limitations remain: SMPL-X struggles with unusual poses and non-photorealistic proportions, crowd scenes with many individuals still produce misalignments, and the multi-tasking ablation shows a performance-efficiency trade-off. Extending the chain paradigm beyond humans to general objects, and improving 3D body representations (e.g., SMPLerX), are natural next steps.
