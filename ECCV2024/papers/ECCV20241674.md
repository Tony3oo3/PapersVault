## Problem

Visual counterfactual explanations have been treated as an entirely local technique, generated independently per image, with no mechanism to capture global structure in how a classifier makes decisions. In black-box settings—where only input-output pairs are available—this limitation is especially acute, as the weak signal makes targeted image modifications difficult and no internal mechanism indicates which changes actually influenced the decision. The paper asks whether the semantic latent space of a Diffusion Autoencoder encodes the classifier's inference process as reusable global directions discoverable from a single image.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a single source image (any image from the target dataset) and black-box access to the classifier (input-output pairs only), plus a pretrained DiffAE with a 512-dimensional semantic latent code. It produces two types of global directions in that latent space: a single g-direction (gradient-based) and a set of orthogonal h-directions (Hessian-based), each paired with an optimal step size α found via line search. Applying any direction to an arbitrary image's latent code yields a counterfactual image, and the same directions feed into BB-LIG to produce pixel-level attribution maps. No classifier training data, gradients, or per-image optimization beyond line search is needed.

## Method

The core mechanism is a lightweight MLP proxy (5 linear layers, sigmoid activations, 2 outputs) trained to locally approximate the counterfactual loss—classifier score plus LPIPS perceptual distance—around a single source image's semantic latent code z_sem. Training data is generated by sampling perturbations δ from a uniform distribution on an n-ball, decoding DAE(z_sem + δ, z_T) into images, and querying the black-box classifier and LPIPS. The g-direction is the gradient of the proxy's approximate CF loss w.r.t. z_sem (steepest descent), while h-directions are eigenvectors of the proxy's Hessian sorted by eigenvalue magnitude, yielding orthogonal directions of maximal local variation. A simple line search over uniformly spaced α values determines the optimal step for each direction. For attribution, BB-LIG replaces the gradient integral in Latent Integrated Gradients with finite-difference approximations along a GCD between the factual image and a semantically plausible baseline (the CE where classifier output ≈ 0), yielding a fully black-box attribution map.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, GCD achieves a Flip Rate of 96.0% (Age) and 97.5% (Smile) using a single g-direction derived from one source image, confirming its globality. The method attains sFID of 10.11 (Age) and 7.94 (Smile), substantially outperforming the SOTA black-box method TiME (32.9 and 23.8 respectively) while using ~6× fewer parameters. On CelebA, GCD reaches 96.0% and 97.2% FR with sFID of 9.11 and 7.66, and on CheXpert achieves 74.7–91.4% FR across five disease classes, demonstrating transfer to real-world medical imaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TiME (the SOTA black-box method based on Stable Diffusion), GCD outperforms in 6 out of 8 metrics on CelebA-HQ for both Age and Smile classes, with the most dramatic gap in sFID (10.11 vs. 32.9 for Age; 7.94 vs. 23.8 for Smile), while using only a single image and a model ~6× smaller. On CelebA, GCD beats TiME in every metric except COUT for the Age class. Against the white-box ACE method, GCD is highly competitive: for Age on CelebA-HQ it wins on sFID (10.11 vs. 21.7/28.2), FS (0.8170 vs. 0.8085/0.7743), and CD (3.92 vs. 5.40/4.21), but trails on FVA (99.0 vs. 99.6) and FID (9.47 vs. 5.31 for ACE l1). For Smile, GCD achieves the highest FR (97.5%) and best sFID (7.94) but is slightly behind ACE l1 on FVA (99.0 vs. 100.0) and FS (0.8800 vs. 0.8941). Against DiME and STEEX (both white-box/non-DM), GCD outperforms in every available metric across all four dataset-class combinations, confirming that the black-box constraint does not preclude competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: (1) Diffusion Autoencoders (DiffAE), which demonstrated that their semantic latent space contains global editing directions for specific attributes, and (2) visual counterfactual explanations (CEs), which have been treated as an entirely local, per-image technique. The key conceptual departure is showing that the DiffAE latent space also encodes a *classifier-specific* global direction that flips decisions across an entire dataset, discoverable from a single image via a lightweight proxy MLP. The method also extends Latent Integrated Gradients (LIG) from MedXGAN to a fully black-box setting (BB-LIG) by replacing analytic gradients with finite differences, removing the white-box assumption that prior LIG formulations required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single image suffices to extract a direction that flips a black-box classifier on ~96% of a dataset is a strong empirical result that opens a new research direction for global CEs and suggests a deep coupling between generative representation learning and supervised decision boundaries. Practical limitations include the requirement of a DiffAE (unavailable for arbitrary domains, requiring training from scratch for CelebA and CheXpert) and the fact that globality is demonstrated empirically rather than proven theoretically. Open questions include whether this phenomenon generalizes beyond face and medical imaging, whether the proxy's local approximation degrades for more complex classifiers, and whether the globality property holds under distribution shift.
