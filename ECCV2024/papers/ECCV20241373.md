## Problem

Frame-based video deblurring methods fail under severe motion blur because they cannot establish accurate temporal correspondence between neighboring frames. While event cameras offer microsecond-level temporal resolution that could guide alignment, prior event-guided deblurring works operate on single images and do not exploit the long-range temporal dependencies available in video sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241373/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes P consecutive blurred RGB video frames (P=5 or 7) along with their corresponding event streams, which are first converted to voxel grid representations and then temporally subdivided into N intervals per exposure window. Event features spanning the exposure interval between each pair of adjacent frames are additionally encoded to serve as alignment cues. The output is a single restored sharp frame at the target time step, produced by a U-Net decoder operating on multi-scale temporally aligned feature pyramids.

## Method

CMTA comprises two key modules. The CRIFE module performs recurrent cross-attention within a single frame's exposure time: the blurred frame feature serves as a query that is iteratively updated by attending to temporally segmented event features (N iterations), using transposed attention for O(C²) efficiency at high resolution. The ECITFA module performs coarse-to-fine temporal alignment across a 3-level feature pyramid, where event features encoding inter-frame motion replace optical flow or deformable convolution, enabling alignment at multiple spatial scales. Alignment is cascaded: frames t−1 and t+1 are first aligned to t using their respective event cues, then t is aligned using the already-aligned neighbors, propagating non-local information. Within each alignment step, a spatially variant dynamic filter refines the event-guided aligned features before a final cross-attention fuses them with the current frame's features. The multi-scale aligned pyramids feed a U-Net decoder that produces the sharp output via a residual connection to the blurred input.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, CMTA-5 (5 input frames) achieves 36.55 dB PSNR and 0.977 SSIM, outperforming the best frame-based method Shift-Net (35.49 dB) by 1.06 dB and the best prior event-guided method SpkNet (36.12 dB) by 0.43 dB, while using fewer parameters (9.7M vs. 13.5M). With 7 input frames (CMTA-7), the method reaches 36.78 dB PSNR. On the real-world EVRB dataset with extreme motion blur, CMTA achieves 31.38 dB PSNR, surpassing the best frame-based method Shift-Net (30.56 dB) by 0.82 dB and the best event-guided method EFNet (30.98 dB) by 0.40 dB. On HighREV, the method attains 39.12 dB PSNR and 0.980 SSIM, leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMTA outperforms all 15 baselines across all three datasets. Against frame-based video deblurring methods, CMTA-5 surpasses the strongest baseline Shift-Net [16] by 1.06 dB on GoPro and by 0.82 dB on the real-world EVRB dataset, where frame-based methods degrade most due to extreme blur. Among event-guided deblurring methods, CMTA-5 exceeds SpkNet [7] (the prior best) by 0.43 dB on GoPro while using 3.8M fewer parameters, and outperforms EFNet [36] by 1.07 dB and REFID [37] by 0.64 dB. On HighREV, CMTA leads REFID [37] by 0.75 dB. On EVRB, the gap between the best frame-based (Shift-Net, 30.56 dB) and best event-guided (EFNet, 30.98 dB) methods narrows to 0.42 dB, whereas CMTA extends the lead to 0.82 dB over Shift-Net, confirming that event-guided temporal alignment is particularly beneficial under severe real-world motion blur where optical-flow-based alignment fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: frame-based video deblurring (which relies on optical flow or deformable convolutions for temporal alignment) and event-guided single-image motion deblurring (which fuses event and RGB features within one exposure window). The key departure is repurposing event data not merely as a cross-modal fusion signal but as a direct temporal alignment mechanism across multiple video frames, thereby eliminating the need for explicit motion estimation (optical flow, deformable offsets) that limits prior methods to low spatial resolutions. The CRIFE module extends transformer-based cross-attention with a recurrent temporal dimension over sub-exposure event segments, while the ECITFA module introduces a cascaded coarse-to-fine alignment strategy that has no direct analogue in prior event-guided deblurring work. The EVRB dataset extends prior real-world blur benchmarks (ReBlur, [31], [52–54]) by adding synchronized event streams and longer sequences suitable for video-level evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is demonstrating that event cameras can substitute for optical flow in temporal feature alignment, unlocking multi-scale alignment that was previously computationally prohibitive — a finding the authors explicitly generalize to other event-guided video restoration tasks (e.g., super-resolution). The EVRB dataset addresses a real gap: prior event-guided deblurring benchmarks (ReBlur) were indoor, short, and lacked high-quality RGB, making them unsuitable for video deblurring evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer temporal contexts (P > 2), how sensitive performance is to event sparsity in low-motion scenes, and whether the hardware requirement of a co-registered event camera limits real-world deployment. The method's reliance on a specific hybrid camera setup for training data also raises questions about domain generalization to unconstrained event+RGB pairs.
