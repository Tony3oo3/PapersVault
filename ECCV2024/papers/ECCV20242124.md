## Problem

CLIP-LIT, the state-of-the-art CLIP-guided backlit image enhancement method, requires iterative cycles of prompt tuning in text embedding space followed by enhancement model fine-tuning, making training slow, unstable, and dependent on the text encoder. This paper asks whether CLIP guidance can be achieved more efficiently by operating directly in the latent space or by eliminating prompt learning entirely in favor of a fixed, precomputed guidance vector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242124/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The enhancement model is a U-Net that takes a single backlit RGB image and outputs a spatially varying illumination map of shape H×W×1; the final enhanced image is computed as the element-wise division of the input by this map. Training uses either paired backlit/well-lit images from BAID (380 pairs) or unpaired data combining 380 BAID backlit images with 384 DIV2K well-lit images. The residual vector used for guidance is precomputed once from the CLIP image embeddings of all training images before any network training begins.

## Method

The paper proposes two modifications to CLIP-LIT's guidance mechanism. CLIP-LIT-Latent replaces learnable text prompts with two 512-dimensional vectors tuned directly in the CLIP latent space via binary cross-entropy, removing the text encoder from the optimization loop and enabling use of vision-only models. RAVE eliminates prompt learning entirely: it computes a residual vector as the L2-normalized difference between the mean CLIP embeddings of well-lit and backlit training images, then trains the U-Net with a single-stage loss combining an identity loss (multi-layer CLIP feature matching) and a residual loss that penalizes the squared difference between the enhanced image's projection onto the residual vector and the well-lit mean's projection. This fixed-vector guidance removes the iterative prompt-refinement loop, converges up to 25× faster, and produces fewer artifacts. The residual vector is also shown to be interpretable—its alignment with CLIP vocabulary tokens reveals dataset biases (e.g., geographic/semantic skew in unpaired data), enabling a simple bias-correction step that projects out the unwanted semantic component.

## Results

On the BAID test dataset, RAVE achieves state-of-the-art performance in the paired setting across all four metrics: PSNR 22.26, SSIM 0.880, LPIPS 0.139, and FID 36.01, outperforming CLIP-LIT (21.93/0.875/0.156/41.16) and Diff-Retinex (22.07/0.861/0.160/38.07). In the unpaired setting, RAVE shifted achieves the best LPIPS (0.149) and FID (40.90), while CLIP-LIT retains the best PSNR (21.59 vs. 21.37 for RAVE shifted). The paper reports that RAVE converges up to 25× faster than CLIP-LIT and CLIP-LIT-Latent during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the paired setting, RAVE outperforms all baselines on every metric: it beats CLIP-LIT by +0.33 PSNR, +0.005 SSIM, −0.017 LPIPS, and −5.15 FID, and surpasses Diff-Retinex (the strongest non-CLIP baseline) by +0.19 PSNR, +0.019 SSIM, −0.021 LPIPS, and −2.06 FID. CLIP-LIT-Latent matches CLIP-LIT closely on SSIM and LPIPS but lags slightly on PSNR (21.84 vs. 21.93) and FID (42.56 vs. 41.16). In the unpaired setting, RAVE shifted wins on LPIPS (0.149 vs. 0.160 for CLIP-LIT) and FID (40.90 vs. 46.49), but trails CLIP-LIT on PSNR (21.37 vs. 21.59) and SSIM (0.877 vs. 0.874, essentially tied). Restormer, Retinexformer, and DiffIR are only evaluated in the paired setting and are consistently outperformed by both CLIP-LIT and RAVE on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID), though Retinexformer and Diff-Retinex are competitive on PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP-LIT (Liang et al., ICCV 2023), which introduced iterative CLIP prompt learning for backlit image enhancement with unpaired data. The key departure is replacing text-space prompt tuning with two simpler alternatives: direct latent-space vector optimization (CLIP-LIT-Latent) and a fixed residual vector computed as the difference of mean CLIP embeddings of well-lit and backlit training images (RAVE). This positions the work within the broader backlit enhancement literature (Retinex-based methods, Restormer, Retinexformer, Diff-Retinex) while specifically targeting the CLIP-guided unpaired training paradigm, arguing that the learned-prompt mechanism is an unnecessary complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple residual vector in CLIP embedding space outperforms iterative prompt learning while training 25× faster challenges the assumption that learned prompts are essential for CLIP guidance in image enhancement. The interpretability of the residual vector—revealing dataset biases (e.g., BAID's Chinese-city content vs. DIV2K's nature imagery) and enabling a simple bias-correction step—opens a practical avenue for auditing and correcting training data biases in vision-language-guided models. Limitations include the absence of generative components (cannot restore lost texture in severely underexposed regions) and modest quality gains over CLIP-LIT in the unpaired setting, leaving open whether the residual-vector approach generalizes to other enhancement or restoration tasks.
