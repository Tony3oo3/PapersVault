## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient transfer learning (PETL) methods for vision transformers cannot reduce inference FLOPs or latency, and many even introduce additional computational overhead from inserted modules. Additionally, prior early-exit approaches force shallow-layer features to encode high-level semantics, destroying the natural low-level/high-level feature hierarchy and degrading performance. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a dynamic adapter framework that explicitly decouples feature extraction from classification while enabling input-dependent early exiting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241910/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (224×224 for ViT-Base/16) or video frame sequences (8 frames for SSv2), which are tokenized into visual sequences of shape n×d. At inference, the network dynamically selects an exit stage based on a confidence threshold over softmax probabilities, producing a single class label from whichever stage first exceeds the threshold. During training, all S=4 stages produce predictions simultaneously with weighted multi-task supervision, and the final training loss is a weighted sum across stages.

## Method

Dyn-Adapter inserts multiple classification heads at uniform intervals (T=3 blocks, S=4 stages) across a frozen ViT backbone, where only lightweight PETL adapters (LoRA, RepAdapter, or Adapter) are updated. A head-balance design assigns heavier MLP heads to shallow stages and lighter heads to deep stages, aligning with the theoretical requirement that adjacent stage compositions form a chain. An adaptive prioritization schedule starts with high weight on deep supervision and progressively shifts toward shallow supervision, preserving the deep-layer accuracy ceiling while enabling early exits. A bidirectional sparsification strategy applies stronger dropout on shallow layers (p=0.5 vs 0.1) in the forward pass and random gradient masking (p_m=0.1) in the backward pass to enhance generalization. At inference, the network exits at the first stage whose max softmax confidence exceeds a threshold, reducing FLOPs by up to 50% while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 datasets), Dyn-RepAdapter achieves 76.4% average accuracy at 11.7G FLOPs (70% of baseline), surpassing RepAdapter's 76.1% at full 16.8G FLOPs. At the more aggressive 50% FLOPs setting (8.5G), Dyn-RepAdapter matches RepAdapter's full-compute accuracy (76.1%) while doubling throughput from 91.5 to 207.5 imgs/s. Dyn-LoRA similarly improves over LoRA from 74.2% to 74.5% while reducing FLOPs from 11.7G to 8.5G. Under SSL pretraining (MoCo v3), Dyn-LoRA boosts accuracy by 1.5% over LoRA at 50% FLOPs (73.07% vs 70.84%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dyn-Adapter is applied as a plug-in to three PETL baselines (VPT, LoRA, RepAdapter) and consistently matches or exceeds their accuracy at reduced FLOPs. Against RepAdapter, Dyn-RepAdapter achieves identical 76.1% accuracy at 50% FLOPs (8.5G vs 16.8G) and improves to 76.4% at 70% FLOPs, while throughput more than doubles (207.5 vs 90.9 imgs/s at bs=1). Against LoRA, Dyn-LoRA gains +0.3% accuracy (74.5% vs 74.2%) while cutting FLOPs from 11.7G to 8.5G. Against VPT, Dyn-Adapter matches the 73.9% accuracy at half the FLOPs. The remaining baselines (AdaptFormer, NOAH, SSF, Adapter) serve as context: Dyn-RepAdapter at 70% FLOPs (76.4%) surpasses all of them at full FLOPs (74.7–75.7%), establishing a new state-of-the-art on VTAB-1k among PETL methods. In the efficiency comparison (Table 2), Dyn-RepAdapter is the only method that reduces FLOPs (−8.3G) and achieves >120% throughput gain across all batch sizes, whereas all other PETL methods either add FLOPs or maintain them at best.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dyn-Adapter positions itself as a general efficiency booster that layers dynamic early-exiting and bidirectional sparsification on top of existing PETL methods (LoRA, Adapter, RepAdapter), rather than proposing a standalone adapter architecture. It departs from prior dynamic early-exit work (Adaptive Neural Networks, RANet, Dynamic Perceiver) by freezing the pre-trained backbone entirely, so that shallow-layer low-level features are never modified by gradient flow from early classifiers—achieving what the authors call 'fully explicit decoupling' of feature extraction and classification. The bidirectional sparsification (asymmetric dropout in forward pass, random gradient masking in backward pass) draws on regularization ideas but is applied specifically to address the multi-task interference inherent in multi-stage supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 50% FLOPs reduction with 121–137% throughput improvement on a single GPU, with no accuracy loss, makes PETL methods genuinely deployable in resource-constrained settings where prior methods' added latency was a blocker. The framework's plug-and-play nature (applicable to LoRA, Adapter, RepAdapter, and SSL-pretrained variants) broadens its utility. However, the authors acknowledge the lack of a rigorous mathematical formulation for the joint multi-stage optimization, and the accuracy gains over baselines are modest (0.3–0.5%), so the primary value is efficiency rather than representational quality. Open questions include whether the dynamic depth selection generalizes to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation) and whether the fixed supervision interval T=3 is optimal across architectures.
