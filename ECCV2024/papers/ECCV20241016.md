## Problem

Existing compositional action recognition tasks (e.g., Something-else) only require recognizing verbs in unseen verb-object pairs, reducing the problem to a supervised verb classification with sample selection bias rather than true compositional generalization. No prior task or benchmark requires a model to recognize unseen action categories composed of both seen verbs and seen objects in the video modality, leaving the compositional generalization ability of video models untested.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241016/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw video clip of shape T×224×224×3 and produces a full action label (verb-object composition) along with separate verb and object confidence scores. Internally, the video is encoded to a general spatiotemporal representation F_x ∈ R^{T×D}, then decomposed into a dynamic feature f_v and a static feature f_o (each in R^C) via dedicated encoders. The final action score is a consensus of two inference paths (dynamics-centered and static-centered), each computing a conditional component score given the other component's prototype.

## Method

C2C consists of two modules: an Independent Component Learning module that uses a temporal-convolution-based dynamic encoder and a temporal-pooling-based static encoder to extract verb and object features, aligning them with learned prototypes via cosine-similarity contrastive losses; and a Component-to-Composition module that infers action scores through two paths (dynamics path: verb prototype + static visual features → conditional object score; static path: object prototype + dynamic visual features → conditional verb score), averaging the two paths for the final prediction. The enhanced training strategy introduces HSIC-based independence losses to suppress spurious cross-component information, a CutMix-based augmentation that assembles novel verb-object compositions from training batches to encourage imagination of unseen actions, and conditional-score fitting losses that explicitly balance the model's emphasis on seen versus unseen verb-object relations.

## Results

On the proposed Sth-com benchmark, C2C (Enhanced) with VideoSwin-T achieves 44.1% unseen accuracy, 43.4% seen accuracy, 36.7% HM, and 17.4% AUC, outperforming the best baseline OADis (38.3% unseen, 30.2% HM, 12.6% AUC) by 5.8% in unseen accuracy and 6.5% in HM. With TSM-18, C2C (Enhanced) reaches 41.0% unseen and 33.0% HM, surpassing the strongest baseline Compcos (35.1% unseen, 27.5% HM) by 5.9% and 5.5% respectively. On the image-based C-GQA benchmark, C2C with multi-level outputs achieves 23.6% unseen, 23.3% HM, and 8.2% AUC, exceeding the prior SOTA CoT (22.7% unseen, 22.1% HM, 7.4% AUC). When applied on top of CLIP-based methods (Coop, CSP, SPM), C2C (Enhanced) consistently boosts HM by ~8 points and AUC by ~7-8 points over the base methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2C (Enhanced) outperforms all six adapted CZSL baselines on Sth-com across every metric. The largest gaps are over CGE (HM 33.0 vs 20.0 with TSM-18) and TMN (HM 33.0 vs 22.0), while the closest competitor is OADis on VideoSwin-T (HM 36.7 vs 30.2, a 6.5-point margin). Among CLIP-based methods, C2C (Enhanced) applied on top of Coop, CSP, and SPM yields HM improvements of 7.9, 8.3, and 8.3 points respectively, substantially exceeding the marginal gains from SPM's own DFM module (only +0.3 HM over SPM). On C-GQA, C2C with multi-level outputs surpasses CoT by 0.9% unseen, 1.2% HM, and 0.8% AUC, while using a simpler averaging strategy for multi-level features rather than CoT's dedicated module. Notably, the paper observes that VideoSwin-T does not consistently improve over TSM-18 for verb accuracy across all methods, suggesting the ZS-CAR task exposes different biases in dynamic vs. static pattern recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining Zero-Shot Compositional Action Recognition (ZS-CAR) as a distinct task, explicitly arguing that prior compositional action recognition (Something-else) is merely verb classification with sample selection bias rather than true compositional generalization, and that image-based CZSL methods do not transfer to video. The C2C framework repurposes the component-prototype and conditional-score ideas from CZSL (Compcos, OADis) but restructures them around independent dynamic/static encoders and dual-path composition inference tailored to video. The enhanced training strategy draws on HSIC-based independence (from representation learning) and CutMix (from image classification) to address video-specific challenges of component domain and compatibility variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Sth-com benchmark provides the first dedicated evaluation for compositional generalization in video, filling a gap between image CZSL and the narrower verb-only compositional action recognition task. The finding that existing CZSL methods degrade substantially on video (e.g., TMN HM drops from competitive image scores to 22.0) and that VideoSwin-T does not consistently beat TSM-18 on verb accuracy reveals that current video encoders have unaddressed biases in dynamic pattern recognition. Open questions include whether the approach scales to richer compositions (multi-object, multi-verb), whether the Sth-com benchmark's derivation from Sth-V2 limits ecological validity, and whether the CutMix-based 'imagination' of unseen compositions generalizes beyond the controlled verb-object setting.
