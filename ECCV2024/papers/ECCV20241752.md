## Problem

Training 3D point cloud models incurs rapidly escalating computational and memory bandwidth costs as model capacity and dataset sizes grow, yet training-phase acceleration for these models remains largely unexplored. The paper identifies two unique bottlenecks specific to the PointNet-based training pipeline—Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) with its redundant per-epoch distance calculations, and forward/backward aggregation passes with excessive GPU global memory accesses—that together consume 47–79% of total training time.

## Input / Output

The method operates on unordered 3D point cloud scenes of 24,000–64,000 points, each carrying a d-dimensional feature vector, and produces per-point class labels for 3D semantic segmentation. A key operational detail is that L-FPS precomputes all sampling indices offline (stored on disk, ~1–10 GB) and the dataloader loads the appropriate per-epoch index set at each iteration, so the GPU never re-executes FPS during training. Fused aggregation modifies the kernel-level memory access pattern for the downsampling layers, which reduce point count by a configurable stride factor across N_layer building blocks.

## Method

The paper proposes two orthogonal optimizations. First, Lightweight FPS (L-FPS) eliminates redundant distance calculations by running FPS once before training to determine a minimum pairwise spacing threshold, then building a compact O(kN) filter matrix (k=16) via ball query. During training, nepoch sampling results are generated in parallel on GPU by progressively filtering out points within the threshold of already-selected points using the filter matrix and randomly selecting from the remainder, with results saved to disk and loaded per epoch to preserve randomness. Second, fused aggregation merges the grouping (gather) and max-reduction kernels into a single CUDA kernel that reads neighbor features into SRAM, performs max-reduction on the fly, and writes only the reduced result plus a source index table to DRAM; the backward pass then scatters gradients directly via the source index table, eliminating the intermediate scattered gradient matrix. Together these reduce DRAM accesses by ~5.13× in aggregation and remove the quadratic FPS cost, yielding a 2.25× geomean training speedup with no accuracy loss on S3DIS and ScanNet.

## Results

The paper achieves a 2.25× geomean end-to-end training throughput improvement when both L-FPS and fused aggregation are applied, with no accuracy degradation (maximum mIoU loss of 0.06%, with a potential 0.2% gain). L-FPS alone contributes 1.72× geomean speedup, while fused aggregation alone contributes 1.22×. GPU energy efficiency improves by 1.94× geomean, and fused aggregation reduces DRAM accesses by approximately 5.13× compared to the standard aggregation kernel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against the standard PointNet-based training pipeline (baseline FPS + standard aggregation kernels) on NVIDIA RTX 3090. L-FPS matches or slightly exceeds baseline accuracy across all six model-dataset configurations (e.g., 63.39 vs. 63.19 mIoU on S3DIS/PN++, 70.74 vs. 70.67 on S3DIS/MB-XL), confirming no accuracy loss while delivering 1.72× throughput gain. Fused aggregation, being a pure kernel-level optimization with no algorithmic change, preserves accuracy exactly and adds a further 1.22× speedup. The two techniques are orthogonal and combine additively to reach 2.25× overall speedup. The paper also notes that L-FPS outperforms alternative sampling strategies (random sampling, adjustable FPS, grid sampling) in maintaining sampling quality, as those methods consistently degrade model accuracy. On small point clouds (ModelNet40, 1024 points), L-FPS actually causes a 1.16× slowdown due to preprocessing overhead exceeding the minimal FPS cost, so only fused aggregation is recommended in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PointNet++ training pipeline (which introduced FPS as the downsampling mechanism) and the delayed aggregation technique from Mesorasi, which reshaped the aggregation operation into a form that exposes redundant memory accesses. It departs from prior FPS acceleration work (EdgePC, QuickFPS) by targeting the training pipeline rather than inference, exploiting the fact that input coordinates are static across epochs. The fused aggregation technique applies kernel fusion—a strategy well-known in other domains (e.g., FlashAttention for NLP)—to the point cloud aggregation kernel, while L-FPS replaces the greedy farthest-point selection with a threshold-based near-point filter that is precomputed once before training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.25× training speedup with no accuracy loss is practically significant for researchers iterating on PointNet-based architectures and for production pipelines that retrain frequently on growing datasets. A key open question is whether these techniques transfer to sparse convolution models or transformer-based point cloud architectures, which have different bottleneck profiles. The paper also reveals a useful limitation: L-FPS actually slows training for small point clouds (~1024 points), suggesting the preprocessing and dataloader overhead dominates when FPS itself is cheap. Broader applicability to mixed-precision training, multi-GPU settings, and non-PointNet backbones remains unexplored.
