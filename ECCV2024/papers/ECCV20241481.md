## Problem

ASR subtitles from instructional videos are noisy, temporally misaligned, and stylistically unlike human-written captions (containing filler phrases, grammatical errors, and off-topic commentary), making them suboptimal supervision for training text-video models. This paper asks whether LLMs can be prompted to convert these noisy subtitles into high-quality, human-style video captions at scale without any human annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241481/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes blocks of multiple ASR subtitles with their start/end timestamps (aggregated to provide longer context than a single subtitle) along with the corresponding video clip. It produces concise human-style captions (averaging 9 words) each paired with a predicted start timestamp, which are then temporally realigned to the video and filtered, yielding 25M aligned video-caption pairs from 1.2M HowTo100M videos. The downstream video-language model uses a ViT-B/16 image encoder adapted to video via uniform frame sampling (4 frames in training, 12 in evaluation) paired with a BERT_base text encoder, both initialized from BLIP weights.

## Method

The HowToCaption pipeline has three stages. First, Vicuna-13B is prompted with blocks of ASR subtitles (not individual sentences) using carefully engineered instructions—'Write only short sentences,' 'Describe only one action per sentence,' and 'Keep only actions that happen in the present time'—to generate concise captions with predicted timestamps in a zero-shot setting. Second, a BLIP-based dual-encoder video-language model computes caption-video similarity scores across a ±10-second temporal window around each predicted timestamp, selecting the offset that maximizes alignment. Third, pairs below a similarity threshold are filtered out, and this alignment-and-filtering step is repeated over multiple rounds, with the V-L model fine-tuned between rounds using a regularization term (Eq. 2) that anchors embeddings to frozen pre-trained weights to prevent catastrophic forgetting. A key consequence is that the generated captions are no longer direct outputs of the speech signal, effectively decoupling text from audio and enabling text-video+audio retrieval without the regularization tricks previously required to counteract audio-text correlation.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, the model pre-trained on HowToCaption achieves R10 of 44.1 on YouCook2, 73.3 on MSR-VTT, 82.1 on MSVD, and 38.6 on LSMDC, outperforming all other HowTo100M annotation variants by an average of 5.2 p.p. in R10. For text-to-video+audio retrieval, the method reaches R10 of 63.6 on YouCook2 and 41.5 on MSR-VTT, surpassing the directly comparable EAO baseline (60.4 and 31.2 respectively). On video captioning, fine-tuning on HowToCaption yields CIDEr of 116.4 on YouCook2 and 154.2 on MSVD, exceeding the BLIP+HowTo100M baseline (112.9 and 151.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HowToCaption outperforms all HowTo100M annotation variants: it beats sentencified ASRs [16] by 11.6 p.p. on MSR-VTT R10 (73.3 vs 61.7) and 7.1 p.p. on LSMDC R10 (38.6 vs 31.5), and surpasses auto-aligned HTM-AA [16] by 9.0 p.p. on MSR-VTT R10 (73.3 vs 64.3) and 8.1 p.p. on LSMDC R10 (38.6 vs 30.5). Against WebVid2M [4], HowToCaption wins on YouCook2 (44.1 vs 29.0 R10) and MSR-VTT (73.3 vs 71.9 R10), ties on MSVD (82.1), but slightly underperforms on LSMDC (38.6 vs 39.8 R10). Compared to VideoCC3M [34], HowToCaption dominates across all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on YouCook2 (44.1 vs 21.7 R10). In the text-to-video+audio setting (Table 6), the method trained on HowToCaption without any regularization outperforms EAO [47] by 3.2 p.p. on YouCook2 R10 (63.6 vs 60.4) and 10.3 p.p. on MSR-VTT R10 (41.5 vs 31.2), and also beats TAN [16] by 4.1 p.p. on YouCook2 R10. For video captioning, HowToCaption fine-tuning edges out BLIP+HowTo100M on YouCook2 CIDEr (116.4 vs 112.9) and MSVD CIDEr (154.2 vs 151.4), while remaining competitive with larger models like mPLUG-2 [57] and HiTeA [63] that use more pre-training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the HowTo100M dataset and its ASR-based supervision, extending prior efforts to fix noisy subtitles (TAN alignment [16], distant supervision [27], punctuation correction [65]) by fundamentally transforming the annotation style rather than merely correcting it. It leverages the dual-encoder architecture of BLIP [22] and the video adaptation strategy from CLIP4CLIP [30] as the backbone for both training and post-hoc temporal alignment. Conceptually, it departs from concurrent LLM-based video dataset construction (InternVid, VAST, Video-ChatGPT) that distills knowledge from pre-trained image captioners, instead repurposing LLMs as a text-to-text transformation engine that converts noisy spoken language into concise written captions. The closest concurrent work, HowToStep [23], also uses LLMs on ASR subtitles but targets procedural step descriptions rather than general video captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HowToCaption dataset (25M aligned video-caption pairs) provides a freely available, high-quality training resource that outperforms all prior HowTo100M annotation variants and competitive web datasets, making it practically useful for anyone pre-training video-language models. A notable conceptual contribution is the decoupling of text from audio: because captions are no longer direct transcriptions of speech, text-video-audio retrieval can be trained without the regularization hacks (timestamp shifting, loss reweighting) that prior methods required. Limitations include dependence on ASR quality, restriction to narrated instructional videos, and a non-trivial compute cost (~12k GPU-hours for 1.2M videos). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-instructional or non-narrated video, and whether the modest gains from multi-round alignment suggest diminishing returns from further post-processing.
