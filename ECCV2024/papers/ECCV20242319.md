## Problem

Cutting-based mixup methods (CutMix, FMix, SaliencyMix, ResizeMix) randomly mix spatial regions of two images while keeping a fixed linearly-combined label, causing a 'Label MisMatch' where occluded or absent object features no longer correspond to the label proportions. This mismatched supervision misleads gradient computation during training and degrades classifier performance. SUMix addresses this by learning a per-sample mixing ratio from semantic similarity and modeling the uncertainty introduced by stochastic mixing.

## Input / Output

SUMix is a plug-and-play module applied on top of any existing mixup method. It takes the mixed sample and its two original source images (RGB, 32×32 to 224×224 depending on dataset), encodes all three through the backbone network to obtain feature vectors, and outputs a redefined per-sample mixing ratio λ̃ plus a regularized loss term. The final output remains a standard class label; the modification occurs entirely in the loss computation during training.

## Method

SUMix consists of two modules operating on feature vectors extracted from the backbone. First, a mix-ratio learning module computes a per-sample λ̃ via a softmax-normalized exponential of the L2 distance between the mixed sample's feature and each source sample's feature (Eq. 4), so that when the mixed image semantically resembles one source more, that source's label weight increases. Second, an uncertainty estimation module (adapted from introspective metric learning) passes the feature vectors through an MLP with softmax and L2 norm to produce scalar uncertainty values, then combines the total uncertainty with the semantic distance into a single scalar Z_su (Eq. 6) that acts as a gradient modulator: high uncertainty or large semantic mismatch yields a small gradient, effectively down-weighting unreliable mixed samples. The total training loss (Eq. 3) is the standard mixed cross-entropy computed with the redefined λ̃ plus a weighted regularization term using Z_su, with the weight ζ tuned per dataset and architecture.

## Results

SUMix is a plug-and-play module that redefines the mixup ratio λ via a learnable similarity function and adds an uncertainty-based regularization loss. On CIFAR-100 with ResNet18, SUMix improves CutMix from 78.17% to 79.78% top-1 accuracy (+1.61%), FMix from 79.69% to 80.20% (+0.51%), and ResizeMix from 80.01% to 80.38% (+0.37%). On Tiny-ImageNet with ResNeXt50, the average gain across four mixup methods reaches +2.07%, and on ImageNet-1K with ResNet18 the average gain is +0.47%. On fine-grained CUB-200 (ResNet18), FMix improves from 77.28% to 79.24% (+1.96%), the largest single-method gain reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SUMix is evaluated as an add-on to four hand-crafted mixup methods (CutMix, FMix, SaliencyMix, ResizeMix) and two saliency-based methods (PuzzleMix, AutoMix). It consistently outperforms every baseline across all six datasets: on CIFAR-100 ResNet18, the largest gain is CutMix +1.61% (78.17→79.78) and FMix +0.51% (79.69→80.20); on Tiny-ImageNet ResNeXt50, CutMix gains +2.27% (66.47→68.74) and SaliencyMix +2.37% (66.55→68.92). On fine-grained CUB-200 ResNet18, FMix improves by +1.96% (77.28→79.24) and SaliencyMix by +1.21% (75.77→76.98). For saliency-based methods on CIFAR-100, AutoMix with SUMix reaches 83.82% on ResNeXt50, nearly matching the state-of-the-art AdAutoMix (83.81%), while PuzzleMix with SUMix gains +0.91% on ResNeXt50. The gains are most pronounced on smaller datasets and fine-grained benchmarks, while on ImageNet-1K the average improvement is a more modest +0.47%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUMix sits at the intersection of two lines of mixup research: (1) label-based mixup methods (Saliency Grafting, TransMix, Mixpro) that recalculate the mixing ratio λ from attention scores or mask areas, and (2) uncertainty-aware training (introspective deep metric learning, Bayesian classifiers) that regularize against data noise. Unlike prior label-recalibration approaches that rely on architectural byproducts (attention maps, saliency masks), SUMix learns a similarity function in feature space to derive a per-sample λ, and borrows the introspective uncertainty metric from Wang et al. to add a second regularization term. It is explicitly positioned as a plug-and-play module orthogonal to the mixing strategy itself, applicable to both hand-crafted (CutMix, FMix, ResizeMix) and saliency-guided (PuzzleMix, AutoMix) mixup variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Label MisMatch' problem is a genuine and under-addressed weakness of random patch-based mixup: when a cut occludes the target object, the fixed λ still assigns it substantial label weight, injecting noisy gradients. SUMix offers a general, architecture-agnostic fix that requires no changes to the mixing procedure, making it easy to adopt. However, the reported gains are modest (typically 0.1–2% top-1), the uncertainty regularization contributes less than the λ redefinition in most ablation settings, and the method has only been validated on classification. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), whether it helps in semi-supervised settings where pseudo-label quality matters, and whether the learned similarity function remains reliable under distribution shift.
