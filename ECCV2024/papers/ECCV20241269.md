## Problem

Weakly-Supervised Group Activity Recognition (WSGAR) must classify collective activities from video using only video-level labels, without actor bounding boxes or individual action annotations. Existing WSGAR methods struggle to identify and track key actors whose motions are critical for distinguishing activities, particularly in long temporal sequences with complex inter-actor dynamics. The paper addresses how to build motion-aware actor representations and complementary spatio-temporal group relations without any actor-level supervision.

## Input / Output

The model takes T RGB video frames (T=18 for NBA, T=5 for Volleyball, each resized to 1280×720) and produces a single video-level group activity class label. Optical flow maps computed by FlowNet2 are used exclusively as a training-time learning signal and are not required at inference. The final prediction is obtained by averaging the outputs of two separate MLP classifiers attached to the actor-centric and group-centric relation paths, rather than a single unified head.

## Method

Flaming-Net first extracts actor features frame-by-frame via a motion-aware encoder that applies multi-head cross-attention from K learnable queries to a CNN feature map, guided by a contrastive loss (L_flm) that aligns the encoder's attention maps with optical flow magnitude maps and a bidirectional temporal contrastive loss (L_tco) that pulls same-index tokens across adjacent frames together. These actor features are then processed through two parallel relation paths: an actor-centric path that aggregates each actor's temporal sequence with 1D convolutions before building inter-actor relations via MHSA, and a group-centric path that first constructs per-frame spatial relations via MHSA and then models spatio-temporal dynamics with a 2D convolution stack. The key design insight is that optical flow serves only as an extrinsic training objective to sharpen motion awareness in the encoder, while the dual-path architecture captures both long-range individual actor dynamics and short-window multi-actor group motion in a complementary fashion.

## Results

On the NBA dataset, Flaming-Net achieves 79.1% MCA and 76.0% MPCA with a ResNet-18 backbone (13.8M params, 307G FLOPs), setting a new state-of-the-art among single-stage weakly supervised methods. This represents a 2.8%p improvement in MPCA over the previous best single-stage method (LRMM+GCM, 73.2% MPCA) and a 4.8%p gain over DFWSGAR (71.2% MPCA). On the Volleyball dataset, Flaming-Net with Inception-v3 reaches 93.3% MCA and 95.2% Merged MCA, surpassing all weakly supervised baselines. Notably, optical flow is used only during training and is not required at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the NBA dataset, Flaming-Net outperforms all single-stage weakly supervised baselines on both MCA and MPCA: it beats DFWSGAR [19] by +3.3%p MCA and +4.8%p MPCA, and LRMM+GCM [9] by +1.3%p MCA and +2.8%p MPCA. Against SPARTAN [6], which achieves a higher MCA (82.1% vs. 79.1%), Flaming-Net still leads on MPCA by +3.2%p (76.0% vs. 72.8%), indicating better handling of class imbalance; however, SPARTAN requires a separate self-supervised pretraining stage and a ViT-Base backbone. On the Volleyball dataset, Flaming-Net with ResNet-18 (91.6% MCA) trails LRMM+GCM [9] (92.8%) and SPARTAN [6] (92.9%) by roughly 1.2–1.3%p, but with Inception-v3 it reaches 93.3% MCA and 95.2% Merged MCA, surpassing DFWSGAR [19] (90.5% MCA) and Dual-AI Fusion [13] (96.5% Merged MCA is the closest competitor at 95.2% vs. 96.5%). The paper also surpasses video backbones TSM [23] (66.6% MCA) and VideoSwin-T [24] (64.3% MCA) on NBA, and all supervised GAR baselines on Volleyball in MCA, while using fewer parameters (13.8M) than most competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flaming-Net extends the detector-free WSGAR paradigm introduced by DFWSGAR [19], which uses learnable queries and cross-attention to localize actors without bounding boxes, by adding optical flow as a training-time contrastive guidance signal to sharpen the encoder's motion awareness. The dual-path relation module draws architectural inspiration from Dual-AI [13], which also employs two parallel aggregation paths, but departs from it by replacing the second path's attention-only design with a 2D spatio-temporal convolution that explicitly models group-level motion. Unlike prior methods that incorporate flow at inference (GroupFormer [21], SBGAR [22]), Flaming-Net uses flow exclusively during training, avoiding any additional inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical insight that optical flow can serve as a free inductive bias at training time—requiring no flow computation at inference—makes motion-aware actor localization accessible without the overhead of multi-modal inference pipelines. The ablation revealing that the temporal consistency loss and flow-guided loss are individually counter-productive but synergistic when combined highlights a non-trivial interaction in auxiliary objective design. Open questions remain around removing the dependency on an external FlowNet2 network, reducing hyperparameter sensitivity (e.g., K_flm, L_flm), and extending the approach to real-time or online settings where pre-computed flow is unavailable.
