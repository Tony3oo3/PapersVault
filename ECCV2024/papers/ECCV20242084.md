## Problem

Existing 3D hand pose estimation methods assume hand crops are centered in the camera's field of view with a fixed focal length, an assumption that breaks down in egocentric images where hands are close to the camera (causing strong perspective distortion) and their 2D image location carries meaningful pose information. Additionally, accurate 3D hand annotations are only available in controlled lab settings, leaving a gap in both training and evaluation for diverse in-the-wild egocentric scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB hand crop and camera intrinsics (principal point and focal length) as input, and outputs MANO shape (β), local joint articulation (θ_local), global root pose (θ_global), and weak perspective camera parameters (scale s, translation T). These are converted to 21 3D hand keypoints, their 2D projections, and a full 3D hand mesh. The KPE encoding is computed from the angular position of the crop's corners and center in the camera's field of view, making the representation camera-invariant rather than pixel-coordinate-based.

## Method

WildHands processes hand crops with a ResNet50 backbone, augments the resulting 7×7×2048 feature map with a sparse KPE (sinusoidal encoding of the angular offsets θx, θy for the crop's corners and center relative to the principal point), and passes the combined features through three convolutional layers to a 2048-dim vector. An iterative HMR-style decoder (3 iterations, each a 3-layer MLP) predicts MANO parameters and weak perspective camera parameters, initialized from the feature vector. Training combines 3D supervision (MANO parameters, 3D/2D keypoints) on lab datasets (ARCTIC, AssemblyHands) with 2D auxiliary supervision on in-the-wild data: hand segmentation masks via differentiable rendering through SoftRasterizer, and grasp classification via a 4-layer MLP head on predicted pose parameters. The key insight is that in egocentric views, the angular position of the hand in the camera's field of view must be explicitly encoded to resolve perspective-distortion-induced shape ambiguity, and that auxiliary 2D supervision from diverse in-the-wild data improves zero-shot 3D generalization even on lab evaluation sets.

## Results

WildHands achieves the best 3D hand pose on the ARCTIC egocentric leaderboard (MPJPE 15.72 mm, MRRPE 23.88 mm), outperforming JointTransformer (16.33/26.07) and DIGIT-HRNet (16.74/25.49). In zero-shot evaluation across 4 diverse datasets, WildHands beats FrankMocap by 7.4%–66% on all metrics and outperforms HaMeR on 3 of 6 metrics (H2O MRRPE: 49.49 vs 147.87; Assembly MRRPE: 148.12 vs 334.52; Ego-Exo4D MPJPE: 55.84 vs 116.46) while being 10× smaller and trained on 5× less data. On the in-the-wild Epic-HandKps benchmark, WildHands achieves 7.20 px L2 error vs. 13.33 for FrankMocap and 4.56 for HaMeR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildHands outperforms FrankMocap across all reported metrics: H2O MPJPE 31.08 vs 58.51, Assembly MPJPE 80.40 vs 97.59, Ego-Exo4D MPJPE 55.84 vs 175.91, and Epic-HandKps L2 7.20 vs 13.33 px, despite FrankMocap being trained on 6 lab datasets. Against HaMeR (ViT-H backbone, ~3M training frames), WildHands wins on absolute-pose metrics (H2O MRRPE 49.49 vs 147.87; Assembly MRRPE 148.12 vs 334.52) and Ego-Exo4D MPJPE (55.84 vs 116.46), but loses on relative-pose H2O MPJPE (31.08 vs 23.82), Assembly MPJPE (80.40 vs 45.49), and Epic-HandKps 2D L2 (7.20 vs 4.56 px). On the ARCTIC egocentric leaderboard, WildHands achieves the best MPJPE (15.72) and MRRPE (23.88), edging out JointTransformer (16.33/26.07) and DIGIT-HRNet (16.74/25.49). Against the internal baselines, adding KPE to HandNet reduces H2O MRRPE from 141.06 to 49.49 (65% improvement) and Epic-HandKps L2 from 31.62 to 11.05, while using crops instead of full images (HandNet vs ArcticNet-SF) cuts H2O MPJPE from 83.84 to 38.06. The paper notes that HaMeR's advantage on relative-pose metrics likely stems from its much larger ViT-H backbone and 5× more training data, suggesting WildHands could close the gap with scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ArcticNet-SF and FrankMocap as architectural bases, adopting the HMR-style iterative decoder and MANO parameterization, while introducing the intrinsics-aware positional encoding (KPE) from a concurrent work [54] specifically for hand crops. It departs from prior egocentric hand pose methods by systematically questioning assumptions inherited from exocentric settings—namely that centered crops with a fixed focal length suffice—and showing that the hand's angular position in the camera's field of view carries critical information for 3D prediction. The training strategy of combining 3D supervision from lab datasets (ARCTIC, AssemblyHands) with 2D auxiliary supervision (segmentation masks, grasp labels) from in-the-wild data (Epic-Kitchens, Ego4D) extends the auxiliary-supervision paradigm used in 3D human mesh recovery [34] and differentiable rendering [42,43] to the hand pose domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic ablation of design choices (crops, camera-aware encoding, auxiliary supervision, multi-dataset scaling) for 3D hand pose in egocentric settings, offering practitioners clear guidance on what actually matters for generalization beyond lab conditions. The finding that a simple sinusoidal encoding of angular position (KPE) outperforms dedicated perspective-correction layers and camera-aware convolutions is practically impactful and broadly applicable to any crop-based 3D estimation task. Key limitations include the requirement for known camera intrinsics and manually tuned loss weights across heterogeneous supervision sources; open questions include whether learned loss weighting, larger backbones, and further data scaling can close the remaining gap to much larger models like HaMeR, and whether the approach transfers to other articulated-body estimation tasks.
