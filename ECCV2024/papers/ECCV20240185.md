## Problem

Existing 3DGS compression methods focus exclusively on parameter values (pruning, vector quantization) while ignoring the spatial structural relations among Gaussians. Scaffold-GS introduces anchors to cluster nearby Gaussians but still treats each anchor independently, leaving substantial spatial redundancy unexploited. This paper asks whether the inherent consistencies among unorganized anchors can be captured by a well-structured hash grid to enable far more compact representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240185/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained Scaffold-GS model (anchors with features, scaling, offsets, and associated 3D Gaussians) and produces a compressed bitstream of five components: anchor attributes encoded via arithmetic coding with context-estimated probabilities, a binary hash grid (mixed 3D-2D, 12+4 levels, D^h=4), offset masks, anchor locations (32-bit), and MLP weights (16-bit). At decode time, the bitstream is decompressed to reconstruct the full 3DGS model for real-time novel view rendering at FPS comparable to Scaffold-GS, since the hash grid is discarded after decoding anchor attributes.

## Method

HAC jointly learns a structured binary hash grid and uses it as context to model the spatial consistencies of Scaffold-GS anchors. Each anchor's location queries the hash grid via trilinear interpolation to obtain a hash feature f^h, which is fed into a shared 3-layer MLP that outputs both adaptive quantization step refinements (restricting steps to (0, 2Q_0) via tanh) and per-attribute Gaussian distribution parameters (μ, σ). These enable differentiable probability estimation for arithmetic coding of quantized anchor attributes, while the hash grid itself is binarized via straight-through estimation and compressed using occurrence-frequency-based entropy coding. An adaptive offset masking module prunes invalid Gaussians and their parent anchors. Training jointly optimizes the Scaffold-GS rendering loss, a normalized entropy loss over anchor attributes and hash grid, and a masking loss, with λ_e varied to achieve different rate-distortion operating points.

## Results

HAC achieves a compression ratio of over 75× compared to vanilla 3DGS and over 11× over Scaffold-GS while maintaining or improving fidelity. On Synthetic-NeRF, HAC-highrate reaches PSNR 33.71 at only 1.86 MB (vs. 68.46 MB for 3DGS and 19.36 MB for Scaffold-GS). On the large-scale BungeeNeRF dataset, HAC-highrate achieves PSNR 27.08 at 29.72 MB, surpassing both 3DGS (24.87 dB, 1616 MB) and Scaffold-GS (26.62 dB, 183.0 MB). Across all five datasets, HAC-highrate consistently matches or exceeds Scaffold-GS in PSNR while reducing file size by 10–12×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAC outperforms all eight baselines in the size-vs-fidelity tradeoff. Against vanilla 3DGS [19], HAC-highrate reduces size by 75× (e.g., 1.86 MB vs. 68.46 MB on Synthetic-NeRF) while matching or exceeding PSNR (33.71 vs. 33.80). Against Scaffold-GS [27], HAC achieves 11× smaller files (1.86 vs. 19.36 MB) with higher PSNR (33.71 vs. 33.41) on Synthetic-NeRF, and similar gains on all other datasets. Among dedicated 3DGS compression methods, HAC-highrate beats Lee et al. [22] (5.54 MB, PSNR 33.33), Compressed3D [32] (3.68 MB, PSNR 32.94), EAGLES [14] (5.74 MB, PSNR 32.54), LightGaussian [11] (7.84 MB, PSNR 32.73), and Navaneet et al. [31] (4.42 MB, PSNR 33.09) on Synthetic-NeRF in both size and fidelity. The closest competitor is Morgenstern et al. [29], which achieves a slightly smaller size (2.20 MB) but at a significant fidelity cost (PSNR 31.05 vs. 33.71, a 2.66 dB gap). On the most challenging BungeeNeRF dataset, HAC-highrate (27.08 dB, 29.72 MB) surpasses all baselines in PSNR while being 54× smaller than 3DGS and 6× smaller than Scaffold-GS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Scaffold-GS, which introduced anchor-centered clustering of 3D Gaussians, and extends it by adding a structured binary hash grid as a spatial context model for entropy coding of anchor attributes. It departs from the dominant 3DGS compression paradigm (pruning, codebooks, vector quantization on parameter 'values') by explicitly exploiting structural/spatial relations among anchors, drawing an analogy to context-based image and video compression. The hash grid compression component borrows from Instant-NGP/NeRF compression literature (CNC, BiRF), while the adaptive quantization and Gaussian distribution modeling follow learned image compression conventions (Ballé et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HAC is the first to demonstrate that context-based entropy coding can be effectively applied to 3DGS, achieving 75× compression over vanilla 3DGS and 11× over Scaffold-GS with comparable or improved fidelity — a practically significant reduction for deploying 3DGS on resource-constrained devices or large-scale scenes. A key limitation is the ~0.9× increase in training time over Scaffold-GS, and the method is tightly coupled to the Scaffold-GS anchor architecture, leaving open whether the hash-grid context idea generalizes to other 3DGS variants without anchors. The finding that a simple 4-dimensional binary hash grid captures enough spatial structure to model anchor attribute distributions is a useful empirical insight for future 3D representation compression.
