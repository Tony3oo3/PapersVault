## Problem

Single-domain image denoising faces a fundamental trade-off: raw-domain denoisers generalize across noise distributions but leave residual noise that ISP processing amplifies, while sRGB-domain denoisers handle ISP variations but cannot model the spatially varying, ISP-distorted noise they observe. No prior learning-based framework has combined both domains in an end-to-end trainable pipeline that adapts to unseen sensors, noise levels, and ISP configurations simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy Bayer-pattern raw image (256×256 patches during training, 512×512 during inference) along with EXIF metadata (black level, white balance gain, color correction matrix, Bayer pattern) to produce a final denoised sRGB image, with an intermediate denoised raw image as a byproduct. A noise map computed from the raw signal (standard deviation √(KR*+σ_r²)) is propagated through the differentiable ISP to generate a corresponding sRGB noise map for the second denoiser. At inference, the differentiable ISP is discarded entirely, and the two denoisers operate as plug-and-play modules within any target ISP pipeline.

## Method

DualDn places two denoising networks (e.g., SwinIR, MIRNet-v2, or Restormer) in the raw and sRGB domains, connected by a custom differentiable ISP that implements demosaicing (modified AHD), white balance, color correction, and a parameterized tone-mapping curve (deep curve with random amplification α). A noise map mechanism bridges the domains: the raw noise map is passed through the differentiable ISP alongside the image signal to produce an sRGB noise map, enabling the sRGB denoiser to target ISP-amplified residual noise specifically. Training synthesizes noisy raw images via a Poisson-Gaussian model with a joint log-normal distribution over gain K and read-noise variance σ_r², while randomly sampling ISP parameters to expose the network to diverse degradations. Both denoisers are jointly optimized end-to-end with a combined L1 loss in raw and sRGB domains (λ=1.0), and the differentiable ISP is discarded at inference, leaving the denoisers as domain-agnostic plug-and-play modules.

## Results

On the MIT-Adobe FiveK synthetic evaluation, DualDn (Restormer backbone) achieves 33.98 / 32.64 / 31.48 dB PSNR at ISP amplification ratios α = 0.2 / 0.5 / 0.8, consistently outperforming both raw-only and sRGB-only single-domain variants by 1.0–2.0 dB across all three tested backbones (SwinIR, MIRNet-v2, Restormer). On the DND real-noise benchmark, DualDn reaches 49.15 dB PSNR (raw) and 40.56 dB PSNR (sRGB), slightly surpassing CycleISP (49.13 / 40.50 dB) which was specifically fine-tuned for that dataset's ISP. The method also generalizes to unseen ISP modules (sharpen, local tone-mapping) where raw-only denoising degrades by up to 12 dB while DualDn remains robust.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is against single-domain variants of three SOTA backbones (SwinIR, MIRNet-v2, Restormer) [38,55,52], where DualDn wins by 1.0–2.0 dB PSNR at every noise level and ISP amplification ratio, with the gap widening under stronger noise (larger K) and higher ISP amplification. On the DND real-noise benchmark [44], DualDn (49.15 dB raw / 40.56 dB sRGB) marginally beats CycleISP [53] (49.13 / 40.50 dB), which was specifically designed and fine-tuned for that dataset's fixed ISP, and clearly outperforms UPI [8] (48.88 dB raw) and DnCNN [59] (47.37 dB raw). In the sRGB domain on DND, DualDn (40.56 dB) surpasses Restormer [52] (40.02 dB), Uformer [47] (39.77 dB), MIRNet [54] (39.72 dB), and Masked denoising [14] (33.75 dB) by 0.5–6.8 dB. Notably, DualDn is trained exclusively on synthetic noise from MIT-Adobe FiveK without any fine-tuning on DND's cameras or ISP, yet still matches or exceeds methods trained directly on DND data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-domain denoising paradigm (either raw or sRGB) into a unified dual-domain framework, building on the Poisson-Gaussian noise model of Foi et al. for synthetic noise generation and repurposing the deep curve formulation of Guo et al. [25] as a differentiable tone-mapping module within a custom ISP. It departs from prior learning-based denoisers by introducing a differentiable ISP as a trainable bridge between two denoising networks, enabling end-to-end joint optimization—a capability absent in both raw-domain methods (which ignore ISP effects) and sRGB-domain methods (which cannot model spatially varying sensor noise). The noise map mechanism, originally designed for raw denoising [60], is extended here to the sRGB domain for the first time, and the framework is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated across SwinIR, MIRNet-v2, and Restormer backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that a model trained exclusively on synthetic MIT-Adobe FiveK data generalizes to unseen commercial smartphone ISPs (Xiaomi, iPhone, HUAWEI) and outperforms their purpose-built on-camera denoisers, suggesting a path toward universal denoising modules that eliminate per-device calibration. Limitations include the simplified differentiable ISP (which omits modules like color stylization and uses a modified AHD demosaicer), the exclusion of fixed-pattern noise, and the reliance on EXIF metadata at inference. Open questions include whether the framework extends to video denoising, whether more complex or proprietary ISP pipelines (e.g., multi-frame HDR pipelines) can be handled without retraining, and whether the dual-domain decomposition remains beneficial when the ISP is extremely aggressive (e.g., heavy AI-based processing).
