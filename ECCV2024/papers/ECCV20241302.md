## Problem

Existing diffusion-based virtual try-on methods (e.g., LaDI-VTON, DCI-VTON) rely on holistic latent-space diffusion guided primarily by CLIP visual embeddings, which are optimized for coarse image-text alignment and cannot capture fine-grained garment textures such as complex patterns and text. This leads to degraded high-frequency detail preservation in the synthesized garment, a critical failure for practical e-commerce applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241302/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frontal-view person image (H×W×3) and an in-shop garment image (H'×W'×3) as input, operating at 512×384 resolution. The garment is warped to conform to the person's pose, and a garment-agnostic image (person with garment region masked) is extracted; both the warped and unwarped garment serve as appearance conditions. The output is a photorealistic person image (H×W×3) wearing the target garment, generated over 100 DDIM denoising steps with classifier-free guidance scale 7.5.

## Method

GarDiff remoulds a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2.1 by replacing the UNet's cross-attention layers with a garment-focused vision adapter that ingests two complementary appearance priors: CLIP visual embeddings from the unwarped garment (for holistic structure) and VAE embeddings from the warped garment (for fine-grained texture recovery). A decoupled cross-attention mechanism processes these two priors separately, and a garment-focused attention mask (downsampled from the warped mask) suppresses attention weights in non-garment regions to enforce local fine-grained alignment. Training is supervised with a composite objective combining the standard MSE denoising loss and a novel appearance loss that couples a DISTS-based spatial perceptual loss with a Sobel-edge high-frequency promoted loss, both applied only within the garment region. The model is fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion 2.1 for 200k steps on 4 RTX 4090 GPUs, with garment embeddings randomly dropped at 5% probability to enable classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On VITON-HD, GarDiff achieves SSIM 0.912, LPIPS 0.036, FID_p 6.02, KID_p 0.019, FID_u 7.89, and KID_u 0.027, outperforming the strongest baseline DCI-VTON (SSIM 0.896, LPIPS 0.043, FID_u 8.09, KID_u 0.028) across all metrics. On DressCode, GarDiff leads in all three sub-categories (upper-body, lower-body, dresses), achieving the best SSIM (0.952/0.939/0.891), LPIPS (0.030/0.035/0.065), and FID_p (8.69/8.01/8.77) scores. A user study on 100 garment-person pairs confirms GarDiff ranks first in both garment detail preservation and human pose alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GarDiff outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across both VITON-HD and DressCode. Against the closest diffusion-based competitor DCI-VTON [12], GarDiff improves SSIM by 1.78% (0.912 vs. 0.896) and LPIPS by 16.3% (0.036 vs. 0.043) on VITON-HD, while also achieving lower FID_u (7.89 vs. 8.09) and KID_u (0.027 vs. 0.028). Compared to LaDI-VTON [25], GarDiff gains 3.7% SSIM and 39% lower LPIPS on VITON-HD, and 2.58% SSIM on DressCode upper-body. Among GAN-based methods, GP-VTON [36] is the strongest (SSIM 0.894, FID_u 9.20 on VITON-HD), yet GarDiff still surpasses it by 1.8% SSIM and 14.5% FID_u. On DressCode, GarDiff beats the best GAN baseline GP-VTON [36] by 0.5% SSIM (upper-body) and 1.3% SSIM (dresses), while also achieving substantially lower FID_p scores (8.69 vs. not reported, but 8.01 vs. not reported for lower-body). The ablation confirms that the garment-focused adapter (GFA) contributes the largest single gain (SSIM 0.809→0.898), with the appearance loss (AL) providing further improvement (0.898→0.912).

## Relation to Prior Work

GarDiff extends the diffusion-based VTON line initiated by LaDI-VTON and DCI-VTON, which adapt pre-trained Stable Diffusion by conditioning on warped garment images. The key departure is twofold: (1) it augments the CLIP visual embedding (used by prior diffusion VTON methods) with VAE embeddings of the warped garment, arguing that VAE's reconstruction objective preserves fine-grained texture that CLIP's coarse image-text alignment discards; and (2) it replaces the standard cross-attention in the UNet with a garment-focused adapter that uses decoupled cross-attention and a spatial mask to enforce local fine-grained alignment. A novel appearance loss (DISTS-based spatial perceptual + Sobel high-frequency) further targets the specific failure mode of texture degradation in the garment region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and visually obvious failure mode of current diffusion-based VTON—loss of high-frequency details like text and complex patterns—through a targeted architectural and loss-function modification rather than a full pipeline redesign. The garment-focused adapter and appearance loss are modular components that could be transplanted into other diffusion-based image editing or inpainting tasks where local fidelity to a reference is critical. Limitations include evaluation only at 512×384 resolution, reliance on a separate warping network as a prerequisite, and testing restricted to upper-body garments and dresses; generalization to full-body try-on, video try-on, or lower-body garments with complex draping remains open. The paper also flags the dual-use risk of the technology for non-consensual image manipulation.
