## Problem

Pretrained Vision-Language Models like CLIP are highly vulnerable to small adversarial perturbations on the image input, yet the most effective defense—adversarial training—is computationally prohibitive for large VLMs due to the need for repeated forward/backward passes through the image encoder at every training iteration. Existing input denoising defenses (super-resolution, rescaling, diffusion purification) offer only partial protection. The paper asks whether adversarial robustness of the image encoder can be improved by modifying only the textual input, without touching the image encoder or the input image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241188/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB image (processed by the frozen CLIP image encoder into an L-dimensional embedding) and a learnable text prompt of M=32 continuous vectors prepended to the class-name token (processed by the frozen CLIP text encoder into K text embeddings), and outputs a class label via softmax over cosine similarities. During training, the image encoder is used only once to generate a one-pass adversarial embedding bank (N×L matrix) via PGD-10 with ε=8/255, after which the image encoder is entirely discarded and only the text encoder is used for gradient backpropagation to update the M learnable vectors.

## Method

AdvPT operates in two decoupled stages. First, it generates an adversarial embedding bank by applying PGD (with KL-divergence loss à la TRADES) to the frozen image encoder on clean training images, storing the resulting adversarial embeddings, and then discarding the image encoder entirely. Second, it optimizes M learnable text-prompt vectors V=[v₁,…,v_M] by backpropagating a cross-entropy loss through the frozen text encoder, where the loss measures how well the text embeddings (produced from [V, c_i]) match the adversarial image embeddings in the bank for the correct class. The key insight is that the text encoder's pretrained knowledge can be leveraged to 'rectify' adversarial embeddings without any gradient flow through the image encoder, reducing the number of trainable parameters to just M×L and eliminating the per-iteration adversarial example generation that makes adversarial training infeasible for VLMs. The resulting learnable vectors are dataset-specific and can be combined additively with input denoising defenses (DiffPure, super-resolution, rescaling) for synergistic robustness gains.

## Results

Under white-box PGD-40 attacks, AdvPT improves robust accuracy over vanilla CLIP by 13.3–38.9 percentage points across 8 datasets (e.g., 6.6%→19.9% on ImageNet, 22.2%→55.4% on EuroSAT for ViT-B/16; 28.5%→42.9% on ImageNet, 23.3%→62.2% on EuroSAT for ViT-L/14). Under black-box RAP attacks, gains are more modest (1.6–23.5 pp), with the largest improvement on Flowers (70.6%→94.1% for ViT-L/14). When combined with input denoising defenses (Super-resolution, DiffPure, Rescale), AdvPT yields synergistic gains, e.g., 13.8%→60.4% on Flowers with Super-resolution (ViT-B/16). AdvPT is at least 100× more time-efficient than PGD-10 adversarial training while achieving superior robust accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CLIP (hand-crafted prompts), AdvPT consistently wins on all 8 datasets under both PGD-40 and RAP attacks, with the largest PGD gains on EuroSAT (+38.9 pp, ViT-L/14) and Flowers (+35.9 pp, ViT-L/14). Compared to CoOp (learnable prompts tuned on clean images), AdvPT achieves substantially higher adversarial robustness at a slight cost to clean accuracy, revealing a generalization-robustness trade-off that narrows with model scale. Against input denoising defenses (Super-resolution, DiffPure, Rescale), AdvPT is complementary: combined with Super-resolution on Flowers (ViT-B/16), accuracy jumps from 13.8% to 60.4%, and with DiffPure on ImageNet (ViT-L/14) from 64.5% to 69.7%. Against PGD-10 adversarial training and Fast AT on Pets, AdvPT is at least 100× faster while delivering higher robust accuracy, making it the only practical defense for large VLMs. On domain-shift evaluation (ImageNet→ImageNetV2/A/R/Sketch), AdvPT-trained vectors transfer and outperform vanilla CLIP in robust accuracy across all four target domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CoOp prompt-learning framework for CLIP (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022), repurposing its learnable text vectors from an accuracy-optimization objective to an adversarial-robustness objective. It departs from the dominant adversarial-defense literature in two ways: rather than robustifying the image encoder via adversarial training (AT, Fast AT, TRADES) — which is computationally prohibitive for large VLMs — or denoising the input image (DiffPure, super-resolution, rescaling), AdvPT modifies only the text prompt to align clean text embeddings with pre-computed adversarial image embeddings. The adversarial embedding bank, a one-shot offline artifact that replaces the iterative adversarial example generation loop of AT, is the key architectural departure that makes the approach tractable for frozen VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdvPT offers a practical, 100×+ more efficient alternative to adversarial training for securing large VLMs, and its demonstrated synergy with input-denoising defenses (e.g., +46.6% over Super-resolution alone on Flowers) suggests a compositional defense strategy that practitioners can adopt without retraining. However, the scope is limited to CLIP-style contrastive models and zero-shot image recognition; applicability to generative VLMs (GPT-4V), VQA, or text-modality attacks remains unexplored. The generalization-robustness trade-off, while mitigated at ViT-L/14 scale, raises open questions about whether prompt-based robustness can match full adversarial training as model scale and task diversity grow.
