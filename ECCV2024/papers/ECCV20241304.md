## Problem

Naively combining triplane-based radiance fields with joint camera pose optimization is prone to local minima because the triplane's local updating policy accumulates irrecoverable errors when poses are inaccurate, and standard feature aggregations (sum or Hadamard product) impose conflicting gradient signals on pose versus scene parameters. No prior work has addressed how to make the triplane representation robust to pose uncertainty during joint optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB images (480×640 on LLFF, 400×400 on NeRF-Synthetic) with known intrinsics and either unknown extrinsics (initialized to identity) or noisy extrinsics (Gaussian perturbation in SE(3)), plus DINOv2 patch features extracted from the first image as a texture prior. It outputs refined camera poses (rotation in degrees, translation scaled by 100), rendered novel views, and a multi-resolution triplane radiance field. Training runs for 70k steps on LLFF (switching to stage 2 at step 4000) and 60k steps on NeRF-Synthetic (switch at step 2000).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline built around three components. First, a Disentangled Triplane Generation Module replaces directly-learned feature grids with a convolution-based generator (three separate unshared networks, one per plane) that takes frozen Gaussian noise tokens as input, so that gradient updates radiate across the entire plane rather than only touching a few interpolated grids, thereby injecting global context and preventing local error accumulation. Second, a Disentangled Plane Aggregation (DPA) formula uses gradient-detached copies of plane features to route the product-form signal to the triplane parameters and the sum-form signal to the camera poses, eliminating the optimization collision that standard Hadamard or sum aggregation causes. Third, a two-stage warm-start strategy discards the generator after the first stage and switches to direct multi-resolution triplane optimization, recovering high-frequency details that the generator's implicit smoothness constraint suppresses. A DINOv2-based cross-attention module further embeds 2D image features into the triplane tokens to reduce pose-scene ambiguity.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset, the proposed method achieves a mean PSNR of 25.90 dB and SSIM of 0.828 for novel view synthesis, outperforming the best baseline HASH-BARF (24.79 dB / 0.772) by over 1 dB. For pose estimation on LLFF, it achieves mean rotation error of 0.259° and translation error of 0.194 (×100), the best among all compared methods. On NeRF-Synthetic, the method reaches a mean PSNR of 31.83 dB (vs. 29.86 for HASH-BARF) and rotation error of 0.156°, demonstrating consistent gains across both real and synthetic benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against four joint pose-NeRF optimization baselines (BARF, GARF, L2G-NeRF, HASH-BARF) and the reference K-Planes with ground-truth COLMAP poses. On LLFF, the proposed method wins on all four metrics: PSNR (25.90 vs. 24.79 for HASH-BARF), SSIM (0.828 vs. 0.772), rotation error (0.259° vs. 0.329° for GARF), and translation error (0.194 vs. 0.258 for HASH-BARF). On NeRF-Synthetic, the gains are even more pronounced in PSNR (31.83 vs. 29.86 for HASH-BARF, a ~2 dB improvement) and rotation error (0.156° vs. 0.189°). Notably, the method also surpasses K-Planes trained with ground-truth poses in pose estimation (mean rotation 0.259° vs. 65.82° for the naive baseline) while matching its NVS quality (25.90 vs. 25.98 PSNR), confirming that the disentangled triplane approach effectively compensates for unknown poses. HASH-BARF is the strongest baseline overall, but the triplane-based method consistently outperforms it by a clear margin on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established lines: triplane-based radiance fields (K-Planes, HexPlane) that assume accurate poses, and joint pose-NeRF optimization (BARF, GARF, L2G-NeRF) that relies on slow MLP-based representations. The key departure is identifying that the naive combination of triplane with pose estimation introduces two previously unaddressed failure modes—local feature-grid updating that accumulates irrecoverable errors, and aggregation-induced gradient conflicts between planes and poses. The triplane generator (a per-scene CNN parameterizing the feature grids) and the Disentangled Plane Aggregation (gradient-detached product/sum hybrid) are novel architectural contributions specifically designed to resolve these triplane-specific pathologies, while the two-stage warm-start strategy bridges the generator's smoothing bias with direct grid optimization for high-frequency detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that triplane representations, long favored for their speed and compactness, can be made compatible with pose-free or pose-noisy settings—a practical gap that has limited their adoption in real-world pipelines where COLMAP is unavailable or unreliable. The gradient-flow analysis showing how Hadamard product vs. sum aggregation differentially biases pose versus scene updates is a useful conceptual tool for anyone designing disentangled multi-plane representations. However, evaluation is confined to small-scale LLFF and NeRF-Synthetic scenes, and the method still requires per-scene optimization with a two-stage schedule, leaving open whether the approach scales to large or dynamic scenes and how it compares to the rapidly evolving 3D Gaussian Splatting pose-estimation literature.
