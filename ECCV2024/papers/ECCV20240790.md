## Problem

Multi-object multi-part scene segmentation suffers from three persistent failures in existing methods: incorrect foreground segmentation that cascades into part errors, poor boundary delineation between objects and parts, and systematic failure to segment small or thin parts. Prior approaches address these through auxiliary tasks or architectural modifications, but lack a unified, broadly applicable solution that can be applied across diverse segmentation architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240790/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image (H×W×3) and augments it at inference/training time with two additional channels—a binary foreground/background mask and a binary boundary edge mask—producing a 5-channel input (H×W×5). The output is a per-pixel multi-object multi-part segmentation mask assigning each pixel to one of 58, 108, or 201 part classes (plus background) across all objects in the scene. The two auxiliary channels are generated by frozen pre-trained networks (an object segmentation model and HED) and are not learned end-to-end.

## Method

OLAF is a three-component plug-and-play recipe applied on top of existing segmentation backbones (DeepLabV3, FLOAT, BSANet, GMNet, SegFormer). First, the RGB input is augmented with foreground and edge channels obtained from frozen pre-trained models, providing a structural inductive bias that guides the network toward object boundaries from the start of training. Second, a Low-level Dense Feature (LDF) module is inserted into the encoder, concatenating 3×3-convolved features from the first two backbone blocks, upsampling them to a consistent resolution, and passing through ASPP to capture multi-scale context for small/thin parts. Third, a targeted weight adaptation procedure initializes the two new input-channel filters by averaging the existing RGB channel weights, followed by a 5-epoch warm-up phase to prevent destabilization of pre-trained backbone weights. The approach requires no changes to the decoder or loss function and adds only modest computational overhead (1.5–20% more parameters, ~5–10% more training time).

## Results

On Pascal-Parts-58, OLAF-augmented FLOAT achieves 62.7 mIoU (vs. 61.0 for FLOAT alone, a +3.3 gain) and 55.4 sqIoU (vs. 54.2). On Pascal-Parts-108, FLOAT+O reaches 50.3 mIoU (+3.5) and 43.4 sqIoU (+2.9). On the most challenging Pascal-Parts-201 variant, FLOAT†+O achieves 40.9 mIoU, a +4.0 improvement over the FLOAT† baseline (37.7). On PartImageNet, Segformer+O (MiT-B2) reaches 65.46 mIOU, surpassing both Segformer (60.52) and the SOTA Compositor with Swin-T (64.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OLAF consistently improves every baseline it is applied to. On Pascal-Parts-58, FLOAT+O (62.7 mIoU) outperforms DeepLabv3+O (59.0), BSANet+O (59.8), and GMNet+O (60.2), and also beats the un-augmented SOTA FLOAT (61.0) by 1.7 mIoU; the only method exceeding FLOAT+O is HIPIE (63.8), which is evaluated only on the 58-variant. On Pascal-Parts-108, FLOAT+O (50.3 mIoU) leads all compared methods, surpassing GMNet+O (47.2) by 3.1 and BSANet+O (47.1) by 3.2. On PartImageNet, Segformer+O (65.46 mIOU) beats both the SOTA Compositor with Swin-T (64.64) and the original Segformer (60.52), while DeepLabv3++O (61.71) surpasses both DeepLabv3+ (57.53) and Compositor with ResNet-50 (61.44). The gains are largest for the SOTA FLOAT baseline (+3.3 to +4.0 mIoU across variants) and smallest for already-competitive methods like BSANet (+1.2 to +1.6), suggesting OLAF is most impactful when the base model is strong but still limited by boundary and small-part errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

OLAF positions itself as a plug-and-play augmentation layer over existing multi-object multi-part segmentation models (FLOAT, BSANet, GMNet, DeepLabv3, SegFormer) rather than a new end-to-end architecture. It departs from prior work that incorporated object and boundary awareness through auxiliary losses or feature-level guidance (Zhao et al. [74], Michieli et al. [45]) by instead injecting these cues directly as additional input channels, framing them as a structural inductive bias. The LDF module extends the low-level feature guidance literature (skip connections, full-resolution networks) by adding ASPP to recover multi-scale context that pure downsampling destroys for small parts. The weight adaptation technique is a practical contribution addressing the underexplored problem of stably extending pre-trained 3-channel backbones to augmented inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt OLAF on top of any existing segmentation backbone without architectural redesign, which lowers the barrier to improving part segmentation performance. The consistent 3.3–4.0 mIoU gains across Pascal-Parts-58/108/201 and PartImageNet, spanning CNN, U-Net, and Transformer families, suggest broad applicability. A key limitation is the dependence on the quality of the frozen auxiliary masks (object segmentation, HED edges); poor-quality masks degrade results, coupling OLAF's performance to upstream model quality. The negative result on depth maps (no gain despite adding a 6th channel) is an interesting open question about what structural cues actually help intra-object part discrimination versus inter-object separation.
