## Problem

Despite growing interest in point cloud transfer learning, there is no principled understanding of when and why different pre-training strategies (supervised vs. contrastive) succeed or fail across architectures and downstream tasks. In particular, the role of early layers in 3D transfer learning remains unexplored, and the claim that supervised pre-training is an upper bound on information gain from pre-training has not been rigorously validated.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242082/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 3D point clouds of 2048 points (with spatial coordinates) and produces pre-trained encoder features that are evaluated via linear probing or full fine-tuning on classification (ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN) and semantic segmentation (S3DIS) tasks. A key analytical output is the per-layer gradient norm and t-SNE visualization of individual layer activations, which reveals how features at different depths behave under domain shift. The proposed regularization additionally predicts pointwise normal vectors (computed via PCA on 30-point neighborhoods) as an auxiliary geometric signal during pre-training.

## Method

The paper conducts the first systematic comparison of supervised (cross-entropy on ShapeNet) and contrastive (PointInfoNCE point-level) pre-training across five diverse backbones—PointNet, PointMLP, DGCNN, MinkowskiNet, and PCT—under both linear probing and fine-tuning protocols. Layer-wise gradient norm analysis reveals that supervised pre-training produces low gradient norms in early layers, indicating features trapped in a local optimization region that resist adaptation to new data, whereas contrastive pre-training yields more adaptable early-layer features. To address this, the authors introduce a simple geometric regularization: an auxiliary normal-prediction head (using absolute cosine similarity loss) is attached to the first two layers during supervised pre-training, injecting category-agnostic geometric information that increases early-layer gradient norms and improves fine-tuning performance by ~1% across all downstream tasks, outperforming contrastive pre-training in several settings.

## Results

In the linear probing setting, supervised pre-training consistently outperforms contrastive pre-training (PointContrast) across nearly all five architectures on both ModelNet40 and ScanObjectNN, even under significant domain shift (synthetic→real). In the full fine-tuning setting, the advantage reverses: contrastive pre-training yields better results for DGCNN, MinkowskiNet, and PCT. The proposed layer-wise geometric regularization (normal prediction on the first two layers) improves supervised fine-tuning by ~1% on ModelNet40 (92.78→93.34) and ~1% on ScanObjectNN (84.94→85.91) with DGCNN, closing the gap with contrastive pre-training. Layer-wise analysis reveals that even the first layer of 3D backbones achieves high discriminative accuracy (e.g., DGCNN 1st layer: 81.80% vs. last layer: 90.92% on ModelNet40), in stark contrast to 2D networks where early layers are near-random.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is PointContrast [Xie et al., ECCV 2020], the point-level contrastive pre-training method. In linear probing, supervised pre-training wins across nearly all architectures and datasets, demonstrating that supervised features are more directly usable as task-ready representations even under domain shift (e.g., synthetic ShapeNet → real ScanObjectNN). In full fine-tuning, the comparison flips: contrastive pre-training outperforms supervised for DGCNN, MinkowskiNet, and PCT, because supervised early layers exhibit low gradient norms and resist adaptation to new data. The proposed geometric regularization (auxiliary normal prediction on the first two layers) narrows this gap substantially: supervised+reg reaches 85.91% on ScanObjectNN FT vs. 85.05% for contrastive alone, and 93.34% on ModelNet40 FT vs. 85.05%, effectively matching or exceeding contrastive performance. On S3DIS semantic segmentation (Table 1), supervised pre-training with DGCNN achieves 49.99 IoU vs. 49.07 for random initialization, while contrastive values were not reported in the main table. The paper also notes that supervised pre-training is not an upper bound on information gain, contradicting the claim in PointContrast [51].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PointContrast [51] as its contrastive pre-training baseline and extends the 2D transfer learning analysis literature (layer-wise feature inspection, gradient norm studies) to the 3D point cloud domain for the first time. It departs from prior 3D pre-training work, which typically demonstrated a single method in a narrow setting, by establishing a unified evaluation framework comparing supervised and contrastive pre-training across five diverse architectures (PointNet, PointMLP, DGCNN, MinkowskiNet, PCT) and three downstream datasets spanning synthetic-to-real and object-to-scene domain shifts. The proposed layer-wise geometric regularization (normal prediction) is a new loss component that repurposes a simple geometric signal to address a specific failure mode identified through the paper's gradient analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, quantitative account of when and why point cloud transfer learning succeeds or fails, offering practitioners a clear decision framework: supervised pre-training for linear probing, contrastive for fine-tuning, and geometric regularization to bridge the gap. The finding that 3D early layers are inherently class-discriminative (unlike their 2D counterparts) reframes how one should think about feature hierarchy in point cloud networks. Limitations include reliance on a single pre-training source (ShapeNet), a modest ~1% regularization gain, and evaluation on a limited set of architectures and tasks; open questions include whether these findings generalize to non-rigid shapes, larger-scale scenes, and whether new architectures can produce truly multi-scale, density-robust features.
