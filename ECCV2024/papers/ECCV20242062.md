## Problem

While diffusion models have proven effective for 2D video synthesis and 3D human motion generation, their application to 4D facial expression animation remains unexplored. Existing guided 4D expression methods (e.g., MO3DGAN, LSTM-based approaches) rely on landmarks or GAN architectures and fail to capture high-fidelity extreme expressions. This paper addresses the gap by formulating the first diffusion process that operates directly on fixed-topology mesh space for controllable, customizable 4D facial expression generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242062/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neutral fixed-topology 3D mesh and an expression signal vector encoding both the expression category and its intensity (a one-hot-like vector of dimension M) as conditioning. It outputs a sequence of per-frame mesh deformations relative to the neutral mesh, which are applied to reconstruct the animated face. For the textured extension, a neutral texture is additionally provided, and the output includes a corresponding per-frame texture sequence generated via a latent diffusion model. Training operates frame-by-frame on individual deformations rather than full animation sequences, enabling high-resolution mesh training without sequence-length memory constraints.

## Method

The core contribution is a mesh-space diffusion process where a Spiral Convolutional Network (SCN) replaces the standard point-cloud MLP as the denoising model, preserving mesh connectivity and capturing local geometric detail that permutation-invariant architectures miss. Training follows a static, frame-by-frame paradigm: each frame is expressed as a deformation from the neutral mesh and denoised under a standard DDPM objective conditioned on the expression stage vector and timestep. For temporal coherence, the paper introduces consistent noise sampling: a single noise realization is shared across all frames, and after the first frame is fully denoised over T steps, subsequent frames begin from a late denoised state (t_s) and require only the remaining t_s steps, both ensuring smooth transitions and enabling parallel generation of later frames. The textured extension pairs this geometry model with a latent diffusion model conditioned on the neutral texture latent, expression intensity, and frame label, trained with the same frame-by-frame protocol and consistent noise sampling strategy.

## Results

On the CoMA dataset (12 identities, 12 extreme expressions), the proposed method outperforms both baselines across all metrics. The best variant (Ours-Varying) achieves 78.90% expression classification accuracy, surpassing even the ground truth (77.77%), with a specificity error of 1.61 mm. Under the fairest non-customizable comparison (Ours-Local), the method achieves 72.88% accuracy, 1.74 mm specificity, and FID 43.01, compared to MO3DGAN's 67.22% / 2.18 mm / 52.21 and LSTM's 45.12% / 3.2 mm / 59.14. The advantage is most pronounced on extreme-expression frames, where the per-frame specificity gap widens relative to baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two prior 4D expression generation methods: MO3DGAN [33] (a GAN-based sparse-to-dense landmark decoder) and LSTM+GNN [40] (a two-stage LSTM/GNN pipeline trained on 4DFAB). The proposed method wins decisively on all three metrics against both baselines. Against MO3DGAN, Ours-Local improves accuracy by 5.66 points (72.88% vs 67.22%), reduces specificity error by 0.44 mm (1.74 vs 2.18), and lowers FID by 9.2 (43.01 vs 52.21). Against the LSTM baseline, the gains are even larger: +27.76 points in accuracy, −1.46 mm in specificity, and −16.13 in FID. The per-frame specificity analysis (Fig. 3) shows the gap widens toward the extreme-expression apex frames, confirming the diffusion model's superior handling of complex, high-intensity deformations that both GAN- and LSTM-based methods struggle to capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the point cloud diffusion formulation of Luo & Hu (CVPR 2021) to fixed-topology meshes by replacing the permutation-invariant MLP denoiser with a Spiral Convolutional Network, thereby preserving mesh connectivity during the denoising process. It departs from the standard DDPM training paradigm (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) in two key ways: training frame-by-frame on deformations from a neutral mesh rather than on full sequences, and introducing a 'consistent noise sampling' strategy for temporal coherence instead of the temporal modules used in video diffusion. Compared to prior 4D expression methods that rely on landmarks (Otberdout et al., 2022) or blendshape parameters, this work operates on the full mesh deformation space, and compared to speech-driven 3D animation methods that have recently adopted diffusion, it targets labeled (guided) expression generation with customizable intensity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: while diffusion models have been widely applied to 3D human motion and speech-driven facial animation, labeled 4D expression generation with extreme expressions remained largely unexplored. The consistent noise sampling strategy is a notably simple alternative to temporal modules for ensuring animation smoothness, and the mesh-space diffusion formulation with GNN denoisers could generalize to other fixed-topology 3D animation tasks. However, the main quantitative evaluation is limited to 12 subjects on CoMA, the textured extension is qualitative-only, and no ablation isolates the contribution of consistent noise sampling versus the mesh-diffusion architecture itself, leaving open questions about scalability and the relative importance of each component.
