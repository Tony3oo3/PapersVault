## Problem

Previous Panoramic Activity Recognition (PAR) methods rely solely on spatial proximity within a single frame to determine social relations, which fails to capture how individuals move together over time to form or dissolve social groups. Additionally, existing hierarchical architectures (individual→social→global) do not account for the mutual influence between global and social group activities, both of which require individual-level context. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing spatio-temporal proximity modeling and a dual-path architecture that lets global and social group recognition reinforce each other.

## Input / Output

The method takes a panoramic video clip of 480×3760 resolution with T=3 frames and per-frame individual bounding boxes as input. A frozen Inception-v3 backbone (pretrained on CAD) extracts frame-wise features, from which RoIAlign crops individual appearance representations that are further reduced via 3D convolution. The network produces four outputs: individual action labels (27 classes), social group activity labels (11 classes), a single global activity label (7 classes), and social group assignments (individual-to-group mapping via K-means clustering on relation-augmented features).

## Method

SPDP-Net operates in two stages. In the proximity-based relation encoding stage, spatio-temporal self-attention (applied sequentially along temporal, height, and width axes) refines individual features using a panoramic positional embedding—derived by extracting individual regions from the sinusoidal positional embedding of the full scene—preserving each person's positional context within the wide panorama. Social relations are then encoded via a matrix combining visual feature similarity (R_s) and a novel Temporal Generalized IoU (TGIoU) that averages per-frame GIoU across the temporal axis, enabling K-means clustering for group detection. In the multi-granular activity recognition stage, the Dual-Path Activity Transformer (DPATr) replaces hierarchical modeling with L layers, each containing an individual-to-global path (individual features plus a learnable global token) and an individual-to-social path (individual features grouped by predicted social membership plus learnable social tokens), so that global and social group contexts mutually reinforce individual representations across layers. Training uses a combined loss with binary cross-entropy for activity tasks and L2 loss for group count estimation, with loss ratios λ_sg:λ_glb:λ_n = 3:2:5.

## Results

On the JRDB-PAR dataset, SPDP-Net achieves a new state-of-the-art overall F1 score of 46.5% for panoramic activity recognition, surpassing the previous best method MUP (41.5%) by 5 percentage points. The per-granularity F1 scores are 51.8% for individual actions, 34.2% for social group activities, and 53.5% for global activities. For social group detection, SPDP-Net achieves 56.4% IoU@0.5, 42.5% IoU@AUC, and 34.3% Mat.IoU, outperforming JRDB-PAR (53.9% IoU@0.5). When evaluated with ground-truth group assignments, SPDP-Net reaches 53.1% overall F1, still exceeding MUP (49.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPDP-Net outperforms all five baselines on the JRDB-PAR dataset across every activity recognition metric. The most direct comparison is with MUP [3], the prior state-of-the-art: SPDP-Net gains +5.0% in overall F1 (46.5 vs 41.5), with the largest relative improvement in social group activity recognition (+9.1% F1, 34.2 vs 25.1) and individual action recognition (+4.1% F1, 51.8 vs 47.7). Against JRDB-PAR [13], the improvement is even more pronounced at +10.9% overall F1 (46.5 vs 35.6), driven primarily by social group activity F1 jumping from 24.8 to 34.2. In social group detection (Table 6), SPDP-Net leads JRDB-PAR by 2.5% in IoU@0.5 (56.4 vs 53.9) and by 4.4% in Mat.IoU (34.3 vs 30.6). When ground-truth group assignments are provided (Table 5), SPDP-Net still beats MUP by 3.9% overall F1 (53.1 vs 49.2), confirming that the gains are not solely attributable to better group detection but also to superior multi-granular activity modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Panoramic Activity Recognition (PAR) task introduced by Han et al. (ECCV 2022) and Cao et al. (ACM MM 2023), which model multi-granular activities hierarchically (individual → social → global). It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) replacing single-frame spatial proximity with spatio-temporal proximity (TGIoU) for social relation encoding, and (2) substituting the sequential hierarchical pipeline with a dual-path transformer (DPATr) that simultaneously processes individual-to-global and individual-to-social paths. The axial attention mechanism is adapted from Axial-DeepLab [41], and the TGIoU metric is a direct temporal extension of GIoU [31].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that spatio-temporal proximity (TGIoU) substantially outperforms spatial-only proximity for social group detection (56.4% vs 48.7% IoU@0.5) challenges the common assumption that single-frame spatial closeness suffices for group membership inference in crowded scenes. The dual-path architecture demonstrates that global and social group activities are not merely hierarchical but mutually reinforcing, a design insight applicable to other multi-granular recognition settings. However, evaluation is limited to a single small dataset (27 videos, T=3 frames), a frozen Inception-v3 backbone, and the task itself remains niche, leaving open whether these findings generalize to longer videos, larger scenes, or other panoramic benchmarks.
