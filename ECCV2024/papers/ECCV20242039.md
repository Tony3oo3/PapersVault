## Problem

Novel view synthesis from unconstrained in-the-wild image collections is hindered by photometric variation and transient occluders. Existing NeRF-based methods rely on a single global appearance embedding shared across the entire scene, which cannot capture per-point local details like highlights and shadows, and they conflate intrinsic material appearance with dynamic environmental effects, making appearance tuning unreliable. Additionally, these volume-rendering approaches incur prohibitive training and inference costs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained RGB image collection (captured at different times with varying camera settings and containing transient objects such as pedestrians and vehicles) along with camera poses estimated via structure-from-motion. Images are downsampled 2× during both training and evaluation. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation that supports real-time novel view rendering at ~200 FPS (with cached appearance features) and enables continuous appearance tuning by scaling the dynamic appearance feature weight, producing renderings that progressively incorporate environmental effects like highlights and illumination changes.

## Method

GS-W represents the scene with 3D Gaussian points and assigns each point a learnable intrinsic appearance feature (sf_i) alongside a dynamic appearance feature (df_i) extracted from a reference image via a Unet-ResNet backbone that produces K+1 feature maps. The key mechanism is adaptive sampling: each Gaussian point owns K learnable 2D sampling coordinates that let it independently select where to sample on the K feature maps, plus a projection-based sample from the projection feature map, enabling per-point focus on local high-frequency details like specular highlights. A two-MLP fusion network combines sf_i, df_i, and 3D position into a holistic appearance feature, which a final MLP decodes with view direction into per-point color. A 2D visibility map (also from a Unet) weights the L1 and SSIM losses to suppress transient-object regions, while a regularization term prevents the map from collapsing to all-invisible. Rendering uses the 3DGS tile-based rasterizer with α-blending, and the total loss combines visibility-weighted L1, SSIM, LPIPS, sampling-coordinate regularization, and visibility-map regularization.

## Results

On the three PhotoTourism scenes, GS-W achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all scenes: 27.96 dB / 0.9319 / 0.0862 on Brandenburg Gate, 23.24 dB / 0.8632 / 0.1300 on Sacre Coeur, and 22.91 dB / 0.8014 / 0.1563 on Trevi Fountain. The strongest baseline, CR-NeRF, trails by 1.43 dB (Brandenburg), 1.17 dB (Sacre), and 1.43 dB (Trevi) in PSNR. In rendering speed, GS-W reaches 38–58 FPS without caching and up to 301 FPS with cached appearance features, roughly 1000× faster than NeRF-based methods (~0.05 FPS) and comparable to vanilla 3DGS (~200–270 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-W outperforms all four baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three PhotoTourism scenes. The closest competitor is CR-NeRF, which uses a cross-ray paradigm; GS-W improves over it by 1.17–1.43 dB in PSNR and by 0.03–0.09 in SSIM, with the largest LPIPS gap on Brandenburg (0.0862 vs. 0.1060). NeRF-W and Ha-NeRF, which rely on a single global appearance embedding, lag further behind by 2.5–4.0 dB in PSNR and also show higher LPIPS, indicating less perceptual fidelity. Vanilla 3DGS, which has no appearance-variation or transient-object handling, performs worst on PSNR and LPIPS (e.g., 19.33 dB on Brandenburg vs. 27.96 for GS-W). In rendering speed, GS-W (55.8–58.3 FPS, or 197–301 FPS with cached features) is approximately 1000× faster than all NeRF-based baselines (~0.05 FPS) and matches or exceeds 3DGS (198–268 FPS) when appearance features are cached.

## Relation to Prior Work

GS-W builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its explicit scene representation and tile-based rasterizer, but departs from 3DGS's static-scene assumption by introducing per-point separated intrinsic and dynamic appearance features. Conceptually, it addresses the same in-the-wild novel view synthesis problem as NeRF-W (global appearance embedding), Ha-NeRF (global feature + anti-transient module), and CR-NeRF (cross-ray 2D feature integration), but replaces their shared/global appearance representations with independent per-point features and an adaptive sampling mechanism over K learned feature maps. The shift from implicit NeRF-based volume rendering to explicit Gaussian splatting is the primary architectural departure, while the visibility map for transient objects and the intrinsic/dynamic separation are methodological innovations layered on top.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~1000× rendering speedup over NeRF-based baselines makes in-the-wild novel view synthesis practically deployable in real-time or near-real-time settings, and the explicit separation of intrinsic and dynamic appearance enables a useful appearance-tuning capability (e.g., adjusting illumination strength) that prior global-embedding methods cannot cleanly support. However, evaluation is limited to three PhotoTourism scenes, the method assumes known camera poses, and it still struggles with complex lighting, specular reflections, and frequently occluded regions. Open questions include whether the per-point adaptive sampling generalizes to larger or more diverse scene collections, and whether the visibility map's trade-off between metric scores and visual artifact suppression can be resolved more elegantly.
