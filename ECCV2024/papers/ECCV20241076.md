## Problem

Transformer-based super-resolution methods rely on window self-attention with fixed small windows (e.g., 8×8), which restricts the receptive field to a single scale and prevents aggregation of multi-scale features such as local textures and repetitive patterns. Expanding windows to capture long-range dependencies is infeasible because W-SA has quadratic computational complexity to window size, and prior work that reduces channels to accommodate larger windows still remains quadratic and introduces a spatial-channel information trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution RGB image at ×2, ×3, or ×4 upscaling factors. It is designed as a general conversion strategy applied to existing transformer-based SR backbones (SwinIR-Light, SwinIR-NG, SRFormer-Light) with minimal architectural changes, using identical hyperparameters (4 transformer blocks, 6 layers each, 60 channels, 6 heads) across all variants. Training uses 64×64 patches with L1 loss, and evaluation is conducted at 720×1280 output resolution on standard SR benchmarks.

## Method

HiT-SR introduces a two-level design: at the block level, fixed small windows are replaced with expanding hierarchical windows (ratios [0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8] on a base 8×8, yielding windows from 4×4 to 64×64) across cascaded transformer layers, enabling gradual receptive-field expansion without costly shifting or masking. At the layer level, window self-attention is replaced by a Spatial-Channel Correlation (SCC) module with linear complexity to window size, comprising: (i) Dual Feature Extraction (DFE) that fuses spatial (convolutional) and channel (linear) features via element-wise multiplication, (ii) Spatial Self-Correlation (S-SC) that uses correlation maps instead of softmax attention and adaptively summarizes large-window values via a spatial linear projection, and (iii) Channel Self-Correlation (C-SC) for transposed channel aggregation. Keys are equated with values (QV formulation) to reduce redundancy, and different head strategies are applied—multi-head for S-SC to exploit channel subspaces, single-head for C-SC to enable full channel interaction. This yields ~7× faster inference with fewer parameters and FLOPs while achieving state-of-the-art PSNR/SSIM across all upscaling factors.

## Results

HiT-SRF achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all five benchmark datasets at all three upscaling factors: 38.26 dB on Set5, 34.01 dB on Set14, 32.37 dB on B100, 33.13 dB on Urban100, and 39.47 dB on Manga109 at ×2. All three HiT-SR variants (HiT-SIR, HiT-SNG, HiT-SRF) outperform their original architectures while using fewer parameters and FLOPs, e.g., HiT-SIR uses 772K params and 209.9G FLOPs versus SwinIR-Light's 910K and 244.4G. Inference speed improves by approximately 7× (e.g., 249 ms vs. 1899 ms for HiT-SIR vs. SwinIR-Light on a 720×1280 output at ×2 on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiT-SRF sets new state-of-the-art PSNR on all five datasets at all three upscaling factors, surpassing the previous best SRFormer-Light by 0.03 dB on Set5, 0.07 dB on Set14, 0.01 dB on B100, 0.22 dB on Urban100, and 0.19 dB on Manga109 at ×2. Each HiT-SR variant consistently outperforms its original architecture: HiT-SIR gains +0.26 dB on Urban100 and +0.26 dB on Manga109 over SwinIR-Light, while HiT-SNG gains +0.23 dB on Urban100 over SwinIR-NG, all with 15–22% fewer parameters and 14–22% fewer FLOPs. Against CNN-based baselines (EDSR-B, RFDN-L, FMEN, etc.), all HiT-SR methods lead by 0.1–0.3 dB on Urban100 and 0.4–0.9 dB on Manga109 at ×2, confirming the advantage of long-range dependency modeling. The most striking efficiency gain is the ~7× inference speedup (e.g., 249 ms vs. 1899 ms for HiT-SIR vs. SwinIR-Light), attributed to replacing shifted window self-attention with the linear-complexity spatial-channel correlation. HiT-SIR slightly trails Omni-SR on Set14 (33.91 vs. 33.98) but leads on Urban100 (33.02 vs. 33.05) and Manga109 (39.38 vs. 39.28), while HiT-SRF dominates across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a general conversion strategy for existing transformer-based SR architectures, specifically SwinIR, SwinIR-NG, and SRFormer, rather than a from-scratch design. It departs from the standard window self-attention (W-SA) paradigm by replacing it with a spatial-channel correlation (SCC) method that achieves linear (rather than quadratic) complexity to window size, and by replacing fixed small windows with expanding hierarchical windows. This contrasts with prior attempts at multi-scale aggregation (e.g., ELAN's GMSA, SRFormer's PSA) that either retained quadratic complexity or sacrificed channel information through splitting/compression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~7× inference speedup combined with consistent PSNR gains across all upscaling factors makes this practically relevant for real-time SR deployment, and the linear-complexity SCC could be adopted as a drop-in replacement in other transformer restoration pipelines. The ablation showing that dropping softmax (using raw correlation matrices) actually improves performance over standard attention is a useful finding for the broader transformer community. Open questions include whether the hierarchical window strategy generalizes to other restoration tasks (denoising, inpainting) and whether the correlation-based formulation scales to even larger windows or higher-resolution inputs beyond the tested 720×1280 setting.
