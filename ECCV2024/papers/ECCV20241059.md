## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models lack fine-grained, continuous control over individual visual attributes: modifying text prompts alters overall image structure, post-hoc editing methods like PromptToPrompt require separate inference passes per concept and risk attribute entanglement, and image-based customization techniques are impractical for iterative editing. No existing approach provides lightweight, plug-and-play adaptors that enable precise, composable, and disentangled control over specific concepts in a single inference pass.

## Input / Output

For training, the method accepts either a small set of text prompts (a target concept, a positive attribute, a negative attribute, and a set of protected concepts) or as few as 3–6 paired before/after images defining a visual concept. At inference, the user provides a standard text prompt plus a continuous alpha scaling parameter per slider, and the system produces a 1024×1024 (SDXL) or 512×512 (SD v1.4) image with the target attribute modulated while preserving structure. The trained output is a lightweight LoRA adaptor attached to all UNet layers except cross-attention, which can be stacked with up to 50+ other sliders without quality degradation.

## Method

Concept Sliders fine-tune LoRA adaptors on a frozen diffusion model using a guided score objective derived from Tweedie's formula: the denoising prediction is shifted by η(ε(X, c_+, t) − ε(X, c_−, t)), pulling the target concept's distribution toward the positive attribute and away from the negative one. A disentanglement extension sums this shift over a set of protected concepts P, producing a parameter direction invariant to those attributes and reducing interference (e.g., age edits no longer alter race or gender). The low-rank constraint is essential: it identifies the minimal concept subspace, yielding finer edits (lower LPIPS) and better generalization than full fine-tuning. For visual concepts, the LoRA is trained on paired images (x^A, x^B) with a symmetric loss that aligns the adaptor direction to transform A→B, enabling transfer of StyleGAN style-space latents into the diffusion model. At inference, an SDEdit-style schedule keeps the LoRA multiplier at zero for early denoising steps to preserve structure, then activates it for remaining steps, while the alpha scaling factor provides continuous, retraining-free control over edit strength.

## Results

On the primary text-based slider evaluation (Table 1), the proposed method achieves substantially higher ΔCLIP scores than Prompt-to-Prompt across all four tested concepts (e.g., 3.93 vs. 1.10 for Age, 5.59 vs. 3.45 for Hair, 1.56 vs. 0.43 for Sky) while simultaneously producing lower LPIPS distances to the original image (e.g., 0.06 vs. 0.15 for Age, 0.09 vs. 0.25 for Rusty), indicating stronger targeted edits with less structural disruption. For visual concept sliders (Table 2), the method achieves a Δ_eye of 1.75 with LPIPS of 0.06, outperforming both Custom Diffusion (Δ_eye 0.97, LPIPS 0.23) and Textual Inversion (Δ_eye 0.81, LPIPS 0.21). In a user study on hand distortion, the 'fix hands' slider reduces the percentage of images rated as having distorted hands from 62% (SDXL baseline) to 22%, and the 'repair' slider is rated as more realistic in 80.39% of paired comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Prompt-to-Prompt [13], the proposed method wins decisively on both metrics: it achieves 2–4× higher ΔCLIP (e.g., 3.93 vs. 1.10 for Age, 1.56 vs. 0.43 for Sky) while reducing LPIPS by 40–64% (e.g., 0.06 vs. 0.15 for Age, 0.09 vs. 0.25 for Rusty), meaning edits are both stronger and more structurally faithful. For visual concept editing (eye size), the method outperforms Custom Diffusion [25] by nearly 2× in Δ_eye (1.75 vs. 0.97) with a 4× lower LPIPS (0.06 vs. 0.23), and outperforms Textual Inversion [6] by 2.2× in Δ_eye (1.75 vs. 0.81) with a 3.5× lower LPIPS (0.06 vs. 0.21), demonstrating that slider-based editing is far more targeted than token-learning or cross-attention fine-tuning approaches. In human preference studies (Table 3), sliders are preferred over text composition and DiffEdit at 94–100% of the time when composing 2+ concepts, and over prompt engineering at 65–84% across all composition levels. The one area where the method is competitive but not dominant is the 'Rusty' concept, where ΔCLIP is nearly tied with Prompt-to-Prompt (7.60 vs. 7.67), though LPIPS is still substantially lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the LoRA low-rank adaptation framework (originally for language models) into a mechanism for learning interpretable, continuous concept directions in diffusion model parameters, building on the DDPM reparametrization trick to derive a guided score training objective. It departs from per-image editing methods like Prompt-to-Prompt and from token-adding customization methods (Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion) by operating at the model-parameter level, enabling single-pass, composable control. The disentanglement formulation (Eq. 8), which explicitly protects a set of concepts during slider training, is a novel extension of the classifier-free-guidance-style score manipulation, and the StyleGAN latent transfer bridges GAN interpretability work with diffusion model control in a way prior methods did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a lightweight, plug-and-play slider that composes with 50+ other sliders without quality degradation gives artists a new control paradigm beyond prompt engineering, and the 'fix hands' slider (reducing distorted hands from 62% to 22%) addresses a persistent, user-visible failure mode in SDXL. Open questions include automated selection of protected concepts to further reduce residual interference, the tradeoff between edit strength and structural coherence under SDEdit inference, and whether the FID–human-perception gap (where distorting images along the opposite slider direction improves FID yet users prefer the realism-enhancing direction) reflects a fundamental limitation of FID as a quality metric. The StyleGAN-to-diffusion transfer also raises the broader question of how much semantic structure in GAN latent spaces is transferable across generative model families.
