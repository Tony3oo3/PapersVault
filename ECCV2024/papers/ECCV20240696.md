## Problem

Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) optimizes each rendered view of a 3D representation independently against a view-agnostic 2D diffusion model, inheriting its lack of multi-view coherence and producing geometrically inconsistent 3D assets with repeated content (Janus artifacts). Existing remedies—prompt engineering and fine-tuning view-aware diffusion models on limited 3D datasets—either yield marginal improvements or overfit, degrading text congruence on complex prompts. The paper asks whether SDS can be fundamentally restructured to enforce inter-view consistency while preserving the generalizability of the underlying 2D diffusion prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240696/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 3D NeRF representation (parameterized via Instant-NGP multiresolution hash encoding with a volume renderer) that can be further converted to a DMTet mesh. During optimization, multiple rendered views at 64×64 resolution are sampled per iteration across V camera poses, and the final asset is evaluated by rendering 20 novel views per prompt. The pipeline runs for 5K iterations with time-annealing and resolution scaling-up, completing in roughly 50–120 minutes depending on the energy function used.

## Method

Joint Score Distillation (JSD) extends the single-view KL-divergence of SDS into a multi-view objective by modeling a joint image distribution p(x̃|c̃,y) ∝ exp(C(x̃,c̃)) ∏ p(xⁱ|cⁱ,y), where the energy function C measures inter-view coherence. The resulting gradient subtracts ∂C/∂xₜⁱ from the standard SDS noise residual for each view, so that views are optimized jointly rather than independently; SDS is recovered as the special case C≡0. Three interchangeable view-aware models instantiate C: a lightweight binary classifier (DINO-ViT backbone + relative-pose MLP) trained on Objaverse, an image-to-image translation model (Wonder3D), and a multi-view synthesis model (MVDream), each providing a different coherence signal. On top of JSD, JointDreamer adds a Geometry Fading schedule (reducing the NeRF density-network learning rate from 1e-2 to 1e-6 after 5K iterations) and a CFG Switching strategy (raising the classifier-free guidance scale from 30 to 50 after 5K iterations) to decouple early geometric convergence from late texture refinement.

## Results

On the object-centric MS-COCO subset (153 prompts), JointDreamer achieves 27.7% CLIP Score and 88.5% CLIP R-Precision, substantially outperforming all baselines. In user preference studies, JointDreamer scores 42.1%, more than doubling the next-best method (MVDream at 23.5%). The R-Precision improvement is particularly striking: 60.8% over DreamFusion and 54.9% over MVDream, demonstrating superior text-to-3D correspondence. Ablations confirm that JSD alone lifts CLIP Score from 20.0 (SDS) to 27.6, with Geometry Fading and CFG Switching further pushing it to 28.8 and reducing FID from 429.2 to 353.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JointDreamer outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against DreamFusion (the original SDS pipeline), JointDreamer improves CLIP Score by 7.6 points (27.7 vs. 20.1) and R-Precision by 60.8 points (88.5 vs. 27.7), with the gap attributed to JSD's inter-view coherence constraint eliminating Janus artifacts. Against MVDream, which fine-tunes a multi-view diffusion model, JointDreamer wins on CLIP Score (27.7 vs. 20.8) and R-Precision (88.5 vs. 33.6), showing that using view-aware models as energy terms in JSD preserves the generalization of the original 2D diffusion model while avoiding overfitting on limited 3D training data. Against ProlificDreamer, which uses variational score distillation with a LoRA model, JointDreamer achieves higher CLIP Score (27.7 vs. 25.0) and dramatically higher R-Precision (88.5 vs. 18.7), as ProlificDreamer's LoRA training introduces ill-posed pose-image associations that worsen multi-view artifacts. Magic3D is compared only qualitatively (via the threestudio framework), where JointDreamer-DMTet shows consistent superiority in geometry and texture over Magic3D's two-stage NeRF-to-DMTet pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion by reformulating the single-view KL-divergence into a multi-view joint distribution, introducing an energy function that enforces inter-view coherence. It departs from prior attempts to fix the Janus problem—prompt engineering (limited effect) and fine-tuning view-aware diffusion models on 3D data (overfitting on scarce text-3D pairs)—by instead repurposing existing view-aware models (binary classifiers, image-to-image translators like Wonder3D, multi-view generators like MVDream) as energy terms within a unified JSD framework. The key conceptual move is showing that SDS is a strict special case of JSD (energy term ≡ 0), providing a clean theoretical unification rather than an ad-hoc patch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

JSD offers a principled and general recipe for injecting multi-view coherence into any SDS-based pipeline without sacrificing the generalizability of the underlying 2D diffusion model, which is a core tension in the field. The practical impact is significant: the 88.5% CLIP R-Precision (vs. 33.6% for MVDream) and 42.1% user preference indicate that the Janus problem, long considered an inherent limitation of SDS, can be largely resolved. Open questions remain: the view-aware energy models still require 3D training data, training time is comparable to SDS rather than faster, and the approach is currently tied to NeRF/Instant-NGP representations—extending to 3D Gaussian Splatting or other representations is left as future work.
