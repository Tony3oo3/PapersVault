## Problem

Current end-to-end scene graph generation methods detect entities and relations separately using only visual representations, requiring post-processing to match subjects and objects to predicted relations and preventing entity semantics and positions from informing relation prediction. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a sparse relationship matrix that explicitly bridges entity detection and relation detection within a single end-to-end pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242187/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a scene graph consisting of N_e=100 entity proposals (bounding boxes and class labels from a deformable DETR) and N_r=100 relation triplets (subject, predicate, object). The key intermediate output is an N_e × N_e sparse relationship matrix whose top-scoring entries determine which subject-object pairs are passed to the relation decoder, eliminating the need for Hungarian matching between independently predicted relations and entities.

## Method

Mg-RMPN predicts the sparse relationship matrix through three Gated Pooling Modules (GPMs) with identical architecture but independent parameters, each trained to filter negative samples at a different granularity: GPM-1 removes background-background pairs, GPM-2 removes entity-background pairs, and GPM-3 removes entity-entity pairs without relations. Each GPM fuses visual similarity (projected subject/object embeddings), a linguistic prior function, and 8-dimensional relative position features plus a 9-dimensional union bounding box encoding, combined via a gated pooling layer. The three matrices are learned hierarchically (M_r2 = M_r1 + GPM-2, M_r3 = M_r2 + GPM-3) with focal-loss-based binary cross-entropy and L1/L2 regularization, using progressively stricter ground-truth supervision at each level. The final sparse matrix M_r3 selects the top-N_r most probable subject-object pairs, whose visual, semantic (GloVe), and positional encodings are fed into a DETR-style transformer relation decoder to predict predicate classes.

## Results

On VG150, Mg-RMPN achieves F@50/100 of 19.3/22.8, surpassing the best two-stage method PE-Net (17.7/20.5) and the best prior end-to-end method Pair-Net (16.6/20.2). On Open Images V6, it attains the best F@50 of 57.4 and score_wtd of 43.6 among all compared methods. On GQA200, Mg-RMPN reaches F@50/100 of 16.5/18.5, outperforming the closest end-to-end baseline Pair-Net (13.9/16.4) and matching the two-stage SHA+GCL on F@50 while trailing it slightly on F@100 (18.5 vs. 18.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mg-RMPN outperforms all 16 baselines on the overall F@K metric across all three datasets. On VG150, it surpasses the strongest two-stage method PE-Net by 1.6/2.3 points on F@50/100 (19.3/22.8 vs. 17.7/20.5) and the closest end-to-end competitor Pair-Net by 2.7/2.6 points (19.3/22.8 vs. 16.6/20.2). Among two-stage baselines, rebalancing methods like SHA+GCL achieve high mR@K (20.9) but sacrifice R@50 severely (14.9), whereas Mg-RMPN maintains competitive R@50 (29.1) while also leading in mR@50 (14.4). On OIv6, Mg-RMPN achieves the best F@50 (57.4) and score_wtd (43.6), beating Pair-Net (56.5/40.3) and BGNN (52.6/42.1). On GQA200, Mg-RMPN leads all end-to-end methods (F@50: 16.5 vs. Pair-Net's 13.9) and slightly trails only SHA+GCL on F@100 (18.5 vs. 18.9), while winning on F@50 (16.5 vs. 16.2). The ablation against Pair-Net specifically confirms that the multi-granularity GPM design and the incorporation of semantic and positional information are the key sources of improvement over the prior pair-then-relation framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'pair then relation' framework introduced by Pair-Net [30], which predetermines subject-object pairs to avoid post-hoc relation matching, and adopts its DETR-style relation decoder. The key departure is the introduction of a multi-granularity sparse relationship matrix predicted by three Gated Pooling Modules that incorporate semantic (GloVe) and positional information alongside visual features, whereas prior end-to-end methods (RelTR, SGTR, Pair-Net) rely solely on visual representations. The prior linguistic bias function from VCTree+TDE [26] is repurposed within the GPM to capture entity-pair correlation. The extreme sparsity of valid relations (over 99.9% negative pairs) motivates the hierarchical negative-sample filtering strategy, which has no direct analogue in prior SGG work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bridging entity detection and relation prediction through a learned sparse relationship matrix, this work eliminates the post-processing matching step that constrains end-to-end SGG and allows entity semantics and positions to inform relation prediction — a capability absent in prior end-to-end methods. The multi-granularity decomposition of negative samples is a practical and interpretable solution to the extreme class imbalance inherent in relationship matrices. Open questions include whether the three-granularity decomposition is optimal (could more or fewer levels help), how the approach scales to larger entity counts beyond 100, and whether the sparse matrix formulation generalizes to other structured prediction tasks beyond SGG. The method's dependence on a fixed number of entity queries (100) and Hungarian matching for supervision also limits flexibility in variable-density scenes.
