## Problem

Semi-supervised action recognition has predominantly targeted coarse-grained actions where scene bias provides strong classification cues, leaving fine-grained action recognition (FGAR) largely unexplored in the limited-label setting. Fine-grained actions (e.g., diving patterns, gymnastics) occur in uniform scenes and require understanding of temporal action-phases, making standard augmentation-based semi-supervised methods ineffective. This paper is the first to systematically study semi-supervised FGAR and identify why existing approaches fail.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a small labeled video set D_l and a larger unlabeled video set D_u, processing each video through two encoders: a frame-wise VTN (producing T×F temporal embeddings) and a video-level 3D-ResNet or ViT backbone (producing a single video-level embedding). The primary output is a fine-grained action class label per video, but the method also produces a learnable alignability score in [0,1] for any video pair, which drives the pseudo-label refinement. Pseudo-labels are generated by combining the action encoder's softmax confidence with a non-parametric softmax over class-wise mean alignability scores computed against a gallery of labeled embeddings.

## Method

FinePseudo is a co-training self-training framework with two branches: an action encoder f_E (trained with cross-entropy) and an alignability encoder f_A (a frame-wise VTN trained with alignability-verification-based metric learning). The key mechanism is computing pairwise alignment cost via softDTW over frame-wise embeddings, then mapping this distance through a learnable non-linear scaling function and sigmoid to produce a differentiable alignability score. Training f_A uses a triplet loss with hard-negative mining from the mini-batch plus a binary cross-entropy score loss, jointly optimizing the alignability verification objective. During pseudo-labeling, the alignability encoder computes per-class mean alignability scores against sampled labeled gallery embeddings, passes them through a temperature-scaled softmax (non-parametric classifier), and adds the resulting prediction to the action encoder's output; only samples exceeding a confidence threshold are retained as refined pseudo-labels for the next self-training iteration.

## Results

On four fine-grained action recognition datasets (Diving48, FineGym99, FineGym288, FineDiving), FinePseudo achieves top-1 accuracy of 37.6% (Diving48), 69.9% (FineGym99), 62.5% (FineGym288), and 46.8% (FineDiving) at the 10% labeled setting, consistently outperforming the best prior method TimeBal by 3.9–4.8 percentage points. At 5% labeled data, the margin widens to 4.1–5.1 points (e.g., 20.9% vs. 15.8% on Diving48). On coarse-grained datasets (Kinetics400, Something-SomethingV2), FinePseudo also achieves the best results, e.g., 56.1% on Kinetics400 at 10% labels with 3D-ResNet50, surpassing TimeBal (61.2% at 10% but with different backbone settings) and SVFormer (58.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FinePseudo outperforms all 18 compared baselines across all four fine-grained datasets and all label ratios (5%, 10%, 20%). The closest competitor is TimeBal [18] (CVPR 2023), a videoSSL-based semi-supervised method; FinePseudo exceeds it by 3.9–5.1 points (e.g., 37.6% vs. 33.7% on Diving48 at 10%, 80.0% vs. 76.1% on FineGym99 at 20%). Video self-supervised baselines (TCLR, VidMoCo, GDT, AVID, RSPNet) and classical pseudo-labeling methods (PL, UPS) trail FinePseudo by 8–12 points on Diving48 at 10% labels, confirming that standard augmentation-based or confidence-threshold pseudo-labeling is insufficient for fine-grained actions. On coarse-grained datasets (Table 3), FinePseudo with 3D-ResNet50 achieves 62.6% on Kinetics400 at 10% labels, surpassing TimeBal (61.2%) and SVFormer (58.1%), and 46.1% on Something-SomethingV2 at 10%, beating SVFormer (42.9%) and TimeBal (not reported at this setting). In the open-world setting (Table 4), where 8 novel classes are present in unlabeled data, FinePseudo achieves 42.21% at 10% labels versus 39.90% for TimeBal and 39.60% for the supervised baseline, demonstrating that the non-parametric alignability-based pseudo-labeling effectively rejects unknown classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling self-training paradigm (Lee, 2013) to the fine-grained action recognition domain, where prior semi-supervised methods relying on scene-bias-exploiting augmentations (token-mix, CutMix, actor-cutmix) are shown to be ineffective. It repurposes softDTW (Cuturi & Blondel, 2017) not as a forced alignment objective (as in prior intra-video alignment work like D3TW or Hadji et al.) but as a discriminative 'alignability' signal for cross-class verification. The collaborative co-training design—pairing a standard video encoder with a frame-wise VTN alignability encoder—departs from prior single-encoder semi-supervised video methods like TimeBal and SVFormer, which the paper shows underperform on fine-grained datasets due to their reliance on temporally-invariant teachers or scene-bias augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap: fine-grained action recognition (surgery, sports, AR/VR) is critical in practice but has been largely ignored by the semi-supervised action recognition community, which has focused on coarse-grained, scene-biased datasets. The alignability-verification framing offers a reusable principle—using whether two temporal sequences *can* be aligned as a class-discriminative signal—that could extend beyond action recognition to other fine-grained temporal tasks. Open questions include whether the softDTW-based alignment cost scales efficiently to very long videos or high frame counts, whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal (audio-visual) fine-grained settings, and whether the 4–5% gains persist under even more extreme label scarcity (e.g., 1% labeled data).
