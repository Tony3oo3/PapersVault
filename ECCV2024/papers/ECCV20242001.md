## Problem

Video streaming contributes over 1.3% of global GHG emissions, with energy consumed across encoding, transmission, decoding, and display stages. Prior work addressed individual tasks—downscaling, film grain removal/synthesis, and energy-aware display—separately, but no unified end-to-end solution existed that could simultaneously reduce energy at every stage of the video chain while preserving quality of experience and enabling lossless recovery of the original content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a high-resolution (H×W×3) grainy RGB image, 3R-INN produces a grain-free, energy-aware low-resolution image at H/2×W/2×3 with a configurable energy reduction rate R∈[0,1]. The lost information from downscaling and grain removal is captured in a latent variable z following a standard Gaussian distribution, which is internally disentangled into z_D (downscaling details) and z_G (film grain). Running the network in reverse with z and the LR image reconstructs either the original HR grainy image (using full z) or a clean HR image (setting z_G=0), requiring no auxiliary metadata transmission.

## Method

3R-INN is a single invertible neural network composed of three block types: a Haar wavelet transform that splits the input into low-frequency and high-frequency sub-bands, eight GLOW-style invertible coupling blocks that jointly process these sub-bands, and a conditional latent encoding block that normalizes the internal latent representation into a standard Gaussian conditioned on the LR output for image-adaptive reconstruction. The network is trained in two stages: first on rescaling and grain removal/synthesis with L2 forward fidelity, Gaussian regularization, and L1 inverse fidelity losses for both grainy and clean reconstructions; then fine-tuned with an additional RGBW OLED power loss and SSIM loss for the energy-aware task. The key design insight is that one lightweight network (1.7M parameters) replaces three separate architectures (23.3M+ parameters), achieving a positive energy balance as soon as content is displayed ~110 times, while the disentanglement of z_D and z_G enables selective reconstruction of grainy or clean HR versions without transmitting any side information.

## Results

3R-INN achieves 78% encoding time savings, 77% decoding time savings, and 5–20% display power reduction (depending on reduction rate R) along the full video chain, with 74–78% total end-to-end energy savings on the RaceHorses sequence. For LR image quality (R=0), 3R-INN outperforms IRN on all three test sets (e.g., PSNR 39.63/SSIM 0.951 vs. 39.06/0.942 on DIV2K). For energy-aware images at R=20% on DIV2K using the P_RGBW model, 3R-INN achieves PSNR-Y 38.02/SSIM 0.993, substantially outperforming LS (27.01/0.991) and R-ACE (26.59/0.967). For HR grainy reconstruction, 3R-INN achieves LPIPS 0.0445 and KLD 0.0177, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For LR image quality (Table 3), 3R-INN outperforms IRN [42] on all three test sets, e.g., PSNR 39.63 vs. 39.06 and SSIM 0.951 vs. 0.942 on DIV2K, with even larger gaps on BSDS300 (39.79 vs. 38.95 PSNR). For energy-aware images (Table 4), 3R-INN clearly beats the simple luminance scaling (LS) and R-ACE [33] baselines at all reduction rates; it is comparable to DeepPVR [26] under the P_Y model but significantly outperforms it under the P_RGBW model (e.g., 38.02 vs. 27.12 PSNR-Y at R=20% on DIV2K). Against InvEAN [25], 3R-INN is slightly below on the P_Y metric (27.32 vs. 27.75 PSNR-Y at R=20%) but substantially ahead on P_RGBW (38.02 vs. 27.75), reflecting that InvEAN was not trained for the RGBW power model. For HR grainy reconstruction (Table 5), 3R-INN achieves the best LPIPS (0.0445) and KLD (0.0177), outperforming Style-FG [5] (0.1592/0.0232), VVC [34] (0.2981/0.0327), and Deep-FG [6] (0.3722/0.0260); it trails Style-FG only on JSD-NSS (0.0088 vs. 0.0024), which the authors attribute to Style-FG's GAN-based distribution modeling. In terms of complexity (Table 2), 3R-INN uses only 1.7M parameters and 230 GMACs versus 23.3M/334 GMACs for the three-network combination, requiring 110 equivalent displays to offset its energy cost versus 161 for the combination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate invertible-network tasks—image rescaling (IRN, [42]), film grain removal/synthesis (Style-FG/Deep-FG, [5,6]), and energy-aware image generation (InvEAN, [25])—into a single multi-task invertible network. The invertible block architecture is directly inherited from GLOW's coupling layers [24], and the Haar wavelet decomposition follows IRN's paradigm, but the key departure is a conditioned latent encoding block that makes the inverse-pass reconstruction image-adaptive rather than case-agnostic as in IRN. The paper also introduces a disentanglement of grain and high-frequency detail within the latent space, enabling separate recovery of grainy and clean HR versions without transmitting auxiliary metadata.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With streaming accounting for over 1.3% of global GHG emissions, an end-to-end energy reduction strategy that simultaneously addresses encoding, transmission, decoding, and display is practically important for the video industry's decarbonization goals. The demonstrated 74–78% end-to-end energy savings and the ability to reconstruct the original content without side-information make this a compelling systems-level contribution. However, limitations remain: the method operates frame-by-frame without temporal modeling, requires separate fine-tuning for each energy reduction rate R, and lacks subjective user-acceptability evaluation. Future work on conditioning the network on R and extending to temporal video processing would be needed for real-world deployment.
