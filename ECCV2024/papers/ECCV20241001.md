## Problem

Adding a new conditioning modality to a diffusion-based text-to-image model typically requires retraining with paired multi-modal data across all tasks, causing training complexity to grow with each added task (the 'scaling issue'). Existing plug-and-play approaches like ControlNet and T2I-Adapter handle a single spatial condition well, but combining multiple conditions demands either retraining from scratch or manual parameter tuning, and naive feature averaging fails when conditions are spatially contradictory. The paper asks whether a single diffusion model can scale across multiple diverse tasks without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241001/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference time, the method takes a text prompt and multiple spatial condition maps (e.g., depth, segmentation, HED edges, pose, sketch, or style), each processed by its own independently trained single-task model (ControlNet or T2I-Adapter) on top of Stable Diffusion v1.5. The output is a single generated image that simultaneously respects all provided conditions, produced over 50 diffusion steps with the UniPC scheduler. The key constraint is that each condition map must have a corresponding pre-trained single-task model; no new paired data or fine-tuning is required at fusion time.

## Method

MaxFusion is a training-free, variance-based feature merging strategy applied at each spatial location across the intermediate layers of multiple single-task control models. The core insight is that the channel-wise variance of intermediate features quantifies the local conditioning intensity: high variance indicates the condition is strongly active at that location. At each spatial point, if the correlation between two models' features exceeds a threshold (δ=0.7), they are averaged to combine complementary information; otherwise, the feature with the highest normalized channel standard deviation is selected to preserve the dominant condition. An unmerging step then rescales non-dominant features back to their original variance to prevent signal loss in deeper layers. The scheme is applied incrementally, allowing extension to N modalities by sequentially fusing one task at a time, and can be plugged into any off-the-shelf ControlNet or T2I-Adapter without modification.

## Results

On the {HED, Segmentation → Image} task (Table 3), MaxFusion achieves FID 37.21, NIQE 2.84, and CLIP score 0.2941, outperforming the multimodal T2I-Adapter baseline (FID 40.14, NIQE 3.94, CLIP 0.2922) and all unimodal baselines (SPADE, PITI-Mask, PITI-Sketch) on perceptual quality and text alignment. For contradictory conditions (Pose + Segmentation), MaxFusion reduces FID from 55.61 (T2I-Adapter) and 63.46 (ControlNet) to 46.72. Scaling to three modalities ({Seg, Depth, HED}), MaxFusion achieves FID 47.48 versus 51.87 (T2I-Adapter) and 50.47 (ControlNet), with the lowest MSE on depth (0.0204) and HED (0.0692) conditioning consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multimodal T2I-Adapter baseline, MaxFusion wins on all five metrics in Table 3: FID (37.21 vs 40.14), NIQE (2.84 vs 3.94), CLIP (0.2941 vs 0.2922), Segmentation MSE (0.0985 vs 0.1506), and HED MSE (0.0774 vs 0.1201), with the largest gains in perceptual quality (NIQE) and conditioning consistency. Against the unimodal baselines, MaxFusion achieves the best NIQE (2.84) and CLIP (0.2941) scores, but PITI-Mask retains a lower FID (35.52 vs 37.21) and PITI-Sketch a lower HED MSE (0.0417 vs 0.0774), reflecting that single-modality models excel at their own conditioning but lack text alignment and cross-modal detail. For contradictory conditions (Table 1), MaxFusion substantially outperforms both T2I-Adapter and multi-ControlNet on FID (46.72 vs 55.61 and 63.46 for Pose+Seg; 47.37 vs 51.47 and 48.18 for Pose+Depth). In the three-modality scalability test (Table 4), MaxFusion again leads on FID (47.48 vs 51.87, 50.47) and achieves the best depth MSE (0.0204 vs 0.0448, 0.0362), confirming that the variance-based fusion generalizes beyond two conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ControlNet and T2I-Adapter, which attach task-specific encoders to a frozen diffusion UNet for single-modality spatial conditioning, and on Unicontrol, which unifies multiple conditions but requires retraining with paired data across all modalities. It departs from the model-merging literature (Task Arithmetic, Fisher merging, GitRebasin) by operating on intermediate feature activations rather than weights, motivated by the observation that weight-level merging fails when input modalities differ. The key conceptual contribution is a training-free, inference-time feature fusion criterion based on per-location variance maps, positioning MaxFusion as a plug-and-play layer atop any existing single-condition adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of MaxFusion addresses a practical bottleneck: adding a new conditioning modality to a diffusion pipeline currently demands paired multi-modal data and full retraining, risking catastrophic forgetting. The variance-based fusion criterion is simple and composable, making it easy to adopt alongside existing ControlNet or T2I-Adapter checkpoints. However, the evaluation is limited to a 2,000-image COCO subset with at most three modalities, and the paper acknowledges a performance trade-off as the number of conditions grows, leaving open whether the incremental pairwise fusion strategy scales gracefully to many simultaneous conditions or to video and 3D generation settings.
