## Problem

Image-guided 3DGS editing relies on 2D diffusion models to edit multi-view rendered images independently, producing severe multi-view inconsistencies (e.g., the same object edited to different styles from different angles). Because 3DGS is an explicit representation with a densification/pruning mechanism, it is especially vulnerable to such inconsistencies, leading to mode collapse characterized by source-target ambiguity and flickering artifacts. The root cause is the lack of 3D awareness in 2D editing models, which process each view in isolation without cross-view information exchange.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3DGS model (a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussians with position, covariance, color, and opacity) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired edit. The 3DGS is rendered to multi-view 2D images, which are edited via a diffusion model (InfEdit) using an annealed timestep schedule from T to 1 with N sampled timesteps. The output is a fine-tuned 3DGS model optimized with a combined MAE and LPIPS loss against the view-consistent edited guidance images. Each editing task requires 1–3 iterations with 400 optimization steps per iteration, taking 10–20 minutes on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

## Method

VcEDIT introduces two consistency modules that exploit 3DGS's explicit representation and fast rendering to enforce multi-view coherence within the 2D editing pipeline. The Cross-attention Consistency Module (CCM) inverse-renders 2D cross-attention maps from all views onto the source Gaussians to produce an averaged 3D attention map, then re-renders it to 2D, replacing the original per-view attention maps so the U-Net attends uniformly to the same 3D region across all views. The Editing Consistency Module (ECM) fine-tunes a cloned 3DGS on the edited images and re-renders it to obtain physically coherent guidance, then applies a local blend with the source latents weighted by the consistent attention maps to preserve unedited details. Both modules operate at every diffusion timestep, and the entire pipeline is wrapped in an iterative pattern (edit → update 3DGS → repeat) where each iteration's output 3DGS becomes the next iteration's source, progressively refining consistency and editing fidelity.

## Results

VcEDIT achieves 63.93% preference rate in a user study (25 participants, 11 samples) and 0.2108 CLIP text-to-image directional similarity, outperforming GSEditor (34.49%, 0.1917) and DDS (1.57%, 0.1470). The user study gap over GSEditor is approximately 29 percentage points, while the CLIP similarity improvement is about 0.019 over GSEditor and 0.064 over DDS. Qualitatively, VcEDIT produces clean, detailed edits without the mode collapse, noisy coloration, or flickering artifacts observed in both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VcEDIT is compared against two baselines: DDS (Delta Denoising Score), which uses an implicit score function loss from a diffusion model as guidance, and GSEditor, which employs image-guided editing with iterative training-set updates. VcEDIT decisively outperforms both on every metric. Against DDS, VcEDIT achieves a 62.36-percentage-point margin in the user study (63.93% vs. 1.57%) and a 0.064 gain in CLIP similarity (0.2108 vs. 0.1470); DDS results are described as exhibiting 'severe noisy coloration and shape distortion,' indicating near-complete editing failure. Against GSEditor, VcEDIT leads by 29.44 percentage points in the user study and 0.019 in CLIP similarity; GSEditor produces better prompt alignment than DDS but still suffers from 'noisy coloration and blurred edges' due to residual multi-view inconsistency. The paper attributes VcEDIT's advantage to its two consistency modules (CCM and ECM) that directly address the multi-view inconsistency problem that both baselines overlook.

## Relation to Prior Work

VcEDIT extends the image-guided 3DGS editing pipeline established by GaussianEditor and Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, which render multi-view images, edit them with a 2D diffusion model, and fine-tune the 3DGS on the edited outputs. The key departure is addressing the multi-view inconsistency that these prior methods overlook: VcEDIT introduces two consistency modules (CCM and ECM) that exploit 3DGS's explicit representation to enforce cross-view coherence during the 2D editing process itself, rather than treating each view independently. The editing backbone follows InfEdit's inversion-free diffusion pipeline, and the iterative refinement pattern is inherited from Instruct-NeRF2NeRF, but the consistency enforcement via inverse-rendering of cross-attention maps and intermediate 3DGS calibration is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-view inconsistency causing mode collapse is a fundamental bottleneck in image-guided 3D editing, and VcEDIT's approach of leveraging 3DGS's explicit geometry to regularize 2D editing is a practical and generalizable strategy that could be adopted by other 3D editing pipelines. However, the method remains dependent on the quality of the underlying 2D editing model and struggles with non-rigid shape changes, limiting its applicability to more dramatic edits. Open questions include whether the consistency modules scale to larger scene edits, whether they can be made more efficient for real-time use, and whether the approach transfers to other explicit 3D representations beyond 3DGS.
