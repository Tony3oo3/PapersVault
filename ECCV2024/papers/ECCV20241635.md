## Problem

Existing portrait relighting methods either replace the entire lighting environment (losing the original ambient illumination) or offer only limited shadow control such as softening or complete removal. There is no method that enables precise, disentangled editing of individual shadow attributes—shape, intensity, and position—while preserving the rest of the scene's lighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB portrait image plus four scalar editing parameters (light position x,y, Gaussian spread σ, and intensity γ, all normalized to [0,1]) and produces an edited portrait with modified shadows while retaining the original ambient lighting. Internally, the pipeline operates at 256×256 for lighting estimation and shadow synthesis, and 768×768 for the light diffusion stage. The final output is a linear composite of a diffuse image and a shadowed image, with blending weights providing an additional continuous intensity knob.

## Method

COMPOSE decomposes the environment map into an ambient component and a single editable 2D isotropic Gaussian representing the dominant light source, parameterized by position, standard deviation (light size), and scale (intensity). The four-stage pipeline is: (1) a VAE regresses an LDR environment map from the input image, from which the dominant light position is extracted by fitting a 2D Gaussian; (2) a hierarchical transformer encoder-decoder removes hard shadows and specularities to produce a diffuse image; (3) a two-stage shadow synthesis network—first a U-Net (adopted from Sun et al. [41]) that takes the diffuse image and a 4-channel spatially-repeated lighting feature map to produce a coarse relit image, then a conditional DDPM that refines shadow boundaries using the coarse image and lighting parameters as spatial conditions; (4) linear compositing of the diffuse and shadowed images with tunable weights. The key insight is that the Gaussian parameterization makes the dominant light independently editable (including shrinking, which has no equivalent operation on a raw environment map), enabling a full suite of shadow operations that were previously infeasible.

## Results

On the OLAT light-stage evaluation set (16 held-out subjects, unseen lighting), COMPOSE achieves the best performance across all four metrics: MAE 0.0965, MSE 0.0349, SSIM 0.7780, and LPIPS 0.1973. These results outperform Total Relighting (MAE 0.1008, LPIPS 0.2120) and Hou et al. (MAE 0.1172, LPIPS 0.2371) on every metric. Qualitatively, COMPOSE also demonstrates full shadow editing control (softening, intensifying, light-size modification, and rotation) on in-the-wild images while preserving ambient lighting, a capability neither baseline supports.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMPOSE outperforms both quantitative baselines on all four metrics. Against Hou et al. [17], COMPOSE reduces MAE by ~18% (0.0965 vs. 0.1172) and LPIPS by ~17% (0.1973 vs. 0.2371); qualitatively, Hou et al. fails to remove existing source-image shadows before relighting and does not model light size, producing inaccurate shadow shapes. Against Total Relighting [31], COMPOSE achieves lower MAE (0.0965 vs. 0.1008) and LPIPS (0.1973 vs. 0.2120) with higher SSIM (0.7780 vs. 0.7754); Total Relighting frequently overshadows or undershadows and produces blurrier shadow boundaries. For the shadow-softening comparison with Futschik et al. [11] (qualitative, Fig. 7), both methods progressively soften shadows across diffusion levels 0.75→0.0, but COMPOSE leaves noticeably fewer residual shadow traces at the 0.0 level (complete removal), attributed to its hierarchical transformer light-diffusion network handling multi-scale shadow removal more effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

COMPOSE builds on the U-Net architecture from Sun et al. [41] for coarse shadow synthesis and extends the environment-map-based relighting paradigm (Total Relighting [31], Hou et al. [17]) by decomposing the lighting representation into an ambient component and an editable 2D isotropic Gaussian dominant light source. This decomposition departs from prior methods that either replace the entire environment map for relighting or restrict shadow editing to softening/removal only (Futschik et al. [11]). The addition of a conditional DDPM refinement stage on top of the U-Net is a natural extension of recent diffusion-based image editing approaches, applied here specifically to sharpen shadow boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COMPOSE provides the first single-image pipeline for comprehensive portrait shadow editing (position, intensity, shape) while preserving ambient scene lighting, filling a practical gap between full relighting and limited shadow softening. Key limitations include reliance on a single dominant light (primarily outdoor/sunny settings), color shifts introduced by the compositing step, and identity degradation at the U-Net stage. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-light-source and indoor environments, improving generalizability beyond the 107-subject light-stage training set, and whether latent diffusion models can replace the current two-stage U-Net + DDPM architecture for better identity preservation.
