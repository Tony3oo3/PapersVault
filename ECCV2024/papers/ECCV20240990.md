## Problem

SGD training is inherently stochastic, causing model predictions to deviate significantly from historical performance on new batches, which harms generalization. Existing regularizers (L2 penalties, sharpness-aware minimization) operate on parameters or loss geometry and are data-agnostic, failing to capture the semantic structure that emerges from the training trajectory itself. The paper asks: can we control SGD trajectory variability by penalizing predictions that are inconsistent with what past model versions would have forecast?

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and outputs class logits, identical in form to vanilla classification. The distinguishing detail is that training operates in logit space (pre-softmax), where the companion model's predictions serve as a soft supervision signal alongside the ground-truth label. The companion network mirrors the primary architecture exactly, so the method adds one forward/backward pass per iteration but no additional parameters at inference time.

## Method

Deep Companion Learning (DCL) trains a companion model ω in parallel with the primary model θ, both sharing the same architecture. At each iteration, the companion is updated to minimize MSE between its own logits and a convex combination of its previous output and the current primary model's output (weighted by a tunable smoothing parameter α), effectively maintaining an exponentially weighted forecast of the training trajectory. The primary model is then penalized via an additional MSE term measuring the gap between its logits and the companion's logits, creating a dynamic, data-dependent regularizer that adapts as training progresses. This mechanism narrows the top non-target class distribution for each class (e.g., consistently selecting Tiger and Lion for Leopard), revealing a latent semantic structure in logit space that improves linear separability and generalization. The method is plug-and-play: it requires no pre-training, no additional data, and can be layered onto semi-supervised, self-supervised (MAE), fine-tuning, and knowledge-distillation pipelines by simply adding the consistency term to the existing loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18, DCL achieves 82.4% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the cross-entropy baseline (78.3%) by 4.1 points and the strongest recent baseline SAF (80.8%) by 1.6 points. On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-50, DCL reaches 77.1% top-1 accuracy versus 76.0% for CE and 76.6% for GAM. DCL also scales to transformer architectures, improving ViT-Tiny on ImageNet-1K from 65.7% to 66.5% and Swin-Tiny from 81.3% to 81.7%. Notably, DCL trained from scratch on CIFAR-100 (32×32) outperforms ImageNet-pretrained models fine-tuned on the same dataset, while requiring only 1298M MACs at inference versus 4198M for the pre-trained counterpart.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCL uniformly outperforms all compared baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), DCL (82.4%) beats SAF (80.8%), GAM (80.5%), PSKD (80.6%), DML (79.9%), and SAM (79.3%) by 1.6–3.1 points. On ImageNet-1K (ResNet-50), DCL (77.1%) leads GAM (76.6%), SAM (76.5%), SAF (76.4%), PSKD (76.3%), and DML (75.8%) by 0.5–1.3 points. In the knowledge distillation setting, DCL without a teacher (74.5%) surpasses standard KD [18] (74.0%), and when combined with SOTA KD methods it improves ReviewKD (75.6→76.2), SimKD (77.8→78.0), and DKD (75.9→76.5). For self-supervised pre-training, MAE+DCL outperforms vanilla MAE on all downstream tasks (e.g., 83.4% vs 82.8% on ImageNet, 80.0% vs 79.2% on CUB-200). In semi-supervised learning, adding DCL to FixMatch yields 1–3% gains, most pronounced with few labels (88.7% vs 86.2% with only 40 labeled samples on CIFAR-10).

## Relation to Prior Work

DCL carves out a distinct niche among consistency-based regularizers by training a dedicated companion model that recursively forecasts logits via exponential smoothing of past predictions, rather than relying on parameter-level EMA (Mean Teacher, SWA) or fixed teacher models (PS-KD). It departs from sharpness-aware and L2 parameter regularizers by operating in prediction space, yielding a data-dependent penalty that adapts to the current batch's semantic content. The connection to variance reduction (Defazio & Bottou) is acknowledged but the authors argue those methods are ineffective for deep models, whereas the continuously updated companion network provides a more effective surrogate for controlling SGD trajectory variability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DCL—applicable to supervised training, fine-tuning, semi-supervised learning (FixMatch), self-supervised pre-training (MAE), and knowledge distillation without architectural changes—makes it a broadly useful training-time regularizer. The finding that a from-scratch DCL model on 32×32 CIFAR-100 images matches or exceeds an ImageNet-pretrained model at 224×224 resolution, while requiring ~3× fewer MACs at inference, suggests that training-scheme improvements can partially substitute for the compute cost of pre-training. Open questions include whether the modest ImageNet-1K gains (0.4–1.1%) will grow with larger models or longer training, whether the companion model's overhead (an additional forward/backward pass of the same architecture) is acceptable at scale, and whether the logit-space consistency objective generalizes to non-classification tasks beyond the reconstruction-loss variant shown for MAE.
