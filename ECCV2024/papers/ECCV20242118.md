## Problem

Indoor radar perception datasets are scarce, small-scale, and typically rely on low-resolution single-view point clouds collected in open-space single-room settings, leaving a significant gap for developing robust perception in cluttered, multi-room environments. This paper addresses that gap by introducing the MMVR dataset, the largest open-source indoor radar dataset to date, with high-resolution multi-view heatmaps, diverse rooms and subjects, and comprehensive annotations supporting three perception tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model input is two orthogonal pre-CFAR radar heatmaps (range-azimuth from a horizontal radar and range-elevation from a vertical radar) at 256×128 resolution with 1.3° angular resolution, obtained by 3D FFT over FMCW data cubes and integrated along the Doppler axis. The paired RGB camera (480×640, 15 fps) is used exclusively for annotation generation, not as model input. Outputs are image-plane bounding boxes, 17 COCO keypoints per person, and binary segmentation masks at 480×640, all curated by humans to remove spurious labels caused by glass reflections, whiteboards, and hanging objects.

## Method

The sensor testbed pairs two TI AWR2243 cascade radars (86 half-wavelength-spaced virtual elements each) in perpendicular horizontal and vertical orientations with an Intel RealSense D455 camera, achieving 1.3° angular resolution versus the 15° typical of single-chip radars used in prior datasets. Annotations are generated by Mask2Former (bounding boxes and instance masks) and HRNet (17 COCO keypoints) pretrained on COCO, followed by human curation. For benchmarks, the authors re-implement RF-Pose with a part-affinity-field extension for multi-person association, modify RFMask to compute BBox loss directly on the image plane rather than on radar-view planes, and add DETR as an additional baseline. Evaluation is conducted under two protocols (open foreground with one subject vs. cluttered space with up to three subjects) and two data splits (random 80/10/10 vs. cross-environment with an unseen room held out), across 395 one-minute segments totaling 345K frames.

## Results

Under the standard P1/S1 setting (single subject, open foreground, random split), RFMask achieves AP50=67.30 and AP=25.53 for object detection, while RF-Pose achieves AP50=62.88 and AP=46.24 for pose estimation, and RFMask achieves IoU=71.98 for instance segmentation. Under the more challenging P2/S2 cross-environment split (unseen cluttered room d8), performance degrades sharply: detection AP50 drops to 22.77, pose AP50 to 11.98, and segmentation IoU to 56.07, revealing limited generalization of current baselines to unseen cluttered environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares two baseline methods: RFMask [29] (modified for image-plane BBox loss) and DETR [7] (adapted for radar heatmaps). On P1/S1, DETR consistently outperforms RFMask across all detection and segmentation metrics: AP 35.78 vs. 24.64, AP50 78.59 vs. 66.17, AP75 28.02 vs. 15.21, and segmentation IoU 72.65 vs. 70.64, indicating that transformer-based query mechanisms extract more discriminative features from radar heatmaps. In a cross-dataset comparison (Table 7), MMVR(P1) evaluated with RFMask under S2 outperforms HIBER(Walk) on every metric (AP 24.46 vs. 17.77, AP50 66.82 vs. 52.46, AP75 11.22 vs. 6.78, IoU 67.03 vs. 48.47), suggesting that the larger, more diverse MMVR dataset yields stronger baseline performance than the smaller HIBER dataset under comparable open-foreground walking conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the indoor radar perception dataset lineage from RF-Pose (Zhao et al., CVPR 2018) and HIBER (Wu et al., IEEE TMM 2023) by scaling up to a multi-day, multi-room, multi-subject setting with high-resolution (1.3°) multi-view radar heatmaps, departing from the low-resolution (15°) single-view point clouds used by earlier datasets like RadHAR, mm-Pose, and MM-Fi. It re-implements RF-Pose for pose estimation and modifies RFMask for detection/segmentation as baselines, and additionally introduces DETR as a stronger detection backbone. The key conceptual departure from HIBER is the inclusion of cluttered rooms with natural occlusions and multiple subjects, moving beyond the open-foreground single-room setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMVR is the largest open-source indoor radar dataset to date (345K frames, 25 subjects, 6 rooms) and the first to support cross-environment generalization evaluation via its S2 split, which reveals a dramatic performance collapse (e.g., detection AP dropping from 31.37 to 6.03 under P2) that highlights how poorly current radar perception methods transfer to unseen cluttered environments. Open questions include whether radar can outperform cameras under natural occlusion (where RGB-based annotations become unreliable), how methods scale beyond 3 subjects, and whether the high-resolution heatmap representation is truly necessary versus simpler point-cloud inputs. The dataset is likely to become the standard benchmark for indoor radar perception research.
