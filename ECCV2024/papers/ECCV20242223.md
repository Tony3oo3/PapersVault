## Problem

Supervised low-light image enhancement (LLIE) methods overfit to training pairs and generalize poorly to unseen degradation conditions, while unsupervised and zero-shot methods suffer from under/over-enhancement, noise, and artifacts. The standard mean-teacher semi-supervised framework, though effective in other domains, cannot adequately capture low-level features of unlabeled data and exhibits domain shift when applied to the diverse real-world degradations encountered in low-light imaging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242223/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes both labeled pairs (low-light + normal-light GT) and unlabeled low-light images simultaneously during training. Unlabeled data are synthesized via Adobe Lightroom by perturbing exposure ([-5,0]), contrast ([-100,100]), and vibrance ([-100,0]) on labeled normal-light images, yielding 5000 additional samples. The network outputs K=4 multiscale enhanced images at progressively coarser resolutions, with training patches of 256×256 and test-time symmetric padding to multiples of 32×32.

## Method

LMT-GP introduces a latent mean-teacher framework where both teacher and student networks (identical encoder-decoder architectures) process labeled and unlabeled data, with the encoder producing latent vectors that the decoder uses alongside intermediate features to generate multiscale outputs. To address domain shift, a mean-teacher-assisted GP learning strategy applies PCA to select representative basis vectors from labeled data, then models unlabeled latent vectors as linear combinations of these bases plus Gaussian noise, using Gaussian Process Regression to compute a posterior distribution that constrains the student's unlabeled latent vectors toward the teacher's pseudo-latent vectors via an assisted GPR loss (simplified to an L2 norm for stability). A Pseudo-label Adaptation Module (PAM) selects which latent vectors (student vs. teacher) to use as supervision by comparing PSNR scores of their respective outputs, ensuring reliable guidance. The total loss combines L1 reconstruction, negative SSIM, VGG-16 perceptual loss on labeled data, L1 consistency on unlabeled data, and the simplified GPR loss, with teacher weights updated via exponential moving average.

## Results

On the LOL dataset, LMT-GP achieves the best PSNR of 24.12 dB and LPIPS of 0.107, surpassing the second-best supervised method LLFlow (23.68 dB, 0.125) by 0.44 dB and 0.018 respectively. On the more challenging VE-LOL dataset, LMT-GP leads with PSNR 28.55 dB and LPIPS 0.071, outperforming LLFormer (27.42 dB, 0.144) by 1.13 dB and 0.073. For downstream tasks, LMT-GP achieves the highest mAP of 43.92% on Darkface (face detection) and the highest mIoU of 39.33% on ACDC (nighttime segmentation), exceeding the best baselines by 0.19% and 3.22% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMT-GP outperforms all 15 baselines on PSNR and LPIPS across both LOL and VE-LOL. On LOL, it beats the strongest supervised competitor LLFlow by 0.44 dB in PSNR and 0.018 in LPIPS, and surpasses LLFormer by 0.77 dB in PSNR. On VE-LOL, the margin widens: LMT-GP leads LLFormer by 1.13 dB in PSNR and LLFlow by 1.94 dB, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.071 vs. 0.090 for LLFlow). For downstream face detection on Darkface, LMT-GP achieves 43.92% mAP, narrowly beating GLADNet (43.73%) and substantially outperforming the unprocessed original (40.47%) by 3.45%. For nighttime segmentation on ACDC, LMT-GP achieves 39.33% mIoU, exceeding the second-best PairLIE (36.11%) by 3.22% and the previous semi-supervised method DRBN (35.02%) by 4.31%. Notably, LMT-GP is the only method (alongside PairLIE) that detects bicycles on ACDC, and it outperforms PairLIE on that category by 31.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-teacher semi-supervised framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, NeurIPS 2017) by introducing latent vectors from both labeled and unlabeled data into the teacher-student supervision loop, creating a 'latent mean-teacher' architecture. It further departs from prior semi-supervised LLIE work (e.g., DRBN, Yang et al. CVPR 2020) by replacing adversarial learning with a Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) loss to model the distribution of unlabeled latent vectors, drawing on the GP-based transfer learning paradigm of Yasarla et al. (CVPR 2020). The key conceptual departure is using labeled data as a GP basis to represent unlabeled data's latent distribution, thereby addressing the domain shift problem that plagues standard mean-teacher in the diverse degradation conditions of real-world low-light imaging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that semi-supervised LLIE with latent vector supervision and GP-based distribution modeling can outperform both purely supervised and unsupervised methods on unseen datasets (VE-LOL, Darkface, ACDC), suggesting a path toward more generalizable enhancement without requiring large paired datasets. However, the GP component is substantially simplified in practice (the covariance matrix is replaced by a constant to stabilize training), raising questions about whether the full GP formulation would yield additional gains. The PAM module's large ablation effect (≈2.6 dB) hints that reliable pseudo-label selection is a critical but underexplored aspect of semi-supervised low-level vision. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse unlabeled pools and whether the GP modeling generalizes beyond the specific encoder-decoder architecture used here.
