## Problem

High-resolution semantic segmentation (e.g., 8-megapixel inputs for autonomous driving) incurs prohibitive computational cost, while simply downsampling the input degrades accuracy on small objects and boundaries. The paper addresses how to recover full-resolution accuracy without paying the full cost of dense high-resolution inference, by exploiting the observation that low-resolution and high-resolution predictions differ only on a sparse set of pixels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241784/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 1024×2048 on Cityscapes) in two stages: a downsampled version (e.g., 512×1024) is fed to a frozen dense segmentation backbone, while only a sparse subset (~10–20%) of full-resolution pixels is passed to the refinement module as raw RGB values. The output is a full-resolution per-pixel semantic segmentation mask, where most pixels retain the low-resolution prediction and only the selected high-entropy pixels receive refined labels.

## Method

SparseRefine is a plug-and-play refinement module with three components. First, an entropy selector computes per-pixel entropy from the frozen backbone's logits and selects pixels exceeding a threshold α, targeting the ~10% of pixels most likely misclassified. Second, a sparse feature extractor—a modified MinkowskiUNet with sparse convolutions and deconvolutions—processes only the raw RGB values of selected pixels, preserving the sparsity pattern to avoid computation on zero activations. Third, a gated ensembler learns a per-pixel weighting factor w∈[0,1] via a two-layer MLP over concatenated predictions and entropies, softly fusing the initial coarse prediction with the sparse refinement. The entire refinement module is trained independently (500 epochs, AdamW) with the dense backbone kept frozen, making it architecture-agnostic across CNN and ViT backbones.

## Results

On Cityscapes, SparseRefine achieves 1.5×–3.7× inference latency reduction across five segmentation models (HRNet-W48, SegFormer-B5, Mask2Former-T/L, SegNeXt-L) while matching or slightly exceeding the full-resolution mIoU (e.g., SegFormer-B5: 140.6 ms → 38.5 ms at 81.2 mIoU vs. 81.1 mIoU for the dense baseline). MACs are reduced by 1.4×–3.1×. Generalization to Pascal VOC, BDD100K, DeepGlobe, and ISIC yields a consistent 1.5×–2.0× speedup with no accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense full-resolution baselines, SparseRefine matches or exceeds mIoU (e.g., HRNet-W48: 80.9 vs. 80.7; SegFormer-B5: 81.2 vs. 81.1) while cutting latency by 1.5×–3.7×. Versus SparseViT (token pruning) on Mask2Former-L, SparseRefine is 1.5× faster (89.9 ms vs. 132.3 ms) at comparable mIoU (83.0 vs. 83.2). Against PointRend (mask refinement) on HRNet-W48, SparseRefine wins on both latency (32.4 ms vs. 32.8 ms) and accuracy (80.9 vs. 79.9 mIoU), because it refines directly on high-resolution RGB rather than relying on downsampled hidden features. Compared to patch-level refinement (PatchRefine at 256 or 512 patch sizes), SparseRefine is 1.4×–1.7× faster (32.4 ms vs. 44.1/55.4 ms) and achieves higher mIoU (80.9 vs. 80.8), since pixel-level sparsity avoids redundant computation within patches. The low-resolution dense baselines (e.g., HRNet-W48 at 512×1024) are 3.7× faster than full resolution but lose 1.5 mIoU; SparseRefine recovers this gap at only ~60% of the full-resolution latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseRefine draws on sparse convolution (MinkowskiUNet) for its feature extractor and on entropy-based uncertainty estimation for pixel selection, but its core contribution is a new 'dense low-resolution + sparse high-resolution' decomposition that is explicitly orthogonal to existing lines of work. Unlike multi-scale models (HRNet, FPN) that process low- and high-resolution branches in parallel, SparseRefine operates sequentially: a frozen dense model handles the bulk of computation at reduced resolution, and a lightweight sparse module refines only uncertain pixels at full resolution. It also departs from token-pruning methods (SparseViT) that skip computation on unimportant tokens, and from mask-refinement methods (PointRend, RefineMask) that upscale output resolution using intermediate features, by instead reducing input resolution and recovering detail from raw RGB pixels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SparseRefine—compatible with both CNN and ViT backbones across five diverse datasets—makes it a practical tool for deploying high-resolution segmentation on latency-constrained hardware, with 1.5–3.7× speedups at no accuracy cost. The oracle gap (92.8 vs. 80.9 mIoU) reveals substantial untapped potential in the paradigm, suggesting that better pixel selection or richer sparse features could close much of the remaining accuracy-accuracy trade-off. Open questions include whether the entropy threshold can be learned end-to-end without the latency cost of the learnable selector, whether the added ~80M parameters of the sparse module are acceptable in memory-constrained settings, and whether the approach extends to other dense prediction tasks (depth estimation, surface normal prediction) where the same 'few pixels need high-res detail' structure holds.
