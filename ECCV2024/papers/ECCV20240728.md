## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud UDA methods either adapt the feature extractor to produce domain-invariant features (losing target-specific semantic information) or adapt the classifier via target-styled feature augmentation (relying on accurate target distribution estimation). In point clouds, scanning noise causes large disparities in distances between individual target samples and the source domain, making direct target distribution estimation unreliable and degrading classifier adaptation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240728/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unordered point clouds of m×3 3D coordinates from a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain, processed by a DGCNN backbone with a simplified single linear-layer classifier. It outputs a class label per target sample from a shared C-class label space. During training, the method maintains feature pools, confidence score pools, and label pools (ground-truth for source, pseudo-labels for target) that are updated at the start of each intermediate domain construction stage.

## Method

PCFEA deeply couples two complementary adaptation strategies: a progressive target-styled feature augmentation (PTFA) for classifier adaptation and an intermediate domain feature extractor adaptation (IDFA) for feature extractor adaptation. PTFA divides training into stages, each constructing an intermediate domain by selecting high-confidence samples with progressively decreasing source ratios and increasing target ratios, then generating target-styled augmentations on source features toward that intermediate domain's estimated mean and covariance. IDFA applies a cosine-similarity-based loss encouraging both source and target features to approach the intermediate domain's category mean while staying far from other categories' means. The two components mutually reinforce: IDFA's compact alignment improves the distribution estimation that PTFA depends on, while PTFA's progressive intermediate domains reduce the domain gap, lowering the risk of IDFA's misalignment from incorrect pseudo-labels. The total loss combines PTFA's cross-entropy on augmented features with IDFA's cosine alignment losses for both source and target, preceded by a 10-epoch warm-up with standard cross-entropy.

## Results

On the PointDA-10 dataset (6 adaptation scenarios), PCFEA achieves 76.5% average accuracy without pseudo-labeling fine-tuning and 78.8% with it, surpassing the previous SOTA DAS* (75.9%) by 2.9% and DAPS (73.9%) by 4.9%. On GraspNetPC-10 (4 scenarios), PCFEA reaches 87.6% average accuracy without PS and 90.1% with PS, outperforming DAS* (89.6%) by 0.5% and DAPS (87.8%) by 2.3%. The method also improves over the no-adaptation baseline by over 30% on GraspNetPC-10 and by 14.3% on PointDA-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCFEA outperforms all 12 baselines across all six PointDA-10 scenarios and all four GraspNetPC-10 scenarios. Against the strongest FEA-only baseline DAPS [39], PCFEA gains 5.7% average accuracy on PointDA-10 (76.5% vs 70.8%) and 6.9% on GraspNetPC-10 (87.6% vs 80.7%). Against the strongest combined FEA+PS baseline DAS* [39], PCFEA still leads by 2.9% on PointDA-10 (78.8% vs 75.9%) and 0.5% on GraspNetPC-10 (90.1% vs 89.6%), despite DAS* employing a more parameter-intensive pseudo-labeling strategy. Compared to the two classifier-adaptation baselines, PCFEA improves over ISDA [37] by 8.7% and over TSA [18] by 6.5% on PointDA-10, and by over 15% on GraspNetPC-10, validating the benefit of the progressive intermediate-domain strategy over direct target distribution estimation. The largest single-scenario gains appear in S*→M (85.1% vs 73.1% for DAPS, +12.0%) and S*→S (80.9% vs 76.2% for DAPS, +4.7%), where the progressive approach most effectively bridges the real-to-sim domain gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier adaptation line initiated by ISDA (Wang et al., NeurIPS 2019) and TSA (Li et al., CVPR 2021) from 2D to 3D point clouds, but departs from TSA's direct target-distribution estimation by introducing progressive intermediate domains that gradually bridge the source-target gap. It also couples this classifier adaptation (PTFA) with a feature extractor adaptation strategy (IDFA) that aligns features to intermediate domain means via cosine-similarity losses, creating a mutual-benefit loop where compact alignment improves distribution estimation and smoother intermediate domains reduce misalignment risk. The progressive sample-selection schedule (decreasing source ratio, increasing target ratio) is a novel 3D-specific design motivated by the heterogeneous scanning noise that makes target-sample distances to the source domain highly variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and previously overlooked issue in 3D UDA: the large and uneven domain gaps caused by scanning noise make direct target-distribution estimation unreliable, which undermines both classifier and feature extractor adaptation. The ablation showing that naively combining TSFA and IDFA without progressive intermediate domains actually degrades performance (68.6% vs. 74.3% for TSFA alone) is a useful negative result that validates the necessity of the progressive strategy. Open questions include whether the progressive intermediate-domain framework generalizes to other 3D tasks (segmentation, detection), whether it benefits 2D UDA with similarly large domain gaps, and whether the added training complexity (multiple inference passes for domain estimation) scales to larger datasets or more classes.
