## Problem

Reconstructing high-speed color HDR video from a single spike camera with a Bayer CFA remains unsolved: existing monochromatic spike methods lack color, hybrid spike-RGB systems suffer from low RGB frame rates and complex alignment, and supervised deep methods trained on synthetic spikes generalize poorly to real data due to imperfect noise modeling. No prior approach achieves full-temporal color HDR reconstruction from a single chromatic spike device in a real-data-driven manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240314/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw mosaicked chromatic spike sequences (single-bit signals per pixel per channel) read at 20000 Hz from a spike camera fitted with a Bayer-pattern CFA. The spikes are first split into four RGGB channels of size h/2 × w/2, then accumulated over a short temporal window W (typically W=10) to form initial mosaicked frames. The output is a full-color HDR video at 20000/W FPS (2000 FPS for W=10), where each frame has extended dynamic range achieved through progressive pseudo-long-exposure accumulation rather than a single long integration window.

## Method

The pipeline combines two complementary modules. First, a self-supervised denoising module (U-Net-inspired FCN with blind-spot masking) is trained exclusively on real-world chromatic spike streams by exploiting the validated zero-mean noise distribution of short-term spike accumulation, following the Noise2Self/Blind2Unblind paradigm to avoid identity mapping without requiring clean targets. Second, a progressive warping module iteratively warps K accumulated frames at each of L steps using off-the-shelf optical flow estimated from denoised and demosaicked intermediate frames, progressively building a pseudo-long exposure that extends dynamic range while avoiding motion blur. The denoising module is integrated into every warping step to improve flow estimation quality. The entire system is trained end-to-end on unlabelled real spike data with a regularized re-visible loss plus an image-quality-assignment loss, requiring no synthetic spike simulation or paired clean/noisy data.

## Results

On the synthetic chromatic spike dataset, the proposed method achieves 30.78 dB PSNR, 0.883 SSIM, 0.208 LPIPS, 7.991 HDR-VDP3, and 0.3023 HDR-VQM, outperforming all baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, MS23 [21], reaches 28.41 dB PSNR and 0.635 SSIM, while the best bio-inspired baseline TFP (W=100) [46] achieves 28.79 dB PSNR and 0.720 SSIM. The method reconstructs 2000 FPS color HDR video from a single spike camera with a Bayer-pattern CFA, using W=10 as the temporal accumulation window.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three families of baselines: bio-inspired monochromatic methods (TFI, TFP, TFSTP) adapted to chromatic spikes, and the SPAD-based color method MS23. The proposed method dominates all baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor MS23 [21] (28.41 dB PSNR, 0.635 SSIM), the proposed method gains +2.37 dB PSNR and +0.248 SSIM, with a particularly large advantage in HDR-VQM (0.3023 vs. 0.5765, a 47% reduction). Against TFP (W=100) [46] (28.79 dB PSNR, 0.720 SSIM), the gain is +1.99 dB PSNR and +0.163 SSIM, while also avoiding the motion blur that TFP (W=100) exhibits in high-speed scenes. The bio-inspired baselines TFI and TFSTP [43] are substantially outperformed (by ~10 dB PSNR), confirming that deep learning with self-supervised denoising and progressive warping is necessary for chromatic spike reconstruction. Qualitative results on real-world data further confirm that only the proposed method simultaneously suppresses noise, avoids motion blur, and preserves color fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spike-camera video reconstruction line (TFI, TFP, TFSTP, Spk2ImgNet) from monochromatic spikes to mosaicked chromatic spikes produced by placing a Bayer CFA on the sensor. It departs from hybrid spike-RGB camera systems (e.g., Chang et al. 2023) that require temporal synchronization and beam splitters, instead achieving color HDR from a single device. The denoising module adapts the blind-spot self-supervised paradigm of Noise2Self and Blind2Unblind to the chromatic spike setting, while the progressive warping module draws on burst-photography-style optical-flow alignment to simulate pseudo-long exposure without motion blur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-device 2000 FPS color HDR reconstruction removes the engineering burden of hybrid camera alignment, making high-speed color imaging more deployable in compact form factors. The validated zero-mean noise hypothesis for chromatic spikes provides a principled basis for self-supervised training without synthetic noise models, which is broadly applicable to other neuromorphic sensors. Open questions remain: the CFA inherently reduces per-pixel photon count, degrading low-light texture recovery, and the denoising module risks over-smoothing fine details—both flagged by the authors as directions for future CFA design and more sophisticated denoisers.
