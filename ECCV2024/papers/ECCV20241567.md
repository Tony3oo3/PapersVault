## Problem

Ranking-based loss functions (AP Loss, RS Loss) outperform score-based losses in object detection by better aligning with evaluation metrics, requiring fewer hyperparameters, and being robust to class imbalance. However, they require O(PN) pairwise comparisons between positives and negatives, which is prohibitive when N reaches 10^8 (e.g., ATSS on COCO), making GPU-vectorized computation infeasible and preventing their use with transformer-based detectors. This paper closes the efficiency gap between ranking-based and score-based losses.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the classification logits (scores) of object hypotheses produced by a detector's head, taking RGB images at 1333×800 as input and producing bounding boxes with class labels or segmentation masks as output. The key internal detail is that the loss consumes a set of P positive and N negative logits (where N can exceed 10^7), and the bucketing step reduces these to at most 2|P|+1 effective logits (positives plus one prototype per negative bucket) before pairwise comparisons are computed, enabling the comparison matrix to fit in GPU memory.

## Method

The core idea is to sort all logits by score and group consecutive negatives between successive positives into buckets, representing each bucket by its mean score (prototype logit). This reduces the number of pairwise comparisons from O(PN) to O(max(N log N, P²)), since only P positives are compared against at most |P|+1 prototype negatives. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that when the smoothing parameter δ=0, the bucketed AP and RS Losses yield exactly the same gradients as their unbucketed counterparts, and (Theorem 2) establishes the improved time complexity. The bucketed primary terms use a weighted probability mass function proportional to bucket size to distribute ranking error, and gradients are back-propagated by normalizing the prototype gradient by bucket size. Applied to Co-DETR, this enables the first training of a transformer-based detector with a ranking-based loss, achieving 50.1 AP on COCO with ResNet-50 in 12 epochs while reducing per-iteration time by 6×.

## Results

On COCO val, BRS-DETR (Co-DETR + BRS Loss) achieves 50.1 AP with ResNet-50, 300 queries, and only 12 epochs, outperforming all existing DETR variants with the same backbone (DN-DETR: 48.6, DINO: 49.4, H-DETR: 48.7, Co-DETR: 49.3). With Swin-L backbone, BRS-DETR reaches 57.2 AP. The bucketing approach reduces loss computation time by up to ~40× and overall detector training time by 1.9×–6.0× (e.g., 0.17 s/iter vs 0.50 s/iter for Faster R-CNN, 0.69 s/iter vs 4.14 s/iter for Co-DETR) while preserving or slightly improving AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-bucketed RS Loss, BRS Loss matches or slightly improves AP (e.g., 39.5 vs 39.4 for Faster R-CNN R-50; 41.1 vs 41.1 for Cascade R-CNN) while reducing iteration time by 2.0×–5.3×. Against AP Loss, BAP Loss achieves comparable or marginally better AP (38.5 vs 38.1 for ATSS) at 2.1× less time. For transformer-based detectors, BRS-DETR outperforms Co-DETR by +0.8 AP (ResNet-50), +0.6 AP (Swin-T), and +0.3 AP (Swin-L), and beats DN-DETR by +1.5 AP with fewer training epochs (12 vs 50). Compared to other ranking-based losses, BRS surpasses aLRP Loss by +2.1 AP on both Faster R-CNN and ATSS, and DR Loss by +1.7 AP on ATSS, while also requiring fewer hyperparameters (1–3 vs 5). Against score-based losses (CE+L1, Focal+GIoU), BRS achieves +1.9 AP and +0.5 AP respectively with similar or fewer hyperparameters and comparable iteration time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ranking-based loss lineage initiated by AP Loss (Chen et al., 2019/2020) and extended by RS Loss (Oksuz et al., 2021), which reformulated object detection training around pairwise score comparisons rather than per-example classification. The core departure is a bucketing strategy that groups sequential negatives between successive positives into prototypes, reducing the O(PN) pairwise comparison cost to O(max(N log N, P²)) while provably preserving gradients (Theorem 1, δ=0). This efficiency gain also enables the first application of ranking-based losses to transformer-based detectors, specifically Co-DETR (Zong et al., 2022), which prior work explicitly excluded due to prohibitive training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the training-time gap between ranking-based and score-based losses, this work removes the primary practical barrier to adopting ranking-based objectives, which already offer fewer hyperparameters, better imbalance robustness, and alignment with AP evaluation. The first successful integration of ranking losses into Co-DETR opens a new direction for transformer-based detector training that was previously infeasible. Open questions include whether the δ=0 gradient-equivalence guarantee degrades under the smoothed step function (δ>0) used in practice, how the approach extends to other ranking formulations (e.g., DR Loss, aLRP), and whether further bucketing refinements can push the constant factors closer to score-based losses.
