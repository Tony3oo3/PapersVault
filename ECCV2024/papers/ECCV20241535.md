## Problem

Classical parametric 3D shape models (e.g., SMAL for quadrupeds) require expert knowledge to produce novel shapes, and naively sampling their latent spaces yields unrealistic mixtures of species. The paper asks whether language can serve as an intuitive control interface to navigate these parameter spaces and generate shapes for species or varieties never seen during training, without resorting to analysis-by-synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241535/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method accepts either a free-form text prompt (encoded via CLIP ViT-B/32-LAION-2B) or a single RGB image, and outputs a triangular mesh: for animals, a rigged mesh sharing the SMAL+ skeleton and topology (145-dim shape space); for trees, a procedural mesh with branches and leaves generated by the Tree-Gen Blender add-on (60 of its 105 parameters). The output is constrained to the parametric model's shape space, so it cannot produce details outside that space (e.g., a rhino horn), but it guarantees consistent topology and rigging suitable for animation and motion transfer.

## Method

AWOL learns a conditional mapping from CLIP embeddings to shape parameters using a Real-NVP normalizing flow whose input is the concatenation of the CLIP encoding and the shape vector, with a unit-Gaussian latent target. Three key architectural modifications distinguish it from prior normalizing-flow text-to-shape work: (1) the binary partitioning masks in the affine coupling layers are learned rather than fixed (checkerboard or dimension-wise), (2) training uses an L1 reconstruction loss on shape parameters instead of the original density-estimation objective, and (3) two additional fully-connected compression layers (1024→512) are inserted in the scale and translation MLPs, which the authors show is necessary for the learned-mask variant to perform well. The flow uses 5 coupling layers and is trained in two stages—first on text–shape pairs (6000 epochs), then jointly on text and image encodings (3000 epochs)—with only 3–10 images per species or breed. The central hypothesis is that CLIP's latent space is sufficiently smooth that interpolation or extrapolation in language/image space translates into realistic, novel shape parameters, enabling generation of unseen dog breeds, quadruped species, and tree species from a very small training set.

## Results

In the primary quantitative evaluation (a perceptual study on the StanfordExtra test set), AWOL receives 884 votes versus BITE's 971 votes overall (p=0.02, favoring BITE). However, after removing images of puppies (where AWOL without an age attribute defaults to adult), AWOL outperforms BITE with 850 vs. 830 votes (p=0.3, not significant). The ablation studies (Table 1) confirm that learned masks with hidden-space compression yield the best CLIP-based scores (62:38 vs. dimension-wise masking, p=0.13), and that adding a density loss degrades performance dramatically (86:14 in favor of reconstruction-only training, p=1.24e-7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BITE [47] (the strongest quantitative baseline), AWOL loses the overall perceptual study (884 vs. 971 votes, p=0.02), but this gap is driven by puppy images where AWOL defaults to adult morphology; excluding those, AWOL edges out BITE (850 vs. 830, p=0.3). Against DeepTree [73], the comparison is purely qualitative (Fig. 13): AWOL generates trees from text prompts for species unseen in training, while DeepTree relies on situated latents learned from procedural growth rules; AWOL's trees are noted to capture species-level structure but lack the fine environmental detail of DeepTree. Against Instant Mesh [67] and Genie (LumaAI) [1] (Fig. 2), the comparison is also qualitative: AWOL produces rigged, topology-consistent animal meshes suitable for animation, whereas these methods generate unrigged meshes or NeRFs without articulated skeletons. Overall, AWOL's advantage is not in raw visual fidelity but in producing semantically controlled, rigged, and topology-consistent 3D assets from text or images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-Forge formulation of mapping CLIP encodings to shape model parameters via a Real-NVP normalizing flow, but departs in three key ways: it uses trainable binary masks instead of fixed checkerboard/dimension-wise masks, replaces the density estimation loss with a reconstruction loss, and adds compression layers to the coupling networks. For the animal model, it builds directly on the SMAL lineage (SMAL, D-SMAL, hSMAL) to create SMAL+, a broader multi-species parametric model. The tree generation component is a novel application, repurposing the non-differentiable Tree-Gen Blender add-on as a parametric model whose parameters are predicted from language, with no prior work performing language-driven procedural tree generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AWOL is the first system to generate rigged, articulated 3D animals and 3D trees from text or images, producing triangular meshes compatible with standard graphics engines for animation and motion transfer—something NeRF- or SDF-based text-to-3D methods cannot provide. The approach works with extremely small training sets (3–10 examples per species), making it practical for underrepresented categories. Key limitations include the inability to generate geometric details outside the parametric model's shape space (e.g., no horns on a rhino), restriction to quadrupeds and trees, and the fact that the perceptual study shows AWOL still trails the image-based BITE method overall (though it wins when puppy images are excluded). Open questions include whether the CLIP-space smoothness assumption holds for more complex or less semantically structured parametric models, and whether the approach can be extended to other articulated categories like humans or birds.
