## Problem

Existing incremental learning methods assume a fixed type of increment across sequential tasks—either only new classes appear (CIL) or only new domains appear (DIL). In realistic deployments, however, the next task may introduce new classes, new domains, or both, with no prior signal about which. This paper introduces Versatile Incremental Learning (VIL) to formalize this class- and domain-agnostic setting and shows that existing CIL and DIL methods fail under it due to intra-class domain confusion, inter-domain class confusion, and severe classifier drift.

## Input / Output

The model receives a stream of RGB image classification tasks where each task may contain new classes, new domains, or both relative to all previously seen tasks. A frozen ViT backbone with trainable adapters processes each image, and the output is a class label. Unusually, the classifier head can have multiple output nodes per class (one per domain in which that class was learned); at inference, the node producing the maximum logit (minimum energy) is selected for the final prediction, and the number of nodes per class grows dynamically as new domains are encountered.

## Method

ICON combines two mechanisms on a frozen ViT with trainable adapters. First, CAST (Cluster-based Adaptation Shift Control) tracks the weight-shift vector of the adapters after each task, clusters all historical shifts via K-Means, and during training of the current task regularizes the instantaneous adapter shift to be orthogonal to shifts belonging to other clusters, thereby preventing the current learning direction from colliding with directions of dissimilar past tasks. Second, the Incremental Classifier (IC) uses a class-wise dynamic threshold (derived from the accuracy gap between the new domain and previously learned domains for that class) to decide whether to add a new output node for a class; for classes with multiple nodes, a Max-logit selection picks the most in-distribution node, and KL-divergence distillation from the previous task's logits is applied to unselected nodes to preserve prior knowledge. The total loss is a weighted sum of the CAST orthogonality term and the IC cross-entropy plus distillation term.

## Results

ICON achieves the best average accuracy across all three IL scenarios (CIL, DIL, VIL) on all three benchmarks. In the novel VIL setting, ICON reaches 75.11% average accuracy on iDigits (vs. 63.30% for the best baseline CODA-Prompt), 83.18% on CORe50 (vs. 77.11% for LAE), and 53.37% on DomainNet (vs. 49.45% for CODA-Prompt), while also achieving the lowest forgetting (9.13, 4.72, and 11.25 respectively). Overall average accuracy across all scenarios is 77.15% (iDigits), 84.34% (CORe50), and 59.74% (DomainNet), with notably lower standard deviations than competing methods, indicating more stable learning across random task orderings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICON outperforms all seven baselines (EWC, LwF, L2P, S-Prompts, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, LAE) on every metric across all three datasets and all three IL scenarios. The largest gains appear in the VIL setting: on iDigits, ICON improves average accuracy by 11.8 points over CODA-Prompt (75.11 vs. 63.30) and reduces forgetting from 16.43 to 9.13; on CORe50, it beats LAE by 6.1 points in VIL accuracy (83.18 vs. 77.11) and halves its forgetting (4.72 vs. 8.23). In the traditional CIL and DIL scenarios, ICON also leads, e.g., 84.83% DIL accuracy on iDigits vs. 79.09% for LAE, and 89.01% DIL on CORe50 vs. 86.50% for S-Prompts. Notably, ICON exhibits substantially lower standard deviations (e.g., ±0.51 on iDigits DIL vs. ±2.31 for S-Prompts), indicating more robust performance regardless of the random ordering of class and domain increments. The older regularization baselines (EWC, LwF) are far behind, confirming that prompt/adapter-based methods are necessary for competitive VIL performance, but ICON's CAST and IC components provide a clear advantage over the strongest prompt-based competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the two dominant incremental learning formulations—Class IL (CIL) and Domain IL (DIL)—into a unified Versatile Incremental Learning (VIL) scenario where the model has no prior knowledge of whether incoming tasks add new classes, new domains, or both. Methodologically, it builds on parameter-efficient fine-tuning with adapters (as in LAE, CODA-Prompt) and knowledge distillation (as in LwF), but departs from prior regularization approaches (EWC, SI, MAS) by introducing a shift-direction-based regularizer (CAST) that clusters historical parameter updates via K-Means and enforces orthogonality against dissimilar learning directions. The Incremental Classifier draws on energy-based model theory for node selection, extending the expandable-classifier line (DER, DyTox, ESN) with a class-wise dynamic threshold that handles the same-class-different-domain case those methods ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIL is a natural and arguably necessary generalization for real-world deployment (e.g., autonomous driving where both object categories and environmental conditions shift unpredictably), and the paper convincingly demonstrates that methods tuned for CIL or DIL degrade substantially when the increment type is unknown. However, the scenario still assumes a fixed number of classes and domains per task, and experiments are limited to three mid-scale benchmarks with a ViT backbone; scaling to larger datasets, variable task sizes, and non-ViT architectures remains open. The CAST mechanism's reliance on a shift pool that grows linearly with task count also raises practical concerns for very long task streams.
