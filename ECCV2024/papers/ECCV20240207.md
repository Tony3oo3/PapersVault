## Problem

Large vision-language models (LVLMs) suffer from knowledge bias inherited from training data, causing them to rely on memorized facts rather than genuinely reasoning about visual content, which leads to hallucinations and poor generalization to novel scenarios. Existing counterfactual VQA methods address this with simple image masking or low-level feature manipulation, but lack systematic knowledge-level editing and detailed reasoning process annotations needed to train models for bias-robust step-by-step reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a natural-language question, and a set of retrieved in-context example pairs (each containing an original and a counterfactual image-question-reasoning-answer tuple). The output is a final VQA answer accompanied by an explicit step-by-step reasoning process structured as a sequence of reasoning functions (e.g., Find(), Count(), Filter()) with their associated knowledge parameters (entities and relationships). In-context examples are selected by Needleman-Wunsch alignment on reasoning function sequences rather than by knowledge or question similarity, ensuring the model generalizes reasoning logic across diverse factual contexts.

## Method

CoCT comprises two components: (1) a CoBRa dataset built by constructing knowledge graphs from Wikipedia images via scene graph generation and knowledge retrieval, then applying 254 compositional graph-editing rules (e.g., replaceEntityClass, addRelationship) to generate counterfactual knowledge graphs, which guide diffusion-model inpainting validated by CLIP similarity; each pair carries annotated reasoning processes decomposed into functions and parameters. (2) A Translation Language Model (TLM) trained with LXMERT embeddings to predict reasoning processes, supervised by a novel bias-robust loss L_br that enforces similar reasoning functions across original/counterfactual pairs while allowing parameters to differ, combined with standard denoising auto-encoding and back-translation losses from unsupervised NMT. At inference, the TLM predicts the reasoning process for the test question, and the LVLM is prompted with both original and counterfactual in-context examples retrieved by reasoning-function similarity, explicitly separating bias-robust logical steps from bias-sensitive factual knowledge.

## Results

On the CoBRa benchmark, CoCT (with CoBRa examples) reduces the original-vs-counterfactual performance gap (Δ) from 44.2 to 38.1 on LLaVA-1.5 and from 41.5 to 37.5 on GPT-4V, while substantially improving counterfactual accuracy (18.9→38.3 and 36.7→41.8). On hallucination benchmarks, CoCT achieves the largest relative gains: Bingo improves from 12.5→29.8 (LLaVA-1.5) and 9.4→31.4 (GPT-4V), and HallusionBench from 29.0→32.0 and 37.3→41.2 respectively. On comprehensive benchmarks, CoCT also outperforms the base models, reaching 71.3 on ScienceQA and 68.9 on MMBench with LLaVA-1.5, and 75.8 and 78.5 with GPT-4V. CoCT also achieves the fastest inference time (39 ms) among all compared CoT methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCT (CoBRa) outperforms all three CoT baselines (AutoCoT, AP, DDCoT) on every bias-evaluation benchmark for both LLaVA-1.5 and GPT-4V. The most dramatic win is on Bingo, where CoCT (CoBRa) scores 29.8 vs. 23.4 (DDCoT) and 18.8 (AutoCoT/AP) on LLaVA-1.5, and 31.4 vs. 23.4 (DDCoT) and 12.5 (AP) on GPT-4V. On HallusionBench, CoCT leads with 32.0 (LLaVA) and 41.2 (GPT-4V), exceeding DDCoT by 1.2 and 3.5 points respectively. The one exception is ScienceQA, where DDCoT achieves higher scores (74.6 on LLaVA-1.5, 80.2 on GPT-4V) than CoCT (71.3 and 75.8), which the authors attribute to DDCoT's fine-tuning on a large language model exacerbating bias on other tasks. On MMBench, CoCT (CoBRa) matches or exceeds all baselines (68.9 for LLaVA-1.5, 78.5 for GPT-4V), confirming that bias-robust reasoning does not sacrifice general capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the counterfactual VQA line of work (CSS, MUTANT) by replacing simple image masking with systematic knowledge graph editing rules, and departs from standard chain-of-thought methods by explicitly decoupling reasoning functions from knowledge parameters rather than treating rationales as monolithic sequences. It repurposes the translation language model (TLM) architecture from unsupervised NMT to generate bias-robust reasoning processes, and adapts the Needleman-Wunsch sequence alignment algorithm for selecting in-context examples based on reasoning similarity rather than surface-level knowledge similarity. The approach builds on LVLMs (GPT-4V, LLaVA-1.5) and their known susceptibility to knowledge bias and hallucination, positioning counterfactual thinking as a structured mitigation strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CoBRa dataset is the only counterfactual VQA benchmark with explicitly annotated knowledge graphs and step-by-step reasoning processes, filling a gap where prior bias-evaluation datasets (VQA-CP, GQA-OOD, HallusionBench) lack such fine-grained reasoning annotations. The conceptual separation of reasoning functions from parameters offers a clean framework for understanding how LVLMs can generalize across factual shifts, though the approach adds a TLM training stage and diffusion-model-based image generation that may limit scalability. Open questions include whether the knowledge graph editing rules capture the full diversity of real-world bias, whether the method extends to multi-step compositional reasoning beyond single-image VQA, and whether the counterfactual images generated by diffusion models introduce artifacts that confound evaluation.
