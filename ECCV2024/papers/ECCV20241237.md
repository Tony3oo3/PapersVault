## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods rely on RGB-D scans of static indoor scenes with a ~5 m sensing range, making them unsuitable for large-scale dynamic outdoor environments where service robots and autonomous vehicles operate. No prior work addresses 3D visual grounding in the wild using online-captured multi-sensor data (LiDAR + camera) with temporal dynamics and free-form language descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241237/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two consecutive frames (1 s apart) of synchronized 128-beam LiDAR point clouds and 1920×1200 RGB images from a calibrated sensor rig, along with a single free-form natural language utterance. It outputs a single 3D bounding box parameterized as (x, y, z, l, w, h, θ) in the LiDAR coordinate frame. Because the LiDAR point cloud is unordered and sparse with no strict one-to-one correspondence to image pixels, the fusion must be learned rather than geometrically aligned, and the spatial relations in descriptions are defined relative to the sensor view rather than a global scene frame.

## Method

WildRefer is a one-stage DETR-like pipeline with three core modules. A Dynamic Visual Encoder (DVE) applies spatial-temporal attention—self-attention on the current frame followed by cross-attention with the previous frame—to extract motion features from both point clouds and images, automatically tracking the same object across frames in large-scale scenes. A Triple-modal Feature Interaction (TFI) module then performs ordered cross-attention: point features first interact bidirectionally with language features to produce semantic-enhanced point and visual-enhanced text features, and the enhanced point features subsequently cross-attend with dense image features, preserving 3D positional accuracy for localization. A DETR-form decoder with non-parametric confidence-ranked queries decodes box parameters and a word-level probability map to select the target. Training is supervised by five losses (focal, GIoU, L1, contrastive visual-word alignment, and soft token prediction) with PointNet++, ResNet-34, and RoBERTa as pretrained backbones projected to 288-D.

## Results

On STRefer, WildRefer achieves 62.01% Acc@0.25, 54.97% Acc@0.5, and 38.77 mIOU, outperforming the best two-stage baseline BUTD-DETR (57.60% Acc@0.25) by over 4 points. On the more challenging LifeRefer dataset, WildRefer reaches 38.89% Acc@0.25, 18.42% Acc@0.5, and 19.47 mIOU, surpassing the strongest one-stage competitor BUTD-DETR* (32.46% Acc@0.25) by 6.4 points. Inference time is 151 ms, comparable to other one-stage methods (130–154 ms) and significantly faster than two-stage approaches (156–291 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildRefer outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across both datasets. On STRefer, the closest competitor is BUTD-DETR (two-stage) at 57.60% Acc@0.25, which WildRefer surpasses by 4.4 points; among one-stage methods, EDA* (57.41%) is the nearest rival, trailing by 4.6 points. On LifeRefer, the gap widens: WildRefer leads BUTD-DETR* (32.46%) by 6.4 points in Acc@0.25 and EDA (31.44%) by 7.5 points, while also achieving the highest mIOU (19.47 vs. 15.96 for BUTD-DETR*). Notably, two-stage methods that rely on a pretrained 3D detector degrade significantly on LifeRefer due to low detection quality (77.11% R@0.25 vs. 90.77% on STRefer), whereas WildRefer's one-stage design avoids this bottleneck. Inference speed is competitive at 151 ms, slightly slower than 3D-SPS (130 ms) and BUTD-DETR* (138 ms) but far faster than EDA (291 ms) and MVT (242 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D visual grounding from the established static indoor RGB-D setting (ScanRefer, Nr3D, Sr3D) to large-scale dynamic outdoor scenes captured by calibrated LiDAR-camera pairs. Methodologically, it builds on the one-stage DETR framework of BUTD-DETR (Jain et al., ECCV 2022) and adds two novel modules: a Dynamic Visual Encoder for temporal feature tracking across frames, and a Triple-modal Feature Interaction module that cross-attends point-cloud, image, and language features. The fusion strategy draws on transformer-based LiDAR-camera fusion literature (TransFusion, DeepFusion) but repurposes it for vision-language grounding rather than detection alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two introduced datasets (STRefer, LifeRefer) fill a genuine gap: no prior 3D visual grounding benchmark covers dynamic, human-centric, outdoor scenes with multi-sensor data, which is the actual operating regime for service robots and autonomous vehicles. The finding that one-stage grounding outperforms two-stage pipelines in complex outdoor settings (where 3D detection quality degrades significantly) is practically useful for system design. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond human targets, whether longer temporal windows or more sophisticated motion modeling would help, and how the method performs under real-time latency constraints on embedded hardware.
