## Problem

Existing identity-preserved personalization methods entangle identity (image) and semantic (text) information within a shared representation space, creating an inherent trade-off: PhotoMaker-style methods compress image features into text embeddings, weakening ID fidelity, while IP-Adapter-style methods inject identity directly into the U-Net, distorting the semantic space and compromising text-prompt consistency. No prior approach simultaneously achieves high identity fidelity and strong semantic control from a single reference image.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image (which is first aligned via a face alignment module) and a free-form text prompt as input. The reference image is processed through two parallel extractors — an OpenCLIP ViT-H/14 image encoder (yielding 257 local embeddings) and an ArcFace face-recognition backbone (yielding a 512-d global embedding) — while the text prompt is encoded by SDXL's dual text encoders. The output is a full-resolution personalized image (decoded from SDXL's latent space) that preserves the reference person's identity while following the scene, action, and style described in the prompt.

## Method

Infinite-ID introduces an ID-semantics decoupling paradigm with two key mechanisms. During training (identity-enhanced training), the original text cross-attention modules in the U-Net are deactivated, and an additional trainable image cross-attention module is trained exclusively on identity embeddings (concatenated CLIP and ArcFace features), so the image stream learns to faithfully encode identity without textual interference. During inference, a mixed attention mechanism replaces the original self-attention by concatenating identity K/V features with text K/V features, allowing the query to attend jointly to both streams; a separate cross-attention merging step adds the text cross-attention output to the image cross-attention output for further semantic refinement. An AdaIN-mean operation (shifting the mean of identity K/V to match text K/V) is applied in both the mixed attention and cross-attention paths to enable precise style control while preserving identity fidelity. Only the face mapper, CLIP mapper, and image cross-attention parameters are trained; the pre-trained SDXL backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the raw photo generation benchmark, Infinite-ID achieves a M_FaceNet score of 0.689, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: FastComposer at 0.556). Semantic consistency (CLIP-T) is competitive at 0.340, nearly matching PhotoMaker's 0.343, while image-image similarity (CLIP-I) reaches 0.913, close to IP-Adapter-Face's 0.919. The method thus achieves the best identity fidelity by a wide margin while maintaining strong text-prompt alignment, resolving the trade-off that limits prior approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Infinite-ID dominates all four baselines on identity fidelity (M_FaceNet), achieving 0.689 versus 0.556 (FastComposer), 0.474 (IP-Adapter), 0.513 (IP-Adapter-Face), and 0.502 (PhotoMaker) — a 24–46% relative improvement over the strongest competitor. On semantic consistency (CLIP-T), Infinite-ID (0.340) is within 0.003 of PhotoMaker (0.343) and clearly ahead of IP-Adapter-Face (0.313), FastComposer (0.292), and IP-Adapter (0.274). On image-image similarity (CLIP-I), IP-Adapter-Face edges out Infinite-ID by 0.006 (0.919 vs. 0.913), while PhotoMaker trails significantly (0.814). The key takeaway is that prior methods force a trade-off: PhotoMaker preserves text semantics but sacrifices identity, while IP-Adapter variants boost identity at the cost of semantic drift; Infinite-ID's ID-semantics decoupling paradigm avoids this trade-off, delivering near-best scores on all three metrics simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion XL as its base diffusion architecture and adopts IP-Adapter's pattern of appending image cross-attention modules to the U-Net, but departs conceptually from both major prior approaches to identity-preserved personalization. PhotoMaker compresses identity into the text embedding space (sacrificing ID fidelity for semantic consistency), while IP-Adapter injects identity directly into the U-Net (sacrificing semantic consistency for ID fidelity). Infinite-ID's key departure is the ID-semantics decoupling paradigm: training with text cross-attention deactivated so the image stream learns identity in isolation, then merging the two streams at inference via a mixed attention mechanism and AdaIN-mean style alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling paradigm offers a clean conceptual resolution to the long-standing ID-fidelity-vs-semantic-consistency trade-off in identity-preserved personalization, with practical implications for personalized AI portraits, virtual try-on, and content creation at scale. However, the method lacks multi-object personalization, produces artifacts when the face occupies a small image region, and the choice of image encoders (CLIP + ArcFace) remains an open design question. The AdaIN-mean operation for style control is a lightweight but effective addition that could be adopted more broadly in diffusion-based generation pipelines.
