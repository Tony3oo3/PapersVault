## Problem

Existing evaluation frameworks for object detector calibration (D-ECE-style, LaECE-style, CE-style) use inconsistent operating thresholds, ignore localization quality in confidence scores, rely on domain-shifted validation sets for post-hoc calibrators, and compare against only Temperature Scaling as a post-hoc baseline. These flaws lead to misleading conclusions about which calibration methods are effective, and post-hoc calibration for object detection remains largely unexplored despite its practical advantages.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained object detector (any architecture: one-stage, two-stage, DETR-like, or open-vocabulary) and a held-out in-distribution validation set with ground-truth annotations. It produces the same detection set (bounding boxes, class labels) but with recalibrated confidence scores that align with per-detection IoU rather than binary TP/FP status. Calibration is performed class-specifically with cross-validated thresholds: a calibration threshold u^c filters detections before calibration, and an operating threshold v^c (selected via LRP) filters after calibration, ensuring the output matches practical deployment conditions.

## Method

The paper introduces two post-hoc calibrators tailored to object detection: Platt Scaling (PS), which fits a scale and bias to logits using IoU as the regression target under a Bernoulli NLL objective, and Isotonic Regression (IR), which directly fits a monotonic piecewise-linear mapping from predicted confidence to IoU. Both are trained on class-specific subsets of an in-distribution validation set, with detection thresholds cross-validated using LRP to ensure proper joint accuracy-calibration evaluation. The paper also proposes LaECE_0 and LaACE_0, calibration metrics that enforce confidence to match per-detection IoU (using τ=0) rather than precision or precision×IoU, providing finer-grained and unambiguous confidence semantics. A key finding is that these simple post-hoc calibrators, when designed with an ID validation set and proper thresholding, outperform all training-time calibration methods (e.g., Cal-DETR) by 7+ D-ECE and 4+ LaECE_0 on COCO, while preserving accuracy.

## Results

On COCO minitest using the proposed evaluation (LaECE₀, LaACE₀, LRP), Isotonic Regression (IR) achieves 7.7 LaECE₀ and 23.1 LaACE₀ while preserving D-DETR's accuracy (57.2 LRP), outperforming the SOTA training-time method Cal-DETR (11.6 LaECE₀) by 3.9 points. On Cityscapes minitest, IR achieves 9.0 LaECE₀ vs. Cal-DETR's 21.3, a gap of 12.3 points. Under the conventional D-ECE-style evaluation, IR for D-ECE reaches 1.3 D-ECE on COCO minitest, improving over Cal-DETR (8.7) by 7.4 points, while Platt Scaling for D-ECE reaches 0.9 D-ECE. Across 14 diverse detectors (one-stage, two-stage, DETR-like, open-vocabulary, SOTA), IR consistently reduces LaECE₀ by 4–27 points over uncalibrated baselines while maintaining LRP accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA training-time method Cal-DETR [43], IR achieves 7.7 LaECE₀ on COCO minitest vs. 11.6 (−3.9) and 9.0 on Cityscapes vs. 21.3 (−12.3), while also improving LRP accuracy (57.2 vs. 56.2 on COCO). IR outperforms all other training-time baselines—BPC [41] (12.4 LaECE₀), TCD [42] (13.0), MDCA [19] (13.1), and MbLS [34] (16.5)—by 4.7–8.8 LaECE₀ on COCO. Compared to the standard post-hoc baseline Temperature Scaling [15], Platt Scaling improves LaECE₀ by ~3 points on COCO and 11.4 on Cityscapes, primarily due to the added bias term and use of an in-distribution validation set rather than a domain-shifted one. Against the uncalibrated D-DETR [71] baseline, IR reduces LaECE₀ from 12.7 to 7.7 on COCO and from 20.3 to 9.0 on Cityscapes while preserving LRP accuracy. The one notable loss is on Obj45K (natural domain shift), where IR's LaECE₀ (17.2) is slightly worse than Cal-DETR's (18.8 is actually worse, so IR still wins) but PS and IR both show a small degradation relative to ID, an expected limitation of post-hoc methods trained only on in-distribution data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant training-time calibration paradigm (Cal-DETR, BPC, TCD, MDCA, MbLS) that has defined the field since 2020, by repositioning post-hoc calibration as the primary contribution rather than a weak baseline. It builds directly on LaECE from Oksuz et al. (CVPR 2023) to derive LaECE_0 and LaACE_0, and adapts classical Platt Scaling and Isotonic Regression—long-established in classification calibration—to the detection setting with IoU-based targets. Conceptually, it also challenges the D-E-style evaluation framework (fixed 0.30 threshold, AP for accuracy, domain-shifted validation sets) that all prior SOTA calibration papers adopt, arguing it produces misleading rankings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a two-parameter Platt Scaling or a piecewise-linear Isotonic Regression fitted on an ID validation set outperforms every published training-time calibrator by 4–8 points in LaECE_0 across COCO, Cityscapes, and LVIS, while preserving accuracy. This reframes the field's SOTA narrative and provides a cheap, detector-agnostic baseline that any practitioner can apply to an off-the-shelf model in seconds. Open questions include whether post-hoc methods degrade under large natural domain shifts (the Obj45K results hint at this), whether the LaECE_0/LaACE_0/LRP framework transfers cleanly to instance segmentation, and whether training-time methods can recover their advantage once the evaluation protocol is fixed.
