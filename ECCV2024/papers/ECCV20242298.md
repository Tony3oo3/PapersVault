## Problem

General-purpose large vision-language models (e.g., Qwen-VL, LLaVA) perform poorly on crop disease diagnosis, failing to correctly identify crop types, diagnose diseases, or provide actionable prevention and treatment advice. Moreover, the standard finetuning paradigm of freezing the visual encoder is ill-suited to this domain because visually similar crop diseases require fine-grained visual discrimination that a frozen, general-domain encoder cannot provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242298/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB crop disease image together with a free-form text question (average 6.11 words for diagnosis, 9.69 words for knowledge queries) and produces a multi-turn conversational response that identifies the crop category, diagnoses the disease, and elaborates on causes, symptoms, and management strategies (average answer lengths of 8.92 words for diagnosis and 130.41 words for knowledge). Evaluation for diagnosis uses keyword matching on crop and disease labels over a 3,000-image held-out test set, while knowledge QA is scored by GPT-4 on a 1–10 scale across helpfulness, relevance, and accuracy, normalized to a 100-point scale.

## Method

The paper introduces the CDDM dataset (137,000 images across 16 crop and 60 disease categories, paired with ~1 million GPT-4-generated QA conversations that deliberately include negative-response examples to counter LVLMs' affirmative bias) and a novel LoRA-based finetuning strategy. Unlike LLaVA and Qwen-VL-Chat, which freeze the visual encoder during instruction tuning, the proposed method applies low-rank adaptation simultaneously to all three components of Qwen-VL-Chat—the visual encoder, the position-aware vision-language adapter, and the language model—so that the encoder can learn to capture the subtle local patterns distinguishing visually similar diseases. The same strategy is applied to LLaVA-v1.5-7B for comparison, and experiments show that unfreezing the visual encoder yields substantial gains (e.g., disease classification rising from 66.1% to 91.5% for Qwen-VL-Chat) over the frozen-encoder baseline.

## Results

On the CDDM test set (3,000 images), the proposed LoRA finetuning strategy without freezing the visual encoder achieves 97.4% crop classification and 91.5% disease classification for Qwen-VL-Chat-AG, and 98.0% and 91.8% respectively for LLaVA-AG. For crop disease knowledge VQA (GPT-4-scored, 100-point scale), LLaVA-AG* (frozen encoder) reaches 98 and LLaVA-AG reaches 96.5, while Qwen-VL-Chat-AG* and Qwen-VL-Chat-AG score 88.5 and 84 respectively. The unmodified baselines (Qwen-VL-Chat and LLaVA-v1.5-7b) score only 28.4%/5.0% and 24.5%/5.9% on crop/disease classification, and 41/47.5 on knowledge QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two unmodified LVLM baselines: Qwen-VL-Chat and LLaVA-v1.5-7b. Both baselines perform very poorly on crop disease diagnosis, with disease classification accuracy of only 5.0% and 5.9% respectively, indicating they cannot reliably identify specific crop diseases without domain-specific training. After LoRA finetuning on CDDM without freezing the visual encoder, Qwen-VL-Chat-AG improves disease classification from 5.0% to 91.5% (+86.5 pp) and crop classification from 28.4% to 97.4% (+69 pp); LLaVA-AG improves from 5.9% to 91.8% (+85.9 pp) and 24.5% to 98.0% (+73.5 pp). On knowledge QA, LLaVA-AG (96.5) and LLaVA-AG* (98) both substantially outperform the LLaVA baseline (47.5), while Qwen-VL-Chat-AG (84) and Qwen-VL-Chat-AG* (88.5) outperform the Qwen-VL-Chat baseline (41). Notably, freezing the visual encoder (AG*) yields a slight edge on knowledge QA for LLaVA (98 vs 96.5) but a large drop on disease classification (82.1% vs 91.8%), confirming that the visual encoder's ability to distinguish visually similar diseases is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the general-purpose LVLM paradigm (Qwen-VL-Chat, LLaVA) into the crop disease domain by constructing a large-scale multimodal instruction-following dataset and adapting the standard finetuning recipe. It departs from the prevailing strategy of freezing the visual encoder during LVLM finetuning, instead applying LoRA simultaneously to the visual encoder, adapter, and language model to better capture fine-grained visual distinctions between visually similar diseases. Prior agricultural VQA work (e.g., Lan et al.'s co-attention model) was limited in scope and architectural capacity, while traditional crop disease diagnosis relied on single-modal classification or detection without conversational knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CDDM dataset (137K images, 1M QA pairs) and the accompanying benchmark provide a much-needed resource for developing domain-specific agricultural chatbots, filling a gap where general LVLMs perform poorly (e.g., 5% disease classification accuracy for unmodified Qwen-VL-Chat). The finding that unfreezing the visual encoder yields a 13–25 percentage point improvement over the frozen-encoder baseline is practically important for any domain where visual discrimination is subtle. However, the authors acknowledge that fine-tuned models struggle with out-of-domain diseases, and they suggest in-context learning as a potential remedy, leaving this as an open question for future work.
