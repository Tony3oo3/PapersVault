## Problem

Existing occluded person ReID methods overlook Multi-Person Ambiguity (MPA): when a bounding box contains multiple individuals, the model cannot determine which person is the intended retrieval target, leading to feature mixing or focus on the wrong person. No prior method explicitly disambiguates the target during inference, and no standard ReID dataset provides pixel-level target identification for such multi-person scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 128×256 or 128×384) plus optional positive and negative semantic keypoints, each organized into K+1 groups (K body parts + one negative group). Keypoints are rendered as Gaussian heatmaps (σ = 0.1 × image width) on a (K+1)-channel spatial tensor, tokenized at the same 4×4 patch resolution as the image, and summed with image tokens before encoding. The output is K part-based embeddings (one per body part, e.g., head, torso, arms, legs, feet) paired with binary visibility scores; invisible parts are excluded from the cosine-distance-based similarity computation at retrieval time.

## Method

KPR is a Swin Transformer backbone augmented with a Multi-Stage Feature Fusion (MSF) module that upsamples and concatenates all stage outputs to preserve high-resolution spatial tokens. The key architectural insight is prompt-aware appearance encoding: keypoint heatmaps are tokenized and summed with image tokens *before* the encoder, so the network conditions feature extraction on the prompt from the earliest layers, enabling disentanglement of target and occluder features. A Part-based Head applies a token-wise classifier (K+1 classes) to assign each spatial token to a body part or background, then averages same-part tokens into K embeddings with visibility scores. Training combines a GiLt contrastive loss (identity + batch-hard triplet over part distances) with a cross-entropy Part Prediction loss. A critical component is the BIPO data augmentation, which overlays a second person from the same training batch onto the image to synthesize multi-person occlusions, compelling the model to rely on the keypoint prompt as the sole reliable target signal.

## Results

On the novel Occluded PoseTrack-ReID dataset (featuring strong multi-person occlusions), KPR with the ImageNet-pretrained Swin backbone achieves 92.31% Rank-1 and 82.31% mAP, outperforming the best prior method (SOLIDER, 84.4% R-1) by ~8 points. On Partial-ReID, KPR with the SOLIDER backbone reaches 90.71% R-1 and 94.01% R-3, setting a new state of the art. On Occluded-ReID, KPR_SOL achieves the best mAP (82.61%), while on Market-1501 it is competitive with SOLIDER (96.62% vs. 96.91% R-1). The keypoint prompt alone boosts performance by at least 7.0% R-1 and 6.2% mAP on the multi-person occlusion benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the novel Occluded PoseTrack-ReID benchmark, KPR_IN (92.31% R-1) surpasses all 13 prior methods by a wide margin: SOLIDER (84.4%), BPBreID (84.9%), TRANS (83.5%), and PCB (81.7%), with the gap over the strongest baseline (SOLIDER) reaching ~8 R-1 points. On Partial-ReID, KPR_SOL sets a new state of the art at 90.71% R-1 / 94.01% R-3, beating PAT (88.03/92.3), HOReID (85.3/91.0), and VGTri (85.7/93.73). On Occluded-ReID, results are more nuanced: FED leads on R-1 (86.31 vs. 85.42 for KPR_IN), but KPR_SOL achieves the best mAP (82.61 vs. PFD's 81.52 and FED's 79.33). On Market-1501, SOLIDER retains a slight edge (96.91 vs. 96.62 R-1), while KPR_SOL is within 0.7 mAP points. For multi-person pose tracking on PoseTrack21, KPRTrack achieves 63.0 HOTA, outperforming Tracktor++ (58.3) and CorrTrack+ReID (56.9) by 4.7 and 6.1 points respectively, with the keypoint prompt contributing +1.5 HOTA and reducing identity switches by 486.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own BPBreID framework (part-based embeddings with visibility scores, GiLt loss, human parsing labels) by introducing an explicit keypoint-prompting mechanism inspired by SAM's promptable segmentation paradigm. It departs from prior pose-guided ReID methods (HOReID, PFD, PVPM) in a fundamental way: rather than using pose information only during training or after appearance encoding, KPR conditions the feature extraction itself on input keypoints, enabling prompt-aware disentanglement of overlapping persons. The paper also introduces a BIPO data augmentation that forces the model to actually rely on prompts during training, addressing a gap where prior methods assumed single-person bounding boxes and never confronted Multi-Person Ambiguity at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Occ-PTrack and keypoint annotations for four standard ReID benchmarks fills a clear gap: no prior dataset explicitly identifies the ReID target in multi-person occlusion scenarios, making it impossible to evaluate disambiguation ability. The prompt-optional design and the BIPO augmentation offer practical value for sports tracking and surveillance where overlapping athletes are common. Open questions include whether segmentation masks (which the architecture supports in principle) would outperform keypoints, whether the pseudo-labeled keypoints for standard datasets introduce systematic bias, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively narrow sports/surveillance domains tested. The SOLIDER domain-gap failure on Occ-PTrack also raises questions about the transferability of human-centric foundation models to non-street settings.
