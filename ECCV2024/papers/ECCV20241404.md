## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models face a trilemma: fine-tuning-based approaches (ESD, CA, SA) consume thousands of training steps and degrade generation quality, while the closed-form UCE method is fast but leaves incomplete erasure that red-teaming tools can exploit to regenerate inappropriate content. No prior method simultaneously achieves thorough erasure, minimal parameter modification, and preservation of unrelated generation ability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241404/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net (specifically its cross-attention K/V projection matrices), a set of text concepts to erase (encoded as CLIP text embeddings), and a set of concepts to preserve. It outputs a modified U-Net in which only the K/V projection matrices are altered (2.23% of total parameters), with the entire edit completing in approximately 3 seconds on a single RTX 3090. The derived adversarial embeddings are used as direct text-conditioning vectors in place of CLIP-encoded prompts during the iterative erasure loop.

## Method

RECE builds on UCE's closed-form weight editing and introduces an iterative derive-erase loop. In each epoch, it first derives a new embedding c′ via a closed-form least-squares solution (Eq. 8) that, when projected through the edited cross-attention matrices, reproduces the original concept's output in the unlearned model—effectively finding the residual representation of the erased concept. A regularization term λ‖c′‖² is added to keep c′ near zero, which (by Theorem 1) minimizes perturbation to unrelated concepts. The derived embedding is then erased using the same UCE closed-form formula, and the loop repeats for T epochs (5 for unsafe content, 10 for artistic styles). All operations—embedding derivation, weight editing, and regularization—are closed-form, eliminating any gradient-based fine-tuning and enabling the entire multi-epoch process to complete in seconds.

## Results

On the I2P dataset (4,703 prompts), RECE detects only 66 nude body parts in total, the lowest among all methods (vs. 646 for SD v1.4, 186 for UCE, and 95 for CA). On COCO-30k, RECE achieves a CLIP score of 30.95 and FID of 2.82, closely matching UCE (FID 1.82) and SLD-Med (FID 2.60) while erasing far more nudity. Against red-teaming attacks, RECE achieves the lowest average attack success rate (47.89%) and the best black-box robustness (13.38% ASR under Ring-A-Bell). The entire erasure process completes in 3.4 seconds, modifying only 2.23% of U-Net parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECE outperforms all baselines on total nude body part detection (66 vs. 95 for CA, 121 for ESD-u, 163 for SA, 186 for UCE, 354 for SLD-Med, and 485 for SD v2.1). CA achieves the second-lowest count (95) but at a much higher FID cost (7.87 vs. RECE's 2.82), indicating greater damage to general generation quality. UCE achieves the best FID (1.82) but leaves 186 nude parts, nearly 3× more than RECE. On robustness, RECE achieves the lowest average attack success rate (47.89%) and the best black-box defense (13.38% under Ring-A-Bell vs. 25.35% for CA and 33.10% for UCE); CA is slightly better on white-box attacks (60.56% P4D vs. 64.79% for RECE). In terms of efficiency, RECE (3.4 s, 2.23% parameters) is comparable to UCE (1.2 s, 2.23%) but dramatically faster than ESD (3,720 s, 94.65%) and CA (1,400 s, 100%). For artistic style removal, RECE achieves the best overall LPIPS_d (0.23 for Van Gogh, 0.25 for Kelly McKernan), surpassing UCE (0.20, 0.22) and ESD (0.14, 0.16).

## Relation to Prior Work

RECE builds directly on UCE's closed-form editing of cross-attention K/V projection matrices, which itself departs from the fine-tuning-based erasure methods (ESD, CA, SA) that require thousands of optimization steps. The key conceptual departure is an iterative adversarial loop: after each closed-form erasure, RECE derives new embeddings (via a closed-form least-squares solution) that would regenerate the erased concept in the edited model, then erases those derived embeddings in the next epoch. This adversarial-derivation idea is borrowed from adversarial fine-tuning but adapted to the closed-form setting, and a regularization term (penalizing the norm of derived embeddings) is introduced to limit collateral damage to unrelated concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-second erasure time (vs. hours for fine-tuning methods) makes concept removal practically deployable for open-source T2I models, and the iterative adversarial scheme addresses the well-documented incompleteness of single-pass erasure. However, the paper reveals a persistent gender bias: the 'nudity' concept in SD is overwhelmingly female-coded, and no erasure method (including RECE) adequately removes male nudity, suggesting that concept-level erasure inherits and cannot fix dataset-level biases. Open questions include whether the closed-form iterative scheme generalizes to compositional or multi-concept erasure, and whether the 2.23% parameter modification is sufficient for more semantically entangled concepts.
