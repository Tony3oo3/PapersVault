## Problem

Prior urban layout generation methods—whether procedural, pixel-based, or graph-based—treat individual city blocks in isolation, ignoring the context-sensitive multi-layer semantics (building → block → community → city) that govern realistic urban form. This leads to generated layouts that are either over-diverse or over-similar among neighboring blocks, failing to capture the harmonization patterns observed in real cities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a road-network-derived graph where nodes are city blocks (each carrying 4 shape/location features: aspect ratio, area, convex-hull ratio, and distance to city centroid) and edges encode centroid-to-centroid distances. It optionally accepts any percentage of pre-existing building layout priors (0–100%) for completion tasks. Output is a vector-based 2.5D layout: polygonal building footprints with heights for every block, produced as quantized categorical indices decoded through the BVAE rather than as pixel grids.

## Method

The core contribution is a Graph-based Masked Autoencoder (GMAE) operating on a canonical city graph. A block-level GAT variational autoencoder (BVAE) first encodes each block's building layout into a 512-dim latent vector, which is then per-dimension quantized into L=20 bins to form a compact categorical codebook. During self-supervised training, building layout features are masked at a dynamic ratio sampled from a truncated Gaussian N(0.55, 0.25) clipped to [0.5, 1.0], while shape features and edges remain visible; a 3-hop GAT encoder performs context-aware message passing and an MLP decoder reconstructs the masked quantized indices via cross-entropy loss. At inference, a priority-based cosine schedule β(t)=1−cos(t/T) over T=12 iterations progressively accepts the highest-confidence predicted nodes, ensuring distinctive or representative blocks are generated first to guide stylistic consistency across the community.

## Results

On the COHO test set (100 communities, 3,700 city blocks), COHO achieves the best scores on 7 out of 8 metrics: FID 23.63 (vs. 49.55 for the next-best GlobalMapper), KID 0.005 (vs. 0.024), LPIPS 0.20 (vs. 0.32), WD-5D 2.28 (vs. 2.92), WD-CO 1.91 (vs. 4.14), O-Blk 0.42% (vs. 0.68%), and CTS 0.21 (closest to zero, indicating the most contextually realistic generation). The only metric where COHO is not best is Overlap (1.27% vs. VTN's 1.24%), a near-tie. SDXL, evaluated only on pixel-based perceptual metrics, trails substantially (FID 120.24, KID 0.079, LPIPS 0.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

COHO outperforms all four baselines on 7 of 8 metrics, with the sole exception being the Overlap metric where VTN edges ahead by 0.03 percentage points (1.24% vs. 1.27%). Against the closest urban-layout competitor GlobalMapper [20], COHO nearly halves the FID (23.63 vs. 49.55), reduces KID by a factor of ~5 (0.005 vs. 0.024), and achieves a CTS of 0.21 versus 0.62, indicating far more contextually faithful community generation. VTN [2] and LayoutDM [29] both exhibit severe context failures: VTN shows over-diversity (CTS = −1.14) and LayoutDM shows extreme over-diversity (CTS = −2.20) with the worst WD-CO (12.50), reflecting style shifts between neighboring blocks. SDXL [48] is only comparable on pixel-level perceptual metrics (FID, KID, LPIPS) since it cannot ingest a road-network graph, and it trails COHO by 5× on FID and 16× on KID. Overall, COHO's graph-based context modeling yields the most realistic and geometrically correct city-scale layouts across all evaluated dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE, MaskGIT, MAGE) from image patches to city-scale graph structures, combining a block-level variational autoencoder (BVAE) with a city-level graph masked autoencoder (GMAE) in a hierarchical fashion. It departs from prior urban layout generators (BlockPlanner, GlobalMapper, BuildingGAN) that treat each city block independently by introducing a canonical graph representation of the entire city where node features are reconstructed with awareness of neighboring blocks and communities. The priority-based cosine scheduling for iterative generation is adapted from MaskGIT/MAGE but applied to the novel problem of prioritizing representative city blocks before filling in the rest.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The open dataset of 330 US cities (833K blocks, 17.6M buildings) provides a much-needed benchmark for city-scale urban layout generation, and the context-sensitivity framing addresses a genuine gap where prior methods produced either over-diverse or over-similar neighboring blocks. Limitations include difficulty with complex polygon shapes (e.g., buildings with internal gardens), concave blocks (cul-de-sacs), and the absence of explicit road-network-to-context modeling. Future directions toward photo-realistic multi-view synthesis and 3D city modeling could enable synthetic data generation for autonomous driving and world-model training.
