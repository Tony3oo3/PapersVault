## Problem

Real-world aerial scene understanding is limited by the absence of densely annotated datasets captured under controlled, reproducible variations in weather, time of day, altitude, and pitch. Densely annotating a single 4K UAV image can take up to 2 hours, and existing synthetic aerial datasets lack the ability to reproduce the exact viewpoint under differing conditions, limiting their utility for diagnosing model sensitivity to single-variable changes. The synthetic-to-real generalization gap in aerial imagery reaches up to 50%, far exceeding the ~20% gap in ground-view settings, indicating that current syn-to-real methods do not transfer effectively to the aerial domain.

## Input / Output

SKYSCENES provides 2160×1440 RGB aerial images captured from UAV perspectives at three altitudes (15m, 35m, 60m) and four pitch angles (0°, 45°, 60°, 90°) across 8 map layouts and 5 weather/daytime conditions, each paired with dense semantic (28 classes), instance segmentation, and depth annotations. The dataset also stores full scene metadata (camera world coordinates, orientation, all actor positions and orientations) enabling exact viewpoint reproduction under altered conditions. For multi-modal experiments, depth images are consumed alongside RGB by the M3L architecture to produce joint semantic segmentation masks.

## Method

SKYSCENES is curated from the CARLA 0.9.14 simulator by positioning the ego vehicle (equipped with RGB, semantic, and depth sensors) at specified altitude and pitch to generate aerial views, then procedurally traversing the scene with fixed-distance camera translations to avoid correlated frames. A key algorithmic contribution is HumanSpawn(), which explicitly spawns 40–200 additional human instances per snapshot to counter the severe underrepresentation of pedestrians in aerial views, improving human pixel proportion by ~10× and boosting human-class mIoU from 43.03 to 61.79 in-distribution. Realistic imperfections are introduced via Gaussian jitter on height (Δh ~ N(1, 2.5m)) and pitch (Δθ ~ N(1, 5°)), and comprehensive per-frame metadata with consistency checks enables deterministic regeneration of any viewpoint under different weather, daytime, or sensor conditions. The resulting 33,600 images (70 per town × 8 towns × 5 conditions × 12 height/pitch combos) are evaluated using DeepLabv2 (R-101), DAFormer (MiT-B5), and Rein (DINOv2 + LoRA) for semantic segmentation, demonstrating superior syn-to-real transfer over SynDrone and utility as a diagnostic test-bed for model sensitivity to individual condition changes.

## Results

Models trained on SKYSCENES achieve strong zero-shot transfer to three real-world aerial datasets. With DAFormer (MiT-B5), SKYSCENES training yields 47.09 mIoU on UAVid, 40.72 on AEROSCAPES, and 25.91 on ICG DRONE, outperforming SYNDRONE training (42.31, 30.53, 15.92) by 4.8, 10.2, and 10.0 mIoU respectively. The strongest absolute performance comes from Rein (DINOv2) fine-tuned on SKYSCENES, reaching 54.19 mIoU on UAVid and 28.10 on ICG DRONE. In low-shot regimes (5–25% of UAVid), augmenting real data with SKYSCENES via joint training or fine-tuning consistently improves mIoU by 3–11 points over real-only training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is SYNDRONE [34], a prior CARLA-based synthetic aerial dataset with 72k images across 3 (h,θ) settings and 8 maps. SKYSCENES wins on 11 of 12 individual (architecture × dataset) comparisons in Table 2, with the largest margins on ICG DRONE (nadir view): +6.94 mIoU for DeepLabv2, +9.99 for DAFormer, and +8.09 for Rein. The only loss is Rein on UAVid (54.19 vs 54.92, a 0.73 mIoU gap), likely because UAVid's oblique viewpoint aligns more closely with SYNDRONE's capture geometry. For tail classes (vehicles, humans), the gap widens substantially: DAFormer trained on SKYSCENES reaches 63.64 mIoU on UAVid vehicles vs 42.52 for SYNDRONE, and 10.21 vs 8.27 for humans, confirming that SKYSCENES's HumanSpawn() algorithm and broader viewpoint coverage specifically benefit underrepresented classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SKYSCENES extends the line of CARLA-based synthetic dataset construction (SynDrone, SELMA, SHIFT) by introducing a controlled-variation framework: comprehensive per-viewpoint metadata enables reproducing the exact scene layout and actor configuration under different weather, time-of-day, altitude, and pitch conditions—something no prior synthetic aerial dataset supports. It departs from ground-view synthetic-to-real work (GTAV, Synthia, VisDA-C) by explicitly addressing the aerial-specific challenges of long-tailed class distributions (humans, vehicles) and multi-altitude/multi-pitch capture, and from prior synthetic aerial datasets (MidAir, Espada, TartanAir, UrbanScene3D, VALID) by providing simultaneous semantic, instance, and depth annotations across 28 classes with jittered sensor parameters to mimic real-world imperfections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights that the synthetic-to-real generalization gap for aerial imagery (~50%) remains substantially larger than for ground-view (~20%), and that domain-generalization algorithms developed for ground-view settings fail to close this gap—pointing to a fundamental difference in the problem structure that demands aerial-specific algorithmic research. The controlled-variation diagnostic framework opens a new avenue for systematically studying model sensitivity to individual environmental factors, which is difficult or impossible with real-world data. Key open questions include whether the gap can be bridged with better simulators or novel aerial-specific algorithms, and whether the findings generalize beyond the three real datasets tested (UAVid, AEROSCAPES, ICG Drone).
