## Problem

Existing event-based deblurring methods rely on polarity accumulation, which only captures the net sum of event polarities and ignores the absolute intensity changes and the temporal order of event arrivals. This causes distinct motion patterns to map to identical representations, injecting ambiguity into the deblurring process and producing edge artifacts, particularly in complex multi-directional motion scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurred RGB image and the corresponding asynchronous event stream recorded during the same exposure window. The event stream is preprocessed into six event frames at three symmetric time scales (T/2, T/3, T/6) using the proposed Deviation Accumulation, rather than the conventional single polarity-accumulated frame. Training uses 256×256 patches with a batch size of 2, and the network outputs a single deblurred image at the same spatial resolution as the input.

## Method

The core contribution is a Deviation Accumulation (DA) event representation that, upon each new event, accumulates the running deviation from the initial moment rather than simply summing polarities, thereby encoding event arrival order and absolute intensity trajectory. A Recurrent Motion Extraction (RME) module processes the multi-scale event frames from longest to shortest time scale, using a shared-parameter recurrent mechanism with a learnable blending weight to merge global slow-motion features into local fast-motion features. A bi-directional Feature Alignment and Fusion (FAF) module first aligns image and event features via element-wise multiplication and addition in both directions, then applies multi-head attention with Q/K/V drawn from opposite modalities in each direction to fuse them in a consistent space. The full encoder-decoder network (built on NAFNet-style conv blocks) is trained end-to-end with PSNR loss, and fine-tuned on HS-ERGB and REBlur after pre-training on GoPro.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, the proposed method achieves 36.07 dB PSNR and 0.976 SSIM, surpassing the strongest event-based baseline EFNet by 0.61 dB PSNR and the best image-only method UFPNet by 2.01 dB. On HS-ERGB, it reaches 27.93 dB / 0.812 SSIM (0.25 dB over the second-best UFPNet+), and on REBlur it attains 38.47 dB / 0.978 SSIM (0.35 dB over EFNet). The method uses only 13.9M parameters, well below MSDI-Net (241.3M) and HINet (88.7M), while outperforming all of them.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against image-only SOTA methods, it leads UFPNet [6] by 2.01 dB on GoPro (36.07 vs 34.06), NAFNet [3] by 2.36 dB, and MSDI-Net [17] by 2.79 dB, demonstrating the substantial benefit of incorporating event data. Among event-based methods, it surpasses EFNet [33] (the strongest event-guided baseline) by 0.61 dB on GoPro, 1.25 dB on HS-ERGB (27.93 vs 26.68), and 0.35 dB on REBlur. The event-enhanced variants HINet+ and UFPNet+ (which simply concatenate the DA representation to the original input) gain 1.92 dB and 1.16 dB over their image-only counterparts on GoPro, confirming the DA representation's standalone value. EDI [25] and EVDI [49], earlier event-based approaches, trail the proposed method by 7+ dB on GoPro, highlighting the gap between optimization-based and learned event-guided deblurring. The method achieves these gains with only 13.9M parameters, fewer than EFNet's 8.5M is close but the paper's architecture is more compact than HINet (88.7M) and MSDI-Net (241.3M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based motion deblurring line initiated by the EDI model [25], from which the Deviation Accumulation (DA) representation is mathematically derived, and builds directly on EFNet [33]'s cross-modal attention fusion by making it bi-directional and adding an explicit feature alignment step. The key conceptual departure is from the prevailing polarity-accumulation paradigm for event encoding: the authors argue that simply summing polarities discards the temporal ordering of events and the absolute intensity trajectory, and that tracking cumulative deviation from the initial moment preserves this information. The Recurrent Motion Extraction module further departs from prior single-timescale event processing by explicitly modeling motion at multiple temporal granularities through a shared-parameter recurrent mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DA representation is a lightweight, physically-motivated preprocessing step that can be dropped into existing deblurring networks (demonstrated via HINet+ and UFPNet+ gaining 1.92 dB and 1.16 dB respectively), making it a practical tool for the broader event-based restoration community. The consistent gains across synthetic, semi-synthetic, and real-world datasets suggest the representation captures genuinely useful motion structure rather than dataset-specific artifacts. Open questions include whether DA generalizes to other event-based tasks (e.g., frame interpolation, HDR generation), whether the recurrent multi-timescale design scales to very long exposures, and whether the reliance on simulated events for GoPro training limits real-world transfer.
