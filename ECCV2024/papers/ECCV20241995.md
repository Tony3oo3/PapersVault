## Problem

Existing low-illumination adaptation methods either perform pixel-level enhancement that introduces noise and ignores semantic content, or apply unidirectional domain adaptation that loses critical color and texture information when transferring from bright to dark. Crucially, these approaches treat the entire image uniformly, overlooking that the task-relevant target's appearance degrades non-uniformly depending on its distance to light sources, which hinders high-level semantic understanding.

## Input / Output

At inference, the framework takes a single low-illumination RGB image or video clip as the sole input and produces task-specific outputs (class labels, bounding boxes, or action labels) through a downstream head. During training, it additionally consumes well-lit images from COCO/VOC for the domain alignment and contrastive learning branches, but these are not required at test time. The framework is task-agnostic: the same representation learning pipeline can be plugged into classification, detection, or action recognition backbones without architectural modification.

## Method

The framework first performs a two-level bi-directional domain alignment: low-lit images are brightened via prior-driven enhancement parameters to approximate an intermediate state, while well-lit images are darkened by a learned encoder-decoder network; the two approximations are then aligned at the image level via adversarial loss with cycle consistency and at the feature level via MMD in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. For target-aware representation learning, spectral residual saliency highlights static target regions in images, while a Temporal Gaussian Mixture Model detects moving targets in videos by identifying pixel-value mismatches against a background model. Different masking strategies are then applied—erasing for target patches to enforce robustness and a 'leaky-patching' scheme for background patches to preserve representational diversity. Finally, a BYOL-style non-negative contrastive loss with dual-branch EMA optimization aligns features from the daytime-trained model and the intermediate-state branch, yielding illumination-invariant target representations.

## Results

On the CODaN classification benchmark, the proposed framework achieves 70.32% top-1 accuracy with a ResNet-18 backbone, outperforming the strongest baseline (Similarity Min-Max, 65.87%) by 4.45% and the unenhanced ResNet-18 baseline (53.32%) by 17%. On the more challenging ExDark dataset, it reaches 51.58% accuracy vs. 49.03% for the best competitor. For object detection on ExDark, it achieves 64.2 mAP@0.5 (vs. 60.8 for Similarity Min-Max), and for action recognition on ARID, it attains 58.08% accuracy (vs. 52.01%), surpassing even some fully supervised models like C3D (46.17%) and 3D-ResNet-18 (54.68%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 23 baselines across all four evaluated tasks (CODaN classification, ExDark classification, ExDark detection, and ARID action recognition). The strongest competitor is Similarity Min-Max [34], which also employs dual-level (image + feature) adaptation; the proposed method beats it by 4.45% on CODaN, 2.55% on ExDark classification, 3.4 mAP on ExDark detection, and 6.07% on ARID action recognition. Low-light enhancement methods (EnlightenGAN, LEDNet, Zero-DCE++, RUAS, SCI, URetinexNet) consistently underperform or barely exceed the unenhanced baseline, with the paper noting that pixel-level enhancement introduces noise harmful to semantic tasks. Domain generalization methods like MixStyle [60] and IRM [1] perform at or below baseline levels, while CIConv [22] (60.32% on CODaN) and MAET [8] (56.48%) show moderate gains. Notably, on ARID the unsupervised proposed method (58.08%) surpasses fully supervised C3D [44] (46.17%) and 3D-ResNet-18 [15] (54.68%), narrowing the gap to the best supervised DTCM [45] (96.36%) by roughly 10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of day-night domain adaptation and self-supervised representation learning, building directly on BYOL's non-negative contrastive loss for feature refinement, MAE's patch-based masking for view construction, and the spectral residual method for saliency. It departs from the dominant unidirectional adaptation paradigm (dark→bright or bright→dark) by framing the problem as an ill-posed entropy-production task and proposing bi-directional alignment toward a shared intermediate state at both image and feature levels. The key conceptual departure from prior two-level methods (e.g., Similarity Min-Max, HLA-face) is the introduction of target-aware masking that distinguishes task-relevant regions from background, rather than treating the entire image uniformly during representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, task-agnostic recipe for improving high-level vision under low illumination without requiring paired day-night data, which is a significant practical constraint in real deployments. The consistent 3–6% gains across classification, detection, and action recognition on three datasets suggest the target-aware masking principle generalizes beyond any single task. However, the approach still depends on a pre-trained feature extractor for the MMD alignment and on a learned darkening network, and the intermediate state remains an approximation rather than a physically grounded quantity. Open questions include whether the bi-directional alignment strategy extends to more extreme illumination gaps (e.g., complete darkness) and whether the saliency/TGMM target-highlighting degrades in cluttered or multi-target scenes.
