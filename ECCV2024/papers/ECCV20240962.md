## Problem

Urban waterlogging detection from surveillance imagery remains difficult due to scarce and low-diversity labeled data, adverse conditions (low-light, strong reflections, clear/shallow water), and the inability of existing CNN-based methods to generalize to real-world deployment. While SAM offers strong zero-shot segmentation, it lacks task-specific knowledge and requires manual prompts, making it sub-optimal for this narrow downstream task without adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240962/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image of an urban road scene resized to 1024×1024 pixels, sourced from surveillance cameras or handheld devices. It produces a binary pixel-level segmentation mask identifying waterlogged regions, obtained by thresholding the predicted mask at 0.5. The small model branch additionally generates spatial prompts (masks, bounding boxes, or grid points) that are fed into SAM's prompt encoder alongside the image embedding.

## Method

LSM-Adapter couples a frozen SAM (ViT-B) large model with a task-specific CNN small model (SiNet, Mask R-CNN, or U2Net) through a co-adapter paradigm. A Histogram Equalization Adapter (HE-Adapt) laterally injects contrast-enhanced, high-frequency features into each transformer block of the image encoder to compensate for weak waterlogging cues. A Triple-S Prompt Adapter (TSP-Adapt) generates three complementary prompts: a spatial prompt from the small model's output, a semantic prompt via prototype-based masked average pooling over the large model's image embedding, and a style prompt from the amplitude spectrum of the input image's 2D FFT. A Dynamic Prompt Combiner (DPC) blends these prompts with learnable element-wise weights and an adaptive embedding before feeding them to the mask decoder. Training follows a two-stage strategy: first, the small model, HE-Adapt, and mask decoder are independently optimized; second, the prompt modules (TSP-Adapt, DPC, prompt encoder) are jointly trained with the image encoder, HE-Adapt, and small model frozen.

## Results

On the proposed UW-Bench, LSM-Adapter achieves the best F1-score of 74.76% and IoU of 59.69% on UW-all (using SiNet as the small model), and 73.77% F1 / 58.45% IoU on the harder UW-hard subset (using Mask R-CNN). Compared to the strongest SAM-based baseline (SAM-Adapter with prompts), LSM-Adapter improves F1 by 3.73–9.02% and IoU by 4.62–8.53% across the three small-model variants. Against the best standalone CNN baseline (SegFormer, F1=70.98%, IoU=55.01%), LSM-Adapter^S gains 3.78% in F1 and 4.68% in IoU on UW-all.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LSM-Adapter consistently outperforms all eight baselines on both UW-all and UW-hard in F1-score and IoU. Against the strongest CNN segmentation baseline (SegFormer, F1=70.98%, IoU=55.01% on UW-all), LSM-Adapter^S gains +3.78% F1 and +4.68% IoU; against the best SAM-based baseline with prompts (SAM-Adapter^S, F1=71.03%, IoU=55.07%), the gain is +3.73% F1 and +4.62% IoU. The improvement over the corresponding small model is most dramatic for Mask R-CNN: LSM-Adapter^M boosts IoU from 37.84% to 57.73% (+19.89%) on UW-all, showing that even a weak standalone detector benefits substantially from co-adaptation with SAM. On the harder UW-hard subset, LSM-Adapter^M achieves the highest F1 (73.77%) and IoU (58.45%), surpassing SAM-Adapter^M (F1=69.31%, IoU=53.03%) by +4.46% and +5.42% respectively. Notably, LSM-Adapter^U with U2Net as the small model shows a large recall advantage (72.56% vs. 60.87% for SAM-Adapter^U), indicating the co-adapter paradigm recovers waterlogged regions that the large model alone misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM adaptation literature (SAM-Adapter, SAMed, RSPrompter) by introducing a dual-level adaptation strategy: a Histogram Equalization Adapter for the frozen image encoder and a Triple-S Prompt Adapter for the mask decoder, rather than adapting only one side as prior work does. It further departs from existing SAM adaptation by incorporating a task-specific CNN small model (SiNet, Mask R-CNN, or U2Net) as a 'domain expert' that generates spatial prompts, creating a large-small model co-adapter paradigm not seen in prior SAM fine-tuning or prompting studies. On the task side, it builds on CNN-based urban waterlogging detection (Mask R-CNN, DeepLabv3+) and sensor/remote-sensing approaches, but is the first to apply a vision foundation model to this domain and to construct a benchmark explicitly targeting adverse conditions (low-light, strong reflection, clear water).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UW-Bench benchmark (7,677 images with pixel-level masks under diverse adverse conditions) addresses a real data-scarcity gap in urban waterlogging detection and provides a standardized evaluation that prior small, homogeneous datasets lacked. The large-small co-adapter paradigm offers a practical recipe for data-scarce downstream tasks where a strong foundation model lacks task-specific knowledge and a small model lacks generalization. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes beyond waterlogging to other transparent/reflective object detection tasks, whether the benchmark is sufficiently large for meaningful pre-training of the large model, and whether the two-stage training overhead is justified in deployment settings where end-to-end training is preferred.
