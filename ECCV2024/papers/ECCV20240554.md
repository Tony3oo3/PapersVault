## Problem

CLIP models achieve strong zero-shot image classification but fail at dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation, yielding only 3.1% mIoU on ADE20K. The paper identifies the root cause as a location misalignment in CLIP's self-attention: the mechanism produces spatially invariant features where local tokens attend broadly and similarly regardless of position, rather than the spatially covariant features needed for pixel-level localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240554/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to a 336-pixel shorter side, processed via sliding-window inference with 224×224 patches and 112-pixel stride) and a set of text class names encoded by CLIP's frozen language encoder. It outputs a per-patch segmentation score map (14×14 for ViT-Base/16) that is upsampled to pixel-level masks. No post-processing such as PAMR or DenseCRF is required, and the entire pipeline is training-free with no additional parameters beyond the pretrained CLIP weights.

## Method

SCLIP introduces a Correlative Self-Attention (CSA) mechanism that replaces the standard self-attention in CLIP's last transformer block. Instead of computing attention scores as X·Wq·Wkᵀ·Xᵀ (separate query and key projections), CSA uses a single shared projection: X·Wr·Wrᵀ·Xᵀ, so that attention scores reflect pairwise cosine similarity between projected tokens. This naturally amplifies diagonal attention (each token attends to itself) while still allowing tokens with similar semantic content to attend to each other, producing spatially covariant features. In practice, the pretrained Wq and Wk from CLIP are ensembled as the two CSA projections (Eq. 3), making the adaptation entirely training-free with zero additional parameters. The paper demonstrates that CSA is robust to the choice of projection matrix—random initializations or even the identity matrix yield competitive results—confirming that the mechanism's benefit comes from its correlation-based structure rather than learned weights.

## Results

SCLIP achieves a 38.2% average zero-shot mIoU across eight semantic segmentation benchmarks (PASCAL VOC, PASCAL Context, Cityscapes, ADE20K, COCO-Stuff, COCO-Object), substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art TCL (33.9%) by 4.3 percentage points. The improvement over vanilla CLIP (14.1% average) is 24.1 points, and over MaskCLIP (30.3%) is 7.9 points. Notably, SCLIP is a training-free adaptation that introduces no additional parameters and requires no fine-tuning, simply replacing the last self-attention layer in CLIP's ViT-Base/16 encoder with a Correlative Self-Attention module that reuses the pretrained W_q and W_k projections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCLIP outperforms all eight compared baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. Against the strongest prior method TCL [8], SCLIP leads by 7.9 points on PASCAL VOC (59.1 vs. 51.2), 6.1 on PASCAL Context (34.2 vs. 30.3), 8.7 on Cityscapes (32.2 vs. 23.5), and 1.2 on COCO-Stuff (22.4 vs. 19.6). Compared to MaskCLIP [71], which forces identity attention in the last layer, SCLIP gains 15.7 points on VOC21 and 5.7 on COCO-Stuff, demonstrating that purely local attention is insufficient. GroupViT [61] and SegCLIP [39] are competitive on VOC21 (52.3 and 52.6 respectively) but fall 6–7 points behind SCLIP on PASCAL Context and Cityscapes. ReCo [49] and OVSegmentor [63] trail SCLIP by 14+ points on average, confirming that retrieval-based or image-level supervision strategies are less effective than the architectural self-attention modification proposed here. With PAMR post-processing, SCLIP further extends its lead to 40.1% average mIoU versus TCL's 37.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCLIP builds directly on CLIP's ViT-Base/16 encoder, replacing the standard self-attention in the final transformer block with a Correlative Self-Attention (CSA) module that computes attention scores via a single shared projection rather than separate query and key matrices. This departs conceptually from MaskCLIP, which forces an identity attention map in the last layer (equivalent to window-size-1 local attention), and from GroupViT and TCL, which introduce additional architectural components or learned modules. The paper positions CSA as a middle ground: it naturally enhances diagonal (self-) attention to localize features while preserving semantic correlations across spatially distant but semantically similar tokens, all without any additional parameters or fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of SCLIP—requiring only a swap of the attention computation in one layer while reusing CLIP's pretrained W_q and W_k—makes it immediately deployable for open-vocabulary segmentation without domain-specific adaptation, a practical advantage over methods like TCL or GroupViT that require additional training. The diagnostic insight that CLIP's dense-prediction failure stems from spatial-invariant (holistic) features rather than poor feature extraction reframes how practitioners should think about adapting contrastive-pretrained models to pixel-level tasks. Open questions include whether the CSA mechanism generalizes to larger CLIP variants (ViT-Large, ViT-Huge), to other dense tasks such as instance or panoptic segmentation, and whether the insensitivity to the projection matrix W_r persists under different pretraining regimes.
