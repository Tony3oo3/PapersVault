## Problem

Pre-trained multimodal models for region-level captioning suffer from caption degeneration: they predict the most frequent training captions while systematically missing less frequent ones, because the model's output occupies a caption space smaller than the training distribution. This limits the diversity and informativeness of generated descriptions, particularly for fine-grained or domain-specific content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240997/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a referred bounding box, and optionally a set of control words (either self-predicted by the model or interactively specified by a user/expert model such as SAM, a text spotter, or an object detector). The visual encoder processes both a downscaled global image and a cropped-and-resized region patch through a frozen ViT, while control words are drawn from a 4585-class vocabulary spanning entities, attributes, actions, and scenes. The output is a specialized region-level caption constrained to a sub-space defined by the control words, enabling generation beyond the training caption space when interactive controls from new domains (e.g., fine-grained ImageNet labels or scene text) are provided.

## Method

ControlCap builds on a frozen EVA ViT + Q-former + frozen Flan-T5 XL backbone and introduces three components. First, a contextual visual embedding module extracts dual features: detail-rich embeddings from a cropped region patch and context-aware RoI embeddings from the global image, merged via a learnable MLP. Second, a discriminative region tagging module (Query2Label-based, 4585×2 classes with asymmetric loss) predicts control words from the visual embedding, which are then encoded into a control embedding using the LLM's own tokenizer and word embedding layer, with a learnable memory parameter handling empty control strings. Third, a bidirectional bridging (BiB) module with paired cross-attention and feed-forward layers maximizes information exchange between the visual and control embeddings in both directions (F_c→F_v and F_v→F_c), addressing the misalignment caused by varying control words. Training jointly optimizes a tagging loss and a captioning loss, and at inference the model can accept interactive controls to generate captions in sub-spaces it was never explicitly trained on.

## Results

On RefCOCOg, ControlCap (4.2B) achieves 17.0 METEOR and 111.4 CIDEr, outperforming the best 7B baselines (RegionGPT: 109.9 CIDEr; Alpha-CLIP+LLaVA: 109.2 CIDEr) by 1.5 and 2.2 CIDEr respectively. On Visual Genome, ControlCap reaches 20.4 METEOR and 181.9 CIDEr, surpassing the strongest competitor Alpha-CLIP+LLaVA (160.3 CIDEr) by 21.6 CIDEr. Under interactive controls (first noun of GT caption), ControlCap further jumps to 21.3 METEOR / 168.7 CIDEr on RefCOCOg and 28.8 METEOR / 302.3 CIDEr on VG. For dense captioning with GT localization, ControlCap achieves 42.8% mAP on VG V1.2 and 43.2% on VG-COCO, exceeding BLIP-2 (37.9% / 36.9%) by 4.9 and 6.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ControlCap outperforms all 16 baselines on both RefCOCOg and VG for REG. On RefCOCOg CIDEr, it edges out RegionGPT (109.9) and Alpha-CLIP+LLaVA (109.2) by 1.5 and 2.2 points respectively, while being substantially ahead of GLaMM (105.0), Osprey (108.3), Kosmos-2 (62.3), GRiT (71.6), and SLR+Rerank (66.2). On VG CIDEr, the margin is much larger: ControlCap (181.9) beats Alpha-CLIP+LLaVA (160.3) by 21.6, GLaMM (157.8) by 24.1, RegionGPT (145.6) by 36.3, GPT4RoI (145.2) by 36.7, and GRiT (142.0) by 39.9. Notably, ControlCap achieves these gains with a 4.2B model versus 7B for most LMM-based competitors. For dense captioning (Table 1), ControlCap leads all methods by 2.8–5.1 mAP without GT localization (18.5% on VG V1.2 vs. GRiT's 16.4%) and by 2.5–6.3 mAP with GT localization (43.2% on VG-COCO vs. BLIP-2's 36.9%). Under interactive controls, ControlCap's CIDEr more than doubles on VG (302.3 vs. 181.9), a capability none of the baselines possess.

## Relation to Prior Work

ControlCap builds directly on the BLIP-2 architecture (frozen ViT + Q-former alignment + frozen LLM) and incorporates the Query2Label recognition decoder as its discriminative region tagging module. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: LMM-based region-level captioning (Kosmos-2, GLaMM, RegionGPT, Osprey) and controllable text generation (PromptCap, Tag2Text), transplanting the idea of conditioning generation on explicit keywords into the region-level setting. The key departure from prior LMM-based captioners is the introduction of a discriminative tagging module to produce control words that partition the caption space into sub-spaces, combined with a bidirectional bridging module to align visual and control embeddings—neither of which appears in the prior region-captioning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The caption degeneration issue is a fundamental limitation of generative captioning models that this paper addresses with a clean, modular solution, and the 21.6-point CIDEr gain on Visual Genome demonstrates the practical impact. The controllable inference paradigm—where external models (SAM, text spotters, detectors) supply control words—opens a path toward composable, domain-specialized captioning pipelines without retraining. However, the interactive-control evaluation uses the first noun from ground-truth captions as a privileged proxy, and the 4.2B parameter count remains substantial; open questions include how well the approach generalizes to truly open-vocabulary controls and whether the 4585-class tagging vocabulary is sufficient for long-tail regions.
