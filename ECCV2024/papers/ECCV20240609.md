## Problem

Low-light video enhancement without paired ground truth remains challenging because spatial degradation (noise, exposure, contrast) and temporal inconsistency are intricately intertwined, and existing unpaired image-based methods either ignore temporal coherence or lack mechanisms to prevent over/under-exposure. Progressive optimization via deep unfolding has not been explored for this video task, leaving a gap in interpretable, controllable, reference-free video enhancement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240609/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light video clip (960×512 spatial resolution) and produces enhanced normal-light video frames with improved illumination, suppressed noise, and temporal consistency. No paired ground truth is required; instead, unpaired expert-retouched images from MIT-Adobe FiveK serve as spatial illumination priors, and pre-trained RAFT optical flow provides temporal alignment cues. A soft mask derived from the residual between the current frame and its temporally estimated structure selectively balances noise removal against texture preservation.

## Method

UDU-Net formulates low-light video enhancement as a MAP estimation problem with separate spatial and temporal regularizers, then unrolls the ADMM decomposition into a stage-wise trainable deep network. The Intra subnet handles spatial illumination in two stages: first learning coarse illumination statistics from unpaired retouched images via a Relativistic Average HingeGAN discriminator, then refining illumination under controllable human-perception feedback (BRISQUE-guided gamma correction and linear scaling in HSV space). The Inter subnet enforces temporal consistency using 3D convolutions to aggregate adjacent warped frames (aligned by fine-tuned RAFT optical flow) for structure estimation, followed by a detail-enhancement branch that generates residual details while a soft exponential mask (Eq. 19) prevents noise from corrupting inter-frame consistency. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with semantic VGG feature losses, content self-supervised losses, and flow-based alignment losses, requiring no paired low/normal-light video data.

## Results

On the SDSD dataset, UDU-Net (a reference-free method) achieves 23.94 dB PSNR and 0.7446 SSIM on outdoor scenes, and 22.41 dB PSNR and 0.7368 SSIM on indoor scenes. Compared to the best reference-free baseline, it gains 2.05 dB PSNR and 0.1158 SSIM (outdoor) and 1.76 dB PSNR and 0.0153 SSIM (indoor). For temporal smoothness, it achieves the lowest warp error (0.24 outdoor, 0.41 indoor) and MABD (0.21 outdoor, 1.05 indoor) among all reference-free methods, with reductions of 0.85 in warp and 0.27 in MABD over the next-best reference-free method on outdoor scenes. Notably, its outdoor SSIM (0.7446) slightly exceeds that of the supervised SDSDNet (0.7445), and its PSNR is within 0.36 dB of SDSDNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among reference-free methods, UDU-Net substantially outperforms all baselines: on outdoor scenes it surpasses the next-best SRIE (21.89 dB) by 2.05 dB PSNR and CLIP-LIT (20.88 dB) by 3.06 dB; on indoor scenes it exceeds MF (20.65 dB) by 1.76 dB PSNR. For temporal consistency, UDU-Net achieves the best warp (0.24) and MABD (0.21) among reference-free methods on outdoor, beating SALVE (warp 1.09, MABD 0.48) by 0.85 and 0.27 respectively. Against supervised methods, UDU-Net's outdoor SSIM (0.7446) marginally exceeds SDSDNet (0.7445) and its PSNR (23.94) is within 0.36 dB of SDSDNet (24.30), while on indoor it trails SDSDNet by 4.62 dB PSNR. Compared to the self-supervised SALVE, UDU-Net gains 5.22 dB PSNR and 0.1558 SSIM on outdoor, and 5.32 dB PSNR and 0.0153 SSIM on indoor, while also halving the warp error. The method also outperforms other unrolling-based approaches (RUAS, SCI) by large margins, confirming the benefit of the progressive spatial-temporal decomposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm (originally from ADMM-based image restoration [6]) to the low-light video domain, where prior unfolding methods (RUAS, SCI, URetinex-Net) were restricted to single-image enhancement. It departs from existing unpaired video enhancement approaches (EnlightenGAN, SALVE, StableLLVE) by formulating the problem as a MAP estimation with explicit spatial and temporal regularizers, then unrolling the ADMM decomposition into a progressive Intra/Inter subnet architecture. The use of BRISQUE as a proxy human-perception feedback loop to control illumination in a second unrolling stage is a novel mechanism not seen in prior unpaired enhancement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first interpretable, reference-free unrolled network for low-light video enhancement, offering a principled decomposition of intertwined spatial and temporal degradation that is absent in purely data-driven video enhancers. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (SDSD), and the reliance on BRISQUE as a quality proxy is acknowledged as a limitation; the authors suggest diffusion-based or more powerful no-reference metrics could further improve the controllable feedback. Open questions include whether the progressive unrolling framework scales to longer video sequences or more complex real-world degradations, and whether the optical-flow-based temporal alignment remains robust under extreme motion or occlusion.
