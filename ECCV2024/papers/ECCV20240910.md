## Problem

Existing 3D language grounding methods rely on multi-view perception or external priors (voxel maps, knowledge graphs) to improve performance, but they fail to address the fundamental domain gap between pre-trained vision-language encoders (e.g., CLIP trained on WebImageText) and the target 3D grounding domain. This domain mismatch degrades cross-modal alignment, and no prior work has explored domain adaptation as a mechanism to bridge it for 3D object grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240910/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language description (e.g., 'the chair with black legs') and rendered RGB images of two candidate 3D objects from ShapeNet, observed from discrete viewpoints (8 views for multi-view, 1 bird's-eye view for single-view). It outputs a binary grounding score per candidate object, selecting the one whose conditional probability p(o_i | l) is highest. In the simulation setting, inputs additionally include physical scene backgrounds and worktops rendered in MuJoCo, with diverse and non-fixed viewpoints.

## Method

DA4LG employs a pseudo-siamese visual encoder architecture: a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 Vision Encoder captures source-domain features, while a Domain-specific Encoder (also initialized from CLIP ViT-B/32) learns target-domain 3D representations via LoRA-style low-rank adapters (W*_A, W*_B) injected into the Q, K, V projections of multi-head attention. An Embedding Reweighting Layer modulates domain features by their cosine similarity to the language embedding, suppressing irrelevant visual cues. Training uses multi-task learning combining three objectives: a primary binary cross-entropy grounding loss (LGR), a bidirectional vision-language contrastive loss (VLC) that aligns fused object features with text, and a caption generation loss (VGC) using a frozen GPT-2 decoder conditioned on object features. This design requires only ~79.5M trainable parameters (vs. 168M for VLG) and avoids external priors, achieving 86.8% multi-view and 83.8% single-view accuracy on SNARE.

## Results

On the SNARE benchmark (multi-view setting), DA4LG achieves 86.8% validation accuracy and 81.9% test accuracy, tying the previous SOTA (MAGiC) on validation and surpassing it by 0.2% on test. In the single-view setting, DA4LG reaches 83.8% validation accuracy, outperforming MAGiC (82.0%) by 1.8%. On the newly introduced Simulation-SNARE benchmark, DA4LG achieves 63.9% (multi-view) and 62.6% (single-view) accuracy, outperforming the next-best method VLG by 2.5% and 2.1% respectively. DA4LG requires only 79.5M trainable parameters, less than half of VLG's 168M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA4LG ties MAGiC (the prior SOTA multi-view method) at 86.8% validation accuracy but edges it out by 0.2% on the test set (81.9% vs. 81.7%). Against external-prior methods, DA4LG improves over VLG by 1.9% validation / 2.9% test, over LOCKET by 2.1% / 2.9%, over MATCH by 4.6% / 5.4%, and over CLIP by 12.3% / 11.0%. In the single-view setting (Table 2), DA4LG (83.8%) surpasses MAGiC (82.0%) by 1.8% and VLG (81.8%) by 2.0%, demonstrating that domain-adapted multimodal alignment is effective even without multi-view inputs. On the Simulation-SNARE benchmark, DA4LG outperforms all baselines in both multi-view (63.9% vs. VLG's 61.4%) and single-view (62.6% vs. VLG's 60.5%) settings, indicating superior generalization to diverse viewpoints and physical scenes. Notably, DA4LG achieves these gains with only 79.5M trainable parameters (LoRA-style domain adapters), compared to VLG's 168M, highlighting a clear parameter-efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes 3D visual language grounding as a domain adaptation problem, drawing directly on LoRA-style low-rank adapters from NLP (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and the pseudo-siamese dual-encoder architecture from cross-domain few-shot learning (Gong et al., 2023). It departs from the two dominant prior lines—multi-view perception (MAGiC, LAGOR) and external prior injection (VLG, LOCKET, ViLBERT)—by instead adapting a frozen CLIP vision encoder with a lightweight domain-specific branch, arguing that the core bottleneck is the domain gap between WebImageText pre-training and 3D grounding rather than insufficient viewpoints or missing 3D priors. Multi-task learning with contrastive and caption-generation objectives (inspired by BLIP-2) is introduced to enforce cross-modal alignment within the target domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a parameter-efficient domain adapter (79.5M trainable params) can match or exceed methods requiring 168M+ parameters or external knowledge graphs, offering a practical recipe for grounding in data-scarce 3D settings. The single-view result (83.8%) surpassing several multi-view baselines suggests that closing the domain gap may be more important than adding viewpoints, a finding with implications for embodied agents with limited sensors. However, evaluation is confined to binary-choice grounding on ShapeNet-derived objects (SNARE) and a small simulation set, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to open-vocabulary scenes, larger candidate sets, or real-world 3D environments with occlusion and clutter.
