## Problem

Pixel-level out-of-distribution detection remains challenging because existing methods either require OOD training samples (real or synthetic), are tailored to specific applications (road anomaly, industrial inspection), or rely on image-level formulations that cannot handle the complex intra-class variability inherent to pixel-level tasks. In particular, the single-mean-per-class representation used in the image-level GROOD approach is insufficient to model the diverse appearances within a single semantic class at pixel granularity (e.g., wheels vs. windshield for a 'car' label).

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, splits it into ViT-sized patches, and encodes each patch into a 1024-dimensional feature vector using a frozen DINOv2 ViT-L backbone. It produces a per-pixel OOD score in (0,1) that is calibrated as the false-negative rate on in-distribution data, along with a predicted class label per pixel; the score map is upsampled from patch resolution to full image resolution. No OOD or anomalous samples are used during training, and the method operates on a single unified model across all classes rather than per-class models.

## Method

PixOOD constructs a per-class 2D projection space whose axes are (i) an MLP logit score and (ii) the distance to the nearest etalon from a novel Incremental Soft-to-Hard Data Condensation algorithm. This condensation algorithm replaces K-means with a smooth, differentiable objective using a softmin operator with temperature scheduling (soft-to-hard assignment), L1 distance for outlier robustness, and per-etalon adaptive scale parameters β_k that regularize via a spherical Laplace likelihood; it is theoretically shown to be a lower bound on the complete-data log-likelihood in an EM formulation for a mixture of spherical Laplace distributions. An etalon re-initialization mechanism resets poorly-supported etalons to random data points to escape local minima. The ID/OOD decision is then solved as a Neyman-Pearson likelihood-ratio test in this 2D space, yielding a calibrated, explainable anomaly score without any OOD training data.

## Results

PixOOD achieves AP 95.42 / FPR 5.11 on Road Anomaly and AP 92.31 / FPR 0.80 on FishyScapes LaF without any OOD training data, outperforming the best no-OOD baseline DaCUP (89.75 / 1.45) on FishyScapes LaF. On the SMIYC benchmark, PixOOD attains the best no-OOD results on the Obstacle Track (AP 88.90, FPR 0.30, F1 50.82). On MVTec AD, PixOOD reports Image-level AUROC 97.0, AUPRO 91.3, and Pixel-level AUROC 94.6 with a single unified model, and on LaRS it places 4th with Q = 73.9. Overall, the method achieves state-of-the-art results on four of the seven evaluated datasets while remaining competitive on the rest.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among methods that use no OOD training data, PixOOD clearly outperforms DaCUP [39] on FishyScapes LaF (AP 92.31 vs 89.75, FPR 0.80 vs 1.45) and is competitive on Road Anomaly (AP 95.42 vs 96.19, FPR 5.11 vs 5.46). On the SMIYC Obstacle Track, PixOOD achieves the best no-OOD results (AP 88.90, FPR 0.30), surpassing DaCUP (81.50 / 1.13) and RbA (87.85 / 3.33). However, PixOOD underperforms NFlowJS [17] on SMIYC LaF NoKnown (AP 85.07 vs 89.28, FPR 4.46 vs 0.65) and cDNP [13] on SMIYC Anomaly Track (AP 68.88 vs 89.90), where the authors attribute the gap to PixOOD flagging semantic/domain shifts (e.g., mountains, forests) that the Anomaly Track does not annotate. On MVTec AD, PixOOD (Image AUROC 97.0, Pixel AUROC 94.6) trails the best unified model HVQ-Trans [26] (98.0 / 97.3) but remains competitive, and combining PixOOD with DRAEM [43] lifts AUPRO from 91.3 to 95.3. On LaRS, PixOOD ranks 4th (Q = 73.9) behind Snarciv3 (78.1), Swin-L (77.8), and Mask2Former-DLMU (75.7), with the main weakness being over-segmentation reducing precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixOOD is a direct pixel-level extension of the authors' own GROOD framework (Vojíř et al., ICCVW 2023), which performed calibrated OOD detection at the image level using a single class mean in a 2D projection space. The key departures are: (i) replacing the single mean with a novel soft-to-hard data condensation algorithm that maintains up to K etalons per class, theoretically linked to EM optimisation of a spherical Laplace mixture; (ii) replacing the linear probe with a small MLP to handle the richer intra-class variability at the pixel level; and (iii) adding pixel-level adaptations (mixed-patch filtering, feature-map up-sampling). The method deliberately avoids the task-specific biases common in the OOD literature—no synthetic anomalies, no auxiliary OOD datasets, no reconstruction or energy-based regularisation—positioning itself as a general-purpose detector applicable across road, industrial, and maritime domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single, task-agnostic pipeline can be competitive across three very different pixel-level anomaly benchmarks, which is notable given that most prior methods are designed for one domain. The data condensation algorithm is a standalone contribution with a clean EM interpretation and a practical re-initialisation mechanism, potentially useful beyond OOD detection. Limitations include under-segmentation of small anomalies, over-segmentation of large regions, and an inability to detect 'logical' anomalies (e.g., a switched cable) that require scene-level reasoning rather than appearance-based detection. An open question is whether the fixed condensation budget K (set to 1000) can be made adaptive per class or whether the approach degrades when the number of in-distribution classes grows substantially.
