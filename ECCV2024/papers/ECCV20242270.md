## Problem

Certifiable robustness methods for deep learning have been developed almost exclusively for closed-set image classification, yet face recognition operates in an open-set (metric learning) scenario with no fixed class set, making direct adaptation non-trivial. This paper establishes that the 1-Lipschitz condition suffices for certifiable robustness in open-set face recognition, derives the corresponding certified radius, and identifies that standard FR training causes the feature norm to collapse, rendering the certified radius impractically small.

## Input / Output

The system takes a pair of RGB facial images (pixels normalized to [-0.5, 0.5]) and, at inference, discards the training-time header to compute a cosine similarity score between the backbone feature vectors of the two images. A binary match decision is made by comparing this score against a threshold τ, and the certified robustness radius is the maximum ℓ₂ perturbation norm ε under which the decision is provably unchanged. The certified radius is proportional to the minimum feature norm M = min(||f(x)||₂, ||f(y)||₂) and depends on the angle θ between the two feature vectors.

## Method

The backbone is a ResNet variant where every convolution is replaced by an SDP-based Lipschitz Layer (SLL) to enforce the 1-Lipschitz condition, trained with the ElasticFace-Cos margin loss plus a proposed feature-norm regularization L_Reg,ξ(f(x)) = max{-ξ log(||f(x)||₂/ξ), 0}. The key theoretical result (Theorem 3) shows that for a 1-Lipschitz backbone with cosine similarity, the certified radius against dodging or impersonation attacks is M·(√(1-τ²)cosθ ∓ τ sinθ), where M is the minimum feature norm and θ is the inter-feature angle. The paper proves a fundamental upper bound on the certified radius independent of model architecture, and empirically demonstrates that without regularization the feature norm decays toward zero during training (because small norms permit angle adjustment with smaller ℓ₂ changes), collapsing the certified radius to near-zero. The regularization loss penalizes feature norms below ξ, trading some accuracy for a meaningfully larger certified radius, as validated by ablation studies on LFW, CFP-FP, and AgeDB.

## Results

On LFW (FAR=1e-3), the proposed 1-Lipschitz FR model achieves 83.97% TAR with 100% certifiably-robustified accuracy (CRA^U) at ε=1.5 and 74.76% at ε=3.0, with a median certified radius of 3.136. Against PGD and C&W attacks, the model maintains 100% empirical robustness (ERA^U) at both ε=1.5 and ε=3.0 for untargeted attacks, and 88.88% ERA^I at ε=0.75 for impersonation attacks. On the harder CFP-FP and AgeDB benchmarks, TAR drops to 18.86% and 16.83% respectively, reflecting the difficulty of distinguishing 'hard' samples under the 1-Lipschitz constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 1-Lipschitz model trades ~15–16 percentage points of TAR (83.97% vs. 99.6–99.75%) for dramatically superior adversarial robustness. On untargeted attacks, the proposed method achieves 100% ERA^U at both ε=1.5 and ε=3.0, while ArcFace drops to 26.06% and 0.22%, AdaCos to 0.77% and 0%, and ElasticFace to 14.58% and 0.11%. For impersonation attacks at ε=0.75, the proposed method retains 88.88% ERA^I versus 0% (ArcFace, ElasticFace) and 63.57% (AdaCos). The one area where the proposed method underperforms is dodging at small ε=0.25, where ArcFace (98.03%) and ElasticFace (97.30%) exceed the proposed 74.22%, which the authors attribute to more same-identity pairs lying near the decision threshold under the 1-Lipschitz constraint. The C&W median attack radius for the proposed model (5.203 for untargeted, 5.892 for impersonation) is roughly 5–6× larger than all baselines, confirming substantially stronger resistance to optimization-based attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1-Lipschitz certifiable robustness framework (originally developed for closed-set image classification by Tsuzuku et al. and Singla & Feizi) to the fundamentally different open-set face recognition setting, where no fixed class set exists and similarity is measured via cosine distance between feature vectors. It departs from the only prior certifiable-robustness-for-FR work (Pérez et al., randomized smoothing) by providing a deterministic, iteration-free certification criterion rather than a probabilistic one. The construction leverages the SDP-based Lipschitz Layer (Araujo et al.) for the backbone and ElasticFace for the training loss, but the key theoretical contribution is deriving robustness criteria specific to cosine similarity under the 1-Lipschitz constraint, which has no direct analogue in the classification literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical guarantee of certifiable robustness for open-set face recognition, filling a gap that is significant for security-critical deployments where empirical defenses (adversarial training, ensembles) offer no per-sample guarantee. The feature-norm analysis reveals a fundamental tension: the 1-Lipschitz constraint combined with cosine-similarity training causes feature norms to collapse, capping the certified radius at a level that is impractical without a regularization loss that costs substantial accuracy (83.97% vs. 99.75% TAR on LFW). Open questions include extending the results beyond ℓ2, converting existing high-accuracy FR models to certifiably robust ones via spectral normalization, and closing the large accuracy gap while maintaining meaningful certified radii.
