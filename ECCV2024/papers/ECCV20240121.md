## Problem

Semi-supervised medical image segmentation relies on teacher-student frameworks with EMA-updated teachers to generate pseudo-labels, but a single teacher inevitably produces noisy predictions that reinforce confirmation bias. Existing remedies either add a second trainable student (increasing training cost and losing EMA stability) or use multiple teachers with poorly designed updating strategies that fail to enforce sufficient diversity, while also discarding conflicting predictions rather than learning from them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240121/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D cardiac MRI slices (ACDC, resized to 256×256) and 3D CT/MRI volumes (Pancreas-NIH cropped to 112×112×80, Left Atrium cropped to 96×96×96), with only 3–12 labeled instances per dataset. It produces pixel-wise segmentation masks: binary masks for LA and Pancreas, and three-class masks (right ventricle, myocardium, left ventricle) for ACDC. Weak augmentations (random cropping, flipping) generate pseudo-labels from both teachers simultaneously, while two distinct strong augmentations (color-jitting and copy-paste) are applied to the student's unlabeled input depending on which teacher's turn it is.

## Method

AD-MT maintains a single trainable student and two non-trainable teacher models updated by EMA of the student weights, but only one teacher is updated per iteration in an alternating schedule. The Random Periodic Alternate (RPA) module enforces diversity by assigning complementary unlabeled data batches, distinct strong augmentations (color-jitting for T1, copy-paste for T2), and randomly sampled switching periods (T_m ~ Uniform(0, T_max)) so the two teachers evolve along different trajectories. The Conflict-Combating Module (CCM) computes an entropy-weighted ensemble of the two teacher predictions, then for pixels where the teachers disagree, it compares the entropy of the student's own prediction against the ensemble and selects the lower-entropy one as the final pseudo-label, thereby learning from conflicts rather than discarding them. The total loss combines a standard supervised loss on labeled data with a consistency loss on unlabeled data driven by these conflict-aware pseudo-labels, requiring no extra trainable parameters or auxiliary constraints.

## Results

AD-MT achieves the best Dice score across all three benchmarks in the low-label regime. On Pancreas-NIH with 10% labeled data (6 instances), AD-MT reaches 80.21% Dice, surpassing the previous SOTA BCP (73.83%) by 6.38%. On Left Atrium with 5% labeled data (4 instances), AD-MT achieves 89.63% Dice and 6.56 95HD, outperforming BCP by 1.61% Dice. On ACDC with 5% labeled data (3 instances), AD-MT obtains 88.75% Dice and 1.48 95HD, beating BCP by 1.16% Dice without requiring an additional pre-training stage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AD-MT consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines on the low-label settings across all three datasets. Against the strongest competitor BCP [2] (which requires a separate pre-training stage), AD-MT gains 6.38% Dice on Pancreas (10%), 1.61% Dice on LA (5%), and 1.16% Dice on ACDC (5%), while also achieving the lowest 95HD and ASD in every setting. The only exception is Pancreas at 20% labeled data, where BCP edges out AD-MT by 0.3% Dice (82.91 vs. 82.61), though AD-MT still achieves the best 95HD (4.94 vs. 6.43) and ASD (1.38 vs. 2.25). Compared to multi-student co-training methods (MC-Net+ [32], CPS [5]), AD-MT avoids extra trainable parameters yet surpasses MC-Net+ by over 6% Dice on LA with 4 labeled instances. Among multi-teacher ensembling methods, AD-MT outperforms PS-MT [15] by 3.27% Dice on Pancreas (10%) and 1.84% on LA (5%), demonstrating that the RPA updating strategy and CCM conflict handling yield more effective diverse supervision than prior ensembling approaches. Supervised-only baselines (VNet, U-Net) are outperformed by 20–35% Dice in the 5% labeled regime, confirming the substantial benefit of the semi-supervised framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

AD-MT extends the mean-teacher consistency regularization framework for semi-supervised medical image segmentation by introducing two non-trainable teacher models updated via EMA, departing from both multi-student co-training (which adds training cost and loses EMA stability) and prior multi-teacher ensembling methods like PS-MT (which use simple fixed updating strategies). The key conceptual departure is treating the teacher updating strategy itself as the core mechanism for diversity: the RPA module schedules alternate updates with complementary data batches, distinct augmentations (color-jitting vs. copy-paste), and randomized switching periods, while the CCM explicitly learns from conflicting teacher predictions rather than discarding them. This positions the work as a refinement of the teacher-student paradigm rather than a new training objective or architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that AD-MT achieves strong SOTA results without requiring an additional pre-training stage (unlike BCP) or extra trainable parameters (unlike co-training methods), making it a drop-in improvement for existing semi-supervised medical segmentation pipelines. The finding that learning from teacher conflicts (CCM) yields a meaningful ~2% Dice gain over simply dropping them challenges the common practice of discarding disagreement. Open questions include whether the specific augmentation pairing (color-jitting vs. copy-paste) is optimal or dataset-dependent, whether the approach extends to more complex multi-organ or 4D segmentation tasks, and whether the random period strategy generalizes beyond the tested 2D/3D CNN backbones.
