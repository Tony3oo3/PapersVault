## Problem

Existing multi-concept personalization methods in diffusion models suffer from identity degradation when generating multiple concepts simultaneously, layout conflicts at occluded regions (e.g., Mix-of-Show's regionally controllable sampling fails when concept regions overlap), and illumination disharmony between foreground and background. Additionally, methods like Custom Diffusion and Mix-of-Show require computationally expensive model merging or joint optimization to combine multiple concepts into a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240821/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a global text prompt describing multiple objects (e.g., 'a man and a woman'), multiple pre-trained single-concept models (LoRA adapters or InstantID), and their corresponding reference images. It produces a single RGB image containing all specified concepts with preserved identity and coherent spatial layout. Notably, the single-concept models can be sourced directly from community platforms like civitai.com in a plug-and-play fashion without any additional tuning or model merging; concept masks are automatically derived via visual comprehension (Blip-2/Segment Anything) from the Stage-1 generated image.

## Method

OMG employs a two-stage sampling framework on SDXL. Stage 1 generates a non-customized image with a coherent layout using only class-name text prompts (no identifiers), while simultaneously collecting cross-attention maps at every denoising step and deriving concept masks via visual comprehension. Stage 2 performs multi-concept personalized denoising via a Concept Noise Blending strategy: at each timestep, each single-concept model independently predicts noise for its assigned region, and these region-specific noises are composited into a single latent using the concept masks (Eq. 6). To preserve the occlusion-aware layout from Stage 1, the stored cross-attention maps are overwritten into the UNet at every denoising step (Eq. 7), ensuring spatial consistency even where concepts overlap. The initiation timestep for noise blending (approximately 35 out of 50 DDIM steps) is a critical hyperparameter that balances layout coherence (governed by early steps) against identity preservation (governed by later steps), and also controls foreground-background illumination harmony.

## Results

On single-concept personalization, OMG (using LoRA) achieves the best Text Alignment (0.693 for characters, 0.730 for objects), Identity Alignment (0.514), and Image Alignment (0.842) among all compared methods. For multi-concept generation, OMG shows minimal identity degradation (Δ Identity Alignment = −0.004 for characters, Δ Image Alignment = −0.033 for objects), whereas baselines like Custom Diffusion drop by −0.048 (Identity) and −0.061 (Image). In the diverse-concept generation evaluation (Table 2), OMG achieves an average score of 0.480, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline DreamBooth (0.384) and Mix-of-show (0.365).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMG outperforms all four training-based baselines (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion, Custom Diffusion, Mix-of-show) on single-concept metrics: it leads in Text Alignment (0.693 vs. 0.677/0.673/0.629/0.675), Identity Alignment (0.514 vs. 0.456/0.292/0.370/0.422), and Image Alignment (0.842 vs. 0.805/0.784/0.840/0.791). In multi-concept generation (Table 2), OMG's average score of 0.480 exceeds DreamBooth (0.384), Mix-of-show (0.365), Custom Diffusion (0.361), and Textual Inversion (0.248) by a wide margin, with the largest gap on man+woman (0.487 vs. 0.361 for Mix-of-show). Against InstantID, OMG trades a small single-concept advantage (InstantID: Text Align 0.698, ID Align 0.534 vs. OMG: 0.692, 0.500) for a plug-and-play, tuning-free multi-concept capability that InstantID does not natively support; the authors note InstantID benefits from fine-tuning on extensive facial data. Overall, OMG's key differentiator is near-zero identity degradation when scaling from one to multiple concepts (Δ ≈ 0), whereas Custom Diffusion and Mix-of-show show noticeable drops.

## Relation to Prior Work

OMG builds on the cross-attention control paradigm from Prompt-to-Prompt (Hertz et al., 2022) for layout preservation, and operates on top of single-concept personalization backbones such as LoRA and InstantID. It departs from prior multi-concept methods (Mix-of-Show, Custom Diffusion) that require merging multiple fine-tuned models into a single network through additional optimization; instead, OMG keeps each single-concept model separate and fuses their outputs via a Concept Noise Blending strategy at both latent and attention levels during sampling. The two-stage design—first generating a coherent layout with a base T2I model, then injecting identities regionally—repurposes the image-editing mechanism of attention map overwriting for the multi-concept setting, addressing occlusion conflicts that regionally-controllable sampling (Mix-of-Show) cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play compatibility with community LoRA models (e.g., from civitai.com) without any retraining or model merging significantly lowers the barrier to multi-concept personalization, making it accessible to practitioners who already have single-concept adapters. The occlusion handling via attention map preservation from Stage 1 addresses a concrete failure mode of prior methods that produces layout conflicts and identity degradation. Limitations include the heuristic nature of the optimal initiation timestep (~35/50), the requirement that single-concept models be pre-trained separately, and evaluation limited to SDXL with up to 5 concepts. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) and whether the noise blending strategy can be made adaptive per-region rather than relying on a single global timestep.
