## Problem

Knowledge distillation of large-scale vision models (e.g., ViT, Swin) suffers severe performance degradation when the teacher-student capacity gap is large (e.g., 10× compression). While multi-step distillation with an intermediate teacher-assistant mitigates this, selecting the optimal teacher-assistant scale requires expensive trial-and-error across all possible sizes, and no principled metric exists to guide that selection automatically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241748/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at 224×224 resolution (transformer experiments) or 32×32 (CNN experiments) and outputs class labels for image classification. A key structural detail is that the student is not an independently designed network but a structurally pruned subset of the teacher architecture, meaning the student directly inherits parameters from the teacher through the pruning process rather than being trained from scratch.

## Method

AMD decomposes multi-step distillation into three stages: (1) Structural Pruning discretizes the continuous scale space into m evenly-spaced candidates via gridding, then applies structural pruning to generate teacher-assistant architectures at each scale; (2) Joint Optimization exploits the incremental property of structural pruning (smaller candidates are parameter subsets of larger ones) to share parameters across all candidates, enabling simultaneous distillation from the teacher to every candidate in a single training run with a combined loss of cross-entropy, KL-divergence on softened logits, and MSE on last-layer hidden states; (3) Optimal Selection applies the Negative Performance-Scale Derivative (NPSD = −(P_t − P_ta)/(S_t − S_ta)) metric to pick the teacher-assistant with the best performance-to-scale trade-off, followed by a final distillation step to the student. This eliminates the need for exhaustive scale search while achieving comparable or superior accuracy to manual multi-step baselines at roughly 10× lower GPU cost.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ViT-Base as teacher (10% student), AMD achieves 95.52% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best single-step baseline CRD (94.18%) by 1.34% and the best multi-step baseline DGKD (92.78%) by 2.74%. On CIFAR-100, AMD reaches 80.19% top-1 accuracy vs. CRD's 78.29% and DGKD's 78.33%. On ImageNet, AMD achieves 72.27% (ViT-Base 10%) and 76.81% (Swin-Base 10%), surpassing CRD by 2.61% and 1.98% respectively. AMD requires only 2.2× GPU hours compared to 20× for TAKD/MMD and 84× for DGKD, demonstrating a ~10× speedup over other multi-step methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMD outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and teacher architecture tested. Against single-step methods, AMD leads CRD [73] by 1.34% on CIFAR-10 and 1.90% on CIFAR-100 (ViT-Base), and by 2.61% on ImageNet; the gap over logit-based methods KD [31] and DKD [100] is even larger (15.89% and 9.81% on CIFAR-10 respectively). Against multi-step baselines, AMD surpasses TAKD [55] by 8.09% on CIFAR-10 and DGKD [71] by 2.74%, while requiring only 2.2× GPU hours versus 20× and 84×. AMD matches the manually-tuned MMD baseline within 0.02–0.08% accuracy while reducing training cost by ~9× (2.2× vs. 20× GPU hours). On ImageNet, AMD achieves 72.27% (ViT-Base) and 76.81% (Swin-Base), beating CRD by 2.61% and 1.98% and DGKD by 3.02% and 2.42% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-step distillation paradigm introduced by Mirzadeh et al. (TAKD, AAAI 2020) and Son et al. (DGKD, ICCV 2021), which use intermediate teacher-assistant models to bridge large capacity gaps. The key departure is automating the teacher-assistant selection: rather than requiring exhaustive trial-and-error over candidate scales (as in MMD) or fixed heuristics (as in DGKD), AMD introduces the NPSD metric and a joint optimization framework with parameter sharing across structurally pruned candidates. The structural pruning approach draws on Michel et al. (NeurIPS 2019) for generating candidate architectures, and the overall focus shifts the distillation literature from CNN-centric settings to transformer-based vision models (ViT, Swin), where most prior KD methods were not originally designed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling ~10× compression of large vision transformers with minimal accuracy loss (e.g., 95.52% vs 98.01% teacher on CIFAR-10) at a fraction of the training cost of existing multi-step methods (2.2× vs 20× GPU hours). However, the accuracy gains over a well-tuned manual multi-step baseline (MMD) are marginal (≤0.1%), suggesting the primary value is in automation and efficiency rather than a fundamental performance breakthrough. Open questions include whether the NPSD-based selection generalizes to heterogeneous teacher-student pairs, whether the approach extends to generative or multi-task vision models, and whether the joint optimization strategy scales to even larger compression ratios or deeper transformer stacks.
