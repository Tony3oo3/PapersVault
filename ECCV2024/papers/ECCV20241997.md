## Problem

Self-supervised category-level articulated object pose estimation from a single-frame point cloud remains largely unsolved: prior methods either require shape annotations, multi-frame observations, or rely on iterative part-by-part reconstruction (as in EAP) that sacrifices real-time inference speed. The core difficulty is that part segmentation and part-level pose estimation are ill-posed when inputs vary in both shape and pose simultaneously, making it hard to disentangle joint movement from overall object pose without ground-truth labels.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-frame point cloud of 1024 points (no RGB) of an unknown articulated object within a known category, segmented from an RGB-D image via Mask-RCNN or SAM. The number of parts P and joint types (revolute or prismatic) are provided as known information, with the assumption that each joint connects exactly two parts (J = P−1). The model outputs per-point part segmentation probabilities, per-part rotation and translation, per-joint pivot and direction vectors, and a canonical reconstruction point cloud; all poses are predicted relative to the reconstruction rather than in an absolute frame.

## Method

OP-Align reorders the reconstruction pipeline into two non-iterative phases. In the object-level phase, an E2PN backbone produces SE(3)-equivariant features over 60 icosahedral anchors; a PoseHead selects the anchor minimizing chamfer distance to a canonical reconstruction (generated by a ReconHead plus a learnable category-common base shape), yielding an object-level aligned input that reduces overall pose variance. In the part-level phase, two weight-shared PointNets independently predict segmentation, joint pivots, directions, and joint states for the aligned input and the reconstruction; part-level alignment is then computed by simulating joint movement (rotating or translating each part along its joint axis) to align each input part with its reconstruction counterpart. Training is fully self-supervised via segmentation-weighted density-aware chamfer distance between part-level aligned inputs and the reconstruction, plus regularization terms enforcing fixed canonical joint state, shape stability, local density, minimum part size, and joint-pivot proximity to the object surface. The absence of iterative operations enables real-time inference, and the approach is evaluated on both a synthetic dataset and a newly created real-world RGB-D dataset spanning five articulated object categories.

## Results

On the real-world dataset, OP-Align (self-supervised, no annotations) achieves a part pose mAP of 46.09% at 10°/10cm and 59.76% at 15°/15cm, comparable to the supervised 3DGCN baseline (46.77% and 68.66%) while outperforming supervised PointNet++ (23.25% and 39.82%) by a large margin. For joint estimation, OP-Align reaches 63.59% at 5°/5cm and 14.79% at 15°/15cm, surpassing PointNet++ (57.38% and 4.47%) and approaching 3DGCN (85.79% and 13.07%). On the synthetic dataset, OP-Align significantly outperforms the self-supervised EAP baseline across segmentation IoU, part rotation/translation error, and joint direction/pivot error, while remaining comparable to supervised 3DGCN. OP-Align also achieves real-time inference speed with lower GPU memory usage than EAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the self-supervised EAP baseline [24] on the synthetic dataset, OP-Align significantly outperforms on all metrics (segmentation IoU, part rotation/translation error, joint direction/pivot error), attributed to the object-level alignment reducing pose variance before part-level processing. On the real-world dataset, OP-Align (no annotations) outperforms supervised PointNet++ [33] on nearly all metrics: part pose mAP at 15°/15cm is 59.76% vs. 39.82%, and joint mAP at 5°/5cm is 63.59% vs. 57.38%. Against supervised 3DGCN [22], OP-Align is comparable on part pose (46.09% vs. 46.77% at 10°/10cm) and slightly better on joint 15°/15cm (14.79% vs. 13.07%), but substantially lags on segmentation IoU (23.79% vs. 83.31%) and joint 5°/5cm (63.59% vs. 85.79%), which the authors attribute to the indirect segmentation learning via point-distance differences and the difficulty of recovering exact joint pivots without annotations. NPCS [20] with EPN backbone is reported on the synthetic dataset as an additional supervised reference, with OP-Align achieving comparable or better part-level metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

OP-Align extends the SE(3)-equivariant self-supervised pose estimation framework of SE(3)-eSCOPE [21] from rigid to articulated objects by introducing a two-phase alignment strategy: first reducing overall pose variance via object-level alignment, then simulating joint movement for part-level alignment. It departs conceptually from EAP [24], the only prior self-supervised method for this exact single-frame, annotation-free setting, by replacing EAP's iterative part-by-part reconstruction (with Slot-Attention) with a non-iterative pipeline that reorders the process—holistic alignment before part segmentation—enabling real-time inference. The use of a category-common base shape as a learnable parameter to stabilize reconstruction is a practical addition not present in either predecessor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of real-time inference speed with performance comparable to supervised methods makes self-supervised articulated object pose estimation practically viable for robotics and manipulation applications where annotation is prohibitive. The introduced real-world dataset (5 categories, 6 objects each) addresses a notable gap, as prior work relied almost exclusively on synthetic data that lacks real-world noise and occlusion. Key limitations remain: the method requires known part counts and joint types, struggles with small parts whose movement barely affects overall shape, and predicted joint pivots may not coincide with physical ones. The open question of a universal model that auto-detects parts, joints, and joint types from within-category data is explicitly flagged as future work.
