## Problem

Reconstructing temporally consistent dynamic surfaces from 3D point cloud sequences without correspondences remains challenging because existing supervised methods require ground-truth surfaces and correspondences, have poor generalization, and are limited to fixed-length sequences, while optimization-based approaches depend on depth inputs or pre-established template shapes. The paper tackles the gap of performing this task in a fully unsupervised manner that handles arbitrary sequence lengths, sparse point clouds, and large non-rigid deformations without any shape prior.

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous dynamic 3D point cloud sequence of arbitrary length (no fixed frame count) with unknown inter-frame correspondence and no camera information, and produces a temporally consistent mesh sequence where all frames share identical face connectivity and vertices are explicitly corresponded across time. A keyframe is adaptively selected via minimum aggregate Chamfer distance, and the template surface is jointly refined during deformation so that the final mesh topology is determined by the keyframe's deformable tetrahedron extraction rather than a fixed input mesh.

## Method

DynoSurf operates in two jointly optimized stages: (1) a coarse-to-fine template surface construction using a deformable tetrahedron (DMTet) representation, where an MLP first learns the SDF of the convex hull then refines against the keyframe point cloud via Chamfer distance, normal consistency, and IMLS-SDF losses; and (2) a control-points-blending deformation field where FPS-sampled control points (positions also learned) each carry a per-frame rotation and translation predicted by an MLP, and template vertices are deformed via a convex combination weighted by learned blending coefficients from a second MLP. The key insight is that the blending weights adapt to motion information, allowing spatially close points with different motions to deform independently, while the spectral bias of the MLP ensures smooth spatial-temporal variation. Training is fully unsupervised, using a robust Chamfer distance (with exponential confidence weighting), a robust SDF orientation loss, temporal smoothness regularization, and a shape-preservation term that anchors the keyframe deformation to identity.

## Results

DynoSurf achieves the best performance across all metrics on all three benchmark datasets. On DFAUST, it attains a Chamfer Distance of 0.688×10⁻⁴, Normal Consistency of 0.953, F-0.5% of 0.894, and Correspondence Error of 1.02×10⁻². On the more challenging AMA dataset (human body with large deformations), DynoSurf reduces CD to 0.320×10⁻⁴ and F-1% to 0.943, while supervised baselines degrade severely (CD > 71×10⁻⁴). Notably, DynoSurf is fully unsupervised, requiring no ground-truth surface or correspondence labels, yet outperforms supervised methods by large margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynoSurf outperforms both supervised baselines (LPDC and Cadex) on every metric across all three datasets, despite requiring no ground-truth supervision. On DFAUST, DynoSurf reduces CD by 35% over Cadex (0.688 vs. 1.062) and 72% over LPDC (0.688 vs. 2.430), while also achieving substantially higher F-scores (F-0.5%: 0.894 vs. 0.519 and 0.299). On DT4D, the gap widens dramatically: DynoSurf achieves CD of 1.036×10⁻⁴ versus Cadex's 22.377×10⁻⁴ (a 21× improvement). The most striking result is on AMA, where both supervised methods collapse (CD > 71×10⁻⁴, F-1% < 0.12) while DynoSurf maintains CD of 0.320×10⁻⁴ and F-1% of 0.943, suggesting the supervised methods fail to generalize to the larger deformation ranges and smaller data volume of this dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynoSurf builds on the deformable tetrahedron representation and differentiable marching tetrahedra from DMTet (Shen et al., 2021) to construct a template surface, but departs from prior dynamic reconstruction work in its unsupervised formulation. Optimization-based approaches (Dynamic Fusion, KillingFusion) rely on depth input and frame-by-frame tracking, while learning-based methods (O-Flow, LPDC, Cadex) require ground-truth surfaces and correspondences for supervision and often fix sequence length. The paper's key conceptual departure is combining a learned template with an explicit control-point blending deformation field, where both the template and deformation are jointly optimized without any shape prior or correspondence labels, and where the control points and blending weights are themselves learnable rather than fixed by spatial heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unsupervised nature of DynoSurf is practically significant: it removes the need for ground-truth surfaces, correspondences, and fixed-length sequences, making it applicable to real-world point cloud data where such annotations are unavailable. However, the method's reliance on Chamfer distance for alignment supervision limits its ability to establish correspondences in regions with sparse point clouds or elongated structures undergoing large non-rigid deformations, as the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether incorporating more robust shape-alignment metrics (e.g., F-score-based or normal-aware losses) could close this gap, and whether the template-plus-deformation decomposition generalizes to sequences with topological changes or very long temporal extents.
