## Problem

Adversarial training is the dominant paradigm for robust image classification but is computationally expensive (3.92× wallclock cost per iteration). Prior work showed that simply regularizing the L1 norm of loss-input gradients on natural examples was far inferior to adversarial training, leading to the belief that gradient norm regularization is a much weaker approach. This paper identifies that the failure of gradient norm regularization was caused by non-smooth ReLU activations and demonstrates that it becomes highly effective on modern architectures with smooth non-linearities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K and outputs one of 1000 class labels. Critically, training operates exclusively on natural (unperturbed) examples, requiring no adversarial perturbation generation. The gradient norm penalty is computed via double backpropagation through the network, and for the edge-alignment regularizer, Sobel-filter-derived oriented energy maps of the input are used as targets for cosine similarity with class gradients.

## Method

The core training objective adds an L1-norm penalty on the loss-input gradient (∇ₓL_CE) to the standard cross-entropy loss, optimized via double backpropagation on natural examples only. The key insight is that this regularization critically depends on activation smoothness: on ResNet-50 with ReLU it fails catastrophically, but with GeLU or SiLU it achieves robust accuracy within ~5% of state-of-the-art PGD-3 adversarial training on Swin Transformers (51.58% vs. 56.12% AutoAttack-L∞ at ε=4/255) while using only 60% of the computational cost. A secondary contribution shows that aligning class gradients to image edges (via cosine similarity with Sobel-filter oriented energy) independently yields 60% of SOTA robustness without any norm regularization, suggesting perceptual alignment directly causes robustness. Experiments use Swin-B pretrained from timm with the Liu et al. training recipe (RandAugment, mixup, AdamW, model averaging) over 100 epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with Swin Transformer, Gradient Norm Regularization achieves 51.58% AutoAttack-L∞ robust accuracy at ε=4/255, compared to 56.12% for state-of-the-art PGD-3 Adversarial Training, while using only 60% of the computational cost (5 network passes vs. 8 per batch). At smaller perturbation strengths, the gap narrows to 1.18% at ε=2/255 and 0.42% at ε=1/255. Clean accuracy is 77.78% (GradNorm) vs. 77.20% (Adversarial Training) vs. 84.19% (Natural Training). Additionally, a separate edge-alignment regularizer (without any norm penalty) achieves 35.02% robust accuracy, representing 60% of SOTA performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the SOTA Adversarial Training (PGD-3) from Liu et al. [32], which achieves 56.12% AutoAttack-L∞ robust accuracy at ε=4/255. Gradient Norm Regularization achieves 51.58%, a gap of 4.54 percentage points (roughly 92% of SOTA robustness), while requiring only 60% of the computational cost and no inner maximization loop. At smaller perturbation strengths the gap shrinks dramatically: 0.42% at ε=1/255 and 1.18% at ε=2/255. Both methods achieve nearly identical clean accuracy (77.78% vs. 77.20%), and both smoothly degrade to 0% as ε grows unboundedly. The paper also demonstrates that on ResNet-50, replacing ReLU with smooth activations (GeLU/SiLU) is critical: Gradient Norm Regularization jumps from 6.82% to 30.58% robust accuracy, while Adversarial Training only improves by ~1%, highlighting that activation smoothness is the key architectural factor enabling this approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits the gradient norm regularization objective originally formulated by Simon-Gabriel et al. [44] and optimized via double backpropagation by Ross & Doshi-Velez [40], which prior works (Finlay & Oberman, Seck et al., Jakubovitz & Giryes) had shown to be non-competitive with adversarial training on ImageNet. The key departure is identifying activation function smoothness as the critical missing factor: on ReLU-based CNNs the regularization fails, but on modern Swin Transformers with GeLU/SiLU it recovers >90% of state-of-the-art robustness. The paper also inverts the perceptually-aligned-gradients literature [25, 42, 45] by asking whether enforcing gradient-to-edge alignment *causes* robustness rather than merely correlating with it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can obtain near-SOTA adversarial robustness with 60% of the compute cost and no inner-loop adversarial optimization, simply by choosing smooth activations and adding an L1 gradient norm penalty. The finding that activation smoothness is the decisive architectural factor (23% robustness gain from ReLU→GeLU for gradient norm vs. 1% for adversarial training) reframes how one thinks about robustness-friendly architecture design. Open questions include what closes the remaining ~5% gap to full adversarial training, whether the unexplained green-channel sparsity in adversarially trained gradients reflects a deeper structural property, and whether edge-aligned architectures can achieve robustness even under standard (non-robust) training.
