## Problem

Existing federated learning dataset distillation methods (e.g., FedDM) perform distillation on client devices and upload synthetic data to the server, compromising privacy and failing to generalize across heterogeneous model architectures. No prior framework enables server-side distillation that leverages generative priors while allowing clients to use smaller surrogate models and the server to train a larger global model, all while transmitting only model parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242075/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Clients upload only trained surrogate model parameters (a 5-layer ConvNet) after local SGD on their private RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNet subsets at 128×128); no raw or synthetic data leaves the client. The server consumes these parameters alongside a pre-trained StyleGAN-XL to produce a small synthetic dataset (10 images per class by default) in the GAN's latent space, which is then used to train a larger global classifier (ConvNet, ResNet18/34, VGG11, or ViT) whose architecture can differ entirely from the local surrogate.

## Method

FedDGM is a server-centric FL framework where, each round, clients train compact surrogate models locally and upload only their parameters. The server distills per-client synthetic data via an adaptive Matching Training Trajectories (MTT) loss that aligns a student network's training trajectory on synthetic data with the uploaded client parameters, but crucially optimizes the synthetic data in the latent space of a pre-trained StyleGAN-XL rather than in pixel space, yielding more realistic and architecture-generalizable samples. The server aggregates all per-client synthetic sets into a single multi-modal dataset and trains both the global model and a global surrogate model on it, then broadcasts the updated surrogate back to clients. A theoretical analysis via a bilevel SDE formulation shows that in the asymptotic limit the procedure converges to the ideal of centralized training on the full heterogeneous dataset, with the distilled data distribution concentrating on the intersection of per-client optimal solution sets.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with extreme non-i.i.d. partitioning (α=0.01), FedDGM achieves 63.4% average accuracy across architectures (ConvNet, ResNet18, VGG11, ViT), compared to 45.3% for FedDM, 20.4% for FedAvg, 19.7% for FedProx, and 13.3% for FedNova. On ImageNet subsets (128×128), FedDGM achieves 64.1% average on ImageWoof (ResNet34: 72.7%) and 59.7% on ImageMeow, consistently outperforming all baselines. The method also converges faster than aggregation-based baselines, reaching comparable accuracy in fewer communication rounds due to training on a unified multi-modal synthetic distribution rather than averaging heterogeneous local parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDGM outperforms all four baselines across every dataset, architecture, and heterogeneity level tested. Against the aggregation-based methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova), the gap is dramatic under extreme non-i.i.d. conditions: at α=0.01 on CIFAR-10, FedDGM achieves 63.4% average accuracy versus 20.4% (FedAvg), 19.7% (FedProx), and 13.3% (FedNova) — a 40%+ absolute improvement. Against the closest competitor, FedDM (the only other dataset-distillation-based FL method), FedDGM leads by 18.1 points at α=0.01 (63.4% vs. 45.3%) and by 2.3 points at the milder α=0.5 setting (67.5% vs. 65.2%). On high-resolution ImageNet subsets, FedDGM's advantage over FedDM is even more pronounced: on ImageWoof with ResNet34, FedDGM reaches 72.7% versus FedDM's 60.3% (+12.4 points), and on ImageMeow with ResNet34, 65.1% vs. 61.2%. The advantage grows monotonically as data heterogeneity increases, while FedDGM maintains relatively stable performance (63.4% at α=0.01 vs. 67.5% at α=0.5) whereas all baselines degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset distillation (DD) paradigm into federated learning by relocating the distillation process from client devices to the server, departing from FedDM which performs DD locally and uploads synthetic data. It builds directly on Matching Training Trajectories (Cazenavette et al., CVPR 2022) for trajectory-based distillation and on the deep generative prior approach (Cazenavette et al., 2023) for optimizing in latent space, but repurposes both for a server-centric FL setting where only model parameters (not data) cross the network. The theoretical analysis connects the bilevel DD optimization to an asymptotic equivalence with centralized training on the full heterogeneous dataset, a framing not present in prior FL+DD work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The server-side design addresses two practical pain points simultaneously: it eliminates the privacy risk of uploading synthetic data (only surrogate model parameters are shared) and enables architecture heterogeneity, letting resource-constrained clients train small models while the server trains a larger global model. The consistent 18% improvement over FedDM and the architecture-agnostic generalization (5-layer ConvNet locally → ResNet/ViT globally) make this a practically deployable framework. Open questions include the dependence on a domain-specific pre-trained generative model (StyleGAN-XL), the hand-waviness of the SDE-based theoretical analysis (the authors themselves call it 'idealistic formalism'), and whether the approach extends beyond image classification to other modalities or tasks where suitable generative priors are unavailable.
