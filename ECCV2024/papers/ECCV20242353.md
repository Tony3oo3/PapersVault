## Problem

In distributed semantic segmentation, the cloud side must run both a source decoder (to reconstruct bottleneck features) and a task decoder (to produce segmentation masks), making per-channel computational cost a bottleneck for scaling to millions of edge devices. Prior SOTA (Ahuja et al., CVPR 2023) already reduced cloud complexity by compressing features rather than images, but still requires a separate source decoder, leaving substantial room for further efficiency gains without sacrificing rate-distortion performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242353/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The edge device processes an RGB image (513×513 for COCO-2017, 769×769 for Cityscapes) through a ResNet-50 encoder followed by a hyperprior-based source encoder, producing a quantized latent bitstream (b_r, b_h) that is transmitted to the cloud. The cloud's joint decoder outputs a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over 21 classes (COCO, PASCAL-VOC subset) or 19 classes (Cityscapes). Notably, the original image cannot be reconstructed from the transmitted features, providing inherent data protection, and the cloud receives only the compressed bottleneck representation rather than the full image.

## Method

The key architectural change is replacing the separate source decoder (SD) and task decoder (D) with a single joint decoder (JD) that directly consumes the quantized latent features (b_r, b_h) and produces the segmentation mask in one pass, eliminating the intermediate feature reconstruction step. The JD contains an ASPP block with dilated convolutions (d=5,10,15) and upsampling layers, and is trained end-to-end with the source encoder under a standard rate-distortion objective (α·cross-entropy + (1−α)·expected bitrate) without any auxiliary loss to explicitly enforce source decoding. To recover high-bitrate performance lost from the smaller network, the paper introduces training-time over-parameterization in the ASPP subblocks: K parallel dilated-convolution branches plus one pointwise-convolution branch are summed during training but merged into a single convolution at inference (K=1 for COCO, K=3 for Cityscapes). This yields a cloud DNN with only 1.66M parameters (COCO) and 4.92M (Cityscapes) versus 16.78M and 42.44M for the prior SOTA, while matching or exceeding its mIoU across the full bitrate range.

## Results

On COCO-2017, the proposed joint decoder (JD) uses only 1.66M parameters and 10G FLOPs in the cloud, compared to 16.78M parameters and 521G FLOPs for the previous SOTA (Ahuja et al.), representing 9.8% of parameters and 1.9% of FLOPs. On Cityscapes, JD uses 4.92M parameters and 39G FLOPs versus 42.44M and 1366G for the SOTA (11.59% and 2.8%, respectively). In terms of RD performance (mIoU vs. bpp), the proposed method achieves SOTA over a wide range of bitrates on both datasets, reaching the No-Compression DeepLabv3 performance at 0.65 bpp on COCO while using K=1 over-parameterization (COCO) and K=3 (Cityscapes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed JD outperforms the previous SOTA (Ahuja et al., CVPR 2023) on COCO across the full bitrate range and is comparable on Cityscapes over a wide range of bitrates, while using only 9.8–11.59% of the cloud parameters. Against conventional image-compression baselines (JPEG, HEVC, and the learned codec of Song et al.), the proposed feature-compression approach with joint decoding achieves substantially better mIoU at equivalent bitrates on both datasets. The JSDE baseline (Torfason et al.) and the feature-compression methods (Singh et al., Matsubara et al.) are also outperformed, confirming that joint source-and-task decoding with re-parameterizable ASPP branches yields superior RD trade-offs. On COCO, the method reaches the No-Compression DeepLabv3 upper bound at 0.65 bpp, whereas on Cityscapes it remains close to the No-Compression curve at higher bitrates. The edge-device computational complexity is kept identical to the Ahuja et al. SOTA (same ResNet-50 encoder + source encoder split point), so all gains come from the cloud-side efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the distributed semantic segmentation line initiated by Ahuja et al. (CVPR 2023), which compresses bottleneck features from a ResNet-50 encoder using a hyperprior-based source codec (following Ballé et al.) and decodes them in the cloud with separate source and task decoders. The key architectural departure is merging the source decoder and task decoder into a single joint decoder (JD) that directly consumes quantized latents, eliminating the explicit feature reconstruction step. Additionally, the authors repurpose the training-time over-parameterization trick from RepVGG (Ding et al., CVPR 2021)—previously applied only to image encoders—into the ASPP block of the joint decoder to recover high-bitrate performance without increasing inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for edge-cloud deployments: using only 9.8–11.59% of the cloud DNN parameters of the prior SOTA while matching or exceeding its mIoU across a wide bitrate range makes large-scale serving of millions of edge devices computationally feasible. A notable limitation is that the method is restricted to DeepLabv3, which is no longer SOTA in general semantic segmentation; the authors explicitly call for adapting modern transformer-based or foundation-model segmentation architectures to the distributed setting. An open question is whether the joint-decoding principle (consuming quantized latents without an explicit reconstruction loss) generalizes to architectures with encoder-decoder skip connections or to other downstream tasks like object detection.
