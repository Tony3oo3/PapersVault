## Problem

Existing world model pre-training methods (APV, ContextWM) pre-train as action-free video prediction models, which only capture transition dependencies between sequential states. However, the final world model is action-conditional and must model the causality connecting actions to state transitions. This architectural mismatch between pre-training and fine-tuning limits the effectiveness of unsupervised pre-training in improving world model learning efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240601/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes action-free RGB video sequences (from RLBench or Something-Something-V2) and an inverse dynamics encoder extracts implicit action representations from pairs of adjacent frames. During fine-tuning, real 4-dimensional action vectors from the Meta-world environment are mapped into the same implicit action space via a learned action encoder, and the model outputs reconstructed future observations, predicted rewards, and a control policy. The visual backbone uses 13-layer ResNets for both encoding and decoding, with the action encoder implemented as a 2-layer MLP (1024 hidden units, 64 output units).

## Method

PreLAR pre-trains the world model in an action-conditional manner by encoding observations at two adjacent timesteps into a learnable implicit action representation via an inverse dynamics encoder, then conditioning an RSSM-based transition model on this representation. Two self-supervised losses constrain the implicit action: an action KL loss regularizes it toward a standard normal distribution (preventing shortcut learning via information bottleneck), and a novel action-state consistency loss enforces that the ordering of pairwise action differences matches the ordering of pairwise predicted-state differences within a minibatch, using a sign-function proxy for the non-differentiable argsort operator. During fine-tuning, a lightweight action encoder maps real environment actions into the pre-trained implicit action space, and a reward predictor is added, while the representation, transition, and decoder modules are initialized from pre-training. Policy learning then proceeds with DreamerV2-style actor-critic on imagined trajectories from the fine-tuned world model.

## Results

The paper evaluates PreLAR on six Meta-World manipulation tasks (e.g., Drawer Open, Lever Pull, Door Lock) using interquartile mean with bootstrap confidence intervals over 8 runs. PreLAR consistently achieves higher final performance and better sample efficiency than DreamerV2 (no pre-training), APV, and ContextWM on most tasks, with the most pronounced gains on Drawer Open and Lever Pull. When pre-trained on the more challenging real-world SSv2 dataset and transferred to Meta-World, PreLAR still generally outperforms APV and ContextWM, though it underperforms APV on the Door Lock task due to negative transfer from domain mismatch. Ablation (Fig. 5b) shows that naive fine-tuning of an action-free pre-trained model performs on par with DreamerV2, while PreLAR's action-conditional pre-training clearly surpasses both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreLAR is compared against three baselines: DreamerV2 (trained from scratch without pre-training), APV (action-free video pre-training), and ContextWM (context-aware action-free pre-training). PreLAR outperforms DreamerV2 on most of the six Meta-World tasks, confirming that pre-training from action-free videos is beneficial for world model learning. Against APV and ContextWM, PreLAR achieves superior final performance and faster convergence on the majority of tasks, with particularly large margins on Drawer Open and Lever Pull, which the authors attribute to higher similarity between pre-training demonstrations and those tasks. Under the harder cross-domain setting (SSv2 pre-training → Meta-World fine-tuning), PreLAR still generally beats both APV and ContextWM, but loses to APV on Door Lock, likely due to negative transfer from the large visual domain gap. The most revealing comparison is the ablation in Fig. 5b: a naive fine-tuning scheme that simply fine-tunes an action-free pre-trained model (matching APV's architecture) performs on par with DreamerV2, whereas PreLAR's action-conditional pre-training clearly outperforms both, directly demonstrating that incorporating learnable action representation during pre-training is the key factor bridging the pre-training/fine-tuning gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the world model pre-training line initiated by APV (Seo et al., 2022) and improved by ContextWM (Wu et al., 2023), both of which pre-train an action-free latent video prediction model before stacking an action-conditional dynamics model on top during fine-tuning. PreLAR departs from this by making the pre-training itself action-conditional: it introduces an inverse dynamics encoder that infers an implicit action representation from consecutive frames, so the pre-trained architecture matches the final action-conditional world model (RSSM-based, following DreamerV2). The action-state consistency loss, which enforces ordering preservation between action differences and predicted state differences, is a novel self-supervised signal not present in prior pre-training work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete architectural mismatch in world model pre-training that had been overlooked: action-free pre-training captures transition dependencies but not the causal action-to-state mapping that the downstream world model requires. The ablation showing that naive fine-tuning of an action-free pre-trained model performs on par with no pre-training at all (DreamerV2) is a useful cautionary result for the field. However, the action representation is inferred from only two consecutive frames, and the evaluation is limited to six Meta-world manipulation tasks, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer-horizon or higher-dimensional action spaces and whether richer temporal context could yield more faithful implicit actions.
