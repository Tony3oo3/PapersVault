## Problem

Learning structured 3D shape abstractions as interpretable primitive assemblies from sparse, disparate RGB views remains unsolved. Prior primitive-assembly methods (CSG trees, superquadric fitting) require 3D inputs such as voxels or point clouds, while sparse-view reconstruction methods (NeRF, Gaussian splatting) produce unstructured representations that do not reflect shape semantics and cannot be easily edited. No existing approach can recover a meaningful, editable union-of-convexes decomposition from as few as three RGB images without any 3D supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–6 RGB images from significantly different viewpoints (as low as 64×64 for ShapeNet, 300×400 for DTU) along with pre-estimated camera poses and binary object masks. It outputs a hierarchical 3D primitive assembly: 4,096 convex quadric primitives (parameterized as |a|x²+|b|y²+|c|z²+dx+ey+fz+g=0) grouped via a learnable selection matrix into 256 convexes (each an intersection of quadrics), which are then unioned to form the final shape. The resulting assembly is textured and directly exportable to CAD tools (MeshLab, OpenSCAD) for part-level editing.

## Method

DPA-Net replaces the density-prediction branch of an image-conditioned NeRF (pixelNeRF-style) with a Differentiable Primitive Assembly (DPA) module that outputs a 3D occupancy field serving as opacity for volume rendering. Multi-view image features from a ResNet34 encoder are fused via weighted pooling into a single shape feature, which an MLP decodes into quadric primitive parameters; two CSG-based layers (learnable intersection via a selection matrix T, then union via min-pooling) assemble these into the occupancy field. Training uses only image-space losses (RGB photo-consistency + mask) with no 3D supervision, followed by a three-phase test-time adaptation that progressively tightens the assembly: float T/w → exact occupancy with exp(-10a*) opacity → binary T with an overlap-penalty loss. Additional improvements include silhouette-aware adaptive pixel sampling (1,000 contour pixels + 256 random) to recover thin structures, and a primitive dropout mechanism that removes up to 29% redundant quadrics by measuring shape variation upon exclusion.

## Results

On the ShapeNet chair benchmark (category-specific), DPA-Net achieves CD=0.79, NC=0.78, ECD=4.01 with 8.15 parts, outperforming EMS by 88%/17%/68% on CD/NC/ECD while using 45% fewer parts. In the category-agnostic setting across 13 ShapeNet categories, DPA-Net reduces CD by ~60% (1.47 vs 3.67) and ECD by ~55% (4.91 vs 11.01) compared to pixelNeRF. On the DTU real-image benchmark, DPA-Net achieves a mean CD of 3.41 with 7.33 parts, yielding 18% lower CD than DBW (4.12) while producing structured, editable primitive assemblies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against EMS (superquadric fitting from 3D points), DPA-Net wins decisively on CD and ECD across all three evaluation settings (e.g., CD 0.79 vs 6.93 on ShapeNet chairs; 1.47 vs 5.15 category-agnostic; 3.41 vs 4.75 on DTU) while also using fewer parts in the chair setting. Against pixelNeRF, DPA-Net is slightly worse on the ShapeNet chair benchmark (CD 0.79 vs 0.50) but substantially better in the category-agnostic setting (CD 1.47 vs 3.67, a ~60% improvement) and on DTU (CD 3.41 vs 3.01 is close, but DPA-Net outputs structured primitives vs. unstructured mesh). Against DBW on DTU, DPA-Net achieves 18% lower mean CD (3.41 vs 4.12) at the cost of more parts (7.33 vs 3.83). Against DiViNet on DTU, DiViNet achieves the lowest CD (1.90) but produces unstructured, non-editable surfaces, whereas DPA-Net's structured output enables downstream CAD editing and shape generation tasks that DiViNet cannot support.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPA-Net sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: image-conditioned NeRFs (pixelNeRF, IBRNet) for sparse-view 3D reconstruction, and CSG-based primitive assembly (CAPRI-Net, BSP-Net) for structured 3D shape representation. The key departure is that prior primitive-abstraction methods (UCSG, CAPRI-Net, D²CSG) all require 3D inputs such as voxels or point clouds, while prior sparse-view NeRF methods produce unstructured density fields. DPA-Net replaces the NeRF density branch with a differentiable CSG assembly (intersection of quadrics → union of convexes) whose predicted occupancies serve directly as volume-rendering opacities, enabling training with image-space losses alone. Only three very recent works (PartNeRF, ISCO, DBW) had attempted 3D primitive abstraction from RGB without 3D supervision, but with limited primitive types or poor sparse-view performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured output (a union of convexes assembled via interpretable CSG operations) opens a path to directly editable 3D abstractions from as few as three disparate views, which unstructured NeRF/Gaussian outputs cannot provide. This has practical implications for CAD workflows (MeshLab, OpenSCAD editing) and for using abstractions as structural prompts in text-conditioned 3D generation (SPiC-E). Key limitations remain: the method requires GT camera poses and object masks, handles only convex primitives (struggling with concave shapes), and texture quality is secondary to geometry. Open questions include adding difference operations to the differentiable assembly, robustness to noisy pose estimates, and whether the silhouette-aware sampling and primitive dropout strategies generalize to other abstraction frameworks.
