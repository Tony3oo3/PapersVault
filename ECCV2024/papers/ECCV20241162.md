## Problem

Memory-efficient transfer learning (METL) methods avoid backpropagating through large backbones but suffer from two overlooked issues: cross-layer feature redundancy during aggregation dilutes small yet discriminative semantic cohorts, and the complete bypass of pre-trained layers forfeits the regulation capability that parameter-efficient methods exploit, creating an inherent accuracy gap. No prior METL approach addresses both the redundancy interference in cross-layer consolidation and the lost pre-trained input-output transformation simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241162/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SHERL consumes intermediate hidden states from all shallow layers (layers 1 through N-1) of a frozen pre-trained backbone, which are down-projected to a reduced dimension d = D/r before aggregation. The consolidated representation is up-projected back to the original dimension and injected into the last deep layer via a learnable gate, after which standard task-specific heads produce the final output (retrieval similarity scores, VQA answers, grounding boxes, or NLP classification labels). The module is fully detached from the backbone, so it can be attached to CNNs, single/cross-modal Transformers, or Encoder-Decoder architectures without architectural modification.

## Method

SHERL introduces a Multi-Tiered Sensing Adapter (MTSA) that decouples adaptation into an early anti-redundancy aggregation route and a late pre-trained regulation route. In the early route, cross-layer features are mapped to a low-dimensional space, channel-modulated via learned Sigmoid gates, then aggregated using linear attention where attention scores are divided by per-token redundancy rates (computed as row sums of ReLU-rectified cosine similarity matrices), preventing large semantic cohorts from dominating over small discriminative ones. In the late route, the aggregated features are up-projected and passed through the last frozen pre-trained layer with a tanh gate (zero-initialized) controlling the mixing ratio between enhanced and original inputs, thereby leveraging the backbone's learned input-output projection for domain-specific regulation. The entire MTSA module is unbound from the backbone, requiring no gradient flow through the large network and reducing training memory by 40–84% compared to PETL baselines while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with T5-base, SHERL achieves 84.3% average accuracy with only 2.9 GB training memory (84% reduction vs. Fully-FT's 17.6 GB), outperforming LST (84.1%) with 47% less memory and half the trainable parameters. Scaling to T5-large and T5-3B, SHERL reaches 87.5% and 88.2% average respectively, consistently exceeding LST (87.1%, 88.1%) while saving 42% and 3% training memory. On vision-language tasks, SHERL surpasses LST/UniPT by 4.1/1.1% R@1 on image-text retrieval (Flickr30K Rsum: 542.3 vs. 529.5/537.4) and by 20.8/3.3% on visual grounding (RefCOCO Val: 83.02 vs. 81.63/82.71), approaching Fully-FT performance with minimal memory overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PETL baselines on T5-base GLUE, SHERL (84.3% avg) trails Adapter and LoRA (both 85.3%) by ~1% but uses 78% less training memory (2.9 GB vs. 13.0/12.6 GB) and half the trainable parameters; it substantially outperforms Prompt Tuning (72.2%) and matches BitFit (84.1%) with far lower memory. Against the primary METL competitor LST, SHERL wins on all three T5 scales (84.3 vs. 84.1 on base, 87.5 vs. 87.1 on large, 88.2 vs. 88.1 on 3B) while using roughly half the trainable parameters and 42–47% less training memory on T5-base/large. On VL tasks, SHERL consistently outperforms both LST and UniPT: on Flickr30K image-text retrieval it gains +12.8/+4.9 Rsum over LST/UniPT, on MSR-VTT video-text retrieval +6.4/+2.4 Rsum, on VQAv2 +0.38/+0.29 accuracy, and on RefCOCO visual grounding +1.39/+0.31 Val accuracy. UniPT is the closest competitor on VL tasks, with SHERL matching its training memory (e.g., 3.4×4 GB on VTR) while delivering modest but consistent accuracy gains. Overall, SHERL achieves the best accuracy–memory trade-off, approaching Fully-FT accuracy with 66–84% training memory reduction across all seven backbone architectures tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHERL extends the memory-efficient transfer learning (METL) line initiated by Side-Tuning, LST (sequential bottom-up side network), and UniPT (parallel top-down side module), but departs from both by identifying and addressing two overlooked issues: cross-layer feature redundancy during aggregation and the absence of pre-trained layer regulation. Specifically, it introduces a cosine-similarity-based anti-redundancy normalization (inspired by attention mechanisms) in the early aggregation route, and routes the consolidated features through a minimal number of frozen pre-trained layers for 'compulsive regulation' in the late route, borrowing the gating mechanism from LST. The Multi-Tiered Sensing Adapter (MTSA) module is explicitly designed to be backbone-agnostic and unbound from the base network, positioning SHERL as a composable layer that can also cooperate with PETL methods like LoRA and Adapter Tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHERL offers a practically significant efficiency win—reducing training memory by ~84% while maintaining near-fully-fine-tuning accuracy across 7 backbone architectures and 18 datasets—making large-model adaptation feasible on consumer or single-GPU hardware. The anti-redundancy insight (that cross-layer feature cohorts of different sizes bias aggregation weights) is a useful diagnostic for anyone designing side-network or multi-layer fusion strategies. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger generative models (LLMs, diffusion models), whether the fixed single-layer late regulation is optimal for very deep backbones, and how the method interacts with emerging techniques like quantized or sparse fine-tuning. The paper also notes that combining SHERL with Prompt Tuning can be counterproductive, suggesting non-trivial interactions between different parameter-efficient strategies that remain unexplored.
