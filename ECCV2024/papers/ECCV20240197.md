## Problem

Current 3D visual grounding models localize objects based on explicit descriptions of category, attributes, and spatial relationships, but cannot interpret implicit human instructions that require reasoning about intent, function, or context (e.g., 'I am thirsty, can I have something to drink?'). This paper introduces the task of 3D reasoning grounding, which demands that a model jointly reason over a complex implicit query and the 3D environment before predicting target object locations, and provides the ScanReason benchmark (12,929 QA-location pairs across five reasoning types) to evaluate this capability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene and an implicit free-form text query as input. It outputs 3D bounding boxes for a flexible number of target objects (any object satisfying the query's requirements) along with a textual answer and explanation. The visual reasoning module consumes multi-view 2D images back-projected into 3D space via a Q-Former, while the grounding module directly accesses the raw 3D point cloud for fine-grained geometric details, creating a complementary two-level scene representation.

## Method

ReGround3D decouples reasoning from grounding into two modules: a visual-centric reasoning module (3D-LLM based on BLIP-2) that jointly reasons over the scene and instruction and predicts a special <LOC> token encoding the target object's semantics and rough location, and a 3D grounding module that uses this token embedding in cross-attention with point-cloud features to select relevant object queries and decode precise 3D bounding boxes. The key design choice is that the MLLM never directly outputs coordinates; instead, the <LOC> token acts as a soft grounding query that guides a geometry-enhanced 'look-back' into the 3D scene, capturing fine-grained spatial details the 2D-projected visual tokens lack. A Chain-of-Grounding (CoG) mechanism further interleaves reasoning and grounding steps during inference: the model first grounds explicitly mentioned objects, inserts their 3D information back into the query, and re-runs reasoning to refine the final target prediction. Training is end-to-end with a weighted sum of next-token prediction loss and 3D detection loss (IoU + contrastive), using LoRA to efficiently fine-tune the 3D-LLM while preserving its pretrained scene understanding.

## Results

On the proposed ScanReason benchmark (Acc@0.25), ReGround3D with Chain-of-Grounding (CoG) achieves 30.62 overall, outperforming the strongest MLLM baseline Chat3D-v2 (14.98) by 15.6 points and 3D-LLM(vg) (13.29) by 17.3 points. On the traditional 3D visual grounding task (ScanRefer), ReGround3D achieves 53.1 Acc@0.25 and 41.1 Acc@0.5, surpassing the best specialist method L3Det (52.8/40.2) and all generalized MLLMs (best: Chat3D-v2 at 35.9/30.4). The CoG mechanism further improves overall ScanReason performance from 28.98 to 30.62, with the largest gains on spatial reasoning (+1.73) and logical reasoning (+2.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGround3D(CoG) decisively outperforms all three MLLM baselines on ScanReason: Chat3D-v2 (14.98), Mask3D+InternLM2-7B (14.86), and 3D-LLM(vg) (13.29), with margins of 15.6, 15.8, and 17.3 points respectively. The Mask3D+InternLM2-7B baseline shows a notable strength in functional reasoning (36.12) but is severely limited in spatial reasoning (10.34), highlighting the difficulty of encoding 3D spatial relationships as text. On the traditional ScanRefer grounding task, ReGround3D (53.1 Acc@0.25) surpasses all specialist methods including L3Det (52.8), BUTD-DETR (52.2), and ViL3DRel (47.9), as well as all generalized MLLMs (Chat3D-v2 at 35.9, 3D-LLM(vg) at 33.1, LLM-Grounder at 17.1). Even without ScanReason in training (ReGround3D*, 23.27), the method still exceeds all baselines by at least 8.3 points, indicating the architecture's generalization beyond the benchmark. The CoG mechanism provides consistent gains across all five reasoning categories, with the largest improvements on spatial (+1.73) and logical reasoning (+2.12), confirming that interleaved grounding feedback is most beneficial when spatial relationships are central to the question.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D-LLM (Hong et al., NeurIPS 2024) as its visual-centric reasoning backbone, extending it with a special <LOC> token and a separate 3D grounding module that 'looks back' to a point-cloud encoder for fine-grained geometry. It departs from prior 3D visual grounding methods (ScanRefer, BUTD-DETR, L3Det) that rely on explicit object descriptions, and from MLLM-based approaches (3D-LLM, Chat3D-v2) that attempt to output coordinates directly, by decoupling reasoning from localization into two specialized modules. The Chain-of-Grounding mechanism adapts the Chain-of-Thought paradigm to interleave reasoning and grounding steps, drawing conceptual inspiration from CoT3DRef's anchor-object chaining but operating at a higher level of instruction complexity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ScanReason benchmark (12.9K pairs, 5 reasoning categories) provides the first systematic evaluation of whether embodied agents can ground objects from implicit, reasoning-heavy instructions rather than explicit descriptions—a capability critical for practical human-robot interaction. The authors acknowledge that the three high-level reasoning categories (logical, emotional, safety) exhibit question overlaps, and the overall Acc@0.25 of ~30% indicates the task remains far from solved. Open questions include whether GPT-4-assisted annotation introduces systematic biases, whether the CoG mechanism scales to genuinely multi-step spatial reasoning, and whether the approach transfers to real-time embodied settings beyond static ScanNet scenes.
