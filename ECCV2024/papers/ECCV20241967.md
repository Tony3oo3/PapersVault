## Problem

Existing visual tokenization methods either use fixed grid-shaped patches that ignore uneven information density and natural semantic boundaries, or rely on object-level detection that captures high-level semantics but loses the low-level visual details needed for faithful reconstruction. This paper asks whether an ideal visual token representation can simultaneously respect both the non-uniform information density and the inherent semantic boundaries present in natural images.

## Input / Output

Reader takes a single RGB image (256×256 on ImageNet, 512×512 on CelebAMask-HQ) and produces an adaptive-length sequence of region-based tokens, where each token pairs a binary mask (encoding shape, position, scale) with a learned embedding (encoding visual content). The number of tokens is not fixed but determined by image complexity—simpler images yield fewer regions. The decoder then reconstructs the full-resolution image from this variable-length token sequence, and the same tokens can be manipulated (erased, swapped, geometrically transformed) to produce edited images.

## Method

Reader comprises an encoder and a cross-attention decoder. The encoder uses a ViT (patch size 8×8) with an FPN as a feature extractor, followed by a Transformer decoder with N learnable region queries that segment the image into non-overlapping regions via an argmax over a similarity matrix, yielding both region embeddings and binary masks. A region embedder then fuses visual features (via straight-through differentiable pooling and masked cross-attention) with geometric information (mask flattened and projected through a linear layer) to produce the final tokens. The decoder uses M learnable patch queries with positional embeddings to attend over the variable-length token sequence via cross-attention, mapping back to pixel space. Training combines VQGAN-style reconstruction losses (L1, perceptual, GAN) with a cross-entropy segmentation loss supervised by SAM pseudo-annotations or manual masks, using Hungarian matching to handle the one-to-one correspondence between predicted regions and ground-truth masks.

## Results

On ImageNet validation (256×256), Reader achieves PSNR 24.75, SSIM 0.72, LPIPS 0.20, and rFID 2.43 using only 79.09 tokens per image on average, compared to 256 tokens for all grid-based baselines. This represents a 3.2× reduction in token count while improving rFID by 2.5–5.6 points over the best baselines (DQ-VAE: 4.08–7.32; VQGAN-16384: 4.92). Reader also uses only 26.60M parameters and 40.24G FLOPs, roughly 1/3 the compute of the baselines. On the CelebAMask-HQ smile-transferring benchmark (512×512), Reader achieves FID 37.91 and ID Score 0.8470, outperforming all GAN-based editing baselines on image quality and identity preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three grid-based reconstruction baselines on ImageNet, Reader dominates on every metric while using roughly one-third the tokens: it improves rFID from 4.92 (VQGAN-16384) and 5.24 (RQ-VAE) to 2.43, and from 4.08–7.32 (DQ-VAE, the only other dynamic-length method) to a single value of 2.43. PSNR jumps from ~19.4–20.0 to 24.75 and SSIM from ~0.48–0.50 to 0.72, indicating substantially sharper reconstructions. On the smile-transferring benchmark, Reader achieves the best FID (37.91 vs. 39.42 for InterFaceGAN and 40.83 for EditGAN+30) and the best ID Score (0.8470 vs. 0.7452 for EditGAN+30), confirming that region-level token manipulation preserves identity far better than GAN latent-space editing. Reader's Attribute Accuracy (84.2%) is slightly below EditGAN+30 (85.8%) but well above MaskGAN (77.3%) and LocalEditing (26.0%), showing a minor trade-off in semantic correctness for a large gain in perceptual quality and identity fidelity. Overall, Reader wins on reconstruction quality, efficiency (parameters and FLOPs), and editing quality (FID, ID Score), with only a marginal loss in smile-detection accuracy relative to the strongest GAN baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

Reader departs from the dominant grid-based tokenization paradigm (VQGAN, RQ-VAE, DQ-VAE) where each token corresponds to a fixed-size patch, and from object-based tokenization (Bottom-Up Attention, GroupViT) that relies on external detection models and discards low-level detail. It builds on the VQGAN autoencoder framework and Transformer-based segmentation (DETR, Mask2Former) for its region decoder, while borrowing the straight-through estimator from VQVAE and the region-aggregation idea from GroupViT. The key conceptual departure is learning arbitrarily-shaped, non-overlapping regions end-to-end as the fundamental token unit, rather than using pre-defined grid patches or externally detected object boxes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The region-native formulation offers a principled way to handle the non-uniform information density in natural images, and the disentanglement property means diverse editing operations (erasing, adding, replacing, shape modification) emerge without any additional modules or training data. However, the current evaluation is constrained to a ViT-tiny backbone at 256×256 resolution, and the authors acknowledge blurring at higher resolutions, leaving open whether the efficiency and editing advantages persist at scale. Future work on scaling parameters and data, and on integrating region tokens with LLMs or multimodal generation, could substantially broaden the impact of this representation.
