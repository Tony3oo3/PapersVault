## Problem

Previous retinal coding models relied on static images or artificial stimuli and could not generalize across different natural scene videos, leaving the temporal relationship between dynamic visual pixels and retinal ganglion cell (RGC) responses largely unexplored. The key gap is building a model that captures the spatiotemporal encoding patterns of RGC populations in complex natural scenes while maintaining strong cross-video generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242355/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes grayscale video clips (360×360 pixels, 30 fps, converted from RGB) as a stack of frames, along with per-cell RGC receptive field maps (90×90, downsampled to 16×16) used as a conditioning signal. Output is a simultaneous population prediction of spike counts for 90 RGCs, binned at 33 ms intervals (30 Hz). During training, the video is cropped into 128-frame rolling-window clips, while validation uses the full video as a single sample.

## Method

Vi-ST comprises a Video Feature Extractor and a Spikes Alignment module. The extractor uses frozen DINOv2 (ViT-L/14 with registers) to produce 16×16 patchified spatial features from multiple ViT layers, then processes them through a Causal 3D Temporal Convolutional Network (C3TCN) with dilated causal 3D convolutions along the time axis and 3×3 spatial kernels to model spatiotemporal dynamics. The Spikes Alignment module fuses the RGC receptive field with spatiotemporal features via a 3D AdaLN Zero conditioning mechanism, then passes them through four Causal Multiscale Spatiotemporal (CMST) blocks whose temporal kernel sizes (e.g., 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 13, 21, 25) are derived from kernel density estimation peaks of actual neural response durations. Training uses a composite Vi-ST loss combining RMSE, a negative-ReLU penalty to suppress spurious negative predictions, and SoftDTW computed over rolling subsequence windows of length 6 and 12 to capture temporal alignment without distorting local dynamics. A key empirical finding is that earlier ViT layers (richer in positional than semantic information) yield better retinal encoding, and that predicting a larger population of neurons (complementary coding) stabilizes performance.

## Results

The primary metric is Pearson correlation coefficient (CC) between predicted and ground-truth RGC spike responses. On cross-video generalization (the paper's primary focus), Vi-ST achieves 0.334 CC (Mov1→Mov2) and 0.281 CC (Mov2→Mov1), substantially outperforming I3D+MSTCN (0.108 / 0.074) and DINOv2+MSTCN (0.101 / 0.100). On within-video evaluation, Vi-ST scores 0.789 (Mov1→Mov1) and 0.570 (Mov2→Mov2), which are lower than CRNN's 0.857 and 0.718 respectively, but the authors note CRNN uses 80 RGCs while Vi-ST uses 90, and within-video results are provided only for reference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Vi-ST against three baselines: CRNN [50] (a convolutional recurrent network), I3D+MSTCN (a 3D CNN backbone with multi-scale temporal convolution), and DINOv2+MSTCN (DINOv2 features with the same MSTCN downstream). On within-video evaluation, Vi-ST underperforms all three baselines (e.g., 0.789 vs. CRNN's 0.857 on Mov1→Mov1), which the authors attribute to differences in RGC count (90 vs. 80) and regularization choices. However, on the cross-video generalization task—which the authors identify as the critical and previously unsolved challenge—Vi-ST achieves a 3× improvement over I3D+MSTCN (0.334 vs. 0.108 on Mov1→Mov2) and a 2.8× improvement over DINOv2+MSTCN (0.334 vs. 0.101). The reverse direction (Mov2→Mov1) shows a similar pattern: Vi-ST reaches 0.281 CC versus 0.074 for I3D+MSTCN and 0.100 for DINOv2+MSTCN. CRNN is not evaluated on cross-video settings, so no direct comparison is possible there. The authors emphasize that prior models, including CRNN, cannot transfer to videos with different pixel complexities, making Vi-ST's cross-video performance the central contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vi-ST extends the temporal convolutional modeling lineage of MSTCN (Farha & Gall, 2019) by replacing its 1D dilated convolutions with Causal 3D Temporal Convolutional Networks (C3TCN), and adapts the AdaLN Zero conditioning mechanism from diffusion transformers (Peebles & Xie, 2023) to fuse RGC receptive field information into spatiotemporal features. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior retinal coding models like CRNN (Zheng et al., 2021) and I3D-based approaches, which achieve strong within-video prediction but fail to generalize across different natural scene videos. The key architectural departure is the use of a frozen self-supervised DINOv2 ViT prior for spatial feature extraction, combined with a dedicated Spikes Alignment module, to bridge the gap between pixel-space video representations and population-level RGC spike outputs in a cross-video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that cross-video generalization in retinal neural encoding is achievable with a foundation-model prior, achieving roughly 3× the CC of I3D-based baselines on unseen videos (0.334 vs. 0.108), though absolute performance remains modest. The introduction of the SD-KL metric, which evaluates prediction quality through spike-duration distribution similarity, offers a new lens on temporal fidelity beyond Pearson correlation. However, the study is constrained to two salamander retina videos and 90 RGCs, and the authors acknowledge that SD-KL is noise-sensitive and that robustness must be validated on larger, more diverse datasets. Extending the framework to primate retina or visual cortex neurons, and scaling to more cell types and stimulus categories, are the natural next steps.
