## Problem

The Triplane NeRF representation, despite its convenient 2D structure that enables use of standard 2D networks, lags behind methods like INGP and 3D Gaussian Splatting in reconstruction quality because only entries covered by training-view rays are learned, leaving random initial values in uncovered regions that produce artifacts in novel views. Additionally, existing NeRF super-resolution approaches either require dedicated 3D supervision (paired LR/HR multiview data) or lack a natural mechanism to connect low- and high-frequency content across resolutions.

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, the method takes a set of multiview RGB images with known camera poses and produces a wavelet-domain triplane representation that can render novel views at approximately 1.5 FPS. For super-resolution, it takes low-resolution multiview images (e.g., 100×100 or 200×200) and produces high-resolution novel views (×4 upscaling to 400×400 or 800×800), leveraging a pre-trained Stable Diffusion upscaler that natively supports 128→512 and is extended to other resolution pairs via zero-padding or random cropping. The wavelet planes use Bior6.8 filters with configurable base and final resolutions (e.g., N_base=256 to N_final=1024 for the 100→400 SR setting).

## Method

TriNeRFLet replaces direct optimization of Triplane feature planes with optimization of their 2D multiscale wavelet decomposition (LL, LH, HL, HH coefficients at multiple resolutions), applying L1 sparsity regularization to all high-frequency channels so that uncovered regions inherit coherent estimates from neighboring trained coefficients rather than retaining random values. Training proceeds in a coarse-to-fine manner, starting at a lower plane resolution and progressively adding higher-frequency wavelet levels, which mitigates the per-step inverse wavelet transform overhead. For super-resolution, the LR and HR representations share their first L_LR wavelet levels, so only the additional high-frequency levels need to be learned; a Stable Diffusion upscaler (modified to condition on the LR image rather than text) generates HR guidance images via an SDrefine step that adds noise to the current HR render and runs the diffusion process from a random timestep, and these guidance images are stored in a periodically refreshed HR-set used as pseudo-ground-truth for training the full-resolution wavelet coefficients alongside LPIPS and L1 wavelet losses.

## Results

On the Blender 3D reconstruction benchmark, TriNeRFLet Large achieves 34.02 dB average PSNR, outperforming 3D Gaussian Splatting (33.32), INGP (33.18), and the original Triplane (31.26). The lighter TriNeRFLet Base variant reaches 33.43 dB in 6 hours of training, already surpassing INGP's 6-hour rerun (32.98). For ×4 super-resolution on Blender (200→800), TriNeRFLetSR attains 29.49 dB PSNR, 0.051 LPIPS, and 0.930 SSIM, and on LLFF (378×504→1512×2016) it reaches 25.00 dB PSNR with 0.203 LPIPS and 0.771 SSIM, outperforming all unsupervised baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriNeRFLet Large (34.02 dB avg PSNR) outperforms all baselines on Blender reconstruction: it beats 3D Gaussian Splatting (33.32) by 0.70 dB, INGP paper results (33.18) by 0.84 dB, and the original Triplane (31.26) by a substantial 2.76 dB. Even the lighter TriNeRFLet Base (33.43 dB, 6 hr training) surpasses INGP's 6-hour rerun (32.98) and Triplane's 6-hour rerun (31.64). For super-resolution, TriNeRFLetSR outperforms NeRF-SR(SS) on Blender 100→400 (28.55 vs 28.07 PSNR) and LLFF (25.00 vs 25.13 PSNR but 0.203 vs 0.244 LPIPS, 0.771 vs 0.730 SSIM), and matches or slightly exceeds the 3D-supervised NVSR reference on Blender 200→800 (29.49 vs 29.53 PSNR, 0.051 vs 0.054 LPIPS). The method's render speed (~1.5 FPS) is competitive with INGP (~1 FPS) though far slower than 3DGS (135 FPS), while its training time is higher than INGP due to per-step inverse wavelet transforms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reparameterizes the Triplane NeRF representation (Chan et al., 2022) by learning wavelet coefficients instead of raw feature plane entries, drawing on the multiscale structure of INGP (Müller et al., 2022) for its coarse-to-fine training strategy and MLP architecture. For the super-resolution application, it departs from prior NeRF SR methods (NVSR, NeRF-SR) that require 3D supervision or dedicated 2D SR networks, instead leveraging a pre-trained Stable Diffusion upscaler (Rombach et al., 2022) as a 2D guidance signal. The wavelet-domain formulation is conceptually distinct from Rho et al.'s masked wavelet 3D grid, which applies wavelets to 2D planes as an intermediate step toward a 3D grid, whereas TriNeRFLet operates directly on the Triplane planes without 3D grid constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The wavelet reparameterization addresses a concrete failure mode of Triplane—uncovered regions retaining random initialization values—by enabling information sharing across scales, and it closes the performance gap with INGP and 3D Gaussian Splatting on Blender. The diffusion-guided SR pipeline is notable for matching or slightly exceeding NVSR (a 3D-supervised method) without requiring paired LR/HR multiview data, suggesting pre-trained 2D diffusion priors can substitute for 3D supervision in NeRF SR. However, the inverse wavelet transform at every training step imposes a significant runtime overhead (6 hours vs. 5 minutes for INGP at comparable quality), limiting practical adoption. Open questions include whether the wavelet-domain learning generalizes beyond Triplane to other NeRF variants and whether the training-time cost can be further reduced through hardware-aware wavelet implementations.
