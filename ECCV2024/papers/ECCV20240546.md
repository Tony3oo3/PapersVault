## Problem

Controllable scene generation via scene graph diffusion faces two open challenges: (1) scene graphs are dynamic structures with varying node counts and manipulator-induced edge operations, demanding network adaptability, and (2) existing methods either simplify graphs to node sets (losing structural relations), use token-based transformers that scale poorly (token count grows as Q·P!), or isolate per-node denoising processes (losing global consistency). No prior approach simultaneously handles complex graph manipulation and maintains inter-object coherence during generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a semantic scene graph with directed edges (15 relationship types) enriched with CLIP text features as semantic anchors, plus Gaussian noise sampled per branch. The graph can be dynamically edited at inference (node addition, relation change) via a latent-space manipulator. Outputs are 8-parameter bounding boxes (x, y, z, l, h, w, sin θ, cos θ) for layout and VQ-VAE latent codes decoded to 3D meshes for shapes. Generated scenes are textureless but directly compatible with off-the-shelf texture generators such as SceneTex.

## Method

EchoScene uses a dual-branch diffusion model (layout + shape) where each scene graph node is allocated an individual denoising process with weight-shared denoisers, eliminating per-node parameter overhead. The key mechanism is the information echo scheme: at every denoising step, each process sends its current noisy data concatenated with its latent node feature and temporal embedding to a triplet-GCN-based information exchange unit, which aggregates features across all graph edges and echoes the result back as the conditioning signal for each denoiser. In the layout branch, diffused bounding boxes are exchanged; in the shape branch, diffused VQ-VAE codes are first passed through 3D convolutions and flattened before exchange. Both branches are jointly trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of noise-prediction losses, enabling synchronized optimization without the VAE/GAN coupling required by prior methods.

## Results

On SG-FRONT, EchoScene (full generative) achieves FID 48.85 / FID_CLIP 4.26 / KID 1.77 (bedroom), 75.95 / 6.73 / 0.60 (living room), and 62.85 / 6.28 / 1.72 (dining room), outperforming the closest generative baseline CommonScenes by 15% FID, 12% FID_CLIP, and 73% KID in bedroom generation. The layout-only variant EchoLayout achieves FID 46.53 / FID_CLIP 4.24 / KID 0.33 (bedroom), surpassing CommonLayout (52.69 / 5.22 / 2.82) across all room types. For inter-object shape consistency (Chamfer Distance), EchoScene reduces CD by up to 74% over CommonScenes (e.g., dining-room chairs: 0.99 vs 1.96 ×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest generative baseline CommonScenes [58], EchoScene wins on every metric across all three room types, with the largest gap in KID (e.g., 1.77 vs 6.59 in bedroom, a 73% reduction). EchoScene also outperforms Graph-to-3D [15] by a wide margin (FID 48.85 vs 63.72 in bedroom), confirming that the diffusion-based dual-branch architecture surpasses VAE-based generation. Among retrieval-based methods, EchoLayout beats CommonLayout [58] on all metrics (e.g., bedroom FID 46.53 vs 52.69) and DiffuScene [53] on most metrics, though DiffuScene achieves a lower dining-room KID (0.09 vs 2.63). InstructScene [32] attains a slightly lower bedroom FID (45.40 vs 48.85) and FID_CLIP (3.87 vs 4.26), but EchoScene substantially outperforms it on KID across all rooms (e.g., 0.60 vs 4.15 in living room) and on dining-room FID_CLIP (6.28 vs 6.49). The text-to-shape baseline EchoLayout+SDFusion [12] outperforms CommonLayout+SDFusion on FID (57.68 vs 68.08 in bedroom), demonstrating that the layout-echo mechanism transfers benefit to downstream shape generation even without the full shape branch.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoScene extends the scene-graph-conditioned 3D scene generation line initiated by Graph-to-3D and CommonScenes, inheriting their triplet-GCN-based graph encoding and manipulation mechanisms. Its key architectural departure is the information echo scheme: rather than treating each node's denoising process in isolation (CommonScenes) or encoding the entire graph as a fixed-length token sequence for a transformer denoiser (InstructScene), EchoScene inserts a graph-convolutional information exchange unit at every denoising step, allowing all per-node processes to share their current noisy states. This positions the work between the scalable-but-isolated per-node diffusion paradigm and the expressive-but-computationally-expensive token-based paradigm, offering a middle ground that preserves local controllability while restoring global coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information echo scheme provides a lightweight, general-purpose mechanism for injecting global graph constraints into per-node diffusion processes, which could transfer to other structured generative tasks beyond indoor scenes. A notable limitation is that EchoScene produces only textureless geometry, requiring an external texture generator (e.g., SceneTex) for photorealistic output, and the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (SG-FRONT) with three room types. Open questions include whether the echo mechanism scales to much larger graphs (hundreds of nodes), whether it generalizes to outdoor or multi-room scenes, and whether the simple concatenation-based feature assembly (which outperformed cross-attention in ablations) remains optimal at higher complexity.
