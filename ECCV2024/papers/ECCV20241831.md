## Problem

While 3D object generation has advanced significantly, automatically texturing compositional indoor scenes remains challenging due to style inconsistency across multiple objects and severe occlusion between objects and interior surfaces. Existing texturing methods either target single objects or rely on optimization-based scene-level approaches that cannot support fine-grained per-object control or flexible scene editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241831/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a compositional untextured scene mesh (assembled from off-the-shelf 3D object generative models and a 2D room layout, with potentially imperfect geometry), a text prompt, and optionally a user-provided sketch for fine-grained control. The output is a fully textured 3D indoor scene represented as per-object colored point clouds (not UV-mapped textures), enabling free novel-view rendering and direct geometric editing (add, remove, move, rescale objects). A panoramic room image is also produced as an intermediate coarse reference.

## Method

RoomTex is a training-free coarse-to-fine framework built entirely on pretrained ControlNet and depth-aware inpainting models. In the coarse stage, the scene mesh is unwrapped to an equirectangular panoramic depth map, and ControlNet synthesizes a panoramic image that serves as a global style reference. In the fine stage, each object is textured iteratively: the panorama is re-projected to a perspective view targeting the object, refined via depth-guided ControlNet, then the partially-painted object is warped to a series of surrounding camera poses and inpainted to fill occluded regions until fully covered. A key technical contribution is a misalignment removal module that detects texture-depth misalignment using the intersection of Canny edges (RGB) and dilated Laplacian edges (depth), excluding these regions from unprojection to prevent foreground texture from dilating into background areas across iterations. The compositional point-cloud representation also enables interactive fine-grained texture control by accepting user sketches as additional ControlNet conditions for targeted repainting.

## Results

RoomTex achieves the highest Aesthetic Score (AS) of 5.20 among all methods, outperforming TEXTure-C (5.11), SceneTex (4.77), and TEXTure-H (4.33). On CLIP Score (CS), RoomTex scores 29.16, slightly below TEXTure-C (30.17) but well above TEXTure-H (27.86) and SceneTex (26.43). On BRISQUE (BQ, lower is better), RoomTex scores 30.91, beating TEXTure-C (39.78) and TEXTure-H (47.54) but trailing SceneTex (26.91), which the authors attribute to SceneTex's more pronounced lighting that BRISQUE favors. Overall, RoomTex delivers the best perceptual quality (AS) while remaining competitive on text-alignment and no-reference quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoomTex is compared against two baselines: TEXTure (in two configurations, holistic and compositional) and SceneTex. Against TEXTure-H, RoomTex wins decisively on all three metrics (AS 5.20 vs 4.33, CS 29.16 vs 27.86, BQ 30.91 vs 47.54), as the holistic TEXTure approach struggles with interior surfaces and produces low-quality textures. Against TEXTure-C, RoomTex achieves a higher AS (5.20 vs 5.11) and substantially lower BQ (30.91 vs 39.78), but trails on CS (29.16 vs 30.17) because TEXTure-C applies the text prompt independently to each object, inflating its CLIP alignment. Against SceneTex, RoomTex wins on AS (5.20 vs 4.77) and CS (29.16 vs 26.43) but loses on BQ (30.91 vs 26.91); the authors note that SceneTex's more saturated, high-contrast lighting artificially lowers BRISQUE. Qualitatively, RoomTex produces more coherent, detailed textures with fewer artifacts (e.g., spurious objects on walls, blurry occluded regions) compared to both baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoomTex extends the text-to-3D object texturing line (TEXTure, TexFusion, Text2Tex) from individual objects to full compositional indoor scenes by introducing a coarse-to-fine pipeline: a ControlNet-generated panoramic image provides global style consistency, followed by per-object iterative inpainting to resolve occlusions. It departs from panorama-only scene generators (MVDiffusion, Ctrl-Room) by treating the panorama as a reference rather than the final representation, and from optimization-based scene texturing methods (SceneTex, SceneWiz3D) by adopting an inpainting-based framework that natively supports fine-grained interactive control. The misalignment removal module, combining Canny edges of RGB with Laplacian edges of depth, is a targeted technical contribution to address a known failure mode of depth-guided diffusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoomTex offers a practical pipeline for automatically texturing compositional indoor scenes assembled from off-the-shelf 3D object generators, reducing reliance on professional artists while supporting interactive editing (add, remove, move, rescale objects) and sketch-guided fine-grained texture control. The main limitation is that iterative inpainting cannot guarantee full multi-view consistency in a single pass, which the authors suggest multi-view diffusion models could address. An open question is whether the compositional point-cloud representation scales to larger or more complex scenes without accumulating geometric artifacts from repeated warp-inpaint-unproject cycles.
