## Problem

Domain generalization (DG) aims to train models on multiple source domains that perform well on unseen target domains, but existing methods lack a principled decomposition of why cross-domain test errors arise. This paper extends the classical bias-variance decomposition to the DG setting, showing that test errors are dominated by cross-domain bias (systematic discrepancy between source-trained predictions and target-domain ground truth) and cross-domain variance (instability of predictions when the model encounters distributional shift), and that both must be addressed jointly for robust generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input and produces class labels via a ResNet-50 backbone pretrained on ImageNet-1K. During training, the student model is split at one of four randomly selected middle-layer positions (ends of residual blocks 1–4); the intermediate feature map is transformed to the frequency domain via 2D FFT, augmented in the amplitude spectrum only, and reconstructed via iFFT before being fed to the remaining layers for classification. At test time, the teacher model (stabilized via mutual EMA) is used for inference on unseen target domains.

## Method

RES comprises two complementary modules grounded in the extended bias-variance decomposition. The Representation Enhancement (RE) module reduces cross-domain bias by applying frequency-domain augmentations—random noise, random dropout, and mixup—exclusively to the amplitude spectrum of intermediate feature maps while preserving the phase spectrum to retain semantic content; this broadens the cross-domain feature representation space during training. The Representation Stabilization (RS) module reduces cross-domain variance through a Mutual Exponential Moving Average (MEMA) strategy in which a student model (actively trained with ERM loss) and a teacher model (passive, updated only via EMA) mutually exchange parameters with small coefficients (τ^T = 0.001 fixed, τ^S tuned per dataset), allowing the student to gradually absorb stable characteristics while the teacher provides a consistent reference. The two modules operate in tandem: RE widens the representation to cover target-domain variations, and RS smooths the optimization trajectory to prevent the model from overfitting to source-domain fluctuations.

## Results

RES achieves an average out-of-domain accuracy of 67.9% across five DG benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), outperforming the strongest baseline SWAD (66.9%) by 1.0 points. The largest gains appear on PACS (90.0% vs. 88.2% for FAD) and OfficeHome (71.8% vs. 70.6% for SWAD/SMA). On DomainNet, RES reaches 46.7%, edging out SWAD (46.5%) and SMA (46.0%). The method also exhibits the lowest standard deviation among top performers (e.g., 0.3 on PACS vs. 1.1 for ERM), reflecting reduced cross-domain variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RES outperforms all 13 baselines on the average score (67.9 vs. 66.9 for the next-best SWAD). On PACS, RES leads by a clear margin of 1.8–2.5 points over the strongest competitors (FAD 88.2, SWAD 88.1, DomainDrop 87.9). On OfficeHome, RES gains 1.2 points over SWAD and SMA (both 70.6), and on DomainNet it edges out SWAD by 0.2 points (46.7 vs. 46.5). On VLCS, RES (79.8) ties with DomainDrop (79.8) and is marginally below SAGM (80.0), while on TerraIncognita it essentially matches DomainDrop (51.4 vs. 51.5). The flatness-aware baselines (SWAD, SMA, SAGM, FAD) are the closest competitors, confirming that RES's combined augmentation + stabilization strategy addresses both bias and variance more effectively than either approach alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of DG research—feature-level data augmentation (Li et al. 2021, DomainDrop, AdaIN) and flatness-aware optimization (SWAD, SMA, SAGM)—under a single bias-variance decomposition framework extended to the cross-domain setting. The RE module extends image-level Fourier-based augmentation (Xu et al. 2021) to the feature level, operating on amplitude spectra while preserving phase, whereas the RS module introduces a bidirectional (mutual) EMA between teacher and student models, a small but distinct twist on the unidirectional EMA strategies in SWAD and Ensemble of Averages. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a theoretical re-framing that gives a principled justification for combining augmentation and stabilization, rather than proposing an entirely new mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bias-variance lens provides a useful diagnostic for DG practitioners: it clarifies that cross-domain error has two distinct sources (systematic distributional mismatch vs. training instability) that call for different remedies, and the RES framework operationalizes this cleanly. However, the decomposition in Eq. (2) is largely motivational—the domain shift δ is never formally defined or estimated, and the three-term split is not empirically validated by measuring each component independently. The method is also evaluated only with ResNet-50 on standard benchmarks, leaving open whether the frequency-domain feature augmentation and mutual EMA transfer to ViT backbones or more challenging multi-source settings. A key open question is whether the mutual EMA's benefit over standard SWAD-style EMA is due to the bidirectionality itself or simply the additional parameter averaging.
