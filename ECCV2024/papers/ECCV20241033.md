## Problem

Feature correspondence learning is fundamentally limited by the absence of accurate per-pixel ground truth labels, which are nearly impossible to annotate manually. Existing self-supervised approaches either require specialized sensors (MoCap, depth cameras, LiDAR), rely on expensive SfM preprocessing, or are restricted to specific model architectures with isolated teacher-student training that propagates errors. This paper addresses the gap by enabling correspondence learning directly from arbitrary uninterrupted videos with no geometric labels at all.

## Input / Output

The method consumes uninterrupted RGB video sequences, processing them in small batches of B=4 frames where each frame is matched against its n=2 preceding frames to accumulate reprojection errors across 5 frame pairs per batch. The feature correspondence network outputs K 2D pixel correspondences per frame pair, while the coupled bundle adjustment simultaneously recovers camera poses in SE(3) and 3D landmark positions. The framework is plug-and-play: it wraps around any existing differentiable matcher (expectation-based like CAPS or regression-based like DKM) without altering its architecture, preserving the original inference speed.

## Method

iMatching formulates feature correspondence as a bilevel optimization: the lower level runs bundle adjustment to find the camera poses and 3D landmark positions that best explain the network-predicted correspondences, while the upper level updates network parameters to minimize the resulting reprojection error. The key technical insight is exploiting the stationary point of the converged BA—since ∂R/∂T* ≈ 0 and ∂R/∂p* ≈ 0 at convergence—the gradient through the iterative Levenberg-Marquardt optimization vanishes, reducing backpropagation to a single Jacobian evaluation through the 2D correspondences only. This enables the use of a robust, non-differentiable BA with alternating outlier rejection and re-triangulation (3 iterations, following COLMAP-style pruning) while still training end-to-end. The BA is initialized via EPnP from a triangulated anchor frame pair, and the coarse-to-fine training scheme propagates the same gradient to both rough and fine correspondence estimates.

## Results

On TartanAir feature matching, iDKM achieves 65.5% mean matching accuracy at 1px tolerance (vs. 63.8% for pretrained DKM), and iASpan reaches 58.4% (vs. 51.5% for pretrained ASpanFormer), representing a 13.6% relative gain. On ETH3D-SLAM relative pose estimation, iDKM achieves 76.7% AUC at 5° threshold, a 31.8% improvement over pretrained DKM (58.2%), surpassing even SuperGlue (69.7%). On KITTI360 outdoor scenes, iASpan improves 5° pose AUC from 74.4% to 80.6% (+8.3%). The paper reports an average 30% accuracy gain over SOTA matching models, with a maximum 8.9× boost on the weakly-performing Patch2Pix model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iMatching consistently outperforms all baselines across both feature matching and pose estimation. Against the strongest pretrained supervised model DKM (63.8% at 1px), iDKM gains 1.7 points to 65.5%; against ASpanFormer (51.5%), iASpan gains 6.9 points to 58.4% (+13.6% relative). The self-supervised SGP baseline (46.8% at 1px) is outperformed by iCAPS (49.0%) by 5%, and OmniMotion (30.7%) is substantially behind all iMatching variants. On ETH3D-SLAM pose estimation, iDKM (76.7% AUC at 5°) beats SuperGlue (69.7%), CAPS (71.5%), and SGP (70.9%), while iCAPS (75.3%) edges out CAPS by 5.3%. The largest relative gain is on Patch2Pix, where iMatching achieves an 8.9× improvement (0.8% → 7.1% at 1px), demonstrating robustness to poor initialization. On KITTI360 outdoor scenes, iASpan improves 5° pose AUC by 8.3% over ASpanFormer, while iDKM shows only marginal gain (92.6% vs. 91.8%) due to DKM's already saturated performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the imperative learning (IL) framework—previously applied to path planning (iPlanner) and SLAM (iSLAM)—to feature correspondence, formulating matching as a bilevel optimization where bundle adjustment (BA) serves as the lower-level objective. It departs from prior self-supervised matching methods (SGP, OmniMotion) by being end-to-end and model-agnostic rather than restricted to epipolar-geometry models or test-time optimization. The key technical departure from differentiable BA in deep learning (DROID-SLAM, ∇SLAM) is the use of implicit differentiation at the BA stationary point, avoiding the memory-heavy unrolling of iterative LM optimization and enabling the use of a non-differentiable, robust BA with outlier rejection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any ground-truth geometric labels (poses, depth, SfM), iMatching makes high-quality feature matching trainable on arbitrary uninterrupted video, dramatically lowering the data barrier that has constrained the field. The plug-and-play nature—boosting four architecturally diverse SOTA models by an average of 30%—suggests broad practical adoption, and the end-to-end design opens the door to online adaptation in downstream tasks like visual odometry. Limitations include the requirement for uninterrupted video (ruling out discrete image collections like MegaDepth/ScanNet), the need for sufficient parallax and texture, and the fact that training still requires a GPU and a non-trivial BA solver per sample. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences, robustness in textureless or highly dynamic scenes, and whether the approach can be integrated directly into online SLAM/VO pipelines.
