## Problem

Most post-training quantization (PTQ) methods for diffusion models target unconditional models, leaving the quantization of widely-used pretrained text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion and Stable Diffusion XL largely unexplored. Additionally, prior evaluation metrics (e.g., FID to COCO) are inaccurate due to a distribution gap between model-generated images and COCO photographs, and they ignore prompt-generalization ability on unseen prompts outside the calibration set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241503/cropped_page7_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantized model takes a text prompt as input and produces an RGB image (512×512 for Stable Diffusion v1-4, 768×768 for Stable Diffusion XL) using 50-step PNDM or Euler sampling. Calibration requires only a small prompt subset (200 for SD, 30 for SDXL from COCO training), and the quantized checkpoint is then evaluated on both COCO validation prompts and 5,000 unseen prompts from the Stable-Diffusion-Prompts dataset to test generalization to diverse artistic styles.

## Method

PCR (Progressive Calibration and Relaxing) consists of two strategies. First, progressive calibration quantizes activations at timestep t using data generated with all subsequent steps (t+1, …, T) already quantized, so the calibration distribution reflects accumulated quantization error; the paper proves via Theorem 1 that the final error is bounded by a linear combination of per-step errors, making this necessary. Second, activation relaxing exploits a 'sensitivity discrepancy'—image fidelity degrades near x₀ while text-image matching degrades near x_T—by raising the bitwidth (e.g., 8→10-bit) for a small proportion τ ≤ 0.20 of the most critical timesteps, adding negligible cost (e.g., 5% relaxation yields only 8.1 average bits). The accompanying QDiffBench benchmark replaces FID-to-COCO with FID-to-FP32 (distance between quantized and full-precision generated images) and adds a prompt-generalization evaluation on stylistically diverse unseen prompts.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion (COCO validation, W8/A8), PCR (τ=0.20) achieves a FID-to-FP32 of 8.35 and CLIP score of 26.47, compared to 14.60/26.33 for PTQ4DM and 18.64/26.15 for Q-diffusion. On Stable Diffusion XL (W8/A8), PCR (τ=0.20) reaches FID-to-FP32 of 12.00 and CLIP score of 24.05, while both baselines collapse to CLIP scores around 16 and FID-to-FP32 above 38. The method adds only ~4% extra BOPs (45.47T vs 43.31T for baselines at W8/A8 on SD), confirming negligible computational overhead from the relaxation strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCR outperforms both Q-diffusion and PTQ4DM on every metric and every bit-width setting tested. On Stable Diffusion W8/A8, PCR reduces FID-to-FP32 by 43% over PTQ4DM (8.35 vs 14.60) and 55% over Q-diffusion (8.35 vs 18.64), while matching or exceeding the full-precision CLIP score (26.47 vs 26.46). The advantage is dramatically larger on Stable Diffusion XL, where both baselines suffer catastrophic text-image matching failure (CLIP ≈ 16 vs FP32's 26.44), while PCR (τ=0.20) recovers to CLIP 24.05 and FID-to-FP32 12.00. The paper also demonstrates that the previously used 'FID to COCO' metric is misleading—quantized models sometimes score lower (better) than FP32 on that metric despite visibly worse outputs—motivating the proposed FID-to-FP32 evaluation. On the unseen Stable-Diffusion-Prompts generalization set, PCR maintains its lead, confirming the gains are not overfitting to the COCO calibration prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the post-training quantization (PTQ) line of work for diffusion models (PTQ4DM, Q-diffusion) from unconditional models to text-to-image models like Stable Diffusion and SDXL. It departs from prior methods in two conceptual ways: a progressive calibration strategy that accounts for accumulated quantization error across denoising timesteps (formalized via Theorem 1), and a timestep-wise activation relaxing strategy that allocates higher bitwidth to sensitivity-critical steps. Additionally, it introduces QDiffBench, which challenges the standard 'FID to COCO' evaluation protocol by demonstrating a distribution gap that makes prior metrics unreliable for quantized text-to-image models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The QDiffBench finding that 'FID to COCO' can be misleadingly optimistic for quantized text-to-image models is practically important, as it suggests prior quantization results in the literature may have been overestimated. Being the first to achieve functional quantization on SDXL (3.5B parameters) while preserving text-image matching opens the door to deploying large foundation diffusion models on resource-constrained hardware. Open questions include whether the progressive calibration overhead scales to longer sampling schedules or video diffusion models, and whether the sensitivity discrepancy pattern (fidelity vs. matching) generalizes beyond the Stable Diffusion family to other architectures like DiT-based models.
