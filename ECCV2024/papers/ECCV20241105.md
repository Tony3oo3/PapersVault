## Problem

Distillation-based SSL methods (DINO, iBOT) over-compress input information through sharpened unimodal target distributions, discarding semantically meaningful nuisances that are critical for dense prediction tasks. Additionally, existing multi-level objectives suffer from semantic misalignment: patch-level constraints (e.g., per-patch contrastive loss) contradict natural image semantics, and shared projectors enforce uniformity between fundamentally different granularities of information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images augmented into 12 views (2 global 224×224 views for teacher/student plus 10 local 96×96 crops for the student) and produces multi-granularity visual representations: a regular [CLS] token (z_cls) for image-level semantics, an extra [CLS+] token (z_cls+) encoding multimodal image composition, and patch-level cluster representations obtained via self-attention pooling. Both class tokens are used for downstream evaluation, with z_cls+ showing particular advantage on transfer learning and dense prediction tasks.

## Method

UDI introduces three key mechanisms within a DINO-style teacher-student joint-embedding framework using ViT-S/16. First, a Stratified Random Sampling (SRS) module applies self-attention as soft-masked pooling over dense patch features to produce context-aligned cluster representations, analogous to RoI pooling in object detection, ensuring patch-level semantics naturally align with image-level semantics. Second, a shared projector is used across both granularities to maintain semantic-type consistency. Third, a novel multimodal objective (L_cls+) constructs a blended target distribution p*(y|Z) = α·p(y|Z) + (1−α)·p(y|z_cls) by averaging teacher patch-level predictions from stratified samples, which an extra class token z_cls+ learns to predict—effectively unsqueezing the CLS bottleneck to preserve the image's semantic composition. The total loss sums three cross-entropy terms: standard image-level distillation, the multimodal objective, and patch-level distillation on the sampled patches.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ViT-S/16 and 1,200 effective pretraining epochs, UDI achieves 77.6% top-1 linear probing accuracy and 75.6% k-NN accuracy, outperforming iBOT (77.4%/74.6%) and MST (76.3%/75.0%). Under fine-tuning, UDI+ reaches 83.0% top-1 accuracy, surpassing iBOT (82.0%) by 1.0% with the same pretraining budget. On COCO object detection (Mask R-CNN, 1× schedule), UDI attains 43.2 AP^bb and 39.4 AP^mk after only 300 pretraining epochs, exceeding SelfPatch (42.1/38.5 at 200 epochs) and iBOT (41.7/38.1 at 800 epochs). On ADE20K semantic segmentation with UperNet, UDI achieves 43.7 mIoU, improving over iBOT (42.9) and SelfPatch (43.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDI outperforms iBOT by 0.2% on linear probing and 1.0% on k-NN at 1,200 effective epochs, and by 0.7% under fine-tuning (83.0% vs. 82.0%). Against DINO (1,200 epochs), UDI gains 1.5% linear and 2.8% k-NN; against MST, it gains 1.3% linear and 0.6% k-NN. On COCO detection, UDI surpasses SelfPatch by +1.1 AP^bb and +0.9 AP^mk with fewer pretraining epochs (300 vs. 200), and exceeds iBOT by +1.5 AP^bb despite using only 300 vs. 800 pretraining epochs. On ADE20K segmentation (UperNet), UDI leads iBOT by +0.8 mIoU and SelfPatch by +0.5 mIoU. On DAVIS-2017 video segmentation, UDI achieves the best (J&F)m of 62.9, edging out SelfPatch (62.7) and iBOT (62.1). UDI+ (the multimodal class token) trades off linear accuracy (75.1% vs. 77.6%) but gains on transfer learning (e.g., +0.8 on iNaturalist-19) and fine-tuning (83.0% vs. 82.6%), confirming that the enriched representation benefits downstream adaptation at the cost of linear separability.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDI builds directly on DINO's self-distillation framework (EMA teacher-student, sharpened targets, shared projectors) but departs from it by diagnosing the sharpening-induced over-compression as a fundamental limitation through a multiview information bottleneck lens. It extends the multi-level SSL line (iBOT, SelfPatch, DenseCL) by replacing patch-wise discriminative constraints with self-attention-based soft-masked pooling, which the authors show yields cluster-aligned representations whose semantic types naturally match image-level semantics. The key conceptual departure is the multimodal prediction objective (L_cls+), which constructs a blended target distribution from stratified random samples of patch predictions plus the image-level prediction, effectively 'unsqueezing' the CLS bottleneck to preserve meaningful nuisances alongside dominant semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real tension in SSL—compression for semantic clarity versus preservation of informative detail—and provides a principled mechanism (multimodal targets) to balance them, yielding simultaneous SOTA on both classification and dense prediction with a single ViT-S/16 model. The self-attention-as-soft-masked-pooling insight for patch representations is a clean, reusable design choice that aligns with RoI pooling intuition in detection. However, the evaluation is limited to ViT-S/16 on ImageNet-1K, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger backbones, longer training, or non-ViT architectures. The modest margins over iBOT and SelfPatch (0.2–1.5% depending on task) suggest the gains, while consistent, are incremental rather than transformative.
