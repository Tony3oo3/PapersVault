## Problem

Semi-supervised semantic segmentation methods achieve good boundaries but confuse visually similar classes (e.g., cow/dog, boat/chair) due to insufficient labeled examples for learning precise semantic decision boundaries. Vision-language models like CLIP capture rich semantics from web-scale image-caption data but produce noisy dense predictions because they are trained at the image level. No prior work has effectively transferred VLM semantic knowledge into semi-supervised segmentation under label-constrained settings.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (512×512 crops for VOC/COCO/ADE20K, 801×801 for Cityscapes) alongside two text inputs: class name prompts formatted as 'a photo of a [CLS]' and dataset-specific class definition instructions (e.g., 'Dining table includes only tables for eating at. Not coffee tables, desks...'). The text is encoded by a CLIP Transformer text encoder into embeddings that serve as semantic anchors in the decoder and as concept sets for a frozen CLIP guidance model. The output is a per-pixel class probability map over the target class set (21 for VOC, 81 for COCO, 150 for ADE20K, 19 for Cityscapes).

## Method

SemiVL builds on UniMatch-style consistency regularization and introduces five components to inject VLM knowledge. First, the ViT-B/16 encoder is initialized from CLIP's vision encoder rather than ImageNet pre-training. Second, spatial fine-tuning selectively updates only the multi-head attention layers (which model inter-patch interactions) while freezing the per-patch MLPs, preserving CLIP's semantic reasoning while adapting to dense prediction. Third, a language-guided decoder computes dense vision-text cosine similarity maps, then processes them through decoupled spatial reasoning (7×7 conv + residual ASPP, shared across classes) and semantic reasoning (2 Transformer blocks with class-name text embeddings as anchors, shared across spatial locations), reducing parameters from 5M to 2.2M. Fourth, a frozen CLIP model generates high-confidence dense pseudo-labels that anchor the self-training and prevent pseudo-label drift, with the guidance weight linearly decayed over training. Fifth, class definitions from annotation guidelines are decomposed into concept sets that steer the frozen CLIP's predictions toward dataset-specific boundaries (e.g., assigning coffee tables to background rather than dining table). The overall loss combines supervised cross-entropy on labeled images with the consistency loss (over two strong augmentations and feature perturbation) augmented by the dense CLIP guidance term.

## Results

SemiVL achieves 84.0 mIoU on Pascal VOC with only 92 labeled images, improving over the re-produced UniMatch† baseline (77.9 mIoU) by +6.1 mIoU. On COCO with 232 labels, the gain is even larger at +13.5 mIoU (50.1 vs. 36.6), and on ADE20K with 158 labels it reaches +9.7 mIoU (28.1 vs. 18.4). With the full labeled set (1464 images on VOC), SemiVL still leads by +3.3 mIoU (87.3 vs. 84.0), demonstrating consistent gains across all label budgets. On Cityscapes, improvements are smaller (+2.4 mIoU at 100 labels) due to many small classes not well represented in VL pre-training captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiVL outperforms all 14 compared baselines across all four datasets and all label budgets. Against the strongest prior semi-supervised methods (UniMatch [67], 3-CPS [37], LogicDiag [38], ESL [43]), SemiVL leads by +6.1 to +8.3 mIoU on VOC with 92 labels and by +3.3 to +5.6 mIoU with 1464 labels. The open-vocabulary method ZegCLIP [76], even when combined with UniMatch's consistency training (ZegCLIP+UniMatch†), is still surpassed by SemiVL by +6.0 mIoU at 92 labels, confirming that SemiVL goes beyond a simple CLIP integration. On COCO, the gap widens dramatically: SemiVL beats UniMatch† by +13.5 mIoU (232 labels) and LogicDiag [38] by +17.0 mIoU, highlighting the advantage of VL guidance when many classes share similar visual appearance. On ADE20K, SemiVL exceeds UniMatch† by +9.7 mIoU (158 labels) and AEL [30] by +9.7 mIoU (632 labels). On Cityscapes, gains are more modest (+2.4 mIoU at 100 labels over UniMatch†) but still consistent, with the paper attributing the smaller margin to small, caption-sparse classes like distant poles and traffic signs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemiVL builds directly on UniMatch's consistency regularization framework for semi-supervised segmentation, adding five vision-language components to address the semantic confusion that plagues purely consistency-based methods. It departs from open-vocabulary segmentation methods (ZegCLIP, CAT-Seg) that require large-scale annotated segmentation datasets, and from zero-shot VLM segmentation (MaskCLIP, GroupViT) that produce noisy predictions without dense supervision. The spatial fine-tuning strategy—freezing local MLP layers while fine-tuning only attention layers for spatial reasoning—is a new label-efficient adaptation mechanism distinct from prior parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, adapters, prompt tuning). The class definition guidance mechanism repurposes dataset annotation guidelines as text instructions, a novel use of language in the segmentation training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to systematically integrate VLM guidance into semi-supervised segmentation, and the large, consistent gains across four datasets (up to +13.5 mIoU on COCO with only 232 labels) demonstrate that VLMs can substantially reduce annotation requirements. The spatial fine-tuning finding—that selectively fine-tuning attention layers outperforms full fine-tuning under label constraints—provides a practical recipe for adapting any VLM backbone to dense prediction tasks. A noted limitation is the smaller improvement on Cityscapes, where small objects (distant poles, traffic signs) are rarely captured in web-scale image captions, suggesting the approach's ceiling depends on VLM pre-training coverage. The class definition mechanism opens a path toward interactive, instruction-guided segmentation with minimal additional annotation effort.
