## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pre-training (VLP) methods are predominantly limited to single-modality 2D images (e.g., X-rays), and no paired 2D-3D medical image-report data exists in practice. The dimensional heterogeneity between 2D X-rays and 3D CT scans obstructs their effective integration within a unified VL framework, leaving the question of how to learn a shared representation space across modalities largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242162/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 2D X-ray images (224×224), 3D CT volumes (128×128×32), and free-text radiology reports. 3D volumes are tokenized via 3D patch embedding (16×16×4) while 2D images use standard 2D patch embedding (16×16), both feeding into a shared ViT-B/16 backbone. The output is a unified visual representation in a language-guided common semantic space, which downstream tasks exploit for classification (linear probe or fine-tuning), semantic segmentation (frozen encoder + trained decoder), and object detection (ViTDet with frozen backbone).

## Method

UniMedI introduces three synergistic designs: (1) an attentive slice selection strategy that computes relevance scores between the [CLS] token and all patch tokens across layers and heads, then selects top-k 2D slices from a 3D volume to form pseudo 2D-3D pairs without extra annotations; (2) CLIP-style VL contrastive learning that aligns the visual [CLS] token with the BioClinicalBERT report [CLS] token, providing language supervision that simultaneously drives the attention-based slice selection; (3) a DINO-style self-distillation auxiliary task where an EMA teacher network guides a student network to recover masked tokens, with masking guided by attention weights to preserve report-relevant regions. The key insight is that language serves as the coordinate system for the common semantic space: the [CLS] token is directly supervised by reports, making its attention weights a reliable signal for identifying lesion-relevant slices, and the resulting pseudo-pairs bridge the 2D-3D gap within a single forward pass and single VLP process.

## Results

On 2D linear classification, UniMedI (ViT-B/16) achieves 90.5% AUROC on CheXpert, 91.5% AUROC on RSNA, and 94.6% accuracy on COVIDx at 100% training data, outperforming the strongest baseline MGCA (ViT-B/16) by +0.8%, +0.7%, and +2.3% respectively. On 3D linear classification (CC-CCII), UniMedI reaches 89.4% accuracy at 100% data, surpassing UniMiSS by +5.3%. For 3D fine-tuning, UniMedI achieves 93.8% ACC on CC-CCII and 95.9% ACC on LUNA16, and for 3D semantic segmentation on BCV, it attains 85.4% Dice at 100% labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniMedI consistently outperforms the strongest 2D VLP baseline, MGCA (ViT-B/16), across all three 2D classification datasets and all data ratios: +0.6–0.8% AUROC on CheXpert, +0.5–0.9% AUROC on RSNA, and +2.3–7.6% ACC on COVIDx. Against ConVIRT and GLoRIA-MIMIC, the margins are even larger (e.g., +3.2% AUROC on CheXpert 100% over ConVIRT). On 3D classification, UniMedI surpasses UniMiSS by a substantial +22.6% ACC at 1% data on CC-CCII, narrowing to +5.3% at 100%. In 3D fine-tuning, UniMedI (93.8% ACC on CC-CCII) is marginally behind Joint (94.2%) but outperforms supervised ResNet3D101 (85.5%) and CovidNet3D-L (88.7%). For 3D segmentation on BCV, UniMedI slightly trails UniMiSS at 20% labels (77.5 vs 78.0 Dice) but leads at 40% and 100% (81.6 vs 81.0; 85.4 vs 85.0). On 2D segmentation (RSNA), UniMedI beats MGCA (ViT-B/16) by +1.6 to +1.7% Dice across all splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends medical Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) from its established 2D-only setting (GLoRIA, ConVIRT, MGCA, LOVT, PRIOR) to a unified 2D+3D framework, using diagnostic reports as a shared semantic coordinate. It builds directly on CLIP-style contrastive loss for image-text alignment and DINO/Attentive Mask CLIP self-distillation for the auxiliary masking task, but departs from prior multi-dimensionality approaches (UniMiSS, Joint) by introducing language-guided attentive slice selection to create pseudo 2D-3D pairs, rather than relying on dimension-agnostic tokenization alone. The key conceptual departure is treating the radiology report not merely as a supervision signal but as the explicit bridge that makes cross-dimensional integration meaningful, since reports of 2D and 3D images of the same patient already occupy a common semantic space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine data-scarcity bottleneck in medical VLP: paired 2D-3D image-report triplets essentially do not exist, and prior work either ignored 3D volumes or destroyed their structure by slicing. The attentive slice selection mechanism offers a practical, annotation-free solution that could be adopted by other multi-modality medical pre-training efforts. However, all experiments are confined to chest imaging (X-rays and CT), leaving open whether the language-bridge strategy generalizes to MRI, ultrasound, or non-thoracic anatomy. A further open question is whether the pseudo-pair approach scales to more than two dimensions or to modalities where the report-to-image correspondence is less direct.
