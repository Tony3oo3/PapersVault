## Problem

Existing generative image compression methods rely on discriminators (e.g., PatchGAN) that provide only coarse, patch-level adversarial feedback, limiting perceptual quality especially at low bit-rates. Additionally, multi-realism compression—allowing users to traverse the distortion-perception curve from a single model—typically demands large parameter overhead or multiple inference cycles (e.g., up to 100 diffusion sampling steps). The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that diffusion models are necessary for state-of-the-art generative compression and shows that a carefully designed GAN discriminator with semantic guidance can match or exceed them at a fraction of the cost.

## Input / Output

The codec takes a single RGB image (evaluated at resolutions up to 2000×2000 px on CLIC 2020 and DIV2K) and produces a compressed bitstream via an entropy model, plus a reconstructed image whose fidelity is controlled by a scalar α ∈ [0,1]. At α=0 the output approximates a traditional MSE-optimized reconstruction (nearly matching VTM-20.0 in PSNR), while at α=1 it produces a fully GAN-synthesized perceptual reconstruction; intermediate values yield smooth interpolation. The method requires only a single forward pass through the decoder regardless of α, unlike diffusion-based baselines that need up to 100–250 sampling steps.

## Method

EGIC introduces two core components. First, OASIS-C redefines the GAN discriminator as an (N+1)-class semantic segmentation network (U-Net backbone pre-trained with DeepLabV2), where the extra class labels the reconstruction as 'fake'; this provides pixel-level spatial and semantic gradient feedback to the generator, conditioned on the latent code via a projection-based scheme rather than concatenation. Weight normalization replaces spectral norm to increase model capacity and accelerate training. Second, Output Residual Prediction (ORP) is a lightweight retrofit head that implicitly predicts the residual R between the MSE-optimized and GAN-optimized decoder outputs; at inference, the reconstruction is computed as G₂(x) + (1−α)·R, enabling continuous D-P curve traversal with a single inference cycle. Training is two-stage: stage one optimizes the encoder, entropy model, and first decoder under rate-distortion loss; stage two fine-tunes the GAN decoder (and ORP head) with the segmentation-guided adversarial objective while keeping the encoder and entropy model frozen.

## Results

On CLIC 2020, EGIC (α=1.0) outperforms HiFiC, MS-ILLM, MRIC (β=2.56), DIRAC-100, and HFD/DDPM in FID while achieving considerably better PSNR across the low-to-medium bit range. On the distortion end (α=0.0), EGIC almost matches VTM-20.0 (the state-of-the-art non-learned codec) while outperforming MRIC and DIRAC-1. EGIC requires only 0.03× the model parameters of HFD/DDPM, 0.18× of HiFiC/MS-ILLM, and a single inference cycle (vs. up to 100 steps for DIRAC-100). On DIV2K, EGIC (α=1.0) dominates MS-ILLM in perception and outperforms HiFiC-lo even when using 30% fewer bits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HiFiC, EGIC (α=1.0) achieves lower FID with higher PSNR across the low-to-medium bit range on CLIC 2020, and outperforms HiFiC-lo even at 30% fewer bits. Against MS-ILLM, EGIC dominates in perception on DIV2K, with the gap being most pronounced at lower bit-rates. Against DIRAC-100, EGIC matches or exceeds FID while requiring only a single inference cycle versus up to 100 diffusion sampling steps, and uses 0.24× the model parameters. Against HFD/DDPM, EGIC achieves competitive perception with 0.03× the model parameters (vs. >1B additional parameters for HFD/DDPM), though the authors acknowledge HFD/DDPM can produce more aesthetically pleasing reconstructions in some cases. Against MRIC, EGIC (α=0.0) outperforms on distortion while using only 0.15× the additional parameters for multi-realism control (ORP vs. Beta conditioning), and EGIC (α=1.0) outperforms MRIC (β=2.56) in FID. On the distortion end, EGIC (α=0.0) nearly matches VTM-20.0, the strongest non-learned codec, while surpassing BPG, JPEG, DIRAC-1, and MRIC (β=0.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

EGIC builds directly on the HiFiC codec architecture (encoder, decoder, entropy model) and the OASIS semantic segmentation-guided discriminator from the semantic image synthesis literature, repurposing both for the compression setting. The key conceptual departure is generalizing the semantic-label-conditioned discriminator—originally restricted to constrained domains like Cityscapes in GC(D+)—to the full image domain via careful architectural choices (weight norm, pre-training, projection-based conditioning). The Output Residual Prediction (ORP) module departs from the prevailing trend toward diffusion-based residual prediction (DIRAC) or loss-conditional training (MRIC) by showing that a lightweight implicit residual prediction suffices to traverse the distortion-perception curve, drawing on the theoretical result that simple image interpolation between MSE and perceptual decoders is sufficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EGIC offers a practical, lightweight alternative to diffusion-based generative compression: it requires only a single inference cycle and a fraction of the model parameters (0.03× HFD/DDPM, 0.15× MRIC) while matching or exceeding their perceptual quality in the low-to-medium bit range. The ORP component is model-agnostic and can be retrofitted to any pre-trained generative codec, making it broadly applicable. Open questions remain: whether the GAN-over-diffusion advantage holds at larger model scales (the authors concede HFD/DDPM sometimes produces more pleasing reconstructions), whether the requirement for large labeled training data can be alleviated via semi-supervised or foundation-model-based segmentation (e.g., SAM), and whether content-weighted learning can address the sub-optimal preservation of small faces and text.
