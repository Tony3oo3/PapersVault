## Problem

Full fine-tuning of large pre-trained foundation models for domain generalization destroys the generalized features acquired during pre-training, leading to poor out-of-distribution performance. While Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) methods like LoRA mitigate this by freezing most parameters, they still suffer from overfitting to source domains and can produce features that conflict with the pre-trained representation, limiting generalization to unseen domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241357/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from multiple source domains (e.g., PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet) and outputs class labels via a classification head. The backbone is a CLIP-pretrained ViT (B/16 or L/14), with LoRA modules injected only into the query and value projection matrices of each transformer block. At inference, the group of LoRA modules is merged into the pre-trained weights (W_final = W + Σ B_i A_i), so there is no additional testing latency compared to the original model.

## Method

PEGO replaces the single LoRA module per layer with a group of N LoRA modules and introduces two orthogonal regularization losses. The 'preserve' loss (L_preserve = ‖W^T(BA)‖₁) constrains each LoRA weight to be orthogonal to the frozen pre-trained weight, indirectly ensuring that newly learned features are orthogonal to pre-trained features and thus preserving generalization. The 'diversify' loss (L_diversify = Σ‖(B_iA_i)^T(B_jA_j)‖₁) enforces pairwise orthogonality among LoRA modules within the group, encouraging them to capture distinct optimization trajectories and diverse knowledge. The final training objective is L_cls + α·L_OR, where L_OR sums both losses across all transformer blocks, and only the LoRA group and classification head are updated. At deployment, all LoRA modules are merged into the pre-trained weights, incurring zero additional inference cost.

## Results

On the five DomainBed benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet), PEGO achieves a leave-one-domain-out average accuracy of 76.1%, outperforming the strongest DG baseline GESTUR (75.5%) and the PEFT baseline LoRA (75.0%). The largest gains appear on the challenging TerraIncognita dataset, where PEGO reaches 57.3% versus 55.7% for GESTUR and 35.3% for full fine-tuning (ERM). When compared against methods that explicitly exploit pre-trained models (Table 3, four benchmarks), PEGO achieves 80.3% average accuracy, surpassing the previous best WiSE-FT (77.5%) by 2.8%, with a particularly large 9.8% improvement on TerraIncognita (47.5% → 57.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEGO outperforms all five DG baselines (SWAD, SMA, MIRO, GESTUR, ERM) on every one of the five DomainBed datasets, with the most notable margin on TerraIncognita (+1.6% over GESTUR, +22.0% over ERM). Among PEFT baselines, PEGO beats LoRA by 1.1% in average accuracy (76.1% vs. 75.0%) and VPT by 1.0%, while Adapter lags further behind at 69.1%. Against methods that explicitly preserve pre-trained knowledge (L2-SP, LP-FT, LwF, WiSE-FT), PEGO wins on PACS, OfficeHome, and TerraIncognita but is 0.7% lower than WiSE-FT on VLCS (83.2% vs. 83.9%); the overall four-benchmark average still favors PEGO by 2.8% (80.3% vs. 77.5%). Compared to Zero-shot CLIP, PEGO is marginally lower on PACS (96.5% vs. 96.8%) but substantially higher on TerraIncognita (57.3% vs. 31.3%), yielding a 7.1% average improvement. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that both orthogonal losses contribute: removing L_preserve drops PACS from 96.55% to 96.14%, and removing L_diversify drops it to 96.37%, while using a LoRA group without either loss (95.34%) underperforms even single LoRA (95.96%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning to the domain generalization setting, building directly on the LoRA architecture (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and adapting the orthogonal subspace concept from O-LoRA (Wang et al., EMNLP 2023) which was designed for continual learning in language models. It departs from prior DG work that focuses on domain-invariant feature extraction or data augmentation by instead addressing how to preserve the pre-trained foundation model's generalization during fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is treating the pre-trained weights as a 'gradient subspace' to be orthogonalized against, and replacing the single LoRA module with a group of mutually orthogonal LoRA modules to encourage diverse feature learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As foundation models (CLIP, ViT) become the default backbone for downstream tasks, the practical question of how to fine-tune them without destroying their generalization is increasingly important, and PEGO offers a clean, low-overhead answer with no additional inference cost. The consistent gains across five diverse benchmarks and two model scales (ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the orthogonal regularization generalizes to non-transformer architectures or other PEFT methods (adapters, prompt tuning), whether the group size N should scale with model size or domain diversity, and whether the L1 norm choice is optimal versus Frobenius or spectral norms.
