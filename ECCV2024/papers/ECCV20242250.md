## Problem

Instance-discrimination self-supervised methods for visual representation learning are prone to feature collapse and rely on hard positive/negative pair classifications that discard the rich relative metric information contained in the full distribution of pairwise similarities within a batch. Contrastive methods impose binary same/not-same labels, while distillation methods ignore the majority of negative pairs entirely, leaving the informative structure of the attention graph underexploited.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of n RGB images, each augmented into k random views (typically 2 large global views plus 6 small local views via multi-crop with ContrastiveCrop), and produces a feature embedding from the encoder f that is used downstream for probing, fine-tuning, or dense prediction. The projection head g maps embeddings to a latent space solely for loss computation during training and is discarded at inference. The method is architecture-agnostic, validated on both ViT-S/16, ViT-B/16, and ResNet-50 backbones.

## Method

BAM (Balanced Self Attention Matching) replaces direct feature matching between augmented views with matching of their self-attention distributions — the softmax of each latent's cosine similarities to all other latents in the batch. Three key design choices prevent collapse and improve quality: (1) positive entries (same-image pairs) are zeroed before softmax so the attention distribution captures only negative-pair structure; (2) the target distribution is a doubly-stochastic, entropy-regularized version of the similarity matrix computed via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport, which enforces global balance and symmetry; (3) a lower target temperature (τ_B=0.05 vs. τ=0.1) maintains an entropy gap that drives exploitation. The loss is a cross-entropy between the balanced target B and the source attention A in a swapped fashion, with gradients flowing only through A, requiring no negative pairs, memory banks, or asymmetric network structures.

## Results

On ImageNet with ViT-B/16, BAM achieves 78.1% top-1 accuracy on linear probing and 83.2% on fine-tuning, yielding the best average (80.65%) among all compared methods, surpassing DINO (80.5%) and MAE (75.8%). In the semi-supervised setting with only 1% labeled data, BAM reaches 66.8% top-1 accuracy, outperforming DINO (65.0%) and MAE (57.4%). On DAVIS-2017 video instance segmentation, BAM achieves 64.0 (J&F)m at T=28, exceeding DINO (63.1) by +0.9%. On COCO object detection, BAM matches MAE at 50.3 AP and slightly edges out DINO (50.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAM is most competitive with DINO: it matches on linear probing (78.1% vs 78.2%) but surpasses it on fine-tuning (83.2% vs 82.8%), semi-supervised 1% (66.8% vs 65.0%), and video segmentation (+0.9% at T=28), while DINO retains a marginal 0.1% edge on semi-supervised 10% (76.0% vs 75.9%). Against MAE, BAM dramatically outperforms on linear probing (78.1% vs 68.0%) and semi-supervised 1% (66.8% vs 57.4%), but trails by 0.4% on fine-tuning (83.2% vs 83.6%) where MAE benefits from 1600 epochs of training. BAM clearly beats MoCoV3 across all protocols (e.g., 78.1% vs 76.5% linear probing, 83.2% vs 83.2% fine-tuning at equal epochs, 66.8% vs 66.3% semi-supervised 1%). On transfer learning fine-tuning (ViT-B), BAM matches DINO on CIFAR-10 (99.0%) and is within 0.3–0.9% on other datasets, while on linear evaluation (ResNet-50) it leads SimCLR, BYOL, and NNCLR on Food-101 (78.2 vs 68.4/75.3/76.7) and CIFAR-100 (80.2 vs 71.6/78.4/79.0). On COCO detection and ADE20K segmentation, BAM is within 0.1–0.7 of DINO and MAE, confirming competitive dense-prediction performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAM positions itself within the instance-discrimination paradigm of self-supervised learning, departing from both contrastive methods (SimCLR, MoCo) that explicitly push apart negative pairs and distillation methods (BYOL, DINO) that match only positive pairs via student-teacher networks. Its core novelty is a new loss that matches self-attention distributions (softmax of pairwise cosine similarities over the full batch) between augmented views, with the target being a doubly-stochastic (balanced) version computed via Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport. The method also extends ContrastiveCrop from CNN backbones to ViTs by leveraging attention maps for semantic-aware cropping, and draws on SwAV's swapped-prediction structure for the cross-entropy formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BAM offers a clean, single-mechanism solution to feature collapse (balanced normalization) that avoids the architectural asymmetries, memory banks, or negative-pair engineering required by competing methods, while achieving competitive performance on both linear probing and fine-tuning — a dual strength that DINO and MAE each lack in one direction. The ablation showing that removing attention balancing causes complete collapse, while all other components yield only modest drops, highlights the centrality of the doubly-stochastic constraint. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger ViT models (e.g., ViT-L or ViT-H), whether the 4096 batch size requirement can be relaxed, and whether the balanced self-attention matching principle transfers to non-visual modalities such as audio or text.
