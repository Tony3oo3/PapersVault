## Problem

Diffusion models achieve state-of-the-art image quality but require dozens to hundreds of iterative sampling steps, precluding real-time generation. Existing few-step distillation methods (LCM, progressive distillation) still produce blurry, artifact-laden samples at 1–4 steps, while single-step GANs sacrifice sample quality and compositional text alignment. The paper addresses this gap by combining the fidelity of diffusion models with the speed of GANs in a unified training objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image in as few as one forward pass of a U-Net (or up to four for iterative refinement). At inference, no classifier-free guidance is required, reducing memory overhead. The student can optionally perform multiple denoising steps to refine an initial sample while preserving the overall layout, enabling fast exploration followed by targeted improvement.

## Method

ADD trains a student U-Net (initialized from a pretrained diffusion model) with two simultaneous objectives: (1) an adversarial loss using a frozen DINOv2 ViT-S feature network with lightweight trainable discriminator heads, conditioned on both CLIP text embeddings and DINOv2 image embeddings, with hinge loss and R1 gradient penalty; and (2) a score distillation loss that measures the L2 distance between the student's output and a frozen teacher diffusion model's denoised prediction applied to the student's own diffused output. The adversarial loss forces generated samples onto the real-image manifold, eliminating the blur typical of pure distillation, while the distillation loss preserves the teacher's compositional knowledge and text alignment. The student is trained on four discrete timesteps (including t=1000 with zero-terminal SNR), and the distillation weighting uses an exponential or NFSD schedule over the teacher's noise level. This hybrid objective allows the student to outperform its teacher (SDXL) in four steps and all few-step baselines in a single step.

## Results

On COCO zero-shot evaluation (5k samples), ADD-M (860M params, SD1.5 backbone) achieves FID 19.7 and CLIP score 0.326 in a single sampling step, outperforming all other few-step methods including InstaFlow-1.7B (FID 22.4, CLIP 0.309) and UFOGen (FID 22.5, CLIP 0.311). In user preference studies, ADD-XL (3.1B params, SDXL backbone) with a single step outperforms LCM-XL (4 steps) and all other few-step baselines, and with four steps surpasses its teacher model SDXL-Base (50 steps) in both image quality and prompt alignment. The inference time for a single 512×512 sample is approximately 0.09 seconds on an A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADD-M decisively outperforms all single-step distillation baselines on both FID and CLIP score: it beats InstaFlow-1.7B by 2.7 FID points (19.7 vs. 22.4) and 0.017 CLIP (0.326 vs. 0.309), and UFOGen by 2.8 FID (19.7 vs. 22.5). Against multi-step methods, ADD-M in one step surpasses DPM-Solver at 8 steps (FID 19.7 vs. 31.7) and Progressive Distillation at 4 steps (FID 19.7 vs. 26.4), while also exceeding the 25-step DPM-Solver (FID 19.7 vs. 20.1). In the larger-scale user preference study, ADD-XL (1 step) wins against LCM-XL (4 steps), StyleGAN-T++, InstaFlow, and OpenMUSE (16 steps) on both image quality and prompt alignment, losing only to full SDXL (50 steps). With four steps, ADD-XL surpasses SDXL-Base, IF-XL, and OpenMUSE in human preference, establishing state-of-the-art in both single-step and multi-step regimes. The retrained StyleGAN-T++ baseline (the strongest GAN comparison) is outperformed by ADD-XL in the single-step setting, confirming that the hybrid adversarial + distillation objective yields better text-to-image fidelity than pure GAN training at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADD sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: GAN-based single-step generation (StyleGAN-T [54]) and score distillation from pretrained diffusion models (SDS from DreamFusion [47]). Prior few-step distillation methods (progressive distillation, LCM, InstaFlow) reduce sampling steps but suffer from blurriness and artifacts, while standalone GANs lack the compositional strength and guidance mechanisms of diffusion models. The paper's key departure is a hybrid training objective that simultaneously applies an adversarial loss (using a DINOv2 ViT feature network as discriminator backbone, following [54]) and an SDS-based distillation loss from a frozen teacher DM, initialized from pretrained diffusion weights rather than trained from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADD is the first method to achieve single-step, real-time image synthesis from foundation-scale diffusion models, and with four steps it outperforms its own 50-step teacher (SDXL-Base) in human preference studies. This opens the door to interactive and real-time generative applications that were previously infeasible with diffusion models. Limitations include reduced sample diversity compared to the teacher, dependence on a frozen teacher model during training, and evaluation limited to 512×512 resolution. An open question the authors raise is whether continued improvements in adversarial training and discriminator design could eventually eliminate the need for the distillation loss entirely, given that the adversarial objective alone already achieves FID 20.8.
