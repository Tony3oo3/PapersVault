## Problem

WSI classification via MIL typically demands extensive self-supervised pre-training on large pathology datasets (tens of terabytes, months of compute) to overcome the domain shift from natural images, while methods that skip pre-training suffer severe performance degradation. No prior work has shown that a small amount of domain-adapted pre-training combined with a sufficiently expressive MIL-pooling can match or exceed these resource-heavy baselines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242383/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WSIs are tiled into 256×256 patches at 20× magnification with background patches discarded; a frozen or adapter-fine-tuned ViT backbone converts each patch into a fixed-size embedding vector. The Snuffy MIL-pooling then aggregates these embeddings to produce a WSI-level probability (tumor vs. normal) and per-patch scores that drive ROI detection, evaluated via FROC. The method also reports Expected Calibration Error (ECE) to assess reliability in the clinical setting.

## Method

Snuffy couples two components: (1) continual few-shot SSL pre-training that starts from an ImageNet-1K ViT and adapts it to pathology using AdaptFormer adapters (PEFT) with MAE or DINO objectives, cutting pre-training time by roughly an order of magnitude; and (2) a sparse-transformer MIL-pooling whose attention sparsity pattern has three parts — Class-related Global Attentions (the top λ_top patches identified by a parallel max-pooling stream), Random Global Attentions (λ_r randomly sampled patches per layer to capture tissue-microenvironment context), and Diagonal Attentions (self-attention preserving each patch's own embedding). The paper proves this pattern is a universal approximator of sequence-to-sequence functions requiring only O(n log 2 / λ_r) layers, the tightest probabilistic layer-count bound for sparse transformers to date, and demonstrates that the max-pooling and attention-pooling components co-optimise each other during training.

## Results

On CAMELYON16, Snuffy DINO Exhaustive achieves a WSI-level AUC of 0.987 and FROC of 0.675 for ROI detection, setting new state-of-the-art on both metrics. The efficient Snuffy DINO Adapter variant reaches AUC 0.936 and FROC 0.552 while requiring only 1,536 minutes of training (vs. 15,393 for exhaustive), a ~10× reduction. On TCGA Lung Cancer, Snuffy SimCLR Exhaustive attains 0.972 AUC, outperforming the next-best DGMIL (0.970) and KAT w/ KCL (0.971). Across the classical MIL benchmarks (Musk1, Musk2, Elephant), Snuffy achieves AUC of 0.989, 0.985, and 0.967 respectively, consistently surpassing all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Snuffy DINO Exhaustive dominates all 8 baselines on CAMELYON16 WSI AUC (0.987 vs. best baseline DSMIL at 0.858, a +12.9 pp gap) and ROI detection FROC (0.675 vs. best baseline DGMIL at 0.488, +18.7 pp). The efficient Snuffy DINO Adapter variant (AUC 0.936, FROC 0.552) still outperforms every baseline except DGMIL on FROC, while using only 6.7 GB system memory versus DGMIL's 76 GB and completing training in 1,536 minutes versus DGMIL's 1,612,920 minutes (~1,050× faster). HIPT and KAT underperform substantially (AUC 0.641 and 0.551 respectively), which the authors attribute to error accumulation from sequential dimension reduction and domain shift from ImageNet pre-training. On TCGA Lung Cancer, Snuffy SimCLR Exhaustive (AUC 0.972) edges out DGMIL (0.970) and KAT w/ KCL (0.971) by narrow margins, while clearly surpassing TransMIL (0.949), CLAM-MB (0.949), and DTFD-MIL AFS (0.946). On the classical MIL benchmarks, Snuffy achieves the highest AUC on all three datasets (Musk1: 0.989, Musk2: 0.985, Elephant: 0.967), confirming the MIL-pooling architecture generalizes beyond pathology.

## Relation to Prior Work

Snuffy sits at the intersection of three research threads: sparse transformers for sequence modeling (building on Yun et al.'s universal approximability framework), MIL-pooling architectures for WSI classification (ABMIL, DSMIL, TransMIL, KAT, DTFD-MIL), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning of vision backbones (AdaptFormer, LoRA). The paper departs from prior sparse-attention work by proposing a pathology-specific sparsity pattern—Class-related Global, Random Global, and Diagonal attentions—motivated by how pathologists examine tissue microenvironments, rather than the locality-windowed patterns designed for NLP. It also introduces the first application of continual PEFT (AdaptFormer) from ImageNet-1K to histopathology, bridging the domain shift that previously necessitated months-long exhaustive SSL pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PEFT-based continual pre-training reduces SSL training time by roughly an order of magnitude (1,536 min vs. 15,393 min for DINO), making WSI classification more accessible to labs without multi-month GPU budgets. The theoretical contribution—a tight O(n log 2 / λ_r) layer bound for universal approximation, improving on the prior Õ(n) bound—provides a principled foundation for sparse MIL-pooling design. However, the paper candidly notes that its SOTA results still depend on exhaustive SSL training, and the theoretical layer requirement is far higher than what works empirically (one layer), leaving a notable theory-practice gap. Open questions include whether the sparsity pattern generalizes beyond binary tumor/normal classification and whether lighter MIL-pooling variants can close the efficiency-performance trade-off further.
