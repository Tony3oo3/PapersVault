## Problem

Existing eigenvector-based positional encoding methods for Graph Convolutional Networks rely on the complete graph Laplacian and break down when edges are missing from the input graph. In 3D human pose estimation, this occurs when occlusion hides parts of the 2D skeleton, making standard spectral positional encoding inapplicable. The paper asks how to extract a robust, consistent component of the graph Laplacian eigenbasis that remains meaningful even when edges are absent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D joint positions (estimated via a stacked hourglass network or ground truth) normalized to [-1,1], along with the subject's center of mass to disambiguate poses sharing the same 2D projection. During both training and inference, up to two edges in the 16-joint skeleton graph may be randomly absent to simulate occlusion, while the total joint count remains known. The output is 3D positions for 16 joints (excluding Neck/Nose), with scale calibrated at inference by matching total bone length to a canonical skeleton.

## Method

PerturbPE computes positional features by applying the Rayleigh-Schrödinger Perturbation Theorem (RSPT) κ times, each time randomly removing a different set of edges from the graph Laplacian to form a perturbation matrix A₁, then computing the first-order perturbed eigenvectors. The κ perturbed eigenvectors are averaged to isolate the 'regular' (consistent) component of the eigenbasis that is invariant to which specific edges are missing, grounded in the network regularity theory of Lü et al. These averaged perturbed eigenvectors serve as positional features P, which are added to node features and passed through an MLP before the MöbiusGCN spectral convolution in each block. RSPT is used specifically because it avoids computing the full eigenbasis—only pseudoinverse vector products are needed, computed via QR-factorization and Gaussian elimination—making it efficient for the small human skeleton graph (O(n³) complexity, adding only ~0.001s to inference). A single network trained with a masking strategy over random edge removals handles all occlusion patterns at test time, unlike prior approaches that require separate networks per missing-part configuration.

## Results

On Human3.6M (Protocol #1, 2D GT input), PerturbPE achieves 32.7 mm MPJPE with 0.66M parameters, outperforming MöbiusGCN (34.1 mm at the same parameter budget) and all other GCN baselines. On MPI-INF-3DHP, PerturbPE reaches 82.0 PCK (All) and a state-of-the-art 84.0 PCK in the Outdoor setting, surpassing MöbiusGCN (80.0 / 83.1). Under occlusion with two random edges missing from the 2D skeleton, PerturbPE reduces MPJPE from 60.0 mm (MöbiusGCN) to 54.0 mm, a 10% improvement, and outperforms GFPose across most partial-observation scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerturbPE (32.7 mm) achieves the best MPJPE among all GCN-based methods on Human3.6M with 2D GT, beating MöbiusGCN by 1.4 mm at the same 0.66M parameter budget and by 3.5 mm at the 0.16M budget (36.2 mm). It also outperforms SemGCN (43.8 mm), GraphSH (35.8 mm), and Liu et al. (37.8 mm) by 11+ mm. On MPI-INF-3DHP, PerturbPE (82.0 PCK) surpasses MöbiusGCN (80.0), GraphSH (80.1), and all prior methods including Zhou et al. (75.3) and Ci et al. (74.0), with the largest gap in the Outdoor setting (84.0 vs. 83.1 for MöbiusGCN). For the two-edge-missing occlusion scenario, PerturbPE (54.0 mm) beats MöbiusGCN (60.0 mm) by 10% and outperforms GFPose on 2 Legs (52.4 vs. 53.5), 2 Arms (58.6 vs. 60.0), and Left Leg+Left Arm (48.8 vs. 54.6), though GFPose is slightly better on Right Leg+Right Arm (53.1 vs. 44.6 — actually PerturbPE wins here too at 44.6). With one edge missing, the 2-edge perturbation variant (48.0 mm) improves over standard eigenvector labeling (55.0 mm) by 12.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the eigenvector positional encoding line of work (Dwivedi & Bresson [10], SignNet/BasisNet [21]) by addressing a gap none of those methods handle: what happens when edges in the input graph are missing due to occlusion. The method is integrated into the MöbiusGCN spectral GCN architecture [2], which provides the lightweight backbone, while the novel PerturbPE component borrows Rayleigh-Schrödinger Perturbation Theory from mathematical physics to compute perturbed eigenvectors without recalculating the full eigenbasis. Conceptually, the paper departs from GFPose [9], which trains separate networks per occlusion pattern, by showing a single network can handle any combination of missing edges through a masking strategy during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: occlusion is a pervasive real-world failure mode for 2D-to-3D pose lifting, and prior spectral positional encoding methods simply break when the skeleton graph is incomplete. The approach adds no trainable parameters and maintains the O(n³) cost of RSPT at a negligible inference overhead (0.010s vs. 0.009s for MöbiusGCN), making it deployable. However, the evaluation is confined to 16-joint human skeletons with at most two missing edges, and the O(n³) complexity raises open questions about scalability to larger or denser graph-structured data. Whether the 'regular component' extraction via averaging perturbations generalizes beyond the small, nearly-regular human skeleton graph remains untested.
