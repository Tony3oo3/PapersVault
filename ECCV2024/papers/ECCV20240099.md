## Problem

Existing video colorization methods either stack multiple frames along the temporal dimension (consuming large GPU memory and failing to leverage spatial-temporal priors) or recurrently propagate features (accumulating errors and unable to explore long-range temporal information). Additionally, commonly used feature extractors like VGG or ResNet-101 are less effective at modeling non-local and semantic structures in complex scenes. The paper addresses how to establish reliable connections with far-apart frames while reducing memory consumption and avoiding error accumulation from inaccurately estimated features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of grayscale video frames (H×W×1) and a single colorful exemplar image (H×W×3, typically the first frame of the clip) as input, and produces a sequence of colorized RGB video frames (H×W×3). Colorization is performed in the CIE LAB color space, where the network predicts the two chrominance channels (a, b) conditioned on the luminance channel. The architecture is specifically designed for low GPU memory (1.9 GB peak vs. 34.9 GB for BiSTNet) and fast inference (0.07 s/frame at 960×536), making it practical for long video sequences.

## Method

ColorMNet comprises three modules: (1) a Pretrained Visual model Guided Feature Estimation (PVGFE) module that fuses global semantic features from DINOv2 (ViT S/14, last 4 layers concatenated) with local features from ResNet50 (stage-4, stride 16) via cross-attention, where DINOv2 features serve as queries and ResNet50 features as keys/values; (2) a Memory-based Feature Propagation (MFP) module that keeps every γ=5 frames as key-value pairs, computes cumulative similarity scores to identify the most frequently used (and thus most reliable) features, and compresses earlier frames to only the top-M=128 most-used spatial locations, reducing memory from O(z·Ĥ·Ŵ) to O(M + (z−N_e)·Ĥ·Ŵ) while mitigating error accumulation; (3) a Local Attention (LA) module that aggregates features from a λ×λ spatial patch across d=1 past frame to exploit the redundancy between adjacent frames. A lightweight ResNet18 extracts chrominance features for the value stream, and a ResBlock-based decoder with up-sampling produces the final chrominance channel. The entire network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss for 160K iterations at learning rate 2×10⁻⁵.

## Results

On the DAVIS validation set, ColorMNet achieves 35.77 dB PSNR, 38.39 FID, 0.970 SSIM, 0.035 LPIPS, and 180.84 FVD, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the unseen NVCC2023 validation set, it reaches 33.26 dB PSNR and 20.16 FID, demonstrating strong generalization. In terms of efficiency, ColorMNet runs at 0.07 s per frame with only 1.9 GB peak GPU memory, compared to 1.62 s and 34.9 GB for the strongest exemplar-based competitor BiSTNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColorMNet outperforms all seven baselines on every effectiveness metric (PSNR, FID, SSIM, LPIPS, FVD, Colorfulness) across all three datasets. The largest gains are over the exemplar-based methods: on DAVIS, ColorMNet improves PSNR by 1.75 dB over BiSTNet (35.77 vs 34.02) and by 2.52 dB over DeepRemaster (35.77 vs 33.25), while reducing FVD by 46% over BiSTNet (180.84 vs 333.56). On the unseen NVCC2023 set, the PSNR margin over BiSTNet narrows to 0.08 dB (33.26 vs 33.18), but FVD still drops by 47% (80.03 vs 151.43), indicating stronger temporal coherence. In efficiency, ColorMNet is ~23× faster (0.07 s vs 1.62 s) and uses ~18× less GPU memory (1.9 GB vs 34.9 GB) than BiSTNet, and ~8× faster than DeepRemaster and DeepExemplar. For temporal consistency (CDC), ColorMNet achieves the lowest value (0.003763) among all exemplar-based methods, slightly ahead of BiSTNet (0.003870).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the exemplar-based video colorization lineage (DeepExemplar, DeepRemaster, BiSTNet) but departs from the dominant recurrent-propagation and frame-stacking paradigms by introducing a memory-based feature propagation mechanism inspired by XMem's Atkinson-Shiffrin memory model for video segmentation. It also repurposes large pretrained visual models (DINOv2) as a feature-guidance signal via cross-attention with a CNN backbone, a strategy borrowed from the broader vision-language and self-supervised representation literature but not previously applied to video colorization. The local attention module for adjacent frames is a straightforward but effective addition that complements the long-range memory mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant contribution is the dramatic efficiency gain: 1.9 GB peak GPU memory versus 16.6–34.9 GB for competing exemplar-based methods, and 0.07 s per frame versus 0.61–1.62 s, making long-video colorization feasible on consumer hardware without sacrificing accuracy. The memory-based propagation with usage-frequency-based feature selection offers a principled way to handle arbitrarily long sequences without error accumulation, addressing a core limitation of recurrent approaches. The main limitation is the relatively large model size (123.61 M parameters) and the dependency on DINOv2 as a frozen feature extractor, which adds inference overhead and limits deployment flexibility. Open questions include whether the memory mechanism generalizes to other video restoration tasks and whether the DINOv2 guidance can be replaced by lighter alternatives without significant quality loss.
