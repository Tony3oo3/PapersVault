## Problem

Existing arbitrary-scale video super-resolution (AVSR) methods rely on conditional neural radiance fields whose high computational cost restricts spatiotemporal modeling to only two adjacent frames, leading to suboptimal temporal consistency. Additionally, no prior work has exploited the scale-dependent structure-texture transition in natural scenes, which is critical for faithfully reconstructing both structured and textural regions at arbitrary scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241512/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video sequence of T frames (each H×W) and two user-specified scaling factors (α, β) ≥ 1, which can be non-integer and asymmetric (e.g., ×2.5, ×3.5, ×7.2/×6). It produces a high-resolution video at resolution (αH)×(βW) per frame. During training, LR frames are generated via bicubic downsampling with scaling factors sampled uniformly from U[1,4], and the model is trained end-to-end with a Charbonnier loss over 15-frame patches of size 80×80.

## Method

ST-AVSR builds on a baseline (B-AVSR) composed of three building blocks: (1) a flow-guided recurrent unit that warps previous hidden states using PWC-Net optical flow to capture long-term spatiotemporal dependencies; (2) a flow-refined cross-attention unit that rectifies flow inaccuracy via learned offsets and modulation scalars applied through deformable convolution, then uses local cross-attention plus a squeeze-and-excitation global self-attention to selectively aggregate features from L future frames; and (3) a hyper-upsampling unit where an MLP with periodic activations predicts scale-aware, content-independent upsampling kernels from scaling factors, relative coordinates, and spatial indices (with sinusoidal positional encoding), enabling pre-computation for fast inference. The key contribution over B-AVSR is the incorporation of a multi-scale structural and textural prior derived from multi-stage feature maps of a frozen pre-trained VGG network, which is upsampled, channel-reduced, and concatenated with each input frame to replace all instances of x in the pipeline (except the final residual connection), providing the network with explicit cues about where and at which scale content should be treated as structure versus texture.

## Results

On the REDS validation set, ST-AVSR achieves the best PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS across all scaling factors (×2 to ×8), reaching 36.91/0.969/0.041 at ×2 and 26.04/0.746/0.298 at ×8. On Vid4 (generalization test), ST-AVSR attains 29.09/0.913/0.069 at ×2.5/3.5 and 26.16/0.852/0.127 at ×4, while running at 0.0495 s/frame — roughly 4–8× faster than competing AVSR methods (MoTIF: 0.4053 s, VideoINR: 0.2364 s). The multi-scale structural and textural prior contributes up to 1.5 dB PSNR gain over the B-AVSR baseline without the prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-AVSR outperforms all 8 compared methods on every metric and every scaling factor on both REDS and Vid4. Against the strongest fixed-scale VSR baseline EDVR, ST-AVSR gains +0.88 dB PSNR at ×2 and +0.66 dB at ×8, with LPIPS reduced from 0.072 to 0.041 (×2) and 0.411 to 0.298 (×8). Among AISR methods, the best implicit-representation baseline (SwinIR+LTE) reaches 34.73 PSNR at ×2, while ST-AVSR achieves 36.91 — a 2.18 dB margin. The dedicated AVSR methods VideoINR and MoTIF trail ST-AVSR by 5.32 and 5.88 dB PSNR respectively at ×2 on REDS, and by 6.07 and 5.54 dB at ×2.5/3.5 on Vid4. In inference speed, ST-AVSR (0.0495 s/frame) is ~8× faster than MoTIF (0.4053 s), ~5× faster than VideoINR (0.2364 s), and ~2.5× faster than the fastest AISR method OPE (0.1242 s), thanks to pre-computable hyper-upsampling kernels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the flow-based alignment and bidirectional propagation paradigm established by BasicVSR++ [6], repurposing its deformable convolution and recurrent hidden-state machinery into a forward-only pipeline that pairs a flow-guided recurrent unit (past frames) with a flow-refined cross-attention unit (future frames). For arbitrary-scale upsampling, it departs from the implicit neural representation (INR) approach used by VideoINR and MoTIF—which restricts spatiotemporal modeling to two adjacent frames due to per-pixel query cost—in favor of a lightweight hyper-network that predicts content-independent, scale-aware kernels amenable to pre-computation. The most distinctive departure is the introduction of a multi-scale structural and textural prior derived from frozen VGG feature maps, adapting the scale-space discrimination insight of Ding et al. [12] (originally for image quality assessment) to the video SR setting for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ST-AVSR addresses a practical gap: existing AVSR methods either sacrifice temporal consistency (INR-based, limited to 2 frames) or cannot handle non-integer/asymmetric scales gracefully, while also being slow at inference. The demonstrated ~5× speedup over the next-fastest competitor (0.05 s vs 0.12 s per frame at ×4) combined with consistent quality gains makes this a viable near-real-time option for applications like mobile pinch-zoom or surveillance. Open questions include whether the frozen VGG prior can be replaced by a learned or task-specific encoder, whether the structural/textural decomposition generalizes to more complex real-world degradations (the paper only tests mild noise+compression), and whether the approach extends to space-time (3D) arbitrary-scale SR as the authors themselves note as future work.
