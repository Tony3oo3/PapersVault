## Problem

Existing automated radiology report generation methods operate on a single timestamp, neglecting the temporal dimension of patients' imaging histories that radiologists routinely use to assess disease progression. While a few recent approaches incorporate one prior image, they cannot handle variable-length patient histories or capture the complex, multi-visit evolution of thoracic conditions, leaving a gap in generating temporally coherent reports.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241464/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a variable-length series of frontal chest X-rays (up to 5 studies per patient, resized to 384×384) along with their associated study dates, and produces a radiology report (truncated to 60 words) for each study in the series. The study dates are critical: they drive date-aware temporal embeddings that encode actual inter-visit intervals rather than assuming equidistant spacing. For the auxiliary temporal classification task, the model outputs one of three progression labels (improving, stable, worsening) for each of five thoracic findings.

## Method

HERGen's core contribution is a group causal transformer that treats all visual tokens from the same image as a group: attention is bidirectional within an image but strictly causal across images, so each study's tokens can attend to all tokens in prior studies but not future ones. This design naturally handles variable-length patient histories and encodes the temporal ordering of visits. A CvT image encoder (pretrained on ImageNet-21K) with a projection layer produces per-image visual tokens, which are augmented with learnable date-aware positional embeddings conditioned on relative study dates. A GPT-2 decoder generates the report, optimized by cross-entropy loss plus an auxiliary contrastive alignment loss that pulls paired image-report embeddings together in a shared space. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) standard encoder-decoder on single image-text pairs, (2) incorporation of the contrastive alignment module, and (3) integration of the group causal transformer for full longitudinal aggregation, progressively stabilizing optimization of the complex multi-module framework.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, HERGen achieves BLEU-4 of 0.122, METEOR of 0.156, and ROUGE-L of 0.285, outperforming the strongest directly comparable baseline (CvT-212DistilGPT2) by +5.9% on averaged NLG metrics. On the Longitudinal MIMIC-CXR subset, HERGen further improves by +6.5% NLG and +13.0% F1 over CvT-212DistilGPT2, and surpasses the longitudinal baseline Prefilling [59] across all metrics. On MS-CXR-T temporal classification, HERGen achieves the best macro-accuracy on 4 of 5 findings (e.g., 66.7% on Pneumonia vs. 52.0% for BioViL-T), though it trails BioViL-T on Edema (48.1% vs. 52.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERGen outperforms all seven directly re-run baselines on MIMIC-CXR across every NLG metric, with the largest gap over CvT-212DistilGPT2 [27] (the strongest baseline) at +5.9% averaged NLG and +3.6% F1. On the Longitudinal MIMIC-CXR subset, HERGen beats both single-image baselines (CvT-212DistilGPT2: +6.5% NLG, +13.0% F1) and the longitudinal baseline Prefilling [59] (BLEU-4 0.117 vs. 0.099, ROUGE-L 0.282 vs. 0.271), confirming that multi-visit temporal aggregation is more effective than using only one prior image. On MS-CXR-T temporal classification, HERGen surpasses BioViL-T [5] (the strongest temporal pretraining baseline) by +11.1% on Consolidation, +4.9% on Pleural Effusion, +14.7% on Pneumonia, and +3.7% on Pneumothorax, but underperforms it on Edema (48.1% vs. 52.0%). Against cited (non-re-run) methods like KIUT [16] and METTransformer [50], HERGen's NLG scores are competitive (BLEU-4 0.122 vs. 0.113 and 0.124) though these comparisons are not strictly fair due to different dataset preprocessing. The statistical bootstrap analysis (Table 4) confirms that HERGen's gains over CvT-212DistilGPT2 are significant at 95% confidence across all NLG metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERGen builds directly on the CvT-212DistilGPT2 encoder-decoder architecture [27] and extends the emerging line of longitudinal chest X-ray analysis (BioViL-T [5], Prefilling [59], ChexRelNet [20]) by replacing the common limitation of using only one prior image or two consecutive pairs with a group causal transformer that processes variable-length patient histories. The group causal attention mechanism is a conceptual departure from both bidirectional (BERT-style) and standard causal (GPT-style) attention, introducing a hybrid that permits intra-image full attention while restricting inter-image attention to strictly preceding studies. The auxiliary contrastive alignment module draws on CLIP-style vision-language pretraining (BioViL [7], MedCLIP [51]), and the three-stage curriculum learning strategy is a novel training recipe specific to this longitudinal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine clinical workflow gap: radiologists routinely compare prior scans to assess disease progression, yet most automated report generators ignore this temporal dimension. The group causal attention mechanism is a clean, generalizable architectural idea that could transfer to other variable-length temporal sequence tasks beyond radiology. Key limitations include the restriction to 5 studies per patient (resource-driven), the absence of anatomical consistency constraints in the contrastive alignment, and evaluation on a single primary dataset (MIMIC-CXR). Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer histories, whether incorporating anatomical priors would further improve temporal coherence, and whether the curriculum strategy generalizes to other multi-stage multimodal training pipelines.
