## Problem

Existing neural video codecs rely exclusively on short-term temporal context via optical-flow-based motion compensation, which accumulates errors over long prediction chains and restricts context diversity to a narrow temporal window. This limits compression performance, particularly under the challenging single intra-frame setting where no periodic keyframe resets the error propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241768/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec processes a video frame sequence at 1080p resolution, extracting multi-scale features at full, 1/2, and 1/4 resolution. A user-defined quantization parameter q_t controls the target bitrate and is fed as an additional prior into the entropy model. The long-term reference buffer is updated every T=32 frames, and the output is a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed frame that feeds back into the feature propagation loop.

## Method

Built on the DCVC-FM conditional coding framework, the paper introduces a Long-term temporal Context Gathering (LCG) module that retrieves relevant context from a buffered long-term reference feature. The LCG module first clusters the long-term key features into M=4 semantic groups via learned cluster centers, then performs intra-cluster top-N (N=4) similarity search to aggregate relevant context while filtering out cross-semantic noise. The retrieved long-term context is upsampled and fused with the short-term reference feature before feeding into the context fusion module. Additionally, the quantization parameter q_t is used to generate a gating signal that modulates the hyper prior, temporal prior, and previous latent in the entropy model, explicitly accounting for distribution shifts across the variable-bitrate range. This design reduces the search space by up to M-fold and mitigates error propagation by anchoring to a high-quality long-term reference.

## Results

On the most challenging setting (all frames, intra-period=-1, YUV420), DCVC-LCG achieves an average BD-Rate of −25.6% over VTM-17.0 across UVG, MCL-JCV, and HEVC Class B 1080p datasets. This translates to an 11.3% bitrate saving over the best traditional codec ECM-5.0 (−16.4%) and a 10.8% saving over the previous SOTA neural codec DCVC-FM (−16.4%). Under the 96-frame intra-period=32 setting, DCVC-LCG still leads with −21.1% average BD-Rate over VTM-17.0, outperforming DCVC-FM (−16.4%) and ECM-5.0 (−13.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCVC-LCG outperforms all eight baselines across every tested setting. Against the strongest traditional codec ECM-5.0, it achieves an 11.3% average BD-Rate improvement (−25.6% vs. −16.4% over VTM-17.0) in the all-frames YUV420 setting. Against the previous SOTA neural codec DCVC-FM, the gain is 10.8% (−25.6% vs. −16.4%), and notably DCVC-LCG achieves this with fewer MACs (2184G vs. 2225G) and lower encoding/decoding times. DCVC-DC, the earlier neural baseline, is surpassed by 29.9% in the same setting (−25.6% vs. +4.3%). In the RGB colorspace (96 frames, intra-period=32), DCVC-LCG (−16.2%) beats DCVC-FM (−12.3%), DCVC-DC (−14.1%), and DCVC-HEM (+4.9%), while older methods like DCVC (+136.9%) and CANF-VC (+69.4%) are far behind. The advantage is consistent across all three 1080p test datasets (UVG, MCL-JCV, HEVC Class B), with the largest per-dataset gain on UVG (−29.4% vs. −20.7% for DCVC-FM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DCVC-FM (CVPR 2024), a conditional-coding neural video codec that uses feature modulation for short-term temporal context. It departs from the entire NVC lineage (DVC → M-LVC → FVC → DCVC → DCVC-DC → DCVC-FM) by introducing long-term temporal context gathering, a concept borrowed from traditional codecs' long-term reference picture mechanisms but reimagined in feature space via non-local, intra-cluster searching. The intra-cluster mechanism adapts non-local attention ideas to temporal context mining, and the q_t-aware entropy model extends prior variable-bitrate NVCs that did not explicitly model quantization-induced distribution shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.3% BD-Rate saving over ECM and 10.8% over DCVC-FM on 1080p with a single intra-frame setting represents a meaningful step for neural codecs to become competitive with the best traditional codecs in the most demanding configuration. The efficiency result—achieving lower MACs than DCVC-FM despite adding a new module, because long-term context reduces the burden on short-term generation—is practically important for deployment. However, the authors acknowledge degradation on low-resolution videos and scene changes, leaving open whether the clustering-based search generalizes to highly dynamic or semantically diverse content. The work also raises the question of whether further extending the temporal range (beyond the 32-frame update interval) or combining with transformer-based long-range modeling would yield additional gains.
