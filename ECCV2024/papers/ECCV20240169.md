## Problem

Existing controllable text-to-image diffusion models (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, UniControl, etc.) still produce generated images that significantly deviate from their input image conditions, yet no prior work provides a concrete strategy for improving this controllability. The gap is that all existing methods only implicitly encourage condition adherence through latent-space denoising loss, without any explicit pixel-level optimization linking generated outputs back to the input conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240169/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 image-based conditional control (segmentation mask, canny/hed/lineart edge map, or depth map) together with a free-form text prompt, and produces a 512×512 generated image. During training, rather than sampling from random noise through all T denoising steps, the method adds small Gaussian noise (t ≤ t_thre) to real training images and uses the single-step denoised result as a proxy for the final image, which is then fed to a frozen discriminative reward model to extract the corresponding condition for consistency loss computation.

## Method

ControlNet++ frames controllable generation as a cycle-consistency problem inspired by CycleGAN: a pre-trained discriminative model (e.g., UperNet for segmentation, FCN for edges) extracts the condition from the generated image, and a pixel-level loss (cross-entropy, SSIM, RMSE, etc.) is minimized between this extracted condition and the original input condition. The key efficiency insight is that full multi-step sampling from random noise is unnecessary for computing this reward; instead, adding small noise to training images and performing a single denoising step yields a sufficient approximation of x_0, reducing memory from ~340 GB (50-step DDIM with stored gradients) to ~6.8 GB per sample. The total loss combines the standard diffusion training loss with the reward consistency loss (weighted by λ), applied only at small timesteps (t ≤ t_thre) where single-step denoising remains accurate. During reward fine-tuning, the base text-to-image model and the discriminative reward model are frozen, and only the ControlNet adapter parameters are updated, preserving original generation quality while improving condition adherence.

## Results

On ADE20K segmentation masks, ControlNet++ achieves 43.64 mIoU, improving over ControlNet (32.55) by 11.1 percentage points. For line-art edge conditions on MultiGen-20M, it reaches 0.8399 SSIM versus ControlNet's 0.7054 (+13.4%), and for depth maps it reduces RMSE from 35.90 to 28.32 (−7.6). Across all six condition types (segmentation, canny edge, hed edge, line-art edge, depth), ControlNet++ consistently outperforms every baseline, while also achieving competitive or superior FID (e.g., 13.88 vs. 17.44 on line-art) and CLIP-score (31.96 vs. 31.53 on ADE20K), confirming that improved controllability does not degrade image quality or text alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ControlNet++ outperforms ControlNet (SD1.5) on every reported metric: +11.1 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation, +7.1 mIoU on COCO-Stuff, +2.4 F1 on canny edges, +0.048 SSIM on hed edges, +0.134 SSIM on line-art edges, and −7.6 RMSE on depth maps. Against T2I-Adapter (SD1.5), the gains are even larger (e.g., 43.64 vs. 12.61 mIoU on ADE20K), and it also beats the SDXL variants of both ControlNet and T2I-Adapter on the metrics they report (e.g., 28.32 vs. 40.00 RMSE on depth). UniControl, the strongest prior unified method, is surpassed by ControlNet++ on all shared conditions (43.64 vs. 25.44 mIoU on ADE20K; 0.8399 vs. – SSIM on line-art). GLIGEN and Uni-ControlNet trail by wider margins, confirming that the cycle-consistency reward strategy yields substantial controllability gains over both adapter-based and unified-control approaches. On image quality (FID) and text alignment (CLIP-score), ControlNet++ matches or slightly exceeds baselines, indicating the controllability improvements come without sacrificing generation fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ControlNet (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) by adding an explicit pixel-level cycle consistency loss, drawing conceptual inspiration from CycleGAN's reconstruction objective and the reward-model literature in RLHF. Unlike prior controllable generation methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, UniControl) that achieve controllability implicitly through latent-space denoising, ControlNet++ repurposes pre-trained discriminative models (UperNet, DeepLabv3) as objective reward signals to directly optimize consistency between input conditions and generated images. The efficient single-step denoising strategy for reward fine-tuning departs from the multi-step sampling approach used in prior reward-based diffusion fine-tuning (e.g., ImageReward, DPOK), which requires storing gradients across all timesteps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic quantitative evaluation of controllability gaps in existing methods, revealing that ControlNet achieves only 32.55 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation versus 50.7 on real data — a gap the field had not explicitly measured. The efficient reward strategy (adding small noise + single-step denoising instead of full 50-step sampling) makes cycle-consistency fine-tuning practical on standard hardware, reducing memory from ~340GB to ~6.8GB per sample. Open questions include extending the approach to conditions lacking differentiable reward models (pose, sketch, bounding boxes) and whether the method generalizes beyond the ControlNet architecture to other conditional generation frameworks.
