## Problem

DETR-based detectors suffer from slow convergence because self-attention introduces no structural bias over inputs, forcing the model to learn even basic positional relationships entirely from training data. This paper identifies that explicit position relations between objects are statistically significant (quantified via a macroscopic correlation metric) yet remain unexplored as an inductive bias for transformer decoders in detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241324/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (resized and augmented following DETR conventions) and produces N bounding boxes with class labels via a set of object queries. Unusually, the position relation encoder consumes the predicted bounding boxes from each decoder layer as its input, computing pairwise normalized relative geometry features (log-scaled center offsets and size ratios) that are invariant to translation and scale. During training, two parallel query sets (900 matching + 1500 hybrid) are decoded, but only the matching queries are used at inference, incurring no extra computational cost.

## Method

Relation-DETR introduces a position relation encoder that computes pairwise relative geometry features between all predicted bounding boxes, encodes them via sine-cosine positional encoding, and projects them into per-head scalar attention biases. These biases are integrated into self-attention through progressive attention refinement, where the relation computed from layers i-1 and i modulates the attention that produces layer i+1 predictions. To resolve the conflict between non-duplicate predictions and sufficient positive supervision, the method extends DETR's streaming pipeline into a contrast relation pipeline: matching queries use position-relation-augmented self-attention with one-to-one Hungarian matching, while hybrid queries skip the relation and use one-to-many matching (ground truth repeated K times) for additional positive supervision. The position relation encoder is designed as a plug-in-and-play lateral branch, requiring only a few modifications to integrate into existing DETR variants.

## Results

On COCO val2017 with ResNet-50 under the standard 1× (12-epoch) setting, Relation-DETR achieves 51.7% AP, surpassing the previous best DETR detector DDQ-DETR (50.7%) by 1.0% AP and DINO (49.9%) by 1.8% AP. Under the 2× (24-epoch) setting, it reaches 52.1% AP, and with Swin-L backbone it achieves 57.8% AP, outperforming Rank-DINO (57.6%). Notably, Relation-DETR converges to over 40% AP in just 2 training epochs, the fastest among all DETR variants tested. On task-specific datasets, it gains +1.4% AP on CSD (54.4%) and +2.0% AP on MSSD (57.4%) over the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Relation-DETR outperforms all 16 compared DETR baselines on COCO val2017 under the 1× setting, with the largest margins over earlier methods: +6.3% AP over Deformable-DETR, +5.4% over DAB-DETR, +4.5% over DN-DETR, and +1.8% over DINO. Against the most competitive recent baselines, it leads DDQ-DETR by 1.0% AP (51.7 vs 50.7), Rank-DETR by 1.3%, Stable-DINO by 1.3%, and Align-DETR by 1.5%. On the Swin-L backbone (Table 2), Relation-DETR achieves 57.8% AP, edging out Rank-DINO (57.6%) and DINO (56.8%) by 0.2% and 1.0% respectively. On task-specific datasets, it beats DINO by 1.4% AP on CSD and 6.4% AP on MSSD, and surpasses Salience-DETR by 2.0% AP on MSSD. The transferability experiments (Table 6) confirm that simply adding the position relation encoder to Deformable-DETR, DAB-DETR, DN-DETR, and DINO yields +1.6%, +2.0%, +0.1%, and +0.4% AP respectively, with the largest gains on AP_S for earlier baselines that lack structural bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR family (Carion et al., ECCV 2020) and its iterative refinement mechanism from Deformable-DETR (Zhu et al., ICLR 2020) by injecting an explicit geometric position relation as an attention bias into the self-attention of the transformer decoder, rather than relying on purely data-driven attention weights. It draws conceptually from relation networks (Hu et al., CVPR 2018) but operates at the instance level using normalized bounding-box geometry rather than learned feature distances. The contrast pipeline generalizes the hybrid matching strategy of H-DETR (Jia et al., CVPR 2023) by coupling one-to-one and one-to-many matching with the position relation to simultaneously enforce non-duplication and provide sufficient positive supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The position relation encoder is a lightweight, plug-and-play module that consistently improves a range of DETR variants (Deformable-DETR, DAB-DETR, DN-DETR, DINO) without architectural redesign, making it broadly applicable to the growing DETR ecosystem. The demonstrated convergence speedup (40% AP in 2 epochs) has practical value for reducing training costs. However, the relation is purely geometric; the authors acknowledge failure cases with occluded or densely packed objects where semantic or occlusion-aware relations would be needed, leaving open the question of how to incorporate richer relational priors. The introduced SA-Det-100k dataset also opens a direction toward class-agnostic universal detection benchmarks.
