## Problem

Seam carving, the dominant content-aware retargeting method, operates through discrete, greedy removal of single-pixel-wide seams found via dynamic programming. This discrete formulation restricts solutions to a narrow class, cannot natively extend to continuous domains such as Neural Radiance Fields or polygon meshes, and fails to exploit non-continuous or steeply angled low-content regions. The paper asks whether a unified, continuous deformation-based formulation can generalise seam carving's content-awareness principles across heterogeneous visual data formats.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image (represented as a continuous MLP), a trained NeRF (from which surface point clouds are extracted via rendered RGB and depth), or a polygon mesh, together with a target scale factor alpha along a chosen deformation axis. It produces a retargeted output in the same domain (resized image, deformed NeRF scene, or deformed mesh) plus the learned scalar deformation field D. For NeRFs, an auxiliary inverse deformation network U is also trained to map target-space surface points back to source space, keeping optimisation restricted to a sparse surface set rather than the full volume.

## Method

The core contribution is formulating retargeting as a global, continuous optimisation over a neural deformation field D (a simple MLP) that adds a scalar offset along a fixed axis v, mapping each output point p back to a source point p + D(p)*v. Two auxiliary MLPs are trained: an energy field E (colour gradient or curvature) and a cumulative energy field Sigma, whose difference between two points approximates the interval energy, enabling efficient content-aware penalty computation. The total loss combines four terms: L_e penalises stretching/compression weighted by cumulative energy between neighbouring points, L_s penalises shearing (deformation change orthogonal to v) in high-content regions, L_b enforces boundary preservation, and L_m enforces monotonicity of D along v to prevent content repetition. For NeRFs, an inverse deformation network U is alternately trained to identify surface points in the target space, restricting optimisation to a sparse representative set. The energy formulation is swappable, allowing integration of learned saliency maps (e.g. FullGrad) without modifying the deformation optimisation pipeline.

## Results

On the RetargetMe benchmark, the proposed method achieves a mean FID of 46.67 (x-axis) and 53.57 (y-axis), outperforming seam carving's 52.57 and 65.48 respectively, with the largest gap at 110% scaling (11.17 vs 20.86 FID on x-axis). In a 1200-vote user study on images, participants prefer the proposed method 44.57% of the time versus 25.1% for seam carving. On the 3D NeRF retargeting task, a 252-vote user study shows near-unanimous preference for the proposed method over video seam carving (e.g., 21-to-0 on the Farm scene at 50% x-axis).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original seam carving [2], the proposed method wins on both FID (mean 46.67 vs 52.57 on x-axis; 53.57 vs 65.48 on y-axis) and the image user study (44.57% preference vs 25.1%), with the advantage most pronounced at moderate scaling factors (110–120%). Against video seam carving [43] on 3D NeRF scenes, the proposed method is overwhelmingly preferred in the user study, with scores like 21-to-0 on the Farm and Harbour scenes at 50% x-axis, indicating that retargeting the 3D scene before rendering avoids the temporal inconsistencies inherent in frame-by-frame video carving. In comparisons with more recent image retargeting methods, the proposed method (using FullGrad saliency) edges out CarvingNet [46] (45.16% vs 41.93% preference), clearly beats the RL-based approach [22] (46.85% vs 32.43%), and outperforms Cycle-IR [49] (40.3% vs 34.46%), though the margins over the latter two are smaller and draw rates are higher. The paper notes that the FID advantage over seam carving narrows at extreme scaling (150%), where both methods struggle, and that the method's primary strength is its domain generality (images, NeRFs, meshes) rather than a large margin on any single 2D metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the discrete, greedy seam-finding process of Avidan & Shamir's seam carving as a continuous, global optimization over a learned neural deformation field (a simple MLP). While NeRF deformation methods (NeRF-Editing, D-NeRF, Voltemorph) use deformation fields for animation or dynamic scene reconstruction, this work repurposes the same architectural primitive for content-aware retargeting, injecting plausibility constraints (monotonicity, boundary preservation, shearing penalties) that encode the implicit rules of seam carving. The formulation is deliberately energy-agnostic, positioning the method as a flexible backbone compatible with any saliency formulation from the seam carving family (gradient-based, FullGrad, CarvingNet, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical impact is enabling content-aware retargeting of 3D scenes (NeRFs) and meshes, which was infeasible with discrete seam-based methods and for which no prior dedicated solution existed. The cross-domain generality (images, NeRFs, meshes with only minor energy-term changes) makes the framework a useful template for future editing operations. Key limitations include the per-input optimization cost (~1 min vs. ~0.5 s for seam carving on images), reliance on gradient-based energy that can produce flawed outputs without better saliency, and the absence of a principled quality metric for retargeting. Open questions raised by the authors include invertible neural networks for implicit inverse deformation in 3D and deep-learned deformation evaluation.
