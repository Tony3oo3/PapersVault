## Problem

Existing NeRF-based reconstruction methods require multi-view sharp images with calibrated poses, while single-image NeRF approaches depend on large pre-trained priors to address the ill-posed problem. Event-enhanced NeRF methods like E2NeRF still need multiple blurry views and longer event streams. This paper tackles the harder setting of recovering a full 3D scene representation and continuous camera trajectory from just a single motion-blurred image and its corresponding event stream, with no prior knowledge of camera poses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single motion-blurred RGB image (e.g., 100 ms exposure) and its temporally aligned event stream from a DAVIS346-style event camera. Camera poses are not provided as input; instead, the 6-DoF trajectory is jointly optimized. The output is a learned NeRF (MLP mapping 3D position and view direction to density and color) together with a cubic B-Spline trajectory in SE(3), from which deblurred sharp images and novel views can be rendered at arbitrary timestamps within the exposure window.

## Method

The camera motion is parameterized as a differentiable cubic B-Spline in SE(3) with four learnable control knots, enabling smooth continuous pose interpolation at any timestamp. The blurry image is modeled as the average of n=19 virtual sharp images rendered from the NeRF along the spline, while the event stream is accumulated over a normalized time interval Δt and compared against the log-intensity difference between the NeRF-rendered start and end poses. Both the NeRF MLP parameters and the spline control knots are jointly optimized via test-time optimization (80K iterations, Adam) by minimizing a combined photometric L2 loss on the blurry image and a normalized L2 loss on the accumulated event image, with no pre-training or external priors required.

## Results

On the five synthetic sequences (Livingroom, Whiteroom, Pinkcastle, Tanabata, Outdoorpool), BeNeRF achieves an average PSNR of 33.65 and LPIPS of 0.0675, substantially outperforming the best single-image deblurring baseline (SRN-Deblur at 25.85 PSNR) by over 7.8 dB. On the E2NeRF synthetic dataset, BeNeRF reaches 29.66 average PSNR and 0.0588 LPIPS, matching E2NeRF (29.77 PSNR) while surpassing it on LPIPS (0.0588 vs 0.0725), despite using only a single blurry image versus E2NeRF's multi-view input. On real-world DAVIS346 data, BeNeRF achieves a BRISQUE score of 23.87, significantly lower (better) than E2NeRF (35.94), EDI (31.20), and SRN-Deblur (38.30).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeNeRF decisively outperforms all six learning-based single-image deblurring methods (DeblurGANv2, SRN-Deblur, MPRNet, HINet, NAFNet, Restormer) by 7–10 dB in PSNR and by roughly 4× in LPIPS, confirming that these methods fail to generalize to severely motion-blurred, domain-shifted inputs. Against event-enhanced deblurring baselines, BeNeRF beats EDI [33] by ~4.3 dB average PSNR (33.65 vs 29.34) and eSLNet [47] by ~22 dB, the latter showing very poor generalization (11.24 avg PSNR). On the E2NeRF synthetic benchmark, BeNeRF nearly matches E2NeRF [34] in PSNR (29.66 vs 29.77) while surpassing it in LPIPS (0.0588 vs 0.0725), and clearly outperforms vanilla NeRF (22.91 PSNR) and Deblur-NeRF (21.71 PSNR). On real-world DAVIS346 data, BeNeRF achieves a BRISQUE of 23.87, beating E2NeRF (35.94) by 12 points, EDI (31.20) by 7 points, and SRN-Deblur (38.30) by 14 points—despite E2NeRF requiring multi-view images and a longer event stream. The only comparison where BeNeRF does not strictly win is the PSNR metric on the E2NeRF synthetic set, where it trails E2NeRF by a marginal 0.11 dB, likely due to E2NeRF's access to additional multi-view constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on NeRF's MLP-based implicit scene representation and volume rendering, but departs fundamentally from the multi-view paradigm by showing that a single motion-blurred image paired with a short event stream suffices for joint NeRF and camera trajectory recovery. It extends the event-enhanced deblurring line (EDI, eSLNet) from 2D image restoration to 3D scene reconstruction, and contrasts with E2NeRF's two-stage pipeline (event-based deblurring → COLMAP → NeRF) by formulating everything as a single differentiable optimization. The use of a cubic B-Spline in SE(3) for continuous trajectory modeling is a key methodological departure from prior NeRF-deblurring work that relies on discrete per-frame poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a single blurry image plus a brief event stream can match multi-view E2NeRF in reconstruction quality (and surpass it in LPIPS) suggests that event cameras provide a powerful, low-latency signal for 3D scene understanding that was previously underexploited in the NeRF context. This opens the door to 3D reconstruction in scenarios where multi-view capture is impractical (single-frame snapshots, high-speed motion, resource-constrained devices). Limitations include the requirement for a co-located event camera, the assumption of relatively short exposure intervals (four B-Spline control knots), and evaluation primarily on synthetic scenes with ground-truth poses; scaling to longer exposures, more complex dynamic scenes, and real-time inference remain open.
