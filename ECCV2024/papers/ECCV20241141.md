## Problem

Existing acceleration methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., LCM-LoRA, Guided-Distill) require extensive fine-tuning of the full network and are not universally applicable to different fine-tuned Stable Diffusion variants. Moreover, they rarely ensure that the generated content remains consistent before and after acceleration, and they often simplify or discard negative prompts, weakening controllability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241141/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a positive text prompt and a negative text prompt (composed of 0–100 sampled phrases from a pool of 832) as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image in just 4 diffusion steps without requiring classifier-free guidance. The negative prompt is consumed by a dedicated 18.5M-parameter adapter that computes per-layer cross-attention offsets, eliminating the need for a second forward pass through the U-Net. The adapter is trained once on SD v1.5 and then plugged training-free into other fine-tuned SD models, inpainting pipelines, image-to-image generation, and ControlNet-compatible workflows.

## Method

SUN introduces a lightweight adapter of 16 cross-attention modules (18.5M parameters) placed alongside each U-Net block, where each module shares the query Q with the original cross-attention but introduces new key K′ and value V′ projections for the negative prompt embedding. The final feature is computed as Z = Z_p − g(Z_n), where g is an Attention Normalization function with learnable α and β that balances the scale of positive and negative contributions, improving generalization across models. Training uses a combined loss: a CFG-distillation term that matches the student's single-pass output to the teacher's two-pass CFG result, plus a Multi-Step Consistency (MSC) loss that divides the original dense sampling trajectory into stages and forces the student to map back to the original trajectory at each stage (with probability p of using the student's own previous output as input), thereby preventing cumulative drift. Only the adapter parameters are optimized while the base U-Net remains frozen, and the key insight enabling universality is that the negative-to-positive offset δ is largely independent of the model's style, as visualized in Fig. 1.

## Results

On LAION-Aesthetics 6+ (512×512, guidance scale 8.0), SUN (18.5M params) achieves FID of 13.23 / 12.08 / 11.98 and CLIP score of 0.288 / 0.297 / 0.328 at 4 / 8 / 12 steps, outperforming all other adapting-free methods. In terms of inference speed, SUN (4 steps) reduces U-Net time to 0.274 s on V100 (FP32) versus 3.16 s for the 25-step DPM-Solver++ baseline, yielding a >10× speedup. On fine-tuned style models (Realistic Vision v5.1, Rev Animated, Anything v5), SUN achieves the lowest FID against 25-step ground truth at both 4 and 8 steps across all three models (e.g., 19.60 vs. 21.88 for LCM-LoRA on Realistic Vision v5.1 at 4 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among adapting-free methods (those pluggable into any fine-tuned SD without retraining), SUN dominates: it beats LCM-LoRA by 3.6 FID points at 4 steps (13.23 vs. 16.83) and by 0.017 CLIP (0.288 vs. 0.271), and beats DPM-Solver++ by 5.2 FID points at 4 steps (13.23 vs. 18.43). Against the full-model distillation baselines, SUN trades a small FID gap to LCM at 4 steps (13.23 vs. 11.10) but matches or exceeds LCM on CLIP at 4 steps (0.288 vs. 0.286) while using only 18.5M vs. 860M parameters and remaining adapting-free. SUN also outperforms Guided-Distill on FID at 4 steps (13.23 vs. 15.12) and CLIP at 4 steps (0.288 vs. 0.272). On the fine-tuned-model consistency evaluation (Table 2), SUN achieves the best FID at both 4 and 8 steps across all three style models, e.g., 19.60 vs. 21.88 (LCM-LoRA) and 20.31 (Guided-Distill) on Realistic Vision v5.1 at 4 steps. The only metric where SUN trails is 12-step CLIP vs. DPM-Solver++ (0.328 vs. 0.336), a negligible gap that disappears at fewer steps where SUN's advantage is largest.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUN sits at the intersection of CFG distillation (Guided-Distill, LCM, LCM-LoRA) and lightweight adapter methods (ControlNet, IP-Adapter), but departs from both in a key way: rather than fine-tuning the full U-Net or applying LoRA to existing weights, it introduces a small cross-attention adapter that exclusively encodes the negative-prompt offset, exploiting the observation that this offset is largely style-invariant across fine-tuned SD variants. The Multi-Step Consistency loss extends the consistency-distillation idea from LCM/CM by explicitly correcting for cumulative student-teacher drift across multiple inference stages, rather than mapping the entire trajectory to a single step. The Attention Normalization mechanism is a practical addition not present in prior adapter or distillation work, designed to balance positive and negative feature magnitudes for cross-model generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play property (train once on SD v1.5, apply to any same-architecture fine-tuned model without retraining) addresses a real practical bottleneck: stylized community models on Civitai are constantly updated, and full distillation per model is infeasible. The 18.5M parameter count versus 860M for full-network distillation makes the approach accessible on consumer hardware. However, universality is bounded to models sharing the SD v1.5 U-Net architecture and noise-prediction scheme, and the FID evaluation on stylized models uses 25-step DPM++ as a proxy ground truth rather than a curated reference set, leaving open whether consistency claims hold for architecturally different diffusion backbones (e.g., SDXL, DiT-based models). The paper also does not explore how SUN interacts with newer guidance schemes or multi-prompt conditioning.
