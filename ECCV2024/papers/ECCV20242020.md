## Problem

Existing traffic video datasets predominantly focus on vehicle and driver behavior, leaving pedestrian-centric fine-grained spatial-temporal understanding underexplored despite pedestrians being involved in over 20% of traffic accidents. Current traffic event models lack behavioral granularity, and traditional word-level caption metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, CIDER) fail to capture semantic correctness in long, structured descriptions of multi-agent traffic interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242020/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a traffic video segment (1–15 s, 1080p at 24 fps) from one of three synchronized camera views (overhead, driver recorder, or ego-centric Tobii Pro Glasses 3), a target instance bounding box from the first frame, and a structured text prompt. It produces a dense caption of ~58.7 words organized into four semantic aspects—Location, Attention, Behavior, and Context—aligned to one of five pedestrian behavior phases (Pre-recognition, Recognition, Judgement, Action, Avoidance). The dataset additionally provides per-frame 3D gaze direction vectors and 3D head positions for the target pedestrian, synchronized across all camera views via analog radio audio signals.

## Method

The paper introduces three coupled contributions. First, the WTS dataset is built through a semi-automatic annotation pipeline: professional traffic-safety analysts define phase boundaries, annotators check a structured 180-item factual checklist (position, action, attributes, attention, environment) per segment, and GPT-3.5 converts the checked items into natural-language captions, followed by two rounds of human verification. Second, Instance-VideoLLM extends the Video-LLaMA architecture (EVA-CLIP ViT-G/14 encoder + Video Q-Former + frozen Vicuna-7B LLM) by projecting a binary bounding-box mask (resized to 224×224, flattened) into a spatial token that is concatenated with the video query embedding as an instance-aware soft prompt, guiding the LLM to caption the specific target pedestrian or vehicle. Third, LLMScorer evaluates captions by decomposing them into four aspects and computing a weighted combination (0.7/0.3) of a semantic score (per-aspect binary correctness judged by GPT-3.5-turbo) and a syntactic score (cosine similarity of text-embedding-3-small vectors over answers to aspect-specific questions), achieving near-parity with human evaluation (0.242 vs. 0.243 average).

## Results

On the WTS dataset (staged + BDD100K), the proposed Instance-VideoLLM achieves the best performance across all metrics: BLUE-4 0.121, METEOR 0.409, ROUGE-L 0.417, and CIDER 0.389 under the best prompt setting (P-C). On the novel LLMScorer metric, Instance-VideoLLM scores 0.351 (Semantic 0.285, Syntactic 0.508), substantially outperforming Video-LLaMA (0.117) and Video-ChatGPT (0.143). The semantic sub-score gap is particularly large (0.285 vs. 0.008/0.004), indicating that fine-tuned instance-aware models capture far more correct domain-specific meaning. The LLMScorer correlates closely with human evaluation (0.242 vs. 0.243 on 50% of the validation set).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Instance-VideoLLM outperforms both Video-LLaMA and Video-ChatGPT on every reported metric. On traditional n-gram/overlap metrics (P-C prompt), it leads Video-LLaMA by +0.076 METEOR (0.409 vs. 0.247) and +0.179 CIDER (0.389 vs. 0.210), and leads Video-ChatGPT by +0.142 METEOR (0.409 vs. 0.267) and +0.107 CIDER (0.389 vs. 0.282). The most striking gap appears on the proposed LLMScorer: Instance-VideoLLM scores 0.351 versus 0.117 (Video-LLaMA) and 0.143 (Video-ChatGPT), a 2.5–3× improvement. The semantic sub-score (0.285 vs. 0.008/0.004) reveals that off-the-shelf Video LLMs produce captions that look syntactically plausible but are almost entirely wrong on domain-specific facts (location, attention, behavior, context). Fine-tuning alone (VideoLLM vs. Video-LLaMA) yields a large jump (METEOR 0.389 vs. 0.247), but adding the instance spatial token (Instance-VideoLLM) provides a further consistent gain across all metrics, confirming the value of region-conditioned prompting for pedestrian-centric captioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Video-LLaMA architecture (EVA-CLIP ViT-G/14 encoder, Video Q-Former, positional embeddings) by introducing a spatial token derived from target-instance bounding boxes, inspired by Pixel LLM's binary-mask approach, to enable instance-level video captioning. It departs from prior traffic datasets like BDD-X and BDD100K, which focus on driver/vehicle behavior, by centering on pedestrian-centric analysis with 3D gaze annotations and multi-view (overhead + ego) synchronization. The LLMScorer metric builds on GPTScore's evaluation paradigm but adds aspect-specific semantic and syntactic decomposition tailored to structured traffic descriptions, addressing the known weakness of word-overlap metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, CIDER) on long, domain-specific captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a genuine gap: pedestrian behavior accounts for over 20% of traffic accidents yet remains underrepresented in existing traffic video datasets, and the 3D gaze data (52,823 frames across 6 subjects) is unprecedented for traffic safety analysis. The stark semantic-vs-syntactic score gap (0.285 vs 0.508 for the best model) reveals that current VideoLLMs produce structurally plausible but semantically incorrect descriptions in this domain, a finding that cautions against relying on traditional metrics for long-form domain-specific captioning. Open questions include whether the staged, controlled-environment data generalizes to natural traffic, how multi-view caption consistency can be enforced, and whether the instance-aware spatial token approach scales beyond single-target scenarios.
