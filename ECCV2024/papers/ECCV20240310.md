## Problem

Existing video editing methods rely on coarse textual instructions that cannot express fine-grained, localized semantic edits such as adding a specific object at a precise location, removing a body part, or inserting a stock photo into a video. While image editing tools (e.g., Photoshop, inpainting models) enable such precise pixel-level modifications on still frames, propagating those edits across video frames while preserving temporal and geometric consistency remains unsolved. Naive DDIM inversion of video latents fails because cumulative approximation errors cause only the first frame to reconstruct correctly, producing incoherent videos.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (14 frames, 16:9 aspect ratio) and a user-edited first frame produced by any image editing tool (Photoshop, ControlNet, text-based inpainting, etc.) as input, and outputs a full 14-frame edited video where the first-frame modifications are propagated to all subsequent frames. The edited first frame serves as the conditioning signal for the video diffusion model, replacing the original first frame. The method is entirely training-free and operates on the Stable Video Diffusion latent space, with all normalization and rescaling performed per-channel on VAE-encoded latents.

## Method

VIDEOSHOP inverts the source video's latents in the EDM framework using a novel noise extrapolation scheme that exploits the near-linear trajectory of latents during denoising (measured cosine similarity of 0.9919 between consecutive steps). Instead of the standard naive approximation that substitutes x_t for x_{t+1} in the inversion formula, the method linearly extrapolates the noise component (x_t − x_0) scaled by the ratio of noise levels σ_{t+1}/σ_t, with a threshold Σ below which fresh Gaussian noise is sampled to avoid division by near-zero values. A second contribution is latent normalization: the VAE-encoded latents are normalized to unit standard deviation before inversion to counteract the unnormalized encoder's high variance, and the denoised output is rescaled to match the target image's latent statistics before VAE decoding. The entire pipeline—encode, normalize, invert with noise extrapolation, denoise conditioned on the edited first frame, rescale, and decode—requires no training and completes in roughly 2 minutes per 14-frame video.

## Results

On the MagicBrush dataset, VIDEOSHOP achieves the best edit fidelity (CLIP_tgt 88.80, TIFA 64.40) and source faithfulness (Flow 1.90, FVD 1478.76, SSIM 71.92) among all methods, while maintaining competitive temporal consistency (CLIP_TC 95.16). On the expert-curated HD-VILA-100M dataset, VIDEOSHOP again leads in edit fidelity (CLIP_tgt 87.96, TIFA 66.14) and source faithfulness (Flow+ 4.47, FVD 1718.31, SSIM 65.63). In human evaluation, VIDEOSHOP is preferred over all baselines in 96.93% of editing quality comparisons and 84.62% of video generation quality comparisons, while running 2.23× faster than the average baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIDEOSHOP outperforms all five baselines on the majority of edit fidelity and source faithfulness metrics across both datasets. Against BDIA [62], VIDEOSHOP achieves substantially lower optical flow error (1.90 vs. 2.83) and FVD (1479 vs. 3483), indicating far better motion preservation, while BDIA produces large visual inconsistencies. Against Pix2Video [6] and RAVE [28], VIDEOSHOP leads on nearly every metric; both baselines undesirably alter the style of the source video. Against Fate/Zero [40], VIDEOSHOP wins on edit fidelity (CLIP_tgt 88.80 vs. 84.87) and flow (1.90 vs. 4.42), though Fate/Zero scores slightly higher on CLIP_src (92.41 vs. 90.95). Against Spacetime [58], VIDEOSHOP dominates on all fidelity and faithfulness metrics (e.g., FVD 1479 vs. 4816), while Spacetime retains a small edge in temporal consistency (CLIP_TC 96.58 vs. 95.16). On the expert dataset, RAVE [28] edges out VIDEOSHOP on overall Flow (5.59 vs. 5.85), but VIDEOSHOP still leads on Flow+ (4.47 vs. 4.54), FVD, SSIM, and all edit fidelity scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIDEOSHOP builds on the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model and its underlying EDM denoising framework, extending the DDIM inversion paradigm from images to video. It departs from the dominant text-based video editing paradigm (Pix2Video, Fate/Zero, Spacetime, RAVE) by conditioning on an arbitrary edited first frame rather than a text prompt, and from exact inversion methods (BDIA, EDICT, FPI) by introducing a lightweight noise extrapolation step that exploits the near-linear trajectory of video latents during denoising. The latent normalization and rescaling steps address a previously unacknowledged instability in unnormalized VAE-encoded video latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIDEOSHOP democratizes precise video editing by reducing it to a well-studied image-editing task, making Photoshop-level control accessible without any model fine-tuning. Key limitations include VAE information loss for fine details, degraded temporal consistency under large or flickering motion, and reliance on the base model's knowledge (no new motion can be introduced). Open questions include scaling to longer videos beyond 14 frames, combining image-based edits with explicit motion/trajectory controls, and extending the approach to 3D content.
