## Problem

LLM-based autonomous driving methods that fine-tune on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning suffer from a misalignment problem: the model's textual reasoning can correctly describe a scene (e.g., identifying a stopped car ahead) while the consequent trajectory planning contradicts that reasoning (e.g., planning to accelerate forward). This inconsistency between explanation and decision remains unaddressed by prior LLM-based AD approaches, undermining both safety and interpretability.

## Input / Output

The system consumes six-camera multi-view images, ego vehicle status (velocity, heading angular velocity, acceleration, and the last three frames' trajectory), and a structured multi-turn CoT prompt. The multi-view images are first projected into a bird's-eye-view feature map via a Lift-Splat-Shoot-inspired encoder, then partitioned into h×w spatial grids and flattened into visual tokens that are concatenated with text tokens before being fed to the LLM. The output is a sequential CoT response covering perception, prediction, and decision-making, followed by a 3-second trajectory of 6 waypoints.

## Method

RDA-Driver builds on a LLaVa-style architecture (BEV encoder → region-flattening adapter → LLaMA) but introduces a reasoning-decision alignment objective that couples CoT quality with planning accuracy. Two complementary contrastive losses are applied: a ranking loss over k model-generated candidates (ranked by trajectory L2 error) that pushes the model to assign higher token-average log-probability to better CoTs, and a binary loss over data-augmented pairs where CoT labels from similar scenes with different decisions are permuted to create negative samples. The redesigned multi-turn CoT prompt enforces a human-like reasoning chain—perception (identify key objects), prediction (estimate motion and priority of interactions), decision-making (determine direction and speed), and planning (output 6 waypoints)—making the full pipeline interpretable. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning on only 4,072 DriveLM-nuScenes samples with the total loss combining standard cross-entropy, the ranking loss, and the binary alignment loss.

## Results

On the nuScenes benchmark (UniAD metrics), RDA-Driver achieves a state-of-the-art average L2 error of 0.80 m and collision rate of 0.32%, outperforming GPT-Driver (0.84 m / 0.44%) and DME-Driver (0.98 m / 0.29%). Under ST-P3 metrics, RDA-Driver reaches 0.40 m L2 and 0.10% collision, matching VAD's L2 (0.37 m) while halving its collision rate (0.10% vs. 0.14%). On the more challenging DriveLM-nuScenes benchmark (UniAD metrics), RDA-Driver maintains 0.82 m L2 and 0.38% collision, substantially outperforming GPT-Driver (0.87 m / 0.67%) and UniAD (3.00 m / 0.72%), which degrade significantly when ego-status reliance is removed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under UniAD metrics on nuScenes, RDA-Driver (0.80 m L2, 0.32% collision) beats GPT-Driver (0.84 m, 0.44%) by 0.04 m in L2 and 0.12% in collision, and outperforms DME-Driver (0.98 m, 0.29%) in L2 by 0.18 m while trailing slightly in collision (0.32% vs. 0.29%). Against the non-LLM end-to-end baselines UniAD (1.03 m, 0.31%) and ST-P3 (2.11 m, 0.71%), RDA-Driver achieves substantially lower L2 error with comparable or better collision rates. Under ST-P3 metrics, RDA-Driver matches VAD's L2 (0.40 vs. 0.37 m) while achieving a markedly lower collision rate (0.10% vs. 0.14%), and outperforms DriveVLM (0.40 m, 0.27%) in collision by 0.17%. On the ego-status-robust DriveLM-nuScenes benchmark, RDA-Driver (0.82 m, 0.38%) dramatically outperforms UniAD (3.00 m, 0.72%) and GPT-Driver (0.87 m, 0.67%), with the latter's collision rate nearly doubling relative to nuScenes, highlighting RDA-Driver's robustness when ego history is less predictive.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDA-Driver builds on the LLaVa-style vision-language architecture (CLIP/BEV encoder + adapter + LLM decoder) and the Lift-Splat-Shoot mechanism for multi-view-to-BEV feature encoding, while adapting the contrastive alignment loss from AFT to the driving domain. It departs from prior LLM-based AD methods (GPT-Driver, DME-Driver, DriveVLM, Reason2Drive) by explicitly identifying and constraining the misalignment between generated CoT reasoning and the resulting trajectory, a gap the authors argue has been overlooked. The redesigned multi-turn CoT (perception → prediction → decision → planning) extends the single-prompt approaches in earlier work by structuring reasoning to mirror human driving cognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and address the CoT-decision misalignment problem in LLM-based autonomous driving, offering a practical contrastive-loss remedy that improves both reasoning quality (CoT score 62→71) and planning metrics with only 1/7 of the typical training data. However, the evaluation is limited to open-loop settings on nuScenes variants, the input modality is restricted to static multi-view images (no video or temporal context), and the alignment loss, while effective, is a relatively standard contrastive objective applied in a new domain. Open questions include whether the alignment strategy transfers to closed-loop simulation, whether it scales to more complex multi-agent interactions, and whether the BEV-flattening adapter generalizes beyond the specific grid resolution used here.
