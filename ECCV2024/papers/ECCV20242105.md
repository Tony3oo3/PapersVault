## Problem

Diffusion models require full network inference at every denoising step, but not all steps demand equal computation—early steps reconstruct coarse structure while later steps refine fine details. Existing early-exiting methods designed for single-step classification tasks fail in diffusion because the multi-step, time-series nature of denoising causes error accumulation that uniform or static exit strategies cannot handle, leading to severe quality degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, the model consumes a noisy latent image (32×32 for CIFAR-10, 64×64 for CelebA, 256×256 for ImageNet and MS-COCO) along with a class label or text prompt, and progressively produces a clean generated image. Uniquely, at every intermediate Transformer layer the UEM outputs a scalar uncertainty score in [0,1] that gates whether subsequent layers are executed, so the effective network depth varies per sample and per timestep. The backbone is U-ViT with 13 layers (Small) or 21 layers (Large), and sampling uses 1000 steps (Euler-Maruyama) or 50 steps (DPM-Solver) depending on the dataset.

## Method

AdaDiff introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a timestep-aware Uncertainty Estimation Module (UEM)—a lightweight fully-connected layer with sigmoid activation, conditioned on timestep embeddings and attached to each intermediate layer—estimates per-prediction uncertainty; its pseudo-ground-truth is the Tanh-smoothed absolute error between the intermediate prediction and the target noise, making it suitable for the regression nature of denoising rather than the confidence/entropy proxies used in classification-based early exiting. Second, an Uncertainty-Aware Layer-wise Loss (UA-Loss) reweights each layer's denoising objective by (1 − u_{i,t}), so that low-uncertainty timesteps and layers receive higher training weight, explicitly teaching the network to preserve information in the steps where early exiting is most likely and thereby mitigating cross-step error accumulation. The two losses are jointly optimized with the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss (equal weighting λ=β=1), and at inference the UEM's uncertainty score is compared against a threshold to decide whether to skip remaining layers, yielding an average 40–48% layer reduction with minimal FID degradation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32, 1000 steps), AdaDiff achieves FID 3.7 with 47.7% average layer reduction, compared to the full U-ViT baseline FID of 3.11. On CelebA (64×64), it reaches FID 3.9 with 46.2% layer reduction. On ImageNet 256×256 (50 steps), AdaDiff obtains FID 4.5 with 45.2% layer reduction, and on MS-COCO 256×256 it achieves FID 7.4 with 43.6% reduction. Notably, the full model trained with AdaDiff's uncertainty-aware layer-wise loss (without any early exiting) improves FID to 2.7 on CIFAR-10 and 2.63 on CelebA, surpassing the original U-ViT (3.11 and 2.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDiff substantially outperforms both BERTxiT [48] and CALM [39] across all four datasets: on CIFAR-10, AdaDiff achieves FID 3.7 at 47.7% layer reduction versus BERTxiT's FID 20.5 and CALM's FID 20.0 at only ~20% reduction; on ImageNet, the gap widens to FID 4.5 vs. 23.5 (BERTxiT) and 21.4 (CALM). Against the static early-exiting method [31], AdaDiff achieves FID 3.9 with 46.2% layer reduction on CelebA versus Static EE's FID 5.04 at 32.1% reduction, demonstrating that dynamic per-sample exiting is critical. Compared to S-Pruning [10] (structural pruning, 100 DDIM steps), AdaDiff achieves FID 4.12 vs. 5.29 on CIFAR-10 and 4.67 vs. 6.24 on CelebA at a similar ~45% layer reduction ratio. Finally, the full model trained with AdaDiff's uncertainty-aware layer-wise loss (no early exiting) improves upon the original U-ViT [3] baseline, reaching FID 2.7 vs. 3.11 on CIFAR-10 and 2.63 vs. 2.87 on CelebA, indicating the loss itself provides a training benefit independent of the adaptive inference mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaDiff repurposes the early exiting paradigm—originally developed for single-step classification and regression tasks (BERTxiT, CALM)—to the fundamentally different multi-step denoising setting of diffusion models. The key conceptual departure is that prior early exiting methods treat each forward pass independently, whereas diffusion's iterative nature means premature exits at early timesteps can accumulate errors across subsequent steps. The paper addresses this by introducing a timestep-aware uncertainty estimation module (inspired by BERTxiT's regression extension) and an uncertainty-weighted layer-wise loss that explicitly accounts for cross-step error propagation, building on the observation from Min-SNR weighting [11] that specific timesteps disproportionately influence neighboring steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~45% computation reduction with under 1.5 FID degradation across four datasets and three generation paradigms (unconditional, class-conditional, text-guided) makes this practically relevant for real-time and resource-constrained deployment of diffusion models. The method's model-agnostic design (applicable to both CNN and Transformer backbones) and composability with step-reduction solvers like DPM-Solver broaden its utility. However, the authors acknowledge that performance degrades sharply beyond 60% efficiency, and the method only addresses depth (layer count) while leaving adaptive sampling-step allocation unexplored. The finding that the uncertainty-weighted loss improves even the full model's FID (2.7 vs. 3.11 on CIFAR-10) suggests the loss formulation itself may have independent value beyond acceleration.
