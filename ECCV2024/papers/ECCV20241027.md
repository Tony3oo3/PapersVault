## Problem

Existing Lidar Panoptic Segmentation methods are locked to fixed, manually-labeled class vocabularies and cannot adapt to new object categories or geographic variations without retraining. No prior work achieves class-agnostic instance segmentation in Lidar, and zero-shot classification of segmented objects in the Lidar domain remains unexplored. A fundamental obstacle is the absence of any labeled data that would support training a general, promptable Lidar segmentation model.

## Input / Output

At inference, SAL takes a full 360° Lidar point cloud (N×4: xyz coordinates plus sensor intensity) and a list of free-form text prompts (e.g., 'car', 'trash bin', 'streetcar') as its only inputs—no image features are required. For each learned object query, the model outputs a binary segmentation mask over the point cloud, a binary objectness score, and a CLIP-space token; the token is matched against CLIP-encoded text prompts to yield a zero-shot class probability distribution. This design allows arbitrary vocabulary changes at test time without any retraining.

## Method

SAL comprises a pseudo-label engine and a zero-shot model. The engine uses SAM to generate class-agnostic masks in camera images, MaskCLIP to extract per-mask CLIP image tokens, and a calibrated multi-modal setup to unproject both into the Lidar frame; DBSCAN-based geometric refinement compensates for calibration noise. The zero-shot model pairs a Minkowski U-Net sparse-convolutional backbone with a Transformer decoder that predicts, per query, a segmentation mask (dot-product with point features), an objectness score, and a regressed CLIP token via a three-layer MLP. Training jointly optimizes binary cross-entropy (objectness), mask cross-entropy + dice (segmentation), and cosine distance (token distillation), effectively distilling SAM's objectness and CLIP's semantics into a purely Lidar-based network. To handle the sparse pseudo-label coverage (14% on SemanticKITTI), the authors remove unlabeled points and apply a FrankenFrustum augmentation that replicates labeled frustum regions around the vertical axis, closing the domain gap between partially-labeled training and full-scan inference.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, SAL's zero-shot model achieves 24.8 PQ (42% of the best fully-supervised baseline GP-S3Net at 63.3 PQ), rising to 48.5 PQ (59% of MaskPLS's 81.7 PQ) when prompted with super-classes. On nuScenes, the zero-shot model reaches 38.4 PQ (54% of the best supervised baseline PHNet at 74.7 PQ), improving to 52.6 PQ with super-class prompting. For class-agnostic segmentation (Semantic Oracle), SAL achieves 70.7 PQ on the camera-visible frustum, substantially outperforming the best hand-crafted baseline SAM+DBS(replace) at 48.7 PQ. When trained with pseudo-labels on the full 360° point cloud, SAL matches or exceeds fully-supervised MaskPLS on nuScenes (70.5 vs 57.7 PQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAL is the first zero-shot LPS model, so all comparisons are against fully-supervised baselines trained on human-labeled data. On SemanticKITTI, the zero-shot SAL (24.8 PQ) trails the best supervised method GP-S3Net (63.3 PQ) by 38.5 PQ, but narrows the gap to 33.2 PQ when using super-class prompts (48.5 vs 81.7 PQ for MaskPLS). On nuScenes, zero-shot SAL (38.4 PQ) reaches 54% of PHNet (74.7 PQ) and 61% of PolarSeg (63.4 PQ), with the gap further reduced under super-class evaluation (52.6 PQ). When trained with pseudo-labels (no human labels), SAL outperforms MaskPLS on nuScenes (70.5 vs 57.7 PQ) and is competitive on SemanticKITTI (59.5 vs 59.8 PQ), while surpassing MaskPLS on super-class PQ (81.7 vs 78.4). For class-agnostic segmentation, SAL (70.7 PQ) substantially outperforms the best hand-crafted baseline SAM+DBS(replace) (48.7 PQ) by 22 PQ, demonstrating that distilling foundation models into a learned architecture is far more effective than directly lifting image features to 3D at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAL builds on the MaskPLS Transformer decoder architecture (Minkowski U-Net backbone + query-based decoder) for Lidar panoptic segmentation, but repurposes it for a fundamentally different training regime: instead of manual per-point labels, it distills SAM masks and CLIP tokens generated by a pseudo-label engine. This departs from prior work like OpenScene, which lifts image features to 3D at inference time and thus requires camera coverage, by internalizing 2D foundation model knowledge into a pure Lidar model that operates on full 360° point clouds. The FrankenFrustum augmentation and partial-label training strategy are novel contributions addressing the challenge of training on sparse pseudo-labels (only 14% coverage on SemanticKITTI).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAL establishes the first zero-shot Lidar Panoptic Segmentation pipeline, demonstrating that 2D vision foundation models can be distilled into a Lidar model that segments and classifies arbitrary objects via text prompts without retraining. The practical impact is significant: the pseudo-labeling engine enables rapid adaptation to new datasets (Waymo example in under a week) and the clear scaling trend (more pseudo-labeled data → better performance) outlines a path toward universal Lidar segmentation. However, the zero-shot gap remains substantial (42–54% of supervised PQ), cross-sensor generalization is unaddressed, and the method is currently limited to training on specific datasets, leaving open whether the approach can converge to supervised-level performance with sufficient data scale.
