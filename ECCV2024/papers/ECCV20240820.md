## Problem

In Multi-Label Class-Incremental Learning, only the current task's classes are labeled during training while past and future labels remain unavailable (task-level partial label). This disjoint label space causes models to produce over-confident multi-label predictions with high false-positive rates, which previous SOTA methods like KRT and AGCN have not addressed, thereby exacerbating catastrophic forgetting as the label space grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240820/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a vector of confidence scores over all classes seen so far (the accumulated label space C^{1:t}), not just the current task's classes. During training, supervision is available only for the current task's labels; old-class supervision comes from knowledge distillation of the previous model's predictions, and the model must simultaneously predict across the full disjoint label space at test time.

## Method

CSC comprises two components: (1) a Class-Incremental Graph Convolutional Network (CI-GCN) that bridges isolated label spaces via a stacked general GCN (with a learnable, dynamically extended correlation matrix updated by gradient descent using both ground-truth and pseudo-labels) followed by a specific GCN (with an image-dependent correlation matrix adaptively estimated from the input graph nodes via global pooling and a convolutional layer), and (2) a max-entropy regularization that penalizes over-confident output distributions on old classes using Shannon entropy, directly reducing false-positive errors. The final prediction is the sum of class-related outputs (class activation maps passed through global pooling) and graph-based representations (V2 passed through a fully connected layer). Training combines cross-entropy on current-task labels, knowledge distillation from the previous model on old classes, and the max-entropy term, all optimized jointly with a TResNetM backbone.

## Results

On MS-COCO B0-C10 (the most challenging scenario with 80 classes), CSC without replay achieves 72.8% last mAP, 64.9% CF1, and 66.8% OF1, surpassing the previous SOTA KRT by 6.9%, 9.3%, and 10.2% respectively. With a 20/class replay buffer, CSC-R reaches 74.8% last mAP and 79.6% average mAP. On PASCAL VOC B0-C4, CSC-R achieves 87.9% last mAP, outperforming KRT-R (83.4%) by 4.5%. The method also demonstrates strong robustness across scenarios, with only a 1.3% mAP drop from B0-C4 to B4-C2 on VOC, compared to a 16.7% drop for AGCN-R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSC outperforms all 10 compared baselines across every scenario and buffer size on both MS-COCO and PASCAL VOC. Against the current SOTA KRT [10], CSC (no replay) gains 6.9% mAP on MS-COCO B0-C10 (72.8 vs 65.9) and 4.5% on PASCAL VOC B0-C4 (87.9 vs 83.4); with 20/class replay, CSC-R leads KRT-R by 4.6% mAP on MS-COCO (74.8 vs 70.2). The multi-label baseline AGCN [15] is surpassed by 11.4% mAP in the no-replay setting (72.8 vs 61.4), while single-label methods (oEWC, LwF, PODNet, DER++, ER) lag substantially, with oEWC achieving only 24.3% mAP. The replay-based multi-label methods PRS [17] and OCDM [22] perform poorly (27.9–28.5% mAP), indicating their sampling strategies are ineffective under the partial-label MLCIL setting. Notably, CSC without any replay buffer (72.8% mAP) exceeds KRT-R with 20/class replay (70.2% mAP), and CSC-R with 29.4M parameters (TResNetM) outperforms the prompt-based L2P [47] with 86.0M parameters (ViT-B/16) in both MS-COCO scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLCIL line initiated by AGCN [15] and KRT [10], which addressed the partial-label problem via fixed statistical correlation matrices built from pseudo-labels and knowledge distillation, respectively. The key architectural departure is replacing these fixed, error-accumulating statistical CMs with a learnable, dynamically extended CI-GCN whose general and specific correlation matrices are updated via gradient descent, eliminating the need for prior co-occurrence counting. The max-entropy regularization draws on the broader confidence-calibration literature (Pereyra et al. [35], Mukhoti et al. [34]) but is the first to apply it specifically to the over-confident multi-label output distributions characteristic of MLCIL under partial supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying over-confident false-positive predictions as a primary (and previously underappreciated) failure mode in MLCIL, the paper reframes part of the catastrophic-forgetting problem as a calibration problem, offering a simple and effective remedy. The CI-GCN's ability to learn cross-task label relationships without relying on error-prone pseudo-label statistics is a practical architectural improvement that could transfer to other incremental multi-label settings. Limitations include evaluation on only two relatively small label spaces (80 and 20 classes), a single CNN backbone family (TResNetM), and the max-entropy term being applied only to old classes; open questions include whether the approach scales to very large label spaces, integrates naturally with transformer backbones, and whether the calibration benefit persists under more aggressive incremental schedules.
