## Problem

Fine-tuning quantized diffusion models for downstream tasks such as personalization has been unexplored. When applying the PEQA-style baseline (freezing quantized integers and updating only per-channel scales), the method fails to capture inter-channel weight update patterns and cannot adequately model the distinct roles of different denoising timesteps, resulting in degraded prompt fidelity, subject fidelity, and image quality that worsens with more training iterations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-quantized diffusion model checkpoint (4-bit or 8-bit, from Q-Diffusion), a set of reference images for a target concept, and text prompts with a unique identifier token [V]. It produces a small set of fine-tuned inter-channel and intra-channel scale parameters (~2.48 MB total) that are applied to the frozen quantized integer weights at inference time. These scale parameters are stored per dataset and can be swapped to switch between different personalization tasks without re-quantizing the base model.

## Method

TuneQDM introduces two key mechanisms for memory-efficient fine-tuning of quantized diffusion models. First, the multi-channel-wise scale update (MCSU) decomposes the quantization scale into separable inter-channel (s_in ∈ R^n) and intra-channel (s_out ∈ R^m) functions, so the fine-tuned weight becomes (s_out + Δs_out)·(W_q − z)·(s_in + Δs_in), capturing inter-channel weight update patterns that the baseline's single per-channel scale cannot represent. Second, the timestep-aware scale update (TAS) uniformly partitions the denoising timestep interval into N segments and independently fine-tunes a separate copy of the scale parameters for each segment, creating N lightweight 'experts' that reflect the distinct roles of different timesteps in the diffusion process. During inference, the quantized integers remain fixed and only the appropriate scale set is selected based on the current timestep. The total trainable parameters are only (m+n) per layer (~0.62M total vs. 859M for full precision), making the approach both parameter- and memory-efficient.

## Results

On single-subject generation (4-bit), TuneQDM achieves DINO-I 0.551, CLIP-I 0.802, and CLIP-T 0.306, improving subject fidelity by +6.16% over the PEQA baseline while using only 0.62M trainable parameters (vs. 859M for full-precision). In the 8-bit setting, TuneQDM matches or slightly exceeds the baseline on all three metrics (e.g., CLIP-T 0.307 vs. 0.300). On CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, TuneQDM outperforms QLoRA in both IS and FID with fewer parameters, and the ablation shows the best FID of 3.80 (vs. 4.53 full-precision) when combining multi-channel-wise scale update with 2 timestep experts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against a PEQA-based baseline (freezing quantized integers and updating only intra-channel scales). In the 4-bit single-subject setting, TuneQDM wins decisively on subject fidelity (DINO-I 0.551 vs. 0.519, +6.16%) and image quality (CLIP-I 0.802 vs. 0.787), at a small cost in prompt fidelity (CLIP-T 0.306 vs. 0.313, −2.23%). In the 8-bit setting the gap narrows, with TuneQDM slightly trailing on DINO-I and CLIP-I but leading on CLIP-T. Against QLoRA on CIFAR-10 unconditional generation, TuneQDM outperforms it in both IS and FID while using fewer trainable parameters. The paper also notes that in multi-subject generation both quantized methods (baseline and TuneQDM) show a performance drop relative to full-precision, though TuneQDM consistently captures both subject tokens more reliably than the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the PEQA paradigm (Kim et al., NeurIPS 2024) for fine-tuning quantized LLMs to the diffusion model setting, using Q-Diffusion checkpoints as the quantized starting point and DreamBooth as the personalization objective. It departs from PEQA in two key ways: (1) decomposing the per-channel quantization scale into separable inter-channel and intra-channel components to capture cross-channel weight update patterns that the baseline's intra-channel-only scales cannot represent, and (2) introducing timestep-specific scale experts inspired by multi-expert diffusion training (e-Diffi, MEME). The authors explicitly position this as the first work to fine-tune a quantized diffusion model for a downstream task, carving out a new intersection between model compression and personalization that neither the quantization nor the personalization literature had addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As diffusion models scale toward 5B+ parameters, the ability to fine-tune a 4-bit checkpoint with only ~0.6M trainable parameters (vs. 859M for full precision) and store just ~2.5MB of task-specific scales per dataset has direct practical value for deployment on resource-constrained hardware and for rapid task switching. However, the evidence is limited to a single architecture (SDXL UNet) and personalization/unconditional generation tasks; whether the multi-channel scale decomposition and timestep-aware strategy generalize to DiT-based models, lower bit widths (2-bit), or more complex downstream tasks (inpainting, video) remains open. The paper also acknowledges failure cases in multi-subject generation where the quantized model cannot recover what full precision can, suggesting a fundamental capacity ceiling at very low bit widths that scale-only updates may not overcome.
