## Problem

UAV-based intelligent plant protection requires precise detection of tree pests and diseases, yet no dedicated high-resolution UAV dataset exists for this task, and existing agricultural detection datasets are limited to indoor/close-range captures with only one or two classes. This gap prevents models from learning real-world environmental variability and robust multi-class discrimination needed for large-scale, high-precision aerial operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242023/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes UAV aerial RGB images at two resolutions: a low-resolution (LL) version of 640×640 patches extracted via sliding window (630×630 step) from high-resolution (LH) 5472×3648 orthomosaics, and a multi-class CWC dataset at 800×600 (with minor variations at 586×444 and 643×500). Outputs are bounding boxes with class labels—single-class 'unhealthy' for PDT and 11 plant species for CWC. During inference, both LL and LH images are detected simultaneously, with the LH version processed at full resolution to handle dense small targets.

## Method

YOLO-DP extends YOLOv5s with three key modifications: (1) an Adaptive Large Scale Selective Kernel in the backbone that combines multi-scale convolutions (k=5/p=2 for shallow features, k=7/p=9 for deep features) with GhostConv-based feature selection and a spatial attention mechanism (channel-wise average/max pooling → 7×7 convolution → Sigmoid gating) to capture dense small-target location information; (2) GhostConv layers in the FPN+PAN neck to reduce parameter count and computational cost while enlarging the receptive field for texture discrimination; and (3) decoupled detection heads that separate classification and regression branches to minimize loss interference. Anchor boxes are determined via Kmeans clustering on the actual bounding box distribution. The dataset is annotated using a 'Human-in-the-loop' pipeline where YOLOv5s pre-labels are manually verified, and target-free images are retained to suppress false positives.

## Results

On the PDT dataset (LL), YOLO-DP achieves F1=0.89, mAP@.5=94.5%, and mAP@.5:.95=67.5% with only 5.2M parameters and 11.7 GFLOPs, delivering the highest inference speed (109 FPS) among all compared models. On the CWC dataset, YOLO-DP reaches P=92.9% (4.3% above YOLOv5s) and F1=0.90, while on SugarBeet2017 it attains R=73.8%, surpassing all non-pretrained baselines. Across all three datasets, YOLO-DP consistently achieves the lowest computational cost (11.7 GFLOPs, 5.2M params) and the highest FPS (109/72/62), making it the most efficient detector in the comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary PDT dataset (LL), YOLO-DP ties YOLOv5s for the best F1 (0.89) and achieves the highest mAP@.5 (94.5%) among non-pretrained models, while running at 109 FPS versus 93 FPS for YOLOv5s and 60 FPS for YOLOv8s. On the CWC dataset, YOLO-DP's precision (92.9%) exceeds YOLOv5s by 4.3 percentage points and approaches YOLOv8s (92.0%), though YOLOv8s edges ahead on F1 (0.91 vs. 0.90) and mAP@.5:.95 (86.2% vs. 81.0%); however, YOLO-DP is 2× faster (72 vs. 38 FPS) and uses less than half the GFLOPs. On SugarBeet2017, YOLO-DP achieves the best recall (73.8%) among all non-pretrained models, outperforming YOLOv8s (65.0%) and YOLOv5s (58.3%), but trails pre-trained models like Faster-RCNN (87.4% R) and SSD (83.6% R) on overall F1. Against the domain-specific WeedNet-R, YOLO-DP substantially outperforms it on F1 across all three datasets (0.89 vs. 0.62 on PDT, 0.90 vs. 0.65 on CWC, 0.35 vs. 0.78 on SugarBeet2017 where WeedNet-R benefits from pre-training). YOLO-DP consistently achieves the lowest computational cost (11.7 GFLOPs, 5.2M parameters) and highest inference speed across all datasets, making it the most deployment-friendly option for real-time UAV operations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on YOLOv5s as its architectural foundation, augmenting it with an Adaptive Large Scale Selective Kernel (borrowed from remote sensing detection) in the backbone and GhostConv in the neck for parameter efficiency. It positions itself against a lineage of agricultural UAV detection datasets—SugarBeet2016/2017, MPP, and Ribworth—that the authors argue are limited by indoor/close-range capture, single or double class labels, and lack of environmental variability. The CWC dataset extends the multi-class weed/crop detection line (Corn weed, Lettuce weed, Fresh-weed-data) by aggregating 11 visually similar plant classes to specifically train classification discrimination, a gap the authors identify in prior single/double-class benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PDT dataset fills a genuine gap as the first public UAV-captured dataset for tree pest/disease detection (Red Turpentine Beetle), with a practical dual-resolution design (640×640 training, 5472×3648 inference) that mirrors real operational workflows. However, the model contribution is incremental—YOLO-DP matches YOLOv5s on F1 (0.89) while being smaller (5.2M vs 7.0M params) and faster (109 vs 93 FPS)—and the notably poor SugarBeet2017 results (F1=0.35 across all models) highlight a significant domain-shift limitation that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the 3D point cloud data (collected but not released) enables meaningful 3D detection, and whether the single-class PDT design generalizes to multi-species pest scenarios.
