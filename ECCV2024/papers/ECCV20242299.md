## Problem

Multimodal learning typically assumes all modalities are available during training and inference, but real-world data (e.g., medical images without clinical reports) often lacks complete pairs. Prior missing-modality methods rely on a pretrained joint multimodal encoder trained on large paired datasets, which may not exist in many domains. This paper tackles the more general setting where only independently pretrained unimodal encoders are available and modalities are missing in both training and testing.

## Input / Output

The method accepts any combination of image and text inputs (image-only, text-only, or both) processed through separate transformer-based unimodal encoders. For each available modality, it produces a class prediction via a late-fusion of pre-softmax logits; for each missing modality, it outputs a predicted CLS-token embedding in that modality's representation space. The read-only prompts are appended to the input token sequence and, through masked self-attention, extract task-relevant information without altering the original token or CLS embeddings.

## Method

The framework fine-tunes separate pretrained unimodal transformers using BitFit (updating only bias terms, ~0.11% of parameters) to preserve pretraining knowledge while adapting to the downstream task. Trainable read-only prompts are concatenated to each modality's input; a dedicated attention mask ensures prompts can attend to input tokens but input tokens cannot attend to prompts, so the encoder's internal representations remain unchanged. A feature predictor maps the prompt embeddings to the CLS embedding of the missing modality, trained with a VICReg objective (invariance MSE between predicted and ground-truth embeddings, plus variance and covariance regularization to prevent collapse) and an auxiliary cross-entropy loss on the predicted features. At inference, the predicted embedding of the absent modality is fed through its classifier and fused with the available modality's logits via simple late fusion, enabling robust classification under any missing-modality configuration.

## Results

Under a 70% missing-rate training setting, the proposed method achieves average F1-Macro of 48.99–49.64 on MM-IMDb, accuracy of 81.25–82.72 on UPMC Food-101, and AUROC of 63.75–65.38 on Hateful Memes, consistently outperforming all baselines across three training configurations (100%/30%, 30%/100%, 65%/65% image/text). The largest gains appear in cross-scenario evaluation (test missing pattern differs from training), where the method maintains robustness while prior multimodal approaches degrade sharply. On the complete-training setting (Fig. 2), the method also outperforms the multimodal ViLT baseline and unimodal baselines, particularly when text is severely missing at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViLT [18] (multimodal pretrained transformer), the proposed method wins decisively on all three datasets: e.g., on MM-IMDb with 100%/30% training, Ours achieves 48.99 F1-Macro vs. ViLT's 29.97, and on Food-101 with 65%/65% training, Ours reaches 82.72% accuracy vs. ViLT's 68.99%. Against Lee et al. [22] (multimodal prompting with missing modalities), Ours wins in most cross-scenario settings where the test missing pattern differs from training; Lee et al. degrades severely in unseen configurations (e.g., 21.68 F1 on MM-IMDb when trained with 100% image/30% text but tested with 30% image/100% text, vs. Ours's 54.67). Against the unimodal baseline (same encoders, no predictor), Ours wins in the majority of cross-scenario evaluations but the gap narrows when training and test missing patterns align (e.g., Food-101 30%/100% training, 30%/100% test: Ours 87.11 vs. Unimodal 87.44). Overall, the method's advantage is most pronounced in the challenging regime where the missing-modality distribution shifts between training and testing, confirming the robustness benefit of the VICReg-based feature predictor with read-only prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior missing-modality work (Ma et al. 2022, Lee et al. 2023) that assumes a pretrained multimodal joint encoder (e.g., ViLT) trained on large paired datasets. Instead, it operates in the more general and realistic setting where only independently pretrained unimodal encoders are available, combining BitFit-style PEFT with read-only prompts (Lee et al. 2023) and the VICReg objective (Bardes et al. 2021) to predict missing modality embeddings. Conceptually, it extends the JEPA principle of cross-modal predictability into a practical fine-tuning framework, while the read-only attention masking ensures that feature prediction does not corrupt the unimodal encoder's task-relevant representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the dependency on a pretrained multimodal encoder, which is often unavailable for domain-specific or low-resource settings (e.g., multilingual text, medical imaging). The method's robustness across mismatched train-test missing configurations—where prior methods degrade sharply—makes it more deployable in real-world pipelines where data availability is unpredictable. Limitations include evaluation restricted to two modalities (image + text) and three datasets, and the scaling experiment with a 'Large' encoder is limited to one dataset. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to three or more modalities, whether it benefits from truly domain-specific unimodal encoders (e.g., multilingual BERT, medical ViT), and how it interacts with emerging foundation models that natively handle missing inputs.
