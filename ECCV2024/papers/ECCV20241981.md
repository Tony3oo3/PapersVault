## Problem

Existing change detection methods either flag all pixel-level differences without distinguishing monotonic (e.g., urbanization, deforestation) from cyclic/seasonal changes, or require supervised labels for specific change types. This paper asks whether a model can be trained purely self-supervised to discover and localize only those changes that correlate monotonically with time, while remaining invariant to periodic or stochastic variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241981/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an unordered sequence of F RGB images (shuffled from a temporal video or multi-temporal image set) and produces two outputs: a frame ordering (which image occupies each temporal position 0…F−1) and a patch-level attribution map of size F×H×W highlighting the spatial regions driving the ordering decision. The architecture handles arbitrary sequence lengths up to a fixed F_max via learnable positional queries, and the attribution map can be thresholded for patch-level segmentation or used as a point prompt for SAM to obtain pixel-level masks.

## Method

The architecture is a lightweight (4M parameter) DETR-like transformer encoder-decoder. The TimeSFormer-based encoder processes all F×N patches jointly with 3D positional encoding (spatial + frame identity, deliberately excluding absolute temporal position), while the decoder uses F learnable queries—one per ordinal position—that cross-attend to encoder features. A cosine similarity matrix between encoder and decoder features is spatially max-pooled to yield ordering predictions, and the same matrix directly provides the attribution map by selecting, for each query, the image with maximum activation and reshaping its patch-level similarity scores back to image resolution. Training uses a binary cross-entropy loss on a permutation matrix with a reversibility trick (taking the minimum of forward and backward ordering losses) to handle the fact that many monotonic changes are direction-agnostic. No additional supervision, fine-tuning, or post-hoc attribution method is needed for localization to emerge.

## Results

On the Monotonic MUDS benchmark, the proposed method achieves 83.3% pointing-game localization accuracy and 37.9% mIoU at patch-level granularity, improving to 45.1% mIoU when the attribution map is used to prompt SAM for pixel-level segmentation. On standard image ordering benchmarks, the method attains 93.9% exact-match accuracy on 5-frame MNIST sequences and 77.3% on 5-frame SVHN sequences, outperforming all prior ordering approaches. The model also generalizes to unseen satellite (CalFire: 76.6% EM) and medical (OASIS-3: 84.3% EM) sequences without any task-specific supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against change detection baselines on Monotonic MUDS, the proposed method outperforms the supervised Siamese network [27] by 10 points in localization (83.3% vs 73.3%) and 26.8 points in segmentation mIoU (37.9% vs 11.1%), as the Siamese model only detects building changes. It also surpasses CVA [43] (71.7% loc, 34.6% mIoU) and Deep CVA [55] (70.0% loc, 35.5% mIoU), which over-segment non-monotonic seasonal changes because they lack a notion of temporal direction. Against self-supervised proxy-task methods (S&L [47], OPN [40]), the gap is even larger: the proposed method achieves 83.3% loc vs 23.3–25.0%, and 45.1% mIoU (with SAM) vs 20.9–24.1%, demonstrating that the ordering proxy with the transformer architecture yields far more informative localization than conv5-based CAM attribution. On image ordering benchmarks, the method beats DSort [52] (93.9% vs 83.4% EM on 5-frame MNIST; 77.3% vs 64.1% on SVHN), DSv2 [53] (93.9% vs 84.9%; 77.3% vs 68.5%), and Ptr-Net [63] (93.9% vs 91.9%; 77.3% vs 76.3%), while also generalizing to unseen sequence lengths (EM5) where Ptr-Net fails (1.1% on 16-frame MNIST). The method wins across all eight baselines on every reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes temporal ordering — previously used as a self-supervised proxy for representation learning (Misra et al. 2016, Lee et al. 2017) — as a direct training signal for a new task: localizing monotonic temporal changes. It departs from prior change detection (CVA, Siamese networks) that flags all pixel differences without distinguishing monotonic from cyclic changes, and from self-supervised task-solving work (depth, optical flow, sound localization) by introducing ordering as the proxy. Architecturally, it combines a TimeSFormer-style encoder with a DETR-like decoder whose learnable queries encode ordinal positions, and the attribution map emerges naturally from the encoder-decoder cosine similarity matrix rather than requiring post-hoc interpretability tools.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to automatically identify and localize changes that are monotonic with time — while ignoring seasonal or stochastic variation — is directly relevant to remote sensing monitoring, longitudinal medical imaging, and long-term scene analysis, and the self-supervised formulation eliminates the need for manual change annotations. Key limitations include the inability to handle periodic/cyclic patterns, the lack of enforced one-to-one query-image matching, and patch-level (7×7) granularity that requires SAM prompting for pixel-level masks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences and larger datasets, how it generalizes to more complex temporal structures (multi-scale, non-monotonic), and whether the learned representations transfer to other downstream tasks beyond the demonstrated building-change fine-tuning.
