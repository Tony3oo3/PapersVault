## Problem

Existing 3D diffusion models rely on voxel grids (memory-hungry, resolution-limited) or point clouds (lacking connectivity and surface structure), and the one prior method using tetrahedral grids (MeshDiffusion) embeds them in a higher-resolution voxel grid, incurring a cubic memory blowup. No 3D diffusion model had natively operated on a tetrahedral representation with dedicated convolutional operators, leaving a gap in efficient, high-resolution mesh generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes random Gaussian noise defined on a tetrahedral grid (A15-lattice tiling of a cuboid) where each vertex carries a signed distance value, a 3D displacement vector, and a color vector. It outputs a textured triangle mesh extracted via differentiable Marching Tetrahedra, with no post-processing required. The grid supports resolutions up to R=192 (over 5×10⁶ tetrahedra) and can be pruned to the convex hull of training data for further efficiency.

## Method

TetraDiffusion introduces TetraConv, a convolution operator defined directly on the tetrahedral grid by exploiting a collision-free spatial ordering of neighbors derived from k-means clustering of local edge orientations in the A15 lattice, yielding kernels ~40% smaller than equivalent 3D voxel convolutions. This enables a U-ViT architecture (residual conv layers with group normalization and SiLU activations, alternating with attention layers) that performs denoising diffusion over per-vertex SDF, displacement, and color features. A multi-resolution hierarchy with k-nearest-neighbor parent-child relations supports strided and transposed convolutions for efficient encoding/decoding. Grid pruning removes unoccupied vertices and their connections, reducing memory without quality loss. The entire pipeline is single-stage and fully differentiable, requiring no separate surface reconstruction network or adversarial training.

## Results

On ShapeNet (airplane, car, chair, bike), TetraDiffusion achieves the best 1-NNA (CD) scores across all four categories (64.9, 60.1, 61.2, 58.5), the lowest MMD-CD in every category, and the highest COV-CD in 5 of 8 sub-metrics. Its high-resolution variant (R=192) attains the best Shading-FID in all categories (15.6 airplane, 107.2 car, 39.9 chair, 47.9 bike) and the best CLIP-FID (1.02, 5.15, 4.17, 2.94). Inference takes ~3 seconds per sample on consumer hardware, roughly 200× faster than MeshDiffusion (714 s), while using 12–21 GB GPU memory versus 76.6 GB for MeshDiffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TetraDiffusion outperforms all five baselines on the majority of metrics across all four ShapeNet categories. Against MeshDiffusion (MD), the closest competitor also using a tetrahedral grid, TetraDiffusion achieves lower 1-NNA (e.g., 64.9 vs. 92.7 on airplane CD), lower MMD, higher COV, and substantially better FID/CLIP-FID, while being ~200× faster at inference (3.4 s vs. 714 s) and using 12–21 GB vs. 76.6 GB GPU memory. Against GET3D, the only other method generating colored meshes, TetraDiffusion wins on all geometry metrics and also on texture FID (e.g., 41.6 vs. 68.9 FID on chairs; 52.2 vs. 87.8 on bikes). Against NWD, TetraDiffusion achieves better 1-NNA and COV in most categories and lower FID/CLIP-FID, though NWD edges out on MMD-EMD for chairs (2.18 vs. 2.66) and KID for chairs (0.69 vs. 2.67). Against SDF-StyleGAN and NFD, TetraDiffusion dominates across nearly all metrics, with the largest gaps in FID (e.g., 15.6 vs. 80.7 for SDF-SG on airplanes) and 1-NNA. The only category where TetraDiffusion does not rank first on every single metric is Bike 1-NNA-CD, where MD (54.0) slightly edges out Ours (58.5), though Ours still leads on all other bike metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

TetraDiffusion builds directly on the Deep Marching Tetrahedra (DMTet) representation for mesh extraction and extends it to arbitrary per-vertex feature vectors (e.g., color). Its closest conceptual predecessor is MeshDiffusion, which also performs diffusion on a deformable tetrahedral grid but embeds that grid in a higher-resolution voxel lattice to reuse standard 3D convolutions; TetraDiffusion departs by defining native tetrahedral convolution operators (TetraConv) with a spatially ordered neighborhood derived from the A15 lattice, eliminating the voxel overhead. The broader lineage follows the 2D-to-3D diffusion transfer (DDPM, score-based models) but sidesteps the voxel/point-cloud/triplane representations that dominate prior 3D diffusion work in favor of a hybrid tetrahedral structure that retains connectivity while allowing per-vertex displacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~200× inference speedup and 3–4× memory reduction over MeshDiffusion make high-resolution 3D shape generation (>5M tetrahedra) feasible on a single consumer GPU, removing a practical bottleneck that has constrained prior mesh diffusion models. The ablation confirming that spatial ordering in the convolution kernel is essential (random ordering and permutation-invariant graph convolutions fail to converge) provides a useful design principle for any future work on irregular-grid convolutions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scene-level generation, whether text or image conditioning can be integrated without sacrificing the efficiency gains, and whether the tetrahedral formulation generalizes beyond the four ShapeNet categories evaluated here.
