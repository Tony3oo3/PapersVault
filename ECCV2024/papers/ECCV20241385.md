## Problem

State-of-the-art VLMs like CLIP are trained on text-image matching tasks that do not explicitly model the hierarchical structure of descriptions (e.g., 'mammal' → 'giraffe' → 'funny giraffe'), and existing hierarchical representation methods require costly training from scratch in hyperbolic space. The paper asks whether foundation VLMs exhibit emergent visual-semantic hierarchical understanding despite never being trained for this purpose, and how to probe and enhance such understanding without discarding pretrained knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with four-tier hierarchical caption sets: each image has four positive captions ordered from most generic (e.g., 'animal') to most specific (e.g., 'a goat eating leaves of a lemon tree on the island'), plus four negative captions that contradict the corresponding positive tier while still entailing the previous tier. The output is a hierarchical text-image match: for a given image, the system retrieves texts at multiple levels of specificity along a radial path from the entailment root, producing an ordered hierarchy rather than a single best caption. Evaluation uses precision, recall, and an order-aware Kendall correlation metric (τ_d) that checks whether retrieved texts respect the ground-truth generality ordering.

## Method

The Radial Embedding (RE) framework relaxes the Entailment Cone (EC) geometry to match the Euclidean configuration already learned by pretrained VLMs. It uses the embedding of the empty string as a learnable entailment root, measures concept genericness via distance from this root, and measures entailment via the exterior angle between embeddings without requiring an absolute cone threshold. The key training contribution is a contrastive RE loss that compares exterior angles of positive (entailing) and negative (contradicting) pairs, which is fundamentally asymmetric—unlike standard triplet losses—because logical entailment is directional. Fine-tuning updates only the text encoder (image encoder frozen) for one epoch on HierarCaps, combining the RE loss with a cosine-similarity regularization term that anchors fine-tuned text embeddings to their pretrained values, preserving general multimodal knowledge while enhancing hierarchical structure. The HierarCaps dataset (73K train, 1K test) is constructed by feeding Conceptual Captions through Llama 2 with tier-specific prompts, filtering with NLI, and distilling into a Flan-T5 model that generates both positive hierarchies and hallucinated negative captions.

## Results

On HierarCaps, zero-shot CLIP-B achieves precision 0.14, recall 0.36, and order-aware τ_d 0.89; after RE-based fine-tuning these improve to 0.15, 0.47, and 0.99 respectively. On HyperLex, fine-tuned CLIP-B reaches Spearman ρ_all 0.51 and ρ_N 0.55, while on BREEDS fine-tuned CLIP-L achieves R@1 0.32 and R@5 0.65. Standard multimodal performance is largely preserved: fine-tuned CLIP-L maintains 96% CIFAR-10 accuracy and 0.61 R@5 on COCO text→image retrieval, confirming that RE alignment enhances hierarchical knowledge without degrading pretrained capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is MERU (Desai et al., ICML 2023), a hyperbolic dual-encoder model trained from scratch for hierarchical image-text matching. On COCO text→image retrieval, fine-tuned CLIP-L achieves R@5 0.61 versus MERU's reported 0.32, a nearly 2× improvement. On HierarCaps hierarchical retrieval, MERU (ViT-L) yields precision 0.11, recall 0.11, and τ_d 0.79, while fine-tuned CLIP-L achieves 0.15, 0.44, and 0.97 — substantially outperforming MERU on all three metrics. Internally, replacing the RE loss with the EC loss (Ganea et al., 2018) on CLIP-B drops HierarCaps recall from 0.47 to 0.30, confirming that RE's relaxed geometry better matches pretrained VLM representations. On HyperLex, the best RE-aligned model (ρ_all 0.51) still trails dedicated text-only lexical entailment methods such as LEAR (0.69) and DOA-E (0.59), indicating that while VLMs capture substantial hierarchical knowledge, specialized unimodal models retain an edge on purely textual entailment prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the Entailment Cone (EC) framework of Ganea et al., which models logical entailment as cone inclusion in hyperbolic space and requires training from scratch. The proposed Radial Embedding (RE) framework relaxes EC's partial-order constraints to operate directly in the Euclidean geometry already learned by pretrained dual-encoder VLMs (CLIP, ALIGN, OpenCLIP), using the empty-string embedding as a learnable entailment root. It also builds on Desai et al.'s (MERU) hierarchical image-text matching methodology and genericness measurement, but replaces hyperbolic training with text-only fine-tuning of existing foundation models. The HierarCaps dataset addresses a gap identified in prior work: existing image-caption datasets lack hierarchically-arranged multi-level captions, forcing prior studies to rely on qualitative evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that foundation VLMs already encode visual-semantic hierarchies zero-shot — outperforming models explicitly trained for hierarchical representations — suggests that the contrastive pretraining objective implicitly captures entailment structure, which can be unlocked with minimal text-only fine-tuning while preserving pretrained knowledge. This has practical implications for hierarchical retrieval, classification, and organization of image collections without costly retraining. Key limitations include the assumption of a single linear hierarchy per image (real semantic hierarchies branch), restriction to dual-encoder architectures, and the four-tier structure that may not capture deeper or more complex hierarchies. Open questions include whether branching hierarchies can be modeled, whether the approach extends to decoder-based or autoregressive VLMs, and whether the emergent hierarchy generalizes to non-English or non-visual domains.
