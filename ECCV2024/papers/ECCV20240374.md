## Problem

Cross-domain semantic segmentation suffers from a domain gap between source and target domains, and prior proposal-free approaches rely on heavy encoders pre-trained for image classification on unrelated datasets, making them poorly suited to the segmentation task. No prior work has applied proposal-based transformers (with learnable query tokens) to cross-domain segmentation, nor has anyone decomposed the cross-attention mechanism itself into domain-independent and domain-specific components to explicitly control what the query tokens attend to.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240374/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ADFormer consumes RGB images from the source domain (GTA or SYNTHIA) and, during training, domain-mixed composite images that stitch together source- and target-domain regions. Each query token in the decoder outputs a quadruplet (semantic class, semantic mask, domain class, domain mask), which is aggregated via Hungarian matching into a pixel-wise C-class segmentation map over the target-domain image. The model uses a lightweight Swin-S backbone (64.3M parameters) with a 7-layer transformer decoder, operating at the resolution of the backbone feature map.

## Method

ADFormer extends Mask2Former by splitting the decoder into two parallel branches: a domain-independent (DI) branch that predicts semantic class and mask, and a domain-specific (DS) branch that predicts a binary domain label (source vs. target) and its spatial mask. The key mechanism is attention decomposition: the DI mask is computed by subtracting the DS mask from the entangled Mask2Former mask (Eq. 10), and query tokens are refined exclusively through DI cross-attention (Eqs. 12–13), forcing them to learn domain-invariant class prototypes. Gradient Reverse Layers (GRL) with a small coefficient (λ=1e-4) are inserted in the DS branch to enable adversarial training that suppresses domain-specific feature learning. Training follows a student-teacher self-training protocol on both source-domain and domain-mixed images, where the teacher provides pseudo-labels for target-domain regions in the composites.

## Results

On GTA→Cityscapes, ADFormer (Swin-S backbone, 64.3M params) achieves 69.2 mIoU, outperforming the strongest proposal-based baseline DAFormer (68.3 mIoU) by 0.9 points while using 76% of DAFormer's parameters and running 1.7× faster (6.7 vs 3.9 fps). On SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, ADFormer reaches 62.1 mIoU, surpassing DAFormer (60.9) by 1.2 points. The ablation study confirms each component contributes: adding the DS branch (+5.2), attention decomposition (+0.9), GRL (+5.6), and pixel-wise segmentation loss (+4.1) over the Mask2Former baseline (44.1 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the proposal-based group, ADFormer (69.2 mIoU) clearly leads all competitors on GTA→Cityscapes, beating the next-best CaCo/SePiCo (61.0) by 8.2 points and ProDA (57.5) by 11.7 points. On SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, ADFormer (62.1) edges out DiGA (62.1, tied) and CaCo (61.5), while surpassing ProDA (55.5) by 6.6 points. Against the proposal-free group, ADFormer trails HRDA (73.8) and MIC (70.6) on GTA→Cityscapes, but these methods rely on the much heavier MiT-B5 encoder (>85M params) pre-trained on ImageNet; ADFormer matches or exceeds DAFormer (68.3) and DiGA/CDAC (62.1/61.5) on SYNTHIA→Cityscapes with a lighter Swin-S backbone (64.3M params). The paper attributes its advantage on challenging 'thing' classes (e.g., sidewalk, person) to the absence of DAFormer's ImageNet feature-distance regularization, which biases toward ImageNet-specific class priors that do not generalize across arbitrary domain pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADFormer extends Mask2Former's proposal-based transformer decoder by splitting its single semantic-segmentation head into parallel domain-independent (DI) and domain-specific (DS) branches, thereby decomposing cross-attention rather than the feature vectors themselves as prior domain-decomposition work (e.g., [39–41]) does. For training, it adopts the self-training and domain-mixed-image strategies from DAFormer [13] and DACS [30], but applies the gradient reverse layer in the decoder rather than the encoder, and uses a much lighter Swin-S backbone instead of the heavy MiT-B5 encoder common in prior proposal-free cross-domain methods. The key conceptual departure is that proposal-based transformers with query tokens had never been used for cross-domain segmentation before this work, and the paper argues that decoder-level domain alignment is more natural than encoder-level alignment for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADFormer demonstrates that a proposal-based transformer with a 64M-parameter Swin-S backbone can outperform proposal-free methods using 85M+ parameter MiT-B5 encoders, challenging the prevailing assumption that heavier pre-trained encoders are necessary for cross-domain segmentation. The attention-decomposition mechanism provides a clean, low-complexity way to enforce domain-invariant class prototypes without explicit feature disentanglement. However, evaluation is limited to two domain shifts (both targeting Cityscapes), and the DI/DS decomposition relies on an orthogonality assumption between DI and DS components that is never empirically validated; whether the approach generalizes to more diverse domain pairs or to tasks beyond urban-scene segmentation remains open.
