## Problem

Existing video LLMs struggle with temporal understanding because they either compress frames via mean pooling (losing dynamic information) or introduce dedicated temporal modules that require expensive multi-stage alignment training. The paper investigates an unexplored question: can all spatial-temporal video tokens be fed directly into an LLM, delegating temporal sequence modeling entirely to the LLM's native transformer architecture?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241508/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (resized to 224×224) and a text instruction as input. Each frame is independently encoded by a BLIP-2 Q-Former into K visual tokens; during training, 16 frames are sampled per video yielding ~256 tokens on average (comparable to LLaVA), while inference uses 1 fps with 4–16 local frames. For long videos, a global-local scheme pools 64 frames into a single global representation added to the local subsampled sequence. The output is a free-form text response generated autoregressively by the LLM.

## Method

ST-LLM concatenates all per-frame visual tokens into a single spatial-temporal sequence and feeds it directly into the LLM (Vicuna-7B, initialized from InstructBLIP) without any additional temporal module, separator tokens, or learned position embeddings—relying on the LLM's native Rotary Position Embeddings to distinguish token positions. To manage context length and improve robustness to varying frame counts, a dynamic masking strategy randomly masks ρ video tokens (ρ ~ N(0.5, 0.1), clamped to [0.3, 0.7]) during training, paired with a Masked Video Modeling (MVM) loss that computes MSE between the LLM's hidden states at unmasked positions under masked vs. unmasked inputs. For extremely long videos, a global-local input module average-pools all frames into a global representation and adds it (via a zero-initialized MLP) to the local subsampled spatial-temporal sequence, letting the LLM model fine-grained temporal dynamics while incorporating holistic video context. The entire model is trained with a single stage of video instruction tuning (full fine-tuning of the LLM, frozen visual encoder), avoiding the multi-stage alignment pipelines required by prior video LLMs.

## Results

On MVBench, ST-LLM achieves a 54.9% average across all 20 video understanding tasks, surpassing the previous best (VideoChat2) by 3.8 percentage points (51.1%). The advantage is most pronounced on motion-sensitive tasks (Moving Direction, Moving Count, Moving Attribute), where ST-LLM scores 59.2% versus VideoChat2's 36.3%. On VideoChatGPT-Bench, ST-LLM attains a 3.15 mean GPT-evaluated score, outperforming Chat-UniVi (2.99) and VideoChat2 (2.98). On zero-shot video QA, ST-LLM leads on MSVD-QA (74.6%) and MSRVTT-QA (63.2%), exceeding prior SOTAs by 4.6% and 4.3% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-LLM's closest competitor is VideoChat2 [25], which uses a dedicated video encoder with three-stage alignment training on 30M samples; ST-LLM outperforms it by 3.8% on MVBench average (54.9 vs 51.1) using only single-stage video instruction tuning with frozen CLIP/Q-Former weights. The gap is dramatic on motion-related tasks: ST-LLM scores 59.2% average on Moving Direction/Count/Attribute versus VideoChat2's 36.3%, though ST-LLM trails on fine-grained spatial tasks (FA: 44.0 vs 49.5; FP: 44.5 vs 49.0). On VideoChatGPT-Bench, ST-LLM (3.15) surpasses Chat-UniVi (2.99), LLaMA-VID-13B (2.99), and VideoChat2 (2.98), with the largest margin on the Temporal dimension (2.93 vs 2.66 for VideoChat2). On zero-shot QA, ST-LLM leads VideoChat2 on MSVD-QA (74.6 vs 70.0) and MSRVTT-QA (63.2 vs 54.1), and edges ahead on ActivityNet-QA (50.9 vs 49.1). Earlier video LLMs relying on mean pooling (VideoChatGPT, VideoLLaMA, VideoChat) are substantially outperformed, with MVBench averages of only 32.7–35.5%, confirming that temporal compression before the LLM is a significant bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

ST-LLM builds directly on InstructBLIP (BLIP-2 Q-Former + Vicuna-7B) as its base image dialogue model, but departs fundamentally from prior video LLMs in how temporal information is handled. Where earlier models (VideoChat, VideoChatGPT, Valley) relied on mean pooling over frames, and more recent ones (VideoChat2, BT-Adapter, Chat-UniVi) introduced dedicated video encoders or temporal adapters requiring multi-stage alignment training, ST-LLM instead feeds all raw spatial-temporal tokens directly into the LLM, drawing conceptual inspiration from joint spatial-temporal modeling in video generation (Sora, DIT). The dynamic masking strategy adapts ideas from BERT-style masked modeling and VideoMAE to the autoregressive LLM setting, while the global-local input module is a novel asymmetric design for handling long videos without inflating context length.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that LLMs' native sequence modeling capability is sufficient for effective temporal understanding, eliminating the need for auxiliary video encoders or multi-stage alignment training that prior methods required. This has practical implications: ST-LLM achieves SOTA on MVBench and VideoChatGPT-Bench with a single-stage video instruction tuning pipeline, significantly reducing GPU costs. However, the approach still struggles with fine-grained spatial tasks (Fine-grained Action, Fine-grained Pose), likely due to CLIP's limitations as a 2D image encoder, suggesting that pairing this temporal modeling strategy with stronger video-native visual encoders remains an open direction. The finding that adding explicit separators or spatial-temporal position embeddings actually degrades performance also challenges common design assumptions in multimodal LLMs.
