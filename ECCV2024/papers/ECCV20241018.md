## Problem

Exocentric-to-egocentric cross-view translation—synthesizing a first-person view from a third-person video—remains largely unexplored, especially for hand-object manipulation tasks. Existing geometry-aware novel view synthesis methods (NeRF, SRN) are regressive and fail under the large viewpoint changes, occlusions, and underdetermined nature of this task, while prior cross-view translation methods (P-GAN, STA-GAN) handle only basic locomotion activities and require ground-truth semantic maps at test time. The paper also highlights that camera parameters, which most view synthesis methods depend on, are rarely accessible in real-world settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single exocentric RGB frame concatenated channel-wise with its corresponding 2D hand pose layout (rendered as an image), both cropped and resized to 256×256, and processes them in 30-frame video clips. It produces a photorealistic egocentric RGB frame along with a predicted egocentric 2D hand pose layout (joint coordinates in [0,1] rendered to an RGB image). Notably, no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or 3D scene geometry are required as input, and the target-domain semantic map is not needed at inference time.

## Method

Exo2Ego decouples the translation into two sequentially trained stages. First, a pure transformer encoder-decoder (6 encoder blocks, patch-based input embedding with learnable positional encodings) maps the exo frame and exo hand layout to an ego hand layout via a regression head trained with bipartite matching loss on 2D joint coordinates, explicitly encouraging cross-view correspondence. Second, a conditional latent diffusion model (DiT-XL/2 denoising architecture, DDPM formulation) generates the photorealistic ego frame in VAE latent space, conditioned on the channel-wise concatenation of the exo frame and the predicted ego hand layout, using a squared-error denoising loss over a noise schedule. The hand layout prior is the key design choice: it provides a geometric anchor for the diffusion model to hallucinate pixel-level details around, improving hand fidelity without requiring camera parameters or target-domain segmentation. Both modules are trained separately and applied sequentially at inference.

## Results

On the new-action generalization setting, Exo2Ego achieves the best FID across all three datasets: 37.03 on H2O, 25.99 on Aria Pilot, and 25.23 on Assembly101, outperforming the closest competitor DiT (45.23, 32.71, 26.33 respectively). Hand feasibility scores (Feasi.) reach 0.9764 on H2O and 0.5977 on Aria Pilot, a 11.93% absolute gain over DiT on Aria Pilot. Exo2Ego also leads on all LPIPS variants (P_Squeeze, P_Alex, P_Vgg) and SSIM/PSNR across every dataset, confirming superior perceptual and pixel-level fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Exo2Ego outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest competitor DiT (a latent diffusion model without the hand-layout prior), Exo2Ego improves FID by 8.2 points on H2O (37.03 vs. 45.23), 6.7 points on Aria Pilot (25.99 vs. 32.71), and 1.1 points on Assembly101 (25.23 vs. 26.33), while also gaining 1.6–11.9% in hand feasibility. The GAN-based baselines pix2pixHD, P-GAN, and vid2vid are substantially worse on FID (85–192 on H2O) and hand feasibility, confirming that the diffusion-based generative approach handles the underdetermined exo-to-ego mapping more robustly. pixelNeRF, the only 3D-aware baseline, fails catastrophically (FID 470, Feasi. 0.0086 on H2O), producing heavily blurred outputs with no detectable hands, which the authors attribute to its regression-based training and inability to handle sparse single-view inputs with large viewpoint changes. On the unseen-object/subject/scene splits (Table 2), Exo2Ego maintains its lead over DiT in FID (e.g., 101.45 vs. 105.18 for unseen objects; 150.87 vs. 183.06 for unseen subjects) and hand feasibility (0.9786 vs. 0.9689; 0.9666 vs. 0.8405), while all methods degrade significantly under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

Exo2Ego builds on the latent diffusion paradigm (DDPM + VAE + DiT) for pixel-level generation, but departs fundamentally from geometry-aware novel view synthesis (NeRF, pixelNeRF) by treating exo-to-ego translation as a probabilistic, generative problem that does not require camera parameters. It also extends prior cross-view translation work (P-GAN, STA-GAN) by relaxing the need for target-domain semantic maps at inference and by focusing on hand-object manipulation rather than simple locomotion. The key architectural departure is the two-stage decoupling: a transformer-based layout translator predicts ego hand poses as an intermediate geometric signal, which then conditions a diffusion model for photorealistic pixel synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first comprehensive benchmark for exo-to-ego cross-view translation covering hand-object interactions across four datasets, filling a gap that limits reproducibility in this underexplored area. The decoupling strategy (structure → pixels) offers a practical recipe that could be extended with temporal modeling, 3D object priors, or camera-aware conditioning. Key open questions include whether the hand layout prior generalizes beyond tabletop manipulation, how to achieve temporal coherence without sacrificing per-frame quality, and whether explicit geometric reasoning can be integrated without reverting to the brittleness of regressive NeRF-style approaches. The applications in robot learning, AR skill coaching, and rehabilitation monitoring suggest broad practical relevance if generalization improves.
