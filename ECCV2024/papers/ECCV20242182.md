## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding methods require training on small, biased datasets, leading to significant performance degradation under out-of-distribution and cross-dataset settings. A naive zero-shot baseline using frozen VLMs to score proposals against queries fails because VLMs are trained on single images or trimmed clips, making them insensitive to (1) temporal boundaries of multiple sequential events within one video and (2) dynamic transitions at the onset of an event, causing predictions to skip the beginning of actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242182/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (downsampled to 3 FPS) and a free-form natural language query as input, and outputs a single temporal segment defined by start and end timestamps. The query is first decomposed by GPT-4 Turbo into sub-event descriptions with annotated temporal order and pairwise relationships (single, sequential, or simultaneous). The VLM localizer returns top-k=3 proposals per sub-event, which are then filtered by order constraints and merged (intersection for simultaneous, union for sequential) into the final prediction.

## Method

TFVTG is a fully training-free pipeline combining a frozen LLM (GPT-4 Turbo) and a frozen VLM (BLIP-2 Q-Former). The LLM decomposes the query into sub-events, assigns their chronological order, and labels pairwise relationships as sequential or simultaneous. For each sub-event, the VLM localizer computes per-frame cosine similarity between the text embedding and frame embeddings, then scores each candidate proposal by summing a dynamic score (sum of positive frame-to-frame similarity differences exceeding a threshold δ=5×10⁻⁴, capturing the rapid relevance rise at event onset) and a static score (difference between mean similarity inside and outside the proposal, ensuring the event stands out from surrounding content). The best split point k within each proposal is selected to maximize the combined dynamic+static score. Finally, top-k proposals across sub-events are filtered by the LLM-inferred order constraint (discarding combinations where a 'before' event starts after a 'later' event ends) and merged by the relationship constraint (intersection for simultaneous, union for sequential) to produce the final grounded segment.

## Results

On Charades-STA (IID), TFVTG achieves R@0.5 = 49.97 and mIoU = 44.51 in the zero-shot setting, surpassing the best prior zero-shot method VTG-GPT by 6.29% on R@0.5 and the best unsupervised method SPL by 9.27%. On ActivityNet Captions, it reaches R@0.5 = 27.02 and mIoU = 34.10. Under OOD and cross-dataset settings, TFVTG outperforms fully supervised methods (e.g., 45.9 vs. 44.4 R@0.5 on Charades-STA OOD-1 vs. DCM; 49.97 vs. 10.38 R@0.5 cross-dataset vs. Debias-TLL), demonstrating superior generalization without any task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot methods, TFVTG outperforms VTG-GPT by 6.29% on R@0.5 (49.97 vs. 43.68) and Luo et al. by 7.04% on Charades-STA, while also leading on ActivityNet Captions (27.02 vs. 28.25 R@0.5 for VTG-GPT, though VTG-GPT is slightly ahead on AN-Captions R@0.5). Against unsupervised baselines, TFVTG beats SPL by 9.27% on R@0.5 and PSVL by 18.68%, confirming that training-free VLM+LLM reasoning substantially exceeds pseudo-label-based approaches. However, on the IID setting, TFVTG still trails the best fully supervised methods (EaTR: 68.47, MGSL-Net: 63.98, EMB: 58.33 on R@0.5), as expected without task-specific training. The critical advantage emerges in OOD and cross-dataset settings: TFVTG achieves 45.9 R@0.5 on Charades-STA OOD-1, surpassing the best fully supervised method DCM (44.4), and reaches 49.97 R@0.5 cross-dataset (trained on AN-Captions, tested on Charades-STA) versus only 10.38 for Debias-TLL, a 5x improvement. In the novel-word OOD setting on Charades-CG, TFVTG scores 56.26 R@0.5, outperforming VISA (42.35) and CPL (45.90), while in novel-composition it reaches 43.84, competitive with but slightly below DeCo (47.39) and VISA (45.41).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of training-free video temporal grounding initiated by Luo et al. (zero-shot retrieval from frozen VLMs) and VTG-GPT (LLM-based caption similarity), but departs from both by explicitly decomposing multi-event queries via LLM prompting and modeling dynamic transitions separately from static states in the VLM scoring. The static scoring draws conceptual inspiration from SPL's pseudo-label framework, while the dynamic scoring (measuring rate of similarity increase) is novel. The overall pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf BLIP-2 Q-Former and GPT-4 without any task-specific fine-tuning, positioning itself against the dominant paradigm of training dedicated models on annotated video-query-timestamp triplets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free formulation directly addresses the well-documented generalization failures of supervised video temporal grounding under OOD and cross-dataset settings, offering a practical recipe that avoids costly annotation. The dynamic/static decomposition provides a useful inductive bias for VLM-based localization that could transfer to other temporal tasks. However, the method's reliance on LLM correctness for temporal reasoning (order, simultaneity) remains a vulnerability, and the authors acknowledge that validating LLM outputs for temporal structure is an open problem. The approach also inherits the computational cost of proposal enumeration and per-frame VLM scoring, which may limit scalability to very long videos.
