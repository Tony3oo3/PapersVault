## Problem

Existing camouflaged object detection methods rely heavily on spatial features that suffer from locality limitations and are easily confused by background similarity. Frequency-based approaches that have emerged only exploit high- and low-frequency bands, ignoring the full spectrum of intermediate frequencies and the interaction between all frequency components. No prior method jointly models all frequency bands and spatial features through a unified entanglement mechanism to overcome these complementary limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240154/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 416×416 pixels and produces a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask identifying the camouflaged object. Internally, the network generates five multi-scale predicted maps (from the JDPM and four ETB levels) that are jointly supervised with a weighted BCE + IoU loss, where higher-resolution maps receive greater weight (1/2^(i-1)). The encoder backbone (PVTv2, ResNet-50, or Res2Net) is pre-trained on ImageNet and fine-tuned end-to-end.

## Method

FSEL introduces three components that jointly optimize features in both frequency and spatial domains. The Joint Domain Perception Module (JDPM) applies atrous convolutions at multiple dilation rates to capture local spatial features, then uses FFT with element-wise multiplication and modulus to extract global frequency features, fusing them into a coarse semantic guide. The Entanglement Transformer Block (ETB) is the core unit: its Frequency Self-Attention computes Q, K, V via FFT, handles the resulting complex-valued attention map by separately applying Softmax to real and imaginary parts before recombining, and its Entanglement Feed-Forward Network performs two-stage cross-domain interaction where frequency and spatial features are projected, gated with GELU, concatenated, and re-optimized independently before aggregation. The Dual-domain Reverse Parser (DRP) aggregates multi-level ETB outputs by splitting each feature into frequency and spatial branches for independent optimization, then applies a hybrid reverse attention map (combining spatial and frequency-domain sigmoid of the auxiliary feature) to suppress background. The key design insight is treating frequency and spatial representations as two entangled states whose mutual information transfer yields more discriminative features than either domain alone.

## Results

With the PVTv2 backbone, FSEL achieves the best results across all three benchmark datasets: on CAMO, M=0.040 and F_φ^m=0.891; on COD10K, M=0.021 and F_φ^m=0.853; on NC4K, M=0.030 and F_φ^m=0.895. With ResNet50, FSEL surpasses the prior SOTA FEDER by 5.97%, 3.23%, and 4.76% on the M metric across CAMO, COD10K, and NC4K respectively. The Res2Net variant achieves average gains of 9.23% (M), 2.30% (F_φ^m), 2.16% (F_φ^a), 2.94% (F_φ^w), 1.34% (S_m), and 1.24% (E_m) over the second-best method on CAMO. FSEL-Pvt has 67.13M parameters and 54.73G FLOPs, placing it in the medium-to-high efficiency range while far outperforming methods of similar cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSEL with PVTv2 backbone outperforms all 21 compared methods on every dataset and metric. On COD10K, FSEL-Pvt (M=0.021, F_φ^m=0.853) beats the strongest frequency-based baselines FPNet (M=0.029, F_φ^m=0.817) and EVP (M=0.032, F_φ^m=0.802) by 38.10% and 52.38% on M respectively, and surpasses the transformer-based FSPNet (M=0.026, F_φ^m=0.816) by 19.2% on M. On CAMO, FSEL-Pvt (M=0.040) improves over FSPNet (M=0.050) by 20% and over FPNet (M=0.056) by 28.6%. On NC4K, FSEL-Pvt (M=0.030, F_φ^m=0.895) leads HiNet (M=0.037, F_φ^m=0.879) and FSPNet (M=0.035, F_φ^m=0.878) by 23.3% and 14.3% on M. With the lighter ResNet50 backbone, FSEL still surpasses FEDER (the prior SOTA) by 5.97–4.76% on M across the three datasets, and the Res2Net variant achieves the best M on CAMO (0.065) among non-PVTv2 methods. SAM, a general-purpose segmentation model, underperforms all COD-specific methods, confirming the need for task-specific frequency-spatial modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSEL positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: frequency-domain COD methods (FDNet, EVP, FPNet, FEDER), vision transformer architectures (Swin, PVTv2), and frequency learning in computer vision (FCANet, SPA-Net). It departs from prior frequency-based COD work by modeling all frequency bands through a dedicated frequency self-attention mechanism rather than restricting to high/low bands, and by introducing an 'entanglement' formulation where frequency and spatial features are treated as interacting states within a transformer block. The architecture repurposes standard operations (FFT, softmax attention, GELU gating) in a novel dual-domain pipeline (JDPM → ETB → DRP) rather than proposing fundamentally new mathematical primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-spatial entanglement framework offers a reusable design pattern for tasks where global spectral structure and local spatial detail are both critical (medical segmentation, defect detection, camouflage), and the paper demonstrates transfer to salient object detection and polyp segmentation. However, the method relies on pre-trained heavy backbones (PVTv2 at 67M params, 54.7 FLOPs), limiting its appeal for resource-constrained settings. An open question is whether the entanglement mechanism provides genuine representational benefits over simpler frequency-spatial concatenation, since the internal ETB ablation (Table 4) shows frequency-only attention can match or slightly exceed the full entangled block on some metrics. The field still lacks a clear theoretical account of why joint frequency-spatial attention should outperform either domain alone for camouflage specifically.
