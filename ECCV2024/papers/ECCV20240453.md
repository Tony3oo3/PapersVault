## Problem

The impact of training data attributes—type, quality, and scale—on modality alignment in large multi-modal models (LMMs) remains under-explored. It is unclear whether detailed captions outperform VQA data for alignment, whether LMM-generated captions can substitute for costly human annotations, and whether LMM performance scales with the volume of high-quality caption data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240453/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images at 336×336 resolution (converted to 576 visual tokens via CLIP-Large with patch size 14) paired with free-form text prompts, and produces text responses including detailed image descriptions, VQA answers, and multi-turn conversational replies. The ShareGPT4V captions average 942 characters and explicitly encode world knowledge, spatial relations, object attributes, and aesthetic evaluations, going well beyond the ~52-character COCO captions or ~609-character LLaVA captions used by prior work.

## Method

The ShareGPT4V-7B model follows the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP-Large vision encoder + 2-layer MLP projector + Vicuna-7B LLM) but is distinguished by its training data and pre-training strategy. A 100K-caption dataset (ShareGPT4V) is generated by GPT4-Vision using data-specific prompts across diverse image sources, and a lightweight fine-tuned open-source model (Share-Captioner) is used to scale this to 1.2M captions (ShareGPT4V-PT) for pre-training. During pre-training, the vision encoder's latter-half blocks, the projector, and the LLM are jointly fine-tuned at learning rate 2e-5 over ~4700 steps, a departure from the standard practice of freezing the vision encoder. SFT then freezes the vision encoder and fine-tunes only the projector and LLM on LLaVA-1.5's 665K instruction data with the 23K caption subset replaced by ShareGPT4V samples. The key empirical finding is that detailed captions from LMMs approach human annotator quality and that performance improves monotonically with caption volume from 100K to 1.2M.

## Results

ShareGPT4V-7B (7B parameters, Vicuna-7B backbone) achieves the best score on 9 out of 11 benchmarks, including 72.6 on LLaVA-Bench (In-the-Wild), 1567.4 on MME Perception, 68.8 on MMBench, 69.7 on SEED-Bench (Image), and 57.2 on VizWiz. It surpasses the previous best 7B model LLaVA-1.5-7B by 9.2 points on LLaVA-W, 56.7 on MME-P, and 7.2 on VizWiz, and even outperforms the larger LLaVA-1.5-13B on 9 of 11 benchmarks despite using half the parameters. The model trails only on MMBench-CN (62.2 vs. 63.6 for LLaVA-1.5-13B) and ScienceQA-IMG (68.4 vs. 71.6 for LLaVA-1.5-13B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LLaVA-1.5-13B (Vicuna-13B), ShareGPT4V-7B wins on 9 of 11 benchmarks, with the largest margins on MME-Cognition (+81.0 points, 376.4 vs. 295.4), MME-Perception (+36.1, 1567.4 vs. 1531.3), and VizWiz (+3.6, 57.2 vs. 53.6), while losing on MMBench-CN (62.2 vs. 63.6) and ScienceQA-IMG (68.4 vs. 71.6). Compared to the same-size LLaVA-1.5-7B, the gains are even more pronounced: +9.2 on LLaVA-W, +56.7 on MME-P, +7.2 on VizWiz, and +2.1 on MMBench. Against mPLUG-Owl2 (LLaMA2-7B), ShareGPT4V-7B leads on every reported benchmark, most notably LLaVA-W (72.6 vs. 59.9) and MME-P (1567.4 vs. 1450.2). Versus Qwen-VL-Chat (Qwen-7B, trained on 1.4B data pairs), ShareGPT4V-7B achieves higher scores on all overlapping benchmarks, including MME-P (+79.9), MMBench (+8.2), SEED-I (+11.5), and VizWiz (+18.3). The paper also shows that replacing only the 23K SFT captions with GPT4-Vision-generated ones (Table 1) yields consistent gains across all seven benchmarks tested, confirming that the improvement stems from data quality rather than architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP vision encoder + MLP projector + Vicuna-7B LLM) but deliberately shifts the focus from architectural innovation to training data quality. Prior LMMs relied on brief human captions (COCO), model-generated short captions (BLIP2), or 'imagined' descriptions where GPT4 processed bounding boxes without seeing the image (LLaVA). The key departure is using GPT4-Vision—which actually perceives the image—to generate dense, knowledge-rich captions, then distilling this capability into an open-source Share-Captioner for cost-effective scaling to 1.2M pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reproducible recipe showing that data quality (detailed, accurate captions) can substitute for architectural complexity, with the ShareGPT4V and ShareGPT4V-PT datasets becoming foundational public resources for the LMM community. The finding that a 7B model with better data outperforms 13B and 80B competitors challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling parameters is the primary lever for improvement. Open questions include whether the scaling curve continues beyond 1.2M captions, whether the approach transfers to video or audio modalities, and whether the gains persist when the base LLM is significantly larger or the architecture is fundamentally different.
