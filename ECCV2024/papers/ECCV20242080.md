## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised 3D hand reconstruction methods rely on only partial types of hand knowledge (e.g., biomechanical joint limits alone) and ignore the inherent uncertainty in image observations such as occlusion, motion blur, and self-similarity. This paper addresses both gaps by systematically incorporating hand biomechanics, functional anatomy, and physics as differentiable training losses, and by explicitly modeling per-joint observation uncertainty in the data term.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB hand image (resized to 224×224 with aspect-ratio preservation) and outputs MANO pose parameters (15 joints × 3 Euler angles), shape parameters (10 PCA coefficients), and a per-joint 2D landmark position variance (heteroscedastic, input-dependent). The 3D mesh is recovered via forward kinematics from the predicted pose and shape, while the variance is used only during training to weight the NLL data loss and is discarded at inference.

## Method

A ResNet-50 backbone feeds an iterative error-feedback regressor that predicts MANO parameters and per-joint 2D variance. The training prior combines three differentiable losses derived from hand literature: (1) a pose loss enforcing biomechanical joint ranges with dynamically refined bounds from functional-anatomy inter-joint dependencies (MCP bending–splaying coupling and DIP-induced PIP bending), and (2) a non-penetration loss that uses the generalized winding number to identify vertices embedded inside the mesh and pulls them outward beyond a tolerance distance. The data term replaces standard MSE with a per-joint Gaussian Negative Log-Likelihood loss, where the predicted variance automatically down-weights 2D labels in high-uncertainty regions (occlusion, blur, truncation), yielding a more robust training signal without requiring any 3D annotations.

## Results

On FreiHAND, the proposed method achieves E_J = 8.5 mm and E_V = 8.9 mm, representing a ~21% reduction in joint error over the best prior weakly-supervised baseline (Ren et al., 10.7 mm). On HO3D, it reaches E_J = 10.0 mm, E_V = 9.8 mm, AUC_J = 0.800, and AUC_V = 0.804. On DexYCB, it attains E_J = 6.7 mm and E_RV = 22.0 mm. The ablation (Table 1) shows that adding hand biomechanics, functional anatomy, physics, and the NLL loss progressively reduces E_J from 22.4 mm to 8.5 mm and penetration rate from 38.8% to 1.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five weakly-supervised baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. On FreiHAND, it reduces E_J from 10.7 mm (Ren et al. [57], the best prior method without 3D data) to 8.5 mm, a 21% improvement, and from 10.8 mm (Jiang et al. [30], a probabilistic model-based + model-free framework) to 8.5 mm. On HO3D, it beats Jiang et al. [30] by 0.5 mm in E_J and 0.9 mm in E_V, and outperforms Chen et al. [12] by 1.5 mm in E_J. On DexYCB, it achieves E_J = 6.7 mm versus 7.1 mm for Spurr et al. [63] (which uses 3D synthetic data) and E_RV = 22.0 mm versus 27.3 mm for Boukhayma et al. [5]. In the generalization study (Table 3a), the generic hand-knowledge prior also surpasses a data-driven prior extracted from FreiHAND-3D on DexYCB (E_J 6.7 vs. 8.3 mm), demonstrating that domain-agnostic knowledge transfers better than dataset-specific priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the KNOWN framework [84]—which combined body-specific knowledge priors with uncertainty modeling for human body reconstruction—to the hand domain, extending the knowledge sources from biomechanics alone to a systematic triad of biomechanics, functional anatomy, and physics. It departs from prior weakly-supervised hand methods that rely on data-driven priors (PCA bases, GANs) or limited biomechanical range constraints by introducing novel inter-joint dependencies from functional anatomy (MCP bending-splaying coupling, DIP-PIP coupling) and a vertex-level non-penetration loss using generalized winding numbers that handles deep penetration, unlike the triangle-collision approach of Tzionas et al. [69]. The NLL-based uncertainty modeling, while studied in other vision tasks, is applied here for the first time to monocular 3D hand reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a generic, dataset-independent knowledge prior outperforms a data-driven prior extracted from FreiHAND when evaluated on DexYCB (Table 3a) suggests that physics-grounded constraints may generalize better than empirical distributions, an important consideration for deploying hand reconstruction across diverse domains. The NLL loss provides a principled mechanism for down-weighting unreliable 2D annotations, which is broadly applicable to any regression task with heterogeneous observation quality. However, the method is limited to static single-hand reconstruction from a single image; extending to hand dynamics from video, multi-hand scenarios, and hand-object interaction remains open. The reliance on the MANO model also constrains the method's ability to capture soft tissue deformations during contact.
