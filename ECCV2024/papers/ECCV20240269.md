## Problem

Visual-LiDAR odometry suffers from an intrinsic data structure mismatch: image pixels are regular and dense while LiDAR points are unordered and sparse, making effective cross-modal fusion difficult. Prior methods either fuse features globally (missing fine-grained pixel-to-point correspondences) or locally (lacking sufficient receptive field for global context), and none explicitly address the structural misalignment between the two modalities in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240269/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes two consecutive LiDAR point clouds (N×3, no downsampling) and their corresponding monocular camera images (padded to 384×1280). LiDAR points are projected onto a cylindrical surface to form 64×1800 pseudo images, and a fusion mask is applied to handle the large spatial range difference between camera and LiDAR. The primary output is the relative pose (rotation quaternion and translation vector) between the two frames, estimated iteratively from coarsest to finest scale; the same fusion module also produces 3D scene flow when applied to the scene flow task.

## Method

DVLO introduces a Local-to-Global (LoGo) fusion module with bi-directional structure alignment. In the Local Fuser, image feature maps are reshaped into pseudo points (image-to-point alignment), LiDAR points are projected onto the image plane as cluster centers, and a pure clustering-based aggregation (no CNN or transformer) merges pseudo point features around each center using learnable sigmoid-weighted cosine similarities. In the Global Fuser, point clouds are converted to structured pseudo images via cylindrical projection (point-to-image alignment), and an adaptive sigmoid-MLP mechanism fuses the local fused features with point features for global information interaction. This local-to-global pipeline is applied hierarchically at multiple scales, followed by iterative pose refinement from an attentive cost volume. The clustering-based local fusion achieves comparable accuracy to attention-based alternatives at roughly half the inference time (98.5 ms vs. 183.76 ms).

## Results

On the KITTI odometry benchmark (sequences 00–10), DVLO achieves a mean translational RMSE of 0.82% and rotational RMSE of 0.41°/100m, outperforming the best single-modal LiDAR method EfficientLO (0.86%, 0.41°) and the best prior multimodal method H-VLO (1.36%, 0.43°). On the FlyingThings3D scene flow validation set, DVLO attains EPE_2D of 1.69 px, ACC_1px of 87.6%, EPE_3D of 0.048 m, and ACC_.05 of 88.6%, surpassing the previous SOTA CamLiRAFT (1.73, 87.5%, 0.049, 88.4%). Inference time is 98.5 ms per frame, meeting the real-time threshold for 10 Hz LiDAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against deep visual odometry baselines (DFVO, Cho et al.), DVLO reduces mean t_rel by 63.4% and r_rel by 43.8% on sequences 07–10, despite being trained on fewer sequences (00–06 vs. 00–08). Against the strongest LiDAR-only method EfficientLO, DVLO achieves a 4.9% lower mean t_rel (0.82% vs. 0.86%) while matching its r_rel (0.41°/100m), and outperforms TransLO (0.99%, 0.50°) and PWCLO (1.47%, 0.72°) on most individual sequences. Against prior multimodal methods, DVLO beats H-VLO by 47.0% in t_rel on the held-out sequences 09–10 (0.87% vs. 1.64%) and surpasses the traditional PL-LOAM by 28.7% in mean t_rel across all 11 sequences (0.67% vs. 0.94%). On the scene flow task, DVLO marginally outperforms CamLiRAFT on all four metrics (EPE_2D 1.69 vs. 1.73; EPE_3D 0.048 vs. 0.049), demonstrating that the local-to-global fusion module generalizes beyond odometry. In terms of efficiency, DVLO's 98.5 ms inference time is faster than all compared multimodal odometry systems (100–200 ms) and only slightly slower than the convolution-based local fusion ablation (87.24 ms), while being roughly half the cost of the attention-based alternative (183.76 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

DVLO extends the cylindrical-projection and hierarchical feature extraction pipeline from EfficientLO [54] and the iterative cost-volume pose refinement from PWCLO [55] into a multi-modal setting. Its core architectural departure is the bi-directional structure alignment: it repurposes the 'image as set of points' clustering paradigm from Ma et al. [38] (originally a single-modal visual backbone) as a cross-modal local fuser, while simultaneously converting sparse LiDAR points into structured pseudo images via cylindrical projection for global adaptive fusion (borrowed from DELFlow [43]). This local-to-global hierarchy addresses a structural mismatch—dense regular pixels vs. sparse unordered points—that prior visual-LiDAR odometry methods (e.g., H-VLO, Self-VLO) handled only through global feature-level fusion or simple point-to-image projection without explicit structural reorganization in both directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clustering-based local fuser offers a practical, efficient alternative to both CNN and transformer architectures for fine-grained multi-modal fusion, achieving comparable or better accuracy at roughly half the inference cost of attention-based methods. The demonstrated generalization of the same fusion module to scene flow estimation (outperforming the task-specific CamLiRAFT) suggests it could serve as a reusable building block for other camera-LiDAR tasks. However, the odometry evaluation is confined to KITTI (a single, relatively easy driving dataset), and the scene flow generalization is on synthetic FlyingThings3D data, leaving open questions about robustness in more challenging real-world scenarios (adverse weather, urban clutter, dynamic scenes) and whether the clustering approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-sensor configurations.
