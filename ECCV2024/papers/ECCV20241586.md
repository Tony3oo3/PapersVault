## Problem

Existing blind image restoration methods face a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based approaches (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN, SCUNet) handle unknown degradations well but produce over-smoothed results lacking realistic texture details, while diffusion-based zero-shot restoration methods (DDRM, DDNM) generate realistic content but only work under clearly defined degradation models. No unified framework existed that could simultaneously handle general/unknown degradations across multiple BIR tasks (super-resolution, face restoration, denoising) and generate visually pleasing, high-fidelity details.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (low-resolution, noisy, or blurred) as input and produces a restored high-quality RGB image. At inference, the user selects a task-specific restoration module (BSRGAN for BSR, SwinIR for BFR, SCUNet for BID) and a tunable guidance scale s ∈ [0, 1] that controls the fidelity–quality trade-off. The generation module operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion 2.1-base with 50 DDPM sampling steps, and the positive text prompt is left empty while negative prompts like 'low quality' and 'blurry' are used to suppress degradation artifacts.

## Method

DiffBIR decouples BIR into two independently trained stages: a Restoration Module (RM) that removes image-independent degradations using task-specific off-the-shelf models, and a Generation Module (GM) built on IRControlNet that regenerates lost content. IRControlNet encodes the RM's clean output via the frozen pretrained VAE encoder into latent space, then feeds the concatenation of this condition latent and the noisy latent z_t into a trainable copy of the UNet encoder and middle block (following ControlNet's copied-encoder design with zero-initialized first layer), whose multi-scale features modulate the frozen UNet denoiser via addition through zero convolutions. A key design choice is training the RM with a wide-range classic degradation model to produce diverse, artifact-free condition images, since directly using raw LQ images as conditions causes the diffusion model to interpret degradation patterns as semantic content. Additionally, a training-free region-adaptive restoration guidance is applied at every sampling step: a Sobel-based gradient magnitude map weights a pixel-space MSE loss between the decoded clean latent and the RM output, so low-frequency (flat) regions are pulled toward the high-fidelity guidance while high-frequency (textured) regions retain generative freedom, with a user-tunable guidance scale controlling the overall strength.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val for BSR, DiffBIR(s=0) achieves the best no-reference quality scores: MUSIQ 73.10, MANIQA 0.3836, and CLIP-IQA 0.7656, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: PASD at 0.2354 MANIQA). With guidance scale s=1, DiffBIR also attains the best fidelity metrics (PSNR 21.92, SSIM 0.4986). For BFR on LFW-Test and Wider-Test, DiffBIR achieves the lowest FID (40.91 and 35.81) and highest MANIQA (0.4499 and 0.4443) and CLIP-IQA (0.7948 and 0.8085). For BID on real-world data, DiffBIR(s=0) scores MUSIQ 69.73, MANIQA 0.3404, CLIP-IQA 0.7420, exceeding the best baseline SCUNet-GAN by 11.5, 0.158, and 0.238 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For BSR, DiffBIR(s=0) dominates all seven baselines (FeMaSR, DASR, Real-ESRGAN+, BSRGAN, SwinIR-GAN, StableSR, PASD) on every no-reference IQA metric, with MANIQA 0.3836 vs. the next-best PASD at 0.2354 (+63%); at s=1 it also wins PSNR (21.92 vs. BSRGAN's 21.45) and SSIM (0.4986 vs. Real-ESRGAN+'s 0.4899). For BFR, DiffBIR outperforms all ten baselines (CodeFormer, DifFace, DMDNet, DR2, GCFSR, GFP-GAN, GPEN, RestoreFormer++, VQFR, PGDiff) on FID (40.91 on LFW vs. PGDiff's 41.58; 35.81 on Wider vs. VQFR's 38.17), MANIQA, and CLIP-IQA, while its MUSIQ is within ~0.5 of the best (GPEN 76.62 vs. DiffBIR 76.42 on LFW). For BID, DiffBIR(s=0) achieves a decisive margin over all five baselines (CBDNet, DeamNet, Restormer, SwinIR, SCUNet-GAN), e.g., MUSIQ 69.73 vs. SCUNet-GAN's 58.22 (+19.7%) and MANIQA 0.3404 vs. 0.1822 (+87%). Notably, DiffBIR's IRControlNet is not fine-tuned on face-specific data yet surpasses face-specialized methods like CodeFormer and PGDiff, demonstrating strong generalization from the general-image diffusion prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffBIR builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and ControlNet's copied-encoder architecture, but departs from both in key ways: it replaces ControlNet's from-scratch condition encoder with the pretrained VAE encoder and introduces a two-stage decoupling that separates degradation removal (via task-specific restoration modules like Real-ESRGAN or SCUNet) from content regeneration (via the diffusion prior). This contrasts with prior BIR methods (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, SCUNet) that attempt both degradation removal and detail generation in a single GAN-based pass, and with zero-shot restoration methods (DDRM, DDNM, GDP) that assume known degradation operators rather than handling arbitrary unknown degradations. The region-adaptive restoration guidance is a novel training-free inference-time technique that has no direct counterpart in prior diffusion-based restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffBIR provides the first unified pipeline that achieves state-of-the-art results across three distinct blind restoration tasks (BSR, BFR, BID) without task-specific retraining of the generation module, which is practically valuable for deployment. The two-stage design is modular and extensible—any off-the-shelf restoration model can be swapped in for stage one—making it a flexible template for future BIR tasks. The main limitation is computational cost (50 DDPM sampling steps per image), which limits real-time applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other degradation types (e.g., deblurring, dehazing), whether fewer sampling steps or distillation can reduce inference cost, and whether the region-adaptive guidance can be extended to handle more complex user preferences beyond a single fidelity-quality scalar.
