## Problem

Existing text-to-motion synthesis methods are tailored to either single-person or two-person scenarios and cannot simultaneously generate motions for arbitrary numbers of individuals. Moreover, spatial control techniques developed for single-person motion do not transfer to multi-person settings, and no text-annotated multi-person motion datasets exist beyond two individuals, making scalability a fundamental barrier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240194/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt describing multi-person interaction (decomposed into per-person descriptions via an LLM) and optionally a spatial guidance signal of shape F×3J (per-frame, per-joint 3D positions) as input. It outputs 3D human motion sequences in a non-canonical representation (global joint positions, velocities, local rotations, and foot-contact flags, 262 dimensions per frame) for any number of individuals, generated sequentially via recursive conditional sampling. The spatial guidance can be partially specified (randomly masked frames and joints during training), enabling flexible partial trajectory control.

## Method

The core insight is to decompose the n-person joint motion distribution p(x¹,…,xⁿ) = p(x¹)∏ᵢ p(xⁱ⁺¹|xⁱ,…,x¹) via the chain rule of conditional probability, reducing multi-person synthesis to a sequence of conditional single-person generations. The architecture decouples this into a Generation Module (a Transformer-based diffusion denoiser trained on single-person motion with CLIP text conditioning via adaptive layer normalization) and an Interaction Module (a ControlNet-style branch that injects N−1 condition motions through stacked Interactive Blocks). Each Interactive Block uses two sequential global self-attention layers with random masking of condition hidden states, enabling the model to handle a variable number of motion conditions without architectural changes. Training is two-stage: the generation module is first trained on single-person data, then frozen while the interaction module (initialized from the generation module's weights with zero-initialized output projections) is trained on two-person data with randomly shuffled person ordering. Spatial control is achieved by combining explicit classifier guidance (L2 gradient perturbation at each denoising step) with implicit guidance (spatial signal added to hidden states), both integrated into the interaction module so that single-person control transfers seamlessly to multi-person generation.

## Results

On the InterHuman test set for two-person motion synthesis, FreeMotion achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.326, Top-2: 0.462, Top-3: 0.544) and the lowest FID (6.740) among all compared methods, outperforming the strongest baseline InterGen* (R-Precision Top-1: 0.264, FID: 13.404). FreeMotion also attains the best MM Dist (3.848) and Diversity (7.828), approaching the real-data values (MM Dist: 3.755, Diversity: 7.748). The only metric where FreeMotion trails is MModality (1.226 vs. 1.451 for InterGen*), suggesting slightly less multi-modal diversity in generated motions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeMotion outperforms all five baselines on R-Precision (all three levels), FID, and MM Dist. Against the closest competitor InterGen* (modified to support single-person inference), FreeMotion improves R-Precision Top-1 by 0.062 (0.326 vs. 0.264) and nearly halves the FID (6.74 vs. 13.40), while matching MM Dist (3.848 vs. 3.882). Against ComMDM, FreeMotion achieves a 2.3× lower FID (6.74 vs. 7.07) and substantially better MM Dist (3.85 vs. 5.21), though ComMDM retains a higher MModality (1.82 vs. 1.23). Single-person baselines (TEMOS, T2M, MDM) are clearly inferior on all metrics, with FID values ranging from 9.2 to 17.4 compared to FreeMotion's 6.74. The sole metric where FreeMotion underperforms is MModality, where MDM (2.36) and ComMDM (1.82) generate more diverse motion modes, suggesting the conditional generation paradigm may slightly constrain the diversity of individual motion realizations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based text-to-motion paradigm (MDM, Motion Diffuse) and the multi-person interaction modeling of InterGen, adopting its non-canonical motion representation and the InterHuman dataset. The key conceptual departure is the decomposition of the joint multi-person motion distribution into a chain of conditional single-person distributions via the chain rule of probability, reframing multi-person synthesis as recursive conditional single-person generation. Architecturally, it draws on ControlNet's zero-initialized adapter pattern for the interaction module and on OmniControl's explicit/implicit spatial guidance, but replaces cross-attention with global self-attention to handle a variable number of conditioning motions. The use of an LLM to decompose multi-person text descriptions into per-person prompts is a practical addition not present in prior T2M work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeMotion provides the first unified framework that can generate motions for any number of individuals from a single model, addressing a practical gap where prior methods were hard-coded to one or two people. The ability to generalize from 2-person training data to 3+ person inference is practically valuable given the scarcity of multi-person motion datasets, and the seamless integration of spatial control for multi-person scenarios opens the door to choreography and scene-level motion planning. However, the 3-person results are purely qualitative, the LLM-based text decomposition introduces a potential mismatch between text and motion, and the authors acknowledge interpenetration issues at scale—highlighting that high-quality multi-person datasets remain an open need for the field.
