## Problem

State-of-the-art prompt learning methods for Vision-Language Models (CoOp, CoCoOp, VPT, MaPLe, PromptSRC) all require annotated training samples and exhibit poor generalization to unseen classes and datasets. This paper asks whether prompt learning can be made fully unsupervised—eliminating both label supervision and, in a further variant, even knowledge of training class names—while still improving zero-shot generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled images (16 per class in the few-shot setting) and either a known set of class names or, in class-agnostic mode, a ~20K-class dictionary from Open Images V7 from which the teacher dynamically selects the top-K=1000 most relevant classes per batch. The output is a set of learned prompt parameters γ (textual, visual, or multimodal depending on the integrated technique) that, when applied to a frozen student CLIP, yield class probabilities via cosine similarity with text embeddings. No ground-truth labels or class assignments are ever provided during training.

## Method

KDPL replaces the supervised cross-entropy loss in any prompt learning framework with a symmetric KL-divergence between the zero-shot class probabilities of a frozen, larger teacher CLIP (ViT-H/14) and the prompt-conditioned probabilities of a smaller student CLIP (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/32). The teacher uses fixed handcrafted prompts ('a photo of [CLASS]') and its predictions are precomputable, while only the student's prompt parameters γ are updated via backpropagation through the distillation loss. This makes the approach a drop-in replacement for the training objective in CoOp, CoCoOp, VPT, MaPLe, or PromptSRC, rendering each label-agnostic without architectural changes. In the class-agnostic variant (CA-KDPL), the teacher's per-image probabilities over the full ~20K vocabulary are averaged across the batch to select the K most relevant class names per iteration, so the student never needs to know which classes the training images actually belong to.

## Results

In the base-to-novel class generalization setting (Table 3), KDPL consistently improves over supervised baselines: with ViT-B/32, CoOp+KDPL reaches 66.35% average accuracy vs. 64.52% for CoOp (+1.83%), MaPLe+KDPL reaches 71.41% vs. 69.79% for MaPLe (+1.62%), and PromptSRC+KDPL reaches 71.61% vs. 70.45% for PromptSRC (+1.16%). In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), PromptSRC+KDPL achieves the best average of 62.31% across 10 target datasets, outperforming the zero-shot CLIP student (60.17%) by 2.14%. In the class-agnostic setting where even training class names are unknown, CoOp+CA-KDPL (ViT-B/32) still improves source accuracy from 62.00% to 64.73% and cross-dataset average from 60.17% to 60.74%, all without any labels or class-name knowledge.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KDPL is integrated into five supervised prompt-learning baselines (CoOp, CoCoOp, VPT, MaPLe, PromptSRC) and compared against each in its original supervised form. CoOp+KDPL consistently outperforms CoOp across all three evaluation settings, with the largest gain in base-to-novel generalization (+1.83% avg with ViT-B/32, +2.98% with RN50). CoCoOp+KDPL improves over CoCoOp in cross-dataset transfer (+0.56% avg) and base-to-novel (+2.41% avg with RN50), though it slightly underperforms on ImageNet-R in domain generalization. VPT+KDPL yields only marginal gains (+0.03% cross-dataset, +0.74% base-to-novel), suggesting VPT's visual prompts are less amenable to distillation. MaPLe+KDPL shows strong improvements in base-to-novel generalization (+1.62% avg), particularly on EuroSAT (+9.0%) and FGVCAircraft (+6.94%). PromptSRC+KDPL achieves the best overall average in cross-dataset transfer (62.31%) and base-to-novel (71.61%), though it slightly regresses in domain generalization (−0.12%). All KDPL variants substantially outperform the zero-shot CLIP student baseline (e.g., 71.61% vs. 70.05% for PromptSRC+KDPL vs. CLIP on base-to-novel), confirming that unsupervised distillation learns more transferable prompts than no adaptation at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

KDPL sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: parameter-efficient prompt learning for VLMs (CoOp, CoCoOp, VPT, MaPLe, PromptSRC) and knowledge distillation. All prior prompt learning methods rely on ground-truth labels to optimize prompt parameters; KDPL replaces the supervised cross-entropy loss with a symmetric KL-divergence distillation loss from a frozen, larger CLIP teacher, making the entire pipeline label-free. It also departs from the closest prior work, UPL (which uses pseudolabels from a larger model and requires many unlabeled images) and PromptKD (which pre-trains the teacher with labels and adds an MLP projection), by requiring neither pseudolabeling nor any labeled pre-training of the teacher, and by introducing a class-agnostic variant that filters relevant classes online from a ~20K vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that KDPL removes the annotation bottleneck from VLM adaptation, enabling prompt learning in settings where labels are unavailable or class names are unknown — scenarios common in real-world deployment. The finding that distillation-learned prompts generalize better to unseen classes and domains than supervised ones suggests that the 'soft' distributional knowledge of a larger model is more transferable than hard class labels, which could reshape how practitioners think about few-shot adaptation. Key limitations include the dependence on a large teacher (ViT-H/14, ~381 GFLOPs) and the modest 1–3% average gains; open questions raised by the authors include whether the approach scales to very different downstream tasks, smaller student models, and whether careful hyperparameter tuning (not performed here) could widen the gap.
