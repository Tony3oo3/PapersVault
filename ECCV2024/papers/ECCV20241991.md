## Problem

Existing remote sensing MLLMs fail to adequately capture the diverse geographical landscapes and varied objects in RS imagery, largely because they rely on limited public datasets and only align high-level visual semantics with language. This neglects the fact that multi-level visual information is crucial for unified RS image understanding across different granularities, and overlooks the extensive worldwide RS features available through volunteered geographic information sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241991/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single RS image (resized to 224×224 pixels, sourced from 1 m-resolution Google Earth imagery) paired with a free-form text instruction, and a task identifier token ([CLS], [VQA], or [VG]) to route the appropriate output. Depending on the task, it produces a class label (7-class to 62-class scene classification), a short text answer (VQA), a bounding box coordinate (visual grounding, evaluated at 0.5 IoU), or a multi-sentence conversational response. The model is evaluated with greedy decoding and, on LHRS-Bench, with four repeated generations where any incorrect attempt marks the answer as wrong.

## Method

LHRS-Bot combines a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 vision encoder, a vision perceiver bridge, and a LLaMA2-7B LLM. Rather than using only the final hidden state, it extracts multi-level visual features from three encoder depths (L/3, 2L/3, L−1) and summarizes each level with a set of learnable queries processed through stacked cross-attention and MLP layers; a descending query allocation (64, 48, 32 queries) reflects increasing redundancy at deeper layers. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) pre-training the perceiver on 1.15 M image-caption pairs from LHRS-Align (geo-aligned Google Earth images with OpenStreetMap VGI attributes captioned by Vicuna-13B), (2) multi-task pre-training with LoRA (r=128, α=256) on reorganized public RS instruction datasets plus detailed descriptions, and (3) supervised fine-tuning on complex GPT-4-generated reasoning and conversation data from LHRS-Instruct mixed with LLaVA-Med reasoning samples. The key insight is that globally sourced VGI attributes provide semantically rich, geographically diverse supervision at scale, while multi-level feature summarization enables the model to retain both fine-grained spatial detail and high-level semantics in a compact token budget.

## Results

On the seven RS classification benchmarks, LHRS-Bot achieves 71.83% average accuracy, surpassing the second-best method (mPLUG-OWL2, 44.32%) by 27.51 percentage points, with a particularly large margin of 40% on the fine-grained fMoW dataset (56.56% vs. 17.85%). On RSVQA, LHRS-Bot reaches 89.19% (LR) and 92.55% (HR) average accuracy, matching or exceeding RS-specific models like RSGPT and SkyEyeGPT. On visual grounding, LHRS-Bot attains 80.78% average IoU@0.5 accuracy, slightly outperforming SkyEyeGPT (79.55%). On the proposed LHRS-Bench, LHRS-Bot scores 39.38% average, outperforming all open-source MLLMs and approaching GPT-4-Turbo (44.56%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LHRS-Bot decisively outperforms all six general-purpose MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPTv2, InstructBLIP, mPLUG-OWL2, QWen-VL-Chat, InternLM-XComposer) on classification, with a 27.51-point average margin over the strongest general model (mPLUG-OWL2). On VQA, LHRS-Bot (89.19% LR / 92.55% HR) is competitive with the RS-specific RSGPT (92.29% / 90.47%) despite RSGPT being fine-tuned for five epochs on RSVQA, and clearly surpasses GeoChat (91.81% / 70.82%) and SkyEyeGPT (84.16% / 82.56%), particularly on the high-resolution HR subset. On visual grounding, LHRS-Bot (80.78% avg) edges out SkyEyeGPT (79.55%) and substantially outperforms QWen-VL-Chat (62.71%) and MiniGPTv2 (66.32%). On the authors' own LHRS-Bench, LHRS-Bot (39.38%) beats all open-source models by a wide margin (next best: LLaVA-1.5 at 37.45%) and approaches GPT-4-Turbo (44.56%), though it trails on the Area and Quantity sub-dimensions. The paper notes that most classification test sets (AID, WHU-RS19, EuroSAT, SIRI-WHU) are absent from the multi-task training data, underscoring strong generalization from the LHRS-Align pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard MLLM architecture (vision encoder + bridge + LLM) to the remote sensing domain, following the lineage of RSGPT, GeoChat, and SkyEyeGPT, but departs from them in two key ways: (1) it constructs a large-scale alignment dataset (LHRS-Align, 1.15M pairs) by geographically pairing RS images with OpenStreetMap VGI attributes rather than relying on existing public RS caption datasets, and (2) it replaces the single-level final hidden state with a multi-level vision perceiver using learnable queries and a descending allocation strategy, drawing on observations about feature redundancy at different network depths. The three-stage curriculum learning strategy (pre-training → multi-task pre-training → supervised fine-tuning) is analogous to training pipelines in general MLLMs but adapted to progressively incorporate RS-specific visual knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the RS community with a practical, reproducible pipeline for building domain-specific MLLMs from freely available VGI data, and introduces LHRS-Bench, a structured evaluation benchmark that was previously absent. The finding that a smaller, domain-aligned dataset (1.15M) outperforms a larger general-purpose one (5M RS5M) reinforces the importance of data relevance over sheer scale for specialized domains. However, performance on fine-grained dimensions like Area (25.33%) and Quantity (18.25%) in LHRS-Bench remains weak, and the authors acknowledge persistent hallucination issues, leaving open questions about how to improve spatial reasoning and quantitative understanding in RS MLLMs.
