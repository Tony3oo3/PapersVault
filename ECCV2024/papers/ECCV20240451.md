## Problem

Semi-supervised LiDAR object detection relies heavily on pseudo-labeling, which produces noisy teacher labels requiring complex filtering pipelines and extensive hyperparameter tuning. Object copy-paste, a standard data augmentation in fully-supervised LiDAR detection, has never been successfully adapted to the semi-supervised setting because naive placement generates false positives that accumulate destructively across self-training iterations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240451/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HotspotNet takes an unannotated LiDAR scan and a set of annotated objects (each with point cloud and 3D bounding box metadata: position, dimensions, heading, class) as input, and outputs candidate placement locations (x, y, z, w, l, h, θ, class) with per-location fitness scores indicating semantic coherence. The downstream detector is trained on partially-labeled scans where only the inserted objects carry ground-truth annotations, while original objects in the unlabeled scan remain unannotated—requiring a spatial-aware loss to avoid misclassifying them as background.

## Method

The core contribution is HotspotNet, a transformer-based placement network with object encoders (PointNet/PointNeXt for point clouds; Fourier embedding + CLIP/one-hot for box metadata), a scene encoder (CenterPoint/SST backbone augmented with spatial cross-attention to object features), and a location decoder using learnable queries. It is trained with asymmetric supervision: an L1 loss for location regression on annotated scans, and a scoring loss where positive samples are matched queries while negative samples are selectively chosen based on three fitness scores—position (deviation from local ground plane), orientation (consistency with predominant heading of same-category objects), and category compatibility (preventing e.g. bicycles on highways). The CSOT pipeline uses HotspotNet to insert annotated objects into unlabeled scans in a semantically coherent manner, creating noise-free partially-labeled training data. A spatial-aware classification loss masks negative samples within a radius σ of any foreground object center to prevent the classifier from learning unlabeled objects as background, and randomly samples 1% of remaining heatmap locations as negatives to maintain a clear decision boundary. The approach is orthogonal to self-training and can be combined with it for additional gains, achieving 99% of fully-supervised NDS on nuScenes with 5% annotations and matching fully-supervised performance on Waymo with only 1% annotations.

## Results

On nuScenes with only 5% of labeled data, CSOT achieves mAP 56.4 and NDS 64.8, reaching 97.2% and 98.9% of the fully supervised baseline (mAP 58.0, NDS 65.5). On Waymo with just 1% of labeled data, CSOT achieves All L2 APH of 68.2, matching or exceeding the fully supervised CenterPoint baseline (67.6) while using 100× fewer annotations. The method also demonstrates that its learned object transfer, when used as a data augmentation for fully supervised training, boosts mAP by 2.8 points over standard GT-Aug.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSOT decisively outperforms all compared label-efficient methods on both datasets. On Waymo with 1% annotations, CSOT (CenterPoint) achieves All L2 APH 68.2, surpassing the fully supervised baseline (67.6) and exceeding the next-best method MIXSUP (62.3 at 10%) by 5.9 points, AD-PT (61.6 at 3% with external pre-training) by 6.6 points, and ProposalContrast (34.8 at 1%) by 33.4 points. On nuScenes with 5% annotations, CSOT (mAP 56.4, NDS 64.8) outperforms AD-PT (mAP 45.0, NDS 53.0, which uses external pre-training) by 11.4 mAP, GCC3D (mAP 41.1, NDS 46.8) by 15.3 mAP, and SceneFlow (mAP 36.0, NDS 48.3) by 20.4 mAP. Notably, CSOT requires no external pre-trained models, whereas AD-PT and SSDA3D rely on them. The learned object transfer also surpasses rule-based alternatives: in ablation, replacing HotspotNet-guided placement with GT-Aug, CA-Aug, or LiDAR-Aug reduces mAP from 56.4 to 49.7 when combined with self-training, confirming that semantic coherence in placement is critical for semi-supervised success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pseudo-labeling paradigm in semi-supervised 3D object detection (STAC, 3DIoUMatch, LabelMatch, and many follow-ups) by introducing a fundamentally different mechanism: physically transferring annotated objects into unlabeled scans via a learned placement network (HotspotNet) rather than generating noisy pseudo-labels. It builds on the copy-paste data augmentation lineage (GT-Aug from SECOND, CA-Aug, LiDAR-Aug) but repurposes it from a fully-supervised augmentation trick into a semi-supervised supervision signal generator, and replaces hand-crafted context rules with a transformer-based learned scorer. The partially-labeled training problem it addresses connects to sparsely annotated object detection (SAOD), where the spatial-aware classification loss specifically tackles the false-negative issue of treating unlabeled objects as background.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× annotation reduction while matching (and on one category, exceeding) fully-supervised performance on Waymo, and the 20× reduction reaching 99% of fully-supervised NDS on nuScenes, represent a significant practical efficiency win for autonomous driving teams where 3D box annotation is the primary cost bottleneck. The demonstrated orthogonality with self-training means CSOT can be layered onto existing pseudo-labeling pipelines for additional gains without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the learned placement paradigm generalizes beyond LiDAR to camera-based or multi-sensor detection, whether HotspotNet's reliance on ground-plane estimation and heading consistency limits it to structured driving scenes, and whether the unexplained cyclist performance boost on Waymo reflects a genuine advantage or a dataset-specific artifact.
