## Problem

Long-term action anticipation in egocentric vision has been dominated by representation-learning approaches trained on large video datasets, which struggle with the complexity and variability of human activities and generalize poorly to long-tail classes. While LLMs have shown promise for planning and decision-making, they have not been effectively adapted for grounded, video-based prediction of extended action sequences. PALM bridges this gap by translating visual context into discrete text that LLMs can reason over.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed egocentric video with P=8 annotated action segment boundaries as input. Two parallel pathways consume the video: a frozen EgoVLP encoder processes n=4 consecutive segments via a sliding window (stride 1) for action recognition, while VideoBLIP captions the middle frame of each segment for visual context. The output is a sequence of Z=20 future verb-noun pairs; K=5 predictions are generated per test video and the minimum Damerau-Levenshtein edit distance is reported.

## Method

PALM combines three modules: (1) an action recognition module using a frozen EgoVLP video encoder followed by transformer layers and n independent classification heads to predict verb-noun pairs over sliding windows of 4 segments, with top-1 determined by majority vote; (2) an image captioning module using VideoBLIP with an intention-based question prompt to generate contextual narrations from middle frames; and (3) an LLM-based anticipation module (Llama 2 7B) that receives a structured prompt containing an instruction, MMR-selected few-shot exemplars from the training set, and the query with recognized past actions plus narrations. The LLM is fine-tuned via LoRA with 8-bit quantization (lr=5e-4, 10 epochs) to produce valid action sequences, and raw outputs are mapped back to the predefined verb/noun taxonomy via rule-based post-processing. The key design choice is the complementary use of action sequences (capturing temporal flow and key objects) and visual narrations (capturing dynamic motions and environmental details) as a unified textual prompt for in-context learning.

## Results

On the Ego4D v2 long-term action anticipation benchmark, PALM achieves an action edit distance of 0.8503 (verb ED 0.6471, noun ED 0.6117), outperforming the previous best method AntGPT (action ED 0.8770) by 0.0267. On Ego4D v1, PALM reaches action ED 0.8613, again surpassing AntGPT (0.8814). On EK-55 and EGTEA, PALM achieves mAP of 40.4 (ALL) and 80.7 (ALL) respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on the ALL and FREQ splits while remaining competitive on RARE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PALM outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the primary Ego4D LTA task. Against the closest competitor AntGPT [63], PALM reduces action edit distance from 0.8814 to 0.8613 on Ego4D v1 (−0.0201) and from 0.8770 to 0.8503 on v2 (−0.0267), with the gap widening on noun ED (0.6401 vs 0.6546 on v1; 0.6117 vs 0.6498 on v2). Representation-learning baselines (SlowFast, VCLIP, ICVAE, HierVL) trail PALM by 0.06–0.08 in action ED on v1, and VideoLLM [9] on v2 is 0.071 behind. On EK-55, PALM achieves 40.4 mAP (ALL), edging out AntGPT (40.1) and Anticipatr [41] (39.1), though it falls slightly short of AntGPT on the RARE split (30.3 vs 31.9). On EGTEA, PALM leads all methods across ALL (80.7), FREQ (85.0), and RARE (73.5) splits, surpassing AntGPT by 0.5, 0.2, and 0.6 mAP respectively. Traditional video-based methods (I3D, ActionVLAD, Timeception, VideoGraph, EGO-TOPO) are consistently 2–18 mAP behind PALM on EK-55 and 5–13 mAP behind on EGTEA, underscoring the advantage of LLM-based anticipation over pure representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PALM extends the emerging line of LLM-based action anticipation (AntGPT, VideoLLM) by introducing a dedicated visual-context extraction pipeline: a frozen VLM (VideoBLIP) generates intention-conditioned narrations while a separate action recognition module (EgoVLP encoder + transformer heads) produces past verb-noun sequences, both of which are composed into a structured prompt for the LLM. Unlike prior LLM-based methods that rely on a single modality bridge, PALM explicitly separates the 'what happened' (actions) from the 'what the scene looks like' (captions) and uses MMR-based exemplar retrieval to enable in-context learning, a strategy borrowed from information-retrieval reranking. The framework departs from the dominant representation-learning paradigm (SlowFast, EgoVLP, HierVL) by performing the prediction entirely in the language domain, treating the video as a source of discrete textual context rather than a continuous feature vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that LLM-based prediction can surpass dedicated video representation models for long-term action anticipation, and that simple retrieval/repeat baselines can even outperform SlowFast on Ego4D v2—suggesting the representation-learning approach may be fundamentally limited for this task. However, the ablation showing a large gap between ground-truth actions and the best encoder (EgoVLP) reveals that action recognition accuracy remains the primary bottleneck, and the modest gains over AntGPT (~0.02 ED) indicate the current framework is not yet fully exploiting LLM potential. Open questions include whether fine-tuning the video encoder on egocentric data, or using richer video-level captioning, would close the remaining gap, and whether the approach generalizes to domains with very long action taxonomies or multi-person interactions.
