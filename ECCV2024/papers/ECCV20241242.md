## Problem

Existing 3D object detectors are restricted to closed-vocabulary settings, specific input modalities, and single scene types (indoor or outdoor), with no unified architecture to handle missing sensors or novel categories. The scarcity of annotated 3D data and the absence of pre-trained point-text models in the 3D domain make open-vocabulary 3D detection particularly challenging, while the much larger and more diverse 2D detection data remains underutilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241242/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes three data types: 3D point clouds, 3D detection images with 3D box annotations aligned to point clouds, and 2D detection images with only 2D box annotations (from Objects365). At inference, it accepts any single modality (point cloud only, RGB only, or both) or multi-view images, with camera extrinsics predicted when absent. Outputs include 7-dim oriented 3D boxes, 4-dim 2D boxes, category labels, and confidence scores, with a dual-branch output head that discards the 2D-image branch at test time to avoid noisy pseudo-labels.

## Method

OV-Uni3DETR builds on Uni3DETR's 3D detection transformer with a voxel-based point cloud backbone and a ResNet50+FPN image backbone, projecting both into a shared voxel space and combining them via 3D convolution with batch normalization to prevent modality dominance. The key innovation is cycle-modality propagation: (1) 2D→3D, a pre-trained Detic open-vocabulary detector generates 2D boxes that are projected into 3D space to create pseudo 3D boxes for novel classes, injecting large-vocabulary semantic knowledge; (2) 3D→2D, a class-agnostic 3D detector (trained on 3D detection images) predicts 3D boxes and camera parameters for 2D detection images, with Hungarian matching assigning category tags to provide geometric supervision. Switched-modality training randomly feeds the transformer with multi-modal, point-cloud-only, or image-only features to ensure robustness to missing sensors, and a dual-branch output structure isolates noisy pseudo 3D boxes from accurate ground-truth annotations.

## Results

On SUN RGB-D (open-vocabulary, 46 classes), OV-Uni3DETR achieves 12.96% AP₂₅ novel and 20.85% AP₂₅ all with point cloud + RGB inputs, surpassing the previous best (CoDA, point cloud only) by 6.25% on novel classes. On ScanNet (60 classes), it reaches 15.21% AP₂₅ novel and 17.99% AP₂₅ all, an 8.67% improvement over CoDA. Notably, RGB-only inference yields 5.41% AP₂₅ novel on SUN RGB-D and 8.10% on ScanNet, matching or exceeding CoDA's point-cloud-only results. On outdoor KITTI, the method achieves 23.04% AP₂₅ for the novel pedestrian class (P+I), and on nuScenes it obtains 18.96% AP novel and 17.05% NDS novel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prior open-vocabulary 3D detector CoDA [4], OV-Uni3DETR wins decisively: +2.95% AP₂₅ novel on SUN RGB-D (P-only) and +6.25% (P+I), +5.55% on ScanNet (P-only) and +8.67% (P+I). The CLIP-based baselines (Det-PointCLIP, Det-PointCLIPv2, Det-CLIP2, 3D-CLIP) are all outperformed by large margins—e.g., 3D-CLIP achieves only 3.61% AP novel on SUN RGB-D versus 12.96% for OV-Uni3DETR. On the ScanNet 20-class setting (Table 2), OV-Uni3DETR surpasses OV-3DET [30] by 7.31% and CoDA by 6.01% mean AP₂₅. For closed-vocabulary monocular 3D detection, OV-Uni3DETR (ResNet50) achieves 39.7% AP³ᴰ on SUN RGB-D, beating ImVoxelNet [40] by 3.4% and Cube RCNN [2] by 4.3%; on KITTI it reaches 15.95% AP³ᴰ (moderate car), exceeding MonoFlex [63] by 2.06% and GUPNet [28] by 1.75%. The method also outperforms RegionCLIP [65] on 2D open-vocabulary detection (6.52% vs. 2.24% AP₂ᴰ novel with P+I), demonstrating that the unified architecture transfers effectively to the 2D task as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

OV-Uni3DETR builds directly on Uni3DETR [54] for its 3D detection transformer and point cloud branch, and adapts the uncertainty-weighted regression from Omni3D [2]. It departs from prior open-vocabulary 3D detectors (CoDA, OV-3DET) that were restricted to point-cloud-only inputs and indoor scenes, by introducing a unified multi-modal architecture with cycle-modality propagation that bridges 2D semantic knowledge and 3D geometric knowledge. The switched-modality training scheme and dual-branch output structure represent a conceptual departure from prior multi-modal 3D detectors that treated modalities as fixed at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to conduct open-vocabulary 3D detection on outdoor datasets (KITTI, nuScenes), and the finding that RGB-only inference can match or exceed point-cloud-only baselines challenges the assumption that 3D geometry is indispensable for 3D detection. The cycle-modality propagation offers a practical recipe for leveraging the vast 2D detection data (e.g., Objects365) to compensate for the scarcity of 3D annotations. Open questions include whether the non-differentiable 2D→3D box projection and the simplified camera parameter estimation can be made end-to-end learnable, and whether the approach extends to even more modalities (e.g., LiDAR + multi-camera + thermal) or to video-based 3D detection.
