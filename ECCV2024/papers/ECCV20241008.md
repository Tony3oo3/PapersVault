## Problem

Point cloud-based neural rendering methods like PointNeRF degrade significantly when the input point cloud is sparse or incomplete, a common situation with real-world LiDAR or photogrammetry data. The root cause is the absence of multi-scale modeling: a single-scale representation cannot fill in 'holes' where no points exist, and failure-prone heuristics (pruning/growing) do not reliably recover these regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241008/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (from LiDAR, mesh reconstruction, or COLMAP) together with RGB training images and their camera intrinsics/extrinsics. The point cloud is resampled onto a hierarchy of 5 voxel-grid scales (including one global voxel spanning the entire scene), with tri-plane feature planes of resolution 512×512 at the global level and 4×4/2×2 pyramids at the two coarsest local levels. Rendering queries 400–1000 quadrature points per ray (depending on scene size) and produces a novel-view image via standard volume rendering, with a Mip-NeRF 360 contraction applied outside the point cloud bounding box.

## Method

PointNeRF++ builds a sparse, multi-scale point cloud hierarchy by clustering input points into voxel grids at S resolution levels, up to a single global voxel that covers the whole scene. During volume rendering, each query point aggregates features across all 'valid' scale levels—those with at least one point within a threshold distance τV_s—using normalized inverse-distance weighting, then passes the combined embedding through a shared MLP to predict density and radiance. At the two coarsest local scales, the per-point feature function F is replaced by a local tri-plane factorization (three orthogonal planes queried at relative 3D coordinates), which provides greater representational capacity over large support regions without the cost of deep MLPs. The global voxel at the coarsest scale is equivalent to a standard point-agnostic NeRF, so the formulation unifies classical and point-based NeRF in a single architecture. Training minimizes L2 photometric loss over 200k iterations on a single V100, with no pruning or growing heuristics required.

## Results

On KITTI-360 (the most challenging outdoor benchmark), PointNeRF++ achieves 22.44 PSNR / 0.828 SSIM / 0.212 LPIPS on the hidden test set, outperforming PointNeRF (19.44 PSNR) by 3.0 dB and matching or exceeding semantic-supervision methods like PlaNeRF (22.64 PSNR) using only color supervision. On ScanNet, it reaches 30.56 average PSNR, surpassing PointNeRF (25.92) by 4.6 dB and Gaussian Splatting with points (29.93) by 0.6 dB. On NeRF Synthetic, it achieves 33.70 average PSNR, edging out Gaussian Splatting with points (33.60) and PointNeRF (31.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PointNeRF [54], the closest prior point-based method, PointNeRF++ wins by a large margin on all three datasets: +3.0 dB PSNR on KITTI-360 test (22.44 vs 19.44), +4.6 dB on ScanNet average (30.56 vs 25.92), and +1.9 dB on NeRF Synthetic (33.70 vs 31.77). Against 3D Gaussian Splatting [24] with point cloud input, PointNeRF++ achieves higher PSNR on KITTI-360 test (22.44 vs 22.08) and ScanNet (30.56 vs 29.93), but Gaussian Splatting retains an edge on SSIM/LPIPS for KITTI-360 test (0.844/0.139 vs 0.828/0.212) due to its SSIM-based loss. Against image-only NeRF baselines (NeRF [30], Mip-NeRF [2]), PointNeRF++ leads by 1.3–3.3 dB PSNR on KITTI-360 test. Against semantic-supervision methods on KITTI-360 (Nerflets 21.69, PNF 22.07, PlaNeRF 22.64, FVS 20.00), PointNeRF++ is competitive, trailing only PlaNeRF by 0.2 dB while using no semantic labels. On NeRF Synthetic, PointNeRF++ slightly outperforms Gaussian Splatting with points (33.70 vs 33.60 PSNR) and clearly beats all other point-based baselines including TetraNeRF (32.53) and FreqPCR (31.24).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends PointNeRF [54] by replacing its single-scale, per-point feature lookup with a multi-scale voxel-grid hierarchy, drawing an explicit analogy to the PointNet → PointNet++ progression in point cloud processing. The tri-plane feature representation at coarse scales is borrowed from K-Planes [13], and the volume-rendering pipeline, contraction function, and proposal network follow Mip-NeRF 360 [3]. A key conceptual departure is the introduction of a single global voxel at the coarsest scale, which the authors show is mathematically equivalent to standard (point-agnostic) NeRF [30], thereby unifying the two formulations within one architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world LiDAR point clouds (e.g., KITTI-360) are sparse and incomplete, and prior point-based NeRF methods degrade sharply in those regions. The multi-scale-plus-global design handles this without the failure-prone pruning/growing heuristics of PointNeRF, and the ablation showing robustness down to 1% point density is practically relevant for autonomous-driving and AR applications. The main limitation is that computational cost remains bounded by the classic NeRF backbone (~20% overhead), and the authors themselves suggest that combining the multi-scale strategy with 3D Gaussian Splatting's rendering efficiency is a natural next step that remains open.
