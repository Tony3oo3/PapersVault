## Problem

Adapter-based style control methods for diffusion models inject image representations through separate cross-attention layers, causing interference between style and text conditions and degrading prompt-following ability. Additionally, existing encoders like CLIP are trained on coarse-grained semantics and cannot independently extract style from a single reference image without entangling content information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240732/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single arbitrary style reference image (no fine-tuning or multiple examples required) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired content. The style image is encoded into a style embedding, tokenized into 8×768 style tokens, and concatenated with text embedding tokens before being fed into the SD v1.5 text encoder. The output is a generated image that exhibits the visual style of the reference (line, texture, color, emotion) while faithfully rendering the content specified by the text prompt.

## Method

StyleTokenizer operates in two stages. First, a Style Encoder (CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone) is trained on the newly curated Style30K dataset (300+ style categories, 30K professionally annotated images) using supervised representation learning combined with contrastive loss, forcing the encoder to cluster same-style images while separating different styles and discarding content information. Second, a 2-layer MLP Style Tokenizer maps the style embedding into the word embedding space as 8×768 tokens, which are concatenated with text tokens and passed through SD's text encoder—aligning style and text in a common semantic space so they do not interfere. At inference, a modified classifier-free guidance scheme (inspired by InstructPix2Pix) applies separate guidance weights s_t and s_s to independently control the strength of text and style conditions during denoising.

## Results

On the 52-prompt / 28-style-reference benchmark, StyleTokenizer achieves the highest Style-Similarity (0.482) and Aesthetic Score (6.68), while maintaining Text-Image Similarity (0.277) nearly identical to the base Stable Diffusion model (0.279), confirming that style control does not degrade prompt following. In the user study, StyleTokenizer receives 61.8% votes from professional designers and 68.3% from ordinary users, far exceeding the next-best method (CAST at 19.5% / 14.6%). The Style Encoder also outperforms CLIP, VGG, and BlendGAN on clustering quality (Silhouette 0.308 vs. 0.168, 0.039, 0.129; Calinski-Harabasz 120.0 vs. 60.8, 62.8, 63.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleTokenizer achieves the best Style-Sim (0.482) and Aesthetic Score (6.68) among all compared methods, narrowly edging out IP-Adapter (0.480 / 6.59) on style fidelity while dramatically outperforming it on Text-Sim (0.277 vs. 0.116), which reflects IP-Adapter's well-known tendency to leak reference-image content into the output. Against the base Stable Diffusion model, StyleTokenizer matches prompt-following (0.277 vs. 0.279 Text-Sim) while boosting Style-Sim from 0.178 to 0.482 and Aesthetic from 6.55 to 6.68. Compared to GAN-based and inversion-based methods (CAST, StyTr², InST), StyleTokenizer leads on every metric: Style-Sim by 0.04–0.30, Aesthetic by 0.6–0.8, and Text-Sim by 0.02–0.04. In the user study, StyleTokenizer dominates with 61.8% (professional) and 68.3% (ordinary) vote share, roughly 3× the runner-up CAST (19.5% / 14.6%). The Style Encoder ablation (Table 2) further shows that the contrastive-trained encoder nearly doubles the Silhouette Coefficient over CLIP (0.308 vs. 0.168) and more than doubles the Calinski-Harabasz index (120.0 vs. 60.8), confirming that generic encoders are insufficient for fine-grained style discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion as the base generation framework and adopts the independent classifier-free guidance formulation from InstructPix2Pix to separately weight text and style conditions. It departs from the dominant adapter-based paradigm (IP-Adapter, StyleAdapter, InstantStyle) that injects image representations via additional cross-attention layers in the UNet, arguing that this causes interference between text and style control signals. Instead, inspired by LLaVA's visual tokenization strategy, it maps style embeddings into the text token space via a lightweight MLP (Style Tokenizer), so that style and content conditions occupy the same semantic space without overlap. The Style Encoder, initialized from CLIP ViT-B/32, is further trained with contrastive supervision on the newly curated Style30K dataset to specialize in style-specific features rather than generic semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, single-reference-image style control approach removes the need for per-style fine-tuning (Dreambooth, Textual Inversion) or manual prompt engineering, making style transfer practical for end users. The Style30K dataset (300+ categories, 30K professionally annotated images) is the largest open-source style dataset to date and provides a reusable resource for the community. Limitations include dependence on SD v1.5 architecture, a two-stage training pipeline, and the style encoder's generalization to styles outside the 300+ trained categories. Open questions include whether the text-space alignment strategy transfers to newer diffusion backbones (SDXL, DiT-based models) and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation.
