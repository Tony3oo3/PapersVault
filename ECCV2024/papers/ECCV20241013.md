## Problem

Existing procedural visual programming methods like ViperGPT and VISPROG generate code to answer image questions but cannot transfer to video because their modules are image-centric, lack temporal reasoning, and must iterate frame-by-frame, making them prohibitively slow and fragile on long videos. Meanwhile, end-to-end video QA models do not perform explicit procedural decomposition, and large multimodal models like GPT-4V are closed-source, expensive, and uninterpretable. The paper asks whether a code-generation framework with purpose-built video modules can answer complex video questions zero-shot while remaining interpretable and extensible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video sampled at 1 FPS at native resolution, a natural-language query, and optionally a set of multiple-choice options or an existing audio transcript. For open-ended benchmarks, the raw program output string is post-processed by embedding it with FastText and matching to the closest vocabulary entry (constrained per question category when available); for multiple-choice, the LLM is prompted to select from the given options. Beyond QA, the same pipeline can output tracked object trajectories or a spliced video clip, depending on the generated program.

## Method

ProViQ prompts GPT-3.5-turbo with a documented API of nine video-centric modules (filter_property, filter_object, find, track_objects, video_query, get_summary, get_script, get_caption, best_text_match, choose_option), the input query, and 3–4 in-context example programs retrieved by embedding similarity, then compiles and executes the resulting Python program via exec(). The modules wrap pre-trained models—BLIP-2 for frame/clip QA and captioning, GroundingDINO for text-conditioned detection, ByteTrack for multi-object tracking, LaViLa for video-to-text summarization, and Whisper for transcription—enabling frame-level, clip-level, and video-level reasoning without any fine-tuning. For long videos, a dedicated summarization module partitions the video into 1-second chunks, captions each with LaViLa, and aggregates the caption stream into a coherent narrative paragraph via an LLM, which is then used as context for answer selection. The key architectural insight is that video-specific modules (temporal filtering, multi-frame voting, narrative summarization) replace the single-frame image predicates used by prior visual-programming work, allowing the LLM to write concise programs that locate relevant segments before applying expensive vision-language models only where needed.

## Results

ProViQ (3-shot) achieves top-1 accuracy of 66.1 on TGIF-QA, 50.7 on iVQA, 42.3 on ActivityNet-QA, 63.8 on NeXT-QA, 66.3 on TVQA, and 57.1 on EgoSchema, setting state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on all eight benchmarks. The method surpasses the supervised SOTA on iVQA (50.7 vs. 40.9) and NeXT-QA (63.8 vs. 63.1), and is competitive on TGIF-QA (66.1 vs. 66.3) and ActivityNet-QA (42.3 vs. 43.2). The largest single-benchmark gain is 25 percentage points on EgoSchema (57.1 vs. 32.1 supervised SOTA), driven by the long-video summarization module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProViQ outperforms ViperGPT (the closest prior procedural method) on every shared benchmark, with gains of +14.9 on TGIF-QA (66.1 vs. 51.2), +9.3 on iVQA (50.7 vs. 41.4), +11.7 on ActivityNet (42.3 vs. 30.6), and +3.8 on NeXT-QA (63.8 vs. 60.0), attributed to video-specific modules enabling temporal reasoning. Against FrozenBiLM, ProViQ leads by a wide margin on all overlapping datasets, most dramatically on NeXT-QA (63.8 vs. 26.1) and iVQA (50.7 vs. 26.8). On TVQA, ProViQ (66.3) substantially exceeds SeViLa (38.2) and FrozenBiLM (59.7), benefiting from the get_script and best_text_match modules for multimodal reasoning. ProViQ surpasses the supervised SOTA on iVQA (+9.8) and NeXT-QA (+0.7) while remaining within 1 point on TGIF-QA and ActivityNet, though it trails on MSVD (37.5 vs. 54.8), MSRVTT (22.1 vs. 47.0), and TVQA (66.3 vs. 86.1), which the authors attribute to noisy labels and language-dominated question types.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProViQ directly extends the visual programming paradigm established by ViperGPT, VISPROG, and CodeVQA, which use LLMs to generate short Python programs over image-centric APIs for compositional reasoning. The key departure is replacing image-only modules with a video-specific API (temporal filtering, video-level QA, multi-object tracking, long-video summarization) that enables reasoning over time rather than single frames. This positions ProViQ against both end-to-end video QA methods (FrozenBiLM, SeViLa, Just Ask) that require training, and large closed-source multimodal models (GPT-4V, Gemini) that are expensive and opaque, offering a training-free, interpretable alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProViQ demonstrates that procedural code generation over a modular video API can match or exceed supervised state-of-the-art on multiple video QA benchmarks without any task-specific training, which is notable given the typical gap between zero-shot and supervised methods. The modular design also enables capabilities beyond QA (grounded tracking, video editing) simply by composing existing modules, suggesting a flexible framework for video understanding. Key limitations include dependence on the quality of underlying video-to-text models (especially for long-video summarization), slower inference than end-to-end approaches due to code generation and execution, and vulnerability to LLM misuse of modules when the API grows large. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the module set can be expanded to cover currently unsupported question types.
