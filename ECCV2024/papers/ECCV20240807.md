## Problem

Deep neural networks suffer from information bottleneck: as input data passes through successive layers, critical information needed for the target task is lost, producing unreliable gradients and poor convergence. Existing remedies—reversible architectures, masked modeling, and deep supervision—each carry significant drawbacks such as increased inference cost, loss-function conflicts, or error accumulation that particularly harms lightweight models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240807/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (640×640 on MS COCO) and outputs anchor-free bounding boxes with class labels via a simplified prediction head. All YOLOv9 variants are trained from scratch for 500 epochs without ImageNet pre-training, and evaluation reports the full COCO AP suite (AP50:95, AP50, AP75, AP_S/M/L). A transfer-learning variant is also evaluated on VOC using COCO-pretrained weights.

## Method

YOLOv9 combines two contributions: (1) Programmable Gradient Information (PGI), a training-only auxiliary framework consisting of a main branch, an auxiliary reversible branch (RCN built on CBNet's DHLC linkage), and a multi-level auxiliary information module that aggregates gradients from all prediction heads before back-propagating to the main branch—ensuring reliable gradients without any extra inference cost; and (2) Generalized ELAN (GELAN), which extends ELAN's layer-aggregation design to accept arbitrary computational blocks (CSP, Res, Dark, Conv) while maintaining linear scaling of parameters, FLOPs, and accuracy with depth. The key insight is that the reversible branch acts as a deep-supervision path that preserves I(Y,X) during training, allowing even shallow, under-parameterized networks to receive correct gradient signals and avoid the information-broken problem that plagues conventional multi-head deep supervision.

## Results

On MS COCO, YOLOv9-E (57.3M params, 189.0G FLOPs) achieves 55.6% AP₅₀:₉₅, surpassing YOLOv8-X (68.2M, 257.8G) by 1.7% AP while using 16% fewer parameters and 27% fewer FLOPs. YOLOv9-C (25.3M, 102.1G) matches YOLOv7 AF's 53.0% AP with 42% fewer parameters and 22% fewer FLOPs. On the small VOC dataset, YOLOv9-S achieves 65.1% AP (train-from-scratch) and 74.4% AP (COCO-pretrained), outperforming YOLOv8-S (67.1% pretrained) and YOLOv5-S (62.4% pretrained). In latency, YOLOv9-C runs at 6.1 ms on T4 GPU (TensorRT FP16), 10% faster than YOLOv7 AF (6.7 ms) at the same accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOLOv9 dominates across all size tiers. At the compact level, YOLOv9-C (25.3M, 102.1G) matches YOLOv7 AF's 53.0% AP with 42% fewer parameters and 22% fewer FLOPs, and is 10% faster in inference (6.1 ms vs 6.7 ms on T4). At the large level, YOLOv9-E (57.3M, 189.0G) beats YOLOv8-X (68.2M, 257.8G) by 1.7% AP (55.6 vs 53.9) while using 16% fewer parameters and 27% fewer FLOPs. Against depth-wise-convolution-based YOLO MS, YOLOv9-S achieves higher AP (46.8 vs 46.2) with fewer parameters (7.1M vs 8.1M) and fewer FLOPs (26.4G vs 31.2G), demonstrating superior parameter utilization with standard convolutions. YOLOv9 also outperforms YOLOv6, DAMO YOLO, and Gold YOLO at comparable model sizes across all reported metrics. On the VOC small dataset, YOLOv9-S with COCO pretraining reaches 74.4% AP, surpassing YOLOv8-S (67.1%) and YOLOv5-S (62.4%), confirming PGI's advantage in transfer learning. In Figure 5, YOLOv9 (train-from-scratch) surpasses RT DETR, RTMDet, and PP-YOLOE which all use ImageNet pre-trained weights, highlighting the strength of PGI in extracting reliable gradients without external pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLOv9 generalizes ELAN into GELAN by allowing arbitrary computational blocks (CSP, Res, Dark) in place of fixed convolutions, and inherits its overall detector framework from YOLOv7. The key conceptual departure is the Programmable Gradient Information (PGI) framework, which reframes the information-bottleneck problem in deep networks: rather than embedding reversible connections in the main branch (as in RevCol or PreAct ResNet, which inflate inference cost) or relying on traditional deep supervision (which causes error accumulation in lightweight models), PGI introduces an auxiliary reversible branch (RCN, derived from DynamicDet and CBNet's DHLC linkage) that exists only during training to supply reliable gradients. This positions the paper at the intersection of reversible-architecture theory and practical real-time detection, arguing that gradient reliability—not gradient vanishing—is the root cause of poor convergence in deep CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PGI is presented as a general training technique demonstrated across object detection, instance segmentation, panoptic segmentation, and image captioning, suggesting broad applicability beyond any single architecture. The finding that a train-from-scratch YOLOv9 can match or exceed ImageNet-pre-trained RT DETR in accuracy while using only 66% of the parameters is practically significant for deployment scenarios where pre-training is infeasible. However, the theoretical grounding of the information-bottleneck argument remains somewhat informal (mutual-information inequalities are stated but not rigorously tied to specific layer operations), and PGI adds 30–40% training time, which may limit adoption in compute-constrained settings. Open questions include whether PGI's benefits persist at very large model scales and whether the auxiliary reversible branch can be further compressed or made adaptive.
