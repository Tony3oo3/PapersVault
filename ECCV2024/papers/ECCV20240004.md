## Problem

Existing image-to-LiDAR distillation methods (e.g., SLidR, Seal) treat each LiDAR scan as an isolated snapshot, ignoring the rich temporal dynamics inherent in sequential LiDAR acquisition. Additionally, class-agnostic superpixels introduce 'self-conflict' in contrastive objectives, and varying point cloud density across scenes undermines feature consistency. These limitations lead to sub-optimal 3D representation learning that fails to capture spatiotemporal structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240004/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive LiDAR-camera pairs (keyframe plus T sweep scans within a time window, typically 2 sweeps at 20 Hz) along with sensor calibration matrices (intrinsics Γ_K and extrinsics Γ_{c←l}) for projection. The 3D backbone is a MinkUNet sparse convolutional network (18/34/50/101 layers) and the 2D teacher is a frozen DINOv2 ViT (S/B/L) with OpenSeeD for superpixel generation. Output is a pretrained 3D point feature encoder that, when linearly probed or fine-tuned with a lightweight head, produces per-point semantic class predictions across 16–19 classes depending on the dataset.

## Method

SuperFlow introduces three complementary modules operating on calibrated LiDAR-camera sequences. First, a view consistency alignment module fine-tunes a VFM segmentation head with CLIP text prompts to generate language-guided semantic superpixels that are unified across camera views, eliminating the 'self-conflict' of instance-level superpixels. Second, a dense-to-sparse (D2S) regularization concatenates multi-sweep LiDAR scans (transformed via Γ_{t←s}) into a dense point cloud and enforces cosine-similarity consistency between dense and sparse superpoint features, making the encoder invariant to point density. Third, a flow-based contrastive learning (FCL) module takes three LiDAR-camera pairs at timestamps {t−Δt, t, t+Δt} and applies both a spatial contrastive loss (distilling 2D superpixel features into 3D superpoint features via InfoNCE) and a temporal contrastive loss (aligning superpoint features across consecutive scenes), jointly capturing structural 3D layout and 4D temporal dynamics.

## Results

Using MinkUNet-34 as the 3D backbone and DINOv2 ViT-B for distillation, SuperFlow achieves 47.66% mIoU on nuScenes linear probing, 48.09% at 1% fine-tuning, and 76.57% on full nuScenes, outperforming the strongest prior method Seal (46.59%, 45.98%, 75.41%) by 1.07, 2.11, and 1.16 points respectively. On cross-domain transfer, SuperFlow reaches 48.40% mIoU on SemanticKITTI 1% and 50.20% on Waymo Open 1%, surpassing Seal by 1.16 and 1.29 points. Scaling the 2D backbone to ViT-L further lifts SuperFlow to 48.01% (LP) and 77.19% (Full) on nuScenes, demonstrating consistent gains with larger teacher models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperFlow consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluation setting and dataset. Against the strongest prior method Seal (NeurIPS'23), SuperFlow gains +1.07 mIoU on nuScenes LP, +2.11 at 1% fine-tuning, +1.16 on full nuScenes, +1.16 on KITTI 1%, and +1.29 on Waymo 1%. Compared to the original SLidR (CVPR'22), the improvements are larger: +2.31 on LP, +6.45 at 1%, and +2.90 on KITTI 1%. HVDistill (IJCV'24) achieves the highest full-data nuScenes score (76.60) but lags SuperFlow on LP (39.50 vs 47.66) and all few-shot settings, indicating weaker representation quality. On the domain generalization study (Table 2), SuperFlow leads all 14 fine-tuning tasks across seven heterogeneous datasets, with the largest margin on Synth4D 10% (69.43 vs 66.96 for Seal). In the Robo3D robustness benchmark (Table 3), SuperFlow achieves the best average mIoU of 63.68% under full fine-tuning with MinkUNet-34, surpassing Seal (62.81%) and SLidR (56.83%), and the best LP score of 35.99% vs Seal's 33.88%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperFlow extends the image-to-LiDAR distillation paradigm initiated by SLidR and advanced by Seal, which distills knowledge from pretrained 2D backbones (DINOv2) into 3D point cloud networks via superpixel-driven contrastive objectives. The key departure is the introduction of temporal (4D) information: prior methods treat each LiDAR scan as an isolated snapshot, whereas SuperFlow exploits consecutive LiDAR-camera pairs to formulate flow-based contrastive learning and dense-to-sparse consistency regularization. Additionally, the view consistency alignment module departs from instance-level superpixels (SLIC or VFM panoptic heads) by using CLIP-guided semantic categories to unify superpixels across camera views, addressing the 'self-conflict' problem identified in ST-SLidR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that temporal cues inherent in sequential LiDAR acquisition—previously underutilized in pretraining—yield consistent 1–2% mIoU gains across 11 heterogeneous datasets, offering a practical recipe for improving 3D representation learning without additional annotations. The non-monotonic scaling behavior of 3D backbones (MinkUNet-101 underperforming MinkUNet-50) raises open questions about the optimal capacity for 3D pretraining and whether larger architectures require different training strategies. A key limitation is that the dense-to-sparse module relies on accurate multi-sweep registration, and the temporal contrastive loss assumes semantic superpoint correspondence across frames, which may degrade under high vehicle speeds or rapid scene changes.
