## Problem

Prompt-based continual learning methods rely on learned keys to select relevant prompts from a shared pool, but these keys drift as new tasks are introduced, causing the selection mechanism itself to suffer catastrophic forgetting. Prompts learned for one task get incorrectly retrieved for another, degrading performance in ways that existing approaches largely overlook. The paper asks how to make prompt selection stable across an unbounded sequence of tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241616/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a class label over all classes seen so far. The image is processed by two frozen backbones in sequence: CLIP ViT-L/14 produces a visual embedding used as a query, while an ImageNet-pretrained ViT-B/16 produces the final classification. At inference, the task identity is unknown, so the correct prompt is selected per-instance via cosine similarity in CLIP's embedding space, and the ViT's CLS token is passed through a task-specific linear head.

## Method

STAR-Prompt introduces a two-level prompting pipeline that decouples stability from plasticity. The first level trains a single learnable token per class, concatenated with the class name and fed to CLIP's text encoder, yielding stable class-prototype keys in CLIP's multimodal space. These keys index a second codebook of per-layer semantic residual vectors, which are added (rather than concatenated) to the ViT's MLP inputs, forcing CLIP-derived class semantics into the frozen transformer. A confidence modulation scales each residual by the CLIP similarity score, and a Mixture-of-Gaussians generative replay in both stages mitigates forgetting without storing raw samples. Orthogonality losses and class-specific query weighting further reduce cross-task interference.

## Results

STAR-Prompt achieves a Final Average Accuracy (FAA) of 87.92% on the natural/specialized domain (Split ImageNet-R, CIFAR-100, Cars-196, CUB-200) and 77.88% on the aerial/medical domain (EuroSAT, RESISC45, CropDiseases, ISIC, ChestX-ray), outperforming the runner-up SLCA by +5.96% on average. On domain-shifted benchmarks, the gains are especially pronounced: 93.70% on EuroSAT vs. 54.50% for zero-shot CLIP, and 92.28% on RESISC45 vs. 63.15%. The method closely approaches its Joint (stationary) upper bound with an average gap of only −3.59%, indicating near-negligible forgetting across the task sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR-Prompt outperforms all prompt-based CL baselines (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, AttriCLIP, PromptFusion) on average across both domain groups, with the largest margins over CODA-Prompt (+22.67 on natural/specialized) and AttriCLIP (+15.74). Against the strongest full-fine-tuning competitor SLCA, STAR-Prompt gains +5.96% on average while using only a fraction of the learnable parameters. On domain-shifted aerial/medical data, STAR-Prompt surpasses even rehearsal-based methods (DER++: 77.88 vs. 67.72; GDumb: 77.88 vs. 65.73), whereas prompt-based competitors like L2P and CODA-Prompt are outperformed by these rehearsal baselines. The method also substantially exceeds zero-shot CLIP, with gaps of +37.75% on average for aerial/medical and +13.86% for natural/specialized, confirming that continual adaptation is necessary even when a strong foundation model is available. The only notable exception is CIFAR-100, where SLCA (91.53%) slightly edges out STAR-Prompt (90.12%), and CUB-200, where the Joint ViT-B/16 upper bound (88.00%) exceeds the incremental result (84.10%).

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR-Prompt extends the prompt-tuning lineage for continual learning (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) by identifying and addressing a previously overlooked instability in the prompt selection mechanism itself. The first-level prompting stage directly builds on CoOp's text-encoder conditioning, while the query weighting and orthogonality constraints are adopted from CODA-Prompt. The key departure is the two-level architecture: CLIP's text encoder provides stable class-prototype keys (borrowing the stability argument from PromptFusion), and a second pool of semantic residual prompts adapts a separate ImageNet-pretrained ViT for plasticity. The generative replay component extends SLCA's single-Gaussian feature modeling to a Mixture of Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central insight—that prompt selection in existing CL methods is itself subject to catastrophic forgetting—reframes how the community should think about the stability-plasticity tradeoff in prompt-based CL, shifting attention from the prompts themselves to the retrieval mechanism. The striking domain-gap results (e.g., 93.70% vs. 54.50% zero-shot CLIP on EuroSAT) underscore that even powerful foundation models require task-specific adaptation, a point with practical implications for deploying CL in specialized domains like remote sensing and medical imaging. Open questions include whether the two-backbone requirement (CLIP ViT-L/14 + ViT-B/16) limits scalability, whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal CL, and whether the semantic residual mechanism generalizes to architectures beyond ViT.
