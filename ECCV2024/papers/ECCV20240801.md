## Problem

Generic pre-trained face ID models fail to adapt to the cinematic domain's extreme pose, lighting, and appearance variations, and traditional clustering algorithms require non-intuitive user-defined hyperparameters such as distance thresholds or cluster counts. Additionally, no comprehensive movie face clustering benchmark exists for evaluating real-world performance in the video production domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240801/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw video as input, which is preprocessed into face tracks via scene-cut detection (PySceneDetect), RetinaFace detection, BoT-SORT motion tracking, and SER-FIQ quality filtering; crops are sampled every 12th frame (assuming 24 fps) to capture pose/expression variation. The output is a cluster ID assignment for each face track (with an 'Unknown' label for low-quality tracks) plus a finetuned face ID model whose embedding space is optimized for the specific video's identity set.

## Method

VideoClusterNet operates in two stages. Stage 1 is a self-supervised finetuning loop: a randomly initialized MLP head is attached to a pre-trained face ID model (e.g., ArcFace-R100), duplicated into teacher and student branches, and trained with a BYOL-style similarity loss using only positive pairs (no negative pairs or temporal co-occurrence constraints). Coarse cross-track matching is performed by fitting a multivariate Gaussian to each track's crop embeddings and computing a per-track PDF-based threshold; matched tracks generate additional positive pairs for the next finetuning iteration. Stage 2 is a parameter-free agglomerative clustering algorithm that uses the SSL loss itself as the similarity metric (rather than Euclidean or cosine distance), computes a custom per-track threshold from intra-track pair similarities, and iteratively merges track pairs whose inter-track mean similarity falls below the ratio of their respective thresholds, terminating when no new merges occur.

## Results

On the novel MovieFaceCluster dataset (9 movies, 3,619 tracks, avg. 23.2 unique characters), VideoClusterNet achieves an average WCP of ~93.0%, outperforming the best baseline VCTRSF (~88.3%) by ~4.7 points. On the BBT-S01 benchmark, the method reaches 98.70% combined WCP, surpassing VCTRSF (94.20%) and BCL (89.63%). The method also reports a Predicted Cluster Ratio (PCR) close to 1.0 on most movies (e.g., 1.04 on The Hidden Soldier, 1.02 on The Fortress), indicating accurate cluster count estimation without requiring the ground-truth number of identities as input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoClusterNet outperforms all seven compared baselines across every MovieFaceCluster movie. Against the closest competitor VCTRSF [53], the gap ranges from +0.9 (Elephant's Journey: 97.2 vs 96.3) to +10.5 (The Fortress: 89.3 vs 78.8), with VCTRSF unable to report PCR since it requires the ground-truth cluster count as input. On BBT-S01, the method achieves 98.70% combined WCP versus VCTRSF's 94.20% and BCL's 89.63% [47]. TSiam and SSiam [41] (self-supervised pair-mining methods) trail by 5–20 points on MovieFaceCluster, particularly on high-variety movies like The Fortress (68.6/71.1 vs 89.3) and Under the Shadow (71.8/68.3 vs 82.5). JFRAC [61] and CCL [42] show similar weaknesses on movies with many characters (The Fortress: 65.3/62.7 vs 89.3). The MvCorr [43] and CP-SSC [44] baselines are only reported on BVS S5E2 (97.7 and 65.2 respectively), where MvCorr is competitive but CP-SSC lags significantly.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from BYOL-style self-distillation (adapted for finetuning rather than training from scratch) and agglomerative clustering, but departs from the dominant trend in video face clustering of relying on negative pairs for contrastive learning. Prior methods (TSiam, SSiam, JFRAC, CCL) all mine negative pairs through temporal co-occurrence or pseudo-relevance feedback, whereas VideoClusterNet eliminates negatives entirely, using only positive pairs within and across coarsely matched tracks. The parameter-free clustering stage is novel in repurposing the SSL loss itself as a per-track adaptive distance metric, rather than imposing a generic Euclidean or cosine threshold. The introduction of MovieFaceCluster also fills a gap, as prior benchmarks (BBT, BVS) are TV series with relatively controlled settings, while movies present more extreme cinematic variations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, positive-pairs-only design removes two major practical pain points: hyperparameter tuning across datasets and the fragile negative-pair mining strategies that can introduce sub-optimality from incorrect pair selection. The MovieFaceCluster dataset, hand-curated by film post-production specialists, provides a more realistic evaluation benchmark for the video production domain where generic face ID models are known to underperform. However, the authors acknowledge that pre-existing biases in the generic face ID model can propagate through the self-supervised finetuning, and the iterative finetuning-clustering loop may not suit real-time applications. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large cast sizes (hundreds of characters) and whether the multivariate Gaussian assumption for track distributions holds under extreme appearance changes (e.g., aging, prosthetics).
