## Problem

Existing few-shot defect image generation methods are trained per-product, ignoring two key consistencies: intra-product background consistency (defects originate from normal regions of the same product) and inter-product defect consistency (similar defects share morphology across products). This leads to poor generation quality, limited diversity, and inability to control defect strength or automatically produce mask annotations from limited training samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242040/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two separate single-object text prompts (product name and defect name) encoded by CLIP, along with two scalar perturbation scales (w_p for background representation and w_d for defect strength), and produces a 512×512 defect image paired with a pixel-level binary mask. Unlike standard text-to-image pipelines that use a single combined prompt, DefectDiffu deliberately uses disentangled single-object prompts to avoid catastrophic neglect of either background or defect features. The mask is derived from cross-attention maps in the defect blocks rather than requiring separate annotation.

## Method

DefectDiffu divides a pre-trained DiT backbone into three stacked parts—background, defect, and fusion—each conditioned on a different CLIP text embedding (product name, defect name, and their combination, respectively) to disentangle and then integrate background and defect generation. The double-free strategy, built on classifier-free guidance, computes two perturbation directions: F_p^t (intra-product background consistency) and F_d^t (inter-product defect consistency), which are scaled by w_p and w_d to control product type and defect strength independently, enabling generation beyond the training distribution. An adaptive attention-enhanced loss dynamically upweights the reconstruction loss in defect regions based on cross-attention maps, preventing the model from overlooking small or multiple defects. Cross-attention maps from the defect blocks are concatenated and decoded to produce mask annotations simultaneously with image generation, and the defect perturbation direction can be transferred to unseen products for zero-shot defect generation.

## Results

On the MVTecAD dataset (15 product categories), DefectDiffu achieves an average FID of 69.35 and LPIPS of 0.21 for generation quality, substantially outperforming Defect-Gen (FID 233.70), DFMGAN (FID 145.19), and StyleGAN2 (FID 138.99). For downstream defect segmentation, DefectDiffu-augmented training yields an average mIoU of 67.1, F1 of 79.0, mAP of 84.5, and AUC of 98.7, surpassing the real-data-only baseline (mIoU 60.3, AUC 97.7) and all competing generation methods. The method also enables zero-shot defect generation by transferring defect perturbation directions across products without requiring target-product defect samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DefectDiffu outperforms all three baselines on average across both generation quality and downstream detection. In generation (Table 1), DefectDiffu achieves an average FID of 69.35, roughly 2× better than DFMGAN (145.19) and 3.4× better than Defect-Gen (233.70); StyleGAN2 (138.99) is competitive on FID but does not produce masks. In detection (Table 2), DefectDiffu improves average mIoU from 60.3 (baseline) to 67.1, beating Defect-Gen (63.5) by 3.6 points and DFMGAN (60.3) by 6.8 points; AUC rises from 97.7 to 98.7. Defect-Gen edges out DefectDiffu on two categories (hazelnut mIoU 87.4 vs 87.2; toothbrush mIoU 44.8 vs 44.0) but trails on the remaining 13. DFMGAN is consistently the weakest baseline, with particularly large gaps on grid (mIoU 37.4 vs 47.3) and metal_nut (73.0 vs 91.5), and it fails catastrophically on toothbrush (AUC 58.3 vs 96.2). Notably, DefectDiffu is the only method that simultaneously generates both defect images and pixel-level masks, eliminating the need for separate mask annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DefectDiffu builds directly on DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) as its backbone, repurposing the transformer's hierarchical stacking to inject separate text conditions into background, defect, and fusion blocks—a structural departure from the UNet-based diffusion architectures used in prior defect generators (DefectGAN, DFMGAN, Defect-Gen). The double-free strategy extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) from a single conditional/unconditional pair to two independent perturbation directions (product identity and defect type), enabling simultaneous control over both attributes. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant single-product paradigm in defect generation by explicitly modeling intra-product background consistency and inter-product defect consistency via CLIP text prompts, and it borrows the cross-attention-to-mask pipeline from DiffuMask and Attn2Mask for automatic annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to transfer defect perturbation directions across products for zero-shot generation is practically significant for industrial settings where collecting defect samples for every new product is prohibitively expensive. However, the zero-shot results are presented only qualitatively (Fig. 5) without quantitative metrics, and all experiments are confined to the 15-category MVTecAD benchmark, leaving open how well the consistency modeling generalizes to more diverse product geometries, 3D surface defects, or products with highly variable normal appearances. The adaptive attention-enhanced loss, while effective, is a relatively straightforward reweighting strategy, and the overall framework's dependence on accurate product/defect name prompts may limit robustness in open-vocabulary industrial scenarios.
