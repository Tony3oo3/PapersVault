## Problem

While Stable Diffusion demonstrates strong image generation capabilities, it remains unclear whether and how its denoising pre-training knowledge can be efficiently transferred to spatially demanding dense prediction tasks like semantic segmentation and depth estimation. Prior work either neglects the efficiency of tuning the large UNet backbone or ignores critical factors such as noise injection and textual conditioning that affect transfer quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241111/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, projects it into the latent space via a pre-trained autoencoder, and deliberately adds small Gaussian noise (t=10 out of 1000 DDPM steps) before feeding it to the frozen SD UNet in a single denoising step. A text prompt (e.g., all 150 ADE20K class names or BLIP2 captions) is encoded by the CLIP text encoder to condition cross-attention. The output consists of multi-scale feature maps from the UNet's upsampling stages concatenated with cross-attention maps at 16×16 and 32×32 resolutions, which are fused via a lightweight Semantic FPN head to produce per-pixel segmentation masks or depth maps.

## Method

The core pipeline repurposes the Stable Diffusion UNet as a single-step feature encoder: an image is projected to latent space, perturbed with small noise to bridge the denoising pre-training distribution, and passed through the UNet once, with cross-attention maps (computed from CLIP-encoded class tokens) concatenated to the upsampling-stage features to provide explicit spatial-textual alignment. For parameter-efficient tuning, the authors formulate a multi-objective reward (accuracy × parameter-efficiency) and use an RNN controller optimized with PPO to search over where to insert LoRA modules and adapters within the UNet's residual and transformer blocks. The RL search discovers that placing LoRA exclusively in cross-attention layers and adapters after transformer FFN blocks achieves 52.1 mIoU with only 1.7% tunable parameters, outperforming uniform LoRA (51.3 mIoU at 2.2%) and approaching full tuning (54.1 mIoU) while being far more memory-efficient.

## Results

On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the proposed method (SD-UNet + Semantic FPN) achieves 54.1 mIoU, outperforming the strongest ImageNet-pretrained backbone ConvNeXt-XL (53.3) by 0.8% and the CLIP-based DenseCLIP (50.6) by 3.5%, while using fewer GFlops (891 vs. 834/1043). On NYUv2 depth estimation, the method achieves an RMSE of 0.241, surpassing the previous best VPD (0.254) by 5.2% relative improvement and SwinV2-L (0.287) by 16.0%. For parameter-efficient tuning, the RL-searched protocol (LoRA on cross-attention + adapter after FFN) achieves 52.1 mIoU with only 1.7% tunable parameters, closing the gap to full tuning (54.1) to just 2.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method decisively outperforms all ImageNet-pretrained backbones: ConvNeXt-XL (53.3), Swin-L (52.0), and ViT-B variants (48.3–49.1) on ADE20K mIoU, with the largest margin over ResNet101-based methods (FCN 39.9, PSPNet 43.6). Against CLIP-based encoders, the SD UNet surpasses DenseCLIP (50.6) by 3.5% and raw CLIP (49.4) by 4.7%, which the authors attribute to the UNet's superior spatial localization via cross-attention. Among prior SD-based methods, the paper improves over VPD (53.7) by 0.4% on segmentation and by 5.2% relative on NYUv2 depth RMSE (0.241 vs. 0.254), while also beating SwinV2-L (0.287), ZoeDepth (0.270), and AiT (0.275) on depth. For parameter-efficient tuning, the RL-searched protocol (52.1 mIoU, 1.7% params) outperforms naive LoRA (51.3, 2.2%) and Adapter (50.9, 2.3%) applied uniformly, though it still trails full tuning (54.1, 100%) by 2.0%. The method does not directly compete with multi-modal pre-training approaches like BEiT-3 (62.8) or ViT-Adapter-L (61.5) in Table 3, which use different training paradigms and are listed only as upper-bound references.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Stable Diffusion UNet architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and repurposes its denoiser as a feature encoder for dense prediction, departing from prior work that restricted diffusion models to generative tasks. It extends the line of diffusion-to-perception transfer initiated by VPD and TADP, but explicitly addresses the efficiency gap those works left open by introducing a reinforcement-learning-based search for parameter-efficient tuning protocols. Conceptually, it also draws on NLP parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods (LoRA, adapters, prefix-tuning) and adapts them to the more complex UNet architecture with cross-attention and residual blocks, while positioning SD UNet as a spatially stronger alternative to CLIP-based encoders like DenseCLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-ablated recipe for leveraging pretrained diffusion models as perception backbones, which is timely as the community seeks to unify generation and understanding. The finding that small-scale noise injection (t=10) is necessary to avoid train-finetune inconsistency, and that cross-attention maps significantly boost spatial localization, offers actionable design guidance beyond what prior diffusion-perception papers provided. However, the UNet's 859M parameters and 891 GFLOPs remain a significant deployment barrier, and the authors acknowledge that feature distillation to compress these capabilities is an open problem. The RL-based PEFT search, while effective here, has not been validated across architectures or tasks beyond ADE20K segmentation.
