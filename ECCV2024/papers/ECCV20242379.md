## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture video generation methods rely on 2D skeleton representations that lack 3D body shape constraints, leading to blurred, distorted, or missing hands in generated videos. While 3D mesh-based gesture generation has been explored, prior work stops at producing gestures in parameter space rather than synthesizing the final photorealistic video, leaving a gap between audio-driven motion prediction and realistic video output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242379/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input audio sequence and a speaker identity (a single short training video of the character at 30 fps, 720×1280, cropped to 512×512) and produces a photorealistic co-speech gesture video with synchronized lip movements, facial expressions, and hand gestures. The pipeline is per-character: a UV texture map is optimized and a GAN generator is trained for each speaker, with the SMPL-X mesh sequence serving as the intermediate 3D representation that bridges audio prediction and video synthesis.

## Method

The pipeline decomposes generation into three stages: (1) an audio-to-mesh module (TALKSHOW) predicts per-frame SMPL-X parameters using a wav2vec2+TCN face encoder-decoder and a two-stage VQ-VAE + Gated PixelCNN body/hand generator; (2) a shared UV texture map is optimized via differentiable rendering against ground-truth frames to ground the appearance of each body region; (3) a UNet-based conditional GAN with SPADE normalization synthesizes each frame from the rendered textured mesh, depth map, normal map, and a bidirectional context window of 2k neighboring textured meshes (k prior + k subsequent) to ensure temporal consistency without error propagation. The key insight is that textured 3D meshes provide strong geometric and appearance priors—especially for highly dynamic hands—that 2D keypoints cannot, and the GAN then adds high-frequency details and realistic lighting on top of this grounded structure.

## Results

On the TalkSHOW dataset (4 speakers, 512×512 resolution), the full method consistently outperforms the 2D-keypoint baseline across all metrics. For example, on the 'Conan' speaker, the method achieves PSNR 21.125 vs. 20.002, SSIM 0.891 vs. 0.866, and LPIPS 0.398 vs. 0.410. In the user study, MTurkers prefer the full method over the 2D-keypoint baseline by a large margin (70–96% preference across speakers). The ablation shows that textured meshes contribute the largest gain (e.g., +0.45 dB PSNR on Oliver), while depth/normal maps provide a smaller but consistent improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against a single 2D-keypoint baseline (inspired by Ginosar et al. and Lu et al.), which extracts upper-body, face, and hand joints from the same SMPL-X meshes to ensure identical temporal consistency. The proposed 3D mesh-based method wins on all three metrics (SSIM, PSNR, LPIPS) for all four speakers. The PSNR gain ranges from +0.16 dB (Chemistry) to +1.12 dB (Conan), while LPIPS improves by 0.01–0.07. In the user study, the full method is preferred over the 2D-keypoint baseline by 70% (Oliver), 96% (Chemistry), 84% (Conan), and 96% (Seth) of MTurkers. The authors note that direct comparison with prior co-speech gesture video methods (Ginosar et al., Liu et al., Qian et al.) was not possible due to unavailable code for the full video generation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the co-speech gesture video generation line of work (Ginosar et al., Qian et al., Liu et al.) by replacing the standard 2D skeleton intermediate representation with explicit 3D SMPL-X meshes, building directly on TalkSHOW (Yi et al., 2023) for audio-to-mesh prediction. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a UV texture map optimization step via differentiable rendering, which grounds the appearance of body regions before a conditional GAN (using SPADE normalization and a UNet generator) synthesizes the final photorealistic video. For temporal consistency, the paper modifies the sequential frame conditioning of Vid2Vid and WC-Vid2Vid into a bidirectional context window of textured meshes, avoiding error propagation. Prior 3D mesh-based gesture generation works (Habibie et al., Yi et al.) stop at producing 3D parameters and do not generate final RGB video, which this paper explicitly bridges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D textured meshes serve as an effective and robust intermediate representation for co-speech video generation, particularly for the challenging hand region where 2D skeletons fail to provide sufficient geometric grounding. However, the method requires per-character texture optimization and GAN training, limiting scalability, and struggles with novel poses, dynamic backgrounds, and limited training data. Open questions include whether the pipeline can be made character-agnostic (e.g., via encoder-based appearance modeling), whether more expressive 3D representations can be made robust enough for hands, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the four-speaker TalkSHOW dataset. The finding that a simple UV texture map outperforms view-dependent NeRF representations for hand regions is a practical insight that may influence future 3D-to-video pipelines.
