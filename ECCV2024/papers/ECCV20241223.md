## Problem

Adversarial attacks have been shown to severely degrade visual object trackers, yet no dedicated adversarial defense method has been proposed for the tracking task. Existing defenses target image classification and cannot transfer directly due to structural differences between classifiers and siamese trackers, while retraining entire trackers for robustness is prohibitively expensive in time and compute.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241223/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AADN takes a single image patch (either the template crop from the first frame or the search region crop from a subsequent frame) that may contain imperceptible adversarial perturbations and outputs a defense-transformed patch of the same spatial dimensions. The network is instantiated as UNet-128 for the template branch and UNet-256 for the search branch, and can be deployed on one or both branches independently. The transformed patch is then fed into the frozen siamese tracker as if it were a clean input, requiring no modification to the tracker's forward pass.

## Method

AADN is a U-Net-based pre-processing network trained via a two-pass adversarial procedure guided by a Dua-Loss that combines cross-entropy classification loss (coarse localization) and Smooth-L1 regression loss (fine localization). In the first forward pass, the network processes a Gaussian-noise-perturbed input; the resulting Dua-Loss gradient is used to construct an FGSM-style adversarial perturbation (Eq. 6). In the second forward pass, the adversarially perturbed input is processed again, and the defense network parameters are updated by backpropagating the Dua-Loss. The tracker parameters remain frozen throughout training, making AADN a plug-and-play module that can be attached to heterogeneous siamese or anchor-free trackers without retraining. The use of Dua-Loss ensures that generated adversarial samples simultaneously stress both the classification and regression heads, producing more aggressive training signals than either loss alone.

## Results

On OTB100 under CSA attack with both-branch defense, AADN improves Success from 0.324 to 0.559 (+72.5%) and Precision from 0.471 to 0.777 (+65.0%) in the non-adaptive setting. On VOT2018, the largest gain is a 126.8% EAO improvement (0.123→0.279) under CSA with template-only defense. On LaSOT, both-branch defense recovers Success from 0.168 to 0.337 (+100.6%) under CSA. The defense network adds only 5.36 ms/frame overhead (14.71 ms total vs. 9.35 ms for the baseline SiamRPN++), and transfers to SiamRPN, SiamMask, and OCEAN without retraining, yielding 3–6% Success improvements under IoU Attack.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Since no prior adversarial defense method exists for object tracking, the primary comparison is the attacked tracker (no defense) versus the same tracker augmented with AADN. Across all three datasets and all three attack methods (CSA, DFA, IoU Attack), AADN consistently outperforms the undefended tracker, with non-adaptive improvements ranging from +15.6% (IoU Attack on LaSOT Success) to +126.8% (CSA on VOT2018 EAO). Under adaptive attacks—where the attacker is retrained against the integrated defense network—AADN still maintains positive gains in most settings, though the margin narrows (e.g., CSA on OTB100 drops from +72.5% to +24.4%). A notable exception is IoU Attack, where adaptive results actually exceed non-adaptive ones (e.g., VOT2018 EAO: 0.334 adaptive vs. 0.265 non-adaptive) because the iterative attack mechanism reuses prior-frame perturbations when the defense restores tracking. For transferability, AADN trained on SiamRPN++ is applied without retraining to SiamRPN, SiamMask, and OCEAN, achieving 3.1–5.5% Success improvements under IoU Attack, confirming plug-and-play compatibility across siamese and anchor-free architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to address adversarial defense specifically for visual object tracking, a gap the authors explicitly identify given that prior defenses target image classification (distillation, input transformations) and prior tracking work focuses exclusively on attack generation (SPARK, CSA, DFA, IoU Attack). Methodologically, it repurposes the Fast-AT/Free-AT adversarial training paradigm but redirects it toward training a lightweight U-Net pre-processing module rather than retraining the tracker itself, and introduces a Dua-Loss (combining cross-entropy classification and Smooth-L1 regression) to generate adversarial samples that simultaneously stress both the classification and regression heads of siamese trackers. The plug-and-play deployment philosophy departs from the standard adversarial-training recipe of modifying the target model's parameters, instead treating defense as an independent, swappable front-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By filling the absence of tracking-specific adversarial defense, this work provides a practical, low-overhead (≈5 ms/frame) tool that deployers can attach to existing trackers without retraining, which is significant for real-time applications where retraining is infeasible. However, notable limitations remain: clean-sample accuracy drops of 8–14% on the search branch, degraded defense under adaptive attacks for CSA/DFA, and evaluation limited to a small set of siamese and one anchor-free tracker. Open questions include whether the U-Net formulation and Dua-Loss generalize to transformer-based or foundation-model trackers, whether the defense degrades over long sequences with compounding perturbations, and whether the clean-sample cost can be eliminated via conditional or gated defense.
