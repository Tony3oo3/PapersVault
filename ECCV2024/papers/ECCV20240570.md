## Problem

Existing Transformer-based adverse weather removal methods restrict self-attention to fixed spatial windows or the channel dimension to manage computational cost, which prevents them from capturing the long-range spatial dependencies that weather-induced degradation (rain streaks, fog, snow) naturally exhibits across distant pixels. This compromise limits restoration quality, particularly for large-scale or spatially distributed degradation patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240570/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single weather-degraded RGB image (3×H×W) and outputs a restored clean image of the same spatial dimensions. It is trained as a unified all-in-one model on 128×128 patches drawn from three datasets (Snow100K, Outdoor-Rain, RainDrop) to simultaneously handle desnowing, deraining, dehazing, and raindrop removal. The architecture uses a U-Net-style encoder-decoder with pixel-shuffle/unshuffle for multi-scale processing and skip connections between corresponding stages.

## Method

Histoformer introduces a Dynamic-range Histogram Self-Attention (DHSA) module that sorts spatial features into intensity-based bins and applies self-attention along two complementary reshaping strategies: bin-wise (BHR) for global cross-bin feature integration and frequency-wise (FHR) for fine-grained within-bin processing, with the two branches combined via element-wise multiplication. A dynamic-range convolution pre-sorts pixels horizontally and vertically before separable convolution, enabling kernels to operate on similar-intensity pixels rather than fixed spatial neighbors. A Dual-scale Gated Feed-Forward (DGFF) module uses parallel 5×5 and dilated 3×3 depthwise convolutions with a Mish-activated gating branch to capture multi-range, multi-scale information. Training employs a Pearson correlation loss alongside L1 reconstruction loss to enforce that restored pixels preserve the same intensity ordering as ground truth, addressing the limitation of pixel-wise losses in capturing patch-level linear structure.

## Results

Histoformer achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all three weather removal tasks: 37.41 dB on Snow100K-S (desnowing), 32.08 dB on Outdoor-Rain (deraining & dehazing), and 33.06 dB on RainDrop (raindrop removal). Among unified all-in-one models, it outperforms the previous best (AWRCP) by 0.49 dB on Snow100K-S, 0.69 dB on Outdoor-Rain, and 0.68 dB on RainDrop. It also surpasses task-specific baselines such as Restormer by 1.40 dB on Snow100K-S, 2.05 dB on Outdoor-Rain, and 0.88 dB on RainDrop. The only metric where a unified competitor edges ahead is SSIM on Snow100K-L, where AWRCP scores 0.9341 versus Histoformer's 0.9261.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Histoformer outperforms all unified all-in-one baselines on PSNR across every task: it leads AWRCP [82] by 0.49 dB on Snow100K-S, 0.69 dB on Outdoor-Rain, and 1.13 dB on RainDrop, and surpasses WGWSNet [100] by 2.80 dB on Snow100K-S and 0.68 dB on RainDrop. Against the strongest task-specific baseline Restormer [86], Histoformer gains 1.40 dB (Snow100K-S), 2.05 dB (Outdoor-Rain), and 0.88 dB (RainDrop) in PSNR. The sole exception is SSIM on Snow100K-L, where AWRCP (0.9341) slightly exceeds Histoformer (0.9261), though Histoformer still leads on PSNR (32.16 vs. 31.92). Compared to the diffusion-based WeatherDiff64 [53], Histoformer improves by 1.58 dB on Snow100K-S and 2.44 dB on Outdoor-Rain, indicating that the histogram self-attention mechanism captures long-range spatial degradation patterns more effectively than patch-based denoising diffusion. Overall, Histoformer sets new SOTA on 7 out of 8 PSNR/SSIM metric-dataset combinations, with the single SSIM loss on Snow100K-L being the only margin where a competitor prevails.

## Relation to Prior Work

Histoformer departs from the dominant paradigm in Transformer-based image restoration (Restormer, Swin-IR, TransWeather) where self-attention is confined to fixed spatial windows or the channel dimension to manage computational cost. Instead, it repurposes the histogram concept from signal processing to create a dynamic-range spatial attention: pixels are sorted by intensity into bins, and attention is computed within and across these bins, effectively giving the model an adaptive, content-dependent receptive field. The dynamic-range convolution (sorting pixels before applying separable conv) and the Pearson correlation loss are additional departures from standard building blocks, though the overall U-shaped encoder-decoder architecture and progressive training pipeline follow Restormer's design closely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified all-in-one weather removal setting (snow, rain, fog, raindrops in one model) is practically important for real-world deployment where the specific degradation type is unknown. Histoformer's histogram self-attention offers a principled way to achieve long-range spatial modeling without the quadratic cost of full self-attention, which could inspire similar intensity-based grouping strategies in other restoration or segmentation tasks. However, the approach's reliance on paired training data and its specific motivation around weather-induced intensity patterns limit immediate generalizability; whether histogram-based attention transfers to non-weather degradations (e.g., blur, noise, compression artifacts) remains an open question. The sorting operations also introduce non-differentiable steps that may constrain further architectural flexibility.
