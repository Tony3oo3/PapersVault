## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting-based novel view synthesis suffers from two practical limitations: performance degrades sharply when trained on only a few views due to insufficient geometric constraints, and the rendered resolution is capped by the training image resolution. Existing remedies (monocular depth, COLMAP, external SR models) are either too sparse, inconsistent across views, or require inconvenient additional data collection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240067/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M pairs of wide-angle and telephoto images (captured by an asymmetric dual-lens system, e.g., iPhone 12) along with their camera poses as input. Wide-angle images are pre-upsampled via a SISR network (HAT) to match telephoto resolution before 3D-GS optimization. The output is a high-resolution novel view at an arbitrary camera pose, exceeding the original wide-angle resolution by a 2× factor in experiments. The refinement module operates in a self-training regime at inference, requiring no ground-truth data.

## Method

DL-GS consists of two components built on 3D Gaussian Splatting. First, a consistency-aware training strategy regularizes 3D-GS optimization for few-shot settings: it pre-upsamples wide-angle images to resolve focal-length asymmetry, then applies a dual-lens-consistent loss that warps the rendered wide-angle view toward the corresponding telephoto image using RAFT optical flow (serving as a coarse disparity without stereo calibration), plus a depth-wise loss using Pearson correlation to supervise the non-overlapped FoV region. Second, a multi-reference-guided refinement module selects the two nearest telephoto and wide-angle training images by camera pose distance, then transfers HR detail via similarity-aware patch-level attention from telephoto images and multi-view information via deformable convolution with pixel-wise attention from wide-angle images, fusing all pseudo-references with the synthesized view through lightweight convolutions. The entire refinement is trained with a self-supervised loss combining aligned telephoto L2, pre-upsampled wide-angle L2, and contextual loss, making it deployable without ground-truth data.

## Results

On the simulated dual-lens dataset with 2× super-resolution, DL-GS achieves PSNR 19.67 / SSIM 0.5772 / LPIPS 0.3877 in the 10-shot setting, 21.77 / 0.6366 / 0.3366 in 20-shot, and 25.61 / 0.7692 / 0.2076 in 90-shot, outperforming all baselines across every setting. On the real-captured iPhone 12 dataset, DL-GS reaches PSNR 24.05 (5-shot forward-facing) and 31.28 (50-shot forward-facing), and 24.07 (15-shot inward-facing) and 30.72 (50-shot inward-facing), again leading all competitors. Training time is 14 min 35 s and rendering time is 0.728 s per image, comparable to vanilla 3D-GS (9 min 18 s / 0.584 s) and far faster than NeRF-based methods (5–16 h training, 20–46 s rendering).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DL-GS outperforms all nine baselines across every training-sample setting on both simulated and real-captured data. Against the strongest few-shot baselines (FSGS+HAT, SparseNeRF+HAT, DS-NeRF+HAT), DL-GS gains 0.55–0.62 dB PSNR in 10-shot and 0.23–1.09 dB in 20-shot, with the gap widening to 3.1–3.3 dB in 90-shot where NeRF-based few-shot methods degrade severely. Against the dense-training baselines (3D-GS+HAT, 3D-GS+DCSR, NeRF-SR), DL-GS leads by 1.0–1.8 dB PSNR in 10-shot and 0.7–1.0 dB in 90-shot, confirming that the dual-lens geometric regularization and multi-reference refinement are effective even when more views are available. The dual-lens SR baseline (3D-GS+DCSR) is the closest competitor in dense settings (24.60 vs 25.61 PSNR at 90-shot) but still trails DL-GS by over 1 dB, indicating that the consistency-aware training strategy contributes beyond what a post-hoc SR module can provide. On the real iPhone 12 dataset, DL-GS achieves the best PSNR in all four configurations (5/50-shot forward-facing, 15/50-shot inward-facing), with margins of 0.97–1.97 dB over the next-best method (FSGS+HAT or NeRF-SR). NeRF-SR, which directly synthesizes HR views, collapses in the few-shot regime (12.53 PSNR at 5-shot forward-facing) while DL-GS maintains 24.05, highlighting the importance of geometric regularization under extreme sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the rendering backbone and addresses two well-studied limitations of NVS—few-shot training and resolution ceiling—by repurposing the asymmetric dual-lens hardware (wide-angle + telephoto) ubiquitous on smartphones. For few-shot regularization, it departs from prior depth-based approaches (DS-NeRF, SparseNeRF, FSGS) that rely on COLMAP or monocular depth estimators, instead exploiting the inherent stereo geometry of the dual-lens pair via a dual-lens-consistent loss that uses optical flow as a calibration-free proxy for disparity. For resolution improvement, it moves beyond post-hoc SR (HAT, SwinIR) or external HR references (NeRF-SR, RefSR-NeRF) by treating telephoto images as in-domain HR guidance, combined with a multi-reference refinement module using similarity-aware and pixel-wise attention. The authors explicitly position this as the first work to leverage the asymmetric dual-lens system for NVS, carving a new application niche at the intersection of dual-camera super-resolution and 3D-GS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in exploiting hardware already present on billions of smartphones, eliminating the need for additional capture rigs or external SR models to achieve both few-shot robustness and super-resolution in a single pipeline. The self-training formulation of the refinement module (no ground-truth required at inference) makes the approach deployable in real mobile AR/VR scenarios. However, the method is constrained to static scenes captured with a specific dual-lens configuration, and the pre-upsampling step introduces a dependency on SISR quality that could propagate artifacts. Open questions include generalization to dynamic scenes, more complex indoor environments, and whether the dual-lens geometric prior can be extended to other multi-sensor configurations (e.g., LiDAR + RGB) or to focus/depth-of-field control as the authors briefly suggest.
