## Problem

Previous 3D human pose estimation methods rely on fixed-length sliding windows that treat past and future frames symmetrically, so achieving high accuracy requires large chunk sizes that impose significant inference latency (e.g., 12+ seconds at 10 Hz for a 243-frame window). No prior method decouples the need for long-term temporal context from the latency cost of waiting for future frames, leaving a gap between accuracy and real-time deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240868/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a stream of 2D keypoint sequences (17 joints per frame, detected by CPN or ground truth) and produces 3D joint positions for every frame in the current chunk via a seq2seq formulation. During inference, the stream is processed in non-overlapping chunks of size T_s (e.g., 9, 27, or 81 frames), with the model maintaining an explicit cross-chunk recurrent state across chunks. The chunk size at test time can be set independently of the (larger) training chunk size, which is the key mechanism for reducing latency without retraining.

## Method

The paper introduces NC-RetNet, which replaces RetNet's lower-triangular causal mask within a chunk with a full (non-causal) mask D_nm = gamma^|n-m|, allowing each frame to attend to both past and future frames within the current chunk while the cross-chunk recurrent state S_i carries long-term history from all previous chunks. This design means the temporal receptive field is unbounded in the past (via the state) but limited to T_s frames in the future, so reducing T_s at inference time only sacrifices a small amount of future context. A knowledge transfer strategy trains the model using the parallel representation with a large chunk size T_l (243 for Human3.6M) and switches to the chunkwise-recurrent representation with a smaller T_s at test time; the xPos relative position embedding and the accumulated cross-chunk state make the model insensitive to the reduced future window. Joint-related decay coefficients gamma_p are assigned per joint to account for the more erratic motion of distal joints, and the overall architecture is built on MixSTE with its temporal encoder replaced by NC-RetNet.

## Results

On Human3.6M with CPN-detected 2D keypoints, the proposed NC-RetNet achieves 40.4 mm average MPJPE at T=243, 40.9 mm at T=81, and 42.1 mm at T=27, outperforming STCFormer (40.5/42.0/44.1 mm) and MixSTE (40.9/42.4/45.1 mm) at every chunk size. On MPI-INF-3DHP, the method achieves 22.2 mm MPJPE at T=81 and 24.1 mm at T=9, surpassing STCFormer by 0.9 mm and 4.1 mm respectively. The method also improves continuity, achieving 2.2 mm/frame MPJVE at T=81, matching the best prior result (StridedFormer) while using a much smaller chunk size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is STCFormer [38], which the proposed method outperforms at every tested chunk size on both datasets: by 0.1 mm at T=243, 1.1 mm at T=81, and 2.0 mm at T=27 on Human3.6M, and by 0.9 mm at T=81 and 4.1 mm at T=9 on MPI-INF-3DHP. MixSTE [46], the base architecture whose temporal encoder is replaced, is beaten by 0.5 mm at T=243, 1.5 mm at T=81, and 3.0 mm at T=27 on Human3.6M. Earlier transformer-based methods (PoseFormer [50], Xue et al. [43], P-STMO [31], MHFormer [18]) are all surpassed by 1–2 mm at comparable chunk sizes. On MPI-INF-3DHP, the method also outperforms CrossFormer [10] (76.3→24.1 mm at T=9), UGCN [39] (68.1→24.1 mm), Hu et al. [12] (42.5→22.2 mm at T=81), Einfalt et al. [8] (46.9→22.2 mm), and Lin et al. [20] (79.8→22.7 mm at T=27). In terms of continuity (MPJVE), the method matches StridedFormer [17] at 2.2 mm/frame while using T=81 instead of T=351, and beats MixSTE [46] (2.3) and PoseFormer [50] (3.1) at the same chunk size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Retentive Networks (RetNet), originally designed for large language models, as the temporal backbone for 2D-to-3D human pose lifting, replacing the transformer encoder in MixSTE. It departs from the standard causal masking of RetNet by introducing a non-causal variant (NC-RetNet) that allows the model to attend to a few future frames within the current chunk while relying on the cross-chunk recurrent state for long-term history. The knowledge transfer strategy—training with a large chunk size and inferring with a much smaller one—draws an analogy to length extrapolation in NLP but inverts the direction (large-to-small rather than short-to-long), and is enabled by the relative position embedding (xPos) and the persistent cross-chunk state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time 3D HPE: reducing the inference chunk from 243 to 27 frames cuts latency by roughly 9× while maintaining accuracy comparable to prior SOTA at 81 frames, and the FLOPs (430M) are ~180× lower than STCFormer's. The ablation showing that removing the cross-chunk state collapses the knowledge transfer (54.2mm vs. 42.1mm at T=27) cleanly isolates the mechanism. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge the fundamental theory behind why the cross-chunk state makes the model insensitive to chunk size is unclear, and the method has only been validated on the 2D-to-3D lifting task, leaving its applicability to end-to-end or other sequential CV tasks unexplored.
