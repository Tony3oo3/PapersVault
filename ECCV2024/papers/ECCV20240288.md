## Problem

In Generalized Continual Category Discovery, standard feature distillation prevents catastrophic forgetting but imposes rigid regularization that limits the model's plasticity to learn new categories. Simply relaxing this constraint increases forgetting, creating a fundamental tension. The paper shows that projected distillation (which allows more drift) and centroid adaptation (which recovers old category positions) each offer only modest gains individually, but their combination is essential for simultaneously achieving high plasticity and low forgetting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a sequential stream of partially labeled tasks, where each task contains known classes (with a 1:1 labeled-to-unlabeled ratio) and novel classes (fully unlabeled), with a 4:1 known-to-novel class ratio. A ViT-small backbone (pretrained with DINO, first 11 blocks frozen) extracts features, and classification is performed at inference via a Nearest Centroid Classifier using stored and adapted centroids, requiring no task ID. The method operates in an exemplar-free setting by default, though optional buffers of 5 or 20 exemplars per class can be incorporated.

## Method

CAMP trains in three phases per task: (1) the feature extractor is trained with a combined self-supervised (SimCLR + pseudo-label CE) and supervised (SupCon + CE) loss, regularized by knowledge distillation through a learnable 2-layer MLP projector that maps current-task features back to the previous task's latent space rather than directly matching features; (2) semi-supervised k-means identifies centroids for known and novel classes in the new latent space; (3) an auxiliary linear adapter network is trained to predict the drift of old centroids from the previous latent space to the current one, updating stored centroids for classification. The critical insight is that the projector makes the latent-space drift more structured and predictable, enabling the centroid adapter to accurately recover old category positions—neither component achieves strong results without the other, as direct feature distillation produces drift that is harder to model.

## Results

CAMP achieves state-of-the-art task-agnostic accuracy across most GCCD scenarios. Without exemplars, CAMP reaches 52.1% All accuracy on CIFAR-100, 58.9% on CUB-200, and 48.8% on Stanford Cars, outperforming the second-best method (GCD+EWC) by 8.6–15.5 points. With 20 exemplars, CAMP further improves to 58.0% (CIFAR-100) and 66.1% (CUB-200). In the Class Incremental Learning setting, CAMP attains 65.0% average incremental accuracy on CIFAR-100 (N=5) and 73.1% on ImageNet-Subset (N=5), surpassing the best exemplar-free baseline FeTrIL by 6.5 and 10.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAMP (0 exemplars) dominates on CIFAR-100, CUB-200, and Stanford Cars, beating the strongest exemplar-free baselines GCD+EWC and GCD+FD by 8.6–15.5 points in All accuracy (e.g., 58.9% vs. 50.3% on CUB-200). Against SimGCD [38], CAMP gains 20.9 points on CIFAR-100 (52.1% vs. 31.2%), and against MetaGCD [39] it gains 16.1 points (52.1% vs. 35.8%). The one notable exception is DomainNet, where GCD+FD achieves 10.0 points higher novel-class accuracy (39.7% vs. 29.7%), though CAMP still leads in overall accuracy (36.7% vs. 32.7%). With 20 exemplars, CAMP outperforms IGCD [45] by 6.2 points on CIFAR-100 (58.0% vs. 51.8%) and by 2.1 points on CUB-200 (66.1% vs. 64.0%), while PA [20] and MetaGCD [39] trail by 14–20 points. In the CIL setting (Table 2), CAMP surpasses FeTrIL [29] by 6.5 points on CIFAR-100 (N=5: 65.0% vs. 58.5%) and by 10.2 points on ImageNet-Subset (N=5: 73.1% vs. 62.9%), with consistent margins across all task splits and datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAMP builds on the Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) framework and its continual extension (GCCD), inheriting the semi-supervised k-means clustering and Nearest Centroid Classifier from Vaze et al. and SimGCD. It departs from standard feature distillation (LwF, PODNet) by introducing a learnable MLP projector for distillation, following the projected functional regularization line of work (Gomez-Villa et al.), and from prior centroid/feature adaptation methods (SDC, Iscen et al.) by showing they are far more effective when the drift is induced by projected rather than direct feature distillation. The key conceptual departure is the demonstrated synergy: projected distillation and centroid adaptation are individually modest but complementary, addressing opposing pressures (plasticity vs. stability) that neither can resolve alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAMP provides a practical, exemplar-free recipe for GCCD and CIL that achieves large SOTA margins across five datasets, and the synergy finding offers a reusable design principle: relax distillation rigidity with a projector, then recover old positions with a lightweight adapter. Limitations include the requirement for centroid-based clustering (incompatible with parametric classifiers like SimGCD or IGCD) and a persistent gap between known and novel class accuracy. Open questions include whether the projector-adapter pairing generalizes to non-centroid classifiers, whether deeper or task-specific projectors further improve drift predictability, and whether the approach extends to longer task sequences or more extreme domain shifts.
