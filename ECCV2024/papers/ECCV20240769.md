## Problem

Existing multimodal self-supervised learning methods align all modalities into a single common latent space, suppressing modality-unique information that cannot be extracted from other sensors. This forces potentially orthogonal representations into shared embeddings, limiting the model's ability to capture detailed, modality-specific features. The paper asks whether explicitly decoupling common and unique representations can improve both cross-modal and single-modal downstream performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240769/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired images from two modalities at resolutions of 264×264 (SAR-optical), 250×250 (RGB-DEM), or variable sizes (RGB-depth), each passed through modality-specific encoders and 3-layer MLP projectors producing 8192-dimensional embeddings. These embeddings are partitioned into common and unique subspaces (e.g., 87.5% common for SAR-optical, 75% for RGB-DEM/depth), and the resulting decoupled representations are transferred to downstream heads for scene classification (19-class multi-label) or semantic segmentation (10- or 37-class), supporting both multimodal and single-modal (modality-missing) inference.

## Method

DeCUR extends Barlow Twins' redundancy-reduction framework by splitting the embedding dimension K into Kc common and Ku unique parts. For cross-modal pairs, the correlation matrix over common dimensions is driven toward the identity (aligning shared information) while the correlation matrix over unique dimensions is driven toward zero (decorrelating modality-specific information). To prevent the unique dimensions from collapsing into meaningless random values, an intra-modal loss is added that drives the full-dimension correlation matrix between two augmented views of the same modality toward the identity, ensuring unique dimensions learn meaningful within-modality structure. The total loss is the sum of cross-modal common, cross-modal unique, and two intra-modal terms. Additionally, a deformable attention module with residual connection is inserted into the last two stages of ResNet-50 backbones to encourage the network to focus on modality-informative spatial regions during pretraining.

## Results

On SAR-optical scene classification (BigEarthNet-MM), DeCUR achieves 79.8/81.5 mAP (linear probing/fine-tuning) with 1% labels and 86.2/89.8 mAP with 100% labels, outperforming Barlow Twins by 1.1–3.0% mAP. On RGB-DEM semantic segmentation (GeoNRW), DeCUR reaches 34.7/36.6 mIoU (frozen/fine-tune) with 1% labels, surpassing SimCLR-cross by up to 11.7% mIoU. On RGB-depth segmentation, transferring DeCUR backbones into the CMX fusion model improves mIoU from 49.7% to 50.6% (SUN-RGBD) and from 56.9% to 57.3% (NYU-Depth v2) without tuning any hyperparameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCUR consistently outperforms contrastive baselines (SimCLR-cross, CLIP) by 2.4–3.4% mAP in linear probing on SAR-optical multimodal classification, and by up to 11.7% mIoU on RGB-DEM segmentation. Against the closest redundancy-reduction baselines, DeCUR beats Barlow Twins by 1.1–3.0% mAP (SAR-optical) and 3.0–3.7% mIoU (RGB-DEM), and surpasses VICReg by 4–5% mAP. In the modality-missing setting, DeCUR also outperforms single-modal Barlow Twins pretraining (BT-SAR) by 2.7–3.2% mAP with 1% labels, confirming that joint multimodal pretraining benefits individual modalities. On optical-only BigEarthNet-S2 (10% labels), DeCUR (83.3/87.2 mAP with ResNet-50) trails the larger CROMA (85/88.3, ViT-L) and SkySense (88.7, ViT-L*) but exceeds GFM and SatMAE. For RGB-depth segmentation, DeCUR-pretrained backbones improve the CMX fusion model by 0.9–0.4% mIoU over its original initialization, and surpass SA-Gate (49.4), SGNet (48.6), and ShapeConv (48.6) on SUN-RGBD when combined with CMX-B2 (50.6 mIoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCUR is explicitly positioned as a multimodal extension of Barlow Twins, replacing the dominant cross-modal contrastive learning paradigm (SimCLR, CLIP, MoCo) with a negative-free redundancy-reduction objective. Its key departure is the explicit decoupling of embedding dimensions into cross-modal common and modality-unique subspaces, a concept borrowed from supervised multimodal fusion (modality separation networks, gradient modulation) but applied here for the first time in a self-supervised, dimension-level loss design. The intra-modal training component addresses a gap the authors identify in prior multimodal SSL: without it, unique dimensions collapse into meaningless decorrelated noise. Deformable attention from DAT/DAT++ is repurposed as a lightweight modality-informative module in ConvNet backbones, adding a spatial focus mechanism not present in the original Barlow Twins formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple loss modification—splitting embedding dimensions and applying different correlation targets—yields consistent gains across three architecturally and sensor-diverse scenarios (SAR-optical, RGB-DEM, RGB-depth), suggesting the common/unique decomposition is a broadly applicable principle rather than a domain-specific trick. The finding that multimodal pretraining improves single-modal (modality-missing) performance by 2–4% over single-modal pretraining has practical value for real-world deployment where one sensor is unavailable. Open questions include whether the fixed common-unique ratio can be made adaptive per scene or per modality, how the approach scales beyond two modalities, and whether the grid-search cost for the optimal ratio is prohibitive at foundation-model scale.
