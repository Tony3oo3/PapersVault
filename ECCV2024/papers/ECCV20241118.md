## Problem

Existing feature matching methods focus on improving modeling capacity but fail to address three fundamental constraints on the number of ground-truth matches: scarcity of matchable keypoints in small-scale source images, matching conflicts caused by one-to-one assignment under large scale variations, and heavy reliance on keypoint repeatability in sparse methods. These issues cap the theoretical upper bound ('ceiling') on achievable matches regardless of how well the matcher is trained.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (resized and padded to 832×832 during training, tested at 1216 longer dimension) and produces sub-pixel feature correspondences. A key asymmetry is that 1024 keypoints are detected in the source image while the target image uses dense grid features at 1/8 or 1/16 resolution, and a learned view switcher dynamically assigns which image serves as the sparse source to maximize matchable points in the overlapping region.

## Method

RCM integrates a semi-sparse paradigm with a coarse-to-fine architecture in four modules: a U-Net extracts multi-scale coarse and fine features; a lightweight CNN-based view switcher performs binary classification on the coarse correlation map to swap source and target images so the larger-scale image always provides sparse keypoints; a conflict-free coarse matching module replaces the standard Dual-Softmax (one-to-one) with a single row-wise Softmax over a score matrix augmented with a learnable dustbin, enabling many-to-one assignment where multiple sparse features can independently match the same dense feature; and a fine matching module refines each coarse match to sub-pixel precision via cross-attention and correlation-based expectation. The many-to-one strategy is the core mechanism that eliminates matching conflicts in large-scale-variation scenes, while the view switcher directly increases the number of matchable source points, together raising ground-truth matches by 260% over one-to-one baselines.

## Results

On MegaDepth two-view pose estimation, RCM achieves AUC@5°/10°/20° of 53.2/69.4/81.5, outperforming LoFTR (52.8/69.2/81.2) and significantly exceeding SuperGlue (42.2/59.0/73.6). On HPatches homography estimation, RCM attains 56%/88%/93% accuracy at 1/3/5 px thresholds, leading LoFTR and MatchFormer by 9–12% in viewpoint-change scenarios. On the extreme-scale MegaDepth subset (overlap ratio ≥4×), RCM produces 887 matches at 78.9% precision, yielding 2.8× more matches than LightGlue (313) and 2.1× more than ASpanFormer (425). RCM_Lite achieves 49.7/66.5/79.3 AUC on MegaDepth while running in 134 ms, outperforming SuperGlue by +7.5% at 5° with a 2× speed advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCM outperforms LoFTR on MegaDepth pose estimation (53.2 vs 52.8 AUC@5°) and on HPatches viewpoint-change homography (80% vs 69% at 3 px), attributing gains to the view switcher and many-to-one matching. RCM surpasses SuperGlue by +11.0 AUC@5° on MegaDepth and +5% overall on HPatches, while RCM_Lite beats SuperGlue by +7.5% at 5° with a 2× runtime advantage (134 ms vs 72 ms). On ScanNet indoor scenes, RCM (17.3/34.6/52.1) edges out LoFTR-OT (16.9/33.6/50.6) and SuperGlue (16.2/33.8/51.8) in zero-shot transfer. RCM trails RoMa (63.0 AUC@5°) on MegaDepth but uses only 8.8% of RoMa's parameters (9.8M vs 111M) and runs 4.4× faster (208 ms vs 913 ms). On the extreme-scale subset, RCM achieves 2.8× more matches than LightGlue and 2.1× more than ASpanFormer, while removing the view switcher drops matches from 887 to 653 and flipping the switch collapses performance to 76 matches.

## Relation to Prior Work

RCM inherits its detection backbone from SuperPoint (frozen encoder and detection head) and its coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline from LoFTR, while adopting the semi-sparse matching paradigm originally proposed by S2DHM and S2DNet. The key departure is twofold: (1) a lightweight binary view switcher that dynamically reassigns which image serves as the sparse source versus the dense target based on scale, and (2) replacing the Dual-Softmax one-to-one assignment with a single row-wise Softmax (many-to-one matching with a dustbin), which removes the column-sum constraint that caused matching conflicts under large scale variation. Prior semi-sparse methods (S2DNet) used full-resolution dense features in the target image, incurring prohibitive memory costs; RCM integrates the semi-sparse paradigm with LoFTR's coarse-to-fine architecture to retain efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'raising the ceiling' framing shifts the optimization target from matcher accuracy to the theoretical upper bound of ground-truth matches, yielding a 260% increase in available correspondences and a 2.2× speedup over LoFTR with comparable or better accuracy. The many-to-one matching and view switcher are simple, low-parameter additions that practitioners can potentially adopt in other matchers. However, the method still assumes at least one image is at a larger scale (both-small-scale remains unsolved), lacks discrete tracks needed for SfM pipelines, and retains dependence on keypoint detection quality in the source image, limiting performance in texture-less scenes. Open questions include whether the semi-sparse paradigm can be combined with learned detection to further reduce the source-image bottleneck, and whether the many-to-one formulation generalizes to multi-view or video matching settings.
