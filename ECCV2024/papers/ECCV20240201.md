## Problem

The mapping between human motion and action semantics is inherently many-to-many: a single semantic label (e.g., 'walk forwards') corresponds to perceptually diverse motions, and a single motion can carry different meanings depending on context. Direct-mapping generative models struggle with this gap, producing unreliable outputs, while existing evaluation metrics (FID, R-Precision) rely on black-box pretrained latent spaces and fail to reliably assess motion-semantic consistency, as evidenced by higher-than-GT R-Precision scores and contradictions with human judgments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D skeleton sequences (17 joints at 30 Hz) or SMPL/SMPL-X parameter sequences, which are converted to a 147-dim per-frame representation (6D rotation + root velocity). Kinematic Phrases are extracted as per-frame categorical vectors of 392 values in {-1, 0, 1}, where each value encodes the sign of a scalar kinematic indicator (e.g., whether a joint moves along or against the forward reference vector). For the KPG benchmark, 7,796 text prompts are generated from KP templates in four categories (atomic, repetitive, sequential, simultaneous), and evaluation is performed by checking whether specific KP patterns appear in the extracted phrases of the generated motion—requiring no black-box model.

## Method

The core contribution is Kinematic Phrases (KP), an intermediate representation of 392 qualitative categorical phrases across six types (Position, Pairwise Relative Position, Pairwise Distance, Limb Angle, Limb Orientation, Global Velocity) spanning four kinematic hierarchies. Each phrase is defined by the sign of a scalar indicator computed from egocentric reference vectors (up, right, forward), deliberately avoiding numerical thresholds to minimize subjective bias. A motion-KP joint latent space is learned via two transformer-based VAEs (Motion VAE and KP VAE) aligned in the style of TEMOS, with random corruption of ~20% of KP entries during training to mine inter-phrase correlations and improve robustness. For text-to-motion generation, the Motion VAE latent space replaces MLD's latent space within a latent diffusion framework. The KPG benchmark converts KP into templated text prompts and evaluates generation accuracy by verifying the presence of corresponding KP patterns (≥5 consecutive frames) in the generated motion, enabling fully white-box, automatic semantic-consistency assessment.

## Results

On the conventional HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, the proposed method (replacing MLD's latent space with the motion-KP joint space) achieves R-P@1=0.496, FID=0.275, Diversity=9.975, and Multimodality=2.218, competitive with T2M-GPT (R-P@1=0.492, FID=0.141) and MLD (R-P@1=0.481, FID=0.473). On the novel KPG benchmark, the method achieves 57.86% overall accuracy, outperforming the best baseline T2M-GPT (55.59%) and MoMask (47.79%), with particularly strong performance on atomic prompts (98.80%) and sequential prompts (71.32%). For motion interpolation, the method achieves the best FID of 0.192 and Diversity of 9.623. Notably, the paper demonstrates that conventional R-Precision is unreliable, with several methods showing higher-than-GT R-P@1 values, motivating the KPG white-box evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On HumanML3D, the method is competitive but not clearly superior: R-P@1=0.496 is within 0.03 of most top methods (T2M-GPT 0.492, MLD 0.481, M2DM 0.497), and FID=0.275 trails T2M-GPT (0.141) and MDD (0.072). The paper itself acknowledges that metric differences are marginal and that R-Precision is unreliable (several methods exceed GT R-P@1=0.511). On the novel KPG benchmark, the method clearly wins: 57.86% overall accuracy vs. T2M-GPT (55.59%), MoMask (47.79%), and MotionGPT (44.96%), with the largest gaps on sequential prompts (71.32% vs. 70.24% for T2M-GPT) and simultaneous prompts (43.08% vs. 42.47%). T2M-GPT dominates repetitive prompts (1.02% vs. 26.02% for Ours—actually Ours wins here too, T2M-GPT nearly fails at 1.02%). ReMoDiffuse performs poorly on KPG (30.56% overall), attributed to its retrieval-based design being incompatible with novel prompts. The ablation confirms KP's contribution: removing KP drops KPG accuracy from 57.86% to 39.94%, and removing joint KP or joint-pair KP causes the largest individual degradations (to 50.03% and 47.24% respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant direct-mapping paradigm in text-to-motion generation (text → latent → motion) by introducing Kinematic Phrases (KP) as an interpretable intermediate representation grounded in objective kinematic facts. It draws conceptual inspiration from qualitative pose representations (Posebits, PoseScript) and kinematic notation systems (Labanotation), but extends them to temporal, multi-hierarchy motion descriptions with 392 categorical phrases. Architecturally, it builds directly on MLD by replacing its motion latent space with a joint motion-KP VAE, and adapts the VAE alignment strategy from TEMOS to couple the two modalities in a shared latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical weakness in current text-to-motion evaluation: R-Precision can exceed ground-truth values and contradict human judgments, undermining confidence in widely-used metrics. KPG offers a fully white-box, automatically computable alternative that isolates specific kinematic capabilities (atomic, repetitive, sequential, simultaneous), revealing that even SOTA models achieve under 60% accuracy on compositional prompts. Limitations include KP's inability to capture amplitude or speed, the 17-joint skeleton excluding fine-grained finger motion, and the modest overall accuracy gains. Open questions include extending KP to 2D/egocentric modalities, scaling KPG with paraphrased prompts, and integrating the joint latent space with more advanced generative architectures.
