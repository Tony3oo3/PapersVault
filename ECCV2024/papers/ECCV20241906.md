## Problem

Existing inverse rendering methods model geometry, materials, and illumination separately using deep implicit neural networks, requiring hours to days of per-scene training. A critical bottleneck is illumination modeling via environment maps, which demands expensive multi-bounce ray tracing to simulate light visibility and indirect lighting. The paper asks whether all scene properties can be learned jointly through a single explicit representation to dramatically reduce optimization time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241906/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 100–200 multi-view RGB images per scene (MII synthetic) or 5 real-world scenes (NeRD, some under varying illumination) and produces a voxelized SDF field for geometry, spatially-varying albedo and roughness for materials, and a per-position incident light field parameterized by 16 Spherical Gaussian lobes. For varying-illumination scenes, a learnable view embedding (dimension 6) is appended to the SG prediction network. The voxel grid operates at 96³ (coarse) and 160³ (fine) resolution, with optional multi-resolution hash encoding for memory reduction.

## Method

UniVoxel encodes a scene into two learnable volumetric embeddings: a scalar SDF field for geometry and a 6-channel semantic field for materials and illumination, both queried via trilinear interpolation. Lightweight 3-layer MLPs (192 channels) decode albedo, roughness, and Spherical Gaussian parameters from the interpolated semantic features, while surface normals and opacity are derived directly from the SDF gradient. The key insight is representing local incident light radiance as a sum of 16 Spherical Gaussian lobes whose parameters are predicted by the same lightweight MLP from the semantic voxelization, thereby modeling direct lighting, indirect illumination, and visibility jointly without any ray tracing. Training follows a coarse-to-fine schedule (10k iterations per stage) with a composite loss combining PBR reconstruction, a stabilizing radiance-field branch, smoothness constraints on albedo/roughness/normals, and illumination regularizations (SG-parameter smoothing and a white-light color-shift penalty).

## Results

On the MII synthetic dataset (4 scenes, 200 novel validation views), UniVoxel achieves the best NVS PSNR of 36.23 dB and the lowest Roughness MSE of 0.007, while completing per-scene training in just 18 minutes on a single RTX 3090. This represents a 40× speedup over MII (14 hours) and a 13× speedup over Nvdiffrec-mc (4 hours), with only a marginal gap to TensoIR (3 hours) in Albedo PSNR (29.93 vs. 30.58) and Relighting PSNR (29.45 vs. 29.69). The hash-encoding variant (UniVoxel-Hash) trades a small NVS PSNR drop (35.87) for reduced memory while still finishing in 26 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniVoxel outperforms all four baselines on NVS PSNR (36.23 vs. 35.80 for TensoIR, 34.29 for Nvdiffrec-mc, 30.73 for MII, 22.80 for NeRFactor) and achieves the best Roughness MSE (0.007). Against the closest competitor TensoIR, UniVoxel wins on NVS PSNR (+0.43 dB), Albedo SSIM (0.957 vs. 0.946), Relighting SSIM (0.960 vs. 0.951), and Roughness MSE (0.007 vs. 0.015), but trails slightly on Albedo PSNR (29.93 vs. 30.58) and Relighting PSNR (29.45 vs. 29.69). Compared to Nvdiffrec-mc, UniVoxel gains +1.94 dB NVS PSNR and +5.23 dB Relighting PSNR while training 13× faster. Against MII, the improvement is +5.5 dB NVS PSNR and +0.8 dB Relighting PSNR with a 47× speedup. NeRFactor is substantially outperformed across all metrics, confirming that explicit voxelization with unified illumination modeling is a decisive advance over fully implicit multi-stage pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniVoxel extends the explicit-representation line of work initiated by DVGO and TensoIR, but departs from both by unifying geometry, materials, and illumination into a single voxelization framework rather than treating illumination separately via environment maps. It builds on NeuS's SDF-to-opacity formulation for geometry and DVGO's coarse-to-fine voxel optimization paradigm, while introducing local Spherical Gaussians as a per-position incident light representation that replaces the expensive multi-bounce ray tracing required by environment-map-based methods (NeRFactor, MII, TensoIR). The key conceptual departure from TensoIR—the closest prior explicit method—is that illumination is no longer a global environment map but a spatially-varying local field decoded from the same semantic voxel grid as materials, enabling all scene properties to be learned through lightweight MLPs on shared voxel features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing per-scene training time from hours or days to 18 minutes, UniVoxel makes inverse rendering practically viable for applications that previously required prohibitive compute budgets, such as content creation pipelines or large-scale scene capture. The local Spherical Gaussian illumination model is a clean design choice that eliminates multi-bounce ray tracing while jointly capturing direct lighting, indirect illumination, and visibility—addressing a core bottleneck in the field. Limitations include slightly lower relighting quality than TensoIR on some metrics, dependence on multi-view input, and evaluation limited to relatively small synthetic and real-world scenes; open questions include scalability to large or dynamic scenes and whether the approach extends to more complex material models beyond Disney BRDF.
