## Problem

Pre-training video transformers via masked video modeling requires large volumes of real video data, incurring high collection costs and raising privacy, licensing, and bias concerns. Existing synthetic-data approaches either still depend on real videos for pre-training or are restricted to CNN architectures and fail to generalize to ViTs. This paper asks whether video transformers can be effectively pre-trained using only static images—potentially synthetic ones—without any real video data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static image (224×224, from real datasets like ImageNet/PASS or synthetic datasets like Shaders1k/FractalDB/Visual Atom) and recursively applies image transformations to synthesize a 16-frame pseudo-motion video. The output is a pre-trained ViT-Base video transformer (trained with VideoMAE at 75% mask ratio) that is then fine-tuned on standard action recognition benchmarks (UCF101, HMDB51, Diving48, etc.) to produce action class predictions. Notably, the input is always a single image rather than a video, and the temporal dimension is entirely synthesized.

## Method

The core contribution is the Pseudo Motion Generator (PMG), which recursively applies a randomly selected image transformation (from an optimal set of Zoom-in/out and Affine Transformation) with a random intensity parameter to a static image T−1 times, producing a sequence of frames with trackable patch correspondence across time. These pseudo-motion videos are then fed into VideoMAE for self-supervised pre-training, where the model reconstructs 75% masked spatiotemporal patches. Video-level Mixup is applied to each generated video to further diversify motion and appearance patterns. The key insight is that VideoMAE primarily learns low-level patch-correspondence features rather than high-level semantics, so the pseudo-motion videos need clear inter-frame patch tracking—which the recursive geometric transformations provide—rather than realistic motion. The framework is validated to work entirely on synthetic images (Shaders1k being most effective), eliminating all real-data dependencies.

## Results

Using only 100k synthetic images from Shaders1k, the proposed PMG + VideoMAE framework achieves 89.4% top-1 accuracy on UCF101, 59.7% on HMDB51, and 72.3% on Diving48, matching or exceeding methods that require real video data. On the SynAPT benchmark, the Shaders1k variant reaches 89.4/59.7/68.3/72.3/60.7/40.0 across six datasets, partially surpassing PPMA (which uses 300k real+synthetic videos with labels) on MiniSSV2, Diving48, and UAV-Human. On Kinetics400, the framework achieves 74.7% top-1 accuracy from Shaders1k alone, outperforming from-scratch training (68.8%) but trailing VideoMAE with real K400 videos (81.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VideoMAE pre-trained on real fine-tuning data (91.3/62.6/79.3 on UCF101/HMDB51/Diving48), the proposed method with Shaders1k (89.4/59.7/72.3) trails by 1.9–7.0 points, but the gap narrows to 1.9 points on UCF101 and the method wins on Diving48 when using PASS (69.2 vs. 79.3 is still a loss, but the PMG Aug variant with real+pseudo-motion reaches 92.2 on UCF101). Against PPMA (92.5/71.2/64.0), which uses 300k real+synthetic videos with action labels, the proposed method loses on UCF101 (89.4 vs. 92.5) and HMDB51 (59.7 vs. 71.2) but wins on Diving48 (72.3 vs. 64.0), suggesting reduced scene/object bias. Against TimeSformer with SynAPT (89.0/54.4/44.9), the proposed method wins on all three datasets, most dramatically on Diving48 (+27.4 points). Against MoSI, which also uses static images but is CNN-specific, the proposed method achieves 89.4 vs. 82.8 on UCF101 and 59.7 vs. 51.8 on HMDB51; critically, MoSI fails entirely on ViT (48.0/27.3) while the proposed method works natively with ViT-Base. Against VPN-based methods, the proposed method substantially outperforms VPN-only pre-training (64.9/30.3) and is competitive with VPN+Kinetics400 (89.9/61.8) on UCF101/HMDB51 without requiring any real video. Against MoCo v3 (81.7/39.2), the proposed method wins by 7.7 and 20.5 points respectively, demonstrating the advantage of masked video modeling over contrastive learning in this setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-motion-from-static-images idea of MoSI (Huang et al., 2021) to transformer architectures, which MoSI could not support due to insufficient motion diversity. It builds directly on VideoMAE's masked video modeling as the self-supervised objective, but replaces real video inputs with recursively transformed pseudo-motion videos generated by the proposed Pseudo Motion Generator (PMG). The work also departs from prior synthetic-video pre-training efforts (VPN, SynAPT, PPMA) that still required real video data, by demonstrating that entirely synthetic images (e.g., Shaders1k) suffice when combined with PMG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to video pre-training free of collection costs, privacy risks, licensing issues, and dataset biases, which is increasingly important as video models scale. A key empirical insight is that VideoMAE's effectiveness depends on trackable patch correspondence between frames rather than semantic content or realism, explaining why Shaders1k works while FractalDB and Visual Atom do not. However, the method remains inferior to large-scale real-video pre-training (e.g., K400), and the hand-crafted, globally-applied transformations limit motion diversity, leaving open whether learned or more flexible motion generation could close this gap.
