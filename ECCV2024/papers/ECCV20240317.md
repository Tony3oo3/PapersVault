## Problem

Biomedical vision models trained on small, biased datasets often rely on spurious correlations (e.g., hospital markers, co-occurring support devices) rather than true pathology features, leading to substantial performance drops in real-world deployment. Existing diffusion-based editing methods (LANCE, DiffEdit) produce artefacts in medical images—such as inadvertently removing correlated structures or altering anatomical boundaries—making them unsuitable for generating realistic stress-test sets that isolate specific dataset shifts.

## Input / Output

RadEdit takes a 512×512 chest X-ray, a text prompt describing the desired edit (e.g., 'No acute cardiopulmonary process'), an edit mask specifying where changes should occur, and a keep mask specifying regions that must remain unchanged; the two masks need not be mutually exclusive. All operations are performed in the latent space of the frozen SDXL VAE, and the text conditioning uses the BioViL-T text encoder. The output is a minimally modified chest X-ray that preserves anatomical structure outside the edit region, enabling reuse of the same masks for downstream segmentation evaluation.

## Method

RadEdit introduces a dual-mask editing procedure into a latent diffusion model trained on 487K chest X-rays (MIMIC-CXR, ChestX-ray8, CheXpert) with BioViL-T text conditioning. At each denoising step, classifier-free guidance (weight 15) is applied only within the edit mask, while the region outside the edit mask is updated via unconditional generation to maintain global image consistency. After each step, the keep mask region is explicitly reverted to the DDPM-inversion encoding, preventing spurious correlations from propagating edits into correlated structures (e.g., preserving a chest drain when removing a pneumothorax). The process begins from the DDPM-inversion latent x̂_T rather than pure noise, and a BioViL-T-based directional similarity score filters out low-quality edits (threshold 0.2).

## Results

Across three stress-testing scenarios, RadEdit-generated synthetic data exposes severe model failures: a COVID-19 classifier drops from 99.1% to 5.5% accuracy under acquisition shift (Table 1), a pneumothorax classifier drops from 93.3% to 17.9% under manifestation shift (Table 2), and a lung segmentation model trained on mostly healthy patients loses 12.4 Dice points (97.4→85.0) when pacemakers are added (Table 3). In each case, a more robust 'strong predictor' trained on diverse data shows much smaller degradation, confirming the edits faithfully simulate real-world shifts rather than introducing artefacts. For the population shift scenario, RadEdit with DDPM inversion achieves 93.9 Dice / 22.8 AHD in preserving lung borders, substantially outperforming LANCE (80.1 / 69.5) and LANCE with DDIM (78.9 / 65.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RadEdit is compared against two prior editing methods: LANCE [51] and DiffEdit [10]. Against LANCE, RadEdit with DDPM inversion preserves lung border geometry far better (Dice 93.9 vs. 80.1; AHD 22.8 vs. 69.5), and even with DDIM inversion RadEdit (86.2 / 39.8) substantially outperforms LANCE (78.9 / 65.1). LANCE also alters lung position and shape and spuriously removes support devices when prompted to remove pathologies, making it unsuitable for stress-testing segmentation or manifestation-shift scenarios. Against DiffEdit, the comparison is on mask quality: DiffEdit's automatically predicted pneumothorax masks achieve only 18.4 Dice / 256.8 AHD (tuned on validation) or 33.8 / 97.7 (tuned per image), and frequently include the spuriously correlated chest drain in the edit region. RadEdit sidesteps this by using manually annotated edit and keep masks, which precisely isolate the target pathology while forcing correlated structures (e.g., chest drains) to remain unchanged. Overall, RadEdit wins on all quantitative editing-quality metrics and is the only method that successfully enables stress-testing of segmentation models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based image editing (SDEdit, Blended Diffusion, DiffEdit, LANCE) to the biomedical domain, where prior methods fail due to spurious correlations between co-occurring features (e.g., pneumothorax and chest drains). The key methodological departure is the introduction of dual masks (edit + keep) that explicitly decouple these correlations, combined with DDPM inversion for better structural preservation. For stress-testing, the paper builds on the bias-identification work of DeGrave et al. (acquisition shift) and Rueckel et al. (manifestation shift), but is the first to use generative editing to create synthetic stress-test sets for segmentation models, not just classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RadEdit provides a practical, data-free tool for quantifying model robustness to clinically relevant dataset shifts before deployment, complementing qualitative XAI methods like Grad-CAM. However, it cannot handle all shift types (e.g., cardiomegaly requires post-edit segmentation map adjustment), 2D X-ray editing is limited by 3D structural overlap, and the BioViL-T editing score for quality filtering introduces reliance on a potentially biased vision-language model. Open questions include automating failure-mode discovery, extending to 3D CT editing, and developing better edit-quality metrics that don't depend on external models.
