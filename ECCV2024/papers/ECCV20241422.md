## Problem

Existing spatial conditioning for text-to-image diffusion models relies on hand-crafted descriptors that are either semantically ambiguous (edges, depth maps) or require expensive pixel-level annotations (semantic segmentation). No single conditioning input simultaneously captures rich semantic content and detailed scene geometry while remaining cheap to obtain at scale, limiting the ability to generate diverse yet spatially faithful images for downstream perception tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241422/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a text prompt as input. From the reference image, dense features are extracted from a foundation model (primarily Stable Diffusion's U-Net at layers 2, 5, and 8, concatenated and upsampled to a common resolution), then projected via PCA to N components (typically 10–20) to form the neural layout. The output is a full-resolution synthesized image that preserves the semantic classes, object positions, and scene geometry of the reference while allowing diverse appearance controlled by the text prompt.

## Method

LUMEN introduces neural semantic image synthesis by using PCA-projected dense features from pretrained foundation models as conditioning for a ControlNet adapter on a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone. The key insight is that the principal components of dense FM features (e.g., SD U-Net activations) encode separable semantic/spatial structure from nuisance appearance variation; retaining only the top N PCA components yields a 'neural layout' that specifies scene composition without pixel-level labels. Training uses the standard diffusion denoising loss with the neural layout fed through the ControlNet adapter alongside the text embedding. The number of PCA components acts as a tunable knob trading off faithfulness to the reference (mIoU, SI Depth) against sample diversity (LPIPS) and text editability (TIFA). Among four foundation model backbones tested (DINO, DINOv2, CLIP, SD), SD features yield the best overall quality due to their strong spatial layout sensitivity.

## Results

On COCO-Stuff, LUMEN with neural layout conditioning (N=20 PCA components) achieves 52.9 mIoU, 21.1 SI Depth, and 11.8 FID, outperforming all other conditioning types including ground-truth semantic segmentation (43.3 mIoU, 28.8 SI Depth, 15.3 FID). On ADE20K, neural layout reaches 45.7 mIoU and 16.1 FID, again the best across all methods. For downstream multi-task learning on NYUv2, synthetic data from neural layout conditioning improves semantic segmentation mIoU from 44.6 (baseline) to 47.3 and surface normal mean error from 25.8 to 24.9. In domain generalization to ACDC, LUMEN achieves 52.9 average mIoU, surpassing FreestyleNet (52.2) and ControlNet with SemSeg (50.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neural layout conditioning decisively outperforms all label-free baselines: Canny edges (44.4 mIoU), HED edges (49.3 mIoU), and MiDaS depth (45.3 mIoU) on COCO-Stuff, with gaps of 8.5, 3.6, and 7.6 mIoU points respectively. Most strikingly, neural layout also surpasses ground-truth semantic segmentation (43.3 mIoU, 28.8 SI Depth) by 9.6 mIoU and 7.7 SI Depth points, despite requiring no pixel-level annotations at all. HED edges are the closest competitor, achieving 49.3 mIoU and 12.1 FID, but still trail neural layout on both semantic alignment and image quality. In the domain generalization setting (Table 5), LUMEN (52.9 avg mIoU) beats FreestyleNet (52.2), ControlNet+SemSeg (50.3), and PnP-Diffusion (48.7), with the largest gap on the Night condition (35.1 vs 32.9 for FreestyleNet). The trade-off is that neural layout produces less diverse samples (LPIPS 0.36 vs 0.48–0.65) and lower text controllability (TIFA 0.66 vs 0.74–0.88) compared to weaker conditioning signals, since the richer spatial constraints leave less freedom for variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ControlNet's adapter architecture for spatially conditioned diffusion synthesis, but shifts the focus from the synthesis mechanism to the conditioning representation itself. Where prior controllable T2I work (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, Uni-ControlNet, FreestyleNet) relies on hand-crafted descriptors—edges, depth, or semantic segmentation maps—this paper introduces 'neural layouts' extracted via PCA from dense features of foundation models (Stable Diffusion, DINO, DINOv2, CLIP). The idea that foundation model features encode separable semantic/spatial and appearance information draws on recent work on linear probes and semantic correspondence, but the application as a diffusion conditioning signal with a PCA-based semantic separation step is new. The paper is explicitly complementary to existing architectures: it can be plugged into any ControlNet-style framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The label-free nature of neural layout conditioning removes the bottleneck of pixel-level annotation for scaling synthetic data generation, making it directly applicable to data augmentation for perception tasks (segmentation, depth, normals) and domain generalization. The PCA component count provides an unusually clean, interpretable knob to trade off faithfulness, diversity, and text controllability—something absent in edge or segmentation conditioning. Open questions include whether learned task-specific projectors could replace PCA for targeted information removal, and whether the approach extends to 3D or video generation. A limitation is that the method still requires a foundation model backbone whose features must be appropriate for the target domain, and the PCA projection is computed on a fixed training set.
