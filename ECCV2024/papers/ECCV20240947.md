## Problem

Current 3D anomaly detection methods face two practical limitations: memory-bank-based approaches incur prohibitive storage and inference costs by storing all training features and comparing each test sample against the entire bank, while MAE-based reconstruction methods only rebuild masked regions, leaving defects in unmasked portions intact and violating the core assumption of comparing input to a clean reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud (downsampled to 4096 points on Real3D-AD or 2048 on Anomaly-ShapeNet, centered and scaled to [-1,1]) and produces a reconstructed anomaly-free point cloud aligned with the input via a uniform spatial transformation. Anomaly scores and segmentation maps are obtained by direct per-point Euclidean distance between input and reconstruction, eliminating the need for RANSAC registration or feature-space comparison against stored templates.

## Method

R3D-AD formulates anomaly-free reconstruction as a conditional denoising diffusion process where the forward pass fully masks the entire point cloud into Gaussian noise (avoiding MAE's unmasked-region blind spots), and the reverse pass is conditioned on a 256-dim latent shape embedding extracted by a PointNet-based encoder. Instead of predicting absolute coordinates, a Step-Wise Displacement (SWD) decoder (PointwiseNet) predicts per-point displacement vectors at each reverse step, ensuring transformation consistency and preserving non-anomalous structure. A novel Patch-Gen augmentation randomly rotates normal samples, selects surface patches via random cube viewpoints, and applies Gaussian-distributed translations to simulate bulges, sinks, and damage, narrowing the train-test domain gap. Training is self-supervised with MSE loss between ground-truth and denoised points over 40,000 iterations, and inference requires only a single forward pass through the diffusion chain without any memory bank.

## Results

On the Real3D-AD dataset, R3D-AD achieves 73.4% image-level AUROC using only raw point cloud coordinates, outperforming the previous best method IMRNet (72.5%) and Reg3D-AD (70.4%). On the Anomaly-ShapeNet dataset, R3D-AD reaches 74.9% image-level AUROC, surpassing IMRNet and Reg3D-AD (both at 65.9%) by a 9-point margin. Notably, R3D-AD requires no pre-trained feature extractor (FPFH or PointMAE) and no memory bank, achieving these results with a single-step diffusion-based reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R3D-AD outperforms all six compared baselines on both datasets in terms of average image-level AUROC. On Real3D-AD, it beats the closest competitor IMRNet (72.5%) by 0.9 points and Reg3D-AD (70.4%) by 3.0 points, while the memory-bank-based methods (BTF, M3DM, PatchCore, CPMF) all fall below 64%. On Anomaly-ShapeNet, the margin widens substantially: R3D-AD (74.9%) surpasses IMRNet and Reg3D-AD (both 65.9%) by 9.0 points and BTF (49.3–52.8%) by over 22 points. A key practical advantage is that R3D-AD uses only raw point coordinates as input, whereas most baselines rely on pre-trained PointMAE or handcrafted FPFH features, and memory-bank methods (BTF, M3DM, PatchCore, CPMF) incur significant storage and inference-time overhead. R3D-AD also avoids the RANSAC alignment step required by Reg3D-AD, thanks to the spatial invariance learned through Patch-Gen's random rotation augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the DDPM framework (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) to the 3D anomaly detection setting, departing from two dominant paradigms: memory-bank-based methods (BTF, M3DM, CPMF, Reg3D-AD) that store and compare features, and MAE-based reconstruction methods (IMRNet) that only rebuild masked regions. The key conceptual departure is using the diffusion forward process as a full-mask mechanism (eliminating MAE's unmasked-region blind spot) and learning step-wise point displacements rather than absolute coordinates, conditioned on a PointNet-derived latent shape embedding. Patch-Gen, the proposed anomaly simulation strategy, extends the 2D synthetic-anomaly augmentation literature (CutPaste, RealNet) to the 3D point cloud domain with geometrically motivated deformations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in industrial 3D inspection: the prohibitive memory/inference cost of memory-bank methods and the fundamental flaw of MAE-based reconstruction preserving anomalies in unmasked regions. The efficiency gains (no memory bank, single-pass reconstruction) make the approach more deployable on production lines. However, open questions remain: the method is evaluated on only 4 training samples per category, the tested categories are relatively simple shapes, and it is unclear whether the diffusion-based reconstruction generalizes to highly complex or textured industrial parts. The modest ablation gains from Patch-Gen (≈2% I-AUROC) also suggest the data augmentation strategy may have limited headroom.
