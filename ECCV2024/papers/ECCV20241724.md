## Problem

Previous CZSL methods assign each primitive a single representation in embedding space, effectively capturing only the intersection of its semantic meanings across compositions. This overlooks conditional variance—the phenomenon where a primitive like 'old' conveys 'senior' in 'old tiger' but 'historical' in 'old town'—limiting the model's ability to generalize to unseen state-object compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241724/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder followed by a trainable 2-layer MLP (1024-dim). The output is a composition label (state, object) selected by maximizing a weighted sum of attention weights from three branches (composition, state, object) over the prediction space. In closed-world settings the prediction space is the union of seen and unseen compositions; in open-world settings it expands to the full Cartesian product S × O, which can be substantially larger than the test set.

## Method

MRSP constructs multiple representations for each primitive by initializing a compositional graph with one universal node plus one context-specific node per composition the primitive appears in, all seeded from CLIP text encoder features. A 2-layer GCN with pre-norm and residual connections propagates information through undirected edges linking primitives to their compositions, causing the context-specific nodes to diverge from the universal node and capture the union of primitive semantics. Decoding uses a three-branch multi-head cross-attention module (16 heads) where image features serve as queries and the GCN-updated text representations serve as keys/values; the final prediction score is a weighted combination of attention weights from the composition, state, and object branches (with hyperparameters α, β). Training jointly optimizes a cross-entropy loss on GCN node representations (L_MR) and a cross-entropy loss on the three branch outputs (L_CA), balanced by a scalar γ.

## Results

On the closed-world setting, MRSP achieves AUC of 23.8 on MIT-States (S=53.5, U=52.7, HM=41.3) and 52.5 on UT-Zappos (S=71.9, U=81.7, HM=65.2), setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. On Clothing16K, MRSP reaches 96.4 AUC in closed-world and 86.0 AUC in open-world, with best unseen accuracy of 99.7% and 88.4% respectively. In the open-world setting on UT-Zappos, MRSP achieves 38.8 AUC, a 25% improvement over the previous best (PLO-VLM at 33.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRSP outperforms all 18 baselines on the primary AUC metric across all three datasets in both closed-world and open-world settings. On MIT-States (closed-world), MRSP improves AUC by 1.6 points over PLO-VLM (23.8 vs 22.2) and by 1.7 over Troika (23.8 vs 22.1), while also achieving the highest seen accuracy (53.5 vs 49.6). On UT-Zappos (closed-world), the margin is substantially larger: MRSP's AUC of 52.5 exceeds PLO-VLM (42.0) by 10.5 points and Troika (41.7) by 10.8 points, with best unseen accuracy jumping to 81.7% versus 75.6% for PLO-VLM. On Clothing16K, MRSP achieves 96.4 AUC (closed-world) and 86.0 AUC (open-world), surpassing the previous best ADE by 4.0 and 18.0 points respectively. In the open-world setting on UT-Zappos, MRSP's 38.8 AUC represents a 25% relative improvement over PLO-VLM (33.1). The only minor trade-off is a slight decrease in best seen accuracy on MIT-States open-world (46.5 vs 49.5 for PLO-VLM), but unseen accuracy improves from 18.7 to 23.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

MRSP departs from the dominant CZSL paradigm of assigning a single embedding to each primitive (the 'intersection area' approach used by CGE, Co-CGE, and most CLIP-based methods) by instead learning multiple, composition-conditioned representations per primitive (a 'union area' strategy) to capture conditional variance. It repurposes GCN—typically used for node classification—to propagate information across a compositional graph where the same primitive appears as multiple nodes, and employs a three-branch cross-attention decoder (inspired by CrossViT and ADE) to perform concept-level decoding from image features. The framework builds on CLIP for both text and image encoding, extending the graph-embedding line (Naeem et al., Mancini et al.) with a multi-representation construction that prior graph-based CZSL methods did not attempt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The union-vs-intersection framing for primitive representations offers a clean conceptual explanation for why single-embedding CZSL methods struggle with conditional variance, and the resulting 25% AUC gain on UT-Zappos plus notably smaller open-world degradation (10.7% vs. 26–40% for prior methods) suggest the approach is robust to the expanded prediction space. However, the paper acknowledges that maintaining multiple representations per primitive increases computational and storage costs, and the GCN graph construction remains somewhat heuristic (neighborhood scope is hard to control), leaving open the question of more principled or efficient multi-representation construction. For practitioners, the three-branch cross-attention decoder with attention-level scoring is a reusable component that could be transplanted into other compositional or multi-attribute recognition settings.
