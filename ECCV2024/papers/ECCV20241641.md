## Problem

Existing cross-view video geo-localization (CVGL) methods rely on camera intrinsics and odometry data that are typically unavailable in uncontrolled real-world settings, incur high computational costs from multi-frame encoders, and produce temporally inconsistent GPS trajectories by treating each frame's localization as an independent nearest-neighbor retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241641/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query street-view video (e.g., ~40 seconds at 1 FPS on GAMa, or ~7 frames on SeqGeo) and a gallery of geo-tagged aerial images in two scales: small per-frame images and large regional images covering ~49× the area. It produces a GPS trajectory via a two-stage hierarchical pipeline: first a sequence-to-image match localizing the entire video to a large aerial region, then frame-to-frame GPS coordinates within that region. No camera parameters or odometry are required at any stage.

## Method

GAReT extends the TransGeo image geo-localization transformer to video via GeoAdapter, a lightweight adapter inserted into every ViT block consisting of two 2-layer MLPs (GeLU) flanking a temporal self-attention (TSA) layer that operates only on [CLS] tokens while reusing spatial attention weights, avoiding full patch-level temporal computation. Training is two-stage: the DeiT encoder is first trained on street-view/aerial image pairs with soft-margin triplet loss, then frozen while GeoAdapter is optimized on video/large-aerial pairs. For temporally consistent frame-to-frame predictions, TransRetriever formulates retrieval as a Generalized Traveling Salesman Problem, encoding each frame's top-k candidate aerial images as 3D tokens (UTM_x, UTM_y, cosine similarity) and autoregressively decoding one selection per frame conditioned on the previous frame's choice via a 6-layer encoder and 2-layer decoder transformer.

## Results

On the GAMa dataset, GAReT achieves 50.69% top-1 recall for sequence-to-image (large aerial) localization, substantially outperforming general-purpose video models (VideoSWIN: 20.37%, TimeSformer: 20.10%) and the prior state-of-the-art GAMa Screening Network (12.2%). For the primary frame-by-frame geo-localization task, GAReT scores 54.64% top-1 recall, nearly tripling the best prior method (GAMa-Net Hierarchical: 18.3%). On the SeqGeo benchmark, GAReT nearly doubles the state-of-the-art, reaching 3.34% top-1 recall versus 1.80% for SeqGeo [43]. With TransRetriever, frame-by-frame recall further improves to 67.66% (k=10) and 70.70% (k=20), surpassing both nearest-neighbor retrieval (54.64%) and Dominant Sets (56.09%/60.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAReT outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Against general-purpose video models (TimeSformer, VideoSWIN), GAReT's GeoAdapter achieves roughly 2.5× higher top-1 recall on GAMa sequence-to-image (50.69% vs. ~20%), demonstrating that image-level pretraining plus lightweight temporal adaptation beats dedicated video architectures. Against the prior CVGL state-of-the-art (GAMa-Net and its Hierarchical variant [32]), GAReT improves frame-by-frame top-1 recall from 18.3% to 54.64%, a ~3× gain, while also using top-10 large aerial images for gallery construction versus top-1% for GAMa-Net. On the SeqGeo benchmark, GAReT nearly doubles the best prior method (3.34% vs. 1.80% for SeqGeo [43], 0.63% for SAFA [25], 0.54% for VIGOR [46]). For temporally consistent retrieval, TransRetriever surpasses both nearest-neighbor (54.64%) and Dominant Sets [29] (56.09% at k=10) by a wide margin, reaching 67.66% at k=10 and 70.70% at k=20. Among adapter alternatives, GAReT's [CLS]-token-based temporal self-attention outperforms AIM [36] (48.06%) and ST-Adapter [20] (34.29%) on sequence-to-image recall, confirming that attending only over global tokens is both more effective and computationally lighter for CVGL.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAReT extends TransGeo, a pure transformer-based cross-view image geo-localization method, to the video setting by introducing GeoAdapter, a parameter-efficient adapter module inspired by AIM and ST-Adapter but simplified to compute temporal self-attention only on [CLS] tokens rather than all patches. It departs from prior CVGL methods (GAMa, CVLNet) that require camera intrinsics and odometry, and from hierarchical approaches that use separate models per stage, by unifying sequence-to-image and frame-to-frame localization within a single encoder. The TransRetriever component draws on the Transformer-for-TSP formulation and NLP encoder-decoder architectures, casting the frame-level retrieval as a Generalized Traveling Salesman Problem solved via autoregressive decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for camera parameters and odometry, GAReT makes cross-view video geo-localization applicable to uncontrolled real-world capture scenarios where such metadata is unavailable. The adapter-based design is computationally lighter than dedicated video models while outperforming them, suggesting that for geo-localization specifically, full spatiotemporal modeling is unnecessary. Open questions include whether the [CLS]-only temporal attention generalizes to more complex video understanding tasks, how the method scales to significantly longer videos or denser aerial galleries, and whether the GTSP formulation of TransRetriever remains tractable as the number of candidate neighbors grows.
