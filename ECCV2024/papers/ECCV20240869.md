## Problem

Existing video lane detectors either process frames independently or aggregate past features without explicitly accounting for occlusions, causing unreliable detection when lanes are hidden by nearby vehicles. The closest prior work, RVLD, relies on motion estimation to warp a single previous frame, which produces inaccurate motion fields in occluded regions and leads to missed or flickering lane detections. This paper addresses the gap by explicitly detecting latent obstacles and leveraging long-term memory to recover occluded lane features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240869/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames resized to 384×640 and processes them recursively: the OMR module receives the current frame's encoded feature map (96×160×64), a binary obstacle mask, the previous frame's already-refined feature map and lane mask, plus an internal ConvLSTM memory state. Output is a binary lane mask (96×160), a per-pixel lane probability map, and a lane coefficient map in a 6-dimensional eigenlane space, from which lane curves are reconstructed via non-maximum suppression over the probability map.

## Method

The pipeline has four stages: (1) a ResNet18 encoder fuses three multi-scale feature maps into a single 64-channel feature map; (2) a lightweight head predicts a binary obstacle mask by thresholding a sigmoid output, trained with pseudo-labels from SegFormer; (3) the OMR module channel-concatenates the current feature map, obstacle mask, previous refined feature map, and previous lane mask, passes them through convolutions, then updates a ConvLSTM memory state whose hidden state is added to the current features to produce a refined map; (4) a decoder with deformable convolution regresses eigenlane coefficients and a probability map for lane extraction. Training is two-stage (encoder/decoder first, then OMR with frozen front-end), and a data augmentation scheme overlays fully-shaped objects from the KINS dataset onto training frames with linearly varying size and position to simulate realistic occlusion sequences.

## Results

On VIL-100, the proposed OMR achieves the best F1 score (0.936), lowest flickering rate (R_F = 0.026), and lowest missing rate (R_M = 0.038) among all compared methods, though its mIoU (0.774) is slightly below RVLD's (0.787). On the larger and more challenging OpenLane-V dataset, OMR achieves the best mIoU (0.742), F1 (0.836), and missing rate (R_M = 0.162), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art RVLD by margins of 0.015, 0.011, and 0.005 respectively. The processing speed is approximately 105 FPS, faster than RVLD's ~80 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary video baseline RVLD [11], OMR wins on F1 (+0.012), R_F (−0.012), and R_M (−0.012) on VIL-100, and on mIoU (+0.015), F1 (+0.011), and R_M (−0.005) on OpenLane-V, though it trails RVLD by 0.002 on R_F for OpenLane-V (0.016 vs. 0.014). Against the strongest image-based detector ADNet [34], OMR achieves a higher F1 (0.936 vs. 0.920) and substantially lower R_M (0.038 vs. 0.043) on VIL-100, but a slightly lower mIoU (0.774 vs. 0.781). On OpenLane-V, OMR outperforms all image-based baselines (CondLaneNet, GANet, CLRNet, MFIALane) by large margins in both F1 (0.836 vs. 0.723–0.801) and temporal stability (R_M 0.162 vs. 0.198–0.300). Among older video baselines (MMA-Net, ConvLSTM, ConvGRUs, TGC-Net, LaneATT-T), OMR dominates across all reported metrics, with the largest gaps seen in R_M (e.g., 0.038 vs. 0.127 for MMA-Net on VIL-100). The only metric where OMR does not achieve the best score is mIoU on VIL-100, where RVLD edges it by 0.013, suggesting that the memory-based refinement slightly trades off per-frame IoU for improved temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recursive video lane detection framework of RVLD (Jin et al., ICCV 2023) by replacing its motion-estimation-based feature warping with an occlusion-aware memory-based refinement (OMR) module. Where RVLD relies on a single previous frame and a motion field to propagate state, OMR instead fuses a latent obstacle mask (generated via SegFormer pseudo-labels), the previous refined output, and a ConvLSTM memory cell to handle occluded regions more robustly. The decoder retains the eigenlane representation from Jin et al. (CVPR 2022), and the overall pipeline (encode → detect obstacles → refine → decode) is a deliberate departure from the multi-frame aggregation strategies of earlier video detectors (MMA-Net, TGC-Net, LaneATT-T).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical safety concern in autonomous driving: temporally unstable lane detection under occlusion, quantified through flickering and missing rates that prior methods report but do not minimize. The 105 FPS runtime (vs. RVLD's 80 FPS) makes the approach viable for real-time deployment, and the synthetic data augmentation strategy using KINS amodal masks offers a reusable recipe for training occlusion-robust video models. Open questions include the dependence on SegFormer for generating obstacle pseudo-labels (a potential error source), whether the ConvLSTM memory degrades over very long sequences, and whether the approach generalizes to occlusion types beyond vehicles (e.g., construction zones, heavy rain).
