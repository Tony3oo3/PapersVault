## Problem

Traditional federated learning assumes homogeneous hardware across clients, but real-world deployments involve devices ranging from resource-constrained Raspberry Pis to GPU-equipped PCs, making a single global model infeasible. When clients train models of different architectures and parameter dimensions, standard weight averaging on the server breaks down, and existing model-heterogeneous FL methods either require public datasets, suffer from high client-side computation, or discard non-overlapping parameters that carry important information.

## Input / Output

Each client contributes private local image data (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64) and uploads short text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of real bird') to the server's prompt pool. The server's pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1-4 model generates synthetic images from these prompts, which are then used as the shared medium for inter-cluster knowledge exchange. The output is a set of cluster-based ResNet18 models with different pruning rates (e.g., 1.0, 0.8, 0.6), where each cluster's model has a distinct architecture matched to its members' computational capacity.

## Method

FedTSA introduces a two-stage aggregation pipeline. First, clients are clustered by their proxy-task training durations using Gaussian kernel density estimation, and each cluster is assigned a pruning rate proportional to its mean processing speed, yielding homogeneous sub-models within each cluster. Stage 1 performs conventional FedAvg weight averaging within each cluster. Stage 2 aggregates the heterogeneous cluster models on the server via deep mutual learning: a pre-trained diffusion model generates synthetic images from client prompts, each cluster model produces soft logits on these images, and all models are updated by minimizing KL divergence toward the consensus (average) logits across clusters. This eliminates the need for a global model or public dataset, offloads data generation to the server, and allows seamless task switching by simply changing prompts.

## Results

FedTSA achieves 89.28% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 (IID), 60.56% on CIFAR-100 (IID), and 36.28% on Tiny-ImageNet (IID), outperforming all model-heterogeneous baselines across all three datasets under both IID and non-IID (Dir(0.6), Dir(0.3)) settings. Under the most heterogeneous data distribution Dir(0.3), FedTSA maintains 84.61% on CIFAR-10, 59.57% on CIFAR-100, and 35.07% on Tiny-ImageNet, exceeding the upper-bound accuracy of homogeneous FedAvg (80.91%, 48.31%, 27.49% respectively) on the two harder datasets. The method also demonstrates robustness to increasing model heterogeneity, retaining 80.09% on CIFAR-10 even when five distinct pruning rates [0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0] are used, while HeteroFL drops to 67.79%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTSA consistently outperforms all four model-heterogeneous FL baselines across every dataset and data distribution. Against HeteroFL (the strongest baseline), FedTSA gains +1.67% on CIFAR-10 IID (89.28 vs 87.61), +1.64% on CIFAR-100 IID (60.56 vs 58.92), and +1.71% on Tiny-ImageNet IID (36.28 vs 34.57), with margins widening under non-IID settings (e.g., +1.84% on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.3)). Against FedDF, the gap is substantially larger: +10.27% on CIFAR-10 IID and +20.85% on CIFAR-100 IID, reflecting FedDF's reliance on unlabeled data of uncertain quality. FedMD and FedProto trail further behind, with FedProto achieving the lowest accuracy overall (e.g., 28.46% on CIFAR-100 Dir(0.3)), attributed to degraded prototype quality from simpler pruned models. Compared to homogeneous FedAvg/FedProx, FedTSA slightly underperforms the unpruned upper bound on CIFAR-10 IID (89.28 vs 90.86) but exceeds the pruned lower bound by 4–5%, and surpasses both upper bounds on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet across all settings, demonstrating that the DML-based cross-cluster knowledge transfer compensates for reduced model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedTSA extends the model-heterogeneous FL line initiated by FedMD, FedDF, FedProto, and HeteroFL, which each address the dimension-mismatch problem through logits, data-free distillation, prototypes, or partial subnetwork aggregation, respectively. It departs from these by introducing a two-stage aggregation that first performs conventional weight averaging within resource-homogeneous clusters (identified via KDE on a proxy-task duration) and then aggregates across clusters using Deep Mutual Learning on diffusion-model-generated data. The use of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as a server-side data generator is the key architectural departure, replacing the public-dataset or client-side GAN requirements of prior KD-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedTSA addresses a practically important but underexplored axis of FL heterogeneity (system resources rather than data distribution) and demonstrates that a single pre-trained diffusion model can serve as a flexible, privacy-preserving data source for cross-model knowledge transfer. However, the approach is validated only on CV classification tasks with ResNet-18, and the server-side diffusion inference introduces non-trivial computational overhead that may limit deployment in bandwidth- or compute-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to NLP or multimodal tasks, whether the DML aggregation can be replaced by lighter-weight mechanisms, and how the method behaves when the number of clusters or the pruning-rate spread grows substantially.
