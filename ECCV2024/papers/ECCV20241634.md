## Problem

Diffusion models excel at generating diverse, high-quality images but suffer from slow training and inference that scales quadratically with resolution, making high-resolution synthesis (1024²+) computationally prohibitive. Flow matching offers straighter trajectories and faster ODE-based sampling but currently produces less diverse samples than diffusion models. The paper addresses how to combine these complementary strengths so that a small, fast diffusion model handles semantic diversity at low resolution while an efficient flow matching model handles the expensive high-resolution detail enhancement.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text prompt (for text-to-image) or an unconditional signal, which is fed to a frozen small diffusion model (e.g., SD1.5 or LCM-SD1.5) operating at a reduced resolution (256² or 512² pixels). The resulting low-resolution latent code is optionally noise-augmented and passed to the CFM module, which outputs a high-resolution latent code (128² in latent space, corresponding to 1024² pixels) that is decoded by a pre-trained VAE. Cascading two CFM stages can extend output to 2048² pixels. The CFM U-Net receives the interpolated latent along the ODE trajectory as its spatial input and the noise-augmented low-res latent as conditioning.

## Method

FMBoost inserts a Coupling Flow Matching (CFM) model between a frozen compact diffusion model and a pre-trained VAE decoder, all operating in latent space. The key architectural insight is using data-dependent couplings: instead of starting from Gaussian noise, the flow begins at the encoded low-resolution image (x₀ = E(x_low)), so the vector field regresses toward the high-resolution latent (x₁ = E(x_high)) along a straight path, inherently satisfying optimal transport. The vector field is parameterized as a U-Net conditioned on the low-res latent, trained with an L2 loss on intermediate vector fields, and augmented with Gaussian noise on x₀ (optimal at timestep 400) to smooth the base distribution. At inference, a fixed-step Euler ODE solver (40 NFE) integrates the flow in just a few steps, and the entire pipeline is orthogonal to existing diffusion acceleration techniques like LCM-LoRA or DDIM.

## Results

On FacesHQ, CFM achieves FID 1.36 and p-FID 1.62, outperforming the diffusion-based baseline (FID 2.72, p-FID 4.71) and Naive Flow Matching (FID 1.37, p-FID 2.10). On LHQ, CFM reaches FID 2.27 and p-FID 2.38, again beating DM (FID 4.29, p-FID 4.55) and FM (FID 2.31, p-FID 2.61). In the end-to-end text-to-image setting on LAION-5k at 1024², SD1.5+CFM (512²→1024²) achieves FID 21.67 and p-FID 15.96 in 3.16 s/image, substantially outperforming LCM-LoRA SDXL (FID 28.98, p-FID 24.00 in 1.83 s/image). In the full pipeline comparison (Table 3), CFM consistently beats the diffusion-based upscaler at both 4 and 40 NFE, e.g., FID 21.61 vs. 26.53 at 4 steps for 512²→1024².

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFM decisively outperforms both L1 and L2 regression baselines on FID and p-FID across both datasets (e.g., FID 1.36 vs. 4.52/5.73 on FacesHQ), confirming that simple pixel-level regression is insufficient for latent-space upscaling due to mode-averaging blur. Against the diffusion-based upscaler (DM, [62]), CFM achieves lower FID (1.36 vs. 2.72 on FacesHQ; 2.27 vs. 4.29 on LHQ) and substantially lower p-FID (1.62 vs. 4.71; 2.38 vs. 4.55), while also converging faster during training (Fig. 7). Compared to Naive Flow Matching (FM, [41]), CFM achieves a marginal FID improvement (1.36 vs. 1.37 on FacesHQ) but a clear p-FID advantage (1.62 vs. 2.10), attributed to the data-dependent coupling that guarantees optimal transport. In the end-to-end text-to-image comparison (Table 1), SD1.5+CFM (512²→1024²) beats LCM-LoRA SDXL by 7.3 FID points (21.67 vs. 28.98) and 8.0 p-FID points (15.96 vs. 24.00) on LAION-5k. The paper also reports competitive results against Wuerstchen [54], SDXL [55], and CogView3 [83] on COCO 1024×1024 (Tab. 5 in Appendix), achieving a favorable speed–accuracy trade-off by offloading resolution enhancement to the lightweight CFM module.

## Relation to Prior Work

FMBoost composes three established ingredients into a new pipeline: a compact Latent Diffusion Model (Rombach et al., 2022) for diverse low-resolution content generation, a Coupling Flow Matching module (Lipman et al., 2023; Albergo et al., 2023) that maps the low-res latent to a high-res latent via a straight, data-dependent optimal-transport path, and a pre-trained VAE decoder for pixel-space reconstruction. The noise-augmentation trick is borrowed from cascaded diffusion models (Ho et al., 2022), and the latent-space training strategy follows the LDM paradigm. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior work by arguing that the stochasticity of diffusion is unnecessary for the resolution-boosting stage, replacing it with a deterministic ODE-based flow that is both faster to train and to sample.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a small SD1.5 model boosted with CFM matches or exceeds SDXL at 1024² resolution with roughly 3× lower inference time, and the pipeline is explicitly orthogonal to existing acceleration techniques (DDIM, DPM-Solver, LCM-LoRA), making it a drop-in module for any LDM. Open questions include whether the VAE decoder remains a quality bottleneck at even higher resolutions (2048²+), whether the approach transfers cleanly to video or 3D generation, and whether the CFM stage can be further distilled to 1–2 NFE without quality loss. The paper does not explore conditioning beyond text prompts in depth, leaving open how well the CFM upscaler handles structured or multi-object scenes at scale.
