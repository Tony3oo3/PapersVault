## Problem

While pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models have been leveraged for image understanding, the potential of pre-trained text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models for video understanding tasks remains largely unexplored. The key question is whether the latent representations of a fixed generative T2V model, trained to produce temporally coherent video sequences, inherently encode the temporal semantic consistency and robustness needed for video segmentation, outperforming discriminatively-pretrained video backbones like Video Swin Transformer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240322/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of T frames (resized to 640px longest side, 5 frames per clip) and a natural-language referring expression as input. It produces per-frame binary segmentation masks at full resolution (H×W), along with per-frame bounding boxes and binary classification scores indicating object presence. The T2V diffusion model (ModelScopeT2V) is kept entirely frozen; only the noise prediction module, text-guided image projection layer, and the Deformable Transformer mask decoder are trained end-to-end.

## Method

VD-IT extracts visual features from a fixed pretrained T2V diffusion model (ModelScopeT2V) rather than using a discriminatively-pretrained video backbone. Two dedicated designs enable this: (1) Text-Guided Image Projection, which fuses referring text tokens (as queries) with CLIP visual tokens (as keys/values) via cross-attention to form a conditional prompt that simultaneously ensures temporal semantic consistency and preserves fine-grained spatial detail; and (2) Video-specific Noise Prediction, which replaces standard Gaussian noise with a learned, video-correlated noise pattern (via a convolutional layer followed by normalization) to preserve feature fidelity at the minimal noise step. The resulting multi-scale features (4×–32× downsampling) are fed into a Deformable Transformer decoder where RoBERTa-encoded text instance queries are matched to visual features, producing final masks via dynamic convolution and 8× upsampling.

## Results

On Ref-YouTube-VOS, VD-IT achieves J&F=64.8 (J=63.1, F=66.6) without pre-training and J&F=66.5 (J=64.4, F=68.5) with RefCOCO pre-training, surpassing the previous best SgMg with Video-Swin-B (61.6 / 65.7) by 3.2 and 0.8 points respectively. On Ref-DAVIS17, VD-IT reaches J&F=63.0 (no pre-training) and J&F=69.4 (with pre-training), outperforming SgMg (56.7 / 63.3) by 6.3 and 6.1 points. On A2D-Sentences and JHMDB-Sentences, VD-IT achieves mAP of 61.4 and 46.5, exceeding SgMg (Video-Swin-B) by 2.9 and 1.5 points. The method runs at 21 FPS on Ref-YouTube-VOS, slower than SgMg's 40–65 FPS but competitive with other transformer-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VD-IT outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. The primary SOTA competitor is SgMg [29] (ICCV 2023), which uses a discriminatively fine-tuned Video Swin Transformer; VD-IT surpasses it by 5.9 J&F points on Ref-YouTube-VOS (64.8 vs. 58.9, Video-Swin-T) and by 6.3 points on Ref-DAVIS17 (63.0 vs. 56.7). Even against the stronger SgMg with Video-Swin-B (61.6 J&F), VD-IT leads by 3.2 points without pre-training and by 0.8 points with RefCOCO pre-training (66.5 vs. 65.7). On A2D-Sentences and JHMDB-Sentences, VD-IT beats SgMg (Video-Swin-B) by 2.9 and 1.5 mAP points respectively. On the single-frame RefCOCO/+/g benchmark, VD-IT (76.7/66.5/70.3 Overall IoU) is nearly on par with SgMg (76.3/66.4/70.0), confirming that the gains come from temporal consistency rather than single-frame mask quality. Older baselines such as ReferFormer [47] (56.0 J&F), MTTR [3] (55.3), and MANet [5] (55.6) are surpassed by 8–10 J&F points, while earlier methods like URVOS [38] (47.2) and CMPC-V [25] (47.5) trail by over 17 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the emerging line of work that repurposes pretrained diffusion model features for discriminative understanding tasks (e.g., [49, 53] for image segmentation) to the video domain, using a fixed pretrained text-to-video diffusion model (ModelScopeT2V [42]) as a feature extractor for R-VOS. It departs from the dominant R-VOS paradigm—where discriminatively pretrained video backbones like Video Swin Transformer are jointly finetuned with a mask decoder—by treating the generative T2V U-Net as a frozen representation source and introducing two task-specific designs: Text-Guided Image Projection (fusing referring text and CLIP visual tokens as the conditioning prompt) and Video-specific Noise Prediction (replacing standard Gaussian noise with learned video-correlated noise). The mask prediction head follows the standard query-based Deformable Transformer architecture shared with SgMg and ReferFormer, so the contribution is squarely in the feature extraction stage rather than the segmentation head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that a fixed generative T2V model's latent features exhibit stronger temporal semantic consistency than discriminatively pretrained video backbones—challenges the prevailing assumption that discriminative pretraining is necessary for high-quality video understanding features, and opens a path toward unifying generative and discriminative video models. However, the evidence is limited to a single T2V architecture (ModelScopeT2V), the inference speed is notably lower (21 FPS vs. 65 for SgMg), and the paper itself acknowledges that without its dedicated prompt and noise designs, the raw T2V features do not outperform existing methods. Open questions include whether the temporal-consistency advantage generalizes to other T2V models (e.g., Stable Video Diffusion, CogVideoX), whether it extends beyond R-VOS to other video understanding tasks (action recognition, video retrieval), and whether the fixed-backbone constraint can be relaxed to allow lightweight adaptation without sacrificing the generative prior.
