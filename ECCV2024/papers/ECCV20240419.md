## Problem

Applying 3D Gaussian Splatting to large-scale city scenes (1.5–2.7 km²) faces two critical bottlenecks: training requires over 20 million Gaussians, causing out-of-memory errors even on 40 GB GPUs, and rendering millions of Gaussians via depth-sorted rasterization drops to ~21 FPS. Existing NeRF-based large-scale methods (Mega-NeRF, Switch-NeRF, GP-NeRF) avoid the memory issue but sacrifice rendering fidelity and real-time speed, while prior 3DGS work targets only objects or small scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image collection with COLMAP SfM camera poses; MatrixCity images are rescaled to 1600 px width and real-world scenes (Residence, Rubble, Building, Sci-Art) are downsampled by 4×. The output is a fused 3D Gaussian scene (up to 23.7 M primitives with position, opacity, covariance, and 3rd-order SH coefficients) plus three compressed LoD variants at 50 %, 34 %, and 25 % retention, enabling real-time novel-view rendering at camera altitudes from street level to 500 m.

## Method

CityGS employs a divide-and-conquer training pipeline: first a coarse global Gaussian prior is trained on all views for 30 K iterations to provide geometric guidance; the unbounded scene is then contracted into a bounded cubic via ScaNeRF's mapping, uniformly partitioned into spatial blocks, and each block is fine-tuned in parallel using only views whose SSIM contribution from that block exceeds a threshold (Eq. 3) or whose camera lies inside the block (Eq. 4), with the global prior as initialization to prevent inter-block interference. For rendering, multiple LoD levels are generated by applying LightGaussian compression at different rates, and a block-wise frustum-culling strategy—using MAD-based bounding boxes to reject floaters and assigning a single detail level per block based on its minimum corner-to-camera distance—feeds only necessary Gaussians to the rasterizer, achieving 53.7 FPS with near-full-quality fidelity (SSIM 0.855 vs 0.865 without LoD).

## Results

On the 2.7 km² MatrixCity scene, CityGS achieves PSNR 27.46, SSIM 0.865, and LPIPS 0.204, outperforming the strongest baseline 3DGS† (PSNR 23.67, SSIM 0.735, LPIPS 0.384) by a large margin and being the first method to successfully reconstruct the entire city. On real-world scenes (Residence, Rubble, Building), CityGS consistently leads on SSIM and LPIPS, e.g., SSIM 0.813 vs. 0.791 (3DGS†) on Residence and SSIM 0.813 vs. 0.777 on Rubble. The LoD strategy further boosts rendering speed from 21.6 FPS (no-LoD) to 53.7 FPS while retaining near-best quality (PSNR 27.32 vs. 27.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CityGS outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all scenes where comparisons are available. Against the NeRF-based methods (MegaNeRF, Switch-NeRF, GP-NeRF), CityGS leads by a wide margin: on Rubble, SSIM improves from 0.553–0.565 (NeRF baselines) to 0.813, and LPIPS drops from ~0.50 to 0.228. Against the strongest baseline 3DGS† (a tuned 60k-iteration 3DGS), CityGS gains +3.79 dB PSNR on MatrixCity (27.46 vs. 23.67) and +0.036 SSIM on Residence (0.813 vs. 0.791), while using roughly 1.6× more Gaussians (23.7M vs. 9.7M on MatrixCity). On the street-view Block_A scene, CityGS also beats Mip-NeRF 360 (SSIM 0.808 vs. 0.717, PSNR 22.98 vs. 22.00) and 3DGS† (SSIM 0.808 vs. 0.701). Notably, MegaNeRF and Switch-NeRF could not be evaluated on MatrixCity due to configuration difficulties, underscoring the scalability advantage of the divide-and-conquer approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

CityGS extends the divide-and-conquer paradigm established by Mega-NeRF and Block-NeRF to the explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, addressing the memory and rendering bottlenecks that prevent direct 3DGS application to city-scale scenes. It borrows the unbounded-space contraction function from ScaNeRF for balanced block partitioning and repurposes LightGaussian's compression pipeline to generate multi-resolution detail levels. The key conceptual departure from prior large-scale NeRF work is the block-wise LoD selection strategy, which treats spatially adjacent Gaussian blocks as atomic units for frustum culling and detail assignment, avoiding the per-point distance computation that would be prohibitively expensive at 20M+ primitive scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CityGS is the first method to successfully reconstruct and render the full 2.7 km² MatrixCity scene in real time, demonstrating that explicit Gaussian representations can match or exceed NeRF-based approaches at city scale when paired with appropriate training and rendering strategies. The practical impact is significant: the LoD strategy nearly triples rendering speed (21.6 → 53.7 FPS) while preserving near-finest-level quality, making large-scale 3DGS viable for AR/VR and autonomous driving applications. However, the static-scene assumption and the observed degradation when combining aerial and street-level viewpoints remain open challenges that limit broader deployment. The block-wise LoD design also raises questions about optimal block granularity and whether learned (rather than distance-based) detail selection could further improve the quality-speed tradeoff.
