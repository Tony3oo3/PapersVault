## Problem

Existing visual text generation methods cannot simultaneously satisfy fidelity (photo-realistic rendering with correct content), reasonability (text regions and contents coherent with scene context), and utility (downstream OCR task performance). Rendering-based approaches produce obvious artifacts and nonsensical text placement, while diffusion-based methods are constrained to limited text scales and regions due to whole-image latent generation, and often produce inaccurate annotations that undermine utility.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text-erased background image and pre-defined prompts as input to TRCG, which outputs text region bounding boxes (including curved regions parameterized by eight Bezier control points) and contextually appropriate text content strings in language format. LVTR then consumes these regions/contents along with the background to generate local text crops that are stitched back into the full image. Unlike whole-image latent diffusion methods, LVTR operates on small extended region crops (boundaries expanded by 10%), enabling text generation at arbitrary scales including small characters that VAE-based latent spaces struggle with.

## Method

SceneVTG follows a two-stage paradigm. The Text Region and Content Generator (TRCG) fine-tunes an MLLM via LoRA (with visual backbone and projection layer training) in a coarse-to-fine two-step process: first locating key points and generating semantically coherent text contents, then refining into detailed (potentially curved) text regions, inspired by ReAct-style task decomposition. The Local Visual Text Renderer (LVTR) is a conditional diffusion model with a UNet backbone that generates text in small local crops rather than whole-image latent space, conditioned on image-level inputs (background crop, line mask, word mask) and embedding-level inputs (frozen OCR image and text embeddings). Training combines the standard conditional diffusion noise-prediction loss with a foreground loss enforcing OCR feature-level consistency of text content and a background loss preserving pixel-level continuity of surrounding regions, yielding accurate OCR annotations and seamless background integration.

## Results

On SceneVTG-benchmark, SceneVTG achieves a FID of 26.28 in end-to-end inference, substantially outperforming SynthText (48.19) and all diffusion-based methods (73.07–88.02). In local image generation, SceneVTG attains FID 27.66, FID-R 33.34, F-score 75.73, and Line Accuracy 53.21, surpassing SynthText (FID 37.36, FID-R 46.40, F 66.07, LA 40.30) and TextDiffuser-2 (FID 42.00, FID-R 47.93, F 69.26, LA 26.13). For reasonability, SceneVTG achieves IoU 33.95 and PD-Edge 0.68×10⁻⁴ versus SynthText's 7.31 and 1.09×10⁻⁴. In utility, training OCR models on SceneVTG-generated data yields detection F-scores of 75.36/66.31/53.90 (IC13/IC15/MLT17) and recognition LA of 47.34 (avg), approaching real-data performance (76.34/79.15/56.09; 50.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneVTG decisively outperforms SynthText [15] on every fidelity metric (FID 26.28 vs 48.19 end-to-end; 27.66 vs 37.36 local) and by a wide margin on reasonability (IoU 33.95 vs 7.31, a 4.6× improvement), confirming that MLLM-guided region/content selection is far more contextually coherent than rule-based feature-map approaches. Against diffusion-based baselines (TextDiffuser [6], GlyphControl [61], AnyText [55], TextDiffuser-2 [5]), SceneVTG achieves the lowest FID in both end-to-end and local settings, while also matching or exceeding their OCR F-scores; the sole exception is TextDiffuser's slightly higher end-to-end F-score (53.43 vs 52.03), which the authors attribute to TextDiffuser's detector-friendly but recognition-poor outputs. In the utility evaluation (Table 3), SceneVTG-generated data trains text detectors to 75.36/66.31/53.90 F-score on IC13/IC15/MLT17, surpassing all rendering-based baselines (SynthText 72.38/56.65/47.17, VISD 75.19/65.49/50.21, UnrealText 73.73/61.80/47.62, LBTS 68.83/52.69/40.59) and all diffusion-based baselines (TextDiffuser 52.50/34.08/29.06, GlyphControl 35.04/40.64/24.37, AnyText 48.05/42.45/30.75, TextDiffuser-2 21.47/10.79/12.23). For recognition, SceneVTG achieves 47.34 average LA, exceeding all baselines and reaching 94.7% of the real-data upper bound (50.01), with the largest gains on the Irregular subset (35.50 vs 26.08 for SynthText) thanks to its ability to synthesize curved text.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneVTG carves new ground by repurposing two established ideas—MLLM visual reasoning (in the ReAct-style coarse-to-fine decomposition) and conditional diffusion rendering—into a two-stage pipeline that neither rendering-based methods (SynthText, VISD, UnrealText) nor whole-image diffusion methods (TextDiffuser, GlyphControl, AnyText, TextDiffuser-2) attempt. It departs from rendering-based work by replacing rule-based feature-map region selection and random text sampling with an MLLM that reasons about scene context, and from diffusion-based work by operating on local crops rather than full-image latents, thereby sidestepping the scale and diversity limitations of VAE-encoded latent diffusion. The local conditional diffusion renderer with image-level and embedding-level conditions is a novel architectural combination, though each component (UNet diffusion, OCR-embedding conditioning, mask guidance) draws on existing sub-image generation and inpainting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for OCR data augmentation: 10K SceneVTG-generated samples bring detection and recognition performance within a few points of training on real data, and the 155K-image SceneVTG-Erase dataset fills a gap in paired scene-text/background resources. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include single-language (English) support, limited font and color diversity, and lack of end-to-end controllability, leaving open questions about multilingual extension (e.g., Chinese), user-controllable text attributes, and whether the MLLM-based region planning generalizes to highly cluttered or novel scene types. The finding that diffusion-based methods produce worse detection utility than rendering-based methods—despite higher visual fidelity—highlights an unresolved tension between perceptual quality and annotation accuracy in synthetic data generation.
