## Problem

Existing open-set object detectors fuse language and vision information in only one or two phases of the detection pipeline (neck, query initialization, or head), leading to sub-optimal cross-modality alignment and limited generalization to novel objects. Additionally, most prior methods rely on CLIP for concept generalization, which is less effective for region-text detection, and the important Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) scenario is largely overlooked in evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image and a text prompt: for open-set detection, all target category names are concatenated into a single string (up to 256 BPE tokens); for REC, a natural-language referring expression is provided. It outputs up to 900 bounding boxes each paired with a corresponding noun phrase extracted from the input text. For REC, the single highest-scoring box-phrase pair is returned as the answer. The image backbone (Swin-T or Swin-L) extracts multi-scale features at 4×–32× (or 8×–64×) resolutions.

## Method

Grounding DINO introduces tight cross-modality fusion across all three phases of a DINO-based detector: (1) a feature enhancer in the neck that stacks deformable self-attention, text-to-image cross-attention, and image-to-text cross-attention over six layers; (2) a language-guided query selection module that computes max-pooled image-text similarity to pick the top 900 image tokens as decoder queries; and (3) a cross-modality decoder that adds a text cross-attention layer to each of six decoder layers alongside the standard image cross-attention. A key text-side innovation is the sub-sentence level representation, which applies attention masks to block interactions between unrelated category names during BERT encoding, preserving per-word features while eliminating spurious cross-category dependencies. The model is trained with grounded pre-training (contrastive/focal loss between predicted regions and language tokens) on large-scale detection, grounding, and caption data, followed by optional fine-tuning on target benchmarks.

## Results

Grounding DINO L (Swin-L backbone, 341M params) achieves 52.5 AP on COCO zero-shot detection without any COCO training data, and sets a new record of 26.1 mean AP on the ODinW zero-shot benchmark. On ODinW full-shot, Grounding DINO T (172M) reaches 70.7 AP, outperforming DINO-Swin-L (218M) at 68.8 AP. On RefCOCO/+/g with fine-tuning, Grounding DINO L achieves 90.56/82.75/86.13 top-1 accuracy (val), surpassing DQ-DETR (88.63/81.66/82.76) and MDETR (86.75/79.52/81.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ODinW zero-shot, Grounding DINO L (26.1 AP) outperforms all prior methods including Florence (~841M, 25.8 AP), DetCLIP (24.9 AP), and GLIPv2-T (22.3 AP), despite being smaller than Florence by 2.5x. Grounding DINO T (172M) matches GLIPv2-T (232M) on AP_average (22.3) but significantly outperforms it on AP_median (11.9 vs. 8.9), indicating more consistent performance across the 35+ ODinW datasets. On ODinW full-shot, Grounding DINO T (70.7 AP) surpasses DINO-Swin-L (68.8 AP) despite having fewer parameters (172M vs. 218M), demonstrating the benefit of language-guided design. On COCO zero-shot, Grounding DINO L achieves 52.5 AP, beating GLIP-L (49.8 AP) by +2.7 AP and DINO-Swin-T (46.2 AP) by +6.3 AP. On LVIS zero-shot, Grounding DINO T (27.4 AP) outperforms GLIP-T (26.0 AP) but trails DetCLIPv2 (40.4 AP), which benefits from a much larger training corpus (CC15M). On RefCOCO/+/g with fine-tuning, Grounding DINO L (90.56/82.75/86.13 val) sets new state-of-the-art, surpassing DQ-DETR (88.63/81.66/82.76) and MDETR (86.75/79.52/81.64) by 1.6–5.5 points across splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

Grounding DINO is built directly on DINO's DETR-based architecture (denoising anchor boxes, deformable attention, Hungarian matching) and adopts GLIP's grounded pre-training paradigm that reformulates detection as phrase grounding with contrastive region-text loss. Its key departure from prior open-set detectors (GLIP, OV-DETR, ViLD) is performing tight cross-modality fusion across all three architectural phases—neck (feature enhancer), query initialization (language-guided selection), and head (cross-modality decoder)—rather than the single or dual-phase fusion used by previous methods. The sub-sentence text representation further refines GLIP's word-level approach by using attention masks to prevent unrelated category names from interacting during feature extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that multi-phase tight fusion is a principled design choice for open-set detection, yielding consistent gains across zero-shot COCO (52.5 AP), ODinW (26.1 AP), and REC benchmarks without requiring COCO training data. It also highlights an underexplored evaluation axis—referring expression comprehension—showing that existing open-set detectors perform poorly on attribute-rich queries without explicit REC data, a gap the community should address. Limitations include the inability to perform segmentation (unlike GLIPv2), occasional hallucinated detections, and training data scale still below the largest GLIP variants, leaving open whether further scaling or architectural changes can close the remaining gap to fully supervised detectors.
