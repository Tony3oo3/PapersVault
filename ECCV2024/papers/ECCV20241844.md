## Problem

Efficient 2D human pose estimation remains challenging, especially for whole-body estimation with 133 keypoints, where CNN-based methods struggle to capture long-range keypoint correlations and Transformer-based methods introduce significant redundancy by encoding all keypoints simultaneously. The core gap is how to fully exploit the Transformer's dynamic attention and variable-length input advantages while minimizing computational overhead as the number of keypoints grows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241844/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a cropped human RGB image at 256×192 resolution, processed through a shallow ShuffleNet V2 backbone into a feature map that is uniformly divided into non-overlapping patches to form visual tokens. Output is structured as 1D horizontal and vertical heatmaps (following SimCC), where keypoint localization is treated as a classification problem over discretized coordinate bins rather than direct regression. The pipeline is two-stage top-down: a person detector (56.4% AP) first localizes the human, which is then cropped for keypoint estimation.

## Method

GTPT introduces keypoints in a coarse-to-fine manner: a single human token in shallow layers expands into sparse body keypoint tokens and five part tokens (face, both hands, both feet), which are later converted into dense keypoint tokens, progressively reducing the token count in early layers. Keypoints are grouped into head, upper body, and lower body, with group-specific channel-attention masks adapting visual tokens to each group's spatial needs, and group-based pruning that removes more background tokens for dense regions (face) while retaining more for sparse regions (lower body). Multi-Head Group Attention (MHGA) computes attention in parallel within each group while sharing sparse keypoint tokens as keys/values across groups to capture global inter-group relationships at negligible extra cost. Training uses a Global Perceived Loss that distills knowledge from the unpruned model into the pruned model to prevent local optimality, combined with curriculum learning that first trains on 17 body keypoints before introducing the full 133-keypoint whole-body task.

## Results

On COCO-WholeBody (133 keypoints, 256×192 input), GTPT-B achieves 61.7 whole-body AP with only 4.0 GFLOPs, outperforming RTMPose-l (61.1 AP at 4.5 GFLOPs) and approaching ZoomNAS (65.4 AP at 18.0 GFLOPs) with 78% fewer FLOPs. On COCO (17 keypoints, 256×192 input), GTPT-B reaches 74.9 AP at 3.6 GFLOPs, matching HRNet-W48 (75.1 AP) while using only 25% of its computation. The smallest variant GTPT-T achieves 54.9 whole-body AP at just 0.8 GFLOPs, surpassing ViTPose+-S (54.4 AP) with 85% fewer FLOPs, all without any pre-training on large-scale datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTPT consistently outperforms same-compute baselines: GTPT-S (2.0 GFLOPs, 59.6 AP) beats RTMPose-m (2.2 GFLOPs, 58.2 AP) by 1.4 AP on whole-body, and GTPT-B (4.0 GFLOPs, 61.7 AP) beats RTMPose-l (4.5 GFLOPs, 61.1 AP) by 0.6 AP. On COCO, GTPT-S (1.6 GFLOPs, 73.6 AP) surpasses EfficientPose-C (1.6 GFLOPs, 71.3 AP) by 2.3 AP at identical FLOPs, and outperforms both PPT-S (2.0 GFLOPs, 72.2 AP) and TokenPose-S-v1 (2.4 GFLOPs, 72.5 AP) by 1.4 and 1.1 AP respectively while using fewer FLOPs. GTPT-B (3.6 GFLOPs, 74.9 AP) matches HRNet-W48 (14.6 GFLOPs, 75.1 AP) within 0.2 AP at only 25% of the computation. The smallest variant GTPT-T (0.8 GFLOPs, 54.9 AP) exceeds ViTPose+-S (5.4 GFLOPs, 54.4 AP) by 0.5 AP with 85% fewer FLOPs. However, GTPT-B still trails the strongest pre-trained baselines ZoomNAS (65.4 AP) and ZoomNet (63.0 AP) by 3.7 and 1.3 AP respectively, though at a fraction of their computational cost (4.0 vs. 18.0 and 28.5 GFLOPs).

## Relation to Prior Work

GTPT builds directly on TokenPose's dual-tokenization paradigm (keypoint tokens + visual tokens) and SimCC's coordinate-classification head, while departing from PPT's uniform token pruning by introducing group-based pruning with a global perceived loss to prevent the pruned model from losing global context. The coarse-to-fine keypoint introduction strategy (human token → sparse keypoints → part tokens → dense keypoints) is a novel architectural departure from prior Transformer-based HPE methods that feed all keypoint tokens from the start. The Multi-Head Group Attention mechanism, which uses shared sparse keypoint tokens as global context in the key/value computation, is a new design that addresses the inter-group interaction problem created by the grouping strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTPT offers a practical recipe for efficient whole-body pose estimation (133 keypoints) where the density imbalance between body parts (face: many keypoints in a small area; lower body: few keypoints in a large area) makes uniform token budgets wasteful. The group-based pruning with per-group masking and MHGA provides a clean, modular design that practitioners can adapt to other dense-keypoint or multi-region tasks. Limitations include reliance on a two-stage top-down pipeline, random initialization without large-scale pre-training, and evaluation restricted to COCO/COCO-WholeBody; whether the coarse-to-fine and grouping strategies transfer to other domains (e.g., animal pose, hand-only) remains open.
