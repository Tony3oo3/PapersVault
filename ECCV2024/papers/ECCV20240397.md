## Problem

Existing dual-modal salient object detection models assume high-quality, complete inputs and degrade significantly when faced with noisy images or missing modalities in real-world deployment. No prior work simultaneously addresses robustness to both noisy inputs and modality-missing conditions while preserving accuracy under ideal inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240397/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of dual-modal images (RGB + Thermal or RGB + Depth) along with a fixed text prompt ('A photo of high quality.') fed into a frozen CLIP text encoder. During Stage II training, one modality is randomly replaced with a zero tensor to simulate missing conditions. The output is a binary salient object mask produced by a simple decoder that applies CBAM, convolution, and upsampling across five feature levels.

## Method

CoLA is a two-stage training framework. In Stage I, a Language-driven Quality Assessment (LQA) module computes per-modality quality scores by measuring cosine similarity between CLIP image embeddings and a text embedding augmented with a single learnable prompt vector ω; these scores reweight the layer-wise fusion of dual-modal features before decoding. In Stage II, Conditional Dropout freezes the Stage I encoder, duplicates it as a trainable copy, and connects the two via zero-initialized convolutions; training randomly samples from three conditions (both modalities present, RGB missing, Thermal missing), forcing the trainable copy to extract modality-specific features while the frozen branch preserves complete-modality knowledge. The zero convolution ensures the new branch contributes minimally at initialization, progressively integrating enriched single-modality representations without disrupting the original model.

## Results

On VT821, CoLA achieves the best Average Em of 0.895 and Average Fβ of 0.806 across all three modality conditions, with the smallest Average Drop (Em: −0.040, Fβ: −0.065) compared to all baselines. On VT1000, CoLA reaches Average Em of 0.943 with the lowest degradation (Drop Em: −0.018, Drop Fβ: −0.027). On VT5000, CoLA attains Average Em of 0.909 and Average Fβ of 0.813, again with the smallest performance drop (Drop Em: −0.027, Drop Fβ: −0.045). Under modality-complete conditions, CoLA also leads on VT821 (Em=0.922, Fβ=0.849) and VT1000 (Em=0.955, Fβ=0.904).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLA outperforms all seven RGB-T baselines on both Average and Average Drop metrics across VT821, VT1000, and VT5000. The largest gaps are against CSRNet (Drop Em: −0.186 vs. −0.040 on VT821) and ADF (Drop Em: −0.139 vs. −0.040), which suffer severe degradation when either modality is missing. Among the more robust competitors, TAGFNet is the closest rival (Drop Em: −0.049 on VT821, −0.025 on VT1000), yet CoLA still leads by 0.009–0.007 in Drop Em and 0.011–0.011 in Drop Fβ. DCNet shows asymmetric robustness (good when Thermal is missing but poor when RGB is missing), while TNet and MIDD remain competitive on VT1000 but trail CoLA by 0.02–0.04 in Average Em. For quality assessment (Table 3), LQA surpasses BRISQUE, GIE, and both CLIP-IQA variants on all three datasets, e.g., on VT1000 LQA achieves Fβ=0.904 vs. CLIP-IQA+ at 0.874 and GIE at 0.880. The Conditional Dropout module is also validated as a plug-in: when applied to LSNet and TNet, it significantly reduces modality-missing degradation while preserving modality-complete performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLA sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) vision-language models for image quality assessment, where it adapts CLIP's frozen encoders with a learnable prompt (following CoOp [59] and CLIP-IQA [48]) to reweight modality contributions without quality annotations; and (2) conditional control from text-to-image diffusion [55], whose freeze-original + trainable-copy + zero-convolution paradigm is repurposed here as a training strategy for modality-missing robustness. It departs from prior quality-aware SOD methods (GIE [9], pseudo-label approaches [4,6]) by making quality estimation dataset-adaptive and parameter-efficient, and from direct modality dropout [51] by preserving complete-modality performance through the frozen encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: dual-modal SOD systems in the wild face sensor noise and modality dropout, yet prior models assume ideal, complete inputs. The Conditional Dropout module's plug-in nature (demonstrated on LSNet and TNet) makes it immediately applicable to existing dual-modal SOD architectures without redesign. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two modalities, whether CLIP-based quality assessment remains reliable under severe corruption, and whether the two-stage training protocol generalizes to other multi-modal tasks beyond SOD.
