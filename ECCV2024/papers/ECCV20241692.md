## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation models are limited by severe data scarcity (the largest 3D motion dataset, AMASS, contains only ~11k motions compared to tens of millions of videos in other domains) and by requiring the duration of each atomic action as explicit input, which hampers usability and generalization to open-vocabulary descriptions. Prior approaches also struggle with unseen actions and lengthy, semantically rich descriptions composed of multiple atomic actions.

## Input / Output

The model accepts free-form natural language descriptions of arbitrary length (potentially containing multiple atomic actions with transitions) and produces a full-body 3D motion sequence without requiring per-action duration specifications; it also supports the inverse task (Motion2Text) where a 3D motion sequence yields a natural language description. Motions are internally represented as discrete VQ-VAE codebook tokens, and the model autonomously determines generation length via special end-of-motion tokens, analogous to end-of-sentence tokens in language models. Textual inputs are first augmented by GPT-4 to increase semantic diversity before tokenization through the T5 embedding table.

## Method

TMT formulates text-conditioned motion generation as a bi-directional language translation problem, treating both 3D motions and text as discrete tokens processed by a shared T5 transformer backbone. A Motion VQ-VAE (pretrained on the newly constructed LaViMo dataset of 140k+ clips extracted from in-the-wild videos via a HybrIK-initialized HuMoR pipeline, plus AMASS) discretizes motions into codebook tokens, while text is tokenized through the standard T5 embedding table. Training employs four multi-task objectives—Text2Motion, Motion2Text, Text2Text (15% masking), and Motion2Motion (50% masking)—with task-specific adaptive masks and a <task> token, which jointly improve robustness, motion modeling, and semantic understanding. GPT-4 is used to generate semantically similar alternative descriptions for each atomic action, filtered by heuristics checking body-part consistency, static/dynamic classification, and directional semantics, enabling robust open-vocabulary generation. Contextual continuity between atomic actions is maintained by inserting the last k motion tokens from the preceding action or a <SOM> token, with <EOAM> and <EOM> tokens marking action and sequence boundaries.

## Results

On the BABEL benchmark, TMT achieves the best R-Precision (0.53) and MultiModal-Distance (5.88) among all compared methods, outperforming the runner-up PriorMDM (0.48 R-Precision, 6.97 MultiModal-Dist). On APE/AVE metrics (Table 3), TMT is on average 33% better than the runner-up (PriorMDM) across all eight sub-metrics, e.g., APEr drops from 0.388 to 0.237 and APEg from 0.464 to 0.285. On HumanML3D, TMT achieves the highest R-Precision (0.528) and competitive FID (0.184), matching or exceeding diffusion-based methods like MLD and PriorMDM. Ablating LaViMo pretraining degrades performance on 9 of 12 BABEL metrics, confirming the dataset's contribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TMT outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary BABEL benchmark. Against the strongest competitor PriorMDM (a diffusion-based prior), TMT improves R-Precision from 0.48 to 0.53 and MultiModal-Distance from 6.97 to 5.88, while also achieving a lower FID (0.72 vs. 0.79); the only metric where PriorMDM edges ahead is Diversity (8.16 vs. 8.33). On the more granular APE/AVE metrics (Table 3), TMT dominates across all eight sub-metrics, with APEr improving by 39% (0.237 vs. 0.388) and AVEg by 12% (0.153 vs. 0.174) over PriorMDM. On HumanML3D, TMT achieves the best R-Precision (0.528) but trails T2M-GPT on FID (0.184 vs. 0.116) and PriorMDM on MultiModal-Distance (3.091 vs. 2.96), indicating that on simpler single-sentence descriptions the advantage narrows. The ablation 'Ours w/o LaViMo' still beats most baselines on 9 of 12 BABEL metrics, isolating the model architecture's contribution from the dataset's. The LLM augmentation without post-processing actually degrades APE/AVE (e.g., APEr rises to 0.349), confirming that the filtering heuristics are essential for maintaining quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

TMT departs from the dominant VAE/diffusion/GAN paradigm in Text2Motion by reframing the problem as discrete-token language translation, drawing on the NMT tradition where both source and target are token sequences processed by a shared transformer (T5). Motion tokenization via VQ-VAE echoes TM2T and MotionGPT, but TMT's key conceptual departure is the multi-task regularization (Text2Text, Motion2Motion, Text2Motion, Motion2Text) and the LLM-based semantic augmentation pipeline, neither of which appears in prior Text2Motion work. The bi-directional formulation and the elimination of per-action duration inputs distinguish it from sequential-composition methods like TEACH and EMS, which require explicit temporal segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LaViMo dataset (140k+ clips, ~3.3× AMASS) extracted from in-the-wild video via 3D pose estimation addresses the field's most cited bottleneck—data scarcity—and is likely to become a standard pretraining resource. The LLM augmentation + heuristic filtering pipeline offers a practical recipe for open-vocabulary robustness that other motion-generation systems could adopt. However, the authors acknowledge a persistent quality gap relative to text-to-image and text-to-video generation, and the model still struggles with descriptions far outside the pretraining distribution, leaving generalization as an open problem. Whether the discrete-token translation framing scales to even larger, more diverse motion corpora remains to be seen.
