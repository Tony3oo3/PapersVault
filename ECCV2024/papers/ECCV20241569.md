## Problem

Existing rolling shutter bundle adjustment (RSBA) methods rely exclusively on sparse point features, which are fragile in degenerate environments common in man-made scenes. Although lines encode richer structural information and are more robust, the rolling shutter effect curves the projection of 3D straight lines, making it non-trivial to adapt line-based bundle adjustment to the RS camera model. No prior work has addressed line-based RSBA or identified the degeneracies specific to this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241569/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes rolling shutter images with detected line features, where RS curves are segmented into short-line segments and fitted to obtain 2D curve observations (point coordinates and tangent directions). It jointly optimizes camera poses (R, t), per-frame instantaneous motion (angular velocity ω, translational velocity d), and 3D line coordinates parameterized as 4-DoF Plücker matrices, solved via Levenberg-Marquardt. The 4-DoF Plücker representation is notable for enabling unconstrained optimization on spatial lines, in contrast to the 5-DoF over-parameterized representation used in prior RS line work.

## Method

RSL-BA derives the rolling shutter line projection by combining the Plücker matrix with the time-varying RS projection matrix, showing that a 3D line projects to a cubic polynomial curve whose coefficients are explicit functions of camera pose and motion parameters. On top of this curve model, the paper defines three reprojection error terms—perpendicular distance, horizontal distance, and tangent direction error—and combines them into two weighted variants (e₁ = e_d1 + λ₁e_t, e₂ = e_d2 + λ₂e_t). The key insight is that the tangent error term is essential for degeneracy resistance: while distance errors alone vanish under plane and X-Y pure translation degeneracies, the tangent constraint produces indeterminate 0/0 forms that break the degenerate solution. The paper provides theoretical proofs that RSL-BA resists three degeneracies (plane, 2-view pure translation, and a newly discovered X-Y pure translation case), and experiments on WHU-RSVI and TUM-RSVI show accuracy comparable to the best point-based RSBA (NWRSBA) with equivalent runtime.

## Results

On the WHU-RSVI synthetic dataset, RSL-BA achieves the best ATE in 3 out of 4 sequences (0.0393, 0.0234, 0.0207) and is second-best in the remaining one (0.0444 vs. NWRSBA's 0.0406). On the real TUM-RSVI dataset, RSL-BA achieves the best ATE in 3 out of 10 sequences (seq1: 0.0378, seq3: 0.0482, seq8: 0.0250) and is consistently competitive with NWRSBA, trailing it by less than 0.005 in most sequences. The paper also demonstrates that RSL-BA suppresses three degeneracy modes (plane, 2-view pure translation, and X-Y pure translation) where point-based NMRSBA collapses to degenerate solutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSL-BA is compared against two global-shutter methods (GSBA [Lourakis, TOMS 2009] and GLBA [Taylor, TPAMI 1995]) and two rolling-shutter methods (NMRSBA [Albl, ECCV 2016] and NWRSBA [Liao, CVPR 2023]). On WHU-RSVI, RSL-BA clearly outperforms all four baselines in 3 of 4 sequences and is second only to NWRSBA in one, with ATE improvements of up to 45% over GSBA. On TUM-RSVI, RSL-BA beats GSBA, GLBA, and NMRSBA in 8–10 out of 10 sequences but is slightly behind NWRSBA in 7 of 10 sequences (by margins of 0.001–0.042 ATE), which the authors attribute to the TUM-RSVI dataset lacking prominent line features. In the degeneracy-resistance experiments (Fig. 5), RSL-BA successfully suppresses both plane and X-Y pure-translation degeneracies where NMRSBA converges to a degenerate solution, while NWRSBA is not tested in those specific degenerate configurations. Computationally, RSL-BA matches NWRSBA in runtime, confirming that line-based optimization does not introduce significant overhead over the point-based counterpart.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: rolling shutter bundle adjustment (RSBA), pioneered by Albl et al. [1] and refined by Liao et al. [14], and line-based bundle adjustment, rooted in Bartoli & Sturm's Plücker-parameterized framework [2]. The key departure is that all prior RSBA methods operate exclusively on point features, while all prior line-based BA assumes global shutter cameras; this paper is the first to derive the rolling shutter line projection theory and formulate line-based reprojection errors under the RS model. The degeneracy analysis extends Albl et al.'s plane-degeneracy proof to line features and introduces a previously unknown X-Y pure translation degeneracy, positioning the work as both a theoretical extension and a practical bridge between the two communities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first line-based RSBA formulation, this paper provides the foundational optimization kernel for future line-based or point-line-hybrid RS-SfM and RS-SLAM pipelines, which are particularly valuable in man-made environments where lines encode stronger structural constraints than sparse points. The demonstration that line features can suppress degeneracies (including the newly discovered X-Y pure translation case) that point-based RSBA cannot is a meaningful robustness gain. However, the practical advantage is modest: on TUM-RSVI, where line features are not visually prominent, RSL-BA is slightly weaker than NWRSBA, suggesting the method's benefit is environment-dependent. Open questions include how to efficiently integrate line detection and matching into a full RS-SfM pipeline and whether the degeneracy-resistance advantage persists at scale with thousands of lines.
