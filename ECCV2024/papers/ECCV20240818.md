## Problem

Existing omnidirectional image super-resolution (ODISR) methods are predominantly end-to-end trained, producing deterministic outputs with inferior visual realness and poor out-of-domain generalization. While diffusion models offer strong image priors for restoration, naively applying planar diffusion-based SR to tangent projections of ERP images causes pixel-level discontinuities in overlapping regions when reprojected, compromising global continuity. No prior method has achieved zero-shot ODISR by leveraging pretrained diffusion priors while maintaining both fidelity and perceptual quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240818/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution ERP omnidirectional image (bicubic-downsampled from 1024×2048) and produces a high-resolution ERP image at 1024×2048, evaluated at ×2 and ×4 scales. The input is first bicubic-upsampled to target resolution before being projected onto 18 tangent planes via gnomonic projection; the 18 super-resolved TP images are then fused back into a single ERP output. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, requiring no training or fine-tuning on any omnidirectional data.

## Method

OmniSSR builds on StableSR's pretrained time-aware adapter and Controllable Feature Wrapping module (all kept untrained) to perform iterative latent diffusion denoising on tangent-projection patches. The key architectural contribution is Octadecaplex Tangent Information Interaction (OTII), which at every denoising step transforms intermediate results back and forth between ERP and 18 TP images, fusing information across adjacent tangent planes to enforce global consistency and bridge the domain gap between omnidirectional and planar distributions. A Gradient Decomposition (GD) correction, derived as an approximate analytical solution of gradient descent on the data-fidelity term, is applied at each step by decomposing the update into a fidelity component (γA†E_init) and a realness component ((I−γA†A)E_0|t), with a hyperparameter γ balancing the two. A pre-upsampling strategy before each projection transformation mitigates the information loss inherent in discrete pixel interpolation during ERP↔TP conversion.

## Results

On the ODI-SR dataset at ×2 scale, OmniSSR achieves WS-PSNR 28.57, WS-SSIM 0.8540, FID 13.01, and LPIPS 0.1575, outperforming all zero-shot diffusion-based baselines (DDRM, DPS, GDP, PSLD, DiffIR, StableSR) on every metric. At ×4 scale, OmniSSR reaches WS-PSNR 25.77 and FID 30.97 on ODI-SR, and WS-PSNR 26.01 and FID 34.58 on SUN 360 Panorama, again leading all compared methods. Against supervised end-to-end ODISR methods (SwinIR, OSRT) at ×4, OmniSSR trades ~1 dB WS-PSNR for substantially better perceptual quality (FID 30.97 vs. 27.39 for OSRT; DISTS 0.1541 vs. 0.1695).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSSR outperforms all six zero-shot diffusion-based baselines (DDRM, DPS, GDP, PSLD, DiffIR, StableSR) on every metric at both ×2 and ×4 scales across both datasets. The closest competitor is DDRM, which achieves FID 12.28 at ×2 on ODI-SR (slightly better than OmniSSR's 13.01) but trails on WS-PSNR (27.90 vs. 28.57) and LPIPS (0.1661 vs. 0.1575); at ×4, OmniSSR clearly leads DDRM on all four metrics. DPS and GDP perform poorly, with DPS producing FID values above 120 at ×4, indicating severe distribution mismatch. Against supervised end-to-end ODISR methods (SwinIR, OSRT) at ×4, OmniSSR sacrifices ~1 dB WS-PSNR (25.77 vs. 26.89 for OSRT on ODI-SR) but achieves better perceptual quality on FID (30.97 vs. 27.39), LPIPS (0.2977 vs. 0.3258), NIQE (5.29 vs. 5.44), and DISTS (0.1541 vs. 0.1695), demonstrating that the zero-shot diffusion prior yields more realistic textures despite lower pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-prior-based super-resolution paradigm (DDRM, DPS, GDP, StableSR) from planar images to omnidirectional images, which had previously been addressed exclusively by end-to-end supervised networks (SphereSR, OSRT, SwinIR). The key architectural departure is the Octadecaplex Tangent Information Interaction (OTII), which iteratively shuttles intermediate results between ERP and tangent-projection formats at every denoising step, effectively repurposing a pretrained planar StableSR backbone without any ODI-specific training. The Gradient Decomposition correction draws on the convex-optimization / gradient-descent lineage of DDNM and DPS but applies it in the ERP domain to enforce data fidelity, distinguishing it from prior work that either ignores the omnidirectional geometry or handles it through learned distortion-aware modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniSSR demonstrates that strong generative priors from Stable Diffusion can substitute for task-specific training in omnidirectional SR, opening a path to zero-shot ODI restoration that avoids the high cost of collecting paired high-resolution panoramic data. The OTII mechanism is a reusable bridge between spherical and planar image domains that could transfer to other ODI tasks (inpainting, editing, depth estimation). However, the ~14-minute per-image inference time and the need for 18 tangent-plane conversions per denoising step significantly limit real-time applicability, and the fidelity gap versus supervised methods (e.g., ~1 dB lower WS-PSNR at ×4) remains a practical concern. Open questions include adaptive gradient coefficients, extension to omnidirectional video, and whether the ERP↔TP interaction can be made more efficient.
