## Problem

Current Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs) are limited to localizing a single, pre-existing object and cannot detect multiple objects, refuse non-existing targets, or handle multi-category prompts simultaneously. Existing work either invokes external visual expert models via APIs or adds specialized detection/segmentation heads, breaking the unified LVLM structure and preventing generalization to unseen categories through LLM knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241126/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 448×448 with bilinear interpolation of position embeddings) and a free-form text prompt that can range from a single referent to multiple category names of varying granularity. Outputs are generated autoregressively as a sequence of 'label-[x1,y1,x2,y2]' tokens with normalized coordinates at 0.001 precision, multiple objects concatenated with '&', and a 'None' token for non-existing targets. A training-free confidence score is computed per detected object from the product of conditional probabilities of its label and coordinate tokens.

## Method

Griffon builds on the LLaVA architecture (ViT-L/14 CLIP encoder, a fully-connected projection layer, and Llama2-13B) without introducing any special tokens, detection heads, or external expert models. It unifies all four localization scenarios (single referent, one category with multiple objects, non-existing referent, multi-category) under a single conversational input-output format where the label precedes the coordinates to increase recall. Training follows a progressive two-stage pipeline: Stage I pre-trains the entire network on ~6M prompted samples (from Visual Genome and Objects365) to build multi-object perception, and Stage II instruction-tunes the LLM and projector on ~900K scenario-specific samples (including Flickr30K Entities and LVIS negatives) to align with user intent. A training-free confidence scoring mechanism ranks predicted objects by the geometric mean of label and localization conditional probabilities, improving multi-object matching without additional training.

## Results

On the RefCOCO series (REC task), Griffon-13B* (trained with REC data only) achieves 90.1/93.4/86.1 ACC@0.5 on RefCOCO val/test-A/test-B, surpassing Qwen-VL (89.4/92.3/85.3) with only 4M training samples versus Qwen-VL's 77M. On Flickr30K Entities (phrase grounding), Griffon achieves 83.7/84.2 under the ANY-BOX protocol, setting a new state-of-the-art. On MSCOCO object detection, Griffon reaches 24.8 mAP with a single forward pass, approaching Faster R-CNN (26.3 mAP) and outperforming it on small-object AP_S (5.9 vs. 4.6) at the same 448 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among generalist LVLMs, Griffon-13B* (REC-only training) outperforms Qwen-VL on all RefCOCO metrics (e.g., 93.4 vs. 92.3 on test-A) while using only 4M samples versus Qwen-VL's 77M, and also beats Ferret-13B and Shikra-13B by 1–3 points across the board. Against specialist detectors, Griffon-13B* still trails G-DINO-L and UNINEXT-L on RefCOCO test-B (86.1 vs. 88.2/88.9) but narrows the gap to under 2 points. On Flickr30K Entities, Griffon achieves the best ANY-BOX score (83.7 val / 84.2 test), surpassing MDETR (82.5/83.4) and Ferret (not reported for ANY), though it slightly trails MDETR on MERGED-BOXES test (82.8 vs. 83.8). On MSCOCO detection, Griffon (24.8 mAP) is close to Faster R-CNN (26.3 mAP) at the same 448 resolution and notably exceeds it on small-object AP_S (5.9 vs. 4.6), while decisively outperforming DETR-DC5 (15.5 mAP) by nearly 10 points. Critically, existing LVLMs like Qwen-VL and Ferret cannot perform multi-category detection in a single pass (mAP ≈ 0), making Griffon the first generalist to approach specialist-level detection without external modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

Griffon builds directly on LLaVA's architecture (CLIP ViT-L/14 + Llama2-13B + projection layer) but departs fundamentally from prior LVLMs like Qwen-VL, Shikra, and KOSMOS-2, which are limited to single-referent localization and require repeated forward passes for multiple objects. It also rejects the two dominant strategies in localization-equipped LVLMs—invoking external expert models via APIs (HuggingGPT, AutoGPT) or attaching specialized detection/segmentation heads (VisionLLM, GPT4RoI, LISA)—instead treating all localization as pure autoregressive text generation with a unified 'label-[x1,y1,x2,y2]' output format. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a comprehensive Language-prompted Localization Dataset covering four scenarios (single referent, one-category-multi-object, non-existing referent, multi-category-multi-object), which prior REC-focused training data did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a structurally unmodified LVLM can approach Faster R-CNN detection performance (24.8 vs. 26.3 mAP at 448 resolution) without any detection head, special tokens, or external models, challenging the prevailing assumption that fine-grained spatial localization requires dedicated architectural components. The released 6M+900K sample dataset is a practical resource for the community. However, limitations remain: the model still trails the best specialist detectors (G-DINO-L) on REC benchmarks, experiments are confined to localization tasks (no segmentation or other vision tasks), and the training-free confidence scoring, while effective, adds inference complexity. An open question is whether this unified autoregressive paradigm can be extended to richer tasks like segmentation or video grounding without the same architectural purity.
