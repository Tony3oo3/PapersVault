## Problem

Self-supervised 3D human pose estimation relies on reprojection consistency, but suffers from two inherent limitations: (1) a mismatched supervision problem where orthographic projection is used to map predicted 3D poses to 2D while the 2D supervision comes from perspective projection, creating an offset between prediction and supervision spaces; and (2) a Z-axis overall reverse problem where the reprojection loss cannot constrain absolute 3D orientation, allowing a fully reversed pose to still produce consistent 2D projections.

## Input / Output

The method takes scale- and position-normalized 2D poses (x ∈ R^{2×J}) from K training views as input, discarding absolute position and scale information. It outputs a 3D pose X_c^i ∈ R^{3×J} in a learned canonical coordinate system along with a rotation R_{c→i} ∈ R^{3×3} (parameterized as 6D continuous rotation) that transforms the canonical frame to each camera frame. At inference, the rotation compensation is not applied, so the network produces the 3D pose directly without any additional geometric correction.

## Method

The framework follows CanonPose's multi-view cross-reprojection setup with a SemGCN PoseNet and MLP CameraNet, but introduces three key modifications. First, Rotated Orthographic Projection (ROP) computes a rotation compensation matrix R_{o→p} from the reference point's offset from the image center (using arctangent of the offset over an assumed focal length), rotating the 3D pose so the object-camera axis aligns with the optical axis before orthographic projection, thereby geometrically approximating perspective projection. Second, Group Rotated Orthographic Projection (G-ROP) partitions the skeleton into 7 groups by relative joint distance (e.g., right lower limb, left upper limb, torso) and computes a separate reference point and rotation per group, reducing over- or under-compensation for extremities. Third, a joint reverse loss penalizes configurations where the angle between the local reference plane normal (formed by hip-hip-knee or shoulder-shoulder-elbow) and the distal bone (shin or forearm) falls within [0°, 90°−d], explicitly forbidding Z-axis-reversed poses that reprojection alone cannot detect.

## Results

On Human3.6M with 2D ground truth input, the proposed method achieves 42.9 mm N-MPJPE and 33.2 mm P-MPJPE, reducing the mean error by 34.9% over the previous SOTA self-supervised method CanonPose (65.9 mm N-MPJPE). With predicted 2D poses from CPN, it reaches 55.2 mm N-MPJPE and 44.9 mm P-MPJPE, still outperforming all self-supervised and weakly-supervised baselines. On MPII-INF-3DHP, the method achieves 75.0 mm N-MPJPE, 44.8 mm P-MPJPE, and 89.9% PCK, surpassing all prior self-supervised approaches. Notably, the gap between N-MPJPE and P-MPJPE shrinks to under 10 mm, indicating the method corrects the global rotation error that plagued prior reprojection-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline is CanonPose [34], on which the method is built; it reduces N-MPJPE from 65.9 mm to 42.9 mm (34.9% improvement) and P-MPJPE from 51.4 mm to 33.2 mm on Human3.6M with GT 2D input. Against the most recent SOTA self-supervised method MHCanonNet [16], the improvement is 26.9% in N-MPJPE (58.7 → 42.9 mm). The method also outperforms ElePose [32] (64.0 → 42.9 mm N-MPJPE), Iqbal [13] (64.5 → 42.9 mm), and PoseTriplet [9] (68.2 → 42.9 mm) on Human3.6M. On MPII-INF-3DHP, it beats CanonPose (104.0 → 75.0 mm N-MPJPE), MHCanonNet (102.1 → 75.0 mm), and ElePose (54.0 → 44.8 mm P-MPJPE). The method slightly trails fully supervised approaches like SemGCN [44] (42.14 mm) and PCLs [40] (40.1 mm) in N-MPJPE, but matches or exceeds them in P-MPJPE (33.2 vs. 33.53 and no P-MPJPE reported for PCLs), demonstrating that the self-supervised framework now closes most of the gap to full supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CanonPose framework (Wandt et al., CVPR 2021), which established multi-view cross-reprojection consistency in a canonical coordinate system as the backbone for self-supervised 3D human pose estimation. Its key departure is identifying and correcting two previously overlooked limitations of reprojection consistency: the geometric mismatch between the orthographic projection used for prediction and the perspective projection underlying the 2D supervision signal, and the unconstrained Z-axis reversal ambiguity. Rather than learning camera parameters or relying on additional supervision (as in CLIFF, SPEC, or bone-length constraints), the paper introduces a purely geometric correction—rotated orthographic projection (ROP) and its grouped variant (G-ROP)—that approximates perspective projection by rotating the 3D pose before orthographic projection, plus a joint reverse loss that encodes skeletal kinematics priors to eliminate the Z-axis flip.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a large portion of the error in self-supervised 3D pose estimation stems from a systematic projection-model mismatch (evidenced by the persistent N-MPJPE vs. P-MPJPE gap in prior methods) rather than from fundamental depth ambiguity is a useful diagnostic that reframes how the community should interpret residual errors. The 34.9% reduction in mean error over the prior SOTA self-supervised method, achieved with a training-only geometric correction that requires no additional data or inference-time computation, makes the technique broadly adoptable. Open questions include whether the geometric approximation can be made exact without camera parameters, whether the approach extends to single-view training settings, and whether the joint reverse constraint generalizes to more complex articulations beyond elbows and knees.
