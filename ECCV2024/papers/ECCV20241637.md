## Problem

Traditional visual data mining methods rely on pairwise similarity comparisons between all visual elements in a dataset, causing computational cost to scale quadratically and limiting them to small, curated collections. This paper asks whether generative models, which implicitly encode a summary of their training data in their weights, can be repurposed as scalable mining tools that identify typical visual elements without explicit pairwise matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled image dataset (e.g., 10K–1.8M images) where each image carries a text tag such as a decade, country name, scene category, or disease label; tags are converted to CLIP text embeddings for conditioning. Images are rescaled to dataset-specific resolutions (256 px height for cars, 256×256 for faces, 512×756 for street view, ≥512 px for Places). The primary output is a pixel-wise typicality map per image, from which the top 1000 most typical non-overlapping patches are extracted and clustered into 32 k-means groups using DIFT features; a secondary output is a sequence of translated patches across class labels ranked by co-typicality.

## Method

The core mechanism is a pixel-wise typicality score defined as the expected difference in denoising loss between null-conditioned and label-conditioned generation: T(x|c) = E_{ε,t}[L_t(x,ε,∅) − L_t(x,ε,c)], computed over t ∈ [0.1, 0.7] and Gaussian noise. A Stable Diffusion V1.5 latent diffusion model is finetuned on the target dataset with CLIP text features as conditioning, so that the model's reconstruction quality becomes sensitive to dataset-specific label correlations. Typicality is aggregated over image patches, the top 1000 patches are selected, and they are clustered with k-means using DIFT features extracted at t = 0.161 from the finetuned model. For cross-label trend analysis, Plug-and-Play diffusion translation generates a parallel dataset of images translated across all class labels, and a co-typicality measure (median typicality over locations) ranks sequences of translated patches, which are then clustered after UMAP projection of DIFT features.

## Results

The paper's main visual mining results are qualitative, demonstrating that the typicality-based approach identifies semantically meaningful, class-specific visual elements (e.g., aviator glasses in the 1920s, French roof windows, American post boxes) across four datasets ranging from 10K to 1.8M images. The only quantitative evaluation is in the medical imaging application (ChestX-ray8), where finetuning the diffusion model improves disease localization AUC-PR from 3.2% (pretrained) to 9.6% (finetuned), with per-disease gains ranging from +3.5% (Pneumonothorax: 3%→6%) to +14.6% (Mass: 2%→16.6%). The method scales to datasets an order of magnitude larger than prior visual mining work without requiring pairwise element comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Doersch et al. [12] ('What Makes Paris Look Like Paris?'), a discriminative correspondence-based mining method. The authors reimplemented it in Python and applied it to the same G^3 street-view subset. Qualitatively, the proposed diffusion-based method produces clusters that are 'much more semantic' and contain 'much less similar elements' than Doersch et al., whose results 'demonstrate more textures' and appear 'much less semantic.' The Doersch et al. method also requires training a separate linear SVM detector per visual element, making it computationally prohibitive at scale, whereas the diffusion approach avoids pairwise comparisons entirely. In the medical imaging setting, the comparison is against the unfinetuned pretrained Stable Diffusion V1.5 model: finetuning on ChestX-ray8 consistently improves AUC-PR across all 7 diseases (overall 3.2%→9.6%), confirming that dataset-specific finetuning is critical for meaningful localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion V1.5) as data mining tools by defining a pixel-wise typicality score from the reconstruction loss gap between conditioned and unconditioned denoising, a direct pixel-level adaptation of the image-level classification insight in Li et al. (ICCV 2023). It departs from the dominant correspondence-based visual mining paradigm (Doersch et al. 2015; Lee et al. 2013) that requires quadratic pairwise similarity computation, and from prior generative-model approaches to temporal analysis (Chen et al. 2023; Dalens et al. 2019) that perform image translation without extracting typical elements. The method combines this typicality signal with DIFT features and k-means clustering to produce interpretable visual summaries without any explicit matching step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach scales to datasets an order of magnitude larger than prior visual mining (1.8M Places365 images vs. tens of thousands) and generalizes across content domains (cars, faces, street views, scenes, medical X-rays) with a single pipeline, suggesting a broadly applicable recipe for summarizing labeled visual corpora. Key limitations include k-means producing mixed or redundant clusters, sensitivity to dataset artifacts (printing noise, post-processing), and the absence of a principled theoretical justification for the typicality score beyond empirical validation. Open questions include whether the typicality measure can be made differentiable or integrated into end-to-end learning, whether it transfers to video or 3D data, and whether the finetuning requirement can be relaxed to a zero-shot regime.
