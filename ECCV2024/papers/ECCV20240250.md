## Problem

Prevailing NeRF-based talking head methods represent facial motions by directly modifying point appearance (color and density), but the appearance space is jumping and unsmooth, causing continuous neural fields to produce distorted features like messy mouths and transparent eyelids in dynamic regions. Additionally, the face and inside-mouth regions exhibit conflicting motions that a single motion field cannot accurately capture, further degrading reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240250/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training requires a few-minute monocular speech video (~6500 frames at 25 FPS, 512×512 or 450×450) of a single person, with camera poses estimated via a 3DMM model. At inference, the system consumes an audio clip (encoded by a pre-trained DeepSpeech model), 7 upper-face action units as expression conditioning, and a camera pose, then renders the fused talking head frame by compositing the face branch output over the inside-mouth branch output using per-pixel opacity blending.

## Method

TalkingGaussian replaces appearance modification with a pure deformation paradigm on 3D Gaussian Splatting: a Persistent Gaussian Field stores fixed color and opacity per primitive, while a Grid-based Motion Field (tri-plane hash encoder + MLP) predicts point-wise offsets (Δμ, Δs, Δq) conditioned on audio and expression features, so only geometry changes while appearance stays intact. A Face-Mouth Decomposition splits the head into two independently optimized branches—the face branch uses region attention over audio and upper-face expression features, while the lightweight inside-mouth branch predicts translation only from audio—resolving the motion conflict between lips and oral cavity. An Incremental Sampling strategy schedules training frames by action-unit magnitude in a sliding window to prevent gradient vanishing during deformation learning. Training proceeds in three stages: static 3DGS initialization, joint motion-field optimization per branch, and a final color fine-tuning for fusion, taking ~0.5 hours on an RTX 3080 Ti.

## Results

In the self-reconstruction setting, TalkingGaussian achieves the best rendering quality (PSNR 33.61, LPIPS 0.0259, SSIM 0.910) and motion quality (LMD 2.586, AUE-L 0.53, AUE-U 0.22, Sync-C 6.516) among all 3D methods, while also being the fastest with 0.5 h training time and 108 FPS inference. In the lip-synchronization setting with cross-domain audio, TalkingGaussian achieves the best Sync-C on the challenging cross-gender 'May' portrait (5.441 for Audio B) and the lowest Sync-E on 'Obama' (8.635/8.627 for Audio A/B), demonstrating superior generalization. The user study confirms these gains, with TalkingGaussian scoring highest on lip-sync accuracy (3.94/5), image quality (4.06/5), and video realness (3.88/5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among NeRF-based methods, TalkingGaussian outperforms all baselines on every metric: it beats the closest competitor ER-NeRF by +0.78 PSNR, −0.003 LPIPS, +0.021 SSIM, −0.09 LMD, and +1.22 Sync-C, while training 4× faster (0.5 h vs 2.1 h) and rendering 3.5× faster (108 vs 31.2 FPS). Against 2D generative methods, Wav2Lip achieves a higher Sync-C (8.749) but at the cost of much worse landmark distance (6.861 vs 2.586) and lower-face expression error (1.46 vs 0.53), indicating it sacrifices personal talking style for lip timing; DINet similarly trades structural fidelity (LMD 6.411) for lip sync. In the cross-domain lip-synchronization setting, TalkingGaussian shows the strongest generalization, particularly on the cross-gender 'May' portrait where it achieves Sync-C 5.441 (Audio B) versus ER-NeRF's 4.141 and RAD-NeRF's 2.426, while maintaining the lowest Sync-E on 'Obama' (8.63/8.63). The user study (32 videos, 16 raters) confirms TalkingGaussian leads all 8 methods on lip-sync accuracy (3.94), image quality (4.06), and video realness (3.88), with ER-NeRF as the runner-up on all three dimensions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant appearance-modification paradigm in NeRF-based talking head synthesis (AD-NeRF, RAD-NeRF, ER-NeRF, GeneFace, DFRF), where neural networks predict per-point color and density changes to represent facial motion. Instead, it adopts a deformation-based representation on top of 3D Gaussian Splatting, where a persistent head structure is maintained and only the position and shape of Gaussian primitives are warped by a grid-based motion field. The motion field architecture borrows the tri-plane hash encoder and region attention mechanism from ER-NeRF, but applies them to predict geometric offsets rather than appearance features. The face-mouth decomposition and incremental sampling strategy are novel contributions specific to this deformation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode—facial distortion in dynamic regions (messy mouths, transparent eyelids)—that prior NeRF-based methods exhibited but did not explicitly diagnose, reframing the problem as one of learning smooth deformation rather than fitting discontinuous appearance changes. The practical impact is significant: 108 FPS inference and ~0.5h training versus 0.04–31.2 FPS and 2.1–22.4h for NeRF baselines, making real-time talking head synthesis feasible. Limitations include person-specific training on a single identity, reliance on 3DMM for pose estimation, and dependence on off-the-shelf face parsing for the mouth mask. Open questions include whether the deformation paradigm generalizes to multi-person scenes, unseen identities, or longer unbounded sequences without retraining.
