## Problem

Existing grasp generation methods rely primarily on object geometry or coarse affordance vectors, making it difficult to produce grasps that reflect manipulation intent or respond to natural language instructions. The core challenge is designing a grasp representation that is both physically plausible and semantically interpretable, so that language can directly condition the generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object point cloud (N×3, processed by PointBERT with size passed as a separate <OS> token) and a free-form language instruction as input, and outputs a static single-hand grasp in the object's canonical space parameterized by MANO (4×4 transformation T, 15×3 joint angles θ, 10 shape parameters β). Internally, the grasp is also represented as three discrete integer tokens <o, m, r> drawn from a shared codebook of 512 entries (dimension 256), which the MLLM generates autoregressively alongside natural-language conversation.

## Method

SEMGRASP discretizes the grasp into three hierarchical tokens—orientation <o>, manner <m>, and refinement <r>—via a three-level VQ-VAE where each decoder conditions on all preceding tokens, mirroring the human planning sequence from global pose to fine contact adjustment. A Vicuna-7B-based MLLM (fine-tuned with LoRA r=64) then aligns three modalities—discrete grasp tokens, PointBERT object features, and SentencePiece text—into a unified semantic space through projection layers, trained in two stages: multimodal alignment (predicting grasp tokens from object + language) followed by instruction tuning (joint grasp + language generation). The approach is trained on CAPGRASP, a newly compiled dataset of ~50k hand-object pairs with ~300k GPT-4-generated captions spanning low-level contact states, high-level manipulation intent, and multi-turn conversational annotations, augmented with 1.5M additional dialogues from Llama-3.

## Results

On the language-guided grasp generation task (Table 2), SEMGRASP achieves a P-FID of 2.28, GPT-4 semantic consistency score of 74.5, and perceptual score of 4.6/5, substantially outperforming the BERT-based classification baseline (P-FID 3.32, GPT-4 47.3, PS 3.7). On the reconstruction task (Table 1), the discrete VQ-VAE representation achieves 14.97 mm MPVPE and 0.46 cm penetration depth, improving 26% in MPVPE over the no-refinement-token variant. With test-time adaptation, SEMGRASP attains SOTA penetration depth (0.37 cm) and solid intersection volume (1.27 cm³) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the BERT-based classification baseline on the language-guided grasp generation task, SEMGRASP wins on every major metric: P-FID improves from 3.32 to 2.28 (31% reduction), GPT-4 semantic consistency jumps from 47.3 to 74.5 (+57%), and perceptual score rises from 3.7 to 4.6, while SD std is marginally worse (2.33 vs 2.26). On the reconstruction benchmark (Table 1), SEMGRASP's discrete representation achieves 14.97 mm MPVPE, beating GrabNet (27.49 mm) and Jiang et al. (33.84 mm) by large margins, and also achieves the lowest penetration depth (0.46 cm vs 0.54 and 0.58 cm). However, SEMGRASP trades off simulation stability: its SD mean (2.14 cm) and SD std (2.37 cm) are higher than GrabNet (1.77/2.36) and Jiang et al. (1.53/1.77) without TTA. With test-time adaptation, SEMGRASP achieves the best PD (0.37 cm) and SIV (1.27 cm³) but still lags on SD metrics, suggesting the discrete tokens capture geometry and semantics well but leave room for physics-aware refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VQ-VAE discretization paradigm popularized by MotionGPT and ShapeGPT to the grasp generation domain, decomposing continuous MANO hand poses into three semantically meaningful tokens (orientation, manner, refinement) via a hierarchical VQ-VAE. It builds directly on the LLaVA two-stage training recipe (multimodal alignment followed by instruction tuning) and the OakInk hand-object interaction dataset, but departs from prior grasp generation methods (GrabNet, Jiang et al.) that rely solely on geometric conditioning or coarse affordance vectors by embedding full language instructions into the generation loop. The conceptual departure is treating grasp generation as a token-prediction problem in a unified semantic space rather than a continuous regression or classification task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CAPGRASP is the first dataset to provide low-level contact states, high-level manipulation intent, and conversational annotations aligned with grasp poses, filling a gap that limits prior semantic grasp research. The demonstrated applications in AR/VR (via D-grasp) and dexterous robotics (via UniDexGrasp) suggest practical utility, though both rely on off-the-shelf RL policies rather than end-to-end learning. Key open questions remain: the method handles only single-hand static grasps, two-hand coordination and continuous motion synthesis are explicitly left as future work, and the GPT-4-assisted annotation pipeline introduces potential hallucination risks that manual review only partially mitigates.
