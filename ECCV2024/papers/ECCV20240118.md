## Problem

Bird's-eye-view (BEV) representations for self-driving remain high-dimensional, with most pixels encoding road rather than the vehicles that actually cause infractions. Existing object-level approaches rely on hand-specified attribute vectors (position, heading, speed) that may be incomplete or scene-dependent. The paper asks whether a learned, self-supervised object-centric representation can capture the information needed for driving without explicitly enumerating object attributes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of T=2 ego-centric 8-channel semantic BEV maps (192×192×8), which a frozen SAVi model converts into K=30 continuous slot vectors of dimension 128. These are combined with discretized scalar inputs (target point quantized to 32 bins, ego speed to 14 bins, binary traffic-light flag) and two 6-dimensional route vectors, all projected to a shared hidden dimension H=768. The model outputs W continuous waypoints via a GRU head (used for control) and 2W quantized waypoint tokens predicted autoregressively by the transformer, plus future slot representations for world-modeling evaluation.

## Method

CarFormer is a two-stage system: a frozen SAVi slot-attention model extracts temporally consistent object slots from BEV sequences, and a GPT-2-style autoregressive transformer decoder (6 layers, H=768) jointly learns driving and scene dynamics over these slots. The key architectural modification replaces the standard causal attention mask with a block-triangular mask, so that all K slot tokens and the two route vectors within a block attend bidirectionally to each other, enabling the model to reason about inter-object relationships. Training uses imitation learning with a combined loss on continuous GRU waypoints, quantized transformer waypoints, and an auxiliary forecasting term (MSE on future slots, weighted α=40) that forces the model to anticipate other vehicles' motions. The approach is validated on CARLA's Longest6 benchmark, where slots outperform both scene-level VQ-VAE tokens and PlanT-style exact attribute vectors in driving score while halving run-to-run variance.

## Results

On the Longest6 benchmark, CarFormer with slot representations achieves a Driving Score of 74.89±1.44, Route Completion of 92.90±1.28, and Infraction Score of 0.79±0.02. This outperforms the scene-level baselines (AIM-BEV: DS 45.06; ROACH: DS 55.27) by a wide margin and surpasses the object-level attribute baseline PlanT (DS 73.36±2.97, RC 87.03±3.91) in both driving score and route completion, while exhibiting roughly half the variance across runs. The slot-based model also significantly outperforms CarFormer's own scene-level VQ-VAE representation (DS 17.07), confirming that object-centric slots are the critical design choice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PlanT (the closest competitor using exact object-level attributes), CarFormer with slots wins on Driving Score (74.89 vs. 73.36) and Route Completion (92.90 vs. 87.03) but trails slightly on Infraction Score (0.79 vs. 0.84); however, the standard deviation of DS drops from 2.97 to 1.44, indicating substantially more stable behavior across runs. Against the scene-level baselines, CarFormer with slots dominates: it outperforms ROACH (RL, DS 55.27) by ~20 points in DS and AIM-BEV (IL, DS 45.06) by ~30 points, while also achieving higher RC than both. Notably, CarFormer with the same architecture but scene-level VQ-VAE tokens scores only DS 17.07, confirming that the gains come from the object-centric slot representation rather than architectural changes. The paper also notes that CarFormer with explicit object attributes (DS 71.53) performs worse than PlanT (DS 73.36), further isolating the slot representation as the source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

CarFormer sits at the intersection of two research threads: self-supervised object-centric learning (slot attention, SAVi) and transformer-based driving (PlanT, TransFuser). It adapts SAVi's video slot extraction to BEV sequences as a frozen front-end, then feeds the resulting slot vectors into a GPT-2-style autoregressive transformer modified with block attention to model inter-object dynamics. The key conceptual departure from PlanT is replacing hand-specified object attribute vectors (position, heading, speed, size) with learned, self-contained slot representations that implicitly encode these properties from spatio-temporal context, positioning slots as a more scalable and generalizable alternative to explicit attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely learned, self-supervised object representation can match or exceed explicit object attributes for driving while exhibiting substantially lower run-to-run variance, suggesting greater robustness to scene variation. However, the privileged assumption of ground-truth BEV maps limits immediate practical impact, and the authors acknowledge that BEV perception accuracy remains insufficient for real-world slot extraction. Open questions include whether slots can be extracted end-to-end from raw camera inputs, whether the approach generalizes beyond vehicles to pedestrians and cyclists, and whether the autoregressive decoder can be extended to multi-step reward-conditioned planning as in Decision Transformer-style RL.
