## Problem

Most bundle adjustment solvers, including the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm, require a good initialization of camera poses and 3D landmarks. While the Variable Projection (VarPro) algorithm offers a wide convergence basin that enables solving BA from arbitrary initialization, all existing initialization-free BA works rely on direct factorization (Cholesky, QR) and are limited to problems with fewer than a few hundred cameras. This paper addresses the scalability gap: no efficient solver existed for initialization-free BA at the scale of thousands of camera viewpoints.

## Input / Output

The method takes only 2D image measurements (2D observations m_ij linking pose i to landmark j) and known camera intrinsics K_i as input, with no initial pose or landmark estimates required — poses are randomly drawn from an isotropic Gaussian (mean 0, variance 1) and landmarks are deduced via the closed-form VarPro solution. The output is camera poses (rotation R_i ∈ SO(3), translation t_i ∈ R³) and 3D landmark positions x_j ∈ R³. The solution proceeds through a stratified pipeline: a projective separable stage, a homogeneous-coordinate projective refinement on a Riemannian manifold, and a final metric upgrade to enforce SE(3) camera structure.

## Method

The core contribution is Power Variable Projection (PoVar), which extends the power series expansion of the inverse Schur complement to the VarPro algorithm. The key theoretical result (Lemma 1) proves that eigenvalues of U_λ^{-1} W V_0^† W^T lie in [0,1), guaranteeing convergence of the truncated series for the VarPro Schur complement S^V = U_λ − W V_0^{-1} W^T; the pose update is then computed as a finite sum of matrix-vector products, and landmark updates follow from the closed-form pseudo-inverse solution. For the projective refinement stage, the authors introduce RiPoBA, which projects the normal equations onto the tangent space of the homogeneous camera and landmark parameters (removing the local scale freedom) and applies the same power series expansion to the resulting Riemannian Schur complement, with a memory-efficient block storage exploiting the BA sparsity structure. Unlike PoBA, where both pose and landmark Jacobians are damped, PoVar damps only the pose Jacobian, which fundamentally alters the convergence behavior and makes the method robust to the ill-conditioning that arises from random initialization.

## Results

Evaluated on 97 real-world BAL problems (16 to 13,682 poses) with random initialization, PoVar is the most competitive solver at the highest accuracy threshold (τ=0.001) for the first stage, slightly outperforming PoBA at τ=0.003, and challenging PoBA at τ=0.01. For the full two-stage stratified BA problem, the PoVar + RiPoBA combination outperforms all competitors in both runtime and accuracy across all tolerance levels. RiPoBA outperforms the Riemannian preconditioned conjugate gradients baseline (RiPCG) for the highest accuracy (τ=0.001) at all relative runtimes greater than α=2, and across all tolerances when paired with the same first-stage solver.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PoBA [23] (power series for traditional BA), PoVar wins decisively at the highest accuracy (τ=0.001) and slightly at τ=0.003, while the two are competitive at the loosest tolerance (τ=0.01); PoVar also converges more smoothly, avoiding the early-iteration stagnation that PoBA exhibits. Against pOSE with direct sparse Cholesky factorization [11], PoVar is clearly superior — the direct factorization solver shows poor scalability and performance, particularly as problem size grows. Against VarPro with preconditioned conjugate gradients (iterative) [10], PoVar is substantially faster, with the iterative method converging much more slowly to the same cost thresholds. For the second (projective refinement) stage, RiPoBA outperforms RiPCG (Riemannian preconditioned CG) at the highest accuracy for all relative runtimes >2, and the PoVar+RiPoBA combination dominates all other solver pairings on the full two-stage problem, most notably on the largest instance (Venice-1672 with 1,672 poses).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: the Variable Projection (VarPro) algorithm for separable nonlinear least squares (Golub & Pereyra 1973, extended by Hong et al. 2017), the power-series expansion of the inverse Schur complement for scalable BA (PoBA, Weber et al. 2023), and the stratified initialization-free BA formulation (Hong et al. 2016, 2018). The key conceptual move is recognizing that the power-series technique, previously applied only to the standard Levenberg-Marquardt BA normal equations, can be transplanted into the VarPro normal equations (yielding PoVar) and further extended to the Riemannian manifold setting required for projective refinement (yielding RiPoBA). The paper departs from prior initialization-free BA work, which relied on direct factorizations impractical beyond a few hundred cameras, by showing that the undamped landmark Jacobian in VarPro's Schur complement still satisfies the spectral-radius condition needed for power-series convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that initialization-free bundle adjustment can scale to thousands of cameras, opening a path toward initialization-free structure-from-motion that does not require a separate global alignment or PnP initialization step. The practical impact is significant for large-scale reconstruction pipelines where obtaining a good initial pose estimate is itself a bottleneck. However, the approach still assumes outlier tracks are pre-filtered and known intrinsics for the metric upgrade stage, and the stratified formulation (projective → metric) remains a multi-stage pipeline rather than a single unified solve. Whether the power-series approach maintains its advantage at the extreme scales (tens of thousands of cameras) encountered in internet photo collections remains an open question.
