## Problem

Fine-tuning zero-shot vision-language models like CLIP for downstream tasks creates a tension: full fine-tuning improves in-distribution accuracy but degrades out-of-distribution generalization and demands large computational resources, while parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) is lightweight but lags significantly under distribution shift. Existing robust fine-tuning methods (WiSE-FT, MaskFill, Model Soup) preserve OOD performance but require tuning the entire model and are limited to classification. No prior method simultaneously achieves parameter efficiency and OOD robustness across diverse vision-language tasks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture, taking RGB images and text as input. For classification, text is constructed from class templates (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}'); for cross-modal retrieval, each image is paired with five captions; for open-vocabulary segmentation, masked images from COCO Captions are used. Adapters are inserted after both MHA and FFN sub-layers in every Transformer block of both the image and text encoders, and at inference the adapters are re-parameterized into the preceding pre-trained linear layers, yielding zero additional computation. The method supports both full-rank (d×d) and low-rank (rank-r bottleneck) adapter configurations, with the latter enabling extremely few-shot settings (e.g., rank-1 adapters with only 0.06M trainable parameters).

## Method

R-Adapter inserts lightweight linear adapter modules (a single weight matrix with residual connection, no nonlinearity or bias) after MHA and FFN in both CLIP encoders, then applies three self-ensemble strategies to boost OOD robustness without extra inference cost: (1) adapter dropping, which randomly deactivates adapters during training with probability p to create a dynamic ensemble of subnetworks combining pre-trained and adapter layers; (2) temporal ensemble via exponential moving average accumulation of adapter weights over training iterations; and (3) weight-space ensemble by re-scaling accumulated adapter weights by a coefficient α before re-parameterizing them into the preceding pre-trained layer, enabling linear interpolation between pre-trained and fine-tuned weights within a single model. The proposed MPM-NCE loss replaces InfoNCE with soft labels that assign equal probability to all positive pairs (handling multiple captions per image or multiple templates per class) and introduces an angular margin δ on negative pairs to enforce more discriminative embeddings. Together, these components tune only ~13% of CLIP encoder parameters while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning baselines on both ID and OOD metrics across classification, retrieval, and segmentation.

## Results

On ImageNet classification with CLIP ViT-B/16, R-Adapter achieves 82.0% ID top-1 accuracy and 64.8% OOD average accuracy using only 20.5M trainable parameters (vs. 86.7M for full fine-tuning), improving OOD average by 1.5 percentage points over the previous best (Mask-Fill, 63.3%). With CLIP ViT-L/14@336px, it reaches 78.9% OOD average, a 2.0-point gain over WiSE-FT (76.9%) while tuning only 64.5M vs. 305.1M parameters. On cross-modal retrieval (COCO 5K), R-Adapter (ViT-L/14) achieves 58.1% R@1 text-to-image, surpassing supervised specialists trained on both COCO and Flickr30K. For open-vocabulary segmentation, R-Adapter improves mIoU by up to 2.7% over the original OVSeg on Pascal Context (PC-59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-Adapter outperforms all robust fine-tuning baselines on OOD average accuracy while using roughly 4× fewer trainable parameters: it beats WiSE-FT by 1.2–2.0 points, Mask-Fill by 1.2–1.5 points, and WiSE-FLYP by 1.7 points on OOD average (ViT-B/16), while matching or exceeding their ID accuracy. Compared to Uniform Soup (Model Soups), R-Adapter gains 2.7 points OOD average with ViT-B/32 while requiring only 20.5M vs. 6,364.8M parameters. Against PEFT baselines (AdaptFormer, RepAdapter) in the ablation, R-Adapter improves OOD accuracy by 5.8–6.6 points, showing that naive adapter tuning without self-ensemble is insufficient for distribution shift. In few-shot (16-shot) classification, R-Adapter (Full-Rank) surpasses CLIPood by 2.0 points OOD average with 4× fewer parameters, and the rank-1 variant beats CoOp and CoCoOp by 2.3 and 1.7 points respectively. On cross-modal retrieval, R-Adapter (ViT-L/14) trained only on COCO outperforms supervised specialists (Unicoder-VL, Uniter, Oscar, ERNIE-ViL) that were trained on both COCO and Flickr30K, achieving 83.4% R@1 text-to-image on Flickr30K. For open-vocabulary segmentation, R-Adapter improves mIoU over the original OVSeg by 1.3–2.7 points across four benchmarks, confirming gains in generalization to unseen classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: robust fine-tuning (WiSE-FT, FLYP, MaskFill, Model Soup), which preserves OOD generalization but requires full-model updates, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (AdaptFormer, RepAdapter, LoRA), which is computationally cheap but degrades under distribution shift. The method builds directly on Houlsby-style linear adapters and RepAdapter's re-parameterization trick, but introduces three self-ensemble mechanisms (adapter dropping, EMA accumulation, and re-scaling) that replicate the benefits of weight-space ensembling (WiSE-FT, Model Soup) within a single model without storing multiple copies. The MPM-NCE loss extends InfoNCE to handle the multi-positive structure inherent in vision-language downstream tasks, addressing a limitation of FLYP's direct reuse of the pre-training contrastive objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By tuning only ~13% of CLIP encoder parameters while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning robustness methods, R-Adapter makes robust adaptation of large vision-language models substantially more accessible in terms of memory, storage, and compute. The extension of the robust fine-tuning evaluation to cross-modal retrieval and open-vocabulary segmentation broadens the scope beyond the classification-only focus of prior work, providing a more complete picture of where PEFT methods stand under distribution shift. Open questions include whether the self-ensemble strategies transfer to other PEFT paradigms (prompt tuning, LoRA), whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP to other vision-language models (e.g., BLIP-2, SigLIP), and whether the linear adapter design is truly sufficient for tasks requiring more complex feature transformations.
