## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D generation models (e.g., LRM, Instant3D) can produce 3D objects in seconds but are limited to low-resolution training (32 triplane, 128 rendered pixels) due to the heavy computational cost of transformer backbones and triplane-based volume rendering. This bottleneck prevents them from capturing fine textures and complex geometry, leaving a gap between fast generation and high-fidelity output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240081/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the pipeline accepts a single-view RGB image, a text prompt, or both. Multi-view diffusion models (MVDream for text, ImageDream for image) synthesize four views at 256×256 with fixed azimuths [0°, 90°, 180°, 270°] and zero elevation. The U-Net consumes these as 9-channel inputs (RGB + Plücker ray embedding) at 256×256 and outputs 14-channel Gaussian feature maps at 128×128 per view, yielding 65,536 total Gaussians rendered at 512×512. Optionally, a NeRF2Mesh pipeline converts the Gaussians into a textured polygonal mesh in ~1 minute.

## Method

The core contribution is an asymmetric U-Net backbone that replaces both the transformer and triplane volume rendering used in prior feed-forward models. Four input views with Plücker ray embeddings are processed by a U-Net (6 down, 1 middle, 5 up blocks) with cross-view self-attention at deeper layers to share information across views; the output resolution (128×128) is smaller than the input (256×256), capping the Gaussian count while permitting high-resolution input. Each output pixel is interpreted as one 3D Gaussian (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color), and Gaussians from all four views are concatenated and rendered via differentiable splatting for end-to-end supervision with MSE + LPIPS on RGB and MSE on alpha across 8 views. To bridge the domain gap between rendered training data and diffusion-synthesized inference inputs, two augmentations—random grid distortion on three views and orbital camera jitter—are applied at 50% probability. The asymmetric design and Gaussian splatting representation together enable training at 512×512 rendering resolution on 32 A100 GPUs in ~4 days, achieving 5-second inference with substantially higher fidelity than triplane-based baselines.

## Results

The primary quantitative evaluation is a user study (20 raters, 90 videos) on image-to-3D quality rated on a 1–5 scale. LGM achieves 4.18/5 on Image Consistency and 3.95/5 on Overall Quality, substantially outperforming TriplaneGaussian (3.02 / 2.67) and DreamGaussian (2.30 / 1.98). The method generates 65,536 3D Gaussians at 512×512 rendering resolution in approximately 5 seconds end-to-end, using only ~10 GB of GPU memory at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DreamGaussian [48] (SDS-based 3D Gaussian optimization), LGM wins decisively on both user-study metrics: 4.18 vs 2.30 for image consistency and 3.95 vs 1.98 for overall quality, reflecting DreamGaussian's tendency to produce floaters and inconsistent geometry. Against TriplaneGaussian [63] (feed-forward Gaussian splatting with triplane features), LGM also leads clearly (4.18 vs 3.02 consistency; 3.95 vs 2.67 quality), attributed to LGM's higher Gaussian count (65,536 vs a smaller set) and 512-resolution training. Against LRM [15] (transformer-based NeRF regression), the comparison is qualitative (Figure 6): LGM's multi-view setting mitigates LRM's blurry back views and flat geometry, yielding richer detail in unseen views. For text-to-3D, LGM is compared against Shape-E [16] and DreamGaussian [48] qualitatively (Figure 7), showing better text alignment and freedom from multi-face artifacts due to its multi-view diffusion front-end. No baseline is beaten by LGM in any reported metric; the method is preferred across all comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

LGM departs from the transformer-based triplane NeRF paradigm established by LRM and Instant3D, replacing both the 3D representation (triplane → 3D Gaussian splatting) and the backbone (transformer → asymmetric U-Net with cross-view self-attention). The pixel-wise Gaussian prediction mechanism is directly inspired by Splatter Image, but LGM generalizes it from single-view to a 4-view multi-view setting and introduces an asymmetric design (higher input resolution than output) to decouple input image resolution from the number of Gaussians. The multi-view diffusion front-end (MVDream, ImageDream) follows the Instant3D pipeline, but the key architectural contribution is showing that a U-Net backbone can match or exceed transformer-based methods while enabling 512-resolution training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that LGM demonstrates 512-resolution feed-forward 3D generation in ~5 seconds, a regime previously inaccessible to regression-based methods (LRM capped at 128). This opens the door to high-fidelity 3D asset creation without per-object optimization. However, the pipeline's quality is bottlenecked by the 256×256 resolution and 3D inconsistencies of current multi-view diffusion models, and the domain gap between rendered training data and diffusion-synthesized inputs requires careful augmentation. Open questions include whether better multi-view diffusion models (e.g., Zero-1-to-3++) will unlock further gains, and whether the U-Net architecture scales to even higher resolutions or more views.
