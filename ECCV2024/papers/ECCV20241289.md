## Problem

Existing pedestrian detection models are specialists tailored to one or a pair of sensor modalities (e.g., RGB-D or RGB-T), requiring separate models for each modality combination and failing to generalize across domains. No unified generalist model or comprehensive multi-modal benchmark exists that can fairly evaluate methods across diverse sensor types and application scenarios simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241289/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any dynamic combination of up to five modalities (RGB, IR, Depth, LiDAR, Event) as 2D image inputs, where LiDAR point clouds are projected into sparse depth images and event streams are integrated over 10–20 ms time windows into 2D representations. Missing modalities are handled by padding empty images and masking their tokens, enabling the same model to process unimodal or multi-modal inputs without architectural changes. The output is a set of pedestrian bounding boxes with class scores, evaluated via COCO AP (IoU 0.5:0.95, maxDets=100).

## Method

MMPedestron employs a single unified Dual-ViT encoder shared across all modalities, where each stage contains a modality-specific patch embedding, stacked transformer blocks, and a modality unifier module. Two learnable tokens drive the fusion: the Modality-Aware Fuser (MAF) token is passed through an MLP to produce per-modality confidence scores that weight the token features in a weighted average, while the Modality-Aware Abstractor (MAA) token is added to the unified output to inject domain-specific knowledge about the active modality combination. Training follows a two-stage schedule—RGB pretraining on Objects365-Persons, COCO-Persons, and CrowdHuman, followed by multi-modal fine-tuning on LLVIP, InOutDoor, STCrowd, and EventPed—with a modality dropout (p=0.3) that randomly removes modalities to enforce robustness to missing inputs. The unified tokens feed a Co-DINO detection head, and the shared-branch design reduces parameter count by a factor of N compared to N independent modality branches.

## Results

On the multi-modal fusion evaluation (Table 3), MMPedestron achieves 72.6 AP on LLVIP, 74.9 AP on STCrowd, 65.7 AP on InOutDoor, and 79.0 AP on EventPed, outperforming all prior fusion methods by 10+ AP on most datasets. On standard RGB benchmarks, MMPedestron reaches 71.1 AP on COCO-Persons (62M params) and 97.1 AP / 30.8 MR⁻² on CrowdHuman, matching InternImage-H (1.09B params) with 30× fewer parameters. On the cross-dataset transfer to PEDRo (event data), MMPedestron achieves 81.5 Val AP and 73.3 Test AP, surpassing all event-specific detectors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On COCO-Persons, MMPedestron (71.1 AP, 62M) outperforms InternImage-XL (64.8 AP, 387M) by 6.3 AP, DINO-Person (61.8 AP, 218M) by 9.3 AP, and Faster R-CNN-Person (54.1 AP, 41M) by 17 AP. On CrowdHuman, MMPedestron (97.1 AP, 62M) surpasses Iter-D-DETR (94.1 AP, 207M) by 3.0 AP, UniHCP (92.5 AP, 109M) by 4.6 AP, and PATH (90.8 AP, 320M) by 6.3 AP, while matching InternImage-H (95.4 AP, 1.09B) with 30× fewer parameters. In multi-modal fusion, MMPedestron beats the strongest prior method CMX (150M) by 13.0 AP on LLVIP, 13.9 AP on STCrowd, 3.4 AP on InOutDoor, and 21.0 AP on EventPed. On PEDRo (event data), MMPedestron (81.5 Val AP) outperforms YOLOX (73.9), Co-Dino (73.4), and Meta-Transformer (67.5) by 7.6, 8.1, and 14.0 AP respectively. On FLIR (RGB+IR multi-category), MMPedestron achieves 86.4 AP50, exceeding CMX (82.2), CFT (77.7), and GAFF (72.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-modal pedestrian detection, where each modality pair (RGB+IR, RGB+Depth, RGB+LiDAR) requires a dedicated model with separate encoder branches. It also extends the generalist-model line of work (ImageBind, LanguageBind, Meta-Transformer) by replacing per-modality encoders with a single shared transformer encoder and introducing MAF/MAA learnable tokens for adaptive, combination-aware fusion. The detection head (Co-DINO) and backbone (Dual ViT) are repurposed from existing components, but the unified multi-modal encoder architecture, modality dropout training strategy, and the MMPD/EventPed benchmark are novel contributions that carve out the 'generalist multi-modal pedestrian detection' problem space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMPD benchmark (260K images, 5 modalities, 4 combinations) and the EventPed dataset fill a critical gap: no prior benchmark covered more than one modality pair, making fair cross-modality comparison impossible. The practical impact is significant—one model replaces an entire fleet of modality-specific detectors, reducing deployment complexity. Open questions include whether the 2D-projection approach for LiDAR and event data limits performance compared to native 3D or temporal representations, whether the architecture scales gracefully beyond five modalities, and whether the modality dropout strategy generalizes to scenarios with sensor degradation rather than complete absence.
