## Problem

Existing imbalanced multimodal learning methods identify 'worse-learnt' modalities purely by prediction performance and then over-emphasize their training, ignoring the intrinsic capacity limitation of scarcely informative modalities that naturally contain little label-related signal and much noise. Additionally, these methods narrowly target only the selected worse-learnt modalities, sometimes suppressing the training of well-learnt ones, which degrades their representation quality. The paper addresses both gaps by diagnosing each modality's true learning state and balancing all modalities simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard multimodal inputs (audio clips + video frames, RGB + optical flow, or audio + video + text) processed by uni-modal encoders (ResNet-18 or Transformer backbones) and produces a single class label via one multimodal cross-entropy loss. Crucially, it requires no additional uni-modal classifiers or auxiliary loss functions; it only needs access to the intermediate uni-modal encoder features on both training and validation sets to compute clustering-based diagnostics. The method is agnostic to the fusion strategy (concatenation, cross-modal attention, etc.) and the number of modalities.

## Method

The Diagnosing & Re-learning method periodically (every H epochs) estimates each modality's learning state by extracting uni-modal features on training and validation sets, running k-means clustering, and computing the purity gap g^k = |P_D^k − P_V^k|, where a large gap indicates over-training and a small gap indicates under-fitting or intrinsic information scarcity. Based on this gap, a re-initialization strength α_k = tanh(λ · g^k) is computed, and each uni-modal encoder is softly re-initialized as θ_k = (1−α_k)·θ_k^current + α_k·θ_k^init. Well-learnt modalities receive stronger re-initialization, breaking the model's over-reliance on them and preventing over-training, while under-fitting or scarcely informative modalities receive only slight re-initialization that helps escape noise memorization without forcing unproductive emphasis. The soft blending preserves previously learned inter-modal correlations, and the entire procedure requires no architectural modifications or additional loss terms.

## Results

On CREMA-D (audio/vision), the proposed method achieves 75.13% accuracy and 76.00% Macro F1, outperforming the best baseline AGM (70.16% / 70.67%) by over 5 points. On Kinetics Sounds, it reaches 69.10% accuracy and 69.39% Macro F1, surpassing G-Blending (68.60% / 68.64%). On UCF-101 (RGB/optical flow), it attains 82.11% accuracy and 80.87% Macro F1, edging out G-Blending (81.73% / 80.84%). The method also extends to three-modality settings (CMU-MOSI: 77.99% Acc) and Transformer-based backbones (CREMA-D with MBT: 71.64% Acc), consistently outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against AGM [9] (the strongest baseline on CREMA-D at 70.16% Acc), the gap is the largest at ~5 points (75.13% vs 70.16%), suggesting that gradient-modulation approaches that suppress well-learnt modalities are particularly limited. Against G-Blending [20] (the strongest baseline on Kinetics Sounds and UCF-101), the method still leads by 0.5% Acc on Kinetics Sounds and 0.38% Acc on UCF-101, and by a wider margin on Macro F1. OGM-GE [15] and PMR [6] are consistently outperformed by 1–5 points depending on the dataset. Greedy [25] is the weakest baseline, trailing the proposed method by 6–7 points on CREMA-D and Kinetics Sounds. Notably, in the scarcely informative modality case (Table 4), all five baselines degrade below the joint-training baseline, while the proposed method still achieves 69.49% Acc vs 65.86% for joint-training, highlighting a qualitative advantage in handling intrinsically noisy modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the line of imbalanced multimodal learning methods (G-Blending, OGM-GE, Greedy, PMR, AGM) that use inter-modal prediction performance discrepancy as the basis for balancing, arguing they conflate under-training with intrinsic modality capacity limits. It repurposes the network re-initialization idea from generalization-focused work (Alabdulmohsin et al., Ash & Adams) with a fundamentally different intention: using soft re-initialization as a modality-balancing mechanism driven by a representation-separability diagnostic. The method is deliberately framework-agnostic, unlike prior approaches that often depend on specific fusion strategies or additional uni-modal classifiers to estimate per-modality learning state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode—scarcely informative modalities with high noise—that causes all existing balancing methods to degrade below the joint-training baseline, a finding that should caution practitioners against blindly emphasizing underperforming modalities. The soft re-initialization strategy is simple, requires no architectural changes, and generalizes across CNN and Transformer backbones as well as two- and three-modality settings. Open questions remain for non-classification tasks (e.g., regression), where the clustering-purity diagnostic may not directly apply, and for scenarios where modality capacity limits are more subtle than the synthetic noise case tested here.
