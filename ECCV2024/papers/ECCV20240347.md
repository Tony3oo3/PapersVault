## Problem

Selecting the most informative viewpoints for radiance field reconstruction requires quantifying how much new information a candidate view provides to the model parameters. Existing approaches rely on indirect uncertainty proxies—variance outputs, hypothetical perturbation fields, or Monte Carlo sampling—that do not directly measure information gain on the model and are incompatible with recent explicit representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting. No prior method computes the observed information on radiance field parameters in a way that is both principled and efficient enough for real-time view selection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained radiance field model (3D Gaussian Splatting or Plenoxels) and a pool of candidate SE(3) camera poses as input; critically, it requires no ground-truth images at candidate views since the Fisher Information depends only on camera parameters and the current model state. It outputs a ranked set of selected views (single or batch), an information-gain heat map over the viewing sphere, and pixel-wise uncertainty maps derived from per-parameter diagonal Hessians. In the active mapping setting, the input extends to posed RGB-D images, and the output is an exploration path chosen to maximize cumulative information gain along the trajectory.

## Method

The core insight is that the Fisher Information of a radiance field model equals the Hessian of the negative log-likelihood with respect to model parameters, and in volumetric rendering this Hessian is independent of ground-truth observations, reducing to J^T J where J is the Jacobian of the rendering function. The Expected Information Gain between a candidate view and the training set is approximated by the trace of the candidate Hessian times the inverse of the accumulated training Hessian, exploiting the additivity of Fisher Information. To handle 200M+ parameters, a Laplace approximation retains only diagonal entries plus a log-prior regularizer, and the inherent sparsity of structured local parameters (each 3D Gaussian or grid cell affects only a limited spatial region) makes the trace computation as cheap as a single back-propagation pass, enabling 70 fps view evaluation. Batch selection uses a greedy submodular algorithm with 1/e approximation guarantee, and the active mapping extension sums Hessians along candidate trajectories to select the most informative exploration path. Pixel-wise uncertainty is obtained by summing the diagonal Hessians of parameters intersected by each casted ray, weighted by volumetric transmittance.

## Results

On the Blender dataset (20 training views), FisherRF with 3D Gaussian Splatting achieves 29.53 PSNR / 0.944 SSIM / 0.043 LPIPS, outperforming random selection (28.73 / 0.939 / 0.053) and ActiveNeRF (26.61 / 0.905 / 0.081). On the real-world Mip-NeRF360 dataset, the method reaches 20.35 PSNR / 0.601 SSIM, a clear margin over random (17.91 / 0.564) and ActiveNeRF (17.89 / 0.533). For active mapping on Gibson, FisherRF achieves 92.89% scene coverage (5.64 cm mean nearest distance), surpassing Active Neural Mapping (80.45%, 7.44 cm) and frontier-based exploration (68.91%, 14.42 cm). On the Light Field uncertainty benchmark, the method attains the best average AUSE of 0.22, marginally beating BayesRays (0.23) and substantially outperforming CF-NeRF (0.38) and ActiveNeRF (0.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ActiveNeRF [43], FisherRF wins decisively on all view-selection metrics: on Blender, 3D Gaussian + Ours achieves 29.53 PSNR vs. 26.61 for ActiveNeRF (a 2.9 dB gap), and on Mip-NeRF360 the gap widens to 2.46 dB (20.35 vs. 17.89). Against the BayesRays uncertainty-based baseline [16], FisherRF also outperforms on both Blender (29.53 vs. 22.50 PSNR with Nerfacto) and Mip-NeRF360, confirming that Expected Information Gain is a superior selection criterion to raw per-pixel uncertainty. For active mapping, FisherRF (built on SplaTAM) surpasses Active Neural Mapping [67] by 12.44 percentage points in scene coverage on Gibson (92.89% vs. 80.45%) and 16.26 points on MP3D (89.41% vs. 73.15%), while also improving rendering PSNR from 19.64 to 22.58 and depth MAE from 0.2943 m to 0.0924 m on Gibson. Compared to frontier-based exploration [64] and UPEN [15], FisherRF achieves the best coverage and rendering quality across both Gibson and MP3D. On the Light Field uncertainty benchmark, FisherRF (AUSE 0.22) narrowly beats BayesRays (0.23) and clearly outperforms CF-NeRF (0.38) and ActiveNeRF (0.33), validating that parameter-level Fisher Information yields well-calibrated pixel-wise uncertainty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior active view selection methods for radiance fields (ActiveNeRF, BayesRays) that use proxy uncertainty signals—variance outputs or hypothetical perturbation fields—to approximate information gain. Instead, it directly computes the Fisher Information (Hessian of the negative log-likelihood) on radiance field parameters, drawing on the information-theoretic unification of active learning by Kirsch et al. [28]. The method is implemented on top of modern explicit representations (3D Gaussian Splatting, Plenoxels) and builds its active mapping system on SplaTAM, making it compatible with current radiance field pipelines where older NeRF-specific uncertainty methods are inapplicable. A key enabling observation is that the Hessian of the volumetric rendering loss is independent of ground-truth images, and the sparsity of the rendering Jacobian (each parameter affects only a limited spatial region) makes the computation tractable via a diagonal Laplace approximation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to directly quantify observed information on radiance field model parameters, unifying active view selection, active mapping, and pixel-wise uncertainty quantification under a single information-theoretic objective. The practical impact is significant: the 70 fps view evaluation rate and the large margins in active mapping coverage (92.9% vs. 80.5% on Gibson) suggest this could become a standard component in embodied 3D reconstruction systems. However, the method is limited to static scenes in confined settings, and extending Fisher Information quantification to dynamically changing radiance fields remains an open problem. The reliance on a diagonal Hessian approximation also means it captures only per-parameter uncertainty, potentially missing important cross-parameter correlations in complex scenes.
