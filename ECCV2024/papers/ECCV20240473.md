## Problem

Existing latent diffusion methods for 3D generation lack highly compressed latent spaces, which slows both training and inference, and they condition generation solely on image embeddings that capture only 2D appearance rather than 3D geometry. This results in generated meshes with poor geometric quality and weak alignment with the input image, while also requiring large training datasets (≥1M shapes) and long training times.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view RGB image as input, from which a CLIP image embedding is extracted and a shape embedding is estimated via a diffusion prior. The output is a textured 3D mesh extracted via FlexiCubes from a 128-resolution triplane, with the entire pipeline completing in 7 seconds on a single A100 GPU. The compressed triplane latent space is only 0.10M parameters (32×32×32, 32 channels), roughly 10× smaller than Shap-E's 1.05M-parameter latent.

## Method

Compress3D introduces a triplane autoencoder that replaces parameter-free mean pooling with learnable 3D convolutions in three orthogonal directions to project point-cloud features onto triplanes, followed by a 3D-aware cross-attention mechanism where low-resolution triplane latents query a downsampled 3D feature volume to recover lost geometric detail. The generation pipeline is two-stage: a lightweight MLP-based diffusion prior first estimates a shape embedding from the image embedding (both extracted via OpenShape's aligned space), and then a UNet triplane diffusion model with TriConv generates the triplane latent conditioned on both embeddings using classifier-free guidance. The decoder reconstructs geometry via FlexiCubes (dual marching cubes) and texture via MLP color prediction, trained end-to-end with differentiable rendering losses (RGB, mask, depth) plus a KL regularization on the latent distribution.

## Results

On the filtered Objaverse test set, Compress3D achieves a FID of 53.21, CLIP similarity of 0.776, PSNR of 16.82 dB, and LPIPS of 0.272, outperforming all three baselines on every quality metric. The method uses a latent space of only 0.10M parameters (roughly 10× smaller than Shap-E's 1.05M) and generates a 3D model in 7 seconds on a single A100 GPU. Training requires only 95k models and 1900 A100 GPU-hours, substantially less than OpenLRM (951k models, 9200 hours) and LGM (80k models, 3072 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compress3D outperforms Shap-E [10] by a wide margin on all quality metrics: FID drops from 146.14 to 53.21, CLIP similarity rises from 0.731 to 0.776, PSNR improves from 14.54 to 16.82 dB, and LPIPS decreases from 0.350 to 0.272, while also reducing the latent space from 1.05M to 0.10M parameters and cutting inference time from 11 s to 7 s. Against OpenLRM [6], Compress3D wins on every quality metric (FID 53.21 vs 86.93; CLIP 0.776 vs 0.764; PSNR 16.82 vs 14.36; LPIPS 0.272 vs 0.335) and uses roughly 10× less training data (95k vs 951k models) and 5× less training time (1900 vs 9200 A100-hours), though OpenLRM is slightly faster at inference (5 s vs 7 s). Compared to LGM [37], Compress3D achieves substantially better quality (FID 53.21 vs 88.64; PSNR 16.82 vs 13.23; LPIPS 0.272 vs 0.381) and is nearly 8× faster per shape (7 s vs 55 s), while requiring only marginally more training data (95k vs 80k). Qualitatively, the paper notes that OpenLRM and Shap-E are sensitive to viewing angle (failing on top/bottom views) and that OpenLRM's output up-axis often misaligns with the z-axis, whereas Compress3D produces axis-aligned, view-robust results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the triplane latent diffusion paradigm established by 3DGen [5] and HexaGen3D [21], which project 3D point features onto triplanes via parameter-free mean pooling and then refine them with a UNet. Compress3D departs from this by introducing learnable 3D convolutions for the 3D-to-2D projection and replacing the expensive UNet refinement with a 3D-aware cross-attention that queries a downsampled feature volume, achieving a 10× smaller latent space (0.10M vs. 1.05M parameters) than Shap-E [10]. It also diverges from prior single-condition approaches by introducing a two-stage generation pipeline: a diffusion prior model first estimates a shape embedding from the image embedding (leveraging OpenShape's aligned shape-text-image space), and the triplane diffusion model then conditions on both embeddings simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× latent compression with simultaneous quality gains (FID 53.21 vs. 86.93 for OpenLRM) and reduced training requirements (95k samples, 1900 A100-hours vs. 951k samples, 9200 hours) makes high-quality image-to-3D generation substantially more accessible. The two-stage conditioning strategy addresses the inherent ill-posedness of single-view 3D recovery by injecting richer 3D geometric priors through shape embeddings, a design choice that could generalize to other 3D generation settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex scenes, whether the OpenShape-based shape embedding bottleneck limits ceiling quality, and whether the 7-second generation time can be further reduced without sacrificing the geometric fidelity that FlexiCubes provides.
