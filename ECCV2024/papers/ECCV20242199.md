## Problem

Camera-only BEV perception models trained on a source domain suffer significant performance degradation on target domains due to cross-domain discrepancies in illumination, weather, city, and camera configurations. While unsupervised domain adaptation has been well-studied for 2D vision tasks, it remains largely unexplored for camera-only BEV perception, where BEV features face more severe domain gaps than image-view features because they explicitly encode camera configurations beyond image appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242199/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras for nuScenes) along with their corresponding camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, and produces 3D object bounding boxes (10 categories) or 3D scene segmentation maps in the bird's-eye-view space. During training, an additional image-view decoder with learnable queries is attached to the base BEV model to extract 2D semantic features, but this module is discarded at inference so the testing pipeline and latency remain identical to the underlying BEV detector.

## Method

DA-BEV exploits the complementary nature of image-view features (local 2D semantics, less domain variation) and BEV features (global 3D spatial information, more domain variation) through two query-based adaptation techniques. Query-based Adversarial Learning (QAL) applies domain classifiers to both image-view and BEV decoded query features, then uses the measured inter-domain distance of one modality to scale the adversarial loss of the other, so that samples with large 3D BEV gaps receive stronger 2D alignment and vice versa. Query-based Self-Training (QST) performs cross-view denoising by multiplying image-view classification predictions with BEV detection predictions, then fits a per-class Gaussian distribution over a FIFO queue of denoised predictions to dynamically determine pseudo-label thresholds that select the top-v% predictions per class, mitigating class imbalance. The overall objective jointly minimizes source supervised losses, QST pseudo-label losses, and QAL adversarial losses while maximizing the domain classifiers, and the framework is generic across BEV backbones (PETR, BEVFormer, CAPE) and tasks (3D detection, 3D segmentation).

## Results

On the Daytime→Nighttime adaptation (nuScenes), DA-BEV achieves 20.27 mAP and 26.98 NDS, improving over the PETR baseline (15.92 mAP, 22.44 NDS) by 4.35% and 4.54% respectively. On Clear Weather→Rainy Weather, DA-BEV reaches 30.36 mAP and 36.81 NDS (+5.61% mAP, +3.97% NDS over baseline). On the cross-city Singapore→Boston scenario, DA-BEV attains 20.40 mAP and 28.35 NDS (+3.89% mAP, +6.61% NDS). For cross-camera-system adaptation (Lyft→nuScenes), DA-BEV achieves 15.62 mAP and 24.78 NDS, a 5.60% mAP and 8.34% NDS gain over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-BEV consistently outperforms all four UDA baselines (SFA, MTTrans, STM3D) and the source-only PETR baseline across every adaptation scenario tested. On Daytime→Nighttime, DA-BEV leads the strongest UDA baseline MTTrans (17.00 mAP) by 3.27 mAP and STM3D (16.85 mAP) by 3.42 mAP. The gap widens on Clear Weather→Rainy Weather where DA-BEV (30.36 mAP) surpasses MTTrans (26.00 mAP) by 4.36 mAP, and on cross-camera-system Lyft→nuScenes where DA-BEV (15.62 mAP) beats STM3D (12.81 mAP) by 2.81 mAP. For 3D scene segmentation (Daytime→Nighttime), DA-BEV improves mIoU from 28.01 (PETR) to 35.59, a 7.58-point gain. The paper also validates generalization across baselines: DA-BEV applied to BEVFormer (15.73→19.15 mAP) and CAPE (16.29→20.05 mAP) yields consistent 3.4–3.8 mAP improvements, confirming the method is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

DA-BEV builds directly on PETR, a transformer-based camera-only BEV perception model, by inserting an additional image-view decoder and two query-based adaptation modules (QAL and QST) into its two-stage architecture. Conceptually, it departs from prior UDA work that targeted 2D detection (SFA, MTTrans) or monocular 3D detection (STM3D) by exploiting the structural duality of BEV models: image-view features carry local 2D semantics with less domain shift, while BEV features carry global 3D geometry with more domain shift. The paper positions itself as the first to formulate and solve UDA specifically for camera-only BEV perception, where prior 3D UDA methods either required LiDAR supervision (M²ATS) or operated on single-view monocular inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As autonomous driving systems must deploy across diverse cities, weather conditions, and camera rigs, a practical UDA solution for camera-only BEV perception addresses a real deployment bottleneck that supervised retraining cannot scale to. The complementary-feature insight (2D image-view regularizing 3D BEV adaptation and vice versa) is architecturally grounded and generalizes across backbones and BEV baselines, making it a reusable design principle. Limitations include reliance on single-frame input (no temporal cues), evaluation limited to nuScenes and Lyft, and the additional image-view decoder adding ~25% training time. Open questions include whether the approach extends to temporal BEV models, whether the Gaussian-based pseudo-label thresholding generalizes to more extreme domain shifts, and whether the query-based formulation can be made more efficient for real-time adaptation.
