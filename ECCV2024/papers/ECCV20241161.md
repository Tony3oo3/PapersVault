## Problem

Most diffusion-based video generation methods are straightforward 3D extensions of 2D image diffusion models that attempt to learn motion implicitly, which is difficult given the lack of large-scale high-quality video datasets and the limited capacity of 3D UNets. This paper argues that motion should be explicitly modeled and controlled, identifying video depth (per-frame spatial layout and object distances) and optical flow (cross-frame correspondences) as two complementary representations that existing video generators fail to leverage.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video, a text prompt is first converted to a key frame by a public text-to-image latent diffusion model; for image-to-video, an RGB key frame is provided directly. The key frame is encoded by an open-sourced image-text bi-encoder (CLIP-style) to obtain an unpooled image embedding that preserves spatial layout, and a frames-per-second scalar controls motion magnitude. The system outputs a 17×256×256 video at 3 fps, along with intermediate video depth maps and bidirectional optical flows (image-to-video and video-to-image) that are generated jointly before the video itself.

## Method

MoVideo is a four-stage pipeline: (1) key frame generation, (2) joint video depth and optical flow generation via a 3D diffusion UNet with alternating spatial (2D conv + 2D attention) and temporal (1D conv + 1D attention) blocks, conditioned on the unpooled image embedding via cross-attention and fps via MLP addition, (3) latent video generation by a second 3D diffusion UNet that concatenates the generated depth, an optical-flow-warped latent video, and a computed occlusion mask as additional input channels, and (4) flow-augmented decoding using a 3D VQGAN decoder with deformable-convolution-based cross-frame alignment to the key frame. A key design choice is using image-to-video and video-to-image flows (rather than frame-to-frame) to avoid warping error accumulation, with normalized depth and flows whose magnitude is recovered at inference via an optimization over depth consistency. The warped latent video is randomly zeroed with probability 0.5 during training to prevent the network from copying imperfect motions while still benefiting from its semantic content.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-video generation, MoVideo achieves the best FID of 12.71 and CLIPSIM of 0.3213 on MSR-VTT, and the best IS of 34.13 and FVD of 313.41 on UCF-101, outperforming all baselines. In open-domain text-to-video, MoVideo attains 0.9867 frame consistency, 0.3631 prompt consistency, and an 81.3% user preference rate over VideoFusion. For image-to-video generation on DAVIS, MoVideo achieves PSNR 29.88, LPIPS 0.079, FID 40.14, and FVD 335.53, substantially surpassing Gen1 (PSNR 28.39, LPIPS 0.290, FID 149.73, FVD 973.71).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoVideo outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. On MSR-VTT, it beats the closest competitor Make-A-Video [51] by 0.46 FID (12.71 vs. 13.17) and 0.0164 CLIPSIM (0.3213 vs. 0.3049), and surpasses Latent-Shift [1] by 2.52 FID and 0.044 CLIPSIM. On UCF-101, MoVideo achieves a 53.8-point FVD improvement over Video LDM [4] (313.41 vs. 550.61) and a 388-point margin over CogVideo [31] (313.41 vs. 701.59). In the open-domain user study, MoVideo is preferred by 81.3% of users over VideoFusion [41], which also shows higher frame consistency (0.9867 vs. 0.9759) and prompt consistency (0.3631 vs. 0.3143). For image-to-video on DAVIS, MoVideo reduces FID by 73% (40.14 vs. 149.73) and FVD by 66% (335.53 vs. 973.71) compared to Gen1 [16], while also improving PSNR by 1.49 dB and LPIPS by 0.211. NUWA [64] and CogVideo [31] are the weakest baselines, trailing MoVideo by over 35 FID and 0.06+ CLIPSIM on MSR-VTT.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoVideo extends the latent diffusion framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and VQGAN autoencoder (Esser et al., CVPR 2021) to video generation, but departs from the dominant paradigm of simply adding temporal dimensions to a 2D UNet (as in Video Diffusion Models, Imagen Video, MagicVideo). Instead, it introduces an explicit motion decomposition stage that generates video depth and optical flow from a single key frame before video synthesis, drawing on the Make-A-Video self-supervised training paradigm but adding structured motion conditioning. The flow-augmented decoding stage repurposes deformable convolution (from video super-resolution) for cross-frame alignment during latent-to-pixel reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single static image contains sufficient cues to generate temporally consistent video motion, offering a more interpretable and controllable alternative to implicit motion learning in 3D UNets. However, the four-stage pipeline (key frame → depth/flow → latent video → decoded video) introduces significant inference overhead and error propagation risk, and performance depends heavily on the quality of pretrained depth and flow estimators used for pseudo-labels. Open questions include whether end-to-end joint training of the motion and video stages could reduce the multi-stage cost, and whether the explicit depth+flow decomposition generalizes to longer or higher-resolution videos beyond the 17×256×256 setting tested here.
