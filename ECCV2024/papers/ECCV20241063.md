## Problem

CLIP-based data filtering for image-text contrastive learning only measures cross-modal alignment, leaving it unable to identify underspecified samples that are well-aligned yet semantically vague (e.g., a generic screenshot paired with its URI). This paper argues that alignment alone is insufficient for high-quality data curation and that each unimodal data point's specificity—how uniquely it is defined relative to other samples—must also be considered to eliminate noisy, low-information pairs from billion-scale web-crawled datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy image-text pair dataset (e.g., DataComp small/medium pools of ~12M–128M pairs) and, for each sample, computes four per-sample metrics: image specificity ε_i, text specificity ε_t, negative Lorentzian distance −d_L from MERU, and CLIP cosine similarity cos(θ), plus a binary ImageNet clustering indicator c_IN. These are linearly combined into a single HYPE score used to rank and threshold samples. Notably, ε_i can be applied independently to an image-only pool (no text required) to induce a filtered dataset for image-only self-supervised learning, producing a ranked list of iconic images at any desired size.

## Method

HYPE leverages the hyperbolic embedding space of MERU (a Lorentz-model extension of CLIP) to define an entailment cone around each text embedding, where the cone's half-aperture is determined by the embedding's radial distance in hyperbolic space. Image specificity ε_i is computed as the average entailment loss (the angular deviation of an image embedding from the cones of a reference text set), and text specificity ε_t is defined symmetrically; both are invariant to the choice of reference set as verified empirically. The MERU model is retrained from scratch on CC3M + CC12M + RedCaps with ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14 backbones using the standard InfoNCE loss with Lorentzian distance replacing cosine similarity. The final HYPE score is a simple equal-weight linear combination of ε_i, ε_t, −d_L, cos(θ), and c_IN, requiring no additional training beyond the single MERU model and matching the computational cost of vanilla CLIP scoring. This combination captures both cross-modal alignment and unimodal specificity simultaneously, enabling HYPE to filter out underspecified and misaligned samples that alignment-only methods retain.

## Results

On the DataComp medium filtering track, HYPE 10% combined with DFN achieves a state-of-the-art average score of 0.379, surpassing DFN alone (0.372), CIDS (0.365), T-MARS (0.357), and the CLIP L/14 30% baseline (0.328). HYPE 20% on medium also achieves the best retrieval score (0.286) among all methods, outperforming DFN (0.284) and CIDS (0.258). On the small scale, HYPE 20% reaches 0.176 average, competitive with the weak-supervision baseline WS (0.180) while using fewer samples (2.3M vs. 4.1M). For image-only SSL, ε_i-based filtering consistently outperforms CLIP cosine similarity filtering across all dataset sizes, e.g., MoCo-v3 achieves 59.70% vs. 53.40% ImageNet-1K linear probing at 1.28M images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYPE 10% + DFN achieves the best overall average (0.379) on DataComp medium, beating DFN alone (0.372) by 0.007, CIDS (0.365) by 0.014, T-MARS (0.357) by 0.022, and the CLIP L/14 30% baseline (0.328) by 0.051. HYPE 20% on medium achieves the highest retrieval score (0.286) among all standalone methods, edging out DFN (0.284) and significantly outperforming CIDS (0.258) and T-MARS (0.231). On the small scale, HYPE 20% (0.176) is competitive with WS (0.180) while using 44% fewer samples (2.3M vs. 4.1M), and clearly beats the CLIP 30% baseline (0.173). The combination HYPE + CIDS (0.373) also surpasses CIDS alone (0.365), confirming that HYPE's uni-modal specificity metric is orthogonal to and complementary with cross-modal filtering methods. Notably, HYPE achieves these results using only 27M training data for its MERU filter, compared to DFN's 357M proprietary high-quality pairs, making it substantially more data-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPE repurposes the entailment cone machinery from MERU (Desai et al., ICML 2023), which itself draws on Ganea et al.'s hyperbolic entailment cones (ICML 2018) and Le et al.'s concept-hierarchy embeddings, but redirects it from a training objective to a post-hoc filtering metric. Prior DataComp filtering methods—CLIP score, DFN, CIDS, T-MARS—measure only cross-modal alignment or distributional similarity, leaving underspecified unimodal samples (vague captions, generic images) unfiltered. HYPE departs from this alignment-only paradigm by introducing uni-modal specificity scores (ε_i, ε_t) derived from the average entailment loss against a reference set, a use of the entailment cone that was not explored in MERU's original formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that filtering for specificity in addition to alignment yields a new SOTA on DataComp small and medium (when combined with DFN), and that the image-specificity metric ε_i alone outperforms CLIP-score-based filtering for inducing image-only SSL datasets across SimCLR and MoCo-v3. This suggests that a large fraction of 'well-aligned' web-crawled pairs are semantically underspecified and actively harmful to contrastive training, a problem invisible to alignment-only filters. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at DataComp large/xlarge scales, whether a larger hyperbolic CLIP further amplifies the gap, and whether ε_t can induce a useful text-only corpus for vision-language pre-training. The method's practical appeal is high: it requires no OCR, no additional clustering, and trains its filtering model on only 27M public samples versus DFN's 357M proprietary pairs.
