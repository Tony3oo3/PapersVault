## Problem

2D human pose estimation under extremely low-light conditions remains largely unsolved because existing SOTA methods require paired well-lit and low-light images with ground-truth annotations, which are impractical to obtain due to the difficulty of annotating in near-darkness. Image enhancement and generic domain adaptation methods fail to bridge the severe domain gap (average pixel intensity drops from ~90.5 to ~2.0), leaving a critical gap for real-world nighttime applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241250/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled extremely low-light RGB images as input during inference, with no requirement for paired well-lit counterparts or low-light ground truths. During training, it consumes well-lit images with CrowdPose-format annotations (2,065 pairs in ExLPose) plus unlabeled low-light images. The output is a set of 2D human keypoints per person, represented internally as a person center plus per-keypoint offsets (main teacher/student) and keypoint heatmaps with identity tag maps (complementary teacher), supporting multi-person bottom-up estimation at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages: (1) pre-training a center-based main teacher (DKER) and a keypoint-based complementary teacher (HigherHRNet) on well-lit data augmented with Extreme Low-Light Augmentation (ELLA — gamma correction, brightness scaling, contrast reduction, Gaussian noise), and (2) dual-teacher knowledge acquisition where both teachers generate pseudo labels on real unlabeled low-light images, fused via confidence-thresholded NMS. A novel Person-specific Degradation Augmentation (PDA) selectively darkens individual person crops to mimic low-contrast conditions, challenging the student to surpass the teachers. The student (same architecture as the main teacher) is trained with a combined supervised loss on ELLA-augmented well-lit images and an unsupervised loss on PDA-augmented real low-light images guided by the fused pseudo labels, eliminating any need for low-light ground-truth annotations.

## Results

On the real-world ExLPose-OCN benchmark (360 extremely low-light images captured at night), the proposed dual-teacher method achieves 37.6 AP@0.5:0.95 on average, outperforming the SOTA method LSBN+LUPI [29] by 2.4 AP (6.8%) without using any low-light ground-truth annotations. On ExLPose-test, the method reaches 21.1 AP on the LL-A subset, nearly tripling the best bottom-up baseline (UDA-HE at 7.4 AP) and matching the best top-down baseline (LIME at 21.1 AP) while using only well-lit labels. The method also maintains strong well-lit performance (61.6 AP on WL subset), confirming no catastrophic forgetting during domain adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 14 baselines on ExLPose-OCN. Against the SOTA LSBN+LUPI [29] (which uses paired well-lit and low-light images with ground truths), the method achieves +2.4 AP (37.6 vs 35.2) using only well-lit labels. Among top-down baselines trained with both LL and WL labels, the method beats CPN-all by 5.4 AP, LIME by 6.9 AP, DANN by 8.3 AP, and AdvEnt by 9.0 AP. Among bottom-up baselines using only well-lit labels, the method surpasses the best (LLFlow at 21.4 AP) by 16.2 AP, demonstrating the superiority of the dual-teacher domain-adaptive approach over image enhancement or single-teacher adaptation. On ExLPose-test, the method ties LIME (21.1 AP on LL-A) and outperforms CPN-all (17.6) and DANN (17.8), while also maintaining the best well-lit accuracy (61.6 AP vs. 59.9 for CPN-well), confirming no source-domain degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two bottom-up pose estimation architectures—DKER (center-based) and HigherHRNet (keypoint-based)—and repurposes them as a dual-teacher pair for domain adaptation, a configuration previously explored in semi-supervised pose estimation but not in cross-domain settings. It departs from the SOTA low-light pose estimation method (LSBN+LUPI), which requires paired well-lit and low-light images with ground truths, by eliminating the need for any low-light annotations. The Extreme Low-Light Augmentation (ELLA) and Person-specific Degradation Augmentation (PDA) are novel contributions that bridge the well-lit-to-low-light gap without relying on image enhancement pipelines or generic domain adaptation augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: annotating human poses in extremely low-light images is inherently difficult, so a method that matches or exceeds SOTA performance using only well-lit ground truths removes a major barrier to real-world deployment. The 6.8% (2.4 AP) improvement over the paired-data SOTA on ExLPose-OCN is compelling, though the evaluation is confined to a single dataset family, leaving open whether the dual-teacher + PDA strategy generalizes to other domain gaps (e.g., synthetic-to-real, cross-season). The added computational cost of maintaining two teachers (139s vs. 103s per epoch) and the reliance on a specific backbone (DKER-W32) are practical considerations for adoption.
