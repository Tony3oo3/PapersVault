## Problem

Discriminative anomaly detection methods rely on a two-stage architecture where a reconstructive network restores normal appearance and a separate discriminative network localizes anomalies. This design suffers from overgeneralization (reconstructing anomalous regions) and loss-of-detail in normal regions, and the lack of information exchange between the two stages limits detection of near-in-distribution anomalies. Simply replacing the reconstructive network with a diffusion model, as in DiffAD, does not resolve these issues.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image (resized from 256×256, scaled to [-1,1]) and at each of 20 diffusion steps consumes the current reconstruction estimate, the binarized previous-step mask, a 2D sinusoidal positional encoding, and the timestep index. It produces three outputs per step (anomaly appearance, anomaly mask, normal appearance) that are combined via a closed-form update to generate the next-step image. The final anomaly mask is a weighted combination (λ=0.95) of the pixel-wise mean of all step masks and the reconstruction error, smoothed with a 7×7 mean filter; the image-level score is the maximum of this mask.

## Method

TransFusion reformulates the two-stage discriminative pipeline into a single-stage iterative transparency-based diffusion process: each image is decomposed into normal and anomalous components blended by a transparency factor β, and the reverse process progressively increases β from 0 to 1 over 20 steps, replacing anomalous pixels with their normal appearance while preserving anomaly-free regions. The architecture is a ResUNet with three prediction heads (normal appearance, anomaly mask, anomaly appearance) that jointly estimate the quantities needed for the closed-form step update x_{t-1} = x_t − (β_t−β_{t−1})(M_t⊙ε_t) + (β_t−β_{t−1})(M_t⊙n_t). Training uses synthetic anomalies (Perlin-noise-shaped patches pasted from out-of-distribution regions) with a composite loss of SSIM+L1 for normal appearance, focal+Smooth-L1 for the mask, L2 for anomaly appearance, and a consistency loss enforcing agreement between predicted and ground-truth next-step images. The iterative design lets each step's localization cue guide the next step's reconstruction, directly addressing both overgeneralization and loss-of-detail without requiring a separate discriminative stage.

## Results

TransFusion achieves state-of-the-art anomaly detection AUROC of 98.5% on VisA and 99.2% on MVTec AD, yielding a 98.9% mean AUROC across both datasets. For anomaly localization, it reaches 88.8% AUPRO on VisA and 94.3% AUPRO on MVTec AD (91.6% mean). The method outperforms the previous best overall method (EfficientAD) by 0.3 pp in mean detection AUROC and outperforms the next best discriminative method (DSR) by 4.0 pp in mean detection AUROC, reducing the error by 78.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransFusion outperforms all 10 baselines in mean detection AUROC across VisA and MVTec AD (98.9%), beating the closest competitor EfficientAD (98.6%) by 0.3 pp and the best prior discriminative method DSR (94.9%) by a substantial 4.0 pp margin. On VisA specifically, TransFusion (98.5%) surpasses EfficientAD (98.1%), RD4AD (96.0%), and AST (94.9%), while on MVTec AD it leads with 99.2% versus EfficientAD's 99.1% and AST's 99.2%. In localization (AUPRO), TransFusion (91.6% mean) ranks second overall, trailing only EfficientAD (93.7%) but exceeding all other methods including PatchCore (86.2%) and RD4AD (82.4%). Among diffusion-based baselines, TransFusion significantly outperforms AnoDDPM (80.9% avg det), AnomDiff (not in Table 3 but 83.7% on VisA), and DiffAD (94.1% avg det), confirming that a standard diffusion process is insufficient for surface anomaly detection. The iterative transparency-based formulation gives TransFusion a clear advantage over the two-stage discriminative paradigm (DR/EM, DSR, DiffAD) particularly on the harder VisA dataset with near-in-distribution anomalies.

## Relation to Prior Work

TransFusion reformulates the two-stage discriminative anomaly detection architecture introduced by DRAEM (DR/EM) into a single-stage iterative process, replacing the sequential reconstructive-then-discriminative pipeline with a transparency-based diffusion process that simultaneously localizes and reconstructs anomalies. It adopts the synthetic anomaly generation strategy from MemSeg and the ResUNet backbone common to diffusion models, but departs from prior diffusion-based anomaly detection work (AnoDDPM, DiffAD, AnomDiff) by designing a custom diffusion process where the forward direction increases anomaly prominence rather than adding Gaussian noise. The key conceptual departure from DiffAD, which simply swapped DRAEM's autoencoder for a latent diffusion model while retaining the two-stage structure, is that TransFusion makes localization and reconstruction mutually informative across iterative steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the overgeneralization and loss-of-detail problems plaguing discriminative anomaly detection are not merely artifacts of weak reconstructive architectures but stem from the two-stage formulation itself, since even a powerful diffusion model (DiffAD) fails to resolve them without the iterative reformulation. The 4.0 percentage point gap over the next-best discriminative method (a 78.5% error reduction) on the mean across VisA and MVTec AD is substantial and suggests the iterative information exchange between localization and reconstruction captures complementary normality signals. Practical limitations include the 0.34s inference time (7× slower than DRAEM), though the ablation showing SOTA at only 5 diffusion steps and the applicability of existing diffusion acceleration techniques offer a path forward. Open questions include whether the transparency-based formulation generalizes to other anomaly detection settings (e.g., video, 3D) and whether the iterative process can be distilled into a single-pass model.
