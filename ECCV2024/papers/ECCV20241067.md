## Problem

Personalized image aesthetic assessment (PIAA) requires tailoring score prediction to individual users from very few samples, but existing meta-learning approaches are bottlenecked by the scarcity of PIAA databases (fewer than 500 annotator tasks), severely limiting scalability and generalization to unseen domains. This paper eliminates the dependency on expensive, individually-curated PIAA databases by repurposing readily available general aesthetic and image quality assessment databases as sources of personalization signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241067/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of user-provided RGB image-score pairs (10 or 100 samples) and produces a continuous personalized aesthetic score for any query image. The backbone is an OpenCLIP ViT (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14), and personalization requires training only 912–1,776 scalar coefficients (n×L, where n=6 databases and L is the number of layers), while all backbone and task-vector parameters remain frozen. Inference runs at real-time speeds (≥30 FPS on RTX 3090) because the weighted task vectors are merged into a single ViT model.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes a shared pre-trained ViT backbone independently on six GIAA/IQA databases (AVA, TAD66K, Flickr-AES, PARA, KonIQ-10K, SPAQ) to obtain layer-wise task vectors τ_i^l = θ_ft_i^l − θ_pre^l, each encoding distinct aesthetic/quality tendencies. For personalization, learnable coefficients α_i^l are introduced so that the personalized model is θ_p^l = θ_pre^l + Σ_i α_i^l τ_i^l, with only the n×L coefficients trained via a Bradley-Terry pairwise rank loss on the user's few samples. Coefficients are adaptively initialized by applying softmax to the zero-shot SROCC of each fine-tuned model on the user's samples, so that task vectors most aligned with the user receive higher initial weights. This parameter-efficient design leverages 415,043 samples across databases while requiring only ~1,000 learnable parameters per user, and the frozen task vectors prevent overfitting in the few-shot regime.

## Results

On the Flickr-AES intra-database benchmark (37 users), the proposed method achieves SROCC of 0.668 (10-shot) and 0.748 (100-shot), outperforming the strongest baseline MTCL (0.667 / 0.737). On the cross-database AADB test set, the method reaches 0.556 (10-shot) and 0.654 (100-shot), surpassing even intra-database-trained baselines (MTCL: 0.540 / 0.622). On REAL-CUR, it achieves 0.577 (10-shot) and 0.621 (100-shot), exceeding the best prior method MTCL (0.495 / 0.599) by 8.2 and 2.2 SROCC points respectively. The method requires only 1,776 learnable parameters (n×L coefficients) versus over 23 million for full fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 10 prior PIAA methods spanning meta-learning (MTCL, BLG-PIAA, TAPP-PIAA), attribute-based (PA-IAA, PAM, PIAA-SOA), reinforcement learning (UG-PIAA), and interaction-matrix approaches (IM-PIAA, USAR, PASS). On the Flickr-AES intra-database benchmark, the proposed method marginally outperforms MTCL (0.668 vs 0.667 at 10-shot; 0.748 vs 0.737 at 100-shot) and clearly surpasses PIAA-SOA (0.618/0.691) and IM-PIAA (0.620/0.708). The most striking result is on cross-database AADB evaluation, where the method (0.556/0.654) beats even baselines trained directly on AADB (MTCL intra-database: 0.540/0.622; TAPP-PIAA intra-database: 0.534/0.612), demonstrating superior generalization. On REAL-CUR, the gap widens substantially: 0.577 vs MTCL's 0.495 at 10-shot (8.2-point margin) and 0.621 vs 0.599 at 100-shot. Attribute-based methods (PAM, PA-IAA) and older approaches (PASS, USAR) trail by 10–15 SROCC points, confirming that meta-learning and task-vector-based methods represent the current frontier.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends task arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) from its original multi-task model editing context to personalized image aesthetic assessment, introducing learnable per-layer coefficients to combine task vectors from multiple fine-tuned models. It departs from the dominant PIAA paradigm of meta-learning on individual-annotator databases (BLG-PIAA, TAPP-PIAA, MTCL), which is bottlenecked by the high cost of collecting per-user image collections, by instead treating each publicly available GIAA/IQA database as a single regression task whose task vector encodes a distinct 'flavor' of aesthetic preference. The approach also builds on the broader model-merging literature (Model Soups, Robust Fine-tuning) but is the first to apply coefficient-optimized task vector combinations for few-shot personalization of a regression model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck in PIAA: existing meta-learning methods are limited to fewer than 500 individual tasks, while this approach leverages 415K samples across six databases with only 1,776 learnable parameters, enabling cross-database generalization that surpasses methods trained directly on the test database. The key open question is what happens when a user's preferences are poorly captured by all available task vectors (all zero-shot SROCCs near zero), a case the authors acknowledge but show to be rare. Broader implications include the potential to incorporate additional IQA/GIAA databases or partition large ones to further expand the task vector pool, and the applicability of the coefficient-optimization framework to other few-shot personalization tasks beyond aesthetics.
