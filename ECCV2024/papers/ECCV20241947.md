## Problem

Existing embodied visual tracking methods trained via online reinforcement learning require 12–26 hours of trial-and-error interaction and generalize poorly to unseen targets, environments, and visual distractors. The paper addresses this by asking whether a robust tracking policy can be learned efficiently (within one hour on a consumer GPU) from a fixed offline dataset, while maintaining strong generalization across domains and target categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241947/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives first-person RGB frames and a text prompt (e.g., {"person", "obstacles"}) specifying the target category and relevant background objects. Rather than feeding raw pixels to the policy, the RGB is first processed by a visual foundation model (DEVA or SAM-Track) to produce a text-conditioned segmentation mask where the target is rendered white, obstacles are colorful, and background is black. The policy outputs a continuous 2D action vector: angular velocity in [−30°, 30°]/s and linear velocity in [−1, 1] m/s, executed in the agent-centric coordinate frame.

## Method

The framework has three key technical components. First, a text-conditioned segmentation mask serves as the state representation: a VFM (DEVA or SAM-Track) segments the scene using text prompts, and a re-targeting mechanism (saliency-based scoring by area and centrality) consistently assigns the target a white color across episodes, decoupling identity from arbitrary VFM color assignments. Second, a multi-level data collection pipeline in UnrealCV generates 42k-step offline demonstrations by adding calibrated noise to a state-based PID expert, producing trajectories spanning random walking to tight following. Third, a CNN-LSTM recurrent policy network encodes sequences of segmentation masks into temporal state representations, trained via CQL-SAC (Conservative Q-Learning with Soft Actor-Critic) to avoid Q-value overestimation on out-of-distribution actions. The recurrent architecture provides temporal memory for handling occlusions and abrupt target motion, while the offline RL paradigm eliminates the need for costly online environment interaction.

## Results

On the mean across five unseen environments, the proposed method (trained in 1 hour) achieves a Success Rate of 0.95 and Episode Length of 490/500, outperforming all online RL baselines (12–26 hours training) on both metrics. In Urban City, it achieves a perfect 1.00 SR and 500 EL, and in Snow Village it reaches 0.87 SR vs. 0.80 for the strongest baseline RSPT. The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is Accumulated Reward: RSPT scores 329 vs. 296 for the proposed method, attributed to RSPT's depth-based structure-aware representation for low-height obstacle navigation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline RSPT (RGB-D, 12 h training), the proposed method (1 h) wins on mean Episode Length (490 vs. 478) and Success Rate (0.95 vs. 0.92), and decisively in Snow Village (EL 471 vs. 410, SR 0.87 vs. 0.80), but trails on mean Accumulated Reward (296 vs. 329) and Urban Road SR (0.96 vs. 1.00), where RSPT's depth-based structure representation aids low-obstacle navigation. Compared to TS (26 h), the proposed method achieves higher EL (490 vs. 474) and SR (0.95 vs. 0.86) despite a slightly lower AR (296 vs. 312). Against AD-VAT+ (24 h), the gap widens substantially: the proposed method gains 36 steps in EL (490 vs. 454) and 0.19 in SR (0.95 vs. 0.76). All remaining baselines (SARL, AD-VAT, DiMP, BC, Dagger) are clearly outperformed on every metric, with BC and Dagger showing particularly poor generalization (mean SR 0.32 and 0.64 respectively). The headline finding is that a 1-hour offline training pipeline matches or exceeds methods requiring 12–26 hours of online interaction, while also demonstrating superior robustness to distractors (Table 2: SR 0.63–0.92 vs. 0.02–0.54 for baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant online RL paradigm in embodied visual tracking (SARL, AD-VAT, RSPT, TS), which requires 12–26+ hours of trial-and-error interaction, by introducing offline RL (CQL-SAC) as the training paradigm. For state representation, it replaces raw RGB/depth inputs with text-conditioned segmentation masks generated by open-vocabulary video segmentation models (DEVA, SAM-Track), building on the broader trend of using visual foundation models as mid-level representations for embodied agents. The re-targeting mechanism (recoloring the target mask white via a saliency-based scoring rule) and the multi-level imperfect expert data collection strategy are practical contributions that bridge the gap between VFM outputs and a learnable policy, distinguishing this work from prior VFM-for-robotics efforts that use VFMs only for goal specification or navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1-hour training time on a single consumer GPU (vs. 12–26 hours for online baselines) makes robust EVT policy training accessible to labs without large compute clusters, and the text-prompt-driven generalization to unseen target categories (animals) without retraining is a practical advantage for deployment. However, the sim-to-real validation is limited to a single mobile robot in one corridor and a qualitative VOT video analysis, leaving open whether the approach scales to more complex real-world dynamics (weather, crowds, long-horizon tasks). The paper also acknowledges that cross-domain robustness to dynamic visual distraction remains imperfect, and the reliance on a VFM at inference time introduces a latency and compute cost that may constrain real-time deployment on resource-limited hardware.
