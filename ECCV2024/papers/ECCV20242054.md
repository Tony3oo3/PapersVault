## Problem

Existing UDA methods for adverse scene semantic segmentation face a fundamental dilemma: training on all adverse scenes simultaneously causes hallucinations (e.g., failing to segment sky in night scenes), while training on a single scene leads to underfitting. Current approaches lack structured, multi-granularity instructions to guide models through the varying difficulties across scenes and within individual images, resulting in chaotic adaptation with accumulating pseudo-label errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242054/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB driving scene images (fog, rain, snow, night) at standard resolution alongside labeled Cityscapes source images, and produces pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks over 19 driving scene classes. During training, target-domain images are augmented with five 64×64×3 learnable visual prompt patches placed at the four corners and center of the image; these prompts and the associated Meta-Adapters are entirely discarded at inference, so the deployed model operates on raw RGB input with no additional computational overhead.

## Method

CoDA combines a scene-level Chain-of-Domain (CoD) strategy with an image-level Severity-Aware Visual Prompt Tuning (SAVPT) mechanism on a SegFormer backbone. CoD partitions adverse scenes into easy (daytime: fog, rain, snow, plus a 1200-image mini-dataset generated via a 4-stage GPT-4V/CLIP/Stable-Diffusion pipeline) and hard (night) intermediate domains, training sequentially S→M₁→M₂→T′ with 1.2k and 4k iterations before switching to traditional random sampling for diversity. SAVPT introduces a Severity Perception Trigger that classifies each image as high- or low-severity based on the ratio of grayscale pixels below a threshold σ, then activates one of two parallel branches containing Meta-Visual Prompts (learnable patches added to image corners/center) and Meta-Adapters (lightweight downsample-attention-upsample modules) to focus the model on severity-invariant rather than scene-specific features. Both the visual prompts and adapters are shown to be discardable at inference, confirming they enhance the backbone's inherent domain-invariant feature extraction rather than acting as permanent architectural components.

## Results

CoDA achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all adverse scene benchmarks. On the all-scenes ACDC benchmark, CoDA reaches 72.6% mIoU, outperforming the baseline MIC (70.4%) by 2.2% and HRDA (68.0%) by 4.6%. The most striking gains are on foggy benchmarks: 60.9% mIoU on Foggy Zurich (10.3% over the previous SOTA FogAdapt at 50.6%) and 61.0% mIoU on Foggy Driving (4.6% over SAM-EDA at 56.4%). On nighttime scenes, CoDA achieves 66.4% mIoU on ACDC-Night, a 9.2% improvement over MIC (57.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDA outperforms all 15 compared baselines on every benchmark where both methods report results. Against the direct baseline MIC [25], CoDA gains 4.8% mIoU on ACDC-Fog (71.8 vs 67.0), 7.6% on FZ (60.9 vs 53.3), 4.4% on FD (61.0 vs 56.6), 9.2% on ACDC-Night (66.4 vs 57.2), 1.0% on DZ (61.2 vs 60.2), 0.6% on ND, 0.6% on BD, 3.0% on ACDC-Rain, 4.3% on ACDC-Snow, and 2.2% on ACDC-All. Against the strongest scene-agnostic competitors, CoDA surpasses HRDA [24] by 4.6% on ACDC-All (72.6 vs 68.0) and STA [19] by 11.7% on ACDC-All (72.6 vs 60.9). Among scene-specialized fog methods, CoDA exceeds FogAdapt [26] by 10.3% on FZ and SAM-EDA [54] by 4.6% on FD. Among night-specialized methods, CoDA beats MCGDA [44] by 18.7% on DZ (61.2 vs 42.5) and DANNet [58] by 16.4% on ACDC-All (72.6 vs 50.0). The largest absolute margin is on ACDC-Night, where CoDA's 66.4% mIoU exceeds the next-best scene-agnostic method (MIC at 57.2%) by 9.2 points, confirming that the Chain-of-Domain strategy effectively addresses the night-scene hallucination problem.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoDA builds directly on the MIC (Masked Image Consistency) architecture as its segmentation backbone and extends the Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) paradigm by introducing severity-conditioned, branch-specific visual prompts and adapters that are activated only on target-domain images. Conceptually, it transplants the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning strategy from LLMs into the UDA training loop, creating a 'Chain-of-Domain' curriculum that adapts from easy to hard adverse scenes through intermediate domains—a step-by-step instruction mechanism absent from prior UDA methods that treat all target scenes uniformly. The approach also leverages the finding from Darcet et al. that visual prompts can be omitted at inference, extending this observation to adapters as well, and employs LMMs (GPT-4V, CLIP, Stable Diffusion 2) in a training-free pipeline to synthesize intermediate-domain images that bridge source and target distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.3% mIoU gain on Foggy Zurich and the 85% reduction in cross-seed variance demonstrate that structured, difficulty-ordered adaptation is a practical and effective strategy for UDA under adverse conditions, offering a drop-in improvement over strong baselines like MIC and HRDA. The finding that both Meta-Visual Prompts and Meta-Adapters can be discarded at inference without performance loss is conceptually significant: it suggests these lightweight components act as training-time scaffolds that reshape the backbone's feature representations rather than serving as permanent inference-time modules. Open questions remain about why adapters exhibit this discarding property, whether the simple grayscale-threshold severity metric generalizes beyond driving scenes, and whether the LMM-generated mini-dataset pipeline scales to other domain pairs without manual prompt engineering.
