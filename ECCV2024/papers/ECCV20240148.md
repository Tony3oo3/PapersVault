## Problem

Neural SDFs trained from unoriented point clouds typically produce rough surfaces with noise and miss fine geometric details such as edges and corners, because existing methods impose only point-wise constraints (eikonal, gradient direction) and overlook the geometric information available in the neighborhood of each query point. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a neighborhood-aware filtering mechanism that smooths the implicit field while preserving high-frequency shape features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240148/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw, unoriented 3D point cloud (no normals, no signed distances) as input and produces a learned neural SDF from which a 3D mesh is extracted via marching cubes on the zero level set. For non-watertight scene reconstructions, the 0.001 level set is used instead. Query points are sampled near the surface using the NeuralPull strategy, and neighbor searching is performed within the input point cloud, which is assumed to lie on the surface.

## Method

The core contribution is a non-linear bilateral filter applied directly to the implicit SDF field. Rather than averaging neighbor positions (which over-smooths sharp features), the filter minimizes a weighted bidirectional projection distance: each query point is penalized by its projection onto the normalized gradients of neighboring input points, with weights based on spatial proximity (Gaussian in position) and gradient similarity (Gaussian in angle). The filter is extended from the zero level set to all non-zero level sets by pulling query points to S₀ along the gradient and inversely projecting input points onto the query's level set, enforcing consistency across the entire field. To prevent gradient degeneration (where ∇f=0 becomes a trivial solution), a Chamfer distance loss between pulled query points and the raw point cloud provides a stable gradient constraint. The final training loss combines the zero-level filter, the multi-level field filter, a distance-to-surface term, and the Chamfer constraint, trained on an OccNet-style MLP with SAL geometric initialization.

## Results

On the ABC dataset, the method achieves CD_L2 of 0.011 and F-Score of 0.691, outperforming the closest competitor DIGS (CD_L2=0.021, F-S=0.667) by roughly 48% in Chamfer distance. On FAMOUS, it reaches CD_L2=0.008 and F-S=0.778, again surpassing DIGS (0.015, 0.772). On ShapeNet (3000+ objects), the method attains CD_L2×100=0.0032 and F-Score(0.002)=0.9976, improving over GridPull (0.0086, 0.9896) by 63% in Chamfer distance. On the 3D Scene dataset, it achieves the lowest CD_L2 across all five scenes (e.g., 0.133 on Burghers vs. 0.401 for DeepLS), demonstrating consistent superiority in complex real-world settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves state-of-the-art results across all 14 baselines on every evaluated dataset. On ABC and FAMOUS (Table 1), it outperforms the closest competitor DIGS [5] by 48% in CD_L2 on ABC (0.011 vs. 0.021) and 47% on FAMOUS (0.008 vs. 0.015), while also improving F-Score by 2.4 and 0.6 points respectively. On ShapeNet (Table 2), it surpasses GridPull [10] (the previous best) by 63% in CD_L2 (0.0032 vs. 0.0086) and by 0.8 points in F-Score(0.002) (0.9976 vs. 0.9896). On the SRB real-scan benchmark (Table 4), it achieves the lowest CD_L1 and one-sided Chamfer distance across all five objects, e.g., 0.052 vs. 0.063 for DIGS on Anchor. On the 3D Scene dataset (Table 5), it dominates all five scenes in CD_L2 and CD_L1, with the largest margin on Burghers (0.133 vs. 0.401 for DeepLS [7], a 3× improvement). The method also leads in edge Chamfer distance (Table 3), achieving ECD_L2×100=0.399 versus 1.055 for P2S [12] and 1.255 for NeuralPull [4], confirming superior preservation of sharp geometric features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'pull' paradigm from NeuralPull [4] by adding a bilateral filtering operator that leverages neighborhood geometric information in the implicit field, rather than relying solely on per-point gradient constraints (eikonal, direction) as in IGR, SIREN, and DIGS. The filtering mechanism itself draws on the bidirectional projection distance from edge-aware point set resampling (EAR [16]) and the local projection operator tradition, but repurposes these for implicit SDF fields where the 'image' is an infinite-resolution continuous function. The key conceptual departure is extending the filter from the zero level set to non-zero level sets by projecting input points along the gradient, which regularizes consistency across the entire signed distance field rather than just the surface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent practical limitation in unsupervised neural SDF learning: reconstructed surfaces exhibit noise and lose fine geometric details (edges, corners) when point density is insufficient. The implicit bilateral filter offers a principled, differentiable mechanism to denoise while preserving high-frequency features, and the extension to non-zero level sets provides a useful regularization that aligns the entire field. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very sparse or heavily noisy point clouds, whether the neighbor-search approximation (using NN(q) instead of the pulled point) degrades at scale, and whether the filtering can be combined with learned priors for generalization beyond single-shape fitting.
