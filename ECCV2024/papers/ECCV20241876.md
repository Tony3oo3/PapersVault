## Problem

Mamba (selective state space models) has demonstrated superior long-range dependency modeling over Transformers, yet its application to multi-task dense scene understanding remains unexplored. Specifically, how to adapt Mamba for multi-task training and how to achieve effective cross-task correlation within a Mamba-based architecture are open questions that this paper addresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241876/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 448×576 for NYUDv2 or 512×512 for PASCAL-Context) and simultaneously produces multiple full-resolution dense prediction maps: semantic segmentation, depth, surface normal, object boundary, human parsing, and saliency. A Swin-Large encoder pretrained on ImageNet-22K extracts multi-scale features, and lightweight task-specific heads (patch expand + linear layer) generate pixel-wise outputs at the original input resolution.

## Method

MTMamba introduces a Mamba-based decoder with two core block types. The Self-Task Mamba (STM) block uses a Mamba-based Feature Extractor (MFE) that applies the SS2D operation—extending 1D S6 selective scan to 2D by scanning along four spatial directions and summing results—to capture long-range spatial dependencies per task, followed by an input-dependent SiLU gate for adaptive feature selection. The Cross-Task Mamba (CTM) block explicitly models inter-task interaction by concatenating all task features, processing them through a shared MFE to produce a global representation, and then using a task-specific sigmoid gate to adaptively fuse each task's own MFE output with the global representation. The three-stage decoder alternates patch expansion with skip connections from the encoder, two STM blocks, and one CTM block per stage, enabling progressive refinement with cross-task knowledge exchange at each resolution level.

## Results

On NYUDv2, MTMamba achieves 55.82 miIOU (semantic segmentation), 0.5066 RMSE (depth), 18.63 mErr (normal), and 78.70 odsF (boundary), outperforming all CNN-based and Transformer-based baselines on every task. On PASCAL-Context, MTMamba reaches 81.11 mIoU (segmentation), 72.62 mIoU (parsing), 84.14 maxF (saliency), 14.14 mErr (normal), and 78.80 odsF (boundary). The headline gains over the previous best Transformer-based method (InvPT) are +2.08 mIoU on segmentation, +5.01 mIoU on parsing, and +4.90 odsF on boundary detection on PASCAL-Context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTMamba outperforms all CNN-based decoders (Cross-Stitch, PAP, PSD, PAD-Net, MTI-Net, ATRC, ASTMT) by a wide margin on both datasets, with the largest gaps on PASCAL-Context segmentation (+13.44 mIoU over ATRC-BMTAS) and parsing (+9.69 mIoU over ATRC-BMTAS). Against the strongest Transformer-based baselines, MTMamba beats InvPT by +2.08 mIoU on segmentation, +5.01 mIoU on parsing, and +4.90 odsF on boundary on PASCAL-Context, and by +2.26 mIoU on segmentation and +0.60 RMSE on depth on NYUDv2. Compared to MQTransformer, MTMamba leads by +2.18 mIoU on segmentation, +5.21 mIoU on parsing, and +4.90 odsF on boundary on PASCAL-Context. The only metric where MTMamba does not achieve the best result is saliency detection on PASCAL-Context (84.14 maxF vs. InvPT's 84.81), a gap of 0.67 points. On NYUDv2, MTMamba achieves the best score on all four tasks, confirming consistent superiority across both indoor (NYUDv2) and mixed indoor/outdoor (PASCAL-Context) settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTMamba extends the Mamba state-space model (Gu & Dao, 2023) and its 2D visual adaptation via SS2D (V-Mamba, Liu et al., 2024) into the multi-task dense prediction setting, where prior Mamba work has been limited to single-task problems. It departs from the dominant Transformer-based decoder line (InvPT, MQTransformer) by replacing attention-based cross-task fusion with a Mamba-based cross-task Mamba (CTM) block that fuses task-specific and global representations through a learned gating mechanism. The overall encoder-decoder architecture with skip connections follows the established multi-task dense prediction paradigm, but the decoder's internal structure—pairing self-task Mamba blocks for intra-task long-range modeling with a shared CTM block for inter-task knowledge exchange—is a novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that Mamba-based decoders can outperform both CNN and Transformer decoders in multi-task dense scene understanding while offering linear (rather than quadratic) sequence complexity, which is practically important for high-resolution dense prediction. The CTM block provides a concrete mechanism for cross-task interaction in the SSM setting, an area previously unexplored. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets and a single encoder family (Swin), and the method still relies on a heavy pretrained Swin-Large encoder, so the full efficiency advantage of Mamba is not yet realized end-to-end. Open questions include whether the CTM mechanism generalizes to larger task suites or 3D/point-cloud settings, and whether lighter Mamba-native encoders can close the gap with Transformer encoders.
