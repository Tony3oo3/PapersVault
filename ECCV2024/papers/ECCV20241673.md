## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time rendering and fast training for novel-view synthesis but demands millions of Gaussians with high-dimensional attributes (spherical harmonic color coefficients, rotation quaternions, opacity, scaling, position), resulting in over 1 GB of storage and ~20 GB of GPU RAM per scene. This makes 3D-GS impractical for memory-constrained devices and low-bandwidth applications, and the frequent densification process produces many redundant Gaussians that slow down both training and rendering without improving quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241673/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images of a scene along with an SfM-initialized sparse point cloud and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and produces a compressed 3D Gaussian representation consisting of integer latent vectors (for color, rotation, and opacity) plus a small MLP decoder, alongside full-precision position, scaling, and base-band color attributes. The SH degree is fixed at 3 to balance quality and memory. Rendering proceeds at full resolution after a one-time amortized decode of all latents, and the progressive training schedule starts at 30% image resolution and scales to 100% over 70% of the 30K training iterations.

## Method

EAGLES compresses per-Gaussian attributes by replacing the high-dimensional color (SH) and rotation (quaternion) vectors with low-dimensional integer latents decoded by a shared MLP, using the Straight-Through Estimator to maintain differentiability during training; opacity is also quantized, which acts as a soft regularizer that prevents opacity saturation and reduces floater artifacts. A coarse-to-fine progressive training strategy renders at 30% resolution initially and increases to full resolution via a cosine schedule over 70% of iterations, stabilizing optimization and reducing per-iteration cost. An influence-pruning stage computes per-Gaussian weights W_i = Σ_p α_i·T_i directly during rasterization (zero extra cost) and removes the lowest-weight 15% of Gaussians every 5000 iterations, eliminating redundant points that are occluded or negligibly scaled. Together these three components reduce storage by 10–20×, cut training time by ~20%, and increase rendering FPS while matching 3D-GS reconstruction quality.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, EAGLES achieves 27.23 dB PSNR with only 54 MB storage (vs. 734 MB for 3D-GS), a ~14× reduction, while matching 3D-GS's 27.21 dB PSNR and training 2× faster (21m34s vs. 41m33s). On Tanks&Temples, EAGLES reaches 23.37 dB PSNR at 29 MB storage and 227 FPS (vs. 3D-GS's 23.61 dB at 411 MB and 154 FPS). On Deep Blending, EAGLES actually surpasses 3D-GS in PSNR (29.86 vs. 29.41 dB) while using 13× less storage (52 MB vs. 676 MB) and training 1.7× faster. The EAGLES-Small variant further reduces storage to 47 MB (Mip-NeRF360) and 19 MB (T&T) with only ~0.3 dB PSNR loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3D-GS (the primary baseline), EAGLES matches reconstruction quality within 0.22–0.26 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and Tanks&Temples, while reducing storage by ~14× (54 MB vs. 734 MB) and training ~2× faster (21m34s vs. 41m33s). On Deep Blending, EAGLES actually outperforms 3D-GS by 0.31 dB PSNR (29.86 vs. 29.41) with 13× less storage. Compared to INGP, EAGLES achieves 1.6–1.7 dB higher PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and T&T while rendering 15–16× faster (131 vs. 9 FPS, 227 vs. 14 FPS), though INGP trains slightly faster. Against Plenoxels, EAGLES dominates on all metrics: +4.15 dB PSNR, 39× less storage, and 19× higher FPS on Mip-NeRF360. Versus Mip-NeRF360, EAGLES trades a small 0.45 dB PSNR drop for 3000× faster rendering (131 vs. 0.06 FPS) and 48h→21m training time, making it practical for real-time applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

EAGLES builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) by Kerbl et al., applying quantization techniques borrowed from neural network compression (vector quantization with Straight-Through Estimator) to per-point attributes like spherical harmonic color coefficients and rotation quaternions. It also draws conceptual inspiration from neural field compression work (VQAD, SHACIRA) but repurposes these ideas for explicit point cloud representations rather than implicit grids. The progressive coarse-to-fine training and influence-based pruning are novel contributions specific to the Gaussian splatting pipeline, addressing the redundancy introduced by its aggressive densification schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D-GS has become the dominant real-time novel view synthesis method, but its memory footprint (millions of Gaussians, >1 GB storage, ~20 GB GPU RAM) severely limits deployment on consumer hardware and bandwidth-constrained applications. EAGLES demonstrates that 10–20× storage reduction is achievable with negligible quality loss, making the representation practical for a much wider range of devices. Open questions include whether position, scale, and base-band color can also be quantized (currently excluded due to initialization sensitivity), and whether rendering can be performed in lower precision after decoding to unlock further speedups. The opacity-quantization-as-regularizer insight suggests that quantization constraints can improve optimization stability beyond mere compression, a direction worth exploring more broadly.
