## Problem

Existing tuning-free image editing methods based on text-to-image diffusion models either require careful hyperparameter tuning that limits them to narrow edit types, or demand computationally expensive fine-tuning and high-quality inversion to preserve the source image. There is no single approach that is simultaneously fast, versatile across diverse edit types (local attribute changes, emotion edits, object replacement, global stylisation), and maintains a strong balance between applying the desired edit and preserving unedited regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image, a source prompt describing the initial image, and a target prompt specifying the desired edit, and produces a single edited image. It operates on the Stable Diffusion v1.4 latent diffusion model with DDIM sampling at T=50 steps, encoding the input via a VAE into latent space. No fine-tuning, additional training data, or exact inversion is required; the only user-specified parameters are the two prompts and a small set of fixed hyperparameters (guidance scales and rescaling bounds).

## Method

The core mechanism modifies the DDIM denoising step by adding two self-guidance energy functions (guiders) to the classifier-free guidance term. A Self-attention Guider matches self-attention maps between the current sampling trajectory and the DDIM inversion trajectory across all UNet layers to preserve spatial layout, while a Feature Guider applies L1 (or L2 for stylisation) norms on UNet up-block outputs to preserve local visual appearance. Both guiders require two forward passes per step—one on the inversion latent and one on the current latent, both conditioned on the source prompt—to compute aligned inner representations. A noise rescaling mechanism then dynamically scales the combined guider gradient by a factor derived from the ratio of the CFG norm to the guider gradient norm, clamped within user-defined bounds, ensuring a stable editing-to-preservation balance throughout all 50 sampling steps without per-image tuning.

## Results

On 80 examples across 4 edit types, Guide-and-Rescale achieves LPIPS of 0.228/0.243 and CLIP of 39.07/24.26, providing the best trade-off between editing quality and original-image preservation. On the 500-sample dog-to-cat FID evaluation, it outperforms all baselines. Inference time is 24.26 s per image on a single A100 GPU, roughly 8× faster than PnP (197 s) and ~3× faster than EDICT (68 s), while being ~3× slower than the P2P+NPI variants (~8.5 s). In a 62-user study (960 pairwise comparisons), the method is preferred over all baselines on editing quality (50–85%) and preservation (49–70%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PnP [4], Guide-and-Rescale achieves a comparable CLIP score (39.07 vs. 39.55) but dramatically better LPIPS (0.228 vs. 0.366) and is ~8× faster (24 s vs. 197 s), since PnP severely corrupts the original image structure. Against EDICT [26], the method trades a slightly higher LPIPS (0.228 vs. 0.221) for a much lower CLIP (39.07 vs. 47.13) and ~3× faster inference (24 s vs. 68 s), making it a better quality–speed–preservation balance. Against the P2P+NPI family [7,13,14], Guide-and-Rescale is ~3× slower (24 s vs. ~8.5 s) but achieves substantially better CLIP scores (39.07 vs. 42–44) and competitive LPIPS, whereas P2P+NPI Prox wins on LPIPS (0.170) at the cost of weaker editing fidelity. Against MasaCtrl and ProxMasaCtrl [2,6], the method dominates on both LPIPS (0.228 vs. 0.267–0.306) and CLIP (39.07 vs. 94–100), reflecting that those methods introduce significant artifacts and geometry distortions. In the user study, Guide-and-Rescale is preferred over MasaCtrl (85% editing / 70% preservation) and ProxMasaCtrl (82% / 63%) by a wide margin, and over EDICT (56% / 59%) and PnP (60% / 61%) by a moderate margin, while it is roughly tied with P2P+NPI Prox on editing (50%) but preferred on preservation (55%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-guidance mechanism of Epstein et al. [5], which was limited to simple edits (object position, size, color), into a general-purpose framework for arbitrary real image editing. It departs from the three dominant families of prior work—optimization-based fine-tuning (Imagic, SINE), internal-replacement methods (Prompt-to-Prompt, MasaCtrl, PnP), and inversion-improvement methods (NTI, NPI, EDICT)—by instead treating the DDIM inversion trajectory as a continuous guidance signal via energy functions (self-attention and feature guiders) rather than as a source of cached features to substitute or a reconstruction target to optimize. The noise rescaling mechanism, which dynamically balances the norms of classifier-free guidance and guider gradients at each step, is a novel practical addition not present in prior self-guidance or proximal-guidance work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, tuning-free editing pipeline that is roughly 8× faster than PnP and 3× faster than EDICT while achieving a better CLIP/LPIPS trade-off, making it attractive for interactive or batch editing applications. However, the approach still requires manually specified source and target prompts, is validated only on Stable Diffusion v1.4, and the user study (62 raters, 960 pairwise comparisons) is modest in scale. Open questions include whether the guider design and rescaling strategy transfer to newer architectures (SDXL, DiT-based models), whether the method handles multi-object or spatially complex edits without additional conditioning, and whether the fixed 50-step DDIM schedule is optimal or whether fewer steps suffice with the rescaling mechanism.
