## Problem

Fine-grained cross-view localization models suffer significant performance degradation when deployed in a new target area different from their training area, due to the domain gap. In realistic deployment, collecting fine ground truth (accurate camera locations) in the target area is expensive or infeasible, while noisy ground truth (rough locations with tens-of-meters error) is readily available. The paper addresses how to adapt a pre-trained model to a new area using only ground-aerial image pairs without fine ground truth locations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240829/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground-level RGB image and a geo-referenced aerial image patch covering the camera's local surroundings as input, with the pairing between the two known from a noisy localization prior. The output is a coarse-to-fine set of heat maps (7 levels for CCVPE, 3 levels for GGCVT) at increasing spatial resolution, from which the predicted 2D location is extracted as the argmax of the highest-resolution map. The pseudo ground truth is generated as a single-mode Gaussian-smoothed distribution centered at the teacher's argmax prediction, rather than the full multi-modal heat map.

## Method

The approach uses knowledge self-distillation where a pre-trained teacher model generates pseudo ground truth for target-area images to train a student model initialized from the teacher's weights. Three key designs reduce the teacher's uncertainty and noise: (1) mode-based pseudo GT extracts only the single argmax mode and applies Gaussian label smoothing, avoiding propagation of multi-modal uncertainty; (2) coarse-only supervision computes the infoNCE loss only on the first K' lower-resolution levels of the student, suppressing positional noise from the limited-accuracy pseudo GT; (3) outlier filtering trains an auxiliary student model on all target data, then retains only the top T% of samples where the auxiliary student's predicted location is closest to the teacher's, removing unreliable predictions before training the final student model.

## Results

On the VIGOR cross-area test set, the proposed weakly-supervised student model reduces CCVPE's mean localization error from 4.38 m to 3.85 m (↓12%) with known orientation, and from 5.35 m to 4.27 m (↓20%) with unknown orientation. For GGCVT, the mean error drops from 5.19 m to 4.34 m (↓16%). On KITTI, CCVPE's longitudinal mean error improves from 6.55 m to 6.18 m (↓6%) and GGCVT's from 9.27 m to 8.56 m (↓8%), with lateral errors also consistently reduced. The student models remain below the Oracle (fully supervised fine-tuning) gap: CCVPE student 3.85 m vs. Oracle 2.31 m, GGCVT student 4.34 m vs. Oracle 2.91 m on VIGOR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art cross-view localization methods used as teacher models deployed directly in the target area: CCVPE [56] (convolutional, 7-level coarse-to-fine heat maps) and GGCVT [39] (transformer-based, 3-level coarse-to-fine heat maps). The proposed knowledge self-distillation approach wins on every metric for both baselines across both datasets. The largest gains appear on VIGOR with unknown orientation, where CCVPE's mean error drops by 20% (5.35→4.27 m) and median by 15% (1.97→1.67 m). On KITTI, improvements are more modest (6–13% mean error reduction), likely due to the narrower field of view and more constrained geometry. Notably, the student models still trail the Oracle (supervised fine-tuning with fine GT) by 1.5–1.4 m in mean error, but the Oracle requires fine ground truth that is typically unavailable in practice. The paper also shows that a naive student without mode-based pseudo GT or outlier filtering (St-M-OF) actually performs worse than the teacher baseline (5.34 m vs. 5.16 m for GGCVT), confirming that the proposed uncertainty-reduction and filtering components are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes knowledge self-distillation (Born-Again Networks, Self-distillation) for source-free unsupervised domain adaptation in fine-grained cross-view localization, a setting where no prior work had addressed adapting to a new area without fine ground truth. It builds directly on two state-of-the-art coarse-to-fine localization backbones (CCVPE, GGCVT) and departs from standard UDA techniques (entropy minimization, adversarial alignment) by instead generating mode-based pseudo ground truth from the teacher's highest-resolution heat map and filtering outliers via an auxiliary student model. The key conceptual departure from prior KD-for-UDA work is recognizing that standard uncertainty measures (e.g., entropy) are ill-suited for spatial heat maps, motivating the single-mode pseudo GT and the teacher-student distance-based outlier filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: fine ground truth is expensive or infeasible to collect in new deployment areas, yet noisy localization priors (e.g., degraded GNSS) are readily available. The consistent 12–20% mean-error reduction across two SOTA methods and two benchmarks, achieved with only ~6 hours of extra training on a single GPU, makes this a low-cost, broadly applicable adaptation recipe. Limitations include the gap to an oracle (supervised fine-tuning with true fine GT) and evaluation on only two architectures; open questions include whether the mode-based pseudo GT and outlier filtering transfer to other spatial-prediction tasks (e.g., depth, segmentation) and whether the approach degrades when the domain gap is very large.
