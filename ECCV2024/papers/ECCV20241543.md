## Problem

Existing image enhancement methods, whether pixel-prediction CNNs or 3D LUT weight-prediction networks, still rely on convolutional computation during inference, incurring high FLOPs and latency that are prohibitive on edge devices with tight power and compute budgets. The paper asks whether a trained neural network can be entirely replaced by lookup tables, eliminating all neural network arithmetic while preserving near-state-of-the-art retouching quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes an 8-bit RGB image, downsamples it to an unprecedented 32×32, and splits each pixel into 4-bit MSB and 4-bit LSB maps fed into two parallel branches. At inference, the entire pipeline is table lookups: the MSB/LSB values index a 3D Channel LUT (FP32), and quantized pooled features index 2D Weight LUTs (INT8) to produce 20 weights that linearly combine 20 basis 3D LUTs. The final enhanced image is obtained by trilinear interpolation over the resulting image-adaptive 3D LUT, with total model storage under 1 MB.

## Method

ICELUT uses six 1×1 convolution layers (3-channel depth) per branch to extract per-pixel color features while keeping the receptive field at 1×1, making the network directly convertible to a 3D LUT. An adaptive average pooling layer compresses spatial features to 1×1, and a novel split fully connected (SFC) layer divides the C=10 pooled channels into K=5 groups of L=2, each mapped by a small FC to N=20 weights that are summed to weight the basis LUTs; this reduces LUT memory from V^C to K·V^L. After training, the CNN backbone becomes a 3D Channel LUT and the SFC becomes K 2D Weight LUTs, so inference requires only index lookups and interpolation with 26K FLOPs (vs. 713M for the CNN counterpart). The key empirical finding is that channel depth, not spatial receptive field, drives enhancement quality, and the 1×1 RF makes the model robust to extreme downsampling (32×32), enabling 0.4 ms GPU / 7 ms CPU latency.

## Results

On MIT-Adobe FiveK, ICELUT achieves 25.27 dB PSNR, 0.918 SSIM, and 7.51 ΔE, within 0.26 dB of the SOTA CLUT (25.53 dB) while being 6.4× faster on a low-end smartphone (7.8 ms vs. 141.8 ms). On PPR10K, ICELUT trails CLUT by only 0.12–0.16 dB PSNR across three retoucher splits. The key efficiency gain is dramatic: ICELUT's pure-LUT inference requires just 0.026 M FLOPs (vs. 75 M for CLUT) and 780 KB storage, running in 0.35 ms on GPU and 0.97 ms on desktop CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICELUT is closest in quality to CLUT [22], the current SOTA 3D-LUT method, trailing by only 0.26 dB PSNR on FiveK and 0.12–0.16 dB on PPR10K, yet it is 6.4× faster on a Cortex-A55 smartphone (7.8 ms vs. 141.8 ms) and uses 33% less storage (780 KB vs. 1,168 KB). Against the original 3D LUT [21], ICELUT actually edges ahead by 0.08 dB PSNR on FiveK while cutting FLOPs from 77 M to 0.026 M. Compared to pixel-prediction networks, ICELUT outperforms CSRNet [5] by 0.06 dB PSNR and is 65× faster on smartphone; it beats HDRNet [4] by 0.95 dB and DPE [3] by 1.52 dB with orders-of-magnitude lower latency. The only metric where ICELUT is clearly behind is SSIM (0.918 vs. CLUT's 0.926), a small gap the authors attribute to the 1×1 receptive field omitting spatial context. Overall, ICELUT trades a negligible ~0.2 dB PSNR for a >10× speedup and near-zero FLOPs, making it the fastest image retoucher evaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D LUT weight-prediction framework (Zeng et al., TPAMI 2020) and CLUT (Zhang et al., ACM MM 2022), which use a lightweight CNN to predict coefficients for combining basis 3D LUTs, by eliminating the CNN entirely at inference. It draws on the CNN-to-LUT conversion paradigm from super-resolution work (Jo et al., CVPR 2021) but departs critically by showing that multi-channel (RGB) inputs are essential for color enhancement, unlike the single-channel LUTs sufficient for super-resolution. The key architectural departure is the fully pointwise convolution backbone combined with a split fully connected layer, both designed specifically to be convertible into compact lookup tables, yielding the first purely LUT-based image enhancer with no neural network computation at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing inference to pure table lookups (0.4 ms GPU, 7 ms CPU, 26K FLOPs vs. 713M for the CNN counterpart), ICELUT makes real-time image retouching feasible on low-end smartphones (Cortex-A55) where even lightweight CNNs are too costly. The finding that a 32×32 downsampled input suffices—thanks to the 1×1 receptive field—further shrinks the computational footprint without meaningful quality loss. The ~0.2 dB PSNR gap versus CLUT, attributable to the absence of spatial context, remains an open question: whether hybrid approaches (e.g., a tiny spatial module plus LUT) could close this gap while preserving the efficiency advantage. More broadly, the split-FC-to-LUT conversion technique may generalize to other tasks where a small network predicts per-pixel or per-region parameters.
