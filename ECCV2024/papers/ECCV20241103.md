## Problem

Existing diffusion-based domain generalization methods (DSI, CDGA) rely solely on offline augmentation with LDMs, incurring prohibitive computational costs (e.g., 5M+ generated images) while failing to achieve state-of-the-art performance. More fundamentally, they ignore the latent space of LDMs, which encodes rich domain-invariant visual knowledge that could directly benefit discriminative DG models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241103/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DG classifier takes RGB images and produces class predictions, while the LDM (Stable Diffusion v1-4) receives the same images along with the class label as a text prompt to generate N candidate augmented images. The class label plays a dual role: it conditions the LDM's denoising process and serves as the target for the confidence-based pseudo-generation step. The synthetic dataset is refreshed every T=5 epochs, with each image replaced by a single sampled image formed from the most distinct style and most similar content among the candidates.

## Method

DomainFusion simultaneously exploits LDM in two spaces. In the latent space, Latent Distillation (LD) creates a pseudo-generation bridge: the input image is element-wise multiplied by the DG model's confidence for its true class (a contrast-reduction corruption), then passed through the LDM's denoiser; the resulting diffusion loss backpropagates gradient priors into the DG model, where lower confidence yields more corruption and thus higher loss, providing a supervised signal. In the pixel space, an online lightweight augmentation strategy generates N candidates per image every T epochs, decomposes them into per-channel style (mean, std) and content (normalized pixels), selects the most style-distinct candidate via KL divergence and the most content-similar candidate via a weighted combination of feature cosine similarity and classification confidence, then recombines them via AdaIN to form one new image. The total training objective combines cross-entropy on the raw source data, cross-entropy on the synthetic data, and the LD loss, all optimized end-to-end with a single shared LDM requiring no finetuning.

## Results

With a ResNet-50 backbone, DomainFusion achieves SOTA average accuracy of 67.5% across five DG benchmarks (PACS 90.0, VLCS 79.2, OfficeHome 72.4, TerraIncognita 51.1, DomainNet 44.6), outperforming the best non-generation method MIRO by +1.6pp on average. With a stronger RegNet-Y-16GF backbone (SWAG pre-trained), DomainFusion reaches 75.3% average accuracy, surpassing MIRO by +1.2pp on average and ERM by +7.3pp. Notably, DomainFusion reduces the number of generated images by over 97% on DomainNet compared to CDGA while simultaneously improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest non-generation baseline MIRO (ResNet-50), DomainFusion wins on all five datasets with margins of +1.5pp (PACS), +0.2pp (VLCS), +1.9pp (OfficeHome), +0.7pp (TerraIncognita), and +0.3pp (DomainNet). With the RegNet-Y-16GF backbone, DomainFusion outperforms MIRO on VLCS (+0.1pp), OfficeHome (+3.0pp), TerraIncognita (+1.7pp), and DomainNet (+2.1pp), but trails by 0.8pp on PACS (96.6 vs 97.4). Against diffusion-based DG methods, DomainFusion substantially surpasses CDGA on PACS (90.0 vs 88.5), OfficeHome (72.4 vs 68.2), and DomainNet (44.6 vs 43.1), and DSI on PACS (90.0 vs 78.3) and OfficeHome (72.4 vs 67.3), while using a shared LDM without finetuning and generating 97% fewer images than CDGA. Non-diffusion generation methods (Mixstyle, Mixup, GroupDRO) all underperform the ERM baseline on several datasets, whereas DomainFusion consistently exceeds them by 3–5pp on average. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that both Latent Distillation (+4.6pp over ERM) and online augmentation (+4.9pp over ERM) contribute independently, with their combination yielding the full 83.4% average on OfficeHome.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based domain generalization initiated by DSI and CDGA, which used LDMs solely as offline data generators in the pixel space. DomainFusion departs from this by introducing a dual-use framework: it extracts gradient priors from the LDM's latent space (via a Latent Distillation module inspired by Score Distillation Sampling) to directly supervise the discriminative DG network, while simultaneously performing online lightweight augmentation in the pixel space. The sampling strategy for augmentation borrows the content-style decomposition from Dai et al. and AdaIN style transfer, but applies them in a novel iterative, online setting with a single shared LDM rather than per-domain fine-tuned models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making diffusion-based DG computationally feasible: reducing generated images by over 97% while outperforming prior diffusion-based methods that required millions of samples or per-domain LDM fine-tuning. The conceptual contribution—that LDM's latent space encodes domain-invariant visual knowledge transferable to discriminative tasks via gradient distillation—opens a path for leveraging pretrained generative models beyond mere data augmentation. Open questions include whether the confidence-based contrast reduction is the optimal bridge between discriminative and generative spaces, whether the approach extends to video or other modalities, and whether the online iterative generation can be further accelerated for very large-scale DG scenarios.
