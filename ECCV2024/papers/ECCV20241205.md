## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration methods focus on distinguishing degradation types (degradation priors) via classifiers or prompt modules, leading to imbalanced performance across tasks and poor scalability as more degradation types are added. The paper argues that the essence of restoration is extracting intrinsic image content rather than identifying corruption patterns, and that Mask Image Modeling (MIM) offers a natural mechanism for this but has not been properly adapted to low-level vision tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241205/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single degraded RGB image of arbitrary corruption type (haze, rain, Gaussian noise, motion blur, low-light, kernel blur, JPEG artifacts) at 64×64 (SwinIR) or 128×128 (PromptIR) resolution and outputs a restored RGB image of the same size. During pre-training, the input is a pixel-wise masked version of the degraded image (50% of pixels zeroed), while at inference the full unmasked degraded image is fed in — this input integrity gap is the central challenge the fine-tuning stage must resolve. The pipeline is plug-and-play and introduces no additional inference-time computational overhead on top of the base restoration network.

## Method

RAM is a two-stage pipeline. In the pre-training stage, degraded images are randomly masked at the pixel level (1×1 patch, 50% ratio) and the network is trained with L1 loss to reconstruct the corresponding clean image only at masked positions, forcing it to learn intrinsic content priors from corrupted inputs rather than degradation-specific patterns. In the fine-tuning stage, the input integrity gap (masked training input vs. full inference input) is bridged by selectively fine-tuning only the most critical layers, identified via the proposed Mask Attribute Conductance (MAC). MAC extends Integrated Gradients and neuron conductance along a nonlinear Mask Attribute Path (MAP) that progressively reveals pixels from zero to full input, quantifying each layer's contribution to closing the distribution shift. Only the top-k% layers by MAC score (as few as 10%) are fine-tuned while the remainder stay frozen, preserving the learned image priors while adapting to full-image inference.

## Results

Across seven restoration tasks (dehazing, deraining, denoising, motion deblurring, LLIE, kernel deblurring, JPEG artifact removal), RAM-PromptIR achieves a 0.59 dB average PSNR gain over the second-best all-in-one method, while RAM-SwinIR yields a 2.40% relative PSNR improvement over vanilla SwinIR. On Gaussian denoising (Table 2), RAM-SwinIR reaches 30.82 dB average PSNR on BSD68 and 31.07 dB on Urban100, surpassing SwinIR (30.65/30.62) and matching Restormer (30.95/31.07). RAM-PromptIR further improves over PromptIR by 0.21 dB on Urban100 (30.97 vs. 30.76), with the largest gains at higher noise levels (σ=50: +0.28 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAM consistently outperforms its base architectures: RAM-SwinIR improves over SwinIR by 0.17 dB (BSD68) and 0.45 dB (Urban100) average PSNR, while RAM-PromptIR gains 0.02 dB and 0.21 dB over PromptIR respectively, with the largest per-level gain of +0.28 dB at σ=50 on Urban100. Against single-task specialists, RAM-SwinIR matches Restormer on Urban100 (31.07 dB) but trails by 0.13 dB on BSD68 (30.82 vs. 30.95), while surpassing NAFNet by 0.45/1.15 dB and MPRNet by 0.99/1.97 dB on the two datasets. Among all-in-one methods, RAM dominates: it outperforms TAPE by 0.71/1.50 dB and AirNet by 2.83/5.06 dB on BSD68/Urban100, confirming that the MIM-based pre-training strategy yields substantially more balanced multi-degradation performance than degradation-classification or prompt-based approaches. The DL baseline [8] is far behind all learned methods (16.15/18.92 dB), serving as a lower bound. Notably, the paper reports that RAM-PromptIR achieves the best average score across all seven tasks, edging out the second-best all-in-one method by 0.59 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAM adapts the Masked Image Modeling paradigm from MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) to low-level vision, departing from vanilla MIM by using pixel-level (1×1) masking at 50% ratio with paired degraded-to-clean supervision rather than token-level masking at 75% for self-reconstruction. Conceptually, it shifts the all-in-one restoration objective from learning degradation-specific priors (as in AirNet, PromptIR) to learning a shared image content prior, a direction partially anticipated by TAPE's natural-image prior but realized here through a fundamentally different mechanism. The fine-tuning stage introduces Mask Attribute Conductance, extending the neuron conductance framework of Dhamdhere et al. (ICLR 2019) and Integrated Gradients to a novel mask-attribution path that identifies the minimal set of layers needed to bridge the masked-to-complete input gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of RAM (applicable to SwinIR, PromptIR, and presumably other backbones without architectural changes or extra inference cost) makes it a practical tool for practitioners building unified restoration systems. The observed trade-off—fine-tuning more layers improves in-distribution PSNR but degrades out-of-distribution generalization—highlights an underexplored tension in transfer learning for restoration that warrants further study. Open questions include whether the MIM pre-training strategy scales to video restoration or to substantially larger degradation spaces, and whether MAC-based layer selection generalizes beyond the specific masked-input-to-complete-input gap addressed here.
