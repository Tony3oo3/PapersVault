## Problem

Naturalistic driving datasets are heavily biased toward straight-driving trajectories, causing maneuver prediction models to fail on minority classes like left and right turns. Existing remedies—oversampling, SMOTE, and loss reweighting—only resample or reweight within the same imbalanced dataset, leaving the fundamental scarcity of diverse turning-maneuver samples unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of (x, y, θ) states for each surrounding vehicle over a fixed observation horizon, along with a lane-graph representation of the HD map. At inference, rather than outputting a single hard class, it produces continuous probability scores for each maneuver (LT, ST, RT) by evaluating the map-adapted maneuver context against per-class Gaussian KDE density functions learned from the balanced intersection dataset, enabling the planner to weigh low-probability alternatives.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage pipeline that decouples maneuver-representation learning from geometric generalization. In stage 1, a maneuver backbone (1D CNN + MLP encoder → GRU autoregressive model → ScaleNet interaction module) is trained self-supervisedly via Contrastive Predictive Coding (infoNCE loss) on a newly collected drone-based intersection dataset with balanced LT/ST/RT distribution, maximizing mutual information between historical maneuver context and future state embeddings. In stage 2, the learned representations are transferred into a LaneGCN map-adaptation module trained with cross-entropy on the ego-centric Argoverse dataset to absorb diverse road geometries. Crucially, at inference the biased classification head is discarded; maneuver scores are instead computed by evaluating the map-adapted context against per-class Gaussian KDEs (Epanechnikov kernel) fitted to the balanced intersection training set, preventing the model from defaulting to the dominant straight class.

## Results

On the Argoverse validation set, the proposed two-stage heterogeneous-dataset method achieves the highest AUC across all three maneuver classes: 0.952 (LT), 0.944 (ST), and 0.952 (RT). For the critical minority classes, recall improves dramatically over sampling baselines: LT recall rises from 0.743 (oversampling) / 0.753 (SMOTE) to 0.942, and RT recall rises from 0.696 / 0.815 to 0.989. The vanilla Argoverse-only baseline completely fails on turning maneuvers (0.000 recall for both LT and RT), confirming severe class-imbalance bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three sampling-based baselines trained on the Argoverse ego-centric dataset: vanilla (no balancing), oversampling, and SMOTE augmentation. The vanilla baseline is catastrophically biased, predicting nearly all samples as Straight (1.000 ST recall) while achieving 0.000 recall on both LT and RT, with AUCs below 0.70 for turning classes. Oversampling and SMOTE improve turning-maneuver recall substantially (LT: 0.743/0.753; RT: 0.696/0.815) but still trail the proposed method by 19–29 percentage points on recall for minority classes. The proposed heterogeneous-dataset approach achieves the best AUC in all three classes (0.952/0.944/0.952) and the highest F1 for LT (0.790) and RT (0.773), while also attaining the highest ST precision (0.987), indicating that the intersection-centric pre-training stage provides maneuver representations that sampling alone cannot replicate. The authors argue that sampling methods merely rebalance sample counts without introducing the geometric and scenario diversity needed to learn robust turning-maneuver features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) for self-supervised representation learning and LaneGCN for map-aware trajectory prediction, but departs fundamentally from prior long-tail mitigation strategies (sampling, loss reweighting) by introducing a two-stage heterogeneous-dataset training pipeline. Rather than manipulating the composition of a single ego-centric dataset, the authors pair a balanced intersection-centric drone dataset (for maneuver representation learning via infoNCE) with an ego-centric driving dataset (for geometric generalization via GCN-based map adaptation), then replace the learned classifier head at inference with Gaussian KDE-based likelihood scoring. This positions the work at the intersection of self-supervised representation learning, transfer learning, and non-parametric density estimation for autonomous driving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-tail problem in maneuver prediction is fundamentally a data-diversity problem that cannot be solved by resampling or loss reweighting within a single ego-centric dataset, offering a practical recipe (balanced intersection data + diverse driving data in two stages) that practitioners can adopt. The publicly released VDC-Trajectory-Dataset (15,479 trajectories, 10 locations, 30 Hz) is a useful resource for intersection-centric research. However, the evaluation is limited to three maneuver classes (LT/ST/RT) on a single ego-centric dataset, baselines are restricted to vanilla/oversampling/SMOTE without comparison to more sophisticated long-tail or prediction architectures, and the method does not model ego-vehicle action dependency, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to richer maneuver taxonomies, multi-interaction scenarios, or closed-loop planning.
