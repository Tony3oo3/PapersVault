## Problem

ProbCover, a leading low-budget active learning method, selects points to maximize data coverage using a top-hat (binary) radius δ, but its performance is extremely sensitive to this hyperparameter and the original heuristic for choosing δ frequently fails. The paper asks whether a smoother notion of coverage can retain the theoretical guarantees of ProbCover while being far more robust to hyperparameter choice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242226/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-extracted self-supervised feature embeddings (SimCLR, SCAN, or DINO) of an unlabeled image pool of size N (typically tens of thousands), a small initial labeled set L, and a per-iteration budget B (typically equal to the number of classes). At each iteration it outputs a batch of B points from the unlabeled pool to be annotated; the final classifier is either a 1-NN or a linear (multiclass logistic regression) model trained on the accumulated labeled set. No raw pixel manipulation is needed at selection time—only kernel evaluations in feature space.

## Method

The paper defines a generalized coverage C_k(L) = E_x[max_{x'∈L} k(x,x')] using a smooth kernel (default: Gaussian with lengthscale 1), which subsumes ProbCover's top-hat coverage as a special case (Corollary 1) and is backed by a generalized 1-NN error bound (Theorem 1). MaxHerding greedily maximizes this coverage by selecting, at each step, the point that maximizes the marginal increase in coverage; Proposition 3 shows this has the exact form of kernel herding but with a 'max kernel' that only credits the nearest labeled point rather than summing contributions, preventing redundant selections in dense regions. The greedy procedure enjoys a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee via submodularity (Proposition 2) and runs in O(N) per selected point using a dynamic-programming trick. A non-greedy variant solves the same objective via kernel k-medoids (Proposition 4 shows equivalence to k-means/k-medoids for constant-diagonal kernels), achieving marginally better coverage at >10× the runtime.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet (using 1-NN classifiers with SimCLR/DINO features), MaxHerding consistently achieves the highest test accuracy across all 10 active learning iterations, outperforming the strongest baseline Typiclust by a clear margin. Uncertainty-based methods (Entropy, Margin, BADGE) perform worse than random selection in the low-budget regime, while ProbCover is highly sensitive to its radius hyperparameter and underperforms MaxHerding on CIFAR datasets. In the imbalanced setting, MaxHerding's advantage over Typiclust widens further, and in the high-budget regime (1000 samples/iteration), Margin and Entropy eventually catch up, though MaxHerding still significantly outperforms Typiclust and ProbCover.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaxHerding outperforms all seven baselines across the four benchmark datasets. Against Typiclust [19] (the most competitive representation-based method), MaxHerding achieves consistently higher accuracy on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, with the gap widening on imbalanced datasets; the authors attribute this to Typiclust's typicality scores drawing it toward dense regions while MaxHerding's Gaussian kernel spreads selections more evenly. ProbCover [55] performs best on TinyImageNet but is significantly worse on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, demonstrating the instability of its δ-radius heuristic; MaxHerding's Gaussian kernel is far more robust to hyperparameter choice. KernelHerding [10] is substantially better than uncertainty-based methods but still clearly below Typiclust and MaxHerding. Coreset [46] and BADGE [3] are fast but achieve the lowest downstream accuracy among representation-based methods. Entropy [52] and Margin [42] are worse than random selection in the low-budget regime (≤10× number of classes), though Margin recovers in the high-budget regime (1000 samples/iteration). The non-greedy kernel k-medoids variant marginally outperforms MaxHerding in coverage and accuracy but requires more than 10× the runtime, making MaxHerding the recommended default for practitioners.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes ProbCover's (Yehuda et al., 2023) top-hat-kernel coverage objective to an arbitrary kernel, showing that the original method is a special case (Corollary 2) while smooth kernels like the Gaussian are far more robust to hyperparameter choice. The greedy maximization algorithm, MaxHerding, is shown to be kernel herding (Chen et al., 2010) with a 'max kernel' that counts only the nearest labeled point's contribution rather than summing all nearby contributions (Proposition 3), distinguishing it from standard herding which would over-sample dense regions. The non-greedy variant connects to k-medoids (Kaufman & Rousseeuw, 2009) and thereby to Typiclust (Hacohen et al., 2022) through k-means clustering, unifying several previously separate low-budget active learning approaches under a single generalized coverage objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners in low-budget active learning (e.g., medical imaging where only tens of labels are available), MaxHerding offers a simple, fast, and hyperparameter-robust method that consistently outperforms both uncertainty-based and representation-based baselines across CIFAR, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet. The key practical limitation is that the method is specifically designed for the low-budget regime; in mid- and high-budget settings, standard uncertainty-based methods like margin sampling still dominate, so the approach does not replace the full active learning toolkit. An open question the authors raise is whether these low-budget coverage-based strategies transfer to the increasingly common fine-tuning of large pretrained models, where the feature geometry and budget dynamics differ substantially.
