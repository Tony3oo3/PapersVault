## Problem

Existing end-to-end autonomous driving methods couple perception and planning through 2D bird's-eye-view (BEV) representations, which cannot capture volumetric geometry, occlusion, off-ground objects, or subtle 3D motion cues critical for safe navigation. Moreover, these methods typically require extensive manual 3D annotations (e.g., BEV segmentation) for supervision, hindering scalability. The paper asks whether self-supervised learning of 3D volumetric representations—without such annotations—can yield more expressive features that directly improve motion planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240443/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the agent consumes two timesteps of 6-camera RGB images (current and previous), a scalar ego-vehicle speed, and a categorical navigational command (left/right/forward), and outputs a set of K future waypoints relative to the ego-vehicle. During the self-supervised pre-training stage, the same multi-view images additionally produce rendered RGB reconstructions and per-voxel occupancy predictions over a 50 m × 50 m × 8 m volume discretized at 0.5 m (200 × 200 × 16 voxels); LiDAR point clouds are used only to generate pseudo-occupancy labels and are not required at test time.

## Method

NeMo is a two-stage framework. In stage 1 (self-supervised pre-training), a deformable-attention transformer encoder projects 2D image features into a 3D volumetric grid via camera-parameterized reference points, and a NeRF-style volumetric rendering module composites per-voxel features along camera rays using predicted occupancy to reconstruct RGB images; a motion flow module predicts both per-voxel 3D flow fields and SE(3) ego transformations to warp current volumetric features into future frames, providing spatiotemporal consistency supervision alongside occupancy prediction against LiDAR-voxelized pseudo-labels. In stage 2 (planning fine-tuning), the predicted future volumetric features are fused with current features through a temporal self-attention module, and a three-layer transformer decoder cross-attends with navigational-command and ego-speed embeddings to regress waypoints; the fine-tuning loss jointly optimizes waypoint L2 error, image reconstruction, and occupancy prediction to preserve the quality of the estimated future features.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop evaluation, NeMo achieves an average L2 displacement error of 0.84 m and an average collision rate of 0.30%, outperforming the prior best method UniAD (1.03 m, 0.31%) by 18.4% in L2 error. On CARLA Town05 closed-loop evaluation, NeMo achieves a Driving Score of 75.87 (Short) and 42.57 (Long), surpassing VAD-Base (64.29 / 30.31) by 18% and 40% respectively. The volumetric representation alone (NeMo Scratch, no pre-training) already matches or exceeds BEV-based baselines, and the full pipeline with self-supervised pre-training, motion flow, and temporal attention yields the largest gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeMo outperforms all 11 baselines on both L2 error and collision rate in the nuScenes open-loop evaluation. Against the strongest prior methods, NeMo reduces average L2 error from 1.03 m (UniAD) and 1.07 m (VAD*) to 0.84 m, a 18–21% improvement, while also achieving the lowest collision rate (0.30% vs. 0.31% for UniAD and 0.38% for VAD*). On CARLA closed-loop, NeMo's Driving Score of 75.87 (Town05 Short) exceeds VAD-Base (64.29) by 18% and TransFuser (54.52) by 39%; on the harder Long route, the gap widens to 40% over VAD-Base (42.57 vs. 30.31). Notably, NeMo does not use any BEV segmentation supervision or privileged labels, whereas UniAD, VAD, and TransFuser rely on such annotations. Even without self-supervised pre-training, the volumetric architecture alone (NeMo Scratch) matches BEVFormer's L2 error (1.02 vs. 1.03) while halving its collision rate (0.59% vs. 0.60%), suggesting the 3D representation itself is a key contributor.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeMo departs from the dominant BEV-based paradigm in end-to-end driving (UniAD, VAD, BEVFormer) by replacing the 2D bird's-eye-view representation with a full 3D volumetric feature space, arguing that planar BEV cannot capture occlusion, off-ground geometry, and subtle 3D motion. It repurposes neural volumetric rendering (NeRF-style ray integration) as a self-supervised pre-training signal for driving, extending prior static-scene 3D representation work (Lai et al., Gkioxari et al.) to dynamic outdoor settings through a per-voxel motion flow module that disentangles ego SE(3) motion from object-level deformation. The deformable attention encoder is adapted from Deformable DETR's 2D-to-3D cross-attention, and the planning head follows the transformer-decoder waypoint regression design of UniAD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that a 3D volumetric representation, learned self-supervisedly via neural rendering and occupancy prediction, yields meaningfully better motion planning than the widely adopted BEV alternative, with the gap widening under dynamic conditions. However, the voxel-based formulation carries a cubic memory/compute cost that limits spatial resolution and scalability, and the authors acknowledge ray artifacts from the ill-posed rendering supervision. Open questions include whether more efficient volumetric encodings (occupancy networks, octrees) can close the gap, whether the self-supervised pre-training transfers across driving domains without retraining, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively small nuScenes and CARLA Town05 benchmarks.
