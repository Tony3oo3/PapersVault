## Problem

Object detection in crowded scenes requires extensive manual annotation (~42.4 s per object), and while SAM offers powerful zero-shot segmentation, it produces many false positives and struggles with occluded, densely packed objects when using dense grid prompts. No prior work effectively adapts SAM for few-shot detection in such crowded, occlusion-heavy settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241851/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at inference and requires only 10 labeled images with bounding-box annotations (not pixel-level masks) for training. SAM generates high-quality pseudo masks from these boxes to supervise the lightweight adapter and PWD-Net. At inference, the pipeline outputs per-instance segmentation masks (256×256) that are trivially converted to bounding boxes, with a multi-crop strategy applied to improve small-object recall.

## Method

Crowd-SAM freezes both SAM (ViT-L) and DINOv2 (ViT-L) and trains only a small set of learnable parameters. A DINOv2-based binary segmentation head produces a semantic heatmap that restricts point-prompt generation to foreground regions, reducing false positives from background prompts. An Efficient Prompt Sampler (EPS) iteratively decodes batches of prompts, uses PWD-Net confidence scores to select valid masks, and prunes prompts already covered by those masks, enabling very dense grids (up to 192×192) without out-of-memory errors. The Part-Whole Discrimination Network (PWD-Net) scores each of SAM's four candidate masks by combining a parallel IoU head over SAM's Mask and IoU Tokens with a DINOv2 semantic token (Hadamard product of the mask and DINOv2 features, globally pooled), then multiplies the IoU score with the foreground/background score to rank masks. Training uses only 2,000 iterations with Adam (lr=1e-5) on a single GPU, supervised by SAM-generated pseudo masks via dice loss and MSE on the discrimination score.

## Results

On CrowdHuman val, Crowd-SAM with 10 labeled images achieves 71.4% AP and 83.9% Recall, while the multi-crop variant (Crowd-SAM*) reaches 78.4% AP and 85.6% Recall, approaching the best fully-supervised detector DINO (86.7% AP) with only 10 annotated images versus full supervision. In the few-shot setting, Crowd-SAM outperforms the strongest few-shot baseline De-FRCN (46.4% AP) by 25 AP points. On CityPersons (50 shots), Crowd-SAM achieves 33.3% AP, surpassing all few-shot baselines, and on OCC-Human it leads the fully-supervised Pose2Seg by 9.2% AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against fully-supervised detectors on CrowdHuman, Crowd-SAM* (78.4% AP) surpasses FCOS (76.3%) by 2.1 AP and is within 1.9 AP of ATSS (80.3%), but trails the strongest fully-supervised methods Iter-SRCNN (85.9%) and DINO (86.7%) by 7.5–8.3 AP, a gap attributable to using only 10 labeled images. In the few-shot setting, Crowd-SAM dominates all baselines: it exceeds De-FRCN (46.4% AP) by 25 AP, TFA (46.9%) by 24.5 AP, and FSCE (43.0%) by 28.4 AP, while also achieving substantially lower MR⁻² (75.1 vs. 84–86). Among SAM-based methods, Crowd-SAM* improves Recall over the SAM* baseline (79.6%) by 6 points and over SAM (65.6%) by 18.3 points, and vastly outperforms Matcher (8.0% AP, 23.9% Recall). On CityPersons (50 shots), Crowd-SAM (33.3% AP) beats TFA (30.6%), FSCE (32.2%), and De-FRCN (25.5%), and on OCC-Human it leads the fully-supervised Pose2Seg (22.2% AP) by 9.2 AP. On COCO, the multi-class Crowd-SAM (22.0% AP) lags behind supervised Faster R-CNN (33.5%) and BCNet (34.6%) by roughly 11–13 AP, but the drop from COCO to COCO-OCC is only 1.4 AP, indicating robustness to occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

Crowd-SAM builds directly on SAM as its segmentation backbone and DINOv2 as a semantic feature extractor, positioning itself at the intersection of foundation-model adaptation and few-shot object detection. It departs from prior few-shot detectors (TFA, FSCE, De-FRCN) that fine-tune standard CNN-based detectors, instead leveraging SAM's pre-trained segmentation knowledge with only a few learnable parameters (EPS and PWD-Net). Compared to other SAM adaptation methods (HQ-SAM, Matcher, Per-SAM), Crowd-SAM specifically targets the challenges of crowded and occluded scenes—dense prompt generation and part-whole mask discrimination—rather than general domain shift or speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a foundation model like SAM, with minimal adaptation, can serve as a practical smart annotator for crowded scenes, reducing annotation cost from ~42 seconds per object to near-automated labeling with only 10 reference images. The 78.4% AP on CrowdHuman (vs. 86.7% for fully-supervised DINO) shows a small gap that may narrow with more shots or better prompt strategies. Limitations include reliance on visible annotations, significant latency increase from multi-cropping (8.1s vs. 1.7s per image), and validation primarily on pedestrian detection; open questions include generalization to diverse object categories and whether the PWD-Net design transfers to non-crowded or 3D settings.
