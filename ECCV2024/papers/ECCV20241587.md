## Problem

Instance-wise quality assessment of generated samples has traditionally relied on pre-trained feature extractors (e.g., VGG16) to embed real and fake samples into a shared space, but different extractors yield inconsistent rankings and the approach fails entirely for domains lacking a robust universal encoder, such as medical images, 3D shapes, or anime-style art. This paper asks whether sample quality can be assessed directly from the generative model's own latent space, eliminating the need for any external feature extractor.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single latent code z_g sampled from the generative model's latent space and a reference set Z of latent codes extracted from ~60k training samples, and outputs a scalar latent density score. For GANs (StyleGAN2, SDF-StyleGAN), latent codes are obtained from the W-space of the mapping network; for VAEs and LDMs, they come from the image encoder output, with 2D spatial representations flattened for LDMs. Crucially, the score is computed entirely in latent space without generating any image pixels, enabling pre-generation quality estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is a latent density score function that computes the average Gaussian kernelized Euclidean distance between a query latent code and all training latent codes, parameterized by a bandwidth σ. The key insight is that generative models map similar data points to nearby latent embeddings, so high-density latent regions correspond to well-represented training data (and thus higher-quality reconstructions), while low-density regions correspond to underrepresented cases that receive less training supervision. The hyperparameter σ controls the trade-off between local and global density: small σ emphasizes local clusters (yielding more diverse high-score selections), while large σ captures global manifold structure. The approach is model-agnostic, requiring only access to the latent space and training data encodings, and has been validated across VAEs, GANs, and Latent Diffusion Models on 2D images, 3D shapes, and text-conditioned generation.

## Results

On few-shot image classification, using top-k latent density score codes as support samples yields 67.15% 1-shot accuracy on miniImageNet (vs. 63.87% for random codes, a +3.28% gain) and 73.60% 5-shot accuracy. On tieredImageNet, top-k codes achieve 68.39% 1-shot and 77.42% 5-shot accuracy, outperforming random-k by 2.35% and 0.32% respectively. Qualitatively, the latent density score ranks generated samples consistently with visual quality across VAEs (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CelebA), GANs (FFHQ, AFHQ Dog/Cat), and Latent Diffusion Models (CelebA-HQ, LSUN-Bedrooms, LSUN-Churches, Stable Diffusion), as well as 3D shapes (ShapeNet) and non-ImageNet domains (BreCaHAD, Danbooru).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary quantitative baseline is the realism score [Kynkäänniemi et al., NeurIPS 2019], which measures sample quality via a pre-trained VGG16 feature extractor. On ImageNet-like domains (FFHQ, CelebA-HQ), the latent density score correlates with the realism score (Figure 8b), confirming alignment. However, on non-ImageNet-like domains (BreCaHAD medical images, Danbooru anime), the realism score fails to produce visually distinguishable rankings between top and bottom samples, while the latent density score clearly separates high-quality from low-quality generations (Figure 6). The latent density score also extends to 3D shape generation (SDF-StyleGAN on ShapeNet), where the realism score is entirely infeasible due to the absence of a suitable 3D feature extractor. Computationally, the latent density score operates directly on latent codes without requiring image generation, offering a significant efficiency advantage over the realism score which must decode and embed full images. The few-shot classification experiment (Table 1) further validates the quality ranking: top-k density codes outperform random-k by 3.28% on 1-shot miniImageNet, demonstrating that the score identifies genuinely higher-quality samples that benefit downstream tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of instance-wise quality assessment (realism score, rarity score) that projects generated and real samples into a pre-trained feature extractor's space (VGG16, DINO, CLIP) and measures proximity or rarity there. Instead, it repurposes the latent space of the generative model itself as the assessment space, applying a standard Gaussian kernel density estimate over training-data latent codes to score newly sampled codes. The conceptual link to the rarity score is acknowledged (both estimate local density on a data manifold), but the key distinction is that the manifold here is the model's own latent geometry rather than an external classifier's embedding, eliminating the dependency on a domain-specific feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for domains lacking robust pretrained feature extractors (medical imaging, 3D shapes, anime art) and for large-scale text-to-image pipelines where pre-generation scoring avoids expensive pixel synthesis. The σ hyperparameter provides a useful trade-off between local and global density, enabling diversity-aware selection. Open questions include whether the approach extends to very high-dimensional latent spaces (deep hierarchical VAEs, video models) and whether always selecting high-density codes systematically under-represents rare but important cases (uncommon medical conditions, rare species), a limitation the authors acknowledge but only partially address via small-σ selection.
