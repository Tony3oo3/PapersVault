## Problem

Existing affordance representations for human-object interaction focus almost exclusively on contact regions, ignoring equally important non-contact patterns such as relative body-part orientations and spatial positioning. Capturing these holistic HOI patterns would require laborious multi-view 3D annotations that no existing dataset provides, making it difficult to learn a comprehensive affordance representation at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241368/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 3D object mesh (any geometry, from datasets like BEHAVE, ShapeNet, SAPIEN, or SketchFab) and automatically generates text prompts via a vision-language model. It produces a Comprehensive Affordance (ComA) distribution: for every pair of object surface point i and human surface point j, a joint probability P_ij(p, n) over relative 3D position p (voxelized grid) and relative surface-normal orientation n (Fibonacci-spiral grid on S²). From this single distribution, three derived affordance maps are extracted: contact scores (proximity × antiparallel normal alignment), orientational tendency (negated Shannon entropy of the orientation marginal), and spatial occupancy (counted position marginal).

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, the object is rendered from multiple viewpoints (8 for static, 40 for dynamic objects), and a pre-trained 2D inpainting diffusion model inserts a human into each rendering using text prompts; the key innovation is Adaptive Mask Inpainting, which progressively refines the inpainting region over diffusion timesteps by applying PointRend to intermediate denoised latents to detect the generated human, thereby preserving the original object geometry and preventing false affordances. Second, each 2D HOI image is lifted to 3D by predicting SMPL-X pose/shape with a single-view model, then resolving depth ambiguity via RANSAC-based joint re-projection across multiple views combined with a collision loss (SDF-based, from COAP) to enforce physical plausibility. Third, ComA is learned by aggregating Gaussian kernels over the canonicalized relative positions and orientations of all human-object vertex pairs across the synthetic 3D HOI samples, yielding a discrete joint distribution that encodes both contact and non-contact interaction patterns without any manual annotation.

## Results

On the BEHAVE dataset, ComA achieves 52.82% SIM and 0.126 MAE for human contact distribution, and 70.55% SIM and 0.0288 MAE for object contact distribution. These results are obtained by evaluating the contact distributions derived from ComA against ground-truth contact scores from BEHAVE. The comparison is conducted on the same set of 2D HOI images generated by the paper's pipeline, isolating the quality of the 3D lifting step. ComA outperforms both baselines (DECO and IAGNet) on all reported metrics when applied to the identical input images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two SOTA contact-estimation methods: IAGNet (object-side contact) and DECO (human-side contact), both evaluated on the same 2D HOI images generated by the paper's pipeline to isolate the 3D lifting quality. ComA substantially outperforms DECO on human contact: 52.82% SIM vs. 21.66% SIM, and 0.126 vs. 0.246 MAE (×100), roughly a 2× improvement in both metrics. Against IAGNet on object contact, ComA achieves 70.55% SIM vs. 66.34% SIM and 0.0288 vs. 0.0329 MAE (×100), a more modest but consistent gain. Notably, both baselines also perform worse when applied to BEHAVE test images (IAGNet: 63.86% SIM; DECO: 17.39% SIM), suggesting the synthetic HOI images from the diffusion pipeline are at least as representative of real-world affordance as the lab-captured BEHAVE test set. The authors argue this validates the pipeline's ability to extract 3D contact from 2D cues more faithfully than methods trained on manual annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the affordance learning literature, which has predominantly focused on contact-based representations (2D heatmaps, 3D contact scores on objects or humans), by introducing Comprehensive Affordance (ComA) that jointly models contact, orientational, and spatial patterns between all human-object vertex pairs. It builds directly on CHORUS [20] for spatial relation modeling and prompt generation, and on COAP [51] for SDF-based collision losses in depth optimization. A key methodological departure is the use of a pre-trained 2D inpainting diffusion model as a data source for 3D HOI samples, replacing laborious manual annotation with a rendering-inpainting-uplifting pipeline, and the proposed Adaptive Mask Inpainting to prevent the diffusion model from corrupting object geometry during human insertion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ComA provides a unified, scalable affordance representation that goes beyond the contact-only paradigm, enabling downstream tasks like HOI reconstruction that require both proximity and orientational cues simultaneously. The synthetic-data pipeline removes the bottleneck of manual annotation and extends affordance learning to an unbounded set of 3D objects, though it inherits limitations from single-view 3D human prediction and the depth ambiguity resolved only weakly by multi-view consistency. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to articulated or deformable objects, whether the 2D diffusion model's affordance knowledge is truly 3D-consistent or merely a 2D projection artifact, and whether the optimization-based application can be made real-time for robotics or interactive systems.
