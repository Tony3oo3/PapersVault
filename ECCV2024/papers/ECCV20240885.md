## Problem

The log-sum-exp pairwise (LSEP) loss, effective for standard multi-label classification, underperforms BCE in long-tailed multi-label classification (LTMLC). The paper identifies two root causes: (1) LSEP indiscriminately exploits label co-occurrence dependencies that shift between training and test distributions, especially for tail classes, and (2) it becomes overconfident in negative labels whose features resemble positive ones, producing false positives with high predicted probabilities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB images (randomly cropped and resized) and outputs per-class probabilities for 20 classes (VOC-LT) or 80 classes (COCO-LT). A key structural detail is that the loss is computed column-wise across the M×N logit matrix (one column per class, spanning all samples in a mini-batch) rather than row-wise per sample, meaning positive and negative examples for a given class are drawn from different images. The backbone is a ResNet-50 pretrained on ImageNet, with class-aware resampling to balance the long-tailed distribution during training.

## Method

The proposed DR loss has two components. First, C-LSEP restructures the LSEP computation from sample-wise to class-wise: for each class k, all samples containing k form the positive set and all others form the negative set, forcing the model to learn discriminative class-specific features rather than relying on co-occurrence shortcuts. A gradient scaling factor γ₁ (set to 5) modulates the loss to emphasize hard examples. Second, the Negative Gradient Constraint (NGC) adds a term Σ_{j∈neg} e^{γ₂·s_j} to guarantee that negative labels always receive non-zero gradients, lowering the probability threshold at which they are penalized and preventing overconfident false positives. The final loss is log(1 + Σ_{i∈pos}Σ_{j∈neg} e^{γ₁(s_j−s_i)} + Σ_{j∈neg} e^{γ₂·s_j}), with γ₂ (7 for VOC-LT, 10 for COCO-LT) independently tunable. The paper further demonstrates that DR loss is complementary to BCE-based losses (e.g., DB Focal), and their combination yields the best overall performance.

## Results

On VOC-LT, the proposed DR loss achieves a total mAP of 78.01%, and when combined with DB Focal, reaches 78.75%. On COCO-LT, DR loss achieves 54.10% total mAP, and DR+DB Focal reaches 54.55%. Compared to the closest prior SOTA (DB Focal: 78.29% on VOC-LT, 53.45% on COCO-LT), DR loss is competitive on VOC-LT (−0.28%) and superior on COCO-LT (+0.65%), while the combined variant outperforms DB Focal on both datasets. Against the directly related LSEP loss, DR loss improves mAP by approximately 5% on VOC-LT and 7% on COCO-LT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DR loss outperforms LSEP [17] by ~5% mAP on VOC-LT (78.01 vs 72.99) and ~7% on COCO-LT (54.10 vs 47.05), confirming that class-wise computation and NGC substantially fix LSEP's over-reliance on co-occurrence. Against the previous SOTA DB Focal [35], DR loss is marginally lower on VOC-LT (78.01 vs 78.29) but higher on COCO-LT (54.10 vs 53.45); the combined DR+DB Focal variant surpasses DB Focal on both datasets (78.75 and 54.55). DR loss also beats Circle Loss [30] (75.20/52.00), CB Focal [7] (75.24/49.06), ASL [27] (76.40/50.21), ZLPR [29] (75.10/49.90), BalPoE [1] (73.76/50.02), Focal Loss [19] (73.88/49.46), RS [28] (75.38/46.97), ML-GCN [6] (68.92/44.24), and LDAM [4] (70.73/40.53) on total mAP across both datasets. The paper highlights that DR loss and DB Focal are complementary: DR excels on medium and tail classes while DB Focal is stronger on head classes, and their integration yields the best overall scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the LSEP ranking loss (Li et al., CVPR 2017) by reformulating it at a class-wise level rather than sample-wise, motivated by the observation that sample-wise LSEP over-relies on co-occurrence statistics that shift between training and test distributions in long-tailed settings. It draws on gradient-scaling ideas from DB Loss (Wu et al., ECCV 2020) and ASL (Ridnik et al., ICCV 2021) to introduce a scaling hyperparameter, and adds a novel negative gradient constraint to prevent overconfidence in negative labels. The paper also positions itself against the broader family of BCE-based long-tailed losses (Focal, CB Focal, DB Focal) and identifies a previously unexplored complementarity between LSEP-based and BCE-based objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a well-established MLC loss (LSEP) underperforms simple BCE in long-tailed multi-label settings challenges the assumption that ranking-based losses are universally superior for multi-label tasks, and the proposed fix (class-wise computation + NGC) is simple enough to be adopted broadly. However, the practical gains over the strongest baseline (DB Focal) are modest—roughly 0.5–0.7% mAP—and are demonstrated on only two small-scale datasets with a single ResNet-50 backbone, limiting confidence in generalizability. Open questions include whether the class-wise formulation scales to very large label spaces, whether the complementarity between LSEP-based and BCE-based losses can be exploited more systematically (e.g., adaptive weighting), and whether the approach transfers to other long-tailed recognition settings beyond image classification.
