## Problem

Self-supervised neural cloth simulators randomly instantiate garment states during training, which is fundamentally incompatible with existing collision detection and response techniques (IPC, SDF-based, penalty-based) that all require collision-free initial conditions. As a result, cloth self-collision — a pervasive artifact in realistic garment animation — remains unaddressed in the neural simulation literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives the current cloth mesh (vertex positions, faces), the body mesh, and an optional external force vector per vertex as nodal/edge features, and autoregressively predicts the next-frame cloth vertex positions via explicit Euler integration. Self-collision edges are dynamically constructed each frame by connecting all cloth vertex pairs within a 2 cm spatial radius (excluding original mesh edges), so the graph topology changes with the garment's deformation rather than being fixed to mesh connectivity.

## Method

SENC introduces two coupled components: (1) a self-collision loss computed via Global Intersection Analysis, which closes garment holes, remeshes so intersections lie on edges, traces intersection paths, identifies penetration faces via parallel flood fill (choosing the smaller enclosed region), and sums signed tetrahedral volumes to obtain a differentiable penetration-volume penalty; and (2) a self-collision-aware GNN that augments the topological mesh graph with spatial-proximity edges, allowing message passing between topologically distant vertices that are physically close. Training is self-supervised using a composite physical energy (stretching, bending, inertia, friction, body-collision SDF, external force) plus the GIA-based self-collision term, executed in two phases — 120 k iterations without the self-collision loss to stabilize outputs, then 70 k iterations with all losses — on 52 AMASS motion sequences.

## Results

On 4 unseen AMASS test sequences (2175 frames), SENC achieves a mean self-collision loss of 1.80×10⁻³ on t-shirts and 1.59×10⁻³ on skirts, dramatically outperforming the best baseline HOOD (26.10×10⁻³ and 7.33×10⁻³ respectively). SENC reduces the percentage of frames with self-collision loss above 0.1 to 0% for t-shirts and 0.28% for skirts, compared to 8.92% and 1.52% for HOOD. The improvement over NCS is particularly stark on skirts, where SENC's loss (1.59×10⁻³) is roughly 300× lower than NCS's (476.03×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SENC outperforms all three baselines by large margins on both garment types. Against HOOD (the strongest baseline and the architecture SENC builds upon), SENC reduces the t-shirt self-collision loss by 14.5× (1.80 vs 26.10) and the skirt loss by 4.6× (1.59 vs 7.33), while also eliminating nearly all frames exceeding the 0.1 threshold. Against SNUG, SENC achieves a 28.9× reduction in t-shirt loss (1.80 vs 52.00); SNUG has no skirt checkpoint available. Against NCS, the gap is most extreme on skirts where NCS's loss reaches 476.03×10⁻³ with 99.91% of frames above the 0.1 threshold, while SENC keeps the loss at 1.59×10⁻³ with only 0.28% of frames above that threshold. The ablation further confirms that the self-collision-aware edges are critical for garments with large topological distances between colliding regions (e.g., skirt hems), where removing them causes the skirt loss to nearly double.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENC builds directly on the GNN-based neural cloth simulator architecture of HOOD [17] and MeshGraphNets [39], extending their message-passing graph with spatial-distance-based self-collision edges to capture interactions between topologically distant cloth regions. Its core novelty is repurposing Global Intersection Analysis (GIA) from Baraff et al.'s 2003 untangling algorithm as a differentiable penetration-volume loss, which sidesteps the fundamental incompatibility between existing collision-response methods (IPC, SDF-based, penalty-based) and self-supervised training that requires random initial states. This positions SENC as the first self-supervised neural cloth simulator to explicitly handle self-collision, filling a gap that prior methods (SNUG, NCS, HOOD) all leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Self-collision is a persistent visual artifact in neural cloth animation that has blocked realistic simulation of garments with close-contact regions (underarms, layered skirts). The GIA-based volume loss is modular and can be integrated into existing neural cloth frameworks, making it practically adoptable. However, significant limitations remain: the remeshing and GIA pipeline is computationally expensive (48-hour training), the method only handles meshes that can be trivially closed, and complex topologies like long skirts create a dilemma where closing the boundary inhibits valid deformation while leaving it open prevents learning from hem-to-hem collisions. Open questions include accelerating GIA, developing boundary-free penetration losses (e.g., via winding numbers or electrostatic flux), and extending to multi-layer garments and 3D deformable characters.
