## Problem

Adapting videos to different production styles (documentaries, dramas, vlogs, etc.) requires selecting appropriate visual transitions, but no existing method or dataset supports style-aware transition recommendation. Video style is abstract and lacks a concise formulation, and no public dataset provides transition annotations tailored to multiple production styles for the same video, making it impossible to learn style-specific transition patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242138/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an ordered sequence of up to 8 video clips (16 frames sampled at 224×224 from each clip) and a desired production style label (one of 5 categories: vlog, anime, influencer, photo slideshow, nature/urban) as input. It outputs a discrete sequence of n−1 visual transition class labels drawn from 30 pre-defined transition types (e.g., dissolve, wipe, fade), where each label specifies the transition to apply between consecutive clips in the adapted video.

## Method

V-Trans4Style follows a bottom-up, three-component architecture. First, a multi-task learning network (MLN) jointly classifies transition type and video style from transition segments to produce pre-trained transition and style embeddings. Second, a transformer encoder-decoder (2 layers, d_model=512, 8 heads, SlowFast backbone) maps the ordered clip sequence to a latent embedding z and autoregressively decodes n−1 transitions using masked causal self-attention, trained with cross-entropy plus a masked triplet loss against the pre-trained transition embeddings. Third, a Style Conditioning Module (SCM) operates at inference time: at each decoding step, it performs 5000 iterations of gradient-based activation maximization on z, minimizing a combined embedding loss (cosine distance between the running mean of predicted transition embeddings and the target style embedding) and a reconstruction loss (L1 distance to the original encoder output via a trained inverse decoder), thereby steering the decoder toward style-appropriate transitions without retraining the encoder-decoder per style. An optional RRT-inspired finetuning step explores K nearest transition embeddings per step to further align the sequence with the target style.

## Results

On AutoTransition++, V-Trans4Style's encoder-decoder model outperforms the AutoTransition baseline across all metrics regardless of embedding type. With MLN pre-trained transition embeddings, V-Trans4Style achieves Recall@1 of 22.59%, Recall@5 of 61.03%, and Mean Rank of 5.90, compared to AutoTransition's 12.12%, 36.66%, and 10.48 respectively — representing improvements of ~86% in Recall@1 and ~66% in Recall@5. The Style Conditioning Module (SCM) with RRT finetuning further improves style alignment, achieving cosine similarity scores of 0.188 (Vlog), 0.227 (Influencer), 0.729 (Photos), and 0.356 (Nature), substantially exceeding both the unconditioned encoder-decoder and the AutoTransition baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is AutoTransition [23] (Shen et al., ECCV 2022), the prior state-of-the-art transition recommendation system. V-Trans4Style's encoder-decoder consistently outperforms AutoTransition on all three metrics (Recall@1, Recall@5, Mean Rank) across all three embedding settings, with the largest gains observed when using MLN pre-trained embeddings (Recall@1: 22.59 vs. 12.12; Recall@5: 61.03 vs. 36.66; Mean Rank: 5.90 vs. 10.48). The advantage is attributed to the transformer decoder's ability to capture temporal dependencies between transitions via masked self-attention, which AutoTransition's architecture does not model. For the Style Conditioning Module evaluation (Table 3), V-Trans4Style with SCM+RRT achieves the highest cosine similarity to the target style embedding in 4 out of 5 styles (Vlog, Influencer, Photos, Nature), with the Photos style showing the most dramatic improvement (0.729 vs. −0.05 for AutoTransition). The one exception is the Anime style, where AutoTransition slightly edges out V-Trans4Style (0.031 vs. −0.001), though both values are near zero, indicating limited style signal for that category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual transition recommendation framework of AutoTransition (Shen et al., ECCV 2022), which learns to predict transition classes from video content, by introducing an explicit video production style dimension. The key architectural departure is the Style Conditioning Module, which repurposes activation maximization (Erhan et al., 2009) — originally a tool for visualizing deep network features — as an inference-time gradient-based mechanism to steer a frozen encoder-decoder's latent embedding toward style-appropriate transitions. The transformer encoder-decoder with masked triplet loss follows standard generative sequence modeling, but its application to causal, step-by-step transition generation conditioned on pre-trained transition embeddings is a specific design choice not present in the AutoTransition baseline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally connect video production styles to transition recommendation, and the AutoTransition++ dataset (6k videos, 5 style categories, 1379 style-annotated samples) provides a starting point for future research in this underexplored intersection. However, the scope is narrow: only 5 styles are defined, only visual transitions are considered (no audio, camera motion, or cuts), and the style-annotated subset is small. The SCM's mixed results — notably the anime style showing no improvement or even degradation — suggest that style conditioning via latent-space optimization is not yet a robust solution. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more styles, longer videos, or multimodal editing elements, and whether the bottom-up (content-first, then style) strategy is fundamentally superior to joint content-style modeling.
