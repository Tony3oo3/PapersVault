## Problem

Fine-grained classification of esophageal cancer EUS images is challenging because lesions disrupt the hierarchical wall structure and can appear at arbitrary radial positions due to the annular imaging geometry, making rotation-sensitive models unreliable. Prior approaches either required time-consuming manual pre-segmentation of lesion regions or excluded infiltrative tumors entirely, and rotation invariance had not been systematically addressed in transformer-based fine-grained classification for this modality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240827/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes complete 224×224 grayscale EUS images without any pre-segmentation of the lesion region, and outputs a 5-class label distinguishing tumor origin (muscularis propria, muscularis mucosae, submucosa, adventitia) and infiltration pattern. The same architecture is also evaluated on a 4-class Brain Tumor MRI dataset (7023 grayscale images) to demonstrate cross-modality generalizability.

## Method

SRRM-ViT extends ViT-B/16 with three coupled mechanisms. First, a soft histogram texture feature module uses Radial Basis Functions with 3×3 convolutions (rather than 1×1, to handle low-contrast ultrasound) to produce rotation-invariant bin-aggregated feature maps. Second, a rotation-invariant reinforcement mechanism concatenates the soft histogram key/value matrices with the original self-attention key/value matrices, so the query attends jointly over both streams and learns to weight rotation-invariant texture evidence at any radial position. Third, a token selection module recursively multiplies attention weights across all layers (with a transpose on odd layers to handle the doubled sequence length) to identify the top-K most discriminative patches, which are concatenated with the [CLS] token as input to the final transformer layer. Training combines cross-entropy with a margin-based contrastive loss (α=0.7) that pushes apart hard-negative class pairs while pulling same-class tokens together, initialized from ImageNet-21k pretrained weights.

## Results

On the in-house 5-class esophageal cancer EUS dataset (1,324 training / 334 test images), SRRM-ViT achieves 78.7% accuracy, 92.1% precision, 41.1% recall, and 36.8% F1-score, outperforming the strongest SOTA FGVC baseline SIM-Trans (75.9% accuracy) by 2.8 percentage points and the ViT-B/16 baseline (61.6%) by 17.1 points. On the Brain Tumor MRI dataset (7,023 images, 4 classes), SRRM-ViT reaches 99.2% accuracy and 87.8% recall, surpassing the ViT baseline (96.9% accuracy) by 2.3 points and the next-best method CSA-MLP (98.6%) by 0.6 points. The model requires 32.5 G FLOPs per inference, roughly 2× the FLOPs of the ViT-B/16 baseline (16.9 G) due to the dual key-value attention mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRRM-ViT outperforms all 11 baselines on the in-house EUS dataset. Against CNN baselines, it leads ResNet-50 (69.5%) by 9.2 points and VGG-16 (65.6%) by 13.1 points in accuracy, while also substantially improving recall (41.1% vs. 30.6% and 29.3% respectively). Among ViT-based FGVC methods, it surpasses SIM-Trans (75.9%) by 2.8 points, AA-Trans (74.3%) by 4.4 points, FFVT (73.8%) by 4.9 points, and IELT (66.7%) by 12.0 points. The Cross-X method (ResNet-50 backbone, 74.2%) is outperformed by 4.5 points in accuracy. On the Brain Tumor MRI dataset, SRRM-ViT (99.2%) beats CSA-MLP (98.6%) by 0.6 points, ConvAttenMixer (97.9%) by 1.3 points, Ensemble Learning (98.0%) by 1.2 points, and the ViT baseline (96.9%) by 2.3 points, confirming cross-modality generalizability. The recall gap is particularly notable: SRRM-ViT achieves 41.1% recall on the EUS task versus 37.0% for SIM-Trans and 36.1% for Cross-X, indicating the rotation-invariant texture mechanism helps the model correctly identify harder-to-detect infiltrative tumor sub-classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture by integrating soft histogram texture features—originally proposed for texture analysis by Peeples et al.—directly into the self-attention mechanism, fusing them into the key and value matrices to achieve rotation invariance. The token selection module adapts the TransFG approach (He et al., 2022) with a modified recursive attention aggregation that accounts for the expanded attention matrix introduced by the rotation-invariant reinforcement. The work also departs from prior EUS classification studies (Zheng et al., Wu et al.) that required manual lesion pre-segmentation and excluded infiltrative tumors, instead operating on complete EUS images with an attention-based region selection strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically relevant gap: EUS-based esophageal cancer diagnosis where lesions appear at arbitrary radial positions due to the annular imaging geometry, making rotation sensitivity a practical obstacle for transformer-based classifiers. The soft histogram integration into attention is a lightweight, interpretable mechanism that could generalize to other rotation-sensitive medical imaging tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to a small in-house dataset (1,324 training images, 5 categories) and a single public MRI dataset of a different modality; the acknowledged confusion between Infiltration Propria and Infiltration Submucosa classes, and the modest 1.1% gain from the rotation-invariant component specifically, suggest the approach's ceiling on this task. Open questions include whether the handcrafted-feature-in-attention paradigm scales to larger, more diverse EUS datasets and whether the rotation invariance benefit persists with larger transformer backbones.
