## Problem

No federated learning framework existed for video anomaly detection, leaving a gap between the privacy needs of surveillance deployments and the data-heterogeneity challenges inherent in multi-client training. Simple model averaging (FedAvg) fails to capture scene-specific foreground-background contexts critical for anomaly interpretation, and the extreme rarity of anomalous events further limits unsupervised and weakly-supervised detection efficacy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241413/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds private surveillance video clips and uploads only model weights W_i and a background object distribution vector O_i (extracted via a lightweight YOLOv4 detector) to the server, never sharing raw frames. On the server side, a public video dataset is augmented with per-frame captions generated by BLIP-2 and semantically calibrated by GPT-4 into anomaly categories, forming video-text pairs that feed a multimodal teacher network. The final output is a frame-level anomaly score per client (evaluated as AUC on ShanghaiTech, UBnormal, UCF-Crime and AP on XD-Violence), with the system also assigning anomaly category labels during the GPT-4 calibration step.

## Method

FedVAD introduces a two-stage server-side pipeline. First, Federated Visual Consistency Clustering (FVCC) assigns clients to M groups using a weighted distance over both model-weight proximity and background object-distribution proximity (Eq. 2), then updates cluster centers via weighted averaging (Eq. 3), preserving privacy by never exchanging raw data. Second, Adaptive Semantic-Enhanced Distillation (ASED) selects public videos whose object distributions match each cluster center, generates frame-level captions with BLIP-2, and calibrates them with GPT-4 (iteratively re-describing misclassified segments with category-specific prompts). A multimodal teacher network fuses pre-trained visual features Φ(V) and text features Ψ(T) through multi-head self-attention with layer normalization (Eq. 4), and the cluster's student network S_m is updated via L2 feature-level distillation loss (Eq. 5). The refined student weights are redistributed to clients, which fine-tune locally on their private data, closing the federated loop.

## Results

FedVAD achieves AUC of 84.07 on ShanghaiTech and 69.83 on UBnormal under the unsupervised setting, and AUC/AP of 97.63, 85.13, and 66.78 on ShanghaiTech, UCF-Crime, and XD-Violence under the weakly supervised setting, respectively. These results surpass the strongest FL baseline (FedFTG) by 1.71–2.27 points across all five dataset-setting combinations and approach the centralized upper bound (e.g., 84.07 vs. 85.90 on ShanghaiTech unsupervised). With Differential Privacy applied, performance drops by only 0.56–0.57 points, confirming robustness to privacy constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedVAD outperforms all ten FL baselines across every dataset-setting combination. Against the strongest FL competitor FedFTG, gains range from +1.71 (ShanghaiTech unsupervised: 84.07 vs. 82.36) to +2.27 (ShanghaiTech weakly supervised: 97.63 vs. 95.96). Personalized FL methods (FedBN, FedRep, FedALA, FedPAC) cluster tightly around 81.9–82.1 on ShanghaiTech unsupervised, while FedVAD reaches 84.07, a gap of roughly 2 points. Pre-trained FL variants (FedAvg SGD, FedAdam SGD, FPS) improve over vanilla FedAvg by ~0.3–0.5 points but remain 1.5–2.5 points below FedVAD. In the exploratory comparison (Table 3b), GPT-4V with text prompts scores only 49.25–66.28 accuracy across the four datasets, far below FedVAD's 85.27–94.38, confirming that direct LLM inference cannot substitute for the learned federated detection pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FedAvg framework (McMahan et al., 2017) and clustered federated learning (Ghosh et al., 2020; Sattler et al., 2020), but departs from standard model-averaging aggregation by introducing a two-stage server-side process: visual consistency clustering based on both model weights and object-distribution vectors, followed by LLM-augmented semantic distillation. The semantic generation pipeline repurposes BLIP-2 for frame-level captioning and GPT-4 for anomaly-category calibration, drawing on the broader trend of leveraging large language models to enrich visual representations (Sato et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2023). Unlike prior FL-for-vision work that addresses heterogeneity through personalization (FedBN, FedRep, FedALA) or pre-training (FedFTG, FPS), FedVAD targets the specific foreground-background contextual challenge of video anomaly detection, where the same visual pattern (e.g., two people running) can be normal or anomalous depending on scene context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first federated learning framework purpose-built for video anomaly detection, FedVAD addresses a practical gap: surveillance deployments cannot centralize raw video due to privacy regulations, yet standard FL aggregation degrades under the severe scene heterogeneity inherent to multi-camera urban monitoring. The LLM-driven semantic distillation offers a reusable recipe for injecting public-domain knowledge into privacy-constrained FL pipelines, potentially transferable to other video understanding tasks. However, the authors acknowledge that all experiments use simulated client partitions rather than real-world deployments, leaving open critical questions about bandwidth constraints, Byzantine/poisonous clients, and whether the GPT-4 captioning pipeline scales cost-effectively to thousands of surveillance cameras. The comparison showing GPT-4V performs poorly on frame-level VAD (49–66% accuracy) also underscores that general-purpose multimodal LLMs are not yet substitutes for task-specific detectors.
