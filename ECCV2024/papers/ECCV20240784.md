## Problem

Conventional text-driven 3D scene editing requires a three-stage pipeline: COLMAP-based pose estimation, initialization of a 3D model from unedited images, and iterative dataset updates with edited frames. This process is computationally expensive (over 280 minutes per edit for IN2N), depends on SfM preprocessing that fails on casually captured video, and suffers from multi-view editing inconsistencies inherent to T2I diffusion models. No prior method can directly synthesize a text-conditioned 3D scene from arbitrary monocular video without COLMAP poses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video (casually captured, e.g., smartphone 360° footage), a free-form text editing prompt, and camera intrinsics as input, and produces a photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting scene where the specified regions have been edited per the prompt. Unlike prior pipelines, no pre-computed camera poses or unedited 3D model initialization are required; the video's temporal continuity serves as the pose prior. The editing is applied to all frames before any 3D reconstruction begins, and the resulting 3D scene supports real-time rendering and novel view synthesis.

## Method

3DEgo introduces an autoregressive noise blender that edits video frames sequentially, conditioning each frame's diffusion denoising on the w previously edited adjacent frames via a weighted average of image-conditional noise estimates with exponential decay weights, ensuring multi-view consistency without fine-tuning the IP2P diffusion model. For 3D reconstruction, it employs a COLMAP-free incremental 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline: a monocular depth estimator initializes Gaussians from a single frame, then relative SE-3 poses are estimated between consecutive frames by optimizing photometric loss while fixing Gaussian attributes, and the scene is expanded iteratively with all-inclusive optimization. A learnable KEA identity vector is added to each Gaussian to enable precise local editing, supervised by a combined 2D binary cross-entropy and 3D Jensen-Shannon Divergence loss. Pyramidal Gaussian Scoring anchors the attributes of KEA-marked Gaussians at initialization and penalizes deviation during densification, while a Chamfer-distance point cloud loss regularizes the estimated relative poses during global scene expansion.

## Results

Across 200 edits on six datasets, 3DEgo achieves the best or near-best CTIS, CDCR, and E-PSNR in most settings. On the CO3D-V2 dataset, it reaches CTIS 0.179, CDCR 0.936, and E-PSNR 26.02, outperforming IN2N (0.163 / 0.924 / 25.18) and DreamEditor (0.174 / 0.915 / 24.88). The method also delivers an 11.4× speedup over IN2N in total pipeline time (25 min vs. 285 min) by eliminating both COLMAP pose estimation and unedited-model initialization. Against pose-free baselines (BARF, Nope-NeRF), 3DEgo leads by a wide margin on all three metrics across every dataset, e.g., E-PSNR 26.02 vs. 21.15 (BARF) and 17.97 (Nope-NeRF) on CO3D-V2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IN2N (the primary pose-known baseline), 3DEgo wins on CTIS in 4 of 6 datasets and on CDCR in all 6, while IN2N edges ahead on E-PSNR for the IN2N dataset (26.51 vs. 26.39) and on CTIS for NeRFstudio (0.171 vs. 0.163) and Tanks & Temples (0.170 vs. 0.164). DreamEditor is consistently outperformed by 3DEgo on all three metrics across every dataset. Against pose-free reconstruction baselines, 3DEgo dominates: on CO3D-V2 it achieves E-PSNR 26.02 versus 21.15 for BARF and 17.97 for Nope-NeRF, with similar margins on all other datasets. For the object-removal task, 3DEgo qualitatively surpasses Gaussian Grouping [50] in spatial accuracy and multi-view consistency, producing fewer artifacts and a sharper background after removing large objects such as statues. The key practical advantage is speed: 3DEgo completes the full pipeline in ~25 min versus IN2N's ~285 min (13 min COLMAP + 22 min initialization + 250 min iterative editing), an 11.4× reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the explicit 3D representation, InstructPix2Pix (Brooks et al., 2023) as the text-guided 2D editing backbone, and COLMAP-free 3DGS training (Fu et al., 2023) for pose estimation without SfM. It departs from the dominant IN2N-style pipeline—which requires COLMAP poses, initializes an unedited 3D model, and iteratively injects edited images—by performing all 2D editing upfront and reconstructing the 3D scene in a single pass. The autoregressive noise blender, which conditions each frame's denoising on weighted noise estimates from previously edited neighbors, is a novel mechanism absent from prior multi-view consistency work that typically relies on additional training or fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating COLMAP and the unedited-model initialization stage, 3DEgo makes text-conditioned 3D scene editing accessible from arbitrary casual smartphone video, a setting where SfM often fails or is prohibitively expensive. The ~10× speedup (25 min vs. 250 min for scene editing) and the ability to handle 360-degree and object-centric footage broaden the practical scope of 3D content creation. However, the method inherits IP2P's limitations in precise multi-attribute editing (e.g., inadvertently altering adjacent object colors), and the reliance on a pre-trained monocular depth estimator for per-frame initialization may struggle with textureless or highly dynamic scenes. Open questions include scalability to very long video sequences, robustness to significant camera motion between frames, and whether the noise blender generalizes to editing models beyond IP2P.
