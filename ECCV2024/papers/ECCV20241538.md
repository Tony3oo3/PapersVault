## Problem

Existing large-scale floor plan datasets (RPLAN, LIFULL) predominantly contain simple, single-apartment, axis-aligned, Manhattan-shaped layouts, creating a mismatch with real-world multi-apartment building complexes that feature irregular room shapes, inter-apartment connectivity, and fixed structural constraints. This paper introduces MSD, the first large-scale dataset of complex European building complexes, and benchmarks whether current state-of-the-art floor plan generation methods can handle this increased realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241538/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes two inputs: a binary image encoding the fixed building structure (load-bearing walls and columns, identified by thickness above the 60th percentile) and a zoning graph whose nodes carry zone-type labels (private, public, service, outside) and whose edges encode connectivity type (passage, door, front door). The output is a complete floor plan, represented either as a single-channel image with integer room-class pixel labels (plus two coordinate channels) or as a room graph with polygon geometry, room-type, and zone-type attributes per node. The formulation bridges schematic design (zoning) to detailed design (layout), constraining generation on both the building boundary and the functional topology.

## Method

Two baseline methods benchmark the MSD dataset. Modified HouseDiffusion (MHD) extends HouseDiffusion by adding a wall-cross attention (WCA) module that conditions the diffusion denoising process on wall embeddings extracted from the binary structure image, and a separately trained GAT that predicts room types from the zoning graph as a node-classification pre-processing step; it also introduces a minimum rotated rectangle (MRR) approximation to handle the high corner counts in MSD. The second baseline, Graph-informed U-Net (UN), performs direct pixel-level segmentation with a U-Net whose deepest latent feature map is concatenated with a fixed-size graph embedding produced by a GCN over the zoning graph, and uses Segment Anything to pre-process the structure into a 3-channel interior/exterior/structure image. Both methods are trained under supervised learning, and the paper's key empirical finding is that all baselines show a significant performance drop on MSD compared to simpler datasets, with MIoU scores ranging only from 10.9 to 42.4, indicating that current approaches cannot yet generate realistic multi-apartment floor plans.

## Results

On the MSD benchmark, all baseline methods show poor visual fidelity, with average MIoU ranging from 10.9 (MHD with full polygons) to 42.4 (UN with Segment Anything pre-processing). The best segmentation-based model, UN(pre), achieves 42.4% average MIoU, while the best diffusion-based model, MHD(MRR)+WCA, reaches only 21.8% MIoU. In contrast, graph compatibility scores are much higher (71.1–87.1%), indicating that MHD variants largely preserve the topological structure of the zoning graph even when pixel-level accuracy is low. The authors conclude that current state-of-the-art floor plan generation methods cannot yet handle the complexity of multi-apartment building complexes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks two families of methods on MSD: a diffusion-based approach (MHD, modified from HouseDiffusion [21]) and a segmentation-based approach (UN, U-Net + GCN). UN(pre) achieves the highest MIoU (42.4%) across all size bins, outperforming MHD(MRR)+WCA (21.8%) by roughly 20 points, indicating that pixel-level segmentation better captures room placement relative to the building structure. However, MHD(MRR) achieves the best graph compatibility (87.1% avg), surpassing MHD(MRR)+WCA (76.2%) and MHD(POL) (80.3%), showing that the diffusion model preserves topological connectivity more faithfully. Adding the wall cross-attention module (WCA) improves MIoU for MHD (e.g., 11.5→21.8 for MRR) but degrades compatibility (87.1→76.2), suggesting a trade-off between spatial accuracy and topological fidelity. The authors also tested HouseGAN++ [17] and FLNet [28] for generalizability (results in supplementary), but the main takeaway is that no existing method—whether diffusion, GAN, or segmentation—achieves acceptable performance on the more complex MSD benchmark, unlike their success on simpler single-apartment datasets like RPLAN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the boundary- and graph-constrained floor plan generation formulation of Graph2Plan [5] by adding structural constraints (load-bearing walls, columns) as an explicit input modality, and by scaling the task from single-apartment to multi-apartment building complexes. The diffusion-based baseline (MHD) directly modifies HouseDiffusion [21] with a wall-cross attention module and a GAT for room-type prediction, while the segmentation baseline combines U-Net [19] with a GCN [8] for graph-level conditioning. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior datasets (RPLAN, LIFULL) that are limited to axis-aligned, single-unit, Manhattan-shaped layouts, positioning MSD as the first large-scale European dataset that preserves irregular room shapes, compass orientation, and inter-apartment connectivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MSD establishes a realistic benchmark that exposes a significant gap between current floor plan generation methods and the demands of actual architectural practice: multi-apartment connectivity, structural constraints, and irregular geometries all degrade performance substantially (MIoU drops to 10–42). The dissociation between high graph compatibility (74–87%) and very low MIoU in MHD suggests that existing diffusion models capture topological relationships but fail at precise geometric placement in complex layouts, pointing to a need for new architectural inductive biases. Open questions include whether the performance gap is fundamentally due to the increased graph size and irregularity, or whether the diffusion training objective is inherently misaligned with pixel-level evaluation, and whether structural conditioning can be integrated without sacrificing topological fidelity.
