## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation suffers from inherent scale ambiguity, and while supervised methods have begun incorporating ground plane priors to recover metric scale, these approaches cannot be directly transferred to self-supervised settings where no depth labels exist to anchor the scale. Additionally, existing models overfit to specific camera configurations, limiting generalization across poses and unseen driving datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242318/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image along with known camera intrinsics (focal lengths, principal point), extrinsics (rotation and translation), and camera height. From these parameters, a theoretical ground depth image is analytically computed per pixel and normalized before being concatenated as an additional channel to the input. The network outputs a strictly positive metric depth map (via softplus activation) and a per-pixel ground attention map in [0,1] that indicates where the ground prior replaces the predicted depth.

## Method

Building on the Monodepth2 self-supervised pipeline with a ResNet-50 encoder, GroCo introduces a ground attention mechanism that blends the predicted depth D̂ with the computed ground prior G via a learned attention map α: D = (1−α)·D̂ + α·G. Two novel losses drive scale recovery without labels: a regularization term L_reg that penalizes the attention when its mean activation falls below a dataset-specific threshold τ (computed from pathway width and camera height), and a constraint loss L_const = (1/N)Σ αᵢ²|D̂ᵢ − Gᵢ| that simultaneously pushes the attention to activate on ground pixels and forces the depth to converge to the ground prior in those regions. Rotation augmentation (±5° pitch/roll, ±15° yaw) is applied coherently to both images and the ground depth by rotating the camera extrinsic, improving robustness to pose changes. The total loss combines the standard photometric reprojection and smoothness terms with the two new ground-specific penalties, and the model is trained for 20 epochs on KITTI with Adam at lr=1e-4.

## Results

On KITTI (improved labels), GroCo achieves AbsRel 0.089, RMSE 3.815, and δ<1.25 of 0.910, outperforming VADepth (AbsRel 0.091, RMSE 3.871) and Scale Recovery (AbsRel 0.123, RMSE 5.253 on original labels). For camera transfer generalization, GroCo attains a mean AbsRel of 0.56 across back/left/right cameras, beating the best supervised baseline (BTS+GeDepthAdaptive at 0.62). In zero-shot transfer to DDAD, GroCo achieves RMSE 15.366 with only 35M parameters, surpassing GeDepth-Adaptive (RMSE 16.132, 277M params) despite using ~8× fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Scale Recovery [19] (Wagstaff & Kelly, IROS 2021), GroCo wins on all error metrics on KITTI original labels (AbsRel 0.113 vs 0.123, RMSE 4.756 vs 5.253) and all accuracy metrics (δ<1.25: 0.870 vs 0.840). Against VADepth [20] (Xiang et al.), GroCo achieves lower AbsRel (0.089 vs 0.091) and RMSE (3.815 vs 3.871) on improved KITTI labels, though VADepth edges ahead on δ<1.25 (0.913 vs 0.910). For camera transfer (Table 3), GroCo (mean AbsRel 0.56) outperforms both BTS+GeDepthAdaptive (0.62) and DepthFormer+GeDepthAdaptive (0.66), and notably beats the supervised BTS+GeDepthAdaptive on the back camera (0.43 vs 0.62). In zero-shot DDAD evaluation (Table 4), GroCo achieves a lower RMSE (15.366) than GeDepth-Adaptive (16.132) with 8× fewer parameters, though GeDepth-Adaptive retains a lower AbsRel (0.261 vs 0.424), likely due to ego-vehicle visibility in side cameras. Under rotation stress tests (Table 2), GroCo dramatically outperforms the Monodepth2+scale-recovery baseline, e.g., at 15° yaw (AbsRel 0.136 vs 0.209) and −5° pitch (0.086 vs 0.145), confirming the ground constraint's robustness to camera pose changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GroCo builds directly on the Monodepth2 self-supervised framework [5] and adapts the ground attention mechanism from GeDepth [22], which was previously restricted to supervised settings requiring stereo or LiDAR annotations. The key departure is the introduction of two novel loss terms (a constraint loss and an attention regularisation) that enable the ground prior to resolve scale ambiguity without any depth labels, bridging a gap that prior scale-recovery methods (Wagstaff [19], VADepth [20]) addressed only at inference time. The rotation augmentation strategy and the use of softplus activation to enforce positive depth coherent with the ground prior are additional methodological contributions layered on the GeDepth architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that ground-plane priors can be integrated into purely self-supervised training to recover metric scale, removing the dependency on stereo/LiDAR annotations that limited prior ground-prior methods. The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving: the model generalizes to unseen camera poses and datasets (DDAD) without retraining, and achieves comparable RMSE to supervised methods with roughly 8× fewer parameters. However, the approach is constrained to ground-based vehicles with visible flat ground, and the threshold parameter τ must be set manually per dataset, limiting fully automated deployment. Open questions include whether scale can be propagated temporally when ground is occluded, and whether τ can be learned end-to-end.
