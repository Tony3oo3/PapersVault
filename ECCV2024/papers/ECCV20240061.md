## Problem

Prior work on improving diffusion model training (P2-Weight, Min-SNR, ANT, E-TSDM) identified that different timesteps carry distinct semantic information or exhibit gradient inconsistencies, and addressed this through loss re-weighting. However, all these methods retained DDPM's uniform timestep sampling strategy, overlooking that the sampling distribution itself is a critical factor. This paper demonstrates through Fokker-Planck analysis that distribution variations during forward diffusion are fundamentally non-uniform, with the most drastic shifts concentrated in the initial stages, making uniform sampling inherently sub-optimal.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a data sample x0 drawn from the data distribution and a noise vector epsilon ~ N(0, I), producing a denoising prediction at a beta-sampled timestep t. At inference, it takes pure Gaussian noise and generates unconditional RGB images at resolutions ranging from 32x32 (CIFAR-10, ImageNet) to 256x256 (AFHQ-D, CelebAHQ). The method is architecture-agnostic and compatible with standard fast samplers (DDIM, DPM-Solver, Respacing) without requiring any modifications to the inference pipeline.

## Method

The paper proves via the Fokker-Planck equation that the norm of the time derivative of the distribution q_t(x_t) is bounded by the norm of q_t(x_t)/(2t), which diverges as t approaches 0, establishing that distribution changes are most extreme at early timesteps. To align training with this non-uniformity, B-TTDM replaces uniform timestep sampling with a Beta(alpha, beta) distribution where alpha < 1 and beta = 1, yielding a reverse J-shaped density that concentrates sampling near t = 0. A singularity at t = 0 is handled by clamping the density to f(1/T; alpha, beta), introducing a negligible tuned error of (1/T)^alpha. The optimal parameters (alpha = 0.8, beta = 1) are determined via ablation on AFHQ-D. This sampling strategy is orthogonal to loss re-weighting methods and can be combined with P2-Weight, Min-SNR, and DDPM-IP to achieve further FID improvements, e.g., 2.46 on CIFAR-10 with DDPM-IP.

## Results

B-TTDM achieves the best FID across all four resolution datasets in the main comparison: 2.89 on CIFAR-10 (32×32), 10.28 on FFHQ (128×128), 13.81 on AFHQ-D (256×256), and 13.62 on CelebAHQ (256×256). On the larger ImageNet (32×32) dataset, B-TTDM reaches an FID of 2.87 with roughly 20% of the training iterations required by DDPM to reach comparable quality, and achieves a 4.71× training speedup to match DDPM's FID. The method also improves sFID consistently, e.g., 4.22 vs. 4.69 on CIFAR-10 and 45.25 vs. 49.03 on AFHQ-D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

B-TTDM outperforms all four re-weighted-loss baselines (Debias, Min-SNR, P2-Weight) and the DDPM baseline on every dataset and metric in Table 1. The most dramatic gains are on higher-resolution data: on FFHQ, B-TTDM improves FID from 14.07 (DDPM) to 10.28, a 3.79-point reduction, while on AFHQ-D it drops FID from 17.21 to 13.81. Notably, the re-weighted-loss methods (Debias, Min-SNR, P2-Weight) actually degrade performance on low-resolution CIFAR-10 relative to DDPM (FID 3.17–3.23 vs. 3.01), whereas B-TTDM still improves it to 2.89. When combined with DDPM-IP (Table 2), B-TTDM further reduces CIFAR FID from 2.72 to 2.46 and FFHQ FID from 7.07 to 6.69, confirming its plug-and-play compatibility. In the ablation against alternative non-uniform sampling (Table 6), B-TTDM (α=0.8, β=1) beats the exponential-distribution variant (DDPM-E), the β>1 beta variant (B-TTDM^α), and ANT-UW on both CIFAR and AFHQ across all four metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of work that improves diffusion model training by re-weighting the loss function across timesteps (P2-Weight, Min-SNR, ANT, E-TSDM, Debias), which all retain DDPM's uniform timestep sampling. Instead, B-TTDM targets the sampling distribution itself, grounding the choice in a Fokker-Planck/Ornstein-Uhlenbeck analysis showing that distributional change is most extreme near t=0. The method is explicitly positioned as orthogonal and complementary to those loss-reweighting approaches, functioning as a plug-in that can be stacked with P2-Weight, Min-SNR, or DDPM-IP without modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: a 4.71× training speedup on ImageNet and consistent FID gains across resolutions (32×32 to 256×256) from a single-line change in the sampling distribution, making it an attractive drop-in for existing diffusion pipelines. The theoretical characterization (unbounded time-derivative norm as t→0, exponential KL convergence) provides a principled justification rather than a purely empirical heuristic. Open questions include whether the optimal α (found to be 0.8) is dataset- and architecture-independent, whether the benefit persists in conditional or video generation settings, and whether the speedup compounds with further architectural advances like U-ViT or latent diffusion.
