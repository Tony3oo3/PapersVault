## Problem

LLM-based agents for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) suffer a large performance gap (~40% SR on R2R) compared to VLN-specialized models, while fine-tuning LLMs for VLN discards their interpretive and communicative language capabilities. The paper addresses how to close this performance gap without sacrificing the LLM's ability to generate human-interpretable navigational reasoning and support interactive communication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240176/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each navigation step, the agent receives multi-view RGB images (one per candidate navigable node, rendered as equirectangular panoramic views centered on the agent's heading) along with a free-form text instruction. Each view carries an associated relative angle direction. The system produces two outputs: a navigation action (selecting the next node from a maintained topological graph, with the agent following the shortest path to that node) and a natural-language navigational reasoning trace explaining the environmental perception, instruction progress, and chosen action.

## Method

NavGPT-2 builds on InstructBLIP, using a frozen ViT-g/14 vision encoder and a frozen LLM (FlanT5-XL/XXL or Vicuna-7B/13B), with only the Q-former and a downstream policy network trained. The Q-former encodes each candidate view into 32 instruction-aware visual tokens via cross-attention with learnable queries, which are projected into the LLM's input space. A two-stage training procedure is used: first, the Q-former is instruction-tuned on 10K GPT-4V-generated single-step navigational reasoning traces (frozen LLM), and second, a topological graph-based navigation policy is fine-tuned using the frozen VLM's hidden representations as visual-linguistic features, trained with a combined behavior-cloning and DAgger loss. The policy network employs a graph-aware self-attention (GASA) mechanism that incorporates pairwise L2 spatial distances among observed nodes, enabling effective long-horizon planning and backtracking over the maintained topological graph while the frozen LLM retains its full language generation capability for reasoning output.

## Results

On R2R Test Unseen, NavGPT-2 (FlanT5-XXL, 5B) with PREVALENT pretraining achieves 71% SR and 60% SPL, outperforming the DUET baseline (69% SR, 59% SPL) by 2% SR and matching the SOTA DUET+ScaleVLN (77% SR, 68% SPL) which uses additional training environments (HM3D, Gibson) beyond MP3D. On Val Unseen, the 5B model reaches 72% SR and 61% SPL, surpassing NaviLLM (7B) by 3% SR while maintaining the ability to generate human-interpretable navigational reasoning. The 1.5B variant (FlanT5-XL) with PREVALENT achieves 70% SR / 59% SPL on Val Unseen, already exceeding all zero-shot GPT-4-based methods (best: DiscussNav at 43% SR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NavGPT-2 (5B) outperforms the DUET baseline (same graph-based policy architecture, LXMERT language branch) by 2% SR on Test Unseen (71% vs 69%) and 4% SR on Val Unseen (72% vs 68%), confirming that VLM latents are a superior visual-linguistic representation. Against the SOTA DUET+ScaleVLN (77% SR, 68% SPL on Test Unseen), NavGPT-2 is competitive when restricted to the same MP3D training scale, as ScaleVLN's advantage comes from additional HM3D and Gibson environments. NavGPT-2 decisively surpasses all zero-shot GPT-4 methods (NavGPT 34%, MapGPT 39%, DiscussNav 43% SR on Val Unseen) and fine-tuned LLM approaches (NavCoT 40%, NaviLLM 67% SR), with the 5B model beating NaviLLM (7B) by 3% SR on Test Unseen while retaining language generation capability. On cross-dataset generalization (RxR, HM3D), NavGPT-2 outperforms DUET by 3.67% SR on RxR and 21.6% SR on HM3D, demonstrating stronger out-of-domain robustness from the shared LLM latent space.

## Relation to Prior Work

NavGPT-2 positions itself as a middle ground between two extremes in LLM-based VLN: zero-shot prompting approaches (NavGPT, MapGPT, DiscussNav) that rely on fragile prompt engineering and text-only observations, and fine-tuning approaches (NavCoT, LangNav, NaviLLM) that sacrifice the LLM's general language capabilities. Architecturally, it builds on InstructBLIP's Q-former for multi-view visual encoding and DUET's topological graph policy for action planning, but the key departure is using the frozen VLM's latent space as a shared visual-linguistic representation that simultaneously feeds both language decoding (navigational reasoning) and action decoding (graph-based policy). This avoids the information loss of captioning-based zero-shot methods and the capability degradation of full LLM fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen 5B VLM with a lightweight policy network can match or exceed SOTA VLN specialists trained on the same data scale, while retaining the ability to generate human-interpretable navigational reasoning and support multi-round user interaction. The data efficiency result (matching full-data DUET with 50% less training data) and the cross-dataset generalization to RxR and HM3D suggest the VLM latent representation is more robust than task-specific encoders. However, the approach still depends on a specialized graph-based policy network, and the finding that encoder-decoder LLMs (FlanT5) substantially outperform larger decoder-only models (Vicuna-13B) for VLN raises open questions about which LLM architectures are best suited for embodied decision-making. The reliance on GPT-4V-generated reasoning data also introduces a dependency on proprietary models for training data curation.
