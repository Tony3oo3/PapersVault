## Problem

Traditional embodied instruction following benchmarks assume a static environment and fully specified instructions, producing artificially 'complete' language that bears little resemblance to how humans actually communicate. Real-world instructions are inherently underspecified, ambiguous, and their meaning unfolds through the speaker's concurrent actions and movements—capabilities that current state-of-the-art EIF models demonstrably lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241627/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent (modeled after Boston Dynamics Spot) receives egocentric RGB and depth frames at 640×480 resolution with a 79° horizontal FOV from a 0.73 m camera height, plus a text goal instruction and a human relocation utterance delivered at the start of the task phase. A Detic-based semantic mapper converts observations into an allocentric top-down map of obstacles, object categories, and human position, which is serialized into text as episodic memory. The agent outputs discrete high-level actions (Go to Room X, Explore Room X, Follow Human, Grab Obj, Put Obj, Give Obj to Human) executed by an FMM navigation planner and heuristic manipulation tools, with a 600-timestep budget per task.

## Method

SIF introduces a two-phase benchmark (exploration + task) across three task types—static Pick-and-Place (PNP), situated-object (S_obj, where the target object is relocated between phases), and situated-human (S_hum, where the human receptacle walks during the task)—each probing ambiguity, temporal intent unfolding, and dynamic action-dependent reasoning. Two LLM-based baselines are evaluated: REASONER, a closed-loop agent combining a Detic semantic mapper, a ReAct-style prompt generator that interleaves observations with past actions, and a GPT-4o planner that periodically performs ambiguity calibration (every 20 steps for S_hum, asking whether the human's destination is sufficiently determined); and PROMPTER, an open-loop GPT-3.5 planner that generates a fixed plan at task onset and queries the LLM for room-level object search. The key finding is that even with oracle perception, REASONER drops from ~80% SPL on PNP to ~45% on S_obj/S_hum, and strategic errors (incorrect room grounding, premature disengagement from human tracking) dominate failures, revealing that current models over-rely on commonsense heuristics rather than reasoning about the human's evolving behavior.

## Results

On the SIF benchmark, REASONER (closed-loop, GPT-4o) with oracle perception achieves 73–81% SPL on static PNP tasks but drops sharply to 49–58% SPL on S_obj and 29–34% SPL on S_hum tasks, revealing a fundamental gap in situated reasoning. PROMPTER (open-loop, GPT-3.5) with oracle perception scores 52–61% SPL on PNP but only 19–30% on S_obj and 19–28% on S_hum. With learned perception, both models degrade substantially: REASONER falls to 18–29% SPL on PNP and 2–15% on situated tasks, while PROMPTER drops to 18–24% on PNP and 3–11% on situated tasks. The oracle baseline with learned perception still achieves 44–69% SPL, confirming that perception (semantic segmentation + manipulation) remains a major bottleneck independent of reasoning quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REASONER (closed-loop, GPT-4o, with semantic map and iterative prompting) consistently outperforms PROMPTER (open-loop, GPT-3.5, single-shot planning) across all task types and splits. On PNP tasks with oracle perception, REASONER achieves 73–81% SPL versus PROMPTER's 52–61%, a gap of roughly 15–25 points. The advantage widens dramatically on situated tasks: on S_hum (Test Unseen), REASONER scores 34% SPL while PROMPTER manages only 19%, and on S_obj (Test Seen), REASONER reaches 58% versus PROMPTER's 25%. Both models, however, struggle far more on S_obj and S_hum than on PNP, indicating that current LLM-based agents lack the holistic, temporally-aware reasoning needed to interpret ambiguous human intent. Error analysis (Table 8) shows that REASONER's failures are dominated by strategic errors (incorrect room/human grounding), while PROMPTER additionally suffers from parsing errors due to its open-loop design, where the LLM sometimes refuses to generate plans for atypical but valid instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Embodied Instruction Following (EIF) line of work (ALFRED, TEACh, DIALFRED) by introducing a physically present, moving human whose actions and trajectory are integral to instruction interpretation. Methodologically, it builds on the modular LLM-agent architecture popularized by FILM, ReAct, LLMPlanner, and Voyager, adapting their semantic-mapper-plus-LLM-planner structure into a closed-loop REASONER baseline and an open-loop PROMPTER baseline. The key conceptual departure is from static, decontextualized pick-and-place to situated tasks where object relocation and human movement create ambiguity that must be resolved through the agent's own actions (e.g., following the human, targeted room search), rather than through commonsense alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark and consistent failure mode: SOTA EIF models that achieve ~80% SPL on static pick-and-place drop to ~30–45% when the environment is dynamic, revealing that current LLM-based planners cannot calibrate uncertainty or use their own actions to reduce ambiguity. This has direct implications for deploying robotic assistants in homes where instructions are inherently underspecified and the speaker is physically present. Open questions include whether richer perception (beyond Detic segmentation), longer-horizon planning, or interactive clarification strategies can close the gap, and whether the templated-language and 10-house scope limits generalizability of the findings.
