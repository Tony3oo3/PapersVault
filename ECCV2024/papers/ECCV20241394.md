## Problem

Existing text-to-avatar generation methods produce a single entangled 3D mesh combining body, clothes, and hair, which hinders downstream tasks such as animation, virtual try-on, and editing. While a few works explore disentangled reconstruction from video, no method enables the arbitrary *creation* of disentangled avatars from text prompts with guaranteed body-clothes alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing the desired avatar and an A-pose joint map (rendered from 3D skeleton joints) as ControlNet conditioning. It outputs two separate meshes—an unclothed human body and a clothes mesh—each with an MLP-based albedo texture, all parameterized within the SO-SMPL framework (SMPL-X shape parameter β, vertex offsets, and a binary mask). The body is animatable via standard SMPL-X pose parameters, while the clothes mesh can be independently driven by cloth physics simulation in tools like Marvelous Designer.

## Method

The core contribution is the SO-SMPL representation, which sequentially adds a learnable vertex offset O_h to SMPL-X for the body and a second offset O_c (gated by a pre-defined garment mask M_c) for clothes, guaranteeing geometric alignment by construction. Generation proceeds in two SDS-based stages: Stage I optimizes the body (β, O_h, albedo MLP) using an OpenPose-ControlNet-conditioned diffusion model with the A-pose map, while Stage II freezes the body and optimizes clothing parameters using SDS gradients computed on both the clothed-human and separated-clothes renders (RGB and normal maps). A simple Phong shading model with a randomly sampled point light prevents shadows from baking into the albedo, and an albedo smoothness loss plus Laplacian/offset geometry regularizers further suppress artifacts. Pre-defined vertex mask templates for six garment types (long/short shirts, long/short pants, vests, overalls) initialize the clothing region, and additional offset layers can be stacked for multi-layer garments.

## Results

On the main quantitative evaluation (30 prompts, 9000 rendered views), the proposed method achieves FID of 84.9, CLIP-FID of 26.2, and CLIP Score of 28.4, outperforming all three quantitative baselines on every metric. In a user study with 26 participants, the method is preferred by 83.4% for texture & geometry quality, 89.7% for animation realism, and 86.8% for prompt alignment, compared to single-digit percentages for TEXture and TADA. The method also produces separated clothes meshes that fit the underlying body, enabling realistic cloth simulation in Marvelous Designer driven by AMASS and AIST++ motion sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AvatarCLIP [35], the proposed method achieves substantially lower FID (84.9 vs. 166.0) and CLIP-FID (26.2 vs. 34.9) and a higher CLIP Score (28.4 vs. 19.2), with AvatarCLIP noted for geometry artifacts and poor facial details. Against TEXture [74], the method wins on all three metrics (FID 84.9 vs. 132.8, CLIP-FID 26.2 vs. 42.8, CLIP Score 28.4 vs. 23.6); TEXture suffers from texture inconsistency across views because it textures a fixed SMPL-X mesh. Against TADA [51], the closest quantitative competitor, the method still leads on all metrics (FID 84.9 vs. 90.2, CLIP-FID 26.2 vs. 27.4, CLIP Score 28.4 vs. 24.9), with TADA producing unnatural geometric artifacts and unaligned outfits. For clothes generation specifically, both TextMesh [84] and ProlificDreamer [92] fail to produce meaningful garment shapes or textures and cannot control position/size to fit an avatar, whereas the proposed method generates clothes that naturally conform to the underlying body. In the user study, the proposed method dominates all three preference categories (83.4–89.7%) while TEXture and TADA each receive only 3–8% preference, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceptual quality and animation realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SDS-based text-to-3D generation paradigm (DreamFusion) to the human avatar domain, building on the SMPL-X parametric body model and the offset-based body representation introduced by TADA!. The key departure is from prior text-to-avatar methods (AvatarCLIP, TADA!, TEXture) that produce a single entangled mesh, and from prior disentangled reconstruction work (DELTA, SCARF) that only operates on video input. The SO-SMPL representation introduces a sequential offset hierarchy—body offset first, then clothes offset masked on top—that is natively designed for disentangled generation rather than post-hoc separation as in AvatarFusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By natively generating separate body and clothes meshes, the method unlocks physically-based animation (forward kinematics for the body, cloth simulation in Marvelous Designer for garments) and identity-preserving virtual try-on, capabilities that entangled representations fundamentally cannot support. Remaining limitations include cartoonish color over-saturation, inability to handle long hair or garments with sewing patterns and physical material properties (stretch, friction), and the inherent over-smoothing of distillation-based frameworks. Open questions include whether the sequential offset approach scales to more complex multi-layer garments with realistic material interactions and whether the representation can be extended to capture fine garment details like stitching and fabric weave.
