## Problem

High-accuracy instance segmentation of moveable parts (doors, drawers, etc.) from real RGB images remains difficult because existing methods (OPD, OPDMulti) rely on 3D-to-2D projection annotations that introduce reconstruction and re-projection errors, while direct manual annotation on real images is prohibitively expensive. This paper introduces the first active learning framework for this task, achieving fully validated segmentations by manually annotating only 11.45% of images—a 60% time saving over the best non-AL baseline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image of an indoor scene containing one or more articulated objects (cabinets, fridges, dishwashers, microwaves, ovens, washers) and produces per-part 2D bounding boxes, polygon segmentation masks, and semantic labels (door/drawer). Intermediate outputs include a per-object binary mask, a 6DoF camera pose (translation + rotation matrix), and a 6-way interaction direction (±x, ±y, ±z), which together define a refined interaction-surface mask that constrains the fine-stage decoder to the relevant object region.

## Method

The architecture is a two-stage transformer pipeline. The coarse stage uses a modified Deformable DETR encoder-decoder where the standard object query embedding is replaced with one parameterized by normalized center coordinates and width/height from the detected 2D bounding box, enabling the decoder to jointly predict object class, 6DoF pose, interaction direction, and object mask; the interaction direction and pose yield a 3D oriented bounding box whose face along the interaction axis is projected to crop a refined binary mask. The fine stage feeds this refined mask into a three-layer Mask2Former masked-attention decoder cascade to produce part-level segmentation. The active learning strategy operates in two phases: coarse AL validates/rectifies interaction directions to establish correct refined masks, then fine AL iteratively categorizes predictions as perfect (auto-accepted), missed (manually annotated via labelme), or fair (re-evaluated next iteration), continuing until the test set is fully validated. The network is pre-trained end-to-end on ~32K synthetic PartNet-Mobility renders, then fine-tuned on real images with MLP weights frozen since ground-truth poses and object masks are unavailable.

## Results

On the 2,000-image test set, the full coarse-to-fine AL method achieves 91.3 segmentation mAP@IoU=0.5, substantially outperforming the SOTA OPDFormer-C (68.4) and OPD-C (45.2). The non-AL variant (Ours w/o AL) already reaches 77.3 mAP, surpassing all competing methods. Active learning on the 500-image enhancement set requires only 1.6 hours of manual annotation (64 images, 184 parts) across 3 iterations, translating to a 60% time saving over OPDFormer-C to reach equivalent accuracy. On the external OPDReal and OPDMulti benchmarks, the non-AL variant scores 51.6 and 31.5 mAP respectively, outperforming OPDFormer-C (46.3 and 27.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three external baselines: Grounded-SAM (zero-shot vision-language model), OPD-C (MaskRCNN-based openable part detection), and OPDFormer-C (Mask2Former-based, current SOTA). On the authors' 2,000-image test set, the full AL method (91.3 mAP) outperforms OPDFormer-C by 22.9 points and OPD-C by 46.1 points; even the non-AL variant (77.3) exceeds OPDFormer-C by 8.9 points. On OPDReal, Ours w/o AL achieves 51.6 mAP vs. 46.3 for OPDFormer-C and 44.5 for OPD-C, while on OPDMulti it scores 31.5 vs. 27.6 and 25.6 respectively. Grounded-SAM performs worst across all settings (23.1/16.5/8.0 mAP), confirming that generic foundation models lack understanding of articulated object parts without task-specific training. In annotation efficiency, the coarse-to-fine AL strategy requires only 64 images (1.6 hr) for the 500-image set versus 324 images (5.62 hr) for OPDFormer-C without AL, and 229 images (6.5 hr) for 2,000 images versus 792 (16.3 hr) for OPDFormer-C.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mask2Former masked-attention decoder for fine-grained part mask generation and a modified Deformable DETR encoder-decoder for the coarse object/pose stage, but departs from the OPD/OPDMulti line of work by replacing their 3D-to-2D projected annotations with direct 2D active learning on real photographs. It also positions against generic foundation models (SAM, Grounded-SAM), demonstrating that these lack articulated-object understanding despite strong zero-shot performance on general segmentation. The coarse-to-fine AL strategy—first validating interaction directions, then refining part masks—is a novel pipeline design not present in prior active segmentation work, which typically relies on point/region-based supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% reduction in manual annotation effort and the contribution of a 2,550-image dataset with direct 2D annotations address a practical bottleneck in building moveable-part datasets, potentially accelerating research in robot manipulation and articulated-object understanding. However, the scope is limited to six indoor object categories with only door/drawer labels, and the method's reliance on 6DoF pose estimation and interaction direction prediction may not transfer cleanly to more complex or outdoor articulations. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine AL strategy generalizes to richer part taxonomies, whether the pose-aware attention can be replaced by learned features as foundation models improve, and how the approach scales to scenes with many overlapping articulated objects.
