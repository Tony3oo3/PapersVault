## Problem

Existing face swapping methods face a fundamental training dilemma: target-oriented approaches rely on a conflicting seesaw game between reconstruction and identity losses, causing unstable training and target identity leakage, while source-oriented self-supervised methods avoid leakage but fail to transfer target skin color and illumination. Additionally, both paradigms inadequately handle shape misalignment and facial volume discrepancies between source and target faces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241475/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image and a target face image at 256×256 resolution, extracting 3DMM parameters, identity embeddings (via ArcFace with random color jittering), skin color vectors (via a dedicated encoder), and iris keypoint stickmen renderings from each. It outputs a single face-swapped image that inherits source identity (contour, inner traits, skin details) while preserving target non-identity attributes (pose, expression, skin color, illumination, background, hair); for resolutions beyond 256×256, an off-the-shelf super-resolution model is applied post-hoc.

## Method

SAMAE replaces the seesaw game with a self-supervised self-reconstruction objective that provides clear ground truth, conditioning an ADM U-Net generator on a rendered 3DMM mesh (with neutralized white albedo to remove color information), a masked non-facial attribute image, an ArcFace identity embedding (color-jittered to prevent skin color transfer), and a low-dimensional skin color embedding from a dedicated encoder. Two novel training-time augmentations address shape and volume issues: perforation confusion randomly unions the facial mask with a mask from a randomly sampled 3DMM shape, forcing the model to rely solely on the rendered mesh for contour rather than the mask; random mesh scaling samples the 3DMM scale parameter from U(-4,1) to prevent over-reliance on pixel-aligned mesh geometry. The discriminator uses a StyleGAN2 architecture, and the full model is trained on FFHQ for 500k iterations on two RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ (1K source-target pairs), SAMAE achieves the best Identity Similarity (0.578), Identity Consistency (0.628), Shape Distance (0.108), Head Pose Distance (0.013), and FID (21.22) among all baselines. Expression Distance is 0.190, essentially tied with the best baseline BlendFace (0.189). The method is trained solely on FFHQ at 256×256 without additional identity-labeled or video datasets, yet outperforms methods that augment training with multiple datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMAE outperforms all seven baselines on Identity Similarity (0.578 vs. next-best InfoSwap 0.527) and Identity Consistency (0.628 vs. next-best E4S 0.588), confirming superior source identity preservation and reduced target identity leakage. On Shape Distance, SAMAE (0.108) beats the best baseline E4S (0.118), validating the perforation confusion and random mesh scaling techniques for handling source-target shape misalignment. FID of 21.22 is the best overall, surpassing BlendFace (23.11) and SimSwap (26.77), indicating higher perceptual realism. Expression Distance is essentially tied with BlendFace (0.190 vs. 0.189), while Head Pose Distance matches BlendFace at 0.013. Among source-oriented methods, SAMAE substantially outperforms FSGAN (FID 42.56) and E4S (FID 52.90) in realism, while also achieving better identity scores, showing that the self-supervised SAMAE training avoids the illumination leakage and identity shift issues inherent to reenactment-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in face swapping by proposing a self-supervised training paradigm (SAMAE) that departs from both the dominant target-oriented seesaw game (reconstruction + identity loss, as in SimSwap, InfoSwap, DiffSwap) and the source-oriented reenactment-and-paste pipeline (FSGAN, E4S). It repurposes the masked autoencoder concept—originally from image pre-training—into a face-swapping training regime where the model reconstructs a ground-truth image from disentangled identity and non-identity conditions, thereby eliminating the conflicting multi-objective optimization. The architecture itself is a straightforward U-Net (ADM) conditioned on 3DMM-rendered meshes, ArcFace identity embeddings, and a learned skin-color vector, with StyleGAN2 as discriminator; the novelty lies in the training scheme and the two augmentation tricks (perforation confusion, random mesh scaling) rather than in a new network design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing seesaw game training recipe, used by nearly all target-oriented face-swapping methods, is a source of instability and identity leakage, and that a self-supervised alternative with explicit ground truth can outperform it across identity, shape, and realism metrics simultaneously. This reframing could influence how future face-swapping (and more broadly, identity-transfer) models are trained. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 256×256 without a separate super-resolution step, how it handles extreme expressions beyond 3DMM's representational capacity, and whether incorporating diffusion-based generative priors (as the authors suggest) would further close the realism gap. The ethical dimension—face swapping as a vector for misinformation—remains an unresolved societal concern that the authors acknowledge but do not technically address.
