## Problem

Category-Agnostic Pose Estimation (CAPE) requires detecting keypoints of arbitrary unseen categories from only a few labeled examples, but existing methods must first train on extensive base-category data, which is labor-intensive and still generalizes poorly to categories far from the base set (e.g., furniture or vehicles when trained on faces and bodies). This paper asks whether the rich spatial-structural knowledge embedded in off-the-shelf text-to-image diffusion models can replace the need for carefully prepared base categories entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–5 few-shot example images (RGB, H×W×3) with per-keypoint heatmap labels (H×W×1 each) of an unseen category, plus unlabeled test images of the same category. It outputs 2D keypoint locations as the argmax positions of per-keypoint cross-attention heatmaps (H×W×1), one heatmap per keypoint. The number of keypoints N varies by category but is fixed within a category; pseudo prompts are learned in the Stable Diffusion text-embedding space (R^{1×1×c}) and cross-attention maps are averaged across all U-Net layers after bilinear upsampling to original resolution.

## Method

The core insight is that cross-attention maps in Stable Diffusion's U-Net already encode text-to-image spatial correspondences: a text prompt highlights the semantically matching region in an image. PPM exploits this by learning pseudo prompts (randomly initialized vectors in the embedding space) that, when fed alongside an image into the frozen U-Net, produce cross-attention maps aligned with the target keypoint heatmap. In the prompt-optimization stage, each pseudo prompt P^n is optimized via L2 loss against the keypoint heatmap plus the diffusion denoising loss (to keep the prompt in a valid embedding region), using only the few-shot examples. In inference, the fixed learned prompts drive cross-attention on test images, and keypoints are located at the maximum-attention positions. The optional CPT scheme additionally trains a single category-shared prompt and a two-layer MLP on base-category data to inject common keypoint-localization priors (e.g., preference for corners and edges), which is concatenated with the category-specific prompt before inference.

## Results

On the MP-100 benchmark, PPM (without base categories) achieves a mean PCK@0.2 of 68.68 under Cross Super-Category Generalization (1-shot), surpassing the best baseline CapeFormer (65.57) by 3.1 points. Under Cross Sub-Category Generalization, PPM reaches 85.82 (1-shot) and 90.17 (5-shot) mean PCK, while PPM+CPT further improves to 87.54 and 92.58 respectively, outperforming CapeFormer (85.31 / 89.30) by 2.2 and 3.3 points. Notably, PPM achieves these results without any training on base categories, whereas all baselines require extensive base-category training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPM outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting without using any base-category training data. Against the strongest baseline CapeFormer [45], PPM gains +0.51 PCK in 1-shot and +0.87 in 5-shot Cross Sub-Category settings, and +3.11 in Cross Super-Category (1-shot). The gap widens substantially with CPT: PPM+CPT beats CapeFormer by +2.23 (1-shot) and +3.28 (5-shot) in Cross Sub-Category, and by +4.49 in Cross Super-Category. Against POMNet [57], the improvement is even larger (+5.82 to +11.87 PCK depending on setting). The older few-shot baselines (ProtoNet, MAML, Fine-tune) are far behind, with mean PCK below 65 in all settings, highlighting that meta-learning and fine-tuning approaches struggle with the domain gap between base and unseen categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper addresses Category-Agnostic Pose Estimation (CAPE), a task formalized by POMNet [57] and subsequently tackled by CapeFormer [45] and meta-learning approaches (MAML, ProtoNet, Fine-tune). All prior CAPE methods share a common dependency: extensive training on predefined base categories to acquire generalizable pose knowledge before adapting to unseen categories via few-shot examples. PPM departs from this paradigm by repurposing the cross-attention mechanism of an off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion model as a keypoint localizer, inspired by SLIME's [20] observation that diffusion cross-attention maps highlight text-relevant image regions. The key conceptual shift is treating keypoint localization as a prompt-matching problem in the diffusion model's embedding space rather than as a dedicated regression or heatmap prediction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for labor-intensive base-category annotation, PPM offers a more data-efficient path to category-agnostic pose estimation, which is practically significant given the combinatorial explosion of possible object categories. The finding that a frozen text-to-image diffusion model's cross-attention already encodes sufficient spatial-structural knowledge for keypoint localization suggests broader potential for repurposing generative models for perception tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion to other diffusion architectures, how it scales to 3D pose estimation, and whether the per-category test-time prompt optimization can be made more efficient. The reliance on a single benchmark (MP-100) and a single diffusion model limits the breadth of validation.
