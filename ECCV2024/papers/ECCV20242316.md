## Problem

Existing BEV future instance prediction methods rely on redundant pipelines with multiple auxiliary outputs (centerness, offsets, motion flow) and complex post-processing (clustering, offline ID assignment), where errors in each auxiliary prediction compound and degrade overall performance. No fully end-to-end framework existed that could directly predict future instance masks without these intermediate steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242316/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6 surround camera images from T_in consecutive history frames (past 1 second plus current state) and produces dense BEV instance segmentation masks for T_out future frames (2 seconds) on a 200×200 grid covering 100m×100m (0.5m per cell). At inference, masks are obtained by simply multiplying each instance query's object score with its mask logits and thresholding—no clustering, ID assignment, or other post-processing is required. The framework also simultaneously outputs 3D bounding boxes and class labels as auxiliary supervision.

## Method

FipTR is a fully end-to-end framework built on three components: a temporal BEV encoder (BEVDet4D or BEVFormer), a flow-aware BEV predictor, and an instance masks decoder. The key architectural insight is using learnable instance queries to directly estimate future occupied masks via element-wise product with BEV features, eliminating centerness estimation and clustering. The flow-aware BEV predictor iteratively propagates BEV features to future frames using a Flow-aware Deformable Attention (FADA) module that concatenates a predicted backward flow map with the query to compute sampling offsets, establishing dense spatial correspondence between adjacent BEV frames. A novel future instance matching strategy incorporates accumulated multi-frame mask cost into the Hungarian assignment, ensuring the same ground-truth object across all future frames is always matched to the same instance query, thereby enforcing temporal coherence without post-hoc ID tracking.

## Results

On the NuScenes validation set, FipTR-S (Swin-S backbone, BEVDet4D encoder) achieves 61.7 IoU and 56.1 VPQ under the 30 m ROI, and 40.4 IoU and 37.0 VPQ under the 100 m ROI, setting new state-of-the-art among BEVDet4D-based methods. With CBGS data augmentation (FipTR-S†), VPQ further improves to 58.0 (30 m) and 39.5 (100 m). When paired with the BEVFormer encoder (FipTR-R101), the method reaches 62.9 IoU and 57.2 VPQ (30 m), surpassing UniAD on VPQ by +2.5 and +4.3 points while remaining competitive on IoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BEVerse-S† (same Swin-S + BEVDet4D setup with CBGS), FipTR-S† wins on all four metrics, with the largest gains on VPQ: +3.7 (30 m) and +3.4 (100 m), while IoU improves by +0.8 and +0.2 respectively. Compared to PowerBEV (EfficientNet, BEVDet4D), FipTR-S† slightly trails on IoU(n) (62.2 vs 62.5) but leads on IoU(f) (+1.8) and VPQ(f) (+5.7), indicating substantially better temporal instance consistency. Against UniAD (R101, BEVFormer), FipTR-R101 achieves a lower IoU(n) by 0.5 points but surpasses it on VPQ(n) by +2.5 and VPQ(f) by +4.3, confirming that the end-to-end instance-query design yields more temporally coherent masks. FIERY and StretchBEV are clearly outperformed across all metrics, with FipTR-S† exceeding FIERY by +12.8 VPQ(n) and +9.6 VPQ(f). Overall, FipTR achieves SOTA VPQ on both BEV encoder variants while maintaining competitive IoU, validating that the flow-aware predictor and future instance matching strategy improve temporal coherence without sacrificing segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FipTR adapts the instance-query paradigm from DETR and Deformable DETR to the BEV future instance prediction setting, replacing the multi-stage pipeline (centerness estimation, clustering, offline ID assignment) used by FIERY, BEVerse, PowerBEV, and TBPFormer with a fully end-to-end mask decoder. The flow-aware deformable attention (FADA) is a targeted modification of standard deformable attention that injects predicted backward flow into offset computation, drawing on optical-flow ideas from occupancy flow fields. The future instance matching strategy extends the single-frame Hungarian matching used in video instance segmentation (VisTR, GenVIS) to accumulate multi-frame mask costs, ensuring a single query tracks one object across all predicted frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating auxiliary outputs and post-processing, FipTR offers a cleaner, more interpretable pipeline that is easier to integrate into end-to-end autonomous driving stacks like UniAD, while matching or exceeding prior SOTA on VPQ. The ablation revealing that the 3D box branch is nearly indispensable for mask quality (IoU collapses to 5.2 without it) highlights a strong coupling between detection and segmentation that prior work had not quantified. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer prediction horizons, whether the flow-aware attention generalizes to sensor suites beyond six cameras, and whether the non-weight-shared per-frame modules remain practical under real-time latency constraints.
