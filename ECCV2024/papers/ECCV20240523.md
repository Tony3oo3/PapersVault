## Problem

While dataset distillation algorithms have advanced significantly in matching strategies and optimization, it remains unclear which real data samples are actually essential for the distillation process itself. The paper reveals that real datasets contain severe redundancy during distillation—pruning up to 99.9% of samples can maintain or even improve performance—yet no principled criterion existed for identifying and selecting the most valuable real samples for the distillation task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a full real dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNet-1K at 224×224, Kinetics-400 videos) along with a meta loss function defining the distillation objective. In a pre-processing stage, the full dataset is used to compute per-sample empirical losses; during distillation, mini-batches from the pruned dataset are consumed iteratively. The output is a small synthetic dataset (1–50 instances per class) plus the pruned real dataset, where the synthetic set can train networks to near full-dataset accuracy while the pruned real set reduces downstream training cost by up to 60%.

## Method

BiLP proposes a two-level data pruning strategy grounded in NTK dynamics and causal inference. First, preemptive pruning uses per-sample empirical loss as a static criterion to remove high-loss (high-gradient) real samples before distillation begins, justified by the rank-deficiency of the real-data NTK when synthetic data is small. Second, adaptive pruning computes the Individual Treatment Effect (ITE) of each real sample—defined as the difference in meta gradient with and without that sample—to dynamically select samples during training, removing both low-ITE (uninformative) and high-ITE (outlier) samples. Three efficiency optimizations make ITE computation tractable: a Taylor approximation that reduces per-sample gradient calls to a single Hessian-vector product, a running-mean/variance estimator (analogous to batch normalization) to approximate the global ITE distribution from mini-batches, and lazy selection that updates the pruned batch every 10 iterations, collectively reducing ITE overhead to ~7% of original training time.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (IPC=1), BiLP+IDC(x3) achieves 55.9% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best IDC (50.6%) by 5.3% and DREAM (51.1%) by 4.8%. On CIFAR-100 (IPC=1), BiLP+IDC(x3) reaches 34.0%, outperforming IDC (28.2%) by 5.8% and DREAM (29.5%) by 4.5%. On SVHN (IPC=1), BiLP+IDC(x3) attains 77.2% vs. IDC's 68.5% (+8.7%). On large-scale ImageNet-1K (IPC=1), BiLP+DM achieves 1.95% accuracy in 9.9h vs. 1.58% in 22.4h for full-data DM, and BiLP+MTT reaches 2.10% in 31.2h vs. an estimated 205h for full-data MTT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiLP+IDC(x3) consistently outperforms the strongest baselines across all datasets: it beats DREAM by 4.8% on CIFAR-10 IPC=1, 4.5% on CIFAR-100 IPC=1, and 7.4% on SVHN IPC=1, while matching or exceeding PDD on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 (76.9% vs. 76.5%). Against IDC, the base BiLP+IDC improves by 0.9–2.1% across settings, and the x3 variant adds another 4–8.7% gain, most pronounced on CIFAR-100 IPC=1 (+5.8%) and SVHN IPC=1 (+8.7%). BiLP+DC improves over DC by 0.3–1.0% on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, confirming the plug-and-play nature of the method. On large-scale ImageNet-1K, BiLP+DM surpasses full-data DM by 0.37% at IPC=1 while cutting training time from 22.4h to 9.9h (56% reduction), and BiLP+MTT achieves 2.10% in 31.2h versus an estimated 205h for full-data MTT. The method does not surpass HaBa on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 (69.8% vs. 69.9%) but exceeds it on all other reported settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of dataset distillation and data selection, but carves new ground by being the first to systematically analyze which real samples are essential for the distillation process itself. It departs from prior data pruning work (which targets regular training) and from DREAM [24] (which prunes within batches via feature distribution) by formulating sample value through NTK-based dynamics and causal inference (ITE). The bi-level structure—static empirical-loss pruning before distillation plus dynamic causal-effect pruning during distillation—repurposes ideas from causal inference [34] and NTK theory [12] into a plug-and-play framework that wraps around existing distillation algorithms (DC, IDC, DM, MTT) without modifying their core optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale distillation: preemptive pruning reduces training time by up to 60% on ImageNet-1K and Kinetics-400 while maintaining or improving accuracy, and the method is a drop-in enhancement for any existing distillation pipeline. A key open question the paper identifies is how to incorporate higher-order sample interactions (e.g., diversity) into the selection criterion without sacrificing efficiency; the per-sample ITE framework currently fails on low-diversity datasets like MNIST. Future work on modeling these interactions, possibly via annealing or Monte-Carlo methods, could unlock further gains, and the observation that some samples are actively 'detrimental' to distillation opens a new direction for understanding dataset pathology in the distillation context.
