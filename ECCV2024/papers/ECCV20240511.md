## Problem

Open-source pre-trained backbones make it trivial to fine-tune models for new tasks, raising safety concerns that bad actors could rapidly develop harmful applications. The paper asks whether one can design a pre-trained model that is specifically difficult to fine-tune for a set of restricted downstream classes while remaining competitive on all other classes, focusing on few-shot classification (FSC) as the testbed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240511/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained backbone weights (ResNet-18 for classical FSC, CLIP-ResNet50 for language-vision FSC), a task distribution providing N-way-K-shot support and query splits, a restricted class set R (typically one superclass of ~38 ImageNet classes or one CelebA attribute), and a known FSC algorithm F. It outputs modified backbone parameters θ that, when used as initialization for F, cause degraded accuracy on R while preserving accuracy on R'. The data is partitioned into three disjoint sets: D_A for LTO training (further split into D_fsc for the FSC learner and D_obs for obstruction evaluation), D_F for downstream FSC meta-training, and D_eval for meta-testing.

## Method

LTO is a MAML-like meta-learning algorithm that inverts the standard 'learn a good initialization' objective: instead of minimizing post-adaptation loss, it minimizes L_{R'} − L_R, where L_R and L_{R'} are the FSC cross-entropy losses evaluated on restricted and other classes respectively. Concretely, for each sampled task, the FSC learner F (e.g., ProtoNet, MetaOptNet, CoOp, Tip-Adapter) is applied to the current backbone to produce intermediate parameters (θ̃, φ̃) via unrolled gradient steps; the outer loop then backpropagates the difference L_{R'} − L_R through this learner to update the backbone toward a 'poor initialization' for R. For CLIP-based FSC, text-encoder gradients are computed via unbiased resampling of a prompt subset to manage GPU memory. The algorithm is evaluated using a DropRatio metric (Δ@β = δ_R / δ_{R'}), where higher values indicate stronger selective obstruction with minimal collateral damage to unrestricted classes.

## Results

LTO is evaluated using the DropRatio metric Δ@2 (ratio of accuracy drop on restricted classes to drop on other classes; higher = better obstruction). On ImageNet with classical FSC, LTO achieves average Δ@2 of 4.42 (ProtoNet, 1-shot) and 13.40 (MetaOptNet, 5-shot). On CIFAR-100 with CLIP-based methods, LTO achieves Δ@2 of 11.99 (CE), 6.73 (CoOp), and 10.16 (Tip-Adapter); on ImageNet, 7.65, 4.58, and 5.86 respectively. On CelebA attribute learning, LTO reduces average AUROC by Δ@2 = 8.04%. Compared to the machine unlearning baseline SSD, LTO achieves 11.99/6.73/10.16 vs. SSD's 1.09/1.17/1.07 on CIFAR-100 (5-shot), demonstrating far stronger obstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is against SSD (Selective Synaptic Dampening), a state-of-the-art machine unlearning method. On CIFAR-100 with 5-shot data, SSD achieves Δ@2 of only 1.09 (CE), 1.17 (CoOp), and 1.07 (Tip-Adapter), meaning it barely obstructs restricted classes relative to other classes. LTO dramatically outperforms SSD, achieving 11.99, 6.73, and 10.16 respectively — roughly 6–11× higher DropRatio. Even when SSD is given 4× more training data (20-shot), its Δ@2 rises to only 2.98/4.57/3.55, still far below LTO's 5-shot results. Internally, LTO also outperforms two ablation baselines: OnlyR (which directly maximizes restricted-class loss without the FSC learner) and NoF (which omits the FSC learner from the optimization). On CIFAR-100, LTO's average Δ@2 of 11.99/6.73/10.16 exceeds OnlyR (1.48/1.80/2.08) and NoF (6.57/6.15/4.43) across all three FSC methods, confirming that incorporating the FSC learner into the meta-optimization is critical for effective obstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

LTO inverts the MAML framework: where MAML learns a good initialization for fast adaptation, LTO learns a poor initialization that specifically obstructs adaptation on a restricted class set while preserving performance elsewhere. The paper explicitly distinguishes its task from machine unlearning (which removes existing knowledge) and data poisoning (which corrupts training data), positioning obstruction as a third category: preventing future learning on specific classes. The FSC methods targeted (ProtoNet, MetaOptNet, CoOp, Tip-Adapter) represent the standard landscape of few-shot classification built on pre-trained backbones, and LTO is designed to be agnostic to which specific FSC method is subsequently applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work introduces a concrete mechanism for the 'safer release' of open-source pre-trained models, addressing a real governance concern as fine-tuning barriers continue to drop. Key open questions include whether the obstruction survives adaptive adversaries who are aware of LTO, whether it generalizes beyond classification to tasks like detection or segmentation, and whether the required knowledge of the downstream FSC method limits practical deployment. The finding that obstruction partially persists even with 4× more data or epochs suggests a non-trivial modification to the representation space, but the 200-step training budget and single-dataset-per-setting evaluation leave the robustness of the effect under scale uncertain.
