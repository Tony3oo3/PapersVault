## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods and the SIRC heuristic for selective classification lack a theoretical characterization of the optimal strategy, and it remains unclear whether the SCOD problem is learnable from ID data alone. This paper derives the exact form of the optimal SCOD strategy, proves its non-PAC-learnability in a distribution-free setting with only ID data, and proposes a practical learning method grounded in the theory.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two data sources: a labeled ID training set and an unlabeled mixture of ID and OOD samples (which can be collected passively from real deployment by simply recording inputs). The OOD prior in the training mixture need not match the test-time prior, since the SCOD objective is independent of the OOD fraction. The output is a selective classifier that either predicts a class label or issues a reject decision, with the rejection probability determined by a linear threshold on a 2D score.

## Method

The paper proves that the optimal SCOD strategy pairs the Bayes ID classifier with a stochastic linear selector operating on two features: the conditional risk of the ID classifier and the OOD/ID likelihood ratio. To learn a plug-in estimate (POSCOD), the method first trains a standard CNN with cross-entropy loss to obtain the class posterior and conditional risk, then trains a binary classifier using a novel corrected sigmoid model (CSM) on the unlabeled ID/OOD mixture to estimate the likelihood ratio. The CSM differs from the standard sigmoid by incorporating an additional parameter that accounts for the unknown OOD fraction in the training mixture, enabling recovery of that fraction from the learned parameters. The final selector score is a linear combination of the conditional risk and the estimated likelihood ratio, with the threshold tuned on ID data to meet a target TPR. The approach requires no hyperparameters beyond the problem parameters (α, tpr_min) and modifies only two lines of PyTorch code relative to standard training.

## Results

On ImageNet with α=0.5, the POSCOD linear strategy (combining conditional risk r̂(x) and learned likelihood ratio ĝ(x)) achieves AuSRT of 6.37% on SSB_Hard, 7.44% on NINCO, 4.16% on iNaturalist, 4.38% on Textures, and 6.48% on OpenImage_O. These results substantially outperform the best single-score OODD methods (e.g., ReAct at 18.88% on SSB_Hard) and the SIRC strategy (14.27% on SSB_Hard with the same POSCOD LR). Even in the tuned setting (optimizing hyperparameters on test data), the linear strategy (5.88% on SSB_Hard) beats SIRC (6.59%), confirming the theoretical prediction that a linear combination of r(x) and g(x) is the optimal selector form.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The POSCOD linear strategy dominates all baselines by a wide margin when using the learned likelihood ratio: on SSB_Hard it achieves 6.37% AuSRT versus 14.27% for SIRC with the same LR [42], 18.88% for ReAct [35], and 40.72% for ViM Residual [41]. Against SIRC specifically, the linear strategy wins on every OOD dataset (e.g., 4.38% vs 10.50% on Textures, 4.16% vs 8.02% on iNaturalist), confirming the theoretical result that the optimal selector is linear in (r(x), g(x)) rather than the nonlinear SIRC combination. Among single-score OODD methods, ASH [9] and ReAct [35] are the strongest (6.95% and 7.23% on iNaturalist respectively), but both are still 2–3× worse than POSCOD. When contemporary OODD scores are used in the linear combination (without POSCOD LR), the linear strategy only marginally improves over single-score (e.g., ASH: 18.74% vs 19.04% on SSB_Hard), and in some cases SIRC with GradNorm [16] slightly outperforms the linear strategy (16.99% vs 17.40% on SSB_Hard). The corrected sigmoid in POSCOD also outperforms the standard sigmoid used in prior work [18,27]: on SSB_Hard, the corrected sigmoid linear strategy achieves 6.37% versus 7.28% for the standard sigmoid, a consistent ~12% relative improvement across all OOD datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SCOD problem formulation and SIRC heuristic from Xia & Bouganis (ACCV 2022), extending the non-PAC-learnability result for OODD from Fang et al. (NeurIPS 2022) to the SCOD setting, and drawing on the semi-supervised novelty detection framework of Katz-Samuels et al. (ICML 2022) and Narasimhan et al. (2023) for learning from unlabeled ID/OOD mixtures. The key departure is providing the first closed-form characterization of the optimal SCOD strategy as a linear selector over conditional risk and OOD/ID likelihood ratio, replacing the nonlinear SIRC combination and all single-score OODD methods with a theoretically grounded 2D linear rule. The corrected sigmoid model (CSM) generalizes the standard sigmoid used in prior semi-supervised OODD work by jointly estimating the unknown OOD prior in the training mixture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that the optimal SCOD selector is a simple linear combination of two interpretable scores (conditional risk and likelihood ratio) provides a clear design principle that narrows the search space for future SCOD methods and explains why existing nonlinear heuristics are suboptimal. The non-PAC-learnability theorem establishes a fundamental limitation: no algorithm using only ID data can reliably solve SCOD in a distribution-free setting, which has broad implications for the design of OOD-aware systems. Open questions include whether the CSM assumption (approximately normal feature distributions) holds for non-CNN architectures or highly structured data, how the method behaves under severe distribution shift where the proper-novelty assumption fails, and whether the 2D linear structure generalizes to multi-class OOD or sequential prediction settings.
