## Problem

Conditional diffusion models for image translation produce 'structural hallucinations'—realistic-looking but structurally incorrect reconstructions—when the conditional image contains out-of-distribution regions (e.g., tumors in brain MRIs trained only on healthy tissue). This is especially dangerous in medical imaging, where a hallucinated tumor can be rendered as healthy tissue, leading to misdiagnosis. Prior OOD generalization methods either require retraining, are confined to style transfer, or do not specifically target the structural fidelity of generated features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditional image (28×28 for MNIST super-resolution, 224×224 for BraTS MRI translation and MVTec anomaly detection), a pre-trained conditional DDPM or DDIM model, and an OOD probability map produced by PatchCore (WideResNet-50 embeddings matched against an in-distribution memory bank). It outputs a single translated image, but internally generates separate OOD and IND region predictions that are fused via a pixel-wise selection operator. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates at inference time, with the number of diffusion steps (500 for DDPM, or 0.1T/0.5T for DDIM) controlling the branching-to-fusion transition point.

## Method

The core insight is that hallucinations arise from local OOD regions contaminating the denoising process, and that partitioning both the noisy estimate and the condition into IND/OOD components yields a tighter ELBO bound (Eq. 6). The framework has three stages: (1) PatchCore produces a soft OOD probability map over the conditional image; (2) a 'branching' module runs two parallel local diffusion processes—one conditioned on the OOD-masked image, one on the IND-masked image—during the early-to-mid reverse steps where hallucinations are most prevalent, with selective masking applied to OOD predictions to prevent cross-region interference; (3) a 'fusion' module merges the two predictions via a pixel-wise S operator (Eq. 8) in the later reverse steps to eliminate boundary artifacts and restore natural image appearance. An auxiliary classifier evaluates the fused image after the first fusion iteration to decide whether to continue fusing or insert an additional branching phase, balancing faithfulness against realism. The approach is plug-and-play, requiring no fine-tuning of the pre-trained diffusion model.

## Results

On the downstream hallucination-evaluation tasks, DDPM+Ours achieves 98.0% digit classification accuracy on MNIST, 0.590 Dice coefficient on BraTS tumor segmentation, and 85.0% anomaly detection accuracy on MVTec AD. These represent more than a 3× improvement in BraTS Dice over DDPM (0.194) and a 26.6-point gain in MVTec accuracy over DDPM (58.4%). In overall image quality (Table 1), DDPM+Ours also leads on PSNR and SSIM across all three datasets (e.g., 21.2 PSNR / 0.720 SSIM on BraTS vs. 19.5 / 0.709 for DDPM), with p-values ≤ 0.02 confirming statistical significance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: DDPM [10], DDIM [29] (at 0.1T and 0.5T steps), and DDPM+DSI [35] (Distribution Shift Inversion, the only prior training-free OOD method for diffusion-based image translation). DDPM+Ours outperforms DDPM on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gains on BraTS Dice (0.590 vs. 0.194, a >3× improvement) and MVTec AD accuracy (85.0% vs. 58.4%). Against DDIM, the proposed method also wins consistently; notably, DDIM at 0.1T actually degrades MVTec accuracy to 53.9% relative to DDPM's 58.4%, while DDIM+Ours recovers to 66.8% and DDIM+Ours at 0.5T reaches 83.2%. DSI [35] is the weakest baseline on BraTS (Dice 0.100, worse than DDPM's 0.194) and only marginally beats DDPM on MVTec (60.1% vs. 58.4%), confirming that its style-transfer-oriented approach does not generalize to structural hallucination in medical or industrial imaging. The paper reports a 40% reduction in misdiagnosis risk on BraTS and 25% on MVTec relative to DDPM, underscoring the practical impact of the hallucination reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM framework for conditional image translation and introduces a training-free 'Local Diffusion' strategy that partitions the reverse denoising process into parallel OOD and IND branches, followed by a fusion step. It departs from prior OOD generalization work (Deep Image Prior, in-context learning, patch-based auto-regressive models) by targeting a specific failure mode—structural hallucination—rather than overall image quality, and from DSI (the only other training-free OOD method for diffusion-based image translation) by operating on spatially localized regions rather than global distribution alignment. The ELBO analysis provides a theoretical justification that conditioning on partitioned in/out-of-distribution data yields a tighter bound than the standard reverse process, grounding the branching design in variational inference principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly identify and mitigate structural hallucination in diffusion-based image translation, with demonstrated 40% and 25% reductions in misdiagnosis risk on medical and industrial datasets respectively—a practically significant outcome for safety-critical applications. Key limitations include heavy dependence on OOD detector quality (PatchCore underperforms manual/U-Net segmentation by a notable margin), a realism-faithfulness trade-off in choosing the branching-to-fusion transition point, and evaluation restricted to 2D images and three datasets. Open questions include extending the framework to 3D medical volumes, addressing color and texture hallucinations beyond structural ones, and developing more interpretable and accurate OOD estimation that does not require task-specific segmentation.
