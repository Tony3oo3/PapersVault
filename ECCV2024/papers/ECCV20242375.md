## Problem

Self-supervised contrastive learning tends to prioritize general visual similarities over the subtle, fine-grained patterns needed to distinguish closely related categories (e.g., bird species, car models), a limitation termed 'coarse-label bias.' Existing SSL methods are also distracted by task-irrelevant features, failing to capture the discriminative cues that fine-grained visual recognition demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242375/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (ResNet-50 backbone, 100-epoch pre-training with batch size 128) and produces a latent feature vector used for downstream linear-probe classification and nearest-neighbor image retrieval. Unusually, the training pipeline also generates synthetic image pairs by decoding perturbed latent vectors back into pixel space, so the encoder is trained on both real augmented views and decoder-reconstructed images that differ only in non-discriminative regions.

## Method

The approach identifies non-discriminative latent dimensions via two criteria: (1) Grad-CAM scores computed from the SSL contrastive loss with respect to the encoder's feature vector (low scores indicate task-irrelevant dimensions), and (2) per-dimension variance estimated from a memory bank of feature vectors (low-variance dimensions fail to separate categories). Gaussian noise is then injected into the feature vector with magnitude inversely proportional to each dimension's Grad-CAM score and variance, preserving discriminative features while perturbing irrelevant ones. A decoder reconstructs images from both the original and perturbed feature vectors, and the resulting pair is treated as a positive contrastive pair in an additional InfoNCE-style loss. The total training objective combines the standard contrastive loss, a reconstruction loss (keeping the decoder aligned with the encoder), and the contrastive loss on generated pairs, encouraging the encoder to become invariant to non-discriminative variations while sharpening its focus on fine-grained cues.

## Results

On the three primary fine-grained datasets, the proposed method (MoCo v2 + feature perturbation) achieves 66.17%, 65.60%, and 55.28% top-1 linear-probe accuracy on CUB-200, Stanford Cars, and FGVC-Aircraft respectively, surpassing MoCo v2 by 2.19, 3.58, and 4.15 percentage points. In image retrieval, rank-1 accuracy improves by 2.34, 5.30, and 3.25 points over MoCo v2 on the same datasets. The method also extends to SimSiam (57.80% on CUB-200 vs. 46.75% for vanilla SimSiam) and to additional domains (GTSRB: 91.67% vs. 88.25%; ISIC2017: 66.92% vs. 64.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms every baseline across all three primary datasets in both classification and retrieval. Against the closest SOTA competitor LCR [35], it gains 0.93% (CUB-200), 1.64% (Cars), and 2.06% (Aircraft) in top-1 accuracy, and 0.80–1.72 points in rank-1 retrieval. Over the augmentation-based methods DiLo [44] and ContrastiveCrop [31], the gains are larger (3.2% and 1.9–3.2% respectively), suggesting that perturbing latent features and reconstructing images is more effective than modifying raw pixels. Compared to attention-alignment methods SAM [36] and CVSA [12], the advantage is 1.2–2.8% in classification. The method also substantially exceeds the generic SSL baselines: over MoCo v2 [5] it improves by 2.2–4.2% in classification and 2.3–5.3% in rank-1 retrieval, and over SimSiam [6] the SimSiam+Ours variant gains 11.05% on CUB-200. Notably, the supervised upper bound (77.46% on CUB-200) remains well above the best self-supervised result, indicating substantial headroom.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSL-for-FGVR line of work that has focused on either modifying input augmentations (SAGA, CAST, ContrastiveCrop, DiLo) or training auxiliary networks to align encoder attention with saliency maps (CVSA, LCR, SAM, LEWEL). Its key departure is operating entirely in the latent feature space: it identifies non-discriminative dimensions via Grad-CAM scores and cross-dataset dimension variance, perturbs only those dimensions, and uses a decoder to reconstruct synthetic image pairs from the original and perturbed feature vectors. The method is modular, plugging into existing contrastive frameworks (MoCo v2, SimSiam) without altering their core architecture, and it reframes dimensional collapse—from a pathology to be avoided—as a useful mechanism when selectively induced on task-irrelevant feature dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented limitation of SSL in FGVR (the 'coarse-label bias') with a principled, plug-and-play mechanism that does not require labeled data or task-specific augmentations. However, the approach is evaluated only with ResNet-50 on relatively small fine-grained datasets (≤200 classes), and the decoder introduces additional training overhead and a pre-training step that may limit scalability. Open questions include whether the latent-space perturbation strategy transfers to transformer backbones, whether it generalizes to large-scale general recognition (the paper notes only modest gains outside FGVR), and whether the specific combination of Grad-CAM and variance-based perturbation is optimal or whether other saliency/variance criteria could be substituted.
