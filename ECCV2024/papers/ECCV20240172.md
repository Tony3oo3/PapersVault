## Problem

Existing industrial defect datasets provide only binary masks or coarse single-class labels, failing to distinguish multiple defect types within a single image or capture fine-grained boundaries, which limits their utility for practical quality-control workflows. Additionally, defective samples are extremely scarce (e.g., only 900 in DAGM, 1258 in MVTec), making it difficult to train diverse and robust segmentation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240172/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For segmentation, the model takes an industrial product RGB image and outputs a pixel-level mask with one of 125 distinct defect class labels, supporting multiple co-occurring defect types per image. For Defect-Gen, the input is Gaussian noise, and the output is a synthetic defective image paired with its corresponding multi-class mask; internally, the RGB image and one-hot-encoded mask are channel-wise concatenated (3 + n_defect channels) as the diffusion target. Each sample also carries a manually refined descriptive caption for VLM integration.

## Method

Defect-Gen is a two-stage diffusion generator that tackles few-shot overfitting by modeling patch-level rather than image-level distributions: it reduces the U-Net's down-sampling layers to shrink the receptive field, effectively treating local patches as independent samples and lowering the data-to-sample ratio. During inference, a large-receptive-field model runs the early diffusion steps to establish global geometry, then a switch timestep u transfers control to the small-receptive-field model, which generates diverse local defect patterns in the remaining steps. The dataset itself refines four industrial benchmarks (MVTec, VISION, DAGM2007, Cotton-Fabric) through expert re-annotation, adding 125 defect classes, multi-class labels for 552 images, and detailed captions, while Defect-Click (built on Focal-Click with multi-level crop training and edge-sensitive losses) accelerates the labeling pipeline by ~60%.

## Results

On the Defect Spectrum benchmark, no single segmentation model dominates across all subsets: DeepLabV3+ leads on DS-MVTec (51.58 mIoU mean), HRNet on DS-VISION (53.14), and Mask2Former on DS-Cotton-Fabric (64.09). In the industrial simulation experiment, training on Defect Spectrum annotations yields a 96.07% recall (vs. 85.33% for original annotations) and a 16.50% false positive rate (vs. 49.60%), a 10.74-point recall gain and 33.10-point FPR reduction. Defect-Gen synthetic data boosts mIoU by up to 9.85 points (PSP on DS-Cotton: 48.73→58.58) and 9.76 points (SegFormer-MiT-B0 on DS-MVTec: 46.45→56.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the eight segmentation baselines, DeepLabV3+ (DL) achieves the highest mean mIoU on DS-MVTec (51.58) and DS-DAGM2007 (86.82), while Mask2Former (M2F) leads on DS-VISION (54.12) and DS-Cotton-Fabric (64.09), and HRNet (HR) edges out DL on DS-VISION (53.14 vs. 52.86). ViT-Tiny-Segmenter (V-T) is the weakest on DS-DAGM2007 (52.42) but competitive on DS-Cotton-Fabric (51.29), confirming that no universal architecture exists. For generation quality (Figure 7), Defect-Gen clearly outperforms CycleGAN and DDPM, both of which merely replicate training samples with no diversity, and surpasses SinDiffusion and SinGAN, which produce diverse but visually unrealistic outputs. In the data-augmentation experiment (Table 3), Defect-Gen synthetic data improves all three tested models (DeepLabV3+, Mask2Former, SegFormer-MiT-B0) across DS-MVTec, DS-VISION, and DS-Cotton, with the largest gains on DS-Cotton (up to +9.85 mIoU for PSP) and DS-MVTec (up to +9.76 for MiT-B0), while DS-VISION sees smaller but consistent improvements of 1.1–1.3 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the industrial defect inspection dataset lineage (MVTec, VISION, DAGM2007, Cotton-Fabric) by refining annotations to pixel-level multi-class semantic masks and adding descriptive captions, departing from the binary-mask paradigm that dominates prior benchmarks. For generation, it builds on DDPM but introduces two key modifications: patch-level modeling via reduced U-Net receptive field to combat few-shot overfitting, and a two-stage inference (large-receptive-field model for global geometry, small-receptive-field model for local diversity) inspired by the observation that diffusion timesteps encode different information granularities. The Defect-Click annotation tool adapts the Focal-Click interactive segmentation framework to the industrial domain with multi-level crop training and edge-sensitive losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Defect Spectrum benchmark fills a critical gap: prior industrial datasets either lack pixel-level annotations, provide only binary masks, or merge multiple defect types into a single class, making them insufficient for practical quality-control workflows that require distinguishing defect severity and type. The Defect-Gen's ability to produce diverse, high-fidelity defect images from as few as 25 training samples addresses the fundamental data-scarcity bottleneck in industrial settings where defective samples are rare and collecting more is costly. Open questions include whether the semantic granularity translates to improved downstream decision-making beyond segmentation mIoU, whether Defect-Gen generalizes to entirely unseen defect categories, and whether the VLM-caption integration will yield practical gains in zero-shot defect understanding.
