## Problem

Sequence-based HMER methods treat expressions as flat LaTeX sequences and only implicitly learn structural syntax, making them struggle with complex nested expressions where position and hierarchical relationships between symbols are critical. Existing tree-based methods that explicitly model structure suffer from limited generalization due to insufficient diversity of tree structures across expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240571/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single handwritten mathematical expression image of size H×W as input and autoregressively outputs a LaTeX symbol sequence of length T. A key design choice is that the position forest encoding is derived entirely from the ground-truth LaTeX sequence via a deterministic parsing algorithm (Algorithm 1), requiring no additional annotations. During inference, the position forest coding and its decoder heads are completely removed, so the model produces the LaTeX sequence with zero additional latency or computational cost over the baseline CoMER architecture.

## Method

PosFormer introduces two components on top of the CoMER encoder-decoder baseline (DenseNet backbone + 3-layer Transformer decoder). First, a Position Forest encodes each LaTeX sequence into a forest of trees where each symbol receives a position identifier (a string of M/L/R characters) denoting its nested level and relative spatial position (middle/left/right) within substructures like fractions, radicals, and superscripts; two auxiliary heads predict the nested level and relative position of each symbol, providing explicit position-aware supervision during training. Second, an Implicit Attention Correction (IAC) module modifies CoMER's coverage-based attention refinement by zeroing out attention weights associated with previously decoded structure symbols (e.g., ^, _, {, }) before accumulating the refinement term, preventing attention drift when the model transitions from decoding structural tokens to entity symbols. The model is trained end-to-end with a combined loss of expression recognition cross-entropy and position recognition cross-entropy, and the position forest component is shown to be a plug-in that also improves RNN-based baselines like DWAP and CAN.

## Results

On the single-line CROHME benchmarks (with scale augmentation), PosFormer achieves 62.68%/61.03%/64.97% ExpRate on CROHME 2014/2016/2019, surpassing the previous SOTA (BPD-Coverage, CoMER) by 2.03%/1.22%/2.00% respectively. On the multi-line M²E dataset, PosFormer reaches 58.33% ExpRate and 0.0366 CER, improving over LAST by 1.83% in ExpRate. On the complex MNE dataset, PosFormer achieves a remarkable 4.62% average ExpRate gain, and on HME100k it reaches 69.51% ExpRate (+2.20% over CAN). All gains are achieved with no additional inference latency or computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PosFormer outperforms all 15 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On CROHME (with scale augmentation), it beats the closest competitors BPD-Coverage [20] (60.65/58.50/61.47%) and CoMER [43] (59.33/59.81/62.97%) by 2.03%/1.22%/2.00% ExpRate on CROHME-14/16/19, while also substantially improving error-tolerance metrics (e.g., ≤2: 84.69% vs. 75.66% for CoMER on CROHME-14). On the multi-line M²E dataset, PosFormer surpasses the purpose-built multi-line method LAST [34] by 1.83% ExpRate (58.33% vs. 56.50%) and reduces CER from 0.0530 to 0.0366. Older RNN-based methods (DWAP [37], BTTR [44], ABM [2]) and tree-based methods (TSDNet [45], SAN [35]) are beaten by 5–10+ points in ExpRate. In the cross-method comparison (Table 6), PosFormer also outperforms the positional-enhancement method RobustScanner [36] by 3.69% average ExpRate and the language-model-based RLFN [5] by 6.25%, confirming that explicit position-forest modeling is more effective than either positional feature enhancement or post-hoc language correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

PosFormer extends the sequence-based encoder-decoder paradigm for HMER (building directly on CoMER's transformer architecture) by introducing an explicit structural prior that tree-based methods (BLSTM, TSDNet, SAN) model through full syntax-tree decoding. The key conceptual departure is the position forest: a lightweight, annotation-free encoding of LaTeX sequences into a forest of position trees (M/L/R identifiers) that provides explicit spatial-structural supervision as an auxiliary training task, rather than requiring the model to implicitly learn LaTeX syntax. The Implicit Attention Correction module further refines CoMER's coverage mechanism by zeroing out attention contributions from structure symbols, addressing an alignment-drift failure mode specific to nested expressions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The position forest's plug-in nature—demonstrated by 7–8% ExpRate gains when attached to RNN-based baselines like DWAP—suggests that explicit structural priors are a broadly transferable inductive bias for sequence-to-structure tasks, not just a transformer-specific trick. The zero-inference-cost design (position forest removed at test time) makes the approach immediately deployable without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the position forest generalizes beyond LaTeX to other markup or structured-output domains, whether the fixed M/L/R vocabulary suffices for arbitrarily deep nesting, and how the method scales to expressions with more than the observed maximum of 3 nested levels.
