## Problem

Existing point cloud understanding methods are either single-domain (trained and tested on one dataset) or single-task, and In-Context Learning approaches like PIC are constrained to a single dataset, limiting cross-domain generalization. No prior work handles multiple domains and multiple tasks simultaneously within a unified model, and test-time domain generalization has not been explored for point cloud ICL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240160/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 1024-point XYZ point cloud (split into 64 patches of 32 points) as the query, paired with a prompt point cloud from the nearest source domain, and produces a reconstructed, denoised, or registered 1024-point output. At test time, pre-computed global and local prototypes for each of the R source domains are also consumed to compute shifting coefficients. The Transformer is fully frozen during inference—no gradient updates or parameter changes occur at test time.

## Method

DG-PIC pre-trains a Transformer-based Masked Point Modeling (MPM) encoder-decoder on multi-domain query-prompt pairs (70% mask ratio), where prompts are sampled from different source domains to reinforce cross-domain correlation. At test time, two dual-level modules operate without any model updates: (1) dual-level source prototype estimation computes per-domain global prototypes (max-pooled) and local prototypes (patch-wise averages) stored from the final training epoch, and (2) dual-level feature shifting applies a macro-level semantic coefficient α (softmax of global distances) and a micro-level positional coefficient β (softmax of per-patch local distances) to pull target patch features toward the weighted combination of source prototypes. The nearest source domain is identified via a λ-balanced global-local distance, and the most similar sample within that domain is selected as the prompt for the frozen Transformer to generate the output.

## Results

On the proposed multi-domain multi-task benchmark (trained on ModelNet40, ShapeNet, ScanNet; tested on ScanObjectNN), DG-PIC achieves Chamfer Distance ×10⁻³ of 4.1 (reconstruction), 15.2 (denoising), and 5.8 (registration), substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, PIC (In-Context Learning on a single compiled dataset), scores 72.9/80.0/12.7 on the same three tasks, while the best task-specific model (PCT) reaches 30.8/35.8/32.5. DG-PIC requires no model updates at test time and handles all three tasks within one unified Transformer-based MPM framework.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DG-PIC decisively outperforms all baselines across all three tasks. Against the closest ICL baseline PIC [10], DG-PIC reduces Chamfer Distance from 72.9 to 4.1 on reconstruction (17.8× improvement), from 80.0 to 15.2 on denoising (5.3×), and from 12.7 to 5.8 on registration (2.2×). Among task-specific models, PCT (30.8/35.8/32.5) and Point-MAE (30.4/36.0/31.2) are the strongest but remain 7–10× worse than DG-PIC on reconstruction and denoising. Train-time DG baselines Pointmixup (40.5/42.3/41.7) and PointCutMix (44.8/43.5/41.3) perform comparably to or worse than plain supervised models, indicating that simple augmentation-based domain mixing is insufficient for the multi-domain multi-task setting. The multi-task variants of conventional backbones (e.g., PointNet multi-task at 41.3 on reconstruction) show that sharing a single head across tasks without domain-aware mechanisms does not close the gap to DG-PIC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: Point In-Context Learning (PIC) for multi-task point cloud understanding, and test-time Domain Generalization (DG) that shifts unseen test features toward source domains without model updates. PIC handles multiple tasks within a single domain but has never been extended to cross-domain scenarios, while existing test-time DG methods (e.g., test-time style shifting) are task-specific and do not support multi-task inference. DG-PIC unifies these by pre-training a PIC-style Transformer under a multi-domain prompt-pairing scheme and then applying a dual-level (global/local, macro/micro) feature shifting mechanism at test time to align target samples with the nearest source domain prototypes, all within one frozen model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first benchmark and unified model for the practically important multi-domain, multi-task point cloud setting, showing that a simple test-time feature alignment toward pre-computed source prototypes can close a large performance gap (e.g., Chamfer Distance 4.1 vs. 72.9 for reconstruction) without any gradient updates. However, the evaluation is limited to a single target domain (ScanObjectNN) and three relatively simple geometric tasks, leaving open whether the dual-level shifting generalizes to more complex tasks (segmentation, detection), larger domain gaps, or higher-resolution point clouds. The reliance on stored source prototypes also raises practical questions about how the method scales when the number of source domains grows or when the target domain is far from all sources.
