## Problem

Existing 3D-aware GANs for full-head synthesis (e.g., PanoHead) suffer from two persistent artifact types: mirroring-face artifacts caused by feature entanglement in Cartesian tri-plane/tri-grid representations, and multiple-face artifacts caused by discriminators that evaluate image quality without enforcing view-image consistency. These artifacts are especially severe in back-view synthesis due to the severe supervision imbalance between abundant frontal and scarce posterior training data.

## Input / Output

The generator consumes a sampled latent code z and a camera extrinsic pose to synthesize 512×512 view-consistent full-head RGB images from any 360° viewpoint, along with an implicit 3D geometry (color and density fields), a super-resolved foreground mask, and a face parsing map. For the single-view inversion task, a single RGB head image is optimized into the latent space via L2 + LPIPS loss followed by PTI, yielding a full 3D head reconstruction. Training operates on a combined 180k-image collection spanning frontal, profile, and back views at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

SphereHead replaces the Cartesian tri-plane with a dual spherical tri-plane representation defined in spherical coordinates (r, θ, φ), where three feature planes P_θr, P_φr, and P_θφ explicitly separate features by head position, eliminating mirroring artifacts at the geometric level. Two orthogonal spheres (A and B) with perpendicular polar axes are fused via a 2D weight map w(θ,φ) = (1+cos(2θ−π))/2 · (1+cos(φ))/2 that tapers to zero at seams and poles, so each sphere compensates the other's discontinuity regions. A view-image consistency (ViCo) loss is added to the discriminator by shuffling real camera labels to form negative real pairs, compelling D to penalize realistic-but-misaligned image-viewpoint combinations rather than scoring purely on pixel quality. A parsing branch renders a face parsing map from the 3D features, providing low-frequency semantic supervision that smooths intra-region features while sharpening inter-region boundaries. Training proceeds in three phases over 25M images on 8 A100 GPUs: near-frontal initialization with alternating sphere training, full-dataset training, and finally ViCo loss integration with re-weighted sphere fusion.

## Results

SphereHead achieves the best FID of 7.8 on the combined WildHead/CelebA/FFHQ/LPFF training set, outperforming PanoHead (8.6), EG3D (10.3), GIRAFFEHD (67.8), and StyleSDF (105.5). In the user study measuring semantic mismatch artifacts, SphereHead achieves a 0% fake-face rate (FF) and 92.6% high-quality rate (HQ), compared to PanoHead's 23.7% FF and 26.1% HQ, and EG3D's 76.1% FF and 3.7% HQ. In single-view GAN inversion, 82.1% of participants preferred SphereHead's reconstruction over PanoHead's across 20 comparative scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SphereHead outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against PanoHead (the closest competitor and first full-head 3D GAN), SphereHead reduces FID from 8.6 to 7.8, eliminates fake-face artifacts entirely (0% vs. 23.7% FF), and raises the high-quality rate from 26.1% to 92.6%. EG3D, while producing reasonable front views, suffers severe mirroring-face artifacts (76.1% FF) and a very low high-quality rate (3.7%), reflecting its inability to handle back-view synthesis. GIRAFFEHD and StyleSDF are excluded from the user study because they completely fail to generate reasonable back-view head images, with FIDs of 67.8 and 105.5 respectively. In the single-view inversion user study, SphereHead is preferred over PanoHead in 82.1% of pairwise comparisons, confirming its advantage in artifact-free 3D head reconstruction from a single input image.

## Relation to Prior Work

SphereHead builds directly on PanoHead, the first 3D-aware GAN for full-head (360°) synthesis, and on EG3D's tri-plane representation as the underlying implicit encoding. It departs from the Cartesian coordinate system used by both PanoHead's tri-grid and EG3D's tri-plane, replacing it with a dual spherical tri-plane that exploits the head's approximate spherical geometry to eliminate feature entanglement. The view-image consistency loss adapts the negative-real-pairs technique from conditional GAN training (Reed et al.) to the 3D-aware setting, where camera parameters serve as the conditioning signal. The publicly released WildHead dataset (180k images) addresses a practical gap left by PanoHead's private back-view collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complete elimination of fake-face artifacts (0% FF vs. 23.7% for PanoHead) and the jump in high-quality rate (92.6% vs. 26.1%) make full-head synthesis substantially more reliable for applications like digital avatars and interactive 3D content. The ViCo loss is a simple, drop-in technique (shuffling camera labels as negative pairs) that the authors argue generalizes to any 3D-aware GAN with imbalanced supervision, broadening its impact beyond head synthesis. Limitations remain: flickering textures, lack of fine high-frequency detail, and persistent data bias toward frontal views. The publicly available WildHead dataset and processing toolbox lower the barrier for the community to build on this line of work.
