## Problem

Existing sophisticated masking strategies for Masked AutoEncoders (adversarial-guided, attention-based, semantic-guided) depend on input data, requiring additional network components and increasing computational cost. Data-independent masking has been scarcely explored beyond simple random masking, leaving open the question of whether MAE performance can be improved without input-dependent mask generation or extra computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240534/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed color noise tensors (3072 patterns of 256×256 spatial dimension, stored in GPU memory) and applies random spatial transformations (crop, horizontal flip, vertical flip) to extract a √P × √P window matching the 196-patch grid of a 224×224 image with 16×16 patches. The output is a binary mask of shape [B, P] where the top 75% values are set to 1 (masked) and the remainder to 0 (visible), which is then used to select which patches the asymmetric ViT encoder processes and the lightweight decoder reconstructs.

## Method

ColorMAE generates four types of data-independent masks by applying frequency-domain filters to random noise: a Gaussian low-pass filter produces Red noise (low-frequency dominant), a high-pass filter (W minus Gaussian-blurred W) produces Blue noise, a band-pass filter (difference of two Gaussian blurs with σ₁ < σ₂) produces Green noise (mid-frequency), and a band-stop filter (W minus Green noise) produces Purple noise. During pre-training, the filtered noise is pre-computed offline and stored in GPU memory; at each step, random spatial transformations yield a patch-sized window, and the top values according to the mask ratio are selected to form the binary mask. The key finding is that Green masking (band-pass filtered noise) provides the best balance between pretext task difficulty and randomness, yielding a 2.72 mIoU improvement over random masking in semantic segmentation without adding any learnable parameters or FLOPs to the MAE architecture.

## Results

ColorMAE-G (band-pass filtered noise masking) achieves 49.2 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation and 83.6% Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K after 800 pre-training epochs, outperforming the MAE random-masking baseline (46.5 mIoU, 83.2%) by +2.7 mIoU and +0.4% Top-1. At 1600 epochs, ColorMAE-G reaches 49.3 mIoU and 83.8% Top-1, maintaining its lead over MAE (48.1 mIoU, 83.6%). On COCO object detection, ColorMAE-G achieves 49.5 AP^bbox and 43.7 AP^mask at 800 epochs, and 50.1 AP^bbox and 44.4 AP^mask at 1600 epochs, with no additional parameters or FLOPs over standard MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct MAE random-masking baseline, ColorMAE-G consistently wins on ADE20K mIoU (+2.7 at 800 epochs, +1.2 at 1600) and ImageNet Top-1 (+0.4 and +0.2 respectively), while slightly trailing on COCO AP^bbox at 1600 epochs (50.1 vs. 50.6). Among data-independent MIM methods, ColorMAE-G surpasses BEiT (45.6 mIoU) and MixedAE (48.7 mIoU) on ADE20K, though MixedAE edges it out on COCO AP^bbox (50.3 vs. 49.5 at 800 epochs). Against data-adaptive masking methods, ColorMAE-G outperforms AttMask (45.3), UM-MAE (42.6), and SemMAE (44.9) on ADE20K mIoU, and is competitive with HPM (48.5 mIoU, 50.1 AP^bbox) while adding zero extra parameters or FLOPs. Among non-MIM baselines, ColorMAE-G beats MoCo v3 (47.2 mIoU) and DINO (47.2 mIoU) on segmentation but trails DropPos (47.8 mIoU, 84.2% Top-1) on ImageNet classification. Overall, the key advantage is that ColorMAE-G achieves the best ADE20K mIoU among all compared methods at both 800 and 1600 epochs without any additional computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-independent masking lineage of MAE (He et al., 2022) by replacing uniform random noise with spectrally-filtered noise patterns drawn from the color-noise literature in image processing. While prior data-adaptive methods (AttMask, SemMAE, HPM, ADIOS) condition mask selection on input content via attention maps or adversarial signals, ColorMAE deliberately stays in the data-independent camp, arguing that structured randomness alone—without any per-image computation—can improve representation quality. The conceptual bridge from signal-processing filters (Gaussian low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, band-stop) to mask generation is the paper's core novelty, repurposing a well-studied DSP concept for self-supervised pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a zero-parameter, zero-FLOP modification to MAE's masking step yields consistent downstream gains (notably +2.72 mIoU on ADE20K segmentation), making it an easy drop-in improvement for practitioners already using MAE. The finding that the mask with the lowest reconstruction loss (Blue) does not produce the best downstream features challenges the intuitive assumption that easier reconstruction implies better representations, reinforcing the idea that an optimal balance between task difficulty and randomness is what matters. Open questions include whether the benefit persists at larger masking ratios, on non-ImageNet pre-training corpora, or with non-ViT backbones, and whether the 2.8% memory overhead from pre-stored noise patterns becomes prohibitive at scale.
