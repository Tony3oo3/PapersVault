## Problem

Despite steady improvements in camouflaged object segmentation (COS) performance, the underlying mechanisms that make camouflage effective remain a black box—no prior work has quantitatively examined how specific camouflage attributes (e.g., environmental pattern matching, shape mimicry) contribute to concealment success. Additionally, existing COS methods rely solely on visual features and overlook the potential of integrating high-level textual scene descriptions to guide segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241471/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes a single RGB camouflage image resized to 336×336 pixels and produces a binary segmentation mask as its primary output. During training, it additionally ingests a textual description (~26 words on average) from the newly introduced COD-TAX dataset, which also provides ground-truth fixation maps and 17-dimensional attribute contribution vectors. The textual branch is entirely omitted at test time, making the deployed model a purely visual pipeline.

## Method

ACUMEN is a dual-branch framework that mirrors hierarchical human perception: a frozen CLIP text encoder processes scene descriptions during training to provide high-level semantic supervision, while a CLIP ViT-L@336 visual encoder extracts multi-scale features (layers 8, 16, 24) for the visual branch. The visual branch contains two auxiliary predictors—a cross-attention decoder that generates a fixation map and a linear projection head that predicts 17 attribute contribution scores—whose outputs are fused into the visual features via an Attributes-Fixation Embedding (AFE) module that applies Squeeze-and-Excitation gating for attribute recalibration and softmax-weighted fixation attention, with depth-weighted multi-scale summation (weights 1:2:4). A transformer decoder and 2D convolution projector then regress the final mask. Training is supervised by a composite loss combining weighted BCE/IoU for the mask, KL+CC for fixation, MSE for attributes, and a novel cosine-similarity consistency loss that aligns projected text and visual features in a shared latent space, ensuring the visual features remain semantically coherent with the textual description without requiring text at inference.

## Results

ACUMEN achieves state-of-the-art performance on CAMO (Sα=0.886, Eφ=0.939, Fβω=0.850, M=0.039) and COD10K (Sα=0.852, Eφ=0.930, Fβω=0.761, M=0.026), outperforming all nine compared methods on every metric. On NC4K, ACUMEN leads in Eφ (0.932) and Fβω (0.826) but trails FSPNet slightly on Sα (0.874 vs. 0.879) and M (0.036 vs. 0.035). Notably, ACUMEN achieves these results using the smallest input resolution (336×336) among all compared methods, which use 352²–512² inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACUMEN decisively outperforms all nine baselines on CAMO, with the largest margins over FSPNet (Sα +3.0%, Fβω +5.1%) and PopNet (Sα +8.0%, Fβω +10.6%). On COD10K, ACUMEN edges out FSPNet by 0.1% in Sα and leads by 2.6% in Fβω, while matching FSPNet's M score of 0.026. On NC4K, ACUMEN is the only method to surpass 0.93 in Eφ and 0.82 in Fβω, beating FSPNet by 1.7% in Fβω, though FSPNet retains a narrow lead in Sα (0.879 vs. 0.874) and M (0.035 vs. 0.036). LSR+² is the closest competitor on Eφ across all three datasets (0.924 on CAMO/COD10K/NC4K vs. ACUMEN's 0.939/0.930/0.932) but does not report Fβω. All baselines use larger input resolutions (352²–512²) than ACUMEN's 336², suggesting further gains are possible at matched resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established Camouflaged Object Segmentation (COS) task by introducing a dual-branch multimodal framework that leverages CLIP's pretrained visual and textual encoders, departing from prior COS methods that rely solely on visual cues (edges, gradients, uncertainty, multi-view inputs). It repurposes fixation prediction from the visual attention literature and the Squeeze-and-Excitation mechanism as auxiliary signals within a segmentation pipeline, while the COD-TAX dataset introduces a novel analytical dimension—quantifying 17 camouflage attributes' contribution to effectiveness—that no prior COS work has attempted. The textual branch is used only during training for consistency supervision, making inference purely visual, which distinguishes it from recent LVLM-based approaches that require language models at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The COD-TAX dataset and the 17-attribute taxonomy provide the first quantitative framework for understanding *why* a camouflage pattern works, shifting COS from a purely performance-driven task toward an interpretable design-and-breaking paradigm. The finding that Surrounding Factors (e.g., Environmental Pattern Matching) dominate in some scenes while Object-Self Factors (e.g., Shape Mimicry) dominate in others offers actionable insights for both camouflage design and detection. Open questions include whether the attribute taxonomy generalizes beyond the 17 hand-defined factors, whether the GPT-4-Vision-generated descriptions introduce systematic biases, and whether the training-time textual supervision strategy transfers to other fine-grained segmentation tasks. The approach's dependence on a frozen CLIP backbone and a relatively small training set (4,040 images) also limits its scalability story.
