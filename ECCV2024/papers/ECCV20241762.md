## Problem

Existing data augmentation methods apply random or fixed magnitudes uniformly to all training samples, ignoring both the inherent difficulty of individual images and the evolving state of the model during training. This uncontrolled randomness can introduce noisy samples, cause distribution shifts, or leave insufficient variation to prevent overfitting, ultimately degrading generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241762/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) paired with class labels during supervised training. A key distinction is that the augmentation magnitude is not a pre-set hyperparameter but is computed on-the-fly per sample from the model's current softmax output, making the input pipeline dynamically coupled to the model's internal state at each training step. The output remains a standard class label prediction.

## Method

EntAugment computes a per-sample augmentation magnitude as mag(x) = 1 + (1/log k)·Σ g(x)_i·log g(x)_i, where g(x) is the softmax output; this yields values in [0,1] that are inversely proportional to the model's prediction entropy, so harder samples (high entropy) receive conservative augmentation while easier samples (low entropy) receive more aggressive variation. The magnitude evolves dynamically throughout training as the model's confidence shifts, implementing an implicit curriculum. To prevent magnitudes from stagnating at low values, EntLoss adds an entropy regularization term (1/log k)·Σ g(x)_i·log g(x)_i to the standard cross-entropy loss, pushing the model toward higher-confidence predictions and thus unlocking larger augmentation magnitudes in later training. A theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) proves that minimizing CE + EntLoss yields a model distribution with strictly lower KL divergence from the empirical data distribution than CE alone, providing a formal guarantee of improved distributional alignment.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, EntAugment+EntLoss (EA+EL) achieves 97.70% top-1 accuracy with WRN-28-10 and 97.55% with Shake-Shake-26-32, surpassing the best prior DA method (TrivialAugment at 97.18% WRN) by ~0.5%. On CIFAR-100, EA+EL reaches 83.16% (WRN) and 82.49% (R-50), exceeding the strongest baseline (MADAug at 83.01% WRN) by ~0.15–1.0% depending on architecture. On ImageNet (ResNet-50), EA+EL attains 78.3% top-1 accuracy, a 1.3% improvement over the ~77.0% baseline, though slightly below AdvMask's 78.5%. All gains are achieved with negligible additional computational cost (O(k×N) overhead, equivalent to vanilla CE loss).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 16 DA baselines including Cutout, CutMix, AutoAugment, RandAugment, TrivialAugment, MADAug, SoftAug, and AdvMask. On CIFAR-10, EA+EL outperforms all baselines across every architecture tested; the closest competitor is TrivialAugment (97.18% WRN vs. 97.70% for EA+EL, a 0.52% gap). On CIFAR-100, the advantage is more pronounced: EA+EL surpasses MADAug (83.01% WRN) by 0.15% and RandAugment (82.90% WRN) by 0.26%, with larger margins on smaller networks (e.g., +1.0% over baseline on R-18). On ImageNet (ResNet-50), EA+EL (78.3%) is slightly below AdvMask (78.5%) but exceeds all other methods including TrivialAugment (77.9%) and MADAug (not listed in Table 2 but reported at 78.5% in Table 1 context). Notably, EA+EL achieves these gains with the lowest training time among all compared methods (comparable to CutMix and TrivialAugment in wall-clock time, while methods like AdvMask, AutoAugment, and TeachAugment incur significantly higher overhead). The EntLoss component alone (without EntAugment) also improves over vanilla CE loss by 0.13–2.88% across architectures on CIFAR-10/100, confirming its independent utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the augmentation operation spaces established by AutoAugment, TrivialAugment, and SelectAugment, but departs from their core assumption that augmentation magnitudes should be randomly sampled or fixed. Instead, it introduces an entropy-driven mechanism that dynamically scales each sample's augmentation strength based on the model's current classification confidence, effectively coupling DA to curriculum learning principles. The EntLoss component is a novel entropy-regularized term added to cross-entropy, distinguished from prior entropy regularization in RL (which maximizes entropy for exploration) by minimizing entropy to sharpen predictions, with a KL-divergence-based theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) that the combined loss reduces model-to-dataset distributional dissimilarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tuning-free, zero-overhead nature of EntAugment and EntLoss makes them practical plug-and-play additions for standard CNN training pipelines, and the consistent gains across CIFAR, ImageNet, and long-tailed benchmarks (ImageNet-LT, Places-LT) suggest broad applicability. However, the improvements are modest (≈0.5–1.2% over strong baselines), the methods are validated only on supervised image classification with CNNs, and the authors themselves flag extension to self-supervised learning and object detection as future work. An open question is whether the entropy-based difficulty signal remains informative in regimes where the model is already highly confident (e.g., very large models on easy datasets), potentially limiting the adaptive range of magnitudes.
