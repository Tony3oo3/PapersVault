## Problem

Existing scanpath prediction models can predict where and when humans shift their gaze but provide no explanation for what each fixation represents or why it was made. This lack of explainability limits interpretability and hinders real-world applications where understanding the rationale behind visual attention is essential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 384×512) and a free-form text instruction encoding the task context (e.g., 'What do you see in the image?' for free-viewing, 'Is there a [target] in the image?' for search, optionally augmented with the observer's behavioral answer). It outputs a sampled scanpath of K' fixations with positions and log-normal durations, plus a 5–20 word natural language explanation for each fixation, generated sequentially in scanpath order.

## Method

GazeXplain builds on a general vision-language encoder (RoBERTa + visual backbone) and introduces an attention-language decoder: a transformer attention module predicts spatiotemporal fixation distributions via cross-attention with joint vision-language embeddings, while a language sub-decoder extracts local image features at each predicted fixation, fuses them with the task's semantic embedding through learnable projections, and feeds the combined representation into a pre-trained BLIP captioner to generate per-fixation explanations. A novel semantic alignment loss enforces consistency by computing pairwise cosine similarities among visual features (frozen ResNet), explanation embeddings, fixation features, and cross-modal features across all fixation pairs, using the visual similarity as pseudo-labels to supervise the other three. Cross-dataset co-training standardizes all three eye-tracking datasets (AiR-D, OSIE, COCO-Search18) into a unified VQA instruction format and trains a single model jointly, improving generalizability beyond single-task training. The total training objective combines the scanpath prediction loss, a language generation loss, and the semantic alignment loss.

## Results

GazeXplain achieves the best scanpath prediction performance across all four dataset splits, with ScanMatch scores of 0.386 (AiR-D), 0.380 (OSIE), 0.480 (COCO-Search18 TP), and 0.373 (COCO-Search18 TA), consistently outperforming the strongest baseline ChenLSTM (0.350, 0.377, 0.448, 0.366 respectively). On saliency metrics, GazeXplain leads in CC (e.g., 0.662 on AiR-D vs. 0.629 for ChenLSTM), NSS (1.851 vs. 1.727), and sAUC (0.719 vs. 0.702) on AiR-D. The model also generates natural-language explanations per fixation, achieving CIDEr-R of 123.1 on AiR-D, 48.6 on OSIE, 139.6 on COCO-Search18 TP, and 106.5 on COCO-Search18 TA, substantially exceeding the off-the-shelf BLIP captioner baseline (61.9, 23.6, 111.2, 77.0 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GazeXplain outperforms all five baselines (SaltiNet, PathGAN, IOR-ROI, ChenLSTM, Gazeformer) on nearly every metric across all four dataset splits. The closest competitor is ChenLSTM, which GazeXplain surpasses by 0.036 in ScanMatch on AiR-D (0.386 vs. 0.350), 0.032 on COCO-Search18 TP (0.480 vs. 0.448), and 0.007 on OSIE (0.380 vs. 0.377). Gazeformer, the most recent prior work, is consistently outperformed by GazeXplain by 0.029–0.047 in ScanMatch and by 0.04–0.09 in sAUC across datasets. The sole exception is SED on COCO-Search18 TP, where ChenLSTM achieves a marginally lower (better) value of 1.932 versus GazeXplain's 1.981. Older baselines (SaltiNet, PathGAN, IOR-ROI) are substantially outperformed, with ScanMatch gaps exceeding 0.15 on most datasets, confirming that the attention-language decoder, semantic alignment, and cross-dataset co-training collectively yield meaningful gains over prior scanpath prediction approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

GazeXplain builds directly on Gazeformer's (CVPR 2023) transformer-based attention decoder for predicting fixation spatiotemporal distributions and on ChenLSTM's (CVPR 2021) Monte Carlo sampling for fixation positions and durations. It departs from this lineage by appending a language decoder (BLIP-based) that generates per-fixation natural language explanations, and by introducing a semantic alignment loss that uses frozen ResNet features as pseudo-labels to enforce consistency between fixation features, explanation embeddings, and visual features. The cross-dataset co-training strategy also distinguishes it from prior scanpath models (e.g., ChenLSTM on AiR-D, Gazeformer on COCO-Search18) that are each tied to a single task-specific dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first fixation-level natural language annotations on eye-tracking data (86,407 fixations across three datasets), creating a reusable resource for explainable attention research. The ablation finding that generating explanations improves scanpath prediction accuracy (SM 0.356→0.386) suggests a mutual-information benefit between the two tasks, though the magnitude is moderate. Open questions include whether the LLaVA-generated ground-truth explanations introduce systematic biases that the model merely learns to replicate, whether the approach generalizes to longer or more cognitively complex scanpaths, and whether the semantic alignment mechanism scales to higher-resolution or video-based gaze data.
