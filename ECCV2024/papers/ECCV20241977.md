## Problem

Existing reference segmentation tasks each rely on a single cue modality—VOS uses a first-frame mask, R-VOS uses text descriptions, and AVS uses audio—yet none can simultaneously exploit multimodal cues (audio, visual, and temporal) to segment a specific object of interest in dynamic audio-visual scenes. This paper introduces Ref-AVS, a task that requires the model to interpret natural-language expressions enriched with audio, visual, and temporal attributes to produce pixel-level segmentation of the referred object.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10-second video clips sampled at 1 fps (frames resized to 384×384), a mono-channel audio waveform encoded offline via VGGish into 128-dim per-second features, and a natural-language expression (up to 25 tokens) that may reference sound volume/rhythm, object appearance/spatial position, and temporal ordering. The output is a binary pixel-level mask per 1-second snippet identifying the single referred object; in the null-reference setting, the correct output is an empty mask, and performance is additionally measured by an S metric that penalizes spurious segmentation.

## Method

EEMC (Expression Enhancing with Multimodal Cues) builds on Mask2Former as the segmentation decoder, with frozen Swin-base, VGGish, and RoBERTa encoders for visual, audio, and text features respectively. A Temporal Bi-modal Transformer first fuses each modality with the text expression via self-attention, then a cached-memory mechanism amplifies temporal deviations by subtracting the running mean of past features (scaled by hyper-parameter β), making the representation sensitive to dynamic changes. Modality-identify tokens [aud] and [vis] are inserted to segment the concatenated sequence, and a Multimodal Integration Transformer applies self-attention over the full multimodal cue sequence. Finally, cross-attention uses the integrated multimodal cues as key/value to update 100 learnable mask queries, which are passed to the Mask2Former decoder. The key design choice is to use text as the semantic carrier of multimodal information rather than directly fusing raw audio-visual features, yielding more robust and generalizable guidance—particularly on unseen categories and null-reference cases.

## Results

On the Ref-AVS Bench, EEMC achieves 34.20% Jaccard and 0.513 F-score on the Seen test set, 49.54% Jaccard and 0.648 F-score on the Unseen test set, and 41.87% Jaccard and 0.581 F-score on the mixed (Seen+Unseen) set. On the Null reference test set, EEMC achieves an S-score of 0.007, indicating near-perfect suppression of false segmentation when the expression does not match any object. These results substantially outperform all five baselines across every test subset and metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEMC outperforms all five baselines on every test subset and metric. On the Seen set, EEMC (34.20% J) beats the strongest baseline AVSegFormer (33.47% J) by 0.73 points in Jaccard and 0.043 in F-score, while the other AVS baselines (GAVS 28.93%, AVSBench 23.20%) and Ref-VOS baselines (ReferFormer 31.31%, R2VOS 25.01%) trail further behind. The gap widens dramatically on the Unseen set, where EEMC reaches 49.54% Jaccard versus AVSegFormer's 36.05% (a 13.5-point margin), demonstrating superior generalization to novel object categories. On the Null reference set, EEMC's S-score of 0.007 is an order of magnitude better than the best baseline (AVSegFormer at 0.171), confirming that EEMC correctly refrains from segmenting when no object matches the expression. The Ref-VOS baselines (ReferFormer, R2VOS) are particularly weak on the Unseen set (30.40% and 27.93% J respectively), highlighting that text-only referring cannot adequately handle multimodal audio-visual scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining Ref-AVS as a task that simultaneously leverages audio, visual, and textual cues for referring segmentation, whereas prior work handles only pairs of modalities: R-VOS (text+visual) and AVS (audio+visual) each cover two of the three channels. The EEMC method repurposes standard components—Mask2Former as the segmentation decoder, Swin Transformer for visual encoding, RoBERTa for text, and VGGish for audio—into a unified pipeline with a cross-attention prompting mechanism and a cached-memory module for temporal dynamics. Conceptually, the paper departs from the AVS line of work (which segments all sounding objects indiscriminately) and the R-VOS line (which relies solely on textual descriptions) by treating the text expression as a semantic carrier that is enriched with audio-visual context rather than fusing raw modalities directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ref-AVS Bench is the first pixel-level benchmark for multimodal-cue referring segmentation, filling a clear gap between R-VOS and AVS for real-world scenes where sound, appearance, and language jointly identify a target. The inclusion of a null-reference test subset is a useful evaluation dimension that probes whether a model truly follows the expression or merely segments salient/sounding objects. However, the dataset (≈4,000 videos, 20K expressions) is modest in scale, and the single proposed method (EEMC) leaves open whether the architectural choices—particularly the cached-memory mechanism and the text-as-carrier design—generalize to larger, more diverse corpora or to foundation-model-scale training. Scaling the benchmark and exploring whether the unseen-category advantage (49.5% J vs. 34.2% J on seen) reflects genuine generalization or dataset-imbalance artifacts are natural next steps.
