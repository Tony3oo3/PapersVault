## Problem

Existing floorplan reconstruction methods rely on corner regression or box regression, which either lack robustness to noisy or missing input signals (corner-based) or cannot represent non-Manhattan room shapes (box-based). Neither approach directly models the global geometric shape of individual rooms, leading to irregular or incomplete polygon outputs when input quality degrades.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240834/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud as input, which is preprocessed into a 256×256 image combining a density map (projected point count) and a wall height map (extracted via normal-based segmentation and RANSAC plane fitting). The output is a vectorized floorplan where each room is defined not by an explicit corner sequence but by a set of line parameters (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) and a binary selection matrix that groups lines into convex primitives, whose union forms the room polygon.

## Method

FRI-Net represents each room as a neural implicit occupancy function: given query point coordinates, it outputs whether each point lies inside or outside the room. Inspired by BSP-Net, the room-wise decoder predicts three types of 2D lines (horizontal, vertical, diagonal) via separate MLPs, groups them into convex primitives using a learned binary selection matrix T, and assembles the final shape via min-pooling (union) across primitives. A DETR-based encoder (ResNet-50 backbone, 6 encoder + 7 decoder transformer layers, 20 learnable room queries) extracts per-room feature codes from the input image. Training follows a three-stage structural regularization strategy: first optimizing only axis-aligned lines to establish basic room geometry, then jointly optimizing all line types, and finally switching to a discrete occupancy loss (S*) with threshold-based discretization of T to sharpen the reconstruction. Bipartite matching via the Hungarian algorithm establishes correspondence between predicted and ground-truth rooms during training.

## Results

On the Structured3D test set, FRI-Net achieves 99.1% Room F1, 87.8% Corner F1, and 86.9% Angle F1, outperforming the previous best (RoomFormer) by +1.8, +0.6, and +5.7 F1 points respectively. With PolyDiffuse post-optimization, FRI-Net+PD reaches 99.1% Room F1, 91.1% Corner F1, and 89.2% Angle F1, surpassing RoomFormer+PD on all three metrics. On SceneCAD, FRI-Net attains 89.2% Corner F1 and 75.9% Angle F1, the best among all compared methods. In cross-data generalization (trained on Structured3D, tested on SceneCAD), FRI-Net achieves 72.4% Corner F1 and 61.3% Angle F1, substantially outperforming RoomFormer (60.3% / 46.2%) and HEAT (51.0% / 41.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRI-Net outperforms all six baselines on the Structured3D test set across Room, Corner, and Angle F1 scores. The most notable gain is in Angle F1 (+5.7 over RoomFormer, +2.5 over SLIBO-Net), reflecting the benefit of the room-wise implicit representation for capturing angular geometry. Against the strongest baseline RoomFormer, FRI-Net leads by +1.8 Room F1 and +0.6 Corner F1 while running at 9× the inference time (0.09 s vs 0.01 s). With PolyDiffuse post-optimization, FRI-Net+PD edges out RoomFormer+PD on all three metrics (99.1 vs 98.4 Room F1, 91.1 vs 91.0 Corner F1, 89.2 vs 89.1 Angle F1). On SceneCAD, FRI-Net achieves the best Corner F1 (89.2) and Angle F1 (75.9), narrowly beating RoomFormer (88.8 / 75.8) and clearly surpassing HEAT (82.5 / 70.4) and Floor-SP (87.6 / 73.1). In the cross-data generalization experiment, FRI-Net's global-shape learning strategy proves markedly more robust than corner-based methods, yielding +12.1 Corner F1 and +15.1 Angle F1 over RoomFormer when transferred from Structured3D to SceneCAD.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRI-Net adapts BSP-Net's line-based implicit representation (convex primitives formed by intersecting half-planes) to the floorplan domain, where each room is characterized by an occupancy field defined through predicted wall lines. It departs from the dominant corner-regression paradigm (HEAT, Floor-Net, RoomFormer) and the box-slicing approach (SLIBO-Net) by directly learning a continuous inside/outside function per room, which inherently enforces closed polygon boundaries. The encoder follows the DETR-style transformer architecture established by RoomFormer and SLIBO-Net, while the multi-stage training strategy (axis-aligned lines first, then diagonal) is a novel structural regularization specific to floorplan geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The room-wise implicit representation addresses a concrete weakness of corner-based methods—fragility to missing or noisy corner detections—by allowing the network to complete room shapes from partial evidence, and it handles non-Manhattan geometries that box-based methods cannot represent. However, the method struggles with fine inner-wall structures due to over-smoothing inherent in the implicit formulation, and it remains limited to 2D reconstruction. Open questions include whether combining explicit and implicit representations can recover high-frequency local details, and whether the height-map pre-processing can be leveraged for full 3D floorplan reconstruction.
