## Problem

Existing compositional temporal grounding methods construct negative queries via random primitive recombination, producing semantically implausible combinations (e.g., 'eating the table') and ignoring non-dominant primitives like prepositions and adverbs. Additionally, DETR-based temporal grounding models exhibit irrational saliency responses to hard negative queries with only subtle word substitutions, failing to capture the nuanced semantic differences needed for compositional generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video and a natural language query sentence as input, and outputs start/end timestamps for the relevant moment along with per-clip saliency scores temporally aligned with localization boundaries. During training, each video is paired with a set of five queries: the positive query, three LLM-generated hierarchical hard negatives (progressively more different), and one random negative sampled from the same mini-batch, all processed jointly through the DETR encoder to produce five distinct saliency score sequences.

## Method

SHINE introduces two key components integrated into DETR-based backbones (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR). First, an LLM-driven hierarchical hard negative construction pipeline uses spaCy POS tagging to progressively mask primitives in order of linguistic importance (verb→noun→adjective→preposition→adverb) at increasing ratios, then queries GPT-3.5 Turbo to fill masks with semantically plausible replacements drawn from a training-set vocabulary dictionary. Second, a coarse-to-fine saliency ranking loss enforces multi-granularity semantic relationships: the coarse-grained loss (L_cr) imposes dual constraints ensuring saliency peaks inside the ground-truth interval and that positive-query saliency exceeds negative-query saliency, while the fine-grained loss (L_fr) uses negative log-likelihood distance to enforce a strict hierarchical divergence in saliency score distributions across the four negative levels, requiring each successive negative to be progressively less similar to the positive. The total training objective is L = L_base + αL_cr + βL_fr, where L_base includes the standard DETR bipartite matching, localization, and saliency losses.

## Results

On Charades-CG, QD-DETR+SHINE achieves 60.66% R1@0.5, 38.60% R1@0.7, and 52.53% mIoU on Test-Trivial, and 50.23% R1@0.5, 27.69% R1@0.7, and 44.14% mIoU on Novel-Composition, outperforming all prior methods across all three test splits. The method yields the largest gains on the Novel-Composition split, improving QD-DETR by +7.93% R1@0.5 and +6.60% R1@0.7, and Moment-DETR by +5.23% R1@0.5. On ActivityNet-CG, QD-DETR+SHINE reaches 29.56% R1@0.5 and 14.37% R1@0.7 on Novel-Composition, surpassing VISA (which relies on external detectors) by 8.05% R1@0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHINE (integrated with QD-DETR) outperforms all 12 compared baselines on every metric across all three Charades-CG test splits. The most direct comparison is with QD-DETR [31] itself: SHINE adds +7.93% R1@0.5 and +6.60% R1@0.7 on Novel-Composition, confirming that the coarse-to-fine saliency ranking loss is the primary driver of improvement. Against the strongest proposal-free competitor Deco [46], QD-DETR+SHINE leads by +2.84% R1@0.5 and +6.63% R1@0.7 on Novel-Composition, while also reporting Novel-Word results that Deco omits. VISA [22] remains competitive on Novel-Composition R1@0.5 (45.41 vs. 50.23) but relies on external object detectors and action recognizers, whereas SHINE operates end-to-end. Among proposal-based methods, MS-2D-TAN+SSL [20] is the closest on Test-Trivial (58.14 vs. 60.66 R1@0.5) but trails by 3.69% on Novel-Composition R1@0.5. On ActivityNet-CG, QD-DETR+SHINE (29.56% R1@0.5) surpasses VISA (31.51%) only marginally on Novel-Composition R1@0.5 but leads on R1@0.7 (14.37 vs. 16.73) and mIoU (32.44 vs. 35.85), while Moment-DETR+SHINE improves over its baseline by +1.31% R1@0.5 on Novel-Composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional temporal grounding line initiated by VISA (Li et al., 2022) and refined by SSL2CG and Deco, which all rely on decompose-reconstruct strategies with random primitive substitution to build negative queries. It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) replacing random sampling with LLM-driven (GPT-3.5 Turbo) generation of semantically plausible hard negatives, and (2) extending primitive coverage beyond dominant verbs/nouns to include prepositions and adverbs. Crucially, it is the first work to connect compositional generalization to the saliency scores in DETR-based architectures (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR), which prior compositional grounding work had not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap by showing that DETR-based temporal grounding models—the current architectural SOTA—exhibit irrational saliency responses to hard negatives, and provides a plug-and-play loss (coarse-to-fine saliency ranking) that corrects this without architectural changes. The LLM-driven negative construction is a practical, reusable technique that could transfer to other video-text tasks. Limitations include dependence on a specific LLM (GPT-3.5 Turbo) for data augmentation, evaluation on only two datasets, and more modest gains on ActivityNet-CG where longer queries reduce the impact of primitive substitution. Open questions include whether the hierarchical ranking generalizes to more than three negative levels, whether smaller or open-source LLMs suffice, and whether the approach extends to longer-form video grounding.
