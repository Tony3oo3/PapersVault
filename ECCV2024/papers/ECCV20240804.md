## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud adversarial attacks rely on global distance constraints (Chamfer/Hausdorff) or global spectral transforms (Graph Fourier Transform) that perturb all points uniformly, failing to preserve the distinct local geometric structures—such as smoothness in flat regions versus sharpness in curved regions. This uniform perturbation disrupts point-to-point structural dependencies and produces perceptible noise, limiting the imperceptibility of generated adversarial examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240804/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud uniformly sampled to 1,024 points (from ModelNet40 or ShapeNetPart test sets, restricted to correctly classified objects) and produces an adversarial point cloud where perturbations are applied to only a subset of points (default 50%, controlled by parameter k). The perturbation vector is structured directionally: for high-curvature patches, noise is projected along per-point normal vectors; for low-curvature patches, noise is projected along tangent plane directions, ensuring the perturbation aligns with local geometry rather than being applied in arbitrary 3D directions.

## Method

WPA first constructs a K-NN graph (K=10) over the point cloud and applies the Spectral Graph Wavelet Transform with a B-spline kernel at scale s=2, yielding a sparse wavelet coefficient matrix where each row encodes the local geometric context around one point. An Intrinsic Shape Signature (ISS) subsampling algorithm then selects critical feature points, and the wavelet coefficients are thresholded (ε=10⁻⁵) to carve out curvature-consistent patches centered at those points. Patches are ranked by curvature magnitude, and the k highest-curvature patches (sharp regions) and k lowest-curvature patches (smooth regions) are selected for attack. Two attack variants are defined: WPA^hc perturbs points in high-curvature patches along their normal vectors, while WPA^lc perturbs points in low-curvature patches along tangent plane directions, with diagonal mask matrices zeroing out all non-selected points. The final perturbation is optimized via the C&W framework (binary search on α, 200 Adam iterations) with a combined Chamfer + Hausdorff regularization loss, ensuring the adversarial sample remains geometrically faithful to the original shape.

## Results

WPA achieves 100% attack success rate (ASR) across all four victim models (PointNet, DGCNN, PointConv, CurveNet) on both ModelNet40 and ShapeNetPart. On ModelNet40, WPA^lc attains the lowest Hausdorff distance (HD: 0.0020–0.0047) and near-lowest Chamfer distance (CD: 0.0004–0.0010) among all methods. On ShapeNetPart, WPA^lc achieves the best CD (0.0006–0.0019) and HD (0.0234–0.0353) across all four models, while maintaining 100% ASR where SI-Adv and GSDA drop to 91–98%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WPA^lc achieves 100% ASR on all four models for both datasets while producing the lowest HD on every model–dataset combination, outperforming PGD (HD 0.0129–0.0628), AdvPC (0.0127–0.0649), GeoA (0.0062–0.0457), and GSDA (0.0142–0.1809) by large margins. On CD, SI-Adv is the closest competitor, achieving slightly lower values on ModelNet40 (e.g., 0.0002 vs. 0.0004 on PointNet) and ShapeNetPart (e.g., 0.0008 vs. 0.0019 on PointConv), but WPA^lc consistently delivers 2–5× lower HD than SI-Adv (e.g., 0.0047 vs. 0.0199 on CurveNet/ModelNet40). WPA also maintains 100% ASR where SI-Adv drops to 91–96% and GSDA to 94–98% on ShapeNetPart. Against the spectral-domain baselines GSDA and GSDA++, WPA reduces HD by 30–97% (e.g., 0.0043 vs. 0.1401 on DGCNN/ModelNet40) while matching their 100% ASR. In defense-robustness experiments (Table 2), WPA^hc retains 98.9% ASR under SOR and 98.1% under DUP-Net, substantially outperforming PGD (52.3%/49.5%) and GeoA (62.5%/59.2%), confirming that curvature-aligned perturbations resist geometric-restoration defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

WPA extends the line of spectral-domain adversarial attacks on 3D point clouds (GSDA, GSDA++, ITA) by replacing the global Graph Fourier Transform with the Spectral Graph Wavelet Transform, whose inherent locality enables patch-level geometric decomposition rather than whole-point perturbation. It also draws on the C&W optimization framework for the adversarial objective and the Intrinsic Shape Signature (ISS) algorithm from classical point cloud processing to select critical points for patch carving. The key conceptual departure from prior spectral attacks is that wavelet coefficients are localized to neighborhoods, allowing the method to identify curvature-consistent patches and apply direction-specific perturbations (normal for sharp regions, tangent for smooth regions) to only a subset of points, whereas GFT-based methods necessarily modify all points simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WPA demonstrates that perturbing only ~50% of points in geometrically sensitive patches can match or exceed the attack success rate of methods that perturb all points, while achieving lower Chamfer and Hausdorff distances and notably better robustness against defenses like SOR and DUP-Net (96–99% ASR vs. 50–68% for baselines). The use of classical signal-processing tools (wavelets, ISS) rather than learned point-selection networks offers interpretability and avoids additional training overhead. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D segmentation or detection tasks, how it scales to much larger point clouds (e.g., LiDAR scenes with millions of points), and whether the curvature-based patch selection generalizes to non-manifold or noisy real-world scans.
