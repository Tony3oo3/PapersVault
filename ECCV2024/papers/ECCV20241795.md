## Problem

Existing data-free quantization (DFQ) methods for ViTs, notably PSAQ-ViT v1/v2, rely on a global patch-similarity metric that treats all patches equally without spatial sensitivity, producing simplistic and semantically vague synthetic calibration data. These methods also ignore the informativeness of generated samples for the quantization process, support only fixed-precision quantization, and suffer from non-smooth loss landscapes that degrade parameter search and test-set generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241795/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pre-trained full-precision ViT, a batch of 32 random Gaussian noise images, and task-specific targets (randomly assigned class probabilities for classification, random bounding-box coordinates for detection, or random pixel-wise class maps for segmentation)—no real calibration data is needed. It outputs a quantized ViT with per-layer bit-widths (integers 2–8) and scale factors, supporting both fixed-precision (e.g., W4/A8) and mixed-precision (e.g., MP4.7/MP5.9) configurations, with activation bit-widths derived from weight bit-widths via b_act = min(8, b×2).

## Method

CLAMP-ViT is a two-stage cyclically adaptive framework. In Stage 1 (data generation), a patch-level contrastive loss operates on MHSA outputs: for each anchor patch in the quantized model, the top-4 most cosine-similar patches in a 3×3 spatial neighborhood (from the FP model) serve as positives and the rest as negatives, driving Gaussian noise toward semantically coherent images via a modified infoNCE loss combined with an MAE output loss. In Stage 2 (quantization), a layer-wise evolutionary search evolves per-layer bit-widths and scale factors using a local contrastive loss on mean-pooled intermediate activations as the fitness function, which captures distributional divergence between FP and quantized layers and yields a smooth loss landscape. The two stages alternate cyclically—data is regenerated every C/2 quantization iterations using the current quantized model as the anchor—ensuring the synthetic data remains informative to the evolving quantization parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification at W4/A8, CLAMP-ViT achieves 77.03% top-1 on DeiT-S, 80.28% on Swin-T, and 82.51% on Swin-S, outperforming the best data-free baseline (PSAQ-ViT v2) by up to 3.65% (Swin-S). On COCO 2017 object detection at W4/A8, CLAMP-ViT reaches 48.5 AP_box with Swin-S, surpassing PSAQ-ViT v2 by 0.6 mAP. On ADE20K segmentation, CLAMP-ViT achieves 42.4 mIoU (DeiT-S) and 45.9 mIoU (Swin-S) with mixed-precision, improving over PSAQ-ViT v2 by 1.5 mIoU on average at a higher compression ratio (~15%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAMP-ViT consistently outperforms both data-free baselines (PSAQ-ViT v1 and v2) across all tasks and precision levels, with the largest gaps at W4/A8 (up to 3.65% on Swin-S classification and 0.6 mAP on detection). Against data-driven PTQ methods, CLAMP-ViT matches or exceeds FQ-ViT and RepQ-ViT on most models at W4/A8 (e.g., 77.03 vs 76.93 on DeiT-S, 82.51 vs 82.14 on Swin-S), though it trails FQ-ViT slightly on Swin-T (80.28 vs 80.73). In the mixed-precision setting (Table 2), CLAMP-ViT beats both LRP-QViT and VT-PTQ on all four models, achieving 71.69% on DeiT-T vs 71.03% (LRP-QViT) and 69.46% (VT-PTQ), while using a lower average bit-width (MP4.9/MP6.2 vs MP6/MP6). For object detection, CLAMP-ViT matches RepQ-ViT at W8/A8 (47.9 AP_box on DeiT-S) and leads PSAQ-ViT v2 by 0.6 AP_box at W4/A8. On segmentation, CLAMP-ViT's mixed-precision approach (MP4.8/MP6.2) yields 42.4 mIoU on DeiT-S versus 39.9 for PSAQ-ViT v2 at fixed W4/A8, demonstrating that adaptive bit-width allocation recovers accuracy at higher compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAMP-ViT extends the data-free post-training quantization (DFQ) line initiated by PSAQ-ViT v1 and v2, which used a patch-similarity entropy metric to refine Gaussian noise into synthetic calibration data for ViTs. It departs from this by replacing the global patch-similarity objective with a patch-level contrastive learning scheme that explicitly models spatially-local positive/negative patch relationships, and by introducing a cyclic adaptation loop between data generation and quantization. For the quantization search itself, it draws on Evol-Q's evolutionary search and contrastive-loss ideas but substitutes a local (per-layer) contrastive objective for Evol-Q's global one, arguing the latter causes premature convergence when starting from a full-precision model. The paper is also the first to unify fixed- and mixed-precision DFQ for ViTs within a single framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any real calibration data while supporting mixed-precision quantization, CLAMP-ViT addresses a practical privacy bottleneck in deploying ViTs at the edge, and the consistent 1–3% accuracy gains over prior DFQ methods at aggressive bit-widths (W4/A8) make it a more viable drop-in for resource-constrained settings. The loss-landscape analysis and ablations (local vs. global contrastive loss, adaptivity on/off, neighborhood size) provide useful diagnostic guidance for future DFQ designs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT-family backbones (the authors note VLMs as future work), how it behaves at even lower bit-widths (2-bit), and whether the synthetic-data generation could inadvertently encode biases or enable misuse (e.g., deepfakes), which the authors briefly acknowledge but do not investigate.
