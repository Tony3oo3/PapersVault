## Problem

Existing 3D semantic occupancy prediction methods rely on dense grid-based representations (voxels, BEV, TPV) that allocate equal computation and storage to every 3D location, ignoring the sparsity of driving scenes and the wide diversity of object scales. This leads to substantial resource waste on empty regions and insufficient detail on complex foreground objects, making it difficult to achieve both efficiency and fine-grained geometric accuracy simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240720/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (6 surrounding cameras at 900×1600 for nuScenes, or a single front camera at 376×1408 for KITTI-360) along with their intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid (200×200×16 with 18 classes on nuScenes; 256×256×32 with 19 classes on KITTI-360). Unusually, the dense grid is not predicted directly but is synthesized from a sparse set of 144,000 (nuScenes) or 38,400 (KITTI-360) 3D semantic Gaussians via a local-aggregation splatting operation, meaning the intermediate scene representation is object-centric rather than grid-centric.

## Method

GaussianFormer represents each scene with P sparse 3D semantic Gaussians, each parameterized by a 3D mean, scale, rotation quaternion, and semantic logits, leveraging the universal approximating ability of Gaussian mixtures to capture fine-grained structure with far fewer parameters than a dense grid. The model iteratively refines these Gaussians through B=4 transformer blocks, each comprising: (1) a 3D sparse convolution over voxelized Gaussian means to model inter-Gaussian interactions, (2) an image cross-attention module that projects covariance-aware 3D reference points onto multi-scale FPN features via deformable attention, and (3) a refinement MLP that updates the mean residually while directly substituting scale, rotation, and semantics to avoid vanishing gradients. A custom CUDA-implemented Gaussian-to-voxel splatting module then converts the learned Gaussians into the final occupancy grid by sorting Gaussian-voxel index pairs and summing only neighboring Gaussian contributions per voxel, reducing complexity from O(XYZ×P) to a local operation. Training is end-to-end with cross-entropy and Lovász-softmax losses applied at every refinement block (deep supervision), and the entire pipeline reduces peak memory by 75–82% compared to grid-based baselines while achieving comparable mIoU.

## Results

On nuScenes (multi-view), GaussianFormer achieves 29.83 SC IoU and 19.10 SSC mIoU, performing on par with OccFormer (31.39 / 19.03) and slightly below SurroundOcc (31.49 / 20.30), while clearly surpassing planar-representation methods like TPVFormer* (30.86 / 17.10) and BEVFormer (30.50 / 16.75). On KITTI-360 (monocular), it reaches 35.38 SC IoU and 12.92 SSC mIoU, trailing the strongest baseline Symphonies (44.12 / 18.58) but exceeding TPVFormer (40.22 / 13.64) on mIoU. The headline efficiency gain is a 75.2%–82.2% reduction in memory consumption (6,229 MB vs. 25,100–35,000 MB for competitors) at a comparable latency of 372 ms on a single NVIDIA 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, GaussianFormer (29.83 SC IoU / 19.10 mIoU) is within ~1.7 SC IoU of the best grid-based methods SurroundOcc (31.49) and OccFormer (31.39), and slightly below TPVFormer* (30.86) on SC IoU but ahead on mIoU (19.10 vs. 17.10). It clearly outperforms BEVFormer (30.50 / 16.75) on mIoU by 2.35 points. On KITTI-360, GaussianFormer (35.38 / 12.92) lags behind Symphonies (44.12 / 18.58) and OccFormer (40.27 / 13.81) on both metrics, though it edges out TPVFormer (40.22 / 13.64) on mIoU. The most striking comparison is efficiency: GaussianFormer uses only 6,229 MB of memory versus 25,100 MB (BEVFormer), 29,000 MB (TPVFormer), and 35,000 MB (PanoOcc), a 75–82% reduction, while maintaining a competitive 372 ms latency that is faster than all dense-grid methods (463–502 ms) and only ~70 ms slower than planar methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) representation from the radiance field rendering community (Kerbl et al., 2023) as an object-centric scene representation for 3D semantic occupancy prediction, a task previously dominated by grid-based formulations (voxels, BEV, TPV). It departs from the dense-grid paradigm by treating each 3D Gaussian as a flexible region of interest with adaptive position, covariance, and semantics, rather than a fixed spatial cell. The model architecture draws on DETR-style iterative refinement with cross-attention for image-to-3D information transfer and 3D sparse convolution for inter-Gaussian interactions, while the Gaussian-to-voxel splatting module adapts the splatting rasterization idea from 3D-GS to produce occupancy predictions instead of rendered images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75–82% memory reduction at comparable accuracy is practically significant for deploying occupancy prediction in resource-constrained autonomous driving systems, and the object-centric representation offers a principled alternative to the wasteful uniform allocation of grid-based methods. However, the authors acknowledge that performance still lags behind the best dense-grid methods (e.g., 29.83 vs. 31.49 SC IoU on nuScenes), and the approach requires a large number of Gaussians (144K) to reach competitive results, suggesting the representation may still be partially redundant. Open questions include whether modeling only solid objects (excluding empty space) could close the performance gap, and whether the Gaussian representation generalizes to dynamic scene understanding or downstream planning tasks without the voxelization step.
