## Problem

Existing GCD methods assign pseudo-labels to unlabelled data based solely on pairwise sample-to-sample or sample-to-cluster similarities, overlooking the global relational structure among each instance's k-nearest neighbors. This neglect of neighborhood context leads to inaccurate positive sample mining and noisy pseudo-labels that degrade downstream representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labelled set of RGB images from seen classes and an unlabelled set containing both seen and unseen classes, with features extracted via a ViT-B-16 backbone initialized with DINO self-supervised weights. It produces cluster assignments (pseudo-labels) for all unlabelled samples and final class predictions spanning both seen and unseen categories. The number of clusters is estimated at evaluation time via Brent's algorithm, and the method operates iteratively over E=200 epochs, re-training NGCN and re-clustering at each epoch.

## Method

The core contribution is a Neighbor Graph Convolutional Network (NGCN), a 4-layer GCN trained on sub-graphs built from each labelled sample's 80 nearest neighbors, where node features are anchor-relative differences (v_j − v_i) and the network predicts binary pairwise linkages via BCE loss. At inference, NGCN generalizes to the joint labelled+unlabelled set to predict an adjacency matrix that drives FINCH hierarchical clustering for pseudo-label assignment, selecting the hierarchy level whose cluster count best matches the labelled set's true class count. A Cross-View Consistency Strategy (CVCS) then filters noisy pseudo-labels by comparing FINCH and k-means cluster assignments, excluding samples that land in different clusters under the two views. The purified data feeds a joint optimization combining instance-level contrastive loss (supervised + self-supervised), a cluster-level contrastive loss with a momentum-updated centroid memory bank, and a robust cluster-level contrastive loss on CVCS-purified samples using k-means centroids.

## Results

On the six k-unknown GCD benchmarks, the proposed NGCN+CVCS method achieves the best or near-best All accuracy: 96.5% on CIFAR-10, 74.6% on CIFAR-100, 78.1% on ImageNet-100, 87.2% on Oxford-IIIT Pet, 61.3% on CUB-200, and 44.3% on Stanford Cars. The largest gains over the GCD baseline [35] appear on fine-grained datasets, e.g., +10.0% All on CUB-200 (61.3 vs 51.3) and +5.3% All on Stanford Cars (44.3 vs 39.0). When integrated with PromptCAL (k-known setting), the method reaches 82.5% All on CIFAR-100 and 54.7% All on Stanford Cars, surpassing both SimGCD and PromptCAL on all four reported benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GCD baseline [35], the proposed method wins on All accuracy across all six benchmarks, with the largest margins on fine-grained data: +10.0% on CUB-200 (61.3 vs 51.3) and +5.3% on Stanford Cars (44.3 vs 39.0). Compared to XCon [9], the method is consistently ahead, e.g., 96.5 vs 96.0 on CIFAR-10 and 61.3 vs 52.1 on CUB-200. Against DCCL [28], results are mixed: DCCL leads on CIFAR-100 (75.3 vs 74.6), ImageNet-100 (80.5 vs 78.1), and Pets (88.1 vs 87.2), while the proposed method wins on CIFAR-10 (96.5 vs 96.3), CUB-200 (61.3 vs 63.5 — actually DCCL leads here too), and Stanford Cars (44.3 vs 43.1). UNO+ [10] and RankStats+ [13] are substantially weaker, particularly on New-class accuracy (e.g., UNO+ scores only 53.8% New on CIFAR-10 vs 94.4% for the proposed method). In the k-known setting (Table 2), integrating NGCN+CVCS into PromptCAL [41] yields consistent improvements over both SimGCD [37] and PromptCAL alone on all four benchmarks, e.g., 82.5 vs 81.2 vs 80.1 All on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GCD framework of Vaze et al. (2022), which established the problem of clustering unlabelled data spanning both seen and unseen classes using contrastive learning. The key departure is in the pseudo-labeling stage: prior GCD methods (OpenCon, GCD, DCCL) mine positive samples via pairwise cosine similarities, while this work introduces a Neighbor Graph Convolutional Network (NGCN) that exploits the full kNN sub-graph structure around each instance to predict pairwise linkages, drawing on the message-passing architecture of Kipf & Welling's GCN. For clustering, it adopts FINCH's hierarchical approach but augments it with a Cross-View Consistency Strategy (CVCS) that cross-validates FINCH and k-means assignments to filter noisy pseudo-labels—a mechanism not present in prior GCD pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (NGCN + CVCS can be grafted onto PromptCAL or other GCD frameworks) makes the contribution broadly applicable without requiring architectural changes to the backbone. However, the method adds per-epoch NGCN training and dual clustering (FINCH + k-means), increasing computational cost, and its gains are most pronounced on fine-grained benchmarks (CUB-200, Stanford Cars) where class boundaries are subtle; on generic datasets like CIFAR-10 the improvement over DCCL is marginal. Open questions include whether the graph-based pseudo-labeling scales to very large unlabelled pools (the kNN sub-graph construction is O(N·k)) and whether the approach transfers to non-visual modalities or open-world settings with continuously arriving new classes.
