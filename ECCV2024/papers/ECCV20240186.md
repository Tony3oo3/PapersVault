## Problem

Multi-modality MRI fusion requires different information aggregation patterns across diseases and modality combinations, yet existing methods either rely on fixed, task-specific relevance definitions or perform implicit aggregation driven solely by downstream labels without explicit optimization targets for inter-dependencies and information flow. This makes it difficult to define a unified, adaptable aggregation pattern that generalizes across clinical scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240186/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method ingests multi-modality MRI volumes with modality combinations varying by task (e.g., LGE/cine/T2 for cardiac pathology, T1c/T1n/T2f/T2w for brain tumor, Sagittal T2/Axial PD/Coronal T1 for knee classification). Each modality is encoded independently by a U-Net encoder producing hierarchical feature maps at four scales, which are then fused. Outputs are pixel-wise segmentation masks (BraTS: TC/ET/WT; MyoPS: scar/edema/LV/RV/MYO) or per-patient class labels (MRNet: abnormal/ACL tear/meniscal tear).

## Method

E²PA explicitly quantifies multi-modality fusion through two energy-based modules. EHF (Energy-guided Hierarchical Fusion) uses a multi-scale attention network to fuse modality representations hierarchically, then defines an energy function enforcing equal energy across different patients for the same disease (hierarchical same energy), thereby explicitly optimizing inter-dependencies propagation via iterative minimization with a fixed reference patient. ESA (Energy-regularized Space Alignment) applies QR decomposition to factorize each modality's representation into an orthogonal basis Q and upper-triangular R, computes a transformation T = Q_modality^T @ Q_fusion to align all modalities into the fusion space, and then minimizes an L2-based energy between the aligned modality features and the aggregated output to enforce information-flow consistency. The total loss combines both energy terms with the downstream task loss, making the framework adaptable to segmentation or classification without architectural redesign.

## Results

On BraTS brain tumor segmentation, E²PA achieves the highest average Dice of 90.6% (TC 91.0%, ET 87.3%, WT 93.5%), outperforming the next-best AWSNet (88.8%) by 1.8 points. On MyoPS cardiac pathology segmentation, E²PA reaches 82.2% average Dice (scar 64.7%, edema 73.9%, LV 94.4%, RV 91.1%, MYO 87.0%), surpassing MyoPS-Net (81.9%) and NestedFormer (80.7%). On the MRNet knee MRI classification task, E²PA attains 97.8% AUC for abnormality detection, 97.5% for ACL tear, and 94.4% for meniscal tear, exceeding TransMed by 2.0, 1.2, and 2.1 percentage points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²PA outperforms all ten baselines on both segmentation datasets. On BraTS, it leads AWSNet (88.8% AVG) by 1.8 points and NestedFormer (88.3%) by 2.3 points, with the largest margin on the hardest sub-region ET (87.3% vs. 86.6% for AWSNet). On MyoPS, E²PA beats MyoPS-Net (81.9%) by 0.3 points on average and NestedFormer (80.7%) by 1.5 points, while matching MyoPS-Net on edema (73.9% vs. 74.0%) and RV (91.1% vs. 92.0%). For MRNet classification, E²PA surpasses TransMed (the strongest baseline) by 2.0/1.2/2.1 AUC points on Abnormal/ACL/Meniscal tasks, and outperforms ELNet and MRPyrNet by 3–5 points on meniscal tear detection. The ablation confirms that both EHF (attention fusion + L_{i-d}) and ESA (alignment + L_r) contribute substantially, with alignment providing the largest single gain of 9.6% Dice on BraTS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both fixed (task-driven, clinically pre-defined relevance) and dynamic (concatenation + task-label-driven) multi-modality MRI fusion paradigms by introducing an energy-based formulation that explicitly quantifies inter-dependencies and information flow. It repurposes the energy-based model framework (LeCun et al., 2006) — previously applied to OOD detection, structured prediction, and incremental learning — to impose a 'same energy among patients' constraint (EHF) and a consistency-of-information-flow constraint via QR-decomposition-based space alignment (ESA). The attention-based fusion backbone draws on multi-scale attention mechanisms (Dai et al., 2021), while the encoders are standard U-Net architectures, so the architectural novelty is concentrated in the energy losses and alignment procedure rather than in the network topology.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit energy-based quantification offers a principled, task-agnostic way to characterize how modalities interact, potentially improving interpretability of multi-modality fusion and reducing the need to redesign pipelines for each new disease or modality combination. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (≈1–2% Dice on BraTS, ≈0.3% on MyoPS), and the energy formulation's theoretical grounding (e.g., the 'same energy' proposition, the implicit-regression analogy in ESA) remains somewhat informal. Open questions include whether the QR-decomposition alignment generalizes to non-orthogonal or high-dimensional feature spaces, whether the energy losses scale to more than four modalities, and whether the explicit quantification translates into clinically actionable insights beyond segmentation metrics.
