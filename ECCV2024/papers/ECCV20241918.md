## Problem

Decomposing large-scale urban driving scenes into static and dynamic components with NeRFs remains challenging due to unconstrained dynamics, large temporal gaps from ego-vehicle motion, and heavy occlusions. Existing multi-branch NeRF methods either require expensive per-object 3D annotations (bounding boxes, instance tracking) or rely on simplified setups with slow camera motion and few dynamic actors, leading to suboptimal separation with floating artifacts in the background and incorrect dynamic segmentation in realistic driving sequences.

## Input / Output

RoDUS consumes a sequence of RGB images with associated camera poses and timestamps, augmented by pseudo-ground-truth 2D semantic segmentation from a pre-trained Mask2Former (trained on Mapillary Vistas) and optional LiDAR depth measurements. At inference, it independently renders per-timestamp static and dynamic RGB images, per-branch segmentation maps, depth maps, and a binary motion segmentation mask (obtained by thresholding dynamic opacity at 0.5), all of which can be combined for a full scene view. Notably, no 3D bounding boxes or instance-level tracking are required, making the pipeline applicable to datasets lacking such annotations.

## Method

RoDUS employs a two-pathway NeRF architecture: a static branch with a 3D multi-resolution hash grid conditioned on a per-frame latent embedding for lighting, and a dynamic branch with a 4D hash grid (adding normalized timestamp) plus a shadow head that scales down static radiance. The key differentiator is a robust kernel-based initialization using an IRLS trimmed estimator with spatial filtering, which reduces L2 sensitivity of the static model to dynamic outliers and is applied as a dedicated first-epoch training phase before enabling full reconstruction losses. A view-independent semantic head is added to both branches, with a 'foreground-only mask' on the dynamic branch's semantic output that selectively blocks gradient flow for non-movable classes, preventing the dynamic field from over-explaining background regions while still refining high-frequency detail. Additional losses enforce sky transparency, road planarity via SVD on road-labeled patches, and dynamic sparsity, collectively guiding clean separation without any 3D object-level supervision.

## Results

On KITTI-360, RoDUS achieves the best Static NVS quality (PSNR 23.43, SSIM 0.802, LPIPS 0.181), outperforming the next-best EmerNeRF (PSNR 20.59) by nearly 3 dB. For motion segmentation on KITTI-360, RoDUS dramatically leads with IoU 64.84 and F1 76.52, compared to EmerNeRF's IoU 12.56 and F1 19.92. On Pandaset, RoDUS attains the best SSIM (0.877) and LPIPS (0.135) for full image reconstruction and the best SSIM (0.825) and LPIPS (0.156) for Static NVS, though EmerNeRF edges ahead on Static NVS PSNR (27.47 vs. 25.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RobustNeRF, RoDUS wins decisively on Static NVS across both datasets (e.g., PSNR 23.43 vs. 19.50 on KITTI-360), and additionally provides motion segmentation which RobustNeRF does not support. Against D²NeRF, RoDUS outperforms on every reported metric on both datasets, with particularly large gains in Static NVS PSNR (+4.73 dB on KITTI-360) and motion segmentation IoU (64.84 vs. 4.67). Against SUDS, RoDUS loses on full-image reconstruction PSNR (26.47 vs. 28.16 on KITTI-360; 28.39 vs. 30.28 on Pandaset) but wins on SSIM and LPIPS for both image reconstruction and Static NVS, and dominates motion segmentation (IoU 64.84 vs. 5.35, F1 76.52 vs. 9.34). Against EmerNeRF, the closest competitor, RoDUS leads on KITTI-360 Static NVS PSNR (23.43 vs. 20.59) and motion segmentation by a wide margin (IoU 64.84 vs. 12.56), but EmerNeRF achieves higher Static NVS PSNR on Pandaset (27.47 vs. 25.83), while RoDUS still wins on SSIM and LPIPS for that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoDUS extends the multi-branch NeRF architecture for static-dynamic decomposition (NSFF, D²NeRF, SUDS, EmerNeRF) by addressing a key weakness: both branches can learn incorrect information as long as the composed output matches ground truth. It departs from prior methods that rely on expensive 3D annotations (NGS, PNF, DisCoScene) or joint rejection-factor optimization (NeRF-W, Ha-NeRF) by introducing a robust IRLS-based initialization for the static branch (drawing on RobustNeRF and Barron's kernel family) combined with a view-independent semantic field and a 'foreground-only mask' that selectively prevents the dynamic branch from over-explaining background regions. The addition of sky, road (SVD-based planar regularization), and shadow modeling tailors the framework specifically to urban driving scenes, going beyond the generic two-branch factorization used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving self-supervised decomposition without 3D bounding boxes or instance tracking, RoDUS removes a major practical barrier to deploying NeRF-based scene understanding in autonomous driving pipelines where such annotations are unreliable or unavailable. The paper's emphasis on individual branch quality (rather than just composed output) exposes a previously underappreciated failure mode in multi-branch NeRFs. However, the method remains limited by its dependence on 2D segmentation pseudo-label quality and cannot recover regions that are persistently occluded (e.g., behind large slow-moving trucks), pointing toward the need for uncertainty-aware inpainting as a future direction.
