## Problem

Free-viewpoint video rendering of outdoor sports scenes is challenging because athletes perform fast, unpredictable motions across large-scale environments, making dense fixed camera arrays impractical and handheld cameras insufficient for capturing all viewing angles. Existing monocular systems either require the subject to self-rotate in a limited space or rely on depth sensors that fail outdoors, leaving no viable approach for real-world sports where the athlete cannot cooperate with the camera.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single monocular RGB video (30 fps) captured by an autonomously orbiting drone following a predefined arc trajectory at constant linear speed and height around the athlete. It produces a 4D dynamic human model (SMPL-parameterized with per-frame latent codes for appearance) and a 3D unbounded scene model, jointly enabling 360-degree free-viewpoint video rendering at any arbitrary time and viewpoint. A calibration board with 0.1 m grid is placed in the scene during capture to recover real-world scale.

## Method

The pipeline combines four key modules: (1) a modified COLMAP calibration that enforces a Drone Motion Constraint (DMC) during bundle adjustment, penalizing non-uniform inter-frame camera displacements to exploit the known constant-speed orbit; (2) Drone-based Sequential MoCap (DSM) that recovers per-frame SMPL parameters by optimizing 2D reprojection error (using a ViT-based keypoint detector) plus temporal smoothness, leveraging the fast-varying viewpoints of the orbiting drone for 3D consistency; (3) a canonical-space NeRF human model that deforms observation-space points into a T-pose via SMPL blending weights and queries a shared MLP with per-frame latent codes for temporal appearance consistency; and (4) a Mip-NeRF360 background model. A layered ray-sampling strategy uses the per-frame SMPL 3D bounding box to partition rays into human and background segments, enabling joint supervised training with a single L2 photometric loss and compositional volume rendering without requiring 2D segmentation masks.

## Results

On the AerialRecon dataset, the proposed drone-based system achieves PSNR of 24.24 (Climb), 22.94 (Jogging), 23.29 (Kungfu), and 22.41 (Football), consistently outperforming all baselines by 2–9 dB. SSIM scores range from 0.793 to 0.891, and LPIPS from 0.203 to 0.268, all superior to competing methods. On the NeuMan and HOSNeRF datasets, the method also achieves the best or near-best PSNR (e.g., 27.32 on SEATTLE, 28.91 on LOUNGE), confirming generalizability beyond the authors' own data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all four baselines on the AerialRecon dataset. Against HOSNeRF [25], it gains approximately 2.3–2.4 dB PSNR and 0.10–0.15 SSIM across all four scenes, while reducing LPIPS by 0.03–0.07. Against NeuMan [16], the improvement is 2.4–4.0 dB PSNR and 0.11–0.21 SSIM, with the largest gap on Kungfu (4.0 dB) where NeuMan's lack of pose priors causes severe artifacts. NeRF-T [21], which does not use human pose priors at all, is the weakest baseline, trailing by 5.6–9.7 dB PSNR. On the NeuMan and HOSNeRF datasets (Tables 4–5), the method still achieves the best PSNR in most scenes (e.g., 27.32 vs. 26.35 on SEATTLE, 28.91 vs. 27.84 on LOUNGE), though the margin narrows to 0.5–2.0 dB, suggesting the advantage is most pronounced in the challenging fast-motion, large-scale outdoor sports setting the system was designed for.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of monocular dynamic human rendering (NeuMan, HOSNeRF, HumanNeRF) and drone-based motion capture (ActiveMoCap, Zhou et al.), but departs from both by introducing system-level designs—Drone Motion Constraint (DMC) for calibration and Drone-based Sequential MoCap (DSM) for pose estimation—that exploit the predictable orbiting trajectory of an autonomous drone. For scene reconstruction, it adopts Mip-NeRF360 as the background backbone and a canonical-space NeRF for the human, combining them via a layered compositional rendering strategy that avoids 2D segmentation masks. The key conceptual departure from prior monocular systems is the use of a fast-orbiting drone to simultaneously provide multi-sided human observations and dense 360° background views, eliminating the need for the subject to self-rotate or for fixed camera arrays.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing dynamic NVS systems either require expensive multi-camera rigs or assume the subject stays in a controlled area, making them inapplicable to real outdoor sports where athletes move unpredictably over large spaces. The AerialRecon dataset (400 clips, 120K+ images, multi-view synchronized) fills a notable void in publicly available outdoor sports reconstruction data. However, the system's reliance on a precisely controlled orbiting trajectory (constant speed, fixed height, 30fps) limits its generality to unstructured or multi-subject scenarios, and the single-author scope with a modest dataset size leaves open questions about scalability to more complex scenes, real-time rendering, and handling of occlusions from multiple athletes simultaneously.
