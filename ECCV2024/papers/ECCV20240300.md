## Problem

Existing NeRF enhancers face a fundamental trade-off: 2D enhancers (e.g., NeRFLiX) learn general degradation-removal functions but are agnostic to scene geometry and fail when the degradation distribution shifts, while 3D enhancers (e.g., Boosting View Synthesis) transfer detail from training views but are sensitive to inaccurate camera calibration and propagate geometric errors into renderings. No prior method combines the generalizability of 2D learning with the geometric grounding of 3D alignment while remaining robust to pose estimation errors.

## Input / Output

Given a trained NeRF model, the enhancer consumes a rendered novel-view image, a small set of neighbouring training images (5 for LLFF/360v2, 2 for DTU, selected by camera pose proximity), their camera poses, and NeRF-rendered depth maps. Neighbouring images are encoded into 64-dimensional feature maps at full resolution via lightweight convolutional encoders before reprojection. The output is a single enhanced RGB image at the same resolution as the input render, produced by a modified Uformer operating on fused feature maps rather than raw pixels.

## Method

RoGUENeRF combines three modules: (1) 3D Alignment reprojects neighbouring training image features into the novel view using the pinhole camera model with NeRF depth maps, applying a Heaviside visibility test with a leniency threshold to reject occluded pixels; (2) Non-Rigid Refinement applies a lightweight iterative optical flow network in feature space to correct residual misalignment caused by pose or geometry errors, exploiting the fact that flow networks can match global structure even across domain gaps between blurry renders and sharp training images; (3) Geometry-Aware Attention computes per-pixel weights from content similarity and depth differences plus per-image weights from camera Euler angles and positions, multiplicatively suppressing ghosting in misaligned regions. Attention-regulated features from all neighbours are fused via maxpooling (supporting variable neighbour counts) and processed by a Uformer backbone. The model is pre-trained for 3000 epochs on NeRF+LLFF render-GT pairs, then fine-tuned per scene in under 60 minutes with L1 and VGG-19 perceptual losses, enabling rapid adaptation to new degradation distributions.

## Results

RoGUENeRF achieves consistent PSNR improvements across all six NeRF baselines and three datasets: +1.34 dB on Nerfacto (360v2), +1.11 dB on NeuS2++ (DTU), +1.10 dB on NeRF (LLFF), +0.70 dB on TensoRF (LLFF), +0.63 dB on MipNeRF360 (360v2), and +0.33 dB on ZipNeRF (360v2). LPIPS reductions range from 6.6% (ZipNeRF) to 37.4% (NeRF), and SSIM improves in every setting. The method also demonstrates robustness to camera pose noise, with MUSIQ gains of up to +19.2 over Nerfacto under large pose perturbation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoGUENeRF outperforms all three competing NeRF enhancers (NeRFLiX, NeRFLiX++, and Boosting View Synthesis) in every setting where they overlap. On LLFF with NeRF, RoGUENeRF reaches 27.67 dB PSNR versus 27.25 for NeRFLiX++ and 27.08 for Boosting View Synthesis, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.1495 vs. 0.1695 for NeRFLiX). On 360v2, RoGUENeRF's advantage over NeRFLiX is largest for Nerfacto (+1.34 vs. +0.81 dB) and MipNeRF360 (+0.63 vs. +0.18 dB), and it is the only enhancer that improves SSIM for MipNeRF360 (+0.025 vs. −0.001 for NeRFLiX). On DTU, RoGUENeRF gains +1.11 dB over NeuS2++ compared to only +0.05 for NeRFLiX. The method also shows greater robustness to camera pose noise: under large Gaussian perturbation, RoGUENeRF improves Nerfacto's MUSIQ by +19.2 points versus +0.7 for the no-noise setting, indicating the geometry-aware alignment mechanism effectively compensates for calibration errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoGUENeRF sits at the intersection of two existing paradigms for improving NeRF renderings: 2D enhancers (NeRFLiX, NeRFLiX++) that learn general degradation-removal functions but ignore scene geometry, and 3D residual-transfer methods (Boosting View Synthesis) that leverage training-view detail but are fragile to camera pose errors. The paper departs from both by combining 3D feature reprojection (pinhole camera model + NeRF depth) with a lightweight iterative optical flow refinement, then regulating residual misalignment via a geometry-aware spatial attention module, all atop a pre-trained Uformer backbone. This hybrid 3D-alignment + 2D-enhancement architecture, together with a pre-train-then-fine-tune strategy, is the core conceptual departure from prior NeRF enhancers that commit to one paradigm or the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent gains across six diverse NeRF baselines and three datasets (up to 1.34 dB PSNR on Nerfacto) demonstrate that post-rendering enhancement is a broadly applicable and underexploited lever for closing the gap between NeRF outputs and ground truth. The robustness to camera pose noise—where improvements actually grow larger as noise increases—is practically significant given that COLMAP errors are a persistent real-world bottleneck. Key limitations remain: the method requires storing all training images (prohibitive for very large scenes), does not achieve real-time inference, and the pre-training is tied to a single NeRF baseline/dataset, raising open questions about how well the learned enhancement function transfers to fundamentally different degradation distributions (e.g., dynamic scenes, extreme lighting changes).
