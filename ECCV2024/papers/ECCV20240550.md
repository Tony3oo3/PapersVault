## Problem

Existing safety-critical traffic simulation methods either generate static open-loop scenarios that do not react to the ego planner's actions, or lack the controllability to vary adversarial behavior (collision type, aggressiveness) within the same scene. No prior framework simultaneously provides closed-loop interaction, realistic multi-agent behavior, and fine-grained control over the adversarial agent's collision characteristics, leaving a gap in comprehensive AV safety evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an agent-centric rasterized map and T_hist historical states of neighboring vehicles as context, encoded via a ResNet backbone. At inference, the user specifies control parameters (TTC cost weight for aggressiveness, relative speed targets) and optionally trajectory proposals for partial diffusion to dictate collision type. The output is a full multi-agent trajectory set over a T-step horizon (3.2 s at 2 Hz) under unicycle dynamics, where one adversarial agent is guided to collide with the ego while all other reactive agents follow realistic, route-constrained behavior; the simulation re-plans every 0.5 s in closed loop.

## Method

SAFE-SIM builds on a trajectory diffusion model (ResNet map encoder + 1D temporal-convolution UNet, K=100 steps) trained on real driving data, and introduces two key mechanisms for controllable adversarial simulation. First, a composite guidance objective J(τ) = ρ(J_coll + J_v + J_ttc) + J_route + J_Gauss is applied at every denoising step via reconstruction (clean) guidance, where the adversarial terms (collision distance, relative speed, time-to-collision) push the adversarial agent toward the ego while regularization terms (Gaussian inter-agent distance, route adherence) preserve realism. Second, Partial Diffusion injects rule-based trajectory proposals—generated from centerline intersection analysis with lateral offsets—at a user-chosen partial diffusion ratio γ, so the model only denoises the remaining steps, giving precise control over collision type (e.g., rear-end vs. side-swipe) while the diffusion process refines the trajectory into a realistic path. The entire pipeline operates in closed loop: the ego planner and all reactive agents re-plan at 2 Hz, and the adversarial agent's trajectory proposals are updated each cycle to account for the evolving ego trajectory.

## Results

On nuScenes with a rule-based planner, SAFE-SIM achieves a 43.2% ego-adversarial collision rate with a realism score of 0.38 (Wasserstein distance), outperforming STRIVE (36.4% collision, 0.85 realism) and DiffScene (18.2% collision, 0.52 realism). On nuPlan, SAFE-SIM reaches an 80% collision rate with 0.27 realism, compared to DiffScene's 56.7% and 0.42. SAFE-SIM also runs significantly faster than STRIVE (104.5s vs. 427.2s per scenario on nuScenes) while maintaining lower off-road rates for non-adversarial agents (1.8% vs. 2.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFE-SIM outperforms STRIVE on every reported metric on nuScenes: higher collision rate (43.2% vs. 36.4%), lower non-adversarial off-road rate (1.8% vs. 2.2%), substantially better realism (0.38 vs. 0.85 Wasserstein distance), and roughly 4× faster simulation time (104.5s vs. 427.2s). Against DiffScene, SAFE-SIM achieves a much higher collision rate on both datasets (43.2% vs. 18.2% on nuScenes; 80% vs. 56.7% on nuPlan) while also producing more realistic trajectories (0.38 vs. 0.52 on nuScenes; 0.27 vs. 0.42 on nuPlan). The one area where DiffScene has an edge is the adversarial agent's off-road rate on nuPlan (5.0% vs. 11.7%), though SAFE-SIM's non-adversarial off-road rate is lower (9.4% vs. 14.0%). Overall, SAFE-SIM is the only method that simultaneously achieves high collision rates, low realism error, and fast inference across both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFE-SIM builds directly on the guided conditional diffusion architecture of CTG (Zhong et al., ICRA 2023) for trajectory generation and the reconstruction (clean) guidance from Ho et al. (2022), while using the BITS simulation framework as its closed-loop backbone. It departs from prior safety-critical scenario generators (STRIVE, DiffScene, AdvSim) by introducing two key mechanisms absent in those works: (1) an adversarial guidance term embedded in the diffusion denoising process that makes a specific agent actively challenge the ego planner in a closed-loop setting, and (2) a partial diffusion procedure that injects rule-based trajectory proposals mid-denoising to control collision type. The paper positions itself as the first framework to simultaneously achieve safety-critical, closed-loop, controllable-adversarial, and real-world-data-grounded simulation, a combination no single prior method provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical gap in AV safety evaluation: prior methods either generated static/open-loop scenarios or lacked the ability to vary adversarial behavior within the same scene, limiting the breadth of planner stress-testing. The partial diffusion mechanism for controlling collision type and the TTC/relative-speed guidance for tuning aggressiveness offer a useful toolkit for systematic safety evaluation. However, the paper acknowledges that the adversarial agent sometimes collides with non-adversarial agents before reaching the ego, and some generated collisions are not ego-at-fault, which limits the direct applicability for liability-focused testing. Open questions include whether the framework can be used for closed-loop policy training (not just evaluation) and whether the controllable parameters can be automated to explore the long-tail scenario space without manual tuning.
