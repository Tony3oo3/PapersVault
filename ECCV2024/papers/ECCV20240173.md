## Problem

Existing low-light image enhancement (LLIE) methods handle illumination recovery (low-frequency) and noise reduction (high-frequency) within a single unified optimization, causing these distinct tasks to interfere with each other and produce suboptimal results such as inaccurate illumination corrections or residual noise artifacts. The paper asks whether a generic frequency disentanglement paradigm can be designed to seamlessly plug into diverse SOTA architectures (CNNs, Transformers, flow-based, diffusion-based) and consistently improve them with minimal additional parameters.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a low-light noisy RGB image and produces an enhanced high-quality image, operating as a lightweight add-on to any existing LLIE backbone. Concretely, the input image and the coarse-adjusted intermediate are each decomposed into a 4-level Laplace pyramid (K=4), yielding multi-scale high-frequency difference-of-Gaussians maps plus a coarsest low-frequency map; these are stacked along the channel dimension and fed into the re-designed backbone, whose output is reconstructed via inverse Laplace summation. The entire disentanglement module adds only 88K parameters and 2.53 GFLOPS for 256×256 inputs, with an inference overhead of ~8 ms on an RTX 3090.

## Method

The proposed framework decouples LLIE into two sequentially trained phases. In the coarse phase, the ACCA module (88K params) performs low-frequency illumination recovery using a hybrid dual-branch design: a local branch with Window-based Convolutional Composition Attention (W-CCA) blocks that factorize a 3D spatial-channel similarity map into three 1D tensors (height, width, channel) via tensor composition, enabling pixel-adaptive aggregation at linear cost; and a global ISP branch predicting a colour matrix and gamma. In the coarse-to-fine phase, the LDRM decomposes both the original input and the coarse result into Laplace pyramids, stacks all maps, and passes them through a minimally re-designed SOTA backbone (only first/last conv layers modified) to restore multi-scale frequency components. A novel low-frequency consistency loss constrains the coarsest restored Laplace level to match the coarsest level from the ACCA output, enforcing proper frequency separation during training. ACCA is trained first for 100 epochs, then frozen while LDRM is trained for 40K iterations, and this decoupled schedule consistently outperforms end-to-end training across all six tested baselines.

## Results

The proposed frequency disentanglement framework improves all six SOTA baselines across five benchmarks (LOL-v2, SID, SDSD-in, SDSD-out, SMID) with only 88K extra parameters and 2.53 GFLOPS. The largest gains are on SDSD-out, where Restormer-De achieves 32.47 dB PSNR (+7.68 dB over Restormer) and LLFlow-De reaches 34.58 dB (+8.83 dB). On LOL-v2, Diff-L-De gains +4.98 dB PSNR (23.93 vs 18.95) and Restormer-De gains +4.62 dB (24.56 vs 19.94). The ACCA module alone achieves 23.80 dB PSNR on LOL-v2 with only 88K parameters, surpassing much larger models like Retinexformer (22.80 dB, 1.61M params) and SNR (21.48 dB, 39.12M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method universally improves all six primary baselines (MIR-Net, Restormer, LLFlow, SNR, Retinexformer, Diff-L) on every benchmark, with gains ranging from +0.20 dB (Retinexformer on SID) to +8.83 dB (LLFlow on SDSD-out). Against the segmentation-guided SKF approach [54], the proposed method achieves higher PSNR and SSIM with fewer extra parameters (88K vs 320K for SNR-SKF, 2.23M for LLFlow-SKF) and without requiring segmentation maps. As a standalone coarse module, ACCA outperforms lightweight baselines ZeroDCE (17.63 dB), Star (18.26 dB), IAT (23.50 dB), and PairLIE (18.80 dB) on LOL-v2, and also surpasses larger models MIR-Net-v2 (21.10 dB) and LLformer (20.69 dB) while using only 88K parameters. When integrated as the coarse stage, ACCA yields the best Restormer-De result (24.56 dB) compared to using IAT (23.97 dB), Star (22.40 dB), PairLIE (22.36 dB), or ZeroDCE (21.53 dB). The decoupled (frozen ACCA) optimization consistently outperforms end-to-end training across all six baselines, with the largest margin on LLFlow (+0.96 dB PSNR on LOL-v2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in LLIE where a single unified network simultaneously handles illumination recovery (low-frequency) and noise reduction (high-frequency). While prior frequency-decomposition methods (Xu et al. 2020, Liang et al. 2023) split images into frequency bands, they still optimize both components jointly within one network. Here, the authors explicitly decouple the optimization into a lightweight coarse stage (ACCA, drawing on tensor-composition attention from Omni Aggregation Networks and ECA-Net) and a fine stage (LDRM, a minimally modified SOTA backbone operating on Laplace pyramid representations), linked by a novel low-frequency consistency loss. The Laplace pyramid itself is a classical signal-processing tool repurposed as the interface between the two stages, and the plug-and-play design means the fine stage can be any existing architecture (CNN, Transformer, flow-based, or diffusion).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the universal plug-and-play nature: with only 88K extra parameters and 2.53 GFLOPS, the framework consistently improves six architecturally diverse SOTA models across five benchmarks, suggesting that coupled frequency optimization is a genuine bottleneck rather than an architectural limitation. The ablation showing that decoupled (frozen-ACCA) training consistently outperforms end-to-end training across all six baselines supports the hypothesis that joint low/high-frequency optimization is actively harmful. The stated limitation—that the method cannot hallucinate severely corrupted details—points toward future integration with generative models (e.g., Stable Diffusion) as a natural next step, and the paradigm could potentially transfer to other restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring) where low- and high-frequency degradations co-occur.
