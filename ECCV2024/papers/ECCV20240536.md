## Problem

Medical multi-modal contrastive learning suffers from two intertwined issues: extreme data scarcity (expert-annotated samples number in the hundreds rather than millions) and the modality gap, where image and text embeddings occupy segregated regions of the shared hypersphere, causing high intra-modal similarity that prevents the model from distinguishing between different pathologies. This paper asks whether scarce radiologist eye-gaze heatmaps can be integrated into CLIP-style training to improve embedding alignment, uniformity, and discriminative power without altering the base architecture.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes 224×224 chest X-ray images, Wordpiece-tokenized radiology reports (max 256 tokens), and—only for a small subset (~1080 of ~200K samples)—normalized radiologist eye-gaze heatmaps. At inference, no heatmap is required; the model produces L2-normalized image and text embeddings in a shared d-dimensional space. Downstream, these embeddings support zero-shot classification via cosine similarity to prompt-averaged label embeddings, nearest-neighbor report retrieval through a FAISS index, and RAG-based report generation by prompting a frozen Mistral 7B LLM with the five closest retrieved reports.

## Method

eCLIP augments standard CLIP with a lightweight Heatmap Processor that applies multi-headed attention over patchified sequences: heatmap-overlaid image patches serve as queries while original image patches provide keys and values, and the output is reconstructed to image format before passing through the unchanged CLIP vision encoder. To compensate for the scarcity of expert annotations, a mixup strategy blends the original image I_i with its expert-processed version I_i^E using λ ~ Beta(0.3, 0.3), yielding a synthetic image whose embedding v_i^λ forms an additional positive pair (v_i^λ, t_i) in the InfoNCE loss. A three-phase curriculum (cold start → warmup → cooldown) gradually introduces expert examples from 0% to 50% probability, and an auxiliary MSE priming loss forces the heatmap processor to behave as an identity map when the heatmap is all-ones, ensuring graceful degradation when no annotation is available. The entire pipeline is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated with Swin Tiny, ViT Small/Base, and ResNet50 (via GLoRIA).

## Results

On zero-shot chest X-ray classification, eCLIP variants consistently outperform the base CLIP model across four datasets. The best eCLIP configuration (ViT Base + expert) achieves 0.563 macro-F1 on CheXpert 5×200 (vs. 0.540 base) and 0.477 on MIMIC 5×200 (vs. 0.465 base), while eCLIP^P with Swin Tiny reaches 0.819 on RSNA (vs. 0.808) and 0.192 on CXR 14×100 (vs. 0.169). For cross-modal retrieval on Open-I, eCLIP (Swin Tiny) improves Recall@1 from 3.1 to 3.7 and Recall@10 from 11.3 to 13.4 over base CLIP. In RAG-based report generation with a frozen Mistral 7B LLM, eCLIP yields a small but consistent BLEU-2 gain (0.177 vs. 0.172) and CheXBERT embedding similarity improvement (0.506 vs. 0.492).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base CLIP model, eCLIP variants achieve consistent gains: on CheXpert 5×200, the best eCLIP (ViT Base + expert) reaches 0.563 F1 versus 0.540 for base CLIP (+2.3 points), and on CXR 14×100, eCLIP^P (Swin Tiny) reaches 0.192 versus 0.169 (+2.3 points). Compared to m3-mixup [33], eCLIP is competitive but does not uniformly dominate: m3-mixup edges ahead on MIMIC 5×200 with Swin Tiny (0.469 vs. 0.463 for eCLIP^P), while eCLIP wins on CheXpert (0.558 vs. 0.554) and CXR 14×100 (0.192 vs. 0.179) with the same encoder. Against DACL [49], eCLIP variants clearly outperform across nearly all configurations, with DACL sometimes degrading below the base model (e.g., 0.465 vs. 0.517 on CheXpert with Swin Tiny). Versus GLoRIA [15] (Resnet50), eCLIP with Swin Tiny or ViT encoders substantially outperforms on all four datasets, e.g., 0.558 vs. 0.478 on CheXpert and 0.192 vs. 0.155 on CXR 14×100. The 'naive' baseline (adding expert samples without mixup or curriculum) is consistently worse than both eCLIP and m3-mixup, confirming that the mixup and curriculum strategies are essential for effective use of scarce expert annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

eCLIP extends the CLIP contrastive learning framework by introducing radiologist eye-gaze heatmaps as a source of expert annotations, creating additional positive pairs through a multi-headed attention heatmap processor and mixup augmentation. It is positioned as an alternative to cross-modal mixup strategies (m³-mixup, DACL) that address the modality gap by interpolating between modalities, instead enriching the image modality with semantically meaningful expert signal. In the medical imaging contrastive learning lineage (GLoRIA, MedCLIP, CXR-CLIP), eCLIP is the first to incorporate human attention data as a training signal, building on prior work that used eye-tracking for classification (GazeGNN, van Sonsbeek et al.) but not for contrastive pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even a small set (~1000) of expert eye-gaze annotations can measurably improve embedding alignment, uniformity, and downstream zero-shot performance in medical contrastive learning, offering a practical path for domains where expert data is scarce but high-value. The RAG pipeline with a frozen LLM (Mistral 7B) for radiology report generation without medical fine-tuning is a promising direction for clinical deployment. Key limitations include the very small expert dataset (1080 samples), lack of clinical validation of generated reports, and additional training compute from the extra forward pass. Open questions include whether scaling expert annotations or incorporating temporal eye-tracking dynamics would yield larger gains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond chest X-rays.
