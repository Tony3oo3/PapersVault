## Problem

Wide-angle lens distortion in portrait photography produces stretched faces and curved background lines, but existing deep-learning correction methods struggle with complex facial projections when training data is limited, and the inherent central symmetry of barrel distortion in non-face regions has never been explicitly exploited as a correction constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240775/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single wide-angle RGB portrait as input. The face region is cropped and resized to 256×256 for FaceCNet, while the full background is processed at 512×384 by LineCNet. Outputs include a fully corrected portrait image plus separate face and background optical-flow maps; a post-processing stage uses ParseNet for face segmentation and LaMa inpainting to seamlessly fuse the corrected face back into the corrected background.

## Method

The framework decouples correction into two U-Net networks trained in parallel. FaceCNet incorporates a generative face prior by inverting the input face into StyleGAN's W+ space via a fine-tuned e4e encoder and feeding multi-scale StyleGAN intermediate features into the U-Net to guide deformation-flow prediction toward natural facial structure. LineCNet is a plain U-Net augmented only with a symmetry regularization loss that penalizes deviations from horizontal, vertical, and central flip symmetry of the predicted background flow, exploiting the radial nature of barrel distortion. The e4e encoder is fine-tuned on synthetically distorted faces (corners stretched outward) to generalize to wide-angle stretching. A face-fusion block reattaches the corrected face using ParseNet masks and translation alignment, then fills boundary gaps with LaMa inpainting.

## Results

On the full wide-angle portrait dataset (5000+ training, 129 test images), the proposed method achieves LineAcc 67.304, ShapeAcc 99.012, and Landmark Distance 5.013, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The ShapeAcc improvement is particularly striking: 99.012 vs. 97.500 (Zhu et al.) and 97.490 (Tan et al.), representing a ~60% reduction in facial landmark error. On the Note subset, LineAcc reaches 69.891 (vs. 68.683 for Tan et al.), and on the Vivo subset, ShapeAcc reaches 99.091 (vs. 96.076 for Zhu et al.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against three baselines: Shih et al. [38] (optimization-based warp mesh), Tan et al. [40] (two-stage deep network), and Zhu et al. [48] (semi-supervised multi-scale transformer). The proposed method wins on ShapeAcc across all three test splits, with the largest gap on the Vivo subset (99.091 vs. 96.076 for Zhu et al., a 3-point margin). On Landmark Distance (lower is better), the method achieves 5.013 vs. 5.840 (Zhu) and 6.035 (Shih), confirming substantially better facial geometry preservation. On LineAcc, the method edges out Zhu et al. on the full dataset (67.304 vs. 67.209) and clearly leads on the Note subset (69.891 vs. 68.683 for Tan et al.), though it trails Zhu et al. slightly on the Vivo subset (65.459 vs. 66.572). Overall, the generative face prior drives the large ShapeAcc gains while the symmetry prior contributes to the LineAcc improvements, with the two priors providing complementary benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep-learning-based wide-angle portrait correction line initiated by Tan et al. [40] and Zhu et al. [48], which moved away from camera-parameter-dependent optimization (Shih et al. [38]) toward learned warping. The key departure is the dual-prior strategy: StyleGAN intermediate features (obtained via e4e inversion) are repurposed as structural guidance for face flow prediction, while a symmetry regularization loss exploits the inherent central symmetry of barrel distortion for background correction. The multi-scale fusion of GAN features into a U-Net flow predictor and the explicit face-background separation with ParseNet/LaMa fusion represent architectural choices not present in prior two-stage or single-network approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The symmetry prior is a remarkably simple geometric constraint that improves line straightness without requiring camera calibration, suggesting that physical lens properties can be encoded as lightweight loss terms rather than explicit parameters. The use of pre-trained StyleGAN features as flow guidance opens a path for leveraging generative priors in warping-based restoration beyond face-specific tasks. However, the method is limited to moderate poses and produces unnatural body regions (e.g., elongated feet), and the generative prior's dependence on StyleGAN's training distribution constrains generalization to diverse face appearances. Extending the framework with body priors and handling extreme poses remain open challenges.
