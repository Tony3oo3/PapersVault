## Problem

Large-scale text-to-image diffusion models demand substantial computational resources and extensive high-resolution data, while existing control-specialized models (e.g., ControlNet variants) operate in incompatible latent spaces and fixed resolutions, preventing their joint use for multi-control synthesis without costly retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts bilingual (Chinese/English) text prompts, optional reference images, and pose/edge maps, producing synthesized RGB images at arbitrary resolutions around 1024×1024. Text conditioning uses concatenated embeddings from a pretrained Chinese text encoder and an English text encoder, while multi-resolution support is achieved via sinusoidal positional embeddings on resolution indices. Coop-Diffusion additionally accepts pre-trained diffusion models (with their own VAEs and resolutions) as plug-in components, requiring no retraining.

## Method

PanGu-Draw introduces a Time-Decoupling Training Strategy that splits a 5B U-Net denoiser into a 0.5B structure generator (handling early timesteps T…T_struct, T_struct=500) and a 0.5B texture generator (handling T_struct…0), each trained in isolation to avoid model sharding overhead. The structure generator trains on all available resolutions including upscaled low-res images (avoiding the ~48% data discard of prior methods), while the texture generator trains at 256px but samples at 1024px, yielding a 51% reduction in training resources. For multi-control and multi-resolution synthesis, Coop-Diffusion bridges latent-space mismatches by decoding a model's noise prediction to pixel space and re-encoding into the target latent space, then fusing predictions via weighted averaging (Composable-Diffusion style). It bridges resolution gaps by upsampling the predicted clean image (not the intermediate noisy latent) and re-noising, preserving the IID pixel assumption and avoiding the severe artifacts caused by bilinear upsampling of intermediate noise maps.

## Results

On the COCO English text-to-image benchmark, PanGu-Draw (5B) achieves a FID of 7.99, outperforming DALL-E 2 (10.39) and SDXL (11.93), and is the best among released models. On COCO-CN for Chinese T2I, it attains FID 21.81, IS 37.00, and CN-CLIP-score 36.62, leading all released Chinese T2I models. In a user study across 339 prompts, PanGu-Draw scores 4.58/5 on average (Align 4.50, Fidelity 4.52, Aesthetics 4.72), reaching approximately 99% of Midjourney 5.2 and 98% of DALL-E 3 performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On English T2I (COCO FID), PanGu-Draw (7.99) clearly beats all released baselines: DALL-E 2 (10.39), SDXL (11.93), PixArt-α (10.65), GLIDE (12.24), and LDM (12.63), with the largest margin over DALL-E (27.50). It trails only the unreleased Imagen (7.27) and RAPHAEL (6.61), making it the best publicly available model. On Chinese T2I (COCO-CN), PanGu-Draw dominates all three metrics over AltDiffusion (FID 25.31), Taiyi-Bilingual (24.61), and Taiyi-CN (23.99), with a 2.18 FID improvement over the closest competitor. In the user study, PanGu-Draw (avg 4.58) surpasses SDXL (4.46) and SD (4.12) across alignment, fidelity, and aesthetics, but falls slightly short of DALL-E 3 (4.69) and Midjourney 5.2 (4.64), achieving roughly 98–99% of their scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanGu-Draw builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and the SDXL VAE/U-Net design, while adopting the linear fusion mechanism from Composable-Diffusion (Liu et al., ECCV 2022) for multi-control blending. It departs from two dominant training paradigms: Cascaded Training (GLIDE, DeepFloyd), which chains multiple models at increasing resolutions, and Resolution Boost Training (Stable Diffusion, AltDiffusion), which refines a single model. The Time-Decoupling Training Strategy is conceptually related to eDiff-I's ensemble-of-experts idea of splitting denoising across time steps, but PanGu-Draw explicitly targets data and compute efficiency rather than generation quality alone, and the Coop-Diffusion algorithm addresses the latent-space and resolution incompatibility problem that no prior fusion method has tackled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 48% data-preparation reduction and 51% training-resource reduction are practically significant for groups without hyperscale compute, and the released 5B model on the Ascend platform makes the approach accessible. Coop-Diffusion's ability to fuse pre-trained models with different VAEs and resolutions without retraining opens a path toward composable image generation pipelines, though it has only been demonstrated with two models at a time and the ablation evidence is limited to a 1B-parameter subset. Open questions include whether the time-decoupling split generalizes to more than two generators, how Coop-Diffusion scales to three or more heterogeneous models, and whether the lower-resolution texture training strategy holds at substantially higher target resolutions (e.g., 2048+).
