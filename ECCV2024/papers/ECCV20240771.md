## Problem

Diffusion models for joint trajectory prediction in autonomous driving face two computational bottlenecks that prevent real-time deployment: the reverse diffusion process requires many denoising steps because it starts from a standard Gaussian prior far from the data distribution, and guided sampling for controllable generation demands expensive gradient backpropagation through the entire network at each step. This paper addresses both to enable efficient, high-quality joint trajectory prediction and controllable generation on limited onboard hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240771/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes scene context features (history, map, and neighboring-agent encodings extracted by a pre-trained QCNet encoder) along with diverse marginal trajectory samples and their likelihoods from a pre-trained marginal predictor, and optionally a user-specified guidance cost function (e.g., goal points at a target time). It outputs joint trajectory predictions for all n vehicles in the scene, represented as 120-dimensional trajectories in a 10-dimensional latent space, with 128 samples per scene. A key design choice is that the optimal Gaussian prior is computed analytically from marginal statistics at inference time rather than learned, enabling flexible tuning of diffusion steps without retraining.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mechanisms. First, Optimal Gaussian Diffusion (OGD) replaces the standard Gaussian prior with an analytically derived optimal Gaussian whose mean and covariance are closed-form functions of the data distribution's statistics (estimated from a pre-trained marginal predictor like QCNet), allowing the reverse diffusion to start from a much smaller noise level T (e.g., 40 vs. 500 steps) while achieving equal or better quality. Second, Estimated Clean Manifold (ECM) Guidance reformulates controllable generation as a lexicographic multi-objective optimization directly on the clean manifold: it iteratively injects noise and denoises to project samples onto the estimated clean manifold, then takes a single gradient step on the guidance cost at the clean estimate, eliminating the need for full-network backpropagation. The extended variant ECMR further warm-starts this optimization using reference joint trajectories assembled from marginal samples to navigate the multi-modal joint distribution and avoid local optima.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 Multi-world Forecasting leaderboard, OGD achieves avgMinFDE6* = 1.31, avgMinADE6* = 0.60, and avgBrierMinFDE6* = 1.95, ranking 4th overall and outperforming GNet, Forecast-MAE, and FJMP on all metrics. For joint trajectory prediction, OGD with only 40 diffusion steps (T=40) outperforms vanilla diffusion trained with T=500 on avgMinFDE128 (0.47 vs 0.48) and avgMinADE128 (0.77 vs 0.91), using roughly 1/12 of the reverse steps. For controllable generation, ECMR with 10 DDIM steps achieves minJFDE = 0.053 and meanJFDE = 0.146, compared to 0.538 and 2.339 for the best baseline (SF) with 50 steps, while also improving realism (minJRDE 0.110 vs 0.158). Inference time per step drops from 247 ms (SF) to 111–116 ms (ECM/ECMR), and GPU memory from 7.96 GB to ~3.2 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-diffusion joint prediction baselines on the Argoverse 2 leaderboard, OGD outperforms GNet (avgMinFDE6*: 1.31 vs 1.46), Forecast-MAE (1.31 vs 1.55), and FJMP (1.31 vs 1.89) on all reported metrics, but trails QCNext (1.31 vs 1.02), which is the strongest non-diffusion method listed. For controllable generation, OGD+ECMR dramatically outperforms both guided-sampling baselines: compared to NNM [49] (minJFDE 0.778, meanJFDE 2.913 with 50 DDIM steps) and SF [16,28] (minJFDE 0.538, meanJFDE 2.339 with 50 steps), ECMR achieves minJFDE 0.053 and meanJFDE 0.146 with only 10 steps, a ~10× reduction in guidance cost. ECMR also improves realism over SF (minJRDE 0.110 vs 0.158; meanJRDE 0.154 vs 0.500), indicating that avoiding backpropagation through the full network does not sacrifice sample quality. In terms of efficiency, ECM/ECMR run at ~111–116 ms per step with 3.2 GB GPU memory, versus 247 ms and 7.96 GB for SF, making them roughly 2× faster and 2.5× lighter in memory. The vanilla diffusion baselines (VD100, VD500) serve as internal ablations confirming that the optimal Gaussian prior, not architectural changes, drives the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM/VP-SDE diffusion framework and the score-based SDE formulation, but departs from the standard practice of initializing reverse diffusion from a non-informative standard Gaussian prior. OGD replaces this with an analytically derived informative Gaussian prior computed from marginal trajectory statistics, contrasting with prior truncated-diffusion work [48] that requires training an additional GAN and fixing the noise level at training time. On the guided-sampling side, ECM reformulates controllable generation as a lexicographic multi-objective optimization on the clean manifold, eliminating the costly backpropagation through the full diffusion network that prior approaches (MotionDiffuser, Trace and Pace, Guided Conditional Diffusion) require to project noisy samples onto the clean manifold before computing guidance gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/12 reduction in reverse diffusion steps and 1/5 reduction in controllable-generation inference time are practically significant for real-time autonomous driving deployment, where onboard compute is severely constrained. A key limitation is the dependence on the quality of pre-trained marginal trajectory predictors (QCNet) for estimating the data statistics that drive OGD; the block-diagonal covariance assumption also ignores inter-vehicle correlations. Open questions include whether the optimal prior can be learned end-to-end from joint statistics rather than decomposed marginals, and whether the ECM multi-objective formulation generalizes to guidance costs that are not easily differentiable on the clean manifold.
