## Problem

Existing multimodal agents either rely on end-to-end LMM training (which yields strong visual understanding but cannot easily incorporate diverse skills like segmentation or generation) or on prompt-based tool chaining (which is fragile and cannot reliably select and compose tools from a large, diverse toolset). No prior work combines end-to-end visual instruction tuning with learned tool use, and in existing tool-augmented LLMs the image is only consulted when a tool is activated rather than being actively engaged throughout the interaction.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image query, a natural-language instruction, and optionally visual prompts (user-drawn points, boxes, or sketches) as input. The LMM produces a structured prediction with three fields—Thought (reasoning about which tool to invoke), Action (a JSON list of API_name/API_params function calls), and Value (the natural-language response)—and this format is used for both skill-oriented dialogues (tool invocation) and user-oriented dialogues (final answer). During serving, only the Value field of the final response is shown to the user; the intermediate tool-call dialogues are hidden unless debugging.

## Method

LLaVA-Plus extends LLaVA with a skill repository of ~15 vision and vision-language specialist models (Grounding DINO, SAM, BLIP-2, Stable Diffusion, Instruct-Pix2Pix, EasyOCR, CLIP retrieval, etc.) and trains the LMM as a planner via visual instruction tuning on a newly curated multimodal tool-use dataset (~100K samples generated with GPT-4). The training data follows a four-step dialogue format: the user provides an image and instruction, the LMM outputs a Thought-Action-Value sequence selecting a tool and writing its prompt, the tool's execution result is fed back as a Human turn, and the LMM produces a final aggregated answer. Two serving modes are supported: 'All Tools' (all understanding tools run on the image and their outputs are prepended as symbolic context) and 'Fly' (only the tool selected by the LMM is invoked on the fly). The key architectural distinction from prior tool-augmented LLMs is that the raw image remains in the LMM's context throughout the entire multi-turn session, enabling the planner to ground its tool-selection decisions in visual evidence rather than relying solely on text descriptions.

## Results

On MM-VET, LLaVA-Plus-13B (All Tools, V1.3, 336px) achieves a total score of 35.0, outperforming LLaVA-13B (32.5) and LLaVA-1.5-13B (33.3), with the largest gains in OCR (29.4 vs 22.3) and Spatial (37.3 vs 31.3). On VisIT-Bench, LLaVA-Plus (13B) attains an ELO rating of 1203, surpassing LLaVA (1095) by over 100 ELO points and achieving a 35.07% win rate against human references versus LLaVA's 18.53%. On the newly introduced LLaVA-Bench (Tools), LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) scores 82.3 overall, substantially exceeding LLaVA (58.7), MM-REACT (76.5), and commercial systems like Bard (76.3) and Bing Chat (70.2). On SEED-Bench, LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) improves the average from 44.45 (LLaVA) to 55.01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-Plus consistently outperforms LLaVA [24] across all five benchmarks: +2.7 on MM-VET (35.0 vs 32.5 at 13B), +108 ELO on VisIT-Bench (1203 vs 1095), +23.6 on LLaVA-Bench (Tools) (82.3 vs 58.7), and +10.56 on SEED-Bench (55.01 vs 44.45). Against GPT4Tools [46], which trains an LLM (not LMM) for tool use, LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) leads by 11.8 points on LLaVA-Bench COCO (83.9 vs 72.1) and 28.8 on In-the-Wild (69.5 vs 40.7), demonstrating the importance of visual instruction tuning over pure text-based tool-use training. Compared to MM-ReAct [47] (a prompt-engineering tool-chaining system), LLaVA-Plus (All Tools) achieves 82.3 vs 76.5 on LLaVA-Bench (Tools) and 35.0 vs 44.6 on MM-VET; however, MM-ReAct-GPT-4 still leads on MM-VET overall due to GPT-4's superior language reasoning, though LLaVA-Plus wins on the 7B-scale comparison (27.5 vs 27.9 for MM-ReAct-GPT-3.5). Among other open-source LMMs on MM-VET, LLaVA-Plus-13B surpasses InstructBLIP-14B (35.0 vs 25.6), LLaMA-Adapter v2-7B (35.0 vs 31.4), Otter-9B (35.0 vs 24.6), MiniGPT-4-14B (35.0 vs 24.4), BLIP-2-12B (35.0 vs 22.4), and OpenFlamingo-9B (35.0 vs 21.8). On VisIT-Bench, LLaVA-Plus (ELO 1203) ranks first, ahead of LLaVA (1095), mPLUG-Owl (1087), LlamaAdapter-v2 (1066), InstructBLIP (1000), Otter (962), and MiniGPT-4 (926).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms in multimodal AI: end-to-end visual instruction tuning (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) and tool chaining via prompt engineering (MM-REACT, Visual ChatGPT, GPT4Tools). It adopts ReAct's Thought-Action-Value format as the unified prediction structure, but departs from prior tool-use work by using an LMM (not a text-only LLM) as the planner, keeping raw visual signals engaged throughout the entire interaction rather than only at tool-activation time. The 'Society of Mind' framing positions each specialist tool (Grounding DINO, SAM, Stable Diffusion, etc.) as a single-skill agent whose compositions yield emergent capabilities, a design philosophy distinct from both monolithic LMMs and ad-hoc prompt-chained tool pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LLaVA-Plus provides the first practical recipe for systematically expanding an LMM's capability set through learned tool use, making it a natural foundation for building general-purpose multimodal agents. The paper acknowledges that hallucinations and tool-use conflicts remain unresolved in practice, and the skill repository is currently hand-curated rather than automatically discoverable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of tools, how to handle conflicting tool outputs, and whether the GPT-4-generated instruction data introduces biases that limit generalization beyond the curated skill set.
