## Problem

Existing online vectorized HD map construction methods represent instances as polylines or parameterized curves that are inherently sensitive to the rotation and translation the ego vehicle undergoes, and their vanilla self-attention computes interactions between all token pairs without separating shape-specific from relation-specific geometry. As a result, common Euclidean properties of road systems—parallelism, perpendicularity, equal lane widths, rectangle shapes—remain under-exploited, limiting both precision and robustness to rigid transformations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (6 cameras on nuScenes, 7 on Argoverse 2) and optionally LiDAR point clouds, which are projected into a unified BEV feature map via a shared CNN backbone (ResNet-50 or VoVNetV2-99) and a GKT PV-to-BEV transformer. The output is a set of N vectorized map instances, each an ordered sequence of N_v 2D polyline points with a class label (pedestrian crossing, lane divider, or road boundary), plus auxiliary G-Representation signals (displacement-vector magnitudes and inter-vector angles) used only during training for Euclidean supervision.

## Method

GeMap introduces a G-Representation that decomposes each polyline into displacement vectors between consecutive points and further encodes them as magnitudes and angles, yielding translation- and rotation-invariant shape clues (intra-instance) and relation clues (inter-instance, via cross-pair angles and point-to-point distances). A Euclidean Loss applies L1 regression on these magnitudes and on sin/cos of angles (avoiding arctan), with a distance-based weight to down-weight weakly related instance pairs. To complement this supervision, the Transformer decoder replaces vanilla self-attention with Geometry-Decoupled Attention (GDA), which applies two masked attention passes per block: a shape mask that restricts attention to tokens within the same instance and a relation mask that restricts attention to tokens across different instances, allowing the model to learn shape geometry and inter-instance relations independently. The full pipeline—BEV encoder, GDA decoder with deformable cross-attention, and an MLP polyline head—is trained end-to-end with focal classification loss, point-to-point regression, edge-direction loss, segmentation loss, depth loss, and the proposed Euclidean loss.

## Results

On nuScenes with camera-only input and ResNet50 backbone, GeMap achieves 69.4% mAP, surpassing the previous best (MapTRv2 at 68.7%) by +0.7%. With the stronger VoVNetV2-99 backbone and dense prediction loss, GeMap reaches 76.0% mAP on nuScenes (+2.6% over MapTRv2). On the large-scale Argoverse 2 dataset, GeMap achieves a headline 71.8% mAP with ResNet50, outperforming MapTRv2 by +4.4% and surpassing the 70% mAP threshold for the first time. Inference speed is maintained at 13.8–15.8 FPS depending on configuration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeMap outperforms all seven compared baselines across both datasets. Against the closest competitor MapTRv2 [23], GeMap gains +0.7% mAP (camera-only R50), +1.4% (R50+LiDAR), +2.6% (V2-99) on nuScenes, and a substantial +4.4% on Argoverse 2 (71.8% vs. 67.4%). Over MapTR [22] (58.7% mAP, camera-only), GeMap improves by +10.7% mAP, and over VectorMapNet [26] (46.0%) by +23.4%. GeMap also surpasses MapVR [49] (63.5% with LiDAR) by +6.9% in the multi-modal setting, BeMapNet [36] (64.8% camera-only) by +4.6%, PivotNet [4] (57.4%) by +12.0%, and HDMapNet [18] (23.0% camera-only) by +46.4%. Notably, GeMap maintains or exceeds the inference speed of MapTRv2 (15.8 vs. 15.0 FPS in the camera-only dense-loss setting), indicating the geometric components add negligible computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeMap builds directly on the MapTR pipeline (BEV encoder via GKT + Transformer decoder with N×N_v fused queries + polyline prediction head) but departs from it by explicitly encoding Euclidean geometric properties—magnitudes of displacement vectors and angles between them—into both the loss function and the attention mechanism. Prior vectorized HD map methods (VectorMapNet, MapTR, BeMapNet, PivotNet) represent instances as polylines or Bezier curves and optimize point-to-point or edge-direction losses, leaving geometric invariances like parallelism, perpendicularity, and rectangle shapes implicit. GeMap's G-Representation and Geometry-Decoupled Attention make these invariances explicit and structurally enforced, which is a conceptual shift from the coordinate-regression paradigm that dominates the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric invariance framing offers a principled explanation for why road-system geometry (parallel lanes, right-angle crossings, uniform widths) should be first-class in map construction, and the decoupled attention mechanism is a lightweight, generalizable modification that could transfer to other structured-prediction tasks. The paper acknowledges that its geometric modeling remains 'fundamental'—limited to pairwise angles and magnitudes—and leaves open questions about richer geometric priors (e.g., higher-order shape constraints, topological relations) and whether the approach extends to dynamic or 3D map elements. The 70%+ mAP milestone on Argoverse 2 is a useful reference point, but the broader impact hinges on whether the GDA/Euclidean-Loss recipe proves transferable beyond the specific HD map construction setting.
