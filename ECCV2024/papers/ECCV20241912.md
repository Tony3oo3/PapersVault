## Problem

Source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) methods rely on nearest-neighbor-based pseudolabeling, which is inherently biased toward majority classes when a class distribution shift (CDS) exists between source and target domains. The only prior SFDA-under-CDS work (ISFDA) circumvents this by resampling the source data to a uniform distribution, violating the strict SFDA constraint that the source model's training cannot be manipulated. This paper identifies the nearest-neighbor calculation itself as the root cause of CDS-induced bias, rather than the logits-level predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target RGB images, a frozen source model (ResNet-101/50 feature extractor + classifier trained on the source domain), and a frozen generic pretrained model (ResNet, ViT-B/32, or Swin-B/4 from ImageNet-1K/21k). The generic model's feature bank is computed once at the start of adaptation, while the source model's feature bank is updated every training iteration. Output is an adapted source model (in SFDA mode) or per-sample class predictions (in TTA mode), with no additional trainable parameters introduced.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the standard k-nearest neighbors (k=10) in the source model's feature space with 'robust nearest neighbors' defined as the intersection of the source k-NN with K-nearest neighbors (K=100) in the generic model's feature space. A conservative selection rule prioritizes source neighbors when the intersection is smaller than k, filling remaining slots from the source-only neighbors. These robust neighbors are then used for soft-voting pseudolabeling, and the source model is adapted via standard cross-entropy loss. The generic model remains entirely frozen throughout, acting as a CDS-free 'second opinion' in a knowledge-distillation-like fashion. Because no extra modules or loss terms are added, the same robust-NN pseudolabeling can be applied at inference time for test-time adaptation without any retraining.

## Results

On VisDA-C RSUT (the primary SFDA-CDS benchmark), the proposed method with Swin-B as the generic model achieves 88.84% class-wise average accuracy with guided pseudolabeling, improving over the PL guided baseline (83.59%) by +5.25 points and over the previous SFDA-under-CDS SOTA ISFDA (76.69%) by +12.15 points. On Office-Home RSUT, PL guided + Swin-B reaches 69.04% average, surpassing ISFDA (65.36%) and PL guided alone (61.05%). On DomainNet (naturally imbalanced), PL guided + ViT-B achieves 83.9% average, outperforming ISFDA (79.58%) and PL guided (80.12%). In the TTA setting, the method outperforms all TTA baselines on Office-Home (60.16% vs. 52.78% for TENT+Adapter) and DomainNet (73.23% vs. 71.63%), though it trails the label-shift adapter [27] on VisDA-C (52.49% vs. 72.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all SFDA and SFDA-under-CDS baselines across all three datasets. The most direct competitor, ISFDA [20] (the only prior SFDA-under-CDS method), is beaten by +12.15 points on VisDA-C RSUT (88.84% vs. 76.69%), +3.68 on Office-Home RSUT (69.04% vs. 65.36%), and +4.32 on DomainNet (83.9% vs. 79.58%). The SFDA SOTA PL guided [22] is improved by +5.25 points on VisDA-C and +7.99 on Office-Home when augmented with Swin-B robust neighbors. UDA baselines (PADA, MCD, BSP, COAL, MDD, SHOT) are all substantially outperformed, confirming that source-free methods with robust NN are superior when source data is unavailable. In the TTA setting, the method beats all standard TTA baselines (ONDA, LAME, CoTTA, NOTE, TENT) on all three datasets, and also surpasses the TTA-under-CDS SOTA (label-shift adapter [27]) on Office-Home (+7.38 points) and DomainNet (+1.60 points), though it loses on VisDA-C (52.49% vs. 72.97%) where the adapter's explicit label-shift modeling provides an advantage. Notably, the method achieves these gains without modifying the source model's training or adding learnable modules, strictly adhering to the SFDA-CDS constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the guided pseudolabels SFDA framework [22], which uses nearest neighbors in the source model's feature space for pseudolabeling, and on the ISFDA line of work [20] that first identified CDS as a challenge in SFDA. The key departure is conceptual: rather than treating CDS as noise at the logits level (as [20] does) or relaxing SFDA constraints by resampling source data, the authors identify the nearest neighbors calculation itself as the fundamental bottleneck and introduce a frozen generic feature extractor as a 'second opinion' to filter biased neighbors. This positions the work at the intersection of SFDA, knowledge distillation from pretrained models [41,42], and robust NN under imbalance, while strictly adhering to the no-source-data, no-source-manipulation setting that prior CDS-SFDA work had to relax.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SFDA method to handle class distribution shift in both source and target without any manipulation of source model training, addressing a gap that made prior CDS-SFDA results somewhat unrealistic. The practical takeaway—that a simple intersection of two NN sets from different feature spaces can outperform more complex logits-level corrections—offers a lightweight, plug-and-play improvement applicable to both SFDA and TTA pipelines. Limitations include the need for an additional (frozen) model at inference, the dependence on the generic model being stronger than the source model (ResNet50 actually degrades DomainNet performance), and the open question of how the method scales to multiple generic models or large foundation models. The paper also leaves open whether the K=100 hyperparameter generalizes across domains or requires per-dataset tuning.
