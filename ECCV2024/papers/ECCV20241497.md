## Problem

Existing LiDAR point cloud generation methods are limited to unconditional synthesis, with no framework supporting text-controllable generation. The dominant U-Net convolutional denoising architecture is ill-suited to equirectangular images due to their circular topology and elongated geometry, and no high-quality paired text-LiDAR dataset exists to train such a system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241497/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a noised equirectangular range image (64×1024×2 for 64-beam KITTI-360, 32×1024×2 for 32-beam nuScenes) along with a CLIP text embedding and timestep embedding, and outputs the predicted noise at each diffusion step; iterative denoising from t=1 to t=0 yields a full LiDAR scan. For densification, a sparsified LiDAR point cloud (50–90% beams or points removed) serves as the conditioning input. The equirectangular representation encodes depth and intensity per (elevation, azimuth) bin, preserving the native LiDAR geometry.

## Method

Text2LiDAR is a DDPM denoiser built on a custom equirectangular transformer with four key components. Equirectangular Attention (EA) replaces convolution with self-attention to respect the circular topology, injects Fourier features of elevation/azimuth to preserve 3D positional priors, and applies mutually overlapping unfolding at multiple scales to handle the extreme aspect-ratio distortion of range images. A Control-signal Embedding Injector (CEI) fuses text and timestep embeddings via a global-to-focused mechanism: dominant features, text, and timestep tokens are concatenated for self-attention, then split into Q/K/V streams for cross-attention, preserving the denoising signal while injecting semantic guidance. A Frequency Modulator (FM) applies DWT, learned frequency gating, and IDWT to counteract the smoothing tendency of MLP layers and recover high-frequency point-cloud boundaries. The full model is trained end-to-end with MSE noise-prediction loss, and the authors additionally construct nuLiDARtext (34,149 manually corrected text-LiDAR pairs across 850 nuScenes scenes) to provide the paired supervision needed for text-controlled training.

## Results

On KITTI-360, Text2LiDAR achieves the best FPD (4.81), MMD (0.49×10⁻⁴), and JSD (2.01×10⁻²) among all compared methods, outperforming the closest competitor R2DM (6.24, 1.91×10⁻⁴, 3.05×10⁻²). R2DM retains a slight edge on FRD (149.66 vs. 164.16), but the gap is small relative to the large margins over GAN/VAE baselines. For LiDAR densification (90% point removal), Text2LiDAR also outperforms R2DM on all metrics: depth MAE 0.035 vs. 0.039, intensity MAE 0.073 vs. 0.090, and semantic IoU 41.37% vs. 40.16%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN/VAE baselines (LiDARGAN, LiDARVAE [4], ProjectedGAN [59]), Text2LiDAR achieves dramatic improvements: FPD drops from unavailable to 4.81, FRD from 2117–3004 to 164, and JSD from 8.5–16.1×10⁻² to 2.01×10⁻², confirming that diffusion-based generation substantially outperforms adversarial and variational approaches for LiDAR synthesis. LiDARGen [89] (NCSNv2-based) is also far behind, with FPD 90.29 vs. 4.81 and JSD 7.38 vs. 2.01×10⁻², highlighting the advantage of the DDPM formulation with the equirectangular transformer. The closest competitor is R2DM [50], which uses an Efficient U-Net denoiser; Text2LiDAR wins on FPD (4.81 vs. 6.24, −23%), MMD (0.49 vs. 1.91×10⁻⁴, −74%), and JSD (2.01 vs. 3.05×10⁻², −34%), while R2DM retains a narrow FRD advantage (149.66 vs. 164.16, ~10% lower). For the densification task, Text2LiDAR also beats R2DM on every metric (depth MAE, RMSE; intensity MAE, RMSE; semantic IoU), with the largest relative gain in intensity MAE (0.073 vs. 0.090, −19%). Overall, Text2LiDAR is the only method enabling text-controlled generation, a capability none of the four baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based LiDAR generation line initiated by LiDARGen and R2DM, which both rely on U-Net denoisers operating on equirectangular images. It departs from this lineage by replacing the convolutional backbone with a purpose-built equirectangular transformer (EA/REA) that explicitly models the circular topology, 3D positional priors via Fourier features, and multi-scale deformations inherent to equirectangular projections. The text-control capability is borrowed conceptually from CLIP-conditioned image/video diffusion (e.g., Imagen, DALL-E), but the Control-Signal Embedding Injector is a novel fusion mechanism designed to handle the modality gap between CLIP text tokens and LiDAR range-image features. The densification task builds directly on Repaint's inpainting formulation applied to LiDAR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to demonstrate text-controllable LiDAR generation, this work opens a practical pathway for synthesizing scenario-specific sensor data (e.g., rainy-day, pedestrian-dense) without expensive real-world collection, which is directly relevant to autonomous driving testing and simulation. The nuLiDARtext dataset (34K pairs) is a tangible resource that the field lacked, though its text diversity is bounded by the nuScenes annotation vocabulary. Key open questions include whether the equirectangular transformer generalizes to higher-resolution or multi-sensor LiDAR, whether single-frame text control can be extended to temporally consistent sequences, and whether the manually curated text descriptions scale beyond 850 scenes without prohibitive annotation cost.
