## Problem

Existing trajectory-based motion control methods (DragNUWA, MotionCtrl) represent entities by single pixel points or pixel regions, which cannot capture the semantic identity of an object. The paper demonstrates through a toy experiment that dragging a pixel on a cloud moves the camera rather than the cloud, and that pixels near the drag point receive disproportionate influence instead of the object moving as a coherent whole. This pixel-level paradigm fails to achieve true entity-level motion control, especially for amorphous backgrounds like starry skies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a first-frame RGB image (320×576), an entity mask obtained via SAM, and a user-drawn trajectory (or Co-Tracker-predicted trajectory during training) as input, and produces a 25-frame video at 320×576 with the selected entity following the specified motion path. During training, ground-truth trajectories are generated by extracting each entity's incircle center from VIPSeg masks and tracking it with Co-Tracker across 14 frames at 256×256. The entity representation is extracted via a single forward pass of the diffusion U-Net on the inverted latent of the first frame, indexed by mask coordinates and average-pooled into a fixed-size embedding per entity.

## Method

DragAnything introduces an Entity Representation that extracts latent diffusion features from the first frame at entity-mask coordinates, average-pools them into per-entity embeddings, and inserts these embeddings into a spatially-indexed tensor at each frame's trajectory position. This entity embedding is combined with a 2D Gaussian representation (centered on the trajectory point with radius from the entity's incircle) to focus influence on the central region. Both representations are encoded by a 4-block convolutional encoder (1/8 resolution output) and added to the video latent noise in a ControlNet-style fashion, conditioning the SVD 3D U-Net denoiser. Training uses MSE loss masked to entity regions only, so non-target areas are unaffected. The key architectural difference from prior work is that the conditioning signal carries semantic identity (the diffusion latent feature) rather than merely spatial coordinates, enabling the model to move the entire entity coherently regardless of which pixel the user drags.

## Results

On the VIPSeg validation set (256×256, 14 frames), DragAnything achieves ObjMC of 305.7, FVD of 494.8, and FID of 33.5, outperforming DragNUWA (324.6, 519.3, 39.8) on all three metrics. In the user study, DragAnything surpasses DragNUWA by 26% in human voting for motion control and by 12% for video quality. Ablation studies confirm that both the Entity Representation and 2D Gaussian Representation contribute substantially to ObjMC (92.3 and 71.4 improvement respectively), with the combined representation yielding the best result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares exclusively against DragNUWA [48], the closest prior trajectory-based method built on the same SVD backbone. DragAnything wins on all three automatic metrics: ObjMC improves by 18.9 (305.7 vs. 324.6), FVD by 24.5 (494.8 vs. 519.3), and FID by 6.3 (33.5 vs. 39.8). In the user study, DragAnything is preferred over DragNUWA by 26% for motion control accuracy and 12% for video quality. The authors note that other relevant baselines (e.g., MotionCtrl [42]) could not be included because they did not release source code compatible with the SVD architecture, limiting the breadth of quantitative comparison to a single baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DragAnything builds directly on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as its denoising backbone and adapts the ControlNet-style conditioning architecture to inject motion guidance into the 3D U-Net. It departs from prior trajectory-based motion control methods (DragNUWA, MotionCtrl) that encode sparse strokes or point coordinates into flow/vector maps, arguing that a single pixel point cannot semantically represent an entire entity. The key conceptual departure is the 'entity representation': extracting latent diffusion features at mask-indexed coordinates to form a semantic embedding of the object, combined with a 2D Gaussian for spatial focus. This draws on the broader observation (AnyDoor, VideoSwap, DIFT) that diffusion-model latent features carry rich object-level semantics, but repurposes them specifically for motion control rather than customization or editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entity-level framing enables control of amorphous backgrounds (clouds, starry skies) and simultaneous multi-object motion, capabilities that box-based or single-point methods structurally lack. However, the experimental validation is narrow: a single dataset (VIPSeg), a single numerical baseline (DragNUWA; MotionCtrl's SVD code was unavailable), and evaluation at 256×256 resolution with 14 frames, limiting confidence in scalability. Open questions include whether the entity representation generalizes to longer videos, higher resolutions, and more complex multi-entity scenes, and whether the diffusion-inversion step for feature extraction remains practical at scale. The technique of using frozen diffusion latent features as open-domain entity embeddings is a reusable idea that could transfer to other controllable generation settings.
