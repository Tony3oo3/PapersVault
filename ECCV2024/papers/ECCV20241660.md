## Problem

In Semi-Supervised Temporal Action Localization, noisy pseudo labels remain a key bottleneck: existing methods assess classification confidence and localization quality separately (e.g., learned class scores combined with manually computed boundary variance), ignoring their interdependence and producing suboptimal pseudo-label rankings. Moreover, these methods cannot identify false positives among selected high-scoring instances or recover false negatives from low-scoring candidates, limiting the exploitation of unlabeled data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241660/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted video features (I3D or TSN, pretrained on Kinetics) of shape D×T for each untrimmed video, partitioned into a small labeled set and a large unlabeled set (10%–60% labeled). A fixed number of frames is sampled per video for training. The model outputs per-frame action category scores and temporal boundary offsets (start/end times), from which action instances are extracted via Soft-NMS and the proposed adaptive selection pipeline.

## Method

The APL framework builds on ActionFormer (or BMN) and introduces three components. First, ALQA adds two parallel branches to the regression head that learn to predict tIoU and tND (the two terms of the DIoU decomposition) as reliability indicators, then computes a joint score as the element-wise product of this localization reliability and the classification score, with a DIoU-based soft label replacing one-hot targets to couple the two sub-tasks. Second, the ICD is a small MLP discriminator trained on labeled instances to predict pairwise similarity; at inference it scores each predicted instance against all labeled instances of the same category, enabling removal of ambiguous positives (high joint score, low similarity) and recovery of potential positives (low joint score, high similarity). Third, ACP is an unsupervised pre-training stage using K-means clustering to generate pseudo action/background labels, then applying coarse-grained (binary action-vs-background) and fine-grained (multi-class inter-action) infoNCE contrasts on FPN multi-scale features to improve frame-level discriminability.

## Results

On THUMOS14 with 20% labeled data, APL(ActionFormer) achieves 42.8% average mAP, surpassing the closest competitor NPL(ActionFormer) by 5.0% absolute (37.8%). With 10% labeled data, APL reaches 24.5% average mAP on THUMOS14 and 33.5% on ActivityNet v1.3, outperforming NPL by 4.2% and 1.0% respectively. The method also shows strong gains at high tIoU thresholds, improving mAP@0.95 on ActivityNet by 3.1–3.6% over NPL across labeling ratios. When combined with the two-stage BMN detector (TSN features), APL achieves 33.3% average mAP on THUMOS14 at 10% labels, exceeding SPOT by 2.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APL consistently outperforms NPL [46], the most recent SS-TAL method, across all labeling ratios on both datasets: on THUMOS14 (I3D), the gap ranges from +4.2% (10% labels) to +2.1% (60% labels) in average mAP, with the largest absolute gain of +5.0% at 20% labels (42.8% vs 37.8%). Against SPOT [26] (TSN backbone), APL(BMN) achieves +2.0% average mAP on THUMOS14 at 10% labels (33.3% vs 31.3%) and +2.2% at 20% (36.6% vs 34.4%). On ActivityNet v1.3, APL(ActF) leads NPL(ActF) by +1.0% at 10% labels and +0.2% at 60% labels, with the most notable margin at mAP@0.95 (+3.1% to +3.6%). Compared to the supervised-only ActionFormer [52], APL(ActF) gains +8.9% average mAP on THUMOS14 at 10% labels (24.5% vs 15.6%), demonstrating the value of semi-supervised learning. Against older SS-TAL methods SSP [11] and SSTAP [43], APL(BMN) leads by +7.5% and +6.3% average mAP on THUMOS14 at 10% labels respectively, confirming that the adaptive pseudo-label selection strategy substantially improves over earlier SSL-for-TAL pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SS-TAL line initiated by SPOT (proposal-free masking) and most directly by NPL, which introduced adaptive pseudo-label reranking using learned class scores combined with manually computed boundary variance. The key departure is replacing NPL's decoupled, hand-crafted location score with an end-to-end ALQA module that jointly learns classification confidence and localization reliability via two parallel tIoU/tND prediction branches derived from the DIoU decomposition. The ICD module introduces an instance-level consistency discriminator for pseudo-label refinement, and the ACP pre-training generalizes the self-supervised pretext task in SPOT into a simpler, backbone-agnostic contrastive objective with both coarse- and fine-grained contrasts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint learning paradigm for pseudo-label quality assessment addresses a fundamental weakness in SS-TAL where classification and localization errors compound, and the ICD's ability to recover potential positives from the candidate pool (not just filter negatives) opens a path toward more complete exploitation of unlabeled data. The ACP, being unsupervised and backbone-agnostic, could serve as a drop-in pre-training component for other TAL or SSL methods. Open questions include whether the fixed ICD thresholds (τ_icd=0.3, ζ_icd=0.7) generalize across datasets and labeling ratios, and whether the framework's gains persist when applied to newer TAL backbones beyond ActionFormer and BMN.
