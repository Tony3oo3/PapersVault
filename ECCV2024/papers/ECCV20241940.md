## Problem

Prototypical learning methods for point cloud few-shot semantic segmentation rely on direct point-level matching between support prototypes and query points, which is fragile to scene discrepancies. This leads to two failure modes: incorrect background activation (superficial local similarities cause false matches) and incomplete foreground mining (intra-class diversity in scale and pose prevents support prototypes from providing sufficient cues for full target excavation).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241940/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in an episodic N-way K-shot paradigm, receiving K support point clouds with binary masks and one query point cloud per episode. Points are extracted from 1m×1m spatial blocks with 2048 points randomly sampled per block. The output is a per-point segmentation mask for the query, produced via multi-prototype transductive inference after the decoupled localization and expansion pipeline generates a refined pseudo-mask.

## Method

DLE decouples segmentation into two stages. The Structural Localization Module (SLM) replaces fragile point-level matching with distribution-level matching: a set of semantically-aware agents (initialized via farthest-point sampling and K-means on support foreground features, then adapted to query context via cross-attention) serves as reference features, and similarity is computed between the agent-correlation vectors of the support prototype and each query point, with a high threshold (τ=0.7) selecting confident target regions. The Self-Expansion Module (SEM) then expands this partial region using intra-object similarity: a target indicator derived from the confident region's features (via masked average pooling) guides expansion, and a conservative bidirectional confirmation strategy retains only newly expanded points whose most similar counterpart falls within the original confident region. Background is handled with K-means prototypes adapted via mask cross-attention, and a self-loss penalizes false-positive background activations within the expanded region.

## Results

On S3DIS, DLE achieves 77.67% mean-IoU in the 1-way 1-shot setting and 62.46% in the 2-way 1-shot setting, surpassing the strongest baseline AttMPTI+QGE by 1.71% and 2.89% respectively. On the more challenging ScanNet dataset, DLE reaches 62.33% (1-way 1-shot) and 47.97% (2-way 1-shot), outperforming AttMPTI+QGE by 1.97% and 3.02%. The improvements are most pronounced in the 1-shot regime, where support information is scarcest and intra-class diversity is most impactful.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLE outperforms all four baseline families across every setting on both S3DIS and ScanNet. Against the prototypical baseline ProtoNet [22], DLE gains 11.05% mIoU on S3DIS 1-way 1-shot (77.67 vs. 66.62) and 5.43% on ScanNet 1-way 1-shot (62.33 vs. 56.90). The transductive inference baselines MPTI and AttMPTI [36] are stronger, with AttMPTI reaching 67.84% on S3DIS 1-way 1-shot, but DLE still leads by 9.83%. The most competitive comparison is against AttMPTI+QGE [18], which incorporates query-guided enhancement; DLE surpasses it by 1.71% (S3DIS 1-way 1-shot) and 3.02% (ScanNet 2-way 1-shot), with the gap widening in 2-way settings where cross-class confusion is more severe. SCAT [33], a stratified attention transformer, trails DLE by 7.71% on S3DIS 1-way 1-shot, confirming that the distribution-level matching paradigm offers a substantial advantage over point-level attention-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the prototypical learning paradigm for PC-FSS established by Zhao et al. (AttMPTI, CVPR 2021), which introduced attention-aware multi-prototype transductive inference for 3D point clouds, and on the query-guided enhancement (QGE) line of work that adapts support prototypes to query context. It departs from these approaches by replacing fragile point-level cosine similarity matching with a distribution-level matching scheme mediated by semantically-aware agents, and by decoupling the segmentation problem into a localization step (SLM) and an expansion step (SEM) that exploits intra-object similarity within the query itself. The mask cross-attention mechanism for background adaptation borrows from Mask2Former (Cheng et al., CVPR 2022), and the feature backbone follows DGCNN as in prior PC-FSS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled localization-and-expansion framing offers a clean conceptual decomposition that addresses two distinct failure modes (background false positives and foreground incompleteness) separately, which prior methods attempted to handle jointly. The agent-based distribution-level matching is a lightweight yet effective alternative to query-aware prototype adaptation, and the conservative bidirectional expansion check is a practical trick that could transfer to other few-shot segmentation settings. However, the method is evaluated only on two indoor benchmarks (S3DIS, ScanNet) with a DGCNN backbone, leaving open questions about scalability to outdoor scenes, larger category sets, and transformer-based backbones. The reliance on hand-tuned thresholds (τ=0.7, θ=0.8) and a fixed number of agents (N_a=100) also limits generalizability.
