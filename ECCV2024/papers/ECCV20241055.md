## Problem

Global SfM rotation averaging typically operates on full 3-DoF rotations, ignoring the gravity direction that is readily available from IMUs in modern consumer devices. While planar PGO methods like LAGO exploit this to reduce the problem to 1-DoF, they fix angular periods heuristically from a coarse initialization, making them fragile to outliers and error-prone initialization. No principled, robust formulation existed for gravity-aligned rotation averaging that handles both full and partial gravity availability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy relative rotations between image pairs (estimated via LO-RANSAC on 5-DoF essential matrices), per-image gravity directions (from IMU or vertical vanishing points), and the pose graph edge connectivity. It outputs a single yaw angle θ_i per camera around the gravity-aligned y-axis; combined with the pre-alignment rotation U_i, this yields the full 3D camera orientation. When only a subset of cameras have gravity, the method augments the 1-DoF variables with angle-axis parameters for the remaining cameras in a mixed linear system.

## Method

The key insight is to pre-align each camera so gravity lies along the y-axis, reducing each rotation to a single angle θ_i and turning the relative-rotation constraint into a 1-DoF angular equation with a 2kπ periodicity ambiguity. Rather than fixing the integer periods k_ij from a coarse initialization (as LAGO does), the paper formulates the problem via circular regression motivated by the von Mises distribution, and solves it with an alternating procedure: (1) solve a linear least-squares system with k_ij fixed, then (2) re-select k_ij to minimize the wrapped residual. This iteration is guaranteed to monotonically decrease the total error and terminates in finitely many steps, inheriting convergence guarantees from the underlying linear solver. Robustness to outliers is achieved through a two-stage strategy (L1 then Geman-McClure re-weighting). For partially known gravity, cameras without IMU data are represented in angle-axis form and coupled to gravity-known cameras through a stratified mixed linear system, and a majority-voting mechanism identifies and refines error-prone gravity directions using neighbor relative poses.

## Results

On KITTI, the proposed method achieves 0.39° average rotation error and AUC@1° of 69.79, outperforming Theia (0.68°, 47.48) by 22 AUC points while running 8× faster. On EuRoC, it reaches 0.64° average error and AUC@1° of 54.81, closely approaching the gravity upper bound. On the partially-known-gravity LaMAR dataset (25% of images with gravity), it achieves 2.93° average error and AUC@1° of 23.17, outperforming Theia-based methods while running 1.5× faster. On 1DSfM with noisy gravity (0.5° std), it attains 1.54° average error and AUC@1° of 29.41, roughly 15× faster than Theia-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Theia [68] (the standard global SfM rotation averaging), the proposed method wins decisively on all four datasets: on KITTI it gains 22 AUC@1° points (69.79 vs 47.48) while running 8× faster, and on EuRoC it gains 11 points (54.81 vs 43.85). Against Theia^reg [20], which adds a gravity penalty term to the 3-DoF optimization, the proposed method matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (e.g., 69.79 vs 64.85 AUC@1° on KITTI) while being 30× faster, since it solves a 1-DoF problem directly rather than a 3-DoF one with regularization. Against LAGO [11] and CPL-Sync [27] (planar PGO methods), the proposed method achieves substantially higher accuracy—on KITTI, 69.79 vs 27.15 and 31.08 AUC@1° respectively—because its iterative period correction handles outliers that the fixed-period approaches cannot. Against COLMAP [62] (incremental SfM), the proposed method is both more accurate (0.39° vs 3.76° avg error on KITTI) and orders of magnitude faster (seconds vs hours). In the SLAM comparison (Table 3), the proposed method is the most accurate on EuRoC (2.16° RMS on V1_01 vs 4.70° for ORB-SLAM2) and competitive on KITTI, despite SLAM systems also leveraging IMU data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the observation by Fraundorfer et al. that a known gravity direction reduces camera orientation to a single degree of freedom, and on the iterative angular regression framework of Gould's Barber's pole model. It departs from prior planar PGO methods (LAGO, CPL-Sync) that fix period assignments heuristically and from Theia-based approaches that treat gravity as an auxiliary penalty term in 3-DoF optimization. The key conceptual move is to formulate gravity-aligned rotation averaging as a circular regression problem solved by alternating period assignment and linear least-squares, inheriting convergence guarantees from the underlying linear solver while remaining robust to outliers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making gravity a structural simplification rather than a soft constraint, the method achieves large accuracy gains (23 AUC@1° over standard planar PGO) at a fraction of the runtime, and is already integrated into COLMAP's glomap pipeline, suggesting broad practical adoption. The partial-gravity extension and gravity refinement mechanism address real-world heterogeneity in multi-device capture. Open questions include whether the approach extends to scenarios without any IMU (e.g., purely visual gravity estimation from vanishing points at scale) and whether the iterative period-assignment strategy can be made globally optimal for very sparse or disconnected pose graphs.
