## Problem

Solving Electromagnetic Inverse Scattering Problems (EISP) faces two intertwined challenges: the inherent nonlinearity and multiple-scattering effects make inverse estimation of the scatterer's relative permittivity from measured fields extremely difficult, and the necessary discretization of continuous spaces into finite grids inherently limits imaging resolution. Existing approaches either split the problem into a coarse traditional inversion followed by deep-learning refinement (losing physical data in the second stage) or optimize discrete matrix representations that cannot escape the low-resolution curse.

## Input / Output

The method consumes scattered electromagnetic fields measured by receivers (32 for standard, 8 for sparse settings) along with known incident fields from transmitters (16 for synthetic, 8–18 for real-world), all generated via the method of moments on a 224×224 grid mesh to avoid inverse crime. The output is the continuous relative permittivity distribution ε_r(x) of the scatterer, queryable at arbitrary spatial resolution despite training on a fixed 64×64 discretization of the ROI. The forward simulation internally operates on the 64×64 discrete grid, but the implicit representation decouples the final imaging resolution from this computational grid.

## Method

The paper represents the scatterer's relative permittivity ε_r(x) and induced current J(x, x^t) as two separate 8-layer MLPs (256 channels, ReLU) with positional encoding, and optimizes them within a forward estimation loop rather than performing inverse estimation. The data loss compares scattered fields predicted from the induced-current MLP (via the discrete Green's function) against measured fields, while the state loss enforces consistency between the directly queried induced current and the current computed from the permittivity MLP through the Lippmann-Schwinger state equation, thereby coupling both networks. A random Gaussian sampling scheme around 64×64 grid centers ensures comprehensive spatial coverage during optimization. This two-MLP design avoids the expensive matrix inversion that would be required in the explicit forward equation, reducing per-iteration cost from 289 ms to 117 ms while improving reconstruction quality.

## Results

On the Circular-cylinder dataset (5% noise), the proposed method achieves RRMSE 0.016, SSIM 0.968, and PSNR 39.20, outperforming the best baseline (BPS) by 41% in RRMSE and 5.0 dB in PSNR. On the MNIST dataset (5% noise), it reaches RRMSE 0.017, SSIM 0.972, and PSNR 37.90, surpassing the next-best method (Physics-Net) by 78% in RRMSE and 10.2 dB in PSNR. On the real-world Institut Fresnel's database, the method achieves RRMSE 0.127, SSIM 0.897, and PSNR 27.06, maintaining a consistent lead over all seven baselines across every evaluation setting including 30% noise and sparse measurement conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (RRMSE, SSIM, PSNR) across all five evaluation settings. Against the strongest traditional method Gs SOM, the gap is most pronounced on MNIST (5% noise): RRMSE 0.017 vs. 0.101 (78% reduction) and PSNR 37.90 vs. 24.63 (+13.3 dB). Among deep learning baselines, Physics-Net is the closest competitor on MNIST (5% noise) with RRMSE 0.079, yet the proposed method still achieves a 78% lower error. On the real-world Institut Fresnel's database, the proposed method leads Gs SOM (the best baseline) by 6% in RRMSE (0.127 vs. 0.135) and 2.1 dB in PSNR (27.06 vs. 24.95), while all CNN-based methods (BPS, CS-Net, Physics-Net, PGAN) degrade substantially on real data, with PSNR dropping to 18–25 dB. Under 30% noise, the advantage widens further: on MNIST the proposed method achieves PSNR 29.70 versus 23.73 for Gs SOM and 24.44 for Physics-Net, confirming superior noise robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Implicit Neural Representation (INR) paradigm from NeRF and related work to represent the scatterer's relative permittivity as a continuous coordinate-to-value function, departing from the discrete matrix formulations used by traditional EISP solvers (SOM, CSI, DBIM) and the two-stage CNN pipelines (BPS, CS-Net, Physics-Net, PGAN). The key conceptual departure is reframing EISP as a forward-estimation problem—optimizing a learnable representation so that forward computation of scattered fields matches measurements—rather than attempting direct inverse estimation. Architecturally, the use of two separate MLPs (one for permittivity, one for induced current) to sidestep the matrix inversion in the Lippmann-Schwinger equation is a novel design choice not present in prior INR or EISP work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the discretization bottleneck inherent to grid-based representations, the INR formulation enables resolution-flexible imaging and improved handling of sparse measurements, addressing two long-standing practical limitations of EISP. The forward-estimation framing also sidesteps the ill-posedness and nonlinearity of direct inversion, which has plagued iterative methods sensitive to initialization. Open questions include whether the per-object optimization cost (4K iterations on a V100) is acceptable for real-time or clinical deployment, whether the 64×64 forward-computation grid still limits ultimate resolution despite the continuous representation, and how the approach scales to more complex multi-frequency or 3D clinical scenarios.
