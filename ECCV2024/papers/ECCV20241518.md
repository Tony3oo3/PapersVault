## Problem

Existing one-step natural image matting models inevitably produce artifacts on difficult cases and lack a mechanism to iteratively correct their own errors. While iterative refinement has proven effective in segmentation, it cannot be directly transferred to matting because matting relies on trimaps for guidance rather than coarse masks, and there is no reliable way to convert a predicted alpha matte back into a usable trimap for the next iteration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241518/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 4-channel input formed by concatenating an RGB image with its trimap (c = Concat(Image, Trimap)), supporting high-resolution inputs up to 2048×2048. It produces a single-channel alpha matte in [0,1] through a multi-step denoising process that starts from pure Gaussian noise and converges over T iterations (T is freely configurable at inference, e.g., 1–10 steps). The diffusion decoder is designed as a universal plug-in that can be attached to any matting encoder (ResNet-34, Swin-Tiny, ViT-S, ViT-B) without modifying the encoder.

## Method

DiffMatte decouples the matting pipeline into a heavy encoder B (run once to produce context features F) and a lightweight symmetric UNet-like diffusion decoder D (5.3M parameters, run iteratively) that receives the noisy alpha sample X_t, the condition c, and F to predict the denoised matte at each step. The forward diffusion process adds Gaussian noise to the ground-truth alpha matte with a linear noise schedule and input scaling factor b=0.2, while the reverse process uses DDIM sampling to iteratively refine the prediction. To combat sampling drift—the mismatch between clean training samples and error-containing inference samples—the paper introduces a UTI (Uniform Time Intervals) self-aligned strategy: during later training stages, a frozen model generates an intermediate prediction X̂_{0|t+δ} that replaces the ground truth, forcing the network to learn from noisy guidance across all time steps. This strategy is activated after the 90th epoch to avoid destabilizing early training, and the combined matting loss (L1 + L2 + Laplacian + gradient penalty) supervises the decoder at each sampled time step.

## Results

On the Composition-1k benchmark, DiffMatte with a ViT-B encoder and 10 refinement steps achieves SAD 18.63 and MSE 2.54, surpassing the previous best method ViTMatte-B (SAD 20.33, MSE 3.0) by 8.4% and 15.3% respectively. Even with a single step (S1), DiffMatte-ViTB already outperforms ViTMatte-B (SAD 18.84 vs. 20.33). On the in-the-wild AIM-500 benchmark, DiffMatte-ViTB trained on Distinctions-646 achieves SAD 16.73 and MSE 1.70 with 10 steps, outperforming ViTMatte-B (SAD 17.93, MSE 1.88) across all four metrics. The method also generalizes well to D646 (SAD 19.07) and SIMD (SAD 25.60), consistently beating corresponding baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffMatte consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines on Composition-1k across all four metrics (SAD, MSE, Grad, Conn). Against the strongest prior method ViTMatte-B [74], DiffMatte-ViTB (S10) improves SAD from 20.33 to 18.63 (−8.4%) and MSE from 3.0 to 2.54 (−15.3%). Against the transformer-based Matteformer [54] (SAD 23.80) and RMat [11] (SAD 22.87), the gains are even larger (−21.6% and −18.4% in SAD respectively). On the generalization benchmarks D646 and SIMD, DiffMatte-ViTB (S1) already beats ViTMatte-B by 1.29 SAD points on D646 and 0.32 on SIMD, with 10-step refinement further widening the gap on SIMD (SAD 25.60 vs. 27.15). On the in-the-wild AIM-500 benchmark, DiffMatte-ViTB trained on the broader Distinctions-646 domain achieves the best results in all metrics (SAD 16.73, MSE 1.70, Grad 14.78, Conn 16.35), surpassing ViTMatte-B by 6.7% in SAD. Notably, even the one-step DiffMatte predictions (S1) exceed the corresponding non-iterative baselines, suggesting the UTI self-aligned training strategy strengthens the base model itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffMatte builds on the decoupled encoder-decoder architecture of ViTMatte [74] and the DDIM sampling procedure [62], repurposing the diffusion denoising process as an iterative refinement mechanism for alpha matte prediction. It departs from prior one-step matting models by introducing a multi-step iterative paradigm, and from segmentation refinement methods (e.g., CascadePSP) by using a noised alpha matte as guidance rather than a coarse mask, thereby sidestepping the fundamental incompatibility between trimap inputs and alpha matte outputs. The UTI self-aligned training strategy extends the self-aligned idea from DDP [30] by explicitly modeling error accumulation across time intervals rather than treating each step independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to apply denoising diffusion to natural image matting, and the plug-and-play decoupled decoder design means the approach can be attached to any existing matting encoder without retraining the backbone. The 8% SAD and 15% MSE improvements on Composition-1k are meaningful, but the AIM-500 results reveal a limitation: models trained on Adobe Image Matting actually degrade with more iteration steps on real images, suggesting the synthetic-to-real gap still constrains the iterative benefit. Open questions include whether the UTI strategy generalizes to other dense prediction tasks, whether the linear noise schedule finding holds at scale, and whether further iteration steps beyond 10 yield diminishing or negative returns on in-the-wild data.
