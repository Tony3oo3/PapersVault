## Problem

Existing LNL methods assume that the majority of samples within a labeled class are clean, causing them to fail when noise is dominant (over 50% of a class is mislabeled) or when hard-negative samples are visually dissimilar to their class. The paper identifies that noise for a given class typically originates from a limited, known set of source categories (e.g., a cat mislabeled is more likely a dog than a hippopotamus), yet this noise source knowledge is largely overlooked by prior LNL work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy labeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, variable resolution for cell painting and natural image datasets) together with noise source knowledge D_ns, which can take the form of a full k×k probability matrix, a set of label pairs, or be derived from dataset metadata such as control-group design in cell profiling experiments. The output is a per-sample clean/noisy indicator (used to gate training) and a final class prediction. Notably, the noise source knowledge need not be complete or correct—partial or noisy knowledge still yields gains.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified clean-sample detection framework that replaces the standard threshold test p(c|x_i) > δ with a cross-class comparison: a sample labeled c is deemed clean if p(c|x_i) exceeds the maximum probability over all known noise source classes c_n ∈ D_{c-ns}. This single modification is applied to adapt five state-of-the-art LNL methods (CRUST, FINE, SFT, UNICON, DISC) by altering only their sample-selection criterion while preserving the rest of each method's training pipeline. For example, FINE+k fits a GMM on the alignment difference between the labeled class and the noise source class rather than on alignment alone, and DISC+k computes its dynamic threshold over the union of the label class and noise source classes. The paper also introduces a novel 'dominant noise' setting where noisy samples constitute the majority of a class distribution, simulating scientific datasets where control-group images dominate treatment-labeled classes.

## Results

Under the novel dominant noise setting (noise ratio 0.8), LNL+K methods achieve up to 15% accuracy gains over their unadapted counterparts (e.g., CRUST+k reaches 80.54% vs. CRUST's 65.79% on CIFAR-10). On the real-world BBBC036 cell dataset, DISC+k achieves a 23% boost (63.32% vs. 40.55%), though only CRUST+k (65.07%) surpasses standard training (63.49%). Under asymmetric noise (ratio 0.4), FINE+k gains up to 8% on CIFAR-100 (72.87% vs. 65.11%), and UNICON+k leads on Animal-10N (88.28%). On Clothing1M, UNICON+k (75.13%) and DISC+k (73.87%) outperform all prior LNL baselines including DivideMix (74.76%) and SOP (73.50%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All five adapted LNL+K methods (CRUST+k, FINE+k, SFT+k, UNICON+k, DISC+k) consistently outperform their unadapted counterparts across both dominant and asymmetric noise settings, with the largest gains appearing at high noise ratios. CRUST+k shows the most dramatic improvement under dominant noise, jumping from 65.79% to 80.54% on CIFAR-10 (0.8) and from 63.06% to 65.07% on BBBC036, where it is the only LNL+K method to beat standard training. DISC+k achieves the largest absolute gain on BBBC036 (40.55% → 63.32%, +22.77%) but still falls slightly below the unmodified baseline (63.49%). FINE+k delivers the strongest asymmetric-noise improvement on CIFAR-100 (65.11% → 72.87% at ratio 0.4, +7.76%), surpassing SFT+k by 2% in that setting. On Clothing1M, UNICON+k (75.13%) and DISC+k (73.87%) both exceed DivideMix (74.76%), ELR (72.87%), CORES2 (73.24%), and SOP (73.50%). DualT, used as a noise-transition estimation baseline, degrades severely under dominant noise (46.96% on CIFAR-10 at 0.8 vs. 78.99% for standard training), highlighting that estimation-only approaches fail when noisy samples dominate the class distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL) literature by introducing noise source knowledge as a first-class input to clean sample detection, generalizing the limited noise supervision concept from Han et al. (Masking, NeurIPS 2018), which was restricted to classifier-consistent methods and required complete transition knowledge. The authors provide a unified cross-class comparison framework (Eq. 2) that adapts five classifier-inconsistent LNL methods—CRUST, FINE, SFT, UNICON, and DISC—by replacing within-class similarity thresholds with comparisons against noise source classes. They also introduce a novel dominant noise setting that mirrors scientific datasets where a control class serves as a known noise source and noise ratios exceed 50%, a regime where prior LNL methods' informative-dataset assumptions break down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that standard LNL methods can actually underperform baseline training on real-world scientific datasets (e.g., cell painting), and that integrating even partial or noisy noise source knowledge is necessary to make them effective—highlighting a gap between LNL theory and practical deployment in domains like biology. The knowledge absorption rate metric reveals that method rankings shift when knowledge is added, suggesting that LNL+K should be studied as its own task rather than treated as a trivial add-on. Open questions include how to systematically estimate noise sources when metadata is unavailable, how to handle multi-source and instance-dependent noise, and whether the framework extends to non-image modalities or multi-label settings.
