## Problem

Multimodal hallucination in vision-language models remains poorly understood and inadequately evaluated: existing benchmarks rely on constrained answer formats (multiple choice, one-word responses), draw images from limited real-world sources like MS-COCO and Flickr, and lack systematic coverage of distinct hallucination triggers such as false premises, insufficient context, and visual complexity. No prior resource simultaneously provides a challenging open-ended VQA benchmark, a scalable image source, and an automatic evaluation mechanism for free-form hallucinated responses.

## Input / Output

Each example pairs an RGB image (real from Open Images or synthetic from Midjourney/Stable Diffusion galleries, selected for high view counts and positive ratings) with a free-form text question from one of three hallucination-trigger categories. The VLM produces an open-ended text answer, which is then scored by an LLM-based Auto-Eval system (Gemini with a Langfun schema) that extracts the main point of both the prediction and ground truth and judges agreement, yielding a binary correct/incorrect score per example. The full dataset contains 7,748 entries across 3,157 unique images, split into 7,140 training and 608 evaluation examples.

## Method

HaloQuest is constructed via a machine-human-in-the-loop pipeline: images are filtered by annotators for interestingness and comprehensibility, then questions are authored by crowdworkers (two per image, targeting unanswerable visual elements and subtle details) and by LLMs using the IdealGPT framework (GPT-4 + BLIP2) to generate atomic image statements that are human-validated before being converted into question-answer pairs. A filtering stage discards questions that high-performing VQA models answer too easily or that yield ambiguous answers. For evaluation, the Auto-Eval mechanism prompts Gemini with an advanced Langfun schema to reason about the main points of the model response and ground truth before deciding agreement, achieving 95.3% agreement with human raters versus 93.4% for basic text-only prompting. For hallucination mitigation, four VLMs (BLIP2, mPLUG-Owl1, MiniGPT4 at 7B and 13B) are instruction-tuned on VQA v2 data augmented with HaloQuest entries converted into natural-language instruction templates, which substantially reduces hallucination rates without degrading standard VQA v2 performance.

## Results

On the HaloQuest zero-shot benchmark, all open-source VLMs achieve below 36% Human Eval accuracy, with BEiT-3 (0.7B) as the best open-source model at 35.9% and LLaVA (13B) at just 10.9%. Proprietary models perform substantially better: GPT-4 reaches 62.9%, GPT-4o 68.1%, and Gemini 1.5 Pro 76.1% Human Eval. Fine-tuning on HaloQuest dramatically reduces hallucination (e.g., BLIP2 8B improves from 11.2% to 33.9% Human Eval) while preserving or slightly improving VQA v2 performance (70.5% → 71.0% overall). The proposed Auto-Eval mechanism achieves 95.3% agreement with human raters, far outperforming standard metrics like BLEU, CIDER, ROUGE, and METEOR which show poor correlation with human judgment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 11 VLMs on HaloQuest, revealing that model size does not guarantee hallucination resistance: the 0.7B BEiT-3 outperforms all larger open-source models (13B LLaVA at 10.9%, 13B MiniGPT4 at 18.7%, 12B BLIP2 at 21.1%). Among proprietary models, Gemini 1.5 Pro leads at 76.1% Human Eval, followed by GPT-4o (68.1%) and GPT-4 (62.9%), though all still exhibit substantial hallucination. After fine-tuning on HaloQuest, BLIP2 8B jumps from 11.2% to 33.9% and MiniGPT4 13B from 18.3% to 35.5% on HaloQuest, while VQA v2 scores remain stable or improve slightly (e.g., BLIP2 8B: 70.5% → 71.0%). On the POPE hallucination benchmark, HaloQuest-trained mPLUG-Owl improves from 0.50 to 0.64 Random accuracy and MiniGPT4 from 0.72 to 0.80, confirming generalization to unseen hallucination scenarios. The Auto-Eval mechanism (95.3% human agreement) substantially outperforms text-only prompting (93.4%) and basic schema prompting (94.8%), validating the Langfun-based reasoning approach as a practical alternative to costly human evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HaloQuest carves new ground at the intersection of hallucination evaluation and synthetic image generation. Prior hallucination benchmarks (POPE, NOPE, HallusionBench) rely on real images from MS-COCO or Visual Genome and use constrained answer formats (binary yes/no, short phrases, multiple choice), limiting their ability to probe nuanced, open-ended hallucinations. This paper departs from that lineage by (1) incorporating Midjourney and Stable Diffusion images alongside real ones, (2) designing questions in three targeted categories (false premise, visually challenging, insufficient context) that elicit specific hallucination triggers, and (3) introducing an LLM-based Auto-Eval mechanism using a Langfun schema to score free-form responses. The data generation pipeline repurposes the IdealGPT framework (GPT-4 + BLIP2) for atomic-statement extraction, but the overall dataset construction and evaluation recipe are novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The r=0.97 correlation between synthetic and real image performance validates prompt-based image generation as a scalable, cost-effective path for building hallucination benchmarks beyond the limited diversity of MS-COCO/Flickr. The Auto-Eval mechanism (r=0.99 with human raters) addresses a practical bottleneck: open-ended hallucination evaluation has previously required expensive manual annotation. However, the fine-tuning gains, while consistent across models, remain modest in absolute terms (e.g., BLIP2-8B improves from ~11% to ~34% on HaloQuest), and the 7.7K-example dataset is small relative to the breadth of hallucination phenomena. Open questions include whether the synthetic-real gap will persist as image generators improve, whether the three question categories capture the full taxonomy of multimodal hallucination triggers, and whether Auto-Eval generalizes to domains beyond VQA.
