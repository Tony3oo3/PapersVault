## Problem

Existing scene coordinate regression (SCR) methods train on all image regions, including dynamic objects and texture-less surfaces, which introduces noisy gradients and destabilizes training. Prior filtering strategies (RANSAC, differentiable end-to-end optimization) discard outliers without leveraging the semantic structure of the image, and the paper's analysis shows that low-reprojection-error regions do not align with fixed semantic categories across scenes, making pre-defined category-based selection infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces per-pixel 3D scene coordinates, a per-pixel confidence score, and ultimately a 6-DoF camera pose via PnP with RANSAC. During training, the method additionally consumes ground-truth camera poses to compute reprojection errors and generate EGFS masks; during inference, only the scene coordinates and confidence map are needed, with the median confidence score used as a threshold to retain reliable 2D-3D correspondences before pose solving.

## Method

The core contribution is Error-Guided Feature Selection (EGFS), an iterative training mechanism built on the ACE backbone. In each iteration, the τ% (10%) of pixels with the lowest reprojection errors are selected as point prompts and fed into EfficientViT-SAM-L0 to expand them into semantically coherent masks; these masks are then refined by a learned confidence map that filters out low-confidence pixels. Only features within the refined masks are sampled into the training buffer for the scene-specific MLP, which is trained for k epochs (5) before the masks are regenerated. A dedicated confidence head (replacing the last three 1×1 conv layers of ACE's MLP with two) is jointly optimized with a loss that down-weights reprojection errors in uncertain regions via confidence-weighted tanh-clamped error and penalizes overconfident invalid predictions. This iterative prompt-expand-refine loop dynamically adapts the training focus across epochs without requiring any 3D model or pre-defined semantic categories.

## Results

On the Cambridge Landmarks dataset, EGFS achieves an average median translation/rotation error of 17 cm / 0.3°, matching ACE(quad.) (17/0.3) while using a smaller model (4.5 MB vs. 16 MB) and shorter training time (12 min vs. 20 min). The dual-ensemble EGFS(dual) further improves to 15/0.3. On Indoor6, EGFS reaches a 56.1% success rate (proportion of poses within 5 cm / 5°), substantially outperforming ACE (35.6%) and DSAC* (35.1%), while EGFS(dual) achieves 61.2%, surpassing ACE(quad.) (58.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGFS (4.5 MB, 12 min) matches ACE(quad.) (16 MB, 20 min) on Cambridge Landmarks at 17/0.3 average error while being 3.5× smaller and 1.7× faster to train, and outperforms both ACE (25/0.4) and DSAC* (19/0.4) by a wide margin. On Indoor6, EGFS (56.1%) substantially beats ACE (35.6%) and DSAC* (35.1%) but falls slightly short of ACE(quad.) (58.6%); however, EGFS(dual) (61.2%) surpasses ACE(quad.) while using only 9 MB vs. 16 MB. Against feature-matching baselines (hLoc, pixLoc), EGFS(dual) achieves comparable or better average errors (15/0.3 vs. 11/0.2 and 15/0.2) with orders-of-magnitude smaller model sizes (9 MB vs. 600–800 MB) and far shorter mapping times. The SCR methods that require a 3D model (FocusTune, NeuMap, SACReg-L) are not directly comparable since EGFS operates without 3D information, but EGFS(dual) still achieves competitive or superior accuracy to several of them (e.g., 15/0.3 vs. FocusTune 19/0.3, NeuMap 12/0.3, SACReg-L 10/0.2). The key takeaway is that EGFS achieves state-of-the-art among SCR methods without 3D information while maintaining ACE-level efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ACE (Accelerated Coordinate Encoding) architecture, modifying its scene-specific MLP to add a confidence head and introducing an iterative training loop where features are sampled from error-guided masks rather than uniformly. It departs from prior SCR methods (DSAC, DSAC++, DSAC*) that rely on differentiable RANSAC or robust loss terms to down-weight outliers during training, and from semantic-guided approaches (FocusTune, VS-Net) that require pre-defined categories or 3D models. The key conceptual departure is repurposing SAM as a prompt-based mask generator: low-reprojection-error pixels serve as point prompts that SAM expands into semantically coherent regions, eliminating the need for hand-defined area categories while preserving the training efficiency of ACE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that concentrating SCR training on semantically coherent low-error regions—identified adaptively per scene via SAM—yields consistent improvements over uniform sampling and RANSAC-based outlier rejection, without requiring 3D models or pre-defined semantics. This is practically relevant for deployment scenarios where scene-specific training must be fast and the scene contains dynamic objects or texture-less surfaces. Limitations include the added training time (12 min vs. 5 min for ACE) due to iterative SAM mask generation, the dependency on a large foundation model (EfficientViT-SAM-L0) during training, and evaluation limited to two relatively small datasets. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large-scale outdoor scenes with thousands of images, whether the SAM dependency can be replaced by lighter alternatives, and whether the iterative mask refinement converges reliably in highly dynamic environments.
