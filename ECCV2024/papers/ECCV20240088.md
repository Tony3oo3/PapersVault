## Problem

Existing camera-based semantic scene completion methods that exploit temporal information (e.g., VoxFormer-T) simply stack history frames, implicitly assuming pixel-level correspondence across different viewpoints. Since shared semantic content undergoes uncertain positional shifts between perspectives, this straightforward aggregation introduces blurred and ambiguous predictive signals, degrading the stability and accuracy of dense 3D semantic occupancy predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240088/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of temporal RGB images (current frame plus 3 previous frames) in either stereo (HTCL-S) or monocular (HTCL-M) configuration, along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics for epipolar warping. Depth is modeled via 192 hypothesis planes from off-the-shelf monocular or stereo depth estimators. The output is a dense 3D semantic voxel grid (e.g., 256×256×32 at 0.2 m resolution on SemanticKITTI) with per-voxel class labels over 19–21 semantic categories, predicted via an SSC head with upsampling and softmax.

## Method

HTCL decomposes temporal context learning into two hierarchical steps. First, Cross-frame Pattern Affinity (CPA) measures contextual correspondence by computing scale-aware isolated cosine similarity across three groups of 3D atrous convolutions (dilation rates 1, 2, 4) applied to both current and historical feature volumes, then concatenating the resulting affinity matrices along the channel dimension. Second, Affinity-based Dynamic Refinement (ADR) uses a cascade of three 3D deformable convolutions conditioned on the affinity weights to adaptively shift sampling locations toward high-affinity regions and their neighbors, compensating for incomplete observations. The aligned temporal volume is constructed via explicit epipolar homography warping (using a lightweight PoseNet) rather than a cost volume, preserving fine-grained feature context. A Weighted Voxel Attention module with a learnable coefficient α (initialized to 0) progressively integrates the refined temporal content into the LSS-based voxel feature volume during training, enabling stable convergence.

## Results

On the SemanticKITTI test set, HTCL-S (temporal stereo) achieves 44.23 IoU and 17.09 mIoU, surpassing the strongest temporal baseline VoxFormer-T (43.21 IoU, 13.41 mIoU) by 1.02 IoU and 3.68 mIoU. On the OpenOccupancy validation set, the monocular variant HTCL-M reaches 14.1 mIoU, exceeding all LiDAR-based methods (JS3C-Net: 12.5, LMSCNet: 11.5) and all camera-based methods (TPVFormer: 7.8, MonoScene: 6.9) in semantic accuracy. The method also outperforms temporal stereo variants of MonoScene, TPVFormer, and OccFormer (12.96–13.57 mIoU) by 3.5–4.2 mIoU while running at comparable speed (~0.297 s/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HTCL-S decisively outperforms all camera-based baselines on SemanticKITTI: it gains +3.68 mIoU over VoxFormer-T (the closest temporal competitor), +4.77 mIoU over OccFormer, and +6.01 mIoU over MonoScene. On OpenOccupancy, HTCL-M (monocular temporal) achieves 14.1 mIoU, beating LiDAR-based JS3C-Net (12.5) and LMSCNet (11.5) by 1.6 and 2.6 mIoU respectively, though it trails them in geometry (IoU 21.4 vs. 30.2 and 27.3) due to the inherent depth ambiguity of monocular inputs. Against camera-only baselines on OpenOccupancy, HTCL-M leads TPVFormer by 6.3 mIoU and MonoScene by 7.2 mIoU. In the fair temporal-stereo comparison (Table 3), HTCL-S (17.13 mIoU) surpasses VoxFormer-T (13.35), OccFormer† (13.57), TPVFormer† (13.21), and MonoScene† (12.96) by 3.6–4.2 mIoU, confirming that the hierarchical temporal context learning paradigm, rather than simply stacking more frames, is the source of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VoxFormer-T, the first temporal pipeline for camera-based SSC, which simply stacked history frames alongside the current frame. HTCL departs from this naive aggregation by introducing explicit epipolar homography warping (borrowed from video depth estimation) to align temporal features, a cross-frame pattern affinity mechanism with scale-aware isolation and multi-group atrous convolutions, and deformable-convolution-based dynamic refinement. The voxel feature volume construction follows the Lift-Splat-Shoot paradigm as used in MonoScene and OccFormer, while the weighted voxel cross-attention aggregation is a new component not present in prior camera-based SSC methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a camera-only method can surpass LiDAR-based approaches in mIoU on OpenOccupancy (14.1 vs. 12.5) while still trailing significantly in IoU (21.4 vs. 30.2) highlights that semantic understanding from RGB temporal context is now competitive, but geometric accuracy remains the bottleneck for camera-based SSC. The structured two-step temporal modeling (affinity measurement → dynamic refinement) offers a reusable paradigm for other dense 3D perception tasks that require temporal context. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer temporal windows, highly dynamic scenes with occlusion, and whether the reliance on auxiliary depth estimation (stereo or monocular) limits deployment in unconstrained environments.
