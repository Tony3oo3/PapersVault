## Problem

Existing one-class video anomaly detection methods output only anomaly scores without any rationale, limiting public trust in real-world deployments. While LLMs offer strong reasoning, their direct application to VAD fails because their pre-trained implicit knowledge reflects general-world assumptions that misalign with scenario-specific anomaly definitions (e.g., GPT-4V treats skateboarding as normal even when a restricted campus requires flagging it). No prior work provides a reasoning-based, one-class VAD approach that adapts to specific scenarios without full-shot training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of normal reference frames (default: 10 batches × 1 frame each, randomly sampled from the training set) for rule induction, and continuous test video frames for deduction. A VLM (CogVLM-17B) converts each frame into a structured text description separating human activities from environmental objects. The output is a per-frame anomaly score (via secondary EMA), a binary anomaly/normal label, and a natural-language reasoning explanation that cites which induced rule was violated, enabling frame-level interpretability comparable to SOTA score-based methods.

## Method

AnomalyRuler operates in two stages. In induction, a VLM describes few normal reference frames, then a frozen LLM generates rules using three prompting strategies—Normal-and-Anomaly contrast (deriving anomaly rules by negating normal patterns), Abstract-and-Concrete analogy (generalizing from abstract concepts to concrete examples), and Human-and-Environment separation (splitting rules into activity and object categories). A rule aggregation module applies randomized smoothing: n independently generated rule sets are voted on by an LLM to retain only consistently present elements, yielding a robust rule set. In deduction, test frames are described by the VLM, then Exponential Majority Smoothing (combining EMA with a windowed majority vote) corrects perception errors using temporal consistency, and a robust reasoning module feeds the smoothed description plus a dummy answer back to the LLM for rechecking against the induced rules, reducing false negatives and aligning reasoning with the rule set.

## Results

On the four one-class VAD benchmarks, AnomalyRuler (no training required) achieves AUC of 97.9% on Ped2, 89.7% on Ave, 85.2% on ShT, and 71.9% on UB. It outperforms all Image-Only methods on the challenging ShT and UB datasets and matches full-shot-trained Image-Only methods on Ped2 and Ave without any tuning. In the LLM-based comparison on ShT, AnomalyRuler reaches 81.8% accuracy and 64.3% recall, improving over direct LLM use (52.1% accuracy, 6.2% recall) by +29.7% accuracy and +58.1% recall. For reasoning, AnomalyRuler achieves 99% Doubly-Right (RR) with corrected perception, versus 81% for the best GPT-4V baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLM-based baselines on ShT, AnomalyRuler (81.8% acc, 64.3% recall) substantially outperforms Ask LLM Directly (52.1% acc, 6.2% recall), Ask LLM with Elhafsi et al. (58.4% acc, 15.2% recall), and Ask Video-LLaMA Directly (54.7% acc, 8.5% recall), with the largest gap in recall (+58.1% over direct LLM use). Among 15 SOTA one-class VAD methods, AnomalyRuler (no training) is competitive with full-shot-trained methods: it trails SSL (99.0/92.2/84.3) and HSC (98.1/93.7/83.4) on Ped2 and Ave by 1–4 AUC points, but matches or exceeds them on ShT (85.2 vs. 84.3 SSL, 83.4 HSC) and UB (71.9 vs. 71.8 STG-NF, 62.7 FPDM, 59.3 BAF). The only method that edges out AnomalyRuler on ShT is STG-NF (85.9 vs. 85.2), which uses non-image features (pose) and full-shot training. In the cross-domain setting (ShT→target), AnomalyRuler-base achieves 97.4% on Ped2 and 83.5% on ShT, outperforming zxVAD (95.7/71.6), rGAN (81.9/77.9), and MPN (84.7/73.8) by an average of 9.88% AUC. For reasoning quality (Doubly-Right metric), AnomalyRuler reaches 99% RR with corrected perception versus 81% for GPT-4V with Cao et al., and only 1% WW versus 17% for the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two lines of work simultaneously. Against traditional one-class VAD methods (reconstruction-based, distance-based), it replaces full-shot self-supervised training with a few-normal-shot prompting pipeline that produces interpretable rules rather than opaque anomaly scores. Against prior LLM-based anomaly detection efforts (Elhafsi et al.'s predefined-concept approach, Cao et al.'s direct GPT-4V prompting, Lv et al.'s fine-tuned video-LLM), it introduces an explicit induction-deduction structure inspired by the scientific method, borrowing randomized smoothing (Cohen et al.) for rule aggregation and divide-and-conquer prompting (Lee et al.) for the Human/Environment decomposition. The conceptual lineage is closest to Chain-of-Thought and Hypotheses-to-Theories reasoning, but applied to the one-class VAD setting where no anomaly examples are available at training time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that AnomalyRuler eliminates the expensive full-shot training loop and provides human-readable rationales, which is critical for deploying VAD in safety-critical settings (autonomous driving, campus security) where regulatory and public-trust requirements demand explainability. The domain-adaptability finding—that language descriptions serve as a domain-invariant intermediate representation, enabling rules induced on ShanghaiTech to transfer to Ped2 or UBnormal—suggests a broader principle for cross-domain anomaly detection. Open questions include scalability to truly long-tailed or multi-modal anomalies, the cost of GPT-4 calls during induction in production, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the four relatively small, single-scene benchmarks evaluated here.
