## Problem

Feed-forward image-to-3D models trained on single-object datasets like Objaverse exhibit a 'multi-object gap': they fail on complex compositional assets due to camera-setting bias (objects assumed centered and normalized), dataset bias (near-absence of occlusion and multi-object examples), and geometry/texture leaking between objects. The paper asks whether a compositional pipeline can generate high-quality 3D assets containing multiple interacting objects from a single reference image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240625/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image containing multiple objects, user-specified 2D bounding boxes for each object, and a text prompt that includes spatial-relationship tokens (e.g., 'sitting on', 'riding') extracted via LLM or user annotation. It produces a single compositional textured 3D mesh (downsampled to 50k faces per object) with correct relative scale, rotation, and translation. The SSDS optimization stage runs in approximately 2 minutes, and the pipeline handles scenes with up to ~5 objects.

## Method

ComboVerse is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (single-object reconstruction) segments each object with SAM, inpaints occluded regions using Stable Diffusion with a noise-background strategy and bounding-aware mask, then reconstructs each object independently with Wonder3D. Stage 2 (multi-object combination) fixes the geometry and texture of each object and optimizes only their scale, rotation, and translation parameters, initialized from bounding-box ratios and monocular depth estimation. The key contribution is the Spatially-Aware SDS (SSDS) loss: rather than optimizing shape and texture from scratch as in standard SDS, it reweights the cross-attention maps of spatial-relationship tokens (identified from the text prompt) by a multiplier c=25, forcing the pretrained diffusion model to prioritize spatial layout over content matching. SSDS is computed on novel views sampled from high-noise timesteps (800–900), where spatial structure is most influential, and is combined with a reference-view RGB+alpha reconstruction loss to stabilize optimization.

## Results

On the 100-image ComboVerse Benchmark, ComboVerse achieves a CLIP-Score of 86.58% and a GPT-3D Score of 65.63%, outperforming all three baselines on both metrics. The gap is most pronounced on the GPT-3D Score, where ComboVerse leads Wonder3D (56.25%) by 9.4 points and SyncDreamer (13.54%) by 52.1 points, indicating substantially better perceptual quality in compositional 3D reconstruction. The CLIP-Score margins are smaller (1.0 point over Wonder3D, 2.9 over OpenLRM, 5.1 over SyncDreamer), suggesting that semantic alignment is relatively easier to achieve while spatial composition quality is the harder differentiator.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ComboVerse is compared against three state-of-the-art single-image-to-3D methods: SyncDreamer (ICLR 2024), OpenLRM (ICLR 2024), and Wonder3D (CVPR 2024). ComboVerse wins on both evaluation metrics across all three comparisons. The most dramatic margin is against SyncDreamer, which scores only 13.54% on GPT-3D Score versus ComboVerse's 65.63%, reflecting SyncDreamer's severe difficulty with multi-object geometry and texture blending. OpenLRM and Wonder3D are closer competitors on CLIP-Score (83.65% and 85.57% respectively) but still trail ComboVerse by 2.9 and 1.0 points; on GPT-3D Score the gaps widen to 12.5 and 9.4 points, confirming that the compositional pipeline with spatially-aware SDS guidance yields meaningfully better spatial arrangement and object separation. Qualitatively, the paper notes that baselines struggle with small non-centered objects, occlusion blending, and cross-object texture leakage, all of which ComboVerse addresses through its two-stage decompose-then-combine strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

ComboVerse sits at the intersection of two lines of work: feed-forward image-to-3D models (Wonder3D, LRM, SyncDreamer) trained on Objaverse, and SDS-based optimization from DreamFusion. The key departure is a compositional paradigm—generate each object independently with a feed-forward model, then optimize only spatial parameters (scale, rotation, translation) rather than shape and texture from scratch. The spatially-aware SDS loss (SSDS) directly adapts the cross-attention reweighting trick from Prompt-to-Prompt [14] to the 3D optimization setting, scaling attention maps for spatial-relationship tokens to bias the diffusion score toward positional correctness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underexplored failure mode—the 'multi-object gap' in Objaverse-trained models—and offers a practical, modular pipeline that sidesteps it without retraining. However, the approach is limited to fewer than ~5 objects, does not refine geometry or texture during combination (so quality is capped by the backbone), and the 100-image benchmark with GPT-based pairwise evaluation leaves open questions about robustness and generalization. A key open direction is whether the SSDS spatial guidance can be integrated into end-to-end scene generation or whether better multi-object training data would eliminate the need for the compositional workaround.
