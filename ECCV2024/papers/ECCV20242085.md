## Problem

When 3D-from-single-image models operate on object crops rather than full images, they lose information about where the crop sits in the camera's field of view. The paper identifies that this loss creates a fundamental ambiguity—Perspective Distortion-induced Shape Ambiguity in Image Crops (PSAC)—where two objects at different distances and lateral positions can produce visually identical crops, making 3D shape and pose unrecoverable from crop appearance alone. This ambiguity, distinct from the well-known scale-depth ambiguity, has not been previously analyzed or addressed in the literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB image crops (object-specific crops for pose/detection, or 3:4 aspect-ratio crops rescaled to 384×512 for depth) together with camera intrinsics (focal lengths f_x, f_y and principal point p_x, p_y). Outputs are task-specific: a 7-dimensional pose vector (axis-angle rotation, camera-frame translation, articulation angle) for articulated objects, a dense per-pixel metric depth map, or 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation). The intrinsics are not fed as raw numbers but are used to compute per-pixel or per-corner angular coordinates in the camera's field of view before encoding.

## Method

The core contribution is Intrinsics-Aware Positional Encoding (KPE), which computes each pixel's angular position in the camera's field of view as θ_x = arctan((x−p_x)/f_x) and θ_y = arctan((y−p_y)/f_y), then encodes these angles via sinusoidal positional encodings (4 frequencies). Two variants are provided: Dense KPE computes the encoding per-pixel and is best for dense prediction (depth), while Sparse KPE encodes only the 4 corners and center of the crop and is best for object-level tasks (pose, 3D detection). The encoding is injected at a task-dependent layer—concatenated with spatial feature maps from a ResNet-50 or MiDaS/BEiT backbone, or broadcast to match feature resolution—allowing the network to condition its 3D geometric interpretation on where the crop appears in the field of view. A parallelepiped case study demonstrates that without this encoding, a 6-layer MLP cannot fit training data (loss saturates), while with absolute angular coordinates it converges cleanly, confirming the ambiguity is resolved.

## Results

On ARCTIC (3D pose of articulated objects), sparse KPE reduces AAE from 6.4° to 5.2° (−19%) and improves Success rate from 53.9% to 64.1% (+19%) on the test split. On NYU Depth v2, dense KPE improves REL from 0.088 to 0.082 (−6.8%) and log₁₀ error from 0.040 to 0.035 (−12.5%) with a pre-trained MiDaS backbone. On KITTI/nuScenes 3D object detection, sparse KPE boosts AP₃D from 54.59 to 57.54 (+5.4%) and AP₇₅₃D from 14.23 to 16.58 (+16%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ArcticNet-SF [12] (the original ARCTIC model using full images), KPE with crops achieves substantially better results: 5.2° vs. 6.4° AAE and 64.1% vs. 53.9% Success on the test split, confirming that focusing on the object crop while retaining field-of-view information is superior to processing the full image. Against Cam-Conv [11], which also encodes pixel locations in the camera's field of view but without sinusoidal encoding, KPE wins on every metric across all three tasks (e.g., 5.9° vs. 6.3° AAE on ARCTIC, 0.082 vs. 0.096 REL on NYU, 57.54 vs. 56.82 AP₃D on detection). Against the base ZoeDepth [4] model, adding dense KPE yields consistent 5–13% relative improvements in REL, RMSE, and log₁₀, while Cam-Conv actually degrades performance (0.096 REL vs. 0.088 baseline). Against Cube R-CNN [6], sparse KPE improves AP₃D by 5.4% and AP₇₅₃D by 16%, outperforming both 2D image-location encodings and Cam-Conv. In all comparisons, KPE is the best-performing encoding, validating that the combination of sinusoidal encoding and camera-field-of-view (rather than 2D image) coordinates is the critical design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on three representative 3D-from-single-image architectures (ArcticNet-SF for articulated object pose, ZoeDepth for metric depth, and Cube R-CNN for 3D detection) and incorporates a new encoding, Intrinsics-Aware Positional Encoding (KPE), into each. Conceptually, KPE is related to prior camera-aware encodings such as Cam-Conv [11] and pixel-level geometric embeddings [14,15,38], but departs from them by encoding the crop's angular position in the camera's field of view via sinusoids rather than 2D image coordinates or raw camera parameters. The paper's key departure is identifying Perspective Distortion-induced Shape Ambiguity in Image Crops (PSAC) as a distinct, previously unanalyzed ambiguity that arises even with a single fixed camera, separate from the well-known scale-depth ambiguity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of PSAC is practically important because cropping is ubiquitous in 3D-from-image pipelines (object detectors, region-based networks), and the paper shows this cropping can prevent models from even fitting training data when the object's field-of-view location is unknown. KPE is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic fix that consistently improves metrics across CNN and transformer backbones, making it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether KPE can work with predicted (rather than known) intrinsics, whether shading or other photometric cues can further alleviate the ambiguity, and whether the encoding helps for shallower networks or tasks like normal and full-shape prediction.
