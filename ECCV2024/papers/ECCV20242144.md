## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting, despite its speed and quality on curated benchmarks, is significantly more vulnerable to image blur (motion, defocus, downscaling) than NeRF-based methods, tending to overfit observation noise and produce concentrated artifacts. Existing deblurring approaches for NeRF rely on sparse kernel estimation due to ray-based rendering limitations, and none address the additional challenge that 3DGS requires a good point cloud initialization, which is hard to obtain from blurry images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242144/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view images with arbitrary blur degradation, camera poses estimated via COLMAP/SfM, and a (potentially sparse) point cloud initialization. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene for novel view synthesis, along with interpretable per-pixel dense convolution kernels (up to 17×17) and per-pixel quality masks that localize and characterize the blur. Training operates at three scales (downsampling factors 4×, 2×, 1×) with correspondingly scaled kernel sizes (5, 9, 17), and the base rasterizer is Mip-Splatting for alias-free multi-resolution rendering.

## Method

BAGS introduces a Blur Proposal Network (BPN) that estimates a dense per-pixel convolution kernel and a quality mask jointly with 3D scene optimization. The BPN takes Multi-Modal Features—concatenating a learnable view embedding, positional embedding, and RGBD features extracted by a 3-layer CNN from the rendered color and depth—as input to a 4-layer MLP that outputs the kernel (via softmax) and mask (via sigmoid). The rendered clear image is convolved with the estimated kernel to produce a blur-modeled image, which is then blended with the clear image using the mask before comparing to the observed image. A coarse-to-fine optimization scheme starts at low resolution with small kernels and progressively upscales both image resolution and kernel size (adding new MLP head layers at each scale), which stabilizes joint optimization under sparse point cloud initialization and reduces computational cost. The training loss combines photometric L1, D-SSIM, and a sparsity penalty on the mask to encourage the network to default to the clear rendering unless blur is present.

## Results

On the Deblur-NeRF camera motion blur benchmark, BAGS achieves 26.70 PSNR / 0.824 SSIM / 0.096 LPIPS on average, outperforming the best NeRF-based deblurring baseline PDRF (26.10 / 0.777 / 0.183) by 0.6 dB in PSNR and halving the LPIPS. On defocus blur, BAGS reaches 23.95 PSNR / 0.754 SSIM / 0.095 LPIPS, again leading PDRF (23.85 / 0.741 / 0.107). On the unbounded mixed-resolution and low-light drone scenes, BAGS achieves 27.11 and 29.86 PSNR respectively, surpassing Mip-Splatting (26.11 and 26.50) by over 1 dB in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAGS outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated scenario. Against the closest NeRF-based deblurring competitors (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF), BAGS leads by 0.6–1.1 dB PSNR on camera motion blur and by 0.1–0.5 dB on defocus blur, while achieving roughly 50% lower LPIPS on motion blur (0.096 vs. 0.160–0.183). Vanilla NeRF and Mip-Splatting degrade substantially under blur (22.69 and 21.87 PSNR on motion blur), confirming that 3DGS's discrete representation is more susceptible to overfitting than NeRF's continuous volume. On the unbounded mixed-resolution and low-light drone benchmarks, BAGS beats Mip-Splatting by 1.0 dB (27.11 vs. 26.11) and 3.4 dB (29.86 vs. 26.50) PSNR respectively, and also surpasses Mip-NeRF 360, NeRFacto, and the SR/deblurring pre-processing baselines (Mip-Sp-HAT, Mip-Sp-MPR) which suffer from domain shift and lack of multi-view consistency. The previous NeRF-based deblurring methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF, ExBlurF, BAD-NeRF) cannot handle unbounded 360° geometry at all, so they are excluded from Table 2 comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAGS adapts the forward blur model from Deblur-NeRF to the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, leveraging 3DGS's efficient rasterization to estimate dense per-pixel convolution kernels rather than the sparse deformable kernels used in prior NeRF-based deblurring methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, PDRF). The Blur Proposal Network introduces multi-modal features (spatial position, view embedding, and RGBD features from a small CNN) to capture scene-dependent blur variation, and a residual mask to selectively apply blur modeling. The coarse-to-fine multi-scale optimization scheme is a novel training strategy that addresses the ambiguity of jointly optimizing kernels with a sparse SfM point cloud, a problem less acute in NeRF-based methods that only require camera poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes the dominant paradigm for real-time scene reconstruction, its fragility to real-world image degradation (blur, mixed resolution, low light) is a critical practical limitation that this paper addresses. The finding that 3DGS overfits to blur more severely than NeRF—despite its speed advantage—highlights a fundamental vulnerability of discrete representations that the field should account for. Open questions include scaling per-pixel kernel estimation to very high resolutions (the authors note quadratic complexity), handling multiple simultaneous degradations (e.g., blur + noise + exposure), and extending to dynamic scenes. The interpretable mask output also opens a path toward automated image quality assessment for reconstruction pipelines.
