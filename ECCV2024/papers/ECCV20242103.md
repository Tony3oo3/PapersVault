## Problem

Tracking cells in terabyte-scale 3D time-lapse microscopy datasets remains an open problem because existing methods either require scarce 3D segmentation annotations, cannot scale to millions of instances, or decouple segmentation from tracking so that segmentation errors propagate into tracking. No prior method simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art on both nuclei-based and membrane-based cell tracking while scaling to multi-terabyte real-world datasets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a binary foreground map (cell vs. background) and a fuzzy contour map (continuous edge-strength values indicating likely cell boundaries) per 3D frame, which can be produced by a U-Net, traditional image processing (difference of Gaussians + Otsu), or off-the-shelf segmenters (Cellpose, Stardist) whose binary contours are averaged into a single contour map for ensembling. It outputs per-frame cell segmentation masks selected from the hierarchy and full cell lineages with division, appearance, and disappearance events. The contour-map representation is continuous rather than discrete labels, which is what enables combining multiple segmentation sources and avoids committing to a single potentially wrong segment per cell.

## Method

For each frame, a hierarchical watershed is computed on the foreground's connected components, yielding a dendrogram of candidate segments (an ultrametric contour map) that is pruned by minimum/maximum size and contour-strength thresholds. The joint segmentation-and-tracking problem is then cast as an integer linear program (MTIoU) that selects a set of mutually disjoint segments from the hierarchy across all frames, maximizing the sum of IoU weights between selected segments in adjacent frames while enforcing biological flow constraints (one incoming, up to two outgoing for division, appearance/disappearance penalties). A power transformation on the IoU weights suppresses over-segmentation by favoring fewer high-overlap segments over many low-overlap ones. To scale to terabyte-scale data, association weights are computed sparsely via KDTree nearest-neighbor queries, and the ILP is solved in overlapping time windows using a two-pass interleaved scheduling scheme that stitches solutions across window boundaries.

## Results

On the Cell Tracking Challenge, the method achieves 1st place in the combined CTB score on 3 of 5 datasets: CE (0.844), DRO (0.708), and TRIF (0.841), while ranking 4th on TRIC (0.754) and 7th on MDA (0.683). On the Epithelial Cell Benchmark, using MALA's edge predictions as input, the method achieves SEG scores of 0.909 (PER) and 0.817 (PRO), matching or surpassing the original MALA deep learning baseline. The ILP formulation scales with a linear-fit slope of 1.37±0.06, faster than Türetken (1.75±0.12) and Malin-Mayor (1.84±0.06). On a 3.4 TB zebrafish embryo dataset, the full pipeline completes in 9.5 hours of distributed computing, producing 21.5 million cell instances, compared to 44 hours for ~7 million instances in Malin-Mayor et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the best CTB score on CE (0.844 vs. 0.829 for KTH-SE and 0.808 for KIT-GE/EmbedTrack), DRO (0.708 vs. 0.617 for KTH-SE and 0.591 for JAN-US), and TRIF (0.841 vs. 0.804 for MPI-GE and 0.785 for RWTH-GE), representing the largest improvement in the competition on DRO (+0.091 over 2nd place). On MDA, the method ranks 7th (CTB 0.683), trailing KIT-GE (0.797) and KTH-SE (0.757), likely due to poor generalization of the edge-detection U-Net to the test set. On TRIC, the method ranks 4th (CTB 0.754) behind KTH-SE (0.867), with errors concentrated at the periphery of the cartographical projection where inflated cell movements reduce IoU weight reliability. On the Epithelial Cell Benchmark, the method with MALA's boundaries (SEG 0.909 PER / 0.817 PRO) marginally surpasses the original MALA deep learning baseline (0.907/0.817) and clearly outperforms FFN (0.879/0.796) and PlantSeg (0.787/0.761), while the no-deep-learning variant (Gaussian blur input) remains competitive at 0.853/0.804. For ILP runtime, the proposed formulation (slope 1.37) is ~22% faster than Türetken (1.75) and ~25% faster than Malin-Mayor (1.84), and the full terabyte-scale pipeline runs in 9.5 hours versus 44 hours for Malin-Mayor on a comparable-resource mouse embryo dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in cell tracking: hierarchical segmentation (ultrametric contour maps via watershed) and ILP-based joint segmentation-tracking (Türetken et al. [61], Jug et al.'s moral lineage tracing [23]). The key conceptual departure is treating the hierarchy not as a structure to be cut at a single level, but as a fixed menu of candidate segments from which the ILP selects disjoint instances across adjacent frames, thereby decoupling segmentation quality from tracking accuracy. It also contrasts with flow-based methods [18–20, 31, 58] that avoid segmentation entirely, arguing that explicit segments remain essential for downstream analysis, and with multi-cut formulations [23, 50] whose NP-hardness limits scalability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is the first to simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art on both nuclei-based (CTC 3D datasets) and membrane-based (Epithelial Cell Benchmark) cell tracking while scaling to multi-terabyte light-sheet recordings (3.4 TB zebrafish embryo, 21.5M instances in ~9.5 h), a regime where prior ILP-based methods were infeasible. Its flexibility—accepting any segmentation model or ensemble via fuzzy contour maps—makes it practical for biologists who cannot curate 3D annotations, a persistent bottleneck in fluorescence microscopy. Open questions include whether a learned model can predict the ILP weights (currently hand-tuned), and how performance degrades when the input contour map systematically misrepresents cell boundaries.
