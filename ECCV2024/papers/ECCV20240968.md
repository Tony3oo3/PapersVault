## Problem

Few-shot action recognition requires discerning subtle motion and appearance cues from very limited labeled examples, but existing methods treat video features holistically without explicitly separating what moves from what it looks like. The paper argues that, unlike large-scale settings where deep networks implicitly learn these cues from abundant data, few-shot scenarios demand a deliberate disentanglement of motion and appearance to achieve a higher signal-to-noise ratio with minimal training samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240968/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video clips from which 8 frames are uniformly sampled; from these, CoTracker extracts point trajectories at 10 FPS and DINOv2 produces per-frame patch token features. In the N-way K-shot episode protocol, a support set of N classes (K labeled videos each) and a query video are provided, and the model outputs a class label by matching the query embedding against support embeddings via the bidirectional Mean Hausdorff Metric. Evaluation averages accuracy over 10,000 randomly sampled episodes from the test set.

## Method

The core contribution is Trajectory-Aligned Tokens (TATs): points are uniformly sampled on a grid across multiple frames (not just the first) and tracked with CoTracker, while DINOv2 patch tokens provide appearance semantics; a grid sampler with nearest-neighbor interpolation aligns each point's spatial location to its corresponding DINOv2 patch feature, fusing motion and appearance into a single token per point per frame. These TATs are processed by a Masked Space-Time Transformer that applies temporal attention along each point's trajectory and spatial attention across all points within a frame, with a masked attention mechanism to handle points initialized at different frames. Only the transformer (10.8M parameters) is trained using a combination of cross-entropy loss on a [CLS] token and a contrastive loss based on Bi-MHM, while CoTracker and DINOv2 remain frozen. This design drastically reduces trainable parameters compared to end-to-end baselines like MoLo (89.6M) while achieving state-of-the-art accuracy across Kinetics, Something-Something, UCF-101, HMDB-51, and Fine-Gym.

## Results

On Kinetics (5-way), the method achieves 81.9% (1-shot), 86.5% (2-shot), 89.9% (3-shot), 90.6% (4-shot), and 91.1% (5-shot) accuracy, outperforming the previous best (CCLN) by 6.1%, 4.4%, 4.9%, 4.5%, and 3.6% respectively. On SSV2-Full, it reaches 57.7% (1-shot) and 74.6% (5-shot), surpassing MoLo by 1.1% and 4.0%. On UCF-101, it achieves 92.0% (1-shot) and 96.8% (3-shot), though it trails CCLN by 0.6% in the 5-shot setting. On Fine-Gym, the method gains 8.5% over MoLo in the 1-shot setting (81.8% vs. 73.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves state-of-the-art results on Kinetics across all shot settings, with the largest margin over CCLN (the strongest prior method) being +6.1% in 1-shot (81.9% vs. 75.8%) and +3.6% in 5-shot (91.1% vs. 87.5%). On SSV2-Full, it outperforms MoLo (the previous best) by 1.1% in 1-shot and 4.0% in 5-shot, and surpasses HyRSM++ by 2.7% and 4.8% respectively. On UCF-101, the method leads CCLN by 5.1% in 1-shot (92.0% vs. 86.9%) and 2.6% in 3-shot (96.8% vs. 94.2%), but falls 0.6% short in 5-shot (95.5% vs. 96.1%). On HMDB-51, the method underperforms CCLN by 5.1%, 4.4%, and 1.8% in the 1-, 3-, and 5-shot settings respectively, which the authors attribute to the dataset's emphasis on static or low-motion actions where trajectory information provides less discriminative signal. On Fine-Gym, the method beats MoLo by 8.5% (1-shot), 5.8% (3-shot), and 3.1% (5-shot), demonstrating particular strength on fine-grained actions with clear motion cues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in few-shot action recognition, where 3D CNNs or end-to-end transformers jointly learn spatio-temporal features, by explicitly disentangling motion and appearance into separate frozen components: CoTracker for point trajectories and DINOv2 for semantic patch tokens. The trajectory-aligned tokens (TATs) that fuse these two streams are then processed by a Masked Space-Time Transformer (building on ViViT/ST-Transformer attention) with a Bi-MHM matching loss inherited from HyRSM. The key conceptual shift is that motion is no longer implicitly learned from raw pixels but is instead provided by a dedicated tracking model, while appearance semantics come from a large-scale self-supervised pre-trained encoder, leaving only a small transformer to be trained.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach achieves strong SOTA (e.g., 81.9% on Kinetics 1-shot vs. 74.0% for MoLo) while training only 10.8M parameters versus 89.6M for MoLo, since CoTracker and DINOv2 remain frozen. This 'frozen heavy components + small trainable head' recipe is attractive for data-efficient settings and could generalize to other video understanding tasks. However, the method is sensitive to camera motion and scene cuts, and gains diminish for actions where motion carries little discriminative signal (e.g., smoking, talking). Open questions include incorporating point visibility, developing smarter point sampling strategies, and whether TATs transfer to dense or long-tail action recognition.
