## Problem

MLP-based Implicit Neural Representations suffer from two coupled limitations: their global continuous nature makes them slow to converge on high-frequency details (edges, fine textures), and they require large per-instance parameter sets. While hyper-networks can generate MLP weights in a single forward pass, the underlying MLP architecture still inherits the spectral bias and parameter bloat, leaving a gap in both reconstruction quality and representation efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241400/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes spatial coordinate queries (2D pixel positions for 128×128 or 256×256 images, 3D ray coordinates for NeRF view synthesis, or lat/lon for ERA5 temperature fields) and a compact instance-specific R-Token matrix (d×N, typically 131k parameters) predicted by a transformer-like hyper-network. The ANR function itself is shared (data-agnostic) across all instances; only the R-Tokens vary per data sample. Output is the reconstructed signal at each queried coordinate — RGB values, (σ, RGB) for radiance fields, or scalar temperature — forming a continuous mapping that supports arbitrary-resolution interpolation and super-resolution via denser coordinate sampling.

## Method

ANR replaces the linear layers of a standard MLP-based INR with a Localized Attention Layer (LAL) followed by a small MLP. Coordinate inputs are mapped through Fourier positional encoding to form attention Queries, while the instance-specific R-Tokens are linearly projected to Keys and Values; the LAL fuses them via an L-softmax operation that thresholds attention weights below a constant m to zero, restricting each query to only its most relevant tokens and blocking irrelevant gradient flow. A transformer-like hyper-network (with cross-attention) predicts the R-Tokens from the raw data instance, decoupling instance-specific content from the shared ANR converter. Theoretically, the LAL is shown to act as a signal filter on wave-signal representations, and variational coordinates (Gaussian perturbations added to query positions during training) are introduced to prevent aliasing artifacts from overly high-frequency positional encodings, enabling meaningful super-resolution at inference.

## Results

On CelebA (128×128), ANR achieves a PSNR of 47.25 dB, substantially outperforming the best MLP-based baseline IPC (37.95 dB) by over 9 dB while using the same 131k representation parameters. On LSUN (256×256), ANR reaches 27.54 dB (depth 5) and 28.30 dB (depth 7), surpassing IPC (26.00 dB) and TransINR (24.21 dB). On the ERA5 temperature field, ANR attains 51.49 dB PSNR versus 44.65 dB for TransINR and 44.05 dB for IPC. For 3D view synthesis on ShapeNet, ANR NeRF edges out IPC NeRF by 0.13 dB on Cars and 0.38 dB on Chairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANR is compared against two MLP-based INR baselines: TransINR (Chen & Wang, ECCV 2022), which uses a transformer hyper-network to predict full MLP weights, and IPC (Kim et al., CVPR 2023), which uses a hyper-network to predict modulated MLP columns. On CelebA, ANR (47.25 dB) outperforms IPC (37.95 dB) by 9.3 dB and TransINR (36.25 dB) by 11 dB, while matching IPC's 131k representation parameter budget. On LSUN, ANR (27.54–28.30 dB) beats IPC (26.00 dB) by 1.5–2.3 dB and TransINR (24.21 dB) by 3.3–4.1 dB. On ERA5, ANR (51.49 dB) surpasses TransINR (44.65 dB) by 6.8 dB and IPC (44.05 dB) by 7.4 dB, indicating particular strength on smoother, less varied signals. For 3D view synthesis on ShapeNet, ANR NeRF marginally outperforms IPC NeRF (26.57 vs 26.44 dB on Cars; 21.45 vs 21.07 dB on Chairs), with the gap being smaller than on 2D reconstruction tasks. Notably, ANR uses a zero-depth representation MLP (all capacity in the global attention + MLP converter), whereas IPC requires at least one representation MLP layer, yet ANR still achieves superior quality with the same 131k token budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hyper-network paradigm for INR generation (TransINR, IPC) by replacing the predicted MLP weights with compact instance-specific R-Tokens, and by inserting a localized attention layer (LAL) directly inside the INR function rather than using attention only as an external conditioning mechanism. The LAL's design is grounded in the wave-signal propositions of Yüce et al., which the authors use to argue that the attention filtering operation is the core differentiator from a plain MLP. The anti-aliasing strategy via variational coordinates draws on VAE-inspired stochastic perturbation, applied here to coordinate sampling rather than latent variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9.3 dB PSNR gain on CelebA over the strongest MLP-based hyper-network baseline (IPC) is substantial and suggests that the spectral-bias limitation of MLPs can be meaningfully mitigated by embedding attention within the representation function itself. The parameter-efficiency story (same 131k R-Tokens, far better quality) makes ANR a practical drop-in for downstream generative pipelines, as demonstrated by the preliminary diffusion experiment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or video/4D settings, whether the L-softmax threshold is sensitive across domains, and whether the variational-coordinate anti-aliasing generalizes beyond the tested frequency ranges.
