## Problem

In source-free domain adaptation, pseudo-labels assigned to target samples are inherently noisy due to domain shift, and existing methods either iteratively refine these labels or filter low-confidence samples. This paper identifies that the noise in pseudo-labels has a structured, class-dependent pattern that can be explicitly modeled, and that the source model's outputs contain information about this noise distribution that prior label-noise-learning methods (designed for human annotation errors) do not exploit.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target images, a frozen source-trained classifier, and a pre-trained Swin-B feature extractor. Pseudo-labels are generated once at the start of adaptation (not updated per epoch as in Co-learning) by computing class centroids in Swin-B feature space weighted by source-model softmax probabilities, then assigning each target sample to its nearest centroid via cosine distance. The output is a K×K noise transition matrix T̂ that, when multiplied with the adapted network's softmax, yields the predicted noisy pseudo-label distribution; at inference the matrix is discarded and the clean class posterior is used directly.

## Method

DCPL reformulates target adaptation as learning with noisy labels by introducing a learnable noise transition matrix T̂ (initialized as identity) on top of the adapted classifier's softmax, following the noise-adaptation-layer paradigm. The key novelty is a domain-adaptation-specific prior: the softmax values of the pseudo-labels (computed as temperature-sharpened cosine similarities to class centroids) are averaged per pseudo-label class to form a prior confusion matrix T_c, which regularizes T̂ learning via an L2 term γ‖T̂ − T_c‖² alongside the standard trace penalty λ·tr(T̂). The full loss replaces the cross-entropy term in existing SFDA frameworks (SHOT, SHOT++, AaD) with CE(T̂·p_θ(y|x_t), ỹ_t) + λ·tr(T̂) + γ‖T̂ − T_c‖², while retaining each method's additional regularization losses. This makes DCPL a drop-in loss replacement that requires no architectural changes and no per-epoch pseudo-label updates.

## Results

DCPL achieves state-of-the-art source-free domain adaptation accuracy across all three benchmarks. On VisDA (ResNet-101), AaD w/ DCPL reaches 89.4% average accuracy, surpassing the previous best (AaD w/ Co-learn at 88.8%) and SHOT++ w/ DCPL reaches 89.3%. On OfficeHome (ResNet-50), SHOT++ w/ DCPL achieves 77.8% average, outperforming Co-learn (76.7%) and AaD w/ co-learn (76.7%). On DomainNet (ResNet-50), SHOT++ w/ DCPL reaches 70.5% average and AaD w/ DCPL reaches 71.6% average, both exceeding their respective co-learn counterparts (69.3% and 71.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCPL consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across the three datasets. Against the strongest pseudo-label refinement baseline, Co-learn [63], DCPL gains +1.2% on VisDA (89.4% vs 88.2%), +0.8% on OfficeHome (77.8% vs 76.7%), and +1.2% on DomainNet (70.5% vs 69.3% for SHOT++). Compared to the base SFDA methods without pseudo-label refinement, DCPL improves SHOT by +3.8% on VisDA (86.7% vs 82.9%), +5.7% on OfficeHome (77.5% vs 71.8%), and +2.3% on DomainNet (69.6% vs 67.3%). Against AaD [59], DCPL gains +2.0% on VisDA (89.4% vs 87.4%), +4.8% on OfficeHome (77.5% vs 72.7%), and +0.1% on DomainNet (71.6% vs 71.5%). Against SHOT++ [29], DCPL gains +2.0% on VisDA (89.3% vs 87.3%), +4.8% on OfficeHome (77.8% vs 73.0%), and +1.2% on DomainNet (70.5% vs 69.3%). The gains are most pronounced on OfficeHome and VisDA where domain shifts are larger, while DomainNet improvements are more modest, suggesting the transition matrix learning is most beneficial when pseudo-label noise is more structured.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCPL repurposes the noise transition matrix framework from learning-with-label-noise (Sukhbaatar et al. 2015; Tanno et al. 2019) for the source-free domain adaptation setting, where label noise originates from domain shift rather than human annotator error. It departs from the dominant SFDA paradigm of iteratively refining pseudo-labels (as in SHOT, Co-learning) by instead treating pseudo-labels as fixed and learning a confusion matrix to correct for their systematic bias. The key methodological novelty is a domain-adaptation-specific prior on the transition matrix derived from the source model's softmax outputs, which has no analogue in standard LLN where noise is assumed instance-independent or instance-dependent but not structured by a known generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle comparison on DomainNet reveals a 7.2% accuracy gap between DCPL and a version using the true transition matrix, indicating that better noise estimation is the primary bottleneck rather than pseudo-label quality from Swin-B. This opens a clear research direction: improving transition matrix estimation (e.g., via iterative refinement or richer priors) could yield substantial further gains without changing the pseudo-labeling pipeline. Limitations include the closed-set, single-source assumption, and the fact that the learned matrix still shows estimation errors on well-separated classes (e.g., Knife). Whether the approach extends to open-set or multi-source SFDA remains an open question.
