## Problem

Existing concept-removal methods for text-to-image diffusion models specify the target concept via text (e.g., the word 'nudity'), which fails to capture the nuanced, context-dependent judgments humans actually make about harmful content. The model's internal ethical criteria systematically diverge from human standards, leaving a gap that text-based targeting cannot bridge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241785/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, a set of human feedback annotations (binary labels for well-defined concepts like nudity, or pairwise rankings for ambiguous concepts like artist style) collected on model-generated images, and produces a fine-tuned diffusion model that suppresses the target concept while preserving generation quality for unrelated prompts. The human feedback is condensed into a single soft token in the text-embedding space, which then replaces the text token in any downstream concept-removal procedure, making the framework composable with existing inference-time and fine-tuning baselines.

## Method

HFI first trains a reward model (MSE loss for binary feedback, Bradley-Terry pairwise loss for ranking feedback) on CLIP image embeddings, then learns a soft token v* via reward-weighted textual inversion that maximizes the expected harmfulness score of generated images. SDD then fine-tunes the diffusion model using a self-distillation objective: the student's conditional noise estimate given v* is regressed toward the stop-gradient unconditional estimate, with an EMA teacher model supplying the latents for training so that only not-yet-removed content is targeted. Only cross-attention layers of the U-Net are updated, and timesteps are sampled from a Beta(3,3) distribution to concentrate distillation on mid-range timesteps where conditional and unconditional noise diverge most. The EMA teacher gradually shifts its own distribution away from the target concept, providing increasingly clean self-supervision signals throughout training.

## Results

On the primary harmful-concept removal task, HFI+SDD reduces NSFW image generation from 74.18% (SD v1.4) to 0.28% and exposed-body-part detection from 32.14% to 0.32%, while reducing the I2P shocking-content ratio from 65.77% to 23.36%. On the artist-style removal task, HFI+SDD achieves an average target-artist ratio (%ART) of 12.7% and photo-similarity ratio (%PHO) of 12.0% across three artists, compared to 97.8% and 1.9% for the original SD. Utility on MSCOCO-10K is preserved with FID of 17.023, CLIPScore of 0.2643, and LPIPS of 0.1818, all competitive with or better than most baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFI+SDD achieves the best removal performance across all three safety metrics: 0.28% NSFW (vs. 0.80% for ESD-u-3, 2.42% for ESD-u-1, 4.18% for ESD-x-3), 0.32% NUDE, and 23.36% I2P shocking content. Among fine-tuning baselines, ESD-u-3+HFI is the closest competitor on removal (0.80% NSFW) but degrades image quality more (FID 18.615 vs. 17.023, LPIPS 0.2247 vs. 0.1818). Inference-time methods (SLD, SEGA, NEG) and cross-attention methods (FMN, UCE) with HFI still leave substantial unsafe content (42–61% NSFW for SLD/SEGA/FMN), though UCE+HFI achieves the best FID (16.541) and LPIPS (0.1264) at the cost of weaker removal. On artist-style removal (Table 1), HFI+SDD attains the lowest combined %ART+%PHO for Van Gogh (14.6) and Afremov (11.8), but is outperformed by ESD-x-1+HFI (31.7) and NEG+HFI (32.6) on Edward Hopper, where the paper acknowledges a few HFI-enhanced baselines edge it out. Overall, HFI+SDD consistently surpasses all non-HFI baselines and delivers the strongest safety–utility trade-off among fine-tuning methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the textual inversion (TI) framework for learning soft tokens and the growing family of concept-removal methods for diffusion models (inference-time steering via SLD/SEGA, cross-attention optimization via FMN/UCE, and fine-tuning via ESD). Its key departure is replacing text-specified target concepts with human-feedback-informed soft tokens, drawing on reward-weighted NLL from Lee et al. and the Bradley-Terry model for ranking-based feedback. The proposed SDD loss is a self-distillation simplification of ESD's training objective, eliminating the need to manually specify replacement concepts or which concepts to retain, while an EMA teacher provides stable, gradually-shifting distillation targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a modular, human-aligned mechanism for concept removal that can be plugged into existing baselines, addressing a recognized gap where text-based specifications fail to capture the nuance of harmful or copyrighted concepts as humans perceive them. The dramatic reduction in NSFW generation (74% → 0.28%) and the observation that text-based methods over-correct (e.g., converting paintings to photographs when removing an artist's style) highlight the practical value of human judgment in safety alignment. Limitations include dependence on the quality and consistency of human feedback, the relatively small annotation scale (200–450 images per concept), and the fact that the approach is validated only on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with a limited set of target concepts, leaving open questions about scalability to newer architectures and broader concept taxonomies.
