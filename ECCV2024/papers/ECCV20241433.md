## Problem

Fine-tuning CLIP models on sequential tasks in continual learning causes catastrophic forgetting that degrades their zero-shot transfer ability, yet existing methods either over-prioritize zero-shot preservation at the cost of adaptability or fail to exploit the rich intra- and inter-modal relational structure inherent in multi-modal models. The paper addresses the unresolved tension between maintaining generalization and adapting to new data distributions by proposing a distillation strategy that preserves relational proximity rather than individual outputs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 224×224 RGB images encoded by a ViT-B/16 (patch size 16) and natural-language text prompts (class labels) encoded by a text encoder, producing class labels via cosine similarity between image and text embeddings. Distillation is performed on unpaired reference data (wild images and sentences) rather than the original pre-training pairs, and the student model is fine-tuned sequentially on each task's labeled dataset while the proximity distillation loss is computed on the reference set.

## Method

The core mechanism constructs a bipartite cross-modal graph where image and text samples form nodes and inter-modal connections form edges, then defines first-order proximity (direct image-text cosine similarity, capturing inter-modal classification relationships) and second-order proximity (similarity of conditional neighborhood distributions, capturing intra-modal relationships mediated through the other modality). Both proximities are distilled from two teachers—the original pre-trained CLIP (C₀) and the model after the previous task (C_{i-1})—using cross-entropy losses on the proximity distributions. A sample-wise re-weighting scheme dynamically computes per-sample weights via a softmax over the distances between the current student and each teacher, so that samples where the student has drifted far from C₀ receive stronger pull toward zero-shot preservation, while samples well-aligned with C_{i-1} receive stronger pull toward task adaptability. The final training objective combines this adaptive dual-teacher proximity distillation loss with the standard cross-entropy fine-tuning loss on the current task's labeled data.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (11 cross-domain tasks), the proposed method achieves 69.8% Transfer, 76.9% Avg., and 85.1% Last accuracy under Order I, exceeding the original zero-shot CLIP (69.4% Transfer) while substantially improving over all continual learning baselines. On CIFAR-100 CIL (10 steps), it reaches 86.15% Avg. and 79.75% Last accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline ZSCL by 4.0% and 6.1% respectively. On TinyImageNet CIL (5 steps), the method achieves 82.43% Avg. and 77.53% Last, again leading all competitors. Notably, the method is the only approach that surpasses the original zero-shot CLIP on the Transfer metric, demonstrating simultaneous preservation of zero-shot capability and adaptation to new data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor, ZSCL [45], is outperformed on every metric across both MTIL orders and both CIL benchmarks: on MTIL Order I, the proposed method gains +1.7% Transfer, +1.5% Avg., and +1.5% Last over ZSCL; on CIFAR-100 (10-step CIL), the gap widens to +4.0% Avg. and +6.1% Last. Among conventional continual learning baselines, LwF-VR [4] (57.2% Transfer) and WiSE-FT [34] (52.3% Transfer) suffer the most severe zero-shot degradation, with the proposed method exceeding them by 12.6% and 17.5% on Transfer respectively. iCaRL [27] and LwF [17] show moderate forgetting (50.4% and 56.9% Transfer) and are outperformed by 19.4% and 12.9% on Transfer. On the CIL setting, the method also surpasses non-CLIP-specific baselines (UCIR [11], DER [36], DyTox+ [6], EWC [13]) by large margins, e.g., 86.15% vs. 79.35% Avg. over iCaRL on CIFAR-100 (10 steps). The only baseline the method does not clearly dominate is the original zero-shot CLIP on Transfer (69.8% vs. 69.4%, a marginal +0.4%), but it substantially outperforms it on Avg. (+11.6%) and Last (+19.8%), confirming that fine-tuning with the proposed distillation adds value beyond the frozen model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ZSCL framework for CLIP continual learning by replacing its single-teacher KD-divergence distillation with a dual-teacher graph-based proximity distillation. The core conceptual import comes from LINE's first-order and second-order proximity definitions, which are repurposed here to model inter-modal (image-text) and intra-modal (image-image or text-text) relationships within CLIP's embedding space. Unlike prior CLIP continual learning methods (LwF-VR, ZSCL) that distill individual output distributions or rely on reference data to anchor zero-shot performance, this work distills relational structure via a cross-modal graph, and introduces a sample-wise adaptive weighting mechanism to balance the original pretrained CLIP (C₀) against the previous iteration (C_{i-1}).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in CLIP continual learning—preserving zero-shot generalization while adapting to new domains—and shows that modeling relational proximity rather than individual features provides a more flexible constraint. The consistent improvements over ZSCL across MTIL and CIL benchmarks, along with faster convergence, suggest the graph-based formulation is a practical and effective regularization. However, the method is evaluated only on ViT-B/16 CLIP and two standard continual learning settings; whether the second-order proximity computation scales efficiently to larger models or longer task sequences, and whether the reference-data requirement limits real-world applicability, remain open questions.
