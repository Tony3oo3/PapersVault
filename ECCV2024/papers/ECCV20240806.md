## Problem

Existing cross-domain video anomaly detection methods rely on unsupervised learning, which cannot capture the specific characteristics of anomalies and yields performance well below real-world expectations. While weakly-supervised VAD works well in single-domain settings, directly applying it across domains causes significant performance drops due to context-dependent anomaly definitions, class imbalance, and limited labeled data. The paper asks whether combining a small weakly-labeled source set with abundant unlabeled external videos can close this cross-domain gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips at 30 FPS, extracting frame-level features via a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder and segment-level features via an I3D 3D-convolution backbone. Frame-level CLIP features are bilinearly interpolated to a fixed number of segments (n_s=64) to reduce temporal redundancy. At inference, only the CLIP-based main model P_m is used; segment-level anomaly scores are mapped to frame-level scores by assigning each segment's score to its consecutive frames, with the last segment absorbing any remainder.

## Method

The framework trains two prediction heads with contrasting inductive biases—P_m on CLIP features and P_a on I3D features, each topped with a 4-head transformer encoder and FC layers—using a ranking loss on weakly-labeled data. Pseudo-labels for external unlabeled videos are generated iteratively, and uncertainty is quantified by computing cosine similarity between the penultimate-layer representations of P_m and P_a, transformed via an exponential function with a temperature parameter to yield bounded scores in [0,1]. During training on the union of labeled and external data, the BCE prediction bias on external segments is adaptively reweighed by these uncertainty scores (high score → high confidence → full weight; low score → high uncertainty → down-weighted), and a cosine-similarity loss term explicitly encourages the two models to agree in feature space. This iterative pseudo-label refinement, uncertainty-driven reweighing, and feature-space agreement loss constitute the core mechanism that distinguishes the approach from prior probability-space uncertainty methods like KL-divergence-based reweighing.

## Results

On the primary cross-domain evaluation (UCF-Crime as weakly-labeled source, XD-Violence as target), the proposed CDL framework with HACS external data achieves 65.14% AP on XDV and 90.53% AUC on UCF-R, surpassing the best prior cross-domain method (zxVAD: 40.68% AP, 69.39% AUC) by 24.46% and 21.14% respectively. Using XDV itself as external data further boosts XDV AP to 68.37%. In the reverse direction (XDV as source, UCF-Crime as target), the method reaches 88.50% AUC on UCF-R with HACS external data, a 24.89% gain over zxVAD. The paper reports average absolute improvements of 19.6% on UCF-Crime and 12.87% on XD-Violence over state-of-the-art cross-domain baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three cross-domain unsupervised baselines (rGAN, MPN, zxVAD) are substantially outperformed on both cross-domain metrics: the proposed method gains 24–28% AP on XDV and 21–25% AUC on UCF-R over zxVAD, the strongest unsupervised cross-domain baseline. Among non-cross-domain weakly-supervised methods, the proposed approach without external data already surpasses most (Sultani, MIST, RTFM, S3R, CU-Net, MGFN) on UCF-R AUC (89.96% vs. 84.63–88.15%), though SSRL (87.02% UCF-R) and CLIP-TSA (87.58% UCF AUC) remain competitive on in-domain UCF-Crime AUC. With external data, the gap widens decisively: Ours (UCF+HACS) reaches 90.53% UCF-R AUC and 65.14% XDV AP, exceeding SSRL by 3.51% and 13.54% respectively. In the reverse cross-domain setting (Table 3), the method achieves 88.50% UCF-R AUC with HACS, outperforming zxVAD (63.61%) by 24.89%, though it trails CLIP-TSA (80.67%) on XDV AP (78.61%), which the authors attribute to annotation noise in XDV's test set. In the open-set setting (Table 4), the method with CDL surpasses all baselines (Wu et al., RTFM, Zhu et al.) across all values of c (1–9 anomaly classes), with the largest margin at c=1 (85.39% vs. 76.73% UCF-R AUC).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-domain video anomaly detection (VAD) line of work (rGAN, MPN, zxVAD), which has been exclusively unsupervised, into a weakly-supervised regime by leveraging video-level labels on a source domain. Methodologically, it adapts the uncertainty-driven pseudo-label learning framework of Zheng et al. (IJCV 2021) from domain-adaptive semantic segmentation to VAD, but replaces KL-divergence-based uncertainty in probability space with cosine similarity in high-dimensional feature space, motivated by the limited support of binary classification posteriors. The model-augmentation strategy—using two architecturally distinct backbones (CLIP ViT and I3D) to estimate prediction variance—departs from MC Dropout or data-augmentation-based uncertainty approaches, which the authors argue are ill-suited for video's temporal structure and fixed-backbone training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: deployed VAD systems must handle anomalies in unseen scenes, yet prior cross-domain methods lack explicit anomaly knowledge because they are unsupervised. The substantial gains (19.6% on UCF-Crime, 12.87% on XD-Violence) demonstrate that cheap video-level labels combined with abundant unlabeled data can close much of the cross-domain gap. However, the comparison is primarily against unsupervised baselines; a fair comparison against strong weakly-supervised methods adapted to cross-domain settings would be needed to fully validate the magnitude of improvement. Open questions include how the framework scales to more heterogeneous external data, whether the two-backbone requirement can be relaxed, and whether the iterative pseudo-label refinement converges reliably in the presence of severe domain shift.
