## Problem

Generalizable human rendering from very sparse views (e.g., 3 images) without test-time optimization remains unsolved: NeRF-based methods are too slow to combine with auxiliary modules like inpainting, while existing 3D Gaussian approaches either require per-subject optimization or fail when input views lack sufficient overlap for depth estimation. The complex geometry of humans—articulations, self-occlusions, hair, and loose clothing—makes accurate reconstruction from a handful of views particularly difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242099/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3 sparse multi-view RGB images at 1024×1024 resolution, their camera extrinsics, and a pre-fitted SMPL human template as input, and produces photorealistic novel-view renderings via a set of 3D Gaussians whose parameters are stored as 2D maps in the template's UV space. Unusually, the Gaussians are not free-floating in 3D but are anchored to five concentric scaffold surfaces (the template mesh dilated outward at 1 cm intervals), with each scaffold carrying its own parameter map. The RGB appearance map is computed by visibility-weighted back-projection from all input views, and an inpainting GAN fills unobserved UV regions before the main network runs.

## Method

The core insight is to reformulate 3D Gaussian parameter fitting as a 2D regression problem on the UV map of a human template, enabling the use of a U-Net that exploits local pixel neighborhood for consistent parameter prediction. The network receives two encoded cues—geometry (offset maps between consecutive scaffolds) and appearance (multi-scaffold RGB maps, with inpainting applied to the base scaffold)—and outputs per-scaffold Gaussian parameter maps (rotation quaternion, scaling, opacity) for all five scaffold levels. A multi-scaffold representation (S=4 outer shells, d=1 cm) bridges the gap between the parametric template and real geometry such as hair and clothing, with Gaussian scaling confined to the inter-scaffold distance to ensure stable training. An attention-based inpainting GAN, trained separately on pseudo ground-truth UV textures, hallucinates unobserved regions, a combination enabled by the 2D UV formulation. The entire pipeline is feed-forward: trained once on THuman 2.0 with multi-view RGB and mask supervision (L1 + SSIM + BCE), it generalizes to unseen subjects and even cross-dataset (RenderPeople) without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On the in-domain THuman→THuman sparse-view (3 views) setting, GHG achieves LPIPS 133.41 and FID 61.67, substantially outperforming NHP (LPIPS 184.69, FID 136.56) and NIA (LPIPS 181.82, FID 127.30) on perceptual metrics, though with slightly lower PSNR (21.90 vs. 23.32/23.20). In the cross-domain THuman→RenderPeople setting, GHG again leads on LPIPS (137.73) and FID (60.85) over both NeRF baselines. Against Gaussian-based methods with 5 views on THuman, GHG achieves PSNR 22.06 and FID 37.10, beating GPS-Gaussian (PSNR 20.69, FID 46.26) and Vanilla-GS (PSNR 17.62, FID 210.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two NeRF-based baselines (NHP and NIA), GHG achieves substantially better perceptual quality: LPIPS improves by ~50 points and FID by ~65–75 points in both in-domain and cross-domain settings, while PSNR is ~1.3 dB lower. The paper attributes the PSNR gap to pixel-wise sensitivity of PSNR to slight geometric deviations, noting that NHP's blurry single-surface output inflates its PSNR. Against GPS-Gaussian (5 views), GHG wins on PSNR (+1.37 dB) and FID (37.10 vs. 46.26) but trails on LPIPS (132.42 vs. 123.30), and does so with only 3 input views versus GPS-Gaussian's 5. Against per-subject Vanilla 3D-GS, GHG dominates on all three metrics (PSNR 22.06 vs. 17.62, LPIPS 132.42 vs. 220.30, FID 37.10 vs. 210.03), demonstrating the benefit of the feed-forward generalizable formulation over per-subject optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) by Kerbl et al. but fundamentally repositions it from a per-scene optimization problem to a feed-forward regression task in the 2D UV space of a human template (SMPL). It departs from NeRF-based generalizable human renderers (NHP, NIA) by replacing slow per-pixel volume rendering with fast rasterization, enabling integration of 2D inpainting modules. The multi-scaffold representation extends the single-surface shell-map idea from prior human rendering work into a generalizable, input-conditioned setting, addressing the geometry gap between template and real human surfaces (hair, loose clothing).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UV-space formulation is a practical architectural choice that unlocks 2D CNN local context and seamless inpainting integration—capabilities that are difficult to achieve with per-pixel NeRF pipelines. Cross-dataset generalization (THuman → RenderPeople) without fine-tuning demonstrates robustness to distribution shift in race, age, and apparel. However, the method still depends on accurate SMPL fitting at inference, and the lower PSNR (despite superior perceptual metrics) relative to NeRF baselines suggests residual geometric misalignment that remains an open challenge. Extending the approach beyond humans to other articulated or deformable objects, and reducing reliance on template fitting quality, are natural next steps.
