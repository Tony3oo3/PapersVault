## Problem

Estimating per-point 3D scene flow from mmWave radar point clouds for human motion sensing is non-trivial because radar point clouds are extremely sparse (~100 points), contaminated by multi-path ghost points, and lack reliable Doppler velocity for sub-0.5 m/s human movements. Existing scene flow methods target LiDAR/RGB-D or automotive radar with rigid-body assumptions, making them inapplicable to the non-rigid, sparse, and temporally inconsistent radar data encountered in human-centric scenarios.

## Input / Output

The network consumes two consecutive Vayyar vTrigB mmWave radar point clouds (each ~100 points with per-point intensity) and outputs a 3D displacement vector per source point, clamped to a fixed range of ±ε (e.g., 0.1 m) on each axis to prevent non-physical magnitudes. Pseudo ground-truth labels are generated automatically from a co-located RealSense D455 RGB-D camera via OpenPose 2D keypoint detection and depth-based 3D uplift, then propagated to radar points through nearest-skeleton rigid-body transformation, with a validity mask filtering low-confidence or implausible keypoints.

## Method

milliFlow is a five-module end-to-end network: (1) multi-scale local feature extraction via four parallel SA (PointNet++) layers with varying grouping radii, (2) global feature aggregation using attention-weighted pooling to produce a holistic frame descriptor concatenated back to each point, (3) flow embedding generation through a cost-volume correlation layer between the two frames' local-global features, (4) temporal propagation via a GRU that updates only the global vector (not per-point embeddings) to handle variable point counts across frames, and (5) constrained regression with per-axis clamping. Training uses a weighted L2 loss that up-weights points with large displacements to prevent convergence to trivially small uniform flows, supervised entirely by automatically generated pseudo labels requiring no manual annotation.

## Results

On the milliFlow dataset, the proposed method achieves a cm-level average EPE3D of 4.6 cm (0.046 m) and a relaxed Acc3D of 70.3%, ranking first on all five scene flow metrics with a large margin over the next-best baseline (RaFlow at 10.7 cm EPE3D and 42.7% Acc3D Relax). For downstream tasks, incorporating scene flow as a plug-and-play module improves human activity recognition accuracy by +10.56% (47.32% → 57.88%) and human parsing mIoU by +3.63% (49.09% → 52.72%). The model runs at ~13.5 Hz with only 134 MB GPU memory, enabling real-time deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

milliFlow outperforms all nine scene flow baselines on every metric in Table 1. The closest competitor is RaFlow [24], the only other radar-based method (also by the same first author), which achieves 10.7 cm EPE3D and 42.7% Acc3D Relax; milliFlow reduces EPE3D by 57% (0.046 vs 0.107 m) and nearly doubles Acc3D Relax (70.3% vs 42.7%). Among LiDAR/RGB-D deep learning methods, PV-RAFT [83] is the strongest at 16.1 cm EPE3D and 29.2% Acc3D Relax, still 3.5× worse in EPE3D than milliFlow. Classical optimization-based methods (Graph Prior [62], NSFP [50]) perform poorly, with Graph Prior achieving only 0.7% strict accuracy, confirming that non-learning approaches struggle with the extreme sparsity of mmWave radar. For downstream HAR, scene flow augmentation improves both the authors' base network (+10.56%) and the two SOTA mmWave HAR methods MMPointGNN [30] (+7.70%) and RadHAR [69] (+5.33%), demonstrating that the scene flow module is a general-purpose plug-in rather than being tied to a specific backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scene flow estimation paradigm from LiDAR/RGB-D point clouds (FlowNet3D, PointPWC-Net, PV-RAFT) and automotive radar (RaFlow, cross-modal 4D radar flow) to the fundamentally different setting of mmWave radar point clouds for non-rigid human motion. It repurposes the cost-volume mechanism from PointPWC-Net and the multi-scale local encoding from PointNet++, but departs from prior radar scene flow work by replacing the rigid-body assumption with a skeleton-based pseudo-labeling scheme and adding GRU-based temporal propagation to compensate for the absence of Doppler velocity. The cross-modal automatic labelling via co-located RGB-D and OpenPose is a novel contribution that avoids the labour-intensive annotation required by prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that per-point scene flow can be reliably estimated from sparse, noisy mmWave radar point clouds in human-centric scenarios, and that the resulting flow vectors provide tangible gains across three downstream tasks (HAR, human parsing, body-part tracking) without requiring any manual annotation. Key limitations include evaluation on a single radar model (Vayyar vTrigB), a modest dataset (~36k in-set frames), and tracking drift that accumulates over multi-frame sequences. Open questions include generalization to other mmWave radar hardware (e.g., TI IWR6843), multi-person scenes, and whether the skeleton-based pseudo-labeling assumption holds for highly dynamic or occluded motions.
