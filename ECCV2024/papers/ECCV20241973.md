## Problem

Existing neural implicit surface reconstruction methods rely on either viewing direction or reflection direction as the directional input to their radiance network, but each has a distinct failure mode: viewing direction cannot correctly decouple geometry from appearance on specular surfaces, while reflection direction produces overly smooth reconstructions that miss concave or complex structures. No prior work had analyzed why reflection direction fails on complex geometry, and no unified parameterization existed to handle both scenarios simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241973/cropped_page1_idx10.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (with optional foreground masks) and outputs a 3D signed distance function (SDF) from which a mesh is extracted via zero-level-set extraction. The hybrid directional parameterization is a drop-in modification to the radiance network's directional input, requiring fewer than 10 lines of code to integrate into existing pipelines like NeuS or NeuDA. The only additional learnable parameter is a single scalar γ (initialized via γ_b = 0.3 for most scenes), and the SDF gradient is detached in the blend-weight computation to avoid coupling with geometry optimization.

## Method

The proposed hybrid directional parameterization computes a per-sample-point blend between the reflection direction d_ref and the viewing direction d_view, weighted by α = exp(−γ · detach(|f(x)|)), where f(x) is the predicted SDF value and γ is a learnable positive scalar controlling transition speed. Near the surface (SDF ≈ 0), α → 1 so the direction aligns with the reflection direction, preserving the specular-decoupling benefit; far from the surface, α → 0 so the direction reverts to the non-learnable viewing direction, avoiding the two identified failure modes of reflection direction (irrelevant-surface normal contamination and high-frequency directional variation from geometry updates). The blended vector is normalized to unit length and fused before positional encoding, which ablation studies show yields better reconstructions than post-PE fusion. The approach is nearly parameter-free, requires no architectural changes beyond the directional input, and is validated as a plug-in for both MLP-based (NeuS) and voxel-grid-based (NeuDA) surface reconstruction frameworks.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, Ours (NeuDA) achieves the best average Chamfer Distance of 0.63, outperforming NeuDA (0.67), Voxurf (0.72), NeuS (0.77), and IDR (0.90). On the Shiny Blender dataset (specular objects), Ours (NeuS) achieves the best overall performance with 0.45 average Accuracy and 0.63 average MAE, surpassing the closest competitor Ref-NeuS (0.46 Acc, 0.91 MAE) by a large margin on MAE. The hybrid directional parameterization consistently improves specular object reconstruction while maintaining parity with the original viewing-direction baseline on diffuse objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original NeuS and NeuDA baselines (which use viewing direction), the hybrid parameterization yields significant improvements on specular objects (e.g., DTU Scan 63: 1.10 vs 1.13 CD for NeuS; Shiny Blender helmet: 0.29 vs 4.88 Acc) while maintaining comparable performance on diffuse objects. Against Ref-NeuS [8], the closest competitor that also uses reflection direction, Ours (NeuS) achieves a lower average MAE (0.63 vs 0.91) and marginally better average Accuracy (0.45 vs 0.46) on Shiny Blender, with the largest gap on the 'coffee' object (0.55 vs 0.99 MAE) where Ref-NeuS fails to recover concave cup geometry. On DTU, Ours (NeuDA) achieves the best average Chamfer Distance (0.63) among all compared methods including Voxurf (0.72), NeuS (0.77), and IDR (0.90). The reflection-direction variant of NeuS/NeuDA degrades significantly on diffuse DTU objects (e.g., Scan 37: 4.39 vs 0.98 CD for NeuS), confirming the paper's analysis that reflection direction is harmful for complex geometry, whereas the hybrid direction avoids this failure mode. On the real-captured 'toycar' scene, only the hybrid direction successfully recovers both the reflective glass and complex concave geometry, while both viewing and reflection directions produce visibly incorrect surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the neural implicit surface reconstruction framework established by NeuS and NeuDA, and specifically addresses the directional parameterization choice in the view-dependent radiance network. It departs from Ref-NeRF (Verbin et al., 2022) and Ref-NeuS (Ge et al., 2023), which established reflection direction as a superior parameterization for specular objects, by identifying and analyzing the failure mode of reflection direction on concave/complex geometries. The proposed hybrid direction is a minimal, spatially-aware modification that blends viewing and reflection directions based on SDF distance, rather than introducing a new architecture or training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid directional parameterization is nearly parameter-free and requires fewer than 10 lines of code to integrate into any existing neural surface reconstruction method, making it broadly adoptable. The in-depth analysis of why reflection direction fails for concave structures—specifically that normals at sampled points away from the surface are contaminated by unrelated geometry—provides a useful diagnostic framework for the community. The main open question is the per-object tuning of the initial γ_b value, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; an automated selection strategy would make the method fully plug-and-play.
