## Problem

Existing Open-Vocabulary VIS methods suffer from poor generalization to novel categories due to two overlooked issues: a domain gap between frozen VLM (CLIP) features and the instance queries generated by the segmentation backbone, and the underutilization of temporal consistency because both training and inference are conducted at the image level rather than the video level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241871/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip of T frames (T=2 during training, up to 5 at inference) at 360px (ResNet-50) or 480px (SwinB) shorter side, along with a vocabulary of category names formatted as 'A photo of [category name]' fed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder. It produces 100 video-level instance queries per clip, each yielding a per-pixel segmentation mask across all T frames, an open-vocabulary class score over K categories, and an instance score; instances are then associated across consecutive clips via cosine similarity and Hungarian matching during semi-online inference.

## Method

OVFormer builds on Mask2Former's query-based architecture but introduces three key changes. First, it extends positional encoding to the spatio-temporal domain so that a single set of N=100 instance queries represents the entire clip rather than individual frames. Second, the core contribution is a Unified Embedding Alignment (UEA) module: a lightweight cross-attention layer (1.2M params, 0.1G FLOPs) where instance queries serve as Q and CLIP image embeddings (concatenated across T frames) serve as K and V, producing feature-aligned class embeddings that bridge the domain gap before dot-product classification against text embeddings. Third, it trains at the video level (sampling T frames per clip) and deploys a semi-online inference scheme that processes non-overlapping clips sequentially and associates instances between clips via cosine similarity with the Hungarian algorithm, balancing temporal consistency with scalability to long videos.

## Results

On the primary open-vocabulary VIS benchmark LV-VIS, OVFormer with ResNet-50 achieves 21.9 mAP (22.1 mAP_base, 21.8 mAP_novel), exceeding the previous state-of-the-art OV2Seg by +7.7 mAP. With Swin-B backbone, performance further improves to 24.7 mAP on LV-VIS. For zero-shot generalization on close-vocabulary datasets, OVFormer (R50) obtains 34.8 mAP on YouTube-VIS 2019, 29.8 mAP on YouTube-VIS 2021, and 15.1 mAP on OVIS, all surpassing the open-vocabulary baseline OV2Seg by +7.6, +6.2, and +3.9 mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary open-vocabulary VIS baseline OV2Seg [36], OVFormer wins decisively across all four evaluation datasets: +7.7 mAP on LV-VIS (21.9 vs 14.2), +7.6 on YouTube-VIS 2019 (34.8 vs 27.2), +6.2 on YouTube-VIS 2021 (29.8 vs 23.6), and +3.9 on OVIS (15.1 vs 11.2). The gap is especially pronounced on novel categories, where OVFormer achieves 21.8 mAP_n vs OV2Seg's 11.9 mAP_n on LV-VIS (+9.9), demonstrating the effectiveness of unified embedding alignment. Compared to pipeline baselines (Detic-SORT, Detic-OWTB, Detic-XMem, DetPro-SORT), OVFormer outperforms all by 13+ mAP on LV-VIS, confirming that end-to-end training with video-level queries substantially surpasses the propose-reduce-association paradigm. Against close-vocabulary fully-supervised methods, OVFormer (R50, zero-shot) outperforms SipMask [2] on YouTube-VIS 2019 (34.8 vs 33.7) and MaskTrack [43] on YouTube-VIS 2021 (29.8 vs 28.6), but trails Mask2Former-VIS [5] on YouTube-VIS 2019 (34.8 vs 46.4) and OVIS (15.1 vs 17.3), which are trained with full supervision on the target dataset. The paper notes that in a close-set training setting (with novel labels retained), OVFormer reaches 51.4 mAP on YouTube-VIS 2019, exceeding Mask2Former-VIS by +5.0 mAP.

## Relation to Prior Work

OVFormer builds directly on the Mask2Former query-based segmentation architecture and the OV2Seg end-to-end open-vocabulary VIS framework, adopting their video-level instance query paradigm from Mask2Former-VIS. The key departure is the introduction of a lightweight cross-attention module (unified embedding alignment) that explicitly bridges the domain gap between VIS instance queries and frozen CLIP image features, rather than computing classification scores via direct query-to-text similarity as in OV2Seg and prior propose-reduce-association pipelines. Additionally, the paper shifts from image-level training (as in OV2Seg and OVIS) to video-level training with a semi-online clip-by-clip inference scheme, a design choice more aligned with offline VIS methods but made practical through Hungarian matching between clips.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-parity between base and novel category performance (22.1 vs. 21.8 mAP on LV-VIS) challenges the prevailing assumption that novel categories are inherently much harder to segment, suggesting that the domain gap between VLM and segmentation features was the primary bottleneck rather than data scarcity. The simplicity of the solution—a 1.2M-parameter cross-attention module—makes the unified embedding alignment technique potentially transferable to other open-vocabulary tasks beyond VIS. However, absolute performance remains low (21.9 mAP on LV-VIS), the method requires careful clip-length tuning, and scalability to very long videos with many instances remains an open question. Whether the alignment strategy generalizes to other VLMs (e.g., DINOv2, SigLIP) or benefits from joint fine-tuning rather than frozen CLIP features is unexplored.
