## Problem

Existing LiDAR semantic segmentation methods assume a closed-set label space, making them unable to handle unknown classes that inevitably appear during real-world autonomous driving deployment. While on-the-fly category discovery (OCD) has been explored for image classification, extending it to LiDAR point clouds introduces unique challenges: the sparse nature and lack of texture in LiDAR make hash-code-based representations highly sensitive to intra-class variance, and no dedicated mechanism exists for discovering unknown classes in this modality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single LiDAR scan (a set of 3D points with coordinates) as input and produces per-point semantic labels through hash code-based clustering. The projected representation is an L-dimensional vector (L=12) whose sign pattern forms a binary hash code serving as the class descriptor; points sharing the same hash code are assigned to the same class. This design yields an expandable prediction space where novel classes are discovered as new hash codes emerge at test time, enabling instant per-sample inference without access to the full test distribution.

## Method

The architecture uses MinkUNet34 (sparse convolution) as a feature extractor followed by an MLP projection head mapping to an L-dimensional space. Dual Prototypical Learning (DPL) introduces a second set of parametric hash prototypes alongside standard cosine-similarity prototypes, enforcing via L2 distance on tanh-transformed representations that intra-class points share the same hash code, thereby reducing sensitivity to intra-class variance. Mixing-based Category Learning (MCL) then adds three modules: a mixing module applies manifold mixup between different-class representations to synthesize OOD samples, a discrimination module maximizes entropy of mixed representations w.r.t. both prototype sets (with stopped gradients on the mixed features) to push them away from known-class prototypes, and a grouping module applies contrastive loss plus hash-code consistency loss on mixed representations sharing the same mix label to learn semantic clustering of OOD points. The total loss combines the standard cross-entropy, hash prototype loss, and the three MCL losses, trained entirely on known-class data.

## Results

On Scenario-A (observed-unknown classes), the proposed method achieves 40.23% All mIoU under Strict-Hungarian on SemanticPOSS (vs. 28.30% for SMILE and 25.85% for MLDG) and 36.44% on SemanticKITTI (vs. 26.59% for SMILE and 22.62% for MLDG). Under Greedy-Hungarian, the gains are even larger: 47.53% on SemanticPOSS and 42.93% on SemanticKITTI. In Scenario-B (unobserved-unknown), the method reaches 45.08% All mIoU (Strict) on SemanticKITTI, outperforming SMILE (29.67%) and MLDG (25.70%). In Scenario-C (mixed unknown types), it achieves 42.45% All mIoU (Strict), again surpassing both baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms both baselines (MLDG and SMILE) on All mIoU across every scenario and both datasets. On SemanticPOSS Scenario-A, it gains +11.93% over SMILE and +14.38% over MLDG in Strict-Hungarian All mIoU, with the gap widening to +9.69% and +16.72% under Greedy-Hungarian. On SemanticKITTI Scenario-A, the improvements are +9.85% over SMILE and +13.82% over MLDG (Strict). The one exception is Scenario-B (unobserved-unknown), where MLDG achieves a higher Unknown mIoU under Strict-Hungarian (25.80% vs. 21.73%), likely because its meta-learning simulation of unknown classes aids generalization to truly unseen classes; however, the proposed method still dominates on Known (47.83% vs. 25.68%) and All (45.08% vs. 25.70%) mIoU. In Scenario-C, SMILE achieves a higher Unknown mIoU under Greedy-Hungarian (51.86% vs. 37.19%), suggesting it captures some unknown semantics, but the proposed method wins decisively on Known (47.76% vs. 43.89%) and All (46.09% vs. 45.15%) mIoU, indicating a more balanced recognition-and-discovery capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the on-the-fly category discovery (OCD) framework from Du et al. (CVPR 2023), originally formulated for 2D image classification, to the 3D LiDAR semantic segmentation domain. It departs from both open-set recognition (which merely rejects unknown classes) and novel category discovery (which is transductive and requires unknown-class data at training time) by requiring inductive, per-sample instant inference. The method builds on the hash-coding-based expandable prediction space from [14] as its architectural backbone, but introduces dual prototypical learning to address LiDAR-specific intra-class variance and a mixing-based category learning framework (leveraging manifold mixup) to explicitly model out-of-distribution representations during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OCDSS is practically relevant for autonomous driving, where novel objects (e.g., construction equipment, unusual vehicles) inevitably appear at test time and must be semantically grouped rather than simply flagged as unknown. However, the performance gap between known and unknown classes remains substantial (e.g., 46.6% vs. 21.5% mIoU on SemanticPOSS), and the authors acknowledge that exploring better representation learning for unknown class discovery is an open direction. The hash-coding inference, while enabling instant per-sample prediction, constrains the model to a fixed-dimensional binary code space, raising questions about scalability to a large number of unknown classes. The evaluation protocol (Greedy/Strict-Hungarian matching) is borrowed from image OCD and may not fully capture the spatial coherence expected in segmentation.
