## Problem

The curvature of ODE trajectories in diffusion models severely degrades sample quality when the number of function evaluations (NFE) is reduced below ~10. Existing curvature-reduction methods based on coupling optimization (trajectory relocation) struggle to preserve the marginal noise distribution, require additional regularization, and break compatibility with SDE-based downstream tasks like image editing. This paper asks whether trajectory intersection can be reduced without relocating trajectories, while retaining the full flexibility of score-based models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242175/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At generation time, the method takes a random Gaussian noise vector and a single condition code (a d-dimensional integer vector drawn from a learned codebook of size up to 2^20, with per-channel levels L=2) as input, and produces an RGB image at 32×32 (CIFAR-10) or 64×64 (FFHQ, AFHQv2). For zero-shot editing tasks (super-resolution, colorization, inpainting), a reference image is additionally provided and the method uses SDE-based sampling with Karras's schedule at N=40 steps. The condition code is not a spatial feature map but a single compact vector, keeping the encoder under 0.8M parameters.

## Method

The core mechanism is a lightweight quantized encoder that assigns each training image a discrete condition code via Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) with sigmoid-based per-channel discretization and Straight-Through Estimator gradients, effectively creating pseudo-labels that partition the data space. During training, the denoiser is conditioned on this code through two MLP layers added to the time embedding, and the conditional CFM loss is minimized jointly with the encoder. A key design choice is online sampling-weight collection via EMA-synchronized counting of code indices per mini-batch, which resolves the time-inconsistency between EMA model parameters and the code distribution. At sampling time, a code index is drawn from the collected weights, and the reverse ODE/SDE is simulated conditioned on that code, allowing trajectories to pass through intersection regions without curvature. The approach adds only ~1% training parameters, requires no trajectory relocation or extra regularization, and preserves SDE-based editing capabilities that coupling-optimization methods lose.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32), QAC achieves 5.14 FID at 6 NFE, outperforming EDM's 5.32. On FFHQ (64×64), QAC reaches 6.91 FID at 6 NFE versus EDM's 8.50, and on AFHQv2 (64×64) it achieves 3.10 FID at 6 NFE versus EDM's 3.78. The advantage is most dramatic at very low NFE: at 3 NFE on AFHQv2, QAC scores 8.32 FID while EDM scores 15.60, a gap of over 7 points. At 20 NFE on CIFAR-10, QAC matches the best full-sampling quality (2.10 FID) while using only 1/50th the function evaluations of DDPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QAC outperforms all 13 baselines on CIFAR-10 at 6 NFE (5.14 FID), beating the closest accelerated sampler AMED (6.63), DPM-Solver-v3 (8.56), DEIS (9.40), UniPC (11.10), and DPM-Solver++ (11.85) by 1.5–6.7 FID points. Against the Rectified Flow baseline, QAC reduces FID from 37.19 to 19.68 at 5 NFE (Table 5), and when combined with RNE the FID drops further to 18.20. Compared to RNE alone (24.54 FID at 5 NFE), QAC alone (19.68) is substantially better, and the combination (18.20) is best. On FFHQ and AFHQv2, QAC consistently beats EDM at every NFE level, with the largest relative gains at 3 NFE (e.g., 8.32 vs. 15.60 on AFHQv2, a 47% reduction). At full sampling (20 NFE), QAC achieves 2.10 FID on CIFAR-10, matching LSGM's 2.10 at 147 NFE and approaching NCSN++'s 2.20 at 2000 NFE, demonstrating that the method also improves quality under generous budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Rectified Flow / conditional flow matching framework (Liu et al., ICLR 2022) and the EDM training recipe (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2022), but departs from the coupling-optimization line of work (Lee et al. 2023; Pooladian et al. 2023) that reduces ODE trajectory curvature by relocating forward trajectories. Instead of moving trajectories, the authors introduce a lightweight quantized condition encoder (adapted from Finite Scalar Quantization, Mentzer et al. 2023) that acts as a 'traffic light,' allowing backward PF-ODE paths to pass through intersection regions without curvature or trajectory relocation. The method is explicitly designed to be complementary to the Reparamized Noise Encoder (RNE), sharing the encoder backbone and combining additively, while preserving the full score-based model properties (SDE sampling, likelihood evaluation) that coupling optimization sacrifices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a <1% parameter overhead yields 5.14 FID on CIFAR-10 at 6 NFE and 3.10 FID on AFHQv2 at 6 NFE, closing most of the gap to full-sampling quality while remaining compatible with SDE-based editing (inpainting, colorization, super-resolution). The theoretical Wasserstein bound (Theorem 1) connecting the conditional CFM loss to per-step discretization error provides a principled justification for why reducing forward-flow intersection helps few-step generation. Open questions include whether feature-map conditions (rather than single code vectors) can further improve expressiveness, whether better noise schedules exploit the method's ability to reconstruct from high noise levels, and how the approach extends to distillation or fine-tuning pipelines at scale.
