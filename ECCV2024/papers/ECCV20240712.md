## Problem

Post-training quantization (PTQ) relies on a small calibration set (e.g., 1,024 images) to quantize a full-precision model, but existing methods train the quantized model solely on this calibration data without any validation set, leading to severe overfitting. No prior PTQ work introduces a mechanism to validate the quantized model during the quantization process or to create a separate training distribution from the limited calibration data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240712/cropped_page12_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained full-precision CNN (ResNet-18, ResNet-50, or MobileNetV2) and a calibration set of 1,024 ImageNet training images as input. It produces a quantized model at target bit-widths (4/4, 3/3, 2/4, 2/2 for weights/activations) along with a learned UNet transformation network. The UNet maps each original calibration image to a modified version that serves as training data, while the original images are held out as validation data during the quantization process.

## Method

MetaAug formulates PTQ as a bi-level (meta-learning) optimization: a UNet transformation network T modifies calibration images to create a training set, while the original calibration images serve as a validation set for the quantized model. The inner loop quantizes the model block-by-block (following BRECQ) by minimizing an activation-matching loss on the modified data, using Genie-style learnable scale/rounding for weights and LSQ for activations. The outer loop updates T to minimize a KL-divergence validation loss of the quantized model on original images, combined with a margin loss that penalizes T for being too close to an identity mapping and a probabilistic knowledge transfer (PKT) distribution preservation loss that ensures the modified images retain the feature-space distribution of the original calibration set. This separation of training and validation data during quantization is the key distinction from all prior PTQ methods, which train and implicitly evaluate on the same calibration set.

## Results

On ImageNet, MetaAug* (with 8-bit second-layer activation) achieves top-1 accuracy of 69.97% (ResNet-18), 75.78% (ResNet-50), and 69.22% (MobileNetV2) at 4/4 bit-width, and 57.89%, 60.50%, and 19.61% at the extreme 2/2 setting. In the 2/2 setting, MetaAug* outperforms the prior state-of-the-art Bit-Shrinking* by 0.56%, 1.47%, and 1.38% on ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and MobileNetV2 respectively. The method also yields the smallest train-test accuracy gap (23.42% vs. 26.39% for QDrop and 30.16% for PD-Quant at 2/2), confirming reduced overfitting on the 1,024-image calibration set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaAug* outperforms all six PTQ baselines (AdaRound, BRECQ, QDrop, PD-Quant, Genie-M, Bit-Shrinking) across all three architectures and all four bit-width settings, with the largest margins at 2/2 (e.g., +4.18% over Genie-M on ResNet-18, +1.47% over Bit-Shrinking* on ResNet-50). The only exception is ResNet-50 at 4/4, where Bit-Shrinking* (76.04%) slightly edges out MetaAug* (75.78%). Against the two augmentation baselines (Mixup and CutMix) evaluated at 2/2 on ResNet-18, MetaAug alone (54.22%) beats both Mixup (54.05%) and CutMix (54.15%), and combining MetaAug with CutMix yields the best result (54.63%), indicating complementary effects. The overfitting analysis (Table 3) shows MetaAug achieves the smallest train-test gap (23.42% at 2/2) compared to QDrop (26.39%), PD-Quant (30.16%), and Genie-M (27.01%), while simultaneously attaining the highest test accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the block-wise PTQ pipeline established by BRECQ and Genie (learnable scale/rounding for weights, LSQ for activations) and repurposes the MAML-style bi-level optimization framework for a new purpose: jointly training a UNet-based transformation network and the quantized model. It departs from all prior PTQ methods (AdaRound, QDrop, PD-Quant, Bit-Shrinking, Genie) that train the quantized model solely on the original calibration set without any validation signal, by splitting the role of calibration data into a modified training set and an original validation set. The distribution preservation loss draws on PKT's probabilistic knowledge transfer, and the margin loss is a novel regularization to prevent the transformation network from collapsing to an identity mapping. Unlike prior meta-learning-for-quantization work (MetaQuantNet, MEBQAT, MetaMix), which targets QAT and quantization policy search, this is the first to apply meta-learning to the data side of PTQ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized weakness of PTQ—overfitting to a small calibration set—by introducing a principled train/validation split via learned data modification, and the train-test gap analysis (Table 3) provides direct evidence that the approach reduces overfitting. However, the absolute gains over the strongest baselines (Genie-M, Bit-Shrinking) are modest (0.5–1.5% Top-1), and the transformation network is limited to photometric-style modifications without geometric transforms, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. An open question is whether the bi-level optimization cost (500 iterations of UNet updates per block, plus 2×10⁴ quantization iterations) remains practical for very large models or for settings where even 1,024 calibration images are unavailable. The demonstrated complementarity with Mixup and CutMix suggests the framework could serve as a general augmentation backbone for PTQ pipelines.
