## Problem

Deep learning models frequently learn spurious correlations that inflate performance on certain subgroups while degrading accuracy on others. Existing mitigation methods either demand exhaustive spuriousness annotations across the entire dataset (high cost) or sacrifice competitive performance when such labels are absent. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether a minimal, targeted set of human annotations can suffice to construct a feature-balanced training subset that yields robust models without expensive multi-stage training procedures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241224/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (e.g., 224×224 for ResNet-50) and produces class labels, but its distinguishing input is a small set of binary yes-no human judgments on whether a reference model's GradCAM attention is correct, required for fewer than 3% of instances in the relevant split. The output is not a single model but a curated, feature-balanced data subset (5–30% of the original set) from which a robust classifier is then trained via standard ERM or DFR-style last-layer retraining.

## Method

SLIM is a three-phase data construction pipeline built around a novel attention representation space. First, it projects attention-weighted feature vectors (F_A = A ⊙ F, where A is the GradCAM attribution in latent space) via UMAP into a 2D space where local neighbors share both data features and model attention patterns. Second, it samples representative instances via k-means clustering, collects binary attention-correctness feedback from humans, and propagates labels to all instances using a label-spreading algorithm. Third, it filters out instances with incorrect attention, disentangles core features (attention-weighted F_A) from environment features (inverse-attention-weighted F_A̅) in the latent space, clusters each set, and applies a consistency-weighted sampling strategy that upweights less-consistent clusters to ensure balanced core-environment feature combinations. The final curated subset is trained with a straightforward ERM procedure (SLIM_Tr) or DFR-style last-layer retraining (SLIM_Val), avoiding the multi-iteration or data-augmentation overhead of competing methods.

## Results

On binary classification tasks, SLIM_Tr achieves the best worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds (89.1%) and ISIC (79.1%), while SLIM_Val leads on Waterbirds (91.0%). On multi-class tasks, SLIM_Tr attains the highest worst-group accuracy on NICO (78.0%) and ImageNet-9 (68.1%). Critically, SLIM requires attention annotations for only 0.12%–2.5% of the training set and trains on just 0.1×–0.3× of the original data, compared to 5×–12× additional training cost for methods like JTT and CNC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLIM_Tr outperforms all no-label baselines on worst-group accuracy: it beats CNC (88.5→89.1 on Waterbirds, 68.2→79.1 on ISIC) and JTT (83.1→89.1 on Waterbirds, 28.1→79.1 on ISIC) while incurring far lower training cost (0.1×–0.2× vs. 2×–12× and 5×–6×, respectively). Against label-based methods, SLIM_Tr surpasses GDRO on ISIC (79.1 vs. 66.8) and DFR on ISIC (79.1 vs. 71.9) and CelebA (89.4 vs. 87.3), though GDRO edges out SLIM_Tr on Waterbirds (89.9 vs. 89.1) and DFR slightly leads on Waterbirds (89.6 vs. 89.1). SLIM_Val achieves the overall best Waterbirds worst-group accuracy (91.0%), exceeding GDRO (89.9) and DFR (89.6). On multi-class NICO, SLIM_Tr leads all methods (78.0 vs. CNC 72.6, GDRO 73.3, DFR 70.9), and on ImageNet-9 it achieves 68.1% worst-group accuracy versus CNC's 58.9%. The only notable losses are SLIM_Tr trailing SSA by 0.4 points on CelebA worst-group (89.4 vs. 89.8) and GDRO by 0.8 points on Waterbirds, both within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLIM positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: spuriousness mitigation via group-balanced training (GDRO, DFR, SSA) and human-in-the-loop model debugging (attention maps, bounding boxes, yes-no feedback). It departs from the first by replacing expensive spuriousness labels with minimal binary attention-correctness feedback, and from the second by reducing annotation from 100% of the dataset to under 3% via a novel attention representation space that enables label propagation to neighbors. The training stage builds directly on DFR's last-layer retraining for the validation variant, while the data curation philosophy (balanced feature distribution over complex optimization) echoes GDRO's core insight but operationalizes it through model-attention-derived features rather than human-defined spuriousness groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: matching or exceeding SOTA worst-group accuracy while requiring less than 3% human annotation and 5–30% of the original training data makes spuriousness mitigation far more accessible in real-world deployment where full spuriousness labeling is infeasible. The finding that attention-correctness annotation is both faster and more consistent than spuriousness labeling (94–95% vs. 70–82% inter-annotator agreement) provides empirical justification for shifting the human-in-the-loop interface. Open questions include whether the attention-space mechanism generalizes to more complex spuriousness structures (multi-level, compositional), whether the approach holds for non-CNN architectures at scale, and whether the data curation pipeline can be automated further to eliminate even the minimal human input.
