## Problem

Existing deep stereo matching methods predict disparities independently for each frame, regressing from scratch over a global disparity range. This leads to temporally inconsistent outputs—especially in ill-posed regions such as occlusions, reflective surfaces, and low-texture areas—where small camera or object motion causes severe disparity jitters that degrade downstream tasks like SLAM and autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240826/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stereo video stream (rectified left-right image pairs) together with per-frame camera poses and intrinsics, processing frames sequentially in an online manner. It supports both multi-frame mode (using projected previous-frame disparity as initialization) and single-frame mode (falling back to a semi-dense disparity derived from the cost volume via winner-take-all with a confidence threshold). The output is a dense, temporally consistent disparity map per frame, with the final hidden state and disparity projected forward to serve as temporal context for the next frame.

## Method

TC-Stereo formulates video stereo matching as temporal disparity completion followed by iterative dual-space refinement. First, a Temporal Disparity Completion (TDC) module projects the previous frame's disparity into the current viewpoint (or extracts a semi-dense map from the cost volume for the first frame) and completes it via a lightweight encoder-decoder, yielding a well-initialized dense disparity and state features. A GRU-like Temporal State Fusion module then combines these current state features with the past refinement hidden state to produce a coherent initial hidden state, avoiding the pitfalls of directly reusing warped past states. The core refinement alternates between disparity-space updates (multi-level GRU retrieving cost volume, as in RAFT-Stereo) and disparity-gradient-space updates (computing local gradients, refining them via an encoder-decoder, and propagating disparity to neighbors under a local planar hypothesis), so that smoothness constraints propagate from well-constrained regions into ill-posed areas over iterations. Training uses a composite loss combining a contrastive cost-volume loss (from HITNet), L1 disparity losses at each refinement stage with exponential decay weighting, and L1 gradient losses, all supervised on TartanAir, SceneFlow, and pseudo-labeled KITTI data.

## Results

On KITTI 2015, TC-Stereo achieves a D1-all error of 1.46% (All) and 1.38% (Noc), outperforming the previous best IGEV-Stereo (1.59% D1-all) by 8.18% while running at 0.09 s/frame versus 0.18 s for IGEV-Stereo. On SceneFlow, TC-Stereo attains the lowest >1px (4.93%) and >3px (2.39%) error rates among all compared methods, with an EPE of 0.50 px (slightly behind DLNR's 0.47 px). On TartanAir, TC-Stereo with only 5 iterations matches or surpasses RAFT-Stereo with 32 iterations on all metrics (e.g., EPE 0.52 vs. 0.53 px in ALL regions), and on ETH3D SLAM zero-shot it achieves the best EPE (6.27 px) with 5 iterations versus 6.36–6.41 px for 32-iteration baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TC-Stereo outperforms all ten baselines on KITTI 2015 D1-all (both Noc and All splits), with the closest competitor IGEV-Stereo [45] trailing by 0.13% (1.59% vs. 1.46%) in the All split. On SceneFlow, TC-Stereo wins on >1px (4.93% vs. 5.38% for DLNR [52]) and >3px (2.39% vs. 2.75% for TemporalStereo [50]) but loses narrowly on EPE to DLNR (0.50 vs. 0.47 px), which the authors attribute to DLNR's stronger backbone and more iterations. On TartanAir, TC-Stereo with 5 iterations beats RAFT-Stereo [22] with 32 iterations on EPE in occluded regions (1.36 vs. 1.45 px) and >3px (16.36% vs. 16.67%), while TemporalStereo [50] is outperformed on all three metrics in both OCC and ALL splits. On ETH3D SLAM zero-shot, TC-Stereo (N=5) achieves the best EPE (6.27 px) and >3px (23.29%), surpassing both RAFT-Stereo (N=32, 6.36 px) and IGEV-Stereo (N=32, 6.41 px). In terms of efficiency, TC-Stereo at 0.09 s/frame is 2× faster than IGEV-Stereo (0.18 s) and 4× faster than RAFT-Stereo (0.38 s), while HITNet [35] is faster (0.02 s) but substantially less accurate (1.98% D1-all). CODD [20] and DynamicStereo [15] are compared only on SceneFlow, where TC-Stereo leads on both >1px and >3px error rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the iterative stereo matching paradigm of RAFT-Stereo by integrating temporal information into the refinement loop, and borrows the contrastive cost-volume loss and disparity-gradient refinement concept from HITNet. It departs from prior video stereo methods (XR-Stereo, TemporalStereo, DynamicStereo) in a key architectural decision: rather than performing post-hoc fusion or simply reusing the previous frame's disparity and hidden state as initialization, it first completes the semi-dense projected disparity via a dedicated encoder-decoder module and fuses current and past hidden states through a lightweight GRU-like gate before entering refinement. This 'complete-then-refine' strategy addresses the non-overlapping regions between consecutive frames that naive temporal reuse ignores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal inconsistency in disparity sequences directly degrades downstream tasks in autonomous driving, SLAM, and AR/VR, yet most SOTA stereo methods still operate frame-by-frame. TC-Stereo's 4× reduction in iterations (5 vs. 32) while matching or exceeding RAFT-Stereo's accuracy makes temporally consistent stereo matching practical for real-time deployment, and the dual-space (disparity + gradient) refinement offers a reusable architectural pattern for stabilizing ill-posed regions. Open questions remain around robustness to large dynamic object motion, significant pose errors, and minimal inter-frame overlap, where the method degrades to global search. Whether the temporal completion and state-fusion components generalize to longer sequences or more aggressive camera motion is not fully explored.
