## Problem

Current 3D scene understanding methods rely on fine-tuning LLMs/VLMs with 3D data, but 3D data collection is expensive, labor-intensive, and severely limited in diversity (confined to CAD models, indoor scans, and driving scenarios). This paper asks whether a VLM can understand 3D scenes in a zero-shot manner by actively selecting and interpreting multiple 2D viewpoints, mimicking how humans perceive 3D space without explicit reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240495/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh and camera intrinsics as input, rendering a bird's-eye-view (BEV) image augmented with Set-of-Line Prompting (grid lines, tick marks, and a Cartesian coordinate overlay). The VLM outputs camera extrinsic parameters as grid coordinates (e.g., (0,0)) and discrete orientations from ['left','right','front','back'], which are then used to render N multi-view images. Downstream outputs include text answers for 3D QA, scene captions, task decompositions, and 3D semantic segmentation maps (via SAM-based 2D segmentation back-projected with depth).

## Method

Agent3D-Zero is a training-free agent framework that uses GPT-4V to iteratively select informative viewpoints for 3D scene understanding. The key technical contribution is Set-of-Line Prompting (SoLP), which superimposes uniform grid lines, tick marks, and directional markers on the BEV image, enabling the VLM to reason about spatial positions and generate precise camera poses without explicit 3D data. Camera parameters are constrained: x,y are predicted by the VLM as grid points, z is fixed at average scene height, pitch/roll are zero, and azimuth is discretized to four cardinal directions. The VLM selects viewpoints iteratively (N' at a time) rather than all at once, leveraging prior selections to refine subsequent choices. For perception tasks like 3D semantic segmentation, the agent orchestrates SAM for 2D region segmentation, assigns semantic labels via VLM reasoning, and back-projects results into 3D point clouds using depth information.

## Results

On the ScanQA test set, Agent3D-Zero (zero-shot, using GPT-4V) achieves METEOR 16.9, ROUGE-L 39.3, and CIDEr 77.5, surpassing the best fine-tuned baseline 3D-LLM (BLIP2-flant5) which scores METEOR 14.9, ROUGE-L 35.3, and CIDEr 69.6. However, Agent3D-Zero trails on n-gram-based metrics: BLEU-1 is 31.4 vs. 38.3 and EM is 21.3 vs. 23.5 for 3D-LLM. On the 3D-LLM held-in dataset, Agent3D-Zero substantially outperforms all fine-tuned baselines on Task Decomposition (BLEU-1 42.0, METEOR 22.9, ROUGE-L 45.1 vs. best baseline 34.1/16.5/35.4) and matches or exceeds them on 3D-assisted dialog (METEOR 19.3 vs. 18.9). For zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation on ScanNet v2, iterative viewpoint selection improves mIoU from 5.6 (random views) to 8.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest fine-tuned baseline 3D-LLM (BLIP2-flant5), Agent3D-Zero wins on semantic-level metrics (METEOR +2.0, ROUGE-L +4.0, CIDEr +7.9) but loses on n-gram precision metrics (BLEU-1 −6.9, BLEU-4 −6.5, EM −2.2), which the authors attribute to BLEU/EM rewarding exact word matches over diverse phrasing. Compared to two-stage pipelines (VoteNet+MCAN, ScanRefer+MCAN, ScanQA), Agent3D-Zero achieves higher METEOR, ROUGE-L, and CIDEr while remaining training-free. On the 3D-LLM held-in dataset, Agent3D-Zero dramatically outperforms all fine-tuned methods on Task Decomposition (BLEU-1 42.0 vs. 34.1 for 3D-LLM BLIP2-opt; METEOR 22.9 vs. 16.5) and matches 3D-LLM (BLIP2-flant5) on 3D-assisted dialog ROUGE-L (39.3 vs. 39.3) while exceeding it in METEOR (19.3 vs. 18.9). The zero-shot LLaVA single-image baseline is far weaker (BLEU-1 7.1, CIDEr 5.7 on validation), confirming that multi-view active selection is critical. Overall, Agent3D-Zero trades some exact-match accuracy for substantially better semantic coherence and generalization without any 3D-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant fine-tuning paradigm for 3D understanding (3D-LLM, 3DMIT) that requires collecting and annotating 3D data to train a dedicated perceiver, instead repurposing a general-purpose VLM (GPT-4V) in a zero-shot agent loop. Conceptually, it builds on the Set-of-Mark prompting idea for visual grounding and extends it to a spatially-structured Set-of-Line Prompting (SoLP) for camera pose planning, while drawing on agent-based multi-step reasoning frameworks. The task formulations (3D QA, dialog, task decomposition, segmentation) follow 3D-LLM, but the methodological contribution is the active viewpoint-selection agent and the visual prompting mechanism rather than any new 3D representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a zero-shot VLM agent can match or exceed fine-tuned 3D-specific models on semantic metrics (METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr) for 3D QA without any 3D training data, suggesting that the bottleneck for 3D understanding may be less about 3D-specific representations and more about strategic multi-view observation. However, the approach is tightly coupled to GPT-4V's API, requires a pre-existing 3D mesh for rendering, and achieves only 8.7 mIoU on 3D segmentation—far below practical thresholds. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to real-time sensor inputs without mesh access, whether SoLP transfers to other VLMs, and whether the agent loop can be made computationally tractable at scale.
