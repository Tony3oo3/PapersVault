## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image compression methods either operate at extremely low bitrates where reconstructed content diverges from the original, require costly fine-tuning of the diffusion backbone, or perform the full iterative diffusion sampling process (tens to hundreds of steps) from pure noise. No prior work leverages a foundation diffusion model as a denoiser for quantization error in a practical compression setting with per-content, inference-time bitrate control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241632/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and produces a compressed bitstream (entropy-coded quantized latent plus side information: per-channel quantization parameters γ and predicted denoising timestep t) and, at the receiver, a reconstructed RGB image. The quantized latent is inverse-transformed and fed into the diffusion model starting from a partially noisy state rather than pure noise, so only a small subset of denoising steps (typically 2–7% of the full process) is needed. Training uses 256×256 crops from Vimeo-90k; evaluation is on Kodak (768×512), CLIC2022 (768×768 center crops), and MSCOCO 30k (256×256).

## Method

The key insight is that quantization error in the latent space is analogous to Gaussian noise, so a pre-trained latent diffusion model can be repurposed as a denoiser that recovers information lost during quantization without any architectural modification or fine-tuning of the backbone. A fully convolutional parameter estimation network P_φ(y, λ) jointly predicts per-channel adaptive quantization parameters γ and the optimal number of denoising steps t, conditioned on the target rate-distortion trade-off λ, enabling a single model to operate across a range of bitrates. The frozen Stable Diffusion v2.1 VAE encoder/decoder and diffusion model handle encoding, decoding, and denoising, while only P_φ and an Entroformer-based entropy model are trained (36M trainable parameters vs. 1.3B total). During training, a single DDIM step with the predicted x̃₀ is used as a surrogate for the full denoised output to keep backpropagation tractable, and continuous functions replace discrete timestep lookups to allow gradient flow through the predicted timestep.

## Results

The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art rate-realism performance, outperforming all baselines (HiFiC, ILLM, CDC, HFD) on FID-bitrate curves across Kodak, CLIC2022, and MSCOCO 30k. In pixel-wise distortion, it is competitive with the best generative codecs on LPIPS and MS-SSIM but, as expected for a generative approach, trails in PSNR. In the user study (Elo rating), the method is significantly preferred over all baselines, including CDC which uses on average double the bitrate. Decoding takes 3.49 s per image on an RTX 3090 (vs. 6.87 s for CDC), using only 2–7% of the full diffusion sampling process.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN-based baselines HiFiC [32] and ILLM [34], the proposed method achieves lower FID (better realism) at comparable or lower bitrates, while remaining competitive on LPIPS and MS-SSIM; however, both GAN methods retain an advantage in PSNR, consistent with the rate-distortion-realism tradeoff. Against the diffusion-based CDC [44], the proposed method wins on both FID and perceptual distortion metrics while using roughly half the bitrate and decoding nearly twice as fast (3.49 s vs. 6.87 s). Against HFD [24], the proposed method produces more faithful reconstructions—HFD frequently synthesizes incorrect content (e.g., wrong doors, flowers, murals) or over-smoothed images, whereas the proposed method preserves structural fidelity while still recovering realistic detail. In the Elo user study, the proposed method ranks first under both tournament strategies (per-comparison and per-participant), significantly ahead of CDC despite CDC's ~2× bitrate advantage, confirming that the FID advantage translates to human-perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Diffusion v2.1's latent diffusion architecture (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022), repurposing its VAE encoder/decoder and denoising network as a compression backbone without architectural modification. It departs from prior diffusion-based compression methods (CDC, HFD, Careil et al.) that either regenerate images from pure noise requiring full diffusion sampling or alter the base model and require large-scale fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is reframing quantization error removal as a partial denoising task, so only 2–7% of the full diffusion process is needed, combined with a learned module that jointly predicts adaptive quantization parameters and the optimal denoising timestep per content and target bitrate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first practical diffusion-based image compression codec that leverages a foundation model without fine-tuning, dramatically reducing training cost (under 100k images vs. millions) while achieving state-of-the-art FID and subjective user preference even at half the bitrate of competitors. The inference-time bitrate control via a single model is a practical advantage for deployment. However, the method remains significantly slower than non-diffusion codecs (3.49s vs. 0.27s for ILLM), can warp small objects or straight lines due to the VAE's limited feature dimension, and raises ethical concerns about content misgeneration at very low bitrates. Open questions include whether more efficient diffusion backbones (e.g., Simple Diffusion, adaptive computation) can close the speed gap, and how to give users explicit control over the rate-distortion-realism tradeoff.
