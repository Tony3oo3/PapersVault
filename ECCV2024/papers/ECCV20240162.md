## Problem

Existing score distillation methods for text-to-3D generation struggle to scale to large prompt corpora: SDS suffers from numerical instability due to excessively large gradients, CSD provides ineffective unconditional gradients for prompt-amortized training, and VSD requires finetuning the pretrained diffusion model to align with rendered image distributions, which impairs its text comprehension capability and causes mode collapse at scale (e.g., 100k prompts). The core challenge is how to reduce the noise prediction error between the diffusion prior and the rendered image distribution without modifying the pretrained model's weights.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 3D representation (iNGP, Hyper-iNGP, 3DConv-net, or Triplane-Transformer) that can be rendered from arbitrary camera views. Rendered images are 256×256 for Hyper-iNGP and 64×64 for 3DConv-net/Triplane-Transformer due to GPU memory constraints. Evaluation renders 120 surrounding views per prompt, scored by CLIP recall (R@1) and CLIP text-image similarity (Sim). The 2D diffusion prior is Stable Diffusion v2.1-base (CFG=7.5) or MVDream, and the method supports both prompt-specific (one 3D per prompt) and prompt-amortized (one generator for all prompts) settings.

## Method

ASD exploits the observation that pretrained diffusion models exhibit lower noise prediction errors at earlier timesteps (closer to T_max). Instead of finetuning the diffusion model as VSD does, ASD approximates the rendered image distribution by evaluating the frozen diffusion model at a shifted, earlier timestep t+Δt, using its noise prediction ε_φ(x_{t+Δt}; t+Δt, y^π) as a stand-in for the rendered image score ε_θ. The gradient for updating the 3D generator θ is the weighted difference between noise predictions at t and t+Δt, backpropagated through the rendered image. The timestep shift Δt is sampled uniformly from [0, η(t−T_min)] with η=0.1, so the shift grows as t approaches T_max, matching the empirically observed gap between pretrained and fine-tuned noise prediction error curves. This keeps the diffusion model entirely frozen, preserving its text comprehension capability and enabling stable training across up to 100k prompts.

## Results

With 3DConv-net as the generator, ASD achieves CLIP similarity (Sim) of 0.237 and recall (R@1) of 0.276 on DF415, 0.285/0.058 on AT2520, and 0.199/0.117 on the 100k-prompt CP100k corpus, outperforming CSD and VSD on every metric across all three scales. On the smaller MG15 dataset with Hyper-iNGP, ASD reaches Sim=0.284 and R@1=1.000 in the prompt-amortized setting, surpassing VSD (0.259/0.987) and CSD (0.264/0.972). SDS fails entirely with 3DConv-net due to numerical instability, while VSD collapses to R@1=0.000 on CP100k, demonstrating mode collapse at scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASD outperforms CSD on all metrics across all three corpus sizes: on DF415, ASD achieves Sim 0.237 vs. CSD's 0.176 and R@1 0.276 vs. 0.062; on CP100k, the gap narrows (Sim 0.199 vs. 0.195) but ASD still leads on R@1 (0.117 vs. 0.108). Against VSD, ASD's advantage is dramatic and grows with scale: on CP100k, VSD collapses to R@1=0.000 and Sim=0.103, while ASD maintains R@1=0.117 and Sim=0.199, confirming that VSD's fine-tuning of the diffusion prior causes mode collapse under large prompt corpora. SDS is not comparable with 3DConv-net (training diverges within thousands of iterations) but on the smaller MG15/iNGP setting, ASD matches SDS's R@1=1.000 while achieving slightly higher Sim (0.289 vs. 0.288). On the prompt-amortized Hyper-iNGP task (MG15), ASD (Sim 0.284, R@1 1.000) substantially outperforms ATT3D's reported SDS-based results (Sim 0.195, R@1 0.468), showing that the instability of SDS in amortized training is the key bottleneck that ASD resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely in the score distillation lineage for text-to-3D: SDS (DreamFusion) introduced the KL-divergence-based gradient, CSD replaced the Dirac-distribution assumption with the unconditional diffusion term, and VSD (ProlificDreamer) finetuned the 2D diffusion prior to minimize noise prediction error for better distribution alignment. ASD departs from VSD by observing that earlier diffusion timesteps naturally exhibit lower noise prediction error, so a simple timestep shift (Δt) can approximate VSD's finetuning objective without modifying any pretrained weights. The paper also extends the evaluation scope from prompt-specific optimization to prompt-amortized generator training at unprecedented scale (100k prompts), building on ATT3D's Hyper-iNGP and 3DConvNet architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ASD addresses a practical bottleneck: existing score distillation methods (especially VSD) degrade in text comprehension and suffer mode collapse when the prompt corpus grows beyond a few hundred, limiting their utility for diverse, large-scale 3D generation. By freezing the diffusion model and using only a timestep shift, ASD preserves the pretrained model's prompt understanding while achieving stable training at 100k prompts — a scale no prior score distillation method has reached. The authors acknowledge that for highly regular man-made objects (chairs, airplanes), data-driven methods with abundant 3D training data still outperform, leaving open the question of how to effectively combine score distillation with supervised 3D data for comprehensive text-to-3D coverage.
