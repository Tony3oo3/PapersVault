## Problem

Existing 3D human motion synthesis methods either focus on single-person generation or require additional annotations (action labels, text prompts) to drive two-person interaction synthesis, failing to capture fine-grained temporal synchronization between interacting partners. A key gap remains in synthesizing realistic, temporally-aligned reactive full-body and hand motions of one person conditioned solely on the other person's 3D motion, without any external supervision signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240965/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the actor's 3D body joint positions (N × J_B × 3) and hand joint positions (N × J_H × 3) as the sole conditioning signal, with no text prompts, action labels, or other auxiliary inputs. The reactor's body and hand coordinates are expressed in root-relative and wrist-relative frames respectively for stability. The output is the reactor's full reactive motion as a concatenation of body joints (N × J_B × 3) and finger joints (N × J_H × 3), generated over 500 diffusion steps in a cascaded body-then-hand pipeline.

## Method

ReMoS employs a cascaded two-stage denoising diffusion framework: a first transformer decoder generates the reactor's body joints conditioned on the actor's body via a combined spatio-temporal cross-attention (CoST-XA) that builds an N·J_B × N·J_B attention matrix to jointly model spatial and temporal inter-person dependencies, and a second decoder generates hand joints conditioned on the actor's hands plus binary hand-interaction masks derived from wrist-to-body distances. The hand stage uses a hand-interaction-aware cross-attention (H-XA) that element-wise masks query and key vectors to focus attention on actively interacting hands. Training combines a standard diffusion reconstruction loss, a distance-aware reaction loss with exponentially decaying weights to emphasize spatially proximate joints, and kinematic regularizers (velocity, acceleration, bone-length, foot-sliding). At inference, a spatial guidance function nudges arm-joint alignment by minimizing masked distances between actor and reactor arm joints, improving contact fidelity for fast-paced interactions.

## Results

On the ReMoCap dataset, ReMoS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all metrics. For Lindy Hop, it reaches 40.7 mm MPJPE and 0.12 FID (body), outperforming the best baseline (ComMDM: 59.4 mm, 0.32 FID) by ~31% and ~63% respectively. For Ninjutsu, ReMoS achieves 139.2 mm MPJPE and 0.16 FID (body) versus the best baseline (InterGen: 172.6 mm, 0.32 FID). On the hand FID metric, ReMoS scores 0.26 (Lindy Hop) and 0.35 (Ninjutsu), roughly 50% lower than the best baselines (0.53 and 0.57). On the external ExPI and 2C datasets, ReMoS also achieves the best MPJPE (97.9 mm and 59.1 mm) and FID (0.41 and 0.34) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMoS outperforms all five baselines on every metric (MPJPE, MPJVE, FID body, FID hands) across both ReMoCap scenarios and both external datasets (ExPI, 2C). The largest margins are against GAN-based MixNMatch (70.2 mm vs. 40.7 mm MPJPE on Lindy Hop, ~42% improvement) and transformer-based InterFormer (66.6 mm vs. 40.7 mm, ~39% improvement). Among diffusion-based baselines, ComMDM and InterGen are the closest competitors on body FID (0.32 and 0.30 vs. ReMoS's 0.12 on Lindy Hop), but ReMoS still achieves roughly 2× lower FID. On the hand FID metric, ReMoS's advantage is most pronounced: 0.26 vs. 0.53 (ComMDM) and 0.61 (InterGen) on Lindy Hop, reflecting the benefit of the cascaded hand synthesis with H-XA. On Diversity (closer to GT is better), ReMoS matches or exceeds all baselines on Ninjutsu (10.26 vs. GT 10.51) and is competitive on Lindy Hop (7.62 vs. GT 7.57), while GAN-based methods (MixNMatch: 2.48) and InterFormer (4.54) produce notably less diverse outputs. On the external ExPI dataset, ReMoS edges out InterFormer (97.9 vs. 99.1 mm MPJPE) and achieves the closest diversity to GT (1.98 vs. 2.01), while on 2C it leads by a wider margin (59.1 vs. 62.4 mm MPJPE over MixNMatch).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMoS builds on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the cascaded diffusion strategy (Ho et al., 2022) for two-stage generation, adapting them to the two-person reactive motion setting. It departs from prior two-person interaction methods (InterFormer, MixNMatch, ComMDM, RAIG, InterGen) that require action labels, text prompts, or seed poses by conditioning solely on the actor's 3D motion, and introduces a combined spatio-temporal cross-attention (CoST-XA) that jointly models spatial and temporal inter-person dependencies rather than treating them sequentially. The inference-time spatial guidance draws from guided motion diffusion (Karunratanakul et al., 2023; Rempe et al., 2023), repurposed here to correct arm-joint misalignment in fast-paced contact interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ReMoS addresses a practical gap in animation pipelines: generating plausible, temporally-aligned partner motions without manual annotation, which is especially valuable for contact-heavy scenarios like dancing and martial arts where text labels are imprecise. The ReMoCap dataset (~275.7K frames with finger articulation and multiview RGB) fills a notable resource gap, as no prior two-person interaction dataset provides finger-level motion capture. Open questions include scaling to more than two persons, scene-aware interactions, and reducing the ~24-second inference time for 50 frames to enable real-time or near-real-time use in interactive applications.
