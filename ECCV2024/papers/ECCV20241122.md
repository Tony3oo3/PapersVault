## Problem

Existing controllable text-to-video methods require temporally dense structural condition maps (one per frame), imposing a heavy practical burden on users who must prepare conditions for every synthetic frame. Moreover, most approaches redesign the model architecture to accommodate extra condition inputs, demanding costly full retraining even when a well-trained T2V backbone is already available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241122/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt together with one or a few pixel-aligned condition maps (sketches, depth maps, or RGB images) for arbitrarily chosen keyframes, plus a binary mask channel indicating which frames are conditioned. Unconditioned frames are represented by black placeholder images. The output is a temporally coherent video whose structure follows the sparse conditions while the text prompt guides content and style. The sparsity is fully flexible at inference time, ranging from a single keyframe to near-dense coverage, all handled by the same trained model.

## Method

SparseCtrl adds a lightweight condition encoder on top of a frozen pre-trained T2V diffusion model (AnimateDiff), leaving the backbone untouched. The encoder replicates a ControlNet-style architecture but introduces two key modifications: (1) temporal attention propagation layers that spread condition signals from conditioned keyframes to unconditioned frames, solving the temporal inconsistency problem of a naive frame-wise encoder; and (2) removal of the noised sample z_t from the encoder input, which prevents the encoder from relying on noise rather than actual conditions and eliminates quality degradation in the general domain. A binary mask channel is concatenated with the condition maps to unify arbitrary sparsity levels into a single model input. Training uses a random masking strategy that samples both the number and positions of conditioned frames per iteration, and only the encoder parameters are optimized with the standard diffusion MSE loss.

## Results

On sparse depth-to-video generation (Table 1), SparseCtrl achieves MAE errors of 7.42 and 7.28 at sparsity ratios r_mask=3/4 and 7/8 respectively, substantially outperforming the AnimateDiff+ControlNet frame-wise baseline (12.74 and 15.02) while maintaining temporal consistency above 95. At the dense setting (r_mask=0), SparseCtrl is slightly behind dense baselines (MAE 8.87 vs. 8.30–8.42) but remains competitive. On image animation (Table 2), SparseCtrl achieves the best LPIPS of 0.1448 (vs. 0.2760 for VideoComposer and 0.5540 for DynamiCrafter) with a CLIP score of 92.59, demonstrating strong first-frame fidelity without task-specific design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense-only baselines VideoComposer [59] and Text2Video-Zero [30], SparseCtrl is slightly worse on MAE at full density (8.87 vs. 8.34/8.30) but comparable on temporal consistency (~95.6 vs. 95.3/96.1); critically, these baselines cannot operate at any sparsity level. Against the frame-wise AnimateDiff+ControlNet baseline [20,72], SparseCtrl is competitive at r_mask=0 (8.87 vs. 8.42 MAE) but pulls ahead dramatically as sparsity increases: at r_mask=3/4 the gap widens to 7.42 vs. 12.74 MAE, and at r_mask=7/8 to 7.28 vs. 15.02, indicating the frame-wise encoder degrades while SparseCtrl's temporal propagation layers maintain fidelity. On image animation, SparseCtrl outperforms VideoComposer [59] on LPIPS (0.1448 vs. 0.2760) and CLIP (92.59 vs. 91.56), and substantially beats DynamiCrafter [64] on LPIPS (0.1448 vs. 0.5540), though DynamiCrafter retains an edge on CLIP (96.05) and user preference scores. Overall, SparseCtrl's key advantage is robustness to sparsity: it is the only method that maintains or improves control fidelity as fewer condition frames are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseCtrl extends the ControlNet paradigm of add-on condition encoders from the image domain to video generation, building directly on AnimateDiff's plug-and-play motion module architecture. It departs from prior controllable T2V methods (VideoComposer, Text2Video-Zero, Gen-1) that require temporally dense per-frame condition sequences, instead introducing temporal propagation layers within the condition encoder to interpolate sparse keyframe signals across unconditioned frames. A key architectural departure from vanilla ControlNet is the removal of the noised sample input from the auxiliary encoder, which the authors identify as critical to prevent quality degradation in the video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the user burden from providing conditions for every frame to just one or a few keyframes, SparseCtrl makes controllable video generation substantially more practical for real-world workflows like storyboarding, keyframe animation, and video interpolation. The method's compatibility with both general and personalized T2V backbones (via AnimateDiff) broadens its applicability without requiring retraining of the base model. Limitations include dependence on the pre-trained backbone's domain coverage (e.g., failure on highly stylized inputs absent from WebVid-10M) and the open question of how sparse conditioning scales to longer video sequences or more complex multi-object scenes.
