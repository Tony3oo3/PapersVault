## Problem

Real-world image super-resolution must handle complex, unknown degradations (blur, noise, JPEG compression) that differ substantially from the bicubic assumption used in most SR methods. Existing GAN-based approaches suffer from training instability and produce artifacts due to their one-step generation, while recent diffusion models achieve better quality but require 50–200 iteration steps at inference. The paper asks whether a transformer can iteratively refine SR results in only a few steps by selectively re-predicting uncertain regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241742/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (e.g., 64×64 for ×4 SR) corrupted by real-world degradations and produces a high-resolution RGB image (e.g., 256×256). Internally, the LR image is bicubically upsampled and tokenized by a VQGAN encoder into a 32×32 grid of 1024 discrete tokens matching the HR token grid. For arbitrary input resolutions, the LR image is split into overlapping patches (128-pixel overlap), each processed independently, and the resulting HR patches are aggregated in pixel space using a centered Gaussian kernel with adaptive instance normalization for color consistency.

## Method

RealSRT operates in the discrete token space of a pre-trained VQGAN and uses a masked transformer with two components: a generator that predicts masked HR tokens conditioned on unmasked HR tokens and LR tokens via self- and cross-attention, and a critic that predicts per-token confidence scores by distinguishing originally-masked tokens from unmasked ones. Starting from a fully masked canvas, the model iterates T=4 steps: at each step the generator fills all masked positions in parallel, the critic scores every token, and only the highest-confidence tokens are retained while the rest are re-masked for the next round. A key design choice is using critic confidences (which capture inter-token correlations through attention) rather than the generator's independent per-token probabilities, enabling reliable iterative refinement without the blurring that generator confidences produce. A Conditional Controlling Module (CCM) — a trainable VQGAN encoder plus an attention block with zero-initialized residual — injects LR image features into the VQGAN decoder to enforce color and detail consistency with the input, trained with pixel, perceptual, and adversarial losses while the transformer remains frozen.

## Results

On RealSR, RealSRT achieves the best LPIPS (0.2527) and DISTS (0.1525) among all methods, along with the highest PSNR (25.60 dB) and competitive MS-SSIM (0.8928), using only 4 iterative steps. On DRealSR, RealSRT again leads in LPIPS (0.2640) and DISTS (0.1368), with PSNR of 27.37 dB (nearly matching DASR's 27.39) and MS-SSIM of 0.9061. Compared to diffusion-based methods requiring 50 (DiffBIR) or 200 (StableSR) steps, RealSRT achieves superior full-reference perceptual metrics with only 4 steps, though it trails DiffBIR on no-reference metrics NIQE and CLIP-IQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RealSRT outperforms all six GAN-based baselines (RealSR, BSRGAN, DASR, FeMaSR, Real-ESRGAN+, SwinIR-GAN) on perceptual metrics LPIPS and DISTS on both RealSR and DRealSR, with the largest gap over DASR (LPIPS 0.2527 vs 0.3113 on RealSR). Against diffusion-based methods, RealSRT achieves better LPIPS and DISTS than both DiffBIR (LPIPS 0.2527 vs 0.3625 on RealSR) and StableSR (DISTS 0.1525 vs 0.1582 on RealSR), while requiring only 4 steps versus 50 and 200 respectively. However, RealSRT trails DiffBIR substantially on no-reference metrics: NIQE (5.084 vs 3.708) and CLIP-IQA (0.4832 vs 0.6731) on RealSR, and NIQE (5.323 vs 2.964) and CLIP-IQA (0.5288 vs 0.7045) on DRealSR, which the authors attribute to DiffBIR producing artifacts inconsistent with inputs that nonetheless score well on no-reference measures. On PSNR, RealSRT leads on RealSR (25.60) but is marginally behind DASR on DRealSR (27.37 vs 27.39). The ablation confirms that the critic-based confidence mechanism improves LPIPS from 0.2923 to 0.2850 and DISTS from 0.2065 to 0.1974, while the CCM further boosts PSNR from 24.85 to 25.60 and LPIPS from 0.2850 to 0.2527.

## Relation to Prior Work

RealSRT extends the non-autoregressive masked generation paradigm of MaskGIT and the dual generator-critic confidence mechanism of Token-Critic to the real-world super-resolution setting, adding cross-attention from LR tokens to HR tokens to condition generation on the degraded input. It departs from the dominant GAN-based real-world SR methods (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN, DASR) by replacing their single-step generation with a confidence-guided iterative process, and from diffusion-based SR (DiffBIR, StableSR) by requiring only 4 iterations instead of 50–200. The conditional controlling module (CCM) is a novel addition that addresses color inconsistency in latent-space decoding, a known weakness of VQGAN-based generative pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4-iteration inference cost versus 50–200 for competing diffusion models makes iterative token-based SR practically viable for real-time or resource-constrained applications, while matching or exceeding their perceptual quality. The ablation revealing that generator confidences actively degrade perceptual metrics with more iterations (producing blur) while critic confidences improve them highlights a non-trivial design choice that practitioners building on MaskGIT-style architectures should consider. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolution factors (×8, ×16), whether the VQGAN codebook size and token granularity are optimal for fine-grained texture synthesis, and whether the CCM can be generalized to other conditional restoration tasks beyond SR.
