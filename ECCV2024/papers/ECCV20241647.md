## Problem

Adversarial perturbations crafted to fool one model often transfer to other models, but their transferability is inconsistent because source models exploit model-specific, human-imperceptible features that do not exist in target models. Existing remedies modify the attack algorithm, require ensembles, or alter the model's forward/backward pass, leaving a gap for a simple, model-agnostic technique that improves the source model itself without changing the attack procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241647/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained source model (e.g., ResNet50, ViT-B/16) and an independently trained witness model of the same or different architecture, along with the original ImageNet training set. It produces a fine-tuned (aligned) source model whose parameters have been updated for one epoch; this aligned model is then used as the attack oracle to generate ℓ∞-bounded perturbations (ε=4/255) via standard attacks like PGD. No new data or architectural changes are required, and the aligned model can be plugged into any existing attack pipeline unchanged.

## Method

Model alignment fine-tunes the source model's parameters to minimize a KL-divergence loss between its softmax outputs and those of a witness model, effectively performing one-epoch knowledge distillation from the witness into the source. The key insight is that this process steers the source model toward exploiting shared low-frequency semantic features (verified via DCT analysis of perturbations) rather than model-specific high-frequency artifacts, and geometrically smooths the loss landscape so that PGD-generated perturbations land on flatter maxima that generalize across architectures. Smaller-capacity witness models (e.g., ResNet18 for a ResNet50 source) yield the largest gains, likely because they rely more heavily on semantic features. The approach is orthogonal to the attack algorithm—no changes to the forward or backward pass are needed—and can be combined with embedding-space alignment metrics (EGA, NCE) for further improvement.

## Results

On ImageNet (1000 samples, ℓ∞-PGD with ε=4/255, 20 iterations), model alignment consistently improves adversarial transferability across all source-witness-target combinations. For Res50 source aligned with Res18 witness, error rates increase by +35.30% (Res18 target) to +19.18% (IncV3 target). The gains are most dramatic for ViT-B/16 source models: aligning with DN121 yields +47.36% on ViT-T/16 and +31.22% on ViT-B/16 targets. A single aligned Res50* model (92.03% on Res18, 95.73% on Res50) outperforms a four-model ensemble (Res50+Res18) which achieves only 91.25% and 91.56% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original unaligned source model, and model alignment produces consistent positive transferability gains across all 5 source architectures and all 10 target models (Table 2). Against the ensemble-of-logits baseline from Dong et al. [10] (Table 6), a single aligned Res50* model (92.03% on Res18, 95.73% on Res50) surpasses the Res50+Res18 ensemble (91.25%, 91.56%), and 2×Res50* (98.70%, 99.44%) outperforms the 4-model ensemble 2×(Res50+Res18) (97.69%, 98.73%) while requiring fewer models. Among embedding-space alignment metrics (Table 4), KL-based output alignment (+35.30 on Res18) substantially outperforms RKD (+2.95) and HINT (+5.95), while EGA (+35.35) and NCE (+21.51) are competitive; the combined KL+EGA objective achieves the best overall transferability (+35.74 on Res18, +14.05 on SWIN). CNN source models benefit more from CNN witness models than ViT witnesses, whereas ViT-B/16 sources benefit from both families, with CNN witnesses (Res50, DN121) yielding the largest gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes knowledge distillation machinery (KL divergence, soft labels, embedding-space alignment metrics like EGA and NCE) for a purpose outside their original scope: not to train a student model, but to fine-tune a source model so that PGD-style attacks against it produce more transferable perturbations. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the feature-sharing hypothesis for transferability (Ma et al. 2023, Wang et al. 2020, Yin et al. 2019) and the geometric view that flat loss maxima yield transferable examples (Gubri et al. 2022, Qin et al. 2022), providing a mechanistic bridge between the two. Unlike prior model-modification attacks (LinBP, BPA) that alter the forward or backward pass, model alignment is orthogonal to the attack algorithm, making it composable with any existing transfer-enhancing method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The technique is remarkably simple—one epoch of fine-tuning with a KL-divergence loss against an independently trained witness model—yet it consistently improves transferability across all tested CNN and ViT architectures, and it is the first method shown to substantially close the CNN-to-ViT transferability gap (e.g., +43 pp on ViT-T/16 targets from a ViT-B/16 source). The finding that smaller-capacity witness models (Res18) are markedly more effective than larger ones (Res50, Res101) suggests a practical design principle: align toward models that are forced to rely on semantic features. The main open question, acknowledged by the authors, is the absence of a theoretical framework explaining why the alignment loss produces the observed smoothing of the loss landscape; a formal connection between KL-based fine-tuning, Jacobian norm regularization, and transferability would strengthen the contribution considerably.
