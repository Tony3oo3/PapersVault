## Problem

State-of-the-art incremental SfM pipelines like COLMAP rely almost exclusively on point features and fail catastrophically in weakly textured indoor scenes or poorly constrained view configurations. Although line segments are abundant in human-made environments and provide stronger geometric constraints through their spatial extent and structured relations (parallelism, orthogonality), no prior work has integrated them into a full end-to-end incremental SfM system that matches COLMAP's versatility and robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240956/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an unordered collection of RGB images (calibrated or uncalibrated) with no radial or tangential distortion, and produces camera parameters plus a sparse 3D map containing points, lines (in Plücker coordinates), vanishing point tracks, and their geometric relations, each annotated with propagated 3D uncertainty. Line triangulation requires at least three views for verification (versus two for points), so incremental line mapping only begins after the fourth image is registered. The pipeline uses DeepLSD for line detection, GlueStick for line matching, and JLinkage for VP detection as default front-ends.

## Method

The paper introduces a complete incremental SfM system with three key technical contributions. For triangulation, it extends point-based incremental operations to lines via Continue/Create operations with a scoring function combining perpendicular and angular reprojection errors, plus a 'complete' strategy to recover missing supports from the matching graph. For refinement, it proposes caching inactive supports (labeling them rather than deleting) and a two-step bundle adjustment that optimizes reliable tracks jointly with poses while refining unreliable tracks with fixed poses, preventing premature track removal without corrupting pose estimates. For registration, it employs a hybrid RANSAC framework with six minimal solvers (P3P, P2P1LL, P1P2LL, P3LL, and two VP-based gravity solvers) and uncertainty-aware reweighting of correspondences. A central theoretical contribution is the first analytical method to propagate 3D uncertainty for optimized lines using second-order sensitivity analysis, since the line reprojection residual's derivative depends on the optimized 3D line itself, making standard Jacobian-based propagation intractable.

## Results

On Hypersim (indoor, structured scenes), the hybrid system improves AUC@1° from 71.3 to 82.1 (SIFT+NN) and from 80.1 to 87.0 (SP+SG) over COLMAP, with valid registration rates of 93.9% and 97.0% respectively. On the more challenging ETH3D dataset, gains are substantially larger: AUC@1° jumps from 16.2 to 24.3 (SIFT+NN, a 50% relative improvement) and valid registration increases from 46.4% to 59.4%. The uncertainty-aware localization module further improves median error and recall on Cambridge (6.4 cm / 48.0% recall at 5cm/5deg vs. 7.1 cm / 43.1% without uncertainty) and 7Scenes (2.9 cm / 79.0% vs. 3.1 cm / 76.0%) under both point-alone and hybrid settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against COLMAP (point-only) [79], the hybrid system wins on every metric across all four dataset/feature combinations, with the largest gains on ETH3D where valid registration improves by 13 percentage points (46.4%→59.4% with SIFT+NN) and AUC@1° nearly doubles. Compared to the LIMAP post-refinement approach [52] (which applies global line triangulation and bundle adjustment after COLMAP), the full hybrid system achieves higher AUC (e.g., 24.3 vs. 19.2 at 1° on ETH3D) and more valid registrations (59.4% vs. 47.6%), demonstrating that integrating lines into the incremental loop is superior to a post-hoc refinement. On sequential TUM RGB-D data, ORB-SLAM [62] and Structure PLP-SLAM [84] achieve lower ATE RMSE at 30 FPS but degrade sharply at lower frame rates (ORB-SLAM fails entirely at 3 FPS; PLP-SLAM rises from 1.64 to 3.88 m at 6 FPS), whereas the proposed SfM system remains stable (1.39–1.80 m across 30–3 FPS). For camera localization, the uncertainty-aware module improves over the HLOC point-alone baseline [75] on both Cambridge and 7Scenes, and also surpasses the hybrid LIMAP-based localization [52] (e.g., 29.0% vs. 25.4% recall at 5cm/5deg on Cambridge with point+line features).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the COLMAP incremental SfM framework by integrating line segments and vanishing points throughout all three pipeline stages (triangulation, refinement, registration), building on the global line triangulation of LIMAP [52] but making it incremental so that all images need not be pre-posed. It departs from earlier line-based SfM work (Bartoli & Sturm 2005, Schindler 2006) and SLAM systems with lines (PL-SLAM, Structure PLP-SLAM) by operating on general unstructured image collections rather than sequential video, and by leveraging modern deep-learning line detectors (DeepLSD) and matchers (GlueStick) that were unavailable to prior efforts. The analytical uncertainty propagation for 3D lines via second-order sensitivity analysis is a new theoretical contribution that extends Jacobian-based point uncertainty modeling to the non-least-squares line reprojection residual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because COLMAP is the de facto standard SfM backend for a wide range of downstream applications (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting, AR, scene reconstruction), a more robust and accurate hybrid pipeline has broad practical impact, and the open-source release lowers the barrier for adoption. The first analytical uncertainty model for 3D optimized lines provides a principled tool that can be plugged into any localization or mapping system, as demonstrated by consistent gains on Cambridge and 7Scenes. Open questions include whether the 1–3× runtime overhead from line features limits scalability to very large scenes, whether the requirement of undistorted images is a practical constraint, and whether joint point-line detection/matching (rather than separate pipelines) can close the remaining gap to point-only speed.
