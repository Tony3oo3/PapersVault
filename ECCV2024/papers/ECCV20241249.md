## Problem

Both NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting overfit severely when trained on only a handful of input views, degrading novel view synthesis quality. NeRF-based few-shot methods add regularization or priors but remain slow (hours to days for training and rendering), while 3DGS is real-time yet lacks the geometric constraints needed to prevent overfitting in sparse-view settings. The paper asks how to extract sufficient multi-view geometric and appearance cues from just 3–5 images to guide 3DGS optimization without sacrificing its real-time rendering speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241249/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–5 posed RGB images (with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics) as input and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel views at 132–531 FPS depending on resolution. A pre-trained MVSFormer network is run on the input views to generate dense view-consistent depth maps, and a pre-trained DPT model provides monocular depth estimates; both are used as frozen priors during optimization. Experiments cover resolutions from 504×378 (1/8 LLFF) up to 800×600 (1/2 DTU), and the forward-warping step generates pseudo-appearance images for randomly sampled unseen poses that serve as additional training targets.

## Method

MVPGS initializes 3D Gaussians from MVSFormer-derived point clouds (replacing COLMAP sparse points), which provides richer geometric and appearance priors in few-shot settings. The key novelty is a forward-warping appearance constraint: using MVS depth and camera poses, the method warps known-view pixels into randomly sampled unseen views via reversed bilinear sampling and depth-weighted occlusion handling, producing pseudo-ground-truth images that supervise rendering at those poses (L_fwd). To prevent Gaussian positions from drifting during optimization, two geometric regularizers are added: a consistent-structure loss (L_CS) that penalizes deviation from MVS depth in high-confidence regions, and a monocular rank loss (L_mono) using DPT depth to enforce relative depth ordering in regions where MVS fails. The total loss combines photometric (L1+SSIM), forward-warping, and both geometric terms, optimized with 3DGS's adaptive densification and pruning schedule.

## Results

On LLFF with 3 input views at 1/8 resolution, MVPGS achieves PSNR 20.39, SSIM 0.715, and LPIPS 0.203 at 132 FPS, outperforming all NeRF-based baselines (best: SparseNeRF at PSNR 19.86) while rendering ~1900× faster. On DTU at 1/4 resolution, it reaches PSNR 20.50, SSIM 0.871, LPIPS 0.106 at 365 FPS. On the large-scale T&T dataset, MVPGS achieves PSNR 25.57 and SSIM 0.846, substantially outperforming SparseNeRF (PSNR 20.62) and FreeNeRF (PSNR 18.49). On NVS-RGBD (ZED2), it attains PSNR 26.62 and SSIM 0.841, again leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVPGS outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest NeRF-based competitor SparseNeRF, it gains +0.53 dB PSNR and +0.095 SSIM on LLFF (1/8), and +1.04 dB PSNR and +0.105 SSIM on DTU (1/4), while rendering at 132–365 FPS versus 0.07–0.18 FPS (a ~2000× speedup). Compared to FreeNeRF, the gains are +0.76 dB PSNR on LLFF and +0.58 dB on DTU. Against RegNeRF, improvements are larger: +1.31 dB PSNR on LLFF and +1.61 dB on DTU. The vanilla 3DGS baseline (COLMAP initialization) is far behind at PSNR 14.87 on LLFF, and even 3DGS with MVS point-cloud initialization (3DGS*) only reaches 16.16, confirming that the forward-warping appearance constraint and geometric regularization—not just better initialization—are essential for the full performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as an explicit point-based scene representation and departs from the dominant NeRF-based few-shot NVS line (RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) by exploiting the geometric structure already embedded in MVS-derived initialization. Its key conceptual departure is a forward-warping strategy that uses known-view geometry from MVSFormer to synthesize appearance priors for unseen views, in contrast to backward-warping approaches (GeoAug, GeCoNeRF) that rely on target-view geometry, and in contrast to methods that invoke external generative models (diffusion, normalizing flows) for pseudo-observations. Monocular depth priors from DPT serve as a compensatory global geometry regularizer, extending the depth-supervision idea from DS-NeRF and SparseNeRF into the Gaussian Splatting setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By combining MVS-based initialization, forward-warping appearance constraints, and view-consistent geometric regularization within 3DGS, the paper demonstrates that real-time rendering (366 FPS) and SOTA few-shot NVS quality are simultaneously achievable, removing the traditional speed-quality tradeoff that plagued NeRF-based methods. The forward-warping formulation is a practical, geometry-grounded alternative to diffusion-model supervision that avoids the cost and instability of external generative models. The stated limitation—no handling of non-Lambertian surfaces or lighting variation—leaves open the question of whether this paradigm extends to complex material rendering, and whether the MVS quality bottleneck in textureless or low-overlap regions can be further alleviated.
