## Problem

Existing multi-control image synthesis methods face three key limitations: they cannot freely combine arbitrary numbers and modalities of spatial conditions (relying on fixed-length channels or MoE with handcrafted weights), they fail to model complex spatial relationships and occlusions among multiple conditions, and they often sacrifice textual prompt fidelity in favor of spatial condition adherence. These issues collectively degrade the quality and user experience of controllable generation.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a free-form text prompt together with any arbitrary subset of spatial conditions (edge, depth, segmentation, and human pose maps), where conditions may originate from different image sources and need not be spatially aligned or occlusion-free. Unlike prior methods that require a fixed set of input channels or per-modality encoders, AnyControl consumes conditions as variable-length visual token sequences from a pre-trained CLIP image encoder, with a shared positional embedding added to each condition's tokens. The output is a single generated image at the resolution supported by the underlying Stable Diffusion 1.5 backbone.

## Method

AnyControl introduces a Multi-Control Encoder built from alternating multi-control fusion blocks (cross-attention between learnable query tokens and multi-level visual tokens extracted from a frozen CLIP image encoder) and multi-control alignment blocks (self-attention between query tokens and CLIP text tokens, with an appended task prompt to bridge modality gaps). The query tokens act as a bridge that progressively accumulates spatial information from all conditions and aligns it semantically with the text, enabling the model to resolve occlusions and prioritize conditions coherently. The encoder is plugged into a frozen Stable Diffusion 1.5 UNet via cross-attention layers, preserving the base model's generation quality. Training uses the MultiGen dataset (2.8M images) plus 0.44M synthetically unaligned samples (foreground/background decomposed via inpainting) to close the gap between aligned training conditions and misaligned user inputs at inference time.

## Results

On the newly introduced COCO-UM benchmark (1,480 samples with occluded, unaligned multi-control conditions), AnyControl achieves the best FID of 44.28 and CLIP-Score of 26.41, outperforming the next-best Cocktail (FID 47.39, CLIP 25.33) by a substantial margin. AnyControl also leads on segmentation mPA (43.34) and pose mAP (18.81), while remaining competitive on depth RMSE (18.00 vs. Multi-ControlNet's 17.81) and edge RMSE (45.25 vs. Uni-ControlNet's 43.28). On the single-control COCO-5K evaluation, AnyControl achieves the best FID across all four conditions (e.g., 18.04 for depth, 18.89 for segmentation) and the highest segmentation mPA (48.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyControl achieves the best overall performance on COCO-UM, winning on FID (44.28 vs. 47.39 for Cocktail, the runner-up) and CLIP-Score (26.41 vs. 25.73 for Multi-Adapter) by clear margins, indicating superior image quality and text alignment under occluded multi-control conditions. On condition-fidelity metrics, AnyControl leads in segmentation mPA (43.34 vs. 42.78 for Multi-ControlNet) and pose mAP (18.81 vs. 18.40 for Uni-ControlNet), but trails slightly on depth RMSE (18.00 vs. 17.81 for Multi-ControlNet) and edge RMSE (45.25 vs. 43.28 for Uni-ControlNet). UniControl and Multi-Adapter show the weakest performance overall, with UniControl's pose mAP dropping to 4.06 and Multi-Adapter's to 6.28, suggesting their MoE-based architectures struggle with unaligned multi-condition inputs. On the single-control COCO-5K benchmark, AnyControl outperforms all single-control baselines (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) and multi-control methods on FID for every condition, with the largest gap in segmentation mPA (48.73 vs. 45.77 for UniControl). The ablation on unaligned training data confirms that the 0.44M synthetic occluded samples contribute a 7.82-point FID improvement (52.10 → 44.28) and a 0.78-point CLIP gain, validating the necessity of the COCO-UM-style evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyControl follows the ControlNet paradigm of locking a pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone and injecting a trainable encoder, but replaces the per-modality encoder or MoE-based weighted summation used by UniControlNet, Cocktail, and UniControl with a transformer-based Multi-Control Encoder inspired by Q-Former. The key departure is the use of learnable query tokens that alternate between cross-attention over multi-level visual tokens (fusion) and self-attention with textual tokens (alignment), enabling dynamic, data-driven composition of arbitrary condition combinations rather than hand-crafted weights. The paper also introduces COCO-UM, a benchmark of unaligned multi-control conditions, addressing a practical gap that prior evaluations on fully aligned MSCOCO conditions fail to capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The COCO-UM benchmark and the finding that synthetic unaligned training data yields a substantial FID improvement (52.10 → 44.28) highlight a previously underappreciated train-test distribution gap in multi-control synthesis, where real user-provided conditions are rarely pixel-aligned. The plug-and-play compatibility with existing modules (e.g., IP-Adapter for style/color) broadens practical applicability. However, the method still suffers from miss-blending when the number of spatial conditions exceeds roughly six, and the evaluation is limited to SD 1.5 with four condition types, leaving open whether the attention-based composition generalizes to newer diffusion backbones or a wider modality space.
