## Problem

Existing 3D shape generation methods either rely on memory-intensive 3D voxel representations (e.g., T-SDF) that limit geometric detail, or use tri-plane encodings that naively concatenate or roll out the three orthogonal planes, ignoring the 3D spatial correlations between them. This neglect of cross-plane coherence produces artifacts, displaced details, and limited shape diversity in generated outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240485/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts flexible conditioning signals—partial point clouds (bottom-half or octant subsets), a single RGB image, or a text prompt—and produces a watertight 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from a continuous signed distance field. Internally, each object is represented as a NeuSDF: three 64×64 feature planes (XY, YZ, XZ) decoded by a shared MLP into SDF values, which are then compressed into lower-resolution latent tri-planes before diffusion-based generation.

## Method

NeuSDFusion is a three-stage pipeline: (1) per-object fitting of a continuous SDF onto three orthogonal 2D planes (NeuSDF) using SDF, normal, and Eikonal losses; (2) a U-shaped transformer autoencoder that compresses raw tri-planes into latent tri-planes, where each stage applies group convolution for per-plane downsampling followed by a linear-attention transformer block with a spatial-aware position embedding (SAPE) that adds distinct learnable offsets per plane to preserve 3D cross-plane correlations; (3) a latent diffusion model (U-Net with cross-attention for condition injection and classifier-free guidance) that generates latent tri-planes from Gaussian noise. The key architectural insight is replacing naive channel concatenation or roll-out strategies with per-plane convolution plus global transformer attention, enabling high-resolution tri-plane processing (12,288 tokens) without the quadratic cost of standard self-attention.

## Results

On unconditional generation (ShapeNet), NeuSDFusion achieves the best 1-NNA scores across all three categories: 52.33 CD / 52.47 EMD (Airplane), 51.95 CD / 52.60 EMD (Chair), and 53.06 CD / 51.11 EMD (Car), outperforming the next-best 3DQD by 3–4 points in CD. On single-view reconstruction (Pix3D), the method achieves a CD of 0.92 and F-Score of 0.61, representing roughly a 50% improvement over SDFusion (CD 1.85, F-Score 0.43). For text-guided generation (ShapeGlot), NeuSDFusion attains the best CLIP-S (32.52), FPD (55.01), and TMD (3.20), while tying 3DQD on PMMD (1.49).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuSDFusion outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task. In unconditional generation, it beats the closest competitor 3DQD (TSDF) by 3.96 CD on Airplane and 2.69 CD on Chair, and surpasses LION (point cloud) by 15+ CD points, demonstrating the advantage of the NeuSDF tri-plane representation over both voxel-based and point-cloud encodings. For single-view reconstruction on Pix3D, the method nearly halves the Chamfer Distance of SDFusion (0.92 vs. 1.85) and AutoSDF (0.92 vs. 2.28), while improving F-Score from 0.43 to 0.61. In multi-modal shape completion, NeuSDFusion achieves the lowest MMD (2.29 bottom-half, 3.03 octant) compared to 3DQD (2.93, 4.69) and AutoSDF (3.51, 5.72), while maintaining competitive TMD diversity scores. For text-guided generation, it ties 3DQD on PMMD (1.49) but leads on CLIP-S (32.52 vs. 32.11), FPD (55.01 vs. 59.00), and TMD (3.20 vs. 2.80), indicating better text-shape alignment and diversity. Rendering-based and occupancy-based methods (IM-GAN, SDF StyleGAN, GET3D) are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing by 20–40 CD points in unconditional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuSDFusion builds directly on the tri-plane representation introduced by Chan et al. (CVPR 2022) and the latent diffusion framework of Rombach et al. (CVPR 2022), but departs from prior tri-plane generative models (NFD, DiffusionSDF, Rodin) in two key ways: it replaces discrete occupancy grids or point-cloud intermediates with a continuous SDF encoded on orthogonal 2D planes (NeuSDF), and it replaces CNN-based autoencoders that naively concatenate or roll out planes with a transformer-based spatial-aware autoencoder using linear attention and a dedicated position embedding (SAPE) to enforce cross-plane 3D correlations. The paper positions itself against both rendering-based approaches (LRM, Zero123) that ignore available 3D priors and raw 3D representation methods (T-SDF, point clouds, meshes) that are memory-prohibitive at high resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial-aware autoencoder addresses a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in tri-plane generative models—spatial misalignment between planes that produces visible artifacts—making it a practical architectural fix likely to be adopted by subsequent tri-plane-based systems. The NeuSDF representation's memory efficiency relative to 3D voxel SDFs opens the door to higher-resolution shape generation on consumer hardware. However, the evaluation remains largely confined to ShapeNet categories and the Pix3D benchmark, leaving open questions about scalability to open-vocabulary or scene-level generation, and the reliance on a separate tool (Meshy) for texturing highlights that the method still produces untextured geometry only.
