## Problem

Video-to-image transfer learning has lagged behind the well-studied image-to-video direction, and existing methods that jointly learn from both modalities either treat images as videos (repeating frames) or rely solely on masked image modeling, missing the strong temporal augmentations that videos naturally provide. The paper asks whether contrastive learning and masked image modeling can be seamlessly unified in a single self-supervised framework that treats short video segments as augmented views, yielding strong representations for both image and video downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pairs of 224×224 RGB inputs: either two frames sampled from the same video via distant sampling (splitting the video into non-overlapping intervals and picking one frame per interval, yielding ~1.06 s separation) or two augmented views of a single image (random crop, flip, color distortion, Gaussian blur). Each input is independently masked at 75% before tokenization into 16×16 patches. The model outputs two types of features: patch-level local representations used for pixel reconstruction, and a single global vector obtained by mean-pooling all local tokens, which serves as the contrastive embedding for downstream classification, detection, or segmentation after fine-tuning.

## Method

ViC-MAE uses a siamese ViT backbone (shared weights) where each branch independently masks and encodes one input. The encoder's local patch features feed a lightweight MAE decoder that reconstructs masked patches via an ℓ₂ loss, while simultaneously a global pooling operator (mean pooling over all token embeddings) aggregates local features into a single vector. This pooled vector is passed through a predictor MLP and a target MLP (each a 2-layer Linear→BN→ReLU block) to produce contrastive embeddings, optimized with an InfoNCE loss over the batch. The key architectural insight is that the pooling layer before the predictor prevents representational collapse without requiring a stop-gradient or negative-free regularization, because the pooled features are simultaneously trained to reconstruct patches. The total loss is L_MASK + λ·L_NEG, with the contrastive term gradually introduced via a schedule so the model first learns strong local features. For video, distant frame sampling provides the positive-pair augmentation; for images, strong color and spatial augmentations are applied, and the two modalities are mixed in the same training batch.

## Results

ViC-MAE (ViT-L, trained on K710+MiT+IN1K) achieves 87.1% top-1 on ImageNet-1K, 87.8% on Kinetics-400, 60.7% on Places-365, and 75.9% on Something-Something-v2, outperforming the closest self-supervised competitor OmniMAE (ViT-L, K400+IN1K) by +2.4%, +3.8%, +1.3%, and +2.5% respectively. When trained on video only (MiT), ViC-MAE reaches 85.3% on ImageNet-1K, the best video-to-image transfer among self-supervised methods. On COCO detection/segmentation, ViC-MAE (IN1K+K710+MiT) achieves 53.2 AP_box and 46.9 AP_mask, surpassing C-MAE (52.4/46.5) and MAE (50.3/44.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest self-supervised competitor OmniMAE (ViT-L, K400+IN1K), ViC-MAE wins on all four benchmarks: +2.4% on IN1K (86.0 vs 84.7), +3.8% on K400 (86.8 vs 84.0), +0.6% on Places-365 (60.0 vs 59.4), and +1.6% on SSv2 (75.0 vs 73.4). Compared to ST-MAE (ViT-L, K400), ViC-MAE trained on the same video-only data gains +3.3% on IN1K (85.0 vs 81.7) and +0.5% on SSv2 (73.7 vs 73.2), while VideoMAE (ViT-L, K400) slightly edges ViC-MAE on SSv2 (74.3 vs 73.7) but lacks IN1K and Places-365 results. On the broader transfer-learning suite (Table 2), ViC-MAE (ViT-L, K710+MiT+IN1K) outperforms both MAE (ViT-L, IN1K) and OmniMAE (ViT-L, SSv2+IN1K) across all 8 image classification datasets, with the largest margins on Birdsnap (+3.7% over MAE, +3.4% over OmniMAE) and SUN397 (+4.9% over MAE, +5.5% over OmniMAE). On COCO detection, ViC-MAE (IN1K+K710+MiT) surpasses C-MAE by +0.8 AP_box and MAE by +2.9 AP_box. Against supervised baselines, ViC-MAE (87.1% IN1K) outperforms OMNIVORE (86.0%) and COVeR (86.6%) but trails TubeViT (90.2% K400, 76.1% SSv2) on video benchmarks, remaining within 0.2–2.4% of the best supervised methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViC-MAE sits at the intersection of masked autoencoders (MAE) and contrastive learning, building directly on the MAE encoder-decoder architecture for local feature learning and the SimCLR-style predictor-target siamese framework with InfoNCE loss for global representation alignment. Its key conceptual departure from prior work is treating short video segments (frames sampled ~1 second apart) as temporal augmentations of a single view, rather than the reverse approach used by OmniMAE and ST-MAE of treating images as degenerate videos. The use of a global pooling layer over ViT patch features—rather than the [CLS] token as in C-MAE—serves as a collapse-avoidance mechanism that eliminates the need for gradient-stop operators, and the method is positioned as orthogonal to OmniMAE's masking-only approach, with the authors suggesting the two could be combined for further gains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViC-MAE addresses a recognized asymmetry in self-supervised learning: video-to-image transfer has historically lagged behind image-to-video transfer, and this paper narrows that gap substantially (85.3% IN1K top-1 from video-only pre-training, 86% from joint video+image). The practical finding that distant frame sampling and strong spatial augmentations suffice for video (without color distortions) simplifies training pipelines, while the data-efficiency comparison (matching CAN's JFT-300M accuracy with ~4.25M samples) suggests the combined MAE+contrastive objective extracts more signal per sample. Open questions remain about whether the approach scales to longer videos, whether additional modalities (text, audio, 3D geometry) can close the remaining gap to supervised and text-augmented models, and whether the pooling-based collapse avoidance generalizes to other architectures beyond ViT.
