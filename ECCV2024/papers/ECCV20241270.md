## Problem

Existing multi-GPU NeRF methods (BlockNeRF, MegaNeRF, ProgressiveNeRF) train independent NeRFs per spatial region, which introduces model capacity redundancy, requires blending at region boundaries (causing artifacts), and produces inconsistent per-camera embeddings. Consequently, these approaches fail to improve reconstruction quality or rendering speed as more GPUs are added, undermining the core purpose of multi-GPU scaling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241270/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and optionally a sparse point cloud (e.g., from SFM) used solely for balanced spatial partitioning across GPUs. It outputs a single coherent neural radiance field distributed across K GPUs that renders novel views without any blending step, producing pixel colors and depths identical to a single-GPU NeRF of equivalent total capacity. The spatial partitioning is computed in seconds by recursively splitting the point cloud (or discretized ray samples) at CDF=0.5 planes along each axis.

## Method

NeRF-XL jointly trains non-overlapping NeRFs (one per spatial tile) across multiple GPUs, requiring communication only during the forward pass: each GPU gathers color/density from all other GPUs to compute a global volume rendering, then back-propagates gradients locally without any gradient synchronization. The key efficiency gain comes from a partition-based volume rendering formulation that rewrites the integral equation (and distortion loss) as a per-segment composition, reducing inter-GPU data exchange from O(KS²) per-sample to O(S²) per-tile values. Spatial partitioning uses CDF-based recursive splitting of a sparse point cloud or ray samples to balance ray-sample counts across GPUs. The entire pipeline is mathematically equivalent to training a single large NeRF on one GPU, and the first to reveal multi-GPU scaling laws for NeRFs, showing near-linear PSNR and rendering-speed improvements with additional GPUs.

## Results

NERF-XL demonstrates near-linear improvements in both PSNR (reconstruction quality) and rendering speed (Rays Per Second) as GPUs scale from 1× to 16× across all tested captures, while prior multi-GPU baselines fail to improve with additional GPUs. The method scales to the largest open-source NeRF dataset to date, MatrixCity (258K images, 25 km²), using 64 GPUs. Partition-based volume rendering reduces multi-GPU communication costs by over 2× compared to the naive sample-based approach. Training speed is 1×–1.5× slower than independent-training baselines due to forward-pass synchronization overhead, but rendering speed scales nearly linearly with GPU count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NERF-XL against BlockNeRF (2D blending, 50% overlap) and MegaNeRF (3D blending, 15% overlap), both of which train independent NeRFs per GPU. On all four primary captures (Garden, University4, Building, MatrixCity), NERF-XL consistently outperforms both baselines in PSNR, and the gap widens as GPU count increases from 1× to 16×, whereas BlockNeRF and MegaNeRF plateau or degrade in quality with more GPUs. In rendering speed, NERF-XL also surpasses both baselines at higher GPU counts because it avoids the duplicate pixel evaluation (2D blending) or duplicate 3D sample evaluation (3D blending) required by the baselines. The paper attributes the baselines' failure to scale to three issues: model capacity redundancy (e.g., MegaNeRF wastes 38%/56%/62% of ray samples outside tiled regions at 2×/4×/8× tiles on University4), blending-induced artifacts, and inconsistent per-camera embeddings. Against PyTorch DDP (which distributes rays rather than parameters), NERF-XL achieves substantially higher PSNR on University4 because it enables N× more model parameters, though DDP renders faster due to balanced ray-level parallelism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the NeRF volume rendering formulation and the Instant-NGP hash-grid representation, but departs fundamentally from prior multi-GPU NeRF approaches (BlockNeRF, MegaNeRF, ProgressiveNeRF) that train independent, overlapping NeRFs per GPU. The key conceptual shift is from independent training with post-hoc blending to joint training of non-overlapping tiles, enabled by a principled rewriting of the volume rendering equation and distortion loss into a tile-based alpha-compositing form that reduces inter-GPU communication from O(KS²) to O(S²). Spatial partitioning draws on the observation from F2-NeRF that free camera trajectories create uneven view coverage, motivating CDF-based tile balancing rather than uniform grid division.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NERF-XL is the first to establish multi-GPU scaling laws for NeRFs, demonstrating that reconstruction quality and rendering speed improve nearly linearly with additional GPUs—something prior independent-training methods failed to achieve. The demonstration on MatrixCity (258K images, 25 km², 64 GPUs) sets a new practical ceiling for open-source NeRF training. Key limitations include persistent multi-GPU communication overhead (1×–1.5× slower training than independent baselines) and validation only with Instant-NGP; open questions include extending the framework to other representations (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting), dynamic scenes, and addressing workload imbalance through better partitioning.
