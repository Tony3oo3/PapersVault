## Problem

Accurately reconstructing 3D hands and objects from egocentric views remains difficult due to severe camera distortion, rapid viewpoint changes from head movement, and heavy hand-object occlusion. Prior hand challenges (HANDS17, HANDS19) relied on depth sensors, leaving a gap in benchmarking affordable RGB/monochrome image-based reconstruction under realistic multi-view egocentric conditions with dexterous bimanual object manipulation.

## Input / Output

For AssemblyHands, the input is a single egocentric monochrome image (from one of four headset cameras) plus camera intrinsics and extrinsics, producing wrist-relative 3D hand keypoints in world coordinates evaluated by MPJPE in millimeters. For ARCTIC, the input is a 224×224 or 384×384 RGB image (allocentric or egocentric), and the output is MANO pose/shape parameters (θ, β) for both hands plus a 7-dimensional articulated object parameter vector Ω, from which 3D meshes are generated and evaluated primarily by Contact Deviation (CDev) in mm. Multi-view fusion is optional in AssemblyHands, where four egocentric views can be combined at train or test time.

## Method

This is a benchmark and empirical study rather than a single proposed architecture. The HANDS23 challenge organizes two tasks—egocentric 3D hand pose estimation (AssemblyHands) and consistent bimanual hand-object motion reconstruction (ARCTIC)—with carefully designed train/test splits and standardized metrics. The analysis compares five AssemblyHands methods (heatmap-based, regression-based, and hybrid) and five ARCTIC methods (MLP regressors, transformer decoders, and segmentation-guided approaches), revealing that explicit distortion correction via virtual-camera perspective cropping, high-capacity vision transformers (Hiera, ViT-G with frozen DINOv2 weights), and adaptive multi-view fusion (IoU-based view selection, Feature Transform Layers, weighted averaging) are the key factors driving ~40% MPJPE reduction and ~30% CDev reduction over baselines. The study further identifies persistent failure modes: fast hand motion, object reconstruction from narrow moving egocentric views, and close-contact bimanual interactions remain intractable with current state-of-the-art methods.

## Results

On AssemblyHands (egocentric 3D hand pose estimation), JHands achieves the best test MPJPE of 12.21 mm, a ~40% reduction over the Base baseline (20.69 mm), with PICO-AI close behind at 12.46 mm. On ARCTIC (consistent hand-object motion reconstruction), JointTransformer reduces Contact Deviation (CDev) by 32.7% in allocentric and 27.2% in egocentric settings compared to the ArcticNet-SF baseline. In the egocentric ARCTIC setting, AmbiguousHands excels in motion smoothness (ACC and MDev metrics), while JointTransformer dominates on contact quality (CDev).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AssemblyHands, JHands (12.21 MPJPE) and PICO-AI (12.46) substantially outperform the Base toolkit (20.69), achieving a ~40% error reduction; FRDC (16.48) and Phi-AI (17.26) improve by 20.3% and 16.5% respectively. JHands leads on most verb classes, particularly 'tilt down' (9.61 vs. 13.12 for Base) and 'remove screw from' (11.72 vs. 16.47), while PICO-AI is slightly better on 'inspect' (15.30 vs. 16.27) and 'pull' (15.13 vs. 11.06 — actually JHands wins here). On ARCTIC, JointTransformer (ViT-G backbone with frozen DINOv2 weights) achieves the lowest CDev in both allocentric and egocentric views, beating ArcticNet-SF by 32.7% and 27.2% respectively. AmbiguousHands outperforms other methods on motion consistency metrics (ACC_h/o, MDev) in the egocentric setting, while DIGIT and UVHand show more moderate improvements over the ArcticNet-SF baseline. UVHand underperforms due to incomplete training (only 36 backbone epochs vs. 50 for the decoder).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the lineage of 3D hand pose estimation (from Rehg & Kanade through InterHand2.6M and HOI reconstruction) and prior hand challenges (HANDS17, HANDS19) by shifting from depth-sensor benchmarks to RGB/monochrome egocentric settings with explicit object interaction. It repurposes recent high-capacity vision transformers (Hiera, ViT-G with DINOv2, Swin-L) and multi-view fusion techniques (Feature Transform Layers, adaptive view selection) originally developed for general vision or allocentric hand tasks into the egocentric hand-object domain. The paper's contribution is primarily empirical: it organizes and analyzes challenge submissions and leaderboard baselines rather than proposing a single new architecture, positioning itself as a community knowledge synthesis in the spirit of prior challenge reports.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HANDS23 benchmarks provide the first standardized, large-scale evaluation protocol for egocentric hand-object reconstruction using affordable image sensors, filling a gap left by depth-based prior challenges. The analysis identifies concrete failure modes—fast hand motion, object reconstruction from narrow moving views, and close two-hand-object contact—that remain intractable with current SOTA, giving the community clear targets. Open questions include whether template-free reconstruction, temporal/motion modeling, and 3D foundation priors can close these gaps, and whether the frozen-large-backbone strategy (ViT-G + DINOv2) generalizes beyond the single JointTransformer method where it was observed.
