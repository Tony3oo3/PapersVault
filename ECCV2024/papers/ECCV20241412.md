## Problem

Estimating 3D poses and shapes of multiple people in close physical interaction (e.g., hugging, kissing) from sparse multi-view videos remains challenging because strong occlusions and body contact make 2D joint detections unreliable, and depth ambiguity worsens with contact. Existing multi-view methods either depend heavily on noisy 2D joint triangulation or use coarse SMPL body priors that are still constrained by inaccurate 2D observations, while learning-based approaches suffer from generalization issues due to limited annotated 3D data.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse multi-view RGB videos (4 or 8 views) along with initial 3D pose estimates from an off-the-shelf estimator (4DAssociation) and SAM-Track foreground segmentation masks as supervision. It produces refined per-person SMPL parameters (10-D shape, 72-D pose, 3-D translation) and personalized canonical-space neural avatar models that can be re-animated at interactive rates. The avatars are represented as hash-encoded radiance fields in canonical space, deformed via inverse linear blend skinning, and rendered jointly through layered volume rendering.

## Method

The core idea is to reconstruct a personalized implicit neural avatar (Instant-NGP hash-encoded radiance field in canonical space + SMPL-based deformation) for each individual and use it as a strong prior for pose optimization, bypassing reliance on noisy 2D joint detections. Pose is optimized by minimizing RGB rendering loss and a cross-entropy silhouette loss between the reposed avatar rendering and multi-view observations, rather than 2D reprojection error. A novel collision loss penalizes 3D sample points simultaneously occupied by multiple avatar density fields to prevent interpenetration during contact. The pose parameterization uses Θ₀ + MLP(Θ₀) for robustness, and the overall procedure alternates between avatar refinement (minimizing rendering losses with layer regularization) and pose optimization for N steps, exploiting the mutual benefit that better poses improve avatar learning and better avatars improve pose precision.

## Results

On the Hi4D dataset (8 views), AvatarPose achieves 32.10 mm MPJPE, 96.90% PCP, and 91.48 AP50, outperforming the closest competitor 4DAssociation (41.29 mm MPJPE, 88.62% PCP, 80.87 AP50) by a substantial margin. On CHI3D (4 views), the method reaches 32.98 mm MPJPE, 99.79% PCP, and 93.20 AP50, again leading all baselines. The method also achieves 98.78% recall on Hi4D and 99.85% on CHI3D, matching or exceeding all competitors in detection completeness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AvatarPose outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both Hi4D and CHI3D. The closest competitor is 4DAssociation [72], which achieves 41.29 mm MPJPE on Hi4D versus AvatarPose's 32.10 mm (a 22% reduction) and 80.87 AP50 versus 91.48; however, 4DAssociation ties on AP100 (97.27 vs 97.33) and Recall (98.78%). The SMPL-guided method MVPose [18] is second-closest at 42.63 mm MPJPE but lags significantly in AP50 (71.79 vs 91.48), reflecting its sensitivity to noisy 2D joint detections under occlusion. Learning-based methods (MvP, Graph, Faster VoxelPose) are substantially worse, with MPJPE values of 68–93 mm, and Faster VoxelPose additionally suffers from low recall (83.55%) due to missed detections in close contact. The paper attributes the gap to AvatarPose's use of pixel-wise color and silhouette rendering losses rather than 2D joint reprojection, making it robust to the occlusions and body contact that degrade all baseline methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-view multi-person 3D pose estimation (MVPose, 4DAssociation, VoxelPose, MvP) and neural avatar reconstruction (Instant-NGP, InstantAvatar, ST-NeRF). It departs from the dominant paradigm of relying on 2D joint detections or SMPL body priors for pose refinement by instead using personalized implicit neural avatars—learned via layered volume rendering—as the prior for pose optimization. The key conceptual shift is replacing 2D reprojection error with pixel-wise color and silhouette rendering loss, and introducing a density-field-based collision loss to handle interpenetration, neither of which is available with standard SMPL priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Close human interaction (hugging, kissing, physical support) is a practically important but underexplored scenario where existing multi-view pose methods degrade significantly due to occlusion and contact. The paper demonstrates that personalized avatars provide a fundamentally stronger geometric and appearance prior than SMPL for this setting, and the alternating avatar-pose optimization is a practical recipe that practitioners can adopt. Open questions include whether the approach extends to hand modeling (explicitly noted as a limitation), whether it generalizes beyond the tested 4–8 camera setups, and whether the local-minima sensitivity to poor initialization can be mitigated. The method also requires a multi-view capture setup, limiting its applicability to single-camera scenarios.
