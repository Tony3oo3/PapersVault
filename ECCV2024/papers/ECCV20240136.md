## Problem

Moving object segmentation in long-range videos captured with ultra-telephoto lenses is severely degraded by atmospheric turbulence, which introduces irregular, time-varying geometric distortions that break the rigid-motion assumption underlying most segmentation algorithms and corrupt optical flow estimates. Existing supervised and unsupervised methods fail under strong turbulence, and no real-world dataset with ground-truth segmentation masks exists for this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240136/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a turbulent video clip (originally 1920×1080, resized to 240×432 for processing) and produces per-frame, per-object binary segmentation masks without requiring any labeled training data. It handles multiple moving objects simultaneously, assigning each a consistent integer ID across all frames. The only tunable parameter is the region-growing threshold δ_seed, which must be adjusted based on turbulence strength (multiplier of 0.1–0.3).

## Method

The approach follows a detect-then-grow pipeline with three stages. First, an epipolar geometry-based consistency check disentangles rigid object motion from turbulence and camera motion: RAFT optical flow is stabilized by temporal averaging, then Sampson distances are computed using fundamental matrices (estimated via LMedS) to produce motion feature maps where high values indicate likely object pixels. Second, a region-growing scheme selects seed pixels via sliding windows on the motion feature map (requiring high mean and low variance) and expands masks outward using a similarity threshold on feature values, with K-means unifying object IDs across frames. Third, a U-Net (Refine-Net) refines the coarse masks using bidirectional cross-entropy consistency losses (comparing refined masks against flow-warped versions of both the input mask and itself) plus an iterative K-means grouping function that reassigns pixel labels every 3 epochs to enforce spatial coherence.

## Results

On the DOST dataset, the proposed method achieves an overall Jaccard Index (IoU) of 0.703 and F1-Score of 0.723, substantially outperforming the best baseline TMO (J=0.439, F=0.536) by 60.1% in J and 34.9% in F. Under severe turbulence, the method maintains J=0.557 and F=0.634, while all baselines drop below J=0.25 and F=0.35. On camera-shake videos, the method achieves an average IoU of 0.712 versus 0.305 (TMO), 0.267 (DS-Net), and 0.235 (DSprites). Ablation studies confirm that optical flow stabilization (removing it drops IoU to 0.354) and the geometric consistency check (removing it drops IoU to 0.685) are both critical components.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three state-of-the-art unsupervised motion segmentation methods: TMO (WACV 2023), Deformable Sprites (CVPR 2022), and DS-Net (DSP 2022). The proposed method decisively outperforms all three baselines on every metric (J, F, G) across both normal and severe turbulence conditions. TMO is the strongest baseline, achieving J=0.439 overall, but the proposed method exceeds it by 0.264 in J and 0.187 in F. Under severe turbulence, the gap widens dramatically: the proposed method scores J=0.557 while TMO drops to 0.235, DSprites to 0.101, and DS-Net to 0.191. On the synthetic DAVIS 2016 dataset with varying turbulence strengths, the proposed method shows the best robustness curve, though TMO slightly edges it out at zero turbulence (strength=0) due to its richer visual cues. The paper also notes that supervised methods like SAM fail to generalize to turbulent videos, reinforcing the advantage of the unsupervised approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes epipolar geometry tools (Sampson distance, fundamental matrices) originally developed for camera calibration and 3D reconstruction into a motion-disentanglement mechanism for video segmentation under turbulence. It departs from the dominant restoration-first paradigm in turbulent video processing by segmenting directly in the distorted domain, and from standard unsupervised motion segmentation methods (TMO, DSprites, DS-Net) by inserting a geometric consistency check before region growing. The region-growing and U-Net refinement pipeline draws on classical segmentation and deep learning techniques, but the specific combination with turbulence-aware geometric filtering is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DOST dataset is the first real-captured long-range turbulent video benchmark with per-frame segmentation masks, filling a clear gap for a practically important application (surveillance, remote sensing, environmental monitoring). The unsupervised formulation is well-motivated given the scarcity of labeled turbulent data, and the method's robustness across turbulence strengths and camera shake is practically valuable. However, the 0.95 FPS latency limits real-time deployment, and the method struggles with overlapping objects. Open questions include whether foundation models (e.g., SAM) can be integrated to improve both speed and boundary quality, and whether the geometric consistency approach generalizes to other non-rigid distortion sources beyond turbulence.
