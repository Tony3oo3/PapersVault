## Problem

Existing no-reference IQA models embed a fixed assessment requirement at training time, so adapting to new quality criteria (e.g., different distortion types, face quality, AI-generated images, underwater scenes) requires fine-tuning on newly constructed datasets, which is time-consuming and labor-intensive. The paper asks whether a model can instead comprehend a new assessment requirement at inference time from a small set of exemplar image-score pairs, eliminating the need for fine-tuning or large new datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240014/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model receives one target RGB image (no ground-truth score) plus k−1 image-score pairs (ISPP) sampled from a dataset that encodes the desired assessment requirement; the default k is 10 and pairs are selected by interval or random sampling over the score distribution. The ISPP scores may be MOS or DMOS with arbitrary ranges (e.g., [0,1], [1,100], [1,5]), and the model outputs a single scalar quality score normalized to [0,1]. During training, one of the k ISPs serves as the target with its GT label, while the remaining k−1 act as prompts.

## Method

PromptIQA uses MoNet as a visual encoder to extract per-image features, then concatenates each image feature with its expanded score to form an ISP feature. A 3-block ViT prompt encoder processes each ISP feature to capture the image–score relationship, and a 3-block ViT ISPP fusion module interacts all k−1 prompt features into a single assessment-prompt feature; an 8-block ViT image-prompt fusion module then combines this with the target image feature before a two-layer FC regression head produces the score. The key training insight is that without intervention the model can ignore the prompt and shortcut to image-only prediction, so two data augmentation strategies—random scaling (dividing all scores by the max) and random flipping (converting MOS↔DMOS via α−x)—are applied with probabilities 0.5 and 0.1 to dynamically shift the GT relative to the ISPP, forcing the model to read the requirement from the prompt. The model is trained end-to-end with L1 loss on a mixed set of 12 datasets spanning 5 IQA tasks, and at test time a new ISPP from any requirement simply re-conditions the frozen model without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the 12-dataset mixed evaluation across 5 IQA tasks, PromptIQA achieves average SROCC/PLCC of 0.9231/0.9318 (SDN-IQA), 0.9198/0.9328 (ADN-IQA), 0.9698/0.9702 (F-IQA), 0.8509/0.9013 (AIG-IQA on AGIQA3k), and 0.8766/0.8839 (U-IQA). On ADN-IQA, PromptIQA surpasses the best task-specific method (MoNet, 0.9130/0.9257) by 0.68%/0.71% in SROCC/PLCC, and outperforms mixed-training baselines UNIQUE (0.8693/0.8880) and StairIQA (0.8304/0.8614) by substantial margins. In the generalization experiment (Table 3), PromptIQA-MDT with only 10 ISPs as prompt achieves PLCC of 0.8802 (FSIM) and 0.8064 (LPIPS) on TID2013, far exceeding MANIQA-MDT (0.6869/−0.6238) and MoNet-MDT (0.5433/−0.5215) which produce negative correlations on LPIPS-labeled data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two mixed-training baselines, PromptIQA dominates: it outperforms UNIQUE by 5.05%/4.48% (SROCC/PLCC) on ADN-IQA and by 14.75%/15.24% on U-IQA, while beating StairIQA by 8.94%/7.14% on ADN-IQA and 13.42%/10.73% on U-IQA. On SDN-IQA, PromptIQA (0.9231/0.9318) is slightly below TOPIQ-FR (0.9658/0.9665), but TOPIQ-FR is a full-reference method that uses reference images as additional input; against the no-reference variant TOPIQ-NR (0.8853/0.8969), PromptIQA leads by 3.78%/3.49%. On ADN-IQA, PromptIQA surpasses the strongest task-specific baselines MoNet (0.9130/0.9257) and MANIQA (0.9110/0.9235) by 0.68%/0.71% and 0.88%/0.93% respectively. On F-IQA, PromptIQA matches GPFIQA (0.9698/0.9702) and edges out TOPIQ-Face (0.9644/0.9652) by 0.54%/0.50%. On AIG-IQA and U-IQA, PromptIQA outperforms all task-specific methods (CLIPIQA, PSCR, URanker, UIQI, FDUM, UCIQE) by margins of 0.83–13.23% in SROCC, confirming that the ISPP prompt mechanism enables a single model to match or exceed task-specific models without per-task fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the mixed-training IQA line (UNIQUE, StairIQA, hierarchical curriculum learning) by replacing complex network modifications or dataset transformations with a lightweight prompt mechanism: a small set of image-score pairs (ISPP) that encodes the assessment requirement at inference time. The concept of prompt-based adaptation borrows from few-shot and in-context learning in NLP/LLMs, but repurposes it for a regression task where the 'prompt' is a sequence of visual examples with associated scores rather than text. The visual backbone (MoNet) and ViT-based fusion blocks are standard components; the novelty lies in the ISPP formulation and the two data augmentation strategies (random scaling, random flipping) that prevent the model from ignoring the prompt and taking a shortcut through the image alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: when a new assessment requirement emerges (e.g., a new domain or scoring rubric), practitioners no longer need to construct a full IQA dataset and fine-tune; ten image-score pairs suffice. This could significantly lower the barrier to deploying IQA in diverse real-world applications. Limitations include the need for at least a handful of labeled examples for the new requirement, and slightly weaker performance on DMOS-labeled datasets (LIVE, CSIQ) compared to MOS-labeled ones. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly zero-shot novel requirements, how it scales to video or multi-modal quality assessment, and whether the ISPP concept can be combined with text-based prompts for richer requirement specification.
