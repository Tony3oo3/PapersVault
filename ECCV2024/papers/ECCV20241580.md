## Problem

Pose-driven human image animation methods face two persistent challenges: (1) fine-tuning ControlNet on a target domain causes overfitting and loss of generalization, while using it frozen without adaptation leads to severe texture degradation due to misalignment between pose and appearance features; and (2) off-the-shelf pose estimators produce noisy or erroneous keypoints that cause temporal inconsistencies and artifacts in generated videos. No prior work simultaneously addresses both the overfitting/generalization trade-off and the robustness to pose detector errors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source human image (or animation character) and a driving video, from which OpenPose sequences are extracted frame-by-frame. For characters with non-human body proportions (e.g., chibi), a pose re-targeting step rescales the driving pose to match the source image's proportions before feeding it to the model. Output is a temporally consistent animated video; for sequences longer than the model's temporal window, MultiDiffusion is applied along the temporal axis by overlapping pose sequences and averaging predicted noise in the overlap region.

## Method

TCAN uses a two-stage training framework built on Stable Diffusion 1.5. In Stage 1, the pre-trained OpenPose ControlNet is kept frozen while an Appearance-Pose Adaptation (APPA) layer—implemented as LoRA adapters on the denoising UNet's attention projections—aligns the frozen pose features with newly trained appearance features, preventing texture degradation without sacrificing ControlNet's generalization. In Stage 2, temporal layers (AnimateDiff motion modules) are inserted into both the denoising UNet and the frozen ControlNet; the Temporal ControlNet smooths erroneous poses by referencing neighboring frames along the temporal axis. Additionally, a Pose-driven Temperature Map (PTM) is computed from the driving OpenPose sequence as a normalized distance-to-foreground map, which modulates the temperature of temporal attention scores to suppress background flickering while preserving dynamic foreground motion.

## Results

On the TikTok dataset evaluation, TCAN (with AnimateDiff motion module v2) achieves the best L1 (8.81E-05), SSIM (0.745), FID-VID (19.42), and FVD (154.84) among all compared methods, while TCAN (mm1) achieves the best FID (29.07). The only metric where TCAN does not lead is LPIPS, where MagicAnimate scores 0.239 versus TCAN's 0.294. In the Bizarre dataset evaluation (generalization to chibi animation characters), a user study with 27 participants confirms TCAN's superiority across all four criteria (motion/identity, background, flickering, overall preference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCAN (mm2) outperforms MagicAnimate on L1 (8.81E-05 vs 3.13E-04), SSIM (0.745 vs 0.714), FID-VID (19.42 vs 21.75), and FVD (154.84 vs 179.07), but trails on LPIPS (0.294 vs 0.239), suggesting MagicAnimate produces slightly more perceptually similar individual frames. Against DisCo, TCAN wins on every metric except LPIPS, where DisCo edges ahead by 0.002 (0.292 vs 0.294); the gap is most dramatic on FVD (154.84 vs 292.80) and FID-VID (19.42 vs 59.90), indicating substantially better temporal coherence. The IP-Adapter + ControlNet baselines (both image-only and video-extended) are far behind TCAN on all metrics, with FVD values of 802.44 and 666.27 respectively versus TCAN's 154.84. Qualitatively, TCAN handles erroneous OpenPose detections (e.g., missing limbs) far more gracefully than DisCo, which produces severe blurring artifacts, and unlike MagicAnimate, does not collapse to follow erroneous DensePose outputs. In the Bizarre dataset user study, TCAN achieves the highest scores on all four criteria, while DisCo receives the lowest due to flickering and identity loss, and MagicAnimate suffers identity drift toward the driving video's body shape.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCAN extends the pose-driven human image animation line of work (DisCo, MagicAnimate) by fundamentally changing how the pose conditioning network is handled: rather than fine-tuning ControlNet on the target domain as prior methods do, it freezes the pre-trained OpenPose ControlNet and introduces a lightweight LoRA-based APPA layer to align appearance and pose features in the denoising UNet. The temporal robustness mechanism (Temporal ControlNet) borrows the motion module architecture from AnimateDiff but applies it to the ControlNet branch rather than the main UNet, and the long-horizon inference strategy repurposes MultiDiffusion along the temporal axis. The cross-attention mechanism for source image conditioning follows MagicAnimate's design, but the overall two-stage training paradigm (image-level then temporal) is standard in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that freezing ControlNet improves both generalization to unseen domains (e.g., chibi animation characters) and identity preservation challenges the common practice of fine-tuning conditioning networks on target-domain data, offering a simpler and more robust training recipe. The pose re-targeting methodology and PTM for background stability address practical deployment concerns that prior methods largely ignored. However, the method still depends on off-the-shelf pose estimators (OpenPose), and the paper itself flags the deepfake misuse risk, leaving open questions about whether the frozen-ConditionNet strategy scales to more complex multi-person or 3D-consistent animation settings.
