## Problem

Egocentric videos captured by wearable devices present two coupled challenges for 3D scene understanding: natural (non-scanning) camera motion provides incomplete multi-view coverage, and the scenes contain hundreds of rapidly moving objects that create geometric inconsistencies across views. Existing open-world 3D segmentation methods either assume static scenes or require multi-camera setups, and no prior system simultaneously performs 3D reconstruction and open-world instance decomposition from a single egocentric video stream with dynamic content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241152/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes undistorted egocentric RGB frames (from Project Aria fisheye cameras), high-frequency 6DoF camera trajectories, and SAM-generated 2D instance masks as weak supervision. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene where each Gaussian carries a 16-dimensional feature vector, enabling both query-based segmentation (a single click yields a 2D mask or 3D bounding box via Euclidean distance thresholding) and clustering-based full scene decomposition via HDBSCAN. The transient prediction network outputs a per-pixel probability map that is learned entirely from photometric reconstruction loss without any explicit dynamic-object annotation.

## Method

EgoLifter augments 3D Gaussian Splatting with per-Gaussian feature embeddings (d=16) that are differentially rendered into 2D feature maps alongside RGB. A supervised contrastive loss (Gaussian RBF kernel, temperature γ=0.01) pulls rendered features belonging to the same SAM mask together and pushes features from different masks apart, computed independently per image to avoid requiring consistent cross-view instance IDs. A U-Net with MobileNet-v3 backbone predicts a transient probability mask that reweights the photometric loss (Eq. 3) to suppress dynamic-object contributions and restricts contrastive sampling to pixels below a transient threshold (δ=0.5), all trained jointly with a single L1 regularization term to prevent trivial all-transient solutions. The overall loss is a weighted sum of the transient-weighted RGB loss, the contrastive loss, and the L1 regularization, optimized with Adam using the standard 3DGS density control schedule.

## Results

On the Aria Digital Twin (ADT) benchmark, EgoLifter achieves 54.57 mIoU (overall) for in-view 2D instance segmentation and 48.84 mIoU for cross-view segmentation, outperforming Gaussian Grouping (34.81 and 21.58 mIoU respectively) and SAM (50.69 mIoU in-view only). For 3D instance detection of static objects, EgoLifter reaches 23.11 mIoU, substantially exceeding Gaussian Grouping's 7.48 mIoU. In novel view synthesis, EgoLifter attains 20.28 PSNR (overall) and 22.14 PSNR on static regions, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoLifter outperforms Gaussian Grouping [61] by a wide margin across all metrics: +19.76 mIoU on in-view segmentation (54.57 vs 34.81), +27.26 mIoU on cross-view segmentation (48.84 vs 21.58), and +15.63 mIoU on 3D instance detection (23.11 vs 7.48). Against SAM [22], EgoLifter improves in-view overall mIoU from 50.69 to 54.57 and uniquely enables cross-view and 3D segmentation, which SAM cannot perform. The transient prediction module is the key differentiator: EgoLifter achieves 58.15 mIoU on static in-view objects versus 55.67 for the EgoLifter-Static ablation (no transient filtering) and 54.23 for EgoLifter-Deform (dynamic 3DGS), confirming that explicitly filtering dynamic content yields cleaner static reconstruction and segmentation. The trade-off is a slight drop on dynamic objects (37.74 vs 39.61 in-view mIoU), which is by design since EgoLifter focuses on the static scene. Overall, EgoLifter achieves the best performance in every evaluation setting on the ADT benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoLifter builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its geometric representation and on Contrastive Lift's supervised contrastive learning loss for associating 2D segmentation masks across views into consistent 3D instance features. It departs from prior open-world 3D segmentation work (which typically assumes static scenes or multi-camera rigs) by introducing a transient prediction network that filters dynamic objects during reconstruction without requiring any additional supervision beyond photometric loss. The paper positions itself against concurrent 3DGS+feature-rendering efforts (FMGS, LangSplat, Feature3DGS) and NeRF-based lifting methods, distinguishing itself as the first to explicitly handle the dynamic-object challenge inherent to natural egocentric video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first quantitative benchmark for open-world 3D segmentation from dynamic egocentric video, filling a gap that limits evaluation of AR/VR-ready perception systems. The transient prediction module's design—requiring no extra labels and training jointly with standard photometric loss—is a practical recipe likely to be adopted in other egocentric reconstruction pipelines. Key limitations remain: the transient predictor can conflate genuinely dynamic objects with hard-to-reconstruct static regions (e.g., over-exposed windows), and overall performance is bounded by the quality of the underlying 2D segmentation model (SAM). Privacy and digital-ownership questions around 3D digitization of personal environments are raised but left open.
