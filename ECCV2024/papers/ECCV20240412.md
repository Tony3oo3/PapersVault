## Problem

Existing human motion generation models are trained on a canonical mean-shape body and ignore individual body shape and proportions, resulting in homogenized motions that lack physical realism for diverse body types. The central challenge is that paired data—different body shapes performing the same motion—is essentially unavailable in large-scale mocap datasets like AMASS, making direct supervised shape-conditioned training infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a source motion sequence represented as SMPL root translations and 23 root-relative 6D joint rotations (canonicalized to start at the origin with a fixed forward direction), along with the source identity (SMPL shape betas and gender) and a target identity (betas and gender). It outputs a non-autoregressive sequence of T=200 frames of SMPL parameters (root position + joint rotations) for the target body shape, generated in a single forward pass rather than autoregressively.

## Method

HUMOS is a Transformer-based conditional VAE where the encoder maps a source motion and its identity into a shape-agnostic latent code, and the decoder reconstructs the motion for an arbitrary target identity. Training is self-supervised via cycle consistency (encode-decode-encode-decode back to the original identity), but this alone admits trivial solutions that ignore the target identity. To prevent collapse, the authors introduce differentiable intuitive-physics losses (ground penetration, floating, foot sliding) applied to the retargeted motion, plus a novel dynamic stability loss that minimizes the distance between the Zero Moment Point and the Center of Pressure—extending the static stability concept from IPMAN to dynamic sequences. The total loss combines cycle consistency, physics terms, dynamic stability, KL regularization, and a latent embedding cycle loss, all trained jointly on unpaired AMASS data.

## Results

On the shape-conditioned motion reconstruction task evaluated on AMASS, HUMOS achieves 1.04 cm Float, 7.37% Skate, 71.9% dynamic stability, and 14.62 cm BoS distance, substantially outperforming all baselines on these four metrics. HUMOS's only metric where it does not achieve the best score is Penetrate (1.23 cm), where the heuristically grounded variants (TEMOS-Simple-G and TEMOS-Rokoko-G) reach 0.75 cm by explicitly translating the sequence to the ground plane. In a perceptual study, HUMOS receives an average realism rating of 3.64/5, compared to 3.25 for TEMOS-Rokoko and 3.19 for TEMOS-Rokoko-G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUMOS is compared against four baselines built from TEMOS (a state-of-the-art text-to-motion model) combined with either naive identity copying (TEMOS-Simple) or the commercial Rokoko retargeting system (TEMOS-Rokoko), each with and without a heuristic grounding step (-G). HUMOS wins decisively on Float (1.04 cm vs. 3.85–6.55 cm), Skate (7.37% vs. 20.05–27.07%), and dynamic stability (71.9% vs. 45.85–55.92%), indicating that the learned shape-conditioned generation produces far fewer physical artifacts than post-hoc retargeting. The only metric where HUMOS trails is Penetrate, where the -G variants achieve 0.75 cm by rigidly translating the entire sequence to the ground; however, this grounding heuristic inflates their Float scores (4.39–4.44 cm) and does not improve Skate or stability. In the perceptual study, HUMOS is rated 3.64/5 in realism versus 3.25 for TEMOS-Rokoko and 3.19 for TEMOS-Rokoko-G, with participants notably preferring the ungrounded Rokoko variant over the grounded one, suggesting that heuristic retargeting struggles to generalize across diverse body shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUMOS extends the Transformer-based VAE encoder from TEMOS by conditioning it on SMPL body shape and gender parameters, and adapts the cycle-consistency training paradigm from CycleGAN to the shape-motion space to handle the absence of paired identity-motion data. The differentiable intuitive-physics terms (penetration, float, foot sliding) are generalized from IPMAN's static-pose formulation to dynamic sequences, and a novel dynamic stability loss based on the Zero Moment Point is introduced to enforce biomechanical balance during locomotion. This positions the work at the intersection of data-driven motion generation and physics-based constraints, departing from both heuristic retargeting pipelines and non-differentiable RL-based physics simulators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUMOS is the first data-driven motion model to simultaneously produce realistic, physically plausible, and dynamically stable motions conditioned on body shape, enabling learned retargeting between characters without paired data — a practical need for game and animation pipelines. Key limitations include subtle motion-style differences across very different body shapes (possibly due to limited shape diversity in AMASS), no handling of self-penetration, and conditioning only on body shape rather than broader motion style attributes. Open questions include how training data distribution affects generalization to extreme body shapes, and whether the framework extends to style-specific conditioning (emotion, physiological differences) once annotated data becomes available.
