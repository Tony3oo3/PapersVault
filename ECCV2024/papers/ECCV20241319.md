## Problem

Human-scene interaction (HSI) generation is bottlenecked by the scarcity of paired human-3D-scene data, since simultaneously capturing humans and high-quality scene scans is expensive and yields limited diversity. The paper argues that for static HSI, the scene's space occupancy is the primary physical constraint, not the full geometric or semantic scene, and that this insight can unlock large-scale training data from existing motion-only corpora.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241319/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive step the controller consumes a canonicalized historical motion window (joint trajectories, root orientation, velocities), the current SMPL pose state, a 25×25×25 binary occupancy grid (8 cm voxels) computed in the human's egocentric frame, and optional target signals (root/hand/foot positions or future end-effector trajectories). It outputs the next SMPL pose in the egocentric canonical frame plus a short future trajectory window for end-effectors, operating at 10 FPS with a one-frame history/future window. Because the occupancy grid is re-computed in real time relative to the moving human, the same interface naturally extends to dynamic scenes without architectural changes.

## Method

The core contribution is reframing static HSI as Human-Occupancy Interaction: for any motion sequence, the human mesh is voxelized and the complement is treated as a pseudo-scene occupancy, converting 98k motion-only clips from 13 datasets into the Motion Occupancy Base (MOB) with extremely stringent geometric constraints. A lightweight autoregressive transformer (2-layer encoder, 1-layer decoder, 512-dim, 8 heads) is trained fully supervised on MOB to predict the next motion frame given the occupancy and target signals, using a mixture of L1/L2 losses plus a penetration loss and an occupancy-field loss. A Field Regulation module, inspired by potential-field robot planning, injects a corrective velocity component proportional to proximity to occupied voxels, compelling the model to decelerate and avoid collisions; it is trained jointly so the network adapts rather than post-hoc velocity edits causing foot sliding. The autoregressive formulation and real-time canonical occupancy computation enable seamless generalization to dynamic scenes (e.g., an automatic door) despite training exclusively on static data.

## Results

On the MOB goal-reaching benchmark, the proposed controller achieves a 77.91% success rate, substantially outperforming the best prior method DIMOS (15.73%) by over 62 percentage points. Penetration drops from 123.12 voxels/frame (DIMOS) to 15.21, and average distance-to-target improves from 48.97 cm to 15.30 cm. On the CIRCLE hand-reaching task, the MOB-trained controller reaches 73.01% success with 9.56 cm collision depth, and combining MOB with CIRCLE training further boosts success to 78.97%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three prior HSI methods: NSM (Starke et al., ACM TOG 2019), SAMP (Hassan et al., ICCV 2021), and DIMOS (Zhao et al., ICCV 2023). All three baselines fail dramatically on the complex MOB occupancy: success rates are 11.76%, 13.94%, and 15.73% respectively, while the proposed method reaches 77.91%. The gap is most pronounced in penetration: DIMOS penetrates 123.12 voxels/frame versus 15.21 for the proposed method, and SAMP reaches 145.74. The authors attribute the baselines' failures to their locomotion-interaction decomposition, which relies on rigid-body path-finding (A* or NavMesh) that fails on 69–70% of MOB samples due to the extreme geometric complexity. On the CIRCLE benchmark (Table 2), no direct baseline comparison is provided, but the MOB-only model already achieves 73.01% success with lower collision depth (9.56 cm) than the CIRCLE-only model (13.74 cm), demonstrating that training on the harder MOB occupancy generalizes well to realistic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant HSI generation paradigm that requires paired motion-scene data (PROX, CIRCLE, HUMANISE) by reframing static HSI as interaction with space occupancy alone, eliminating the need for realistic 3D scene scans. Methodologically, it unifies the two prevailing architectural choices—end-to-end scene-to-motion transformers (CIRCLE, HUMANISE) and two-stage locomotion-interaction decomposition (GAMMA, DIMOS)—into a single autoregressive controller that treats locomotion and interaction as one continuous process. The Field Regulation module borrows the potential-field concept from classical robot motion planning (Hwang & Ahuja, 1992), adapting it to human joint-level collision avoidance. The data pipeline repurposes large-scale motion-only corpora (AMASS and 12 other datasets) by inverting the human mesh occupancy to synthesize a 'pseudo-scene,' a trick not previously exploited for HSI training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The occupancy-based reframing substantially lowers the data barrier for HSI research: practitioners can train on freely available motion capture without expensive scene scanning, and the MOB dataset (98k instances) provides a new benchmark with geometrically stringent constraints. The demonstrated generalization from over-complex MOB to simpler real-world scenes (CIRCLE, Replica) and the ability to handle dynamic obstacles (automatic door) from purely static training data open a path toward more robust, deployment-ready HSI systems for embodied AI and animation. Key open questions include whether the occupancy abstraction suffices for richer HOI (beyond target-pose control), how to address the remaining foot-sliding artifact with principled metrics, and whether the occupancy-changing-rate gap between training and dynamic testing can be closed.
