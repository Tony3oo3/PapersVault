## Problem

Standard Masked Image Modeling (MIM) methods rely on random token masking that ignores the varying importance of individual tokens within an image, and they exhibit significant performance instability with even minor changes to the masking ratio. No existing approach can adapt the masking ratio to the unique content of each sample, leaving a gap between one-size-fits-all masking and the diverse visual compositions present in real datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242093/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224, patchified into 196 tokens of dimension D for ViT) and produces two outputs: a binary token mask M ∈ {0,1}^{N×L} indicating which tokens to hide, and a per-sample adaptive masking ratio R_dyna that varies across the batch. The salience computation operates on the token representation tensor X ∈ R^{N×L×D} via a single batch matrix-matrix product, adding negligible overhead to the forward pass.

## Method

SBAM computes token salience as the column-wise sum of the softmax-normalized affinity matrix (i.e., the outgoing attention weight of each token), which quantifies how much each token contributes to others in the image—distinct from prior attention-based methods that use incoming weight. Uniform noise is added to the salience scores to preserve masking diversity, and tokens are then sorted by adjusted salience with the top-K selected for masking. The Adaptive Masking Ratio (AMR) component dynamically sets the masking proportion per sample by measuring the fraction of tokens exceeding a salience threshold δ, scaling the base ratio r within a range of ±Δr. This makes the masking ratio content-aware: images with large salient objects receive higher masking ratios while those with small or diffuse objects receive lower ones. The entire pipeline is a drop-in replacement for the random masking step in any MIM framework (MAE, BootMAE, iBoT, CMAE) without modifying the encoder-decoder architecture or reconstruction loss.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K fine-tuning accuracy, SBAM consistently improves over its respective baselines across all tested MIM frameworks. The largest gains appear on iBoT (ViT-B, 100 epochs), where SBAM lifts accuracy from 71.5% to 74.4% (+2.9 pp), and on MAE (ViT-L, 400 epochs), from 84.3% to 85.1% (+0.8 pp). For ViT-B models at 400 epochs, SBAM adds 0.7–0.8 pp over MAE, BootMAE, and CMAE baselines. SBAM also outperforms the dedicated masking strategies AMT (83.6% vs. 82.8%) and AttMask (74.4% vs. 72.8%) under matched experimental settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBAM is evaluated as a plug-in masking strategy on top of five MIM baselines and two dedicated masking methods, winning in every comparison. Against MAE [14], SBAM improves fine-tuning accuracy by 0.8 pp on ViT-L (85.1 vs. 84.3) and 0.7 pp on ViT-B (83.6 vs. 82.9), while also demonstrating superior robustness to masking-ratio variation (measured via PIMR). Against BootMAE [11] and CMAE [17] (both ViT-B, 400 epochs), SBAM adds 0.7 pp (84.8 vs. 84.1 and 84.5 vs. 83.8 respectively). The most dramatic gain is on iBoT [43] (ViT-B, 100 epochs), where SBAM yields a +2.9 pp improvement (74.4 vs. 71.5). Against the attention-driven masking strategy AMT [25] on MAE ViT-B, SBAM achieves 83.6% versus AMT's 82.8% and basic AM's 82.5%. Against AttMask [18] within the iBoT framework, SBAM reaches 74.4% compared to AttMask-Hint's 72.8% and AttMask-High's 72.5%, without requiring the distillation overhead those methods incur.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Masked Image Modeling (MIM) paradigm established by MAE and SimMIM, and positions itself against prior strategic masking methods (AMT, AttMask, object-wise MAE) that use attention or semantic cues to select tokens. Its key conceptual departure is using the *outgoing* weight (column-wise sum of the normalized affinity matrix) rather than the incoming weight to define token salience, arguing that a token's contribution to others better captures its pivotal role in the image. The Adaptive Masking Ratio (AMR) component extends the fixed-ratio assumption shared by all prior MIM methods by computing a per-sample masking ratio from the salience distribution, a degree of 'tailoring' the authors note is unavailable in existing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value of SBAM lies in its plug-and-play simplicity (no distillation, no auxiliary detection, no duplicated forward passes) combined with demonstrated robustness to masking-ratio sensitivity—a known pain point in MIM pre-training that complicates hyperparameter transfer across datasets. The AMR idea of per-sample adaptive ratios is a reasonable next step, though its incremental gain over SBM alone is modest. Open questions include whether the outgoing-weight salience signal generalizes beyond ImageNet-1K and ViT backbones, whether the robustness claim holds under different masking-ratio ranges or architectures, and whether the self-defined PIMR metric fairly captures the robustness advantage. The iBoT improvement (+2.9 pp) is notably larger than gains on other baselines (~0.7 pp), suggesting the method may be most beneficial when the baseline masking is weakest, but this warrants further investigation.
