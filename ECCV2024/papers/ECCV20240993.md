## Problem

Existing anomaly detection methods excel in either high-level semantic benchmarks (e.g., detecting unseen objects) or low-level industrial benchmarks (e.g., detecting cracks), but rarely achieve cross-domain proficiency. Leading semantic models like Transformaly and MSAD drop significantly on industrial datasets, while industrial-focused models like SimpleNet and Recontrast underperform on semantic tasks, suggesting overfitting of methodology to specific dataset characteristics. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a single framework that handles semantic, near-distribution, and industrial anomaly detection with minimal per-task modification.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (rescaled to 518×518) and extracts spatial patch tokens and per-head attention maps from a frozen DINOv2 ViT backbone, deliberately omitting the classification token to maintain task generality. At inference, it produces per-patch anomaly scores that are aggregated into an image-level score by averaging the top-K highest patch scores, where K is set to 1369 (all patches) for semantic/near-distribution tasks and K=10 for industrial tasks with small defect regions. The per-patch scores also yield interpretable anomaly localization maps without requiring a separate segmentation head.

## Method

GeneralAD combines a self-supervised anomaly feature generation (SAG) module with a cross-patch attention discriminator. The SAG module creates pseudo-abnormal features by applying one of three distortion strategies to normal DINOv2 patch features: adding Gaussian noise (ε=0.25) to all patches, adding noise to a random subset of patches, or shuffling the N highest-attention patches (selected via a randomly sampled attention head) to simulate logical anomalies. The discriminator is a lightweight transformer block that applies multi-head attention (4 heads) over all patch features simultaneously—augmented with positional embeddings—followed by a 3-layer MLP (hidden dim 2048) that outputs a per-patch anomaly score. Training uses cross-entropy loss between predicted patch scores and the distortion mask, with the distortion strategy selected per benchmark (Noise All Patches for semantic, Noise Random Patches for industrial, plus Attention Shuffle for logical anomalies). The key architectural insight is that attending across all patches enables the discriminator to capture both local structural defects and global semantic shifts, unlike patch-wise methods that lack cross-patch context.

## Results

GeneralAD (DINOv2-Large) achieves image-level AUROC of 99.3 on CIFAR-10, 94.6 on Aircraft-FGVC, 99.2 on MVTec-AD, and 99.0 pixel-level AUROC on VisA, setting state-of-the-art on 6 of 10 evaluated datasets. On the remaining four, it matches or comes within ~1% of the best baseline. In few-shot settings (1–8 shots on MVTec-AD), GeneralAD (Large) reaches 87.5–93.6 AUROC, consistently outperforming PatchCore by 2–5 points across all shot budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against semantic-focused baselines (Transformaly, KDAD*, MSAD, FITYMI), GeneralAD (Large) wins on every dataset where both methods report, with the largest margins on near-anomaly detection (Aircraft-FGVC: 94.6 vs. 88.7 for FITYMI, +5.9 pts) and industrial detection (MVTec-AD: 99.2 vs. 87.9 for Transformaly, +11.3 pts); the sole exception is Stanford-Cars, where FITYMI leads 90.8 vs. 87.3. Against industrial-focused baselines (SimpleNet, Recontrast, PatchCore, RDAD), GeneralAD dominates on semantic and near-distribution tasks (e.g., CIFAR-10: 99.3 vs. 86.5 for SimpleNet) and matches or exceeds them on industrial tasks, losing only marginally to SimpleNet on MVTec-AD (99.2 vs. 99.6) and to Recontrast on VisA image-level (95.9 vs. 97.5) and MVTec-AD (99.2 vs. 99.5). On pixel-level localization, GeneralAD (Large) achieves 99.0 on VisA (SOTA, beating Recontrast's 98.2 and PatchCore's 98.8) and 97.7 on MVTec-AD (within 0.7 of Recontrast's 98.4). In few-shot anomaly detection, GeneralAD (Large) outperforms PatchCore at every shot count (e.g., 87.5 vs. 84.1 at 1-shot; 93.6 vs. 92.2 at 8-shot), confirming sample efficiency despite requiring a training stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying three previously separate anomaly detection sub-fields—semantic, near-distribution, and industrial—under a single framework, rather than building on any one prior method. Conceptually, it repurposes the patch-based structure inherent to pretrained Vision Transformers (DINOv2) and introduces a self-supervised anomaly generation module that creates pseudo-abnormal samples via simple feature-space operations (Gaussian noise, attention-guided patch shuffling), departing from the diffusion-model-based pseudo-anomaly generation of FITYMI and the reconstruction-based approaches of SimpleNet. The cross-patch attention discriminator also departs from per-patch independent scoring (as in SimpleNet) by modeling inter-patch correlations, enabling detection of both local structural defects and global semantic shifts within one architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single trained model with minimal per-task tuning (primarily the top-K aggregation parameter and distortion strategy) achieves competitive or state-of-the-art results across ten diverse datasets spanning three benchmarks, reducing the need to select and tune domain-specific methods. However, the method does not fully close the gap everywhere—Stanford-Cars performance lags behind FITYMI, and the K parameter still requires manual specification based on expected anomaly size, limiting true zero-shot generality. Open questions include whether the self-supervised distortion strategies can be made fully adaptive (eliminating per-dataset hyperparameter choices) and whether the approach extends to video or multi-modal anomaly detection.
