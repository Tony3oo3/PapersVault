## Problem

While graph neural networks and standard transformers have been widely applied to multi-agent trajectory prediction, the hypergraph transformer architecture—which could naturally capture both individual pair-wise interactions and group-level behaviors within a unified attention mechanism—remains unexplored. Existing group-aware methods (GroupNet, DynGroupNet) rely on message-passing rather than transformer attention, and prior group-reasoning approaches either enforce non-overlapping groups or require manual specification of group sizes, limiting their ability to model complex real-world social dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model consumes past 2D trajectories of N agents (10 frames/2.0 s for NBA, 8 frames/3.2 s for ETH-UCY and SDD), using both absolute and relative positions for NBA (input dim 4) and relative positions only for the pedestrian datasets (input dim 2). It outputs k=20 future trajectory predictions per agent (20 frames/4.0 s for NBA, 12 frames/4.8 s for ETH-UCY and SDD), with the variety loss selecting the best-of-k prediction via minimum L2-norm during training.

## Method

MART's core encoder (MARTE) pairs a standard Pair-wise Relational Transformer (PRT) with a novel Hyper Relational Transformer (HRT) that extends relational attention to hypergraph structures: hyperedge features are aggregated and injected into the query, key, and value projections, acting as group-aware relative positional information that steers attention toward group-wise relations. The Adaptive Group Estimator (AGE) infers overlapping group memberships by computing a cosine-similarity affinity matrix and applying a learnable threshold through a unit step function, with gradients propagated via a modified Straight-Through Estimation trick—eliminating the need for manual group-size specification. Node, pair-wise edge, and hyperedge features are initialized from past trajectories via MLPs, encoded through L=4 layers of PRT and HRT, then concatenated with the initial node features and decoded by k independent 3-layer MLP heads. The entire network is trained end-to-end with the variety loss, and achieves 1.5M parameters and 43.3M MACs, substantially fewer than competing SOTA methods.

## Results

On the NBA dataset (4.0 s prediction horizon), MART achieves min ADE₂₀/min FDE₂₀ of 0.73/0.90 m, reducing ADE by 3.9% and FDE by 11.8% over the previous SOTA EqMotion (0.76/1.02 m). On SDD (4.8 s), MART reaches 7.43/11.82 m, improving min ADE₂₀ by 7.8% over EigenTrajectory (8.05 m) while matching LED's FDE. On ETH-UCY, MART attains an average of 0.21/0.33 m, comparable to LED (0.21/0.33 m), while using 7.3× fewer parameters (1.5 M vs. 10.9 M) and 347.3× fewer MACs (43.3 M vs. 15.0 G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the NBA dataset, MART outperforms all eight compared methods, with the most notable margin over EqMotion (0.76/1.02 → 0.73/0.90, i.e., 3.9% ADE / 11.8% FDE reduction) and LED (0.81/1.10 → 0.73/0.90). On SDD, MART achieves the best min ADE₂₀ (7.43 m), beating ET (8.05 m) by 7.8% and LED (8.48 m) by 12.4%, while its min FDE₂₀ (11.82 m) is slightly worse than LED (11.66 m) but better than ET (13.25 m). On ETH-UCY, MART ties LED on the average (0.21/0.33 m) and leads on the ETH subset (0.35/0.47 vs. 0.36/0.53), though it trails LED on HOTEL (0.14/0.22 vs. 0.11/0.17) and UNIV (0.25/0.45 vs. 0.26/0.43). In terms of computational efficiency, MART uses only 1.5 M parameters and 43.3 M MACs, compared to LED's 10.9 M / 15.0 G and EqMotion's 3.0 M / 147.1 M, making it 7.3× and 347.3× lighter than LED respectively. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the MARTE encoder (0.727/0.903) surpasses both the standard Transformer (0.745/0.913) and the Relational Transformer (0.731/0.912), and that the AGE module (0.727/0.903) outperforms the group estimation in GP-Graph (0.740/0.913) and GroupNet (0.732/0.912).

## Relation to Prior Work

MART extends the Relational Transformer (RT) [13] from pairwise graph structures to hypergraph structures via the proposed Hyper Relational Transformer (HRT), which injects hyperedge (group) features into the attention mechanism. It also adapts the Straight-Through Estimation trick from binarized neural networks [8] to learn a differentiable group incidence matrix in the Adaptive Group Estimator. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior hypergraph trajectory predictors (GroupNet, DynGroupNet) that relied on message-passing, and from GP-Graph's non-overlapping group partitioning, by unifying transformer-based relational reasoning with overlapping, adaptively-sized hyperedges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MART is the first hypergraph transformer applied to multi-agent trajectory prediction, and its 11.8% FDE reduction on the challenging NBA dataset alongside a 7.3× parameter reduction over LED makes it practically attractive for real-time deployment. The AGE module's ability to infer overlapping groups without manual size specification addresses a practical limitation of prior group-reasoning methods. However, the model still treats past trajectories as a static embedding without explicit temporal modeling and ignores scene context (maps, images), leaving open questions about whether spatio-temporal and multi-modal extensions would further close the gap on complex scenes.
