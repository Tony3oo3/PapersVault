## Problem

While text-to-image diffusion models have demonstrated strong sentence-level visual grounding capabilities, their potential for context-aware phrase-level understanding remains largely unexplored. Existing diffusion-based segmentation approaches rely on complete sentences or prompt-crafted descriptions, leaving open whether these models can localize and segment individual noun phrases within longer narrative contexts without any task-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241409/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 512×512) and a long narrative text containing multiple noun phrases as input. Because CLIP's text encoder has a fixed maximum token length, the narrative is split into sub-paragraphs that each fit within this limit, and all sub-paragraphs are encoded to preserve full contextual semantics. The output is a set of binary segmentation masks (one per noun phrase) at the original image resolution, produced entirely through zero-shot inference with no access to training images, texts, or mask annotations.

## Method

DiffPNG repurposes Stable Diffusion's U-Net during the reverse diffusion process as a segmentation engine, decomposing PNG into a locate-then-segment paradigm. The Locate-to-Segment Processor (LSP) first uses cross-attention maps to identify anchor pixels (those exceeding a confidence threshold β) for each noun phrase, then aggregates the self-attention rows at those anchor locations to produce a spatially coherent segmentation mask via binarization. A Subject-Focused Feature Aggregator weights individual word-level attention maps within each phrase by their cosine similarity to the final noun token, emphasizing the head noun. Finally, the SAM-based Mask Refinement (SMR) module generates candidate masks via SAM's 'segment everything' pipeline and matches them to the LSP output using two IoU-style scores (addressing under- and over-segmentation separately), taking the union of matched candidates to sharpen mask boundaries. The entire pipeline requires no training—only Null-text Inversion to obtain consistent latent noise and standard forward inference through the frozen diffusion U-Net.

## Results

On the PNG benchmark, DiffPNG achieves 33.54% Overall Average Recall in a fully zero-shot setting, and the enhanced DiffPNG* (with SAM-based Mask Refinement) reaches 38.49%. These results substantially outperform other zero-shot diffusion-based methods: +10.08% over DatasetDiffusion (23.46%) and +9.46% over DiffSeg (24.08%). For reference, the best fully-supervised method (PPO-TD) achieves 66.1%, highlighting the remaining gap between zero-shot and supervised approaches. The random baseline scores only 15.53%, underscoring the effectiveness of the diffusion-based localization-segmentation paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the zero-shot category, DiffPNG decisively outperforms all competing methods: it surpasses DatasetDiffusion [47] by +10.08% and DiffSeg [69] by +9.46% in Overall Average Recall, with the SMR-enhanced DiffPNG* extending the lead to +15.03% and +14.41% respectively. Against the random baseline (15.53%), DiffPNG* gains +22.96%, confirming that the cross-attention localization and self-attention segmentation pipeline captures meaningful image-text alignment. Against fully-supervised methods, DiffPNG* (38.49%) still trails the best supervised approach PPO-TD [22] (66.1%) by a substantial 27.61% margin, as expected given the absence of any task-specific training. Among supervised baselines, PPO-TD [22] and PiGLET [17] lead at 66.1% and 65.9% respectively, while earlier methods like PNG [16] (55.4%) and MCN [40] (54.2%) lag further behind. The paper's key contribution is thus not to match supervised performance but to demonstrate that a pre-trained diffusion model, used entirely zero-shot, can achieve phrase-level grounding that far exceeds prior zero-shot diffusion-based segmentation attempts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growing line of work repurposing text-to-image diffusion models for vision understanding tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) by applying them to Panoptic Narrative Grounding in a zero-shot setting. It departs from prior diffusion-based segmentation approaches (DatasetDiffusion, DiffSeg) that either generate synthetic training data or directly threshold attention maps, instead proposing a decoupled localize-then-segment paradigm where cross-attention identifies anchor points and self-attention aggregates them into spatially coherent masks. The framework is built on Stable Diffusion's U-Net attention layers, Null-text Inversion for consistent latent encoding of real images, and SAM for post-hoc mask refinement, combining three independently developed tools into a unified zero-shot pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that pre-trained diffusion models encode sufficient phrase-level semantic information in their attention maps to perform PNG without any task-specific training, validating a broader hypothesis about the representational richness of generative models for discriminative tasks. However, the 38.49% zero-shot Average Recall remains roughly 28 points below the best fully-supervised method (66.1%), leaving open whether this gap can be closed with better attention extraction strategies or multi-step refinement. The approach is also limited to a single dataset (PNG) and a single diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.4), so generalizability to longer narratives, other diffusion architectures, or additional grounding tasks remains untested.
