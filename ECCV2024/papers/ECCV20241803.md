## Problem

Self-attention reconstruction models for unified anomaly detection (e.g., UniAD) rely solely on contextual information from target features, which can perfectly reconstruct both normal and anomalous regions—especially camouflaged anomalies that blend seamlessly into their surroundings. Additionally, reconstruction in low-resolution latent space (1/16 of input) limits pixel-level segmentation accuracy. The paper asks how to elegantly combine contextual and global structural information to improve unified anomaly detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241803/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB test image (default 224×224, up to 320×320) and a single normal image prompt from the same class, both passed through a frozen EfficientNet-b4 backbone to extract offline features. At inference, the prompt is selected by computing cosine similarity between the test feature and a pre-extracted class-aware prompt pool. The output is a combined pixel-level anomaly segmentation map (weighted sum of upscaled reconstruction error and a supervised refiner prediction) and an image-level anomaly score taken as the maximum value of the segmentation map.

## Method

OneNIP builds on UniAD's encoder-decoder transformer but replaces the learned query embedding with a normal image prompt feature, then introduces a bidirectional cross-attention decoder that dynamically updates both prompt and target features in alternating prompt-to-features and features-to-prompt directions across multiple layers. An unsupervised restoration stream synthesizes pseudo-anomalies (via CutPaste or DRAEM) and trains the network to restore their features back to the corresponding normal features under prompt guidance, increasing reconstruction difficulty and strengthening prompt reliance. A lightweight supervised refiner (two transposed convolution blocks plus a 1×1 conv) regresses the 1/16-resolution reconstruction error up to 1/4 resolution using Dice loss on both real normal and pseudo-anomalous samples with their pixel-level masks. At inference, the restoration stream is removed, and the final anomaly map is a weighted combination of the upscaled reconstruction error and the refiner output.

## Results

On MVTec, OneNIP achieves 97.9% I-ROC and 63.7% P-PR, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art UniAD (96.5% I-ROC, 44.7% P-PR) by 1.4 and 19.0 points respectively. On BTAD, OneNIP reaches 92.6% I-ROC and 56.8% P-PR versus UniAD's 92.2% and 50.9%. On VisA, OneNIP attains 92.5% I-ROC and 43.3% P-PR compared to UniAD's 90.8% and 33.6%. The largest relative gains appear in pixel-level segmentation (P-PR), where OneNIP improves over UniAD by 19.0, 5.9, and 9.7 points on MVTec, BTAD, and VisA respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneNIP outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all three datasets. The closest competitor is UniAD [52], the reconstruction-based method that OneNIP builds upon; OneNIP surpasses it by 1.4 points in I-ROC and 19.0 points in P-PR on MVTec, with similar margins on BTAD (+0.4 I-ROC, +5.9 P-PR) and VisA (+1.7 I-ROC, +9.7 P-PR). Among embedding-based methods, PatchCore [37] is the strongest, reaching 89.8% I-ROC and 50.1% P-PR on MVTec, yet OneNIP still leads by 8.1 and 13.6 points respectively. Discriminator-based methods show the largest degradation in the unified setting: SimpleNet [25] drops from 99.6% to 78.2% I-ROC on MVTec, and DRAEM [54] is highly inconsistent, achieving 49.6% P-PR on MVTec but only 12.3% on BTAD and 15.1% on VisA. The remaining embedding-based baselines (CS-Flow, PaDiM, DFM, CFA) all trail OneNIP by at least 5 points in I-ROC and 10+ points in P-PR on MVTec, with DFM [1] and CFA [20] performing particularly poorly in the unified setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on UniAD's transformer-based feature reconstruction framework for unified anomaly detection, replacing its learned query embedding with a normal image prompt and converting the unidirectional layer-wise query decoder into a bidirectional cross-attention decoder. Conceptually, it draws on predictive coding theory from neuroscience and repurposes the prompt paradigm (previously explored with textual prompts in CLIP-based methods) as a visual image prompt for reconstruction guidance. The restoration stream borrows the synthetic-anomaly generation strategies from discriminator-based methods (CutPaste, DRAEM) but repurposes them for an unsupervised feature-restoration objective rather than a classification or segmentation loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified anomaly detection setting (one model for all classes) is practically critical for industrial deployment where maintaining per-class models is infeasible, and this paper demonstrates that a single normal image prompt can substantially close the gap between unified and per-class performance, particularly for pixel-level segmentation. The large P-PR gains (e.g., 44.7%→63.7% on MVTec) are encouraging, though the supervised refiner accounts for much of the improvement, raising the question of how well the purely unsupervised components generalize to truly novel anomaly types. The method's sensitivity to incorrect class prompts (I-ROC dropping from 91.4% to 67.3% with a wrong-class prompt) and the acknowledged simplicity of the bidirectional decoder and refiner leave clear room for more robust and scalable prompt mechanisms.
