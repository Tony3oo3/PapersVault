## Problem

Class-agnostic object counting requires a single model to count arbitrary novel object classes from only a few exemplars or a class name, demanding both accurate characterization of the target object and detailed understanding of image contents (textures, shapes, spatial layout). Existing methods rely on hand-crafted feature matching or CLIP-based semantic alignment, which struggle to capture the fine-grained spatial and structural information needed for accurate counting. The paper identifies that pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models implicitly encode comprehensive image understanding but lack a mechanism to extract this knowledge for counting.

## Input / Output

In the few-shot setting, the input is an RGB image with 3 exemplar bounding boxes specifying the target class; in the zero-shot setting, the input is an RGB image plus a class name string. The output is a 64×64 density map whose pixel values sum to the predicted object count. The method operates on Stable Diffusion v1-4 latents with the DDPM scheduler, and all attention maps are resized to a common 64×64 spatial resolution before being fed to the regression head.

## Method

CountDiff freezes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model and learns two embeddings in its text-embedding space: an object-specific embedding E_os (obtained by RoI-pooling exemplar crops and projecting them via a learned exemplar projector in few-shot mode, or by encoding the class name with the pre-trained text encoder in zero-shot mode) and an object-agnostic embedding E_oa (a single learnable vector shared across all images). These embeddings serve as keys in the diffusion UNet's cross-attention layers, producing cross-attention maps that highlight target-object regions, while self-attention maps provide pixel-level semantic grouping; a weighted combination yields an enhanced attention map. A regression head takes the concatenated cross-attention, self-attention, and enhanced attention maps to predict the density map. Training minimizes a normalized MSE loss on the density map plus a weighted diffusion denoising loss that constrains the learned embeddings to remain within the diffusion model's text-embedding manifold. A lightweight test-time adaptation step (3 gradient iterations) fine-tunes E_os using a synthetic density signal derived from the exemplar box locations and sizes.

## Results

On FSC-147 (few-shot, 3 exemplars), CountDiff achieves MAE=8.43 and RMSE=31.03 on the validation set, and MAE=9.24 and RMSE=53.41 on the test set, outperforming the previous best method LOCA (MAE=10.79, RMSE=56.97) by 1.55 MAE and 3.56 RMSE on test. In the zero-shot setting (class name only), CountDiff reaches MAE=14.83 and RMSE=103.15 on the FSC-147 test set, beating the prior best Amini-Naieni et al. (MAE=15.73, RMSE=106.88). Cross-dataset generalization on CARPK confirms the advantage: CountDiff achieves MAE=8.36 (few-shot) and MAE=10.32 (zero-shot), both SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CountDiff outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both the few-shot and zero-shot FSC-147 test sets. In the few-shot setting, the margin over the closest competitor LOCA [43] is 1.55 MAE (9.24 vs 10.79) and 3.56 RMSE (53.41 vs 56.97); over the next tier (CounTR [6], SPDCN [21], SAFECount [47]) the gap widens to 2.7–5.1 MAE. In the zero-shot setting, CountDiff beats Amini-Naieni et al. [1] by 0.90 MAE (14.83 vs 15.73) and Jiang et al. [16] by 2.95 MAE (14.83 vs 17.78), while the earliest zero-shot method Xu et al. [45] trails by 7.26 MAE. Cross-dataset on CARPK, CountDiff improves over LOCA [43] by 1.61 MAE in few-shot (8.36 vs 9.97) and over Jiang et al. [16] by 1.64 MAE in zero-shot (10.32 vs 11.96), confirming that the diffusion-model-derived attention knowledge generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

CountDiff departs from the dominant paradigm in class-agnostic counting, which relies on feature matching between exemplars and image patches (FamNet, LOCA) or CLIP-based semantic alignment for zero-shot (Jiang et al., Xu et al.). Instead, it repurposes a frozen text-to-image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as a knowledge source: by learning object-specific and object-agnostic embeddings in the model's text-embedding space, it extracts cross-attention and self-attention maps that explicitly highlight semantically relevant regions for counting. This is the first work to leverage both cross-attention and self-attention maps from a pre-trained diffusion model, guided by specifically learned embeddings, for class-agnostic counting, carving new ground by treating the diffusion model's implicit image understanding as an explicit, extractable signal rather than a black-box feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the rich spatial and semantic knowledge implicitly encoded in a generation-oriented diffusion model can be explicitly surfaced for a perception task (counting) without fine-tuning the model itself, opening a broader question of how much task-specific knowledge is recoverable from frozen generative models. The notable few-shot-to-zero-shot gap (8.43 vs. 15.50 MAE on FSC-147 validation) highlights that exemplars still carry substantially more information than class names, an open problem for truly annotation-free counting. Limitations include reliance on a single frozen Stable Diffusion 1.4 backbone, evaluation on relatively moderate-density scenes (FSC-147), and the computational cost of running the full UNet at inference. Whether the attention-map extraction strategy transfers to newer, larger diffusion architectures or to denser crowd-counting regimes remains untested.
