## Problem

Gait recognition models struggle to extract identity-intrinsic features because they become entangled with non-identity confounders such as clothing, carrying conditions, and noise. The prior causal approach GaitGCI only intervenes at the final output stage and exclusively in the spatial domain, overlooking confounders that form across multiple network stages and in temporal and spectral domains. Additionally, the absence of explicit semantic consistency constraints can degrade the coherence of learned identity representations for the same subject.

## Input / Output

The method takes gait silhouette sequences (default 64×44 spatial resolution) as input and produces an identity label as output. CLTD is a plug-and-play training-only module that integrates at multiple stages of any existing gait backbone (e.g., DyGait, GaitSet, GaitPart), incurring zero additional computational cost or memory overhead at inference time. The module operates on intermediate feature maps of shape T×C×H×W at each insertion point, generating factual and counterfactual feature vectors that feed into contrastive and cross-entropy losses during training.

## Method

CLTD (Causality-inspired Learning in Triple Domains) eliminates confounders across spatial, temporal, and spectral domains via two components: (1) a Cross Pixel-wise Attention Generator (CPAG) that computes cross-pixel correlations in time, height, and width dimensions using decoupled 1D convolutions and separate FC layers, reducing attention complexity from O(t²h²w²c) to O(t²(c+hw)) while producing high-quality factual and counterfactual attention maps; and (2) a Fourier Projection Head (FPH) that applies FFT to project spatial features into the spectral domain, followed by a Low-frequency Selector (k×k window) and FC layer to obtain compact spectral representations. Training supervises each CLTD with a Factual and Counterfactual Loss combining InfoNCE contrastive learning (enforcing semantic consistency across same-subject sequences) and a Total Direct Effect (TDE) cross-entropy term that forces the factual feature to capture all ID-intrinsic information while the counterfactual feature captures only non-ID confounders. Three CLTDs are inserted at different depths of the backbone to progressively eliminate confounders across feature scales, and the overall loss combines triplet loss, standard cross-entropy, and weighted multi-stage FCF losses.

## Results

On the challenging wild dataset GREW, the proposed CLTD achieves 78.0% Rank-1 and 87.8% Rank-5 accuracy, surpassing the previous best (DyGait) by 6.6% and 4.6% respectively. On Gait3D, it reaches 69.7% Rank-1 and 85.2% Rank-5, improving over DyGait by 3.4% and 4.4%. On the controlled datasets CASIA-B and OU-MVLP, the method achieves 94.8% and 98.6% mean Rank-1 (excluding invalid probes), setting new SOTA by 0.2–0.3% over prior best methods. The largest single improvement is 11.1% on GREW when CLTD is plugged into GaitGCI without its CIL block (60.2% → 71.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 14 compared baselines across every dataset. Against the primary backbone DyGait [50], CLTD yields +0.7% on CASIA-B (94.8 vs 94.1), +6.6% on GREW (78.0 vs 71.4), and +3.4% on Gait3D (69.7 vs 66.3). Against the closest causal competitor GaitGCI [8], the gains are +0.3% on CASIA-B (94.8 vs 94.5), +9.5% on GREW (78.0 vs 68.5), and +19.4% on Gait3D (69.7 vs 50.3), highlighting the advantage of multi-domain (spatial + temporal + spectral) confounder elimination over GaitGCI's spatial-only intervention. On GREW, the improvement over GaitCSV [48] is +13.1% (78.0 vs 64.9), and over GaitBase [9] is +17.9% (78.0 vs 60.1). On the more saturated CASIA-B benchmark, the margin over DANet [31] is modest (+0.2%, 94.8 vs 94.6), consistent with the authors' note that controlled datasets approach performance saturation. The versatility ablation (Table 4) confirms that CLTD improves every tested backbone (GaitSet, GaitPart, GaitGL, GaitBase, GaitGCI, DyGait) on all four datasets, with the largest single gain of +11.1% on GREW when applied to GaitGCI without its CIL block.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GaitGCI [Dou et al., CVPR 2023], which first introduced causal intervention into gait recognition, by addressing three key limitations: (1) applying confounder elimination at multiple network stages rather than only the final output, (2) extending causal intervention from spatial features alone to triple domains (spatial, temporal, spectral), and (3) adding contrastive learning for cross-sequence semantic consistency. The CPAG module repurposes the criss-cross attention mechanism from CCNet [Huang et al., ICCV 2019] for gait-specific temporal-spatial correlation, while the FPH draws on Fourier-domain processing validated in domain generalization and image restoration. The overall design is explicitly plug-and-play, positioning CLTD as a training-time module compatible with diverse backbones (GaitSet, GaitPart, GaitGL, GaitBase, GaitGCI, DyGait) rather than a standalone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that confounders are distributed across multiple network stages and domains, and that multi-stage elimination yields complementary gains (6.6% on GREW vs. 2.2% for a single stage), provides a practical recipe for improving gait robustness without architectural redesign. The 11.1% improvement on GREW (60.2%→71.3%) with GaitGCI-minus-CIL as backbone is the most striking result, suggesting that prior causal methods were under-utilizing the intervention opportunity. However, the authors acknowledge that FPH's fixed low-frequency window is a limitation, and the modest gains on saturated datasets (OU-MVLP +0.3%, CASIA-B +0.7%) indicate diminishing returns in controlled settings. Open questions include whether adaptive spectral selection (e.g., attention-based) could further improve FPH, and whether the causal framework generalizes to other biometric or recognition tasks beyond gait.
