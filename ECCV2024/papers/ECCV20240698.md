## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for 3D perception encourage feature invariance under input transformations, which conflicts with inherently equivariant downstream tasks like object detection where bounding boxes must rotate with the scene. Moreover, prior equivariant approaches (e.g., PointContrast) only consider spatial augmentations and ignore natural temporal deformations such as object motion between sequential LiDAR frames, leaving a gap in leveraging realistic scene dynamics for representation learning.

## Input / Output

The pre-training stage consumes individual LiDAR point clouds (for spatial equivariance) and sequential frame pairs (for temporal equivariance) from KITTI-360 or Waymo, with a frozen PV-RAFT scene-flow network providing per-point 3D displacement vectors between consecutive frames. The trained output is a sparse convolutional 3D feature backbone (SparseVoxel) that maps point clouds to dense voxel features compressed into 2D BEV feature maps, which is then plugged into downstream detectors (SECOND, VoxelRCNN) that produce 3D bounding boxes with category labels.

## Method

E-SSL3D jointly trains a 3D feature encoder for spatial and temporal equivariance through a combined loss. For spatial equivariance, it applies two complementary objectives: a PointInfoNCE contrastive loss for translation, scaling, and flip (where the transformation range is too fine-grained for classification), and a cross-entropy classification loss that predicts which of 10 discrete vertical-axis rotations was applied (where the larger angular range makes classification effective). For temporal equivariance, it adopts a BYOL-style online-target architecture in which the previous frame's voxel feature map is warped into the current frame's coordinate system using the estimated 3D scene flow, and the online network's predictor is trained to match the target network's representation via an L2 loss. The scene flow is computed by a frozen PV-RAFT network, and the warping is performed at the voxel level by applying the per-point displacement to input coordinates and re-voxelizing to sample features from the previous frame's sparse tensor. The final loss is a weighted sum of the contrastive, classification, and flow-equivariance terms, with coefficients tuned to bring all components to comparable scales.

## Results

On KITTI 3D object detection with VoxelRCNN (pre-trained on KITTI-360), E-SSL3D achieves 71.63% mAP at 20% training data, surpassing the no-pre-training baseline (68.78%) by +2.85 points and approaching full-data performance (71.77%). At 100% data, E-SSL3D reaches 72.41% mAP, outperforming PointContrast (70.72%) and STRL (70.71%) by ~1.7 points. The method also shows strong results when pre-trained on Waymo Open Dataset (Table 1), achieving 69.33% mAP at 20% KITTI data. A key finding is that 20% annotated data with E-SSL3D pre-training nearly matches 100% data without pre-training, demonstrating significant data efficiency gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-SSL3D consistently outperforms PointContrast and STRL across all data splits: at 20% KITTI data, it achieves 71.63% mAP vs. 68.97% (PointContrast) and 69.59% (STRL), a margin of +2.66 and +2.04 points respectively. Against ALSO (the strongest baseline, a generative occupancy-prediction method), E-SSL3D wins at 20% (71.63 vs. 70.95) and 100% (72.41 vs. 72.06) but trails slightly at 5% (66.52 vs. 67.00). The paper notes that ALSO trains both the 3D backbone and 2D detection layers, whereas E-SSL3D pre-trains only the 3D backbone, making the comparison favorable to ALSO. E-SSL3D also converges much faster (~10–20 epochs vs. 75 for ALSO). The gains are most pronounced in low-data regimes, where the joint spatio-temporal equivariance signal provides the strongest regularization benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends equivariant self-supervised learning from 2D images (Dangovski et al., FlowE) to 3D LiDAR point clouds, and is the first to incorporate 3D scene flow as a temporal equivariance signal for LiDAR. It builds on BYOL's online-target architecture for the flow equivariance branch and PointContrast's point-level contrastive loss for spatial equivariance, but departs from both by arguing that equivariance (not invariance) is the appropriate symmetry for detection tasks where bounding boxes must transform with the input. The joint spatio-temporal framework and the per-transformation choice between contrastive and classification objectives represent the main conceptual departures from prior equivariant SSL work, which had been evaluated primarily on invariant tasks like image classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that E-SSL3D pre-training with only 20% of KITTI annotations matches full-data training from scratch is directly relevant to reducing annotation costs in autonomous driving. The empirical result that the optimal loss function (contrastive vs. classification) depends on the specific geometric transformation provides actionable guidance for designing equivariant pre-training pipelines. However, the method does not consistently outperform the generative approach ALSO, gains diminish at 100% data, and the framework is limited to sparse convolutional backbones, leaving open whether the same principles transfer to transformer-based detectors or whether combining discriminative and generative pre-training could yield further gains.
