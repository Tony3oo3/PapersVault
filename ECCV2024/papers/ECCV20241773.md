## Problem

Conventional multi-label recognition assigns each label a confidence score but ignores the partial order relations that reflect human preference for which labels are most relevant to a scene's primary theme. Existing Learning-to-Rank methods target documents or recommendation lists rather than label sets, and prior label-ranking work is purely theoretical, lacking semantic understanding of label meaning relative to multimodal input. This paper is the first to formulate and solve multimodal label relevance ranking, where labels of diverse types (objects, events, attributes, character identities) must be ordered by their association with a video clip's content.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities per video clip: N extracted keyframes (processed by ViT), a free-text plot description (processed by RoBERTa), and a standardized set of 20 text labels (concatenated with the description before encoding). The output is a relevance score per label, from which a full ranking is derived by sorting scores in descending order. Labels span a broad semantic range—15,234 distinct classes across 2,551 clips in LRMovieNet—making the task substantially richer than single-modality object tagging.

## Method

LR²PPO is a three-stage actor-critic framework built on a two-stream Transformer with cross-attention fusion. Stage 1 trains a supervised base ranking model (actor) on the source domain using SmoothL1 regression against three relevance categories. Stage 2 trains a reward model on the target domain from a small set of annotated label-pair orderings (as few as 5–10%) augmented with source-domain pairs, using a margin-based pairwise loss. Stage 3 jointly optimizes the actor and a critic via a modified PPO where the state is defined as the current order of a label pair and the action is the reranked order; the standard PPO probability ratio is replaced by a partial-order ratio r′(θ) that applies a hinge penalty H_partial to the predicted score difference, with the direction of the penalty determined by the sign of the estimated advantage relative to a small negative threshold δ. This design lets the policy learn correct pairwise orderings directly from the reward model's preference signal without requiring absolute relevance scores, and the final actor incurs no additional inference overhead.

## Results

On the proposed LRMovieNet dataset, LR²PPO achieves NDCG@1 of 0.6820, NDCG@3 of 0.6714, NDCG@5 of 0.6869, NDCG@10 of 0.7628, and NDCG@20 of 0.8475, outperforming the best LTR baseline (RankFormer) by 4.7–9.7 percentage points across all cutoffs. On the MSLR-WEB10k → MQ2008 transfer task, LR²PPO reaches NDCG@1 of 0.6496 and NDCG@20 of 0.8240, surpassing the strongest baseline (GSF) by 4.9 and 3.5 points respectively. The full three-stage LR²PPO also improves over its own Stage-1 base model (LR²PPO-S1) by over 3% at every NDCG@k, confirming the benefit of the RL fine-tuning stages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LR²PPO wins decisively against all eight baselines on both evaluation settings. On LRMovieNet, the best LTR baseline (RankFormer) achieves NDCG@1 of 0.6350 and NDCG@10 of 0.6655, while LR²PPO reaches 0.6820 and 0.7628 respectively — a gap of 4.7 and 9.7 points. Open-vocabulary methods (CLIP, MKT) perform substantially worse, with MKT scoring only 0.3517 NDCG@1, because they rely on label confidence rather than relevance and handle only object-level labels. On the MSLR-WEB10k → MQ2008 transfer benchmark, LR²PPO (NDCG@1 = 0.6496) outperforms the strongest LTR baseline GSF (0.6004) by nearly 5 points, and beats SetRank (0.5299) and RankFormer (0.5684) by even larger margins. Notably, the Stage-1 supervised base model (LR²PPO-S1) already matches or slightly exceeds most LTR baselines, and the RL stages (2 and 3) add a further 3–10 point improvement, demonstrating that the partial-order reward signal is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both Learning to Rank (which ranks retrieved documents or recommendation lists) and classical label ranking (which learns order from positive/negative instance differences without semantic awareness) by introducing label *relevance* ranking over multimodal inputs. Methodologically, it adapts the PPO framework from RLHF (InstructGPT) to a ranking setting, but replaces the standard probability-ratio surrogate with a novel partial-order ratio that operates on pairwise score differences rather than action probabilities. The multimodal backbone (ViT + RoBERTa with cross-attention) follows the two-stream vision-language pretraining lineage (CLIP, ALBEF, BLIP-2), while the three-stage training paradigm (supervised base → reward model → RL fine-tuning) mirrors the RLHF pipeline but is instantiated for label ordering rather than text generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first dedicated benchmark (LRMovieNet) for multimodal label relevance ranking, filling a gap where existing LTR datasets target documents and label-ranking datasets are single-modality and semantically shallow. The finding that only ~10% partial-order annotation in the target domain yields near-optimal transfer performance is practically valuable for scenarios where full relevance labeling is infeasible. Open questions include whether the RL stage is strictly necessary over simpler pairwise margin losses at scale, how the method behaves with very large label vocabularies (beyond 20 per clip), and whether the partial-order ratio generalizes to listwise or setwise ranking formulations beyond pairs.
