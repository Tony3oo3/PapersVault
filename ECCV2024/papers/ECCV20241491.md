## Problem

Existing video editing tools rely on text prompts (e.g., Tune-A-Video) that are imprecise and introduce unintended style alterations, while directly extending image drag-editing methods (DragGAN, DragDiffusion) frame-by-frame to video causes severe spatio-temporal inconsistency. No prior work provides a unified framework for pixel-accurate, drag-style video editing that simultaneously achieves faithful manipulation, minimal artifacts, and temporal coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The user specifies handle-target point pairs and editable region masks only on the first and last frames of a video clip (up to 16 frames at AnimateDiff's capacity); these instructions are automatically propagated to all intermediate frames via PIPs point tracking and TAM mask tracking. The output is a full drag-edited video where the manipulated region follows the specified displacement while unmasked regions remain unchanged. End-to-end processing (including LoRA training) takes 5–10 minutes on a single RTX-4090 or RTX-A6000 GPU.

## Method

DragVideo operates in four stages: (1) a sample-specific LoRA is fine-tuned on the input video to anchor identity during denoising; (2) user-specified points and masks are propagated across all frames; (3) the core drag editing is performed by first applying DDIM Inversion to obtain a noisy video latent, then iteratively optimizing it with a video-level motion supervision loss (Eq. 3) that uses intermediate U-Net feature maps to pull a local patch around each handle point toward its target while a mask-regularized term constrains updates to the editable region, with embedded nearest-neighbor point tracking between iterations to update handle positions; (4) the edited noisy latent is denoised using a video-level Mutual Self-Attention mechanism that replaces the edited latent's keys and values with those from the original video latent, preserving identity and spatial structure. The key insight is that the video diffusion model's latent space retains pixel-level correspondence across frames, enabling a single video-level drag objective to enforce temporal consistency without per-frame optimization.

## Results

On a 30-video evaluation set, DragVideo achieves the best temporal consistency across all three metrics: CLIP Consistency of 0.9893 (vs. 0.9833 for DragDiff-Ext and 0.9829 for Tune-A-Video, with the original video at 0.9903), RAFT Optical Flow of 2.3780 (vs. 3.3807 and 2.7075, original at 2.3463), and FVD of 397.43 (vs. 400.89 and 2682.37). Paired t-tests confirm statistical significance (p < 5%) for all comparisons. User studies with 53 participants yield a 98% confidence interval of (7.47, 8.51) for editing effectiveness and (7.74, 8.96) for temporal consistency over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DragVideo is compared against two baselines: (1) DragDiff-Ext, which directly applies the image-level DragDiffusion [24] to every frame independently, and (2) Tune-A-Video [33], a prompt-based video editing method. DragVideo outperforms both on all three metrics. Against DragDiff-Ext, DragVideo reduces RAFT optical flow from 3.3807 to 2.3780 (a 30% reduction in jitter) and improves CLIP consistency from 0.9833 to 0.9893, while FVD is comparable (397.43 vs. 400.89). Against Tune-A-Video, DragVideo achieves substantially lower FVD (397.43 vs. 2682.37, a 6.7× improvement), indicating far less overall deviation from the original video, and also wins on both CLIP consistency and RAFT flow. Qualitatively, DragDiff-Ext exhibits visible temporal inconsistency and identity shift across frames, while Tune-A-Video introduces unintended style alterations and fails to achieve the precise spatial manipulation the user intends. All p-values from paired t-tests are below the 5% significance threshold, confirming the improvements are statistically significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

DragVideo extends the drag-style editing lineage from DragGAN (GAN-based) and DragDiffusion (diffusion-based, image-level) to the video domain by formulating a video-level motion supervision loss that operates on spatiotemporally correlated latents. It upgrades the Mutual Self-Attention mechanism from MASActrl (image) to video denoising and introduces sample-specific LoRA fine-tuning to preserve identity across frames. For point and mask propagation, it repurposes existing tracking tools (PIPs, TAM) rather than proposing new tracking methods, keeping the core contribution focused on the drag optimization and denoising pipeline. The paper explicitly positions itself against prompt-based video editing (Tune-A-Video, CoDeF, Rerender A Video), arguing that text-guided style changes cannot achieve the pixel-accurate, intent-faithful control that drag instructions provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end framework for interactive, pixel-level video editing via drag instructions, filling a clear gap between image drag editing and text-guided video editing. The practical limitation is significant: the pipeline requires ~5–10 minutes per 16-frame clip on a single high-end GPU, and the authors acknowledge residual blurriness and spatial inconsistency in a minority of outputs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos (AnimateDiff's 16-frame limit), whether it transfers cleanly to newer transformer-based video diffusion models (e.g., Sora-class), and whether the per-sample LoRA training can be amortized or replaced by a more efficient identity-preservation mechanism.
