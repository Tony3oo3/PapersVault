## Problem

Specialist OCR models (for scene text, handwriting, or formulas) cannot generalize across text-reading sub-tasks, while generalist MLLMs like GPT-4V handle multiple tasks but suffer from poor accuracy on fine-grained recognition and low inference speed. No existing model simultaneously achieves specialist-level precision and generalist-level versatility across diverse text forms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240925/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images resized to 1024 px (full scene/document images) or 768 px (cropped text/formula images) on the longer edge, conditioned by a set of prompts: a spatial prompt (point or quadrilateral box), a task-category embedding (scene, document, cropped text, cropped formula, or unspecified), a writing-type embedding (printed/handwritten/unspecified), and a granularity embedding (word/line/unspecified). Output is an autoregressively generated text sequence at the requested granularity, or a LaTeX string for formula inputs.

## Method

Platypus is a 118.8 M-parameter encoder–decoder that borrows the prompt-conditioning paradigm from SAM: a Swin-B + FPN image encoder extracts multi-scale visual features, a Prompt Encoder converts task category, writing type, granularity, and spatial location into learned embeddings (with an extra 'unspecified' slot for robustness), and a 6-layer, 8-head autoregressive Transformer decoder fuses these to generate text. The key design choice is unifying four recognition scenarios (RAT, PPR, BPR on full images; RAT on cropped images) under a single cross-entropy loss rather than separate task heads, so one model handles STS, STR, HTR, and MER. Training is two-phase: 1,000 k steps of full-image pre-training (with multi-prompt sampling per image, random granularity selection, and heavy augmentation) followed by 500 k steps of joint training that interleaves full-image and cropped-image batches, all on 8× A100 GPUs. This single-pass, prompt-driven design yields 3× faster inference than SPTS on STS while matching or exceeding specialist accuracy on every benchmark.

## Results

On Scene Text Spotting, Platypus with box prompt (BPR) achieves H-mean of 96.9 (IC13 word), 88.2 (IC15 word), 93.0 (TotalText word), and 91.9 (CTW1500 word), surpassing the best specialist (DeepSolo: 90.1, 79.1, 82.5, 64.2) by 6.8, 9.1, 10.5, and 27.7 points respectively. On Scene Text Recognition, Platypus reaches 96.4% average WAICS across six benchmarks, matching the best specialist PARSeq (96.4%) while outperforming GPT-4V (46.2% all / 76.7% valid). On Handwritten Text Recognition, Platypus sets new SOTA at 96.4% (IAM), 91.4% (CVL), and 93.6% (RIMES), and on Mathematical Expression Recognition achieves 7.2% CER and 7.8% WER on LaTeX-OCR, beating the specialist LaTeX-OCR model (8.7% CER, 9.6% WER).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On STS, Platypus with BPR outperforms the strongest specialist DeepSolo by 6.8–27.7 H-mean points across four benchmarks, and even in the no-localization RAT setting it matches or exceeds specialists on IC13 (92.7 vs. 90.1) and CTW1500 (80.9 vs. 64.2). On STR, Platypus achieves 96.4% average WAICS, tying the best specialist PARSeq (96.4%) and beating MGP-STR (93.3%) and ABINet (92.6%), while GPT-4V drops to 46.2% (all) or 76.7% (valid). On HTR, Platypus surpasses the previous SOTA DiG-ViT-Base by 8.2 points on IAM (96.4 vs. 87.0) and 1.2 points on RIMES (93.6 vs. 92.4 for SeqCLR), while GPT-4V scores only 40.4% on IAM. On MER, Platypus reduces CER from 8.7% to 7.2% and WER from 9.6% to 7.8% over the LaTeX-OCR specialist, whereas GPT-4V fails badly at 47.2% CER. On the challenging CAT benchmark with artistic/overlapped text, Platypus BPR reaches 89.8% H-mean, outperforming GPT-4V RAT (82.5%), PaddleOCR (51.4%), and EasyOCR (42.9%). In terms of efficiency, Platypus runs at 1.0 FPS on STS (vs. 0.3 for SPTS and GPT-4V), 11.9 FPS on STR (vs. 25.9 for PARSeq but 0.7 for GPT-4V), and 13.0 FPS on HTR (vs. 4.6 for DiG-ViT-Base).

## Relation to Prior Work

Platypus positions itself at the intersection of two existing research threads: specialist OCR models (scene text spotting, scene text recognition, handwritten text recognition, mathematical expression recognition) and generalist multimodal large language models. Architecturally, it borrows the prompt-encoding paradigm from SAM (Segment Anything) and adapts it to text reading, using a Swin-B + FPN image encoder and an autoregressive transformer decoder. Conceptually, it departs from the traditional two-stage detect-then-recognize pipeline and from the massive-parameter MLLM approach, instead proposing a compact (118.8M) unified encoder-decoder that handles all four text-reading sub-tasks through a shared architecture guided by task-specific prompts (category, writing type, granularity, point/box location).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Platypus offers a practical middle ground: a single lightweight model that matches or exceeds task-specific SOTA on STS, STR, HTR, and MER while running orders of magnitude faster than 7–8B MLLMs, making it deployable in latency-sensitive applications. The interactive prompt mechanism (point, box, granularity) adds a level of user control absent from both specialist pipelines and MLLMs. Key limitations include English-only evaluation, reliance on a curated but partially re-labeled training set (Worms), and the fact that the model still requires explicit task-category prompts at inference, limiting its true zero-shot generality. Open questions include multi-language extension, whether the prompt-based design scales to more diverse text forms (e.g., tables, charts), and how performance degrades on truly out-of-distribution artistic or degraded text beyond the CAT benchmark.
