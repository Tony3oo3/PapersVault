## Problem

Existing diffusion-based Real-ISR methods either fail to preserve pixel-wise image structures (e.g., ControlNet produces content-inconsistent outputs) or rely on skip connections that require extra image-space training, preventing the model from being applied to latent-space tasks like personalized stylization. The paper addresses this by enabling pixel-level structural perception within the latent diffusion framework without any image-space skip connections, unifying Real-ISR and stylization in a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240274/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-quality RGB image as primary input, along with auto-extracted high-level semantic features (ResNet classification, YOLO detection, BLIP captioning encoded via CLIP) and optional negative text prompts. It produces a high-quality RGB image for Real-ISR, or a stylized/restored image when the frozen base SD model is swapped with a personalized (DreamBooth/LoRA) or aesthetic variant. The adjustable noise schedule parameter α̅_a (set empirically to 0.1189) controls the perception-fidelity trade-off at inference time, and the model operates entirely in the SD VAE latent space with 20-step DDIM sampling.

## Method

PASD builds on a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model and introduces four trainable modules: (1) a Pixel-Aware Cross Attention (PACA) module that replaces ControlNet's zero-convolution with a cross-attention mechanism where the conditional feature (not passed through the VAE encoder, thus preserving original pixel structures) serves as the context, enabling precise pixel-level structural guidance; (2) a Degradation Removal pyramid network that extracts multi-scale degradation-insensitive features with L1 supervision to reduce the diffusion model's burden; (3) an Adjustable Noise Schedule (ANS) that blends the LQ latent into the initial noise at the terminal timestep with a tunable coefficient α̅_a, providing a continuous perception-fidelity knob; and (4) a high-level information module using frozen ResNet/YOLO/BLIP/CLIP networks to supply semantic conditioning. Training freezes all SD parameters and only optimizes the new modules with a standard ε-prediction diffusion loss plus the degradation removal L1 loss, while 50% of text prompts are randomly nulled to encourage the model to extract semantics from the LQ image itself.

## Results

On the DRealSR real-world test set, PASD achieves the best FID (14.20), DISTS (0.143), and QAlign (4.38) among all 8 methods, outperforming the next-best diffusion model SeeSR (FID 16.22, DISTS 0.178, QAlign 4.22). On RealSR, PASD leads in FID (47.34), DISTS (0.160), QAlign (4.13), and MUSIQ (65.60), while on DIV2K-valid it achieves the best QAlign (4.32) and DISTS (0.178). PASD trades a small PSNR/SSIM deficit (e.g., 29.09 dB vs. StableSR's 29.30 dB on DRealSR) for substantially better perceptual quality, and in user studies it receives the most rank-1 votes across all three tasks (Real-ISR, cartoonization, old-photo restoration).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among GAN-based baselines, PASD decisively outperforms RealASRGAN (EID 14.2 vs 23.2), FeMaSR (EID 14.2 vs 19.6), and SwinIR (EID 14.2 vs 24.6) on DRealSR, winning on every perceptual metric while sacrificing only ~0.2 dB PSNR versus SwinIR. Against diffusion-based baselines, PASD beats SeeSR (EID 14.2 vs 16.2, QAlign 4.38 vs 4.22), DiffBIR (EID 14.2 vs 16.8, DISTS 0.143 vs 0.211), and StableSR (EID 14.2 vs 17.7, QAlign 4.38 vs 4.24) on DRealSR, though StableSR retains a slight PSNR edgedge (29.30 vs 29.09 dB). On the cartoonization task, PASD achieves the best FID (37.67) and QAlign (3.998) on FFHQ, beating ControlNet (EID 37.96), SD Img2Img (EID 63.06), InstructPix2Pix (EID 39.33), and all GAN baselines (CartoonGAN EID 53.7, AnimeGAN EID 58.4, DCTNet EID 50.7). For old-photo restoration, PASD leads all seven baselines on every metric, with EID 240.3 versus DiffBIR's 262.2 and Wan et al.'s 268.3, and QAlign 3.98 versus DiffBIR's 3.92.

## Relation to Prior Work

PASD builds directly on the Stable Diffusion latent diffusion framework and the ControlNet architecture for conditional control, but departs from both in a key way: where ControlNet's zero-convolution connection fails at pixel-level structural fidelity and prior diffusion-based SR methods (StableSR, DiffBIR) resort to skip connections outside the U-Net that require image-space training, PASD introduces a pixel-aware cross-attention (PACA) module that operates entirely in latent space. The adjustable noise schedule extends the standard DDIM sampling by injecting LQ signal at the terminal timestep to mitigate train-test discrepancy, and the degradation removal module with pyramid supervision is borrowed from multi-scale restoration literature. The stylization capability is enabled by the SD ecosystem's model-swapping paradigm (DreamBooth, LoRA), which the paper repurposes for pixel-faithful stylized generation without pairwise training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PASD addresses a practical gap in diffusion-based image restoration: the inability of prior methods to simultaneously preserve pixel-level structure and generate perceptually realistic details, while remaining compatible with latent-space operations like stylization. The model-swapping approach for personalized stylization (cartoonization, old-photo restoration) is immediately deployable with existing community models, lowering the barrier for diverse style applications. However, the paper acknowledges that heavily degraded inputs or inaccurate semantic information can still degrade performance, and the fidelity-perception trade-off remains an open tension. A more robust degradation estimation module and finer-grained semantic extraction are identified as future directions.
