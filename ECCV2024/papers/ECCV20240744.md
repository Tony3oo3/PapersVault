## Problem

Unsupervised visual tracking remains challenging because existing methods (e.g., ULAST*, USOT) rely on siamese networks trained with pseudo-labels and struggle to exploit rich semantic and structural information in video frames. This paper asks whether the extensive visual knowledge embedded in pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models—understanding of object texture, shape, spatial layout, and even video contextual relationships—can be directly leveraged for unsupervised tracking without any annotated training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sequence at 512×512 resolution with only a bounding box annotation on the first frame as input, and produces a bounding box for the target in every subsequent frame. The bounding box is derived by extracting the smallest axis-aligned box enclosing the activated region on a 64×64 cross-attention map from the frozen diffusion UNet. During training, pseudo-labels are generated via an off-the-shelf optical flow model on unlabeled videos (GOT-10k, ImageNet VID, LaSOT, YouTube-VOS), and the GT cross-attention map is simply a binary mask over the bounding box area.

## Method

Diff-Tracker repurposes a frozen Stable Diffusion model as a tracker by learning a 1024-dim prompt embedding that activates the target region on the UNet's cross-attention maps. An initial prompt learner optimizes this prompt via MSE loss against a bounding-box GT map, augmented by an attention harmonization step that fuses the cross-attention map with the self-attention map (weighted by α=0.5) to encode target-background relationships. An online prompt updater then refines the prompt frame-by-frame: two ResNet18-based motion encoders extract long-term (T-frame window) and short-term (2-frame) motion, which are made target-conditioned via cross-attention with appearance features from the first-frame template, fused by an MLP, and blended with the previous prompt through a residual connection (β=0.7). The diffusion model's denoising loss is retained during prompt learning to constrain the prompt within the valid text-embedding space, and only the prompt and updater parameters are trained while all diffusion weights remain frozen.

## Results

Diff-Tracker achieves state-of-the-art unsupervised tracking across all five benchmarks. On VOT2016, it reaches EAO 0.430, Acc 0.605, and Rob 0.206; on VOT2018, EAO 0.365, Acc 0.580, Rob 0.273; on TrackingNet, Suc 0.675, Pre 0.614, NPre 0.751; on OTB2015, Suc 0.661, Pre 0.898; and on LaSOT, Suc 0.486, Pre 0.472. These results surpass the strongest prior unsupervised tracker ULAST*-on [46] on every metric of every dataset, and remain competitive with supervised methods such as DiMP and SiamRPN++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-Tracker outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across all five benchmarks. Against the strongest unsupervised competitor ULAST*-on [46], it gains +0.021 Suc and +0.022 Pre on TrackingNet, +0.013 EAO and +0.002 Acc on VOT2016, +0.010 EAO and +0.009 Acc on VOT2018, +0.013 Suc and +0.019 Pre on OTB2015, and +0.015 Suc and +0.021 Pre on LaSOT. The margin over USOT* [68] is even larger, e.g. +0.059 Suc on TrackingNet and +0.128 Suc on LaSOT. Among supervised trackers, Diff-Tracker's VOT2016 EAO (0.430) exceeds DaSiamRPN (0.411) and approaches DiMP (0.440), while its TrackingNet Suc (0.675) trails SiamRPN++ (0.733) and DiMP (0.740) but surpasses ATOM (0.703). On VOT2018, its EAO of 0.365 falls short of DiMP (0.440) and SiamRPN++ (0.414), indicating that supervised methods with full annotation still hold an advantage on the harder benchmark. Overall, Diff-Tracker establishes a new state-of-the-art among unsupervised trackers with consistent gains of 1–3% over the previous best across all evaluation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as a tracking backbone, drawing directly on the SLIME [27] observation that text prompts can activate semantically relevant regions on cross-attention maps within a diffusion UNet. It departs fundamentally from the dominant unsupervised tracking paradigm (UDT, S2SiamFC, USOT, ULAST*), which relies on Siamese CNN architectures trained with pseudo-labels from optical flow, by instead using a frozen diffusion model's attention maps as the localization signal and a learned prompt embedding as the target template. The online prompt updater with combined long- and short-term motion encoding is a novel architectural addition not present in prior diffusion-model-based vision tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a text-to-image diffusion model's internal attention structure can serve as a competitive unsupervised tracker, opening a path to leverage the rich semantic and structural priors in large generative models for perception tasks without task-specific training. However, the approach inherits significant practical limitations: it requires running a full Stable Diffusion UNet per frame (high compute cost), the initial prompt must be learned per target (not truly zero-shot), and training still depends on optical-flow pseudo-labels. Open questions include whether the method scales to longer sequences without drift, whether it extends to multi-object or category-agnostic tracking, and whether lighter-weight diffusion backbones could match the performance at lower cost.
