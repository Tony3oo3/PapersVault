## Problem

Video diffusion models suffer from poor temporal consistency and unnatural dynamics at inference. The paper identifies the root cause as an implicit training-inference gap: during training, the forward diffusion process fails to fully corrupt low-frequency spatio-temporal components of real videos, so the initial noise at t=1000 retains temporal correlations, whereas at inference the i.i.d. Gaussian noise is entirely uncorrelated. This distribution mismatch degrades generation quality because the denoising process is heavily influenced by the low-frequency content of the initial noise.

## Input / Output

FreeInit is a plug-and-play inference-time strategy applied to pretrained text-to-video diffusion models (AnimateDiff, ModelScope, VideoCrafter) without any additional training or learnable parameters. It takes the same text prompt as the base model and operates entirely in the autoencoder's latent space, where all frequency-domain operations (3D FFT, Gaussian Low Pass Filtering) are performed on the spatio-temporal latent tensor. The output is a generated video clip with improved temporal consistency, produced after multiple DDIM sampling passes rather than a single pass.

## Method

FreeInit iteratively refines the spatio-temporal low-frequency components of the initial noise to bridge the training-inference distribution gap. In each iteration, a Gaussian noise is denoised via DDIM sampling to obtain a clean latent z_0, which is then re-noised via the DDPM forward process using the *same* original noise ε (to avoid introducing mid-frequency uncertainty). The resulting noisy latent z_T is transformed to the frequency domain via 3D FFT, and its low-frequency components (selected by a Gaussian Low Pass Filter with stop frequency 0.25) are fused with the high-frequency components of a fresh random Gaussian noise η. This reinitialized noise serves as the starting point for the next DDIM pass. After 4 iterations, the initial noise has been progressively guided toward the correlated low-frequency distribution seen during training, yielding substantially improved temporal consistency and subject appearance without any model modification.

## Results

FreeInit improves temporal consistency (DINO score) by 2.92–8.62 points across all three base models on both UCF-101 and MSR-VTT, with the largest gain on AnimateDiff (85.24→92.01 on UCF-101). On motion quality, FVD drops substantially for every model (e.g., AnimateDiff: 1340.96→1032.47; VideoCrafter: 730.04→675.39), and Motion Smoothness scores move closer to the real-video reference (e.g., AnimateDiff |ΔUCF|: 7.33→0.04). Dynamic Degree also converges toward the UCF ground truth (e.g., AnimateDiff |ΔUCF|: 20.2→1.53), confirming that the gains are not due to over-smoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeInit is evaluated as a plug-and-play inference-time refinement on three text-to-video diffusion baselines: AnimateDiff, ModelScope, and VideoCrafter. On the headline DINO temporal-consistency metric, FreeInit wins on every model and every dataset: AnimateDiff improves from 85.24 to 92.01 (UCF-101) and 83.24 to 91.86 (MSR-VTT); ModelScope from 88.16 to 91.11 and 88.95 to 93.28; VideoCrafter from 85.62 to 89.27 and 84.68 to 88.72. On FVD, all three models see large reductions (AnimateDiff −308, ModelScope −83, VideoCrafter −55), confirming improved realism. Motion Smoothness gaps to the UCF reference shrink dramatically (AnimateDiff |ΔUCF| from 7.33 to 0.04), while Dynamic Degree gaps also narrow (AnimateDiff from 20.2 to 1.53), showing the method does not simply over-smooth. The paper further demonstrates that FreeInit with 2n DDIM steps outperforms vanilla DDIM with 5n steps, ruling out the explanation that gains come merely from extra sampling steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of diffusion model noise-schedule analysis and video generation inference. Prior work such as PYoCo [7] and [23] identified that standard diffusion schedules fail to fully corrupt signal, but addressed the issue at training time through rescheduled noise or fine-tuning. FreeInit departs from this lineage by targeting the inference-time initial noise distribution directly, using 3D frequency-domain filtering to iteratively refine the spatio-temporal low-frequency components without any parameter updates. The method is built on top of DDIM sampling [39] and demonstrated across three distinct video diffusion architectures (AnimateDiff, ModelScope, VideoCrafter), all initialized from Stable Diffusion, making it a model-agnostic inference strategy rather than an architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a training-free, plug-and-play inference trick that consistently improves temporal consistency across multiple video diffusion models, with the first iteration alone capturing most of the gain. The frequency-domain diagnosis of the training-inference gap (low-frequency SNR > 0 dB at t=1000) provides a mechanistic explanation for why vanilla Gaussian initialization underperforms, which could inform future training schedules. Key limitations include the increased inference cost from iterative DDIM passes and the need to tune the Gaussian low-pass filter cutoff per base model. Open questions include whether the gap persists in models trained with corrected noise schedules, whether the approach extends to longer video generation or video-to-video tasks, and whether the low-frequency dominance finding generalizes beyond the specific architectures tested.
