## Problem

Existing source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) methods for adverse-condition semantic segmentation learn condition-invariant features by directly matching adverse and normal image features, which causes catastrophic forgetting of source-domain knowledge and is highly sensitive to the imperfect alignment between GNSS-paired images. FREST addresses this by decoupling condition-specific information from semantic content and restoring adverse features to simulate normal conditions, rather than forcing the two to converge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240753/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of unlabeled images: an adverse-condition target image and a GNSS-matched normal-condition reference image, which are only roughly aligned due to camera pose and shooting-time differences. A UAWarpC dense-matching network warps the reference onto the target, and patch-level warping confidence scores (threshold 0.2) determine which patches participate in training. At inference, only the adverse-condition image is needed, and the output is a standard pixel-wise segmentation mask over 19 Cityscapes classes.

## Method

FREST alternates two training steps on a pre-trained SegFormer encoder. In Step 1, a lightweight condition strainer (adapter-style module, 2.1M params) attached to the frozen encoder is trained with a contrastive loss to learn a condition embedding space where images cluster by adverse condition type; a positive queue of adverse embeddings provides the representative positive sample. In Step 2, the segmentation network is trained with the strainer frozen, using an L1 regression loss that projects adverse encoder features onto the condition embedding space and pulls them toward the normal condition-infused features, plus an MLP-based discriminator that pushes adverse encoder features away from their condition-infused counterparts at every layer. This alternating scheme lets the strainer adapt to the evolving segmentation network while the network progressively restores adverse features to resemble normal-condition features, with no additional parameters required at inference.

## Results

FREST achieves state-of-the-art mIoU of 70.7% on Cityscapes→ACDC, improving over the previous best SFDA method CMA [1] by 1.6 percentage points, with particularly large gains on fine-grained vehicle classes (car: 89.1 vs 86.2, truck: 75.1 vs 68.6, bus: 79.4 vs 76.6). On Cityscapes→RobotCar, which covers 8 adverse conditions versus ACDC's 4, FREST reaches 58.8% mIoU, outperforming CMA by 4.5 points. In the UDA comparison on ACDC, FREST (source-free) surpasses all UDA methods that have access to labeled source data, including HRDA+MIC (70.4%) and HRDA (68.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FREST outperforms all SFDA baselines on both primary benchmarks: on Cityscapes→ACDC it beats CMA [1] (ICCV 2023) by 1.6 mIoU (70.7 vs 69.1), URMA+SimT [9] by 5.0, URMA [37] by 5.4, and HCL [15] by 9.9; on Cityscapes→RobotCar the margin over CMA widens to 4.5 mIoU (58.8 vs 54.3), suggesting FREST scales better with condition diversity. Notably, FREST as a source-free method surpasses all UDA methods on ACDC that have access to labeled source data, including HRDA+MIC [14] (70.4%) and HRDA [13] (68.0%). On the unseen ACG benchmark, FREST leads all competitors across fog, night, rain, and snow (52.6% all-conditions vs CMA 51.3%, URMA 44.4%). On Cityscapes-lindau40 (normal condition), FREST (72.5%) slightly trails the source-trained SegFormer (72.7%) but exceeds CMA (71.8%), confirming that feature restoration does not degrade normal-condition performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

FREST builds directly on the source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) setup for adverse conditions introduced by Brüggemann et al. (CMA, ICCV 2023), which pairs unlabeled adverse images with GNSS-matched normal reference images. The key conceptual departure is from CMA's contrastive approach of pulling adverse and normal features together (condition invariance), which risks catastrophic forgetting of source-domain knowledge; instead, FREST extracts condition-specific information via a lightweight adapter-style 'condition strainer' and uses it to guide one-directional feature restoration from adverse to normal. The strainer design draws on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (adapters) from NLP and vision, while the alignment pipeline reuses UAWarpC and the pseudo-labeling relies on class-balanced self-training (CBST), both established components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FREST demonstrates that a source-free method (no labeled source data during adaptation, only ~4% extra parameters) can match or exceed UDA methods that do have access to labeled source data, which is practically significant for privacy-constrained or data-scale-limited deployments. The generalization results on unseen datasets (ACG, Cityscapes-lindau40) suggest the learned condition embedding space captures transferable condition structure rather than dataset-specific artifacts. A stated limitation is that the framework does not yet cover image degradation or camera artifacts (e.g., motion blur, sensor noise), and the reliance on GNSS-matched pairs with imperfect warping remains a practical constraint. Open questions include whether the alternating strainer/segmentation training strategy generalizes to other backbone architectures beyond SegFormer and to conditions without clean normal references.
