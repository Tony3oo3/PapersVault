## Problem

Open-source text-to-image models can be maliciously adapted via fine-tuning methods (LoRA, DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) to generate unauthorized content such as artistic style mimicry or NSFW material. Existing defenses like Glaze and MIST rely on data poisoning, placing the protection burden on individual content creators rather than on model releasers. This paper asks whether the model itself can be immunized before release so that adaptation methods inherently fail to learn targeted malicious concepts.

## Input / Output

IMMA takes a pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion V1-4), a small set of ~20 images representative of the target malicious concept, and the specification of the adaptation method to defend against. It outputs modified model weights (specifically, updated cross-attention layer parameters) that can be released as a drop-in replacement. The method is applied once before release and does not require any runtime overhead or user-side intervention; the immunized model behaves identically for non-target concepts but resists adaptation to the specified concept.

## Method

IMMA formulates immunization as a bilevel optimization: the lower-level task performs the adaptation (minimizing the adaptation loss L_A w.r.t. adaptation parameters φ), while the upper-level task maximizes that same loss w.r.t. a subset S of model parameters θ (restricted to cross-attention layers), effectively learning a poor initialization that makes adaptation fail. The lower-level is approximated with a single gradient step per outer iteration (MAML-style), and two practical choices improve convergence: φ is warm-started from the previous outer iteration rather than re-initialized, and overlapping parameters between θ and φ are synchronized after each lower-level update. The algorithm iterates for I outer steps using Adam on the upper-level, producing immunized weights θ^I that resist the target adaptation while preserving the model's ability to personalize other concepts, as validated by the proposed RSGR metric.

## Results

For re-learning erased models via LoRA, IMMA achieves average SGR of 21.84% (LPIPS), 5.5% (CLIP), and 23.35% (DINO) across 8 artistic styles, and 21.84%, 11.41%, and 41.62% across 10 object classes. In classification accuracy, without IMMA the target concept accuracy surges from 1.3% to 45.5% after just 3 LoRA epochs, while with IMMA it remains at 2.2%. For personalized content adaptation, IMMA yields positive SGR across all three methods (TI: 9.05% CLIP / 36.55% DINO; DB: 2.27% / 11.97%; DB-LoRA: 6.62% / 27.74% average), with consistently positive RSGR confirming other concepts are preserved. On NSFW re-learning, IMMA reduces detected nudity by 80% relative to base SD V1-4, compared to 60% without IMMA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary baseline comparison is against MIST (data poisoning via adversarial examples) in the personalized content setting. Both MIST and IMMA successfully prevent the model from learning the target concept after Textual Inversion, and a user study confirms that on 7 out of 10 datasets the majority of 30 participants rated the non-poisoned/non-immunized generation as higher quality. However, under JPEG compression (a known attack on data poisoning), MIST fails to prevent target concept generation while IMMA remains robust, since IMMA can incorporate JPEG-compressed images into its training data. The paper also ablates against a direct-maximization baseline (upper-level task only), which degrades the model's ability to generate other concepts, and against removing Algorithm 1's warm-start (line 5) and overlap-update (line 7) steps, both of which allow the adaptation to successfully recover the target concept. Overall, IMMA wins on robustness to JPEG compression and on preserving other-concept adaptability (positive RSGR), while MIST is simpler to deploy but more fragile.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the MAML-style bilevel optimization framework: where MAML learns a good initialization for fast adaptation, IMMA learns a deliberately poor initialization to resist adaptation to malicious concepts. It departs from the dominant data-poisoning paradigm (Glaze, MIST, EUDP) that protects individual images by adding adversarial perturbations, instead shifting the protection burden from content creators to model releasers by modifying pre-trained weights before release. The approach also builds on the concept-erasing literature (ESD, Ablating Concepts) by addressing the vulnerability those methods leave open—namely, that erased concepts can be trivially re-learned via LoRA in just a few epochs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from data poisoning to model immunization is practically significant because it removes the enforcement burden from individual artists and places it on model distributors, who have more leverage over the release pipeline. However, the current formulation is limited to single-concept immunization and incurs a notable trade-off: protecting the target concept degrades other concepts by roughly 20% in classification accuracy. Open questions include whether multi-concept immunization is feasible without compounding degradation, whether restricting optimization to cross-attention layers is sufficient or whether broader parameter subsets are needed, and how the approach scales to larger models like SDXL or video generation.
