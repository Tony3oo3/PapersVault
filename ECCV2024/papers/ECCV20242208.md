## Problem

Gaze estimation models suffer significant cross-domain performance degradation because training with numerical regression labels causes overfitting to gaze-irrelevant factors (appearance, wearables, environment). Existing domain generalization techniques (adversarial training, data augmentation) offer limited improvement, and while vision-language models like CLIP provide rich semantic priors, their discrete contrastive alignment paradigm is ill-suited for the continuous regression nature of gaze estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242208/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224×3 RGB face image as visual input and a 3D gaze direction vector (yaw, pitch, roll) as the supervision signal. The gaze vector is not used directly as a regression target during the alignment phase; instead, it is converted into a continuous text prompt by interpolating among 91 learnable anchor embeddings (uniformly distributed over yaw ∈ [−180°, +179°] and pitch ∈ [−90°, +90°] at 30° steps) via spherical bilinear interpolation, then fed through a frozen CLIP text encoder. The final output is a 3D gaze vector predicted by an MLP regressor trained with an angular (arccosine) loss.

## Method

LG-Gaze reframes gaze estimation as a vision-language alignment problem: a ResNet-18 image encoder extracts visual features that are contrastively aligned with text features produced by a frozen CLIP text encoder processing learnable gaze prompts. The key novelty is the Multimodal Contrastive Regression (MCR) loss, which assigns adaptive weights to negative samples proportional to their label-space distance (cos(g_i, g_j)), preserving fine-grained ordinal structure that standard InfoNCE discards. For the image-to-text direction, 256 global negative text features—uniformly distributed on the gaze sphere via the Fibonacci algorithm—are concatenated with in-batch negatives to provide a controlled, distribution-representative contrast set, avoiding the staleness and randomness issues of MoCo-style queues. A geometry-aware spherical bilinear interpolation scheme computes each gaze embedding from four nearest anchor embeddings, and an auxiliary L1 loss constrains anchor-embedding pairwise cosines to match label-space cosines, ensuring the prompt space faithfully reflects gaze geometry.

## Results

LG-Gaze achieves an average angular error of 6.84° across four cross-domain gaze estimation tasks (ETH-XGaze→MPIIFaceGaze, ETH-XGaze→EyeDiap, Gaze360→MPIIFaceGaze, Gaze360→EyeDiap), outperforming the best domain generalization baseline CLIP-Gaze (6.97°) and the CNN baseline (8.57°). It attains the best result on three of four tasks (7.22°, 6.83°, 6.86°) and is second-best on ETH-XGaze→MPIIFaceGaze (6.45° vs. CLIP-Gaze's 6.41°). Notably, LG-Gaze matches or approaches UDA methods (e.g., UnReGA at 5.51°) without requiring any target-domain data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among domain generalization baselines, LG-Gaze (6.84° avg) outperforms CLIP-Gaze (6.97°), CDG (7.25°), Xu et al. (7.63°), and PureGaze (8.29°) on average, with the largest margin over PureGaze (~1.45°). Against UDA methods that require target-domain data, LG-Gaze trails UnReGA (5.51°), CRGA (5.88°), and RUDA (6.01°) but beats Liu et al. (6.94°) and is competitive with PnP-GA (6.38°) and LatentGaze (6.51°) without any target-domain access. In the VLM comparison (Table 2), LG-Gaze dramatically outperforms Vanilla CLIP (81.04°), CoOp (11.07°), and OrdinalCLIP (9.21°), confirming that continuous geometry-aware prompts are essential for regression. For contrastive loss ablations (Table 3), the proposed MCR loss (6.84°) edges out MoCo (6.96°), RNC (6.97°), CDG (7.19°), and KL-Loss (7.05°), with the advantage attributed to adaptive negative-sample weighting and global negative samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) domain generalization for gaze estimation (PureGaze, CDG, Xu et al., CLIP-Gaze), which relies on adversarial training or data augmentation as indirect regularization; (2) vision-language prompt tuning (CoOp, OrdinalCLIP), which handles discrete or ordinal labels but not continuous regression; and (3) contrastive learning for regression (RNC, MoCo), which lacks label-distance-aware weighting. LG-Gaze departs from prior DG methods by reframing gaze estimation as a vision-language alignment problem, and from prior VLM regression methods by introducing continuous, geometry-aware prompts (spherical + bilinear interpolation) and a contrastive loss (MCR) with adaptive negative-sample weights and global negatives, rather than treating gaze labels as discrete categories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of gaze estimation as a language-guided alignment task offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc regularization for domain generalization, and the MCR loss with geometry-aware interpolation is a reusable recipe for any continuous regression task where labels have geometric structure. Limitations include reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder (whose quality bounds the approach), evaluation on only four cross-domain pairs with a small ResNet-18 backbone, and the fact that the closest baseline (CLIP-Gaze) already uses a similar language-guided paradigm, making the incremental gain modest. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger vision backbones, whether the spherical interpolation generalizes to higher-dimensional continuous outputs, and whether the global-negative-sample strategy transfers to other regression domains.
