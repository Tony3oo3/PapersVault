## Problem

Existing test-time adversarial defenses are either computationally expensive (8–500× a single forward pass) or break under strong adaptive attacks, leaving no efficient defense that maintains robustness gains against worst-case threats. This paper identifies that successful adversarial attacks shift activations from neurons important to the ground-truth class toward neurons important to the target class, and asks whether restricting this shift via interpretability-guided masking can yield a training-free, low-overhead defense that survives adaptive attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240994/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-1k) and outputs a class label, operating on a pretrained adversarially-trained CNN (ResNet or WideResNet). The input is consumed in two forward passes: the first pass adds Gaussian noise (randomized smoothing) to produce a soft-pseudo-label, and the second pass applies a class-conditioned mask to the penultimate-layer activations before the final classification head. The mask is a soft, probability-weighted binary vector of length equal to the number of channels in the masked layer (e.g., 512 for ResNet18 layer4, 2048 for ResNet50 layer4).

## Method

IG-Defense is a training-free, test-time masking strategy that restricts the activation shift exploited by adversarial attacks. A one-time neuron importance ranking step (either LO-IR, which measures the average logit change when each neuron is masked, or CD-IR, which scores neurons by soft-WPMI similarity between their activation patterns and CLIP text embeddings of class names) produces a top-k binary mask per class. At inference, a dual forward pass is performed: the first pass computes a temperature-scaled softmax pseudo-label over Gaussian-noised inputs (randomized smoothing with n_s=1, σ_d=ε/2), and the second pass multiplies the penultimate-layer activations by the soft-pseudo-label-weighted mask m·ŷ, retaining only the k neurons most important to the predicted class. This soft masking preserves gradient flow between passes (avoiding gradient masking) and requires only 2× inference time. The key insight is that by zeroing out activations of neurons important to non-predicted classes, the defense blocks the activation redistribution that successful adversarial perturbations rely on, yielding consistent worst-case robustness gains (+1.5% average) without any retraining.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ε=8/255), IG-Defense achieves average IW-WC robust accuracy gains of +1.1% (CD-IR) and +1.28% (LO-IR) over base models, with AutoAttack gains of +2.5% and +2.55% respectively. On CIFAR-100, IW-WC gains are +1.13% (CD-IR) and +1.8% (LO-IR), and on ImageNet-1k (ε=4/255), gains are +0.6% (CD-IR) and +0.85% (LO-IR). The method incurs only 2× inference time (two forward passes with n_s=1 randomized smoothing), making it 4× faster than the strongest existing test-time defense (HD at 46×). Clean accuracy drops are marginal (≤0.3% for LO-IR on CIFAR-10, ≤1.5% for CD-IR on ImageNet-1k).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three state-of-the-art test-time defenses: CAAA (input purification, 8× inference), HD/Hedge Defense (input purification, 46× inference), and SODEF (model adaptation, 2× inference). IG-Defense (CD-IR or LO-IR) outperforms all three on IW-WC robust accuracy across all three base models tested. Specifically, on OAAT (RN18), IG-Defense achieves 52.35% IW-WC vs. CAAA's 51.00% and HD's 50.68%; on DAJAT (RN18), 53.36% vs. SODEF's 52.95% and CAAA's 52.45%; on TR-AWP (WRN), 57.46–57.88% vs. CAAA's 56.11% and SODEF's 56.05%. Notably, HD and SODEF actually reduce worst-case robustness below the base model on 2 out of 3 models, while CAAA matches the base model exactly. IG-Defense achieves these gains at 2× inference time, matching SODEF's speed but substantially outperforming it in robustness, and being 4× faster than CAAA and 23× faster than HD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes neuron-level interpretability methods (NetDissect, CLIP-Dissect) for a new purpose: test-time adversarial defense via activation masking. It departs from the two dominant paradigms in test-time defenses—input purification (CAAA, HD) and model adaptation (SODEF)—by instead restricting activation shifts at an intermediate layer using class-wise neuron importance rankings. The analysis builds on NetDissect's qualitative observation that adversarial attacks shift activations from GT-class neurons to target-class neurons, but extends it with quantitative average-activation-change metrics and an explicit defense mechanism. The randomized smoothing component borrows from certified-robustness literature to harden the pseudo-label step against adaptive attacks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in offering a training-free, 2×-overhead defense that consistently improves worst-case robustness (up to +1.76% on CIFAR10) while surviving adaptive attacks that break all prior test-time defenses—a gap identified by Croce et al. (2022). Limitations include a small but non-zero clean accuracy drop (especially with CD-IR on larger class sets), dependence on the pseudo-label quality, and evaluation restricted to CNN architectures on standard benchmarks. Open questions include whether the masking principle generalizes to transformer-based vision models, whether deeper or multi-layer masking could yield larger gains, and whether the importance ranking can be made fully label-free to avoid the probing-set requirement.
