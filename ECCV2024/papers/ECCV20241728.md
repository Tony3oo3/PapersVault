## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods are limited to single-person or two-person motions trained on small, studio-captured datasets with narrow prompt distributions. The fundamental bottleneck is the absence of large-scale, text-annotated multi-person motion data, making it impossible to generate diverse group motions from open-domain textual descriptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241728/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language text prompt as input and produces a multi-person motion sequence in SMPL parameter format (24 joint rotations in 6D representation, 11 shape parameters, and 3D global translation per subject per frame), supporting up to 10 subjects and 61 frames. The number of subjects is automatically inferred from the prompt via a Mistral language model. Generation is two-stage: a single-frame multi-person pose is first produced, then animated into a full motion sequence with that pose as the center frame.

## Method

The core architecture is a transformer-based diffusion model with interleaved pose and motion encoder layers. The key mechanism is a dimension-reshaping strategy: before each pose layer, the temporal axis is folded into the batch dimension so the layer attends over subjects (learning per-frame group interaction); before each motion layer, the subject axis is folded into the batch dimension so the layer attends over time (learning per-subject temporal dynamics). Training is two-stage: a text-to-pose model is first trained on single-frame data, then motion layers are inserted after each frozen pose layer and the joint model is trained with both text and a reference middle-frame pose as conditions. The model is jointly trained on five heterogeneous data sources (single/multi-subject, single/multi-frame) using a common SMPL representation, and at inference time leverages separate text-to-pose and text-to-single-person-motion models as additional guidance terms to improve interaction quality and motion realism respectively.

## Results

On the two-person motion generation benchmark (Table 2), the proposed method achieves P-R-Precision Top-1 of 0.323, P-FID of 0.435, and M-FID of 0.667, substantially outperforming all three baselines. Against the strongest baseline InterGen, the method improves P-R-Precision Top-1 by 4.4× (0.323 vs. 0.073), reduces P-FID by 58% (0.435 vs. 1.038), and reduces M-FID by 9% (0.667 vs. 0.734). The method also achieves the best P-Div (1.329) and P-MM (0.856) among all methods, indicating superior pose diversity and motion-matching quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three two-person baselines (InterGen, RIG, ComMDM) on every reported metric. On P-R-Precision Top-1, Ours(2) achieves 0.323 versus 0.073 (InterGen), 0.037 (RIG), and 0.043 (ComMDM) — a 4–9× improvement, reflecting far better text-pose alignment on open-domain prompts. On P-FID (lower is better), Ours(2) scores 0.435 compared to 1.038 (InterGen), 1.376 (RIG), and 1.160 (ComMDM), indicating generated poses are much closer to the real data distribution. On motion quality, Ours(2) achieves M-FID of 0.667, beating InterGen (0.734), RIG (0.925), and ComMDM (0.821), while also leading on M-Div (1.213) and M-MM (0.803). The large gap is attributed to the baselines being trained on narrow, studio-captured prompt distributions, whereas the proposed method leverages large-scale in-the-wild pose and motion data for broader text generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MDM (Tevet et al., ICLR 2023) transformer-diffusion framework for text-to-motion generation from single-person to multi-person settings by introducing interleaved pose and motion layers that reshape subject and temporal dimensions into the batch axis, drawing architectural inspiration from video generation (AnimateDiff). It departs from prior multi-person motion work (InterGen, ComMDM, RIG), which is confined to two subjects and studio-captured data with narrow prompt distributions, by instead constructing large-scale training data from in-the-wild images and videos via BEV and TRACE pose estimation. The two-stage training strategy—first learning a text-to-pose model, then inserting frozen motion layers—mirrors the spatial-to-temporal layer insertion pattern in video diffusion, but applied to the subject-to-frame axis of human motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of LAION-Pose (8M multi-person pose-text triplets) and WebVid-Motion (~3K multi-person motion-text tuples) addresses a fundamental data bottleneck that has kept multi-person motion generation limited to two subjects, and these datasets are likely to be reused by the community. However, the decomposed evaluation (scoring per-frame poses and per-subject motions separately) sidesteps the absence of ground-truth multi-person motion data, leaving open questions about physical plausibility, inter-subject collision avoidance, and whether the generated group dynamics are truly coherent rather than merely individually plausible. Scaling beyond 10 subjects, handling longer sequences, and reducing reliance on noisy monocular pose estimation remain significant open challenges.
