## Problem

Existing online centerline graph methods (e.g., STSU, TopoNet, MapTR) can predict lane segment positions and topological connectivity but neglect spatial continuity between connected segments. Even when the adjacency structure is correct, the resulting graph produces spatially discontinuous paths that are unusable for autonomous driving motion planning, since vehicles must follow smooth, continuous trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241931/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes 6 surrounding-view RGB images from onboard cameras and produces a complete centerline graph in BEV space without any post-processing: up to 50 centerline segments (each a 20-point polyline in a 0.3 m BEV grid over a 30 m × 60 m perception range) plus a T×T binary adjacency matrix encoding segment-to-segment connectivity. The Bezier Space Connection module is a training-only auxiliary head that is removed at inference, so the deployed model outputs only polylines and the adjacency matrix.

## Method

CGNet builds on the MapTR/DETR parallel-regression paradigm (ResNet-50 + FPN backbone, GKT BEV encoder, deformable-attention decoder) and adds three modules targeting continuity at different granularities. (1) Junction Aware Query Enhancement (JAQ) decodes a junction-point heatmap from BEV features and uses linear attention to inject junction positional priors into the positional part of each lane query, improving junction-point localization. (2) Bezier Space Connection (BSC) concatenates the embeddings of any two ground-truth-connected segments, projects them through the pseudo-inverse of the Bernstein matrix into a compact Bezier control-point space, and supervises the control points with L1 loss—this training-only constraint forces connected-segment embeddings to be spatially smooth and more similar than disconnected pairs. (3) Iterative Topology Refinement (ITR) stacks a parameter-shared GCN (consuming lane embeddings and the previous layer's adjacency matrix) with a GRU memory to iteratively refine the adjacency matrix across decoder layers. Training uses Hungarian matching for label assignment and a weighted sum of focal classification, L1 polyline, binary-cross-entropy topology, direction, Bezier, and junction-aware focal losses.

## Results

On nuScenes (24 epochs), CGNet achieves GEO F1 of 54.7, TOPO F1 of 42.2, JTOPO F1 of 34.1, APLS of 30.7, and SDA of 8.8, outperforming the second-best MapTR (53.3/39.7/32.4/26.8/7.6) by 4.4% in GEO F1, 6.4% in TOPO F1, and 7.8% in APLS. With extended training (110 epochs), CGNet further improves to GEO F1 63.9, TOPO F1 53.2, and APLS 41.4, maintaining a clear lead over MapTR (61.2/50.0/38.4) and TopoNet (59.9/49.6/37.4). On Argoverse2, CGNet achieves TOPO F1 44.5 and APLS 23.6, surpassing MapTR (42.8/22.3) and substantially outperforming TopoNet (30.2/15.3). CGNet runs at 11.2 FPS with 38.8M parameters, comparable to MapTR (12.7 FPS, 36.0M) and TopoNet (10.1 FPS, 40.3M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGNet outperforms all five baselines across every point-level metric on nuScenes. Against MapTR (the strongest baseline), CGNet gains +1.4 GEO F1, +2.5 TOPO F1, +1.7 JTOPO F1, +3.9 APLS, and +1.2 SDA at 24 epochs, with the gap widening to +2.7 GEO F1 and +3.0 APLS at 110 epochs. Against TopoNet, the advantage is larger: +3.9 GEO F1, +2.6 TOPO F1, and +4.5 APLS at 24 epochs, reflecting CGNet's superior junction-point accuracy (SDA 8.8 vs. 5.6). VectorMapNet and HDMapNet are substantially weaker, with VectorMapNet lagging by 6.3 GEO F1 and HDMapNet by 9.2 GEO F1, largely because neither was originally designed for topology modeling. On Argoverse2, CGNet leads MapTR by +1.7 TOPO F1 and +1.3 APLS, while the gap over TopoNet is much wider (+14.3 TOPO F1, +8.3 APLS), confirming generalization. On segment-level metrics (Table 3), CGNet also wins across the board: IoU 56.3 vs. MapTR 53.7, mAP_cf 35.2 vs. 33.1, and DET_l 22.0 vs. 18.9.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGNet builds directly on MapTR's end-to-end parallel regression framework for vectorized map construction and STSU's segmented centerline graph formulation, adopting the DETR-style query-and-Hungarian-matching paradigm. It departs from prior topology-focused methods (TopoNet, STSU series) by explicitly targeting spatial continuity of connected segments rather than treating them as independent entities, and from Bezier-based lane detectors (BezierLaneNet, BeMapNet) by projecting lane embeddings into Bezier space in a high-dimensional feature space rather than predicting control points in Cartesian coordinates. The Junction Aware Query Enhancement module adapts the positional-prior idea from DAB-DETR to the elongated-segment setting, while the Iterative Topology Refinement module extends TopoNet's graph-based connectivity prediction with a GRU memory for progressive refinement across decoder layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical gap in autonomous driving: even topologically correct centerline graphs can produce spatially discontinuous paths that are unusable for motion planning, and CGNet addresses this with a clean, modular solution that adds no inference cost for its Bezier constraint (training-only). The continuity-preserving framing is likely to influence how future online map reconstruction methods are evaluated, particularly through the point-level metrics (APLS, SDA) that capture spatial smoothness. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger perception ranges or more complex urban topologies, and whether the iterative refinement converges reliably in real-time deployment settings. The authors note the method could be extended toward production-grade online lane topology understanding.
