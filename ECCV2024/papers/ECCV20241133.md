## Problem

Generative model-based transfer attacks have shown superior adversarial transferability over iterative methods, but existing approaches that leverage vision-language models (e.g., GAMA) use a monolithic contrastive loss that conflates heterogeneous surrogate and CLIP features and rely on heuristic text prompts. No prior work has introduced prompt learning to enhance the transferability of generative adversarial attacks, leaving the full semantic guidance potential of CLIP's text encoder underexploited.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image from any domain (trained on ImageNet-1K, evaluated on CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, FGVC Aircraft) along with its ground-truth class label, and produces a single bounded adversarial image with an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of 10/255. Unlike iterative attacks, the generator produces one perturbation per forward pass, making inference computationally efficient. During training, the GT class label is fed through a prompt-augmented CLIP text encoder to extract text features that guide the contrastive loss.

## Method

PDCL-Attack is a three-phase pipeline. Phase 1 pre-trains M=16 learnable context word vectors for the CLIP text encoder via 16-shot cross-entropy on ImageNet-1K, yielding a Prompter(·) that produces more generalizable text features under distribution shift. Phase 2 trains a perturbation generator G_θ with two losses: a surrogate model loss (cosine similarity of VGG-16 Maxpool.3 mid-layer features between clean and adversarial images) and a novel prompt-driven contrastive loss L_PDCL that uses the CLIP image feature of the adversarial image as anchor, the least-similar text feature (from 16 randomly sampled class prompts) as positive, and the GT class label text feature as negative with a margin α=1. Phase 3 freezes G_θ for black-box inference on arbitrary domains and architectures. The key design choice over GAMA is separating the heterogeneous surrogate and CLIP feature spaces into distinct loss terms and leveraging adversarial CLIP image features to provide effective back-propagated gradients to the generator.

## Results

On the cross-domain black-box setting (trained on ImageNet-1K with VGG-16 surrogate, evaluated on CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars, and FGVC Aircraft), PDCL-Attack achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 43.91% (lower is better), outperforming GAMA (48.56%), BIA (51.07%), LTP (49.91%), CDA (69.94%), and GAP (79.30%). On the cross-model setting (ImageNet-1K domain, 8 victim architectures), PDCL-Attack achieves 38.60% average accuracy versus GAMA (40.91%) and BIA (42.93%). The method also demonstrates superior efficiency: at a lower perturbation budget of ℓ∞≤6, it achieves 72.07% cross-domain accuracy compared to GAMA's 75.23% and BIA's 78.76%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDCL-Attack outperforms all five baselines on both cross-domain and cross-model settings. On cross-domain (the most challenging black-box scenario), it reduces average top-1 accuracy to 43.91%, beating the closest competitor GAMA by 4.65 percentage points and BIA by 7.16 points. On cross-model, it achieves 38.60% versus GAMA's 40.91% and BIA's 42.93%. The gap over older methods (GAP, CDA) is even larger, with improvements of over 20 and 26 points respectively on cross-domain. Notably, PDCL-Attack also achieves the best per-model results on 7 out of 9 cross-domain victim models and 6 out of 8 cross-model victim models, with the largest margins on ResNet-50-based victims (e.g., 22.97% vs. 34.47% for GAMA on CUB Res-50). The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the improved CLIP-guidance loss alone (Ours†) already surpasses GAMA by 1.87 points, and adding prompt learning yields the full 4.65-point gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative model-based transfer attack lineage (GAP → CDA → LTP/BIA → GAMA) by integrating CLIP's vision-language contrastive space into the perturbation generator's training objective. Its closest predecessor, GAMA, was the first to use CLIP features for generative attacks, but PDCL-Attack departs by (1) separating heterogeneous surrogate and CLIP features in the contrastive loss, (2) anchoring text features to ground-truth class labels rather than heuristic prompts, and (3) introducing a learnable prompt context (following CoOp/CoCoOp) to produce more generalizable text representations. This makes it the first work to combine prompt learning with generative adversarial attacks for transferability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that text-driven semantic guidance from a frozen vision-language model, enhanced by a small set of learned context vectors, can meaningfully improve adversarial transferability across both unknown domains and architectures without per-instance optimization. However, the approach requires ground-truth class labels during training, incurs roughly 3× the training cost of prior generative attacks (~33 vs. 12 hours), and is evaluated only on image classification. Open questions include whether the prompt-driven contrastive framework extends to detection, segmentation, or other tasks, and whether the gains persist as surrogate and victim models grow in scale or diversity.
