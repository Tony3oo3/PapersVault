## Problem

Existing LMMs process images at fixed 1:1 aspect ratios and low resolutions (e.g., 224x224), causing shape distortion, blur, and degraded fine-grained capabilities such as OCR and small-object understanding. The paper further exposes that even GPT-4V's high-resolution slicing introduces systematic counting errors due to overlapping slices, and LLaVA-1.5's padding strategy wastes computation and creates adversarial vulnerabilities. No prior method efficiently and correctly encodes arbitrary aspect-ratio, high-resolution images for LMMs without padding, distortion, or quadratic cost blowup.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a native-resolution RGB image of any aspect ratio (up to 672x1008) plus a text prompt, and produces a free-form text response. The image is dynamically partitioned into variable-sized slices whose dimensions depend on the input resolution and aspect ratio; a low-resolution overview image in native aspect ratio is also encoded alongside the slices. Slice tokens are arranged in a grid using comma and newline separator tokens so the LLM can infer spatial layout, and the total token count scales as 144 times (N+1) where N is the number of slices.

## Method

LLaVA-UHD introduces three components built on LLaVA-1.5 (CLIP-ViT-L/14 + Vicuna-13B). First, an image modularization strategy factorizes the ideal slice count N into m-by-n partitions, selecting the factorization that minimizes log-aspect-ratio deviation from ViT's 336x336 pretraining setting, with N-1 and N+1 candidates to avoid extreme splits for prime N. Each slice is encoded at its native aspect ratio by 2D-interpolating ViT's position embeddings, avoiding padding or distortion. Second, a spatially constrained resampler compresses each slice's 576 visual tokens to 144 by ROI-Aligning into 12x12 spatial regions and applying cross-attention with one learnable query per region, preserving spatial structure while keeping token count fixed regardless of resolution. Third, a spatial schema organizes compressed slice tokens in a grid using comma and newline separator tokens. Training is two-stage: pretraining on CC-558K tunes only the resampler (about 4 hours on 8xA100), then instruction-tuning on a 665K mixture fine-tunes the resampler and LLM with ViT frozen (about 22 hours).

## Results

LLaVA-UHD achieves 80.7 on VQA-v2, 64.9 on GQA, 67.0 on TextVQA, 88.9 on POPE, 55.0 on VizWiz, 1532.3 on MME-P, 68.2 on MMBench-en, and 68.4 on SEED-I, while supporting 672×1008 resolution images in any aspect ratio (6× the resolution of its LLaVA-1.5 backbone). The most notable gains over LLaVA-1.5 are +5.7 on TextVQA and +3.0 on POPE, demonstrating the critical role of native high-resolution perception for fine-grained OCR and hallucination reduction. The model uses only 558K pretraining and 665K instruction-tuning samples, yet outperforms models trained with 2–3 orders of magnitude more data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-UHD outperforms its direct backbone LLaVA-1.5 on all 8 benchmarks, with the largest margins on TextVQA (+5.7, 67.0 vs 61.3) and POPE (+3.0, 88.9 vs 85.9), confirming that native high-resolution perception is the key driver. Against high-resolution competitors, LLaVA-UHD beats SPHINX-2k on TextVQA (67.0 vs 61.2) and POPE (88.9 vs 87.2) while using far less pretraining data (558K vs 1B); it surpasses OtterHD-8B on POPE (88.9 vs 86.0) and MMBench (68.2 vs 58.3); and it exceeds Monkey on VQA-v2 (80.7 vs 80.3), GQA (64.9 vs 60.7), and POPE (88.9 vs 67.6). Compared to models trained on substantially more data, LLaVA-UHD outperforms Qwen-VL (1.4B pretraining samples) on VQA-v2 (80.7 vs 78.8), GQA (64.9 vs 59.3), TextVQA (67.0 vs 63.8), and MMBench (68.2 vs 38.2), and beats mPLUG-Owl2 (400M pretraining) on GQA (64.9 vs 56.1), TextVQA (67.0 vs 58.2), and POPE (88.9 vs 86.2). The only benchmark where a baseline leads is VizWiz, where Monkey achieves 61.2 vs LLaVA-UHD's 55.0, though LLaVA-UHD still improves over LLaVA-1.5 (53.6). Overall, LLaVA-UHD achieves the best or near-best scores across all 8 benchmarks while supporting 6× higher resolution and arbitrary aspect ratios with minimal additional training cost (~22 hours on 8×A100s for instruction tuning).

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaVA-UHD builds directly on LLaVA-1.5, retaining its CLIP-ViT-L/14 encoder, Vicuna-13B LLM, and two-stage training pipeline, but replaces the fixed 336×336 padding-based encoding with a modularized strategy that splits images into variable-sized slices. It departs from fixed-crop approaches (SPHINX, Monkey) by using truly arbitrary aspect ratios, from enumerated-ratio methods (UReader, LLaVA-1.6) by avoiding any predefined shape set, and from encoder-free approaches (Fuyu-8B, OtterHD) by preserving the CLIP-ViT backbone. The spatially constrained resampler is a novel hybrid that borrows ROI-Align from Mask R-CNN and cross-attention queries from the perceiver resampler (Flamingo), while the pilot experiments on GPT-4V constitute the first mechanistic investigation of a proprietary LMM's visual encoding flaws.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic demonstration that GPT-4V's object-counting failures stem from overlapping slices, and that LLaVA-1.5's padding creates an exploitable adversarial vulnerability, reframes how the community should think about visual encoding design in LMMs. Practically, LLaVA-UHD shows that 6× resolution improvement (336×336 → 672×1008) with only 558K pretraining pairs yields meaningful gains on fine-grained tasks (TextVQA +5.7, POPE +3.0), suggesting that encoding strategy matters as much as data scale. Open questions include whether the independent slice encoding (no cross-slice interaction) limits global reasoning at higher resolutions, and whether the spatially constrained resampler's advantage over a plain MLP persists at larger pretraining data volumes where perceiver resamplers have shown their full potential.
