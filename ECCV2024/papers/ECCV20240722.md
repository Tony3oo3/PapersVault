## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for Vision Transformers rely on fixed logarithm bases (2 or √2) to handle the power-law distributions of post-Softmax and post-GELU activations, causing either excessive rounding errors for large values or severe truncation errors for small values at low bit-widths (≤4 bits). Moreover, the log-√2 quantizer introduces element-wise floating-point scaling that is not hardware-friendly, and the sparse grid search over scaling factors is prone to local optima.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained ViT model (ViT, DeiT, or Swin variants) and a small calibration set of 32 unlabeled ImageNet images to determine per-layer quantization hyperparameters. It produces a fully quantized ViT where weights use channel-wise integer quantization and activations use layer-wise quantization; the AdaLog quantizer specifically handles post-Softmax and post-GELU activations, while uniform quantizers handle the remaining layers. The resulting model performs inference entirely in integer arithmetic via table lookups and bit-shifts, with lookup tables of length 2^bit per layer adding negligible memory (e.g., ~3 KB for 12 layers at 4-bit).

## Method

AdaLog replaces the fixed logarithm base with an adaptively searched base b, approximating log₂(b) as a rational q/r so that de-quantization reduces to two table-lookup operations and one right-shift, preserving hardware friendliness. For post-GELU layers where activations are concentrated in (−0.17, 0], a bias reparameterization shifts the input by 0.17·1 to make it non-negative before applying AdaLog, and the bias is reparameterized as b_rep = b − 0.17·Ŵ·1_m to maintain consistency with the de-quantized weight. The Fast Progressive Combining Search (FPCS) strategy determines the optimal base (via q), scaling factors, and zero points by iteratively selecting top-k candidates and expanding them with finer partitions, achieving O(pn) complexity versus O(n²) for brute-force search while avoiding the local-optimum trap of alternating search.

## Results

On ImageNet, AdaLog achieves the highest top-1 accuracy across all seven ViT architectures at every bit-width tested (W3/A3, W4/A4, W6/A6). At the critical W4/A4 setting, AdaLog delivers an average 5.13% improvement over the second-best method (RepQ-ViT), e.g., 79.68% on ViT-B/224 vs. 68.48% for RepQ-ViT and 30.69% for PTQ4ViT. At the extreme W3/A3 setting, where competing methods collapse to 0.10% on most architectures, AdaLog retains meaningful accuracy (e.g., 37.91% on ViT-B/224, 69.75% on Swin-B/224). At W6/A6, AdaLog also leads or matches the best baseline, e.g., 84.80% on ViT-B/224 vs. 83.62% for RepQ-ViT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaLog consistently outperforms all three PTQ baselines (PTQ4ViT, APQ-ViT, RepQ-ViT) across every architecture and bit-width. Against RepQ-ViT (the strongest baseline), AdaLog gains 5.13% on average at W4/A4, with the largest margins on ViT-B/224 (+11.20%) and DeiT-T/224 (+6.09%). At W3/A3, the gap is dramatic: RepQ-ViT and PTQ4ViT collapse to 0.10% on most models, while AdaLog retains 13.88–69.75% depending on architecture. APQ-ViT does not report W3/A3 results and trails AdaLog by 4–12 points at W4/A4. PTQ4ViT is the weakest baseline overall, losing 10–49 points to AdaLog at W4/A4. The FPCS search strategy also outperforms the Alternating search (used by PTQ4ViT) by 3.15% on DeiT-T W3/A3 while being 45× faster than Brute Force search (4.1 min vs. 183 min GPU time).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the logarithm-based non-uniform quantizers introduced by FQ-ViT (log₂) and RepQ-ViT (log√₂), which both handle power-law activation distributions in ViTs but are locked to fixed bases. The key conceptual departure is making the logarithm base a searchable hyperparameter (AdaLog) and, crucially, showing that an arbitrary base can still be made hardware-friendly via rational approximation of log₂(b) and table-lookup dequantization—something the log√₂ quantizer could not achieve. The bias reparameterization for post-GELU layers and the FPCS search strategy are additional contributions that address gaps left by prior PTQ-for-ViT methods that largely ignored these layers or relied on sparse grid search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Low-bit (3–4 bit) ViT quantization is essential for deploying vision transformers on edge and mobile hardware, yet prior methods collapse to near-random accuracy at 3 bits; AdaLog's adaptive base selection recovers meaningful accuracy (e.g., 37.9% vs 0.1% for ViT-B at W3/A3), making the technique practically relevant for resource-constrained settings. The hardware-friendliness guarantee (integer-only inference with negligible lookup-table overhead) addresses a real deployment bottleneck that the log√₂ quantizer left unresolved. Open questions include whether the rational-approximation error of log₂(b) becomes problematic at extremely low bit-widths or for architectures with different activation statistics, and whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT-family backbones to other transformer variants or hybrid CNN-Transformer models.
