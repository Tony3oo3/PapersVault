## Problem

Good weight initialization is critical for stabilizing training, enabling higher learning rates, and accelerating convergence, yet selecting appropriate initialization schemes across different tasks remains challenging, time-consuming, and prone to human error. While HyperNetworks have recently shown promise for generating model parameters, no prior work has demonstrated a general-purpose weight generator that can synthesize effective initializations across diverse tasks and concepts, particularly for the large, heterogeneous weight spaces of GANs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242209/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The weight generator takes a text prompt describing a target concept/style (encoded via a frozen CLIP text encoder) and a block index identifying the position of a specific weight block among 854 total blocks, and outputs a 256-dimensional weight vector for that block. All 854 blocks are generated in a single batch (1.19 s), yielding 0.22M LoRA parameters that initialize a GAN generator. The GAN then undergoes a 20-epoch fine-tuning (42.1 s) on 400 paired 256×256 images to produce the final style-translated output, totaling 43.3 s per new concept.

## Method

The core contribution is a diffusion-based weight generator (a 1-D UNet with ResBlocks and Transformer blocks) trained to denoise LoRA weight vectors conditioned on both a CLIP text embedding and a sinusoidal positional encoding of the block index (added to the timestep embedding at each residual block). To make the problem tractable, the full 7.06M GAN generator weights are first reduced to 0.22M via LoRA, then flattened and partitioned into 854 equal-sized 1-D blocks of 256 weights, with per-layer grouping (LoRA down/up + BN) preserving statistical coherence. At inference, a single-step direct recovery at a noise-side timestep reconstructs all weight blocks without iterative denoising. The training dataset comprises ~85K+ concepts (generated via ChatGPT-3.5 and Vicuna augmentation) with paired images produced by multiple diffusion models (Stable Diffusion, Instruct-Pix2Pix, Null-Text Inversion, ControlNet, InstructDiffusion), on which GANs are trained to 100 epochs and multiple checkpoints saved as ground-truth LoRA weights.

## Results

On the FFHQ-based image-to-image translation benchmark (256×256, 1,000 images), the proposed method achieves a Clean FID of 89.93 while requiring only 43.3 seconds of total training time per new concept (1.19 s for weight-generator inference + 42.1 s for LoRA fine-tuning). This represents a 15× speedup over Pix2pix (659.8 s) and a 4.6× speedup over the efficient-training baseline E²GAN (198.5 s), while simultaneously delivering the best FID among all compared methods. The weight generator reduces the number of predicted parameters from 7.06 M to 0.22 M via LoRA, and a single-step denoising inference suffices to produce the initialization.

**Table 1: FID and time consumption comparison on FFHQ (256×256). FID is computed between GAN-generated images and diffusion-model reference images, averaged over all test concepts.**

| Method | FID ↓ | Time Consumption ↓ |
|---|---|---|
| Pix2pix-zero-distilled | 144.81 | 112 min |
| Pix2pix | 99.20 | 659.8 s |
| E²GAN | 93.86 | 198.5 s |
| **Ours** | **89.93** | **43.3 s** |

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on both FID and training time. Against Pix2pix (trained from scratch, 200 epochs on 800 samples), it achieves a 9.27-point FID improvement (89.93 vs. 99.20) while being 15× faster (43.3 s vs. 659.8 s). Against Pix2pix-zero-distilled (distilled Co-Mod-GAN, 112 min), the FID gap is even larger (89.93 vs. 144.81) with roughly a 152× time reduction. Against the closest competitor E²GAN (which also uses LoRA fine-tuning with 400 samples for 100 epochs), the proposed method improves FID by 3.93 points (89.93 vs. 93.86) and cuts training time by 4.6× (43.3 s vs. 198.5 s), thanks to the diffusion-based weight initialization providing a substantially better starting point that requires only 20 fine-tuning epochs instead of 100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the HyperNetwork paradigm (HyperDreamBooth, HyperDiffusion, Neural Network Diffusion) from generating weights for a single fixed task or architecture to generating GAN weight initializations across diverse image-editing concepts. It departs from prior weight-generation work by using a diffusion model with sinusoidal block-index conditioning rather than direct parameter prediction, and by applying LoRA to reduce the weight space from 7.06M to 0.22M parameters, making cross-concept generation tractable. The efficient-training baseline E2GAN shares the LoRA fine-tuning strategy but relies on a pre-trained base model rather than generated initializations, positioning this work as a complementary initialization mechanism rather than a replacement for the fine-tuning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15× speedup over Pix2pix and 4.6× over E2GAN while achieving better FID demonstrates that learned weight initializations can substantially reduce the cost of adapting generative models to new concepts, a practically important result for iterative design workflows. However, the approach is currently validated only on FFHQ at 256×256 with a single GAN architecture, and the training data pipeline depends on expensive diffusion-model image generation, raising open questions about scalability to higher resolutions, other architectures, and domains where paired data is harder to synthesize. The finding that a single-step denoising inference (1.19 s) suffices for useful initialization, and that combining GAN loss with the diffusion objective does not improve results, suggests the weight space is relatively smooth and well-captured by the diffusion model, but whether this holds at larger scale remains untested.
