## Problem

Existing video motion magnification methods amplify motion along all spatial axes simultaneously, which preserves the complexity of multi-directional vibrations and reduces legibility in applications where only a specific axis matters (e.g., axial vibration in rotating machinery fault diagnosis). No prior method allows users to selectively magnify the motion component along a user-specified direction while suppressing orthogonal motion, leaving a gap in axis-aware motion analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241437/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive video frames (384×384 RGB), a user-specified direction angle φ, and two independent axial magnification factors (α^φ and α^φ⊥) as input, and produces a single axially magnified output frame where only the displacement component along φ is amplified. Setting α^φ⊥ = 0 yields pure axial magnification, while setting α^φ = α^φ⊥ recovers generic motion magnification without retraining. Training data is synthesized via copy-paste compositing of COCO backgrounds and PASCAL VOC foregrounds with per-object translation parameters and magnification maps.

## Method

The core contribution is the Motion Separation Module (MSM), which replaces DMM's 2D-convolution Shape branch with weight-shared 1D convolutions whose kernels are spatially transposed to extract separate x-axis and y-axis shape representations. A projection layer P^φ, structured as a 2×2 rotation matrix [cos φ, sin φ; −sin φ, cos φ] motivated by steerable filters, rotates these canonical-axis representations into the user-specified φ and φ⊥ directions. The Manipulator then independently scales each axial representation by its corresponding magnification factor, and an inverse projection P^{−φ} maps the results back to the x-y frame for the Decoder. The network is trained end-to-end on a purpose-built 100k-sample synthetic dataset that encodes per-object axial magnification maps Λ and the angle φ, enabling the model to learn axis-disentangled motion representations that generalize to real video.

## Results

The paper evaluates using SSIM as the primary metric on both axial and generic motion magnification tasks. In the axial subpixel test (motion amounts 0.04–1.0 px), the proposed method consistently achieves higher SSIM than the modified phase-based baseline across all motion magnitudes. In the generic subpixel test, the method outperforms the phase-based, Singh et al., and Pan et al. baselines, while showing favorable performance relative to DMM and STB-VMM, exceeding the visually acceptable SSIM threshold. In noise tests, the method demonstrates growing SSIM advantage over the phase-based approach as noise increases, and maintains high noise tolerance in the generic setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the modified phase-based method (Wadhwa et al. [39] adapted for axial use), the proposed method wins on SSIM in the axial scenario across all input motion amounts (0.04–1.0 px) and all noise levels, with the gap widening as noise increases. In the generic motion magnification setting, the method outperforms the original phase-based method [39], Singh et al. [31], and Pan et al. [26] on SSIM in the subpixel test. Compared to DMM [24] and STB-VMM [17], the method achieves favorable SSIM performance that exceeds the visually acceptable threshold, though the paper does not report a clear large margin over these two strongest learning-based baselines. In the noise test for generic magnification, the method shows comparable noise tolerance to other methods overall but exhibits the highest SSIM retention as the noise factor increases. The ablation further confirms that the Motion Separation Module (MSM) contributes to the SSIM gains over a modified DMM baseline using 2D convolutions, particularly in the subpixel regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learning-based Eulerian motion magnification lineage initiated by DMM (Oh et al., ECCV 2018), reusing its overall Encoder–Texture/Shape–Manipulator–Decoder architecture, synthetic copy-paste data generation protocol, and loss function. The key conceptual departure is from all prior motion magnification methods—both traditional (phase-based, Riesz pyramid) and learning-based—which amplify motion indiscriminately across all spatial directions. By introducing the Motion Separation Module with weight-shared 1D convolutions and a rotation-matrix projection layer (inspired by steerable filters), the paper reframes the problem as axis-selective amplification, a capability absent from the existing literature. The synthetic training pipeline is further extended with per-object magnification maps and explicit angle conditioning to induce axis-wise disentanglement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: in machinery fault diagnosis and structural health monitoring, the vibration along a specific vulnerable axis is the diagnostic signal, while dominant orthogonal motions (e.g., rotation) act as interference that existing magnification methods amplify alongside it, degrading legibility. The paper demonstrates that axis-selective magnification yields artifact-free, interpretable results where both generic and modified phase-based methods produce ringing or entangled trajectories. Limitations include the assumption of fixed 2D viewpoints, reliance on synthetic-to-real generalization, and the absence of real-time inference; the authors identify moving viewpoints and real-time processing as natural next steps. Whether the axis-decomposition framework extends to 3D or multi-view settings remains open.
