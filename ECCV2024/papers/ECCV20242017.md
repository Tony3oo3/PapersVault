## Problem

Diffusion-based inverse problem solvers suffer significant performance degradation when the test data distribution differs from the training distribution (e.g., phantoms vs. real patient scans), producing artifacts and hallucinations. The prior adaptation method SCD addresses this but requires independent per-slice optimization for 3D volumes, incurring O(N) compute and memory and exceeding 6 hours for a single 256³ volume, while also lacking a clear theoretical explanation for why it works.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242017/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded 3D measurement Y (sparse-view CT projections at 60 angles, variable-density undersampled MRI k-space at ×8, or multi-coil compressed-sensing MRI at ×4) from an unknown OOD distribution, together with a pretrained 2D VP diffusion model (trained on ELLIPSES phantoms or fastMRI BRAIN) and LoRA adaptation parameters. It produces a reconstructed 3D volume X ∈ R^{n×N} with N slices (hundreds for CT/3D-MRI, ~30 for multi-coil MRI), spatially coherent across all three dimensions, using only 50 NFE DDIM sampling steps with η=0.85.

## Method

The paper establishes that SCD is formally a multi-scale deep image prior (DIP) constructed along the probability-flow ODE path, revealing that SCD's adaptation and sampling steps use inconsistent posterior mean estimates (1 vs. K CG iterations); correcting this yields DDIP. For 3D volumes, D3IP replaces per-slice independent adaptation with a single shared LoRA parameter set optimized via Monte Carlo sampling of K neighboring slices per diffusion step, reducing compute and memory from O(N) to O(1) while improving quality by leveraging cross-slice statistics. D3IP(mbir) further integrates a 3D solver (DiffusionMBIR with inter-slice TV regularization solved via ADMM) for spatially coherent reconstruction, and D3IP(meta) applies Reptile-style meta-learning to learn a volume-level meta-parameter before per-slice fine-tuning. Technical advances include constraining adaptation to t ∈ [ζ, T−ζ] to avoid unstable score estimates and initializing with slerp-interpolated noise plus the measurement pseudo-inverse to exploit non-zero terminal SNR.

## Results

On 3D SV-CT (ELLIPSES→AAPM), D3IP(mbir) achieves the best PSNR of 33.69 dB and SSIM of 0.919, while D3IP(meta) reaches 33.96 dB PSNR. On 3D MRI (ELLIPSES→BRATS), D3IP(mbir) achieves 33.89 dB PSNR and 0.907 SSIM, a >3 dB improvement over SCD (26.00 dB). On multi-coil CS-MRI (BRAIN→KNEE), D3IP(meta) achieves 29.52 dB PSNR and 0.216 LPIPS. Crucially, D3IP reduces compute from O(KNT) to O(KT) and memory from O(N) to O(1), cutting reconstruction time for a 256³ volume from ~6.2 hours (SCD) to ~40 minutes (D3IP base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SCD [3] (the prior adaptation method), D3IP(base) already surpasses it on 3D MRI by >3 dB PSNR (30.59 vs. 26.00) and on LPIPS across all three tasks, while being ~9× faster (40 min vs. 6.2 h for a 256³ volume). D3IP(mbir) further extends the lead to 33.89 dB on 3D MRI and 33.69 dB on 3D SV-CT. Against non-adapted DIS baselines, the gap is even larger: DDS [7] achieves only 24.59 dB on 3D MRI versus 33.89 dB for D3IP(mbir), and DPS [6] drops to 17.85 dB on 3D SV-CT. DiffusionMBIR [8] (the only 3D-aware non-adapted solver) reaches 28.92 dB on 3D SV-CT, still ~5 dB below D3IP(mbir). D3IP(meta) achieves the best PSNR on 3D SV-CT (33.96 dB) and CS-MRI (29.52 dB) by trading additional compute for per-slice fine-tuning, outperforming DDIP on CS-MRI (29.52 vs. 29.11 dB) while matching it on 3D SV-CT. The only cases where D3IP(base) trails DDIP are PSNR on 3D SV-CT (31.73 vs. 33.13) and CS-MRI (29.00 vs. 29.11), but D3IP(mbir) and D3IP(meta) recover and exceed DDIP on both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formally unifies two previously separate threads: the Steerable Conditional Diffusion (SCD) adaptation framework and the Deep Image Prior (DIP) paradigm, showing that SCD is in fact a multi-scale DIP constructed along the probability-flow ODE trajectory. Building on this reframing, the authors generalize SCD's per-slice, O(N) adaptation to a shared-parameter 3D formulation (D3IP) that reduces compute and memory to O(1) via Monte Carlo sampling over slices, and further integrate 3D-aware solvers (DiffusionMBIR) and Reptile-style meta-learning for volume-level initialization followed by slice-level fine-tuning. The core diffusion inverse solvers (DDS, DDIM) and the LoRA-based parameterization are inherited directly from SCD and prior DIS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing OOD adaptation inference from over 6 hours to ~40 minutes for a 256³ volume makes diffusion-based inverse solvers viable in settings where gold-standard training data is unavailable (e.g., black hole imaging, cryo-EM, rare-pathology CT). The formal DIP connection also clarifies *why* SCD works, enabling principled improvements (e.g., matching CG iterations between adaptation and sampling steps). Open questions include whether the shared-parameter assumption degrades for highly heterogeneous volumes, whether the approach extends to non-medical 3D imaging (e.g., volumetric ultrasound, tomographic electron microscopy), and whether the meta-learning variant can be made as efficient as the base D3IP without sacrificing the per-slice fine-tuning benefit.
