## Problem

Existing omni-directional image synthesis methods face a trade-off between training cost and output quality: GAN-based approaches suffer from training instability, while VQGAN-based methods like OmniDreamer require 14 days of training because the VQGAN encoder and decoder must be fine-tuned to represent geometric distortions in equirectangular projection (ERP), particularly at the poles. This paper addresses how to synthesize high-quality omni-directional images without the expensive VQGAN fine-tuning step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240772/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditional image in ERP format—typically a single NFoV image (126.87° × 112.62° FoV) embedded at the center, or a masked omni-directional image for inpainting/outpainting tasks—and produces a full 360° omni-directional image in ERP at 1024×2048 pixels (evaluated at 512×1024). The second stage internally generates 26 overlapping NFoV images of 256×256 pixels each, oriented along the face normals of a rhombicuboctahedron with 60° FoV, which are then blended into the final ERP output.

## Method

2S-ODIS uses a pre-trained VQGAN (1024-codebook, trained on ImageNet) entirely frozen, eliminating the fine-tuning step that dominates prior training time. A two-stage architecture built on 8-layer MaxViT models with MaskGIT-style iterative unmasking (16 steps, cosine mask schedule) generates VQGAN codes: the first stage produces a low-resolution 16×32 ERP code grid (256×512 image) with circular padding for edge continuity, while the second stage produces high-resolution 16×16 code grids for each of 26 NFoV directions, conditioned on the first-stage image. The key insight is that the pre-trained VQGAN cannot represent ERP geometric distortions (e.g., straight lines at poles), but it correctly reconstructs NFoV images; therefore, the second stage synthesizes in NFoV space and corrects distortions by integrating the 26 images via distance-weighted blending. Both stages are trained independently with cross-entropy loss on VQGAN code prediction, reducing total training to 4 days versus 14 days for OmniDreamer, and inference to 1.54 s versus 39.33 s per image.

## Results

On SUN360, 2S-ODIS achieves IS 5.969, FID 18.263, and LPIPS 0.662, outperforming all baselines on every metric. Training takes only 4 days (2 days per stage) compared to 14 days for OmniDreamer, and inference is 1.54 s per image versus 39.33 s for OmniDreamer. Even with only 2 days of training (1 day per stage), the method still surpasses all baselines (IS 5.857, FID 18.656, LPIPS 0.668).

## Baselines & Comparisons

2S-ODIS outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric (IS, FID, LPIPS). Against the closest competitor, OmniDreamer [2], it improves IS from 4.458 to 5.969 (+34%), FID from 23.101 to 18.263 (−21%), and LPIPS from 0.655 to 0.662, while reducing training time from 14 days to 4 days and inference time from 39.33 s to 1.54 s per image. The GAN-based baselines (CNN cGAN [10] and MLP-Mixer cGAN [13]) are substantially outperformed, with FID gaps of over 20 and 29 points respectively, reflecting the instability and lower quality typical of GAN training. LAMA [15], a general-purpose inpainting method, achieves a competitive IS (5.784) but a much worse FID (69.485) and LPIPS (0.478), indicating it produces less realistic and less perceptually similar omni-directional images. The paper also shows that 2S-ODIS generalizes to inpainting, ground-region filling, and two-image outpainting tasks where OmniDreamer fails, further widening the performance gap in those settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MaskGIT iterative unmasking strategy for parallel VQGAN code prediction, the Muse two-stage low-to-high resolution generation pipeline, and the MaxViT multi-axis attention architecture. Its key departure from prior VQGAN-based omni-directional synthesis (notably OmniDreamer) is the elimination of VQGAN fine-tuning on ERP images: rather than encoding geometric distortions into the latent space, the method synthesizes multiple undistorted NFoV images with a pre-trained ImageNet VQGAN and integrates them via geometric distortion correction. This reframes the distortion problem from a representation challenge (fix the encoder/decoder) to an integration challenge (fix the compositing), a conceptual shift not present in earlier omni-directional GAN or VQGAN approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.5× reduction in training time (14→4 days) and ~25× speedup in inference (39.3→1.5 s) make omni-directional image synthesis substantially more accessible, particularly for practitioners without multi-GPU clusters. The broader implication is that pre-trained generative models for one image domain can be repurposed for a geometrically distorted domain without domain adaptation, provided the generation is decomposed into undistorted sub-regions. Open questions include whether the fixed 26-direction rhombicuboctahedron sampling and 60° FoV are optimal, whether discontinuities at NFoV boundaries can be resolved with a lightweight refinement network, and whether the architecture extends naturally to text- or class-conditioned omni-directional generation.
