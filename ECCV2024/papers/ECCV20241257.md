## Problem

Existing cross-view synthesis methods generate ground views from satellite imagery by hallucinating plausible scenes from semantics or approximate geometry, sacrificing the actual texture and structural details visible in satellite data. No prior approach produces ground views that are simultaneously photorealistic and geospecific—faithfully reflecting the true appearance at a given geolocation—while bridging the ~10× resolution gap between satellite (0.3 m/px) and street-level (~3 cm/px) imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view satellite images (0.3 m/pixel) and a geospecific text prompt (e.g., 'High resolution street view in HongKong') as input, and produces a high-resolution ground-view panorama image at a 10×+ resolution boost. The satellite images are first processed via stereo matching into 3D geometry and then projected to ground level using RPC camera model, yielding a low-resolution ground-view satellite texture and an edge map that serve as conditioning signals. The geospecific prompt is encoded as a text vector that modulates the diffusion model's cross-attention to inject regional visual style (vegetation types, facade features, lighting).

## Method

The pipeline projects satellite textures to ground level via a 2D-3D-2D RPC projection (rather than the orthographic approximation used by prior work), preceded by a texture-friendly geometry refinement that uses a SegFormer U-Net to extract building footprints, regularizes boundaries into polygons, and flattens non-building regions via plane fitting to minimize facade texture distortions. For generation, a Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent diffusion model is conditioned on two ControlNet-style encoders—one on the ground-view satellite RGB texture and one on a U-Net-extracted building facade edge map—replacing the semantics-only conditioning of prior methods. A geospecific prior is injected via LoRA low-rank matrices in the cross-attention module, trained on ~500 street-view images per city to bias the diffusion distribution toward region-specific visual characteristics. Only the ControlNet encoders and LoRA parameters are fine-tuned, keeping the base diffusion weights frozen.

## Results

On the DFC 2019 dataset (Jacksonville, FL), the proposed method achieves the best performance on 7 out of 9 metrics: edge IoU (I_E = 0.089), building IoU (I_B = 0.570), ground IoU (I_G = 0.864), sky IoU (I_S = 0.874), LPIPS (0.449), FID (71.04), and DreamSIM (0.315). The method trails only on pixel-level metrics PSNR (19.95 vs. 21.02 for Sat2Ground) and SSIM (0.397 vs. 0.407 for CrossMLP), which the authors attribute to the inherent stochasticity of diffusion-based generation. The FID improvement over the strongest baseline (PanoGAN, 98.81) is 28.2%, and the ground IoU improvement over PanoGAN (0.457) is 89.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every structural and perceptual metric. Against PanoGAN (the strongest baseline overall), it improves FID by 28.2% (71.04 vs. 98.81), DreamSIM by 9.2% (0.315 vs. 0.348), LPIPS by 8.0% (0.449 vs. 0.488), building IoU by 51.3% (0.570 vs. 0.376), and ground IoU by 89.5% (0.864 vs. 0.457). Against Sat2Ground, the geometry-guided method with the highest PSNR (21.02), the proposed method still wins on all semantic and perceptual metrics, with FID improved by 55.7% (71.04 vs. 160.6) and ground IoU more than doubled (0.864 vs. 0.324). CrossMLP achieves the best SSIM (0.407) but the worst ground IoU (0.214) and FID (170.8) among the four baselines, while Sat2Density is consistently the weakest across all metrics. The only metrics where the proposed method does not lead are PSNR and SSIM, which measure pixel-level fidelity and are less informative for evaluating the structural and perceptual quality of diffusion-generated images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-3D-2D projection pipeline from Sat2Ground [22] and Sat2Vid [19] by replacing the approximate orthographic projection with a mathematically accurate RPC camera model and adding a texture-friendly geometry refinement step to reduce facade distortions. It departs from the dominant paradigm of conditioning on projected semantics (used by Sat2Ground, CrossMLP, PanoGAN) by instead conditioning a latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion + ControlNet) on the actual projected satellite RGB texture and an extracted edge map. The geospecific prior, implemented as LoRA adapters in the cross-attention layers, is a novel use of low-rank adaptation to inject region-specific visual style into a pre-trained diffusion model, building on the LoRA mechanism from [11] and the ControlNet conditioning framework from [46].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper is the first to frame cross-view synthesis as a geolocation-fidelity problem rather than a plausibility problem, and demonstrates that texture conditioning consistently outperforms semantics across multiple generator architectures (Table 3), challenging the field's default assumption that semantic labels are the appropriate conditioning signal. The geospecific prior via LoRA is a lightweight and practical mechanism for injecting regional visual style, though its generalizability beyond the five tested cities is unverified. A key open limitation is the lack of view consistency across neighboring ground views due to diffusion model randomness, which would be critical for applications like city-scale scene synthesis or autonomous driving simulation. The single-dataset evaluation (DFC 2019, Jacksonville) also leaves open questions about performance in denser or more architecturally diverse urban environments.
