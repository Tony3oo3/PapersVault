## Problem

Existing Concept Bottleneck Models require pre-selecting task-relevant concepts (often via LLM queries) and then training a bottleneck to detect them, with no guarantee the feature extractor can actually recognize the specified concepts. This paper inverts the paradigm: instead of naming concepts first and hoping the model can detect them, it first discovers what concepts the model already knows and then names them, yielding a task-agnostic bottleneck that can be frozen and reused across downstream classification tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242072/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images encoded by a frozen CLIP vision backbone (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, or ViT-L/14) and produces both a class label and a sparse set of named concepts with activation strengths. The SAE is trained once on CLIP features from CC3M (no labels needed), and the resulting concept bottleneck is frozen and shared across all downstream datasets (ImageNet, Places365, CIFAR-10/100). Concept naming uses a fixed vocabulary of the 20k most frequent English words embedded via the CLIP text encoder, so the output concepts are single-word labels with cosine-similarity-based confidence.

## Method

The core pipeline has three stages: (1) a sparse autoencoder (SAE) is trained on CLIP image features from CC3M with L2 reconstruction loss plus L1 sparsity, mapping the d-dimensional CLIP space to a much higher-dimensional (h >> d) sparse latent space where individual dimensions correspond to disentangled concepts; (2) each SAE neuron is automatically named by computing the cosine similarity between its decoder dictionary vector (a column of W_D) and all text embeddings in the vocabulary, assigning the closest word as the concept name; (3) the frozen SAE serves as the concept bottleneck layer, and a linear probe with an additional L1 sparsity penalty is trained on top for classification on each downstream dataset. The key insight is that SAE dictionary vectors in CLIP space naturally align with text embeddings of the concepts they represent, enabling naming without any LLM queries, and that this task-agnostic discovery yields concepts that are both semantically consistent and sufficient for competitive classification accuracy.

## Results

DN-CBM achieves 72.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet and 53.5% on Places365 with CLIP ResNet-50, and 79.5% and 55.1% respectively with CLIP ViT-B/16, while remaining task-agnostic (a single concept bottleneck trained on CC3M is reused across all datasets). These numbers are within 0.3–2.8 pp of the uninterpretable linear-probe upper bound (e.g., 72.9 vs. 73.3 on ImageNet/ResNet-50) and consistently outperform all label-free CBM baselines. The largest gains over the strongest baseline (CDM) appear on Places365: +0.8 pp (ResNet-50) and +2.5 pp (ViT-B/16). On the Waterbirds-100 intervention task, keeping only bird-related concepts improves worst-group accuracy by +15.3 pp (Landbird@Water) and +13.8 pp (Waterbird@Land).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LF-CBM [36], DN-CBM wins on every reported dataset and backbone, with the largest margin on Places365 (+4.5 pp ResNet-50, +4.5 pp ViT-B/16) and ImageNet (+5.4 pp ResNet-50, +4.1 pp ViT-B/16). Against CDM [41], the closest competitor, DN-CBM edges ahead on ImageNet (+0.7 pp R50, +0.2 pp ViT), Places365 (+0.8 pp R50, +2.5 pp ViT), and CIFAR-10 (+1.1 pp R50, +0.7 pp ViT), but is essentially tied on CIFAR-100 (−0.1 pp R50, +1.6 pp ViT). Against LaBo [58], DN-CBM surpasses it on ImageNet (+4.0 pp R50, +0.6 pp ViT) and CIFAR-100 (−1.6 pp R50, +0.9 pp ViT), though LaBo is not evaluated on Places365. DCLIP [34] is substantially weaker (59.6% on ImageNet R50) and is not evaluated on CIFAR datasets. In the user-study comparison against CLIP-Dissect [37] as a concept-naming baseline, SAE-discovered concepts score significantly higher on both semantic consistency and name accuracy, confirming that the SAE decomposition yields more interpretable units than raw CLIP neurons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse autoencoder (SAE) framework from mechanistic interpretability in language models (Bricken et al., Cunningham et al.) to vision, applying it to CLIP feature extractors to disentangle representations into sparse, interpretable concepts. It departs from prior Concept Bottleneck Models (LF-CBM, LaBo, CDM, DCLIP) by inverting the paradigm: rather than pre-selecting task-specific concepts (often via LLM queries) and training a bottleneck to detect them, DN-CBM first discovers what concepts the model already encodes, then names them by matching SAE dictionary vectors to CLIP text embeddings. This contrasts with prior concept-discovery methods like CLIP-Dissect, which assign concepts to individual (often polysemantic) neurons, by first decomposing the representation space into monosemantic features before naming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The task-agnostic nature of the approach eliminates per-task LLM queries and separate bottleneck training, making it more scalable and computationally efficient for deploying interpretable classifiers across many downstream tasks. A key open question is whether the naming accuracy and concept coverage improve substantially with a larger vocabulary or a CLIP-scale SAE trained on far more data, as the current 20k-word vocabulary and CC3M training set limit granularity. The faithfulness of CBM explanations in general remains contested in the literature, and while DN-CBM's concepts are grounded in what the model actually encodes (addressing one faithfulness concern), the modest performance gains over task-specific baselines (1–2.5 pp) suggest the interpretability–accuracy tradeoff is not yet fully resolved.
