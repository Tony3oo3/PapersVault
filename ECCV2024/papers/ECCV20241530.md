## Problem

Large multimodal models are trained to follow instructions with high compliance, yet they lack the meta-awareness to detect self-contradictory instructions that arise naturally as context windows expand to 1M+ tokens and multimodal interactions proliferate. No systematic benchmark existed to evaluate whether LMMs can recognize instruction discordance, and prior work on information inconsistency focused on knowledge conflicts or hallucination rather than contradictions within the user's own input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241530/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The SCI benchmark presents 20,000 self-contradictory instructions in two paradigms: language-language (rule violations, attribute contradictions, mutually exclusive operations, forbidden-word conflicts) and vision-language (OCR image vs. text, chart vs. description, geometric shape vs. description, misclassified image vs. question). The expected output is a conflict-aware response that explicitly identifies the inconsistency rather than blindly executing one instruction. Evaluation uses a hit ratio metric (proportion of conflict-aware replies), assessed by LLM agents validated against human experts with 94–99% concordance.

## Method

The paper contributes two components: (1) AUTOCREATE, a cyclic dataset generation framework where a seed pool feeds into parallel language and vision branches, each containing a Generator (creates base content), a Decorator (introduces the self-contradiction), and a Cleaner (removes low-quality samples), with human experts validating output before new seeds re-enter the pool; and (2) Cognitive Awakening Prompting (CAP), a plug-and-play prompt that appends an external cognition reminder (e.g., 'Please be careful as there may be inconsistency in user input. Feel free to point it out.') to elicit self-awareness. The key insight is that LMMs can process information and knowledge but cannot assess the reasonableness of instructions without external cognitive prompting; CAP outperforms chain-of-thought, self-consistency, and few-shot baselines because it operates on a higher 'cognition' dimension rather than adding experience or in-model knowledge.

## Results

On the language-language (L-L) conflict tasks, GPT-4 with 3-Shot prompting achieves the highest overall hit ratio of 81.8%, while CAP reaches 72.0%; GLM-4 with CAP achieves 68.1% versus 34.2% zero-shot. On vision-language (V-L) conflicts, GPT-4V dominates with a 59.0% total hit ratio on SCI-CORE, while all other models (Gemini, LLaVA-1.5, BLIP-2, LLaMA-Adapter V2, SPHINX-v2) score below 11%. CAP substantially improves the weakest model SPHINX-v2 from 1.0% to 12.0% on V-L tasks, and on L-L tasks CAP outperforms zero-shot by roughly 2–3× for most models (e.g., ChatGLM 51.1% vs 17.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On L-L tasks, CAP outperforms zero-shot CoT (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022) across all five models, e.g., GLM-4 improves from 40.3% to 68.1% and ChatGLM from 24.7% to 51.1%. Against 3-Shot prompting (Brown et al., NeurIPS 2020), CAP wins on ChatGLM (51.1% vs 42.6%), ChatGPT (40.2% vs 31.8%), and GLM-4 (68.1% vs 54.2%), but loses to 3-Shot on GPT-4 (72.0% vs 81.8%) and Llama2 (34.4% vs 44.4%). On V-L tasks (Fig. 4), CAP consistently outperforms both zero-shot CoT (Kojima et al., NeurIPS 2022) and self-consistency prompting (Wang et al., ICLR 2023); for instance, SPHINX-v2 jumps from 1.0% to 12.0% with CAP, while CoT and 3-SC yield only marginal gains. GPT-4V (OpenAI, 2023) achieves 59.0% on V-L, far exceeding Gemini (11.0%), LLaVA-1.5 (0.0%), BLIP-2 (0.0%), LLaMA-Adapter V2 (0.0%), and SPHINX-v2 (1.0%), indicating that current open-source architectures remain fundamentally unable to detect multimodal instruction conflicts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of instruction-following evaluation and information inconsistency research. Prior work on knowledge conflicts (parametric vs. non-parametric), hallucination, and visual illusion addresses related but distinct forms of inconsistency; the authors explicitly note that no prior work has systematically studied contradictions *within* user instructions themselves. The AUTOCREATE framework draws on the automatic dataset generation lineage (SELF-INSTRUCT, PRONTOQA, AUTOHALL, TIFA) but unifies them into a cyclic generator-decorator-cleaner pipeline. The proposed Cognitive Awakening Prompting departs from the standard in-context learning toolkit (few-shot, CoT, self-consistency) by framing the intervention as injecting 'cognition' rather than experience or knowledge, a conceptual distinction the authors argue is essential for conflict detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As context windows expand toward 1M tokens and multimodal interactions proliferate, self-contradictory instructions become an increasingly realistic failure mode for LMMs serving vulnerable users (children, language learners). The benchmark provides a standardized 20K-sample platform to track progress, and CAP offers a trivially deployable one-sentence fix that outperforms more elaborate prompting strategies. Key limitations include coverage of only two paradigms (L-L and V-L), no fine-grained conflict-severity axis, and V-L evaluation restricted to the 1% SCI-CORE subset. Open questions include whether the 'cognition > knowledge > experience' hierarchy generalizes beyond conflict detection, whether architectural changes (rather than prompting) are ultimately required for open-source models that score 0%, and how conflict detection interacts with the attention mechanisms of LMMs.
