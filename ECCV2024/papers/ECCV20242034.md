## Problem

Algorithmic fairness in medical AI has been studied almost exclusively in single-domain settings, yet real-world deployment routinely involves domain shifts (e.g., different retinal imaging modalities across clinics). This paper identifies that domain transfer significantly exacerbates group performance disparities in both segmentation and classification, and that no prior work has systematically investigated fairness under both domain adaptation and domain generalization for medical segmentation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fundus image (En face OCT or SLO) paired with a patient's demographic attribute vector (gender, race, or ethnicity) as a separate input stream. For segmentation, it outputs pixel-wise binary masks for the optic cup and optic rim; for classification, it outputs a normal/glaucoma label. A key design choice is that the dataset pairs images from the same patient cohort across both modalities, so demographic distributions are identical between source and target domains, isolating the effect of domain shift from demographic confounding.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-and-play Fair Identity Attention (FIA) module that injects demographic awareness into any backbone network. The module embeds the input image (E_i) and the demographic attribute (E_a) separately, adds a shared position embedding (E_p), then uses (E_i + E_p) to produce attention keys and values while (E_a + E_p) produces the query. Scaled dot-product attention over these tensors yields attribute-conditioned feature selections, followed by a residual connection, layer normalization, and an MLP. This allows the network to dynamically reweight which image features matter for each demographic group, preventing the amplification of existing biases during domain transfer. FIA is integrated into DAFormer for segmentation (both DA and DG) and into CDTrans/IRM for classification, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone.

## Results

On the FairDomain segmentation dataset (En face → SLO fundus), DAFormer-FIA achieves the highest ES-Dice of 0.802 for cup and 0.528 for rim under domain adaptation (gender attribute), and 0.816 for cup and 0.704 for rim under domain generalization, outperforming all baselines. For glaucoma classification, CDTrans-FIA reaches ES-AUC of 0.633 (gender), 0.606 (race), and 0.607 (ethnicity) under DA, while IRM-FIA achieves ES-AUC of 0.670 (gender), 0.671 (race), and 0.614 (ethnicity) under DG. The FIA module yields the largest relative gains on the rim segmentation task, e.g., improving ES-Dice from 0.344 (DAFormer) to 0.528 under DA with gender, a 53.5% relative increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For segmentation DA, DAFormer-FIA outperforms PixMatch (ES-Dice 0.802 vs 0.768 for cup) and DAFormer (0.802 vs 0.781) on cup, but CBST achieves a higher rim ES-Dice (0.693 vs 0.528), indicating CBST's class-balanced self-training is particularly effective for the smaller rim region under DA. For segmentation DG, DAFormer-FIA dominates all three baselines (GIN+IPA, AADG, DAFormer) on both cup and rim across all three demographic attributes, with the largest margin over GIN+IPA (ES-Dice 0.816 vs 0.741 for cup). For classification DA, CDTrans-FIA marginally beats CDTrans (ES-AUC 0.633 vs 0.631 for gender) and clearly outperforms CGDM (0.633 vs 0.611), with the biggest gains for minority groups (Blacks +0.005–0.016 AUC, Hispanics +0.01–0.035 AUC). For classification DG, IRM-FIA surpasses IRM (ES-AUC 0.670 vs 0.667 for gender; 0.671 vs 0.666 for race) and substantially outperforms GroupDro (0.670 vs 0.513) and G2DM (0.670 vs 0.610), with the most pronounced improvement for the race attribute where overall AUC increases by 0.024. Overall, the FIA module consistently improves both accuracy and fairness (ES-metrics) over its respective backbone across all 10 baseline comparisons, with the sole exception being CBST's rim DA performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously separate lines of work: domain adaptation/generalization for medical imaging (DAFormer, PixMatch, CBST, GIN+IPA, AADG, CDTrans, IRM, GroupDro, G2DM) and fairness-aware learning in medical AI (FairVision, FairSeg, Harvard Glaucoma Fairness). Prior fairness methods assumed identical source and target distributions, while prior DA/DG methods optimized accuracy without regard to demographic equity. The FIA module departs from both by introducing a demographic-conditioned self-attention mechanism that is plug-and-play across DA and DG backbones, and the paired-modality dataset design (same patients, two imaging technologies) explicitly isolates domain shift from demographic distribution shift—a confound that prior cross-domain fairness studies (Pham et al. 2023, Truong 2023) did not control for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FairDomain dataset and benchmark fill a critical gap: no prior medical imaging dataset allowed researchers to attribute fairness degradation solely to domain shift rather than to shifting demographic compositions. The finding that group performance disparities systematically worsen under domain transfer (Fig. 2) provides empirical motivation for fairness-aware DA/DG in clinical deployment, where different clinics use different imaging hardware. Limitations include a single hospital cohort, two imaging modalities, and evaluation on only three of six available demographic attributes; whether FIA generalizes beyond ophthalmology or to more complex multi-source domain shifts remains open. The plug-and-play nature of FIA makes it a practical tool, but the moderate magnitude of improvements (typically 1–5% ES-Dice) suggests that fairness under domain shift may require deeper architectural or training-strategy changes beyond attention-based feature reweighting.
