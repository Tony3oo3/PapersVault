## Problem

Estimating temporally consistent 3D keypoints on deformable shapes without ground-truth annotations remains unsolved. Existing self-supervised methods either assume object symmetry (which breaks under asymmetric deformation), are restricted to human skeletons, or rely on supervised pre-training on synthetic data. No prior approach can produce semantically stable keypoints across arbitrary deformable shapes (humans and animals) purely from geometric invariants of the deforming surface.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes a sequence of T point clouds (each N=2048 points, normalized to unit volume) of a deforming shape, along with precomputed pairwise geodesic distance matrices (5-nearest-neighbor graph shortest paths) for each frame. At inference, only a single raw PCD is required—no geodesic precomputation. The output is K=12 keypoints per frame, each expressed as a convex combination (probability-weighted sum) of the input points, yielding a continuous 3D position rather than a discrete point index.

## Method

SelfGeo uses a PointNet++ encoder followed by a Conv1D and Softmax layer to predict, for every input point, a K-dimensional probability of being each keypoint; a 4-layer Conv1D decoder then reconstructs the PCD from the expected keypoint positions. The core novelty is a differentiable geodesic loss that enforces the expected pairwise geodesic distances between keypoints (computed as W^t D^t W^{t⊤}) to remain constant across all frames in the sequence, exploiting the fact that surface geodesics are approximately invariant under non-rigid deformation while Euclidean distances are not. This is paired with a temporal smoothing loss to prevent keypoint swaps at symmetric locations, and a shape loss (Chamfer-distance reconstruction, coverage, and surface adherence) to ensure keypoints are informative and lie on the surface. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with no keypoint annotations, using only the geometric structure of the deforming sequence as supervision.

## Results

SelfGeo achieves the best performance across all four metrics (Inclusivity, Coverage, T_con, Reconstruction Error) on all three datasets. On CAPE, SelfGeo reaches 85.87 Inclusivity, 91.87 Coverage, 90.43% temporal consistency, and 0.012 reconstruction error. On the challenging real-world ITOP dataset, SelfGeo attains 84.23 Inclusivity, 91.13 Coverage, 80.64% T_con, and 0.012 reconstruction error. On Deforming Things 4D (animals), SelfGeo scores 85.22 Inclusivity, 88.11 Coverage, 80.53% T_con, and 0.038 reconstruction error, demonstrating generalization beyond human shapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfGeo outperforms both baselines (ULCS and SC3K) on every metric across all three datasets. Compared to ULCS [Fernandez-Labrador et al., ECCV 2020], SelfGeo gains substantial margins: +38.05 Inclusivity and +24.28 Coverage on CAPE, +42.15 Inclusivity and +26.85 Coverage on ITOP, and +37.16 Inclusivity and +38.78 T_con on Deforming Things 4D, where ULCS's symmetry assumption breaks down for asymmetric animal poses. Against SC3K [Zohaib et al., ICCV 2023], SelfGeo leads by +6.55 Inclusivity and +5.41 Coverage on CAPE, and by a dramatic +37.78 in T_con on ITOP (80.64 vs 42.86), indicating SC3K's rigid-object design fails to maintain temporal consistency under non-rigid deformation. On Deforming Things 4D, SelfGeo edges out SC3K on T_con (80.53 vs 77.44) and achieves a 4× lower reconstruction error (0.038 vs 0.153), confirming the geodesic-consistency loss is critical for skeleton-free deformable shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelfGeo extends the self-supervised 3D keypoint estimation framework established by SC3K [44] and ULCS [8] from rigid to non-rigid deformable shapes. The shape loss components (reconstruction, coverage, surface) are directly adapted from these prior works, while the key departure is the introduction of a geodesic distance preservation loss that enforces topological consistency of keypoints across deforming frames. Unlike skeleton-based or symmetry-based approaches (e.g., GC-KPL [33], ULCS [8]), SelfGeo requires no a priori body structure or symmetry assumptions, making it applicable to arbitrary deformable objects including animals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for ground-truth keypoint annotations on deformable shapes, SelfGeo addresses a significant practical bottleneck in 3D pose estimation and shape analysis for AR/VR and robotics applications. The geodesic consistency principle is a clean and generalizable inductive bias that could inform other correspondence-preserving tasks beyond keypoints. However, the method's reliance on pre-computed geodesic distances introduces sensitivity to surface topology errors (e.g., when body parts touch), and symmetry-induced keypoint swaps remain an open problem that the temporal smoothing loss only partially mitigates.
