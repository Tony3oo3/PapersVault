## Problem

While point cloud registration is mature and NeRF registration has been explored via voxel conversion or SfM on rendered images, registration of scenes represented by Gaussian Splatting (GS) remains entirely unexplored. The core difficulty is that GS-derived point clouds are noisy and geometrically distorted (no explicit geometric constraints during GS training), making standard point cloud registration insufficient, while the implicit-to-explicit conversion strategies used for NeRF do not transfer to GS's explicit Gaussian representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240400/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two trained GS models (each storing per-Gaussian position, opacity, rotation, scale, and spherical harmonic coefficients) along with the camera poses of their training images. Points are extracted by thresholding opacity above 0.7, yielding (x, y, z, α, r, g, b) tuples as input channels. The output is a similarity transformation {s, R, T} (scale, rotation, translation) aligning scene B to A, followed by a fused GS model where Gaussian attributes—including SH coefficients—are transformed via a pseudo-inverse-based linear mapping.

## Method

GaussReg is a coarse-to-fine framework. The coarse stage follows the GeoTransformer pipeline (shared KPConv-FPN for multi-scale features, Superpoint Match at coarsest level, ICP-based Point Match at finest level) but augments input channels with opacity and color, and adds scale augmentation to handle monocular reconstruction uncertainty. The fine stage is the key novelty: it first selects highly overlapping camera pairs via a three-step procedure (orientation cosine filtering, depth-map-based overlap area scoring, neighborhood expansion), renders 5 images per scene, then applies an Image-Guided 3D (I3D) feature extractor that builds MVS-style cost volumes from rendered images to produce volumetric features, which are projected into 3D and matched to refine the transformation. The total loss combines a cross-entropy depth supervision (λ=10) with the same overlap-aware circle loss and point matching loss used in coarse registration.

## Results

On the ScanNet-GSReg dataset (82 test scenes), GaussReg achieves a 100% success ratio with RRE of 2.827°, RTE of 0.042, and RSE of 0.032, while running in 4.8 seconds per pair — 44× faster than HLoc (212.3s). On the Objaverse dataset, the coarse-only variant already outperforms DReg-NeRF (RRE 2.47° vs. 9.67°, ATE 3.46 vs. 3.85). On the in-the-wild GSReg dataset, the full method achieves RRE 2.989°, RTE 0.065, and RSE 0.047, demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HLoc (SuperPoint + SuperGlue), GaussReg achieves a comparable RRE (2.827° vs. 2.725°) but substantially better RTE (0.042 vs. 0.099) and RSE (0.032 vs. 0.098), while improving the success ratio from 75.6% to 100% and reducing runtime by 44× (4.8s vs. 212.3s). Against the traditional point-cloud method FGR, GaussReg is dramatically more accurate (RRE 2.827° vs. 157.126°) at a modest 1.4× speed cost (4.8s vs. 3.4s). Against the deep registration method REGTR, GaussReg again dominates in accuracy (RRE 2.827° vs. 80.095°) with similar runtime. On the Objaverse dataset, even the coarse-only variant of GaussReg (RRE 2.47°, ATE 3.46) outperforms DReg-NeRF (RRE 9.67°, ATE 3.85) without any fine-tuning, demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization to object-level registration.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussReg sits at the intersection of two mature research threads: deep point cloud registration (GeoTransformer, REGTR, FGR) and 3D scene representation via Gaussian Splatting. For the coarse stage, it directly adapts GeoTransformer's KPConv-FPN + superpoint/point matching pipeline, augmenting input channels with GS-specific attributes (opacity, SH-derived color). The fine stage departs from prior NeRF registration work (DReg-NeRF's voxel-based features, NeRFuser's SfM on rendered images) by exploiting GS's dual nature as both a point cloud and a differentiable renderer, projecting multi-view rendered images into volumetric features via an MVS-style cost volume. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first registration method designed for the GS representation, filling a gap left by implicit NeRF registration approaches that struggle with unbounded scenes and voxel resolution limits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first dedicated benchmark (ScanNet-GSReg, 1379 scenes) and in-the-wild dataset (GSReg) for GS registration, a task that will become increasingly relevant as GS replaces NeRF for large-scale scene reconstruction. The 44× speedup over HLoc with 100% success rate (vs. 75.6%) makes GS-based scene stitching practically viable for real-time or near-real-time pipelines. The image-guided fine registration strategy—rendering a small number of images from the overlapping region and extracting volumetric features—is a generalizable idea that could transfer to other GS-related tasks (e.g., scene editing, continual learning). A key limitation is the simple fusion strategy, which fails when scenes are captured under different lighting conditions, leaving open the question of appearance-consistent GS merging.
