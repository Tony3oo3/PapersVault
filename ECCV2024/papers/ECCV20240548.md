## Problem

Existing Online Temporal Action Localization (OnTAL) methods, such as OAT, rely solely on short-term context within a sliding window and neglect long-term historical information. This limitation is particularly detrimental for procedural egocentric (PREGO) scenarios where actions are contextually dependent on preceding steps, and where the egocentric viewpoint often occludes the current action. The integration of long-term history into anchor-based transformer models for OnTAL had remained largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming video processed frame-by-frame, with features extracted by a pre-trained encoder (I3D at 24 FPS with stride 12 for EGTEA, SlowFast for EPIC-Kitchen-100, two-stream TSN for THUMOS'14, or I3D for MUSES) projected into a 1024-dim space. The input queue is partitioned into a long-term history segment (L_h frames, e.g., 48 for PREGO, 256 for THUMOS) and a short-term window (L_s frames, e.g., 16 for PREGO, 64 for THUMOS). Output consists of discrete action instance proposals (category, start time, end time) generated online; once emitted, a proposal is irreversible, and an Online Suppression Network replaces offline NMS to filter redundant detections under the streaming constraint.

## Method

HAT augments OAT's anchor transformer with a Future-Supervised History Module that compresses long-term history (L_h frames) into a compact representation (L_comp << L_h) via a transformer decoder with learnable history tokens. An action anticipation head provides weak window-level supervision during training, calibrating the compressor's attention toward frames pivotal for predicting the current action, while a future-driven refinement stage uses cross-attention with the short-term encoder output to align compressed history with the present context. The refined history is then fused with anchor features (produced by a standard encoder-decoder over the short-term window) through a cross-attention decoder, yielding history-augmented anchor representations for classification and boundary regression. A novel Adaptive Focal Loss dynamically adjusts per-class focal factors based on the ratio of accumulated positive-to-negative gradients, addressing both foreground-background and inter-foreground class imbalance simultaneously.

## Results

On the Procedural Egocentric (PREGO) datasets, HAT achieves 21.5% average mAP on EGTEA and 15.3% on EPIC-Kitchen-100, outperforming the prior SOTA OAT by 1.9 and 1.1 percentage points respectively. On the standard non-PREGO benchmarks, HAT reaches 44.8% average mAP on THUMOS'14 (vs. OAT's 44.6%) and 10.8% on MUSES (vs. OAT's 9.8%), showing comparable or slightly superior performance. The model operates at 147 FPS on an RTX 4090 GPU under the online execution constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAT outperforms OAT [19], the primary OnTAL baseline, on all four datasets: EGTEA (21.5 vs. 19.6 avg mAP), EK-100 (15.3 vs. 14.2), THUMOS'14 (44.8 vs. 44.6), and MUSES (10.8 vs. 9.8), with the largest gains on PREGO datasets where historical context is most informative. Against earlier OnTAL methods, HAT substantially surpasses CAG-QIL [18] (44.8 vs. 29.7 on THUMOS'14; 10.8 vs. 4.8 on MUSES), 2PESNet [21] (44.8 vs. 30.9 on THUMOS'14), and SimOn [33] (44.8 vs. 36.9 on THUMOS'14). In ablation-style comparisons of history modules on EGTEA, HAT's Future-Supervised History Module (20.8 avg mAP) outperforms LSTR [40] (19.5) and GateHUB [4] (19.9), demonstrating that action-anticipation-guided compression is more effective than two-stage compression or gating mechanisms for OnTAL. The proposed Adaptive Focal Loss also exceeds Regular Focal Loss [25] (20.8 vs. 19.9) and GateHUB's Background-Suppression Focal Loss [4] (20.8 vs. 20.2) on EGTEA, confirming the benefit of gradient-adaptive per-class focal factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAT extends the Online Anchor Transformer (OAT, ECCV 2022), which introduced anchor-based transformer features for Online Temporal Action Localization, by adding a Future-Supervised History Module that compresses and refines long-term context. The history compression strategy draws on OAD literature (LSTR's two-stage compression, GateHUB's gating mechanism) but repurposes it for instance-level prediction via a novel action anticipation head that calibrates the compressor's attention. The Adaptive Focal Loss adapts the equalized focal loss concept from dense object detection to the OnTAL setting, addressing both foreground-background and inter-class foreground imbalance simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that long-term historical context yields meaningful gains specifically in procedural egocentric (PREGO) scenarios where actions are sequentially dependent, while offering only marginal improvements on standard non-PREGO benchmarks—suggesting history integration is most valuable when temporal dependencies are strong. A key open limitation is the fixed history length, which requires manual tuning and cannot adapt per-instance; the authors note that adaptive attention span mechanisms (analogous to those in language models) would be a natural next step. The action anticipation head as a training-time calibration signal for history compression is a reusable idea that could transfer to other online video understanding tasks.
