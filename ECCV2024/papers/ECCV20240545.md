## Problem

Transformer-based diffusion models are bottlenecked by quadratic attention complexity, and while Mamba offers linear-complexity sequence modeling, existing Mamba-based vision methods flatten 2D image tokens in row/column-major order, ignoring spatial continuity between neighboring patches. This paper identifies that neglecting spatial continuity in the scan scheme is a critical oversight that limits Mamba's ability to capture local visual structure, and that multi-directional scanning within a single block introduces unnecessary parameter and memory overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240545/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in VAE latent space, taking a text prompt (processed via cross-attention) and random noise as input, and producing generated images at up to 1024×1024 resolution (4,096 latent tokens from a 128×128 latent with patch size 2) or 16-frame videos at 256×256. Text conditioning is injected through a dedicated cross-attention block with skip connections alongside the Mamba scan, while timestep and text embeddings jointly modulate the Mamba block via AdaLN-style conditioning.

## Method

ZigMa is a DiT-style backbone where each of L layers contains a single Mamba scan block, but the token order is permuted by a different spatially-continuous zigzag path (chosen from 8 heuristically designed space-filling schemes via modulo cycling) before scanning and restored afterward. This heterogeneous layerwise scan amortizes multi-directional coverage across layers with zero additional parameters or memory cost, unlike bidirectional or multi-directional Mamba variants that duplicate SSM parameters per direction. For 3D video, the architecture factorizes into a 2D spatial zigzag Mamba followed by a 1D temporal sweep Mamba (e.g., 'sst' ordering), avoiding the suboptimal optimization of a monolithic 3D zigzag. Training follows the Stochastic Interpolant framework with a linear path (α_t = 1−t, σ_t = t), estimating the velocity field, and sampling is performed via the probability-flow ODE for speed.

## Results

On FacesHQ 1024×1024 (4,096 latent tokens), ZigMa achieves FID 37.8 and FDD 50.5, outperforming Bidirectional VisionMamba (FID 51.1, FDD 66.3); doubling the batch size further reduces FID to 26.6 and FDD to 31.2. On MS COCO 256×256, the full Zigzag-8 scheme reaches FID 41.8 versus 60.2 for VisionMamba. On unconditional CelebA-256, ZigMa matches or slightly beats transformer baselines (FID 14.27 vs. U-ViT 14.50 and DiT 14.64) while using substantially less GPU memory (17.80 GB vs. 35.10 GB and 29.20 GB) and fewer FLOPS (5.2 G vs. 12.5 G and 5.5 G). On UCF101 video, the factorized 3D Zigzag achieves Frame-FID 216.1 and FVD 210.2, surpassing Bidirectional Mamba (256.1 / 320.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VisionMamba (Zhu et al., ICML 2024), ZigMa consistently wins across all evaluated datasets: on FacesHQ-1024 it improves FID from 51.1 to 37.8 (and 26.6 at 2× batch), on MS COCO the Zigzag-8 variant drops FID from 60.2 to 41.8, and on UCF101 video it reduces Frame-FID from 256.1 to 216.1 and FVD from 320.2 to 210.2. Against U-ViT (Bao et al., CVPR 2023) on unconditional CelebA-256, ZigMa achieves a marginally better FID (14.27 vs. 14.50) while cutting GPU memory by nearly half (17.80 GB vs. 35.10 GB) and FLOPS by more than half (5.2 G vs. 12.5 G). Against DiT (Peebles et al., 2022), ZigMa again edges out FID (14.27 vs. 14.64) and reduces memory from 29.20 GB to 17.80 GB with comparable FLOPS (5.2 G vs. 5.5 G), confirming that the Mamba backbone matches transformer quality at significantly lower memory cost. The key differentiator is that ZigMa's heterogeneous layerwise scan adds zero extra parameters or memory overhead per additional scan direction, whereas multi-direction Mamba baselines (e.g., bidirectional or k-direction) incur linear parameter and memory growth with the number of directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Mamba/SSM-based sequence modeling, DiT-style diffusion backbones, and the Stochastic Interpolant framework. It departs from prior Mamba-for-vision work (VisionMamba, V-Mamba, Mamba-ND) by identifying that flattening 2D tokens in row/column-major order destroys spatial continuity, and by distributing heterogeneous zigzag scan paths across layers rather than duplicating parameters within a single block. The DiT architecture provides the layer-wise structure, while Stochastic Interpolants supply the training/sampling formulation, extending prior SiT work from 512×512 to 1024×1024 and to video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a zero-parameter, plug-and-play scan-order change can close most of the gap between naive Mamba and transformer-based diffusion backbones, offering a practical path to linear-complexity generative models at high resolution. The introduction of 'Order Receptive Field' as a free knob (more scan diversity without more parameters or memory) is a useful design principle for future SSM-based architectures. Limitations include constrained training budgets (16–32 A100s), the suboptimal optimization of the non-factorized 3D zigzag, and the fact that the eight hand-designed scan patterns may not be optimal; whether the approach generalizes beyond faces and short video clips to broader image/video distributions remains open.
