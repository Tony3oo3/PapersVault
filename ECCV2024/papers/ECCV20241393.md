## Problem

Existing diffusion-based blind super-resolution methods ignore degradation constraints on the diffusion process and assume spatially invariant blur kernels, which fails in real-world scenarios involving motion jitter and depth-dependent defocus. This lack of structured conditioning causes generated high-resolution images to deviate from the actual degradation physics, producing hallucinated or inconsistent textures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241393/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image as input and produces a ×4 upscaled high-resolution image. Internally, it derives two auxiliary modalities from the LR input: a monocular depth map (via a pre-trained depth estimator) and a spatially variant 21×21 anisotropic Gaussian blur kernel field, one kernel per pixel. These three modalities (LR image, depth map, kernel field) are jointly encoded and used to condition a pre-trained latent diffusion model, so the final output is a single SR image whose generation is constrained by all three signals simultaneously.

## Method

SSR introduces two key modules around a pre-trained Stable Diffusion backbone. The Spatially Variant Kernel Refinement (SVKR) module iteratively (T=3 steps) alternates between monocular depth estimation on the current SR estimate, depth-informed spatially variant kernel estimation via DKENet (a two-branch CNN with SFT layers and MAconv blocks that fuses LR and depth features), and a lightweight RRDB-based non-blind SR step, with exponential moving-average kernel updates (γ=0.9) to stabilize convergence. The Adaptive Multi-Modal Fusion (AMF) module encodes the three modalities with differently-scaled encoders (half-UNet for LR, smaller encoders for depth and kernel) and aligns them in a shared semantic space via per-modality MLP-based scale-and-shift operations (analogous to SFT), then injects the aligned features into multiple UNet levels of the diffusion model. Training combines the standard DDPM denoising loss with L1 regularization terms on each modality's encoded features, encouraging the network to exploit complementary information across modalities while keeping the diffusion prior intact.

## Results

On DIV2K (×4), SSR achieves the best PSNR (22.77 dB), SSIM (0.75), LPIPS (0.30), MUSIQ (71.44), CLIP-IQA (0.69), and NIQE (3.82) among all compared methods. On BSDS100, SSR leads in SSIM (0.90), LPIPS (0.25), MUSIQ (68.86), CLIP-IQA (0.72), and NIQE (3.78), though DDNM achieves a higher PSNR (29.48 vs. 27.13). On DRealSR, SSR trails CNN-based methods on PSNR (28.33 vs. 31.00 for DAN) and SSIM (0.85 vs. 0.93 for HAT) but dominates all perceptual/no-reference metrics (LPIPS 0.30, MUSIQ 63.58, CLIP-IQA 0.64, NIQE 5.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR achieves the best scores on all six metrics simultaneously on DIV2K, outperforming the closest diffusion-based competitor ResShift by 0.39 dB in PSNR (22.77 vs. 22.38) and 0.86 in MUSIQ (71.44 vs. 70.58). On BSDS100, SSR dominates five of six metrics (SSIM 0.90, LPIPS 0.25, MUSIQ 68.86, CLIP-IQA 0.72, NIQE 3.78) but trails DDNM on PSNR (27.13 vs. 29.48), a gap of 2.35 dB. On Urban100, SSR wins on LPIPS (0.27, tied with ResShift), MUSIQ (72.74), and CLIP-IQA (0.70) but loses PSNR to ResShift (21.23 vs. 21.71) and NIQE to RealESRGAN (4.68 vs. 4.10). The most notable gap appears on DRealSR, where CNN-based methods (DAN 31.00, DASR 30.98, HAT 30.96) substantially outperform SSR on PSNR (28.33) and SSIM (0.85 vs. 0.93), yet SSR still leads all perceptual metrics by large margins (e.g., MUSIQ 63.58 vs. 51.95 for SwinIR, CLIP-IQA 0.64 vs. 0.57 for ResShift). Overall, SSR consistently wins on no-reference and perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, NIQE) across all four datasets, while its PSNR/SSIM advantage is dataset-dependent—strong on DIV2K and BSDS100 but weaker on DRealSR where real-world degradations favor pixel-faithful CNN reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSR sits at the intersection of three established threads: (1) blind SR with kernel estimation (IKC, DCLS, DASR, MANet), which typically assumes a single spatially invariant kernel; (2) spatially variant kernel estimation (MANet, KOALAnet, CMOS), which addresses local degradation but without depth-awareness; and (3) diffusion-model-based SR (SR3, ILVR, CDPMSR, DDNM, ResShift), which leverages generative priors but largely ignores degradation constraints. The paper's key departure is jointly estimating a depth-informed spatially variant kernel and a depth map via iterative refinement (SVKR), then fusing all three modalities (LR image, depth, kernel) through an AMF module to condition a latent diffusion model, thereby constraining its sampling space with explicit degradation information rather than relying solely on the LR image as a condition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a genuine gap: diffusion-based SR methods can hallucinate textures inconsistent with the actual degradation, and the paper shows that injecting spatially variant kernel and depth constraints measurably improves perceptual quality (MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, NIQE) across four benchmarks. However, the method's reliance on monocular depth estimation from blurry LR images introduces a circular dependency that the iterative SVKR only partially resolves, and the DRealSR results reveal a significant PSNR gap versus non-diffusion baselines (28.33 vs. ~31 dB), suggesting the approach trades pixel-level fidelity for perceptual realism. Open questions include how the method scales to video SR, its sensitivity to depth estimation errors in challenging scenes, and whether the three-iteration SVKR loop can be made more efficient for practical deployment.
