## Problem

Existing model stealing attacks against self-supervised learning encoders suffer from two key deficiencies: (1) suboptimal efficacy because they use a single augmentation's embedding as the optimization objective, which can be biased or divergent, and (2) high query costs because end-to-end methods like Cont-Steal must query the target encoder with new augmentations every epoch. This paper addresses both gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240899/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker provides unlabeled surrogate images (2,500–60,000 from Tiny ImageNet, resized to 32×32 for CIFAR-10/STL-10 targets or 224×224 for ImageNet/CLIP targets) and queries the target encoder's API only once per sample using n=10 augmented crops to build a static prototype. The output is a ResNet18 surrogate encoder trained in a fully query-free manner, evaluated via linear probing and KNN accuracy on seven downstream classification datasets. The prototype memory bank is pre-computed before training begins, decoupling the query phase from the optimization phase.

## Method

RDA (Refine, Discriminate, Align) first generates a sample-wise prototype for each surrogate image by averaging the target encoder's embeddings over n=10 random crops, yielding a stable and less-biased optimization objective that eliminates per-epoch queries. During query-free training, each image is further cropped into m=5 patches fed to the surrogate encoder, and a two-part multi-relational extraction loss is applied: a discriminating loss (L_D) that contrasts each embedding against mismatched prototypes and other samples' embeddings (extending Cont-Steal's contrastive structure), and an aligning loss (L_A) that matches each embedding to its own prototype in both amplitude (MSE) and angle (cosine similarity), with logarithmic reweighting so that small residual deviations are penalized more heavily than large ones. The final loss is a weighted sum λ₁·L_D + λ₂·L_A, and the combination is shown to be more robust across diverse downstream datasets than either component alone.

## Results

On the encoder pre-trained on CIFAR-10, RDA with 2,500 queries (1% of Cont-Steal's budget) achieves SA/TA × 100% of 83.84 on CIFAR-10, 77.49 on CIFAR-100, and 98.97 on MNIST, outperforming Cont-Steal (80.43, 73.94, 98.03) on average by 1.22% across seven downstream datasets. With 25,000 queries (10% of Cont-Steal), the average gap widens to 5.20%, with RDA reaching 101.60% on GTSRB and 100.84% on Fashion-MNIST (i.e., surpassing the target encoder). Against the large-scale ImageNet and CLIP encoders, RDA achieves SA/TA ratios of 94–99% and 81–97% respectively using only 3.07% and 0.015% of the original pre-training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDA is compared against three baselines: Conventional (single-query MSE matching), StolenEncoder (single-query with augmentations), and Cont-Steal (end-to-end contrastive stealing requiring 250,000 queries). At the same 2,500-query budget, RDA outperforms StolenEncoder on 5 of 7 datasets (e.g., +4.81 on CIFAR-10, +8.71 on CIFAR-100, +30.57 on GTSRB) but trails it slightly on F-MNIST (94.69 vs. 95.91) and is marginally below Cont-Steal on GTSRB (94.27 vs. 95.55). At 25,000 queries, RDA dominates all three baselines on every downstream dataset, achieving an average SA/TA of 94.25% versus 80.43% for Cont-Steal (which requires 10× more queries). The paper reports that RDA's average advantage over Cont-Steal is 1.22% at 1% query cost and 5.20% at 10% query cost. Under equal time cost (300 epochs for baselines vs. 100 for RDA), RDA still leads, confirming that the gain is not merely from longer training.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDA builds directly on the contrastive stealing framework of Cont-Steal (Sha et al., CVPR 2023), adapting its InfoNCE-style loss structure for the discriminating component, and on StolenEncoder (Liu et al., CCS 2022) as the augmentation-based baseline it seeks to surpass. Its key conceptual departure is the introduction of a sample-wise prototype—averaging the target encoder's embeddings over multiple augmentations of a single image—borrowed from prototype learning in federated and few-shot settings, to replace the single-embedding optimization objective used by prior methods. This transforms the training from an end-to-end, query-per-epoch paradigm into a query-free phase guided by a static memory bank, while the multi-relational extraction loss (discriminating + amplitude/angle aligning) goes beyond the pure contrastive objective of Cont-Steal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the dramatic query-cost reduction (1% of Cont-Steal's budget) while matching or exceeding its downstream accuracy, which has direct economic implications for API-based encoder services. The finding that RDA effectively unlearns a backdoor watermark (99.87% → 17.04% watermark rate) suggests that model stealing and watermarking defenses interact in non-trivial ways, raising open questions about the reliability of watermark-based ownership claims. Limitations include the unexplored extension to transformer-based encoders and the observation that the aligning loss can overfit to the surrogate distribution, hinting at a fundamental tension between fidelity to the target and generalization to out-of-distribution data.
