## Problem

High-resolution vision-language models can perceive fine image details but exhaustively process all high-resolution tokens in self-attention, causing quadratic computational cost growth that makes them impractical. Existing high-resolution VLMs (e.g., LLaVA-HD, CogAgent) either concatenate all high-resolution tokens as regular inputs or use cross-attention at every layer, neither of which dynamically focuses computation on the regions actually relevant to the current question.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240661/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution RGB image (1008×1008, three times the base LLaVA-1.5 resolution of 336×336) and a text question as input, producing a free-form text answer. The image is simultaneously encoded at both high and low resolution by a shared vision encoder; only the low-resolution tokens and text tokens are fed through the first N_SA decoder layers, while the high-resolution tokens are selectively injected in subsequent layers. At each FlexAttention layer, approximately 10% of high-resolution tokens are dynamically selected based on the previous layer's attention map.

## Method

FlexAttention replaces the standard self-attention in the last N_FA decoder layers with two cooperating modules. The high-resolution feature selection module extracts the last column of the previous layer's attention map (importance of low-res image tokens to the last text token), reshapes it to a 2-D spatial mask, binarizes and resizes it to the high-resolution patch grid, and uses it to select a small subset f_SHR of high-resolution tokens. The hierarchical self-attention module then computes attention where queries come only from the low-resolution and text hidden state H (N tokens), while keys and values are the concatenation of H and the selected high-resolution tokens f_SHR (N+M tokens), yielding O((M+N)ND) complexity instead of O((M+N)²D). The resulting attention map feeds back into the next layer's selection module, creating an iterative coarse-to-fine attention loop across layers. The method is plugged into LLaVA-1.5-7b and fine-tuned for one epoch on the standard LLaVA-1.5 dataset, achieving ~40% TFLOPs reduction over high-resolution baselines while improving V*Bench accuracy by ~9% and TextVQA by ~7%.

## Results

On V*Bench, LLaVA-FlexAttn achieves 54.5% overall accuracy (47.8% attribute, 64.5% spatial), surpassing the base LLaVA-1.5-7b (47.6%) by 6.9% and approaching GPT-4V (55.0%) while exceeding it on the spatial sub-category. On MagnifierBench it reaches 35.0%, on TextVQA 48.9%, and on RSVQA-HRBEN 72.7% overall. Crucially, these gains come with a ~30–37% reduction in TFLOPs (17.1 vs. 24.9 for LLaVA-HD and 27.1 for LLaVA-XAttn) and ~30–40% faster inference on a single V100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two high-resolution baselines, LLaVA-FlexAttn wins on every benchmark: it outperforms LLaVA-HD by 2.7 points on V*Bench overall (54.5 vs. 51.8), 3.3 points on TextVQA (48.9 vs. 45.6), and 4.3 points on RSVQA-HRBEN (72.7 vs. 68.4), while tying on MagnifierBench (35.0); it outperforms LLaVA-XAttn by 6.3 points on V*Bench (54.5 vs. 48.2), 2.8 on MagnifierBench (35.0 vs. 32.2), 3.4 on TextVQA, and 1.6 on RSVQA-HRBEN. All of these gains come with substantially lower compute: 17.1 TFLOPs versus 24.9 (LLaVA-HD) and 27.1 (LLaVA-XAttn), a 31–37% reduction. Compared to GPT-4V on V*Bench, FlexAttn is slightly lower overall (54.5 vs. 55.0) but clearly higher on the spatial sub-category (64.5 vs. 60.5). On the domain-specific RSVQA-HRBEN benchmark, FlexAttn also edges out the specialist remote-sensing model GeoChat (72.7 vs. 72.3). On general multimodal benchmarks (GQA, VQAv2, POPE, MM-Bench, MME, MM-Vet), FlexAttn maintains parity with the base LLaVA-1.5-7b, confirming that the added high-resolution pathway does not degrade general capabilities, while improving RefCOCO by 3.5 points (79.3 vs. 75.8), especially for small objects (+10.0 points).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (vision encoder + LLaMA decoder) and addresses the quadratic computational cost that high-resolution VLMs like LLaVA-1.5-HD and CogAgent incur when processing all image tokens through self-attention. It departs from LLaVA-HD's approach of concatenating all high-resolution tokens as regular sequence elements, and from CogAgent's per-layer cross-attention over the full high-resolution feature map, by introducing a dynamic attention-map-driven selection of only ~10% of high-resolution tokens combined with a hierarchical self-attention that maintains linear complexity in the number of selected tokens. The hierarchical self-attention design draws conceptual inspiration from hierarchical attention networks for document classification (Yang et al., 2016), but is specifically adapted to fuse multi-grained visual features into an autoregressive LLM's hidden state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlexAttention offers a practical, drop-in solution to the efficiency bottleneck that has limited the adoption of high-resolution inputs in VLMs, achieving ~40% computational cost reduction while simultaneously improving accuracy over both low-resolution and existing high-resolution baselines. The coarse-to-fine selection mechanism mirrors human visual attention, suggesting a principled path toward more biologically plausible and computationally efficient multimodal models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the LLaVA-1.5 backbone, how it scales to even higher resolutions or video inputs (which the authors identify as future work), and whether the fixed 10% selection ratio is optimal across diverse task types. The method's reliance on a single attention map for selection also raises questions about robustness when attention is diffuse or misaligned with the true region of interest.
