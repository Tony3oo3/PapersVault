## Problem

Existing data-driven image-to-video diffusion models learn motion from internet-scale video data but do not incorporate real-world physics, resulting in temporally incoherent videos with unrealistic object dynamics and no fine-grained controllability over forces or torques. Conversely, model-based physics simulators produce controllable, physically correct motion but cannot render photorealistic footage of real-world scenes. PhysGen addresses this gap by grounding video generation explicitly in rigid-body physics while leveraging generative priors for appearance realism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image and a user-specified initial force vector F₀ and/or torque τ₀ as input, and produces a 16-frame video (uniformly sampled from 120 simulation steps) at 512×512 resolution. The force/torque specification is what enables interactive controllability: varying direction and magnitude of the initial impulse yields different physically plausible trajectories from the same input image. The entire pipeline is training-free, operating solely at inference time with off-the-shelf models.

## Method

PhysGen is a three-stage, training-free pipeline. First, a perception module uses GPT-4V for object recognition and physical property reasoning (mass, elasticity, Coulomb friction coefficient), Grounded-SAM for instance segmentation, shape-primitive fitting (circles or polygons), intrinsic decomposition (albedo, normals, lighting), and generative inpainting to recover the background. Second, an image-space rigid-body dynamics simulator (Pymunk) integrates Newton's equations of motion with collision detection, rolling/sliding friction, and elasticity to produce per-object 2D poses over time. Third, a rendering pipeline forward-warps and alpha-composites the moving objects onto the inpainted background, applies parametric relighting to account for shading changes, and then refines the result with a pretrained video diffusion model (SEINE) using an SDEdit-style procedure: the guidance video is encoded to latent space, partially noised (s=0.5), and denoised with DDIM while progressively fusing foreground latents from the guidance to preserve motion fidelity while synthesizing realistic shadows and boundary details.

## Results

In human evaluation (5-point scale), PhysGen achieves 4.14 physical-realism and 3.86 photo-realism, substantially outperforming the best baseline I2VGen-XL (2.11 / 2.25). In quantitative evaluation against ground-truth videos, PhysGen attains the lowest Motion-FID (30.20) among all methods, indicating the most realistic motion, while its Image-FID (105.70) is competitive with DynamiCrafter (99.64) and I2VGen-XL (104.62). The perception module achieves 0.93 precision and 0.82 recall at 0.5 IoU on 118 annotated movable instances across 10 open-world images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysGen decisively outperforms all three I2V diffusion baselines on physical-realism in human evaluation (4.14 vs. 1.39 for SEINE, 1.68 for DynamiCrafter, 2.11 for I2VGen-XL), with a gap of over 2 points over the strongest competitor. On photo-realism, PhysGen also leads (3.86 vs. 2.25 best baseline). In quantitative FID metrics, PhysGen achieves the best Motion-FID (30.20) by a wide margin over SEINE (38.76), I2VGen-XL (82.04), and DynamiCrafter (109.61), confirming its superior motion fidelity; however, its Image-FID (105.70) is slightly higher than DynamiCrafter (99.64) and I2VGen-XL (104.62), reflecting a minor trade-off in per-frame appearance. Against Motion Guidance [28] (qualitative only), PhysGen produces more temporally consistent results, particularly in multi-object interaction scenes where Motion Guidance introduces artifacts and new content. The paper notes that DynamiCrafter generates minimal motion (low Image-FID, high Motion-FID) while SEINE produces realistic motion but poor image consistency (high Image-FID, moderate Motion-FID), whereas PhysGen balances both.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysGen sits at the intersection of three research threads: image-based physics reasoning (which has relied on synthetic data or inverse physics from video), data-driven image-to-video diffusion models (SEINE, I2VGen-XL, DynamiCrafter), and classical image animation with motion fields. Its key departure is replacing learned image-space dynamics with an explicit rigid-body physics simulator (Pymunk) whose parameters are inferred zero-shot from a single image via GPT-4V, then using the simulated trajectories as guidance for a pretrained latent video diffusion model (SEINE) in an SDEdit-style refinement. This positions the work as a 'model-based video generative model' in contrast to the prevailing purely data-driven paradigm, and it is entirely training-free, assembling off-the-shelf components (Grounded-SAM, GPT-4V, Pymunk, SEINE, inpainting) into a new pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit physics simulation can be plugged into a generative video pipeline without any task-specific training, yielding videos that are both physically grounded and photo-realistic, with fine-grained controllability over forces and torques. This opens a path toward interactive, physics-aware image animation and downstream applications like virtual prototyping or educational visualization. However, the method is limited to 2D rigid-body dynamics (no out-of-plane motion, no deformable objects), relies on a single 2D physics engine, and the quantitative evaluation is restricted to one scene with 50 ground-truth videos, leaving open whether the approach scales to complex 3D scenes, non-rigid materials, or long-horizon interactions.
