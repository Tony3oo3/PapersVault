## Problem

Existing image customization methods suffer from three key limitations: they cause unintended changes in non-target regions when regenerating entire images, they rely on either a reference image or text as guidance but not both simultaneously, and they require time-consuming fine-tuning or dataset-specific training. No prior tuning-free method concurrently leverages both image and text guidance for precise, region-specific subject customization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242033/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene image, one or more reference images containing the subject(s) to insert, a text prompt specifying desired attribute modifications, and a binary region mask defining where to edit. It produces a single edited image where the masked region contains the reference subject with text-specified attributes while all non-target pixels remain unchanged. The entire pipeline runs in seconds without any parameter updates, and it generalizes to multi-subject, multi-region, and cross-domain (e.g., cartoon-style) scenarios.

## Method

The core mechanism is a three-stream denoising architecture with a self-attention blending strategy. First, the reference subject is directly pasted into the target region (collage) and inverted using DPM-Solver++ rather than DDIM to obtain higher-fidelity latent codes. During denoising, three parallel U-Net passes run: a reconstruction stream (null prompt) preserving non-target content, a text-driven stream (target prompt) providing attribute editing signals, and an edited-image stream where Gaussian noise is filled between the subject and non-target area to enable natural interaction. At each denoising step, self-attention features from all three streams are blended via weighted averaging and injected back into the edited-image stream's self-attention block. A temporal gating schedule restricts blending to a specific window (τ_a·T to τ_b·T) of the denoising process, since semantic information is primarily formed in later stages and injecting blended attention too early or too late degrades layout and introduces artifacts.

## Results

On the 3000-image evaluation set spanning 30 DreamBooth categories and 10 COCO scenes, the proposed method achieves DINO 51.18, CLIP-I 78.08, and CLIP-T 26.86, substantially outperforming all single-driven baselines (best prior: Blended Latent Diffusion at DINO 17.17, CLIP-I 63.21, CLIP-T 21.14). In the user study (2513 valid ratings), the method scores 4.02/5 on Fidelity, 3.93/5 on Quality, and 4.28/5 on Text Alignment, exceeding the next-best two-step pipeline (IP2P+DCCF) on all three dimensions. For reconstruction quality, DPM-Solver++ inversion achieves MAE 0.041, LPIPS 0.106, and SSIM 0.806 versus DDIM's 0.128/0.472/0.697.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-driven image-based methods, Paint-by-Example [42] (DINO 15.26, CLIP-I 66.94, CLIP-T 20.62) and AnyDoor [9] (DINO 59.19, CLIP-I 78.92, CLIP-T 21.22) are both outperformed: the proposed method gains +35.9 DINO over Paint-by-Example and +3.7 DINO over AnyDoor, while also achieving the highest CLIP-T (26.86 vs. 20.62 and 21.22), confirming superior text-driven attribute editing. Against text-only methods, Blended Latent Diffusion [2] (DINO 17.17, CLIP-I 63.21, CLIP-T 21.14) and Stable Diffusion Inpainting [31] (DINO 14.43, CLIP-I 62.17, CLIP-T 21.04) trail by large margins on all three metrics, reflecting their inability to preserve reference-subject identity. In the two-step pipeline comparisons (user study), DCCF+IP2P [41,4] (Fidelity 3.63, Quality 3.55, Text 3.88) and IP2P+DCCF [4,41] (Fidelity 3.91, Quality 3.31, Text 4.11) are both surpassed, with the proposed method leading by +0.39 Fidelity and +0.17 Text Alignment over the best two-step baseline. MasaCtrl [5] (Fidelity 2.33, Quality 2.34, Text 2.52) performs worst overall, consistent with its limited editing scope to pre-trained subjects. For inversion quality, DPM-Solver++ [25] reduces MAE from 0.128 to 0.041 and improves SSIM from 0.697 to 0.806 over DDIM [36], validating the choice of high-order ODE solver for collage reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: image-conditioned customization (Paint-by-example, AnyDoor) and text-driven editing (Blended Latent Diffusion, MasaCtrl, Null-text Inversion). It departs from both by proposing a three-stream denoising architecture that simultaneously injects reference-image semantics and text-prompt attributes through a weighted self-attention blending mechanism, rather than replacing text embeddings with image tokens (as AnyDoor does) or relying solely on textual descriptions. The DPM-Solver++ inversion step is borrowed from TF-ICON [26], and the self-attention manipulation draws on MasaCtrl's mutual attention control, but the specific combination of dual-modality guidance in a tuning-free, region-constrained setting is the paper's core architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tuning-free, seconds-scale operation makes dual-modality region editing practically deployable in creative photography, graphic design, and interactive editing pipelines where fine-tuning per subject is infeasible. However, the authors acknowledge that non-rigid motion editing and multi-perspective generation remain problematic, as the self-attention blending without learned adaptation cannot fully preserve subject identity under large geometric changes. An open question is whether the three-stream blending strategy generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion's U-Net to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) where self-attention operates differently, and whether the fixed threshold schedule (τa, τb) can be made adaptive per input.
