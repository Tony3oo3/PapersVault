## Problem

LLM-based knowledge VQA systems under the in-context learning paradigm inherit biases from both the VQA training data and external knowledge bases, leading to unfair context descriptions and homogeneous in-context examples that mislead the LLM reasoner. Existing debiasing methods (counterfactual examples, ensemble learning) target dataset-level biases and are poorly suited to the in-context learning setting or to LLMs due to high fine-tuning costs and limited ensemble options.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a natural-language question, and external knowledge (implicit knowledge from GPT-3 and explicit knowledge from WikiText-2) to produce a balanced context graph with 300-dimensional node and edge embeddings. The graph is translated into a natural-language context description via an off-the-shelf graph-to-caption model, and 20 in-context examples are retrieved from the training set. The final output is the VQA answer generated by an LLM (GPT-3, GPT-4, Llama2, or LLaVA-1.5) prompted with the balanced context and diverse examples.

## Method

GRACE operates in two stages. First, a variational autoencoder with an AGRU encoder fuses visual features and external knowledge into a context graph, trained unsupervised with two novel fairness losses: a semantic fairness loss that minimizes the Wasserstein distance between feature distributions of sensitive groups (e.g., male vs. female), and a structural fairness loss that maximizes meta-path diversity via Simpson's Index of Diversity, ensuring balanced graph topology. Second, in-context examples are retrieved using a combined score of graph-based semantic similarity (average pairwise cosine similarity of node features) and reasoning similarity (SimRank over compact subgraphs obtained by pruning nodes whose attention weights from a symbolic reasoning network fall below a threshold), weighted by λ=2.33. This dual-similarity retrieval yields more diverse examples than feature-similarity-only baselines, and the balanced context graph ensures the LLM receives fair, under-represented concepts rather than dominant dataset associations.

## Results

GRACE achieves the best WEAT scores (lower is better) across all three datasets: 1.52 on OK-VQA, 1.51 on VQA-CP, and 1.63 on GQA-OOD, outperforming all baselines. On out-of-distribution benchmarks, GRACE attains 57.35% accuracy on VQA-CP (vs. 53.74% for the best baseline PromptCap) and 50.14% tail accuracy on GQA-OOD with a head-tail gap of only 7.49% (vs. 8.81% for PromptCap). On the in-distribution OK-VQA benchmark, GRACE achieves a competitive 60.32% accuracy, slightly below Prophet (61.11%) and PromptCap (60.47%), indicating minimal accuracy cost for the fairness gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRACE outperforms all five LLM-based knowledge VQA baselines (PICa, KAT, REVIVE, PromptCap, Prophet) on WEAT across every dataset, with the largest margin over Prophet on GQA-OOD (1.63 vs. 1.78). On VQA-CP accuracy, GRACE leads by 3.61 points over PromptCap (57.35% vs. 53.74%) and by 3.94 points over Prophet (57.35% vs. 53.41%), while on GQA-OOD tail accuracy it surpasses PromptCap by 0.41 points and reduces the head-tail gap from 8.81% to 7.49%. On in-distribution OK-VQA, GRACE trails Prophet by 0.79 points (60.32% vs. 61.11%) and PromptCap by 0.15 points, a small trade-off for substantially better fairness and OOD generalization. Against the non-LLM baseline LXMERT, GRACE improves VQA-CP accuracy by 13.21 points (57.35% vs. 44.14%) and GQA-OOD tail accuracy by 4.04 points. When conventional debiasing methods (LMH, CSS) are applied on top of GRACE, WEAT further drops to 1.44 on OK-VQA and VQA-CP and VQA-CP accuracy rises to 61.37%, whereas the same methods applied to Prophet degrade OK-VQA accuracy from 61.11% to 57.28%, confirming that GRACE's graph-based approach is more compatible with additional debiasing.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRACE extends the in-context learning paradigm for knowledge-based VQA (Prophet, PromptCap, PICa) by replacing free-form captioning with an explicit graph representation of visual and external knowledge, learned via a VAE with fairness-aware regularization. It departs from prior VQA debiasing methods (LMH, CSS) that operate on dataset-level biases through counterfactual synthesis or ensemble learning, instead targeting biases in the context representation and in-context example retrieval pipeline that are specific to LLM-based prompting. The dual similarity retrieval (graph-based semantic similarity plus SimRank-based reasoning similarity over a symbolic reasoning subgraph) is a novel combination not present in prior retrieval-based VQA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underexplored failure mode—bias inheritance through in-context examples and biased context descriptions in LLM-based VQA—and provides a generally applicable graph-based remedy that can be plugged into existing prompting pipelines. The gender gap reduction (from ~4.4% to 0.9% on VQA-CP) and the finding that GRACE without additional debiasing outperforms Prophet+LMH+CSS on OOD datasets suggest that addressing bias at the representation level is more effective than post-hoc ensemble corrections. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to sensitive attributes beyond gender, how it scales to larger knowledge bases, and whether the symbolic reasoning component can be replaced by learned alternatives.
