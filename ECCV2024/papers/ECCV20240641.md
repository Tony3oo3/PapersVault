## Problem

Point-based crowd counting methods rely on the Hungarian algorithm to match predicted point proposals to ground truth targets, but this matching is unstable across training epochs—different proposals are assigned to the same target in successive iterations. This instability arises because no explicit learning signal guides the network toward consistent proposal selection, resulting in vague per-proposal objectives and localized under- or over-estimation in crowded scenes.

## Input / Output

The method ingests a single RGB crowd image (longer side capped at 1920 px for large datasets) and produces two outputs: a scalar crowd count (sum of proposal confidence scores) and a set of 2D point coordinates for each detected person. Proposals are generated on a stride-8 feature grid with K reference points per pixel (K=4 for most datasets, K=8 for UCF-QNRF), and each proposal yields a confidence score plus (Δx, Δy) offsets scaled by γ=100 to recover absolute coordinates.

## Method

APGCC introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of the P2PNet point-proposal framework. First, Auxiliary Point Guidance (APG) randomly generates k_pos positive points (within ±n_pos pixels of each GT) and k_neg negative points (between n_pos and n_neg pixels away) during training; the network is penalized if positive auxiliary points do not receive near-1 confidence with offsets matching the perturbation, and if negative auxiliary points do not receive near-0 confidence with near-zero offsets, thereby teaching the network a stable positive/negative boundary that reduces matching instability. Second, because these auxiliary points fall at arbitrary sub-pixel locations not aligned to the feature grid, the paper proposes Implicit Feature Interpolation (IFI): an MLP takes the four nearest latent features, their Euclidean distances to the query point, and a sinusoidal positional encoding of those distances, then produces a weighted interpolated feature vector. The full pipeline uses a VGG-16 backbone (conv3/conv4 features), ASPP for multi-scale enrichment, IFI for arbitrary-position feature lookup, and a shared four-layer MLP head for confidence and offset prediction, trained with the standard point-based loss plus the APG auxiliary loss (λ₅=0.2).

## Results

On the four primary counting benchmarks, APGCC achieves MAE of 48.8 (SHHA), 5.6 (SHHB), 80.1 (UCF-QNRF), and 54.3 (JHU-Crowd++), setting new state-of-the-art on three of four datasets. On the challenging UCF_CC_50 (50-image) benchmark, APGCC reaches MAE 154.8 / MSE 205.5, and on the large-scale NWPU-Crowd dataset it achieves MAE 71.7 / MSE 284.4. For localization, APGCC attains F1 of 76.4% (large threshold) and 68.9% (small threshold) on SHHA, and 48.7% (σ=4) and 78.4% (σ=8) on NWPU-Crowd, outperforming all prior point-based and detection-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APGCC outperforms the strongest point-based competitor PET [24] on SHHA (MAE 48.8 vs 49.3), SHHB (5.6 vs 6.1), and JHU-Crowd++ (54.3 vs 58.5), but is marginally behind PET on UCF-QNRF (80.1 vs 79.5 MAE). Against the original P2PNet [43] framework it builds upon, APGCC reduces SHHA MAE by 3.9 points (52.7→48.8) and SHHB MAE by 0.6 (6.2→5.6). All map-based methods (HMoDE, AMSNet, ChfL, etc.) and detection-based methods (LSC-CNN, TopoCount) are surpassed on every dataset where they report results. On the UCF_CC_50 benchmark, APGCC (MAE 154.8) beats PET (159.9) and P2PNet (172.7); on NWPU-Crowd it leads PET (71.7 vs 74.4) and CLTR (71.7 vs 74.3). For localization on SHHA, APGCC achieves F1 of 76.4% (large) and 68.9% (small), exceeding PET (74.2% / 67.5%) and P2PNet (71.2% / 67.5%); on NWPU-Crowd it reaches F1 48.7% (σ=4) and 78.4% (σ=8), surpassing CLTR (43.2% / 74.2%) and P2PNet (40.6% / 74.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the P2PNet point-based crowd counting framework [43], which established the Hungarian-algorithm-based proposal-target matching paradigm, and extends it with two targeted components: Auxiliary Point Guidance (APG) to stabilize the matching process during training, and Implicit Feature Interpolation (IFI) to extract features at arbitrary sub-pixel positions. The IFI component draws conceptually from the implicit neural representation literature (NeRF, DeepSDF) and adapts MLP-based continuous feature interpolation with positional encoding to the crowd counting setting. The paper positions itself against the broader taxonomy of map-based, detection-based, and point-based crowd counting methods, specifically targeting the matching instability problem identified in prior point-based work (P2PNet, CLTR, PET) without altering the inference architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of proposal-target matching instability as a concrete, measurable bottleneck (via the Instability Rate metric) in point-based crowd counting is a useful diagnostic that the field had not previously formalized. The APG mechanism is a training-only intervention with zero inference overhead, making it a low-cost drop-in improvement for existing point-based pipelines. However, the absolute gains are modest (e.g., 48.84 vs. 49.3 MAE on SHHA over PET), the backbone is the relatively dated VGG-16, and it remains open whether the matching instability is the dominant limiting factor or one of several interacting issues. Future work could explore whether APG generalizes to other matching-based detection tasks or whether more modern backbones and architectures yield larger absolute improvements.
