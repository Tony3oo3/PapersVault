## Problem

Existing automatic metrics evaluate audio or video quality in isolation, and prior AV synchrony methods (AVST, SparseSync, Diff-Foley) reduce the problem to detecting a single temporal offset. There is no reference-free, interpretable metric that captures the full range of synchronization issues humans perceive in 'in the wild' videos, including speed changes, intermittent muting, fragment shuffling, and flickering.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip and its paired audio track, extracting features over fixed 0.96-second windows (I3D yields 1024-dim visual vectors; VGGish yields 128-dim acoustic vectors). It produces a single scalar score on a 1-to-5 perceptual scale where 5 indicates perfect synchrony and 1 indicates severe desynchronization, with intermediate levels mapped to predefined distortion severity guidelines provided to human raters. The metric is reference-free, requiring no ground-truth video or audio for scoring.

## Method

PEAVS uses frozen I3D and VGGish backbones, projects visual features to 128 dimensions via a 1D convolution, and feeds both streams into cross-modal transformer layers where the Query of one modality attends to the Key and Value of the other, followed by self-attention refinement. The averaged self-attention outputs from both branches are concatenated and passed through a 3-layer MLP to produce the final score. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 pre-trains with a contrastive loss on ~190K AudioSet clips subjected to ten levels of temporal misalignment, embedding temporal regularity into the frozen features; Stage 2 fine-tunes the full network on the 10.6K-video AVS benchmark using concordance correlation coefficient (CCC) as the regression objective to maximize agreement with human 1–5 ratings. The key design choice is the cross-modal attention mechanism, which explicitly models inter-modality interactions rather than treating audio and video as independent streams, and the interpretable output scale is directly anchored to perceptual guidelines used during annotation.

## Results

PEAVS achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.794 at the set level and 0.536 at the clip level with human-annotated synchrony scores on the AVS Benchmark test set. This represents a 51% relative improvement over the FAVD baseline (0.527 set-level PCC), which itself outperforms FAD (0.475) by 11% and FVD (0.321) by 64%. Ablations confirm that contrastive pre-training contributes a 7% set-level gain (0.736→0.794) and cross-modal transformer layers contribute a 9.4% set-level gain (0.720→0.794) over their respective ablated variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEAVS substantially outperforms all three baselines. Against FVD (Unterthiner et al., NeurIPS 2018), PEAVS achieves 0.794 vs. 0.321 set-level PCC — a 147% relative gain — confirming that video-only Fréchet statistics are poorly suited to capturing cross-modal synchrony. Against FAD (Kilgour et al., Interspeech 2019), the gap is 0.794 vs. 0.475, a 67% relative improvement, showing that audio-only statistics similarly miss synchronization cues. Against SparseSync (Iashin et al., BMVC 2022), PEAVS achieves 50.5% vs. 42.3% accuracy on a 400-video binary classification task (clean vs. shifted), with PEAVS showing a 136% higher rate of correctly identifying clean content while SparseSync has an 18% edge in detecting distortions. The FAVD metric, introduced as a natural extension of Fréchet-based approaches to the joint audio-visual space, serves as the strongest reference-free baseline and is itself 11% and 64% better than FAD and FVD respectively, validating the utility of joint AV covariance modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AV synchrony evaluation line (AVST, SparseSync, SyncNet) which has focused almost exclusively on temporal offset detection, by broadening the problem to nine distortion types (speed changes, muting, shuffling, flickering, gaps) and reframing the output as a perceptual 5-point score rather than a binary or regression target. Architecturally, it repurposes the cross-modal transformer formulation from multimodal NLP [Tsai et al.] and the frozen I3D/VGGish backbones from FVD/FAD, adding a contrastive pre-training stage inspired by SyncNet. The FAVD baseline is a natural Fréchet-distance extension of FAD and FVD to the joint audio-visual embedding space, filling a gap the authors identify in the literature where prior AV generation works (MM-Diffusion, Diff-Foley) had to rely on single-modality metrics or train ad-hoc classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100+ hour, 120K-annotation AVS benchmark is a practically valuable resource for the growing AV generation community that currently lacks a standardized synchrony evaluation protocol. However, the benchmark's scope is constrained: only 200 source videos covering 40% of AudioSet labels, no talking-face content, and a $30K annotation budget limit generalizability. Open questions include whether PEAVS transfers to speech-centric or face-centric AV content, whether pre-training on the full distortion taxonomy (rather than just audio shift) further improves correlation, and whether the moderate clip-level Pearson of 0.536 is sufficient for fine-grained model comparison in practice.
