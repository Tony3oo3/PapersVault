## Problem

Existing video summarization methods either rely on hierarchical structures that are sensitive to imprecise sequence segmentation, or adopt parallel modeling of global and local cues with simple fusion strategies that ignore the potential correspondence between temporal cues at different granularity. This lack of explicit modeling of cross-granularity contextual relationships limits the model's ability to achieve detailed video understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242332/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sub-sampled at 2 fps and extracts per-frame visual features from the penultimate layer (pool5) of a GoogLeNet pre-trained on ImageNet, yielding a T×D feature matrix. It outputs frame-level importance scores via a prediction head, which are then averaged within KTS-detected shots to produce shot-level scores. A final 0/1 knapsack formulation solved by dynamic programming selects the summary at a configurable length percentage (e.g., 15% of total duration).

## Method

Bgm4Video comprises a Temporal Encoder (TE) and a Bidirectional Graph Transmission (BGT) module. The TE simultaneously aggregates coarse-grained context via self-attention over the full sequence and fine-grained context via depth-separable temporal convolutions with window sizes {3, 5}, all operating in a shared representation space. The BGT module then models cross-granularity correspondence by computing bidirectional L2-norm pairwise similarities between each pair of feature streams (X_g↔X_l^(1), X_g↔X_l^(2), X_l^(1)↔X_l^(2)), selecting top-L (L=3) nearest neighbors to form graph edges, and performing softmax-weighted message passing with residual connections in both directions. A split-transform-merge convolution further refines the aggregated features before a linear prediction head produces importance scores. The model is trained end-to-end with MSE loss using Adam (lr=5e-5, 300 epochs, batch size 1) under 5-fold cross-validation, with no sequence segmentation hyperparameter required.

## Results

Under the standard 5-fold cross-validation setting, Bgm4Video achieves an F-score of 0.517 on SumMe and 0.612 on TVSum, outperforming all 15 compared baselines on both datasets. The model uses only 7.62M parameters, substantially fewer than VJMHT (35.44M), SUM-FCN (36.58M), and SUM-GAN (295.86M). In the rank-based evaluation on TVSum, Bgm4Video reaches Kendall's τ of 0.169 and Spearman's ρ of 0.221, approaching the human-annotated upper bound (τ=0.177, ρ=0.204) while the next-best method (VJMHT) achieves only τ=0.097 and ρ=0.105.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bgm4Video achieves the highest F-score on both SumMe (0.517) and TVSum (0.612), beating the closest competitor LMHA [61] by 0.006 and 0.002 respectively, and VJMHT [19] by 0.011 and 0.003. Against hierarchical methods (HSA-RNN 0.423/0.587, RSGN 0.450/0.601, LMHA 0.511/0.610), the parallel modeling approach avoids the segmentation-sensitivity issue and yields consistent gains. Compared to the parallel-modeling CSNet [17] (0.486/0.585), Bgm4Video improves by 0.031 on SumMe and 0.027 on TVSum, attributed to the removal of a manual segmentation hyperparameter. In the more reliable rank-based evaluation (Table 4), Bgm4Video (τ=0.169, ρ=0.221) nearly matches the human upper bound (τ=0.177, ρ=0.204) and surpasses the next-best VJMHT (τ=0.097, ρ=0.105) by roughly 70% in Kendall's τ. Notably, Bgm4Video uses only 7.62M parameters, about 4.6× fewer than VJMHT and 39× fewer than SUM-GAN, while still achieving the top F-scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the graph-matching concept from structured object similarity and cross-modal retrieval into the video summarization setting, specifically to model correspondence between multi-granularity temporal cues. It departs from hierarchical summarization frameworks (HSA-RNN, RSGN, LMHA) that rely on sequence segmentation for local modeling, and from parallel approaches (CSNet) that use pre-set segmentation hyperparameters, by combining self-attention and multi-scale temporal convolution in a segmentation-free parallel architecture. The bidirectional graph transmission module is the key novel addition, drawing on k-NN-based graph construction and message passing to refine cross-granularity representations without requiring explicit shot boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a previously underexplored aspect of video summarization—explicitly modeling the correspondence between coarse- and fine-grained temporal cues—which provides a principled justification for why simple fusion of global and local features is insufficient. The notably large gap in rank-based evaluation (Kendall's τ nearly doubled over the strongest baseline) suggests the method captures relative importance ordering far better than prior work, even when F-score gains are modest. However, the reliance on GoogLeNet features, the weaker transfer-setting results, and the fixed k-NN neighborhood size limit the practical ceiling and generalizability. Open questions include whether the graph-matching refinement scales to much longer videos and whether the approach benefits from modern visual backbones.
