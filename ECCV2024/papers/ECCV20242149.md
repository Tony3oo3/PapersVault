## Problem

Passive non-line-of-sight (NLOS) imaging from penumbra photographs has been limited to reconstructing only the light-reflecting (non-occluding) portion of a hidden scene, requiring the occluder's shape to be known, approximately planar, or pre-calibrated. No prior method can simultaneously recover the 3D geometry of hidden light-occluding objects and the 2D image of the non-occluding scene from a single ordinary photograph of a soft shadow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242149/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D RGB photograph (4-megapixel in experiments) of a penumbra cast on a visible wall and produces two outputs: a 3D point cloud (2,048 points) of the hidden occluder, converted to a mesh via a learned SDF network and marching cubes, and a 2D radiosity image of the non-occluding hidden scene plane. The reconstruction is constrained to a computational FOV defined as a frustum between the camera's field of view and a fixed-depth hidden scene plane, and the 3D shape is generated view-consistently but must be localized via a grid search over bounding boxes since the diffusion model does not predict absolute position.

## Method

The core reformulation decomposes the hidden scene into light-occluding and non-light-occluding components, casting the inverse problem as a separable non-linear least squares (SNLLS) system where occlusion is modeled via a pinspeck (anti-pinhole) visibility function. The primary solution, Soft Shadow Diffusion (SSD), is a conditional denoising diffusion model with a UNet-based 1D-convolution architecture that generates 3D point clouds from a CrossFormer-encoded latent of the penumbra photograph, trained on ~260,000 simulated ShapeNet instances. At inference, a three-stage pipeline operates: (1) conditional diffusion samples the occluder's 3D shape, (2) a physics-based grid search localizes the shape within the hidden volume, and (3) a TV-regularized least-squares inversion recovers the 2D non-occluding radiosity using the mesh-rendered soft shadow. A complementary gradient-based method solves the same SNLLS problem via alternating minimization over a uniform voxel grid with sigmoid-relaxed binary constraints.

## Results

The paper reports MSE for 2D non-occluding scene reconstructions and Chamfer Distance (CD) for 3D occluder reconstructions, evaluated under varying background illumination (SBR) and measurement noise. Key quantitative findings show that 3D reconstructions remain accurate even at SBRs as low as ~10 dB, while 2D reconstructions degrade gradually below ~20 dB SBR. SSD demonstrates similar robustness to measurement noise, with 3D reconstruction quality relatively unaffected even at SNRs as low as 10 dB. The paper also reports that SSD generalizes to unseen 3D shape classes in both simulation and real-world experiments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper explicitly states that direct and fair comparisons with prior methods are not possible because prior work operates under fundamentally different acquisition scenarios (video frames, pre-calibrated occluder visibility functions, known occluder shapes, or fully known occluders). The authors note they are solving a strictly harder problem (joint 3D occluder + 2D non-occluder reconstruction from a single photograph) and that their 2D reconstructions are, in expectation, no better than existing methods. The only internal comparison is between the two proposed methods: SSD (physics-inspired neural network) versus the gradient-based alternating minimization (Algorithm 1). SSD outperforms the gradient-based approach on complex occluder shapes, as the voxelized gradient method does not scale well beyond coarse discretizations (e.g., 10×5×10) due to computational and memory demands. The gradient-based method is shown to struggle with non-planar occluders (Figure 5), while SSD produces plausible 3D meshes even for complex shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends computational periscopy (Saunders et al. [52]) from 2D reconstruction of light-reflecting hidden scenes to joint 3D reconstruction of hidden occluders, by reformulating the inverse problem as a separable non-linear least squares (SNLLS) problem built on a pinspeck occluder model. The 3D reconstruction leverages denoising diffusion probabilistic models (Ho et al. [18], Song & Ermon [60]) adapted for conditional point cloud generation (Luo et al. [36]), with the diffusion process conditioned on a CrossFormer-encoded soft shadow photograph. The physics-inspired inversion pipeline unifies the learned shape prior (SSD) with a physics-based localization step adapted from [52] and a TV-regularized 2D reconstruction, creating a hybrid model that neither pure optimization nor pure learning achieves alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly reconstruct 3D occluder geometry and 2D non-occluder radiance from a single passive penumbra photograph, removing the prior-knowledge requirement (known occluder shape or calibration) that limited all earlier passive NLOS approaches. The surprising robustness of 3D reconstructions to noise (10 dB SNR) and ambient background illumination, combined with simulation-to-real generalization, suggests the strong shape prior from diffusion is doing substantial work. Key open questions include: the gradient-based method's inability to scale beyond simple shapes, the need for a grid search over bounding boxes because SSD does not predict occluder location, and the assumption of known occluder size in uncontrolled real-world scenarios.
