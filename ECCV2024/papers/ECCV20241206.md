## Problem

Existing transformer-based image restoration methods rely on self-attention for global modeling, which incurs O(N²) computational cost and fails to exploit the common frequency-domain prior shared across diverse degradation types (e.g., rain and blur manifest in high frequencies, while haze and low-light manifest in low frequencies). This leads to suboptimal performance on specific tasks and an unfavorable tradeoff between accuracy, parameter count, and FLOPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single degraded RGB image of arbitrary spatial resolution (H×W×3) and outputs a restored RGB image of the same resolution via residual addition (x̂ = x + s). Training is performed on 256×256 patches with a 5-stage hierarchical encoder-decoder that progressively downsamples and upsamples spatial resolution by factors of 2. FLOPs are reported at a fixed 256×256 input resolution for fair cross-method comparison.

## Method

SFHformer replaces vanilla self-attention with a Spatial-Frequency Hybrid structure inside the Local-Global Perception Mixer (LGPM): a spatial branch (depth-wise + dilated convolutions) captures local and regional features, while a frequency branch applies 2D FFT, then Frequency-Conditional Positional Encoding (FCPE) to assign unique identities to each frequency component, and Frequency Dynamic Convolution (FDC) to learn input-adaptive convolution weights per frequency coordinate, before IFFT returns to the spatial domain. The Multi-kernel ConvFFN (MCFN) aggregates multi-scale local-global features using parallel depth-wise convolutions of varying kernel sizes. Training uses a dual-domain loss combining spatial L1 and frequency-domain L1 (weighted by λ=0.1), and the fully-convolutional design employs batch normalization rather than layer normalization. The FFT-based global modeling reduces complexity from O(N²) to O(N log N), yielding a 3.87M-parameter model with 26.59G FLOPs that achieves SOTA results across 31 datasets spanning 10 restoration tasks.

## Results

SFHformer achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 31 benchmark datasets spanning 10 restoration tasks. On deraining, it reaches 41.85 dB PSNR on Rain200L and 50.11 dB on SPA-Data, surpassing DRSformer by 0.62 and 1.58 dB respectively. On dehazing, it attains 43.03 dB PSNR on SOTS-indoor and 38.83 dB on SOTS-outdoor, outperforming MB-TaylorFormer by 0.39 and 0.74 dB. On motion deblurring (GoPro), it achieves 34.01 dB PSNR, a 1.09 dB gain over Restormer, while on low-light enhancement (LOL-v2 real) it reaches 23.78 dB PSNR, exceeding Retinexformer by 0.98 dB—all with only 3.87M–7.63M parameters and 26.59G–50.59G FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFHformer consistently outperforms all 63 compared baselines across every evaluated task and dataset. On deraining, it surpasses the previous SOTA DRSformer [16] by 0.62 dB (Rain200L) and 1.58 dB (SPA-Data) PSNR while using only 22.6% of its parameters and 20.8% of its FLOPs. On dehazing, it beats MB-TaylorFormer [65] by 0.39–0.74 dB PSNR on SOTS with 52.1% fewer parameters and 30.2% fewer FLOPs. On motion deblurring, it gains 1.09 dB PSNR over Restormer [98] on GoPro and 0.04 dB over GRL [44] on HIDE. On low-light enhancement, it exceeds Retinexformer [11] by 0.98 dB PSNR on LOL-v2-real with 64.6% fewer parameters. On underwater enhancement, it outperforms URSCT-SESR [71] by 0.87 dB (LSUI) and 0.82 dB (UIEB) PSNR with only 34.4% of the parameters. On desnowing and raindrop removal, it achieves the best PSNR/SSIM on all datasets, beating IRNeXt [21] by 0.16 dB on CSD and RainDropDiff [57] by 0.67 dB on Raindrop-A.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFHformer sits at the intersection of the MetaFormer paradigm (Yu et al., 2022), which demonstrated that self-attention can be replaced by alternative token mixers, and the growing line of frequency-domain restoration methods (FFC, GFNet, SFNet). Rather than using FFT as a complement to attention or as a filtering mechanism, the paper repurposes 2D FFT as the primary global token mixer, replacing self-attention entirely, and introduces frequency-conditional positional encoding (FCPE) and frequency dynamic convolution (FDC) to handle per-frequency-component processing. The spatial-frequency hybrid structure in LGPM and the multi-kernel ConvFFN draw from Inception-style multi-scale learning and depth-wise/dilated convolution, while the dual-domain (spatial + frequency) L1 loss is a straightforward extension of prior frequency-aware losses. Conceptually, the paper's key departure is providing a degradation-physics motivation for why frequency features serve as a restoration prior, going beyond the purely computational-efficiency rationale used by prior frequency-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, efficient backbone (3.87M params, 26.59G FLOPs) that achieves SOTA across 10 restoration tasks, making it a strong default baseline for the field. The frequency-prior analysis—showing that rain/blur manifest in high frequencies while haze/low-light manifest in low frequencies—provides a unifying interpretive lens that could inform future architecture design beyond this specific model. However, the model still requires task-specific training (no single model handles all 10 tasks simultaneously), and the frequency-domain analysis, while insightful, remains largely qualitative. Open questions include whether the FFT-based global mixer generalizes to video restoration or higher-resolution settings where the O(N log N) advantage becomes more pronounced, and whether the FCPE/FDC design is truly necessary or whether simpler frequency-domain operations would suffice.
