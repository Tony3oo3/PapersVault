## Problem

In-domain image animation methods (e.g., for faces, fluids, hair) offer precise control but are limited to specific object categories, while diffusion-based image-to-video models are general but produce only weak, text-guided or idle motions with no fine-grained spatial control. The paper asks whether a single unified framework can support meticulous, multi-domain motion control (trajectories, facial landmarks, camera motion) over arbitrary in-the-wild images within a frozen video diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (256×256 at training, up to 1024×1024 at inference) plus sparse motion control signals: hand-crafted trajectories (P joint points per trajectory), facial landmark sequences (K 2D points per frame), or a binary motion mask for regional control. Audio can drive facial landmarks via SadTalker as an upstream module. The output is a 14-frame video clip (extendable to longer sequences via periodic sampling with 7-frame overlap) along with intermediate dense optical flow fields. Multiple sparse control signals from different domains can be combined simultaneously without retraining.

## Method

MOFA-Adapter is a trainable module attached to a frozen Stable Video Diffusion model, following the ControlNet injection pattern. Its core mechanism is a sparse-to-dense (S2D) network (borrowed from CMP) that converts sparse motion hints into dense per-frame optical flow, which then warps multi-scale features extracted from the reference image via a Conv-SiLU-ZeroConv encoder; the warped features are injected into a trainable copy of the SVD encoder whose outputs are added to the frozen decoder. Different motion domains (open-domain trajectories, facial landmarks) are unified as sparse motion vector problems, enabling separate domain-specific adapters that can be combined zero-shot. Training proceeds in two stages: first as a flow-based reconstruction model without the S2D network, then fine-tuning with the S2D module added. A periodic sampling strategy (14-frame groups with 7-frame latent overlap, averaging predicted noise across overlapping frames) extends generation beyond the base 14-frame limit while maintaining temporal consistency.

## Results

For trajectory-based image animation on WebVid-10M, MOFA-Video achieves FID 16.82 and FVD 86.76, outperforming DragNUWA (FID 19.66, FVD 91.38) while also improving LPIPS from 0.2705 to 0.2274 and frame consistency from 0.9302 to 0.9390. For portrait animation, the method achieves CPBD 0.4075 and identity preservation (ID) 0.9293, surpassing both SadTalker (CPBD 0.3218, ID 0.9188) and StyleHEAT (CPBD 0.2577, ID 0.7993). User studies confirm the advantage, with MOFA-Video scoring 3.58/5 on control precision vs. DragNUWA's 2.76/5, and 4.8/5 on fidelity vs. SadTalker's 4.15/5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DragNUWA on trajectory-based animation, MOFA-Video wins on every metric: FID drops from 19.66 to 16.82, FVD from 91.38 to 86.76, and LPIPS from 0.2705 to 0.2274, with user preference for control precision jumping from 2.76 to 3.58 out of 5. The key qualitative advantage is that DragNUWA incorrectly propagates motion to background regions (e.g., the beach moves when only the ship should), while MOFA-Video's explicit dense optical flow constrains motion to the intended region. For portrait animation, MOFA-Video outperforms SadTalker on all five metrics (CPBD +0.086, ID +0.011, fidelity +0.65, naturalness +0.85, visual quality +0.55) and dominates StyleHEAT even more decisively (CPBD +0.150, ID +0.130). Compared to MotionCtrl for camera motion, the paper reports comparable results with fewer visual artifacts and larger motion magnitudes, though this comparison is qualitative only. The method also uniquely supports zero-shot combination of multiple adapters (e.g., facial landmarks + trajectories simultaneously) without retraining, a capability none of the baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOFA-Video extends the ControlNet paradigm of attaching trainable adapters to a frozen diffusion model, but repurposes it for video generation with explicit motion field control rather than spatial conditioning. The sparse-to-dense motion generator borrows its architecture from CMP (Zhan et al., 2019), and the base model is the frozen Stable Video Diffusion (Blattmann et al., 2023). The key departure from prior controllable I2V methods (DragNUWA, MotionCtrl) is the unification of multiple sparse motion domains—hand-crafted trajectories, facial landmarks, camera motion—under a single flow-based representation, enabling zero-shot combination of domain-specific adapters without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot composability of MOFA-Adapters (e.g., simultaneously controlling facial expression and background object motion) offers a practical path toward multi-signal video animation without the combinatorial training cost of joint models. However, the method remains constrained by SVD's 14-frame limit (mitigated but not eliminated by periodic sampling), cannot generate content substantially different from the input image, and produces artifacts under large motion guidance. Open questions include whether the sparse-to-dense formulation generalizes to 3D motion or multi-object interactions, and whether the domain-specific tuning requirement can be relaxed through a truly universal adapter.
