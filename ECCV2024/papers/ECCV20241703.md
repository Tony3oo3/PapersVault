## Problem

Natural behavior is inherently hierarchical—composed of nested subroutines from atomic movemes to stereotyped actions to species-typical activities—yet existing skeletal action benchmarks focus on single-level granularity and SOTA supervised methods have saturated on popular datasets. There is a notable absence of hierarchical action segmentation benchmarks and of unsupervised models that learn interpretable, multi-scale behavioral representations from raw pose data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241703/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequences of 2D or 3D joint coordinates (25–26 keypoints per individual, variable-length trajectories) that are patched into space-time cubes before embedding. For multi-individual scenarios (e.g., MABe22 mice triplets), the keypoint dimension is expanded by stacking individuals. The output is a set of hierarchical behavioral embeddings—one per encoder block, each at a progressively coarser spatio-temporal resolution—plus reconstructed pose coordinates for masked tokens; downstream evaluation uses linear probing on these frozen embeddings, with average pooling across body parts or individuals when multiple embeddings exist per frame.

## Method

hBehaveMAE generalizes the Hiera hierarchical ViT to the spatio-temporal domain by organizing the encoder into at least three blocks with progressively increasing layers, hidden dimensions, and attention heads, connected by spatio-temporal fusion operators (temporal, spatial, or joint) implemented via query pooling attention. Masking is applied at the highest scale using a block-masking scheme and projected down to lower levels to maintain consistent fusion throughout the forward pass. Local self-attention is used in the initial block to capture fine-grained kinematics, while later blocks employ global attention for coarser behavioral context. A single-layer transformer decoder reconstructs masked pose coordinates with an L2 loss, and the model is trained entirely self-supervised (no auxiliary contrastive objectives or domain-specific features). The key finding is that this architectural hierarchy induces a topographic mapping: lower blocks best decode movemes, middle blocks decode actions, and upper blocks decode activities, a property absent in the non-hierarchical BehaveMAE variant with the same total depth.

## Results

On the novel Shot7M2 benchmark, hBehaveMAE achieves 64.7% All F1, with 67.3% on movemes, 58.5% on actions, and 80.9% on activities, outperforming the non-hierarchical BehaveMAE (64.3% All F1) and all other baselines. On MABe22 mice triplets, hBehaveMAE reaches 30.7% All F1 (inductive) and 32.9% (transductive), surpassing the previous SOTA BAMS (29.9% / 30.8%) without any domain-specific feature extraction. On hBABEL, hBehaveMAE achieves the best Top-60 sequence-level F1 (10.9%) while BehaveMAE† leads on Top-10 sequence-level (23.4%), with both substantially outperforming BAMS (6.0% / 15.5% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Shot7M2, hBehaveMAE (64.7% All F1) beats the non-hierarchical BehaveMAE (64.3%) and all other baselines, with the largest margins on activities (+4.2% over BehaveMAE, +12.8% over PCA-30) and movemes (+6.3% over BehaveMAE). PCA-30 is surprisingly competitive (61.9% All F1), while BAMS (53.7%) and TS2Vec (59.6%) underperform despite incorporating temporal hierarchy. On MABe22, hBehaveMAE (30.7% All F1 inductive) outperforms BAMS (29.9%), T-BERT (29.7%), T-PointNet (28.2%), and T-Perceiver (27.1%) without using any additional behavioral features or contrastive objectives. On hBABEL, the picture is more nuanced: BehaveMAE† leads on Top-10 sequence-level F1 (23.4% vs. 22.8% for hBehaveMAE), but hBehaveMAE achieves the best Top-60 sequence-level score (10.9% vs. 10.4%), while BAMS struggles significantly (15.5% Top-10 sequence), likely due to optimization difficulties on this dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the Hiera hierarchical vision transformer (Ryali et al., ICML 2023) from purely spatial token merging to a spatio-temporal setting, introducing temporal, spatial, and spatio-temporal fusion operators via query pooling attention. The non-hierarchical BehaveMAE variant is a direct adaptation of spatiotemporal video MAEs (Feichtenhofer et al., NeurIPS 2022) to skeletal pose trajectories, replacing image patches with pose-space-time cubes. Conceptually, the work departs from prior behavior-decomposition methods (MOSQ, B-SOId, BAMS, TS2Vec) that either operate at a single fixed time-scale or learn multi-scale representations through contrastive objectives, instead using a single masked-reconstruction objective within a hierarchically structured encoder to capture multiple behavioral granularities simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Shot7M2 and hBABEL fills a clear gap: no prior skeletal benchmark provides multi-level (moveme/action/activity) annotations suitable for hierarchical action segmentation, and the synthetic nature of Shot7M2 enables precise ground-truth at all three levels. The finding that a topographic mapping from encoder depth to behavioral granularity emerges without explicit hierarchical supervision is a useful inductive-bias result for the field. Key limitations include the synthetic (and potentially unnatural) movements in Shot7M2, the two-level-only hierarchy in hBABEL, and the requirement that the structural hierarchy (number of blocks, fusion strides) be pre-defined by the user. Open questions include whether the functional hierarchy can be discovered end-to-end, whether the approach scales to multi-agent interactions with more than a few individuals, and whether the modest performance gains over the non-hierarchical variant grow with dataset complexity.
