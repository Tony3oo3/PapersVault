## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time novel view synthesis but is limited to static scenes. Existing dynamic extensions either share a single canonical representation across all frames (degrading quality on long sequences with large motion or topological changes) or use per-frame representations that lack temporal consistency. The paper addresses how to reconstruct arbitrary-length dynamic sequences with high rendering quality while avoiding both intra-window flickering and inter-window temporal inconsistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences from calibrated time-synchronized cameras (e.g., 2028×2704 at 30 FPS for Neural 3D Video, 2048×1088 for Technicolor) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and a COLMAP point cloud for initialization. RAFT optical flow is computed per camera view to drive adaptive window partitioning before training. The output is a set of per-window 3D Gaussian Splatting models (each containing a canonical Gaussian set plus a deformation MLP) that collectively enable real-time novel view rendering at 71.51 FPS (Neural 3D Video) and 23.79 FPS (Technicolor), viewable in an interactive dynamic viewer.

## Method

SWinGS partitions the input sequence into sliding windows of adaptive length using a greedy algorithm over accumulated RAFT optical flow magnitude, with one overlapping frame between adjacent windows. Each window is trained as an independent dynamic 3DGS model: a small MLP (depth 4, width 16, 6 sinusoidal frequency components) learns a temporally-local deformation field mapping from a per-window canonical 3D Gaussian set to per-frame Gaussians (displacements in position, rotation, and scaling). To handle static/dynamic imbalance, the MLP is made tunable via M sets of weights blended by per-Gaussian learnable parameters α, initialized from 2D projection-based motion labels and optimized jointly. After independent per-window training (15K iterations with a 2K warm-up phase), a sequential fine-tuning stage (3K iterations per window) enforces inter-window temporal consistency by minimizing L1 loss on overlapping-frame renders from randomly sampled novel views (interpolated in SE(3)), while freezing the deformation MLP and only updating the canonical representation.

## Results

On the Neural 3D Video dataset (averaged over 6 scenes, 300 frames at 1014×1352), SWinGS achieves the best PSNR (31.10 dB), SSIM (0.940), LPIPS (0.096), and rendering speed (71.51 FPS) among all compared methods. On the Technicolor dataset at full resolution (2048×1088), it attains PSNR 33.65 dB, SSIM 0.934, and LPIPS 0.117, outperforming all baselines on image quality metrics, though Dynamic 3D Gaussians renders faster (86.96 vs. 23.79 FPS). The method thus achieves the best overall quality while maintaining real-time interactive rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWinGS outperforms all seven baselines on the Neural 3D Video dataset across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FPS). The closest competitor is HyperReel [2], which trails by 0.32 dB in PSNR (30.78 vs. 31.10), 0.009 in SSIM, and 0.005 in LPIPS, while rendering ~20× slower (3.60 vs. 71.51 FPS). 4DGS [65] is the only other real-time method (30 FPS) but lags significantly in quality (PSNR 27.61, LPIPS 0.135). On the Technicolor dataset, SWinGS again leads on all image-quality metrics: it beats HyperReel [2] by 1.15 dB PSNR (33.65 vs. 32.50) and DyNeRF [25] by 1.85 dB, while Dynamic 3D Gaussians [35] is the weakest baseline (PSNR 27.02). The sole metric where SWinGS is outperformed is rendering speed on Technicolor, where Dynamic 3D Gaussians [35] reaches 86.96 FPS versus SWinGS's 23.79 FPS, though SWinGS still operates at interactive rates. Overall, the sliding-window + tunable-MLP approach yields consistent quality gains of 0.3–1.9 dB PSNR over the strongest baselines while maintaining or exceeding real-time rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWinGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from static to dynamic scenes by introducing per-window deformation MLPs, and adapts the tunable convolution mechanism [Maggioni et al., 2023] to MLPs for disentangling static and dynamic Gaussians. It departs from the dominant dynamic NeRF paradigm (D-NeRF, Nerfies, K-Planes, HexPlane) that relies on a single canonical representation shared across all frames, instead using temporally-local canonical spaces within adaptively-sized sliding windows. The adaptive window sampling driven by optical flow magnitude and the self-supervised temporal consistency fine-tuning step are practical contributions not present in prior dynamic 3DGS or NeRF-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sliding-window strategy makes dynamic 3DGS practical for arbitrary-length sequences with significant geometric changes, a setting where single-representation methods degrade. Achieving 71+ FPS on Neural 3D Video while outperforming NeRF-based baselines on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS positions this as a strong real-time option for dynamic scene rendering. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences (hundreds of windows), generalization to uncalibrated or sparse-view inputs, and whether the per-window canonical reset can handle truly non-repeating content (e.g., continuous walking sequences) without visible seams despite the consistency fine-tuning.
