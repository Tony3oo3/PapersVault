## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods rely on RGB diffusion or reconstruction models that bake lighting and shadows directly into object textures, producing non-relightable 3D assets. This fundamental limitation prevents generated objects from being used in applications like gaming and AR/VR that require accurate relighting under arbitrary illumination conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240112/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input and produces a relightable 3D mesh with full PBR material properties (albedo, normal, roughness, and metallic maps). Internally, the pipeline operates on four orthogonal views at 256×256 resolution for diffusion and reconstruction, with final PBR rendering at 512×512. The albedo and normal are generated and fixed in earlier stages, while roughness and metallic parameters are optimized separately in the final stage, ensuring clean material decomposition.

## Method

UniDream introduces a three-stage generation pipeline built around a novel albedo-normal aligned multi-view diffusion model (AN-MVM) that simultaneously provides PBR material prior and multi-view geometry prior. The AN-MVM uses multi-view self-attention across four orthogonal views and multi-domain self-attention between albedo and normal channels (distinguished by a class label) to enforce cross-view and cross-domain consistency. A transformer-based reconstruction model (TRM) using DINO-v2 features converts the generated albedo views into a coarse triplane-NeRF prior, which is then refined via SDS optimization with the AN-MVM (weighted 0.8 albedo / 0.2 normal) followed by DMTet mesh extraction. In the final PBR stage, albedo and normals are frozen while roughness and metallic parameters are optimized via SDS against Stable Diffusion, with a single-channel ambient light to prevent color contamination from the 2D prior.

## Results

On 68 text prompts evaluated with CLIP ViT-B/32, UniDream achieves a CLIP Score of 77.9 and CLIP R-Precision of 80.3% (R@1), 97.4% (R@5), and 98.5% (R@10), outperforming all baselines. In a user study with 22 participants rating geometric texture quality and realism, UniDream secures 50.3% preference votes, nearly doubling the next-best method (MVDream at 32.1%). The method also uniquely produces relightable PBR materials, a capability absent in all compared RGB-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDream outperforms all four quantitative baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor MVDream [40], UniDream gains +2.2 CLIP Score (77.9 vs 75.7), +3.5% R@1 (80.3 vs 76.8), and +18.2 percentage points in user preference (50.3% vs 32.1%). The gap over DreamFusion [34] is especially large in R@1 (80.3 vs 54.2), reflecting DreamFusion's well-known 'Janus problem' and multi-view inconsistency. Magic3D [18] and ProlificDreamer [45] are compared qualitatively in Fig.1; UniDream produces smoother geometry (due to normal supervision) and semantically clearer shapes. For PBR material quality, UniDream is compared against Fantasia3D [4] in Fig.7: Fantasia3D bakes lighting and shadows into the albedo, while UniDream cleanly separates albedo from illumination, enabling realistic relighting under arbitrary environment maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDream sits at the intersection of three established lines: SDS-based text-to-3D optimization (DreamFusion, Magic3D), multi-view diffusion for 3D consistency (MVDream), and transformer-based 3D reconstruction (LRM, Instant3D). Its key conceptual departure is replacing RGB supervision with albedo-normal paired data in the diffusion model, which prevents lighting from being baked into textures. The three-stage progressive pipeline—coarse reconstruction via a DINO-v2-based transformer, SDS refinement with the albedo-normal diffusion model, then PBR parameter optimization with Stable Diffusion—borrows architectural components from each lineage but reorganizes them around the explicit goal of material disentanglement, following the Disney BRDF formulation from Nvdiffrec.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Relightable 3D assets are a practical necessity for gaming, AR/VR, and production pipelines, yet prior text-to-3D methods produced objects with lighting permanently baked into textures, making them unusable in new scenes. UniDream demonstrates that a carefully staged pipeline can produce genuinely relightable PBR materials from text alone, a capability absent from all prior SDS-based methods. However, the approach is constrained by its ~300K Objaverse training set (limiting semantic generalization), cannot handle transparent or complex materials, and relies on a multi-stage optimization that is computationally expensive. Open questions include whether the albedo-normal aligned diffusion formulation generalizes to more complex BRDF models and whether path-tracing-based rendering (acknowledged as future work) would close the remaining realism gap.
