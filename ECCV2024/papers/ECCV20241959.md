## Problem

Perspective distortion (PD) causes significant accuracy drops in standard deep learning models, yet no dedicated PD benchmark exists and synthesizing realistic PD requires estimating camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters—a challenging and often infeasible task. Existing distortion-correction methods force CV pipelines into an inefficient two-stage process (rectify then learn) and do not produce generalizable, PD-robust representations. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a parameter-free method to synthesize PD and introducing a dedicated benchmark to measure model robustness against it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241959/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet experiments) and maps each pixel coordinate (x, y) to a complex vector z = x + iy before applying the Möbius transform; for crowd counting it additionally transforms point annotations, and for object detection it transforms bounding boxes. The output is a perspectively distorted image (with optional integrated padding background to replace black borders) that mimics real-world PD at controllable intensity and orientation. The complex parameter c is the sole free knob: its real part governs horizontal (left/right) distortion and its imaginary part governs vertical (top/bottom) distortion, with magnitudes in [0.2, 0.3] for classification and [0.2, 0.6] for downstream tasks.

## Method

MPD exploits the non-linear, conformal nature of Möbius transforms in complex space to model perspective distortion without any camera calibration. Pixel coordinates are mapped to the complex plane, transformed via Φ(z) = (az+b)/(cz+d) with fixed a=1, b=0, d=1 and a tunable complex parameter c, then mapped back to real-valued pixel space and discretized. The key insight is that the real and imaginary components of c independently control the orientation (left/right/top/bottom) and intensity of the synthesized distortion, enabling fine-grained, parameter-free PD augmentation. MPD is integrated as a probabilistic data augmentation (P ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8}) into supervised ResNet50 training, SimCLR contrastive pre-training, and DINO self-supervised pre-training, and is further adapted to crowd counting (MPD-AutoCrowd, which also synthesizes crowd points via reflection), fisheye transfer learning, person re-identification (CLIP-ReIdent), and object detection (Faster R-CNN, FCOS). The authors additionally construct ImageNet-PD, an eight-subset benchmark (four orientations × two background variants) derived from the ImageNet validation set, to systematically evaluate PD robustness.

## Results

On the newly introduced ImageNet-PD benchmark, supervised:MPD achieves an average Top-1 accuracy improvement of 9.42% over standard ResNet50 (e.g., 73.54% vs. 65.84% on PD-R), while ssl:MPD (SimCLR) achieves ~10.32% improvement, all while maintaining performance on the original ImageNet validation set (~76.3% Top-1). On existing benchmarks, supervised:MPD reduces accuracy drops on ImageNet-E by 2.91% (position), 2.14% (direction), and 1.19% (size), and improves ImageNet-X accuracy from 60.64% to 63.23% (error ratio 1.44 vs. 1.55). In real-world applications, MPD-AutoCrowd achieves MAE 50.81 on ShanghaiTech Part A and 96.80 on UCF-CC50, ssl:MPD reaches 94.79% accuracy on VOC-360 fisheye, MPD(Clip-ReIdent) attains mAP 97.02/98.30 on DeepSportRadar, and MPD-OD gains up to 3% mAP on COCO object detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard ResNet50 (P=0.0), both supervised:MPD and ssl:MPD substantially outperform on all four ImageNet-PD orientation subsets (e.g., PD-R: 73.81% vs. 65.84% Top-1 for ssl:MPD at P=0.8), while preserving original ImageNet accuracy within ~0.1%. On ImageNet-X, supervised:MPD (63.23%) and ssl:MPD (62.99%) both beat ResNet50 (60.64%), with lower error ratios (1.44/1.41 vs. 1.55). In crowd counting, MPD-AutoCrowd surpasses all 14 prior methods in Table 2, achieving the best MAE on SHTech Part A (50.81 vs. P2P-Net's 52.74 and SDA's 52.90) and UCF-CC50 (96.80 vs. SDA's 159.1). For person re-identification, MPD(Clip-ReIdent) marginally improves over Clip-ReIdent (mAP 97.02 vs. 96.90 w/o re-ranking; 91.95 vs. 88.50 with ResNet50x16 backbone). MPD also consistently outperforms popular augmentation baselines (Mixup, Cutout, AugMix, PixMix) on both ImageNet-PD and ImageNet-E, and in object detection, MPD-OD improves FasterRCNN from 37.10 to 40.00 mAP and FCOS from 38.60 to 40.20 mAP on COCO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Zhou et al.'s (2021) use of Möbius transforms as data augmentation by restricting to a specific family of transforms (controlled by parameter c) and introducing fine-grained parameterization for orientation and intensity to synthesize perspective distortion specifically. It departs from the dominant distortion-correction literature (GeoNet, PCL, PerspectiveNet, etc.) that follows a two-stage 'correct-then-learn' pipeline, instead proposing a one-stage approach where PD is incorporated directly into training as an augmentation. The paper also positions itself against existing robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-E, ImageNet-X) that only partially capture PD, motivating the dedicated ImageNet-PD benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a camera-parameter-free method to synthesize realistic perspective distortion, removing a major barrier (precise intrinsic/extrinsic estimation) that has limited robust CV research. The ImageNet-PD benchmark fills a clear gap and will likely become a standard evaluation tool for PD robustness. However, the conformal nature of Möbius transforms (angle-preserving) may not capture all real-world distortions such as lens barrel/pincushion effects or motion blur, and the narrow parameter range (c: 0.2–0.3) limits the diversity of synthesized distortions. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to compound distortions (PD + lens + motion) and whether the augmentation ceiling is fundamentally lower than true correction-based methods for extreme distortion regimes.
