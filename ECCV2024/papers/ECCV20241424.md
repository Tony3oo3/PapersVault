## Problem

Contemporary color difference (CD) measures for photographic images rely on co-located pixel or patch comparisons in perceptually uniform color spaces, which fail to capture human color perception when images are misaligned—a common scenario in digital photography where the same scene is captured by different devices. Moreover, these measures treat color in isolation, ignoring the well-established perceptual principle that color and structure are inextricably interdependent as a unitary process of perceptual organization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB photographic images (resized to 256×256) that may be arbitrarily misaligned (global motion, local displacement, flipping, or different viewpoints) and produces a single scalar color difference value ΔE(X,Y). The base MS-SWD is entirely training-free with fixed hyperparameters (K=5 pyramid scales, 11×11 patch size with stride 1, P=128 random projections), and the same measure can be used as a differentiable loss function for image and video color transfer optimization.

## Method

MS-SWD constructs Gaussian pyramids in CIELAB color space at K=5 scales, extracts overlapping 11×11×3 patches at each scale via img2col, and computes the Sliced Wasserstein Distance (SWD) between the two patch distributions using P=128 random unit projections. The critical mechanism is the sort() operator applied to projected patch values: patches of similar color and structure—regardless of spatial location—receive similar ranks after sorting and are thus paired for distance computation, enabling efficient non-local patch matching without explicit nearest-neighbor search. The final CD is the average of SWD values across all scales. This design is training-free and reduces the Wasserstein computation from O(M^2.5) to O(M log M) per projection. An optional variant replaces random linear projections with a lightweight learnable non-linear projection (11×11 conv + leaky ReLU + 1×1 conv, 0.05M parameters), trained on SPCD to maximize PLCC.

## Results

On the SPCD dataset (30,000 image pairs), MS-SWD achieves STRESS 28.363, PLCC 0.841, and SRCC 0.805 on non-perfectly aligned pairs, substantially outperforming all 19 competing methods (next best: CIECAM02 at STRESS 29.339, PLCC 0.679). On perfectly aligned pairs, CIECAM16 (STRESS 23.901, PLCC 0.818) and CIEDE2000 (PLCC 0.827) remain stronger, but MS-SWD delivers the best overall PLCC (0.794) and SRCC (0.772) without any training. Under geometric transformations (translation, dilation, flipping), MS-SWD maintains STRESS below 28.4 across all conditions, while all other methods degrade severely (e.g., CIELAB drops to STRESS 42.956 under flipping).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS-SWD's decisive advantage is on non-perfectly aligned pairs, where it achieves STRESS 28.363 and PLCC 0.841, beating the next-best CIECAM02 (STRESS 29.339, PLCC 0.679) by a wide margin and outperforming all CIE-based metrics, spatial extensions (S-CIELAB, Ouni08), and general-purpose IQA models (LPIPS, DISTS, ST-LPIPS). On perfectly aligned pairs, MS-SWD (STRESS 34.040, PLCC 0.778) is outperformed by CIECAM16 (STRESS 23.901, PLCC 0.818) and CIEDE2000 (PLCC 0.827), indicating that co-located CIELAB-based comparisons remain strong when alignment is guaranteed. The deep-learning baselines CD-Net and CD-Flow achieve the best aligned-pair scores (STRESS 20.891 and 16.613 respectively) but degrade sharply under geometric transformations (e.g., CD-Flow drops to STRESS 36.546 under flipping), whereas MS-SWD stays below STRESS 28.4 across all transformation types. General-purpose image quality models (LPIPS, DISTS, A-DISTS, ST-LPIPS, DeepWSD) and just-noticeable-difference methods (Butteraugli, PIM-5) perform poorly on CD assessment, with PLCC values often below 0.5 on misaligned pairs. Overall, MS-SWD achieves the best PLCC (0.794) and SRCC (0.772) across all 30,000 pairs without any training, while CIECAM16 retains the lowest STRESS (26.817) on the full set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD) from Rabin et al. [38]—originally developed for texture mixing and generative modeling—as a perceptual color difference metric, drawing conceptual inspiration from Elnekave and Weiss [17] on patch distribution matching for image synthesis. It departs from the dominant paradigm in CD assessment (CIELAB, CIEDE2000, S-CIELAB, CD-Net, CD-Flow) that relies on co-located pixel or patch comparisons, instead computing a statistical distance between multiscale patch distributions in CIELAB space. The multiscale Gaussian pyramid structure is inherited from MS-SSIM [54], and the random projection count (P=128) is borrowed from GPDM [17], making the method a training-free composition of well-established components applied to a new purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a training-free statistical distance outperforms 60M-parameter trained networks (CD-Flow) on misaligned CD assessment challenges the assumption that learned representations are necessary to handle spatial misalignment in perceptual metrics. The empirical verification of metric properties (triangle inequality, identity of indiscernibles) opens the door to using MS-SWD as a principled loss function, demonstrated in image and video color transfer. Open questions include whether MS-SWD is provably a metric under specific hyper-parameter configurations, whether alternative pyramids (Laplacian, steerable, VGG features) would improve performance, and whether the non-local sort() mechanism generalizes to other perceptual tasks beyond color difference. Evaluation is limited to the SPCD dataset at 256×256 resolution, leaving scalability to higher resolutions and diverse content untested.
