## Problem

Vision-language models are often assumed to be inherently open-set due to their internet-scale training, but this paper shows they introduce closed-set assumptions through their finite query set of class labels. When VLMs encounter objects not in the query set, they misclassify them as known classes with high confidence, yet no systematic benchmark or evaluation protocol existed to quantify this vulnerability. The paper establishes a revised open-set definition for the VLM era and demonstrates that naively enlarging the query set worsens both task and open-set performance.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (or image region for detection) and a finite set of query text labels, producing a class prediction, bounding box (for detection), an uncertainty score, and a binary open-set rejection decision. The evaluation protocol is unusual in that each image is tested twice: once in closed-set mode (query set = all dataset classes) to collect true positives, and once in open-set mode (query set = all dataset classes *except* those present in the image) so that every prediction is guaranteed to be an open-set error. Uncertainty measures (softmax score, cosine similarity, or entropy) are thresholded to separate TPs from OSEs, and performance is summarized via precision-recall curves where TPs are the positive class and OSEs are the negative class.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a new evaluation protocol and systematic empirical study rather than a single new architecture. It redefines the open-set problem for VLMs around the finite query set (rather than the finite training set) and introduces a dual-testing protocol on ImageNet-1k (classification) and COCO (detection) that definitively identifies open-set errors without requiring FP categorization. Two families of baseline open-set recognition strategies are evaluated: (1) predictive uncertainty thresholding using softmax scores, cosine similarities, or entropy of the softmax distribution, and (2) dedicated negative embeddings added to the query set, generated either as random words passed through the text encoder or as random vectors drawn from a Gaussian matching the query embedding statistics. The study tests six VLM classifiers (CLIP, ALIGN, CoCa, ImageBind, SigLIP, LanguageBind) and seven open-vocabulary detectors (OVR-CNN, ViLD, OV-DETR, RegionCLIP, Detic, VLDet, CORA), and further analyzes how query set size and the number/type of negative queries affect the closed-set vs. open-set trade-off.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k, all six VLM classifiers perform poorly under open-set conditions: at 95% recall, precision ranges from only 46.2% (CLIP Softmax) to 58.4% (LanguageBind Entropy), meaning nearly half of predictions are open-set errors. At 95% precision, recall drops to just 3.4%–11.1%, indicating most true positives are lost. On COCO, all seven open-vocabulary detectors produce 112K–1.5M open-set errors across 4,952 images, with P@95R ranging from 5.9% (OV-DETR) to 21.9% (VLDet) and R@95P from 15.4% (ViLD) to 48.5% (VLDet). Entropy and Softmax uncertainty consistently outperform raw Cosine similarity for open-set rejection across both classification and detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the six VLM classifiers, LanguageBind achieves the best open-set performance (AuPR 81.7%, P@95R 58.4%, R@95P 11.1%) and highest closed-set accuracy (78.5%), outperforming CLIP (AuPR 71.5%, P@95R 46.2%) by roughly 10 points in AuPR and 12 points in P@95R. ImageBind is the second-best classifier (AuPR 80.1% with Entropy), while CLIP and CoCa trail at the bottom. For uncertainty measures, Entropy consistently yields the highest AuPR and P@95R across all classifiers, while Cosine similarity is the weakest (e.g., CLIP Cosine P@95R = 42.5% vs. CLIP Entropy P@95R = 45.4%). Among the seven detectors, VLDet achieves the best P@95R (21.9%) and OV-DETR the best R@95P (47.3%), but ViLD—despite the highest mAP (46.4%)—has the worst AuPR (60.1%), breaking the expected closed-set/open-set correlation. No single VLM or uncertainty measure achieves acceptable open-set performance: even the best configuration retains fewer than 12% of true positives at 95% precision, confirming that current VLMs are not inherently open-set robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-set recognition framework of Scheirer et al. (2013) from the pre-VLM era, where the closed-set assumption arose from a finite training set, to the VLM era, where it arises from a finite query set of class labels. It repurposes existing uncertainty-based rejection and negative-embedding strategies from the classical open-set literature and applies them systematically to 13 state-of-the-art VLM classifiers and detectors. The paper carves new ground by being the first to formally redefine the open-set problem for VLMs and to demonstrate that internet-scale pretraining does not eliminate the closed-set vulnerability, directly challenging the assumption that VLMs are open-set by default.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the field with a standardized benchmark and evaluation protocol for open-set VLM recognition, filling a critical gap as VLMs are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains like robotics and medical imaging. The finding that all tested baseline approaches (predictive uncertainty, random negative embeddings/words) remain insufficient for reliable open-set rejection underscores that the problem is far from solved and motivates research into learned negative representations, prompt-tuning, and VLM-specific uncertainty calibration. A key open question is whether the poor performance stems from the VLM architecture itself or from the evaluation protocol, and whether more sophisticated negative embedding strategies (e.g., learned via prompt-tuning or auto-labelling) can close the gap.
