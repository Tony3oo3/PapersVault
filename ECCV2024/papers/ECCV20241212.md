## Problem

Dataset distillation relies on bi-level optimization where each synthetic-data update requires training a novel model in a nested loop and backpropagating through an unrolled computation graph, incurring over 50 GiB GPU memory and hundreds of hours of runtime even for small datasets. This makes scaling to large-scale benchmarks like full-size ImageNet-1K (224×224, 1000 classes) practically infeasible, as the optimization pixel space is thousands of times larger than CIFAR-10.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original RGB image dataset (64×64 Tiny-ImageNet or 224×224 ImageNet-1K) with class labels and a single base model (randomly initialized or pre-trained ResNet18) as input. It produces a synthetic dataset of IPC (images-per-class) condensed images, each paired with soft labels generated by ensemble prediction from the weak teacher pool after data augmentation. Notably, the original dataset's feature statistics are consumed via running mean/variance cached in the batch normalization layers of the weak teachers, avoiding repeated forward passes over the full original dataset at each iteration.

## Method

Teddy applies a first-order Taylor expansion to the meta-learning DD objective, proving that the multi-step gradient-matching loss along the student training trajectory is equivalent to matching first- and second-order feature statistics (per-layer mean and variance) between synthetic and original data, thereby decoupling the bi-level optimization into a first-order problem. To further eliminate the per-iteration cost of retraining a student model, Teddy pre-caches a pool of weak teacher models from a single base model using two strategies: prior-generation (caching early-stage checkpoints every m steps) or post-generation (pruning a well-trained model via DepGraph with random strategy plus 0–2 epochs of fine-tuning). During optimization, a random subset of weak teachers is selected per iteration, and the synthetic data is updated by matching its per-layer feature statistics against the running mean/variance stored in the teachers' BN layers. Finally, soft labels are produced by ensemble prediction from the full model pool on augmented synthetic images, providing richer label information and improving cross-architecture generalization.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (full-size, 224×224), Teddy (post-generation) achieves 32.7% top-1 accuracy at IPC 10, 52.5% at IPC 50, and 56.2% at IPC 100, outperforming the previous SOTA (SRe²L) by 11.4%, 5.7%, and 3.4% respectively. On Tiny-ImageNet (64×64), Teddy reaches 44.5% at IPC 50 and 51.4% at IPC 100, surpassing SRe²L by 3.4% and 1.7%. In cross-architecture evaluation on ImageNet-1K IPC 10, Teddy (prior-generation) improves over SRe²L by up to 12.8% (DenseNet121: 34.3% vs 21.5%) and by 10–13% across all six tested architectures. Teddy also reduces total runtime by 46.6% compared to SRe²L while using the same peak GPU memory with a single teacher model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Teddy outperforms all eight baselines (DC, DSA, DM, IDM, MTT, FTD, TESLA, SRe²L) across every dataset and IPC setting where comparisons are available. The most dramatic gains are on full-size ImageNet-1K: at IPC 10, Teddy (post) reaches 32.7% vs. SRe²L's 21.3% (+11.4%) and TESLA's 17.8% (+14.9%); at IPC 50, 52.5% vs. 46.8% (+5.7%). On Tiny-ImageNet, Teddy leads SRe²L by 3.4% (IPC 50) and 1.7% (IPC 100), and surpasses FTD (the next-best Tiny-ImageNet method) by 13.0% at IPC 50. In cross-architecture evaluation (ImageNet-1K IPC 10), Teddy (prior) beats SRe²L by 9.1–13.2% across ResNet50/101, DenseNet121, MobileNetV2, ShuffleNetV2, and EfficientNetB0, with the largest margin on DenseNet121 (+12.8%) and MobileNetV2 (+13.2%). Notably, most prior methods (DC, DSA, DM, IDM, MTT, FTD) could not be evaluated on full-size ImageNet-1K, and TESLA results are on a downsized version, making Teddy's full-size ImageNet-1K results a first for the DD literature.

## Relation to Prior Work

Teddy directly extends the bi-level meta-learning formulation of dataset distillation (Wang et al., 2018) by applying a first-order Taylor expansion to decouple the inner-loop training from the outer-loop data update, reducing the problem to a first-order statistic-matching objective. The gradient-to-statistic equivalence in Proposition 2 builds on the FRePo assumption (Zhou et al., 2022) that only the final linear layer is updated, while the use of batch-normalization running statistics generalizes SRe2L's (Yin et al., 2023) single well-trained model to a pool of weak teachers generated via early-stage checkpointing or DepGraph-based pruning. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a unifying theoretical framework that shows gradient matching, distribution matching, and performance matching objectives are all first-order approximations of the same meta-learning loss, a connection not previously made explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Teddy is the first method to perform dataset distillation on the original full-size ImageNet-1K (224×224, 1000 classes) rather than downsized or subset variants, achieving up to 12.8% accuracy gains over prior SOTA while cutting runtime by 46.6%. The finding that weak, partially-trained teachers outperform fully converged ones for distillation challenges the prevailing assumption that stronger teachers yield better synthetic data, and the unified theoretical framework provides a principled lens for future DD method design. Open questions include whether the Taylor approximation remains tight for very deep or transformer-based backbones, whether the weak-teacher pool strategy transfers to non-CNN architectures, and how the method scales to datasets with even larger class counts or modalities beyond RGB images.
