## Problem

Pre-training reinforcement learning agents with in-the-wild videos is hindered by two obstacles: the absence of action annotations and the domain gap between generic video content and downstream task environments. Existing approaches either pre-train only state representations (underutilizing dynamics knowledge) or treat videos as imitation demonstrations (failing when action spaces or visual domains differ). The paper asks whether a more abstract form of video-derived knowledge—visual dynamics representations—can bridge this gap and be efficiently adapted to domain-shifted downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m context frames (resized to 128×128 RGB) and a single visual goal image as input; during pre-training, it additionally uses l future frames as supervision. All frames are first compressed via a VQ-VAE into grids of discrete visual tokens before being processed by the Transformer-based dynamics model. The primary output is a 4-dimensional robot action vector, produced by first sampling N visual dynamics representation candidates from the learned prior, selecting the one whose decoded visual plan is closest to the goal, and then passing the selected representation through an action alignment module. Intermediate outputs include the visual plans (decoded future token sequences) and the visual dynamics representations themselves (continuous latent vectors from the CVAE).

## Method

PVDR employs a Transformer-based Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE) as the visual dynamics model, comprising an encoder (context+future frames → latent z), a decoder (context+z → future frames), and a prior (context → latent distribution), all operating in a VQ-VAE compressed discrete token space. In the pre-training stage, the CVAE is trained via a self-supervised video prediction objective with reconstruction, KL information-bottleneck, and prior-matching losses, causing the latent variable to encode abstract visual dynamics rather than pixel-level details. During online adaptation, the dynamics model is fine-tuned on collected experiences, the prior is further optimized with a goal-oriented loss to bias candidate generation toward task-relevant dynamics, and a separate action alignment module (Π_φ) is trained jointly with supervised regression on (z, x_t)→a pairs and PPO-based RL using a reward that combines goal proximity and plan-adherence. At inference, the agent performs planning-based decision-making: it samples N representation candidates from the prior, selects the one whose decoded plan best matches the goal, and executes the aligned action—effectively decoupling what to do (visual plan selection) from how to do it (action generation).

## Results

PVDR is evaluated on 12 goal-conditioned tasks in Meta-World (500 steps, sparse reward) and 3 tasks in RLBench (200 steps), with success rate as the primary metric. On Meta-World, PVDR achieves the best final success rate in 7 out of 12 tasks, while APV matches PVDR on the remaining 5 tasks but with lower sample efficiency. On RLBench, PVDR outperforms all baselines across all three tasks. The paper reports results as learning curves over environment steps (up to 200×10⁴ steps on Meta-World, 100×10⁴ on RLBench) rather than a single summary table of final success rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO and PVDR-without-pre-training (both without video pre-training), PVDR consistently achieves higher success rates and faster convergence, confirming that the pre-trained visual dynamics representations transfer useful prior knowledge to downstream tasks. Against STG (intrinsic reward pre-training), PVDR shows a clear performance gap, which the authors attribute to STG's reliance on in-domain imitation without a fine-tuning mechanism, making it vulnerable to the domain gap between BAIR pushing videos and Meta-World/RLBench environments. Against FICC (latent action + MCTS), PVDR shows a notable edge, likely because PVDR encodes more abstract, compressed dynamics from longer horizons rather than discrete latent actions from two adjacent frames, making it more robust to action-space gaps. Against APV (observation-only dynamics pre-training), PVDR wins on 7/12 Meta-World tasks and matches on the rest; the advantage is attributed to PVDR's explicit visual dynamics prior (a single latent variable encoding the entire snippet's dynamics) versus APV's per-frame observation representations that lack an explicit dynamics prior. On RLBench, PVDR outperforms all four baselines on all three tasks, further validating the approach in more challenging and visually diverse settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PVDR builds on the video prediction paradigm (VQ-VAE + causal Transformer) established by recent video generation work, repurposing it as a pre-training objective for RL rather than for pixel-level generation. It departs from two dominant prior lines: (1) state-representation pre-training (APV, R3M, VIP) that compresses observations without explicitly encoding dynamics, and (2) imitation-intended methods (FICC, STG, IDM-based approaches) that treat videos as action-free demonstrations to be directly imitated. The key conceptual departure is using a CVAE to distill longer-horizon visual dynamics into a single latent vector that serves as an abstract, action-agnostic prior, which is then aligned to executable actions only during online adaptation—avoiding the brittle latent-to-real action mapping that FICC requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled middle ground between state-only pre-training and full imitation, suggesting that abstract dynamics priors transfer better across domain gaps than concrete behavioral imitations. However, the experimental scope is limited to one pre-training dataset (BAIR robot pushing) and relatively simple manipulation tasks (Meta-World, RLBench), with a moderate domain gap (robot pushing → robot manipulation). Open questions include whether this approach scales to more diverse video sources (human videos, multi-robot), larger domain gaps, or higher-dimensional action spaces, and whether the planning-based inference (sampling N candidates per step) remains tractable in real-time control settings.
