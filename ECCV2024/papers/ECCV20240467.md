## Problem

Deploying multi-modal large language models for facial affective behavior analysis is hindered by three gaps: no instruction-following FABA dataset exists for MLLM training, no benchmark jointly evaluates recognition and text-generation quality on FABA tasks, and standard vision encoders (e.g., CLIP) fail to capture facial structure information while full fine-tuning of billion-parameter MLLMs is computationally prohibitive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240467/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an aligned, cropped face image (from AffectNet, processed via Dlib) and a free-form text instruction drawn from 100 templates (e.g., 'What is the emotion in this face?'). Rather than outputting a discrete class index or binary AU vector, EmoLA generates a natural-language description that includes the reasoning chain over facial movements, the degree of expression, and inter-AU relationships. For evaluation, discrete labels (7 basic emotions or 12 AUs) are extracted from the generated text via synonym counting to compute recognition scores alongside ROUGE for generation quality.

## Method

EmoLA extends LLaVA-1.5 (CLIP-L/14 visual encoder + 2-layer MLP projector + Vicuna-7B LLM decoder) with two key additions: a facial prior expert that extracts landmark features via a pre-trained InsightFace detector and projects them into a single token through a learned MLP, and a LoRA module (rank 128) on the LLM for parameter-efficient adaptation. The facial prior token is concatenated with the visual embedding tokens and language instruction tokens before feeding into the frozen LLM decoder, supplying complementary face-structure information that the general-purpose CLIP encoder overlooks. Training optimizes only the prior projector, visual projector, and LoRA matrices (~10% of total parameters) via auto-regressive next-token prediction on FABA-Instruct (19,877 images with 30K GPT-4V-generated fine-grained emotion and AU descriptions), reducing both memory and compute costs relative to full fine-tuning while avoiding inference overhead.

## Results

On the novel FABA-Bench, EmoLA achieves the best REGE scores on both tasks: 96.2 for emotion recognition (S_re=64.5, S_ge=31.7) and 91.5 for AU recognition (S_re=56.3, S_ge=35.2), outperforming LLaVA-1.5 (93.9/91.4), Shikra (94.6/86.6), mPLUG-Owl2 (82.0/55.7), and MiniGPT4-v2 (77.8/37.8). On traditional FER, EmoLA achieves 92.05% accuracy on RAF-DB, tying the SOTA POSTER. On AUR benchmarks, EmoLA attains 65.1% avg F1 on DISFA (+1.3% over PIAP), 62.1% on GFT (+3.5% over EmoCo), and 64.2% on BP4D (0.6% behind ReCoT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FABA-Bench, EmoLA outperforms all four MLLM baselines on the REGE metric for both emotion and AU tasks, with the largest margin over mPLUG-Owl2 (+14.2 on emotion, +35.8 on AU) and MiniGPT4-v2 (+18.4 on emotion, +53.7 on AU). Against LLaVA-1.5 (its backbone), EmoLA gains +2.3 on emotion REGE and +0.1 on AU REGE while tuning only ~10% of parameters via LoRA. On RAF-DB, EmoLA ties POSTER at 92.05% accuracy and surpasses all other FER baselines (APViT 91.98%, EAC 90.35%, DAN 89.70%). On DISFA, EmoLA achieves 65.1% avg F1, beating the previous best PIAP (63.8%) by 1.3 points; on GFT it reaches 62.1%, exceeding EmoCo (58.6%) by 3.5 points. On BP4D, EmoLA (64.2%) trails ReCoT (64.8%) by 0.6%, which the authors attribute to ReCoT's consistency regularization and co-training strategy specifically designed for that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (CLIP visual encoder + MLP projector + Vicuna LLM decoder) and the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm, extending both to the facial affective behavior analysis domain. It departs from the long-standing discriminative framing of FER and AUR as multi-class/multi-label classification by recasting them as instruction-following sequence-to-sequence generation tasks, motivated by the success of MLLMs in general visual understanding. A key architectural departure is the addition of a lightweight facial prior expert (a single landmark token from InsightFace) to complement CLIP's general-purpose visual features, addressing the observation that CLIP was never trained on facial structure. The GPT-4V-annotated FABA-Instruct dataset and the REGE metric (combining recognition accuracy/F1 with ROUGE generation scores) represent new evaluation infrastructure absent from prior FABA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work is the first to bring instruction tuning and free-form descriptive output to FABA, opening a path toward interpretable, reasoning-capable facial affect models that can handle compositional, exaggerated, and undefined emotions beyond the seven-category paradigm. The competitive results against task-specific SOTA on RAF-DB, DISFA, BP4D, and GFT—achieved with only ~10% of parameters tuned—suggest MLLMs are a viable and efficient alternative to heavily specialized FABA architectures. Open questions include whether the single-token facial prior can be enriched (parsing, recognition features), whether the approach extends to video/micro-expressions, and whether GPT-4V annotation quality is sufficient for large-scale training without human verification. The REGE metric, while novel, relies on ad-hoc synonym counting for emotion classification from free text, which may not generalize cleanly to more diverse description styles.
