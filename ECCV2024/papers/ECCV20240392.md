## Problem

Existing fisheye image rectification methods, whether regression-based or generation-based, learn fixed position-mapping relationships tied to a single distortion degree and degrade significantly when the distortion changes, requiring costly retraining on new datasets. This lack of generalization across varying distortion levels limits practical deployment in real-world scenarios where lens distortion is not fixed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240392/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a 256×256 RGB fisheye image and a user-selected learnable query (a spatial map of the same resolution) as input, and produces a rectified RGB image. The query encodes the target distortion degree and can be linearly interpolated between two queries to achieve smooth continuous rectification at arbitrary intermediate distortion levels. A flow estimation module first warps the input for coarse-grained rectification before the main rectification network operates.

## Method

QueryCDR introduces a Distortion-aware Learnable Query Mechanism (DLQM) that maintains N spatial queries (one per distortion degree), each processed through a control extractor and per-layer fully-connected layers to produce position-dependent control conditions fed into every rectification layer. Two controllable modulating blocks consume these conditions: CCMB (CNN-based) dynamically predicts a per-pixel fusion ratio between original and element-wise-modulated features to preserve local texture, while CAMB (Transformer-based) uses a control-attention mechanism where controlled features act as queries and input features as keys/values to capture global distortion patterns. The U-shaped network places CCMB in layers with larger feature maps and CAMB in layers with smaller feature maps to balance local detail and global dependency. Training follows a two-stage strategy: pre-training on a single distortion degree with one query, then fine-tuning on nine distortion degrees with their corresponding queries, enabling the model to generalize across all levels without per-degree retraining.

## Results

On the COCO synthetic fisheye dataset with 9 distortion degrees, QueryCDR achieves an average PSNR of 20.32 dB and SSIM of 0.676, outperforming the best baseline SimFIR (18.53 dB / 0.601) by 1.79 dB and 0.075 respectively. QueryCDR leads on every individual distortion degree (d1–d9) for both metrics, with the largest margin at d1 (20.01 vs. 16.57 dB PSNR) and the smallest at d8 (19.11 vs. 18.65 dB). The model also demonstrates robustness on real-world fisheye images (Woodscape dataset), maintaining high-quality rectification despite the synthetic-to-real gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QueryCDR outperforms all seven baselines across every distortion degree (d1–d9) on both PSNR and SSIM. Against the strongest generation-based baseline SimFIR [10], QueryCDR gains 1.79 dB in average PSNR (20.32 vs. 18.53) and 0.075 in SSIM (0.676 vs. 0.601). The second-best method DDA [54] is surpassed by 1.99 dB in average PSNR; notably, even on d5 where DDA peaks (20.12 dB), QueryCDR still leads by 0.60 dB. Among regression-based methods, Blind [23] (11.57 dB) and DeepCalib [4] (11.04 dB) trail QueryCDR by roughly 9 dB, while the traditional SC [40] method (10.73 dB) is the weakest overall. The generation-based DR-GAN [24] (17.74 dB) and PCN [52] (17.97 dB) also fall 2.4–2.6 dB short of QueryCDR, confirming that the learnable query mechanism and hybrid CCMB/CAMB architecture provide a substantial advantage over prior rectification approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PCN architecture (Yang et al., CVPR 2021) for its U-shaped network and appearance flow module, but departs from the broader controllable image restoration literature (CFSNet, MM-RealSR, AdaFM) by arguing that scalar control conditions are fundamentally inadequate for fisheye rectification due to the spatially varying nature of lens distortion. Instead of a single scalar, the authors introduce spatially-structured learnable queries that encode position-dependent rectification conditions, bridging the gap between restoration-style controllability and the geometric mapping problem inherent to fisheye correction. The hybrid CNN-Transformer design (CCMB + CAMB) also draws on the complementary strengths of local and global feature extraction established in recent low-level vision work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need to retrain rectification models for each new distortion degree, which is costly given the difficulty of collecting fisheye image data. The two-stage training strategy (pre-train on one degree, fine-tune on multiple) makes the approach feasible with limited data. However, the method still requires the user to select or interpolate between discrete queries, and the authors themselves note that fully automatic control remains an open problem. Evaluation is primarily on synthetic data with only qualitative real-world validation, leaving questions about robustness to real lens variations and whether the approach extends to other geometric distortions (e.g., wide-angle, panoramic).
