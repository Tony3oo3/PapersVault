## Problem

Existing CAD retrieval and alignment methods are computationally intensive, requiring per-object encoding followed by iterative pose optimization, with runtimes ranging from 2.6 seconds to over 20 minutes per scene. This precludes real-time applications such as augmented reality and robotics that demand simultaneous retrieval and alignment of all objects in a scene within tens of milliseconds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240381/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

FastCAD takes a point cloud of up to 50,000 points, sourced either directly from an RGB-D scan or sampled from a mesh produced by an online reconstruction method (DG-Recon) applied to a video. For every detected object it outputs a category label, oriented 3D bounding box parameters, a 4-way front-facing side classification, and a 256-dimensional shape embedding vector. The embedding is matched against a pre-computed CAD embedding space via nearest-neighbor search, and the retrieved CAD model is placed inside the predicted bounding box according to the predicted front-facing side, yielding the final aligned CAD output in a single forward pass.

## Method

FastCAD is a single-stage detection network built on sparse 3D convolutions and generative transposed convolutions (FCAF3D-style backbone) whose shared detection head simultaneously predicts category, oriented bounding box, front-facing side, and shape embedding for all objects. The shape embeddings are learned via knowledge distillation from a separate Perceiver encoder trained in a contrastive learning framework with triplet loss plus two auxiliary tasks: foreground/background segmentation of the noisy scan and regression of the Chamfer distance between positive and negative CAD model embeddings. At inference, the predicted embedding retrieves the nearest CAD model from the pre-computed space, and alignment is obtained directly from the predicted bounding box and front-facing side—eliminating any iterative optimization. The front-facing side is predicted as a separate 4-way classification (with symmetry-aware soft targets) rather than encoded in the embedding, which reduces the embedding database size by a factor of four and improves alignment accuracy. This design achieves 50 ms per scan (100 ms total with DG-Recon for video), a 50× speedup over prior scan-based methods while matching or exceeding their alignment accuracy.

## Results

On the Scan2CAD alignment benchmark with RGB-D scan input, FastCAD achieves 61.7% instance alignment accuracy in 50 ms, slightly outperforming SceneCAD (61.2%) while being 52× faster (50 ms vs. 2,600 ms). In the video setting, FastCAD reaches 48.2% instance alignment accuracy at 100 ms per frame (10 FPS), surpassing RayTran (43.0%) and Vid2CAD (38.6%) by 5.2 and 9.6 percentage points respectively. FastCAD also introduces reconstruction and shape accuracy metrics, achieving 41.7% reconstruction accuracy and 83.1% shape accuracy on scans, and 29.6% reconstruction accuracy and 87.7% shape accuracy on videos (using the same retrieval pool as Vid2CAD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against scan-based methods, FastCAD (61.7%) marginally beats SceneCAD (61.2%) on instance alignment accuracy while running 52× faster (50 ms vs. 2,600 ms), and substantially outperforms End-to-End CAD Retrieval (50.7%) and the original Scan2CAD (31.7%). In the video setting, FastCAD (48.2%) clearly surpasses RayTran (43.0%), Vid2CAD (38.6%), and ODAM (29.2%) on instance alignment accuracy, while also being at least 3× faster than the fastest video baseline (ODAM at 366 ms). On the newly introduced shape accuracy metric, FastCAD (83.1% on scans) matches the offline annotation tool ScanNotate (83.5%) despite being 13,200× faster, though ScanNotate benefits from ground-truth pose initialisation. When constrained to the same retrieval pool as Vid2CAD, FastCAD improves reconstruction accuracy from 22.9% to 29.6% and shape accuracy from 76.6% to 87.7%, confirming that the gains come from better alignment rather than a larger retrieval set.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastCAD departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in CAD retrieval (Scan2CAD, SceneCAD, End-to-End CAD Retrieval) where each detected object is individually encoded and then aligned via iterative optimisation or correspondence-based registration. Instead, it repurposes the single-shot 3D object detection architecture of TR3D (sparse convolutions + transposed convolutions) to directly predict alignment parameters and shape embeddings in one forward pass, and introduces an embedding distillation step that transfers knowledge from a separately trained contrastive encoder (itself building on the joint scan-CAD embedding space of Dahnert et al.) into the detection head. For video input, it leverages the online reconstruction pipeline of DG-Recon as an intermediate point-cloud representation, avoiding the brittle per-frame detection-and-tracking pipelines of ODAM and Vid2CAD and the expensive 3D feature volume of RayTran.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× speedup (50 ms per scan) while matching or exceeding prior alignment accuracy makes CAD-based scene representation viable for real-time AR and robotics, a setting where previous methods (2.6 s to 20 min) were impractical. The introduction of reconstruction and shape accuracy metrics addresses a gap in the Scan2CAD evaluation protocol that previously ignored the quality of retrieved geometry. Open questions remain around temporal consistency in the online video setting (jittering of object shapes/alignments across frames), enforcement of physicality constraints (support relations, collision avoidance), and whether the approach generalises beyond the Scan2CAD/ShapeNet category set to open-world CAD libraries.
