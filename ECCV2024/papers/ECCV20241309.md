## Problem

Event-to-video reconstruction methods either rely on supervised training with synthetic event data (lacking interpretability and overfitting to simulator settings) or on self-supervised optical flow estimation (vulnerable to occlusions, non-convergence, and flow errors). This paper identifies that the event generation model—a PDE linking discrete events to temporal derivatives of logarithmic intensity—provides a direct, interpretable self-supervised signal that eliminates both synthetic data and optical flow dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241309/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw event stream (x, y, t, polarity) from an event camera, stacking events into 1/32-second event frames before optimization. The INR is queried at arbitrary spatiotemporal coordinates (x, y, t) and outputs a continuous logarithmic intensity value, which is exponentiated to HDR and tone-mapped via the Reinhard function to a [0,1] LDR frame. Evaluation resolutions range from 240×180 (IJRR, HQF) to 408×306 (AED), with each 5-second sub-sequence trained independently in ~8 seconds on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

EvINR parameterizes the logarithmic intensity function L(x,y,t) as a SIREN MLP (3 hidden layers, 512 neurons) and trains it by minimizing the residual between its temporal derivative (obtained via double back-propagation) and the event-accumulated intensity change prescribed by the event generation equation (Eq. 4). A Tikhonov spatial regularization term penalizes spatial gradients to suppress noise without requiring a separate denoising network. Three acceleration techniques enable near-real-time operation: frame-based optimization (mapping timestamp t to a full H×W intensity matrix rather than individual (x,y,t) coordinates, reducing convergence time by two orders of magnitude), coarse-to-fine training (3 stages with temporal upsampling at 100 and 200 iterations), and network ensembling (training multiple INRs in parallel on overlapping time segments to exploit GPU parallelism). The approach requires no end-to-end training, no optical flow, and no synthetic ground truth—only the physical event generation model as the supervision signal.

## Results

On the IJRR dataset, EvINR achieves MSE 0.047, SSIM 0.628, and LPIPS 0.251, matching the best supervised method (ET-Net, MSE 0.047) while surpassing it on SSIM. On the AED dataset (ALPIX-Eiger sensor), EvINR outperforms all supervised baselines, achieving MSE 0.067 vs. E2VID+ (0.074) and ET-Net (0.084). Compared to the best prior SSL method (SSL-E2VID), EvINR improves MSE by 35% on IJRR (0.047 vs. 0.097), 31% on HQF (0.048 vs. 0.070), and 29% on AED (0.067 vs. 0.094). Training takes approximately 8 seconds per 5-second sequence on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against supervised methods, EvINR ties ET-Net on IJRR MSE (0.047) but exceeds it on SSIM (0.628 vs. 0.617); on HQF, ET-Net retains a clear MSE advantage (0.032 vs. 0.048) due to higher event density in that dataset. On the AED dataset (ALPIX-Eiger sensor), EvINR decisively outperforms all supervised baselines, beating E2VID+ by 9% MSE (0.067 vs. 0.074) and ET-Net by 20% MSE (0.067 vs. 0.084), suggesting that DAVIS-trained supervised models overfit to sensor-specific settings. Among SSL methods, EvINR dominates across all three datasets: it improves MSE by 35% over SSL-E2VID on IJRR (0.047 vs. 0.097), 31% on HQF (0.048 vs. 0.070), and 29% on AED (0.067 vs. 0.094), while also achieving substantially better SSIM and LPIPS. The HF baseline [40] performs worst among SSL methods on all datasets (e.g., LPIPS 0.943 on AED), and ELRP [60] is consistently second-best among SSL but still trails EvINR by 25–41% in MSE. Inference speed favors EvINR as well: 178 fps at 240×180 and 55 fps at 408×306, compared to 118/29 fps for FireNet and 36/18 fps for ET-Net.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions event-to-video reconstruction as a PDE-solving problem by directly applying the event generation model (Gallego et al., 2020) as a self-supervised training signal, rather than relying on photometric constancy and optical flow (SSL-E2VID, ELRP) or synthetic simulator data (E2VID, FireNet, ET-Net). The key architectural choice is a SIREN-based implicit neural representation (Sitzmann et al., 2020) whose temporal derivative is supervised by event accumulation, borrowing the 'learn from derivatives' property of INRs. The event frame representation from E2VID is retained for practicality, but the optimization paradigm shifts from end-to-end supervised training to per-scene PDE solving with Tikhonov spatial regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both synthetic training data and optical flow estimation, EvINR addresses two major failure modes of prior approaches: the sim-to-real gap that degrades supervised methods on non-DAVIS sensors (demonstrated on the new AED dataset), and the error propagation from inaccurate flow in SSL methods. The introduction of the ALPIX-Eiger dataset is practically important for evaluating generalization beyond the DAVIS-centric evaluation landscape. Open questions include whether the per-scene INR optimization (5-second segments, ~1 GB parameters per segment) can be made truly online with a single amortized model, whether the approach extends to color reconstruction, and whether network compression can reduce the parameter overhead that currently matches the raw event data size.
