## Problem

Structured pruning relies on ranking the importance of sub-structures to decide what to remove, but modern vision models (ViTs, ConvNext) contain heterogeneous components—self-attention, MLPs, depth-wise convolutions, residual connections—that exhibit diverged parameter scales and importance distributions. Global ranking across these heterogeneous structures is unreliable, leading to biased pruning that over-removes some components while under-pruning others, ultimately degrading the efficiency-performance trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240793/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained vision model (DeiT-Base, ConvNext-Base, ResNet-152/101/50, MobileNet-V2) and a target pruning ratio as input. For importance estimation, it consumes 100 mini-batches of 64 ImageNet images (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) to compute Taylor expansion gradients. The output is a compressed network with reduced parameters and MACs that is then fine-tuned for 300 epochs; the final model accepts standard RGB images and produces ImageNet-1K class labels.

## Method

Isomorphic Pruning decomposes a network into minimal removable sub-structures modeled as graphs, where vertices are (parameter matrix, pruning axis, index) triplets and edges encode inter-layer dependencies. Sub-structures are then clustered into isomorphic groups by checking graph isomorphism: two graphs are isomorphic if they have the same number of edges and corresponding vertices share identical labels (same layer type and pruning dimension). Importance scores (first-order Taylor expansion) are aggregated per sub-structure and ranked exclusively within each isomorphic group, rather than globally across all structures. The least important p% sub-structures in each group are removed simultaneously, and the resulting model is fine-tuned. This grouped ranking ensures that importance comparisons are made only among structurally equivalent units with comparable parameter scales and computational topologies, avoiding the biased pruning that global ranking induces on heterogeneous architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Isomorphic Pruning achieves its most dramatic gain on DeiT-Tiny, improving top-1 accuracy from 74.52% (scratch-trained DeiT-T) to 77.50% while using fewer parameters (5.74M vs 5.91M) and MACs (1.21G vs 1.27G). For DeiT-Small, the pruned model reaches 82.41% accuracy with 20.69M parameters and 4.16G MACs, surpassing the scratch DeiT-S (81.17%, 22.44M, 4.64G). On CNNs, ConvNext-Tiny is improved from 82.06% to 82.19% with fewer parameters (25.32M vs 28.59M), and ResNet-101 achieves lossless compression at both 4.5G (77.56%) and 3.8G (77.43%) MAC budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DeiT-Tiny, Isomorphic Pruning (77.50%) outperforms all transformer-specific pruning baselines: NViT-T (76.21%), SAViT+KD (76.95%), UP-DeiT-T (75.79%), and ViT-Slim (77.94% but at 11.40M params vs 5.74M). For DeiT-Small, the method achieves 82.41%, beating NViT-S (82.19%), SAViT (81.66%), and Upop (81.10%) while using fewer parameters. On ResNet-101 at 4.5G MACs, Isomorphic Pruning (77.56%) surpasses SFP (77.51%), IE/Taylor (77.35%), and FPGM (77.32%), though GFP (78.33%) remains stronger at a slightly lower MAC budget (3.90G). For ResNet-50 at ~2G MACs, the method (75.91%) beats DepGraph (75.83%) and Taylor (74.50%) but trails GFP (76.42%). On MobileNet-v2 at 0.15G MACs, Isomorphic Pruning (68.91%) outperforms DepGraph (68.49%) and MetaPruning (68.20%) but is slightly below GFP (69.16%). Overall, the method shows its largest advantage on heterogeneous architectures (ViTs) where global ranking is most unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DepGraph [11] for its graph-based modeling of removable sub-structures and dependency rules, and on Taylor-based importance estimation [39] as the scoring criterion. It departs from the dominant global-ranking paradigm (used by GFP, DepGraph, and most structured pruning methods) by arguing that heterogeneous sub-structures in modern architectures (ViTs with attention/MLP/skip connections, CNNs with depth-wise convolutions) have fundamentally different importance distributions, making cross-structure comparison unreliable. The isomorphic grouping idea—ranking only within topologically identical sub-structures—is a conceptual departure from both global ranking and per-layer normalization, positioning the method as architecture-agnostic rather than tailored to a specific family like NViT (ViTs) or FPGM (CNNs).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is a single, simple pruning recipe that works across ViTs, ResNets, ConvNexts, and MobileNets without architecture-specific engineering, which is appealing for practitioners compressing diverse model portfolios. The key limitation is that the method remains one-shot (no iterative refinement) and depends on Taylor expansion requiring a data pass, and the graph isomorphism grouping, while principled, requires careful manual specification of dependency patterns for novel architectures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to generative models (diffusion, autoregressive LLMs) where sub-structure heterogeneity is even more extreme, and whether the non-uniform width it produces can be further exploited for hardware-aware deployment.
