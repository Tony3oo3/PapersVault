## Problem

Semi-supervised 3D semantic segmentation methods have not yet leveraged the rich semantic information encoded in large language-vision models (LVMs), and they typically discard unlabeled points with low-confidence predictions, losing potentially useful information. LASS3D addresses both gaps by integrating LVM-generated multi-level captions into 3D feature learning and by designing a progressive strategy to exploit unreliable points rather than simply removing them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240069/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes point clouds together with their corresponding RGB images and text captions (generated offline by KOSMOS-2 and GroundedSAM); the images serve as a geometric bridge to associate pixel-level text with 3D points via camera extrinsic/intrinsic parameters. At inference, only the teacher branch is used and takes point clouds alone as input, producing per-point semantic class labels. The text and image modalities are thus training-time-only auxiliaries, keeping the deployment pipeline identical to standard point-cloud segmentation.

## Method

LASS3D is built on the MeanTeacher framework. In the student branch, a Semantic-Aware Adaptive Fusion Module (SAFM) injects semantic information from CLIP text embeddings of image-level and entity-level captions into 3D backbone features: cross-attention adaptively fuses image-level embeddings (which carry holistic scene semantics) with 3D features, while entity-level embeddings (fine-grained object semantics) are added directly to their corresponding point features. In the teacher branch, a fusion adapter aligns plain 3D features to the text-enhanced student features, and a KL-divergence loss distills the semantic knowledge across branches. For unreliable unlabeled points (identified by a dual-score separation using both prediction confidence and inter-branch variance), a progressive negative learning strategy iteratively selects the least-likely class for each point, applies a negative cross-entropy loss, discards the used class, and repeats for up to five iterations, exploiting the observation that identifying what a point is not is more reliable than identifying what it is.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, LASS3D achieves 58.5% mIoU at 1% labeled data, 63.0% at 10%, 64.1% at 20%, and 64.5% at 50%, consistently outperforming all baselines. On ScanNet V2, LASS3D reaches 56.6% mIoU at 5% labeled data, 63.1% at 10%, 67.4% at 20%, 70.4% at 30%, and 72.0% at 40%. The largest gains over the strongest single-modality baseline (LiM3D on SemanticKITTI, GPC on ScanNet) are approximately 0.9–1.1 mIoU points, and LASS3D also surpasses the multi-modal baseline IGNet (which uses point clouds + images) by 0.7–1.7 mIoU on SemanticKITTI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASS3D outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets across every labeled ratio tested. On SemanticKITTI, the closest competitor is LiM3D [26] (58.4/62.2/63.1/63.6 mIoU at 1/10/20/50%), which LASS3D surpasses by 0.1–0.9 mIoU; the multi-modal IGNet [38] (49.0/61.3/63.1/64.8) is beaten by 0.7–9.5 mIoU, with the gap narrowing at higher label ratios. On ScanNet, GPC [17] (54.8/60.5/66.7/68.9/71.3) is the strongest baseline, and LASS3D leads by 1.8/2.6/0.7/1.5/0.7 mIoU respectively. The 2D-domain baselines (MeanTeacher, CBST, CPS) trail by 8–13 mIoU on SemanticKITTI and 5–8 mIoU on ScanNet, confirming that task-specific 3D semi-supervised designs are substantially stronger. Notably, LASS3D's advantage over IGNet—which also uses images but without text—demonstrates that the language-assisted fusion contributes meaningfully beyond simply adding an image modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASS3D is built upon the MeanTeacher framework and extends the semi-supervised 3D semantic segmentation line (GPC, LaserMix, LiM3D) by introducing language-vision models as an additional modality, which the authors claim is the first such integration in this setting. It departs from prior language-assisted 3D learning methods (PointCLIP, CLIP2Scene) that are either projection-based or alignment-based, instead proposing a fusion-based strategy (SAFM) that adaptively combines multi-level text embeddings with 3D features via cross-attention and additive fusion. The progressive exploitation strategy extends conventional negative learning by selecting multiple negative classes iteratively rather than a single one, and is designed to be modular and pluggable into existing semi-supervised methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that semantic information from LVM-generated captions can complement geometric 3D features in a semi-supervised setting, offering a practical path toward reducing annotation burden without requiring full 3D labels. The progressive exploitation strategy is a useful, lightweight module that can be dropped into existing pipelines (demonstrated with GPC) to recover information from discarded unreliable points. A key limitation is the reliance on images as a bridge between text and point clouds, which introduces accumulated projection errors; a more direct point-text pairing mechanism remains an open question. The modest gains (~1–2 mIoU over strong baselines) suggest the language-assistance signal, while helpful, is not yet a dominant factor in semi-supervised 3D segmentation performance.
