## Problem

Model Soup achieves strong ID and OOD performance by averaging weights from dozens of fine-tuned models, but this requirement for 48+ models makes it computationally impractical. The paper asks whether an effective merged weight can be obtained from only a handful of fine-tuned models, and identifies that the key geometric property enabling this is the thin-shell structure of fine-tuned weights in layer-wise weight space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241169/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained CLIP ViT backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) fine-tuned on ImageNet-1K (224×224 RGB images, 1000 classes). At inference time the I/O is standard: an RGB image in, a class label out. The method's actual 'input' is the weight tensors of two independently fine-tuned models plus the original pre-trained weights, and its 'output' is a single merged weight vector computed layer-wise via a closed-form interpolation, requiring no additional forward passes or training at merge time.

## Method

Model Stock exploits two geometric observations: (1) fine-tuned weights with different random seeds occupy a thin shell layer-wise with nearly constant norms and pairwise angles, and (2) proximity to the shell's center μ improves both ID and OOD accuracy. Given the pre-trained weight w₀ and two fine-tuned weights w₁, w₂, the method defines a plane spanned by these three points and computes the perpendicular foot w_H from μ onto that plane. Crucially, w_H is expressible in closed form as w_H = t·w₁₂ + (1−t)·w₀ where t = 2cos θ/(1+cos θ) and θ is the layer-wise angle between w₁ and w₂, so no extra training or hyperparameter search is needed. The interpolation is applied independently per layer, yielding different ratios for weight vs. bias layers. A periodic-merging variant applies this merge at the end of every training epoch, progressively pulling the weights toward the center and achieving the best results with only two fine-tuning runs total.

## Results

On CLIP ViT-B/16, Model Stock achieves 84.1% ImageNet top-1 accuracy and 62.4% average accuracy across four OOD benchmarks (ImageNet-V2, R, A, Sketch), while Model Stock* (with strong augmentation) reaches 85.2% ImageNet and 60.1% avg shifts. On the larger CLIP ViT-L/14, Model Stock* attains 87.7% ImageNet top-1 and 73.5% average OOD accuracy, surpassing all compared methods. On CLIP ViT-B/32, Model Stock* achieves 81.19% ImageNet top-1 with only 2 fine-tuning runs, matching or exceeding Model Soup (81.03%) that requires 71 fine-tuned models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Model Stock (84.1% ImageNet, 62.4% avg shifts) outperforms LP-FT (81.7%, 60.5%), CAR-FT (83.2%, 59.4%), and FLYP (82.6%, 60.5%) on both ID and OOD metrics on CLIP ViT-B/16. Against FTP, which achieves the highest ImageNet accuracy (84.2%), Model Stock trades 0.1% ID accuracy for a substantial 12.7% gain in average OOD performance (62.4% vs. 49.7%), indicating FTP's poor distribution-shift robustness. On CLIP ViT-L/14, Model Stock* (87.7%, 73.5%) surpasses TPGM (87.0%, 69.4%) and CAR-FT (87.1%, 67.8%) on both metrics. Against Model Soup on CLIP ViT-B/32, Model Stock* matches or slightly exceeds Greedy Model Soup on ImageNet (81.19% vs. 81.03%) while using only 2 fine-tuned models versus 71, though it trails on average OOD shifts (48.69% vs. 50.75%), suggesting Model Soup's diversity from varied hyperparameters still provides some OOD benefit that Model Stock's seed-only variation does not fully capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Model Soup [35], which showed that averaging weights from many fine-tuned models improves both ID and OOD performance, and on WiSE-FT [36], which interpolates between fine-tuned and pre-trained weights to preserve OOD robustness. The key departure is a geometric characterization of fine-tuned weight space—showing that weights from different seeds lie on a thin shell with consistent layer-wise angles and norms—which allows the optimal interpolation ratio toward the pre-trained anchor to be computed analytically from the angle between just two fine-tuned models, eliminating the need for dozens of models or heuristic hyperparameter search. This positions Model Stock as a principled, closed-form alternative to both the brute-force averaging of Model Soup and the empirical interpolation of WiSE-FT, while also extending the flat-minima/weight-averaging literature (SWA, SWAG, SWAD) with a concrete geometric mechanism for locating the center.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24× reduction in fine-tuning cost (2 models vs. 48+) while matching or exceeding Model Soup's accuracy makes weight merging practical in settings where training many variants is infeasible, and the periodic-merging variant offers a drop-in modification to standard fine-tuning loops. The Gaussian-shell hypothesis, if it generalizes beyond CLIP-ViT architectures, could inform weight merging for LLMs and other large pre-trained models where the authors note potential extension. Open questions include whether the geometric regularity holds for non-CLIP initializations, whether the method scales to ViT-G or larger, and whether the thin-shell property persists under more aggressive fine-tuning (e.g., full fine-tuning with large learning rates) or different task distributions.
