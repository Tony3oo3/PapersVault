## Problem

Feed-forward 3D scene reconstruction from very sparse views (as few as two images) remains challenging because existing methods like pixelSplat predict probabilistic depth distributions directly from image features, an inherently ambiguous mapping that yields noisy geometry and floating artifacts. The core gap is the lack of a principled, lightweight mechanism to accurately localize 3D Gaussian centers in a single forward pass without per-scene optimization or expensive 3D convolutions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240557/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes K posed RGB images (K ≥ 2, evaluated at 256×256) along with their camera projection matrices as input and produces H×W×K pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians (one per pixel per view), each parameterized by position, opacity, covariance, and spherical-harmonic color. The architecture is agnostic to the number of input views at test time, and the predicted Gaussians are rendered to novel views via differentiable splatting. Training uses only photometric supervision (L2 + LPIPS) against ground-truth target images, requiring no depth or geometry labels.

## Method

MVSplat's central contribution is a cost-volume-based depth estimation module that reformulates Gaussian center localization as a feature-matching problem rather than a data-driven 3D regression. Multi-view features are extracted with a ResNet backbone followed by a Swin Transformer with cross-view attention; for each view, features from other views are warped to D=128 inverse-depth candidates via plane sweeping, and dot-product correlations form a per-view cost volume. A lightweight 2D U-Net with cross-view attention refines the cost volume, and a softmax-weighted average over depth candidates yields per-view depth maps, which are unprojected and unioned to form Gaussian centers. Opacity is derived from matching confidence (max softmax value), while covariance and color are predicted by separate convolution branches. The entire pipeline uses only 2D convolutions and attentions (no 3D convolutions), is trained end-to-end with photometric loss alone, and achieves 10× fewer parameters and >2× faster inference than pixelSplat while delivering superior appearance, geometry, and cross-dataset generalization.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, MVSplat achieves PSNR 26.39, SSIM 0.869, and LPIPS 0.128, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On ACID, it reaches PSNR 28.25, SSIM 0.843, and LPIPS 0.144. MVSplat runs at 0.044 s per scene (22 fps) with only 12.0 M parameters, making it the fastest and most compact feed-forward method evaluated—more than 2× faster and 10× smaller than the closest competitor pixelSplat (0.104 s, 125.4 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVSplat outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both RealEstate10K and ACID. Against the closest competitor pixelSplat [1], MVSplat gains +0.50 dB PSNR and −0.014 LPIPS on RealEstate10K, and +0.11 dB PSNR and −0.006 LPIPS on ACID, while using 10× fewer parameters (12.0 M vs 125.4 M) and running 2.4× faster (0.044 s vs 0.104 s). Against the best NeRF-based method MuRF [41], MVSplat improves PSNR by +0.29 dB (RE10K) and +0.16 dB (ACID) with a 4.2× speed advantage. The gap over earlier feed-forward methods is larger: AttnRend [10] trails by 1.6 dB PSNR on RE10K, GPNR [33] by 2.3 dB, and pixelNeRF [46] by a substantial 5.96 dB. In cross-dataset generalization (Table 2), MVSplat trained only on RealEstate10K also beats pixelSplat on both ACID (+0.51 dB PSNR) and DTU (+1.05 dB PSNR, −0.175 LPIPS), confirming that the cost-volume-based feature matching is more robust to domain shift than pixelSplat's direct feature-to-depth regression.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVSplat sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (building on 3DGS [18] and pixelSplat [1]) and multi-view stereo cost volume construction (building on plane-sweeping [7] and MVSNet-style architectures [45]). Its key conceptual departure from pixelSplat is reframing Gaussian center localization as a feature-matching problem solved via a cost volume, rather than a data-driven regression of probabilistic depth distributions from image features. The architecture further departs from feed-forward NeRF methods (pixelNeRF, MuRF) by avoiding expensive per-pixel volumetric sampling entirely, relying instead on purely 2D convolutions and Swin-Transformer attention for depth estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× parameter reduction and 2× speedup over pixelSplat while simultaneously improving both appearance and geometry quality makes feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction substantially more practical for real-time applications. The cross-dataset generalization advantage—where a model trained only on indoor scenes slightly outperforms a model trained on the target outdoor dataset—suggests that cost-volume-based relative similarity is a more transferable geometric signal than absolute feature aggregation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to reflective/transparent surfaces, whether training on more diverse and larger-scale datasets closes the remaining gap to in-the-wild performance, and whether the cost volume formulation can be extended to unposed or pose-free settings.
