## Problem

Existing data-driven driving simulators (e.g., nuPlan) rely on replaying pre-recorded logs, requiring over 2 TB of storage and limiting evaluation to ~15-second routes with restricted controllability. The irregular, variable-count, and topologically-connected nature of driving scene entities (lane graphs, agents, traffic lights) makes direct application of modern generative models to this task non-trivial, as they operate on uniformly sized representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a one-hot city label and Gaussian noise over a fixed 8×8×64 rasterized latent map (RLM) covering a 64m×64m BEV field of view. For conditional generation, known lane tokens or a warped previous scene tile are injected via inpainting. The output is a full scene state: lane polylines (20×2 BEV points each) with a reconstructed adjacency matrix, red/green traffic-light polylines, oriented bounding boxes (position, heading, extent, speed) for vehicles, pedestrians, and static obstacles, and a 2D ego velocity vector — all in nuPlan-compatible format ready for rule-based simulation.

## Method

The core contribution is a Raster-to-Vector Autoencoder (RVAE) that encodes all entity types into a single fixed-size RLM (8×8×64) via a ResNet-50 raster encoder, then decodes them back into vector entities using a DETR-style transformer decoder with learnable queries and Hungarian matching. The RLM channels are split into lane and agent groups, and a binary cross-attention mask prevents information leakage between groups, enabling lane-conditioned agent generation. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT-L/XL) is trained on the frozen RLMs with DDPM and AdaLN-Zero conditioning on the city label. The inpainting capability of the diffusion model is exploited for two tasks: generating agents given a known lane map, and iteratively extrapolating routes beyond 64m by warping the previous tile's RSI and denoising the new region, enabling 500m+ simulations with a 64m simulation radius around the ego vehicle.

## Results

On the 64 m × 64 m lane graph generation task, DiT-XL with the RLM representation achieves the best route length (35.37 ± 10.28 m), precision (78.07% with RVEnc features), and recall (72.63%), while also attaining the lowest Fréchet distances across all four urban-planning metrics (Connectivity 0.27, Density 2.47, Reach 0.20, Convenience 0.47). In the SLEDGE simulation, PDM-Closed's failure rate rises from 6% (replay, 100 m) to over 49% (hard route + hard traffic, 500 m, joint generation), revealing previously hidden failure modes. SLEDGE requires only ~3 GB of storage versus nuPlan's 2 TB, a 500× reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DiT with RLM against four baselines: a convolutional VAE generating RSIs, the RVAE decoder (sampling from the autoencoder), HDMapGen (autoregressive hierarchical GNN), and DiT operating on RSI (image-space diffusion, similar to DriveSceneGen). DiT-XL with RLM dominates all baselines on every metric: it achieves 78.07% precision vs. 7.48% for HDMapGen and 0.00% for the VAE, and a Fréchet Reach of 0.20 vs. 2.49 for HDMapGen and 2.86 for the VAE. The RLM representation is critical—DiT-L with RLM (63.99% precision) substantially outperforms DiT-L with RSI (19.20%), confirming that the compact latent space is far more suitable for diffusion than the 524 KB rasterized state image. HDMapGen, the only prior work specifically targeting lane graph generation, produces the longest routes among non-DiT baselines (28.17 m) but suffers from high Fréchet Convenience (18.10), indicating poor path structure. In the simulation experiment, SLEDGE's generative environments expose PDM-Closed failure rates (up to 49%) far exceeding the 6–26% seen in replay-based nuPlan evaluation, demonstrating that the generated scenarios are meaningfully harder.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLEDGE builds on the latent diffusion framework (LDM) and the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, adapting them to the structured, multi-entity domain of driving scenes. Its vector decoder follows the DETR paradigm of learnable queries with Hungarian matching, while the raster-to-vector autoencoder draws on online mapping techniques (MapTR) to unify heterogeneous entities into a fixed-size 2D latent. The paper departs from prior lane-graph generation (HDMapGen's autoregressive approach) and concurrent image-space diffusion (DriveSceneGen) by jointly generating all scene elements—lanes, vehicles, pedestrians, obstacles, and traffic lights—in a single scalable model, and from replay-based simulators (nuPlan, Waymax) by learning the underlying data distribution rather than re-annotating logs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SLEDGE's 500× storage reduction (<4 GB vs. nuPlan's 2 TB) and support for 500 m routes substantially lower the barrier to entry for vehicle motion planning research, making rigorous long-horizon evaluation accessible to smaller groups. The revelation that PDM-Closed, the 2023 nuPlan challenge winner, fails on over 40% of hard 500 m routes exposes planning limitations (inability to overtake, lane-change failures) invisible on existing 15-second benchmarks, reframing what 'state of the art' means for planners. Open questions include whether the simplistic IDM-based traffic and 64 m simulation radius suffice for downstream tasks like reinforcement learning, and whether diffusion inference cost can be reduced via distillation or consistency models to make real-time generation practical.
