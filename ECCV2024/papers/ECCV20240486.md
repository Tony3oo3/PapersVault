## Problem

Existing image aesthetics assessment (IAA) methods evaluate artistic quality (composition, lighting, color harmony) but conflate this with 'content appeal'—the positive interest an image's subject matter generates in viewers, such as how appetizing food looks. This paper identifies image-content appeal assessment (ICAA) as a fundamentally distinct metric, demonstrating that professional photos of unappealing content (a moldy burger, a dirty room) receive high IAA scores despite low content appeal. No dedicated datasets or algorithms exist for ICAA, and manual annotation of large-scale assessment datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images (upscaled to 512×512) along with BLIP-generated text descriptions and CLIPSeg-derived domain-relevancy maps as auxiliary inputs. The relative comparator consumes pairs of images and outputs a scalar appeal difference; the absolute estimator takes a single image and produces a 1–10 content appeal score. For enhancement, the system additionally generates a per-pixel content appeal heatmap that spatially controls where and how strongly Stable Diffusion inpainting modifies the image, leaving already-appealing or background regions untouched.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully automated dataset creation pipeline: stock photos are collected via positive/negative adjective search queries, filtered for domain relevance using BLIP descriptions matched against WordNet lexnames and CLIPSeg segmentation, then upsampled with ESRGAN. Synthetic training data is generated by learning 'appealing' and 'unappealing' embeddings via Textual Inversion, linearly interpolating between them to produce intermediate appeal levels, and applying Stable Diffusion inpainting masked by the domain-relevancy map (with the inverse mask for background diversity). A Siamese network with shared CLIP encoders is trained on these synthetic pairs as a relative appeal comparator, which then auto-labels a large collection of real images (70K+ per domain) via a voting mechanism against exemplar reference images. The absolute appeal estimator reuses the CLIP backbone with fully connected heads, and the enhancer generates a sliding-window content appeal heatmap to spatially constrain Stable Diffusion inpainting, targeting only unappealing regions while preserving color, texture, and structure elsewhere.

## Results

The absolute content appeal estimator achieves a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.6756 on the FOOD dataset (78,917 images) and 0.6332 on the ROOM dataset (75,287 images), both on a 1–10 scale. Correlation analysis (Table 1) reveals near-zero PLCC/SRCC/KRCC values between content appeal labels and three IAA baselines (e.g., PLCC = 0.168 for DIAA on Food, −0.147 for NIMA on Room), confirming that content appeal is fundamentally distinct from image aesthetics. In the user study (28 participants, 2,072 responses), 76.53% of participants preferred the appeal-enhanced food images and 82.74% preferred the enhanced room images over their originals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three IAA baselines (DIAA [22], MPADA [53], NIMA [57]), the paper does not aim to outperform them but rather to demonstrate that content appeal is a fundamentally different axis: all correlation coefficients (PLCC, SRCC, KRCC) fall between −0.15 and 0.17, and RMSE values are large (1.79–6.71 on a 1–10 scale), confirming that a high-aesthetic image can have low content appeal and vice versa. For the enhancement task, the proposed method is compared visually against four diffusion-based editing baselines: InstructPix2Pix (IP2P) [5], Null-text Inversion (NTI) [35], pix2pix-zero (P2P0) [39], and Text2LIVE (T2L) [3]. The paper reports that NTI tends to enlarge objects without meaningfully changing appeal, P2P0 and T2L introduce blurring and shadowing artifacts with minimal appeal improvement, and IP2P over-modifies the image. In contrast, the proposed heatmap-guided inpainting (using the content appeal heatmap M_D^H to control both location and magnitude of edits) preserves the original color, texture, and structure while effectively boosting content appeal, as validated by the 76.53% (Food) and 82.74% (Room) user preference rates for enhanced images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new assessment axis—image content appeal (ICAA)—by explicitly separating the positive interest a viewer feels toward the depicted subject (e.g., wanting to eat the food) from the artistic/photographic quality of the image (IAA). Methodologically, it repurposes components from the diffusion-model editing literature (Stable Diffusion inpainting, Textual Inversion, ControlNet) and CLIP-based vision-language models (Siamese comparator, BLIP captioning, CLIPSeg segmentation) into a unified pipeline for both dataset synthesis and appeal enhancement. It departs from prior IAA work (DIAA, MPADA, NIMA) not by improving on them but by arguing they measure a fundamentally different quantity, and from prior dataset-creation efforts (IQA/IAA) by replacing costly human annotation with an automated relative-comparator labeling scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero correlation between content appeal and aesthetics scores (PLCC ≈ 0.005–0.17) is a useful reminder that 'appealing' and 'beautiful' are not the same, with direct implications for food-service, retail, and vacation-rental applications where content desirability drives conversion. The automated pipeline (stock-photo retrieval → domain-relevancy filtering → synthetic interpolation → Siamese labeling) offers a reusable template for building large assessment datasets without manual annotation. Open questions include generalization beyond the two tested domains, the explicitly deferred role of background context on perceived appeal, and whether the stock-photo bias (professional images only) limits transfer to consumer photography despite the authors' claim of generalization.
