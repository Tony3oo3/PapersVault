## Problem

Existing anti-face-recognition methods either require slow per-image optimization (Fawkes, TIP-IM take >100 s per image) or produce per-user masks that degrade under lossy image-processing pipelines (OPOM). No prior approach simultaneously delivers instant per-user protection that generalizes to unseen images, preserves visual quality, remains robust against FR models unknown at training time, and supports cost-effective de-obfuscation for authorized providers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242205/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Offline, the system takes a set of ~70–115 facial images of a single user (from FaceScrub or LFW) and outputs one pixel-level P3-Mask bounded by ε=0.063. Online, a single facial image is face-cropped via MediaPipe, the mask is quantized and resized to match the crop resolution, and protection is applied by subtracting the mask (clipped to [0,255]); the same mask added back serves as a personalized de-obfuscation key for authorized FR. The mask is a small per-pixel perturbation, not a full image, so storage and transmission costs are negligible.

## Method

Chameleon generates a per-user P3-Mask via iterative PGD-style optimization (SIGN gradients, η=0.001, 50 epochs) that jointly minimizes a protection loss (negative mean arccosine distance between protected and unprotected embeddings across a team of FR models) and a perceptibility loss (SSIM degradation thresholded at ω=0.03). The key architectural choice is a focal diversity-optimized ensemble: from a pool of N ArcFace-based models with varying backbones and training data, the system selects the k-model team whose members make the most decorrelated recognition errors, maximizing transferability of the mask to unseen FR models. Image-processing operations (quantization, resizing, padding) are folded into the forward pass so the mask is robust to the end-to-end ML pipeline. At inference, protection is a single subtraction of the pre-computed mask from the detected face region, completing in ~7.6 ms with no model inference required.

## Results

On FaceScrub and LFW, Chameleon achieves 100% Protection Success Rate (PSR) against the two known FR models used during mask generation, and a mean PSR of 96.25% (two-model team) or 98.27% (three-model team) against six unknown FR models. Protection is applied in 0.0076 seconds per face, compared to 105.12 seconds for per-image methods. The protected images maintain a mean SSIM of 0.9493, substantially higher than OPOM (0.8839) and TIP-IM (0.8850), though slightly below Fawkes (0.9612).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chameleon outperforms OPOM by a large margin in PSR; OPOM's mask degrades by approximately 33% when lossy image-processing operations (quantization, resizing) are applied, whereas Chameleon incorporates the end-to-end ML pipeline into its optimization. Against TIP-IM and Fawkes, which require per-image iterative optimization taking ~105 seconds on a GPU, Chameleon achieves equal or better PSR while completing protection in under 8 milliseconds with only simple arithmetic on an edge device. On image quality, Chameleon (SSIM 0.9493) substantially outperforms OPOM (0.8839) and TIP-IM (0.8850), though Fawkes retains a slight edge (0.9612) at the cost of its much slower per-image optimization. The focal diversity-optimized two-model team (EN-MC, RN50-GL) yields a mean PSR of 96.25% across all eight FR models, compared to only 72.56% for the least diverse team, confirming that ensemble diversity—not individual model accuracy—drives cross-model robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

Chameleon extends the per-image adversarial perturbation line (Fawkes, TIP-IM, LowKey) by shifting to a cross-image, per-user mask that is generated once and applied instantly to any photo of the same person. It departs from OPOM, the only prior per-user mask method, by incorporating end-to-end ML pipeline awareness into the optimization and by using a focal diversity framework [13,14] to select the FR model ensemble, rather than a fixed or accuracy-ranked team. The de-obfuscation key mechanism—using the same P3-Mask to restore facial identity for authorized providers—is a service-usability feature absent from all prior anti-FR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 0.0076-second protection on an edgege device versus 105 seconds of GPU optimization per image makes anti-FR feasible for ordinary users without AI accelerators. The focal diversity finding—that the best-protecting ensemble is not the highest-accuracy one—provides a principled, cheap selection criterion that generalizes beyond the eight models tested. Open questions include whether the approach scales to non-ArcFace model families in the ensemble, whether the mask survives more aggressive real-world degradations (camera noise, heavy compression chains), and whether the de-obfuscation key introduces new attack surfaces if leaked.
