## Problem

Transformer Architecture Search (TAS) has advanced significantly for RGB vision, but no systematic benchmark exists for searching transformer architectures tailored to hyperspectral image (HSI) classification, where inputs have 100–220+ spectral bands and lower spatial resolution. Existing TAS methods are computationally prohibitive (e.g., 24 GPU days for AutoFormer), and it remains unclear whether zero-cost proxy-based search can effectively identify optimal HSI transformers without full training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240847/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The search operates on hyperspectral image patches (103–224 spectral bands, spatial resolutions from 145×145 to 349×1905) that are patchified and tokenized along the spectral dimension, with a batch size of 64 for proxy scoring. Candidate architectures are drawn from a search space of 2000 encoder-only transformers parameterized by depth (4–10), embedding dimension (32–240), number of heads (3–6), and MLP ratio (1–6) per block. The output is a ranked list of architectures by proxy score, from which the top model is selected for supervised retraining and evaluated by test Overall Accuracy, model size, and Spearman correlation between proxy scores and final OA.

## Method

HyTAS defines a search space of 2000 encoder-only transformer subnetworks and evaluates 12 zero-cost proxies (GradNorm, SNIP, GraSP, Synflow, LogSynflow, Fisher, JacobCov, NASWOT, DSS, CRoZe, T-CET, ZiCo) plus a novel ZiCo++ proxy that replaces gradient statistics with Fisher information of activations and introduces a layer-decay mechanism to mitigate the bias toward deeper models. A key empirical finding is that proxies exhibit strong correlation with embedding dimension and depth rather than actual model performance, show negligible sensitivity to input data (random inputs yield nearly identical rankings), and produce heavily imbalanced scores between MSA and MLP modules (e.g., DSS MSA scores ~2472 vs. MLP ~268). The paper further demonstrates that combining proxy scores as features in a Random Forest predictor achieves 0.80 Spearman correlation with true OA—9% above the best single proxy—using only 50 trained networks, suggesting proxies are most valuable as complementary signals rather than standalone selectors.

## Results

Across five HSI datasets, the proposed ZiCo++ proxy achieves the highest Spearman correlation with test OA on Indian Pines (ρ=0.73) and Houston2013 (ρ=0.75), outperforming all 11 other proxies. In terms of selected-model OA, JacobCov attains the best accuracy on Indian Pines (0.82), Houston2013 (0.88), and KSC (0.90), while GradNorm and T-CET lead on PaviaU (0.90) and Salinas (0.94) respectively. All top-performing proxies exceed the human-crafted SpectralFormer baseline (OA 0.79–0.89) within 10 minutes of search time, though they select substantially larger models (25–30M params) compared to the oracle optimum (3–20M). The oracle upper bound reaches OA of 0.85–0.96 across datasets, revealing a persistent gap between proxy-selected and truly optimal architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZiCo++ (the paper's proposed proxy) decisively outperforms its direct predecessor ZiCo on Spearman correlation across all five datasets (e.g., 0.73 vs. 0.52 on Indian Pines, 0.75 vs. 0.62 on Houston2013, 0.83 vs. 0.63 on KSC), while matching or slightly trailing ZiCo on selected-model OA. Against the broader proxy pool, ZiCo++ achieves the best ρ on Indian Pines and Houston2013, but JacobCov wins on OA for three datasets (Indian Pines 0.82, Houston2013 0.88, KSC 0.90) at the cost of lower Spearman correlations (0.52–0.72). All data-dependent proxies (GradNorm, SNIP, Fisher, JacobCov, ZiCo, ZiCo++) surpass the human-crafted SpectralFormer on OA by 1–5 percentage points, yet select models 50–60× larger (25–30M vs. 0.48M params). GraSP is a clear outlier, producing negative Spearman correlations (−0.63 to −0.88) and the lowest OA (0.39–0.78), indicating it is unsuitable for HSI transformer search. Synflow, LogSynflow, and DSS select notably smaller models (~7–10M) with competitive OA but lower ρ than gradient-norm-based proxies, suggesting their sign-based scoring limits discriminative power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-cost proxy literature (GradNorm, SNIP, Synflow, NASWOT, DSS, ZiCo, etc.) from RGB image classification to hyperspectral imaging, a domain with fundamentally different input structure (200+ spectral bands vs. 3 RGB channels, lower spatial resolution). It builds directly on ZiCo's inverse-coefficient-of-variation principle to propose ZiCo++, which replaces raw gradient statistics with Fisher information of activations and adds a layer-decay mechanism to mitigate depth bias. Conceptually, the paper departs from compute-intensive TAS methods like AutoFormer and ViTAS by framing the problem as a training-free proxy evaluation over a fixed 2000-architecture search space, positioning HSI as an underexplored testbed for the broader TAS community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark is practically important because hyperspectral data acquisition is expensive and domain experts (agriculture, remote sensing) lack the ML expertise to design transformers; showing that proxies can beat the human-crafted SpectralFormer within minutes on a single GPU democratizes access. The key open question the paper surfaces is why proxies are largely data-independent and biased toward larger models, creating a systematic gap with oracle performance—addressing this would require proxies that genuinely incorporate task-specific input statistics. The finding that MSA and MLP modules contribute asymmetrically to proxy scores (MSA dominates by an order of magnitude) suggests that module-aware scoring or separate metrics per module type is a promising but unexplored direction.
