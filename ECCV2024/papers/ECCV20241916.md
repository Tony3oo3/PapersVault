## Problem

Image matching is fundamentally a 3D problem—corresponding pixels observe the same 3D point—yet virtually all existing methods treat it as a 2D task in image space, limiting robustness under extreme viewpoint and illumination changes. DUSt3R demonstrated that 3D-grounded matching is highly robust but produces imprecise correspondences because it was never explicitly trained for matching. This paper closes that gap by augmenting DUSt3R with a dedicated matching head and an efficient matching pipeline.

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of RGB images (variable aspect ratios supported, largest dimension 512 px per forward pass) and jointly outputs two 3D pointmaps, two confidence maps, and two dense local feature maps of dimension 24 per pixel. For high-resolution inputs (e.g., 1M+ pixels), a coarse-to-fine scheme first matches downscaled versions, then selects overlapping 512-px window pairs covering 90% of coarse correspondences and re-matches at full resolution. The final output is a set of dense pixel correspondences extracted via a fast reciprocal nearest-neighbor search (subsampled to k=3000 by default), which can be fed into PnP or essential-matrix solvers for camera pose.

## Method

MASt3R extends DUSt3R's Siamese ViT-Large encoder / ViT-Base decoder architecture with a second prediction head—a 2-layer MLP with GELU activations—that regresses unit-norm dense local feature maps (d=24) from the concatenated encoder-decoder representations. Training combines DUSt3R's confidence-aware pointmap regression loss with an InfoNCE matching loss over ground-truth 3D correspondences, which acts as a cross-entropy objective rewarding only the exact correct pixel rather than nearby ones. A key efficiency contribution is a fast reciprocal matching algorithm that iteratively propagates an initial sparse set of k pixels via nearest-neighbor lookups (using FAISS for 24-d features, K-d trees for 3D points), achieving O(kWH) complexity versus O(W²H²) for exhaustive search while also filtering outliers. The coarse-to-fine pipeline handles arbitrary-resolution images by greedily selecting window pairs that cover most coarse matches, then running the full network independently on each pair. The model is trained from DUSt3R weights for 35 epochs on 14 diverse datasets (650k pairs/epoch) with aggressive random-cropping augmentation to expose the network to multiple scales.

## Results

On the Map-free localization test set, MASt3R with auto-predicted metric depth achieves 93.3% VCRE AUC and 74.0% pose AUC (median translation 0.36 m, median rotation 2.2°), a 30-point absolute improvement over the previous best (LoFTR+KBR at 63.4% VCRE AUC). Even the purely matching-based variant (DPT depth) reaches 72.6% VCRE AUC, surpassing all prior methods. On CO3Dv2 relative pose estimation, MASt3R attains 81.8 mAA(30) and 91.9 RTA@15, outperforming DUSt3R (77.2 / 88.4) and PoseDiff (66.5 / 79.8). On RealEstate10K, MASt3R improves mAA(30) by 8.7 points over the best multi-view method and 15.2 points over pairwise DUSt3R.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASt3R decisively outperforms all 2D matching baselines on Map-free: LoFTR+KBR (63.4% VCRE AUC), RoMa (66.9%), Mickey (74.8%), and SIFT (50.4%) are all surpassed by a wide margin, with the auto-depth variant reaching 93.3% AUC. Against the closest 3D-grounded competitor DUSt3R, MASt3R improves VCRE AUC from 69.7% to 93.3% (auto) or 72.6% (DPT), and reduces median translation error from 0.97 m to 0.36 m. On CO3Dv2 and RealEstate10K relative pose, MASt3R beats DUSt3R by 4.6 and 15.2 mAA(30) points respectively, and outperforms PoseDiff (66.5 / 48.0) and RayDiff (93.3 RRA@15 on 8 views) on translation accuracy. On Aachen Day-Night, MASt3R top40 (82.2/93.9/99.5) is slightly below SP+SuperGlue (89.8/96.1/99.4) on daytime but competitive on nighttime; on InLoc, MASt3R top40 (71.0/87.0/91.6 DUC2) significantly outperforms SP+SuperGlue (53.4/77.1/82.4) and DKM (63.4/82.4/87.8). On DTU zero-shot MVS, MASt3R (0.374 Chamfer) is the only zero-shot method approaching domain-trained networks like GeoMVSNet (0.295), while DUSt3R alone performs poorly (1.741).

## Relation to Prior Work

MASt3R builds directly on DUSt3R, a transformer-based framework that jointly solves camera calibration and 3D reconstruction by regressing dense pointmaps from image pairs. The key departure is that DUSt3R was never explicitly trained for matching—correspondences were merely a by-product of 3D reconstruction—whereas MASt3R adds a dedicated matching head that regresses dense local features trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss, explicitly optimizing for pixel-accurate correspondences. This positions the work at the intersection of two previously separate lines of research: 3D reconstruction (DUSt3R, pointmap regression) and dense 2D matching (LoFTR, RoMa), unifying them under a single architecture that leverages 3D geometric context to improve matching robustness. The fast reciprocal matching algorithm and coarse-to-fine windowing scheme are practical contributions that address the quadratic complexity bottleneck of dense matching, enabling high-resolution inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30-point absolute improvement on Map-free localization (93% vs. 63% VCRE AUC) represents a substantial leap for a task that has been considered extremely challenging, and the gains are consistent across relative pose estimation, visual localization, and MVS benchmarks. A key open question is the gap between small-scene settings (Map-free, CO3D) where MASt3R excels and large-scale scenes (Aachen, InLoc) where it still trails keypoint-based methods with many retrieved images, suggesting that 3D-grounded matching may need further adaptation for large-scale localization. The finding that direct regression without explicit matching performs comparably to matching on Map-free but catastrophically fails on Aachen/InLoc highlights that the benefit of 3D grounding is scale-dependent, an important nuance for practitioners. Future work could explore extending the binocular formulation to multi-view settings and investigating whether the 3D grounding advantage persists as scene scale and viewpoint diversity increase.
