## Problem

Existing generative methods for histopathology data augmentation either cannot produce image-label pairs simultaneously, lack user-controllable spatial context for nucleus placement, or fail to incorporate tissue-type and structural context into synthesis. No prior method can co-generate realistic histopathology images with aligned multi-class semantic labels while giving users precise control over both the spatial layout of nuclei and the tissue/cell content of the generated samples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240332/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a nucleus centroid point map (encoding spatial positions and class identities of individual nuclei) and a free-form text prompt specifying tissue type, staining method, and cell types present. It produces a 256×256 histopathology RGB image, a normalized distance map, and a multi-class semantic label simultaneously; instance-level labels are then derived post-hoc via marker-controlled watershed using the distance map, semantic label, and point map as markers. The point map provides only centroid-level spatial guidance (one point per nucleus), not full pixel-wise constraints, enabling diverse label realizations from the same layout.

## Method

The core contribution is a single joint diffusion model that co-generates three modalities—image and distance map via Gaussian diffusion, and semantic label via categorical diffusion—under a composite weighted loss. Two conditioning signals are injected: a point map encoded with Residual Dense Blocks (RRDB) to control nucleus spatial layout and class distribution, and a text prompt encoded with PLIP (a pathology-specific vision-language model) to control tissue type and staining appearance. Classifier-free guidance is applied to the text condition to balance semantic alignment against overfitting. The key architectural insight is that point-map conditioning, unlike full pixel-label conditioning, allows the model to generate diverse label configurations from the same spatial layout while still faithfully reconstructing tissue structure (e.g., glandular patterns around epithelial cell points). Instance labels are extracted by applying marker-controlled watershed to the synthesized distance map with the point map as markers, yielding high-quality per-nucleus segmentation masks suitable for downstream instance segmentation tasks.

## Results

On the Lizard dataset, the proposed method achieves the best FID (38.78), IS (2.40), and FSD (0.13) among all compared generative methods, substantially outperforming SDM (FID 45.99) and Park et al. (FSD 65.06). On PanNuke, it achieves the best FID (37.35) and FSD (1.44), while on EndoNuke it achieves the best FID (69.94) and FSD (29.57). In downstream nuclei segmentation with Hover-Net, the method achieves Dice 0.824 / AJI 0.662 on PanNuke and Dice 0.899 / AJI 0.645 on EndoNuke, matching or exceeding SDM-based augmentation, and on Lizard achieves Dice 0.716 / AJI 0.484, within 0.4% of SDM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Yu et al. [38] (unconditional two-stage diffusion), the proposed method dominates on FSD by orders of magnitude (0.13 vs. 963.36 on Lizard), and Yu et al. is excluded from downstream comparison due to poor label quality. Against Semantic-Palette [19] (class-proportion conditioned), the proposed method achieves substantially lower FID (38.78 vs. 86.17 on Lizard) and lower FSD (0.13 vs. 0.55), while also generating distance maps that Semantic-Palette does not. Against Park et al. [26] (text-conditioned joint diffusion), the proposed method wins on both FID (38.78 vs. 52.65) and FSD (0.13 vs. 65.06) on Lizard, with similar margins on other datasets. Against SDM [33] (full-pixel-label conditioned diffusion, the strongest image-only baseline), the proposed method achieves better FID on all three datasets (38.78 vs. 45.99, 37.35 vs. 107.80, 69.94 vs. 105.17) while also producing labels and distance maps. On downstream segmentation, SDM edges out the proposed method by ~0.2% Dice on Lizard (0.718 vs. 0.716), but the proposed method wins on PanNuke (Dice 0.824 vs. 0.821, AJI 0.662 vs. 0.654) and EndoNuke (AJI 0.645 vs. 0.642), and achieves higher classification accuracy on PanNuke (Acc 0.736 vs. 0.702).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: (1) joint diffusion models for image-label pair generation (Park et al., SDM) and (2) structure-aware point layout generation for histopathology (Abousamra et al.). Prior diffusion-based histopathology synthesis methods (NASDM, SDM) condition on full pixel-level labels, limiting label diversity, while unconditional or randomly-labeled approaches (Yu et al.) lack spatial control. The key architectural departure is modeling a tripartite joint distribution (image + distance map + semantic label) under dual conditioning—point maps for spatial layout and PLIP-encoded text for tissue/cell type—within a single diffusion model, rather than the two-stage or single-target pipelines used previously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in computational pathology by producing user-controllable, spatially faithful synthetic nuclei data with both semantic and instance labels in one pass, directly usable for downstream segmentation and classification. The concurrent distance map generation elegantly solves the common failure mode of semantic labels merging adjacent nuclei, and the point-conditioned diverse label generation (Fig. 8) shows continuous downstream improvement where full-label-conditioned SDM saturates. Open questions include reducing the 1000-step sampling cost for large-scale synthesis and developing automatic, realistic point layout generation rather than relying on external methods. The approach is currently validated on three datasets with modest patch counts, so scalability to whole-slide imaging remains untested.
