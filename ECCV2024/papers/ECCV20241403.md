## Problem

Visible-infrared person re-identification (VI-ReID) suffers from significant cross-modality discrepancy, yet existing methods fail to fully mine modality-invariant information. They typically attend to only one dimension (spatial or channel) and operate exclusively in deep layers, overlooking specific-modality multi-dimension information available in shallow layers. This paper tackles the gap of simultaneously mining non-local spatial and channel interactions across both specific-modality and shared-modality representations.

## Input / Output

The network takes visible and infrared person images resized to 384×144 as input and produces combined feature embeddings for re-identification ranking. Features are extracted at two depths: Block4 yields a global feature R_g and N local features R_1..R_N (N=2 for SYSU-MM01/LLCM, N=6 for RegDB), while Block3 yields an auxiliary global feature Q_g and M local features Q_1..Q_M. At inference, all these features are concatenated to form the final representation used for matching query and gallery images across modalities.

## Method

WRIM-Net builds on a pre-trained ResNet50 backbone and introduces two key components. The Multi-dimension Interactive Information Mining (MIIM) module compresses spatial and channel dimensions, then applies Multi-Head Attention via a Global Region Interaction (GRI) sub-module where higher-resolution features serve as Query and lower-resolution features serve as Key/Value, establishing long-range spatial and channel dependencies at low computational cost. Separate MIIMs are inserted after Blocks 1 and 2 for specific-modality mining, while a shared MIIM after Blocks 3 and 4 captures shared-modality information. The Auxiliary-Information-based Contrastive Learning (AICL) approach introduces a Cross-Modality Key-Instance Contrastive (CMKIC) loss that selects top-K least-similar same-ID cross-modality samples as positives, increasing task difficulty to force the network toward modality-invariant features, and supplements this with an ID loss (triplet + cross-entropy) on auxiliary Block3 features. The total training objective is a weighted sum of cross-entropy, CMKIC, and ID losses with λ₁=0.5 and λ₂=0.1.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (All-Search, Multi-shot), WRIM-Net achieves 83.2% Rank-1 and 71.1% mAP, outperforming the previous best (PartMix: 80.5% R1, 69.8% mAP) by 2.7% and 1.3% respectively. On RegDB, WRIM-Net reaches 94.5% R1 / 90.5% mAP (VIS2IR) and 93.7% R1 / 89.7% mAP (IR2VIS), setting new mAP records in both directions. On the large-scale LLCM benchmark, WRIM-Net achieves 58.4% R1 / 64.8% mAP (IR2VIS) and 67.0% R1 / 69.2% mAP (VIS2IR), surpassing the prior best (DEEN: 54.9% R1 / 62.9% mAP) by 3.5% and 1.9% in the IR2VIS direction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WRIM-Net achieves the best or near-best results across all three benchmarks. On SYSU-MM01, it outperforms the strongest competitors (PartMix, CMT, CAL) by 2.7–6.1% in Rank-1 and 1.3–4.6% in mAP in the Multi-shot settings, with the largest gains in the Indoor-Search Multi-shot mode (92.1% R1 vs. PartMix's 88.0%). On RegDB, WRIM-Net sets new mAP records in both VIS2IR (90.5% vs. CAL's 88.7%) and IR2VIS (89.7% vs. CAL's 87.6%), though CMT and MUN marginally lead in VIS2IR Rank-1 (95.2% vs. 94.5%). On the challenging LLCM benchmark, WRIM-Net dominates with substantial margins: 3.5% R1 and 1.9% mAP over DEEN (IR2VIS), and 6.6% R1 and 3.4% mAP over DART (VIS2IR). Among attention-mechanism baselines, MIIM outperforms CBAM by 4.6% R1 and 3.7% mAP, and Non-Local by 4.65% R1 and 4.31% mAP at comparable FLOPs (7.19B vs. 7.27B). The CMKIC loss also surpasses both triplet loss (56.53% R1) and standard contrastive loss (56.79% R1) by 1.85% and 1.59% respectively in the ablation on LLCM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-level VI-ReID line by combining spatial and channel attention interactions within a single plug-and-play module (MIIM), whereas prior methods like AGW, DDAG, and MPANet applied attention in only one dimension. It departs from the common dual-stream paradigm (separate shallow / shared deep parameters) by inserting the same MIIM module in both shallow and deep blocks, enabling specific-modality multi-dimension mining in layers where prior work only performed simple parameter separation. The CMKIC loss builds on contrastive learning for cross-modality alignment but introduces a top-K least-similar cross-modality positive selection strategy, distinguishing it from standard InfoNCE-style losses that use a single augmented positive or random negatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The low-complexity design of MIIM (spatial and channel compression before MHA) is practically important because it makes multi-dimension attention feasible in shallow layers where feature maps are large, a regime where prior non-local or CBAM-style modules were too expensive to deploy. The consistent SOTA across three datasets of increasing difficulty (RegDB → SYSU-MM01 → LLCM) suggests the approach generalizes beyond small-scale benchmarks. Open questions include whether the specific compression ratios and top-K selection transfer to other cross-modality recognition tasks (e.g., vehicle ReID), and whether the single-stream ResNet-50 backbone limits the ceiling compared to transformer-based alternatives.
