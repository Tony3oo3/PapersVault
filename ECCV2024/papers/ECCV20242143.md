## Problem

The Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), the de-facto fidelity metric for evaluating generative spherical image models, fails to detect geometric distortions unique to the spherical format—such as reduced vertical field-of-view and seam misalignments—because its Inception V3 backbone was trained on ordinary 2D linear-perspective images. This paper demonstrates that FID scores remain nearly unchanged even when the vertical FOV is reduced from 180° to 140°, exposing a critical blind spot for spherical image quality assessment.

## Input / Output

OmniFID takes two sets of equirectangular spherical images (e.g., 1024×512), converts each to a six-face tangential cubemap projection, and outputs a single scalar fidelity score comparing the two distributions. DS takes a single equirectangular image and outputs a per-image scalar measuring seam discontinuity at known border locations. The cubemap conversion is essential: each face is a square rectilinear view, preserving aspect ratio when resized to Inception V3's 299×299 input, unlike the 2:1 equirectangular format.

## Method

OmniFID projects each equirectangular image into six square cubemap faces, extracts 2048-d Inception V3 features per face, groups faces into three semantically coherent subsets (frontal {F,R,B,L}, up, down), averages features within each group to control for sample-size bias, computes FID per group, and returns the mean of the three group-level FID scores. This grouping is empirically justified: frontal-view feature means are nearly identical (L² ≈ 0.34) while up/down views differ substantially (L² ≈ 24–33) due to polar stitching distortion. The Discontinuity Score (DS) applies a 3×3 Scharr kernel for horizontal edge detection on 6-pixel-wide strips straddling each known seam in the equirectangular image, then computes the relative intensity ratio across the seam (|edge response on one side| / |edge response on the other side| + c) to amplify abrupt semantic discontinuities while suppressing clutter. The per-seam scores are summed and scaled by seam length relative to image height, so representations with more seams can yield proportionally higher scores.

## Results

On the 360-Indoor dataset (3,335 equirectangular images, 1920×960), reducing vertical field-of-view from 180° to 140° yields an FID of only ~10, indicating FID is nearly blind to this geometric degradation, while OmniFID captures the difference substantially. For noise robustness, OmniFID closely tracks FID across salt-and-pepper, Gaussian noise, and Gaussian blur at four intensity levels, confirming the extension preserves FID's noise sensitivity. The Discontinuity Score (DS) using a Scharr kernel (c=0.1) yields near-zero scores on ground-truth datasets (360-Indoor, PolyHaven, Laval), a substantial increase on artificially corrupted Laval images (0.25% border crop), and the highest scores on generated images with semantic seam misalignments, with conclusions consistent across four image resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard FID computed on equirectangular projections, which is the de-facto metric in prior spherical image generation work (Text2Light, AOG-Net, etc.). On the critical field-of-view reduction task (180°→140°), FID scores only ~10—barely distinguishable from the sample-size bias floor for 3,335 images—while OmniFID produces a substantially higher score, clearly flagging the geometric degradation. On noise-type evaluations (salt-and-pepper, Gaussian noise, Gaussian blur at four levels), OmniFID closely mirrors FID's trend, confirming the cubemap-based extension does not sacrifice FID's well-known noise sensitivity. The paper also demonstrates that FID and OmniFID both fail to detect seam discontinuities (e.g., objects stopping abruptly across image borders), which is the gap filled by the proposed Discontinuity Score. Overall, OmniFID wins on the geometric-fidelity axis (FOV) while matching FID on the noise axis, and DS provides a complementary signal for border alignment that neither FID nor OmniFID captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FID metric (Heusel et al., 2017) to the spherical image domain, departing from the common practice of applying FID directly to equirectangular projections. Rather than adapting the Inception backbone to spherical geometry (as SphereNet, Eder & Frahm, or Tateno et al. do for classification/segmentation), the authors take the inverse approach: they re-project spherical images into cubemap views that better conform to the rectilinear perspective the Inception network was trained on. The Discontinuity Score is a new, lightweight kernel-based metric with no direct prior in the spherical image evaluation literature, drawing on standard edge-detection practice (Scharr kernels) but repurposed for seam alignment scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since FID is the de-facto standard for evaluating spherical image generators, demonstrating its blindness to field-of-view degradation (FID ≈ 10 for a 40° vertical FOV loss) is practically important for the growing VR/immersive content community. The paper's limitations are notable: cubemap faces are scored independently, losing global cross-seam information; experiments rely on a single dataset (360-Indoor) with only ~3.3k samples; and the generative model evaluations use an internal Imagen-based pipeline not publicly reproducible. Open questions include how perceptual metrics like LPIPS behave across different spherical projections, whether finer polyhedral partitions (e.g., icosahedra) are needed as generative quality improves, and how these metrics scale to video or higher-resolution spherical content.
