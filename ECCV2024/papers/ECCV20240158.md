## Problem

Current MLLMs lack fine-grained localization capabilities, preventing them from grounding textual understanding to specific image regions. Existing solutions either force the LLM to regress coordinates (computationally expensive and ill-suited for dense prediction) or bolt on external modules like SAM (adding architectural complexity). This paper asks whether localization can be embedded directly into the visual tokenization process, decoupling it from the language model entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240158/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a 448×448 RGB image, a text instruction, and optionally user-specified bounding boxes. The image is encoded by DINOv2 at full resolution, then 2D-merged (4×4 neighbor patches → 1 token) to limit LLM sequence length to ~356 visual tokens total. Outputs are grounded text responses where specific phrases are wrapped in <p>...</p> tags and anchored to regions via proxy tokens like <roi><r4></roi>, or a single bounding box for referring expression comprehension queries. A [grounding] special token in the prompt triggers the grounded response format.

## Method

Groma decomposes grounding into localization (handled by the visual tokenizer) and recognition (handled by the LLM). A DDETR-based class-agnostic region proposer generates 300 proposals from DINOv2-L/14 feature pyramids, filtered by NMS and objectness score to the top 100; a multiscale ROIAlign region encoder converts these (and any user-specified boxes) into continuous region tokens that are semantically aligned with their underlying ROIs. Proxy tokens <r1>…<rn> register these region tokens into the LLM's vocabulary, allowing Vicuna-7B to reference regions by name rather than regressing coordinates. Training proceeds in three stages: detection pretraining (frozen encoder, train only the region proposer on ~2M+ boxes from COCO/Objects365/OpenImages/V3Det/SA1B), alignment pretraining (train MLP projection + region encoder on region-level vision-language data), and instruction finetuning (unfreeze LLM on Groma Instruct, a 30k-sample grounded chat dataset generated by GPT-4V using Set-of-Mark visual prompting on Visual Genome images, plus LLaVA Instruct and ShareGPT-4V).

## Results

On the three standard referring expression comprehension benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg), Groma achieves an average accuracy of 86.52% (IoU=0.5), surpassing all generalist MLLMs of similar size (Qwen-VL: 85.83%, Ferret: 83.91%, MiniGPT-v2: 84.29%) and approaching specialist detectors (UNINEXT-L: 86.95%, G-DINO: 86.60%). On the newly introduced LVIS-Ground benchmark, Groma achieves 28.8 AR, outperforming the next-best method Ferret (16.8 AR) by over 12 points. On region captioning, Groma reaches 16.8 METEOR / 107.3 CIDEr on RefCOCOg and 19.0 METEOR / 158.4 CIDEr on Visual Genome without task-specific finetuning, matching or exceeding GLaMM (16.1/101.9 and 19.0/163.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Groma outperforms all generalist MLLMs on the REC benchmarks: it beats Qwen-VL (85.83) by 0.69 points, Ferret (83.91) by 2.61 points, MiniGPT-v2 (84.29) by 2.23 points, and Shikra (82.93) by 3.59 points in average accuracy. Against specialist detectors, Groma (86.52) is within 0.43 points of G-DINO (86.60) and 0.43 points of UNINEXT-L (86.95), demonstrating that the localized tokenization approach achieves near-specialist grounding without task-specific architecture. On the LVIS-Ground benchmark, Groma (28.8 AR) leads Ferret (16.8) by 12.0 points, MiniGPT-v2 (11.4) by 17.4 points, and Shikra (4.9) by 23.9 points, with the largest gap on AR@0.75 (30.3 vs. 16.3 for Ferret). On region captioning, Groma (16.8 METEOR, 107.3 CIDEr on RefCOCOg) surpasses GLaMM (16.1, 101.9) and ViP-LLaVA (16.6, 105.9) without any task-specific finetuning, though it trails GLaMM on Visual Genome CIDEr (158.4 vs. 163.9). On LLaVA-Bench conversational VQA, Groma (85.2 average) slightly trails Ferret (86.7) but exceeds LLaVA (81.9) and LLaVA-G (81.2), with its strongest sub-score in detailed description (84.0 vs. 79.4 for Ferret).

## Relation to Prior Work

Groma departs from two established paradigms for grounded MLLMs: (1) LLM-based localization that quantizes coordinates into discrete tokens (Kosmos-2, Shikra), and (2) external-module approaches that bolt on segmentation models like SAM (LISA, GLaMM). Instead, it draws on the open-vocabulary detection insight of decomposing grounding into localization and recognition, embedding a DDETR-based region proposer and ROIAlign-based region encoder directly into the visual tokenization pipeline. The token-merging strategy follows MiniGPT-v2 with a 2D modification, and the GPT-4V-assisted data generation pipeline builds on Set-of-Mark prompting and ShareGPT-4V-style captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean decoupling of localization from the LLM is validated by the frozen-LLM ablation showing near-full grounding accuracy without any LLM finetuning, suggesting a practical recipe for adding grounding to existing MLLMs without expensive joint training. The introduction of LVIS-Ground (multi-object, 1203 categories) and Groma Instruct (first grounded chat dataset with visual prompts) fills notable gaps in evaluation and training data. However, the current design is limited to bounding-box regions and does not support free-form inputs or pixel-level grounding, leaving mask-based and arbitrary-shape grounding as open problems.
