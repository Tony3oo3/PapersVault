## Problem

Web-crawled AltTexts used for CLIP pre-training are often noisy, uninformative, or irrelevant to the image content, limiting image-text alignment. Existing LLM-based caption rewriting methods (e.g., LaCLIP) can only rephrase existing text syntax and cannot introduce new image-specific visual concepts, making them ineffective on noisy data. The paper asks whether a scalable, cost-effective pipeline can enrich noisy web-crawled captions with visual information extracted directly from images at scale (up to 300M+ samples).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes RGB images paired with noisy web-crawled AltTexts as input. LLaVA generates a short visual description (≤20 words) from the image alone, independent of the AltText, and a text-only LLM (Vicuna-1.1-13B) then fuses both into a final VeCap. During CLIP training, each image-text pair is randomly assigned either the original AltText or the VeCap (mixed training), so the model sees both caption styles. The trained model outputs standard CLIP image and text embeddings for downstream retrieval and zero-shot classification tasks.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage recaptioning pipeline combined with a mixed training scheme. First, LLaVA is prompted with an AltText-independent instruction ('Describe the image concisely, less than 20 words') to extract visual concepts directly from the image, producing a Visual-enriched Caption (VeC). Second, Vicuna-1.1-13B fuses the original AltText and VeC into a single sentence under constraints (attributes before nouns, no 'The image' prefix, no new semantic content), yielding the final VeCap. During CLIP contrastive training, a mixed sampling strategy randomly selects either the AltText or VeCap for each pair per epoch, preserving data diversity while improving caption quality without filtering any data. The pipeline is executed via batch inference (up to 64× faster on A100) and includes failure-case handling for ethical content and length overruns, enabling scaling to 300M samples.

## Results

At the 12M scale, VeCLIP achieves 47.78% R@1 on COCO I2T and 73.90% R@1 on Flickr30k I2T, representing +23.26% and +29.20% gains over vanilla CLIP respectively. At 100M, VeCLIP (64.82% COCO I2T R@1) surpasses the 1.4B in-house CLIP (61.38%) using only 7% of the data, and at 200M it outperforms 1.8B ALIGN (58.60%) by +8.60% on COCO I2T R@1 with just 11.1% of the training data. For zero-shot classification, the H/14 VeCap+DFN model reaches 83.1% ImageNet top-1 accuracy, and VeCLIP consistently improves VTAB average accuracy by 1.9–5.0% across scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ALIGN (1.8B data), VeCLIP at 200M achieves +8.60% on COCO I2T R@1 (67.20 vs 58.60) and +2.80% on COCO T2I R@1 (48.40 vs 45.60) while using only 11.1% of the training data. Against FLIP (400M, ViT-L/14), VeCLIP at 100M (ViT-B/16) already surpasses it on COCO I2T (64.82 vs 60.20) and Flickr30k I2T (89.30 vs 89.10). Against OpenAI CLIP (400M), VeCLIP at 100M leads by +11.06% on COCO I2T R@1 and +1.30% on Flickr30k I2T R@1. Against DFN, VeCap alone wins on retrieval (e.g., 66.3 vs 63.0 COCO I2T R@1 at B/16) but loses on ImageNet classification (76.2 vs 76.2 tied at B/16; 81.1 vs 81.4 at L/14); the VeCap+DFN combination achieves the best overall, reaching 83.1% ImageNet at H/14. Against MetaCLIP (H/14, 224), VeCap+DFN (H/14, 336) outperforms on COCO I2T (72.8 vs 67.2) and Flickr30k I2T (93.6 vs 92.1) but trails slightly on ImageNet (83.1 vs 80.5 is actually a win; MetaCLIP reports 80.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP contrastive pre-training framework by addressing the quality of noisy web-crawled AltTexts, building directly on LaCLIP's LLM-based caption rewriting but departing from it in a key way: rather than relying solely on LLMs to rephrase existing text (which cannot introduce new visual information), it first extracts image-specific concepts via LLaVA and then fuses them with the original AltText using a separate LLM (Vicuna). The mixed training scheme—alternating between AltText and VeCap during training—repurposes the idea of data augmentation from the image side to the text side, a dimension largely unexplored in prior CLIP training. Unlike prior LLM-rewriting work evaluated only on small curated datasets (CC3M/CC12M), this paper targets the noisy, unfiltered WIT-scale data that underpins most large-scale CLIP training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a cost-effective, scalable pipeline that improves CLIP pre-training on noisy web data without discarding samples, achieving competitive retrieval performance with only 11–14% of the data used by ALIGN or vanilla CLIP. The finding that visual-enriched captions substantially boost retrieval but can hurt zero-shot classification (due to prompt distributional shift) highlights an important quality-diversity trade-off that the mixed training scheme partially resolves. Open questions remain around hallucination in LLaVA-generated captions, potential bias inherited from the foundation models, and whether the VeCap pipeline generalizes to other VLM architectures beyond CLIP's contrastive objective.
