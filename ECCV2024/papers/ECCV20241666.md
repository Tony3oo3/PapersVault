## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation for semantic segmentation has not exploited the domain-agnostic properties of text embeddings from vision-language models. While RISE demonstrated that text can regularize domain-invariant features for image classification, this idea has not been transferred to segmentation, where pixel-level spatial resolution must be preserved and text and visual features reside in separate latent spaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241666/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes 1024×1024 source-domain image crops with pixel-level ground truth masks (19 or 16 classes) alongside unlabeled target-domain images. At inference, a single target-domain RGB image is fed to the segmentation encoder-decoder to produce a full-resolution pixel-wise class mask. Domain-agnostic text embeddings are precomputed once at initialization by passing LLM-generated domain attribute prompts through a frozen ViT-B/32 CLIP text encoder and stored in a memory bank.

## Method

CoPT introduces a covariance-based pixel-text loss that enforces the pairwise distance structure between class representations in the segmentation encoder's latent space to match that of domain-agnostic text embeddings. Domain-agnostic text embeddings are produced by an LLM Domain Template process: ChatGPT generates K long-form attribute descriptions per domain, each formatted into a class-specific prompt, embedded by a frozen CLIP text encoder, and averaged; source and target embeddings are then averaged to yield a single domain-agnostic vector per class. Class pixel features are extracted by downsampling ground truth masks to the encoder's spatial resolution, applying the binary mask to encoder feature maps, and spatially averaging. The pixel covariance matrix (cosine similarities between class pixel features) and the text covariance matrix are compared via cosine distance, minimized as an auxiliary loss (weight 1) on top of existing UDA pipelines such as MIC. A memory bank with linear decay (λ=0.5) stabilizes class pixel features across small-batch iterations where not all classes appear simultaneously.

## Results

On the primary GTA→Cityscapes benchmark, CoPT (built on MIC) achieves 76.08% mIOU across 19 classes, a 1.47-point improvement over the MIC baseline (74.61%) and setting a new state of the art. On Synthia→Cityscapes, CoPT reaches 67.4% mIOU, marginally surpassing MIC (67.3%). On CS→ACDC adverse conditions, CoPT improves the Fog condition by 3.6 points (60.4% vs 56.8%) over MIC, yielding a 0.4-point overall mIOU gain (63.7% vs 63.3%). CoPT also outperforms all prior methods on CS→DarkZurich, achieving the best mIOU and top-1-or-2 performance on nearly every class.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoPT outperforms all five baselines on the GTA→Cityscapes benchmark. Against the strongest prior method MIC (74.61% mIOU), CoPT gains +1.47 points to reach 76.08%, with notable per-class improvements on Train (+14.9), Terrain (+3.4), Fence (+3.4), and Wall (+1.9). CoPT also surpasses PiPa (HRDA) by roughly 0.5 mIOU points and HRDA by over 2 points. On Synthia→Cityscapes, CoPT (67.4%) edges out both MIC and PiPa (HRDA) (67.3%), with the largest gains on Bus (+4.6) and Wall (+2.5), though it trails on Road (−3.2) and Sidewalk (−6.2) due to occlusion artifacts in the synthetic source. On CS→ACDC, CoPT's advantage is concentrated in the Fog condition (+3.6 over MIC), while Rain, Snow, and Night remain nearly identical, suggesting CoPT primarily improves intra-class consistency rather than handling drastic appearance shifts. The one class where CoPT underperforms MIC is Traffic Sign (70.5 vs 73.1), attributed to the loss's reliance on spatially downsampled features that lose fine boundary detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RISE (ICCV 2023), which used domain-agnostic CLIP text embeddings to guide domain-general image classification, to the segmentation setting where pixel and text features occupy separate latent spaces. It departs from the dominant UDA-for-segmentation paradigm of self-training with EMA teachers (MIC, HRDA, DAFormer) by introducing a cross-modal regularization loss rather than relying on pseudo-label bootstrapping or adversarial alignment. The LLM Domain Template process replaces RISE's hand-crafted domain descriptors with LLM-generated long-form domain attribute descriptions, and the covariance-matching objective (CoPT) is the key technical bridge that makes text-space structure transferable to a segmentation encoder's feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoPT demonstrates that domain-agnostic text embeddings can serve as a useful geometric anchor for segmentation encoder features, opening a path for vision-language models to inform UDA beyond classification. However, the gains are modest (~1.5% mIOU) and the LLM template advantage over hand-crafted ones is marginal (0.13%), suggesting the core benefit comes from the covariance-matching mechanism rather than the specific text generation strategy. A notable limitation is reliance on downsampled encoder features, which hinders improvement on fine-grained classes like traffic signs; the authors acknowledge this but leave the fix (cropped upsampled features) as future work. Whether this cross-modal regularization transfers to other dense prediction tasks or to settings without source labels remains open.
