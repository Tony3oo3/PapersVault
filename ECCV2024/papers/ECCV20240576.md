## Problem

Existing 3D wireframe methods either reconstruct wireframes from images or point clouds without generating novel structures, or generate 3D meshes by modeling vertices, edges, and faces as independent sequences, causing error propagation and ignoring semantic relationships among geometric primitives. No prior work produces unconditional 3D wireframes that are both structurally coherent and semantically organized into meaningful components like walls, roofs, and rooms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model requires no input and autoregressively samples a sequence of quantized geometric tokens from a learned vocabulary of size 8192 (with D=2 residual levels, yielding 4 embeddings per line segment at 13 dimensions each). The output is a set of 3D line segments (constrained to fewer than 400 per wireframe, spatially discretized at 128³ resolution) whose connectivity naturally partitions the wireframe into semantic sub-components. An optional text prompt can be injected via cross-attention for conditional generation, and the resulting wireframe is directly convertible to a mesh model.

## Method

The approach uses a unified wire-based representation where line segments are graph nodes and their junctions are edges, replacing the separate vertex/edge/face modeling of prior work. A two-phase pipeline first learns a geometric token vocabulary via a graph autoencoder: SAGEConv layers capture local topology while Local Multi-Head Attention exchanges global context across disconnected subgraphs, and Residual Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ) compresses vertex features into discrete codes. A coarse-to-fine decoder-only transformer then autoregressively predicts the token sequence—first at the line-segment level (coarse), then at the vertex level along the residual depth (fine)—with tokens ordered by BFS traversal of each disconnected subgraph in z-y-x hierarchical order to encode semantic grouping. A decoder network (LMH Attention + 1D ResNet34) maps the predicted codes back to 3D coordinates, and duplicate vertices are merged to form the final wireframe.

## Results

On the 3D House Wireframe Dataset, the proposed method achieves the best scores across all metrics: COV of 56.15% (CD) and 58.64% (EMD), MMD of 8.11 (CD) and 5.75 (EMD), 1-NN of 55.21% (CD) and 51.35% (EMD), structural validity of 99.53% (2L-CVP) and 99.26% (3L-CVP), and KLD of 0.73. The structural validity metrics are particularly striking, with near-perfect vertex connectivity (99.53% of vertices connected to ≥2 segments) compared to 81.47% for PolyGen and 80.91% for MeshGPT. On the more diverse ABC dataset, the method also leads with COV 44.10%, MMD 22.12, and 1-NN 62.96%. A user study with 60 participants confirmed perceptual superiority, with the method preferred over PolyGen (0.84) and MeshGPT (0.75) in pairwise comparisons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two autoregressive 3D generation baselines: PolyGen (ICML 2020) and MeshGPT (2023), both modified to predict line segments instead of mesh vertices/faces for a fair comparison. The proposed method outperforms both on every metric. Against PolyGen, the gains are most dramatic in structural validity (99.53% vs. 81.47% 2L-CVP) and KLD (0.73 vs. 12.75), reflecting PolyGen's tendency to produce floating vertices and disconnected segments due to its sequential vertex-then-edge generation. Against MeshGPT, the method leads on COV (56.15% vs. 54.78% CD), MMD (8.11 vs. 9.13 CD), and structural validity (99.53% vs. 80.91% 2L-CVP), with the gap in KLD (0.73 vs. 8.98) indicating much better preservation of the component-count distribution. The 1-NN distance is also lower for the proposed method (55.21% vs. 74.43% for PolyGen, 55.21% vs. 64.61% for MeshGPT), confirming closer spatial correspondence to test-set wireframes. The user study further validates these quantitative findings, with participants preferring the proposed method's output 92% of the time over PolyGen and 87% over MeshGPT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive 3D generation paradigm established by PolyGen, MeshGPT, and SolidGen, which serialize vertices, edges, and faces as independent primitive sequences. The key departure is a unified wire-based representation where line segments are the atomic generation unit, eliminating the error propagation that occurs when one primitive type's inaccuracy cascades into others. The semantic-aware BFS ordering of disconnected subgraphs (walls, roofs, rooms) is a novel sequencing strategy not present in prior coordinate-based ordering schemes, and the encoder combines SAGEConv graph convolutions with LMH Attention to handle both local topology and global spatial context across disconnected components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to generate 3D house wireframes with explicit semantic grouping, and it introduces a new ~78K-sample dataset that did not previously exist. The near-perfect structural validity (99.5% 2L-CVP) demonstrates that treating wires as atomic units fundamentally resolves the connectivity failures plaguing vertex-first generation. However, the approach is scoped to house wireframes where semantic components (walls, roofs, rooms) are naturally disconnected subgraphs; generalizing to arbitrary 3D wireframes without such clear semantic partitioning remains open. The authors note future work on conditional generation (text, images, point clouds) and deeper semantic integration, suggesting the current semantic structure is largely inherited from the domain rather than learned.
