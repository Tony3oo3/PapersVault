## Problem

Existing personalized object swapping methods are limited to replacing the main subject, fail to perfectly preserve surrounding context pixels, and struggle to harmonize the new concept with the source image's style, scale, and spatial layout. SwapAnything addresses these gaps by enabling precise, arbitrary object (or partial-object) replacement while keeping all non-target pixels unchanged and seamlessly adapting the swapped concept to the original scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240857/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a reference image of the concept to insert (tokenized via DreamBooth into a single text token), and a binary mask delineating the swapping region; for text-based swapping, the reference is replaced by a text prompt. The mask can be obtained from an off-the-shelf segmentation model or extracted during the diffusion process. Output is a full-resolution edited image where only the masked region is replaced while all other pixels remain pixel-identical to the source. The framework handles arbitrary aspect ratios despite relying on Stable Diffusion 2.1, and supports iterative multi-object swapping by chaining single-object operations.

## Method

The core insight is that the U-Net latent feature z in a latent diffusion model exhibits pixel-level correspondence with the generated image, enabling localized editing by selectively swapping only masked regions of intermediate variables. Targeted Variable Swapping applies a binary mask to the latent feature, cross-attention map, self-attention map, and self-attention output, preserving context pixels while allowing the target region to be regenerated from the concept token. Appearance Adaptation then integrates the new concept through four mechanisms: Location Adaptation (mask-gated local swapping of latent features), Style Adaptation (Masked AdaIN that modulates concept features with source-image statistics), Scale Adaptation (classifier-free guidance with a shape-energy term aligning the generated object's cross-attention shape to the mask), and Content Adaptation (Gaussian-blurred mask dilation plus gradual temporal boundary transition to eliminate high-frequency edge artifacts). The entire pipeline is training-free at inference time, relying on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2.1 backbone, DreamBooth for concept tokenization, and null-text DDIM inversion for source-image encoding.

## Results

SwapAnything is evaluated via human preference studies across four metrics: Object Swapping (SS), Object Gesture (SG), Background Preservation (BP), and Overall Quality (OQ). The method achieves the highest human preference in every comparison against all seven baselines, with preference rates ranging from 40.0% (vs. BLIP-Diffusion on BP) to 71.1% (vs. CopyPaste on OQ). The largest margins are seen against MasaCtrl (64.3% SS, 65.3% SG) and CopyPaste (71.1% OQ), while the closest competitor is PnP on SS (52.3% vs. 29.9%). Notably, all attention-based baselines were augmented with SwapAnything's mask components for a fair comparison, yet SwapAnything still leads on appearance adaptation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwapAnything outperforms all seven baselines (Photoswap, P2P, PnP, MasaCtrl, BLIP-Diffusion, DreamEdit, CopyPaste) on every human-evaluation metric. The strongest margins are against MasaCtrl (64.3% SS, 65.3% SG preference) and CopyPaste (71.1% OQ preference), indicating that methods lacking targeted variable swapping or relying on simple copy-paste compositing are clearly inferior. PnP is the closest competitor on Object Swapping (29.9% baseline preference vs. 52.3% for SwapAnything), but still trails by over 20 percentage points. On Background Preservation, the gap narrows somewhat (e.g., 41.5% vs. 32.0% against Photoswap), reflecting that the baselines were augmented with SwapAnything's mask components for fair comparison. On Overall Quality, SwapAnything leads by 25–60 percentage points across all baselines, with the largest gap against CopyPaste (71.1% vs. 10.5%), underscoring the importance of the full appearance-adaptation pipeline (style, scale, and content adaptation) beyond mere region-level swapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwapAnything builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the personalized concept encoding of DreamBooth, but departs from prior attention-manipulation editing methods (P2P, PnP, MasaCtrl) by directly targeting the latent feature z in addition to attention variables, and from prior personalized swapping methods (Photoswap, CustomEdit, BLIP-Diffusion) by introducing mask-based region control that preserves context pixels exactly. The key conceptual departure is the 'targeted variable swapping' strategy, which applies a binary mask to selectively replace only the object region in intermediate U-Net variables while leaving background variables untouched, combined with a multi-dimensional appearance adaptation pipeline (location, style via Masked AdaIN, scale via classifier-free guidance, content via mask smoothing) that has no direct analogue in prior swapping work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to swap arbitrary objects (not just the main subject) while perfectly preserving all context pixels addresses a practical gap in personalized image editing that prior methods could not handle, with demonstrated generality across single-object, multi-object, partial-object, and cross-domain settings. However, the method is tightly coupled to Stable Diffusion 2.1's U-Net architecture and requires DreamBooth fine-tuning for each personalized concept, limiting scalability. The authors acknowledge ethical risks around face swapping and note that extension to 3D/video swapping remains open, suggesting the current framework is a 2D image-only solution whose principles may or may not transfer to higher-dimensional settings.
