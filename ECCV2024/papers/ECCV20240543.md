## Problem

Existing Active Visual Exploration methods are constrained to selecting glimpses from rigid, fixed-scale grids, which fails to exploit the arbitrary position and scale capabilities of modern optical hardware such as pan-tilt-zoom cameras and UAVs. This paper bridges the gap between software and hardware by enabling the agent to choose both where and how much to zoom at every exploration step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240543/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each exploration step the model receives a growing sequence of previously captured square glimpses (each at a fixed camera sensor resolution, e.g. 32×32 or 48×48 pixels), their normalized top-left coordinates, per-patch importance scores from attention rollout, and per-patch latent tokens from the ViT encoder. The RL agent outputs a continuous 3-tuple (x, y, z) ∈ [0,1]³ specifying the next glimpse's position and scale, while the task-specific head simultaneously produces a class label with probability, a full-resolution reconstruction, or a pixel-wise segmentation mask over the entire scene.

## Method

AdaGlimpse formulates AVE as a Markov Decision Process with a continuous action space and trains a Soft Actor-Critic agent whose actor predicts the next glimpse's (x, y, z) and whose critic estimates the state-action value. The backbone is a ViT-B encoder augmented with ElasticViT positional encoding so that patches sampled at arbitrary positions and scales receive correct spatial embeddings, followed by a task-specific head (linear layer for classification, MAE-style transformer decoder with mask tokens for all positions for reconstruction and segmentation). The RL state concatenates raw patches, their coordinates, attention-rollout importance values, and latent tokens, each processed by dedicated encoders before an attention-pooling layer feeds the actor and critic MLPs. Training alternates between optimizing the backbone (with task-specific loss on the final glimpse) and the RL agent (with reward defined as the reduction in task loss between successive steps), preceded by 600 epochs of pre-training on 196 uniformly random glimpses per image.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k classification, AdaGlimpse achieves 77.54% top-1 accuracy with 14 glimpses of 32×32 (28.57% of image pixels), surpassing the best baseline STAM (76.13%) at the same pixel budget. With early stopping at 85% confidence, it reaches 76.30% accuracy using only ~16.94% of pixels — over 40% fewer than STAM. For reconstruction on ImageNet-1k, AdaGlimpse achieves 14.5 RMSE with 12 glimpses (24.49% pixels), dramatically outperforming AME (30.3 RMSE at 25% pixels). On ADE20K segmentation, it matches AME's pixel accuracy (70.0% vs 70.3% PA) while requiring 35% fewer pixels (36.73% vs 56.25%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaGlimpse outperforms all 11 baselines across the three evaluated tasks. On ImageNet-1k classification, it beats STAM [35] (76.13%) by 1.41 points at the same pixel budget, and surpasses PatchDrop [46] (76.00%), GFNet [47] (75.93%), TNet [30] (74.62%), STN [37] (71.40%), Saccader [12] (70.31%), and DRAM [3] (67.50%) by even larger margins. On reconstruction, the improvement is most dramatic: AdaGlimpse achieves 14.5 RMSE on ImageNet-1k versus 30.3 for AME [32] at comparable pixel percentages, and 14.7 versus 23.4–26.2 for AME and SimGlim [21] at ~18.75% pixels. On ADE20K segmentation, AdaGlimpse matches AME [32] in pixel accuracy (70.0% vs 70.3% PA) and slightly exceeds it in mPA (32.8% vs 32.2%) and IoU (25.7% vs 24.4%), while using 35% fewer pixels; it substantially outperforms AttSeg [41] (47.9% PA) and GlAtEx [39] (52.4% PA). The key differentiator is the adaptive glimpse regime: at equal pixel budgets, AdaGlimpse consistently wins, and with early stopping it achieves near-parity accuracy at roughly 40–60% of the pixel cost of fixed-grid baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Active Visual Exploration (AVE) literature by replacing the fixed-grid, fixed-scale glimpse paradigm (used by AME, STAM, SimGlim, and others) with a continuous action space over both position and scale. It builds directly on Beyond Grids (ElasticViT) for the variable-scale positional encoding that makes arbitrary patch coordinates tractable for a ViT encoder, and on Soft Actor-Critic for the RL policy that selects glimpses. The MAE-style decoder is adapted to predict the full image rather than only masked regions, a necessary modification given that variable-scale sampling leaves irregular, non-grid-shaped gaps. Conceptually, the paper bridges a gap between what AVE software assumes (rigid grids) and what real hardware (pan-tilt-zoom cameras, UAVs) can actually provide (arbitrary zoom and position).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: embodied agents with optical zoom or altitude control have been underutilized by AVE software that forces them into fixed-grid sampling. The 40% pixel reduction for classification (via early stopping) and the large reconstruction margin (14.5 vs. 30.3 RMSE at comparable pixel budgets) suggest that matching the software to the hardware's true degrees of freedom yields substantial efficiency gains. Key limitations remain: the ViT backbone incurs quadratic cost in the number of patches, the evaluation is restricted to static scenes (no dynamic environments), and the reconstruction comparison uses different image resolutions than most baselines, making the gap partly attributable to resolution rather than purely to the adaptive regime. Open questions include whether state-space models (e.g., Mamba) can replace the ViT to scale to longer exploration sequences, and whether the approach generalizes to truly dynamic, non-stationary scenes.
