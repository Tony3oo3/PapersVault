## Problem

Current vision-language models possess only generic knowledge and cannot recognize or reason about user-specific concepts such as a person's own dog, friend, or a particular object. Personalizing VLMs is further complicated by the fact that pretrained vision encoder features lack the expressiveness to distinguish semantically similar objects, and full fine-tuning is computationally expensive and prone to catastrophic forgetting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240328/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes only 3–5 images of a target concept (object or person) each paired with a caption containing a concept identifier (an uncommon word for objects, a short name for individuals, following DreamBooth convention). At inference, a single new RGB image and a language instruction (a captioning prompt or an open-ended VQA question) are provided, and the model produces a personalized caption or answer that naturally weaves the concept identifier into a contextually accurate description of the scene. The approach is applied to both BLIP-2 and LLaVA without modifying their original weights.

## Method

MyVLM introduces a two-stage plug-in: (1) external concept heads (a linear classifier over CLIP embeddings for objects, a pretrained face-recognition network for people) detect whether a target concept is present in the image, and (2) a single learned concept embedding vector, appended to the frozen vision encoder's output, guides the language model to incorporate the concept identifier into its response. The embedding is optimized via cross-entropy loss on the small training set, with two key regularizations: norm-matching of the embedding's key/value projections to the average norm of image features, and an L2 penalty on the attention mass assigned to the concept token by all Q-Former query tokens, preventing it from dominating and degrading image alignment. For VQA, the optimization randomly samples from 10 question–answer pairs per step to encourage generalization to unseen questions. The entire VLM (vision encoder, Q-Former/linear projection, and LLM) remains frozen; only the concept heads and the single embedding vector are trained.

## Results

On the MyVLM Personalization Dataset, MyVLM achieves 87.11% recall (concept identifier present in generated caption) on BLIP-2 and 95.97% on LLaVA, averaged over all 45 concepts and 5 validation sets. For user-specific objects, MyVLM reaches 95.10% recall on BLIP-2 and 94.76% on LLaVA, compared to only 29.30% and 25.86% for the Simple Replace baseline. Even with a single training image, MyVLM still outperforms all baselines by significant margins (75.42% recall on BLIP-2, 88.93% on LLaVA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MyVLM substantially outperforms both handcrafted baselines across all settings. Against Simple Replace (keyword substitution), MyVLM gains +57.8% recall on objects (BLIP-2) and +69.2% on objects (LLaVA), where the baseline struggles because LLaVA generates abstract captions that rarely contain the target keyword. Against the LLM-Guided baseline (which prompts a language model to insert the concept identifier), MyVLM still leads by 44% on BLIP-2 objects and 30% on LLaVA objects, demonstrating that learned concept embeddings generalize better than prompt-based insertion. In qualitative comparison with GPT-4V, MyVLM correctly distinguishes hard negative examples (e.g., a different cup with a similar textual description) that GPT-4V misidentifies as the target concept, suggesting GPT-4V relies heavily on textual similarity rather than visual identity. The only case where a baseline is competitive is People on BLIP-2, where Simple Replace reaches 84.33% vs. MyVLM's 79.76%, likely because BLIP-2's simpler captions more naturally include gender keywords.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions VLM personalization as distinct from both model editing (which performs precise factual corrections without requiring generalization to new visual inputs) and prior personalized captioning work (which adapts writing style rather than teaching the model to recognize specific user concepts). It builds directly on BLIP-2's Q-Former cross-attention architecture and LLaVA's linear projection to inject a learned concept embedding into the VLM's intermediate feature space, while relying on external concept heads (CLIP classifiers, face recognition networks) for recognition rather than fine-tuning the frozen vision encoder. The approach draws conceptual inspiration from text-to-image personalization (DreamBooth, textual inversion) but repurposes the embedding-optimization paradigm for understanding rather than generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of teaching VLMs to recognize and reason about user-specific concepts, introducing both a method and a benchmark dataset for a previously undefined task. The key open questions include scaling to many concurrent concepts, handling temporal changes (e.g., a person aging or changing appearance), and whether the approach extends to more complex multi-concept reasoning or long-form dialogue. A notable limitation is that the method requires a separate concept head per user concept, and the evaluation is confined to two VLM architectures and a relatively small dataset of 45 concepts, leaving unclear how the technique behaves at scale or with more diverse VLM backbones.
