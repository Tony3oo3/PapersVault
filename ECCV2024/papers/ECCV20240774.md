## Problem

Reconstructing hand-held objects from a single RGB image is highly ill-posed due to hand and self-occlusion, yet existing methods rely on deterministic SDF/DDF representations that produce over-smoothed or fragmented results and cannot model the inherent uncertainty. Naive point-cloud diffusion models applied to this task suffer from uncontrolled centroid drift during denoising and lack explicit modeling of hand-object interaction, leading to physically implausible reconstructions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and additionally consumes off-the-shelf MANO hand pose parameters (45 DoF) and 6 DoF camera view as auxiliary conditioning signals, requiring no object templates, category labels, or depth. The output is a 3D point cloud of the hand-held object expressed in the hand wrist coordinate system, with the object centroid explicitly fixed at a predicted location to decouple shape from position.

## Method

D-SCo formulates reconstruction as conditional point-cloud denoising diffusion with two key architectural innovations. First, a hand-constrained centroid-fixing scheme uses a small PointNet+ResNet-18 network to predict the object centroid from hand vertices and image features, then re-centers the point cloud at every forward and reverse diffusion step to lock the centroid, reducing the problem to pure shape denoising. Second, a dual-stream denoiser processes semantic and geometric hand-object priors in parallel branches: the semantic stream uses a unified hand-object embedding that projects both object and hand mesh vertices onto the image for pixel-aligned feature extraction (with one-hot object/hand labels), while the geometric stream encodes hand articulation by transforming the partially denoised point cloud into each of 15 joint frames via forward kinematics. The two branch outputs are concatenated and passed through stacked MLPs to predict the noise, and training combines a standard MSE denoising loss with a 2D projective mask loss for additional shape regularization.

## Results

On the synthetic ObMan dataset, D-SCo achieves F-5=0.61, F-10=0.81, and CD=0.11 mm, surpassing the best prior method DDF-HO (F-5=0.55, F-10=0.67, CD=0.14) by 10.9% and 20.9% relative improvement in F-5 and F-10, respectively. On real-world datasets with finetuning, D-SCo reaches F-5=0.41 / F-10=0.63 / CD=0.34 on HO3D and F-5=0.30 / F-10=0.47 / CD=1.35 on MOW, and F-5=0.63 / F-10=0.82 / CD=0.13 on DexYCB. Even in zero-shot transfer (no finetuning), D-SCo outperforms all baselines on both HO3D and MOW, demonstrating strong synthetic-to-real generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D-SCo outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and metric. On ObMan, the closest competitor DDF-HO [80] achieves F-5=0.55 and F-10=0.67, while D-SCo reaches 0.61 and 0.81, with a 21.4% lower Chamfer Distance (0.11 vs. 0.14 mm). On HO3D (finetuned), D-SCo (F-5=0.41, F-10=0.63, CD=0.34) substantially exceeds DDF-HO (F-5=0.27, F-10=0.40, CD=0.86) and iHOI (F-5=0.28, F-10=0.50, CD=1.53). On MOW (finetuned), D-SCo (F-5=0.30, F-10=0.47, CD=1.35) leads DDF-HO (F-5=0.17, F-10=0.24, CD=1.59) by a wide margin. On DexYCB, D-SCo (F-5=0.63, F-10=0.82, CD=0.13) surpasses the best prior gSDF (F-5=0.44, F-10=0.71, CD=0.34) by 43% in F-5 and 62% in CD reduction. In zero-shot transfer without finetuning, D-SCo still beats DDF-HO on HO3D (F-5: 0.27 vs. 0.24) and MOW (F-5: 0.24 vs. 0.14), confirming robust synthetic-to-real generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the point cloud denoising diffusion framework established by PVD and PC² to the specific and more challenging setting of hand-held object reconstruction, where hand occlusion and self-occlusion create severe ambiguity. It departs from the dominant deterministic SDF/DDF paradigm (iHOI, gSDF, DDF-HO) by adopting a probabilistic generative formulation, and introduces two architectural departures: a hand-constrained centroid-fixing scheme that decouples position from shape learning, and a dual-stream denoiser that processes semantic (unified hand-object embedding) and geometric (hand articulation) priors in separate branches before aggregation. The hand pose estimation component relies on off-the-shelf MANO-based methods, positioning the contribution squarely in the object shape inference stage rather than the full hand-object pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first diffusion-based approach for hand-held object reconstruction, D-SCo demonstrates that probabilistic modeling meaningfully improves handling of the inherent ill-posedness of single-view reconstruction, particularly under heavy occlusion where deterministic methods degrade sharply. The strong zero-shot synthetic-to-real transfer (71–89% F-score gains on MOW) suggests the learned shape prior generalizes well, which is practically valuable for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object or two-hand scenes, whether the per-step re-centering overhead limits real-time applicability, and whether the reliance on accurate hand pose estimation as a hard input becomes a bottleneck in the wild where hand tracking is itself error-prone.
