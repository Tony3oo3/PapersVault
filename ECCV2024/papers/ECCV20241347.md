## Problem

Existing sparsification-based pruning methods rely on a 'suppressed sparsification paradigm' that drives dropped weights toward zero via penalty terms, causing expressivity damage to the unpruned network before pruning and limiting the performance upper bound of the final compact model. This paper asks whether an alternative 'enhanced sparsification' paradigm—enhancing kept weights while preserving dropped ones—can achieve lossless structured pruning without pre-pruning capacity loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241347/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a dense network (ResNet-50, MobileNetV3, WRN28-10, ViT, or Swin Transformer) with randomly initialized parameters and a target FLOPs ratio (e.g., 15%, 35%, 55%) as input, and produces a structured pruned network that can be directly deployed without fine-tuning. Unlike sparsity-ratio-based methods, the FLOPs-aware constraint ensures accurate practical speedup, and the structured mask operates at the filter/channel level across both depth and width dimensions. The pruned network retains standard classification or detection/segmentation output heads.

## Method

STP (Stimulative Training guided Pruning) exploits the relative sparsity effect observed in stimulative training (ST), where self-distillation from the main network to weight-sharing subnets naturally enhances kept weights while leaving dropped weights at baseline magnitude, avoiding the capacity damage of penalty-based suppression. Three key components complete the framework: (1) a multi-dimension FLOPs-aware sampling space that jointly varies depth (layer count) and width (output channels) to explore fine-grained architecture trade-offs; (2) a KD-guided architecture exploration strategy that maintains a candidate pool of subnet architectures, scores them via exponential moving average of the normalized KL distillation loss, and progressively removes the worst-scoring architectures every k iterations until one optimal mask remains; (3) a subnet mutating expansion that generates larger support subnets by uniformly sampling sparsity ratios above the target subnet's per-layer ratios, providing additional capacity support during training to mitigate performance degradation from focusing on tiny subnets. The entire process is one-stage: the main network is trained from scratch with cross-entropy loss plus two KD losses (target subnet and mutating support subnet), and the final pruned network is extracted directly from the converged main network.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, STP achieves 72.43% Top-1 accuracy at 15.26% FLOPs (95.11% retention of the 76.15% dense accuracy) and 77.48% at 75% FLOPs, exceeding the unpruned network. On CIFAR-100, STP-pruned ResNet-50 reaches 79.64% Top-1 at 15% FLOPs, surpassing the dense baseline (78.14%) without fine-tuning. On Tiny ImageNet, the pruned ResNet-50 achieves 65.93% at 15% FLOPs, again exceeding the dense model (64.28%). For downstream COCO detection/segmentation, STP at 55% FLOPs improves Det mAP@0.75 by +0.3% and Seg mAP@0.75 by +0.9% over the unpruned baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STP consistently outperforms all 16 baselines across every FLOPs budget tested. At the most aggressive 15% FLOPs on ImageNet, STP (72.43%) beats OTOv2 (70.10%) by 2.33% and Refill (66.83%) by 5.60%. On CIFAR-100 at 15% FLOPs, STP (79.64%) surpasses RST-S (75.02%) by 4.62%, OTOv2 (77.04%) by 2.60%, and Group-SL (49.04%) by 30.60%, with Group-SL collapsing severely under aggressive pruning. At moderate FLOPs (~35%), STP (75.68%) leads GReg-1 (74.85%), GReg-2 (74.93%), and CAIE (72.39%) by 0.75–3.29%. At higher FLOPs (~55–75%), STP still edges out CHIP (76.30% at 55.2% FLOPs vs. STP's 77.13% at 53.49% FLOPs) and SSS (75.44% at 84.94% FLOPs vs. STP's 77.48% at 75% FLOPs), achieving both higher accuracy and lower FLOPs simultaneously. On Transformers (ViT, Swin), STP also dominates RST-S, with near-lossless compression at 55% FLOPs (only 0.25% and 0.02% drop for ViT and Swin respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'suppressed sparsification' paradigm (penalty-based methods like RST, GReg, AFP) that drives pruned weights toward zero, arguing this causes capacity damage before pruning. Instead, it builds on Stimulative Training (ST) [62, 60], repurposing its self-distillation mechanism as an 'enhanced sparsification' tool that boosts kept weights while preserving dropped weights' magnitudes. The method extends ST's depth-only subnet sampling to a multi-dimension (depth + width) FLOPs-aware space and adds KD-guided architecture exploration and subnet mutating expansion to form a complete one-stage structured pruning framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual reframing—enhancing kept parameters rather than suppressing dropped ones—challenges a widely adopted assumption in sparsification-based pruning and offers a cleaner mechanism for lossless compression, particularly under aggressive budgets where penalty-based methods degrade sharply. The one-stage design (no post-pruning fine-tuning) and consistent gains across CNNs and Transformers make it practically attractive for edge deployment. Open questions include whether the heuristic KD-loss-guided exploration can be made more principled or scalable to very large architecture spaces, and whether the enhanced sparsification paradigm transfers to other compression axes like quantization or mixed-precision.
