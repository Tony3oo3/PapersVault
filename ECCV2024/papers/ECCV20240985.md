## Problem

Sweep surfaces are highly expressive and compact for representing curvy, tubular geometry, but their construction relies on non-differentiable optimization or numerical continuation, preventing integration into deep learning pipelines. Existing shape abstraction methods either use simple parametric primitives (cuboids, superquadrics) that lack expressiveness for curved objects, or neural primitives that sacrifice post-creation editability. This paper bridges the gap by making sweep surfaces learnable end-to-end without supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240985/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a voxelized 3D shape (or alternatively a point cloud via a DGCNN encoder) along with its extracted medial axis as a skeletal prior, and outputs a set of sweep surface primitives each described by as few as 14 floats (B-spline control points, superellipse parameters a/b/d, and polynomial scaling coefficients). At inference, these compact parameters are fed directly to a non-differentiable off-the-shelf sweeper to produce explicit meshes, yielding a full shape description in only tens of floats total.

## Method

SweepNet parameterizes each sweep surface with a superellipse profile (3 parameters), a third-order B-spline axis (default 3 control points), and a low-degree polynomial scaling function, with parallel transport frames resolving profile orientation ambiguity. A differentiable neural sweeper, built on POCO, predicts the implicit occupancy field from sparsely sampled point clouds of each sweep surface, replacing the non-differentiable traditional sweeping pipeline. The overall architecture is a 3D CNN encoder followed by an MLP that feeds a dual head: one predicting sweep surface parameters (guided by the medial axis) and another selecting a parsimonious subset. Training is fully unsupervised and two-phase (neural sweeper first, then SweepNet), optimizing a combined loss of Boltzmann-weighted reconstruction, overlap penalty, sublinear parsimony, and chamfer-based axis alignment to the medial axis.

## Results

On the GC-Object dataset, SweepNet achieves the best performance across all three metrics: IoU of 0.608, Chamfer Distance of 0.0168, and F1 of 0.984, outperforming all five baselines. On the more challenging Quadrupeds dataset, SweepNet (voxel input) leads in CD (0.0176) and F1 (0.967) but trails SQ slightly in IoU (0.482 vs. 0.512); the point-cloud variant (Ours^pcd) recovers the lead on all three metrics with IoU 0.562, CD 0.0168, and F1 0.989. Notably, SweepNet requires only 2,000 training iterations per shape with no pre-training, compared to 40,000–60,000 iterations for CSG-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SweepNet outperforms all five baselines on the GC-Object dataset across IoU, CD, and F1. Against the closest competitor SQ [39] (superquadrics), SweepNet gains +0.011 IoU, −0.0014 CD, and +0.007 F1 on GC-Object. On the harder Quadrupeds dataset, SweepNet (voxel) still wins on CD (0.0176 vs. 0.0179) and F1 (0.967 vs. 0.964) but concedes IoU to SQ (0.482 vs. 0.512), a gap the point-cloud variant closes (0.562). The sketch-and-extrude baselines SECAD-Net [29] and ExtrudeNet [45] trail substantially in IoU (0.584 and 0.420 on GC-Object; 0.270 and 0.197 on Quadrupeds), reflecting their difficulty with curvy geometry. UCSG [24] and Cuboid [73] are competitive on F1 but lag in IoU and CD, confirming that sweep surfaces offer a better compactness–expressiveness trade-off for objects with tubular or curvy features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions sweep surfaces as a new primitive type within the well-established shape abstraction literature, which has previously relied on cuboids, superquadrics, CSG trees, and sketch-and-extrude operations. The key methodological departure is the introduction of a differentiable 'neural sweeper' (built on POCO) to approximate the implicit field of swept volumes, circumventing the non-differentiability of traditional swept-volume construction methods like spacetime numerical continuation [50]. The compact parameterization—superellipse profiles, B-spline axes, and polynomial scaling functions—draws on classical CAD concepts but is specifically designed for end-to-end learning, with the medial axis [58] providing a skeletal prior to resolve the inherent ambiguity in profile-axis decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sweep surfaces are ubiquitous in human-made objects yet have been absent from learning-based shape abstraction, and this work demonstrates that a 14-float parameterization can match or exceed more complex representations (CSG, sketch-and-extrude) on curvy geometry while enabling intuitive post-hoc editing. The neural sweeper concept is potentially generalizable to other complex primitives lacking closed-form implicit functions, opening a path for incorporating diverse geometric constructs into differentiable pipelines. However, the method is limited to per-shape fitting (risking local optima), struggles with high-porosity or very thin objects, and the quantitative gains over the strongest baseline (superquadrics) are modest, particularly on the quadrupeds dataset where IoU actually trails SQ.
