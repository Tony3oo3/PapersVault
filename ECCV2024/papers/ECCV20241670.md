## Problem

Existing reward fine-tuning methods for diffusion models are constrained to small prompt sets (≤398 prompts) within narrow domains and are limited to differentiable reward functions, making them impractical for web-scale, multi-objective alignment. This paper addresses the lack of a scalable RL framework that can fine-tune diffusion models across millions of prompts with arbitrary combinations of reward functions, including non-differentiable and distribution-level objectives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241670/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes free-form text prompts as input and produces 512×512 RGB images via a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion v2 model. Training consumes millions of prompts drawn from multiple sources (1.5M real-user prompts from DiffusionDB, 240M Pinterest captions for fairness, 1M+ compositional prompts), and the reward signal is computed on the final generated image (or on the empirical distribution of a minibatch for diversity rewards). The output is a single fine-tuned diffusion model that simultaneously optimizes human preference, skintone diversity, and object composition.

## Method

The paper formulates the iterative denoising process of a diffusion model as a multi-step MDP and applies a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective (REINFORCE with importance sampling) to optimize arbitrary reward functions. Two key design choices enable scalability: per-batch reward normalization (replacing DDPO's per-prompt running statistics, which required storing history for every prompt) and the inclusion of the original DDPM denoising loss as a regularizer to prevent reward hacking. The framework supports distribution-based rewards—specifically statistical parity computed over minibatch outputs—to optimize diversity and fairness at the population level rather than per-image. Multi-task joint training samples prompts from all reward tasks in each iteration and applies sequential gradient steps, allowing a single model to improve on human preference, skintone diversity, and compositional fidelity simultaneously while mitigating the alignment tax observed when optimizing individual objectives in isolation.

## Results

On the human preference task, the proposed method achieves an ImageReward score of 0.845 and Aesthetic score of 5.918 on DiffusionDB, and 0.731 / 5.477 on PartiPrompts, attaining the highest Aesthetic score and human preference rate (80.3% win rate over base SDv2) across both test sets. For fairness, the method reduces Statistical Parity from 0.556 (SDv2) to 0.453 on the Occupation test set and from 0.576 to 0.498 on HRS-Bench. For compositionality, the method raises the object detection score from 0.102 (SDv2) to 0.231 on unseen objects and from 0.094 to 0.221 on seen objects. Training converges in ~1,000 steps (12.1h on 128 A100s), faster than DRaFT (~4,000 steps, 8.4h) and Reward-weighted (33.8h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning three methodological families: gradient backpropagation (ReFL, DRaFT), dataset augmentation (RAFT, Reward-weighted), and RL (DDPO). On the primary human-preference task, the proposed method achieves the highest Aesthetic score on both DiffusionDB (5.918 vs. 5.845 for ReFL) and PartiPrompts (5.477 vs. 5.413 for RAFT), and the highest human preference rate (80.3% win rate). ReFL attains the highest ImageReward score (1.290 on DiffusionDB) but the authors attribute this to reward hacking, producing over-detailed images with high-frequency noise; the proposed method's lower IR score (0.845) is considered more robust. RAFT diverges during training and underperforms all other methods. DRaFT and DDPO both trail the proposed method on both IR and Aesthetic scores. On fairness (Statistical Parity), the proposed method outperforms RAFT (0.453 vs. 0.464) and Reward-weighted (0.453 vs. 0.562) on the Occupation set, and outperforms both on HRS-Bench (0.498 vs. 0.527). On compositionality, the proposed method nearly doubles the object detection score of SDv2 (0.231 vs. 0.102 on unseen objects), substantially outperforming Reward-weighted (0.136) and RAFT (0.094).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDPO's formulation of diffusion model fine-tuning as a multi-step MDP with PPO-style policy gradients, but addresses its critical scalability limitation (per-prompt reward normalization restricted to ~398 prompts) by switching to per-batch normalization, enabling training on millions of prompts. It also departs from the gradient-backpropagation reward optimization line (ReFL, DRaFT) by using policy gradients, which the authors argue are more robust to reward hacking. The multi-objective joint training framework and the introduction of distribution-level reward functions (statistical parity computed over minibatches) represent a conceptual extension beyond prior work that treated each reward as an independent, per-image signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that RL-based fine-tuning scales to web-scale prompt sets (1.5M prompts) and converges in ~1,000 steps challenges the perception that RL is impractically slow for diffusion models, and the multi-objective setup showing >80% of individual fine-tuning performance across all three metrics simultaneously offers a practical recipe for holistic model improvement without the alignment tax. Limitations include evaluation only on SDv2 at 512×512, reliance on specific reward models (ImageReward, UniDet, a 4-class skintone classifier), and the joint model still trailing individual fine-tuning on each metric. Open questions include whether distribution-based rewards generalize beyond demographic parity, how the approach scales to larger models (SDXL, video diffusion), and whether more complex compositional or causal relationships can be captured with the current reward design.
