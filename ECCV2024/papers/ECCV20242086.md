## Problem

State-of-the-art video foundation models (e.g., VideoLLaMA, ViCLIP) fail at long-term temporal reasoning for scene retrieval, particularly when key-frames are rare or queries involve complex multi-event sequences. The root cause is that these models aggregate per-frame semantics into a single latent vector, making precise localization over long videos intractable. No existing method provides formal, interpretable guarantees for identifying temporally extended scenes from natural-language or formal specifications.

## Input / Output

The system consumes individual RGB video frames (not video clips) along with a temporal logic specification expressed in PCTL/LTL syntax (e.g., (A ∧ B) U C), which is derived by decomposing a natural-language query into atomic propositions. Output is a set of frame-index sequences constituting the identified scene, accompanied by a probabilistic confidence score obtained from model checking. Because perception operates per-frame, the method scales to arbitrarily long videos without the context-length constraints that limit video-language models.

## Method

NSVS-TL decouples neural perception from symbolic temporal reasoning in a four-stage pipeline. First, a pluggable perception model (YOLOv8, CLIP, Masked R-CNN, or Grounding DINO) detects atomic propositions per frame, with raw confidences calibrated via a generalized logistic mapping and empirically chosen true/false-positive thresholds. Second, each frame undergoes dual validation: a detection check (all required propositions present) and a symbolic check that evaluates first-order logic operators (∧, ∨, ¬) and temporal relevance of each proposition within the TL formula. Third, validated frames are encoded into a probabilistic automaton with 2^|P| states per frame, where transition probabilities are products of calibrated detection confidences, constructed dynamically rather than over the full video. Fourth, the probabilistic model checker STORM verifies whether the automaton satisfies the PCTL specification; upon satisfaction, the corresponding frames are emitted as a scene and the automaton resets. This neuro-symbolic decomposition is the key architectural insight: state machines and temporal logic inherently capture memory, avoiding the information loss of latent-vector aggregation.

## Results

NSVS-TL with Masked R-CNN as the perception backbone achieves the highest F1 scores across all TLV datasets, reaching 0.83 on Waymo (A∪B) and 0.80 on Waymo ((A∧B)∪C), and 0.84 on COCO-TLV (□A). The TL-based reasoning component improves F1 by 9–15% over GPT-4-based LLM reasoning on complex multi-event specifications (A∪B and (A∧B)∪C). On long videos (up to 2400 s / 40 min), NSVS-TL maintains consistent F1 while GPT-4 suffers a sharp performance decline beyond 1000 s and GPT-3.5 degrades within 500 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against video-language foundation models (VideoLLaMA, ViCLIP), NSVS-TL demonstrates consistently higher event-identification accuracy as video length and query complexity increase, whereas these models' accuracy drops significantly (Fig. 2). Against GPT-4 used as an LLM reasoner over per-frame annotations, NSVS-TL's TL-based reasoning yields a 9–15% F1 improvement on complex multi-event specifications (A∪B and (A∧B)∪C) across Waymo and NuScenes. On long-video stress tests, GPT-4's F1 degrades sharply for clips exceeding 1000 s, and GPT-3.5 / GPT-3.5 Turbo Instruct degrade within 500 s, while NSVS-TL sustains stable F1 through 2400 s (40 min) videos. For single-event queries (◇A, □A), the gap between NSVS-TL and LLM baselines narrows, as these do not require complex temporal reasoning. Overall, NSVS-TL wins on all multi-event and long-horizon comparisons, with the largest margins on the (A∧B)∪C specification over the longest videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by decoupling per-frame neural perception from temporal reasoning, repurposing formal verification techniques (probabilistic automata, PCTL model checking via STORM) from systems engineering into the video understanding domain. It departs from the dominant paradigm of video-language models (VideoLLaMA, ViCLIP) that aggregate temporal information into latent vectors, and from prior symbolic video understanding work that relies on graph structures or latent-space representations lacking interpretability. The closest prior work is Yang et al. [35] on specification-driven video search with formal verification, but NSVS-TL extends this with dynamic probabilistic automaton construction and per-frame confidence calibration, enabling scene identification of arbitrary temporal length rather than fixed-length event detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in long-video search where SOTA foundation models degrade sharply, and provides formal confidence guarantees that purely neural approaches cannot offer—valuable for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving verification. The introduced TLV datasets (synthetic and AV-annotated) fill a benchmarking void for temporally extended video queries. However, the per-frame perception limitation means multi-frame semantic events (e.g., 'man falling from a horse') remain out of scope, and the method's reliance on accurate object detection confidence scores makes it sensitive to perception model quality. Open questions include extending to video-level temporal semantics, handling more complex PCTL specifications, and scaling to truly long videos beyond the 40-minute tested range.
