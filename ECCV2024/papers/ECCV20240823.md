## Problem

Transcription-only supervised text spotting requires locating text in scene images using only transcriptions without boundary annotations, but existing methods like NPTS suffer from arduous optimization convergence due to undefined ordering among text instances, while DETR-based approaches like TOSS remain limited by the absence of positional supervision. The core unresolved challenge is how to reliably detect where each transcription appears in an image when no location ground truth is available.

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene text image and its associated text transcriptions (no bounding boxes or polygons) as input and produces transcriptions paired with single anchor points indicating text locations. Because the output is a point rather than a full boundary, evaluation uses single-point matching and edit-distance metrics rather than the standard IoU-based bounding box protocol. The system requires high-resolution input images to accurately detect small text, as the character-wise appearance matching depends on sufficient pixel detail.

## Method

WeCromCL decomposes transcription-only text spotting into two stages: (1) detecting an anchor point per transcription via weakly supervised atomistic cross-modality contrastive learning, and (2) training a single-point text spotter (SRSTS-A or SPTS) using the predicted anchor points as pseudo location labels. The key architectural distinction is 'atomistic' contrastive learning: instead of modeling holistic image-text semantic correlation as in CLIP or oCLIP, WeCromCL learns character-wise appearance consistency between a transcription and its correlated image region. The image encoder (BiFPN + deformable transformer) produces pixel-level features, while a character-wise text encoder (per-character embeddings + positional embeddings + transformer encoder) produces a sequence-aware text embedding. A soft activation map is computed via cross-modality cross-attention (text embedding as query, image features as keys/values), and its peak identifies the anchor point; the activation map also aggregates correlated visual features for the contrastive loss. A negative-sample mining scheme augments unpaired transcriptions during image-to-text contrastive construction to introduce harder negatives, and the overall loss combines text-to-image and image-to-text contrastive objectives.

## Results

On the four benchmarks, WeCromCL+SRSTS-A achieves F-measure (single-point) of 83.7 on ICDAR 2013 (Generic), 68.8 on ICDAR 2015 (Generic), 81.4 on Total-Text (Full), and 75.7 on CTW1500 (Full). Under the edit-distance metric, the corresponding values are 84.6, 66.2, 79.1, and 79.9. The method surpasses the prior transcription-only baselines NPTS and TOSS by 9.2% and 16.4% respectively on ICDAR 2015 (Generic lexicon), and outperforms TOSS by 5% on Total-Text (None) and 10.4% on CTW1500 (Full). Despite using only pseudo location labels from WeCromCL, the system approaches the performance of the semi-supervised TTSweak, which additionally leverages fully-annotated synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two prior transcription-only methods, WeCromCL+SRSTS-A dominates: it beats TOSS by 16.4 points on ICDAR 2015 (Generic, single-point: 68.8 vs 52.4), by 5 points on Total-Text (None: 70.1 vs 65.1), and by 10.4 points on CTW1500 (Full: 75.7 vs 65.3), though it trails TOSS by 3 points on CTW1500 (None: 51.2 vs 54.2). Against NPTS (edit-distance), it gains 9.2 points on ICDAR 2015 (Generic: 66.2 vs 57.0), 6.5 points on Total-Text (None: 68.1 vs 61.6), and 9.4 points on CTW1500 (Full: 79.9 vs 70.5). Compared to the semi-supervised TTSweak (which uses fully-annotated synthetic data), WeCromCL+SRSTS-A is slightly lower on ICDAR 2015 (Generic: 68.8 vs 70.1) and Total-Text (None: 70.1 vs 75.1; Full: 81.4 vs 83.5), yet it does so without any boundary annotations on any data. Against fully supervised state-of-the-art methods like ESTextSpotter, the gap is larger (e.g., 78.1 vs 68.8 on ICDAR 2015 Generic), which is expected given the absence of text boundary supervision. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the atomistic contrastive learning of WeCromCL substantially outperforms the holistic contrastive learning of oCLIP (e.g., 83.4 vs 41.7 F-measure on ICDAR 2015 test), validating the core design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeCromCL builds on the CLIP-style contrastive learning framework and the character-wise text encoding strategy from oCLIP, but departs fundamentally from the holistic image-text semantic matching paradigm by introducing 'atomistic' contrastive learning that models character-level appearance consistency between a transcription and its localized region in a scene image. For the downstream spotting stage, it adapts SPTS and SRSTS v2 to operate on single-point pseudo labels rather than full boundary annotations. The paper positions itself as the first to formally define weakly supervised atomistic cross-modality contrastive learning, distinguishing it from prior transcription-only methods (NPTS, TOSS) that either treat spotting as sequence prediction or rely on DETR-style queries without explicit location supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for text boundary annotations entirely, this two-stage approach (contrastive anchor detection followed by single-point spotting) offers a practical path to scaling text spotting to large unlabeled corpora where only transcriptions are available. The substantial performance gap over prior transcription-only methods (e.g., +16.4% over NPTS on ICDAR 2015 with generic lexicon) validates the decomposition strategy. However, the method requires high-resolution inputs to detect small texts, and the quality of downstream spotting is tightly coupled to the accuracy of the pseudo anchor points, leaving open whether the approach degrades gracefully in cluttered or heavily occluded scenes. The atomistic contrastive learning formulation could potentially transfer to other weakly supervised localization tasks beyond text.
