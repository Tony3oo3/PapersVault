## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting methods for urban scene rendering depend on SfM or LiDAR point initialization, which produces incomplete geometry in low-texture, sky, and distant regions, leading to blank or blurry renderings. Additionally, storing high-order spherical harmonics for view-dependent color in large-scale urban scenes requires gigabytes of disk space, and multi-camera systems with limited overlap make consistent rendering across viewpoints difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241590/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only multi-camera RGB images as input (Waymo: 3 cameras at 1920×1280; Argoverse: 7 cameras at 1920×1200), requiring no SfM, LiDAR, or depth supervision. It produces real-time novel view renderings at 71 FPS and a compact 3D Gaussian scene representation of 123 MB (compared to 557 MB for standard 3DGS), with every tenth frame held out for testing and the remainder used for training over 30K iterations.

## Method

HO-Gaussian jointly optimizes a grid-based NeRF volume and a 3D Gaussian splatting pipeline in an end-to-end hybrid scheme. A point densification mechanism queries the volume's predicted density along rays to inject new Gaussian positions into regions where SfM points are absent (sky, distant, low-texture areas), while Gaussian Positional Encoding compresses the unbounded scene into a finite sphere to manage memory. Gaussian Directional Encoding replaces spherical harmonics with a hash-grid + MLP network that predicts view-dependent color, reducing model size by ~78%. Neural Warping perturbs existing camera poses (±10° rotations) and renders warped images through the volume, supplying additional virtual viewpoints to the Gaussian pipeline for cross-camera consistency. Training minimizes a combined loss: L1 + SSIM on Gaussian-rendered images plus MSE on volume-rendered images, with clone/split densification and opacity-based pruning applied to the Gaussians.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset (8 sequences, 3 cameras), HO-Gaussian without any point initialization achieves 28.03 PSNR / 0.8364 SSIM / 0.3282 LPIPS, outperforming all SfM-free baselines (MERF: 26.28, NGP: 23.88, Block-NeRF: 23.60, 3DGS without SfM: 18.31). On Argoverse (4 sequences, 7 cameras), it reaches 30.98 PSNR / 0.9043 SSIM / 0.2287 LPIPS. The method also surpasses SfM/LiDAR-dependent methods: 3DGS+SfM (24.90/27.83 PSNR), S-NeRF+SfM (24.07/24.36), and EmerNeRF+LiDAR (28.62/30.14) on PSNR, while using only 123 MB of model storage versus 557 MB for 3DGS and 3762 MB for LocalRF, at 71 FPS real-time rendering speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HO-Gaussian (RGB only, no point initialization) decisively outperforms all SfM-free baselines: it beats MERF by +1.75 PSNR on Waymo and +3.46 on Argoverse, and surpasses 3DGS without SfM by a large margin (+9.72 PSNR on Waymo, +9.48 on Argoverse). Against SfM/LiDAR-dependent methods, HO-Gaussian outperforms 3DGS+SfM by +3.13 PSNR (Waymo) and +3.15 (Argoverse), and S-NeRF+SfM by +3.96 and +6.62 PSNR respectively. On Argoverse, HO-Gaussian also beats EmerNeRF+LiDAR by +0.84 PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.3210 to 0.2287 (−28.7%). The one notable exception is LocalRF (RGB+Depth), which achieves slightly higher PSNR on Argoverse (31.79 vs 30.98), but HO-Gaussian reduces LPIPS by 30.1% (0.2287 vs 0.2976) and uses 30× less storage (123 MB vs 3762 MB) while rendering at 71 FPS versus 0.11 FPS. In the ablation, adding point densification to the SfM+3DGS baseline improves PSNR from 27.83 to 30.58, and neural warping further lifts it to 30.98, confirming each component's contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

HO-Gaussian builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as its rendering backbone while incorporating a NeRF-style grid-based volume (hash grid + MLP) to generate new Gaussian positions, effectively bridging the implicit and explicit representation paradigms. It departs from 3DGS by eliminating the dependency on SfM or LiDAR point initialization—a critical practical barrier in urban scenes where SfM fails on low-texture and distant regions—and replaces high-order spherical harmonics with a learned view-dependent color network (Gaussian Directional Encoding). The Gaussian Positional Encoding draws on Mip-NeRF 360's domain compression, and the Neural Warping module extends the training signal with virtual viewpoints, a technique analogous to data augmentation but applied in 3D space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for SfM/LiDAR initialization, HO-Gaussian lowers the practical barrier to deploying 3DGS in large-scale urban and autonomous-driving simulation, where obtaining reliable point clouds is often infeasible. The 78% reduction in model size (557 MB → 123 MB) while matching or exceeding the quality of point-initialized baselines makes real-time multi-camera rendering more deployable. Open questions remain around dynamic scene handling, scalability to city-scale environments beyond the tested 12 sequences, and whether the hybrid volume-Gaussian optimization generalizes to other unbounded or sparse-geometry settings outside driving.
