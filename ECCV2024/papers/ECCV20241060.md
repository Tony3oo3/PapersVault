## Problem

SAM produces poor segmentation on domains sparsely represented or outside its training distribution (aerial, medical, X-ray, SAR, sonar images), and existing adaptation methods rely on large annotated target datasets that are costly to collect. A critical underexplored challenge is that SAM's image encoder (308.3 M parameters) is vastly larger than its mask decoder (4.1 M), so naively tuning both under few-shot supervision leads to tuning imbalance and suboptimal adaptation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241060/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAT-SAM takes an image of arbitrary size (H×W) together with a geometric prompt—bounding box, point, or coarse mask—and outputs a pixel-level segmentation mask up-sampled to the original resolution. During training, a mixture of prompt types is used, including degraded coarse masks with random Gaussian noise added to boundary regions, to improve robustness. The method is designed to adapt with as few as one target image (1-shot) while preserving SAM's zero-shot capability and geometric prompting flexibility at inference.

## Method

CAT-SAM freezes the entire pre-trained SAM and introduces a decoder-conditioned joint tuning strategy via a lightweight 'prompt bridge' that maps domain-specific features from the mask decoder back into the image encoder, mitigating the parameter imbalance between the two components. A learnable CAT-Token in the decoder generates dynamic weights for a three-layer MLP that produces the final mask, while the prompt bridge projects this token into each encoder transformer layer to condition the encoder tuning. Two variants are developed: CAT-SAM-T injects learnable prompt tokens into the encoder's input space (3.3 M trainable params, 1.1% of SAM), and CAT-SAM-A inserts lightweight adapter sub-networks with high-frequency image features (1.9 M trainable params, 0.6% of SAM). In both variants, the prompt bridge replaces (PB-T) or augments (PB-A) the encoder tuning signal with decoder-derived information, enabling synergistic adaptation from as few as one target sample.

## Results

Under the extremely challenging one-shot adaptation setting, CAT-SAM-A achieves an average IoU of 85.2 across WHU (88.2), Kvasir (85.4), and SBU-Shadow (81.9), while CAT-SAM-T reaches 82.7 average. This substantially outperforms the best competing tuning methods (LoRA at 79.3, AdaptFormer at 79.1) and the SAM-based adaptation method HQ-SAM (69.5), as well as the unadapted SAM baseline (61.6). With 16 shots, CAT-SAM-A matches or exceeds state-of-the-art fully supervised learning methods trained on full data (e.g., 90.3 IoU on WHU vs. 91.4 for CBR-Net; 93.6 mIoU on Kvasir vs. 90.5 for DUCK-Net). On non-RGB domains, one-shot CAT-SAM-T lifts JSRT mIoU from 78.5 to 93.0 and HRSID AP from 38.2 to 46.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT-SAM-A outperforms the strongest general tuning baselines (LoRA at 79.3 avg, AdaptFormer at 79.1) by 5.9 and 6.1 points respectively on the one-shot average, with the largest gap on Kvasir (+7.9 over LoRA) and SBU-Shadow (+7.5 over LoRA). Against SAM-specific adaptation methods, the margin is even wider: CAT-SAM-A exceeds HQ-SAM (69.5) by 15.7 points, and substantially surpasses MedSAM (37.9), SimAda (38.9), and Med-SA (28.4), which degrade severely under one-shot due to overfitting from their full-shot training designs. On the intricate structural segmentation benchmark (Table 6), both CAT-SAM variants beat HQ-SAM by ~2.5 mIoU (91.7/91.6 vs. 89.1) and the original SAM by ~12 mIoU. In the full-shot regime (Table 5), CAT-SAM-A with only 16 shots matches or exceeds dedicated FSL methods (e.g., 90.3 vs. 91.4 IoU on WHU; 93.6 vs. 90.5 mIoU on Kvasir; 3.80 vs. 3.15 BER on SBU-Shadow), demonstrating that the foundation model's transferred knowledge compensates for limited target data. CAT-SAM-A consistently edges out CAT-SAM-T by 1–3 points, attributed to its structural adapter modifications over input-space prompt tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT-SAM sits at the intersection of two research threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) for foundation models (prompt tuning, adapters, LoRA) and SAM-specific downstream adaptation (MedSAM, HQ-SAM, SimAda). Prior SAM adaptation work either fine-tunes only the lightweight mask decoder (HQ-SAM, MedSAM) or applies generic PEFT to the image encoder alone (VPT, AdaptFormer, LoRA), leaving the two components to be tuned independently. CAT-SAM departs from both by introducing a 'prompt bridge' that maps decoder features back into the encoder, formulating decoder-conditioned joint tuning that explicitly addresses the 75× parameter imbalance between SAM's ViT-L encoder (308M) and mask decoder (4M). The mask decoder tuning follows HQ-SAM's CAT-Token design, and the adapter variant borrows high-frequency visual prompting from Liu et al. (CVPR 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: with only one annotated target image, CAT-SAM approaches or matches the performance of fully supervised methods trained on entire datasets, dramatically reducing the annotation cost for deploying SAM in new domains (aerial, medical, SAR, sonar). The ablation revealing that independent encoder+decoder tuning is *worse* than decoder-only tuning validates the tuning-imbalance hypothesis and suggests that naive PEFT application to asymmetric architectures may be counterproductive. However, the much smaller gains on FLS (sonar, 11 classes) and the reliance on ground-truth box prompts during evaluation limit the generality claim. Open questions include whether the prompt bridge generalizes to other asymmetric foundation-model architectures and whether the approach extends to video or 3D segmentation.
