## Problem

Existing video QA models may exploit language biases or spurious visual-text correlations rather than genuinely grounding answers in relevant video content. The grounded VQA (GVQA) task demands models provide both an answer and explicit temporal evidence, but temporal annotations are unavailable during training, making it difficult to supervise the grounding component. Prior approaches either require costly pretraining on large-scale video grounding datasets (SeViLA) or lack directed supervision for accurate moment localization (NG+).

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (32 uniformly sampled frames), a text question, and a set of candidate answers as input, and produces an answer label along with a grounded temporal segment parameterized as a Gaussian N(μ, σ²) over the video timeline. At inference, the temporal segment is reported as a confidence interval (μ−γσ, μ+γσ)·d where d is the video duration. During training, an LLM-generated dual Q&A pair (reversing the causal/temporal relationship) is also consumed as an auxiliary input to drive the bi-directional reasoning pipeline.

## Method

TimeCraft constructs a bi-directional reasoning framework where two parallel paths process the original and its LLM-generated dual Q&A pair, each performing a 'ground-then-answer' pipeline: a learnable Gaussian predictor localizes key moments, and a Gaussian-conditioned attention mechanism refines the answer using only the highlighted temporal region. Cycle-consistency losses enforce agreement between corresponding intermediate outputs of the two paths—KL divergence aligns the grounded Gaussian moments, and L2 distance aligns reconstructed question embeddings with their ground-truth counterparts. An auxiliary dual-question reconstruction head forces the model to understand deep causal/temporal relationships beyond surface correlation. The total loss combines standard QA cross-entropy, reconstruction, and grounding terms, trained end-to-end; at inference, only a single forward pass is needed, preserving the backbone's efficiency.

## Results

On the NExT-GQA test set, TimeCraft with Temp[CLIP] backbone achieves 18.2% Acc@GQA, 65.6% Acc@QA, 28.1 mIoP, and 15.6 mIoU, while with FrozenBiLM it reaches 18.5% Acc@GQA and 74.7% Acc@QA. These represent new state-of-the-art results for grounded VQA, surpassing the previous best (NG+ FrozenBiLM: 17.5% Acc@GQA, 73.1% Acc@QA) by 1.0 and 1.6 points respectively. On Env-QA, TimeCraft achieves 49.02% overall accuracy, outperforming the prior best MIST (48.97%) with particularly strong gains on event (43.86 vs 42.54) and ordering (58.37 vs 56.83) question types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NExT-GQA, TimeCraft (Temp[CLIP]) outperforms NG+ (Temp[CLIP]) by +2.2 Acc@GQA and +3.5 mIoU, and outperforms SeViLA by +1.6 Acc@GQA, though SeViLA retains an edge on mIoP (29.5 vs 28.1) and mIoU (21.7 vs 15.6), likely due to its costly pretraining on TSG grounding data. Against the Post-hoc baselines (VGT, Temp[CLIP], FrozenBiLM), TimeCraft leads by 2.4–6.1 points in Acc@GQA and by 8.5–12.6 points in mIoU, confirming that explicit bi-directional supervision substantially improves temporal grounding over post-hoc attention. On Env-QA, TimeCraft (49.02%) marginally surpasses MIST (48.97%) overall but shows clearer advantages on temporally demanding categories: +1.32 on Event and +1.54 on Order, while trailing slightly on Attribute (43.74 vs 44.05). All other Env-QA baselines (ST-VQA, STAGE, AIO, Temp[ATP], TSEA) are outperformed by 2–7 points in overall accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the grounded VQA (GVQA) task introduced by NExT-GQA (Xiao et al., 2023), which requires models to answer questions while providing temporal evidence. It departs from SeViLA (Yu et al., 2024), which relied on BLIP-2 pseudo-labels and costly multi-stage pretraining on a separate video grounding dataset, by instead generating dual Q&A pairs via LLMs and enforcing cycle consistency between two reasoning paths. The learnable Gaussian grounding module draws from the weakly-supervised video grounding literature, while the cycle-consistency training objective repurposes a concept from image-to-image translation into the video QA setting. The dual Q&A generation via LLMs serves as both a data augmentation strategy and the structural backbone of the bi-directional supervision signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical reliability concern: whether VQA models genuinely ground their answers in video content or exploit language biases and spurious correlations. The bi-directional cycle-consistency framework offers a principled way to self-supervise temporal grounding without any temporal annotations, which is valuable given the cost of such labeling. However, the approach doubles training time, depends on LLM quality for dual Q&A generation, and the performance gains, while consistent, are moderate (e.g., 18.2 vs. 17.5 Acc@GQA over NG+ on FrozenBiLM). Open questions include whether the cycle-consistency principle generalizes to other weakly-supervised video tasks and whether the LLM-based dual generation can be replaced by learned transformations to reduce external dependencies.
