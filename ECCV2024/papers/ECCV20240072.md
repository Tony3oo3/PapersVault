## Problem

In real-world driving scenes, environmental diversity (lighting, road materials, unknown objects) distorts the manifolds of pixel embeddings in the embedding space, causing inlier pixels to deviate from their expected high-logit regions and outlier pixels to approach them. Existing Outlier Exposure-based anomaly segmentation methods ignore this distortion and directly use raw pixel embeddings for logit prediction, resulting in inaccurate anomaly scores with high false positive and false negative rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240072/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

RWPM operates as an inference-time post-processing module on pre-trained anomaly segmentation models (e.g., PEBAL, RbA, Mask2Anomaly, Balanced Energy). It consumes the pixel embedding map (d × H × W) produced by the model's encoder-decoder, partitions it into n×n sub-maps (n=2 for mask-based, n=4 for pixel-based backbones) to keep the affinity matrix tractable for large driving images (e.g., 512×1024), and outputs a refined embedding map that replaces the original before the classifier computes logits and the scoring function produces the final anomaly score map. No additional training or architectural modification is required.

## Method

RWPM constructs a locally constrained graph over pixel embeddings using cosine-similarity affinities normalized by a softmax with a small temperature (τ=0.01) to enforce neighborhood structure without expensive k-NN search. It then performs random walks with restart (m^(t+1) = αS·m^t + (1−α)·m^0, α=0.99) to diffuse and update each pixel's embedding as a weighted ensemble of manifold neighbors, making same-class embeddings form tighter clusters and improving logit-based anomaly scoring. To handle the prohibitive memory cost of the full HW×HW matrix (e.g., 524288² for a 512×1024 image), the Partial Random Walk strategy partitions the embedding map into n×n sub-maps, limits iterations to T=5–20 instead of using the closed-form inverse, and applies a boundary calibration to align anomaly score baselines across adjacent sub-maps. The entire procedure is training-free and plug-and-play, directly integrated into existing OE frameworks at inference time.

## Results

On the SMIYC online test set, RbA + RWPM achieves the best average AP of 86.00 and FPR95 of 5.46, outperforming the previous SOTA RbA (84.73 AP, 6.33 FPR95) and Mask2Anomaly (82.87 AP, 9.49 FPR95). On Road Anomaly, RbA + RWPM reaches 87.34 AP and 5.27 FPR95, surpassing RbA (85.42 AP, 6.92 FPR95) and all pixel-based methods. On the SMIYC Obstacle Track, RbA + RWPM achieves 93.30 AP and 0.28 FPR95, and on the Anomaly Track it reaches 92.00 AP and 10.15 FPR95, both leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RbA + RWPM outperforms the strongest prior mask-based method RbA [34] on every reported metric: +0.35 AP and −1.65 FPR95 on Fishyscapes, +1.92 AP and −1.65 FPR95 on Road Anomaly, +1.14 AP and −1.44 FPR95 on SMIYC Anomaly Track, and +1.45 AP and −0.18 FPR95 on SMIYC Obstacle Track. Against Mask2Anomaly [36], RbA + RWPM leads by +1.72 AP on Fishyscapes, +7.30 AP on Road Anomaly, and +3.38 AP on Anomaly Track, though Mask2Anomaly retains a marginal FPR95 edge on Obstacle Track (0.20 vs. 0.28). Among pixel-based methods, RbA + RWPM surpasses RPL-CoroCL [32] by +0.55 AP on Fishyscapes and +15.73 AP on Road Anomaly, and outperforms PEBAL [37] by +12.35 AP on Fishyscapes and +42.24 AP on Road Anomaly. Older baselines (Synboost [15], SML [26], Meta-OOD [7], DenseHybrid [18], GMMSeg-DL [28], M2F-EAM [19]) are all dominated by RbA + RWPM on both AP and FPR95 across all benchmarks where they report results. In component-level evaluation (Table 3), RbA + RWPM achieves 58.44 mean F1 on Anomaly Track and 69.85 on Obstacle Track, exceeding RbA (46.80 / 60.85) and Maskomaly [2] (50.00 / –) by substantial margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the random-walk-on-manifolds framework (Zhou et al. 2003; Iscen et al. 2018), previously used for retrieval ranking and label propagation, to instead diffuse and refine pixel embeddings at inference time. It sits squarely within the Outlier Exposure (OE) line of anomaly segmentation (PEBAL, RbA, Mask2Anomaly, Balanced Energy), which relies on inlier-class logits for anomaly scoring but ignores the distortion of pixel-embedding manifolds caused by real-world scene diversity. The key conceptual departure is treating the random walk as an embedding-level post-processing step rather than a state-propagation or ranking mechanism, and introducing the Partial Random Walk strategy to make it computationally feasible for large driving-scene images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RWPM is a training-free, architecture-agnostic plug-in that consistently improves multiple SOTA OE methods across pixel-based and mask-based backbones, making it immediately adoptable by practitioners without retraining pipelines. The Partial Random Walk strategy (embedding partitioning + limited iterations) keeps GPU memory and latency within practical bounds for real-time driving deployment. A stated limitation is that RWPM cannot compensate for fundamental inaccuracies in the underlying pre-trained model, and the paper acknowledges the need for improved training-time generalization to handle a broader range of unknown anomalies. Whether the manifold-refinement idea transfers to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth, normal estimation) under domain shift remains an open question.
