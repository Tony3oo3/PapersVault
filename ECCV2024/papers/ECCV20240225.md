## Problem

NeRF-based 3D generative models require ground-truth radiance fields as training supervision, but obtaining them is extremely challenging. Existing pipelines render dense multi-view images from meshes and fit NeRFs to those renderings, introducing artifacts from occlusions and under-fitting, while pixel-level rendering loss provides weak per-point supervision since each pixel constrains all sampled points along a ray. This paper asks whether radiance fields can be derived analytically from meshes to provide direct, per-point 3D supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a textured 3D mesh (geometry + texture map) plus an environment lighting setup as input and produces a complete radiance field specifying density (alpha) and view-dependent color at every sampled ray point. For conditional generation, sparse-view images (1–4 views at 128×128) guide the diffusion sampling, while unconditional generation starts from random Gaussian noise. The output radiance field supports both novel view synthesis at arbitrary camera poses and mesh extraction via isosurface extraction from the learned density.

## Method

Mesh2NeRF analytically constructs a ground-truth radiance field from a textured mesh by (1) modeling density as a binary occupancy function—alpha equals 1 within a fixed surface thickness h of the mesh and 0 elsewhere, approximating the limit of a Dirac delta via top-hat functions—and (2) assigning each ray point's color via a Phong BRDF evaluated at the first ray-mesh intersection, incorporating view direction, surface normal, and light direction. Ray-mesh intersections are computed efficiently using Embree's bounding volume hierarchy, with stratified sampling in both empty space and a narrow band around the surface. During NeRF training, the standard pixel-level rendering loss is replaced with per-point alpha and color L2 losses (plus an optional integral consistency term), giving each sampled point its own supervision signal. In the SSDNeRF diffusion framework, this Mesh2NeRF loss replaces the multi-view rendering loss during auto-decoder training, while the rendering loss is retained only during conditional sampling to guide generation toward observed views.

## Results

On the ABO dataset for single scene fitting, Mesh2NeRF NGP achieves 33.28 PSNR, 0.969 SSIM, and 0.018 LPIPS, outperforming Instant NGP (30.16 PSNR) by +3.12 dB and TensoRF (31.33 PSNR) by +1.95 dB. On Poly Haven, Mesh2NeRF NGP reaches 25.30 PSNR and 0.825 SSIM, again the best across all methods. For conditional NeRF generation on ShapeNet, the method improves over SSDNeRF by +0.69 PSNR on 1-view Cars and by up to +2.61 PSNR on 3-view Chairs (23.03 vs. 20.42), with consistent gains across all view counts and categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRF [38], Mesh2NeRF NGP wins decisively on both ABO (+8.19 PSNR) and Poly Haven (+4.06 PSNR), confirming that direct mesh supervision far exceeds pixel-level volume rendering loss. Against TensoRF [12], Mesh2NeRF NGP leads by +1.95 PSNR on ABO and +1.98 PSNR on Poly Haven, while Mesh2NeRF TensoRF also surpasses the original TensoRF on SSIM and LPIPS. Against Instant NGP [40], the headline gain is +3.12 PSNR on ABO and +0.91 PSNR on Poly Haven, with the largest LPIPS improvement (−0.021 on ABO, −0.056 on Poly Haven). Against SSDNeRF [15] in conditional generation, Mesh2NeRF consistently outperforms: +0.69 PSNR on 1-view Cars, +2.57 PSNR on 2-view Chairs, and +2.61 PSNR on 3-view Chairs, with the gap widening as shape diversity increases (Chairs > Cars). In unconditional generation on Objaverse Mugs, Mesh2NeRF produces more geometrically faithful extracted meshes compared to SSDNeRF, which struggles with self-occluded regions under image-only supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SSDNeRF [15], a single-stage diffusion NeRF framework that jointly learns triplane scene codes, a shared NeRF decoder, and a latent diffusion prior, but replaces its multi-view rendering loss with an analytic per-point supervision signal derived from textured meshes. The radiance field representation itself follows the standard NeRF formulation [38], and the density modeling draws on the top-hat approximation of a Dirac delta to produce a practical occupancy-based alpha function. The key conceptual departure from prior NeRF generation work (SSDNeRF, DiffRF) is the argument that rendering dense multi-views from meshes and then fitting NeRFs to those renderings is both redundant and lossy, since each pixel color must supervise all points along a ray; Mesh2NeRF instead provides independent density and color targets at every sampled point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that direct 3D supervision from meshes yields measurably better NeRF representations (3.12 dB PSNR gain on ABO) and more faithful geometry in unconditional generation, addressing a practical pain point for anyone training NeRF generative models on mesh collections. However, the approach bakes lighting into appearance via a fixed Phong model, limiting its applicability to scenes with complex or dynamic illumination, and still relies on virtual-camera ray sampling rather than fully exploiting the known mesh geometry for sampling efficiency. Open questions include whether the method extends to open scenes, multi-object scenes, or meshes without clean UV textures, and whether the supervision advantage persists at the scale of Objaverse-XL (10M+ objects).
