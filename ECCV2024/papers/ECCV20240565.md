## Problem

Instruction-driven image editing models (e.g., InstructPix2Pix) can significantly alter images at the semantic level, rendering all existing robust watermarking methods ineffective with bit error rates near 50%. No prior watermarking approach addresses this novel class of semantic-level distortion, leaving a critical gap in IP protection and misuse detection for the era of AI-driven image editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240565/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The embedding network takes a 512×512 RGB image and an L-bit binary watermark message (default 64 bits) and produces a watermarked image that is visually and semantically consistent with the original. The extraction network takes either the watermarked image or its instruction-edited version and outputs the L-bit message. During training, the PIDSG module additionally consumes a text editing instruction and the frozen InstructPix2Pix pipeline (VAE, U-Net, CLIP text encoder) to simulate the editing distortion end-to-end.

## Method

Robust-Wide follows the classic encoder–noise-layer–decoder deep watermarking framework but introduces a Partial Instruction-driven Denoising Sampling Guidance (PIDSG) module as the noise layer. PIDSG integrates the actual InstructPix2Pix denoising sampling process into training by selectively enabling gradient flow only in the last k steps (k=3) of the T-step sampling, making the otherwise non-differentiable diffusion sampling partially trainable while keeping memory overhead manageable. Diverse editing instructions are injected during training to expose the encoder and decoder to a wide range of semantic-level distortions, forcing the watermark to be embedded in robust, concept-aware regions (e.g., subject contours and background structures) rather than pixel-level features. The joint training objective combines pixel-space L2 fidelity, VAE-latent-space L2 fidelity, and MSE losses on both the edited and unedited watermarked images; the latter loss (L_ex2) is essential for convergence, as it anchors the extractor to the watermark location before semantic distortion is applied.

## Results

Robust-Wide achieves a Bit Error Rate (BER) of 2.6579% for 64-bit watermarks and 4.1867% for 256-bit watermarks after instruction-driven image editing via InstructPix2Pix, while maintaining PSNR of 41.91 dB and SSIM of 0.9910. All seven baseline methods fail catastrophically under the same editing, with BER values ranging from 28.15% (SepMark) to 49.96% (PIMoG), essentially equivalent to random guessing. The method also preserves editability, with CLIP-I similarity of 0.8430 and CLIP-T of 0.2148 for watermarked images versus 0.8402 and 0.2183 for originals.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robust-Wide decisively outperforms all six baselines on the primary metric of BER after instruction-driven editing. The closest competitor, SepMark [28], achieves 28.15% BER (still far above random-guessing threshold of 50% but an order of magnitude worse than Robust-Wide's 2.66%), while PIMoG [5] (49.96%), DWT-DCT [19] (49.23%), MBRS [12] (46.77%), CIN [14] (44.99%), and RivaGAN [30] (40.53%) all fail essentially completely. In terms of visual quality, Robust-Wide achieves the highest SSIM (0.9910) and competitive PSNR (41.91 dB), surpassing MBRS (SSIM 0.9870) and CIN (SSIM 0.9763) while trailing only MBRS in PSNR (43.98 dB). The paper also demonstrates generalization: Robust-Wide extends to ControlNet-InstructPix2Pix (0.96% BER), MagicBrush (9.34% BER), Inpainting, and DDIM Inversion without retraining, whereas none of the baselines were tested under these settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

Robust-Wide extends the classic encoder-noise layer-decoder framework established by HiDDeN, MBRS, StegaStamp, and PIMoG, which simulate pixel-level distortions (JPEG, screen-shooting) in the noise layer to achieve robustness. The key departure is replacing these pixel-level noise simulations with a Partial Instruction-driven Denoising Sampling Guidance (PIDSG) module that integrates the actual InstructPix2Pix denoising sampling process into the training loop, making the non-differentiable diffusion sampling partially differentiable by allowing gradients to flow only in the last k steps. This shifts the watermark embedding from pixel-level to semantic-level regions, a fundamentally different robustness target than any prior deep watermarking method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As instruction-driven editing tools (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, etc.) become widely accessible, the ability to trace unauthorized semantic modifications to images is an urgent practical need for IP protection and misinformation detection. Robust-Wide demonstrates that a single training pipeline generalizes to multiple editing models (ControlNet-InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, Inpainting, DDIM Inversion) without retraining, suggesting the semantic-level embedding strategy captures a more fundamental robustness property. Open questions include scalability to higher resolutions, robustness against future editing architectures, and whether the PIDSG approach can be adapted to other semantic distortion types beyond instruction-driven editing (e.g., style transfer, object replacement).
