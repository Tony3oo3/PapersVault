## Problem

Existing face reenactment and swapping methods rely on GAN frameworks with limited generative quality, while diffusion-based alternatives require costly full-model training and still produce blurry or identity-inconsistent results. No prior work provides an efficient, plug-and-play adapter for large pre-trained diffusion models that simultaneously handles both tasks with fully disentangled control over identity, motion, and attributes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image and a target face image (both 512×512 RGB crops) and produces a single generated face image. At inference, the source provides identity (via ArcFace embedding) and attributes (via CLIP features), while the target provides motion (pose, expression, gaze via 3D landmarks) and background context. Both reenactment and swapping are framed as conditional inpainting: the model generates only the adapting area (head region) while preserving the surrounding background, with the spatial control image combining 3D-projected landmarks and the invariant background.

## Method

Face-Adapter comprises three modules plugged into a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net: (1) a Spatial Condition Generator that recombines source identity coefficients with target expression/pose via D3DFR to project 3D landmarks, and predicts an adapting-area mask to define the inpainting region; (2) an Identity Encoder that maps an ArcFace embedding into the text semantic space through a 3-layer transformer decoder with 77 learnable queries, requiring no U-Net fine-tuning; and (3) an Attribute Controller that feeds spatial control (landmarks + background) through a ControlNet copy and attribute features (CLIP patch + global tokens projected via another 77-query transformer decoder) to supplement missing lighting, hair, and partial background. Training uses a 50/50 data stream between reenactment and swapping pairs from VoxCeleb1/2, with classifier-free guidance via 5% joint and 45% attribute-only condition dropping, for 70K steps on 8×V100 GPUs at batch size 32.

## Results

On the VoxCeleb1 face reenactment benchmark, Face-Adapter achieves the best LPIPS (0.1281), FID (29.27), pose error (0.0243), and gaze error (0.0415) in same-identity evaluation, and the best pose (0.0355), gaze (0.0543), and FID (41.09) in cross-identity evaluation. On FaceForensics++ for face swapping, it attains 96.47% ID retrieval, competitive with SimSwap (96.78%) and HifiFace (94.26%), while dramatically outperforming the diffusion-based DiffSwap (19.16% ID). The method is trained for only 70K steps on 8×V100 GPUs with all U-Net parameters frozen, making it significantly more training-efficient than from-scratch diffusion baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For face reenactment, Face-Adapter outperforms all GAN-based baselines (FOMM, TPSM, DAM, PIRenderer, DG) and the diffusion-based FADM on FID (29.27 vs. 31.19–102.17), LPIPS (0.1281 vs. 0.1344–0.4928), pose error (0.0243 vs. 0.0276–0.0707), and gaze error (0.0415 vs. 0.0566–0.112), though it trails TPSM slightly on expression error (3.24 vs. 2.70) and FOMM on PSNR (22.36 vs. 22.77). In cross-identity reenactment, it achieves the best pose (0.0355) and gaze (0.0543) errors and the best FID (41.09), but lags FOMM on identity similarity (CSIM 0.7146 vs. 0.8499). For face swapping on FaceForensics++, Face-Adapter achieves 96.47% ID, narrowly trailing SimSwap (96.78%) and InfoSwap (99.26%) but surpassing HifiFace (94.26%), BlendFace (89.91%), and FaceShifter (87.99%); it dramatically outperforms the only other diffusion-based method DiffSwap (19.16% ID) by a margin of ~77 points. The paper attributes its advantages to the Spatial Condition Generator (providing background context for inpainting) and the frozen U-Net (preserving pre-trained priors without overfitting), while acknowledging that expression transfer is limited by the sparsity of 3D landmarks from D3DFR.

## Relation to Prior Work

Face-Adapter sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) diffusion-based face editing methods (FADM, DiffSwap) that require costly full-model training, and (2) lightweight adapter plugins for pre-trained diffusion models (IP-Adapter, InstantID) that sacrifice fine-grained spatial control for text editability. The paper departs from both by combining ControlNet-style spatial conditioning with InstantID-style identity-to-text-space projection, while introducing a Spatial Condition Generator that explicitly handles background consistency — an issue overlooked by prior reenactment/swapping methods. The 3D Landmark Projector (built on D3DFR) provides shape-aware landmarks that bridge the gap between coarse 5-point pose control in prior adapters and the full 3DMM representations used by GAN-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in training efficiency: by freezing the entire U-Net and training only a lightweight adapter, Face-Adapter achieves competitive results at a fraction of the compute cost of full fine-tuning, making high-quality face editing accessible without large-scale training infrastructure. The 'one model, two tasks' unification via conditional inpainting is an elegant formulation that simplifies deployment. However, the paper acknowledges a key limitation — no temporal stability for video — and the expression transfer error remains moderate due to landmark sparsity. Open questions include whether the adapter approach generalizes to other pre-trained diffusion backbones beyond SD v1.5, and how to incorporate temporal consistency without sacrificing the plug-and-play property.
