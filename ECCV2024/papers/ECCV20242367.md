## Problem

Existing GNN OOD generalization methods (IRM, GIB, DIR, GSAT, CIGA) predominantly assume Fully Informative Invariant Features (FIIF), where the causal subgraph alone suffices for prediction across all environments. Under Partially Informative Invariant Features (PIIF), where spurious subgraphs also carry label-relevant information, these methods either fail to identify the correct causal subgraph or inadvertently incorporate unstable non-causal features. Moreover, CIGA, which does consider PIIF, does not exploit environmental features, limiting its robustness across distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph G with node and edge features plus an associated environmental feature vector e as input, and produces both a class label prediction and an invariant causal subgraph G_c ⊆ G. The environmental features are concatenated with GNN node representations to condition the generation of environment-specific subgraphs, while the causal subgraph is extracted via stochastic Bernoulli edge sampling guided by per-edge probability scores from an MLP. The final prediction is made on the extracted causal subgraph, with the environmental-conditioned subgraph serving as an auxiliary signal for the invariance constraint.

## Method

CSIB integrates Invariant Causal Prediction (ICP) with a Graph Information Bottleneck (GIB) into a unified min-max optimization: min_{g_φ, f_φ} max_{f_θ} L_c + βL(G, g_φ(G)) + λ(L_c − L_d). A GNN g_φ generates node representations, and an MLP computes per-edge existence probabilities; stochastic edge selection via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization yields the causal subgraph G_c. A separate environmental graph generator concatenates environmental features with GNN outputs to sample environment-conditioned subgraphs, enabling the invariance term I(Y;E|G_c) to be estimated empirically. The GIB term I(G;G_c) is upper-bounded by a per-edge KL divergence between the learned edge distribution and a Bernoulli(π) prior, acting as a sparsity filter that removes spurious features particularly critical under PIIF. The adversarial max over f_θ ensures the model cannot rely on environment-specific shortcuts, while the min over g_φ and f_φ drives the causal subgraph toward invariant, informationally minimal representations.

## Results

CSIB achieves the best or near-best accuracy across four of five GOOD benchmark datasets. On CMNIST-color (feature shift), it reaches 37.40% accuracy, a 12.7% relative improvement over the best baseline (DIR, 33.20%). On Motif-size (structural shift), it attains 60.99%, surpassing the next-best method (GREA, 54.13%) by 12.6% relatively. On the real-world HIV-scaffold and HIV-size domains, CSIB scores 72.53% and 61.33% respectively, outperforming all baselines. The only dataset where CSIB is not the top performer is Motif-base, where VREx edges ahead (71.47% vs. 70.18%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSIB outperforms all 13 baselines on four of five datasets: CMNIST-color (37.40% vs. best baseline DIR at 33.20%), Motif-size (60.99% vs. GREA at 54.13%), HIV-scaffold (72.53% vs. DropEdge at 70.78%), and HIV-size (61.33% vs. GREA at 60.71%). On Motif-base, VREx achieves the highest accuracy (71.47%) with CSIB close behind at 70.18%, a gap within one standard deviation. Among graph-specific OOD methods, CSIB most clearly separates from CIGA (the closest causal-subgraph competitor), beating it by 5.18 points on CMNIST-color and 11.85 points on Motif-size, suggesting that the added environmental conditioning and GIB constraint are critical. General OOD methods (ERM, IRM, GroupDRO, VREx) are consistently outperformed on the real-world HIV datasets and CMNIST, while graph augmentation baselines (DropEdge, GREA) struggle on structural-shift datasets (Motif-base, Motif-size) where CSIB's causal subgraph extraction provides a clear advantage. The largest absolute gains over the strongest baseline appear on Motif-size (+6.86 pts over GREA) and CMNIST-color (+4.20 pts over DIR), indicating CSIB is particularly effective when shifts are structural or feature-level rather than purely covariate.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSIB extends the invariance-principle-plus-information-bottleneck framework of Ahuja et al. (NeurIPS 2021) from Euclidean data to graph-structured data, and builds directly on CIGA's causal subgraph generation by adding environmental features and a GIB-based spurious-feature filter. It departs from prior graph OOD methods (IRM, GSAT, DIR, CIGA) that implicitly assume Fully Informative Invariant Features (FIIF) by explicitly modeling the Partially Informative Invariant Features (PIIF) regime, where spurious subgraphs partially determine the label and can mislead invariant-feature extraction. The GIB component is borrowed from Yu et al.'s subgraph recognition work and repurposed here as a capacity constraint that prevents the causal subgraph from absorbing spurious edges under PIIF conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FIIF/PIIF distinction addresses a practical gap: real-world graph data often contains partially informative causal structures, yet most existing OOD methods are designed under the FIIF assumption, leaving a blind spot that can cause silent performance degradation. The main limitation is that the FIIF/PIIF regime is assumed rather than detected, and the evaluation is confined to five GOOD-benchmark datasets with a single GIN backbone, so generalizability to node-level tasks, larger graphs, or different architectures remains untested. Open questions include how to automatically identify whether a given dataset exhibits FIIF or PIIF, whether the approach scales to heterogeneous or temporal graphs, and whether the environmental-feature requirement (which is often unavailable in practice) can be relaxed.
