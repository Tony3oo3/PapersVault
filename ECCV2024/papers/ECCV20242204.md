## Problem

Continual test-time adaptation (CTTA) must adapt a source model to a single-pass, unlabeled stream of continually shifting target domains without access to source data. Existing methods either rely on static source-domain buffers (violating the source-free constraint) or address forgetting only through parameter/feature-space alignment, leaving the problem of how to dynamically identify, buffer, and organize reliable information from the unsupervised target stream largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen pre-trained source model (no source data) and processes an unlabeled, single-pass stream of target-domain RGB images in real time. For classification, inputs are standard-resolution images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet); for segmentation, inputs are 960×540 down-sampled images processed with batch size 1. Outputs are per-sample class labels or pixel-wise segmentation masks, produced immediately upon encountering each input. A dynamic buffer of capacity 200 stores high-certainty samples with teacher-generated pseudo-labels, and a pre-computed source-domain Class Relation Graph (from classifier weights or class prototypes) serves as a fixed reference throughout adaptation.

## Method

The core contribution reshapes how online data is buffered and organized for CTTA. An uncertainty-aware buffering mechanism computes per-sample entropy and retains only samples below a threshold α·ln C in a dynamic buffer (capacity 200), replacing the highest-uncertainty entries when full, thereby aggregating reliable information from the single-pass stream without any source data. Buffered samples serve dual roles: (1) they are replayed via a pseudo-target cross-entropy loss (L_PCE) to suppress error accumulation, and (2) their class centroids form a current Class Relation Graph (CRG) whose edge-weight topology is constrained to match the source-domain CRG through a negative Pearson-correlation loss (L_CRP), penalizing topological distortion while permitting vertex movement for adaptability. The full objective combines a symmetric cross-entropy self-training loss between an EMA-updated teacher and student model with L_PCE and λ·L_CRP, all optimized end-to-end on the unsupervised target stream.

## Results

On the ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C CTTA benchmark (15 corruption types, severity 5, online single-pass), the proposed method achieves a 59.0% average classification error, outperforming the previous best DSS (64.6%) by 5.6 percentage points and RMT (60.2%) by 1.2 points. On Cityscapes-to-ACDC segmentation (10 rounds of Fog→Night→Rain→Snow), the method reaches 61.3% mean mIoU, a 4.6% relative improvement over CoTTA (58.6%). On CIFAR100-to-CIFAR100C, the average error drops to 29.0% (vs. 30.4% for SANTA and RMT), and on the gradually changing CIFAR10C setup, the error is reduced to 9.9% (vs. 10.4% for RMT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On ImageNet-to-ImageNet-C, it reduces the average error from 64.6% (DSS, the previous best) to 59.0%, and beats RMT (60.2%) and CoTTA (62.7%) by 1.2 and 3.7 points respectively; it achieves the lowest error on 14 out of 15 corruption types. On CIFAR100-to-CIFAR100C, the method reaches 29.0% average error, surpassing SANTA (30.4%), RMT (30.4%), and DSS (30.9%), with the largest gap on the 'contrast' corruption (25.1% vs. 26.7–27.4%). On the Cityscapes-to-ACDC segmentation task, the method achieves 61.3% mean mIoU over 10 rounds, a 2.7-point absolute gain over CoTTA (58.6%) and a 9.0-point gain over TENT-cont. (52.3%), which degrades severely over time. On the gradually changing CIFAR10C setup, the method attains 9.9% average error, edging out RMT (10.4%) and CoTTA (10.9%). The method also outperforms PETAL (16.0%) and SANTA (16.1%) on CIFAR10-to-CIFAR10C (15.8%), confirming consistent gains across both parameter-space and feature-space alignment baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CTTA framework established by CoTTA (Wang et al., CVPR 2022) and the teacher-student architecture with symmetric cross-entropy loss from RMT (Döbler et al., CVPR 2023). It departs from prior CTTA methods that mitigate forgetting via parameter-space recovery (PETAL) or feature-space alignment (RMT, SANTA) by instead organizing buffered target samples into a class relation graph and constraining its topology. The key conceptual shift is replacing static source buffers (which violate the source-free constraint) with a dynamic, uncertainty-aware target buffer that aggregates reliable pseudo-labeled samples from the online stream, then using those samples both for replay and for graph-based relation preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper is one of the first to simultaneously address all three CTTA challenges (online single-pass, unsupervised, source-free) within a unified buffering-and-organizing mechanism, making it more deployable in real-world scenarios where source data is unavailable. The CRG-based constraint is a more flexible anti-forgetting signal than direct feature-space alignment because it permits vertex movement while preserving inter-class topology. However, the absolute improvements are modest (e.g., 59.0% vs. 60.2% error on ImageNet-C), and the paper acknowledges that the entropy-based uncertainty measure is a simplification; exploring energy-based or other uncertainty formulations is left as future work. The method's reliance on a fixed buffer capacity and threshold coefficient also limits its adaptability to highly non-stationary streams.
