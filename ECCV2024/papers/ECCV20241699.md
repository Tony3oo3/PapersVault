## Problem

Current image-model explanations (activation maps, saliency masks) are instance-based and operate in pixel space, making them difficult to interpret and impractical for understanding global, dataset-level model behavior. Permutation importance is a well-established global explanation method for tabular data, but it cannot be directly applied to images because one cannot simply shuffle pixels to permute a semantic concept like 'presence of a chair' across a dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241699/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a labeled test set of images paired with concept-annotated captions (e.g., '2 person, 1 couch'), a black-box image classifier f, a fine-tuned text-conditioned diffusion model g (Stable Diffusion), and a concept classifier h for validation. It produces a per-concept importance score defined as the mean AUROC drop across P permutations (500 for synthetic, 25 for real data), yielding a full ranking of D concepts. Unlike GradCAM or LIME, DEPICT does not require access to model parameters or pixel-level masks, and outputs a distribution of importance values rather than a single point estimate per concept.

## Method

DEPICT's key insight is that while permuting concepts in pixel space is intractable, permuting them in text space is trivial: one simply shuffles the presence/absence of a concept across image captions. For each concept j, the method permutes the j-th entry of the binary concept vector across all N captions, feeds the permuted captions through a text-conditioned diffusion model to generate a new image set, and measures the classifier's AUROC drop relative to the unpermuted generated set. This is repeated P times to obtain a distribution of importance values per concept. Three testable assumptions underpin the approach: (1) permutable concepts exist in the text space, (2) effective generation (the classifier performs comparably on generated vs. real images, verified by <0.12 AUROC gap), and (3) independent permutation (permuting concept j does not alter other concepts, verified via the concept classifier). The method is validated on synthetic shape data, COCO scene classification (primary and mixed feature models), and MIMIC-CXR chest X-rays, consistently outperforming GradCAM and LIME in correlation with oracle feature weights, AUROC, and top-k accuracy.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset (100 models), DEPICT achieves a Pearson correlation of 0.92 [0.90–0.93] with the oracle feature importance, compared to 0.73 for LIME and 0.08 for GradCAM. On COCO primary-feature models, DEPICT reaches 0.73 [0.59–0.83] correlation versus −0.10 for GradCAM and 0.15 for LIME; on COCO mixed-feature models, DEPICT scores 0.35 [−0.05–0.66] versus 0.15 (GradCAM) and 0.27 (LIME). In the MIMIC-CXR case study, permuting BMI, age, or sex drops classification AUROC from 0.97/0.85/1.00 to 0.70/0.59/0.53 respectively, confirming the models rely on these demographic concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPICT consistently outperforms both GradCAM and LIME across all three experimental settings. On the synthetic dataset, DEPICT's correlation with the oracle (0.92) is substantially higher than LIME (0.73) and far exceeds GradCAM (0.08, essentially uncorrelated). On COCO primary-feature models, the gap widens: DEPICT achieves 0.73 correlation while GradCAM is slightly negative (−0.10) and LIME is near zero (0.15). On the harder COCO mixed-feature setting, DEPICT still leads (0.35 vs. 0.27 for LIME and 0.15 for GradCAM), though the confidence intervals overlap. In the MIMIC-CXR case study, GradCAM and LIME cannot be applied at all because concept-level masks are unavailable for X-ray images, making DEPICT the only viable method. Overall, DEPICT wins on every comparable metric (correlation, AUROC, top-k accuracy) and is the only method applicable in the healthcare setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPICT extends permutation importance—originally formulated for tabular data (Breiman, 2001)—to the image domain by introducing a text-space intermediary: concepts are permuted in captions and a text-conditioned diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) bridges the gap to pixel space. This departs from the dominant paradigm of instance-based, pixel-space explanations (GradCAM, LIME) by producing dataset-level, concept-space rankings, and from concept bottleneck models by requiring no access to model internals. It is also related to generative-AI-enabled counterfactual probing of classifiers, but differs in generating a global ranking of concept importance rather than explaining a single prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEPICT offers a practical path to dataset-level, human-interpretable explanations for black-box image classifiers without requiring model weights or pixel-level masks, which is valuable for auditing and safe deployment. However, its effectiveness is tightly coupled to the diffusion model's ability to permute concepts independently and faithfully; the MIMIC-CXR case study reveals that independent permutation can fail when non-permutable text (e.g., radiology reports) conflicts with the permuted concept. Open questions include how the method scales to open-vocabulary concept sets, whether it remains reliable when the diffusion model is not fine-tuned on the target domain, and how to handle scenarios where only a subset of relevant concepts is permutable.
