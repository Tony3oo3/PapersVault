## Problem

Direct text-to-video diffusion models generate all frames simultaneously from text-only conditioning, producing videos with lower quality and temporal consistency than text-to-image models. The paper hypothesizes that video generation, being inherently a time-series task, benefits from progressively stronger conditioning signals, but prior approaches either rely on weak text-only conditioning or require deep cascades of multiple models to reach high resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241654/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-video, the model takes a text prompt and first generates a 512×512 image, then uses that image plus the text to produce a 16-frame 512×512 video at 4fps, which is subsequently interpolated to 37 frames at 16fps by a separate interpolation model. For image-to-video, a user-supplied image replaces the generated first frame as the conditioning signal. The model operates in an 8-channel 64×64 latent space (8× spatial downsampling), and the image conditioning is injected by zero-padding the first frame to full temporal length and channel-concatenating it with a binary position mask and the noised video latent.

## Method

EMU VIDEO factorizes text-to-video generation into two steps: generating a first-frame image from text, then generating the full video conditioned on both that image and the text, using a single latent diffusion U-Net initialized from a pretrained T2I model. The architecture adds 1D temporal convolutions and temporal attention layers after each spatial block, with all 2.7B spatial parameters frozen and only 1.7B temporal parameters trained. Three critical design decisions enable high-resolution generation without a model cascade: (1) a zero terminal-SNR noise schedule that eliminates the train-test discrepancy at the final diffusion step, (2) multi-stage multi-resolution training (70K iterations at 256px 8fps, then 15K at 512px 4fps), and (3) finetuning on an automatically identified 1.6K-video high-motion, high-aesthetic subset. A separate interpolation model, initialized from the video model and trained only on temporal parameters, upsamples 8 frames to 37 frames at 16fps using noise-augmented frame conditioning.

## Results

In human evaluations, EMU VIDEO achieves an average win-rate of 91.8% for video quality and 86.6% for text faithfulness against all prior work, including commercial solutions (RunwayML Gen2, Pika Labs). On the UCF101 zero-shot T2V benchmark, EMU VIDEO achieves the best FVD of 317.1 (vs. 355.2 for PYOCO, 367.2 for Make-A-Video, 550.6 for Align Your Latents) and a competitive IS of 42.7. For image-to-video animation, EMU VIDEO is preferred 96.9% of the time over VideoComposer and 84.6% over Pika Labs across two prompt sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMU VIDEO outperforms all 13 baselines in human evaluation. Against the strongest academic competitors, it wins 81% in quality vs. Imagen Video [35], 90% vs. PYOCO [30], and 96% vs. Make-A-Video [68]; it also beats commercial solutions Gen2 [54] and Pika Labs [47]. On automated UCF101 metrics, EMU VIDEO achieves the lowest FVD (317.1) but a slightly lower IS (42.7) than PYOCO (47.8), while substantially outperforming Make-A-Video (FVD 367.2) and Align Your Latents (FVD 550.6). In the image-to-video setting, EMU VIDEO dominates VideoComposer [78] (96.9–97.4% quality win-rate) and Pika Labs [47] (84.6%), and also outperforms concurrent methods Stable Video Diffusion [7] (72.3%) and I2VGen-XL [89] (69.2%). The closest competitor overall is Imagen Video on faithfulness, where EMU VIDEO still leads by 56% win-rate, though Imagen Video's released prompts target text-character rendering—a known weakness of latent diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the EMU text-to-image model [17] for its frozen spatial U-Net backbone and on Make-A-Video [68] for its temporal parameter design (1D convolutions and attention layers added alongside frozen spatial layers). The key conceptual departure is a factorized generation strategy: rather than directly mapping text to all video frames (as in Imagen Video, Make-A-Video, or Align Your Latents), the model first generates a single image from text, then generates the video conditioned on both that image and the text, drawing an analogy to autoregressive sequence generation in NLP where conditioning progressively strengthens. The zero terminal-SNR noise schedule, previously identified as critical for high-resolution image generation [51], is shown here to be equally essential for high-resolution video, and the multi-stage 256px→512px training strategy bypasses the deep model cascades (e.g., 7 models in Imagen Video) that prior work relied on for resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The factorized approach is architecturally simple (a two-model cascade totaling 6B parameters versus 7–11B in prior cascades) yet achieves strong SOTA in both text-to-video and image-to-video generation, suggesting that explicit image conditioning is a more effective use of model capacity than direct text-to-video mapping. The JUICE evaluation protocol, which asks annotators to justify pairwise choices, addresses a real gap in reproducibility of human evaluation for video generation. Open questions remain around extending video duration beyond 4 seconds, modeling physical plausibility, handling unrepresentative conditioning frames, and whether the factorization advantage persists at longer horizons where autoregressive diffusion decoding might become more attractive.
