## Problem

NeRF-based volumetric rendering requires evaluating an MLP at hundreds of sample points per ray, making it too slow for interactive use. Existing real-time alternatives like MobileNeRF and BakedSDF bake features onto meshes but rely on binary opacities, so they cannot represent transparent or volumetric objects such as glass, hair, or clouds. The paper addresses how to retain full volumetric rendering fidelity while leveraging the speed of modern polygon rasterization and ray-tracing hardware.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of posed RGB images (360° captures at 1920×1080 for Shelly and MipNeRF-360, or standard NeRF-Synthetic resolutions) and produces a triangular mesh with an 8-bit texture map encoding per-vertex colour, continuous alpha, and spherical Gaussian lobe parameters. The mesh is extracted via marching cubes from a learned quadrature field on a 1024–2048 voxel grid, and the final rendering pipeline uses Nvidia OptiX ray-tracing to alpha-composite at ray–mesh intersection points, achieving over 100 FPS at 1920×1080.

## Method

The key contribution is a learned quadrature field Q(x) = sin(ω·F(x)) whose zero-crossings deterministically define where quadrature points should lie along each ray, replacing stochastic importance sampling. The field F is trained with a loss that matches its directional gradient to the volumetric weight function w from the pre-trained NeRF, so that more zero-crossings (and thus more quadrature points) appear where the scene is volumetric, while hard surfaces yield a single intersection. Marching cubes on Q extracts the polygonal mesh, which is then fine-tuned via a deformation field that perturbs vertices along the ray direction to minimize photometric error. Neural features (colour, alpha, spherical Gaussian lobes) are baked into a texture map, and rendering proceeds by ray-tracing the mesh, alpha-compositing at intersection points, and evaluating view-dependent effects from the texture — all within a standard OptiX pipeline requiring no custom CUDA kernels.

## Results

On the Shelly dataset (non-opaque shapes), the fine-tuned method achieves 37.29 average PSNR using only 2.40 samples per ray, compared to Instant-NGP's 40.13 PSNR at 7.13 samples and MobileNeRF's 31.62 PSNR at <1 sample. On MipNeRF-360, the method achieves 28.13 PSNR (indoor) and 24.12 PSNR (outdoor) with 5.15 samples per ray, outperforming BakedSDF (27.06/23.52) and MobileNeRF (23.06 outdoor) while using fewer samples than Adaptive Shells (17.41). Runtime reaches 570 FPS on NeRF-Synthetic, 323 FPS on Shelly, and 140 FPS on MipNeRF-360 at 1920×1080 with 1× super-sampling on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MobileNeRF and BakedSDF (surface-only methods using <1 sample/ray), the proposed method wins decisively on all datasets: +5.67 PSNR on Shelly (37.29 vs 31.62), +1.06 PSNR on NeRF-Synthetic (31.00 vs 30.90), and +1.06 PSNR outdoor on MipNeRF-360 (24.12 vs 23.06), confirming that multiple quadrature points are essential for volumetric effects. Against Adaptive Shells (the closest volumetric competitor), the method is competitive on Shelly (+1.27 PSNR: 37.29 vs 36.02) and wins outdoors on MipNeRF-360 (+0.95 PSNR: 24.12 vs 23.17) while using only ~1/3 the samples (5.15 vs 17.41); it trails slightly on NeRF-Synthetic (31.00 vs 31.84) and indoor MipNeRF-360 (28.13 vs 29.19). Against 3D Gaussian Splatting, the method loses 1.01 PSNR on Shelly (37.29 vs 38.30) and 2.31 PSNR on MipNeRF-360 indoor (28.13 vs 30.41), but produces an explicit triangular mesh with texture maps that GS cannot. Against Instant-NGP (the full NeRF baseline), the method trades 2.84 PSNR on Shelly for a 3× reduction in samples per ray (2.40 vs 7.13) and real-time rendering. On NeRF-Synthetic, the method outperforms SNeRG (31.00 vs 30.38) and VMesh (31.00 vs 30.70) while using fewer samples than Adaptive Shells (1.69 vs 3.53).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MobileNeRF/BakedSDF line of work, which bakes neural features into a triangular mesh with texture maps for real-time rendering, by replacing their binary opacity assumption with a learned quadrature field whose zero-crossings produce multiple ray-mesh intersection points per pixel. The quadrature field is trained to align with the surface-field from nerf2nerf, and the volumetric rendering equation is inherited directly from NeRF/Instant-NGP. The key conceptual departure from Adaptive-Shells (which uses SDF-defined shells for volumetric sampling) is that the quadrature field is a general scalar field rather than an SDF, enabling faithful representation of glass-like media where SDFs fail. The rendering pipeline leverages OptiX ray-tracing rather than rasterization, trading the simplicity of MobileNeRF's WebGL pipeline for the ability to handle ordered alpha-compositing across multiple intersection points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that volumetric effects (hair, fur, glass) can be rendered at interactive rates (100+ FPS at 1080p) using standard GPU ray-tracing hardware, removing the need for custom CUDA kernels that methods like Adaptive-Shells require. This makes non-opaque novel-view synthesis accessible on consumer devices and integrates naturally with existing graphics frameworks. However, the baking step introduces a measurable quality drop (e.g., 37.29 → 35.13 PSNR on Shelly), thin surfaces can still be missed, and the memory footprint for large scenes remains a concern. The quadrature field formulation is a clean abstraction, but the overall performance is competitive rather than dominant compared to 3D Gaussian Splatting, which achieves higher FPS with simpler primitives.
