## Problem

Existing masked autoencoder approaches for trajectory prediction rely on uniform random token masking, implicitly assuming all scene components are equally informative. This sub-optimality means the model often reconstructs simple cruising trajectories or straight lanes while under-representing complex behaviors like overtaking, leading to weaker behavior representations and limited generalization to unseen driving scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240073/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes past agent trajectories (2D coordinates over a 5 s / 8-step observation window) and vectorized HD-map lane segments, tokenized as polylines via an FPN (trajectories) and a simplified PointNet (lanes) in an agent-centric coordinate system within a 100 m radius. During pre-training the output is the reconstruction of masked trajectory and lane polylines; after fine-tuning it produces K multi-modal future trajectories (6 s on Argoverse2, 4.8 s on ETH-UCY) with per-trajectory confidence scores.

## Method

DySeT introduces two coupled mechanisms within a transformer-based MAE (depth-5 encoder-decoder). First, a dynamic sampling network (multi-head attention → MLP → Softmax) assigns a categorical distribution over all tokens; it is optimized via a REINFORCE policy-gradient objective that maximizes the expected reconstruction error of masked tokens, thereby rewarding tokens that depict complex driving behaviors (higher reconstruction difficulty = higher informativeness). Second, a masked self-distillation loss distills the EMA-teacher's full-scene representation into the student's masked-scene representation using an iBOT-style online quantizer that converts features into soft codeword distributions, with cross-entropy as the distillation metric. The two objectives are trained jointly: the sampler is updated w.r.t. the sampling loss while the MAE is updated w.r.t. reconstruction (L1 for trajectories, MSE for lanes) plus a weighted distillation term (λ = 0.1), progressively guiding the sampler toward informative tokens and enriching the visible-token representation.

## Results

On Argoverse2, DySeT achieves minADE6 of 0.67, minFDE6 of 1.28, and MR6 of 0.16, outperforming the strongest SSL baseline Forecast-MAE (finetune) by up to 8% on minFDE6 (1.28 vs. 1.39) and matching or exceeding the best supervised method QCNet on b-FDE6 (1.93 vs. 1.91) and minFDE6 (1.28 vs. 1.29). On ETH/UCY, DySeT attains an average minADE20/minFDE20 of 0.20/0.33, surpassing the previous best (EqMotion at 0.21/0.35), with a 25% improvement on minFDE for the ETH subset (0.46 vs. 0.61). In cross-domain generalization experiments, DySeT improves over Forecast-MAE by up to 17% on unseen-city Argoverse2 validation and achieves the best average across four unseen ETH/UCY domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DySeT outperforms all SSL-based baselines on Argoverse2: it beats Forecast-MAE (finetune) by 8% on minFDE6 (1.28 vs. 1.39) and 15% on minFDE6 over T-MAE (1.28 vs. 1.51), while also surpassing SSL-Lane's best pretext task by 23% on minFDE6. Against supervised methods, DySeT is competitive with QCNet (the strongest supervised baseline), trailing by only 0.02 on minADE6 (0.67 vs. 0.65) and 0.11 on minFDE1 (4.41 vs. 4.30), but matching or exceeding it on b-FDE6 (1.93 vs. 1.91) and minFDE6 (1.28 vs. 1.29). On ETH/UCY, DySeT achieves the best average minADE20/minFDE20 (0.20/0.33), outperforming EqMotion (0.21/0.35) and MID (0.21/0.38), with the largest margin on the ETH subset where it improves minFDE by 25% over EqMotion (0.46 vs. 0.61). In cross-domain generalization, DySeT surpasses Forecast-MAE by up to 17% on unseen-city Argoverse2 validation (minFDE6: 1.328 vs. 1.613 with finetuning) and ranks first in three of five ETH/UCY source-domain settings. On adversarial robustness, DySeT achieves a perturbation resistance score of 90.37% (non-targeted) and 86.83% (targeted), compared to 81.46% and 78.51% for Forecast-MAE, confirming superior robustness under both attack types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder (MAE) framework for trajectory prediction established by Forecast-MAE [15] and T-MAE [14], both of which rely on uniform random masking. The key departure is replacing random token selection with a dynamic, RL-optimized sampling network (grounded in REINFORCE [69]) that learns to prioritize tokens depicting complex driving behaviors, combined with a masked self-distillation objective that borrows the mean-teacher EMA mechanism [63] and the online quantizer from iBOT [82]. While self-distillation and adaptive masking have been explored in vision and NLP, their integration into trajectory prediction with a policy-gradient-trained sampler is novel in this domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving better prediction accuracy with fewer visible tokens (higher masking ratio), reducing pre-training compute while improving robustness to adversarial perturbations and cross-domain generalization—both critical for real-world autonomous driving deployment. A notable limitation is underperformance on dense pedestrian scenes (UNIV subset) where group-level social interactions dominate, suggesting the token-level informativeness signal may be insufficient for collective behavior. Open questions include whether group-wise or interaction-aware masking could address this gap, and whether the RL-based sampling strategy generalizes to other modalities (e.g., LiDAR, camera) or longer prediction horizons.
