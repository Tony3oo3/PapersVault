## Problem

Existing view-guided point cloud completion methods (e.g., XMFnet) rely on stacked cross-attention layers that capture abstract global semantics from images rather than the geometric structural information critical for completing missing regions. This indiscriminate fusion lacks explicit guidance on which image regions correspond to the occluded parts, leading to sub-optimal completion. The paper asks: how can the network be explicitly guided to identify and transfer only the critical structural information from the image to the point cloud?

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial (occluded) point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} and a single-view RGB image I ∈ R^{H×W×C} captured from the viewpoint corresponding to the occlusion, and outputs a complete point cloud. Both inputs are tokenized into the same number N' of tokens: the image via a large-kernel convolution that partitions it into spatial patches, and the point cloud via multi-step Farthest Point Sampling with Ball-query feature aggregation plus positional embeddings. The output is decoded from fused features using a decoder architecture similar to XMFnet, predicting 3D coordinates directly.

## Method

EGIInet introduces a two-stage pipeline: (1) a Unified Encoder that tokenizes both modalities into a shared token sequence and processes them through a single shared ViT-based encoder (SFE) to reduce latent-space domain gap; and (2) a Shared Feature Transfer Network (SFTnet), a separate ViT-based module that performs indirect information interaction between the two modalities without direct feature contact. The interaction is explicitly guided by a Feature Transfer Loss (FT-Loss) composed of an Informational Loss that aligns Gram matrices of the two modalities' features (transferring structural criticality bidirectionally) and a Structural Loss that preserves the 3D point cloud feature organization. After this guided interaction, a single cross-attention layer fuses the transferred features, and a decoder predicts the complete point cloud. The total loss combines the FT-Loss (weighted by α=0.01) with l1-Chamfer Distance on the output.

## Results

On the ShapeNet-ViPC benchmark, EGIInet achieves a mean CD of 1.211×10⁻³ and F-score of 0.836, outperforming the previous SOTA XMFnet (CD 1.443×10⁻³, F-score 0.796) by 16% in CD while using fewer parameters (9.03M vs. 9.57M). The method also substantially surpasses other view-guided baselines CSDN (CD 2.570) and ViPC (CD 3.308), as well as strong non-view-guided methods like PoinTr (CD 2.851) and Seedformer (CD 2.902). On zero-shot unknown categories (bench, monitor, speaker, cellphone), EGIInet achieves CD 2.354×10⁻³ and F-score 0.750, again outperforming XMFnet (CD 2.671, F-score 0.710).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGIInet outperforms all 14 compared baselines on both CD and F-score across all 8 ShapeNet-ViPC categories. The most direct comparison is with XMFnet [1], the previous SOTA view-guided method: EGIInet reduces CD from 1.443 to 1.211 (16% improvement) and raises F-score from 0.796 to 0.836, while using fewer parameters (9.03M vs. 9.57M). Against the other view-guided methods, the gains are even larger — CSDN [58] (CD 2.570) and ViPC [55] (CD 3.308) are surpassed by over 2× and 2.7× in CD respectively. Among non-view-guided point cloud completion methods, EGIInet also beats the strongest baselines PoinTr [49] (CD 2.851), PointAttN [35] (CD 2.853), and Seedformer [56] (CD 2.902) by a wide margin, demonstrating that the view-guided formulation with explicit information interaction is substantially more effective than point-cloud-only approaches. On zero-shot unknown categories, EGIInet maintains its lead over XMFnet (CD 2.354 vs. 2.671; F-score 0.750 vs. 0.710), confirming generalization beyond the training categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the View-Guided Point Cloud Completion (ViPC) task introduced by Zhang et al. (CVPR 2021) and builds most directly on XMFnet (Aiello et al., NeurIPS 2022), which used stacked cross-attention layers to fuse image and point cloud features in latent space. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) replacing separate 2D/3D backbones with a unified shared ViT encoder to reduce modal disparity before any interaction occurs, and (2) introducing an explicitly guided information interaction stage (SFTnet + Feature Transfer Loss via Gram matrices) that tells the network *which* structural information to transfer, rather than letting cross-attention discover it implicitly. This positions EGIInet as a response to the observation that indiscriminate cross-attention stacking tends to capture abstract global semantics rather than the geometric structure needed for completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16% CD improvement over XMFnet with fewer parameters (9.03M vs 9.57M) demonstrates that explicit structural guidance in cross-modal fusion can outperform brute-force attention stacking, a principle potentially transferable to other multi-modal 3D tasks. However, evaluation is limited to the synthetic ShapeNet-ViPC benchmark (13 categories) with only qualitative KITTI results, leaving open questions about robustness to real-world noise, occlusion patterns, and unseen object geometries. The authors note the unified encoder + explicit interaction paradigm could extend to other modalities and tasks, but no such experiments are provided. A practical limitation is that the Gram-matrix-based structural alignment assumes a meaningful correspondence between 2D image structure and 3D point cloud structure, which may break down for highly occluded or non-rigid shapes.
