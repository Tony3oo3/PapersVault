## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods suffer from three critical limitations: generation is slow (7–12 hours) and often produces low-quality outputs, 3D consistency is poor outside the specific camera poses used during training, and objects cannot be separated from environments, preventing flexible per-element editing. These issues collectively hinder practical deployment in gaming, film, and architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241978/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing a scene, which GPT-4 decomposes into individual object descriptions and an environment description, plus a scene layout diagram specifying object positions. Point-E generates sparse point clouds as initial object representations. The output is a complete 3D Gaussian Splatting scene with independently editable object and environment components, supporting up to 20 objects, generated in approximately 1 hour on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU.

## Method

DreamScene's core contribution is Formation Pattern Sampling (FPS), which replaces single-timestep SDS with three coordinated mechanisms. First, Multi-timestep Sampling (MTS) draws m timesteps within a linearly decreasing time window at each iteration, aggregating Classifier Score Distillation gradients across timesteps to simultaneously capture semantic richness (large t) and shape consistency (small t), with DDIM inversion ensuring content coherence. Second, a 3D Gaussian Filtering score—computed as the sum over rendered rays of Gaussian volume divided by squared distance to the rendering plane—prunes redundant internal Gaussians during optimization to stabilize training. Third, Reconstructive Generation exploits the observation that small timesteps (0–200) yield texture-rich pseudo-GT images without shape change, enabling direct 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction in ~15 seconds for final texture refinement. A progressive three-stage camera sampling strategy (center → ground → full scene) and separate object generation with affine placement ensure scene-wide 3D consistency and prevent multi-head artifacts while preserving per-object editability.

## Results

In a 100-participant user study (scored out of 5), DreamScene achieves the highest scores across all three metrics: quality (3.92), consistency (4.24), and rationality (4.05), outperforming all four scene-generation baselines. DreamScene also has the shortest generation time at 1.0 hour, compared to 7.5–13.3 hours for the other non-editable methods and 1.5 hours for the only other editable method (Set-the-Scene). For the 3D Gaussian filtering ablation, compression of 66.1% is achieved in the generation task with no significant quality loss, and 73.9% in the reconstruction task (NeRF-360) with slight blurring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamScene outperforms all four scene-generation baselines on every user-study metric. Against Text2Room (13.3 h, no editing), DreamScene is 13× faster and leads by 0.99 in quality, 1.67 in consistency, and 1.45 in rationality. Against Text2NeRF (7.5 h, no editing), the margins are 0.87 (quality), 1.53 (consistency), and 1.07 (rationality). Against ProlificDreamer (12.0 h, no editing), DreamScene gains 0.44 in quality, 1.05 in consistency, and 1.10 in rationality while being 12× faster. The closest competitor is Set-the-Scene (1.5 h, editable), which DreamScene surpasses by 1.47 in quality, 0.72 in consistency, and 1.17 in rationality, while also being 1.5× faster. For single-object text-to-3D generation (Fig. 4b), DreamScene's FPS produces higher-quality and more semantically faithful results than DreamFusion, Magic3D, DreamGaussian, and LucidDreamer, with DreamGaussian being faster but at substantially lower quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamScene extends the score-distillation lineage from DreamFusion (SDS) and DreamTime (monotonically decreasing timesteps) by proposing Formation Pattern Sampling, which hybridizes information from multiple timesteps within each iteration to simultaneously constrain shape and enrich semantics. It adopts 3D Gaussian Splatting as the differentiable representation (rather than NeRF or SDF), leveraging its explicit structure for easier object-environment composition and real-time rendering. The pseudo-GT estimation and DDIM inversion are borrowed from LucidDreamer, while the distillation loss uses Classifier Score Distillation. A key departure from prior scene-generation methods (Text2Room, Text2NeRF, ProlificDreamer) is the explicit separation of objects and environments via LLM-based prompt decomposition and affine placement, enabling per-element editing that inpainting-based approaches cannot support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1-hour generation time (vs. 7–13 hours for baselines) combined with scene-wide 3D consistency and per-object editing addresses the three practical bottlenecks that have limited text-to-3D scene generation in gaming, film, and architecture pipelines. The FPS insight—that small timesteps provide texture detail while large timesteps provide semantic content, and that blending them per iteration accelerates convergence—is a reusable principle for any SDS-based 3D generation. However, the authors acknowledge that outdoor scenes sacrifice photorealism relative to inpainting-based methods to maintain global consistency, and depth supervision for outdoor generation remains an open problem. The reliance on GPT-4 for scene decomposition and the need for manual layout diagrams also limit full automation.
