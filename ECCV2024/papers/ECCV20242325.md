## Problem

Existing text-to-3D human avatar generation methods rely on per-instance optimization (e.g., score distillation), requiring minutes to hours per avatar and making interactive or large-scale generation infeasible. No prior method combines fast feed-forward inference with multimodal control over text, image, body pose, and shape to produce rigged, textured 3D human assets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242325/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt, a target body pose and shape (encoded as GHUM parameters θ, β rendered into a dense 6D vertex-color control signal G), and optionally a single RGB image of a target subject. It outputs a textured, rigged 3D human mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from a learned signed distance field, at 256³ (standard, ~2 s) or 512³ (high quality, ~10 s) resolution on a single A100 GPU. The mesh is natively aligned to the conditioning body model, enabling direct rigging and animation without post-processing.

## Method

AvatarPopUp factorizes the joint distribution p(X|c) via ancestral sampling into three stages: (1) a fine-tuned Latent Diffusion model generates a front-view image conditioned on text and a dense GHUM pose/shape rendering, (2) a second fine-tuned Latent Diffusion model generates a consistent back-view image conditioned on the front view and optional text, and (3) a pixel-aligned neural implicit network (inspired by PHORHUM) lifts the front/back pair plus optional body signal into a textured SDF. The key training strategy is partial fine-tuning: the input convolutional layer is padded with zero-initialized channels for the new conditioning, and only the encoder's convolutional weights are optimized while all decoder and attention layers remain frozen, preventing catastrophic forgetting on small-scale 3D datasets. The 3D reconstruction encoder produces pixel-aligned features that are bilinearly sampled at projected 3D point locations, concatenated with positional encodings, and passed through an MLP to predict signed distance and surface color.

## Results

For text-to-3D generation, AvatarPopUp (high quality, ~10s) achieves CLIP color R-Precision of 0.62 and Top-3 of 0.77, and shape R-Precision of 0.11 and Top-3 of 0.17, while running 48× faster than DreamHuman (8h) and 18× faster than TADA (3h). For single-image 3D reconstruction, AvatarPopUp achieves Chamfer distance of 0.91×10⁻³, IoU of 0.70, and Normal Consistency of 0.86, matching PHORHUM on front-view quality (Color FID 11.0) while substantially improving back-view texture (Color.B FID 20.0 vs. 40.8) and back normals (Normal.B FID 25.8 vs. 38.8). The standard-quality model generates a full 3D avatar in just 2 seconds using 5 DDIM steps at 256³ resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In text-to-3D generation, DreamHuman achieves the highest color CLIP scores (R-Prec 0.68, Top-3 0.92) but requires 8 hours per instance, while AvatarPopUp (high quality) reaches 0.62/0.77 in ~10 seconds—a 48× speedup. TADA (3h) and CHUPA (3m) are both outperformed on color alignment; CHUPA also fails to respect text prompts qualitatively. On shape alignment, AvatarPopUp (high quality) achieves the best shape R-Precision (0.11) and ties TADA on Top-3 (0.17), while being orders of magnitude faster. In single-image 3D reconstruction, AvatarPopUp matches PHORHUM on front-view metrics (Chamfer 0.91 vs 0.84, IoU 0.70 vs 0.71, NC 0.86 vs 0.87) but substantially outperforms it on back-view quality (Color.B FID 20.0 vs 40.8; Normal.B FID 25.8 vs 38.8). Against PIFu, PIFuHD, ICON, and ECON, AvatarPopUp achieves the best or near-best Chamfer distance, IoU, and Normal Consistency, with the largest gaps on back-view FID scores where prior methods either do not produce back views or produce significantly less realistic ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

AvatarPopUp decouples text-to-image diffusion (building on Latent Diffusion and ControlNet-style conditioning) from 3D lifting (building on pixel-aligned implicit field methods like PHORHUM and PIFuHD), distributing the generative workload between a web-scale image prior and a small-scale 3D reconstruction network. This departs fundamentally from optimization-based text-to-3D avatar methods (DreamHuman, TADA, CHUPA) that perform per-instance score distillation over hours, replacing them with a purely feed-forward cascade. The partial fine-tuning strategy—freezing the decoder and attention layers while only adapting encoder convolutions—differs from both full fine-tuning and the heavier ControlNet architecture, offering a lighter path to task-specific adaptation without catastrophic forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The four-orders-of-magnitude speedup (hours to seconds) makes interactive, at-scale 3D human avatar generation practically feasible for entertainment, virtual try-on, and content creation pipelines that were previously locked out by per-instance optimization costs. As a byproduct, the method achieves state-of-the-art single-image 3D reconstruction, suggesting that generating a consistent back view via diffusion is a more effective strategy for resolving occlusion ambiguity than prior normal-map or probabilistic approaches. Open questions include whether the pixel-aligned implicit representation can be replaced by topology-free alternatives for better handling of loose clothing, and whether the partial fine-tuning strategy generalizes beyond human-centric tasks to broader 3D generation domains.
