## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models produce repetitive patterns and structural distortions when generating images at resolutions beyond their training size. While ScaleCrafter identified convolution layers as the root cause, its solution relies on empirical observations and requires offline pre-computation of kernel transformations, limiting compatibility across different kernel sizes and target resolutions. This paper seeks a theoretically grounded, training-free approach that eliminates the need for any offline computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates arbitrary-size, high-resolution RGB images (up to 16× the training resolution, e.g., 4096×4096 from SDXL's ~1024×1024 training size) without retraining. It handles arbitrary aspect ratios through a padding-then-cropping strategy, and operates as a drop-in modification to the UNet's convolution layers at inference time. The method is compatible with any pre-trained diffusion model using a UNet architecture (tested on SD 1.5, SD 2.1, and SDXL 1.0).

## Method

FouriScale provides a frequency-domain theoretical justification for why dilated convolution is the ideal cross-resolution kernel: it proves that the Fourier spectrum of the ideal high-resolution kernel k′ must be an s×s tiling of the original kernel's spectrum, and that a dilated kernel naturally exhibits this periodic structure. To address aliasing-induced scale inconsistency, a low-pass filter (spectral pooling) is applied to the input feature before convolution, removing frequencies above the new Nyquist limit. A padding-then-cropping strategy extends the method to arbitrary aspect ratios by zero-padding to a square multiple of the training size, applying the modified convolution, then cropping back. Finally, a FouriScale guidance mechanism generates an additional conditional estimation with a milder low-pass filter and swaps its attention maps with those from the full FouriScale-processed estimation, preserving structural correctness while retaining fine details in the final classifier-free guidance output.

## Results

On Laion-5B, FouriScale achieves the best FID_r and KID_r across all tested resolutions (4×, 6.25×, 8×, 16×) and all three models (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, SDXL 1.0). At the most extreme 16× setting, FouriScale reaches FID_r of 30.84 (SD 1.5), 39.49 (SD 2.1), and 56.66 (SDXL), compared to 32.00, 40.91, and 84.58 for ScaleCrafter respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on SDXL, where FouriScale reduces FID_r by up to 28 points over ScaleCrafter at 16×. Inference speed is also competitive: 540 s/image vs. 577 s/image for ScaleCrafter at 16× SDXL on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Attn-Entro [23] provides virtually no improvement over the vanilla model at any resolution or model (e.g., FID_r drops from 26.96 to 26.78 on SD 1.5 at 4×), confirming that attention-entropy adjustment alone cannot resolve the structural repetition problem. ScaleCrafter [14] is the strongest baseline, consistently outperforming both Vanilla and Attn-Entro, but FouriScale surpasses it on nearly every metric. The gap widens with resolution: at 16× on SDXL, FouriScale achieves FID_r 56.66 vs. ScaleCrafter's 84.58 (a 28-point margin), and FID_b 49.59 vs. 85.91, indicating far better preservation of the model's original generation quality. Notably, ScaleCrafter actually degrades below Vanilla on SDXL at 8× 1:2 (FID_r 106.57 vs. 90.23), while FouriScale maintains a clear advantage (71.77). FouriScale also runs faster (540 s vs. 577 s per image at 16× SDXL), avoiding ScaleCrafter's need for offline kernel-transform pre-computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the training-free high-resolution synthesis line, directly building on ScaleCrafter's empirical finding that convolution layers are the bottleneck for pattern repetition at higher resolutions. However, it departs from ScaleCrafter by replacing the ad-hoc re-dilation and offline linear-transformation calibration with a frequency-domain derivation: the DFT of a dilated kernel exhibits the exact s×s periodic tiling that structural consistency (Eq. 3) demands, providing a theoretical justification where ScaleCrafter offered only empirical evidence. The low-pass filtering component draws on spectral pooling and aliasing theory to address scale consistency, a dimension ScaleCrafter does not explicitly handle. The padding-then-cropping strategy and FouriScale guidance are practical extensions that go beyond prior training-free methods (Attn-Entro, ElasticDiff, DemoFusion) in handling arbitrary aspect ratios without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FouriScale offers a simple, training-free, and computationally efficient recipe for arbitrary-resolution generation from any pre-trained UNet-based diffusion model, eliminating the offline pre-computation that ScaleCrafter requires and thus improving scalability across kernel sizes and target resolutions. The frequency-domain framing (dilation → periodic spectrum; downsampling → aliasing) provides a principled lens that could guide future modifications to other resolution-sensitive components. Key limitations remain: the method struggles at ultra-high resolutions (e.g., 4096²), is restricted to CNN/UNet architectures and does not extend to purely transformer-based diffusion models (e.g., DiT), and the SDXL-specific finding that an ideal low-pass filter is suboptimal hints at unresolved interactions between model architecture and the filtering strategy.
