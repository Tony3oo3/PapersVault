## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods are trained on limited text-motion pairs and cannot generalize to open-vocabulary prompts. CLIP-based approaches (AvatarCLIP, OOHMG, MotionCLIP) attempt to bridge this gap by aligning text and pose spaces, but CLIP's image-oriented text space is poorly matched to motion descriptions and lacks temporal priors, resulting in unrealistic in-place motions that cannot produce global body translation or rotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240724/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes free-form natural language prompts (e.g., 'Jump on one foot', 'Experiencing a profound sense of joy') and produces a full 3D human motion sequence in SMPL-H format, including 21 body joint rotations (6D continuous representation), root orientation, and root translation (velocity + Z-axis position). By default, the pipeline generates 8 keyframes that are interpolated into a longer motion sequence, with the go-denoiser estimating global translation and rotation from the key poses alone.

## Method

PRO-Motion is a three-stage divide-and-conquer framework. First, a Motion Planner uses GPT-3.5 with five structured rules (bending degree, relative distance, relative position, orientation, ground contact) to decompose the natural language prompt into a sequence of simple posture scripts, bridging the gap between open-vocabulary text and a constrained pose-description space. Second, a Posture-Denoiser (a diffusion model with residual blocks and cross-attention over DistillBERT text embeddings) generates multiple candidate poses per script, and a Posture Planning module applies the Viterbi algorithm to select the temporally consistent and semantically aligned key pose sequence. Third, a Go-Denoiser (a transformer-based diffusion model) treats the selected key poses as discrete tokens and generates the full motion sequence with global translation and rotation via classifier-free guidance, learning interpolation and spatial priors from adjacent key poses.

## Results

On the ood368 test subset, PRO-Motion achieves the best R@10 (20.25%), R@30 (53.14%), FID (1.49), and MultiModal Distance (1.53), outperforming all baselines including the supervised MDM (FID 3.50) and open-vocabulary OOHMG (FID 3.98). On the kungfu subset, PRO-Motion leads in R@10 (20.31%), R@30 (50.00%), and MM Distance (2.37), though AvatarCLIP achieves a lower FID (1.97 vs. 4.12). For text-to-pose generation (Table 2), PRO-Motion surpasses PoseScript on both FID (0.24 vs. 0.48 on PoseScript-A) and mRecall in both retrieval directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRO-Motion consistently outperforms all five baselines on R-precision (R@10/20/30) and MultiModal Distance across both test subsets, indicating superior text-motion semantic alignment. On ood368, it reduces FID from 2.23 (MotionCLIP, best baseline) to 1.49 and MM Distance from 2.15 (OOHMG) to 1.53. On kungfu, PRO-Motion wins on R@10 (20.31 vs. 15.62 for MotionCLIP) and MM Distance (2.37 vs. 2.47 for OOHMG), but AvatarCLIP achieves a notably lower FID (1.97 vs. 4.12), suggesting PRO-Motion's generated motions are less diverse in distribution on this subset. The Smooth metric is highest for PRO-Motion (0.13–0.16), which the authors note reflects more dynamic motions rather than poor quality. For the text-to-pose sub-task, PRO-Motion beats PoseScript [18] on FID (0.24 vs. 0.48) and mRecall on both automatic and human captions, confirming the advantage of LLM-guided script decomposition over direct CLIP-based matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP-based alignment paradigm (AvatarCLIP, OOHMG, MotionCLIP) that attempts to bridge natural language and motion through image-space features, arguing that CLIP's text space is fundamentally mismatched with motion descriptions. Instead, it repurposes LLMs as a structured 'motion planner' that decomposes free-form prompts into compositional pose scripts, drawing on the LLM-as-planner literature from embodied robotics. The two-stage diffusion architecture (posture-denoiser for local poses, go-denoiser for global translation/rotation and interpolation) builds on keyframe-based motion in-betweening and diffusion-based motion synthesis (MDM, TEMOS), but is the first to predict whole-body translation and rotation from local key poses in a zero-shot, open-vocabulary setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The divide-and-conquer insight—that infinite motions reduce to a finite pose manifold, and that LLMs can bridge natural language to structured pose descriptions—offers a scalable alternative to collecting large-scale text-motion pairs, which is prohibitively expensive. The ability to generate global body translation and rotation (not just in-place motion) addresses a critical realism gap in prior open-vocabulary methods. However, the approach remains dependent on LLM output stability, generates a fixed number of keyframes (8), and exhibits foot-sliding artifacts, leaving open questions about scaling to longer sequences, replacing the LLM with a fine-tuned model, and handling contact-rich motions.
