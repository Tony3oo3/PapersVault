## Problem

Template matching for 3D tomography requires computing cross-correlations at every sampled rotation, leading to cubic computational complexity O((360/ε)³) in angular accuracy. For cryo-ET tomograms of typical size (~1000×1000×250 voxels), achieving sub-10° precision demands thousands of hours even with GPU acceleration. This paper asks whether the rotation dimension can be eliminated from the per-voxel computation entirely, yielding a method whose cost is independent of angular accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D tomogram (e.g., 960×928×230 voxels at 13.68 Å in cryo-ET) and a cubic, odd-sized 3D template density whose dimensions span the structure's bounding-box diagonal. A tensorial template is precomputed once per template (≈16 min for a ribosome) by integrating over a uniform SO(3) sampling, then reused across all tomograms. Output is a ranked list of P peaks, each with a voxel position, a rotation quaternion, and an LNCC similarity score; a minimum exclusion distance (twice the mask radius) suppresses duplicate detections, and a user-defined LNCC threshold selects final instances.

## Method

TTM encodes the template's appearance under all SO(3) rotations into a single tensor field T(t) of symmetric degree-n tensors (n=4, d′=4, giving 35 independent components), computed once via numerical integration over a uniform quaternion sampling. At inference, the tensorial field C_n(x) is obtained with exactly 35 Fourier-domain cross-correlations regardless of desired angular precision, and its Frobenius norm serves as a cheap scalar proxy whose local maxima approximate the true rotation-maximized correlation. The optimal rotation at each detected peak is recovered by the shifted symmetric higher-order power method (SS-HOPM) on the 4th-order tensor, initialized with 1000 random quaternions to handle symmetric templates. A position-refinement step (TTM-ref) searches a 3-voxel sphere around each peak, recomputing LNCC at the predicted optimal rotation in real space to correct sub-voxel shifts that would otherwise degrade rotation accuracy. The entire pipeline is training-free, runs on CPU only, and processes a full tomogram in under four minutes.

## Results

On synthetic tomograms with 8 templates, TTM-ref achieves perfect position recovery (0.0/0.0 voxels mean/max) and rotation accuracy of 0.03–0.14° mean / 0.06–0.26° max, compared to PyTOM(TM) at 0.32–2.35 voxels and 2.20–5.70° respectively. On the real EMPIAR-10988 cryo-ET dataset (ribosome detection), TTM reaches an F1-score of ~0.8 at a picking factor of 1, exceeding PyTOM across the entire precision-recall curve. In terms of speed, TTM processes a 960×928×230 tomogram in under 4 minutes on CPU alone (90 s more with refinement), while GPU-accelerated PyTOM requires several hours to achieve angular precision below 10°, and TTM's runtime is independent of angular accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is PyTOM (v1.1, GPU-optimized), the standard template-matching tool in cryo-ET. TTM-ref dominates PyTOM on every metric: position error drops from 0.32–2.35 voxels (mean) to 0.0 voxels across all 8 templates; rotation error drops from 2.20–5.70° (mean) to 0.03–0.14°; and on the real EMPIAR-10988 dataset, TTM's F1-score (~0.8 at picking factor 1) exceeds PyTOM's across the full curve. The speed advantage is dramatic: TTM runs in <4 min on CPU with runtime independent of angular accuracy, whereas PyTOM needs several hours on an RTX 4090 GPU to reach 10° precision due to its O((360/ε)³) complexity. The paper also briefly compares against DeepFinder (a DL method), noting comparable detection F1-scores but emphasizing that DeepFinder cannot assign per-instance rotations, which TTM provides natively. TTM's only noted weakness is slightly higher sensitivity to noise at very low SNR (0.1) compared to PyTOM, though mean rotation deviations remain under 3°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the mathematical framework of Almira et al. [3], which proved that a tensor field of degree-n symmetric tensors can encode all rotations of a template, enabling rotation-aware cross-correlation with a fixed number of correlations independent of angular sampling density. It departs from classical template matching (as implemented in PyTOM [8,20]) where 3D rotation search incurs O((360/ε)³) complexity, and from spherical-harmonic-based approaches [9,10] that require explicit concentric shells and are harder to combine with FFT-based correlation. The method also contrasts with steerable filters [13,11], which offer similar fixed-basis evaluation but were previously limited to 2D or simple kernels, and with deep-learning particle pickers [31,37] that cannot retrieve per-instance rotations or operate on volumetric data without extensive training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing 3D rotation-aware template matching from hours (GPU-accelerated PyTOM) to under four minutes on CPU, TTM removes the primary computational bottleneck for macromolecular detection in cryo-electron tomography and makes high angular precision (sub-degree) practically accessible. The honest reporting of TTM's greater noise sensitivity relative to PyTOM at low SNR, and the open question of whether degree-4 tensors suffice under realistic image distortions (beam tilt, anisotropic resolution), delineate the current boundary of the method. Broader impact extends to any 3D template-matching application where rotation search was previously prohibitive, though validation so far is limited to a single real dataset (EMPIAR-10988) and synthetic tomograms.
