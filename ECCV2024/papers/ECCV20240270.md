## Problem

Existing weakly supervised audio-visual video parsing (AVVP) methods uniformly exploit cross-modal contexts, which improves audible-visible event detection but degrades unaligned (audible-only or visible-only) event detection by injecting irrelevant modality information. Additionally, explicitly modelling cross-class co-occurrence relationships improves performance but imposes heavy computational overhead (T×C×C) at inference. The paper asks whether cross-modal integration can be optimized in embedding space so the network selectively uses it for aligned events while filtering it for unaligned ones, and whether class relationships can be leveraged during training without inference cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video sequence of T temporal segments with paired audio and visual signals, extracted as 512-dim tokens via pre-trained VGGish (audio) and ResNet152 + R(2+1)D (visual). It produces per-segment, per-class binary predictions for three mutually exclusive event types (audible-only, visible-only, audible-visible) across C classes. Training requires only modality-agnostic video-level labels; the paper also introduces new evaluation metrics (Ao, Vo, Type@AVo, Event@AVo) that jointly consider both modalities when scoring unaligned events, correcting a bias in traditional A/V metrics that inflate true positives by counting audible-visible predictions as correct.

## Method

CoLeaF employs two simultaneously trained branches: a Reference branch (self-attention only, with learnable class tokens concatenated to input tokens to model cross-class co-occurrence) used exclusively during training, and an Anchor branch (self- + cross-attention, e.g., CMPAE) deployed at inference. The key training signal is an event-aware NCE contrastive loss that pulls Anchor's segment tokens toward Reference's corresponding tokens, with the pull strength (vartheta) scaled by the fraction of unaligned events estimated from Reference's pseudo-labels—stronger alignment for videos dominated by unaligned events, weaker for those dominated by audible-visible events. Two knowledge-distillation losses close the loop: a self-modality-aware loss where Anchor's modality-specific predictions supervise Reference (compensating for its modality-agnostic video-level labels), and a co-occurrence class loss that transfers Reference's C×C class-correlation matrices to Anchor via MSE. This design lets the network benefit from explicit cross-class modelling (adding ~42% FLOPs to Reference) while incurring zero additional inference cost, since only Anchor is deployed.

## Results

On the LLP dataset (standard threshold setting), CoLeaF achieves 49.3% Ao, 62.4% Vo, and 58.6% AV F-score at the segment level, and 44.1% Ao, 62.0% Vo, and 52.1% AV F-score at the event level, improving the previous state-of-the-art (CMPAE) by an average of 1.9% F-score across all new metrics and AV. On the UnAV-100 dataset, CoLeaF achieves 41.5% and 47.8% AV F-score at the segment and event levels respectively, outperforming the best prior method (CMPAE) by 1.8% and 3.0%. The framework also generalizes: when embedded around HAN, it improves average F-score by 2.4% without architectural changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLeaF outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every new metric (Ao, Vo, T@o, E@o) and AV F-score on LLP. Against the closest prior SOTA, CMPAE [3], CoLeaF gains +1.1% Ao, +3.6% Vo, and +1.1% AV at the segment level, and +0.5% Ao, +2.2% Vo, and +2.7% AV at the event level (standard setting). Against JoMoLD [2], the improvements are larger: +3.1% Ao, +3.6% Vo, and +1.4% AV at the segment level. The gap over HAN [17] is most pronounced on Vo (+11.7% segment-level), reflecting HAN's inability to distinguish visible-only from audible-visible events. Under the CLIP/CLAP annotation setting (Table 7), CoLeaF still leads VALOR [9] by +2.2% Ao and +1.3% T@o at the segment level. On UnAV-100, CoLeaF beats CMPAE [3] by +1.8% and +3.0% AV F-score at segment and event levels, and surpasses HAN [17] by +6.5% and +6.4% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLeaF builds directly on the HAN architecture (Tian et al., ECCV 2020) that defined the weakly supervised AVVP task and the CMPAE backbone (Gao et al., CVPR 2023) that added modality presence-absence confidence. Its key conceptual departure is the argument that uniformly integrating cross-modal contexts—standard in all prior AVVP methods—actively degrades detection of unaligned (audible-only or visible-only) events, motivating a dual-branch design where a Reference branch (unimodal + class tokens) guides an Anchor branch (unimodal + cross-modal) via event-aware contrastive learning. For cross-class relationships, it departs from CVCMS and MGN, which incur O(T×C²) inference cost, by confining class-token modeling to the training-only Reference branch and distilling correlations to Anchor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The new Ao/Vo metrics expose a significant evaluation gap: prior methods' reported F-scores for audible-only and visible-only events were inflated because audible-visible predictions were counted as true positives, with HAN's segment-level Ao dropping from 60.1% (A metric) to 33.1% (Ao metric). This reframes how the community should assess AVVP progress and may prompt re-evaluation of existing methods. The framework's generality (any AVVP backbone can serve as Anchor) and the zero-inference-overhead class-relationship transfer make it a practical drop-in improvement. Open questions include whether the pseudo-label-based collaboration degrades when Anchor is weak early in training, and whether the approach extends to the language-model-assisted setting the authors mention as future work.
