## Problem

Existing class-agnostic counting methods either require exemplar images to define the target class or are restricted to images containing only a single object type, and no dataset exists to train or evaluate multi-class class-agnostic counting. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing MCAC, the first multi-class class-agnostic counting dataset, and ABC123, a method that counts multiple object types simultaneously without any exemplar input during training or inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240287/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ABC123 takes a single 224×224 RGB image with no exemplar or text query and produces up to 5 predicted counts, each paired with a pixel-wise density map. During training and evaluation, a Hungarian-algorithm matching stage pairs predictions to ground-truth labels; during deployment, similar predictions are merged and zero-count predictions are discarded. An optional example-discovery stage then uses SAM to segment and crop representative instances for each count, giving the user visual confirmation of what was enumerated.

## Method

ABC123 uses a ViT-Small backbone (initialized with DINO self-supervised weights) topped by m̂=5 convolutional up-sampling heads, each regressing a 224×224 density map whose integral gives a count. Because multi-class exemplar-free settings have no natural prediction-to-label pairing, the authors introduce a matching stage that solves an assignment problem via the Jonker-Volgenant algorithm, using the L2 distance between normalized density maps as the cost; the supervision loss is the summed L1 density-map error over matched pairs only, leaving unmatched heads free to capture valid-but-unknown groupings. The key paradigm shift is that examples are discovered *after* counting rather than supplied *before*: high-density points are selected with maximum latent-feature diversity and fed as prompts to a frozen SAM model to produce interpretable instance crops. The method is trained on the synthetic MCAC dataset (objects from ShapeNetSem with random placement, scale, and lighting) and generalizes to the photographic FSC-147 benchmark without fine-tuning.

## Results

On MCAC (the multi-class class-agnostic counting benchmark), ABC123 achieves a test-set MAE of 9.52, RMSE of 17.64, NAE of 0.28, and SRE of 2.23, outperforming the best exemplar-based method LOCA (MAE 10.91, SRE 4.05) without requiring any exemplar images. On FSC-147 (restricted to images with <300 objects), ABC123 with sub-class density map summation achieves MAE 11.75 and NAE 0.11 on the test set, surpassing CounTR (MAE 21.09) and LOCA zero-shot (MAE 33.96). On the single-class subset MCAC-M1, ABC123 trained on the full multi-class dataset achieves MAE 6.82, outperforming RCC (MAE 8.81) and LOCA zero-shot (MAE 6.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABC123 outperforms all five compared methods on MCAC without using any exemplar images. Against the strongest exemplar-based baseline LOCA [4], ABC123 reduces test MAE from 10.91 to 9.52 and SRE from 4.05 to 2.23, while requiring zero shots versus LOCA's 3 exemplar images per class. CounTR [13] (MAE 16.12) and BMNet+ [21] (MAE 17.29) are substantially outperformed, with the paper noting that both tend to count the 'most obvious' objects regardless of the provided exemplar. FamNet+ [19] performs worst among exemplar-based methods (MAE 26.40), frequently failing to discriminate between visually similar classes. On the single-class MCAC-M1 subset, ABC123 (MAE 6.82) beats RCC [7] (MAE 8.81) and LOCA zero-shot (MAE 6.94), and on FSC-147 (<300 objects) ABC123 with density-map summation (MAE 11.75) surpasses CounTR (MAE 21.09) and LOCA zero-shot (MAE 33.96) by large margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the class-agnostic counting line (Lu et al. 2018; FamNet; BMNet; CounTR; LOCA) from the single-class, exemplar-required setting to a multi-class, exemplar-free setting. It repurposes the density-map regression paradigm from exemplar-based methods but replaces the exemplar-matching mechanism with a multi-head ViT backbone and a Hungarian-algorithm matching stage for supervision, drawing on open-set class-discovery and clustering literature for the assignment formulation. The example-discovery stage inverts the exemplar paradigm by using SAM post-hoc to locate instances corresponding to each predicted count, a conceptual departure from all prior counting methods that use exemplars as input rather than output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key empirical finding—that exemplar-based methods assumed to handle multi-class scenes actually degrade significantly when multiple types are present—challenges a working assumption in the field and motivates the need for dedicated multi-class benchmarks. MCAC fills a critical gap as the first dataset enabling training and evaluation in this setting, though its synthetic nature and 224×224 resolution limit direct transfer to high-density real-world scenes. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real generalization holds for more complex photographic scenes, how the method scales beyond 4 classes or 300 instances, and whether the 'valid-but-unknown count' ambiguity (where the method discovers finer-grained groupings than human annotations) can be resolved or is simply a feature of the task.
