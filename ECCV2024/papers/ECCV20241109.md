## Problem

Standard self-attention in vision transformers assigns specific roles (query, key, value) to input features via learned linear projections, but the paper argues the essential component for global context modeling is the softmax-based global non-linearity, not these role assignments. Additionally, existing spatial reduction attention methods face a rigid computation-performance trade-off: reducing key-value resolution at training saves FLOPs but degrades accuracy, and users must retrain separate models for different efficiency targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241109/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images at 512×512 (ADE20K, COCO-Stuff) or 2048×1024 (Cityscapes) and produces pixel-wise class masks at H/4 × W/4 resolution. The hierarchical encoder produces four feature maps at progressively lower spatial resolutions (H/4 through H/16), which are fused in the decoder. A key operational detail is that the Inference Spatial Reduction (ISR) mechanism allows the user to adjust the key-value reduction ratio at inference time without any retraining, effectively turning a single pretrained model into a family of models with different computational costs.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is the Embedding-Free Attention (EFA) module, which omits the linear projections for Q, K, and V: the input feature is used directly as the query, while a spatially-reduced (average-pooled) version serves as both key and value, with softmax providing the global non-linearity. The EDAFormer pairs an EFT encoder (4 hierarchical stages, each with patch embedding + EFT blocks containing EFA and a depth-wise-convolution FFL) with an all-attention decoder that allocates EFT blocks in a {3-2-1} pattern, giving more attention layers to higher-level (more semantic) features. The Inference Spatial Reduction method trains with base reduction ratios [8,4,2,1]–[1,2,4] but allows increasing them at inference (e.g., to [16,8,2,1]–[2,4,8]), exploiting the fact that the query learned rich key-value information during training and can tolerate reduced key-value resolution at test time. This decoupling reduces FLOPs by up to 61% with ≤0.6% mIoU loss, and the embedding-free structure is shown to be particularly robust to this inference-time reduction compared to standard QKV-embedded attention.

## Results

EDAFormer-T (w/ ISR) achieves 42.1% mIoU on ADE20K, 78.7% mIoU on Cityscapes, and 40.3% mIoU on COCO-Stuff with only 4.9M parameters and 4.7 GFLOPs (ADE20K/COCO-Stuff) or 94.9 GFLOPs (Cityscapes). The larger EDAFormer-B (w/ ISR) reaches 48.9%, 81.6%, and 45.8% mIoU respectively with 29.4M parameters. On ImageNet-1K classification, the EFT encoder achieves 72.3% Top-1 (EFT-T, 3.7M params) and 82.4% Top-1 (EFT-B, 25.4M params), outperforming PVTv2-B0 (70.5%) and PVTv2-B2 (82.0%) at comparable compute. The ISR method reduces computational cost by up to 61% on Cityscapes (EDAFormer-T) and 25.3% (EDAFormer-B) with at most 0.2% mIoU degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the lightweight tier, EDAFormer-T (w/ ISR) outperforms SegFormer-B0 by 4.7% mIoU on ADE20K (42.1% vs 37.4%), 2.5% on Cityscapes (78.7% vs 76.2%), and 4.7% on COCO-Stuff (40.3% vs 35.6%), while using fewer GFLOPs (4.7 vs 8.4 on ADE20K). It also beats FeedFormer (39.2% ADE20K) and VWFormer-B0 (38.9% ADE20K, 77.2% Cityscapes, 36.2% COCO-Stuff) by 2.9–4.2% mIoU across datasets. In the larger tier, EDAFormer-B (w/ ISR) surpasses SegFormer-B2 by 2.4% on ADE20K (48.9% vs 46.5%) and 1.2% on COCO-Stuff (45.8% vs 44.6%), and edges out FeedFormer-B2 (48.0% ADE20K) and VWFormer-B2 (48.1% ADE20K, 81.7% Cityscapes) on ADE20K and COCO-Stuff, though VWFormer-B2 leads by 0.1% on Cityscapes. Against heavier models like Mask2Former (47.7% ADE20K, 47M params) and MaskFormer (46.7%), EDAFormer-B achieves higher mIoU with roughly half the parameters. The ISR ablation (Table 4) further shows that applying ISR to EDAFormer-T reduces Cityscapes GFLOPs from 151.7 to 94.9 (−37.4%) with zero mIoU loss, and to 59.1 (−61.0%) with only 0.6% mIoU drop, while the same reduction applied at training time (w/o ISR) causes 0.5% mIoU degradation at the same inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDAFormer builds directly on the spatial reduction attention (SRA) mechanism from PVTv2, which reduces key-value resolution via average pooling for efficiency, but departs fundamentally from the standard self-attention formulation by eliminating the learnable linear projections that assign query, key, and value roles to input features. The authors argue that the essential component of global context modeling is the softmax-based non-linearity between query and key, not the specific role assignments, and that removing these embeddings (Embedding-Free Attention) is both lighter and more effective. The Inference Spatial Reduction (ISR) method reinterprets the SRA structure from a train-inference asymmetry perspective: rather than fixing the reduction ratio at training time as in PVTv2, SegFormer, and FeedFormer, ISR trains with a smaller reduction and applies a larger one at inference, a perspective not explored in prior SRA-based segmentation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ISR method offers a practical, training-free knob for adjusting inference cost of any pretrained transformer-based segmentation model, demonstrated across nine different architectures (CvT, MViT, LVT, Swin, DaViT, PVTv2, MiT, SegFormer, FeedFormer) with 20–50% FLOP reductions and ≤0.7% mIoU loss. The embedding-free attention provides a clean architectural simplification that removes parameters and FLOPs while matching or exceeding embedding-based attention, suggesting the field may be over-parameterizing the attention mechanism. Open questions include whether the embedding-free design generalizes to tasks requiring explicit role differentiation (e.g., cross-attention with distinct source/target modalities), whether ISR can be pushed to even higher reduction ratios without degradation, and whether the train-inference asymmetry principle extends beyond spatial reduction to other forms of inference-time compression.
