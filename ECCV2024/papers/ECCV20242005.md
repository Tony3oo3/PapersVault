## Problem

Existing text-to-3D synthesis methods based on score distillation (SDS) and variational score distillation (VSD) suffer from mode collapse, producing nearly identical 3D models across multiple optimization runs for the same text prompt. The paper identifies that the unimodal Gaussian assumption in SDS and the limited representational capacity of VSD's particle distribution cause this low-diversity behavior, and proposes a principled way to break the collapse by diversifying the text conditioning per particle.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt y and K reference images (K=6) sampled from Stable Diffusion with a 'white background' guidance to facilitate clean textual inversion. It produces K diverse 3D models represented as hash-grid NeRFs (256×256 rendering resolution, 50K optimization iterations) or 3D Gaussian Splats (initialized from 3DTopia or Shape-E point clouds). Each output model inherits structural and textural cues from its corresponding reference image while remaining semantically consistent with the original prompt.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (HiPer tokens inversion): for each reference image x_i^r, a set of L2=5 HiPer tokens h_i is optimized via diffusion denoising so that the concatenated prompt [y; h_i] faithfully reconstructs x_i^r, yielding personalized embeddings h_i*. Stage 2 (Textual Score Distillation, TSD): K NeRF particles θ_i are jointly optimized via particle-based variational inference, where each particle is conditioned on an augmented prompt y_i' = [y; h_i*; φ]. Here φ is a shared set of L3=8 learnable tokens that replaces ProlificDreamer's LoRA domain adapter (~30K vs. ~1.3M parameters), trained separately with an MSE denoising objective. The TSD gradient (Eq. 8) is the difference between the frozen SD noise prediction under the original prompt and under the augmented prompt, weighted by the rendering Jacobian. The key insight is that per-particle prompt augmentation alters the optimization landscape p_SD(x_t|y_i'), preventing all particles from converging to the same mode of the text-to-image prior.

## Results

On 70 text prompts (60 from DreamFusion + 10 from ChatGPT), TSD achieves the best diversity scores: IV of 5.075 (vs. 4.586 for VSD and 4.577 for SDS) and CosineSim of 0.380 (vs. 0.476 for VSD and 0.720 for SDS). The quality metric IQ is 3.614, slightly worse than VSD's 3.345 but better than SDS's 3.695, reflecting a modest fidelity-diversity trade-off. TSD with shared learnable tokens also reduces training time from ~10.2 hours (VSD with LoRA) to ~7.2 hours per 50K-iteration run.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSD clearly outperforms both baselines on diversity metrics: it improves IV by 10.7% over VSD (5.075 vs. 4.586) and by 10.8% over SDS (5.075 vs. 4.577), and reduces CosineSim by 20.2% over VSD (0.380 vs. 0.476) and by 47.2% over SDS (0.380 vs. 0.720). On quality (IQ, lower is better), TSD is slightly worse than VSD (3.614 vs. 3.345, a gap of ~0.27) but better than SDS (3.614 vs. 3.695), indicating a small fidelity cost for the diversity gain relative to VSD. The paper notes this trade-off is consistent with known fidelity-diversity tensions in text-to-image generation. Qualitatively, VSD tends to collapse into a single shape variant (e.g., cone-shaped ice cream for 'ice cream sundae'), while TSD produces structurally distinct variants (glass shapes, different toppings) that inherit texture and structure from their respective 2D reference images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-3D synthesis lineage from DreamFusion's SDS loss through ProlificDreamer's VSD framework, which already models 3D outputs as a distribution over particles. The key departure is shifting the source of diversity from the variational approximation q^μ (as in VSD) to the target distribution p_SD(x_t|y) itself, by augmenting the text prompt per-particle with HiPer tokens inverted from 2D reference images. Additionally, the paper replaces ProlificDreamer's LoRA-based domain adapter (~1.3M parameters) with a shared set of learnable text tokens (~30K parameters), drawing an analogy to the 2D personalization literature where Textual Inversion/HiPer can substitute for DreamBooth/LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diversity in text-to-3D generation is a recognized but underexplored failure mode, and this paper provides both a clean analytical explanation (mode-seeking KL behavior) and a practical, plug-and-play remedy that generalizes across SDS, VSD, and 3DGS backbones. The fidelity-diversity trade-off (IQ degrades slightly while IV and CosineSim improve) and the dependence on HiPer inversion quality for outlier reference images remain open concerns. The Janus problem inherited from VSD is acknowledged but not addressed, and the authors note future work on extending the augmented-embedding technique to other text-to-3D methods and 3D representations.
