## Problem

Existing methods for representing shapes as unions of convex polyhedra (e.g., CVXNet, BSPNet) rely on implicit field supervision (SDF or occupancy), which requires watertight ground-truth meshes, expensive pre-processing into discrete sample points, and introduces approximation errors through sigmoid/softmin/softmax compositing. This paper asks whether convex polyhedra can be made fully differentiable and optimized directly from image losses, eliminating the dependency on implicit fields entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (16 rendered views for ShapeNet, 49+ views for DTU) as the sole supervision signal and produces a 3D mesh parameterized by a set of convex polyhedra (32 in main experiments), each defined by a collection of hyperplane equations. Because the mesh topology is non-static (vertices appear/disappear as planes are added or removed), standard UV unwrapping is infeasible; textured outputs use volumetric textures instead. The method is per-shape optimization with no learned network, so it is evaluated in an overfitting regime where training and test sets coincide.

## Method

The key mechanism splits convex polyhedron construction into a non-differentiable and a differentiable stage. First, a duality transform maps each hyperplane to a point in dual space; computing the convex hull of these dual points (via QHull) identifies which three planes intersect to form each primal vertex. Second, with those plane IDs fixed, vertex positions are recomputed by solving a 3×3 linear system (Ax = b) through PyTorch's differentiable linear algebra solver, enabling gradient flow from image loss back to plane parameters. The union of multiple convex polyhedra is handled implicitly by the differentiable renderer (NVDiffRast) via z-buffering, so no explicit CSG compositing is needed. Optimization is driven purely by L1 image loss, with heuristics borrowed from 3DGS—convex purging, plane purging, Loop-subdivision-based densification, and random convex spawning—to balance sparsity against detail capture in high-curvature regions.

## Results

On ShapeNet (13 categories, 100 shapes each), the method achieves a mean L1 Chamfer Distance of 0.022, outperforming VP (0.048), CVXNet (0.024), and BSPNet (0.027), and a Normal Consistency of 0.925, surpassing CVXNet (0.891), BSPNet (0.745), and VP (0.738). On L2 Chamfer Distance (×1000), CVXNet leads with 0.526 vs. the proposed method's 0.592, though the gap is small. In multiview reconstruction (Table 2), the method outperforms DBW on 5 of 6 DTU scenes and all 8 synthetic/real mesh scenes, e.g., reducing L2 CD from 1.07 to 0.25 on the NeRF Chair scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VP (32 cuboids), the proposed method wins decisively on all three metrics: mean L1 CD 0.022 vs 0.048, L2 CD 0.592 vs 1.861, and Normal Consistency 0.925 vs 0.738. Against CVXNet (64 convexes), the method wins on L1 CD (0.022 vs 0.024) and Normal Consistency (0.925 vs 0.891) but trails on L2 CD (0.592 vs 0.526), with CVXNet also winning on individual categories like sofa, phone, and display. Against BSPNet (64 convexes), the method wins on L1 CD (0.022 vs 0.027) and Normal Consistency (0.925 vs 0.745) but loses on L2 CD (0.592 vs 0.699 is actually a win; BSPNet is 0.699). In multiview reconstruction against DBW, the method achieves substantially lower L2 CD on most scenes (e.g., 0.25 vs 1.07 on NeRF Chair, 0.65 vs 1.03 on Mesh Spot), though DBW retains a slight edge on DTU scene S24 (3.25 vs 3.87). The authors note that performance on categories with large cavities (e.g., cabinet) is weaker due to occlusion inherent to RGB-only supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of supervising convex polyhedra optimization through implicit fields (SDF/occupancy), as done by CVXNet and BSPNet, by instead using differentiable rendering with image-based losses. The key technical insight repurposes the classical duality transform from computational geometry (Preparata & Shamos) to non-differentiably identify which three planes form each vertex, then re-solves vertex positions through a differentiable linear system, thereby enabling gradient flow to plane parameters. The optimization heuristics (densification via Loop subdivision, purging, spawning) are explicitly adapted from 3D Gaussian Splatting, bridging the gap between primitive-based shape representation and modern differentiable rendering pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for watertight meshes and precomputed implicit field supervision, this approach unlocks training with purely image-based data at much larger scale, and produces more accurate vertex positions through explicit three-plane intersection rather than implicit field sampling. However, the method inherits limitations of differentiable mesh rendering (no gradients for implicit edges from self-intersections), suffers from heuristic densification/purging, and cannot handle large cavities well due to occlusion in RGB supervision. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full scenes, whether more principled spawning strategies can replace random re-initialization, and whether the non-static topology can be addressed to enable standard UV-based texturing.
