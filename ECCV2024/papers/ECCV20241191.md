## Problem

SAM's zero-shot segmentation degrades significantly on medical ultrasound images due to low contrast, faint boundaries, and small target structures, and existing adaptations like SAMUS rely on computationally expensive end-to-end CNN fine-tuning and cross-attention fusion that do not fully exploit the complementary local-global information. This paper addresses how to make SAM both more accurate and more parameter-efficient for ultrasound segmentation without full backbone retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241191/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an ultrasound image and a GPT-4-generated natural-language description of the target anatomy as input. The text description is embedded by Med-BERT and fed to Grounding DINO (untrained on medical data) to produce a bounding box, which serves as the spatial prompt to SAM's prompt encoder. The output is a binary segmentation mask for the specified anatomical structure, produced by SAM's mask decoder conditioned on the fused image features and the bounding-box prompt.

## Method

CC-SAM replaces SAMUS's trainable CNN with a frozen RadImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 and adds lightweight adapters (a position adapter plus five bottleneck feature adapters on the ViT branch, and a single FC layer on the CNN branch) so that only adapter parameters are updated. The two branches' features are fused via a Variational Attention Fusion block that models each modality's latent representation as a diagonal Gaussian (using the reparameterization trick for gradient flow) and then computes modality-specific attention weights also drawn from a Gaussian, so that higher-confidence modalities receive greater weight in the fused representation. For prompting, GPT-4 generates a task-specific text description that is embedded by Med-BERT and passed to Grounding DINO to produce a bounding box, replacing the point prompts used by prior medical SAM adaptations. Training uses a composite loss of binary cross-entropy, Dice, and KL-divergence regularization terms that encourage the latent distributions to remain close to a standard normal, promoting robust and diverse feature representations.

## Results

On the foundational model comparison (Table 3), CC-SAM achieves the highest Dice score across all five ultrasound segmentation tasks: 85.59% on TN3K, 86.22% on BUSI, 92.85% on CAMUS-LV, 85.61% on CAMUS-MYO, and 93.51% on CAMUS-LA, while also attaining the lowest Hausdorff Distance in every case (e.g., 10.88 px on CAMUS-LV vs. 11.76 for SAMUS). Against task-specific SOTA methods (Table 2), CC-SAM similarly leads on Dice for all five tasks (e.g., 87.01% on BUSI vs. 82.22% for TransUNet) and on HD for four of five, with only a marginal HD gap on CAMUS-MYO. The frozen, unmodified SAM baseline performs poorly (Dice as low as 17.28% on CAMUS-LA), underscoring the necessity of the proposed adaptations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CC-SAM outperforms all five foundational-model baselines (SAM, MedSAM, SAMed, MSA, SAMUS) on both Dice and HD across every evaluated dataset. The closest competitor, SAMUS, is surpassed by 2.54 Dice points on TN3K (85.59 vs. 83.05), 1.68 on BUSI (86.22 vs. 84.54), 1.72 on CAMUS-LV (92.85 vs. 91.13), 2.50 on CAMUS-MYO (85.61 vs. 83.11), and 1.51 on CAMUS-LA (93.51 vs. 92.00). Against task-specific SOTA methods, CC-SAM leads on Dice for all five tasks, with the largest margins over transformer-only methods (e.g., 87.01 vs. 67.23 Dice on BUSI vs. SwinUNet) and a smaller but consistent edge over CNN-Transformer hybrids like TransUNet (87.01 vs. 82.22 on BUSI) and H2Former (87.01 vs. 81.48 on BUSI). The only metric where CC-SAM does not achieve the absolute best is HD on CAMUS-MYO in Table 2, where it trails FAT-Net by 0.18 px (16.11 vs. 15.93). The frozen SAM baseline is dramatically inferior (Dice < 55% on all tasks), confirming that the adapter + VAF + text-prompt pipeline is essential for medical-domain performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CC-SAM is a direct refinement of SAMUS, which itself adapts SAM for ultrasound segmentation by adding a CNN branch and cross-attention fusion. The key departures are: (1) replacing SAMUS's end-to-end trainable CNN with a frozen RadImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 plus a lightweight FC adapter, (2) substituting the cross-attention module with a variational attention fusion block that models per-modality uncertainty via diagonal Gaussians and reparameterized sampling, and (3) introducing GPT-4-generated text descriptions fed through Med-BERT and Grounding DINO as bounding-box prompts instead of point prompts. The adapter design follows the parameter-efficient fine-tuning lineage (Houlsby et al.), and the uncertainty regularization draws on the variational information bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen backbone with small adapters can match or exceed end-to-end fine-tuning for medical SAM adaptation, reducing training to a single 24 GB GPU — a practical win for clinical deployment. The finding that GPT-4-generated text prompts (routed through Grounding DINO) outperform standard point prompts for ultrasound segmentation is a useful signal that language-grounded prompting may be a more natural interface for domain-specific SAM adaptation. However, the evaluation is limited to ultrasound modalities, the GPT-4 prompt generation is semi-manual (one description per target class), and Grounding DINO is not trained on medical data, leaving open whether the text-prompting advantage generalizes to other imaging modalities or scales to open-vocabulary settings without per-class prompt engineering.
