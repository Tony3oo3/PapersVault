## Problem

Existing diffusion-based customization methods (e.g., IP-Adapter, DreamBooth) can generate personalized portraits but cannot place a specific person at a desired location within an existing multi-person scene, while local editing methods (e.g., Paint-by-Example, AnyDoor) can edit a masked region but fail to preserve fine-grained facial identity from a reference image. The core gap is the absence of a tuning-free method that simultaneously injects a reference identity into a specified spatial location in a group photo while maintaining natural body interactions with surrounding characters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference face image, a 512×512 target scene image, a user-provided position mask (bounding box or arbitrary shape for fine-grained control over height and body shape), and an optional text prompt. It produces a composited image where the reference person is inserted at the masked region with identity-consistent appearance and contextually appropriate pose, clothing, and scale. Inference is zero-shot (no per-image fine-tuning), completing in ~2.44 s with 6.51 GB peak memory, and is compatible with spatial control tools such as ControlNet and T2I-Adapter.

## Method

AddMe builds on Stable Diffusion's U-Net and introduces two plug-and-play modules forming a Human Essence Module (HEM). First, a Disentangled Identity Adapter encodes the reference face via a frozen CLIP image encoder into 257 ID tokens, then applies a separate cross-attention with its own Q/K/V projections running in parallel to (not coupled with) the text cross-attention, preventing identity leakage into other characters in multi-person scenes. Second, an Enhanced Portrait Attention (EPA) module—a lightweight transformer in each U-Net block—captures contextual cues (pose, clothing, scale) from surrounding characters and feeds them into the ID-Adapter's query via residual connections, encouraging natural person-to-person interactions. Training is self-supervised (the cropped face from the target image serves as its own reference) in two stages: first the ID-Adapter on mixed single/multi-person data, then the EPA exclusively on multi-person data, with data augmentations (flip, rotation, brightness, blur) and a geometric-center-aware cropping strategy to prevent trivial copy-paste learning. The EPA's output projection is zero-initialized (following ControlNet) so the module is gradually enabled during training, and the entire HEM is trained on the newly constructed AddMe-1.6M dataset of 1.6M scene images with instance-level face, bounding-box, and text annotations.

## Results

On the 10,000-image benchmark, AddMe achieves the best CLIP-H score (65.52%) and Face Similarity (60.15%), substantially outperforming all baselines on identity preservation. It also attains the lowest FID (26.11) and highest CLIP-IQA (55.81%), indicating superior overall image quality and harmony. In terms of efficiency, AddMe runs in 2.44 s with 6.51 GB peak memory, making it the fastest and most memory-efficient method by a wide margin (e.g., 26× faster than UniPaint).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AddMe dominates all three exemplar-based baselines on the critical identity metric (Face Similarity): 60.15% vs. 15.16% (PbE), 14.21% (AnyDoor), and 37.53% (UniPaint), reflecting its disentangled ID-Adapter's ability to avoid identity leakage in multi-person scenes. On CLIP-H (likelihood of generating a person in the mask), AddMe leads with 65.52%, narrowly beating AnyDoor (64.83%) and PbE (61.82%), while UniPaint lags at 57.35%. For global image quality, AddMe achieves the best FID (26.11) and IQA (55.81%), though PbE edges it out on QS (14.83 vs. 12.09), suggesting PbE produces slightly sharper local textures at the cost of identity fidelity. In efficiency, AddMe is 26× faster and 5.6× less memory-hungry than UniPaint (64.93 s / 36.7 GB), and also outperforms PbE and AnyDoor in both time and memory. The user study (Table 2) confirms these quantitative gaps: 83.33% of participants preferred AddMe for face similarity, 82.15% for quality, and 80.95% for naturalness, versus single-digit percentages for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AddMe builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent U-Net architecture and repurposes IP-Adapter's image cross-attention mechanism, but decouples it into a fully parallel attention branch with independent query/key/value projections to prevent identity leakage in multi-person scenes. The Enhanced Portrait Attention module draws on ControlNet's zero-initialization strategy for plug-and-play integration, while the self-supervised training paradigm (using cropped faces as reference IDs) departs from the paired-data assumption of prior exemplar-based editors like Paint-by-Example and AnyDoor. Conceptually, the paper positions itself between text-driven inpainting (which cannot preserve identity) and exemplar-based editing (which cannot control spatial placement or handle multi-person interaction), framing the gap as a need for disentangled identity conditioning combined with contextual person-interaction modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AddMe-1.6M dataset with instance-level face-position-text annotations and the accompanying 10,000-image benchmark fill a practical gap for evaluating identity-preserving local editing, a task that previously lacked standardized evaluation. The disentangled attention design offers a reusable architectural pattern for any multi-condition diffusion model where one condition (identity) must be spatially localized without contaminating other regions. Key open questions remain: the VAE bottleneck degrades fidelity for small faces, facial attributes (hair color, expression) are entangled with the ID token limiting attribute editing, and whether the EPA module's multi-person interaction modeling generalizes to scenes with more than a few characters or to non-human subjects.
