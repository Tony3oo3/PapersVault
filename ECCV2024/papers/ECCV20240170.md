## Problem

Existing vision-language FAS methods (e.g., FLIP, VL-FAS) rely on coarse-grained or single-element text prompts during fine-tuning, failing to fully exploit the potential of language supervision for cross-domain generalization. This limitation means the model cannot adequately decouple domain-specific content (lighting, background, facial attributes) from task-relevant features, nor can it capture the diverse sub-types of attacks within each class, resulting in unsatisfactory generalization to unseen domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240170/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image (224×224×3, segmented into 16×16 patches) and produces a binary real/spoof classification via cosine similarity between the image embedding and class-specific text embeddings. The text encoder (6-layer transformer) is frozen throughout training; only the ViT-B/16 image encoder and learnable prompt weight parameters are updated. Content prompts are generated offline by LLAVA-13B and categorical prompts by GPT-4, so no text generation occurs at inference time.

## Method

TF-FAS builds on a frozen-text-encoder CLIP backbone and introduces two modules for twofold-element fine-grained semantic guidance. The Content Element Decoupling Module (CEDM) uses MLLM-generated prompts describing environment and facial attributes, then enforces an orthogonal loss between image features and content-prompt features to strip domain-specific semantics from the visual representation. The Fine-Grained Categorical Element Module (FCEM) generates 64 paraphrased categorical prompts per class via GPT-4, filters them by dataset-distribution compatibility, and adaptively integrates them through learnable softmax weights to model intra-class diversity. The overall training objective combines cross-entropy classification, the orthogonal decoupling loss, and a simCLR contrastive loss on augmented image views, with the text encoder kept frozen to preserve the joint text-image space.

## Results

In Protocol 1 (leave-one-domain-out across MSU-MFSD, CASIA-MFSD, Idiap, OULU-NPU), TF-FAS achieves an average HTER of 2.19% without CelebA-Spoof and 1.27% with CelebA-Spoof, outperforming the strongest vision-language baseline FLIP-MCL* (3.01% with CelebA-Spoof) by 1.74 points. In Protocol 2 (single-source-to-single-target, 12 scenarios), TF-FAS attains an average HTER of 3.73% without CelebA-Spoof and 1.99% with CelebA-Spoof, surpassing the prior SOTA CDFTN-L (13.2%) by 9.47 points and FLIP-MCL* (4.84%) by 2.85 points. The best individual scenario result is 0.58% HTER on OMI→C with CelebA-Spoof, with AUC reaching 99.99%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TF-FAS outperforms all 28 baselines across both protocols. Against the closest vision-language competitor FLIP-MCL* (ICCV'23), TF-FAS reduces average HTER from 3.01% to 1.27% (with CelebA-Spoof) and from 3.01% to 2.19% (without), a gain of 1.74 points in both settings. Against the prior best pure-vision DG method IADG (CVPR'23, avg HTER 8.40%), TF-FAS without CelebA-Spoof improves by 6.21 points. Against the most recent CVPR'24 method GAC-FAS (6.52%), the improvement is 4.33 points. In Protocol 2, TF-FAS (3.73% avg HTER without CelebA-Spoof) beats the prior SOTA CDFTN-L (AAAI'23, 13.2%) by 9.47 points, with the largest single-scenario gain of +25.74 on C→O. The only scenario where TF-FAS does not achieve the absolute best HTER is OMI→C without CelebA-Spoof (0.81% vs. FLIP-MCL*'s 0.54%), but TF-FAS still leads in AUC (99.92 vs. 99.98 is a near-tie) and dominates all other three scenarios decisively.

## Relation to Prior Work

TF-FAS extends the CLIP-based language-guided FAS paradigm initiated by FLIP (ICCV 2023) and VL-FAS (ICASSP 2024), which relied on coarse-grained or single-element text prompts during fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is the twofold-element design: CEDM leverages MLLM-generated content descriptions (via LLAVA) to orthogonally disentangle domain-specific semantics from task-relevant features, while FCEM uses GPT-4 to generate 64 fine-grained categorical prompts per class with adaptive weighted integration. This positions the work at the intersection of vision-language pre-training, prompt engineering, and feature disentanglement for domain generalization, moving beyond the single-prompt paradigm of prior VLP-based FAS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that richer language supervision—specifically fine-grained categorical prompts combined with content decoupling—can substantially narrow the domain gap in FAS, achieving an average HTER of 1.27% (with CelebA-Spoof) that surpasses FLIP-MCL's 3.01% even when the latter has access to the auxiliary dataset. This suggests that the quality of language guidance can partially compensate for data volume, which is practically significant for deployment in low-data regimes. However, the approach is constrained to a two-class setting, a frozen CLIP text encoder, and a small batch size (3), and its reliance on GPT-4 and LLAVA for prompt generation introduces external model dependencies that may limit reproducibility and scalability to more granular attack taxonomies. Open questions include whether the orthogonal disentanglement strategy generalizes to multi-class or open-set FAS, and whether the fine-grained prompt paradigm transfers to other biometric verification tasks.
