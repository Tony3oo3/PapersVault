## Problem

Modern LLM-driven AI agents remain confined to text-based environments or simplistic simulations, lacking any tangible connection to the visual and sensory richness of the real world. Existing embodied AI relies on narrow simulated environments (game engines, tabletop scenes) that cannot capture the unpredictability and geographic diversity of actual urban settings. The paper asks: how can we ground agents in an environment as rich and diverse as the real world without the constraints of physical hardware?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241187/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The platform's core input is a pair of geographic coordinates, which serves as the universal key to unlock street view imagery, valid movement queries, nearby place information, and route planning via the Google Maps Platform. Agents additionally consume text navigation instructions, place metadata from external APIs (real estate, reviews), and user-defined character metadata (background, intention, interoceptive state). Outputs are structured as discrete navigation actions (turn, forward, stop), detection bounding boxes with category labels, place-type classifications from 96 options, multiple-choice VQA answers, and structured itinerary plans with cost and well-being estimates.

## Method

V-IRL is a hierarchical platform with three capability layers built atop a geospatial foundation: (1) Environment/Action components use GMP to provide navigable city representations and geolocation-based tool access; (2) Vision/Perception components combine pretrained localization models (GLIP), open-world recognition (CLIP, EVA-CLIP), feature matching (LightGlue) for cross-view deduplication, and multimodal VQA/captioning models; (3) Language/Reasoning components use LLMs (GPT-4, Llama 2) for planning, tool orchestration, and multi-agent collaboration. Agents are instantiated by writing task-specific run() routines that compose these components with character metadata. The paper introduces three benchmarks with an automated data-collection pipeline spanning 14 districts across 12 cities on 6 continents, and a key empirical finding is that cascading category-agnostic object proposals (GLIP) to zero-shot recognizers (CLIP) outperforms fine-tuned open-world detectors (GroundingDINO, Owl-ViT) on diverse place types, while vision models exhibit significant performance drops in non-English-dominant and less-developed urban regions.

## Results

On the V-IRL VLN benchmark (18 routes, 9 regions), the Oracle (no vision) achieves a perfect 1.0 success rate, while the best vision-based method, CLIP (L/14@336px), reaches only 44% success, highlighting a large perception-to-action gap. On V-IRL Place Detection, CLIP with GLIP proposals achieves the highest AR20 of 23.7%, substantially outperforming dedicated open-world detectors like GroundingDINO (5.8%) and Owl-ViT v2 (3.4%). For Place Recognition, CLIP ViT-L/14@336px leads at 41.3% mAcc over 96 categories, and for Place VQA, BLIP-2 (FlanT5xxl) achieves 69.6% mAcc, far exceeding LLaVA-1.5-13B (61.9%) and MiniGPT-4 (3.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On V-IRL Place Detection, CLIP with GLIP proposals (AR20 = 23.7%) outperforms all dedicated open-world detectors: OpenSeeD (16.7%), GLIP (9.0%), Owl-ViT (7.1%), GroundingDINO (5.8%), and Owl-ViT v2 (3.4%), suggesting that cascading category-agnostic proposals to a zero-shot recognizer is more robust than fine-tuned detectors with limited vocabulary. On Place Recognition, CLIP ViT-L/14@336px (41.3%) edges out Eva-02-CLIP ViT-L/14@336px (40.7%) and SigLIP ViT-L/16@384px (37.3%), while OpenCLIP ViT-L/14 (31.0%) and smaller variants trail significantly. For Place VQA, BLIP-2 (69.6%) and InstructBLIP (68.0%) dominate, with LLaVA-1.5-13B (61.9%) and LLaVA-1.5-7B (60.1%) following; LLaVA (23.5%), Shikra (10.9%), mPLUG-Owl (5.5%), and MiniGPT-4 (3.9%) perform poorly, largely due to inconsistent answers in circular evaluation. On the end-to-end VLN task, CLIP (L/14@336px) achieves the best vision-based success rate (44%), outperforming EVA-02-CLIP BigE/14-plus (39%), PP-OCR+GPT-3.5 (28%), and LLaVA-1.5-13B (11%); the authors attribute LLaVA's underperformance to the alignment tax from instruction tuning degrading its CLIP vision encoder. Across all benchmarks, vision models show systematic degradation in non-English-dominant cities (Tokyo, Hong Kong, Buenos Aires) and in Lagos, revealing geographic and linguistic biases in current open-world vision models.

## Relation to Prior Work

V-IRL carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-driven agents, embodied AI, and open-world computer vision, but does not build on a single prior method. It departs from text-only LLM agents by grounding them in real geospatial and street-view data, from embodied AI by replacing simulated game-engine or tabletop environments with actual Google Street View imagery across 12 global cities, and from open-world CV by exposing the gap between 'open-Internet' training distributions and truly diverse real-world scenes. Rather than proposing new model architectures, the paper repurposes existing pretrained components (CLIP, GPT-4, GroundingDINO, LightGlue, etc.) within a new hierarchical platform and benchmark suite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The platform and its four benchmarks (Place Detection, Place Recognition, Place VQA, VLN) provide the first scalable, geographically diverse testbed for evaluating vision-language models on real-world street-view data, revealing that current 'open-world' models are effectively 'open-Internet' models that degrade sharply in non-English, non-Western cities. The VLN results expose a critical bottleneck: even with a perfect LLM (GPT-4), vision-model noise collapses navigation success from 100% (oracle) to 22–44%, highlighting that perception quality is the limiting factor for embodied agents. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond the 14 evaluated districts, how the platform scales as street-view coverage expands, and whether the simple CLIP+GLIP-proposal cascade can be made competitive with dedicated detectors at scale.
