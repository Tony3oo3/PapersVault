## Problem

Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) achieves strong length extrapolation in language models, but its applicability to 2D vision data remains underexplored. Existing position embeddings for ViTs (APE and RPB) degrade when inference resolution differs from training resolution, and the 2D Axial RoPE used in prior vision works cannot represent diagonal spatial relationships that are critical for convolutional-style attention patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240259/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (trained at 224×224, evaluated from 96×96 up to 512×512) and produces class labels, bounding boxes, or pixel-wise segmentation masks depending on the downstream head. The key operational detail is that RoPE is injected only into the backbone (ViT or Swin Transformer) by replacing APE or RPB, while downstream detectors (DINO) and segmentation heads (UperNet, Mask2Former) remain architecturally unchanged. The same checkpoint trained at 224×224 is directly evaluated at higher resolutions without any retraining or position-embedding interpolation.

## Method

The paper extends 1D RoPE to 2D images by applying complex rotations e^{iθ_t p} to query and key vectors via a Hadamard product, where the rotation matrix encodes spatial position. Two variants are proposed: RoPE-Axial splits the head dimension in half and applies independent 1D RoPE along x and y axes, while RoPE-Mixed uses a unified rotation e^{i(θ_t^x p^x + θ_t^y p^y)} with learnable per-axis frequencies that can represent diagonal directions. The mixed formulation generalizes the axial case (recovered when one frequency → 0) and is motivated by a 2D Fourier analysis showing axial frequencies produce artifacts along axis lines. Frequencies are learned per-head and per-layer, adding only ~0.01% of ViT-B parameters, and the pre-computed rotation matrix incurs negligible inference cost (~0.01% of FLOPs). The approach is validated by replacing APE in ViT or RPB in Swin Transformer, with optional co-use of the original position embedding for improved interpolation at lower resolutions.

## Results

On MS-COCO object detection with DINO-ViTDet, RoPE-Mixed improves box AP by +1.8 over APE for both ViT-B (51.2 vs 49.4) and ViT-L (52.9 vs 51.1). On ADE-20k semantic segmentation with ViT-UperNet, RoPE-Mixed+APE achieves +2.3 mIoU (single-scale, 50.0 vs 47.7) and +2.5 mIoU (multi-scale, 50.9 vs 48.4) over APE for ViT-B. For Swin Transformer backbones, RoPE-Mixed consistently outperforms RPB across detection and segmentation, with the largest gain being +0.9 mIoU on ADE-20k (Swin-S: 51.1 vs 50.2). In multi-resolution classification on ImageNet-1k, RoPE-Mixed maintains or improves accuracy at all extrapolation resolutions (>224) where APE degrades significantly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViT with APE [6], RoPE-Mixed wins decisively: +1.8 AP on COCO detection (ViT-B: 51.2 vs 49.4), +2.3 mIoU on ADE-20k segmentation (ViT-B: 50.0 vs 47.7), and consistent gains at all extrapolation resolutions (>224) on ImageNet-1k multi-resolution classification where APE degrades sharply. Against Swin Transformer with RPB [17], RoPE-Mixed also wins across the board: +0.3 AP on COCO detection (Swin-B: 54.5 vs 54.2), +0.9 mIoU on ADE-20k (Swin-S: 51.1 vs 50.2), and superior multi-resolution performance at both interpolation and extrapolation resolutions. Against ResFormer-S [30] (a dedicated multi-resolution ViT using depth-wise conv position embeddings and self-distillation training), RoPE-Mixed+APE outperforms at all extrapolation resolutions (e.g., 78.5 vs 77.7 at 512×512) but underperforms at small resolutions ≤160 (e.g., 58.5 vs 57.8 at 96×96), indicating that APE is still needed for strong interpolation while RoPE handles extrapolation. Notably, RoPE-Mixed alone (without APE) already matches or exceeds ResFormer at 224 and above, and the combined RoPE-Mixed+APE configuration dominates across the full resolution range.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) from its 1D language-modeling origin (RoFormer) to 2D vision, proposing two implementations: RoPE-Axial (a straightforward per-axis extension already used in EVA-02, Unified-IO 2, and FiT for generation/multimodal tasks) and the novel RoPE-Mixed, which introduces learnable mixed-axis frequencies to handle diagonal spatial directions that axial frequencies cannot express. It positions RoPE as a drop-in replacement for the two dominant ViT position embeddings—Absolute Positional Embedding (APE) in standard ViT and Relative Position Bias (RPB) in Swin Transformer—while contrasting with multi-resolution-specific methods (CAPE, ResFormer, FlexiViT) that require special training recipes. The key conceptual departure from prior 2D RoPE work is the argument that fixed axial frequencies are suboptimal for vision because they cannot represent diagonal relative positions, and that learnable mixed frequencies resolve this limitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RoPE-Mixed offers a near-zero-overhead (~0.01% FLOPs) position embedding that improves extrapolation to higher resolutions without altering training recipes, making it directly applicable to dense prediction pipelines (detection, segmentation) where inference resolution exceeds training resolution. The practical trade-off that RoPE+APE improves interpolation but degrades extrapolation gives practitioners a clear knob to tune based on target resolution. Open questions include whether learnable mixed frequencies generalize to 3D video inputs, whether the approach interacts well with other recent position-embedding improvements (e.g., LaPE, iRPE), and whether the gains persist at very large model scales or with alternative attention mechanisms (e.g., linear attention).
