## Problem

Generative modeling has become the dominant paradigm across NLP, vision, and multimodal tasks, yet its application to image retrieval remains unexplored. Practical retrieval systems separate feature representation learning from approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search, requiring per-task hyperparameter tuning and preventing end-to-end optimization toward the final retrieval objective. This paper asks whether image retrieval can be reframed as a generative sequence-to-sequence task, enabling direct differentiable optimization from raw query images to nearest-neighbor identifiers.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB query image as input and autoregressively generates a short sequence of M discrete visual tokens (e.g., M=4) that constitute the identifier of the query's nearest neighbor in the database — not the query's own representation. At inference, beam search with a prefix tree of valid database identifiers constrains generation to produce only existing image IDs, enabling Top-K retrieval. The extremely short sequence length (4 tokens vs. 256 for typical spatial tokenizers) is critical for near-real-time inference, with reported latency of ~0.07s per query at beam size 10.

## Method

IRGen comprises two components: a semantic image tokenizer and an encoder-decoder autoregressive model. The tokenizer extracts the ViT class token (a single global feature vector) rather than spatial patch embeddings, then applies residual quantization with M codebooks to produce M ordered discrete codes; it is trained with classification loss on both original and partially-reconstructed embeddings (rather than pixel-level reconstruction) to inject semantic structure into the codes. The encoder (ViT-base) processes the query image, and a standard Transformer decoder with causal self-attention and cross-attention autoregressively predicts the M-token identifier of the nearest neighbor, trained with softmax cross-entropy. The key insight is that RQ's recursive residual structure naturally aligns with autoregressive token generation, while the global class token reduces sequence length from 256 to 4, making autoregressive inference tractable. At inference, beam search over a prefix tree of valid identifiers performs approximate nearest neighbor search where node scores come from the differentiable model rather than a fixed distance metric.

## Results

IRGen achieves state-of-the-art precision across three standard benchmarks using beam search: 92.4% P@1 and 87.0% P@10 on In-shop, 82.7% P@1 and 82.7% P@2 on CUB200, and 90.1% P@1 and 89.9% P@2 on Cars196. The headline improvements over the strongest baseline (FT-CLIP with linear scan) are a 20.2% gain in P@10 on In-shop (87.0 vs. 66.8), a 6.0% gain in P@2 on CUB200 (82.7 vs. 76.7), and a 2.4% gain in P@2 on Cars196 (89.9 vs. 87.5). On million-scale datasets, IRGen reaches 76.0% MAP@100 on ImageNet and 44.3% MAP@100 on Places365, surpassing FT-CLIP (65.5% and 30.3% respectively). Notably, IRGen's precision remains nearly flat as K increases (e.g., 87.0→86.5 on In-shop from K=10 to K=30), whereas baselines degrade substantially.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IRGen outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric reported. Against the strongest embedding-based baselines, IRGen beats FT-CLIP (linear scan) by 20.2 points in P@10 on In-shop (87.0 vs. 66.8) and by 5.5 points in P@2 on CUB200 (82.7 vs. 77.6); it also surpasses FT-CLIP with SPANN search by 2.4 points in P@2 on Cars196 (89.9 vs. 88.1). CGD and IRTR, the prior SOTA retrieval-specific methods, are outperformed by 10–20 points in precision at higher K values, with IRGen's precision remaining nearly constant while theirs drops sharply. On the supervised deep quantization comparison (Table 4, 32-bit, 100 ImageNet classes), IRGen achieves 93.52% MAP@1000, exceeding DPQ (87.70%), ADSVQ (75.21%), and DTQ (68.12%). On million-scale ImageNet and Places365, IRGen's 76.0% and 44.3% MAP@100 surpass FT-CLIP+SPANN (65.5% and 30.3%) by 10.5 and 14.0 points respectively. The only metric where IRGen is slightly behind is Recall@K for K>1 (e.g., 96.8 vs. 98.1 for CGD* at Recall@10 on In-shop), which the authors attribute to the autoregressive loss optimizing primarily for top-1 generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

IRGen reframes image retrieval as an autoregressive generation task, drawing directly on the DSI paradigm (Tay et al., 2022) of generating document identifiers via a Transformer, but adapts it to the visual domain with a purpose-built semantic tokenizer. The tokenizer borrows the residual quantization mechanism from RQ-VAE (Lee et al., 2022) and the codebook/straight-through training from VQ-VAE (Van Den Oord et al., 2017), but departs fundamentally by using the ViT class token with classification supervision instead of spatial patches with pixel reconstruction. Unlike joint-learning approaches (JPQ, DPQ, DQN) that co-train embeddings with quantization but still rely on post-hoc ANN search, IRGen eliminates the explicit embedding-compression-indexing pipeline entirely, making the entire retrieval process differentiable and optimizable toward the final retrieval objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The end-to-end differentiable formulation unifies feature learning and search into a single optimizable objective, potentially eliminating the need for the reranking stage that is standard in production retrieval systems. The massive storage reduction (4.9 MB vs. 3.67 GB for ImageNet-scale databases) and the ability to generalize to 5% unseen data without retraining are practically significant. However, key open questions remain: scaling to billion-scale databases, the autoregressive inference bottleneck (0.19 s/query at beam size 30), the recall@K trade-off at K>1, and the requirement to fully retrain when the gallery distribution shifts substantially. Whether the generative-retrieval paradigm can be extended to cross-modal or multi-query settings without prohibitive retraining costs is an important direction.
