## Problem

End-to-end multi-task 3D perception from multi-view cameras faces three intertwined challenges: (1) high-resolution inputs, long temporal sequences, and large image encoders are mutually incompatible under fixed compute budgets; (2) naive temporal fusion (sum/concat) degrades with longer sequences because moving objects become misaligned in BEV space; and (3) jointly optimizing 3D detection and BEV segmentation causes task conflicts whose root cause had not been systematically analyzed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241340/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence of 6-camera surround-view images split into two streams: 2 short-term frames at high resolution (640×1152) and 7 long-term frames at low resolution (256×704), processed by separate encoders. It produces 3D object bounding boxes (center, size, orientation, velocity, class) via a CenterPoint head and a BEV semantic segmentation map (vehicles, drivable area, lane/road divider) via a SegNet head, with the two tasks operating on different BEV grid resolutions (0.4 m vs. 0.8 m per cell).

## Method

HENet introduces a hybrid image encoding network that routes short-term high-resolution frames through a large backbone (VoVNet-99) with a complex stereo depth estimator (BEVStereo) and long-term low-resolution frames through a small backbone (ResNet-50) with a simple monocular depth estimator (BEVDepth), both producing multi-scale BEV features via BEVPoolv2. A Temporal Feature Integration module then fuses these heterogeneous BEV features using an Adjacent Frame Fusion Module (AFFM) that applies cross-attention between only neighboring frames in a backward-then-forward sweep, enabling dynamic alignment of moving objects without the noise of global attention. To resolve multi-task conflict, the paper identifies that detection prefers a fine 256×256 BEV grid (local foreground localization) while segmentation prefers a coarse 128×128 grid (global scene understanding), and assigns each task its own grid size, an adaptive channel-attention feature selector, and an independent BEV encoder (three ResNet blocks + FPN) before feeding task-specific decoders.

## Results

On the nuScenes val set, HENet (V2-99 & ResNet50, 2+7 frames) achieves 59.9 NDS and 49.9 mAP for 3D object detection, and 58.0 mIoU for BEV semantic segmentation, setting new state-of-the-art end-to-end multi-task results. On the nuScenes test set, HENet reaches 63.8 NDS and 57.5 mAP for 3D object detection, surpassing all prior methods. Training efficiency is also improved: HENet requires only 71 min/epoch on 8×A800 GPUs, compared to 213 min for BEVFormer and 253 min for UniAD, while processing more temporal frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HENet outperforms BEVFormer [19] by +7.9 NDS, +8.7 mAP, and +8.6 mIoU in the multi-task setting, while also training 3× faster (71 vs. 213 min/epoch). Against PETRv2 [28], HENet gains +10.4 NDS and +8.8 mAP on detection but trails by 0.7 mIoU on segmentation, indicating a slight trade-off. Compared to UniAD [8], HENet improves NDS by +10.0 and mAP by +11.7 while also reporting full segmentation metrics. On the nuScenes test set (Table 3), HENet achieves 63.8 NDS / 57.5 mAP, edging out Sparse4Dv2 [25] (63.8 NDS / 55.6 mAP) and SparseBEV [26] (63.6 NDS / 55.6 mAP) by +1.9 mAP. For single-task BEV segmentation (Table 4), HENet reaches 58.8 mIoU, slightly below PETRv2's 60.3 mIoU but with substantially better detection performance, confirming the multi-task conflict analysis. The ablation (Table 5) further shows that the hybrid encoding network alone (without AFFM) already matches BEVStereo's 59.2 NDS, and adding AFFM pushes it to 59.9 NDS at the same training cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

HENet builds directly on BEVDepth and BEVStereo as its two constituent encoders, repurposing them into a hybrid architecture where a large, high-resolution encoder handles short-term frames and a small, low-resolution encoder handles long-term temporal frames. It departs from prior temporal fusion approaches (BEVDet4D's ego-vehicle alignment, BEVFormer's global cross-attention) by introducing an adjacent-frame attention mechanism (AFFM) with backward-forward propagation, and from prior multi-task frameworks (BEVFormer, PETRv2, UniAD) by assigning task-specific BEV grid sizes and independent BEV encoders rather than sharing a single unified BEV representation. The paper positions itself as addressing two underexplored problems: the computational infeasibility of combining high-resolution, large-encoder, and long-sequence inputs simultaneously, and the unanalyzed task conflict in end-to-end multi-task 3D perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid encoding strategy offers a practical recipe for deploying high-accuracy 3D perception under real compute budgets, achieving SOTA multi-task results on nuScenes at roughly one-third the training time of BEVFormer. The empirical finding that 3D detection and BEV segmentation prefer different BEV grid sizes (0.4m vs 0.8m) provides a concrete, actionable insight for multi-task architecture design that prior works identified as a problem but never diagnosed. Open questions include whether the grid-size preference generalizes beyond nuScenes and to other sensor suites, whether the hybrid approach extends to sparse-query methods as the authors suggest, and whether the small but persistent multi-task performance gap (0.3 mAP, 0.8 mIoU) can be further closed.
