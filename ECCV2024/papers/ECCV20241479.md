## Problem

Most long-term dense action anticipation methods produce a single deterministic prediction, ignoring the inherent uncertainty that grows with longer horizons. The few stochastic approaches that exist (e.g., UAAA, GAN-based encoders) assume observed-frame action labels are already known, thereby discarding ambiguity in the observation itself caused by occlusions, poor lighting, or limited context. This paper tackles the joint modelling of uncertainty in both the observed and the future parts of a video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241479/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a partially observed video (α fraction of total frames, α ∈ {0.2, 0.3}) whose per-frame features are extracted by a frozen I3D or TSM backbone and then zero-padded to span the full sequence length N = N_o + N_a. At each diffusion step, these padded features are channel-wise concatenated to the noisy action state, providing per-frame conditioning. The output is M = 25 distinct full-sequence action label predictions (one-hot over C classes), from which both multiple interpretations of the observed part and multiple future action sequences (β fraction of frames, β up to 0.5) are read off.

## Method

GTD formulates stochastic anticipation as a single continuous-state diffusion process over a unified sequence Y^{1:N} that concatenates observed and future action variables, so that one denoising trajectory simultaneously resolves ambiguity in both parts. The generator is a Gated Anticipation Network (GTAN) with S stages of dilated gated temporal convolution blocks: each block runs a feature convolution and a gate convolution in parallel, multiplies their outputs element-wise through a sigmoid, and uses dilation 2^l to expand the receptive field. This gating lets the network learn data-driven, frame-specific information flow between past and future entries rather than treating all positions uniformly. Discrete action classes are handled via the 'analog bits' trick (one-hot vectors modelled by continuous diffusion with argmax at inference), and self-conditioning from the previous denoising step stabilises generation. Training minimises an l2 reconstruction loss accumulated over all stages; inference uses DDIM sampling with 50 steps and M different initial noise samples to produce M diverse predictions.

## Results

On the Breakfast dataset (α=0.2, β=0.1), GTD achieves 24.0 Mean MoC and 51.2 Top-1 MoC in stochastic anticipation, outperforming UAAA [23] (15.7 / 28.9) by +8.3 and +22.3 points respectively. On Assembly101 (α=0.2, β=0.1), GTD reaches 6.4 Mean MoC and 18.0 Top-1 MoC versus UAAA* (2.7 / 6.9). In the deterministic setting, GTAN achieves 28.8 MoC on Breakfast (α=0.2, β=0.1) and 9.0 MoC on Assembly101, surpassing the previous best FUTR [26] (27.7 and 7.5 respectively). Gains are most pronounced at longer horizons (β=0.5), where GTD leads UAAA by +7.6 Mean MoC on Breakfast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GTD substantially outperforms UAAA [23] (the only prior stochastic method with a comparable protocol) across all three datasets: on Breakfast, Mean MoC improves from 15.7 to 24.0 (+8.3) and Top-1 MoC from 28.9 to 51.2 (+22.3) at α=0.2, β=0.1; on Assembly101, Mean MoC nearly triples from 2.7 to 6.4. The tri-gram baseline [23] is consistently weaker than UAAA and is dominated by GTD by even larger margins. In the deterministic setting, GTAN surpasses FUTR [26] (the previous SOTA) on Breakfast by +1.1 MoC at β=0.1 and +3.9 at β=0.5 (α=0.2), and on Assembly101 by +1.5 MoC at β=0.1. GTAN also beats earlier deterministic methods including Cycle Consistency [1] (+2.9 MoC), TempAgg [52] (+4.6 MoC), Time-Conditioned [32] (+10.4 MoC), and the original RNN/CNN [2] (+10.7 MoC) on Breakfast at α=0.2, β=0.1. In architecture ablations, the proposed gated convolution outperforms the gated TCN of Aslan et al. [4] by +5.2 MoC, partial convolution [39] by +0.8 MoC, and squeeze-and-excitation [31] by +1.6 MoC on Breakfast, confirming that data-driven temporal gating is critical for dense anticipation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stochastic long-term dense action anticipation formulation introduced by Farha et al. (UAAA) and Zhao et al. (GAN-based encoder-decoder), both of which assume observed action labels are pre-estimated or ground-truth and thus ignore ambiguity in the observation itself. The key methodological departure is casting the problem as a diffusion process over a joint sequence of observed and future action variables, using the 'analog bits' discretization trick from Chen et al. to handle categorical labels in a continuous diffusion framework. The GTAN generator, built on dilated gated temporal convolutions, is positioned as a novel backbone that adaptively controls information flow between past and future frames, departing from both standard TCNs and the gated temporal convolutional networks of Aslan et al., which the ablations show to be unsuitable for dense anticipation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly modelling uncertainty in both the observation and the future within a single diffusion process, this work addresses a gap that prior stochastic anticipation methods left open: ambiguous observations (poor lighting, occlusions, limited context) propagate into future predictions, and ignoring this leads to overconfident and less diverse outputs. The practical limitation is inference speed (~3.8 s per prediction on Breakfast), which restricts deployment to mid-term planning rather than real-time applications; the authors point to distillation and DeepCache as paths forward. An open question is whether the joint observation-future formulation generalises to longer horizons or more complex multi-agent settings, and whether the gated architecture's advantage over simpler conditioning persists at scale.
