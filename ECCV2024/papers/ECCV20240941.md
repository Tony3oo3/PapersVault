## Problem

Existing NeRF-based rolling shutter rectification methods (e.g., USB-NeRF) require strictly sequential video frame input, limiting their applicability to unordered image collections. Additionally, rolling shutter distortions and inaccurate COLMAP-initialized poses degrade implicit 3D reconstruction quality, and the added velocity parameters in RS-aware bundle adjustment make optimization prone to local minima without additional constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240941/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of unordered rolling shutter RGB images (no temporal ordering required), known camera intrinsics, and scanline readout time as input, with initial SE(3) poses from COLMAP perturbed by Gaussian noise (σ_trans=0.10 m, σ_rot=1.15°). It outputs a Tri-MipRF implicit scene representation, per-frame camera poses with linear and angular velocity vectors, global shutter images rendered from novel views, and synthetic rolling shutter images at arbitrary motion speeds (1×, 3×, 5×) for dataset generation.

## Method

URS-NeRF extends Tri-MipRF with a rolling shutter camera model that parameterizes per-row camera pose via frame-dependent rotation, translation, linear velocity, and angular velocity (Eq. 6), eliminating the need for sequential frame ordering. A coarse-to-fine training strategy is employed: the coarse stage uses downsampled images to accelerate convergence and establish rough pose estimates, while the fine stage refines at full resolution with reinitialized learning rates. To prevent local minima, a scene graph is built from SuperPoint/SuperGlue keypoint matches, and rolling shutter epipolar geometry constraints (Eq. 9) are evaluated on pairwise nearby views to flag outlier poses, which are then corrected by interpolation with neighboring poses. The entire pipeline is differentiable, jointly optimizing the MLP network, Tri-Mip mipmap features, camera poses, and velocity parameters via photometric L2 loss between rendered and captured RS images.

## Results

On the WHU-RS synthetic dataset with unordered rolling shutter images, URS-NeRF achieves the best rolling shutter removal quality across all motion speeds: PSNR 27.55 / SSIM 0.85 / LPIPS 0.10 (fast), 27.90 / 0.88 / 0.08 (medium), and 29.42 / 0.89 / 0.08 (slow). For camera pose estimation, URS-NeRF attains the lowest translation error (0.007–0.014 m) and rotation error (0.381–2.871°) on both WHU-RS and ZJU-RS datasets, outperforming the next-best method DiffSfM by roughly 30–50% in translation. On sequential video input, URS-NeRF matches USB-NeRF-RE within ~1 dB PSNR, confirming that the unordered formulation does not sacrifice quality when order is available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URS-NeRF outperforms all five baselines on unordered rolling shutter images across every motion speed. Against the closest competitor DiffSfM (which shares the same RS camera model but only uses two frames), URS-NeRF gains ~2.7 dB PSNR in fast mode and ~1.7 dB in slow mode by leveraging multi-view photometric consistency. USB-NeRF-RE, which relies on a cubic B-Spline trajectory requiring strictly sequential input, degrades catastrophically on unordered data (PSNR drops to 16.91 in fast mode vs. 29.07 in sequential mode), while URS-NeRF remains stable. BARF and Tri-MipRF-BA, which do not model the RS effect, produce substantially worse reconstructions (PSNR 16–28) and larger pose errors (up to 0.15 m translation, 8.4° rotation). On sequential input, URS-NeRF is within 1.4 dB PSNR of USB-NeRF-RE (27.70 vs. 29.07 in fast mode), confirming that the unordered formulation incurs only a marginal cost when frame order is available. For pose estimation on the real ZJU-RS dataset, URS-NeRF achieves 0.007 m / 1.732° (sequential) and 0.007 m / 2.871° (unordered), beating DiffSfM (0.013 m / 1.237°) in translation while USB-NeRF-RE fails entirely (0.124 m / 15.912°).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the BARF-style joint pose-and-NeRF optimization framework by incorporating a rolling shutter camera model with per-frame linear and angular velocity parameters, building on Tri-MipRF as the efficient anti-aliased backbone. It departs from USB-NeRF, which parameterizes camera motion via cubic B-splines over sequential video frames, by replacing the spline with a constant-velocity model that requires no temporal ordering, thereby enabling unordered image input. The coarse-to-fine training strategy with rolling-shutter epipolar geometry for outlier detection is a novel addition not present in prior NeRF+BA or RS+BA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since rolling shutter sensors dominate consumer imaging (smartphones, action cameras), removing the sequential-input constraint makes implicit 3D reconstruction from RS footage far more practical and generalizable. The ability to synthesize GS images and generate synthetic RS datasets with controllable motion speeds also benefits the broader RS research community (SLAM, SfM). Limitations include the constant-velocity assumption during exposure (which degrades at high speeds), the requirement for known intrinsics and scanline readout time, and evaluation on relatively small indoor scenes; scaling to large-scale or outdoor RS sequences remains open.
