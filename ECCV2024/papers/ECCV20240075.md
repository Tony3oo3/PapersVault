## Problem

Existing LMMs for image quality assessment are limited to single-image scoring or pairwise comparison, and cannot handle open-ended quality comparison across multiple images (3–4) with detailed reasoning. No instruction-tuning dataset or benchmark exists for this multi-image comparative setting, and open-source LMMs trained on single-image data perform poorly even on two-image comparison tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240075/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 2–4 RGB images (padded to square, resized to 448×448) interleaved with explicit text identifiers (e.g., 'The first image:', 'The second image:') followed by a free-form text query about visual quality. Each image is encoded by CLIP-ViT-L14 and compressed from 1,025 to 65 visual tokens via an abstractor module before concatenation with text embeddings, keeping multi-image inputs within the LLaMA-2 context window. Outputs are free-form text responses that can be direct comparison statements, detailed multi-paragraph reasonings, or multi-choice answers depending on the query format.

## Method

Co-Instruct is built on mPLUG-Owl2 (LLaMA-2 + CLIP-ViT-L14) and fine-tuned on the newly constructed Co-Instruct-562K dataset, which combines three sources: Q-Instruct-200K (single-image), Merge2Compare (~100K groups where a single-modal LLM merges human-written single-image quality descriptions into comparative texts after top-similarity pair removal via E5-Mistral embeddings), and Teach2Compare (~30K groups where GPT-4V generates pseudo-labeled comparisons and Q&A pairs on unlabeled images). Two key architectural adaptations distinguish it from standard LLaVA-style models: (1) a visual abstractor that reduces per-image tokens from 1,025 to 65 to accommodate multiple images within the context window, and (2) an image-text interleaved input format where each image is preceded by an ordinal text label, which ablations show outperforms both simple image-piling and learnable separator tokens. The collaborative training across hetero-sourced subsets (human-merged vs. GPT-4V-labeled) is what enables the student to surpass its GPT-4V teacher on MCQ benchmarks.

## Results

On Q-Bench PAIR-A1 (1,999 pairwise MCQs), Co-Instruct achieves 80.18% overall accuracy, surpassing its GPT-4V teacher (78.07%) by 2.7% and non-expert human (80.12%), while outperforming the best open-source LMM (InternLM-XComposer2-VL, 65.16%) by 23%. On the proposed MICBench (996 multi-image MCQs with 3–4 images), Co-Instruct reaches 83.33%, exceeding GPT-4V (78.82%) by 5.7% and human (78.35%) by 6.4%. On 2AFC-LMM, Co-Instruct attains a weighted-average Pearson correlation of ρ=0.754 and swap-consistency κ=0.864, both surpassing GPT-4V (ρ=0.721, κ=0.823). On Q-Bench SINGLE-A1, comparative training also boosts single-image accuracy to 77.12%, a 5% gain over the single-image-only variant and 4% above GPT-4V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Co-Instruct decisively outperforms all five open-source LMMs: it gains 64% over its direct baseline mPLUG-Owl2 (80.18% vs. 48.94% on Q-Bench PAIR-A1), 23% over the strongest open-source model InternLM-XComposer2-VL (65.16%), and 28–33% over LLaVA-v1.5-13B (52.05%), BakLLaVA (52.75%), and EMU2-Chat (47.08%). Against proprietary models, Co-Instruct surpasses Gemini-Pro by 20% (80.18% vs. 60.46%) and Qwen-VL-Max by 13% (80.18% vs. 66.99%) on Q-Bench PAIR-A1. Most notably, Co-Instruct exceeds its own GPT-4V teacher on every benchmark: +2.7% on Q-Bench PAIR-A1, +5.7% on MICBench (83.33% vs. 78.82%), and +0.033 on 2AFC-LMM weighted ρ (0.754 vs. 0.721), while matching GPT-4V on Q-Bench PAIR-A2 detailed-reasoning scores (4.82 vs. 4.89). The ablation (Table 6) confirms that the superiority over GPT-4V arises specifically from co-instruction on heterogeneous data subsets (Merge2Compare + Teach2Compare-Q&A), not from GPT-4V labels alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Q-Bench/Q-Instruct line of work on LMM-based image quality assessment from single-image and pairwise settings to open-ended multi-image (3–4) comparison with detailed reasoning. Architecturally, it builds on mPLUG-Owl2's visual abstractor to reduce per-image token count from 1,025 to 65, making multi-image inputs feasible within standard LLM context windows. The key methodological departure is the Co-Instruct data strategy: rather than relying on expensive human comparative annotations, it synthesizes training data from two 'weak supervisors'—an LLM that merges human single-image descriptions (Merge2Compare) and GPT-4V pseudo-labels on unlabeled image groups (Teach2Compare)—a collaborative distillation approach not previously applied to visual quality comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source LMM to match or exceed GPT-4V on visual quality comparison, and the first to surpass non-expert human accuracy on related benchmarks, making high-quality comparative assessment accessible without proprietary API access. The Co-Instruct-562K dataset and MICBench fill clear gaps: no prior instruction-tuning data or benchmark existed for open-ended comparison beyond two images. A notable open question is whether the 'weak supervisor' co-instruction strategy generalizes to other perceptual comparison domains (video, audio), and whether the approach scales beyond four images where combinatorial complexity grows. The finding that comparative training also boosts single-image quality perception suggests a broader principle about cross-task transfer in LMMs that warrants further investigation.
