## Problem

Existing LiDAR-guided cross-modal knowledge distillation methods for camera-based 3D object detection transfer LiDAR features that carry inherent imperfections—aleatoric uncertainty from sparsity on distant or occluded objects—directly to the image detector. Additionally, indiscriminate alignment of all image feature channels to LiDAR features suppresses the complementary semantic information that cameras uniquely provide, limiting the student's full potential.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241482/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes only multi-view camera images (256×704 for ResNet50, 512×1408 for ResNet101, 900×1600 for ConvNeXt-B) along with up to 4 previous frames for temporal context, producing 3D bounding boxes with class labels, orientation, and velocity. During training, LiDAR point clouds and ground truth 3D annotations are additionally required to train the teacher (CenterPoint) and the label encoder, but neither is needed at test time, so no extra sensor or computational cost is incurred at inference.

## Method

LabelDistill introduces a label encoder that approximates the inverse of the pretrained LiDAR detection head (CenterPoint) via an autoencoder: the encoder maps ground-truth 3D bounding boxes into BEV feature space, and the frozen LiDAR head acts as the decoder, so the encoder is trained to produce features that, when decoded, reconstruct the original labels (achieving 94% mAP). These aleatoric-uncertainty-free label features are then distilled into the image detector alongside conventional LiDAR feature- and response-level distillation. A feature partitioning strategy splits the image BEV channels into three groups—LiDAR-distilled, label-distilled, and undistilled—so that the student learns spatial information from both teachers while preserving its own semantic channels. Training proceeds in two stages: first the label encoder is trained for 12 epochs, then the image detector (BEVDepth) is trained for 24 epochs with a combined loss of detection, LiDAR feature MSE, label feature MSE, and LiDAR response losses.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with ResNet50 (256×704), LabelDistill achieves 41.9 mAP and 52.8 NDS, improving over the BEVDepth baseline (33.3 mAP, 44.1 NDS) by 8.6 and 8.7 points respectively. With ResNet101 (512×1408), it reaches 45.1 mAP and 55.3 NDS, and on the nuScenes test set with ConvNeXt-B (900×1600), it achieves 52.6 mAP and 61.0 NDS. The method also outperforms all prior LiDAR-guided cross-modal distillation approaches, with the largest gain over DistillBEV (40.3 mAP) being 1.6 mAP points in the ResNet50 setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LabelDistill (ResNet50) outperforms all camera-based 3D detectors at the same resolution, beating the closest competitor SOLOFusion (40.6 mAP, 49.7 NDS) by 1.3 mAP and 3.1 NDS, and P2D (37.4 mAP) by 4.5 mAP. In the ResNet101 setting, it surpasses Sparse4D (43.6 mAP, 54.1 NDS) by 1.5 mAP and 1.2 NDS, and P2D (43.3 mAP) by 1.8 mAP. Among LiDAR-guided cross-modal distillation methods (Table 3), LabelDistill achieves the largest improvement over its BEVDepth baseline: +5.1 mAP and +4.5 NDS (ResNet50), compared to +3.9/+2.6 for DistillBEV, +3.1/+3.3 for X3KD, and +3.7/+3.0 for TiG-BEV. On the nuScenes test set with ConvNeXt-B, LabelDistill improves over the reproduced BEVDepth baseline by 5.1 mAP and 4.9 NDS. Notably, LabelDistill achieves these gains without using CBGS, a data augmentation strategy that several competing methods (marked with †) rely on to extend effective training epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LiDAR-guided cross-modal knowledge distillation for camera-based 3D object detection (BEVDistill, X3KD, DistillBEV) by introducing two key departures: (1) a label distillation strategy that approximates the inverse of the pretrained LiDAR detection head to embed ground-truth 3D boxes into the teacher's feature space, and (2) a feature partitioning scheme that splits student channels into LiDAR-guided, label-guided, and teacher-free groups. Unlike prior label-guidance methods (LabelEnc, LGD) that train label encoders against student features, this work uses the frozen teacher head as a decoder, ensuring label features live in the teacher's representational space. The student architecture (BEVDepth) and teacher (CenterPoint) are standard, so the contribution is entirely in the distillation objective and training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-addressed failure mode of cross-modal distillation: LiDAR features carry aleatoric uncertainty (sparsity at range, occlusion, weather sensitivity) that is blindly transferred to the image student, degrading performance on distant or occluded objects. The label distillation mechanism and feature partitioning are simple, add no inference cost, and yield a 5.1 mAP / 4.9 NDS gain over BEVDepth on nuScenes, which is meaningful for deployment where LiDAR is unavailable. However, the method still lags behind LiDAR detectors, depends on high-quality ground-truth annotations, and has been validated only on nuScenes with a single student architecture family, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other datasets, sensor suites, or student backbones.
