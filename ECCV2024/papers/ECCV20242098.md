## Problem

Despite the clinical adoption of undersampled MRI via compressive sensing and deep learning, no fully rigorous and scalable uncertainty quantification method exists for the total variation (TV) minimization reconstruction that underpins current industrial implementations. Classical confidence-interval approaches fail because the nonlinearity of the reconstruction prevents closed-form characterization of the error distribution, and prior rigorous UQ results were limited to ℓ₁/sparse models rather than the gradient-sparse TV setting used in practice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes complex undersampled k-space data (156×156 images), a fixed sampling mask (radial or spiral), an estimated noise level σ̂, and a regularization parameter μ. It outputs the TV reconstruction, a debiased TV estimator, and per-pixel confidence circles in the complex plane from which magnitude and phase confidence intervals are derived. A key practical detail is that the correction matrix M depends only on the sampling pattern and is computed once via nodewise LASSO, independent of the specific image or noise realization, so the debiasing step reduces to a single matrix-vector product after the convex solve.

## Method

The core contribution is a debiased TV estimator x̂ᵘ = x̂ + (1/m)M(PF)†(b − PF x̂), where x̂ solves the anisotropic TV-minimization problem and M is a correction matrix constructed by a nodewise LASSO adapted to complex subsampled Fourier matrices (Algorithm 1). The error x̂ᵘ − x⁰ decomposes into a remainder R = √m(MΣ̂ − I)(x̂ − x⁰) that vanishes under standard sampling conditions and a Gaussian term W = (1/√m)M(PF)†ε with covariance σ²MΣ̂M*. Because W is complex Gaussian, the per-pixel error magnitude follows a Rayleigh distribution, yielding a closed-form confidence radius δᵢ(α) = σ√(log(1/α))/(√m·√((MΣ̂M*)ᵢᵢ)) and hence pixel-wise confidence intervals for both magnitude and phase. The approach requires no Monte Carlo sampling, no prior distribution on the image, and no additional data acquisition beyond the initial undersampled measurement, making it directly applicable as a post-processing step to any TV-based MRI reconstruction pipeline.

## Results

The proposed debiased TV estimator achieves empirical hit rates of 90.64% (support pixels) and 93.82% (all pixels) for 43% spiral undersampling, and 91.95% and 94.20% for 50% radial undersampling, against a target 95% confidence level (α=0.05). These results hold across relative noise levels of 6.5%–12.5% and are validated on in vivo brain MRI (156×156, Siemens 3T). Q-Q plots confirm the debiased estimator's error follows the predicted complex Gaussian distribution, and the remainder term ‖R‖∞ is numerically small relative to the Gaussian term ‖W‖∞, validating the asymptotic normality assumption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing UQ methods; the authors explicitly state that, to their knowledge, no prior work provides honest (distribution-free) confidence intervals for gradient-sparse MR images reconstructed via total variation minimization. The closest prior results are the ℓ₁-based UQ methods of Hoppe et al. [28,29], which the present work extends to the TV setting but does not run side-by-side. Validation is instead performed against the theoretical guarantee: the 95% confidence regions should contain the true pixel value in at least 95% of repeated noise realizations, and the observed empirical probabilities (90.6–94.2%) fall slightly below this nominal level, consistent with the finite-sample remainder term not being exactly zero. The paper also notes that confidence intervals of comparable width are known to be minimax-sharp for related high-dimensional linear problems [13], providing an indirect benchmark on interval tightness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the debiased-estimator framework from high-dimensional statistics (Javanmard & Montanari, Zhang & Zhang, van de Geer et al.) and its prior application to ℓ₁-regularized sparse MRI (Hoppe et al. 2022, 2023) to the total variation minimization setting, which is the standard variational formulation in industrial MRI reconstruction. The nodewise LASSO algorithm for constructing the correction matrix M is adapted from the Gaussian-design debiasing literature to complex subsampled Fourier matrices. The key conceptual departure is that TV minimization enforces gradient sparsity rather than pixel sparsity, making the prior ℓ₁-UQ results inapplicable without the new theoretical bridge provided here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous, non-asymptotic confidence interval construction for TV-based undersampled MRI, the reconstruction method actually deployed in clinical scanners (e.g., GE HyperSense). The method requires no Monte Carlo sampling, no prior distribution, and only a single correction-term addition after the standard reconstruction, making it computationally lightweight. Key limitations include dependence on accurate noise estimation (breaks down above ~20% noise), the computational cost of nodewise LASSO for complex sampling trajectories (e.g., Lissajous), and validation only on 2D brain data. The authors explicitly position this as a stepping stone toward UQ for learned reconstruction methods (variational networks, vision transformers), where the black-box nature makes certified uncertainty especially critical for clinical adoption.
