## Problem

Video-text pairs in existing datasets are imperfectly aligned (descriptions omit fine-grained visual details) and exhibit skewed concept distributions (e.g., MSRVTT over-represents video game and singing topics). Aggressively minimizing standard contrastive loss on such data distorts cross-modal representations and biases the model toward majority topics, degrading performance on minority subjects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242132/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes video clips (randomly sampled frames embedded into visual token sequences) paired with textual descriptions, and optionally answer labels for VideoQA. For the dual architecture (CLIP-ViP, VGT), visual and textual tokens are pooled into global embeddings whose cosine similarity serves as the cross-modal score; for the bidirectional architecture (VIOLET), visual and textual tokens are concatenated into a single sequence processed by Transformer attention layers, with a [CLS] token fed to an FC layer producing either a similarity score or an answer log-likelihood. The LVLM augmentation pipeline takes 12 key frames (extracted via density-peak clustering) concatenated into a 3×4 grid image and prompts LLaVA to generate a sentence-level caption.

## Method

The paper introduces a subtractive angular margin contrastive loss that replaces the positive-pair angle λ_{i,i} with [λ_{i,i} − μ]_+ in the numerator of the InfoNCE objective, provably decaying the gradient norm (Theorem 1) so that imperfectly aligned positive pairs are regularized away from perfect similarity; the margin μ is adaptively scheduled via a sigmoid-like function μ^{(k)} = a₀/(a₁ + e^{−a₂k}). To handle concept imbalance, a small MLP maps each sample's training loss to a weight, and this MLP is trained via a meta-learning loop (Algorithm 1) on a small unbiased validation set: the video-language model is first updated with current weights, the MLP is then updated to minimize meta-data loss under the estimated model, and finally the video-language model is re-updated with the new weights. This meta-optimized weighting lets the model dynamically balance emphasis on minority classes (high-loss samples) versus cleaner, better-aligned samples (low-loss samples) without manually specifying a fixed weighting schedule. An LVLM-based augmentation strategy further enriches training data by extracting key frames via density-peak clustering and prompting LLaVA to generate captions, improving diversity and alignment of the video-text corpus.

## Results

On open-ended VideoQA, the method (VIOLET backbone) achieves 46.4% on MSRVTT-QA, 55.8% on MSVD-QA, and 71.7% on TGIF-Frame, outperforming the strongest LVLM-augmented baselines (LaViLa: 44.9/53.7/70.7) by 1.5–3.7 points. On multi-choice VideoQA, the VGT backbone reaches 66.3% overall on NExT-QA and 68.2% on Causal-VidQA (counterfactual), surpassing LaViLa by 1.5 and 2.5 points respectively. For text→video retrieval (CLIP-ViP backbone), the method attains R@1 of 60.0 on MSRVTT, 62.7 on DiDeMo, and 60.0 on ActivityNet, improving over the unmodified CLIP-ViP by 4.3, 7.0, and 0.9 R@1 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated dataset and task. On open-ended VideoQA, it beats the strongest LVLM-augmented baselines LaViLa and Vid2Seq by 1.5% (MSRVTT), 2.1% (MSVD), and 3.7% (TGIF-Frame), and surpasses the best non-LVLM model VindLU by 1.8% on MSRVTT. On multi-choice VideoQA (NExT-QA, Causal-VidQA), the VGT backbone achieves 66.3% overall, exceeding LaViLa (64.8%) and the unmodified VGT (61.8%) by 1.5 and 4.5 points. For text→video retrieval, the CLIP-ViP backbone gains 4.3 R@1 on MSRVTT (60.0 vs. 55.7) and 7.0 R@1 on DiDeMo (62.7 vs. 55.7) over the unmodified CLIP-ViP, and outperforms LaViLa by 4.0 R@1 on MSRVTT. The VGT backbone shows smaller absolute gains than VIOLET/CLIP-ViP, suggesting the framework's benefits are most pronounced with architectures that already have strong cross-modal alignment capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the angular margin concept from deep metric learning (e.g., ArcFace) in a fundamentally different direction: rather than increasing discriminative separation between different classes, the subtractive margin regularizes positive video-text pairs from reaching perfect cosine similarity, addressing the well-known imperfect alignment between video content and its textual description. For sample reweighting, the paper departs from hand-designed schemes (focal loss, self-paced learning, L2RW) by parameterizing the weighting function as an MLP trained via meta-learning on a small unbiased validation set, drawing on the Meta-Weight-Net lineage but applying it to the joint problem of label noise and concept imbalance simultaneously. The LVLM augmentation strategy builds on LaViLa and Vid2Seq but introduces a density-peak key-frame extraction and grid-image prompting pipeline that is more lightweight than fine-tuning an LLM on visual frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical data-quality issues (imperfect cross-modal alignment and non-uniform concept distribution) that are widely acknowledged but rarely tackled jointly in video-language pretraining. The architecture-agnostic framework (validated on dual and bidirectional backbones) and the meta-learned weighting function offer a reusable recipe for practitioners working with noisy, imbalanced video-text corpora. However, the approach requires manually curating a small unbiased meta-data set, and the angular margin's benefit is most pronounced in the mid-training regime, leaving open questions about its interaction with very large-scale pretraining or with more sophisticated alignment-aware objectives. The reliance on an off-the-shelf LVLM for augmentation also introduces a dependency on the quality and instruction-following ability of that model.
