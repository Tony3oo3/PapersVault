## Problem

Existing diffusion models are trained exclusively on rectangular canvases and fail to generate coherent, shape-faithful visual content within irregularly shaped font outlines. Simply adapting ControlNet or inpainting models to font masks produces artifacts, misplaced content outside the shape, and inconsistent styles across letters. The paper addresses both the single-letter generation gap and the multi-letter consistency challenge for multilingual font effect creation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241552/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt, a binary font shape mask (rasterized into a rectangular placeholder), and optionally a reference letter image for style transfer. It produces a font effect image with an irregular spatial extent plus a refined alpha mask that delineates the generated content boundary, eliminating the need for a separate segmentation model. The irregular canvas is handled by rasterizing it into a fixed-resolution rectangle, with the mask conditioning the generation to the interior region.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage shape-adaptive diffusion model: a Shape-adaptive Generation Model (SGM) trained ControlNet-style (frozen UNet, fine-tuned ControlNet branches) on 240K DALL-E3/SAM/BLIP triplets, and a Shape-adaptive Refinement Model (SRM) that regenerates edges via partial-noise SDEdit and predicts a refined alpha mask through a fine-tuned VAE decoder with an extra mask channel. A key architectural innovation is shape-adaptive attention, which partitions key/value embeddings into foreground (inside the mask) and background (outside) groups and routes queries accordingly, preventing content leakage outside the font shape. For multi-letter consistency, a training-free Shape-adaptive Effect Transfer (SAET) scheme initializes target latents with partially noised reference latents (font effect noise prior) and concatenates the reference latent at each denoising step so that self-attention propagates style information from source to target.

## Results

On the GENERATIVEFONT benchmark (145 multilingual test cases), FONTSTUDIO achieves a CLIP score of 29.44 and CLIP-I score of 84.63, outperforming Adobe Firefly (28.48 and 81.74 respectively). In a user study with 25 evaluators, FONTSTUDIO achieves a 78% win rate in aesthetics and 66% in shape fidelity compared to Firefly's 10% and 6%. GPT-4V evaluation corroborates these findings with 65% win rate in aesthetics, 76% in shape fidelity, and 74% in style consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conventional rectangle-canvas diffusion models (SDXL-ControlNet-Canny and SDXL-ControlNet-Depth), FONTSTUDIO's shape-adaptive generation model achieves the highest M-CLIP-Int score (27.26 vs. 26.03 and 24.11), confirming superior content generation within the font shape, though it scores lower on M-CLIP-Ext (18.11 vs. 21.52 and 23.24), indicating slightly more content leakage outside the canvas. For style consistency across letters, FONTSTUDIO's shape-adaptive effect transfer (SAET) outperforms StyleAligned by a substantial margin: CLIP-I 84.63 vs. 82.77 and DINO 67.07 vs. 60.79, demonstrating that the font effect noise prior and concatenated latent propagation are more effective than shared-attention-based style transfer. Against the commercial Adobe Firefly system, FONTSTUDIO wins on both prompt fidelity (CLIP) and inter-character style consistency (CLIP-I), and the user study reveals a dramatic preference gap (78% vs. 10% aesthetics; 66% vs. 6% shape fidelity), with Firefly's outputs suffering from fragmented strokes and mismatched patterns that reduce character clarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the ControlNet architecture (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023) for conditional diffusion, repurposing the ControlNet-depth-sdxl-1.0 pipeline by substituting depth-map conditioning with rasterized font shape masks and freezing the UNet during fine-tuning. It departs from the broader font-generation literature (GAN-based style transfer, stroke-based techniques, statistical text effects) by reframing the problem as shape-adaptive text-to-image generation on irregular canvases rather than style transfer onto pre-rendered glyphs. The shape-adaptive attention mechanism—a foreground/background partitioning of cross-attention keys and values—is a novel architectural modification not present in prior conditional diffusion work, while the training-free effect transfer draws conceptually from SDEdit's noise-prior initialization and StyleAligned's shared-attention paradigm but operates in a concatenated latent space without any additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the GENERATIVEFONT benchmark (145 multilingual test cases) and a practical two-stage pipeline (SGM + SRM) that addresses a gap between rectangular-canvas diffusion models and the irregular geometry of font shapes, with the shape-adaptive attention mechanism potentially generalizing to other non-rectangular generation tasks. The training-free effect transfer is immediately applicable to any diffusion-based pipeline, and the 78% aesthetic win-rate over Adobe Firefly suggests the approach is competitive with commercial systems. Open questions include scalability to very complex or highly connected shapes (e.g., cursive scripts), the sensitivity of the two-stage pipeline to hyperparameters (noise strength, reference letter choice), and whether the shape-adaptive attention generalizes beyond the 240K-instance training set to truly arbitrary geometric constraints.
