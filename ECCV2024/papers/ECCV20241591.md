## Problem

Self-supervised 6DoF pose estimation methods that bridge the simulation-to-real domain gap typically rely on iterative template/viewpoint matching or 2D mask/3D point cloud alignment, which are computationally expensive and limited in expressiveness. This paper formulates the sim2real gap as a measurable distance in a high-dimensional feature space and applies a learnable kernel in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) to close it, which is the first use of RKHS-based domain adaptation for 6DoF pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241591/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a concatenated RGB image and depth map (W×H×4), where depth is locally normalized to [0,1] and RGB is ImageNet-standardized. It outputs n projected 2D keypoints with per-keypoint classification labels and confidence scores, which are ranked and grouped before being fed into ePnP for the final 6DoF pose; an optional ICP refinement step uses the depth map to polish the estimate. During training, real images without GT labels are converted into 'syn/real' composite images by overlaying CAD models rendered at pseudo-poses estimated from pseudo-keypoints, providing the supervision signal for the Adapter network.

## Method

RKHSPose comprises a main network M_rv (a modified FCN-ResNet-18 regression branch M_r producing radial distance maps, plus a CNN voting branch M_v that accumulates votes and extracts peaks) and a novel Adapter network M_A. M_A encodes pairs of intermediate feature maps (from syn/real and real inputs) into high-dimensional RKHS representations via convolutional blocks followed by a trainable linear product kernel K_w, then minimizes the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) between the two distributions as the domain-gap loss. Training proceeds in two phases: 120 epochs on purely synthetic data with automatic GT poses (M_A frozen), followed by 80 alternating epochs on syn/real composites and unlabeled real images where both M_rv and M_A are updated. The key insight is that dense feature-map comparison in RKHS with a learnable kernel captures the sim2real discrepancy more effectively than sparse output-level matching, and the pseudo-keypoint → pseudo-pose → syn/real rendering loop provides self-supervision without any real GT annotations.

## Results

On the self-supervised setting (Syn+Self), RKHSPose achieves 95.8% ADD(S) on LINEMOD, 68.6% AUC on Occlusion LINEMOD, and 82.8% ADD-S AUC on YCB-Video, outperforming the previous best self-supervised method TexPose by +4.2%, +2%, and +2.8% respectively. Against fully supervised baselines on the six BOP core datasets, RKHSPose (without real GT labels) achieves within −11.3% to +0.2% of the top fully supervised results, with notable wins on HB (+0.1% over ZebraPose) and TUDL (+0.6% over ZebraPose). The method runs at 34 fps on an RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised methods, RKHSPose clearly outperforms all competitors: it beats TexPose [9] by +4.2% ADD(S) on LM and +2% AUC on LMO, SMOC-Net [75] by +4.5% on LM, and Self6D++ [79] by +7.3% on LM and +2.8% on YCB ADD-S AUC. The gap over older self-supervised methods (Self6D [80], Sock et al. [68], DSC [86]) is even larger, exceeding 35% on LM. Against fully supervised baselines in Table 3, RKHSPose (using no real GT labels) wins on HB (92.2 vs. ZebraPose 92.1), TUDL (96.2 vs. ZebraPose 95.6), and TLESS (85.5 vs. RADet+PFA 85.0), while trailing on LMO (68.2 vs. ZebraPose 86.2) and ITODD (68.6 vs. ZebraPose 92.1). On YCB, RKHSPose (83.6) falls short of ZebraPose (89.9) and RADet+PFA (88.8) but surpasses SurfEmb [30] (79.9). Overall, the paper demonstrates that a self-supervised keypoint method with RKHS-based domain adaptation can approach or match fully supervised performance on most BOP core datasets without any real pose annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the radial keypoint voting framework of Wu et al. (ECCV 2022) by replacing the CPU-based parallel accumulator with a CNN-based voting network, and introduces a novel RKHS-based adapter network for sim2real domain adaptation in 6DoF PE. It departs from the dominant self-supervised paradigm of iterative template/viewpoint matching (Self6D, Self6D++, TexPose, SMOC-Net) by formulating the domain gap as a statistical distance in a high-dimensional kernel space measured via MMD, rather than through iterative 2D mask or 3D point cloud alignment. The learnable inner-product kernel in the adapter is the first application of RKHS to 6DoF pose estimation, drawing on kernel methods previously applied to classification and image registration domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RKHSPose demonstrates that a principled statistical framework (RKHS + MMD) can close the sim2real gap in 6DoF PE using only 320 unlabeled real images, achieving SOTA among self-supervised methods and approaching fully supervised performance within a few percent on BOP core datasets. This reduces the annotation burden for 6DoF PE, which is notoriously expensive due to the need for precise 6DoF ground truth. Open questions include whether the dense adapter generalizes to category-level or unseen-object settings, whether the pseudo-pose bootstrapping degrades with more severe domain shifts, and whether the approach extends to datasets without available CAD models.
