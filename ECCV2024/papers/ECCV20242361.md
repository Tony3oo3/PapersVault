## Problem

Existing controlled text-to-image generation methods (ControlNet, UniControl, Uni-ControlNet, Cocktail) operate as feedback-control systems where the controlling network receives only sparse, low-bandwidth feedback from the generation process, creating long delays before newly generated features receive corrective signals. This forces the controller to possess its own generative capacity to anticipate the generation process, leading to unnecessarily large control networks and suboptimal control fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242361/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a single guidance image (depth map, canny-edge map, semantic segmentation map, or human pose keypoints) and produces a generated image at 512×512 resolution (or 1024×1024 with SDXL). Guidance images are extracted from training data using MiDaS for depth, OpenPose for keypoints, Canny for edges, and ground-truth annotations for segmentation. The control network operates on the latent representation of the frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net, requiring only 55M parameters (20M for SDXL) versus the 2.6B-parameter generative model it controls.

## Method

ControlNet-XS redesigns the communication between the controlling and generating networks as a bidirectional, high-frequency, large-bandwidth feedback loop. Instead of ControlNet's architecture where the control encoder is a full copy of the U-Net encoder that only injects signals at the decoder level, ControlNet-XS (Type B) connects the two encoders block-by-block: the control encoder receives features from the generative encoder at every block and sends corrective signals back at every block, eliminating the multi-block delay. Because the controller no longer needs to anticipate the generation process, it can be drastically smaller (55M vs. 361M parameters, a 6.5× reduction) while simultaneously improving both image quality (FID) and control fidelity (MSE-depth, LPIPS, HDD). Training uses the standard diffusion denoising objective with all generative model weights frozen, random initialization for the control network, and zero-convolutions to stabilize early training. The approach is about 2× faster in both inference and training compared to ControlNet.

## Results

ControlNet-XS (55M parameters) achieves the best FID scores across all four guidance types: 16.36 for depth, 15.13 for canny-edges, 17.70 for semantic maps, and 23.58 for human poses, while using only ~15% of the parameters of the largest baselines (Uni-ControlNet at 459M). For pixel-accurate depth control, it also achieves the best MSE-depth (19.6) and LPIPS (0.468), and for canny-edges the best FID (15.13) and LPIPS (0.417). Inference time is reduced from 1 min 11 sec (ControlNet) to 38 sec, and training time from ~500 A100 hours to ~200 A100 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet (361M), the original baseline, ControlNet-XS (55M) wins on FID for depth (16.36 vs. 19.01), canny-edges (15.13 vs. 21.18), and semantic maps (17.70 vs. 35.35, though the latter is noted as potentially inflated due to test-set overlap), while roughly tying on human pose FID (23.58 vs. 23.82). Against Uni-ControlNet (459M), the strongest competitor, ControlNet-XS wins on FID for depth (16.36 vs. 18.38), canny-edges (15.13 vs. 17.37), and semantic maps (17.70 vs. 22.26), but Uni-ControlNet edges ahead on canny-edge HDD (15.94 vs. 15.22) and semantic mIoU is comparable (0.25 vs. 0.31). GLIGEN (231M) and UniControl (374M) both exhibit a quality–control trade-off: they achieve competitive control metrics but suffer large FID degradations (e.g., UniControl FID of 26.80 for depth and 54.63 for poses), whereas ControlNet-XS maintains high quality across all metrics without negative outliers. T2I-Adapter (77M), an open-loop system, is consistently outperformed on FID for depth (20.29 vs. 16.36), canny (18.34 vs. 15.13), and semantic (23.76 vs. 17.70), confirming the paper's hypothesis that feedback-control with high-frequency communication is superior. Cocktail (378M) is only reported for semantic and pose guidance; ControlNet-XS wins on FID for both (17.70 vs. 26.07 for semantic, 23.58 vs. 26.44 for poses). For the loosest guidance (human poses), all methods perform similarly on HDD (~8.5–9.9), and ControlNet-XS is competitive, achieving the best CLIP-Aesthetics score (6.21) among all controlled methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ControlNet [88], which introduced the paradigm of training a separate controlling network to steer a frozen text-to-image diffusion model. The key conceptual departure is reframing this interaction through the lens of feedback-control systems, revealing that existing approaches (ControlNet, UniControl, Uni-ControlNet, Cocktail) all suffer from sparse, low-bandwidth feedback signals between the controller and the generation process. By redesigning the communication to be bidirectional, high-frequency, and large-bandwidth (Type B architecture), the authors eliminate the need for the control network to possess its own generative capacity, enabling a 6.5× parameter reduction while improving both image quality and control fidelity. The work also positions itself against open-loop approaches (T2I-Adapter) and attention-map manipulation methods (GLIGEN), showing that the feedback-control framing is both more principled and more effective for pixel-accurate guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 55M-parameter control network outperforms all state-of-the-art methods for depth, canny-edge, and semantic-map guidance while running roughly 2× faster in both inference and training, and the same principle scales to controlling the 2.6B-parameter Stable Diffusion XL with only 20M parameters. The feedback-control analysis provides a generalizable design principle that could be integrated into multi-image guidance systems, video translation, and controlled 3D generation. Key limitations include the absence of a unified benchmark with consistent evaluation protocols, reliance on COCO validation sets, and the finding that large control models introduce a semantic bias that resists adjustment via control strength. The paper leaves open whether the communication mechanism yields similar gains for multi-modal or multi-image guidance settings.
