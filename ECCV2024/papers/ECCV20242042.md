## Problem

LVLMs frequently hallucinate objects, attributes, and relations in their generated text, limiting real-world deployment. Existing DPO-based hallucination-reduction methods rely on paid GPT-4/GPT-4V APIs for both data generation and preference ranking, require additional training data, and depend on ensembles of external LVLMs—making them expensive, non-scalable, and susceptible to propagating the ranker's own hallucinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242042/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (cropped and resized to 336×336 px) drawn from the same SFT data pool used to train the base LVLM, along with diverse text prompts for caption generation. It produces 750K positive-negative preference pairs (50K question-answer pairs and 700K caption pairs) that are then used for DPO fine-tuning. The final trained model outputs free-form text captions or answers given an image and a text prompt, with no architectural changes to the base LVLM.

## Method

CLIP-DPO replaces GPT-4V-based ranking with a pre-trained CLIP model (ViT-H/14 DFN) that scores image-text alignment via cosine similarity, exploiting CLIP's continuous scores for finer-grained preference signals than GPT-4's discrete ratings. The pipeline has three stages: (1) generation, where MobileVLM-v2 models produce 5 diverse captions per image and Mistral 7B generates per-image Q&A pairs with plausible negatives; (2) annotation, where CLIP ranks all captions, followed by global filtering (category-based downsampling of text-heavy images, score thresholds of 28.0 for captions and 25.0 for questions, removal of long captions) and pair filtering (regex-based question-to-caption parsing, CLIP score margin >2.0, length similarity constraints); (3) optimization, where the target LVLM is fine-tuned for 1 epoch using the standard DPO objective (β=0.1) on the constructed preference data. The key insight is that CLIP's contrastive pre-training on hundreds of millions of image-text pairs makes it a natural, free, and continuous-quality judge for grounding LVLM outputs to visual evidence, eliminating the need for paid APIs, external LVLMs, or additional data.

## Results

On the AMBER hallucination benchmark, CLIP-DPO improves LLaVA-1.5 7B from 83.5 to 89.6 AMBER score, MobileVLM-v2 1.7B from 81.4 to 85.3, MobileVLM-v2 3B from 86.0 to 87.8, and MobileVLM-v2 7B from 84.9 to 88.3. For zero-shot image classification across 9 datasets, CLIP-DPO yields large gains: LLaVA-1.5 7B improves from 40.0% to 47.4% average Top-1, and MobileVLM-v2 7B from 41.6% to 54.5%. On the LLaVA benchmark (GQA, ScienceQA, TextVQA, POPE, MME, MMBench), performance is largely preserved, with average scores shifting by less than 1 point (e.g., LLaVA-1.5 7B: 68.8 → 67.9; MobileVLM-v2 7B: 72.1 → 72.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIP-DPO substantially outperforms HA-DPO [83], the closest competing DPO-based hallucination reduction method: on LLaVA-1.5 7B, CLIP-DPO achieves 89.6 AMBER vs. HA-DPO's 86.7, and also surpasses it on zero-shot classification (47.4% vs. 39.7% average Top-1). CLIP-DPO on LLaVA-1.5 7B (89.6) nearly matches Qwen-VL (89.7), which was trained on 1.4B pre-training and 77M multitask samples, while CLIP-DPO fine-tunes on only 0.7M samples. The method approaches GPT-4V's AMBER score of 91.4 (achieving 89.6) without using any GPT-4V outputs during training. Against the base LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5 7B, MobileVLM-v2 1.7B/3B/7B), CLIP-DPO consistently improves AMBER scores by 3.4–3.9 points and zero-shot classification by 7.4–12.9 points, while LLaVA-bench performance remains within ~1 point of the baseline, confirming no significant degradation on standard vision-language tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-based preference optimization framework (Rafailov et al.) to LVLM hallucination reduction by replacing the standard LLM-based annotation step with a contrastive CLIP image-text similarity ranking. Prior DPO-for-LVLM works (HA-DPO, Silkie, and others) rely on GPT-4/GPT-4V APIs for ranking, additional external data, and ensembles of large LVLMs for generation; CLIP-DPO departs from all three by reusing the original SFT data pool, generating captions with small efficient models (MobileVLM-v2), and using a frozen CLIP scorer as the preference signal. Conceptually, it repurposes the contrastive alignment objective of CLIP—trained on hundreds of millions of image-text pairs—as a grounding oracle, rather than treating preference construction as a generative LLM task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating paid APIs, additional data collection, and external LVLM dependencies, CLIP-DPO makes preference-based hallucination reduction substantially more accessible and reproducible for the community. The large zero-shot classification gains (e.g., +12.9 avg for MobileVLM-v2 7B) suggest the method improves fundamental visual grounding rather than merely gaming hallucination metrics, which is a stronger claim than typical DPO fine-tuning papers make. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based signal generalizes to hallucination types beyond object existence (e.g., fine-grained spatial relations), whether the approach scales to much larger LVLMs, and how sensitive the results are to the quality and distribution of the initial SFT data pool.
