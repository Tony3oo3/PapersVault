## Problem

Existing approximate machine unlearning methods are either computationally expensive (influence functions, Fisher Information Matrix) or risk re-memorizing forgetting data through fine-tuning with random labels (e.g., SalUn). The paper addresses the need for an efficient unlearning approach that reliably erases data influence while preserving model utility, across both classification and text-to-image generation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241258/cropped_page9_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model's parameters θ, a forgetting subset D_f, and the remaining data D_r. Notably, the trimming phase requires only a small fraction (~3%) of D_f to compute connection sensitivity, making it data-efficient. The output is a scrubbed model θ* that minimizes influence of D_f while retaining performance on D_r; in the diffusion model setting, this translates to generated images that no longer contain the target concept (e.g., nudity) when prompted with associated text.

## Method

Scissorhands operates in two phases. First, it computes connection sensitivity scores s_j ≈ E[∂ℓ/∂θ_j · θ_j] over the forgetting data (the SNIP criterion) and reinitializes the top-k% most salient parameters with a uniform distribution, effectively performing a targeted 'lobotomy' on the model's memory of D_f. Second, it repairs the trimmed model by optimizing L(θ; D_r) − λL(θ; D_f) via gradient descent with a projection step: whenever the combined gradient g_o has a positive inner product with the forgetting gradient g_f, the component along g_f is removed by solving a one-dimensional dual problem for scalar v*, yielding the projected direction g = g_o − v*·g_f. This ensures each update never reduces the loss on D_f, preventing re-memorization while restoring utility on D_r.

## Results

On the primary sample-wise unlearning benchmark (forgetting 10% of training data), Scissorhands achieves the lowest Avg. Gap to retraining across all three classification datasets: 4.11 on CIFAR-100, 1.62 on CIFAR-10, and 1.43 on SVHN, outperforming the next-best baseline (SalUn) by 2.5×, 0.5×, and 2.2× respectively. On CIFAR-100, Scissorhands reduces forgetting-data accuracy to 68.76% (closest to the 75.13% retrain oracle) while retaining 99.24% accuracy on remaining data. On CelebAMask-HQ (class-wise identity forgetting), Scissorhands achieves an Avg. Gap of 2.82 with 99.83% MIA accuracy, marginally outperforming SalUn (2.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scissorhands achieves the lowest Avg. Gap (distance to retrain oracle) on all three classification datasets, beating the strongest baseline SalUn by 6.34 points on CIFAR-100 (4.11 vs 10.45), 0.53 on CIFAR-10 (1.62 vs 2.15), and 2.56 on SVHN (1.43 vs 3.99). On CIFAR-100, Scissorhands reduces forgetting-data accuracy to 68.76%—closest to the 75.13% retrain target—while SalUn retains 88.56% and ℓ₁-sparse retains 96.35%, indicating substantially more complete erasure. On CIFAR-10, ℓ₁-sparse achieves a marginally lower Acc_Df (94.17% vs 95.40%) but at the cost of 2.28 points lower test accuracy (90.64% vs 92.92%) and 2.29 points lower remaining-data accuracy, making Scissorhands the better overall trade-off. On SVHN, Scissorhands nearly matches the retrain oracle on forgetting accuracy (91.62% vs 91.81%) while exceeding it on test accuracy (93.05% vs 91.17%), outperforming IU (2.63) and SalUn (3.99) on Avg. Gap. For class-wise identity forgetting on CelebAMask-HQ, Scissorhands (Avg. Gap 2.82) narrowly edges out SalUn (2.93) while maintaining 99.83% MIA accuracy versus SalUn's 100%, with both far outperforming FT (49.06) and GA (35.56). In the Stable Diffusion nudity-erasure case study, Scissorhands produces comparable results to SalUn, with no exposed sensitive content detected under explicit nudity prompts, though SalUn shows a slight edge on I2P-detected female breast/genitalia (partly attributed to NudeNet false positives).

## Relation to Prior Work

Scissorhands repurposes the SNIP connection-sensitivity criterion (Lee et al., ICLR 2019) from network pruning to identify and reinitialize parameters most influential on forgetting data, effectively performing a targeted 'lobotomy' before relearning. The repairing phase adapts the gradient-conflict resolution framework from gradient episodic memory (Lopez-Paz & Ranzato, NeurIPS 2017) into a dual-formulation projection that prevents the model from reducing loss on forgetting data during fine-tuning. The most direct competitor, SalUn (Fan et al., ICLR 2024), also uses saliency-based weight selection but relies on random-label fine-tuning; Scissorhands departs by arguing that random labels can still memorize data distributions (citing Zhang et al., ICLR 2017) and instead explicitly projects away the forgetting-data gradient direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient projection mechanism offers a principled alternative to random-label fine-tuning that avoids the memorization risk identified in prior work, which is practically important for sensitive applications where even associating forgotten data with random labels is unacceptable. However, the paper acknowledges incomplete concept erasure in diffusion models, the risk of introducing bias through selective deletion, and the difficulty of balancing forgetting with image quality in generative models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to regression, NLP, and sequential data, and whether it remains effective when training data are unavailable.
