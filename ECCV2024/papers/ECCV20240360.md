## Problem

Existing feature-based noisy sample selection methods for long-tailed noisy-label learning estimate class centroids using only high-confidence samples within each target class, which is unreliable under long-tailed distributions because mislabeled samples are excluded, leaving too few data points—especially for tail classes—and no mechanism actively improves representation quality. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a distribution-aware centroid estimation that leverages all samples across classes and a confidence-aware contrastive learning strategy that tailors representation learning to sample reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240360/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, standard resolution for Red mini-ImageNet) from long-tailed training sets with noisy labels, generating two strong augmentations, one weak augmentation, and a mixup sample per input during training. At test time, the final prediction is the average of outputs from a conventional classifier and a Balanced Softmax classifier, producing a single class label per image.

## Method

DaSC introduces Distribution-aware Class Centroid Estimation (DaCC), which computes each class centroid as a weighted average of features from all samples across all classes, with weights derived from temperature-scaled model predictions rather than restricting to target-class high-confidence samples. A two-component GMM is then fit to the assignment probabilities between sample features and centroids to separate clean from noisy samples. For representation learning, the method splits samples by pseudo-label confidence: high-confidence samples are trained with a Semi-supervised Balanced Contrastive Loss (SBCL) that normalizes per-class in the denominator to counteract head-class dominance, while low-confidence samples are trained with a Mixup-enhanced Instance Discrimination Loss (MIDL) that uses mixup-generated hard negatives stored in a memory bank. The overall training follows a DivideMix-style semi-supervised framework with co-training, where noisy samples are treated as unlabeled data and a balanced classifier trained with Balanced Softmax complements the conventional classifier.

## Results

On long-tailed CIFAR-10 with symmetric noise 0.4, DaSC achieves 89.04% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best prior methods SFA (86.81%) and TABASCO (85.53%) by 2.23% and 3.51% respectively. Gains grow with difficulty: on CIFAR-100 with symmetric noise 0.6, DaSC reaches 54.40% versus 47.71% (SFA) and 45.98% (TABASCO), a 6.69% and 8.42% improvement. On real-world datasets, DaSC leads by 2.04% over TABASCO on Red mini-ImageNet (noise 0.2), 5.40% on CIFAR-10N, and 4.27% on CIFAR-100N.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DaSC outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and noise configuration tested. The most direct competitors are SFA [27] and TABASCO [33], the two most recent NL-LT methods; DaSC surpasses them by 2.23–6.69% on CIFAR-10/100 (symmetric noise) and by 2.04–5.40% on real-world datasets (Red mini-ImageNet, CIFAR-10N, CIFAR-100N). NL-only methods such as DivideMix (82.67% on CIFAR-10 Sym 0.4) and UNICON (84.25%) are 4.4–6.4% behind DaSC, while LT-only methods (LA, LDAM, IB, BBN) lag by 15–28% on CIFAR-100, confirming that neither addressing long-tail nor noise alone is sufficient. Among NL-LT baselines, ULC [17] is the closest on CIFAR-10 (84.46% vs. 89.04%) but still trails by 4.58%; RoLT [52] and HAR [3] are further behind at 81.62% and 77.44% respectively. The gap widens substantially on CIFAR-100 and real-world data, where DaSC's distribution-aware centroid estimation and confidence-aware contrastive learning provide the largest absolute gains over all prior approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DaSC builds directly on the DivideMix semi-supervised framework (MixMatch loss, co-training, pseudo-label generation) and extends the centroid-based noisy sample selection paradigm introduced by RoLT and SFA. The key conceptual departure is in DaCC: rather than averaging features only from high-confidence samples of the target class (as in RoLT, SFA, TABASCO), it performs a weighted average over all classes' samples using temperature-scaled model predictions as weights, thereby incorporating informative cross-class information. The confidence-aware contrastive learning component repurposes SCL (for high-confidence samples via SBCL) and nonparametric instance discrimination (for low-confidence samples via MIDL with mixup negatives), combining them in a split strategy not previously explored in the NL-LT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combined NL-LT setting is highly realistic for web-crawled data, and the paper demonstrates that the centroid estimation bottleneck (using only target-class samples) is a significant source of degradation, especially for tail classes. The confidence-aware contrastive split provides a principled mechanism to extract signal from uncertain samples rather than discarding them, which could inform broader noisy-label learning pipelines. However, evaluation is limited to CIFAR-scale datasets and ResNet-18 backbones, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level datasets or transformer architectures. The GMM-based clean/noisy separation and the fixed threshold schedule for confidence are also somewhat heuristic, and their sensitivity to noise type and ratio is not deeply analyzed.
