## Problem

Pre-training procedural video models for step localization and instruction generation has relied on massive, noisy web-scale datasets of raw ASR transcripts (≈15M videos), which are computationally expensive to train on and suffer from a significant domain gap between spoken language and the human-written instructions the model must ultimately produce. This paper asks whether a much smaller, cleaner pre-training corpus can match or exceed the performance of these large noisy datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single untrimmed procedural video (cooking domain) as input, with no ASR transcript or other text provided at inference time. It outputs a sequence of non-overlapping temporal segments (start and end timestamps) each paired with a free-form English instruction sentence. Video features are pre-computed with S3D at 16 fps and resized to a fixed 256-frame temporal length; the model generates a variable number of proposals (up to N) via a set-based transformer decoder, selecting the top-k at inference using IoU-aware confidence scores.

## Method

The paper contributes two components. First, Sieve & Swap is an automatic data-curation pipeline that pairs cooking videos from HowTo100M with text-only recipes from RecipeNLG via title and content-word overlap, then uses MPNet sentence embeddings to retrieve the nearest recipe step for each ASR segment; segments below a cosine-similarity threshold (0.75) are discarded (sieve) and the rest are replaced with the retrieved human-written step (swap), yielding ~48K videos and 0.51M segments—five times fewer segments than the raw ASR baseline. Second, ProcX is a set-based transformer extending PDVC with three architectural changes: (i) key-aware deformable attention that samples both keys and values from multi-scale temporal features and computes standard scaled-dot-product attention over the sampled points, preventing the attention collapse observed in vanilla deformable attention; (ii) a contrastive transformer captioner using MIL-InfoNCE during pre-training and standard InfoNCE during fine-tuning, replacing the LSTM decoder; and (iii) a gIoU-guided varifocal loss (gVFL) that ties confidence scores to temporal localization quality, addressing the mismatch between high confidence and poor boundary prediction in the base model.

## Results

ProcX pre-trained on the Sieve & Swap dataset (0.5M segments) achieves state-of-the-art SODA-C of 7.29 on YouCook2 and 11.66 on Tasty, outperforming all video-only baselines by a wide margin. On YouCook2, this represents a +1.13 SODA-C gain over PDVC (6.16) and +3.08 over Vid2Seq (4.21) under the same pre-training protocol. On Tasty, the gains are even larger: +4.02 over PDVC (7.64) and +4.38 over Vid2Seq (7.28). Notably, ProcX achieves these results using only 0.5M pre-training segments, roughly 1800× fewer than Vid2Seq's ~900M segments, while also improving localization (SODA-D: 37.61 on YouCook2, 52.16 on Tasty).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Vid2Seq [33], ProcX with Sieve & Swap pre-training wins decisively on SODA-C: 7.29 vs 4.21 on YouCook2 (+3.08) and 11.66 vs 7.28 on Tasty (+4.38), while using only 0.5M segments versus Vid2Seq's ~900M. Even without any pre-training, ProcX (6.03 SODA-C) surpasses Vid2Seq (3.61) on YouCook2, indicating the architectural improvements (contrastive transformer, key-aware deformable attention, gVFL) are independently effective. Against PDVC [30], ProcX improves SODA-C by 1.13 points on YouCook2 (7.29 vs 6.16) and 4.02 points on Tasty (11.66 vs 7.64) under identical Sieve & Swap pre-training, with the larger Tasty gap attributed to PDVC's LSTM captioner struggling with the longer, more varied instructions in that dataset. On localization (SODA-D), ProcX leads PDVC by 4.81 points on YouCook2 (37.61 vs 32.80) and 7.22 points on Tasty (52.16 vs 44.94). The only methods that exceed ProcX's YouCook2 SODA-C are Vid2Seq variants that receive ASR transcripts as input at inference time (8.60 and 10.30), but these are not directly comparable since they convert the task into text-to-text translation rather than pure video-to-language generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PDVC architecture for procedural dense video captioning by replacing its LSTM captioner with a contrastive transformer (following CoCa's decoupled design), upgrading vanilla deformable attention to a key-aware variant that samples both keys and values from multi-scale features, and introducing a gIoU-guided varifocal loss for confidence scoring. On the data side, it departs from the prevailing strategy of pre-training on massive raw ASR transcript corpora (e.g., Vid2Seq's ~1M videos) by proposing Sieve & Swap, which fuses HowTo100M cooking videos with RecipeNLG text-only recipes to produce a smaller, cleaner pre-training set. The approach is conceptually related to prior work on distant supervision for procedural activities (Lin et al., Zhou et al.) but differs in that it replaces rather than merely aligns ASR text with human-written instructions, directly targeting the spoken-to-written domain gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is a data curation pipeline that reduces pre-training data by roughly three orders of magnitude in video count (and 5× in segments) while improving downstream performance across multiple architectures, suggesting that transcript quality and stylistic alignment with target instructions matter more than raw scale for procedural video pre-training. The Sieve & Swap technique is domain-agnostic in principle and could be extended to other instructional domains (e.g., WikiHow), though the paper is currently limited to cooking. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger compute budgets, whether the 5× segment reduction generalizes beyond the cooking domain, and whether the zero-shot localization gains observed on YouCook2 (but not Tasty) reflect a genuine transfer benefit or a segment-duration artifact.
