## Problem

In assistive driving, only the driver's upper body is visible within the cockpit, yet existing skeleton-based emotion recognition methods rely on full-body poses and GCNs with handcrafted adjacency matrices limited to physically-connected edges, missing long-range joint relationships. Additionally, naturalistic driving datasets exhibit severe class imbalance, degrading recognition of underrepresented emotions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240672/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3-second upper-body skeleton sequences (joint coordinates extracted via AlphaPose from in-vehicle camera frames), preprocessed into joint position features (original coordinates concatenated with positions relative to the center spine joint) and bone features (inter-joint lengths). It outputs one of five fine-grained emotion labels: peace, happiness, weariness, anxiety, and anger, evaluated with both fine-grained and coarse-grained (positive/neutral/negative) accuracy and F1 scores.

## Method

UbH-GCN decomposes the upper-body skeleton into an N-ary tree rooted at a chosen node (hip or nose), then constructs a hierarchical graph (UbH-Graph) by adding fully-connected edges between all nodes in adjacent hierarchy levels, expanding the receptive field beyond physically-connected edges to capture long-range joint interactions. The spatial module performs subset-wise GCN operations over five edge types (identity, centripetal, centrifugal, 2-hop centripetal, 2-hop centrifugal) with concatenation along the channel dimension rather than summation, followed by batch normalization and residual addition. A four-way ensemble combines two UbH-Graphs (rooted at hip and nose) across joint and bone streams without motion streams, with equal-weight averaging to avoid dataset-specific coefficient tuning. To handle class imbalance, a modified logit-variation loss adds Gaussian noise to logits scaled inversely to class frequency during training, expanding minority-class representations in feature space while removing the perturbation at inference.

## Results

On the AIDE assistive driving dataset, UbH-GCN with 4-way ensemble achieves 77.50% fine-grained accuracy and 75.70% weighted F1, outperforming the multimodal AIDE baseline (74.87% Acc) and the strongest skeleton-only competitor HD-GCN 4-ensemble (76.68% Acc, 74.32% F1). On the Emilya daily-action dataset, UbH-GCN (2-ensemble) reaches 93.98% ± 0.80% accuracy under 10-fold cross-validation, approaching the full-body ST-ITE method (94.42% ± 0.68%) while using only upper-body skeleton data. In terms of efficiency, UbH-GCN requires only 0.11 GFLOPs and 1.04M parameters in single-stream mode, making it the most parameter-efficient method among those achieving >74% accuracy on AIDE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On AIDE, UbH-GCN (4-ensemble) outperforms all skeleton-only baselines: it beats HD-GCN (4-ensemble) by 0.82% Acc and 1.38% F1, CTR-GCN by 5.58% Acc, and all EfficientGCN variants by over 10% Acc. Notably, it also surpasses the full-modality AIDE baseline (which uses scene, face, body, gesture, and posture) by 2.63% Acc, demonstrating that upper-body skeleton alone can match or exceed multimodal approaches for driver emotion recognition. On Emilya, UbH-GCN (10-fold) achieves 93.98% Acc, narrowly trailing the full-body ST-ITE method (94.42%) by 0.44% while using only upper-body data, and clearly outperforming MS-Shift (92.00%) and Multiscale CNN (91.31%). Under 3-fold cross-validation on Emilya, UbH-GCN (91.28%) is slightly below both ST-ITE (93.01%) and MS-Shift (92.00%), indicating a small gap when full-body data is available and the evaluation protocol is more stringent. The complexity comparison (Table 5) shows UbH-GCN single-stream uses fewer parameters (1.04M) and FLOPs (0.11 GFLOPs) than CTR-GCN (1.64M, 0.14) and HD-GCN (1.13M, 0.12) while achieving higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical graph decomposition introduced by HD-GCN (Lee et al., ICCV 2023) by adding fully-connected edges between adjacent hierarchy node sets, creating the UbH-Graph to capture long-range joint relationships that physically-connected (PC) edges alone miss. It reuses the temporal module and subset-wise GCN operations from CTR-GCN (Chen et al., ICCV 2021) and adapts the four-stream ensemble of AGCN (Shi et al., CVPR 2019) into a four-way ensemble that drops motion streams in favor of two joint and two bone streams rooted at different nodes (hip vs. nose). The class-imbalance loss is adapted from the logit-variation technique of Wang et al. (CVPR 2023), originally designed for long-tailed semantic segmentation, repurposed here for imbalanced emotion categories in naturalistic driving data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in driver monitoring: in-vehicle cameras only capture the upper body, yet most skeleton-based emotion recognition methods assume full-body visibility. The finding that upper-body-only data can match or exceed full-body and even multimodal baselines on the AIDE dataset suggests that driving-specific emotion recognition need not rely on expensive full-body capture. However, the persistent confusion between 'weariness' and 'peace' hints at inherent limits of body-movement-only signals for subtle affective states, and the evaluation is limited to two datasets with modest class counts (5 and 8). Open questions include real-time deployment on in-vehicle hardware, generalization to more emotion categories, and whether the FC-edge density becomes prohibitive at scale.
