## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing models rely on additional modalities (bounding boxes, masks, keypoints) to control where new objects are placed, but text is the most natural and flexible interaction modality in real-world scenarios. The core challenge is that the object to be generated is absent from the input image, so standard attention mechanisms cannot simply attend to existing visual features to determine placement—spatial localization must be inferred from weak linguistic supervision in complex multi-object scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image (resized to 480×480) and a natural-language prompt that is decomposed into an appearance description (e.g., 'a cute dinosaur with round face, animation style') and a spatial condition (e.g., 'standing in front of the door'). It produces a full edited image where the described object is generated at the semantically appropriate location while preserving shadows, geometry, illumination, and occlusion in irrelevant regions. The framework is training-free with respect to the underlying LDM: only lightweight pixel-space modules (patch expanding/partition layers and multimodal fusion transformer) are trained, while the Stable Diffusion V1.5 U-Net and VAE remain frozen.

## Method

ST-LDM comprises two parallel Swin-Transformer branches operating in pixel space. The first is a hierarchical autoencoder with a trainable patch-expanding layer that adaptively compresses the image to a configurable latent resolution (factors 2, 4, or 8) while retaining pretrained Swin-T modeling capacity. The second is a multimodal fusion Transformer that injects spatial text embeddings into visual features via cross-attention, enhanced by a deformable feature alignment (DFA) module that learns per-query 2D offsets (tanh-constrained) and modulation scalars (sigmoid) from concatenated multi-scale visual and linguistic features, then applies bilinear sampling and a matrix-completion function to produce a refined spatial guidance map G. This map is integrated into the frozen LDM's denoising U-Net through a training-free region-wise backpropagation scheme: an energy function penalizes the mismatch between the U-Net's cross-attention scores and the thresholded, dilated guidance map, and gradients update the latent at each of the first 10 diffusion steps. Training proceeds in two phases—autoencoder calibration on OpenImages with KL regularization, followed by multimodal transformer training on VrR-VG and ComCOCO—while all LDM parameters stay fixed.

## Results

On the constructed COCO2014 benchmark (1,500 images), ST-LDM achieves the best scores across all five metrics: FID 6.16, LPIPS 15.54, HPS 64.28, CLIPScore 81.97, and VISOR 92.39. These results are obtained using only text as the conditioning modality, outperforming methods that rely on additional spatial inputs such as masks or bounding boxes. The closest competitor, GLIGEN (bounding-box conditioned), trails by 0.08 in FID, 1.55 in LPIPS, 1.87 in HPS, 1.35 in CLIPScore, and 1.86 in VISOR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-LDM outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric, a notable result given that it uses only text as input while competitors require masks or bounding boxes. Against text-only baselines, the gains are substantial: over InstructPix2Pix, ST-LDM improves FID from 22.25 to 6.16 and VISOR from 72.15 to 92.39; over SmartEdit, FID drops from 18.47 to 6.16 and VISOR rises from 77.86 to 92.39. Against mask-conditioned methods (BlendedLatentDiffusion, PaintByWord), ST-LDM leads by 6.77 and 3.56 in FID respectively, and by 4.27 and 4.98 in VISOR. Against bounding-box-conditioned methods, the margins are tighter but consistent: ST-LDM beats GLIGEN by 1.86 in VISOR (92.39 vs 90.53) and 1.87 in HPS (64.28 vs 62.41), and beats BreakAScene by 0.44 in VISOR (92.39 vs 91.95) and 5.03 in HPS (64.28 vs 59.25). The key takeaway is that text-only spatial grounding via deformable feature alignment matches or exceeds the precision of explicit geometric constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

ST-LDM builds on two key threads: latent diffusion models (Rombach et al.) as the generative backbone, and training-free cross-attention backpropagation (Chen et al.) for spatial control during denoising. It departs from prior spatially controllable generation (GLIGEN, Make-A-Scene, SpaText) by eliminating the need for explicit spatial annotations like bounding boxes or keypoints, relying instead on natural language alone. It also extends vision-language transformer approaches (LAVT, SLViT) by addressing the unique challenge that the target object is absent from the input image, which breaks standard cross-attention assumptions. The deformable feature alignment mechanism is the paper's core architectural contribution, borrowing from deformable convolutions to resolve the conflict between spatial and appearance cues when the referenced object does not yet exist in the scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: language is the most natural and scalable interaction modality for image editing, and requiring per-object bounding boxes or keypoints is cumbersome for large-scale or video applications. The training-free, plug-and-play integration into any pretrained LDM makes the approach broadly applicable without retraining the diffusion model. However, the evaluation is limited to a self-constructed 1,500-image benchmark, the training data includes inpainting artifacts from VrR-VG, and the approach has not been tested on video or 3D scenes. Open questions include whether the deformable alignment generalizes to more complex multi-object spatial reasoning and whether the Swin-T backbone is essential or merely convenient.
