## Problem

Supervised dense point trackers are limited by the diversity and scale of synthetic training data and by their restricted temporal field of view, making them struggle with long-term occlusions. Test-time optimization methods like Omnimotion overcome the data limitation but lack external visual priors, relying solely on optical flow and reconstruction from a single video. DINO-Tracker closes this gap by combining per-video test-time training with the rich semantic features of a pre-trained DINOv2 model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240692/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB video (evaluated at 256px and 480px resolutions) and a set of query points in a reference frame as input. It outputs dense 2D position estimates for every query point across all T frames, along with per-point visibility (occlusion) labels. DINOv2 ViT-L/14 features are extracted at the 16th layer at low spatial resolution, and final sub-pixel positions are recovered via a weighted sum over a local neighborhood of the cost-volume maximum.

## Method

The core architecture is Delta-DINO, a lightweight CNN that predicts a residual added to frozen DINOv2 features, preserving the semantic prior while adapting to the specific video. Tracking is performed via cosine-similarity cost volumes between a bilinearly-sampled query feature and target-frame features, refined by a small CNN and spatial softmax to yield sub-pixel positions. Training is entirely self-supervised on the single test video, combining five loss terms: RAFT optical-flow supervision for short-range accuracy, a contrastive DINO best-buddies loss that distills long-range semantic correspondences into the refined feature space, a self-distilling refined best-buddies loss, a cycle-consistency loss, and a prior-preservation loss that constrains refined features to maintain DINO's cosine similarity and norm. Occlusion is predicted post-hoc via trajectory agreement: a point is deemed visible at frame t if re-tracking from the predicted position at t reproduces the original trajectory at anchor frames.

## Results

On TAP-Vid-DAVIS-480, DINO-Tracker achieves 80.4% position accuracy (δ_avg^x), 88.1% occlusion accuracy (OA), and 64.6% Average Jaccard (AJ), outperforming the best supervised tracker Co-Tracker (79.4 / 89.5 / 65.6) on position accuracy while matching it on OA and AJ. On TAP-Vid-Kinetics-480, it reaches 74.3% δ_avg^x and 89.2% OA, surpassing Co-Tracker (72.8 / 88.9) and TAPIR (69.8 / 86.7). On BADJA, DINO-Tracker achieves 72.4% δ^seg and 14.3% δ^3px, substantially outperforming all baselines including Co-Tracker (64.0 / 11.2) and TAPIR (68.7 / 10.5). Compared to the only other test-time training method, Omnimotion, DINO-Tracker gains 10.7 points on DAVIS-256 position accuracy (78.2 vs 67.5) and 27.2 points on BADJA δ^seg (72.4 vs 45.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINO-Tracker outperforms the best supervised tracker Co-Tracker on position accuracy at 480px resolution on both DAVIS (80.4 vs 79.4) and Kinetics (74.3 vs 72.8), while matching it closely on OA and AJ. Against TAPIR, the gap is larger: +3.1 δ_avg^x on DAVIS-480 and +4.5 on Kinetics-480, with a 3.7-point advantage on BADJA δ^seg. The most dramatic comparison is against Omnimotion, the only other test-time training method: DINO-Tracker gains 6.3 points on DAVIS-480 position accuracy (80.4 vs 74.1) and a massive 27.2 points on BADJA δ^seg (72.4 vs 45.2), demonstrating the critical benefit of incorporating DINO's semantic prior over relying solely on optical flow and video reconstruction. Raw DINOv2 features used as a simple nearest-neighbor baseline already outperform RAFT and TAP-Net on DAVIS-256 (61.4 vs 56.7 and 53.4), but DINO-Tracker's refinement adds 15.7 points over raw DINOv2 on DAVIS-480. The paper also shows that under high occlusion rates, DINO-Tracker's advantage over all baselines widens significantly, confirming that DINO's semantic information is most valuable when pixel-level cues are unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

DINO-Tracker sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: test-time optimization for tracking (Omnimotion) and self-supervised feature learning (DINOv2). It adopts Omnimotion's per-video optimization paradigm and TAP-Net's cost-volume tracking architecture, but departs fundamentally by injecting an external semantic prior through frozen DINOv2 features refined via a lightweight CNN residual (Delta-DINO). This is the first work to combine test-time training with pre-trained self-supervised image features for dense tracking, whereas Omnimotion relies solely on optical flow and video reconstruction, and supervised trackers (Co-Tracker, TAPIR) are trained from scratch on synthetic data without leveraging external representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a self-supervised, test-time-trained tracker can match or exceed supervised SOTA methods, particularly under long-term occlusions where semantic priors provide a decisive advantage. This opens a path toward tracking methods that adapt to individual videos without requiring large-scale synthetic supervision. Key limitations remain: the method does not model trajectories behind occluders, and in scenes with multiple semantically similar objects and little optical flow signal, tracks can jump between objects. An open question is whether the DINO prior can be further adapted (e.g., via temporal consistency losses) to handle these ambiguous multi-object scenarios without sacrificing the semantic grounding that drives the occlusion advantage.
