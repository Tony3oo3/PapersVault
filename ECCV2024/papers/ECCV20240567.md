## Problem

Despite complementary strengths, images and point clouds are predominantly processed by separate models in visual-geometric representation learning, and constructing large-scale paired datasets is prohibitively expensive due to manual annotation, cross-modality alignment, and copyright/ethical concerns. No prior method achieves supervised pre-training on a single unified transformer that simultaneously improves both image and 3D object recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240567/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model simultaneously ingests a 224×224 RGB fractal image and an 8192-point 3D fractal point cloud, both derived from the same 3D-IFS formula, and outputs a single class prediction over C categories (the formula-supervised consistency label). After fine-tuning, the same unified transformer produces task-specific outputs: class labels (classification), bounding boxes (detection via 3DETR or MaskR-CNN), or per-point/per-pixel masks (segmentation). The key constraint is that the image is not independently generated but is a perspective projection of the point cloud from a single virtual camera, guaranteeing geometric alignment without manual correspondence.

## Method

FSVGP constructs VG-FractalDB by generating fractal point clouds via 3D Iterated Function Systems (7 affine transformations per category, 8192 points), projecting each onto a 224×224 image plane with a randomly positioned virtual camera, and assigning a formula-supervised consistency label based on the variance of 3D coordinates across a threshold. A unified transformer—combining ViT patch embeddings for images and PointT embeddings for point clouds with a shared class token and classification MLP—processes both modalities jointly and is trained with standard cross-entropy loss on the consistency label. The critical design choice is that no cross-modal attention modules or multi-task losses are introduced; the shared label space and simultaneous dual-modality input alone drive visual-geometric representation learning. FractalNoiseMix (20% points from random other categories) augments intra-category diversity, and the entire pipeline requires no real data, manual annotation, or cross-modality alignment.

## Results

FSVGP (VG-FractalDB-1k) achieves 92.0% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-100, 68.3 AP50 on COCO detection, and 65.6 AP50 on COCO instance segmentation for visual recognition, while simultaneously achieving 83.7% accuracy on ScanObjectNN, 63.7 mAP25 on ScanNet detection, and 84.1 mIoUcat on ShapeNet-parts for geometric recognition. These results are obtained with only 1M synthetic pre-training samples (1/21 the data of VisualAtom-21k), and FSVGP outperforms both VisualAtom-21k and PC-FractalDB-1k across all six tasks. At 384×384 resolution, the scaled VG-FractalDB-21k reaches 83.8% on ImageNet-1k, approaching JFT-300M pre-training (84.2%) with roughly 1/14 of the data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the latest FDSL baselines, FSVGP wins across the board: it surpasses VisualAtom-21k by +0.7% on ImageNet-100, +2.0 AP50 on COCO detection, and +2.3 AP50 on COCO segmentation, while using only 1/21 of the pre-training data; it also beats PC-FractalDB-1k by +0.4% on ScanObjectNN, +0.7 mAP25 on ScanNet, and +0.4 mIoUcat on ShapeNet-parts. Against SSL methods, FSVGP trails MAE (92.5% vs 90.0% avg on image classification; 50.5 vs 45.4 AP on COCO detection) and DINO (92.1% vs 90.0% avg), and also falls short of PointBERT (89.5% vs 88.5% avg) and PointMAE (89.4% vs 88.5% avg) on 3D classification, likely due to domain overlap between ShapeNet pre-training and the fine-tuning sets. However, FSVGP outperforms ImageNet supervised pre-training and DINO on COCO detection/segmentation, and beats MaskPoint by +1.4 mAP50 on ScanNet 3D detection (42.0 vs 40.6). In few-shot 3D classification (Table 8), FSVGP achieves 96.4/96.8% (5-way 10/20-shot) and 92.2/93.3% (10-way 10/20-shot), matching or exceeding all SSL and FDSL baselines. The ablation (Table 12) confirms that the formula-supervised consistency label is critical: replacing it with MAE-style self-supervision drops ImageNet-100 accuracy from 92.0% to 80.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Formula-Driven Supervised Learning (FDSL) paradigm, which previously addressed single modalities in isolation—VisualAtom-21k for image pre-training and PC-FractalDB-1k for 3D point cloud pre-training—by unifying both visual and geometric modalities within a single transformer architecture. It departs from prior visual-geometric representation learning (e.g., CrossPoint, Pri3D) that relies on contrastive learning or pixel-point correspondence, instead using formula-supervised consistency labels derived from fractal geometry to create a shared classification label space across modalities. The architectural foundation draws on ViT and Point Transformer with minimal modifications limited to input processing, preserving the model's generality for downstream fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSVGP offers a transparent, copyright-free, and annotation-free alternative to large-scale real-data pre-training, which is increasingly important as concerns over data provenance, privacy, and social bias grow. The demonstrated data efficiency—matching or exceeding VisualAtom-21k with 1/21 the synthetic data—is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. However, the domain gap between synthetic fractal data and real-world objects remains a limitation: FSVGP underperforms strong SSL methods (MAE, DINO) on absolute accuracy, and linear probing is notably weaker. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex real-world 3D scenes (e.g., autonomous driving with bird's-eye-view images) and how to design efficient fine-tuning strategies that bridge the synthetic-to-real gap.
