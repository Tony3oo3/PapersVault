## Problem

RAFT-style optical flow methods require many iterative refinement steps (12–32) for convergence, rely on complex custom-designed encoders and recurrent units, and use a standard L1 loss that overfits to ambiguous occluded pixels. This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a simpler, faster, and more accurate variant that reduces iterations to 4–12 while improving accuracy and cross-dataset generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240164/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB frames (I₁, I₂) at arbitrary resolution and internally operates at 1/8 resolution; for 1080p inputs it uses a downsample-by-2, infer, bilinear-upsample protocol. Beyond the standard per-pixel 2D flow field, the network outputs Mixture-of-Laplace parameters (mixing coefficient α, scale β₂, and mean μ) at each pixel, providing a per-pixel uncertainty estimate that distinguishes ordinary from ambiguous flow regions.

## Method

SEA-RAFT replaces RAFT's custom encoders with standard ImageNet-pretrained ResNets and swaps the ConvGRU for two ConvNeXt blocks, while introducing three key training innovations: (1) a Mixture-of-Laplace loss where one component has fixed variance (β₁=0) to align with L1 endpoint-error for ordinary pixels and a second learnable component absorbs ambiguity from occlusion; (2) direct regression of the initial flow by feeding both frames through the context encoder, eliminating zero-initialization and reducing required iterations from 12–32 to 4–12; and (3) rigid-flow pre-training on TartanAir to improve generalization. The probabilistic parameters (α, β₂) are re-predicted at every refinement iteration, and the sequence loss exponentially downweights early iterations, enabling competitive accuracy with only 4 training iterations.

## Results

On the Spring test benchmark, SEA-RAFT(M) achieves a state-of-the-art 0.363 EPE and 3.686 1px outlier rate, representing 22.9% and 17.8% error reduction over the next best published results (Flowformer++: 0.447 EPE, 4.482 1px). Even the smallest model SEA-RAFT(S) (0.377 EPE, 3.904 1px) surpasses all other methods. On KITTI test, SEA-RAFT(L) achieves 4.30 Fl-all, matching CCMR+ (12.9 Fl-all on train) while running at 108 ms vs. 498.9 ms for DIP. On Sintel test, SEA-RAFT(L) achieves 1.31 clean / 2.60 final EPE, competitive with top methods but trailing DDVM (2.00 final) and Flowformer++ (1.94 final).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Spring test, SEA-RAFT(M) decisively outperforms all 21 baselines: it beats Flowformer++ (the next best without extra data) by 22.9% on EPE (0.363 vs. 0.447) and 17.8% on 1px (3.686 vs. 4.482), and beats MS-RAFT+ (best with extra data) by 35.6% on EPE (0.363 vs. 0.643) and 35.7% on 1px (3.686 vs. 5.724). Even SEA-RAFT(S) surpasses CroCoFlow (0.498 EPE) and Win-Win (0.475 EPE) by 24% and 21% on EPE respectively. On Sintel test, SEA-RAFT(L) (1.31 clean / 2.60 final) is competitive but trails DDVM (1.75/2.48) and Flowformer++ (1.07/1.94) on the final pass, though it matches RAFT's clean-pass performance (1.44 vs. 1.61) while being 2× faster (71 ms vs. 141 ms). On KITTI test, SEA-RAFT(L) achieves 4.30 Fl-all, matching CCMR+ (3.86) and beating RAFT (5.10) by 15.7%, while running at 108 ms versus 498.9 ms for DIP and 335.6 ms for FlowFormer. In terms of efficiency, SEA-RAFT(M) at 71 ms is the only method achieving better-than-RAFT accuracy at under 100 ms latency, making it at least 2.3× faster than all methods with comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEA-RAFT is a direct variant of RAFT (Teed & Deng, ECCV 2020), retaining its core iterative refinement loop with multi-scale 4D correlation volumes and recurrent updates, but replacing RAFT's custom-designed feature/context encoders with standard ImageNet-pretrained ResNets and its ConvGRU with ConvNeXt blocks. It borrows the direct flow regression idea from the FlowNet family (Dosovitskiy et al., ICCV 2015) to initialize the flow field rather than zero-initializing, and introduces a Mixture-of-Laplace loss inspired by probabilistic regression in keypoint matching (PDC-Net+, LoFTR) but reformulated with a fixed-variance component to align with L1-based flow metrics. The paper explicitly positions these contributions as orthogonal to other RAFT variants that focus on transformer replacements (FlowFormer, CRAFT) or efficient implementations (EMD-L, RPKNet), and introduces rigid-flow pre-training on TartanAir as a new ingredient in the RAFT-style pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SEA-RAFT provides a practical, well-ablated recipe that simultaneously improves accuracy (22.9% EPE reduction on Spring), efficiency (2.3× faster, 4 iterations vs. 12–32 in RAFT), and simplicity (standard off-the-shelf modules), making it an attractive baseline for future optical flow research. The finding that rigid-flow pre-training on TartanAir—despite its extremely limited motion diversity—improves cross-dataset generalization is counterintuitive and suggests that scene/texture diversity may matter more than motion diversity for flow generalization. A notable limitation is the unexplained underperformance on Sintel final pass in the zero-shot C+T setting, which the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. Open questions include whether the Mixture-of-Laplace loss and direct-regression initialization transfer to other iterative-refinement tasks (e.g., stereo, depth) and whether the simplification to standard backbones holds at larger scales.
