## Problem

Text-guided 3D avatar generation suffers from multi-view inconsistency in 2D diffusion supervision (causing 'Janus face' artifacts), over-saturation and texture loss from standard Score Distillation Sampling, and limited geometric expressiveness when constrained to parametric models. Existing methods either lack the diversity of unconstrained 3D optimization or produce low-quality results due to conflicting multi-view signals and high classifier-free guidance weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241761/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt (e.g., 'A portrait of a [source description]') and optionally a small set of subject photos for personalization via DreamBooth fine-tuning. It produces a 3D head avatar parameterized as a conditional NeRF with both geometry (density) and texture (color), optimized simultaneously in up to 1,000 iterations at 128×128 render resolution on 4 TPUs (~15 minutes). Normal maps for the geometric prior are computed analytically from NeRF density gradients rather than rendered separately.

## Method

MagicMirror constrains the 3D optimization domain with a conditional NeRF (Preface architecture) pre-trained on 1,450 multi-view faces, providing a flexible yet bounded solution space that accelerates convergence. A second key contribution is a view-invariant geometric prior: a text-to-image diffusion model (Imagen) is fine-tuned via DreamBooth on 60 world-space normal map renderings to generate surface normals under arbitrary text prompts, enabling direct geometry optimization beyond color-only edits. The test-time objective combines Variational Score Distillation (VSD) applied to both a color diffusion model and the normal-map diffusion model, with LoRA adapters optimized alongside the NeRF parameters, avoiding the over-saturation and texture loss of standard SDS. The framework further supports concept composition by linearly combining positive and negative VSD objectives with per-concept weights, enabling semantic interpolation and element removal.

## Results

Evaluation is conducted via a human study (36 raters scoring 18 recognizable subjects on 3 rendered views each, rated 1–5 on visual quality and identity similarity) and PickScore. MagicMirror outperforms the best baseline by more than 1.5 points on both human-study dimensions, and is preferred over baselines the majority of the time in PickScore comparisons. The paper does not report a single numerical metrics table; the headline quantitative finding is the >1.5-point margin over the strongest competitor on both realism and identity-preservation scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AvatarStudio [49] (identity-preserving text-guided editing via SDS on a dynamic NeRF), MagicMirror achieves significant improvements in both visual detail and realism, largely attributed to the constrained conditional-NeRF solution space; additionally, MagicMirror only requires casual photos for DreamBooth personalization rather than camera-pose estimation. Against MVDream [60] (multi-view diffusion fine-tuned on Objaverse for general 3D asset generation), MagicMirror produces substantially more realistic and faithful avatars, while MVDream exhibits noticeable color over-saturation. Against HumanNorm [29] (normal- and depth-adapted diffusion for human geometry plus normal-aligned diffusion for texture), MagicMirror again achieves higher realism and fidelity; HumanNorm struggles with fine details such as teeth and eyes and yields a cartoon-like appearance. In all three comparisons, MagicMirror wins on visual quality, with the advantage being most pronounced in geometric detail and texture realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

MagicMirror sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) conditional volumetric priors for faces (Preface/Mip-NeRF360), which it repurposes as a constrained optimization domain rather than a reconstruction tool; (2) distillation-based 2D-to-3D lifting (DreamFusion's SDS, later refined by ProlificDreamer's VSD), which it adopts as the test-time objective; and (3) subject-driven fine-tuning of diffusion models (DreamBooth), which it extends to generate surface normal maps as a geometric prior. The key conceptual departure from prior text-to-3D avatar work (AvatarStudio, MVDream, HumanNorm) is the explicit use of a learned multi-view NeRF subspace to constrain the search space, combined with a normal-map diffusion prior that provides view-invariant geometric supervision without requiring multi-view-consistent 2D supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that constraining the 3D optimization to a learned face subspace, paired with a normal-map geometric prior, can substantially reduce the multi-view inconsistency problem that plagues unconstrained text-to-3D methods, yielding avatars with markedly better geometry and texture fidelity. However, the approach still requires a curated multi-view dataset of ~1,450 subjects to train the conditional NeRF, 4 TPUs and ~15 minutes per avatar, and multiple diffusion model instances (color + normal + optional concept mixing), limiting accessibility. Open questions include whether the constrained subspace can be expanded to handle more extreme out-of-distribution edits (e.g., non-human characters), whether the geometric prior can be made subject-agnostic without per-subject fine-tuning, and whether the 15-minute optimization can be reduced toward real-time interaction.
