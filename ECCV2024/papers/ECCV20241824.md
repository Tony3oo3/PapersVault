## Problem

Transformer-based detector-free matching methods face a fundamental trade-off: linear attention is computationally efficient but lacks expressive efficacy, while standard attention captures rich interactions at prohibitive computational cost. Additionally, existing methods lack a mechanism for global self-understanding of local features before cross-view interaction, limiting discriminability in low-texture or repetitive regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241824/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of grayscale images, each encoded as a set of points containing the pixel intensity and normalized (x, y) coordinates at 1/2 and 1/8 resolutions. It produces coarse-level correspondences at 1/8 resolution via dual-softmax and mutual nearest neighbour selection, which are then refined to sub-pixel positions at 1/2 resolution through a patch-wise coordinate expectation module. The relative positional encoding embedded in each point is essential for meaningful clustering, playing an analogous role to positional encodings in Transformers.

## Method

EcoMatcher replaces Transformer attention with Context Clusters (CoCs), a clustering-based feature extraction paradigm that captures long-range dependencies while sharing identical messages among clustered features at varying similarity-scaled magnitudes. The architecture uses a ResNet-18 backbone for local feature extraction, followed by Self-CoC blocks (with region partition removed for global receptive field) to enhance self-understanding, and Cross-CoC blocks that dispatch messages from cross-anchors in the target image to achieve cross-view interaction without an aggregating phase. A key efficiency contribution is an implementation that flattens tensors into vectors and manipulates index mappings instead of operating on large sparse similarity matrices, reducing inference time by ~50% and memory by ~62% compared to the standard CoC operation. The postprocess follows LoFTR's dual-softmax, mutual nearest neighbour, and patch-wise refinement pipeline, with focal loss at coarse level and L2 loss at fine level for supervised training.

## Results

On MegaDepth, EcoMatcher achieves AUC@5°/10°/20° of 56.5/72.0/83.4, outperforming the best semi-dense baseline ASpanFormer (55.3/71.5/83.1) and LoFTR (52.8/69.2/81.2). On ScanNet, it reaches 25.8/46.2/64.0, surpassing ASpanFormer (25.6/46.0/63.3) and LoFTR (22.0/40.8/57.6). In terms of efficiency, EcoMatcher requires only 257.88 GFLOPs and 0.081 s runtime on ScanNet (640×480), the lowest GFLOPs among all compared semi-dense methods while achieving the highest AUC@20° of 64.1. On HPatches homography estimation, EcoMatcher scores 68.0/77.8/86.4 (AUC@3/5/10 px), slightly ahead of ASpanFormer (67.4/76.9/85.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among semi-dense methods, EcoMatcher consistently outperforms all baselines on both MegaDepth and ScanNet: it leads ASpanFormer by +1.2° AUC@5° on MegaDepth and +0.2° on ScanNet, and surpasses LoFTR by a substantial +3.7° AUC@5° on ScanNet (25.8 vs. 22.0). Against sparse pipelines (SP+SG, SP+LG, DISK+NN), EcoMatcher gains roughly 6–7° AUC@5° on MegaDepth and 9–11° on ScanNet, confirming the advantage of semi-dense global interaction. The only method that outperforms EcoMatcher is the dense matcher DKM, which achieves 60.4/74.9/85.1 on MegaDepth and 29.4/50.7/68.3 on ScanNet, reflecting the inherent capacity advantage of dense correspondence. EcoMatcher also edges out ASpanFormer on HPatches homography estimation (68.0 vs. 67.4 AUC@3px) and requires 34% fewer GFLOPs (257.88 vs. 391.38) than ASpanFormer while running at comparable speed (0.081 s vs. 0.099 s on ScanNet). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that replacing Cross-CoC blocks with standard or linear attention degrades AUC@5° by 1.7 and 2.9 points respectively, validating the clustering-based interaction design.

## Relation to Prior Work

EcoMatcher departs from the dominant CNN+Transformer paradigm in detector-free matching (LoFTR, MatchFormer, ASpanFormer, QuadTree) by replacing self- and cross-attention with Context Cluster operations (Self-CoC and Cross-CoC blocks) derived from the 'Image as Set of Points' framework (Ma et al., ICLR 2023). While prior works have focused on enhancing linear attention's expressiveness through focused attention mechanisms or additional priors at increased computational cost, this paper instead introduces an entirely different interaction mechanism that shares messages via learned clustering centers. The two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline, ResNet-18 backbone, and postprocess (dual-softmax, patch-wise refinement, focal loss) are inherited directly from LoFTR, making the Context Cluster blocks the sole architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency gains: 61.9% memory reduction and ~50% inference time reduction from the efficient Context Cluster implementation, while maintaining or slightly exceeding SOTA detector-free performance (64.1 AUC@20° on ScanNet vs. 63.3 for ASpanFormer). However, the paper explicitly acknowledges it prioritizes efficient feature interaction over pushing final performance, and still trails the dense matching method DKM by a notable margin (68.3 vs. 64.0 AUC@20° on ScanNet). Open questions include whether the clustering-based interaction can be scaled to handle more challenging scenarios (large viewpoint changes, severe occlusions) where dense methods still dominate, and whether the paradigm generalizes beyond the 1/32 anchor resolution used here.
