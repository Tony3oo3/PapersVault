## Problem

Source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SFUDA) for 3D semantic segmentation suffers from a critical degradation issue: after initial gains, model performance drops sharply because the optimization is under-constrained without source data or target labels. Existing work masks this problem with rules of thumb or early stopping based on inaccessible ground truth, obscuring true method comparisons. The paper asks how to stabilize SFUDA training and select hyperparameters entirely without target ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240510/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a white-box pre-trained source model (Minkowski U-Net, 10 cm voxels, 49 BN layers), unlabeled target lidar point clouds, and the known source class distribution D^s. Only the BN layers are replaced by optimizable affine parameters (0.06% of total parameters); the rest of the network remains frozen. Beyond per-point class predictions, the framework outputs a stopping signal (first reversal in agreement with a PTBN reference model) and a validator score (agreement percentage) used to select hyperparameters like learning rate and margin without any target labels.

## Method

TTYD combines three components: (1) a training loss that sums a hinge-clipped entropy term (discriminability) and a hinge-clipped KL divergence between the predicted target class distribution and the *source* class distribution (not uniform, as in SHOT), both with margin λ=0.02; (2) an unsupervised stopping criterion that tracks class-assignment agreement A(f,g) between the adapting model f and a frozen PTBN-adapted reference model g, halting training at the first local maximum in the agreement trajectory; and (3) a self-training phase where the stopped model (TTYD_core) generates pseudo-labels via an EMA teacher with Dynamic Teacher Update for further refinement (full TTYD). The key insight is that a non-learned, hyperparameter-free BN-adapted model serves as a reliable anchor: it is better than the source-only model but does not itself degrade, making it a natural guardrail against the catastrophic drift that plagues training-based SFUDA methods.

## Results

In the strict (hyperparameter-free) SFUDA setting, TTYD_core achieves 44.5% mIoU on NS→SK10, 28.2% on SL→SK19, 35.9% on SL→SP13, 61.1% on NS→SP6, 51.4% on NS→WO10, and 63.3% on NS→PD8, outperforming the best parameterless baseline (MeanBN) by up to +8.2 pp. In the loose SFUDA setting, the full TTYD method (with self-training) reaches 45.4% on NS→SK10, 32.4% on SL→SK19, 39.1% on SL→SP13, 64.5% on NS→SP6, 55.5% on NS→WO10, and 65.7% on NS→PD8, achieving state-of-the-art performance across all six adaptation pairs. TTYD closes the gap to UDA upper bounds (CoSMix, SALUDA) to an average of 1.2 mIoU points on the four pairs where UDA results are reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against strict SFUDA baselines (AdaBN, PTBN, MeanBN), TTYD_core wins on all six adaptation pairs, with the largest margin of +8.2 pp on SL→SP13 (35.9 vs. 27.7 for MeanBN) and +4.8 pp on NS→SK10 (44.5 vs. 39.9 for AdaBN). In the loose SFUDA setting, TTYD outperforms all training-based baselines: it beats SHOT+ELR by +4.9 pp on NS→SK10 (45.4 vs. 40.5), DT-ST by +9.8 pp on NS→SK10 (45.4 vs. 35.6), and SHOT by +10.5 pp on NS→SK10 (45.4 vs. 34.9). Against UDA upper bounds, TTYD narrows the gap to an average of 1.2 mIoU (e.g., 45.4 vs. 46.2 for SALUDA on NS→SK10, 64.5 vs. 65.8 on NS→SP6). For the stopping criterion (Table 2), TTYD_stop selects checkpoints within 0.1–1.6 pp of the oracle (ground-truth) best model, consistently outperforming Entropy, SND, IM, BNM, and RankME validators by 1–3 pp on most pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SFUDA training paradigm initiated by SHOT [28] and TENT [56] by replacing the uniform class distribution constraint with a KL divergence toward the known source class distribution, and by freezing the backbone while adapting only BN layers (inspired by AdaBN/PTBN [27,39] and MixedBN from SALUDA [36]). The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an agreement-based stopping criterion that uses a low-cost, training-free reference model (PTBN) as an anchor to detect when the adapted model begins to drift, a mechanism absent from prior SFUDA methods that rely on fixed iteration counts or target-label-based early stopping. The self-training phase borrows the Dynamic Teacher Update from DT-ST [71] for stability, positioning TTYD as a composition of existing ingredients into a coherent, hyperparameter-free pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The degradation problem in SFUDA has been a persistent practical obstacle that prior work often sidestepped with target-label-dependent early stopping, making fair comparisons difficult; TTYD's agreement-based criterion provides a genuinely unsupervised solution that narrows the SFUDA-to-UDA gap to ~1.2 mIoU on average. The repurposing of the stopping criterion as a validator enables fully unsupervised hyperparameter selection, which is important for real-world deployment where target labels are unavailable. Limitations include the reliance on a reference model (even if low-cost) and the 20–40% agreement slack that caps potential improvement; open questions include whether the approach transfers to other modalities or architectures beyond sparse-voxel CNNs, and whether the source class distribution assumption holds under severe domain shift.
