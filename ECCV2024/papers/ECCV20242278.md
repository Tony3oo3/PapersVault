## Problem

Existing full-body generation methods either produce clothing uncorrelated with the scene (StyleGAN-based methods trained on studio/fashion data) or require explicit text prompts to specify appropriate attire (text-to-image diffusion models). The paper asks whether visual scene context alone—without any textual description—can drive the generation of a person wearing semantically appropriate, environment-inspired clothing that can be seamlessly inpainted into the original photograph.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a context image (environment photo with the human removed via segmentation masks) and a 2D pose skeleton rendering (32×64) as input. It produces a 512×512 inpainted image where a full-body person in the specified pose is inserted into the scene wearing context-appropriate clothing, without altering the original background. Internally, the pipeline generates a 3-channel human parsing map (HPM) at 32×64 (tall format to minimize background pixels) which is then upscaled to 512×512 as guidance for the diffusion inpainting step.

## Method

ESP is a three-stage pipeline combining VAE, StyleGAN2, and latent diffusion. First, a VAE encodes the masked context image (64×64) into a 512-d context embedding capturing global structure, illumination, and color tone. Second, a StyleGAN2 generator conditioned on the concatenation of this embedding with a 512-d random vector (forming a 'contextual style vector' that captures the one-to-many mapping from environment to outfit) and a 2D pose (injected via pix2pix-style feature concatenation into style blocks) jointly generates an HPM and a blurry background reconstruction, with a composite (HPM, background) discriminator enforcing the clothing-context link. Third, a ControlNet module trained on ~23K HPM-to-person image pairs provides semantic guidance to a pre-trained Stable Diffusion inpainting model, which synthesizes the final 512×512 person while preserving the original background; the ControlNet strength β (set to 0.5) balances pose/clothing fidelity against image quality.

## Results

On the AHP dataset, ESP achieves a FID of 21.0 at β=0.5 for inpainting environment-specific people, compared to 78.7 for the 2D-pose-conditioned ControlNet baseline using the same Stable Diffusion inpainting model — a 3.7× improvement. At higher guidance strengths (β=0.75, β=1), ESP maintains FIDs of 19.8 and 20.2 respectively, while the baseline degrades to 86.7 and 90.0. In a user study with 16 participants, ESP outputs were preferred 62% of the time over the pose-conditioned LDM baseline, with only 3% undecided. The context-awareness ablation (Table 1) confirms that fixing the context vector yields substantially better background reconstruction (PSNR 23.33 vs. 11.81; LPIPS 0.123 vs. 0.361) and more uniform, semantically appropriate clothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESP is compared against three baselines in Figure 4 and Table 3. Against StyleGAN-Human [13] (unconditional GAN), ESP produces contextually appropriate clothing and correct lighting, whereas StyleGAN-Human generates people with no scene–clothing correlation due to its studio-style training data. Against TextHuman [21] (dense-pose-conditioned GAN), ESP avoids missing body parts and respects the scene context in terms of clothing semantics and color harmonization. The most direct quantitative comparison is against the 2D-pose-conditioned ControlNet + SD-2.1-Inpainting baseline [55]: ESP achieves FID 21.0 vs. 78.7 at β=0.5 (a 3.7× gap), and the gap widens at higher β values (19.8 vs. 86.7 at β=0.75; 20.2 vs. 90.0 at β=1). Qualitatively, the ControlNet baseline frequently fails to respect the input pose, alters the original scene by inserting extraneous objects, and sometimes generates multiple humans, while ESP preserves the background, generates a single correctly-posed person, and achieves higher contextual alignment. In the perceptual user study, ESP was preferred over the ControlNet baseline 62% of the time.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESP builds on three established threads: StyleGAN-based full-body generation (InsetGAN, StyleGAN-Human), which produces high-quality humans but lacks scene context; diffusion-based inpainting (Stable Diffusion, ControlNet), which can fill masked regions but requires text prompts or ignores clothing semantics; and scene-compatible human generation (Brooks et al., Kulal et al.), which focuses on pose compatibility rather than environment-driven clothing. The paper's key departure is introducing 'contextual style vectors'—VAE-encoded scene embeddings concatenated with random noise—that drive a StyleGAN2 HPM generator to predict clothing semantics purely from visual context, then using those HPMs as tight semantic masks (rather than binary masks) to guide ControlNet-conditioned Stable Diffusion inpainting. This positions ESP as a purely image-based, text-free pipeline that unifies GAN-based semantic prediction with diffusion-based photorealistic synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ESP demonstrates that visual scene context alone, without any textual description, is sufficient to drive generation of semantically appropriate clothing, which removes a significant practical barrier for automated photo editing and virtual try-on applications. However, the method is limited to simple poses, depends heavily on the quality of the generic Stable Diffusion inpainting model, and inherits demographic biases from its ~23K-image training set (noted by the authors as a concern). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to complex or occluded poses, whether the small in-the-wild dataset can be scaled to cover more diverse environments and demographics, and whether the HPM-as-mask strategy can be extended to multi-person scenes.
