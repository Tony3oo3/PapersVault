## Problem

Recovering a person's complete 3D motion (global trajectory and body kinematics) from in-the-wild videos with moving cameras remains unsolved. Prior approaches either reconstruct humans only in the camera frame, losing world-space context, or rely on MoCap-derived locomotion priors to estimate trajectory scale, which fails on complex real-world motions like stairs or parkour. Additionally, standard monocular SLAM diverges when dynamic humans occupy large foreground regions and cannot produce metric-scale trajectories without external references.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video with known focal length as input and produces the full 3D human motion in world space: an SE(3) root trajectory {H_t} in the world frame and a sequence of SMPL parameters (23 joint rotations, 10 shape coefficients, root orientation and translation) in the camera frame. The intermediate outputs include a metric-scale camera trajectory and per-frame scene depth. The human is always reconstructed relative to the camera, and the world-frame trajectory is obtained by composing the camera motion with the camera-relative human position.

## Method

TRAM decomposes the problem into two stages. For camera trajectory, it robustifies DROID-SLAM with dual masking (zeroing dynamic regions in both input images and the dense bundle adjustment confidence map) to prevent divergence from moving humans, then estimates metric scale by robustly aligning SLAM depth to ZoeDepth metric depth predictions per keyframe (German-McClure loss, BFGS optimization, median over keyframes, far-region thresholding). For body motion, VIMO builds on the frozen ViT-Huge backbone of HMR2.0 and adds two temporal transformers: one applies attention across time on each spatial patch token (factorized spatial-temporal modeling in the image domain), and the other encodes and decodes a sequence of SMPL poses directly (motion-domain denoising). The two temporal transformers and the original transformer decoder are finetuned end-to-end on video data (3DPW, Human3.6M, BEDLAM) with 2D reprojection, 3D joint, SMPL parameter, and vertex losses, while the ViT backbone remains frozen to preserve pre-trained representations.

## Results

On EMDB, TRAM achieves a root translation error (RTE) of 1.4%, a 60% reduction over the best prior method WHAM (4.6%), and a 92% reduction over TRACE (17.7%). For global pose, TRAM reaches WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ of 76.4 mm and W-MPJPE₁₀₀ of 222.4 mm, substantially outperforming WHAM (133.3 mm and 343.9 mm). For local body motion, VIMO achieves the best PA-MPJPE of 35.6 mm on 3DPW and 45.7 mm on EMDB, with the lowest acceleration error (4.9 m/s²) among all temporal methods, confirming superior motion smoothness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For camera trajectory, Masked DROID-SLAM (TRAM's SLAM component) achieves 0.32 m ATE on EMDB, dramatically outperforming vanilla DROID-SLAM (2.42 m) and ORB-SLAM2 (1.05 m, which also fails in 9/25 sequences). For global human trajectory, TRAM's 1.4% RTE beats WHAM (4.6%) by 3.2×, SLAHMR (10.2%) by 7.3×, GLAMR (11.4%) by 8.1×, and TRACE (17.7%) by 12.6×, with the largest gains on long-range and curved trajectories where MoCap-based priors fail. For local body motion, VIMO (PA-MPJPE 35.6 mm on 3DPW, 45.7 mm on EMDB) edges out WHAM-ViT (35.9/50.4 mm) and clearly surpasses all per-frame methods (HMR2.0: 44.4/60.7 mm; SPIN: 59.2/87.1 mm) and other temporal methods (VIBE: 51.9/81.6 mm; GLoT: 50.6/79.1 mm; MPS-Net: 52.1/81.4 mm; TCMR: 52.7/79.8 mm). VIMO also achieves the best acceleration error (4.9 m/s²) among all temporal baselines, indicating smoother reconstructed motion. The paper's key differentiator is that TRAM avoids human motion priors for trajectory estimation entirely, relying instead on scene-background SLAM, which explains its robustness to out-of-distribution motions (stairs, skateboarding) where WHAM and TRACE degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRAM builds on two well-established pillars: DROID-SLAM for monocular camera trajectory recovery and HMR2.0 (a large pre-trained ViT-H-based regressor) for per-frame 3D human pose and shape. The key conceptual departure is from prior human-centric methods (GLAMR, SLAHMR, WHAM, TRACE) that rely on MoCap-derived locomotion priors to estimate metric scale and global trajectory; TRAM instead adopts a scene-centric viewpoint, deriving scale from background semantic cues via metric depth prediction and decoupling the problem into robust SLAM plus a video transformer (VIMO) for local body motion. The dual-masking modification to DROID-SLAM and the two-temporal-transformer extension of HMR2.0 are targeted architectural contributions rather than new paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60% reduction in root trajectory error over prior methods is practically significant for applications requiring metric-scale human motion in the world (gait analysis, imitation learning, scene interaction reasoning), and the method generalizes to complex trajectories (stairs, parkour, skateboarding) where MoCap-prior-based methods fail. Key limitations remain: the method requires known focal length, depends on the accuracy of monocular metric depth prediction for scale, and enforces a strict separation between camera and human motion that precludes joint physics-based refinement (e.g., foot sliding). Future work on focal length estimation during bundle adjustment and joint optimization with physics priors are natural next steps.
