## Problem

Existing story visualization models rely on autoregressive, frame-by-frame generation that only supports unidirectional synthesis, making story backtracking, insertion, and replacement infeasible. They also train and infer separate models for story visualization and continuation, forcing users to switch between large models, and they introduce heavy multimodal encoders (e.g., BLIP) to process history frames, inflating computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241507/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of per-frame story captions (text) and, optionally, a subset of already-generated or user-provided story frames at arbitrary positions. Individual frames are 256×256 RGB images arranged into a single 512×512 four-panel storyboard, which is the unit of generation. At inference, the user specifies which frames to synthesize via a binary mask; unmasked frames are preserved while masked frames are denoised, and final 256×256 frames are cropped from the completed storyboard.

## Method

StoryImager reframes story generation as single-image storyboard synthesis on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone, exploiting the model's pre-trained ability to produce multi-panel compositions. A Target Frame Masking Strategy unifies story visualization (all frames masked) and story completion (partial masking) by adding noise only to masked regions and computing the diffusion loss exclusively on those regions, so one model handles all tasks. A Frame-Story Cross Attention Module decomposes each U-Net cross-attention layer into a frame-level branch (aligning each panel's latent with its caption) and a storyboard-level branch (aligning the full latent with global context), implemented as two LoRA adapters (α=4, r=128) on the pretrained weights. A Contextual Feature Extractor uses learnable context and prior query embeddings through a two-layer Transformer to produce a global context vector and a frame-aware latent prior (4×64×64), replacing the need for external encoders like BLIP. The entire U-Net is fine-tuned via LoRA while the autoencoder and text encoder remain frozen, reducing training to ~30 hours on 8×A6000 GPUs versus ~104 hours for the autoregressive AR-LDM baseline.

## Results

On Pororo-SV, StoryImager achieves FID 15.63 / FSD 28.13 for story visualization and FID 15.45 / FSD 27.10 for story continuation, outperforming the strongest prior method AR-LDM (FID 16.59 / FSD 35.33 and FID 17.40 / FSD 37.52 respectively). On Flintstones-SV, StoryImager reaches FID 22.27 / FSD 36.51 (visualization) and FID 18.32 / FSD 35.33 (continuation), again surpassing AR-LDM (FID 23.59 / FSD 39.70 and FID 19.28 / FSD 43.32). StoryImager is also the first model to support story completion, achieving FID 14.72 / FSD 26.77 on Pororo-SV and FID 18.11 / FSD 34.20 on Flintstones-SV. In terms of efficiency, StoryImager requires only 12 GB GPU memory for training and 8 GB for inference (vs. 38+38 GB and 16+16 GB for AR-LDM), with 8.0 s inference time versus 14.5 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StoryImager outperforms all nine baselines on every supported task and dataset. Against the strongest autoregressive baseline AR-LDM [26], StoryImager improves FSD by 7.2 points on Pororo-SV visualization (28.13 vs. 35.33) and by 7.99 points on Flintstones-SV continuation (35.33 vs. 43.32), while also achieving lower FID in all four task-dataset combinations. Causal-Story [36], which adds causal reasoning to AR-LDM, reports only FID (no FSD); StoryImager still beats it on FID for both visualization (15.63 vs. 16.28 on Pororo-SV) and continuation (18.32 vs. 19.03 on Flintstones-SV). Make-A-Story [29] and the earlier GAN-based methods (StoryGAN, CP-CSV, DUCO, VLC, WL-SV) are substantially outperformed, with FSD gaps exceeding 20 points on Pororo-SV visualization. StoryImager is the only method that supports story completion (backtracking and infilling), a task none of the nine baselines can handle. In human evaluation, StoryImager also ranks best on Visual Consistency (e.g., 1.45 vs. 1.92 for AR-LDM on Pororo-SV), confirming the quantitative FSD advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant autoregressive story-generation paradigm (AR-LDM, Make-A-Story, StoryDALL-E) that transforms pre-trained text-to-image models into frame-by-frame generators with unidirectional attention. Instead, it leverages the observation that Stable Diffusion already possesses a latent storyboard-generative ability from pretraining, and fine-tunes it via LoRA to produce all frames simultaneously in a single 512×512 storyboard image. The Target Frame Masking Strategy unifies story visualization, continuation, and the newly defined 'completion' task (backtracking, insertion, replacement) into a single masked-diffusion objective, eliminating the need for separate models. The Frame-Story Cross Attention Module replaces the heavy BLIP-based history encoders used by AR-LDM with a decomposed local/global cross-attention, reducing both parameter count and computational cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-model framework with bidirectional generation is practically significant: users no longer need to load, store, or switch between separate models for visualization vs. continuation, and can now perform story editing operations (frame insertion, replacement, backtracking) that were impossible under autoregressive architectures. The efficiency gains (12G vs. 76G training memory, 8G vs. 32G inference memory, ~45% speedup) make the approach accessible on consumer hardware. However, the evaluation is limited to two cartoon datasets with only 4 frames per story at 256×256 per frame, leaving open questions about scalability to longer narratives, photorealistic content, and open-ended story generation. Whether the storyboard-based approach generalizes to more than 4–6 frames or to higher resolutions remains untested.
