## Problem

Existing 3D hand recovery methods perform poorly on motion-blurred hand images, and the only prior dataset (BlurHand) uses synthetic blur from frame averaging that doesn't match real-world blur characteristics. Moreover, a single blurry image inherently contains multiple hand positions along a trajectory, creating temporal ambiguity that makes it impossible to determine the hand's exact pose at any specific time step without additional temporal information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241581/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

EBHNet accepts a 256×256×3 blurry hand image, a color-encoded event stream (accumulated in 1 ms windows from a Prophesee EVK4 camera), or both simultaneously. The output is a sequence of 3D hand meshes parameterized by MANO pose and shape, along with 21 3D joint coordinates, generated at user-specified normalized time steps t ∈ [0, 1] within the 80 ms exposure window. A key design choice is that the same network weights produce hands at arbitrary time steps by varying only the scalar temporal embedding, so no additional parameters are needed for extra outputs.

## Method

EBHNet extracts image features via a pre-trained FPN and event features via shallow convolutions, concatenates them (when both are available), and fuses them through five residual blocks into a single feature map F. The core contribution is a Transformer-based temporal splitter: the scalar temporal embedding t is appended to the channel dimension of F and consolidated via a 1×1 convolution, then self-attention (query/key/value derived from three separate 1×1 convolutions) produces a time-specific feature F_t. During training, t is restricted to the discrete ground-truth time steps (e.g., {0, 0.2, 0.6, 0.8}); during testing, t can take any continuous value in [0, 1], enabling prediction at novel, unsupervised time steps without retraining. From F_t, 3D joints are obtained via soft-argmax over depth-discretized heatmaps, and MANO pose/shape are regressed through grid-sampling and fully connected layers. For image-only input, temporal ambiguity is handled by computing losses on both the original and temporally reversed ground truth and selecting the smaller one.

## Results

On the proposed EBH dataset with image-only input, EBHNet achieves MPJPE of 14.29 mm at t=0.2 and 15.23 mm at t=0.6, outperforming BlurHandNet (15.53 and 16.13 mm) and Pose2Pose (17.28 mm at t=0.6). With both image and event inputs, EBHNet reaches MPJPE of 12.00 mm at t=0.2 and 14.24 mm at t=0.6, with MPVPE of 16.93 mm and acceleration error of 3.19, the best across all settings. On the synthetic BlurHand dataset, EBHNet achieves MPJPE of 16.45 mm (mid) and MPVPE of 15.02 mm, improving over BlurHandNet (16.80 and 15.30) and substantially over I2L-MeshNet (24.32 MPJPE). EBHNet also generates 3D hands at novel time steps (e.g., t=0.4) without additional training, achieving 14.03 MPJPE with I&E input versus 14.66 for linear interpolation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBHNet consistently outperforms all five baselines. Against I2L-MeshNet [22] and Pose2Pose [20], which do not account for motion blur, EBHNet achieves substantially lower MPJPE (15.23 vs. 28.12 and 17.28 mm at t=0.6 on EBH), confirming that blur-aware modeling is critical. METRO [12] (20.54 MPJPE on BlurHand mid) also fails to handle blur, trailing EBHNet by over 4 mm. The closest competitor is BlurHandNet [27], which EBHNet beats by 0.9–1.3 mm MPJPE across time steps on EBH and by 0.35–1.04 mm on BlurHand, while also reducing acceleration error (4.69 vs. 5.78 on EBH) and supporting novel time-step generation that BlurHandNet cannot. For event-only input, EBHNet marginally outperforms EventHand [35] (28.59 vs. 28.91 MPJPE at t=0.6, 25.30 vs. 25.70 MPVPE), with a larger gap in acceleration error (9.25 vs. 10.75). The combined I&E input yields the strongest results overall, with EBHNet achieving 12.00 MPJPE at t=0.2—nearly 4 mm better than image-only—demonstrating the complementary value of event streams for resolving temporal ambiguity in blurry hands.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D hand recovery line of work (I2L-MeshNet, Pose2Pose, METRO) by addressing motion blur, a gap explicitly noted as underexplored. It departs from BlurHand [27], the closest prior work, in two key ways: replacing synthetic blur (frame averaging) with real motion blur captured via extended-exposure cameras, and augmenting the input with event camera streams to resolve temporal ambiguity. The temporal splitter—a Transformer-based module with a simple channel-concatenated temporal embedding—repurposes self-attention for per-time-step hand extraction from a shared feature, contrasting with BlurHandNet's dedicated per-step modules. The event stream integration draws from the event-camera 3D tracking literature (EventHand, differentiable event simulators) but applies it specifically to complement blurry RGB rather than replace it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EBH dataset is the first to provide real motion-blurred hand images paired with multi-view 3D ground truth and continuous event streams, filling a critical gap for training and evaluating blur-robust hand recovery. The temporal splitter's ability to generate 3D hands at arbitrary, untrained time steps from a single shared module is a practical advantage over fixed-step architectures, enabling denser hand sequences without parameter inflation. However, the dataset's limited shape diversity (10 subjects) constrains generalization, and the moderate MPJPE improvements over BlurHandNet (1–2 mm) suggest the task is not yet fully solved. Open questions include scaling to diverse hand shapes, handling occlusions in blurry sequences, and whether the event-camera dependency limits real-world deployability.
