## Problem

Existing tri-modal 3D pre-training methods (e.g., OpenShape, Uni3D) align point cloud representations with image-text pairs only at the instance level, ignoring the intricate intra-modal and cross-modal structural relations already encoded in CLIP's pre-aligned embedding space. This neglect leads to underutilization of CLIP's learned priors and produces fragmented, less discriminative 3D representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240882/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes synthesized triplets of 3D point clouds (10,000 points with coordinates and color for ModelNet/Objaverse; 2,048 points for ScanObjectNN), 2D images rendered from 12 preset camera angles, and text descriptions sourced from curated captions, LLM-generated descriptions, and retrieval data. The frozen CLIP ViT-BigG-14 encoders process the image and text branches, while only the trainable PointBERT 3D encoder is optimized. The output is a unified 3D shape embedding usable for zero-shot classification (top-k class predictions) and text-to-3D retrieval (ranking 3D shapes by relevance to an external text query).

## Method

MRD introduces a relational distillation objective on top of standard instance-level contrastive alignment. It defines three candidate relation representations—Euclidean distance, normalized similarity, and partial order—each computed for both intra-modal (within a single modality) and cross-modal (between two modalities) sample pairs. The key insight is that the 3D modality should not merely match its corresponding image/text instance but should also replicate the pairwise relational structure observed in the frozen CLIP space. To handle the inherent distribution mismatch between image and text modalities, MRD employs dynamic distillation: learnable weights (α, β, γ), parameterized via softmax of DARTS-style architecture parameters, are optimized to balance which teacher relation (e.g., image-image vs. text-text for intra-modal; image-text vs. text-image for cross-modal) the 3D relations should mimic. Empirically, normalized similarity with Jeffrey divergence as the distillation loss outperforms the other forms, and the full loss combines instance-level InfoNCE alignment with the weighted intra- and cross-modal relation distillation terms scaled by a hyperparameter λ.

## Results

On the Ensembled training setting, MRD-B (88.4M params) achieves 53.2% top-1 accuracy on Objaverse-LVIS, 88.8% on ModelNet40, and 63.7% on ScanObjectNN, outperforming the same-size Uni3D-B (51.7%, 86.3%, 63.8%) and even the much larger Uni3D-L (306.7M; 53.1%, 86.3%, 58.2%). Scaling to MRD-L (306.7M) further improves Objaverse to 53.6% and ScanObjectNN to 64.1%. On cross-modality text-to-3D retrieval, MRD achieves 20.5% instance-level and 57.9% category-level top-1 accuracy, surpassing Uni3D (18.9% / 55.9%) and OpenShape (15.4% / 49.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRD-B (88.4M) matches Uni3D-B in parameter count but wins on Objaverse by 1.5% (53.2 vs 51.7) and ModelNet40 by 2.5% (88.8 vs 86.3), while essentially tying on ScanObjectNN (63.7 vs 63.8). Against the 3.5× larger Uni3D-L (306.7M), MRD-B still leads on Objaverse (53.2 vs 53.1) and ModelNet40 (88.8 vs 86.3), and pulls ahead by 5.5% on ScanObjectNN (63.7 vs 58.2). At the smaller scale, MRD-S (22.6M) trails OpenShape+PointBERT (32.3M) on Objaverse (45.2 vs 46.8) but substantially outperforms it on ModelNet40 (88.5 vs 84.4) and ScanObjectNN (63.0 vs 52.2). On cross-modality retrieval, MRD consistently leads both Uni3D and OpenShape across all instance-level and category-level metrics, with the largest gap over OpenShape reaching 12.3% in category-level top-1 accuracy (57.9 vs 49.6). Overall, MRD achieves the best or near-best results across all three benchmarks and both evaluation tasks, with the most pronounced gains on the harder ScanObjectNN and Objaverse datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tri-modal contrastive pre-training line (CG3D, ULIP, OpenShape, Uni3D) that aligns 3D point cloud features with CLIP's pre-aligned image-text space, but departs from their instance-level-only alignment by additionally distilling inter-sample relational structure (intra-modal and cross-modal) from the frozen CLIP encoders into the 3D backbone. It also draws on the relation-modeling literature in contrastive learning (CyCLIP, RINCE, Soft-InfoNCE), which has been applied to 2D or bi-modal settings, and generalizes it to the tri-modal 3D case where the 3D modality must simultaneously reconcile relations from both image and text. The dynamic distillation mechanism, inspired by differentiable architecture search, is a novel addition to handle the modality-gap conflict between image and text relational signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that instance-level alignment alone underutilizes the rich structural priors in CLIP's embedding space, and that explicitly preserving relational geometry yields consistently better 3D representations across zero-shot classification and cross-modal retrieval. The finding that naively combining intra- and cross-modal relation constraints without dynamic weighting actually degrades performance highlights a non-trivial conflict problem in multi-modal distillation that the field has not previously addressed. Open questions include whether relation distillation generalizes beyond CLIP-based teachers, whether finer-grained (patch-level or part-level) relations would yield further gains, and whether the dynamic weighting strategy transfers across different 3D backbones or training scales.
