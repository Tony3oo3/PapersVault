## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces millions of Gaussians with an inefficient spatial distribution characterized by 'overlapping' (clustering in some areas) and 'under-reconstruction' (blurry artifacts in others), which limits both rendering quality and speed. Existing compression approaches (pruning by opacity, scale, or gradient sensitivity) reduce Gaussian count but ignore this spatial redundancy, yielding suboptimal simplification. The paper tackles the underexplored problem of representing scenes with a constrained number of Gaussians while preserving rendering fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and an SfM sparse point cloud (same initialization as 3DGS) and produces a compact 3D Gaussian scene representation (e.g., ~0.5M Gaussians vs. ~3.4M for vanilla 3DGS on Mip-NeRF360) that renders novel views in real time. During depth reinitialization, approximately 3.5 million depth points are generated per scene and used to reorganize Gaussian positions. A storage-compression variant (Mini-Splatting-C) further encodes the representation using transform coding and zip compression for compact deployment.

## Method

Mini-Splatting reorganizes Gaussian spatial positions through a two-stage densification-then-simplification pipeline rather than directly pruning. Densification comprises (1) blur split, which identifies Gaussians whose maximum-contribution area exceeds a threshold (indicating under-reconstruction) and splits them, and (2) depth reinitialization, which renders per-pixel depth using the mid-point of ray-ellipsoid intersection (avoiding alpha-blending artifacts), reprojection to world space, and repeated reinitialization of the Gaussian set from these dense depth points. Simplification then applies (1) intersection preserving, retaining only Gaussians that are the maximum contributor to at least one pixel across all views, and (2) importance-weighted stochastic sampling based on accumulated blending weights, which preserves local geometry better than deterministic pruning. The entire pipeline integrates into the standard 3DGS rasterization and optimization loop with 30K training steps, enabling SH coefficients only after simplification to reduce densification cost.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, Mini-Splatting-D achieves SSIM 0.831, PSNR 27.51 dB, and LPIPS 0.176 with 4.69M Gaussians, outperforming the 3DGS baseline (SSIM 0.815, PSNR 27.47, LPIPS 0.216, 3.35M) on all three metrics. The constrained Mini-Splatting variant uses only 0.49M Gaussians (~7× fewer) while maintaining comparable quality (SSIM 0.822, PSNR 27.34, LPIPS 0.217). On Deep Blending, Mini-Splatting actually surpasses 3DGS* in both PSNR (29.98 vs 29.54) and SSIM (0.908 vs 0.904) despite using only 0.35M vs 2.82M Gaussians. In resource consumption, Mini-Splatting reduces training time from 30m8s to 17m56s and rendering memory from 2.79 GB to 0.40 GB on outdoor scenes, while achieving 410 FPS vs 98 FPS for 3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary 3DGS baseline [17], Mini-Splatting-D wins on SSIM and LPIPS across all three datasets (e.g., LPIPS 0.176 vs 0.216 on Mip-NeRF360), while Mini-Splatting matches 3DGS quality with ~7× fewer Gaussians. Mini-Splatting-D surpasses Zip-NeRF [3] on Mip-NeRF360 in SSIM (0.831 vs 0.828) and LPIPS (0.176 vs 0.189), though Zip-NeRF retains a PSNR advantage (28.54 vs 27.51). Both Mini-Splatting variants clearly outperform the NeRF-based baselines Plenoxels [40], INGP-Big [26], and Mip-NeRF 360 [2] on all metrics. In the number-quality curves (Fig. 7), Mini-Splatting outperforms VQ (3DGS + voxel pruning from Li et al. [21]), LightGaussian [11], and Lee et al. [20] at comparable Gaussian counts, indicating that reorganizing spatial distribution is more effective than direct pruning. For storage compression (Fig. 9), Mini-Splatting-C achieves superior rate-distortion performance compared to Niedermayr et al. [27] and Lee et al. [20], which rely solely on pruning-based compression. The one notable weakness is on Tanks&Temples, where Mini-Splatting-D's PSNR (23.23) falls below 3DGS* (23.66), attributed to large sky areas that the depth-based strategy does not model well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by Kerbl et al., extending its adaptive density control with a reorganization strategy that departs from the direct-pruning approach taken by prior compression works (LightGaussian, Lee et al., Niedermayr et al.). Conceptually, it pivots the simplification problem from 'which Gaussians to remove' to 'where to place a constrained set of Gaussians,' drawing on point cloud simplification techniques (importance-weighted sampling, geometric preservation) and depth-based reinitialization ideas from NeRF literature. The key methodological departure is the two-phase pipeline: first densify via blur split and depth reinitialization to fix spatial distribution, then simplify via intersection preserving and sampling — rather than pruning a vanilla 3DGS model directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies that the spatial distribution of Gaussians (overlapping and under-reconstruction) is a fundamental bottleneck limiting both rendering quality and efficiency, shifting the community's focus from merely reducing Gaussian count to optimizing their placement. The demonstrated 7× reduction in Gaussians with comparable quality, and successful training on a GTX 1060 6G, point toward practical on-device neural rendering. Open questions include whether the depth-based reinitialization generalizes to dynamic scenes, how the method handles large sky/empty regions (where it degrades on Tanks&Temples), and whether the reorganization strategy can be made fully differentiable or integrated into a single optimization pass rather than the current multi-stage pipeline.
