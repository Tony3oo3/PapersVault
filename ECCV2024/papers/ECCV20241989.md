## Problem

Existing multi-view hand reconstruction methods such as POEM achieve high accuracy but rely on computationally intensive cross-set feature interactions (e.g., Transformers) that prevent real-time inference. The paper asks whether a real-time multi-view single-hand reconstruction method can be built without sacrificing reconstruction accuracy, and answers affirmatively with MLPHand, the first such method operating at 71 FPS on an RTX 3090.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241989/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N (typically 4–8) RGB images at 256×256 resolution from cameras with known intrinsic and extrinsic matrices. It first estimates 21-joint 2D hand skeletons per view via a CNN backbone (ResNet34/18 or MobileNetV2) with soft-argmax, triangulates them into a 3D reference skeleton via DLT, and ultimately outputs a MANO-style hand mesh of 778×3 vertices. The intermediate 3D skeleton (21×3) is also available as a by-product, and the mesh is produced by per-bone offset regression rather than direct vertex regression.

## Method

MLPHand comprises two MLP-based modules. The Skeleton2Mesh model decomposes the non-convex hand into 20 bone-wise convex components and uses a parameter-sharing architecture: each bone is identified by a one-hot order code plus position-encoded endpoint coordinates and a learnable Global Spatial Descriptor, and three parallel 3-layer MLPs (one per spatial axis, exploiting coordinate-separability of rigid transforms) regress 100×3 vertex offsets relative to the bone midpoint. The Multi-view Geometry Feature Fusion Prediction (MGFP) module then injects multi-view visual features into the frozen Skeleton2Mesh via a Multi-view Feature Infuser that mirrors the offset-regression layers with trainable counterparts and Zero-FC layers (adapted from ControlNet) to add geometry detail without destabilizing the pretrained weights. Training is two-stage: first the Skeleton2Mesh is trained on ground-truth skeleton–mesh pairs (300 epochs), then the full pipeline is fine-tuned end-to-end on multi-view images (100 epochs). This design reduces Multi-Adds by ~90% and parameters by ~75% relative to POEM while maintaining comparable MPVPE/MPJPE.

## Results

On OakInk-MV with ResNet34, MLPHand achieves 6.53 mm MPVPE and 6.37 mm MPJPE at 57 FPS, compared to POEM's 6.2 mm MPVPE at 19 FPS and MVP's 9.69 mm MPVPE at 22 FPS. With the lighter ResNet18 backbone, MLPHand reaches 71 FPS while reducing parameters to 16.13M (75% fewer than POEM's 40.49M) and Multi-Adds to 14.97G (52% fewer than POEM's 31.35G). On DexYCB-MV, MLPHand (ResNet34) achieves 6.20 mm MPVPE at 54 FPS versus POEM's 6.13 mm at 16 FPS. On the more challenging HO3D-MV, MLPHand (ResNet34) reports 18.69 mm MPVPE at 58 FPS, slightly behind POEM's 17.2 mm at 18 FPS but substantially ahead of MVP's 20.95 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLPHand is compared against two multi-view hand reconstruction baselines: MVP [53] (a direct multi-view multi-person 3D pose estimator) and POEM [51] (a point-embedded multi-view stereo hand reconstructor). Against MVP, MLPHand clearly wins on all accuracy metrics across all three datasets (e.g., 6.53 vs. 9.69 mm MPVPE on OakInk-MV) while also being 2.6–3× faster (57 vs. 22 FPS). Against POEM, MLPHand trades a small accuracy gap for a large speed gain: on OakInk-MV, MPVPE is 6.53 vs. 6.20 mm (≈0.3 mm worse) but FPS jumps from 19 to 57 (3× faster); on DexYCB-MV the gap is even smaller (6.20 vs. 6.13 mm MPVPE) with 54 vs. 16 FPS. On the harder HO3D-MV dataset, the accuracy gap widens (18.69 vs. 17.20 mm MPVPE with ResNet34), but MLPHand still runs at 58 FPS versus POEM's 18 FPS. In terms of model size, MLPHand (ResNet34) uses 26.24M parameters versus POEM's 40.49M (35% fewer) and MVP's 53.57M (51% fewer), and the skeleton-to-mesh sub-module alone reduces Multi-Adds from POEM's 6.76G to 0.05G (99% reduction) per Table 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

MLPHand sits at the intersection of two research threads: MLP-based implicit hand geometry modeling (HALO, LISA, COAP, NASA) and multi-view hand reconstruction (POEM, MVP). It departs from POEM's cross-set Transformer for multi-view fusion by replacing it with a lightweight per-bone MLP skeleton-to-mesh mapping plus a ControlNet-style Zero-FC feature infuser, drawing the conditional-control mechanism directly from Zhang et al. [54]. The per-bone parameter-sharing strategy with one-hot order encoding is a deliberate simplification of prior implicit per-bone models that used separate networks per bone, reducing parameters by roughly an order of magnitude.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 71 FPS with ~90% fewer FLOPs while matching POEM's accuracy within ~0.5 mm MPVPE, MLPHand makes multi-view hand reconstruction viable for latency-sensitive VR/AR pipelines where prior methods were impractical. The two-stage training (pre-train Skeleton2Mesh on GT skeleton-mesh pairs, then fine-tune end-to-end) is a practical recipe that decouples geometry learning from multi-view fusion, and the robustness experiments suggest the Skeleton2Mesh module is a drop-in component compatible with any upstream pose estimator. Open questions include whether the small accuracy gap to POEM can be closed by a stronger skeleton estimator, whether the approach extends to two-hand or hand-object interaction, and whether the per-bone convex decomposition generalizes to more complex articulated shapes.
