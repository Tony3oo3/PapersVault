## Problem

NeRF and 3DGS methods assume static scenes with consistent lighting and persistent object positions, but real-world multi-view captures frequently contain distractors (moving people, objects, illumination changes) that violate this assumption and cause severe rendering artifacts. Existing distractor-removal approaches either require semantic segmentation priors, hand-crafted loss ratios, or degrade significantly when applied to clean scenes, limiting their practical versatility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (which may or may not contain distractors) along with their camera poses, and produces a per-pixel purity attention mask with discrete values in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1} that modulates the reconstruction loss during training. The mask is computed hierarchically across seven spatial scales (pixel-wise kernels of size 1, 3, 5, 7 and tile-wise pooling of size 4, 8, 16), and the final rendered output is a distractor-free novel view. No prior identification of distractor content is needed; MemE adapts automatically whether or not distractors are present.

## Method

MemE exploits the memorization effect observed in NeRF/3DGS optimization, where parameters first fit clean (purity) pixels and only later overfit to distractor pixels. It operates through three modules: (1) Hierarchical Residual Measurement computes L2 residuals between rendered and input images, then applies multi-scale pixel-wise average convolutions and tile-wise average pooling to produce residual vectors at seven spatial levels; (2) Purity-Distractor Mixture Modeling fits a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model to the residual distribution at each scale via the EM algorithm, updated every T training steps to track the evolving bimodal separation; (3) Purity Attention Generation derives adaptive thresholds that exponentially interpolate from the distractor mean to the purity mean over training, produces per-scale binary masks, and fuses them (OR for fine-grained pixel-wise masks, then averaging with coarse tile-wise masks) into the final attention map. This mask weights the L2 reconstruction loss so that distractor pixels are progressively excluded from gradient updates, preventing overfitting without requiring any external supervision or pre-trained model.

## Results

On the RobustNeRF dataset, MemE-NeRF (64 patches) achieves PSNR of 21.99 (Statue), 21.74 (Android), 30.64 (Crab), and 31.36 (BabyYoda), surpassing RobustNeRF by up to 1.1 dB (Statue) and 0.49 dB (BabyYoda). On the Kubric dataset, MemE-NeRF reaches PSNR of 40.86 (Car), 44.34 (Cars), 48.79 (Bag), 47.87 (Chairs), and 45.97 (Pillow), outperforming RobustNeRF by up to 6.97 dB (Bag) and 6.74 dB (Chairs). MemE-3DGS improves over vanilla 3DGS by up to 1.52 dB (Statue) and 3.20 dB (Cars) on the respective datasets, while also achieving the best LPIPS scores (e.g., 0.14 on Statue vs. 0.20 for 3DGS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemE-NeRF outperforms Mip-NeRF 360 by 2.3–8.4 dB PSNR across all four RobustNeRF scenes, confirming that vanilla NeRF severely degrades under distractors. Against the closest competitor RobustNeRF, MemE-NeRF wins on Statue (+1.1 dB) and BabyYoda (+0.49 dB) while matching on Android (21.74 vs. 21.72) and slightly trailing on Crab (30.64 vs. 30.75), all with fewer training patches (16 vs. 64 in the fair-comparison setting). On the Kubric dataset, MemE-NeRF dominates RobustNeRF by large margins (up to +6.97 dB on Bag, +6.74 dB on Chairs) and also surpasses the 16-patch RobustNeRF* variant, indicating the memorization-effect-based filtering is more effective than RobustNeRF's hand-crafted pixel-error ranking. MemE-3DGS consistently improves over vanilla 3DGS (e.g., +1.52 dB on Statue, +1.18 dB on Android) and outperforms D²NeRF, NeRF-W, NSFF, and NeuralDiff on all five Kubric scenes, with the largest gaps over NSFF (which collapses to 4.55 PSNR on Pillow vs. MemE-NeRF's 45.97). Notably, MemE is non-intrusive in distractor-free scenes, matching Mip-NeRF 360 and 3DGS performance, whereas RobustNeRF suffers significant degradation in the same setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mip-NeRF 360 and 3D Gaussian Splatting as the underlying view synthesis backbones, and draws a conceptual bridge from the noisy-label learning literature (Co-teaching, DivideMix) where the memorization effect—networks learning clean samples before noisy ones—has been well studied. It departs most sharply from RobustNeRF, the closest prior method for distractor removal, by being fully unsupervised, requiring no hand-crafted ratios or semantic segmentation, and by being adaptive to scenes both with and without distractors without prior knowledge. The core novelty is repurposing the memorization effect observed during NeRF/3DGS optimization as a signal for unsupervised purity-distractor separation via a multi-scale GMM over hierarchical residuals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MemE offers a practical, drop-in solution for a common real-world failure mode of NeRF/3DGS (transient objects, lighting changes, moving people) without requiring additional supervision, pre-trained models, or scene-specific tuning, which broadens the applicability of these methods to unconstrained photo collections. A key open question is whether the GMM-based residual modeling generalizes to more complex distractor types (e.g., partial occlusions, severe illumination shifts) and to very large-scale outdoor scenes beyond the six Phototourism scenes tested. The method's reliance on accurate camera poses and its current evaluation on relatively small datasets leave scalability and robustness to extreme noise ratios as areas for future work.
