## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently produce anatomically incorrect human images with distorted limbs, wrong finger counts, and twisted hands, yet no large-scale benchmark exists to systematically measure or address these anomalies. The lack of a quantitative metric for limb quality and a dedicated dataset of abnormal human images has prevented both fair evaluation and targeted improvement of human generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240845/cropped_page1_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a free-form text prompt describing a human scene and outputs a 1024×1024 refined human image. Internally, the system consumes intermediate artifacts: a pose keypoint map extracted from the initially generated image (via OpenPose) and bounding-box detections with 18-category labels from the Abnormal Detector. The AbHuman benchmark itself provides 56K synthesized images with 147K bounding-box annotations across 18 anomaly categories (e.g., abnormal hand, abnormal foot, abnormal head), split 4:1 for training and testing.

## Method

HumanRefiner is a plug-and-play, coarse-to-fine refinement pipeline built on a fine-tuned SDXL backbone. It first applies negative prompting with abnormal captions (e.g., 'Ab. Hand') and an Abnormal Scorer (CLIP ViT-L + MLP) for classifier guidance during denoising. For coarse refinement, it extracts a pose keypoint map from the initially generated image and injects it via T2I-Adapter into the encoder features, enabling pose-reversible generation without user-provided pose input. For fine-grained refinement, an Abnormal Detector (fine-tuned YOLOv8/RT-DETR on AbHuman) localizes residual limb anomalies as bounding boxes, which are then corrected via text-conditioned inpainting diffusion. The entire pipeline is trained with supervised fine-tuning on the AbHuman dataset and operates as a post-hoc refinement module compatible with any text-to-image diffusion model.

## Results

On the AbHuman LAION test split, HumanRefiner achieves the lowest Abnormal Score (0.590), highest CLIP Score (34.85), and best FID (13.634) among all compared methods. On the harder HumanArt test split (prompts involving acrobatics, dancing, drama), HumanRefiner again leads with Abnormal Score 0.778, CLIP Score 33.90, and FID 9.145. In human evaluation, HumanRefiner receives 2.9× preference over SDXL (32.3% vs. 11.20%) and 1.4× preference over DALL-E 3 (51.42% vs. 36.03%) for limb quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanRefiner outperforms all nine baselines on the primary Abnormal Score metric across both test splits, achieving 0.590 (LAION) and 0.778 (HumanArt) versus the next-best DeepFloyd-IF (0.595) and HumanSD (0.801) respectively. Against the base SDXL model, HumanRefiner reduces the Abnormal Score from 0.659 to 0.590 (LAION) and from 0.857 to 0.778 (HumanArt), while also improving FID from 30.024 to 13.634 and 20.796 to 9.145. Compared to pose-conditioned methods (Pose-ControlNet, Pose-T2I-Adapter, HumanSD), HumanRefiner achieves lower Abnormal Scores without requiring user-provided pose inputs, and substantially better FID (e.g., 13.634 vs. 50.546 for HumanSD on LAION). In the human preference study, HumanRefiner is preferred over DALL-E 3 by a 1.4× margin (51.42% vs. 36.03%) and over SDXL by 2.9× (32.3% vs. 11.20%) for limb quality. The ablation confirms each component contributes: negative prompting alone reduces Abnormal Score from 0.659 to 0.614, and the full pipeline (negative prompt + abnormal guidance + detector inpainting + pose guide) reaches 0.590.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SDXL as its base diffusion model and repurposes T2I-Adapter and off-the-shelf pose detectors (OpenPose) within a self-diagnostic refinement loop, departing from prior human-generation methods (HumanSD, Pose-ControlNet) that require user-supplied pose conditions and thus restrict pose diversity. Conceptually, it extends the classifier-guidance and negative-prompting paradigms of diffusion models by training an Abnormal Scorer (CLIP ViT-L + MLP) and an Abnormal Detector (YOLOv8/RT-DETR) on a purpose-built anomaly dataset, then chaining them into a coarse-to-fine pipeline. The AbHuman benchmark itself is novel in positioning: unlike existing human datasets (COCO, Human-Art, PoseTrack) that annotate only normal anatomy, it provides bounding-box-level labels for 18 categories of anatomical anomalies in synthesized images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AbHuman benchmark and Abnormal Scorer fill a genuine gap: prior to this work there was no quantitative metric for limb quality in generated humans, making cross-model comparison and targeted improvement difficult. The plug-and-play design means the refinement pipeline can be attached to any T2I model without retraining the backbone. However, the evaluation is largely circular (trained and tested on the same AbHuman distribution), the dual-generation-plus-inpainting pipeline is slow, and the dataset covers only synthesized humans from SDXL, limiting generalizability to other generators or real-world scene diversity. Open questions include whether the anomaly taxonomy transfers to video generation, multi-person scenes, or non-photorealistic styles.
