## Problem

Existing methods for monocular dynamic human rendering model non-rigid motions (e.g., cloth wrinkles) as pose-dependent 3D spatial offsets, which are computationally expensive to learn and lack direct supervision from RGB images. This leads to either prohibitively long training times (tens of hours for HumanNeRF) or failure to capture coherent non-rigid deformations from novel viewpoints, even with extended optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241049/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video (one camera out of 23 in ZJU-MoCap, or the standard camera set in MonoCap) along with per-frame SMPL skeletal pose parameters (23 joint positions) as input. It produces a compositional radiance representation: a canonical T-pose radiance field (color and density) plus a pose-conditioned radiance residual field, which are combined during volume rendering to synthesize novel-view RGB images. Training uses 32×32 image patches with 256 samples per ray, and the MHE has 16 resolution levels with 2-dimensional features per level.

## Method

MoCo-NeRF decomposes a single multiresolution hash encoding (MHE) into two branches: a rigid branch that learns the canonical T-pose radiance field via inverse linear blend skinning, and a non-rigid branch that learns pose-dependent radiance residuals by freezing the first half of the MHE features (as a canonical prior) and optimizing only the second half. A pose-embedded implicit feature is generated via cross-attention between a learnable 64-dim base code and sinusoidally-encoded 23 joint positions, then concatenated into the non-rigid MLP decoder to enable pose-coherent residual learning. The final rendering composes rigid and residual color/density through standard volume integration. For multi-subject training, the architecture extends with a shared global MHE for generic body representations, per-subject local MHEs for discriminative details, and a dictionary of learnable identity codes that allow a single MLP decoder to be shared across all subjects while maintaining distinct appearances.

## Results

On ZJU-MoCap (single-subject), MoCo-NeRF achieves 31.06 PSNR, 0.9734 SSIM, and 28.83 LPIPS×10³, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On MonoCap, it reaches 33.01 PSNR, 0.9874 SSIM, and 13.02 LPIPS×10³, again leading on PSNR and SSIM. In the multi-subject setting (3 subjects on ZJU-MoCap), MoCo-NeRF achieves 31.97 PSNR and 24.54 LPIPS×10³ in ~2 hours, surpassing NHP (26.64 PSNR, ~86 h) and GHuNeRF (27.28 PSNR, ~123 h) by a wide margin while using only monocular input. Training time is 1.9 h per subject (vs. 38.9 h for HumanNeRF) with 16.2 GB GPU memory (vs. 23.7 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HumanNeRF [73], MoCo-NeRF wins on all three metrics on ZJU-MoCap (PSNR +0.88, SSIM +0.0025, LPIPS* −1.30) and on PSNR/SSIM on MonoCap, while HumanNeRF retains a marginal LPIPS* edge (12.93 vs. 13.02); however, MoCo-NeRF trains ~20× faster (1.9 h vs. 38.9 h) and uses 7.5 GB less GPU memory. Against Instant-NVR [14], MoCo-NeRF dominates on every metric for both datasets (e.g., LPIPS* 28.83 vs. 38.87 on ZJU-MoCap), with the paper attributing Instant-NVR's weakness to its heavy reliance on accurate SMPL parameters that smooth out non-rigid details. Against GauHuman [21], MoCo-NeRF leads on all ZJU-MoCap metrics (PSNR +0.08, SSIM +0.0114, LPIPS* −1.33) and on PSNR/SSIM on MonoCap, with a small LPIPS* gap (13.02 vs. 13.54) that the authors argue reflects GauHuman's 3D Gaussian representation advantage rather than superior non-rigid modeling. In the multi-subject setting, MoCo-NeRF outperforms NHP [31] by +5.33 PSNR and −15.93 LPIPS* while being 43× faster, and outperforms GHuNeRF [33] by +4.69 PSNR and −16.28 LPIPS* while being 61× faster, all from monocular input versus NHP's multi-view requirement.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCo-NeRF departs from the dominant spatial-offset paradigm for non-rigid motion modeling in dynamic human NeRFs (HumanNeRF, Instant-NVR, GauHuman), which learn per-point 3D displacements conditioned on pose. Instead, it reframes non-rigid motion as a radiance residual field learned on top of a canonical rigid radiance field, using a single multiresolution hash encoding split into rigid and non-rigid branches. The pose-embedded implicit feature via cross-attention over joint positions draws on the broader trend of conditioning implicit representations on body pose, while the multi-subject extension (global MHE + local MHEs + identity codes) is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior monocular human NeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 61× training-time reduction for multi-subject rendering (2 hours vs. 233 hours on one GPU) makes photo-realistic dynamic human modeling from monocular video practically accessible, removing the main barrier to deployment. The radiance-residual formulation offers a cleaner inductive bias than unbounded spatial offsets, potentially guiding future work on deformable implicit representations beyond humans. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more extreme non-rigid deformations (e.g., flowing fabric, hair) where geometric displacement matters more than appearance, and whether the SMPL dependency for skeletal parameters can be removed for fully markerless settings.
