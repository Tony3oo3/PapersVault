## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary detection methods pretrain CLIP backbones at the image level and then train detector heads from scratch on limited detection data, creating a mismatch between image-level contrastive learning and the region-level recognition that detection requires. This leads to sub-optimal generalization because detector heads lack exposure to the rich open-vocabulary knowledge in large-scale image-text pairs. The paper asks whether detector modules can be integrated directly into image-text pretraining without pseudo-labels or box annotations to bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes 224×224 RGB images paired with natural language captions from LAION-2B or DataComp-1B; during detection finetuning and inference, it processes 1024×1024 images with text queries specifying target categories. The output is a set of bounding boxes and segmentation masks with open-vocabulary class labels, where detection scores are computed as cosine similarity between RoI-Align region embeddings and text embeddings, ensembled with a VLM score via geometric mean. At test time, the text embedding set is expanded to include novel categories absent from training, enabling zero-shot recognition of unseen object classes.

## Method

DITO introduces Region-centric Pretraining (REP), which attaches FPN, Faster R-CNN classifier, and RPN heads to the ViT backbone during CLIP pretraining: random box regions are sampled across four feature pyramid levels, RoI-Align features are extracted and passed through the classifier, and a multi-level image-text contrastive loss (InfoNCE) is applied with max-pooling over RoI embeddings per level to encourage salient, text-aligned regions to dominate the image representation. The RPN is supervised by visual-text cosine similarity at RoI centers, requiring no box annotations. For detection finetuning, Shifted-Window Learning (SWL) runs the windowed-attention ViT twice—once normally and once with the patch token map rolled by half the window size—then averages the outputs, mitigating fixed-grid bias while preserving the locality structure of pretrained low-resolution features. A frozen copy of the pretrained ViT provides cosine-distance distillation targets on RoI embeddings during finetuning to prevent loss of open-vocabulary knowledge.

## Results

On the LVIS open-vocabulary detection benchmark, DITO with ViT-L/16 pretrained on LAION-2B achieves 37.6 mask AP_r, outperforming the previous best ViT-based method RO-ViT (32.4) by +5.2 points and CFM-ViT (33.9) by +3.7 points. Using the DataComp-1B dataset, DITO sets a new state-of-the-art of 40.5 mask AP_r. On COCO, DITO achieves 39.6 novel AP (LAION-2B) and 40.2 novel AP (DataComp-1B) without pseudo-labeling or weak supervision, surpassing RO-ViT by +6.3 and CFM-ViT by +5.3. In zero-shot transfer detection from LVIS_base to Objects365, DITO reaches 20.0 AP, improving over CFM-ViT (18.7) and RO-ViT (17.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DITO consistently outperforms all ViT-based baselines across all three evaluation settings. On LVIS, DITO (37.6 mask AP_r) beats RO-ViT [17] (32.4) by +5.2 and CFM-ViT [16] (33.9) by +3.7, and surpasses the strongest ConvNet-based method F-VLM [18] (32.8) by +4.8. On COCO, DITO (39.6 novel AP) leads RO-ViT (33.3) by +6.3 and CFM-ViT (34.3) by +5.3, while remaining competitive with pseudo-labeling methods like EdaDet [32] (40.2) and CORA [36] (41.7) that rely on additional supervision. In transfer detection to Objects365, DITO (20.0 AP) improves over CFM-ViT (18.7) by +1.3 and RO-ViT (17.1) by +2.9. Notably, DITO achieves these gains without pseudo-labeling, weak supervision, or extra grounding datasets, whereas methods like RegionCLIP [44], Detic-CN2 [46], and OWL-ViT v2 [25] require such additional signals. The ablation in Table 4a confirms that region-centric pretraining contributes +2.6 AP_r, shifted-window learning +2.8 AP_r, and frozen backbone distillation +1.2 AP_r over the RO-ViT baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DITO extends the CLIP dual-encoder pretraining paradigm by integrating detector heads (FPN, Faster R-CNN classifier, RPN) directly into the image-text contrastive training loop, departing from prior region-aligned VLMs like RegionCLIP that rely on pseudo-labeling or methods like GLIP and Grounding DINO that require additional grounding annotations. The baseline is RO-ViT's cropped positional embedding and ViTDet windowed-attention architecture, which DITO augments with a Shifted-Window Learning strategy that applies shifted windows as separate forward passes rather than Swin Transformer's layer-alternating scheme. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a bridge between image-level contrastive pretraining and region-level detection, arguing that detector heads should be warm-started from large-scale image-text data rather than trained from scratch on limited detection vocabularies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The +3.7 mask AP_r improvement over prior SOTA on LVIS without pseudo-labeling or extra datasets demonstrates that the pretraining-detection gap can be closed through architectural integration rather than complex multi-stage label generation, offering a simpler and more scalable pipeline for open-vocabulary detection. The finding that shifted-window learning outperforms full global attention when adapting low-resolution pretrained ViTs to high-resolution detection inputs challenges the assumption that more global context is always better, suggesting locality preservation from pretraining is critical. Open questions include whether the two-forward-pass SWL overhead is prohibitive at scale, whether the approach generalizes to other backbone architectures beyond ViT, and whether the random-region contrastive signal can be further refined with lightweight region proposals.
