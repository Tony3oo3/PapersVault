## Problem

In multi-modal federated learning, different modalities learn at inconsistent paces (modality imbalance), causing the global model to develop a severe bias toward the dominant modality. Existing client selection methods designed for uni-modal FL ignore inter-modal interactions and fail to address this bias, as they select whole clients rather than considering which modalities on each client should participate in training and aggregation. The paper further reveals that uni-modal training on certain clients can contribute more to the global model than multi-modal training, a phenomenon unexploited by prior work.

## Input / Output

Each client holds paired modalities: audio spectrograms (257×299 for CREMA-D, 257×1004 for AVE) with randomly sampled visual frames, colored and grayscale MNIST images, or front/back 3D shape views from ModelNet40. The key design choice is that the selection unit is the modality on a client rather than the client itself—some selected clients train with both modalities while others train with only the weak modality, and only the networks corresponding to selected modalities are uploaded for aggregation. The output is a single class label per sample from a fusion classifier that concatenates modality-specific encoder representations.

## Method

BMSFed operates in two stages per communication round. First, a Modal Enhancement (ME) loss is added to local training: aggregated global class prototypes (weighted means of encoder representations across clients) are used in a softmax-contrastive objective that specifically pulls the weak modality's representations toward its correct global prototype, with the loss applied only to the weaker modality as determined by a local imbalance ratio ρ_I^k. Second, a balanced modality selection mechanism treats each local modality as the sampling unit and solves two decoupled submodular (facility-location) maximization problems in parallel via stochastic greedy: one selects multi-modal clients S_M whose joint CE gradients best approximate the full multi-modal CE gradient, and the other selects uni-modal clients S_I whose CE+ME gradients best approximate the full ME gradient contribution. A conflict resolution rule assigns each candidate client to S_M or S_I based on whether its local imbalance ratio exceeds a threshold χ, ensuring the weak modality receives extra representation in the global aggregation while maintaining gradient diversity.

## Results

BMSFed achieves the best top-1 accuracy across all four datasets under both IID and non-IID settings: 64.5% (IID) / 61.6% (non-IID) on CREMA-D, 64.7% / 62.1% on AVE, 70.2% / 66.7% on CG-MNIST, and 88.7% / 87.5% on ModelNet40. The largest gains are on CREMA-D (up to 5.8% over the best baseline FedProto) and CG-MNIST (up to 4.1% over FedPMR), while improvements on ModelNet40 are more modest (~1%). On the weak visual modality specifically, BMSFed lifts accuracy from ~20% (FedAvg) to ~42% on CREMA-D and from ~27% to ~40% on AVE, demonstrating effective mitigation of modal bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMSFed outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and setting. The strongest competitor is FedPMR (PMR + FedAvg), which reaches 66.1% on CG-MNIST IID and 61.6% on AVE non-IID, but BMSFed still leads by 4.1% and 0.5% respectively. Client-selection baselines pow-d and DivFL perform poorly in the multi-modal setting, sometimes falling below plain FedAvg (e.g., pow-d drops to 41.2% on CG-MNIST IID vs. 42.3% for FedAvg), confirming that uni-modal selection criteria fail to address modal bias. FedProto and FedOGM, which target statistical heterogeneity and local modality imbalance respectively, achieve mid-range results (54–58% on CREMA-D) but lack the global-level modality balancing that BMSFed provides. On ModelNet40, where modalities (front/back 3D views) are more balanced, the gap narrows to ~1%, suggesting BMSFed's advantage is most pronounced when modality imbalance is severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

BMSFed extends the submodular-maximization framework for diverse client selection (DivFL, Balakrishnan et al., ICLR 2022) by shifting the selection unit from individual clients to individual modalities on each client, thereby reframing the combinatorial problem as a modality-level selection. It also adapts the prototype-based modal rebalancing idea from PMR (Fan et al., CVPR 2023) but departs from it by aggregating class prototypes globally across clients rather than using local prototypes, and by applying the enhancement loss to both multi-modal and uni-modal clients. The key conceptual departure from prior MFL work (FedMSplit, CreamFL) is that those methods address modality incongruity or statistical heterogeneity but ignore the inter-modal interaction during joint training, whereas BMSFed explicitly targets the modality imbalance that arises from inconsistent learning paces across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in MFL—global modal bias—that existing client selection methods (pow-d, DivFL) cannot mitigate, providing a principled mechanism (submodular modality selection with conflict resolution) to balance modal contributions. The empirical finding that selectively dropping a dominant modality on some clients can improve global multi-modal performance challenges the default assumption that all available modalities should always be used in joint training. Limitations include the restriction to exactly two modalities, the heuristic nature of the conflict-resolution threshold χ, and the assumption that the weak modality can be reliably identified from the aggregated imbalance ratio; extending to three or more modalities and to more complex fusion architectures remains open.
