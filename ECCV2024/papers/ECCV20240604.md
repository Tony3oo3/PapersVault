## Problem

State-of-the-art monocular depth estimation models degrade significantly on out-of-distribution inputs such as adverse weather (rain, night, fog) and non-Lambertian surfaces (transparent, mirror, specular), yet existing solutions address these challenges in isolation—requiring separate GANs per condition, extra sensors, or real paired easy/challenging samples. This paper seeks a unified, data-efficient framework that improves robustness across multiple challenging scenarios simultaneously without requiring any real challenging images or prior knowledge of their visual characteristics.

## Input / Output

At inference, the fine-tuned network accepts a single RGB image (padded/cropped to 384 or 518 px on the long or short side depending on the backbone) and outputs a per-pixel depth map. During training, the pipeline consumes easy (well-lit, Lambertian) RGB images, their depth maps predicted by the pre-trained network, and user-defined text prompts describing the target adverse condition; the diffusion model then synthesizes the corresponding challenging RGB image while preserving the original 3D geometry, so the same depth map serves as the supervision signal for the challenging counterpart.

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, a conditional text-to-image diffusion model (T2I-Adapter or ControlNet) is conditioned on both a depth map extracted from an easy image and a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'rainy city street at night with colorful neon lights') to generate a visually challenging image that retains the original scene's 3D structure. Second, the pre-trained monocular depth network is fine-tuned via self-distillation: it acts as a teacher by predicting depth on the easy source images, and as a student by learning to match those pseudo-labels on the generated challenging images, using a scale-and-shift-invariant loss. Because the diffusion model preserves geometry, the depth labels transfer directly to the challenging images without any manual annotation or active-sensor capture. The approach is model-agnostic—any pre-trained depth network can be fine-tuned—and the text prompts allow arbitrary, user-defined challenging conditions to be synthesized from a single easy image, eliminating the need for real adverse-weather or non-Lambertian training data.

## Results

On nuScenes, fine-tuning md4all-DD with diffusion-generated data (using Depth Anything for conditioning) reduces night absRel from 0.242 (baseline) to 0.191 and day-rain absRel from 0.157 to 0.139, while also improving day-clear absRel to 0.128. On RobotCar, the method cuts night absRel from 0.391 to 0.129, matching or exceeding Gasperini et al. [27] (0.122) without requiring prior knowledge of target conditions. For non-Lambertian (ToM) surfaces on Booster, Depth Anything fine-tuned with the proposed approach achieves δ<1.25 of 98.91% and MAE of 54.31 mm, substantially outperforming the 84.23%/137.96 mm baseline and surpassing Costanzino et al. [18] (96.68%/70.68 mm) which relies on real annotated data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gasperini et al. [27] (md4all-DD), which uses a ForkGAN to translate day-clear images into rainy/night scenes and requires prior knowledge of target condition properties, the proposed method achieves comparable or superior performance on nuScenes (night absRel 0.191 vs 0.192; day-rain 0.139 vs 0.141) without needing any real challenging samples or condition-specific knowledge. On RobotCar, the method (0.129 night absRel) slightly trails Gasperini et al. (0.122) but dramatically outperforms the md4all baseline (0.391) and all other methods that require target-condition knowledge (DeFeatNet 0.334, ADIDS 0.287, RNW 0.185, WSGD 0.174). For non-Lambertian surfaces, the approach matches or exceeds Costanzino et al. [18] (Depth4Tom) on Booster ToM (δ<1.25: 98.91% vs 96.68%; MAE: 54.31 vs 70.68 mm) while using only text prompts instead of real annotated images and segmentation masks. The method also improves all four general-purpose baselines (MiDaS, DPT, Depth Anything, ZoeDepth) on nuScenes across every condition, with the largest gains in night (e.g., DPT δ1: 60.97→67.87) and day-rain (e.g., DPT absRel: 0.237→0.199). Notably, the approach works with daytime-only datasets (Table 4), a setting where Gasperini et al. cannot be applied at all, yet still achieves strong cross-dataset generalization to adverse conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes conditional text-to-image diffusion models (T2I-Adapter, ControlNet) — originally designed for image synthesis — as a data generation engine for monocular depth estimation, a role they had not previously served. It departs from GAN-based domain translation approaches like ForkGAN (Gasperini et al. [27]), which require paired easy/challenging samples and separate generators per condition, by using a single diffusion model with free-form text prompts to produce arbitrarily diverse challenging scenes. The self-distillation training protocol, where the pre-trained depth network provides its own pseudo-labels on easy images that are then transferred to the generated challenging counterparts, is the key training mechanism that makes the approach model-agnostic and independent of any ground-truth depth in the challenging domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for real challenging-condition data (rain, night, non-Lambertian surfaces) with depth annotations — a bottleneck that has limited robustness improvements in depth estimation. The ability to work from daytime-only datasets and generalize to unseen adverse conditions (Table 4) is a meaningful operational advantage for deployment. However, the approach inherits a fundamental assumption: that the 3D structure (depth) is preserved between the easy source image and the diffusion-generated challenging image, which may not hold for extreme transformations. Open questions include how well the method handles conditions that fundamentally alter scene geometry (e.g., heavy fog occluding distant objects) and whether the self-distillation ceiling (bounded by teacher quality) limits ultimate performance.
