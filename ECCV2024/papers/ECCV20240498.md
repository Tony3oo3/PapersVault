## Problem

Pseudo-word token-based Zero-Shot CIR methods project image embeddings into the text encoder's embedding space via a learned projection module, which distorts the original discriminative image representation and confines composed embeddings to the text side, limiting their ability to capture joint image-text information. Additionally, supervised CIR approaches depend on expensive manually-annotated triplets, restricting scalability and domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240498/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image and a free-form text query describing desired modifications, with no required prompt template (unlike pseudo-token methods that need e.g. 'a photo of [ ]'). At inference, the user can adjust the Slerp balancing scalar α (set to 0.8–0.9 depending on dataset) to control the relative contribution of image versus text in the composed embedding. The output is a ranked list of gallery images scored by cosine similarity between the Slerp-composed query embedding and pre-computed gallery image embeddings.

## Method

The core composition mechanism replaces the projection module with Spherical Linear Interpolation (Slerp): given l2-normalized image embedding v and text embedding w from a VLP model (CLIP or BLIP), the composed embedding c is computed as Slerp(v, w; α) = sin((1−α)θ)/sin(θ) · v + sin(αθ)/sin(θ) · w, where θ is the angle between v and w. This works because VLP contrastive training places both modalities on a shared hypersphere, so an intermediate point on the great-circle arc naturally represents a blend of visual and textual semantics. To make Slerp more effective, the paper introduces Text-Anchored-Tuning (TAT): the text encoder is frozen while the image encoder is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16, α=16) under the standard contrastive loss, pulling image embeddings closer to their paired text anchors and reducing the modality gap. TAT requires only a single training epoch with fewer than 0.5% trainable parameters and as few as 0.58M image-text pairs, yet yields substantial gains over both the untrained Slerp baseline and prior pseudo-token methods across CIRR, CIRCO, and FashionIQ benchmarks.

## Results

On CIRCO (the most challenging ZS-CIR benchmark with a 123K gallery), Slerp+TAT with CLIP-ViT-L/14 achieves mAP@5 of 18.46 and mAP@50 of 22.41, substantially outperforming the best prior method LinCIR (12.59 / 15.85) by over 5 mAP points. On CIRR, Slerp+TAT with CLIP-ViT-L/14 reaches Recall@1 of 30.94 and Recall@50 of 89.18, surpassing LinCIR (25.04) and SEARLE (24.22). On FashionIQ, Slerp+TAT with BLIP-ViT-L/16 attains average R@10 of 32.77 and R@50 of 53.32, the best among all compared methods. Notably, plain Slerp without any fine-tuning already matches or approaches pseudo-word token methods, and TAT training requires only a single epoch with <0.5% trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Slerp+TAT decisively outperforms all pseudo-word token-based ZS-CIR baselines across all three benchmarks. On CIRCO with CLIP-ViT-L/14, Slerp+TAT (mAP@5 = 18.46) beats LinCIR (12.59) by 5.9 points, SEARLE (11.68) by 6.8 points, and Pic2Word (8.72) by 9.7 points. On CIRR with the same backbone, Slerp+TAT (R@1 = 30.94) surpasses LinCIR (25.04) and SEARLE (24.22) by 5.9 and 6.7 points respectively. On FashionIQ with BLIP-ViT-L/16, Slerp+TAT achieves the best average R@10 (32.77) and R@50 (53.32), exceeding SEARLE (25.56 / 46.23) and LinCIR (26.28 / 46.49). Against the fine-tuned BLIP baselines CASE and CoVR on CIRR, plain Slerp (no additional training) achieves R@1 of 39.08, edging out CoVR (38.48) and CASE (35.40), while also leading on all Recall_subset metrics. The key differentiator is training efficiency: Slerp+TAT requires only one epoch with <0.5% trainable LoRA parameters, whereas SEARLE and Pic2Word need tens to hundreds of epochs and full-scale datasets (3M+ pairs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant pseudo-word token paradigm in Zero-Shot CIR (Pic2Word, SEARLE, PALAVRA, LinCIR), which projects image embeddings into the text encoder's vocabulary space via a learned module. Instead, it repurposes the classical Slerp interpolation (Shoemake, 1985) as a composition operator directly on the VLP hypersphere, arguing that the projection step distorts discriminative image features and confines composed embeddings to the text side. The Text-Anchored-Tuning strategy builds on LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning but inverts the typical freezing convention (freezing text, adapting image) to close the modality gap identified in prior VLP analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: Slerp provides a training-free composition mechanism with a user-tunable α that balances image vs. text contribution, and TAT delivers a highly efficient fine-tuning recipe (single epoch, <0.5% trainable parameters, ~20% of typical training data) that also improves downstream supervised CIR checkpoints. A key open question is whether this approach generalizes to retrieval settings where both image and text appear in the gallery, and whether the hypersphere-interpolation assumption holds for VLP models trained with objectives other than cosine-similarity contrastive loss. The paper does not explore multi-turn or iterative retrieval, leaving the compositional reasoning depth of Slerp-based queries untested.
