## Problem

Cross-domain object detection in adverse weather relies heavily on the mean-teacher paradigm, but pseudo-labels generated on degraded target-domain images are noisy and low-quality. Existing filtering strategies depend solely on the teacher model's classification confidence, ignoring regression uncertainty and the risk of the teacher misleading the student under heavy domain shift (e.g., dense fog or rain).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240957/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source-domain images (Cityscapes or BDD100K-daytime, clear weather) and unlabeled target-domain images (synthetic foggy/rainy Cityscapes, real-world RTTS fog, or BDD100K-night), all resized to 960×960 RGB. It produces per-image bounding boxes with class labels using YOLOv5L or YOLOv7 as the detection backbone. During training, the target domain is additionally augmented with restored (RIDCP/STB/SMG) and super-resolved versions of the same images to increase the pool of usable pseudo-labels.

## Method

The framework has two stages. In the generation phase, a novel IAoU loss (Intersection Auxiliary over Enclosing Convex) replaces CIoU to provide non-zero gradients even when predicted and ground-truth boxes do not intersect, improving bounding-box regression accuracy that underpins feature alignment; this is combined with image-to-image translation (rendering for clear→adverse, restoration for adverse→clear) and a GRL-based global feature alignment module. In the composition phase, a mean-teacher model is trained with a joint-filtering and student-aware pseudo-label strategy: classification confidence is split into high (θ₁) and low (θ₂) thresholds, and a Gaussian regression confidence (derived from per-coordinate variances) provides a second gate (θ₃); both teacher and student predictions are consulted so that the student is not forced to follow a misguiding teacher. Uncertain pseudo-labels between θ₂ and θ₁ are retained as soft labels with selective loss computation (regression-only or classification-only), effectively converting them into usable supervision.

## Results

On the real-world foggy dataset RTTS, the proposed method (YOLOv5L) achieves 58.9 mAP, surpassing the strongest baseline SSDA-YOLO (53.0 mAP) by 5.9 points and the source-only model (46.5 mAP) by 12.4 points. On synthetic Foggy Cityscapes, Ours-YOLOv5L reaches 58.4 mAP (+2.5 over SSDA-YOLOv5L), and on Rain Cityscapes it reaches 57.5 mAP (+4.9 over SSDA-YOLOv5L). The YOLOv7 variant further improves to 60.5 mAP on RTTS and 59.5 mAP on Foggy Cityscapes, approaching or exceeding the Oracle upper bound in several individual categories (e.g., motorcycle, truck).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all ten baselines across every evaluated dataset. On RTTS, the closest competitors are SSDA-YOLO (53.0 mAP, −5.9), R-YOLO (52.5 mAP, −6.4), and ConfMix (52.3 mAP, −6.6) with YOLOv5L; the Faster R-CNN-based methods (CMT 51.8, TDD 49.4, HTCN 38.8, DM 37.7, PDA 34.1) trail by 7–25 mAP points. On Foggy Cityscapes, the gap over SSDA-YOLO narrows to 2.5 mAP (58.4 vs. 55.9), while ConfMix and CMT lag by 9.3 and 6.5 points respectively. On Rain Cityscapes, the method leads SSDA-YOLO by 4.9 mAP and R-YOLO by 3.0 mAP. Notably, the YOLOv7 variant extends the lead to 5.6–5.7 mAP over both SSDA-YOLOv7 and R-YOLOv7 on RTTS, and the small-object categories (motorcycle, bicycle) show the largest relative gains, validating the IAoU loss design for tiny targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mean-teacher framework for unsupervised domain-adaptive object detection by introducing a two-stage generation-composition paradigm. The generation phase builds on CIoU loss (Zheng et al., AAAI 2020) to propose IAoU, on Gaussian YOLOv3 (Choi et al., ICCV 2019) for regression uncertainty modeling, and on gradient-reversal-based domain alignment. The composition phase departs from prior mean-teacher methods (UMT, CMT, SSDA-YOLO) that rely solely on teacher predictions for pseudo-label filtering, by incorporating a student-aware joint-filtering strategy with dual classification thresholds (borrowed from Xu et al., 2023) and regression variance gates from both models. The overall positioning is as a systematic rethinking of pseudo-label quality in the mean-teacher loop rather than a new architectural primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.4% mAP gain on real-world RTTS and consistent improvements across synthetic fog/rain benchmarks demonstrate that pseudo-label quality is a more binding bottleneck than previously acknowledged in mean-teacher domain adaptation. The student-aware filtering addresses a practical failure mode—teacher misguidance under heavy degradation—that prior methods implicitly assumed away. Limitations include dependence on external restoration models (RIDCP, STB, SMG) whose quality directly caps the pipeline, the two-stage training cost, and evaluation restricted to YOLOv5/YOLOv7 backbones. Open questions include whether the IAoU loss generalizes to transformer-based detectors and whether the joint-filtering strategy extends to online or streaming adaptation settings.
