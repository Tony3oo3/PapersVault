## Problem

While Video Scene Graph Generation (VidSGG) identifies object relationships in observed frames, no prior work formally addresses anticipating how these pairwise relationships evolve into the future. This paper introduces the Scene Graph Anticipation (SGA) task and highlights that traditional sequential (auto-regressive) modeling is ill-suited for capturing the continuous, potentially stochastic dynamics governing relationship evolution over extended horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242351/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an observed video segment (a fraction F ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9} of the full video) and must produce scene graphs for the remaining H future frames. Depending on the evaluation setting, input ranges from raw frames only (AGS) to frames plus ground-truth bounding boxes (PGAGS) or bounding boxes plus object categories (GAGS). A key constraint is that the set of objects is assumed fixed to those observed at the last frame; the model predicts only the evolution of relationship predicates, bounding boxes, and object categories for those persistent objects, not the emergence of new objects.

## Method

SceneSayer processes observed frames through three units: an Object Representation Processing Unit (Faster R-CNN features refined by a temporal transformer), a Spatial Context Processing Unit (concatenated object features refined by a spatial transformer), and a Latent Dynamics Processing Unit (temporal transformer followed by a generative latent dynamics model). The core novelty is replacing auto-regressive sequential prediction with a continuous-time formulation: the relationship representation from the last observed frame serves as the initial condition of either a NeuralODE (SceneSayerODE, deterministic) or a NeuralSDE (SceneSayerSDE, stochastic with Stratonovich interpretation), and future representations are obtained by numerically solving the corresponding differential equation over the anticipation horizon. The SDE variant explicitly models uncertainty arising from occlusions and blurry imagery in single-viewpoint video. Training is supervised with a composite loss combining cross-entropy for object/predicate classification on both observed and anticipated representations, Smoothed L1 for bounding box regression, and a reconstruction loss anchoring anticipated representations to the temporal encoder output.

## Results

On the Action Genome dataset (AGS setting, F=0.9, With Constraint), SceneSayerSDE achieves R@10 of 34.8 and mR@10 of 15.1, outperforming the best baseline DSGDetr++ (R@10=24.4, mR@10=7.4) by approximately 43% on R@10 and 104% on mR@10. SceneSayerODE achieves R@10 of 33.9 and mR@10 of 14.0, also substantially ahead of baselines. Under the most informative GAGS setting (F=0.9), SceneSayerSDE reaches R@10 of 60.3 and mR@10 of 28.5, a ~26% improvement over the best baseline (STTran++: R@10=46.0, mR@10=19.6). The SDE variant consistently outperforms the ODE variant across all settings and observation fractions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SceneSayerODE and SceneSayerSDE against two adapted VidSGG baselines: STTran [7] and DSGDetr [9], each in two variants (+ and ++). SceneSayerSDE wins decisively across all three evaluation settings (AGS, PGAGS, GAGS) and all observation fractions (F=0.3 to 0.9), achieving up to ~70% improvement on R@10 over the best baseline (DSGDetr++) in the most challenging AGS setting. SceneSayerODE also consistently outperforms both baselines but is slightly behind SceneSayerSDE, confirming the benefit of stochastic modeling. Between the two baselines, DSGDetr++ marginally outperforms STTran++ in AGS (e.g., R@10: 24.4 vs 23.6 at F=0.9), while STTran++ edges out DSGDetr++ in PGAGS and GAGS settings. Both ++ variants (which add a temporal encoder and dual-decoding loss) substantially outperform their + counterparts, validating the importance of temporal context encoding. On mean recall metrics, SceneSayer models show the largest relative gains, indicating reduced prediction bias toward frequent predicates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VidSGG pipeline of STTran (Cong et al., ICCV 2021) and DSGDetr (Feng et al., WACV 2021), retaining their object representation and spatial context processing units while replacing the latent dynamics component with continuous-time differential equation solvers. The key conceptual departure is from sequential/autoregressive video prediction: rather than predicting frame-by-frame in discrete steps, SceneSayer models the evolution of relationship representations as solutions to NeuralODEs or NeuralSDEs, drawing on the continuous-time dynamics literature (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2018; Kidger et al., ICML 2021). This positions the work at the intersection of scene graph generation and latent dynamical systems modeling, repurposing differential equation frameworks previously applied to trajectory prediction and multi-agent simulation for the structured prediction of object interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces Scene Graph Anticipation as a formally defined task with clear downstream relevance to surveillance, robotics, and anomaly detection, filling a gap between retrospective scene graph generation and pixel-level video prediction. However, the evaluation is confined to the Action Genome dataset (single-actor, real-world videos), and the authors explicitly exclude prediction of new object emergence and future-frame object localization, limiting the practical scope. The consistent SDE-over-ODE advantage suggests that stochastic modeling of relationship evolution is important, but the absence of ablations isolating the differential equation component from the surrounding transformer architecture makes it difficult to attribute gains precisely. Open questions include whether the continuous-time formulation scales to multi-actor scenes, longer horizons, or open-vocabulary predicates, and whether commonsense priors from LLMs/VLMs can complement the learned dynamics.
