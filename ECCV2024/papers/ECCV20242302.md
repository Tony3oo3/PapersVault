## Problem

Vision-language models frequently generate captions containing hallucinated objects, attributes, or spatial relationships, especially in long-form captioning. Existing mitigation strategies rely on expensive human-annotated or GPT-generated data and detect hallucinations only at the sentence or paragraph level, overlooking which specific tokens are hallucinated and what type of hallucination they represent. No prior method provides token-level, type-specific hallucination identification in a fully unsupervised manner.

## Input / Output

During training, ESREAL consumes only RGB images (no image-text pairs) from the Stanford Image Paragraph dataset; the VLM generates a long paragraph caption from each image, which is then fed to SDXL Turbo (4 reconstructions per caption for variance reduction) to produce a reconstructed image. The detection pipeline outputs per-token penalties of three types—binary object-hallucination scores, continuous CLIP-similarity-based attribute scores, and cosine-similarity-based spatial-relationship scores—along with a single holistic reconstruction reward appended at the end of the sequence. The final trained VLM outputs a hallucination-reduced image caption given an image and text prompt.

## Method

ESREAL is a fully unsupervised fine-grained PPO framework whose reward signal is derived entirely from a reference-free hallucination detection pipeline. The pipeline first reconstructs the image from the generated caption using SDXL Turbo, then aligns caption phrases to bounding boxes in both the original and reconstructed images via Grounding DINO, and finally scores each aligned region: non-existent objects receive a binary −1 penalty, unfaithful attributes receive a CLIP-similarity-based penalty in [−1, 0], and inaccurate spatial relationships receive a penalty based on the cosine similarity of inter-object center vectors. These type-specific penalties are assigned to individual tokens during PPO training, supplemented by a holistic CLIP-similarity reconstruction reward at the final timestep to prevent reward hacking through token omission, and a KL-divergence penalty to constrain policy drift. The approach is model-agnostic and requires no annotated image-text pairs, reducing CHAIR by up to 32.81% on LLaVA while preserving or improving CIDEr, ROUGE-L, and BLEU scores.

## Results

ESREAL reduces the CHAIR_s hallucination metric by 32.81% on LLaVA (0.64→0.43), 27.08% on InstructBLIP (0.48→0.35), and 7.46% on mPLUG-Owl2 (0.67→0.62), while maintaining or slightly improving Coverage. On FaithScore, overall faithfulness improves from 0.7401 to 0.7846 (LLaVA), 0.7113 to 0.7834 (InstructBLIP), and 0.7171 to 0.7202 (mPLUG-Owl2). GPT-4V-aided evaluation confirms reductions in total hallucinations per caption from 2.06→1.52 (LLaVA), 2.42→1.49 (InstructBLIP), and 2.51→2.32 (mPLUG-Owl2). Task-specific metrics (CIDEr, ROUGE-L, BLEU) are preserved or improved, with LLaVA's CIDEr nearly doubling from 4.52 to 9.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESREAL is applied as a post-hoc RL fine-tuning on three open-source VLMs (LLaVA 1.5 7B, InstructBLIP FlanT5-XL 3B, mPLUG-Owl2 7B), each compared against its own unmodified baseline. On the primary CHAIR_s metric, ESREAL wins decisively on LLaVA (0.43 vs. 0.64, −32.8%) and InstructBLIP (0.35 vs. 0.48, −27.1%), with a more modest but still significant gain on mPLUG-Owl2 (0.62 vs. 0.67, −7.5%). On FaithScore, LLaVA and InstructBLIP show clear improvements (≈+0.04–0.07 in overall faithfulness), while mPLUG-Owl2's gain is marginal (+0.003), consistent with its smaller CHAIR improvement. GPT-4V-aided evaluation confirms the trend: total hallucinations per caption drop by 26% (LLaVA), 38% (InstructBLIP), and 8% (mPLUG-Owl2). Importantly, generative quality is not sacrificed—CIDEr improves substantially for LLaVA (4.52→9.10) and InstructBLIP (5.99→7.85), while mPLUG-Owl2 remains essentially unchanged (3.71→3.99), indicating ESREAL preserves the model's descriptive capacity while suppressing hallucinated content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on PPO-based reinforcement learning for sequence generation and extends the fine-grained reward paradigm from sentence-level to token-level penalties. It departs from the dominant data-driven hallucination mitigation approaches (GAVIE, HACL, MMHal-Bench, MHalDetect) that rely on GPT-generated or human-annotated hallucination data, instead proposing a fully unsupervised, reference-free detection pipeline. The core conceptual departure is repurposing a text-to-image diffusion model (SDXL Turbo) as a semantic reconstruction oracle: by regenerating an image from the VLM's own caption and comparing it to the original, hallucinated tokens become locally identifiable without any ground-truth text. This positions ESREAL at the intersection of diffusion models, open-vocabulary grounding (Grounding DINO), and RL-based VLM training, a combination not previously explored for hallucination suppression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unsupervised, model-agnostic nature of ESREAL makes it a practical plug-in for any VLM performing image captioning, eliminating the costly annotation pipeline that prior methods require. However, the approach is inherently restricted to full-image captioning (semantic reconstruction demands the entire scene), and its reliability is coupled to the accuracy of three external models (diffusion, grounding, CLIP), creating a compounding-error risk. Open questions include whether the reconstruction-based detection can be adapted to VQA or region-level tasks, whether non-spatial relationship hallucinations (e.g., causal, functional) can be captured, and whether the 78% reward win rate is sufficient for stable training at scale. The finding that removing the holistic reconstruction reward triggers reward hacking (51% CIDEr drop) highlights a broader open problem in fine-grained RL: how to prevent models from gaming sparse token-level penalties by collapsing output length.
