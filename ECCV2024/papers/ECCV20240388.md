## Problem

Event cameras offer high temporal resolution (<10 μs) and high dynamic range (>120 dB) that could handle rapid head movements and extreme lighting, yet they have never been applied to head pose estimation. The field also lacks any paired event-and-head-pose dataset, preventing systematic evaluation of event-based HPE methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240388/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes asynchronous event streams from neuromorphic sensors (Prophesee EVK4 or DAVIS346) discretized into voxel grids over 10 ms or 40 ms time slices, at resolutions of 600×600 (Prophesee-HP, cropped from 1280×720) or 346×260 (Davis-HP). It outputs a 3×3 rotation matrix (regressed from six parameters via a GS mapping) and the corresponding Euler angles (pitch, yaw, roll), with both representations used for loss computation and evaluation.

## Method

The EV-HPE network processes voxel-grid event inputs through an Event Spatial-Temporal Fusion (ESTF) module that applies mean and max pooling along the temporal axis, extracts horizontal/vertical edge contours via an Adaptive Sobel Module with learnable kernels, and fuses these with the original representation to enrich sparse spatial information. Four lightweight RepVGG blocks, each paired with an Event Motion Perceptual Attention (EMPA) module, then extract features; EMPA generates attention weights through grouped local convolution followed by deep dilated convolution (large receptive field) and applies them to the original features via a residual 1×1 convolution. A final MLP regresses six parameters mapped to a rotation matrix via GS mapping, which is converted to Euler angles. Training uses a unified loss combining geodesic distance on rotation matrices and L1 angle loss on Euler angles (weighted by α=0.001), optimized with Adam (lr=5e-5, weight decay 0.5) for 20 epochs on 8 A800 GPUs.

## Results

On the Prophesee-HP dataset (10 ms event slices), the proposed EV-HPE network achieves a mean Euler angle error (MAE) of 6.01° and a mean vector error (MAEV) of 9.53°, outperforming the best baseline 6DRepNet by 0.84° (MAE) and 1.30° (MAEV). On the Davis-HP dataset (10 ms slices), the method records MAE 6.49° and MAEV 10.24°, again leading 6DRepNet by 0.79° and 1.26° respectively. When evaluated at 40 ms slices on Davis-HP, the event-based EV-HPE (MAE 6.46°, MAEV 10.11°) substantially outperforms the same methods fed with RGB frames (e.g., 6DRepNet RGB: MAE 9.64°, MAEV 15.25°), demonstrating the advantage of event data in challenging lighting and motion conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed EV-HPE network outperforms all five baselines on every metric (pitch, yaw, roll, MAE, and MAEV) across both datasets. The closest competitor is 6DRepNet [11], which uses a 6D rotation matrix representation; EV-HPE improves upon it by 0.84° MAE and 1.30° MAEV on Prophesee-HP and by 0.79° and 1.26° on Davis-HP. WHENet [47] and HopeNet [34] are mid-tier, with MAE values around 8.1–8.3° on Prophesee-HP, roughly 2° worse than the proposed method. FSA-Net [44] and TokenHPE [46] perform the weakest, with TokenHPE [46] (a transformer-based approach) achieving the highest errors (MAE ≈ 10.1° on Prophesee-HP), suggesting that its orientation-token design does not transfer well to sparse event inputs. Notably, in the 40 ms comparison on Davis-HP (Table 5), all RGB-frame baselines degrade substantially (e.g., 6DRepNet RGB: MAE 9.64° vs. 7.32° for its event variant), confirming that event data compensates for poor lighting and rapid motion where RGB fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established head pose estimation (HPE) task to the event camera modality, a direction explicitly absent from prior HPE literature. Methodologically, it builds on the 6D rotation matrix representation and geodesic loss from Hempel et al. (6DRepNet, ICIP 2022) for the prediction head, and adopts RepVGG blocks for lightweight feature extraction. The ESTF and EMPA modules are novel architectural contributions specific to the sparsity and motion-centric nature of event streams, drawing on adaptive Sobel filtering and dilated attention respectively. In the broader event-camera literature, prior work has addressed object detection, SLAM, hand pose, and human pose estimation, but HPE had not been tackled, making this the first event-native HPE pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing two large-scale, multi-scenario event-based HPE datasets (Prophesee-HP and Davis-HP) with synchronized gyroscope ground truth, this paper provides the first benchmark for event-based HPE, enabling future work to be evaluated under standardized conditions. The finding in Table 5 that event data consistently outperforms RGB data for all five existing HPE methods under challenging lighting and motion is a notable conceptual result suggesting event cameras could serve as a drop-in modality upgrade for HPE in adverse conditions. However, the evidence is limited to a single dataset with paired RGB/event data, and the absolute error levels (~6–7° MAE) remain moderate. Open questions include generalization to other event sensor hardware, real-time deployment feasibility, and whether RGB+event fusion could further close the gap in well-lit, static scenarios where RGB already excels.
