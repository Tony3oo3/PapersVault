## Problem

When zooming between ultra-wide-angle (UW) and wide-angle (W) cameras on mobile phones, the lens switch at the boundary causes noticeable jumps in geometric content and image color, degrading the user's zoom experience. Frame interpolation (FI) is a natural solution, but real-world ground-truth intermediate frames are impossible to capture because no physical camera exists between the two lenses, making it infeasible to train or fine-tune FI models for this specific task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241333/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The FI model takes a dual-camera image pair (UW and W, each 1632×1216) along with a scalar temporal encoding t_i ∈ [0,1] indicating the relative zoom position, and produces a single intermediate frame at that zoom level. The data factory generates 33-frame zoom sequences per scene by linearly interpolating extrinsic parameters in the SE(3) Lie algebra, intrinsic parameters, and a camera-specific encoding between the UW and W endpoints. The synthetic training set comprises 155 sequences from 78 scenes, while the real-world evaluation set contains 100 non-overlapping scenes with no ground truth.

## Method

ZoomGS extends 3D Gaussian Splatting with a Camera Transition (CamTrans) module that constructs a camera-specific 3D model from a shared base (UW camera) Gaussian representation. Given a scalar camera encoding e (0.0 for UW, 1.0 for W, interpolated for virtual cameras) and the base Gaussian properties {x, c, Σ, α}, a small MLP predicts position offset Δx and color offset Δc, yielding camera-specific Gaussians {x+Δx, c+Δc, Σ, α}. Two regularization terms are critical: an identity loss that forces the MLP to output zero residuals when e = e_uw, and a Lipschitz regularization (via weight normalization with a trainable bound) that ensures smooth, artifact-free transitions as e varies continuously. Virtual camera parameters are constructed by interpolating extrinsics in SE(3), intrinsics linearly, and the camera encoding linearly; the resulting zoom sequences are then used to fine-tune pre-trained FI models (RIFE, IFRNet, AMT, UPRNet, EMAVFI, EDSC) with a Laplacian pyramid L1 loss plus VGG-19 perceptual loss.

## Results

On the synthetic DCSZ dataset, fine-tuned FI models achieve substantial gains over their pre-trained counterparts: EMAVFI improves from 22.21 to 23.76 dB PSNR (+1.55 dB) and from 0.220 to 0.088 LPIPS, while UPRNet jumps from 19.46 to 23.61 dB PSNR (+4.15 dB). On the real-world dataset (no ground truth), fine-tuned models consistently outperform originals on all four no-reference metrics; for example, Ours(RIFE) achieves the best CLIP-IQA (0.5605) and MUSIQ (65.383), and Ours(EMAVFI) attains the lowest NIQE (3.64) and PI (3.65). The largest relative improvement is seen in LPIPS on the synthetic set, where all fine-tuned models reduce the distance by roughly 55–60% compared to their pre-trained versions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new FI architecture; instead, it fine-tunes six existing FI methods (EDSC, IFRNet, RIFE, AMT, UPRNet, EMAVFI) on synthetic zoom sequences generated by the ZoomGS data factory. Every fine-tuned model outperforms its pre-trained counterpart on both the synthetic and real-world datasets, confirming the data factory's effectiveness. The largest PSNR gain is for UPRNet (+4.15 dB, from 19.46 to 23.61), while EMAVFI achieves the best absolute synthetic PSNR (23.76 dB) and the lowest LPIPS (0.088). On the real-world set, Ours(RIFE) leads in CLIP-IQA (0.5605) and MUSIQ (65.383), whereas Ours(EMAVFI) achieves the best NIQE (3.64) and PI (3.65). In the ablation (Table 2), ZoomGS also outperforms vanilla 3DGS [34] trained on both cameras (27.69 vs. 25.96 dB average PSNR), validating the camera-specific encoding design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by introducing a camera-specific encoding and a lightweight CamTrans MLP that transforms a base (UW camera) Gaussian model into camera-specific variants, decoupling scene geometry from camera-dependent imaging characteristics (ISP, distortion). It also builds on the Lipschitz regularization framework for smooth neural function learning to ensure that small changes in camera encoding produce smooth changes in the rendered 3D model. On the application side, it repurposes a suite of established frame interpolation architectures (RIFE, IFRNet, AMT, UPRNet, EMAVFI, EDSC) by fine-tuning them on synthetic zoom sequences generated by the proposed data factory, rather than proposing a new FI network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a tangible mobile-phone UX problem (the visible jump when switching between ultra-wide and wide-angle lenses) and provides a practical pipeline—3D reconstruction → virtual camera interpolation → FI fine-tuning—that could be adopted by smartphone manufacturers. A key open question is how well the synthetic-to-real gap closes for scenes with strong reflections, transparent objects, or complex ISP pipelines that 3DGS may not fully capture; the paper validates generalization only via no-reference metrics on a 100-scene real-world set. The data factory paradigm (using 3D reconstruction to generate training data when physical ground truth is infeasible) is broadly applicable to other multi-sensor interpolation tasks, but its scalability to arbitrary camera counts or large-baseline setups remains unexplored.
