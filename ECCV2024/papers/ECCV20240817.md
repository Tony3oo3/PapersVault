## Problem

Cap3D, the primary 3D-text dataset for Objaverse, produces hallucinated captions because its rendering pipeline places cameras at heuristic horizontal angles relative to each object's arbitrary default orientation, yielding atypical views that standard image captioning models misinterpret. These per-view errors compound when an LLM aggregates the captions into a final description, since the text-only summarizer cannot detect or correct visual misperceptions. The paper identifies this view-selection failure as the root cause and asks how to automatically identify which rendered views are most representative of a 3D object's true characteristics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object (mesh or point cloud) and renders it into 28 views using two complementary strategies: 8 views on a grey background with Blender's CYCLES ray-tracer and 20 views on a transparent background with EEVEE real-time rendering. Each view is captioned 5 times by BLIP2, producing 140 candidate captions. The output is a single holistic 3D caption generated by GPT4-Vision from only the top-6 ranked views, accompanied by 20 rendered images per caption with intrinsic/extrinsic camera parameters, depth maps, and segmentation masks (60 million PNG images total across the 1M-object dataset). For the VQA extension, the input is a pair of images plus two candidate declarative statements, and the output is the statement with the highest diffusion-based alignment score.

## Method

DiffuRank scores the alignment between each candidate caption and the 3D object by exploiting the denoising objective of a pre-trained text-to-3D diffusion model (Shap-E). Given the object's latent code E_encoder(O) extracted by Shap-E's transmitter, the method samples multiple (timestamp t, Gaussian noise ε) pairs, adds noise to the latent to form O_t, and computes the diffusion loss ‖D_text-to-3D(O_t | c) − O_0‖ for each caption c; captions that accurately describe the object yield lower average loss because they provide a more informative conditioning signal for denoising. No gradients are computed—only forward passes accumulate the score estimate. The 140 captions are ranked by their negative average loss, and the top-6 views are forwarded to GPT4-Vision for final caption synthesis. The same scoring principle extends to 2D by substituting Stable Diffusion's ε-prediction loss, enabling zero-shot VQA that outperforms CLIP on the MMVP benchmark.

## Results

On the Objaverse captioning evaluation (5k objects), DiffuRank + GPT4-Vision achieves a CLIP score of 74.6, CLIP R@1 of 26.7, R@5 of 48.2, and R@10 of 57.5, outperforming Cap3D (71.2 / 20.5 / 40.8 / 51.9) and human-authored captions (66.2 / 8.9 / 21.0 / 27.8). In human A/B testing, our captions are preferred over Cap3D in 60.2% of quality comparisons and 63.9% of hallucination comparisons. On the MMVP VQA benchmark, DiffuRank with Stable Diffusion achieves 30.7% accuracy, substantially surpassing CLIP (13.3%) though trailing Gemini (40.7%) and GPT4-Vision (38.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cap3D, DiffuRank + GPT4-Vision wins on every CLIP metric (74.6 vs. 71.2 score; 26.7 vs. 20.5 R@1) and in human A/B testing (60.2% quality win rate, 63.9% hallucination win rate). Against human-authored captions, the gap is even larger: 74.6 vs. 66.2 CLIP score and 26.7 vs. 8.9 R@1, with a 62.1% quality win rate. Among ablation baselines, using all 28 views (73.5) or horizontal 6-views (73.8) both underperform the DiffuRank-selected top-6 (74.6), while the bottom-6 views (72.8) perform worst, confirming that view selection quality matters more than view quantity. In downstream text-to-3D fine-tuning (Table 2), fine-tuning Shap-E on Cap3D captions actually degrades performance (CLIP 68.2 vs. 68.8 pre-trained), whereas fine-tuning on the corrected captions improves it (69.4 at 330k, 69.8 at 825k). On the MMVP VQA benchmark, DiffuRank (30.7%) decisively beats CLIP (13.3%) but remains below Gemini (40.7%) and GPT4-Vision (38.7%), indicating the diffusion-based alignment signal is strong but not yet competitive with the strongest VLMs on fine-grained visual reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Cap3D 3D captioning pipeline by introducing DiffuRank, a view-ranking mechanism that repurposes a pre-trained text-to-3D diffusion model (Shap-E) as an alignment scorer between rendered views and the underlying 3D object. The conceptual foundation draws from the observation that diffusion denoising loss can serve as a zero-shot similarity signal (Li et al., 2023), here generalized from 2D image classification to 3D view selection. The final captioning stage leverages GPT4-Vision as a holistic vision-language model, replacing Cap3D's text-only GPT4 summarization that cannot correct view-level hallucinations. The 2D VQA extension applies the same diffusion-loss-as-score principle with Stable Diffusion, positioning DiffuRank as a general cross-modal alignment tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Correcting ~200k hallucinated captions in Cap3D and expanding to 1M entries with 60M rendered images addresses a data-quality bottleneck that affects a large body of downstream text-to-3D, image-to-3D, and 3D-LLM research. The finding that fine-tuning Shap-E on Cap3D captions actually degrades performance relative to the pre-trained model underscores how pervasive caption errors can be harmful at scale. Open questions include whether DiffuRank's ranking quality improves with stronger text-to-3D diffusion backbones, whether the 'fewer views is better' phenomenon generalizes beyond GPT4-Vision, and whether the approach transfers to non-Objaverse 3D corpora with different rendering conventions.
