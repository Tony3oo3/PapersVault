## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting models produce files of over 1 GB per scene, creating significant barriers for transmission and storage. Existing compression approaches rely on intensive training to distill information into compact surrogate models, which is impractical for resource-constrained deployment and incompatible with 3D-GS extensions. The paper addresses this by designing a post-training codec that compresses an already-trained 3D-GS model without requiring extensive retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240886/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian Splatting checkpoint (30,000 iterations, 32-bit float) as input and produces a single compressed file containing an octree, DC and quantized AC coefficients, a vector-quantization codebook with index mapping, and metadata (min-max values, octree depth, block size), all packed via LZ77. The compressed file is decoded back into a 3D-GS model that renders novel views at the original resolution (e.g., 1600×1063 for Mip-NeRF 360). An optional finetuning stage (fixing coordinates, updating only attributes with a straight-through estimator through quantization) can be applied to recover quality.

## Method

MesonGS is a four-stage post-training codec. First, it prunes insignificant Gaussians using a combined importance score: a view-dependent term (the alpha-composited rendering contribution of each Gaussian across training views) multiplied by a view-independent term (normalized volume raised to a power β), which outperforms gradient-based or opacity-only criteria. Second, geometry is compressed via an octree, and rotation quaternions (4 values) are losslessly replaced by Euler angles (3 values), avoiding the positive-definiteness violations that plague covariance-based alternatives after quantization. Third, important attributes (opacity, 0D-SH, scales, Euler angles) undergo Region-Adaptive Hierarchical Transform (RAHT) to concentrate energy into low-entropy AC coefficients, followed by block-wise quantization (8 or 16 bits) that prevents quality collapse from channel-level quantization; RAHT is deliberately skipped for scales at 8-bit due to exponential activation amplifying errors. Fourth, the dominant 1+D SH coefficients (85.7% of file size) are compressed via vector quantization with a batched k-means codebook. All components are packed with LZ77, and an optional finetune with straight-through gradients restores rendering quality.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, MesonGS compresses 3D Gaussians from 641.70 MB to 27.62 MB (~23× reduction) while achieving PSNR 28.61 dB after finetuning, only 0.37 dB below the uncompressed 3DGS (28.98 dB). On Tank&Temples, the compressed file is 16.99 MB (25× reduction) with PSNR 23.32 dB, matching the uncompressed baseline. On Deep Blending, MesonGS-FT reaches PSNR 29.51 dB at 24.76 MB (28× reduction) versus 29.56 dB for the original 703.77 MB model. Encoding time is dramatically faster: 4 s on Synthetic-NeRF and 1 min on Mip-NeRF 360, compared to 30 s and 5 min for C3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against C3DGS [33], MesonGS-FT achieves slightly higher PSNR on all three datasets (28.61 vs 28.49 on Mip-NeRF 360; 23.32 vs 23.32 on Tank&Temples; 29.51 vs 29.38 on Deep Blending) while producing smaller files (27.62 vs 27.82 MB; 16.99 vs 17.28 MB; 24.76 vs 25.30 MB). Against Lee et al. [25], MesonGS-FT trades a small PSNR drop on Deep Blending (29.51 vs 29.79) for a substantially smaller file (24.76 vs 43.20 MB, a 43% reduction). On Synthetic-NeRF at the ~1 MB budget, MesonGS-FT (PSNR 31.75, 1.03 MB) is competitive with VQRF (31.77, 1.43 MB) and ACRF (31.79, 1.15 MB) while being the smallest file. In the pruning ablation (Table 3), MesonGS's combined view-dependent and view-independent importance score (I_d·I_i) outperforms both C3DGS's gradient-based score (avg PSNR 29.02 vs 26.84) and LightGaussian's opacity-based score (29.02 vs 26.84) when pruning 66% of Gaussians. Encoding time is the most dramatic advantage: MesonGS completes in 4 s on Synthetic-NeRF and 1 min on Mip-NeRF 360, versus 30 s / 5 min for C3DGS and 480 s / 33 min for Lee et al.

## Relation to Prior Work

MesonGS positions itself as a post-training codec for 3D Gaussians, deliberately departing from concurrent compression works (C3DGS, LightGaussian, Lee et al.) that rely on intensive retraining or distillation to fit information into compact models. It repurposes classical point cloud compression machinery—octree geometry encoding, region-adaptive hierarchical transform (RAHT), and entropy coding—from the MPEG point cloud compression literature, adapting them to the richer attribute space of 3D Gaussians. A key conceptual departure is replacing rotation quaternions with Euler angles (a lossless 4→3 reduction) rather than the covariance-matrix substitution used by C3DGS, motivated by the observation that quantized covariance matrices frequently lose positive definiteness. Block quantization and vector quantization for higher-degree SH coefficients are borrowed from the LLM quantization and classical VQ literature, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling 3D Gaussian scenes to be transmitted and stored at a fraction of their original size (e.g., ~27 MB vs. 642 MB on Mip-NeRF 360) without the multi-hour retraining that concurrent methods demand, making compression feasible on resource-constrained devices. The paper's first-of-its-kind comparison between compressed 3D Gaussians and compressed NeRF at the 1 MB budget reveals that grid-based NeRF retains a slight quality edge, suggesting that the explicit point-based representation of 3D-GS carries inherent redundancy that is harder to eliminate than the structured grid of NeRF. Open questions include whether the fixed-coordinate finetuning limitation can be relaxed, whether the pruning criterion generalizes to dynamic or 4D Gaussian extensions, and whether the RAHT+quantization pipeline can be made differentiable end-to-end to enable learned entropy models.
