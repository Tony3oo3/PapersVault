## Problem

Existing 3D-aware image editing methods either rely on GANs with limited modeling capacity or train specialized diffusion models on synthetic multi-view datasets, both of which fail to generalize to open-domain images with diverse layouts and styles. The paper asks whether pre-trained large-scale text-to-image diffusion models can be leveraged directly—without multi-view training—to perform 3D manipulations (rotation, translation) on arbitrary single images while preserving appearance and geometric consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241505/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image, a binary object selection mask (produced by SAM), and a 3D transformation (rotation and/or translation) as input, and outputs a single edited image showing the object from the new viewpoint composited into the scene. A monocular depth map (ZoeDepth) is estimated from the input as an intermediate 3D representation, and a brief 60-step LoRA tuning on the input image (~10 s) provides global semantic conditioning for the diffusion model. The entire pipeline runs in approximately 20 seconds per edit on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-phase iterative algorithm (view synthesis → undistortion → shape alignment) that exploits pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 in a dual role: as an appearance prior for texture completion and as a geometry critic that corrects 3D shape distortions. View synthesis lifts the image to a 3D mesh via depth warping, then fills holes through a layered inpainting scheme (background inpainting with depth-guided ControlNet, foreground warping with edge replication for thickness, and a final combined generative inpainting). Undistortion adapts SDEdit by replacing random noise with deterministic DDIM inversion and adding LoRA conditioning, so the diffusion model rectifies geometric distortions (bent lines, stretched textures) while preserving appearance; a max-noise-level parameter σ controls the trade-off between correction strength and fidelity. Shape alignment optimizes the depth map by minimizing reprojection error over dense correspondences (bridged through the pre-undistortion image for reliable matching), producing a corrected 3D shape for the next iteration. Over 3 iterations with σ decreasing from 0.5 to 0.3, geometric distortion progressively reduces while consistency with the original image increases.

## Results

On a 160-case benchmark spanning 10 object categories, the method achieves the best scores on all four appearance consistency metrics: Perceptual Similarity 0.905, LPIPS 0.102, Mean Confidence 0.203, and Confident Area 0.356. In a user study with 33 participants, the method's edits were preferred in 71.36% of cases, more than 5× higher than the second-best method (Stable Zero123). The iterative process (3 iterations with σ = 0.5, 0.4, 0.3) progressively improves all metrics, with Confident Area rising from 0.235 to 0.356 across iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two trained 3D-aware editing methods: OBJect-3DIT [31] and Zero123 [28] (including a Stability AI retrained checkpoint, Stable-Zero123). The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest baseline (Stable-Zero123), the gains are: +0.035 in Perceptual Similarity (0.905 vs 0.870), −0.030 in LPIPS (0.102 vs 0.132), +0.057 in Mean Confidence (0.203 vs 0.146), and +0.111 in Confident Area (0.356 vs 0.245). OBJect-3DIT underperforms most notably on layout plausibility, failing on a large portion of test cases due to its limited synthetic training data. Zero123 struggles with complex textures (e.g., furry animals) and produces blurrier results, partly due to its lower 256×256 training resolution versus the 512×512 resolution used here. The user study confirms these quantitative findings, with participants preferring the proposed method in 71.36% of cases versus roughly 14% for Stable-Zero123.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior 3D-aware image editing methods (OBJect-3DIT, Zero123) that require training specialized diffusion models on synthetic multi-view renderings, instead operating entirely tuning-free with a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion model. The undistortion phase directly adapts SDEdit's noising-denoising framework, modified with LoRA conditioning for semantic consistency and DDIM inversion for appearance preservation. The broader lineage draws from tuning-free diffusion editing (Prompt-to-Prompt, Null-text Inversion) and depth-based novel view synthesis, but the key conceptual departure is repurposing the diffusion model's denoising process as a geometry critic that corrects 3D shape distortions, creating a feedback loop with explicit depth optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that pre-trained diffusion models can simultaneously serve as appearance priors and geometry critics without any multi-view training, this work removes the domain gap that has limited prior 3D-aware editing to narrow synthetic distributions. The iterative depth-alignment loop is a practical recipe that could be adapted to other depth-assisted generation tasks. Limitations remain: fine detail preservation is bounded by the base model, lighting/shadow physical correctness is not guaranteed, and extreme transformations still fail due to compounding depth errors. Open questions include whether the geometry-critic role can be formalized beyond the heuristic iterative loop, and whether the approach extends to multi-object scenes with complex occlusion reasoning.
