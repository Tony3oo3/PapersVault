## Problem

State-of-the-art monocular 3D human digitization methods achieve impressive geometric accuracy but produce poor texture quality, particularly in occluded regions such as the back of a person in frontal-view images. This limitation stems from the scarcity of large-scale, diverse 3D scan datasets, while abundant 2D fashion datasets remain underutilized for 3D reconstruction. No prior work has effectively bridged this domain gap to leverage 2D texture diversity for 3D human digitization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single frontal RGB image of a clothed person as input and produces a fully textured 3D mesh as output. Intermediate outputs include a synthesized back-view image (generated by the hallucinator conditioned on SMPL-X segmentation maps and silhouette), front and back normal maps, and per-vertex texture predictions. The geometry is reconstructed via PIFuHD's dual-resolution implicit function, and the texture is refined at per-vertex level using a UNet+MLP completion network with SIREN activations for high-frequency detail.

## Method

FAMOUS introduces a Distributionally Aligned Hallucinator (DAH) built on a pretrained StyleGAN-based person image synthesizer (NTED), which generates a back-view image from the frontal input to provide an additional viewpoint for 3D reconstruction. The key technical contribution is Disentangled Domain Alignment (DDA), a three-stage self-supervised procedure that progressively aligns the hallucinator from the 2D fashion source domain to the 3D target domain: Semantic Alignment generates full-body front/back pseudo-pairs from partial fashion images, Pose Alignment shifts the pose distribution using target 3D dataset guidance (with discriminator-based filtering to prevent degenerate samples), and Simultaneous Training jointly fine-tunes on both aligned 2D fashion images and 2D renderings of the 3D dataset for style alignment. For geometry, the DAH-predicted back view is rerouted as input to PIFuHD's back normal estimator, improving occluded-region reconstruction. For texture, front and back views are projected onto pixel-aligned query points and refined by a UNet+MLP network with SIREN activations using L1 loss.

## Results

On the RenderPeople test set (85 scans), FAMOUS achieves the best texture quality with FID of 55.07 (vs. 67.69 for PIFu, a ~19% relative improvement) and IS of 3.87 (vs. 3.52 for PIFu, ~6% improvement). For shape reconstruction, FAMOUS outperforms all baselines across all three metrics: Chamfer distance 1.41 (vs. 1.57 for PIFuHD), point-to-surface distance 1.27 (vs. 1.40 for PIFuHD), and normal MSE 0.037 (vs. 0.053 for PIFuHD). The method also achieves the best LPIPS (0.1425) and SSIM (0.9140) among texture-predicting methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAMOUS outperforms PIFu [33] on all four texture metrics (FID 55.07 vs. 67.69, IS 3.87 vs. 3.52, LPIPS 0.1425 vs. 0.1551, SSIM 0.9140 vs. 0.9101) and on all three shape metrics, making it the strongest method that predicts both shape and texture. Against PaMIR [52], FAMOUS wins decisively on every reported metric, with particularly large gaps in shape (Chamfer 1.41 vs. 2.62, P2S 1.27 vs. 2.01). Among shape-only methods, PIFuHD [34] is the closest competitor (Chamfer 1.57, P2S 1.40, NmlMSE 0.053), but FAMOUS still leads by 10–30% on each shape metric while additionally producing high-quality textures. ICON [44] and ECON [43] perform worse than PIFuHD on the RenderPeople benchmark, which the authors attribute to these SMPL-X-based methods excelling primarily on extreme acrobatic poses not prevalent in the casual-pose RenderPeople dataset. The paper notes that SSIM slightly favors PIFu's smoother predictions, but deep perceptual metrics (FID, IS, LPIPS) confirm FAMOUS preserves finer high-frequency texture detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAMOUS builds on the PIFuHD implicit-function pipeline for geometry and the NTED StyleGAN-based hallucinator for view synthesis, but departs from prior monocular 3D human digitization work (PIFu, PaMIR, ICON, ECON, PHORHUM) by introducing a Disentangled Domain Alignment (DDA) strategy that leverages large-scale 2D fashion datasets (DeepFashion) to compensate for the limited texture diversity in 3D scan collections. Rather than treating 2D and 3D data as separate domains to be bridged by standard domain adaptation, the paper factorizes the domain gap into semantic, pose, and style components and aligns them sequentially through self-supervised pseudo-pair generation. This positions the work at the intersection of pose-guided person image synthesis and implicit 3D reconstruction, a combination not previously explored for the joint shape-and-texture task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that abundant 2D fashion imagery can meaningfully improve both texture fidelity and geometric accuracy in 3D human digitization—addresses a fundamental data bottleneck that has constrained the field. The released full-body front-back pair dataset with COCO keypoints and the open-sourced codebase lower the barrier for follow-up work. However, the approach remains limited to casual/fashion poses and common clothing types, and the authors acknowledge that rare garments or acrobatic poses outside the training distribution remain challenging. An open question is whether the DDA framework generalizes beyond human digitization to other 3D reconstruction tasks where 2D data is abundant but 3D scans are scarce, and whether the recently available diffusion-based hallucinators (which the authors could not use due to memory constraints) would further close the gap.
