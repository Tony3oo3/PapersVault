## Problem

Existing CLIP-based approaches for zero-shot sketch-based image retrieval (ZS-SBIR) rely on uni-modal or unidirectional multi-modal prompting that fails to fully exploit CLIP's integrated visual-textual synergy, leading to suboptimal generalization across seen and unseen classes. Additionally, prior patch-shuffling strategies for fine-grained SBIR indiscriminately match permuted sketch-photo pairs without regard to natural object structure, risking overfitting and poor local-to-global alignment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a hand-drawn sketch query (partitioned into fixed-size patches for the ViT-B/32 encoder) paired with a text prompt template (e.g., 'sketch of a [CLS]' for ZS-SBIR or 'visual representation of [CLS]' for FG-ZS-SBIR) and produces a ranked list of gallery photos via nearest-neighbor search in CLIP's visual embedding space. For GZS-SBIR, the gallery includes both seen and unseen class photos; for FG-ZS-SBIR, retrieval is restricted to a single category at a time with instance-level matching. During inference, the multi-class visual prompt tokens are generated exclusively from seen-class names to avoid dependence on test labels.

## Method

SpLIP introduces a bi-directional, layer-wise prompt-sharing mechanism between frozen CLIP's text and vision encoders: (1) vision-guided textual prompting maps image patch embeddings through a linear block B_t to produce learnable tokens injected at every layer of the text encoder, replacing random initialization with visually-conditioned tokens; (2) text-guided visual prompting injects two types of tokens into each vision encoder layer — domain-specific tokens from the prompt template via B_v, and class-agnostic consolidated tokens from all seen-class text embeddings at the corresponding text encoder layer via B_vt. Training combines an adaptive-margin triplet loss (where the margin is the cosine similarity between positive and negative class text embeddings, making it larger for semantically close classes), a text-image classification loss, and a novel conditional cross-modal jigsaw loss that trains a transformer decoder to predict sketch patch permutations using a positive photo as auxiliary context, with a hinge objective ensuring the positive photo yields lower prediction error than a negative photo. Only LayerNorm parameters and the prompt mapping blocks are trained, keeping the CLIP backbone frozen.

## Results

On categorical ZS-SBIR, SpLIP achieves mAP@all of 80.2 on Sketchy-1-Ext, 76.4 on Sketchy-2-Ext, 73.1 on TU-Berlin-Ext, and 34.2 on QuickDraw-Ext, outperforming the best CLIP-based baseline (MARL) by 1.5–7.3% in mAP across datasets. On the more challenging GZS-SBIR setting, SpLIP reaches 68.2 mAP@200 on Sketchy-2-Ext and 66.7 mAP@all on TU-Berlin-Ext, surpassing the second-best method by 4.8% and 4.1% respectively. For fine-grained ZS-SBIR on Sketchy, SpLIP attains 33.45% Acc@1 and 66.71% Acc@5, leading the next-best method (CLIP-AT) by 3.21% and 4.37% respectively. In the cross-dataset generalization setting (train on Sketchy-Ext, test on TU-Berlin-Ext/QuickDraw-Ext), SpLIP improves mAP@all by 14.2% and 15.1% over CLIP-AT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpLIP consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest CNN-based method (BDA, 15.4 mAP@all on QuickDraw-Ext), SpLIP gains 18.8%; against the best ViT-based method (ZSE-Ret, 14.2 mAP@all on QuickDraw-Ext), the margin is 20.0%. Among CLIP-based competitors, SpLIP surpasses MARL (the prior best) by 1.5% mAP on QuickDraw-Ext, 2.6% on TU-Berlin-Ext, and 7.3% on Sketchy-2-Ext, while also leading CLIP-AT by 4.1% on Sketchy-2-Ext mAP@200. On GZS-SBIR, SpLIP beats MARL by 5.9% mAP@200 on Sketchy-2-Ext and 4.1% mAP@all on TU-Berlin-Ext, demonstrating stronger resistance to training-class bias. For FG-ZS-SBIR, SpLIP's 33.45% Acc@1 exceeds CLIP-AT (28.68%) by 4.77% and MARL (29.96%) by 3.49%, with the conditional cross-modal jigsaw loss contributing an additional ~3.4% over the base model. In the cross-dataset protocol, SpLIP's advantage over CLIP-AT widens substantially to 14.2% (TU-Berlin-Ext) and 15.1% (QuickDraw-Ext) in mAP@all, indicating superior generalization to unseen domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-based zero-shot SBIR methods, most directly building on CLIP-AT [37] which introduced visual prompts and patch shuffling for sketch-photo alignment, and on multi-modal prompt learning frameworks like MAPLE [19], SRP [20], and CGPL [36] that apply simultaneous visual and textual prompting to CLIP. The key conceptual departure is from the uni-directional or limited token sharing in prior multi-modal prompting to a bi-directional, layer-wise knowledge exchange between CLIP's text and vision encoders, where visual patch embeddings inform textual prompt tokens and conversely, class-conditioned textual embeddings enrich visual prompt tokens. Additionally, the paper repurposes the jigsaw puzzle task [29] into a conditional cross-modal objective, diverging from prior patch-shuffling approaches [32, 37] that use uniform permutation matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bi-directional prompt-sharing mechanism offers a more principled way to exploit CLIP's dual-modality synergy for cross-domain retrieval, and the consistent gains across ZS-SBIR, GZS-SBIR, and FG-ZS-SBIR settings (plus cross-dataset generalization) suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. However, the improvements over the strongest CLIP-based baseline (MARL) remain modest (1.5–4.8% mAP), and the frozen-backbone constraint limits how much the method can adapt to domain-specific sketch characteristics. Open questions include whether the bi-directional prompting strategy transfers to other vision-language retrieval tasks beyond SBIR, and whether partial fine-tuning of CLIP layers could complement rather than conflict with the prompt-based approach.
