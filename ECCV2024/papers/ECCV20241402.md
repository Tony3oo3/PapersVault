## Problem

Existing deep clustering methods rely on center-based algorithms (K-means, FCM) that are sensitive to initialization and lack fine-grained cluster boundaries, or on contrastive frameworks that impose balanced-cluster assumptions and require extensive hyperparameter tuning. The alternating schedule of offline clustering and model training introduces instability and high computational cost, while online batch variants suffer from collapsing without explicit size constraints. The paper asks whether clustering can be performed without cluster centers at all, in a form that is naturally amenable to online mini-batch deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for STL-10 and ImageNet subsets) and produces a K-dimensional soft probability vector per sample, from which a hard cluster label is obtained by argmax. Each image is augmented into two views (one SimCLR-style, one RandAugment-style) for the contrastive head, while the OPA clustering head operates on a single mini-batch of B samples, requiring only one B×B distance matrix multiplication per step. No cluster-size or orthogonality constraints are imposed on the output probabilities.

## Method

PAC replaces cluster centers with probability-vector inner products: the objective J_pac = Σᵢ Σⱼ pᵢᵀpⱼ‖xᵢ−xⱼ‖² minimizes intra-cluster distance weighted by pairwise probability agreement, and the Lagrangian solution yields an iterative update p_{i,k} = s_{i,k}^{−α} / Σᵣ s_{i,r}^{−α} where s_{i,k} = Σ_{j≠i} p_{j,k} d̃_{i,j} aggregates all other samples' probabilities weighted by distance. The OPA module extends this to online mini-batch clustering by computing S = D̃·P̂ (a single matrix multiply), normalizing to target codes Q, and training the network via KL(Q‖P̂) — a supervised-style loss that avoids solving the full constrained optimization. DPAC pairs OPA with a weighted contrastive loss (WCL) that gates negative-sample repulsion by (1 − p̂ᵢᵀp̂ⱼ) to prevent semantic distortion, and trains end-to-end with ResNet-34 over 1000 pre-training + 200 clustering epochs without any balanced-cluster regularizer.

## Results

DPAC^opa achieves 90.7% ACC on CIFAR-10, 92.6% ACC on STL-10, 96.2% ACC on ImageNet-10, and 65.5% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs, outperforming all online deep clustering baselines without any cluster size constraint. With self-labeling fine-tuning (DPAC^opa†), performance further improves to 93.4% ACC on CIFAR-10, 93.4% ACC on STL-10, 97.0% ACC on ImageNet-10, and 72.6% ACC on ImageNet-Dogs, surpassing the best fine-tuned baselines (SPICE†, SeCu†, TCL†) across all benchmarks. On the machine clustering side, PAC achieves 65.1% ACC on Coil-100 and 61.8% ACC on Isolet, outperforming KM, FCM, SC, and FINCH on these datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPAC^opa (90.7% ACC on CIFAR-10) narrowly beats the strongest online baseline TCC (90.6%) and substantially outperforms GCC (85.6%), CC (79.0%), and PICA (69.6%), while also surpassing offline methods PCL (87.4%) and IDFD (81.5%). On STL-10, DPAC^opa (92.6%) leads SPICE (90.8%) by 1.8 points and TCC (81.4%) by 11.2 points. On ImageNet-10, DPAC^opa (96.2%) exceeds IDFD (95.4%) and all online methods (TCC 89.7%, SPICE 92.1%). On CIFAR-100, DPAC^opa (51.6%) is slightly behind offline PCL (52.6%) but ahead of all online baselines (SeCu 51.6%, TCC 49.1%). With self-labeling fine-tuning, DPAC^opa† (93.4% on CIFAR-10, 97.0% on ImageNet-10, 72.6% on ImageNet-Dogs) surpasses all fine-tuned competitors including SPICE† (92.6/95.9/67.5) and SeCu† (93.0/–/–), confirming the extensibility of the OPA module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant center-based clustering paradigm (K-means, FCM) by eliminating cluster centers entirely, instead formulating clustering as a probability aggregation problem over pairwise distances. The PAC algorithm is directly derived from FCM's Lagrange optimization with a fuzzy exponent, but replaces center assignment with a score computed by aggregating other samples' probabilities weighted by distance. On the deep learning side, DPAC builds on SimCLR's contrastive framework, modifying it with a cluster-aware weighted contrastive loss, and positions itself against both batch clustering methods (ODC, CoKe) that require center update rules and contrastive clustering methods (PICA, CC, TCC) that impose balanced cluster constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The centerless formulation offers a conceptually cleaner alternative to center-based clustering, eliminating initialization sensitivity and the need for balanced cluster constraints that plague most online deep clustering methods. The OPA module's simplicity (a single matrix multiplication, power, and normalization per batch) makes it an attractive plug-in for existing self-supervised pipelines. However, the O(N²K) complexity of PAC limits scalability to very large datasets, the fuzzy exponent m has a narrow optimal range that tightens as features become less separable, and the method still requires the number of clusters K to be specified in advance. Open questions include whether the probability aggregation idea extends to streaming or truly online settings with non-stationary data, and whether the approach generalizes beyond image clustering to other modalities.
