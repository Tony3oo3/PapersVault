## Problem

Generating 3D objects from a single image remains challenging because sparse-view reconstruction produces blurred outputs from unseen views, while 3D-aware diffusion priors trained on synthetic data yield oversimplified geometries. Existing methods also lack a principled mechanism to balance diversity in the unseen 'darkside' of objects with 3D consistency, often producing either overly smooth or inconsistent results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240586/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (segmented and recentered via SAM) and an optional text prompt (auto-generated by GPT-4 or user-specified) as input, and produces a textured triangle mesh extracted from an 80³ FlexiCubes voxel grid. Two operating modes are provided: Vista3D-S completes generation in ~5 minutes using only the 3D-aware prior, while Vista3D-L runs 15–20 minutes with dual diffusion priors for enhanced detail and darkside diversity. Rendering resolution is progressively increased from 64 to 512 pixels during optimization.

## Method

Vista3D employs a coarse-to-fine pipeline: the coarse phase rapidly learns basic geometry via 3D Gaussian Splatting optimized with SDS from Zero-1-to-3 XL, stabilized by a Top-K gradient-based densification strategy and novel scale/transmittance regularization terms. The fine phase extracts an SDF from the Gaussian density field, refines it with FlexiCubes (a differentiable isosurface with learnable deformations and interpolation weights), and learns texture through a disentangled representation using two hash encodings (front-facing and back-facing) blended by the relative azimuth angle. The key novelty is an angular-based score composition that constrains the gradient magnitude ratio between the 3D-aware prior (Zero-1-to-3 XL) and a 2D diversity prior (Stable Diffusion) within upper and lower bounds that vary with camera azimuth and optimization iteration, enabling diverse darkside textures without sacrificing 3D consistency.

## Results

On the RealFusion dataset (15 images), Vista3D-S achieves a CLIP-Similarity of 0.831 in just 5 minutes, surpassing Magic123 (0.802, 2 h) and DreamGaussian (0.738, 2 min) while being 24× faster than Magic123. Vista3D-L, which additionally employs angular diffusion prior composition, reaches 0.868 CLIP-Similarity in 15 minutes, outperforming DreamCraft3D (0.842, 3.5 h). On the GSO dataset (30 objects), Vista3D-L attains SOTA reconstruction quality with PSNR 26.31, SSIM 0.929, and LPIPS 0.062, exceeding Magic123 (24.89 / 0.875 / 0.084) and DreamGaussian (23.43 / 0.832 / 0.092).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vista3D-S outperforms Magic123 on CLIP-Similarity (0.831 vs. 0.802) while being 24× faster (5 min vs. 2 h), and beats DreamGaussian by a wide margin (0.831 vs. 0.738) despite taking 2.5× longer. On GSO reconstruction, Vista3D-S surpasses Magic123 (PSNR 25.42 vs. 24.89) and DreamGaussian (25.42 vs. 23.43), and Vista3D-L extends the lead to 26.31 PSNR. Vista3D-L achieves the highest CLIP-Similarity (0.868) among all optimization-based methods, exceeding DreamCraft3D (0.842) by 0.026 while running 14× faster. In the user study, Vista3D-L scores 3.24/4 in view consistency and 3.33/4 in overall quality, clearly ahead of Magic123 (2.11 / 1.83) and DreamGaussian (1.78 / 2.02). Inference-only methods (One-2-3-45, Point-E, Shap-E) trail significantly on CLIP-Similarity (≈0.59) and are not competitive on GSO metrics, confirming the advantage of optimization-based approaches for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vista3D sits at the intersection of three active threads: Gaussian Splatting for fast inverse rendering (Kerbl et al., 2023), differentiable isosurface extraction for mesh refinement (FlexiCubes, Shen et al., 2023; Magic3D, Lin et al., 2023), and score distillation sampling with 2D diffusion priors (DreamFusion, Poole et al., 2023). Its key departure is a deliberate coarse-to-fine pipeline that uses Gaussian Splatting only as a transient geometry initializer before transitioning to an SDF-based FlexiCubes representation, rather than committing to a single representation end-to-end. The angular-based composition of a 3D-aware prior (Zero-1-to-3 XL) and a general 2D prior (Stable Diffusion) via gradient-magnitude bounding is a new mechanism not seen in prior SDS-based image-to-3D work, which typically relies on fixed loss weights or a single prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5-minute generation time with competitive CLIP-similarity (0.831 for Vista3D-S, 0.868 for Vista3D-L) makes high-quality single-image-to-3D practical for interactive applications like gaming and VR, where prior methods required hours. The angular composition strategy offers a principled, tunable knob for the consistency-diversity trade-off that has been handled empirically in prior work. However, the method remains per-object optimization (not truly zero-shot), requires a GPU, and the 'darkside' generation is still bounded by the quality of the 2D diffusion prior; scaling to scenes, handling multi-object inputs, and reducing reliance on per-object optimization remain open challenges.
