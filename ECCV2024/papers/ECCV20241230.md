## Problem

Existing VLMs require 32–256+ tokens per image frame, making long video processing infeasible (a 10K-frame video would exceed 320K tokens, surpassing current model capacity). Simple temporal compression strategies further degrade long-term representations, leaving a gap in how to encode hour-long videos without sacrificing critical visual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241230/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts video frames sampled at 1 FPS or a single RGB image, along with a free-form text instruction. For video input, each frame is compressed to exactly 2 tokens (1 context + 1 content), enabling 64K-token sequences covering 3+ hour movies; for single-image input, the content token expands to up to 576 tokens (at 336×336 resolution) to preserve spatial detail. Output is a free-form text response (QA answer, summary, or reasoning) generated autoregressively by the LLM.

## Method

LLaMA-VID encodes each frame with a dual-token strategy: a context token produced by cross-attending instruction-guided text queries (from a QFormer or BERT decoder) against the visual embedding via a mean-pooled softmax attention (Eq. 1), and a content token obtained by adaptive average pooling of the visual embedding to n tokens (n=1 for video, n=576 for images). Both tokens are projected into LLM space with linear layers and concatenated per frame. Training proceeds in three stages—modality alignment on 790K image/video-caption pairs, instruction tuning on ~700K multimodal QA pairs, and long-video tuning on 15K movie-level conversations generated with GPT-4 and Claude-2 from MovieNet scripts—using a frozen EVA-G visual encoder and Vicuna/LLaMA as the language backbone.

## Results

With only 2 tokens per frame, LLaMA-VID (Vicuna-7B) achieves 69.7% accuracy on MSVD-QA, 57.7% on MSRVTT-QA, and 47.4% on ActivityNet-QA, surpassing the previous best (BT-Adapter) by 2.2%, 0.7%, and 1.7% respectively. On the long-video EgoSchema benchmark, LLaMA-VID reaches 41.2–41.7% accuracy across 10–180 frames, outperforming InternVideo (31.4–32.1%) by over 9 points. On image-based benchmarks with 1 context + 576 content tokens, LLaMA-VID (Vicuna-7B) attains 64.3% on GQA, 65.1% on MMBench, 1521.4 on MME, and 54.2% on VizWiz, exceeding LLaVA-1.5 by up to 4.2% on VizWiz.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaMA-VID outperforms all 14 baselines across every benchmark evaluated. On video QA (Tables 1–2), the closest competitor is BT-Adapter [34], which LLaMA-VID surpasses by 2.2% on MSVD-QA and 1.7% on ActivityNet-QA; earlier methods like Video-ChatGPT [39] and VideoChat [30] trail by 4.8% and 13.4% on MSVD-QA respectively. On the long-video EgoSchema benchmark (Table 3), LLaMA-VID leads InternVideo [56] by ~10 points (41.2% vs 31.4% at 10 frames) and FrozenBiLM [60] by ~15 points, while also maintaining stable accuracy at 180 frames where other methods cannot operate. On SSv2 (Table 4), LLaMA-VID nearly doubles Video-ChatGPT's accuracy (20.12% vs 11.59%). On image benchmarks (Table 5), LLaMA-VID (Vicuna-7B) beats LLaVA-1.5 [32] on 6 of 8 benchmarks (e.g., +2.3% GQA, +10.7 MME, +4.2% VizWiz), outperforms Qwen-VL-Chat [4] on GQA and MME, and surpasses InstructBLIP [13], BLIP-2 [29], IDEFICS [23], and Shikra [9] by wide margins on most metrics. The only near-tie is MMBench with Vicuna-13B, where LLaMA-VID (66.6%) slightly trails LLaVA-1.5 (67.7%) by 1.1%.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaMA-VID builds directly on the BLIP-2/InstructBLIP Q-Former cross-attention mechanism for instruction-guided visual queries and the LLaVA visual instruction tuning pipeline (visual encoder + linear projector + LLM). It departs from prior video VLMs (VideoLLaMA, VideoChat, Video-ChatGPT, BT-Adapter) that represent each frame with 32–256+ tokens by introducing a dual-token strategy: a single instruction-conditioned context token generated via cross-attention, plus an adaptively pooled content token. The key conceptual departure is that the context token is not a fixed visual summary but is dynamically shaped by the user's question, effectively repurposing the Q-Former's query mechanism from a multi-token interface into a single-token condensation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-token-per-frame representation reduces per-frame cost by roughly 1/128 relative to uncompressed 256-token settings while retaining competitive accuracy, making hour-long (3+ hour, 64K-token) video understanding feasible in standard 7B/13B LLMs without architectural changes. The paper acknowledges a key limitation: extreme compression prioritizes temporal consistency at the expense of fine spatial detail within each frame, and the authors propose future work on maintaining dense spatial information outside the LLM with in-LLM token fusion. An open question is whether the instruction-guided context token generalizes to tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning (e.g., counting, localization) where the single-token bottleneck becomes a hard constraint.
