## Problem

Existing Byzantine-robust FL defenses rely on coarse-grained model-level statistics (e.g., Euclidean/cosine distances, PCA clustering) or greedy coordinate-wise filtering, which are ineffective against fine-grained attacks like Fang and AGR-agnostic that selectively poison a small subset of parameters to remain stealthy. These defenses either fail to detect such attacks or overreact by discarding benign clients, causing up to 18% accuracy degradation even under moderate attack fractions.

## Input / Output

The server receives frozen local model updates from n clients and a small clean root dataset (~100 samples, non-IID, no overlap with training/test data). It produces per-client binary parameter-level masks (same dimensionality as the model, values in {0,1}), a benign/malicious classification via GMM clustering, and an updated global model computed by FedAvg over only the detected benign clients. A trusted root model (trained l iterations on the root dataset) serves as a reference for cluster identification but is excluded from aggregation.

## Method

SKYMASK assigns a learnable mask of the same size as the model to each client's frozen update, initialized to 1s, and trains all masks jointly on the root dataset by minimizing the loss of the aggregated masked model (an element-wise weighted average using sigmoid-approximated binary masks). During this training, masks applied to malicious updates learn to suppress toxic parameters (driving them toward 0) while benign masks remain predominantly 1, creating a high-dimensional representation that separates malicious from benign updates at the parameter level. After convergence, a hard threshold binarizes the masks, and a Gaussian Mixture Model clusters them; clients whose masks fall in the same cluster as the trusted root model's mask are classified as benign. The global model is then updated via standard FedAvg over only the benign clients, making the approach attack-agnostic, modular, and effective even when up to 80% of clients are malicious.

## Results

On Fashion-MNIST (CNN), SKYMASK achieves 0.89 testing accuracy under all seven attacks, matching unattacked FedAvg exactly. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet20), SKYMASK reaches 0.76–0.77 across attacks, with the largest gap under Fang-Trim (0.76 vs. 0.68 for FLTrust, 0.19 for Trim, 0.52 for Krum). On CIFAR-100 (ResNet20), SKYMASK maintains 0.44 under every attack, outperforming the best baseline (FLTrust) by up to 14 percentage points (e.g., 0.44 vs. 0.30 under Min-Max, 0.44 vs. 0.34 under Fang-Trim). Under high malicious fractions (up to 80%), SKYMASK is the only method that retains defensive capability, while FLTrust degrades and other baselines collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKYMASK matches unattacked FedAvg accuracy on all three datasets (≤1% loss), whereas Krum suffers 6–27% accuracy degradation even without attacks. Against FLTrust (the strongest baseline), SKYMASK gains 8 pp on CIFAR-10 under Fang-Trim (0.76 vs. 0.68) and 14 pp on CIFAR-100 under Min-Max (0.44 vs. 0.30); FLTrust also degrades under high malicious fractions (>50%) while SKYMASK remains stable. Trim, Krum, DeepSight, and FLAME are effectively defeated by fine-grained attacks on CIFAR-10/100 (e.g., Krum drops to 0.19 under Fang-Krum on CIFAR-10, 0.03 under Fang-Krum on CIFAR-100), while SKYMASK maintains 0.77 and 0.44 respectively. In the malicious-client-detection comparison (Table 2), SKYMASK achieves FPR < 5% and FNR < 3% across attacks, while Tolpegin defense shows 36–39% FPR and FLDetector reaches 100% FPR/FNR under Min-Max and Min-Sum attacks, rendering it completely ineffective. For targeted attacks, SKYMASK reduces attack success rate to 0.01–0.11, comparable to or better than FLAME and DeepSight, while FedAvg and FLTrust leave success rates at 0.51–1.00.

## Relation to Prior Work

SKYMASK builds directly on FLTrust's introduction of a small clean root dataset on the server, repurposing it not for trust-weighted aggregation but as the training signal for parameter-level learnable masks. It departs from the two dominant defense paradigms—model-level statistical outlier detection (Krum, DeepSight, FLAME) and greedy coordinate-wise filtering (Trimmed-Mean)—by instead learning a binary mask per client that is optimized to correct poisoned parameters, then clustering the converged masks via a Gaussian mixture model to classify clients. The use of sigmoid-approximated binary masks for differentiable training draws on the lottery-ticket/supermask literature, but their application to Byzantine detection in FL is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SKYMASK is the first defense to operate at the parameter level for Byzantine detection in FL, closing the gap that fine-grained attacks (Fang, AGR-agnostic) exploit against model-level defenses, and it does so without requiring knowledge of the specific attack. Its ability to maintain near-unattacked accuracy even with 80% malicious clients and its attack-agnostic design make it a practical drop-in module for existing FL pipelines. Open questions include the practical availability and representativeness of the root dataset in real deployments, the computational overhead of mask training for very large models, and whether adaptive attackers aware of the mask mechanism could craft updates that produce benign-looking masks.
