## Problem

Supervised deep learning for medical image reconstruction requires large paired datasets of ground-truth images, which are expensive and often unavailable in clinical settings. Existing self-supervised and unsupervised methods either train from scratch per sample (computationally prohibitive) or fail to generalize when test samples deviate from the training distribution. This paper asks how to efficiently adapt a pre-trained unrolling network to each individual test sample at inference time, without any ground-truth supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240975/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes incomplete linear measurements (undersampled k-space for CS-MRI or sparse-view Radon projections for SV-CT) together with a pre-trained PGDA-based unrolling network whose main weights are frozen. During test-time adaptation, only the lightweight AdaptBlock parameters (two 3×3 convolutions plus one step size per stage, totaling ~0.03% of model size) are updated over T iterations using a self-supervised loss. The final reconstructed image is produced as an ensemble average of T forward passes, each with independently injected Gaussian noise on the measurement.

## Method

AdaptNet embeds a lightweight AdaptBlock—one gradient-descent step followed by two 3×3 linear convolution layers without ReLU—at the end of each denoising sub-network in a 12-stage PGDA unrolling architecture. The key insight is that a linear CNN approximates a Wiener filter tuned to the training data's second-order statistics, and a small additional convolution can shift this filter toward the oracle Wiener filter of the test sample, effectively aligning the denoiser with the new sample's spectral characteristics. During test time, AdaptBlock parameters are optimized by minimizing a dual-domain self-supervised loss: a range-space R2R loss (injecting Gaussian noise into measurements) plus a null-space consistency loss (injecting Poisson noise into the pre-trained model's estimate and enforcing fixed-point consistency). This design requires no ground truth, adds less than 1% to model size, and converges in as few as 20 adaptation iterations to outperform full fine-tuning, bias-tuning, and gain-tuning baselines.

## Results

On the ADNI CS-MRI benchmark (noisy), AdaptNet achieves 34.82 dB PSNR / 0.91 SSIM at Gaussian 1/3 undersampling, outperforming the best supervised method (ADMMNet, 31.04 dB) by 3.78 dB and the best prior model-adaptation method (MetaCS, 31.80 dB) by 3.02 dB. On fastMRI at 4× Poisson acceleration, AdaptNet reaches 36.51 dB / 0.90 SSIM, surpassing Restormer (34.17 dB) and MetaCS (34.88 dB). For SV-CT at the lowest X-ray intensity (I₀=5×10³), AdaptNet attains 31.14 dB / 0.81 SSIM, exceeding DDSSL (28.42 dB) by 2.72 dB and Restormer (30.17 dB) by 0.97 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaptNet outperforms all ten baselines across every setting. Among unsupervised methods, REI [2] (best at ~30.22 dB on Radial 1/3) and ASGLD [38] (~29.85 dB) trail AdaptNet by 3.9–4.3 dB; BNN [22] and GAN [16] are further behind at ~29.6 and ~26.7 dB respectively. Supervised methods ADMMNet [45] (~31.0 dB) and Restormer [46] (~30.5 dB) are surpassed by 3–4 dB, confirming that limited training data caps their generalization. Score-MRI [5] (30.49 dB on fastMRI 4× Poisson) is outperformed by 6 dB. Among model-adaptation baselines, DDSSL [29] (31.62 dB on Gaussian 1/3) and MetaCS [25] (31.80 dB) are the closest competitors but still lag AdaptNet by 3.0–3.2 dB. For SV-CT, FISTANet [44] (35.32 dB at I₀=10⁵) is the strongest supervised baseline, yet AdaptNet leads by 1.29 dB, with the gap widening to 2.72 dB over DDSSL at the lowest intensity (I₀=5×10³).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PGDA-based unrolling network architecture (Diamond et al., 2017) and the R2R self-supervised denoising loss (Pang et al., 2021), extending the dual-domain self-supervised framework of DDSSL (Quan et al., 2022) with a key architectural departure: rather than relying solely on loss-function design for test-time adaptation, it introduces lightweight linear AdaptBlocks whose role is interpreted through the Wiener filter as an optimal linear estimator. This positions AdaptNet between self-supervised internal learning (which trains from scratch per sample) and prior TTA methods (MetaCS, DDSSL) that fine-tune existing parameters, instead adding a minimal set of new parameters that capture the distributional shift between training and test data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in medical imaging where ground-truth acquisition is expensive and training data is limited, offering a parameter-efficient (<0.03% of model size) adaptation mechanism that consistently outperforms both supervised and unsupervised baselines across CS-MRI and SV-CT. The Wiener filter interpretation provides a principled justification for why a small linear convolution suffices for distribution alignment, though the theory is restricted to linear CNNs and zero-mean assumptions. Open questions include extending the analysis to non-linear denoisers, broader architectures beyond unrolling networks, and formalizing the conditions under which the small-filter approximation to the oracle Wiener filter holds.
