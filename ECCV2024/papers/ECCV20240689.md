## Problem

Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) models like CLIP exhibit strong robustness to 2D distribution shifts but suffer significant performance degradation under 3D viewpoint changes, a critical limitation for real-world deployment. This gap is compounded by two obstacles: the scarcity of large-scale multi-view image-text training data with textual descriptions, and the inadequacy of existing adversarial-training paradigms, which introduce accuracy-robustness trade-offs and require expensive 3D reconstruction and neural rendering.

## Input / Output

During training, OVT consumes multi-view image-text pairs (224×224 RGB images with VLLM-generated captions) where multiple views of the same object are batched together to compute cross-viewpoint alignment. The text encoder remains frozen throughout fine-tuning; only the visual encoder's LoRA weights and VIFormer parameters are updated. At inference, the fine-tuned model accepts a single image (for zero-shot classification or captioning) or an image paired with a text question (for VQA), producing class labels, free-form text descriptions, or short text answers respectively.

## Method

Omniview-Tuning (OVT) introduces a Cross-Viewpoint Alignment loss (L_VC) that minimizes cosine distance between visual embeddings of the same object from different viewpoints, formulated in a minimax-like optimization: the maximization step identifies top-K outlier viewpoints with the greatest representational deviation from a nearest-neighbor-weighted anchor centroid, and the minimization step pulls only those outliers toward the anchor, preventing concept drift and overfitting. For parameter efficiency, OVT freezes the pre-trained visual encoder and text encoder, updating only LoRA low-rank matrices (rank 8) on self-attention layers and a VIFormer module that applies learnable self-attention to the image embedding with a residual connection (α=0.1) to preserve original representations. The training objective combines the standard image-text contrastive loss (L_ITC) with the viewpoint consistency loss (L_VC) weighted by λ=1.0. The approach is trained on the newly constructed MVCap dataset (4.6M multi-view image-text pairs across 94.6K objects and 1,600+ categories), where captions are generated by InstructBLIP using category-guided prompts to ensure viewpoint-consistent textual descriptions.

## Results

Across three CLIP architectures (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14), OVT-OpenCLIP improves average Top-1 accuracy on viewpoint-OOD benchmarks by 9.6%, 10.2%, and 8.9% over the corresponding OpenCLIP baselines, while sacrificing only 2.6%, 1.4%, and 0.2% on 2D-OOD benchmarks respectively. On the strongest configuration (OVT-MetaCLIP ViT-B/16), viewpoint-OOD average Top-1 reaches 57.2%, surpassing MetaCLIP (50.3%), EVA-CLIP (48.2%), and OpenCLIP (46.4%). OVT-BLIP achieves an 8.6% average improvement on viewpoint-OOD over the original BLIP. In image captioning with LLaVA-13B, OVT-CLIP gains +11.1 points over the original CLIP on ImageNet-V+ (β@Adp.: 40.9 vs. 32.5) while matching clean-distribution performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVT-OpenCLIP consistently outperforms its corresponding OpenCLIP baseline on all viewpoint-OOD benchmarks: +9.6% (B/32), +10.2% (B/16), and +8.9% (L/14) average Top-1, with the largest single-benchmark gain being +22.4 points on ImageNet-V+ for ViT-B/32. Against the stronger MetaCLIP and EVA-CLIP baselines, OVT-MetaCLIP (B/16) achieves the best viewpoint-OOD average (57.2%) by a margin of 6.9 and 9.0 points over MetaCLIP and EVA-CLIP respectively, while OVT-MetaCLIP (L/14) reaches 64.5% vs. 56.7% for MetaCLIP. On clean and 2D-OOD benchmarks, OVT models trade only 0.2–2.6% accuracy, confirming that the viewpoint-invariance gain does not come at the cost of original performance. In the image captioning task (Table 4), OVT-CLIP surpasses both adversarially-trained baselines TeCoA and FARE on all viewpoint-OOD splits (e.g., +18.4 points over TeCoA on ImageNet-V+ β@Adp. for LLaVA-7B), while TeCoA and FARE suffer 10–20 point drops on viewpoint-OOD relative to clean performance. OVT-BLIP improves viewpoint-OOD average by 8.6% over the original BLIP while also gaining 4.7 points on clean data, indicating that the framework generalizes beyond the CLIP architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the viewpoint invariance literature (VIAT, Viewfool, adversarial viewpoint training) from task-specific classifiers to large-scale VLP models, where the problem had not been systematically addressed. It departs from prior adversarial-training paradigms that require 3D reconstruction and NeRF rendering by instead using a minimax-like cross-viewpoint alignment objective on pre-rendered multi-view data, combined with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA + a novel VIFormer module) inspired by CLIP-Adapter. The MVCap dataset itself builds on existing 3D asset collections (Objaverse, IM3D, MVImgNet) but is the first to provide large-scale multi-view image-text pairs with category-consistent captions generated via a VLLM, filling a gap that prior multi-view datasets (which lack textual descriptions) could not serve for VLP training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLP models are increasingly deployed in embodied and physical-world settings (autonomous driving, robotics), the demonstrated ~9–10% viewpoint-OOD accuracy gain with minimal 2D-OOD sacrifice (≤2.6%) makes this a practically relevant improvement for foundation-model practitioners. The MVCap dataset (4.6M pairs, 100K+ objects, 1600 categories) is a reusable community resource that was previously unavailable. Open questions include whether the minimax outlier-focusing strategy is optimal versus other sampling schemes, whether the approach transfers to video-based or 3D-native VLP models, and whether the gains persist when the VLP encoder is further fine-tuned for downstream tasks beyond zero-shot classification.
