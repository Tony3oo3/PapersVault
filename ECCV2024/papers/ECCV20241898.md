## Problem

Early-stage urban design relies on rapid iteration of building massing (coarse 3D volumes), yet existing 3D modeling software is too cumbersome for this ideation phase, and no sketch-based tool exists for city-scale massing. The core technical challenge is that sparse, textureless sketches provide extremely limited geometric signal, urban scenes suffer from extreme self-occlusions in perspective views, and the system must respond in under 3 seconds to remain interactive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241898/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two user-drawn 2D sketches: a top-down plan view of building footprints and a perspective view of the site, both rendered on 2D canvases within a web-based interface. The output is a 3D heightfield mesh (2.5D representation) of the city block, where building heights are encoded as depth values relative to a ground plane, rather than a full 3D mesh. The top-down sketch controls building layout and footprints, while the perspective sketch controls building heights and roof shapes; the two views are complementary and neither alone is sufficient.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) a UNet++ with ResNet-50 encoder predicts a building occupancy mask from the top-down sketch, followed by connected-component labeling for instance segmentation; (2) a novel perspective depth predictor injects a 3D occupancy volume—constructed by slicing the camera frustum with depth planes and marking voxels above occupied vs. free regions—into a UNet++ encoder alongside the perspective sketch features, replacing the photometric cost-volume matching that fails on textureless sketches; (3) a latent diffusion model (adapted from DiffusionDepth) conditioned on both the top-down sketch and a partial top-down depth map (backprojected from the perspective depth) completes the heightfield, using pre-trained ResNet-50 and Stable Diffusion depth encoders for the conditioning. The key architectural insight is that the occupancy volume resolves spatial ambiguity in single-view depth estimation without requiring feature matching between the two views, and the diffusion model handles the remaining unobserved regions by generating plausible building geometry consistent with both sketch constraints.

## Results

For perspective depth estimation, the full model (OV_L with occupancy volume) achieves an Abs Diff of 3.49 and a5 of 89.20%, substantially outperforming the monocular baseline (Mono_L: Abs Diff 5.57, a5 68.82%). For the final top-down depth completion, the full diffusion model (with pre-trained encoders and normal loss) achieves Abs Diff 0.1056, Abs Rel 0.0214, and RMSE 0.1200, compared to the HeightFields baseline at 0.1099, 0.0225, and 0.1341 respectively. In 3D mesh evaluation, the method reaches an F-Score of 78.3% and Chamfer distance of 3.94, versus 66.0% and 6.17 for HeightFields. The full pipeline runs end-to-end in under 2.7 seconds on multi-building scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines. Against the monocular depth predictor (Mono, from Sayed et al. [66]) on perspective depth estimation, the occupancy-volume model (OV_L) wins decisively: Abs Diff drops from 5.57 to 3.49 (−37%), RMSE from 8.58 to 6.54 (−24%), and a5 rises from 68.82% to 89.20% (+20.4 pp), confirming that injecting top-down occupancy cues is critical for resolving spatial ambiguity in sparse sketch inputs. Against the deterministic HeightFields baseline [75] on the final top-down depth completion, the diffusion model achieves lower Abs Diff (0.1056 vs 0.1099) and RMSE (0.1200 vs 0.1341, a 10.5% improvement). In 3D mesh metrics (Table 3), the advantage is more pronounced: F-Score improves from 66.0% to 78.3% (+12.3 pp), Recall from 63.9% to 82.0% (+18.1 pp), and Chamfer distance drops from 6.17 to 3.94 (−36%). The HeightFields baseline, despite being trained with an additional normal loss, fails to produce correct building heights and sharp rooftop details, while the diffusion model generates plausible, flat-topped building masses aligned with both input sketches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion framework of Duan et al. (DiffusionDepth) for depth estimation, adapting it to accept multiple conditioning signals (top-down sketch and partial depth from a perspective view). It departs from prior sketch-to-3D work, which typically targets single objects using voxel, point cloud, or implicit representations, by instead regressing a 2.5D heightfield for multi-building urban scenes. The key architectural departure is the occupancy volume: rather than attempting feature matching between the top-down and perspective views (as in multi-view stereo), the paper encodes the top-down building mask as a binary 3D occupancy signal aligned with the perspective frustum, injecting spatial constraints into a UNet++ depth predictor. This sidesteps the infeasibility of photometric matching on textureless sketches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GroundUp addresses a genuine workflow gap in early-stage urban design, where architects iterate in 2D sketches but lack a fast path to 3D massing previews. The sub-2.7-second end-to-end inference makes the tool interactive, which is critical for the iterative design loop the paper targets. However, evaluation is limited to synthetic sketches with a small user study (5 architects, 5 novices), and the heightfield representation inherently restricts geometry to terrain-like surfaces, precluding overhangs or complex roof forms. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real-world sketches with inconsistent line quality, whether multi-view extension (mentioned but not fully evaluated) closes the remaining occlusion gaps, and whether the method can be extended beyond buildings to trees and terrain as the authors suggest.
