## Problem

Structure-based visual localization methods store thousands of high-dimensional local descriptors per database image, resulting in maps that are impractically large for large-scale scenes (e.g., 6.3 GB for Aachen Day-Night). Existing map compression methods rely on heuristic nearest-neighbor matching or pre-trained matchers without retraining, which limits achievable compression to roughly 1/8 of original descriptor size. The paper asks whether extreme compression (1/256) is possible if the descriptor reduction module and the matching network are trained jointly.

## Input / Output

At localization time, the system takes a single RGB query image and a compressed 3D map containing 8-bit quantized EigenPlace global features (256-dim) plus 4-bit quantized low-dimensional descriptor embeddings (4/8/16-dim) with associated 2D/3D keypoint positions. The compressed database descriptors are decoded on-the-fly back to full dimension before matching, while query-image descriptors remain uncompressed. The output is a 6-DOF camera pose (3D translation + 3D rotation) estimated via PnP and RANSAC on the resulting 2D-3D correspondences.

## Method

The core contribution is an asymmetric encoder-decoder module (stacked MLPs with ReLU and batch normalization) that compresses database descriptors to a bottleneck dimension (4/8/16) and reconstructs them to the original dimension (256), embedded within a SuperGlue-style attention-based graph neural network matcher. The asymmetry is deliberate: only database descriptors pass through the encoder-decoder, while query descriptors remain full-resolution, preventing descriptor-space co-shift under extreme compression. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on MegaDepth with a matching NLL loss plus an optional L2 descriptor reconstruction loss, and post-training 4-bit quantization is applied to the stored embeddings. The key insight is that attention-based context aggregation compensates for the information lost in heavily compressed descriptors, but only when the matcher weights are retrained to expect the reconstructed (rather than original) descriptor distribution—using pre-trained SuperGlue weights on compressed descriptors causes a significant accuracy drop.

## Results

On Aachen Day-Night, the proposed pipeline (SuperPoint + SuperGlue with D_LR=4, 4-bit quantization) achieves 84.5%/80.6% localization rate under (0.25m, 2°) for day/night queries with a map size of only 4.1 MB, compared to 6336 MB for the uncompressed SP+SG baseline. On Cambridge Landmark, the method achieves median translation errors of 0.05–0.17 m and rotation errors of 0.23–0.31° across four scenes with maps of 0.12–0.94 MB. On InLoc (indoor), the method localizes 48.0%/45.8% of queries under (0.25m, 2°), representing only a 1.5%/5.3% drop from the uncompressed baseline (49.5%/51.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The headline comparison is against SceneSqueezer [72], the previous SOTA map compression method: the proposed method achieves 80.6% vs. 50.0% night localization under (0.25m, 2°) while using only 13.2% of the map size (4.1 MB vs. 31 MB). Against the uncompressed SP+SG baseline [52], the method incurs only a 3.6%/5.1% drop in day/night accuracy while reducing map size by 99.9% (4.1 MB vs. 6336 MB). On Cambridge Landmark, the method achieves half the median translation error of SceneSqueezer on three of four scenes (e.g., 0.17 m vs. 0.37 m on Old Hospital) with smaller maps. Against end-to-end methods, the proposed pipeline substantially outperforms NeuMap [64] (80.6% vs. 37.8% night) and ESAC [12] (80.6% vs. 6.1% night) under (0.25m, 2°). On InLoc, the method (48.0%/45.8%) dramatically outperforms Dong et al. [22] at dimension 16 (24.2%/15.3%) and at dimension 64 (33.3%/26.0%), demonstrating that joint training with the attention-based matcher is critical for extreme compression. A carefully tuned DISK+MNN+PQ baseline (21.8 MB, 80.6% night) is also beaten on memory (4.1 MB) at comparable accuracy, highlighting the efficiency of the 4-bit quantized 4-dim embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical structure-based localization pipeline (hloc) by integrating a lightweight MLP encoder-decoder for descriptor dimension reduction directly into the SuperGlue-style attention-based matching network, jointly trained end-to-end. It departs from prior map compression work (SceneSqueezer, hybrid methods) that either relies on heuristic nearest-neighbor matching or feeds compressed descriptors into frozen pre-trained matchers, by co-optimizing the compression bottleneck and the matching model. The asymmetric design—compressing only database descriptors while keeping query descriptors at full dimension—differs from the symmetric dimensionality reduction of Dong et al. and is motivated by the practical constraint that query features are computed on-the-fly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/256 compression ratio (256-dim → 4-dim, 16-bit per descriptor) with a 4.1 MB map achieving >80% accuracy on Aachen Day-Night is a significant practical advance for deployment-constrained visual positioning systems, where map storage and transmission are bottlenecks. The finding that descriptor reconstruction loss is optional—matching loss alone implicitly recovers sufficient structure—simplifies the training recipe and suggests the attention-based matcher is the true workhorse, not the descriptor fidelity. Open questions include whether this extreme compression generalizes to even larger city-scale maps, whether it interacts well with other recent matching architectures (LightGlue, LoFTR), and how the method performs when the 3D map itself is imperfect (non-SfM data beyond the InLoc setting).
