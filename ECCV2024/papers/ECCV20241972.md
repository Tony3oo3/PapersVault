## Problem

Out-of-distribution detection for semantic segmentation has been almost exclusively studied on road scenes with only 19 Cityscapes classes, limiting the semantic diversity of the in-distribution domain. This paper challenges that constraint by extending the task to general natural images with 150 ADE20k classes, and introduces the first diffusion-model-based approach for dense OoD detection that is robust to this increased diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241972/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a pixel-wise OoD score map at the resolution of the transformer feature patches (e.g., 16×16 patches for ViT), which is coarser than full pixel resolution. The score map is computed by operating on individual feature vectors extracted from a pre-trained segmentation encoder, so the output granularity is tied to the patch size of the backbone rather than the input image resolution. A binary OoD mask is obtained by thresholding the score map, and the final score is a compound of the diffusion-based directional error and the segmentation model's LogSumExp uncertainty, both standardized on training-set statistics.

## Method

DOoD trains a small MLP-based diffusion model (DDPM formulation) on individual feature vectors extracted from a pre-trained segmentation encoder (Segmenter-B or SETR-L), deliberately discarding spatial context so the model cannot extrapolate anomalies from neighboring features. At inference, each feature is perturbed with Gaussian noise via the forward diffusion process, the MLP denoiser estimates the score of the smoothed inlier distribution, and the OoD score is computed as the directional error (negative cosine similarity) between the estimated score and the perturbation direction. Scores are aggregated across multiple diffusion timesteps with weights proportional to the perturbation magnitude, enabling a runtime–accuracy trade-off. The key design choices—MLP over U-Net to prevent spatial extrapolation, and directional error over MSE to improve robustness across timesteps—are validated ablation-wise and yield consistent gains over both reconstruction-based and prior score-based diffusion OoD methods.

## Results

On the RoadAnomaly benchmark, DOoD (Segmenter-B) achieves 89.1 AP / 8.8 FPR, outperforming RbA (85.6/9.8) and NFlowJS (80.8/12.0) without outlier exposure or driving-specific priors. Across all road scene benchmarks, DOoD attains the best average rank (2.5 for Segmenter-B, 2.2 for SETR-L), indicating the most consistent performance. On the proposed ADE-OoD benchmark (150 in-distribution classes, diverse indoor/outdoor scenes), DOoD achieves 63.03 AP and 36.50 FPR, ranking second in AP (behind RbA at 66.82) but first in FPR (vs. RbA's 82.42 and cDNP's 39.20). Inference time is 0.125 s per image with 5 timesteps (comparable to RbA's 0.124 s) and 0.322 s with 25 timesteps, using only 1.87 GB GPU memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RbA [31], DOoD wins on RoadAnomaly (89.1 vs. 85.6 AP), SMIYC Anomaly (90.8 vs. 88.9 AP), and Fishyscapes Static (75.0 vs. 63.1 AP), but loses on Fishyscapes L&F (43.0 vs. 66.2 AP with Segmenter-B; 64.4 vs. 66.2 with SETR-L). Against NFlowJS [16], DOoD wins on RoadAnomaly (89.1 vs. 80.8 AP) and Fishyscapes Static (75.0 vs. 68.8 AP) but trails on SMIYC Anomaly (90.8 vs. 93.4 AP). Against M2A [35] (which uses both outlier exposure and driving-specific priors), DOoD wins on RoadAnomaly (89.1 vs. 79.7 AP) and Fishyscapes L&F (64.4 vs. 46.0 AP with SETR-L) but loses on Fishyscapes Static (79.3 vs. 90.5 AP). On the ADE-OoD benchmark, DOoD substantially outperforms GMMSeg (63.03 vs. 47.6 AP) and Maskomaly (63.03 vs. 28.23 AP), and edges out cDNP on FPR (36.50 vs. 39.20) while being competitive on AP (63.03 vs. 62.35); however, RbA achieves higher AP (66.82) at the cost of a much worse FPR (82.42). Against the driving-specific JSRNet [44], DOoD trails on RoadAnomaly (89.1 vs. 94.4 AP) but JSRNet collapses on SMIYC Anomaly (33.6 AP) and SMIYC Obstacle (28.1 AP), confirming its domain limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOoD sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) dense OoD detection in segmentation, where it replaces the non-parametric kNN retrieval of cDNP [11] with a parametric diffusion-based density estimator, and (2) score-based anomaly detection, where it follows Shin et al. [37] in using the diffusion model's score estimate rather than expensive reconstruction, but substitutes directional (cosine) error for the L2 distance. A key architectural departure from standard diffusion practice is the use of an MLP denoiser operating on individual feature vectors instead of a 2D U-Net, motivated by the observation that spatial context allows the model to 'cheat' by extrapolating from neighboring inlier features. The benchmark contribution extends the OoD segmentation task from the 19-class Cityscapes road-scene setting to the 150-class ADE20k general-scene setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ADE-OoD benchmark is a meaningful step toward evaluating OoD detection in realistic, semantically diverse settings beyond autonomous driving, and the results (best AP ≈ 67) demonstrate that current methods are far from solving the problem at this scale. The finding that spatial context is actively harmful for OoD scoring in diffusion models is a useful conceptual insight that could inform future generative-model-based detection designs. However, the paper itself acknowledges 'much room for future improvements' on ADE-OoD, and the method's competitive (but not dominant) performance on road-scene benchmarks suggests the approach is a solid but not definitive solution. Open questions include whether the MLP design generalizes to even higher-dimensional or multi-scale feature spaces, and whether the directional-error scoring advantage persists under different noise schedules or architectures.
