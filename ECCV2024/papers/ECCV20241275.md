## Problem

Current diffusion video editing systems succeed at rigid edits (style transfer, object overlay) but fail at non-rigid edits (motion change, pose modification) because the initial latent noise built via DDIM inversion still retains the input video's visual structure, imposing structural rigidity that prevents the model from altering geometry or motion. This paper identifies and addresses this structural persistence in the latent noise as the root cause of the editing limitation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241275/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (32–128 frames, encoded to 64×64 latent spatial resolution via VQ-VAE) and a target text prompt (encoded via CLIP ViT-L/14) as input, and produces an edited video as output. DNI is a plug-and-play, model-agnostic preprocessing step that modifies only the initial latent noise before it enters any existing diffusion video editing system (tuning-based or tuning-free), requiring no retraining of the underlying model. Hyperparameters α and β control the intensity of rigid vs. non-rigid dilution, with recommended ranges of 0.3 < α < 0.7 for rigid edits and β > 0.6 for non-rigid edits.

## Method

DNI introduces a two-stage 'noise dilution' pipeline applied to the DDIM-inverted initial latent noise z. First, Noise Disentanglement uses an Adaptive Spectral Filter (ASF) — a 3D-DFT-based frequency pass filter constructed from the min-max normalized spectrum of the input video's latent z₀ — to separate z into a visual branch z_v (retaining input video structure) and a Gaussian noise branch z_g. Second, Noise Dilution constructs an editing guidance mask m_edit from cross-attention maps in the UNet (down-block 16×16 maps for noun/adjective reference words, mid-block 8×8 maps for predicate reference words), then blends fresh Gaussian noise ε into z_v only within the masked region. The final dilutional latent noise z* = z_g + m_edit × ε + (1 − m_edit) × z_v is fed into the standard denoising loop, selectively weakening the input video's structural constraint in the target editing area while preserving fidelity elsewhere.

## Results

On DAVIS and TVGVE, DNI consistently improves all four evaluation criteria across all four baselines. For non-rigid editing, textual alignment (CLIP score) improves by 4.1–5.4 points (e.g., VP2P: 22.5→27.9, TAV: 22.6→27.6), while fidelity (SSIM) gains 0.06–0.08 (e.g., TF: 0.653→0.731). Human preference scores jump dramatically, from 0.14–0.41 for baselines to 0.59–0.86 with DNI (e.g., TAV: 0.14→0.86). For rigid editing, gains are more modest but still consistent, with CLIP scores rising 1.2–2.2 points and FVD decreasing by 90–240 across all systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNI is applied as a plug-and-play module on top of four state-of-the-art video editing systems: Tune-A-Video (TAV), FateZero (FZ), Video-P2P (VP2P), and TokenFlow (TF). DNI wins on every metric for every baseline, with the largest gains in non-rigid editing where baselines struggle most. The most striking improvement is in human preference: TAV+DNI jumps from 0.14 to 0.86, FZ+DNI from 0.34 to 0.66, VP2P+DNI from 0.38 to 0.62, and TF+DNI from 0.41 to 0.59. For textual alignment on non-rigid edits, VP2P+DNI achieves the highest CLIP score (27.9) and TF+DNI the highest on rigid edits (29.6). Fidelity improvements are notable for tuning-based models (TAV, VP2P) where overfitting to the input video is a known issue, while tuning-free models (FZ, TF) see smaller but consistent gains. No baseline outperforms its DNI-augmented counterpart on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the diffusion video editing pipeline established by Tune-A-Video, Video-P2P, FateZero, and TokenFlow, which all rely on DDIM inversion to construct an initial latent noise from the input video before denoising toward a target prompt. The key conceptual departure is the observation that this inverted latent noise is not truly Gaussian but retains the input video's spatial-temporal structure, which the authors isolate via a 3D-DFT-based adaptive spectral filter. The editing guidance mechanism draws on cross-attention maps from the UNet (analogous to Prompt-to-Prompt for images) but extends it to video by combining rigid (down-block) and non-rigid (mid-block) attention at different resolutions. The overall contribution is a plug-and-play preprocessing step on the latent noise rather than a modification to the diffusion model itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-rigid editing (e.g., changing a walking motion to jumping) was a clear and acknowledged gap in diffusion video editing, and DNI provides a simple, model-agnostic mechanism to address it without retraining. The plug-and-play design means it can be retrofitted onto any existing diffusion editing system, which has practical value for the community. Limitations include the need to tune α and β hyperparameters per editing type, the reliance on cross-attention maps that may be imprecise for complex multi-object scenes, and evaluation limited to 32–128 frame clips. Open questions include whether the frequency-based disentanglement generalizes to higher-resolution or longer-duration videos, and whether the dilution concept transfers to other modalities (audio, 3D) where analogous 'structure-in-noise' effects may exist.
