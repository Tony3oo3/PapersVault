## Problem

When fine-tuning pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) sequentially on downstream tasks, two problems arise simultaneously: catastrophic forgetting of previously learned task knowledge and degradation of the model's zero-shot classification capability. Existing approaches like ZSCL address only zero-shot degradation by distilling from the frozen pre-trained model, but cannot preserve knowledge accumulated from prior tasks since the pre-trained model has never seen them. No prior method simultaneously mitigates both issues without access to previous task data.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes the current task's labeled RGB images (processed by a ViT-B/16 image encoder) plus an unlabeled reference dataset (e.g., ImageNet) from which reference images are sampled for distillation. The text encoder remains frozen throughout, with prompts standardized to 'a photo of a <CLASS>'. At inference, given an RGB image and a set of class labels, the model outputs class probabilities via cosine similarity between the image feature and each text feature, scaled by a learned temperature parameter. Notably, no data from previous tasks is available at any training stage—only the two teacher models and the unlabeled reference set.

## Method

The framework treats the most recent fine-tuned VLM (g_{k-1}) and the original pre-trained VLM (g_0) as dual teachers and introduces a selective distillation mechanism. For each reference image, it computes a dual-teacher discrepancy (Euclidean distance between the two teachers' feature representations) and maps it through a sigmoid-based selection score η(x) ∈ [0,1] using hyperparameters δ and γ for normalization. The distillation loss is a soft-weighted combination: η(x)·d(g_{k-1}(x), g_k(x)) + (1−η(x))·d(g_0(x), g_k(x)), so images with large discrepancy (aligned with prior task distributions) pull the student toward g_{k-1} to prevent forgetting, while images with small discrepancy (out-of-distribution) pull toward g_0 to preserve zero-shot capability. The total training objective is L = L_CE + L_KD^dual, where L_CE is standard cross-entropy on the current task. Only the image encoder is updated; the text encoder stays frozen across all sequential fine-tuning stages.

## Results

On the Multi-Domain Task-Incremental Learning (MTIL) benchmark across 8 training sequences, the proposed Selective Dual-Teacher Knowledge Transfer achieves a mean catastrophic forgetting of 1.20%, zero-shot degradation of 1.96%, and average accuracy of 84.92%. On the more challenging Multi-Domain Class-Incremental Learning (MCIL) setting (where task identity is unknown at inference), the method achieves 1.35% forgetting, 1.65% zero-shot degradation, and 83.76% average accuracy. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (MoE-Adapters) by 1.51% in forgetting and 0.21% in zero-shot degradation on MTIL, while surpassing ZSCL by 2.34% in forgetting and 1.04% in zero-shot degradation on MCIL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both MTIL and MCIL settings. Against the closest competitor MoE-Adapters [46] (MTIL only), the method reduces catastrophic forgetting from 2.71% to 1.20% (a 56% relative reduction) and improves average accuracy from 82.29% to 84.92%, while achieving comparable zero-shot degradation (1.96% vs. 2.17%). Against ZSCL [48], the most directly related prior work, the method halves catastrophic forgetting (1.20% vs. 3.64% on MTIL; 1.35% vs. 3.58% on MCIL) and reduces zero-shot degradation by roughly 35% (1.96% vs. 3.00%; 1.65% vs. 3.07%), while gaining ~2% average accuracy. The rehearsal-based baselines iCaRL [33] and LwF [22] are substantially worse on all three metrics, with forgetting 6–10× higher and zero-shot degradation 5–10× higher, confirming that data-free dual-teacher distillation is far more effective than memory-buffer or single-teacher approaches for VLM continual learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends ZSCL [48], which introduced the use of an unlabeled reference dataset to distill knowledge from the frozen pre-trained VLM and preserve zero-shot capability during sequential fine-tuning. The key departure is that ZSCL relies on a single teacher (the pre-trained model), which cannot retain task-specific knowledge acquired in prior fine-tuning rounds; here, the most recent fine-tuned model is added as a second teacher, and a dual-teacher discrepancy score selects which teacher to distill from per reference image. The paper also positions itself against rehearsal-based CL (iCaRL, LwF) and data-free CL methods, arguing those are designed for closed-set classification and do not address the open-vocabulary, zero-shot-preserving nature of VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective dual-teacher mechanism offers a practical, data-free solution to a gap that ZSCL explicitly left open: preserving previously fine-tuned knowledge while simultaneously retaining zero-shot generalization. The authors' own limitation note highlights an important open question—when the reference dataset is far from the previously fine-tuned domain (e.g., medical images), the discrepancy signal weakens and forgetting may resurface, suggesting the approach's effectiveness is bounded by reference-dataset coverage. The 8-sequence evaluation protocol and the addition of a class-incremental (open-set) setting beyond the task-incremental benchmark used by ZSCL also raise the bar for future VLM-CL work.
