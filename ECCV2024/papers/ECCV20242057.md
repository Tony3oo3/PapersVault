## Problem

Zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation of meshes is hindered by the scarcity of annotated 3D data and the ambiguity of untextured geometry, where regions like car doors or hair vs. clothing are geometrically indistinct. Existing zero-shot 3D methods rely solely on shape, and applying powerful 2D segmentation models directly to untextured meshes introduces a significant domain gap because those models were trained on textured images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D mesh (vertex and face arrays), an object class text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a car'), and a grounding text specifying the target region (e.g., 'door'). It renders the mesh from a fixed multi-view camera trajectory (radius 2, polar angles 75° and 115°, with K/2 azimuthal samples per ring) and outputs a binary per-face label indicating membership in the text-specified region, with a global threshold of 0 applied to aggregated confidence scores.

## Method

MeshSegmenter is a training-free pipeline with three stages: (1) text-guided texture synthesis via Stable Diffusion (using TEXTure's multi-view conditioning) generates a textured mesh from the untextured input, bridging the domain gap between 2D model training data and untextured geometry; (2) 2D zero-shot segmentation is performed independently on both textured and untextured rendered views using GroundingDINO for text-guided bounding-box detection followed by SAM for mask extraction, with full-object detections filtered out; (3) Face Confidence Revoting (FCR) aggregates multi-view results by assigning positive confidence to masked faces and negative confidence to visible unmasked faces, summing across all views to produce a global per-face score that corrects per-view errors through neighboring-view compensation. The final segmentation is the average of textured-branch and untextured-branch global scores, thresholded at zero, followed by neighbor-face smoothing.

## Results

On the PartNet subset (20 objects per category), MeshSegmenter achieves 69.0% and 78.3% mIoU across the two evaluation settings, substantially outperforming SATR (43.4% / 50.2%) and 3DHighlighter (5.8% / 2.4%). Per-category mIoU reaches 68.2% (Bag), 57.2% (Knife), and 72.2% (Earphone). A user study with 30 respondents on 20 3D meshes confirms the quantitative advantage, with MeshSegmenter receiving the highest ratings in both single-query and multiple-query tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshSegmenter decisively outperforms all three baselines. Against 3DHighlighter [9], which relies on CLIP-space distance between rendered images and text prompts, MeshSegmenter gains over 63 mIoU points (69.0 vs. 5.8 in Setting 1), reflecting the superiority of explicit 2D detection-and-segmentation over implicit CLIP alignment. Against SATR [2], which aggregates multi-view GLIP segmentations by selecting the highest-confidence prompt per facet, MeshSegmenter improves by 25.6 mIoU (69.0 vs. 43.4) and 28.1 mIoU (78.3 vs. 50.2), demonstrating that texture synthesis and face confidence revoting are critical for handling geometrically ambiguous regions. Against ZSC [1], which uses ChatGPT-generated prompts and 3D shape correspondence, MeshSegmenter shows qualitative advantages in Fig. 6(b), particularly for regions with weak geometric signals. The ablation further confirms that the texture branch alone adds ~7 mIoU over the shape-only baseline, while face confidence revoting contributes an additional ~10 mIoU, with the full pipeline (both branches + revoting) reaching 69.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D zero-shot semantic segmentation line initiated by 3DHighlighter, SATR, and ZSC, which all operate exclusively on untextured meshes and rely on shape-only cues. The key departure is the introduction of text-guided texture synthesis via Stable Diffusion (building on TEXTure) to close the domain gap between 2D segmentation models trained on textured images and untextured 3D meshes. The Face Confidence Revoting (FCR) aggregation strategy departs from SATR's per-facet max-confidence selection by assigning negative local confidence to unmasked faces, enabling cross-view error correction. The overall pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf 2D components (GroundingDINO for detection, SAM for segmentation) rather than training any 3D-specific model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that texture information, even when synthetically generated, substantially improves 3D segmentation in geometrically ambiguous regions (e.g., car doors), addressing a practical limitation of shape-only approaches. However, the evaluation is limited to 20 objects per category on ShapeNetPart, and the method inherits the local-detection weaknesses of 2D models not designed for part-level tasks. Open questions include scalability to complex multi-object scenes, sensitivity to texture generation quality, and whether the multi-view rendering cost (K viewpoints × 2 mesh types) is prohibitive for real-time applications. The approach also extends naturally to point clouds via mesh optimization, suggesting broader applicability.
