## Problem

State-of-the-art image classifiers remain highly sensitive to realistic (non-cyclic) translations of just one pixel, where pixels enter and exit the field of view as in a subtle camera rotation. Two existing remedies—large-scale training with data augmentation and sampling-theory-based architectures—both fail on these realistic shifts, with ~40% of test images in models like DINOv2 and Open-CLIP producing different predictions under a single-pixel translation, and even cyclic-shift-invariant models like AFC failing 11% of the time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image, internally evaluates all 140×140 sub-crops within a 224×224 frame, and outputs the feature representation (or class label) of the single selected crop passed through a frozen pretrained network. No retraining is required; the scoring and crop-selection step is a deterministic preprocessing layer applied at inference time. For cyclic-translation robustness, the same pipeline is modified to consider cyclic crops (wrapping around image boundaries), yielding 100% invariance to any integer cyclic shift.

## Method

RICS (Robust Inference by Crop Selection) converts any classifier into a translation-robust one by selecting a single sub-crop at inference time via a deterministic scoring function. All possible k×k crops are scored (using either a random Gaussian filter dot-product followed by a modulo operation, or a Mexican Hat kernel with non-maximum suppression), and the crop with the highest score among local maxima is fed to the frozen network. The key theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that any pseudo-random deterministic score function guarantees adversarial consistency bounded by ((n−k+1−2Δ)/(n−k+1+2Δ))², meaning the adversary can only succeed if the selected crop lies within Δ pixels of the image boundary. For cyclic translations, Theorem 2 proves that using cyclic crops makes the set of candidate crops identical across all integer shifts, so any deterministic scorer yields 100% robustness with no accuracy loss beyond the base model.

## Results

On ImageNet, RICS-MH applied to DINOv2 achieves 82.43% top-1 accuracy with 95.14% adversarial-consistency (1-NN) to 1-pixel realistic translations, compared to DINOv2's 62.42% without RICS. The theoretical lower bound for adversarial-consistency at 1-pixel shift is 91.02%, and the method exceeds this bound in practice. For cyclic integer translations, RICS-MH (DINOv2) achieves 100% consistency with 83.93% accuracy, outperforming all prior shift-invariant methods (AFC: 82.11%, A-SwinV2-T: 79.91%) while requiring no retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RICS dominates all baselines on adversarial-consistency for realistic 1-pixel translations: RICS-MH (DINOv2) reaches 95.14% vs. DINOv2's 62.42%, ConvNext-L's 65.58%, Open-CLIP's 57.01%, and even AFC's 89.77% (the strongest prior method for this setting). The accuracy cost is modest—approximately 1.5–1.7% for DINOv2 (84.11% → 82.43%)—while the adversarial-consistency gain is over 32 percentage points. For cyclic integer translations (Table 2), RICS-MH (DINOv2) achieves 100% consistency at 83.93% accuracy, surpassing AFC (82.11%), A-SwinV2-T (79.91%), A-MViTv2-T (77.46%), LPS (69.11%), and APS (67.6%), all of which also achieve 100% consistency but at lower accuracy. Unlike AFC and the polyphase-based methods, RICS requires no architectural modification or retraining—it is a drop-in inference-time preprocessing step applicable to any pretrained model. The method also exceeds its own theoretical lower bound (91.02% adversarial-consistency at 1-pixel shift), confirming that the counting argument is conservative in practice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying a critical gap between two existing approaches to translation robustness: the 'robustness by large-scale' paradigm (DINOv2, Open-CLIP trained on billions of images) and the sampling-theory paradigm (AFC, APS, LPS) that guarantees invariance to cyclic translations. The key conceptual departure is distinguishing cyclic shifts from 'realistic' (non-cyclic) translations that simulate small camera rotations, where pixels enter and exit the field of view. The proposed RICS method repurposes the classical object-recognition paradigm (localize, then classify) as a training-free inference-time mechanism, showing that even a pseudo-random deterministic score function suffices for robustness—a departure from prior work that either modifies architectures or relies on data scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~40% of test images in DINOv2 or Open-CLIP change their nearest-neighbor retrieval under a single-pixel realistic translation is practically significant for anyone deploying foundation models where representation consistency matters. The method's training-free nature and applicability to any pretrained model make it immediately deployable, and the 100% cyclic robustness with SOTA accuracy (no retraining) surpasses all prior architecture-level solutions. Key limitations remain: the method only handles integer translations, the theoretical bound degrades with translation size, and finding meaningful (non-random) score functions that improve accuracy without sacrificing the robustness guarantee is left open. Extending to sub-pixel/fractional translations and integrating learned consistency losses are natural next steps.
