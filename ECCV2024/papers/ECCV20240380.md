## Problem

Existing Audio-Visual Question Answering (AVQA) methods assume complete visual and audio inputs, but real-world deployment frequently encounters missing modalities due to device malfunctions or environmental constraints, causing severe performance degradation. Prior missing-modality approaches handle modalities in one-to-one pairs, overlooking the inter-dependencies among audio, visual, and text that AVQA uniquely requires for context-aware visual-audio reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts a video clip, an audio track, and a text question, and produces a question answer. Crucially, it is designed for inference-time missing modality: during training all three modalities are present, but at test time either the audio or visual input may be absent, in which case the RMM generator and AVR diffusion model synthesize and enhance a substitute feature from the two available modalities. The method is plug-and-play, attaching to any existing AVQA backbone (AVST, AVSD, Pano-AVQA, PSTP-Net) that expects individual audio, visual, and text feature inputs.

## Method

The framework has two core components. First, the Relation-aware Missing Modal (RMM) generator uses a slot-based architecture with L learnable vectors per modality; it computes addressing vectors via softmax-normalized dot products between available modality features and their corresponding slots, then fuses two addressing vectors (e.g., visual and text) through element-wise multiplication and softmax to weight the missing modality's slots, yielding a pseudo feature that recalls the absent modality. Second, the Audio-Visual Relation-aware (AVR) diffusion model is a denoising diffusion process trained on concatenated real audio-visual features; at inference it takes the pseudo feature concatenated with the real counterpart feature and runs the reverse diffusion to enhance both representations through cross-modal knowledge. Training combines AVQA cross-entropy on three input configurations (complete, pseudo-audio, pseudo-visual), an L2-based RMMR loss aligning pseudo features to real features, and a standard diffusion noise-prediction AVE loss, all optimized jointly with a single Adam optimizer.

## Results

On MUSIC-AVQA with the AVST backbone, the proposed method achieves 69.71% overall accuracy when audio is missing (vs. 36.60% baseline) and 67.98% when visual is missing (vs. 59.14% baseline). When applied to four different AVQA networks (AVSD, Pano-AVQA, AVST, PSTP-Net), the method consistently improves accuracy by 7–28 percentage points depending on the backbone and missing modality. On the AVQA dataset, AVST+Ours reaches 70.28% (audio missing) and 46.65% (visual missing), outperforming the respective baselines of 57.03% and 32.84%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four competing missing-modality approaches (Lee et al. CVPR'23, ShaSpec CVPR'23, Woo et al. AAAI'23, Yao et al. AAAI'24) on both missing-modality scenarios of MUSIC-AVQA. In the audio-missing case, the method achieves 69.71% overall accuracy, surpassing the best competitor (Woo et al. at 67.37%) by 2.34 points and the baseline AVST (36.60%) by 33.11 points. In the visual-missing case, it reaches 67.98%, beating Yao et al. (67.18%) by 0.80 points and Woo et al. (67.08%) by 0.90 points. The gains are most dramatic for audio-missing scenarios, where the RMM generator's trimodal relation modeling and the AVR diffusion's cross-modal enhancement provide the largest improvements over one-to-one pair-based methods. On the AVQA dataset, the method also consistently improves all four AVQA backbones, with the largest absolute gain (28.31 points) observed for AVST under audio-missing conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the missing-modality literature (ShaSpec, ActionMAE, missing-aware prompting) from one-to-one modality pairing to a trimodal (audio-visual-text) relational framework, arguing that AVQA requires question-conditioned, cross-modal reasoning rather than simple pairwise feature imputation. It repurposes denoising diffusion models—previously applied to single-modality generation or restoration—as a cross-modal feature enhancer that jointly refines a real and a pseudo feature through concatenated audio-visual inputs. The slot-based RMM generator draws on memory/attention mechanisms for associative recall, while the overall plug-and-play design builds on existing AVQA backbones (AVST, AVSD, Pano-AVQA, PSTP-Net) without modifying their internal architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap: real-world AVQA deployments face sensor failures and environmental degradation that silently remove a modality, yet all prior AVQA methods assume complete inputs. The plug-and-play nature (validated across four distinct backbones and two datasets) makes the approach immediately adoptable. A key limitation is that missing-modality robustness is only handled at inference; the authors acknowledge that training-time modality dropout remains open. Broader applicability to other multi-modal tasks (e.g., video understanding, robotics) is suggested but not demonstrated, and the method has not been tested with more than one modality missing simultaneously.
