## Problem

Existing video super-resolution methods focus on reconstruction quality (PSNR/SSIM) and lack generative capability, producing blurry, detail-poor results that are perceptually unsatisfying. Applying single-image diffusion-based super-resolution to individual frames introduces temporal inconsistency, while prior video diffusion approaches do not specifically address VSR or use fine-texture temporal guidance. The paper tackles the challenge of synthesizing realistic, temporally-consistent details in VSR using diffusion models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N low-resolution video frames and produces a corresponding sequence of N high-resolution frames at ×4 upscaling, built on the Stable Diffusion ×4 Upscaler VAE decoder. Training uses randomly cropped 256×256 patches with horizontal flip augmentation, while inference operates at 1280×720 target resolution using 50 DDPM sampling steps (1000 during training). Optical flow is computed on the low-resolution frames via RAFT for spatial alignment of temporal guidance.

## Method

StableVSR turns a pre-trained latent diffusion model for single-image super-resolution into a VSR method by adding a Temporal Conditioning Module (TCM) injected into the denoising UNet decoder via ControlNet. The TCM is guided by a novel Temporal Texture Guidance: at each sampling step, the adjacent frame's latent is projected to its noise-free initial state (t=0) via Eq. 6, decoded to RGB through the VAE, and spatially aligned to the current frame using RAFT optical flow and motion compensation. At inference, a Frame-wise Bidirectional Sampling strategy takes each sampling step on all frames simultaneously before advancing in time, alternately reversing the frame order so information propagates both forward and backward, preventing error accumulation and balancing temporal information flow. Only the TCM is trained (20,000 steps with Adam at lr=1e-5, batch size 32) using a ControlNet-style objective that conditions the noise prediction on the temporally-aligned texture guidance from the previous frame.

## Results

On Vimeo-90K-T, StableVSR achieves the best perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.070, DISTS 0.087) and temporal consistency (tLP 3.89, tOF 1.37) among all compared methods, outperforming the previous best (RVRT) by 20% on LPIPS and 9% on tLP. On REDS4, it similarly leads on all perceptual and temporal metrics (LPIPS 0.097, DISTS 0.045, tLP 5.57, tOF 2.68). However, StableVSR trades off reconstruction quality: PSNR drops to 31.97 dB (Vimeo-90K-T) and 27.97 dB (REDS4) versus 36.30 and 32.74 for RVRT, consistent with the perception-distortion trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableVSR outperforms all eight baselines on every perceptual quality metric (LPIPS, DISTS) and every temporal consistency metric (tLP, tOF) on both datasets. On Vimeo-90K-T, it beats the strongest reconstruction-oriented method RVRT by 20% on LPIPS (0.070 vs 0.088) and 9% on tLP (3.89 vs 4.28), and surpasses BasicVSR++ by 24% on LPIPS (0.070 vs 0.092). On REDS4, the margin over RVRT widens: LPIPS 0.097 vs 0.128 (24% improvement) and tLP 5.57 vs 8.97 (38% improvement). The only other generative method, RealBasicVSR (GAN-based), is clearly outperformed by StableVSR on both perceptual and temporal metrics on REDS4 (LPIPS 0.097 vs 0.134, tOF 2.68 vs 4.74), confirming that diffusion-based synthesis yields more natural and temporally stable details. The trade-off is reconstruction quality: StableVSR's PSNR is 4.3 dB lower than RVRT on Vimeo-90K-T (31.97 vs 36.30) and 4.8 dB lower on REDS4 (27.97 vs 32.74), which the authors attribute to the perception-distortion trade-off inherent to generative models.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableVSR repurposes a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model for single image super-resolution (Stable Diffusion ×4 Upscaler) into a video super-resolution method by adding a Temporal Conditioning Module, following the ControlNet paradigm for injecting conditional signals into a frozen diffusion backbone. It departs from the dominant regression-based VSR lineage (BasicVSR, RVRT, etc.) that optimizes reconstruction metrics, and from video-generation diffusion works (Align Your Latents, VideoFusion) that do not address the specific alignment and fine-detail consistency requirements of VSR. The Temporal Texture Guidance and Frame-wise Bidirectional Sampling are novel mechanisms that bridge the gap between single-frame generative quality and multi-frame temporal coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to bring diffusion-model-based generative synthesis to the VSR setting, directly addressing the long-standing perception-distortion trade-off that limits reconstruction-focused methods. The practical impact is currently constrained by a ~35× parameter overhead and ~100 s/frame inference time, which limits deployment in real-time or resource-constrained scenarios. Open questions include whether faster samplers (flow matching, consistency models) can close the inference gap, whether the approach generalizes to arbitrary upscaling factors beyond ×4, and how to balance perceptual and reconstruction quality in a single model rather than forcing a trade-off.
