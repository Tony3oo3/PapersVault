## Problem

Existing LMM benchmarks predominantly evaluate visual reasoning on common photographic-style images, leaving models' robustness to diverse visual distribution shifts—artistic styles, non-RGB sensors, and domain-specific applications—largely unexplored. It remains unclear whether strong performance on common-style data generalizes to other styles, and whether LMMs can adapt their reasoning when the visual input deviates from the photographic norm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241338/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark feeds images across 15 distinct style categories (5 artistic, 5 sensor, 5 application) paired with a text question, and the LMM produces a free-form text answer. Evaluation uses the ChatGPT API to score answer similarity on a 0.0–1.0 scale against ground-truth labels. Under SPE, an additional instruction ('Determine the style of the image before answering the question') is prepended, so the model first outputs a style prediction before producing the final answer.

## Method

BenchLMM constructs a systematic evaluation suite spanning three orthogonal axes of visual distribution shift: artistic style (Cartoon, Painting, Sketch, Handmade, Tattoo from COCO-O), sensor modality (Infrared, Low/High-energy X-ray, MRI, CT from street-scene, security, and medical datasets), and application domain (remote sensing, autonomous driving, robot action, game, defect detection). The key technical contribution is Style Prompt Enhancement (SPE), a training-free, zero-shot prompting strategy that instructs the LMM to first identify the image's style before answering, inspired by human cognitive behavior of recognizing artistic context prior to object identification. SPE requires no fine-tuning, no additional prompt engineering per style, and is compatible with existing chain-of-thought prompting (ZS-COT), yielding additive gains of +1.4 to +7.0% across style categories. The paper further introduces an error-reflection protocol where the LMM is prompted to explain its own mistakes given the correct answer, revealing that stronger models (GPT-4V) can self-diagnose and derive correct reasoning while weaker models merely restate the answer.

## Results

On the cross-artistic-style evaluation, GPT-4V achieves 81.5% on Common (photo) style but drops to 62.6% average across five artistic styles (Cartoon, Handmake, Painting, Sketch, Tattoo). LLaVA-1.5-13B follows with 74.6% Common and 59.3% artistic average, while the weakest model (MiniGPT4-v2-7B) falls to 35.5%. On cross-sensor evaluation, GPT-4V scores only 55.0% (Infrared) and 50.0% (L-Xray), and on cross-application, its average drops to 60.0% with autonomous driving at just 35.8%. The proposed Style Prompt Enhancement (SPE) yields a +3.6% average improvement on artistic styles and +1.4% on sensor/application styles for LLaVA-1.5-13B in a zero-shot, training-free manner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4V leads on Common style (81.5%) and maintains the top artistic-style average (62.6%), but its advantage narrows substantially compared to open-source models like LLaVA-1.5-13B (59.3%) and InstructBLIP-13B (58.9%). A key finding is that ranking on Common does not predict cross-style ranking: InstructBLIP-13B outperforms LLaVA-1.5-13B on the artistic average (58.9% vs. 59.3% is close, but InstructBLIP-13B beats LLaVA-13B by 10.1 points on artistic average despite lower Common score). On cross-sensor data, LLaVA-1.5-13B achieves the best open-source average (47.3%), while GPT-4V is limited to Infrared (55.0%) and L-Xray (50.0%) due to refusing medical images. On cross-application, GPT-4V averages 60.0% but collapses to 35.8% on autonomous driving, where InstructBLIP-7B (36.7%) actually edges it out. The proposed SPE method, when combined with ZS-COT, boosts LLaVA-1.5-13B from 59.3% to 66.1% on artistic styles (+6.8%) and from 45.2% to 52.2% on application styles (+7.0%), demonstrating that style-aware prompting is complementary to existing chain-of-thought approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual question answering (VQA) evaluation paradigm for Large Multimodal Models by introducing cross-style robustness as a new evaluation axis. Prior LMM benchmarks (MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench) focus on linguistic diversity and common photographic styles, leaving visual distribution shifts unexamined. The proposed Style Prompt Enhancement (SPE) technique repurposes the chain-of-thought prompting idea (ZS-COT) by instructing the model to identify the image style before answering, drawing an analogy to human perceptual ordering. The benchmark construction leverages existing datasets (COCO-O, DOTA, BDD100K, SiXray-D, SLAKE, MVTEC) but re-annotates them in VQA format across three shift dimensions: artistic, sensor, and application styles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that model rankings on common-style data do not predict cross-style performance challenges the assumption that a single leaderboard position captures an LMM's general visual capability, suggesting that deployment in specific domains (medical imaging, security screening, autonomous driving) requires domain-specific evaluation. The SPE technique, while simple, offers a practical zero-shot improvement that could be adopted immediately without retraining. Open questions include whether the performance gaps stem from insufficient training data diversity or fundamental architectural limitations, and whether the error-reflection capability observed in GPT-4V can be systematically cultivated in open-source models. The benchmark's scope (100 images per artistic style, 200 per sensor type) limits statistical confidence, and the reliance on ChatGPT-as-judge introduces evaluation noise.
