## Problem

Previous fashion VLP methods perform cross-modal alignment using only global [CLS] embeddings, making it difficult to distinguish positive pairs from hard negatives that share most fine-grained attributes (e.g., same garment type but different collar style or button placement). Additionally, standard InfoNCE contrastive learning suffers from severe class and loss imbalance—overwhelming easy negatives dominate the gradient signal, suppressing the learning of fine-grained distinctions that are critical for fashion retrieval.

## Input / Output

The model encodes fashion images via ViT-B/16 into a [CLS] token plus N patch embeddings, and fashion text via BERT-base into a [CLS] token plus M token embeddings. For cross-modal retrieval, it produces a ranked list of texts for a query image (ITR) or images for a query text (TIR) by computing relative similarity between integrated uni-modal representations. For text-guided image retrieval, it takes a reference image and a modifying text as a joint query and retrieves a target image using the fusion encoder's [CLS] output. The integrated representations are formed by a weighted sum (λ=0.95) of the global [CLS] embedding and a learned local representation, and momentum queues of size Q store recent integrated representations for large-scale contrastive computation.

## Method

Fashion-FINE introduces three modules operating before multimodal fusion. First, a Modality-Agnostic Adapter (MAA) uses a shared-parameter attention network to assign importance scores to each local patch/token embedding, producing a modality-specific local representation that is combined with the [CLS] embedding to form an integrated representation; shared parameters across modalities encourage cross-modal interaction in a common manifold. Second, Hard Negative Mining with Focal Loss (HNM-F) replaces standard InfoNCE with a focal-loss-modulated contrastive objective using soft labels (a weighted blend of one-hot targets and momentum-model pseudo-targets with η=0.4), so that hard negatives receive proportionally larger gradient signal. Third, Comprehensive Cross-modal Alignment (C-CmA) extracts low-level text features via 1-D convolutions at uni-, bi-, and tri-gram kernel sizes and a high-level fashion symbol embedding (nine predefined categories), combines them (δ=0.95), and aligns this multi-level text representation with the integrated image representation through a contrastive loss. The total training objective sums HNM-F, C-CmA, ITM, prompt token prediction, and token replacement prediction losses, optimized end-to-end on the dual-stream+fusion architecture (ViT-B/16 + BERT-base split 6+6 layers).

## Results

On FashionGen cross-modal retrieval (5-fold protocol), Fashion-FINE achieves a SumR of 524.54, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art FashionSAP (521.07) by 3.47 points, with ITR R@1 of 74.80 and TIR R@1 of 70.70. On FashionIQ text-guided image retrieval, Fashion-FINE achieves a SumR of 323.06, outperforming FashionSAP (301.48) by 21.58 points, with the largest gains in Toptee R@10 (46.81 vs. 41.91) and Shirt R@10 (38.76 vs. 33.17). The full-evaluation protocol on FashionGen confirms these gains, with Fashion-FINE reaching SumR 458.11 vs. FashionSAP's 451.82.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fashion-FINE outperforms the previous state-of-the-art FashionSAP on the primary SumR metric for cross-modal retrieval (524.54 vs. 521.07) and by a wider margin on text-guided image retrieval (323.06 vs. 301.48 SumR on FashionIQ). The gains over FashionSAP are most pronounced in TIR R@1 (+0.58) and TGIR Toptee R@10 (+4.90), indicating improved fine-grained discrimination of hard negatives. Against general VLP baselines, Fashion-FINE leads ALBEF by 40.28 SumR and FashionViL by 28.94 SumR on FashionGen cross-modal retrieval. The only metric where Fashion-FINE trails FashionSAP is TIR R@10 (96.18 vs. 96.38), a negligible 0.20-point difference. On FashionIQ TGIR, Fashion-FINE surpasses FashionVLP by 32.72 SumR and FashionViL by 58.32 SumR, with the largest per-category gain in Toptee R@10 (46.81 vs. 34.98 for FashionViL). The paper also validates applicability to the BLIP foundation model, where adding all three modules improves SumR from 451.70 to 479.87 (+28.17).

## Relation to Prior Work

Fashion-FINE is a direct extension of FashionSAP (CVPR 2023), adopting its dual-stream+fusion architecture, pre-training tasks (prompt token prediction, token replacement prediction), and encoder initialization, while introducing three new modules that operate on the uni-modal representations before fusion. The contrastive learning formulation draws on ALBEF's momentum distillation and soft-label framework, but departs from all prior fashion VLP work (FashionSAP, FashionViL, FashionBERT, KaleidoBERT) by replacing the sole reliance on global [CLS] embeddings with integrated representations that combine global and local patch features via a modality-agnostic adapter. The hard negative mining with focal loss is a novel combination: focal loss has been applied in classification and detection, but its integration into VLP contrastive learning with integrated (global+local) representations for fine-grained alignment is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap in fashion VLP: global-only representations struggle to distinguish positives from hard negatives that share most fine-grained attributes, a problem particularly acute in e-commerce retrieval. The practical impact is for fashion e-commerce platforms where retrieval quality directly affects user experience. However, the quantitative gains over FashionSAP are modest (1–3 R@1 points), the evaluation is confined to two fashion-specific benchmarks, and the approach has not been validated on other fine-grained domains (e.g., medical imaging, satellite imagery, or general fine-grained object recognition). An open question is whether the modality-agnostic adapter and focal-loss-augmented contrastive learning generalize beyond fashion, and whether the benefits persist at larger model scales or with more diverse negative distributions.
