## Problem

Vision foundation models like SAM produce multi-granular 2D segmentations that are neither view-consistent nor hierarchical: the same object may be split into parts in one view, treated as a single instance in another, and missed entirely in a third. Prior NeRF-based feature field methods (DFF, LeRF, SAM-3D) can lift 2D features into 3D but do not enforce a hierarchical structure, struggle with precise boundaries, or require re-rendering for each granularity level. This paper addresses the gap of distilling thousands of conflicting, view-inconsistent 2D masks into a single 3D representation that is simultaneously view-consistent and hierarchically organized.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view posed RGB images (typically 100 training views) along with their camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and runs SAM on each image to obtain 50–150 overlapping segmentation masks per view at multiple granularity levels. At inference, the user specifies a single scalar threshold ε on feature distance; the system then applies a 3D Watershed transform on a KNN graph over the unprojected point cloud to extract all connected components below that threshold, yielding a segmentation at the corresponding granularity. Varying ε produces a full hierarchy from fine subparts up to the whole scene, and the resulting labels can be projected into arbitrary novel views for view-consistent 2D masks.

## Method

The core contribution is learning an ultrametric feature field inside a NeRF (multi-resolution hash-grid encoder + 3-layer MLP producing 256-dim features, following DFF/InstantNGP). The key insight is that ultrametric distances—satisfying d(x,z) ≤ max{d(x,y), d(y,z)} rather than the standard triangle inequality—make distance-based grouping transitive, so that as a threshold grows, smaller clusters merge into larger ones, automatically producing a hierarchy. Training uses contrastive learning with SAM masks as noisy supervision: positive pairs (pixels within the same mask) are pushed closer and negative pairs (pixels in different masks) are pushed apart via a binary cross-entropy loss on distances, with the loss computed in both ultrametric and Euclidean spaces (the Euclidean term provides gradients to all pairs, while the ultrametric term enforces the hierarchy). A hierarchical sampling strategy organizes overlapping SAM masks into an inclusion tree and samples pairs level-by-level to resolve ambiguity about whether two pixels belong to the same segment. At test time, feature maps and depth maps are unprojected into a 3D point cloud, a KNN graph is built with feature-distance edge weights, and a Watershed transform at threshold t recovers all segments at that granularity; a depth-continuity regularization (penalizing curvature changes within a segment) further improves point cloud quality.

## Results

On the Blender-HS dataset, the proposed method (3D mode) achieves a mean Normalized Covering (NC) score of 70.9%, a perfect Segmentation Injectivity (SI) score of 100%, and a View Consistency (VC) score of 78.9%, outperforming all baselines on NC and SI. On PartNet, the method achieves NC_mean of 65.3% (NC_fine=49.9, NC_mid=63.3, NC_coar=82.8), surpassing SAM3D (59.6%) and LSeg+DFF (47.2%) while remaining competitive with the supervised 3D-PIS point-cloud method (NC_fine=52.2, NC_mid=75.0). The method also outperforms the raw SAM input (NC_mean=62.7, VC=46.2), demonstrating that the ultrametric feature field distills noisy multi-view masks into more accurate and view-consistent segmentations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on the primary NC_mean metric on Blender-HS (70.9% vs. 43.2–62.7%), with the largest margin over LSeg (+27.7 pts) and LSeg+DFF (+23.2 pts). It achieves a perfect SI score of 100% due to the watershed transform's non-overlapping guarantee, whereas LeRF (59.8%) and LSeg (69.8%) produce overlapping masks. On View Consistency, the method (78.9%) beats SAM (46.2%), LeRF (64.1%), SAM3D (72.4%), and LSeg (53.6%), but trails LSeg+DFF (82.7%)—though the authors note DFF's high VC may be inflated by its failure to segment small objects. On PartNet, the method (NC_mean=65.3%) surpasses SAM3D (59.6%) and LSeg+DFF (47.2%), but falls behind the supervised 3D-PIS point-cloud network on middle-level NC (63.3% vs. 75.0%), which the authors attribute to 3D-PIS learning semantic part hierarchies from supervision. The method also outperforms raw SAM (NC_mean 70.9% vs. 62.7%, VC 78.9% vs. 46.2%), confirming that the ultrametric distillation improves upon the source segmentation signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DFF/NeRF feature-field paradigm (Kobayashi et al., 2022; Mildenhall et al., 2020), where a volumetric feature branch is learned alongside density and color. It departs from prior feature-field methods (DFF, LeRF, N3F) by replacing standard Euclidean feature distances with ultrametric distances in the contrastive loss, which by the transitivity property of ultrametrics makes hierarchical clustering emerge naturally from thresholding. The watershed transform, a classical 2D hierarchical segmentation tool, is repurposed here to operate on 3D KNN graphs over NeRF-derived point clouds. Unlike SAM-3D, which propagates one mask at a time, this method jointly distills hundreds of noisy, overlapping SAM masks per view into a single coherent 3D hierarchy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: SAM produces thousands of view-inconsistent, non-hierarchical masks that are difficult to unify into a usable 3D representation, and no prior method produces both view-consistent and hierarchical 3D segmentation from images. The ultrametric formulation is conceptually elegant—hierarchy becomes a property of the distance metric rather than an explicit architectural constraint—but the ablation reveals the ultrametric loss contributes only ~2 points while the hierarchical sampling strategy contributes ~6, suggesting the sampling design may matter more than the metric choice. Key limitations include dependence on NeRF point-cloud quality, a very small evaluation dataset (3 Blender-HS scenes), and the unresolved question of which hierarchy is 'correct' in complex scenes without a downstream task to anchor it.
