## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDRM, DDNM+, ΠGDM) require iterative reverse-diffusion sampling with hundreds to thousands of neural network evaluations per measurement, and each measurement is solved independently via separate optimization. This makes them computationally prohibitive for real-time deployment and prevents generalization to unseen measurements, since no learned function maps new observations to solutions without re-optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241416/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement y (a 256×256 RGB image degraded by a known operator H — Gaussian blur with 61×61 kernel, 4× average-pooling downscale, or 128×128 centered box mask — plus i.i.d. Gaussian noise with σ_y = 0.05) and produces a single restored clean image x̂₀ in one forward pass of the neural network (1 NFE). No test-time optimization or iterative denoising is required; the same trained network handles both seen and unseen measurements at inference time, including unknown noise scales.

## Method

DAVI frames inverse problem solving as amortized variational inference: a neural network I_φ is trained to map a measurement y directly to an implicit posterior distribution q_φ(x₀|y), sampled via the reparameterization trick x̂ = I_φ(y + h·z). Training minimizes the ELBO, decomposed into a data-consistency loss (‖y − Hx̂₀‖²) and an Integral KL divergence (IKL) that upper-bounds the KL between the implicit posterior and the pre-trained diffusion prior by integrating over diffusion timesteps, thereby avoiding the disjoint-support problem of raw KL. The IKL gradient requires the score of the implicit distribution, which is intractable, so a separate score network s_ψ is trained via denoising score matching and updated alternately with I_φ. A Perturbed Posterior Bridge (PPB) further improves generalization by training on intermediary measurements y_a = (1−σ_a)y + σ_a·x₀ + hσ̄_a·z drawn along a noisy bridge between y and x₀, with a monotonically increasing perturbation schedule and beta-distributed sampling of the bridge parameter a.

## Results

On FFHQ 256×256, DAVI achieves FID of 29.92 (Gaussian deblur), 23.96 (4× super-resolution), and 14.31 (box inpainting) with only 1 NFE, outperforming all baselines that require 20–1000 NFEs. On ImageNet 256×256, DAVI similarly leads with FID of 63.29, 36.27, and 63.04 across the three tasks. Inference time is 0.04 sec/image, roughly 42× faster than the next-fastest baseline (DDRM at 1.7 sec) and ~1900× faster than DPS (77.7 sec). DAVI also demonstrates robustness to unknown noise scale σ_y, maintaining PSNR of 23.47 while methods requiring σ_y (e.g., DDRM) degrade to 16.09.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAVI outperforms all six baselines on FID and LPIPS across all three tasks on both FFHQ and ImageNet, while remaining competitive on PSNR. Against DPS [4] (1000 NFE), DAVI improves FID by 12.33 points on super-resolution (23.96 vs. 36.29) and 16.96 points on box inpainting (14.31 vs. 31.27 for DiffPIR), despite using 1000× fewer network evaluations. Compared to DDRM [18] (20 NFE), DAVI achieves lower LPIPS (0.225 vs. 0.269 on deblur) and substantially lower FID (29.92 vs. 56.82), though DDRM retains a slight PSNR edge on deblur (26.26 vs. 25.46). Against RED-diff [23] (1000 NFE), DAVI improves FID by 16.63 points on deblur and 68.86 points on super-resolution, while matching or exceeding PSNR. DDNM+ [39] and ΠGDM [32] (both 100 NFE) are consistently outperformed on all three metrics. The single-step inference (0.04 sec/image) is 42× faster than DDRM and ~1900× faster than DPS, making DAVI the only method suitable for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAVI sits at the intersection of two threads: diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DDRM, DDNM, DPS, ΠGDM, RED-diff) that iteratively run reverse diffusion conditioned on a measurement, and amortized variational inference (Shu et al. 2018) that learns a single network mapping inputs to posterior distributions. The key departure is treating the pre-trained diffusion model purely as a prior (via its score function) rather than as an iterative sampler, and using the Integral KL divergence from Diff-Instruct (Luo et al. 2023) to regularize the implicit posterior toward the prior without requiring tractable score computation. The Perturbed Posterior Bridge is a novel training-time construct that has no direct analogue in prior inverse-problem or VI literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2000× inference speedup (0.04 s vs. 77.7 s for DPS) while matching or exceeding FID/LPIPS quality makes diffusion-prior-based restoration viable on commodity hardware and for real-time applications, a regime previously inaccessible to iterative samplers. However, the method requires training a separate network per degradation operator, limiting its zero-shot applicability to truly unknown forward models; the paper acknowledges this by evaluating only three fixed tasks. Open questions include whether the amortized framework can be extended to arbitrary or unknown degradation operators, whether it scales to higher resolutions or video, and whether the single-step approximation degrades under extreme noise or severe degradation beyond the training distribution.
