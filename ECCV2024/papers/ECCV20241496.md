## Problem

While appearance customization (subject, style) of text-to-video diffusion models has been well studied, motion customization remains under-explored. Directly applying existing adaptation methods (full fine-tuning or LoRA) to learn motion from reference videos causes the learned motion to become entangled with the limited appearances in those videos, preventing generalization to diverse new appearances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241496/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of one or more reference videos (each a short clip of a specific motion concept) and text prompts as input, and produces generated videos that replicate the learned motion while varying freely in appearance. It also accepts a single reference image as an appearance provider to animate with the learned motion, and supports mixing spatial LoRAs from one video with temporal LoRAs from another to compose appearance and motion from different sources. The base models are 3D U-Net video diffusion models (ModelScope, ZeroScope) operating in latent space with 576×320 resolution.

## Method

MotionDirector employs a dual-path LoRA architecture that decouples appearance and motion learning: a spatial path injects trainable spatial LoRAs into spatial self-attention and feed-forward layers (trained on a single randomly sampled frame per step to capture appearance), while a temporal path shares those spatial LoRAs and additionally injects temporal LoRAs into temporal transformer layers (trained on all frames to capture motion dynamics). A key insight is that in latent space, motion governs the internal connectivity structure among frame-level latent codes while appearance governs the distances between different videos' point sets; to exploit this, the authors propose an appearance-debiased temporal loss (AD-Loss) that subtracts an anchor noise from each frame's noise before computing the temporal objective, removing positional bias introduced by appearance. At inference, only the trained temporal LoRAs are injected into the frozen base model, enabling generation of the learned motion across arbitrary text-prompted appearances without any spatial LoRA overhead.

## Results

On the LOVEU-TGVE single-video benchmark, MotionDirector achieves the best Appearance Diversity (28.48), Temporal Consistency (93.00), and Pick Score (20.74) among all methods, while VideoCrafter leads in Text Alignment (28.03 vs. 27.82). In human evaluation, MotionDirector is preferred over all four baselines on Appearance Diversity (69–78% preference) and Temporal Consistency (60–72%), and wins on Motion Fidelity against VideoComposer (61.2%) and VideoCrafter (60.0%). On the UCF Sports Action multi-video benchmark, MotionDirector preserves the base model's Appearance Diversity (28.94 on ZeroScope, +0.54 over base) while coupled tuning degrades it by ~2.9 points, and human raters prefer MotionDirector's motion fidelity 76.5% of the time over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionDirector outperforms all four external baselines on Appearance Diversity in both automatic and human evaluation, with the largest margins against Control-A-Video (28.48 vs. 25.35 automatic; 78.4% human preference) and Tune-A-Video (28.48 vs. 25.95; 69.1% human preference). On Motion Fidelity in human evaluation, MotionDirector wins against VideoComposer (61.2% vs. 38.8%) and VideoCrafter (60.0% vs. 40.0%), but trails slightly behind Control-A-Video (56.5% vs. 43.5%) and Tune-A-Video (56.5% vs. 43.5%), indicating controllable methods retain a small edge in strict motion replication at the cost of appearance diversity. VideoCrafter achieves the highest Text Alignment score (28.03) but MotionDirector is close (27.82) and preferred by human raters 52.7% of the time. Internally, the coupled-tuning baseline degrades Appearance Diversity by ~2.9 points on both ModelScope and ZeroScope, while the ablation without AD-Loss recovers diversity but still lags MotionDirector on motion fidelity in human evaluation (75.0% vs. 76.5% preference on ZeroScope). Overall, MotionDirector is the only method that simultaneously preserves appearance diversity, achieves competitive text alignment, and leads in motion fidelity across the majority of comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionDirector extends the LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm (originally for LLMs, then adapted to image diffusion) to the video domain, but departs from prior appearance-customization methods (DreamBooth, VideoCrafter) by explicitly targeting motion dynamics rather than subject or style. It contrasts with controllable video generation approaches (VideoComposer, Control-A-Video) that rely on spatial control signals like depth maps, which inherently couple appearance and motion. The dual-path architecture—separating spatial LoRAs (trained on single frames) from temporal LoRAs (trained on multi-frame sequences)—is a novel structural decomposition of the 3D U-Net's transformer layers, and the appearance-debiased temporal loss introduces a new training objective that removes positional bias in latent space to isolate motion learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By cleanly decoupling appearance and motion, MotionDirector enables practical downstream applications that were previously infeasible: mixing the appearance of one video with the motion of another, and animating a single image with a learned motion concept. The method is lightweight (9M/12M trainable parameters on a 1.8B-parameter base model) and converges in under 20 minutes, making motion customization accessible. A key open question is generalization to complex multi-subject motions (e.g., a group playing soccer), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. The task of motion customization is still nascent, with several concurrent works emerging, so the field's direction and best practices remain unsettled.
