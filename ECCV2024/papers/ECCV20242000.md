## Problem

Existing NOCS datasets cover fewer than 10 object classes with limited instances, and NOCS prediction models rely on class-specific convolutional heads that cannot scale to large vocabularies. Meanwhile, multi-dataset 3D detection methods like Cube R-CNN produce inconsistent object orientations due to Chamfer-distance supervision and provide no shape information. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a large-scale, canonically-oriented NOCS dataset and a class-agnostic transformer model that generalizes across 97+ classes and unseen domains.

## Input / Output

The model takes a monocular RGB image and 2D object bounding boxes (from an external detector) as input. Per-RoI features are extracted via RoIAlign into a fixed 28×28 grid. Outputs include a 3-channel NOCS map (predicted as 50-bin classification per coordinate with softmax), a binary instance mask, a 3D size vector, and a 6D rotation matrix plus 2D centroid projection, from which a metric-scale 3D oriented bounding box and point cloud are recovered via PnP.

## Method

NOCSformer uses a frozen DINOv2 ViT backbone with a trainable DPT head to fuse multi-scale features and upsample by 8×, then samples per-object features via RoIAlign. A 10-block self-attention transformer head jointly predicts the NOCS map and instance mask (replacing the class-specific MaskRCNN conv heads used in prior work), while a separate size head and a learned PnP head (predicting 6D rotation in allocentric space and 2D centroid projection) recover metric pose. Training combines supervised losses (softmax cross-entropy + L1 for binned NOCS, L2 for mask, size-normalized L1 for dimensions, L1 for rotation and centroid) with a self-supervised reprojection loss from MonoRUn. The key design choices—frozen self-supervised features, class-agnostic transformer heads, and discretized NOCS prediction—enable scaling to 97 classes and zero-shot transfer to unseen datasets like NOCS-Real275.

## Results

On the nuScenes outdoor localization benchmark, NOCSformer achieves the best mIoU (0.377) and lowest orientation error (0.558 rad) among all multi-dataset methods, while remaining competitive with Cube R-CNN on translation (mATE 0.887m vs 0.650m) and scale (mASE 0.291 vs 0.283). On NOCS prediction accuracy, NOCSformer achieves 0.094 mAE and 20.245 dB PSNR on OmniNOCS (75 classes), and outperforms the NOCS baseline [49] on the held-out NOCS-Real275 dataset (0.107 mAE vs 0.121 mAE) without any training on that dataset. For 3D orientation on KITTI-val, NOCSformer reaches 49.16% accuracy at 5° heading error, substantially exceeding Cube R-CNN (19.43%) and its Cubeformer variant (41.99%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the NOCS baseline [49] (Wang et al., CVPR 2019), NOCSformer wins on NOCS prediction accuracy on the held-out NOCS-Real275 dataset (0.107 mAE vs 0.121 mAE; 18.53 dB vs 16.35 dB PSNR; 89.03 vs 86.10 mask IoU) despite never training on that data, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization. Against Cube R-CNN [7] (Brazil et al., CVPR 2023), NOCSformer achieves higher mIoU (0.377 vs 0.349) and dramatically lower orientation error (0.558 rad vs 1.305 rad) on nuScenes, but has slightly worse translation (0.887m vs 0.650m) and scale (0.291 vs 0.283). On cross-dataset generalization to NOCS-Real275, NOCSformer substantially outperforms Cube R-CNN (mAP 3D IoU@25: 43.5 vs 14.9), though both fall short of the supervised NOCS baseline (79.6). Against single-dataset methods (FCOS3D, PGD, EProPNP), NOCSformer has higher translation error but is the only method that generalizes across 97 classes and multiple domains. On KITTI-val orientation accuracy, NOCSformer (49.16% at 5° heading) beats both Cube R-CNN (19.43%) and the directly-supervised Cubeformer (41.99%), confirming that NOCS-based orientation prediction yields more canonical and consistent results than Chamfer-distance supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NOCS representation introduced by Wang et al. (CVPR 2019) from a small, class-specific paradigm (fewer than 10 classes, MaskRCNN-style heads) to a large-scale, class-agnostic transformer architecture. It draws on MonoRUn's self-supervised NOCS loss and the broader trend of using frozen DINOv2 backbones for dense prediction, but departs from prior NOCS models by eliminating all class-specific parameters in favor of a unified transformer head. The dataset construction also builds on Omni3D's multi-domain aggregation strategy while fixing its critical weakness: inconsistent object orientations caused by Chamfer-distance supervision in Cube R-CNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniNOCS is the first NOCS dataset to scale to 97 classes and 380k images, making it a natural benchmark for future work on large-vocabulary 3D object understanding. The zero-shot generalization to NOCS-Real275 (outperforming a model trained specifically on that dataset) suggests that scale and architectural generality can substitute for domain-specific tuning. Key open questions remain around symmetric objects (multiple valid coordinate frames) and reflected geometry (left/right pairs), which the authors acknowledge but do not resolve. The dataset and model also open the door to downstream tasks like 3D panoptic scene graphs and video tracking that require consistent, canonical object frames across diverse categories.
