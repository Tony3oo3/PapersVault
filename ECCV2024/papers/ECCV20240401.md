## Problem

Diffusion Transformers suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity that becomes prohibitive at high resolutions, yet the visual generation community has largely overlooked the substantial query-key redundancy present in their attention layers. This paper identifies that such redundancy is especially pronounced in early denoising steps and gradually diminishes over time, suggesting that full one-to-one attention is unnecessary throughout the entire generation process.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and a random noise latent as input and produces a generated RGB image at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 resolution on ImageNet. A distinctive aspect is that the number of mediator tokens in the attention layers changes dynamically during the multi-step denoising process, so the effective model architecture is not fixed at inference time but adapts per-sample based on the latent distance between adjacent denoising steps.

## Method

The paper introduces attention mediators: a small set of n tokens (n ≪ N, e.g., <10% of image tokens) obtained by spatially pooling the query tokens, which interact with keys and queries separately via two sequential softmax attention operations, reducing per-head complexity from O(N²d) to O(Nnd). This decoupling allows the computation order to be rearranged so that values are first aggregated with precomputed key-mediator attention, yielding overall linear complexity in the number of image tokens. A time-step dynamic adjustment mechanism then modulates the mediator count across denoising steps: fewer mediators are used when the latent distance between adjacent steps is large (high redundancy, early steps), and more mediators are introduced as redundancy decreases (later, detail-rich steps), with switching thresholds determined by a sample-specific ratio of current to initial latent differences. The approach is validated on SiT, where replacing the first four self-attention layers with mediator attention and fine-tuning achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 2.01 on ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, the proposed method (SiT-XL/2 with attention mediators, cfg=1.5) achieves a state-of-the-art FID-50K of 2.01, sFID of 4.49, IS of 271.04, Precision of 0.82, and Recall of 0.60. This outperforms the SiT-XL baseline (FID 2.06) and DiT-XL (FID 2.27) while also reducing inference FLOPs. At higher resolutions, the speedup grows with sequence length: for SiT-B/2, the FLOP reduction increases from 15.7% at 512² to 45.4% at 1024² resolution, while still improving FID (e.g., −4.90 FID for SiT-S/2 at 1024²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best FID (2.01) among all compared class-conditional generators on ImageNet 256×256, beating the closest competitor SiT-XL [51] by 0.05 FID (2.01 vs. 2.06) and 0.01 sFID (4.49 vs. 4.50), while also improving Recall (0.60 vs. 0.59). Against DiT-XL [57], the gap is larger: 0.26 FID (2.01 vs. 2.27) and 0.11 sFID (4.49 vs. 4.60), though DiT-XL retains a slight edge in IS (278.24 vs. 271.04) and Precision (0.83 vs. 0.82). The method also surpasses VDM++ [41] (FID 2.12) and StyleGAN-XL [67] (FID 2.30), the two strongest non-diffusion-Transformer baselines. All GAN-based (BigGAN-deep, StyleGAN-XL), masked-transformer (Mask-GIT), and earlier diffusion baselines (ADM, CDM, RIN, Simple Diffusion) are outperformed by a wide margin of 0.76–8.93 FID. Crucially, the method achieves these quality gains while reducing inference FLOPs relative to the SiT baseline, and the efficiency advantage scales with resolution (45.4% FLOP reduction at 1024² for SiT-B/2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the mediator-token architecture from Agent Attention (Han et al., ECCV 2024), originally designed for visual recognition, into the diffusion generation setting, where the mediators serve as semantically compressed bridges between queries and keys. It is applied on top of SiT (Ma et al., ECCV 2024), a recent interpolant-based diffusion transformer, and borrows the depthwise convolution from FLatten Transformer (Han et al., ICCV 2023) to mitigate feature diversity loss from linear attention. The key conceptual departure from prior linear-attention work (SOFT, Nyströmformer, etc.) is the exploitation of *temporal* redundancy across denoising steps—quantified via Jensen-Shannon divergence—to dynamically adjust mediator count, rather than applying a fixed compression ratio throughout inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: at 1024² resolution the method yields a 45.4% FLOP reduction while simultaneously improving FID, a double win that is increasingly important as diffusion transformers scale to video and higher resolutions. The empirical finding that early denoising steps exhibit near-zero query-key diversity (and that compressing them *improves* generation quality) is a useful diagnostic for the field. Open questions include whether the dynamic mediator schedule transfers to text-to-image or video diffusion settings, how it interacts with other efficiency techniques (e.g., distillation, step reduction), and whether the JSD-based redundancy analysis holds for larger, more diverse training distributions beyond ImageNet class-conditional generation.
