## Problem

SNN-based event-to-video reconstruction methods still incorporate ANN-based operations (e.g., floating-point upsampling, synaptic filtering for ISTA), undermining the energy efficiency that motivates spiking computation. Moreover, the temporal latent representation of event voxels—the shared structure between a voxel and its corresponding frame—has been largely unexplored in SNN-based E2V, leaving the ill-posed nature of sparse event-to-frame recovery inadequately addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241113/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a spatio-temporal event voxel of shape h×w×B (B=5 temporal bins, 128×128 spatial resolution) as input and produces a single grayscale video frame as output. Unlike bin-by-bin recurrent approaches, the entire voxel is processed at once, reducing inference steps to 0.2× those of EVSNN/PA-EVSNN. The sensing matrix Φ (B×C, C=10) is learned jointly, mapping the latent coding back to the voxel space for reconstruction supervision.

## Method

STLR consists of two cascaded SNNs: a Spike-based Voxel Temporal (SVT) encoder and a U-shape SNN decoder. The SVT is a spike-driven spatial unfolding network with K=5 unfolding steps, where each step contains a multi-compartment spiking (MCS) module (Heaviside gate + two LIF neurons) that generates layer-wise binary spikes; the spike rate over K steps is proven (Theorem 1) to approximate the fixed point of non-negative ISTA for the constrained LASSO problem. A Fixed Point Approximation (FPA) loss explicitly penalizes deviation from the ISTA fixed-point equation to guide convergence. The U-shape decoder concatenates all K layers of spiking features into a C×K channel map and reconstructs the frame using LIF neurons with SLTT, with an interpolation spiking layer that converts bilinearly upsampled features back to binary spikes to preserve energy efficiency. Training uses an arctan surrogate gradient for backpropagation through the non-differentiable spike generator.

## Results

On the IJRR dataset, STLR achieves MSE 0.075, SSIM 0.469, and LPIPS 0.264; on HQF, MSE 0.089, SSIM 0.349, LPIPS 0.408; and on MVSEC, MSE 0.128, SSIM 0.251, LPIPS 0.552. STLR outperforms the pure SNN baseline EVSNN on all metrics across all three datasets and matches or exceeds the hybrid PA-EVSNN on most metrics, particularly on MVSEC (MSE 0.128 vs. 0.202, SSIM 0.251 vs. 0.174). In terms of energy efficiency, STLR requires only 5.26%–5.53% of the energy cost of EVSNN and 4.75%–5.00% of PA-EVSNN, with 0.61M parameters versus 4.41M and 4.62M respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pure SNN baseline EVSNN [53], STLR wins on every metric across all three datasets (e.g., IJRR: MSE 0.075 vs. 0.097, SSIM 0.469 vs. 0.383; MVSEC: MSE 0.128 vs. 0.243, SSIM 0.251 vs. 0.110). Compared to the ANN-SNN hybrid PA-EVSNN [53], STLR is competitive on IJRR (MSE 0.075 vs. 0.074, LPIPS 0.264 vs. 0.297) and HQF (MSE 0.089 vs. 0.085, LPIPS 0.408 vs. 0.412), and clearly superior on MVSEC (MSE 0.128 vs. 0.202, SSIM 0.251 vs. 0.174, LPIPS 0.552 vs. 0.603). Against the ANN baselines, STLR's MSE is comparable to SSL-E2VID on MVSEC (0.128 vs. 0.151) and to FireNet on HQF (0.089 vs. 0.089), while its LPIPS beats SSL-E2VID on all datasets (e.g., IJRR: 0.264 vs. 0.370; MVSEC: 0.552 vs. 0.719). However, the strongest ANNs (E2VID, FireNet) still lead on IJRR and HQF in MSE and SSIM. The key differentiator is energy: STLR uses only 0.61M parameters and achieves 5–6% of the energy cost of both SNN baselines, making it the most energy-efficient model in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) event-to-video reconstruction via deep networks (E2VID, FireNet, SPADE-E2VID), (2) SNN-based E2V methods (EVSNN, PA-EVSNN) that aim to reduce floating-point computation, and (3) LASSO/ISTA unfolding for sparse signal recovery in event vision (eSL, CISTA). The key departure from prior SNN-based E2V work is the proposal of a spike-driven spatial unfolding network (SVT) that implements integrate-and-fire dynamics through network depth rather than temporal iterations, with a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) linking the accumulated spike rate to the ISTA fixed point. Unlike prior IF-based ISTA implementations that rely on synaptic filtering (incompatible with multilayer SNNs and introducing floating-point operations), STLR uses a fixed-point approximation loss and a U-shape SNN decoder with an interpolation spiking layer to maintain full binary spike computation throughout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5% energy cost relative to competing SNNs (EVSNN, PA-EVSNN) is practically significant for edge and neuromorphic deployment of event cameras, where power budgets are extremely tight. However, a substantial portion of this savings stems from the much smaller parameter count (0.61M vs. 4.4–4.6M) and ADD-dominated computation rather than a fundamentally new computational paradigm, and the theoretical guarantee (u^K/K → 0) is not enforced in practice. Open questions include whether the SVT can be deepened without degrading the fixed-point approximation, whether the approach extends to color or high-resolution video, and whether the surrogate gradient training can be replaced by more principled SNN learning rules. The FPA loss ablation shows mixed per-metric effects (worse MSE on IJRR but better SSIM/LPIPS), suggesting the loss design may still be suboptimal.
