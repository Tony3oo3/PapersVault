## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based image dehazing methods require retraining the entire diffusion model from scratch and rely on time-consuming iterative reverse sampling at inference, limiting their practical deployment. This paper asks whether the semantic latent space (h-space) of a frozen pre-trained diffusion model can be directly exploited to guide dehazing without any retraining of the diffusion model or iterative sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241178/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image as input and produces a dehazed clean image of the same spatial dimensions. During inference, the hazy image is noised at two fixed time-steps (t₁=0 and t₂=500) and passed through a frozen unconditional DDPM (pre-trained on ImageNet) to extract h-space bottleneck features, which then condition the dehazing network. Training uses 128×128 crops with the Adam optimizer over 1200 epochs on a single 3090Ti GPU.

## Method

The core insight is that h-space features of a hazy image undergo a gradual transformation as the diffusion time-step increases: at small t they primarily encode underlying image content, while at larger t they increasingly capture haze characteristics. DiffLI²D is a U-Net-style dehazing network whose blocks each contain two modules: a Content Integration Module (CIM) that uses multi-head cross-attention to query h_{t₁}^{haz} (t₁=0) for content recovery, and a Haze-Aware Enhancement (HAE) module that applies SFT-style spatial modulation and channel-wise gating driven by h_{t₂}^{haz} (t₂=500) for haze removal. The pre-trained diffusion model is entirely frozen and serves only as a one-shot feature extractor, so no diffusion retraining or iterative sampling is needed. Training is supervised with an L1 reconstruction loss, and the total parameter count is only 8.63M, far smaller than diffusion-based baselines.

## Results

On the synthetic SOTS dataset, DiffLI²D achieves 40.33 dB PSNR, 0.992 SSIM, and 0.004 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest non-diffusion baseline Restormer (38.43 dB) by 1.9 dB and the largest diffusion-based method IR-SDE (33.82 dB) by 6.5 dB, while using only 8.63M parameters. On real-world Dense-Haze, it reaches 16.97 dB PSNR (vs. 16.62 for Dehamer), and on NH-HAZE it attains 20.29 dB PSNR with the best SSIM (0.738) and LPIPS (0.217) among all compared methods. For low-light enhancement on LOL-v1, DiffLI²D achieves 23.30 dB PSNR and 55.88 FID, surpassing the best prior method Restormer (20.61 dB, 73.00 FID) by 2.7 dB and 17.1 FID points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffLI²D achieves the best PSNR on both SOTS (40.33 dB) and Dense-Haze (16.97 dB), beating the strongest CNN/Transformer baselines Restormer (38.43 / 15.17 dB) by 1.9 / 1.8 dB and Dehamer (36.63 / 16.62 dB) by 3.7 / 0.35 dB, while using only 8.63M parameters versus their 26.13M and 132.50M. Against diffusion-model-based methods, the gains are dramatic: IR-SDE (537.21M params) trails by 6.5 dB on SOTS and 7.7 dB on NH-HAZE, and ResShift (114.65M params) lags by 11.3 dB on SOTS. On NH-HAZE, DiffLI²D's PSNR (20.29 dB) is slightly below Dehamer (20.66 dB), but it clearly wins on perceptual quality (SSIM 0.738 vs 0.684; LPIPS 0.217 vs 0.230). For low-light enhancement on LOL-v1, DiffLI²D outperforms all baselines including Restormer (23.30 vs 20.61 dB PSNR) and the diffusion-based WeatherDiff (23.30 vs 16.30 dB), confirming generalizability beyond dehazing. Overall, DiffLI²D matches or exceeds every baseline on at least two of the three metrics across all three dehazing datasets, with the sole PSNR exception being Dehamer on NH-HAZE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the h-space discovery of Kwon et al. [28], who showed that the bottleneck of a frozen pre-trained diffusion U-Net encodes high-level semantics useful for semantic manipulation, to the low-level image restoration domain. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based restoration paradigm (IR-SDE, ResShift) that re-trains or fine-tunes diffusion models conditioned on degraded inputs, and from guidance-based methods (DPS, PGG) that still require iterative reverse sampling. Instead, DiffLI²D treats the frozen diffusion model purely as a feature extractor, feeding h-space representations at two selected time-steps into a lightweight U-Net dehazing network via cross-attention (CIM) and SFT-style modulation (HAE).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a large efficiency win: 8.63M parameters and single-pass inference versus 537M+ parameters and multi-step sampling for re-trained diffusion restoration methods, while achieving competitive or superior PSNR across synthetic and real-world dehazing benchmarks. The empirical finding that h-space representations naturally transition from encoding image content (small t) to encoding haze characteristics (large t) provides a mechanistic explanation for why two time-steps suffice and opens the door to applying similar frozen-model feature extraction to other degradation types. Open questions include whether the h-space property generalizes beyond the single unconditional DDPM tested, whether it holds for latent diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion), and how sensitive performance is to the choice of pre-training data distribution relative to the target degradation.
