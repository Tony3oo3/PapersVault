## Problem

Long-form video understanding requires simultaneously processing multi-modal information, handling very long sequences, and reasoning over them, yet existing models fail to excel at all three: LLMs reason well but cannot perceive visual content, while VLMs perceive but degrade on long contexts. The paper argues that directly feeding entire videos into a model diverges from how humans actually understand long videos, which rely on iterative, question-guided information gathering rather than exhaustive visual processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a long-form video (decoded at 1 fps) and a multiple-choice text question as input, and produces a predicted answer along with the full state-action-observation sequence. The video is never processed as a whole; instead, it is consumed through an initial uniform sample of N frames (5 for EgoSchema) followed by iterative retrieval of a small number of additional frames, averaging only 8.4 frames total. The internal state is maintained entirely as text captions of seen frames rather than raw visual tokens, keeping the LLM context manageable even for hour-long videos.

## Method

VideoAgent formulates video understanding as a state-action-observation loop controlled by GPT-4 as an agent, with a VLM (LaViLa or CogAgent) and CLIP (EVA-CLIP-8B-plus) serving as tools. At each iteration, the LLM first makes a chain-of-thought prediction, then self-reflects to assign a three-level confidence score; if confidence is below threshold, it identifies what specific information is missing and generates segment-level retrieval queries. The video is split into segments bounded by previously seen frame indices, and CLIP retrieves the single most relevant frame within each queried segment, which the VLM then captions to update the state. This multi-round, query-rewritten, segment-constrained retrieval distinguishes VideoAgent from prior single-round or video-level retrieval approaches, enabling adaptive frame budgets that scale with question difficulty (descriptive questions use ~6 frames, temporal reasoning ~8).

## Results

On EgoSchema, VideoAgent achieves 54.1% zero-shot accuracy on the full test set and 60.2% on the 500-question subset, using only 8.4 frames on average per video. On NExT-QA, it reaches 71.3% accuracy on the full validation set (8.2 frames average) and 58.4% on the ATP-hard subset, which specifically tests long-term temporal reasoning. These results surpass the previous SOTA (LLoVi) by 3.8% on EgoSchema and 3.6% on NExT-QA while using 20× fewer frames (8.4 vs. 180).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoAgent outperforms the previous SOTA LLoVi [69] on both benchmarks: 54.1% vs. 50.3% on EgoSchema full (+3.8%) and 71.3% vs. 67.7% on NExT-QA (+3.6%), while using 20× fewer frames (8.4 vs. 180). On EgoSchema, it also beats MC-ViT-L [2] by 9.7% on the full set (54.1% vs. 44.4%), though MC-ViT-L edges ahead on the 500-question subset (62.6% vs. 60.2%). On NExT-QA, VideoAgent surpasses all zero-shot baselines including SeViLA [68] (71.3% vs. 63.6%), ViperGPT [46] (71.3% vs. 60.0%), and AssistGPT [6] (71.3% vs. 58.4%), as well as the best supervised method HiTeA [66] (71.3% vs. 63.1%). On the ATP-hard subset, which isolates long-term temporal reasoning, VideoAgent achieves 58.4%, exceeding the best supervised baseline GF [1] (49.3%) by 9.1%. The frame-efficiency gap is stark: VideoAgent's 8.4 frames match or exceed the accuracy of methods using 90–256 frames (e.g., LongViViT at 256 frames achieves only 33.3% on EgoSchema full).

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoAgent synthesizes two research threads—long-form video understanding (compressive methods like LLoVi, selective retrieval like SeViLA) and LLM agent frameworks (ReAct, Reflexion)—into a unified state-action-observation loop. It departs from prior selective-retrieval methods by performing multi-round, query-rewritten, segment-level frame retrieval rather than single-pass retrieval with the original question, and from prior agent-like video systems (ViperGPT, AssistGPT) by explicitly formulating video understanding as an iterative decision-making process where the LLM controls when to stop searching. The self-reflection mechanism for confidence scoring is borrowed from Reflexion, while the CLIP-based retrieval follows the R-VLM/SeViLA lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper makes a compelling case that iterative, question-guided reasoning over a small number of frames can outperform brute-force long-context processing, with 20× fewer frames than LLoVi yielding higher accuracy—a finding that challenges the prevailing assumption that more visual context is always beneficial. However, the heavy reliance on GPT-4 for structured JSON output limits reproducibility with open-source models, and the zero-shot evaluation on only two benchmarks (both multiple-choice) leaves open whether the approach generalizes to open-ended video QA or longer temporal horizons. The hour-long video case study is promising but anecdotal, and the method's practical deployment is constrained by API costs and latency from multiple LLM/VLM calls per iteration.
