## Problem

Multimodal LLMs built on safety-aligned LLMs are easily jailbroken when image inputs are introduced, as visual features suppress the pre-aligned safety mechanisms inherited from the text-only LLM. The paper asks how to reactivate these dormant safety mechanisms in MLLMs without expensive retraining or external detectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240454/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text query as input. It always outputs a text response: either the initial image-conditioned response (if self-assessed as safe) or a regenerated text-only response conditioned on a query-aware caption of the image plus the original query. The image is consumed twice—once for the initial response and once for captioning—but is discarded before the final safe response is generated, effectively reducing the MLLM to a text-only LLM at inference time.

## Method

ECSO is a training-free, four-step inference pipeline: (1) generate an initial response from the image and query; (2) prompt the same MLLM to self-assess whether that response is safe or unsafe; (3) if unsafe, generate a query-aware image-to-text caption that preserves task-relevant visual information while stripping adversarial visual features; (4) regenerate the response using only the caption and query (no image), with explicit 'HARMLESS and ETHICAL' instructions in the prompt. The key insight is that removing the image restores the pre-aligned LLM's safety mechanism, while the query-aware captioning (conditioned on the original question) retains enough visual context to maintain utility. The approach requires no additional training, external detectors, or auxiliary models, and can additionally serve as a data engine to produce SFT pairs for downstream safety alignment.

## Results

On MM-SafetyBench (SD+OCR) with LLaVA-1.5-7B, ECSO improves the harmless rate from 32.1% (Direct) to 86.4% (+37.6%), and on the OCR split from 31.7% to 90.3% (+42.2%). On VLSafe, ECSO achieves a 71.3% absolute improvement in harmless rate with LLaVA-1.5-7B. Utility is preserved: on MME-P, MME-C, MM-Vet, and MMBench, ECSO scores are within ±0.5% of Direct prompting across all five MLLMs, with slight gains on MME-C (e.g., +1.14% for ShareGPT4V-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline throughout the paper is Direct prompting (querying the MLLM without any protection). ECSO substantially outperforms Direct on all safety benchmarks: on MM-SafetyBench (SD+OCR), the average harmless rate jumps from 32.1% to 86.4% for LLaVA-1.5-7B, and similar large gains are observed for ShareGPT4V-7B, mPLUG-Owl2-7B, Qwen-VL-Chat, and InternLM-XComposer-7B. On VLSafe, ECSO achieves a 71.3% absolute improvement over Direct with LLaVA-1.5-7B. In the safety alignment experiment (Sec. 5.6), ECSO-generated SFT data (ECSO_VLGuard) is compared against human-verified VLGuard data [Zong et al., 2024]; ECSO_VLGuard offers better safety than VLGuard while ECSO itself (training-free) still outperforms both finetuned variants in most scenarios, demonstrating that the generated data is comparable to or better than human-annotated ground truth.

## Relation to Prior Work

ECSO departs from the dominant training-based alignment paradigm (SFT/RLHF with red-teaming data) and from inference-time defenses that rely on external components (safety steering vectors, auxiliary detectors/detoxifiers as in MLLMProtector). Its conceptual lineage is closest to self-moderation approaches (Chen et al.), but it reframes the problem: rather than asking the MLLM to *generate* a safe response while images are present, it exploits the observation that the pre-aligned LLM's safety mechanism is merely suppressed—not destroyed—by image features, and restores it by converting images to query-aware text. This makes ECSO training-free and self-contained, requiring no additional models, datasets, or fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that ECSO offers an immediately deployable, zero-cost safety layer for any MLLM built on a safety-aligned LLM, and its secondary role as an automatic SFT data generator reduces the labor-intensive red-teaming bottleneck in safety alignment. A key limitation is that ECSO's ceiling is bounded by the base LLM's own safety mechanism; if the underlying LLM is poorly aligned, ECSO inherits that weakness. The paper leaves open the broader question of whether multimodality can be turned from a safety liability into an asset, and whether the 'suppression' phenomenon generalizes beyond the specific MLLM architectures tested.
