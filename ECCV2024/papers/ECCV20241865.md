## Problem

Neural inverse rendering methods produce realistic facial avatars but rely on implicit or point-based representations incompatible with industrial animation pipelines that require topology-consistent mesh blendshape rigs. Conversely, traditional mesh-based approaches use simplified rendering models that underfit complex facial materials and arbitrary lighting, degrading reconstruction quality. Additionally, directly optimizing per-vertex deformations on high-resolution meshes leads to non-smooth surfaces and self-intersections, while jointly optimizing expression bases and coefficients introduces semantic ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method accepts single-view or sparse multi-view (4 views in main experiments) RGB videos captured from unsynchronized mobile phones, with no requirement for temporal synchronization or color correction across cameras. It outputs a personalized facial rig comprising a neutral face mesh and 53 expression blendshapes derived from the ICT Face Model, structured with consistent topology so it can be directly imported into Blender for animation. The rig encodes identity-specific geometry in the neutral mesh and person-specific expression deformations in the blendshape bases.

## Method

The core deformation representation parameterizes per-vertex displacements in differential coordinates augmented with internal tetrahedral connections (via TetGen), which propagates gradients to topologically and spatially adjacent vertices to enforce smoothness while preventing self-intersection during extreme expressions. To resolve the ambiguity in jointly optimizing blendshape bases and expression coefficients, three semantic regularizations are applied: locality (constraining updates to each blendshape's original activation region), sparsity (L0.75 norm on blendshape updates), and symmetry (updating only the left half of symmetric blendshapes). A 1D Instant-NGP regressor maps absolute timestamps to per-frame head poses and expression coefficients, implicitly synchronizing unsynchronized multi-view inputs without explicit alignment. Rendering uses mesh-based differentiable rasterization (Nvdiffrast) combined with a deferred neural shader: per-vertex latent codes are interpolated per-pixel and fed to a 3-layer MLP along with normals, view directions, and per-camera latent codes to predict RGB. All components (facial rig, synchronization regressor, and neural shader) are jointly optimized from random initialization over 200 epochs using a composite loss of landmark, mask, photometric, Laplacian, locality, sparsity, and neutral-deformation terms.

## Results

On the Multiface dataset, the method achieves a mean point-to-plane error of 2.31 mm (std 0.05), and on NeRSemble it achieves 2.73 mm (std 0.26). These are the lowest errors among all compared methods on both datasets, with the next-best being NHA at 3.76 mm (Multiface) and 4.98 mm (NeRSemble). The method also shows the smallest standard deviation on Multiface (0.05 mm), indicating highly consistent reconstruction across identities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on both datasets in point-to-plane error. Against NHA (Grassal et al., CVPR 2022), the closest competitor, the method reduces error from 3.76 mm to 2.31 mm on Multiface (~39% reduction) and from 4.98 mm to 2.73 mm on NeRSemble (~45% reduction). Against PointAvatar (Zheng et al., CVPR 2023), the improvement is most dramatic: 7.66 mm to 2.31 mm on Multiface (~70% reduction) and 7.22 mm to 2.73 mm on NeRSemble (~62% reduction). FLARE (Bharadwaj et al., 2023) is beaten by ~59% on Multiface (5.61→2.31 mm) and ~54% on NeRSemble (5.88→2.73 mm). HRN (Lei et al., CVPR 2023), a large-scale in-the-wild face reconstruction network, is outperformed by ~47% on Multiface (4.37→2.31 mm) and ~40% on NeRSemble (4.53→2.73 mm). Qualitatively, the paper reports that the method better reconstructs identity-specific geometric features (e.g., aquiline nose) and expression-specific details (e.g., personalized puckering) compared to all baselines, with error heatmaps confirming lower residuals especially in the forehead and nose regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: mesh-based facial rigging (blendshape generation for animation pipelines) and neural inverse rendering (deferred neural rendering, NeRF-based avatars). It builds on the ICT Face Model as a base blendshape template, adopts differential coordinates and the AdamUniform optimizer from Nicolet et al. for stable per-vertex deformation, uses Instant-NGP as a 1D temporal regressor for implicit multi-view synchronization, and follows Worchel et al.'s deferred neural rendering paradigm for appearance. The key departure from prior neural avatar methods (NHA, PointAvatar, FLARE) is the explicit commitment to topology-consistent mesh output compatible with industrial animation software, and the joint optimization of both blendshape bases and expression coefficients with semantic regularization—whereas prior methods typically fix one or the other to avoid ambiguity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in producing Blender-importable, person-specific facial rigs from as few as four unsynchronized phone videos, removing the studio-level capture requirement that has gated high-quality facial animation. The differential-coordinate-plus-tetrahedral deformation representation is a reusable technique for any high-resolution mesh optimization task where smoothness and self-intersection avoidance matter. Open questions include making the deferred rendering MLP directly usable in animation pipelines (currently it is rendering-only), extending to volumetric details like hair via hybrid representations, and handling tongue motion which the ICT base model lacks. The method also assumes pre-calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, limiting its applicability to fully uncontrolled capture.
