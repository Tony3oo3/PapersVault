## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting offers fast, high-quality rendering but its points are inherently disorganized and unstructured, making them incompatible with standard volumetric neural networks for generation. Existing feed-forward text-to-3D methods either avoid Gaussians entirely or rely on optimization-based approaches that are slow (hours per object) and suffer from the Janus problem. No prior work has demonstrated direct feed-forward generation of 3D Gaussians from text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240214/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input, encoded via CLIP text embeddings, and produces a GaussianVolume: a fixed-resolution 32×32×32 volumetric grid (32,768 Gaussians) where each cell stores a position offset, 3D scale, quaternion rotation, RGB color, and opacity. The output is directly renderable via the standard 3DGS tile-based rasterizer at real-time speed, and the full generation pipeline completes in approximately 7 seconds on a single GPU.

## Method

GVGEN introduces a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. In the data preparation stage, 3D Gaussians are organized into a structured GaussianVolume at fixed resolution N=32, where each Gaussian's center is expressed as a small offset Δμ from its assigned grid point; a novel Candidate Pool Strategy handles pruning and densification by storing deactivated points in a pool and reactivating the nearest pool points when new Gaussians are needed, preserving the fixed-count constraint. In the generation stage, a text-conditioned diffusion model first produces a Gaussian Distance Field (GDF)—an isotropic volume encoding the distance from each grid point to its nearest Gaussian center—capturing coarse geometry. A 3D U-Net (modified from SDFusion) then takes the GDF and text embedding to predict all Gaussian attributes in a single forward pass, trained with a combined 3D MSE loss on the volume and a 2D rendering loss (L1 + SSIM) on rasterized views to balance global structure with local detail.

## Results

On the Objaverse-LVIS evaluation set (300 prompts from 40 LVIS categories, 8 rendered views per object), GVGEN achieves a CLIP score of 28.53, marginally outperforming Shap-E (28.48) while being faster (7 s vs. 11 s). GVGEN substantially surpasses VolumeDiffusion (25.09) and DreamGaussian (23.60) in text-image alignment, and is roughly 25× faster than DreamGaussian (~3 min). The generation speed of ~7 seconds is on par with the fastest feed-forward baselines (Shap-E, VolumeDiffusion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GVGEN achieves the highest CLIP score (28.53) among all four quantitative baselines, edging out Shap-E (28.48) by a narrow margin while generating 36% faster (7 s vs. 11 s). Against VolumeDiffusion, GVGEN gains +3.44 CLIP points, reflecting more realistic textures and better text alignment; VolumeDiffusion tends to produce unrealistic surface appearances. DreamGaussian, the optimization-based baseline, lags significantly at 23.60 CLIP and requires ~3 minutes per object, suffering from the over-saturation artifact common to SDS-based methods. In qualitative image-to-3D comparisons (Fig. 6b), GVGEN outperforms single-view reconstruction models OpenLRM and TGS, which exhibit 'average pattern' artifacts in unseen regions, whereas GVGEN produces plausible geometry and textures. Overall, GVGEN wins or ties on every quantitative metric tested and demonstrates clear qualitative advantages in text fidelity and generation diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

GVGEN sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and feed-forward text-to-3D generation. It departs from optimization-based methods (DreamFusion, DreamGaussian) that rely on SDS and take hours per object, and from prior feed-forward approaches (Shap-E, VolumeDiffusion) that generate implicit representations or point clouds. The key conceptual departure is restructuring unstructured 3D Gaussians into a fixed-resolution volumetric grid (GaussianVolume) via the Candidate Pool Strategy, making them amenable to diffusion-based generation. The coarse-to-fine pipeline—first generating a Gaussian Distance Field via diffusion, then predicting full Gaussian attributes with a 3D U-Net adapted from SDFusion—mirrors the geometry-then-appearance decomposition common in 3D generation but is the first to target explicit Gaussian primitives directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GVGEN demonstrates that explicit 3D Gaussians can be generated feed-forward from text in ~7 seconds, closing the speed gap with implicit-representation methods while retaining the real-time rendering and direct manipulability advantages of Gaussians. The GaussianVolume formulation and Candidate Pool Strategy offer a reusable recipe for converting unstructured point-based representations into grid-compatible formats for generative models. However, the volume resolution is capped at N=32 (32,768 Gaussians), limiting fidelity for complex textures, and performance degrades on prompts far from the training distribution. Open questions include whether higher resolutions are tractable, how the approach scales to scenes (vs. single objects), and whether the structured volumetric constraint fundamentally limits the expressiveness achievable compared to fully unstructured Gaussians.
