## Problem

Large language models have been extended to 2D images, audio, and other modalities, but remain largely unable to directly understand 3D point clouds, which offer unambiguous geometric and appearance information free from depth, occlusion, and viewpoint issues inherent to single-view images. This gap is compounded by the absence of large-scale point-text instruction-following data and the lack of generative benchmarks and appropriate evaluation metrics for assessing MLLM-based 3D comprehension.

## Input / Output

The model takes a colored object point cloud of 8192 points (6 dimensions each: x, y, z, r, g, b) and a natural-language instruction as input, producing a free-form text response such as an object category or a detailed caption. Point tokens are wrapped in special <p_start> and <p_end> markers and interleaved with text tokens in a single sequence fed to the LLM. For ModelNet40 evaluation, point clouds are assigned a uniform black color since they lack RGB information.

## Method

PointLLM couples a pre-trained Point-BERT encoder (trained with ULIP-2 on Objaverse) with a 3-layer MLP projector (GeLU activations, hidden dims 1024→2048) that maps 513 point features of 384 dimensions into 5120-dimensional tokens matching the LLM's embedding space, followed by a LLaMA/Vicuna decoder-only transformer backbone. Training follows a two-stage protocol: first, only the projector is trained on 660K brief-description instruction pairs to align point and text latent spaces; second, the projector and LLM are jointly fine-tuned on 70K complex instructions (detailed descriptions, single-round Q&A, and multi-round conversations) generated automatically by GPT-4 from Cap3D captions. The loss is computed only on response tokens, and an ablation shows that 3 projection layers yield the best feature clustering (verified via T-SNE), while max pooling over point tokens reduces training time by ~75% at a small accuracy cost.

## Results

On the Generative 3D Object Classification benchmark, PointLLM-13B achieves 53.39% average accuracy across ModelNet40 (zero-shot) and Objaverse (open-vocabulary), surpassing the best 2D MLLM baseline (LLaVA-7B at 44.86%) and the best 3D MLLM baseline (3D-LLM at 45.25%) by over 8 points. On the 3D Object Captioning benchmark, PointLLM-13B achieves a human correctness score of 3.10 and GPT-4 score of 48.15, outperforming all baselines and exceeding human annotators (correctness 2.67) in over 50% of samples. PointLLM also exhibits the lowest hallucination rate (0.66 for 7B) and highest precision (82.14%) among all models tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointLLM decisively outperforms all four baselines on both classification and captioning tasks. Against 2D MLLMs (InstructBLIP and LLaVA), PointLLM-13B leads by 8.5–21.9 points in average classification accuracy, demonstrating that direct point cloud input avoids depth ambiguity and viewpoint dependency inherent in single-view images. Against 3D-LLM (which uses multi-view rendered images + 2D foundation models), PointLLM-13B gains 8.1 points in average classification accuracy and 14.7 points in GPT-4 captioning score, while also producing more consistent results across prompt types. Point-Bind LLM, which relies on retrieval-based alignment via ImageBind, performs poorly on open-vocabulary Objaverse classification (6.00%/4.50%) due to hallucination from its retrieval nature, though it is competitive on ModelNet40 (51.90%). On captioning, PointLLM-13B achieves the highest human correctness (3.10 vs. 2.76 for LLaVA-7B) and lowest hallucination among non-human models (0.84), with a 55% win rate over human annotators in pairwise comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointLLM directly adapts the LLaVA-style encoder-projector-LLM architecture and two-stage training (feature alignment followed by instruction tuning) from 2D image MLLMs to 3D point clouds, using Point-BERT (pre-trained via ULIP-2) as the point encoder and LLaMA/Vicuna as the language backbone. It departs from concurrent 3D-LLM work, which renders objects into multi-view images and relies on 2D foundation models (CLIP, SAM, BLIP), and from Point-Bind LLM, which uses retrieval-based alignment with ImageBind; PointLLM instead performs direct end-to-end alignment of point cloud features with the LLM's token space. The automated GPT-4-based data generation pipeline, leveraging Cap3D captions on Objaverse, extends the instruction-data synthesis strategies from LLaVA to the 3D domain, producing 730K training samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MLLM to directly understand colored point clouds in an end-to-end, instruction-following manner, opening a path toward 3D copilots for design software and embodied AI where point clouds are the natural sensor input. The two proposed benchmarks (Generative 3D Object Classification and 3D Object Captioning) and the 730K-sample dataset fill a critical gap in evaluation infrastructure for 3D MLLMs. Key open questions remain: the model is limited to object-level point clouds (scene-level understanding on ScanNet is only preliminary), hallucination persists (and counterintuitively increases with model scale from 7B to 13B), and the benchmarks are small (200 objects), limiting statistical confidence. Extending to point cloud generation and scalable scene understanding are natural next steps.
