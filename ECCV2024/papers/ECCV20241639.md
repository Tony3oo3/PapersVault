## Problem

Existing image anomaly detection methods either require a supervised one-class classification assumption (all training data is purely nominal) or, even when unsupervised, still pre-compute a fixed nominal model that assumes a stationary distribution. This limits applicability to controlled settings and prevents on-the-fly detection in scenarios where the nominal distribution shifts over time, such as changing manufacturing outputs or naturally lit scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241639/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of RGB images (rescaled to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224), processing each image through both a model update step and a prediction step in a single pass. Feature descriptors are extracted via a pre-trained Wide-ResNet50, with 4×4 patches aggregated over local neighborhoods using adaptive average pooling after a Johnson-Lindenstrauss random projection. Outputs include pixel-wise anomaly scores (up-sampled 4× from patch resolution and Gaussian-smoothed), an image-wise anomaly score (maximum pixel score), and a binary anomaly localization mask.

## Method

Online-InReaCh maintains a nominal model as a set of Inter-Realization Channels, where each channel holds at most one patch per image realization and represents a nominal feature that persists across recent samples. In the update step, input patches are matched to existing channel centers via symmetric nearest-neighbor association; unassociated patches seed new channels, while all channels carry a time-to-live counter that decrements when not updated, automatically pruning stale features from distribution shifts. In the inference step, each patch is scored by (1) its minimum L2 distance to any component patch in filtered channels (sufficient span and recency) and (2) a positional consistency term measuring how well the patch's image location aligns with the mean position of its closest channel's components, weighted by that channel's positional variance. The high-precision channel construction—requiring symmetric mutual nearest-neighbor matching—ensures the nominal model remains almost purely nominal (precision >0.99), enabling the use of a simple nearest-neighbor distance as the anomaly score without contamination from anomalous patches.

## Results

In the offline unsupervised setting (40 anomalous training images), Online-InReaCh achieves 0.917 image-wise and 0.931 pixel-wise AUROC on MVTec AD, 0.897/0.955 on BTAD, and 0.846/0.964 on VisA, placing it second among unsupervised methods overall. In the novel fully online setting with 23.8% of images containing anomalies, Online-InReaCh reaches 0.936 image-wise and 0.961 pixel-wise AUROC on MVTec AD, with no prior baselines existing for this protocol. Under the most extreme non-stationary shift (entirely different test class), Online-InReaCh still achieves 0.870/0.927 image/pixel AUROC, substantially outperforming all statically pre-computed baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the supervised (0-anomaly) setting, PatchCore dominates with 0.991 image AUROC on MVTec AD, while Online-InReaCh (0.947) trails by ~4.4 points, as expected since Online-InReaCh is designed for the unsupervised regime. In the unsupervised (40-anomaly) setting, Online-InReaCh is competitive with InReaCh (0.917 vs 0.917 on MVTec AD image AUROC) and SoftPatch (0.917 vs 0.986), and clearly outperforms PatchCore (0.917 vs 0.728) and SPADE (0.917 vs 0.839), which degrade severely under label noise. On VisA pixel-wise AUROC, Online-InReaCh achieves 0.964, surpassing InReaCh (0.897) and SoftPatch (0.935) in the noisy setting. Under non-stationary distribution shifts (Table 2), Online-InReaCh wins decisively across all three shift types: e.g., on the full class shift it scores 0.870/0.927 image/pixel AUROC versus 0.549/0.680 for PatchCore and 0.745/0.903 for InReaCh. In the fully online setting (Table 3), no baselines are reported because no prior method supports on-the-fly prediction with online model creation, making Online-InReaCh the first to establish these benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

Online-InReaCh directly extends InReaCh's Inter-Realization Channels framework from offline pre-computation to fully online operation, adding a time-to-live mechanism for stale channel removal and on-the-fly prediction. It also inherits PatchCore's patch-based feature extraction pipeline (Wide-ResNet50 intermediate features with Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection and adaptive average pooling). The key conceptual departure from both InReaCh and SoftPatch is the shift from pre-computing a fixed nominal model under stationarity assumptions to dynamically maintaining a local, recent model that can track distribution shifts without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to combine fully unsupervised training, online model construction, on-the-fly prediction, and non-stationary distribution tolerance in image anomaly detection, opening the door to industrial and field applications where lighting, product type, or operating conditions change over time. The demonstrated robustness to 23.8% anomaly prevalence with no performance correlation (R²=0.015) and rapid adaptation to entirely new classes within 1–2 frames are practically significant. Limitations include reliance on a pre-trained CNN (bias toward ImageNet-represented features), inability to handle video where temporally coherent anomalies would be absorbed into the nominal model, and the method's patch-based design constraining spatial context. Open questions include how the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-class streaming settings and whether the nearest-neighbor scoring remains competitive as channel counts grow.
