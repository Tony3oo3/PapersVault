## Problem

Estimating global human motion from dynamic cameras is difficult because human and camera motions are entangled. Existing methods that leverage learned motion priors (e.g., PACE, WHAM) produce oversmoothed motions with misaligned 2D projections and fail catastrophically on out-of-distribution motions, because naive Score Distillation Sampling from diffusion models generates inconsistent pseudo-ground-truth due to randomness in the denoising process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dynamic RGB video of T frames and produces both global human motion (per-frame SMPL parameters: global translation, orientation, 23×3 body pose, foot contact labels, and 10-dim shape) and camera trajectory (per-frame rotation and translation) in a shared world coordinate system. Initialization comes from HybrIK for per-frame camera-space SMPL parameters and DROID-SLAM for camera-to-world transforms; because SLAM trajectories are scale-ambiguous, the optimization also jointly recovers the camera scale, first-frame height, and first-frame orientation.

## Method

COIN-SDS replaces the single-step SDS pseudo-ground-truth with a multi-step (10-step) DDIM denoising process augmented by two mechanisms: (1) a ControlNet-style dynamic control branch, where the optimized motion from the previous iteration serves as a self-evolving control signal to guide the pre-trained motion diffusion model, and (2) a soft inpainting strategy that uses a continuous mask (weighted by per-dimension confidence scores and a linearly decreasing timestep function) to preserve high-confidence observed regions while sampling uncertain regions from the diffusion prior. A novel human-scene relation loss (L_HSR) resolves the SLAM scale ambiguity by enforcing that scene point-cloud depths (scaled by the optimized camera scale) must be greater than the depth of the nearest body joint at each projected 2D location, thereby coupling human, camera, and scene consistency. The full pipeline jointly optimizes human motion, camera poses, scale, height, orientation, and shape under a combined objective of 2D/3D reprojection, smoothness, foot-contact, COIN-SDS, and human-scene relation losses.

## Results

On the RICH dataset, COIN achieves a W-MPJPE of 254.5 mm, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art PACE (380.0 mm) by 33% and WHAM (497.6 mm) by 49%. On EMDB, COIN reduces W-MPJPE₁₀₀ to 407.3 mm versus WHAM's 439.2 mm (7% improvement), and on the synthetic HCM dataset it reaches 479.9 mm versus WHAM's 588.9 mm (19% improvement). For camera motion on HCM, COIN achieves an ATE-S of 385.9 mm, a 16% reduction over PACE (459.7 mm). COIN also improves local pose (PA-MPJPE) by 3.3–19.8 mm across the three datasets, demonstrating that the diffusion prior benefits both global and local estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COIN outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric. Against PACE (the prior SOTA for joint human-camera estimation), COIN reduces W-MPJPE by 125.5 mm (33%) on RICH and 381.3 mm (44%) on HCM, while also improving camera ATE-S by 73.8 mm. Against WHAM (the strongest global-motion-only method), COIN wins by 243.1 mm on RICH, 31.9 mm on EMDB, and 109.0 mm on HCM in W-MPJPE. The naive diffusion baselines (Vanilla SDS, Guided Sampling) fail catastrophically—Vanilla SDS actually performs worse than the unoptimized HybrIK+SLAM baseline on RICH (1453.5 vs. 1073.1 mm W-MPJPE)—confirming that the control-inpainting mechanism is essential. Noise Optimization is competitive on RICH (414.8 mm) but still 160 mm worse than COIN. SLAHMR and GLAMR, which rely on VAE-based motion priors rather than diffusion, are consistently outperformed by 100–400 mm in W-MPJPE. On EMDB, COIN also achieves the best root translation error (3.5 m vs. WHAM's 8.4 m) and root orientation error (34.1° vs. 36.3°), indicating superior trajectory recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework from DreamFusion to the domain of temporal human motion, building on the Human Motion Diffusion Model (Tevet et al.) as a pre-trained motion prior and ControlNet for conditional guidance. It departs from prior joint human-camera estimation methods (PACE, SLAHMR, WHAM) that rely on VAE-based motion priors or SLAM alone, which produce oversmoothed motions and fail catastrophically on out-of-distribution inputs. The key conceptual departure is identifying that naive SDS is fundamentally inconsistent for temporal motion due to mode-averaging across randomly sampled denoising steps, and addressing this through a dynamic control-inpainting mechanism that iteratively refines the control signal and applies confidence-weighted soft constraints during multi-step DDIM denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33–49% W-MPJPE improvements over SOTA on three benchmarks demonstrate that diffusion-based motion priors, when properly constrained, can significantly outperform VAE-based priors for global motion estimation from dynamic cameras. The human-scene relation loss offers a generalizable principle for resolving scale ambiguity by exploiting occlusion depth ordering rather than relying solely on human motion quality. However, the method remains an offline optimization requiring SLAM initialization, fails under severe occlusions, and is unsuitable for real-time applications; the authors suggest that a joint human-camera diffusion model could eventually enable real-time estimation via few-step guided sampling.
