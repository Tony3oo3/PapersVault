## Problem

Active visual localization requires selecting camera viewpoints that maximize pose estimation accuracy, but existing approaches either rely on hand-crafted geometric metrics (e.g., Fisher Information Fields) that ignore the spatial distribution of landmarks in the image, or require manually annotated training data. No prior method combines a self-supervised learning strategy with a compact, real-time architecture that can be directly embedded into a motion planning framework for robotics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242300/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse SfM landmark set and a camera pose (position from an 8×8×8 voxel grid, orientation from Fibonacci spherical sampling) as input. Visible landmarks are reprojected into a 30×30 image bin grid, where each bin stores the mean 2D projection, depth, and 3D camera-frame coordinates, yielding a fixed-length flattened vector of 4220 values. The output is a scalar quality score in [0,1] per viewpoint; crucially, multiple viewpoints per voxel location can exceed the threshold, giving planners flexibility to choose the most convenient (e.g., shortest-path) active viewpoint.

## Method

A 3-layer MLP (300-300-1 with ReLU, 0.5 dropout, sigmoid output) predicts viewpoint quality from the binned geometric features. Training is fully self-supervised: for each sampled viewpoint, a dense RGB image is rendered via a simulator (Open3D + Habitat-Matterport 3D meshes), localized against the SfM model using COLMAP in global mode, and labeled positive or negative based on whether the registration error (rotation + translation) falls below a predefined threshold. The key insight is that the spatial distribution of landmarks across the image—captured by the binning scheme—matters more for localization accuracy than Fisher information metrics, which primarily reward landmark proximity and visibility. Visibility filtering uses TSDF integration (with dense depth) or sparse hidden-point removal (without), and the entire sampling pipeline is parallelized in CUDA, reducing initial map construction from ~7387 s (CPU) to ~23 s (GPU) for 648 locations and 25k landmarks.

## Results

On a test set of ~92k camera viewpoints from Habitat-Matterport 3D, the proposed method achieves 72.9% localization accuracy at the finest threshold (0.05 m, 0.4°) and 81.7% at the coarsest (5 m, 10°), following the Long-Term Visual Localization benchmark. This outperforms the best Fisher Information Field variant (FIF det app) by 12.2 percentage points at the finest threshold (72.9% vs. 60.7%) and by 12.3 points at the coarsest (81.7% vs. 69.4%). The method also surpasses the reprojection-with-bins baseline (68.7% / 71.9%) by 4.2 and 9.8 points respectively, and random sampling (54.7% / 61.1%) by 18.2 and 20.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against Fisher Information Fields (FIF) [55] in multiple variants (min-eigenvalue, determinant, trace; exact vs. approximate vs. GP-smoothed), random viewpoint sampling, and a reprojection-with-bins heuristic. The proposed method decisively outperforms all FIF variants across every threshold, with the largest gap of ~12 percentage points at the finest (0.05 m, 0.4°) threshold, suggesting that the spatial distribution of landmarks in the image is more predictive of localization quality than the Fisher information matrix, which primarily captures landmark vicinity. Against the reprojection-with-bins baseline—which already captures some distributional information—the proposed method gains 4.2 points at the finest threshold and up to 9.8 points at the coarsest, indicating that the learned scoring function captures subtler geometric cues beyond simple bin counts. In qualitative planning experiments (Fig. 5), FIF fails to predict valid viewpoints in certain regions, causing planner failures, while the proposed method consistently directs the camera toward high-landmark-density areas even with a noisy SfM model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classification-based active viewpoint selection framework of Hanlon et al. [17] by replacing its reliance on manually annotated labels with a self-supervised training loop that uses COLMAP registration error as the supervision signal. It departs from the Fisher Information Fields (FIF) line of work [55], which evaluates viewpoints through hand-crafted information-theoretic metrics (minimum eigenvalue, determinant, trace of the Fisher matrix), by instead learning directly from the spatial distribution of reprojected landmarks in the image. The compact MLP architecture and voxel-grid map representation are positioned as practical alternatives to end-to-end reinforcement learning approaches [7,14,26] that are tightly coupled to agent action execution and difficult to generalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-supervised training pipeline removes the costly manual labeling step that limited prior data-driven active localization, making the approach more broadly applicable. The finding that a simple binned-landmark distribution outperforms Fisher information metrics suggests that feature spatial coverage in the image is a more relevant signal for localization quality than information-theoretic quantities, which could reshape how active viewpoint selection is formulated. However, the evaluation is confined to indoor Habitat-Matterport 3D scenes with a pre-computed SfM model, and the very simple MLP may not scale to large-scale or dynamic outdoor environments; the paper itself calls for a dedicated active localization benchmark and a more robust model as future work.
