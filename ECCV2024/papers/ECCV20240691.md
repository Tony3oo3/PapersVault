## Problem

BEV-based multi-modal 3D object detection methods like BEVFusion assume accurate LiDAR-to-camera calibration for depth supervision, but real-world calibration errors cause two types of feature misalignment: local misalignment where projected depths are inaccurate for neighboring pixels, and global misalignment where LiDAR and camera BEV features are spatially offset. This leads to significant performance degradation (e.g., BEVFusion drops from 68.5% to 60.8% mAP under noisy misalignment) that existing BEV fusion frameworks do not address.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 32-beam LiDAR point cloud (range ±54m in X/Y, -5m to 3m in Z, voxelized at 0.075×0.075×0.2m) and six synchronized camera images (256×704 resolution, 360° surround coverage) from the nuScenes dataset. It produces 3D bounding boxes across 10 object categories (evaluated via mAP and NDS) and, as a secondary task, BEV map segmentation over six semantic classes (drivable area, pedestrian crossing, walkway, stop line, car park, divider) within a ±50m region. The LiDAR-to-camera projection matrix is used to generate explicit depth supervision for the camera branch, which is the source of the misalignment the method corrects.

## Method

GraphBEV extends BEVFusion with two alignment modules. The LocalAlign module addresses local depth misalignment by using a KD-Tree to retrieve K_graph=8 nearest-neighbor projected pixels for each LiDAR-projected pixel, extracting their depths as neighbor depth features, and fusing them with the original projected depth through a Dual Transform (conv+BN+ReLU) and a DepthNet that produces depth-weighted camera features before LSS-style BEV pooling. The GlobalAlign module addresses global BEV misalignment by injecting random offset noise into the camera BEV feature during training, learning per-pixel (u,v) offsets via a CBR module, and applying grid sampling (deformable convolution) to the LiDAR BEV feature to compensate, supervised by an MSE loss against the clean fused feature. The camera branch uses a Swin Transformer backbone with FPN, the LiDAR branch uses SECOND, and a dense detection head from TransFusion produces final predictions. The framework is trained end-to-end on clean nuScenes data with the offset noise serving as a robustness regularizer, requiring no additional calibration at test time.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, GraphBEV achieves 70.1% mAP and 72.9% NDS, outperforming the BEVFusion-MIT baseline by +1.6% mAP and +1.5% NDS. On the nuScenes test set, GraphBEV reaches 71.7% mAP and 73.6% NDS, surpassing BEVFusion-MIT by +1.5% mAP. Under simulated misalignment noise (following Dong et al.), GraphBEV retains 69.1% mAP versus BEVFusion's 60.8%, an improvement of +8.3% mAP and +6.3% NDS, demonstrating substantially greater robustness to sensor calibration errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphBEV outperforms all BEV-based multi-modal baselines on the nuScenes validation set: +1.6% mAP over BEVFusion-MIT (68.5→70.1), +0.3% mAP over ObjectFusion (69.8→70.1), and +2.2% mAP over BEVFusion-PKU (67.9→70.1). On the test set, GraphBEV (71.7% mAP) surpasses DeepInteraction (70.8%), ObjectFusion (71.0%), and BEVFusion-MIT (70.2%), though the margin over ObjectFusion narrows to +0.7%. The most striking comparison is under noisy misalignment conditions: GraphBEV achieves 69.1% mAP versus BEVFusion's 60.8% (+8.3%) and TransFusion's 66.4% (+2.7%), while suffering only a 1.0% mAP drop from its clean-setting performance (70.1→69.1) compared to BEVFusion's 7.7% drop (68.5→60.8). Per-class gains are largest for small objects (Barrier +4.0%, Pedestrian +2.7%, Bike +2.2% over BEVFusion-MIT), consistent with the paper's argument that small objects are most sensitive to feature misalignment. GraphBEV also outperforms BEVFusion on BEV map segmentation (63.3 vs 62.7 mean IoU), confirming generalization beyond detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraphBEV builds directly on BEVFusion (Liu et al., ICRA 2023) as its architectural baseline, retaining the LiDAR-to-camera depth supervision from BEVDepth and the unified BEV fusion paradigm. It departs from BEVFusion by inserting two alignment modules: LocalAlign, which augments projected depth with KD-Tree-based neighbor depth to correct local depth errors from calibration inaccuracy, and GlobalAlign, which learns deformable offsets to rectify residual global misalignment between LiDAR and camera BEV features. Conceptually, the LocalAlign module extends the graph-matching alignment philosophy of GraphAlign (Song et al., ICCV 2023) from feature-level to the depth-projection stage, while GlobalAlign draws on deformable attention ideas from MetaBEV and AutoAlignV2 but applies them at the BEV fusion level rather than during cross-modal query aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck—sensor calibration drift—that causes BEV-based multi-modal detectors to degrade sharply in the field, and demonstrates that a lightweight (+7.7 ms) correction can recover most of the lost performance (8.3% mAP under noisy conditions). However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a single noise-simulation protocol from Dong et al., leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other sensor suites, more severe or dynamic misalignment, and different driving environments. A key open question is whether the learned offsets in GlobalAlign can adapt online to slowly drifting calibration rather than relying on a fixed training-time noise distribution.
