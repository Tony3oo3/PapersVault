## Problem

Perpetual view generation requires synthesizing unbounded flythrough scenes from a single image, but existing approaches either train scene-specific refiners (limiting generalization to natural scenes) or reconstruct 3D point clouds (preventing infinite generation). Frame-by-frame image warping accumulates blurriness and distortion from non-integer pixel coordinates and depth inaccuracies, and no prior method achieves versatile, training-free generation across diverse scenes, styles, and user-controlled camera trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGBD image (depth estimated monocularly via MiDas), a free-form text prompt, and a user-specified camera trajectory (per-step rotation and translation) as input, and produces a temporally coherent sequence of novel view images forming an unbounded flythrough video. The camera trajectory is fully interactive — users can change direction, altitude, or even swap text prompts mid-sequence to shuttle between scenes or styles without retraining or 3D reconstruction.

## Method

DreamDrone's core insight is to explicitly warp the intermediate latent code of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (at timestep t₁=21 via DDIM inversion) rather than warping pixel-space images, then denoise the warped latent to generate the next view. Three mechanisms ensure quality and consistency: (1) a high-pass filtering strategy that warps only the low-frequency FFT component of the latent while preserving the original high-frequency content, preventing detail loss; (2) a cross-view self-attention module that injects warped keys and values from the current view into the U-Net's self-attention when denoising the next view; and (3) a feature-correspondence guidance term that computes cosine distance between warped intermediate U-Net features and the novel view's features, injecting this as a gradient-based guidance signal into the DDIM sampling step. The pipeline is entirely zero-shot and training-free, requiring only the pre-trained diffusion model and a depth estimator.

## Results

DreamDrone achieves the highest CLIP scores across all sequence lengths (0.320, 0.318, 0.319 for 8, 16, and 32 frames), indicating superior text-image alignment and image quality. It also attains the best PSNR (29.91/29.86/29.79) and SSIM (0.39/0.38/0.35) among all methods at 8 and 32 frames. Notably, DreamDrone's CLIP scores remain nearly stable as sequence length increases, whereas training-based methods like VideoFusion (0.281→0.272) and CogVideo (0.255→0.241) show clear degradation. As a training-free method, DreamDrone outperforms all training-based baselines on CLIP score while matching or exceeding them on PSNR and SSIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-based perpetual view generators, DreamDrone substantially outperforms InfNat (CLIP 0.320 vs. 0.125 at 8 frames) and InfNat-0 (0.320 vs. 0.128), reflecting the limited generalization of refiners trained on natural-scene datasets. Among training-based text-to-video methods, DreamDrone surpasses CogVideo (CLIP 0.320 vs. 0.255) and VideoFusion (0.320 vs. 0.281) on text-image alignment, though CogVideo achieves higher PSNR at 8 frames (31.03 vs. 29.91) due to its dedicated temporal attention training. Among training-free methods, DreamDrone clearly beats T2V-0 on all metrics (PSNR 29.91 vs. 27.25, CLIP 0.320 vs. 0.312 at 8 frames) and edges out SceneScape on CLIP (0.320 vs. 0.318) and SSIM at 32 frames (0.35 vs. 0.34), while matching it on PSNR. A key differentiator is stability: DreamDrone's CLIP score barely changes from 8 to 32 frames (0.320→0.319), whereas SceneScape drops from 0.318 to 0.279 and T2V-0 from 0.312 to 0.287, indicating that DreamDrone's geometric warping and guidance mechanisms better preserve quality over longer trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamDrone departs from two dominant paradigms in perpetual view generation: (1) frame-by-frame image warping with a trained refiner (InfNat, InfNat-0, DiffDreamer), which accumulates interpolation errors and is limited to training-distribution scenes, and (2) 3D point-cloud reconstruction followed by rendering (SceneScape, WonderJourney), which constrains generation to a finite reconstructed volume. Instead, it repurposes the intermediate latent codes of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model as the warping substrate, leveraging the known semantic richness of diffusion features (PnP, DIFT) to perform geometry-aware latent-space warping. The feature-correspondence guidance mechanism draws directly on classifier-guided DDIM sampling and DIFT's point-to-point matching, while the cross-view self-attention borrows from video editing work (TokenFlow, Tune-A-Video).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, training-free nature of DreamDrone makes perpetual view generation broadly accessible without dataset-specific fine-tuning, and the interactive camera-trajectory control plus scene-shuttle capability (switching prompts mid-sequence) open practical applications in virtual tourism, game previsualization, and creative content pipelines. However, the method still operates frame-by-frame, so very long trajectories may accumulate subtle drift; depth estimation quality (MiDas) is a critical dependency that can degrade in complex or occluded scenes; and the DDPM forward step (t₁=21 → t₂=441) introduces a trade-off between detail richness and geometric fidelity that is only partially mitigated by the guidance term. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, whether the high-pass filter threshold and guidance weight generalize across scene types without manual tuning, and whether the latent-warping principle extends to video diffusion models for natively temporal generation.
