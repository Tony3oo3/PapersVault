## Problem

LVLMs exhibit 'text inertia,' where they generate identical hallucinated descriptions even when the image input is removed and only prior text context is provided, revealing that the LLM dominates multi-modal comprehension and image tokens are systematically neglected during generation. Existing hallucination mitigation strategies require additional training, architectural changes, or external tools, leaving no lightweight inference-time intervention that directly addresses this image-neglect mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM inputs: an RGB image encoded into image tokens (576 tokens for linear-projector models like LLaVA, 32 for resampler-based MiniGPT-4), a text instruction, and autoregressively generated historical response tokens. It produces the same outputs as the base model—detailed image descriptions (up to 512 tokens) or VQA answers—but with reduced hallucination. A key operational detail is that the method requires a second forward pass per token (without image tokens) to compute the text-only logit distribution for the refinement step, roughly doubling inference cost while remaining compatible with any decoding strategy.

## Method

PAI intervenes at inference time through two complementary mechanisms. First, during the forward pass of each decoder layer, the pre-softmax attention weights for image-token positions are amplified in their original direction (Ã_{n,j} ← Ã_{n,j} + α·|Ã_{n,j}|), with a layer prior restricting intervention to deeper layers where attention-sink patterns stabilize, ensuring the amplification follows the model's own image-grounded direction without requiring learned projections. Second, at each decoding step, the final logit distribution is refined via a classifier-free-guidance-style subtraction: p = γ·p(y|image, instruction, history) − (γ−1)·p(y|instruction, history), which explicitly penalizes tokens that the LLM would produce from text context alone. Both stages are training-free, require no architectural modification, and compose with any decoding algorithm (greedy, beam search, nucleus sampling).

## Results

On the CHAIR hallucination metric (lower is better), PAI reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S) by 47% on LLaVA with greedy decoding (46.6→24.8) and by 53% with beam search (46.4→21.8). Instance-level hallucination (CHAIR_I) drops from 13.4 to 6.9 (greedy) and 14.3 to 5.6 (beam search) on LLaVA. Across all three models (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, Shikra) and all three decoding strategies (greedy, beam search, nucleus), PAI consistently outperforms both the vanilla baseline and the dedicated decoding-based baselines (OPERA for beam search, VCD for nucleus sampling).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAI is compared against two training-free decoding-based baselines: OPERA (beam-search-specific) and VCD (nucleus-sampling-specific). Under beam search, PAI substantially outperforms OPERA on LLaVA (CHAIR_S 21.8 vs. 44.6) and MiniGPT-4 (24.8 vs. 30.1), and marginally beats it on Shikra (35.8 vs. 36.8). Under nucleus sampling, PAI outperforms VCD on all three models, with the largest gap on LLaVA (CHAIR_S 43.4 vs. 51.8) and MiniGPT-4 (26.7 vs. 34.8), while the advantage on Shikra is smaller (49.9 vs. 57.6). A key practical advantage is that PAI is decoding-agnostic and matches vanilla inference speed, whereas OPERA and VCD are tied to specific decoding strategies and add computational overhead. On POPE (VQA), PAI also improves over vanilla in both single-turn and multi-turn settings across all models, with the multi-turn gains being more pronounced (e.g., LLaVA F1: 81.13→85.27). On MMHal-Bench, PAI shows improvements on image-centric question types (object attributes, adversarial objects, holistic descriptions) but limited gains on logic-heavy types (comparisons, spatial relations).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing LVLM hallucination as an inference-time attention imbalance problem rather than a training-data or alignment issue. It departs from the dominant training-based mitigation strategies (data filtering, contrastive alignment, architecture redesign) and from post-processing pipelines (LURE, Woodpecker) that require external models, instead borrowing the inference-time intervention paradigm from LLM truthfulness work (ITI) and the classifier-free-guidance mechanism from LLM-CFG. Among training-free decoding methods, it distinguishes itself from OPERA (which modifies beam search) and VCD (which modifies nucleus sampling) by operating as a model-agnostic inference intervention compatible with any decoding strategy, and by introducing the 'text inertia' diagnostic to explain *why* image tokens are neglected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'text inertia' diagnosis—that LVLMs reproduce identical hallucinated descriptions even when the image is removed—provides a concrete, testable explanation for hallucination that shifts the field's focus from data quality to inference-time attention allocation. As a training-free, plug-and-play method, PAI is immediately deployable on any existing LVLM without retraining or external tools, making it practically accessible. Key open questions remain: whether the image-neglect phenomenon is specific to LLaMA-family decoders, whether incorporating image-attention balance as an explicit training loss could push performance beyond the current upper bound, and why the method shows diminished gains under nucleus sampling, suggesting the sample pool still contains hallucination tokens that attention amplification alone cannot eliminate.
