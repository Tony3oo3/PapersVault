## Problem

Single-photon detectors (QIS, SPAD) produce 1-bit or few-bit video data corrupted by extreme Poisson-Gaussian noise, and when the scene contains strong motion (1–7 pixels/frame), existing quanta burst algorithms like QBP fail to produce clean reconstructions from the small number of frames needed to avoid motion blur. Conventional video restoration methods assume high-SNR CMOS inputs and cannot handle the few-bit, photon-starved regime. The paper addresses the gap of simultaneously denoising and motion-compensating few-bit quanta video with only a handful of input frames.

## Input / Output

QUIVER takes 11 temporally consecutive 3-bit quanta frames (simulated at 3.25 photons-per-pixel per frame, ~1 lux, 2000 fps; spatial resolution 512×1024 on I2-2000FPS, or 240×320 real SPAD data) and produces a single restored grayscale frame. The input frames are extremely noisy with strong inter-frame motion, and the method also accepts 1-bit SPAD frames that are temporally summed to 3-bit before processing. Evaluation is performed at multiple photon levels (3.25, 9.75, 19.5, 26 PPP) to test robustness across illumination conditions.

## Method

QUIVER is an end-to-end trainable CNN with recurrent components organized into four stages mirroring classical quanta restoration: (1) an RDB-based single-image pre-denoiser that improves SNR without the motion artifacts of naive temporal averaging; (2) a learnable SpyNet optical-flow module combined with 3D convolutions for multi-scale spatio-temporal feature extraction, where a Deformable Convolution–Gated Fusion Unit (DC-GFU) warps and fuses features from both noisy and denoised streams; (3) a Recurrent Multi-scale Residual Dense Feature Fusion Unit (RMDF) that progressively fuses multi-scale features across all frames using a hidden state to capture temporal correlations; and (4) a multi-scale reconstruction head combining Temporal Cross Attention (TCA) over all frames with a Residual Frame Refinement Module (RFRM) that reconstructs from coarse to fine scales. The network is trained with a multi-scale L1-plus-gradient loss under supervised learning, using Adam at a low initial learning rate (2.5×10⁻⁵) to stabilize the recurrent components, and takes ~1.5 days on an A100 GPU.

## Results

On the proposed I2-2000FPS dataset at the lowest light level (3.25 PPP), QUIVER achieves 26.21 dB PSNR and 0.7897 SSIM, outperforming the best baseline (FloRNN) by 5.2 dB PSNR and 0.11 SSIM. At the highest light level (26 PPP), QUIVER reaches 27.94 dB PSNR and 0.8446 SSIM. On the external X4K1000FPS dataset, QUIVER similarly leads all baselines, achieving 21.87 dB PSNR at 3.25 PPP versus 20.83 dB for the next-best method (FloRNN). A scaled-down variant, QUIVER-s, also surpasses all baselines while reducing computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QUIVER outperforms all eight baselines across every light level on both I2-2000FPS and X4K1000FPS. The closest competitor is FloRNN [1], which QUIVER surpasses by 5.2 dB PSNR at 3.25 PPP (26.21 vs. 21.03) and by 0.11 SSIM; at higher light levels the gap narrows to ~0.1 dB PSNR but QUIVER still leads in SSIM. The quanta-specific baselines QBP [47] and Student-Teacher [10] degrade severely under strong motion, with QBP dropping to 15.94 dB PSNR and Student-Teacher to 13.29 dB at 26 PPP, confirming their inability to handle fast motion. General video restoration methods (RVRT [42], EMVD [2], MemDeblur [35]) also struggle, with MemDeblur collapsing to 14.49 dB at 9.75 PPP. Adding BM3D pre-denoising to QBP (QBP+BM3D) improves PSNR to 21.55 dB but still trails QUIVER by 4.7 dB. On the external X4K1000FPS dataset, QUIVER maintains its lead over FloRNN by 1.04 dB PSNR at 3.25 PPP, demonstrating generalizability beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

QUIVER directly extends the four-stage classical quanta restoration pipeline (pre-filtering, flow estimation, fusion, refinement) established by QBP [Ma et al., 2020] and Gyongy et al. [2018], but transforms it from a sequential, stage-independent algorithm into an end-to-end trainable deep network where each stage is jointly optimized. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods use naive temporal averaging (which fails under strong motion) and off-the-shelf optical flow estimators trained on clean CMOS images (which fail under extreme photon noise); QUIVER replaces these with a learnable RDB-based pre-denoiser and a SpyNet-based flow module trained jointly with the rest of the pipeline. The novel architectural contributions—DC-GFU (deformable convolution + gated fusion), RMDF (recurrent multi-scale residual dense fusion), TCA (temporal cross-attention), and RFRM (residual frame refinement)—are composites of established components (deformable conv, RDBs, channel attention) arranged in a new multi-scale recurrent topology specific to the quanta restoration problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The I2-2000FPS dataset (280 videos, 114 scenes, 2000 FPS, 512×1024) fills a critical gap: no prior high-speed video dataset offered both blur-free frames and sufficient motion diversity for training quanta restoration networks, and existing high-FPS datasets are dominated by motion-blurred captures. The practical finding that 3-bit sensing at ~1428 fps provides the optimal SNR-to-motion-blur trade-off for 1–7 pixel/frame motion gives sensor designers a concrete operating point. Limitations include evaluation on monochromatic data only, a relatively small dataset (280 clips), and sensor parameters fixed to a specific QIS prototype; generalization to color imaging, different pixel pitches, and real-time deployment on embedded hardware remain open. The demonstrated generalization from QIS simulation to real SPAD data is encouraging but tested on a single SPAD sensor at one photon level.
