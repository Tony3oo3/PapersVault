## Problem

Gaze estimation models trained on controlled lab datasets fail to generalize to unseen domains and in-the-wild conditions, and existing solutions rely on domain adaptation that requires knowledge of and re-training on the target domain. This prevents plug-and-play deployment in real user applications. The paper addresses this by training general gaze estimation models that exploit arbitrary unlabeled face images to close the domain gap without any target-domain information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240558/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a single RGB face image, from which 128×128×3 patches are cropped around each eye and stacked channel-wise with a face crop to form the input tensor. It outputs two modalities: dense 3D coordinates for 481 vertices of a rigid spherical eyeball mesh per eye (left and right), and a 3D gaze vector per eye. The final gaze direction is computed as the mean of the two per-eye gaze vectors, and any sparse or semantic eye representation can be recovered by indexing the predicted mesh.

## Method

3DGazeNet reformulates gaze estimation as end-to-end dense 3D eye mesh regression using a simple ResNet-18 backbone followed by two fully connected layers that jointly predict 3D eye coordinates and gaze vectors. To exploit in-the-wild face images without gaze labels, the paper generates geometry-aware pseudo-labels by fitting a unified 481-vertex spherical eyeball template via 3D face alignment (RetinaFace) and 2D iris localization. A key training innovation is a multi-view consistency loss: HeadGAN synthesizes novel head poses of the same subject while preserving the relative gaze-head angle, and the model is penalized when its 3D eye predictions for the two views are inconsistent after applying the inter-view transformation. The total loss combines vertex L1, edge-length L2, gaze-angle, and multi-view consistency terms, weighted to balance ground-truth supervision from gaze datasets with pseudo-label and multi-view supervision from 255K in-the-wild faces.

## Results

In cross-dataset gaze generalization on Gaze360, 3DGazeNet trained solely with pseudo-labels and multi-view supervision from ITWG-MV achieves 18.1° gaze error, outperforming the closest baseline [40] by 23% (22.5° → 19.7° with CMU+AVA). When ground truth from GazeCapture is added, 3DGazeNet reaches 17.6° on G360, a 10% improvement over [40]. In the domain generalization benchmark (Table 2), training on EXG+ITWG-MV yields 6.0° on MPII and 7.8° on GazeCapture, surpassing all compared SOTA methods (best baseline: 6.9°/9.5°). Within-dataset, the combined Mesh+Vector target achieves 9.6° on Gaze360 and 4.0° on MPII, matching or exceeding all listed baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DGazeNet consistently outperforms all 11 compared baselines in its primary gaze generalization setting. Against the closest weakly-supervised method [40] (Kothari, CVPR 2021), 3DGazeNet achieves a 23% reduction in G360 error without ground truth (18.1° vs 22.5°) and a 10% reduction with ground truth (17.6° vs 19.5°). In the domain generalization benchmark (Table 2), 3DGazeNet with ITWG-MV beats RAT/RUDA [5] by 1.0° on MPII and 0.4° on GC, CDG/CRGA [70] by 0.9° and 1.7° respectively, and PureGaze [11] by 1.7° and 1.5°—the gap widens notably on GC where baselines lack regularization for noisy pseudo-labels. In within-dataset experiments (Table 3), the Mesh+Vector target matches or beats all SOTA methods: 4.0° on MPII (vs 3.92° for L2CSNet [1], 4.00° for Transformer [13]), 9.6° on G360 (vs 10.1° for [40], 10.4° for L2CSNet), and 3.1° on GazeCapture (vs 3.3° for ETH-XGaze [79]). The only setting where 3DGazeNet trails is EXG-only training on GC (10.7° vs 8.4° for RAT), which the authors attribute to the absence of ITWG-MV regularization; with ITWG-MV added, 3DGazeNet recovers and leads all comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants two ideas from adjacent subfields into gaze estimation: dense 3D coordinate regression (established in face, body, and hand pose estimation) and multi-view consistency regularization (established in weakly-supervised 3D pose learning, e.g., Iqbal et al. 2020). The closest prior work is Kothari et al. (CVPR 2021), which also exploits 3D geometric cues for weakly-supervised gaze, but relies on scene-level social-interaction constraints rather than per-subject synthetic multi-view pairs. The paper departs from the dominant domain-adaptation paradigm (RUDA, CRGA, PureGaze) by targeting plug-and-play generalization that requires no target-domain data or retraining, and it is the first to formulate gaze estimation as end-to-end dense 3D eyeball mesh regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play framing is practically significant: user-facing applications (face unlock, driver monitoring, VR/AR) need gaze models that work out-of-the-box across diverse users and environments without per-deployment calibration. The 23% error reduction without any ground truth and the 10% reduction with ground truth, backed by consistent ablations across four test datasets, make this a credible step toward deployable gaze tracking. Key limitations remain: pseudo-label quality is bounded by 3D face and iris alignment accuracy, the method cannot handle faces looking away from the camera, and the kappa coefficient (individual optical-visual axis offset) is deliberately ignored, which limits person-specific accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video-based temporal gaze tracking and whether the HeadGAN dependency can be replaced by cheaper synthetic-view generators.
