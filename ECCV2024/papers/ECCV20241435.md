## Problem

Face anti-spoofing systems suffer from domain shift when deployed across different capture conditions and attack types, degrading generalization performance. The paper decomposes this shift into two separable factors—image quality (degradation, blur, resolution) and image style (local color/texture statistics)—and notes that existing methods either treat these as undifferentiated noise or rely on imprecise human priors. Additionally, labeled data for novel attack types is extremely scarce, and prior generative approaches (GAN, VAE) either overfit to limited data or produce texture-degraded outputs unsuitable for RGB-based FAS.

## Input / Output

The generative component takes a live face image (identity source) and a randomly selected spoof guide image (style source) as input, producing a high-fidelity spoof face that preserves the live identity while adopting the guide's attack texture. For classification, RGB face images are cropped with RetinaFace and resized to 256×256, with BRISQUE no-reference quality scores computed per batch and fed into the loss function as a quality prior. The final output is a live/spoof (or multi-style spoof) class label, with the generative model also serving as a data augmentation engine that enriches training sets with cross-domain and cross-attack-type samples.

## Method

DiffFAS is a U-Net-based diffusion model trained with a spoof-image reconstruction objective (rather than pure noise-to-image generation) to directly learn spoof textures and avoid the high-frequency suppression inherent in standard denoising. A Spoofing Style Fusion Module (STFM) extracts multi-scale features from a pre-trained ResNet encoder on the guide image, computes asymmetric patch-wise means (large patches to suppress identity) and variances (small patches to capture texture), and fuses them into the U-Net backbone via cross-attention at resolutions ≤32×32. During inference, classifier-free guidance with a tunable γ parameter controls spoof strength, and an image-editing sampling strategy (adding t steps of noise to the live image rather than pure Gaussian noise) preserves identity consistency. For classification, a Sample-Level Relative Quality Loss modulates the additive and angular margins of a margin-softmax objective using per-batch normalized BRISQUE scores, so that high-quality images receive larger margins (encouraging deeper spoof-cue mining) while low-quality images receive smaller margins (preventing over-extraction of blur artifacts).

## Results

On the standard OCIM cross-domain protocol (four leave-one-out settings), DiffFAS-V (MobileViT backbone) achieves an average HTER of 6.86% and AUC of 97.47%, outperforming the best prior method SSAN-R (9.82% HTER, 96.45% AUC) by 2.96% in HTER. On the WMCA cross-attack protocol, DiffFAS-V attains 4.25% ACER on the seen test and 6.68% average ACER across seven unseen attack types, surpassing the next-best EPCR (10.50% average). On limited-source-domain settings (MI→C, MI→O), DiffFAS-V reduces HTER by 4–7 points over the strongest baseline DiV-T-M while improving AUC by 6–7 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffFAS-V achieves the best average HTER (6.86%) and AUC (97.47%) on the OCIM protocol, beating SSAN-R (9.82%/96.45%) by 2.96% HTER and SA-FAS (7.83%/96.42%) by 0.97% HTER. On the WMCA cross-attack protocol, DiffFAS-V's 6.68% average unseen ACER substantially outperforms EPCR (10.50%), MCDeepPixBiS (11.08%), DiVT-M (14.83%), and CCL (18.64%), with the largest gap on the Flexible Mask (0.00% vs. 0.70–22.56%) and Paper Mask (13.72% vs. 7.18–49.7%) categories. In the limited-source-domain setting (MI→C, MI→O), DiffFAS-V reduces HTER by 4.8–7.4 points and improves AUC by 5.9–6.9 points over DiV-T-M, the strongest prior method. On the WMCA↔PADISI cross-dataset protocol, DiffFAS-V (7.09%/97.92% and 12.67%/94.04%) outperforms EPCR (10.06%/97.30% and 16.26%/92.49%) and MCDeepPixBiS (12.83%/95.43% and 17.18%/90.96%). The ResNet-18 variant (DiffFAS-R) is competitive with SA-FAS on average HTER (7.82% vs. 7.83%) but trails on the ICM→O protocol (12.38% vs. 10.00%), which the authors attribute to fewer high-quality images in that target domain and ResNet's weaker extraction capacity relative to MobileViT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of domain-generalized face anti-spoofing (building on SSDG, SSAN, PatchNet, SA-FAS) and conditional image generation (building on denoising diffusion models, classifier-free guidance, and Unitune-style image editing). It departs from prior generative FAS work (GAN-based STDN, VAE-based dual spoof disentanglement) by being the first to employ a diffusion model for spoof synthesis, and from prior DG-FAS methods by explicitly decomposing domain shift into a quantifiable quality component (addressed via BRISQUE-scored margin loss) and an unquantifiable style component (addressed via diffusion-based cross-domain generation with a Spoofing Style Fusion Module). The quality-aware loss draws on AdaFace's quality-adaptive margin concept but repurposes it for FAS classification rather than face recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in FAS deployment: the scarcity of labeled data for novel attack types (e.g., flexible masks, glasses) by generating high-fidelity, identity-consistent spoof samples from easily collected live faces. The quality-prior loss offers a simple, interpretable mechanism to counter cross-domain quality shifts that prior methods handled only implicitly. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the open-world constraint—the model requires the target identity to exist in the training set to generate its spoof version, limiting true zero-shot generalization. Future work on identity-independent generation and broader attack-type coverage would strengthen the approach's practical applicability.
