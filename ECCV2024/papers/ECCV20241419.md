## Problem

CLIP-like vision-language models trained on web-crawled data embed NSFW and toxic concepts in their latent spaces, causing unsafe retrieval results and inappropriate generations when given inappropriate prompts. While prior work has mitigated NSFW content in diffusion models specifically, no approach had addressed the more general problem of making the CLIP embedding space itself invariant to inappropriate inputs while preserving its utility for downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241419/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The fine-tuned Safe-CLIP accepts the same inputs as standard CLIP (text prompts and RGB images) but redirects NSFW inputs to the embedding region of their sanitized counterparts. The ViSU training dataset consists of 165k quadruplets (safe image, safe caption, unsafe image, unsafe caption) generated from COCO Captions. The resulting safe encoders are drop-in replacements: the text encoder plugs into Stable Diffusion v1.4 for text-to-image generation, and the visual encoder plugs into LLaVA for image-to-text generation, requiring no architectural modification to the downstream models.

## Method

The approach has two main components. First, a ViSU dataset is built by fine-tuning Llama 2-Chat (via SFT on 100 curated pairs, then DPO with an automatic ranking based on CLIP similarity and GPT-3.5 NSFW classification) to generate unsafe sentences from safe ones, paired with NSFW images generated by a diffusion model. Second, CLIP (ViT-L/14) is fine-tuned with LoRA (r=16) using four losses: two inappropriate-content redirection losses (a symmetric InfoNCE pulling unsafe text embeddings toward safe image embeddings and vice versa, plus negative cosine similarity to the frozen encoder's safe embeddings) and two structure-preservation losses (cosine similarity between online and frozen encoders on safe inputs, plus a contrastive loss on safe cross-modal pairs). This ensures NSFW inputs map to safe regions while the embedding space geometry for clean content remains intact.

## Results

On the ViSU test set, Safe-CLIP achieves R@1 of 8.0 for unsafe text-to-image retrieval and 19.1 for unsafe image-to-text retrieval, compared to 2.0 and 4.5 for original CLIP, while preserving safe retrieval performance (R@1: 45.9 T-to-V, 45.3 V-to-T). On real NSFW images (Table 3), Safe-CLIP reduces the percentage of retrieved NSFW items by over 45 points for images and 30 points for text across all three sources (NudeNet, NSFW URLs, SMID). For text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion, Safe-CLIP reduces NSFW generation probability by 13.5 points on I2P prompts and 22.6 points on ViSU prompts. For image-to-text generation with LLaVA, Safe-CLIP cuts NSFW text output from 62.6% to 26.7% on NudeNet images and from 46.8% to 19.4% on NSFW URL images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Safe-CLIP substantially outperforms original CLIP [33] on unsafe retrieval: +6.0 R@1 for text-to-image (8.0 vs 2.0) and +14.6 R@1 for image-to-text (19.1 vs 4.5), while also improving safe-only retrieval (45.9 vs 36.8 T-to-V R@1). Against DataComp-1B [12], which was trained on NSFW-cleaned data, Safe-CLIP achieves a 5× improvement on unsafe text-to-image retrieval (8.0 vs 1.6 R@1) and a 3.5× improvement on unsafe image-to-text retrieval (19.1 vs 5.5 R@1), demonstrating that fine-tuning with targeted losses is far more effective than data cleaning alone. For text-to-image generation, Safe-CLIP combined with Stable Diffusion outperforms SLD-Strong [38] (Safe Latent Diffusion) on NSFW generation probability, and the two approaches are complementary—Safe-CLIP further reduces NSFW output when stacked on top of SLD. For image-to-text generation, replacing LLaVA's [25] visual encoder with Safe-CLIP reduces NSFW text output by 35.9 percentage points on NudeNet images (62.6% → 26.7%) and by 27.4 points on NSFW URL images (46.8% → 19.4%), with toxicity scores also halved across all sources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of NSFW content removal in generative models (Safe Latent Diffusion [38], concept erasure [14]) from diffusion-specific settings to the CLIP embedding space itself, which underpins a much broader range of downstream applications including retrieval, text-to-image, and image-to-text generation. It builds on CLIP's contrastive architecture [33] by introducing redirection and structure-preservation losses that push unsafe embeddings toward their safe counterparts while keeping the safe region of the space intact. The data generation pipeline repurposes Llama 2 [43] as a conditioned NSFW text generator via SFT and a DPO variant [35] with automatic (rather than human) preference ranking, paired with an NSFW-capable diffusion model to produce the ViSU quadruplet dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because CLIP serves as the text encoder in Stable Diffusion, the visual encoder in LLaVA, and a feature extractor in numerous retrieval and classification systems, making the embedding space itself safer has a multiplicative impact across the ecosystem without requiring changes to downstream models. The paper demonstrates that targeted fine-tuning on synthetic data outperforms large-scale data cleaning (DataComp-1B) for NSFW mitigation, suggesting that structural intervention in the embedding space is more effective than input filtering. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 20 NSFW categories to broader forms of bias or toxicity, whether it holds for newer CLIP variants (e.g., SigLIP, OpenCLIP), and whether adversarial prompts could bypass the learned safety invariance.
