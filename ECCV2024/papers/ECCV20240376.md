## Problem

t-SVD-based tensor recovery methods rely on fixed invertible transforms (DFT, DCT) applied along a specific tensor dimension, making them highly sensitive to slice permutation variability and non-smooth changes in tensor data (e.g., disordered image sequences, videos with rapidly changing frames). Additionally, extending t-SVD to higher-order tensors typically requires weighted sums over all mode pairs, introducing h(h−1)/2 tuning parameters with no principled way to set them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240376/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partially observed h-order tensor (e.g., a 4th-order color video tensor of size I₁×I₂×3×I₄) together with a binary support mask indicating which entries are observed, and outputs the fully reconstructed tensor. The observed entries are enforced via a linear projection operator Ψ_𝕀, while missing entries are filled by the low-rank completion. Experiments use sampling rates p ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} to control the fraction of observed entries, and evaluation is via PSNR on the reconstructed tensor.

## Method

The key insight is replacing fixed DFT/DCT transforms with learnable unitary matrices U_{k_n} that are jointly optimized with the tensor estimate, yielding a learnable tensor nuclear norm that adapts to the data's inherent structure and handles both slice reordering and non-smooth transitions. The single-objective model (TC-SL) is solved by the proposed Alternating Proximal Multiplier Method (APMM), which alternates between a tensor singular value thresholding step for Z, a Procrustes-type SVD step for each unitary matrix U_{k_n}, and a projection step for the error tensor E, with convergence to a KKT point guaranteed under boundedness conditions. For the multi-objective extension (MOTC), the paper simultaneously minimizes the learnable tensor nuclear norm over all mode pairs (k₁,k₂) using NSGA-II to find the Pareto front, eliminating the need for explicit weight parameters. The APMM sub-problems for each mode pair and the NSGA-II individual evaluations can be parallelized, making the framework scalable to higher-order tensors.

## Results

On the random-shuffling image completion benchmark (Table 3, p=0.3), MOTC achieves an average PSNR of 30.46 dB, outperforming the next-best method WSTNN (27.69 dB) by 2.77 dB and the fixed-transform TNN-DFT (22.92 dB) by 7.54 dB. On the HMDB51 color video inpainting task (Table 4, p=0.3), MOTC reaches 38.74 dB average PSNR, a 3.46 dB gain over WSTNN (35.28 dB) and a 11.03 dB gain over HTNN-DCT (27.71 dB). TC-SL alone already surpasses HTNN-DCT by 9.11 dB on video, demonstrating the benefit of learnable unitary matrices over fixed DCT transforms for non-smooth data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOTC wins on every dataset in Table 3, with the largest margin on GTF (+6.90 dB over WSTNN) and LFW (+1.34 dB over WSTNN). TC-SL also beats all baselines on GTF (32.10 vs. 26.66 for WSTNN) but is outperformed by WSTNN on LFW (31.57 vs. 34.33), showing that the multi-objective formulation is critical for face data. On the HMDB51 video task, MOTC (38.74 dB) surpasses WSTNN (35.28 dB) by 3.46 dB and TC-SL (36.82 dB) by 1.92 dB, while TC-SL itself outperforms HTNN-DCT (27.71 dB) by 9.11 dB, confirming that learnable unitary matrices are far more effective than fixed DCT for rapidly changing video content. The fixed-transform 3rd-order methods (TNN-DFT, TNN-DCT) are consistently the weakest, trailing the best higher-order methods by 5–10 dB, underscoring the importance of exploiting multi-dimensional low-rank structure. SNN and KBR (Tucker-based) are competitive on LFW but degrade sharply on CIFAR-10/100, where t-SVD-based methods with learnable norms have a clear advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the t-SVD-based tensor completion lineage (Kilmer et al. 2014; Lu et al. 2018, 2019) by replacing fixed invertible transforms (DFT, DCT) with learnable unitary matrices, generalizing the slice-permutation-variability solution of Zheng et al. (AAAI 2022) from a DFT-specific Hamiltonian circle formulation to an arbitrary-transform setting. It departs from the weighted-sum-of-nuclear-norms approach (WSTNN, Zheng et al. 2020) by formulating cross-dimensional low-rankness as a multi-objective problem solved via NSGA-II, eliminating the h(h−1)/2 weight parameters. The APMM solver combines proximal gradient steps with ADMM-style multiplier updates, building on the tensor singular value thresholding machinery already established in the t-SVD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learnable tensor nuclear norm addresses a practically important failure mode—performance collapse on shuffled or non-smooth tensor sequences—that prior t-SVD methods silently ignored, and the multi-objective formulation offers a clean, parameter-free alternative to weighted-sum heuristics for higher-order tensors. However, the optimization is non-convex (convergence is only to KKT points), the NSGA-II component is heuristic without theoretical guarantees, and scalability to very high-order or very large tensors remains untested. Open questions include whether the learned unitary matrices transfer across tasks, whether the multi-objective framework extends to other tensor analysis problems (denoising, clustering) as the authors suggest, and how the method behaves under adversarial or structured missingness patterns.
