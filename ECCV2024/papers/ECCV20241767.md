## Problem

Mask-free referring image segmentation (RIS) methods that rely solely on bounding box annotations struggle to generate high-quality pseudo labels because they either ignore the rich semantic information in the referring text or fail to fully exploit the structural cues provided by the bounding box. Existing two-stage approaches train a separate pseudo-label generator on image-only signals, while semi-supervised methods that do use text are not designed to leverage box supervision effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241767/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time the method consumes an RGB image (416×416), a natural language referring expression, and a bounding box annotation; at inference only the image and text are required. The backbone (CRIS with ResNet-101 + CLIP-initialized Transformer) produces a binary segmentation mask in [0,1]^{H×W}. The bounding box is used exclusively to decouple the structural forest and constrain the location loss during training, and the pseudo-label generator itself is parameter-free, so no additional trainable modules are introduced.

## Method

GTMS is a one-stage, parameter-free pseudo-label refinement framework that fuses two complementary signals. First, a minimum spanning tree is built over the image pixel graph (4-neighborhood, Euclidean color distance), and the bounding box decouples it into a structural forest of sub-trees via Union-find, capturing object-level structural boundaries. Second, GradCAM applied to the CLIP text and image features yields an attention map; the maximum attention score within each sub-tree is propagated to all its vertices, producing vertex weights that encode semantic relevance while avoiding the over-concentration problem of raw GradCAM. The model prediction is then element-wise multiplied by these vertex weights and further smoothed by the tree edge weights to produce the pseudo label. Training uses a combined loss: a Dice-based location loss on 1-D height/width projections of the mask versus the bounding box (to enforce coarse localization early in training) and an L1 pixel loss against the pseudo label (ramped up linearly over 10 epochs for fine-grained refinement), with no architectural changes to the CRIS backbone.

## Results

GTMS achieves 66.54%, 69.98%, and 63.41% IoU on RefCOCO Val, TestA, and TestB respectively, using only bounding box annotations (no pixel-level masks). On RefCOCO+ it reaches 57.59% (Val) and 63.46% (TestA), and on RefCOCO-G it attains 54.52% (Val) and 54.75% (Test). These results establish new SOTA among mask-free RIS methods, surpassing the previous best mask-free baselines (Two-stage [6] and Chunk [18]) by roughly 7–9 IoU points on RefCOCO, while also outperforming several fully supervised methods such as VLT, SHNet, and BRINet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among mask-free (bounding-box-supervised) methods, GTMS decisively outperforms both prior approaches: it beats Two-stage [6] by +7.71/+8.67/+7.40 IoU on RefCOCO Val/TestA/TestB, and beats Chunk [18] by +8.42/+8.75/+7.94 on the same splits, while also leading on RefCOCO+ and RefCOCO-G. Against fully supervised methods, GTMS surpasses VLT [4] (+0.89/+1.69/+0.68 on RefCOCO), SHNet [12] (+1.22/+1.42/+1.37), BRINet [10] (+5.19/+6.61/+3.84), CMPC [11] (+5.18/+5.45/+3.77), CMSA [37] (+8.22/+9.37/+8.32), STEP [2] (+6.50/+6.52/+5.44), and MattNet [38] (+10.03/+7.61/+11.71). However, GTMS still trails the strongest fully supervised baselines—CRIS [36] (−3.93/−3.20/−2.69 on RefCOCO), MaIL [22] (−3.59/−1.73/−3.51), and RefTR [20] (−4.02/−3.51/−3.16)—by roughly 2–4 IoU points, indicating that the gap to full mask supervision remains but is substantially narrowed. A controlled experiment (Table 3) swapping the backbone of Two-stage [6] to CRIS [36] shows only a +7.64% average gain, whereas GTMS's one-stage structural-semantic fusion yields an additional ~10.8% over that modified baseline, confirming the contribution is architectural rather than solely attributable to the CLIP backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTMS builds directly on CRIS (CLIP-driven referring image segmentation) as its backbone and departs from the dominant two-stage mask-free RIS paradigm (e.g., Feng et al.) by proposing a one-stage, parameter-free pseudo label generator that operates online during training. It repurposes the minimum spanning tree filter from image smoothing literature as a structural information extractor and GradCAM from model interpretability as a semantic attention mechanism, combining both in a novel way for pseudo label refinement. Unlike prior box-level mask-free segmentation methods (BoxSup, BoxInst, BBAM) that exploit only image information, GTMS explicitly leverages the natural language input unique to RIS, and unlike Chunk (Lee et al.) which models word-level semantics, it fully exploits the bounding box as a structural prior via the structural forest decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTMS demonstrates that a parameter-free, one-stage pseudo label generator combining structural and semantic cues can close most of the gap to fully supervised RIS, which is practically significant given the high cost of pixel-level annotation. The approach is architecture-agnostic (no model modifications needed) and adds no trainable parameters, making it easy to plug into existing RIS backbones. However, the method's reliance on CLIP's pre-trained semantic alignment and the specific CRIS architecture limits generalizability claims, and the modest individual ablation gains (3.1% from tree, 2.61% from GradCAM) suggest the CLIP backbone does much of the heavy lifting. Open questions include whether the structural forest decomposition provides value beyond simple spatial masking, and whether the approach transfers to other weakly supervised settings beyond RIS.
