## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DIS) require hundreds of sampling steps to guide trajectories toward measurement-consistent samples, creating a fundamental trade-off between inference speed and reconstruction quality. While consistency models (CMs) enable single-step generation, they predict x₀ directly rather than its expectation x̂₀, breaking the likelihood score approximation that most DIS methods rely on. This leaves it an open problem how to effectively incorporate CM priors into few-step inverse problem solvers.

## Input / Output

The method accepts downgraded measurements in diverse formats—low-resolution images (for 4× SR), masked images (for block inpainting), blurred images (for deblurring), or sinograms (for sparse-view CT with 23 angles)—all at 256×256 resolution. Before feeding into the network, measurements are projected into signal space via an initial reconstruction stage: pseudo-inverse for linear tasks, filtered back-projection for CT, or simple resizing for nonlinear/unknown operators. The output is a single reconstructed image, with the option to run a second refinement step (2 NFEs total) that re-noises and re-denoises the first-step result while applying a hard measurement constraint.

## Method

CoSIGN builds a three-stage pipeline over a frozen pretrained consistency model backbone. In Stage 2, a ControlNet encoder (initialized from the CM backbone, with zero convolutions at inlets/outlets) is trained with a reconstruction loss L_recon to take the initial reconstruction as a condition and guide the CM's single-step sampling toward measurement consistency—this constitutes the soft measurement constraint. In Stage 3, an optional hard measurement constraint is applied as a plug-and-play optimization: a relaxed projection (Eq. 13) for linear problems or gradient descent with momentum for nonlinear problems, explicitly enforcing A(z)≈y. Multistep sampling iterates by perturbing the previous output with lower-level noise and re-denoising through the guided CM, with the hard constraint applied at each step. The key design choice is freezing the CM backbone so that single-step generation quality is inherited while the ControlNet alone learns to inject measurement information, avoiding the slow convergence of training a full conditional consistency model from scratch.

## Results

On LSUN Bedroom, CoSIGN achieves FID of 38.64 (inpainting), 40.84 (4× SR), and 38.60 (deblurring) at just 2 NFEs, outperforming all low-NFE baselines and matching or exceeding methods requiring ~1000 NFEs (e.g., DPS: 43.54/46.15/42.85 FID). At 1 NFE, CoSIGN still achieves FID of 39.89/41.00/40.48, surpassing SwinIR (75.45/49.98/84.12) by a large margin. On AAPM LDCT sparse-view CT (23 angles), CoSIGN reaches 34.26 dB PSNR and 0.866 SSIM at 2 NFEs, beating FBP-UNet (31.47 dB, 0.874 SSIM at 1 NFE) on PSNR and MCG (29.48 dB, 0.847 SSIM at 1000 NFEs) on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSIGN decisively outperforms all low-NFE baselines on FID across all three natural image tasks at both 1 and 2 NFEs. Against SwinIR (1 NFE), CoSIGN reduces FID by 35–44 points (e.g., 39.89 vs 75.45 on inpainting), though SwinIR retains a slight LPIPS edge on SR (0.200 vs 0.214). Versus CDDB (2 NFE), CoSIGN wins on FID by 6.6 (inpainting) and 13.4 (SR) points, while CDDB achieves a marginally lower LPIPS on inpainting (0.126 vs 0.137), which the authors attribute to LPIPS penalizing the high-fidelity details CoSIGN generates. Compared to high-NFE methods (DPS at 1000 NFEs, I²SB at 999 NFEs), CoSIGN at 2 NFEs matches or exceeds their FID on every task (e.g., 38.64 vs 43.54 inpainting, 40.84 vs 40.40 SR). On CT reconstruction, CoSIGN (2 NFE) surpasses MCG (1000 NFE) by 4.8 dB PSNR and 0.019 SSIM, and beats FBP-UNet (1 NFE) by 2.8 dB PSNR, though FBP-UNet retains a small SSIM advantage (0.874 vs 0.866).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three established components into a new few-step inverse problem solver: a pretrained Consistency Model (CM) as a frozen data prior, a ControlNet encoder for soft measurement conditioning, and a hard measurement constraint (borrowed from DDNM) for multistep refinement. It departs from prior diffusion-based inverse solvers (DPS, DDRM, MCG) that require hundreds of NFEs by exploiting the CM's ability to map any noisy point directly to a clean sample in one step. It also differs from supervised bridge methods (I²SB, CDDB) that train a full generative model from scratch per task, since CoSIGN inherits the CM backbone's generalization and only trains the lightweight ControlNet. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that standard gradient-based DIS guidance fails with CM because CM predicts x₀ directly rather than E[x₀|xₜ], motivating the ControlNet-based conditioning instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By compressing the inference cost from ~1000 NFEs to 1–2 NFEs (under 100 ms per image), CoSIGN makes diffusion-quality inverse problem solving viable for real-time and high-dimensional settings such as video restoration and 3D imaging, which were previously impractical. The frozen CM backbone confers notable out-of-domain robustness (to unseen projection angles and noise levels) that supervised baselines lack, suggesting a path toward more general-purpose inverse solvers. Open questions include whether the CM backbone's limited multistep refinement (no gain beyond 2 steps) can be addressed, whether the approach extends to 3D/video without task-specific ControlNet retraining, and how well it scales to higher-resolution or more complex forward operators.
