## Problem

In federated learning with heterogeneous clients, each client may only observe a subset of the global label space (openset) with additional label noise, making the noise transition matrix locally unidentifiable. Existing noisy-label solutions such as loss correction require the full label space to estimate the transition matrix correctly, and directly applying them locally in FL fails because the missing classes distort the estimated matrix. The paper formalizes this 'local openset noisy label' problem and shows that centralized noisy-label methods cannot be naively transferred to the federated setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240891/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds local RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for Clothing-1M) with noisy labels drawn from a subset of the global K-class label space. The server broadcasts a DP-protected label distribution (a K-dimensional probability vector) that each client uses to sample contrastive labels during local training. The output is a single global ResNet classifier (ResNet-18 for CIFAR, ResNet-50 ImageNet-pretrained for Clothing-1M) that predicts class labels; the entire procedure is single-stage with no separate noise-estimation or pre-training phase.

## Method

FedDPCont combines two components executed every communication round. First, a privacy-preserving label communication protocol: the server generates a symmetric DP noise transition matrix T_DP parameterized by ε, each client flips its noisy labels through T_DP and sends only the flipped labels to the server, which aggregates them and inverts T_DP to recover the global noisy label distribution — this is proven to be ε-labelDP (Theorem 1). Second, each client minimizes a contrastive (peer-loss-style) objective ℓ_PL = ℓ(f(x), ỹ) − ℓ(f(x), y′) where y′ is sampled from the globally recovered distribution rather than the local one; this global sampling is critical because local openset noise breaks the peer-loss invariance property (the local noise ratio becomes degenerate). The paper proves (Theorem 2) that the FedAvg-aggregated gradient update under ℓ_PL equals the corresponding centralized gradient update, establishing that the contrastive mechanism exactly compensates for the openset noise at the expectation level.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with symmetric noise, FedDPCont achieves 84.77% accuracy at noise ratio 0.2, 75.75% at 0.4, 55.50% at 0.6, and 24.64% at 0.8, consistently outperforming all 13 baselines. Under random noise, it reaches 82.15% (0.2), 72.69% (0.4), 54.06% (0.6), and 27.55% (0.8). On real-world datasets, FedDPCont attains 81.86% on CIFAR-N (Aggregate), 52.60% on CIFAR-100-N (Fine), and 70.88% on Clothing-1M, again leading all competitors. The method is also ~5–7× faster than DivideMix (e.g., 4.17 hr vs. 22.33 hr on CIFAR-10 symmetric 0.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDPCont outperforms all 13 baselines across every noise ratio and noise type on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, CIFAR-N, and Clothing-1M. The largest margins are against FedNoRo and RHFL, which degrade severely under openset noise (e.g., FedDPCont 84.77% vs. FedNoRo 57.49% and RHFL 53.49% at symmetric 0.2 on CIFAR-10). Among the stronger baselines, FedDPCont beats Co-teaching by ~5–15 points (e.g., 75.75% vs. 70.60% at symmetric 0.4) and LC by ~5–12 points (84.77% vs. 79.14% at symmetric 0.2). FedCorr and FedRN, which are purpose-built for FL with noisy labels, trail FedDPCont by 10–15 points in most settings, confirming that multi-stage noise-estimation approaches are less effective under openset conditions. Notably, directly applying centralized noisy-label methods (LC, Co-teaching, T-revision) locally in FL is no better than plain FedAvg, validating the paper's core claim that openset noise breaks standard loss-correction assumptions. In the appendix comparison with DivideMix, FedDPCont achieves higher accuracy (e.g., 84.77% vs. 79.28% on CIFAR-10 symmetric 0.2) while training ~5× faster (4.17 hr vs. 22.33 hr).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated learning (building on FedAvg's model-averaging protocol), noisy label learning (adapting the peer loss / contrastive label mechanism from Liu & Guo 2020), and label differential privacy (adopting the T_DP random-response mechanism from Ghazi et al. 2021). Its key conceptual departure is showing that the standard loss-correction paradigm—which underpins most centralized noisy-label methods—fundamentally breaks when each client observes only a subset of the global label space (the 'openset' condition), because the locally estimated transition matrix is provably wrong. Rather than the multi-stage estimation-then-correction pipeline used by FedCorr, FedNoRo, and RHFL, FedDPCont is single-stage: it communicates a DP-protected global label distribution and uses it as a contrastive negative in the local loss, eliminating the need for per-client noise estimation entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world FL deployments (medical imaging across hospitals, regional virus-variant tracking) naturally produce clients with disjoint or partially overlapping label spaces and imperfect annotations, yet most prior FL-noise methods assume identical label spaces. The single-stage, noise-estimation-free design with a provable DP guarantee makes the approach more deployable than multi-stage competitors. Limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-scale datasets, a relatively high DP budget (ε ≈ 0.81–3.21) to maintain performance, and dependence on accurate global label-distribution estimation that degrades when per-class sample counts are small. Open questions raised by the authors include interaction with personalized FL and validation on real mobile-device FL partitions.
