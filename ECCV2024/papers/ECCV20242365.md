## Problem

Neural Radiance Fields have demonstrated strong 3D scene understanding capabilities, yet no scalable self-supervised pretraining strategy existed specifically for NeRFs. Prior 3D representation learning methods (point clouds, voxels, meshes) require careful architectural adaptation due to irregular data structures and uneven information density, while NeRF's dense volumetric grid—similar in regularity to 2D images—had not been exploited for masked autoencoding. The paper asks whether standard Transformer-based masked autoencoders can be directly applied to NeRF's radiance and density grid to learn powerful 3D representations at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242365/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB images (1.8M+ images across 3,600+ scenes from Front3D, HM3D, and Hypersim), trains Instant-NGP NeRFs on each scene, then queries the trained NeRF from all training camera directions to extract a 4D radiance and density grid of shape H×W×D×4 at 0.01m voxel spacing within the traceable scene volume. During pretraining, 75% of 4×4×4 patches are randomly masked and the model reconstructs the missing radiance and opacity values. When fine-tuned, the frozen encoder's multi-resolution feature pyramid feeds task-specific decoders that output 3D oriented bounding boxes, per-voxel semantic class labels, or upsampled voxel grids (e.g., 160³ → 384³).

## Method

NeRF-MAE extracts an explicit 4D radiance and density grid from each trained Instant-NGP NeRF by averaging the NeRF's color and density predictions over all N training camera viewing directions at every grid point, canonicalizing scenes across domains. A standard 3D Swin Transformer (embedding dim 96, depths [2,2,18,2], heads [3,6,12,24]) encodes the unmasked patches, requiring no architectural modifications beyond replacing 2D operations with 3D equivalents, because the regular volumetric grid provides uniform information density analogous to 2D images. A U-Net-style decoder with transposed 3D convolutions and residual skip connections from each encoder stage reconstructs the masked patches. The reconstruction loss is opacity-aware: radiance L2 is computed only over occupied voxels (where α exceeds a threshold derived from the 0.01m voxel spacing), while opacity L2 is computed over all masked voxels, jointly encouraging geometrically faithful reconstruction. The model is pretrained fully self-supervised on 1.8M+ images from four diverse sources, then the encoder is transferred to downstream 3D tasks with task-specific lightweight decoders.

## Results

On 3D OBB prediction, NeRF-MAE (pretrained on Front3D + HM3D + Hypersim with augmentations) achieves 63.0% AP@50 and 85.3% AP@25 on Front3D, and 17.0% AP@50 and 57.1% AP@25 on ScanNet (unseen during pretraining), representing absolute improvements of +21.5% AP@50 over NeRF-RPN on Front3D and +3.0% AP@50 on ScanNet. On semantic voxel labelling (Front3D), NeRF-MAE reaches 45.0% mAcc and 34.5% mIOU, a +11.2% mAcc gain over NeRF-RPN. For voxel super-resolution, NeRF-MAE achieves 17.27 dB PSNR at 256³ resolution versus 16.25 dB for NeRF-RPN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRF-RPN [21] (the primary NeRF-based detection baseline), NeRF-MAE wins decisively on all metrics: +21.5% AP@50 on Front3D (63.0% vs 41.5%) and +3.0% AP@50 on ScanNet (17.0% vs 14.0%), with the cross-dataset transfer to ScanNet (never seen during pretraining) being particularly notable. Against ImVoxelNet [55], a multi-view 3D detection method, NeRF-MAE trails on Front3D AP@50 (63.0% vs 66.4%) but substantially outperforms it on ScanNet AP@50 (17.0% vs 9.8%), suggesting NeRF-MAE's volumetric representation generalizes better to unseen real-world scenes. In the 3D representation learning comparison (Table 3), NeRF-MAE dominates all point-cloud-based pretraining baselines: on ScanNet 3D OBB it achieves 39.5% Recall@50 (pretrained) versus 11.7% for DepthContrast [91] and 12.2% for Scene Context [20]; on Front3D semantic segmentation it reaches 34.5% mIOU versus 17.3% for Point-MAE [45] and 21.1% for Point-M2AE [89]. For semantic voxel labelling, NeRF-MAE improves mAcc by +11.2% (45.0% vs 33.8%) and mIOU by +9.6% (34.5% vs 24.9%) over NeRF-RPN on Front3D, and by +10.4% mAcc on HM3D (26.4% vs 16.0%). The paper also shows that with only 50% of labelled scenes (61 scenes), NeRF-MAE achieves 42% AP@50 on Front3D, matching the full-data (122 scenes) NeRF-RPN baseline of 41% AP@50, demonstrating superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D masked autoencoder paradigm (MAE) to 3D volumetric grids extracted from trained NeRFs, positioning NeRF's radiance and density grid as a natural 3D analogue to 2D images for masked reconstruction. It departs from prior 3D self-supervised pretraining work (Point-MAE, Point-M2AE, Voxel-MAE) that operates on irregular point clouds or sparse voxels requiring architectural adaptations like DGCNN, by leveraging NeRF's dense regular grid to use a standard 3D Swin Transformer without modification. The approach also builds on NeRF-RPN's 3D Swin Transformer formulation and grid extraction procedure, but disentangles representation learning from NeRF training, enabling multi-source pretraining that prior generalizable NeRF methods did not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale self-supervised pretraining recipe specifically for NeRFs, offering a practical pathway to improve downstream 3D understanding (detection, segmentation, super-resolution) without expensive 3D labels, using only posed RGB images. The substantial data-efficiency gain (achieving comparable performance with less than half the labeled data of the SOTA baseline) is practically significant for settings where 3D annotations are scarce. Open questions include whether the approach scales to large outdoor scenes, whether the NeRF training cost for pretraining (6 days on 8 A100s for Front3D alone) limits broader adoption, and whether the opacity-aware loss generalizes to dynamic or multi-object NeRF variants.
