## Problem

Existing differentiable renderers for inferring unsigned distance functions (UDFs) from multi-view images rely on handcrafted equations that are biased at ray-surface intersections, sensitive to unsigned distance outliers, and not scalable to large scenes. These limitations cause large rendering errors even when ground truth UDFs are used as input, preventing accurate geometry recovery for open surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241313/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images as input and produces a neural UDF whose zero-level set is extracted as a 3D mesh via marching cubes. During pre-training, the volume rendering prior is learned from ground truth meshes (one car from ShapeNet, one garment from DeepFashion3D) by rendering 100 depth images at 600×600 resolution per shape. During inference, 128 hierarchical samples are cast per ray, and the frozen prior consumes 30 neighboring unsigned distances plus sampling intervals (not absolute coordinates) to compute opaque densities for alpha blending.

## Method

The core contribution is replacing handcrafted UDF-to-weight equations with a learned 6-layer MLP (the volume rendering prior) that maps a sliding window of M=30 neighboring unsigned distances and their sampling intervals to opaque densities for alpha blending. The prior is pre-trained in a data-driven manner by minimizing L2 depth rendering error between volume-rendered depth images (using ground truth UDFs) and ground truth depth maps from 100 viewpoints per shape, requiring only two training shapes. At test time, the prior's parameters are frozen and used as a fixed differentiable renderer: a separate 8-layer UDF network and 4-layer color network are jointly optimized by minimizing RGB rendering loss plus an Eikonal regularization term (λ=0.1). The key insight is that a larger receptive field (30 neighbors vs. the 2 used by handcrafted methods) combined with data-driven learning makes the renderer 3D-aware, unbiased at intersections, robust to distance outliers, and generalizable across shape categories without fine-tuning.

## Results

On the DF3D dataset, the method achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.71×10⁻³, outperforming all UDF-based baselines (NeUDF 2.01, NeuralUDF 2.15, NeAT 2.10) and the SDF-based NeuS (4.36). On DTU, it reaches 0.85 CD, marginally beating NeuS (0.87) and NeAT (0.88) while substantially outperforming NeUDF (1.58) and NeuralUDF (1.07). On the large-scale Replica indoor scenes, the method achieves 0.04 CD, 0.90 Normal Consistency, and 0.80 F-score, surpassing NeuS (0.07 CD, 0.88 N.C., 0.69 F-score) and all UDF methods by wide margins. In the ShapeNet rendering-quality evaluation (Table 1), the learned prior produces a depth L1 error of 0.41±0.19, lower than even NeuS-SDF (0.97±0.67) rendered with ground-truth SDFs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against NeuS [43] (SDF-based), it achieves lower CD on all three datasets (1.71 vs 4.36 on DF3D, 0.85 vs 0.87 on DTU, 0.04 vs 0.07 on Replica) and higher F-score (0.80 vs 0.69), demonstrating that the learned UDF prior surpasses even the SDF rendering pipeline. Against NeUDF [26], the gains are most dramatic on DTU (0.85 vs 1.58 CD) and Replica (0.04 vs 0.28 CD, 0.80 vs 0.31 F-score), where NeUDF's handcrafted inverse-proportional weight function struggles with boundary artifacts. Against NeuralUDF [27], the method wins on all metrics (e.g., 1.71 vs 2.15 CD on DF3D, 0.80 vs 0.53 F-score on Replica), and notably NeuralUDF's reported numbers exclude its patch-loss fine-tuning step for fair comparison. Against NeAT [32], which uses additional mask supervision, the method still leads on every metric (0.85 vs 0.88 CD on DTU, 0.90 vs 0.75 N.C. on Replica). Against COLMAP [37], the classical SfM baseline, the method achieves substantially lower CD (1.71 vs 3.10 on DF3D, 0.04 vs 0.23 on Replica) and much higher normal consistency (0.90 vs 0.46), confirming the advantage of learned implicit representations over point-cloud-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the volume rendering framework established by NeuS [43] for SDF reconstruction to the UDF setting, where prior methods (NeUDF, NeuralUDF, NeAT, 2S-UDF) rely on handcrafted equations to map unsigned distances to alpha-blending weights. The key departure is replacing these fixed equations with a small 6-layer MLP trained in a data-driven manner to learn the UDF-to-density mapping, using the hierarchical sampling strategy from NeUDF [26] and mesh extraction from MeshUDF [16]. Conceptually, the paper reframes the differentiable renderer as a learnable prior rather than a designed function, drawing on the broader trend of learned neural rendering frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The learned volume rendering prior addresses a persistent weakness in UDF-based reconstruction—boundary artifacts and sensitivity to distance outliers—that handcrafted renderers cannot resolve, yielding state-of-the-art accuracy on open surfaces (clothing, thin structures) where SDF methods fundamentally fail. The striking data efficiency (only 2 training shapes suffice) suggests the UDF-to-density mapping is a low-complexity function, opening the door to even more compact or task-specific priors. Limitations include dependence on known camera poses, the volume rendering paradigm's inherent cost for large scenes, and the fact that the prior is frozen at test time, which may limit adaptability to out-of-distribution geometry. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, sparse-view settings, or other implicit representations beyond UDFs/SDFs.
