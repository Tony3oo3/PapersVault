## Problem

Despite ideal synchronization and calibration, a physical baseline distance between LiDAR and RGB cameras causes systematic misalignment in projected LiDAR depthmaps, where background scans incorrectly overlay foreground objects like cars and pedestrians. The KITTI dataset addresses this with stereo cameras as a heuristic, but most major AV datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, DDAD, KITTI-360) lack stereo imagery, leaving these artifacts unattended and degrading downstream depth estimation and 3D detection performance.

## Input / Output

RePLAy requires only the LiDAR point cloud, the LiDAR-to-camera extrinsic (calibration) matrix, and the RGB camera intrinsic matrix—no RGB images are needed, making it robust to temporal asynchronization. It outputs a cleaned LiDAR-projected depthmap with occluded pixels removed, plus the explicit set of occluded pixel coordinates. The method is entirely parameter-free and analytical, with no learned components or tunable thresholds beyond a relaxation constant set to −∞ in practice.

## Method

RePLAy constructs a binocular vision system by placing a virtual camera at the LiDAR origin (with auto-calibrated origin to guarantee artifact-free projection per Lemma 1) and treating the RGB camera as the second view. The relative pose is decomposed into pure rotation (which induces a homography and causes no occlusion, Lemma 2) and pure translation (which causes occlusion strictly along the epipolar line direction, Lemma 3). For each LiDAR-projected pixel, the method analytically checks inspection pixels along the reverse epipolar line direction using a closed-form occlusion function g(·) that compares the translation-induced displacements of the query pixel and candidate occluders. A pixel is marked occluded if any inspection pixel 'surpasses' it in the RGB view (Eq. 17), with nearest-neighbor interpolation over the densified virtual LiDAR depthmap handling sparsity. This yields a parameter-free, non-learning analytical solution that generalizes across all LiDAR-RGB sensor configurations without requiring stereo sensors.

## Results

On depthmap quality (Table 2, Semi-Dense GT, All region), RePLAy achieves δ₀.₅=0.985, δ₁=0.994, δ₂=0.998, and RMS=1.241, outperforming both Raw (δ₀.₅=0.973, RMS=1.935) and Modified Half-Occ (δ₀.₅=0.983, RMS=1.347). For MonoDE (Table 6), ZoeDepth trained with RePLAy depthmaps improves δ₁ from 0.869 to 0.927 on KITTI Eigen (All), from 0.971 to 0.981 on nuScenes (Foreground), and from 0.941 to 0.962 on Waymo (Foreground). For Mono3DOD (Table 8), CaDDN improves Easy AP₃D from 21.60 to 22.88 and MonoDTR from 24.50 to 26.15 when trained with RePLAy-cleaned depthmaps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RePLAy is compared against three depthmap baselines: Raw (standard half-occlusion/depth buffering), Modified Half-Occ (back-projecting densified virtual LiDAR depthmap to filter RGB pixels), and Semi-Dense (KITTI's stereo-based semi-global matching with 11-frame temporal fusion). On depthmap quality (Table 2), RePLAy outperforms both Raw and Mod. Half-Occ across all metrics; for example, on Semi-Dense GT it reduces RMS from 1.935 (Raw) and 1.347 (Mod. Half-Occ) to 1.241, and improves δ₀.₅ from 0.973/0.983 to 0.985. Notably, RePLAy also surpasses the stereo-based Semi-Dense GT in background regions (δ₀.₅: 0.982 vs. 0.978), suggesting the stereo pipeline aggressively removes valid background pixels near foreground boundaries. For Mono3DOD (Table 8), both CaDDN and MonoDTR show consistent improvements when retrained with RePLAy depthmaps: CaDDN gains +1.28 AP on Easy (22.88 vs. 21.60) and MonoDTR gains +1.65 AP on Easy (26.15 vs. 24.50), with the largest gains in the Easy category where closer objects receive more LiDAR points and thus benefit most from artifact removal. The method is parameter-free and requires only the LiDAR-to-camera extrinsic matrix, making it applicable to all major AV datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, DDAD, KITTI-360, ApolloScape, Argoverse) that lack stereo sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical epipolar geometry and half-occlusion detection from stereo vision to solve a different problem: removing projective artifacts from LiDAR-projected depthmaps in the absence of stereo cameras. The KITTI dataset's semi-dense depthmap pipeline [57] addresses the same artifact but requires stereo sensors and multi-frame temporal fusion, making it inapplicable to nuScenes, Waymo, DDAD, and KITTI-360. RePLAy's key conceptual move is to formalize the artifact as epipolar occlusion in a virtual binocular system (virtual LiDAR camera + RGB camera), then derive an analytical occlusion test (Eq. 17) that requires only the LiDAR-camera extrinsic matrix. The auto-calibration step, which re-estimates the LiDAR point cloud origin to satisfy a no-overlap lemma, is a practical addition not present in prior stereo occlusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Projective artifacts persist unattended in most major AV datasets (nuScenes, Waymo, DDAD, KITTI-360), silently degrading downstream monocular depth estimation and 3D object detection. By providing a parameter-free, non-learning solution and releasing cleaned depthmaps for five datasets, this work removes a practical barrier for the community. The main limitation is the reliance on nearest-neighbor interpolation for densifying the sparse virtual LiDAR depthmap; the authors note that depth completion could further improve accuracy. An open question is whether the analytical occlusion test remains robust under significant temporal asynchronization or dynamic scene changes beyond the static calibration assumption.
