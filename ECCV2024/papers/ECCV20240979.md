## Problem

The functional maps framework for shape correspondence is bounded by the time complexity of sparse eigendecompositions, making it infeasible on meshes with millions of vertices. Existing scalable alternatives, such as Magnet et al.'s sparse sampling (SFM), do not mitigate the adverse effect of small disconnected components and high-frequency local details on the alignment of Laplacian spectra, leading to significant accuracy drops under non-isometric conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240979/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts two arbitrary triangular meshes (source and target) that may contain millions of vertices, including degenerate geometry, non-orientable surfaces, and multiple connected components. It outputs a full point-to-point correspondence between all vertices of the two meshes, recovered via nearest-neighbor search in the spectral embedding space of extended Laplacian eigenfunctions. The intermediate functional map is a compact k×k matrix operating on low-resolution eigenspaces (~3k vertices), which is then lifted to the original resolution through a linear projection map.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, an intrinsic Delaunay remeshing algorithm computes a geodesic Voronoi decomposition via front propagation with a binary max-heap (achieving O(|V|log|V|log(s)) complexity for geodesic FPS), then enforces a novel Flat Union Property (FUP) to guarantee the dual is a proper triangulation, yielding a low-resolution mesh that preserves the original metric. Second, any standard functional maps solver (e.g., ZoomOut or FMaps with product preservation) is applied to the low-resolution eigenspaces to estimate the functional map C. Third, each vertex of the original mesh is projected onto the closest surface point of the low-resolution mesh, constructing a linear map U that extends scalar functions; the correspondence is then recovered by applying C to the extended bases and performing nearest-neighbor search in the full-resolution spectral space. This remeshing step also naturally removes small disconnected components that would otherwise corrupt spectral alignment.

## Results

On the SHREC19 challenge pairs, ReMatching with ZoomOut as backend achieves an average geodesic error (AGE) of 5.87×10⁻² and AUC of 88.82%, substantially outperforming the full-resolution ZoomOut (29.36, 52.02%) and FMaps (28.31, 56.15%) baselines. Under the idealized setting with a ground-truth functional map for initialization, ReMatching (ZoomOut) reaches AGE 2.40 and AUC 95.12%, matching the performance of SFM with ground truth (2.46, 95.22%). On the non-isometric BadTOSCA dataset, SFM suffers a ~50% performance drop while ReMatching remains stable, and on very high-resolution scans (~750k and ~3.5M vertices), the full pipeline completes in approximately 2 minutes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary scalable baseline SFM [35], ReMatching (ZoomOut) wins decisively on SHREC19 with AGE 5.87 vs. 16.84 and AUC 88.82% vs. 70.19%, but loses on the isometric TOSCA dataset where SFM's spectrum-preserving sampling excels; on the non-isometric BadTOSCA variant, SFM degrades by ~50% while ReMatching remains stable. With ground-truth initialization, the two methods are essentially tied (AGE 2.40 vs. 2.46). Against full-resolution FMaps [46] and ZoomOut [38], ReMatching improves AGE by roughly 5× (5.87 vs. 28.31 and 29.36 respectively), confirming that the low-resolution remeshing step is the key enabler. Among alternative remeshing strategies, isotropic remeshing (IRM [21]) is the closest competitor (AGE 8.30) but still trails ReMatching, while anisotropic remeshing (ARM [44]) fails catastrophically (AGE 41.59) due to producing skew triangles and singular Laplacian matrices. In terms of execution time, ReMatching's remeshing step is orders of magnitude faster than the iterative decimation approaches SMD [28] and IEM [31], which are unsuitable for meshes with millions of vertices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the functional maps framework (Ovsjanikov et al. 2012) to handle meshes with millions of vertices by introducing a low-resolution intrinsic Delaunay remeshing step that preserves the original metric and topology. It positions itself as the first alternative to Magnet et al.'s scalable functional maps (SFM), which relies on extreme sparse sampling and cannot mitigate the adverse effects of small disconnected components on spectral alignment. The remeshing algorithm draws on Peyré et al.'s front propagation for geodesic Voronoi diagrams but introduces a novel Flat Union Property (FUP) that makes verification of a proper intrinsic Delaunay triangulation tractable, and a max-heap-accelerated farthest point sampling that avoids the O(|V|·s) bottleneck. The pipeline is backend-agnostic, demonstrated with both ZoomOut and product-preservation FMaps, and contrasts with decimation-based approaches (SMD, IEM) that are too slow for million-vertex meshes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, general-purpose pipeline that makes the functional maps framework applicable to very dense 3D scans (up to 3.5M vertices) in minutes, addressing a long-standing scalability bottleneck in spectral shape analysis. The observation that removing small disconnected components (e.g., eyes) before spectral alignment dramatically improves correspondence quality—while the naive full-resolution or SFM approaches fail—highlights a previously underappreciated topological pitfall in Laplacian eigenfunction alignment. Open questions include establishing a direct algebraic relationship between the low-resolution correspondence and the extended high-resolution one (to avoid the nearest-neighbor bottleneck at scale), exploring non-Laplacian backends, and whether the remeshing paradigm generalizes beyond functional maps to other correspondence formulations.
