## Problem

Concept inhibition methods (ESD, AC, UCE, SA) modify diffusion model weights to erase target concepts, and their evaluations show reduced reproduction under standard single-prompt inference. However, no prior work examined whether the compositional property of diffusion models—where multiple prompts are combined in a single generation—could be exploited to reconstruct the erased concept. This paper demonstrates that the local nature of inhibition modifications leaves the concept recoverable through arithmetic operations on other, uninhibited concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242359/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacks take an inhibited diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion 1.4 with a concept erased) and multiple text prompts constructed by the attacker: a combination prompt (e.g., 'a cake in the shape of zebra'), a detour prompt (e.g., 'a cake'), and optionally the user's original prompt. These are fed simultaneously into the model's compositional inference pipeline, which computes a weighted sum of conditional guidance terms. The output is a generated image that contains the supposedly erased concept, with no optimization, no access to model weights, and no modification of the model itself—only additional forward passes of the U-Net for each extra concept.

## Method

The core insight is that conditional guidance g(c) is approximately linear in CLIP semantic space, so g(c_t + c_d) − g(c_d) ≈ g*(c_t) for a sufficiently distant detour concept c_d. Since inhibition methods (Eq. 3) only constrain the loss at the target concept c_t, the degree of modification λ(c) decays with distance from c_t (Hypothesis H1), meaning g at distant points retains most of its original value. The paper formalizes this with four propositions (P1–P4) showing that linear combinations of inhibited guidance vectors at offset points converge to the original uninhibited guidance. Concretely, the ARC attacks (O1–O3 for objects, N1–N3 for nudity) implement these combinations as compositional inference calls with positive and negative guidance directions, requiring only black-box access to multi-prompt generation and no optimization or weight manipulation.

## Results

On nudity inhibition (95 I2P prompts, NudeNet detection), ARC attacks N1–N3 on ESD-u, UCE, and SA models significantly increase nudity category detection rates compared to standard inference on the inhibited models, with some attacked configurations exceeding the original unmodified SD baseline. On object inhibition (13 concepts from Imagenette/ImageNet plus R2-D2 and Snoopy, measured via NR@p using CLIP Score), attacks O1–O3 produce significantly higher reproduction rates across all percentiles for AC-100, AC-200, ESD-u, and UCE models. The effect is strongest against AC-100 (default 100 iterations), while AC-200 shows reduced but still substantial vulnerability. For ESD-u and UCE, which achieve higher baseline inhibition, the attacks still increase reproduction rates 'manyfold,' sometimes generating multiple images with the target concept where standard inference produced zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates ARC attacks against four concept inhibition methods: ESD [7], UCE [8], Selective Amnesia [SA] [10], and Ablating Concepts [AC] [16]. Against ESD-u, all three nudity attacks (N1–N3) and all three object attacks (O1–O3) significantly increase concept reproduction, with some attacked generations surpassing the original unmodified SD model in nudity detection rates. Against UCE, which uses a closed-form weight edit and achieves strong inhibition, the attacks still increase reproduction rates manyfold, sometimes from zero to multiple images containing the target concept. Against SA (continual-learning-based forgetting), the attacks similarly restore nudity generation capability. Against AC, the default 100-iteration model (AC-100) is most vulnerable with large NR@p increases, while the stronger 200-iteration variant (AC-200) shows reduced but still significant vulnerability. The paper notes that ESD-u and UCE's higher inhibition comes at the cost of reduced quality and variation in non-target concept generation, suggesting a trade-off that the attacks exploit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the compositional inference framework demonstrated by Brack et al. (2022), Liu et al. (2022), and Schramowski et al. (2023), which showed that conditional guidance in diffusion models is approximately linear in semantic space. It departs from prior red-teaming work on concept inhibition (Chin et al. 2023, Tsai et al. 2024), which exploited insufficient generalization near the target concept via prompt optimization, by instead exploiting the compositional property to use inputs *far* from the target where inhibition is weakest. The paper is the first to formalize how the linearity of conditional guidance makes all local concept-inhibition methods (ESD, AC, UCE, SA) structurally vulnerable to arithmetic reconstruction of erased concept vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that every current concept-inhibition method can be circumvented with a simple, optimization-free compositional inference attack fundamentally challenges the assumption that local weight modifications effectively erase information from a model. Practically, this means inhibited models deployed in high-risk settings must still be paired with post-hoc safety checkers, and future inhibition methods must modify the model globally rather than locally to be robust. Open questions include whether global modification approaches can preserve generation quality for unrelated concepts, and whether the linearity assumption (H1) holds more broadly across architectures and training regimes.
