## Problem

Prior category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) methods like POMNet and CapeFormer rely on explicit similarity matching heads, two-stage refinement frameworks, and auxiliary heatmap supervision, making them complex and computationally expensive. The paper observes that transformer self-attention already performs strong implicit similarity matching, rendering these explicit matching modules redundant or even suboptimal, and asks whether a streamlined one-stage architecture can outperform them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240617/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a support image with annotated keypoint locations (1 or 5 exemplars) and a query image of the same category, both processed through a shared backbone (ResNet-50, ViT-B, Swin-S, or DINOv2-pretrained ViT-S/ViT-B). Support keypoints are initialized as local feature tokens extracted via Gaussian kernels around ground-truth locations, augmented with keypoint identifiers; query features are tokenized with positional embeddings. The output is a set of 2D keypoint coordinates directly regressed by a single MLP head, with no intermediate similarity maps or iterative offset refinement.

## Method

SCAPE replaces the explicit matching pipeline with a one-stage architecture of pure self-attention layers and an MLP coordinate regression head, supervised solely by L1 loss on 2D coordinates. Two modules enhance attention quality: (1) a Global Keypoint Feature Perceptor (GKP) uses cross-attention to let support keypoint tokens attend to the full support image, injecting global semantic context that helps distinguish symmetric keypoints; (2) a Keypoint Attention Refiner (KAR) applies multiple learned attention filters (ReLU + MLP) to the keypoint-to-keypoint attention sub-matrix, weighted by a per-keypoint assigner, to denoise inter-keypoint correlations and enable inference from visible to occluded keypoints. The architecture uses unshared Q/K projections for keypoint tokens versus query image tokens, and the full model comprises 2 GKP cross-attention blocks followed by 4 self-attention interactor blocks. This design achieves +2.2 and +1.3 PCK over CapeFormer under 1-shot and 5-shot settings with ResNet-50, while using only 51% of the non-backbone parameters and running faster.

## Results

On the MP-100 dataset with ResNet-50 backbone, SCAPE achieves a mean PCK of 87.55 under the 1-shot setting and 90.66 under the 5-shot setting, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art CapeFormer by +2.2 and +1.3 PCK respectively. With a DINOv2-pretrained ViT-B backbone, SCAPE further reaches 91.95 and 93.98 PCK (1-shot and 5-shot), surpassing CapeFormer with the same backbone by +4.4 and +3.4 PCK. SCAPE also uses only 51% of CapeFormer's non-backbone parameters and runs at 29.43 FPS versus CapeFormer's 26.09 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAPE consistently outperforms all five baselines across both 1-shot and 5-shot settings on MP-100. Against the closest competitor CapeFormer [29], SCAPE gains +2.2 PCK (1-shot) and +1.3 PCK (5-shot) with ResNet-50, and the gap widens to +4.4 and +3.4 PCK when both use a DINOv2-pretrained ViT-B backbone, indicating SCAPE's simpler one-stage architecture benefits more from strong pretrained features. Against POMNet [34], the original CAPE baseline, SCAPE improves by +7.85 PCK (1-shot) and +9.95 PCK (5-shot). The few-shot learning baselines (ProtoNet, MAML, Fine-tune) are substantially outperformed, with SCAPE exceeding them by over 20 PCK in all settings. In terms of efficiency, SCAPE uses only 3.88M additional parameters (vs. CapeFormer's 7.63M) and achieves 29.43 FPS vs. CapeFormer's 26.09 FPS, while the lighter Lite-SCAPE variant (1.95M params) still beats CapeFormer by +0.8 PCK at 36.89 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAPE builds directly on the CAPE task introduced by POMNet (Xu et al., ECCV 2022) and the two-stage refinement framework of CapeFormer (Shi et al., CVPR 2023), both of which treat keypoint localization as an explicit similarity-matching problem requiring dedicated matching heads and, in CapeFormer's case, a second-stage offset refinement. The key conceptual departure is the argument that transformer self-attention already performs implicit similarity matching well enough to render explicit matching heads redundant, collapsing the pipeline into a one-stage architecture with direct MLP coordinate regression. Additionally, SCAPE imports the keypoint-correlation modeling paradigm from category-specific pose estimation (TokenPose, ContextPose) into the category-agnostic setting via the Keypoint Attention Refiner, a connection not previously exploited in CAPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simpler one-stage architecture with 51% fewer parameters outperforms the more complex two-stage CapeFormer by 2.2 PCK (1-shot) challenges the field's assumption that explicit similarity maps and iterative refinement are necessary for CAPE, potentially redirecting future work toward attention-quality improvements rather than matching-head engineering. The strong compatibility with self-supervised ViT backbones (DINOv2) suggests a clear scaling path. However, the current formulation is limited to single-instance, one-to-one keypoint correspondence, and the authors note that multi-instance scenarios and cross-domain generalization remain open. A broader open question the authors raise is whether CAPE-specific models are even needed as large foundation models develop class-agnostic matching capabilities natively.
