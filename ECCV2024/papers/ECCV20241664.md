## Problem

Existing GAN inversion methods are limited to inverting a single latent code (or at most two: latent + camera pose) for single-object scenes, making them unable to edit spatial attributes (scale, translation, rotation) or handle multi-object compositions. Inverting the full set of (5n+3) entangled codes that GIRAFFE uses to compose a multi-object scene—where each object contributes shape, appearance, scale, translation, and rotation codes alongside background and camera pose codes—remains largely unexplored due to the difficulty of disentangling and simultaneously optimizing many interdependent parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single multi-object RGB image (e.g., a scene with two cars from G-CompCars or multiple geometric shapes from CLEVR) and first decomposes it via segmentation into per-object masks and a background mask. It outputs a structured set of (5n+3) attribute codes—per-object shape, appearance, scale, translation, and rotation vectors, plus background shape/appearance vectors and a camera pose matrix—which can be individually or jointly modified to regenerate edited multi-object images with consistent 3D geometry.

## Method

3D-GOI performs a three-stage inversion: (1) scene decomposition via segmentation to isolate each object and the background, reducing cross-object interference; (2) coarse code estimation using a custom Neural Inversion Encoder whose architecture is the structural inverse of GIRAFFE's Neural Rendering Block (a ResNet-50 feature pyramid feeding a CNN downsampling network with skip connections and a final MLP head), with one dedicated encoder per code type trained sequentially while other codes are held at ground-truth values; (3) precise refinement via a round-robin optimization algorithm that, in each round, perturbs every code, computes a loss-sensitivity ranking, and optimizes codes in descending order of impact, halving the learning rate for any code whose loss plateaus. This avoids the local-minima trapping observed when all codes are optimized simultaneously and yields accurate reconstruction in roughly one minute per image on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the GIRAFFE generator, 3D-GOI achieves the best reconstruction quality across all metrics: MSE 0.024, LPIPS 0.262, and ID 0.897, outperforming the closest baselines (TriplaneNet at MSE 0.029, LPIPS 0.296; E3DGE at MSE 0.031, LPIPS 0.299). On the StyleGAN2 generator, 3D-GOI attains MSE 0.017 and ID 0.769, matching or exceeding all baselines, though HyperStyle achieves a marginally lower LPIPS (0.091 vs. 0.098). The round-robin optimization further improves reconstruction over fixed-order baselines, reducing MSE from 0.016–0.019 to 0.008 and LPIPS from 0.184–0.229 to 0.128.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the GIRAFFE generator, 3D-GOI clearly outperforms all five baselines on every metric: it reduces MSE by 0.005–0.007 over TriplaneNet (0.029), e4e (0.031), pSp (0.031), and E3DGE (0.031), and lowers LPIPS by 0.034–0.044. On the StyleGAN2 generator, 3D-GOI achieves the best MSE (0.017) and ID (0.769), but HyperStyle edges it out on LPIPS (0.091 vs. 0.098), indicating that for single-code 2D inversion the dedicated hypernetwork approach retains a slight perceptual advantage. The 2D baselines (e4e, pSp) degrade substantially when connected to the multi-code GIRAFFE generator (MSE 0.031, LPIPS ~0.30), confirming that standard single-code encoders are ill-suited for multi-code 3D scene inversion. The 3D baselines (TriplaneNet, E3DGE) designed for EG3D's two-code structure (latent + camera) also underperform, as they cannot handle the 5n+3 codes required by GIRAFFE's multi-object composition. Overall, 3D-GOI's dedicated per-code Neural Inversion Encoder combined with round-robin optimization provides a consistent and meaningful improvement over all existing inversion methods in the multi-code 3D setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GAN inversion from the single-code (2D GANs like StyleGAN2) or two-code (3D GANs like EG3D with camera pose) setting to a multi-code (5n+3 codes) inversion problem for multi-object 3D scenes generated by GIRAFFE. It builds on encoder-based inversion methods (pSp, e4e) and optimization-based refinement, but introduces a three-stage pipeline—segmentation-based code disentanglement, a custom Neural Inversion Encoder (structurally the reverse of GIRAFFE's Neural Rendering Block), and a round-robin optimization algorithm—that has no direct precedent in prior inversion literature. The work departs from prior 3D GAN inversion (SPI, E3DGE, TriplaneNet), which handles at most two codes for a single object, by treating the multi-object scene as a code-disentanglement problem where each object's five attribute codes and the background/camera codes must be independently recovered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D-GOI is the first framework to enable simultaneous editing of shape, appearance, scale, translation, rotation, and camera pose across multiple objects in a 3D GAN scene, opening a path toward interactive 3D scene manipulation for VR/AR and Metaverse applications. Key limitations include dependence on GIRAFFE as the generative backbone, reliance on segmentation quality (background codes degrade noticeably in multi-object scenes), and evaluation primarily on synthetic datasets (G-CompCars, CLEVR). Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to diffusion-based scene generators (e.g., CompoNeRF), scales to more than a handful of objects, and handles real-world (non-synthetic) multi-object images where ground-truth codes are unavailable.
