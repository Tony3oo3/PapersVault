## Problem

Accurate segmentation of tubular structures (blood vessels, endoplasmic reticulum, roads) remains challenging due to their complex topology, low contrast, and thin geometry. Existing deep learning approaches primarily exploit the raw image input without leveraging the inherent topological self-similarity that characterizes these structures across scales. This paper identifies a gap in integrating fractal features—specifically pixel-level fractal dimension—into deep segmentation models to explicitly encode this self-similarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240788/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGB image concatenated with a precomputed fractal feature map (FFM) as a second input channel. The FFM is generated by applying the box-counting method within a 5×5 sliding window (step size 1) to compute a per-pixel fractal dimension value, then normalizing the result. At inference, only the object segmentation mask is produced; the edge and skeleton predictions are auxiliary outputs used exclusively during training to provide additional supervision signals.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part strategy: (1) Fractal Feature Maps (FFMs) that extend fractal dimension from image-level to pixel-level via sliding-window box-counting, where FFM_image serves as an additional input channel and FFM_label (computed from the ground-truth mask) acts as a pixel-level weight in the object loss to upweight structurally complex regions; (2) a Multi-Decoder Network (MD-Net) that extends U-Net with three parallel decoders—object, edge, and skeleton—trained jointly with a composite loss (Soft IOU for object, BCE for edge and skeleton, weighted α=1.0, β=0.5, γ=0.5). The FFM module is architecture-agnostic and can be plugged into other backbones (demonstrated with HR-Net) without modifying training parameters, making it a general-purpose enhancement for tubular structure segmentation.

## Results

MD-Net with FFM (L_constrained) achieves the best performance across all six datasets. On the four tubular datasets, it reaches IoU of 77.09% (ER), 81.18% (MITO), 65.19% (ROSE), and 68.73% (STARE), with the lowest β Error (19.52, 2.80, 4.89, 2.60) and Hausdorff Distance (6.72, 4.14, 7.06, 6.34 px) among all compared methods. On the ROAD dataset, it achieves IoU 66.16% and β Error 2.06, and on the non-tubular NUCLEUS dataset it reaches IoU 78.77% and HD 4.06 px. The paper reports average IoU improvements of 1.08%, 0.82%, 1.62%, 1.46%, and 1.02% over the best SOTA baselines on ER, MITO, ROSE, STARE, and ROAD respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MD-Net with FFM (L_constrained) outperforms all 14 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest tubular-structure baselines, it gains +1.08% IoU over AF-Net (76.01→77.09) on ER, +0.82% over AF-Net (80.36→81.18) on MITO, +1.62% over GT-DLA (63.57→65.19) on ROSE, +1.46% over AF-Net (67.27→68.73) on STARE, and +1.02% over Dconn-Net (65.14→66.16) on ROAD. The multi-decoder architecture (edge + skeleton decoders) provides consistent gains over the single-decoder U-Net* baseline: e.g., on ER, MD-Net* (L_global) improves IoU from 76.59 to 77.01 and β Error from 20.78 to 19.10. Adding FFM_label as a loss weight (L_constrained) further improves IoU by ~0.08–0.11% and reduces HD by 0.02–0.05 px over L_global. On the non-tubular NUCLEUS dataset, MD-Net* (L_constrained) achieves IoU 78.77%, surpassing U-Net++ (78.33%), TransUNet (78.21%), and DU-Net (78.09%). The plug-in FFM_image alone (without multi-decoder) already improves U-Net by +1.14% IoU on ER and +3.27% on ROAD, and HR-Net by +0.60% on ER and +2.71% on ROAD, confirming FFM's generalizability as a model-agnostic module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the U-Net architecture with additional edge and skeleton decoders (forming MD-Net) and introduces fractal feature maps (FFMs) as a plug-in input channel and loss-function weight for tubular structure segmentation. It departs from the dominant prior approaches—deformable/dilated convolutions, transformer-based architectures, and topology-aware loss functions (clDice, persistence diagram loss)—by instead supplying the network with an explicit, hand-computed measure of topological self-similarity at the pixel level via the box-counting method. Prior fractal-feature work (fractional Brownian motion models) was limited to image-level classification or classical segmentation pipelines; this paper is the first to extend fractal dimension to a pixel-level map and integrate it into a deep segmentation network as both input and loss weight.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FFM-as-plug-in framing is practically attractive: it requires no architectural modification and can be attached to U-Net, HR-Net, or presumably other backbones, making it a low-cost augmentation for any tubular-structure pipeline. However, the gains are modest (roughly 1–3% IoU over strong baselines), the FFM computation adds ~150 ms per 256×256 image, and the method has only been validated on 2D images of relatively small scale. Open questions include whether pixel-level fractal features transfer to 3D volumetric tubular structures (e.g., 3D vasculature or neural tracts), whether the benefit persists at higher resolutions where window-based FD estimation becomes less discriminative, and whether learned alternatives to the box-counting estimator could match or exceed the hand-crafted FFM without the computational overhead.
