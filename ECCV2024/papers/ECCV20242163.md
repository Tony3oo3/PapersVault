## Problem

Prior model inversion (MI) research has focused on attack objectives, regularizations, and distributional priors, but has been entirely agnostic to DNN architecture design. This paper asks whether skip connections—a fundamental design in ResNets, DenseNets, and ViTs that mitigates vanishing gradients during training—also facilitate gradient backpropagation during MI inversion, thereby reinforcing attacks and compromising data privacy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242163/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The target models are face recognition classifiers (ResNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet, MaxViT, IR152) that map RGB face images to identity embeddings. During MI, a generative model G(w) trained on a public dataset (e.g., FFHQ) produces candidate images that are fed through the target model; the attacker optimizes w via gradient descent to maximize the likelihood that the generated image is classified as a specific private identity. Attack success is measured by an independent evaluation model's accuracy in matching reconstructed images to the target identity, while natural accuracy of the target model is tracked to ensure fair comparison.

## Method

The paper first establishes analytically and empirically that skip connections reinforce MI by enabling an additional gradient pathway (∂L/∂z_{i+1}) that bypasses the residual module during backpropagation, and shows that last-stage skip connections are most critical because their degraded gradients propagate to all earlier stages. It then demonstrates that RepVGG reparameterization, which removes skip connections only at inference time, cannot mitigate the vulnerability because the merged inference-time weights preserve the same gradient structure as the training-time multi-branch architecture. Based on these findings, the authors propose three MI-resilient architecture designs: RoLSS (removing only last-stage skip connections during training), SSF (scaling the last-stage skip signal by a factor k < 1 to limit gradient flow), and TTS (a two-stage training scheme that first trains with full skip connections for M epochs, then fine-tunes with RoLSS for N epochs). These designs require no additional hyperparameters (RoLSS) or only one (SSF, TTS), and outperform the SOTA MI defense BiDO in attack accuracy reduction while maintaining competitive natural accuracy.

## Results

The paper evaluates MI-resilient architectures (RoLSS, SSF, TTS) against the SOTA MI defense BiDO on ResNet-34/50/101/152 and DenseNet-121/161/169/201, using FaceScrub, CelebA, and Stanford Dogs as private datasets. The key metric is Δ (ratio of attack accuracy drop to natural accuracy drop): RoLSS achieves Δ of 5.72–9.89 across ResNet variants, consistently outperforming BiDO (Δ = 2.90–7.82). SSF and TTS recover natural accuracy (e.g., TTS on ResNet-34: Acc 94.40% vs. 91.38% for RoLSS) while maintaining competitive MI robustness (AttAcc 81.65% vs. 71.86%). On DenseNet-201, SSF incurs only ~1% accuracy drop while degrading MI attack accuracy by ~12% (AttAcc 77.62% → 65.21%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is BiDO [26], the SOTA MI defense that regularizes the training objective. RoLSS outperforms BiDO on the Δ efficiency metric across all four ResNet architectures: 5.72 vs. 2.90 (ResNet-34), 8.47 vs. 7.04 (ResNet-50), 9.89 vs. 3.50 (ResNet-101), and 8.86 vs. 7.82 (ResNet-152). SSF further extends this advantage, achieving Δ = 22.90 on ResNet-34 and 11.16 on ResNet-101, while also recovering natural accuracy to within 0.5% of the undefended model. TTS achieves the highest Δ on ResNet-34 (31.48) with near-undefended accuracy (94.40% vs. 94.69%). On ResNet-50, BiDO slightly edges out SSF and TTS on Δ (7.04 vs. 6.87 and 5.44), though RoLSS still leads. BiDO is not applicable to DenseNet architectures due to its requirement for extensive per-architecture hyperparameter grid search, leaving RoLSS/SSF as the only evaluated defenses there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by studying how DNN architecture design affects Model Inversion (MI) attacks, a dimension entirely absent from prior MI research which focused on attack objectives, distributional priors, and training-objective regularizations. It repurposes the well-known concept of skip connections (introduced for gradient flow in deep training) to reveal their role in facilitating gradient backpropagation during MI inversion, arriving at a finding opposite to prior work on skip connections and adversarial robustness. The paper also positions itself against RepVGG, a reparameterization technique that removes skip connections at inference time, showing it cannot mitigate MI vulnerability because the underlying gradients remain unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study linking a specific, ubiquitous architectural choice (skip connections) to a concrete privacy vulnerability, giving practitioners a simple architectural lever (remove or scale last-stage skip connections) that is complementary to existing MI defenses like BiDO. The RepVGG negative result is practically important: it warns that common inference-time reparameterization does not protect against MI, closing a potential false sense of security. Open questions include whether similar architectural vulnerabilities exist for other design choices (e.g., attention mechanisms in ViTs, batch normalization), whether findings generalize to black-box MI settings, and whether the proposed RoLSS/SSF/TTS modifications hold under stronger or adaptive attacks.
