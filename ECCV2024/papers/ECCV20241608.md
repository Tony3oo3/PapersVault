## Problem

Existing dataset compression methods (coreset selection, dataset distillation, dataset quantization) allocate samples uniformly across classes, yet the paper shows that some classes are 'stable' (accuracy plateaus with more data) while others are 'sensitive' (accuracy keeps improving). The unexplored question is how to adaptively redistribute a fixed sampling budget across classes to maximize overall model performance at a given data keep ratio.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241608/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled RGB image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny ImageNet) and a target data keep ratio as input. It produces a compressed dataset with imbalanced per-class sample counts (reported as Average IPC) where each retained image has 25% of its patches dropped and is reconstructed via a Masked Auto-Encoder before being used for downstream model training. The class-wise allocation is the key structural difference from prior methods that enforce equal IPC across all categories.

## Method

DQAS introduces two modifications to the Dataset Quantization pipeline. First, an active learning based adaptive sampling strategy replaces the uniform bin sampler: a class-wise initialization phase iteratively adjusts per-class sampling fractions using a rule that increases ρ_c when the current accuracy a_c^i falls below the best historical accuracy a_c, then an expected-error-reduction active learning loop selects top-K samples from the candidate pool that most reduce log-loss on the full dataset. Second, a patchified-image-aware pipeline reorders the DQ stages so that patch dropping and MAE reconstruction are performed before GraphCut-based bin generation, ensuring the submodular gain is computed on reconstructed features rather than original features, which eliminates the feature inconsistency between the bin-generation stage and the final output dataset.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DQAS achieves 95.8% top-1 accuracy at IPC 2500 and 3000, surpassing the previous dataset quantization method DQ (93.9% and 94.8%) and the best coreset method GC (93.2% and 94.2%). On CIFAR-100, DQAS reaches 72.75% at IPC 250, outperforming DQ (69.54%) and FL (70.38%). On Tiny ImageNet, DQAS attains 64.06% at IPC 250, exceeding DQ (63.97%) and GC (60.15%). At the most aggressive compression (IPC 50), DQAS leads by a wide margin: 52.3% on CIFAR-10 vs. 42.8% (GC) and 50.5% (DQ), and 52.61% on CIFAR-100 vs. 45.87% (DQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DQAS outperforms the prior dataset quantization method DQ [39] across nearly all sampling ratios on all three datasets, with the largest gaps at low compression: +1.8% on CIFAR-10 (IPC 50), +6.7% on CIFAR-100 (IPC 50), and +0.7% on Tiny ImageNet (IPC 50). Against the strongest coreset selection baselines, DQAS leads by substantial margins at aggressive compression — e.g., 52.3% vs. GC's 42.8% and FL's 38.9% on CIFAR-10 at IPC 50, and 52.61% vs. FL's 51.43% on CIFAR-100 at IPC 50. At higher IPC values the gap narrows but DQAS still maintains a lead: 95.8% vs. GC's 94.2% on CIFAR-10 at IPC 3000, and 72.75% vs. FL's 70.38% on CIFAR-100 at IPC 250. Compared to gradient-matching methods (Craig [23], GradMatch [16]), DQAS achieves 25+ point improvements at IPC 50 on CIFAR-10 (52.3% vs. 37.1% and 30.8%). In terms of computational efficiency, DQAS requires only 32.5 GPU hours to condense CIFAR-10 at ratio 0.6, compared to 46.7 hours for DREAM [21] and 924.2 hours for DC [38], making it roughly 1.4× faster than DREAM and 28× faster than DC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Dataset Quantization (DQ, Zhou et al., ICCV 2023), a three-stage pipeline for dataset compression involving bin generation, bin sampling, and pixel quantization. The key departure is replacing DQ's uniform sampler with an active learning-based adaptive sampling strategy that allocates different sample counts per class, motivated by the empirical observation that 'stable' classes plateau in accuracy while 'sensitive' classes continue to benefit from more data. Additionally, the paper reorders the DQ pipeline so that patch dropping and MAE reconstruction occur before GraphCut-based bin generation, ensuring feature consistency across stages. The active learning component draws on expected error reduction (Roy & McCallum, 2001) and introduces a class-wise initialization to accelerate convergence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that class-wise sensitivity to sample quantity is a common phenomenon across compression methods (demonstrated with both DQ and DREAM) offers a practical lever for reducing dataset size without proportional accuracy loss, particularly for 'stable' classes. The method achieves consistent improvements over DQ across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet, with the largest gains at low sampling ratios and on CIFAR-100 (~3 points at 50% ratio), while also being computationally cheaper than dataset distillation alternatives (32.5 vs 46.7 GPU hours vs DREAM). However, the approach is tightly coupled to the DQ pipeline, has only been validated on three moderate-scale datasets, and the active learning loop still requires multiple model training iterations, limiting its scalability to very large datasets or diverse architectures. Open questions include whether the stable/sensitive class distinction generalizes to non-image modalities and whether the adaptive sampling principle can be decoupled from DQ and applied to coreset selection or distillation frameworks independently.
