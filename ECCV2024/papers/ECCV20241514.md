## Problem

Existing 3D inpainting methods for NeRFs require per-view masks for all input views, which is time-consuming, reduces inpainting quality, and limits scalability. Moreover, training a NeRF on inconsistent 2D inpainted images from different views produces blurry results in the inpainted regions. This paper eliminates the need for multi-view masks by using a single mask from one selected view and addresses blurriness through a diffusion-model-based refinement step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241514/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of handheld-camera photographs of a forward-facing scene with known camera parameters and a single 2D mask annotated on one randomly selected view (slightly dilated with two 5×5 kernel iterations for complete object coverage). It produces a voxel-based NeRFs scene (density and feature voxel grids with a frozen shallow MLP) that renders inpainted novel views without the removed object. The approach is restricted to forward-facing scenes, where a single mask does not lose significant information compared to per-view annotation.

## Method

Built on DVGO's voxel-grid NeRFs, the method first unprojects the 2D mask into 3D by tracing rays through masked pixels to identify affected voxels, then initializes those voxels as free space to avoid being constrained by the original geometry. A two-step optimization follows: (1) rough inpainting uses LaMa-generated reference color and depth images from the selected view, with L_RGB (density gradients detached) and L_depth losses to jointly fix appearance and geometry; (2) refinement applies a mask-aware SDS loss from Stable Diffusion that restricts gradient updates to mask-region voxels only, with the text embedding optimized per timestep to match the inpainted reference for cross-view semantic consistency. This single-reference strategy avoids the inconsistency-induced blurriness of multi-view 2D inpainting, while the diffusion model adds fine details and removes distortions visible from other viewpoints.

## Results

On the SPIn-NeRF dataset, the proposed single-mask method achieves the best scores across all four metrics: LPIPS 0.186, FID 168.70, MUSIQ 64.20, and Sharpness 585.05. The most striking gain is in sharpness (585.05 vs. 294.92 for SPIn-NeRF, nearly 2×) and FID (168.70 vs. 204.26), indicating substantially sharper and more realistic inpainted regions. LPIPS is essentially tied with SPIn-NeRF (0.186 vs. 0.187), while MUSIQ improves from 56.95 to 64.20, confirming better perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: Object-NeRF (removes 3D points without inpainting), Masked NeRF (trains only on unmasked pixels), LaMa+NeRF (trains NeRF on per-view LaMa-inpainted images), and SPIn-NeRF (state-of-the-art multi-view segmentation + perceptual inpainting). The proposed method outperforms all four on every metric. Against the strongest baseline SPIn-NeRF, the gains are most pronounced in FID (168.70 vs. 204.26, ~17% reduction) and Sharpness (585.05 vs. 294.92, ~98% increase), while LPIPS is a near-tie (0.186 vs. 0.187). The large sharpness advantage is attributed to using a single reference inpainted image (avoiding cross-view inconsistency) plus a 2D diffusion-model refinement step that adds fine detail. Against the simpler baselines (Object-NeRF, Masked NeRF, LaMa+NeRF), the improvements are substantial across all metrics, confirming that dedicated 3D inpainting with geometry-aware supervision is necessary for coherent results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D inpainting line initiated by NeRF-In, SPIn-NeRF, and Remove-NeRF, all of which require per-view masks to identify the object to remove. The key departure is eliminating the per-view mask requirement by unprojecting a single 2D mask into the DVGO voxel grid, then performing a two-stage optimization: rough inpainting via LaMa-generated RGB and depth priors, followed by refinement using a mask-aware SDS loss derived from DreamFusion's score distillation framework. The mask-aware SDS loss (Eq. 8) is a novel adaptation that restricts diffusion-model gradients to only the masked voxels, and the strategy of initializing masked voxels as free space rather than optimizing from the original geometry is a practical contribution not seen in prior NeRF inpainting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-mask formulation significantly simplifies the 3D inpainting workflow, removing the need for video segmentation, 3D segmentation fitting, or point-cloud bounding-box annotation across all views—a practical gain for interactive or large-scale applications. The use of a 2D diffusion model to remove distortions (rather than generate new content) in 3D scenes is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such application, opening a path for leveraging pretrained generative models as 3D scene refiners. However, the method is restricted to forward-facing scenes, relies on potentially inaccurate rendered depth for the inpainting prior, and offers no controllability over the inpainted content, leaving 360° scenes and user-guided inpainting as open problems.
