## Problem

Fine-tuning defenses exploit the catastrophic forgetting property of DNNs to push backdoored models out of backdoor regions, effectively removing implanted backdoors with a small clean dataset. This paper asks whether backdoors can be made resilient to such fine-tuning defenses, and identifies the gap that conventional backdoor learning converges to sharp, easily-escapable minima in the loss landscape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242320/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10, variable for GTSRB and ImageNet-10) and outputs class labels. Training data is split into a mixed set D0 (85% of data, containing both clean and poisoned samples with triggers such as a 3×3 colored square, blended overlays, sinusoidal strips, or input-aware warps) and a clean-only set D1 (10% of data). The remaining 5% serves as the defender's fine-tuning set. The model architecture is ResNet18 by default, with VGG-16 and ResNet-20 used in ablations.

## Method

SBL (Sequential Backdoor Learning) reformulates backdoor training as a two-task continual learning problem. In Step 0, a backdoored model θ_B0 is trained on the mixed clean+poisoned set D0 using Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) to seek a flat backdoor loss landscape. In Step 1, θ_B0 is further trained on the clean-only set D1 with a small learning rate (0.001) and a CL regularization term R(θ_B0, θ) (e.g., EWC, AGEM, or Anchoring), which constrains the model to stay within the backdoor's effective region while reducing clean loss. The key insight is that simulating the fine-tuning defense during training familiarizes the model with clean-data updates, so subsequent fine-tuning by the defender produces only small gradient norms and cannot push the model out of the flat backdoor basin. The combination of SAM-induced flatness and CL-based parameter anchoring traps the model in a backdoor region that is geometrically difficult to escape, as confirmed by mode-connectivity analysis showing persistently low poisoning loss along the interpolation path between the SBL-trained and fine-tuned models.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, SBL with EWC maintains 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) under all four fine-tuning defenses (FT SGD-0.005, FT SGD-0.01, NAD, FT-SAM) while preserving ~91.5–92.1% Clean Accuracy (CA), whereas original BadNets training drops to 1.21–2.50% ASR after defense. On GTSRB, SBL w. EWC sustains 100% ASR across all defenses with ~98% CA, compared to 0.20–3.09% ASR for the original. On the harder ImageNet-10 setting, SBL w. EWC retains 74–100% ASR (e.g., 76.54% under FT SGD-0.01, 86.28% under FT-SAM for BadNets) with CA above 73%, while original training collapses to 5.23–10.47% ASR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBL is evaluated against three fine-tuning defense baselines: standard fine-tuning (FT SGD, from [43]), Neural Attention Distillation (NAD, [39]), and FT-SAM (sharpness-aware fine-tuning, [80]). SBL-trained backdoors defeat all three defenses decisively across every dataset and attack: on CIFAR-10, ASR remains at or above 97.6% under every defense, while original backdoor training drops below 2.5% ASR under NAD and FT-SAM. On GTSRB the gap is even starker—SBL w. EWC holds 100% ASR under all defenses versus 0.20% (FT-SAM) and 1.03% (NAD) for original BadNets. On ImageNet-10, where defenses are most effective against original training (ASR drops to 5–10%), SBL w. EWC still retains 74–100% ASR, with FT-SAM being the strongest defense (reducing ASR to ~74–88% rather than eliminating it entirely). The only setting where SBL shows a notable vulnerability is FT-SAM on ImageNet-10 with Blended/SIG attacks, where ASR drops to 46–88%, suggesting that SAM-based defenses partially counter the flatness mechanism on larger, more complex datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes backdoor attack training through the lens of continual learning (CL), building directly on CL regularization techniques (EWC, AGEM, Anchoring) and sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) to create backdoors that resist fine-tuning defenses. It departs from conventional backdoor learning (CBL), which trains a single model on poisoned data, by introducing a two-stage sequential procedure where the second stage simulates the defender's fine-tuning with CL constraints. The theoretical grounding draws on mode connectivity literature (Mirzadeh et al.) showing low-error paths between multi-task and CL minima, and on the observation that flat minima reduce catastrophic forgetting. The framework is attack-agnostic, wrapping existing backdoor methods (BadNets, Blended, SIG, WaNet) rather than proposing a new trigger or poisoning strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results expose a significant vulnerability: fine-tuning defenses that reduce ASR from ~100% to near 0% on conventional backdoors are essentially neutralized by SBL, which maintains ~100% ASR across multiple defenses (SGD fine-tuning, NAD, FT-SAM) on CIFAR-10 and GTSRB. This shifts the burden to defenders and suggests that the catastrophic-forgetting mechanism underpinning fine-tuning defenses can be systematically countered. Limitations include evaluation only on image classification with CNN backbones, and the ImageNet-10 results show meaningful ASR degradation (60–88%), indicating the method is not universally perfect. Open questions include whether SBL generalizes to transformer architectures, NLP, or federated settings, and what defensive strategies could counter flat-region backdoors without sacrificing clean accuracy.
