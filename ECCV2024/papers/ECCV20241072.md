## Problem

Existing event-based video reconstruction methods prioritize temporal accumulation of event features, which overemphasizes low-frequency texture and causes over-smoothing and loss of high-frequency details in reconstructed frames. The fundamental challenge is bridging the modality gap between differential event signals and integral intensity images while preserving the high-frequency information inherently present in sparse events at the current moment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241072/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of event voxel grids (B temporal bins, segmented so that per-step event density does not exceed 0.25) and the output of a pre-trained intensity predictor (ETNet) applied to the previous frame's events. The diffusion model generates a temporal residual image (the difference between the true frame and the previous frame's intensity estimate), which is then added to the initial intensity estimation to produce the final reconstructed frame. This residual formulation means the diffusion target is a small-magnitude differential signal rather than a full intensity image, reducing the generation difficulty.

## Method

The core insight is to reformulate event-based reconstruction as a conditional diffusion task on temporal residual images (x_0^t = I^t − I~^{t−1}) rather than full intensity frames, which aligns the generation target with the differential nature of event data. A triple-path conditional architecture feeds three signals into the denoising U-Net: (1) multi-scale convolutional features from the pre-trained intensity estimate providing low-frequency structure, (2) a multi-scale ConvLSTM recurrent encoder accumulating long- and short-term event features for temporal motion, and (3) a high-frequency path that concatenates the current event voxel with the noisy residual image. Cross-attention between the three encoder paths (Q from event features, K from intensity features, V from the noisy residual+event features) enables the model to use temporal accumulation to correct intensity estimation errors while preserving high-frequency event details. Training uses standard DDPM with L1 noise-prediction loss over 2000 diffusion steps, and the ConvLSTM hidden state is updated at each temporal step to maintain temporal coherence across the video sequence.

## Results

On three real-world event datasets (IJRR, HQF, MVSEC), the proposed temporal residual guided diffusion framework achieves the best SSIM (0.3856, 0.4496, 0.2912) and LPIPS (0.1694, 0.2039, 0.2789) across all datasets, with SSIM improvements of 4.3%, 3.9%, and 5.4% over the second-best method (E2VID+) respectively. The method also attains the lowest MSE on IJRR (0.0597) and MVSEC (0.0715), though E2VID+ retains a slight MSE advantage on HQF (0.0363 vs. 0.0441). Overall, the framework demonstrates consistent superiority in structural detail and perceptual quality over all compared baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on SSIM and LPIPS across all three datasets, confirming superior structural fidelity and perceptual quality. Against the strongest competitor E2VID+ [37], the method gains 4.3% SSIM on IJRR (0.3856 vs. 0.3688), 3.9% on HQF (0.4496 vs. 0.4327), and 5.4% on MVSEC (0.2912 vs. 0.2762), while also achieving lower LPIPS by 12.1%, 5.5%, and 1.2% respectively. The only metric where the method does not lead is MSE on HQF, where E2VID+ achieves 0.0363 versus 0.0441 for the proposed method, likely due to E2VID+'s stronger low-frequency brightness matching. ETNet [41], the transformer-based baseline and initial intensity predictor used in this work, is consistently outperformed on all nine metric-dataset combinations. FireNet [33], E2VID [28], and SPADE-E2VID [2] are substantially outperformed, with SSIM gaps of 4–14 percentage points, reflecting the limitations of purely temporal-accumulation approaches in preserving high-frequency detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based video reconstruction lineage (E2VID → SPADE-E2VID → ETNet) by reframing the problem through the lens of diffusion-based image restoration. Rather than directly predicting intensity images from accumulated event features as prior methods do, it generates temporal residual images (frame differences) using a DDPM conditioned on three parallel paths: a pre-trained low-frequency intensity estimator, a ConvLSTM-based temporal encoder, and a cross-attention high-frequency event prior module. The residual generation strategy is conceptually borrowed from restoration-oriented diffusion work (ResDiff, UCDIR), where a coarse estimate is refined by a diffusion model generating the difference, here adapted to the temporal domain of event streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and well-documented failure mode in event-based reconstruction—over-smoothing and loss of high-frequency texture—by explicitly separating low-frequency temporal accumulation from high-frequency instantaneous event information. The temporal residual formulation is a practical and interpretable way to close the modality gap between differential (event) and integral (intensity) signals. Limitations include reliance on simulated training data (COCO+ESIM), a computationally expensive 2000-step diffusion sampling process, and dependence on a pre-trained ETNet predictor. Open questions include whether the residual formulation generalizes to other event-based tasks (optical flow, depth), whether fewer diffusion steps suffice with better schedulers, and whether the approach scales to longer video sequences without error accumulation.
