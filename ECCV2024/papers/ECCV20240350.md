## Problem

Event-based bundle adjustment for purely rotating cameras has remained unexplored as a direct (photometric) method: prior work either relied on feature-based (indirect) correspondences or produced only edge maps as a by-product of trajectory refinement. No previous approach jointly refines camera orientations and a panoramic intensity map from raw events without converting them into image-like representations, leaving a gap in the maturity of event-based SLAM back-ends.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of asynchronous events (pixel location, timestamp, polarity), a set of initial camera rotations (e.g., from CMax-ω, EKF-SMT, or IMU dead-reckoning), and camera intrinsics with a chosen projection model. It outputs refined control-pose rotations, a semi-dense panoramic gradient map (only pixels receiving sufficient events are optimized), and a full panoramic intensity map recovered by solving Poisson's equation. Notably, EMBA can operate without any initial map, recovering the gradient map from scratch given only initial rotations, and supports map resolutions up to 2048×1024 px.

## Method

EMBA formulates mosaicing BA as a regularized non-linear least-squares problem where each event contributes a residual based on the linearized event generation model: the dot product of the panoramic gradient at the warped map point and the map displacement equals the known contrast threshold. The key structural insight is that because each event depends on only one map pixel's gradient, the map-block of the normal equations (A₂₂) is block-diagonal with 2×2 blocks, enabling an efficient Schur-complement solver that avoids forming the full Jacobian. An L² regularizer on the gradient map prevents per-pixel instability while preserving the block-diagonal structure. Camera rotations are parameterized on a Lie group with Levenberg-Marquardt damping, and the final intensity panorama is obtained by solving Poisson's equation on the optimized gradient field.

## Results

EMBA achieves a 50% reduction in squared photometric error (PhE) on average across synthetic and real-world sequences, with a maximum relative decrease of 54.5% (CMax-ω on playroom: 0.33→0.15 ×10⁶). On synthetic data, absolute rotation RMSE (ARE) improves substantially when initialized by CMax-ω, e.g., city from 1.53° to 0.97° and town from 1.91° to 0.86°. On real-world ECD sequences, PhE decreases by 30–50% consistently (e.g., poster: 4.37→2.58 ×10⁵), while ARE shows mixed results due to residual translational motion in hand-held recordings. EMBA also produces high-quality panoramas from scratch using VGA (640×480) and HD (1280×720) event cameras without requiring an initial map.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMBA is evaluated as a back-end refiner on top of four front-end methods: EKF-SMT, CMax-GAE, CMax-ω, and RTPT. On photometric error (the primary metric), EMBA consistently wins across all baselines and all sequences where the front-end succeeds, with reductions ranging from ~15% (EKF-SMT on playroom) to 54.5% (CMax-ω on playroom). On synthetic rotation accuracy (ARE), EMBA most clearly improves CMax-ω trajectories (e.g., city 1.53°→0.97°, town 1.91°→0.86°) and CMax-GAE (playroom 4.63°→4.42°), while EKF-SMT shows a slight ARE increase on playroom (5.86°→6.09°) but improves elsewhere. On real-world ECD data, ARE results are mixed (some sequences worsen slightly) because hand-held recordings contain translational motion that the purely-rotational model cannot separate; however, PhE still drops by 30–50% for every front-end. RTPT fails on all synthetic sequences and is only comparable on real data, where EMBA reduces its PhE by ~45% (e.g., poster 4.69→2.58 ×10⁵). CMax-ω initialization benefits the most from EMBA refinement in both ARE and PhE, making it the recommended front-end pairing.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMBA builds directly on the Linearized Event Generation Model (LEGM) from Gallego et al. [12] and the mosaicing framework of SMT [24], but departs from both by replacing the filter-based, local-time estimation with a global, optimization-based refinement. The closest prior work, CMax-SLAM [20], also performs rotational BA for event cameras but maximizes contrast on an edge map rather than minimizing photometric error on an intensity map, and operates in a sliding-window fashion. EMBA's key conceptual departure is formulating the photometric residual directly from LEGM (∇M·Δp = sC), which yields a block-diagonal sparsity structure in the normal equations that no prior event-based BA method has exploited, enabling efficient Schur-complement solving without converting events to image-like representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first event-only direct photometric BA, filling a gap that has limited the maturity of event-based SLAM back-ends; the 50% photometric error reduction and the ability to produce high-quality panoramas from scratch with HD event cameras (VGA/HD) demonstrate practical relevance for smartphone and satellite applications. Key limitations remain: the method assumes purely rotational motion, static scenes, and brightness constancy, and the LM solver can converge to local minima with poor initialization. Open questions include extension to 6-DOF motion, handling dynamic content, and whether the LEGM linearization can be relaxed without losing the beneficial sparsity structure.
