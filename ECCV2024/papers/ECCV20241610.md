## Problem

3+1D imaging radar sensors offer a cost-effective and weather-robust alternative to lidar for 3D object detection, but their sparse point clouds and high measurement noise limit detection performance. Although all available imaging radar datasets include synchronized lidar data, this lidar information has not been systematically exploited to improve radar-only detectors at inference. The paper asks whether lidar point clouds can be leveraged during training to transfer knowledge to radar-only object detection without requiring lidar at test time.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes a 3+1D imaging radar point cloud accumulated over 5 frames (to improve density) and outputs 3D object bounding boxes with class labels for cars, pedestrians, and cyclists, evaluated in short-range (0–30 m) and mid-range (30–50 m) bins. During training, the input additionally includes a 64-layer lidar point cloud that is progressively thinned out by factors of 2 (down to 1/16 or 1/256 of the original) using random, k-nearest-neighbor, or voxel-based sub-sampling. The radar and lidar point clouds are mixed in later training stages, with the pillar/voxel encoder modified to prioritize radar points during random sampling to prevent their exclusion by the vastly larger lidar point count.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary strategies for cross-modal knowledge transfer from lidar to radar-only detection. The Multi-Stage Training Method (MSTM) applies a curriculum-learning schedule: the network is first trained on the full lidar point cloud, then iteratively retrained on progressively sparser lidar (halved each step), then on a mixed radar+lidar point cloud, and finally fine-tuned on radar-only data, with weights carried forward between stages. Three thin-out strategies are compared—random sampling, k-nearest-neighbor sampling (keeping lidar points closest to radar reflections), and voxel-based sampling (uniformly reducing points per voxel while preserving spatial distribution). The second strategy is cross-modal knowledge distillation, where a teacher network trained on lidar or mixed radar+lidar data guides a student trained on radar data via logit-KD, feature-KD, and label-KD losses, with the student initialized from the teacher's weights. Both methods are architecture-agnostic and validated on PointPillars (CNN-based) and DSVT-P (transformer-based), achieving up to 4.2 pp mAP gain with MSTM and 3.9 pp with KD initialization, without any architectural modification at inference.

## Results

On the View-of-Delft radar dataset, the radar-only single-stage baseline (R^SSTM) achieves 36.7 mAP in short-range (SR, 0–30 m) and 11.9 mAP in mid-range (MR, 30–50 m). The best multi-stage training method (MSTM with voxel-based thin-out and mixed radar+lidar pre-training, RL_{1-1/16/vox}^{MSTM}→R) improves this to 39.7 mAP SR and 15.4 mAP MR, a gain of +3.0 and +3.5 percentage points respectively. For knowledge distillation, initializing the student with a teacher trained on mixed radar+lidar data (RL_1^{SSTM}→R) yields 39.0 mAP SR and 14.8 mAP MR. On the DSVT-P transformer-based detector, the same MSTM configuration improves mAP from 38.3/13.0 to 41.5/15.6 (SR/MR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is radar-only single-stage training (R^SSTM), which the proposed methods consistently outperform. The MSTM with voxel-based thin-out and mixed radar+lidar pre-training (RL_{1-1/16/vox}^{MSTM}→R) achieves the best overall mAP (39.7 SR / 15.4 MR), beating the baseline by +3.0 pp SR and +3.5 pp MR, with the largest per-class gains on cyclists (+7.0 pp SR). Among thin-out strategies, voxel-based sampling dominates in SR while random sampling is strongest in MR for lidar-only pre-training; however, with mixed radar+lidar pre-training, voxel-based sampling wins in both ranges. The KD approach (initializing the student with a mixed radar+lidar teacher) reaches 39.0 SR / 14.8 MR, slightly below the best MSTM but above the baseline by +2.3 pp SR. On the DSVT-P transformer architecture, MSTM still provides a clear gain (+3.2 pp SR), but KD offers no improvement over simple initialization, indicating that the KD benefit is architecture-dependent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of curriculum learning [Bengio 2009] and cross-modal knowledge distillation [Hinton 2014], adapting both to the specific sensor pair of 3+1D imaging radar and lidar. Unlike prior cross-modal KD work (X3KD [22], HiddenGems [12], See Beyond Seeing [11]) that requires architectural modifications or additional sub-networks, this paper exploits the shared point-cloud representation of lidar and radar to use an identical network architecture for teacher and student, transferring knowledge purely through input modality and progressive sparsification. The multi-stage thin-out procedure draws on curriculum learning principles, while the KD losses (logit, feature, label) are adapted from 3D detection distillation [Yang 2022] and label assignment distillation [Nguyen 2022], with the radar-priority voxel sampling for mixed point clouds borrowed from [Nobis 2021].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: 3+1D imaging radar is a cost-effective, weather-robust alternative to lidar for production vehicles, and a 3–4 pp mAP gain from training-time lidar access (with no inference-time cost) is meaningful for deployment. However, the findings are dataset-limited (single VoD dataset, small scale) and the KD component's benefit is largely attributable to weight initialization rather than the distillation losses themselves, while KD entirely fails on the transformer-based DSVT-P. Open questions include whether combining MSTM as a teacher with KD as a student yields compounding gains, whether learnable thin-out strategies can replace the hand-crafted sampling heuristics, and whether results generalize to larger, more diverse radar datasets.
