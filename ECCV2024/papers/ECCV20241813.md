## Problem

Existing semi-supervised medical image segmentation methods either enforce consistency constraints or generate pseudo labels, but struggle to maintain continuous, progressive improvement of model capability throughout training. The core challenge is how to consistently produce diverse, high-fidelity pseudo labels that keep enhancing the model rather than plateauing or degrading as training progresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241813/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical volumes (112×112×80 crops for LA MRI, 96×96×96 for Pancreas CT) in batches of 4 containing 2 labeled and 2 unlabeled samples. A data buffer of length B maintains a fixed iteration gap between the two network groups, so each model is always trained on data from a different temporal position. At inference, the final binary segmentation mask is the element-wise average of the two networks' outputs, and a sliding window (stride 18×18×4 for LA, 16×16×16 for Pancreas) reconstructs full-volume predictions.

## Method

PMT employs two homogeneous VNet-based Mean Teacher groups trained in a progressive, alternating fashion: only the most-lagging model (the Current Progressive Model) is updated at each step, preserving a static iteration gap that creates stable performance diversity between the groups. A Pseudo Label Filtering (PLF) module uses supervised-phase Dice performance as a threshold, discarding pseudo labels from models that underperform the CPM and sharpening the retained labels before computing an MSE loss. A Discrepancy Driven Alignment (DDA) regularizer computes the symmetric-difference mask between the binarized predictions of all networks and applies MSE only on those disagreeing regions, pulling the lagging model toward the leading model's predictions. The total loss combines supervised CE+DICE, the PLF-gated unsupervised pseudo-label loss, the DDA alignment term, and the standard MT EMA consistency loss, with Gaussian warm-up schedules controlling the unsupervised and consistency weights over 6,000 iterations.

## Results

On the LA dataset with 10% labeled data, PMT achieves Dice 90.81, Jaccard 83.23, 95HD 5.61, and ASD 1.50, outperforming the previous best (BCP) by 1.19% Dice and 1.92% Jaccard. With only 5% labeled data, PMT still reaches Dice 89.47 and Jaccard 81.04, surpassing MCF's 10% results. On the more challenging Pancreas-NIH dataset at 10% labeled data, PMT achieves Dice 81.00 and Jaccard 68.33, a 7.17% Dice improvement over the best prior method (BCP at 73.83). At 20% labeled data on Pancreas-NIH, PMT attains Dice 83.22 and ASD 1.89, maintaining a lead over BCP in Dice and ASD though BCP retains a slight edge in 95HD (6.43 vs. 7.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMT outperforms all six baselines (VNet, UA-MT, SASSNet, DTC, MCF, BCP) on all four metrics at both 5% and 10% labeled data on the LA dataset. Against the strongest prior method BCP, PMT gains 1.45% Dice and 2.32% Jaccard at 5% labeled data, and 1.19% Dice and 1.92% Jaccard at 10%. On the substantially harder Pancreas-NIH dataset, the margin widens dramatically: at 10% labeled data, PMT achieves Dice 81.00 versus BCP's 73.83 (a 7.17% gap) and MCF's results are not reported. At 20% labeled data on Pancreas-NIH, PMT leads BCP in Dice (83.22 vs. 82.91) and ASD (1.89 vs. 2.25), though BCP retains a 95HD advantage (6.43 vs. 7.60), indicating PMT's edge is strongest in volumetric overlap and surface distance but not uniformly dominant on the most extreme boundary metric. Notably, PMT at 5% labeled data on LA already surpasses MCF's 10% results, demonstrating superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMT builds directly on the Mean Teacher (Tarvainen & Valpola, NeurIPS 2017) architecture, repurposing its EMA-based teacher-student consistency mechanism as the backbone for each network in a multi-model setup. It departs from standard co-training frameworks (CPC, CPS, UCMT, Dual Teacher) that update all models synchronously by introducing a deliberate cross-temporal iteration gap between two homogeneous MT architectures, so that performance diversity emerges naturally from training progress rather than from architectural heterogeneity (as in MCF) or from explicit diversity-promoting losses. The Discrepancy Driven Alignment regularizer and Pseudo Label Filtering algorithm are novel components that operate specifically in this temporal-diversity regime, targeting only the prediction regions where models disagree and gating pseudo-label transfer by measured representational capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive design offers a simple, architecture-agnostic mechanism for generating diverse pseudo-labels without requiring heterogeneous backbones or complex scheduling, which could lower the barrier for practitioners adopting multi-model semi-supervised training in medical imaging. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets and a single backbone (VNet), and the ablation bundles the progressive design with PLF and DDA, making it difficult to isolate the contribution of the temporal gap alone. The authors themselves note that more advanced and stable strategies under the progressive paradigm remain to be explored, and whether the approach generalizes to other 3D architectures (e.g., 3D ResNet, Swin-UNet) or to tasks beyond binary segmentation is an open question.
