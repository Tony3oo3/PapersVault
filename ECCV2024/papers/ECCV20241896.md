## Problem

Existing small-loss-based sample selection methods cannot effectively distinguish hard (correctly labeled but difficult) samples from mislabeled ones, since both exhibit high classification loss and wander near decision boundaries. Additionally, prior global sample selection strategies ignore intra-class feature relationships, limiting their ability to separate these two categories. The paper also identifies an underexplored dimension: how sample features move relative to class centers over the course of training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241896/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images with potentially noisy labels (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, WebVision-50 at standard resolution) and produces a trained PreAct ResNet-18 classifier. Unusually, the training process dynamically maintains a confidence set that grows and shrinks each epoch—samples are added when they exhibit high local feature density and consistent predictions, and removed when their predicted label diverges from the ground truth. The final output includes both the classifier and the confidence sample set, which is used for a 70-epoch fine-tuning stage with Mixup augmentation.

## Method

MTaDCS exploits the observation that clean easy samples, clean hard samples, and mislabeled samples follow distinct moving traces relative to their class centers in latent space during training. Each epoch, LOF is applied locally within each class's feature cluster (rather than globally) to select samples with high local feature density from the non-confidence set, while simultaneously removing samples whose predictions have flipped from the confidence set. A MoCo-based local supervised contrastive loss is applied within the confidence set to accelerate formation of compact high-density clusters, which in turn improves LOF's selection quality—a mutually reinforcing loop. The total training loss combines cross-entropy with Mixup on the confidence set plus the contrastive loss, and a self-ensemble label correction step is applied for high noise rates. This progressive, density-driven selection allows the method to gradually incorporate hard samples (which start as low-density outliers) as the confidence set expands, something global selection or single-epoch LOF cannot achieve.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, MTaDCS achieves the best average accuracy of 93.5% across all noise settings, outperforming TCL (92.7%) and Sel-CL+ (90.7%). On CIFAR-100, MTaDCS achieves the best average of 69.5%, surpassing TCL (67.7%) and RRL (60.2%), with particularly large gains at 90% symmetric noise (61.4% vs. 54.5% for TCL). On the real-world WebVision-50 dataset, MTaDCS attains 79.1% top-1 and 92.4% top-5 accuracy, and on ILSVRC12 validation it reaches 76.2% top-1, improving over the previous SOTA (TCL) by 0.8% top-1 and 0.2% top-5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTaDCS outperforms all 10 baselines on CIFAR-10 across every noise setting, with the largest margin over TCL at 50% symmetric noise (95.4% vs. 93.9%) and 90% symmetric noise (89.6% vs. 89.4%). On CIFAR-100, MTaDCS wins on 80% and 90% symmetric noise (65.7% vs. 65.0% and 61.4% vs. 54.5% over TCL) but trails RRL by 2.0% at 20% and 0.7% at 50% symmetric noise. On the real-world WebVision-50 benchmark, MTaDCS ties TCL and NGC at 79.1% top-1 but edges them out on top-5 (92.4% vs. 92.3%), and on ILSVRC12 it achieves the best top-1 (76.2%) and top-5 (92.6%), surpassing TCL by 0.8% and 0.2% respectively. The method shows its strongest advantage under high noise rates and asymmetric noise, where the gap over DivideMix reaches 20.4% (CIFAR-100, 90% sym) and over MOIT reaches 25.4% (CIFAR-100, 90% sym). Overall, MTaDCS is the only method to achieve the best average on both CIFAR-10 (93.5%) and CIFAR-100 (69.5%), confirming consistent superiority across noise types and datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTaDCS builds on two distinct lineages: the Local Outlier Factor (LOF) density-based outlier detection method from data mining, repurposed here as a local (intra-class) sample selection mechanism in the latent feature space, and the MoCo momentum-contrastive framework, extended into a local supervised contrastive loss applied within a dynamically maintained confidence set. It departs from the dominant 'small loss trick' paradigm in noisy-label sample selection by arguing that loss magnitude alone cannot separate genuinely hard samples from mislabeled ones, and from prior global selection strategies by restricting LOF to intra-class clusters near the decision boundary. The progressive confidence-set mechanism—iteratively adding high-density samples and removing mispredicted ones—distinguishes it from one-shot or threshold-based selection approaches like DivideMix or Co-teaching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent weakness in noisy-label learning: the inability of loss-based criteria to preserve hard but correctly labeled samples, which are critical for generalization. The moving-trace observation (clean hard samples converge toward class centers while mislabeled hard samples drift away) offers a geometrically grounded alternative to loss-thresholding that could inform future selection strategies beyond LOF. Limitations include reliance on a fixed LOF neighborhood parameter (m=10), evaluation primarily on CIFAR-scale and WebVision-50 datasets with a ResNet-18 backbone, and the confidence-set initialization strategy (manual subset for simulated noise, full dataset for real noise) that may not transfer cleanly to very high-noise or long-tail settings. Open questions include whether the local-density distinction holds for non-i.i.d. noise patterns and whether the approach scales to larger backbones and datasets like full ImageNet.
