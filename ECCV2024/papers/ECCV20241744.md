## Problem

Camera pose estimation from textureless, non-Lambertian objects under unknown illumination remains unsolved: pixel correspondences fail on reflective surfaces, and 3D correspondences from single-view normal recovery are corrupted by the generalized bas-relief ambiguity. The paper proves that even combining pixel and 3D correspondences cannot resolve the remaining rotational ambiguity under orthographic projection, leaving no existing method capable of recovering pose from just two views of a shiny, featureless object.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241744/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes exactly two RGB images of a textureless, non-Lambertian object with no requirement for overlapping background or known illumination, and outputs the relative camera rotation and translation, a 3D object shape, per-view surface normal maps, and per-view reflectance maps. The orthographic projection assumption means translation is recovered only up to offsets along the two viewing directions. The pipeline is fully self-contained: no CAD model, no calibration board, and no more than two input views are needed.

## Method

The key insight is introducing 'reflection correspondences'—matches between image locations where specularly reflected light rays originate from the same direction in the two views—detected from camera-view reflectance maps rather than raw images. A CNN feature extractor is trained with InfoNCE contrastive loss on synthetic normal/reflectance map pairs, augmented with random generalized bas-relief (GBR) transformations to make features robust to the inherent single-view geometry ambiguity. A RANSAC-based two-step algorithm first estimates the combined transformation G₂₁ = G₁⁻ᵀ R₂₁ G₂ᵀ from pixel and 3D correspondences, then uniquely decomposes it into G₁, G₂, and R₂₁ by searching over the ambiguous Euler angle η and selecting the solution that maximizes inlier reflection correspondences. A joint iterative framework alternates between multi-view normal/reflectance recovery via DeepShaRM and the two-step pose estimation to refine both quantities simultaneously.

## Results

On the nLMVS-Synth test set, the proposed method achieves a mean relative rotation error of 4.5°, outperforming its ablated variants (6.2° without joint estimation, 6.6° without reflectance maps, 7.4° without GBR data augmentation). On real-world images, the method achieves pose errors of 1.9° (Planck), 12.5° (Horse), 7.2° (Bunny), and 8.6° (Cat), substantially outperforming all baselines. The method also demonstrates robustness to varying levels of shape flatness (GBR parameter λ from 0.69 to 1.44), where the baseline without reflection correspondences degrades significantly for non-λ=1 shapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines on every object where a comparison is possible. COLMAP [25] completely fails on the Horse and Cat objects (N/A) and shows large errors (15.7°, 24.5°) on the remaining two, as it relies on textured background features unavailable in the cropped two-view inputs. LightGlue [13] achieves 5.0° on Planck but degrades severely to 93.6° on Bunny and 35.4° on Cat, indicating its feature matcher breaks down on textureless reflective surfaces. SAMURAI [3] shows moderate errors on Planck (5.6°) but large errors on the other objects (24.3°–68.7°), consistent with its need for many views or good initial pose estimates. The proposed method's advantage is most dramatic on Bunny (7.2° vs. 93.6° for LightGlue, a 13× improvement) and Cat (8.6° vs. 68.7° for SAMURAI, an 8× improvement), where the reflection correspondences resolve ambiguities that pixel and 3D correspondences alone cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DeepShaRM for single-view surface normal and reflectance map recovery, but departs fundamentally from both structure-from-motion methods (which rely on pixel correspondences and fail on textureless, non-Lambertian objects) and neural image synthesis methods like SAMURAI (which require many views or good initial poses). The key conceptual departure is the formalization of 'reflection correspondences' as a third type of correspondence, distinct from pixel and 3D (surface normal) correspondences, and the proof that the first two are provably insufficient under orthographic projection due to the generalized bas-relief ambiguity. Prior work on pose estimation from specular reflection (Lagger et al., Liu et al.) assumed known geometry or illumination, whereas this paper handles arbitrary objects under unknown natural lighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for overlapping background or diffuse texture, this work opens camera pose estimation to a wide range of practical scenarios: appearance modeling of reflective objects (NeRF/3DGS), motion estimation of shiny objects like cars, and camera calibration in uncontrolled environments. The method's reliance on only two views and its orthographic projection assumption limit its immediate applicability to close-range or strongly perspective settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to perspective cameras at close range, how it scales beyond two views, and whether the iterative joint estimation framework can be made more efficient for real-time use.
