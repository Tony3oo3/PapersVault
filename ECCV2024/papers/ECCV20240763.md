## Problem

Existing text-to-motion models map descriptions to entangled latent codes that superimpose all body parts, making fine-grained control over individual joints, specific frames, or iterative editing infeasible. Users cannot easily modify a subtle posture, insert a new action at a particular moment, or adjust speed/emotion while preserving the rest of the motion, limiting practical use in animation and interactive design.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a natural-language description (e.g., "a person is crouching") augmented with GPT-4-generated fine-grained keywords for 10 body parts and overall mood, all encoded via a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32. For editing, it additionally takes an original motion sequence and a textual editing instruction (e.g., "pickup location should be slightly higher"). The output is a 3D SMPL motion sequence (21–22 joints, 20 FPS on HumanML3D / 12.5 FPS on KIT, padded to 196 frames), produced by decoding an intermediate sequence of K-hot vectors over 392 semantically labeled pose codes at a 4× temporal downsampling rate.

## Method

CoMo replaces learned implicit latents with an explicit, interpretable pose codebook of 392 codes grouped into 70 body-part categories (e.g., "left knee slightly bent"), where each time step is a K-hot vector enforcing mutual exclusivity within a category. A PoseScript skeleton parser deterministically encodes motions into these codes, and a 1D CNN decoder is trained (smooth L1 on positions and velocities) to reconstruct motion from summed active codebook entries. The Motion Generator is a decoder-only transformer that autoregressively predicts Bernoulli distributions over each pose-code indicator, conditioned on CLIP embeddings of the original text plus 11 GPT-4 fine-grained keywords. For editing, GPT-4 operates zero-shot on the pose code sequence via a three-step prompting strategy—identify target frames, identify target body-part categories, then rewrite the specific codes—after which the edited codes are seamlessly spliced back and decoded, preserving unedited segments.

## Results

On HumanML3D, CoMo achieves R-Precision Top-1 of 0.502, FID of 0.262, MM-DIST of 3.032, and Diversity of 9.936, ranking within the top 3 across five of seven metrics. On KIT-ML, CoMo attains R-Precision Top-1 of 0.422, FID of 0.332, and MM-DIST of 2.873, placing in the top three on six metrics. CoMo achieves the best Diversity score on both datasets (9.936 on HumanML3D, 10.95 on KIT-ML) but reports the lowest MModality (1.013 on HumanML3D), which the authors attribute to the stronger text-motion binding from semantically meaningful pose codes. In a 54-participant user study, over 70% of annotators preferred CoMo's motion editing results over T2M-GPT and FineMoGen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMo achieves the best Diversity on both HumanML3D (9.936) and KIT-ML (10.95), surpassing all baselines including GraphMotion (9.692) and FineMoGen (9.263). On FID, CoMo (0.262) trails the strongest baselines T2M-GPT and GraphMotion (both 0.116) and FineMoGen (0.151) on HumanML3D, and FineMoGen (0.178) on KIT-ML, indicating slightly lower motion fidelity. CoMo wins on MM-DIST against T2M-GPT (3.032 vs. 3.118) and MLD (3.032 vs. 3.196) but is slightly behind FineMoGen (2.998) and GraphMotion (3.070). R-Precision Top-1 is competitive at 0.502, within 0.002 of the best (GraphMotion/FineMoGen at 0.504). The notable trade-off is MModality: CoMo scores 1.013 versus 2.766 (GraphMotion) and 2.696 (FineMoGen), which the authors explain as a consequence of pose codes creating a tighter text-motion binding that reduces per-text diversity. In the human editing study, CoMo is preferred by >70% of annotators over both T2M-GPT and FineMoGen, with the largest margins on body-part modification and action add/delete edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMo builds directly on PoseScript's semantic pose codebook and skeleton parser for encoding motions into interpretable discrete codes, and on T2M-GPT's decoder-only transformer architecture and 1D convolutional decoder for autoregressive generation and motion reconstruction. The key conceptual departure from prior text-to-motion work (T2M-GPT, MotionGPT, GraphMotion) is replacing learned, implicit latent representations with a predefined, human-readable pose codebook that factorizes motion across space and time. For motion editing, CoMo departs from prior approaches (FineMoGen, FLAME, OmniControl) that regenerate or inpaint motion from modified text or joint trajectories, instead enabling an LLM to directly manipulate the interpretable pose code sequence in a zero-shot manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interpretable pose code representation opens a practical path toward dialog-based, iterative motion editing where users or LLMs can reason about and modify specific body parts and frames without retraining or regenerating entire sequences. However, the current pose codes capture only local kinematic states (joint angles, inter-limb distances) and lack global descriptors for speed, style, trajectory, and repetition, limiting the scope of expressible edits. Additionally, LLM edits on pose codes are not constrained by physical feasibility, which could produce unnatural motions. The observed MModality trade-off—stronger text-motion binding at the cost of reduced diversity for a single prompt—highlights an unresolved tension between consistency and multimodality in discrete-code motion generation.
