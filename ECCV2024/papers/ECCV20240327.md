## Problem

Existing autonomous driving pipelines follow a serial perception-prediction-planning design that requires expensive instance-level bounding box and HD map annotations at every stage, and they only predict object-level box movements, failing to capture fine-grained 3D scene evolution. This paper asks whether a world model operating in the 3D semantic occupancy space can jointly forecast the full surrounding scene and ego trajectory without any instance or map supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240327/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2 seconds of past 3D semantic occupancy (200×200×16 voxels spanning ±40 m laterally and −1 to 5.4 m vertically) plus the corresponding ego trajectory, and autoregressively generates 1 s, 2 s, and 3 s future occupancy frames together with 2D BEV ego waypoints. In end-to-end variants, raw surround-camera images (6 cameras, 360° FOV) replace the occupancy input, with a separate perception network (TPVFormer or SelfOcc) producing the occupancy. The occupancy is first compressed into a 50×50 grid of 512-entry discrete tokens (128-dim) before being fed to the generative transformer.

## Method

OccWorld is trained in two stages. First, a VQ-VAE scene tokenizer converts 3D occupancy into a BEV representation via class-embedding concatenation along height, downsamples it with 2D convolutions, and quantizes each spatial feature to the nearest entry in a learnable 512×128 codebook, producing compact discrete scene tokens; a 2D deconvolution decoder reconstructs the occupancy. Second, a spatial-temporal generative transformer (GPT-like) predicts the next frame's tokens: it applies spatial self-attention and repeated 2×2 merging to build K multi-scale token hierarchies, then performs spatial-wise masked temporal causal self-attention at each scale to forecast per-position tokens, and aggregates multi-scale predictions with a U-net for spatial consistency. A dedicated ego token is interleaved with scene tokens and decoded by a small MLP to yield the ego displacement, enabling joint scene-and-trajectory modeling. Training uses softmax loss on codebook indices for scene tokens and L2 loss on ego displacement; inference is fully autoregressive, feeding predicted tokens back as context for subsequent frames.

## Results

On the 4D occupancy forecasting task (Occ3D/nuScenes), OccWorld-O (oracle 3D occupancy input) achieves an average mIoU of 17.14% and IoU of 26.63% over 1s/2s/3s future frames, substantially outperforming the Copy&Paste baseline (11.33% mIoU, 20.52% IoU). For motion planning on nuScenes, OccWorld-O achieves an average L2 error of 1.17 m and a collision rate of 0.60% without any auxiliary supervision (no boxes, maps, or motion labels), while running at 18.0 FPS. Under the VAD metric protocol (†), OccWorld-O improves to 0.64 m L2 and 0.24% collision rate, surpassing VAD-Base† (0.72 m, 0.22%) on L2 while remaining competitive on collision rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccWorld-O (no auxiliary supervision) outperforms OccNet [54] by a large margin on both L2 (1.17 m vs. 2.14 m) and collision rate (0.60% vs. 0.72%), demonstrating the advantage of the world-model paradigm over the perception-prediction-planning pipeline even without map or box labels. It also beats ST-P3 [17] (L2 1.17 vs. 2.11 m) and IL [44] (L2 1.17 vs. 1.35 m) on L2 error. Against LiDAR-based planners NMP [66], FF [16], and EO [27], OccWorld-O achieves lower L2 error (1.17 vs. 1.48/1.43/1.60 m) but higher collision rates (0.60% vs. 0.34/0.43/0.33%), reflecting the difficulty of learning safety without explicit freespace or box supervision. UniAD [18] remains the strongest baseline, outperforming OccWorld-O on both L2 (1.03 vs. 1.17 m) and collision rate (0.31% vs. 0.60%), though it relies on five types of auxiliary supervision (map, box, motion, tracklets, occupancy). Under the VAD metric protocol (†), OccWorld-O achieves 0.64 m L2 and 0.24% collision, surpassing VAD-Base† [26] on L2 (0.64 vs. 0.72 m) while matching its collision rate (0.24% vs. 0.22%), all without any auxiliary labels and at 4× the FPS (18.0 vs. 4.5). The fully self-supervised OccWorld-S (no 3D occupancy or LiDAR supervision) still produces non-trivial planning (1.83 m L2, 2.02% collision), highlighting the potential for label-free end-to-end driving.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccWorld adapts the GPT autoregressive transformer (Brown et al., 2020) and VQ-VAE discrete tokenization (Oord et al., 2017) to the 3D occupancy domain, creating a spatial-temporal generative transformer that jointly forecasts scene evolution and ego trajectory. It departs from the dominant perception→prediction→planning pipeline (UniAD, VAD, ST-P3) by replacing bounding-box and map-based intermediate representations with a unified 3D occupancy world model, and from prior driving world models (GAIA-1, DriveDreamer) that operate in 2D image space by working in a modality-agnostic 3D occupancy space. The scene tokenizer builds on VQ-VAE but introduces a BEV-compressed encoding of 3D occupancy with a learnable codebook, while the spatial-temporal transformer introduces multi-scale spatial mixing with per-position temporal causal attention and U-net aggregation—designs not present in standard GPT architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OccWorld demonstrates that 3D occupancy alone—without instance boxes, HD maps, or motion annotations—can support competitive motion planning (L2 1.17 m, collision 0.60%) and non-trivial 4D scene forecasting, opening a path toward scalable, interpretable end-to-end driving that avoids expensive per-stage labeling. The fully self-supervised variant (OccWorld-S) producing non-trivial results with zero 3D occupancy supervision suggests the framework can scale to large unlabeled video data. Key open questions include: (1) the model cannot condition forecasts on hypothetical driving commands, limiting counterfactual reasoning; (2) autoregressive error accumulation causes rapid degradation beyond 1–2 s horizons; and (3) collision rate remains worse than methods with explicit freespace supervision, indicating occupancy alone may not fully encode safety-critical spatial constraints.
