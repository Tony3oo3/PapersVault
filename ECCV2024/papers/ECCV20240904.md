## Problem

Current 3D instance segmentation methods rely on manually annotated 3D training data that is expensive to produce, often lacks fine-grained detail (especially for small objects), and limits generalization to unseen classes. The paper asks how to leverage the strong zero-shot generalization of 2D foundation models like SAM to achieve class-agnostic, fine-grained 3D segmentation without any hand-labeled 3D supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240904/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a full 3D point cloud (from a scanner or multi-view reconstruction) and outputs a variable number of class-agnostic binary instance masks, each produced by a learned query that generates a per-point heatmap via cosine similarity with point features, thresholded at 0.5. During training, the input is a partial RGB-D point cloud (back-projected from a single depth frame using known intrinsics/extrinsics), and supervision comes from 2D SAM masks projected to 3D via the same pixel-to-point mapping. For open-vocabulary use, a text prompt is additionally supplied to the downstream OpenMask3D pipeline to retrieve specific objects from the predicted masks.

## Method

Segment3D uses a MinkowskiUNet sparse-convolutional backbone with a MaskFormer-style transformer decoder whose queries are initialized via furthest-point-sampling and Fourier positional encodings. Training is two-stage and fully label-free: Stage 1 pre-trains on partial RGB-D point clouds supervised by pseudo-masks from a Mask Generation Module (MGM) that post-processes SAM's automatic output by removing 'part'/'subpart' masks whose intersection with any 'whole'-branch mask exceeds 90%, thereby eliminating over-segmentation of large objects while preserving small-object masks. Stage 2 fine-tunes on full 3D point clouds using only high-confidence self-predicted masks (selected by the product of mean sigmoid heatmap confidence and object-classification probability above a threshold), bridging the domain gap between partial and full point clouds. The loss combines dice and binary cross-entropy for mask regression with a binary object-validity classification, with bipartite matching assigning queries to target masks.

## Results

On ScanNet++, Segment3D achieves 13.0 AP / 23.8 AP50 / 38.3 AP25 without post-processing and 20.2 AP / 30.9 AP50 / 42.7 AP25 with post-processing, outperforming all baselines by at least +4.3 AP. On ScanNet200, it reaches 27.7 AP / 39.8 AP50, competitive with the fully-supervised Mask3D (34.1 AP) while requiring no manual 3D labels. Zero-shot transfer to outdoor nuScenes yields 37.8 AP50 / 48.0 AP25, more than doubling Mask3D's zero-shot score (15.3 AP50). Inference time is 0.7 s per scene, three orders of magnitude faster than SAM3D (386.7 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mask3D (the strongest fully-supervised baseline), Segment3D wins decisively on ScanNet++ (13.0 vs 8.7 AP without post-processing; 20.2 vs 14.3 AP with post-processing) and on zero-shot nuScenes transfer (37.8 vs 15.3 AP50), but loses on ScanNet200 (27.7 vs 34.1 AP), where Mask3D benefits from 200-class manual annotations. Against SAM3D, Segment3D outperforms on every metric (e.g., 13.0 vs 3.9 AP on ScanNet++) while being ~550× faster (0.7 s vs 386.7 s inference). Against the geometry-based Felzenszwalb method, Segment3D achieves more than 2× the AP on both ScanNet++ (13.0 vs 5.8) and ScanNet200 (27.7 vs 6.1). Against UnScene3D (self-supervised), Segment3D leads by +11.8 AP on ScanNet200 (27.7 vs 15.9), demonstrating that SAM-generated pseudo-labels provide substantially richer supervision than self-supervised color/geometry features alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Segment3D adopts the Mask3D architecture (MinkowskiUNet sparse-convolution backbone with a MaskFormer-style transformer decoder) but fundamentally rethinks its training paradigm: instead of learning from manually annotated 3D masks, it is supervised by pseudo-labels generated from the 2D foundation model SAM via a proposed Mask Generation Module that corrects SAM's over-segmentation. This departs from SAM3D, which naively projects and merges per-frame SAM masks into 3D, by learning a single 3D model that directly segments full scenes, avoiding both the merging inconsistency and the slow inference of the projection-merge pipeline. The two-stage training (RGB-D pre-training followed by self-supervised fine-tuning on full point clouds) bridges the domain gap between partial and complete point clouds, a problem not addressed by prior label-free approaches like UnScene3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the long-standing assumption that high-quality 3D instance segmentation requires expensive manual 3D annotations, showing that automatically generated 2D foundation-model labels can match or exceed fully supervised methods. The zero-shot transfer from indoor ScanNet training to outdoor nuScenes (37.8 vs. 15.3 AP50) suggests a path toward truly general-purpose 3D segmentation without domain-specific re-annotation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to internet-scale RGB-D data with monocular depth estimation, how sensitive performance is to SAM's own limitations, and whether the self-supervised fine-tuning stage can be replaced by more principled domain adaptation.
