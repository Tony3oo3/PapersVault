## Problem

Semi-calibrated relative pose estimation (unknown focal lengths, principal point at image center) is important because focal length estimation is notoriously inaccurate, yet no minimal solver existed for this setting from a single affine correspondence. Prior single-point solvers required either calibrated cameras or additional scene assumptions (planar motion, known gravity), leaving a gap for the semi-calibrated case with monocular depth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241052/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single affine correspondence (two 2D image points and a 2×2 affine matrix) together with non-metric monocular depth values and their spatial derivatives (∂λ/∂u, ∂λ/∂v) at each point, obtained from a pre-trained MiDaS-v3 predictor. It outputs the relative rotation R, translation t, and both focal lengths f₁, f₂. The depth is only known up to an unknown common scale α, and the principal point is fixed at the image center with square pixels, reducing the intrinsics to a single unknown per camera.

## Method

The 1AC+D solver exploits the fact that the affine correspondence constraint decomposes into two 3D vectors per camera that are related by rotation and a common scale, yielding two polynomial equations in f₁² and f₂² solved via a Gröbner basis (9 solutions, 29 μs). Rotation is then recovered from the normalized vector pairs and refined by SVD, while translation follows from the reprojection constraint with the overall scale fixed by the first depth. A second solver (1AC+1PC) handles the fronto-parallel plane degeneracy by expressing the fundamental matrix as F = F₁ + (f₂/f₁)F₂ from the affine correspondence alone, then determining the focal length ratio from one additional point correspondence. Both solvers are integrated into GC-RANSAC with an automatic degeneracy detector (eigenvalue threshold on A/det(A)) that triggers an exhaustive-search model upgrade analogous to DEGENSAC.

## Results

On PhotoTourism, the proposed 1AC+D⁺ solver achieves 14.6° average pose error and 43.0 AUC@5°, outperforming the next-best 5ORB (16.3°, 40.2) and 7PC (19.5°, 41.2). On ScanNet, 1AC+D⁺ reaches 42.9° average error and 25.2 AUC@20°, beating 7PC (43.5°, 23.3) and 3AC (52.6°, 22.1). On KITTI with a 25-frame baseline, 1AC+D⁺ achieves 26.8° average error and 46.6 AUC@20° while running in 11.5 ms—twice as fast as 7PC (23.9 ms, 30.7° error). For focal length estimation, 1AC+D⁺ attains the lowest median relative error on PhotoTourism (20.7%) and KITTI (68.5%), though 3AC is slightly better on ScanNet (15.9% vs. 18.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 1AC+D⁺ solver outperforms all six baselines (7PC, 6PC, 3AC, AC-to-3PC, 4SIFT, 5ORB) on average pose error and AUC metrics across all three datasets (PhotoTourism, ScanNet, KITTI). Against the strongest baseline 7PC, 1AC+D⁺ reduces average error by 4.9° on PhotoTourism (14.6° vs. 19.5°), 0.6° on ScanNet (42.9° vs. 43.5°), and 3.9° on KITTI at d=25 (26.8° vs. 30.7°), while also achieving higher AUC@20° in every case. The affine-based 3AC solver, which previously led in AC-based methods, is clearly surpassed: 1AC+D⁺ improves average error by 2.8° on PhotoTourism and by 9.7° on ScanNet (42.9° vs. 52.6°). The 4SIFT and 5ORB solvers, which rely on learned descriptors, are consistently the weakest, with 4SIFT particularly poor on KITTI (71.9° avg at d=25). For focal length estimation, 1AC+D⁺ achieves the best median relative error on PhotoTourism (20.7%) and KITTI (68.5%), but 3AC edges it out on ScanNet (15.9% vs. 18.9%). In terms of runtime, 1AC+D⁺ is competitive with 7PC and 3AC on all datasets, though the standalone 1AC+D solver (without the degeneracy upgrade) is significantly slower due to increased GC-RANSAC iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the calibrated single-affine-correspondence-plus-depth solver of Eichhardt & Barath (ECCV 2020) to the semi-calibrated setting where focal lengths are unknown, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify: prior single-AC solvers either assumed known focal lengths or required additional scene constraints (planar motion, known gravity). The fronto-parallel plane degeneracy handling borrows the model-upgrade strategy from DEGENSAC (Chum et al., CVPR 2005), adapted here to exhaustively search a second point correspondence. The polynomial system is solved via an automatic Gröbner basis generator, and the overall pipeline is integrated into GC-RANSAC, following the standard robust-estimation framework used by competing solvers (7PC, 6PC, 3AC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the minimal sample from three or more correspondences to a single affine correspondence plus a depth prediction, the method enables exhaustive (deterministic) search over all detected ACs rather than random RANSAC sampling, which is practically valuable in 3D reconstruction pipelines where monodepth is already computed. The consistent accuracy gains over 7PC and 3AC across PhotoTourism, ScanNet, and KITTI—especially at larger frame distances on KITTI—suggest the depth signal provides meaningful geometric information beyond what point correspondences alone offer. Limitations include dependence on monodepth quality (depth noise has larger impact than image or affine noise), the added complexity of the degeneracy detection and model-upgrade step, and the fact that focal length estimation from the fronto-parallel solver requires a supplementary quadratic solve rather than standard F decomposition.
