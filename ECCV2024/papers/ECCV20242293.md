## Problem

Diffusion models for text-to-image synthesis frequently exhibit 'dominant attention' in their cross-attention layers, where one or a few tokens receive disproportionately high attention while other prompt tokens are neglected, leading to missing objects or semantically incomplete images. Existing remedies either require additional training/fine-tuning, demand extra inputs such as segmentation masks or sketches, or optimize latent variables rather than directly controlling attention, limiting their practicality as a lightweight, plug-in solution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt plus a user-specified set of target token indexes (the tokens whose attention should be boosted) and produces a generated RGB image at the resolution of the underlying diffusion model (e.g., 512×512 for Stable Diffusion). No segmentation masks, reference images, or additional conditional inputs are required beyond the prompt and token indexes. The method is a plug-in module that can be toggled off at any time to revert to the original model's behavior, and it operates only on a subset of cross-attention layers (the last down-sampling and first up-sampling layers of the U-Net) to keep inference overhead around 49%.

## Method

Attention regulation formulates the problem of improving semantic fidelity as a constrained optimization over cross-attention maps at each diffusion step: minimize an error function that pushes the 90th-percentile attention of each target token toward 0.9 and equalizes total attention mass across target tokens, subject to an L2 bound on how far the edited map deviates from the original. The edited attention map is parameterized as softmax((QKᵀ + S)/√d), where S is an additive adjustment restricted to target-token columns and further decomposed into a small weight matrix θ multiplied by 2D Gaussian kernels, reducing the number of learnable parameters from M·n to M·n/(4σ²). Optimization is performed via gradient descent on θ at each diffusion step, with an exponential moving average (EMA) of the optimized map enforcing spatial consistency across steps, a temporal decay factor λᵗ reducing edit magnitude as the image becomes clearer, and a hard cutoff after step 25 to avoid disturbing fine-grained detail. The entire procedure is training-free, requires no model modification, and is applied only to two cross-attention layers in the U-Net, yielding a 48.8% computational overhead versus 119–415% for baselines.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 2, attention regulation achieves the best CLIP score (0.337) and detection success rate (72.5%) on the COCO dataset, and the best CLIP score (0.337) and detection rate (66.2%) on the A&E dataset, outperforming all four training-free baselines. The method also has the lowest computational overhead at 48.8%, compared to 79.3%–414.6% for baselines. On FID (Table 2), the method achieves 41.79, second only to Syntax Generation (40.71) and better than the original model (42.72), Composable Diffusion (77.43), and Attend-and-Excite (42.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Attention regulation outperforms all four baselines on both CLIP score and detection success rate across both datasets. Against Attend-and-Excite (the closest competitor), the method gains +0.007 CLIP and +8.0 pp detection rate on COCO, while reducing computational overhead from 414.6% to 48.8%; however, Attend-and-Excite achieves a lower LPIPS (0.393 vs. 0.508 on COCO), indicating slightly less perceptual distortion. Against Syntax Generation, the method improves detection rate by +8.7 pp on COCO and +4.8 pp on A&E, with a dramatically lower overhead (48.8% vs. 378.0%), though Syntax Generation has a marginally lower LPIPS (0.452 vs. 0.508). Against Dense Diffusion, the method wins on CLIP (+0.005), detection rate (+8.5 pp), LPIPS (0.508 vs. 0.729), and overhead (48.8% vs. 79.3%). Composable Diffusion is clearly the weakest baseline, with substantially lower CLIP and detection rates and higher overhead than the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the family of training-free, inference-time interventions for improving semantic fidelity in text-to-image diffusion models. It departs from Attend-and-Excite, which optimizes latent variables guided by an attention-based loss, by directly editing the cross-attention maps themselves through a constrained optimization formulation. It also differs from Dense Diffusion, which requires external segmentation masks, by needing only target token indexes as input. The Gaussian-kernel parameterization of the additive attention adjustment and the EMA/decay schedule for temporal consistency are the paper's distinctive technical contributions, reducing the number of free parameters from M·n to M·n/(4σ²) and mitigating spatial inconsistency across diffusion steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in, training-free nature of attention regulation makes it immediately deployable on any existing latent diffusion model without retraining, and the 48.8% computational overhead (versus 378–414% for the strongest baselines) makes it practical for real-time or resource-constrained settings. The consistent gains across four Stable Diffusion versions and two datasets strengthen the generality claim. However, the paper acknowledges failure modes rooted in the model's learned feature space diverging from human semantics (e.g., incorrect logo placement, concept fusion), and it remains open whether the approach transfers to video diffusion, multi-concept composition, or models with fundamentally different attention architectures (e.g., DiT).
