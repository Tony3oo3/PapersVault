## Problem

Temporal action segmentation methods ignore the long-tailed distribution of actions in procedural videos, where both action frequency and duration create severe head-tail imbalance. Existing long-tail solutions (logit adjustment, re-weighting, ensembling) assume class independence, which breaks the temporal dependencies inherent in action sequences and introduces activity-irrelevant or ordering-violating false positives. This is the first work to address long-tail recognition specifically within temporal action segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-computed I3D video features (D×T tensor) and, during training only, an activity label that determines group membership for the group-wise classifier. At inference, the group is inferred automatically by selecting the group with the lowest average 'others' class probability across all frames. The output is a per-frame action label sequence that constitutes the temporal segmentation; shared actions across groups (e.g., 'spoon sugar' in both tea and coffee) are treated as distinct classes within each group to avoid cross-activity confusion.

## Method

G-TLA combines two mechanisms: (1) group-wise classification, where sequences are partitioned into disjoint groups (by activity label or KL-divergence clustering), each with its own classifier head and an auxiliary 'others' class, so that incompatible actions are structurally excluded; and (2) temporal logit adjustment, which modifies the standard logit adjustment by scaling the prior offset τ·log p(c|G_k) with a temporal factor T_{c,t} that is 1 within the valid temporal bounds [t₁, t₂] for class c (derived from mandatory predecessor/successor action sets S_bf and S_af) and a prior-ratio correction outside those bounds. The training loss sums a reweighted cross-entropy over the target group's classes, a downweighted cross-entropy over non-target groups' 'others' class (controlled by η), and a smoothing loss. At inference, temporal adjustment is disabled and the final prediction is the argmax over the identified group's classes excluding 'others'.

## Results

On YouTube with the AsFormer backbone, G-TLA improves the harmonic mean of head and tail frame accuracy by +7.5% (from 26.0% to 33.5%) and segment F1@25 by +4.6% (from 28.4% to 33.0%) over the unadjusted baseline, while maintaining global accuracy (+0.1%). With the MSTCN backbone on Breakfast, G-TLA achieves the largest gains: +5.0% frame accuracy Hmean (47.7%→52.7%) and +6.7% segment F1@25 Hmean (44.8%→51.5%), with global accuracy also improving by +2.6%. The method consistently outperforms all seven long-tail baselines across both datasets and both backbones, with the largest margin over vanilla logit adjustment (LA) on YouTube (+2.2% frame Hmean, +2.5% F1@25 Hmean).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-TLA outperforms all seven long-tail baselines on both YouTube and Breakfast across both backbones. On YouTube with AsFormer, G-TLA leads the next-best method (LA) by +2.2% frame Hmean and +2.5% F1@25 Hmean, while LA itself sacrifices head F1@25 (−0.7) and global accuracy (−1.9) to gain tail accuracy. Reweighting methods (CB, Focal) and post-hoc methods (τ-norm) provide only marginal gains (+0.1 to +2.8% frame Hmean) and often degrade segment metrics. The ensemble method BAGS is the strongest non-logit-adjustment baseline (+3.3% frame Hmean on YouTube/AsFormer) but still trails G-TLA by 4.2%. On Breakfast with MSTCN, G-TLA's advantage is most pronounced: +6.7% F1@25 Hmean vs. the next-best (BAGS at +2.6%), and it is the only method that simultaneously improves both global accuracy (+2.6%) and balanced segment F1. Against the SOTA DiffAct backbone on Breakfast (Table 4), G-TLA still improves tail frame accuracy by +2.9% and tail F1@25 by +1.2% over the unadjusted model, confirming the method's backbone-agnostic benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the logit adjustment framework of Menon et al. (2020) by replacing the class-independent prior p(c) with an activity-conditioned prior p(c|a) and introducing a temporal factor that encodes action ordering constraints. It departs from all standard long-tail methods (re-sampling, loss re-weighting, LA, LDAM, Seesaw) that assume class independence, arguing this assumption is violated in temporal segmentation where actions within a video are temporally correlated. The group-wise classification architecture is a novel structural addition that partitions the action space by activity, ensuring incompatible actions (e.g., 'stir coffee' in a 'making tea' video) are structurally excluded rather than merely penalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formally address long-tail imbalance in temporal action segmentation, filling a gap that has been overlooked in the literature. The finding that class-independent logit adjustment systematically introduces activity-irrelevant and temporally illogical false positives in segmentation (not just classification) is a useful diagnostic for practitioners applying long-tail methods to sequential tasks. Open questions include how the approach generalizes when activity labels are unavailable (the paper uses clustering as a fallback but with less clarity), whether the temporal ordering priors derived from training data transfer to unseen procedural activities, and how the method performs in truly few-shot tail scenarios where even the ordering structure is underdetermined.
