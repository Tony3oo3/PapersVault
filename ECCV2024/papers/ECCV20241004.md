## Problem

3D object part segmentation suffers from a severe annotation bottleneck: large-scale 3D part datasets are synthetic and expensive to collect, while existing few-shot methods require category-specific fine-tuning or ambiguous language prompts that struggle with fine-grained part terminologies. The paper asks whether 2D semantic correspondences from pretrained diffusion models can enable training-free, language-free 3D part segmentation that flexibly handles multiple granularity levels and transfers part labels across object categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object (point cloud or textured mesh) and a 2D part annotation database (either in-the-wild images from PACO or multi-view renders of a few labeled 3D objects) as input, along with calibrated camera parameters for back-projection. The 3D object is rendered into K multi-view RGB images at 800×800 resolution (15–20 views). The output is a per-point 3D part segmentation mask. Notably, the method requires no 3D segmentation priors (no point-cloud clusters, no mesh surface normals, no depth maps) and no language inputs—only the calibrated cameras, which can be known from rendering or estimated via SfM.

## Method

3-By-2 is a training-free pipeline with three novel components. First, single-pixel 2D label transfer uses DIFT (image diffusion model) features to find the best-matched pixel in the 2D database via cosine similarity, with a coarse-to-fine search strategy that localizes the match region at coarse resolution then refines within a 3×3 feature-space window (~2000× speedup). Second, a non-overlapping 2D mask proposal module takes SAM's overlapping multi-granularity masks, sorts them by area, and stacks smaller masks on top of larger ones to produce mutually exclusive part masks, on which sparse pixel sampling and confidence-weighted majority voting assign a single label per mask. Third, a mask-consistency aggregation module builds an undirected graph whose vertices are 2D masks across all views and whose edges connect masks projecting the same 3D points; it detects undersegmented masks (those whose correspondence set contains same-view masks with conflicting labels), discards them, then performs BFS traversal and majority voting to assign a final label per mask, preserving sharp part boundaries that point-level aggregation would blur.

## Results

On PartNet-Ensembled (few-shot, 45 categories), 3-By-2 achieves 0.642 mIoU average, outperforming PartSLIP (0.603) and PartSLIP++ (0.615) by ~3–4% mIoU, and fully-supervised PointNext (0.502) by 14%. In the zero-shot setting on the 18-category PartNetE/PACO overlap, 3-By-2 reaches 0.430 mIoU, surpassing PartSLIP (0.341) by 9% and SAMPro3D+OpenMask3D (0.146) by 28%. On fine-grained Level-3 PartNet (10 categories), 3-By-2 achieves 0.281 mIoU, beating PartSLIP (0.125) by 16% and matching MvDeCor (0.277) without any training or fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3-By-2 outperforms all language-based baselines (PartSLIP, PartSLIP++) across all three evaluation settings, with the largest margins on fine-grained categories (e.g., +28% mIoU on scissors in zero-shot, +16% average on Level-3 PartNet), highlighting the ambiguity of language prompts for fine-grained part terminologies. Against fully-supervised 3D methods (PointNext, PointNet++, SoftGroup), 3-By-2 achieves comparable or superior mIoU on the 17 overlapping categories (0.604 vs. 0.591 for PointNext) while requiring only 8 labeled objects per category versus 28K. In the zero-shot setting, 3-By-2 (0.430) substantially outperforms the scene-segmentation-based SAMPro3D+OpenMask3D (0.146) and the 2D part segmentation method VLPart-MC (0.222), demonstrating that its DIFT-based semantic correspondence and mask-consistency aggregation are more effective than CLIP/SAM prompting or trained 2D part classifiers for 3D part transfer. On Level-3 PartNet, 3-By-2 (0.281) slightly edges out MvDeCor (0.277) despite MvDeCor being both pretrained and fine-tuned with ground-truth depth and normal maps, whereas 3-By-2 requires no training at all. The cross-category experiment further shows that 3-By-2 can transfer 'wheel' labels from Chair to Table, improving wheel mIoU from 0.000 to 0.600, a capability none of the baselines (which require per-category fine-tuning) can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant language-guided paradigm for 3D part segmentation (PartSLIP, SATR, MvDeCor) by replacing text prompts with visual semantic correspondences derived from image diffusion model features (DIFT). It builds on the multi-view 2D-to-3D segmentation pipeline established by SAM-based approaches (SAMPro3D, OpenMask3D) but introduces mask-level rather than point-level aggregation, and a non-overlapping mask generation module to handle SAM's overlapping multi-granularity predictions. The coarse-to-fine correspondence search strategy and the graph-based mask-consistency aggregation are novel architectural contributions that distinguish this work from prior label-transfer methods (VLPart, SIFT-based approaches) that operated at the pixel or patch level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, language-free nature of 3-By-2 makes it attractive for practical deployment where 3D annotations are scarce and part taxonomies vary across applications (functional vs. structural). The cross-category part transfer results (e.g., chair wheels correcting table wheel predictions) provide empirical evidence for object part compositionality, suggesting a finite shared part vocabulary across categories. However, all evaluations are on synthetic benchmarks (PartNet, PartNet-Ensembled), and the method's reliance on calibrated cameras and a curated 2D database limits immediate real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to unstructured real-world 3D scans, whether the 2D database can be replaced by a single in-the-wild image, and how performance scales with database size and diversity.
