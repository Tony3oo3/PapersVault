## Problem

Industrial 4D head capture relies on multi-view stereo followed by non-rigid alignment and extensive manual artist post-processing to produce temporally consistent topological meshes and high-resolution textures, making the pipeline slow, error-prone, and expensive. Existing deep-learning approaches either use parametric models with limited expressive power or regress per-frame from images without enforcing inter-frame continuity, so they struggle to maintain temporal topology stability and cannot recover pore-level texture details natively.

## Input / Output

The method consumes calibrated multi-view RGB video (16 cameras at 4096×3000, 60 fps from a Light Stage with uniform lighting) along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics; geometry optimization operates at 512×375 while texture optimization runs at the original 4K resolution. It outputs a per-frame sequence of meshes with a fixed 8280-vertex / 16494-face topology in regular wiring arrangement, plus native 8192×8192×3 texture maps with pore-level detail, all directly usable in standard CG pipelines for retargeting and relighting.

## Method

Topo4D introduces a Gaussian Mesh representation that binds each 3D Gaussian's center to a vertex of a predefined topological face mesh, preserving 3DGS rendering fidelity while enforcing geometric structure. For each new frame, the method alternates geometry optimization (tracking the previous frame's Gaussians under physical prior losses—rotation similarity and isometric edge-length constraints—and topology prior losses—neighbor-position smoothing and face-angle flattening) with texture optimization (UV-space densification that inserts N×N sub-grids per quadrilateral via bilinear interpolation to learn high-frequency color). A scale loss during initialization and optimization keeps Gaussian thickness minimal along the surface normal, and a final Gaussian Normal Expansion step offsets each vertex along its normal by the projected scale to recover the true surface. Texture extraction maps the dense Gaussian colors into UV space via rasterization-based forward rendering, yielding 8K maps without any super-resolution network.

## Results

On the authors' 10-identity Light Stage dataset (16 cameras, 4096×3000, 60 fps), Topo4D achieves a mean mesh-to-scan error of 0.686 mm and median of 0.471 mm, with 87.4% of vertices within 1 mm and 97.7% within 3 mm. This substantially outperforms the best single-view baseline HRN (mean 2.871 mm, 20.7% within 1 mm) and the best multi-view baseline DFNRMVS (mean 3.649 mm, 17.1% within 1 mm). For texture, Topo4D is the first method to directly generate native 8K (8192×8192) pore-level textures without super-resolution, and it achieves the highest mean PSNR and lowest variance between consecutive frames for temporal texture stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Topo4D decisively outperforms all four mesh-reconstruction baselines across every accuracy threshold. Against the strongest single-view method HRN, Topo4D improves the fraction of vertices within 0.5 mm from 12.8% to 52.9% (≈4×) and reduces mean error from 2.871 mm to 0.686 mm (≈4.2×). Against the strongest multi-view method DFNRMVS, the improvement is even larger: 8.6% → 52.9% within 0.5 mm and 3.649 mm → 0.686 mm mean error. The single-view DECA and multi-view MVFR baselines are far behind, with mean errors of 8.104 mm and 7.800 mm respectively. For texture generation, Topo4D qualitatively surpasses UnsupTex and HRN, which are limited to low-resolution outputs and lack pore-level detail, whereas Topo4D directly produces 8K textures with visible wrinkles, hair, and pores. On temporal stability, Topo4D shows the lowest inter-frame RMSE for geometry and the highest mean / lowest variance PSNR for textures, while DECA's lower RMSE in some cases is attributed to its failure to capture extreme expressions rather than true stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for its rendering pipeline and on Dynamic 3D Gaussians (Luiten et al.) for its temporal tracking formulation, but departs fundamentally from both by binding each Gaussian to a fixed mesh vertex (the 'Gaussian Mesh'), thereby imposing topological structure that vanilla 3DGS lacks. It also positions itself against two prior lines of face reconstruction: parametric-model fitting (FLAME-based) which is limited in expressiveness, and learned regression from multi-view images which struggles with out-of-domain expressions and temporal coherence. The UV-space densification strategy is a novel departure from 3DGS's spatial adaptive densification, which would destroy the fixed topology needed for mesh extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topo4D is the first method to produce native 8K pore-level textures alongside topology-consistent dynamic meshes from calibrated multi-view video, eliminating the manual artist retopology step that dominates industrial 4D head capture pipelines. However, it remains a per-scene optimization requiring an expensive Light Stage setup with 16 calibrated cameras, and performance degrades noticeably at 4 views, limiting its applicability to consumer or single-camera settings. Open questions include whether the Gaussian Mesh formulation can be extended to non-facial objects with less regular topology, whether the per-scene optimization can be amortized into a learned model, and whether the approach generalizes to uncalibrated or sparse-view captures.
