## Problem

Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as CLIP align images with entire text descriptions but fail to distinguish fine-grained concepts within a category, producing entangled and mislocalized explanations for visually distinct concepts. Existing fine-grained alignment methods rely on expensive human annotations limited to object-level granularity, while concept-level annotations are prohibitively costly or infeasible to obtain at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241044/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (resized to 224×224) and text prompts augmented with LLM-generated discriminative concepts in the template 'An image of [CATEGORY] with [CONCEPT_1], [CONCEPT_2], ..., [CONCEPT_K]'. It produces concept-level explanation heatmaps (via any gradient-based post-hoc method such as Grad-CAM or Chefer et al.) and class predictions computed as the average embedding similarity between the image and all K concept embeddings within each category, rather than the standard single category-name similarity.

## Method

DEAL introduces two self-supervised regularization terms that exploit the discrepancy and consistency between concept-level and category-level explanations, eliminating the need for human annotations. The disentanglement term (R_disen) maximizes pairwise distance between explanation heatmaps of different concepts within the same category, while the localization term (R_local) minimizes the distance between the mean of all concept-level explanations and the category-level explanation, forcing the two constraints to work in an adversarial-like manner. Discriminative concepts for each category are extracted from GPT-3.5 using chain-of-thought prompting to ensure visual measurability. The full objective combines the standard InfoNCE contrastive loss with both regularizers via Lagrange multipliers, and the method operates as a plug-in module that introduces no additional parameters or architectural changes to the underlying VLM (ViT-B/32 or ResNet-50).

## Results

On average across five benchmark datasets (ImageNet, CUB, Food-101, Oxford-Pets, EuroSAT) using ViT-B/32, DEAL achieves 0.435 concept-level explanation disentanglability, 0.661 localizability, and 78.8% prediction accuracy, outperforming the second-best baselines by 8.8%, 10.9%, and 13.1% respectively. On ImageNet specifically, DEAL reaches 70.8% accuracy (vs. CLIP's 63.2% zero-shot and 68.9% full fine-tuning), 0.397 disentanglability, and 0.673 localizability. The method also improves mIoU for object-part localization by 5.20% (ViT-B/32) and 5.52% (RN50) on CUB-Part, and by 3.94% and 4.85% on PartImageNet, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEAL outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all five datasets. Against CLIP (the strongest baseline), DEAL improves disentanglability from 0.400 to 0.435, localizability from 0.593 to 0.661, and accuracy from 65.7% to 78.8% on average. Against PyramidCLIP, which is the second-best on localizability (0.596), DEAL gains 10.9% while also surpassing it by 39.8% in accuracy (78.8% vs. 39.0%). DeCLIP and CLIPpy, despite being designed for fine-grained alignment, show dramatically lower disentanglability (0.043 and 0.386) and accuracy (49.1% and 36.9%), suggesting their alignment strategies do not transfer to concept-level explanation quality. Notably, DEAL uses the same 151M parameter count as CLIP, confirming the gains come from the training objective rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's contrastive learning framework (InfoNCE loss with image-text alignment) and extends it with two novel regularization terms—disentanglement and localization constraints—that operate on post-hoc explanation heatmaps. It departs from prior fine-grained VLM alignment work (X-VLM, PyramidCLIP) that requires expensive human bounding-box annotations at the object/category level, instead using LLM-generated discriminative concepts as a self-supervised signal. The method also connects to the explainable ML literature by providing human-understandable concept-level explanations (rather than raw saliency maps) while avoiding the performance trade-off typical of intrinsic interpretability methods like ProtoPNet or Concept Bottleneck models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that VLMs, despite their strong zero-shot performance, fundamentally fail to distinguish fine-grained visual concepts—a limitation with safety implications in domains like autonomous driving. The finding that disentangling concept-level explanations simultaneously improves prediction accuracy (by reducing reliance on spurious correlations) challenges the common assumption that interpretability and performance are in tension. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-ended scenes with variable numbers of objects, whether LLM-generated concepts are always semantically appropriate across all categories, and whether the plug-in formulation extends to generative VLMs or multi-modal reasoning tasks beyond classification.
