## Problem

Part-level annotation data is orders of magnitude smaller than object-level data, creating a bottleneck that prevents vision foundation models from developing hierarchical comprehension between objects and their semantic parts. Prior work either treats parts as fine-grained objects without explicit hierarchical structure or relies on low-quality pseudo-labels, limiting generalization to novel objects. The paper asks how to leverage abundant object-level data to build a part-level foundation model that can parse any object into its constituent parts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242019/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a set of text category names (separate lists for object-level and part-level categories) as input. It produces a two-level hierarchical prediction: object-level bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and open-vocabulary class labels, followed by part-level bounding boxes, masks, and labels that are spatially constrained within their parent objects. The pixel embedding map used for mask generation is at 1/4 resolution, and the top-50 object queries (by similarity score) are forwarded to the Q-Former to yield 500 part queries (50 objects × 10 parsing queries).

## Method

PartGLEE introduces a Q-Former that acts as a hierarchical decomposer: L=10 learnable universal parsing queries perform cross-attention with N object queries from a MaskDINO-style deformable transformer decoder, producing N×L part-level queries that are fed into a separate part decoder. This top-down design—first detect objects, then parse each into parts—distinguishes the two hierarchy levels during training, unlike prior work that flattens parts into the object query space. A novel restriction loss penalizes part bounding boxes that extend beyond their parent object's bounding box, enforcing spatial containment. The model is jointly trained on a large collection of object-level datasets (COCO, LVIS, Object365, OpenImages, etc.) alongside part-level datasets (PACO, PartImageNet, Pascal Part, ADE20K-Part), with annotation granularity standardized across part datasets by integrating corresponding object-level annotations. This joint training transfers generalization from the object level to the part level, enabling zero-shot parsing of novel objects into semantic parts without requiring extensive part-level annotations for those categories.

## Results

PartGLEE (Swin-L) achieves 50.9 APmask on PartImageNet, 39.1 APmask on Pascal Part, and 27.8 APmask on PACO for part-level tasks, while maintaining competitive object-level performance of 52.0 APbox / 50.2 APmask on COCO-val and 50.6 APbox / 45.0 APmask on LVIS-val. In the Oracle-Obj zero-shot setting (Table 3), PartGLEE (R50) reaches 41.83 harmonic mean IoU on ADE-Part-234 and 37.11 on Pascal-Part-116, surpassing the best OV-PARTS baselines (ZSseg+ at 33.85 and CLIPSeg at 35.04) by 8.0 and 2.1 points respectively. On cross-dataset generalization to PartImageNet, PartGLEE trained only on Pascal Part achieves 9.9 mAPmask, outperforming VLPart's 4.5, and reaches 40.2 mAPmask with pseudo-labels versus VLPart's 7.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartGLEE substantially outperforms VLPart, the closest hierarchical baseline, across all part-level benchmarks: on PartImageNet APmask, PartGLEE (R50) scores 40.2 vs. VLPart (R50) at 31.6; on Pascal Part, 35.5 vs. 24.0; and on PACO APmask, 21.8 vs. 13.8. Critically, VLPart degrades on object-level tasks after joint training (COCO APbox drops to 38.0), whereas PartGLEE maintains 54.4 APbox, demonstrating that the Q-Former's hierarchical design prevents object-part confusion. Compared to the object-level foundation model GLEE (R50), PartGLEE adds strong part-level capability (PACO APopart mask: 15.4 vs. 1.29) while remaining competitive on object detection (COCO APbox: 54.4 vs. 55.0). Against specialist detectors (MaskDINO, ViTDet), PartGLEE (L) achieves comparable or superior object-level AP (59.5 COCO APbox vs. 58.3 MaskDINO-L, 57.6 ViTDet-L) while uniquely providing part-level segmentation. In the Oracle-Obj zero-shot setting, PartGLEE (R50) surpasses all OV-PARTS one-stage baselines (CATSeg, CLIPSeg) and two-stage baselines (ZSseg+) by 8.16 and 2.07 hIoU on ADE-Part-234 and Pascal-Part-116 respectively, confirming the advantage of explicit hierarchical modeling over treating parts as fine-grained objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartGLEE extends the GLEE object foundation model (Wu et al., CVPR 2024) by inserting a Q-Former between the object decoder and a new part decoder, repurposing the BLIP-2-style querying transformer as a top-down 'decomposer' that parses each object query into L part queries. Architecturally it inherits the MaskDINO deformable-attention decoder and the UNINEXT early-fusion module, while conceptually it departs from VLPart and OV-PARTS, which treat parts as fine-grained objects in a flat hierarchy, by explicitly modeling parts as subordinate components generated from their parent objects. The restriction loss (Eq. 4) and the dual Hungarian matching scheme are novel training mechanisms that enforce geometric consistency between the two hierarchy levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that abundant object-level data (COCO, LVIS, Object365, etc.) can be jointly trained with scarce part-level data to substantially improve open-vocabulary part segmentation, effectively transferring CLIP's generalization from objects to parts. This addresses a real data bottleneck in the field and positions PartGLEE as a potential perception backbone for mLLMs that need fine-grained spatial understanding. The main limitation acknowledged is the reliance on CLIP as text encoder, which constrains the model's ability to handle fine-grained or compositional part descriptions; the authors flag improving region-level text perception as future work. An open question is whether the fixed set of L=10 universal parsing queries scales gracefully to objects with highly variable part counts (e.g., humans vs. vehicles).
