## Problem

Aligning a collection of 2D images of semantically similar but non-identical objects into a shared 3D space remains unsolved when no shape templates, camera poses, or consistent illumination are available. Existing multiview reconstruction methods assume identical objects and photometric consistency, while 2D congealing methods cannot handle large viewpoint changes. This paper introduces 3D Congealing, a new task that recovers a canonical 3D representation and per-image poses from in-the-wild image collections with varying shapes, textures, and lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240022/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of N unlabeled RGB images (typically 50–100) of objects sharing semantic components, along with instance masks obtained via Grounded-SAM or dataset annotations; images are cropped to tight foreground bounding boxes. It produces a canonical 3D NeRF (12.6M parameters) defining a shared coordinate frame, per-image SE(3) poses with pinhole intrinsics, and dense forward (2D→3D in NOCS) and reverse (3D→2D) canonical coordinate mappings that enable 2D-2D correspondences between any image pair for downstream editing.

## Method

The key insight is to decouple the entangled shape-and-pose optimization by first obtaining a plausible 3D shape from a frozen Stable Diffusion model via Textual Inversion (learning a single text embedding that best explains the input images) followed by MVDream-style Score Distillation Sampling to optimize the NeRF, then registering images to this frozen shape using DINO semantic features rather than photometric loss. Pose initialization uses an exhaustive search over 3 FoV × 16 azimuth × 16 elevation candidates scored by semantic distance, followed by gradient-based refinement with se(3) Lie algebra parameterization and an IoU mask loss. After optimization, forward canonical coordinate mappings are computed by solving a 2D→2D semantic feature warping (with L2 and smoothness regularization) followed by 2D→3D NOCS rendering, while reverse mappings use nearest-neighbor inversion, yielding dense correspondences that support pose estimation and cross-image editing.

## Results

On the NAVI dataset (single-instance, varying illumination), 3D Congealing achieves a mean rotation error of 26.97° ± 2.24 and translation error of 0.40 ± 0.01 on COLMAP-converged scenes (S_C), and 32.56° ± 2.90 / 0.41 ± 0.04 on COLMAP-failed scenes (~S_C). On the cross-instance Pix3D benchmark, the method achieves an overall rotation error of 33.99° ± 8.26 across 7 object categories, outperforming PoseDiffusion's 35.47° ± 10.0. The method is robust to random seed variation, with consistent performance across 3 independent runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D Congealing significantly outperforms GNeRF (93.15°/80.22°) and PoseDiffusion (46.79°/46.34°) on rotation error across both S_C and ~S_C splits, with roughly 2× improvement over PoseDiffusion. Against NeRS, the improvement is dramatic (122.41° → 26.97° on S_C), as NeRS's photometric reconstruction objective fails under varying illumination. On S_C, the method is on par with SAMURAI (26.97° vs. 26.16°), but SAMURAI requires additional pose quadrant annotations as input, whereas 3D Congealing only needs unlabeled images. On ~S_C (where COLMAP fails), 3D Congealing actually outperforms SAMURAI (32.56° vs. 36.59°), suggesting the generative-model prior is more robust in the hardest cases. On the cross-instance Pix3D benchmark, 3D Congealing achieves a lower overall rotation error (33.99°) than PoseDiffusion (35.47°) with tighter variance (±8.26 vs. ±10.0), though the gap is smaller than in the single-instance setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D image congealing line (Neural Congealing, GANgealing) into 3D by introducing a canonical NeRF representation and per-image coordinate mappings, while departing from multiview reconstruction methods (BARF, SAMURAI, NeRS) that assume identical objects and rely on photometric losses. The key architectural departure is a two-stage strategy: first obtaining a plausible 3D shape via Score Distillation Sampling with a Textual Inversion-optimized prompt (building on DreamFusion/MVDream), then registering images using DINO semantic features rather than pixel-level reconstruction. This 'template-followed-by-implicit-deformation' approach sidesteps the pose-shape entanglement that plagues joint optimization methods, and the use of semantic distance metrics instead of photometric losses is the critical enabler for handling cross-instance appearance variance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework opens a practical path to 3D alignment of arbitrary in-the-wild image collections (landmark photos, product images, personal collections) without requiring shape templates, pose annotations, or controlled capture, enabling downstream tasks like 6-DoF pose estimation and 3D-aware image editing. Limitations include the ~1-hour per-scene optimization cost, dependence on pose initialization (ablation shows significant degradation without it), and evaluation on relatively small datasets (35 NAVI scenes, 7 Pix3D categories). Open questions include scalability to larger collections, handling articulated or highly deformable objects more robustly, and whether the generative-model prior can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives to reduce compute.
