## Problem

Structure-based visual localization requires storing high-dimensional descriptors for every 3D map point, leading to memory footprints (e.g., 6.3 GB for Aachen) incompatible with mobile and AR/VR devices. Existing compression strategies—map compression (removing 3D points) and descriptor quantization (e.g., PQ, binarization)—each cause significant localization drops, and prior differentiable PQ methods must be co-trained with the downstream task, which is incompatible with the non-differentiable PnP-based localization pipeline.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a query image (SuperPoint 256-d descriptors extracted at keypoints) and a compressed 3D map where each retained 3D point stores only M PQ indices (M×log₂(K) bytes per descriptor, e.g., 4 bytes for PQ4 with K=256) plus the 3D coordinates. The D-PQED layer dequantizes these indices back into 256-d descriptors for matching against the query, and the resulting 2D-3D correspondences are fed to a PnP solver within a robust estimation framework to output a 6-DoF camera pose. Memory budgets are tuned from 0.3 MB to 6.3 GB by jointly adjusting the PQ level M and the map compression ratio α.

## Method

The core contribution is the D-PQED layer: a differentiable product quantization encoder (M codebooks of K centroids, made differentiable via a straight-through estimator that uses soft softmax assignments during training and hard argmin during inference) paired with a scene-specific 2-layer MLP decoder that recovers the original 256-d descriptor from the quantized code. Training is standalone (not joint with localization) and uses a combined loss of L2 reconstruction plus two triplet margin losses—one in the raw descriptor space (L_raw) and one in the dequantized space (L_d)—with in-batch hard negative mining to preserve relative descriptor distances critical for matching. The D-PQED layer is combined with QP-based map compression (selecting distinctive, spatially distributed 3D points) to meet a target memory budget B, where the compression ratio α = B/B_M is tuned jointly with the PQ level M. The paper shows that PQ4 with moderate map compression plus D-PQED achieves the best localization accuracy at 0.5–1 MB, outperforming both extreme quantization (PQ2) and low quantization with heavy map compression (PQ32), and that LoRA fine-tuning of the decoder enables cross-scene adaptation at 0.3 MB with only 0.008 MB overhead.

## Results

On Aachen Day-Night, the proposed method (PQ4+Γ₀.₂₅+D-PQED) achieves 86.0%/77.6% accuracy at (0.25m,2°)/(0.5m,5°) thresholds with only 40.75 MB of scene memory, and the variant with PQ-compressed global descriptors (Ours*) retains 85.7%/77.6% at just 6.96 MB. On 7Scenes, Ours achieves 0.03 m / 1.08° median translation/rotation error and 73.0% accuracy at (0.05m,5°) with 1.64 MB. On Cambridge Landmarks, Ours* reaches 0.11 m / 0.27° median error with 8.85 MB, matching the full HLoc pipeline (5440 MB) at 0.09 m / 0.23° while using ~600× less memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Aachen Day-Night, Ours* (6.96 MB) substantially outperforms Scene-Squeezer (31 MB) by +10.2/+27.6 percentage points in accuracy at (0.25,2)/(0.5,5), and surpasses NeuMap (1260 MB) by +4.9/+29.6 points while using ~180× less memory. Against the full HLoc pipeline (6330 MB), Ours* retains 85.7%/77.6% versus 89.6%/86.7%—a modest 3.9/9.1-point drop at ~900× lower memory. On Cambridge Landmarks, Ours* (8.85 MB) achieves 0.11 m / 0.27° median error, matching AS (813 MB) at 0.11/0.26° and beating GLACE (52 MB) at 0.17/0.30° and NeuMap (75 MB) at 0.14/0.33°, while remaining close to HLoc's 0.09/0.23°. On 7Scenes, Ours (1.64 MB) achieves 0.03 m / 1.08° and 73.0% accuracy, slightly behind HLoc (9687 MB) at 0.03/0.93° and 76.8%, but far ahead of descriptor-free methods Go-Match (0.22/5.78°) and DGC-GNN (0.15/4.47°). The paper also shows that even off-the-shelf PQ without the D-PQED decoder retains competitive accuracy (e.g., PQ32 at 38 MB gives 88.6%/95.9% on Aachen daytime), indicating substantial redundancy in the original descriptors, but the D-PQED decoder provides consistent 3–10 point gains at extreme compression levels (PQ4, PQ2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scene compression line of work initiated by SceneSqueezer (Yang et al., CVPR 2022), which used a binary quantization autoencoder to recover descriptor information lost during compression. The key departure is replacing binary quantization with Differentiable Product Quantization (DPQ), which achieves tighter compression ratios, and training the encoder-decoder in a standalone fashion with triplet metric learning rather than jointly with the downstream localization task (as in Chen et al., ICML 2020). The map compression component is inherited from Mera-Trujillo et al. (3DV 2020), and LoRA is borrowed for lightweight cross-scene adaptation. The paper also provides a systematic empirical analysis of the interaction between descriptor quantization level and map compression ratio that prior work did not investigate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for AR/VR and mobile localization where memory budgets are extremely tight (1–4 MB for descriptors), and the demonstrated 86% accuracy at 1 MB on Aachen Day-Night (vs. 88.5% at 6.3 GB for the uncompressed HLoc pipeline) shows that near-full localization performance is achievable with ~6000× compression. The finding that a moderate quantization level (PQ4) combined with moderate map compression outperforms both extremes (PQ32 or PQ2) provides actionable guidance for practitioners tuning memory-performance trade-offs. Open questions include whether the scene-specific decoder generalizes across scenes without retraining, how the approach scales to city-scale maps with millions of points, and whether the triplet loss design transfers to other descriptor families beyond SuperPoint.
