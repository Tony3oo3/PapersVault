## Problem

Multi-exit networks share backbone weights across multiple prediction points, causing gradient conflict where opposing update directions from different exits degrade accuracy. Existing remedies—feature enhancement (extra data, wider layers, distillation) and gradient selection (PCGrad, CAGrad, Meta-GF)—either ignore the root cause or require multiple forward-backward passes per iteration, inflating training time. The paper seeks a mechanism that simultaneously resolves gradient conflict, preserves end-to-end joint optimization, and reduces backpropagation cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-100, 224×224 ImageNet) through a CNN backbone (MSDNet, ResNet-18, or VGG) and emits a class prediction at each of L intermediate exits of increasing depth. A single hyperparameter β ∈ (0,1) controls the partitioning ratio between shared and exit-specific features at every layer; the forward pass concatenates both feature segments before each exit's classifier, while the backward pass routes gradients selectively so that exit-specific weights receive only their own exit's loss signal.

## Method

DFS splits each convolutional layer's weight matrix w_i into a shared block w_i^+ (fraction β of output channels) and an exit-specific block w_i^- (fraction 1−β). In the forward pass, feature referencing concatenates the previous layer's shared and exit-specific features [f_{i-1}^+, f_{i-1}^-] before applying both w_i^+ and w_i^-, so every exit sees the full feature set for prediction. In the backward pass, w_i^- receives gradients only from its own exit's loss (g_{w_i^-}^i), while w_i^+ accumulates gradients from all deeper exits (Σ_{k=i+1}^{L} g_{w_i^+}^k), eliminating the conflicting summation that plagues vanilla training. This selective gradient routing reduces the total backward MACs from (12L−2)N³ to 10LN³ per step (up to 16.67% savings, independent of β), and in practice yields up to 50% lower training latency versus baselines that add distillation or gradient-merging overhead.

## Results

On MSDNet/Cifar100, DFS achieves top-1 accuracy of 72.46%–79.56% across 7 exits, outperforming the strongest baseline (Meta-GF: 67.97%–77.47%) by an average of 4.84% across all exits. On MSDNet/ImageNet, DFS reaches 69.05% average accuracy, exceeding the best baseline (Meta-GF, 67.25%) by 2.35%. In budgeted batch classification on Cifar100, DFS uses approximately 2× fewer average FLOPs per image to match the accuracy of Meta-GF. Training efficiency experiments show DFS reduces training time by up to 50% (vs. BYOT) and 14.43% (vs. DSN) on VGG7-64/Cifar100, with consistent FPS gains on VGG16/ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFS outperforms all 10 baselines on every exit of MSDNet/Cifar100, with the largest margins on early exits (e.g., +4.49% over Meta-GF on Exit 1, +3.37% on Exit 2) and smaller but consistent gains on deeper exits (+2.09% over Meta-GF on Exit 7). On MSDNet/ImageNet, DFS leads all baselines on Exits 1–4 (e.g., +4.37% over Meta-GF on Exit 1) but is marginally below Meta-GF on Exit 5 (72.88% vs. 73.27%), which the authors attribute to MSDNet's limited model capacity on the larger dataset. Gradient-selection methods (PCGrad, CAGrad, Meta-GF, Nash-MTL) are the strongest baselines, yet DFS still exceeds them by 2–5% on average, while also avoiding their extra forward-backward passes for gradient merging. Feature-enhancement baselines (DSN, BYOT) show the largest accuracy gaps (up to 6.94% on ResNet18 Exit 1) and the highest training latency, which DFS reduces by up to 50% in FPS. In budgeted batch classification, DFS achieves ~79.5% accuracy at 170 MFLOPs, requiring roughly 2× fewer FLOPs than Meta-GF for equivalent accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFS repurposes the feature partitioning concept from multi-task learning (GradSplit, ETR-NLP, mask-based routing) and applies it for the first time to multi-exit network training, where exits share a sequential backbone rather than parallel task heads. It departs from the dominant gradient-selection paradigm (PCGrad, CAGrad, Meta-GF, Nash-MTL) by eliminating the need for additional forward-backward passes to compute and merge per-exit gradients, and from feature-enhancement approaches (BYOT, DSN, knowledge distillation) by requiring no model redesign or auxiliary supervision. The method's two components—partitioning shared weights into exit-specific and shared segments, then cross-referencing features in the forward pass while restricting backpropagation—form a unified surgical operation on the feature tensor that simultaneously addresses accuracy and training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the ~2× FLOPs reduction to match baseline accuracy in budgeted batch classification, which directly impacts edge and real-time deployment of early-exiting models. A key limitation is that DFS benefits from over-parameterization; on smaller models like MSDNet on ImageNet, the deepest exit slightly underperforms Meta-GF, suggesting the technique may not transfer cleanly to capacity-constrained settings. Open questions include whether the β partitioning ratio can be learned per-layer or per-exit rather than fixed, how the approach extends to non-CNN backbones (transformers, state-space models), and whether the training-efficiency gains persist at much larger scale where data-loading and communication overheads dominate.
