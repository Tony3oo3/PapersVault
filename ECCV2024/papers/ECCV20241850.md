## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models suffer from a previously uncharacterized failure mode called Latent Concept Misalignment (LC-Mis), where a latent concept C that co-occurs with one of the prompted concepts in training data silently replaces the other prompted concept in the output (e.g., 'a tea cup of iced coke' yields a glass cup because iced coke is strongly associated with glass). Unlike prior misalignment work that addresses mutual encroachment between two explicitly mentioned concepts, LC-Mis involves a third, unmentioned concept that dominates the latent semantic space, making it non-trivial to fix via prompt engineering.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt containing two concepts (A and B) and produces a standard RGB image via SDXL. Unusually, the prompt is not fed to the diffusion model all at once: during the early denoising steps only the 'easily overlooked' concept (determined by GPT-3.5) is provided, and the full prompt is introduced only after a dynamically determined time-step boundary. The output is a single image per concept pair, with up to 20 samples generated per pair for evaluation.

## Method

MoCE (Mixture of Concept Experts) splits the SDXL denoising trajectory into two phases: a first phase where only the easily-lost concept B is conditioned on the model, and a second phase where the full prompt (A + B) is used to complete generation. GPT-3.5 determines the logical drawing order of the two concepts, mimicking how a human painter would sequence them. The boundary time-step t_x between phases is found via binary search guided by a Description-Concept Coordination Score (the max of CLIPScore between the image and the concept itself versus its GPT-3.5-generated textual description) and a Multi-Concept Disparity metric that measures the score gap between concepts A and B. The search iterates until the disparity falls below a threshold, ensuring neither concept is suppressed. The approach is training-free and operates as a post-hoc inference-time modification to any diffusion model's sampling loop.

## Results

On the 173 LC-Mis concept pairs (all rated Level 5, i.e., zero correct images out of 20, under the SDXL baseline), MoCE reduces the Level 5 proportion to below 50%, with over half of the pairs now producing at least some correct images and several reaching Level 1 (16–20 correct). AAE and Anole show little improvement over the SDXL baseline on these Level 5 pairs. Dall-E 3 achieves Level 5 reductions comparable to MoCE overall, but MoCE outperforms it on 3 of 4 sub-patterns (Beverage/Container, Local Cuisine/Location, Mismatched Outfits) while losing on Animal/Habitat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SDXL baseline (100% Level 5), MoCE is the only method that substantially reduces the Level 5 proportion, bringing it below 50% and producing several Level 1 pairs. AAE and Anole, which target traditional spatial misalignment, show negligible improvement on LC-Mis pairs, confirming that LC-Mis is a distinct failure mode. DALL-E 3, benefiting from expensive fine-grained training annotations, achieves overall Level 5 reductions comparable to MoCE, but MoCE wins on 3 of 4 sub-patterns (Beverage/Container, Local Cuisine/Location, Mismatched Outfits) while DALL-E 3 wins on Animal/Habitat. MoCE achieves this without any additional training or data annotation, operating as an inference-time hotfix on the open-source SDXL model, making it significantly more economical than DALL-E 3's approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SDXL as its base diffusion model but departs conceptually from the prior line of text-to-image misalignment work (Attend-and-Excite, GLIGEN, Composable Diffusion), which focused on spatial conflicts between explicitly mentioned entities A and B. The authors introduce Latent Concept Misalignment (LC-Mis) as a distinct failure mode where an unmentioned latent concept C (e.g., 'glass cup') hijacks the generation of concept B due to training-data co-occurrence biases. Methodologically, MoCE draws on the human painting sequence intuition and integrates GPT-3.5 for both concept ordering and automated dataset expansion, making it one of the earliest works to use LLMs as a core component of an image-generation pipeline rather than merely a prompt rewriter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously unrecognized and persistent failure mode in text-to-image models that survives even in expensive, fine-tuned systems like DALL-E 3 (as of mid-2024), suggesting that scaling data and compute alone will not resolve latent concept confounding. The finding that standard evaluation metrics (CLIPScore, Image-Reward) systematically mis-score LC-Mis cases—penalizing a correct 'tea cup of iced coke' more than an incorrect 'glass cup of iced coke'—raises important questions about how the community measures text-image alignment. Open questions include whether MoCE generalizes beyond two-concept pairs, whether learnable search can replace the iterative binary search, and whether the LC-Mis taxonomy can be extended to more complex multi-concept prompts.
