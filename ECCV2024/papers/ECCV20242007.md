## Problem

Restoring ancient manuscripts from small, irregularly shaped fragments is severely hampered by the open-world setting: candidate pools contain outliers (fragments from other manuscripts), fragments carry very little readable text, and the combinatorial space of pairwise comparisons explodes with pool size. Prior methods either assumed a closed-world with no outliers, relied on textual or texture features unavailable for tiny fragments, or required manual pre-selection of candidates, leaving the small-fragment restoration problem largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a pool of m small manuscript fragment images (k true fragments plus m−k outliers) as input. Stage 1 outputs a Top-K subset of fragments with the highest mutual contour similarity; Stage 2 takes each pair from that subset as a visual prompt alongside a role-play text prompt to an MLLM, producing a pairwise matching confidence score and a relative direction label (horizontal or vertical). The CO solver is trained on synthetic shredded data generated by grid-splitting real Dunhuang manuscript images, while the MLLM stage operates entirely zero-shot without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

LLMCO4MR is a two-stage pipeline. The first stage is a cardinality-constrained neural combinatorial solver: a siamese ResNet CNN computes pairwise contour similarity scores, which form edge weights of a graph processed by a GCN; a differentiable optimal-transport layer (Sinkhorn iterations) then enforces the Top-K cardinality constraint in a differentiable manner, and the whole network is trained end-to-end with a combined loss (BCE on pairwise similarity + BCE on node selection + the CO objective). A second-order matching refinement for 3×3 grids first selects the five most-connected central fragments, prunes the similarity graph to edges incident on them, and then selects the remaining four, reducing false-positive pairings. The second stage feeds the selected Top-K fragments pairwise into a multimodal LLM (GPT-4V or LLaVA) with a role-play prompt, which outputs a matching confidence and relative direction in zero-shot; if the MLLM flags a selected fragment as having no viable pairing, it is removed and the CO solver re-runs, creating an iterative refinement loop. The key architectural insight is that the CO solver absorbs the combinatorial explosion of outlier filtering, freeing the MLLM to focus on the nuanced multimodal pairwise judgment that contour-only networks cannot make.

## Results

On real-world Dunhuang manuscript fragment restoration, LLMCO4MR achieves the highest restoration success rate across all evaluated settings, reaching 0.6750 on the 2×2 task with a pool of 10 fragments and 0.5556 on the 3×3 task with a pool of 25 fragments. The CO solver alone (without MLLM) already surpasses all traditional baselines, e.g., 0.5750 vs. JigsawNet's 0.5250 on 2×2/pool-10, while the full LLMCO4MR pipeline adds a further 10-point gain. MLLMs used in isolation (GPT-4V, LLaVA) perform poorly without the CO solver's outlier filtering, with success rates dropping to 0.1250–0.3778.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLMCO4MR outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the strongest traditional method, JigsawNet [19], LLMCO4MR gains 15.0 points on 2×2/pool-10 (0.6750 vs. 0.5250) and 23.3 points on 3×3/pool-25 (0.5556 vs. 0.3222). Papyrus [31] is the closest competitor on 3×3 tasks (0.4111 at pool-25), yet LLMCO4MR still leads by 14.5 points. S3-Net [51] underperforms notably, scoring only 0.2111 on 3×3/pool-25. The two MLLM-only baselines—GPT-4V [26] and LLaVA [21]—perform worst among all methods, confirming that without the CO solver's cardinality-constrained fragment selection to eliminate outliers, MLLMs cannot handle the combinatorial explosion inherent in open-world manuscript restoration. The ablation showing CO Solver alone (0.5750 on 2×2/pool-10) already beats JigsawNet demonstrates that the combinatorial selection stage is the primary source of improvement, with the MLLM pairwise matching stage providing an additional ~10-point refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) neural combinatorial optimization solvers, particularly cardinality-constrained formulations solved via differentiable optimal transport (building on LinSATNet and Sinkhorn-based approaches); (2) vision-based manuscript/puzzle reassembly methods such as JigsawNet, Papyrus, and S3-Net, which rely on siamese contour or texture matching under a closed-world assumption; and (3) multimodal large language models (GPT-4V, LLaVA) for zero-shot visual reasoning. The key departure is the explicit open-world formulation—selecting relevant fragments from a pool containing outliers—which prior restoration pipelines do not address, and the first application of MLLMs to pairwise fragment matching in a jigsaw-puzzle-like setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a practical path toward automating the tedious small-fragment reassembly that archaeologists have long neglected, and the CO-solver-plus-MLLM pipeline offers a template for other combinatorial visual matching tasks where candidate selection precedes fine-grained verification. However, the real-world evaluation rests on a small number of Dunhuang case studies with expert-annotated ground truth, and the method still assumes a grid-like layout and cannot handle missing fragments or mixed-size fragments. Broader validation on larger, more diverse manuscript collections and a deeper analysis of when MLLM matching fails (beyond the aggregate success rate) would be needed before this approach could be deployed in active restoration workflows.
