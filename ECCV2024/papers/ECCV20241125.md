## Problem

Existing motion planning models for autonomous driving are typically trained in open-loop settings, limiting their ability to handle long-horizon closed-loop simulation and interactive agent behavior. No single framework unifies scene generation, reactive simulation, planning, and online RL training, forcing practitioners to maintain separate systems for each task. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified generative model that learns driving-world dynamics and serves as a central component for all learning-based motion planning tasks.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a rasterized static map (encoded by a 2D convolutional encoder), a sequence of key-value token pairs representing dynamic agents (where the key encodes object ID and category, and the value encodes quantized states such as position, heading, velocity, and dimensions), optional text prompts for scene generation, and historical agent states for extrapolation. Outputs include autoregressively generated future agent trajectories, initial scene configurations, ego-vehicle planning trajectories, and probabilistic simulation rollouts. The GRU decoder emits per-agent states in the fixed order {w, l, v_x, v_y, x_0, y_0, θ_0, …, x_T, y_T, θ_T}, and the model can dynamically add or remove objects mid-sequence via special tokens.

## Method

GUMP combines a raster encoder for static context, a key-value pair tokenizer that discretizes continuous agent states into control tokens (object identity) and state tokens (quantized kinematics), a Multimodal Causal Transformer (MCT) built on GPT-2 with Gated Cross Attention blocks for progressive static-dynamic fusion, and a stacked GRU decoder for autoregressive state reconstruction. The key architectural insight is the key-value tokenization: by separating identity from state, the model can selectively query any object, handle object emergence/disappearance, and support both full-autoregressive mode (for scene generation) and partial-autoregressive mode (for fast simulation). In partial-AR mode, intra-frame NAR conversion predicts all agents' next states in parallel via the GRU decoder, then a single MCT forward pass refines them, eliminating intra-frame sequential dependency. A prediction chunking and temporal aggregation module further stabilizes long-horizon rollouts by predicting a multi-step chunk with the lightweight RNN and aggregating outputs via a geometrically weighted average with decay rate γ.

## Results

On the Waymo Sim Agents benchmark, GUMP-m achieves the lowest minADE (1.590 m) and the highest interactive (0.7657) and map-based (0.8290) metrics among all 10 compared methods, with a composite meta metric of 0.6432. On scene generation (WOD Motion), GUMP-m reduces speed MMD by 42.1% and size MMD by 26.6% over the prior SOTA (TrafficGen). On nuPlan closed-loop planning (Hard-14), GUMP hybrid achieves a driving score of 77.77 in the IDM environment and 73.60 in the GUMP reactive environment, outperforming PDM (75.18/72.26) and hoplan (75.06/69.56). In open-loop planning, GUMP-m scores 88.6, surpassing CKS-124m (88.0) and PDM-Open (85.8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUMP-m outperforms all 10 baselines on the WOD Sim Agents benchmark in minADE (1.590 vs. 1.615 for Trajeglish and 1.677 for MVTE*), interactive metrics (0.7657 vs. 0.7646 for Trajeglish), and map-based metrics (0.8290 vs. 0.8271 for MVTE*), though its kinematic metric (0.3994) is slightly below MVTE* (0.4202) and MVTA (0.4175). In scene generation, GUMP-m dominates TrafficGen [19] and SceneGen [73] on all four MMD metrics, with the largest gains in speed (−42.1%) and size (−26.6%). On nuPlan open-loop planning, GUMP-m (score 88.6) edges out CKS-124m [70] (88.0) and CKS-1.5b [70] (86.6), and substantially outperforms PDM-Open [14] (85.8), IDM [75] (37.7), PlanCNN [61] (64.0), and Urban Driver [63] (76.0). In closed-loop planning on Hard-14, GUMP hybrid beats PDM [14] by 2.59 points in the IDM environment (77.77 vs. 75.18) and by 1.34 points in the GUMP environment (73.60 vs. 72.26), while also surpassing hoplan [32], GameFormer [36], planTF [10], GC-PGP [26], UrbanDriver [63], and the Expert baseline [10]. The paper notes that PDM's reliance on a constant-velocity assumption leads to overly conservative decisions, whereas GUMP's stochastic modeling better balances progress and safety (TTC 87.46 vs. 84.19 in IDM env).

## Relation to Prior Work

GUMP builds on the GPT-2 autoregressive architecture and Flamingo's Gated Cross Attention blocks for multimodal fusion, adapting the language-modeling paradigm to driving scene dynamics. It departs from prior closed-loop motion prediction work (Trajeglish, MotionLM, SBTA-ADIA) by introducing a key-value pair tokenizer that uses object IDs as keys and quantized state spaces as values, enabling flexible partial-autoregressive decoding. The intra-frame NAR conversion draws on MaskGIT's parallel decoding idea to eliminate sequential dependencies within a single time step, a design choice not present in prior driving world models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying scene generation, reactive simulation, planning, and RL training under a single generative model, GUMP addresses a practical gap where prior systems required separate pipelines for each task. The demonstrated scaling laws and SOTA results across Waymo Sim Agents and nuPlan planning benchmarks suggest the framework can serve as a scalable foundation model for autonomous driving. Key limitations include reliance on rasterized map inputs rather than vectorized representations, absence of direct sensor data integration, and the need for further engineering optimizations (quantization, KV-caching) before real-time onboard deployment.
