## Problem

Existing human image animation methods rely on skeleton or dense pose guidance that primarily encodes pose while ignoring body shape, making it difficult to maintain geometric consistency when the reference image and driving video have substantially different body shapes. GAN-based approaches further struggle with identity transfer across large domain variations (e.g., paintings, CG renders), producing temporal inconsistencies and visual artifacts. The paper addresses this by introducing a 3D parametric human model (SMPL) as a unified shape-and-pose guidance signal within a latent diffusion framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference human image (which can be a photograph, oil painting, watercolor, or CG render) and a driving motion video as input. The reference image is processed at 768×768 resolution, and the driving video is segmented into 24-frame clips for temporal training, with temporal aggregation at inference to produce longer outputs. The output is a temporally coherent animated video in which the reference person performs the driving motion while preserving identity, appearance, and body shape.

## Method

The core contribution is a parametric shape alignment step that transfers the SMPL shape parameters (β) from the reference image onto the driving video's pose sequence (θ), producing an aligned SMPL sequence that is then rendered into depth maps, normal maps, and semantic segmentation maps, supplemented by a DWpose skeleton for fine-grained facial and hand guidance. A Multi-Layer Motion Fusion (MLMF) module encodes each guidance condition through lightweight convolutional layers followed by self-attention, allowing each layer to learn its own saliency regions (e.g., depth focuses on contours, skeleton on face/hands), and fuses them via summation into a single guidance feature injected into a frozen denoising U-Net. Identity consistency is maintained by a ReferenceNet that encodes the reference image via VAE and CLIP, while temporal coherence is ensured by AnimateDiff-style temporal attention modules. Training proceeds in two stages: first an image-level stage (60k steps, batch 32) trains the guidance encoder, U-Net, and reference encoder, then a temporal stage (20k steps, 24-frame sequences, batch 8) trains the motion modules while freezing the rest.

## Results

On the TikTok benchmark, the fine-tuned version (Ours*) achieves the best scores across all metrics: L1 = 2.94E-04, PSNR = 29.91, SSIM = 0.802, LPIPS = 0.234, FID-VID = 21.07, and FVD = 160.82. The non-fine-tuned version (Ours) also leads on PSNR (29.84), SSIM (0.773), LPIPS (0.235), and FVD (170.20), while MagicAnimate edges it slightly on FID-VID (21.75 vs. 26.14). On the proposed in-the-wild dataset, Ours achieves the best PSNR (29.87), SSIM (0.806), LPIPS (0.211), and FVD (173.5), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the training domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MRAA (GAN-based, CVPR 2021), the proposed method wins decisively on every metric, with FVD dropping from 284.82 to 170.20 (37% reduction) and SSIM rising from 0.672 to 0.773. Against DisCo (diffusion-based, 2023), Ours* improves FVD by 45% (292.80 → 160.82) and SSIM by 0.134, confirming the advantage of 3D parametric guidance over disentangled 2D ControlNets. Against MagicAnimate (2023), Ours* leads on PSNR (+0.75), SSIM (+0.088), LPIPS (−0.005), and FVD (−18.25), while MagicAnimate retains a marginal FID-VID advantage (21.75 vs. 21.07 for Ours*). Against Animate Anyone (2023), Ours* surpasses it on PSNR (+0.35), SSIM (+0.084), LPIPS (−0.051), and FVD (−11.1), though Animate Anyone does not report L1 or FID-VID for a full comparison. On the in-the-wild dataset, Ours extends its lead over all four baselines, with the largest gap over MRAA (FVD 321.5 → 173.5) and a clear advantage over Animate Anyone (FVD 199.2 → 173.5, SSIM 0.727 → 0.806).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based human image animation line initiated by Animate Anyone and MagicAnimate, which rely on sparse skeleton (OpenPose/DWpose) or dense pose (DensePose) as motion guidance. The key departure is replacing these 2D pose representations with a 3D parametric SMPL model that provides a unified shape-and-pose parameterization, enabling 'parametric shape alignment'—transferring the reference image's body shape parameters β onto the driving video's pose sequence θ to produce pixel-level geometrically consistent guidance. Architecturally, it builds on the ReferenceNet and temporal-attention design from Animate Anyone and the AnimateDiff temporal module, but introduces a multi-layer motion fusion encoder with per-condition self-attention to jointly process depth, normal, semantic, and skeleton maps, going beyond the single-condition ControlNet paradigm used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parametric shape alignment mechanism directly addresses a persistent failure mode in human animation—body shape mismatch between reference and driving video—that skeleton-only or DensePose-only guidance cannot resolve, making it a practically useful component for any future diffusion-based animation pipeline. However, the paper acknowledges that SMPL's limited modeling of faces and hands necessitates the auxiliary DWpose input, and the independent solving of SMPL and DWpose introduces a potential consistency gap that remains unresolved. The in-the-wild dataset (~5,000 videos) is a modest but useful addition for evaluating generalization beyond the domain-restricted TikTok and UBC Fashion benchmarks. Open questions include whether richer 3D body models (e.g., with explicit clothing or facial geometry) could further close the face/hand gap, and whether the multi-layer guidance approach scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-person scenes.
