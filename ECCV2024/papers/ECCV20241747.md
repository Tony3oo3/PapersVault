## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-quality real-time rendering but produces an unstructured point cloud that cannot be easily edited or animated. SuGaR recovers editability by extracting a mesh and flattening Gaussians onto it, but this surface-based constraint destroys the volumetric rendering quality needed for fuzzy materials like hair and grass. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a hybrid representation that retains a full volumetric layer around an editable mesh.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241747/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of RGB images and camera poses estimated via COLMAP SfM as input. The user specifies a Gaussian budget N (5 million for real scenes, 2 million for synthetic), and the output is a base triangle mesh plus a variable-thickness layer of 3D Gaussians (the Frosting) parameterized by barycentric coordinates within prismatic cells. The resulting representation supports real-time rendering via Gaussian splatting and can be deformed, rescaled, or animated by manipulating the base mesh in standard tools like Blender, with Gaussian parameters automatically adjusting.

## Method

The pipeline first optimizes an unconstrained 3DGS representation, then applies SuGaR's surface-alignment regularization and extracts a base mesh via Poisson reconstruction with an automatically tuned octree depth. The Frosting layer thickness is determined per-vertex by first computing search intervals from the regularized Gaussians' isosurfaces (capturing local fuzziness), then refining with the unconstrained Gaussians' density function to find inner and outer bounds δ_i^in and δ_i^out along each vertex normal. Gaussians are parameterized using six barycentric coordinates within irregular triangular prisms (prismatic cells) between the inner and outer bounds, which enforces containment and enables automatic parameter adjustment during mesh deformation. A user-specified number of Gaussians is initialized (half uniformly, half volume-proportional) and optimized with the standard 3DGS photometric loss while keeping the count fixed, and optional mesh-based occlusion culling accelerates both training and inference.

## Results

On the Shelly dataset (synthetic scenes with fuzzy materials), Frosting achieves 39.84 PSNR / 0.977 SSIM / 0.033 LPIPS, outperforming vanilla 3DGS (37.66 PSNR) by 2.18 dB and all other methods including Adaptive Shells (36.02) and SuGaR (36.33). On NeRF Synthetic, Frosting reaches 33.03 PSNR, matching 3DGS (33.32) while being the best editable method. On real scenes (Mip-NeRF 360, Tanks&Temples, Deep Blending), Frosting achieves 28.38 / 23.13 / 29.62 PSNR respectively, consistently the best among editable methods and within 0.3 dB of unconstrained 3DGS. Occlusion culling adds a 100% training speedup and 30–60% inference speedup with only a ~1 dB PSNR drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Frosting decisively outperforms all baselines on Shelly, the dataset specifically designed for fuzzy materials: it beats vanilla 3DGS by 2.18 dB PSNR (39.84 vs 37.66), Adaptive Shells by 3.82 dB, and SuGaR by 3.51 dB, demonstrating that the adaptive Frosting layer's volumetric capacity is critical for hair/fur rendering. On NeRF Synthetic, Frosting (33.03 PSNR) is essentially tied with 3DGS (33.32) and Mip-NeRF (33.09) while remaining the best editable method, surpassing SuGaR (32.40) and Adaptive Shells (31.84). On Mip-NeRF 360 real scenes, Frosting (28.38 avg PSNR) trails 3DGS (28.69) by 0.31 dB but leads all mesh-based methods including SuGaR (27.27) and Adaptive Shells (26.61) by over 1 dB. On Tanks&Temples and Deep Blending, Frosting nearly matches or slightly exceeds 3DGS (23.13 vs 23.14; 29.62 vs 29.41 PSNR), confirming that the editable representation does not sacrifice quality on realistic captures. Across all six datasets, Frosting consistently outperforms SuGaR by 1.5–3.5 dB PSNR, validating that the variable-thickness volumetric layer recovers rendering quality lost when SuGaR flattens Gaussians onto the mesh.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework by introducing a structured, mesh-anchored representation that bridges the gap between editable surface models and high-quality volumetric rendering. Conceptually, it departs from SuGaR—which flattens Gaussians onto a mesh surface—by retaining a variable-thickness volumetric 'Frosting' layer around the mesh, parameterized via barycentric coordinates within prismatic cells. The adaptive thickness mechanism is reminiscent of Adaptive Shells' dual-mesh volumetric shell, but Frosting achieves similar effects with a single mesh and per-vertex offsets, combined with the speed and explicit representation of Gaussian Splatting rather than a neural SDF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gaussian Frosting provides a practical path toward real-time, editable 3D content creation from photos, combining the rendering fidelity of unconstrained Gaussian Splatting with the editability of traditional mesh-based tools—a combination that prior methods (SuGaR, Adaptive Shells) could not achieve simultaneously at real-time speeds. Key limitations include the piecewise-linear deformation model, larger model size due to barycentric coordinates and mesh storage, and the fact that on well-initialized real scenes (Mip-NeRF 360) the constrained representation is slightly worse than vanilla 3DGS. Open questions include whether physics-based deformation, compression of the barycentric parameterization, and dedicated CUDA kernels for culling+rasterization can close the remaining gaps and extend the approach to large-scale or dynamic scenes.
