## Problem

Conventional indirect time-of-flight (I-ToF) cameras do not account for scene or camera motion during capture, causing motion artifacts in depth and intensity estimates for dynamic scenes. Reducing integration time to suppress artifacts introduces a fundamental noise-vs-motion tradeoff, and no existing single-camera method can simultaneously recover artifact-free 3D geometry, intensity, and dense 3D motion (both lateral and axial) in dynamic scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequential sets of raw I-ToF correlation images captured with N demodulation phase shifts at two distinct high modulation frequencies (e.g., 40 MHz and 50 MHz), with short per-frame integration times (2–3 ms) to satisfy the small-linear-motion constraint. It produces a motion-artifact-free depth map, an intensity image, and a dense 3D motion field (XY lateral flow plus Z axial velocity) per pixel. The hardware prototype operates at 240×320 resolution using a Chronoptics KeaB camera, and the approach is validated in both simulated (POVray, CARLA) and real indoor/outdoor scenes.

## Method

The core insight (Observation 1) is that under small linear motion, the spatial gradient of the intensity image decoded from each correlation image set preserves brightness along the true XY-motion, even though individual correlation images do not satisfy brightness constancy. This enables standard optical flow (RAFT) to estimate dense XY-motion on intensity gradients, after which Z-motion is derived from the depth difference between the two aligned correlation image sets. To overcome the low SNR imposed by short integration times, the method integrates two complementary strategies: a multi-frequency coding scheme using two high modulation frequencies to improve depth precision, and burst denoising that aligns and merges correlation images sharing the same demodulation phase and frequency to computationally extend the effective integration time. The result is a unified pipeline that recovers high-quality depth, intensity, and 3D motion from a single I-ToF camera without requiring simultaneously captured out-of-phase images.

## Results

The proposed multi-frequency I-ToF burst imaging method achieves motion artifact-free depth and intensity estimates using only 2 ms (indoor) and 3 ms (outdoor) integration times, while simultaneously recovering dense 3D motion. For Z-motion estimation, the depth-difference approach yields a velocity standard deviation (σ_v_ΔZ) approximately 40× lower than Doppler ToF (σ_v_Δf) under practical conditions (f₀ = 10 MHz, T = 5 ms, Δt = 40 ms, Z = 1 m). In depth estimation, the hybrid multi-frequency + burst denoising approach maintains high inlier fractions (percent of pixels within 0.5%, 1%, and 2% of true depth) even under extremely low SNR where multi-frequency coding alone fails to decode correct depths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three main baselines. Against conventional I-ToF imaging, the proposed method eliminates both motion artifacts (present with long integration times) and noise (present with short integration times), recovering high-quality depth and intensity for dynamic indoor and outdoor scenes. Against Doppler ToF [Heide et al., 2015] for axial (Z) motion estimation, the depth-difference approach achieves ~40× lower velocity standard deviation and reliably resolves small axial motions (~4 m/s) where Doppler ToF fails because the corresponding frequency shift (<1 Hz) is negligibly small relative to the MHz-range modulation frequency. Against multi-frequency coding alone, the addition of burst denoising prevents decoding failures under extremely low SNR by reducing noise in interim depth estimates. Against burst denoising alone, multi-frequency coding provides higher effective modulation frequency and thus better depth precision. The combination of both is complementary and outperforms either method in isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-frequency I-ToF coding (Jongenelen et al.) by using two high frequencies to recover two separate depth maps for Z-motion estimation, rather than a single unambiguous depth map. It adapts the burst denoising paradigm from RGB photography (Hasinoff et al.) to I-ToF correlation images, aligning and merging frames with matching demodulation phase and modulation frequency to computationally extend integration time. Conceptually, it departs from prior motion-artifact-reduction methods that require simultaneous out-of-phase correlation images, and from Doppler ToF for axial velocity, by introducing a gradient-based brightness-preservation observation that enables standard optical flow on brightness-varying coded images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously recover 3D geometry, intensity, and dense 3D motion from a single I-ToF camera in dynamic scenes, addressing the long-standing noise-versus-motion tradeoff that has limited ToF cameras in robotics, AR, and autonomous driving. Key limitations include strong dependence on the quality of 2D optical flow (which uses only intensity gradients, not geometry), and the prototype's modest 240×320 resolution and limited source power. Open questions include whether joint geometry-and-intensity motion estimation can further improve accuracy, and whether the approach scales to the >1-megapixel I-ToF sensors now emerging commercially.
