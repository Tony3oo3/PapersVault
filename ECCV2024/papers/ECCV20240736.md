## Problem

SeiT demonstrated that VQ token sequences can replace pixel images for 99% storage reduction in vision training, but it was restricted to fully supervised learning and offered limited token-specific augmentations. Since self-supervised methods (which unlock unlabeled data) demand more data due to the absence of labels, and standard pixel-based augmentations fail in the token domain due to spatial information collapse and inter-token dependencies, a token-compatible self-supervised pre-training strategy with effective augmentation was missing.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted VQ token sequences (each image compressed from H×W×3 pixels to a 1D sequence of codebook indices) stored on disk before training begins, so no pixel images are loaded during the training loop. During MTM pre-training, the output is the predicted codebook indices for masked token positions (optimized via cross-entropy); during downstream fine-tuning, the same ViT backbone produces class labels (ImageNet-1k, fine-grained datasets) or per-pixel segmentation masks (ADE-20k). The framework also generalizes to alternative discrete inputs such as DCT coefficients extracted from 8×8 patches.

## Method

SeiT++ introduces three components on top of SeiT's token-based ViT training. First, Masked Token Modeling (MTM) performs self-supervised pre-training by randomly masking a variable fraction of tokens (truncated-normal ratio), feeding visible token embeddings into a ViT encoder, padding with learned mask tokens, and training a lightweight decoder to reconstruct the original codebook indices via cross-entropy on masked positions only. Second, TokenAdapt learns a pair of lightweight transformer-based functions (f and g) that map token embeddings into a pixel-compatible feature space, apply standard geometric augmentations (flip, resize, crop, Mixup), and map back to the token embedding space before re-quantization, enabling direct reuse of well-established pixel augmentation policies. Third, ColorAdapt applies an AdaIN-style normalization to token embeddings—replacing per-channel mean and standard deviation with those of a reference token sequence—to simulate color changes while preserving encoded object structure. Together, these strategies yield consistent gains across storage-efficient classification, fine-grained recognition, semantic segmentation, and robustness benchmarks.

## Results

On storage-efficient ImageNet-1k classification with ViT-B/16, SeiT++ achieves 77.8% top-1 accuracy using only 1.4 GB of tokenized data (1% of the 140 GB full-pixel dataset), outperforming the closest image-based baseline (JPEG quality 5 at 74.6% / 11 GB) by 3.2 points while using 8× less storage. On ADE-20k semantic segmentation, SeiT++ improves mIoU from 39.0 to 43.2 (+4.2) over SeiT. On fine-grained classification, SeiT++ gains up to +9.3 points (iNat-18) over SeiT, and on robustness benchmarks it improves Gaussian noise accuracy by +7.9 points (50.7→58.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeiT++ (77.8%) outperforms the direct predecessor SeiT (74.0%) by 3.8 points at identical 1.4 GB storage, with the gap widening to 6.7 points at 0.3 GB (60.6 vs 53.9). Against image-based storage-reduction baselines, SeiT++ dominates: it beats C-score sampling [29] (73.3%) by 4.5 points while using 38× less storage (1.4 GB vs 53.3 GB), and surpasses SRe2L [65] (65.9%) and G-VBSM [52] (63.7%) by 11.9 and 14.1 points respectively at comparable or lower storage. Even against the strongest image-compression baselines (JPEG q=5 at 74.6% / 11 GB and resolution ×0.2 at 75.2% / 9.6 GB), SeiT++ leads by 2.6–3.2 points while requiring 7–8× less storage. The gains are consistent across all evaluated storage budgets (0.3–1.4 GB) and transfer to fine-grained, segmentation, and robustness tasks, confirming that the improvement is not an artifact of a single dataset or metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends SeiT, which first demonstrated that ViTs can be trained from offline VQ tokens (ViT-VQGAN) at 1% of pixel storage. The key departure is introducing Masked Token Modeling (MTM) for self-supervised pre-training, analogous to how MAE extends supervised ViT training to a label-free regime, but operating entirely in token space rather than pixel space. The TokenAdapt and ColorAdapt augmentations address a gap identified in the token domain: standard pixel-based augmentations (flip, RRC, Mixup) cause spatial collapse and inter-token artifacts when naively applied to token embeddings, a problem not explored in prior token-based training work. ColorAdapt draws on AdaIN's statistical reparameterization, while TokenAdapt introduces a learned conversion-reversion pipeline to bridge pixel-space augmentation policies into token space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Storage is a critical bottleneck for scaling vision models (LAION-5B requires 240TB), and this work shows that combining self-supervised pre-training with purpose-built token augmentations can push token-based training from 90% to ~95% of full-pixel performance at the same 1% storage budget. The finding that robustness gains (+7.9 on Gaussian noise, +4.7 on ImageNet-R) substantially exceed clean-accuracy gains (+1.5) suggests token augmentations specifically regularize against distribution shifts, which is practically valuable. However, the paper acknowledges it has not validated the approach on truly large-scale datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21k) or very large models, leaving open whether the storage-efficiency advantage and augmentation benefits persist at scale. The generalizability across tokenizers and DCT coefficients is promising but tested only on ImageNet-100.
