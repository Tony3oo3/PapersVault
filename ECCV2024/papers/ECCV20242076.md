## Problem

Fast adversarial training (FastAT) methods that mitigate catastrophic overfitting (CO) at small perturbation budgets (ε≤8/255) still suffer severe robustness collapse at larger ε (e.g., 16/255), where adversarial accuracy can drop to near zero within a few epochs. The paper identifies that CO is accompanied by abrupt, irregular fluctuations in adversarial loss convergence, pointing to training instability as the root cause rather than a purely regularization deficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242076/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 288×288 for ImageNet) and outputs class labels. Adversarial examples are generated via a single-step FGSM perturbation (or prior-guided initialization) within an L∞ ball of radius ε=16/255, and the trained model is evaluated against a battery of attacks (FGSM, PGD-10/20/50, C&W, APGD-CE, AutoAttack) at the same or varying perturbation levels.

## Method

RAT applies the Douglas-Rachford splitting technique to the adversarial training loop, decomposing each parameter update into a 'reflection step' (gradient descent on the adversarial loss) and an 'enforcement step' (a regularization pull toward a target computed from epoch-to-epoch loss differences for both adversarial and benign inputs, weighted by γ₁ and γ₂). The final update is the midpoint θ = 0.5·(θ_ref + θ_enf), which enforces bounded per-epoch loss changes and prevents the sudden adversarial-loss collapse that triggers CO. The regularization term Reg(θ) = γ₁·ΔL_adv + γ₂·ΔL_benign is computed from batch-averaged losses to avoid per-sample memory overhead, and the entire procedure adds only ~15 minutes of overhead over vanilla FGSM training. RAT is plug-and-play with existing FastAT attack initializations (random noise from Fast-AT or prior-guided from MEP-PGI), and a hyperparameter λ∈[0,1] controls the strength of the enforcement step, with λ=0.3 chosen as the default balance between CO prevention and model performance.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet20 at ε=16/255, RAT-MEP achieves 23.91% AutoAttack accuracy and 33.64% PGD-50 robust accuracy, approaching the full PGD-AT baseline (26.85% AA, 34.21% PGD-50) while training in 1.7h vs. 6.3h. On CIFAR-100, RAT-MEP reaches 11.49% AA accuracy, outperforming all FastAT baselines (best competitor: NuAT at 8.75%). On Tiny-ImageNet, RAT-BP achieves 6.25% AA accuracy, surpassing PGI-BP (4.18%) and all other FastAT methods, while remaining ~4.8× faster than PGD-AT (14.2h vs. 68.1h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAT-MEP outperforms all FastAT baselines on CIFAR-10 at ε=16/255: it beats Fast-AT by +8.94% AA (23.91 vs. 14.97), Fast-GA by +6.35% (23.91 vs. 17.56), NuAT by +4.98% (23.91 vs. 18.93), ZeroGrad by +11.16% (23.91 vs. 12.75), and MEP-PGI by +4.60% (23.91 vs. 19.31). Against the full PGD-AT baseline, RAT-MEP closes the gap to within 2.94% AA (23.91 vs. 26.85) and 0.57% PGD-50 (33.64 vs. 34.21) while training 3.7× faster (1.7h vs. 6.3h). On CIFAR-100, RAT-MEP leads all FastAT methods by a wide margin (11.49% AA vs. NuAT's 8.75% and Fast-AT's 7.21%), though it remains slightly below AT PGD (12.54%). On Tiny-ImageNet, RAT-BP surpasses PGI-BP by +2.07% AA (6.25 vs. 4.18) and approaches AT PGD (6.47%) at 4.8× lower training cost. The RAT-AT variant (without MEP-PGI initialization) also consistently outperforms its base Fast-AT by +4.67% AA on CIFAR-10, confirming the DR splitting contribution is independent of the attack initialization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FastAT (Wong et al., 2020) and MEP-PGI (Jia et al., 2022) as its base adversarial training frameworks, wrapping them in a Douglas-Rachford splitting scheme borrowed from convex/non-convex optimization. It departs from the dominant regularization-based line of work (Fast-GA, NuAT, ZeroGrad, LLR, CURE) that combats catastrophic overfitting by adding penalty terms to the loss, instead reframing CO as a training-dynamics stability problem solvable through structured parameter updates. The DR splitting provides a two-step 'reflection + enforcement' update that balances adversarial loss reduction with epoch-level loss-difference constraints, avoiding the double-backpropagation cost that plagues curvature-regularization methods like LLR and CURE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that existing FastAT methods (Fast-GA, NuAT, MEP-PGI) still suffer significant CO at E=16/255, a practically relevant regime where robustness drops by 20+ points compared to E=8/255. The DR splitting approach offers a computationally cheap (no double backprop) mechanism to stabilize training, with RAT-MEP closing much of the gap to full PGD-AT (33.64% vs 34.21% on PGD-50, CIFAR10) at roughly 3.5× less training time. Open questions include whether the hyperparameters (λ, γ₁, γ₂) transfer cleanly to new architectures or even larger perturbation budgets, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-step or free-AT settings beyond single-step FGSM.
