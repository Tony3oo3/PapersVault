## Problem

Existing prototypical neural networks learn prototypes as single points in latent space and rely on indirect interpretation functions (autoencoders, prototype projection, nearest neighbors) to visualize them, creating a persistent human-machine semantic gap. A single point cannot convey what the model actually represents—color, shape, texture, or something else entirely—and indirect mappings are either approximate or lossy. The paper asks whether learning prototypes as full distributions over an invertibly mapped latent space can close this gap while maintaining competitive predictive performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images at resolutions up to 64×64 (constrained by the pre-trained DenseFlow backbone) and simultaneously produces a class label, an exact likelihood under the learned data distribution, and interpretable outputs: prototype distribution visualizations obtained by applying the inverse flow to latent samples, generated images, and part-level heatmaps highlighting the most influential image regions per prototype. Unlike standard classifiers, the invertible architecture means every latent point has a unique, exact data-space counterpart, so prototype visualizations are faithful rather than approximate reconstructions.

## Method

ProtoFlow composes a DenseFlow normalizing flow backbone (initialized via transfer learning from ImageNet) with per-class Gaussian mixture models in the latent space, where each class c has K prototypical distributions parameterized by means, diagonal covariances, and mixture weights. Classification is performed by evaluating the class-conditional latent likelihood p_Z(z|y) at the flow-encoded input, and the inverse flow f⁻¹ provides an exact, constraint-free interpretation mapping from latent prototypes back to data space. Training maximizes cross-entropy with two auxiliary terms: a consistency regularization loss (adapted from Izmailov et al.) that penalizes disagreement under data perturbations, and a diversity loss based on a dimension-rescaled Hellinger distance that penalizes overlap between prototypical distributions within each class. GMM means are initialized via K-means on class-conditional embeddings, and uninformative prototypes are pruned post-hoc using Otsu's threshold on mixture weights.

## Results

ProtoFlow achieves 91.54% accuracy on CIFAR-10, outperforming all flow-based baselines (IB-INN: 91.28%, FlowGMM: 88.44%, Fetaya et al.: 84.00%) and all prototypical networks (ProtoPNet: 84.9%, KMEx: 85.3%). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 69.80% accuracy with 5.03 BPD, surpassing IB-INN (66.22% / 18.4 BPD). Calibration is strong across all datasets, with ECE of 0.083 on CIFAR-10 and 0.292 on CIFAR-100, far below IB-INN's 0.81 and 0.62 respectively. ProtoFlow also establishes baselines on Flowers-102 (59.80%) and Oxford-IIIT Pet (53.58%), where no prior joint generative/predictive flow-based results existed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against flow-based generative classifiers, ProtoFlow wins on CIFAR-10 (91.54% vs. IB-INN's 91.28% and FlowGMM's 88.44%) and CIFAR-100 (69.80% vs. IB-INN's 66.22%), while achieving substantially better BPD (3.95 vs. IB-INN's 17.3) and ECE (0.083 vs. 0.81). On MNIST, FlowGMM edges out ProtoFlow in accuracy (99.63% vs. 99.36%) and ECE (0.004 vs. 0.006), but ProtoFlow reports a BPD of 0.535 where FlowGMM does not. Against prototypical networks (ProtoPNet, KMEx, FLINT, ProtoVAE), ProtoFlow leads by 6–7% on CIFAR-10 (91.54% vs. 84.6–85.3%) while being the only method that also provides exact generative likelihoods. The SCNF baselines [4] are decisively outperformed on MNIST (99.36% vs. 88.44%/83.10%). ProtoFlow is the only method combining both prototypical interpretability and flow-based invertibility, a combination no baseline offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProtoFlow bridges two previously separate research threads: prototypical neural networks (ProtoPNet, ProtoSegNet, MGProto) that learn interpretable prototypes as points or simple distributions in latent space, and normalizing-flow-based joint generative/predictive classifiers (Fetaya et al., Izmailov et al., Ardizzone et al.) that combine density estimation with classification. The key conceptual departure is replacing point-based prototypes with full Gaussian mixture distributions over the latent space, while using an invertible DenseFlow backbone to provide an exact, faithful inverse mapping from latent prototypes back to data-space visualizations—eliminating the need for indirect interpretation functions like prototype projection or nearest-neighbor lookup. The consistency regularization loss is adapted from Izmailov et al., and a novel dimensionality-rescaled Hellinger diversity loss is introduced to reduce prototype overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProtoFlow addresses a persistent weakness in prototypical networks—the human-machine semantic gap—by making prototype visualization exact rather than approximate, which is a meaningful step toward intrinsically explainable classification. However, the finding that ~90% of prototypes can be pruned with negligible accuracy loss, combined with low diversity scores, suggests the model is learning substantial redundancy, raising open questions about principled selection of the number of prototypical distributions. Practical impact is currently limited by the 64×64 resolution ceiling inherited from pre-trained DenseFlow weights and a generalization gap on harder datasets (Oxford-IIIT Pet, Flowers-102). Future work on semi-supervised extension, higher-resolution backbones, and information-theoretic prototype selection could broaden applicability.
