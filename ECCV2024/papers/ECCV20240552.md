## Problem

Existing machine unlearning (MU) evaluations rely predominantly on randomly selected forget sets, which introduce high performance variance and fail to expose the worst-case unlearning difficulty of a given method. This paper asks: how can we systematically identify the data subset that is hardest to unlearn, providing a reliable adversarial benchmark for assessing MU robustness?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240552/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained model (ResNet-18/50, VGG, or Stable Diffusion), the full training dataset, and a forget set size m (e.g., 1%–20% of data). It produces a continuous forget score w ∈ [0,1]^N (relaxed from binary) whose top-m entries define the worst-case forget set and whose bottom-m entries identify the easiest-to-unlearn points. The relaxed formulation also yields a closed-form projection onto the simplex constraint 1ᵀw = m, enabling efficient optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a bi-level optimization (BLO) formulation where the upper level optimizes a data-selection variable w to maximize the training loss on the forget set (i.e., make unlearning hardest), while the lower level performs standard fine-tuning-based unlearning. The key technical insight is that choosing signSGD as the lower-level optimizer collapses the implicit gradient to zero (since d(sign(x))/dx = 0 almost surely), reducing the BLO to a purely first-order alternating scheme: projected gradient descent (PGD) on w at the upper level and K-step signSGD at the lower level. The upper-level objective also includes an ℓ₂ regularizer on w to encourage sparsity and stabilize optimization. The framework extends naturally to class-wise forgetting (w over classes) and prompt-wise forgetting in text-to-image generation (w over text prompts), requiring only a reinterpretation of the selection variable.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 at 10% forgetting ratio, Retrain (exact unlearning) under the worst-case forget set achieves UA=0.00% and MIA=0.00%, confirming that even retraining from scratch struggles to erase the influence of the identified worst-case data. Among approximate methods, CF-k achieves the smallest average performance gap vs. Retrain (0.08) under worst-case, followed by SCRUB (0.82) and SalUn (1.51). In stark contrast, relabeling-based methods BE and BS show average gaps of 34.70 and 37.22 respectively, with MIA scores of 81.45% and 74.88%—indicating they fail to truly unlearn the worst-case data. The worst-case evaluation also dramatically reduces variance: SCRUB's UA standard deviation drops from 19.82 (random) to 0.01 (worst-case).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 9 unlearning methods (1 exact + 8 approximate) under both random and worst-case forget sets. CF-k (catastrophic forgetting of last k layers) is the strongest approximate method under worst-case, with an average gap of only 0.08 vs. Retrain, matching its UA and MIA near 0%. SCRUB and SalUn also perform well under worst-case (gaps 0.82 and 1.51), though SCRUB exhibits extreme variance under random forgetting (UA std=19.82) that vanishes under worst-case (std=0.01). FT and ℓ₁-sparse show moderate gaps (1.37 and 1.72), tracking Retrain's trend but with slightly degraded utility (TA drops of ~3%). EU-k has a larger gap (3.28) primarily due to a 5.02% MIA residual. In sharp contrast, relabeling-based methods fail catastrophically under worst-case: BE (gap 34.70) and BS (gap 37.22) show MIA scores of 81.45% and 74.88%, meaning the model still 'remembers' the forgotten data, while RL (gap 24.88) and SalUn (gap 1.51) diverge—SalUn's relabeling strategy partially works but still shows a 96.43% MIA. The key finding is that worst-case evaluation exposes a clear divide: relabeling-free methods degrade gracefully (mimicking Retrain), while most relabeling-based methods produce a false sense of security by inflating MIA scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes bi-level optimization (BLO), previously applied to coreset selection and dataset pruning (Borsos et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2022), to the machine unlearning (MU) evaluation context, where it had not been used. It departs from the dominant random-forgetting evaluation paradigm in MU by formulating worst-case forget set identification as an adversarial data-selection problem, and introduces a signSGD-based gradient unrolling trick that collapses the implicit gradient to zero, making the BLO tractable without Hessian computations. The evaluation metrics (UA, MIA, RA, TA) and the suite of approximate unlearning baselines (FT, EU-k, CF-k, SCRUB, ℓ₁-sparse, RL, BE, BS, SalUn) are drawn directly from the existing MU literature, positioning the contribution as a new evaluative lens rather than a new unlearning algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical blind spot in MU evaluation: random forgetting masks the worst-case performance of unlearning methods, and relabeling-based approaches (BE, BS) in particular produce misleadingly high MIA scores (74–96%) under worst-case sets, suggesting they 'unlearn' by relabeling rather than erasing data influence. The coreset connection—that the complement of the worst-case forget set approximates a state-of-the-art coreset—offers an interpretable data-attribution signal and hints at curriculum-based unlearning strategies. Open questions include whether the BLO framework scales to LLM unlearning, whether the signSGD lower-level solver is the right trade-off for very deep models, and whether 'exact' unlearning via full retraining remains a meaningful gold standard when even Retrain achieves near-zero UA on worst-case sets.
