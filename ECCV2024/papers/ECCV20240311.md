## Problem

Photometric stereo requires multiple calibrated images under controlled lighting, making data acquisition expensive and impractical for real-world use. While single-image relighting methods exist, they often produce perceptually plausible but physically incorrect results, and none have been validated through photometric stereo benchmarks. The paper asks whether a single image can be relit with sufficient physical fidelity to substitute for the multi-image acquisition that photometric stereo demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240311/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (multiplied by a binary object mask) and a target lighting specification — either a 3×1 point-light direction vector, a 9×1 spherical-harmonic coefficient vector per channel for environment lighting, or both concatenated. It jointly outputs four spatially varying BRDF parameter maps (diffuse albedo, surface normal, depth, and specular roughness) plus a relit image under the target lighting. The relit images are then fed as multi-image input to standard photometric stereo solvers (Fast-NFPS, SDM-UniPS) to recover surface normals, closing the loop from single image to photometric stereo.

## Method

MERLiN is a single-stage attention-guided hourglass CNN with one shared encoder and two decoders (material and relighting) connected by gated-attention skip connections that filter redundant and noisy features from coarse-scale decoder signals. A differentiable microfacet BRDF rendering layer reconstructs the input image from estimated parameters, providing physics-based supervision. A separate global illumination network, trained end-to-end alongside the material decoder, predicts a residual image capturing higher-order light bounces, which is added to the direct-illumination render. The relighting decoder receives encoder features, material-decoder features, and an MLP-encoded lighting vector, with bidirectional feature sharing between the two decoders enabling mutual supervision. The network is trained on a large synthetic dataset with global illumination using L2 losses on all svBRDF parameters, image reconstruction, and relighting, with gradient-based L2 loss on the roughness map to prevent over-smoothing.

## Results

On the Li et al. [22] synthetic test set, MERLiN achieves the best svBRDF estimation across all four parameters: albedo MSE 3.787×10⁻², roughness MSE 8.267×10⁻², normal MSE 3.311×10⁻², and depth MSE 0.975×10⁻², along with the highest relighting SSIM of 0.894. The roughness estimation improvement is particularly dramatic (8.267 vs. 19.431 for Li et al. and 12.781 for Sang et al.). For photometric stereo validation on LUCES, MERLiN-relit images yield an average normal MAE of 15.80°, outperforming both baselines (16.21° and 16.43°) and the single-image estimate (17.06°), though still trailing real-image photometric stereo (14.11°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERLiN outperforms both baselines on every reported metric. Against Li et al. [22] (cascaded CNN with separately trained global illumination), MERLiN reduces roughness MSE by 57% (8.267 vs. 19.431), depth MSE by 35% (0.975 vs. 1.505), and albedo MSE by 22% (3.787 vs. 4.868), while also achieving higher relighting SSIM (0.894 vs. 0.884). Against Sang et al. [34] (single-shot joint svBRDF + relighting without explicit global illumination), the gains are more modest on albedo (3.787 vs. 3.856) and normal (3.311 vs. 3.459) but substantial on roughness (8.267 vs. 12.781, a 35% reduction) and depth (0.975 vs. 1.471, a 34% reduction). In the photometric stereo validation on LUCES (Table 3), MERLiN-relit images produce the lowest average MAE (15.80°) among all relighting methods, beating Li et al. (16.21°) and Sang et al. (16.43°), and notably improving over the single-image normal estimate (17.06°) by 7.7%. The remaining gap to real-image photometric stereo (14.11°) is largest on complex objects like House (36.91° vs. 31.00°) and Queen (20.18° vs. 18.39°), where indirect illumination and cast shadows are most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

MERLiN extends the single-image svBRDF estimation and relighting paradigm established by Li et al. [22] and Sang et al. [34], but departs from both in two key ways: it replaces their cascaded network designs with a single-stage hourglass architecture using attention-gated feature sharing, and it trains the global illumination module end-to-end jointly with material estimation rather than as a separately trained component. The paper also introduces a novel validation strategy—using photometric stereo benchmarks (Fast-NFPS, SDM-UniPS) on relit images to assess physical correctness—which, as the authors note, has not been demonstrated in prior relighting work. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a bridge between shape-from-shading (single image) and classical photometric stereo (multi-image), leveraging deep relighting to synthesize the multi-light inputs that photometric stereo traditionally requires.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical pathway toward reducing the data acquisition burden in photometric stereo: a single image can be relit into multiple lighting conditions sufficient for normal estimation, with MAE (15.80°) approaching but not matching real multi-image results (14.11°). The remaining ~12% gap and the observation that SDM-UniPS is distracted by image semantics (e.g., a cherry picture on a can) highlight that physical correctness of relit images is still imperfect, particularly for complex geometries with cast shadows and inter-reflections. Open questions include whether the gap can be closed with better global illumination modeling (the current residual approach only approximates inter-reflections in image space), whether the approach generalizes to dynamic surfaces as the authors speculate, and whether the single-decoder design's slight albedo underperformance relative to four separate decoders becomes problematic for downstream applications.
