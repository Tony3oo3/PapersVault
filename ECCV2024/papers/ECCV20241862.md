## Problem

Fixed-factor annealing in Graduated Non-Convexity (GNC) forces a poor tradeoff between accuracy (small annealing factor, many stages) and speed (large factor, few stages), while the adaptive annealing scheme of Chitturi & Govindu (CVPR 2023) requires repeatedly computing the minimum eigenvalue of the full Hessian, an O(N_v³) operation that is intractable for averaging problems with hundreds to thousands of variables. This paper bridges that gap by making adaptive annealing scalable to large-scale graph averaging.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse weighted graph G=(V,E) with pairwise relative observations z_ij on each edge (e.g., 3D displacement vectors for vector averaging, unit translation directions for translation averaging) and produces absolute node values x_i (spatial locations or camera translations). In translation averaging, additional scalar edge variables d_ij (displacements) are jointly estimated under the constraint d_ij ≥ 1, and the sum of all translations is constrained to zero. The graph is assumed sparse (M ≪ N²), which is critical for the method's efficiency.

## Method

The key insight is that the Hessian of the robust averaging cost decomposes as a sum of edge Laplacian matrices, each involving only a small q×q weight matrix W_ij(σ) (q=3 in 3D). Rather than tracking positive-definiteness of the full qN×qN Hessian, the method tracks the minimum eigenvalue of each per-edge W_ij, reducing the per-stage cost from O(N³q³) to O(Mc(q)) where c(q) ≪ q³. At each GNC stage, σ_{k+1} is set to the p-th percentile of the per-edge critical values σ_ij (where λ_min(W_ij)=0), starting at p=100% and decreasing toward 50%, with occasional verification of the full Hessian's sign via a power-iteration trick on H−cI (using the Gershgorin bound and the known zero-eigenvalue/ones-eigenvector property of Laplacians). For translation averaging, the edge variables are handled by a Schur-complement reduction that preserves the Laplacian structure of the reduced Hessian, allowing the same per-edge strategy to apply directly.

## Results

On synthetic vector averaging (N=1000, β=0.02), GNCp achieves 1.37 mean error at 50% outlier fraction, compared to 93.83 (IRLS), 13.99 (GNC δ↓), and 86.26 (GNC δ↑). On real-world 1DSfM translation averaging datasets, GNCp-LUD outperforms all SOTA baselines (LUD, 1DSfM, ShapeFit, BATA) in 9 of 15 datasets by mean error and 11 of 15 by median error. For example, on the Alamo dataset (N=586), GNCp-LUD achieves 1.9 m mean error versus 2.7 m (LUD) and 2.0 m (BATA), while on Notre Dame (N=552) it reaches 1.1 m versus 1.2 m (LUD) and 1.0 m (BATA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three annealing baselines on vector averaging, GNCp matches or beats GNC(δ↓) in accuracy (e.g., 0.44 vs 0.46 median at 30% outliers) while requiring far fewer GNC stages, and dramatically outperforms both IRLS and GNC(δ↑) at high outlier fractions (at 50% outliers: 1.37 vs 93.83 and 86.26 mean error). For translation averaging on real data, GNCp-LUD outperforms LUD [30] in 12/15 datasets by mean error, confirming that adaptive annealing of the LUD cost is beneficial. It also substantially outperforms 1DSfM [43] (which suffers errors in the thousands of metres on most datasets) and ShapeFit [19] (which degrades severely on large scenes like Trafalgar at 53.1 m). GNCp-LUD beats BATA [48] in 10/15 datasets by mean error, with the notable exception of Trafalgar where BATA achieves 7.9 m versus GNCp-LUD's 10.4 m — a case where even fixed-factor annealing fails. Overall, the adaptive strategy provides the best accuracy–efficiency tradeoff: it avoids the poor local minima that trap IRLS and GNC(δ↑), while being significantly faster than the many-stage GNC(δ↓) approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the adaptive annealing framework of Sidhartha et al. [38] (CVPR 2023), which tracked Hessian positive-definiteness to choose GNC annealing steps but was intractable for large problems due to O(N_v^3) eigenvalue computation. The key departure is the observation that in graph averaging problems, the Hessian decomposes into a sum of edge Laplacian matrices, reducing the global eigenvalue check to per-edge computations on small q×q weight matrices (q=3 for 3D). This transforms the annealing complexity from O(N^3 q^3) to O(M·c(q)), making principled adaptive GNC feasible for SfM-scale problems with hundreds to thousands of nodes. The method also builds on the LUD [30] formulation for translation averaging and the broader GNC/M-estimation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making adaptive GNC scalable to large averaging problems, this work removes a practical barrier to using principled annealing in SfM and sensor localization pipelines where fixed-factor schedules force a poor accuracy-efficiency tradeoff. The Laplacian-structure insight is general and applies to any averaging problem satisfying Assumption 1 (vector averaging, MDS, translation averaging), offering a reusable template. Limitations include residual dependence on a few λ_min(H) evaluations (mitigated by power iteration tricks) and occasional saddle-point convergence at high outlier fractions. The authors identify extending the framework to non-vector spaces (e.g., SO(3) rotation averaging) as the natural next step, which would broaden applicability to full pose estimation.
