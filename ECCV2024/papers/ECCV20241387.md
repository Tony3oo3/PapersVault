## Problem

Existing VLM-based driving approaches formulate understanding as single-round or single-object VQA, which fails to capture how human drivers reason about multiple objects across multiple logical steps (perception → prediction → planning → action). No prior task, dataset, or benchmark models the graph-structured, multi-object, multi-stage reasoning that underlies real driving decisions, leaving a gap between VLM capabilities and the structured reasoning required for end-to-end autonomous driving.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a low-resolution front-view RGB image plus text context assembled from parent QA nodes in the graph (prepended with a 'Context:' prefix). Outputs are produced in three stages: natural-language QA answers for perception/prediction/planning, a behavior token that discretizes the intended action into one of 25 speed×steering bins (e.g., 'moderate' + 'straight'), and a continuous trajectory of N BEV waypoints whose coordinates are quantized into 256 bins each and emitted as redefined BLIP-2 vocabulary tokens. The graph structure means the number and identity of context QAs varies per frame depending on detected objects and their interactions.

## Method

DriveLM-Agent builds on BLIP-2 fine-tuned with LoRA and introduces a graph prompting scheme where each QA node's question is augmented with the answers of its parent nodes in the DAG, encoding logical dependencies as textual context. The inference pipeline is two-stage: first, the VLM answers P1-3 questions (perception, prediction, planning) using the graph context, then aggregates all P1-3 answers as context to predict a discrete behavior category; second, a separate LoRA head on the same BLIP-2 architecture maps the image plus behavior description to trajectory waypoints via a 256-bin coordinate tokenizer adapted from RT-2. The graph has two edge types—object-level edges capturing inter-object interactions and task-level edges encoding the perception→prediction→planning→behavior→motion chain. Training data (DriveLM-nuScenes with semi-rule-based human annotation, DriveLM-CARLA with a fully rule-based PDM-Lite expert) provides ~1.6M QA pairs organized in this graph structure, enabling supervised fine-tuning on the full reasoning chain rather than a single image-to-trajectory mapping.

## Results

On DriveLM-nuScenes open-loop planning, DriveLM-Agent (Graph) achieves ADE 1.74 and collision rate 1.89%, substantially outperforming UniAD-Single (ADE 2.63, Col. 2.77%) and BLIP-RT-2 (ADE 2.63, Col. 2.77%). In the zero-shot Waymo generalization setting, DriveLM-Agent (Graph) achieves ADE 2.63 and Col. 6.17%, beating UniAD-Single (ADE 2.78, Col. 6.47%) and BLIP-RT-2 (ADE 2.78, Col. 6.47%), with speed prediction accuracy rising from 43.90% (no context) to 54.29% (full graph). The full video-based UniAD still leads on nuScenes (ADE 0.80, Col. 0.17%), but DriveLM-Agent is competitive despite using only a single front-view image and a task-agnostic VLM architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniAD-Single (single-frame, no video), DriveLM-Agent (Graph) wins on both nuScenes (ADE 1.74 vs. 2.63) and Waymo zero-shot (ADE 2.63 vs. 2.78), demonstrating that a VLM with graph-structured reasoning can match or exceed a purpose-built driving architecture. Against BLIP-RT-2 (same BLIP-2 backbone but no VQA context, only trajectory tokenization), DriveLM-Agent consistently outperforms it on both datasets, confirming that the multi-stage P1-3 reasoning provides meaningful gains over direct image-to-trajectory mapping. The graph context specifically benefits zero-shot generalization: on Waymo, speed accuracy jumps from 43.90% (no context) to 54.29% (full graph), while the chain variant (only P3 context) actually underperforms the no-context baseline (41.28%), indicating that the full object-level and task-level dependencies in the graph are necessary for cross-domain transfer. DriveLM-Agent still trails the full video-based UniAD (ADE 0.80, Col. 0.17% on nuScenes), which the authors attribute to the lack of temporal and 360° sensor inputs in the VLM pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on BLIP-2 as a general-purpose VLM backbone and adapts the trajectory tokenization strategy from RT-2 (originally for robotic control) to driving waypoints. It departs from prior language-grounded driving work (GPT-Driver, LLM-Driver, DriveGPT4) that either encodes scene state into flat prompts or uses single-object QA chains, by introducing Graph VQA with logical dependencies at both the object-interaction level and the perception→prediction→planning→behavior→motion task level. It also contrasts with driving-specific end-to-end architectures like UniAD and TransFuser that rely on BEV representations and task-specific heads, positioning a general VLM with graph-structured prompting as a viable alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DriveLM introduces a principled proxy task for human multi-step driving reasoning and provides the largest driving-language benchmark to date (1.6M QAs in CARLA), establishing a foundation for future work on VLM-based driving agents. The zero-shot sensor-configuration transfer (nuScenes→Waymo) where graph context specifically improves generalization is a promising signal, though the open-loop evaluation, single front-view input, and 10× inference cost relative to UniAD limit near-term practical impact. Key open questions include whether the graph-structured reasoning advantage persists in closed-loop settings, whether multi-view and LiDAR inputs can be incorporated, and whether the efficiency gap can be closed as LLM inference accelerates.
