## Problem

Single-view point cloud observations in cluttered scenes suffer from self- and inter-object occlusions, leaving gaps in the perceived object shape and causing grasp failures due to collision or inaccurate pose estimation. Existing scene completion methods are either computationally prohibitive for full-scene voxel prediction or focus on single-object completion that is not jointly optimized with the grasping objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241966/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view point cloud (P ∈ R^{3×N}) from a depth camera and produces top-ranked 6-DoF grasp poses parameterized by grasp point, direction, in-plane rotation, depth, and width, along with a local occupancy map. Candidate grasp points (1,024) are sampled via affordance segmentation, and occupancy is predicted only within cylindrical local regions (radius 0.05 m, depth −0.01 to 0.04 m, voxel size 0.01 m) around each grasp point rather than over the full scene volume.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-group tri-plane representation that captures long-distance scene context by projecting point cloud embeddings and density onto K=3 sets of orthogonal feature planes (64×64 resolution) with rotations uniformly sampled from SO(3) via quaternion Slerp, avoiding expensive 3D volume operations. A local occupancy query fuses bilinearly-interpolated tri-plane features (global context) with nearest-neighbor point cloud embeddings and positional encodings (local context) to predict per-voxel occupancy in each grasp region. Occupancy-enhanced shape features are then extracted by running PointNet++ on predicted occupied voxels and max-pooling sampled implicit features, which feed a grasp direction refinement step and a discrete pose decoder. The entire pipeline—from 3D UNet backbone through tri-plane aggregation, occupancy prediction, and pose decoding—is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss combining binary cross-entropy for occupancy and smooth-L1 terms for affordance, view-affordance, width, and score.

## Results

On the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark, the proposed method achieves an average grasp AP of 53.87 (RealSense) / 45.59 (Kinect), outperforming the strongest baseline GSNet (47.81 / 42.53) by 6.06 / 3.06 AP. Per-split, the method scores 70.88 / 63.41 / 27.31 AP on Seen / Similar / Novel scenes (RealSense), with the largest margin over GSNet on the Similar split (+9.66 AP), attributed to occupancy-enhanced shape features improving generalization to unseen objects. With an additional collision-detection post-processing step, the average AP further improves to 55.56 / 46.89. Local occupancy prediction achieves an average F1-Score of 0.756 and volumetric IOU of 0.642 across all test splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 11 baselines on every split (Seen, Similar, Novel) and both sensor types (RealSense, Kinect). Against the strongest baseline GSNet [42], the gains are +5.18 / +9.66 / +3.33 AP on Seen / Similar / Novel (RealSense), with the Similar split showing the largest improvement because occupancy-enhanced shape features enable better generalization to objects with similar but not identical geometry. Compared to M2T2 [47] (the second-strongest baseline), the method leads by +8.59 / +12.26 / +6.48 AP on the three splits. Older methods such as GPD [31], PointNetGPD [20], and GG-CNN [27] trail by 20–40 AP, reflecting the gap between dense-pose and sparse-grasp-point paradigms. The collision-detection variant (Ours+CD) further extends the lead over GSNet+CD by +6.81 AP on average, confirming that the predicted occupancy map is directly useful for filtering infeasible poses. In real-world grasping with a physical robotic arm, the method achieves a 94.34% success rate versus 87.72% for GSNet, a 6.62% absolute improvement over 50 object attempts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GraspNet-1Billion grasp pose formulation [8] and GSNet's affordance-based grasp point sampling [42] by inserting a local occupancy prediction stage between feature extraction and pose decoding. The key architectural departure is the multi-group tri-plane representation, which generalizes the single-group tri-plane from [13] (originally for autonomous driving occupancy) to K rotationally diverse groups, mitigating the information loss inherent in any single projection. Unlike prior object-completion approaches for grasping [22,23,41] that operate on individual objects and are trained separately, this work formulates shape completion as voxel-level occupancy inference restricted to local grasp regions and jointly optimizes it end-to-end with pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that completing only the small neighborhood around candidate grasp points (≈14K voxels vs. 216K for full-scene methods) recovers most of the benefit of full multi-view shape information (53.87 AP single-view + occupancy vs. 58.78 AP with all 256 views fused), at a fraction of the compute cost. This makes occupancy-enhanced grasping practical for real-time robotic deployment where multi-view capture is infeasible. Limitations remain for severely occluded or highly textured objects, and the approach is validated only on GraspNet-1Billion and a small real-world setup; whether the multi-group tri-plane strategy transfers to larger-scale or multi-view settings is an open question.
