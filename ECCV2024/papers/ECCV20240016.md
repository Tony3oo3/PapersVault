## Problem

Existing X-ray novel view synthesis methods rely on NeRF-based ray tracing, which requires over an hour of training and yields only ~2 fps inference, making them impractical for clinical workflows. While 3D Gaussian Splatting has demonstrated real-time rendering in the RGB domain, it has not been adapted to X-ray imaging because spherical harmonics (which model view-dependent color) are incompatible with the isotropic, view-independent nature of X-ray attenuation, and structure-from-motion initialization fails on low-contrast, overlapping grayscale projections.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 50 grayscale X-ray projections (captured by a circular cone beam scanner over 0–180° with 3% noise via TIGRE) along with scanner hardware parameters (source-to-detector distance L_SD, source-to-object distance L_SO, and azimuth angles φ) that directly determine camera intrinsics and extrinsics without any feature matching. It outputs a novel X-ray projection at an arbitrary view angle and an explicit 3D Gaussian point cloud (positions, covariances, opacities, and feature vectors) that can also be repurposed to generate additional projections for sparse-view CT reconstruction.

## Method

X-Gaussian replaces the spherical harmonics color model of 3DGS with a Radiation Intensity Response Function (RIRF) that computes each Gaussian's radiation intensity as Sigmoid(λ ⊙ f), where f is a learnable per-point feature vector and λ is a fixed set of basis weights, thereby enforcing view-independence consistent with X-ray physics. A custom Differentiable Radiative Rasterization (DRR) CUDA kernel projects the 3D Gaussians onto the detector plane, tiles them, sorts by depth, and blends intensities using the standard alpha-compositing formula, but omits all view-direction-dependent computations from both forward and backward passes. For initialization, the Angle-pose Cuboid Uniform Initialization (ACUI) strategy analytically computes extrinsic and intrinsic matrices from scanner parameters and uniformly samples Gaussian centers on a grid within a bounding cuboid, eliminating the need for SfM. Training minimizes a weighted L1 + SSIM loss over 20,000 iterations with Adam, and the number of Gaussians is adaptively adjusted via the original 3DGS densification/pruning schedule.

## Results

On the novel view synthesis task across five CT scenes (chest, foot, head, abdomen, pancreas), X-Gaussian achieves an average PSNR of 43.40 dB and SSIM of 0.9993, outperforming the best X-ray NeRF baseline NAF (36.94 dB, 0.9627) by 6.5 dB. Inference speed reaches 148 fps versus 2.01 fps for NAF (73× faster), while training time drops to 9 minutes versus 63 minutes for NAF (<15%). On the sparse-view CT reconstruction task, X-Gaussian-assisted FDK, SART, and ASD-POCS yield PSNR improvements of 15.19, 13.01, and 13.53 dB over the no-rendering baseline, exceeding NAF's improvements by 1.32–2.65 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-Gaussian decisively outperforms all five baselines on both PSNR and SSIM across every scene. Against the strongest X-ray-specific baseline NAF [60], X-Gaussian gains 6.5 dB in average PSNR (43.40 vs. 36.94) and 0.037 in SSIM (0.9993 vs. 0.9627), while running 73× faster at inference (148 vs. 2.01 fps) and requiring only ~14% of the training time (9 vs. 63 min). Compared to the SOTA RGB NeRF method TensoRF [9], the PSNR gap widens to 12.93 dB with 192× faster inference. NeAT [42] and NeRF [37] trail NAF by 2.7–6.7 dB in PSNR, confirming that X-ray-specific modeling (attenuation-based rendering) is critical. InTomo [58] is the weakest baseline at 30.19 dB PSNR. On the downstream sparse-view CT reconstruction task, X-Gaussian consistently provides the largest PSNR boost to all three reconstruction algorithms (FDK, SART, ASD-POCS), with margins of 1.32–2.65 dB over NAF, demonstrating that the higher-fidelity novel views translate into better volumetric reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from RGB novel view synthesis to the X-ray domain, departing from the dominant NeRF-based X-ray methods (NAF, InTomo, NeAT) that rely on ray tracing and volume rendering. The key conceptual departure is replacing view-dependent spherical harmonics with a view-independent Radiation Intensity Response Function (RIRF), motivated by the isotropic, penetration-based physics of X-ray imaging as opposed to the reflection-based, anisotropic nature of natural light. Additionally, the Angle-pose Cuboid Uniform Initialization (ACUI) strategy replaces SfM-based initialization, which is ill-suited to grayscale, low-contrast X-ray projections where feature matching degrades.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73× inference speedup (148 fps vs. 2 fps) and <15% training time have direct clinical value by reducing patient and physician wait times and enabling real-time interactive view exploration during diagnosis. The demonstrated improvement in sparse-view CT reconstruction (13–15 dB PSNR gains over baselines) suggests practical utility in reducing radiation dose while maintaining diagnostic quality. The main limitation is non-trivial memory cost (~51 MB), which constrains deployment on low-RAM devices. Open questions include whether the isotropic RIRF assumption holds under polychromatic or energy-resolved X-ray settings, and whether the approach generalizes to other transmission imaging modalities (e.g., gamma-ray, neutron).
