## Problem

The state-of-the-art optimization-based tracker OmniMotion requires prohibitively long optimization times and is highly sensitive to random initialization, producing unstable convergence with large variance across seeds. Its reliance on unconstrained NeRF volume rendering for geometry and purely short-term optical flow for supervision leaves the optimization under-constrained and slow. The paper asks how to make test-time optimization tracking both dramatically faster and robust to initialization while improving long-term tracking accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video sequence (processed at 480p, evaluated at 256×256) and a set of query pixel positions, and produces per-pixel long-term trajectories (2D position at every frame) along with binary visibility/occlusion labels. Geometry is represented as per-frame optimizable depth maps initialized from ZoeDepth metric depth estimates under a fixed pin-hole camera (40° FOV), rather than a learned radiance field. Supervision signals come from two sources: dense short-term RAFT optical flow pairs and sparse long-term DINOv2 feature correspondences filtered by mutual nearest-neighbor and self-similarity criteria.

## Method

The core contribution is CaDeX++, a local invertible deformation network that factorizes the global NVP latent code into a multi-resolution spatial feature grid (queried bilinearly by unchanged coordinates) and a temporal feature grid (queried by frame index), drastically reducing network size while preserving invertibility. The 1D affine coupling step is replaced with a monotonic piecewise-linear function parameterized by B control points whose positive deltas are predicted by a tiny MLP, increasing expressivity within a fixed number of coupling blocks. Instead of NeRF volume rendering, the scene geometry is a list of optimizable per-frame depth maps initialized from ZoeDepth and regularized to stay near the initialization, which acts as a strong stabilizer against random-seed variance. The total loss combines L1 pixel position error, depth consistency between the warped 3D point and the target depth map, and a depth regularization term, all supervised jointly by RAFT flow and DINOv2 long-term correspondences. This yields >10× speedup over OmniMotion on DAVIS and near-deterministic convergence across seeds.

## Results

On DAVIS, the method achieves AJ 59.4, δ_avg 77.4, OA 85.9, and TC 0.68, outperforming the prior optimization-based SoTA OmniMotion (AJ 51.7, δ_avg 67.5, TC 0.74) by 7.7 points in AJ and 9.9 in δ_avg while also achieving the best temporal coherence among all methods. On RGB-Stacking, it reaches AJ 75.4, δ_avg 87.1, OA 93.6, TC 0.15, surpassing all feed-forward baselines including CoTracker (AJ 65.9, δ_avg 80.4) and TAPIR (AJ 66.2, δ_avg 77.4). The method converges more than 10× faster than OmniMotion on DAVIS and ~5× faster on RGB-Stacking, while also showing dramatically lower variance across random seeds (std 0.5 vs. 26.1 on motocross-jump).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary optimization-based baseline OmniMotion, the method wins decisively on DAVIS (AJ +7.7, δ_avg +9.9, TC 0.68 vs 0.74) and matches or slightly trails on RGB-Stacking (AJ 75.4 vs 77.5, δ_avg 87.1 vs 87.0), while being 10× faster to converge. Against the strongest feed-forward method CoTracker, the method loses on DAVIS (AJ 59.4 vs 65.1, δ_avg 77.4 vs 79.0) but wins substantially on RGB-Stacking (AJ +9.5, δ_avg +6.7, OA +8.2), where textureless synthetic content exposes CoTracker's generalization gap. Against TAPIR, the method trades a small DAVIS AJ loss (59.4 vs 59.8) for large RGB-Stacking gains (AJ +9.2, δ_avg +9.7). Against older feed-forward baselines (PIPs, TAP-Net, Flow-Walk, MFT), the method dominates on both datasets across all metrics. Against pure flow-based optimization (Connect RAFT, Deformable Sprites), the method achieves large margins (e.g., AJ 59.4 vs 30.7 on DAVIS), confirming the value of the invertible deformation representation. The robustness analysis (Table 4) further shows the method's δ_avg std drops from 26.1 (OmniMotion) to 0.5 across random seeds, a key practical advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends OmniMotion (Wang et al., 2023), which parameterizes long-term pixel tracking as test-time optimization over a canonical NeRF deformed by global RealNVP coupling blocks (CaDeX). The key architectural departure is CaDeX++, which factorizes the global latent codes into local spatial-temporal feature grids (inspired by Instant-NGP and TensoRF) and replaces 1-D affine coupling with monotonic piecewise-linear homeomorphisms, borrowing the locality principle from efficient NeRF representations. Additionally, the paper replaces OmniMotion's NeRF volume rendering for geometry with optimizable monocular metric depth maps (ZoeDepth) and augments the short-term RAFT optical flow supervision with sparse long-term DINOv2 feature correspondences, introducing visual foundation models as regularizers into the optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup and dramatic variance reduction (std dropping from 26.1 to 0.5 across random seeds) make test-time optimization tracking practically viable for the first time, bridging the gap between learned trackers (which suffer distribution shift) and optimization trackers (which were prohibitively slow and unstable). The finding that a simple depth prior is the primary driver of robustness—rather than the architectural changes alone—suggests that geometry regularization is underexploited in other optimization-based 3D reconstruction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D reconstruction, pose estimation, or content generation as the authors note, and whether the dependence on monocular depth quality limits performance in challenging scenes.
