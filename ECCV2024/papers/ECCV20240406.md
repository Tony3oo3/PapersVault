## Problem

Multi-view 3D human pose estimation methods face three intertwined challenges: they often require scarce 3D-annotated training data, their cross-view fusion strategies do not scale gracefully to arbitrary camera counts, and they remain sensitive to noisy or outlier 2D keypoint predictions. UPose3D targets all three by decoupling 3D estimation from 3D supervision, designing a camera-count-agnostic fusion mechanism, and explicitly modeling prediction uncertainty to down-weight unreliable views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240406/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes synchronized multi-view RGB frames (up to 8 cameras) together with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, processed in temporal windows of 1 or 27 frames. The 2D backbone (CPN-ResNet152 at 384×384 or HRNet-W48 at 384×288) outputs per-joint 2D positions and normalizing-flow parameters encoding a full 2D keypoint distribution. The final output is a set of 3D joint coordinates obtained by iterative MLE, accompanied by per-joint uncertainty estimates from both the image branch and the pose compiler branch.

## Method

UPose3D operates in four stages: (1) an RLE head on a CNN backbone predicts 2D keypoints and their uncertainties via a normalizing-flow density; (2) epipolar geometry projects each view's keypoints onto every reference view, forming per-joint 2D point clouds that are scale-normalized by the subject's bounding box; (3) a pose compiler—comprising a point-cloud transformer encoder and a criss-cross-attention spatiotemporal encoder—refines both positions and uncertainties by exploiting cross-view and temporal consistency; (4) an L-BFGS optimizer iteratively maximizes the joint likelihood from both the image and compiler branches to recover 3D coordinates, initialized by DLT. The pose compiler is trained entirely on synthetic multi-view sequences generated online from AMASS motion-capture data with randomized camera placements and keypoint corruptions, eliminating the need for 3D-annotated multi-view video. The dual-uncertainty MLE formulation allows the optimizer to effectively discard low-confidence views, which is the key mechanism behind the method's robustness in out-of-distribution settings.

## Results

On Human3.6m (InD), UPose3D with a CPN backbone and 27-frame temporal window achieves 26.4 mm MPJPE and 23.4 mm PA-MPJPE, outperforming all 2D-supervision-only baselines (best prior: DLT+CPN at 30.5 mm MPJPE) and approaching 3D-supervised methods like MTF-Transformer+ (25.8 mm). On the RICH dataset (OoD), UPose3D achieves 34.7 mm MPJPE and 32.0 mm PA-MPJPE, dramatically outperforming AdaFuse (524.0 mm MPJPE) and DLT triangulation (66.0 mm MPJPE). The method also demonstrates constant runtime regardless of camera count on CMU Panoptic and HUMBI, with error continuing to decrease as more views are added, unlike DLT and RANSAC which plateau.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 2D-supervision-only methods, UPose3D (CPN, 27f) achieves 26.4 mm MPJPE, beating DLT+CPN (30.5 mm) by 4.1 mm and MetaPose (32.0 mm PA-MPJPE) by 8.6 mm on PA-MPJPE. Against 2D+camera-parameter methods, UPose3D (26.2 mm) ties Cross-view Fusion (26.2 mm) and is within 0.4 mm of TR Loss (25.8 mm) and TransFusion (25.8 mm), but trails Epipolar Transformers (19.0 mm) which exploits precise epipolar geometry between camera pairs. In the OoD setting on RICH, UPose3D (34.7 mm MPJPE) achieves roughly 15× lower error than AdaFuse (524.0 mm) and ~2× lower than DLT triangulation (66.0 mm), demonstrating that uncertainty-aware fusion generalizes far better to unseen environments than methods trained on in-studio 3D annotations. The paper also shows that adding temporal context (27 vs. 1 frame) consistently reduces MPJPE by ~0.5 mm across backbones, and that the pose compiler alone (without the image branch) degrades performance by 9.3 mm, confirming both branches are essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

UPose3D builds on the RLE framework [31] for uncertainty-aware 2D keypoint regression via normalizing flows, the Point Cloud Transformer architecture [11] for encoding cross-view point clouds, and criss-cross attention [17,58] for efficient spatiotemporal modeling. It departs from epipolar-transformer-based cross-view fusion methods [14,47] that operate on camera pairs and struggle to scale, instead projecting all views onto a reference image and treating the resulting 2D point cloud as a unified input. Unlike end-to-end multi-view temporal transformers [55] that require 3D-annotated training data, UPose3D trains its pose compiler entirely on synthetic multi-view sequences generated online from AMASS motion capture, and recovers 3D poses through iterative MLE rather than direct regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses two practical pain points in multi-view pose estimation: scalability to arbitrary camera counts with constant runtime, and the scarcity of 3D-annotated multi-view training data. The OoD results on RICH (34.7 mm vs. 524 mm for AdaFuse) demonstrate that uncertainty-aware fusion generalizes far better to in-the-wild environments than methods trained on in-studio data, though the comparison is somewhat asymmetric since AdaFuse was not designed for OoD. Open questions include whether the L-BFGS optimization step can be replaced by learned surrogates for real-time use, and whether the approach extends to multi-person or articulated-object settings. The reliance on known camera intrinsics/extrinsics remains a constraint for truly uncalibrated deployments.
