## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation relies on view synthesis between existing fixed camera views in video sequences, providing insufficient geometric guidance especially in occluded or invisible regions. Additionally, multi-frame depth methods that explicitly reason about geometry (e.g., cost volumes in ManyDepth) are vulnerable to dynamic objects and inter-frame occlusion, while prior bilateral consistency approaches only target moving-object regions rather than the full image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241190/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video triplets (I_{t-2}, I_t, I_{t+2}) at 640×192 (KITTI) or 416×128 (Cityscapes) resolution, and additionally synthesizes two intermediate frames via a frozen flow-based VFI model to create five target views per triplet. It outputs per-pixel depth maps in both single-frame mode (one depth per input image) and multi-frame mode (one depth per target frame using three-frame feature fusion). The framework also applies affine spatial augmentation (rotation + resize-crop) to generate augmented views whose depth maps are inversely transformed back to the original coordinate frame for consistency losses.

## Method

Mono-ViFI unifies single- and multi-frame depth estimation in a shared-weight framework. A pretrained flow-based VFI model (IFRNet) serves dual purposes: it synthesizes intermediate frames for temporal augmentation (providing extra training views) and supplies bidirectional optical flow plus occlusion merge masks for the multi-frame fusion module. The fusion module aligns neighbor-frame features to the target position via flow warping with Fourier positional encoding of the flow (MAFA), then merges them using the VFI occlusion mask to suppress inter-frame occlusion artifacts (OAFF), before a 1×1 convolution projects the fused features back to the original channel dimension. A triplet depth consistency loss enforces bilateral distillation: a standard view depth consistency (SVDC) between single- and multi-frame depths, plus two scale-aware depth consistency (SADC) losses that exploit the geometric relationship between original and affinely-augmented views (depth scales inversely with zoom factor). The single- and multi-frame models share the encoder weights, keeping the framework compact while the decoders remain separate to avoid representational conflict.

## Results

On KITTI (improved ground truth, 640×192), Mono-ViFI with D-HRNet achieves state-of-the-art performance: 0.072 Abs Rel and 0.342 Sq Rel in single-frame mode, and 0.066 Abs Rel, 0.294 Sq Rel, and 3.099 RMSE in multi-frame mode. On Cityscapes (416×128), the D-HRNet multi-frame model reaches 0.102 Abs Rel and 0.836 Sq Rel, outperforming DynamicDepth (0.104 Abs Rel, 1.011 Sq Rel) and ManyDepth (0.114 Abs Rel, 1.193 Sq Rel). The method consistently improves its backbone architectures without adding parameters: e.g., D-HRNet goes from 0.077 to 0.072 Abs Rel (single-frame) and 0.066 Abs Rel (multi-frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mono-ViFI (D-HRNet) single-frame outperforms RA-Depth (D-HRNet) on all metrics (0.072 vs 0.074 Abs Rel, 0.342 vs 0.363 Sq Rel), and beats MonoViT [64] (0.074 Abs Rel) and DIFFNet [65] (0.076 Abs Rel). In multi-frame mode, Mono-ViFI (D-HRNet) dominates ManyDepth [58] (0.066 vs 0.070 Abs Rel, 0.294 vs 0.399 Sq Rel) and MOVEDepth [56] (0.066 vs 0.065 Abs Rel but 0.294 vs 0.377 Sq Rel, 3.099 vs 3.449 RMSE). On Cityscapes, Mono-ViFI (ResNet18) multi-frame beats ManyDepth on all metrics (0.107 vs 0.114 Abs Rel, 0.934 vs 1.193 Sq Rel), and Mono-ViFI (D-HRNet) multi-frame surpasses DynamicDepth [14] on Abs Rel (0.102 vs 0.104), Sq Rel (0.836 vs 1.011), and RMSE (5.395 vs 5.987), though DynamicDepth edges ahead on δ₂ (0.972 vs 0.971). The method also improves its own backbones: Monodepth2 ResNet18 goes from 0.090 to 0.084 Abs Rel, and Lite-Mono from 0.082 to 0.080 Abs Rel, confirming the framework is orthogonal to architecture choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mono-ViFI builds directly on the Monodepth2 self-supervised training paradigm (photometric loss, auto-masking, pose estimation) and the traditional feature-fusion approach for multi-frame depth from Patil et al. and Ruhkamp et al., rather than the cost-volume geometry reasoning used by ManyDepth. Its key departure is repurposing a pretrained flow-based VFI model (IFRNet) to serve a dual role: synthesizing intermediate frames for temporal augmentation and providing optical flow plus occlusion masks for explicit feature alignment in multi-frame fusion. The spatial augmentation via affine transformation extends the rectification formulation from Planedepth (Wang et al., CVPR 2023) from simple resizing-cropping to general affine transforms, and the triplet depth consistency loss generalizes the unilateral distillation of ManyDepth and the dynamic-only mutual reinforcement of DynamicDepth to a whole-image bilateral scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's orthogonality to backbone architecture (validated on ResNet18, Lite-Mono, and D-HRNet) makes it a practical plug-in for improving existing depth networks without redesign, and the shared-weight design keeps the unified single/multi-frame model compact. A notable limitation is the dependence on a frozen, pretrained VFI model whose quality bounds the temporal augmentation and alignment accuracy; the paper does not explore joint end-to-end training of the VFI component. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to indoor or highly dynamic scenes beyond outdoor urban driving, and whether the affine rectification formulation generalizes to more complex camera models or non-rigid augmentations.
