## Problem

Video face super-resolution remains under-explored because existing strategies face a fundamental trade-off: general VSR networks propagate temporal features but fail to reconstruct fine facial details, while frame-by-frame face SR models produce high-quality but temporally inconsistent results due to the ill-posed nature of single-image restoration. The paper addresses how to maintain a stable, meaningful face prior across video frames so that restored details remain coherent over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240683/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality face video (512×512 frames from VFHQ) and pre-computed optical flow between consecutive frames (via GMFlow) as input, and produces a high-quality, temporally consistent face video. All processing occurs in CodeFormer's low-dimensional codebook latent space (downsampled by 32×), not in pixel space, which makes the latent representations less sensitive to small spatial misalignments. A Gaussian lowpass filter is applied to detected facial landmarks during the alignment step to suppress temporal jitter introduced by discrete cropping.

## Method

KEEP reformulates video face restoration as a Kalman filtering problem in CodeFormer's latent codebook space, where the latent code z_t is the hidden state and the LQ frame x_t is the noisy observation. At each timestep, the state prediction step decodes the previous posterior code, warps it to the current frame using optical flow, and re-encodes it to obtain a prior estimate; the state update step then fuses this prior with the observed state (from the LQ encoder) via a learned Kalman gain produced by a Kalman Gain Network that combines spatial-temporal attention for uncertainty estimation with a per-token gain network. Cross-Frame Attention layers in the decoder further enforce local temporal consistency by matching and fusing similar patches from the previous frame at scales 16 and 32. The entire system is trained in three stages (800k, 400k, 50k iterations) with a combination of L1, VGG perceptual, and GAN losses, leveraging CodeFormer's pre-trained codebook lookup Transformer as the generative backbone.

## Results

On VFHQ-mild, KEEP achieves 28.00 dB PSNR, 0.8267 SSIM, and 0.1619 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline BasicVSR++ (27.20 dB PSNR, 0.8057 SSIM, 0.1958 LPIPS) by approximately 0.8 dB in PSNR. For identity preservation, KEEP scores 0.7960 IDS and 8.82 AKD, substantially better than BasicVSR++ (0.7641 IDS, 11.31 AKD). On temporal consistency metrics, KEEP reduces identity fluctuation (σ_IDS = 3.69×10⁻²) and pose fluctuation (σ_AKD = 3.25) by roughly 30% compared to BasicVSR++ (5.25×10⁻² and 4.64 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

KEEP outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Among general VSR models retrained on VFHQ, BasicVSR++ is the closest competitor (27.20 dB PSNR), yet KEEP still leads by 0.8 dB PSNR, 0.021 SSIM, and 0.034 LPIPS, while reducing AKD from 11.31 to 8.82 and σ_IDS from 5.25 to 3.69 (×10⁻²), indicating substantially better pose accuracy and temporal identity stability. EDVR and BasicVSR trail further behind on both fidelity and consistency metrics. Among image-based FSR models applied frame-by-frame, GFPGAN (26.29 dB) and RestoreFormer (25.57 dB) achieve moderate fidelity but suffer from temporal flicker (σ_IDS 4.57 and 4.72), while CodeFormer performs worst on PSNR (24.66 dB) and identity preservation (0.6272 IDS) with the highest identity fluctuation (σ_IDS 6.37). GPEN is the weakest baseline overall. The paper notes that general VSR models tend to produce structural distortions and artifacts on faces, whereas image-based FSR models hallucinate inconsistent details; KEEP addresses both by maintaining a stable latent face prior through Kalman-inspired temporal propagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

KEEP repurposes the image-based CodeFormer (a codebook-prior face SR model) for video by wrapping its latent code estimation in a Kalman-filter-style predict-update loop, where the 'prediction' is obtained by decoding the previous frame's latent code, warping it via optical flow, and re-encoding it. This departs from both the general-VSR route (BasicVSR, EDVR) that propagates pixel/feature-space information without face-specific priors, and the frame-by-frame FSR route that ignores temporal structure entirely. The learned Kalman gain network (replacing explicit covariance computation, following KalmanNet) and the cross-frame attention in the decoder (borrowed from Tune-A-Video / ReRender A Video) are the two key architectural additions that make the latent-space fusion effective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VFSR remains substantially under-explored relative to image FSR, and KEEP demonstrates that a principled temporal-fusion framework in a discrete latent code space can close the gap between generic VSR fidelity and single-image FSR quality while preserving identity stability across frames. The approach is in principle backbone-agnostic (the authors note the Kalman-filter structure could wrap other FSR models), but only CodeFormer is validated, leaving open whether the same gains transfer to continuous-latent or GAN-inversion-based restorers. Practical limitations include the dependence on optical flow (even if accuracy is forgiving) and the sequential recurrent nature, which may limit throughput on very long sequences.
