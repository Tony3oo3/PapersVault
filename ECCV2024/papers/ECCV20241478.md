## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF methods suffer from blurry artifacts and aliasing when rendering at varying viewing distances, and anti-aliasing techniques like mipmapping have not been extended to the dynamic setting. Additionally, the deformation network in canonical+deformation frameworks tends to overfit, producing fused or compressed canonical geometry that prevents clean separation of shape from motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241478/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of RGB images (monocular or multi-view) of a dynamic scene with precise timestamps and calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics (from SfM or Blender). It produces photorealistic novel view renderings, temporally interpolated frames between input frames, and an explicit canonical geometry. The method is evaluated across multiple input resolutions (full, 2×, 4×, 8× downsampled) to demonstrate resolution-invariant rendering quality, and handles both synthetic (D-NeRF, multi-scale D-NeRF) and real-world (HyperNeRF, NeRF-DS, PlenopticVideo) scenes.

## Method

DMiT replaces the canonical NeRF in the D-NeRF paradigm with a hierarchy of mipmapped tri-planes (Tri-Mip), where cone-cast spheres in observation space are deformed by an MLP into the canonical space and then orthogonally projected onto three feature planes at multiple mipmap levels for anti-aliased feature lookup. A tiny MLP regresses the concatenated tri-mip features and encoded view direction to density and color for volume rendering. To prevent the fast-converging tri-planes from overfitting and fusing geometry across time frames, the paper introduces a joint geometry and deformation refinement: tri-plane resolution is progressively upscaled from 64×64 to 512×512 during training, the deformation network's learning rate is warmed up in sync, and an annealing frequency encoding progressively increases positional encoding dimension from zero to maximum. This balanced training schedule, combined with spatial total variation regularization on the feature planes, yields artifact-free canonical geometry and stable deformation.

## Results

On the D-NeRF synthetic benchmark, DMiT achieves PSNR 34.22, SSIM 0.982, LPIPSv 0.024, and LPIPSa 0.014, outperforming the strongest baselines (4DGS at PSNR 32.30 and V4D at PSNR 32.37) by approximately 1.9 dB in PSNR. On real-world HyperNeRF, DMiT reaches PSNR 27.80 (vs. 27.35 for HyperNeRF), and on NeRF-DS it attains PSNR 24.48, SSIM 0.866, and LPIPS 0.115, surpassing D3DGS (PSNR 23.74) and NeRF-DS (PSNR 23.61). On the multi-view PlenopticVideo dataset, DMiT achieves PSNR 31.73 and MS-SSIM 0.974, leading HexPlane (31.57 / 0.969) and HyperReel (31.54 / 0.965).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the D-NeRF benchmark, DMiT decisively outperforms all eight baselines across every metric, with the closest competitors being 4DGS (PSNR 32.30, LPIPSa 0.021) and V4D (PSNR 32.37, LPIPSv 0.038); DMiT leads by ~1.9 dB PSNR and halves the LPIPSv gap. On HyperNeRF, DMiT wins PSNR (27.80 vs. 27.35 for HyperNeRF) but narrowly trails HyperNeRF on MS-SSIM (0.926 vs. 0.927) and LPIPS (0.153 vs. 0.144), while clearly surpassing 4DGS (PSNR 27.05, LPIPS 0.266) and D3DGS (PSNR 25.57). On NeRF-DS, DMiT achieves the best PSNR (24.48), SSIM (0.866), and LPIPS (0.115), beating D3DGS (23.74 / 0.844 / 0.127) and NeRF-DS (23.61 / 0.853 / 0.128). On PlenopticVideo, DMiT leads in PSNR (31.73) and MS-SSIM (0.974) over HexPlane (31.57 / 0.969) and HyperReel (31.54 / 0.965), though DyNeRF retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.083 vs. 0.086). Overall, DMiT wins or ties on the majority of metrics across all five datasets, with its largest margins appearing on the synthetic D-NeRF and multi-scale D-NeRF benchmarks where anti-aliasing is most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

DMiT sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: anti-aliasing in static NeRF (Mip-NeRF's cone-casting, Tri-MipRF's hierarchical tri-plane mipmapping) and dynamic scene reconstruction via the canonical+deformation paradigm (D-NeRF, NeRFies). The paper's core architectural contribution is transplanting the efficient Tri-Mip encoding into the canonical space of a deformable NeRF, while the joint geometry-and-deformation refinement procedure (progressive tri-plane upsampling plus annealed frequency encoding from NeRFies) addresses the convergence-speed mismatch that causes fused or compressed canonical geometry when these two modules are naively combined. This is explicitly positioned as the first pre-filtering anti-aliasing method applied to dynamic NeRF representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By bringing mipmap-based anti-aliasing to dynamic scenes with a compact tri-plane backbone, DMiT offers a practical path to high-fidelity, resolution-robust dynamic rendering in ~30 minutes of training on a single GPU, compared to hours for MLP-heavy baselines. The joint refinement procedure is a reusable recipe for any hybrid architecture where a fast-converging explicit representation must coexist with a slower implicit deformation network. Open questions remain: the method depends on accurate camera registration, struggles with unbounded or very large scenes due to the fixed-resolution tri-plane factorization, and the authors acknowledge difficulty with complex specular/reflective appearances under monocular input—limitations that will matter as dynamic NeRF moves toward real-world AR/VR applications.
