## Problem

In Fourier compressed sensing, existing approaches to finding optimal sampling masks face fundamental limitations: joint optimization of sampling-reconstruction (H1) uses a fixed mask that is not adaptive to individual inputs, while adaptive sampling (H2) requires difficult optimization over a discrete mask space and suffers from Pareto suboptimality because a single reconstruction network must serve all possible masks. This paper asks whether one can achieve input-adaptive sampling with Pareto-optimal reconstruction without the discrete optimization challenges of H2.

## Input / Output

The method takes low-frequency k-space data (M₀k) as input, where M₀ is a fixed mask sampling only LF components. A normalizing-flow-based super-resolution space generation model produces S diverse HR images from the zero-filled LR reconstruction; the sample variance of their Fourier transforms yields a per-pixel HF Bayesian uncertainty vector. At inference, this vector is matched to one of J pre-computed k-means++ centroids to select a dedicated (M_j, θ_j) pair, and the final output is the image reconstructed by θ_j from the additionally sampled k-space data. Experiments use 160×160 RGB faces (CelebA) and 320×320 multi-coil brain MRI (fastMRI, 16 coils) at 4×–16× acceleration.

## Method

The proposed H1.5 framework pre-computes J sampling-reconstruction pairs (M_j, θ_j) at training time: a flow-based SR space generation model generates diverse HR samples from each training image's LF reconstruction, the sample variance in k-space quantifies HF Bayesian uncertainty, and k-means++ clustering partitions the normalized uncertainty vectors into J groups. Each mask M_j is then constructed via rejection sampling proportional to its centroid (introducing randomness known to improve SSIM), and a dedicated reconstruction network θ_j is trained exclusively on that mask, ensuring Pareto optimality. At inference, the input's uncertainty vector is compared to the J centroids and the nearest (M_j, θ_j) pair is selected, making the method adaptive without any backpropagation through discrete mask space. Two theorems formalize the advantage: H1.5 has a lower infimum true risk than H1 (by adaptivity) and than H2 (by eliminating Pareto suboptimality, assuming |π_φ(M₀K)| ≤ J).

## Results

On CelebA at 8× acceleration, the proposed adaptive selection (H₁.₅) achieves 0.9405 SSIM, outperforming the best non-adaptive method (VD, 0.9073) by 0.033 and the best adaptive sampling method (Policy, 0.8501) by 0.090. In the practical multi-coil CS-MRI 1D setting at 8×, the method reaches 0.9407 SSIM, surpassing the best H₁ baseline (VD, 0.9367) by 0.004 and the best H₂ baseline (Policy, 0.9240) by 0.017. The method consistently achieves the highest SSIM across all six evaluation settings (CelebA 8×/16×, CS-MRI 2D 4×/8×, CS-MRI 1D 4×/8×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three representative baselines spanning both H₁ (non-adaptive) and H₂ (adaptive) classes. Against the variable-density (VD) sampling [27], the strongest H₁ baseline, the proposed method wins in all six settings, with the largest margin on CelebA 8× (+0.033 SSIM) and the smallest on CS-MRI 2D 4× (+0.0006). Against LOUPE [2], another H₁ joint-optimization method, the proposed method dominates across all settings, with gaps ranging from +0.003 (CS-MRI 2D 4×) to +0.066 (CelebA 8×). Against the policy-based adaptive sampling [4] (the only H₂ multi-coil baseline), the proposed method wins in every setting, with the most dramatic improvement on CelebA 8× (+0.090 SSIM) and a clinically meaningful +0.017 on CS-MRI 1D 8×. The consistent superiority supports Theorem 1 (adaptivity over non-adaptive) and Theorem 2 (Pareto optimality over single-network adaptive sampling).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions its adaptive selection framework (H1.5) as a principled middle ground between two established paradigms in Fourier compressed sensing: joint optimization of sampling-reconstruction (H1, e.g., LOUPE, SPARKLING) which uses a single non-adaptive mask, and adaptive sampling (H2, e.g., policy-based methods) which learns a continuous mask generator but suffers from Pareto suboptimality of a single reconstruction network. The key conceptual departure is replacing the learned mask generator with a discrete one-hot selection over pre-trained mask-network pairs, thereby avoiding backpropagation through discrete space entirely. The HF Bayesian uncertainty quantification mechanism is directly repurposed from a flow-based super-resolution space generation model [23], which had not previously been applied to guide sampling in compressed sensing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides clean theoretical guarantees (Theorems 1 and 2) that the adaptive selection scheme dominates both H1 and H2 in true risk, and demonstrates consistent SSIM improvements across facial image reconstruction and realistic multi-coil MRI settings. The practical significance is that it offers a training-free selection mechanism (k-means++ on uncertainty vectors) that avoids the notoriously difficult discrete optimization of mask generators, making it more accessible to practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric MRI, whether the fixed number of segments J can be learned automatically, and whether the SR space generation model must be retrained for each new imaging domain or acceleration regime.
