## Problem

Parametric 3D shape models rely on PCA-based latent spaces that are globally entangled, making it impossible to perform localized, disentangled edits of specific mesh regions without affecting the rest of the shape. Existing disentangled methods (SD, LED) attempt to factorize the latent space but cannot guarantee spatially localized manipulation in 3D vertex space and require costly optimization to achieve direct point control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240355/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed-topology 3D mesh, a binary mask defining the manipulation region (computed as the k-hop geodesic neighborhood of user-selected anchor points), and optionally target positions for those anchor points. It outputs a locally edited mesh where only masked vertices are modified while unmasked regions remain exactly unchanged. The same framework supports unconditioned region sampling, full-shape generation (by masking the entire mesh), and sparse reconstruction from as few as 200 anchor points, all without any optimization step.

## Method

ShapeFusion formulates localized shape editing as a masked diffusion inpainting problem: during forward diffusion, Gaussian noise is added only to vertices within the mask, while unmasked vertices remain untouched, guaranteeing by construction that edits are spatially confined. The denoising network is a 4-layer hierarchical mesh convolution (3 resolution levels per layer, spiral convolutions) that operates directly on vertex coordinates, with a vertex-index positional encoding to break permutation equivariance and preserve mesh topology. Each vertex's initial feature concatenates its 3D coordinates, mask value, positional encoding, and a Fourier-encoded diffusion timestep. Training uses a randomly selected anchor point per sample to define the mask, so the model learns local geometric priors across all possible regions and sizes, enabling both conditioned manipulation (via anchor target positions) and unconditioned region sampling at inference.

## Results

On the MimicMe dataset, ShapeFusion achieves a DIV of 0.34, FID of 0.30, and ID of 0.05, outperforming all baselines across all three datasets. On UHM, the method reaches DIV 0.71, FID 0.53, and ID 0.11; on STAR, DIV 0.98, FID 0.43, and ID 0.09. The FID improvement is particularly dramatic: 0.30 vs. 7.81 (SD) and 3.39 (LED) on MimicMe, indicating far more realistic region samples. For direct point manipulation, ShapeFusion runs in ~3.2 s versus ~22 s for optimization-based baselines (≈10× faster), and for expression editing it is ~20× faster than NFR (3 s vs. 60 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShapeFusion wins on every metric (DIV, FID, ID) across all three datasets against both SD [12] and LED [13]. The FID gap is the most striking: on MimicMe, ShapeFusion scores 0.30 versus 7.81 for SD and 3.39 for LED, indicating substantially more realistic generated regions. Identity preservation (ID) also improves markedly—0.05 mm on MimicMe versus 0.84 mm (SD) and 0.23 mm (LED)—confirming that the masked diffusion approach avoids the identity drift caused by latent-space disentanglement. The internal M-VAE baseline, which uses the same masking strategy but a VAE architecture, achieves competitive FID and ID yet falls short on diversity (DIV 0.25 vs. 0.34 on MimicMe), highlighting the generative advantage of the diffusion formulation. For expression editing, ShapeFusion outperforms NFR [39] in both localization (NFR affects non-edited regions without regularization, and degrades with it) and speed (3 s vs. 60 s per manipulation, a 20× speedup). Overall, the paper demonstrates consistent superiority over all three external baselines with no metric where a baseline matches or exceeds the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D point cloud diffusion framework of Luo et al. [26] to fixed-topology triangular meshes, introducing a vertex-index positional encoding and hierarchical mesh convolutions to preserve topology during denoising. It departs fundamentally from the dominant line of work on disentangled latent spaces (SD [12], LED [13]), which attempts to achieve localization by factorizing the latent code, by instead formulating localized editing as a masked inpainting problem in 3D vertex space. The DDPM formulation of Ho et al. [16] provides the forward/reverse diffusion backbone, but the key conceptual shift is that the masking strategy during training—rather than any explicit disentanglement loss—guarantees spatially localized edits by construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, optimization-free editing tool for 3D artists and aesthetic medicine applications, with a 10× speedup over optimization-based baselines and guaranteed localization that prior latent-space methods cannot provide. A key open question is whether the fixed-topology assumption (required for vertex-index encoding and pre-computed hierarchical downsampling) limits applicability to shapes with varying connectivity, and whether the approach scales to more complex or open surfaces. The finding that a simple VAE with the same masking strategy achieves localization but not diversity highlights that the diffusion process itself is critical for generative expressiveness, suggesting broader implications for how generative priors interact with spatial constraints.
