## Problem

Transferring skeletal motion to diverse target characters traditionally requires explicit rigging, skinning weights, or registration to a canonical template, all of which are costly and limit generalization. Existing data-driven alternatives either produce jittery per-frame predictions or are restricted to registered humanoids on a fixed template, failing to deliver temporally coherent, artifact-free animation on arbitrary unrigged meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241540/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unrigged triangulated mesh (arbitrary topology, non-manifold inputs converted to manifold), a sequence of relative joint angles in SMPL's kinematic tree, and a body shape parameter β as conditioning. It outputs a full animated vertex-position sequence at each time step, handling sequences of 1–3k frames without keyframes—effectively solving a motion extrapolation problem. No registration to a canonical template or paired source-target sequences is required, and the method operates with as few as 5–10 training motion examples.

## Method

Two jointly trained MLPs operate in lockstep: a primary network (f_P) predicts per-frame triangle Jacobians independently (extending Neural Jacobian Fields), while a residual network (f_R) embedded in a neural ODE predicts small corrective Jacobian deltas conditioned on a window of past predictions via multi-head attention encoders. The key insight is that the ODE predicts only residual corrections added to the base per-frame Jacobians, rather than the full deformation, which simultaneously suppresses per-frame jitter and limits ODE drift over long sequences. Spatial coherence is enforced by a differentiable Poisson solve that integrates per-triangle Jacobians into vertex positions, while temporal coherence comes from Euler integration of the ODE over 32-frame windows. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a simple vertex-position L2 loss plus a weighted Jacobian L2 loss, using PointNet features and Wave Kernel Signatures for automatic cross-shape correspondence.

## Results

On the AMASS dataset, Temporal Residual Jacobians (TRJ) achieves the lowest vertex error (L2-V) across all five motion categories: 1.48 cm (Walk), 1.73 cm (Run), 2.64 cm (Jump), 2.86 cm (Punch), and 3.96 cm (Dance). The method also attains the best Jacobian error (L2-J, ranging 0.22–0.37) and normal angular error (L2-N, ranging 5.63°–9.88°) in every category. Compared to the closest baseline NJF(Mt), TRJ reduces vertex error by roughly 40–78% (e.g., 1.48 vs. 6.85 cm on Walk; 2.64 vs. 5.52 cm on Jump). Against VertexODE, the improvement is more dramatic, with vertex errors reduced by 5–10× (e.g., 1.48 vs. 23.92 cm on Walk).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two design-variation baselines rather than prior published methods, since the authors note no existing method addresses the specific motion-extrapolation problem (transferring motion without keyframes). **NJF(Mt)** extends Neural Jacobian Fields by conditioning on per-frame joint angles to predict Jacobians independently at each timestep; it produces plausible single frames but suffers from temporal jitter and occasional frame-level geometric artifacts (extended or shrunken body parts). TRJ outperforms NJF(Mt) on every metric across all five motion categories, with vertex errors roughly 2–4× lower (e.g., 1.48 vs. 6.85 cm on Walk, 2.64 vs. 5.52 cm on Jump). **VertexODE** replaces the Jacobian representation with direct vertex-velocity prediction followed by Neural ODE integration; it produces severely distorted, degenerate shapes and loses the intended motion after a short time, with vertex errors 5–10× higher than TRJ (e.g., 23.92 vs. 1.48 cm on Walk, 26.15 vs. 3.96 cm on Dance) and normal errors exceeding 40° in every category. The qualitative gap is stark: VertexODE completely distorts the mesh, while NJF(Mt) shows visible jitter and linear-path drift in later frames, whereas TRJ maintains smooth, shape-preserving motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Neural Jacobian Fields (NJF) [2], which learns per-triangle affine deformations integrated via a Poisson solve, by coupling it with a Neural ODE [7] that predicts residual Jacobians as temporal corrective factors. The spatial formulation draws on local deformation encoding [33] and the Poisson-based gradient field manipulation framework, while the temporal component departs from discrete-time LSTM-based motion models [10,11,12,27] by operating continuously in Jacobian space. The key conceptual departure from prior rig-free pose transfer methods [2,19,34] is the residual formulation: rather than making the base posing model time-coherent, a separate ODE predicts small corrective Jacobians that are added to per-frame predictions, addressing both jitter and long-sequence drift simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method enables rig-free, template-free motion transfer to arbitrary meshes (including non-humanoids and scans) with only 2–10 training sequences, which is practically significant for animation pipelines where rigging is expensive and generalization to new characters is needed. Open limitations include the absence of physics constraints (allowing self-intersections), residual drift over very long sequences, and reliance on implicit correspondences that can be spurious for topologically dissimilar shapes. The residual-Jacobian formulation and the windowed-attention ODE architecture could inform broader spatio-temporal deformation learning, but the approach remains tied to the Poisson-solve spatial integration and the specific Jacobian parameterization.
