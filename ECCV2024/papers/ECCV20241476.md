## Problem

Egocentric 3D pose estimation suffers from joint invisibility caused by the limited field of view of head-mounted cameras and self-occlusion of body parts. Existing approaches either rely on 2D heatmaps that cannot exploit rich appearance information and fail to scale with larger backbones, or use computationally expensive 3D voxel convolutions whose cost grows with grid size. This paper asks whether a simple transformer-based architecture can overcome these limitations while remaining highly efficient.

## Input / Output

The method takes stereo egocentric images captured by two fish-eye cameras mounted 1 cm apart on the head (or a single monocular egocentric image) and produces 3D Cartesian coordinates for all body joints. The images are processed by a UNet feature extractor (ResNet-18/50 backbone, stride-4 downsampling) that is pre-trained on 2D heatmap prediction; the stereo views are encoded independently without multi-view concatenation in the decoder. The output is structured as per-joint refinement offsets added to coarse pose proposals, with the final layer's prediction serving as the model output.

## Method

EgoPoseFormer uses a two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. The first stage, a Pose Proposal Network (PPN), globally average-pools each view's feature map, concatenates the results, and passes them through a 2-layer MLP with GELU to predict coarse 3D joint locations; surprisingly, this simple MLP alone already surpasses prior state-of-the-art when combined with AdamW optimization and heatmap pre-training. The second stage, a Pose Refinement Transformer (PRFormer) with 3 decoder layers, encodes each joint's identity and initial location into a Joint Query Token (JQT), which iteratively interacts with multi-view features and other JQTs. The key architectural contribution is Deformable Stereo Attention, which replaces standard cross-attention by projecting each joint's 3D position onto every view plane, applying deformable attention around the projected reference point in each view (zeroing out-of-FOV views), and concatenating the per-view results through a linear projection. Self-attention among JQTs propagates kinematic information across joints, and a shallow MLP per layer predicts the 3D refinement offset. The entire model uses only 14.1M parameters and 7.3G FLOPs on UnrealEgo, achieving 45% lower MPJPE than the previous best with 7.9% of its parameters.

## Results

On the stereo UnrealEgo dataset, EgoPoseFormer achieves 33.4mm MPJPE (32.7mm PA-MPJPE), a 27.4mm (45%) improvement over the previous state-of-the-art Ego3DPose (60.8mm MPJPE). On the monocular SceneEgo dataset, it achieves 93.0mm MPJPE (74.3mm PA-MPJPE), a 25.5mm (21%) improvement over SceneEgo (118.5mm MPJPE). The model is also highly efficient, using only 14.1M parameters and 7.3G FLOPs on UnrealEgo (7.9% and 13.1% of Ego3DPose's 178.4M/55.6G), and 27.9M parameters and 50.4G FLOPs on SceneEgo (60.7% and 36.4% of SceneEgo's 45.9M/157.3G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoPoseFormer outperforms all seven baselines on both datasets. On UnrealEgo (stereo), it beats the previous SOTA Ego3DPose [12] by 27.4mm MPJPE (60.8→33.4) and 15.8mm PA-MPJPE (48.5→32.7), while using only 7.9% of its parameters and 13.1% of its FLOPs. It also surpasses UnrealEgo [3] by 45.7mm MPJPE, EgoGlass [45] by 49.9mm, and xR-EgoPose [32] by 79.2mm. On SceneEgo (monocular), it beats the previous best SceneEgo [37] by 25.5mm MPJPE (118.5→93.0) and 18.4mm PA-MPJPE (92.7→74.3), while requiring only 60.7% of its parameters and 36.4% of its FLOPs; it also outperforms EgoPW [36] by 96.6mm, Mo²Cap² [41] by 107.3mm, and xR-EgoPose [32] by 148.3mm. Notably, the SceneEgo baseline required ground-truth depth and semantic masks during training and was pre-trained on a large external dataset, whereas EgoPoseFormer uses neither.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoPoseFormer adapts the DETR-style sparse query decoder (Carion et al., ECCV 2020) and deformable attention (Zhu et al., ICLR 2021) to the egocentric pose estimation setting, replacing the dense 2D heatmap regression used by prior methods (xR-EgoPose, EgoGlass, UnrealEgo, Ego3DPose) and the 3D voxel convolution of SceneEgo. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage coarse-to-fine paradigm: a global-pooling MLP proposes joint locations, then a DETR-style transformer with Joint Query Tokens refines them via Deformable Stereo Attention, which projects 3D joint positions onto each view plane to guide sparse deformable sampling. This avoids the poor scaling behavior of heatmap-based methods (which cannot leverage larger backbones) and the quadratic cost of 3D convolutions on voxel grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 45% MPJPE reduction with only 7.9% parameters and 13.1% FLOPs makes this a compelling efficiency baseline for egocentric pose estimation, and the authors explicitly position it as a starting point for future work. The finding that a 2-layer MLP on globally pooled features already beats prior SOTA (partly due to AdamW and heatmap pre-training) suggests that prior methods may have been under-trained rather than architecturally limited, an insight that could reshape how the community approaches this task. Open questions include whether the approach extends to temporal sequences, real-world (non-synthetic) stereo data, and whether the Deformable Stereo Attention generalizes to more than two views or to dynamic camera motion.
