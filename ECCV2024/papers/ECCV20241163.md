## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection models suffer severe performance degradation under out-of-distribution test conditions (e.g., weather changes, camera corruptions), dropping from ~46 mAP to as low as 0.3 mAP in extreme cases. Existing fully test-time adaptation methods fail in this setting because OOD corruptions cause detection scores to collapse, producing massive false negatives that conflict with pre-defined score thresholds and leave too few reliable positive detections for model optimization.

## Input / Output

At test time, MonoTTA consumes only unlabeled RGB images (batch size B, or B=1 for instance-level methods like MonoNeRD) and the pre-trained model weights, updating exclusively batch normalization layer parameters via SGD. The method dynamically maintains an adaptive threshold α through an exponential moving average of high-score object scores within each batch, and a fixed constant η (default 0.05) filters out extremely low-score detections before negative learning. Output is standard 3D bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores, evaluated via AP_3D|R40 with IoU thresholds of 0.7/0.5/0.5 for cars and 0.5/0.25/0.25 for pedestrians and cyclists.

## Method

MonoTTA combines two complementary adaptation strategies optimized jointly as min L_AO + λ·L_Nreg over BN parameters. First, reliability-driven adaptation exploits the empirical finding that high-score objects remain reliable even under corruption: an adaptive threshold α (updated via EMA with decay β=0.1) selects a reliable subset of detections, and the adaptive optimization loss L_AO maximizes their scores, which empirically boosts confidence across all detections including low-score ones. Second, noise-guard adaptation addresses the scarcity of high-score objects in extreme scenarios by applying negative learning to the abundant low-score detections: for each object with score in [η, α), a random negative class is selected and its score is minimized via L_Nreg, preventing the model from overfitting to noise or collapsing into trivial solutions where all classes receive high scores. This dual mechanism allows MonoTTA to function even when no objects exceed a meaningful score threshold, as L_Nreg can still drive adaptation by denying incorrect categories.

## Results

On KITTI-C (severity level 1, Mean AP_3D|R40), MonoTTA achieves 32.64 / 11.59 / 6.15 for Car / Pedestrian / Cyclist based on MonoGround, outperforming the best prior TTA method (TENT) by 5.1 / 1.3 / 1.0 points respectively. Based on MonoFlex, MonoTTA reaches 29.48 / 8.75 / 8.40, again leading all baselines. On nuScenes real-world day↔night shifts, MonoTTA improves MonoFlex by 6.23 mAP and MonoGround by 8.26 mAP on average. The paper reports approximately 190% average performance gains on KITTI and 198% on nuScenes over source-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoTTA consistently outperforms all five TTA baselines (BN adaptation, TENT, EATA, ActMAD) and both source-only baselines across every category and corruption type on KITTI-C. Against the strongest TTA competitor TENT, MonoTTA gains +5.1 mAP on Car and +1.3 mAP on Pedestrian (MonoGround base), with the gap widening under extreme corruptions like Snow (18.72 vs 10.32 for Car). EATA, which also uses entropy-based sample selection, is surpassed by MonoTTA by +7.4 mAP on Car (MonoGround), confirming that MonoTTA's reliability-driven threshold and negative regularization are more effective than EATA's entropy filtering. ActMAD, which aligns activation statistics, lags significantly behind (21.24 vs 32.64 Car mAP on MonoGround), suggesting distribution alignment alone is insufficient for the severe score collapse in Mono 3Det. For the instance-level method MonoNeRD (batch size 1), MonoTTA still achieves the best average AP (17.38 vs TENT's 16.82 at level 1), demonstrating applicability to single-image inference pipelines. On nuScenes real-world day↔night shifts, MonoTTA is the only method that handles both directions effectively, while TENT fails on Night→Daytime due to overfitting to noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fully test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm—established for image classification by TENT (entropy minimization) and EATA (reliability-weighted entropy)—to the substantially harder setting of monocular 3D object detection. It departs from these prior TTA methods by identifying that OOD corruptions cause a collapse in detection scores, rendering the assumption of sufficient positive detections invalid; the paper instead introduces a reliability-driven adaptive threshold to select trustworthy objects and a negative regularization term borrowed from the noisy-label literature (NLNL) to exploit the abundant low-score predictions. The base detectors (MonoFlex, MonoGround, MonoNeRD) are treated as frozen backbones whose batch-normalization parameters are the only test-time-updated components, consistent with the lightweight adaptation philosophy of TENT and EATA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For autonomous driving, the demonstrated ~190% average AP gain on KITTI-C and ~198% on nuScenes (day↔night) translates directly into safer perception under weather and lighting shifts, a practically critical gap. The paper's key limitation is that it assumes a single OOD distribution per adaptation session and only updates BN layers, so it does not address catastrophic forgetting under dynamically shifting distributions or leverage 3D geometric cues that could further stabilize depth estimation under corruption. Open questions include whether the reliability-driven threshold generalizes to multi-modal sensors, whether the negative regularization term scales to open-vocabulary or long-tail detection, and how the method behaves when the source model is already near its capacity limit on the target domain.
