## Problem

Existing category-level 6D pose estimation datasets such as NOCS cover fewer than 10 object categories with limited instance diversity and overly simplistic scenes, severely restricting practical applicability to complex real-world environments. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a large-vocabulary dataset that incorporates realistic challenges including occlusion, varying lighting, complex backgrounds, and diverse object orientations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240657/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a 640×480 RGBD image rendered with fixed camera intrinsics [577.5, 577.5, 319.5, 239.5], where objects are normalized to a (-1,1)³ m³ canonical space and placed via free-fall simulation in 9 room environments. The provided annotations include the rendered RGB image, depth map (encoded as RGB by multiplying depth by 10,000 and converting to base-256 digits), NOCS map, instance mask, and ground-truth 6D pose with size. Objects undergo random scaling (0.8–1.2×) and are viewed from cameras at 8–9 m radius with 30–90° elevation angles, producing diverse viewpoints and occlusion patterns.

## Method

The dataset is constructed by rendering 4,688 real-scanned textured meshes across 166 categories using BlenderProc with 9 Replica room backdrops, free-fall object scattering, and stratified 7:2:1 train/val/test splits. A key technical contribution is a symmetry-aware evaluation metric that accounts for rotational invariance around all three axes (Sym-0 through Sym-3), exploiting Euler-angle singularity to reduce worst-case computation from 360³ to 4³ rotation comparisons. The paper further proposes a progressive fine-tuning strategy that doubles the category count at each step, copying trained global features and old category parameters into the expanded network while initializing new category parameters, enabling transfer from limited-vocabulary pre-training (e.g., CAMERA) to 48-category evaluation. Shape priors extracted via a variational autoencoder and agglomerative clustering on latent embeddings (20 clusters) support category-wise analysis, revealing that shape-based grouping correlates more strongly with pose estimation performance than semantic category boundaries.

## Results

On the Omni6D test set (166 categories, unseen instances), HS-Pose achieves the best IoU50 (62.65%) and translation accuracy (80.93% at 2 cm, 97.78% at 5 cm), while DualPoseNet leads on rotation-sensitive metrics (9.38% at 5°, 19.18% at 10°) and IoU75 (25.49%). SPD, an implicit method, attains the highest 10° rotation accuracy (21.02%) and 10°5cm score (19.23%), confirming that implicit shape-prior methods excel at rotation while explicit regression methods dominate translation and IoU. On the unseen-category test set (Omni6D_out, 17 categories), DualPoseNet and HS-Pose remain the top performers (IoU50 ≈ 36.8%), while implicit methods (SPD, SGPA) drop to IoU50 < 8%, revealing a severe inter-category generalization gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks six category-level 6D pose estimation methods on the new Omni6D large-vocabulary setting. HS-Pose [47] (explicit) achieves the highest IoU50 (62.65%) and translation accuracy (80.93% at 2 cm), outperforming DualPoseNet [20] (58.84% IoU50, 73.82% at 2 cm) by ~4 points on IoU50. However, DualPoseNet [20] dominates rotation metrics, scoring 9.38% at 5° and 19.18% at 10° versus HS-Pose's 4.96% and 11.75%, and also leads on IoU75 (25.49% vs. 23.02%). SPD [34] (implicit) achieves the best 10° rotation accuracy (21.02%) and 10°5cm (19.23%), confirming that implicit shape-prior deformation methods produce more reliable rotational forecasts. On the unseen-category test (Omni6D_out), DualPoseNet [20] and HS-Pose [47] are the only methods with meaningful performance (IoU50 ≈ 36.8%), while SPD [34] and SGPA [6] collapse to IoU50 < 8%, and RBP-Pose [46] and GPV-Pose [10] are near-zero on rotation metrics (< 0.1% at 5°2cm). The paper concludes that the large-vocabulary setting exposes architectural strengths/weaknesses that are masked on smaller datasets like NOCS, with explicit/hybrid methods generalizing far better across categories than purely implicit ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the category-level 6D pose estimation benchmark established by NOCS (Wang et al., 2019) from 6 categories to 166, leveraging real-scanned objects from OmniObject3D and BlenderProc rendering to create a more realistic evaluation setting. It departs from prior work in two key ways: introducing a symmetry-aware evaluation metric that handles rotational invariance across all three axes (rather than the y-axis-only convention inherited from NOCS), and proposing a progressive fine-tuning strategy for transferring models from small-vocabulary to large-vocabulary settings. The empirical study benchmarks six representative methods spanning implicit (SPD, SGPA), hybrid (DualPoseNet, RBP-Pose), and explicit (GPV-Pose, HS-Pose) architectures, positioning Omni6D as a stress test that reveals architectural strengths and weaknesses obscured by the limited diversity of prior datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omni6D addresses a critical bottleneck in category-level 6D pose estimation: the narrow category range of existing benchmarks (≤6 categories) that limits practical applicability to complex real-world scenes. The finding that method rankings shift substantially when scaling from 6 to 166 categories—particularly that HS-Pose degrades most rapidly while DualPoseNet maintains robustness—suggests that current evaluation on small vocabularies may mislead practitioners about which architectures generalize. Open questions include whether the fine-tuning strategy's diminishing returns at 48 categories reflect a fundamental scaling limitation, how to handle the domain gap between rendered and truly real-world data, and whether the symmetry-aware metric will be adopted as standard. The dataset's reliance on BlenderProc rendering (despite using real-scanned geometry) means it still doesn't capture all real-world challenges like extreme lighting or motion blur.
