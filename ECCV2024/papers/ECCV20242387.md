## Problem

Once-for-all training co-trains a family of weight-shared subnets inside a supernet to enable scalable NAS across multiple deployment targets, but existing approaches incur prohibitive training costs (up to 1200 GPU hours) because they either introduce smaller subnets too early (inflating per-epoch time) or too late (causing supernet weights to over-specialize to the full model and requiring many extra epochs to overcome interference). The paper identifies that the timing and mechanics of when and how to begin shrinking the full model into smaller subnets remain suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100/1k) and outputs class labels. The trained supernet spans a continuous FLOP range (e.g., ~67M to ~600M MACs for MobileNetV3-based spaces), and specialized subnets are extracted post-training for any target latency/FLOP budget without retraining, making the output a family of deployable CNNs rather than a single model.

## Method

DεpS introduces three coupled mechanisms for faster once-for-all training. First, FM-Warmup delays the introduction of smaller subnets until the full model is ~50% trained, reducing per-epoch cost early on and preventing supernet weights from over-specializing. Second, ε-Shrinking gradually ramps the effective learning rate of sampled subnets from 10⁻⁴ to 1 (while the full model retains its full learning rate), ensuring that the combined gradient at the start of shrinking closely approximates the full-model-only gradient and avoiding a ~2% accuracy drop. Third, IKD-Warmup performs in-place knowledge distillation from the partially trained full model to smaller subnets, leveraging the fact that intermediate models transfer richer non-target-class information than fully trained ones, and doing so from multiple progressively better checkpoints rather than a single teacher. Together these components reduce training cost by up to 2.5× while improving subnet accuracy across the full FLOP range.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k (MobilenetV3-based supernet), DεpS achieves 73.8% top-1 accuracy at 150 MACs (smallest subnet) and 77.3% at 230 MACs (largest subnet), with a mean Pareto accuracy of 75.81. Training cost is 700 GPU hours (≈$1,491), a 1.8× reduction over OFA (1,256 GPU hours) and 2.5× over BigNAS (1,778 GPU hours). Across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1k, DεpS consistently outperforms baselines by up to 2.1% accuracy at the same FLOPs or achieves up to 2.3× FLOP reduction at the same accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OFA, DεpS improves smallest-subnet accuracy by 2.0% (73.8% vs. 71.8% at 150 MACs), matches largest-subnet accuracy (77.3% vs. 77.2%), and cuts training cost by 1.8× (700 vs. 1,256 GPU hours). Against BigNAS, DεpS wins on both accuracy (73.8% vs. 70.6% smallest; 77.3% vs. 74.0% largest) and cost (700 vs. 1,778 GPU hours, a 2.5× reduction), with BigNAS also suffering from higher per-epoch time (266 vs. 155 GPU min/epoch). Against CompOFA, DεpS uses fewer total epochs (270 vs. 330) and lower GPU hours (700 vs. 782), though CompOFA does not report per-subnet accuracy for direct comparison. In the lower-FLOP regime (0–100 MACs) on ImageNet-1k, DεpS achieves 72.3% top-1 at 67 MACs versus OFA's 70.5%, a 1.8% gain. Against hand-designed and NAS-based efficient nets (MobileNetV3, EfficientNet-B0, FBNetv2, ProxylessNAS, MnasNet), DεpS matches or exceeds their accuracy at 1.3× fewer FLOPs (77.3% at 230 MACs vs. EfficientNet-B0's 77.3% at 390 MACs). The gains generalize across CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1k, as well as to the ProxylessNAS architecture space where DεpS improves smallest-subnet accuracy by 1.8% over OFA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the once-for-all training lineage (OFA, BigNAS, CompOFA) by rethinking *when* and *how* to introduce weight-shared subnets into training. OFA delays shrinking until the full model is completely trained (causing weight specialization and long convergence), while BigNAS shrinks from the start (inflating per-epoch cost and destabilizing the full model). DεpS carves out a middle ground—delaying shrinking to ~50% of full-model training—and adds two supporting mechanisms: ε-Shrinking (a gradual learning-rate warmup for subnets to prevent gradient-magnitude spikes at the transition) and IKD-Warmup (in-place knowledge distillation from a partially trained, rather than randomly initialized, full model). The approach is complementary to architecture-space modifications like CompOFA's, targeting the training schedule rather than the search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.5× reduction in GPU-hours (and dollar cost) for once-for-all training is practically significant for groups that need to co-train many subnets across deployment targets, making the technique more accessible. The empirical validation that a partially trained full model is a *better* supernet initialization than a fully trained one (Fig. 2) challenges the implicit assumption in OFA that more full-model training is always beneficial before shrinking. Open questions include whether the ~50% sweet spot generalizes to much larger supernets or non-CNN backbones (e.g., ViT-based search spaces), whether ε-Shrinking's fixed linear ramp is optimal versus adaptive schedules, and how the method interacts with more recent supernet training strategies such as dynamic batch sampling or multi-teacher distillation.
