## Problem

Post-training quantization of diffusion models to low bit-widths (3-bit, 2-bit) suffers from severe error accumulation across the iterative denoising process, caused by both error propagation between timesteps and exposure bias between training and inference. Existing PTQ methods for diffusion models address per-step quantization error but leave this cumulative degradation unaddressed, leading to significant quality degradation at low precisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained diffusion models (DDIM on CIFAR-10 at 32×32, LDM-8/LDM-4 on LSUN at 256×256, Stable Diffusion v1.4 for text-to-image) and requires only a small calibration batch to pre-compute per-timestep, per-channel correction coefficients. During inference, the correction is applied at every denoising step with negligible additional computation (element-wise multiplication and subtraction), producing synthesized images at the same resolution as the underlying model.

## Method

TAC-Diffusion decomposes the input error at each timestep into two components—input discrepancy Δx_t and noise estimation discrepancy Δε_t—and corrects them separately. Noise Estimation Reconstruction (NER) scales the quantized noise estimation by a channel-wise, timestep-dependent coefficient K found via a closed-form solution to a convex loss combining MSE and rQNSR terms, with a threshold mask to focus on high-magnitude pixels. Input Bias Correction (IBC) subtracts a pre-calculated average element-wise bias B from the quantized input to mitigate exposure bias. Both corrections are applied at every timestep (timestep-aware), and the entire pipeline is post-training: no retraining or fine-tuning is required, and the pre-computed coefficients are simply looked up during inference.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 100-step DDIM, TAC-Diffusion achieves FID 9.55 at W3A8, a 7.76-point improvement over Q-Diffusion (17.31) and a 105.85-point improvement over PTQ4DM (115.4). At W8A8, it reaches FID 3.68, slightly outperforming the QAT method EfficientDM (3.75) and Q-Diffusion (3.75). On LSUN-Bedroom with LDM-4, TAC-Diffusion pushes PTQ to 2-bit weight precision for the first time, achieving FID 7.79 at W2A8 versus 9.48 for Q-Diffusion at W3A8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAC-Diffusion outperforms Q-Diffusion [24], the primary SOTA baseline, across all tested precisions: FID 3.68 vs 3.75 (W8A8), 4.89 vs 4.93 (W4A8), 9.55 vs 17.31 (W3A8), and 31.77 vs 33.21 (W3A6) on CIFAR-10. Against PTQ4DM [47], the advantage is dramatic at low bit-widths (FID 9.55 vs 115.4 at W3A8), though PTQ4DM is competitive at W8A8 (10.55 vs 3.68). On LSUN-Church (LDM-8), TAC-Diffusion beats Q-Diffusion by 0.28 FID at W8A8 (3.37 vs 3.65) and by 2.02 FID at W3A8 (7.78 vs 9.80), and also surpasses PTQD [15] (12.68) and QuEST [51] (6.55 at W8A8). On LSUN-Bedroom (LDM-4), TAC-Diffusion achieves FID 5.14 at W3A8 versus 7.17 for Q-Diffusion and 6.46 for PTQD, and extends to W2A8 (FID 7.79) where no prior PTQ method reports results. At W4A8 on CIFAR-10, TAC-Diffusion (4.89) trails EDA-DM (4.16) and EfficientDM QAT (3.80), indicating that at moderate precision the correction overhead is less impactful than specialized calibration or QAT approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the rapidly growing line of PTQ methods for diffusion models (PTQ4DM, Q-Diffusion, PTQD, TFMQ-DM, EDA-DM, TDQ), which have primarily focused on calibration strategies, timestep-dependent bit-width allocation, and variance re-scheduling to handle activation range variability. TAC-Diffusion departs from this calibration-centric framing by identifying error accumulation—driven by error propagation (formalized in Li et al. [25]) and exposure bias—as a distinct, underexplored failure mode, and addressing it with a lightweight inference-time correction (channel-wise noise estimation reconstruction inspired by rQNSR from Finkelstein et al. [9], plus input bias correction). The method is complementary to existing PTQ pipelines rather than a replacement, and requires no retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pushing PTQ for diffusion models to 2-bit weights for the first time and achieving near-full-precision FID at 3-bit with negligible overhead, this work demonstrates that accumulated quantization error is a correctable, rather than fundamental, limitation. The practical impact is significant for deploying diffusion models on resource-constrained hardware, since the correction is a pre-computed set of per-timestep, per-channel scalars and a bias map. Open questions include whether the channel-wise linear reconstruction assumption holds for more complex architectures (e.g., DiT/transformer-based diffusion), whether the pre-calculated coefficients generalize to unseen prompts or out-of-distribution inputs, and how the method interacts with other efficiency techniques like distillation or few-step sampling at scale.
