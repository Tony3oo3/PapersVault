## Problem

Matting-level annotations (sub-pixel alpha mattes) are extremely labor-intensive to produce, leaving only a handful of public datasets (e.g., Adobe matting with 493 objects, Distinctions-646 with 646 images). Existing synthetic data generators either require semantic masks as conditions—restricting generation to a closed training domain—or produce only pixel-level masks rather than the finer matting-level annotations needed for realistic compositing and editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241827/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts free-form text prompts (and optionally ControlNet conditioning signals such as sketches or human poses) and produces a 4-channel RGBA image: the object rendered on a pure green-screen background plus a matting-level alpha channel. The green screen is not merely a visual choice but a structural mechanism—the fixed background color enables both foreground/background separation and a post-processing matting refinement step (GreenPost) that converts the coarse latent-space mask into a hair-level alpha matte.

## Method

DiffuMatting builds on Stable Diffusion V1.5 and teaches the model to 'paint on a fixed green-screen canvas,' embedding both the object and its annotation into a single image so that the green color naturally encodes the background. Three key components drive training: (1) a green-background control loss that supervises the cross-attention map of the 'green' text token to match the background mask, keeping the canvas pure without requiring class-specific priors; (2) a detailed-enhancement loss that aligns Sobel-filtered high-frequency features at the foreground-background transition to preserve hair and fur detail; and (3) a lightweight matting head (two 2D convolutions + SiLU) attached to the 128-dim VAE decoder latent features, producing a coarse mask refined by a GreenPost pipeline (K-Means background clustering + BackgroundMattingV2) into a matting-level alpha. Training is two-stage on 2×V100 GPUs—first 1.5 days for pure green-screen generation, then 1 day jointly optimizing generation and matting—to avoid early misalignment degrading the matting parameters.

## Results

On green-screen generation quality, DiffuMatting achieves a GSG score of 98.98 (lower is better), substantially outperforming LoRA fine-tuning (134.17) and Dreambooth fine-tuning (133.34), while also attaining the highest aesthetic score (A_s = 5.26 vs. 4.78 and 4.85). For the downstream general object matting task, incorporating synthesized data at a 2/3 synthetic-to-real ratio reduces MSE by 15.4% (from 0.013 to 0.011) and CONN by 9.4% (from 43.7 to 40.6) compared to training solely on the real DIM dataset. The ablation confirms both the green-background control loss and the detailed-enhancement loss contribute meaningfully, with the latter having a larger impact on aesthetic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD-XL and Midjourney (Fig. 2), DiffuMatting consistently produces objects on a pure green-screen canvas, whereas both SOTA models struggle to maintain a stable green background and often bleed colors into the canvas. Compared to DiffuMask [43], DiffuMatting produces matting-level (sub-pixel, hair/fur-level) annotations directly from the VAE latent space, while DiffuMask only yields pixel-level masks and requires per-class fine-tuning of AffineityNet for post-processing. In the direct fine-tuning comparison (Table 1), DiffuMatting's green-background control loss reduces GSG by roughly 26% over LoRA and 26% over Dreambooth, indicating a much purer and more stable green canvas. On the downstream matting benchmark, the synthesized GOM data at a 2/3 ratio outperforms the real-only baseline (DIM) on all four metrics (SAD, MSE, GRAD, CONN), demonstrating that the generated matting annotations are practically useful for training matting networks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion V1.5 latent diffusion framework and the BackgroundMattingV2 matting refinement pipeline, but departs fundamentally from prior synthetic data generation work (DatasetGAN, DiffuMask, FreeMask) that either requires semantic masks as conditional inputs or is restricted to training-set classes. The key conceptual departure is using a fixed green-screen canvas as an implicit foreground/background separator, which avoids class-priors entirely and inherits the 'anything' generation capability of the pretrained diffusion model. A matting head is added in the VAE decoder's latent space to produce sub-pixel alpha annotations, and two novel losses (green-background cross-attention control and Sobel-based transition boundary detail enhancement) guide the generation toward clean backgrounds and hair-level edge fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Matting-level annotations remain extremely scarce (Adobe Matting: 493 images, Distinctions-646: 646) compared to pixel-level segmentation data (SAM: 11M), making a scalable synthetic data factory for matting a practically important contribution. The 15.4% relative MSE reduction in General Object Matting demonstrates downstream utility, and the seamless compatibility with community LoRAs and ControlNet broadens its applicability to art design and controllable generation. However, the paper acknowledges a domain gap between synthesized and real data, and the method cannot generate non-green-screen images with matting annotations without additional inpainting. Open questions include whether the green-screen trick generalizes to other background strategies, whether matting quality truly matches human-annotated alpha at scale, and whether the approach extends to video matting.
