## Problem

MLLMs have demonstrated strong object-level perception in still images (grounding, detection), but their application to video object tracking remains largely unexplored. Two barriers prevent this: (1) the absence of large-scale video datasets with object trajectories and language descriptions suitable for training MLLMs on cross-frame object perception, and (2) the computational cost of fitting many video frames within an LLM's context window without degrading per-frame localization accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240573/cropped_page13_idx18.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames resized to 336×336 pixels, a text prompt (language expression for RSOT, or a bounding box in the first frame for SOT, or coordinates in a given frame for Video-REG), and produces object bounding boxes as integer coordinate pairs in [0,100) (e.g., "[23,45,46,72]" requiring only 13 LLaMA tokens versus 28 for floating-point formats). Timestamps are embedded into each frame's visual tokens so the LLM can distinguish consecutive frames. For videos exceeding 32 frames, inference proceeds in 8-frame clips with one-frame overlap, chaining the last predicted box into the next clip as a template.

## Method

Elysium is an end-to-end MLLM combining CLIP-ViT-L (visual encoder), a novel T-Selector token compressor, and Vicuna (LLM). T-Selector applies an MLP scoring function followed by Softmax and a KeepTopK gating operation to retain only the top-k most informative spatial tokens per frame (compression ratio α), then projects them to the LLM's hidden dimension via a second MLP—avoiding the severe performance drop observed with spatial fusion (cross-attention or concatenation). Training is progressive: Stage 1 initializes T-Selector on LLaVA-558K with frozen ViT/LLM, then trains end-to-end on mixed image data; Stage 2 finetunes on high-quality image data plus VideoChat and the newly constructed ElysiumTrack-1M (1.27M noun-chunk-trajectory pairs derived from WebVid-10M via spaCy parsing, Grounding DINO grounding, MixFormer tracking, and Kalman-filter drift rejection). The default compression setting (αN=108 tokens per frame at 336×336) balances context length against localization accuracy, as validated by ablations showing a sharp drop below 72 tokens.

## Results

On Referring Expression Comprehension, Elysium achieves 89.07/92.12/84.95 (val/test-A/test-B) on RefCOCO, 82.86/88.93/75.64 on RefCOCO+, and 82.92/83.62 on RefCOCOg, setting new state-of-the-art with only 108 visual tokens per image. On VideoQA, Elysium reaches 75.8% accuracy on MSVD-QA, 67.5% on MSRVTT-QA, and 66.6% on TGIF-QA, outperforming all prior methods. On the proposed ElysiumTrack-1M evaluation split, Elysium achieves 87.5 AUC and 94.5 Precision for RSOT, and 46.7 Meteor / 115.3 CIDEr for Video-REG, substantially surpassing the MiniGPT-v2 baseline (65.4 AUC, 16.9 Meteor).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Image Grounding (Table 2), Elysium outperforms all seven baselines across every RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg split, beating the closest competitor MiniGPT-v2 (which uses a larger ViT at 448 resolution) by 0.4 on RefCOCO val and 1.2 on RefCOCO+ test-A, while using only 108 tokens versus 256–608. On VideoQA (Table 3), Elysium achieves the best accuracy on MSVD-QA (75.8 vs. 75.2 for MovieChat), MSRVTT-QA (67.5 vs. 52.7 for MovieChat), and TGIF-QA (66.6 vs. 51.4 for VideoChatGPT), but trails MovieChat on ActivityNet-QA (43.4 vs. 45.7). For zero-shot SOT (Table 4), Elysium reaches 56.1 AUC on LaSOT, slightly below DiMP (56.9) and SiamRPN++ (49.6 is lower, so Elysium actually beats SiamRPN++), and 56.6 AUC on UAV123, below SiamRPN++ (61.0) and SiamGAT (64.6), reflecting the limitation with small objects. On the novel RSOT task (Table 5), Elysium achieves 87.5 AUC / 94.5 Precision, a 22.1-point AUC gain over the MiniGPT-v2 frame-by-frame baseline (65.4 AUC). For Video-REG (Table 6), Elysium scores 46.7 Meteor and 115.3 CIDEr versus 16.9 and 76.6 for MiniGPT-v2, demonstrating the benefit of temporal context over single-frame generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-for-grounding line of work (Shikra, MiniGPT-v2, Kosmos-2) from static images to the video domain, where object-level tasks like tracking have previously required dedicated Siamese or correlation-based trackers (SiamFC, SiamRPN++, DiMP) or multi-model pipelines. The T-Selector token compressor departs from the spatial-fusion adapters (cross-attention, concatenation) common in prior MLLMs, motivated by the observation that such operations cause drastic performance drops on grounding tasks. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf components—Grounding DINO for box generation, MixFormer for trajectory propagation, spaCy for noun-chunk extraction, and Kalman filtering for drift rejection—into a novel large-scale annotation pipeline that had no direct precedent in the tracking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ElysiumTrack-1M (1.27M trajectories with language expressions) fills a critical data gap for object-level video tasks and introduces RSOT and Video-REG as benchmarks that bridge language and tracking. The demonstration that a single end-to-end MLLM can perform zero-shot tracking comparable to fine-tuned specialized trackers suggests a path toward unified video understanding without task-specific modules. However, the paper is limited to single-object tracking; multi-object tracking, video object segmentation, and referring video object segmentation are explicitly deferred. The struggle with tiny objects (UAV123) and the reliance on a 336×336 visual encoder point to open questions about resolution, scale, and whether the top-k selection strategy generalizes to denser or longer video contexts.
