## Problem

Supervised video denoisers require large paired noisy-clean video datasets that are impractical to collect for dynamic scenes, while existing unsupervised video denoisers trained solely on noisy videos produce artifacts and residual noise. The paper addresses this by asking whether a well-trained image denoiser (where paired data is readily available) can serve as a reliable spatial prior that is augmented with temporal information to achieve competitive video denoising without any paired video supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241500/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes T consecutive noisy video frames (T=5 for synthetic Gaussian denoising, T=3 for real raw video) centered at the target frame, with raw video frames packed into RGBG sequences. It outputs a single denoised central frame at the same spatial resolution. Training uses 160×160 patches with batch size 4, and the noise model is either additive white Gaussian (σ∈[10,55]) or Poisson-Gaussian with camera-specific parameters from CRVD.

## Method

TAP lifts a pre-trained NAFNet encoder-decoder image denoiser by inserting three trainable temporal modules at the skip connections between encoder and decoder levels (levels 1–3), each using deformable convolution to align neighboring frames' features to the central frame with hierarchical offset transfer from deeper to shallower levels. A learnable scalar β (initialized to 0) gates each temporal module's contribution, so the network initially behaves as the pure image denoiser. Training follows a progressive fine-tuning strategy: at each step, pseudo noisy-clean video pairs are constructed by denoising with the current network and re-adding known noise, then only the temporal module at the current level is optimized (all spatial layers frozen) using L1 loss, progressing from the deepest (level-3) to the shallowest (level-1) module. This prevents overfitting to the over-smoothed pseudo labels while incrementally incorporating temporal alignment at multiple scales.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset (synthetic Gaussian noise, σ=10–50), TAP achieves an average PSNR of 35.35 dB and SSIM of 0.926, while TAP-T (trained directly on the test set) reaches 35.48 dB / 0.928, outperforming the best prior unsupervised method RDRF (35.18 dB / 0.926). On Set8, TAP-T achieves 33.95 dB average PSNR, surpassing even the supervised FloRNN (33.55 dB) by 0.40 dB. Compared to the pre-trained image denoiser NAFNet applied frame-by-frame, TAP-T gains 1.24 dB on DAVIS and 1.52 dB on Set8, confirming effective temporal information exploitation. On the CRVD indoor raw video test set, TAP also outperforms all unsupervised baselines and matches FloRNN on PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAP and TAP-T outperform all five unsupervised baselines (MF2F-T, RFR-T, UDVD, RDRF, ER2R-T) on both DAVIS and Set8 across all noise levels. The closest unsupervised competitor is RDRF, which TAP surpasses by 0.17 dB on DAVIS (35.35 vs 35.18) and 0.38 dB on Set8 (33.30 vs 32.92); TAP-T extends this gap to 0.30 dB and 1.03 dB respectively. Against the supervised FloRNN, TAP-T actually wins on Set8 (33.95 vs 33.55 dB) but trails on DAVIS (35.48 vs 36.38 dB), suggesting the advantage is most pronounced when test-set motion is relatively smooth. Compared to the frame-by-frame NAFNet image denoiser baseline, TAP-T gains 1.24 dB (DAVIS) and 1.52 dB (Set8), validating that the plugged temporal modules effectively exploit inter-frame correlation. On the CRVD indoor raw video set, TAP matches FloRNN on PSNR while outperforming UDVD and other unsupervised methods, though the margin is smaller than on synthetic benchmarks due to the limited dataset size and simulated motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAP departs from prior unsupervised video denoisers (UDVD, RDRF, MF2R, ER2R) that train from scratch on noisy videos alone, and from RFR which fine-tunes a pre-trained *video* denoiser (itself requiring paired video data). Instead, TAP repurposes a pre-trained *image* denoiser (NAFNet) as a frozen spatial prior and inserts trainable temporal modules—borrowing the pyramid deformable-convolution alignment design from EDVR and RViDeNet—into its skip connections. The progressive bottom-to-top fine-tuning strategy, which uses pseudo noisy-clean pairs generated by the partially-trained network itself, is the paper's key methodological contribution, distinguishing it from both blind-spot and Noise2Noise-style unsupervised training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: paired image data is far easier to collect than paired video data, so leveraging a well-trained image denoiser as a starting point is a natural and deployable strategy. The 'plugin' framing means the approach is architecture-agnostic in principle (any encoder-decoder image denoiser can be lifted), though the paper only validates this with NAFNet. Open questions include whether the known-noise-model assumption for pseudo-pair construction limits real-world applicability, whether the method extends to other restoration tasks (super-resolution, deblurring), and whether optical flow or attention-based alignment could close the remaining gap to fully supervised methods.
