## Problem

Existing image restoration methods address degradations (noise, blur, haze, rain, etc.) with separate single-task models, while unified approaches either require known degradation formulations or are limited to a small predefined set of tasks. No prior method simultaneously decomposes multiple unknown degradations, restores them in a task-agnostic framework, and supports open-vocabulary user control over the restoration process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241068/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single degraded RGB image (potentially containing multiple simultaneous artifacts) and optionally a user-specified text prompt as input. It operates iteratively: at each step, the SA-BIQA stage identifies the dominant remaining degradation and produces a text embedding (e.g., 'A photo needs noise artifact reduction'), which conditions the restoration stage; the loop terminates when the model predicts 'no' degradation. The final output is a restored RGB image, and the intermediate text prompts can be overridden by the user for open-vocabulary editing at runtime.

## Method

AutoDIR comprises two stages. The SA-BIQA stage fine-tunes CLIP's image encoder with a novel semantic-agnostic constraint loss (L_SA) that penalizes the model for detecting artifacts in clean ground-truth images, forcing the encoder to focus on structural quality rather than semantic content; this yields 97.94% degradation detection accuracy versus 77.65% for a ViT classifier. The AIR stage fine-tunes a Latent Diffusion Model UNet conditioned on both the text embedding and the VAE-encoded latent of the input image, and introduces a lightweight Structural-Correction Module (SCM) that adds a residual correction from the original image to the decoded restoration, with a learnable weight w balancing hallucination (for super-resolution) against structure preservation (for low-light enhancement). Training uses a single-step latent estimation rather than full reverse diffusion, with a combined L2 loss on the final restored image.

## Results

On the multi-task restoration benchmark (Table 4), AutoDIR achieves the best SSIM and PSNR on deraining (0.965 / 35.09 dB), dehazing (0.973 / 29.34 dB), deraindrop (0.924 / 30.10 dB), and deblurring (0.828 / 27.07 dB), while remaining competitive on denoising and low-light enhancement. On super-resolution (Table 2), AutoDIR attains the highest no-reference quality scores (MUSIQ 63.02, CLIP-IQA 0.596, NIQE 4.62, NIMA 5.25) despite lower full-reference PSNR (23.49 dB), consistent with the perception-distortion tradeoff of generative methods. On the zero-shot under-display camera task (Table 3), AutoDIR leads all baselines on MUSIQ (49.27), CLIP-IQA (0.311), NIQE (6.39), and NIMA (4.43). The SA-BIQA stage achieves 97.94% degradation-detection accuracy, substantially outperforming a ViT classifier (77.65%) and original CLIP (61.96%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CNN-based all-in-one baselines, AutoDIR clearly outperforms AirNet [31] on all six tasks in Table 4 (e.g., derain PSNR 35.09 vs. 30.99; dehaze SSIM 0.973 vs. 0.944) and also surpasses PromptIR [42] on deraining, dehazing, deraindrop, and deblurring, while PromptIR retains a slight edge on denoising PSNR (29.89 vs. 29.68) and low-light PSNR (22.42 vs. 22.37). Compared to the task-specific NAFNet [10] trained on all-in-one tasks, AutoDIR wins on derain, dehaze, deraindrop, and deblur, and is competitive on denoise and low-light. The raw Latent Diffusion baseline [49] (LD) is substantially outperformed on every task, confirming the benefit of the structural-correction module and task-specific fine-tuning. On the zero-shot under-display camera (TOLED) and underwater (EUVP) datasets, AutoDIR beats GDP [15], AirNet, PromptIR, and NAFNet on all no-reference metrics (Table 3). For degradation detection (Table 1), SA-CLIP achieves 97.94% accuracy, far exceeding the ViT classifier [14] (77.65%) and original CLIP [46] (61.96%), validating the semantic-agnostic training objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoDIR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) all-in-one image restoration (AirNet, PromptIR, Park et al.), which typically handles a small predefined set of degradations via a classifier-selected model; (2) generative-prior-based restoration (LDM, GDP, ADIR), which requires known degradation formulations; and (3) CLIP-based image understanding, which the paper repurposes for quality assessment by introducing a semantic-agnostic regularization that shifts CLIP's focus from semantic content to structural quality. The key departure from prior all-in-one methods is the combination of open-vocabulary degradation detection (SA-CLIP) with a structural-corrected latent diffusion model, enabling handling of truly multiple unknown degradations without per-image finetuning or assumptions about specific degradation functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoDIR provides the first unified framework that simultaneously detects multiple unknown degradations, restores them in a task-agnostic manner, and supports open-vocabulary user control via text prompts, positioning it as a potential foundation model for image restoration. The finding that the all-in-one backbone transfers effectively to unseen tasks (de-snowing, JPEG) with minimal fine-tuning, outperforming task-specific NAFNet, suggests implicit correlations across restoration tasks that could accelerate future work. Key limitations include computational cost comparable to full diffusion sampling (significantly higher than discriminative networks like NAFNet) and a focus on global rather than local restoration, leaving open questions about acceleration (e.g., one-step diffusion) and extension to local editing or video restoration.
