## Problem

Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) has been extensively studied for natural images but remains unexplored for aerial/remote sensing imagery. Aerial images present unique challenges: overhead viewpoints yield weak appearance features, objects frequently blend with visually similar backgrounds (e.g., AIRPORT vs. HIGHWAY), and class-agnostic RPN recall for novel categories drops to 48% compared to 77% in natural images. Additionally, aerial datasets are far smaller in both size and category vocabulary than their natural-image counterparts, making direct transfer of existing OVD methods ineffective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes aerial RGB images in three streams during training: labeled images with ground-truth boxes and base-category labels, unlabeled images that may contain novel categories, and images sampled from a dynamic label queue carrying pseudo-labels. Category text embeddings are generated by encoding the prompt template 'a photo of [category]' through RemoteCLIP's pre-trained text encoder, serving as the weight matrix of the final classification layer. The detector outputs class-agnostic bounding boxes (a single 4D box per proposal rather than per-category boxes) paired with open-vocabulary category scores computed via cosine similarity between RoI features and text embeddings, enabling detection of categories never seen during training.

## Method

CastDet employs a three-model student-teacher architecture: a Faster R-CNN student (ResNet50-C4 backbone) with a class-agnostic regression head and a semantic classifier, a localization teacher maintained as an exponential moving average of the student, and a frozen RemoteCLIP external teacher. The localization teacher generates candidate boxes filtered by regression jittering variance (RJV), which are cropped and classified by RemoteCLIP to produce high-confidence pseudo-labels (threshold p₀=0.8). A dynamic label queue stores these pseudo-labels with an index dictionary mapping categories to image IDs, enabling iterative overwriting and balanced per-category sampling to maintain label quality across training iterations. The hybrid training loss combines supervised loss on labeled data, unsupervised classification and regression loss on unlabeled data guided by the localization teacher (with weighted negative samples to handle early-training false negatives), and classification-only loss on queue-sampled images to inject novel-category knowledge. This creates a flywheel effect where RemoteCLIP's external knowledge strengthens the localization teacher's novel-object discovery, which in turn yields more precise boxes for RemoteCLIP to classify, progressively expanding the detectable vocabulary without additional annotation.

## Results

On VisDroneZSD, CastDet achieves 46.5% mAP_novel and 40.5% HM (with pre-known novel classes), outperforming the previous state-of-the-art by 21.0% mAP_novel. Without requiring novel classes to be pre-known, it still reaches 46.5% mAP_novel and 38.5% mAP overall. On other aerial datasets, CastDet achieves 87.6% mAP on NWPU VHR-10 and 58.6% mAP on DOTA. The method also shows gains on natural images (COCO), improving mAP from 37.6% (RemoteCLIP) to 47.3% with ground-truth boxes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CastDet decisively outperforms all compared open-vocabulary detectors on VisDroneZSD, with the most dramatic gap on mAP_novel: 46.5% vs. 25.5% (OV-DETR), 19.5% (BARON), 14.2% (ViLD), 8.5% (YOLO-World), 5.4% (GLIP), 4.8% (Detic), and 0.3% (GroundingDINO). The closest competitor, OV-DETR, trails by 21.0% mAP_novel. Notably, CastDet requires neither pre-known novel class names (T_novel) nor auxiliary classification/caption datasets (T_cls), whereas most baselines need one or both. On overall mAP, CastDet (38.5%) also surpasses the strongest baselines GLIP (33.6%) and GroundingDINO (33.4%). When novel classes are pre-known, CastDet further improves to 40.5% mAP and 42.3% HM, confirming that the framework benefits from but does not depend on explicit novel-class information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) paradigm from natural images to aerial imagery, a domain where prior OVD methods (ViLD, Detic, GroundingDINO, GLIP) fail dramatically due to weak top-down appearance features and low RPN recall (48% vs. 77% on natural images). Methodologically, it combines the mean-teacher EMA mechanism from semi-supervised detection (Tarvainen, Soft Teacher) with a frozen RemoteCLIP external teacher for pseudo-label generation, departing from prior CLIP-based OVD approaches that directly transfer zero-shot knowledge without iterative self-learning. The dynamic label queue and regression-jittering box selection strategy are novel components not present in prior student-teacher or CLIP-based detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first OVD benchmark and method for aerial images, this work establishes a foundation for extensible remote-sensing detection without costly re-annotation, addressing a genuine operational need in earth observation. The 21% mAP novel gap over prior SOTA and the striking failure of natural-image OVD methods (e.g., GroundingDINO at 0.3% mAP novel) underscore that aerial OVD is a distinct problem requiring domain-specific solutions. Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets with a single ResNet-50 backbone, dependence on RemoteCLIP's quality for pseudo-labels, and the relatively small category pools tested; open questions include scalability to hundreds of novel categories, extension to SAR/multispectral modalities, and whether the dynamic queue mechanism generalizes beyond the VisDroneZSD protocol.
