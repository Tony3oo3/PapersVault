## Problem

Existing 3D garment generation methods either produce posed geometry with low-quality textures (non-parametric reconstruction from images), are restricted to tight-fitting designs (parametric templates), or rely on slow multiview optimization for texture synthesis. No prior work enables direct, efficient text-driven generation of unposed, textured 3D garment meshes that are immediately usable in simulation and animation pipelines.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'blue denim cargo shorts') as the sole user input. It produces an unposed (canonical/T-pose) 3D garment mesh represented internally as an unsigned distance field and extracted via modified Marching Cubes, along with a UV texture map generated from a 1024×1024 view-composited RGB image. The output mesh requires no post-processing and can be directly fed into standard cloth simulation and animation pipelines.

## Method

WordRobe learns a 32-dimensional garment latent space via a two-stage coarse-to-fine encoder-decoder: a DGCNN encoder maps surface points to a latent code, and two coordinate-based MLP decoders (D_coarse for smooth geometry, D_fine for high-frequency details like wrinkles) jointly reconstruct the UDF. A novel disentanglement loss penalizes the batch covariance matrix of latent dimensions away from identity, promoting organized latent interpolation. A weakly-supervised MLP_map aligns CLIP text embeddings to the garment latent space, with training pairs generated automatically by rendering garment depth maps through ControlNet to produce images that are then CLIP-encoded—eliminating the need for manual text annotations. For texture synthesis, front and back orthographic depth maps are composited into a single image and passed to ControlNet in one feed-forward step to generate a view-consistent 1024×1024 RGB image, which is projected onto the UV texture map, avoiding the slow and view-inconsistent multiview optimization used by prior methods.

## Results

On the 3D Garment Dataset (~20,000 unposed garments, 19 categories), WordRobe achieves a Chamfer Distance of 1.078 and Point-to-Surface distance of 0.329, outperforming DrapeNet (CD 1.796, P2S 0.573) by approximately 40% and 42% respectively. For text-guided texture synthesis, WordRobe attains a CLIP Score of 0.304 (ViT-H/14), 0.265 (ViT-L/14), and 0.241 (ViT-B/16), surpassing Text2Tex (0.263, 0.243, 0.232) across all three CLIP encoder variants. Additionally, the texture synthesis pipeline runs in ~22 seconds per garment versus ~5 minutes for Text2Tex, a 13× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WordRobe is compared against two SOTA baselines, each addressing a different component of the pipeline. For garment shape generation, WordRobe outperforms DrapeNet [12] by a significant margin: CD drops from 1.796 to 1.078 (−40%) and P2S from 0.573 to 0.329 (−42%), with qualitative results showing DrapeNet fails to capture fine garment details like wrinkles and pleats while WordRobe's coarse-to-fine decoder handles them well. For text-guided texture synthesis, WordRobe surpasses Text2Tex [18] on CLIP Score across all three encoder variants (e.g., 0.304 vs. 0.263 on ViT-H/14) while also producing more view-consistent textures without the patchy artifacts seen in Text2Tex's multiview optimization. Critically, WordRobe's single feed-forward ControlNet-based approach is ~13× faster (22 s vs. ~5 min on an RTX 4090), making it far more practical for interactive use. The paper notes that no existing method performs direct text-driven unposed textured garment generation, so the comparisons are component-wise rather than end-to-end.

## Relation to Prior Work

WordRobe builds directly on DrapeNet's UDF-based representation and distance/gradient losses for garment geometry, but extends it with a coarse-to-fine two-stage decoder, a novel latent disentanglement loss, and—critically—text-driven generation and texture synthesis that DrapeNet lacks. For texture, it departs from the multiview-optimization paradigm of Text2Tex and related CLIP-score methods by exploiting an empirical property of ControlNet: compositing front/back depth maps into a single image yields view-consistent RGB in one feed-forward pass. The weakly-supervised CLIP-to-latent mapping scheme repurposes ControlNet-generated images as pseudo-annotations, sidestepping the need for a manually annotated garment-text dataset that does not exist in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce unposed, textured 3D garment meshes directly from text prompts in a single pipeline, and the output meshes integrate into standard cloth simulation and animation tools without post-processing—a practical gap that parametric (SMPL-based) and posed-reconstruction methods leave open. The 13× speedup over Text2Tex for texture synthesis (22 s vs. ~5 min) makes iterative text-guided design feasible. Open questions include whether the ControlNet view-composited trick generalizes beyond front/back garment views to more complex topologies, whether the 32-dim latent space scales to the full diversity of real-world garments, and how the approach handles garments with highly detailed patterns or multi-material compositions that a single text prompt may not capture.
