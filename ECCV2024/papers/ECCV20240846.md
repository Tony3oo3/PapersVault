## Problem

Existing texture and material recognition methods, whether handcrafted or CNN-based, fail to capture the directional relationships among latent texture attributes that underlie perceptual texture properties. Prior work on attribute-based texture recognition (e.g., MAP-net, MPAP) models correlations between visual attributes but ignores directionality and the multiscale nature of texture, leaving a gap in how neighboring latent attributes can enrich each other's representations through structured information flow.

## Input / Output

GTN takes a single RGB texture or material image as input and produces a probability distribution over texture/material categories (47 for DTD, 11 for KTH, 31 for GTOS-M, etc.) via a final FC + Softmax layer. The input image is processed by a frozen pre-trained ConvNeXt (or ResNet-50) backbone, and feature maps from M layers are converted into graph vertices; no fine-tuning of the backbone is required. The compression ratio r (default 4) controls the number of latent texture attributes per graph, targeting approximately 367 vertices across all layers to match the size of the texture lexicon.

## Method

GTN encodes feature maps from M backbone layers into unsigned homogeneous directed graphs where vertices represent latent texture attributes and a learnable masked adjacency matrix (obtained via sigmoid + binary thresholding at λ=0.5, then in-degree normalization) captures directional relationships between them. A self-loop re-calibration mechanism, inspired by Cluster-GCN's diagonal enhancement, inflates preserved self-loop weights by a learnable factor g(ψ_m)∈(1,2) to prevent noise from dominating when vertex counts are small. Residual message passing aggregates weighted neighbor information into each vertex, followed by GELU activation and Layer Normalization. To achieve translation invariance, spatial dimensions are collapsed via mean pooling (orderless aggregation), and the resulting multiscale representations from all M graphs are concatenated and passed through a single FC layer for classification. The entire GTN module is trained end-to-end with focal loss while the backbone remains frozen.

## Results

Using a frozen ConvNeXt-L backbone, GTN achieves 79.3% on DTD, 92.5% on KTH, 84.9% on GTOS, and 93.9% on GTOS-M, outperforming RADAM by 1.9, 3.2, 0.9, and 8.1 percentage points respectively. On GTOS-M, GTN consistently surpasses RADAM across all ConvNeXt variants (N/T/B/L) by 8.3–12.6 points. However, GTN underperforms RADAM on FMD across all backbones (e.g., 88.1% vs 89.3% with ConvNeXt-L), and with a ResNet50 backbone it trails MPAP on DTD (74.6% vs 78.0%) while leading on KTH (89.3% vs 89.0%) and GTOS-M (91.6% vs 88.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RADAM (the most recent SOTA), GTN wins on DTD, KTH, and GTOS-M across all four ConvNeXt backbone sizes, with the largest margins on GTOS-M (up to +12.6 pp with ConvNeXt-B) and KTH (up to +3.1 pp with ConvNeXt-L). GTN loses to RADAM on FMD for every backbone variant (by 0.2–1.4 pp) and narrowly on GTOS with ConvNeXt-N (−0.8 pp). With a ResNet50 backbone, GTN outperforms all 12 baselines on KTH (89.3%) and GTOS-M (91.6%), surpassing the previous best MPAP by 0.3 and 3.5 pp respectively, but trails MPAP (78.0%) and DSR-Net (77.6%) on DTD (74.6%), likely because DTD contains more object-level shape information that GTN's early-stage graph encoding discards. The t-SNE visualizations confirm GTN produces more homogeneous class clusters than RADAM, supporting the claim that directional message passing among latent attributes improves discriminability.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTN carves new ground by repurposing graph neural network message passing to model directional, hierarchical relationships among latent texture attributes, a conceptual framing absent from prior texture recognition work. While methods like MAP-net [51] and MPAP [50] studied correlations between visual texture attributes, and multi-layer feature extraction approaches (MuLTER [17], MSBFEN [40]) captured multiscale information, none modeled the directed, asymmetric dependencies among latent attributes or formalized multiscale interaction through separate graphs per backbone layer. The self-loop re-calibration mechanism draws from Cluster-GCN's diagonal enhancement [7], and the frozen-backbone protocol follows RADAM [38], but the graph-based encoding of feature maps as latent attribute vertices is a novel architectural choice for texture recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GTN demonstrates that graph-based message passing can enrich texture representations without fine-tuning pre-trained backbones, offering a plug-and-play module applicable to various CNN architectures. However, the empirical gains over RADAM are modest (typically 0.5–1.5% on DTD/KTH), and the method underperforms on datasets with significant object-level information (FMD, DTD with ResNet50), suggesting the graph formulation may not capture shape-dependent texture cues. The self-loop re-calibration, a claimed robustness mechanism, yields only marginal improvements (≤0.1%), raising questions about its practical necessity. Open questions include whether the learned directional adjacency matrices are interpretable in terms of known texture attribute hierarchies, and whether the approach generalizes to 3D material recognition or texture segmentation tasks.
