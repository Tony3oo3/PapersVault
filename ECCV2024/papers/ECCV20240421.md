## Problem

Non-autoregressive Transformers (NATs) generate images in few steps via parallel decoding, but require a complex generation policy (re-masking ratio, sampling temperatures, guidance scale at each step) that is typically hand-crafted with static scheduling functions. These manually designed policies are sub-optimal, demand expert knowledge, and apply uniformly across all samples, failing to adapt to the diverse characteristics of individual generations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240421/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (ImageNet 256×256 or 512×512) or a text prompt (MS-COCO, CC3M) as input and produces a single RGB image. Internally, the pretrained NAT model operates in a 1024-codebook VQGAN token space, and the policy network consumes the NAT's intermediate feature representation f_θ(v^(t)) at each decoding step as its state observation. The pretrained NAT backbone remains entirely frozen; only the lightweight policy network and adversarial reward model are trained.

## Method

AdaNAT formulates generation-policy determination as a T-horizon Markov decision process where a lightweight policy network (agent) observes the current generation state (step index + visual tokens) and outputs four actions per step: re-masking ratio m^(t), decoding temperature τ1^(t), re-masking temperature τ2^(t), and classifier-free guidance scale w^(t). The policy is trained with PPO, using a stochastic wrapper (Gaussian noise around the deterministic output) for exploration. The key technical contribution is an adversarial reward design: rather than using FID or a frozen pre-trained reward model (both of which the policy overfits, yielding poor diversity or quality), a discriminator r_ψ is trained concurrently in a minimax game with the policy network, dynamically adjusting the reward signal to prevent overfitting and maintain a balance between fidelity and diversity.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, AdaNAT-L (206M params) achieves FID-50K of 2.86 with 8 steps (0.9 TFLOPs), outperforming all NAT baselines and most diffusion models. On ImageNet 512×512, AdaNAT-L (232M params) reaches FID-50K of 3.66 with 8 steps (1.2 TFLOPs), surpassing U-ViT-H (4.60) and DiT-XL (5.44) at the same step count. For text-to-image, AdaNAT-S achieves FID-30K of 5.75 on MS-COCO (0.3 TFLOPs) and AdaNAT-Muse achieves FID-30K of 6.83 on CC3M (2.8 TFLOPs), both outperforming their respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ImageNet 256×256, AdaNAT-L (8 steps, FID 2.86) substantially outperforms all NAT baselines: MaskGIT (6.18), MaskGIT-RS (4.02), Token-Critic (4.69), MAGE (6.93), and MaskGIT-FSQ (4.53), while also beating the few-step diffusion models U-ViT-H (3.37) and LDM (4.56) at the same 8-step budget. On ImageNet 512×512, AdaNAT-L (FID 3.66, 1.2 TFLOPs) surpasses U-ViT-H (4.60, 3.4 TFLOPs) and DiT-XL (5.44, 9.6 TFLOPs) with significantly lower compute. On MS-COCO, AdaNAT-S (FID 5.75, 0.3 TFLOPs) outperforms U-ViT (6.37, 0.6 TFLOPs) and Frido (8.97). On CC3M, AdaNAT-Muse (FID 6.83, 2.8 TFLOPs) beats Muse (7.67, 2.8 TFLOPs) at equal compute, and still outperforms Muse even when Muse's budget is doubled to 16 steps (7.01, 5.4 TFLOPs). The only method that achieves a lower FID on ImageNet 256×256 is Draft-and-revise (3.41), but it requires 72 steps and 1.4B parameters versus AdaNAT-L's 8 steps and 206M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaNAT builds directly on the non-autoregressive Transformer (NAT) generation framework established by MaskGIT and extended by Token-Critic, MAGE, and Muse, which all rely on hand-designed scheduling functions for parallel decoding parameters (re-masking ratio, temperatures, guidance scale). The key departure is formulating policy configuration as a Markov decision process solvable via PPO, drawing on the RL-for-generation lineage (SeqGAN, RL-GAN-Net) while keeping the NAT backbone frozen. The adversarial reward design repurposes the GAN discriminator as a dynamic reward signal, distinguishing it from AutoNAT which uses FID as a static optimization objective and from diffusion-based RL fine-tuning methods that modify the generator itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that directly optimizing FID as a reward produces deceptively low scores alongside visibly degraded images (FID 2.56 with distortions) is a useful cautionary result for anyone applying metric-driven RL to generation. The adversarial reward offers a practical, annotation-free alternative to expensive pre-trained reward models like ImageReward, and the per-sample adaptive policy shows that static scheduling is measurably suboptimal (40% relative FID improvement over the baseline NAT). Open questions include whether the approach scales to the 3B-parameter regime of Muse, whether the RL training cost (1000 iterations with trajectory sampling) is prohibitive for rapid iteration, and whether the adversarial reward generalizes to other token-based architectures beyond U-ViT.
