## Problem

Existing open-set domain adaptation methods rely on a two-phase heuristic (separate unknown classes, then perform closed-set UDA), which cannot strictly bound the target generalization error because the gap between the true target distribution and heuristic-inferred known samples is uncontrolled. Additionally, the joint error arising from conditional shift is typically ignored, making the error bound loose when domain shift is large. This paper seeks an end-to-end formulation that simultaneously minimizes both the joint error and the open-set risk with a rigorous theoretical guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled RGB images from an incomplete source domain (containing only known classes) and unlabeled RGB images from a target domain (containing both known and unknown classes) as input. It produces a class prediction for each target image: either one of the K−1 known class labels or an 'unknown' label. The architecture uses an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 as a shared feature extractor g, with three separate 2-layer linear classifiers (hypothesis h, source labeling function f_S′, and target labeling function f_T′) operating on the extracted features.

## Method

PUJE formulates OUDA as a multi-class positive-unlabeled learning problem with domain shift, deriving an end-to-end loss that upper-bounds the target error via a joint-error-based bound where intractable source-domain expectations are estimated using PU learning theory (specifically, the Unknown Predictive Discrepancy). The key technical contribution is the Open-set Margin Discrepancy (OMD), a new distance metric between classifiers that quantifies disagreement via log(1−p[K]) margins, enabling the PU learning strategy to be smoothly embedded into the domain adaptation error bound. Training proceeds as an adversarial min-max game: the feature extractor g and hypothesis h minimize the combined classification and discrepancy loss, while the source and target labeling functions f_S′ and f_T′ maximize the discrepancy within their respective hypothesis spaces (defined by semi-supervised regularization including entropy minimization, pseudo labeling, and consistency regularization). The known-class ratio α is estimated online during training, and a PU coefficient β prevents degenerate solutions where all target samples are classified as unknown.

## Results

On Office-Home, PUJE achieves a mean OS of 77.0% and OS* of 78.3%, outperforming the strongest baseline PGL (74.0% OS, 75.9% OS*) by 3.0% in OS. On the more challenging Syn2Real-O benchmark (90% unknown ratio), PUJE reaches 66.0% OS and 67.3% HOS, surpassing the best reproducible baseline PGL* (64.1% OS, 62.0% HOS) by 1.9% and 5.3% respectively. PUJE also achieves the highest HOS on Office-Home (65.4% vs. ANNA's 64.7%), confirming balanced performance on both known-class accuracy and unknown-class recognition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUJE consistently outperforms all seven baselines on the OS* (known-class accuracy) metric across both benchmarks, achieving 78.3% mean on Office-Home vs. PGL's 75.9%, DAOD's 70.4%, and OSBP's 70.1%. On Syn2Real-O, PUJE leads in OS (66.0%) over the best reproducible PGL* (64.1%) and OSBP (54.8%), with a notable 5.3% HOS margin over PGL* (67.3% vs. 62.0%). The paper notes that PGL's originally reported Syn2Real-O OS of 65.5% could not be reproduced; their re-implementation yielded only 64.1% with UNK dropping to ~30%, suggesting a potential issue with the original evaluation. On the HOS metric (Office-Home), PUJE (65.4%) edges out ANNA (64.7%) and substantially surpasses ROS (58.9%) and OSLPP (60.9%). STA achieves the highest UNK scores (e.g., 77.0% on Pr→Ar) but at the cost of very low OS* (48.4%), illustrating the trade-off that PUJE's joint-error-based framework avoids by balancing both known and unknown recognition simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

PUJE extends the joint error-based target error bound from closed-set unsupervised domain adaptation (Zhang et al., TMLR 2023) to the open-set setting by incorporating multi-class positive-unlabeled learning theory (Xu et al., IJCAI 2017) to handle the incomplete source domain. This departs fundamentally from the dominant two-phase paradigm in OUDA (OSBP, STA, DAOD, PGL, ANNA, etc.), which first heuristically separates unknown classes and then applies closed-set UDA, creating an unbounded gap between the true target distribution and the inferred known subset. The novel Open-set Margin Discrepancy (OMD) serves as a bridge, allowing the PU learning estimation of source-domain expectations to plug directly into the joint error-based discrepancy term, yielding an end-to-end training objective rather than a pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first OUDA method with a rigorous generalization bound that simultaneously controls both the joint error (from conditional shift) and the open-set risk, addressing a gap that prior theoretical analyses left unaddressed. The practical implication is that the method avoids the fragile heuristic unknown-separation step that limits existing approaches, particularly under large domain shifts where marginal alignment alone is insufficient. Limitations include the strong Assumption 2 (perfect feature alignment via UDA), the need for three separate classifiers plus a feature extractor trained via a min-max game, and evaluation restricted to image classification on two benchmarks. Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees hold when the feature alignment assumption is relaxed, and whether the approach extends to other modalities or tasks beyond classification.
