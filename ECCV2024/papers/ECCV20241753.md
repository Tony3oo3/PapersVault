## Problem

Fine-tuning CLIP for unsupervised domain adaptation causes catastrophic forgetting, where the model rapidly loses its pre-trained knowledge (up to 25% accuracy drop). Existing mitigations like PADCLIP's catastrophic forgetting measurement operate in image space, making them incompatible with feature-space UDA techniques (discrepancy reduction, feature augmentation), while SKD requires 5× slower training. The paper asks whether converting CLIP features into a more robust representation space can simultaneously prevent forgetting and enable traditional UDA methods to work in the CLIP setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source-domain images, unlabeled target-domain images, and text prompts formatted as 'a [DOMAIN] of a [CLASS]' (e.g., 'a real photo of a car'). CLIP's 512-dimensional image and text features are projected into 4096-dimensional hypervectors via random projection (total 2.1M dimensions), and the generator for Feature Augmentation consumes reshaped hypervectors (4096×512 → 4096×32×16 spatial layout). Outputs are class predictions via softmax over cosine similarities in hypervector space, plus synthesized target-domain hypervectors produced by the GAN for training augmentation.

## Method

HVCLIP converts CLIP's vision and text encoder features into hypervector space using Random Projection (LSH-style), exploiting the redundancy and noise-robustness of high-dimensional vectors so that small fine-tuning perturbations produce only small loss changes. Three losses operate in this space: Forgetting Reduction minimizes cosine distance between cached original CLIP features and fine-tuned features; Discrepancy Reduction minimizes cosine distance between randomly paired source and target hypervectors (enabled by the near-orthogonality of irrelevant attribute bindings); and Feature Augmentation trains a modified ResNet-18 GAN to synthesize labeled target-domain hypervectors from source hypervectors via VSA binding/unbinding operations, with a cycle-consistency loss. The hypervector dimension of 4096 is empirically validated (effective from 1024 onward), and the entire pipeline is model-agnostic, supporting both vision-language (CLIP) and pure vision (BiT, DeiT) backbones.

## Results

On VisDA-2017 with ViT-B/16, HVCLIP achieves 92.5% average accuracy, outperforming VFR (91.7%), SKD (91.2%), and PADCLIP (90.9%). On Office-Home (ViT-B/16), it reaches 92.0% average, surpassing LLaVO (91.6%, which uses an additional LLM) and SKD (89.2%). On DomainNet (ViT-B/16), HVCLIP achieves 66.9% average, improving over LLaVO (64.7%) by +2.2%, with particularly large gains on QuickDraw transitions (e.g., +27% on ClipArt→QuickDraw). In few-shot learning (ImageNet 16-shot), it reaches 70.1%, and in continual learning (CIFAR-100, 20 steps), it achieves 81.7% average accuracy, outperforming ZSCL by +1.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVCLIP achieves the best average accuracy across all four UDA benchmarks. On VisDA-2017 (ViT-B/16), it outperforms VFR by +0.8%, SKD by +1.3%, and PADCLIP by +1.6%. On Office-Home (ViT-B/16), it surpasses LLaVO (which uses an additional FLAN-T5 LLM) by +0.4% and SKD by +2.8%. On DomainNet (ViT-B/16), it leads LLaVO by +2.2% and PADCLIP by +3.2%, with the largest gains on QuickDraw transitions where LLaVO struggles due to absence from LLM training data. In few-shot learning (ImageNet 16-shot), HVCLIP reaches 70.1%, beating VFR (69.2%), PADCLIP (69.0%), and Tip-Adapter-F (68.6%). For continual learning, it outperforms ZSCL by +1.3% average accuracy on CIFAR-100 (20 steps) and +1.3% on TinyImageNet (20 steps). The method is also model-agnostic, beating PADCLIP by +0.8% on CLIP, +0.9% on BiT, and +1.5% on DeiT, confirming generality beyond vision-language models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's vision-language pre-training and Kanerva's hyperdimensional computing to address catastrophic forgetting in UDA fine-tuning. It departs from the dominant paradigm of either freezing CLIP's vision encoder or applying image-space regularization (PADCLIP's CFM) by instead converting features into a high-dimensional hypervector space, where the inherent redundancy provides robustness against small perturbations during fine-tuning. The VSA operations (binding/unbinding/bundling) from VSAIT are repurposed to enable unpaired feature translation for Feature Augmentation and Discrepancy Reduction, effectively bringing traditional discrepancy-based and synthetic UDA methods into the CLIP setting in a way that prior CLIP-based UDA methods (PADCLIP, SKD, VFR) could not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a unified, feature-space framework that simultaneously mitigates catastrophic forgetting, reduces domain discrepancy, and synthesizes target features—addressing the incompatibility between CLIP fine-tuning strategies and classical UDA techniques. The model-agnostic property (working across CLIP, BiT, DeiT) and generalization to few-shot and continual learning broaden its practical relevance. However, the computational overhead (30 hours vs. 16.5 hours baseline, largely from the GAN-based Feature Augmentation) and the somewhat ad hoc choice of 4096 hypervector dimension limit immediate adoption. Open questions include whether the dimension threshold generalizes across architectures and whether the GAN component can be replaced with lighter alternatives.
