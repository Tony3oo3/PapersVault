## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction and generation methods rely on triplane representations with volumetric rendering, which is computationally expensive and forces reduced 3D resolution, limiting representational capacity. While 3D Gaussians offer real-time rendering, their unstructured nature and highly inter-dependent properties make them difficult to predict in a generalizable feed-forward framework. The paper addresses both gaps by combining pixel-aligned Gaussians with a scalable transformer architecture for fast, high-fidelity sparse-view reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240378/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The reconstructor takes 4 sparse-view RGB images at 512×512 resolution along with their camera poses (encoded via Plücker embeddings) and outputs a set of V×H×W ≈ 1 million pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians, each parameterized by depth along the viewing ray, rotation quaternion, scale, opacity, and DC spherical-harmonic color. For generation tasks, a text prompt or single image is first converted to 4 multi-view images by a diffusion model (Instant3D or Zero123++), which then feeds the reconstructor. The resulting Gaussians render novel views in real time via differentiable rasterization.

## Method

GRM uses a pure transformer pipeline: a 24-layer self-attention encoder (channel width 768) processes all views jointly after patch-convolution tokenization, enabling cross-view information exchange, followed by a 4-block transformer-based upsampler that progressively doubles spatial resolution using PixelShuffle and shifted windowed self-attention (window size 4096) to recover high-frequency details. The key architectural insight is constraining Gaussian positions along input viewing rays (μ = c_o + τr) so the network only predicts a 12-channel attribute map per pixel (depth, rotation, scale, opacity, SH-DC) rather than free 3D coordinates, which alleviates the ill-posedness of predicting unstructured Gaussians. Training is supervised with L2 + perceptual loss on rendered novel views plus alpha-mask supervision to remove floaters, and a sigmoid-based linear interpolation between predefined scale bounds replaces the conventional exponential activation for stability. The model is trained on 40M images (GSO dataset) at 512×512 for ~4 days on 32 A100 GPUs, and achieves 0.1s inference by predicting 16× more Gaussians than competing methods.

## Results

On sparse-view 3D reconstruction (64 novel views, 100 objects from GSO), GRM achieves PSNR 30.05, SSIM 0.906, and LPIPS 0.052 with an inference time of 0.11 seconds, substantially outperforming the next-best feed-forward method MV-LRM (PSNR 25.38, LPIPS 0.068) by ~4.7 dB in PSNR. For single image-to-3D generation, GRM reaches PSNR 20.10, CLIP 0.932, and FID 27.4 in 5 seconds, surpassing all baselines including DreamGaussian (PSNR 19.19) and One-2-3-45++ (FID 42.1). For text-to-3D, GRM attains the highest CLIP score (18.5) and user preference (29.5%) among all methods while running in 8 seconds versus 1 hour for the optimization-based MVDream.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRM decisively outperforms all 12 baselines across all three tasks. In sparse-view reconstruction (Table 1), GRM beats MV-LRM by 4.67 dB PSNR and 0.016 LPIPS while using the same 4 input views; it also surpasses SparseNeuS and IBRNet (which require 16 views) by 7.45 and 8.55 dB PSNR respectively. The concurrent LGM is the closest competitor in inference speed (0.07s vs 0.11s) but trails GRM by 6.26 dB PSNR, attributed to GRM predicting 16× more Gaussians. In single image-to-3D (Table 2), GRM leads all baselines on every metric: +0.91 dB PSNR over DreamGaussian, +0.014 CLIP over One-2-3-45++, and 14.7 lower FID than LGM, all within 5 seconds. In text-to-3D (Table 3), GRM achieves the highest CLIP score (18.5 vs 17.9 for Instant3D) and user preference (29.5% vs 25.9% for MVDream), though MVDream retains a slight edge on R-Prec (70.1 vs 67.5) and AP (74.4 vs 72.0) at the cost of 450× slower inference (1 hour vs 8 sec).

## Relation to Prior Work

GRM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) feed-forward sparse-view reconstruction (LRM, MV-LRM, LGM), (2) 3D Gaussian Splatting as a scene representation (Kerbl et al.), and (3) pixel-aligned Gaussians for generalizable reconstruction (PixelSplat, Splatter Image). The key architectural departure is replacing the triplane/NeRF representation used by LRM and MV-LRM with pixel-aligned 3D Gaussians, and replacing the conventional U-Net decoder (as in LGM, TriplaneGaussian) with a pure transformer encoder plus a windowed-attention upsampler inspired by Swin Transformer. For generation, GRM reuses the multi-view diffusion heads from Instant3D and Zero123++ without modification, positioning itself as a drop-in, faster reconstruction backend.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~0.1 s inference time for four-view 3D reconstruction, combined with a large quality gap over triplane-based baselines, makes GRM a practical building block for real-time 3D pipelines (robotics, AR, content creation). The ablations suggest that the pixel-aligned constraint and the transformer upsampler are both critical, pointing to a broader lesson that representation choice (Gaussians vs. implicit fields) and non-local upsampling matter more than previously appreciated for feed-forward 3D. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond object-level scenes to full environments, whether the 4-view requirement can be relaxed, and whether the GSO-trained model generalizes to in-the-wild photos without domain adaptation.
