## Problem

Existing single-image NVS methods that fine-tune pre-trained T2I diffusion models (e.g., Zero-1-to-3, Zero123-XL) require additional expensive fine-tuning to adopt plug-and-play generation modules like ControlNet and LoRA, since those modules were trained against the original T2I parameters. This limits practical deployment and customization. The paper asks whether one can adapt a frozen T2I model for multi-view synthesis without modifying its billions of parameters, while still achieving geometric consistency across views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241290/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB reference image (not corrupted during diffusion to preserve fine details) and N relative camera poses (3×4 extrinsic matrices) as input, and simultaneously outputs N rendered novel-view images in a single forward pass (default N=4). Optionally, ControlNet conditions (depth, canny edges, HED) or LoRA style adapters can be attached at inference time without any retraining. For 3D reconstruction, the multi-view outputs feed into Score Distillation Sampling to produce an InstantNGP radiance field.

## Method

NVS-Adapter inserts two trainable components into every U-Net block of a frozen Stable Diffusion model: (1) a view-consistency cross-attention that first aggregates features across all N target views using Perceiver-style learnable tokens (with Plücker-coordinate ray positional embeddings) and then cross-attends each target view to the reference view features, and (2) a global semantic conditioning cross-attention that injects CLIP image embeddings of the reference into each view. The key architectural shift is replacing the standard independence assumption—where each target view is denoised independently from the reference—with a joint denoising formulation in which each target view's posterior depends on all other target views' noisy latents, explicitly aligning geometric correspondences. Only the added cross-attention parameters are trained (on 800K Objaverse assets for 200K iterations), leaving all T2I weights frozen, which is what makes the module fully compatible with existing ControlNet and LoRA adapters without any additional optimization.

## Results

On the Objaverse validation set, NVS-Adapter (SD1.5) achieves PSNR 17.05, SSIM 0.8338, and LPIPS 0.1480 without any external conditioning. When combined with ControlNet (HED condition), it reaches PSNR 22.02, SSIM 0.8982, and LPIPS 0.0811 on Objaverse and PSNR 22.77, SSIM 0.8820, LPIPS 0.0912 on Google Scanned Objects, substantially outperforming all baselines. On GSO without ControlNet, NVS-Adapter achieves the best LPIPS (0.1207) among all methods, indicating superior perceptual quality while exploiting the full generative capacity of the frozen T2I model. Notably, these results are obtained without fine-tuning any of the pre-trained T2I parameters, training only the additional cross-attention modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Zero-1-to-3, the base NVS-Adapter (without ControlNet) trades PSNR (17.05 vs. 19.52 on Objaverse) for better perceptual quality on GSO (LPIPS 0.1207 vs. 0.1283), reflecting its ability to leverage the frozen T2I model's generative capacity. Against Zero123-XL, the base model is competitive on GSO (PSNR 17.87 vs. 18.46) and achieves the best SSIM (0.8469 vs. 0.8351) and LPIPS (0.1207 vs. 0.1267). The decisive advantage emerges when ControlNet is attached: with HED conditioning, NVS-Adapter surpasses Zero-1-to-3 by +2.50 dB PSNR on Objaverse (22.02 vs. 19.52) and +4.41 dB on GSO (22.77 vs. 18.36), and beats Zero123-XL by +4.31 dB on Objaverse and +4.31 dB on GSO. Crucially, this ControlNet compatibility requires zero additional training, whereas prior methods like Zero123++ must fine-tune the T2I parameters to incorporate new conditions. For 3D reconstruction via SDS (Figure 3), NVS-Adapter produces geometrically consistent 3D shapes that avoid the multi-view inconsistencies of Zero-1-to-3 and Zero123-XL, though no quantitative 3D metrics are reported against DreamFusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in single-image NVS (Zero-1-to-3, Zero123-XL, MVDream, Zero123++) where the pre-trained T2I model's billions of parameters are fine-tuned on multi-view datasets. Instead, it freezes the T2I model entirely and trains only lightweight cross-attention adapters, borrowing the learnable-token aggregation mechanism from Perceiver to align multi-view features. It also explicitly rejects the independence assumption in prior denoising formulations, introducing joint target-view and reference-view alignment to enforce geometric consistency. The plug-and-play positioning connects it to the ControlNet/LoRA/T2I-Adapter ecosystem, but unlike Zero123++ which requires additional fine-tuning to attach ControlNet, NVS-Adapter is compatible out of the box.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can leverage the existing ecosystem of ControlNet and LoRA models for controllable and customized multi-view generation without any retraining, lowering the barrier to NVS in production pipelines. The parameter efficiency (training only cross-attention modules vs. billions of T2I parameters) makes the approach more accessible. However, the method is limited to object-level NVS on fixed viewpoints (N=4), and the frozen T2I backbone may cap the ceiling of geometric fidelity compared to fully fine-tuned approaches. Open questions include whether the adapter approach extends to scene-level NVS, whether it scales to arbitrary numbers of views, and whether the plug-and-play compatibility degrades as ControlNet/LoRA models diverge further from the base T2I distribution.
