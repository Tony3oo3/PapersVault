## Problem

Multi-task 3D perception in autonomous driving (e.g., 3D object detection and 3D lane detection) suffers from feature competition and gradient imbalance when using traditional multi-head architectures, because each task requires fundamentally different geometric representations (cubic bounding boxes vs. ordered 1-D lane lines). This representation mismatch forces task-specific heads to compete for shared backbone features, destabilizing training and preventing the model from exploiting the intrinsic geometric and semantic connections between tasks within the same scene.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images from 5 cameras (front and sides) at a final resolution of 1408×512, along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, within a perception range of ±84.88 m (X/Y) and ±10 m (Z). Rather than producing task-specific outputs directly, RFTR predicts a single unified set of representative vector fields (RepVF) — each target encoded as n=15 3D points with an associated scalar vector — which are then differentially and parameter-free converted into 7-D 3D bounding boxes (x, y, z, l, w, h, θ) for objects and ordered 3D point sequences for lanes, along with category scores. This conversion step allows the model to be supervised entirely with existing task labels without any task-specific loss design.

## Method

RepVF represents each perception target as a set of n unordered 3D sampling points S_i with an associated vector field F_i(S_i) ⊆ R^{3+d}, where the vector encodes heading for objects and inter-point direction for lanes, providing a task-agnostic, anisotropic geometric description. RFTR builds on the DETR/PETR transformer decoder with a ResNet-50 + FPN backbone, but replaces single-point queries with set-level queries (each encoding n=15 3D locations via flattened positional embeddings), enabling the decoder to capture spatial extent rather than just a center. A single unified head with two small MLP branches (geometric regression Φ_g and semantic prediction Φ_s) predicts all RepVF targets simultaneously across 750 queries (600 for objects, 150 for lanes) over 6 decoder layers. Because all tasks share the same representation and head parameters, the approach eliminates task-specific heads entirely, and the paper demonstrates that this naturally reduces gradient disparity (mean 2.47 vs. 2.98 for multi-head baselines) without requiring explicit gradient calibration strategies. Training uses Hungarian matching with L1 regression on coordinates/vectors and focal loss on classification, with empirically chosen loss weights that are similar across tasks.

## Results

On the OpenLane validation set for 3D lane detection, RFTR achieves a F-Score of 61.8 and category accuracy of 91.6%, surpassing the previous best PETRv2-V-400 (61.2 F-Score) while using a far sparser representation. On the Waymo Open Dataset validation set for 3D object detection, RFTR achieves 19.5 mAP / 18.7 mAPH overall, with strong performance on cyclists (27.8 mAP) but trailing PETR on vehicles (22.6 vs. 31.1 mAP) and pedestrians (8.0 vs. 12.1 mAP). The model uses only 37.4M parameters with a single unified head for both tasks simultaneously.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 3D lane detection on OpenLane, RFTR (61.8 F-Score) outperforms all 10 baselines, including the strongest competitor PETRv2-V-400 (61.2) which uses a >26× denser representation, and BEV-LaneDet (58.4). RFTR also achieves the best Z-error in both near (0.073 m) and far (0.107 m) ranges, and the best far-range X-error (0.450 m) among all methods. For 3D object detection on Waymo, RFTR (19.5 mAP) is slightly below PETR (20.9 mAP) overall, with a notable gap on vehicles (22.6 vs. 31.1 mAP) and pedestrians (8.0 vs. 12.1 mAP), but surpasses PETR on cyclists (27.8 vs. 19.5 mAP) and substantially outperforms DETR3D (10.1 mAP). The paper notes the object detection results are preliminary and require further fine-tuning for smaller object classes, while the lane detection results represent the primary contribution of the unified representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DETR3D/PETR lineage of transformer-based multi-view 3D detection, adopting their 3D query mechanism and ResNet-50 + FPN backbone. It departs from the prevailing multi-head multi-task paradigm (as in PETRv2, FULLER, BEVFormer) by proposing RepVF, a unified vector-field representation that encodes both 3D bounding boxes and 3D lane lines as anisotropic point sets with directional vectors, enabling a single shared head to serve all tasks. The key conceptual departure is from prior multi-task optimization strategies (GradNorm, DWA, IMTL) that explicitly calibrate gradients; instead, the paper argues that unifying the representation itself eliminates the source of gradient competition, making such calibration unnecessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-head multi-task paradigm offers a cleaner architectural alternative to the growing zoo of task-specific heads in autonomous driving perception, potentially simplifying deployment and reducing parameter count. However, the evidence is limited to two tasks on one dataset combination, and the 3D object detection result (19.5 mAP) is slightly below PETR (20.9 mAP), suggesting the unified representation may not yet fully match task-specific optimization for all categories. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than two tasks (e.g., adding map segmentation, depth estimation), whether the gradient-balance benefit persists at scale, and whether the modest 16-dimensional positional encoding is sufficient for more complex scene understanding.
