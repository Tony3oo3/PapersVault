## Problem

State-of-the-art LiDAR semantic segmentation methods embed inductive bias tailored to mechanical spinning LiDAR (e.g., cylindrical partition, radial window), which constrains generalizability to other LiDAR technologies such as solid-state and hybrid-solid sensors and complicates hyperparameter tuning. The paper asks whether a single framework can achieve competitive performance across all LiDAR types without introducing type-specific structural assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240131/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes a sorted sparse voxel feature sequence (sparse tensor with per-point properties) extracted from raw LiDAR point clouds, with no projection to range images or BEV grids. It produces per-point semantic class labels for 14–19 categories depending on the dataset. A key design constraint is that the same architecture and hyperparameters are applied unchanged across mechanical spinning (nuScenes, SemanticKITTI), solid-state (PandaSet), and hybrid-solid (S.MID) LiDAR, with no positional encoding or LiDAR-specific partitioning required.

## Method

SFPNet replaces window-attention with Sparse Focal Point Modulation (SFPM), a plug-in module built on three stages: (1) multi-level context extraction via a cascade of 3D submanifold sparse convolutions with progressively increasing kernel sizes (k^l = k^{l-1}+2) to capture hierarchical local-to-long-range features, followed by global average pooling; (2) adaptive gated aggregation that learns spatial-aware weights per level to combine the multi-level contexts, allowing the network to emphasize or suppress each scale per token; and (3) a channel-wise information query implemented as a lightweight element-wise multiplication between a learned query projection and the aggregated context, preserving channel-specific information without full attention. SFPM is translation-invariant (operations are always centered at the query location), requires no positional encoding, and inherits sparsity from submanifold convolution, making it a drop-in replacement for window-attention in mainstream sparse backbones.

## Results

On nuScenes validation, SFPNet achieves 80.1% mIoU, surpassing SphereFormer (79.5%) and Cylinder3D (76.1%). On the novel S.MID hybrid-solid LiDAR dataset, SFPNet reaches 71.9% mIoU (val) and 70.9% (test), outperforming SphereFormer by 4.1% and Cylinder3D by 3.1% on validation. On PandaSet (solid-state LiDAR), SFPNet achieves 64.0% mIoU, beating Cylinder3D by 9.0% and SphereFormer by 0.5%. On SemanticKITTI, SFPNet attains 69.2% mIoU (val), marginally exceeding SphereFormer (69.0%) and Cylinder3D (65.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFPNet outperforms SphereFormer on nuScenes val (+0.6%), SemanticKITTI val (+0.2%), PandaSet val (+0.5%), and both S.MID splits (+4.1% val, +2.6% test), but trails SphereFormer on nuScenes test (−1.7%) and SemanticKITTI test (−4.5%). Against Cylinder3D, SFPNet wins on every dataset, with the largest margin on PandaSet (+9.0%) and S.MID val (+3.1%). On nuScenes val, SFPNet also surpasses all other LiDAR-only methods in Table 3 (e.g., RangeFormer 78.1%, RPVNet 77.6%) and even exceeds several multi-modal (LiDAR+Camera) approaches such as 2D3DNet (79.0%) and 2DPASS (79.4%). On S.MID, where the non-repetitive scanning pattern of Livox Mid-360 breaks the cylindrical/radial inductive biases of prior methods, Cylinder3D gains only +1.2% over the SSCN baseline while SFPNet gains +4.3%, highlighting the generalizability advantage of the focal point modulation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SFPNet is explicitly built on the Focal Modulation Networks paradigm (Yang et al., NeurIPS 2022), adapting its multi-level context extraction and gated aggregation to the sparse 3D LiDAR setting via submanifold sparse convolution. It departs from the dominant trend in LiDAR segmentation—where Cylinder3D introduces cylindrical partitioning and SphereFormer introduces radial windows to match mechanical spinning LiDAR point distributions—by arguing that such LiDAR-specific inductive bias limits generalizability to other sensor types. The paper positions SFPM as a drop-in replacement for window-attention in mainstream backbones, combining the spatial locality of submanifold convolution with the long-range context of attention without assuming any particular point distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LiDAR hardware diversifies beyond mechanical spinning sensors into solid-state and hybrid-solid technologies, the field needs architectures that are not locked to a single sensor's scanning pattern. The S.MID dataset (38,904 frames from Livox Mid-360 in industrial substations) fills a gap for robotic application benchmarks, and the finding that Cylinder3D and SphereFormer barely exceed the SSCN baseline on hybrid-solid data (+1.2% and +0.2% mIoU) while SFPNet gains +4.3% provides concrete evidence that sensor-specific inductive bias can be counterproductive. Open questions include whether SFPM scales to multi-sensor fusion, whether the gated aggregation mechanism generalizes to temporal/sequence-based segmentation, and whether the modest margins on standard benchmarks (0.6% over SphereFormer on nuScenes) would widen with stronger training recipes the authors deliberately excluded.
