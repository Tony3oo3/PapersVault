## Problem

Standard logit-based knowledge distillation can match teacher accuracy but does not ensure the student learns the same function as the teacher, leading to poor agreement, reliance on different (potentially spurious) input features, and loss of the teacher's interpretability properties. This paper asks whether explicitly matching the explanations produced by teacher and student can close this faithfulness gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241956/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (resized to 224×224 with random horizontal flipping and random cropping) and produces both a class prediction and a spatial explanation map for the teacher's predicted class. Explanations are either GradCAM heatmaps (for standard CNNs) or B-cos dynamic weight maps (for B-cos models), and the similarity is computed via cosine similarity between the two maps. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requiring no layer-wise correspondence between teacher and student, so it works identically whether the student is a ResNet, DenseNet, ConvNeXt, or ViT.

## Method

e²KD augments the standard KL-divergence logit distillation loss with a parameter-free explanation similarity term: L = L_KD + λ·(1 − cos_sim(E_T, E_S)), where E_T and E_S are the GradCAM or B-cos explanations of the teacher and student for the teacher's predicted class. Because the loss operates only on model outputs and their explanations, no architecture-specific layer matching is needed, making the method seamlessly transferable across CNNs and ViTs. The paper further introduces 'frozen explanations'—pre-computed teacher explanations that undergo the same spatial augmentations as the images during training—eliminating the need to query the teacher at every step while retaining most of the faithfulness gains. The approach is evaluated across three desiderata: student-teacher agreement under limited data, robustness to distribution shift (Waterbirds-100), and preservation of teacher-specific interpretability properties (VOC localization, ViT shift-equivariance).

## Results

On ImageNet with a ResNet-34→ResNet-18 distillation, e²KD(GradCAM) improves top-1 accuracy and teacher-student agreement over vanilla KD by +5.1/+6.2 p.p. at 50 shots, +1.0/+1.3 p.p. at 200 shots, and +0.0/+0.4 p.p. on full data. For B-cos models the gains are even larger at 50 shots (+8.6/+10.0 p.p.). On the Waterbirds-100 out-of-distribution evaluation, e²KD boosts accuracy from 20.5% to 32.2% and agreement from 55.5% to 64.4% for a ConvNeXt student, demonstrating that students learn to rely on the 'right' features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

e²KD consistently outperforms all five baselines across every dataset size and model family. Against vanilla KD [4,17], the gains are largest under data scarcity: +5.1 p.p. accuracy and +6.2 p.p. agreement at 50 shots (standard models), shrinking to +0.0/+0.4 on full data. Against feature-based methods AT [39] and CRD [33], e²KD leads by a wide margin at 50 shots (54.9% vs. 38.3% and 30.0% accuracy) and maintains a 1–2 p.p. edge on full data. Against the most competitive baselines ReviewKD [8] and CAT-KD [15], e²KD still wins on accuracy at every setting (e.g., 54.9% vs. 51.2% and 32.2% at 50 shots; 71.8% vs. 71.4% and 70.9% on full data) and on agreement at 50 shots (61.7% vs. 55.6% and 34.5%). On the Waterbirds-100 OOD test, e²KD improves accuracy by 11.7 p.p. over KD for ConvNeXt students (32.2% vs. 20.5%), confirming that the explanation-matching loss transfers the teacher's focus on 'right' features more effectively than any logit- or feature-based alternative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends logit-based knowledge distillation (Hinton et al.) by adding an explanation-similarity loss term, positioning itself between feature-based KD (which requires architecture-specific layer matching) and the recently emerged explanation-based KD approaches like CAT-KD. It follows the 'patient and consistent teaching' setup of Beyer et al. and draws on the B-cos model-inherent explanation framework, while explicitly framing the contribution around the faithfulness gap identified by Stanton et al. The model-guidance literature (Rao et al., Petryk et al.) inspires the mechanism of steering student attention via teacher explanations, but here the 'human annotator' is replaced by a teacher model, making the approach fully automated and parameter-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is strong: a single cosine-similarity term on GradCAM or B-cos maps, with no extra parameters or architecture-specific design, consistently improves both accuracy and teacher-student agreement, especially under data-limited settings. The finding that a ViT student can inherit the CNN teacher's shift-equivariant explanation behavior (periodicity 4 vs. its own 16) suggests architectural inductive biases are transferable through explanation matching, opening questions about what else can be distilled this way. Limitations include modest gains on full data (≤1 p.p.), reliance on differentiable explanation methods, and evaluation restricted to image classification; whether the approach generalizes to non-differentiable explanations, other modalities, or generative tasks remains open.
