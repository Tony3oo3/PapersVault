## Problem

Point cloud generation methods struggle to simultaneously achieve high sample quality, diversity, and flexible cardinality while remaining computationally efficient. VAEs lose high-frequency details due to spectral bias, and existing frequency-domain remedies (Fourier features, positional encoding) do not generalize to unstructured point clouds lacking a grid structure. Additionally, standard Gaussian priors in VAEs suffer from the prior hole problem, and direct DDPMs on high-dimensional point clouds incur prohibitive computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes Gaussian noise as input and produces a point cloud in R^3 with arbitrary cardinality (demonstrated from 2048 to 100k+ points). The key structural detail is that each point cloud is modeled as a distribution p(x|z) over its constituent points conditioned on a low-dimensional shape latent z, so the number of output points is determined at generation time rather than fixed at training. The shape latent is sampled from a learned DDPM prior, and individual points are decoded via a conditional normalizing flow, making the output cardinality decoupled from the latent dimensionality.

## Method

FrePolad is a two-stage pipeline: first, a PVCNN-based VAE is trained with a novel frequency-rectified ELBO (FreELBO) that incorporates spherical harmonic analysis to extract frequency information from point clouds and applies Gaussian-weighted rectifiers (r_l = exp(-(L-l)²/2σ²)) to penalize high-frequency reconstruction errors via a KKT-constrained optimization; second, the VAE is frozen and a U-Net-based DDPM is trained on the encoder latents to model the complex latent distribution. The decoder is a conditional continuous normalizing flow (CNF) that transforms Gaussian noise into individual points conditioned on the shape latent, enabling exact likelihood computation and arbitrary-cardinality sampling. The frequency rectification shifts reconstruction emphasis toward complex, less-smooth regions of the point cloud surface, while the latent DDPM avoids the prior hole problem and reduces generation cost by operating in a low-dimensional space rather than the full point cloud dimensionality.

## Results

On ShapeNet (airplane, chair, car classes), FrePolad achieves 1-NNA-CD scores of 65.25, 52.35, and 51.89, and 1-NNA-EMD scores of 62.10, 53.23, and 50.26, respectively—closely approaching the training-set ground truth (64.44/64.07, 51.28/54.76, 51.70/50.00). FrePolad outperforms the prior state-of-the-art LION on CD for all three classes and on EMD for car, while training in only 20 hours versus LION's 550 hours. Generation time remains nearly constant (~6.3 s) regardless of output cardinality, scaling from 2048 to 100k points with negligible overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FrePolad outperforms LION (the prior state-of-the-art) on 1-NNA-CD for all three classes (airplane: 65.25 vs. 67.41; chair: 52.35 vs. 53.70; car: 51.89 vs. 53.41) and on 1-NNA-EMD for car (50.26 vs. 51.14), while LION retains a slight edge on EMD for airplane (61.23 vs. 62.10) and chair (52.34 vs. 53.23). Against PVD, the next-best flow-based method, FrePolad improves CD by 8.57 (airplane), 3.91 (chair), and 2.66 (car), and EMD by 2.71, 0.09, and 3.57 respectively. FrePolad substantially outperforms GAN-based baselines (r-GAN, 1-GAN) by 20–30 points in CD across all classes, and also surpasses flow-based methods like PointFlow, SoftFlow, and DPF-Net by 8–15 points in CD. In terms of computational efficiency, FrePolad trains in 20 hours versus 550 hours for LION and 360 hours for PointFlow, while generating 2048-point shapes in 6.3 s—faster than LION (30.2 s) and DPC (27.34 s), though slower than PointFlow (1.73 s). FrePolad uniquely supports arbitrary output cardinality with near-constant generation time (6.30 s at 2048 points to 6.41 s at 100k points), a capability none of the fixed-cardinality baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

FrePolad sits at the intersection of the VAE+DDPM latent diffusion paradigm (as in LION [89] and latent diffusion for images [77]) and the distribution-of-distributions formulation for point clouds introduced by PointFlow [96]. It departs from LION by replacing the hierarchical two-DDPM latent space with a single flat DDPM and a single conditional normalizing flow decoder, and introduces a novel frequency rectification loss via spherical harmonics to counteract the spectral bias that degrades VAE reconstructions. The variable-cardinality capability, inherited from the PointFlow-style point-distribution decoder, is combined here with the DDPM prior for the first time in a unified, two-stage training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 27× reduction in training time (20 h vs. 550 h for LION) while matching or slightly exceeding quality makes high-fidelity point cloud generation far more accessible, and the constant generation cost across cardinalities (6.3 s for 2048 to 100k points) opens the door to dense LiDAR-style outputs without retraining. The spherical-harmonic frequency rectification offers a principled, grid-free remedy to the spectral-bias problem that plagues VAEs on unstructured data, though its benefit is moderate in the ablation (≈4 points 1-NNA-CD on airplane). Open questions include generalization beyond star-domain shapes, extension to non-spherical manifolds, and whether the frequency-rectification idea transfers to other modalities with high-frequency content.
