## Problem

Despite the proliferation of foundation models across visible, video, remote sensing, and point cloud modalities, no comparable foundation model exists for infrared imaging. Two barriers prevent a direct port of visible-image MAE: the absence of a large-scale infrared dataset for self-supervised pretraining, and the fact that infrared images carry substantially less information (average entropy 6.44 vs. 7.19 for ImageNet-1K) due to the lack of color and texture, making vanilla random masking suboptimal for representation learning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes single-channel infrared images (Inf30 spans resolutions from 40×23 to 6912×1024) and splits them into 16×16 patches. During pretraining, a 1×1 convolution with stride 16 produces an intensity-wise feature map whose per-patch mean gray value determines token ordering for the information-aware mask. The encoder outputs four multi-scale feature maps at H/4, H/8, H/16, and H/32 spatial resolutions, which are plugged into standard downstream heads (UperNet/FCN for segmentation, Mask R-CNN/Cascade R-CNN for detection, ISTR for small-target detection) after fine-tuning.

## Method

InfMAE replaces MAE's random masking with an information-aware strategy: tokens are sorted in descending order of their patch-level gray value (a proxy for information richness), then sampled at a fixed stride S=4 so that information-rich patches are preferentially masked, forcing the model to reconstruct the most informative regions without requiring an auxiliary semantic branch. The encoder is multi-scale, combining two ConvNeXt-style CNN layers (with Conv Attention and FFN, producing F1 at H/4 and F2 at H/8) and one ViT-style Transformer layer (producing learned visible tokens Ts), inspired by MCMAE. The infrared decoder is deliberately shallow—only 2 Transformer layers versus MAE's 8—because infrared images lack the fine texture and color detail that would benefit from deeper reconstruction. Pretraining uses MSE loss on masked patches over the newly released Inf30 dataset (305,241 deduplicated infrared images) for 400 epochs with AdamW, after which the multi-scale encoder features are transferred to downstream tasks.

## Results

On the MSRS-inf semantic segmentation task with UperNet, InfMAE achieves 74.3% mIoU and 82.5% mAcc, surpassing the strongest self-supervised baseline MCMAE (72.1% mIoU) by 2.2 points and the best supervised method DDRNet (67.3% mIoU) by 7.0 points. On M3FD-inf object detection with Mask R-CNN, InfMAE reaches 56.2% mAP, outperforming MCMAE (55.7%) and all supervised detectors (best: Sparse R-CNN at 48.3%). On IRSTD-1k small target detection, InfMAE attains 91.2% AUC, 79.5% F1, and 66.0% IoU, leading all compared methods across these three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfMAE consistently outperforms all self-supervised baselines (Vanilla MAE, MCMAE, UM-PVT) across all three downstream tasks. On semantic segmentation (UperNet), InfMAE gains +2.2 mIoU over MCMAE (74.3 vs. 72.1) and +2.9 over Vanilla MAE (74.3 vs. 71.4), while also surpassing the best supervised method DDRNet by 7.0 mIoU. On object detection (Mask R-CNN), InfMAE achieves the highest mAP (56.2%) but trails MCMAE slightly on AP50 (88.1% vs. 88.4%); with Cascade R-CNN, InfMAE leads both mAP (55.8% vs. 54.7%) and AP50 (86.3% vs. 85.1%). On small target detection, InfMAE dominates with the best AUC (91.2%), F1 (79.5%), and IoU (66.0%), beating MCMAE by 0.4 AUC and 1.1 F1, and UM-PVT by 2.3 AUC. The supervised baselines (DETR, Sparse R-CNN, DINO, YoloV8, LPNetGA, DNANet, ISTR) are all substantially outperformed, with the largest gap being 15.4 mAP over YoloV8 on detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

InfMAE extends the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) framework to the infrared modality, incorporating the multi-scale convolutional-attention encoder from MCMAE while replacing random masking with an information-aware strategy that prioritizes high-intensity regions. It departs from prior semantic-guided masking approaches (SemMAE, ATTMask) by avoiding additional semantic branches, instead using a simple intensity-based sorting criterion. The paper positions itself as the first MIM-based foundation model for infrared, following the pattern established by VideoMAE, Scale-MAE, and Point-M2AE in their respective modalities, and introduces the Inf30 dataset (305K images) to address the data scarcity that has prevented such a model from emerging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a notable gap by providing the first infrared-specific foundation model and a large-scale pretraining dataset, which should accelerate progress across the infrared vision community. The finding that a depth-2 decoder outperforms deeper variants (up to depth 12) is a useful practical insight reflecting infrared's lower information content, and the dataset ablation showing performance degradation from 300K to 500K unprocessed images highlights that diversity matters more than raw scale. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger model sizes, whether the information-aware masking generalizes to other low-texture modalities (e.g., thermal, SAR), and whether the modest gains over MCMAE persist on more challenging or diverse infrared benchmarks.
