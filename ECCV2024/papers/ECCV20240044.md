## Problem

Spectrally multiplexed photometric stereo enables dynamic surface recovery from a single multi-channel frame, but existing methods require precisely calibrated directional lights, aligned sensor spectral sensitivities, and strong surface assumptions (Lambertian, convex, uniform chromaticity), making them impractical for uncontrolled real-world settings. The spectral ambiguity problem—where different combinations of light, surface, and sensor spectra produce identical observations—renders the task ill-posed under traditional physics-based formulations, and no benchmark dataset existed for systematic evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single spectrally multiplexed image of shape h×w×k (where k is an arbitrary, order-agnostic number of sensor channels, e.g., 3 for RGB or 5 for NIR) along with an optional binary object mask of shape h×w, and produces a per-pixel surface normal map of shape h×w×3 at the original resolution. Each channel is split from the image and concatenated with the mask before encoding, so the network never sees wavelength-dependent color information—only grayscale intensity per channel. The architecture is agnostic to both the number of input channels and their ordering, enabling the same trained model to handle RGB, NIR, or mixed-channel inputs without retraining.

## Method

SpectraM-PS is a physics-free, Transformer-based network that directly regresses surface normals from multiplexed observations without explicit lighting or reflectance models. The key training insight is that spectral ambiguity allows narrowband RGB renders (produced in Blender with standard PBR materials) to serve as valid approximations of true multispectral observations, eliminating the need for scarce multispectral BRDFs; the resulting PS-Multiplex dataset contains 106,374 three-channel images. Architecturally, a Global Feature Encoder (ConvNeXt-T backbone per channel, channel-axis Transformer layers for cross-channel communication, and a Feature Pyramid Network) produces global features, which feed a novel Dual-scale Surface Normal Decoder: a low-frequency branch predicts normals at c/4 resolution via channel-axis Transformers and PMA, while a high-frequency branch uses 21×21 patch embeddings for local detail, with both branches merged through spatial-axis Transformers and final MLPs. This dual-scale design is critical because spectrally multiplexed PS typically has far fewer channels (e.g., 3) than temporally multiplexed PS (tens of images), making pixel-wise decoding insufficient for preserving global shape.

## Results

On the SpectraM14 benchmark, SpectraM-PS achieves a mean angular error (MAE) of 8.4°–11.6° across five evaluation conditions (color/NIR sensors, with/without crosstalk), substantially outperforming both baselines. The best result is 8.4° under Condition 4 (NIR, no crosstalk), while the worst is 11.6° under Condition 3 (color, strong crosstalk). In contrast, Lv et al. [46] ranges from 17.1° to 36.0° and Guo et al. [17] from 18.9° to 51.7°, with both degrading sharply as crosstalk increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpectraM-PS outperforms both baselines on every condition of the SpectraM14 benchmark. Against Lv et al. [46] (ELIE-Net, IJCAI 2023), the gap ranges from ~8° under no-crosstalk conditions (8.9° vs 17.1°) to ~24° under strong crosstalk (11.6° vs 36.0°). Against Guo et al. [17] (IJCV 2022), the advantage is even larger, reaching ~40° under strong crosstalk (11.6° vs 51.7°). A critical distinction is that both baselines require calibrated directional light sources and assume Lambertian or uniform-chromaticity surfaces, whereas SpectraM-PS operates without any lighting calibration and handles spatially-varying, non-Lambertian materials. Notably, SpectraM-PS's performance degrades only marginally as crosstalk increases (8.9°→11.6°), while both baselines suffer severe degradation (up to 3× worse), confirming that the data-driven approach is far more robust to realistic spectral overlap between channels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Ikehata's Transformer-based universal photometric stereo architectures (SDM-UniPS, CVPR 2023; Universal PS, CVPR 2022) from the temporally multiplexed regime to the spectrally multiplexed one, replacing multiple temporal observations with multiple sensor channels. It departs sharply from prior spectrally multiplexed PS methods (Guo et al., Lv et al., Ju et al.), which required calibrated directional lights, known light directions, and strong surface assumptions (Lambertian, convex, uniform chromaticity). The key conceptual departure is reframing spectral ambiguity—from an ill-posedness problem that prior work sought to resolve—into a training-data-generation advantage, enabling the use of standard RGB path-traced renders instead of physically accurate multispectral rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SpectraM14, the first real-world benchmark for spectrally multiplexed PS, fills a notable gap and provides the community a standardized evaluation protocol with five difficulty levels spanning crosstalk and spatially-varying lighting. The practical implication is that dynamic surface recovery (manufacturing wear, crop growth, impact deformation) becomes feasible in uncalibrated, uncontrolled settings without specialized multispectral hardware. Open questions include temporal stability of the recovered normal maps across video frames, the current 3–10 second per-frame inference cost precluding real-time use, and whether the narrowband RGB training approximation generalizes beyond three channels or to more extreme spectral compositions.
