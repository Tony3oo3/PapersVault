## Problem

Existing 2D-to-3D distillation frameworks for autonomous driving produce 3D representations whose structure (uniformity, tolerance, modality gap) significantly diverges from the source 2D representation space. Contrastive losses are undermined by self-similarity in AD data, pushing away semantically similar samples and degrading zero-shot alignment, while similarity losses under-constrain the pretraining, yielding compact but poorly distributed representations that hurt few-shot performance. This paper quantifies this structural gap and shows it is negatively correlated with downstream segmentation quality.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes synchronized LiDAR point clouds and RGB images from the nuScenes dataset, using camera-to-LiDAR calibration to form positive point-pixel pairs (or superpoint-superpixel pairs via SAM/SLIC segmentation). The 2D features are extracted by a frozen CLIP or DINOv2 vision encoder, while the 3D encoder is a randomly initialized Minkowski U-Net. At inference, zero-shot segmentation uses hand-crafted text prompts (85 per class) encoded by the CLIP text encoder, and few-shot segmentation fine-tunes a linear classifier on 1% of labels from nuScenes or SemanticKITTI.

## Method

The paper proposes a relational distillation loss composed of two terms: a cross-modal relational loss that constrains each predicted point feature's similarity to all source pixel features to match the corresponding source pixel feature's similarity to the same set, and an intra-modal relational loss that directly penalizes the difference between the point-feature relational graph (KK^T) and the pixel-feature relational graph (QQ^T). This generalizes the cosine similarity loss by breaking the non-uniqueness of the dot-product objective, driving the network toward solutions that preserve the 2D representation's uniformity, tolerance, and modality gap rather than merely matching pairwise similarities. The loss is applied within both pixel-based (PPKT-style) and superpixel-based (SLidR-style) distillation pipelines, requiring no negative samples or temperature tuning. Empirically, the relational loss closes the structural gap to the source representation, improving zero-shot mIoU by 2.7–3.1% over similarity loss and 7–11% over contrastive loss, while also boosting few-shot performance by 1.1–1.6% over similarity loss on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution datasets.

## Results

On nuScenes zero-shot 3D semantic segmentation (CLIP ViT-B16), the superpixel-based relational loss (Rel_sp) achieves 26.03% mIoU, outperforming the similarity baseline Sim_sp (23.93%) and all contrastive baselines (ST-SLidR 18.54%, SLidR 16.82%, PPKT 14.53%). For 1% few-shot finetuning, Rel_sp reaches 47.22% mIoU on nuScenes and 47.52% on SemanticKITTI, surpassing ST-SLidR (47.13% / 46.84%) and SLidR (46.76% / 46.53%). The relational loss also achieves the lowest modality gap (G=0.00019) and brings the 3D representation's uniformity (U=1.099) and tolerance (T=0.870) closest to the CLIP source (U=1.54, T=0.73).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against contrastive distillation baselines, the relational loss dominates on zero-shot segmentation: Rel_sp achieves 26.03% mIoU versus 18.54% for ST-SLidR [27], 16.82% for SLidR [38], and 14.53% for PPKT [25], a gap of 7.5–11.5 points. On few-shot 1% finetuning, Rel_sp edges out ST-SLidR by +0.09% on nuScenes (47.22 vs 47.13) and +0.68% on SemanticKITTI (47.52 vs 46.84), while beating SLidR by +0.46% and +0.99% respectively. Compared to the similarity loss baselines, Rel_sp improves zero-shot by +2.10% (26.03 vs 23.93) and few-shot by +1.59% on nuScenes and +1.10% on KITTI. In the broader zero-shot comparison (Table 4), Rel_pl (23.53%) outperforms MaskCLIP [46] (12.80%), OpenScene [33] (14.60%), and CLIP2Scene [9] (20.80%) without requiring class names or SAM. The only method that surpasses Rel_sp is TLF [8] (26.80%), but TLF requires both class names and SAM during distillation, whereas Rel_sp uses neither, making it more generalizable to open-set settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D distillation lineage for autonomous driving (PPKT, SLidR, ST-SLidR) by identifying a structural mismatch between source 2D and distilled 3D representation spaces, quantified via uniformity, tolerance, and modality gap. It departs from the dominant contrastive distillation paradigm—which the authors show is counterproductive for zero-shot tasks in self-similar AD data—and from the simpler cosine similarity loss, which under-constrains the 3D space. The proposed relational loss draws conceptually from relational knowledge distillation in model compression [31], repurposing it as a cross-modal structural constraint that generalizes the similarity loss by enforcing consistency of pairwise relationships rather than individual point-to-point alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural gap diagnostic (U, T, G) provides a principled way to evaluate distillation quality beyond downstream task performance, and the finding that contrastive distillation degrades zero-shot alignment in AD settings challenges the default assumption that contrastive losses are universally superior. The relational loss is a practical drop-in replacement that improves both zero-shot and few-shot segmentation without requiring class names or negative sample curation. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets (nuScenes, SemanticKITTI) and two 2D encoders (CLIP, DINOv2), and the few-shot gains over similarity loss are modest (~1–1.5%), leaving open whether the structural alignment benefit scales to more diverse 3D tasks or larger-scale AD datasets.
