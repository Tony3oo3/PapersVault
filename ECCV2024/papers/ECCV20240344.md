## Problem

Existing video-to-4D generation methods struggle to simultaneously achieve reference view alignment, spatio-temporal consistency, and motion fidelity from a single-view video. Dynamic NeRF suffers from temporal incoherence due to its implicit nature and SDS stochasticity, while dense 3D Gaussian approaches face the difficulty of predicting accurate per-Gaussian trajectories and rotations under single-view conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240344/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view reference video (32 frames, ~2 seconds, stationary camera) and produces a deformable 4D object represented as ~50k dense 3D Gaussians driven by ~512 sparse control points via Linear Binding Skinning. For the motion transfer application, a text prompt is additionally provided, and the output is a new 4D entity with the same control-point motion but different appearance, conditioned on depth from the original 4D result.

## Method

SC4D decouples appearance and motion into dense Gaussians and sparse control points in a two-stage optimization. In the coarse stage, ~512 spherical control Gaussians and a time-conditioned MLP are jointly optimized under reference-view reconstruction, mask, and SDS (via Zero123) losses, followed by FPS resampling to enforce even distribution. In the fine stage, dense Gaussians are initialized via Adaptive Gaussian (AG) initialization—sampling K Gaussians within each coarse-stage control Gaussian sphere to inherit shape and motion—and are driven by the control points through LBS. A Gaussian Alignment (GA) loss constrains current control point positions to their initial coarse-stage positions, preventing the shape degeneration (thickening, drift, texture blur) that arises when SDS at small timesteps prioritizes texture over geometry. The motion transfer application freezes the learned control points and deformation MLP, then optimizes new dense Gaussians using a depth-conditioned ControlNet with a two-phase guidance schedule (low then high scale) to capture shape first, then refine texture.

## Results

On the Consistent4D benchmark (10 reference videos, 32 frames each), SC4D achieves a PSNR of 29.50 dB, SSIM of 0.95, LPIPS of 0.08, CLIP score of 0.90, and temporal error of 0.0081, outperforming both baselines on every metric. Training time is 1.0 hour on a single V100 GPU, roughly 2× faster than Consistent4D (1.9 h) and 3× faster than 4DGen (3.0 h). A user study with 20 participants across 10 videos further confirms that SC4D is preferred in both reference-view and novel-view renderings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SC4D is compared against two open-source video-to-4D methods: Consistent4D (dynamic NeRF-based) and 4DGen (dynamic 3D Gaussian-based). SC4D decisively outperforms both on all six reported metrics. Against Consistent4D, SC4D gains +5.53 dB in PSNR (29.50 vs 23.97), +0.04 in SSIM, and reduces temporal error from 0.0089 to 0.0081, while also halving training time. Against 4DGen, the PSNR margin is even larger (+7.70 dB, 29.50 vs 21.80), and SC4D trains 3× faster (1.0 h vs 3.0 h). The CLIP score (multi-view consistency) improves from 0.89/0.87 to 0.90, and the user study (200 votes per view type) shows SC4D receiving the highest preference in both reference and novel view evaluations. Qualitatively, Consistent4D exhibits temporal discontinuities and color distortions, while 4DGen shows minor motion and blurring in high-motion regions; SC4D produces sharper, more temporally coherent results with evenly distributed control points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SC4D directly extends the sparse-control-point architecture of SC-GS [14] from dynamic scene reconstruction to the video-to-4D generation setting, replacing per-Gaussian motion prediction (as in 4DGen [69]) or time-varying NeRF (as in Consistent4D [16]) with a two-stage pipeline where ~512 sparse control points drive ~50k dense Gaussians via Linear Binding Skinning. The optimization paradigm follows the SDS lineage from DreamFusion [38] with Zero123 [29] as the novel-view prior, and the Adaptive Gaussian initialization borrows the per-Gaussian sampling strategy from DreamGaussian [53]. The key conceptual departure from prior video-to-4D methods is the explicit decoupling of motion (sparse, low-dimensional) from appearance (dense, high-dimensional), which the authors argue is the root cause of the alignment–consistency–fidelity tradeoff in existing approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sparse-control formulation offers a practical efficiency gain (1h vs. 1.9–3h training) and a clean interface for downstream applications, most notably the motion-transfer pipeline that reuses learned control-point trajectories to animate text-described entities via depth-conditioned ControlNet. However, the method inherits the limitations of its Zero123 backbone (poor performance on complex objects) and, like all current video-to-4D methods, assumes a stationary camera. Open questions include whether the sparse-control paradigm scales to multi-object or scene-level 4D generation, whether the GA loss generalizes beyond the single-object setting, and whether moving-camera inputs can be handled without fundamentally altering the LBS formulation.
