## Problem

Federated Class-Incremental Learning (FCIL) requires a global model to classify all seen classes across multiple stages while each client only observes new-class data under non-IID conditions. Existing FCIL methods either store old-class samples (privacy risk), rely on generative replay (computational overhead), or maintain module pools with similarity-based selection at inference (added latency and memory). The paper identifies that both CIL and FL share two common failure modes—degraded feature separability and classifier drift—and asks whether a single lightweight mechanism can address both simultaneously under low communication budgets.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for the ImageNet-1k subset) belonging only to the current stage's classes, and the frozen ViT-B/16 backbone with LoRA adapters produces a feature vector that is classified by Euclidean distance to per-class prototypes. The global model must output a single class label over all classes seen across all T stages, while each local client only trains on its current-stage subset. The output is a hard class prediction via softmax over negative squared distances to prototypes, with no additional similarity-computation or module-selection step at inference.

## Method

PILoRA freezes a DINO self-supervised ViT-B/16 backbone and trains only LoRA adapters (rank 4, first block) plus per-class prototypes. To mitigate catastrophic forgetting, Incremental LoRA constrains each new task's LoRA matrices to be orthogonal to all previous tasks' matrices via an L1 orthogonality regularizer, and at inference simply sums all accumulated LoRA parameter pairs (A̅ = ΣAᵢ, B̅ = ΣBᵢ) into a single adapter—eliminating the need for similarity-based module selection. To counter classifier bias from non-IID data, a Prototype Re-weight module on the global server computes, for each class, the distance between every client's uploaded prototype and the pooled mean features of that class, converts these distances into softmax-normalized weights, and forms the global prototype as a weighted average rather than a uniform mean. The total training loss combines a distance-based cross-entropy (DCE) term, a prototype-compactness (PL) term, and the orthogonality regularizer, with the backbone never updated.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (10 tasks, 10 classes/task), PILoRA achieves 69.5% A_N and 78.6% Avg. under quantity-based non-IID (α=6), outperforming the best FCIL baseline LGA (64.5%/73.6%) by +5.0/+5.0 points. Under the more extreme distribution-based non-IID (β=0.05), PILoRA retains 54.8%/67.1% while GLFC and LGA fail entirely (no reported results). On TinyImageNet (10 tasks, 20 classes/task), PILoRA reaches 74.4%/81.0% at α=12 and 73.6%/80.2% at β=0.05, consistently surpassing TARGET (72.6%/81.6% at α=12) and FedNCM (71.6%/81.6%) while maintaining robustness where rehearsal-based methods collapse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PILoRA outperforms all eight baselines across both datasets and all non-IID settings. Against rehearsal-based FCIL methods, GLFC (58.2%/70.4%) and LGA (64.5%/73.6%) on CIFAR-100 α=6, PILoRA gains +11.3/+8.2 and +5.0/+5.0 respectively; more critically, both GLFC and LGA fail to report results under distribution-based non-IID (β≥0.5), while PILoRA maintains 68.5%/78.1% at β=0.5. The generative-replay method TARGET (60.9%/71.3%) is the strongest FCIL baseline but still trails PILoRA by +8.6/+7.3 on CIFAR-100 α=6. Among CIL methods adapted to FL, L2P+FL (63.4%/65.1%) degrades catastrophically under extreme heterogeneity (38.7%/32.2% at β=0.05), whereas PILoRA retains 54.8%/67.1%. The FL baseline FedNCM (65.6%/74.4%) is the closest competitor but is surpassed by +3.9/+4.2 on CIFAR-100 α=6 and by +2.8/+0.4 on TinyImageNet α=12. EWC+FL, LwF+FL, and iCaRL+FL all fall 10–30 points below PILoRA, confirming that regularization- or rehearsal-based CIL strategies are insufficient under federated non-IID conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PILoRA synthesizes four distinct research threads into a unified FCIL framework: LoRA as the PEFT backbone, Task Arithmetic's parameter summation for integrating task vectors, CPN's prototype-based discriminative and generative losses for feature learning, and orthogonal subspace regularization from continual learning. It departs from prior FCIL methods (GLFC, LGA, TARGET) that rely on rehearsal buffers or generative replay, and from prompt/adapter-based FCIL methods (FedCPrompt, FedET) that require similarity-based module selection at inference. The paper's conceptual framing—that both CIL and FL share the dual challenges of feature compactness and classifier drift—motivates a single lightweight solution (orthogonal LoRA + prototype re-weight) rather than separate mechanisms for each problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method's robustness under extreme data heterogeneity (β=0.05, where rehearsal-based methods like LGA and GLFC collapse to ~38–41% A_N while PILoRA retains ~55–63%) is practically significant for real-world federated deployments where client data distributions are highly skewed. The dramatic ablation showing prototype re-weight is critical (69.5% → 26.8% without it) highlights that naive averaging of classifiers is a major hidden failure mode in heterogeneous FL. Open questions include scalability beyond 10 incremental stages and 10 clients, applicability to other backbone architectures (CNNs, larger ViTs), and whether the orthogonal regularization's storage cost (keeping all prior LoRA matrices) remains tractable for long incremental sequences.
