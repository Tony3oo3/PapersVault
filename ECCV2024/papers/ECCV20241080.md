## Problem

Existing human-object interaction synthesis methods either handle only static objects, focus on small objects where hand motion dominates, or require complete object motion sequences or past interaction states as input. No prior work synthesizes synchronized motion for both a diverse large object and a full-body human from just a language description, initial states, and sparse scene-derived waypoints, while also enforcing realistic contact and non-penetration constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241080/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a CLIP-encoded language description, initial object and human poses, sparse object waypoints (2D xy-positions sampled every 30 frames plus a final 3D position), and an object geometry encoded as a 1024-point Basis Point Set projected to a 256-dim vector. It outputs a synchronized sequence of object motion (12D per frame: 3D centroid position + 9D relative rotation), full-body human motion (global joint positions + 6D continuous rotations parameterized by SMPL-X), and per-frame binary contact labels for left and right hands. The waypoint sparsity is a key design choice: only a handful of 2D positions are needed, enabling integration with standard path-planning algorithms that produce collision-free routes in cluttered 3D scenes.

## Method

CHOIS employs a transformer-based conditional diffusion model that jointly denoises object motion, human motion, and contact labels in a single forward pass, conditioned on language, initial states, sparse waypoints, and object geometry. During training, an additional object geometry loss (L_obj) is introduced: 100 BPS sample points are transformed by the predicted rotation and translation and compared via L1 distance to ground-truth vertex positions, which significantly improves waypoint adherence over a vanilla diffusion objective. At inference, three analytical reconstruction-guidance terms are applied in the last 10 denoising steps to perturb the predicted clean data: a hand-object contact term that pulls predicted hand positions toward the nearest object mesh surface on frames flagged by the contact labels, a feet-floor term that anchors the lowest toe to a 2 cm threshold height, and an object-floor term that penalizes any object vertex below z=0. This test-time guidance strategy avoids the computational cost and loss-balancing difficulty of adding contact/penetration losses during training, while the sparse waypoint conditioning allows the module to plug directly into a Habitat-based path planner for long-horizon, scene-aware interaction synthesis.

## Results

On the FullBodyManipulation dataset, CHOIS achieves a FID of 0.69 (vs. 6.16 for MDM and 208.0 for InterDiff), contact precision of 0.80, and hand-object penetration of 0.59 cm, while maintaining low foot height (4.20 cm) and foot sliding (0.35 cm). On the 3D-FUTURE generalization test, CHOIS reduces FID to 1.60 and hand penetration to 0.15 cm, outperforming all baselines. The method also achieves the best condition matching among methods that jointly generate object motion (T_e = 6.31 cm, T_xy = 2.87 cm), with only GT-OMOMO (which uses ground-truth object trajectories) achieving zero deviation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHOIS dominates InterDiff [61] across nearly all metrics: FID drops from 208.0 to 0.69, R-precision rises from 0.08 to 0.64, and object position error (T_obj) falls from 88.35 cm to 12.53 cm, reflecting InterDiff's inability to disentangle its entangled conditioning. Against MDM [51], CHOIS improves FID from 6.16 to 0.69, contact precision from 0.72 to 0.80, and foot height from 6.72 cm to 4.20 cm, showing that joint object-human generation with guidance yields substantially more realistic interactions. Compared to Lin-OMOMO [28] (which uses linear interpolation of object positions), CHOIS achieves far better text consistency (R_prec 0.64 vs. 0.29) and motion quality (FID 0.69 vs. 15.33), though Lin-OMOMO trivially matches waypoints since it does not predict object motion. Against Pred-OMOMO (which chains a separate object-motion module with OMOMO), CHOIS wins on FID (0.69 vs. 4.19), foot height (4.20 vs. 7.08 cm), and condition matching (T_e 6.31 vs. 8.03 cm), while Pred-OMOMO slightly edges ahead on contact recall (0.66 vs. 0.64) and contact percentage (0.62 vs. 0.54). GT-OMOMO, which receives ground-truth object trajectories, matches or beats CHOIS on condition matching and object error (by construction) but still suffers from foot floating (H_feet 7.10 vs. 4.20 cm) and higher FID (5.69 vs. 0.69), confirming that the guidance terms are critical even when object motion is known.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the conditional diffusion framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the reconstruction guidance technique from video diffusion (Ho et al., 2022), applying them to the joint generation of human and object motion. It departs from prior HOI synthesis work that either handles only static scenes, focuses on small objects where hand motion dominates, or requires dense object motion sequences as input. The key conceptual departure is treating sparse object waypoints (extractable from path planning) as a sufficient conditioning signal alongside language, enabling a single-stage model that generates both motions simultaneously rather than a multi-stage pipeline. Concurrent works CG-HOI and HOI-Diff also generate both motions from text but do not incorporate waypoint conditions, limiting their integration with scene-aware planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in the seamless integration with path planning (Habitat) to produce long-horizon, environment-aware interactions from language alone, which is a meaningful step toward autonomous scene manipulation. However, the approach still relies on a separate planning module for waypoints and excludes articulated objects, leaving open the question of whether a unified model could handle both planning and interaction. The guidance-based refinement (applied only in the last 10 denoising steps) is a lightweight but effective mechanism, though its transferability to other interaction types or higher-fidelity contact modeling remains unexplored. Scaling to more diverse object categories, real-time inference, and physically grounded contact (beyond geometric proximity) are natural next steps.
