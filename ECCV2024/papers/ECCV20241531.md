## Problem

Feature matching and robust estimation are traditionally performed as separate sequential stages, where failures in either cascade into overall failure. Jointly performing both is computationally intractable in the general case (e.g., estimating an essential matrix from 1000 features requires trying over 10^26 minimal sample combinations), and existing matchers must commit to one-to-one correspondences even when the decision is ambiguous due to repetitive structures. The paper asks whether this combinatorial barrier can be removed to enable a unified matching-and-estimation pipeline.

## Input / Output

StereoGlue consumes a pool of one-to-k tentative correspondences produced by an off-the-shelf matcher (e.g., SuperGlue's optimal-transport score matrix or k-nearest-neighbor descriptor matching), where k is typically small relative to the total number of features. It additionally requires affine correspondences (point locations plus 2×2 local affine transforms) and either a gravity direction vector or a monocular depth map, depending on the solver. The output is a geometric model (relative pose, homography, absolute pose, or 3D rigid transformation) together with a set of consistent one-to-one inlier correspondences whose count serves as the model score.

## Method

The key insight is that single-point minimal solvers (sample size m=1) reduce the joint matching-and-estimation complexity from C(n², m) to O(n²), making the problem tractable. StereoGlue iteratively selects the next-best match from the one-to-many pool using a PROSAC-like quality prior, estimates the model from that single correspondence via a single-point solver (1AC+uG, 1AC+mD, P1AC, Q-REG, or a novel 1AC+uG-H homography solver), and then performs guided matching to find all one-to-one correspondences consistent with the estimated model. A space-partitioning hash function efficiently retrieves candidate matches near the predicted epipolar line or homographic image, and a weighting term inversely proportional to the number of similarly-scored candidates prevents the model from hallucinating inliers along degenerate directions. When a new best model is found, a few iterations of inner RANSAC (local optimization) refine the estimate, and the loop terminates per a standard stopping criterion.

## Results

On PhotoTourism (9900 pairs), StereoGlue with DeDoDe+LightGlue and the 1AC+uG solver achieves the best overall performance: 2.3° average pose error, 0.5° median, and 43.5% AUC@1°, outperforming MAGSAC++ with 5PC (3.2° avg, 38.1% AUC@1°) by a wide margin. On ScanNet (1500 wide-baseline pairs), StereoGlue with SuperPoint+SuperGlue reduces average error from 32.4° (MAGSAC++ 3PC+uG) to 12.9° (1AC+uG), and matches or exceeds the detector-free LoFTR method. For absolute pose on Cambridge Landmarks, P1AC+StereoGlue achieves 62.4% recall at 5cm/1°, surpassing P1AC+GC-RANSAC (53.4%) and P3P+GC-RANSAC (52.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StereoGlue consistently outperforms MAGSAC++ [11] (the main baseline robust estimator) across all feature pipelines and all accuracy metrics on both PhotoTourism and ScanNet; for example, with SuperPoint+SuperGlue on PhotoTourism, AUC@1° improves from 23.0% to 34.5% and average error drops from 4.1° to 2.6°. On 3DLoMatch point cloud registration, StereoGlue with GeoTransformer [79] matches achieves 80.7% registration recall and 36.3 cm RMSE, beating both GeoTransformer+RANSAC (75.0%, 57.3 cm) and GeoTransformer+Q-REG [51] (77.1%, 44.6 cm). For absolute pose, P1AC+StereoGlue [105] surpasses P1AC+GC-RANSAC [8] by 9.0 points at 5cm/1° on Cambridge Landmarks (62.4% vs 53.4%) and also outperforms P3P+GC-RANSAC [78] (52.6%). On ScanNet, StereoGlue with SuperPoint+SuperGlue (12.9° avg) matches or slightly beats the detector-free LoFTR [98] (30.3° avg with 5PC), while also achieving higher AUC@20° (58.4% vs 57.7%). StereoGlue also improves upon pipelines using R2D2 [84] and SOSNet [100] descriptors, e.g., reducing average error from 13.0° to 4.7° (R2D2+NN, 5PC) on PhotoTourism.

## Relation to Prior Work

StereoGlue sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: RANSAC-family robust estimators (MAGSAC++, GC-RANSAC, LO-RANSAC, PROSAC) and single-point minimal solvers (1AC+uG, 1AC+mD, P1AC, Q-REG). The key departure is from the conventional sequential pipeline (detect → match → estimate) by exploiting single-point solvers to reduce the joint matching-and-estimation complexity from C(n², m) to O(n²), and by relaxing the one-to-one matching commitment to one-to-k pools obtained from off-the-shelf matchers like SuperGlue or LightGlue. The guided matching step and the weighting scheme (analogous to Lowe's ratio test) are novel components that prevent the model from hallucinating inliers along epipolar lines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D reconstruction and localization pipelines where matching ambiguity (repetitive structures, wide baselines) cascades into estimation failure. Its plug-and-play nature—working atop existing detectors and matchers without retraining—makes it immediately deployable. A notable limitation is the reliance on affine correspondences (requiring AffNet or equivalent) and gravity/depth priors, which restricts applicability to point-only feature pipelines. Open questions include whether the framework extends to solvers that truly require only point locations, and whether the upright-gravity assumption generalizes to non-terrestrial or heavily tilted-camera scenarios.
