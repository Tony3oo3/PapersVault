## Problem

Existing raindrop removal datasets suffer from two critical gaps: they exclusively contain background-focused images (blurry raindrops on a sharp background) and are limited to daytime conditions. As a result, methods trained on these datasets fail when the camera focuses on raindrops (producing sharp raindrops with a blurry background) or when raindrops appear under nighttime lighting, leaving both scenarios unaddressed in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240135/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides two data structures: pairs (x̃, b₀) for background-focused images where the raindrop-degraded image and clear background are aligned, and triplets (x̃, x₀, b₀) for raindrop-focused images that additionally include the raindrop-free blurry background x₀, enabling computation of pixel-level raindrop difference maps m̃ = x̃ − x₀. Images are captured at up to 4K resolution (3840×2164) using multiple cameras (Sony FDR-AX33, Sony A7R III, iPhone 14 Pro/15 Pro Max), with 15,186 total pairs/triplets split into 5,442 daytime and 9,744 nighttime samples across diverse scenes (urban, countryside, campus, road, aerial).

## Method

The core contribution is a large-scale real-world dataset collected via a controlled procedure: a camera is fixed on a sphere pan-tilt platform behind a glass plate (5–25 cm distance), water is sprayed to create diverse raindrop shapes and densities, and images are captured at three focus settings—on the raindrops (x̃), on a near plane without the glass (x₀), and on the distant background (b₀)—within milliseconds to avoid illumination drift. The benchmark then retrains eight state-of-the-art methods (five raindrop-specific algorithms: AtGAN, RobotCar, RainDS, IDT, RaindropDiffusion; and three restoration backbones: Restormer, Uformer, DiT) on both daytime and nighttime splits, evaluating with PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The key empirical finding is that all existing methods, including those designed for deblurring, fail to simultaneously remove raindrops and recover the background in raindrop-focused and nighttime scenarios, exposing unresolved challenges in the field.

## Results

On the daytime split, Restormer achieves the highest PSNR (26.08) and DiT the best SSIM (0.752) and LPIPS (0.106), while among raindrop-specific algorithms IDT leads with 26.05 PSNR and 0.736 SSIM. On the nighttime split, Restormer again tops PSNR (26.87), IDT leads SSIM (0.851), and DiT achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.111). The input images themselves score 21.92/0.560/0.247 (day) and 24.78/0.726/0.209 (night) on PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, showing that all methods improve over the raw input but leave substantial room for improvement, particularly on raindrop-focused and nighttime samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five raindrop-specific algorithms, IDT [40] consistently outperforms AtGAN [25], RobotCar [24], RainDS [26], and RDdiff [21] on both day and night splits (e.g., 26.05 vs. 23.62–25.52 PSNR on day; 26.81 vs. 24.38–26.48 PSNR on night). Among the three general restoration backbones, Restormer [46] achieves the highest PSNR on both splits (26.08 day, 26.87 night), while DiT [22] attains the best LPIPS on both (0.106 day, 0.111 night) and the best SSIM on day (0.752). Uformer [36] is the weakest backbone, trailing Restormer by 0.30 PSNR on day and 1.61 PSNR on night. The paper's central finding is that even the best-performing methods (Restormer, DiT, IDT) visibly fail on raindrop-focused images and nighttime samples, indicating that current SOTA approaches—designed primarily for daytime, background-focused scenarios—do not generalize to the dual-focus and nighttime conditions that Raindrop Clarity introduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the raindrop removal data landscape by addressing two gaps left by all prior real-world datasets (Qian et al. 2018, Porav et al. 2019, Quan et al. 2021, Soboleva & Shipitko 2021): the absence of raindrop-focused images (where the camera focuses on droplets, blurring the background) and the absence of nighttime scenarios. Rather than proposing a new removal algorithm, it introduces a triplet structure (raindrop image, raindrop-free blurry background, clear background) that enables computing pixel-level difference maps, a data format not present in prior datasets. The empirical benchmark reuses existing raindrop algorithms (AtGAN, IDT, RaindropDiffusion) and general restoration backbones (Restormer, Uformer, DiT) to characterize where current methods break down under the newly covered conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies two concrete open problems—raindrop-focused removal (requiring simultaneous raindrop removal and background deblurring) and nighttime raindrop removal (with artificial-light reflections and high contrast)—and demonstrates that even diffusion-based and transformer-based SOTA methods fail qualitatively on both. This is practically important for autonomous driving, surveillance, and AR applications where wipers are impractical and nighttime operation is common. A key limitation is that the dataset, while large (15,186 pairs/triplets), is still single-frame and does not address video consistency; the triplet structure also requires a stationary camera, limiting applicability to moving-vehicle scenarios.
