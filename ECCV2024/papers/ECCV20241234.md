## Problem

Animating open-domain still images into temporally coherent videos with natural dynamics while preserving visual details remains unsolved. Existing image-to-video diffusion methods (e.g., VideoComposer, I2VGen-XL) employ incomplete image injection mechanisms that cause either abrupt temporal changes or poor visual conformity to the input, and prior animation techniques are restricted to curated domains like fluids, hair, or portraits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241234/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single still image (256×256 or 1024×576) and an optional text prompt describing desired motion, and produces a 16-frame animated video clip at the corresponding resolution. The input image may appear at any position within the generated frame sequence rather than being fixed as the first frame. At inference, DDIM sampling with multi-condition classifier-free guidance is used to jointly condition on both the image and text.

## Method

DynamiCrafter introduces a dual-stream image injection paradigm into a pre-trained text-to-video latent diffusion model (VideoCrafter). The first stream projects the image into a text-aligned context space using CLIP image encoder full visual tokens followed by a learnable query Transformer, which is injected via cross-attention layers with per-layer adaptive tanh gating (λ) that modulates how much image context influences each U-Net layer. The second stream concatenates the full-resolution image with per-frame initial noise to provide pixel-level visual detail guidance. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (i) pre-train the context projection network on a lightweight T2I model (SD v2.1), (ii) adapt it to the T2V model by jointly training the projection and spatial layers, and (iii) fine-tune with visual detail guidance while only updating spatial layers to preserve the pre-trained temporal prior. A random-frame conditioning strategy prevents the model from learning a shortcut that maps the concatenated image to a fixed frame position.

## Results

On UCF-101, DynamiCrafter achieves FVD 429.23, KVD 62.47, and PIC 0.6078, outperforming VideoComposer (FVD 576.81) and I2VGen-XL (FVD 571.11) on FVD and PIC, though I2VGen-XL edges ahead on KVD (58.59 vs. 62.47). On MSR-VTT, DynamiCrafter leads all baselines across every metric: FVD 234.66 (vs. 377.29 and 289.10), KVD 13.74 (vs. 26.34 and 14.70), and PIC 0.5803 (vs. 0.4460 and 0.5352). In user studies at 256×256, DynamiCrafter is preferred for motion quality (38.84%) over PikaLabs (28.60%) and Gen-2 (22.91%), and for input conformity (79.88%) over Gen-2 (64.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VideoComposer, DynamiCrafter wins decisively on all three metrics for both datasets, with FVD improvements of ~147 (UCF-101) and ~143 (MSR-VTT) and PIC gains of ~0.08 and ~0.13 respectively. Against I2VGen-XL, DynamiCrafter achieves substantially lower FVD (429 vs. 571 on UCF-101; 235 vs. 289 on MSR-VTT) and higher PIC, but concedes KVD on UCF-101 (62.47 vs. 58.59), suggesting I2VGen-XL produces slightly more temporally smooth (though less faithful) videos on that benchmark. In user studies at 256×256, DynamiCrafter surpasses both commercial products PikaLabs and Gen-2 on motion quality (38.84% vs. 28.60% and 22.91%) and input conformity (79.88% vs. 79.07% and 64.77%), while Gen-2's main failure mode is sudden content changes and PikaLabs tends toward static, blurry outputs. At 1024×576, DynamiCrafter also outperforms the concurrent SVD on motion quality (36.09% vs. 27.39%), temporal coherence (29.57% vs. 24.35%), and input conformity (86.96% vs. 83.91%), with SVD's limitation being its reliance on camera motion rather than natural object dynamics and lack of text-prompt controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the open-source VideoCrafter T2V diffusion model and CLIP image encoders, extending them with a dual-stream image injection paradigm that combines text-aligned image context (via a query Transformer projecting CLIP visual tokens) with visual detail guidance (concatenating the input image to per-frame noise). It departs from prior image-to-video methods like VideoComposer and I2VGen-XL, which use only one injection stream and suffer from either temporal incoherence or poor detail preservation, and from concurrent SVD which discards text conditioning and shifts the prior space. The three-stage training strategy (T2I-based context learning → T2V spatial-layer adaptation → joint fine-tuning with VDG) is a novel recipe for integrating image conditioning without disrupting the pre-trained temporal prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DynamiCrafter demonstrates that open-domain image animation with video diffusion priors can match or exceed commercial products (Gen-2, PikaLabs) in user studies, providing a practical and open recipe for the community. The dual-stream paradigm and the finding that text prompts remain necessary even with rich image context (to prevent shape distortion) offer actionable design principles for future controllable video generation. Open questions include scaling to longer video durations, higher resolutions without multi-stage fine-tuning, and more fine-grained motion controllability beyond the proof-of-concept text-based dynamic control shown here.
