## Problem

LLaMA has become the de facto architecture for large language models, but it is designed for 1D text sequences, while vision requires 2D spatial modeling, pyramid backbones for dense prediction, and handling of arbitrary input resolutions. This paper asks whether the LLaMA architecture can be directly transferred to the vision modality and resolves the three key mismatches: dimensionality (1D→2D), backbone topology (plain→pyramid), and resolution variability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241751/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image generation, the model consumes a noisy latent (8× downsampled by a pre-trained VAE) plus a text prompt and outputs a 256×256 RGB image via DDPM with classifier-free guidance. For perception tasks, it takes RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (trained at 224×224 but evaluated up to 448×448 without retraining) and produces class labels, pixel-wise segmentation maps, or bounding boxes with instance masks. The AS2DRoPE mechanism auto-scales positional encodings relative to an anchor resolution, so the same weights generalize across resolutions without interpolation of learned embeddings.

## Method

VisionLLaMA retains LLaMA's core components—SwiGLU feed-forward, pre-normalization, and rotary positional encoding—while replacing 1D RoPE with AS2DRoPE, a 2D rotary encoding that applies independent rotation matrices along horizontal and vertical axes and auto-scales coordinates by the ratio of anchor to target resolution. Two variants are provided: a plain transformer (ViT-style with class token) and a pyramid transformer (Twins-style with interleaved local and global sub-sampled attention, no class token, GAP head). The architecture is convolution-free and is validated under both supervised (ImageNet-1K) and self-supervised (MAE) pre-training, then plugged into DiT for generation, UperNet for segmentation, and Mask R-CNN/ViTDet for detection. A theoretical analysis shows that RoPE's multiplicative positional modulation introduces a locality prior resembling convolution while avoiding the content-position cross terms of additive sin-cos encoding, explaining the observed faster convergence and higher final accuracy.

## Results

On image generation (DiT framework, 256×256), DiT-LLaMA-XL/2 with classifier-free guidance achieves FID 2.42, substantially outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 10.67 without CFG) at 2352k steps. On ImageNet-1K supervised classification, VisionLLaMA-L reaches 84.6% top-1 accuracy (vs. DeiT3-L's 84.5%), and Pyramid VisionLLaMA-B matches Twins-SVT-B at 83.2%. Under MAE self-supervised pre-training (800 epochs), VisionLLaMA-Base achieves 84.0% full fine-tuning accuracy and 69.7% linear probing accuracy, exceeding ViT-Base-MAE by 0.8% and 4.6% respectively. On ADE20K segmentation, Pyramid VisionLLaMA-B attains 49.1% mIoU (vs. Swin-S 47.6%, Twins-SVT-B 47.7%), and on COCO detection, Pyramid VisionLLaMA-B reaches 49.1% box mAP (vs. Swin-S 47.6%, Twins-SVT-B 48.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiT [37], VisionLLaMA consistently wins across all model sizes: at 400k steps with CFG, DiT-LLaMA-XL/4 achieves FID 18.69 vs. DiT-XL/4's 22.52, and at 2352k steps DiT-LLaMA-XL/2 with CFG reaches FID 2.42 vs. DiT-XL/2's 10.67 (no CFG). On ImageNet-1K supervised classification, VisionLLaMA-L (84.6%) marginally beats DeiT3-L (84.5%), while Pyramid VisionLLaMA-B (83.2%) ties Twins-SVT-B and outperforms Swin-B (83.3%); VisionLLaMA-S (81.6%) edges out DeiT3-S (81.4%) and CPVT-S (81.5%). Under MAE self-supervised pre-training, VisionLLaMA-Base-MAE (84.0% SFT, 69.7% LP at 800 epochs) outperforms ViT-Base-MAE (83.2%, 65.1%), SemMAE (83.4%, 65.0%), SimMIM (83.8%, 56.7%), MFF-MAE (83.6%, 67.0%), and MaskFeat (84.0%, 62.3% at 1600 epochs). On ADE20K segmentation, Pyramid VisionLLaMA-B (49.1% mIoU) beats Swin-S (47.6%) and Twins-SVT-B (47.7%) by over 1.2% mIoU. On COCO detection, Pyramid VisionLLaMA-B (49.1% box mAP) exceeds Swin-S (47.6%) by 1.5% and Twins-SVT-B (48.0%) by 1.1%; in the ViTDet setting, VisionLLaMA-B (52.2% box mAP) outperforms ViT-B (51.6%) while using only 36% of the training epochs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly ports the LLaMA architecture (SwiGLU activation, rotary positional encoding, pre-normalization) into the vision domain, following ViT's patch-based pipeline for the plain variant and Twins' local-global attention pyramid for the hierarchical variant. The key architectural departure is AS2DRoPE, which extends 1D RoPE to 2D with auto-scaled interpolation to handle arbitrary input resolutions—a problem that 1D RoPE cannot solve (performance drops to zero at doubled resolution). For image generation, it slots into the DiT diffusion framework as a drop-in replacement for the original ViT backbone, and for self-supervised learning it replaces the encoder in the MAE framework, making the comparison a controlled architectural swap rather than a system-level redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single LLaMA-like backbone now serves as a competitive or superior choice across image generation, classification, segmentation, and detection in both supervised and self-supervised settings, meaning the entire LLaMA deployment ecosystem (quantization, inference acceleration, memory optimization) becomes immediately applicable to vision models. The consistent convergence speed advantage (e.g., 30 vs. 100 epochs for COCO detection) is a substantial compute saving at scale. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video and 3D tasks, whether the advantages persist at much larger model scales (beyond XL), and whether the theoretical explanation for RoPE's multiplicative positional bias fully accounts for the observed gains across all tasks.
