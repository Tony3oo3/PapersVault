## Problem

Fixed firing thresholds in spiking neurons are sensitive to initial conditions and fail to adapt to varying input signals, causing neurons to be either overly active or dormant, which degrades both accuracy and energy efficiency. Existing dynamic thresholding methods such as BDETT rely on layer-level averages of membrane potentials that cannot capture individual neuron dynamics, and they lack mechanisms for temporal inertia and spike-efficiency tracking. This paper addresses the gap of maintaining homeostasis—stable, balanced firing rates—in SNNs that process asynchronous event-based vision data.

## Input / Output

The method directly consumes asynchronous event streams from DVS cameras as spike trains without any frame conversion or preprocessing: each event tuple (x, y, t, z) at a pixel location is treated as a single Dirac-delta spike at that timestamp, with polarity discarded to avoid noise sensitivity. The first layer of the spiking MLP has one neuron per camera pixel, so the input dimensionality matches the sensor resolution. Outputs are class labels for image classification (N-MNIST, DVS128 Gesture, CIFAR10-DVS, N-ImageNet) and per-event segmentation masks for object segmentation (ESD-1, ESD-2).

## Method

The ABN introduces a neuron-specific dynamic threshold update rule Θ_i(t+1) = Θ_i(t) + k₁·MG_i − k₂·TRG_i + k₃·SE_i, where each term provides a distinct regulatory signal computed per neuron rather than per layer. MG computes the finite-difference derivative of the neuron's own membrane potential, raising the threshold when the neuron is rapidly depolarizing to prevent over-firing. TRG applies an exponentially-decayed sliding window over past threshold changes to introduce temporal inertia and suppress burst firing, while SE tracks the ratio of output to input spikes over a recent window, making the threshold harder to reach when the neuron is over-active. The ABN is a lightweight plug-in that integrates into standard LIF and SRM neuron models without architectural changes, and the full spiking MLP is trained with Spatio-Temporal Backpropagation (STBP) using experimentally selected weights k₁=0.25, k₂=0.50, k₃=0.25.

## Results

ABN achieves the highest accuracy across all six datasets and both neuron models (SRM and LIF). On classification, it reaches 99.48% on N-MNIST, 98.74% on DVS128 Gesture, 94.74% on CIFAR-10 DVS, and 57.04% on N-ImageNet (LIF). On segmentation, it attains 67.29% event accuracy on ESD-1 and 58.04% on ESD-2 (LIF). In terms of energy, ABN achieves the lowest power consumption at 0.038 W on DVS128, compared to 0.046 W for BDETT and 0.541–1.56 W for CNN baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ABN outperforms all seven SNN baselines on every dataset and both neuron models. The closest competitor is BDETT [11], the most recent dynamic-threshold method; ABN surpasses it by 1.55 pp on DVS128 (LIF: 98.74 vs 96.05), 1.9 pp on CIFAR-10 (SRM: 93.5 vs 91.61), and a substantial 7.5–7.8 pp on N-ImageNet (SRM: 56.73 vs 48.93; LIF: 57.04 vs 49.51). On segmentation, ABN leads BDETT by 1.9 pp on ESD-1 (LIF: 67.29 vs 65.39) and 4.9 pp on ESD-2 (SRM: 56.23 vs 51.36). Older baselines such as Spiking MLP (STBP) [40] and DT1/DT2 [18,21] trail by 5–10 pp on most datasets. In the power-consumption comparison (Table 4), ABN achieves 0.038 W, beating BDETT (0.046 W), the SNN baseline from [3] (0.053 W), and CNN baselines from [8] and [4] (0.541 W and 1.56 W) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic thresholding line of work initiated by BDETT (Ding et al., NeurIPS 2022), which computes a single threshold per layer from the average membrane potential of all neurons. ABN departs from this by replacing the layer-level average with neuron-specific dynamics: the Membrane Gradient captures each neuron's own rate of potential change, the Threshold Retrospective Gradient introduces temporal inertia via a sliding window over past threshold movements, and the Spike Efficiency ratio regulates firing based on recent input-output balance. The event-to-spike encoding (treating each DVS event directly as a spike) is adopted from Abreu et al. (CVPRW 2023), and the overall framework is validated within the Spiking MLP architecture trained via Spatio-Temporal Backpropagation, consistent with the BDETT experimental setup for fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in SNN-based event vision: maintaining homeostatic firing without expensive per-neuron tuning, while simultaneously reducing energy consumption (0.038 W vs. 0.046 W for BDETT on DVS128). The consistent gains across six datasets and five degraded-input conditions (occlusion, low light, speed, motion direction, camera height) suggest the neuron-specific thresholding is more robust than layer-averaged alternatives. However, the evaluation is limited to a Spiking MLP; generalization to convolutional or recurrent SNN architectures, and direct deployment on neuromorphic hardware (which currently supports only LIF/SRM without the sliding-window operations), remain open. The authors also acknowledge that the model does not fully capture biological neuron characteristics, leaving room for richer bioplausible mechanisms such as dynamic refractory periods.
