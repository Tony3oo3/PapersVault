## Problem

Existing parametric 3D head models face a fundamental trade-off: those trained on large-scale 2D face images achieve photorealistic rendering only within a narrow frontal angle range (<±30°), while those trained on limited 3D head models support 360° synthesis but suffer from poor rigging and identity inversion. No prior model simultaneously achieves 360° free-view synthesis, single-image fitting, expression-driven animation, and text-based appearance editing within a single parametric framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241495/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For fitting, the method takes a single frontal RGB image and produces a fully 360°-renderable, animatable 3D head with hair at 512×512 resolution. The parametric model is controlled by three code vectors: a shape code s (from Tucker decomposition of 100 identities), a texture code t (neural texture latent), and a 52-dimensional blendshape parameter b (ARKit-compatible). For editing, a text prompt drives multi-view image modification via InstructPix2Pix before fine-tuning the parametric model; for animation, standard blendshape parameter streams drive the mesh while the neural texture and hex-planes synthesize temporally consistent appearance including hair and eyelashes.

## Method

The core representation decouples shape/motion (a parametric 3D mesh built via Tucker decomposition over 100 identities × 52 blendshapes) from appearance (a generative neural texture), with both conditioned into a neural radiance field using six feature planes (hex-planes) instead of the standard three, enabling full 360° coverage. A dual-branch network separates bald head and hair features, trained with a decoupling strategy that enables free hairstyle swapping. Training combines photometric L1 loss, a dual GAN loss (from Next3D), and density regularization (from EG3D), followed by a pix2pixHD-based RefineNet for high-frequency detail. The fitting pipeline first recovers shape via landmark projection, then optimizes the full neural texture (rather than a low-dimensional texture code) via GAN inversion with Poisson blending to handle the limited 100-identity training space, and separately classifies hairstyle with a ResNet-50 to reattach appropriate hair to the fitted bald head.

## Results

On single-image fitting (Table 2), Head360 achieves 17.41 dB PSNR, 0.873 SSIM, 0.118 LPIPS, and 0.874 CSIM, outperforming all baselines including the closest re-trained PanoHead (17.18 dB PSNR, 0.792 SSIM). On generated head quality (Table 3), the full model reaches 28.27 dB PSNR and 0.631 LPIPS, while the no-RefineNet variant achieves 28.92 dB PSNR and 0.883 SSIM, both surpassing MoFaNeRF (28.09 dB PSNR) and EG3D (26.45 dB PSNR). The method is the first to simultaneously support 360° rendering, single-image fitting, blendshape-driven animation, and text-based editing within one parametric model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Head360 outperforms all five baselines on every metric in the fitting task. Against the original models, the margins are substantial: EG3D (13.25 dB) and HeadNeRF (10.44 dB) fail beyond ±30° frontal views, while MoFaNeRF (15.74 dB) and Next3D (15.72 dB) degrade past 90°. PanoHead (15.10 dB) is the only baseline supporting 360° rendering but produces static, non-animatable heads. When re-trained on the same SynHead100 dataset, PanoHead narrows the gap to 17.18 dB PSNR (vs. 17.41), yet still trails on SSIM (7.92 vs. 8.73) and LPIPS (1.89 vs. 1.18), and critically cannot be animated. Re-trained EG3D and MoFaNeRF perform worse than their original versions due to the limited 100-identity appearance space, whereas Head360's neural-texture optimization circumvents this limitation. In generation quality (Table 3), Head360 (no RefineNet) leads in PSNR (28.92 dB) and SSIM (8.83), while the full model achieves the best LPIPS (6.31), confirming that RefineNet adds perceptually realistic detail at a slight PSNR cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural radiance field + parametric mesh paradigm established by EG3D and Next3D, replacing tri-planes with hex-planes for full 360° coverage and introducing a dual-branch architecture to decouple hair from the head. The parametric shape representation builds on FLAME via Tucker decomposition, while the RefineNet follows MoFaNeRF's pix2pixHD-based detail enhancement. The key conceptual departure is the hair-detachment training strategy and the fitting method that optimizes the neural texture directly (rather than a low-dimensional latent code), which the authors argue is necessary given the limited 100-identity training set. The text-based editing pipeline repurposes InstructPix2Pix as a multi-view image-to-image translator that fine-tunes the parametric model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SynHead100 dataset is the first publicly available large-scale, artist-designed, high-fidelity 3D head dataset with full 360° coverage, hair, and blendshape rigging, filling a critical gap left by proprietary or incomplete prior datasets (e.g., Rodin's non-public data). The unified framework—360° renderable, animatable, fittable, and text-editable in a single model—removes the need to chain separate pipelines for different downstream tasks. However, the 100-identity limit constrains generalization (the authors acknowledge fitting instability on in-the-wild images), and the absence of material/lighting decomposition bakes highlights into textures, limiting relighting applications. Open questions include whether the approach scales to thousands of identities, whether real captured data can supplement the synthetic set, and how to properly separate illumination from albedo.
