## Problem

When texturing 3D meshes with pre-trained text-to-image models, ensuring multi-view consistency remains a fundamental challenge. Existing approaches either average overlapping regions (producing blurry textures) or train on rendered 3D data (overfitting to synthetic appearances and losing photorealistic detail). This paper addresses the gap by decoupling global style/shape consistency from local detail drift, allowing realistic per-view generation while enforcing consistency through post-hoc optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a triangular 3D mesh and a text prompt as input, and produces a seamlessly textured mesh where each face is assigned to one of the selected views. Internally, it generates an over-complete set of depth-conditioned RGB-D images from predefined viewpoints, then operates on a consistent subset. The depth conditioning is essential for alignment between 2D images and the 3D mesh geometry, and the final texture is assembled by cutting and stitching the aligned images along optimized seams.

## Method

The framework proceeds in four stages: (1) a three-phase diffusion procedure generates an over-complete set of multi-view images, using a 3D-prior model at the initial step for global consistency, latent UV mapping in the middle phase for structural alignment, and independent per-view diffusion for detail recovery; (2) a sequential semidefinite program selects a mutually consistent subset of views that covers the mesh, using mean-shift clustering for image quality scoring and color-difference consistency scores; (3) joint non-rigid alignment via free-form deformation on a 40×40 lattice, initialized with SIFTFlow dense correspondences and solved with Gauss-Newton, preceded by a color-adjustment step to reduce illumination variation; (4) a second-order MRF assigns each mesh face to a view, solved with TRWS. Stages 3 and 4 are iterated (2–3 times), with the MRF cuts guiding the alignment to focus on regions near the seams, progressively improving consistency along the final texture boundaries.

## Results

On 300 models from Objaverse, the proposed method achieves the best FID of 29.26 (lower is better), outperforming the strongest baseline SyncMVD (33.92) by 4.66 points and the next-best MVS-Texturing (37.47) by 8.21 points. In the user study (87 participants, scores 1–10), the method leads on Natural Color (8.92) and Less Artifacts (8.23), while TEXTure edges ahead on More Details (7.92 vs. 7.49) and Align with Prompt (8.45 vs. 8.34). The method takes 10–15 minutes per model on a desktop with an NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on FID, with the largest margin over Latent-NeRF (59.71) and Zero123++ (53.93), and a 4.66-point advantage over the closest competitor SyncMVD (33.92). On user-study metrics, the method leads on Natural Color (8.92 vs. SyncMVD's 8.65) and Less Artifacts (8.23 vs. SyncMVD's 7.97), confirming the benefit of the non-rigid alignment and alternating optimization. However, TEXTure achieves a higher More Details score (7.92 vs. 7.49) and a slightly higher Align-with-Prompt score (8.45 vs. 8.34), suggesting that the sequential diffusion-based approach retains marginally richer local texture detail and prompt fidelity. Compared to MVS-Texturing (which solves the same MRF formulation but without view selection or alignment), the method improves FID by 8.21 points and Less Artifacts by 2.72 points, isolating the contribution of the view-selection and alternating-alignment stages. Overall, the method trades a small amount of local detail (vs. TEXTure) for substantially better global consistency and fewer visible seams.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a post-processing optimization framework that bridges two existing families of text-to-texture methods: diffusion-based multi-view generation (TexFusion, Latent-NeRF, SyncMVD) and 3D-aware consistency enforcement (Zero123++, Consistent123). Rather than solving multi-view consistency entirely within the diffusion model, it treats the generated views as an over-complete set and applies a four-stage pipeline—SDP-based view selection, FFD-parameterized non-rigid alignment via SIFTFlow correspondences, and MRF-based cutting/stitching (building on Waechter et al.)—with alternating optimization between alignment and stitching. The key conceptual departure is explicitly permitting local detail drifts while enforcing consistency in global style and shape, which prior averaging-based or 3D-rendered-training approaches do not cleanly separate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, modular recipe for improving multi-view consistency in text-to-texture pipelines without retraining the underlying diffusion model, making it applicable as a drop-in post-processing step. The ablation revealing that view selection alone accounts for a 42% FID degradation when removed highlights that curation of consistent views is more impactful than the alignment itself—a useful insight for practitioners. Open questions include whether the 10–15 minute optimization cost can be reduced via learned surrogates, whether the decoupled pairwise/joint alignment can be unified, and whether the entire pipeline can be integrated end-to-end into a single differentiable network.
