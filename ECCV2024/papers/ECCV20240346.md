## Problem

Existing human motion generation models are task-specific specialists (e.g., text-to-motion, music-to-dance), each trained on narrow data domains with limited generalization. Unifying these tasks into a single generalist model is hindered by three fundamental inconsistencies across datasets: heterogeneous pose representations (keypoints vs. rotations vs. SMPL), varying numbers of body parts, and different frame rates, making cross-task knowledge transfer and joint training extremely difficult.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240346/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

LMM accepts arbitrary combinations of condition signals (text, music, speech, video, action labels) encoded into unified token sequences via ImageBind, along with optional partial motion sequences for prediction or in-betweening tasks. The output is a 3D motion sequence in a TOMATO-derived unified representation (root trajectory, 22 body + 30 hand joint positions/velocities/rotations, facial expression) partitioned into 10 body parts, which is then converted to dataset-specific formats via learned translators. The model natively handles variable frame rates and missing body parts through mask-aware processing.

## Method

LMM is a transformer-based diffusion model built on FineMoGen's SAMI module, extended with a novel ArtAttention mechanism that decomposes attention into spatial (body-part) and temporal branches. The spatial branch uses per-frame attention to handle variable body-part availability, while the temporal branch employs Mixture-of-Expert to process multi-modal condition features with independent normalization of motion and condition tokens (avoiding the softmax interference that degrades quality when condition sequences are long). Training follows a two-stage strategy: unsupervised pre-training with random frame-rate downsampling and random body-part/temporal masking to build robust motion priors, followed by supervised fine-tuning with condition signals and 10% classifier-free guidance dropout. The MotionVerse benchmark unifies 16 datasets (320K sequences, 100M frames) into a single 10-part representation, enabling the model to absorb cross-task knowledge during pre-training.

## Results

On the HumanML3D text-to-motion benchmark, LMM-Large achieves the best FID of 0.040 (vs. 0.045 for MoMask and 0.151 for FineMoGen), R-Precision Top-1 of 0.525, and Diversity of 9.814, surpassing all specialist baselines. On motion prediction (AMASS-BMLrub, 1000 ms), LMM-Large reports 63.1 mm MPJPE, slightly behind specialist methods (GCNext 65.3, SIMLPE 65.7) but with a notable advantage on the out-of-distribution 3DPW benchmark (68.0 mm vs. 72.0 mm for GCNext). On AIST++ music-to-dance, LMM-Large achieves the highest diversity (Divk 9.85) and best alignment score (0.2249) among all compared methods, though its FIDk (22.08) trails TM2D (19.01).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On text-to-motion (HumanML3D), LMM-Large outperforms FineMoGen [157] on every metric (FID 0.040 vs. 0.151; R-Prec Top-1 0.525 vs. 0.504) and MoMask [31] on FID (0.040 vs. 0.045) and Diversity (9.814 vs. not reported), while MoMask retains a large lead on the MM metric (1.241 vs. 2.683). Against MDM [128], LMM-Large dominates on FID (0.040 vs. 0.544) and R-Precision but trails slightly on MM (2.683 vs. 2.799). T2M-GPT [153] is clearly outperformed on FID (0.040 vs. 0.116) and all R-Precision scores. On motion prediction, LMM-Large is competitive but consistently 2–3 mm behind specialist methods (GCNext [133], SIMLPE [35], LTD-10-10 [95]) at short horizons (80–320 ms) on AMASS, yet narrows the gap at 1000 ms (63.1 vs. 65.3 mm) and shows a clearer advantage on the out-of-distribution 3DPW set (68.0 vs. 72.0 mm for GCNext). On music-to-dance (AIST++), LMM-Large surpasses DanceNet [167] and DanceRevolution [43] by large margins on FIDk (22.08 vs. 69.18/73.42) and diversity, beats TM2D [29] on Divk (9.85 vs. 9.45) and alignment score (0.2249 vs. 0.2049), but trails TM2D on FIDk (22.08 vs. 19.01) and Bailando [121] on FIDg (21.97 vs. 9.62).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer-based diffusion architecture for motion generation established by MDM and MotionDiffuse, and directly builds on FineMoGen's SAMI module as its architectural baseline. The key departure from prior multi-task motion generation work (MCM, UDE) is the scale and heterogeneity of unification: whereas those methods handle 2–3 datasets sharing a single motion format, LMM consolidates 16 datasets with fundamentally different representations (keypoints, SMPL, SMPL-X, 6D rotation) and 10 distinct tasks under a single model. The ArtAttention mechanism replaces FineMoGen's fixed body-part coefficients with a learned spatial attention that handles missing parts and variable masking, while ImageBind provides a unified multi-modal condition space analogous to how CLIP unified image-text alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MotionVerse (16 datasets, 10 tasks, 320K sequences, 100M frames) and its unified 10-part body representation provide the field with the first infrastructure for training and evaluating generalist motion models, reducing the need for task-specific pipelines. The ablation finding that random masking is necessary to force the model to actually use condition signals (otherwise it simply denoises) is a practical training insight applicable beyond motion generation. Limitations include: LMM-Tiny underperforms the specialist FineMoGen, short-range motion prediction accuracy lags specialists due to representation translation errors, and music-to-dance FID does not surpass dedicated methods—suggesting the unified approach still has a data-imbalance problem. Open questions include whether further scaling closes the remaining gaps to specialists and how to handle even more heterogeneous formats (e.g., physics-based or interaction-rich motion).
