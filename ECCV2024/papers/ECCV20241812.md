## Problem

Existing video deraining methods rely on synthetic paired data for training, creating a substantial domain gap that degrades performance on real-world rain videos. Self-learning methods that avoid ground-truths assume rain streaks are randomly distributed across frames, an assumption that breaks down for dense rain and cannot handle rain accumulation. No prior method effectively removes both rain streaks and rain accumulation from real-world videos without requiring ground-truths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241812/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method processes 16-frame video clips through a 3D transformer with patch size 4 and 768-dimensional hidden features, producing derained frames of the same temporal length. Before the student network, inputs are augmented via RainMix: hard augmentation adds synthetic rain streaks and depth-conditioned rain accumulation, while easy augmentation blends the rainy input with the teacher's prediction at a random weight w^Easy ∈ (0.5, 1). Loss is computed only in high-confidence regions identified by the two-teacher confidence masks, not over the full frame.

## Method

Dual-Rain introduces a two-teacher framework where an assertive teacher (EMA = 0.003) rapidly accumulates deraining knowledge from the student, and a gentle teacher (EMA = 0.001) slowly gathers stable knowledge to prevent over-suppression of bright regions. Both teachers generate predictions and confidence maps via sliding-window variance over overlapping patches; their averaged predictions and intersected confidence masks yield pseudo ground-truths for the student. The student is trained on RainMix-augmented inputs—hard augmentation synthesizes additional rain streaks and depth-based rain accumulation to create challenging samples, while easy augmentation mixes the input with the teacher prediction to create easier consistency targets. This iterative teacher-student loop, initialized from a synthetic-data pre-trained 3D transformer, progressively addresses increasingly difficult regions in real-world rain videos without any ground-truth annotations.

## Results

On the SynHeavyRain dataset, Dual-Rain achieves 23.89 dB PSNR and 0.8567 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline V-DiT (21.39 dB PSNR, 0.6867 SSIM) by 2.5 dB in PSNR and 0.17 in SSIM. On Haze-NTU, the method reaches 19.06 dB PSNR and 0.7847 SSIM, again leading all baselines. On real-world videos (no ground truth), Dual-Rain achieves the best non-reference scores: 24.32 BRISQUE (lower is better) and 0.6702 HyperIQA (higher is better), surpassing the next-best method SAVD (29.49 BRISQUE, 0.6695 HyperIQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-Rain outperforms all seven baselines on both synthetic datasets across PSNR and SSIM. Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline V-DiT (3D DiT), Dual-Rain gains 2.5 dB PSNR and 0.17 SSIM on SynHeavyRain and 0.84 dB PSNR on Haze-NTU. Against the other diffusion method WDiff, the margin is larger: 3.32 dB PSNR on SynHeavyRain and 2.09 dB on Haze-NTU. Among CNN-based methods trained on synthetic data, Dual-Rain beats ESTIL by 3.46 dB and BIPNet by 3.38 dB on SynHeavyRain, and by 2.15 dB and 1.2 dB respectively on Haze-NTU. The transformer-based Video Swin baseline is surpassed by 4.36 dB on SynHeavyRain. The self-learning methods show the largest gaps: SLDNet (unsupervised) trails by 10.41 dB and SAVD (semi-supervised) by 7.34 dB on SynHeavyRain, reflecting their inability to handle rain accumulation and dense streaks without ground-truth supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student semi-supervised learning paradigm to video deraining, departing from prior self-learning methods (SLDNet, SAVD) that rely on the assumption of randomly distributed rain streaks across frames, and from synthetic-data-trained methods (ESTIL, BIPNet, Video Swin) that suffer from domain gaps on real-world videos. It builds on the rain accumulation physical model from Yang et al. [33] for its hard augmentation and adopts a transformer-based backbone (3D DiT-style) for the student network. The key conceptual departure is the dual-teacher design: rather than a single EMA-updated teacher, the paper introduces an assertive teacher (large EMA, fast knowledge transfer) paired with a gentle teacher (small EMA, slow transfer) to balance aggressive deraining against over-suppression of bright regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation in video deraining: methods trained on synthetic data fail on real-world rain due to domain gaps, while self-learning methods cannot handle rain accumulation. The dual-teacher mechanism and RainMix augmentation offer a principled way to close this gap without requiring real ground-truth pairs. However, the real-world evaluation relies solely on non-reference metrics (BRISQUE, HyperIQA) and qualitative comparisons, leaving open questions about robustness across diverse weather conditions, computational cost of the iterative teacher-student loop, and whether the approach generalizes to other degradation types (snow, fog). The depth-map estimation required for rain accumulation simulation in hard augmentation introduces an additional source of error that is acknowledged but not thoroughly quantified.
