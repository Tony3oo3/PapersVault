## Problem

Generative models for e-commerce advertising images frequently produce substandard outputs—space/size mismatches, indistinct products, and shape hallucinations—resulting in a low available rate that necessitates costly human inspection. The paper tackles how to build a reliable generation pipeline that both automatically inspects generated images and improves the underlying diffusion model to reduce the number of generation attempts needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240532/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generation pipeline takes a transparent-background product image (processed via Canny edge detection into ControlNet) and a text prompt describing the desired background, producing a full advertising image via 40-step DDIM inpainting at 384x384 resolution. The RFNet inspector additionally consumes a depth map, a salience map, and a product caption alongside the product and generated images, outputting a five-class fine-grained availability label (Available, Space Mismatch, Size Mismatch, Indistinctiveness, Shape Hallucination). The RFFT stage outputs updated ControlNet weights that can be swapped across different LoRA adapters and base diffusion checkpoints without retraining.

## Method

RFNet fuses five modalities through Feature Filter Modules (cross-attention between product-image embeddings and BERT/RoBERTa caption embeddings, followed by 1x1 convolutions) to distill vision-relevant product attributes, then integrates all embeddings via stacked self-attention layers before a fully-connected classifier. Recurrent Generation repeatedly samples until RFNet predicts 'Available,' automating the inspection loop. RFFT fine-tunes only ControlNet by back-propagating the negative log-likelihood of the 'Available' class from RFNet through a randomly selected step in the last 10 of 40 DDIM denoising steps. To prevent the feedback gradient from collapsing backgrounds into repetitive, aesthetically degraded outputs, the authors replace the conventional KL regularization with a Consistent Condition (CC) term that penalizes divergence in the classifier-free guidance gradient direction (the text-condition influence on the latent) between the current and reference models, preserving visual quality while still allowing availability to increase.

## Results

On the RF1M test set (1,000 products), RFFT achieves 85.5% availability rate (Ava) and 86.3% human-verified availability (Human Ava) with a single generation attempt, substantially outperforming the original model (56.4% / 70.1%) and the closest RLHF baseline ReFL (84.7% / 84.9%). For the inspection task, RFNet attains 87.58 AP, 85.83 F1, and 86.45 Precision, surpassing the best single-modality baseline (ResNeXt50 at 79.62 AP) by nearly 8 points. The fine-tuned ControlNet generalizes across LoRA variants and base models, maintaining 79.7–85.5% Ava across Maji_v7, Maji_v6, and SD_v1.5 configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFFT outperforms all six baselines on both RFNet-assessed and human-assessed availability. Against the closest competitor ReFL (direct reward backpropagation), RFFT gains 0.8 points in Ava (85.5 vs 84.7) and 1.4 points in Human Ava (86.3 vs 84.9), with the CC regularization preventing the aesthetic collapse that ReFL suffers. PPO (65.9% Ava) and DPO (57.3% Ava) trail significantly, with DPO barely exceeding the original model (56.4%), suggesting that preference-optimization methods struggle with the fine-grained availability criterion. Prompt Engineering (62.9%) provides only a modest improvement over the original model. For the inspection sub-task, RFNet (87.58 AP) beats ResNeXt50 (79.62), ViT (79.31), ResNet50 (77.29), and HRNet (73.07) by 8+ AP points, confirming that multi-modal fusion (product image, generated image, depth, salience, caption) is critical for accurate availability judgment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based advertising image generation (building on Stable Diffusion + ControlNet inpainting pipelines) and RLHF-style alignment of diffusion models (DDPO, DPOK, ReFL, DRaFT). Its key methodological departure is the Consistent Condition (CC) regularization, which replaces the standard KL divergence term by instead constraining the *direction* of classifier-free text guidance rather than the full image distribution, thereby avoiding the adversarial dynamics that KL introduces when the reward pushes toward a narrow 'available' mode. The multi-modal RFNet inspection network and the RF1M dataset extend prior human-feedback datasets (Pick-a-Pic, ImageRewardDB) into the advertising-specific domain with fine-grained failure taxonomy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CC regularization offers a generally applicable recipe for aligning diffusion models on a targeted quality metric without aesthetic collapse, a problem that affects any RLHF-for-diffusion pipeline. The RF1M dataset (1M+ annotated advertising images with multi-modal side information) fills a notable gap in the e-commerce generation literature and could support downstream tasks beyond inspection. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-e-commerce reliability constraints (e.g., medical imaging, safety-critical generation), whether the fine-tuned ControlNet degrades over long production horizons, and whether the RFNet-as-reward-model loop introduces compounding biases at scale.
