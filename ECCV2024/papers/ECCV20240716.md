## Problem

Existing gaze target detection methods rely on estimating gaze direction and searching for salient objects in the full global-view image, which fails in complex scenes where salient objects outside the gaze cone mislead the network. The paper identifies a missing third perspective: a FOV-based local view that tightly crops the region between the person's head and the gaze cone, retaining all task-relevant features while eliminating task-irrelevant distractors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240716/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unconstrained RGB image as input, from which a head crop is extracted and monocular depth is estimated via MiDaS. The local view is computed as the smallest rectangle enclosing the head position and the 3D field-of-view cone (30° half-angle), and all views (head, local, global) are resized to 224×224. Outputs include a 1×64×64 gaze target heatmap, a 3D gaze direction vector, and a binary out-of-frame confidence score.

## Method

A head view branch (ResNet-50 + MLP) extracts 1024×7×7 facial features and predicts 3D gaze direction, which is combined with MiDaS depth to compute a 3D FOV map and crop the local view. A shared ResNet-50 backbone processes both the local and global views (each with RGB, head position map, and 3D FOV map concatenated along channels), and a head attention module multiplies scene features with head-position-conditioned attention maps to enforce global-local position consistency. A contrastive learning objective (InfoNCE-style, τ=0.1) aligns local-view scene features with the corresponding spatial patches from global-view features, encouraging the shared backbone to learn task-consistent representations. Facial, local-view, and global-view features are sequentially fused through a 1×1 CNN and a D-CNN decoder to produce the final heatmap, with independent supervised heatmap losses on each view during training. The head view branch is pretrained across Gaze360, ETH-XGaze, and MPSGaze for cross-dataset generalization of gaze estimation.

## Results

On GazeFollow, the complete method (G+L) achieves SOTA across all metrics: AUC 0.939, Min L2 0.059, Avg L2 0.114, and Angle Error 12.4°, outperforming the previous best (Tafasca et al., AUC 0.936; Jin et al., Min L2 0.064). On VideoAttentionTarget, it reaches AUC 0.920, L2 Distance 0.101, and AP 0.916, surpassing Miao et al. (AUC 0.917, Dist 0.109, AP 0.908). The method runs at 45 FPS with 13.3 GFLOPs on a single Titan Xp GPU, substantially faster than Tu et al. (16 FPS, 79.7 GFLOPs) while achieving better accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves SOTA on every reported metric for both GazeFollow and VideoAttentionTarget, beating the strongest baselines (Miao et al. [23], Tafasca et al. [27], Jin et al. [16]) by 0.3–0.5% AUC, 7–8% relative reduction in Avg L2, and 1.5–2.4° in angle error on GazeFollow, and by 0.3–0.5% AUC, 2.9% relative reduction in L2 distance, and 0.8–1.1% AP on VideoAttentionTarget. Notably, the local-view-only variant (Ours-l) already matches or exceeds most prior SOTA methods (e.g., AUC 0.933 vs. Tafasca's 0.936 on GazeFollow; AP 0.905 vs. Miao's 0.908 on VAT), indicating the local view alone captures most of the gain. The full G+L coordination provides an additional 0.6–0.8% AUC and 0.007–0.008 L2 improvement over either view alone. In the scalability experiment (Table 7), integrating the local view and coordination mechanism into VideoAtt [7] and Fang et al. [10] improves their Avg L2 by 11% and 5.6% respectively, confirming the framework's plug-and-play benefit. The method also outperforms the human-annotated baseline on AUC (0.939 vs. 0.924) and angle error (12.4° vs. 11.0° is the only metric where humans lead), approaching human-level L2 distance (0.114 vs. 0.096).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gaze target detection line initiated by Recasens et al. (2015) and advanced by methods like VideoAtt, Fang et al., ESCNet, and Miao et al., all of which operate on a head-view + global-view formulation. The key departure is the introduction of a third, FOV-based local view that crops the global image to the gaze cone region, reducing task-irrelevant salient objects that mislead existing networks. The coordination mechanism borrows contrastive learning ideas from self-supervised representation learning (SimCLR, MoCo) to enforce representation consistency between the local and global views, and leverages monocular depth estimation (MiDaS) to compute a 3D field of view for local view extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The local view concept offers a simple, interpretable way to focus network attention on the gaze-relevant region, and the demonstrated scalability—where plugging the local view and coordination mechanism into existing methods (VideoAtt, Fang et al.) yields consistent gains—suggests the framework could be adopted broadly. However, the method's reliance on accurate gaze direction estimation remains a vulnerability in low-visibility scenarios, and the field's datasets (GazeFollow, VideoAttentionTarget) lack true ground-truth gaze data, relying on human-annotated best guesses that limit ecological validity. Open questions include whether the local view approach generalizes to multi-person scenes or video sequences without per-frame depth estimation.
