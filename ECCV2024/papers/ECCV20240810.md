## Problem

Transformer-based feed-forward 3D generation models like LRM fail to exploit the geometric priors inherent in the triplane representation, resulting in sub-optimal reconstruction quality and prohibitively long training times given the scarcity of 3D training data. The spatial correspondence between orthographic views and triplane structure remains unexploited in existing architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240810/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image as input and produces a textured 3D mesh (Flexicubes grid of size 80) in approximately 10 seconds on an A800 GPU without any test-time optimization. Internally, the pipeline first generates six 256×256 orthographic view images and corresponding 3-channel canonical coordinate maps (CCMs) as intermediate representations before the reconstruction step.

## Method

CRM's core insight is that the triplane is spatially aligned with six orthographic images, enabling a convolutional U-Net (rather than a transformer) to directly map the rolled-out input images and CCMs to a high-resolution triplane with strong pixel-level alignment. A multi-view diffusion model (initialized from ImageDream and expanded from 4 to 6 views) generates the orthographic images, while a second diffusion model produces CCMs conditioned on those images. The triplane is decoded by small MLPs into SDF values, deformation, color, and Flexicubes weights, from which a textured mesh is extracted end-to-end via dual marching cubes. Training is supervised on ~376k filtered Objaverse objects using a combined loss of MSE, LPIPS, depth-map MSE, mask MSE, and Flexicubes regularizers, requiring only 8 A800 GPUs for 6 days at batch size 32—roughly 1/8 the cost of LRM.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (30 shapes, held out from training), CRM achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0094, Volume IoU of 0.6131, and F-Score of 79.38% for geometry, outperforming the next-best baseline LGM (CD 0.0117, Vol. IoU 0.4685, F-Score 68.69%) by a wide margin. For texture quality, CRM reaches PSNR 16.22, SSIM 0.8381, LPIPS 0.2143, and Clip-Similarity 87.55, surpassing all baselines. The multi-view diffusion component also outperforms Wonder3D (PSNR 29.36 vs. 23.76) and SyncDreamer (PSNR 29.36 vs. 20.30) on novel view synthesis. The full inference pipeline (diffusion + U-Net + mesh extraction) completes in approximately 10 seconds on a single A800 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRM outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. In geometry (Table 1), CRM's Chamfer Distance of 0.0094 is 20% lower than LGM (0.0117), the closest competitor, and nearly 44% lower than OpenLRM (0.0168); its Volume IoU of 0.6131 exceeds LGM's 0.4685 by 14.5 points. In texture (Table 2), CRM leads OpenLRM (the best texture baseline) by 1.92 dB in PSNR (16.22 vs. 14.30) and by 3.35 points in Clip-Similarity (87.55 vs. 84.20). For multi-view synthesis (Table 3), CRM's diffusion model achieves PSNR 29.36, substantially ahead of Wonder3D (23.76) and SyncDreamer (20.30), indicating that the generated six orthographic views are far more consistent. The paper also notes that CRM's end-to-end mesh output avoids the post-processing steps required by LGM and TGS (which use Gaussian Splatting), and its training cost is roughly 1/8 that of LRM (batch size 32 on 8 GPUs for 6 days vs. batch size 1024 for LRM).

## Relation to Prior Work

CRM departs from the dominant transformer-based feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm (LRM, TGS, LGM) by replacing the transformer with a convolutional U-Net, motivated by the observation that triplane features are spatially aligned with six orthographic input images—a geometric prior that transformers do not exploit. It builds on ImageDream for multi-view diffusion initialization and on Flexicubes for end-to-end textured mesh extraction, while introducing Canonical Coordinate Maps (CCMs) as an additional geometric input that prior feed-forward methods like LRM omit. The paper also positions itself against score-distillation methods (DreamFusion, ProlificDreamer) and sparse-view reconstruction pipelines (SyncDreamer, Wonder3D) that require test-time optimization, offering a fully feed-forward alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/8 training cost relative to LRM and the 10-second inference without test-time optimization make high-fidelity single-image-to-mesh generation substantially more accessible, lowering the barrier for both research and deployment. The end-to-end textured mesh output (via Flexicubes + dual marching cubes) eliminates the post-processing step required by NeRF or Gaussian Splatting-based pipelines, simplifying the inference graph. Open questions include whether the convolutional spatial-alignment prior generalizes beyond triplane to other 3D representations, whether the approach scales to multi-object or scene-level generation, and how robust the pipeline is to in-the-wild inputs beyond the Objaverse-trained distribution.
