## Problem

Controllable GANs typically require supervision signals (segmentation maps, 3DMM, pre-trained classifiers) or post-processing steps (PCA, subspace projection) to achieve interpretable latent control, limiting generalizability and adding pipeline complexity. Orthogonal regularizers like Hessian penalty and OroJaR avoid supervision but incur high computational cost from stochastic finite-difference estimation and often degrade image quality (higher FID). This paper seeks fully unsupervised, end-to-end linear controllability without these dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128-dimensional Gaussian noise vector as input, which is split into two 64-dimensional segments corresponding to geometry and appearance. Each segment passes through a dedicated linear mapping network (12 fully connected layers with no non-linear activations) to produce intermediate latent codes w^g and w^a. The geometry code drives flowfield generation (controlling viewpoint/pose) while the appearance code shapes feature maps (controlling texture, color, expression), and the final output is an RGB image at 256², 512², or 1024² resolution. Because the mapping is strictly linear, each dimension of the input noise independently and linearly activates a specific semantic attribute in the output.

## Method

The discriminator is extended with two projection heads (h^g, h^a) that learn geometry and appearance embedding spaces via contrastive feature clustering: augmented images (perspective transforms for geometry, color jittering/erasing for appearance) serve as positive/negative pairs, pulling same-type features together and pushing cross-type features apart in L2-normalized space. The generator enforces correct latent segmentation through conditional resampling—resampling only the geometry segment should alter viewpoint while preserving appearance (and vice versa), validated by the discriminator's contrastive loss on generated images. For fine-grained disentanglement within each segment, a spectral regularizer applies L1 penalty to the eigenvalues of a learnable covariance matrix parameterizing the input Gaussian, automatically zeroing out unimportant dimensions and selecting a sparse set of semantically meaningful features. The mapping networks use Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization on learned basis vectors and omit all non-linear activations to preserve the linearity of the Gaussian distribution, ensuring that linear manipulation of the input noise translates directly to linear changes in the output image.

## Results

LC-GAN achieves FID scores of 3.65 (FFHQ-256), 3.72 (CelebA-HQ-256), 5.77 (AFHQ-V2-256), 3.36 (FFHQ-512), 3.49 (CelebA-HQ-512), 4.74 (AFHQ-V2-512), 3.32 (FFHQ-1024), and 3.46 (CelebA-HQ-1024), matching or approaching non-controllable SOTA methods like StyleGAN3 (FFHQ-1024: 3.07) and StyleSwin (CelebA-HQ-256: 3.25) while providing linear controllability. For identity-preserving generation, LC-GAN achieves Arcface distances of 0.61–0.78, substantially lower than SeFa (0.85–1.13) and EigenGAN (0.91–1.03). For viewpoint-preserving generation, LC-GAN achieves landmark distances of 1.65–9.09, outperforming SeFa (2.41–11.34) and EigenGAN (2.61–13.99) across all resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LC-GAN substantially outperforms the two controllable baselines (SeFa and EigenGAN) on every reported FID score, with margins of 2.8–15.2 FID points (e.g., FFHQ-1024: 3.32 vs. SeFa 9.36 and EigenGAN 18.51; CelebA-HQ-256: 3.72 vs. SeFa 6.43 and EigenGAN 14.05). Against non-controllable SOTA methods, LC-GAN is competitive but not always best: it trails StyleGAN2 (FFHQ-1024: 3.32 vs. 2.70) and StyleGAN3 (FFHQ-1024: 3.32 vs. 3.07; AFHQ-V2-512: 4.74 vs. 4.40) by 0.25–0.62 FID, and StyleSwin on FFHQ-256 (3.65 vs. 2.81) and CelebA-HQ-256 (3.72 vs. 3.25). However, LC-GAN beats StyleSwin at 1024² resolution (FFHQ: 3.32 vs. 5.07; CelebA-HQ: 3.46 vs. 4.43) and all other non-controllable methods at that resolution. On controllability metrics, LC-GAN achieves the lowest Arcface identity distance (0.61–0.78) and landmark distance (1.65–9.09) across all six dataset-resolution combinations, confirming that its linear control preserves identity and viewpoint more faithfully than SeFa or EigenGAN, which introduce entangled changes in both pose and appearance when editing a single semantic axis.

## Relation to Prior Work

LC-GAN builds on the StyleGAN2/StyleGAN3 architecture (mapping networks, synthesis networks with flowfields) but departs fundamentally from prior controllable GAN approaches that rely on supervision signals (segmentation maps, 3DMMs, text), pre-trained classifiers, or post-hoc subspace analysis (GANSpace, PCA). It also moves beyond orthogonal regularizers like HessianPenalty and OroJaR, which require costly stochastic estimation of finite differences. The key conceptual departure is replacing all of these with an end-to-end unsupervised pipeline: contrastive feature clustering (driven by image augmentations) for geometry/appearance separation, and L1 spectral regularization on a learnable covariance matrix for fine-grained feature selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that LC-GAN closes the quality gap between controllable and non-controllable GANs—achieving FID scores on par with StyleGAN3 and StyleSwin while providing linear latent control—without requiring any labels, 3D supervision, or post-processing. This makes controllable generation applicable to novel datasets without additional annotation effort. However, the authors acknowledge that training is computationally expensive (more GPU memory due to contrastive loss), and precise metric-level control (e.g., exactly 20° rotation) remains difficult due to the unsupervised nature. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond face/animal-face domains and whether the spectral regularization can be made more scalable to very high-dimensional latent spaces.
