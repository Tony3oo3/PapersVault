## Problem

Foundation 3D generative models require massive training data, but 3D assets are orders of magnitude scarcer than images, text, or video due to the cost and complexity of 3D capture or modeling. Even the largest public 3D datasets contain only ~10M assets, many of which are duplicates or lack quality textures, creating a fundamental data bottleneck that prevents scaling 3D generative models to the level of their 2D counterparts.

## Input / Output

VFusion3D takes a single 512×512 RGB image as input (or a text prompt, in which case EMU first generates the conditioning image) and produces a 3D tri-plane representation in a single feed-forward pass. The tri-plane is upsampled and queried at 3D points to predict per-point RGB and density, enabling volumetric rendering of novel views at 384×384 resolution from arbitrary azimuth angles (full 360° coverage). Camera parameters are encoded via a small embedding network and fed into the tri-plane decoder alongside DINO image features.

## Method

The core idea is to repurpose a pre-trained video diffusion model (EMU Video) as a synthetic multi-view data engine. EMU Video is fine-tuned on 100K rendered 3D objects (16 views each along a fixed circular trajectory) by freezing all parameters except temporal convolutional and attention layers, then used to generate 4M multi-view videos from web-scale text prompts, of which 2.7M pass a DINO-feature-based SVM quality filter. The resulting VFusion3D model follows the LRM architecture (DINO encoder → cross-attention tri-plane decoder → 3D point feature query → MLP for RGB/density → volumetric rendering) but is trained on the synthetic data with four key strategies: a multi-stage resolution curriculum (128→384), image-level LPIPS loss instead of pixel-level losses to tolerate minor 3D inconsistencies, an opacity loss using saliency masks to suppress background artifacts, and camera noise injection for robustness. A final fine-tuning stage on the original 100K 3D data (rendered at 32 views) combines the generalization of synthetic data with the fidelity of real 3D geometry.

## Results

On the image-to-3D task (Table 1), VFusion3D achieves a CLIP image similarity of 0.851 and CLIP text similarity of 0.253, outperforming OpenLRM (0.793 / 0.234) and LGM (0.796 / 0.241). On the text-to-3D task (Table 2), VFusion3D attains the highest CLIP image similarity of 0.899 among all seven compared methods, while its CLIP text similarity of 0.272 is competitive with but slightly below the best distillation-based methods (ProlificDreamer and Magic3D at 0.293). In a user study over 65 rendered videos, VFusion3D is preferred over SOTA baselines by a considerable margin in both generation quality and image faithfulness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VFusion3D decisively outperforms both feed-forward baselines (OpenLRM and LGM) on every metric in both the image-to-3D and text-to-3D settings, with a notable gap in CLIP image similarity (0.899 vs. 0.832 for LGM and 0.826 for OpenLRM in text-to-3D). Against the distillation-based text-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, Magic3D, ProlificDreamer, MVDream), VFusion3D achieves substantially higher image similarity (0.899 vs. 0.640–0.699), confirming its strength in visual fidelity and 3D consistency. However, the score-distillation methods Magic3D and ProlificDreamer edge out VFusion3D on CLIP text similarity (0.293 vs. 0.272), which the authors attribute to the inherent advantage of iterative optimization in aligning with text prompts. Overall, VFusion3D is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier image similarity and competitive text alignment, and it is preferred by users over 90% of the time in the majority-vote user study.

## Relation to Prior Work

VFusion3D adopts the LRM architecture (tri-plane + DINO encoder + volumetric rendering) without modification, positioning its contribution squarely in the data pipeline rather than model design. It departs from distillation-based text-to-3D methods (DreamFusion, Magic3D) that rely on score distillation sampling, and from concurrent reconstruction approaches (IM-3D) that use fine-tuned video diffusion for per-scene optimization, by instead treating the fine-tuned video diffusion model as a one-time synthetic data engine. The training strategies (multi-stage resolution, image-level LPIPS loss, opacity supervision, camera noise) are specifically designed to handle the noise and imperfect 3D consistency inherent in video-diffusion-generated multi-view data, distinguishing this work from prior feed-forward 3D models trained on real 3D datasets like Objaverse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses the fundamental data bottleneck for foundation 3D generative models by demonstrating that video diffusion models can generate near-unlimited synthetic multi-view training data, with scaling trends showing consistent quality improvement up to 2.7M samples. The finding that 100K real 3D data matches 2.7M synthetic data for common objects, yet synthetic data provides superior generalization to uncommon objects, clarifies the complementary roles of real and synthetic data. Key limitations include poor generation of vehicles and text-bearing objects (inherited from the video diffusion model), and the approach's quality is ultimately bounded by the underlying video diffusion model's 3D understanding. Open questions include whether this paradigm scales to scene-level generation and whether stronger video models will close the remaining gap on challenging object categories.
