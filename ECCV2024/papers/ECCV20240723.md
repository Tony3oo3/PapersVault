## Problem

CLIP's instance discrimination treats all cross-instance pairs as negatives regardless of semantic similarity, failing to encode the semantic structure of training data. Existing cluster discrimination methods (e.g., UNICOM) address this by assigning a single pseudo-label per image, but natural images typically contain multiple objects, attributes, and scene elements that a single label cannot capture. The paper asks how to exploit multi-label signals from automatically clustered large-scale data to learn richer visual representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 during pre-training, up to 448×448 for fine-tuning) from web-scale datasets (LAION-400M, COYO-700M) along with paired text prompts. It produces normalized visual embeddings via a ViT-L/14 (or ViT-B/32) image encoder, with a separately trained text encoder (via LiT) for zero-shot tasks. During the clustering step, each image is assigned l=8 positive class centers out of 1M total, and during discrimination the model outputs similarity scores against sampled positive and negative centers rather than a single class probability.

## Method

MLCD operates in two stages. First, a one-step offline k-means (using pre-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 features and efficient quantization) partitions LAION-400M into 1M centers; each image then receives its top-l=8 nearest centers as multi-label positive assignments. Second, a novel multi-label classification disambiguation loss (MLCD loss, Eq. 5) decomposes the objective into three additive terms inside a log-sum-exp: a contrastive term reducing (s_j − s_i), a negative term explicitly minimizing inter-class similarities s_j, and a positive term explicitly maximizing intra-class similarities s_i. This decomposition elegantly separates positive-class and negative-class losses, resolving the decision-boundary ambiguity inherent in optimizing only the relative gap (s_j − s_i). To handle the 1M-class scale, PartialFC randomly samples a fraction r=0.1 of negative centers per iteration, and the text encoder is trained from scratch with a frozen image encoder following the LiT protocol.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across 25 datasets, MLCD achieves 67.5% average accuracy with ViT-L/14 on LAION-400M, surpassing OpenCLIP (63.6%) by 3.9% and FLIP (66.0%) by 1.5%. On ImageNet, MLCD reaches 87.1% finetuning accuracy, 84.6% linear probe, and 75.6% zero-shot, outperforming OpenCLIP by 0.9%/2.5%/2.8% respectively and FLIP by 1.0% on zero-shot. For zero-shot image-text retrieval, MLCD attains 60.8% I2T Recall@1 on MSCOCO (vs. 58.0% OpenCLIP, 60.2% FLIP) and 75.9% T2I Recall@1 on Flickr30k. On linear probe across 26 datasets, MLCD improves over OpenCLIP by 2.3% on average and over UNICOM by 1.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenCLIP (same LAION-400M data, ViT-L/14), MLCD wins on 25 of 26 linear probe datasets (+2.3% avg), 23 of 25 zero-shot classification datasets (+3.9% avg), and both retrieval benchmarks (e.g., +2.8% I2T R@1 on MSCOCO), making it the most consistent and largest-margin comparison. Against FLIP (also LAION-400M), MLCD wins on 15 of 25 zero-shot classification datasets (+1.5% avg) and on ImageNet zero-shot (75.6% vs. 74.6%), though FLIP remains competitive on retrieval (60.2% vs. 60.8% I2T R@1 on MSCOCO) and slightly ahead on IN-R (86.5% vs. 85.1%). Against UNICOM (single-label cluster discrimination, same data), MLCD improves linear probe by 1.3% on average across 26 datasets and by 2.2% on LAION-400M / 1.6% on COYO-700M, confirming the benefit of multi-label over single-label cluster assignment. Against the original CLIP (WIT-400M), direct comparison is confounded by different pre-training data, but MLCD still achieves 67.5% vs. 66.9% average zero-shot classification and 75.6% vs. 75.3% ImageNet zero-shot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cluster discrimination paradigm (UNICOM, DeepCluster, SwAV) from single-label to multi-label pseudo-assignment, motivated by the observation that natural images contain multiple objects and attributes that a single cluster center cannot capture. The multi-label loss builds directly on the Circle Loss formulation for relative similarity optimization, but introduces a key departure: decomposing the combined contrastive term into separate positive-class and negative-class losses to eliminate decision-boundary ambiguity. The clustering step follows UNICOM's one-shot offline k-means on pre-computed CLIP features, and the negative class sampling strategy is inherited from PartialFC to manage inter-class conflict at one-million-class scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-label loss decomposition (Eq. 5) offers a clean, principled fix to a known weakness in relative-similarity losses, and the consistent 1–4% gains over both instance-discrimination (OpenCLIP) and single-label cluster-discrimination (UNICOM) baselines across linear probe, zero-shot classification, and retrieval suggest the technique is broadly applicable to CLIP-style pre-training pipelines. A notable limitation is the performance drop on OCR-related benchmarks (TextVQA, AI2D) when the model is used as a vision tower in LLaVA, indicating that the clustering-based multi-label signal does not capture fine-grained textual content. Open questions include whether iterative multi-label re-clustering (rather than the single offline pass) would further improve purity, and how to incorporate complementary signals (e.g., OCR embeddings) into the clustering step.
