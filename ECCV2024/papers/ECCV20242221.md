## Problem

Spatio-temporal video grounding (STVG) has always relied on extensive manual annotations—bounding box sequences paired with sentence queries—to train grounding models, making it expensive, slow, and infeasible in privacy-sensitive or data-scarce settings. No prior work has addressed STVG in a zero-shot manner, and the core difficulty is that STVG demands integrated reasoning across both spatial and temporal dimensions in untrimmed videos, which goes well beyond the image-level alignment that pretrained vision-language models provide out of the box.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video (resampled to 128 RGB frames) and a free-form natural language sentence query as input, and produces a spatio-temporal tube: a sequence of bounding boxes spanning the predicted start and end frames that localizes the described object. Object instances are first extracted per-frame by a pretrained Faster R-CNN detector, and the final output is the argmax bounding box in each frame within the predicted temporal window, with the temporal boundary itself determined by clustering and merging atomic events based on temporal relevance scores.

## Method

E3M is a fully training-free algorithm that leverages a pretrained CLIP (ViT-B/32) model and a Faster R-CNN detector, optimizing entirely at test time via an Expectation-Maximization loop that alternates between temporal relevance estimation (E step) and spatial grounding (M step) to resolve the intertwined spatio-temporal dilemma. In the M step, a context-based visual modulation propagates each detected object instance to 2T=20 neighboring frames using a Kalman filter with a constant-velocity model and Hungarian-algorithm association, then aggregates the resulting spatio-temporal visual features via average pooling; concurrently, a prototype-based textual modulation selects K=3 diverse visual prototypes (using a temporal suppression window to ensure diversity across action stages) and adds their CLIP-encoded features to the sentence embedding to bridge the cross-modal gap. In the E step, the spatial grounding results from the M step are used to blur irrelevant instances and compute per-frame temporal relevance scores, which are then clustered via K-Means into atomic events and greedily merged to yield the final temporal boundary. The entire pipeline requires no training data or annotations, running only 2 EM iterations at inference time.

## Results

On HC-STVG, E3M achieves m_vIoU of 19.11, vIoU@0.3 of 29.40, and vIoU@0.5 of 10.60 in a fully zero-shot (training-free) setting. On VidSTG (Declarative Sentence), it reaches m_vIoU 16.21 and vIoU@0.5 11.91, and on VidSTG (Interrogative Sentence) it attains m_vIoU 10.61 and vIoU@0.5 5.44. These results surpass the best weakly-supervised method (Winner) by over 75% in vIoU@0.5 on HC-STVG and are comparable to the fully-supervised STGVT on HC-STVG (19.11 vs. 18.15 m_vIoU), despite requiring no training data whatsoever.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E3M outperforms all weakly-supervised baselines across both datasets: on HC-STVG it exceeds Winner (the strongest weakly-supervised method) by 75% in vIoU@0.5 (10.60 vs. 6.12) and by 34% in m_vIoU (19.11 vs. 14.20). Among zero-shot methods, E3M surpasses ReCLIP by 33% in m_vIoU on HC-STVG (19.11 vs. 14.36) and by 14% on VidSTG Declarative (16.21 vs. 14.21), and it nearly matches Winner on VidSTG Interrogative (5.44 vs. 5.46 vIoU@0.5). Against fully-supervised methods, E3M is competitive with STGVT on HC-STVG (19.11 vs. 18.15 m_vIoU) but remains substantially below the strongest fully-supervised approaches: STCAT achieves 35.09 m_vIoU and TubeDETR 32.40 on HC-STVG, roughly 1.7–1.8× higher than E3M. The gap to top fully-supervised methods is expected given that E3M uses no training data, but the comparison with STGVT demonstrates that zero-shot grounding can match a method trained on fully-annotated spatio-temporal tubes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing the zero-shot capabilities of CLIP for spatio-temporal video grounding, a task previously addressed exclusively through fully- or weakly-supervised training on annotated bounding box sequences. Conceptually, it draws on classical tracking machinery (Kalman filters, Hungarian matching) to propagate object instances across frames, and on the EM algorithm to decouple the inherently intertwined temporal and spatial reasoning. It also extends the zero-shot image grounding line (ReCLIP, RedCircle) from single-frame localization to multi-frame tube prediction, introducing a prototype-based textual modulation to bridge the visual-textual gap that becomes more acute over temporal spans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating all training data and annotations, E3M opens a path to STVG in privacy-sensitive or resource-constrained settings where collecting bounding-box sequences is infeasible. The practical impact is real but tempered: the method still trails the best fully-supervised approaches (STCAT) by a wide margin (~16 m_vIoU on HC-STVG), and it depends on an off-the-shelf detector whose failures propagate directly into grounding errors. Open questions include whether the EM framework scales to longer videos or more complex multi-object queries, and whether the Kalman-based propagation generalizes to non-rigid or occluded objects beyond the relatively clean multi-person scenes in HC-STVG.
