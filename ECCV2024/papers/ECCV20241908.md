## Problem

Part-based recognition models, inspired by the human recognition-by-components theory, show promise for improving adversarial robustness, but prior work has only validated them on small-scale nonstandard datasets (e.g., PartImageNet with 24K images and 158 categories) due to the lack of large-scale part annotations. This prevents direct comparison with state-of-the-art adversarially trained models on the standard ImageNet-1K benchmark and limits the generalizability of findings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241908/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images from ImageNet-1K and produces a 1000-class object label. During training, each image is additionally paired with a composite part segmentation mask of shape H×W×(K+1) (K=3308 part categories plus background), where masks for annotated images come from PIN++ and masks for the remaining ~1.2M training images are pseudo-labels generated by a Mask R-CNN segmenter with a Category Filter post-processing step. At inference, only the vanilla classification head is active, so the model consumes a single RGB image and outputs one class label with zero additional parameters or computation.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages: (1) a Mask R-CNN with Swin-B backbone is trained on PIN++ (100K annotated images) to generate pseudo part labels for all unannotated IN-1K training images, with a Category Filter that zeros out probabilities of part categories unrelated to the known object class; (2) the Multi-scale Part-supervised Model (MPM) is trained by attaching lightweight bypass layers (point-wise convolutions, ~4.5M params) to the last three blocks of a ResNet-50-Gelu backbone, each producing a part segmentation prediction at a different resolution (28×28, 14×14, 7×7), connected by FPN-style top-down paths. The training objective is L = L_cls + λ·L_seg (λ=1), where L_seg averages segmentation losses across the three scales, and adversarial examples are generated via PGD (ε=4/255, t=2) on the classification loss only. Unlike prior two-stage part-based models that require a separate segmenter and classifier at inference (doubling parameters) and are constrained to low-resolution part labels, MPM drops all bypass layers at inference, retains the full 25.6M parameter count of the vanilla backbone, and exploits higher-resolution part supervision to shape intermediate representations.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with l∞(ε=4/255) adversarial training, MPM (ResNet-50-Gelu) achieves 39.1% adversarial accuracy under l∞ attack and 20.8% average accuracy across four attack settings (l∞, l∞×2, l1, l2), outperforming the strongest prior baseline (Debenedetti et al., 17.1% average) by 3.7 percentage points. MPM also improves clean accuracy to 67.8% (matching the best baseline) while adding no extra inference cost. On common corruptions, MPM with AT improves average accuracy from 34.9% to 36.4%, and on OOD datasets the average rises from 24.6% to 25.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPM outperforms all five AT baselines on IN-1K across every attack setting. Against the strongest prior method (Debenedetti et al. [10], 17.1% average), MPM gains +3.7% average robustness, with the largest margin on l2 attacks (+6.4%, 24.3% vs 17.9%) and l1 attacks (+2.9%, 6.2% vs 3.3%). On the standard l∞(ε=4/255) threat, MPM achieves 39.1%, beating the best l∞ baseline (Bai et al. [3] at 36.6%) by 2.5 points. Compared to the two-stage part-based model of Sitawarin et al. [56] (60.9M inference parameters, 38.7% l∞), MPM achieves higher l∞ accuracy (39.1%) with only 25.6M parameters—less than half the inference cost. MPM also matches the best clean accuracy (67.8%, tied with Bai et al. [3]) while simultaneously improving adversarial robustness, indicating that part supervision does not sacrifice standard performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the part-based adversarial robustness line initiated by Li et al. (ROCK, TPAMI 2023) and Sitawarin et al. (ICLR 2023), both of which validated two-stage segment-then-classify models only on the small, animal-heavy PartImageNet (158 categories, 24K images). It departs from these in two key ways: (1) it introduces PIN++, a 100K-image part annotation dataset covering all 1000 ImageNet-1K categories with 3,308 part categories, enabling direct comparison with standard IN-1K baselines; and (2) it replaces the two-stage architecture with MPM, a single-stage model that adds lightweight multi-scale bypass layers (inspired by FPN) to a vanilla ResNet-50 for part supervision, eliminating extra inference parameters. The adversarial training recipe builds on standard PGD-based AT with GELU-activated ResNet-50, following recent IN-1K robustness work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PIN++ fills a critical gap by providing the first part annotation resource spanning all ImageNet-1K categories, unblocking rigorous evaluation of part-based methods against SOTA robustness baselines on the standard benchmark. MPM's design—achieving SOTA ResNet-50 adversarial robustness (20.8% average) with zero extra inference cost—makes part supervision practically deployable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other backbones (ViTs, ConvNeXt), whether the 100K annotated images suffice for all downstream part-related tasks (segmentation, parsing), and whether the part-based inductive bias can be further exploited (e.g., hierarchical part relationships, spatial reasoning between parts). The authors explicitly frame MPM as 'a stepping stone,' leaving the broader potential of part-based models for robustness largely unexplored.
