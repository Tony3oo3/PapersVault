## Problem

Most monocular robot pose estimation methods assume known joint states, limiting their use in multi-robot collaboration, human-robot interaction, and scenarios where joint data is unavailable or untrustworthy. The few methods that estimate pose without joint priors (e.g., RoboPose) rely on iterative render-and-compare optimization, making them too slow for real-time deployment. This paper addresses the gap by enabling holistic robot pose estimation—simultaneously recovering 6D camera-to-robot pose and all joint state parameters—from a single RGB image in one feed-forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image as input and produces four outputs in a single forward pass: camera-to-robot rotation (SO(3) via 6D continuous representation), camera-to-robot translation (derived from the root keypoint's 3D position), robot joint state parameters (angles for revolute joints and displacements for prismatic joints), and absolute 3D keypoint locations at all joint articulations. No iterative refinement, PnP solver, or external segmentation is required at inference; the entire pipeline runs at ~22.6 FPS on a V100 GPU.

## Method

The framework decomposes holistic estimation into four sub-tasks, each handled by a dedicated module sharing a CNN feature extractor: DepthNet predicts a correction factor λ applied to an analytically computed normalized coarse depth (disentangling scale-variant depth from scale-invariant features); JointNet and RotationNet regress joint parameters and SO(3) rotation via MLPs on globally pooled features; KeypointNet estimates root-relative 3D keypoints through per-keypoint 3D heatmaps decoded with differentiable spatial integration. The key design insight is using 3D keypoints as a bridge between pixel-aligned image features and the abstract robot kinematic parameter space, with forward kinematics (FK) linking joint states, rotation, and translation to final 3D keypoint predictions. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised pre-training on domain-randomized synthetic data (with DepthNet pre-trained first to stabilize global translation), followed by self-supervised fine-tuning on real images using keypoint-consistency and mask-consistency regularizations that require no ground-truth labels.

## Results

On the DREAM benchmark, the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art AUC of 82.2 on Panda 3CAM-AK (vs. 70.4 for RoboPose) and 75.2 on Panda ORB (vs. 70.4), with mean ADD errors of 18.9 mm and 24.8 mm respectively. On synthetic datasets, it reaches 82.7 AUC on Panda DR and 82.0 on Panda Photo, outperforming RoboPose on 7 of 9 evaluated datasets. In terms of efficiency, the method runs at 22.6 FPS (44.3 ms per frame) with 25.2 GFLOPs, delivering a 12× speedup over RoboPose (1.8 FPS, 570.8 ms) while maintaining or exceeding accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary competitor is RoboPose [15], the only prior method addressing holistic pose estimation with unknown joint states. The proposed method outperforms RoboPose on 7 of 9 datasets in AUC, with the largest gains on Baxter DR (58.8 vs. 32.7) and Panda 3CAM-AK (82.2 vs. 70.4), while slightly trailing on Kuka DR (75.1 vs. 80.2) and Panda 3CAM-XK (76.0 vs. 77.6). In mean ADD, the method wins on 8 of 9 datasets, reducing error by up to 48% on Baxter DR (45.0 vs. 87.9 mm) and 45% on Panda 3CAM-AK (18.9 vs. 34.3 mm). The DREAM variants (F, Q, H) [18] serve as known-state references; they achieve comparable or higher AUC on some synthetic sets but degrade severely on real-world data (e.g., DREAM-F drops to 24.4 AUC on Panda 3CAM-XK), highlighting the fragility of PnP-based pipelines under occlusion. The critical practical advantage is the 12× inference speedup (44.3 ms vs. 570.8 ms) and 2.3× lower FLOPs (25.2 vs. 57.3 G) compared to RoboPose, enabling real-time deployment at 22.6 FPS versus 1.8 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the iterative render-and-compare paradigm of RoboPose [15] and the PnP-based pipeline of DREAM [18] by proposing a fully feed-forward modular architecture for holistic robot pose estimation with unknown joint states. It borrows the depth-from-intrinsics disentanglement strategy of Moon et al. [26] for its DepthNet module and adapts the self-supervised mask-consistency regularization of Lu et al. [22] for sim-to-real transfer. The key conceptual departure is treating 3D keypoint regression as an intermediary between pixel-space features and the abstract joint-parameter space, thereby decoupling the dual ambiguity of unknown joints and unknown 6D pose that plagues prior iterative methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving real-time (22.6 FPS) holistic robot pose estimation with unknown states, this work removes a practical barrier for multi-robot collaboration, human-robot interaction, and competitive robotics where joint telemetry is unavailable or untrusted. The modular decomposition (separating scale-variant depth from scale-invariant geometry) and the use of keypoints as a bridge to joint parameters offer a reusable design pattern for other articulated-object estimation problems. Limitations include evaluation on only three robot morphologies with 1-DoF joints, a single RGB modality, and reliance on the DREAM benchmark; open questions include scalability to higher-DoF or non-standard kinematic chains, multi-view or video-based estimation, and robustness to adversarial or heavily occluded real-world deployments.
