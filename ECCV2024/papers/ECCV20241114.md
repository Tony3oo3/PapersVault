## Problem

Lymph node detection in CT is extremely challenging because LNs exhibit low contrast and similar shapes/intensities to adjacent anatomies (vessels, muscles, esophagus), leading to high false positives and missed detections even for experienced radiologists. Existing automatic methods either focus on a single body region, detect only enlarged LNs (≥10 mm), or suffer from classification-localization misalignment in DETR-based detectors that produces duplicate and false-positive predictions near true LNs. No prior work provides a universal transformer-based detector covering multiple body parts and both enlarged and small LNs at scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D CT volumes normalized to 0.8×0.8×2 mm resolution and processes them via 2D slice-level feature extraction with 2.5D fusion across adjacent slices to recover volumetric context. It outputs 3D bounding boxes for lymph nodes (short axis ≥ 5 mm), per-query classification scores, IoU confidence predictions, and segmentation masks. During inference, the top 20 ranked queries are selected using the combined classification×IoU criterion, and 2D detections are merged into 3D boxes evaluated by 3D IoBB > 0.3 for true-positive assignment on FROC curves.

## Method

LN-DETR builds on Mask DINO's denoising training and multi-scale deformable attention, adding three key components. First, a multi-scale 2.5D feature fusion scheme feeds fused features from all four ResNet-50 blocks into the transformer encoder, incorporating 3D context while retaining ImageNet pre-trained 2D weights. Second, a location debiased query selection module appends an IoU prediction head (shared MLP) at the encoder output and all decoder layers; queries are ranked by the product of classification score and predicted IoU rather than classification alone, yielding more accurate anchor initialization for the decoder and better final prediction selection. Third, a query contrastive learning module at the last decoder layer uses InfoNCE loss to pull each matched positive query toward its multiple denoising anchor queries (from multi-group denoising) while pushing it away from all unmatched negative queries, explicitly improving discrimination between true LNs and visually similar adjacent structures. The total loss combines classification, box regression, mask, IoU prediction (λ=10), and contrastive (λ=1) terms, trained for 30 epochs on 1067 patients across 7 datasets spanning neck, chest, and abdomen.

## Results

On the internal LN detection test (5 datasets, 1067 patients total), LN-DETR achieves 56.27% average recall across 0.5–4 FPs/patient and 58.47% AP₁₀, outperforming the second-best method Mask DINO (51.32% avg recall, 54.72% AP₁₀) by 4.95% and 3.08% respectively. On the independent external test (2 datasets), LN-DETR reaches 52.04% average recall and 50.24% AP₁₀, surpassing MULAN (48.03%, 47.43%) by 4.01% and 2.81%. On the NIH DeepLesion universal lesion benchmark, LN-DETR attains 88.46% average recall at 0.5–4 FPs/image, matching or exceeding all prior methods (best prior: P3D* at 88.55% with MS-COCO pretraining; LN-DETR uses only ImageNet pretraining).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LN-DETR outperforms all 8 LN detection baselines on both internal and external tests. Against the closest transformer-based competitor Mask DINO [27], LN-DETR gains +4.95% avg recall internally and +4.01% externally, with the largest per-FP gain at 4 FPs/patient (+5.09% internal, +6.98% external), indicating better sensitivity at higher FP budgets. Compared to the best CNN-based medical detector MULAN [62], LN-DETR improves by +5.42% internal and +4.01% external avg recall; notably, MULAN generalizes better than Mask DINO externally (48.03% vs. 46.89%), but LN-DETR surpasses both. Instance segmentation methods (Mask2Former [10], MP-Former [68]) lag significantly, achieving only 47.69% and 44.45% internal avg recall, confirming that direct detection is more effective than segmentation-by-detection for small, scattered LNs. On DeepLesion, LN-DETR (88.46% avg recall) edges out DiffULD [71] (88.00%) and A3D+SATr [29,64] (87.92%), and is within 0.09% of P3D* [70] (88.55%), which benefits from MS-COCO pretraining while LN-DETR uses only ImageNet. LENS [60] shows the largest generalization drop (49.36%→40.35% internal→external), whereas LN-DETR's drop is comparatively modest (56.27%→52.04%), suggesting stronger cross-institution robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

LN-DETR is built directly on Mask DINO (CVPR 2023), inheriting its denoising training, multi-scale deformable attention, and mixed query selection, while adapting the 2.5D feature fusion scheme originally proposed in MULAN (MICCAI 2019) to incorporate 3D CT context without the cost of a full 3D transformer. The two key departures from the Mask DINO baseline are: (1) a location debiased query selection that jointly considers classification and IoU-based localization confidence when choosing decoder initialization queries (conceptually related to but distinct from Cascade-DETR's IoU-aware recalibration, which only operates at the final decoder layer), and (2) a query contrastive learning module using InfoNCE loss to sharpen the separation between true LN queries and visually similar false-positive queries. This is the first application of a DETR-family detector to the lymph node detection task, which had previously been addressed exclusively by CNN-based detectors (Mask R-CNN, nnDetection, LENS, MULAN) or segmentation-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically critical but technically difficult problem—detecting small, low-contrast, scattered lymph nodes across multiple body regions—and provides the largest multi-institutional LN CT dataset to date (1067 patients, 10,000+ annotations spanning neck, chest, and abdomen). The 4–5% average recall improvement over prior best methods is meaningful in a clinical setting where even a few missed metastatic nodes can alter staging and treatment. However, absolute recall remains moderate (~56% internal, ~52% external at 0.5–4 FPs/patient), and the 5 mm short-axis threshold still excludes the smallest potentially metastatic nodes. Open questions include whether the contrastive and debiasing components transfer to other small-object medical detection tasks, whether a full 3D DETR with sufficient compute could surpass the 2.5D approximation, and how the method performs on PET/CT or MRI modalities.
