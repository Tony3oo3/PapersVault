## Problem

Existing image retrieval methods for generative model auditing focus on semantic content matching, leaving style replication by text-to-image models largely unquantified. Artists and users lack a principled way to determine whether a generated image contains stylistic elements attributable to a specific artist's work in the training data, and no large-scale dataset with style labels exists to train such a detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241759/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a fixed-dimensional style descriptor embedding (projected from the final ViT layer). Style similarity between two images is computed as the dot product of their embeddings, yielding a scalar score interpretable as style similarity (scores below 0.5 suggest absence of an artist's style, above 0.8 suggest strong presence). The model operates in a zero-shot retrieval setting: given a query image, it retrieves the nearest style neighbors from a database, with no text prompt required at inference time.

## Method

CSD (Contrastive Style Descriptors) combines a multi-label contrastive loss (MCL) with a style-preserving self-supervised objective on a ViT backbone initialized from CLIP. The MCL computes pairwise cosine similarities over a batch and uses a multi-label indicator (1 if any style tag is shared) as the ground-truth similarity target, trained on the newly curated ContraStyles dataset of 511,921 images with 3,840 style tags. The SSL component deliberately excludes photometric augmentations (color jitter, Gaussian blur) that alter style, retaining only spatial transforms (horizontal/vertical flips, rotation, resize) so that the learned representation remains sensitive to stylistic attributes. The final loss is L = L_MCL + λ·L_SSL with λ=0.2 and temperature τ=0.1, fine-tuned for 80k iterations on 4 GPUs. This design ensures the embedding disentangles style from content, unlike standard SSL pipelines that treat style as a nuisance variable to be invariant to.

## Results

On the WikiArt style retrieval task (chance level 0.09%), CSD ViT-L achieves 64.56% mAP@1, outperforming the next best model (CLIP ViT-L at 59.4%) by over 5 points. On DomainNet, CSD ViT-L reaches 78.3% mAP@1, surpassing CLIP ViT-L (74.0%) by 4.3 points. The base model CSD ViT-B also leads all baselines, achieving 56.2% mAP@1 on WikiArt and 78.3% mAP@1 on DomainNet. In a human study on Artchive, untrained humans (chance 25%) perform worse than most feature extractors, while CSD outperforms all baselines including humans.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSD ViT-L dominates all six baseline families on both WikiArt and DomainNet. Against the strongest competitor CLIP ViT-L, CSD ViT-L gains 5.16 points on WikiArt mAP@1 (64.56 vs. 59.4) and 4.3 points on DomainNet mAP@1 (78.3 vs. 74.0). The dedicated style-attribution model GDA (all three variants) is substantially outperformed: CSD ViT-B beats GDA DINO ViT-B by 10.7 points on WikiArt mAP@1 (56.2 vs. 45.5) and 8.8 points on DomainNet (78.3 vs. 69.5). Classical VGG Gram matrices [16] and copy-detection descriptor SSCD [45] lag far behind, with WikiArt mAP@1 of only 25.9 and 36.0 respectively. Self-supervised models DINO and MoCo, trained with content-preserving augmentations, also underperform CSD by 9–12 points on WikiArt, confirming that standard SSL invariance to photometric changes is detrimental for style representation. On the human study (Artchive), CSD outperforms untrained humans and all feature extractors except SSCD, which is the only method that falls below human performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the self-supervised contrastive learning framework (SimCLR-style) by inverting the standard augmentation philosophy: where typical SSL treats style as a nuisance variable to be discarded via photometric augmentations (color jitter, blur, grayscale), CSD deliberately preserves style through spatial-only augmentations (flips, rotation, resize) and adds a multi-label contrastive loss aligned with human style labels from the new ContraStyles dataset. It departs from prior style retrieval methods (Gatys Gram matrices, Gairola et al., Matsuo et al.) that rely on hand-crafted or single-task features, and from the recent GDA attribution model [63] which trains on synthetic style pairs, by using real image pairs with multi-label style tags. The ViT backbone is initialized from CLIP checkpoints, positioning CSD as a style-specialized fine-tune of a general-purpose vision-language model rather than a from-scratch architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical utility is significant: artists and users can now quantitatively assess whether a generative model replicates a specific artist's style, and the paper demonstrates this by identifying artists (Ruan Jia, Wadim Kashin, Anton Fadeev, Justin Gerard, Amano) likely removed from Stable Diffusion 2.1's training data. The human study revealing that untrained humans perform worse than most feature extractors at style matching underscores both the difficulty of the task and the value of learned representations. Key limitations include the strictly artist-attribution-based definition of style (acknowledged as not a 'golden truth'), the modest dataset size (511K images) relative to the LAION source, and the preliminary nature of the multi-artist prompt analysis. Open questions include whether GSS thresholds generalize across model architectures and whether the style-preserving SSL objective transfers to video or 3D content.
