## Problem

Existing tuning-free image personalization methods face a fundamental trade-off: IP-Adapter injects reference image features without interacting with the target prompt, making texture modification difficult, while BLIP-Diffusion and similar approaches produce detail errors due to lengthy information paths. Domain-specific tuning-free models (InstantID, InstantBooth) cannot generalize beyond their trained class. The paper addresses how to simultaneously achieve high detail fidelity, identity preservation, and prompt faithfulness in a single-reference, tuning-free, open-vocabulary setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image and a free-form text prompt as input. At inference, SAM is used to extract the subject mask from the reference image, which is then replaced with a white background to isolate the object. The text prompt can specify either a new contextual environment (recontextualization) or a texture/material/style change for the subject. The output is a full-resolution personalized image generated by Stable Diffusion v1.5 in latent space, with the same subject identity preserved in the new context or with the modified texture.

## Method

MoMA combines two feature pathways injected into a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet via decoupled cross-attention layers. First, a multimodal generative image-feature decoder (adapted LLaVA-7B) takes the white-background reference image and target prompt, and generates a contextualized CLIP-space embedding that fuses subject identity with prompt semantics. Second, a self-attention shortcut forwards the white-background reference through the same UNet at t=0 to extract fine-grained pixel-level features, which are injected through a separate set of decoupled subject-cross-attention layers. A masking mechanism applies the reference mask inside the attention computation and the generated mask (iteratively approximated from cross-attention maps of the subject label) as an element-wise gate, preventing background interference. Training is two-stage: the MLLM is first pre-trained to predict CLIP image embeddings from the white-background image and caption, then the attention modules are trained with a standard diffusion objective on 282K OpenImage-V7 triplets while keeping both the MLLM and UNet frozen.

## Results

On the DreamBooth evaluation set, MoMA achieves the best scores across all four metrics: DINO 0.618, CLIP-T(c) 0.348, CLIP-I 0.803, and CLIP-T(t) 0.335. In human evaluation, MoMA leads on all four criteria (Subject Fidelity 0.550, Prompt Fidelity 0.633, Texture Editing 0.523, Overall Quality 0.596), substantially outperforming IP-Adapter (0.416, 0.351, 0.316, 0.368) and BLIP-Diffusion (0.034, 0.016, 0.161, 0.037). The largest margin over baselines appears in the texture-editing CLIP-T(t) metric, where MoMA scores 0.335 versus 0.319 for IP-Adapter and 0.271 for BLIP-Diffusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoMA outperforms all three tuning-free baselines on every reported metric. Against IP-Adapter, the closest competitor, MoMA gains +0.006 DINO, +0.018 CLIP-T(c), +0.010 CLIP-I, and +0.016 CLIP-T(t), with the most notable advantage in texture editing (CLIP-T(t): 0.335 vs. 0.319). Against BLIP-Diffusion, the margins are larger: +0.024 DINO, +0.048 CLIP-T(c), +0.024 CLIP-I, and +0.064 CLIP-T(t), reflecting BLIP-Diffusion's known detail errors in tuning-free settings. Textual Inversion and Re-Imagen, which require per-instance fine-tuning, trail further behind on prompt-following (CLIP-T(c) 0.255 and 0.270 respectively) and do not support texture editing. In human evaluation, MoMA's Prompt Fidelity score (0.633) is nearly double that of IP-Adapter (0.351) and roughly 40× that of BLIP-Diffusion (0.016), confirming that the MLLM-based contextualized feature generation effectively resolves the prompt-vs-image fidelity trade-off that plagues prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoMA builds directly on two pillars: LLaVA as a multimodal LLM backbone and IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention mechanism for injecting image features into a frozen diffusion UNet. It departs from prior tuning-free personalization methods (IP-Adapter, BLIP-Diffusion) by repurposing the MLLM not merely as a passive feature extractor but as a *generative* decoder that actively composes reference-image features with the target text prompt before injection. The self-attention shortcut with iterative masking is a novel architectural addition not present in any of the cited baselines, addressing the fine-grained detail loss that plagues purely cross-attention-based feature transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoMA offers a practical, tuning-free, open-vocabulary personalization pipeline that can be plugged into existing Stable Diffusion community checkpoints without retraining, lowering the barrier for subject-driven generation. However, the performance gains over IP-Adapter are modest (e.g., DINO 0.618 vs 0.612; CLIP-I 0.803 vs 0.793), and the method still depends on an external segmentation model (SAM) for masks at inference. Open questions include whether the 7B-parameter MLLM decoder can be distilled or replaced by lighter alternatives, whether the approach extends to multi-subject or video personalization, and whether the self-attention masking generalizes beyond single-object scenes.
