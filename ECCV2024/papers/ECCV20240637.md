## Problem

Existing top-down human mesh reconstruction methods (e.g., SMPL-based) rely on whole-body parametric models with entangled cross-part parameters, causing significant quality degradation when large portions of the body are outside the camera's field of view. This paper addresses the gap of accurately reconstructing visible body parts from images where only 1–4 body parts are visible, a scenario common in medical in-bore cameras, film production, and AR/VR applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240637/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image in which only a subset of body parts (1–4, with ≥50% of each part's bounding box area inside the frame) is visible. It outputs the 3D mesh and 3D joints for each visible body part independently, structured as 15 part-specific HPPM parameter sets (shape, 6D rotation, translation) rather than a single whole-body mesh. During inference, a post-processing fusion module optionally merges adjacent visible parts into a connected mesh using a topology-distance-weighted vertex blending in overlapping regions.

## Method

The core contribution is a bottom-up 'Divide and Fuse' pipeline built on Human Part Parametric Models (HPPM), which decompose the SMPL template into 15 nearly-rigid parts via blend-weight-based vertex grouping and dilation-induced overlaps. Each HPPM part is a linear PCA model (16–42 shape parameters, 360 total) plus a rigid transformation and a part-specific joint regressor, enabling independent reconstruction without inter-part dependency. A Swin Transformer backbone extracts image features that an MLP maps to per-part HPPM parameters, supervised by part-level vertex, 3D/2D joint, shape, rotation, and translation losses. A self-supervised fusion stage adds an overlapping-region alignment loss and a depth-consistency regularization to bring adjacent parts into spatial agreement, followed by a gradual weighted-sum connecting module at inference that blends overlapping vertices by topology distance to produce a seamless fused mesh.

## Results

On the finetuned PV-Human3.6M benchmark, D&F achieves 63.3 mm MPVE and 55.9 mm MPJPE, substantially outperforming the best baseline CycleAdapt (249.4 mm MPVE, 231.9 mm MPJPE). On PV-3DPW, D&F reaches 109.9 mm MPVE and 102.7 mm MPJPE versus CycleAdapt's 189.0 mm MPVE and 137.1 mm MPJPE. Even without finetuning (directly tested), D&F (155.7 mm MPVE on PV-H36M) already surpasses all baselines by over 170 mm in MPVE. The HPPM parametric model uses 360 total shape parameters across 15 body parts, with per-part fitting errors under 2 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D&F decisively outperforms all four baselines on both benchmarks under both directly-tested and finetuned settings. Against the strongest baseline CycleAdapt [30], D&F reduces MPVE by 186.1 mm on PV-Human3.6M (63.3 vs. 249.4) and by 79.1 mm on PV-3DPW (109.9 vs. 189.0) in the finetuned setting. GLoT [42] (CVPR 2023) is the second-strongest baseline, with D&F improving MPVE by 150.8 mm on PV-H36M and 125.1 mm on PV-3DPW. SEFD [50] (ICCV 2023) and MotionBERT [63] (ICCV 2023) show the weakest performance, with MPVE values above 196 mm even after finetuning, roughly 3× worse than D&F. Notably, the partially visible augmentation strategy also benefits the baselines (e.g., CycleAdapt drops from 367.0 to 249.4 mm MPVE on PV-H36M), but the gap to D&F remains large, confirming that the architectural divide-and-fuse design is the primary source of improvement rather than the data augmentation alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant top-down paradigm in human mesh reconstruction (SMPL, STAR-based methods) that regresses global body parameters from whole-body image features, arguing that parameter entanglement across body parts causes visible-part accuracy to collapse when large portions of the body are occluded. It draws conceptual inspiration from bottom-up human pose estimation (keypoint lifting) but applies the divide-and-conquer philosophy to mesh-level reconstruction via part-specific parametric models (HPPM) trained with PCA on SMPL ground truth. The fusion module with overlapping template regions and a gradual weighted-sum connecting strategy is a novel design element not present in prior part-based approaches like the Stitched Puppet [64], which was not learning-based and not designed for partially visible inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in AR/VR, medical imaging, and film production where only fragments of a human body are in view, and provides two new benchmarks (PV-Human3.6M, PV-3DPW) that the community can use to evaluate robustness under partial visibility. Key limitations include reliance on ground-truth visibility annotations at inference (no automatic part detection), limited expressiveness for hands and face (SMPL-X extension noted as future work), and the 15-part segmentation being fixed and SMPL-dependent. An open question is whether the HPPM framework generalizes beyond SMPL-derived templates to more expressive or non-rigid part models, and whether the approach scales to multi-person or video settings.
