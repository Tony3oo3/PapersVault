## Problem

ViT-based Class Activation Maps in weakly supervised semantic segmentation suffer from two persistent issues: imprecise localization along object boundaries due to the absence of spatial supervision, and false positive activations in object-unrelated regions. The paper identifies that these problems are linked to frequency-domain properties—phase carries shape information that the model underutilizes, and frequency shortcuts cause spurious widespread activation—yet no prior WSSS method leverages Fourier-domain structure to address them.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes 224×224 RGB images with image-level multi-label class labels and a DeiT-S backbone pre-trained on ImageNet. It outputs refined CAMs of shape C×N×N (where N is the number of patches per side), which after post-processing (PSA) serve as pseudo-labels to train a separate segmentation network (Deeplab-V1/ResNet38 for VOC, Deeplab-V2/ResNet101 for COCO). During training, the method additionally requires a random image pair for MPC and a per-class set of ~100 images for the FSPM measurement, updated every three epochs.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-module framework operating in the frequency domain. The Magnitude-mixing-based Phase Concentration (MPC) module, applied at ViT layer 8, takes an anchor image and an arbitrary image, mixes their magnitude spectra (with ratio r=0.3) while preserving the anchor's phase, producing two feature sets with identical phase but different magnitude; the model is then trained to classify both correctly and to produce L1-consistent CAMs, forcing reliance on phase-encoded shape information for boundary precision. The Frequency Shortcut Suppression (FSS) module first measures per-frequency classification influence via a masking-and-probing procedure to build a Frequency Shortcut Potential Map (FSPM), then synthesizes filtered images by attenuating the identified shortcut frequencies; the model is trained to classify the filtered image correctly and to minimize the L1 difference between CAMs from the original and filtered images, thereby suppressing shortcut-driven false positives. The total loss combines the standard classification loss with MPC and FSS losses (both weighted by λ=2).

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012, the proposed method achieves 73.2% mIoU on the val set and 73.0% on the test set (WRN38 backbone), setting new state-of-the-art for weakly supervised semantic segmentation. On MS COCO 2014, it reaches 45.7% mIoU on the val set (RN101 backbone), also surpassing all prior methods. For CAM quality on the VOC 2012 train set, the method produces seeds with 69.5% mIoU and pseudo-labels with 73.2% mIoU, both the best among compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 12 prior WSSS methods spanning CNN-based (AdvCAM, OC-CSE, ECS, CPN, AMR, ReCAM, SIPE, AEFT, ACR) and ViT-based (MCT, USAGE) approaches. On PASCAL VOC 2012 val, the proposed method (73.2%) outperforms the strongest prior methods—ACR (71.9%), USAGE (71.9%), and MCT (71.9%)—by 1.3 percentage points. On VOC test, it leads USAGE (72.8%) by 0.2 pp and ACR (71.9%) by 1.1 pp. On MS COCO 2014 val, it achieves 45.7% mIoU, surpassing ACR (45.3%), AEFT (44.8%), and USAGE with RN101 (44.3%), confirming that the frequency-domain shortcut suppression is particularly effective at reducing the class-overlapping false positives that typically limit ViT-based methods on COCO. The CAM seed quality (69.5% mIoU in Table 4) also exceeds the best prior seed (USAGE at 67.7%), indicating the gains originate from improved localization rather than post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ViT-based WSSS methods (MCTformer, USAGE) that extract Class Activation Maps from Vision Transformers, and departs from them by introducing frequency-domain operations into the CAM generation pipeline. It draws on two distinct research threads: (1) the well-established observation that the Fourier phase spectrum encodes shape and high-level semantics while magnitude encodes texture/style (FDA, domain generalization work), and (2) the recent finding that classification networks exploit specific frequency components as shortcuts (Wang et al., ICCV 2023). The key conceptual departure is the first application of DFT-based magnitude/phase manipulation to simultaneously improve CAM boundary precision (via phase concentration) and suppress false-positive activations (via frequency shortcut suppression) in a ViT WSSS framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two persistent and practically important failure modes of ViT-based CAMs—imprecise boundaries and object-unrelated false activations—through a unified frequency-domain lens, achieving new SOTA on both PASCAL VOC 2012 and MS COCO 2014. The empirical demonstration that specific frequency shortcuts directly cause spatial false positives in CAMs (Tab. 3) provides a mechanistic explanation that could inform future regularization strategies beyond WSSS. Limitations include the reliance on a fixed DeiT-S backbone, the additional computational cost of the FIM process for FSPM computation (100 images per class, updated every 3 epochs), and the fact that the method is tightly coupled to the multi-stage WSSS pipeline. Open questions include whether the frequency-shortcut-to-false-positive relationship generalizes to other architectures (e.g., Swin, ConvNeXt) and whether the approach transfers to other weak supervision signals like scribbles or bounding boxes.
