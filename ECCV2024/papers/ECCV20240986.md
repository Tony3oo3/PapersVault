## Problem

In extremely low-light conditions, over 90% of raw image pixels carry near-zero intensities contaminated by sensor noise, making it difficult for NeRF-based methods to recover geometry and texture in dark regions. RawNeRF, which reconstructs scenes from 16-bit raw images, still blurs boundary details in dark areas and fails to converge on fast models with explicit representations (iNGP, 3DGS) because tiny MLPs cannot regress the highly imbalanced intensity distribution. The paper addresses this by introducing thermal imaging as a complementary modality that is inherently robust to illumination variation and can constrain scene geometry where visible signals are unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes short-exposure (1/64 s) 16-bit visible raw images and thermal images (FLIR VUE Pro, 12 ms fixed exposure, 8–14 μm) captured from multiple calibrated viewpoints, with camera poses estimated via COLMAP on scaled raw or fused images. It simultaneously renders linear-space raw visible images and raw thermal images at arbitrary novel viewpoints; the visible output retains full 16-bit dynamic range so that exposure, gamma, and tonemapping can be applied post-hoc, while the thermal output supports 3D localization of warm objects (humans, vehicles, fire). The MVTV dataset provides 20 night scenes: 10 with long-exposure ground truth for evaluation and 10 for thermal application studies.

## Method

Thermal-NeRF combines three mechanisms. First, a Thermal Enhancement head predicts thermal radiance through the same volume-rendering equation, providing a geometry-constraining supervision signal and a thermal-regularized balanced loss (stop-gradient on thermal prediction) that emphasizes foreground objects over empty dark regions. Second, Retinex3D extends the 2D Retinex decomposition to 3D by rendering view-dependent reflectance and illumination fields separately via volume rendering and multiplying them in image space, which smooths noise in dark regions during accumulation. Third, a Multi-Exposure strategy attaches multiple tiny MLP heads that predict different intensity scales (amplification coefficients α_i = 10 or 100), each supervised by exposure-scaled versions of the training image, and composites their outputs recursively through a smoothing sign function to avoid saturation artifacts. The full pipeline is implemented on both Mip-NeRF (2 heads, ~22 h training) and Instant-NGP (4 heads, ~10 min training), and transfers seamlessly to 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time rendering.

## Results

On the MVTV dataset (VS part, 8 night scenes), Thermal-NeRF (Mip-NeRF) achieves the best PSNR across all scenes, ranging from 21.60 dB (Shriver) to 31.68 dB (Road), consistently outperforming RawNeRF (Mip-NeRF) by 0.2–1.7 dB. The fast iNGP-based variant Thermal-NeRF† achieves PSNR of 20.16–29.78 dB with lower LPIPS (better perceptual quality) than RawNeRF†, while training in ~10 minutes on a single RTX 3090 versus ~22 hours for RawNeRF (Mip-NeRF). For thermal view synthesis, adding visible raw supervision improves PSNR from 29.75 to 31.79 dB (VS) and from 31.74 to 33.06 dB (TS) over thermal-only reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RawNeRF (Mildenhall et al., CVPR 2022), evaluated in both Mip-NeRF and iNGP implementations. Thermal-NeRF (Mip-NeRF) wins on all 8 scenes for PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the largest gains on darker scenes: +0.94 dB PSNR on Gilman (25.35 vs 24.41) and +1.74 dB on Road (31.68 vs 30.63). The iNGP-based Thermal-NeRF† also dominates RawNeRF† across all scenes (e.g., +7.72 dB on Gilman, +7.38 dB on Shriver), and adding only thermal supervision to RawNeRF† (RawNeRF†+T) recovers part of the gap but still trails Thermal-NeRF† by 2–5 dB, confirming that the multi-exposure strategy is critical. Notably, Thermal-NeRF† (iNGP, ~10 min training) achieves PSNR within 0.5–1.5 dB of RawNeRF (Mip-NeRF, ~22 h training) while producing lower LPIPS, indicating better perceptual quality at 130× faster training. The paper also reports that RawNeRF on iNGP without thermal enhancement often fails to converge to valid geometry, a stability issue that Thermal-NeRF resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RawNeRF (Mildenhall et al., CVPR 2022), which reconstructs scenes from linear raw images to preserve dark-region information lost by camera ISPs, by introducing a thermal imaging modality as a geometric constraint and a multi-exposure strategy with multiple illumination prediction heads. It departs from sRGB-based low-light NeRF methods (LLNeRF, Aleth-NeRF) that operate on lossy 8-bit images, and from prior multimodal NeRF efforts (Zhu et al., Poggi et al.) that addressed alignment of unpaired multi-sensor data, by establishing strictly aligned thermal-visible pairs and demonstrating mutual benefit between modalities. The Retinex decomposition is generalized from 2D image enhancement to a view-dependent 3D formulation (Retinex3D), and the entire pipeline is shown to transfer to explicit representations (iNGP, 3DGS) where RawNeRF's single-head strategy fails to converge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MVTV dataset is the first aligned multi-view thermal-visible benchmark for 3D reconstruction, opening a research direction for multimodal NeRF that was previously blocked by the absence of suitable data. The finding that thermal supervision is critical for stabilizing geometry in explicit/hybrid models (where RawNeRF simply fails to converge) has practical implications for real-time applications in rescue, surveillance, and autonomous systems operating at night. Limitations include the small dataset (20 scenes, single thermal camera), evaluation limited to RawNeRF as the primary baseline, and the reliance on long-exposure ground truth for evaluation that may not reflect real deployment conditions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other complementary modalities (NIR, LiDAR), scales to larger scenes, and whether the multi-exposure strategy can be replaced by learned adaptive exposure.
