## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation methods rely on CLIP-based contrastive models that struggle with fine-grained classes and compositional text queries. While text-to-image diffusion models offer superior local representations and compositional understanding, their generatively-trained features cannot be directly distilled to 3D, and mask-based segmentation heads do not provide pixel-level features for standard point-wise distillation. This paper is the first to bridge this gap by adapting diffusion model representations for open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multiview RGB images and a reconstructed 3D point cloud (quantized into voxels for the 3D branch), with text prompts supplied at inference time for querying. The 2D branch (ODISE: frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net + Mask2Former head with 100 queries) produces N=100 2D probabilistic masks and their semantically rich mask embeddings. The 3D branch (MinkowskiNet18A) outputs a per-voxel feature map, which is combined with the 2D mask embeddings to yield per-point class probabilities; the final output is a 3D segmentation mask where each point receives the highest-probability label from the ensembled salient and geometric masks.

## Method

Diff2Scene introduces a novel mask-based distillation strategy that decouples semantic content (from 2D mask embeddings) from geometric accuracy (from 3D mask prediction). The 2D branch uses ODISE with a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone to generate N 2D probabilistic masks and their mask embeddings, which serve as fixed linear classifiers over 3D features. The 3D branch (MinkowskiNet) predicts per-voxel features; inner products with the 2D mask embeddings yield 3D probabilistic masks, and a cosine-similarity distillation loss forces these to match the 2D masks lifted to 3D via pixel-point correspondence. At inference, per-mask category logits are computed as the geometric mean of diffusion and CLIP discriminative scores, then the final per-point probability is a weighted ensemble (λ=0.5) of 2D salient masks and 3D geometric masks, enabling the model to exploit both appearance cues and geometric structure without any labeled 3D data.

## Results

Diff2Scene achieves mIoU of 48.6 on ScanNet, 45.5 on Matterport3D, 14.2 on ScanNet200 (All), and 17.5 on Replica (All) in the zero-shot open-vocabulary setting, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art OpenScene (2D/3D) on all four benchmarks. On ScanNet200, the improvement over OpenScene (2D/3D) is 2.6 mIoU points (14.2 vs. 11.6), with particularly strong gains on head categories (25.6 vs. 20.0) and tail categories (6.9 vs. 5.1). On the unseen Replica dataset, Diff2Scene achieves 46.2 mIoU on head classes, substantially exceeding OpenScene's 34.2, demonstrating strong generalization. The gap to fully-supervised methods narrows on tail categories (e.g., 6.9 vs. 7.9 for CSC-Pretrain on ScanNet200 tail), highlighting the method's strength on rare classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary state-of-the-art baseline OpenScene (2D/3D) [62], Diff2Scene wins on every dataset: +1.1 mIoU on ScanNet (48.6 vs. 47.5), +2.9 on Matterport3D (45.5 vs. 42.6), +2.6 on ScanNet200 All (14.2 vs. 11.6), and +2.6 on Replica All (17.5 vs. 14.9). The largest relative gain appears on ScanNet200 head categories (25.6 vs. 20.0, a 28% relative improvement) and on Replica head (46.2 vs. 34.2, a 35% relative improvement), indicating that diffusion features are particularly effective for common and previously-seen object categories. Against earlier open-vocabulary methods ConceptFusion [37] and OpenMask3D [79], Diff2Scene achieves substantially higher mIoU across all reported benchmarks (e.g., 14.2 vs. 8.8 and 10.5 on ScanNet200 All). Compared to fully-supervised methods, Diff2Scene (zero-shot) still trails on overall mIoU (e.g., 14.2 vs. 27.2 for LGround on ScanNet200), but the gap narrows considerably on tail categories (6.9 vs. 7.9 for CSC-Pretrain), suggesting the diffusion-based approach is competitive precisely where supervised methods struggle most due to data scarcity. On the qualitative Nr3D visual grounding task, Diff2Scene handles compositional queries (shape, color, relative location, usage) that OpenScene fails to resolve correctly.

## Relation to Prior Work

Diff2Scene extends the zero-shot 2D-to-3D distillation framework introduced by OpenScene [62], which lifted CLIP-based 2D features into 3D via point-wise feature matching. The key departure is replacing the CLIP backbone with a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net (via ODISE [87]) and switching from point-based to mask-based distillation, where 2D mask embeddings serve as fixed linear classifiers over 3D MinkowskiNet features. This design is motivated by the observation that diffusion features are superior for local/dense prediction but are not directly usable for per-point supervision, necessitating the mask-level consistency loss. The mask-based paradigm from Mask2Former [10] is repurposed as the structural bridge between the generative 2D teacher and the geometric 3D student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that frozen text-to-image diffusion representations can be effectively transferred to 3D dense prediction without any 3D labels, opening a path to leverage the rapidly improving generative model ecosystem for 3D scene understanding. The 12% mIoU gain on ScanNet200 and strong generalization to Replica suggest diffusion features carry complementary semantic information beyond what CLIP provides, particularly for rare and compositional queries. However, the method still struggles with small objects and fine-grained category confusion (e.g., windowsill vs. window), and the reliance on a fixed 50/50 ensemble of salient and geometric masks leaves room for adaptive fusion. An open question is whether the mask distillation recipe generalizes to other generative backbones (e.g., DiT-based models) as the diffusion landscape evolves.
