## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models struggle to faithfully generate scenes with multiple semantically or visually similar subjects, producing failures like incorrect attribute binding, subject fusion, and catastrophic neglect. The paper identifies the root cause as semantic leakage in attention layers, where cross-attention blends token embeddings and self-attention blends visual features across subjects. Existing layout-to-image methods, both trained and training-free, fail to adequately mitigate this leakage, particularly when subjects share semantic similarity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a global text prompt containing n distinct subjects, the individual subject token identifiers, and their corresponding coarse bounding boxes as layout specification. It produces a single RGB image where each subject is localized to its bounding box with correct semantics and appearance. The method is entirely training-free and operates on pre-trained Stable Diffusion and SDXL latent diffusion models, requiring no fine-tuning or additional training data.

## Method

Bounded Attention modifies the attention computation during denoising by adding binary masks (zeros and −∞) to the attention logits, blocking information flow between different subjects' queries and keys in both cross-attention and self-attention layers. It operates in two phases: a Bounded Guidance phase at early timesteps that backpropagates a loss encouraging each subject's attention to concentrate within its bounding box (with opposing subjects' keys masked to avoid pushing queries apart), followed by a Bounded Denoising phase applied throughout all remaining timesteps that confines each subject's attention to its own region plus background. After the guidance phase, coarse bounding boxes are refined into fine segmentation masks via self-attention map clustering, which are periodically updated to track evolving subject boundaries. The key insight is that both cross-attention (semantic leakage) and self-attention (visual/structural leakage) must be bounded simultaneously, and that the leakage originates from different UNet depths depending on whether subjects share semantic or visual similarity.

## Results

On the DrawBench dataset, Bounded Attention achieves the highest Counting Recall (0.91) and ties with BoxDiff on Counting Precision (0.81) and F1 (0.76), substantially outperforming MultiDiffusion (F1 0.57) and vanilla Stable Diffusion (F1 0.73). However, its Spatial Accuracy (0.19) is lower than Layout-guidance (0.35) and BoxDiff (0.28), indicating that the method prioritizes correct subject identity and count over precise spatial placement. Qualitatively, the method consistently generates the correct number of semantically similar subjects (e.g., five kittens with mixed adjectives) where all baselines fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three training-free methods (Layout-guidance, BoxDiff, MultiDiffusion) and three trained methods (GLIGEN, ReCo, AttentionRefocusing). On counting metrics, Bounded Attention achieves the best Recall (0.91 vs. 0.78 for all other methods) and ties BoxDiff on Precision and F1 (0.81/0.76), while MultiDiffusion lags significantly (F1 0.57). On spatial accuracy, Layout-guidance leads (0.35) and Bounded Attention trails (0.19), suggesting the method trades some positional precision for superior semantic fidelity. Qualitatively, all training-free baselines exhibit subject fusion, catastrophic neglect, or incorrect attribute binding, while trained methods (GLIGEN, ReCo) follow layouts but leak attributes between similar subjects (e.g., denim texture bleeding into a corduroy jacket, elephant features merging into a rhino). Bounded Attention is the only method that consistently generates all requested subjects with correct individual attributes across all six sampled seeds in the non-curated comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the growing family of training-free layout-to-image methods (Layout Guidance, BoxDiff, MultiDiffusion) and trained layout-conditioned models (GLIGEN, ReCo), arguing that all of them share a blind spot: they do not address the root cause of multi-subject failures, which the authors diagnose as semantic leakage in both cross- and self-attention layers. Rather than modifying the optimization objective (as Layout Guidance does) or generating subjects in separate denoising passes (as MultiDiffusion does), Bounded Attention intervenes directly in the attention computation itself by masking irrelevant key-value pairs, a mechanism that is applied throughout the entire denoising schedule. The paper also departs from prior work by showing that enforcing query separation through latent optimization is actively harmful (pushing signals out of distribution), a finding that reframes how the community should think about attention-based guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why multi-subject generation fails in diffusion models and a practical, training-free fix that practitioners can adopt immediately on Stable Diffusion and SDXL. The finding that both cross- and self-attention must be bounded simultaneously, and that visual (not just semantic) similarity drives leakage in outer UNet layers, offers actionable diagnostic tools for future work. Key limitations remain: residual leakage persists due to imperfect guidance optimization and coarse initial segmentation, and performance is sensitive to seed-layout alignment, leaving open the question of how to automatically generate layout-compatible seeds. The method's reliance on bounding boxes as input also limits its applicability to settings where precise spatial control is unavailable.
