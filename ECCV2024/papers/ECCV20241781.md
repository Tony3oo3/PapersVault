## Problem

Polarization cues have been widely exploited for shape estimation, BRDF recovery, and reflection removal, but their potential for subsurface scattering (SSS) parameter estimation has never been explored. Translucent objects simultaneously exhibit specular surface reflection and diffuse SSS, creating a diffuse/specular polarization ambiguity that makes joint shape and SSS recovery an ill-posed problem even under homogeneous assumptions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241781/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four linearly polarized images (at 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) and a binary object mask as input, from which it derives Stokes vectors and computes max/min polarization images that are concatenated as additional input channels. Outputs include a normal map and depth map for shape, third-order spherical harmonics (9 coefficients per RGB channel) plus a scalar flashlight intensity for illumination, and three SSS parameters: a 3-channel extinction coefficient, a 3-channel volumetric albedo, and a scalar Henyey-Greenstein phase parameter. During training, the reconstruction network additionally consumes four pure-BSDF polarized images (rendered without SSS) that are not available at inference time.

## Method

The architecture is a stage-wise CNN: a UNet-like backbone first predicts shape (normal, depth) and illumination, and a ResNet-like SSS network then estimates scattering parameters conditioned on those intermediate predictions to reduce ambiguity. Two key design choices distinguish the approach: (1) max & min polarization images are introduced as explicit input cues that encode the specular-to-diffuse polarization ratio, and (2) a novel reconstruction network takes the estimated SSS parameters together with pure-BSDF images and reconstructs the original four polarized images, providing high-dimensional image-space supervision that bypasses the absence of a differentiable polarized SSS renderer. The model is trained end-to-end in two stages on a newly constructed 117K-scene synthetic dataset rendered with Mitsuba 2, combining ShapeNet geometry, bump maps, Laval Indoor HDR environment maps, and randomized homogeneous SSS parameters.

## Results

On the 17K-scene synthetic test set, the full method (4 Pol + MaxMin + Reconstruction) achieves a normal MAE of 0.0651, depth MAE of 0.0473, and SSS parameter MAEs of σ_t = 0.1340, α = 0.0918, and g = 0.1554. Compared to the pure SSS baseline (Che et al.), the method reduces σ_t error by 21.8%, α error by 10.5%, and g error by 22.9%. For shape estimation, the method matches SfPW (0.0652) and outperforms Deep SfP (0.0831) on normal MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SfP baselines, the method achieves a normal MAE of 0.0651, essentially matching SfPW (0.0652) and improving over Deep SfP (0.0831) by 21.8%. The paper notes that SfPW tends to produce overly planar normals while Deep SfP results are noisy, whereas the proposed method yields high-quality normals on both synthetic and real data. Against the inverse rendering baseline Che et al. [9] (a pure SSS method), the proposed method substantially outperforms on all three SSS parameters: σ_t (0.1340 vs. 0.1714), α (0.0918 vs. 0.1026), and g (0.1554 vs. 0.2015), with improvements of roughly 10–23%. The ablation rows further show that adding polarization cues (4 Pol vs. w/o Pol) reduces normal MAE from 0.0750 to 0.0657 and SSS errors by 4–10%, while the max & min representation and reconstruction network provide incremental but consistent gains on SSS parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the stage-wise inverse rendering architecture of Boss et al. [7] (shape/illumination first, then material) and the UNet-based SfP pipeline of Lei et al. [38] to the joint estimation of shape and subsurface scattering parameters, using polarization cues as the primary signal. It departs from prior polarization-based inverse rendering work (e.g., Deschaintre et al. [16], Baek et al. [5]) that targets only surface BRDF, and from pure SSS estimation (Che et al. [9]) that ignores surface reflection, by modeling both specular and diffuse polarization simultaneously. A key architectural departure is replacing the standard differentiable renderer with a learned reconstruction network, motivated by the absence of a differentiable polarized SSS+BSDF renderer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale synthetic dataset (117K scenes) of polarized translucent objects, which is a practical resource for the community working on inverse rendering of participating media. However, several strong assumptions limit applicability: smooth surfaces (excluding rough translucent materials), homogeneous SSS, constant IoR, and sufficient photon travel distance (excluding nearly transparent objects). Open questions include whether the max & min polarization representation generalizes to spatially-varying SSS, whether the reconstruction network can be replaced once differentiable polarized SSS renderers mature, and how the method performs on rough or heterogeneous translucent materials where diffuse polarization from microfacet bounces confounds the SSS signal.
