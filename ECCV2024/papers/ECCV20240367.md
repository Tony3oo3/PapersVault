## Problem

Few-step diffusion models (1–8 denoising steps) drastically cut inference time but still consume ~9.7 GB of GPU memory in FP16, blocking mobile deployment. Existing post-training quantization methods designed for multi-step diffusion fail on few-step models because they apply uniform bit-widths despite a 'long-tail' layer-sensitivity distribution, and they optimize only for image quality while ignoring text-image alignment (content preservation).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240367/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the UNet of SDXL-turbo (1-step) or LCM-LoRA (4-step) text-to-image pipelines, taking a natural-language prompt as input and producing a 512×512 RGB image. Quantization uses asymmetric min-max scaling shared per tensor (activations) or per output channel (weights), with 1024 COCO prompts for calibration. The BOS token's pre-computed floating-point features (640–1280 elements per cross-attention layer) are stored alongside the quantized weights, introducing negligible overhead.

## Method

MixDQ combines three techniques. First, BOS-aware quantization identifies that the first CLIP token (BOS) has a magnitude ~50× larger than other tokens, causing 8-bit quantization to collapse most values to zero; since this token is identical across prompts, its floating-point output through the 'to_k/v' cross-attention layers is pre-computed offline and concatenated with dequantized features of remaining tokens. Second, metric-decoupled sensitivity analysis splits layers into content-related (cross-attention, FFN) and quality-related (self-attention, convolution) groups, evaluating the former with SSIM and the latter with SQNR to prevent SQNR's bias toward content changes from starving quality layers of bit-width. Third, an integer-programming formulation (solved via OR-tools) assigns each layer a bit-width from {2, 4, 8} to maximize total sensitivity score under a memory budget, solved separately for weights and activations within each layer group.

## Results

On the 1-step SDXL-turbo text-to-image task (COCO 512×512), MixDQ achieves W4A8 quantization with only a 0.5 FID increase (17.68 vs. 17.15 FP16) and a 2.5×10⁻³ CLIP Score drop, while baselines (Naive PTQ, Q-Diffusion) fail catastrophically at W8A8 (FID 103.96 and 76.18, respectively). At W3.66A16, MixDQ incurs just 0.25 FID increase while delivering 4.4× model size reduction. On hardware, MixDQ W8A8 achieves 1.52× UNet inference speedup and 1.87× peak memory reduction over FP16 on an RTX 4080.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Naive PTQ (uniform asymmetric min-max quantization) and Q-Diffusion (which adds Adaround rounding and shortcut-splitting). At W8A8 on 1-step SDXL-turbo, both baselines fail severely—Naive PTQ yields FID 103.96 and Q-Diffusion yields FID 76.18—while MixDQ achieves FID 17.03, essentially matching the FP16 baseline (17.15). At W4A16, Q-Diffusion (FID 22.58) is the only baseline that partially survives, but MixDQ (FID 17.23) still outperforms it by 5.35 FID and maintains a higher CLIP Score (0.2693 vs. 0.2685). At W4A8, both baselines produce unreadable images (FID > 100, negative ImageReward), whereas MixDQ retains FID 17.68 and CLIP 0.2698. On the 4-step LCM-LoRA model, the gap narrows (Q-Diffusion W8A8: FID 23.92 vs. MixDQ: 22.54), but MixDQ still wins on all three metrics at every bit-width tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of post-training quantization work for diffusion models (PTQD, Q-DM, Q-Diffusion) by targeting the underexplored regime of few-step (1–8 step) distilled models, where the absence of iterative denoising removes the natural error-correction mechanism that makes multi-step models more quantization-tolerant. It adopts the shortcut-splitting quantization technique from Q-Diffusion [11] and the integer-programming formulation for mixed-precision allocation from LLM-MQ [10], but departs from both by introducing a BOS-aware outlier handling for CLIP text embeddings and a metric-decoupled sensitivity analysis that separates content preservation (SSIM on cross-attention/FFN layers) from quality preservation (SQNR on self-attention/conv layers). The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion quantization is the explicit recognition that uniform bit-width allocation, guided by a single sensitivity metric, fails because content- and quality-related layers compete unfairly under SQNR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving W4A8 quantization with only ~0.5 FID degradation on SDXL-turbo (where baselines produce unreadable images with FID > 100) translates to 3–4× memory reduction and 1.5× latency speedup, making few-step text-to-image generation feasible on 8 GB GPUs and potentially mobile devices. The metric-decoupling insight—that a single sensitivity metric conflates content and quality effects and systematically misallocates bit-widths—is a generalizable diagnostic for any generative model compression task. Limitations include evaluation on only two model families (SDXL-turbo, LCM-LoRA) at 512×512 resolution, the BOS-aware trick being specific to CLIP-based text encoders, and the integer programming search being per-model rather than transferable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion, larger resolutions, or architectures without a separate text encoder.
