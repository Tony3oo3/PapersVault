## Problem

Real-world text-to-image diffusion models face constantly shifting data: new domains are added, usage rights expire, and privacy constraints demand removal of specific samples. Retraining a monolithic model on every data change is prohibitively expensive, while existing compartmentalization approaches (ensembling, mixture-of-experts) scale inference cost linearly with the number of specialist models. The paper asks whether information from models trained on different data shards can be merged into a single model with zero additional memory or inference overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes n pre-fine-tuned diffusion model weight vectors (each derived from a shared pretrained checkpoint such as SD2.1 and fine-tuned on a distinct data shard) and produces a single merged weight vector via weighted averaging. At inference, the merged model accepts a standard text prompt and generates an RGB image at the same resolution and with the same sampling procedure as any single diffusion model, incurring no extra compute. The weighting coefficients k_i (summing to 1) are the only tunable parameters and can be set uniformly or optimized greedily on a validation set.

## Method

Diffusion Soup fine-tunes separate diffusion models on disjoint data shards from a common pretrained checkpoint, then averages their weights to form a single model. The key theoretical insight is that gradient-descent fine-tuning makes the network approximately linear in weight space, so a first-order Taylor expansion (Eq. 4–5) shows that weight averaging provably approximates output ensembling. Proposition 1 further establishes that uniform averaging (k_i = 1/n) causes the souped model to sample from the geometric mean of the constituent data distributions, which acts as an implicit regularizer that counters the overfitting typical of diffusion fine-tuning—explaining why the soup outperforms a paragon model trained on the union of all shards. Proposition 2 connects this geometric-mean sampling to the near-access-freeness (ε-NAF) framework, yielding anti-memorization guarantees. Unlearning is achieved by weighted subtraction of a shard's contribution, and zero-shot style mixing emerges naturally from the geometric-mean sampling of stylistically distinct shards.

## Results

On domain-sharded data (8 categories), Diffusion Soup (uniform) achieves TIFA 86.2, Image Reward 0.45, and CLIP 0.260, outperforming the paragon model trained on the union (TIFA 85.5, IR 0.34, CLIP 0.257) and base SD2.1 (TIFA 85.5, IR 0.30, CLIP 0.259). The Greedy Soup variant further pushes TIFA to 86.5. On aesthetic data (5 shards), the uniform soup reaches TIFA 86.8, IR 0.59, and CLIP 0.263, a 59% improvement in IR over the combined paragon (0.37) and a 97% improvement over base SD2.1 (0.30). Removing any single domain shard degrades IR by at most 1% (0.45 → 0.44), demonstrating robust unlearning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is Stable Diffusion 2.1 (SD2.1) [Rombach et al., CVPR 2022], used as the pretrained checkpoint for all fine-tuning. Diffusion Soup wins decisively on all three metrics: TIFA improves from 85.5 to 86.2–86.5, IR from 0.30 to 0.44–0.45, and CLIP from 0.259 to 0.260. The internal baseline is the 'Combined' paragon model trained on the union of all data shards, which the paper argues is the natural monolithic alternative. Diffusion Soup outperforms this paragon on every metric in both experimental settings: on domain data, IR improves 30% (0.34 → 0.44) and TIFA improves by 1.0 point (85.5 → 86.5); on aesthetic data, IR improves 59% (0.37 → 0.59) and TIFA improves by 1.2 points (85.6 → 86.8). Notably, the paragon model actually underperforms base SD2.1 on IR in the aesthetic setting (0.37 vs. 0.30 is an improvement, but the combined model degrades relative to individual specialists as more shards are added cumulatively), while Diffusion Soup monotonically improves with each additional shard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model souping paradigm (Wortsman et al., 2022) from discriminative models to text-to-image diffusion models, leveraging the Taylor-series linearization of fine-tuning dynamics (Liu & Soatto, 2023) to show that weight averaging approximates output ensembling. It departs from prior compositional diffusion work (Golatkar et al., 2023; Balaji et al., 2022) that merges model outputs at inference time by instead merging weights, eliminating the O(n) inference overhead. The anti-memorization analysis builds directly on the near-access freeness framework of Vyas et al. (2023), showing that the geometric-mean sampling property of souping provides ε-NAF guarantees without explicit algorithmic intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diffusion Soup offers a trivially implementable (average the weights) solution to three practically important problems—continual learning, machine unlearning, and anti-memorization—without any additional inference or memory cost, making it immediately deployable in production pipelines where data provenance and usage rights are dynamic. The geometric-mean interpretation provides a clean theoretical lens for understanding why simple averaging outperforms monolithic training, and the zero-shot style mixing capability opens a new axis of controllability. Key limitations include the need to train n separate models (no training savings), the requirement for a shared pretrained checkpoint, and the fact that averaging must be global rather than per-sample. Whether the geometric-mean property and its benefits transfer to other generative architectures (e.g., autoregressive image models, video diffusion) or to language modeling remains an open question the authors flag for future work.
