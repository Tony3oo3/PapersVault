## Problem

Locating a few-second moment within a 20–120 minute video from a text query (Long Video Temporal Grounding) is far harder than short-video grounding, yet existing methods rely on a disjoint two-stage pipeline where a generic video-retrieval module selects a clip and a separate grounding network localizes the moment. The paper empirically shows that this disjoint retrieval lacks the fine-grained event understanding needed for moment detection and is the primary bottleneck for poor LVTG performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240556/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed long video (average 110 min on MAD, 8.25 min on Ego4D) and a natural-language query. The video is sliced into overlapping clips via a sliding window (180 s / 48 s with half-overlap stride for MAD / Ego4D), and frozen CLIP or EgoVLP extract per-frame visual features while CLIP encodes the query. The network outputs the start and end timestamps of the target moment by first retrieving the single most relevant clip and then regressing the boundary within that clip using learnable anchor queries.

## Method

RGNet replaces the disjoint retrieval-then-grounding pipeline with a single end-to-end transformer. Its core, the RG-Encoder, first applies cross-attention to fuse each clip with the text query, then a Gumbel-softmax sparsifier classifies frames as in-moment or out-of-moment to build a sparse attention mask. A retrieval attention layer, seeded with a learnable retrieval token, aggregates the masked frame features into a clip-level context vector and frame-level content features; the context vector is scored for clip selection while the fused features are passed to a lightweight grounding decoder (2 cross-attention + 2 self-attention layers with DAB-DETR-style anchor queries) that predicts the moment boundary. Training combines three losses: an intra-clip attention margin loss that supervises the sparsifier, an inter-clip InfoNCE contrastive loss with negative-clip mining to simulate the many-distractor retrieval setting of long videos, and a standard L1 + gIoU + cross-entropy grounding loss with Hungarian matching.

## Results

RGNet achieves state-of-the-art performance on both LVTG benchmarks. On Ego4D-NLQ (with NaQ pre-training), it reaches R1.3=20.63 and R5.3=41.67, improving the average of these two primary metrics by 9.7% over the previous best (ReLeR). On MAD, it attains R1.1=12.43 and R5.1=25.12, surpassing the prior best (SOONet) by 1.2% and 1.9% respectively. The overall average score across both datasets is 19.33, compared to 14.34 for CONE and 12.45 for SOONet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGNet outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every metric where they overlap. On Ego4D-NLQ, it surpasses the previous best ReLeR [39] (R1.3=19.31, R5.3=23.62) by 1.32% on R1.3 and a substantial 18.1% on R5.3, and beats CONE [16] (R1.3=14.15, R5.3=30.33) by 6.48% and 11.34% respectively. On MAD, it exceeds the prior best SOONet [37] (R1.1=11.26, R5.1=23.21, R1.3=9.00) by 1.2%, 1.9%, and 0.48% on the respective top-1 metrics, and also outperforms M-Guide [2] (R1.1=9.30) by 3.13% on R1.1. The default network (without NaQ pre-training) already surpasses CONE [16] by 4.13% on Ego4D R1.3 and by 0.61% on MAD R1.1, demonstrating that the architectural unification itself—rather than additional pre-training data—drives the gains. The largest relative improvements come on R5.3 metrics (e.g., +18.1% over ReLeR on Ego4D), suggesting the unified retrieval-grounding design particularly benefits multi-prediction recall where disjoint pipelines fail to retrieve the correct clip.

## Relation to Prior Work

RGNet builds on the transformer-based grounding decoder lineage from Moment-DETR (for the L1 + gIoU + CE loss with Hungarian matching) and DAB-DETR (for learnable anchor queries), but fundamentally departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in long video temporal grounding. Prior methods like CONE, SooNet, and M-Guide employ a separate video-text retrieval module (often CLIP-based dot-product similarity) to select proposal clips, then apply a grounding network to the retrieved clip. RGNet replaces this disjoint pipeline with a single RG-Encoder that performs clip retrieval and grounding through shared features and mutual optimization, incorporating sparse attention and a Gumbel-softmax sparsifier to model fine-grained events at both clip and frame granularity simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear empirical diagnosis that clip retrieval, not grounding, is the primary bottleneck in LVTG (Table 2), which reframes how the community should think about improving long video grounding. The 9.7% average improvement on Ego4D and consistent gains on MAD demonstrate that end-to-end unification of retrieval and grounding is a practical path forward for real-world applications involving hour-long videos (YouTube, AR/VR). A key limitation is the reliance on frozen pre-trained image encoders (CLIP, EgoVLP) for visual features, which the authors identify as a future direction to eliminate. Open questions include whether the unified paradigm extends to even longer videos or to multi-query settings, and whether the contrastive negative clip mining strategy generalizes beyond the specific batch-size regimes tested.
