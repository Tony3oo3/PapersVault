## Problem

Existing full-body pose estimation from sparse end-effector tracking (head and hands) relies on SMPL's unconstrained 3-DoF joint rotations, which accumulate positional errors through the kinematic tree and produce biomechanically impossible joint configurations. This leads to hand position mismatches exceeding 5 cm and ground penetration, while prior solutions—iterative optimization, pure learning, or specialized losses—suffer from slow inference, poor generalization, or lack of anatomical guarantees.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 40-frame sliding window of 6D poses (3D position + 6D orientation) for the head and both hands, augmented with per-frame linear and angular velocities computed via backward finite differences. Rather than regressing all 66 SMPL joint angles directly, the network outputs only torso joint angles, 6D foot poses, and swivel angles (as continuous [cos φ, sin φ] pairs); all limb joint angles are then recovered via a closed-form analytic solver, guaranteeing exact end-effector alignment by construction.

## Method

MANIKIN refines the SMPL skeleton by replacing the 3-DoF arm and leg joints with a biomechanically constrained 7-DoF limb model parameterized by a swivel angle that resolves the one remaining degree of freedom after fixing base and end-effector positions. A neural network (lightweight or temporal-spatial Transformer backbone) predicts torso angles, foot poses, and swivel angles; forward kinematics on the torso yields shoulder and hip positions, from which a fully analytic limb solver computes mid-joint flexion, base swing, base twist, and elbow twist via closed-form trigonometric equations. The entire pipeline is differentiable and trained end-to-end with a composite loss combining local rotation, global orientation, foot pose, torso FK position, swivel angle, and mid-joint position terms. This hybrid neural-analytic design reduces hand position error to 0.01–0.12 cm on real VR data while running at 30–580 FPS, outperforming both optimization-based and pure learning baselines.

## Results

On the AMASS benchmark, MANIKIN-S (lightweight) achieves an MPJPE of 3.36 cm and a hand position error (H-PE) of just 0.02 cm, reducing hand error by over 60× compared to AvatarJLM (1.24 cm) while running ~20× faster (580 FPS vs. 31.1 FPS). The heavier MANIKIN-L further improves MPJPE to 3.19 cm with H-PE of 0.01 cm, matching or beating AvatarJLM on all position metrics. The seq2seq variant MANIKIN-LN achieves the best overall MPJPE of 2.73 cm and lowest ground penetration (0.52 cm) across all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MANIKIN outperforms all three baselines across every metric on the AMASS benchmark. Against AvatarPoser [21] (ECCV 2022), MANIKIN-S reduces MPJPE from 4.20 to 3.36 cm and hand error from 1.98 to 0.02 cm, while maintaining comparable speed (580 vs. 612 FPS). Against AGRoL [13] (CVPR 2023), MANIKIN-S improves MPJPE from 3.86 to 3.36 cm and dramatically reduces jitter from 48.30 to 14.08, indicating much smoother motion. Against the most recent baseline AvatarJLM [74], MANIKIN-L matches or beats MPJPE (3.19 vs. 3.35 cm) while achieving a 124× reduction in hand position error (0.01 vs. 1.24 cm) at similar inference speed (30.5 vs. 31.1 FPS). On real-world VR data (Table 3), MANIKIN-L further reduces hand error to 0.12 cm versus 5.27 cm for AvatarJLM, confirming the advantage generalizes beyond synthetic mocap.

## Relation to Prior Work

MANIKIN extends the authors' own AvatarPoser framework (ECCV 2022) by replacing its optimization-based IK refinement with a closed-form analytic limb solver grounded in classical swivel-angle parameterization (Kallmann 2008; Tolani 2000) and swing-twist decomposition (Grassia 1998). The key conceptual departure from prior SMPL-based methods (AvatarJLM, AGRoL) is the reduction of limb degrees of freedom from 3-DoF per joint to biomechanically constrained 7-DoF per limb, ensuring that predicted joint configurations are anatomically plausible rather than merely positionally accurate. This positions the work at the intersection of traditional analytic IK and modern neural sequence modeling, arguing that the two are complementary rather than competing paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50× reduction in hand position error (from >5 cm to 0.1 cm on real VR data) is practically critical for precise hand interaction in AR/VR, and the 580 FPS inference of the lightweight model makes real-time deployment feasible. The biomechanical constraint approach also yields a side benefit: it can retroactively correct unrealistic joint angles in existing SMPL-based datasets like AMASS, improving data quality for downstream applications. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to complex seated or occluded motions, whether body-shape estimation can be integrated to remove the fixed-bone-length assumption, and whether more advanced backbones (e.g., diffusion models) can further improve the neural prediction component.
