## Problem

Trained neural networks are rigid monoliths with fixed architectures and parameters, requiring separate design, training, and storage for each deployment configuration. Existing flexible models and pruning methods are confined to their training configurations or sacrifice performance, and none can continuously morph to arbitrary unseen network sizes without retraining.

## Input / Output

The INR hypernetwork consumes a 6-dimensional normalized coordinate vector encoding both the network configuration (total layers, input/output channel counts) and the specific weight location (layer index, input/output channel index), expanded via sinusoidal Fourier positional encoding before feeding a 5-layer MLP. At inference, the INR is queried K=50 times with small random perturbations around the target coordinate, and the averaged output yields the final weight value, effectively sampling from a local neighborhood on the weight manifold rather than a single grid point.

## Method

NeuMeta trains an INR (block-based MLP with Fourier features) to map model-space coordinates to weight values, jointly optimizing task loss across randomly sampled network configurations and reconstruction loss against a pretrained reference model. Two smoothness strategies are central: (1) intra-model smoothness is achieved by permuting weight matrices within each fully-connected clique of the network's dependency graph, formulated as a multi-objective Shortest Hamiltonian Path (mSHP) problem solved via 2.5-opt local search, which minimizes total variation while preserving functional equivalence through paired inverse permutations on adjacent layers; (2) cross-model smoothness is enforced by adding uniform coordinate noise during INR training and averaging over a perturbed neighborhood at test time. The composite objective (task + reconstruction + L2 regularization) is backpropagated through the main network to the INR parameters, enabling the single INR to generate weights for any network size—including those outside the training compression range—without retraining.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet20, NeuMeta achieves 91.33% top-1 accuracy at full size (0% compression) and retains 89.24% at 50% compression, closely matching the individually trained oracle (91.76% and 90.56% respectively). At the untrained 75% compression ratio, NeuMeta reaches 71.70%, substantially outperforming INN (18.56%) though trailing Slimmable NN (87.04%). For semantic segmentation on PASCAL VOC2012, NeuMeta maintains 81.94 mIOU consistently from 25% to 75% compression, while Slimmable drops to 61.19 mIOU at 75%. NeuMeta stores only 0.27M parameters (vs. 0.67M for Slimmable) and requires 1.8 GPU hours of training (vs. 5.3 for Slimmable).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Slimmable NN [56], NeuMeta trades a small accuracy gap at trained compression ratios (e.g., 90.50% vs. 90.65% at 25% on CIFAR-10) for dramatically lower storage (0.27M vs. 0.67M params) and training cost (1.8 vs. 5.3 GPU hours). At the untrained 75% compression, Slimmable retains 87.04% accuracy while NeuMeta drops to 71.70%, indicating Slimmable's hard parameter sharing still has an edge in extreme extrapolation for classification. However, on semantic segmentation, NeuMeta decisively wins at 75% compression (81.94 vs. 61.19 mIOU, a 20-point margin). Against INN [49], NeuMeta dominates at all compression levels, with the gap widening dramatically at 75% (71.70% vs. 18.56% on CIFAR-10; 27.60% vs. 1.59% on CIFAR-100), where INN's integral-based continuous representation collapses. NeuMeta also slightly underperforms the individually trained oracle at full size (91.33% vs. 91.76%), attributed to the INR's limited capacity to perfectly reconstruct complex weight patterns, but this gap narrows at intermediate compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuMeta extends the INR-as-hypernetwork paradigm introduced by NERN [1] from fixed-size to variable-size weight generation, while departing from continuous-width networks [45,49] that rely on Riemann integral operations. It also generalizes flexible/slimmable networks [2,56] by learning a single continuous weight manifold rather than training on a discrete set of subnetwork configurations, enabling generalization to previously unseen sizes. The weight permutation strategy, formulated as a multi-objective Shortest Hamiltonian Path problem, is a novel contribution that addresses the non-smoothness of raw weight matrices to make them amenable to INR fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm of generating arbitrary-sized network weights from a single trained implicit function is conceptually appealing and could reduce the cost of deploying models across diverse hardware. However, the practical impact is tempered by significant limitations: the 75% compression extrapolation results (e.g., 71.70% vs. 89.56% oracle on CIFAR10) reveal that the manifold generalization breaks down well outside the training range, and the method still requires a pretrained full-size model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very deep or wide architectures, whether depth (not just width) can be varied, and whether the smoothness-preserving permutation strategy transfers to non-convolutional or transformer-based networks.
