## Problem

Machine unlearning must simultaneously forget specific data samples and retain generalizable knowledge from the remaining set, but existing methods either over-forget by directly perturbing weights or require full access to the remaining set for retraining, which is computationally prohibitive at scale. The paper addresses how to obtain effective, generalizable feedback for both forgetting and remembering using only a small subset of the remaining data, while ensuring the two objectives do not conflict during optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241384/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model's weights, the forget set D_f (samples to be erased), a small subset D̂_r of the remaining set (controlled by ratio ρ, e.g., 30%), and K pre-trained fully-differentiable membership inference models with diverse architectures. It produces updated model weights that have reduced knowledge of D_f while preserving generalization on D_r. Notably, the MI models are used as active optimization signals during unlearning rather than merely as post-hoc evaluation metrics, and the method requires no saved information from the original training process.

## Method

LTU applies a three-phase meta-optimization scheme (meta-tune on a support set, meta-test on disjoint query sets, meta-update) twice per iteration: once for remembering feedback (support set = D_f with random labels, query sets = diverse subsets of D̂_r sampled via feature-space, label-space, and random strategies) and once for forgetting feedback (support/query sets drawn from different MI models to create task gaps). The meta-test phase forces the model to retain only generalizable knowledge that transfers across diverse query distributions, while the MI-model-based meta-optimization encourages thorough, multi-signal forgetting. A Gradient Harmonization strategy then resolves conflicts between the resulting remembering gradient g_r and forgetting gradient g_f by projecting g_f onto the subspace orthogonal to g_r when their cosine similarity is negative, before linearly combining them into the final update direction.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18 at 10% forgetting ratio, LTU (using only 30% of the remaining set) achieves UA 4.37%, RA 98.93%, TA 93.19%, and MI 12.97%, with deltas of 0.87, 0.17, 0.31, and 0.09 from the Gold Model (Retrain) respectively — the smallest gaps among all compared methods. At 50% forgetting, LTU achieves UA 7.88% (Δ0.03), RA 99.12% (Δ0.88), TA 90.77% (Δ0.95), and MI 17.39% (Δ1.90), again closest to the Gold Model. Unlearning time is 2.63 min (10%) and 2.23 min (50%), roughly 16× faster than full retraining (43.29 min). On Tiny-ImageNet with ViT, LTU similarly achieves the smallest deltas across all metrics at both forgetting ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTU (using only 30% of D_r) achieves the smallest delta to the Gold Model across all four accuracy/MI metrics at both 10% and 50% forgetting ratios on CIFAR-10, outperforming the closest competitor SalU (which uses 100% of D_r) by 0.28 on UA-Δ, 0.42 on RA-Δ, 0.76 on TA-Δ, and 0.21 on MI-Δ at 10% forgetting. Training-free methods (Fisher, SSD) catastrophically degrade model utility at 50% forgetting (RA drops to 22.68% and 89.47% respectively), while LTU maintains 99.12% RA. BaT and Annesiac show large gaps on TA (10.67 and 2.28 vs. LTU's 0.31 at 10%), indicating poor generalization retention. GA is faster (0.13 min) and achieves lower UA-Δ (4.55 vs. 0.87) but is far worse on MI-Δ (11.18 vs. 0.09), meaning it leaves strong membership traces. Overall, LTU provides the most balanced profile across forgetting, remembering, and privacy metrics while requiring only a fraction of the remaining data.

## Relation to Prior Work

LTU positions itself at the intersection of machine unlearning and meta-learning, being the first to apply a MAML-style meta-optimization scheme (meta-tune, meta-test, meta-update) to the unlearning problem. It departs from prior training-based unlearning methods (BaT, SalU, Amnesiac) that require full access to the remaining set by instead using only a small subset (ρ=0.3) with distributionally diverse query sets to extract generalizable remembering feedback. The forgetting component repurposes membership inference models as differentiable guidance signals rather than mere evaluation metrics, and the Gradient Harmonization strategy adapts gradient projection techniques from multi-task learning (PCGrad, GradMDM) to resolve the inherent conflict between remembering and forgetting optimization trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the data-efficiency gain: LTU achieves state-of-the-art unlearning performance while accessing only 30% of the remaining set, whereas all compared baselines require 100% access — a critical advantage in real-world GDPR-style deletion requests where the full remaining dataset may not be available. The ablations confirm both the meta-optimization and gradient harmonization components are necessary, with removing the remembering feedback causing a catastrophic RA drop (98.3→72.4). Open questions include whether the meta-optimization's second-order gradient cost scales acceptably to very large models, whether the pre-trained MI models generalize across domains, and whether the approach extends to generative or multimodal unlearning settings.
