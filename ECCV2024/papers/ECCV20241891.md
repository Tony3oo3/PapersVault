## Problem

Existing learned image enhancement methods (tone-curve estimators, LUTs, GANs) manipulate images globally or via arbitrary spatial partitions, ignoring the perceptual structure that professional editors actually use. Photo-editing tools let users adjust familiar named colors (the 'blue' of sky, the 'green' of trees), and this color-naming decomposition has not been exploited as a principled basis for learning enhancement curves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241891/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-quality RGB image as input and produces one enhanced RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. A UNet-like backbone first maps the input into a canonical latent space to normalize for camera and lighting variability before any color-naming decomposition is applied. Inference timing is reported at 480p resolution on a single NVIDIA A40 GPU (26 ms).

## Method

NamedCurves decomposes the standardized image into six color-naming probability maps (grouping 11 basic linguistic colors into six hue families) using the Van de Weijer et al. model. For each color name, a Bezier Control Point Estimator (BCPE) predicts 11 monotonically-increasing control points per RGB channel, defining smooth Bezier tone curves that are applied pixel-wise to produce six globally-adjusted images. An attention module then fuses these six images by using the standardized image as Query and each edited image as Key/Value at 1/8 resolution, enabling spatially-varying blending that mimics local expert edits. The final output is a weighted average of the six edited images, weighted by the (thresholded and renormalized) color-naming probability maps. Training is supervised with a combined L2 loss on the backbone output, L2 loss on the final output, and an SSIM term, optimized with Adam at 1e-4 learning rate.

## Results

On the MIT-Adobe-5K dataset (UEGAN split), NamedCurves achieves PSNR 25.59 dB, SSIM 0.936, LPIPS 0.038, and ΔE₀₀ 6.07, outperforming all compared methods including AdaInt (PSNR 25.49) and SepLUT (PSNR 25.47). On the PPR10K portrait retouching dataset, NamedCurves reaches PSNR 26.81 / ΔE_ab 6.48 (Expert A), 25.91 / 7.18 (Expert B), and 25.69 / 7.27 (Expert C), surpassing the best prior method AdaInt by 0.48 dB in PSNR on Expert A. Inference time is 26 ms per 480p image on a single NVIDIA A40, comparable to or faster than most curve-based competitors (DeepLPF: 136 ms, CURL: 102 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NamedCurves outperforms all 11 compared baselines across every dataset version and metric reported. On the DPE split of MIT-5K, it leads FlexiCurve [17] by 0.54 dB in PSNR (24.91 vs. 24.37) and DeepLPF [21] by 0.98 dB, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.038 vs. 0.040–0.060). On the UEGAN split, the margin over the strongest LUT-based competitors is tighter—0.10 dB over AdaInt [36] and 0.12 dB over SepLUT [37] in PSNR—but NamedCurves still wins on SSIM (0.936 vs. 0.926/0.921), LPIPS (0.038 vs. 0.041/0.042), and ΔE₀₀ (6.07 vs. 6.69/6.71). On PPR10K, NamedCurves beats AdaInt [36] by 0.48 dB PSNR and 0.08 ΔE_ab on Expert A, and by 0.51 dB / 0.15 ΔE_ab on Expert B. The method also runs substantially faster than curve-based rivals: 26 ms vs. 136 ms for DeepLPF and 102 ms for CURL, while remaining competitive with LUT methods (10–13 ms). A user study (2AFC, 15 observers) confirms perceptual superiority over AdaInt and SepLUT with a 95% CI of 0.33.

## Relation to Prior Work

NamedCurves sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: tone-curve-based image enhancement (Zero-DCE, CURL, FlexiCurve, LTMNet) and perceptually-motivated color decomposition. The key architectural departure is conditioning separate Bezier tone-curve branches on linguistic color-name probability maps (from Van de Weijer et al.), rather than on learned spatial features or fixed color-space channels. The attention-based fusion mechanism draws on DeepLPF's local-filter philosophy but replaces elliptical polynomial filters with a query-key-value attention over globally color-adjusted images, and the UNet-like backbone with CBAM and multi-resolution skip connections follows the LPIENet design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a linguistically grounded, human-interpretable decomposition (11 universal color names reduced to 6 groups) can outperform purely data-driven enhancement on both PSNR and perceptual color-distance metrics, while running in 26 ms—faster than competing curve-estimation methods. The acknowledged limitation (degraded advantage on images with only one or two dominant color regions) highlights an open question about how to handle sparse color distributions without sacrificing the interpretability of the color-name branches. More broadly, the work suggests that perceptual/linguistic priors about color categories remain useful inductive biases even in the deep-learning era, and that explicit per-color tone curves offer a transparent editing interface that black-box image-to-image GANs do not.
