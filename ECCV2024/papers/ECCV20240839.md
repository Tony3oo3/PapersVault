## Problem

Under the unified anomaly detection task—where a single model must detect anomalies across multiple classes—normalizing-flow-based AD methods suffer from a 'homogeneous mapping' issue: the single Gaussian prior forces the flow to collapse diverse class distributions into one latent mode, causing both normal and abnormal features to receive high log-likelihoods and making anomalies indistinguishable. Reconstruction-based unified AD methods (UniAD, PMAD) avoid this but face the 'identical shortcut' problem where anomalies are also well reconstructed. The paper asks how to design a distribution-learning-based unified AD model that preserves inter-class discriminability in the latent space.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and its class label (which of the Y product categories it belongs to) as input. A frozen EfficientNet-b6 backbone extracts K-level feature maps, which are fed into 12-layer invertible coupling networks at each level. The output is a pixel-level anomaly score map at the original image resolution (H×W), computed by upsampled intra-class log-likelihoods multiplied element-wise with upsampled inter-class negative entropy, plus a scalar image-level detection score. No pixel-level annotations are required during training—only class-level labels.

## Method

HGAD replaces the standard single N(0,I) prior in the normalizing flow with a hierarchical Gaussian mixture: at the inter-class level, each of Y classes has a learnable center μ_y and a softmax-parameterized weight, so the latent prior becomes a mixture of Y Gaussians; at the intra-class level, each class further decomposes into M sub-centers (a main center plus M−1 learnable delta vectors) to capture intra-class diversity. A mutual information maximization loss and an inter-class entropy loss are added to push class centers apart, preventing the collapse that would otherwise occur with randomly initialized centers. Training uses a decoupled two-stage strategy: the main inter-class centers are first optimized by the mixture log-likelihood, then the intra-class delta vectors are optimized with the main center detached from the gradient graph. The final anomaly score combines intra-class log-likelihood (ensuring anomalies are out-of-distribution within their class) with inter-class negative entropy (ensuring anomalies fall in low-density regions between class centers) via element-wise multiplication.

## Results

On MVTecAD under the unified anomaly detection setting, HGAD achieves 98.4% image-level and 97.9% pixel-level AUROC, outperforming the best NF-based baseline CFLOW (89.0%/94.0%) by 9.4%/3.9% and the SOTA unified method UniAD (96.5%/96.8%) by 1.9%/1.1%. On the combined 40-class Union dataset, HGAD reaches 93.5%/97.5% AUROC, surpassing UniAD (86.9%/95.5%) by 6.6%/2.0%. On MVTec3D-RGB, HGAD achieves 87.1%/97.7%, a 9.6% improvement over UniAD in detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HGAD outperforms all eight baselines on MVTecAD under the unified setting. Against the same-architecture NF baseline CFLOW, HGAD improves detection AUROC from 89.0% to 98.4% (+9.4%) and localization from 94.0% to 97.9% (+3.9%), confirming that the hierarchical GMM design—not the flow architecture—is the key driver. Against the SOTA unified methods, HGAD beats UniAD by 1.9% (detection) and 1.1% (localization), PMAD by 3.9% and 2.3%, and OmniAL by 1.2% in detection, though OmniAL edges out HGAD by 0.4% on pixel-level localization (98.3% vs 97.9%). On the 40-class Union dataset, the gap widens substantially: HGAD achieves 93.5% detection AUROC versus 86.9% for UniAD and 55.7% for CFLOW, demonstrating that the homogeneous mapping problem becomes more severe as class diversity increases. On MVTec3D-RGB, HGAD leads UniAD by 9.6% in detection (87.1% vs 77.5%), the largest single-dataset margin reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CFLOW-AD and FastFlow normalizing-flow architectures for anomaly detection, retaining the same invertible coupling-layer design while replacing the standard single-Gaussian latent prior with a hierarchical Gaussian mixture prior (inter-class centers + intra-class sub-centers). It departs from the reconstruction-based unified AD line (UniAD, PMAD, OmniAL) by arguing that distribution-based NF methods can avoid the 'identical shortcut' problem entirely, and it introduces a mutual information maximization loss to prevent class-center collapse—a mechanism absent from prior GMM-based AD work like DAGMM and PEDENet, which compute mixture parameters analytically rather than learning them jointly with the flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'homogeneous mapping' diagnosis provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why NF-based AD methods degrade sharply under unified (multi-class) training, and the hierarchical GMM fix is a clean, architecture-preserving solution that practitioners can adopt with minimal engineering overhead. The approach still requires class labels during training (though no more than one-for-one methods), and its applicability to truly label-free or open-set unified settings remains unexplored. An open question is whether the mutual information repulsion term generalizes to other distribution-based or score-based AD frameworks beyond normalizing flows.
