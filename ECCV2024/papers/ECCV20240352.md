## Problem

Generating multi-motion sequences (a series of distinct actions) from text descriptions remains challenging: prior methods either generate each action independently and stitch them together, producing abrupt transitions at action boundaries, or require dedicated multi-motion training data that is scarce. The core gap is maintaining high fidelity to each individual action description while ensuring smooth, physically plausible transitions between consecutive actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N natural-language action sentences (e.g., 'a person walks forward, then turns left, then jumps') as input and produces a single continuous 3D human motion sequence (joint positions at 20 FPS for HumanML3D or 12.5 FPS for KIT-ML). Text is encoded via a frozen CLIP text encoder, and motion is represented in a discrete token space (VQ-VAE codebook with K entries, temporal compression by 4×). Notably, the model is trained exclusively on single-action sequences yet generates multi-action sequences at inference time without any additional multi-motion training.

## Method

M2D2M builds on VQ-Diffusion with two key innovations. First, a Dynamic Transition Probability β(t,d) replaces the uniform token-transition probability: during early (high-noise) diffusion steps, transitions between distant codebook tokens are favored to encourage exploration of diverse motion modes, while later steps shift toward closer tokens for exploitation and convergence, with the distance metric defined by L2-sorted codebook rank. Second, a Two-Phase Sampling (TPS) strategy splits the denoising process into a joint phase (all N actions denoised together via self-attention in a single Transformer, establishing coarse motion layout and smooth boundaries) followed by an independent phase (each action denoised separately within its own token window to refine fidelity to its specific text description). The Denoising Transformer uses adaptive layer normalization for diffusion-step conditioning, relative positional encoding to extrapolate beyond training sequence lengths, and classifier-free guidance to balance diversity and text alignment.

## Results

On multi-motion generation (N=4 actions) with HumanML3D, M2D2M achieves FID 0.253, R-Top3 0.733, and MM-Dist 3.165 for individual motion segments, with a transition Jerk of 1.238—close to the ground-truth single-motion Jerk of 1.192 and well below the concatenated-real-motion Jerk of 1.371. For single-motion generation on HumanML3D, M2D2M with dynamic transition probability achieves the best FID (0.087) and R-Top3 (0.799) among all compared methods, while on KIT-ML it attains FID 0.378 and R-Top3 0.753. The model also outperforms baselines on transition FID (3.276 vs. 3.351 for PriorMDM and 3.412 for T2M-GPT), confirming smoother action boundaries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M2D2M substantially outperforms PriorMDM [49] on all individual-motion metrics (FID 0.253 vs. 0.832, R-Top3 0.733 vs. 0.586, MM-Dist 3.165 vs. 5.901), while PriorMDM's Jerk of 0.476 reveals severe over-smoothing at transitions. Against T2M-GPT [63], M2D2M wins on FID (0.253 vs. 0.342), MM-Dist (3.165 vs. 3.512), and transition FID (3.276 vs. 3.412), and produces smoother transitions (Jerk 1.238 vs. 1.321). In single-motion generation, M2D2M achieves the best FID (0.087) and R-Top3 (0.799) on HumanML3D, beating AttT2M [67] (FID 0.112, R-Top3 0.786), T2M-GPT [63] (FID 0.116, R-Top3 0.775), M2DM [32] (FID 0.352), MLD [11] (FID 1.087), MotionDiffuse [64] (FID 0.630), TM2T [21] (FID 1.501), and TEMOS [42] (FID 3.734). The only metric where M2D2M trails is MModality (2.115 vs. 3.587 for M2DM), suggesting a trade-off between fidelity and multi-modality. For multi-motion smoothing, TPS (M2D2M) dominates both Handshake [49] (FID 0.254 vs. 1.279) and SLERP [6] (FID 0.254 vs. 1.402), with SLERP exhibiting a negative Jerk (−3.554) indicating excessive smoothing that destroys motion character.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VQ-Diffusion [17] by replacing its uniform token transition probability β_t with a dynamic, distance-aware β(t,d) that modulates exploration based on codebook proximity, and adapts the VQ-VAE architecture from T2M-GPT [63] for motion tokenization. It departs from prior multi-motion generation approaches (PriorMDM [49], TEACH [6], Multi-Act [36]) that generate individual motions independently and then merge them via post-processing (handshake, SLERP), instead proposing a single-stage Two-Phase Sampling strategy that jointly denoises all actions before refining each independently. The use of Relative Positional Encoding to enable sequence-length extrapolation beyond training length is a practical addition not present in the discrete diffusion motion generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate coherent multi-motion sequences from a model trained only on single-motion data addresses a practical bottleneck given the scarcity of multi-action datasets, and the TPS framework is broadly applicable to any discrete diffusion model. However, the evaluation remains indirect since no ground-truth multi-motion sequences exist, and the introduced Jerk metric, while novel for this domain, captures only transition smoothness rather than semantic coherence across the full sequence. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than four actions, whether the dynamic transition probability's benefit persists at larger codebook sizes, and how to evaluate long-horizon semantic consistency beyond per-segment metrics.
