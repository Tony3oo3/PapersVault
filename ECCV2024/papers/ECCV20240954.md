## Problem

End-to-end learning-based camera calibration methods are locked to a single fixed camera model, requiring architectural changes and retraining when switching models, and their loss functions demand closed-form undistortion equations that exclude powerful but ambiguous models like EUCM and DSCM. This paper seeks a calibration framework that is agnostic to the target camera model and robust to parameter ambiguities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240954/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (zero-padded to a square, 400×400 in experiments) and a user-specified radially symmetric camera model as input, and outputs the intrinsic parameters (focal length and distortion coefficients) for that model. The intermediate representation is the VACR: a 1D array of N = S/2k viewing angles, each mapping a discrete radial distance to the elevation angle of the corresponding bearing vector, which is fully model-independent and unambiguous.

## Method

The framework is two-step: first, a CNN with dual ConvNeXt encoders (one on the polar-transformed image, one on the Cartesian image) regresses the VACR, exploiting radial symmetry to reduce the representation from H×W×2 to N parameters and enabling width-wise global average pooling followed by per-row fully connected regression heads. Second, a trust-region reflective nonlinear optimizer fits the target model's parameters to the VACR using a Cauchy loss, with initial estimates from stochastic evolution. Training uses a Huber loss computed via the ground-truth model's radial projection, and the synthetic dataset is generated by uniformly sampling AFOV (80°–190°) before sampling model-specific parameters, avoiding the AFOV bias introduced by uniform parameter sampling in ambiguous models.

## Results

On camera calibration (Table 1), the proposed method achieves the lowest mean reprojection error (Re) across all four datasets with every tested camera model: 4.015 px (DSCM) and 4.633 px (EUCM) on KITTI-360, 6.676 px (DSCM) on StreetLearn, 5.097 px (DSCM) on SILDa, and 3.239 px (EUCM) on WoodScape. In image rectification (Table 2), using EUCM the method attains the best SSIM (0.610 on KITTI-360, 0.609 on SILDa), PSNR (17.53 on KITTI-360, 19.01 on SILDa), and FID (78.4 on KITTI-360, 76.5 on SILDa), outperforming all 10 baselines. The method uses only 6.6M parameters versus 39M for the model-free Liao et al. baseline, while running at higher FPS than PCN and Liao et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines on both calibration and rectification. Against the closest learning-based calibration competitor, Wakai [34] (KB3 model), the proposed method reduces Re from 7.882 to 5.324 px on KITTI-360 (−32%) and from 5.017 to 3.316 px on WoodScape (−34%). Against DeepCalib [4] (UCM), the improvement is dramatic: Re drops from 21.936 to 5.272 px on KITTI-360 (−76%). López-Antequera [24] performs worst overall (Re > 22 px on all datasets), particularly struggling with large-AFOV cameras. The model-free Liao et al. [20] (DDM) is the closest non-parameterized competitor but still trails by 1–3 px in Re. In rectification (Table 2), the method surpasses reconstruction-based baselines DR-GAN [19] and PCN [36] by 2–4 dB in PSNR and 0.08–0.10 in SSIM, and outperforms the dedicated rectification method Blind [16] as well as Jin et al. [14]. Geometric baselines Auto-DE [5] and Auto-DC [1] are far behind, with SSIM below 0.19 versus 0.58–0.61 for the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end paradigm in learning-based camera calibration (DeepCalib, López-Antequera, Wakai), where a single CNN directly regresses parameters for one fixed camera model, by introducing the VACR as a model-agnostic intermediate representation that decouples the learning step from model-specific parameter fitting. Conceptually, it is closest to Liao et al.'s model-free distortion distribution map, but replaces the 2D distortion ratio with 3D bearing vectors and exploits radial symmetry to compress the representation from H×W×2 to N parameters. The polar-coordinate feature processing and width-wise global average pooling draw on the radial-symmetry exploitation in Zhao et al. [40], while the robust nonlinear fitting step (trust-region reflective with Cauchy loss) is a classical optimization technique repurposed as the second stage of a learned pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VACR framework offers a practical solution to a real limitation: a single trained network can calibrate cameras under UCM, KB3, EUCM, and DSCM without architectural changes or retraining, and it handles parameter-ambiguous models (EUCM, DSCM) that break direct parameter-regression losses. The consistent SOTA improvements across four diverse datasets and the efficiency gain (6.6M vs. 39M parameters) make this a useful tool for practitioners working with wide-angle or 360° cameras. Open questions include whether the VACR concept extends beyond radial symmetry (e.g., to general central or non-central cameras), how well it generalizes to real-world images with non-ideal assumptions (skew, off-center principal point), and whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be further reduced without domain-specific fine-tuning.
