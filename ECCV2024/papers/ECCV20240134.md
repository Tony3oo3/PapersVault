## Problem

Coarse-grained VLN instructions (e.g., 'Go clean the mirror in the bathroom') are too concise for agents to navigate reliably, and existing LLM-assisted methods like MiC trigger help passively (on room change or fixed timesteps) while generating uniform step-by-step planning regardless of the agent's actual difficulty. This paper asks how to make LLM assistance proactive, situation-aware, and diverse in the type of guidance provided.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240134/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a short natural-language instruction, a panoramic RGB observation (K single-view images per node), and an undirected navigation graph of candidate nodes; object bounding boxes are additionally extracted for the grounding sub-task. The LLM never sees raw images — it receives BLIP-generated text descriptions (coarse-grained panoramic caption, fine-grained per-candidate-view captions, and a concatenated trajectory history). The output is a discrete action (next navigable node) plus, when triggered, a guidance string of one of four LLM-selected types that is concatenated onto the original instruction before the agent re-predicts.

## Method

VLN-Copilot introduces an entropy-based confusion score over the agent's softmax distribution on candidate nodes; when this score exceeds a threshold τ, the agent proactively queries an LLM (Vicuna-7B-1.5) rather than relying on room-change or fixed-step triggers. Visual observations are translated into multi-grained text via BLIP (panoramic caption, per-candidate-view captions, and a running trajectory description) to serve as the LLM's 'eyes.' A pre-analysis phase collects 5,000 confused-navigation samples, prompts the LLM to diagnose difficulties and suggest helpful guidance, then clusters the responses with Sentence-BERT embeddings and K-Means (silhouette-coefficient-selected k) to distill four actionable guidance categories: Location, Prioritization, Destination Description, and Previous Observations. At inference, the LLM selects the most appropriate category for the current situation and generates specific guidance text, which is appended to the coarse instruction before the agent re-predicts its action. The entire pipeline is integrated into training (fine-tuning AutoVLN or DUET with the LLM in the loop), and the confusion-score trend decreases over training, indicating the agent becomes less uncertain as it learns.

## Results

On REVERIE Val Unseen, VLN-Copilot achieves 43.63% SPL and 29.75% RGSPL, outperforming the previous LLM-based method MiC (43.60% SPL, 28.72% RGSPL) and the non-LLM SOTA AutoVLN (40.85% SPL, 26.76% RGSPL). On REVERIE Test Unseen, it reaches 42.32% SPL and 26.55% RGSPL. On CVDN-target Val Unseen, VLN-Copilot achieves the best Goal Progress (GP) of 4.47, surpassing AutoVLN (3.98), DUET (3.70), and HAMT (2.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLN-Copilot outperforms the closest LLM-based competitor MiC [31] on both main metrics: +1.03% RGSPL on Val Unseen (29.75 vs 28.72) and +0.38% SPL on Test Unseen (42.32 vs 41.97), with the gap widening on RGSPL at Test Unseen (+0.38%). Against the strongest non-LLM baseline AutoVLN [6], VLN-Copilot gains +2.78% SPL and +2.99% RGSPL on Val Unseen, and +3.44% SPL and +3.87% RGSPL on Test Unseen. Compared to ScaleVLN [42], which relies on 4.9M synthetic instruction-trajectory pairs, VLN-Copilot still leads by +1.79% SPL and +3.70% RGSPL on Val Unseen. On CVDN-target, VLN-Copilot achieves the highest GP (4.47) among all compared methods, beating AutoVLN by +0.49, DUET by +0.77, and HAMT by +1.73, while also reporting a lower TL (40.38) than DUET (78.53) and AutoVLN (61.97), indicating more efficient navigation paths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coarse-grained VLN line initiated by REVERIE and the LLM-assisted navigation work of MiC, which first prompted an LLM to expand short instructions into detailed plans. VLN-Copilot departs from MiC in two key ways: it replaces MiC's passive room-change trigger with an entropy-based confusion score that lets the agent proactively seek help mid-room, and it replaces MiC's uniform fine-grained planning with situation-specific guidance drawn from four pre-analyzed categories (Location, Prioritization, Destination Description, Previous Observations). The base navigation agents are AutoVLN (ECCV 2022) and DUET (CVPR 2022), fine-tuned on REVERIE and CVDN-target respectively, while BLIP provides scene-to-text descriptions that serve as the LLM's visual proxy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a lightweight uncertainty-triggered LLM consultation loop can meaningfully improve coarse-grained VLN without retraining the navigation policy, offering a practical recipe for integrating LLMs into embodied agents. However, the reliance on BLIP-generated text descriptions as the LLM's 'eyes' is a clear bottleneck; the authors themselves flag that multimodal LLMs (e.g., LLaVA, Video-LLaVA) would be a more natural fit. Open questions include whether the four guidance categories generalize beyond MP3D indoor environments, how the approach scales to longer-horizon or outdoor navigation, and whether the confusion-score threshold can be learned rather than grid-searched.
