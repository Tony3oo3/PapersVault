## Problem

Existing video copy localization methods rely on a single global feature per frame, which fails to capture local regions in picture-in-picture or camcording scenarios where copyrighted content occupies only a small portion of the frame. Additionally, training the temporal localization detector requires large amounts of expensive human-annotated data. This paper addresses both limitations with a fully self-supervised framework that learns regional representations and generates training data automatically.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240298/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of untrimmed query and reference videos, extracts frames at fixed intervals, and outputs a list of 4-tuples (s^q, e^q, s^r, e^r) giving the start and end frame indices of every copied segment in both videos. The feature extractor operates on 384×384 RGB frames with ViT-small (patch size 16, embedding dim 384, output projected to 256-d), producing two feature vectors per frame (one from the CLS token, one from the Regional Token). The temporal localization stage consumes the resulting frame-to-frame similarity map and predicts segment boundaries via a YOLOX-s object detector.

## Method

The feature extractor introduces a Regional Token into the ViT architecture, concatenated with the CLS token and patch tokens, and is trained with a two-stage asymmetric contrastive procedure: in stage 1 the Regional Token is frozen while the CLS token learns global features via NT-Xent loss; in stage 2 the Regional Token is unlocked and the similarity function becomes the maximum cosine similarity across all pairwise combinations of the two feature types, creating an asymmetry that drives the Regional Token to focus on local regions. For temporal localization, the paper generates copied video pairs automatically by inserting randomly clipped and augmented source segments into background videos (vanilla strategy), then expands the pair set using the Transitivity Property—two copy videos sharing temporally overlapping segments from the same source form an additional copied pair with ground truth at the intersection. A YOLOX-s detector is trained on these self-supervised pairs to localize segments in the similarity map, eliminating the need for human annotation while achieving state-of-the-art performance on VCSL and VCDB.

## Results

On the VCSL dataset, the self-supervised model (Ours-ssl) achieves a segment-level F1 of 68.83% and video-level F1 of 95.42% without any human-annotated data, outperforming all previous methods. After fine-tuning the detector on the full VCSL training set (Ours-ft), the model reaches 71.56% segment-level F1 and 96.46% video-level F1, surpassing the prior best (TransVCL: 66.51% / 90.06%) by a large margin. On VCDB, Ours-ssl achieves 77.26% segment F1 and 93.31% video F1, while Ours-ft reaches 78.78% and 94.12% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The self-supervised model (Ours-ssl) outperforms all six prior methods (HV, TN, DP, DTW, SPD, TransVCL) on both segment-level and video-level F1 on VCSL, with the largest gap over TransVCL (previous SOTA) being +2.32% segment F1 and +5.36% video F1. After fine-tuning (Ours-ft), the advantage widens to +5.05% segment F1 and +6.40% video F1 over TransVCL. Against the competition-winning solutions evaluated in Table 6, Ours-ft slightly trails MultiCrop [39] on segment F1 (71.56% vs. 72.09%) but edges it out on video F1 (96.46% vs. 96.33%), while clearly surpassing ImConcat [21] (62.00% seg F1) and SAM [28] (66.68% seg F1) on both metrics. Notably, Ours-ssl already exceeds TransVCL without any human annotation, and fine-tuning with only 1% of VCSL training data matches or slightly exceeds the fully supervised model trained on the entire set. On VCDB, Ours-ssl and Ours-ft both lead all baselines on both metrics, with Ours-ft achieving 78.78% segment F1 and 94.12% video F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViT architecture by introducing a single Regional Token trained with an asymmetric two-stage contrastive procedure, building directly on the NT-Xent framework of Chen et al. and the ViT backbone of Dosovitskiy et al. For temporal localization, it departs from prior object-detection-based methods (TransVCL, SPD) that rely on expensive human-annotated similarity maps, instead leveraging the Transitivity Property from Jiang et al. to auto-generate diverse copied video pairs. The approach also contrasts with competition-winning strategies (MultiCrop, ImConcat, SAM) that handle picture-in-picture scenarios through multiple crops or concatenated inputs at substantially higher computational cost, offering a lighter-weight alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for content moderation at scale: achieving state-of-the-art video copy localization without any human-annotated data dramatically reduces the cost barrier for deploying and updating such systems. The Regional Token is a minimal architectural change (one extra token, asymmetric training) that generalizes beyond copy detection to any task requiring simultaneous global and local frame representations. Key open questions remain: the feature extractor is trained purely on images without temporal context, and the self-supervised generation struggles with complex remix/mashup content (the 'kichiku' category), suggesting that temporal modeling and more sophisticated augmentation strategies are needed for full parity with supervised training on all content types.
