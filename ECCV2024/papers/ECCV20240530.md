## Problem

Existing full-body action understanding datasets are predominantly captured from fixed exocentric cameras, limiting practical deployment in flexible, embodied settings such as smart eyewear. Moreover, no prior dataset provides interpretable action judgement annotations—technical keypoint verification, natural language execution comments, and quality scores—that would enable understanding not just *what* and *when* an action occurs, but *how well* it is performed. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a synchronized ego-exo fitness dataset with rich multi-level annotations and benchmarking five tasks spanning these dimensions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240530/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides six synchronized camera streams per recording (three ego: forward, left-downward, right-downward; three exo: front, left-front, right-front) of 3–6 action sequences lasting 33–186 seconds, with 12 action types and 86 predefined sequences. For the GEV task specifically, input is a trimmed single-action video paired with a set of n text technical keypoints, and the model outputs an n-way verification score vector where each score indicates whether the execution satisfies that keypoint (thresholded at inference). Two-level temporal boundaries further subdivide each action into Getting Ready, Executing, and Relaxing sub-steps, enabling fine-grained localization benchmarks.

## Method

The key architectural contribution is GEVFormer, a transformer-based model for Guidance-based Execution Verification that takes a single action video and text keypoints as input and outputs per-keypoint verification scores. It uses frozen CLIP image and text encoders, followed by a Temporal Context Modeling (TCM) Transformer Encoder to capture temporal dynamics in visual embeddings, and a Cross-Modal Verifier (CMV) combining a Transformer Decoder with a linear head to produce n-way scores. Training employs a composite loss L = L_GEV + λ·L_Align, where L_GEV supervises verification accuracy and L_Align is an InfoNCE-based contrastive loss that forces consistent representations across synchronized ego and exo views, bridging the domain gap that prevents simple data mixing from improving performance. The paper additionally benchmarks I3D, TimeSformer, EgoVLP, and CAT on action classification, localization, and cross-view sequence verification to characterize view-specific performance gaps and the challenges of cross-view transfer.

## Results

On the novel Guidance-based Execution Verification (GEV) task, GEVFormer with cross-view alignment loss achieves 0.5439 average F1-score (0.5452 on exo, 0.5425 on ego), outperforming the CLIP-based baseline (0.4881) by 5.6 points and the no-alignment variant (0.5265) by 1.7 points. On Action Classification, TimeSformer pre-trained on Kinetics-600 achieves the best exocentric Top-1 accuracy (0.9274), while TimeSformer pre-trained on Ego-Exo4D achieves the best egocentric accuracy (0.8000), revealing a significant cross-view domain gap. For Cross-View Sequence Verification, the best AUC on ego-exo pairs is 0.7755, substantially lower than inner-view performance (0.803 ego-only, 0.822 exo-only), and cross-view retrieval drops to ~0.30 Rank-1 and ~0.25 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Action Classification, TimeSformer (K600 pretrain) [4] leads on exocentric videos (0.9274 Top-1), slightly ahead of I3D (K400 pretrain) [9] at 0.9194; on egocentric videos, TimeSformer (Ego-Exo4D pretrain) [4] achieves 0.8000, marginally beating EgoVLP (Ego4D pretrain) [42] at 0.7977. All models suffer severe cross-view degradation (e.g., I3D drops from 0.9194 exo to 0.0927 ego), confirming a large ego-exo domain gap. For Cross-View Sequence Verification, the CAT/SVIP model [53] achieves 0.7755 AUC on ego-exo pairs, well below its inner-view scores (0.803 ego, 0.822 exo), and pruning egocentric training data to 30% drops AUC to 0.7072. On the novel GEV task, GEVFormer with alignment [54-CLIP backbone] outperforms the CLIP-GEV baseline [54] by 5.6 F1 points (0.5439 vs. 0.4881 avg) and the no-alignment GEVFormer by 1.7 points, demonstrating that explicit cross-view contrastive alignment is critical for bridging the ego-exo gap in interpretable action verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: full-body action understanding (FineGym, Diving48, FLAG3D) and egocentric video understanding (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D). Existing full-body action datasets are almost exclusively exocentric, while existing egocentric datasets focus on tabletop or daily interactive activities rather than full-body fitness movements. The paper repurposes the cross-view contrastive alignment idea (InfoNCE, as in CMC) to bridge the ego-exo domain gap within a video-language verification architecture (GEVFormer), and extends the Sequence Verification task from single-view to cross-view settings. The interpretable action judgement annotations (keypoint verification, natural language comments, quality scores) have no direct precedent in prior full-body action datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoExo-Fitness fills a clear gap by providing the first synchronized ego-exo full-body fitness action dataset with annotations spanning 'what', 'when', and 'how well', enabling research on AI-assisted fitness coaching from wearable cameras. A consistent and practically important finding across all five benchmark tasks is that simply mixing ego and exo training data does not reliably improve cross-view performance, revealing a substantial domain gap that current architectures cannot bridge through data augmentation alone. Key open questions include how to effectively leverage synchronized exocentric data to boost egocentric performance, how to handle imbalanced ego/exo training sets (a realistic deployment scenario), and whether the GEV task can scale beyond the current modest F1 scores (~0.54) to practical coaching applications.
