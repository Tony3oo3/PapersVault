## Problem

Text-guided infinite image synthesis faces two key gaps: a lack of high-resolution text-image paired datasets with diverse contexts, and the difficulty of maintaining global coherence while generating rich local content during expansion. Prior methods either focus on limited categories like natural landscapes or require expensive high-resolution paired training data, making zero-shot, general-domain outpainting infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241852/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a 512×512 RGB image and a global text caption describing the full expanded scene. It produces an arbitrarily-sized image (e.g., 1536×512 for ×4 or 2560×512 for ×8) by autoregressively shifting a local masked patch in a user-specified direction (top, bottom, left, or right). At each expansion step, a multimodal LLM (LLaVA 1.6) generates a new local caption from the current image, which is fed alongside the global caption and CLIP visual features into the diffusion model.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes Stable Diffusion 1.5 by expanding the U-Net's cross-attention dimension to accept a concatenated 154×768 conditioning vector formed from CLIP image embeddings (77×768) and an MLP-compressed fusion of global and local caption embeddings (77×768). Training data is synthesized without high-resolution images: GPT-3.5 first generates imaginary local captions from annotated captions, then summarizes them into a global caption, creating paired text conditions for four directional masks on 512×512 images. At inference, the model expands autoregressively—each step conditions on the global caption, a freshly generated local caption from the LLM, and the CLIP embedding of the full expanded image so far—enabling zero-shot, arbitrary-size generation with both global semantic coherence and local spatial consistency.

## Results

On the primary MS-COCO dataset, the proposed method achieves an Inception Score (IS) of 16.05 and CLIP-SIM of 27.94 at ×4 expansion, and 9.97 / 27.94 at ×8 expansion, outperforming all three baselines on both metrics. The advantage grows with expansion factor: at ×8 on MS-COCO, the IS gap over the strongest baseline (SD Inpainting, 8.55) reaches 1.42 points. Across Flickr 8k and Pascal VOC, the method similarly leads on IS (e.g., 15.07 vs. 14.53 for SD Inp on Pascal ×4) while maintaining the highest CLIP-SIM scores (28.07–28.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines (SD Inpainting, Blended Latent Diffusion, and PowerPaint) on both IS and CLIP-SIM across every dataset and expansion factor. SD Inpainting is the strongest baseline, achieving IS of 14.31 on MS-COCO ×4, yet the proposed method leads by 1.74 IS points (16.05) and 0.53 CLIP-SIM points (27.94 vs. 27.41). Blended Latent Diffusion and PowerPaint trail further behind, with BLD scoring only 11.88 IS on MS-COCO ×4 and PP 12.91. The performance gap widens at ×8 expansion: on MS-COCO the IS margin over SD Inpainting grows from 1.74 to 1.42 absolute points (9.97 vs. 8.55), while BLD and PP drop to 6.39 and 7.37 respectively. On CLIP-SIM the margins are smaller (≤0.5 points) but consistently favor the proposed method, indicating better text-image alignment. Human evaluation (Table 2) corroborates these quantitative results, with the proposed method winning 65–78% of pairwise comparisons on text matching, image quality, and global coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Stable Diffusion inpainting framework by expanding the U-Net's cross-attention dimension to jointly condition on concatenated textual (global + local captions) and visual (CLIP) features. It departs from prior text-guided outpainting work (Nuwa-Infinity, InfinityGAN, InOut) that was restricted to limited categories like natural landscapes and required high-resolution text-image paired training data. The key conceptual departure is repurposing LLMs (GPT-3.5 for training, LLaVA for inference) to synthesize global and local captions that describe scenes beyond the input image, thereby eliminating the need for high-resolution paired datasets entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a practical workaround for the data bottleneck in text-guided outpainting: by letting an LLM 'imagine' surrounding context from a short caption, one can train on standard 512×512 datasets while generating arbitrarily large, contextually diverse images. However, the method remains bounded by the quality of the underlying Stable Diffusion backbone and the LLM's ability to produce coherent spatial descriptions, and the paper acknowledges reliance on a pre-trained text-to-image model as a limitation. Open questions include whether the LLM-guided autoregressive expansion degrades over very long sequences, how well it generalizes to structured or abstract content, and whether the approach extends naturally to multi-modal guidance (e.g., audio, story narratives) as the authors suggest for future work.
