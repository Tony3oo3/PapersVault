## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-quality reconstruction but requires a large number of input views, making it impractical for sparse-view captures where deploying extensive camera arrays is costly. The paper identifies the lack of spatial autocorrelation among independently optimized splat features as a key factor causing overfitting in sparse settings, and addresses this gap for both static 3D and dynamic 4D scene reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240045/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view calibrated RGB images (or multi-view video sequences for 4D) and an SfM-initialized point cloud as input. It produces a set of 3D or 4D Gaussian splat primitives that are rendered via standard 3DGS rasterization at 120+ FPS for novel view synthesis. The tri-plane CNN generator and neural field MLPs are used exclusively during optimization and discarded at test time, so the final representation is fully compatible with existing splatting pipelines and visualization tools without any neural network inference overhead.

## Method

SplatFields regularizes 3DGS by regressing splat features through a hierarchical neural architecture that introduces spatial autocorrelation bias. A CNN generator (inspired by deep image priors) produces axis-aligned tri-plane representations from random noise; splat positions are projected onto these planes to obtain deep structural features, which are then fed through a deformation MLP to refine positions and through compact neural fields (5–6 layers, 64–128 neurons) to predict each splat's color, scale, opacity, and rotation. For 4D extension, all neural fields are conditioned on a time step t, and a time-conditioned forward-flow field (using the ResFields MLP architecture) warps Gaussian centers to model complex scene dynamics. The approach is trained end-to-end with a photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM) plus adaptive density control, and the spatial bias from the tri-plane CNN and MLP spectral bias together enforce that nearby primitives share similar features, as quantified by improved Moran's I scores.

## Results

On the Owlii multi-view dynamic benchmark (10 input views), SplatFields4D achieves a mean PSNR of 31.12 (100k iterations), outperforming 4DGaussians (29.80), 4D-GS (28.05), and Deformable3DGS (27.76) by 1.3–3.4 dB. Under the more challenging 4-view setting, the gap widens substantially: SplatFields4D reaches 21.92 PSNR versus 21.31 for 4DGaussians, 20.04 for Deformable3DGS, and 17.40 for 4D-GS. On the NeRF-DS monocular dynamic benchmark, SplatFields4D attains 23.84 mean PSNR, edging out 4DGaussians (23.68) and NeRF-DS (23.60) while maintaining 120+ FPS rendering. On static Blender reconstruction with 6 views, SplatFields3D consistently surpasses 3DGS, Mip3DGS, SuGaR, and Light3DGS across all scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatFields4D outperforms all splatting-based dynamic baselines on Owlii: it beats 4DGaussians [76] by 1.3 dB (10 views) and 0.6 dB (4 views), 4D-GS [82] by 3.1 dB (10 views) and 4.5 dB (4 views), and Deformable3DGS [83] by 3.4 dB (10 views) and 1.9 dB (4 views). Against NeRF-based methods (DyNeRF, TNeRF, DNeRF with ResFields [44]), SplatFields4D matches or slightly exceeds their quality (31.12 vs. 30.70 best NeRF) while rendering at 120+ FPS versus <1 FPS. On the NeRF-DS monocular benchmark, SplatFields4D (23.84 PSNR) narrowly surpasses 4DGaussians (23.68) and NeRF-DS (23.60), and clearly outperforms 3D-GS (20.29) and Deformable3DGS (23.54). For static reconstruction, SplatFields3D wins over 3DGS [28], Mip3DGS [89], SuGaR [21], and Light3DGS [16] on Blender at both 6 and 12 views, with the margin growing as views decrease. On the DTU 3-view task, SplatFields improves over ZeroRF [66] and 2DGS [24], confirming the spatial-regularization benefit in extreme sparsity. The paper acknowledges that in the most extreme sparse-dynamic setting (4 views, Owlii), NeRF-based methods with ResFields still hold a slight edge, identifying this as a limitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatFields regularizes 3D Gaussian Splatting by bridging the gap between independent point-based primitives and continuous volumetric representations. It draws on the deep image prior / ZeroRF line of work [66,71] to introduce a triplane CNN generator that provides a deep structural prior on splat features, and on ResFields [44] to model 4D dynamics with a forward-flow field. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior dynamic 3DGS methods [76,82,83] that model deformations with single MLPs or HexPlane-based parameterizations lacking spatial autocorrelation, instead enforcing local coherence through hierarchical neural fields that are discarded after training to preserve real-time rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: 3DGS's dependence on dense multi-view captures is prohibitive for dynamic scene recording, and the proposed regularization closes much of the gap to NeRF-based methods while retaining 120+ FPS rendering and compatibility with existing splatting pipelines. The Moran's I analysis provides a useful diagnostic linking spatial coherence of splat features to generalization quality. However, the authors acknowledge that in extremely sparse (4-view) and highly dynamic scenarios, NeRF-based methods with ResFields still outperform, leaving open the question of whether 3DGS-based approaches can fully match volumetric methods under severe view scarcity. Incorporating learned priors (diffusion models, semantic features) is identified as a promising next step.
