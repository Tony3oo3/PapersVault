## Problem

Traditional BEV grid representations in camera-based 3D object detection incur quadratic computational cost as spatial resolution increases, making high-resolution BEV features infeasible (e.g., BEVFormer runs out of memory at 450×450 on A100 40GB GPUs). While prior efficiency works optimize the 2D-to-3D projection, none address the BEV resolution bottleneck itself, leaving a trade-off between detection accuracy (which benefits from finer grids) and computational feasibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240937/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six-camera RGB images at 1600×900 (nuScenes) or five-camera images covering 252° horizontal FOV (Waymo), along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics for 3D-to-2D projection. Internally, it maintains a low-resolution BEV grid (200×200 base, 150×150 small) plus two 1D vector queries of length 450, and outputs 3D bounding boxes with class, dimensions, orientation, and velocity, evaluated via NDS, mAP, and per-metric errors (mATE, mASE, mAOE, mAVE, mAAE).

## Method

VectorFormer factorizes high-resolution BEV features into two low-rank 1D vector queries (V^X ∈ R^{W_HR×C} and V^Y ∈ R^{H_HR×C}), reducing the representation from O(n²) to O(n) complexity. A Vector Query Scattering module predicts an objectness heatmap from the vector queries, applies directional Top-k selection to identify foreground regions, and constructs sparse HR BEV queries by bilinearly sampling both vectors at those positions, with learned deformable offsets to refine locations. The sparse HR and LR BEV queries jointly interact with multi-view image features through a shared spatial cross-attention module, then a Vector Query Gathering module uses multi-head cross-attention to aggregate the learned sparse HR features back into the vector queries, with cross-dimensional positional embeddings to prevent information loss. Temporal context is fused via multi-head self-attention between current and previous-frame vector queries, and the resulting vector queries directly serve as decoding queries for 3D prediction, replacing randomly initialized decoder embeddings.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, VectorFormer achieves 58.3 NDS and 49.2 mAP, surpassing BEVFormer (56.9 NDS, 48.1 mAP) by +1.4 and +1.1 points respectively. With a large V2-99 backbone on the nuScenes validation set (≤24 epochs), VectorFormer reaches 60.5 NDS and 51.7 mAP, outperforming SparseBEV (57.9 NDS) and StreamPETR (57.1 NDS) by 2.6 and 3.4 NDS points. On Waymo, VectorFormer achieves 53.2 LET-mAP, exceeding BEVFormer (51.0) by 2.2 points. In terms of efficiency, VectorFormer with a 200×200 BEV grid and 450-length vector queries achieves 53.2 NDS at 3.4 FPS and 4.85 GB memory, whereas BEVFormer at 450×450 resolution requires 11.21 GB and runs at only 2.3 FPS (and OOMs on A100 40GB during training).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VectorFormer outperforms BEVFormer by +1.4 NDS and +1.1 mAP on the nuScenes test set, and by +3.1 NDS / +3.5 mAP in the small (ResNet-101) setting on the validation set. Against the strongest sparse-query baselines on the validation set with large backbone (Table 3), VectorFormer leads SparseBEV by +2.6 NDS, SA-BEV by +2.6 NDS, and StreamPETR by +3.4 NDS. In the depth-aware comparison, VectorFormer-S (51.0 NDS) surpasses DFA3D-S (50.1 NDS) without using depth, and VectorFormer-B (53.2 NDS) is essentially tied with DFA3D-B (53.1 NDS). On Waymo, VectorFormer beats BEVFormer by +2.2 LET-mAP (53.2 vs 51.0) but trails MV-FCOS3D++ on LET-mAPL (36.8 vs 37.9) while leading on LET-mAPH (49.1 vs 48.8) and overall LET-mAP (53.2 vs 52.2). The efficiency comparison (Table 4) shows VectorFormer matches or exceeds BEVFormer's NDS at lower BEV grid resolutions while reducing GPU memory by up to 56.7% and increasing FPS by up to 47.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VectorFormer directly extends BEVFormer's query-BEV architecture, reusing its spatial cross-attention module, temporal self-attention, and overall encoder-decoder structure. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the low-rank tensor factorization philosophy from volume rendering (TensorF, K-Planes) to decompose a 2D BEV grid into two 1D vector queries along the x and y axes, enabling high-resolution spatial modeling at O(n) rather than O(n²) cost. The paper also draws on deformable attention for adaptive sampling and heatmap-based foreground localization, but positions itself squarely within the query-BEV lineage rather than the forward-projection (BEVDet) or sparse-query (DETR3D, PETR) families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic scaling of BEV resolution is a well-recognized bottleneck that limits how much spatial detail can be encoded; VectorFormer's vector factorization offers a practical path to higher effective resolution without proportional memory or compute growth, as demonstrated by the 450-resolution vector setting running where BEVFormer OOMs. The generalizability of the vector representation—consistently improving DFA3D when plugged in—suggests it could serve as a drop-in enhancement for other query-BEV detectors. Open questions include whether the sparsity assumption (objects occupy small regions relative to the full BEV) degrades in dense-traffic or close-range scenarios, and whether the factorization extends naturally to downstream tasks like occupancy prediction or segmentation that require dense per-cell features.
