## Problem

Existing HDR image compression systems rely on manually designed tone mapping operators and standard LDR codecs in a two-layer decomposition, yielding suboptimal rate-distortion performance. Critically, no prior method is end-to-end optimized with perceptually aligned distortion metrics for both the stored HDR image and the displayed LDR image simultaneously, leaving the perceptual quality of the LDR output unaddressed as a design goal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241298/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes uncalibrated HDR images (linearly rescaled to [0,1]) at 448×448 resolution and produces two compressed bitstreams: one that decodes to an LDR image displayable on a standard 1–300 cd/m² monitor, and a second (much smaller, ~0.04–0.05 bpp) that encodes HDR side-information feature maps. The LDR synthesis network is conditioned on a maximum scene luminance value (sampled from {10⁴, 10⁵, 10⁶, 10⁷} cd/m² during training), and the automated extension generates four pseudo-multi-exposure LDR images at these luminance levels, fused via multi-exposure image fusion to produce the final LDR output.

## Method

EPIC-HDR employs two parallel analysis–synthesis network pairs following the transform coding paradigm: an LDR path that maps the input HDR image to quantized latent codes and synthesizes an LDR image (conditioned on max scene luminance via a sinusoidal embedding), and an HDR path that produces quantized latent codes decoded into feature-map side information. A separate reconstruction network takes the generated LDR image and the HDR feature maps to recover the full HDR image. Rate is estimated via a scale-hyperprior entropy model with joint autoregressive and hierarchical priors, and codes are saved with arithmetic coding. The key differentiator is the training objective: instead of MSE or SSIM, the loss combines the entropy rate with two perceptually aligned distortion terms — an HDR metric d_H that decomposes the image into a stack of LDR exposures via an inverse display model and computes ADISTS per exposure, and an LDR metric NLPD based on normalized Laplacian pyramid distance — both referenced to the original uncompressed HDR image.

## Results

On the Poly Haven test set (780 images), EPIC-HDR achieves BD-Quality gains of +1.12 on HDR-VDP-3, +1.60 on HDR-VQM, +7.91 on PU21-CRF-PSNR, and +7.63 on d_PSNR* relative to the TMO+BPG anchor, outperforming all seven baselines across every HDR quality metric. The automated extension further improves PU21-CRF-PSNR to +9.77 while maintaining strong performance on other metrics. On LDR quality, EPIC-HDR also produces superior TMQI and NLPD scores at comparable bit rates, with the NLPD gain being particularly pronounced since NLPD is the training distortion function for the LDR branch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPIC-HDR dominates all seven baselines on every HDR quality metric. Against the strongest learned baseline OoDHDR-codec, EPIC-HDR gains +0.95 on HDR-VDP-3 (1.12 vs. 0.17), +1.48 on HDR-VQM (1.60 vs. 0.13), +6.88 on PU21-CRF-PSNR (7.91 vs. 1.03), and +3.65 on d_PSNR* (7.63 vs. 3.98). Traditional codec-based methods (JPEG-HDR, JPEG-XT, Xu05, Boschetti10, Mai11, TMO+WebP) all score negative or near-zero BD-Quality, indicating they are worse than the TMO+BPG anchor on most metrics. VVC, the only standard that natively supports HDR, achieves a small positive gain on HDR-VDP-3 (+0.02) and d_PSNR* (+1.41) but is far surpassed by EPIC-HDR. The automated extension trades a small amount of HDR-VDP-3 and HDR-VQM quality for a large PU21-CRF-PSNR improvement (+9.77), suggesting the multi-exposure fusion post-processing benefits PSNR-type metrics more than perceptual ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPIC-HDR extends the learned image compression paradigm (building on Cheng et al.'s attention-based architecture and Minnen et al.'s joint autoregressive/hierarchical entropy model) to the HDR domain, where prior work relied on manually designed tone mapping operators and off-the-shelf LDR codecs in a two-layer decomposition. The key departure is the end-to-end optimization using two perceptually aligned distortion metrics (an inverse-display-model-based ADISTS for HDR and NLPD for LDR), both referenced to the uncompressed HDR image, rather than the cascaded, separately-optimized TMO + codec pipeline. This positions EPIC-HDR as a learnable generalization of traditional layer-decomposition HDR compression, unifying storage and display objectives in a single differentiable framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learned HDR image compression system jointly optimized for perceptual quality of both the reconstructed HDR image and the displayable LDR image, addressing a gap where prior methods treated LDR output quality as an afterthought. The large and consistent BD-Quality margins over all standard codecs (JPEG-HDR, JPEG-XT, VVC) and the learned OoDHDR-codec suggest that end-to-end perceptual optimization can substantially outperform hand-engineered pipelines in this domain. Open questions include whether the approach extends to HDR video compression, real-time streaming, and the still-manual estimation of maximum scene luminance (the automated extension via multi-exposure fusion is a partial but not fully learned solution).
