## Problem

Existing event-based stereo methods that aggregate temporal information (e.g., DTC with recurrent structures) transfer only low-level features independently per camera, leaving stereo-specific representations like cost volumes unused across time steps. Additionally, training an optical flow network to support temporal propagation is difficult because ground-truth flow is unavailable in event stereo datasets, and purely self-supervised flow losses are unstable when jointly trained with stereo matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240932/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes rectified stereo event streams from left and right event cameras, stacked into voxel grids with a discretized time dimension B (346×260 on MVSEC, 640×480 on DSEC). It processes events sequentially over time steps, where each step t receives the current event voxel pair plus warped representations from step t−1, and outputs a dense disparity map. The stereoscopic flow (a 3-channel field: Δx^L, Δx^R, Δy) is an intermediate prediction that drives all temporal warping operations.

## Method

The core contribution is a jointly trained framework that introduces 'stereoscopic flow'—a triplet flow respecting the epipolar constraint (shared vertical component, independent horizontal components per camera)—to warp both 2D feature maps and 4D cost volumes from the previous time step into the current frame. The flow network is lightweight (9 convolutional layers) and shares encoded features with the stereo encoder, avoiding redundant computation. Cost volume warping extends to the disparity dimension via a derived disparity flow Δd = Δx^L − Δx^R, enabling 3D backward warping in D×H×W space. A novel temporal disparity consistency (TDC) loss supervises the flow using only ground-truth disparity maps (warping previous GT disparity to the current frame and comparing), eliminating the need for flow ground truth. Warped past and current cost volumes are fused via an entropy-based reliability filter, and the full system is trained end-to-end with a combined flow and stereo matching objective.

## Results

On MVSEC Split 1, the proposed method achieves the best mean depth error (13.0 cm), median depth error (5.0 cm), and mean disparity error (0.46 pix), with a 1PA of 92.9%. On MVSEC Split 3, it leads all metrics: 15.0 cm mean depth, 5.8 cm median depth, 0.49 pix mean disparity, and 92.6% 1PA. On DSEC, the method achieves the best 1PE (8.662), 2PE (2.259), MAE (0.493), and RMSE (1.172) while also being the fastest at 71.6 FPS with the lowest computational cost (57.4 GFLOPs), compared to 11.6 FPS and 237.4 GFLOPs for DTC-SPADE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is DTC-SPADE [94], which uses an RNN-based temporal aggregation. On MVSEC Sp1, DTC-SPADE edges out the proposed method on 1PA (93.0% vs. 92.9%) but loses on mean depth (13.5 vs. 13.0 cm) and median depth (5.2 vs. 5.0 cm); on Sp3, the proposed method dominates across all four metrics (15.0 vs. 17.1 cm mean depth, 0.49 vs. 0.60 pix mean disparity, 92.6% vs. 89.7% 1PA). On DSEC, the proposed method outperforms DTC-SPADE by a wide margin on every metric (MAE 0.493 vs. 0.527, RMSE 1.172 vs. 1.291) while running 6.2× faster (71.6 vs. 11.6 FPS) with 4.1× fewer FLOPs (57.4 vs. 237.4 GFLOPs). DDES [74] and EIS-E [52], which lack temporal aggregation, are clearly outperformed on all shared metrics. EITNet [1] (which uses images during training) is surpassed on MVSEC Sp1 mean depth (13.0 vs. 14.2 cm) and Sp3 mean depth (15.0 vs. 19.4 cm). Se-CFF [53] is outperformed on DSEC across all four metrics (1PE 8.662 vs. 9.583, MAE 0.493 vs. 0.519) and is 3.2× slower (22.2 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends event-based stereo matching (DTC, Se-CFF, DDES) by replacing RNN- or attention-based temporal aggregation with a flow-based warping mechanism. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of 'stereoscopic flow'—a triplet of two horizontal and one shared vertical displacement that respects the epipolar constraint—enabling a single lightweight network to warp both 2D features and the 3D cost volume across time. Unlike prior event-flow works (EV-Flow, E-RAFT) that estimate flow independently, here the flow network shares encoder features with the stereo network and is trained via a temporal disparity consistency loss that requires no optical-flow ground truth, making the joint training practical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: the method achieves SOTA on both MVSEC and DSEC while running at 71.6 FPS with 57.4 GFlops, roughly 4× faster and 4× lighter than the prior best (DTC-SPADE at 11.6 FPS / 237.4 GFlops), making temporal event stereo viable on edge hardware. The cost-volume warping insight—that reusing the full 3D stereo representation across time yields larger gains than reusing 2D features alone—could inform temporal aggregation design in other stereo or multi-view settings. The authors note that self-supervised stereo matching from events alone (without disparity GT) remains unexplored, and the TDC loss, while effective, still depends on ground-truth disparity during training, leaving a gap toward fully unsupervised event-based 3D perception.
