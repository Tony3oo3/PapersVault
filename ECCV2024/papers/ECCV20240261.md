## Problem

Existing monocular 3D object detectors are trained and evaluated almost exclusively under clear weather, yet real-world autonomous driving must cope with adverse conditions such as fog, which scatters and absorbs light and severely degrades visual cues. No prior monocular 3D detector provides a principled mechanism to adapt its feature representations to the degree of weather degradation present in the input image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240261/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the method consumes a single monocular RGB image (clear or foggy) and outputs 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation) for the car category, evaluated via AP_3D and AP_BEV at IoU 0.7 (KITTI) or 0.5 (Virtual KITTI). During training it additionally requires paired clear–foggy images from the same scene to compute the fog distribution used as diffusion noise, but at test time only one image is needed. The backbone is DLA-102 and the detection head is a transformer encoder–decoder following MonoDTR.

## Method

MonoWAD introduces two coupled components. First, a weather codebook (K=4096 VQ slots, dim 256) is trained with a Clear Knowledge Recalling loss—combining a KL-divergence term that aligns the quantized clear feature's channel distribution with the original clear feature, and an L2 term that forces the quantized foggy feature to map to the same codebook entries—so that the codebook emits a consistent weather-reference feature regardless of whether the input is clear or foggy. Second, a weather-adaptive diffusion model replaces the standard Gaussian noise with the fog distribution F = x^f − x^c: the forward process iteratively adds F to the clear feature over T=15 steps, and the reverse process uses a conditional autoencoder whose cross-attention layer takes the weather-reference feature as key/value to guide how much enhancement each spatial location needs. A Weather-Adaptive Enhancement loss (L2 between the true fog variant and the predicted one) trains the denoiser, and the total objective also includes the standard 3D detection loss. The key distinction from prior diffusion-based approaches is that the noise model encodes actual weather variation rather than isotropic Gaussian noise, and the codebook-derived reference acts as a learned attention signal that scales the enhancement to the observed weather severity.

## Results

On the KITTI validation set under foggy weather, MonoWAD achieves AP_3D of 27.17 (Easy), 19.57 (Moderate), and 16.21 (Hard), substantially outperforming the best prior method MonoDTR (16.89/11.86/9.87) by over 10 points on Easy. Under clear weather, MonoWAD reaches 29.10/21.08/17.73 AP_3D, also leading all baselines. On the Virtual KITTI dataset, MonoWAD maintains top performance across foggy (13.33 Easy), rainy (14.12 Easy), and sunset (13.38 Easy) conditions, demonstrating generalization beyond fog. The average AP_3D across foggy and clear weather is 28.14/20.33/16.97, compared to 20.71/15.22/12.69 for the next-best method (MonoDTR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoWAD outperforms all five monocular 3D detection baselines (GUPNet, DID-M3D, MonoGround, MonoDTR, MonoDETR) on both foggy and clear KITTI validation, with the largest gap under foggy weather where MonoWAD leads MonoDTR by +10.28 AP_3D (Easy) and MonoDETR by +19.77. Against standard diffusion models (DDPM, CDM) adapted naively to the task, MonoWAD's weather-adaptive design yields +21.85 and +24.43 AP_3D (Easy, foggy) respectively, confirming that fog-distribution noise and the weather codebook are critical. Among dehazing-augmented baselines, MonoWAD surpasses RIDCP (image-level) by +9.94 foggy Easy and +5.08 clear Easy, and outperforms feature-level methods DeNet and Yang et al. by +4.82 and +4.30 foggy Easy respectively, while maintaining far superior clear-weather performance (29.10 vs. 7.10 for DeNet). On Virtual KITTI, MonoWAD leads all baselines across foggy, rainy, and sunset conditions, with the closest competitor MonoDTR trailing by 4.54 AP_3D (Easy, foggy) and 2.39 (Easy, rainy).

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoWAD repurposes the diffusion model framework (DDPM, LDM) from image generation to feature-space enhancement, replacing Gaussian noise with a learned fog distribution (the difference between foggy and clear features) as the forward-process perturbation. The weather codebook draws on vector-quantized codebook ideas from VQ-VAE and Taming Transformers, but applies them to memorize clear-weather knowledge rather than to discretize latent representations. Among monocular 3D detectors (MonoDTR, MonoDETR, GUPNet), this is the first to explicitly model weather as a learnable, reversible degradation in feature space, departing from prior approaches that either apply external dehazing modules or rely on domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Weather-robust monocular 3D detection is a practical necessity for autonomous driving, yet prior SOTA methods degrade sharply under fog (e.g., MonoGround drops to near-zero AP). MonoWAD's use of weather-difference as diffusion noise offers a principled, adaptive mechanism that generalizes across foggy, rainy, and sunset conditions without task-specific tuning. Key limitations remain: training requires paired clear-foggy images (restricting real-world deployment), inference is ~4× slower than MonoDETR due to iterative denoising, and the foggy KITTI benchmark is synthetically generated, leaving open whether gains transfer to naturally occurring fog. Future work on paired-image-free training and faster sampling would be needed for real-time use.
