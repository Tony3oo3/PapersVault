## Problem

Existing point cloud registration methods either rely on strict point-to-point correspondences (ICP, invariant feature matching) that break down under noise and outliers, or use equivariant features in ways that assume exact overlap between input clouds (global pooling, re-established correspondences). No prior method combines correspondence-free registration, full SE(3) equivariance, differentiability, and unsupervised training to handle real-world noisy, outlier-rich, partially-overlapping point clouds.

## Input / Output

The method takes two unordered 3D point clouds of potentially different cardinalities (downsampled to 1024 points in experiments) and produces a full SE(3) relative pose (rotation matrix and translation vector) plus an optimized kernel lengthscale. Unlike correspondence-based methods, no pairwise matching is ever formed; the point clouds are consumed as sets through an equivariant encoder that maps each point to a direct sum of its 3D coordinate and multi-channel steerable vectors. The label function (color, intensity) is supported in the formulation but set to 1 in all experiments for fair comparison with geometry-only baselines.

## Method

EquivAlign represents each point cloud as a nonparametric function in an RKHS, where each point is encoded as a direct sum of its 3D coordinate (type-0 feature, providing translation equivariance) and SO(3) steerable vectors (type-1 features from VectorNeuron, providing rotation equivariance), combined via a DGCNN-style graph convolution. Registration is performed by minimizing the RKHS distance between the two point-cloud functions using a product kernel (RBF on coordinates with learnable lengthscale ℓ, tanh on steerable vectors), which naturally suppresses outlier contributions at large distances. Training is fully unsupervised via a bi-level scheme: an inner loop iteratively optimizes the pose and kernel lengthscale, while an outer loop backpropagates through the converged pose to update encoder weights, with a curriculum schedule progressing from 1° to 90° initial rotations. At inference, the encoder runs once and the pose is refined iteratively in feature space without re-encoding, making the pipeline fully differentiable and correspondence-free.

## Results

On the real-world ETH3D RGB-D dataset, EquivAlign achieves the lowest rotation error (0.53°) and translation error (0.01 m) among all baselines, with the smallest standard deviations (0.99° and 0.02 m respectively). On the synthetic ModelNet40 dataset under the most challenging condition (90° initial rotation, Gaussian noise σ=0.01, 20% outliers), EquivAlign achieves 4.12° rotation error, outperforming ICP (38.00°), GICP (48.40°), Geometric-CVO (30.84°), FPFH+RANSAC (85.60°), FPFH+FGR (43.88°), GeoTransformer (42.58°), and E2PN (70.61°). At 45° initial angle with the same perturbations, EquivAlign (1.96°) is competitive with ICP (1.38°) and GICP (2.53°) while far surpassing all invariant-feature and equivariant baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EquivAlign outperforms all seven baselines on the ETH3D real-world dataset in both rotation and translation. Against classical methods, EquivAlign reduces rotation error by 40% over ICP (0.53° vs 0.88°) and 23% over GICP (0.53° vs 0.69°), while also achieving lower translation error (0.01 m vs 0.03 m and 0.02 m). Against invariant-feature methods, the margin is substantial: FPFH+RANSAC (8.75°), FPFH+FGR (3.60°), and GeoTransformer (2.23°) all suffer large rotation errors and high variance on real data, with GeoTransformer's 13.09° standard deviation indicating frequent registration failures. Against the equivariant baseline E2PN, EquivAlign achieves a 10× reduction in rotation error (0.53° vs 5.20°); the authors attribute this to E2PN's finite-group discretization limiting fine-grained registration, its reliance on ground-truth supervision causing overfitting to new scenes, and its assumption of complete input symmetry that is violated in real data. On ModelNet40 under heavy perturbation (90° + noise + 20% outliers), EquivAlign (4.12°) dominates all baselines by a wide margin, while at 45° it is competitive with ICP (1.38°) and GICP (2.53°) but slightly behind GeoTransformer (0.92°), which benefits from noise-aware data augmentation during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: the RKHS-based correspondence-free registration framework of Continuous Visual Odometry (CVO), which is robust but non-differentiable, and equivariant neural feature learning (VectorNeuron, E2PN), which is differentiable but typically relies on one-to-one correspondences or global pooling. The key architectural departure is a direct-sum representation combining raw 3D coordinates (type-0) with SO(3) steerable vectors (type-1) to achieve full SE(3) equivariance without centering, paired with a product RBF×tanh kernel that keeps the RKHS distance differentiable end-to-end. Unlike invariant-feature methods (GeoTransformer, PREDATOR) that require supervised correspondence learning and extensive data augmentation, EquivAlign trains unsupervised via bi-level optimization, and unlike E2PN's finite-group discretization, it uses continuous steerable features for finer-grained registration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of equivariant-based registration on real RGB-D odometry data (ETH3D), bridging a gap where equivariant learning had been largely confined to physics/chemistry or synthetic benchmarks. The unsupervised training paradigm removes the need for ground-truth pose labels, making the approach more practical for deployment in new environments. Key limitations include a week-long training time on 8 V100 GPUs and degraded performance under low overlap (20% cropping), suggesting the method is best suited for frame-to-frame odometry rather than sparse-view global registration. Open directions include incorporating SE(3)-equivariant transformers for longer-range feature correlations and extending the curriculum to handle more extreme pose differences.
