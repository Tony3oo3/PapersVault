## Problem

Worldwide geolocalization has been studied exclusively for images, while existing video geolocalization methods are retrieval-based and confined to one or two geographic regions. No large-scale video dataset with global coverage exists to train classification-based video geolocalization models, leaving the problem of predicting a video's city, state, country, and continent from visual content entirely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241686/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 15-frame video clip (resized to 224×224; source resolution 1280×720) and produces four hierarchical class predictions: city (166 classes), state/province (157), country (91), and continent (6). At inference, predictions are refined via codependent hierarchical evaluation, where coarser-hierarchy probabilities are multiplied into finer ones and the finest prediction is traced upward through the geographic hierarchy. During training, location-name text strings are additionally fed through a pretrained text encoder to provide alignment targets.

## Method

The architecture uses a VideoMAE (ViT-S) encoder pretrained on Kinetics-400 to produce video feature embeddings, which are passed to four independent MLP classifiers for the four geographic hierarchies. A novel Self-Cross Attention module concatenates the four prediction vectors and applies multi-head attention so that each hierarchy attends to itself and to all others, producing a fused representation used for an auxiliary scene-recognition loss computed against soft-scene labels (the per-class percentage of frames in each video). A TextLabel Alignment (TLA) branch projects the same attended vector through a separate FFN and minimizes negative cosine similarity with CLIP text embeddings of the location names, distilling textual knowledge into the visual feature space. Training combines cross-entropy geolocalization loss, soft-label scene loss, and TLA loss; at inference, codependent hierarchical evaluation multiplies coarser probabilities into finer ones before selecting the most likely city and propagating upward.

## Results

On the proposed CityGuessr68k dataset (13,655 validation videos), the method achieves 69.6% top-1 accuracy on city prediction, 70.2% on state/province, 74.8% on country, and 83.8% on continent. On Mapillary(MSLS) (2,271 validation sequences), it reaches 72.8% city, 72.8% state, 73.2% country, and 88.1% continent accuracy. The method outperforms the strongest image-based baseline (GeoDecoder) by ~5% on city and ~4% on continent, and surpasses the VideoMAE video baseline by 5.1% on city and 9.4% on continent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two image-based geolocalization methods (PlaNet, ISNs, GeoDecoder) and two video encoder baselines (TimesFormer, VideoMAE) on CityGuessr68k. The proposed method wins across all four hierarchies against every baseline. Against the strongest image method GeoDecoder [3], it gains +5.4% on city, +5.7% on state, +5.3% on country, and +3.9% on continent. Against the video encoder TimesFormer [2], improvements are larger: +8.7% on city and +5.4% on continent. Against the VideoMAE [33] baseline (same encoder backbone without the proposed modules), the gains are +5.1% on city, +8.9% on country, and +9.4% on continent, demonstrating the contribution of the Self-Cross Attention and TextLabel Alignment components. On Mapillary(MSLS), the method also outperforms the VideoMAE baseline by +5.2% on city and +6.2% on continent, confirming generalizability across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: fine-grained video geolocalization (retrieval-based, limited to specific regions like BDD or KITTI) and worldwide image geolocalization (classification-based, e.g., PlaNet, ISNs, GeoDecoder). It departs from both by formulating worldwide video geolocalization as a multi-hierarchy classification problem, using VideoMAE as the encoder backbone and introducing a Self-Cross Attention module that fuses hierarchy prediction vectors for scene recognition—a different mechanism from ISNs' separate scene encoders or GeoDecoder's query-based decoder. The TextLabel Alignment strategy repurposes CLIP's text encoder to distill location-name knowledge into visual features, an idea not previously explored in geolocalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CityGuessr68k is the first global-scale video geolocalization dataset, filling a critical gap that prevented training classification models on worldwide video data; it will likely become the standard benchmark for this task. The baseline method, while straightforward, establishes a reference point and demonstrates that temporal information (9% gain over best image setting) and auxiliary signals (soft-scene labels, text alignment) yield consistent improvements across all four hierarchies. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond driving/walking videos, whether 166 cities is sufficient for true global coverage, and whether the Self-Cross Attention and TLA components transfer to other hierarchical video classification tasks.
