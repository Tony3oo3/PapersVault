## Problem

Most large vision-language models rely on CLIP as a shared vision vocabulary, which struggles with dense, fine-grained perception tasks such as high-resolution document OCR, chart parsing, and non-English text recognition—effectively treating these as 'out-of-vocabulary' visual content. The paper asks whether the vision vocabulary of an LVLM can be efficiently scaled up, analogous to text vocabulary expansion in LLMs, without overwriting the original CLIP knowledge or incurring prohibitive training costs on a large LLM.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed at up to 1024×1024 by the new vocabulary encoder) plus a text prompt. The image is encoded in parallel by two vision networks (the new ViTDet-based encoder and CLIP-L), each producing 256 tokens of 1024 dimensions; these are projected and concatenated to 4096 dimensions before being fed to the LLM. Outputs are free-form text: OCR strings, markdown/LaTeX-formatted document content, normalized bounding-box coordinates (scaled ×1000), or short VQA answers, controlled by the prompt.

## Method

Vary operates in two stages. First, a lightweight 'Vary-tiny' pipeline—comprising a SAM-initialized ViTDet encoder (80M params) followed by two strided convolution layers that compress 64×64×256 features to 256×1024 tokens, paired with a tiny OPT-125M decoder—is trained autoregressively on ~5M samples of document OCR, rendered charts, and object detection data to generate a new vision vocabulary specialized for dense perception. Second, this new encoder is grafted in parallel with the original CLIP-L encoder inside the full LVLM (Vary-base 7B or Vary-toy 1.8B); each encoder has its own linear projection layer, and their 256-token outputs are concatenated before the LLM. Both vocabulary networks are frozen during LVLM training to prevent knowledge overwrite, while the projection layers and LLM are fine-tuned on a multi-task pretraining mixture (captions, PDF OCR, charts, detection, NLP conversation, VQA) followed by standard SFT. The key design insight is that autoregressive next-token training of the new vocabulary is more flexible for dense scenes than contrastive learning, and that parallel fusion with CLIP preserves general visual ability while adding fine-grained perception capacity.

## Results

Vary-base (7B, Qwen-7B) achieves 79.1% ANLS on DocVQA (test), 66.3% relaxed accuracy on ChartQA, 88.6% accuracy on RefCOCO val, and 36.4% total on MMVet. The smaller Vary-toy (1.8B, Qwen-1.8B) reaches 65.0% on DocVQA, 59.2% on ChartQA, 88.0% on RefCOCO val, and 30.4% on MMVet. On fine-grained document OCR, Vary-base achieves 0.119 edit distance on English plain text and 80.37% F1 on markdown conversion (formula + table), surpassing the dedicated document model Nougat (0.126 edit distance, 79.97% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DocVQA, Vary-base (79.1% test ANLS) substantially outperforms all baselines, exceeding Pix2Struct (72.1%), Donut (67.5%), and Qwen-VL (65.1%) by 7–14 points. On ChartQA, Vary-base (66.3% average) edges out Qwen-VL-chat (66.3%) and Qwen-VL (65.7%), and clearly surpasses Matcha (64.2%) and mPLUG-DocOwl (57.4%). On RefCOCO, Vary-base (88.6% val) ties Qwen-VL-chat-7B (88.6%) and beats Shikra-13B (87.8%) and Next-chat (85.5%), while Vary-toy (88.0%) with only a 1.8B LLM matches the 7B Qwen-VL-chat. On the general MMVet benchmark, Vary-base (36.4%) improves over LLaVA-1.5-7B (30.5%) by 5.9 points and over LLaVA-13B (32.9%) by 3.5 points, confirming that the expanded vision vocabulary does not degrade general ability. Notably, Vary-toy (30.4% MMVet) achieves near-parity with the 7B LLaVA-1.5 (30.5%) despite using a 4× smaller language model, highlighting the efficiency of the vocabulary-scaling approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vary draws a direct analogy to text vocabulary expansion in LLMs (e.g., adding Chinese characters to an English tokenizer) and applies it to the vision branch of LVLMs. It builds on CLIP as the base vision vocabulary and SAM-initialized ViTDet as the new encoder, but departs from prior LVLMs (BLIP-2, LLaVA, Qwen-VL) that treat CLIP as a fixed, monolithic vision encoder. Rather than unfreezing CLIP during pretraining (as mPLUG-Owl and Qwen-VL do), Vary generates a complementary vocabulary via a tiny autoregressive pipeline (ViTDet + OPT-125M) and then grafts it in parallel to CLIP, freezing both during LVLM training to prevent knowledge overwrite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that CLIP's contrastive-learning vocabulary is a genuine bottleneck for dense, fine-grained perception (document OCR, chart parsing) and that a low-cost parallel encoder trained with autoregressive next-token prediction can fill this gap without degrading general vision ability. The ablation showing DocVQA collapsing from 65.3% to 10.9% when the new vocabulary is removed, while MMVet barely shifts, cleanly separates the two vocabularies' roles. Open questions remain: how to resolve potential competition between the two branches, whether the approach generalizes beyond document/chart/detection to other 'foreign language' visual domains, and how to scale vocabulary expansion more systematically than the current ad-hoc data-engineering pipeline.
