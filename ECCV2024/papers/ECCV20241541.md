## Problem

Existing semantic BEV mapping approaches rely on fully supervised training with large amounts of human-annotated BEV ground truth, or on additional FV semantic labels for self-supervised pretraining. No prior method learns semantic BEV mapping from monocular FV images in a fully unsupervised, label-efficient manner that avoids both BEV and FV label dependencies during pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241541/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of monocular frontal-view RGB images (448×1344 on KITTI-360, 448×896 on nuScenes) with a temporal window of 4 frames, and produces a semantic BEV segmentation map. During pretraining, 75% of non-overlapping 28-pixel patches are randomly masked from the current frame; during finetuning, masking is disabled and a single FV image is processed to generate the BEV output. Only 1% of BEV labels (KITTI-360) or one sample per scene (~1/40, nuScenes) are used for the final supervised finetuning stage.

## Method

LetsMap disentangles semantic BEV mapping into two disjoint neural pathways trained in a fully unsupervised pretraining phase. The geometric pathway uses a DINOv2 ViT-B backbone with a BiFPN adapter and an image-conditioned implicit neural field (following PixelNeRF) to estimate volumetric scene density, supervised by a photometric loss computed via forward and inverse warping across multiple camera views and timesteps. The semantic pathway employs a novel temporal masked autoencoder (T-MAE) that masks 75% of patches in the current frame, lifts the visible features to a 3D voxel grid, warps it to future timesteps using known ego-motion, and reconstructs RGB at both current and future frames via an L2 loss on normalized patches. After pretraining, the network is finetuned for BEV segmentation by orthographically collapsing voxel features along height and applying a cross-entropy loss on the small fraction of available BEV labels, requiring no FV labels or additional annotated data.

## Results

On KITTI-360, LetsMap achieves 28.96% mIoU using only 1% of BEV labels and no FV labels, outperforming the self-supervised SkyEye (26.94% mIoU, which uses 100% FV labels) by 2.02 pp and four of five fully-supervised baselines by more than 2 pp. On nuScenes, LetsMap reaches 27.66% mIoU with approximately 1/40 of BEV labels, remaining competitive with fully-supervised methods (e.g., VPN at 27.30%, VED at 26.98%) while using a tiny fraction of annotations. The ablation confirms that unsupervised pretraining contributes up to 2.49 pp mIoU gain at the 1% label regime, and the geometric pathway adds 1.28 pp while the temporal MAE semantic pathway adds a further 1.21 pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On KITTI-360, LetsMap (28.96% mIoU, 1% BEV, 0% FV) outperforms SkyEye (26.94%, 100% FV + 1% BEV) by 2.02 pp despite using no FV labels at all, and beats VED (25.74%), VPN (26.84%), and PON (26.72%) by 2–3 pp each while those methods use 100% BEV labels. The only fully-supervised method that surpasses LetsMap is PoBEV (30.42% mIoU at 100% BEV), a gap of 1.46 pp; however, when PoBEV is restricted to 1% BEV labels its mIoU drops to 19.09%, a 9.87 pp deficit versus LetsMap. On nuScenes, LetsMap (27.66% mIoU, ~1/40 BEV) trails PoBEV (30.90%, 100% BEV) by 3.24 pp but exceeds VED (26.98%) and VPN (27.30%) while using roughly 40× fewer labels, and is within 0.43 pp of PON (28.09%). The paper attributes the remaining gap on dynamic classes (car, truck) to the static-scene assumption of the implicit field formulation, which is more pronounced on the highly dynamic nuScenes dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

LetsMap builds on two distinct lines of work: image-conditioned NeRF (PixelNeRF) for the geometric pathway, which estimates volumetric scene density from a single monocular view, and masked autoencoders (MAE) for the semantic pathway, which the authors extend into a novel temporal formulation (T-MAE) that reconstructs masked patches across multiple timesteps. It departs from prior BEV mapping approaches (VED, VPN, PON, LSS, PoBEV) that rely on fully supervised training with 100% BEV labels, and from self-supervised methods like SkyEye that still require FV semantic labels. The key conceptual departure is the explicit disentanglement of geometry and semantics into two disjoint neural pathways trained entirely without labels, followed by a label-efficient finetuning stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving deployment: achieving competitive mIoU with only 1% of BEV labels and zero FV labels removes the primary bottleneck of expensive BEV annotation. The modular two-pathway design also facilitates rapid adoption of new backbones or field formulations. However, the static-scene assumption inherent to implicit neural fields limits performance on dynamic objects (car, truck), and the photometric loss is sensitive to lighting changes and large ego-motion. Open questions include whether explicit dynamic object handling, stereo cameras, or cross-view surround-camera correlation can close the remaining gap to fully supervised SOTA.
