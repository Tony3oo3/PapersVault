## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D occupancy prediction remains under-explored because large-scale open-world 3D annotations are prohibitively expensive to produce, and existing methods train end-to-end networks from scratch, capping their performance. The paper asks whether 2D foundation models (MiDaS for depth, CLIP for semantics) can be assembled and adapted to lift 2D semantics into 3D occupancy without manual semantic labels, while overcoming the domain gap in depth, the low spatial resolution of CLIP features, and the long-tail class imbalance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241432/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model consumes only 6 surrounding RGB camera images (512×1408) and CLIP language embeddings for the target class set, producing a 200×200×16 voxel grid (0.4 m voxels, spanning ±40 m in X/Y and −1.0 to 5.4 m in Z) where each voxel receives a binary occupancy flag and a semantic class determined by cosine similarity to CLIP text embeddings. During training, LiDAR point clouds provide depth and binary-occupancy supervision, and an off-the-shelf 2D open-vocabulary segmentor (SAN) generates pseudo semantic labels, so no manual voxel annotations are required. The model supports both zero-label (C_s = ∅) and partial-label (C_s ≠ ∅) training regimes.

## Method

VEON uses a decoupled two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 adapts a frozen MiDaS (BEiT-L) backbone with a ZoeDepth head for relative-to-metric depth conversion, a differentiable metric-to-bin transformation (Eq. 1) for LSS lifting, and LoRA on all linear layers (adding only 0.3% parameters) for domain transfer. Stage 2 attaches a High-resolution Side Adaptor (HSA) in parallel with the CLIP ViT encoder: the HSA body maintains 32×88 residual features fused with CLIP tokens, while the HSA head produces an attention-bias matrix A injected into the last quarter of CLIP transformer layers (Eq. 4) and a supplementary matrix S concatenated with CLIP output tokens (Eq. 5) to recover lost spatial detail. The resulting 2D semantic feature is lifted to 3D via LSS, processed by ResNet3D blocks, and decoded into binary occupancy and a CLIP-aligned embedding per voxel. A class-reweighted cosine loss (Eq. 6) averages voxel losses within each class before across classes, prioritizing rare tail categories. With only 46 M trainable parameters (6.8% of the 678 M total), VEON-L achieves 15.14 mIoU on Occ3D-nuScenes with zero manual semantic labels.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes with zero manual semantic labels (C_s = ∅), VEON-L achieves 15.14 mIoU and VEON-B achieves 12.38 mIoU, surpassing the best prior unsupervised method (OccNeRF, 9.54 mIoU) by 5.60 mIoU. With partial labels (13 seen / 4 unseen classes), VEON-L reaches 19.94 mIoU. On the open-vocabulary language-driven retrieval benchmark, VEON-L achieves 30.9 mAP-all on the test set, outperforming POP-3D (12.6) by 18.3 points, all with only 46M trainable parameters and no manual semantic annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unsupervised occupancy predictors (no semantic labels), VEON-L (15.14 mIoU) decisively outperforms SelfOcc-BEV (6.76), SelfOcc-TPV (7.97), and OccNeRF (9.54) by margins of 8.38, 7.17, and 5.60 mIoU respectively, with particularly large gains on tail classes like barrier (10.4 vs. 0.0/0.0/0.8) and bicycle (10.4 vs. 0.0/0.0/0.8). Against fully supervised methods (BEVFormer 39.19, BEVDet 37.28, CTF-Occ 28.53), VEON-L trails substantially, which is expected given it uses zero manual semantic annotations and only 46M trainable parameters versus the full-network training of those baselines. On the open-vocabulary language-driven retrieval benchmark, VEON-L achieves 30.9 mAP-all on the test set, surpassing POP-3D (12.6) by 18.3 points and MaskCLIP+ (12.0 on test-vis) by a wide margin, demonstrating superior alignment between its 3D representations and CLIP language embeddings. The partial-label setting (13 seen / 4 unseen classes) shows VEON-L reaching 19.94 mIoU, confirming that even a small amount of semantic supervision yields meaningful gains while retaining open-vocabulary capability on unseen classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

VEON departs from the prevailing paradigm of training a unified 2D backbone from scratch for open-vocabulary 3D occupancy (as in POP-3D, SelfOcc, OccNeRF) by instead assembling and minimally adapting two pretrained 2D foundation models: MiDaS for depth and CLIP for semantics. The depth side borrows the relative-to-metric transformation from ZoeDepth and applies LoRA for domain transfer, while the semantic side introduces a novel High-resolution Side Adaptor (HSA) that compensates for CLIP's low-resolution ViT tokens and image-level (rather than pixel-level) attention. The 2D-to-3D lifting follows the standard LSS formulation, and pseudo-labels for open-vocabulary supervision come from the off-the-shelf SAN segmentor, connecting this work to the broader open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding line (3DOVS, OpenScene, OVIR-3D).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that strong 2D data priors from foundation models can be transferred to 3D occupancy with only ~46M trainable parameters (6.8% of total) and zero manual semantic annotations, challenging the assumption that end-to-end training from scratch is necessary for competitive 3D perception. However, the gap to fully supervised methods remains substantial (15.14 vs. 39.19 mIoU), and the approach still relies on LiDAR-derived binary occupancy labels during training, limiting its applicability to purely vision-only settings. Open questions include whether the decoupled foundation-model strategy generalizes beyond nuScenes-scale datasets, whether the HSA design transfers to other vision encoders, and whether the class reweighting strategy scales to truly open-world vocabularies far beyond the 17-class nuScenes taxonomy.
