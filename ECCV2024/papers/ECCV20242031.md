## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods output axis-aligned bounding boxes, which insufficiently describe object geometry for real-world applications requiring physical interaction such as grasping or collision avoidance. Moving to dense 3D grounding (referral-based instance segmentation) exposes a critical weakness: kernel-based segmentation backbones produce limited separability in the latent space between instances of the same semantic class, causing significant performance drops on repetitive instances with same-class distractors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud (N points with 6D features from ScanNet scenes) and a free-form natural language description as input, and produces a dense binary point-level mask (z ∈ {0,1}^N) identifying all points belonging to the referred object. For benchmark comparability, an axis-aligned bounding box is fitted onto the predicted mask for standard ScanRefer evaluation. The kernel-based pipeline first generates sparse instance candidates via centroid heatmaps and local normalized NMS, then reconstructs full-resolution masks through dynamic convolution, avoiding the memory cost of dense per-point language interactions.

## Method

ConcreteNet combines a sparse convolutional UNet visual backbone with a DKNet-style kernel-based instance segmentation pipeline and MPNet language encoding, then fuses the two modalities through a transformer decoder augmented with four novel modules. (1) A Bottom-up Attentive Fusion (BAF) module applies spherical attention masks with progressively increasing radius across decoder layers, restricting inter-instance message passing to local neighborhoods to disambiguate relational referrals. (2) A verbo-visual contrastive loss (Eq. 11) pulls matched sentence–instance embedding pairs together while pushing non-matching pairs apart in cosine-similarity space, directly addressing the separability deficit of kernel features. (3) A learned Global Camera Token (GCT) is injected as an unmasked global token in the attention mechanism and supervised via L2 regression to annotation-time camera positions, resolving view-dependent utterances like 'on the left.' (4) A two-stage Multi-view Ensembling (MVE) algorithm rotates the point cloud across K yaw angles, first selects the consensus object via pairwise IoU energy maximization, then refines the final mask by pointwise majority voting among valid predictions, reducing epistemic uncertainty in both selection and segmentation.

## Results

On the ScanRefer val-set, ConcreteNet achieves 46.53% overall accuracy at 50% IoU (Acc@50), outperforming the previous best method EDA (42.26%) by 4.27 points. On the online test-split, ConcreteNet reaches 49.50% Acc@50, surpassing EDA (45.45%) by 4.05 points. The gains are most pronounced on the 'Multiple' subset (repetitive instances), where ConcreteNet scores 38.39% Acc@50 on val vs. 37.64% for EDA, while on the 'Unique' subset it achieves 82.05% vs. 68.57% for EDA. Notably, ConcreteNet outputs dense 3D instance masks rather than axis-aligned bounding boxes, providing finer geometric detail for downstream tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConcreteNet outperforms all 12 compared baselines on the primary metric (Overall Acc@50) on both the ScanRefer val-set and online test-split. Against the strongest box-based competitor EDA [32], ConcreteNet gains +4.27 points on val (46.53% vs. 42.26%) and +4.05 points on test (49.50% vs. 45.45%), while also producing dense masks instead of boxes. The gap is largest on the 'Unique' subset, where ConcreteNet reaches 82.05% Acc@50 versus 68.57% for EDA and 77.20% for M3DRef-CLIP [37], reflecting the benefit of kernel-based segmentation for precise localization. On the more challenging 'Multiple' subset (repetitive instances), ConcreteNet leads with 38.39% Acc@50, narrowly ahead of EDA (37.64%) and HAM [5] (34.03%), demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed BAF and contrastive loss for disambiguating same-class instances. Compared to other mask-outputting methods (TGNN [14], InstanceRefer [35]), ConcreteNet improves Overall Acc@50 by 16.8 and 13.6 points respectively, showing that the four proposed fusion improvements substantially close the gap between dense segmentation and detection-based grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConcreteNet builds directly on 3DVG-Transformer's [38] attentive verbo-visual fusion framework but replaces its 3D object detection backbone with a kernel-based 3D instance segmentation pipeline (DKNet [33] + DyCo3D [13]) to output dense masks rather than bounding boxes. The four proposed modules each address a specific gap identified in prior work: BAF introduces hard spherical locality where 3DVG-T and 3DJCG rely on global attention or soft distance encodings, the contrastive loss adapts CLIP-style verbo-visual contrast to the 3D grounding setting, the global camera token offers a lighter-weight alternative to MVT's [15] rotation-augmentation strategy for viewpoint sensitivity, and multi-view ensembling performs late aggregation on selected objects rather than early feature fusion. The paper positions dense 3D visual grounding as an underexplored extension of the well-studied box-based formulation, citing only TGNN [14] and InstanceRefer [35] as prior mask-level approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from bounding boxes to dense instance masks is practically significant for embodied applications (robot grasping, AR/VR interaction) where axis-aligned boxes insufficiently capture object geometry. The paper's diagnostic that kernel-based segmentation suffers from reduced latent-space separability for repetitive instances, and its targeted remedies, provide a useful template for improving grounding in any setting with class-level ambiguity. However, the large remaining gap between unique (82.05%) and multiple (38.39%) subsets at Acc@50 signals that disambiguating repetitive instances remains far from solved. The striking 7.3-point gap between learned GCT (43.84%) and direct camera input (51.18%) on the multiple subset highlights how much performance is left on the table by the ill-posed problem of inferring viewpoint from noisy, often view-independent annotations—an open question for future work.
