## Problem

Acquiring simulation-ready 3D tree models at scale is infeasible with current methods: LiDAR is expensive and cumbersome, developmental models require manual parameter tuning, and existing single-image 3D reconstruction approaches (e.g., Magic123, Zero123) produce unrealistic tree geometry due to inadequate tree-specific priors. No large-scale dataset of 3D reconstructed trees grounded in individual real trees has existed to enable ecological simulations such as carbon sequestration estimation or urban shadow modeling.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unposed RGB street-view image of a tree (from Google Street View) and a genus label as a text prompt. It produces a 3D simulation-ready tree model consisting of a detailed branching structure with foliage, generated by growing a genus-conditioned space colonization model within a reconstructed 3D envelope. The released dataset contains 600,000 such models spanning 23 North American cities and over 300 genus categories, each corresponding to a specific real tree in the Auto Arborist Dataset.

## Method

Tree-D Fusion distills scores from two tree-adapted diffusion priors to reconstruct a 3D tree envelope via Score Jacobian Chaining with Perturb-and-Average Scoring. The 2D prior is a Stable Diffusion 1.5 model fine-tuned with LoRA on ~1M real tree images from AAD using genus-conditioned BLIP captions; the 3D prior is a Zero123 model trained from scratch on 24,000 synthetic 3D trees (12 genera × 2,000 each) to be 3D-aware but genus-agnostic. An Instant-NGP NeRF is optimized by minimizing a combined loss of RGB/mask reconstruction (using SAM segmentation), the 2D diffusion prior, and the 3D diffusion prior over random viewpoints. The resulting 3D envelope is then populated with point markers, and a genus-conditioned space colonization algorithm (from Rhizomorph) grows the branching structure within the envelope, yielding a functional-structural tree model that can simulate growth over time and respond to environmental constraints.

## Results

On the San Jose subset of the Auto Arborist Dataset (4,400 trees, 133 genera), Tree-D Fusion achieves an ICTree score of 0.71±0.05, a 44.83% improvement over the next-best baseline (RBV at 0.67±0.12). In terms of geometric fidelity to real LiDAR scans, Tree-D Fusion attains a Chamfer Distance of 3.25×10⁻², a 32.62% reduction over the best baseline (DGauss at 4.25×10⁻²). For perceptual and semantic similarity, the method scores 0.54±0.06 LPIPS (20.21% better than RBV) and 0.63±0.07 CLIP-Similarity (45.34% better than RBV) across four rendered views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tree-D Fusion outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the tree-specific RBV [51] (the strongest baseline), it improves ICTree by 44.83%, LPIPS by 20.21%, CLIP-Similarity by 45.34%, and Chamfer Distance by 32.62%. The three general-purpose diffusion-based methods (Magic123, Zero123, DreamGaussian) cluster tightly around 0.45 ICTree and ~0.72 LPIPS, indicating they produce unrealistic, blob-like tree geometry that fails to capture branching structure. Notably, RBV achieves the highest ICTree score (0.75) on Cupressus specifically, but Tree-D Fusion surpasses it on the other four genera and overall. The Chamfer Distance comparison against real LiDAR scans (Table 4) confirms that Tree-D Fusion's 3D geometry is closest to ground truth, with a total CD of 3.25×10⁻² versus 4.25–5.18×10⁻² for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper specializes the Magic123 [92] single-image 3D reconstruction pipeline for trees by replacing its generic diffusion priors with tree-specific ones: a LoRA-adapted Stable Diffusion model trained on the Auto Arborist Dataset for the 2D prior, and a retrained Zero123 [56] model on synthetic trees for the 3D prior. The reconstructed 3D envelope is then completed by the genus-conditioned space colonization developmental model from Rhizomorph [52], which is the key departure from prior tree reconstruction work like RBV [51] that produces only static geometric proxies. The optimization framework follows Score Jacobian Chaining / DreamFusion [113, 84], and the overall pipeline—diffusion-prior envelope followed by a biologically-grounded growth model—is the paper's central architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Tree-D Fusion provides the first 600,000-tree dataset of simulation-ready 3D models grounded in real street-level imagery, enabling large-scale ecological simulations (carbon sequestration, shadow cover, urban heat island) that were previously infeasible due to the lack of phenotypical data at scale. The paper positions this dataset as a potential ImageNet-equivalent for forestry deep learning, standardizing benchmarking across 23 cities and 300+ genera. Key limitations include sensitivity to tree asymmetry (single-view reconstruction produces different 3D models from different angles), difficulty with high branch density and leaf-obscured structures, and the constraint that 3D shapes are bounded by the simulator's representational capacity. Open questions include whether multi-view or partially occluded training could resolve the asymmetry problem and whether the developmental model can be made more flexible to capture species-specific morphologies beyond the current genus-level conditioning.
