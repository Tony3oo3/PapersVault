## Problem

Scaling driving training data with real-world logs alone is impractical: the majority of driving data is repetitive with little learning signal, while deliberately collecting safety-critical long-tail scenarios is expensive and unsafe. Existing alternatives—hand-designed synthetic scenarios, adversarial optimization against a fixed policy, and symmetric multi-agent self-play—either produce unrealistic scenarios, fail to adapt as the policy improves, or converge to uneventful cooperative behavior. The paper asks how to automatically generate a curriculum of challenging, solvable, and realistic scenarios that scale beyond what real data provides.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a lane graph (K nodes) and per-actor 2D bird's-eye-view states (position, heading, velocity, bounding box, class) over H history timesteps, and outputs deterministic steering angle and acceleration per actor via a kinematic bicycle model. The teacher policy receives additional privileged inputs—an actor-type embedding and PairPose features to a designated target actor—that the student never observes, enabling it to coordinate adversarial behavior. For end-to-end autonomy deployment, the trained traffic agents interact with LiDAR-based perception and planning stacks simulated via LidarSim, producing full sensor-level training data.

## Method

The core mechanism is an asymmetric self-play game between a teacher and student multi-agent policy: the teacher maximizes a reward that encourages student collisions (challenging), penalizes its own collisions when controlling all actors (solvable), and regularizes both rollouts toward the real-data distribution via a Huber-loss-based KL term (realistic), while the student minimizes its own collision rate plus the same realism penalty. A viewpoint-invariant transformer with interleaved actor-to-map, actor-to-actor (using PairPose relative positional encodings), and actor-to-time attention layers encodes the scene and decodes per-actor actions. Two fair-play constraints prevent the teacher from exploiting its coordination advantage: a 3-player split into adversary and demonstrator sub-policies with identical architectures, and action replay that forces the teacher to solve the exact same adversarial trajectory the student faced. The paper proves that at equilibrium the student is α-β-optimal, meaning it solves every scenario that admits a reasonably realistic collision-free solution, and demonstrates zero-shot transfer where the trained teacher generates training scenarios for unseen end-to-end autonomy policies without retraining in expensive high-fidelity simulation.

## Results

On the SAFETY out-of-distribution set, the method achieves 8.16% collision rate, substantially outperforming all baselines (KING 12.65%, SMARTS 13.65%, TrafficSim 26.69%, Emb.Syn. 27.75%, Closed-loop IL 40.41%). On HIGHWAY, the method achieves 0.00% collision and 0.00% offroad while maintaining competitive FDE (5.76 m) and JSD (0.462). On ARGOVERSE2, it achieves the lowest collision rate (0.24%) and offroad rate (3.39%) among all methods, with FDE of 5.04 m and JSD of 0.433.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TrafficSim (closed-loop IL + differentiable collision prior), the method reduces SAFETY collisions from 26.69% to 8.16% and HIGHWAY collisions from 0.37% to 0.00%, while maintaining comparable FDE and JSD. Against KING (gradient-based adversarial scenario generation), the method achieves 8.16% vs 12.65% SAFETY collisions and 0.00% vs 1.42% HIGHWAY collisions, with the paper attributing KING's weaker performance to per-scenario optimization reaching local optima and less generalizable realism. Against SMARTS (multi-agent RL with handcrafted reward), the method dominates across all metrics, with SMARTS showing severe degradation in FDE (20.2 m vs 5.76 m on HIGHWAY) and offroad (17.2% vs 3.39% on ARGOVERSE2). Against Emb.Syn. (difficulty-based curation), the method achieves 8.16% vs 27.75% SAFETY collisions and 0.00% vs 4.34% HIGHWAY collisions, suggesting curation is ineffective at this dataset scale. The method trades a marginal increase in FDE (~0.06 m on HIGHWAY, ~0.09 m on ARGOVERSE2) relative to IL for dramatically lower collision rates, and is marginally worse than TrafficSim on HIGHWAY offroad (1.40% vs 1.39%) and ARGOVERSE2 offroad (3.39% vs 3.36%), but wins decisively on all collision metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts asymmetric self-play—originally proposed for single-agent goal discovery in robotic manipulation (OpenAI 2021, Sukhbaatar 2017)—to the multiagent driving domain, replacing the notion of a 'reachable goal state' with a 'solvable scenario' defined by teacher-student interaction. It departs from adversarial scenario generation methods (KING, Advsim) that optimize per-scenario with fixed solvability surrogates, instead using a jointly trained teacher whose solvability objective directly references the current student policy. The policy architecture builds on viewpoint-invariant transformers (GoRela, Scene Transformer) with factorized actor-to-map, actor-to-actor, and actor-to-time attention, while the training paradigm shifts from closed-loop imitation learning or MARL to a curriculum-emerging self-play loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core data bottleneck in autonomous driving: the inability to safely and cheaply collect long-tail safety-critical scenarios at scale. The zero-shot transfer of the teacher from lightweight bicycle-model simulation to high-fidelity sensor simulation for end-to-end autonomy policies is practically significant, as it decouples expensive scenario generation from the cost of the target policy. Open questions include controllability over the types of scenarios the teacher discovers, extension beyond collision to other failure modes (off-road, perception failures), and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse driving distributions. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) that equilibrium implies the student solves all reasonably realistic scenarios provides a clean convergence argument but relies on universal policy assumptions that may not hold in practice.
