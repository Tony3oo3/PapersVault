## Problem

Existing reference-free image captioning metrics such as CLIP-Score and PAC-Score rely on global image descriptors in a shared embedding space, which limits their ability to capture fine-grained visual details and penalize object hallucinations. Reference-based metrics like CIDEr, meanwhile, depend on human-written captions that may not cover all image content, leading to inaccurate penalties for valid but unmentioned details. BRIDGE addresses this by incorporating dense, fine-grained visual features directly into the evaluation process without requiring reference captions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a candidate caption as input and produces a single scalar evaluation score. Internally, the image is processed by a frozen CLIP vision encoder to extract a grid of patch-level features (e.g., 50×d for ViT-B/32), and the candidate caption is tokenized and encoded by the CLIP text encoder. The score is computed as a weighted combination of the standard CLIP-Score and the cosine similarity between a mean pseudo-caption embedding (which mixes template word tokens with dense visual vectors) and the candidate caption embedding.

## Method

BRIDGE augments a frozen CLIP dual-encoder with a learnable mapping module that converts fine-grained visual features into pseudo-language tokens. Template captions are generated by BLIP and then masked at noun-chunk positions; for each noun chunk, a separate masked replica is fed through the mapping module (a stack of Transformer encoder layers interleaved with cross-attention over the vision encoder's last-layer feature grid) to produce a visually enriched pseudo-token that replaces the mask. Training uses a weighted sum of three contrastive losses: L1 aligns each pseudo-caption with its image embedding, L2 aligns it with the ground-truth caption embedding, and L3 (a regularization branch with an MLP projection) enforces that each pseudo-caption corresponds to its specific noun chunk via a 'a photo of a <NOUNCHUNK>' prompt. At inference, all pseudo-captions for an image are averaged, and the final BRIDGE score is 0.5·[CLIP-Score(I,T) + w·max(cos(t*,t),0)], where t* is the mean pseudo-caption embedding and t is the candidate caption embedding.

## Results

On Flickr8k-Expert, BRIDGE (ViT-L/14) achieves the highest Kendall τc of 55.8 and Spearman ρ of 67.7 among all reference-free metrics, outperforming CLIP-S (53.0/64.7) and PAC-S (55.5/67.3). On Pascal-50S, BRIDGE (ViT-L/14) reaches 82.9% mean accuracy, surpassing PAC-S (82.2%) and CLIP-S (81.7%). On the FOIL hallucination-detection task, BRIDGE (ViT-L/14) achieves 93.0% accuracy, exceeding PAC-S (91.9%) and CLIP-S (90.9%) by +1.1 and +2.1 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRIDGE consistently outperforms CLIP-S across all three correlation datasets: with ViT-B/32, it gains +3.6 Kendall τc on Flickr8k-Expert (54.8 vs 51.2) and +14.0 Spearman ρ on Flickr8k-CF (44.5 vs 30.5); with ViT-L/14, the gap narrows to +2.8 τc on Flickr8k-Expert but persists on Composite (+1.8 τc, 57.2 vs 55.4). Against PAC-S, BRIDGE wins on Flickr8k-Expert (55.4 vs 55.1 τb with ViT-L/14) and Composite (52.9 vs 52.3 τb), but trails slightly on Flickr8k-CF (36.3 vs 36.8 τb). On Pascal-50S accuracy, BRIDGE (ViT-L/14) achieves 82.9%, beating PAC-S (82.2%) by +0.7 and CLIP-S (81.7%) by +1.2. On FOIL hallucination detection, BRIDGE (ViT-L/14) reaches 93.0% accuracy, a +1.1 point margin over PAC-S (91.9%) and +2.1 over CLIP-S (90.9%). Among reference-based metrics, BRIDGE (ViT-L/14) approaches but does not surpass RefPAC-S (55.5/55.9/67.6 on Flickr8k-Expert), confirming that reference captions still provide a modest advantage, while BRIDGE is the strongest reference-free option. Traditional n-gram metrics (BLEU-4, METEOR, CIDEr, SPICE) and text-only learned metrics (BERT-S, BERT-S++) are substantially outperformed, with BRIDGE exceeding CIDEr by +11.8 τb on Flickr8k-Expert.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRIDGE extends the reference-free captioning evaluation paradigm established by CLIP-Score (Hessel et al., 2021) and PAC-Score (Sarto et al., 2023), which rely on global CLIP embeddings to score image-caption pairs. The key departure is the introduction of a learnable mapping module, inspired by Pic2Word (Saito et al., 2023), that converts fine-grained visual grid features into pseudo-tokens injected into template captions, thereby moving beyond the global-alignment limitation of dual-encoder CLIP-based metrics. The template caption mechanism—using BLIP-generated sentences with noun chunks masked and then filled by the mapping network—is a novel architectural choice that bridges the gap between text-only and purely visual representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM-based captioners produce longer, more detailed, and stylistically diverse captions, evaluation metrics that depend on n-gram overlap or global CLIP similarity increasingly misalign with human judgment; BRIDGE's fine-grained visual injection addresses this gap in a reference-free setting, which is practically important given the cost of obtaining reference captions. The demonstrated robustness to template caption quality (even a basic Transformer captioner suffices) reduces the dependency on large-scale captioners. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video captioning, very long-form LLM outputs beyond COCO-style descriptions, and whether the mapping module can be made architecture-agnostic across different visual backbones without retraining.
