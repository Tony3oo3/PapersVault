## Problem

Existing text-to-image models generate images that emulate training distributions, lacking the creative combinatorial ability to fuse two unrelated objects into a single novel entity. Compositional T2I methods produce independent objects side-by-side rather than truly blended combinatorial creatures. This paper tackles Creative Text Pair-to-Object Generation: producing one creative object from two seemingly unrelated object texts, matching the quality of human artists like Les Créatonautes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241917/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two single-word object prompts (e.g., 'frog' and 'broccoli') formatted as 'A photo of [object]' and produces a single RGB image depicting a fused combinatorial object. The pipeline operates entirely training-free on top of a pretrained Stable-Diffusion2 (or SDXL turbo) generator, a CLIP ViT-L/14@336p encoder for distance computation, and SAM for segmentation. For each prompt pair, N=200 candidate images are generated and progressively filtered down to one optimal output, taking approximately 10 minutes on 4 RTX 4090 GPUs (or 40 seconds with SDXL turbo).

## Method

BASS (Balance Swap-Sampling) is a training-free, three-stage sampling procedure. First, a swapping mechanism randomly exchanges column vectors of the two prompt embeddings using binary vectors f ∈ {0,1}^w, producing N=200 novel images via the diffusion generator. Second, a balance region defined by CLIP distances constrains candidates: coarse sampling enforces semantic balance (|cos(I_f,p₁)−cos(I_f,p₂)| ≤ 0.05), and fine sampling enforces geometric balance via a hyperbola (distance-difference ≤ α) and ellipse (distance-sum ≤ 2β) in image space, reducing the pool to ~10 images. Third, SAM segments each candidate and the two anchor images, and the optimal image is selected by maximizing the average CLIP similarity between its segmented components and those of the originals, ensuring meaningful feature fusion from both source objects.

## Results

In the user study (1,060 votes from 106 users), BASS receives 658 votes (62%), substantially outperforming Bing (196, 18.5%), Stable-Diffusion2 (98, 9%), DALLE2 (59, 5%), and ERNIE-ViLG2 (49, 4.6%). On balance metrics, BASS achieves text-balance 0.061 and image-balance 0.126, both lower (better) than MagicMix's 0.100 and 0.220, while MagicMix edges out BASS on text-average similarity (0.282 vs 0.328, lower = more novel). On human-preference scores, BASS scores 0.200 PickScore and 0.242 HPSv2, closely rivaling the PickScore-selected (0.207/0.246) and HPSv2-selected (0.202/0.253) baselines despite requiring no human-preference training. In a separate study with 116 non-artists, 71.3% preferred BASS outputs over Les Créatonautes human artworks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MagicMix (the closest combinational method), BASS wins decisively on image-balance (0.126 vs 0.220) and text-balance (0.061 vs 0.100), indicating a more even fusion of both concepts, but loses slightly on text-average similarity (0.328 vs 0.282), meaning MagicMix produces marginally more novel (less similar to either parent) outputs; however, the paper argues MagicMix's visual results are less intuitive. Against PickScore and HPSv2 as selection strategies, BASS scores within 0.007 (PickScore) and 0.011 (HPSv2) of the best, demonstrating comparable human-preference value without any human-preference training. In the user study, BASS dominates all four T2I baselines by a wide margin: 658 votes vs Bing's 196, SD2's 98, DALLE2's 59, and ERNIE-ViLG2's 49. Against human artists (Les Créatonautes), 71.3% of 116 non-artist evaluators preferred BASS outputs, with the paper noting BASS produces more surprising fusions (e.g., owl-tiger blending owl body with tiger texture/feet/eyes) rather than simple head-swaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by framing 'Creative Text Pair-to-Object Generation' as distinct from compositional T2I (CDMs, CustomDiffusion), which produces images with independent objects rather than a single fused entity. The closest prior work, MagicMix, performs linear interpolation of embeddings to blend semantics, but the authors argue this yields unnatural overlays rather than genuine fusion. The method repurposes off-the-shelf components (Stable Diffusion 2, CLIP, SAM) in a novel inference-time pipeline: column-vector swapping of prompt embeddings creates a candidate pool, and a geometric 'balance region' (hyperbola + ellipse in CLIP distance space) selects high-quality combinations without any training. The creativity evaluation framework draws on Boden's novelty-surprise-value criteria, adapted to the T2I setting via balanced CLIP distances.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that creative combinatorial generation is achievable through purely inference-time embedding manipulation, bypassing the need for task-specific training or human-preference fine-tuning. This opens a lightweight path for creative content generation in entertainment, art design, and OOD image synthesis. However, the balance region can still produce chaotic or non-meaningful images (acknowledged in the limitations), and the evaluation relies on relatively small user studies (106–116 participants) and a limited set of prompt pairs. Open questions include whether the column-swap mechanism generalizes to more than two concepts, whether the geometric selection criterion can be made more principled, and whether the approach scales to more complex or multi-attribute prompts.
