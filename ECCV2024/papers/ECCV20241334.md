## Problem

Existing point cloud completion methods are trained on synthetic datasets like ShapeNet with limited categories, causing them to fail on unfamiliar objects and severely incomplete real-world scans. The core issue is that partial point clouds carry ambiguous or misleading semantic information, and the standard encoder-decoder paradigm cannot reliably recover global semantics from such degraded inputs, leading to poor generalization across categories and sensor types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud (arbitrary orientation, from LiDAR or RGB-D sensors) and an optional simple text prompt in the format 'a/an {category}' as input, producing a completed point cloud as output. The text prompt is not required but significantly improves performance on out-of-distribution objects; without it, the method still outperforms baselines. Training requires only a few hundred partial-complete pairs (e.g., 800 from ShapeNet55), and the pipeline handles arbitrary object orientations unlike the fixed-orientation convention in PCN.

## Method

ProtoComp disentangles completion into two stages: (1) controllable prototype generation and (2) prototype-guided geometric refinement. For prototype generation, a frozen Point-E diffusion model is augmented with a ControlNet-style trainable copy branch that fuses two conditions extracted from the partial input—a low-level vector (scale, position, orientation via max-pooled linear projection) added as a bias to denoising tokens, and a high-level feature list (multi-layer DGCNN-style graph convolutions with learnable position embeddings) injected via cross-attention. For geometric refinement, the generated prototype and the original partial are each encoded by graph-based convolutions (the prototype encoder updated via momentum), then merged through a cross-attention layer where the partial serves as query and the prototype as key/value, before feeding into any standard point cloud decoder (PCN or AdaPoinTr). This transfer-learning approach requires only ~800 training pairs and avoids retraining the base generator, preserving its open-vocabulary generation capacity.

## Results

On the standard PCN benchmark, ProtoComp with the AdaPoinTr decoder achieves 6.47 CD-ℓ1 and 0.847 F-Score@1%, setting a new state-of-the-art over AdaPoinTr (6.53, 0.845) and SeedFormer (6.74). On the novel Real-Sensors Benchmark (ScanNet200 + KITTI, ~1200 objects, 30 categories), ProtoComp with a simple text prompt achieves GD_C=40.8 and GD_I=43.6, substantially outperforming all baselines. Notably, without text guidance ProtoComp still reaches GD_C=29.8, while every existing method (PCN, TopNet, FoldingNet, SnowFlakeNet, PoinTr, AdaPoinTr) scores *below* the partial-input baseline (GD_C=29.6), indicating they degrade real-world completion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the PCN benchmark, ProtoComp with the AdaPoinTr decoder (6.47 CD-ℓ1) narrowly beats AdaPoinTr (6.53) and clearly surpasses SeedFormer (6.74), LAKeNet (7.23), and SnowFlakeNet (7.21); with the PoinTr decoder it scores 7.47, still competitive. On the Real-Sensors Benchmark the picture is stark: every existing completion method—PCN (GD_C=24.7), TopNet (14.0), FoldingNet (19.0), SnowFlakeNet (20.6), PoinTr (24.9), and AdaPoinTr (24.1)—performs *worse* than simply using the partial input without any completion (GD_C=29.6), meaning they actively degrade real-world geometry. ProtoComp without text (GD_C=29.8) roughly matches the partial baseline, but with a simple 'A {category}' text prompt it jumps to GD_C=40.8 and GD_I=43.6, a 10–29 point improvement over the strongest baseline (AdaPoinTr at 24.1/27.2). The Fidelity metric confirms ProtoComp stays close to the input (20.6×10⁻³) while PoinTr's zero fidelity reveals it discards the partial entirely. The ablation (Table 1c) further shows that the ControlNet-style side branch with zero-convolution initialization and copy-weight technique is critical: removing the condition module (Model A→B) drops GD_C from 56.4 to 44.7, and reducing category diversity in training data (Model E→F) drops GD_C from 47.5 to 39.8.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm for point cloud completion (PCN, PoinTr, AdaPoinTr) by disentangling the task into semantic recovery and geometric recovery. It repurposes the ControlNet architecture—originally designed for conditional 2D image diffusion—as a side-branch conditioning mechanism for the Point-E 3D point cloud diffusion model, adapting zero-convolution initialization and copy-branch training to the unordered, unstructured nature of point clouds. The graph-based condition encoder draws on DGCNN-style local geometric aggregation, while the geometric merging stage uses cross-attention to fuse prototype structure with partial input details, a design distinct from prior completion methods that rely solely on partial geometry for both semantic and structural cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical gap: existing completion methods, trained on synthetic ShapeNet data, actually perform worse than simply using the partial input on real-sensor data, undermining confidence in their real-world applicability. The proposed Real-Sensors Benchmark and Geometric Discriminability metric provide the field with a practical evaluation protocol for completion without ground-truth complete scans, which is the norm in real deployment. However, the high computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling, the restriction of the GD metric to 40 ModelNet categories, and the reliance on a single pre-trained generator (Point-E) limit immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether lighter-weight generative backbones or few-step sampling can preserve the cross-domain generalization gains, and whether the prototype-based decomposition generalizes beyond object-level completion to scene-level tasks.
