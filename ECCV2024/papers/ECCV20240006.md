## Problem

Learning a generative model of articulated 3D animal motions has been largely unexplored because existing 3D motion synthesis methods rely on expensive 3D scans, parametric shape models, multi-view captures, or manual pose annotations that are impractical to collect for most animal species. This paper addresses the challenge of learning such a motion distribution purely from raw, unlabeled monocular internet videos, where each clip shows a unique instance with unknown shape, pose, and viewpoint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240006/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes unlabeled monocular video clips (256×256, 30 fps, T=10 frames) of a single animal category, preprocessed with PointRend segmentation, occlusion removal, and optical-flow-based motion filtering. At inference, a single 2D image of an unseen animal instance is fed in, and the model outputs a rest-pose 3D mesh with texture, a sequence of articulated bone rotations (quadruped skeleton), and a rendered 4D animation within seconds. The motion VAE latent space also allows free sampling of diverse 3D motion sequences independent of any specific image.

## Method

The core contribution is a Video Photo-Geometric Auto-Encoding framework built around a spatio-temporal transformer that decomposes each video clip into a rest-pose SDF shape (via DMTet), an image-conditioned deformation field, a texture MLP, and a motion latent code. A spatial transformer encoder fuses bone-specific DINO-ViT feature descriptors per frame into a pose feature, and a temporal transformer encoder maps the full pose-feature sequence to VAE distribution parameters; symmetric decoders recover the articulated pose sequence, which drives linear-blend skinning of the mesh for differentiable re-rendering. Training is fully self-supervised via RGB/mask reconstruction losses, DINO feature-field correspondence in canonical space, KL regularization, and temporal smoothness, with no pose annotations or shape templates. A two-stage schedule first pre-trains a single-image pose predictor on video clips for stable shape learning, then swaps in the motion VAE with a teacher loss from the first-stage predictions to accelerate convergence.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC, Ponymation achieves 48.0% PCK and 71.8% Mask IoU for single-image 3D reconstruction, outperforming the closest baseline MagicPony (42.8% PCK, 64.1% Mask IoU). On APT-36K, it reaches 59.9% PCK and 49.1% Velocity Error, again surpassing MagicPony (53.9% PCK, 57.3% Vel. Err.). For 4D motion generation, Ponymation achieves a Motion Strength of 4.66 (vs. 0.29 for 4D-fy) and is preferred by users 83.0% of the time in a side-by-side comparison, while generating animations in seconds versus 12 hours per instance for 4D-fy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ponymation outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the most direct prior work, MagicPony [78], it improves PCK by 5.2 points on PASCAL (48.0% vs. 42.8%), Mask IoU by 7.7 points (71.8% vs. 64.1%), PCK by 6.0 points on APT-36K (59.9% vs. 53.9%), and reduces Velocity Error by 8.2 points (49.1% vs. 57.3%), attributed to training on video sequences rather than independent images. Against the older canonical-surface-mapping baselines CSM [40] (31.2% PCK), UMR [43] (24.4% PCK), and A-CSM [39] (32.9% PCK), Ponymation leads by 15–24 PCK points on PASCAL. In the 4D motion generation comparison, Ponymation dominates 4D-fy [8] with a Motion Strength of 4.66 versus 0.29 (a 16× difference) and an 83% user preference rate, while also being orders of magnitude faster (seconds vs. 12 hours per instance).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MagicPony's static articulated 3D animal reconstruction (from single images with masks and DINO features) into the temporal domain, learning a generative VAE over articulated pose sequences from monocular video clips. It draws on the human motion synthesis literature (SMPL-based VAEs, transformer motion encoders like MotionGPT) but removes the dependency on parametric shape models and pose annotations by using a coarse quadruped skeleton heuristic and self-supervised DINO-ViT features for cross-instance correspondences. The video photo-geometric auto-encoding framework unifies SDF-based shape representation (via DMTet), linear-blend skinning, and differentiable rendering into an end-to-end pipeline trained solely on 2D reconstruction losses, departing from NRSfM and LASR-style optimization approaches that handle individual videos without learning a motion distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to learn a generative model of 3D animal motions purely from unlabeled Internet videos, enabling feedforward 4D animation from a single image in seconds—a capability with direct applications in game design, film production, and computational biology. Key limitations include the reliance on a fixed quadruped skeleton (limiting generalization to non-quadruped species), small training datasets for non-horse categories (requiring fine-tuning from the horse model), and the need for a brief appearance fine-tuning step at test time. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more diverse body plans, longer and more complex motion sequences, and whether the learned motion latent space supports meaningful semantic conditioning (e.g., text-driven animal behavior).
