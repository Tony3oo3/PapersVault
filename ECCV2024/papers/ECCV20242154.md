## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models rely on CLIP as the text encoder, which is limited to English-only input, a 77-token maximum context length, and relatively small model capacity. While Large Language Models offer multilingual support, longer context, and superior text representation, their features lack visual awareness and training a diffusion model with LLMs from scratch demands prohibitive computational resources (e.g., DALL-E 3 requires 1.2B images over 500K iterations).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242154/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts free-form text prompts in multiple languages (English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, French, German, Spanish, Russian, etc.) with no fixed token-length cap, unlike CLIP's 77-token truncation. Prompts longer than 77 tokens are handled via segmented encoding, preserving full contextual information. The output is a standard RGB image generated through a latent diffusion process, with no special structural constraints beyond typical diffusion model resolutions.

## Method

OmniDiffusion introduces a lightweight 4-layer encoder-decoder transformer adapter with 227 learnable queries, appended to a frozen Baichuan2-7B LLM, to bridge the gap between LLM text features and CLIP's visual-textual embedding space. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) multilingual textual alignment, where only the adapter is optimized via a cosine-similarity loss plus a magnitude-constraint term to match CLIP token-level feature norms, using segmented 77-token chunking to handle long prompts; (2) end-to-end text-image training on 43M pairs where the adapter and SD-XL's UNet are jointly optimized with the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss while the LLM remains frozen; and (3) high-aesthetic finetuning on 40K curated images with descriptive captions to boost visual quality. The entire pipeline runs on 128 A800 GPUs for approximately 2 days, a fraction of the cost of training from scratch.

## Results

On English benchmarks, OmniDiffusion achieves the best CLIP-Score on COCO-en (0.2463) and the best FID on Flickr-30k-en (78.16), while on Chinese benchmarks it attains the best CLIP-Score on COCO-cn (0.3418). Most notably, OmniDiffusion substantially outperforms both baselines on the aesthetics score across all four benchmarks, reaching 6.80–7.03 versus 4.98–5.49 for competitors. On FID, OmniDiffusion is competitive but slightly behind Taiyi-Diffusion on COCO-en (22.38 vs. 18.59) and Flickr-8k-cn (83.53 vs. 78.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Taiyi-Diffusion, OmniDiffusion wins on CLIP-Score for COCO-en (0.2463 vs. 0.2305) and COCO-cn (0.3418 vs. 0.3389), and on FID for Flickr-30k-en (78.16 vs. 80.66), but trails on FID for COCO-en (22.38 vs. 18.59) and Flickr-8k-cn (83.53 vs. 78.00). Against Alt-Diffusion, OmniDiffusion dominates on FID across all four benchmarks (e.g., 22.38 vs. 33.20 on COCO-en) and on CLIP-Score for COCO-en and COCO-cn, while Alt-Diffusion edges ahead on CLIP-Score for Flickr-8k-cn (0.3476 vs. 0.3326). The most consistent advantage is in aesthetics: OmniDiffusion scores 6.62–7.03 versus 4.98–5.49 for both baselines, a gap of roughly 1.3–1.5 points. Qualitatively, the paper also compares against SD-v1.5 and SD-XL (via machine-translated prompts in Fig. 4), where OmniDiffusion produces more realistic textures and better prompt adherence, particularly on multi-person and fine-detail scenes. Human evaluation (Fig. 5) confirms OmniDiffusion is consistently preferred over both Taiyi-Diffusion and Alt-Diffusion in pairwise comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion framework (LDM/SD-XL) by replacing the CLIP text encoder with an LLM (Baichuan2-7B) connected through a lightweight 4-layer encoder-decoder adapter, positioning itself against prior multilingual T2I approaches (CogView, Taiyi-Diffusion, AltDiffusion) that either train bilingual encoders from scratch or rely on machine translation. Conceptually, it draws on the adapter/projector paradigm from multimodal LLM work (LLaVA, MiniGPT-4) but inverts the direction: rather than projecting visual features into LLM space, it projects LLM text features into CLIP's image-text joint embedding space. The three-stage training pipeline (alignment → end-to-end → aesthetic finetuning) is a practical departure from prior work that typically trains T2I models monolithically, and the segmented encoding trick to handle prompts beyond CLIP's 77-token limit is a novel engineering detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniDiffusion provides a resource-efficient recipe (128 A800 GPUs, ~2 days) for upgrading existing diffusion models with LLM-level language understanding, multilingual support, and long-context handling—capabilities that previously required training from scratch at DALL-E-3 scale. This makes LLM-powered T2I generation accessible to smaller research groups and opens the door to applying the same adapter paradigm to other modalities (video, 3D) or larger LLMs. Key limitations include dependence on CLIP's embedding space as the alignment target (which may cap the expressiveness gain), evaluation on only English and Chinese benchmarks, and the use of a single 7B LLM; whether the approach scales to much larger LLMs or benefits from iterative alignment remains open.
