## Problem

Adapting large pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP to downstream tasks via prompt tuning suffers from overfitting and catastrophic forgetting, and existing regularization solutions grow increasingly complex with high storage and inference costs. This paper identifies that quantization error can serve as a controllable form of noise that simultaneously regularizes the model for better generalization and drastically reduces parameter storage, a dual benefit unexplored in prior work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a ViT-B/16 encoder into patch tokens) and a text prompt (e.g., 'A photo of a [CLS]') as input, producing a class label via cosine similarity between the image [CLS] feature and projected text features. The tunable prompts are stored not as 16-bit floats but as b-bit indices (b=1, 2, or 4) into a small K-Means codebook, reducing storage from 16N bits to bN + 2^b × 16 bits — roughly 16× smaller at b=1.

## Method

The core mechanism quantizes the tunable prompts of a vision-language model using K-Means clustering, treating the resulting quantization error as beneficial regularization noise rather than an artifact to minimize. Three design principles guide the algorithm: (1) normalization/denormalization before and after quantization to absorb the rapidly changing variance of prompt weights during training; (2) Constrained Adaptive Clustering (CAC), which re-clusters only when the KL divergence between current and cached prompt weight distributions exceeds a threshold, avoiding wasteful re-clustering given the gentle weight updates observed in practice; and (3) Straight-Through Estimator for gradient propagation through the non-differentiable quantization step. The approach is integrated as QAT into existing prompt-tuning frameworks (CoOp, MaPLe), yielding QCoOp at 0.26KB and QMaPLe at 1774KB, both outperforming their unquantized counterparts on unseen-class accuracy while being orders of magnitude smaller.

## Results

On the base-to-new generalization benchmark (average over 11 datasets), QCoOp achieves 77.43% harmonic mean accuracy with only 0.26KB of additional parameters, outperforming all lightweight competitors. When integrated into MaPLe, QMaPLe reaches 79.12% harmonic mean with 1774KB (vs. 7096KB for original MaPLe), a 0.57% accuracy gain at 4× smaller size. In domain generalization, QCoOp attains 60.20% average accuracy across ImageNet variants, surpassing CoCoOp (59.91%) and ProGrad (59.07%) while using 272× fewer parameters than CoCoOp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QCoOp (0.26KB) decisively outperforms all lightweight baselines in harmonic mean: it beats CoOp (71.66%) by 5.77 points, CoCoOp (75.83%) by 1.60 points, Adapter (76.35%) by 1.08 points, LoRA (74.86%) by 2.57 points, and ProGrad (75.00%) by 2.43 points, while being 16×, 272×, 4043×, 992×, and 63× smaller respectively. Against the zero-shot CLIP baseline, QCoOp gains 5.73 points in harmonic mean. In the heavier comparison, QMaPLe (1774KB) surpasses the original MaPLe (7096KB) by 0.57 points in harmonic mean (79.12 vs. 78.55) while reducing storage to roughly 25% of the original. In domain generalization, QCoOp (60.20% avg) edges out CoCoOp (59.91%) and ProGrad (59.07%) with far fewer parameters, and in cross-dataset transfer it wins on 5 of 10 target datasets with the best average (65.93%). The consistent pattern across all four evaluation settings is that quantization-based regularization improves unseen-class accuracy without sacrificing seen-class performance, yielding a better specialization–generalization trade-off than any non-quantized competitor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CoOp (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) and MaPLe (Khattak et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced prompt tuning for vision-language models to adapt CLIP to downstream tasks. The key conceptual departure is from the quantization literature: while prior quantization work (both PTQ and QAT) treats quantization error as something to minimize, this paper reframes quantization error as a controllable form of noise that actively regularizes the model against overfitting and catastrophic forgetting. It also departs from prior prompt-tuning regularization approaches (e.g., ProGrad, CoCoOp's conditional prompts) by achieving regularization through the quantization mechanism itself rather than through additional losses or architectural constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: QCoOp achieves state-of-the-art harmonic mean accuracy (77.43%) with only 0.26KB of additional parameters, making it feasible for extremely resource-constrained deployment. The method is modular and can be plugged into existing prompt-tuning frameworks (demonstrated with MaPLe), broadening its applicability. However, the exploration is limited to ViT-B/16 and a single quantization strategy (K-Means with CAC); whether the 'moderate noise' principle generalizes to larger backbones, different quantization schemes, or non-prompt parameters remains open. The finding that 1-bit quantization often outperforms 4-bit for prompts is intriguing but underexplored mechanistically.
