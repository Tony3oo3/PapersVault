## Problem

Existing generic event boundary detection (GEBD) methods apply a single fixed protocol to all boundaries regardless of their distinctive characteristics and detection difficulties. Low-level boundaries like shot changes are easily identifiable from shallow appearance features, while high-level boundaries like sub-action changes require deep spatiotemporal modeling; treating them uniformly wastes computation on easy cases and may obscure the coarse-level signals needed for simple boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241137/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T uniformly sampled frames (T=100 for Kinetics-GEBD) from a video and processes them through a shared ResNet50 backbone, unfolding the resulting temporal feature sequence into local centered windows of t_w=17 frames for each candidate timestamp. Output is a per-frame continuous confidence score (sigmoid) over the full sequence; boundaries are extracted as local maxima exceeding a threshold ε, with the dynamic architecture allowing different frames to produce their final prediction at different backbone depths rather than all traversing the full network.

## Method

DyBDet introduces a multi-exit dynamic network where L boundary detectors are attached at varying depths of a shared ResNet50 backbone, coupled with a partial exit criterion that only drops features in a local neighborhood around already-detected boundaries (controlled by t_μ), leaving the rest to continue through deeper layers. Each detector comprises a multi-order difference encoder (MDE) that decomposes temporal features into 0th-, 1st-, and 2nd-order differences via depth-wise convolutions, and a pairwise contrast module (PCM) that computes cosine similarity maps within the local window to amplify diagonal boundary patterns. Crucially, the three detectors use different order combinations (Det.1: 0th order only, Det.2: +1st, Det.3: +1st+2nd), enabling shallow detectors to capture appearance-level changes while deep detectors model complex action-level transitions. The model is trained end-to-end with weighted binary cross-entropy on Gaussian-smoothed soft labels, and the partial exit mechanism with repeat-padding of exited positions preserves local continuity for subsequent detectors.

## Results

On Kinetics-GEBD, DyBDet achieves F1@0.05 of 79.6% and average F1 of 89.0%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art SC-Transformer (77.7% / 88.1%) by 1.9% and 0.9% respectively. With a stronger CSN backbone, DyBDet further reaches 83.1% F1@0.05 and 91.0% average. On TAPOS, DyBDet attains 62.5% F1@0.05 and 74.7% average, outperforming SC-Transformer (61.8% / 74.2%) by 0.7% and 0.5%. In terms of efficiency, DyBDet achieves a 3.2% F1@0.05 improvement over DDM-Net with 11.3× fewer FLOPs, and 4.7% improvement over Temporal Perceiver under the same computational budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyBDet outperforms all 11 baselines on Kinetics-GEBD and all 9 on TAPOS. Against the closest competitor SC-Transformer [25], DyBDet gains +1.9% F1@0.05 on Kinetics-GEBD (79.6 vs 77.7) and +0.7% on TAPOS (62.5 vs 61.8). DDM-Net [38], the previous best in the dense-difference family, is surpassed by +3.2% F1@0.05 on Kinetics-GEBD while requiring 11.3× fewer FLOPs. Temporal Perceiver [37] is outperformed by +4.8% F1@0.05 on Kinetics-GEBD and +7.3% on TAPOS, and under matched compute budgets DyBDet still leads by 4.7%. Earlier methods—BMN [28], TCN [23], PC [35], SBoCo [21], CVRL [26], ISBA [7], CTM [16], and TransParser [33]—trail by 5–60+ points in F1@0.05, reflecting the gap between older fixed-protocol detectors and the proposed dynamic architecture. Notably, the paper shows that even when all frames exit at the first (shallowest) detector, performance remains competitive, underscoring that the dynamic allocation itself is the key driver of gains rather than simply deeper features.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyBDet extends the GEBD task established by Shou et al. (ICCV 2021) by introducing a dynamic multi-exit architecture, departing from prior methods (SC-Transformer, DDM-Net, Temporal Perceiver) that process all boundaries through a fixed, uniform pipeline. The multi-order difference encoder draws on ConvMixer-style local convolutions and the pairwise contrast module builds on the self-similarity matrix ideas from SBoCo (Kang et al., CVPR 2022) and DDM-Net (Tang et al., CVPR 2022), but recombines them with temporal differences of varying orders to enable boundary-type discrimination. Conceptually, the paper repurposes the dynamic neural network literature (GateNet, MSDNet, dynamic routing) for a fine-grained temporal detection task, where the 'sample' is a local video snippet rather than an entire image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain (11.3× fewer FLOPs than DDM-Net at higher accuracy, and 0.84 ms vs. 25.75 ms latency) makes GEBD more deployable in real-time long-form video understanding pipelines. The finding that shot changes are resolvable with shallow appearance features while sub-action changes require high-order temporal differences validates the intuition that a one-size-fits-all protocol is suboptimal, but the broader question of whether this dynamic allocation generalizes beyond the two evaluated datasets and to other temporal localization tasks (action detection, segmentation) remains open. The paper also notes that the dynamic approach's benefit depends on the backbone being jointly trained with detectors, suggesting that pre-trained backbones may not transfer as cleanly to the multi-exit setting.
