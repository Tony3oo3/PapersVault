## Problem

In industrial anomaly detection, a single view of an object may fail to reveal defects—shiny surfaces hide anomalies under certain lighting, and 3D structural defects are invisible in RGB. Existing multi-modality methods (e.g., M3DM, BTF) store and compare 2D and 3D features separately without enforcing cross-view consistency, and no comprehensive approach fuses multiple lighting views with 3D normal geometry in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240880/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of seven views: six RGB images captured under distinct lighting conditions and one 3D normal map, all at the same spatial resolution. The method produces a fused pixel-level anomaly score map (average of RGB and NMap score maps) and an image-level score computed as the mean of the top-3 pixel scores. Because both score maps originate from the same view-invariant ControlNet, no cross-modality alignment is required, unlike prior dual-memory approaches.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes a pretrained Stable Diffusion UNet in a view-agnostic manner, then trains a view-invariant ControlNet via mutual-conditioning: Phase-I feeds each RGB view as input with the 3D normal as condition, while Phase-II swaps the roles, and a feature consistency loss (L_F) enforces blockwise feature-map agreement across all six lighting views. DDIM inversion at t=80 replaces stochastic noise injection to yield deterministic latent trajectories, enabling stable construction of two memory banks (RGB and NMap) from decoder block-1 features. At inference, per-pixel nearest-neighbor search in the respective memory bank produces two score maps that are averaged to form the final anomaly map. The key insight is that a diffusion model's internal decoder features, made view-invariant through ControlNet training, serve as rich multi-modal embeddings for embedding-based anomaly detection.

## Results

On the Eyecandies dataset (primary), the method achieves a mean Img-AUROC of 0.948 (3D+RGB), surpassing the strongest baseline MMRD (0.940) by 0.8% and 3DSR (0.909) by 3.9%. At the pixel level, it reaches 0.941 AUPRO, edging out MMRD (0.936). On MVTec 3D-AD, the method scores 0.949 mean Img-AUROC (3D+RGB), placing it just below the reconstruction-based CFM (0.960) and MMRD (0.950) but above all embedding-based baselines (M3DM 0.945, SG 0.945, BTF 0.873).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Eyecandies (3D+RGB), the proposed method (0.948) outperforms all eight baselines: MMRD (0.940, −0.8%), 3DSR (0.909, −3.9%), M3DM (0.897, −5.1%), CFM (0.881, −6.7%), EasyNet (0.869, −7.9%), and BTF (0.821, −12.7%). The margin over MMRD is modest but consistent across most categories, with the largest gap on Hazelnut Truffle (0.738 vs. 0.747) being the sole category where MMRD edges ahead. On MVTec 3D-AD (3D+RGB), the method (0.949) trails the two reconstruction-based approaches—CFM (0.960) and MMRD (0.950)—by 1.1% and 0.1% respectively, while clearly surpassing all embedding-based baselines (M3DM 0.945, SG 0.945, BTF 0.873). The paper notes that MMRD and CFM are class-dependent and CFM requires separate backbones for 2D and 3D, whereas the proposed method uses a single unified diffusion backbone. In single-modality settings, the method also leads: 0.888 Img-AUROC for 3D-only (vs. 3DSR 0.776) and 0.888 for RGB-only (vs. DSR 0.898, a near-tie).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the embedding-based anomaly detection paradigm (PatchCore, PADiM, BTF, M3DM) by repurposing the internal decoder features of a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as anomaly-detection embeddings, rather than using diffusion for reconstruction or generation. It introduces a view-invariant ControlNet trained with a feature-consistency loss and mutual RGB/3D-normal conditioning, which departs from prior multi-modality fusion methods (M3DM, CFM, MMRD) that rely on separate backbones or explicit alignment between modalities. The deterministic DDIM inversion for memory-bank construction is a practical departure from the stochastic forward diffusion process used in prior diffusion-based AD methods (AnoDDPM, DiffusionAD).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The view-invariant ControlNet with mutual cross-modality conditioning offers a general recipe for fusing heterogeneous views of the same object without requiring separate per-modality networks or explicit feature alignment, which could transfer to other multi-view/multi-modal perception tasks beyond anomaly detection. However, the approach is validated only on Eyecandies (7 fixed views) and MVTec 3D-AD (single view), leaving open whether the feature-consistency loss and mutual-conditioning strategy generalize to arbitrary numbers of views, different modality combinations, or real-world industrial settings with uncontrolled acquisition. The modest margins over strong baselines (e.g., 0.948 vs. 0.940 Img-AUROC on Eyecandies) suggest the gains from diffusion-based embeddings over well-tuned CNN/ViT features may be limited in this regime.
