## Problem

Existing unsupervised bias mitigation methods for vision models either require group annotations during training or validation, or depend on expensive hyperparameter searches. The paper identifies a fundamental issue: without group labels, mean-accuracy-based model selection systematically rejects debiased models in favor of biased ones (the 'Price of Unawareness'), causing many existing methods to fail in practice. This paper seeks a bias mitigation pipeline that is hyperparameter-free and requires no group information at any stage.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard supervised training set of RGB images with class labels (no group annotations) and produces a robust classifier mapping images to class labels. It is evaluated on binary (Waterbirds, CelebA), multi-class (BAR with 6 classes, cMNIST with 10 classes), and fine-grained (CUB with 200 classes) image classification tasks. The only additional data consumed beyond the standard (image, label) pairs is the per-sample loss trajectory recorded during a helper model's training, which is used internally for clustering and augmentation.

## Method

TAB (Targeted Augmentations for Bias Mitigation) is a four-step, hyperparameter-free pipeline. First, a helper model is trained via standard ERM while recording each sample's loss at every epoch, producing a loss-history embedding vector per sample. Second, within each class, k-means (k=2) clusters these loss histories to partition samples into a majority (bias-aligned) and minority (bias-conflicting) pseudo-group, exploiting the observation that bias-conflicting samples exhibit systematically different loss trajectories. Third, the minority group is upsampled via random duplication to match the majority group's size, yielding a group-balanced augmented training set. Fourth, a new model is trained from scratch on this augmented set. The key insight is that the full training history preserves discriminative signal about spurious correlations that a single training snapshot (as used by JTT or LfF) loses, and that simple upsampling to group balance eliminates the need for any weighting hyperparameter or model selection step.

## Results

TAB achieves the best worst-group accuracy (WGA) among all unsupervised methods across all six tasks, reaching 81.85% on Even-Odd (p=99%), 63.26% on cMNIST (p=98%), 55.92% on Waterbirds, and 38.94% on BAR, while maintaining competitive mean accuracy (e.g., 94.98% on Even-Odd, 97.52% on Waterbirds). Crucially, TAB's Price of Unawareness (PoU) is exactly 1.0000 on every task, meaning its WGA is completely unaffected by the absence of group labels during model selection, whereas competing methods suffer PoU values up to 5.26 (LfF on Even-Odd) and 2.39 (MaskTune on BAR). On the unbiased CUB dataset, TAB still improves worst-class accuracy over ERM (18.89% vs. 16.67%) while retaining 72.98% mean accuracy, confirming safe deployment when biases are unknown.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ERM, TAB consistently improves WGA by 5–11 points (e.g., 55.92% vs. 44.86% on Waterbirds, 38.94% vs. 29.56% on BAR) while matching or exceeding mean accuracy on most tasks. Against JTT, TAB wins on WGA in every task (e.g., 55.92% vs. 44.50% on Waterbirds, 63.26% vs. 57.21% on cMNIST) and achieves a PoU of 1.00 vs. JTT's 1.36 on Waterbirds, indicating JTT's hyperparameter search selects biased models. Against LfF, TAB substantially outperforms on Even-Odd (81.85% vs. 2.97% WGA) and cMNIST (63.26% vs. 48.45%), ties on CelebA (40.00%), and leads on Waterbirds (55.92% vs. 51.14%); LfF's PoU reaches 5.26 on Even-Odd, showing its hyperparameter selection is highly unreliable without group labels. Against MaskTune, TAB dominates on WGA across all tasks (e.g., 63.26% vs. 13.94% on cMNIST, 38.94% vs. 17.61% on BAR) and also achieves higher mean accuracy on BAR (61.11% vs. 50.66%). Against G-DRO (the supervised upper bound using group labels), TAB trails on Waterbirds (55.92% vs. 68.54%) and CelebA (40.00% vs. 85.74%), but surprisingly surpasses G-DRO on the highly imbalanced synthetic tasks Even-Odd (81.85% vs. 57.66%) and cMNIST (63.26% vs. 59.29%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TAB extends the unsupervised bias mitigation line initiated by Just Train Twice (JTT) and Learning from Failure (LfF), both of which use a biased helper model to identify bias-conflicting samples for upweighting. The key conceptual departure is replacing a single training snapshot (JTT's fixed stopping epoch) or a jointly-learned biasing loss (LfF) with the model's entire loss-history trajectory, clustered per-class via k-means to discover pseudo-groups. This enables a fully hyperparameter-free pipeline that uses data augmentation (upsampling the minority cluster) rather than loss reweighting, sidestepping the model-selection fragility that the paper formalizes through its new Price of Unawareness (PoU) metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAB addresses a critical practical gap: existing unsupervised debiasing methods require either group-annotated validation sets or expensive hyperparameter searches, making them infeasible in real-world pipelines where biases are unknown. The PoU analysis reveals that these methods can actually select models worse than plain ERM when group labels are absent, a finding that reframes how the community should evaluate unsupervised BM pipelines. Limitations include the need for two training passes, O(NT) loss-history storage, and k-means scalability concerns; open questions include whether richer embeddings (combining loss history with semantics), more than two clusters, or symmetry-invariant augmentations can further improve performance on highly imbalanced tasks like CelebA.
