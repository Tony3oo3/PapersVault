## Problem

Vision Transformers produce feature maps with persistent grid-like noise artifacts that degrade performance on dense prediction tasks such as segmentation, depth estimation, and object discovery. These artifacts are traced to positional embeddings at the input stage but have been largely overlooked because they do not affect classification accuracy. The paper asks whether these artifacts can be effectively removed from pre-trained ViTs without retraining the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242287/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw patch-level feature maps from any pre-trained ViT (e.g., DINOv2, DeiT-III, CLIP, EVA02) at their native resolution (patch sizes 14 or 16). In the first stage, multiple randomly augmented views (crops, flips, resizes) of the same image are processed to learn the decomposition; in the second stage, the generalizable denoiser requires only a single forward pass of the ViT plus one lightweight transformer block at inference. The output is a denoised feature map of the same spatial and channel dimensions as the raw ViT output, suitable for direct use in downstream dense prediction heads.

## Method

DVT decomposes ViT outputs into three terms: an input-dependent semantics field F, an input-independent position-dependent artifact field G, and a residual Δ, learned via neural fields (coordinate networks) that enforce cross-view feature invariance and artifact consistency. The per-image optimization minimizes a regularized reconstruction loss with stop-gradient operators to prevent trivial solutions, first training F and G with a distance loss, then jointly training F and a residual network h with sparsity regularization on Δ. The resulting per-image denoised features serve as pseudo-labels to train a generalizable denoiser—a single transformer block with additional learnable positional embeddings applied post-ViT—which predicts clean features from raw outputs in one feedforward pass, adding negligible latency and generalizing to unseen data.

## Results

DVT consistently improves all six evaluated ViTs across dense prediction tasks. On VOC2012 semantic segmentation, the largest gains are seen for DINO (+3.22 mIoU, 63.00→66.22) and DeiT-III (+2.74 mIoU, 70.62→73.36), while the strongest absolute performance is achieved by DINOv2+DVT at 84.84 mIoU, surpassing the DINOv2-giant (83.0 mIoU) with a 13× smaller model. On ADE20K, DeiT-III+DVT shows the largest relative improvement (+3.84 mIoU, 32.73→36.57). For object discovery (LOST corloc), DINOv2+DVT achieves a dramatic +27.2 improvement on VOC2007 (30.8→58.0), exceeding the DINOv2-reg results reported in [6].

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVT is applied as a plug-in denoiser on top of six pre-trained ViTs, and in every case it improves the base model without retraining the backbone. The largest absolute gains appear for DINO (+3.22 mIoU on VOC2012) and DeiT-III (+2.74 mIoU on VOC2012, +3.84 on ADE20K), suggesting that artifacts are more pronounced in smaller or differently-trained models. For the strongest baselines, DINOv2+DVT reaches 84.84 mIoU on VOC2012, exceeding the DINOv2-giant (83.0) and DINOv2-reg (83.64) with only a single lightweight Transformer block added. DINOv2-reg+DVT further improves over DINOv2-reg by +0.86 mIoU (VOC2012) and +1.12 (ADE20K), confirming that even register-augmented ViTs retain residual artifacts. On object discovery (LOST corloc), DINOv2+DVT jumps from 30.8 to 58.0 on VOC2007 (+27.2), surpassing the DINOv2-reg numbers reported in [6] (55.4), while DINOv2-reg+DVT also improves from 38.0 to 56.1 (+18.1). The only minor regression is DeiT-III on NYUv2 depth (δ₁ drops from 72.16 to 71.36), though its abs rel metric also worsens slightly, indicating a small trade-off on that specific task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the concurrent DINOv2-reg work [6], which first identified 'high-norm' patches as artifacts in large ViTs and proposed register tokens as a fix requiring full retraining. DVT departs from this by attributing artifacts to positional embeddings (a design-level cause present in all ViTs regardless of size) rather than model scale, and by using neural fields—a technique borrowed from 3D reconstruction (NeRF, Instant-NGP)—to perform per-image feature decomposition without touching the pre-trained backbone. The lightweight denoiser (a single transformer block) is then trained on the per-image outputs, creating a plug-and-play module that contrasts with the retraining-from-scratch paradigm of [6].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identifying positional embeddings as the root cause of grid-like artifacts across all ViT training paradigms (supervised, self-supervised, contrastive) challenges a foundational design choice and opens the question of whether positional encodings should be rethought in next-generation architectures. The method's practicality—no backbone retraining, only 10k denoised samples to train a single-block denoiser—makes it immediately adoptable for any existing ViT deployment. Open questions include whether the approximate three-term factorization (f + g + h) is the correct decomposition, whether the denoiser generalizes to substantially different domains (e.g., medical imaging, remote sensing), and whether redesigning positional embeddings at the architecture level could eliminate the need for post-hoc denoising entirely.
