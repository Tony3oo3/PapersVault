## Problem

Existing parametric head models face a fundamental trade-off: mesh-based 3DMMs are constrained by fixed topology and cannot capture complex details like hairstyles or accessories, SDF-based models recover geometry but struggle with high-frequency appearance, and NeRF-based methods sacrifice rendering speed and 3D consistency for photorealism. No prior work has combined the explicit, real-time rendering advantages of 3D Gaussian Splatting with a fully parametric head model that decouples identity and expression control.

## Input / Output

For training, the model consumes multi-view video frames (NeRSemble: 16 synchronized cameras at 72 fps) and 3D head scans (NPHM, FaceVerse) rendered into 80-view synthetic images, all at 512 resolution with BFM-fitted 3D landmarks and head pose as supervision. At inference, a single 512×512 RGB portrait image is fitted in ~30 seconds (300 gradient iterations) to recover identity and expression codes plus subject-specific geometry; expression editing then requires only a second reference image to supply a target expression code. The output is a fully parametric 3D Gaussian head model producing 512-resolution photorealistic renderings in real time, with 32-channel intermediate feature maps upsampled by a shared convolutional network.

## Method

The core architecture decouples identity code z^id and expression code z^exp into separate MLP pathways (f_id, f_exp) that predict per-Gaussian position displacements from a shared mean point cloud, while a separate attribute MLP predicts offsets for scale, rotation, and opacity relative to global means. A key training insight is a two-stage strategy: first, an SDF-based Guiding Geometry Model (using DMTet to extract a mesh) is trained to learn identity/expression deformations on the structured mesh surface; then its trained networks, latent codes, and mesh vertex positions are transferred to initialize the Gaussian model, placing all 100k Gaussian points near the true surface rather than relying on coarse FLAME initialization. This initialization is critical—ablation shows it improves PSNR from 25.7 to 28.0 and prevents Gaussian points from wandering into redundant or noisy configurations. Training is supervised end-to-end with photometric L1 loss on 512-res images, VGG perceptual loss, silhouette IoU, 3D landmark regression, and a regularization term that penalizes displacement magnitude to enforce identity/expression decoupling.

## Results

On the single-image fitting task evaluated over 60 images, the 3D Gaussian Parametric Head Model achieves a PSNR of 30.3, SSIM of 0.86, and Face Distance (FD) of 0.35, outperforming all three baselines. The method gains +1.2 PSNR over the strongest baseline (PanoHead, 29.1) and reduces FD by 0.06 compared to PanoHead (0.41), indicating superior reconstruction fidelity and identity preservation. The fitting process completes in approximately 30 seconds (300 gradient iterations), enabling real-time interactive use.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three SOTA single-image head reconstruction methods: HeadNeRF (NeRF-based), MoFaNeRF (NeRF-based), and PanoHead (3D GAN-based). The proposed method wins on both PSNR and FD across all baselines, achieving 30.3 PSNR versus 28.9 (HeadNeRF), 28.6 (MoFaNeRF), and 29.1 (PanoHead), and the lowest FD of 0.35 versus 0.37, 0.37, and 0.41 respectively. On SSIM, the method ties PanoHead at 0.86 while clearly surpassing HeadNeRF (0.84) and MoFaNeRF (0.82). Qualitatively, HeadNeRF often misses hair and removes the neck, MoFaNeRF struggles with hair due to its training on hat-wearing subjects, and PanoHead, while accurate from the input view, exhibits poor 3D consistency and identity drift from side views due to GAN overfitting. The proposed Gaussian-based model maintains consistent identity and geometry across novel views while handling complex appearances like hairstyles and accessories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the first to combine 3D Gaussian Splatting with parametric head modeling, marrying the explicit Gaussian representation from Kerbl et al. [21] with the identity/expression decoupling framework established by the SDF-based NPHM [15]. It departs from mesh-based 3DMMs (FLAME, BFM) that are topology-constrained and from NeRF-based parametric models (HeadNeRF, MoFaNeRF) that suffer from volumetric rendering inefficiency, while also contrasting with 3D GAN approaches (PanoHead, Next3D) that rely on coarse FLAME conditioning for expression control. The two-stage training strategy—first learning a guiding SDF geometry model, then transferring its parameters to initialize the Gaussian model—draws inspiration from Gaussian Head Avatar [54] but repurposes it as a bridge between structured and unstructured representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that 3DGS can serve as a viable representation for population-level parametric head models, achieving photorealistic rendering at real-time speeds while handling complex appearances (hair, accessories) that stymied both mesh-based and NeRF-based predecessors. The practical impact is significant for VR/AR, telepresence, and film production pipelines that require fast, controllable head avatars. However, the authors acknowledge limited generalization under illumination conditions outside the training distribution, and the model's reliance on a fixed set of ~100K Gaussian points raises open questions about scalability to higher resolutions or more diverse populations. The single-image fitting in 30 seconds is practically useful, but the 3D GAN baselines (PanoHead) remain competitive on identity fidelity, suggesting the gap between generative and parametric approaches is narrowing.
