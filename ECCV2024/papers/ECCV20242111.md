## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks for semantic segmentation (SegPGD, CosPGD) rely on cross-entropy loss, which is ill-suited for pixel-wise tasks because misclassified pixels dominate the gradient and prevent flipping of remaining correct pixels, leading to severe overestimation of model robustness. Additionally, prior adversarial training approaches for segmentation (e.g., DDC-AT) produce models that are completely non-robust even at small radii, while standard adversarial training is computationally prohibitive for large segmentation architectures.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images from PASCAL-VOC (512×512, 21 classes) and ADE20K (512×512, 150 classes), producing per-pixel class predictions as segmentation masks. For attacks, it generates ℓ∞-bounded perturbations (ε∞ up to 12/255) and reports robustness via average pixel accuracy and mIoU. Notably, a background class is explicitly included in both training and evaluation to ensure realistic robustness, since an attacker cannot selectively modify only foreground pixels.

## Method

The paper introduces three complementary attack losses designed for the pixel-flipping challenge: Jensen-Shannon divergence (bounded, with gradients that vanish for already-misclassified pixels, automatically reweighting toward correct ones), Masked Cross-Entropy (excludes misclassified pixels via a hard indicator), and class-balanced MCE (weights pixels by inverse class frequency to target mIoU). These are combined into the SEA ensemble attack, which runs 300 iterations of progressive-radius-reduction APGD (red-ε scheme with radii 2ε, 1.5ε, ε in a 3:3:4 split) for each loss and takes the worst-case result. For training, PIR-AT initializes the segmentation backbone with a pre-trained ℓ∞-robust ImageNet classifier (from RobustBench) rather than a clean one, then performs standard 5-step PGD adversarial training with cross-entropy loss, reducing training cost by up to 6× while achieving SOTA robustness (e.g., 71.7% robust accuracy at ε∞=8/255 on PASCAL-VOC vs. 0.0% for DDC-AT).

## Results

The proposed SEA ensemble attack reduces robust accuracy by up to 28.5% and mIoU by up to 20.9% compared to CosPGD, and by up to 17.2% in accuracy and 10.3% in mIoU compared to SegPGD, across six models on PASCAL-VOC and ADE20K. For robust model training, PIR-AT (UPerNet + ConvNeXt-T) achieves 71.7% robust average pixel accuracy at ε∞=8/255 on PASCAL-VOC (vs. 0.0% for DDC-AT) with only a 0.7% drop in clean accuracy (92.7% vs. 93.4%). On the more challenging ADE20K, PIR-AT yields the first robust models to our knowledge, reaching 55.5% robust accuracy at ε∞=4/255. PIR-AT reduces adversarial training time by up to 6× (e.g., 50 epochs vs. 300 epochs on PASCAL-VOC) while matching or exceeding the robustness of standard AT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CosPGD [1], the SEA ensemble consistently achieves lower (worse for the model) accuracy and mIoU across all six models and radii, with maximum gaps of 28.5% in accuracy (PASCAL-VOC clean UPerNet at ε=0.5/255: 31.1% vs. 46.9%) and 20.9% in mIoU (ADE20K clean UPerNet at ε=0.25/255: 20.9% vs. 24.6%). Against SegPGD [21], SEA improves accuracy by up to 17.2% and mIoU by up to 10.3%, and is the only attack that reveals DDC-AT [48] to be completely non-robust (0.0% accuracy) even at ε=2/255, where SegPGD still reports 3.9% accuracy. For robust model training, PIR-AT outperforms DDC-AT [48] by a massive margin: 71.7% vs. 0.0% robust accuracy at ε=8/255 on PASCAL-VOC, while maintaining comparable clean performance (92.7% vs. 92.8% accuracy). PIR-AT also surpasses standard AT with clean initialization at the same number of steps and epochs (e.g., 71.7% vs. 65.3% at ε=8/255 with 5 steps and 50 epochs), and matches 300-epoch AT with only 50 epochs (6× speedup). On ADE20K, PIR-AT with 32 epochs outperforms 128-epoch AT (4× faster), achieving 55.5% vs. 52.2% robust accuracy at ε=4/255.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PGD-based adversarial training framework (Madry et al.) to semantic segmentation, but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it introduces novel attack losses (Jensen-Shannon divergence, masked cross-entropy) specifically designed for the pixel-wise optimization challenge of segmentation, building on the progressive radius reduction scheme from Croce & Hein's ℓ1-APGD work. Second, it leverages the robust ImageNet classifier ecosystem (RobustBench) by initializing segmentation backbones with adversarially trained ImageNet weights (PIR-AT), a transfer-learning strategy not previously applied to segmentation. The paper also positions itself against SegPGD and CosPGD as stronger evaluation attacks, and against DDC-AT as a prior robust training method that it shows to be fundamentally flawed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DDC-AT — the only previously published robust segmentation method — achieves 0% robust accuracy at ε=2/255 under SEA is a significant correction to the field's understanding of segmentation robustness. PIR-AT offers a practical path forward: by reusing robust ImageNet backbones, it reduces adversarial training cost by up to 6× while achieving SOTA robustness, making robust segmentation feasible for larger architectures and datasets like ADE20K. Open questions include the absence of black-box attacks in SEA (limiting evaluation of gradient-masking defenses), whether other AT techniques (adversarial weight perturbations, synthetic data) further improve segmentation robustness, and whether the approach extends beyond the ℓ∞ threat model.
