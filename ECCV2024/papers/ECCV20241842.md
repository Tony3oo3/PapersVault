## Problem

Popular vision-language models like CLIP exploit spurious correlations as training shortcuts, leading to poor compositional reasoning beyond bag-of-objects: they fail to distinguish object attributes, inter-object relations, and word order, and are easily deceived by single-word counterfactual perturbations. Existing remedies focus on text-only augmentation, leaving the visual modality unaddressed for concept-level disambiguation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241842/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The training pipeline consumes RGB images (113K from MS-COCO plus 567K Stable Diffusion-generated counterfactual images at 1024×1024) paired with captions (1,134K total). At inference, the dual-encoder model (ViT-B/32 image encoder + text encoder) produces a cosine-similarity score between an image and a text prompt, which is used for zero-shot classification, image-text retrieval, and compositional reasoning benchmarks. The counterfactual generation sub-pipeline takes a single caption, masks one concept word, and outputs a modified caption plus a selected synthetic image.

## Method

COMO has two core components. First, a multimodal counterfactual generation pipeline: spaCy parses each caption to identify nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs; one concept word is randomly masked and BERT proposes replacements (filtered against WordNet synonyms); Stable Diffusion then generates 10 candidate images for the counterfactual caption, and the one maximizing combined CLIP similarity to both the counterfactual text and the original image is selected. Second, a counterfactual-guided weighted contrastive loss: factual and its paired counterfactual are forced into the same mini-batch, and the standard CLIP loss is modified so that each negative pair's weight is proportional to its similarity score (an unnormalized von Mises-Fisher form), dynamically up-weighting the hard counterfactual negatives and pushing their embeddings far from the factual anchor while down-weighting easy bag-of-objects negatives.

## Results

COMO achieves state-of-the-art results on both VL-Checklist and Winoground in zero-shot evaluation. On VL-Checklist, COMO scores 71.16 (Relation), 73.44 (Attribute), and 86.20 (Object), yielding a 3.17% relative improvement over the best baseline (NegCLIP) on Attribute. On Winoground, COMO reaches 31.00 (Text), 12.00 (Image), and 9.75 (Group), with a 4.35% relative improvement over SyVic on the Image metric. On the 21-dataset ELEVATER zero-shot classification benchmark, COMO scores 55.87, slightly below CLIP's 56.37 (−0.89%), indicating a minor trade-off in general classification performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COMO outperforms all five baselines on every VL-Checklist and Winoground metric. Against CLIP (the strongest zero-shot baseline), COMO gains +9.36 on Relation, +6.12 on Attribute, +3.29 on Object, +0.50 on Winoground Text, +1.00 on Image, and +1.00 on Group, while sacrificing 0.50 points on 21-Zero-Shot classification. Against NegCLIP, which is the closest competitor on VL-Checklist Attribute (72.23), COMO leads by 1.21 points; NegCLIP does not report 21-Zero-Shot results. Against SyVic (767K training data), COMO wins by +1.77 on Relation, +3.07 on Attribute, +1.58 on Object, and +0.50 on Winoground Image, using roughly 1.4× more training data (1.1M vs. 767K). Against SVLC (3M training data), COMO achieves superior results on all six concept-understanding metrics despite using only ~37% of the training data (1.1M vs. 3M), with the largest gap on Winoground Image (+1.00) and VL-Checklist Attribute (+2.26). CyCLIP is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing COMO by 10+ points on VL-Checklist Relation and Attribute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only counterfactual augmentation line (SVLC, Doveh et al. 2023) by generating both textual and visual counterfactuals, leveraging BERT for concept substitution and Stable Diffusion for image synthesis. The contrastive learning framework builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture and its InfoNCE loss, while the weighted hard-negative mechanism draws from the von Mises-Fisher reweighting scheme of Radenovic et al. (2023). The key conceptual departure is treating counterfactual image-text pairs as matched hard negatives within the same mini-batch, rather than relying on random in-batch negatives or text-only perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The COCO-CF dataset (680K images, 1.1M captions) provides a practical, automatically-generated resource for training VL models on compositional concepts without expensive human annotation, and the multimodal counterfactual + weighted contrastive recipe is a straightforward augmentation strategy that practitioners can adopt. However, the paper acknowledges that VL models still fail on certain compositional cases (e.g., 'closed cabinet' with an open door present), and the 21-zero-shot classification metric actually drops by 0.89%, suggesting the counterfactual training may slightly trade off general recognition for fine-grained concept discrimination. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-relation compositions and whether higher-fidelity diffusion models would further close the gap.
