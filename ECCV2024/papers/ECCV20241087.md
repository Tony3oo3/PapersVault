## Problem

Existing cloud-edge offloading frameworks rely on task-agnostic heuristics (e.g., fixed layer-wise splits or simplistic statistics) that cannot adapt to the dynamic difficulty and safety constraints of real-time, safety-critical mobile systems. This paper asks how to learn a situation-aware routing policy that dynamically balances energy consumption, latency, and navigation safety across varying crowd densities and weather conditions, rather than committing to a static split.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a 480×480 front-view RGB image and a 2D imminent waypoint vector at each timestep. The local and cloud navigation policies share a frozen RegNet feature extractor; the routing policy operates on the extracted image embedding concatenated with a sliding window of the last k actions (each tagged with a local/cloud indicator). The final output is a continuous navigation action (heading angle and speed) plus a binary routing decision. Notably, only the compact embedding—not the raw image—is transmitted to the cloud, reducing communication energy by an order of magnitude.

## Method

UniLCD comprises three policies: a small local navigation policy (fewer parameters, sharing frozen early RegNet layers from the cloud model), a larger cloud navigation policy, and a lightweight PPO-trained routing policy (2-layer MLP, hidden size 16) deployed on-device. The local and cloud policies are first trained offline via imitation learning (L1 loss) on CARLA crowd-navigation data, then frozen while the routing policy is trained with Proximal Policy Optimization. The key design choice is a multiplicative multi-objective reward—geodesic alignment × speed × energy-disadvantage × extreme-action-clip, with a separate sparse collision penalty—which the authors show is more scale-invariant and easier to optimize than additive rewards, particularly when combined with action history. The routing state includes the last k (action, source) pairs, enabling the policy to avoid redundant cloud queries and to anticipate when local inference is sufficient versus when the higher-capacity cloud model is needed.

## Results

On the CARLA crowded navigation benchmark, UniLCD (with history and the proposed multiplicative reward) achieves an Ecological Navigation Score (ENS) of 85.97%, Navigation Score (NS) of 94.58%, Success Rate of 93.33%, and Route Completion of 95.90%, while consuming only 2.90 J/m of energy at 26.49 FPS. This represents a 27.1 percentage-point improvement in ENS over the strongest baseline (Deep Sequential RL at 58.84%) and a 22.5-point gain over the next-best offloading method (Neurosurgeon at 39.85%). The method also achieves the lowest infraction rate (0.02/m) and lowest energy consumption among all methods that maintain a non-zero success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniLCD outperforms all eight baselines on ENS, NS, SR, RC, infraction rate, and energy consumption simultaneously. Against the strongest baseline, Deep Sequential RL [90] (ENS 58.84%), UniLCD gains 27.1 points in ENS and 32.8 points in NS, while also reducing energy from 3.77 to 2.90 J/m; however, Deep Sequential RL achieves a much higher FPS (77.94 vs. 26.49) due to its simpler architecture. Early-exit methods Neurosurgeon [38] (ENS 39.85%) and SPINN [48] (ENS 36.31%) are outperformed by 46 and 50 ENS points respectively, and both suffer from 0% success rate. DNN-based offloading via Selective Query [37] (ENS 24.14%) and Compressive Offloading [100] (ENS 13.98%) fail to handle the dynamic navigation task, with the latter incurring extreme energy costs (90.66 J/m). Adaptive Offloading [88] achieves a high route completion (94.05%) but at the cost of a very high infraction rate (1.22/m) and low NS (40.37%). The cloud-only RegNet model achieves the highest NS (96.47%) but scores 0% ENS due to prohibitive energy (36.49 J/m), while the local-only MobileNetV2 model has 0% success rate, confirming that neither extreme is viable for safety-critical real-time navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniLCD synthesizes ideas from cloud-edge collaborative inference (Neurosurgeon, Selective Query, SPINN), RL-based task offloading (Deep Sequential RL), and imitation-learning-based navigation into a unified framework for safety-critical real-time systems. It departs from prior offloading work, which relies on task-agnostic layer splits or simplistic heuristics, by learning a situation-aware routing policy via PPO with a multiplicative multi-objective reward. The paper also repurposes the early-exit paradigm not as a fixed architectural choice but as a dynamic, RL-learned decision between a lightweight local policy and a heavier cloud policy, with the routing module itself deployed on the edge device.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: safety-critical embodied agents (robots, autonomous vehicles) need both high-accuracy perception and hard real-time guarantees, a combination that neither local-only nor cloud-only approaches satisfy. The introduced Ecological Navigation Score and the CARLA crowded-navigation benchmark provide a reusable evaluation framework for future local-cloud collaboration research. Key limitations include reliance on a single simulator, a fixed energy model, and a single task domain; open questions include validation under real-world communication variability, scaling to larger cloud models, and generalization beyond navigation to other safety-critical perception tasks.
