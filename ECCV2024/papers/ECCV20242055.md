## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models lack precise control over the camera lens geometry used to render a scene. Existing workforces users to describe lens type in text prompts (a crude approximation) or apply post-hoc image warping, which introduces resolution loss in expanded regions, leaves uncovered areas, and cannot handle non-planar surfaces like spheres. This paper addresses the need for a generative model that natively produces images conforming to arbitrary optical geometries with exact geometric fidelity.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt plus a per-pixel conditioning map (either a 2-channel normalized coordinate field specifying each output pixel's location in the undistorted image, or a 3-channel pullback metric tensor plus a scalar distance-to-center channel for non-isometric surfaces) and produces a 512×512 (or 1024×512 for sphere panoramas) RGB image whose geometry exactly follows the specified warp. The conditioning is concatenated with the noised latent at every U-net denoising step, and for sphere texturing the left/right border pixels are overwritten each step to enforce seam alignment.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion architecture) by concatenating a per-pixel warping field to the noised latent input, with zero-initialized conditioning convolutions. Training data is generated by applying random Brown-Conrady lens distortions to standard images and their normalized coordinate grids, then cropping and resizing to model resolution. A key architectural modification reweights self-attention scores by adding ln(det(Jacobian)) of the warping map to each token's score, compensating for spatially varying information density in expanded or contracted regions. For surfaces that cannot be isometrically flattened (e.g., spheres), the coordinate conditioning is replaced by a pullback metric tensor (g₁₁, g₂₂, g₁₂) plus a geodesic distance-to-center channel, enabling geometry-aware generation on arbitrary manifolds. Training runs 500K steps at batch size 256 with a curriculum from mild to aggressive warps, and the model retains base-model FID (17.5 on MS-COCO) under the default uniform grid.

## Results

The fine-tuned per-pixel conditional model maintains the base model's FID of 17.5 on MS-COCO when using the standard uniform grid conditioning, confirming no quality degradation from the added conditioning channels. In a human evaluation of 200 sphere-texture samples (50 prompts × 4), the metrics-conditioned model was preferred 58% of the time versus 12% for the base model, with 30% of pairs skipped. For distortion fidelity, the per-pixel conditional model's generated images align with the target lens geometry as accurately as manually distorted images, as measured by the l2-norm difference between an external undistortion model's prediction and the conditioning displacement field.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the base latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion architecture [20]) without any per-pixel geometric conditioning. On FID (MS-COCO), the proposed method ties the baseline at 17.5, showing the added conditioning does not degrade standard generation quality. In the human evaluation for sphere texturing, the proposed metrics-conditioned model is strongly preferred over the base model (58% vs. 12% preference, 30% skipped), indicating significantly better geometric fidelity on curved surfaces. For distortion fidelity, the per-pixel conditional model matches the error level of manually distorted images, while the base model (which produces undistorted images that are then implicitly distorted) shows measurably higher undistortion prediction error. The paper also contrasts against a simple text-prompt approach (adding 'fisheye' to the prompt), which produces a visually plausible but geometrically misaligned result that fails to match the exact target lens parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion architecture) by fine-tuning a pretrained text-to-image model with two new conditioning signals: per-pixel coordinate maps and metric tensor maps. It departs from prior controllable-generation work (which conditions on depth, segmentation, or text) by introducing spatial geometric conditioning at the pixel level, and from post-hoc warping approaches by baking the geometry into the denoising process itself. The self-attention density reweighting is a novel architectural modification not present in prior diffusion work, and the metric tensor conditioning extends the framework beyond planar warps to non-isometric surfaces like spheres, connecting to differential geometry in a way that prior varying-shape generation work (crops, tiles, cubemaps) did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-pixel coordinate conditioning provides a general, lightweight mechanism for geometric control that could be adopted by other diffusion-based pipelines needing spatially-aware generation (VR content, virtual photography, 3D texturing). The paper demonstrates that this conditioning preserves base-model quality (FID unchanged at 17.5) while enabling precise lens alignment that text prompts cannot achieve. Key limitations remain: the approach requires a compatible metric representation for the target geometry, is limited by extrapolation with repeated content, and does not address depth-of-field or other optical effects. Open questions include whether stronger conditioning mechanisms (beyond channel concatenation) could handle more complex or higher-curvature geometries, and whether the metric tensor framework generalizes to arbitrary manifolds beyond the sphere.
