## Problem

Existing handwritten text generation methods require 10–15 style reference samples (few-shot), which is inconvenient for practical use, while existing one-shot methods perform poorly because a single sample has sparse foreground and noisy backgrounds that hinder style extraction. The paper tackles the challenge of accurately capturing a writer's distinctive style patterns (slant, cursive joins, stroke thickness) from just one reference image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241558/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single style reference image (resized to 64 px height, aspect ratio preserved) and an arbitrary Unicode text string as input. The text string is rendered into a Unifont image (following VATr) rather than tokenized, so any character in the Unicode set can be handled without a fixed vocabulary. The output is a stylized handwritten text image at 64 px height, generated via DDIM sampling in 50 denoising steps.

## Method

The core insight is that Laplacian high-frequency components of a single reference image reveal clearer style patterns (character slant, letter joining) than the raw image. A style-enhanced module runs two parallel CNN+Transformer encoders: one on the original image (whose features pass through a learned gate to suppress background noise) and one on the Laplacian-filtered high-frequency image (trained with a supervised contrastive loss, LaplacianNCE, to produce discriminative style features). These two style feature sets are fused with content features (from a ResNet18 + Transformer encoder processing the Unifont-rendered text) via a cross-attention followed by a self-attention module. The resulting fused embedding conditions a DDPM-based diffusion model, trained with a reconstruction loss plus classifier-free guidance, and further fine-tuned with a CTC loss from a text recognizer to enforce content accuracy.

## Results

On the IAM dataset, One-DM (one-shot) achieves FID scores of 89.47 (IV-S), 98.36 (IV-U), 93.30 (OOV-S), and 102.75 (OOV-U), outperforming all one-shot and few-shot baselines across every scenario. In the most challenging OOV-U setting, One-DM leads the second-best method (VATr, 108.76) by 6.01 FID points while using only 1/15th the style references. On the CVL dataset, One-DM achieves the lowest FID of 51.78 versus 54.44 (VATr) and 58.22 (HWT). For Chinese and Japanese character generation, One-DM attains FID of 27.24 and 95.43 respectively, surpassing the next-best method (WordStylist) by 7.37 and 6.50 FID points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

One-DM outperforms all seven quantitative baselines on every IAM evaluation scenario and both style-agnostic metrics. Against the strongest few-shot competitor VATr [39] (15 style references), One-DM (1 reference) improves OOV-U FID from 108.76 to 102.75 and IV-S FID from 103.75 to 89.47, while also achieving the best GS (1.98×10⁻³ vs. 1.74×10⁻²). Among one-shot methods, the closest competitor WordStylist [37] trails by 8.65 FID in OOV-U (115.52 vs. 102.75) and 2.85 FID in style-agnostic evaluation (18.58 vs. 15.73). GAN-based few-shot methods (GANwriting [24], HWT [4]) are consistently weaker, with GANwriting showing the largest gap (130.68 vs. 102.75 in OOV-U). One-shot GAN methods (TS-GAN [8], HiGAN+ [13]) and the diffusion-based GC-DDPM [10] are all surpassed by 10+ FID points in the hardest OOV-U setting. In qualitative comparisons against industrial methods (DALL-E3 [3], Stable Diffusion [42]), One-DM produces more faithful style mimicry and content preservation, whereas DALL-E3/SD mismatch style details and SD introduces extra backgrounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDPM diffusion framework for image generation, adopts VATr's Unifont-based content representation for arbitrary character sets, and formulates its LaplacianNCE loss from supervised contrastive learning. It departs from the dominant few-shot GAN paradigm (GANwriting, HWT, VATr) that requires ~15 style references, and from prior one-shot GAN methods (TS-GAN, HiGAN+) that use vanilla CNN encoders without explicit style-discrimination supervision. The key conceptual departure is treating high-frequency (Laplacian-filtered) components of a single reference as a complementary style signal, combined with a gating mechanism to suppress background noise—neither of which appears in prior handwritten text generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is clear: reducing style references from 15 to 1 dramatically lowers the barrier to personalized handwriting generation, and the method generalizes across English, Chinese, and Japanese scripts. The finding that a single well-processed reference can outperform 15 raw references challenges the implicit assumption that more samples always yield better style capture, suggesting that signal quality (high-frequency style patterns) can outweigh quantity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to longer text (sentences/paragraphs) rather than word-level generation, whether the ink-color inconsistencies noted by the authors can be resolved, and whether the high-frequency + contrastive learning recipe transfers to other style-mimicry domains beyond handwriting.
