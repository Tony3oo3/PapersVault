## Problem

Existing gaze prediction models handle free-viewing or goal-directed search but cannot predict how a person incrementally integrates word-by-word spoken language with visual information to control attention during object referral. Prior cognitive science studies of this process were limited to small arrays of simple line drawings, leaving the behavior in naturalistic scenes unexplored. No computational model had been developed to predict the dynamic, word-triggered fixation patterns that arise as a person hears a referring expression and progressively narrows down the target object.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image and a referring expression presented sequentially, one word at a time (tokenized with [BOT]/[EOT] markers), along with the full history of previously generated fixations. Output is structured as a sequence of 'packs'—each pack corresponds to one word (or to pre-expression/post-expression periods) and contains a variable number of 2D fixation coordinates (zero to several), terminated by an EOS token when the scanpath ends. Auxiliary outputs include the referred object's bounding box (x, y, w, h) and a target category probability distribution, both produced by dedicated learnable query embeddings in the encoder.

## Method

ART combines a multimodal transformer encoder (ResNet-50 visual backbone + RoBERTa language encoder) that processes image patches and language tokens as a unified sequence with two learnable grounding queries (BBOX, TGT), and an autoregressive transformer decoder that generates variable-length fixation packs per word. A novel fixation context encoder parameterizes each prior fixation with 2D sinusoidal positional embeddings for spatial coordinates and 1D sinusoidal embeddings for pack index and within-pack order, enabling the decoder to condition on the full fixation history. Each pack is predicted via learnable pack queries that attend cross-modally to the encoder output, with a token classifier (FIX/PAD/EOS) determining pack length and Gaussian regression heads predicting coordinates. Training proceeds in two stages: pre-training the encoder on RefCOCO with object localization (L1 + GIoU) and target category (cross-entropy) losses, followed by multi-task fine-tuning where the grounding losses are applied only after the full expression has been uttered or the scanpath has terminated, while the gaze loss (L1 regression + token cross-entropy) is applied at every word step.

## Results

On the RefCOCO-Gaze test set, ART achieves a Sequence Score of 0.359 and pack-level SS of 0.292, approaching the human-to-human upper bound of 0.400/0.317. ART also attains the best Fixation Edit Distance (6.371 overall, 1.143 per-pack), Correlation Coefficient (0.280), and Normalized Scanpath Saliency (3.478) among all methods. The model outperforms the strongest baseline (Gazeformer-ref) by 0.090 in SS, 0.098 in SS_pack, and 0.472 in NSS_pack, while reducing FED_pack by 0.143.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ART outperforms all three baseline families across every metric. Against Gazeformer-ref (the closest competitor), ART improves SS by 0.090 (0.359 vs. 0.269), SS_pack by 0.098, and NSS_pack by 0.472, while reducing FED_pack from 1.286 to 1.143. Against Chen et al. [11], which uses a frozen MDETR model for task guidance, ART gains 0.060 in SS and 1.921 in NSS_pack, with a substantially lower FED (6.371 vs. 8.309). Against OFA [67], which samples fixations within a predicted bounding box, ART achieves a 0.143 SS advantage and reduces FED from 17.084 to 6.371. The Gazeformer-cat variant (which reduces the task to categorical search using only the target category name) performs nearly identically to Gazeformer-ref, underscoring that spatial and attribute information in the full expression contributes little beyond category identification for these baselines, whereas ART leverages the full word-by-word expression to capture waiting, scanning, and verification behaviors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Gazeformer line of work on goal-directed scanpath prediction by introducing an autoregressive, word-by-word fixation prediction framework that replaces Gazeformer's non-autoregressive, first-fixation-conditioned approach. It also departs from the object referral/grounding literature (MDETR, OFA, RefCOCO-based models) by integrating grounding objectives directly into a gaze prediction architecture rather than treating them as separate frozen components. Conceptually, the paper carves out a new intersection: prior scanpath predictors model search or VQA but lack grounding subnetworks, while prior referral models localize targets from complete expressions without modeling the incremental gaze dynamics that cognitive science has documented.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RefCOCO-Gaze dataset (19,738 scanpaths, 220 participants) and the incremental object referral task formulation provide the first large-scale empirical foundation for studying how humans integrate spoken language with visual search word-by-word, a question open in cognitive science for decades. Practically, the ability to predict gaze incrementally from partial spoken instructions is directly relevant to time-sensitive AR/VR applications, foveated rendering, and voice-assisted HCI where eye-trackers are unavailable. A key limitation is that ART processes referring expressions as text rather than audio, ignoring phonological and prosodic cues that influence real-time attention control, which the authors flag as important future work.
