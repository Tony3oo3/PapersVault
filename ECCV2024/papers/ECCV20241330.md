## Problem

Existing 3D object detection metrics (mAP, MOTA) do not capture temporal stability, yet unstable detections—flickering confidence scores, jittering boxes—can cause erroneous velocity estimates and unsafe driving decisions. No prior work provides a principled metric or training strategy to quantify and improve detection stability across consecutive frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241330/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The SI metric operates on pairs of consecutive frames, taking as input the detector's predictions (confidence, 3D box center, extent, yaw) and ground-truth boxes for matched objects, then projecting both predictions onto a geometric-mean pivot box before computing element-wise IoU. PCL consumes paired frames at neighboring timestamps (interval Δt ∈ [−n, n]) with independent random flip/rotation/scale augmentations, and produces a scalar consistency loss that is added to the standard detection loss during training without any inference-time overhead.

## Method

The Stability Index (SI) decomposes temporal consistency into four sub-scores—confidence (calibrated by 99%/1% percentile range), localization, extent, and heading—each computed as IoU between element-decoupled boxes projected onto a shared pivot box, then aggregated as SI_c × (SI_l + SI_e + SI_h)/3, provably satisfying symmetry and marginal unimodality. Prediction Consistency Learning (PCL) samples a neighboring frame pair, applies independent augmentations, matches predictions to ground truths via the Hungarian algorithm and associates them across frames by object ID, de-augments predictions back to the original coordinate system, and penalizes the L1/MSE discrepancy of per-element prediction errors (in the object's ego-coordinate frame) between the two views. The framework is detector-agnostic, introduces no extra inference cost, and when applied to CenterPoint on Waymo Open Dataset raises vehicle-class SI from 80.52 to 86.00 while also improving mAPH.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, the paper benchmarks 10 state-of-the-art 3D detectors using both mAPH (accuracy) and the proposed Stability Index (SI). For the vehicle class, mAPH ranges from 72.60% (SECOND) to 79.00% (TransFusion), while SI ranges from 80.52% (CenterPoint) to 84.90% (DSVT), revealing no clear correlation between accuracy and stability. When the proposed PCL training strategy is applied to CenterPoint, it achieves a remarkable 86.00 SI for vehicle (vs. 80.52 baseline, +5.48), surpassing all baselines in Table 1, while also improving mAPH from 73.73% to 74.54%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares 10 baselines (SECOND, CenterPoint, PointPillar, PartA2Net, PVR-CNN, Voxel R-CNN, VoxelNext, PVR-CNN+, DSVT, TransFusion) on the Waymo Open Dataset. A key finding is that mAPH and SI are largely uncorrelated: TransFusion achieves the highest vehicle mAPH (79.00%) but a relatively low SI (82.32%), while CenterPoint has the lowest SI (80.52%) despite competitive mAPH (73.73%). Among LiDAR-only methods, DSVT leads in SI (84.90% vehicle) and VoxelNext is close behind (84.82%), both outperforming the fusion-based TransFusion in stability. The proposed PCL applied to CenterPoint achieves 86.00 SI for vehicle, beating the best baseline (DSVT at 84.90%) by 1.10 points and the CenterPoint baseline by 5.48 points, while incurring no additional inference cost. For pedestrian and cyclist classes, PCL also improves SI (73.14% and 69.16% respectively), though the gains are smaller than for vehicles. The paper also shows that common tricks like multi-frame input (Table 2) and increased data augmentation provide only marginal SI improvements (e.g., +1.22 for CenterPoint with 4 frames), underscoring the unique value of PCL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying detection stability as an underexplored dimension of 3D object detection, explicitly noting the absence of prior work dedicated to this property. It departs from existing accuracy metrics (mAP, mAPH) and tracking metrics (MOTA, MOTP) by arguing that neither captures frame-to-frame consistency of individual detections, and repurposes IoU-based reasoning with careful modifications (pivot-box projection, element decoupling) to construct a theoretically grounded stability metric. The Prediction Consistency Learning strategy draws on the broader consistency-learning paradigm (analogous to temporal consistency in video or augmentation invariance in semi-supervised learning) but applies it specifically to penalize prediction-error discrepancies across timestamps and augmentations in 3D detection, without requiring architectural changes or extra inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that mAP/mAPH and SI are largely uncorrelated across SOTA detectors challenges the implicit assumption that higher accuracy implies safer, more reliable perception, and suggests the community has been optimizing the wrong proxy for driving safety. PCL offers a practical, zero-inference-overhead training recipe that any existing detector can adopt, making it likely to be widely integrated. Open questions include extending SI to additional prediction elements (velocity, attributes), whether more aggressive PCL variants (e.g., cross-attention or temporal encoders) could push stability further, and whether the metric generalizes to other datasets and sensor configurations beyond Waymo LiDAR.
