## Problem

Generating animatable non-rigid 3D models from monocular video with text guidance remains largely unexplored: existing reconstruction methods are category-specific or require multi-view captures, and direct application of score distillation to deformable objects fails because the canonical model detaches from extracted motions, producing morphologically implausible warped results. The core difficulty is that non-rigid deformations are unconstrained and ill-conditioned, making 4D generation far harder than static 3D generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video of a generic-category non-rigid object (e.g., cat, squirrel, penguin, hand) and a text prompt as input. It produces a NeuS-based canonical implicit model (SDF + color + 16-dim DINOv2 feature descriptor) coupled with a skeleton of B Gaussian bones, dual-quaternion blend skinning weights, and per-frame bone transformations in SE(3). The output model is fully animatable: users can re-pose it by editing the rigid bone transforms, and the same framework also reconstructs the original object with diffusion-prior hallucination of unseen regions. Training renders four 200×200 images per frame for CSD and samples 128 pixels from 32 images for reconstruction, completing in ~5 hours on a single A800 GPU.

## Method

AnimatableDreamer is a two-stage framework: first it extracts a skeleton (bones, skinning, motions) from the monocular video via a NeuS canonical model with DQB warping, then it generates a new text-guided model constrained by that skeleton. The key contribution is Canonical Score Distillation (CSD), which replaces the single-image gradient of SDS with a chain-rule decomposition into a canonical generation term (back-propagating diffusion gradients through the warping function into the static canonical space, simplifying 4D generation to 3D) and a warping refinement term (jointly optimizing bone transforms and skinning weights so the warped surface stays morphologically plausible). CSD uses MVDream for multi-view consistent supervision across four orthogonal viewpoints per frame, and traverses all video frames to enforce temporal consistency. Skeleton construction combines semantic correlation (cosine similarity of DINOv2-weighted bone features) with morphological correlation (shared skinning influence over mesh edges), while SE(3) range constraints on bone-pair relative positions and quaternion angles prevent motion divergence during generation. Gaussian bone density additionally constrains the generated surface via a binary cross-entropy term, and a sparsity penalty on skinning weights prevents over-smoothing.

## Results

On monocular non-rigid reconstruction (1 video), AnimatableDreamer achieves Chamfer distances of 3.65 cm (Cat-Coco), 2.0 cm (Cat-Pikachu), 3.7 cm (Penguin), and 4.54 cm (Shiba), with F-scores of 63.3%, 88.9%, 64.0%, and 53.9% respectively, substantially outperforming BANMo (e.g., 10.7 cm / 15.3% on Cat-Coco) and RAC (e.g., 6.25 cm / 42.2% on Cat-Coco). For text-guided 4D generation, CSD achieves CLIP 38.2, CLIP-T 96.6, R-Precision@10 87.5, and GPT-4 Eval3D elo 1098, surpassing ProlificDreamer (33.1 / 95.9 / 56.3 / 959) and MVDream (34.8 / 94.4 / 31.2 / 979) across all four metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For reconstruction, AnimatableDreamer with a single monocular video outperforms BANMo by a wide margin on all four sequences (e.g., 3.65 vs. 10.7 cm Chamfer distance on Cat-Coco, 63.3% vs. 15.3% F-score), and also beats RAC consistently (e.g., 2.0 vs. 3.60 cm on Cat-Pikachu, 88.9% vs. 60.2% F-score). Notably, BANMo with 4 videos still underperforms the single-video AnimatableDreamer on Cat-Coco (4.66 cm vs. 3.65 cm) and Penguin (3.75 cm vs. 3.7 cm), demonstrating that CSD's diffusion prior effectively compensates for missing viewpoints. For generation, CSD surpasses ProlificDreamer (VSD) by 5.1 CLIP points and 31.2 R-Precision@10 points, and outperforms MVDream by 3.4 CLIP points and 56.3 R-Precision@10 points, with the largest gap in text-image consistency (R-Precision@10: 87.5 vs. 56.3 vs. 31.2). The GPT-4 Eval3D elo score (1098) also confirms superior perceptual quality over both baselines (959 and 979). The ablation removing L_CSD shows a clear drop (e.g., 8.34 vs. 3.65 cm on Cat-Coco), confirming that the canonical score distillation term is the primary driver of the reconstruction gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of score-distillation-based 3D generation (SDS, VSD/ProlificDreamer, MVDream) and non-rigid articulated reconstruction (BANMo, RAC, NeRFies). It departs from prior SDS-based methods by introducing Canonical Score Distillation (CSD), which back-propagates diffusion gradients through a differentiable warping field into a static canonical space rather than supervising each time frame independently. For non-rigid reconstruction, it extends the NeuS + dual-quaternion-blend-skinning formulation by adding DINOv2 feature descriptors for skeleton construction and Gaussian-bone density constraints, and by leveraging multi-view diffusion priors (MVDream) to hallucinate unseen regions from a single monocular video. The skeleton-based generation paradigm—starting from a blank canonical model constrained by extracted bones and motions rather than modifying a pre-reconstructed model—is a deliberate departure from the incremental SDS approach used in static 3D generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AnimatableDreamer is the first framework to unify text-guided non-rigid 4D generation and monocular reconstruction under a single distillation objective, making it a practical tool for creating animatable assets from a single video and a text prompt. The CSD formulation—simplifying 4D generation to iterative 3D optimization in a shared canonical space while refining warping parameters—offers a general recipe that could transfer to other articulated or deformable generation tasks. Key limitations include high VRAM demands from the long camera-to-canonical gradient chain, the need to feed four images into MVDream per iteration, and the 5-hour training time on an A800, which constrains practical adoption. Open questions include whether CSD can be made more memory-efficient (e.g., via gradient checkpointing or distillation of the multi-view model), whether the approach scales to higher-resolution textures and finer geometric detail, and whether the skeleton construction generalizes to objects with highly non-rigid or topologically changing deformations.
