## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods rely on either semantic conditions (e.g., reference images, depth maps) or motion conditions (e.g., bounding box trajectories, moving sketches) in isolation, each capturing only part of user intent. Semantic inputs provide rich scene context but lack precise motion specification, while motion inputs define trajectories but miss broader semantic narrative. This paper is the first to jointly integrate both semantic and motion cues within a single diffusion-based video generation framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes three inputs: a 256×256 RGB initial frame image, a sequence of per-object bounding boxes across F=8 frames (each box paired with a CLIP-encoded category label), and a free-form text prompt. The output is an 8-frame video at 256×256 resolution, generated in latent space (32×32 spatial dimensions after 8× VAE downsampling) and decoded to RGB. The bounding box trajectories are encoded via Fourier positional embeddings and injected as location tokens, while the initial frame is processed through a VAE encoder to produce the image condition in the same latent space as the video frames.

## Method

SMCD extends the ModelScope 3D diffusion UNet with two specialized modules: a Motion Integration Module (MIM) that encodes box trajectories into location tokens via MLP(Fourier(b), CLIP(c)) and injects them through a gated self-attention layer (inspired by GLIGEN) between spatial and text cross-attention in each UNet block, and a Dual Image Integration Module (DIIM) that combines a zero-initialized convolution (ControlNet-style) added to the noisy latent before the UNet with a gated cross-attention layer where the image latent serves as key/value and video features as query. A key finding is that simultaneously training both modules causes competitive interference, so the authors adopt a two-stage pipeline: first train MIM on single image frames (50k steps, batch 32), then freeze MIM and train DIIM plus the temporal attention layer on video data (80k steps, batch 8). Classifier-free guidance is applied at inference with separate dropout probabilities for box (p_b=0.1) and image (p_i=0.25) conditions during training.

## Results

On the GOT10K validation set, SMCD achieves an FVD of 335, substantially outperforming ModelScope (635) and TrackDiff (563). SMCD also attains a First Frame Fidelity score (FFF_DINO) of 0.85, nearly doubling ModelScope's 0.68, while matching TrackDiff's grounding accuracy (SR_50 = 0.783). On YTVIS2021, SMCD further reduces FVD to 329 compared to 368 for the best single image-integration strategy (CtrlNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMCD is compared against two baselines: ModelScope (text-only video generation) and TrackDiff (text + bounding-box trajectory video generation). Against ModelScope, SMCD reduces FVD by nearly half (335 vs. 635) and more than doubles the first-frame fidelity score (0.85 vs. 0.68), demonstrating the substantial benefit of adding both image and motion conditions. Against TrackDiff, SMCD achieves a 40% lower FVD (335 vs. 563) while matching its grounding accuracy exactly (SR_50 = 0.783), showing that the added image condition improves video quality without sacrificing motion adherence. Notably, TrackDiff does not report FFF_DINO, so the first-frame fidelity comparison is only available against ModelScope. The paper notes that no prior work integrates all three signals (image, text, and bounding-box trajectories) simultaneously, making these the closest available baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMCD builds directly on the ModelScope 3D diffusion UNet as its video generation backbone, adapts GLIGEN's gated self-attention mechanism (with Fourier-embedded box tokens) into a Motion Integration Module for trajectory conditioning, and borrows ControlNet's zero-convolution strategy as part of a Dual Image Integration Module for scene conditioning. The key departure from prior work is the simultaneous integration of both semantic (image) and motion (bounding box sequence) conditions within a single diffusion model, whereas previous efforts treated these as mutually exclusive control signals. The two-stage training pipeline—first MIM, then DIIM with MIM frozen—is a practical departure from joint multi-condition training, motivated by observed competitive interference between the two signal pathways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that image and motion conditions can be productively combined in a diffusion-based video generator, opening a path toward more interactive and user-controllable video synthesis. However, the paper is limited to 8-frame, 256²-pixel videos on a single A100, inherits ModelScope's known weaknesses (e.g., poor human generation), and acknowledges that bounding box trajectories alone cannot disambiguate object motion from camera motion. Open questions include whether the two-stage training strategy generalizes to more than two condition types, whether camera pose integration resolves the shaking artifact, and whether the approach scales to longer, higher-resolution sequences without the current architectural constraints.
