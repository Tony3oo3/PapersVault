## Problem

Existing sign language generation methods rely on cascaded pipelines (Text→Gloss→Pose→Video) that suffer from progressive error propagation, loss of contextual and emotional nuances, and inability to produce realistic full-body videos with facial expressions. No end-to-end approach existed that directly translates text into photorealistic sign language videos capturing both semantic precision and emotional resonance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt, an optional anchor image (encoding signer identity, pose, and depth as prior knowledge), and optional motion conditions (optical flow, pose, and depth sequences extracted by pre-trained I3D, DWPose, and DPT models respectively). It outputs a sign language video generated in latent space (8 channels, 32× downsampling) and refined by a FILM frame interpolation model to suppress flickering. Without an anchor image, the signer is sampled randomly and semantic consistency degrades; without motion conditions, generation still proceeds using only text and anchor.

## Method

SignGen is a modified latent diffusion model (U-Net backbone with temporal convolutions) that conditions denoising on two modality groups: text embeddings from CLIP ViTH/14 and a Motion Condition Fusion (MCF) module that fuses optical flow, depth, and pose features via 2D convolutions, batch normalization, linear projection, and FlashAttention for long-range temporal dependencies, with cross-attention injecting text guidance. The denoising U-Net integrates spatial and temporal dimensions into a single channel and incorporates a Spatio-Temporal-Semantic Attention (nested Res-Spatial-Temporal-Res blocks) plus a Fully Semantic-frame Interaction mechanism that applies self-attention across all latent frames and a modified cross-attention where queries are text features and keys/values are video-text concatenations weighted 0.4/0.6. Training follows Composer's conditional dropout (0.1 keep all, 0.1 discard all, 0.5 per-condition) to enable flexible inference, and sampling uses DDIM with classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On the five benchmark datasets, SignGen achieves FVD scores of 424 (CSL-Daily), 493 (WLASL), 556 (AUTSL), 579 (RWTH-2014), and 640 (RWTH-2014-T), substantially outperforming the best baseline (SignDiff) by 2.2–3.1×. Video quality metrics show PSNR of 20.22–34.92 dB and LPIPS of 0.01–0.11, compared to 7.61–21.23 dB and 0.12–0.95 for baselines. On the back-translation (Text2Pose) task, SignGen reaches ROUGE-L of 45.00 (Dev) and 44.56 (Test) with BLEU-4 of 20.21/19.71, surpassing the strongest baseline FS-NET (40.94/40.60 ROUGE-L) by over 4 points without requiring any external dictionary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignGen outperforms all six video-generation baselines (VDM, SVP, VideoLDM, VideoPD, VideoFusion, SignDiff) on every metric across all five datasets. The closest competitor is SignDiff, the only other sign-language-specific diffusion method; SignGen reduces FVD by 2.2× on CSL-Daily (424 vs. 1326) and 2.1× on WLASL (493 vs. 972), while improving PSNR by 16.0 dB (34.92 vs. 18.92) and LPIPS by 6.75× (0.08 vs. 0.54) on CSL-Daily. On RWTH-2014-T, where VideoPD and SignDiff are competitive (FVD 659/661), SignGen still leads with FVD 640 but with markedly higher SSIM (0.89 vs. 0.70–0.71) and PSNR (24.84 vs. 20.31–21.23). On the back-translation task, SignGen (ROUGE-L 45.00 Dev / 44.56 Test) surpasses FS-NET (40.94/40.60), which relies on an external sign-language dictionary, and outperforms the cascaded ProTran pipeline (34.05/31.07) by over 10 ROUGE-L points, confirming that end-to-end generation avoids the information loss inherent in Text→Gloss→Pose pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SignGen builds directly on the Latent Diffusion Model framework (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) and the video diffusion U-Net architecture (Ho et al., 2022), adapting them for sign language video synthesis. It departs from the dominant cascaded pipeline paradigm in sign language generation (Text→Gloss→Pose→Video) by eliminating intermediate representations, and from prior diffusion-based sign work like SignDiff (which uses ControlNet for pose conditioning) by introducing a dedicated multi-modal motion condition fusion module (optical flow, depth, pose) and spatio-temporal-semantic attention. The training protocol for composable conditions follows Composer (Huang et al., 2023), enabling flexible inference with partial conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating intermediate gloss and pose stages, SignGen addresses the error propagation and context loss that have plagued multi-stage sign language generation pipelines, offering a more faithful and emotionally expressive output that includes full-body and facial rendering. The paper acknowledges a key open question: current models may not cover all sign language dialects, limiting applicability across diverse deaf communities. A practical concern is that the extremely low LPIPS scores (e.g., 0.03 on RWTH-2014) when an anchor image is provided may reflect strong conditioning rather than genuine generative diversity, and the primary sign-specific baseline (SignDiff) is a single recent work, leaving the competitive landscape somewhat thin.
