## Problem

Existing night-time semantic segmentation methods predominantly force night images into a day-time distribution (via enhancement, disentanglement, or domain adaptation), leaving the inherent challenges of low-light scenes unaddressed. Specifically, compromised texture details and deceptive background pixels cause direct pixel-prototype similarity matching to produce erroneous associations, yet no end-to-end method explicitly tackles both issues within a unified night-native framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241423/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single night-time RGB image resized to 512×1024 and produces a pixel-level semantic segmentation mask over 19 Cityscapes classes. Internally, the backbone (ResNet101 or Swin-Base) extracts multi-scale features, a lightweight ResNet-18 encodes the Fourier phase texture, and the final mask is generated by multiplying H/4×W/4 high-resolution features against N=32 learned prototype vectors acting as class classifiers.

## Method

NightFormer comprises two end-to-end modules. The pixel-level texture enhancement module applies a 2D Fourier transform to the input, isolates the phase component (fixing amplitude to a constant), and encodes the resulting phase texture map with ResNet-18; a hierarchical amplified decoder then integrates this texture into backbone features at four scales (F5–F2) via an amplified attention that computes the element-wise squared sum of image features and phase features as a spatial weighting map. The object-level reliable matching module replaces direct pixel-prototype cross-attention with a reliable attention mechanism: it first computes a per-pixel reliability score (sum of softmax similarities to all N=32 prototypes), selects the top-K=40 most reliable pixels as bridge points, then factorizes the prototype-pixel similarity into a prototype-to-reliable-point and a pixel-to-reliable-point product, avoiding mismatches caused by deceptive underexposed backgrounds. Training is fully supervised with dice loss, binary cross-entropy for mask prediction, and cross-entropy for prototype classification, using Adam with a two-stage learning rate schedule over 80k iterations.

## Results

On the NightCity dataset, NightFormer achieves 59.8 mIOU with ResNet101 and 63.5 mIOU with Swin-Base, outperforming all prior methods by 2.2 and 2.3 mIOU respectively. On the refined NightCity-fine benchmark, the corresponding scores are 62.8 (ResNet101) and 65.9 (Swin-Base). When jointly trained on NightCity and CityScapes, NightFormer further reaches 65.4 mIOU on NightCity and 82.1 mIOU on CityScapes with Swin-Base, demonstrating cross-domain generalization. On BDD100K-night, the method attains 38.2 mIOU (night-only training) and 55.4 mIOU (mixed night-day training) with Swin-Base.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NightFormer outperforms all eight baselines on both NightCity and NightCity-fine across both backbone settings. With ResNet101, it surpasses the strongest prior method DTP [38] by 2.2 mIOU (59.8 vs. 57.6) on NightCity and 2.4 mIOU (62.8 vs. 60.4) on NightCity-fine, while using fewer parameters (58.4M vs. 63.9M). With Swin-Base, the margin over DTP [38] narrows to 2.3 mIOU (63.5 vs. 61.2) on NightCity and 1.7 mIOU (65.9 vs. 64.2) on NightCity-fine. Compared to NightLab [7], which requires 242.4M parameters with Swin-Base, NightFormer achieves a 3.7 mIOU gain (63.5 vs. 59.8) with roughly half the parameters. General-purpose segmentation methods (PSPNet, DeepLabV3+, DANet, UPerNet) all trail by 4–13 mIOU, confirming that night-specific design is necessary. On BDD100K-night, NightFormer leads DTP by 1.3 mIOU (38.2 vs. 36.9) under night-only training and by 2.3 mIOU (55.4 vs. 53.1) under mixed training.

## Relation to Prior Work

NightFormer departs from the dominant paradigm in night-time semantic segmentation, where prior methods (NightLab, DTP) either map night images into the day-time distribution or decouple illumination before feeding a generic segmentation backbone. Instead, it addresses the intrinsic low-light challenges—compromised texture and deceptive matching—directly, building on transformer-based segmentation with learnable prototypes (Mask2Former, Per-pixel classification) and frequency-domain texture extraction (FDA). The reliable attention mechanism is a novel architectural contribution that has no direct precedent in prior night-time or general segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a fully supervised, end-to-end pipeline that requires no day-night image pairs or manually tuned disentanglement parameters, NightFormer removes practical barriers that limited the generalizability of prior night-time methods. The reliable attention mechanism explicitly targets a failure mode (foreground-background confusion in underexposed regions) that was previously unaddressed, and the consistent ~2–2.5 mIOU gains across four datasets and two backbones suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the method extends to more extreme low-light conditions (eIR, thermal) or to video segmentation, and whether the Fourier phase extraction generalizes beyond the specific driving-scene benchmarks tested.
