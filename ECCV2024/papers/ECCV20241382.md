## Problem

In Class-Incremental Semantic Segmentation, the background class shifts at every incremental step because newly introduced categories were previously labeled as background. Existing methods rely on a single shared background classifier that must be readjusted at each step, causing unstable background predictions and degraded accuracy on both old and novel classes. This paper addresses this instability in an exemplar-free setting, where storing old training samples for replay is not feasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces per-pixel segmentation masks over all classes learned so far. During incremental training at step t, the ground truth only labels the current novel classes C^t; all previously learned classes are replaced with a background label, so the model must recover old-class predictions via pseudo-labeling from the previous step's model. The output logits are structured as per-class-group probability maps, each augmented with an additional background adaptation channel that contributes a residual correction to the global background logit.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the shared background classifier with a per-step background adaptation channel: at each incremental step, a new channel learns a residual that only decreases background logits in the region of the newly introduced classes, while a Filter operation (clamping to negative values) prevents noise from corrupting old-class regions. The final background logit is the sum of the frozen initial-step background logit and the filtered residuals from all subsequent steps, so only the newest residual channel is optimized at each step. Training is guided by a Pseudo Background BCE loss that restricts backpropagation to the current novel classes and the new adaptation channel, two Background Adaptation losses (cross-entropy pushing novel-class-region residuals negative, and a triplet loss keeping background-region residuals in a safe 0.5-1 Sigmoid range), Group Knowledge Distillation on old-category logits, and Background Feature Distillation on intermediate features outside the novel-class region. The backbone is DeepLabV3 with a ResNet-101 encoder, trained with SGD under a poly schedule.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012, the proposed method achieves mIoU of 70.0 (all classes) on the 15-1 sub-task and 62.1 on the challenging 10-1 sub-task (11 incremental steps), surpassing the previous state-of-the-art DKD by 3.0% on 10-1 and 3.0% on 5-3. On ADE20K, the method reaches 35.2 mIoU on 100-10 and 34.1 on 100-5 (11 steps), outperforming DKD by 2.0% on 100-5. The most notable gains are on novel classes: +4.5% mIoU on VOC 10-1 and +5.2% on ADE 100-5 compared to DKD, demonstrating superior plasticity for new categories while maintaining stability on old ones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The closest competitor is DKD [3] (NeurIPS 2022), which also refines the background class. Our method outperforms DKD on novel-class mIoU across all four VOC sub-tasks (e.g., 51.0 vs. 46.5 on 10-1; 57.0 vs. 53.5 on 5-3) and on overall mIoU for 10-1, 5-3, and 19-1, while DKD retains a slight edge on old-class mIoU for 15-1 (78.1 vs. 77.6) and 10-1 (73.1 vs. 72.2). On ADE20K, our method leads DKD on 100-10 (35.2 vs. 34.3 overall) and 100-5 (34.1 vs. not reported), though DKD is marginally ahead on 100-50 (36.0 vs. 35.7) and 50-50 (33.9 vs. 33.7). IDEC [63] (TPAMI 2023) is the second-strongest baseline; our method surpasses it by 2.7–3.0% overall mIoU on VOC 15-1 and 10-1, and by 2.5% on novel classes for 10-1. EWF [56] (CVPR 2023) and RCIL [58] (CVPR 2022) are outperformed by 5–10% overall mIoU on most sub-tasks. Earlier methods—MiB [6], SDR [40], PLOP [21], AWT [25], and GSC [17]—are surpassed by 10–20% overall mIoU, with the largest gaps on novel-class accuracy (e.g., 51.0 vs. 13.1 for MiB on VOC 10-1). The consistent pattern is that our method trades a small (<1%) loss on old-class mIoU for a large (+3 to +5%) gain on novel-class mIoU, aligning better with the incremental learning objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper addresses Class-Incremental Semantic Segmentation (CISS), a task established by MiB [6] and advanced by PLOP [21], SDR [40], DKD [3], and others. Its key departure is from the prevailing paradigm of a single shared background classifier that must be re-optimized at every incremental step; instead, it introduces a residual modeling scheme where each step contributes only a small adaptation channel that is aggregated additively with prior background logits. This is conceptually analogous to residual connections but applied across the temporal dimension of incremental steps rather than spatial depth. The method remains within the exemplar-free, DeepLabV3/ResNet-101 framework common to recent CISS work, and its distillation strategies (Group KD, Background Feature Distillation) extend standard knowledge distillation ideas with region-specific masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The background shifting problem—where the 'background' class silently absorbs all previously unseen categories at each step—is a real and underappreciated source of instability in CISS that prior methods largely glossed over. The residual formulation offers a clean, modular solution that decouples old background knowledge from new adaptation, which could be adapted to other incremental dense-prediction settings. Limitations include evaluation only on DeepLabV3/ResNet-101 (no Transformer backbones), and the approach's generality to very long sequences (beyond 11 steps) or to tasks without a well-defined background class remains untested. Open questions include whether the Filter operation and Triplet loss design generalize to architectures with different logit parameterizations.
