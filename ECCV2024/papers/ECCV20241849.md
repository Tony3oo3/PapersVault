## Problem

Existing CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly segmentation methods rely on product-specific text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a normal wood'), which are infeasible in data-privacy scenarios where the product category is unknown, and are sensitive to wording choices (±8% AP variation from synonym substitution). Moreover, these methods map image and text features into a joint space without any cross-modal interaction, limiting mutual understanding and segmentation accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241849/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB product image resized to 518×518 as input, with no requirement for a product category name or any text description at test time. It outputs a pixel-wise anomaly segmentation map at the original image resolution. Patch-level features are extracted from four intermediate ViT layers ({6, 12, 18, 24}) and fused via weighted summation (α=0.75) between the baseline branch and the Post-VCP branch to produce the final map.

## Method

VCP-CLIP introduces two visual context prompting modules on top of a CLIP ViT-L-14-336 backbone. The Pre-VCP module extracts the global CLS embedding from the image encoder, projects it through a lightweight 1D-convolutional Mini-Net into the word embedding space, and adds it to learnable category vectors, thereby replacing product-name tokens with image-conditioned embeddings in the text prompt. The Post-VCP module performs multi-head cross-attention (8 heads) where the two-class text embeddings (normal/abnormal) act as queries and flattened patch-level image features act as keys and values, producing updated text embeddings that are then aligned with image features via cosine similarity. A Deep Text Prompting mechanism inserts learnable embeddings at the beginning of each text encoder layer to refine the textual space. Training uses focal loss plus dice loss on both the baseline and Post-VCP anomaly maps, and the model is trained on seen products (e.g., VisA) to segment anomalies on entirely unseen products (e.g., MVTec-AD) without any product-specific prompts.

## Results

VCP-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot anomaly segmentation across all 10 industrial datasets, reporting pixel-level AUROC, PRO, and AP. On the two primary benchmarks, it reaches 92.0% AUROC / 87.3% PRO / 49.4% AP on MVTec-AD and 95.7% AUROC / 90.7% PRO / 30.1% AP on VisA. The largest absolute gains over the strongest competitor (CoCoOp) appear on MSD (AP: 61.0 vs. 27.0) and BSD (AP: 70.2 vs. 59.7), while the smallest margin is on VisA AUROC (95.7 vs. 94.9). The model runs on a single RTX 3090 with ViT-L-14-336 at 518×518 resolution, taking ~128 ms per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCP-CLIP outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all 10 datasets, with no losses. Among training-required methods, CoCoOp is the closest competitor on AUROC and PRO (e.g., 88.2 vs. 92.0 AUROC on MVTec-AD), while APRIL-GAN is competitive on AP for some datasets (e.g., 59.7 vs. 70.2 on BSD) but lags significantly on PRO (61.6 vs. 87.0). The training-free methods WinCLIP and AnVoL are consistently the weakest, with WinCLIP scoring as low as 47.0% AUROC on MSD and AnVoL reaching at most 96.3% AUROC on BSD. AnomalyGPT, which relies on a large language model for anomaly localization, is the most inconsistent baseline, dropping to 60.0% AUROC on GC and 67.6% on Road. The paper's own baseline (UTP + DTP without VCP modules) already surpasses CoCoOp on most datasets, and adding Pre-VCP + Post-VCP yields a further 1–6% AP improvement, confirming that both global and fine-grained visual context contribute meaningfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

VCP-CLIP builds directly on CLIP's joint image-text embedding space and on CoCoOp's insight that visual context can condition text prompts, but departs from both in important ways. Unlike CoCoOp, which replaces the entire text sentence with a learned prompt, VCP-CLIP surgically replaces only the product-category tokens with image-derived embeddings, preserving the semantic structure of the prompt template. It also extends the CLIP-based ZSAS line (WinCLIP, AnVoL, APRIL-GAN, AnomalyCLIP) by introducing a Post-VCP cross-attention module that lets fine-grained patch-level visual features update text embeddings, rather than simply aligning independently computed image and text features in a shared space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for product-specific text prompts, which is a critical bottleneck in data-privacy-constrained industrial settings where product categories may be unknown or ambiguous. The demonstrated robustness to state-word changes at test time (Fig. 7) suggests the model has genuinely internalized visual semantics rather than overfitting to particular prompt phrasings. However, the method still requires supervised training on seen products with pixel annotations, limiting its applicability to truly training-free scenarios, and all evaluation is confined to industrial surface-defect datasets, leaving open whether the visual-context-prompting strategy transfers to other zero-shot segmentation domains.
