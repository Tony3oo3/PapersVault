## Problem

Referring perception models are trained and evaluated on clean, delicately annotated data, yet real-world deployment exposes them to environmental noise, human input errors, and sensor imperfections that can severely degrade performance. No prior benchmark systematically quantifies how robust these models are across the full spectrum of realistic perturbations in both the referring guidance and the visual input simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240227/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark spans five tasks with heterogeneous input modalities: text+image (RIS), first-frame annotation+video (VOS), text+video (R-VOS), audio+video frame (AVS), and text query+3D scene (Q3M). Perturbations are applied to both the referring guidance channel and the visual frames, at three discrete severity levels (low/medium/high) plus a dynamic mode where severity and type vary per frame for video tasks. Outputs are segmentation masks (mIoU for RIS) or per-frame masks (J/F scores for video tasks) and 3D object localizations for Q3M.

## Method

R²-Bench introduces a four-category taxonomy (source, environment, sensor, transmission) covering 32 perturbation types across visual, acoustic, and textual modalities, applied in a physically motivated sequential order (source → environment → sensor → transmission) with up to two simultaneous perturbations per sample. The synthesis toolbox supports both static (constant severity) and dynamic (frame-varying severity) perturbation modes to emulate real-world fluctuation. For context-specific evaluation, R²-Agent is an LLM-based system (built on Gemini-Vision-Pro) that uses a multi-agent debating strategy—an operator agent proposes perturbation selections while a guardian agent verifies them via chain-of-thought prompting over two iterations—to automatically compose contextually appropriate mixed perturbations from natural language instructions (e.g., 'outdoor night scene' yields motion blur rather than glass blur). The agent then runs the model, computes metrics, and generates a human-readable robustness report, reducing the labor-intensive expert judgment traditionally required for perturbation design.

## Results

Across five referring perception tasks, all models show significant performance degradation under perturbations. For RIS, PolyFormer achieves the best clean mIoU (0.76 on RefCOCO) and the smallest average performance change (APC = −0.12), while LAVT degrades most severely (APC = −0.21). For R-VOS, ReferFormer suffers the largest drop (APC = −17.4 J / −18.5 F on Ref-YTVOS) compared to SgMg (APC = −6.4/−6.5). For AVS, QSD maintains the best clean J (77.6 on AVS-s4) and a moderate APC of −10.1/−7.9. Dynamic perturbations cause greater degradation than static ones, and frost (FT) is the most damaging visual perturbation while impulse response is the most damaging acoustic perturbation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 19+ models across five tasks under synthesized perturbations. In RIS, PolyFormer (polygon-based sequential prediction) outperforms all baselines in both clean accuracy and robustness, with an APC of only −0.12 on RefCOCO versus −0.21 for LAVT; SEEM matches PolyFormer's robustness (APC −0.12) despite lower clean mIoU, attributed to multi-task joint training. In VOS, DeAOT (decoupled visual/ID embeddings) is the most robust on YTVOS (APC −2.7 J / −3.7 F) compared to Cutie (−4.1/−5.0) and AOT (−5.4/−7.7), while Cutie leads on the simpler DAVIS dataset. In R-VOS, SgMg and OnlineRefer are the most robust (APC ≈ −6 to −10), whereas ReferFormer is the most vulnerable (APC −17.4/−18.5 on Ref-YTVOS), likely due to its heavy reliance on temporal pixel correspondence. In AVS, QSD (quantization-based semantic decomposition) achieves the best clean J (77.6 on AVS-s4, 61.8 on AVS-ms3) and competitive robustness, outperforming CATR and AVSegFormer. The Q3M task is evaluated qualitatively only, showing that ConceptFusion degrades noticeably under text misspellings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by transplanting the perturbation-robustness evaluation paradigm (established in image classification via ImageNet-C and similar benchmarks) into the referring perception domain, where no prior systematic robustness benchmark existed. It extends the single-modality noise taxonomy to a multimodal setting (visual, acoustic, textual) organized by physical origin (source, environment, sensor, transmission), and introduces composite perturbations that mirror real-world co-occurring noise. The R²-Agent component repurposes multi-agent debate and chain-of-thought prompting techniques from the LLM-agent literature to automate context-aware perturbation selection, a novel application of these methods to perception-model evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark exposing how 20+ state-of-the-art referring perception models degrade under realistic noise, revealing that even small perturbations (e.g., misspellings, motion blur) cause 10–20 point drops and that dynamic (frame-varying) perturbations are consistently worse than static ones. The R²-Agent lowers the barrier to context-specific robustness evaluation, but its reliance on a single LLM (Gemini-Vision-Pro) and the absence of quantitative Q3M results limit generalizability. Open questions include whether the 32-type taxonomy is exhaustive, how to design architectures that are inherently robust to composite multimodal noise, and whether the LLM-agent evaluation loop can be trusted for high-stakes deployment decisions.
