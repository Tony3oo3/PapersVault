## Problem

Existing sign language recognition and translation methods rely on RGB videos that degrade under fast hand motion (motion blur), cluttered backgrounds, and raise privacy concerns about facial data. While event cameras naturally capture high-speed dynamic hand movements with microsecond-level response, no benchmark or method existed for event-based continuous sign language recognition (CSLR) and sign language translation (SLT); prior event-based sign language datasets were limited to isolated recognition with small vocabularies (≤56 words) and low spatial resolution (128×128).

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous event streams from a DVXplorer-S-Duo camera at 640×480 resolution, which are binned into voxel grids (bin size B=5) and split into P temporal segments, then cropped to 480×480 and resized to 256×256. For CSLR, the output is a gloss sequence predicted via CTC decoding over a vocabulary of up to 1,387 signs plus a blank class; for SLT, an autoregressive transformer decoder generates a spoken-language sentence conditioned on the gloss-aware tokens. The EvSign dataset provides 6,773 clips (5,570 train / 553 dev / 650 test) with both gloss and Chinese text annotations, while PHOENIX14T is used as a synthetic event benchmark via V2E simulation.

## Method

The framework comprises five stages: (1) a sparse ResNet18 backbone (SConv) that exploits event sparsity to extract visual tokens at 0.34% of the FLOPS of dense convolutions; (2) Local Token Fusion (LTF), which applies two rounds of windowed multi-head self-attention followed by max pooling to aggregate local motion and reduce token count by a factor γ=4; (3) Gloss-Aware Temporal Aggregation (GATA), which decouples temporal modeling into intra-gloss and inter-gloss levels — the intra-gloss stage uses a Gloss-Aware Mask Attention (GAMA) that computes a soft mask from the element-wise product of feature similarity (ρ) and an RBF temporal distance constraint (δ), enabling dynamic fusion of visual tokens into fused tokens without explicit gloss segmentation; the inter-gloss stage applies global multi-head self-attention over the resulting tokens; (4) a recognition head with dual CTC losses on intermediate and final outputs; and (5) an autoregressive transformer decoder for translation supervised by cross-entropy. The entire system is trained end-to-end with Adam and cosine annealing, achieving competitive WER and ROUGE-L with only 14.19M parameters (44.2% of CorrNet).

## Results

On the EvSign dataset (real event streams), the proposed method achieves 28.69% WER on the test set for CSLR, outperforming the best event-based baseline CorrNet (29.95%) by 1.26% and the best RGB-based baseline VAC (30.43%) by 1.74%, while using only 0.84G FLOPS and 14.19M parameters (0.34% and 44.2% of CorrNet's cost). For SLT on EvSign, the method reaches 42.43 ROUGE-L on the test set, surpassing the best event-based baseline SLT (41.54) by 0.89%. On the simulated PHOENIX14T dataset, the method also leads with 24.03% WER (SLR) and 40.21 ROUGE-L (SLT), though the gap over event-based baselines is smaller due to the poor quality of the source RGB frames used for event synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: VAC (ICCV 2021), TLP (ECCV 2022), SEN (AAAI 2023), CorrNet (CVPR 2023), and SLT (CVPR 2020), each evaluated in both RGB and event (EV) modalities. On EvSign SLR, the proposed method wins against all baselines in both modalities, achieving 28.69% WER versus CorrNet-EV's 29.95% (the closest competitor) and VAC-RGB's 30.43%, while using only 0.34% of the FLOPS. On PHOENIX14T SLR, the method also leads (24.03% vs. CorrNet-EV's 24.55%), though the margin is smaller because the simulated events are derived from low-quality, blurry RGB frames. For SLT on EvSign, the method achieves the best ROUGE-L (42.43) and BLEU-1 through BLEU-3 scores, beating SLT-EV (41.54 ROUGE-L) and CorrNet+TH-EV (41.23 ROUGE-L), with the sole exception of BLEU-4 where CorrNet+TH-EV edges ahead (11.83 vs. 12.37). The efficiency advantage is dramatic: the proposed method uses 0.84G FLOPS and 14.19M parameters for SLR (vs. 228–245G and 31–60M for baselines), and 6.99G FLOPS and 28.06M parameters for SLT (vs. 233–253G and 34–46M for baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the established CSLR/SLT pipeline (CTC-based recognition, sign-to-gloss-to-text translation) for event camera data, a modality previously explored only for isolated sign recognition with small vocabularies (SLAnimals-DVS, EvASL). Architecturally, it combines sparse convolutional backbones from the event-based vision literature with a novel gloss-aware temporal aggregation module that decouples intra-gloss and inter-gloss temporal cues, departing from the 1D-CNN and BiLSTM temporal models used in prior RGB-based CSLR work (VAC, CorrNet, TLP). The paper does not build on a single prior method but rather re-frames the entire sign language recognition/translation problem under the event data paradigm, introducing the first continuous benchmark for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EvSign is the first benchmark enabling continuous sign language recognition and translation with event cameras, filling a clear gap where prior event-based sign language datasets only supported isolated recognition with 19–56 word vocabularies. The 0.34% FLOPS and 44.2% parameter efficiency relative to dense RGB baselines is practically significant for edge deployment, though the comparison is partly structural (sparse vs. dense data) rather than purely algorithmic. Key limitations include the small signer count (9), the modest WER improvements over RGB on real data (~1–2 points), and the fact that event advantages vanish on simulated data, suggesting the benefit may be tied to specific recording conditions rather than a fundamental representational advantage. Open questions include whether the gloss-aware mask attention generalizes to larger vocabularies and multi-signer settings, and whether the efficiency gains persist as event data resolution increases.
