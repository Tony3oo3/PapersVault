## Problem

Diffusion-based image editing methods rely heavily on their training distribution and fail to generalize to unseen editing tasks, producing inaccurate or detail-poor outputs when instructions fall outside the training set. Existing in-context learning approaches for vision (e.g., PromptDiffusion, ImageBrush) are not tailored to image editing, and no open dataset provides multiple image pairs under a single editing instruction to support visual in-context learning for editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242343/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2×2 visual prompt grid at 512×512 (two complete input–output image pairs plus one input image with the fourth quadrant masked in grey) concatenated into a single image, along with a free-form text editing instruction. Images are resized to 256×256 for training and testing. The output is a single edited image that should follow both the visual exemplar pattern and the textual instruction, with particular attention to preserving fine details such as human facial features.

## Method

InstructGIE builds on a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone with a trainable ControlNet branch, replacing the standard ConvNet/ViT vision encoder with Zero-VMamba layers that provide a global effective receptive field across all four quadrants of the visual prompt. Three training objectives complement the standard diffusion loss: (1) an editing-shift matching loss that aligns CLIP-embedding-based implicit editing shift vectors between pseudo-outputs and ground truth, (2) a selective area matching loss that uses a frozen Mask2Former to isolate living-creature regions and enforces pixel-level fidelity there, and (3) a language instruction unification step where a frozen Open Llama 3b reformulates 50% of training instructions (and all inference instructions) into a canonical form, reducing output variance from semantically equivalent but lexically different prompts. The authors also release the first image-editing dataset with multiple image pairs per instruction (~12,000 images, ~2,000 instructions), generated via fine-tuned GPT-3 captioning and Prompt-to-Prompt synthesis.

## Results

On the proposed Visual Prompt Dataset for Image Editing (~12,000 images, ~2,000 editing instructions), InstructGIE achieves a FID of 7.57 and CLIP DirSim of 0.27, substantially outperforming all three baselines. The FID improvement is particularly large: 65% lower than SDEdit(E) (21.67), 58% lower than InstructPix2Pix (17.87), and 45% lower than PromptDiffusion (13.75). CLIP DirSim, measuring instruction-following fidelity, improves from 0.11–0.21 for baselines to 0.27 for InstructGIE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstructGIE outperforms all three baselines on both FID and CLIP DirSim. Against SDEdit(E), the largest gap is observed: FID drops from 21.67 to 7.57 (−65%) and CLIP DirSim rises from 0.11 to 0.27 (+145%), indicating SDEdit struggles significantly with visual-prompt-based editing. InstructPix2Pix (FID 17.87, CLIP DirSim 0.17) is a text-instruction-only method that does not support visual prompting, so the comparison highlights the advantage of the unified visual+language framework. PromptDiffusion (FID 13.75, CLIP DirSim 0.21) is the closest competitor as it also leverages in-context visual learning, yet InstructGIE still achieves a 45% FID reduction and a 0.06 absolute CLIP DirSim gain, attributed to the VMamba-based architecture, editing-shift matching, language unification, and selective area matching. Qualitatively, the paper reports that on out-of-domain editing tasks (object add/remove, structure change, size change), all three baselines fail to follow instructions, while InstructGIE produces coherent, detailed outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual in-context learning paradigm for diffusion-based image editing (pioneered by ImageBrush and PromptDiffusion) by addressing their core weakness: poor generalization to unseen editing tasks and degraded detail quality. Architecturally, it builds on ControlNet and Stable Diffusion as the base editing backbone, but replaces the typical ConvNet/ViT vision encoder with a Zero-VMamba state-space model to achieve a global effective receptive field over the multi-image visual prompt grid. The editing-shift matching loss and selective area matching (via Mask2Former + LLM class filtering) are novel training objectives that go beyond the standard denoising loss, while the language instruction unification step (using a frozen 3B LLM to canonicalize prompts) is a lightweight data-augmentation trick not previously applied to diffusion editing pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a genuine gap: current instruction-based image editors (InstructPix2Pix, PromptDiffusion) perform well only on in-distribution tasks and degrade sharply on unseen manipulations, limiting their practical utility. The released dataset—first to pair multiple image pairs under a single editing instruction—is a useful resource for evaluating visual-prompt generalization. However, the evidence base is narrow: all quantitative results come from a single synthetic dataset of ~12K images generated via Prompt-to-Prompt, out-of-domain evaluation is purely qualitative, and training uses only 5000 steps on a small model. Open questions include whether the VMamba-based receptive-field advantage and the selective area matching strategy transfer to higher-resolution or video editing settings, and whether the 3B-LLM unification bottleneck becomes a quality ceiling at scale.
