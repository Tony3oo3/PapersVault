## Problem

GAN training is inherently unstable because the discriminator quickly dominates the adversarial game, leaving the generator with weak or uninformative gradients. Existing remedies (modified loss functions, data augmentation, feature-space shrinkage) adjust hyperparameters or architectures but do not fundamentally alter the asymmetric relationship between G and D. This paper asks whether reframing the generator and discriminator as inverse processes can restore a fairer training dynamic.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is plug-and-play on standard GAN backbones: DCGAN at 32×32 (CIFAR-10, CelebA) and StyleGAN-V2 at 256×256 (AFHQ-Cat, FFHQ, LSUN-Church). The generator still takes a randomly sampled latent code z and produces an RGB image, but the discriminator is augmented with a lightweight affine head that additionally outputs a reconstructed latent code, and the generator receives an extra reconstruction target (the original real image) during training. No changes to the input distribution or output format are required.

## Method

CLR-GAN introduces two auxiliary losses that treat G and D as an inverse pair. First, a Consistent Latent Representation (CLR) loss: the discriminator's feature extractor is followed by a mapping function σ(·) that projects its intermediate features back into the generator's latent space, and the L1 distance between this reconstructed code and the original z is minimized, forcing D's internal representation to stay aligned with G's latent manifold. Second, a Real Image Reconstruction loss: the generator is asked to reconstruct the original real image x from the latent code that D extracted from x, giving G a direct gradient signal from the real data distribution rather than relying solely on D's adversarial feedback. The combined objective adds λ₁·L_CLR to the discriminator loss and λ₂·L_rec to the generator loss (optimal λ₁=5, λ₂=0.5), which the authors show from a regularization perspective penalizes an over-strong discriminator and supplies the generator with an alternative optimization path, thereby stabilizing training and improving both FID and diversity.

## Results

On low-resolution CIFAR-10 (DCGAN backbone), CLR-GAN achieves an FID of 23.3, outperforming the DCGAN baseline (38.56) by 39.6% and the strongest prior method AdaptiveMix (30.85) by 24.5%. On high-resolution AFHQ-Cat (StyleGAN-V2 backbone), CLR-GAN reduces FID from 7.92 to 4.79 (39.5% improvement) while also improving Precision from 0.68 to 0.76. When combined with StyleGAN-V2-ADA, the method further lowers FID to 4.45 on AFHQ-Cat, 3.37 on FFHQ, and 3.43 on LSUN-Church, achieving the best results across all three high-resolution benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLR-GAN outperforms all 16 baselines on the primary FID metric across both low- and high-resolution settings. On CIFAR-10, it beats WGAN (55.96), HingeGAN (42.4), LSGAN (42.01), DCGAN (38.56), WGAN-GP (41.86), all three Realness GAN variants (36.73/34.59/36.21), and AdaptiveMix (30.85), achieving the best FID of 23.3. On CelebA, it surpasses AdaptiveMix (12.43) by a narrow margin (13.63) but dominates all other loss-function baselines. On high-resolution AFHQ-Cat, CLR-GAN (4.79) beats StyleGAN-V2 (7.92), LC-Reg (6.70), StyleGAN-V2-ADA (6.05), and StyleGAN-V2-APA (4.88) on FID, while also achieving the highest Precision (0.76) among all methods. On FFHQ and LSUN-Church, CLR-GAN similarly achieves the best FID (3.44 and 3.52 respectively) and the highest Precision, though StyleGAN-V2-APA is competitive on Recall for AFHQ-Cat (0.30 vs. 0.28). The method is also complementary to StyleGAN-V2-ADA, yielding further FID reductions when combined (e.g., 4.45 vs. 6.05 on AFHQ-Cat), demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard two-player GAN game formulation by reframing the generator and discriminator as inverse processes, a conceptual shift from prior stability-focused work (Dist-GAN, DynamicGAN, AdaptiveMix) that modifies only one side of the game. It is most closely related to GLeaD [2], which also uses reconstruction to improve GAN training, but CLR-GAN extends this by adding a bidirectional constraint: the discriminator must reconstruct the generator's latent code (latent consistency loss) while the generator must reconstruct real images from the discriminator's features (reconstruction loss), so both networks receive gradients from each other. The latent-space alignment idea draws on the rich literature of GAN latent representations (Multi-Code GAN, ClusterGAN, StyleMapGAN), but is the first to directly use latent-space consistency as a training objective rather than a downstream analysis tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of the two L1 losses (applicable to both DCGAN and StyleGAN-V2 without architectural changes) makes this a practical tool for stabilizing GAN training across a wide range of setups, and the demonstrated improvement in the discriminator's feature quality (98.8% CIFAR-10 accuracy with only 3.02M parameters) suggests broader utility for unsupervised representation learning. A key open question the authors flag is why a high λ₁/λ₂ ratio accelerates early convergence yet degrades final performance, pointing to an unresolved tension between tight latent constraints and distributional diversity. The method's reliance on a simple affine mapping σ(·) between feature and latent spaces, and its evaluation primarily on natural image datasets, leave open whether the inverse-process framing generalizes to conditional generation, video, or non-image modalities.
