## Problem

Continual self-supervised learning (CSSL) has been studied primarily through the lens of catastrophic forgetting, but the task confusion problem—where the model fails to draw discriminative boundaries between classes of different tasks—remains largely unexplored. This paper shows that contrastive SSL is inherently susceptible to task confusion even when forgetting is eliminated, because classes from different tasks are never trained concurrently, leading to insufficient negative sample diversity in class-incremental settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) from the current task alongside a small memory buffer of exemplars from previous tasks. A key unusual aspect is the cross-model input: embeddings for memory buffer samples are extracted from a frozen copy of the previous task's encoder, while current-task and mixed-sample embeddings come from the live encoder. At evaluation, the frozen encoder produces visual embeddings that feed a linear probe to predict class labels across all tasks simultaneously without task-ID at inference.

## Method

CroMo-Mixup consists of two coupled components. First, Cross-Task Data Mixup generates inter-task mixed samples via convex interpolation (x_mix = λx_t + (1−λ)x_M) between a current-task sample and a memory-buffer sample, applied to both augmented views. Second, Cross-Model Feature Mixup defines an SSL loss that pulls the mixed sample's embedding (from the current model) toward the current-task sample's embedding (current model) and the old-task sample's embedding (frozen old model), weighted by λ and (1−λ) respectively, while treating all other mixed, current, and old embeddings in the batch as negatives. This cross-model similarity learning forces the encoder to identify task-specific features at a granular level and retrieve old knowledge, directly addressing task confusion. The total objective additionally includes a standard task-specific SSL loss and a temporal-projector distillation term on current-task data, making the framework compatible with any SSL objective (Barlow-Twins, SimCLR, BYOL, CorInfoMax).

## Results

CroMo-Mixup achieves the highest average linear accuracy across all four CIL settings: 88.88% on CIFAR10-Split2 (BYOL), 65.48% on CIFAR100-Split5 (Barlow-Twins), 62.48% on CIFAR100-Split10 (Barlow-Twins), and 45.44% on tinyImageNet-Split10 (BYOL). The method also substantially improves Task-ID Prediction (TP) over the state-of-the-art CaSSLe+, with gains of +4.4%, +3.1%, +5.0%, and +5.2% on the respective datasets, confirming its effectiveness against task confusion. Even without knowledge distillation (CroMo-Mixup*, ζ=0), the method outperforms CaSSLe+ by 2.7–3.4% in linear accuracy, indicating that the cross-model feature mixup mechanism itself is the primary driver of improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CroMo-Mixup consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art CaSSLe and its memory-buffer variant CaSSLe+ across all four datasets and all four SSL objectives. On CIFAR100-Split5, CroMo-Mixup (Barlow-Twins) achieves 65.48% LA vs. CaSSLe+ at 61.25% (+4.2%) and CaSSLe at 60.64% (+4.8%); on the harder CIFAR100-Split10, the gap widens to +5.8% over CaSSLe+. On tinyImageNet-Split10 (BYOL), CroMo-Mixup reaches 45.44% LA vs. CaSSLe+ at 40.24% (+5.2%), the largest absolute gain in the paper. Against replay-based baselines, CroMo-Mixup substantially outperforms ER (e.g., 65.48% vs. 57.23% on CIFAR100-Split5) and Fine-tune (65.48% vs. 54.40%), confirming that a small memory buffer alone is insufficient for contrastive SSL. In Table 1, CroMo-Mixup also beats LUMP (57.8%), Sy-Con (60.4%), EWC (56.7%), and DER (55.3%) on CIFAR100-Split5 with Barlow-Twins. The TP metric shows the most dramatic improvement: CroMo-Mixup gains +5.0% TP over CaSSLe+ on CIFAR100-Split10, directly addressing the task-confusion problem that the paper identifies as underexplored in CSSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the mixup data augmentation framework (Zhang et al., ICLR 2018) and the temporal-projector distillation mechanism from CaSSLe (Fini et al., CVPR 2022), repurposing both for a cross-task, cross-model continual learning setting. It departs from prior CSSL work (CaSSLe, Sy-Con, LUMP) by shifting focus from catastrophic forgetting to task confusion, arguing that contrastive SSL is inherently susceptible to inter-task class overlap even when forgetting is eliminated. Unlike LUMP, which uses mixup to learn general-purpose cross-task features, CroMo-Mixup formulates a weighted similarity objective between mixed and original sample embeddings to learn task-specific discriminative boundaries at a granular level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a previously unaddressed failure mode in CSSL—task confusion arising from insufficient negative diversity in class-incremental settings—providing a clear diagnostic (TP vs. WP decomposition) and a practical remedy compatible with multiple SSL objectives. The finding that supervised learning does not exhibit this issue (absent forgetting) while contrastive SSL does points to a fundamental property of contrastive learning's reliance on diverse in-batch negatives. Open questions include applicability without any memory buffer (privacy-constrained settings), behavior under smoother task transitions rather than discrete splits, and whether the approach extends to non-contrastive SSL objectives or larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet.
