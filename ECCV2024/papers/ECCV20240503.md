## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting's adaptive density control fails to grow points in regions with sparse initial SfM point clouds, producing blurring and needle-like artifacts. The root cause is that the growth condition uniformly averages NDC gradient magnitudes across all viewpoints in which a Gaussian is visible, which systematically disadvantages large Gaussians that span many viewpoints but only produce significant gradients in a few where their projected center falls within the image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240503/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the same inputs as standard 3DGS: multi-view RGB images and an SfM sparse point cloud for initialization, with no additional priors (no depth maps, normals, or extra supervision). It outputs a 3D Gaussian scene representation rendered in real-time (~89–92 FPS on an RTX 3090) to arbitrary novel viewpoints. The only new hyperparameter is γ_depth (set to 0.37), and the original splitting/cloning threshold τ_pos = 2×10⁻⁴ is retained unchanged.

## Method

Pixel-GS replaces the uniform average of per-view NDC gradient magnitudes in the growth condition with a pixel-weighted average, where each viewpoint's weight is the fraction of image pixels the Gaussian covers in that view (m_k^i / total pixels). This amplifies the gradient signal for large Gaussians—whose projected area varies dramatically across views—while leaving small Gaussians (whose pixel coverage is nearly constant across views) essentially unaffected, so point growth is concentrated where it is needed. To counteract the increased floater growth this induces near the camera, the method additionally scales each Gaussian's gradient by a depth-dependent factor f(i,k) = clip((μ_cz / (γ_depth · radius))², 0, 1), which suppresses gradients for Gaussians close to the camera. The final split/clone decision compares the pixel-weighted, depth-scaled average gradient magnitude against the original τ_pos threshold, requiring only minor code modifications to the 3DGS pipeline.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, Pixel-GS achieves 27.88 PSNR, 0.834 SSIM, and 0.176 LPIPS, outperforming the retrained 3DGS baseline (3DGS*) by 0.17 dB in PSNR and 17.8% in LPIPS. On Tanks & Temples, Pixel-GS reaches 24.38 PSNR, 0.850 SSIM, and 0.178 LPIPS, again surpassing 3DGS* on all three metrics. The method maintains real-time rendering at ~89–92 FPS with 0.84–1.2 GB memory, comparable to 3DGS* which runs at 126–135 FPS with 0.41–0.72 GB. The LPIPS improvement is the most pronounced, indicating substantially better perceptual quality in regions with sparse SfM initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pixel-GS consistently outperforms all four baselines on both Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks & Temples across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. Against the closest competitor 3DGS* (retrained 3DGS), Pixel-GS gains +0.17 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.202 to 0.176 on Mip-NeRF 360, and +0.19 dB PSNR with LPIPS dropping from 0.194 to 0.178 on Tanks & Temples. Against Mip-NeRF 360 [3], the improvement is larger: +0.19 dB PSNR and LPIPS reduced from 0.237 to 0.176 (a 25.7% reduction). Against INGP-Big and Plenoxels, Pixel-GS leads by 2.29 and 4.80 dB in PSNR respectively, reflecting the gap between grid-based and explicit Gaussian representations. Notably, simply lowering 3DGS's splitting threshold (τ_pos) to match Pixel-GS's point count increases memory from 0.72 GB to 1.4 GB and drops FPS from 126 to 90, yet still yields worse LPIPS (0.181 vs 0.176), confirming that the pixel-aware weighting—not just more points—is the key factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pixel-GS is a direct extension of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), modifying only its adaptive density control criterion by replacing the uniform average of NDC gradient magnitudes across views with a pixel-coverage-weighted average. The floater-suppression component draws on the gradient-scaling idea from 'Floaters No More' (Philip & Deschaintre, 2023), adapted to the Gaussian splatting setting. Unlike concurrent 3DGS improvements that incorporate external priors such as depths or normals (e.g., GaussianPro), Pixel-GS requires no additional information beyond what the standard 3DGS pipeline already computes, making it a drop-in modification compatible with the broader 3DGS ecosystem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent failure mode of 3DGS—blurring and needle-like artifacts in regions with sparse SfM initialization—that limits real-world deployment where SfM quality is often poor. The demonstrated robustness to up to 99% initialization point dropout is practically significant for applications where high-quality SfM is unavailable. However, the method increases Gaussian count and memory consumption (mitigable via pruning), and the depth-scaling hyperparameter γ_depth may require per-scene tuning, leaving open the question of fully automatic, scene-adaptive density control without manual hyperparameter selection.
