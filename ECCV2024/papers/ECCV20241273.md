## Problem

Compositional generalization research in visual reasoning has focused predominantly on systematicity (generalizing to novel combinations of known primitives), while substitutivity—the ability to generalize when primitives are replaced by their synonyms—remains largely unexplored. Existing substitutivity evaluations are limited to word-level substitutions in the language modality, ignoring synonymous visual entities and referential relationships between modalities that are central to VQA.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241273/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework is model-agnostic and plugs into any VQA model that takes a natural language question and an image (with detected objects or raw pixels) and outputs a categorical answer. During training, each sample is augmented with a question support-set (24 back-translated variants via mBART) and an image support-set (top-128 CLIP-retrieved similar images), from which subsets of size K_q and K_v are sampled per training step to form reconstructed counterparts of the original question and image.

## Method

The proposed SUPS framework constructs support sets for each training sample—synonymous questions via multilingual back-translation (mBART, 24 intermediate languages) and similar images via CLIP retrieval—then reconstructs the original question/image as a cosine-similarity-weighted combination of support-set features at the deep feature level. Training uses two losses: a method-specific loss applied jointly to the original and reconstructed samples (enforcing identical predictions), and a contrastive loss that pulls the original and reconstructed features together while pushing them away from in-batch negatives. The reconstruction step is critical: by using a weighted sum rather than individual support samples as positives, the method avoids wrong primitive-level feature alignment that would reduce discriminability. The framework is validated on five VQA baselines (MAC, LXMERT, ViLT, mPLUG, BEiT-3) and evaluated on newly constructed GQA-SPS and VQA-SPS v2 benchmarks covering three substitution types: synonymous words, visual entities, and referents.

## Results

On GQA-SPS, the SUPS framework consistently improves all five baselines across all three substitution types. The largest gains appear for MAC, where Word SPS consistency rises from 47.5% to 55.6% (+8.1%) and Referent SPS consistency from 36.9% to 44.1% (+7.2%). For LXMERT, HM consistency improves from 65.5% to 67.3%, and for BEiT-3 from 65.3% to 66.2%. On GQA test-dev (IID), gains are more modest (e.g., BEiT-3: 60.8%→61.8%), confirming that the framework's primary benefit is OOD substitutivity rather than in-distribution accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The SUPS framework is applied to five VQA baselines (MAC, LXMERT, ViLT, mPLUG, BEiT-3) and improves all of them on GQA-SPS consistency. MAC shows the largest relative gains (e.g., +8.1% Word SPS consistency, +7.2% Referent SPS), while BEiT-3 and LXMERT, already stronger baselines, see smaller but consistent improvements of 1–2% in HM consistency. Among the nine large VLMs evaluated zero-shot (4B–17B parameters), CogVLM-17B achieves the highest HM consistency (57.7%) but still falls well short of BEiT-3+SUPS (66.2%), indicating that scale alone does not resolve substitutivity. On VQA-Rephrasings, LXMERT+SUPS outperforms LXMERT by 0.56% mean accuracy and 0.9% on the strictest consistency metric CS(4), and also surpasses BAN+CC (48.18% CS(4)) and ConClaT (53.99% CS(4)). On GQA test-dev, the framework yields modest IID gains (e.g., BEiT-3: 60.8→61.8%, mPLUG: 59.7→60.3%), confirming compatibility with in-distribution performance without degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of compositional generalization and VQA by focusing on substitutivity—a dimension of compositionality that prior V&L work has largely neglected in favor of systematicity. In NLP, substitutivity has been studied through word-level synonym substitution (Ren et al., Dankers et al., Li et al.), and in V&L, Whitehead et al. and Gou et al. addressed lexical perturbations for VQA and video localization respectively, but always confined to the language modality. The key departure here is extending substitutivity to three types of primitive substitutions (words, visual entities, referents) spanning both modalities, and proposing a support-set-based training framework that repurposes back-translation (mBART) and CLIP retrieval to construct synonymous support sets, combined with contrastive learning and feature-level reconstruction to enforce invariant representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of GQA-SPS and VQA-SPS v2 provides the first systematic, quantitative benchmarks for evaluating compositional substitutivity in VQA across multiple primitive types, filling a gap where prior evaluation was limited to sentence-level rephrasing (VQA-Rephrasings) or single-modality word substitution. The finding that large vision-language models (4B–17B parameters) still exhibit poor substitutivity despite massive training data suggests that scale alone does not resolve compositional generalization, pointing to a need for explicit structural inductive biases. Limitations include the relatively small Visual Entity SPS split (23K samples) due to GLIGEN quality constraints, the dependence on pre-computed support sets that add training overhead, and the fact that the consistency metric only measures paired-sample agreement rather than open-ended generalization. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to other compositional tasks (e.g., image captioning, grounded reasoning) and whether the three substitution types can be unified under a single generative process.
