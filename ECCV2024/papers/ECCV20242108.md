## Problem

Text-conditional generation of 3D medical imaging (CT, MRI) remains entirely unexplored, despite the critical need for synthetic volumetric data in radiology. While 2D chest X-ray generation from radiology reports has been demonstrated, extending to 3D volumes faces prohibitive computational complexity, the absence of pre-trained 3D generative models, and severe scarcity of paired 3D imaging data with corresponding reports.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242108/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form medical text prompt formatted as "{age} years old {sex}: {impression}" (e.g., "26 years old male: Findings compatible with COVID-19 pneumonia") and produces a full 3D chest CT volume at 512×512×201 resolution in Hounsfield Units clipped to [-1000, +1000]. A distinctive feature is the variable axial field-of-view: the autoregressive CT-ViT encoder can generate an arbitrary number of axial slices, accommodating different cranio-caudal coverage without architectural modification.

## Method

GenerateCT is a three-stage cascaded framework. First, a novel CT-ViT encoder (inspired by ViViT/C-ViViT) extracts spatiotemporal tokens from 3D CT volumes using sequential spatial and causal (autoregressive) attention layers, with vector quantization producing a discrete latent space; it is trained with L2, perceptual, and adversarial losses. Second, a MaskGIT-style bidirectional transformer aligns the frozen CT tokens with T5-encoded text tokens via masked token prediction, learning to reconstruct masked CT codebook IDs conditioned on the radiology report. Third, a cascaded text-conditional diffusion model (3 super-resolution steps) upsamples each slice from 128×128 to 512×512, with cross-attention to T5 text embeddings at the bottleneck replacing self-attention for memory efficiency. The key architectural insight is treating the 3D CT volume as a variable-length "video" with causal attention for flexible slice counts, combined with cascaded diffusion to decouple volumetric structure from in-plane resolution, avoiding the exponential memory cost of direct 3D diffusion.

## Results

GenerateCT (3SCM configuration) achieves the best scores across all metrics: FVD_I3D of 1092.3, FVD_CT-Net of 8.17, FID of 55.8, and CLIP score of 27.1 (close to the real-data benchmark of 27.4). In the clinical multi-abnormality classification task, data augmentation with an equal number of synthetic volumes yields an 11% improvement in mean AP and 6% in mean AUROC over the real-data-only baseline. Training exclusively on 100,000 synthetic volumes (5× the real dataset) surpasses the real-data model by 8% in mean AP and 4% in mean AUROC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenerateCT (3SCM) outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against the 2D baselines (Imagen and Stable Diffusion), the gap is dramatic: FVD_I3D drops from ~3500 to 1092, FID from ~150–160 to 55.8, and CLIP rises from 23.5–24.8 to 27.1, reflecting the severe spatial inconsistency of slice-wise 2D generation in sagittal and coronal planes. Against the 3D text-to-video baseline Phenaki, GenerateCT still leads substantially: FVD_I3D improves by 42% (1092 vs. 1887), FVD_CT-Net by 15% (8.17 vs. 9.55), FID by 46% (55.8 vs. 104.3), and CLIP by 1.9 points (27.1 vs. 25.2). The 2D baselines also suffer from prohibitive memory costs (13 GB per slice for SD), making direct 3D extension infeasible, whereas GenerateCT's cascaded architecture handles 201 slices at 512×512 resolution within a single A100 GPU. In the zero-shot external evaluation on RadChestCT, GenerateCT-generated synthetic data achieves mean AP of 0.146 vs. 0.177 for real data, and combining both yields the best result (AP 0.190, AUROC 0.623), confirming generalization to unseen prompts and external datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenerateCT extends the 2D text-conditional medical image generation line (e.g., Roentgen for chest X-rays) to the 3D volumetric domain, a setting the authors explicitly identify as unexplored. Architecturally, it repurposes video transformer concepts (ViViT's spatial-plus-causal attention, C-ViViT's autoregressive design) as a CT-specific encoder (CT-ViT), borrows MaskGIT's bidirectional masked token prediction for text–image alignment, and applies cascaded diffusion for super-resolution—combining three previously separate ideas into a unified 3D medical generation pipeline. The paper departs from prior text-to-video work by treating axial CT slices as a temporal axis with variable length, and from 2D medical generation by enforcing volumetric spatial consistency rather than slice-wise independence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first text-conditional 3D medical image generator, GenerateCT opens a new research direction with direct implications for data privacy (synthetic volumes can replace real patient data) and data scarcity in 3D radiology. The demonstration that a classifier trained solely on 100,000 synthetic volumes outperforms one trained on all 20,000 real volumes by 8% AP is a strong signal for the field, though it rests on a single dataset and classifier architecture. Key open questions include generalization beyond a single institution's CT-RATE data, the substantial computational cost (184 s per volume, 400 MB each), and whether the generated volumes are safe for any downstream clinical decision-making—the authors themselves caution against direct clinical deployment.
