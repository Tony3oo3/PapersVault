## Problem

Predictor-corrector diffusion samplers like UniPC suffer from a misalignment issue: the corrector refines the intermediate result to x̃^c, but the model output stored in the buffer was computed from the uncorrected x̃, not x̃^c. This misalignment degrades sampling quality, particularly under large classifier-free guidance scales, and naively re-computing the model output at x̃^c would double the number of function evaluations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes Gaussian noise and (for conditional sampling) a text prompt, operating on pre-trained latent diffusion models (SD1.4, SD2.1, SDXL) at resolutions from 512×512 to 1024×1024. It produces a single generated image per noise sample in as few as 5 NFE, with the compensation ratios either searched on 10 datapoints in under 5 minutes or predicted instantly via cascade polynomial regression for arbitrary CFG/NFE combinations. The method introduces no extra NFE beyond the base predictor-corrector schedule.

## Method

DC-Solver introduces dynamic compensation: at each sampling step, it approximates the missing ε_θ(x̃^c, t_i) by performing Lagrange interpolation of K previous model outputs at a shifted timestep t_i' = ρ_i·t_i + (1−ρ_i)·t_{i−1}, then replaces the last buffer entry with this interpolated estimate. The per-step compensation ratios ρ_i are optimized via backpropagation to minimize L2 distance to a 999-step DDIM ground truth trajectory on only 10 datapoints (K=2, 40 AdamW iterations, <5 min on one GPU). A cascade polynomial regression (CPR) with p1=p2=2, p3=4 then predicts ρ_i for unseen NFE/CFG configurations from sparsely searched values, enabling instant configuration changes. The dynamic compensation also functions as a plug-and-play module that boosts predictor-only solvers (DDIM, DPM-Solver++) without requiring a corrector step.

## Results

DC-Solver achieves 10.38 FID at NFE=5 on unconditional FFHQ, reducing the FID by 8.28 over UniPC (18.66) and by 4.51/4.75 over UniPC on LSUN-Church/LSUN-Bedroom. On conditional sampling, DC-Solver reaches 0.394 MSE at NFE=5 with CFG=7.5 on Stable-Diffusion-2.1 (768×768), outperforming DPM-Solver++ (0.443), DEIS (0.436), and UniPC (0.434). The advantage is consistent across SD1.4 (512×512), SD2.1 (768×768), and SDXL (1024×1024) at all NFE from 5 to 10, with the largest gains at the lowest NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-Solver consistently outperforms all three main baselines (DPM-Solver++, DEIS, UniPC) across every tested configuration. On SD2.1 at NFE=5, DC-Solver achieves 0.394 MSE versus 0.443 (DPM-Solver++), 0.436 (DEIS), and 0.434 (UniPC) — a 11.3% relative improvement over the strongest baseline. The gap widens at higher NFE: at NFE=10 on SD2.1, DC-Solver reaches 0.294 while UniPC only achieves 0.373 (a 21% relative gap). On the highest-resolution SDXL (1024×1024), DC-Solver leads by 0.029–0.091 MSE over the best baseline depending on NFE. Against DDIM (a predictor-only solver), applying dynamic compensation reduces FID on FFHQ from 57.92 to 16.56 at NFE=5, demonstrating that DC is a plug-and-play module that also benefits non-predictor-corrector solvers. The paper notes that the performance gain of DC-Solver over UniPC exceeds the differences among DPM-Solver++, DEIS, and UniPC themselves, highlighting the significance of the misalignment fix.

## Relation to Prior Work

DC-Solver builds directly on UniPC's predictor-corrector framework for fast diffusion ODE sampling, targeting the misalignment issue that UniPC itself identified but could not resolve without doubling NFE. The core idea—using a single scalar compensation ratio to control a Lagrange interpolation over previously cached model outputs—repurposes a classical numerical analysis technique to approximate the 'missing' forward pass at the corrected state. The method also sits within the broader family of exponential-integrator-based ODE solvers (DPM-Solver++, DEIS) and extends their applicability by showing that dynamic compensation can be grafted onto predictor-only solvers as well, not just predictor-corrector ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DC-Solver achieves consistent quality gains (e.g., 10.38 FID at NFE=5 on FFHQ, 0.394 MSE at NFE=5/CFG=7.5 on SD2.1) with zero extra NFE and a one-time search cost of under 5 minutes on 10 datapoints, making it a low-friction drop-in for any pre-trained diffusion model. The cascade polynomial regression further removes the need to re-search for each new NFE/CFG combination, which is critical for interactive text-to-image use. A key open question is whether the ℓ₂-based compensation objective can be adapted to SDE-based samplers, where stochasticity invalidates the deterministic trajectory-matching assumption; the authors explicitly flag this as future work.
