## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for urban driving scenes initialize Gaussians directly from sparse LiDAR points, which underutilizes the rich geometric information (depth, surface continuity) embedded in LiDAR data and fails to exploit the complementary strengths of camera imagery. This leads to incomplete geometry, limited color coverage (only ~1/3 of LiDAR points receive color), and weak depth supervision during optimization, degrading both RGB and depth synthesis quality in large-scale, viewpoint-sparse urban environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241675/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes surrounding camera images at full resolution (1920×1280 on Waymo with 3 cameras, 1600×900 on nuScenes with 6 cameras), multi-sweep LiDAR point clouds, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, and vehicle trajectory. It produces novel-view RGB images and dense depth maps rendered in real time (~90 FPS on Waymo, ~120 FPS on nuScenes on a single RTX 3090 Ti), alongside an intermediate colorized 3D mesh and a hierarchical octree feature volume. Unlike standard 3D-GS which outputs only RGB, TCLC-GS simultaneously synthesizes metric depth by rendering through the learned mesh, with sky regions explicitly made transparent via a SegFormer-based mask during training.

## Method

TCLC-GS introduces a hybrid explicit-implicit 3D representation that tightly couples LiDAR and camera data into the Gaussian Splatting pipeline. First, a hierarchical octree stores learnable multi-resolution features at node corners, trained with dual-branch MLP decoders (SDF via BCE + eikonal + smoothness losses; RGB via L1) supervised by LiDAR range measurements and projected image colors. Second, a colorized 3D mesh is extracted via marching cubes from the learned SDF, providing dense, continuous geometry and per-vertex color that far exceed the sparsity of raw LiDAR points. Third, 3D Gaussians are initialized surface-aligned on mesh triangles (following SUGAR), with geometry attributes (position, scale, rotation) from mesh vertices and appearance attributes (opacity, spherical harmonics) enriched by vertex-retrieved octree implicit features passed through shallow MLPs. Finally, dense depth maps rendered from the mesh via ray tracing provide supplementary supervision alongside RGB and sky-opacity losses, replacing the sparse LiDAR depth typically used and significantly improving depth synthesis accuracy.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, TCLC-GS achieves average PSNR 28.11, SSIM 0.86, and LPIPS 0.22 for novel-view image synthesis, outperforming the primary 3D-GS baseline (26.36 / 0.82 / 0.28) by +1.75 dB PSNR and −0.06 LPIPS. For depth synthesis, TCLC-GS reduces AbsRel from 0.42 to 0.03 and RMSE from 10.92 to 1.54 on Waymo, and from 0.34 to 0.05 and 9.38 to 3.69 on nuScenes. On nuScenes, TCLC-GS reaches PSNR 26.92 / SSIM 0.87 / LPIPS 0.21, surpassing 3D-GS (25.44 / 0.84 / 0.26). Rendering speed is approximately 90 FPS at 1920×1280 (Waymo) and 120 FPS at 1600×900 (nuScenes) on a single RTX 3090 Ti.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCLC-GS outperforms the primary 3D-GS baseline [11] on every metric: +1.75 dB PSNR, +0.04 SSIM, −0.06 LPIPS on Waymo image synthesis, and a dramatic depth improvement (AbsRel 0.03 vs 0.42, RMSE 1.54 vs 10.92). Against NeRF-based methods (NeRF [17], NeRF-W [16], Instant-NGP [18], Mip-NeRF [1], Mip-NeRF 360 [2]), TCLC-GS achieves the highest PSNR (28.11 vs 26.24–27.43) and the lowest LPIPS (0.22 vs 0.39–0.47), though its SSIM (0.86) is marginally below the best NeRF variants (0.88–0.89). Against point/mesh-based methods (Point-NeRF [27], NPLF [20], DNMP [15]), TCLC-GS leads in PSNR by 0.49–2.49 dB and in LPIPS by 0.16–0.23. On nuScenes, TCLC-GS surpasses S-NeRF [12] by +1.30 dB PSNR and −0.06 LPIPS. In running-time comparisons, TCLC-GS (~90 FPS on Waymo, ~120 FPS on nuScenes) is orders of magnitude faster than NeRF-based methods (SUDS [25] ~0.01 FPS, StreetSurf [10] ~0.10 FPS, Urban-NeRF [21] ~0.03 FPS, S-NeRF [12] ~0.02–0.04 FPS), while being somewhat slower than vanilla 3D-GS (~200–350 FPS) due to the additional octree feature retrieval and dense depth supervision overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCLC-GS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) by replacing the standard LiDAR-point initialization with a hybrid explicit-implicit representation: a colorized 3D mesh extracted via marching cubes from a learned SDF, combined with hierarchical octree features in the spirit of NGLOD. It adopts surface-aligned Gaussian placement from SUGAR (Guédon et al., 2024) and the incremental binning strategy from DrivingGaussian (Zhou et al., 2023) for long driving sequences. The key conceptual departure from prior LiDAR-GS methods (Street Gaussians, DrivingGaussian) is that LiDAR geometry is not used as a direct point initialization but is instead encoded into a continuous SDF and octree feature volume, from which both the mesh geometry and contextual appearance features are derived to enrich each Gaussian's attributes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method demonstrates that tightly coupling LiDAR and camera data through an intermediate mesh + octree representation yields substantially better depth synthesis (AbsRel 0.03 vs. 0.42 for 3D-GS) while maintaining real-time rendering (~90 FPS at 1920×1280), making it practically relevant for autonomous driving simulation and sensor simulation. A notable limitation is the dependence on LiDAR density: performance degrades on nuScenes (32-line LiDAR) relative to Waymo (denser multi-LiDAR), suggesting the mesh quality bottleneck. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences with dynamic objects, whether the octree implicit features provide meaningful gains beyond the mesh alone (the ablation shows only a small RGB delta), and whether the approach generalizes to scenes without high-quality LiDAR.
