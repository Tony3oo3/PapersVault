## Problem

Existing pre-training methods for image restoration (e.g., DegAE, IPT) require full fine-tuning of all network parameters for each new degradation task, making multi-task deployment on memory-constrained edge devices impractical. Moreover, prior pre-training strategies use either single random degradations or fixed-order degradation sequences, limiting the diversity of learned representations and their transferability to complex real-world degradations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241702/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (rain, raindrop, noise, haze, or blur) and produces a restored clean image at the same resolution. During pre-training, clean images are paired with synthetically degraded versions generated by randomly composing 1–6 degradations from 5 synthetic functions; during fine-tuning, small real-world paired datasets (as few as 16–128 samples) are used. The approach is backbone-agnostic, operating on both CNN (NAFNet, 29M params) and Transformer (Restormer, 26M params) architectures, and the LoRA adapters can be merged into frozen weights at inference with no additional latency.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary components. First, PROD (Pre-training with Random Order Degradation) randomly applies 1 to N synthetic degradations (N=6, from H=5 degradation functions) to clean images, yielding ~137K distinct degradation combinations—roughly 4K× more than single-degradation or fixed-order baselines—producing a more generalizable pre-trained backbone. Second, CoLoRA (Contribution-based Low-Rank Adaptation) uses FAIG (Filter Attribution based on Integral Gradient) scores, computed between the pre-trained and fully fine-tuned model, to quantify each layer's contribution to a new task; it then assigns a per-layer LoRA rank r_i via a threshold-based scaling rule (scale factor α for high-contribution layers with normalized FAIG > 0.5, β otherwise), so that only ~7% of total parameters (2M for NAFNet, 2.9M for Restormer) are tuned per task while bias and normalization layers are also updated. This adaptive rank allocation outperforms uniform-rank LoRA and full fine-tuning with far fewer parameters, and the small per-task adapters can be stored independently for multi-task edge deployment.

## Results

On six real-world IR tasks (Rain, Raindrop, Rain&Raindrop, Haze, Blur, Noise), CoLoRA with PROD achieves PSNR within 0.1–0.8 dB of full fine-tuning while tuning only 2M parameters (7%) for NAFNet and 2.9M (11%) for Restormer, versus 29M/26M (100%) for full tuning. For example, on the Rain task with NAFNet, CoLoRA w. PROD reaches 27.37 dB vs. 27.42 dB for PROD+Full; on Raindrop it actually exceeds full tuning (25.22 vs. 25.02 dB). The PROD pre-training alone (without CoLoRA) achieves state-of-the-art PSNR across all six tasks after full fine-tuning, outperforming DegAE by 0.1–0.5 dB on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest pre-training baseline DegAE [43] with full fine-tuning, CoLoRA w. PROD (NAFNet) wins on Rain (+0.15 dB), Raindrop (+0.26 dB), Rain&Raindrop (+0.53 dB), and Blur (+0.09 dB), while losing slightly on Haze (−0.06 dB); with Restormer it wins on Rain (+0.24 dB), Raindrop (+0.01 dB), Rain&Raindrop (+0.07 dB), and Blur (+0.43 dB) but loses on Haze (−0.24 dB). Compared to random-init full fine-tuning (NAFNet+Full, Restormer+Full), CoLoRA w. PROD outperforms on all five reported tasks for both architectures, confirming that the PROD pre-training provides a meaningful initialization advantage. The PROD pre-training itself (with full fine-tuning) surpasses DegAE+Full on 4 of 5 tasks for NAFNet (Rain, Raindrop, Rain&Raindrop, Blur) and on 3 of 5 for Restormer (Rain, Raindrop, Rain&Raindrop), establishing it as a stronger pre-training strategy. In the limited-data regime (16–128 training samples), CoLoRA w. PROD with 64 samples outperforms DegAE+Full and Random-Init+Full with 128 samples by 0.22 dB and 0.36 dB respectively on NAFNet, demonstrating that the parameter-efficient approach is particularly advantageous when real paired data is scarce. Task-specific SOTA baselines (CCN, SMOKE, PVDNet, etc.) are outperformed by CoLoRA w. PROD on their respective tasks, e.g., 27.37 dB vs. 26.83 dB on Rain (vs. CCN) and 32.08 dB vs. 32.22 dB on Blur (vs. PVDNet, a slight loss of 0.14 dB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm from NLP and high-level vision into low-level image restoration, extending it with a key departure: instead of a fixed rank across all layers, CoLoRA allocates per-layer rank adaptively based on FAIG (Filter Attribution via Integrated Gradients) scores that quantify each layer's contribution to a new task. On the pre-training side, PROD generalizes prior synthetic-degradation strategies (single random degradation in IPT/EDT, fixed-order multi-degradation in DegAE) by applying 1-to-N random-order combinations of degradation functions, expanding the degradation space from ~30K to ~137K representations. The work also contrasts with all-in-one IR methods that require retraining a unified model for new tasks, positioning CoLoRA as a plug-and-play adapter approach that keeps the pre-trained backbone frozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling multi-task IR deployment on memory-constrained edge devices: storing ~2M adapter parameters per task versus ~29M for a full fine-tuned network is a substantial reduction when many degradation types must coexist. The PROD pre-training's ability to outperform fixed-order and single-degradation baselines by 0.05–0.10 dB with 4× fewer training samples suggests a more data-efficient path to robust pre-trained IR backbones. However, the paper acknowledges that adapter parameters still accumulate linearly with the number of tasks, and the FAIG-based allocation is calibrated on six specific degradation types, leaving open whether the contribution hierarchy generalizes to truly unseen or compound real-world degradations. The modest PSNR gaps (often <0.1 dB) between CoLoRA and full fine-tuning also raise the question of whether the efficiency gains justify the added architectural complexity in settings where memory is not the binding constraint.
