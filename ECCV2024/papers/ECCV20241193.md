## Problem

CARLA Leaderboard v2 introduces 39 quasi-realistic urban driving corner cases (e.g., two-way lane invasions, aggressive junction negotiation) that no prior method—rule-based autopilot, model-free RL (Roach), or imitation learning—has successfully solved. The long-tail sparsity of these scenarios, combined with the data inefficiency of model-free RL in such a complex environment, makes learning a competent planner highly non-trivial. This paper presents the first model-based RL approach to achieve 100% route completion across all 39 scenarios.

## Input / Output

The planner consumes privileged BEV semantic segmentation masks (binary channels for static elements like route/lanes in a single mask, and T history masks for dynamic objects such as vehicles and pedestrians) concatenated with ego-vehicle state vectors (speed, previous control action, relative height). The output is a discrete action over 30 pre-defined throttle/steer/brake combinations, chosen to reduce action-space complexity. Unlike end-to-end baselines (e.g., TCP), the method bypasses perception entirely by using simulator-provided ground-truth object states rather than raw camera or LiDAR inputs.

## Method

Think2Drive trains a DreamerV3-style RSSM world model on BEV mask transitions, then uses it as a neural simulator in which an actor-critic planner learns entirely in latent space via parallel rollouts, eliminating costly interaction with the CARLA physics engine. The planner maximizes expected discounted return predicted by the world model's reward head, with a two-hot bucket-sorted critic for stable value estimation and a 15-step horizon clipped by the critic's value estimate. To address AD-specific obstacles, the method introduces: (1) a mid-training reset of all planner weights (keeping the world model fixed) to escape contradictory local optima across scenarios, (2) an automated scenario generator that splits routes into short, scenario-dense segments, (3) termination-priority replay that oversamples frames near episode endings, (4) a steering-cost penalty for heading stability, (5) a curriculum warm-up on simple lane-following before complex scenarios, and (6) an incremental planner-to-world-model training ratio (up to 4:1) to accelerate convergence.

## Results

On the proposed CornerCaseRepo benchmark, Think2Drive achieves a driving score of 83.8±1 and a Weighted Driving Score of 89.0±0.2 with 99.6% route completion, substantially outperforming the model-free Roach baseline (57.5±9 DS, 54.8±0.5 WDS, 96.4% RC). On the official CARLA Leaderboard v2 test routes, Think2Drive attains a driving score of 56.8 and 98.6% route completion, while the model-free PPO expert scores only 0.7 and 1.0% respectively. Training completes in 3 days on a single A6000 GPU, and the model handles all 39 quasi-realistic scenarios with success rates ranging from 0.61 (Accident TwoWays) to 1.0 (Hard Brake, Highway CutIn).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Roach [45], a model-free RL (PPO) expert widely used in end-to-end AD. Think2Drive outperforms Roach by a large margin on CornerCaseRepo: driving score 83.8 vs. 57.5 (+26.3), Weighted DS 89.0 vs. 54.8 (+34.2), and route completion 99.6% vs. 96.4%. Think2Drive also reduces vehicle collisions from 8.42 to 1.2 per route and pedestrian collisions from 0.85 to 0.16. On the official CARLA v2 routes, the model-free PPO expert achieves a near-zero driving score (0.7) and 1.0% route completion, while Think2Drive reaches 56.8 DS and 98.6% RC, demonstrating that model-based RL is essential for the quasi-realistic v2 scenarios. The end-to-end student TCP [42] (trained via imitation on Think2Drive data with raw sensor input) scores only 36.4 DS, highlighting the performance gap introduced by perception and imitation. The paper also notes that PPO's poor performance on v2 stems from its inability to escape local optima even with the reset technique, whereas the world model provides dense, accurate rewards that stabilize learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Think2Drive directly adopts the DreamerV3 architecture (RSSM, symlog loss, two-hot critic, actor-critic training) as its world model and planner backbone, but is the first to successfully apply this latent world model paradigm to quasi-realistic autonomous driving. It departs from the dominant model-free RL line in AD (Roach, MaRLn, PPO-based agents) which the paper shows fails entirely on CARLA v2, and from earlier model-based RL for driving (Diehl et al., Henaff et al.) that only addressed simple benchmarks. The paper also introduces CornerCaseRepo as a scenario-decoupled evaluation benchmark and the Weighted Driving Score metric, both absent from prior CARLA-based work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first reported success on all 39 CARLA v2 scenarios with a purely learning-based planner, validating model-based RL as a viable paradigm for AD where model-free methods hit a wall due to data inefficiency and long-tail corner cases. The CornerCaseRepo benchmark and WDS metric provide the community with tools for fine-grained, scenario-level evaluation that the official long-route leaderboard lacks. Key limitations include reliance on privileged BEV inputs (not raw sensors), the large performance gap when distilling to a raw-sensor student (TCP drops from 83.8 to 36.4 driving score), and the fact that the world model is simulator-specific. Open questions include whether this approach transfers to real-world driving data and whether the engineering bricks (especially the reset technique) generalize beyond CARLA.
