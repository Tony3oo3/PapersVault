## Problem

State-of-the-art motion diffusion models (e.g., MDM, MotionDiffuse) require ~1000 denoising steps, yielding inference times of 12+ seconds per sequence and precluding real-time applications. Existing acceleration strategies either adopt a two-stage latent diffusion pipeline (MLD) that is non-end-to-end and sensitive to embedding quality, or naively increase the DDIM step size, which degrades quality because the denoising distribution becomes non-Gaussian at large step sizes. The paper asks: can one explicitly model the complex, multimodal denoising distribution at large step sizes to enable few-step sampling without quality loss?

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a textual description (encoded by a frozen CLIP-ViT-L-14 into a 1024-d vector) or an action label (learnable embedding) as the condition c, plus a 64-dim random latent z ~ N(0,I) for diversity. The output is an N-frame 3D human motion sequence where each frame is represented by joint rotations, 3D joint positions, velocities, and a binary foot-contact mask (following MDM's representation). At inference, classifier-free guidance with a scale s interpolates between conditioned and unconditioned predictions to trade off fidelity and diversity, and the entire generation completes in T ≤ 10 denoising steps (0.02–0.05 s per sequence).

## Method

EMDM replaces the standard Gaussian denoising assumption with a conditional denoising diffusion GAN that explicitly models the complex multimodal distribution q(x_{t-1}|x_t) at large step sizes. A 12-layer transformer generator G_θ(x_t, z, c, t) predicts the clean motion x̂_0, and a 7-layer MLP discriminator D_φ(x_{t-1}, x_t, c, t) is trained to distinguish whether x_{t-1} is a plausible denoised result of x_t, with both conditioned on the control signal c and timestep t. The generator is jointly optimized with an adversarial loss and task-specific geometric losses (reconstruction, forward-kinematics joint positions, foot contact, and joint velocities) to suppress artifacts that the discriminator alone cannot penalize. During sampling, posterior sampling (Alg. 2) recovers x_{t-1} from the predicted x̂_0 and current x_t, and classifier-free guidance blends conditioned/unconditioned outputs. The entire pipeline is end-to-end trainable with T ≤ 10 steps, avoiding the two-stage VAE+latent-diffusion bottleneck of MLD while matching or exceeding its quality.

## Results

On HumanML3D (text-to-motion), EMDM achieves a FID of 0.112 with a running time of 0.280 ms/frame, making it the fastest method evaluated while remaining competitive with ReMoDiffuse (FID 0.103, 0.959 ms/frame). On KIT, EMDM attains the best R-Precision Top-1 (0.443) and fastest inference (0.284 ms/frame) among all methods. On HumanAct12 (action-to-motion), EMDM achieves 99.1% action recognition accuracy and 0.337 ms/frame, outperforming MDM (41.154 ms/frame) by over 120× in speed while matching its accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMDM achieves the fastest inference speed (0.280 ms/frame) across all three benchmarks, outpacing the next-fastest two-stage methods MLD (0.598 ms/frame) and T2M-GPT (0.886 ms/frame) by 2–3×, and surpassing iterative diffusion baselines MDM (62.5 ms/frame) and MotionDiffuse (38.2 ms/frame) by over 100×. In terms of motion quality (FID), EMDM (0.112) is nearly on par with ReMoDiffuse (0.103) on HumanML3D, though ReMoDiffuse requires a reference motion dataset at inference and is a non-end-to-end two-stage pipeline. On KIT, EMDM wins on all R-Precision metrics (Top-1: 0.443 vs. 0.427 for ReMoDiffuse) and running time, but trails ReMoDiffuse on FID (0.261 vs. 0.155). For action-to-motion on HumanAct12, EMDM matches MDM's accuracy (99.1% vs. 99.0%) while being 122× faster, and slightly underperforms MLD on FID (0.084 vs. 0.077) but leads on accuracy (99.1% vs. 96.4%). Overall, EMDM trades a small FID gap against the best retrieval-based method for a substantial speed advantage and end-to-end trainability.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMDM adapts the denoising diffusion GAN (DDGAN) framework from Xiao et al. [85] to conditional human motion generation, introducing a conditional generator-discriminator pair that jointly considers time step and control signals (text or action labels) to model the complex, non-Gaussian denoising distribution at large step sizes. This departs from standard motion diffusion models (MDM, MotionDiffuse) that require ~1000 denoising steps under a Gaussian assumption, and from DDIM-style acceleration that naively increases step size and degrades quality. It also contrasts with the two-stage latent diffusion approach of MLD, which requires a separately trained VAE and is not end-to-end trainable. Geometric losses (joint positions, foot contact, velocities) are borrowed from MDM to compensate for the GAN's insufficient motion-specific constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMDM achieves a roughly 50× speedup over MDM (0.05s vs. 12.3s per text-to-motion sequence) while maintaining competitive FID and diversity, making real-time motion synthesis feasible for interactive applications such as games, VR, and robotics. The end-to-end training pipeline simplifies deployment compared to two-stage methods like MLD. However, the generated motions lack physical plausibility (floating, ground penetration), and the model currently accepts only textual or action-label inputs, leaving open the integration of physics-based simulation and broader modalities (video, music) for online synthesis.
