## Problem

Existing pose-guided video generation methods either require large-scale human video datasets for pre-training or, in the one-shot setting, depend on actual video frames corresponding to the target poses (as in video editing). This paper tackles the harder problem of generating a personalized video that follows arbitrary target poses while preserving identity from a single reference video, without any ground-truth frames matching those poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241171/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference video (as few as 8 frames at 512×512), a target pose sequence of M frames extracted via DWpose (which includes body, face, and hand keypoints), and a text prompt. It produces an M-frame video that follows the target poses while retaining the identity and background of the reference video. Notably, the target poses can come from a different individual, be artificially designed, or originate from the same person, and training completes in roughly 2 minutes on a single RTX3090 GPU.

## Method

PoseCrafter builds on Stable Diffusion with ControlNet for pose conditioning and adds keyframe and temporal attention modules for video generation, fine-tuning only 68.79M parameters on a single video. The key inference-time innovation is a reference-frame selection-and-insertion strategy: the frame from the training video whose pose is closest (in L2 distance) to the inference poses is selected, replicated to form a pseudo reference video, and DDIM-inverted to initialize all latent variables; the reference pose is then prepended to the inference pose sequence so that temporal attention anchors the generated frames to the reference identity. A second technique, latent editing, computes per-frame affine transformation matrices from facial and hand landmarks (solved via least squares) and applies them to the inverted latent, selectively transferring the transformed face/hand regions to correct pose-mismatch artifacts. This avoids the need for corresponding target-pose videos entirely and outperforms baselines pre-trained on hundreds of videos across 8 metrics.

## Results

On the TED dataset, PoseCrafter (8 training frames) achieves SSIM 0.810, PSNR 23.92, LPIPS 0.142, FID 20.40, FVD 80.01, CLIP-I 0.906, and CLIP-T 0.954, outperforming all baselines on every one of the 8 metrics. On TikTok, PoseCrafter with 32 training frames reaches FID 39.65 and FVD 362.09, surpassing MagicAnimate (FID 57.60, FVD 374.40) and ControlVideo (FID 56.50, FVD 489.30). Notably, PoseCrafter requires only ~12 minutes of training on a single RTX 3090 GPU, whereas baselines like MagicAnimate and Disco are pre-trained on hundreds of videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseCrafter dominates all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. On TED, the gap is most dramatic: PoseCrafter (8 frames) improves FID from 46.96 (MagicAnimate) and 29.01 (ControlVideo) to 20.40, and FVD from 194.03 and 109.36 to 80.01, while also achieving the highest SSIM (0.810 vs. 0.771 for ControlVideo) and CLIP-I (0.906 vs. 0.866). On TikTok, PoseCrafter with 32 frames outperforms MagicAnimate on all 8 metrics (e.g., FID 39.65 vs. 57.60; FVD 362.09 vs. 374.40), and with only 8 frames it already beats ControlVideo on FID (48.09 vs. 56.50) and FVD (440.49 vs. 489.30). Fine-tuned Disco (Disco†) actually degrades relative to its pre-trained version on TikTok (FID worsens from 76.40 to 65.73, FVD from 689.23 to 807.35), which the authors attribute to overfitting from fine-tuning 294.95M ControlNet parameters, whereas PoseCrafter fine-tunes only 68.79M parameters. MagicAnimate, despite being pre-trained on ~350 TikTok videos, shows limited generalization to TED (SSIM drops to 0.498), while PoseCrafter trained on a single TED video achieves SSIM 0.810.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseCrafter directly extends ControlVideo [51], a one-shot video editing framework built on Stable Diffusion and ControlNet with temporal/keyframe attention modules, by replacing its reliance on DDIM inversion of target-pose ground-truth videos with a novel inference pipeline: reference-frame selection, pose insertion, and affine latent editing. This conceptual shift moves the problem from video editing (where corresponding frames exist) to pose-guided video generation (where only poses and a single reference video are available), a setting that prior one-shot methods like ControlVideo and fine-tuned Disco cannot handle. The paper also positions itself against large-scale pre-trained image-to-video methods (Disco, MagicAnimate, Animate Anyone) that require extensive human video corpora, arguing that a single open-domain video suffices when paired with the right inference strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 8 frames of fine-tuning (~2 minutes on one GPU) can match or exceed methods pre-trained on hundreds of videos provides a practical, low-cost pathway to personalized pose-guided video generation, lowering the barrier for applications in entertainment and digital humans. However, the approach inherits ControlNet's limitations on complex poses (e.g., crossed fingers), and body proportions shift when using poses from individuals with different morphologies. Open questions include whether the affine latent editing generalizes to other body parts or more complex motions, whether the method scales to longer videos, and whether the reference-frame selection strategy remains effective when training and inference poses differ substantially.
