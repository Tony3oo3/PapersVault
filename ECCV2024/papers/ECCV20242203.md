## Problem

Reverse diffusion sampling and score distillation are the two dominant paradigms for image manipulation with latent diffusion models, yet they have complementary weaknesses: reverse sampling requires architecture adjustments or feature engineering to incorporate task-specific constraints, while score distillation is model-agnostic but suffers from mode collapse due to its non-stochastic optimization. It remains unclear how to synergistically combine these two approaches into a single framework that retains the diversity of sampling and the flexibility of distillation.

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained LDM (Stable Diffusion v1.5, 512×512 images, 200 NFE). For image editing, it takes a source image (inverted via DDIM) and a text prompt describing the editing direction, producing an edited image while preserving unmentioned content. For inpainting, it additionally takes a binary mask and text prompts per masked region. For SVG vectorization, the generator is a differentiable rasterizer (DiffVG) parameterized by closed Bézier curves, and the input is a noisy measurement (downsampled or blurred) plus a text caption, producing SVG path parameters that, when rasterized, reconstruct the image.

## Method

DreamSampler unifies reverse sampling and score distillation by showing that each DDIM sampling step is a proximal update around the Tweedie posterior mean, and that the proximal loss is algebraically equivalent to the score distillation loss up to a constant factor. The general formulation at timestep t adds structured noise (from the previous reverse-sampling step) to the generator output g(ψ), computes a distillation gradient between the model-predicted noise and the added noise, and updates ψ with an optional regularization term R(g(ψ)) that encodes data consistency or text guidance. For image editing, this reduces to a one-step DDS update but with the key difference that the noise added to the updated latent is the model-estimated noise from the prior timestep rather than fresh Gaussian noise, which preserves bias components and avoids mode collapse. For inverse problems, the regularization term combines masked data consistency with localized text-conditioned distillation, enabling per-region text guidance in inpainting and joint reconstruction-plus-vectorization in SVG recovery.

## Results

On the image editing benchmark (Table 1), DreamSampler achieves the best DINO-ViT structure distance (Dist) across all three tasks: 0.029 (Cat→Dog), 0.038 (Horse→Zebra), and 0.025 (Cat→Cat w/ glasses), while also attaining the highest CLIP accuracy on Horse→Zebra (95.2%). On text-guided inpainting (Table 2), DreamSampler achieves the best PSNR (24.61 dB for Glasses, 27.90 dB for Smile) and best FID (27.10 and 24.33) among all baselines, with comparable CLIP similarity. For SVG vectorization from noisy measurements, DreamSampler outperforms a multi-stage pipeline (PSLD + LIVE + VectorFusion/PDS) by jointly solving the inverse problem and updating SVG parameters via score distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamSampler achieves the best structure distance (Dist) on all three editing tasks, indicating superior preservation of source-image structure while applying edits. On Horse→Zebra, it also achieves the highest CLIP accuracy (95.2%) among all methods, surpassing EBCA (90.4%) and DDIM+word swap (94.0%). On Cat→Dog, EBCA edges out DreamSampler on CLIP accuracy (93.7% vs 90.3%), but DreamSampler's Dist is substantially lower (0.029 vs 0.040), reflecting better fidelity. On Cat→Cat w/ glasses, DreamSampler's CLIP accuracy (48.3%) trails EBCA (81.1%) and p2p-zero (71.2%), though it still achieves the best Dist (0.025); the paper notes some baselines inflate CLIP accuracy by generating glasses unaturally. For inpainting, DreamSampler outperforms both Stable-Inpaint and TReg on PSNR and FID for both Glasses and Smile prompts, while achieving comparable CLIP similarity, suggesting better reconstruction quality through its data-consistency regularization. For SVG vectorization, DreamSampler surpasses the multi-stage PSLD+LIVE+VectorFusion/PDS pipeline by avoiding error accumulation from separate reconstruction and vectorization steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lineages in diffusion-based image manipulation: reverse diffusion sampling (DDIM, Song et al. 2020) and score distillation sampling (DreamFusion, Poole et al. 2022). The key theoretical bridge is showing that the DDIM sampling step is a proximal update whose objective is algebraically equivalent to the SDS loss, thereby subsuming both approaches under a single regularized latent optimization framework. It also recovers Delta Denoising Score (Hertz et al. 2023) as a special case and extends TReg (Kim et al. 2023) by decoupling text guidance from data consistency, enabling per-region text prompts in inpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification provides a principled design space: practitioners can now plug arbitrary regularization terms (data consistency, spatially-localized text guidance, measurement constraints) into the sampling loop without architectural modifications, while retaining the stochasticity that prevents mode collapse. The SVG vectorization-from-blurry-measurement application demonstrates a genuinely new use case combining inverse imaging with generative priors. Open questions include whether the per-timestep latent optimization scales efficiently to 3D representations (NeRF), how the framework behaves with newer diffusion architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and whether the stochastic component's mode-collapse prevention advantage holds quantitatively in high-dimensional parameter spaces.
