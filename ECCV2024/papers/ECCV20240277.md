## Problem

Existing point cloud completion methods rely on Farthest Point Sampling for local neighborhood selection, making them sensitive to noisy inputs, and either compress observations into a single global feature vector (losing varied local patterns) or use local anchor queries (lacking global structural context). There is no framework that simultaneously achieves global geometric perception and local detail disentanglement in a noise-robust, resolution-flexible manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240277/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse partial point cloud (e.g., 2048 points on MVP/PCN) and produces a dense complete point cloud (up to 16,384 points). A key design property is resolution flexibility: by varying the number of 3D viewpoints sampled from the unit sphere, the AED module can output an arbitrary number of points, enabling multi-resolution completion (512, 1024, 2048 points) within a single network. The encoder down-samples to 512 then 64 points, while the decoder hierarchically up-samples back to full resolution.

## Method

AEDNet centers on an Adaptive Embedding and Disentanglement (AED) module used as both encoder down-sampler and decoder up-sampler. The embedding operator employs a modified slot attention that replaces Gaussian-noise slot initialization with a learnable dictionary mapping and adds self-attention across slots to forge inter-slot semantic links, grouping input points into M embeddings each attending to a specific object part. The multiview-aware disentanglement operator distributes n 3D viewpoints on a unit sphere via the Fibonacci sequence, computes relative position encodings between viewpoints and input points, and uses cross-attention to project geometric details from the embeddings onto these external viewpoints, then regresses point coordinates and features. The overall pipeline follows a coarse-to-fine strategy: the encoder (AED + Point Transformer) extracts features, a seed generator predicts an initial 512-point shape fused with input points via differentiable Top-K, and three GAED up-sampling stages progressively refine to 2048 points, trained end-to-end with Chamfer Distance, repulsion, and partial-matching losses.

## Results

On the MVP dataset (16,384 points), AEDNet achieves a CD of 2.24×10⁻⁴ and F-Score@1% of 0.832, outperforming the previous best (VAPCNet: CD 2.40, F1 0.829). At the sparser 2,048-point setting, AEDNet records CD 5.12 and F1 0.522, again leading all baselines. On the PCN dataset, AEDNet attains the lowest average L₁ Chamfer Distance of 6.52×10⁻³, narrowly surpassing AnchorFormer (6.59) and VAPCNet (7.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AEDNet outperforms all 19 compared baselines on both the MVP and PCN datasets across every reported metric. On MVP at 16,384 points, AEDNet (CD 2.24) improves over the nearest competitor VAPCNet (CD 2.40) by ~6.7% and over AnchorFormer (CD 2.60) by ~13.8%; the gap widens at sparser resolutions (e.g., 2,048 points: AEDNet 5.12 vs. VAPCNet 5.40). On the PCN dataset, AEDNet achieves the lowest average L₁ CD of 6.52, edging out AnchorFormer (6.59) by ~1% and VAPCNet (7.02) by ~7%; the margin is larger on individual categories such as watercraft (5.74 vs. 6.03 for AnchorFormer) and table (5.82 vs. 6.03). The per-category MVP results (Table 1) show the largest relative gains on challenging categories like lamp (CD 2.79 vs. VRCNet's 5.57, a ~50% reduction) and skateboard (0.81 vs. 1.57). No baseline is beaten by AEDNet on any metric in any table, and the method consistently leads on both CD and F-Score@1% across all four resolution levels on MVP.

## Relation to Prior Work

AEDNet builds directly on the slot attention mechanism (Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020), modifying it with dictionary-mapping initialization and self-attention-based inter-slot interactions to better handle the complex, repetitive geometries of 3D objects. It departs from the dominant FPS + KNN paradigm used in SnowflakeNet, AnchorFormer, and PoinTr for local neighborhood construction, replacing it with learned global embeddings that establish long-range dependencies. The multiview-aware disentanglement operator, which distributes viewpoints via Fibonacci sequences on a unit sphere to observe geometry externally, represents a conceptual shift from prior methods that query shapes from internal anchor points or global feature vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that combining global slot-based embedding with external multiview disentanglement yields consistent SOTA improvements across both MVP and PCN benchmarks, and the AED module's flexibility (arbitrary point counts via viewpoint adjustment) makes it a practical building block for hierarchical up/down-sampling. However, improvements over the prior SOTA (AnchorFormer) are modest (e.g., 6.52 vs 6.59 L1-CD on PCN), and evaluation is limited to synthetic CAD models with 16 categories, leaving open questions about robustness to real-world sensor noise, larger category counts, and whether the Fibonacci viewpoint strategy generalizes to non-convex or highly articulated shapes.
