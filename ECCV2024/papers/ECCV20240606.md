## Problem

GLIGEN, a widely used spatial-grounding extension of Stable Diffusion, frequently omits details from the text prompt when bounding boxes are provided, a failure mode the authors term 'description omission.' Their analysis traces this to the sequential wiring of gated self-attention (spatial grounding) before cross-attention (textual grounding) in each U-Net layer, which causes spatial cues to dominate and suppress textual conditioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240606/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a free-form text prompt and a set of axis-aligned bounding boxes each paired with a short text label, and outputs a single RGB image at the resolution of the underlying Stable Diffusion v1.4 model (512×512). Crucially, the rewiring is applied purely at inference time to the official pretrained GLIGEN checkpoint—no retraining, no new parameters, and no change in generation time or memory footprint—so the input format and output are identical to standard GLIGEN.

## Method

ReGround rewires the attention modules in every U-Net layer from a sequential pipeline (self-attention → gated self-attention → cross-attention) to a parallel one: both gated self-attention and cross-attention now receive the same input F_SA (the self-attention output), and their outputs are summed together with the residual connection. The key empirical insight enabling this is that gated self-attention does not require cross-attention to follow it for spatial grounding to be preserved, while cross-attention was originally designed to operate on F_SA in the base LDM. Because the pretrained GLIGEN weights were trained under the sequential architecture, the authors verify that simply reconfiguring the forward pass at inference time—without any fine-tuning—already yields substantial gains: on MS-COCO-2014, ReGround recovers 70.25% of the CLIP-score improvement that GLIGEN achieves by reducing its scheduled-sampling parameter γ from 1.0 to 0.1, while sacrificing only 3.31% of the corresponding YOLO-score drop. The same rewiring also improves downstream frameworks that build on GLIGEN, such as BoxDiff.

## Results

On MS-COCO-2014 at γ=1.0, ReGround achieves a CLIP score of 31.29 (vs. GLIGEN's 30.44) while maintaining a YOLO score of 56.96 (vs. GLIGEN's 58.13), capturing 70.25% of GLIGEN's total CLIP improvement with only a 3.31% YOLO compromise. On NSR-1K-GPT-Counting, ReGround reaches CLIP 33.20 (vs. GLIGEN's 32.46) with only a 3.69% YOLO score compromise. In a user study, ReGround is preferred over GLIGEN 70.05% vs. 29.95%, and PickScore favors ReGround 55.66% vs. 44.34% on MS-COCO-2017. ReGround also consistently achieves lower FID than GLIGEN, indicating better image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is GLIGEN (Li et al., CVPR 2023), the original sequential-attention architecture that ReGround rewires. ReGround wins on textual grounding across all datasets: +0.85 CLIP on MS-COCO-2014, +0.74 CLIP on NSR-1K-GPT-Counting, and a 1.57× larger CLIP gap on the COCO-Drop subset where 50% of bounding boxes are removed. Spatial grounding is nearly preserved: YOLO drops only 1.17 points on MS-COCO-2014 (56.96 vs. 58.13) and ~1.44 points on NSR-1K-GPT, compared to GLIGEN's catastrophic 35.38-point YOLO drop when γ is reduced to 0.1 to recover text fidelity. In the user study, ReGround is preferred 70.05% of the time, and PickScore favors it 55.66% (MS-COCO-2017) and 57.57% (COCO-Drop). ReGround also achieves consistently lower FID than GLIGEN, indicating the rewiring improves image quality as a side benefit. The advantage extends to downstream frameworks: when BoxDiff uses ReGround as its backbone instead of GLIGEN, it achieves higher CLIP scores at equivalent YOLO scores and better reflects text-prompt-only entities (e.g., 'sunset') in generated images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GLIGEN (Li et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced gated self-attention as a plug-in module for spatial grounding in Latent Diffusion Models. It positions itself within the broader landscape of layout-guided generation (Layout2Im, LostGAN, OC-GAN) and training-free guidance techniques (FreeU, cross-attention guidance), but departs from all of them by targeting the internal wiring of an already-trained model rather than proposing a new training procedure or guidance signal. The key conceptual departure is identifying the sequential GSA→CA arrangement as the root cause of description omission and replacing it with a parallel configuration, a modification that requires no retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because GLIGEN serves as a backbone for numerous downstream systems (BoxDiff, LayoutGPT, GroundingDINO-based pipelines), a zero-cost rewiring that improves the text-spatial grounding trade-off has immediate practical value for the entire ecosystem. The finding that a model trained with sequential attention benefits from parallel rewiring at inference time suggests that architectural inductive biases can be partially decoupled from training-time wiring, opening questions about whether similar rewiring strategies apply to other plug-in modules or DiT-based architectures. Limitations include evaluation only on Stable Diffusion v1.4 with bounding-box conditioning; generalization to segmentation masks, sketches, or newer model families remains untested.
