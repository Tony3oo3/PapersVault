## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution models require many sequential denoising steps (e.g., 200 DDIM steps) to produce high-quality results, making them computationally prohibitive for practical use at high resolutions. Reducing the number of steps to one causes a drastic quality drop, as the model cannot adequately solve the generative task in a single pass. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether a diffusion SR model can be trained to achieve state-of-the-art quality in a single inference step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240761/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (e.g., 128×128 for ×4 or 64×64 for ×8 magnification) and produces a corresponding high-resolution image (512×512). The LR image is bicubically upsampled to the target resolution before being projected into the latent space of a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 autoencoder, where it is concatenated with the noisy HR latent as conditioning. At inference, only a single DDIM step is performed, and the resulting latent is decoded by a fine-tuned decoder to yield the final HR image.

## Method

The core contribution is scale distillation, a progressive training strategy that iteratively simplifies the SR task: a teacher is first trained on a lower scale factor (e.g., ×2) using standard diffusion training on raw data, then frozen and used to supervise a student trained on a higher scale factor (e.g., ×4) by replacing the ground-truth target with the teacher's prediction. This provides two advantages: the supervisory signal adapts to the current noise level (unlike standard training which uses the same GT for all timesteps), and the target is more accurate because the teacher operates on a less degraded input. The process is repeated iteratively (×2→×4→×8), with each student initialized from its teacher's weights. After obtaining a strong one-step diffusion model, the U-Net is frozen and the autoencoder decoder is fine-tuned on top of the single-step output, yielding a model that outperforms 200-step baselines with only one inference step.

## Results

On DIV2K with RealESRGAN degradations (×4), YONOS-SR achieves FID 21.86, LPIPS 0.2310, PSNR 24.74, SSIM 0.6428, and MUSIQ 70.30 using only 1 DDIM step, outperforming StableSR (FID 24.44, 200 steps) and LDM (FID 26.47, 200 steps) across all five metrics. On real-world datasets (RealSR, DRealSR, DPED-iPhone), YONOS-SR achieves the best LPIPS and MUSIQ scores in every comparison, e.g., LPIPS 0.2479 on RealSR and MUSIQ 66.26 on DRealSR. For ×8 magnification on ImageNet, YONOS-SR achieves FID 34.59 and LPIPS 0.241, substantially outperforming the next-best P2L (FID 51.81, LPIPS 0.386) while using only 1 step versus 200+ steps for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YONOS-SR (1 step) outperforms all diffusion-based baselines: StableSR (200 steps) is beaten by 2.58 FID points and 4.42 MUSIQ points on DIV2K-RealESRGAN, while LDM (200 steps) trails by 4.61 FID and 1.97 MUSIQ. ResShift (4 steps) is surpassed by 8.59 FID and 3.28 MUSIQ on the same benchmark. Among non-diffusion GAN-based methods, YONOS-SR leads BSRGAN by 22.36 FID and 9.11 MUSIQ, Real-ESRGAN+ by 15.78 FID and 9.25 MUSIQ, and DASR by 27.30 FID and 15.11 MUSIQ on DIV2K-RealESRGAN. On real-world datasets, YONOS-SR achieves the best LPIPS and MUSIQ across RealSR, DRealSR, and DPED-iPhone, with the largest margin on DRealSR (MUSIQ 66.26 vs. 58.51 for StableSR). The one exception is DIV2K bicubic PSNR, where DASR (28.55) and BSRGAN (27.32) slightly exceed YONOS-SR (26.26), though YONOS-SR still leads on LPIPS (0.1703 vs. 0.1696 for DASR). For ×8 magnification on ImageNet, YONOS-SR achieves FID 34.59 vs. 51.81 for P2L and 60.36 for GML-DPS, confirming the advantage extends to higher scale factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022) as its backbone and draws conceptual inspiration from progressive temporal distillation (Salimans & Ho, 2022), which reduces inference steps by distilling a multi-step model into a fewer-step one. The key departure is introducing a new distillation axis: rather than distilling across time steps, scale distillation distills across magnification factors, using a teacher trained on a simpler (lower-scale) SR problem to supervise a student trained on a harder (higher-scale) one. This positions the work as orthogonal to existing step-reduction strategies (efficient samplers, higher-order solvers, temporal distillation) and complementary to them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing inference from 200 DDIM steps to 1 step while simultaneously improving quality over the 200-step baseline represents a ~200× speedup that makes diffusion-based SR viable for real-time or resource-constrained applications. The ability to fine-tune the decoder on top of a frozen one-step U-Net is a direct consequence of the speedup and opens a new training pipeline. Open questions include whether scale distillation generalizes to other inverse problems (e.g., inpainting, denoising) where a 'simpler version' of the task can be defined, and whether it composes effectively with temporal distillation for further step reduction.
