## Problem

Existing 3D motion transfer methods deform the entire character—including apparel—via linear blend skinning to body joints, ignoring the local physical dynamics that cause garments to deform independently. This produces unrealistic artifacts such as body-apparel penetration and lacks visual detail. Compounding the problem, publicly available character-motion datasets lack the detailed apparel annotations (rigging, segmentation, physics simulation) needed to learn realistic garment deformation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stylized 3D character mesh (N vertices with known joint positions from a SMPLH-aligned skeleton) and a reference motion sequence of T frames specifying per-joint rotations and translations. It produces T deformed mesh frames with realistic apparel animation, along with a learned binary apparel mask. Notably, no skeleton is assumed for apparel components; the apparel deformation is modeled as a non-linear per-vertex displacement field conditioned on K=3 historic frames, and the effective clip length is limited to T=10 frames due to autoregressive computation.

## Method

The pipeline disentangles body and apparel deformation into separate modules. A PointNet-based binary classifier segments apparel vertices from body vertices. For body, a geodesic attention block converts raw vertex-joint geodesic distances into learnable Softmax attention weights (via an MLP), adaptively aggregating per-joint features to predict skinning weights for LBS deformation—replacing the noisy top-k joint selection in RigNet. For apparel, a GCN with edge-convolution blocks operates on the apparel graph, encoding historic position, velocity, acceleration, and joint motion into per-vertex features, then an MLP decoder predicts a residual non-linear displacement that is accumulated autoregressively. A lightweight joint refinement MLP merges both outputs to enforce continuity at the body-apparel boundary and penalize penetration. The entire system is trained end-to-end on the newly introduced MMDMC dataset (102 characters, 203.4 min of mocap-driven animation with artist-designed apparel rigs and Bullet physics simulation) using vertex L1, edge-length, and smoothness losses.

## Results

On the MMDMC test set, the proposed method achieves a PMD of 1.08 cm, substantially outperforming NBS (4.63 cm) and SPT (2.53 cm). The apparel-specific error (PMDa) shows the largest gap: 1.91 cm for the proposed method versus 8.90 cm (NBS) and 6.62 cm (SPT), reflecting the benefit of explicit non-linear apparel deformation. Body error (PMDb) is also best at 0.75 cm, and the edge length score (ELS) reaches 0.94, indicating the smoothest deformation among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two recent motion transfer methods: NBS (Neural Blend Shapes, TOG 2021), a skeleton-based approach that deforms the entire character including apparel via LBS with body-joint skinning weights, and SPT (Skeleton-free Pose Transfer, ECCV 2022), a skeleton-free method that can infer virtual joints on apparel. The proposed method wins on all four metrics against both baselines. The most striking advantage is on apparel error (PMDa): 1.91 cm versus 8.90 cm for NBS (4.7× reduction) and 6.62 cm for SPT (3.5× reduction), confirming that explicit non-linear apparel displacement modeling is critical. On body error (PMDb), the gap is smaller (0.75 vs. 0.83 for SPT and 1.84 for NBS), attributable to the geodesic attention block. On ELS (smoothness), the proposed method scores 0.94 versus 0.81 (SPT) and 0.73 (NBS), indicating that baselines produce torn or discontinuous apparel surfaces due to inconsistent skinning weights on complex garment geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: skeleton-based 3D motion transfer (NBS, SPT, RigNet) and physics-driven apparel animation (TailorNet, garment generative models). It departs from motion transfer methods by explicitly rejecting the assumption that apparel can be deformed via the same LBS skinning weights as the body, and departs from apparel animation methods by operating on a holistic character mesh without requiring pre-segmented garment templates. The geodesic attention block is a direct modification of RigNet's top-k joint selection, replacing hard selection with a learnable softmax attention over geodesic distances, while the apparel deformation module repurposes edge-convolution (Dynamic Graph CNN) in a temporal, history-conditioned setting that is novel to this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMDMC dataset is the first to provide paired character-motion data with artist-designed apparel rigging, segmentation, and physics simulation, filling a clear gap that prevented prior motion transfer methods from learning realistic garment dynamics. However, the method is constrained to stylized humanoid characters with a unified SMPLH-aligned skeleton, relies on substantial manual annotation effort, and is limited to 10-frame clips due to computational cost, which restricts its applicability to longer sequences and non-humanoid characters. Open questions include whether the learned apparel priors can transfer to real human garments with fine wrinkle dynamics, and whether the explicit segmentation step can be replaced by end-to-end learning to reduce annotation burden.
