## Problem

Multi-label Intention Understanding (MIU) requires recognizing subjective, context-dependent intentions in images, but is hampered by scarce labeled data and a fundamental mismatch with CLIP's sight-centric pre-training, which focuses on observable objects rather than semantic intent. Existing adaptation strategies—prompt learning overemphasizes visual appearance while full fine-tuning risks destroying pre-trained cross-modal alignment—leaving no principled way to balance objective visual knowledge with subjective semantic understanding.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image at 224×224 resolution and outputs a probability score for each of 28 intention categories (multi-label). On the text side, hierarchical intention labels (spanning coarse-to-fine levels) are pre-processed by an LLM into a fixed-length sentence-level descriptor, which is then fed through CLIP's frozen text encoder alongside learnable positive/negative prompt tokens (16 context tokens each) to produce label embeddings in a shared space with image features.

## Method

IntCLIP employs a dual-branch image encoder: a frozen Sight Encoder preserves CLIP's pre-trained visual knowledge, while a Semantic Encoder (with only deeper ResNet layers trainable) adapts to capture intent-relevant semantic cues. Hierarchical Class Integration uses an LLM to consolidate redundant multi-level labels into a coherent sentence-level prompt that aligns with CLIP's sentence-level text encoding. Sight-assisted Aggregation computes a Semantic Query Attention map from the semantic feature map, then uses the sight feature map as values to produce a contextually-weighted fusion that injects object-level visual evidence into semantic representations. Training optimizes learnable prompts and semantic encoder layers via Asymmetric Loss in a shared embedding space, with the text encoder and sight encoder kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the Intentonomy Testing set (2K images), IntCLIP with ResNet-101 achieves Macro F1 of 38.40, Micro F1 of 50.54, Samples F1 of 49.31, and mAP of 42.66, surpassing the previous best method LabCR (34.63 / 48.51 / 48.05 / 37.13) by 3.77, 2.03, 1.26, and 5.53 points respectively. With the ViT-B/32 backbone, IntCLIP further improves to Macro F1 40.15 and Micro F1 53.54. On Image Emotion Recognition, IntCLIP (R101) reaches 71.05% accuracy on EmotionROI_6 and 78.93% on FI_8, outperforming the strongest baseline CGLF-Net (65.01% and 75.61%) by over 6 and 3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntCLIP (R101) outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric in the Intentonomy Testing set. Against the strongest intention-understanding baseline LabCR [32], IntCLIP gains +3.77 Macro F1, +2.03 Micro F1, and +5.53 mAP while using only 15.7M learnable parameters versus LabCR's 56.2M. Among general multi-label methods, DualCoOp [38] (the closest CLIP-based baseline) trails IntCLIP by 7.34 Macro F1 and 11.12 Micro F1, confirming that the dual-branch sight-semantic encoding and hierarchical class integration provide substantial gains over prompt-only adaptation. On the IER task, IntCLIP (R101) beats CGLF-Net [25] by 6.04 points on EmotionROI_6 (71.05 vs. 65.01) and 3.32 points on FI_8 (78.93 vs. 75.61), and surpasses WSEIP [53] by 10.64 and 3.02 points respectively. The ViT-B/32 variant extends the lead further, reaching 53.54 Micro F1 on Intentonomy and 45.32 mAP, demonstrating that the framework scales well with stronger visual backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based prompt learning paradigm (DualCoOp, Sun et al. 2022) to the domain of multi-label intention understanding, where prior MIU methods (HLEG, PIP-Net, CPAD, LabCR) operate without leveraging large-scale vision-language pre-training. The key conceptual departure is the identification of a 'sight-semantic gap': CLIP's pre-trained knowledge is oriented toward observable visual elements, while MIU requires subjective, context-dependent intention reasoning. To bridge this gap, the paper introduces a dual-branch image encoder (frozen Sight Encoder + partially learnable Semantic Encoder) and a Sight-assisted Aggregation module, departing from both full fine-tuning (which destroys CLIP's alignment) and pure prompt learning (which overemphasizes sight). Additionally, Hierarchical Class Integration repurposes LLMs (GPT) to consolidate multi-level label taxonomies into sentence-level descriptors compatible with CLIP's text encoder, a use of LLMs in multi-label classification that prior MIU work (e.g., HLEG's hierarchical transformer) did not explore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IntCLIP offers a practical recipe for adapting CLIP to subjective, data-scarce tasks by preserving pre-trained sight knowledge while selectively adapting semantic layers—a design principle that could generalize beyond MIU to other high-level semantic understanding tasks. The ablation showing catastrophic performance collapse (Micro F1 dropping from ~50% to ~20%) when all layers are trained, even in a dual-branch setup, underscores the fragility of CLIP's cross-modal alignment and provides a cautionary finding for the broader CLIP adaptation community. Limitations include evaluation on a single primary benchmark (Intentonomy, 28 classes, ~13K images), a dependency on LLM-based label preprocessing that complicates reproducibility, and the added inference cost of the dual-branch architecture. Open questions include whether the sight-semantic split generalizes to non-CLIP vision-language models, how the approach scales to hundreds of intention categories, and whether the LLM-generated label descriptors can be replaced by learned alternatives.
