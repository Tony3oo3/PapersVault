## Problem

Open vocabulary image classifiers like CLIP can predict over arbitrary label sets but still underperform on many tasks, and existing continual learning approaches for them require batch training, rigid task boundaries, or storing all training examples. This paper addresses the gap of enabling a system to efficiently incorporate new labeled examples one at a time (anytime) while preserving the ability to predict over any candidate label set at inference, without forgetting previously learned classes or tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240150/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, the system receives triples (x, y, Y) — an RGB image, its ground-truth label, and the candidate label set — one sample at a time. At inference, the user specifies an arbitrary set of candidate text labels Y (which may mix seen and unseen classes), and the system outputs a probability for each candidate plus an implicit 'none of the above' score. The label set is defined entirely at test time with no task identifier, so the system must dynamically decide how much to trust its tuned model versus the frozen open-vocabulary model for each label.

## Method

The method partially fine-tunes only the last transformer block of CLIP's ViT-B/32 encoder while keeping the text label embeddings fixed, so features remain aligned with the text modality and overfitting is limited. Each incoming sample triggers a single gradient step on a class-balanced batch drawn from stored samples, using a modified cross-entropy loss that includes a learnable 'other' bias term to model 'none of the above' predictions. Predictions from the tuned and original models are combined via Online Class-wise Weighting (OCW), which maintains exponential moving average estimates of per-label accuracy for each model and assigns weights proportional to expected correctness. For storage efficiency, intermediate patch and CLS features are compressed via per-image attention-weighted PCA (weighted by CLS-token attention) with 8-bit quantization, achieving 30× reduction with less than 1% accuracy loss.

## Results

On the MTIL task-incremental benchmark (Table 1), AnytimeCL achieves 69.4% Transfer accuracy (zero forgetting relative to CLIP's 69.4%), 77.0% average accuracy across stages, and 85.8% final-task accuracy. The offline variant (AnytimeCL Offline) reaches 86.2% on the Last metric, the best among all methods. Across the broader evaluation in Fig. 3, AnytimeCL consistently outperforms the previous SotA (CLIP+LinProbe with AIM weighting) in every stage of task-incremental, class-incremental, data-incremental, and flexible inference settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnytimeCL achieves zero forgetting (Transfer = 69.4%, identical to CLIP), whereas LwF, iCaRL, and WiSE-FT all suffer 12–19% Transfer drops. Against the closest competitor LinProbe(AIM) [62], AnytimeCL is within 0.1–0.2% on Avg and Last in Table 1, but the paper's Fig. 3 shows consistent and growing advantages across all stages of task-, class-, and data-incremental learning as well as flexible inference, where the online class-wise weighting (OCW) provides the largest gains. Compared to ZSCL [61], AnytimeCL improves Transfer by 1.3 points (69.4 vs. 68.1) and Last by 2.2 points (85.8 vs. 83.6). The offline variant (AnytimeCL Offline) achieves the best Last score (86.2%) among all methods, edging out LinProbe(AIM) by 0.2 points. Overall, the paper's key claim is that AnytimeCL matches or exceeds the previous SotA in every incremental setting while enabling true online, single-sample updates in milliseconds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CLIP's vision-language architecture and the open vocabulary continual learning framework of Zhu et al. [62], which combined CLIP with per-cluster linear probes and AIM-based weighting. It departs from that framework in two key ways: replacing linear probe training with partial fine-tuning of the last transformer block (keeping label embeddings fixed), and replacing AIM's binary seen/unseen weighting with an Online Class-wise Weighting scheme that tracks per-label accuracy via exponential moving averages. The 'anytime' framing—requiring single-example updates and flexible inference at arbitrary times—extends the batch-oriented continual learning setting of prior work toward a truly online, opportunistic learning paradigm, drawing on complementary learning systems theory and the computationally budgeted CL findings of Prabhu et al. [35].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a surprisingly minimal update (one transformer block, one gradient step per example) can sustain continual improvement while preserving open-vocabulary generality, which is practically important for systems that receive data sporadically and must predict over evolving label sets. The attention-weighted PCA compression (30× reduction with <1% accuracy loss) addresses a real deployment bottleneck for storage and privacy in federated or edge settings. Open questions include scalability to millions of examples across dozens of datasets, extension to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the dynamic weighting strategy generalizes beyond the CLIP backbone to other vision-language or self-supervised models at scale.
