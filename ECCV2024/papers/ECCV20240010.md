## Problem

Training 3D object detectors requires expensive 3D bounding box annotations, and existing weakly supervised methods either still depend on partial 3D labels (e.g., WS3D, MTrans) or achieve suboptimal accuracy due to limited supervision signals (e.g., FGR). No prior approach systematically exploits visual guidance from the image domain across multiple levels of the 3D learning pipeline while requiring zero 3D annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240010/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a LiDAR point cloud and its calibrated camera image, with the only supervision being 2D bounding box annotations on the image. A pre-trained CenterNet 2D detector (frozen during 3D training) provides image features, 2D box predictions, and per-object confidence scores that serve as visual guidance; this image branch is entirely discarded at inference. Initial 3D pseudo-labels are generated non-learnedly via FGR's frustum heuristic, and the final output is 3D bounding boxes with detection confidence scores from a PointRCNN backbone.

## Method

VG-W3D introduces three complementary visual guidance constraints that bridge 2D and 3D domains without any 3D labels. At the feature level, point cloud features are projected onto the image plane and an objectness classifier is trained with focal loss against DINO-generated foreground maps, while a KL divergence loss aligns the point-cloud objectness distribution with the image objectness distribution (rather than L2 feature matching, which would discard geometric information). At the output level, predicted 3D boxes are projected to 2D and constrained via a confidence-weighted GIoU loss against ground truth 2D boxes, ensuring proposals fall within the correct frustum region. At the training level, an iterative pseudo-label refinement loop uses Hungarian matching between projected 3D and 2D boxes, retaining only predictions that exceed IoU and dual-modality confidence thresholds (B_overlap) plus high-confidence unmatched detections (B_score), progressively improving label quality from ~47% to ~74% recall at IoU=0.7 over two rounds.

## Results

On the KITTI test set (Car category), VG-W3D achieves AP_3D of 84.09/74.28/67.90 (Easy/Mod/Hard) and AP_BEV of 91.88/85.61/80.67 without using any 3D annotations. On the KITTI validation set, it reaches AP_3D of 91.32/78.89/74.70, surpassing the fully-supervised PointRCNN baseline (88.99/78.71/78.21) on Easy and Mod settings while remaining competitive on Hard. Compared to the same-annotation-cost baseline FGR (no 3D labels), VG-W3D improves AP_3D by 3.83/5.81/6.33 points across the three difficulty levels on the test set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FGR [33], the primary same-cost baseline (2D boxes only, no 3D labels), VG-W3D wins by a large margin: +3.83/+5.81/+6.33 AP_3D on the test set and +4.64/+5.34/+6.79 on the validation set, confirming the benefit of multi-level visual guidance over the non-learning frustum heuristic. Against MTrans [16] with 500-frame 3D annotations, VG-W3D is competitive on the test set (74.28 vs 75.07 AP_3D Mod) and slightly ahead on the validation set (78.89 vs 78.84 AP_3D Mod), while also achieving higher AP_BEV Hard (80.67 vs 78.82 on test). Against MAP-Gen [17] (500 frames), VG-W3D outperforms on all three AP_3D settings on the test set (84.09/74.28/67.90 vs 81.51/74.14/67.55). Against WS3D [21,22] (534 object-level BEV centroids), VG-W3D leads by 3.1–4.3 AP_3D points on the test set. On the validation set, VG-W3D also surpasses VS3D [26] (9.09/5.73/5.03) and WS3DPR [18] (60.01/44.48/36.93) by a very wide margin, though these methods use only class-level labels. The only setting where VG-W3D trails is AP_3D Hard on the validation set vs fully-supervised PointRCNN (74.70 vs 78.21), a gap of 3.5 points that reflects the inherent difficulty of detecting heavily occluded/truncated objects without 3D supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FGR [33], which generates initial 3D boxes from 2D annotations via non-learning frustum geometry, and extends it with three learned visual guidance constraints (feature-level KL divergence, output-level GIoU, and training-level pseudo-label refinement) that prior weakly-supervised methods like WS3D, MTrans, and MAP-Gen do not jointly exploit. It departs from the WS3D/MTrans line of work by eliminating the need for any 3D or BEV centroid annotations entirely, relying solely on 2D bounding boxes. The 3D backbone (PointRCNN) and 2D backbone (CenterNet) are standard off-the-shelf detectors; the contribution lies in the multi-level constraint framework that bridges the two modalities during training, with DINO-based self-supervised segmentation providing free foreground masks for feature-level supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the requirement for any 3D annotations, this work addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying 3D detectors where LiDAR labeling is 3–16× slower than 2D labeling. The modular three-level guidance design is a reusable recipe that could transfer to other weakly-supervised 3D tasks or sensor combinations. However, evaluation is limited to the Car category on KITTI, the 2D detector is frozen and discarded at inference, and generalization to other object classes, datasets, or 3D backbones remains untested. An open question is whether the pseudo-label refinement loop converges reliably in more cluttered or long-tail scenarios beyond the relatively clean KITTI driving setting.
