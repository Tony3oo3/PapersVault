## Problem

Existing scene text detector pre-training methods rely heavily on large-scale labeled synthetic data (LSD), which introduces a significant synth-to-real domain gap when models are fine-tuned on real images. While unlabeled real data (URD) is readily available, its potential for pre-training text detectors has been largely unexplored, and naively combining LSD and URD risks real domain drift that degrades detection performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB scene text images at standard resolutions, combining labeled synthetic images (SynthText, 800K) with unlabeled real images (~460K from LSVT, MLT17, MLT19, ICDAR2013, USTB-SV1K) during pre-training. The detector outputs a pixel-wise binary text region mask of size H×W, from which character-level or word-level bounding boxes are extracted via post-processing. Notably, the pre-training uses character-level bounding box annotations from SynthText rather than word-level ones, enabling language-agnostic feature learning.

## Method

FreeReal operates within a student-teacher framework where the teacher generates pseudo-labels on weakly augmented real images and the student trains on GlyphMix-composed images under mixed supervision. GlyphMix segments character structures (glyphs) from synthetic images via K-means clustering on grayscale values within bounding-box crops, then pastes these glyph regions as graffiti-like units onto real images, achieving 95.7% domain classification accuracy versus 78.9% for prior methods like ClassMix. A text-balanced intra-domain mixing step selects the rectangular region with maximum text content from a second real image to preserve text coverage. A dynamic entropy threshold (γ linearly scheduled from 80 to 20) filters unreliable pseudo-label pixels during training, and the teacher updates via EMA (α=0.996). Character Region Awareness further bridges the language gap by training on character-level annotations, allowing the model to learn language-agnostic character features that adapt to different scripts during fine-tuning.

## Results

FreeReal achieves average F-measure gains of 4.56%, 3.85%, 3.90%, and 1.97% over the respective baselines for DBNet, PANet, PSENet, and FCENet across four benchmarks (Total-Text, CTW 1500, ICDAR2015, MSRA-TD500). The strongest result is DBNet+FreeReal reaching 88.9/87.9/90.0/90.1 F-measure on TT/CTW/IC15/TD, surpassing the previous SOTA (DBNet+VLPT at 86.3 on TT) by 2.6% while using only 10% of the labeled synthetic data. On the unseen-language VinText benchmark, DBNet+FreeReal achieves 90.3 F-measure with a ResNet50 backbone (25M params, 11 FPS), outperforming ESTextSpotter (56M params, 3.4 FPS) at 89.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeReal consistently outperforms all pre-training baselines across every detector architecture and dataset. Against the strongest Paradigm B baseline (DBNet+VLPT, CVPR'22), FreeReal gains +2.6% on TT, +3.5% on IC15, and +1.6% on TD, while using only 80K LSD vs. 800K. Against the most recent Paradigm A baseline (DBNet+TCM, CVPR'23), gains are +3.0% on TT, +2.8% on CTW, +0.6% on IC15, and +1.3% on TD. For PANet, FreeReal surpasses PANet+TCM by +4.1% on CTW and +4.6% on TD; for FCENet, gains over FCENet+TCM are +1.3% on CTW and +2.1% on TD. The only comparison where FreeReal's margin is modest is PSENet+VLPT on TT (+0.7%), though it still leads by +2.8% on CTW and +0.9% on IC15. In the paradigm comparison (Table 2), FreeReal at 10% data (80K LSD + 46K URD) achieves 87.5 F on TT, exceeding DBNet+VLPT (800K LSD, no URD) at 86.3 by 1.2%, and outperforming DBNet+MTM (Paradigm C, 800K LSD + 41K URD) at 85.7 by 1.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeReal departs from three established pre-training paradigms for scene text detectors: (A) direct pre-training on labeled synthetic data (LSD) followed by real-data fine-tuning, (B) LSD pre-training with cross-modal pretext tasks (STKM, VLPT, oCLIP), and (C) URD pre-training via masked modeling followed by LSD supervised learning (MTM). Rather than designing increasingly complex pretext tasks or additional training modules, the paper repurposes the standard student-teacher semi-supervised framework and introduces a text-specific domain-bridging mechanism (GlyphMix) that uses K-means clustering to extract glyph structures from synthetic images and paste them onto unlabeled real images as graffiti-like units. The Character Region Awareness component further extends this by leveraging character-level bounding boxes from SynthText to bridge the English-dominated LSD to multilingual URD, a dimension prior pre-training methods have not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the persistent synth-to-real domain gap in text detection pre-training can be substantially closed (95.7% domain classification accuracy vs. 78.9% for the best prior mixing method) without complex pretext tasks, and that 90% less synthetic data suffices to outperform SOTA pre-training methods. This shifts the field's focus from engineering elaborate cross-modal objectives toward the underexploited potential of unlabeled real data. Open questions include whether the K-means-based glyph extraction generalizes to highly stylized or low-contrast text, whether the student-teacher overhead is justified at very large scale, and whether the character-level bridging strategy extends to scripts with fundamentally different structural units (e.g., cursive Arabic, logographic Chinese).
