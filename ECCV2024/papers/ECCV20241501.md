## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D object detection degrades significantly under adverse weather due to noisy reflections and reduced range, while 4D radar is weather-robust but lacks precise geometry for standalone use. Direct multi-sensor fusion introduces computational overhead, requires both sensors simultaneously, and can degrade LiDAR's localization through joint optimization. The paper addresses how to achieve all-weather robustness at inference using LiDAR alone, by exploiting 4D radar only as auxiliary training information.

## Input / Output

At inference, the student model consumes only a 64-beam LiDAR point cloud (range 0–72 m × ±6.4 m × −2–6 m, voxelized at 0.4 m) and outputs 3D bounding boxes via BEV-space classification and regression maps. During training, the teacher additionally ingests 4D radar points (top 10% by power) processed into voxel features, but this radar branch is entirely discarded at test time. The detection head produces per-voxel classification scores (focal loss) and regression offsets (smooth L1) over a dense BEV grid.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, a 4D radar-based 3D prompt learning module injects radar information into a frozen pre-trained LiDAR encoder: LiDAR voxel features are self-calibrated by a channel-wise reliability score, globally aggregated with radar voxels in 3D space to form a learnable prompt, and then locally aggregated back into LiDAR features via K-nearest-neighbor summation, adding only 1.2 M trainable parameters. Second, four levels of inter-/intra-modal knowledge distillation transfer the teacher's weather-insensitive knowledge to a LiDAR-only student: response-level (L1/smooth-L1 on classification and regression maps), BEV feature-level (L1 on multi-scale dense BEV features), voxel feature-level (cosine similarity on last-layer sparse voxels), and inter-modal structure-level (KL divergence between densified 3D radar-prompt and LiDAR features). The student is trained with the standard detection loss plus all four distillation losses, and at inference only the LiDAR student is deployed, requiring no radar hardware.

## Results

On the K-Radar dataset (IoU=0.3, Sedan class), the LiDAR-only student model (Ours-S) achieves 83.3 AP_BEV and 75.6 AP_3D overall, surpassing the multi-modal teacher (Ours-T: 82.1/73.2) and the strongest fusion baseline BEVFusion* (81.5/69.4) by up to 6.2 AP. Under adverse conditions, Ours-S maintains 84.9 AP_BEV in rain and 85.4 AP_3D in fog, while the LiDAR-only baseline RTNH* drops to 49.1 AP_BEV in sleet. The student adds only 1.2M parameters over the baseline and requires no radar input at inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours-S (LiDAR-only) outperforms all single-modal LiDAR baselines: RTNH* (80.5/70.6), VoxelNext (78.4/67.7), PointPillars (50.6/45.7), and Robo3D (66.2/56.4) in AP_BEV/AP_3D, with the largest gap over PointPillars (+32.7 AP_BEV). Against multi-modal fusion methods, Ours-S beats BEVFusion* (81.5/69.4) by +1.8 AP_BEV and +6.2 AP_3D overall, and InterFusion (71.9/59.2) by +11.4 AP_BEV and +16.4 AP_3D. The only exceptions are sleet and heavy snow, where BEVFusion* retains a small edge (55.3 vs. 46.8 AP_BEV in sleet; 67.8 vs. 57.2 in heavy snow), attributed to scenes with extremely sparse LiDAR data. The teacher Ours-T (82.1/73.2) also surpasses BEVFusion* in AP_3D (+3.8) and InterFusion substantially, validating the prompt-learning fusion strategy. Among KD baselines, Ours-S outperforms UniDistill (67.1/78.6) by +8.5 AP_3D and PointDistiller (71.3/81.0) by +4.3 AP_3D, confirming the benefit of the four-level inter-/intra-modal distillation. Prompt-learning ablations (Table 2) show Ours-T beats concatenation (73.3/81.3), attention (73.2/80.2), and adapted ViPT variants (50.6/64.4, 69.4/81.0) while using only 1.2M additional parameters versus 17.2M for concatenation/attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RTNH voxel-based architecture from K-Radar [25] as its baseline detection backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior LiDAR-radar fusion approaches (InterFusion, BEVFusion) that concatenate or cross-attend features at inference time. Instead, it repurposes the prompt-learning paradigm—previously explored in NLP and 2D vision-language tracking [56]—into the 3D sparse voxel domain, using 4D radar as a training-time auxiliary signal rather than an inference-time input. The knowledge distillation component extends cross-modal KD from the well-studied image-to-LiDAR setting [53] to the previously unexplored 4D-radar-to-LiDAR pair, introducing a four-level hierarchy (response, BEV, voxel, inter-modal structural) that accounts for the misalignment and sparsity of 3D sensor data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that the final deployed model requires only LiDAR hardware, eliminating the need for radar sensors, radar pre-processing pipelines (FFT, CFAR), and dual-sensor synchronization in production systems. However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (K-Radar) and a single object class (Sedan), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other sensor pairs, object categories, or driving scenarios. The modest performance drop under sleet and heavy snow relative to BEVFusion* suggests the LiDAR-only student still struggles when LiDAR returns are extremely sparse, pointing to a fundamental information bottleneck that distillation alone cannot fully overcome.
