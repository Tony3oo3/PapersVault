## Problem

Radar sensors have two orders of magnitude lower angular resolution than cameras, making it difficult to distinguish closely spaced vulnerable road users and classify them by micro-Doppler patterns alone. Existing radar-camera fusion methods rely heavily on camera features for recognition and degrade sharply when cameras fail (low light, adverse weather), while radar-only methods cannot localize azimuth accurately or detect stationary targets. The paper addresses both gaps: improving radar azimuth precision via camera assistance and ensuring graceful degradation when either sensor is unreliable.

## Input / Output

The network consumes N sequential RAD radar tensors (e.g., 5×128×128×64 on CARRADA) and N synchronized RGB camera images (e.g., 5×1232×1028). Camera images are not fed directly into the network; instead, an off-the-shelf YOLOv8 detector produces 2D bounding boxes that are back-projected onto the BEV ground plane as a confidence-score feed-forward array, then pixel-shuffle downsampled to match radar spatial resolution. The output is a BEV map with (3k+2) channels per grid cell: 2 continuous offset values for sub-cell localization relative to predefined anchor points, and 3k binary context labels (k object classes × 3 questions: VRU present, radar-detectable, camera-detectable), from which final detections are extracted via non-maximum suppression.

## Method

CARB-Net is a U-Net-based encoder-decoder that fuses radar micro-Doppler signatures with camera-derived BEV detections at the earliest network stages. The key architectural choice is a Cooperative Vision Feed-Forward: YOLOv8 bounding-box centers are projected to the BEV plane using a statistical depth model (median of zero-height and size-based estimates) and smeared with a Gaussian kernel to absorb projection uncertainty, then pixel-shuffle downsampled by factor D to align with radar resolution without information loss. A second novelty is Context Learning: during training, the network is forced to answer three auxiliary questions per position (is a VRU present, is it radar-detectable, is it camera-detectable), with data augmentation that simulates sensor failure by repeating the latest RAD tensor (eliminating micro-Doppler) or zeroing the camera feed-forward. Pixel-shuffle replaces both max-pooling and deconvolution throughout the U-Net, and an ESPCN-style pixel-shuffle upsampling restores output resolution. Localization uses predefined anchor points on the BEV grid with two learned offset channels, refined by NMS with a 0.8 m radius.

## Results

On the CARRADA dataset, CARB-Net achieves an mAP of 0.73 with a mean distance error of 0.63 m, outperforming the best radar-only method (RAMP-CNN, mAP 0.61) and the best prior radar+camera fusion method (Lim et al., mAP 0.53). On the authors' proposed RadVRU dataset, CARB-Net reaches an mAP of 0.81 with a mean distance error of 0.47 m, surpassing the strongest baseline EchoFusion* (mAP 0.69, 0.54 m error). Per-class AP on CARRADA shows CARB-Net leading in all three categories: Pedestrian 0.82, Cyclist 0.75, and Car 0.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARB-Net outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets. On CARRADA, it beats the strongest radar-only method RAMP-CNN (mAP 0.61) by 12 percentage points and the best prior radar+camera fusion method Lim et al. (mAP 0.53) by 20 percentage points, while also achieving the lowest mean distance error (0.63 m vs. 0.64–0.70 m). On RadVRU, CARB-Net (mAP 0.81) surpasses EchoFusion* (mAP 0.69) by 12 points and Lim et al. (mAP 0.67) by 14 points, with a notably smaller localization error of 0.47 m versus 0.54 m for EchoFusion*. The radar-only CARB-Net-baseline (mAP 0.63 on CARRADA, 0.55 on RadVRU) already exceeds all prior radar-only methods, confirming the value of the micro-Doppler-based U-Net architecture even without camera assistance. The largest absolute gains come from adding the cooperative vision feed-forward and context learning, which together lift mAP from 0.53 (plain U-Net) to 0.73 on CARRADA and from 0.52 (U-Net) to 0.81 on RadVRU.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARB-Net builds on U-Net's multi-resolution encoder-decoder architecture but departs fundamentally from prior radar-camera fusion methods (CRAFT, CRN, Lim et al.) that rely on large pretrained image networks (LSS, BEVFormer, BEVDepth) to project 2D camera features into BEV. Instead of fusing full camera feature maps, it uses a lightweight off-the-shelf detector (YOLOv8) to produce a summarized feed-forward array, and uses pixel-shuffle to align resolution without information loss. The context learning strategy also departs from the common sensor-dropout approach by encoding fusion context as explicit auxiliary classification targets rather than simply removing modalities during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The context learning mechanism addresses a critical practical gap: most fusion methods degrade catastrophically when one sensor fails, yet real-world deployment demands graceful degradation. The RadVRU dataset (78k frames with synchronized RAD tensors, camera, and LiDAR) fills a notable gap in publicly available radar-camera data with micro-Doppler information. Open questions include whether the approximate radar simulation of stationary targets (repeating the latest RAD cube) generalizes to more complex clutter scenarios, whether the reliance on an off-the-shelf camera detector limits end-to-end optimization, and whether the approach scales to additional sensor modalities (thermal, acoustic) as the authors themselves note.
