## Problem

Structure-based jailbreak attacks embed harmful semantic content (e.g., malicious text rendered as images) into the visual modality to bypass MLLM safety alignment, a threat that conventional adversarial defenses like purifiers and adversarial training cannot address because the embedded content carries meaningful semantics rather than minor perturbations. Effective defense against this attack class remained largely unexplored, and existing solutions either require expensive fine-tuning, additional trained modules, or incur significant inference-time overhead.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard image-text pair (an RGB image that may contain typographic or text-to-image rendered harmful content, plus a natural-language instruction) and produces either a safe, task-appropriate response or a refusal message (e.g., 'I am sorry'). At inference, CLIP text and image encoders produce embeddings for the input query, which are concatenated and compared via cosine similarity against a stored pool of malicious-query embeddings; if the maximum similarity falls below a threshold β, the query is classified as benign and no defense prompt is prepended, preventing over-defense on normal inputs.

## Method

AdaShield operates in two tiers. AdaShield-S prepends a manually crafted static defense prompt P_s that instructs the MLLM to (1) thoroughly examine image content for harmful text or items, (2) apply chain-of-thought reasoning to assess the combined image-instruction query, (3) explicitly refuse with a fixed phrase if malicious content is detected, and (4) safely execute benign instructions to avoid over-defense. AdaShield-A extends this with an iterative auto-refinement loop: a defender LLM (D) generates candidate defense prompts, the target MLLM (M) responds to the malicious query, and if the response is still a jailbreak (detected via keyword matching), D receives the failed prompt and jailbreak response as feedback and refines the prompt in a conversational format. This process yields a diverse pool of scenario-specific defense prompts stored as a query-to-prompt dictionary. At inference, the most suitable prompt is retrieved via CLIP embedding similarity, and the prompt is prepended to the input before the MLLM generates its response—requiring no fine-tuning of the target model or training of any auxiliary detector.

## Results

On the QR attack, AdaShield-A reduces the attack success rate (ASR) from 75.75% (Vanilla) to 15.22% on LLaVA-13B, from 83.62% to 1.37% on CogVLM-chat-v1.1, and from 65.75% to 0.00% on MiniGPT-v2-13B. On FigStep, AdaShield-A achieves ASR of 10.47%, 0.00%, and 0.00% respectively, compared to 70.47%, 85.19%, and 95.71% for Vanilla. On the benign MM-Vet benchmark, AdaShield-A preserves general capabilities (e.g., 36.3 total score on LLaVA vs. 36.8 Vanilla), while FSD degrades performance substantially (33.1 on LLaVA, 27.4 on CogVLM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaShield-A decisively outperforms both baselines on all three target MLLMs. Against FSD [18], AdaShield-A reduces QR ASR by 54.28 points on LLaVA (15.22 vs. 69.50) and 36.68 points on CogVLM (1.37 vs. 38.05), while also preserving benign-task performance (MM-Vet Total 36.3 vs. 33.1 on LLaVA; 51.0 vs. 27.4 on CogVLM). Against MLLP [45], AdaShield-A achieves substantially lower ASR (e.g., 15.22 vs. 77.96 on QR/LLaVA; 1.37 vs. 79.97 on QR/CogVLM), indicating that the post-hoc harmful-detector + detoxifier pipeline of MLLP is largely ineffective against structure-based attacks. On MiniGPT-v2-13B, AdaShield-A matches FSD's near-zero ASR on FigStep but recovers benign performance (13.9 vs. 0.9 MM-Vet Total), whereas FSD catastrophically over-defends. The GPT-recheck metric confirms the same ordering, with AdaShield-A consistently achieving the lowest Recheck scores across all models and attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific niche within MLLM safety: defending against structure-based jailbreaks (e.g., FigStep, QR) where harmful content is embedded in images via typography or text-to-image generation, as opposed to perturbation-based adversarial attacks. It departs from training-time defenses like MLLMP (which requires a separate harm detector and detoxifier) and from the static defense prompt proposed in FigStep (FSD), instead introducing an adaptive auto-refinement loop where a defender LLM iteratively optimizes prompts against a target MLLM's jailbreak responses. The retrieval mechanism using CLIP embeddings to select scenario-appropriate prompts from a learned pool draws on retrieval-augmented generation ideas, but applied here to safety prompting rather than knowledge grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, fine-tuning-free nature of AdaShield makes it directly applicable to black-box MLLM-as-a-Service deployments where parameter access is unavailable, addressing a practical gap that training-time defenses cannot fill. However, the method is explicitly scoped to structure-based attacks and the authors acknowledge that a universal defense covering both structure-based and perturbation-based threats remains open. The over-defense problem is partially addressed (AdaShield-A filters benign queries via similarity threshold), but AdaShield-S still degrades benign performance notably on MiniGPT-v2, suggesting the static prompt approach is fragile. A key open question is whether the adaptive refinement framework generalizes to novel attack scenarios not represented in the training pool.
