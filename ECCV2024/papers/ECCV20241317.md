## Problem

Existing unsupervised semantic segmentation methods that use patch-wise contrastive learning rely on per-patch similarity in an imperfect image-level pretrained embedding to determine positive and negative sets. This leads to unreliable supervision: anchors in data-sparse regions or near semantic boundaries gather false positives, while false positives in the negative set cause unwanted repulsion during training. The paper addresses how to identify trustworthy positive and negative relationships by accounting for the local data distribution around each anchor rather than using a fixed k-th nearest neighbor boundary.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and extracts H×W patch features from a frozen DINO-pretrained ViT backbone (ViT-S/8, ViT-S/16, or ViT-B/8). A second, finetuned ViT stream produces projected patch features z that are trained with the contrastive objective. The final output is a pixel-level segmentation mask, refined with a CRF post-processing step, evaluated via Hungarian matching (unsupervised) or a linear probe (supervised) against ground-truth labels.

## Method

PPAP iteratively relocates a proxy anchor for each patch toward the centroid of its gathered positives, starting from a tight initial boundary that ensures the first positive set is highly reliable. At each of T propagation steps, the proxy anchor moves to the mean of the current positive set, and the positiveness threshold is loosened proportionally to the propagation reliability (measured by cosine similarity between consecutive proxy positions), allowing the boundary to expand only when the anchor has converged into a dense semantically-similar region. In parallel, an ambiguity criterion is progressively tightened from a loose initial value, defining an ambiguous zone between the positive and negative boundaries where samples are excluded from both sets to prevent false-positive repulsion. The finetuned stream is trained with a standard contrastive loss over the final trustworthy positive set and the ambiguity-excluded negative set, while the guidance stream remains frozen throughout training.

## Results

On COCO-stuff (27 classes), PPAP with ViT-S/16 achieves 62.9% unsupervised accuracy and 26.5 mIoU, surpassing HP (54.5% / 24.3) by 8.4% in accuracy and 2.2 in mIoU. On Cityscapes with ViT-B/8, PPAP reaches 83.3% unsupervised accuracy and 21.2 mIoU, outperforming HP (79.5% / 18.4). On the large-scale ImageNet-S benchmark, PPAP achieves 25.7 mIoU, a 3× improvement over HP (8.6) and 1.6× over PASS (15.6). Linear probe results also improve consistently, e.g., 46.3 mIoU on COCO-stuff (ViT-S/8) vs. 42.7 for HP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPAP outperforms its primary baseline HP [46] on every dataset and backbone tested: on COCO-stuff with ViT-S/16, PPAP gains +8.4% unsupervised accuracy (62.9 vs. 54.5) and +2.2 mIoU; on Cityscapes with ViT-B/8, it gains +3.8% accuracy and +2.8 mIoU; and on ImageNet-S it achieves 25.7 mIoU versus HP's 8.6 (a 3× margin). Against STEGO [22], PPAP leads by 10.7% accuracy and 3.0 mIoU on COCO-stuff (ViT-S/8) and by 10.1% accuracy and 0.2 mIoU on Cityscapes (ViT-B/8). TransFGU [54] is surpassed on COCO-stuff (59.0 vs. 52.7 Acc) and Cityscapes (82.0 vs. 77.9 Acc) but is not evaluated on Potsdam-3 or ImageNet-S. Older CNN-based methods (DC, MDC, IIC, PiCIE) and classical baselines (K-Means, SIFT, CP, CC) are all substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 20% accuracy on COCO-stuff. The only case where PPAP does not lead is Cityscapes linear-probe accuracy with ViT-S/8 (90.8 vs. HP's 91.2), a 0.4% margin, while still winning on mIoU (31.5 vs. 30.6). Notably, PPAP's advantage is largest with the ViT-S/16 backbone (larger patches), where it gains up to 15.4% accuracy over HP, suggesting the proxy anchor propagation strategy is particularly effective at compensating for coarser patch-level semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends HP (Seong et al., CVPR 2024), which established the use of k-th nearest neighbor boundaries for positive/negative set construction in patch-level contrastive learning for USS. The broader lineage traces through STEGO, TransFGU, and PiCIE, all of which leverage DINO-pretrained ViT features to transfer image-level semantics to pixel-level segmentation. The key departure is replacing HP's fixed per-patch k-NN criterion with a progressive proxy anchor propagation strategy that iteratively relocates the anchor toward dense regions of semantically similar samples, and introducing an instance-wise ambiguous zone that excludes borderline samples from the negative set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in USS contrastive learning: the low precision of positive sets and high false-negative rate in negative sets when relying on per-patch similarity in imperfect image-level embeddings. The ambiguity-excluded negative set is a practical, generalizable principle that could inform other contrastive learning settings where boundary samples are unreliable. Open questions include whether the propagation strategy and its hyperparameters (σ_pos, σ_amb, Φ⁰, Ψ⁰) transfer cleanly to non-ViT backbones or different self-supervised pretraining objectives, and whether the approach scales to even larger class counts beyond ImageNet-S's 919 classes.
