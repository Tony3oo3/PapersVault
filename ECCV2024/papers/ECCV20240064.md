## Problem

Camera-based HD map construction methods lack explicit depth information, forcing reliance on large backbones to achieve competitive accuracy, which undermines the cost advantage of camera-only deployment. Existing BEV-based knowledge distillation methods designed for 3D object detection fail to transfer effectively to HD map construction due to fundamental task differences (vectorized point regression vs. bounding-box localization), leaving KD-based efficient HD map construction largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, only the lightweight student model processes 6 surround-view camera images (360° horizontal FOV) to produce vectorized HD map elements—pedestrian crossings, lane dividers, and road boundaries—each represented as a category label plus a sequence of 2D point positions within a [-15m, 15m] × [-30m, 30m] perception range. The teacher model additionally consumes LiDAR point clouds during training but is discarded at deployment, so the student's ResNet18 backbone and dual BEV transform module constitute the entire inference pipeline.

## Method

MapDistill pairs a camera-LiDAR fusion teacher (ResNet50 + SECOND + GKT, based on MapTR) with a lightweight camera-only student (ResNet18) that employs a Dual BEV Transform module—two parallel 2D-to-BEV branches using different methods (GKT and LSS)—to structurally mimic the teacher's cross-modal feature interaction. Three complementary distillation losses guide training: (1) cross-modal relation distillation aligns the student's inter-subspace attention matrices with the teacher's camera-to-LiDAR and LiDAR-to-camera attention via KL divergence; (2) dual-level feature distillation applies MSE between low-level fused BEV features and high-level Map Encoder outputs; and (3) map head distillation uses Focal loss on category predictions and Manhattan distance on point positions to match the teacher's final outputs. The total objective combines the standard map loss with weighted distillation terms, and only the student model is retained for inference, yielding a 4.5× speedup over the teacher while surpassing prior camera-only methods by over 7.7 mAP on nuScenes.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, MapDistill with a camera-LiDAR fusion teacher (R50+SECOND) and a lightweight camera-only student (R18) achieves 53.6 mAP, surpassing the directly-trained R18 baseline (45.9 mAP) by 7.7 mAP. This outperforms the best adapted 3D-detection KD method (UniDistill, 48.2 mAP) by 5.4 mAP and the LiDAR-teacher variant (BEVDistill, 47.1 mAP) by 6.5 mAP. The student model also achieves a 4.5× speedup over the fusion teacher while retaining most of its accuracy (53.6 vs. 62.5 mAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MapDistill (53.6 mAP) decisively outperforms all three KD methods originally designed for BEV 3D object detection and adapted to HD map construction: BEV-LGKD (46.5), BEVDistill (47.1), and UniDistill (48.2), with margins of 7.1, 6.5, and 5.4 mAP respectively. Against camera-only baselines using the same R18 backbone, MapDistill improves over the directly-trained MapTR baseline (45.9) by 7.7 mAP. Compared to stronger camera-only methods using R50 backbones (PivotNet 57.4, BeMapNet 59.8), MapDistill with R18 is slightly lower in absolute mAP but achieves a 4.5× inference speedup, making it the best speed-accuracy trade-off. The paper also shows that intra-modal distillation (camera teacher → camera student) yields only +2.1 mAP, while cross-modal distillation (LiDAR teacher → camera student) gives +4.2 mAP, and the full fusion teacher (C&L) delivers the largest gain of +7.7 mAP, confirming that cross-modal interaction is the key knowledge being transferred.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm established for BEV-based 3D object detection (BEVDistill, UniDistill, BEV-LGKD) to the distinct task of vectorized HD map construction, where the output is point regression rather than bounding-box localization. It builds directly on MapTR as the architectural backbone for both teacher and student, and introduces a Dual BEV Transform module that uses two heterogeneous 2D-to-BEV methods (GKT and LSS) to create a pseudo-cross-modal structure within a camera-only student, mimicking the teacher's camera-LiDAR fusion. The key conceptual departure from prior BEV-KD work is the task-specific map head distillation loss (focal classification + point-to-point Manhattan regression) and the cross-modal attention relation loss, both of which address the fact that HD map construction requires aligning dense geometric structure rather than sparse foreground objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MapDistill provides the first systematic KD framework for HD map construction, demonstrating that a lightweight ResNet-18 camera-only student can recover ~46% of the performance gap to a ResNet-50+LiDAR fusion teacher while enabling 4.5× faster inference — a practically significant trade-off for cost-sensitive autonomous driving deployment. The paper acknowledges that the student may inherit the teacher's biases and robustness limitations (e.g., adverse weather, long-tail scenarios), which remains an open question for real-world generalization. Future work could explore whether the dual-BEV-transform and cross-modal-relation-distillation ideas transfer to other vectorized prediction tasks or to multi-sensor settings beyond camera-LiDAR.
