## Problem

Diffusion-based video editing models incur heavy memory and computational costs from diffusion inversion and cross-frame attention needed for temporal coherence, yet existing efficiency techniques (e.g., Token Merging) degrade generation quality when naively applied to video editing. Moreover, no prior method exploits the observation that users are far more sensitive to artifacts in edited foreground objects than in background regions, leaving a clear opportunity to redistribute computation toward salient areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241513/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an 8-frame video clip, a text edit prompt, and a binary foreground mask (obtained from DAVIS annotations, Grounded-SAM segmentation, or in-model cross-attention maps) and produces temporally coherent edited video frames. For the ControlNet-based pipeline, per-frame depth maps extracted via MiDaS serve as an additional structural control signal. The foreground mask is padded to a rectangular crop around the object, enabling standard dense convolutions within the UNet rather than sparse operations.

## Method

Object-Centric Diffusion (OCD) combines two training-free techniques applied on top of existing video editing models (FateZero, ControlVideo). Object-Centric Sampling splits the latent into foreground and background regions via a gather operation, runs the foreground through the full diffusion schedule while accelerating the background by a factor φ (fewer denoising steps), then merges them at a blending timestep T_b = γT with an affine normalization to prevent color-shift and boundary artifacts. Object-Centric Token Merging modifies ToMe by multiplying the cosine similarity of foreground source tokens by a down-weighting factor η ∈ [0,1], biasing merges toward background regions, and extends merging to spatiotemporal volumes to exploit temporal redundancy. Both techniques are paired with off-the-shelf accelerations (DPM++ scheduler reducing steps from 50 to 20, and ToMe with paired inversion-generation token indices and per-frame destination resampling) to achieve up to 10× latency reduction with comparable fidelity.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark for inversion-based video editing, OCD achieves a total latency of 17.51 s (8.22 s inversion + 9.29 s generation) with Tem-con 0.967 and CLIP-score 0.331, representing a 10× speedup over the FateZero baseline (177.14 s total) while maintaining comparable fidelity. For ControlNet-based editing, OCD achieves a 6× speedup over ControlVideo with comparable generation quality. Memory consumption is reduced by up to 17× relative to the unoptimized baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCD achieves a 10× total-latency reduction over FateZero (177.14 s → 17.51 s) while matching or slightly exceeding its temporal consistency (Tem-con 0.967 vs 0.961), though CLIP-score drops marginally (0.331 vs 0.344). Against Tune-A-Video+DDIM and TokenFlow+PnP, OCD is roughly 2–3× faster in total latency (17.51 s vs 49.59 s and 39.10 s respectively) with comparable Tem-con and a slightly lower CLIP-score. Compared to VidToMe, OCD is faster (17.51 s vs 49.11 s) and achieves higher scores on both fidelity metrics (Tem-con 0.967 vs 0.961; CLIP-score 0.331 vs 0.326). The framewise Null+p2p baseline is over 76× slower (1340.84 s) and also scores lower on both metrics. In the ControlNet-based setting, OCD delivers a 6× speedup over ControlVideo with comparable quality, while Text2Video-Zero is slightly faster but underperforms in temporal consistency due to its sparse cross-frame attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as an efficiency layer atop existing zero-shot video editing frameworks, specifically FateZero (inversion-based) and ControlVideo (ControlNet-based), rather than proposing a new editing architecture. It builds directly on Token Merging (ToMe) for attention cost reduction, but departs from VidToMe's use of ToMe for temporal consistency by repurposing it for latency reduction and introducing an object-centric re-weighting of merge similarities. The off-the-shelf components (DPM++ sampler, ToMe) are adapted with two non-trivial modifications—pairing ToMe indices between inversion and generation, and per-frame destination resampling—that the authors show are necessary to avoid catastrophic quality collapse in the video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× and 6× latency reductions with training-free, plug-and-play applicability make diffusion-based video editing substantially more practical for interactive or resource-constrained settings, and the object-centric framing (spend more compute where the user looks) is a broadly applicable principle for any spatially-localized generative task. However, the per-sequence hyperparameter search (γ, η) limits true zero-shot deployment, and the methods are explicitly less effective for global style edits, leaving open whether the object-centric decomposition can be generalized to non-local transformations. A key open question is whether the ToMe pairing and resampling tricks generalize beyond the specific UNet-based pipelines tested here to transformer-only video models.
