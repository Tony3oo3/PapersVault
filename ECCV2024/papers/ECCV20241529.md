## Problem

Safety-driven unlearned diffusion models (DMs) are designed to erase harmful concepts, styles, or objects from image generation, yet no systematic benchmark exists to verify whether these unlearning mechanisms are truly robust. The paper asks: can subtle adversarial perturbations to text prompts bypass the unlearning safeguards and compel unlearned DMs to regenerate the supposedly erased content?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241529/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image x_tgt (sourced offline from the internet or a vanilla DM) that exemplifies the erased concept, the original text prompt, and white-box access to the unlearned DM's parameters. The target image is encoded into latent space and noise is added at sampled diffusion time steps. The output is a short adversarial prompt of 3–5 discrete tokens (5 for concept unlearning, 3 for style/object unlearning) prepended to the original prompt, which is then fed to the unlearned DM to generate an image.

## Method

UnlearnDiffAtk exploits the 'diffusion classifier' — the observation that a well-trained DM inherently functions as a classifier via Bayes' rule over generation likelihoods — eliminating the need for an auxiliary DM or external image classifier. The attack objective is to maximize the diffusion classifier's probability of predicting the adversarial prompt c' given the target image x_tgt, which through Jensen's inequality on the exponential of relative denoising errors simplifies to minimizing the expected denoising error ‖ε − ε_θ*(x_tgt,t | c')‖². This is optimized via projected gradient descent in discrete token space (AdamW, 40 iterations, 50 sampled time steps, lr=0.01), where the projection maps continuous token embeddings back to discrete text. The key advantage over concurrent P4D is that no second diffusion process is required: the target image supplies semantic guidance offline, reducing per-attack computation by ~23.5% while matching or exceeding P4D's attack success rate across concept, style, and object unlearning tasks.

## Results

Across concept unlearning (1,625 prompts from I2P), UnlearnDiffAtk achieves 76.05% ASR against ESD on nudity (vs. 20.42% without attack) and 85.01% on illegal activity, consistently outperforming P4D (69.71% and 85.83% respectively) while reducing per-prompt attack time from 34.70 to 26.29 minutes (~23.5% faster). For style unlearning (Van Gogh, 50 prompts), UnlearnDiffAtk reaches 77% Top-1 ASR against ESD (vs. 68% for P4D) and 94% Top-1 against UCE. For object unlearning (200 prompts across 4 classes), UnlearnDiffAtk achieves 60% ASR against ESD on 'Church' (vs. 56% for P4D) and 100% against FMN on 'Parachute' and 'Tench'. ESD is the most robust unlearned DM across all three tasks, yet remains vulnerable to even 3–5 token perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole optimization-based baseline is P4D (Prompting4Debugging, [31]), a concurrent work that requires an auxiliary vanilla diffusion model to compute the attack objective. UnlearnDiffAtk outperforms P4D on ASR in most settings: +6.34 pp on ESD nudity (76.05% vs. 69.71%), +4.93 pp on SLD nudity (82.39% vs. 77.46%), +9.00 pp on ESD Van Gogh Top-1 style (77% vs. 68%), and +4 pp on ESD Church object (60% vs. 56%). The exceptions are minor: P4D edges ahead by 0.82 pp on ESD illegal activity (85.83% vs. 85.01%) and by 2 pp on ESD Van Gogh Top-3 (78% vs. 76%). Critically, UnlearnDiffAtk achieves these gains while reducing per-prompt attack time by 23.5% (26.29 vs. 34.70 min) for concept unlearning, 23.5% (38.87 vs. 50.79 min) for style, and 28.3% (31.32 vs. 43.65 min) for object unlearning, because it eliminates the need for a second diffusion forward pass. Against the 'No Attack' baseline (original prompts), both optimization methods yield dramatic ASR increases (e.g., 20.42% → 76.05% for ESD nudity), confirming that current unlearned DMs are far from robust.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion classifier concept (Li et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2023), which shows that a trained DM's denoising error implicitly encodes class-discriminative information, and repurposes it as a free optimization signal for adversarial prompt generation. It departs from prior adversarial prompting methods for DMs (Maus et al. 2023, Zhuang et al. 2023, P4D/Chin et al. 2023) that require either an auxiliary vanilla DM or an external image classifier to guide attack optimization, eliminating both dependencies. In the unlearning-for-DMs lineage (ESD, FMN, AC, UCE, SLD), the paper positions itself as the first systematic adversarial robustness benchmark, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify: no prior work had evaluated whether unlearned DMs survive subtle prompt perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic benchmark reveals that even the strongest unlearned DM (ESD) achieves only 20–31% pre-ASR on inappropriate prompts and drops to 76–86% post-ASR under UnlearnDiffAtk, indicating that current safety-driven unlearning is far from robust. The diffusion-classifier trick is practically useful because it removes the need to maintain a separate vanilla DM or trained classifier during red-teaming, cutting per-attack compute by ~23.5% versus P4D. Open questions include whether the white-box, 3–5-token setting generalizes to black-box or longer-perturbation regimes, whether the findings transfer to newer SD versions (v2.1, SDXL), and whether any unlearning procedure can be made genuinely adversarially robust rather than merely heuristic.
