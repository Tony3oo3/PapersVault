## Problem

While DNN image classifiers are susceptible to many diversified universal adversarial perturbations (UAPs), each attacking a substantially different set of images, existing single-UAP methods train only one perturbation to attack all targets, leaving the complementary coverage of multiple UAPs unexploited. This paper asks whether properly combining K diversified UAPs with a learned selection mechanism can significantly boost attack effectiveness while preserving real-time inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241746/cropped_page12_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB target image and produces a perturbed image (target + selected UAP, pixel values clipped to [0,255]) along with the index of the chosen perturbation. The selection is made by a lightweight SqueezeNet that maps the target image to a K-dimensional probability vector; the argmax index determines which of the K pre-trained UAPs (each bounded by ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ξ) is added. The entire inference pipeline requires only one forward pass through the selection network (~2.4 ms on an RTX 3060), with no per-image optimization.

## Method

The paper formulates Finding Diversified UAPs (FDUAP) as an NP-hard mixed-integer programming problem that jointly optimizes K perturbations and a binary cluster-assignment matrix to maximize the number of successfully attacked images per cluster. To make the problem tractable, the discrete assignment matrix is relaxed by a selection neural network g_θ (SqueezeNet) that predicts soft cluster-membership probabilities, converting the MIP into a continuous optimization problem. The K UAPs and g_θ are trained end-to-end via alternating updates: PGD with projection handles the ℓ∞ constraint on each UAP, while ADAM updates the selection network parameters. At attack time, a new target image is fed through g_θ, the UAP with the highest predicted cluster probability is selected, and added to the image. Two variants are studied: CK1 (pre-trained UAPs held fixed, only g_θ trained) and CK2 (joint training of UAPs and g_θ), with CK2 consistently outperforming CK1 due to higher UAP diversity.

## Results

On ImageNet (10K train / 50K test), the cocktail attack framework (CK2) consistently achieves the highest fooling ratio (FR) across all six victim models and all perturbation magnitudes. At ξ=6, DF-CK2 reaches 94.5% FR on VGG-16 and 87.0% on ResNet-50, surpassing the best generator-based baselines (GAP: 89.1%/84.2%, TDA: 87.5%/84.4%). At ξ=10, DF-CK2 achieves 99.4% FR on VGG-16 and 99.0% on ResNet-50. The attack speed of CK2 is ~2.4 ms per image, roughly 4× faster than GAP/TDA (~10.1 ms) while delivering higher FR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four single-UAP methods (UAT, DF-UAP, Cosine-UAP, NAG) and two generator-based methods (GAP, TDA). CK2 outperforms all single-UAP baselines by a wide margin: e.g., on ResNet-50 at ξ=6, DF-CK2 (87.0%) beats DF single-UAP (72.7%) by 14.3 points, and UAT-CK2 (87.6%) beats UAT single-UAP (73.6%) by 14.0 points. Against the stronger generator-based baselines, CK2 also wins: DF-CK2 (94.5%) surpasses GAP (89.1%) and TDA (87.5%) on VGG-16 at ξ=6, and UAT-CK2 (88.9%) beats both GAP (83.7%) and TDA (84.4%) on ViT. The only setting where a generator-based method edges out CK2 is ResNet-50 at ξ=6, where TDA (84.4%) slightly exceeds NAG-CK2 (79.2%), though UAT-CK2 (87.6%) still leads. In transfer attacks (Table 3), CK2 again achieves the highest average transfer FR across all source-to-target model pairs, confirming that the diversity of the K UAPs generalizes well across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the universal adversarial perturbation (UAP) literature, which has focused on training a single perturbation to attack as many images as possible (Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., UAT, DF-UAP, Cosine-UAP, NAG). The key departure is formulating the problem as finding K diversified UAPs that attack complementary image subsets, rather than one UAP for all images, and introducing a lightweight selection network to route each target image to its most effective perturbation. The framework is deliberately agnostic to the underlying single-UAP method and loss function, positioning itself as a general wrapper rather than a new perturbation optimization technique. It also contrasts with generator-based image-dependent attacks (GAP, TDA) that produce a unique perturbation per image, showing that a discrete selection from a small diverse set can match or exceed their effectiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cocktail framework offers a practical, drop-in upgrade for any existing single-UAP method with only ~2.4 ms of additional inference cost, making it immediately deployable in real-time attack scenarios. The consistent outperformance of CK2 over both single-UAP baselines and generator-based methods across six architectures and three perturbation magnitudes suggests the diversity-selection principle is broadly applicable. Open questions include how the framework scales with larger K, whether it extends to other modalities (video, audio, text), and how it interacts with adaptive defenses that may be specifically designed to counter multi-perturbation attacks. The paper does not explore robustness against defenses that detect or neutralize the selection network itself.
