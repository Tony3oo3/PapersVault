## Problem

Vision Transformers compute self-attention over all image tokens with quadratic complexity, yet visual information is inherently sparse and redundant. Existing compression approaches (token pruning, pooling, TokenLearner, Perceiver) rely on heuristics, act as post-processing steps, or fail to reduce computation across all network layers. The paper addresses the need for a general-purpose ViT block that intrinsically compresses visual information into a fixed small token set while maintaining performance across diverse tasks without task-specific adjustments.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (224×224 or 384×384) or video clips (224×224×32 frames) and splits them into non-overlapping patches, producing two parallel token streams: full-resolution lookup tokens (e.g., 14×14 = 196) and a fixed set of compressed tokens obtained by bilinear downsampling (e.g., 5×5 = 25). For classification, both token sets feed separate classifiers whose outputs are averaged; for captioning with a frozen encoder, only the compressed tokens are passed to a text decoder. The compression ratio C = N/M is a free parameter, and the same trained model can be run at multiple C values at inference time.

## Method

LookupViT replaces each vanilla ViT block with a dual-stream block featuring Multi-Head Bidirectional Cross-Attention (MHBC). In each block, (1) compressed tokens attend to lookup tokens via cross-attention (Q from compressed, K/V from lookup), (2) compressed tokens undergo a standard ViT sub-block (self-attention + MLP with 4D hidden dim), and (3) lookup tokens update themselves using the transposed attention matrix from step 1 applied to the refined compressed tokens, followed by a low-dimensional MLP (D/2 hidden dim). Crucially, lookup tokens never attend to each other directly—they communicate only through the compressed tokens—eliminating the N² term. The attention matrix is computed once and reused in both directions, and the compressed tokens are constructed by parameter-free bilinear resize of lookup tokens, enabling multi-resolution training where the compressed token size is randomly sampled per batch, yielding a single model that supports multiple compute-performance operating points at inference.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (B/16, 224×224), LookupViT 10×10 achieves 80.2% top-1 accuracy with 33.6 GFLOPs, outperforming ViT (78.6%, 35.1 GFLOPs) while using fewer FLOPs; the 5×5 variant reaches 79.1% at just 16.5 GFLOPs (2.12× reduction). For the L/16 model, LookupViT 10×10 achieves 79.2% (vs. ViT's 75.7%) at 118.5 GFLOPs, and even the 3×3 variant (77.2%, 46.2 GFLOPs) surpasses ViT with 2.67× fewer FLOPs. On ImageNet-21K pre-training followed by 1K fine-tuning, LookupViT 10×10 matches or exceeds ViT (83.9% vs. 83.7% for B/16; 84.1% vs. 84.0% for L/16). On video classification (SSv2, 12 views), LookupViViT 9×9×16 achieves 59.6% vs. ViViT's 52.3% at roughly half the FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ViT, LookupViT wins on both accuracy and efficiency: B/16 10×10 improves ImageNet-1K top-1 by 1.6% (80.2% vs. 78.6%) at lower FLOPs (33.6 vs. 35.1), and L/16 10×10 gains 3.5% (79.2% vs. 75.7%) at 118.5 vs. 123.5 GFLOPs. Against TokenLearner, LookupViT is consistently superior—B/16 5×5 achieves 79.1% at 16.5 GFLOPs versus TokenLearner 1/2's 75.7% at 19.1 GFLOPs, a 3.4% accuracy gain with fewer FLOPs. Against Perceiver, LookupViT matches or exceeds accuracy (79.1–80.2% vs. 78.0%) while reducing FLOPs by over 20× (16.5–33.6 vs. 707 GFLOPs). On video classification (SSv2), LookupViViT outperforms ViViT by 5–6% at roughly half the FLOPs (e.g., 59.6% vs. 52.3% at 12 views, 375.8 vs. 455.1 GFLOPs/view). On robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-C, -A, -R, -O), LookupViT 10×10 improves over ViT by up to 4% (e.g., ImageNet-C: 59.9% vs. 56.4% for B/16; ImageNet-A: 8.1% vs. 6.7%), with no additional robustness training.

## Relation to Prior Work

LookupViT positions itself as a drop-in replacement for the vanilla ViT block, departing from token pruning/merging methods (Token Merging, adaptive sampling) that act as post-processing heuristics, and from TokenLearner which requires 50–75% of the network to be standard ViT before compression kicks in. Its bidirectional cross-attention mechanism distinguishes it from Perceiver's unidirectional pixel-to-latent attention, and its token-level (rather than pixel-level) operation with a parameter-free resize for compressed tokens draws conceptual parallels to FlexiViT's multi-resolution training. The architecture also echoes OctConv's low/high-frequency split but retains Transformer scalability by restricting heavy computation to the compressed stream.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a single trained model can be extracted at multiple compute budgets via the multi-resolution compressed-token knob, and the 2× FLOPs reduction with maintained or improved accuracy makes ViTs more deployable in resource-constrained settings. The out-of-the-box robustness gains (up to 4% on ImageNet-C/R/A/O) without any explicit robustness training suggest that information bottlenecks may be a useful inductive bias for generalization, a finding worth further investigation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dense prediction (detection, segmentation), whether the robustness benefit persists at larger scales, and whether the bidirectional attention can be further simplified without losing the lookup-token update signal.
