## Problem

Existing tri-plane-based head avatar methods render at low resolution (64×64 or 128×128) and rely on a super-resolution module to upscale by 4× or more, which irreversibly discards high-frequency facial details. This deficiency is further compounded by the variability of camera poses and dynamic movements in in-the-wild monocular videos, causing the model to lose fine-grained local textures across different orientations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240996/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short monocular RGB video (~2 min, 7200 frames at 60 fps, originally 2560×1600, cropped and scaled to 736×736) and produces photo-realistic novel-view head avatars at up to 1024×1024 with controllable facial expressions, as well as canonical (zero-pose) appearance renderings. Per-frame conditioning is derived from 3DMM tracking (expression coefficients β_t, transformation matrix P_t) plus a learnable per-frame latent code γ_t ∈ ℝ³² to correct 3DMM misalignments. The intermediate volume-rendered feature maps are 32-channel at 128×128 per scale, concatenated 16-fold into a 32×512×512 tensor before the SR module.

## Method

Tri²-plane replaces the single tri-plane with three cascaded tri-planes (Φ₁₂₈, Φ₂₅₆, Φ₅₁₂) arranged as an inverted feature pyramid: the shallowest plane samples the full 512×512 face downsampled to 128×128 for global structure, the middle plane focuses on a 256×256 quarter-face region, and the deepest plane renders a 128×128 patch (1/16 of the face) at native resolution for high-frequency detail. A StyleGAN-based generator with modulated 2D convolutions and four MLP layers produces the multi-scale tri-plane features from the encoded expression map I_t concatenated with (β_t, γ_t), while a shared MLP across all three scales predicts density and color for volume rendering. A geometry-aware sliding window augmentation shifts the camera sampling window in world space (equivalent to adjusting 3DMM translation coefficients via Eq. 9) to extend the training distribution beyond the canonical pose, improving cross-identity and out-of-frame robustness. The final 16-patch composite is refined by a U-Net super-resolution module, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end for 800K iterations with L1 RGB loss across scales, LPIPS perceptual loss on the full 512×512 image, and a masked l₂ loss.

## Results

On NeRSemble, Tri²-plane achieves the best scores across all quantitative metrics: F-LMD 1.59, PSNR 30.04 dB, LPIPS 0.0482, and SD 0.260, outperforming HAvatar (F-LMD 2.30, PSNR 27.32), PointAvatar (F-LMD 1.95, PSNR 24.19), and GaussianHead (F-LMD 2.17, PSNR 29.12). On the combined self-recorded dataset (Dataset A+B), Tri²-plane similarly leads with F-LMD 2.48, PSNR 27.75 dB, LPIPS 0.0581, and SD 0.350. In the user study, Tri²-plane attains the highest MOS for video realness (4.26), motion synchronization (4.52), and overall quality (4.19) in self-reenactment, and leads all four MOS categories in cross-reenactment (4.01, 3.75, 4.22, 3.94).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tri²-plane outperforms all three baselines on every quantitative metric across both evaluation datasets. Against HAvatar, the largest gap appears in LPIPS (5.81 vs. 15.6 on Dataset A+B; 4.82 vs. 5.45 on NeRSemble) and SD (3.50 vs. 7.01; 2.60 vs. 3.51), indicating substantially sharper and more perceptually faithful reconstructions; HAvatar's only edge is a marginally higher MOS₁ for image quality in self-reenactment (4.49 vs. 4.43). Against PointAvatar, Tri²-plane improves PSNR by 1.4 dB (27.75 vs. 26.35) on Dataset A+B and by 5.85 dB (30.04 vs. 24.19) on NeRSemble, while reducing LPIPS from 8.76 to 5.81 and SD from 3.64 to 3.50 (A+B) and from 16.4 to 4.82 (NeRSemble), reflecting PointAvatar's well-known empty-hole artifacts in dense point clouds. Against GaussianHead, Tri²-plane achieves lower F-LMD (2.48 vs. 2.57; 1.59 vs. 2.17), lower LPIPS (5.81 vs. 8.76; 4.82 vs. 5.36), and lower SD (3.50 vs. 3.64; 2.60 vs. 4.10), while also surpassing it in PSNR (27.75 vs. 26.35; 30.04 vs. 29.12), confirming that 3D Gaussian splatting still struggles with high-frequency torso and neck textures. In the cross-reenactment user study, Tri²-plane leads all four MOS categories, with the largest advantage over HAvatar in motion synchronization (4.22 vs. 4.13) and overall quality (3.94 vs. 3.45), validating the geometry-aware sliding window's contribution to cross-identity robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tri-plane representation (Chan et al., CVPR 2022) from a single-scale deformation field to a cascaded multi-scale pyramid, drawing directly on the top-down lateral connection structure of Feature Pyramid Networks (Lin et al., CVPR 2017) but inverting the typical hierarchy: shallower layers capture global structure while deeper layers focus on local high-frequency patches. It departs from prior monocular avatar methods (HAvatar, PointAvatar, GaussianHead) that rely on a single low-resolution NeRF rendering upscaled by a super-resolution module, arguing that this pipeline inherently loses fine-grained texture. The geometry-aware sliding window augmentation is positioned as a general technique pluggable into any NeRF-based avatar method, addressing out-of-distribution head positions without requiring additional data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in NeRF-based avatar reconstruction—high-frequency detail loss during low-to-high resolution upsampling—offering a principled multi-scale solution that improves perceptual quality (LPIPS, sharpness) even at the cost of slightly lower PSNR. The geometry-aware sliding window is a lightweight, general augmentation that could benefit other conditional-NeRF avatar systems. However, the method still requires ~2-minute monocular videos (7200 frames) for adequate training, limiting practical deployment in the wild, and the person-specific nature of the reconstruction means it does not address cross-identity generalization beyond the tested cross-reenactment setting.
