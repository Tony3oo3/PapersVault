## Problem

In knowledge distillation, the teacher's role is to provide the student with an estimate of the true Bayes conditional probability density (BCPD), and the student's error rate is upper-bounded by the MSE between the teacher's output and the BCPD. However, conventional teachers are trained with cross-entropy (CE) loss, which minimizes CE distance to the BCPD rather than MSE distance. The paper addresses the gap of how the teacher should actually be trained to minimize the MSE-relevant quantity that governs student performance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces class labels. The key I/O distinction is that the teacher, trained with MSE loss, outputs a soft probability vector over C classes that serves as the supervisory signal for the student, replacing the one-hot label used in standard training. The teacher's output is a C-dimensional probability simplex vector (the BCPD estimate), and the student is trained to match this distribution via the chosen KD loss.

## Method

The paper proves a theorem establishing that training a DNN to minimize MSE (resp. CE) loss is equivalent to minimizing the expected MSE (resp. CE) between its output and the true BCPD, showing that CE-trained and MSE-trained teachers produce fundamentally different BCPD estimates. Since prior work (Dao et al., 2020; Menon et al., 2021) shows the student's accuracy is inversely proportional to the MSE between teacher output and BCPD but not to the CE distance, the teacher should be trained with MSE loss. The approach is entirely plug-and-play: one simply replaces the CE-trained teacher with an MSE-trained teacher in any existing KD method (AT, PKT, SP, CC, RKD, VID, CRD, DKD, REVIEW, HSAKD) without modifying the distillation loss, architecture, or hyperparameters. Empirical validation on a synthetic Gaussian dataset confirms that MSE proximity to BCPD correlates with student accuracy while CE proximity does not, and experiments on CIFAR-100, ImageNet, semi-supervised, and binary classification settings show consistent student accuracy gains of up to 2.67%.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, replacing the CE-trained teacher with an MSE-trained teacher consistently improves student accuracy across all 10 KD methods and 6 teacher-student pairs, with gains ranging from +0.06% to +2.67%. The largest improvements occur with cross-architecture pairs (e.g., AT with VGG-13→MobileNetV2 gains +2.67%, PKT with ResNet-32×4→ShuffleNetV2 gains +1.65%). On ImageNet, the MSE teacher yields +0.42% to +0.55% Top-1 accuracy gains over the CE teacher across KD, DKD, REVIEW+KD, and CRD+KD. Notably, the teacher's own accuracy drops slightly (e.g., ResNet-56: 72.34%→72.14%), confirming that teacher self-performance is not the goal of KD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MSE-trained teachers against CE-trained teachers across 11 KD methods (KD, AT, PKT, SP, CC, RKD, VID, CRD, REVIEW, DKD, HSAKD) on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. The MSE teacher wins in every single comparison — there is not one case where the CE teacher matches or exceeds the MSE teacher. Gains are largest for cross-architecture pairs: AT with VGG-13→MobileNetV2 improves by +2.67%, PKT with ResNet-32×4→ShuffleNetV2 by +1.65%, and CC with ResNet-32×4→ShuffleNetV2 by +1.73%. For same-architecture pairs, improvements are more modest (+0.16% to +1.19%), with SP showing the largest same-arch gain (+1.10% for ResNet-56→ResNet-20). On ImageNet, all four tested methods (KD, DKD, REVIEW+KD, CRD+KD) show consistent Top-1 gains of 0.42–0.55%. The teacher's own accuracy decreases slightly under MSE training (e.g., ResNet-50: 79.34%→74.54% for the MSE variant in Table 2), yet the student still benefits, confirming that teacher self-performance is orthogonal to distillation effectiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two threads in the KD literature: (1) the theoretical line showing that the teacher's role is to approximate the Bayes conditional probability density (BCPD) and that student error is bounded by the MSE between teacher output and BCPD (Dao et al. 2020; Menon et al. 2021; Ren et al. 2021), and (2) the practical line of work on training customized teachers for KD (Cho & Hariharan 2019; Wang et al. 2022; Ye et al. 2024). The key departure is a simple but previously unexploited observation: since the student's error is bounded by MSE to BCPD, the teacher should be trained with MSE loss rather than the conventional CE loss, which minimizes CE to BCPD instead. Theorem 1 formalizes this equivalence, and the plug-and-play experimental design (swapping only the teacher's training loss across ten state-of-the-art KD methods) distinguishes this work from prior customized-teacher approaches that modify the distillation loss or add regularization terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is immediate and low-cost: a single change to the teacher's training loss yields consistent student accuracy gains (up to 2.67%) across CIFAR-100, ImageNet, semi-supervised, and binary classification settings without touching any KD hyperparameters. The slight decrease in teacher accuracy when using MSE loss reinforces an important conceptual point—that optimizing the teacher for its own classification accuracy is a different objective than optimizing it as a BCPD estimator for the student. Open questions include whether further tuning of MSE-teacher hyperparameters (learning rate schedules, regularization) could amplify the gains, whether the result extends to non-CNN architectures (transformers, diffusion models), and whether analogous loss-matching principles apply to other teacher-student paradigms such as self-distillation or multi-teacher settings.
