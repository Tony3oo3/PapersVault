## Problem

Self-supervised pre-training for LiDAR point clouds has largely ignored the temporal dimension, operating on single frames and discarding the rich semantic information in adjacent scans. Existing temporal SSL methods rely on complex preprocessing (e.g., HDBSCAN segmentation) and treat multi-frame inputs as augmented views rather than modeling temporal correspondence, while transformer-based backbones remain heavily dependent on annotated data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240280/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two temporally adjacent LiDAR sweeps (constrained to ~0.3 s apart via temporal batch sampling), aligned to a common ego-vehicle coordinate frame and voxelized into infinite-height pillars. During pre-training, the current frame's occupied pillars are masked at 75% while the previous frame is fed in full; the network outputs reconstructed 3D point coordinates per masked pillar (optimized with Chamfer distance). During fine-tuning, the same SiamWCA backbone feeds a CenterPoint-style head that predicts 3D bounding boxes with heading.

## Method

T-MAE pre-trains a SiamWCA backbone by reconstructing a heavily masked current frame using a fully observed previous frame as reference. A weight-shared Siamese encoder maps both frames' pillar features into sparse tokens in a common latent space. The Windowed Cross-Attention (WCA) module then partitions the 3D space into non-overlapping windows and performs sparse regional cross-attention within each window, where queries are the current frame's visible tokens and key-values are the previous frame's tokens; the window is shifted by half its size and cross-attention is repeated to expand each token's receptive field. This windowed design keeps computation tractable for the high-resolution pillar grid (e.g., 468×468) that would be prohibitive for global attention. The temporal modeling simultaneously acts as a natural data augmentation, since ego-vehicle motion changes the viewing angle of the same objects across frames, improving robustness without explicit augmentation.

## Results

On the Waymo validation set, T-MAE with adjacent frames achieves the best overall mAP and mAPH across all data budgets (5%–100%), reaching 51.47/49.46 at 5% and 72.30/70.52 at 100% labeled data. The advantage is most pronounced for pedestrian detection: at 5% data, T-MAE attains 55.28 pedestrian mAPH, surpassing MV-JAR's 47.69 (which uses the same 5% split) and even exceeding MV-JAR at 10% data (54.66). On the ONCE dataset, T-MAE achieves 67.00 mAP, outperforming the best prior SSL method (GD-MAE at 64.92) by 2.08 points and the strongest supervised baseline (SPT at 62.62) by 4.38 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T-MAE consistently outperforms the prior SOTA SSL method MV-JAR at every data budget on Waymo: at 5% data it gains +0.95 mAP and +2.78 mAPH overall, and +7.59 mAPH for pedestrians; at 100% data the gap widens to +3.14 mAP and +4.32 mAPH. Against GD-MAE (the strongest single-frame MAE baseline), T-MAE leads by +3.24 mAP and +4.90 mAPH at 5% data, with the pedestrian mAPH advantage reaching +9.81 points. Contrastive methods (PointContrast, ProposalContrast) are substantially weaker, trailing T-MAE by 5–6 mAP at 5% data. At 100% data, T-MAE also surpasses GCC-3D (+7.01 mAP) and BEV-MAE (+5.38 mAP), confirming that temporal reconstruction pre-training generalizes beyond the low-data regime. On the ONCE dataset, T-MAE (67.00 mAP) beats GD-MAE (64.92) by 2.08 points and the best supervised baseline SPT (62.62) by 4.38 points, with a particularly large pedestrian mAP gain of +1.56 over GD-MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-MAE extends the single-frame masked autoencoder paradigm (GD-MAE, MV-JAR) to a temporal two-frame setting, replacing the contrastive-learning-based temporal SSL approaches (STSSL, TARL) with a reconstruction objective. The architectural backbone builds on SST's sparse regional self-attention for efficient ViT on sparse LiDAR pillars, and the temporal batch sampling strategy is borrowed from TARL. The key departure is the SiamWCA module: rather than concatenating frames (which introduces spurious points for moving objects) or performing contrastive alignment via HDBSCAN segmentation, the paper learns temporal correspondence through windowed cross-attention in latent space, drawing conceptual inspiration from SiameseMAE in video understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that temporal information in LiDAR sequences is a natural and effective data augmentation (ego-vehicle motion changes the viewing angle of the same object), and that exploiting it via reconstruction rather than contrastive learning avoids the need for elaborate preprocessing like HDBSCAN segmentation. The data-efficiency story is practically important: T-MAE with 5% labeled data outperforms MV-JAR with 10%, which matters for expensive LiDAR annotation pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two frames, whether the windowed cross-attention can be made more efficient at higher resolutions, and whether the temporal modeling benefit persists in more dynamic scenes (e.g., urban intersections with heavy cross-traffic).
