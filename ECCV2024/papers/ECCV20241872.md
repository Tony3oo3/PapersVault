## Problem

SDF-based neural surface reconstruction from sparse views of complex indoor scenes suffers from ambiguity due to limited observations, and existing methods that incorporate monocular geometric priors (normals, depth) as first-order supervision are highly sensitive to the noise and errors inherent in those pre-trained estimators. The paper identifies that the second-order statistical correlations among surface normals of spatially connected scene regions remain informative even when individual normal estimates are noisy, and that exploiting this correlation structure can regularize geometry learning without depending on absolute prior accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 50–85 posed RGB images per scene, from which it extracts per-pixel DINO semantic features and monocular normal estimates (via a spatial-rectifier-based estimator) to identify correlated pixel pairs across neighboring views. It outputs a trained SDF-based implicit surface (NeRF backbone + SDF head + color head) extractable as a mesh via marching cubes. The mutual information shaping is a training-time plugin: it adds no architectural changes, no extra inference cost, and no additional storage beyond the standard NeRF+SDF parameters.

## Method

The core mechanism computes surface normals N(x;θ)=∂f/∂x from the SDF, then takes their gradients with respect to a subset of network parameters θ^D and aggregates them along viewing rays using volume-rendering weights. Mutual information between normals of two pixels is approximated by the absolute cosine similarity of these aggregated gradient vectors (Eq. 10), so maximizing MI corresponds to aligning (or anti-aligning) the gradient directions. Correlated pixel pairs are selected by intersecting top-k neighbors under DINO semantic similarity (threshold β_S) and monocular normal similarity (threshold β_G), ensuring only spatially connected regions with consistent normal directions are paired. An InfoNCE contrastive loss (L_M) is then applied over these positive/negative pairs, and the total training objective is L = L_C + λ_E·L_E + λ_M·L_M, where L_C is photometric and L_E is the eikonal constraint. The key design choice of shaping normals rather than raw SDF values provides a more global regularity (normals depend on all parameters) while leaving SDF values free to fit the scene, and the second-order MI formulation is inherently robust to additive noise in the geometric priors.

## Results

On ScanNet++ (8 scenes, 50–85 views), InfoNorm improves the mean Chamfer distance of all seven SDF-based baselines, with the largest gains on VolSDF (0.24→0.05 m, −79%), GeoNeuS (0.24→0.01 m, −96%), and Neuralangelo (0.85→0.37 m, −56%). Mean F-score gains are equally pronounced: GeoNeuS rises from 0.63 to 0.87, I²-SDF from 0.51 to 0.71, and VolSDF from 0.57 to 0.73. On Replica (4 scenes), the same trend holds, with NeuRIS showing a dramatic Chamfer reduction from 1.007 to 0.011 m and Neuralangelo's F-score jumping from 0.69 to 0.88. The already-strong MonoSDF baseline sees only marginal gains (Chamfer 0.02→0.02 m, F-score 0.87→0.88), confirming that the plugin is most beneficial when the underlying geometry prior is noisier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InfoNorm is a drop-in plugin, so every comparison is 'baseline vs. baseline+InfoNorm.' The largest relative improvements appear on methods that rely heavily on noisy monocular priors or lack strong geometric regularization: VolSDF's Chamfer drops by 79% and F-score rises by 0.16; GeoNeuS's Chamfer drops by 96% and F-score rises by 0.24; I²-SDF's Chamfer halves and F-score gains 0.20. NeuRIS, which already uses monocular normal supervision, still benefits (Chamfer 0.08→0.05, F-score 0.65→0.71) because InfoNorm operates on second-order correlations rather than absolute normal values, making it robust to the noise that causes NeuRIS to discard low-confidence regions. MonoSDF, the strongest baseline (using both depth and normal cues), shows only marginal gains (F-score +0.01), suggesting diminishing returns when first-order supervision is already high quality. Neuralangelo, which uses hash-grid encoding and numerical gradients, sees a 56% Chamfer reduction, demonstrating architectural versatility. A small number of individual scenes (e.g., VolSDF on 6ee2, Neuralangelo on a08d) show slight regressions, but the mean across all scenes is consistently positive for every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the mutual-information-under-perturbation framework from JacobiNeRF [34], which shaped color gradients for semantic label propagation, to the geometric branch of SDF-based NeRFs by operating on surface normals instead of pixel values. It departs from the dominant paradigm of using monocular geometric priors (depth, normals) as direct first-order supervision (as in NeuRIS, MonoSDF) by instead exploiting second-order correlations among geometric entities, making the regularization more robust to noisy prior estimates. The method is positioned as a drop-in plugin for any SDF-based neural surface representation, validated across seven distinct architectures from NeuS to Neuralangelo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in providing a simple, architecture-agnostic regularization that consistently improves surface quality across a wide range of SOTA baselines without modifying model structure, which is attractive for practitioners who want to boost reconstruction quality with minimal engineering effort. However, the training-time and memory overhead varies substantially across architectures (e.g., +1.96 h on VolSDF vs. +0.20 h on NeuRIS), and the method is confined to SDF-based representations. Open questions include whether the mutual information shaping can be extended to non-SDF implicit representations, whether the off-the-shelf DINO + monocular-normal correlation identification generalizes to outdoor or highly textured scenes, and whether the computational cost can be reduced via coarse-to-fine strategies as the authors suggest.
