## Problem

Mainstream attention-based MIL methods for whole slide image classification model inter-instance correlation but overlook the inherent phenotypic, spatial, and patient-level diversity among instances, leading to spurious correlations that mislead aggregation. Existing diversity-modeling approaches (clustering/prototype-based MIL) suffer from a chicken-and-egg dependency on pseudo-labels, multi-stage optimization that degrades learning, and a limited number of prototypes constrained by computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted patch feature vectors (1024-d from ResNet-50, 512-d from ResNet-18, or 768-d from ViT) organized as a bag of ~10,000 instances per WSI, with no pixel-level or patch-level annotations required. It outputs a binary bag-level prediction (tumor present/absent) and per-instance importance scores derived from the softmax attention between the tokenized global vector and each instance embedding, enabling tumor localization on the WSI.

## Method

DGR-MIL replaces standard self-attention pooling with a cross-attention mechanism where K learnable global vectors serve as queries and the bag of instance embeddings serve as key-value pairs, reducing complexity from O(n²) to O(Kn) while implicitly modeling instance diversity through similarity to diverse global summaries. A tokenized global vector g_token is appended to the K global vectors to capture the most discriminative aggregate representation for classification. Two learning strategies shape the global vectors: (i) a positive instance alignment module using a triplet loss with momentum-updated positive/negative bag centers pushes global vectors toward tumor-relevant regions, and (ii) a DPP-derived diversity loss L_div = −log det(GGᵀ + εI) with unit-norm constraint enforces orthogonality among global vectors, with Theorem 1 proving that maximizing the DPP determinant under fixed norm yields exactly orthogonal vectors. Nystrom-Attention is applied to both instance vectors (to filter background) and global vectors (to increase their mutual discrepancy) before the cross-attention step.

## Results

On CAMELYON16 with ResNet-50 features, DGR-MIL achieves 91.7% accuracy, 91.3% F1, and 95.7% AUC, outperforming the second-best method DTFD-MIL(AFS) by 0.9%, 3.1%, and 1.1% respectively. On TCGA-NSCLC with the same features, it reaches 90.8% accuracy, 91.1% F1, and 96.3% AUC, surpassing the runner-up ILRA-MIL by 1.3%, 1.5%, and 1.7%. The method consistently leads across all three feature extractors (ResNet-50, ResNet-18, Vision Transformer) on both datasets, with the largest margins observed on CAMELYON16 where instance diversity is higher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGR-MIL outperforms all seven baseline families on both datasets across all three feature extractors. The closest competitor is DTFD-MIL(AFS), which achieves 90.8% accuracy and 94.6% AUC on CAMELYON16; DGR-MIL improves accuracy by 0.9% and AUC by 1.1%, with a larger 3.1% F1 gain. On TCGA-NSCLC, ILRA-MIL is the strongest baseline (89.5% accuracy, 94.6% AUC), and DGR-MIL leads by 1.3% accuracy and 1.7% AUC. The classic AB-MIL and CLAM variants trail by 7–9% in accuracy on CAMELYON16, confirming that modeling inter-instance diversity yields substantial gains over independent-instance attention. Trans-MIL and DS-MIL, which model correlation via self-attention or dual-stream contrastive learning, are outperformed by 5–6% in AUC on CAMELYON16. PMIL, the prototype-based method, underperforms DGR-MIL by 8.6% in AUC on CAMELYON16, supporting the paper's argument that multi-stage prototype selection is suboptimal compared to end-to-end diverse global vectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-based MIL (AB-MIL) framework by replacing the standard self-attention mechanism (which models inter-instance correlation at O(n²) cost) with a cross-attention between instances and a small set of learnable global vectors, reducing complexity to O(Kn). It departs from prior correlation-focused methods (Trans-MIL, ILRA-MIL, DTFD-MIL) by explicitly modeling instance diversity rather than similarity, and from prototype-based approaches (PMIL) by avoiding multi-stage clustering and pseudo-labeling. The diversity enforcement mechanism repurposes the determinantal point process (DPP) from its traditional role in data selection into a theoretically grounded diversity loss, a conceptual shift from prior DPP applications in machine learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap in WSI-MIL: existing methods model correlation but ignore the well-documented phenotypic and spatial diversity of tumor instances, which can cause spurious aggregation. The DPP-based diversity loss with a provable orthogonality guarantee (Theorem 1) offers a principled, low-complexity alternative to heuristic diversity penalties, and the consistent gains across three feature extractors and two datasets suggest robustness. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class or multi-task WSI settings, how the number of global vectors K should be chosen in a principled (rather than grid-search) manner, and whether the unsupervised learning of global vectors can be further constrained to reduce residual negative-instance contamination observed near tumor boundaries.
