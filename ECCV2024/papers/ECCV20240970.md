## Problem

Synchronization of projective transformations on a graph has been solved for 3×3 homographies by normalizing to unit determinant in SL(3), but this trick fails for 4×4 matrices because the fourth root of the determinant is generally complex, making the SL(4,ℝ) normalization infeasible. The 4×4 projective synchronization problem had therefore remained unexplored, leaving no principled way to merge independently reconstructed projective SfM partitions into a globally consistent reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240970/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a connected graph whose edges carry known 4×4 projective transformations defined only up to an unknown scale factor (Z_ij ≃ X_i X_j^{-1}), and recovers the unknown 4×4 projectivities at each node. In the SfM application, the graph is a triplet graph derived from a viewing graph of fundamental matrices: each node is a triplet of cameras and each edge encodes the relative projectivity between two triplets sharing a camera pair. The output is a set of node projectivities (or equivalently, camera matrices) all expressed in a single common projective frame, defined up to one global 4×4 transformation.

## Method

The core contribution is an iterative Gauss-Seidel-style scheme where each node is updated in turn as a scale-aware average of its neighbors' propagated estimates (X_{i|j} = Z_{ij} X_j). The key technical challenge is defining a proper centroid for projectivities that are equivalent only up to scale; the paper proposes three averaging strategies: (1) Direction-based, which stacks orthogonal-complement projectors and solves a least-squares eigenvector problem; (2) Spherical, which normalizes vectorized projectivities to the unit sphere and computes the L1 geodesic mean via a fixed-point iteration derived from Lagrange multipliers; and (3) Euclidean, which applies the Weiszfeld algorithm to the normalized vectors and re-normalizes the result. An IRLS wrapper with Cauchy loss provides outlier robustness by reweighting edges based on residuals after each full sweep. The SPHERE+IRLS variant is selected as the best-performing configuration for real-data experiments.

## Results

On 25 real SfM datasets (Oxford VGG and Olsson-Enqvist collections), the proposed SPHERE+IRLS method reduces mean reprojection error (before bundle adjustment) on 15 out of 25 sequences compared to GPSFM's spanning-tree approach, with the largest gains on Alcatraz Courtyard (14.96 vs 36.73 px) and Park Gate (13.70 vs 18.45 px). In synthetic experiments, SPHERE and EUCLIDEAN variants consistently outperform both the spanning-tree (TREE) and the extended spectral (SPECTRAL) baselines across varying noise, holes density, and outlier levels. The proposed iterative method runs in ~10–13 s per dataset, roughly 10–100× slower than GPSFM's tree concatenation (0.03–0.94 s), but provides better initial estimates for subsequent bundle adjustment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPSFM's spanning-tree concatenation (TREE, [33]), the proposed SPHERE+IRLS method wins on 15 of 25 real datasets, with the most dramatic improvement on Alcatraz Courtyard (14.96 vs 36.73 px, a 59% reduction). The 10 losses are concentrated in very dense triplet graphs (holes density 0.97–1.0) where redundancy is limited and outliers are harder to suppress; the authors attribute these to insufficient redundancy rather than a fundamental flaw. In synthetic experiments, the proposed method (all three variants) consistently outperforms TREE, which degrades with increasing graph size due to error accumulation along the tree path. Against the extended SPECTRAL baseline ([55], originally for 3×3 SL(3) homographies, adapted to 4×4), the proposed method is significantly better across all synthetic settings (noise, holes density, outliers), confirming that the SL(4,ℝ) normalization trick does not generalize and a dedicated 4×4 projectivity synchronization approach is necessary. SPECTRAL is not included in the real-data comparison due to its consistently inferior synthetic performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synchronization framework—well established for rotations, rigid transformations, and permutations—to the case of 4×4 projective transformations, building directly on the iterative Gauss-Seidel update scheme of Hartley et al. [28] for L1 rotation averaging. The 3×3 planar homography case was previously handled by Schroeder et al. [55] via normalization into SL(3), but the authors show this trick fails for 4×4 matrices because the fourth root of the determinant is generically complex. In the SfM context, the paper departs from GPSFM [33], which concatenates relative projectivities along a spanning tree of the triplet graph, by instead exploiting all available redundancies through an iterative averaging procedure with IRLS-based outlier handling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a genuine gap: 4×4 projective synchronization had no principled solution, and the natural extension of the 3×3 SL(3) approach is provably inapplicable. The practical impact is demonstrated through improved camera recovery in partitioned projective SfM (GPSFM pipeline), where exploiting graph redundancies reduces reprojection error on 15 of 25 real datasets. Key limitations include the requirement for sufficient graph redundancy (performance degrades sharply with high holes density), the absence of convergence guarantees for the iterative scheme, and mixed results on high-noise/high-outlier sequences where the method can underperform the simpler spanning tree. Open questions include whether convergence can be formally established, whether the approach scales to very large graphs, and whether the framework generalizes to other projective dimensions or non-invertible transformations.
