## Problem

Applying large-scale pretrained models (LPMs) like CLIP, DINO, and DINOv2 to cross-domain few-shot classification (CDFSC) faces two critical challenges: traditional fine-tuning causes severe overfitting and prior-knowledge damage under low-data conditions, and extreme domain shift renders the visual encoder's features non-robust and poorly discriminative. Existing CDFSC methods rely on lightweight backbones with cumbersome CDFSC-specific pre-training, making them impractical for real-world deployment where LPMs' rich prior knowledge could be leveraged more economically.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 224×224 RGB support images (augmented into weak-strong pairs), textual class labels formatted as 'a photo of {class name}' processed by a fixed CLIP text encoder, and unlabeled query images at test time. The ViT backbone is split into fixed early blocks and trainable late blocks (1–3 blocks for 5-way tasks, 7 blocks for 10-way tasks), with MMIT-Mixup applied at the cls-token position before each trainable block. Classification is performed either by cosine similarity to domain-invariant image prototypes (cls-I) or directly to textual features (cls-T), yielding a single class label per query.

## Method

SRT introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, MMIT-Mixup injects domain-invariant textual features into mid-level image features at the cls-token by performing a modulus-matched linear interpolation: the text feature is scaled to match the image feature's modulus (ratio p ∈ (30,80)), then the mixed vector is rescaled by r = √(1−2λ(1−λ)(1−sim)) to preserve the original modulus, avoiding the distribution damage that naive cross-modal mixing causes. This is applied layer-by-layer before each trainable block, allowing domain-invariant knowledge to propagate through the computation chain without introducing extra parameters. Second, RI-FT is a metric-based fine-tuning loss that computes a softmax distribution over domain-invariant prototypes (built from weakly-augmented, mixup-injected features) for each strongly-augmented, non-mixed feature, then minimizes cross-entropy to pull same-class features toward their prototype while pushing inter-class distances apart. The total loss combines RI-FT with a standard CLIP-style image-text alignment loss (weighted by α=0.2), jointly optimizing robustness and domain-invariance of the visual encoder.

## Results

On the BSCD-FSL benchmark (5-way k-shot, cls-I), CLIP + SRT (ViT-B) achieves 54.91% average accuracy in 1-shot and 67.28% in 5-shot, outperforming the previous SOTA CDFSC method StyleAdv-FT (ResNet-10) by 3.13% in 1-shot. DINO + SRT (ViT-S) reaches 55.33% (1-shot) and 68.01% (5-shot), while DINOv2 + SRT (ViT-B) achieves 55.20% (1-shot) and 67.17% (5-shot). On the EuroSAT 10-way-k-shot CLIP benchmark, SRT outperforms all adapter and prompt-tuning baselines across all shot settings (1, 2, 4, 8, 16-shot), with the largest margins in the most data-scarce 1- and 2-shot regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRT outperforms the strongest CDFSC-specific method StyleAdv-FT (ResNet-10, 53.00% 1-shot) by 3.13–4.33% across LPM backbones, while eliminating the need for complex adversarial pre-training. Against CLIP fine-tuning baselines, SRT beats CoOp (53.99%), Tip-Adapter (48.53%), and FD-Align (49.92%) by 1–11% in 1-shot, with the largest gap over Tip-Adapter and FD-Align which suffer severe overfitting on ViT-B. On the EuroSAT 10-way-k-shot CLIP benchmark, SRT surpasses all adapter methods (CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter) and prompt-tuning methods (CoOp, MAPLE, SRP) at every shot level, with the advantage most pronounced at 1- and 2-shot where overfitting is most acute. Notably, SRT provides consistent gains across three distinct LPM families (CLIP, DINO, DINOv2) using identical hyperparameters, whereas Trad-FT yields negative gains for larger backbones (e.g., DINOv2-FT-last1 drops 14.07% in 1-shot). The method also outperforms lightweight-backbone SOTAs (StyleAdv ViT-S: 52.34% → 53.99% after FT) when applied to DINO ViT-S (51.98% → 55.33%), demonstrating that SRT's robustness tuning is more effective than CDFSC-specific pre-training even at the same model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of cross-domain few-shot classification (CDFSC) and large-scale pretrained model (LPM) adaptation. Prior CDFSC work (StyleAdv, ATA, AFA, NASE) relies on lightweight ResNet-10 backbones with elaborate CDFSC-specific pre-training, while LPM fine-tuning methods (CoOp, Tip-Adapter, FD-Align) address low-data adaptation but ignore extreme domain shift and robustness. SRT departs from both lines by repurposing the mixup mechanism (Zhang et al., 2017) for cross-modal feature injection and adapting CoOp's CLIP-loss (Zhou et al., 2022) into a dual-loss fine-tuning paradigm that explicitly targets robustness rather than direct classifier alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a general LPM plus a simple fine-tuning paradigm can match or exceed methods requiring complex CDFSC-specific pre-training, SRT offers a practical path to deploying few-shot classifiers in real-world cross-domain scenarios without bespoke training pipelines. The finding that standard Trad-FT actively degrades LPM performance (negative gains for DINOv2 with more trainable blocks) is a useful caution for practitioners. Open questions include whether the modulus-matching mixup generalizes to non-ViT architectures or to tasks beyond classification, and whether the approach scales to even larger models (ViT-L, ViT-H) where the overfitting risk is presumably more severe.
