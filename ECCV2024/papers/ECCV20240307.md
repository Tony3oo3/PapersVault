## Problem

Existing topology reasoning methods (e.g., TopoNet) predict lane centerlines via direct point regression, which provides stable localization but biases toward straight-line representations and fails in high-curvature scenarios. Segmentation captures accurate geometric detail but lacks the stable global localization that regression offers. The paper addresses this gap by proposing a unified framework that leverages both regression and segmentation to produce geometrically faithful centerlines with reliable topological associations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes surround-view RGB images (resized to 1024×800) and an optional SD map consisting of vectorized polylines with semantic type labels, which are converted into a BEV grid feature map. Outputs include 11 fixed-order centerline points per instance in a 100m×50m BEV space, per-instance centerline masks, an N_L×N_L topological adjacency matrix, and traffic element bounding boxes. Centerlines are partitioned into 150 real queries (visible lane boundaries) and 150 virtual queries (intersection connectors), with mask-based refinement applied only to real centerlines since virtual ones lack sufficient visual evidence.

## Method

RoadPainter first constructs BEV features via BEVFormer, optionally augmented by an SD map interaction module that fuses semantic-type-embedded grid features with sensor BEV features through a transformer decoder. A 4-layer transformer decoder with hybrid attention (masked cross-attention over generated masks, deformable cross-attention, and a real-virtual separation self-attention that blocks real→virtual information flow) regresses centerline points and predicts topological associations. The key novelty is a points-mask optimization loop: detected centerline points serve as positional priors for mask queries (unlike Mask2Former's position-free learnable embeddings), and the resulting masks are sampled column-wise and row-wise to produce ordered point sets that are filtered, resampled, and averaged with the original regressed points to yield refined centerlines. Training uses separate bipartite matching for real and virtual centerlines, with focal loss for topology, L1 for point geometry, and BCE+Dice for mask supervision.

## Results

On the OpenLane-V2 subset_A dataset, RoadPainter achieves an OLS score of 38.9, surpassing all non-SD-map baselines, with DET_l of 30.7 and TOP_ll of 7.9. When augmented with an SD map (RoadPainter*), the method reaches DET_l of 36.9, TOP_ll of 12.7, and OLS of 42.6, outperforming the SD-map-based SMERF* by 3.5 points on DET_l and 5.2 points on TOP_ll. On subset_B, RoadPainter similarly leads in DET_l (28.7) and TOP_ll (8.5) among non-SD-map methods. The model runs at 6.5 FPS (FP32) on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoadPainter outperforms TopoNet [13] by +2.2 on DET_l (30.7 vs 28.5) and +3.8 on TOP_ll (7.9 vs 4.1), confirming that the points-mask fusion strategy improves both geometric accuracy and topological reasoning over direct point regression. Against TopoMLP [36], RoadPainter wins on DET_l (+2.4) and TOP_ll (+0.7) but trails on DET_t (47.7 vs 50.0), which the authors attribute to TopoMLP's 6-layer PETR architecture being better suited for traffic sign detection. In the SD-map setting, RoadPainter* dominates SMERF* [22] by a large margin: +3.5 on DET_l (36.9 vs 33.4) and +5.2 on TOP_ll (12.7 vs 7.5), demonstrating that the SD map interaction module is more effective than SMERF's approach. RoadPainter also substantially outperforms earlier topology methods STSU [1] (+18.0 DET_l), VectorMapNet [19] (+19.6 DET_l), and MapTR [16] (+13.0 DET_l), though these methods were not originally designed for topology reasoning. On subset_B, RoadPainter leads in DET_l (28.7 vs 26.6 for TopoMLP) and TOP_ll (8.5 vs 7.6), though TopoMLP retains an edge on DET_t (58.3 vs 54.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

RoadPainter extends the topology reasoning paradigm established by STSU and refined by TopoNet, which frame the task as BEV feature construction followed by centerline regression and topological association. Its key conceptual departure is the points-mask fusion strategy: rather than relying solely on direct point regression (as in TopoNet, TopoMLP, and LaneGAP), it generates centerline instance masks guided by the regressed points and then refines those points from the mask, borrowing the DETR-like mask query architecture from Mask2Former but injecting positional priors from the detected centerline geometry. The BEV backbone follows BEVFormer's spatiotemporal transformer, and the optional SD map interaction module is inspired by SMERF's use of navigation maps to augment visual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The end-to-end points-mask fusion eliminates post-processing while addressing a known weakness of pure regression approaches—poor geometric fidelity on high-curvature lanes—making it a practical improvement for production autonomous driving stacks. The large gains from SD map integration (+6.2 DET_l, +4.8 TOP_ll) suggest that fusing prior map knowledge with online perception remains a high-leverage direction, though the approach is limited to settings where SD maps are available. Open questions include whether the mask-based refinement generalizes to denser or more complex urban scenes beyond OpenLane-V2, whether the 6.5 FPS throughput is sufficient for real-time deployment, and how the method performs when traffic element detection (where it trails TopoMLP) is the primary downstream need.
