## Problem

Training diffusion models with disentangled latent spaces (separating spatial content from global style) and incorporating those disentangled conditions during sampling in a controllable, realistic manner remain underexplored. Existing composable sampling methods like CDM assume conditional independence between inputs, which fails when features are correlated (e.g., face shape and gender), producing unnatural compositions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240984/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image and a style image as input, both encoded into the LDM latent space. The content encoder produces a spatial layout mask of shape 1×(h/4)×(w/4) capturing pose and structure, while the style encoder produces a flattened 512-dimensional vector capturing global semantics (gender, color, hairstyle). The output is a single translated image that preserves the content image's spatial structure while adopting the style image's high-level attributes, with controllable blending strength via hyperparameters λ, β_c, and β_s.

## Method

FDiff trains a content encoder (spatial mask) and style encoder (flattened vector) jointly with a denoising UNet, injecting them asymmetrically through a modified AdaGN layer where content is applied spatially and style channel-wise, with residual (1+·) structure enabling conditional dropout. For sampling, GCDM generalizes CDM by breaking the conditional independence assumption: the score function becomes a convex combination (controlled by λ) of joint guidance ε(x_t,t,z_c,z_s) and independent per-condition guidance terms, yielding more realistic compositions. A sigmoid-based timestep-dependent weight schedule modulates content and style strengths across denoising steps, leveraging the inductive bias that low-frequency layout is resolved early while high-frequency details emerge later, further improving identity preservation and realism.

## Results

On FFHQ, FDiff with GCDM (λ=0.9) achieves the best FID of 11.99 and the best LPIPS of 0.34, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline SAE (FID 25.06, LPIPS 0.39). On AFHQ, GCDM (λ=1.0) further reduces FID to 8.58 compared to SAE's 9.29, while CDM alone yields FID 10.57. The method also demonstrates generality: when applied to Stable Diffusion V2 for text-to-image composition, GCDM produces more realistic multi-object compositions than CDM without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDiff with GCDM (λ=0.9) achieves FID 11.99 on FFHQ, more than 2× better than the closest baseline SAE (FID 25.06) and 2.5× better than DiffuseIT (FID 29.99). It also attains the lowest LPIPS (0.34), indicating the most diverse translations, whereas DiffAE+SDEdit (LPIPS 0.64) preserves almost no content identity and DiffAE+MagicMix (FID 84.55) produces the most unrealistic outputs. On AFHQ, GCDM (λ=1.0) further improves FID to 8.58 over SAE's 9.29, confirming that breaking the conditional independence assumption of CDM (FID 10.57) yields more realistic compositions. StarGAN v2 (FID 48.48) and DEADiff (FID 76.84) both struggle with out-of-distribution content and high-level style transfer, respectively. The paper also shows that GCDM generalizes beyond the disentangled setting: when applied to Stable Diffusion V2 for multi-prompt composition, it consistently produces more natural object placements than CDM without any architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LDM architecture for the base diffusion model and extends Composable Diffusion Models (CDM) by relaxing their conditional independence assumption between conditioning inputs, yielding the Generalized Composable Diffusion Models (GCDM) formulation. Conceptually, it draws on the GAN-based content-style disentanglement literature (SAE, StarGAN v2, Diagonal Attention) but adapts the asymmetric encoder design to a diffusion training objective, departing from prior diffusion-based disentanglement efforts (DiffAE, DiffuseIT, DEADiff) that either use a single latent space or achieve weaker separation. The timestep-dependent weight scheduling leverages the well-known frequency decomposition property of diffusion models (low-frequency structure resolved early, high-frequency details late) to modulate how content and style conditions are applied during sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GCDM provides a principled, hyperparameter-controlled interpolation between independent (CDM-style) and joint conditioning, offering practitioners a dial to trade off realism against diversity without retraining. Its demonstrated generalizability to text-to-image generation with Stable Diffusion (without any retraining) suggests the formulation could be broadly applicable to any multi-conditioned diffusion model. However, evaluation is confined to face datasets (FFHQ, AFHQ), and the disentanglement quality—while better than baselines—remains learned implicitly without explicit attribute supervision, leaving open whether the content/style split generalizes cleanly to non-face domains or more complex multi-attribute settings.
