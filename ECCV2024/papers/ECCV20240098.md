## Problem

Existing implicit neural representation methods (e.g., LIIF) map continuous coordinates to colors using only appearance features, ignoring the continuity of semantic information across pixels. When large image regions are missing, the lack of semantic guidance causes the network to produce blurry or semantically inconsistent reconstructions, as it cannot determine which object a missing pixel belongs to.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240098/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (H×W×3) with missing regions specified by a binary mask (H×W) and outputs a fully reconstructed RGB image at the same or arbitrary resolution. A key operational detail is that the semantic branch operates on a much lower-resolution feature map (h×w×c where h≪H, w≪W) produced by the CLIP encoder, which is then upsampled to full resolution via the implicit representation rather than naive resizing. The method supports inference at arbitrary resolutions, a capability the authors highlight as an advantage over diffusion-based inpainting.

## Method

SAIR consists of two coupled implicit representation modules. The Semantic Implicit Representation (SIR) first extracts text-aligned spatial embeddings from a modified CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder (query/key layers removed, value-embedding restructured into 1×1 convolutions to preserve the CLIP feature space without extra parameters), then applies an MLP-based local implicit function over these low-resolution embeddings to interpolate or extrapolate semantic features at arbitrary coordinates, effectively filling in missing semantic information. The Appearance Implicit Representation (AIR) uses a separate CNN encoder (EDSR-style, producing full-resolution H×W×C features) and combines each neighboring pixel's appearance embedding with its SIR-predicted semantic embedding and spatial distance in a second MLP to predict the output color. Training uses a composite loss: L1 on reconstructed pixel colors plus L1 on the SIR-reconstructed semantic features (to keep them in the text-aligned CLIP space), optimized with Adam for 200 epochs.

## Results

On CelebAHQ, SAIR achieves 37.97 PSNR / 0.977 SSIM / 0.010 L1 / 0.016 LPIPS at 0–20% mask ratio, 31.49 PSNR / 0.944 SSIM at 20–40%, and 24.87 PSNR / 0.870 SSIM at 40–60%, consistently outperforming all baselines across every metric and mask ratio. On ADE20K, SAIR reaches 31.01 PSNR / 0.964 SSIM (0–20%), 26.44 PSNR / 0.866 SSIM (20–40%), and 21.88 PSNR / 0.722 SSIM (40–60%), again leading all competitors. The gap widens with increasing corruption: at 40–60% mask ratio on CelebAHQ, SAIR gains +1.74 PSNR over the next-best method (MISF) and +1.93 PSNR over LAMA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAIR outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both datasets and all three mask-ratio ranges. Against the strongest inpainting baseline MISF, SAIR gains +1.74 PSNR (37.97 vs 36.32) and −0.003 LPIPS (0.016 vs 0.019) at 0–20% mask on CelebAHQ, and +1.29 PSNR (24.87 vs 23.91) at 40–60%. Against LAMA, the gains are +1.93 PSNR (31.49 vs 29.14) and +1.2% SSIM at 20–40% on CelebAHQ. Against the implicit-representation baseline LIIF, SAIR improves by +2.69 PSNR and +7.1% SSIM at 20–40% mask on CelebAHQ, and by +2.09 PSNR at 40–60% on ADE20K (21.88 vs 19.79). Traditional inpainting methods (EdgeConnect, RFRNet, JPGNet) trail SAIR by 2–3.5 PSNR at 0–20% and by 2.5–5.5 PSNR at 40–60% on CelebAHQ. In the ablation (Table 6), replacing the CLIP-based SIR with the SAM encoder yields 31.72 PSNR / 0.935 SSIM, still below SAIR's 32.36 / 0.944, confirming that text-aligned CLIP embeddings are more effective than segmentation-oriented features for inpainting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the local implicit image function (LIIF) framework, which maps continuous coordinates to RGB colors using only appearance features, by introducing a parallel semantic implicit representation (SIR) built on a modified CLIP encoder (inspired by MaskCLIP's architectural restructuring). The key departure from prior implicit representation work (LIIF, LTE) is the explicit incorporation of text-aligned semantic embeddings into the coordinate-to-color mapping, so that each pixel's reconstruction is conditioned on both its appearance neighborhood and its semantic category. The AIR module then fuses the enhanced semantic embedding with a CNN-based appearance encoder output, making the method a hybrid of vision-transformer (CLIP ViT-B/16) and CNN (EDSR-style) encoders within a single implicit representation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete failure mode of appearance-only implicit representations—degraded reconstruction when large regions are missing—by showing that semantic continuity can compensate for lost appearance context. The modular design (SIR + AIR) is validated across multiple encoders (CLIP, SAM, EDSR) and implicit functions (LIIF, LTE), suggesting the semantic augmentation strategy is broadly applicable rather than tied to one architecture. However, the approach inherits CLIP's limitations in fine-grained spatial semantics, and the paper itself acknowledges that broader applicability to tasks beyond inpainting (segmentation, denoising) remains unexplored. The practical speed advantage over diffusion-based inpainting (0.043s vs. 12s per image) with competitive fidelity is a notable deployment consideration.
