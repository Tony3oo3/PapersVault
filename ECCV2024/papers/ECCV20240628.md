## Problem

Naively incorporating adversarial data augmentation into self-supervised monocular depth estimation causes severe training collapse and performance degradation, unlike its success in supervised visual tasks. The paper identifies two root causes: (i) long skip connections in UNet-like depth networks amplify adversarial gradients, and (ii) over-regularization from worst-case adversarial examples creates dual optimization conflicts where adversarial and original-data gradients oppose each other.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240628/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on consecutive RGB video frames at 640×192 resolution, using a dual-branch DepthNet and PoseNet to estimate per-pixel depth via photometric reprojection. Adversarial perturbations are applied to the source frame, but reconstruction always targets the clean target frame to prevent erroneous bootstrapping from corrupted supervision. The framework is model-agnostic and can be plugged into any existing self-supervised MDE backbone (MonoDepth2, CADepth, HR-Depth, MonoVit, Robust-Depth) without architectural modification beyond the LSC scaling.

## Method

SCAT introduces two complementary mechanisms to stabilize adversarial training for self-supervised MDE. First, a Scaling Depth Network (SDN) multiplies each long skip connection by a coefficient κ (default 0.7), theoretically bounding the perturbation sensitivity to O(ε_δ · Σκ_i·M_0^i) instead of the unbounded O(N·M_0^N) of standard UNet. Second, Conflict Gradient Surgery (CGS) maintains a History Buffer of adversarial generators from previous iterations and randomly samples multiple generators per step, progressively blending their gradients so that the cosine similarity between adversarial and original-data gradients shifts from negative to positive, eliminating the dual optimization conflict. Training follows a min-max objective where the adversarial noise generator maximizes the reprojection loss while the depth network minimizes it, with both optimized via SGD in alternating steps.

## Results

On the KITTI-C corruption benchmark, SCAT consistently improves all five baseline methods over both offline data augmentation (DA) and vanilla adversarial data augmentation (ADA). The most dramatic gain is on MonoVit, where mCE drops from 80.54% to 62.74% and mRR rises from 88.98% to 95.38%. On the clean KITTI Eigen test set, SCAT preserves the original performance (e.g., MonoDepth2 Abs Rel 0.115 → 0.116 with SCAT vs. 0.121 with vanilla ADA), confirming that generalization gains do not come at the cost of in-domain accuracy. On the real-world NuScenes-Night dataset, SCAT reduces Abs Rel from 0.397 to 0.355 for MonoDepth2 and from 0.276 to 0.263 for Robust-Depth*.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAT is evaluated as a plug-in on five existing self-supervised MDE methods: MonoDepth2, CADepth, HR-Depth, MonoVit, and Robust-Depth*. On KITTI-C, SCAT outperforms both DA and vanilla ADA on every metric for all five baselines; the largest absolute gain is on MonoVit (mCE −17.8 pts, Abs Rel −0.064 over vanilla ADA). On the clean KITTI Eigen test, SCAT recovers the original in-domain accuracy that vanilla ADA degrades (e.g., MonoDepth2 Abs Rel: 0.115 baseline → 0.121 with ADA → 0.116 with SCAT), demonstrating that the stabilization components (SDN + CGS) prevent the over-regularization collapse. On NuScenes-Night, SCAT improves MonoDepth2 Abs Rel from 0.397 to 0.355 and Robust-Depth* from 0.276 to 0.263, with consistent gains in δ<1.25 (e.g., 0.378→0.392 for MonoDepth2). Robust-Depth*, which already incorporates offline weather augmentation, shows the smallest relative improvement (mCE 55.72→53.37), suggesting diminishing returns when the base method already has strong generalization, yet SCAT still achieves the best absolute numbers. The ablation (Table 5) confirms both CGS and SDN contribute independently, with their combination yielding the best results (e.g., KITTI-C Abs Rel 0.193→0.165 for MonoDepth2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends adversarial data augmentation (ADA) from supervised vision tasks into the self-supervised monocular depth estimation setting, where it had been largely unexplored due to the absence of ground-truth depth labels. It departs from prior offline augmentation approaches (Robust-Depth, ADDS-DepthNet) that require pre-defined target distributions, instead adopting an online adversarial generator. The two proposed stabilizing mechanisms draw from distinct lineages: the Scaling Depth Network adapts the skip-connection scaling idea from ScaleLong (diffusion model training stability) to UNet-based depth decoders, while Conflict Gradient Surgery repurposes the gradient projection technique from multi-task learning (Yu et al., 2020) to resolve the specific adversarial-vs-clean gradient conflict in self-supervised photometric reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCAT provides a plug-and-play, model-agnostic framework that consistently improves cross-domain generalization across five SOTA self-supervised MDE methods and five benchmarks, making it practically useful for anyone deploying MDE in open-world driving or robotics. The identification of LSC sensitivity and dual-optimization conflict as the root causes of ADA failure in self-supervised settings offers a diagnostic lens that may generalize to other self-supervised tasks with UNet-like architectures. The authors acknowledge that iterative adversarial augmentation (e.g., multi-step PGD-style updates) remains unexplored, and the fixed κ=0.7 scaling factor, while effective, lacks a principled per-architecture derivation. Whether the framework transfers to other self-supervised 3D perception tasks (e.g., pose estimation, optical flow) is an open question.
