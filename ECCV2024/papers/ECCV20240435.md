## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods for LLMs (e.g., H2O, StreamingLLM) rely on eviction strategies that only activate when sequence length exceeds a fixed cache capacity, and they do not specifically preserve the multimodal instruction-following capability of vision-language models during long-form generation. This paper addresses both limitations by seeking a cache management approach that accelerates inference at any sequence length while maintaining the model's ability to follow visual instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240435/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard multimodal instruction-following inputs (RGB image patches, text instructions, system prompts, and chat history) and produces a generated text response. The key tunable parameter is the KV cache budget ratio (e.g., 0.2–1.0), which controls the compression level independently of absolute sequence length, allowing arbitrary acceleration without requiring the sequence to exceed a threshold. The compressed KV cache itself is an intermediate artifact that replaces the full cache during both encoding and generation phases.

## Method

Elastic Cache applies two distinct sparsification policies to the two inference stages ('One Sequence, Two Policies'). During instruction encoding, it computes a layer-wise sum of attention scores across all heads to rank token importance, selects the top-γ fraction as anchor points, and merges all KV vectors within each anchor's neighborhood into a single averaged vector (cache merging rather than eviction), with all heads in a layer sharing the same anchor positions. During output generation, it uses a fixed-point elimination strategy: a queue-based cache retains initial instruction vectors and recent tokens, while systematically removing vectors at a fixed truncation position (experimentally set to 25 tokens from the recent end) as new tokens are appended. The entire approach is training-free, parameter-free, and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modification or fine-tuning of the underlying LVLM.

## Results

On the GPT-4V win-rate evaluation, Elastic Cache achieves 47.54%, 46.63%, and 37.56% win rates at KV-Cache budgets of 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3 respectively, consistently outperforming both H2O and Local (StreamingLLM) baselines. On LLaVA-1.5 7B with a 0.5 budget, Elastic Cache surpasses H2O by 4.34 in PPL and 0.089 in ROUGE-L F1, and outperforms Local by 28.72 in PPL and 0.165 in ROUGE-L F1. In terms of inference speed, Elastic Cache achieves up to 77.9% actual speedup (476.3 vs. 267.7 token/s throughput) on LLaVA-1.5 7B with a 0.2 KV-Cache budget, and avoids out-of-memory failures at batch size 48 where the full-cache baseline fails entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Elastic Cache is compared against two baselines: H2O (Heavy-Hitter Oracle, frequency-based eviction) and Local (StreamingLLM, distance-based eviction). On the GPT-4V win-rate metric, Elastic Cache wins against H2O at every budget level (e.g., 46.63% vs. 35.26% at budget 0.2, a 11.4-point gap) and against Local at budgets 0.2 and 0.3 (46.63% vs. 35.29% and 37.56% vs. 10.10%), though Local is competitive at the most aggressive 0.1 budget (46.37% vs. 47.54%). On PPL and ROUGE-L F1 across LLaVA-1.5 13B/7B and Qwen-VL 7B, Elastic Cache consistently outperforms both baselines across all tested budgets from 1.0 down to 0.2. The gap widens substantially at lower budgets: at 0.5 on LLaVA-1.5 7B, Elastic Cache beats H2O by 4.34 PPL and Local by 28.72 PPL. Qualitatively, at a 0.5 budget, Local produces repetitive looping text and H2O generates significantly shorter responses, while Elastic Cache maintains coherent, detailed image descriptions. Elastic Cache also delivers up to 78% inference speedup over full-cache inference, a practical advantage neither baseline explicitly targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the KV cache management line of work (H2O, StreamingLLM, Scissorhands) from text-only LLMs to multimodal vision instruction-following models, addressing two gaps the authors identify: existing methods only accelerate when sequences exceed a fixed cache capacity, and they do not specifically preserve multimodal instruction-following capability. The key conceptual departure is replacing cache eviction with importance-driven cache merging (averaging surrounding vectors into anchor points) and applying stage-specific policies—frequency-based merging for instruction encoding and fixed-point elimination for output generation—rather than a single uniform eviction rule. The approach is training-free and plug-and-play, distinguishing it from learned compression methods like Gist tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A training-free, 78% speedup at 20% cache budget that works for any sequence length is practically significant for deploying LVLMs in real-time dialogue and chatbot settings where KV cache memory often exceeds model weight memory. The paper's limitation that attention-score-based importance may not always align with the most computationally optimal caching strategy, combined with evaluation on only three models and two datasets, leaves open whether the merging strategy generalizes to larger or architecturally different LVLMs. An open question is how Elastic Cache composes with orthogonal compression techniques (quantization, offloading) that the paper acknowledges but does not experimentally combine.
