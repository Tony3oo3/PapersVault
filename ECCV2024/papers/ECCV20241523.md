## Problem

Existing HMER methods rely on autoregressive encoder-decoder frameworks that decode LaTeX tokens sequentially, suffering from limited global context utilization, error accumulation across decoding steps, and slow inference speed. No non-autoregressive approach had been explored for HMER, despite the task's complex 2D structural relations making sequential generation particularly costly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241523/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a handwritten mathematical expression and produces a LaTeX string (or equivalently a Symbol Layout Tree). Internally, the output is first constructed as a directed acyclic graph where nodes are symbol tokens with approximate 2D positions on a 1/8-resolution feature map and edges encode directional connectivity; a longest-path traversal from <sos> to <eos> yields the final sequence. No precise bounding boxes or character-level annotations are required—coarse token positions suffice for structural recovery.

## Method

NAMER is a two-stage, bottom-up non-autoregressive pipeline. The Visual Aware Tokenizer (VAT) predicts all visible symbols and local relation tokens (including imaginary tokens like '^' and '_') in parallel via a classification head over a multi-scale CNN feature map, trained with a bipartite matching strategy (Hungarian algorithm) that assigns each ground-truth token to its best-matching spatial position from a pretrained HMER model's attention maps. The Parallel Graph Decoder (PGD) then refines these coarse tokens using three parallel transformer heads with cross-attention to visual features: a Self-node Correction Head revises or deletes misclassified tokens, while Left and Right Connectivity Heads predict directional edges between all token pairs simultaneously. Finally, a DAG is assembled from the corrected nodes and summed connectivity scores, and the recognition result is extracted by finding the longest path from <sos> to <eos> in O(V+E) time via topological ordering.

## Results

On CROHME 2014/2016/2019 (no augmentation), NAMER achieves ExpRates of 60.51%, 60.24%, and 61.72%, surpassing the previous SOTA CoMER (58.57%, 57.89%, 59.71%) by 1.94%, 2.35%, and 1.49% respectively. On HME100K, NAMER reaches 68.52% ExpRate, outperforming CoMER (67.90%) by 0.62%. In inference efficiency, NAMER achieves 15.02 ms decoder time and 30.00 FPS, a 13.7× speedup in decoding and 6.7× in overall FPS over CoMER (206.12 ms, 4.46 FPS), while using only 2569 MB GPU memory versus CoMER's 13441 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAMER outperforms all 20 compared baselines on every CROHME test set without augmentation. Against the closest competitor CoMER [52] (previous SOTA), NAMER gains 1.94%/2.35%/1.49% ExpRate on CROHME 2014/2016/2019, and 0.62% on HME100K. The recent GCN [51], which uses extra symbol-category supervision, is the nearest rival: NAMER edges it out by 0.51%/1.30%/0.09% on the three CROHME sets, though GCN narrows the gap to 0.09% on 2019. Among earlier AR-based methods, NAMER surpasses CAN-ABM [27] by 3.25%/4.09%/5.76%, ABM [4] by 3.66%/7.32%/7.76%, TSDNet [54] by 5.81%/7.76%/5.38%, and SAN [46] by 4.31%/6.64%/8.22% on CROHME 2014/2016/2019 respectively. On HME100K, NAMER beats BTTR [53] by 4.42%, SAN [46] by 1.42%, and CAN [27] by 1.21%. In inference cost, NAMER is 13.7× faster in decoding and 6.7× faster in overall FPS than CoMER, and even 2.36×/1.61× faster than the lightweight RNN-based DWAP [47], while using roughly half the GPU memory of DWAP and one-fifth of CoMER.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAMER departs fundamentally from the autoregressive encoder-decoder paradigm that has dominated HMER since WAP/DWAP, instead adopting a bottom-up NAR strategy inspired by human reading (recognize local symbols first, then determine relations). It repurposes DETR's bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm) to assign spatial positions to tokens without requiring character-level annotations, and draws on NAR transformer decoders from machine translation and scene text recognition, but adapts them to the 2D structural and imaginary-token challenges unique to mathematical expressions. The encoder (DenseNet) and overall image-to-markup formulation remain standard from prior HMER work; the novelty is entirely in the decoding architecture (VAT + PGD) and the DAG-based output representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.7× decoding speedup and 6.7× overall FPS improvement make HMER practically deployable in real-time document understanding pipelines, while simultaneously achieving SOTA accuracy on all CROHME benchmarks and HME100K. The finding that NAR decoding can *improve* accuracy (not merely trade speed for quality) by leveraging global visual-linguistic context for ambiguous symbol correction challenges the assumption that sequential generation is necessary for structural reasoning. Key limitations remain: PGD cannot insert tokens missed by VAT, and the approach has not been tested on online (stroke-trajectory) HMER. Future directions include extending the NAR framework to chemical structure and CAD image recognition, and adding insertion capability to the graph decoder.
