## Problem

Existing uncertainty attribution (UA) methods suffer from a faithfulness gap: gradient-based approaches only capture sensitivity to infinitesimal input changes, producing noisy and misleading maps, while perturbation-based methods rely on fixed or random perturbations that may not representatively reduce uncertainty and require exponentially many evaluations. No prior method directly optimizes for the condition that identified regions, when actually perturbed, yield measurable uncertainty reduction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image, a set of pre-generated binary segment masks from SAM (same spatial dimensions as the input), and a deep ensemble of 5 independently trained classifiers for uncertainty estimation. It produces a binary uncertainty attribution mask expressed as a linear combination of SAM segments, identifying semantically meaningful regions that contribute to predictive uncertainty. The method also generates a learnable Gaussian-blur perturbation applied to the masked regions, and can operate at pixel level (treating each pixel as its own segment) when SAM is unavailable, though at the cost of interpretability.

## Method

The paper formulates UA as a joint optimization over a binary mask M and a perturbation parameter φ, minimizing the ensemble uncertainty of the masked-and-perturbed input plus a sparsity penalty on M. Three key mechanisms enable this: (1) SAM-guided mask parameterization expresses M as a weighted sum of pre-computed SAM segments, reducing the search space from pixel-level to a small set of segment weights and aligning attributions with human-interpretable regions; (2) a learnable Gaussian blur with trainable standard deviation φ adaptively perturbs the selected regions, replacing fixed mask-out or random perturbations that can paradoxically increase uncertainty; (3) Gumbel-sigmoid reparameterization models each segment weight as a Bernoulli variable and samples via a smooth logistic approximation, enabling gradient-based continuous optimization while preserving the binary nature of the mask. The uncertainty function (total, aleatoric, or epistemic) serves directly as the optimization objective, and stochastic gradient descent with one Logistic(0,1) sample per step updates the weight logits and blur parameter jointly over 100–300 iterations.

## Results

On the problematic region detection task, the proposed method achieves the best IoU and ADA across all four datasets: 0.713/0.700 on CIFAR-10, 0.750/0.750 on CIFAR-100, 0.829/0.830 on SVHN, and 0.633/0.635 on PartImageNet. In the faithfulness test (Uncertainty Reduction Rate), the method achieves an average URR of 0.884 with iterative gradient descent refinement (top 0.5%+1% pixels) and 0.743 with Gaussian blurring (top 2%+5% pixels), outperforming the next-best baselines by 11.4% and 21.5% respectively. The method requires approximately 100–300 forward/backward passes per attribution map, with a runtime of 1.54 s on CIFAR-10 and 103 s on PartImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines (Grad, SmoothGrad, IG, UA-Backprop, KernelSHAP, LIME, Occlusion) on every dataset for both IoU and ADA in the problematic region detection task. The closest competitor is KernelSHAP+SAM, which reaches IoU 0.798 on SVHN and 0.676 on CIFAR-10, but the proposed method still leads by 3.1 and 3.7 IoU points respectively. Among gradient-based baselines, UA-Backprop is the strongest (IoU 0.619 on PartImageNet), yet the proposed method exceeds it by 1.4 IoU points there and by a wider margin on the smaller datasets. In the faithfulness test, the method's average URR of 0.884 (gradient descent) surpasses KernelSHAP (0.770) by 11.4%, and its blurring URR of 0.743 beats Occlusion (0.528) by 21.5%. Perturbation-based methods (LIME, Occlusion) tend to underperform gradient-based methods under gradient-descent refinement but recover under blurring, whereas the proposed method consistently leads under both refinement strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from three distinct lines of work: optimization-based attribution (Dabkowski et al., Fong et al.) that learns minimal perturbation regions via continuous optimization; UA-specific methods (CLUE, UA-Backprop) that target uncertainty rather than prediction; and the Segment Anything Model for semantic segmentation. It departs from gradient-based UA by applying actual (learnable) perturbations rather than local gradient sensitivity, and from standard perturbation-based UA by replacing exhaustive pixel-level search with a SAM-parameterized mask optimized end-to-end. The Gumbel-sigmoid reparameterization is borrowed from discrete optimization literature and repurposed here to learn Bernoulli-distributed binary mask weights under continuous gradient descent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Faithful uncertainty attribution is critical for safety-critical deployment where practitioners need to know not just that a model is uncertain, but which input regions cause it, enabling targeted data collection or model repair. The direct optimization of uncertainty reduction as the objective provides a principled faithfulness guarantee that gradient-based and heuristic perturbation methods lack. Limitations include dependence on SAM segmentation quality, the need for an ensemble (5 models) for uncertainty estimation, and moderate runtime (100–300 forward/backward passes per image). Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-ensemble UQ (e.g., single-model MC dropout), video or 3D inputs, and whether the SAM-guided parameterization generalizes to domains without strong pre-trained segmenters.
