## Problem

Existing text-to-motion synthesis methods struggle to generate semantically aligned and coherently coordinated motions, particularly for descriptions involving multiple body parts. Part-based approaches either embed part concepts within a single generator (making it difficult for the network to understand distinct parts) or use independent upper/lower body generators (which lack inter-part communication and produce uncoordinated motions). Scaling to finer-grained parts with additional generators introduces prohibitive computational complexity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241488/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text description of a human motion as input and produces a 3D whole-body motion sequence as output, without requiring ground-truth motion length (unlike diffusion-based baselines). The motion is internally decomposed into six part sequences—right arm, left arm, right leg, left leg, backbone, and root—each represented as a sequence of discrete VQ-VAE code indices (512 codes per part, 128-dim embeddings except 64-dim for root). The framework supports both SMPL (22-joint) and MMM (21-joint) skeleton models used by the HumanML3D and KIT-ML datasets respectively.

## Method

ParCo operates in two stages. Stage 1 (Part-Aware Motion Discretization) partitions whole-body motion into six parts and independently encodes each with a VQ-VAE (downsampling rate r=4), establishing a prior representation of 'what is a part' through separate codebooks. Stage 2 (Text-Driven Part Coordination) employs six lightweight 14-layer transformers (256-dim tokens) that autoregressively generate part motion code sequences conditioned on the text, with a Part Coordination module inserted before every transformer layer except the first. Each ParCo Block fuses the current part's token with tokens from all five other part transformers via a 3-layer MLP followed by LayerNorm, enabling inter-part communication at every depth. The total parameter count (168.4M) and inference time (0.036s) are lower than single-generator baselines like T2M-GPT (237.6M, 0.544s) and ReMoDiffuse (198.2M, 0.091s), while achieving superior R-Precision and MM-Dist on both benchmarks.

## Results

On HumanML3D, ParCo achieves the best R-Precision among all methods (Top-1: 0.515, Top-2: 0.706, Top-3: 0.801) without requiring ground-truth motion length, surpassing ReMoDiffuse (GT length) by 0.005/0.008/0.006 on the three R-Precision metrics. ParCo also reports the lowest MM-Dist (2.927) and MModality (1.382) on HumanML3D, with a competitive FID of 0.109. On KIT-ML, ParCo achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.430) and MM-Dist (2.820) among methods not using GT length, though its FID (0.453) trails ReMoDiffuse-with-GT (0.155). ParCo also uses fewer parameters (168.4M vs. 198.2M for ReMoDiffuse) and achieves faster inference (0.036s vs. 0.091s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParCo outperforms the strongest GT-length baseline ReMoDiffuse on R-Precision (Top-1: 0.515 vs. 0.510) and MM-Dist (2.927 vs. 2.974) on HumanML3D, while ReMoDiffuse retains a marginal FID advantage (0.103 vs. 0.109). Among methods that do not use GT motion length, ParCo clearly leads: it surpasses T2M-GPT (Top-1 R-Prec 0.492, FID 0.141) and AttT2M (Top-1 R-Prec 0.499, FID 0.112) on both R-Precision and MM-Dist, and achieves the lowest MModality (1.382 vs. 1.831 for T2M-GPT), indicating fewer semantically incorrect generations. On KIT-ML, ParCo achieves the best R-Precision (Top-1: 0.430) and MM-Dist (2.820) without GT length, beating AttT2M (0.413, 3.039) and T2M-GPT (0.416, 3.007), though ReMoDiffuse-with-GT still leads on FID (0.155 vs. 0.453). The left-right exchange experiment further demonstrates ParCo's superior part-level control (70% success rate vs. 50% for T2M-GPT, 30% for ReMoDiffuse, and 0% for MotionDiffuse). In terms of efficiency, ParCo uses 168.4M parameters and 211.7 GFLOPs, compared to 198.2M/481.0G for ReMoDiffuse and 237.6M/292.3G for T2M-GPT, with the fastest inference time (0.036s vs. 0.091s and 0.544s respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

ParCo carves new ground at the intersection of VQ-VAE-based motion discretization (as in T2M-GPT) and part-aware generation (as in Bailando, SCA/Hier, and AttT2M), but departs from all of them by replacing a single monolithic generator or two independent upper/lower-body generators with six specialized lightweight transformers, one per body part, linked by a novel Part Coordination module. The coordination mechanism—where each part transformer conditions its next-token prediction on the concurrent predictions of all other parts—is inspired by neuroscientific principles of distributed brain-region communication and has no direct analogue in prior text-to-motion work. The autoregressive, token-by-token generation paradigm follows T2M-GPT, but the per-part VQ-VAE codebooks and the inter-generator communication layer are new architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency–quality trade-off: six coordinated small generators achieve SOTA-level R-Precision and MM-Dist while using fewer parameters (168.4M vs. 237.6M for T2M-GPT) and substantially faster inference (0.036s vs. 0.544s), making part-aware motion synthesis viable in latency-sensitive applications like AR/VR and robotics. The left-right exchange experiment (70% success vs. 50% for T2M-GPT) provides concrete evidence that explicit part decomposition improves fine-grained control, a capability that whole-body generators lack. Open questions include whether the 6-part scheme generalizes to hierarchical or task-specific part decompositions, whether the coordination module scales beyond six generators, and the acknowledged evaluation-protocol artifact where generated motions outperform real test motions due to feature-extractor bias toward the training distribution.
