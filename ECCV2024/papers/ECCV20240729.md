## Problem

Recent blind super-resolution methods over-enhance intentional blur regions (e.g., defocus or motion blur used as a photographic technique), degrading perceptual quality despite high PSNR/SSIM. Over 20% of common SR benchmark samples contain such blur, yet no dedicated dataset or blur-adaptive framework exists to handle this case without compromising general image restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240729/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single LR image (x4 upscaling) and produces an HR image with intentional blur preserved; no blur map or data-type indicator is required. During training, the blur branch additionally consumes a binary blur region map (0 = blur pixel, 1 = non-blur pixel) as a conditioning input to the discriminator, sourced from human annotation (~20%) and D-DFFNet auto-detection (~80%). The framework is architecture-agnostic and operates on 256×256 patches with batch size 16 per GPU.

## Method

PBaSR introduces a dual-branch Cross Disentanglement Module (CDM) where a general branch and a blur branch are trained in parallel; the blur branch employs a conditional adversarial loss that concatenates the blur map with the input to the discriminator, enabling region-aware discrimination that reduces GAN loss in blur areas by ~0.17. A Cross Fusion Module (CFM) periodically interpolates weights between branches every k=20 iterations using an adaptive coefficient λ derived from the cosine similarity of the two weight vectors, maintaining a controlled inter-branch distance. At inference, the two branches are merged via simple equal weight averaging (W = W_G/2 + W_B/2), yielding a single model identical in architecture to the base method with zero additional inference or deployment cost. The framework is plug-and-play across CNN (Real-ESRGAN), VQVAE (FeMaSR), and Transformer (SRFormer) backbones, achieving 0.02–0.10 LPIPS improvements on blur data while matching SOTA on general data.

## Results

On the ReBlurSR-Test benchmark, PBaSR_FeMaSR achieves the best LPIPS of 0.3564 on defocus blur and 0.3624 on motion blur, outperforming the strongest baseline HAT (0.3924 / 0.3856) by 0.036 and 0.023 respectively. On general data (six benchmarks averaged), PBaSR_FeMaSR attains 0.3912 LPIPS, matching the original FeMaSR (0.3986) and SRFormer (0.4284) while surpassing all other methods. Across all six perceptual metrics (LPIPS, AHIQ, DISTS, VIF, GMSD, VSI), PBaSR variants consistently rank first or second on both blur and general data without any additional inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PBaSR_FeMaSR decisively outperforms HAT (the strongest blur-focused baseline) by 0.036 LPIPS on defocus blur and 0.023 on motion blur, while also matching or exceeding HAT on all other metrics (AHIQ, DISTS, VIF, GMSD, VSI). Against SRFormer, PBaSR_FeMaSR improves LPIPS by 0.041 on defocus and 0.052 on motion blur, and also surpasses it on general data (0.3912 vs. 0.4284). Compared to the original FeMaSR anchor, PBaSR_FeMaSR improves defocus LPIPS from 0.4037 to 0.3564 (−0.047) and motion LPIPS from 0.4659 to 0.3624 (−0.104), while slightly improving general LPIPS from 0.3986 to 0.3912. PBaSR_ESRGAN and PBaSR_SRFormer also beat their respective base models on blur data (e.g., ESRGAN 0.4199→0.3986 defocus; SRFormer 0.3974→0.3740 defocus) with minimal or no degradation on general data. CAL-GAN, while preserving blur visually, lags significantly on all quantitative metrics (0.4511 defocus LPIPS) and over-smooths focused regions. The framework's key advantage is that it achieves these gains with zero additional inference cost, as the dual-branch weights are simply averaged at deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blind super-resolution (BSR) literature by identifying that intentional blur (defocus for artistic emphasis, motion blur) is a prevalent and meaningful image property that existing BSR methods inadvertently destroy. It builds on three anchor architectures—Real-ESRGAN (GAN/CNN), FeMaSR (VQVAE), and SRFormer (Transformer)—and wraps them with a dual-branch training strategy (CDM) that uses a conditional adversarial loss guided by blur maps, plus a lightweight cross-branch weight-interpolation mechanism (CFM) to fuse the branches at inference without architectural changes. The dataset construction draws on prior blur-detection tools (D-DFFNet) and diffusion-based synthesis (Stable Diffusion + GPT-3.5) to create a dedicated blur-kept SR benchmark, filling a gap the authors argue has been overlooked in the BSR benchmarking ecosystem (DIV2K, Flickr2K, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that over 20% of DIV2K-Val samples contain noticeable blur, and that SOTA BSR methods degrade by 0.01–0.06 LPIPS on those samples, reframes blur from a nuisance to be removed into a property to be preserved—a shift with implications for any perceptual image enhancement pipeline. The architecture-agnostic, zero-extra-inference-cost design makes PBaSR easy to adopt on top of existing BSR deployments. Open questions include whether the dual-branch + weight-interpolation strategy generalizes to other co-existing degradation types (e.g., noise + blur, compression + blur), whether the 120-sample test set is sufficient for robust evaluation, and whether the conditional discriminator loss could be replaced by lighter alternatives as blur-detection models improve.
