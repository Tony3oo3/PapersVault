## Problem

Existing cross-view geolocalization methods are confined to small city-sized test regions (<150 km²) and rely on street-view panoramas with 360° FOV, known compass orientation, or training data from the target region. This paper addresses the gap by localizing non-panoramic street-view photos with limited FOV and no camera metadata across state-sized regions (~23,000 km²) without seeing any data from the test region during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240966/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single non-panoramic street-view photo (resized to 640×480) with unknown intrinsics, distortion, and orientation, and matches it against a precomputed database of aerial orthophoto embeddings. Each search region cell (30m×30m) is represented by four aerial images at progressively coarser levels of detail (384×384 px each, with metric sidelengths doubling from 76.8m to 614.4m). The output is a geolocation estimate via nearest-neighbor retrieval over ~1.5M cell embeddings, with the 50m-radius recall metric indicating whether the true location falls within the top-N retrieved cells.

## Method

The architecture uses separate ConvNeXt encoders for the street-view and aerial domains, followed by a multi-head attention pooling layer with a single learnable query token that aggregates encoded image tokens into a single L2-normalized embedding. A key design choice is the multi-scale aerial reference: each cell is described by n aerial images whose metric sidelengths double per level (d_i = 2^i · d_0), providing high detail for nearby scene and lower detail for distant scene to compensate for limited camera FOV. The search region is partitioned using a spherical layout with constant metric cell size (avoiding Web Mercator scale distortion), and training uses a decoupled contrastive loss with a curriculum-style hard example mining strategy that progressively increases the scan pool size. The aerial encoder processes all LOD images in batched mode, concatenates their token sequences, and the MHA block performs joint pooling across images and within each image.

## Results

On the Massachusetts test split (~23,000 km², ~11M non-panoramic Mapillary photos), the proposed method achieves 60.6% R@1<50m, 75.5% R@10<50m, and 84.1% R@100<50m, meaning it correctly localizes a photo to within 50 m of its ground-truth position in over 60% of cases. The best existing baseline (Sample4Geo) reaches only 12.8% R@1<50m under the same evaluation protocol. Ablation studies on a smaller ~100 km² test split confirm that multi-scale aerial imagery (48.3% vs. 33.4% for single-LOD) and the MHA pooling layer (48.3% vs. 38.9% for mean pooling) are the key contributors to the performance gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three baselines by a wide margin on every metric. Against the strongest baseline, Sample4Geo [15] (12.8% R@1<50m), the proposed method achieves 60.6% R@1<50m — a nearly 5× improvement — and 75.5% vs. 32.2% at R@10. TransGeo [57] (2.8% R@1<50m) and SAFA [36] (0.5% R@1<50m) are substantially weaker, indicating that the combination of multi-scale aerial reference imagery, the consistent spherical cell layout, and the MHA-based pooling layer collectively drive the large gap. Notably, the baselines are evaluated under a VIGOR-style setup (Mercator grid, single aerial image per cell), so the comparison also reflects the benefit of the proposed search-region layout and multi-LOD design rather than purely architectural differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-view geolocalization (CVGL) line of work initiated by VIGOR's many-to-one formulation and Sample4Geo's contrastive learning with hard example mining, but departs from both in critical ways: it replaces the single-LOD aerial reference with a multi-scale pyramid of overlapping orthophotos, substitutes the Mercator-based cell grid with a geodesically consistent layout, and swaps mean pooling for multi-head attention pooling. The architectural choice (ConvNeXt backbone + MHA pooling) follows the CLIP-style contrastive paradigm, while the decoupled contrastive loss and curriculum-style HEM are adapted from recent contrastive learning literature. The key conceptual departure is treating the search region as a set of fixed-size geodesic cells with multi-LOD aerial context rather than a flat Mercator grid with a single reference patch per cell.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that state-scale (~23,000 km²) visual geolocalization is feasible with non-panoramic, in-the-wild photos and no camera metadata, opening the door to practical deployment in mapping, autonomous navigation, and geospatial verification. The 72.7M-image dataset and 104GB reference database establish a new scale benchmark for CVGL that is over 100× larger than prior work. Open questions include whether the 60.6% recall is sufficient for safety-critical applications, how the method degrades in regions with sparse man-made structure or extreme seasonal change, and whether the 104GB database and sub-10ms query latency are practical for edge deployment. The reliance on up-to-date aerial orthophotos (captured in 2021) also raises questions about maintenance cost and temporal drift in rapidly changing urban areas.
