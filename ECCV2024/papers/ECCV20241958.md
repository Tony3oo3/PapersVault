## Problem

Diffusion models require dozens to hundreds of denoising steps, creating a major latency barrier to practical deployment. Existing zero-shot acceleration methods reduce the number of function evaluations (NFE) via advanced ODE solvers, but this coarse-grained skipping degrades fine-grained high-frequency details. The paper identifies that intermediate feature maps exhibit strong temporal redundancy across adjacent timesteps—an underexploited optimization axis that is complementary to, rather than redundant with, NFE reduction.

## Input / Output

FRDiff is a plug-and-play wrapper around any existing diffusion sampling pipeline (e.g., DDIM with 50 steps). It consumes the same inputs as the base model—a text prompt and a noisy latent at each timestep—and produces the same generated image, but with a subset of residual-block computations skipped. The method is architecture-agnostic, applying to both U-Net backbones (SDXL, SD v1.4, LDM) and diffusion transformers (DiT-256/512), and requires only a single cached feature map per layer, adding 5–180 MB of memory depending on model size.

## Method

The core mechanism decomposes each residual block into a pre-temporal operation S(·) and a temporal operation f(·, t). At designated keyframe timesteps, S(·) is computed and cached; at non-keyframe timesteps, the cached S(·) is reused while f(·, t) is still evaluated with the current time conditioning, skipping the expensive spatial operations (convolutions, self-attention). A score-mixing schedule λ(t) (hard sigmoid with τ=30, b=0.5) linearly interpolates between the FR-estimated score and the cached full-model score, effectively using reduced-NFE behavior in early steps (low-frequency structure) and FR in later steps (high-frequency detail). Auto-FR then automatically searches for the optimal keyframe set by training per-timestep gating parameters via straight-through estimation against a combined L2 reconstruction and latency-cost objective, requiring only ~0.1 h of tuning on a single GPU.

## Results

FRDiff achieves up to 1.76× speedup (LDM-CelebA: 0.748s vs. 1.317s baseline) with an average 1.62× acceleration across five architectures, while maintaining FID within 0.3–1.9 of the full-step baseline. On SDXL, FRDiff (M=2, NFE=50) reaches 4.183s with FID 6.40, compared to DDIM-20 at 3.078s with FID 8.45, demonstrating that feature reuse preserves quality far better than simply reducing NFE. The Auto-FR variant (NFE=35) achieves FID 6.20 at 2.914s on SDXL, outperforming both DDIM-30 (FID 6.31) and DPM-Solver++-20 (FID 6.63) at comparable latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDIM (reduced NFE), FRDiff consistently achieves lower FID at similar or lower latency: on SDXL, FRDiff M=3 (3.117s, FID 7.60) beats DDIM-20 (3.078s, FID 8.45) by 0.85 FID points at nearly identical speed. Against DPM-Solver++-20 (3.075s, FID 6.63), FRDiff M=3 is slightly worse on FID (7.60) but the Auto-FR variant (2.914s, FID 6.20) surpasses it on both latency and quality. Against DeepCache (5.027s, FID 6.34), FRDiff M=2 at NFE=50 (4.183s, FID 6.40) achieves comparable FID with 17% lower latency, and the paper notes FRDiff is architecture-agnostic while DeepCache requires U-Net. Against distillation methods (SDXL-Turbo, LCM), FRDiff is far slower (4.18s vs. 0.73s/0.17s) but achieves dramatically better FID (6.40 vs. 22.58/42.53), positioning it as the quality-preserving option when retraining is infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRDiff extends the training-free diffusion acceleration literature by introducing feature reuse (FR) as a complementary dimension to the well-studied reduced-NFE approach (DDIM, DPM-Solver++). While DeepCache also exploits temporal redundancy via feature caching, FRDiff operates at a finer layer-wise granularity within residual blocks and couples FR with a score-mixing schedule that explicitly exploits the complementary frequency-response characteristics of the two acceleration strategies. The Auto-FR component draws on straight-through estimation and differentiable ODE solving (DDIM) to automate keyframe selection, positioning the method as a lightweight autoML layer on top of standard diffusion sampling pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in a plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic (U-Net and DiT) acceleration that requires no fine-tuning and only modest memory overhead, making it immediately deployable for text-to-image, super-resolution, inpainting, and video tasks. The frequency-response analysis revealing that reduced NFE degrades high-frequency detail while FR degrades low-frequency structure is a useful diagnostic that could inform future hybrid acceleration designs. However, the maximum 1.76× speedup is modest, and the method's applicability is limited when the ODE solver requires non-consecutive score evaluations (e.g., DPM-Solver++), leaving open how FR can be integrated with multi-stage solvers. Whether the Auto-FR tuning cost (0.1 h) and memory overhead (up to 530 MB for SDXL) remain acceptable at even larger model scales is an open question.
