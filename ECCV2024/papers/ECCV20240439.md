## Problem

Video virtual try-on in uncontrolled, real-world settings remains difficult because traditional warping-and-blending pipelines fail under complex limb occlusions and dynamic poses, while video-based methods demand large amounts of high-quality labeled video data and dedicated temporal modules that limit generalization. The paper asks whether a purely image-trained diffusion model can produce temporally coherent try-on videos without any video-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240439/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference person video (N frames at 512×384, or 256×192 for VVT) and a single target garment image. During preprocessing, the person video is decomposed into cloth-agnostic RGB frames (via segmentation-guided inpainting) and pose maps, while the garment is encoded as DINO-V2 features of its RGB image concatenated with a Sobel edge map (257×2048 vector). The output is a full-length try-on video at the same resolution, generated via 30-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance (scale 2) on the garment feature.

## Method

WildVidFit reconceptualizes video try-on as per-frame conditional image generation using a one-stage latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion backbone) that avoids explicit optical-flow warping. A condition branch encodes the person representation (cloth-agnostic image + pose) and injects it into the UNet decoder via convolution, while the garment representation is applied through cross-attention, enabling implicit warping that generalizes to occluded poses. Temporal coherence is achieved without video training by a diffusion guidance module that steers the DDIM denoising process with two pre-trained models: VideoMAE (masked reconstruction loss on decoded frames) for segment smoothness and DINO-V2 (spherical distance between adjacent-frame features) for latent-space alignment. For long videos, a temporal co-denoising strategy averages overlapping clip latents at each timestep to avoid junction artifacts. The network is trained only on image pairs (100K iterations, frozen encoder), with the autoencoder fine-tuned via EMASC skip connections to preserve non-garment details.

## Results

On VITON-HD, WildVidFit achieves FID 8.67, KID 0.10, and a 61.32% user-study preference rate, substantially outperforming the next-best method (LaDI-VTON: FID 13.01, KID 0.66, 16.02%). On the in-the-wild TikTok dataset, WildVidFit attains VFID 9.87 and 73.10% user preference, compared to LaDI-VTON (VFID 14.24, 26.90%) and HR-VTON (VFID 25.43, 0.00%). On the VVT benchmark, WildVidFit matches the video-based ClothFormer on VFID (4.202 vs. 4.192) while remaining competitive on user preference (39.86% vs. 46.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildVidFit decisively outperforms all image-based baselines (CP-VTON, HR-VTON, LaDI-VTON, DCI-VTON) on every metric across VITON-HD, DressCode, and TikTok. On VITON-HD, it achieves the best FID (8.67 vs. 11.80–48.86), KID (0.10 vs. 0.37–4.42), and a dominant 61.32% user preference versus 16.02% for the runner-up LaDI-VTON. On the challenging TikTok in-the-wild setting, HR-VTON completely fails (0% user preference, VFID 25.43) while LaDI-VTON struggles with limb occlusions (VFID 14.24); WildVidFit reduces VFID to 9.87 and earns 73.10% user preference. Against the video-based ClothFormer on VVT, WildVidFit is essentially tied on VFID (4.202 vs. 4.192) but trails slightly on user preference (39.86% vs. 46.44%), suggesting ClothFormer retains a small edge in perceived quality on the simpler walking-scenario VVT benchmark. The key differentiator is that WildVidFit achieves this without any video-specific training, relying solely on image-based diffusion with temporal guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

WildVidFit departs from the dominant two-stage warping-and-blending paradigm in both image and video virtual try-on (VITON, HR-VTON, LaDI-VTON, DCI-VTON) by adopting a one-stage implicit-warping formulation inspired by TryOnDiffusion, where garment fitting is learned via cross-attention rather than explicit optical flow. For temporal coherence, it diverges from video-specific methods (FW-GAN, MV-TON, ClothFormer) that require dedicated temporal modules trained on labeled video data, instead repurposing pre-trained VideoMAE and DINO-V2 as guidance signals within the DDIM sampling loop. The autoencoder enhancement (EMASC) is borrowed from LaDI-VTON, and the fully cross-frame attention mechanism follows ControlVideo, but the combination of these components into a video-training-free try-on pipeline is the paper's architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that high-quality video try-on on complex, in-the-wild footage (dance videos with limb occlusions) becomes feasible without collecting expensive video training data or training a separate temporal module, lowering the barrier for deployment in e-commerce and short-form video applications. The diffusion-guidance mechanism—using a masked video autoencoder and a self-supervised image model as gradient-based steering signals during sampling—is a generalizable technique that could transfer to other video editing tasks. Open questions include scalability to longer videos beyond the fixed VideoMAE clip length, inference speed (multiple guidance models are evaluated at each denoising step), and whether the approach generalizes to full-body garments or multi-person scenes.
