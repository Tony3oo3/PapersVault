## Problem

Transformer-based audio-visual segmentation suffers from two compounding issues: low cross-attention efficacy (because global audio features mix multiple sound sources, providing weak semantic guidance) and unstable bipartite matching (because class-agnostic queries offer little structure for label assignment). Existing remedies like masked attention and anchor denoising do not adequately address these problems in the AVS setting, where the audio modality's lower informativeness amplifies both failures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB video frame (224×224 for low-resolution benchmarks, original resolution for full-resolution evaluation) and a corresponding audio magnitude spectrogram of shape T×F (time × frequency bins); for stereo audio on VPO, the first audio layer is widened to 2 channels. At inference, the model produces N sets of (binary mask, class probability) pairs that are merged via element-wise multiplication with argmax over class scores to yield a final pixel-level segmentation with per-segment class labels. Training additionally requires an off-the-screen noise audio dataset for the ACP corruption step and pixel-level ground-truth masks for the VCP and PCL objectives.

## Method

CPM augments the standard Mask2Former class-agnostic training with a class-conditional prompting stage built on a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) that iteratively models the class-conditional distribution of joint-modal mask embeddings via EM with momentum. Three sub-objectives are trained jointly: (1) Audio Conditional Prompting (ACP) corrupts the spectrogram with off-screen noise and uses GMM-sampled class-conditional queries to reconstruct the original spectrogram via MSE on predicted saliency masks; (2) Visual Conditional Prompting (VCP) feeds noisy class-conditional queries through the transformer decoder to segment and classify the corresponding image regions, bypassing bipartite matching for a stable per-class learning signal; (3) Prompting-based Contrastive Learning (PCL) extracts class-specific audio features via masked average pooling guided by predicted spectrogram saliency masks and aligns them with pixel-level visual features using a supervised InfoNCE loss. The total training loss is the sum of the standard class-agnostic loss (cross-entropy + focal + dice) and a weighted CPM loss combining all three sub-objectives.

## Results

On the AVSBench-Objects benchmark (224×224, per-dataset protocol), CPM achieves 87.64% mIoU (SS) and 65.22% mIoU (MS), surpassing the previous SOTA CAVP by +1.87% and +2.83% respectively. At original resolution on AVSBench-Semantics, CPM delivers the largest gains: 61.71% mIoU (SS), 43.11% mIoU (MS), and 57.25% mIoU (overall), improving over CAVP by +4.80%, +4.50%, and +6.50% respectively. On the VPO synthetic benchmark, CPM reaches 67.09% mIoU (SS) and 65.91% mIoU (MS), outperforming CAVP by +4.78% and +1.60%, though the gain on the multi-source multi-instance (MSMI) subset is marginal (+0.19% mIoU, −0.02% Fβ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPM outperforms all 14 compared baselines on every reported metric across the three benchmarks, with the most substantial margins on AVSBench-Semantics at original resolution (Table 2), where it beats CAVP [7] by +4.80% mIoU (SS), +4.50% (MS), and +6.50% (overall). Against the strongest per-image-protocol baselines, CPM surpasses COMBO [54] (81.70% SS mIoU) by +5.94% and QSD [26] (59.60% MS mIoU) by +5.62%. Among transformer-based methods, CPM improves over AVSegFormer* [14] (the Mask2Former-adapted version) by +7.84% SS mIoU and +11.94% AVSS mIoU under the per-dataset protocol. On the VPO benchmark, CPM leads CAVP [7] by +4.78% mIoU on SS and +1.60% on MS, but the advantage on the MSMI subset is negligible (+0.19% mIoU, −0.02% Fβ), which the authors attribute to the lack of a dedicated stereo positional encoding. Older per-pixel methods such as TPAVI [57], AVSBG [16], ECMVAE [37], and DiffusionAVS [36] trail CPM by 10–15+ mIoU points, highlighting the benefit of the transformer-based query architecture. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that each CPM component (CCDM, VCP, ACP, PCL) contributes incrementally, with the full model reaching 57.25% mIoU versus 53.04% for the AVSegFormer* baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mask2Former architecture (Cheng et al., CVPR 2022) as its segmentation backbone and adapts the GMM-based generative classification paradigm from GMMSeg (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2022) to model class-conditional query distributions. It departs from prior transformer-based AVS methods (AVSegFormer, BAVS, COMBO, etc.) that rely solely on class-agnostic queries by introducing class-conditional prompts sampled from an iteratively updated GMM, and extends the supervised contrastive learning of CAVP (Chen et al., 2023) from global audio representations to class-specific, mask-guided feature extraction. The paper positions its contribution as addressing two specific training pathologies—low cross-attention efficacy and unstable bipartite matching—that are exacerbated in AVS by the weak semantic content of mixed audio signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The class-conditional prompting strategy offers a generalizable training recipe for transformer-based segmentation where query semantics are ambiguous, potentially applicable beyond AVS to other multi-modal or weakly-supervised settings. The entropy-based stability score (STS) provides a useful diagnostic for monitoring bipartite matching quality during training. However, the marginal improvement on VPO-MSMI (0.19%) and the acknowledged limitation in handling stereo audio (jointly encoding positional and semantic information without dedicated spatial reasoning) suggest that complex multi-source and spatially-aware scenarios remain open challenges for the field.
