## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods rely exclusively on visual cues, ignoring the multi-modality nature of human category recognition. While Visual-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP could inject rich textual knowledge, they require predetermined class names that are unavailable for unknown categories in GCD. The paper addresses how to generate relevant textual descriptors for unlabeled images and effectively integrate them with visual features to improve category discovery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241375/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled set of images from known categories and an unlabeled set containing both known and unknown categories, with the total number of classes K predetermined. For each unlabeled image, an offline retrieval step generates a short descriptive text (up to 3 tags + 2 attributes) by matching the image against a pre-built visual lexicon using CLIP cosine similarity. At inference, the model outputs a category label per sample via soft voting that sums the probability vectors from the image and text classifiers, with the number of classes fixed at training time.

## Method

TextGCD operates in two phases. The Retrieval-based Text Generation (RTG) phase constructs a visual lexicon from tags across segmentation, detection, and classification benchmarks, enriched with LLM-generated attributes, then retrieves the top-matching tags and attributes per image via a ViT-H CLIP encoder to produce descriptive text. The Cross-modality Co-Teaching (CCT) phase trains two-stream parametric classifiers (image and text, both on ViT-B-16 CLIP) through three stages: a warm-up stage where both models learn independently with supervised/unsupervised losses plus a cross-modal contrastive loss; a class-aligning stage where high-confidence text-model pseudo-labels guide the image classifier to resolve class-index misalignment; and a co-teaching stage where each modality selects its own high-confidence samples to pseudo-label the other, exploiting the inherent inter-modal disparity as the diversity signal required by co-teaching. At inference, soft voting (sum of both modality probability vectors) produces the final prediction.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, TextGCD achieves 64.8% All accuracy, surpassing the best visual-only competitor SimGCD (DINO) by 7.7% and the CLIP-based SimGCD by 16.6%. On fine-grained CUB, TextGCD reaches 76.6% All accuracy, outperforming SPTNet (the best competitor) by 10.8% and SimGCD (DINO) by 16.3%. Averaged across all four fine-grained datasets, TextGCD improves All accuracy by 11.1% and New accuracy by 12.7% over SimGCD. The method also achieves 98.2% All accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 88.0% on ImageNet-100, setting new state-of-the-art on all eight benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextGCD outperforms all seven compared baselines on every dataset where they overlap. Against SimGCD (the strongest visual-only method), TextGCD gains 7.7% All accuracy on ImageNet-1K (64.8% vs. 57.1%) and 11.1% averaged across fine-grained datasets. Against CLIP-GCD, the only other text-augmented GCD method that simply concatenates frozen text and image features, TextGCD improves by 13.8% on CUB (76.6% vs. 62.8%), 16.3% on Stanford Cars (86.9% vs. 70.6%), and 10.9% on Flowers102 (87.2% vs. 76.3%), demonstrating that dynamic cross-modal co-teaching substantially outperforms static feature concatenation. Against SPTNet (the best fine-grained competitor at 65.8% on CUB), TextGCD leads by 10.8%. On generic datasets, TextGCD edges out PromptCAL (97.9% on CIFAR-10) and SPTNet (85.4% on ImageNet-100) by 0.3% and 2.6% respectively, while the gap widens considerably on the harder ImageNet-1K where most DINO-based methods report no results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) line of work, which has been dominated by purely visual approaches (GCD, SimGCD, DCCL, PromptCAL, SPTNet), by introducing a textual modality through a retrieval-based text generation pipeline that leverages LLMs (GPT-3) and VLMs (CLIP) to produce descriptive tags and attributes for unlabeled images. The closest prior work, CLIP-GCD, also uses text but merely concatenates frozen text and image features; TextGCD departs by treating the text and image models as peer learners in a cross-modality co-teaching framework, repurposing the co-teaching paradigm (originally designed for label-noise robustness within a single modality) to exploit the natural disparity between modalities as the required model diversity. The parametric training baseline is inherited directly from SimGCD, and the cross-modal contrastive loss follows the CLIP-style InfoNCE formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-quality textual descriptions—generated offline via retrieval from a visual lexicon enriched by LLM attributes—can substantially boost GCD accuracy, with a ViT-B model augmented by text outperforming a ViT-H model without text, suggesting that textual knowledge is a more efficient lever than raw model scale for this task. Practical limitations include CLIP's token constraint (at most 3 tags + 2 attributes per image) and the need for a large auxiliary VLM (ViT-H) for fine-grained datasets, both of which the authors flag as open issues. The framework is also modular: the auxiliary VLM can be swapped for newer models (FLIP, CoCa), so gains are expected to compound as foundational models improve. A key open question is whether the retrieval-based text generation can be replaced by direct generative descriptions without the lexicon bottleneck, and whether the co-teaching strategy generalizes beyond the two-modality (image+text) setting.
