## Problem

IMU-based motion capture models require large paired IMU-pose datasets that are expensive and time-consuming to collect via professional systems. Existing data augmentation approaches either add noise directly to IMU signals (failing to capture inter-joint physical constraints) or use generative models conditioned on high-level actions/texts (altering the original data distribution). The paper addresses the gap of generating physically plausible pose augmentations that preserve the original motion distribution while covering the motion space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference SMPL pose sequence (24-joint articulated body at 60 Hz) represented as 240-dimensional motion frames (root position/velocity/6D-rotation plus 19 joints' position/velocity/6D-rotation in the root frame) and produces augmented pose sequences of arbitrary length. From the augmented poses, IMU signals (global accelerations and rotations) are synthesized at 6 body locations (2 wrists, 2 knees, head, pelvis) via forward kinematics and temporal differentials, ready for training sparse-IMU MoCap models.

## Method

PoseAugment is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 uses an MVAE-inspired autoregressive VAE with a Mixture-of-Experts decoder, residual blocks in both encoder and decoder, and an expanded 240-dim latent vector; it is trained with scheduled sampling and at inference uses best-sampling (N=2 candidates selected by MSE to ground truth) plus motion refinement (position/velocity bounds and 6D-rotation renormalization) to prevent error accumulation over long sequences. Stage 2 solves a quadratic program over joint accelerations, reaction forces, and torques subject to the rigid-body equation of motion, a dual PD controller tracking the reference motion, a distance-to-root regularization on reaction forces (penalizing forces on joints near the pelvis), a stationary-support constraint, and a Coulomb friction constraint. Unlike PIP, the method drops the flat-ground and contact-labeling assumptions, allowing reaction forces on any non-root joint to support motions like stair climbing or handrail use. The optimized dynamic properties are then used to simulate final poses, and IMU data are derived by differentiating the resulting kinematics.

## Results

PoseAugment consistently outperforms all baselines across four datasets when used to augment training data for the TransPose IMU-based MoCap model. The largest gains are on the ACTOR dataset, where PoseAugment reduces rotation error (e_rot) by 20.7% and mesh error (e_mesh) by 12.2% relative to the unaugmented baseline, compared to only 4.9% and 2.1% for ACTOR itself. On the HDM05 dataset, PoseAugment achieves a 14.5% reduction in SIP error versus 5.4% for MotionAug. Notably, augmenting only the CMU sub-dataset (9.06 h, 19.9% of AMASS) with PoseAugment matches the performance of training on the full 45.61 h AMASS dataset (e_rot = 10.2°, e_pos = 6.5 cm vs. 6.4 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseAugment wins against all four baseline families across every dataset and metric. Jitter (random noise on IMU signals) provides negligible or even negative gains (e.g., +1.4% e_SIP on HumanML3D), confirming that signal-level noise cannot capture inter-joint physical constraints. MotionAug is the closest competitor, achieving −13.5% e_rot on HDM05 versus PoseAugment's −9.8%, but PoseAugment leads on e_SIP (−14.5% vs. −5.4%) and e_pos (−8.0% vs. −5.7%), and unlike MotionAug it requires no IK keyframe annotation and is not limited to 8 motion types. ACTOR (A2M) and MDM-T2M (T2M) yield only marginal improvements (≤5% on most metrics), while PoseAugment achieves 10–21% reductions, demonstrating that high-level text/action conditioning disrupts the original data distribution. MDM-M2M, the closest generative baseline in the M2M setting, improves e_rot by only 0.7% on HumanAct12, whereas PoseAugment achieves 9.1%, a 13× larger relative gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseAugment combines two established lines of work: the autoregressive, frame-by-frame VAE design from MVAE (Ling et al., 2020) for motion generation, and the dual PD controller with rigid-body dynamics from PIP (Yi et al., 2022) for physics-based motion correction. The key departures are twofold: (1) the VAE is restructured with residual blocks, an expanded latent vector, and inference-time techniques (best sampling, motion refinement) to maintain temporal stability over long autoregressive sequences, and (2) the physical optimization drops PIP's flat-ground and contact-labeling assumptions, replacing them with a distance-to-root regularization and a stationary-support constraint that generalize to motions like stair climbing and skating. The paper positions itself specifically for IMU-based MoCap data augmentation, arguing that M2M (motion-to-motion) generation guided by ground truth is more suitable than A2M or T2M approaches for preserving the original data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that augmenting only the CMU sub-dataset (9.06 h, 19.9% of AMASS) with PoseAugment matches the performance of training on the full 45.61 h AMASS dataset, suggesting a substantial reduction in data collection cost for IMU MoCap. The generalization of the physical optimization module (no ground plane, no contact labels) broadens the range of supported motions beyond what PIP handles, which is relevant for sports, rehabilitation, and other non-terrestrial scenarios. Limitations include higher computational cost versus noise-based augmentation, and the observation that gains are most pronounced on smaller datasets, which narrows the benefit for already data-rich settings. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other sensor configurations (e.g., 1–3 IMUs as in IMU Poser) and whether the VAE can be pre-trained as a reusable generative prior across tasks.
