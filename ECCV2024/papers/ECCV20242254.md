## Problem

Mean-Teacher based Source-Free domain adaptive Object Detection (SFOD) methods rely exclusively on High-confidence Pseudo Labels (HPL) for self-training supervision, which systematically overlooks hard-positive objects—minor classes, small instances, and objects with significant appearance changes under domain shift. This bias toward easily detectable source-domain instances produces high false negative rates and degrades overall adaptation performance, a problem that is especially acute in SFOD where no source data is available to correct the teacher's blind spots.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242254/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source-trained Faster R-CNN detector (ResNet-50 or VGG-16 backbone, pre-trained on ImageNet) and unlabeled target-domain RGB images, with no access to source data or target annotations. During adaptation, each target image is processed in weakly and strongly augmented pairs: the teacher generates proposals on the weak augmentation while the student is supervised on the strong augmentation. The output is standard object detection predictions—bounding boxes with class scores—evaluated via AP50 across five cross-domain benchmarks (Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes, Sim10k→Cityscapes, KITTI→Cityscapes, Pascal-VOC→Clipart, Pascal-VOC→Watercolor).

## Method

The paper introduces a Low-confidence Pseudo Label Distillation (LPLD) loss that complements the standard HPL-based MT loss. LPL are mined from RPN proposals that do not significantly overlap (IoU < 0.4) with HPL, then refined by filtering out overconfident-background proposals, amplifying foreground class scores via L1-norm normalization, and applying a foreground confidence threshold. Rather than using LPL for direct classification/regression supervision (which would propagate localization noise), the LPLD loss applies a KL divergence between the student's categorical prediction and the teacher's amplified class distribution within each LPL region, preserving inter-class relational structure while suppressing noise. An adaptive weighting scheme multiplies each KL term by the cosine feature distance between teacher and student RoI features, prioritizing LPL regions that contain more foreground content and simultaneously encouraging teacher-student feature consistency. The total objective is L_total = L_MT (HPL regression + classification) + L_LPLD (weighted KL on LPL), trained with standard EMA teacher updates.

## Results

On Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes, the proposed LPLD method achieves 40.4 mAP (ResNet-50) and 40.9 mAP (VGG-16), outperforming all SFOD baselines (best prior: LPU at 38.8 mAP). On Sim10k → Cityscapes and KITTI → Cityscapes, it achieves 49.4 and 51.3 AP for the Car class respectively, surpassing LPU (47.3, 48.4) and IRG (45.2, 46.9). On Pascal-VOC → Clipart and Pascal-VOC → Watercolor, it reaches 34.0 and 55.2 mAP, beating IRG (31.5, 53.0) and all other SFOD methods. The method also reduces False Negative Rate by up to 9.58% for small objects and 6.31% for minor classes compared to the MT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed LPLD method outperforms all SFOD baselines on 4 out of 5 domain shift scenarios. On Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes, it achieves 40.4 mAP, surpassing LPU (38.8), IRG (37.1), PETS (35.9), and the MT baseline (34.3) by 1.6, 3.3, 4.5, and 6.1 mAP respectively. On KITTI → Cityscapes, it reaches 51.3 AP for Car, beating LPU (48.4) and IRG (46.9). On Pascal-VOC → Watercolor, it achieves 55.2 mAP vs. IRG's 53.0 and SFOD's 47.9. The one exception is Sim10k → Cityscapes, where PETS (57.8 AP) outperforms the proposed method (49.4 AP), though LPU (47.3) and IRG (45.2) are still surpassed. Against UDAOD methods that require source data access, LPLD outperforms most (e.g., MeGA-CDA 41.8, CR DA 37.4 on Foggy Cityscapes) but falls short of the strongest UDAOD method PT (42.7 mAP), which has access to labeled source data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean-Teacher self-training paradigm that underpins most SFOD methods (LODS, A²SFOD, IRG, PETS) by introducing a complementary Low-confidence Pseudo Label Distillation (LPLD) loss alongside the standard High-confidence Pseudo Label (HPL) supervision. It departs from prior SFOD work that discards low-confidence proposals after NMS and score filtering, and from LPU which attempts to use low-confidence proposals via contrastive loss but struggles with background noise. The key conceptual departure is treating low-confidence RPN proposals as a source of hard-positive signal and supervising the student through KL divergence distillation rather than direct classification/regression, with adaptive feature-distance weighting to focus on object-dominated regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment concern—adapting detectors to new domains without source data—and provides evidence that the proposals typically discarded during pseudo-labeling carry meaningful information about hard positives (minor classes, small objects), reducing false negative rates by up to 9.6% for small objects. The LPLD loss is modular and can be added on top of existing MT-based SFOD pipelines, making it a practical drop-in improvement. Limitations include evaluation only on two-stage Faster R-CNN detectors (unclear generalization to one-stage or transformer-based detectors), and the mAP gains over the strongest SFOD baselines (LPU, PETS) are modest (1–3 points), suggesting the HPL-only paradigm still captures most of the adaptation signal. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to detection architectures without an explicit RPN and whether the adaptive weighting generalizes beyond the cosine-distance formulation.
