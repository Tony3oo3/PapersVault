## Problem

Current UDA semantic segmentation methods struggle with fine structures (poles, signs) and tend to oversegment objects with ambiguous visual appearance. While depth discontinuities naturally align with segmentation boundaries, naively adding depth to existing UDA pipelines fails to fully exploit this complementary geometric information, as the network over-relies on the pretrained RGB encoder and underutilizes depth features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images and estimated depth maps (obtained via MonoDepth2 for monocular or UniMatch for stereo inputs) at multi-resolution feature levels from a frozen pretrained RGB encoder (DAFormer/HRDA/MIC) and a lightweight MiT-B3 depth encoder. It outputs a single semantic segmentation mask via a DAFormer head. The design is explicitly many-to-one (two modalities → one output), distinguishing it from multi-task learning approaches that produce both depth and segmentation from RGB alone.

## Method

MICDrop introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a cross-modal complementary dropout strategy applies blockwise masking to RGB and depth features in an inversely correlated manner (e.g., 70% RGB masked → 30% depth masked), using a dynamic schedule that starts with high depth retention to accelerate depth encoder training and gradually shifts reliance to RGB. This prevents the network from ignoring depth and forces cross-modal feature recovery. Second, a cross-modality feature fusion module combines a global depth-guided cross-attention branch (depth features as queries/keys aggregate RGB values) with a local self-attention branch (sigmoid-gated 3×3 convolutions on depth features to capture boundary discontinuities), fused residually into the RGB features. The entire module is a lightweight plugin trained for only 20k iterations with the RGB encoder frozen, requiring ~11 hours on a single GPU.

## Results

On GTA→Cityscapes, MICDrop achieves a new state-of-the-art of 76.6 mIoU when plugged into MIC HRDA, improving over the baseline by 0.7 mIoU. On the same benchmark, MICDrop yields gains of 1.8 mIoU over DAFormer (70.1 vs. 68.3), 1.0 mIoU over HRDA (74.8 vs. 73.8), and 0.5 mIoU over MIC DAFormer (70.6 vs. 70.1). On SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, improvements are slightly smaller: +1.1 mIoU for DAFormer, +1.0 for HRDA, and +0.6 for MIC HRDA. With a ResNet-101 backbone, MICDrop gains 4.1 mIoU over DAFormer (58.2 vs. 54.2), surpassing the previous depth-guided UDA method CorDA (56.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MICDrop is designed as a lightweight plugin and is evaluated by augmenting three recent UDA baselines: DAFormer (68.3→70.1 mIoU, +1.8), HRDA (73.8→74.8, +1.0), and MIC HRDA (75.9→76.6, +0.7) on GTA→Cityscapes. Against older UDA methods, MICDrop (DAFormer) at 70.1 mIoU substantially outperforms CorDA (56.6), ProDA (57.5), DACS (52.1), AADVENT (45.5), and AdaptSeg (41.4). The paper also shows that MICDrop's complementary feature dropout is orthogonal to MIC's input-level masked image modeling: applying both together (MIC DAFormer + MICDrop) yields 70.6 mIoU, exceeding either technique alone. On SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, the same pattern holds with slightly smaller margins (0.6–1.1 mIoU), which the authors attribute to SYNTHIA's smaller dataset size. Boundary IoU analysis confirms that gains are concentrated on thin structures (pole, sign) and large objects prone to oversegmentation (truck, building), validating the depth-guided design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MICDrop positions itself as a lightweight plugin for existing self-training-based UDA methods (DAFormer, HRDA, MIC), departing from prior depth-in-UDA work that treats depth as an auxiliary prediction task in multi-task learning. Instead, it reframes the problem as a many-to-one modality fusion task, drawing on RGB-D segmentation literature for cross-attention and local gating mechanisms. The complementary dropout strategy is inspired by DropBlock regularization but applied cross-modality at the feature level, and the paper explicitly shows it is orthogonal to MIC's input-level masked image modeling. The depth encoder (MiT-B3) and fusion module are trained from scratch while the RGB encoder remains frozen, distinguishing it from end-to-end multi-task approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that even state-of-the-art Transformer-based UDA methods (already at ~94% of oracle performance) can still benefit from geometric cues, suggesting the domain gap is not purely an appearance problem. The complementary dropout finding—that naively adding depth features yields only marginal gains while complementary masking unlocks the full benefit—offers a practical recipe for multi-modal UDA that avoids the complexity of multi-task balancing. Limitations include reliance on estimated (not ground-truth) depth, evaluation only on GTA/SYNTHIA→Cityscapes, and modest absolute gains (0.7–1.8 mIoU). Open questions include whether the approach extends to other auxiliary modalities (normals, flow), whether it helps in fully supervised settings, and whether the plugin design transfers to panoptic or instance segmentation.
