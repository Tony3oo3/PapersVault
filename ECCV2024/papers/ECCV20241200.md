## Problem

Existing disentangled representation learning methods rely on heuristic hand-craft loss constraints (e.g., fixed β penalties, mutual information terms, or variant-invariant losses) that are difficult to tune adaptively across datasets and scenarios, and they typically require synthetic data or label annotations, limiting generalization to real-world images. Additionally, there is no principled label-free metric to quantitatively evaluate disentanglement quality on natural data. The paper tackles all three gaps by building a self-driven, closed-loop system that eliminates hand-craft constraints and introduces an optical-flow-based evaluation metric.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a 512-dimensional disentangled semantic code z_sem that conditions a diffusion-based decoder to generate images; shifting individual dimensions of z_sem yields isolated property changes (e.g., smile, eye state, hair color) while preserving identity. During inference the β-VAE co-pilot is discarded, so only the Diff-AE backbone is needed. The same semantic encoder can also serve as a feature extractor for downstream discrimination tasks such as face recognition, producing an embedding suitable for linear probing.

## Method

CL-Dis is a dual-branch closed-loop framework that couples a Diff-AE backbone (semantic encoder E_sem + conditional diffusion decoder) with a β-VAE co-pilot through two complementary losses. A distillation loss L_dt transfers the β-VAE's preliminary disentanglement into the Diff-AE's semantic latent, while a novel diffusion-feedback loss L_fd replaces the hand-craft increasing controller C in the β-VAE information-bottleneck objective with a dynamic C_dyn computed from the entropy ratio E_{x0}/E_{xt} of the reverse diffusion denoising trajectory, so that the KL penalty is automatically strengthened as information capacity grows during denoising. This creates a mutually-promoting cycle: diffusion generation quality feeds back to sharpen β-VAE disentanglement, which in turn improves the semantic conditioning of diffusion. A self-supervised Navigation branch then identifies interpretable semantic directions in the disentangled latent space by training a predictor to recover shift direction and magnitude from paired generated images, enabling fine-grained controllable editing without any attribute labels.

## Results

On Shape3D, CL-Dis achieves a FactorVAE score of 0.952±0.017 and DCI of 0.731±0.045, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline DisDiff (0.902, 0.723) and all VAE/GAN-based methods. On CelebA, CL-Dis achieves an FID of 6.54, dramatically surpassing VAE-based baselines (132–139) and on FFHQ achieves FID 12.15, beating NaviNeRF (13.00). For face recognition, the CL-Dis semantic encoder trained for only 5 epochs reaches 91.72% on CASIA-Webface and 87.32% on VGGFace2, exceeding a 50-epoch baseline (89.45% / 81.82%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CL-Dis outperforms all VAE-based baselines (FactorVAE, β-TCVAE) on both FactorVAE score and DCI on Shape3D, with the largest gap over FactorVAE (+0.112 score, +0.120 DCI). Against GAN-based methods, CL-Dis beats DisCo (the strongest GAN baseline) by +0.075 FactorVAE score and +0.023 DCI, and substantially outperforms GANspace and GAN Edit v1 on DCI. The closest competitor is the diffusion-based DisDiff [90], which uses vector-wise representations; CL-Dis still wins on both metrics (0.952 vs 0.902 FactorVAE score; 0.731 vs 0.723 DCI) while using a more compact scalar-based latent. On generation quality (FID), CL-Dis achieves 6.54 on CelebA versus 132–139 for VAE baselines, and 12.15 on FFHQ versus 13.00 for NaviNeRF. For the face recognition task, CL-Dis's semantic encoder trained for only 5 epochs surpasses a 50-epoch learn-from-scratch baseline by +2.27% on CASIA-Webface and +5.50% on VGGFace2, demonstrating that disentangled representations transfer effectively to discrimination.

## Relation to Prior Work

CL-Dis sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines: diffusion-based autoencoders (Diff-AE, Preechakul et al. 2022) for high-quality generation and β-VAE (Higgins et al. 2016) for probabilistic disentanglement. Its key conceptual departure is from Burgess et al.'s (2017) information-bottleneck view of β-VAE, where a hand-crafted capacity controller C is replaced by a dynamic C_dyn driven by the entropy decrease of the reverse diffusion process, creating a closed-loop mutual-promotion cycle between the two branches. This contrasts with prior diffusion-based DRL methods (Asyrp, DisDiff) that impose external heuristic variant-invariant losses, and with VAE/GAN-based DRL that rely on fixed distributional constraints, positioning CL-Dis as a self-driven, label-free alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-loop mechanism offers a principled way to resolve the long-standing reconstruction-vs-disentanglement trade-off in β-VAE without manual tuning, which is practically valuable for practitioners working on controllable generation. The proposed optical-flow-based label-free metric addresses a genuine gap in DRL evaluation for real, unlabeled data. However, the method remains scalar-based (limiting expressiveness compared to vector-wise approaches like DisDiff), the entropy-based C_dyn formula (flattening image matrices into probability distributions) is somewhat ad-hoc and its theoretical grounding is deferred to supplementary material, and experiments are concentrated on face datasets, leaving open whether the closed-loop dynamics generalize to more complex or multi-modal scenarios.
