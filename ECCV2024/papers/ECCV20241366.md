## Problem

Existing 3D human generative models represent humans in a compact 1D latent space, ignoring the articulated structure and body-part semantics, which severely limits fine-grained controllability and local editing. Additionally, 3D-aware GANs have not achieved the generation quality for articulated humans that they do for faces or rigid objects, suggesting the need for a more powerful generative framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241366/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training uses 2D multi-view images or monocular videos with estimated SMPL pose/shape and camera parameters. At inference, the model samples a structured 2D latent map (e.g., 128×128×16) in the UV space of a continuous boundary-free SMPL mesh, then decodes it with specified pose, shape, and camera parameters to produce 512×512 view-consistent RGB renderings along with geometry (normal/depth maps). The 2D latent structure enables part-level operations—swapping, blending, or editing individual body regions—without requiring clothing masks or type annotations.

## Method

StructLDM is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 learns a structured auto-decoder: the body is partitioned into 16 local NeRFs, each conditioned on a corresponding UV patch of a shared 2D structured latent via barycentric interpolation, and a global style mixer (transposed CNN with 4× super-resolution) blends part-level features for full-body appearance consistency; training combines reconstruction, perceptual, face-identity, and adversarial losses. Stage 2 freezes the auto-decoder and trains a latent diffusion model (U-Net ε-prediction) directly in the structured 2D latent space, using a structure-aligned normalization that computes per-UV-pixel mean and variance across the dataset rather than global standardization. The key architectural insight is that the continuous boundary-free UV mapping on SMPL preserves body topology and semantics in the latent, enabling explainable differentiable rendering without an implicit StyleGAN-style mapping network, and supporting downstream tasks like compositional generation, part-aware editing, and 3D virtual try-on through a Diff-Render procedure (partial noising/denoising in the structured latent).

## Results

StructLDM achieves the best FID@50K across all three evaluation datasets: 9.56 on UBCFashion, 13.98 on RenderPeople, and 25.22 on THUman2.0. On UBCFashion, it outperforms the strongest baseline AG3D (11.04) by 1.48 FID points, and on RenderPeople it beats PrimDiff (17.95) by 3.97 FID points. A user study with 20+ participants confirms the quantitative gains: 74.68% of generated images are preferred over AG3D for overall quality and 72.5% for face quality. Inference runs at 9.17 FPS on a V100 GPU, 1.94× faster than AG3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StructLDM outperforms all five baselines on every dataset where they overlap. On UBCFashion, it beats AG3D (11.04) and EVA3D (12.61), the two strongest 3D-aware GAN baselines, by 1.48 and 3.05 FID points respectively. On RenderPeople, it surpasses PrimDiff (17.95), the only other diffusion-based 3D human generator, by 3.97 FID points, and EG3D (24.32) by 10.34 points. On THUman2.0, where only EVA3D reports a result (124.54), StructLDM achieves 25.22, a 99.32-point improvement, largely because EVA3D struggles with the small pose variance in that dataset. Qualitatively, StructLDM produces higher-fidelity faces and more diverse clothing styles than GAN-based methods, and its auto-decoder with global style mixer yields cleaner texture transfer than PrimDiff's decoder-free rendering. The user study further validates that the FID gains translate to perceptual quality, with ~75% preference over AG3D for overall appearance.

## Relation to Prior Work

StructLDM departs from the dominant 3D-aware GAN paradigm for human generation (EVA3D, AG3D, ENARF-GAN) by replacing the GAN with a latent diffusion model and, more fundamentally, by replacing the compact 1D latent with a 2D structured latent defined on the UV surface of a SMPL body template. It inherits the part-level NeRF decomposition from EVA3D but conditions each local NeRF on a spatially-aligned UV feature patch rather than a global 1D code, and it avoids the StyleGAN-style mapping network used by prior structured-latent methods (StylePeople, UV Volumes) in favor of explicit differentiable rendering. The structure-aligned normalization and part-aware diffusion (Diff-Render) are novel design choices specific to this spatially-organized latent space, building on DDIM sampling and transposed-CNN super-resolution from the broader diffusion and 3D-aware GAN literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured 2D latent unlocks a suite of controllable generation and editing tasks—compositional blending of body parts, part-aware clothing editing, 3D virtual try-on, and pose/view/shape control—that are impractical or impossible with a 1D latent, making the approach directly relevant to fashion and virtual-try-on applications. The ablation showing that a 16×16×8 structured latent outperforms a 2048-dim 1D latent highlights that spatial structure, not raw dimensionality, is what enables fine-grained appearance capture. Key limitations include training from scratch on relatively small datasets (500–800 subjects), which constrains diversity, and the lack of text or image conditioning, leaving open the question of how the structured latent integrates with large-scale pretrained diffusion priors for open-vocabulary generation.
