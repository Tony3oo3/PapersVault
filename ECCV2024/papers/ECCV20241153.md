## Problem

Existing text-to-video models generate a single-event video from one prompt, failing to produce coherent multi-event sequences where objects, backgrounds, and actions change across segments. Prior multi-prompt approaches either require extensive training on large video datasets or introduce visual degradation through overlapping denoising, leaving a gap in training-free generation of temporally and semantically coherent multi-event videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241153/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of text prompts (one per event) and optionally a reference RGB image as input, producing a concatenated multi-event video where each 16-frame segment at 256×256 resolution depicts a distinct event. A ChatGPT-based prompt generator can automatically segment a single complex narrative sentence into the required per-event prompt format. The entire pipeline is training-free, operating on a pre-trained latent video diffusion model without any fine-tuning or additional video data.

## Method

MEVG builds on a pre-trained latent video diffusion model and introduces two key mechanisms for cross-clip coherence. First, Last Frame-aware Latent Initialization performs DDIM inversion on the last frame of the previous clip (repeated across all frames) to obtain the initial latent for the next clip, incorporating (a) a dynamic noise schedule F(n)=exp(-n) that adds increasing stochasticity to later frames for motion diversity while anchoring early frames, and (b) a last frame-aware inversion loss that minimizes L2 distance between the denoised observation of the previous clip's last frame and the current clip's first frame at every diffusion step. Second, Structure-guided Sampling iteratively updates the denoised observation during backward diffusion by minimizing L2 loss between consecutive frames' predictions, enforcing global geometric consistency within each clip. An LLM-based prompt generator handles the conversion of complex multi-event narratives into the per-event prompt format required by the pipeline.

## Results

MEVG achieves the best scores on CLIP-Image (0.957), Temporal consistency (3.82/5), Semantic alignment (3.71/5), Realism (3.68/5), and overall Preference (3.68/5) in the human evaluation, outperforming all four baselines. On CLIP-Text, T2V-Zero scores slightly higher (0.322 vs. 0.309), attributed to its single-frame-per-prompt generation strategy, but it trails significantly on all temporal and human-evaluation metrics. Among the video-based baselines sharing the same foundation model (Gen-L-Video, VidRD), MEVG leads by 0.37–0.63 points on human-evaluation criteria, demonstrating superior temporal coherence across multi-event transitions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEVG outperforms all four baselines on every human-evaluation criterion (Temporal, Semantic, Realism, Preference) and on CLIP-Image. Against T2V-Zero [24], which builds on a text-to-image model, MEVG wins decisively on temporal coherence (3.82 vs. 3.61) and preference (3.68 vs. 3.47), though T2V-Zero edges ahead on CLIP-Text (0.322 vs. 0.309) due to its single-frame-per-prompt design. Against DirecT2V [20], also image-based, MEVG leads by 0.86 points on Temporal and 0.38 on Preference, confirming that video-based generation is necessary for natural motion. Among the two video-based baselines sharing the same latent video diffusion foundation model, MEVG surpasses Gen-L-Video [41] by 0.47 on Temporal and 0.63 on Preference, and VidRD [12] by 0.42 on Temporal and 0.54 on Preference. The paper attributes Gen-L-Video's lower scores to visual degradation from overlapping denoising across prompts, and VidRD's to recurrent video patterns (repeated camera/object motion) between clips.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEVG builds on the pre-trained latent video diffusion model [15] and repurposes DDIM inversion [36] as a mechanism to initialize the latent code for a new prompt from the last frame of the preceding clip, rather than sampling from pure Gaussian noise. The dynamic noise component draws on the video noise prior of Ge et al. [11], while the LLM-based prompt generator follows the pattern established by DirecT2V [20] and Free-bloom [22] of using language models to decompose complex descriptions. The key departure from concurrent multi-prompt work like Gen-L-Video [41] is avoiding overlapped denoising (which causes visual degradation) in favor of a sequential, frame-anchored inversion strategy that requires no fine-tuning or additional video data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of MEVG makes multi-event video generation accessible without the large-scale text-video datasets or additional network training that prior multi-prompt methods demand, lowering the barrier for practical storytelling applications. However, the method inherits the single-prompt model's text-format constraints, and the absence of a dedicated multi-text video benchmark limits rigorous quantitative comparison. Open questions include whether the sequential inversion approach scales gracefully to many more than three events without cumulative drift, and whether the exponential noise schedule generalizes to non-uniform event durations.
