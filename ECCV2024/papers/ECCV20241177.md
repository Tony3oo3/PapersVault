## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video editing methods struggle with human-centered videos, suffering from appearance distortion, structural flickering, and motion inconsistency as frame count increases. Prior decompose-edit-combine approaches that separate objects from backgrounds also overlook the critical need for lighting harmonization between the edited targets, producing visually incoherent composites.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241177/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a human-centered video (10–120 seconds) along with separate text prompts for the background scene and the human character. The background is processed at 768×432 resolution via a per-video trained NLA atlas (300K iterations), while the human mesh is rendered hierarchically from 32×32 up to 512×512 for RGB and at 512×512 for normals. The output is a temporally coherent edited video where both the background and the human are independently modified while preserving the original motion trajectory and spatial structure.

## Method

DeCo decouples the editing problem into two independent targets: the background, represented as a Neural Layered Atlas and edited with a depth-guided diffusion model (ControlNet), and the human, represented by a decoupled dynamic SMPL-X mesh whose geometry (shape parameters, expression parameters, vertex displacements) and texture map are optimized under the canonical pose via an extended Score Distillation Sampling loss. The key novelty in the loss is extending SDS beyond the latent space into both normal space (constraining geometric shape via a reconstruction term between rendered and denoised normal maps) and image space (constraining texture fidelity via an RGB reconstruction term), preventing the cartoon-like degeneration typical of latent-only SDS. After optimization, the mesh is animated using pose parameters estimated from the original video via HybrIK, decoupling appearance learning from motion. A lighting-aware video harmonizer then composites the edited human onto the edited background by harmonizing albedo maps and applying a two-stage shading pipeline (Lambertian model followed by a learned refinement network), with temporal smoothness enforced via exponential moving average across frames.

## Results

On 26 edited videos across Neuman, Hosnerf, and 3DPW datasets, DeCo achieves the best CLIP frame consistency (96.91) and text faithfulness (35.25) among all methods. In the user study (15 raters, 13 scene groups, 5-point scale), DeCo scores 3.92 on temporal consistency, 4.15 on editing capability, and 4.23 on image quality, leading all baselines by at least 0.7 points in every category. The closest competitor, RerenderA-Video, reaches 96.76 frame consistency but trails DeCo by 3.03 points on text faithfulness and by over 1.5 points on all user-study metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeCo outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against Tune-A-Video, the closest CLIP frame-consistency competitor (93.58), DeCo gains 3.33 points and leads by 2.90 on text faithfulness (35.25 vs. 32.35); Tune-A-Video also scores lowest on temporal consistency in the user study (2.23). StableVideo and TokenFlow, both NLA-based, trail DeCo by 3.3–5.8 points on frame consistency and by 5.3–5.9 on text faithfulness, reflecting their difficulty editing complex human shapes. RerenderA-Video is the strongest baseline on frame consistency (96.76, only 0.15 behind DeCo) but falls short on text faithfulness (32.22 vs. 35.25) and on all three user-study dimensions, where DeCo leads by 0.84–1.69 points. Qualitatively, the paper notes that Tune-A-Video struggles with motion consistency, StableVideo cannot modify human shapes, TokenFlow produces only minor facial changes, and RerenderA-Video exhibits blurriness in both foreground and background.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCo sits at the intersection of two research threads: NLA-based video editing (Text2Live, StableVideo) that decomposes scenes into editable atlases, and text-to-3D human generation (DreamFusion, TADA, Avatar-CLIP) that optimizes parametric meshes via score distillation. The key architectural departure is the explicit decoupling of appearance optimization (SDS on SMPL-X under canonical pose) from pose-dependent animation (HybrIK-driven linear blend skinning), which prior NLA methods handle implicitly and prior text-to-3D human methods do not address in a video context. The lighting-aware video harmonizer further distinguishes DeCo from prior decompose-edit-combine pipelines that composite rendered objects without addressing illumination mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled representation offers a clean, modular recipe for human-centered video editing that practitioners can adopt: SMPL-X handles motion fidelity while SDS handles appearance, and the harmonizer plugs a previously overlooked gap in composite realism. However, the per-video training of atlas mapping networks and reliance on monocular 3D pose estimation (inheriting their error) limit scalability to long or multi-person sequences. Open questions include whether zero-shot video representations can replace the per-video NLA training, and whether the extended SDS formulation (normal + image space) generalizes beyond SMPL-X to other articulated or non-rigid objects.
