## Problem

Radar raw data is inherently sparse because most pixels in a range-Doppler spectrum contain only noise rather than meaningful target reflections, yet existing radar perception models apply dense CNNs to the full spectrum, wasting computation on irrelevant noise. No prior work has jointly addressed both the selection of informative pixels and the design of a network architecture that efficiently exploits the resulting sparsity pattern.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242292/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a complex range-Doppler spectrum of shape C^(H×W×N_Rx) from an FMCW MIMO radar (12 Tx, 16 Rx on the RADIal dataset), decomposed into real and imaginary parts. Before any feature extraction, the Deep Radar Subsampling module reduces the input to only M=4000 pixels (≈3% of the full 512×256 spatial grid), so the backbone operates on a highly sparse representation. Outputs are vehicle bounding boxes with range and angle offsets (IoU>0.5 within 100 m) and a binary free/occupied driving-space segmentation mask evaluated over [0, 50] m.

## Method

SparseRadNet combines three key components. First, a Deep Radar Subsampling (DRS) module uses Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization with an argtopM operation to dynamically select M pixels per frame, trained end-to-end by perception gradients and pre-trained on a dense FFT-RadNet backbone to obtain full gradient coverage. Second, a two-branch backbone processes the sparse input: a GNN branch builds dynamic graphs over subsampled pixels (K nearest neighbors in feature space, K increasing from 9 to 18 across four ViG-style blocks) to capture global embedding-space affinity from repeated MIMO object signatures, while a sparse CNN branch with a customized MIMO pre-encoder and sparse FPN extracts local spatial context. Third, an attentive fusion module combines the two branches via pixel-wise spatial attention followed by axial attention blocks that decompose attention into row-wise and column-wise windows, aligning the globally-positioned GNN features with the locally-reorganized SCNN features before a dense FPN and range-angle decoder produce final predictions.

## Results

On the RADIal test set, SparseRadNet achieves a state-of-the-art average F1 score of 93.84% in object detection, surpassing the previous best (TransRadar, 92.87%) by nearly 1 point, while using only 3% of the RD spectrum pixels (4000 out of ~131k) as input. It also attains the highest AP (96.00%) and AR (91.78%) among all RD-based methods. For freespace segmentation, SparseRadNet reaches 78.48% mIoU, which is competitive but below TransRadar (82.27%) and Cross Modal DNN (80.40%). In terms of efficiency, SparseRadNet requires 259G FLOPs and 6.9M parameters, representing a 10% reduction in computational cost over the FFT-RadNet baseline (288G FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseRadNet outperforms all RD-spectrum-based baselines in object detection: it beats TransRadar (previous SOTA) by 0.97 F1 points (93.84 vs 92.87), FFT-RadNet by 3.09 points, and T-FFTRadNet by 4.34 points. Against ADC-based methods, it surpasses ADCNet by 1.94 F1 points and T-FFTRadNet by 6.34 points, despite operating on a more compact RD representation. In freespace segmentation, SparseRadNet (78.48% mIoU) trails TransRadar (82.27%) by 3.79 points and Cross Modal DNN (80.40%) by 1.92 points, though it slightly edges out ADCNet (78.59%) and FFT-RadNet (77.37%). The paper attributes the segmentation gap to the inherently dense nature of the task, which benefits less from the 3% subsampling strategy. Notably, SparseRadNet achieves its detection SOTA while using 10% fewer FLOPs than FFT-RadNet and only 3% of the input pixels, demonstrating that the DRS module preserves sufficient signal information for high-accuracy detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseRadNet adapts the Deep Probabilistic Subsampling (DPS) framework from Huijben et al. to radar perception, replacing the static, input-agnostic sampling mask with a dynamic per-frame mask generated by a CNN, and employs Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization for differentiable categorical sampling. The two-branch backbone draws on the isotropic GNN pattern from ViG for global embedding-space neighbor aggregation and on submanifold sparse convolutions for local spatial feature extraction, while the MIMO pre-encoder and range-angle decoder are inherited from FFT-RadNet. The key departure from prior radar GNN work (e.g., GTR-Net, RadarGNN) is the use of dynamic graphs built on feature-space distances over only subsampled pixels, rather than static spatial-neighbor graphs over all pixels or CFAR-filtered point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that only 3% of RD spectrum pixels carry sufficient information for near-SOTA radar perception has practical implications for data compression and computational efficiency in automotive radar pipelines. However, the DRS module's dependence on dense-backbone pre-training (since sparse backbones cannot provide full gradient coverage for the softmax) is a significant architectural constraint that limits end-to-end fine-tuning and may hinder transfer to new sensor configurations. Evaluation is restricted to the single RADIal dataset, and the modest hard-case recall gap (−0.2 F1 vs. TransRadar) suggests that aggressive subsampling can miss critical object signatures in challenging scenarios. Open questions include whether the dynamic sampling strategy generalizes to other radar modalities (e.g., 4D imaging radar) and whether the pre-training bottleneck can be resolved with alternative differentiable sampling formulations.
