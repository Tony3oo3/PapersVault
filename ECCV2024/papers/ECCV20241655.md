## Problem

Deploying text-to-image diffusion models on mobile devices is blocked by two compounding inefficiencies: architectures with billions of parameters and hundreds of GFLOPs, and the iterative denoising process requiring dozens of forward passes. Prior on-device efforts either pruned blocks without systematic architectural analysis or reduced steps to 4–8, still too slow for mobile. Additionally, one-step diffusion-GAN methods like UFOGen had not been shown to remain compatible with separately trained downstream modules (LoRA, ControlNet, inpainting).

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt encoded by CLIP-ViT/L14 (running in ~4 ms on-device) and, for conditional tasks, a Canny edge map, a reference image for style LoRA, or an image with an inpainting mask. All generation occurs in an 8×-downsampled, 8-channel latent space, and the final 512×512 RGB image is produced by a single UNet forward pass followed by a lightweight distilled VAE decoder (~92 ms on iPhone 15 Pro). The one-step design means no iterative denoising loop; the generator directly predicts the clean latent from pure noise in one evaluation.

## Method

MobileDiffusion combines a purpose-built architecture with a refined one-step adversarial fine-tuning recipe. The architecture is a UViT-style UNet under 400 M parameters and 182 GFLOPs, achieved by concentrating transformer layers at the low-resolution bottleneck (1024 channels), decoupling self-attention from cross-attention (removing SA at 64×64 and 32×32 while retaining CA everywhere), sharing key-value projections, replacing GELU with Swish and softmax with ReLU (via short fine-tuning), trimming the FFN expansion ratio to 3, substituting separable convolutions, and pruning residual blocks from 22 to 12. For sampling, the authors conduct an ablation study over UFOGen's design space—comparing diffusion loss, distillation loss, and EMA distillation as reconstruction terms, and full-parameter versus LoRA adversarial fine-tuning—and find that full-parameter fine-tuning with the original diffusion loss yields the best combination of FID and downstream compatibility, allowing LoRA, ControlNet, and inpainting modules trained on the base model to work seamlessly in one-step mode without retraining.

## Results

MobileDiffusion achieves a FID-30K of 8.65 with 50-step DDIM sampling and 11.67 with one-step sampling on MS-COCO 2014, while using only 386M parameters and 182 GFLOPs—less than half the parameters of SD 1.5 (862M) and SnapFusion (848M). In one-step mode, it outperforms UFOGen (FID 12.78) and SnapFusion (FID 13.5) while matching or exceeding their CLIP scores (0.320 vs. 0.317 and 0.308). On-device, MobileDiffusion generates a 512×512 image in 238 ms on an iPhone 15 Pro, compared to 646 ms for UFOGen and 7,429 ms for SD 1.5. On the HPS v2 human preference benchmark, the 50-step model scores 27.30 on average, slightly exceeding SD v2.0 (27.17) despite being more than twice as small.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step baselines, MobileDiffusion (1-step) beats UFOGen by 1.11 FID points (11.67 vs. 12.78) and SnapFusion by 1.83 FID points (11.67 vs. 13.5), while also achieving higher CLIP scores (0.320 vs. 0.317 and 0.308) with roughly half the parameters. In 50-step DDIM mode, MobileDiffusion achieves the best FID among all sub-1B-parameter models (8.65 vs. SD's 9.62 and GigaGAN's 9.09), and its CLIP score of 0.325 exceeds SD's 0.304. On-device latency is the most dramatic advantage: 238 ms total versus 646 ms for UFOGen, 1,740 ms for SnapFusion, and 7,429 ms for SD 1.5—a 31× speedup over SD. The 50-step model also edges out SD v2.0 on HPS v2 (27.30 vs. 27.17 average), confirming that the architectural compression does not sacrifice perceptual quality. The one-step model's HPS score (26.65) is slightly below SD v1.4 (26.95), reflecting the expected quality trade-off of single-step generation, but the gap is small relative to the 31× latency reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobileDiffusion builds on the UViT architecture (Hoogeboom et al., 2023) for its diffusion network, but departs from prior mobile-oriented work (SnapFusion, BK-SDM) that relied on block removal or distillation from larger teachers, instead conducting a comprehensive redesign of both transformer and convolutional components from scratch. For sampling, it extends the UFOGen one-step diffusion-GAN framework (Xu et al., 2023) by systematically ablating the reconstruction loss and fine-tuning strategy, ultimately showing that the original full-parameter adversarial fine-tuning with diffusion loss is the best recipe. The paper also draws on separable convolutions (MobileNets), ReLU-attention (Wortsman et al.), and LoRA (Hu et al.) as individual components, but its contribution is the integrated architectural and training recipe that makes one-step generation compatible with separately-trained downstream modules (ControlNet, LoRA, inpainting) without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 0.2-second 512×512 generation on an iPhone 15 Pro represents a practical milestone for on-device generative AI, making text-to-image synthesis feasible in privacy-sensitive, offline, or cost-constrained settings. The seamless compatibility of one-step sampling with the existing ecosystem of conditional generation plugins (trained on the base diffusion model) is a significant enabler, as prior one-step methods required task-specific adversarial retraining for each new condition. Limitations include reliance on a proprietary 150M-pair dataset (limiting reproducibility), a fixed 512×512 resolution, and the fact that the 386M-parameter model still requires careful quantization for true mobile deployment. Open questions include whether the architectural insights (e.g., ReLU attention, KV sharing, separable convolutions) transfer to higher resolutions or other modalities, and whether the one-step quality gap versus 50-step DDIM (FID 11.67 vs. 8.65) can be closed further.
