## Problem

Tree-Ring Watermarking demonstrates strong robustness in the verification task (distinguishing watermarked from unwatermarked images), but its performance in the more demanding multi-key identification task—critical for source tracing and user attribution—remains unexplored. The paper reveals that a large portion of Tree-Ring's verification robustness comes from an unintentional distribution shift (caused by discarding the imaginary part during imprinting) rather than the pattern design itself, and that this shift provides no benefit for identification. Consequently, Tree-Ring fails to distinguish even 32 keys under rotation or crop/scale attacks, with identification accuracy dropping to near zero.

## Input / Output

RingID operates on the 64×64×4 latent initial diffusion noise of StableDiffusion-V2, imprinting watermarks in the frequency domain via FFT. At detection time, the generated image is inverted through 50 diffusion steps to recover the initial noise, which is then transformed to the frequency domain to extract the watermark pattern. The method produces either a binary verification decision (watermarked vs. unwatermarked) or, in the identification setting, the index of the best-matching key among up to 2048 candidate keys, evaluated under attacks including JPEG-25, 75° rotation, 75% random crop/scale, blur, noise, and brightness shifts.

## Method

RingID introduces a Multi-Channel Heterogeneous (MCH) watermarking framework that imprints different watermark types on different latent channels—Gaussian noise on channel 0 and a discretized tree-ring pattern on channel 3—and identifies keys by taking the minimum of channel-wise ℓ₁ distances, enabling adaptive selection of the most robust channel under each attack. Four key enhancements improve the tree-ring component: (1) discretization restricts each ring's value to ±α (the noise standard deviation), converting n rings into 2ⁿ distinguishable keys; (2) lossless imprinting enforces conjugate symmetry (X = X_cs) by writing the pattern only in the real part aligned to the Fourier center, preserving rotational symmetry; (3) a spatial circular shift moves the pattern's energy from the corners to the image center to survive rotation-induced cropping; and (4) a pixel-rotation technique draws geometrically rounder rings that are truly rotationally invariant. Together these raise identification accuracy from 0.07 to 0.82 (2048 keys, average over attacks) and verification AUC from 0.975 to 0.995, while maintaining comparable CLIP scores and FID to Tree-Ring.

## Results

In the identification task (the paper's primary focus), RingID achieves 0.992 average accuracy (excluding C&S) for 32 keys and 0.942 for 2048 keys, compared to Tree-Ring's 0.465 and 0.077 respectively. Under rotation, RingID improves identification accuracy from nearly 0 (Tree-Ring) to 0.86 for 2048 keys. In the verification task, RingID raises average ROC-AUC from 0.975 (Tree-Ring) to 0.995, while maintaining comparable image quality (CLIP 0.365 vs. 0.364; FID 26.13 vs. 25.93). Both methods remain vulnerable to crop/scale in identification, with RingID achieving only 0.08 accuracy under 75% random C&S for 2048 keys.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Tree-Ring [Wen et al., 2023], the original method that RingID builds upon. RingID decisively outperforms Tree-Ring on identification accuracy across all key counts and nearly all attack types: for 32 keys, average accuracy (excluding C&S) jumps from 0.465 to 0.992, and for 2048 keys from 0.060 to 0.870. The most dramatic gap is under rotation, where Tree-Ring's accuracy is essentially zero (0.000–0.020) while RingID reaches 0.86–1.000, thanks to spatial shifting, lossless imprinting, and rounder ring design. In verification (ROC-AUC), RingID also improves the average from 0.975 to 0.995, with near-perfect scores under rotation, JPEG, and noise. The one area where both methods struggle equally is crop/scale in identification (RingID 0.08 vs. Tree-Ring 0.00 for 2048 keys), which the authors acknowledge as an open limitation. Image quality is essentially matched, with CLIP scores of 0.365 vs. 0.364 and FID of 26.13 vs. 25.93.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tree-Ring Watermarking (Wen et al., 2023), which imprints ring-shaped frequency-domain patterns into the initial noise of latent diffusion models for verification. It departs from Tree-Ring in two key ways: first, it reveals that an unintentional distribution shift (caused by discarding the imaginary part during imprinting) is a major contributor to Tree-Ring's robustness, not merely the ring pattern design; second, it shifts the evaluation from binary verification to the harder multi-key identification task, exposing fundamental design flaws. The proposed RingID framework introduces multi-channel heterogeneous watermarking (combining Gaussian noise and ring patterns across channels), discretization, lossless imprinting, and rotation-invariance fixes as systematic enhancements to the Tree-Ring paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that an implementation artifact (discarding the imaginary part) substantially inflates apparent robustness is a conceptual insight that should inform how frequency-domain watermarks are designed and evaluated going forward. The dramatic gap between verification (AUC ~0.99) and identification (accuracy ~0.07 for 2048 keys) in Tree-Ring highlights that robustness to one task does not transfer to another, a caution for the broader watermarking community. A significant open question remains: both Tree-Ring and RingID are fundamentally vulnerable to crop/scale attacks, and the authors suggest exploring other transform domains as a path forward. The practical impact is meaningful for source tracing and user attribution in diffusion model deployments, where distinguishing among many users is essential.
