## Problem

Graph-based methods for multiplexed immunofluorescence (mIF) image analysis face two fundamental bottlenecks: (1) cellular heterogeneity, where mIF cellular graphs exhibit homophily ratios of only 0.25–0.30, starkly contradicting the homophily inductive bias that GNNs rely on; and (2) scalability, where each image yields graphs with 837–19,611 cells, far exceeding the single-graph scale of standard GNN benchmarks and making neighbor-sampling strategies like SPACE-GM's 3-hop chunks both memory-intensive and limited in global information capture.

## Input / Output

Each 40-plex CODEX mIF image is preprocessed into a graph where nodes are cell nuclei (segmented via DeepCell) with features from biomarker expression and cell size, and edges are derived from Delaunay triangulation of 2D cell coordinates. Cell-type annotations are obtained via PCA and Louvain clustering on biomarker profiles. The model outputs patient-level phenotype predictions across multiple tasks simultaneously: binary classification (primary outcome, recurrence, HPV infection) via AUC-ROC, and hazard modeling (survival length, recurrence interval) via c-index, using a multi-task learning framework with a shared 3-layer MLP prediction head.

## Method

Mew constructs a multiplex network with two layers sharing the same nodes: a Voronoi network capturing local geometric neighborhood structure, and a Cell-type network connecting all nodes of identical cell type across the entire image (restoring homophily to 1.0). For scalability, it adopts SIGN-style precomputation of K-hop adjacency powers (AX) on the Voronoi network, eliminating iterative message-passing during training. For the Cell-type network, where all same-type nodes are 1-hop connected, a stochastic edge sampling technique uses normalized inter-cell distances as Bernoulli probabilities to selectively retain edges, differentiating local from long-range same-type interactions. A Voronoi-Cell-type Attention module then learns per-node weights to fuse the two network embeddings, autonomously determining which layer's information is most relevant for each cell's contribution to the final phenotype prediction.

## Results

On the UPMC-HNC dataset, Mew achieves 0.794 AUC-ROC (Average BC), 0.728 c-index (Hazard Modeling), and 0.743 AUC-ROC (Generalization on DFCI-HNC), outperforming the strongest baselines across all tasks. On Stanford-CRC, Mew reaches 0.659 AUC-ROC (Average BC) and 0.614 c-index (Average HM), a 21.14% relative improvement over SPACE-GM (0.544) in BC. In the scalability analysis, Mew reduces preprocessing time from 5,345 s (SPACE-GM chunk-save) to 76 s and evaluation time from 60.28 s to 0.53 s while processing the full graph (117,974 nodes vs. 1,071).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mew outperforms all eight baselines on every task across both UPMC-HNC and Stanford-CRC. Against the most direct competitor SPACE-GM [57], Mew gains +0.040 AUC-ROC in Average BC (0.794 vs. 0.754) and +0.058 in Generalization (0.743 vs. 0.685), with the largest gap appearing in the cross-institution DFCI-HNC transfer setting where the cell-type network captures shared cell-composition signals. Among standard GNNs, GCN [26] and GAT [56] trail by 0.065 and 0.062 in Average BC respectively; GraphSAGE [21] and SIGN [12] are closer (0.739, 0.751) but still below Mew. The heterophily-aware FAGCN [3] achieves 0.757 Average BC but collapses on Hazard Modeling (0.562 c-index), confirming that handling heterophily alone is insufficient without geometric context. The multiplex-network baseline HDMI [25] is the strongest competitor on Recurrence (0.807 vs. Mew's 0.832) but lacks a dedicated cell-type layer, limiting its generalization (0.585 vs. 0.743). ClusterGCN [6] performs best among clustering-based methods on Generalization (0.676) yet remains 0.067 below Mew.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mew builds directly on SPACE-GM's preprocessing pipeline (Delaunay triangulation, Voronoi diagrams, DeepCell segmentation) and its graph-based mIF analysis paradigm, while adopting SIGN's precomputation strategy for scalable GNN training. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a multiplex network framework—pairing a geometric Voronoi layer with a cell-type layer—to explicitly address the low homophily (~0.25–0.30) observed in mIF cellular graphs, which contradicts the homophily assumption underlying standard GNNs. Unlike SPACE-GM's 3-hop neighbor sampling that restricts the model to local subgraphs, Mew processes the entire graph during training via precomputed adjacency powers and a stochastic edge sampling technique, shifting the scalability burden from training to a one-time preprocessing step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two foundational bottlenecks—cellular heterogeneity and scalability—that have limited the practical deployment of GNNs for mIF image analysis, with the 114× speedup in evaluation time (0.53s vs. 60.28s) making whole-graph processing feasible in clinical settings with constrained compute. The multiplex network design, particularly the cell-type layer, offers a principled way to restore GNN homophily in-domain rather than relying on generic heterophily-robust architectures (e.g., FAGCN), which the experiments show underperform. A key limitation is the reliance on cell-type annotations; the authors outline fallback strategies (pseudo-labeling, K-Means) but the generalization gap when annotations are absent remains unquantified. Future work could extend the multiplex framework to incorporate additional modalities (genomic, clinical) as additional network layers, and validate the approach on larger, more diverse mIF cohorts.
