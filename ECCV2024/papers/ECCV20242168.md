## Problem

In weakly-supervised semantic segmentation, conventional seed propagation methods (e.g., Random Walk) cause spatially minor classes to vanish when adjacent to larger classes, because the propagation process ignores class ratios. This vanishing problem is severe: adjacent regions constitute 35% of VOC and 75% of COCO total area, with the majority being inter-class boundaries. No prior WSS method explicitly addresses this by leveraging both unsupervised and weakly-supervised features in a complementary, hierarchical manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242168/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with image-level class labels (no pixel annotations) and operates on pre-propagated WSS pseudo masks (e.g., from MARS) during the segmentation training phase rather than replacing the initial propagation. It additionally requires unsupervised feature maps (from STEGO or CAUSE, trained from scratch on the target dataset) and weakly-supervised feature maps from the WSS encoder. The output is a refined pixel-wise segmentation mask (C×H×W) that is fed back recursively to retrain the WSS encoder-decoder, progressively improving pseudo mask quality across iterations.

## Method

DHR performs a three-step hierarchical rebalancing: (1) Optimal Transport (OT)-based seed initialization recovers vanished minor-class regions by minimizing false positives in CAMs via an entropy-regularized transport matrix; (2) USS feature-based rebalancing computes class-level average pooling (CAP) centroids from unsupervised features and clusters pixels around them to separate inter-class regions (e.g., vehicle vs. animal); (3) WSS feature-based rebalancing applies the same CAP-clustering with weakly-supervised features to further separate intra-class regions (e.g., car vs. bus), but only conditionally—gated by a pruning operator that activates WSS rebalancing solely where USS centroids exceed a similarity threshold τ, ensuring WSS is used precisely where USS cannot distinguish classes. The final mask is refined with a boundary correction tool (CRF) and used to recursively train the segmentation network with combined classification and per-pixel cross-entropy losses. The key insight is that USS features excel at separating visually dissimilar inter-class regions while WSS features excel at separating visually similar intra-class regions, and their sequential, conditional combination yields a synergistic +6.0% mIoU gain on COCO over either feature alone.

## Results

DHR achieves state-of-the-art mIoU of 79.8% on PASCAL VOC 2012 test (DeepLabv3+ backbone) and 82.3% (Mask2Former backbone), surpassing the previous best WSS method MARS by 2.6% and 4.6% respectively. Across five benchmarks, DHR (DeepLabv3+) reports 53.9% (COCO), 49.0% (Context), 32.9% (ADE), and 37.4% (Stuff) mIoU, while the Mask2Former variant reaches 56.8%, 53.6%, 36.9%, and 41.1%. On VOC validation, DHR closes the gap with the fully supervised upper bound (DeepLabv3+) by 84%, and the Mask2Former variant narrows the FSS gap to just 3.7 mIoU points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DHR outperforms all 28 compared baselines across all five benchmarks using only image-level supervision. Against the closest WSS competitor MARS [27], DHR (DeepLabv3+) gains +1.9% on VOC val, +4.5% on COCO, +9.2% on Context, and +10.9% on ADE, with the largest relative improvements on datasets dominated by adjacent classes (Context, ADE). The Mask2Former variant extends these margins to +4.6% (VOC), +5.1% (COCO), +13.8% (Context), and +14.9% (ADE) over MARS. DHR also substantially surpasses open-vocabulary models: it exceeds Grounded SAM [48] by 33.3% on VOC val and 18.2% on COCO, and outperforms Ferret [61] by 24.9% on VOC val. Among methods using additional supervision (text, SAM, LLM, external datasets), DHR with only image-level labels still beats Sun et al. [54] (I+S+D) by 2.4% on VOC val and FMA-WSSS [60] (I+S+C) by 2.3%, demonstrating that the dual-feature hierarchical rebalancing is more effective than incorporating external model knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

DHR extends the WSS propagation pipeline by introducing a hierarchical dual-feature mechanism that no prior method has combined. It builds directly on MARS (ICCV 2023), which first brought unsupervised features (STEGO) into WSS but only for binary foreground/background separation, and on the Optimal Transport framework as a unifying rebalancing operator. The key departure from prior work is the sequential application of USS features for inter-class segregation followed by WSS features for intra-class distinction, guided by a correlation-based pruning threshold—whereas all previous WSS methods (ToCo, ACR, WeakTr, etc.) rely solely on WSS features for propagation, and MARS uses USS features only in a coarse binary manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By closing the WSS-to-FSS gap to just 0.8% on VOC (79.8% vs. 80.6%) and achieving strong results across five benchmarks without external models (CLIP, SAM, LLMs), DHR demonstrates that carefully combining self-supervised and weakly-supervised features can nearly eliminate the need for pixel-level annotations in standard segmentation. The model-agnostic design (validated across four WSS backbones and two USS methods) makes it a practical drop-in improvement for existing WSS pipelines. Open questions include whether the hierarchical OT-based rebalancing scales to open-vocabulary or video segmentation settings, and whether the correlation threshold τ can be learned rather than manually tuned.
