## Problem

When adapting pre-trained Vision-Language Models (e.g., CLIP) to new tasks in Domain-Class Incremental Learning, existing prompt-tuning methods cause information interference in the attention mechanism that degrades the model's zero-shot generalization on unseen distributions (forward forgetting). Prior solutions either require heavy computation via knowledge distillation on large external datasets or accept significant zero-shot ability loss, leaving a gap for a parameter-efficient approach that retains pre-trained knowledge without external data.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder, and produces class labels via cosine similarity between the image embedding and text embeddings of candidate class names. In the DCIL setting, both the domain distribution and the set of target classes change across sequentially arriving tasks (11 datasets, 1201 total classes on MTIL), and the model must simultaneously maintain high accuracy on learned tasks and preserve zero-shot transfer to unseen distributions. The few-shot variant (MTIL-FS) uses only 16 samples per class to simulate data-deficient scenarios.

## Method

DIKI replaces prompt prepending with a fully residual attention branch: for each task, learnable keys K_r and values V_r are trained to compute a separate cross-attention output O_r = softmax(Q_e K_r^T / √d) V_r that is added to the frozen backbone's self-attention output, leaving the original attention computation untouched. Zero-initialization of V_r ensures the residual branch is an identity at initialization, injecting no noise before any task-specific knowledge is learned. On top of this residual property, a distribution-aware integration calibration mechanism maintains a multivariate Gaussian per task (estimated from frozen encoder features) and computes a log-likelihood score for each test sample; the maximum score is passed through a sigmoid to scale the residual output, automatically reducing the influence of newly learned knowledge on samples from unseen distributions and thereby preserving zero-shot ability.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (11 datasets, 1201 classes), DIKI achieves 68.7% Transfer, 76.3% Avg., and 85.1% Last accuracy using only 1.8M trainable parameters (0.86% of the CLIP ViT-B/16 model), surpassing the previous SOTA ZSCL (68.1/75.4/83.6) which requires 211M parameters and 100k reference images for distillation. On the 16-shot MTIL-FS few-shot benchmark, DIKI further widens the gap, reaching 73.1% average accuracy versus 70.5% for ZSCL. Training completes in 2.3 hours on a single GPU with 24 GB memory, compared to 11.3 hours on 4 GPUs (96 GB) for ZSCL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIKI outperforms all nine baselines on all three metrics (Transfer, Avg., Last) on the MTIL benchmark. Against the previous SOTA ZSCL [91], DIKI gains +0.6% Transfer, +0.9% Avg., and +1.5% Last while using 99.1% fewer parameters (1.8M vs. 211M) and eliminating the need for 100k reference images and knowledge distillation. Compared to parameter-efficient prompt-tuning methods (L2P, DualPrompt, S-Prompts), DIKI achieves a dramatic +15.5 to +16.5% improvement on Transfer (zero-shot retention), confirming that the interference-free residual attention design preserves pre-trained knowledge far better than prepending-based prompts. On the 16-shot MTIL-FS benchmark, DIKI's advantage over ZSCL widens to +2.6% average accuracy, indicating greater robustness under data scarcity. In the CIL ablation (Table 4), replacing the prepending mechanism with IKI in L2P, DualPrompt, and CODA-P consistently improves average accuracy by 0.7–2.3% and reduces forgetting by 0.1–1.1 points, validating the generality of the interference-free injection strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the task-specific prompt pool and query-key retrieval framework established by L2P and DualPrompt, but departs fundamentally from their prepending mechanism. Drawing on LoRA's approach of modifying self-attention parameters, the authors replace prompt prepending with a fully residual attention branch (learnable keys and values added in parallel to the frozen backbone), combined with zero-initialization to ensure the residual path is an identity at initialization. The distribution-aware calibration mechanism, which uses per-task Gaussian feature distributions to gate the residual output, is a novel addition enabled by the residual property that has no direct analogue in prior prompt-tuning or PEFT continual learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies 'forward forgetting'—degradation of zero-shot generalization after continual fine-tuning—as a distinct and under-addressed failure mode in VLM-based continual learning, separate from the backward forgetting that dominates the CL literature. The practical impact is significant: matching or exceeding a 211M-parameter distillation-based method (ZSCL) with only 1.8M trainable parameters, no external reference data, and roughly 5× less training time makes VLM continual learning feasible on a single consumer GPU. Open questions include whether the residual-attention-plus-calibration recipe generalizes beyond CLIP ViT-B/16 to larger or different VLM architectures, and whether the Gaussian distribution assumption for calibration holds under more extreme or multi-modal distribution shifts.
