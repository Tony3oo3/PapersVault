## Problem

Autonomous systems must reason about occluded scene content (e.g., whether a vehicle exists behind a building) to plan safely, yet existing approaches either reduce rich sensor data to low-dimensional object states or apply NeRF-based 3D representations only to simple, deterministic, fully-observed scenes. No prior method performs stochastic 3D scene forecasting in a POMDP that simultaneously models state uncertainty (object existence) and dynamics uncertainty (object motion) from partially observable ego-centric views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242382/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single ego-centric RGB image (potentially with occluded regions) along with its camera pose, encoded by a convolutional ViT-based encoder into a Gaussian latent distribution. It produces a latent-conditioned NeRF (Instant-NGP with hash and occupancy grids) that renders novel views from arbitrary camera angles, a predicted mixture-of-Gaussians distribution over future scene latents, and downstream planning actions (e.g., merge or halt) derived from sampling and probing the 3D belief.

## Method

CARFF employs a two-stage training pipeline to stabilize the otherwise non-convex joint optimization of a VAE and NeRF. Stage 1 trains a Pose-Conditional VAE (PC-VAE) where a convolutional ViT encoder maps images to Gaussian latents N(μ, σ²) and a pose-conditional convolutional decoder reconstructs images from randomly sampled camera poses, enforcing view-invariance; a delayed linear KL-weight schedule balances reconstruction fidelity against probabilistic expressiveness. Stage 2 freezes the encoder and trains a latent-conditioned Instant-NGP NeRF (hash grid + occupancy grid + small MLP F_θ(z)) to synthesize 3D scenes from sampled latents. For temporal forecasting, a Mixture Density Network with multi-headed MLPs predicts a mixture of K Gaussians for the next timestamp's posterior, conditioned on the current posterior; at inference, latents are sampled auto-regressively, the NeRF is probed for object localization, and the result is fed back to the encoder to roll out multi-step predictions, enabling a sampling-based controller to select actions under uncertainty.

## Results

On the CARLA driving datasets, CARFF achieves PSNR of 28.00–28.26 for correctly predicted scene evolutions (matching pairs) versus 22.74–24.56 for incorrect correspondences, confirming accurate 3D scene forecasting across all timestamps. For downstream planning, the CARFF-based controller with n=10 samples achieves a perfect 30/30 success rate in both the Multi-Scene Approaching Intersection and Multi-Scene Two Lane Merge scenarios, outperforming underconfident (0/30 in collision-avoidance) and overconfident (0/30 in advance-when-clear) baselines. The model also achieves high accuracy and recall curves across 1–50 samples, demonstrating robust probabilistic belief modeling under partial observation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper provides only qualitative comparisons, explicitly noting that no existing method aligns closely enough for quantitative benchmarks. NeRF-VAE (Kosiorek et al., ICML 2021), the closest prior method in objective, collapses to black when applied to CARLA driving datasets, failing to handle complex visual information. Li et al. (CoRL 2022) and Adamkiewicz et al. (RA-L 2022) operate in simple, static scenes with deterministic dynamics and do not model state or dynamics uncertainty. Packer et al. (CoRL 2022) handles planning under perceptual uncertainty but operates in 2D rather than 3D. CARFF is the only method in the comparison that simultaneously handles complex multi-agent scenarios, state uncertainty (object existence), dynamics uncertainty (unknown movements), probabilistic 3D forecasting, and downstream planning in a unified NeRF-based framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARFF builds directly on NeRF-VAE (Kosiorek et al., ICML 2021), which first demonstrated that a global latent representation can condition a single NeRF to represent multiple scenes, but which the authors show collapses to black on complex driving data. The key architectural departure is a two-stage training pipeline—first learning a Pose-Conditional VAE in pixel space, then replacing the decoder with a latent-conditioned Instant-NGP NeRF—motivated by the non-convex, inter-dependent optimization that destabilizes joint VAE-NeRF training. For temporal forecasting, the paper extends the latent space with a Mixture Density Network that predicts future posteriors as Gaussian mixtures, framing the problem as a POMDP over belief states, a formulation borrowed from the active visual planning literature (Packer et al., CoRL 2022) but transplanted into a 3D radiance-field representation for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARFF offers a principled way to reason about what might exist behind occlusions in 3D, producing explainable, multi-modal scene beliefs that can be directly queried for planning (density probing, segmentation) rather than reduced to 2D bounding boxes. However, the method requires per-scene optimization of the NeRF and relies on posed images of known environments, limiting immediate deployment to novel, unstructured settings. Open questions include whether the two-stage training generalizes to truly dynamic, multi-actor scenes with large state spaces, whether the MDN forecasting can scale beyond the small latent dimensions tested here, and whether the approach transfers to real-world sensor data with the noise and domain shift that CARLA does not fully capture.
