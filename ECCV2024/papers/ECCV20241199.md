## Problem

Self-training room layout estimation on unseen scenes requires reliable pseudo-labels from multiple noisy model estimates, but the only existing multi-view layout consistency method (360-MLC) performs aggregation in the image domain without any geometry reasoning, treating all estimates equally and producing noisy pseudo-labels that degrade with more data—especially for occluded walls. This paper addresses the gap by introducing a geometry-aware aggregation that explicitly reasons about 3D spatial structure to produce cleaner pseudo-labels for complex and occluded room geometries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241199/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of n panoramic images (H×W) with known SE(3) camera poses from an unseen scene, along with a pre-trained layout model (HorizonNet or LGTNet). It produces per-view pseudo-labels as 3×W floor-boundary vectors in world coordinates, computed via iterative ray-casting in the bird-eye-view (xz plane) with W ray directions. The pseudo-labels are then used to fine-tune the layout model over 15 epochs, and the final output is the refined room layout geometry (boundary in Euclidean space) evaluated by 2D/3D IoU, RMS, and δ₁ metrics.

## Method

The core mechanism is a multi-cycle ray-casting process: multiple noisy layout estimates from all camera views are projected onto W ray directions in the BEV xz-plane, filtered iteratively (m=15 cycles) by taking the median of nearby samples (within δ_r along-ray and δ_n along-normal thresholds) to suppress noise, and finally sampled using min() to select the closest point on each ray—exploiting the principle that non-occluded geometry must be the nearest surface along a viewing direction. This geometry-aware aggregation replaces 360-MLC's image-domain median sampling and naturally handles occluded and non-planar walls without Manhattan World assumptions. A complementary Weighted Distance Loss (L_WD) multiplies the L1 loss by an exponential weight ω_i = exp(κ(|ȳ_i| − d_min))/σ_i² that prioritizes farthest geometries, addressing the known weakness of pre-trained models on distant walls. The entire pipeline requires no human annotations, only unlabeled panoramic images with camera poses from the target scene.

## Results

Using the HorizonNet backbone with 100% of training data, the proposed method achieves 82.99% 2D IoU and 78.95% 3D IoU on HM3D-MVL, 81.65% 2D IoU on MP3D-FPE, and 75.71% 2D IoU on ZInD, consistently outperforming the 360-MLC baseline. The most striking gains appear on the Occlusion Subset, where the method reaches 83.01% 2D IoU on HM3D-MVL versus 79.19% for 360-MLC (a 3.8-point improvement), while 360-MLC actually degrades from 81.66% (10%) to 79.19% (100%) on that subset. With the stronger LGTNet backbone, the method reaches 86.49% 2D IoU and 81.90% 3D IoU on HM3D-MVL, approaching the fully supervised upper bound of 87.64% 2D IoU. All results are medians over 10 self-training runs of 15 epochs each.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is 360-MLC [18], the only prior self-training method exploiting multi-view layout consistency without ground-truth annotations. The proposed method outperforms 360-MLC on every dataset and every metric in both the HorizonNet and LGTNet backbone experiments. The gap is most pronounced on the Occlusion Subset: on HM3D-MVL with 100% data, the method achieves 83.01% 2D IoU versus 79.19% for 360-MLC (+3.8 pts), and on MP3D-FPE it reaches 85.71% versus 84.27% (+1.4 pts). Notably, 360-MLC's occlusion-subset performance *degrades* when scaling from 10% to 100% data on HM3D-MVL (81.66% → 79.19%), indicating its image-domain median aggregation accumulates noise, whereas the proposed ray-casting approach improves monotonically. With the LGTNet backbone, the advantage widens further (86.49% vs. 84.07% 2D IoU on HM3D-MVL), and 360-MLC fails entirely on ZInD due to its inability to handle few-frame scenes with horizon-depth constraints. The fully supervised upper bounds (84.87% for HorizonNet, 87.64% for LGTNet on HM3D-MVL) remain above the self-trained results, confirming a residual gap that the authors identify as future work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 360-MLC [18], the only prior self-training framework for room layout estimation that exploits multi-view layout consistency without human annotations. The key conceptual departure is moving pseudo-label aggregation from the image domain (where 360-MLC samples median re-projected points) to a geometry-aware ray-casting formulation in the bird-eye-view Euclidean plane, enabling explicit handling of occlusions and non-planar walls. The approach also draws on the multi-view layout estimation literature (CoVisPose, Graph-Covis, GPR-Net), which defines geometry reasoning concepts like occlusion and correspondence but relies on ground-truth annotations, and on layout model backbones (HorizonNet, LGTNet) that parameterize geometry in 1D boundary or horizon-depth form. The weighted distance loss extends the standard uncertainty-weighted L1 loss used in 360-MLC by adding an exponential distance prior that prioritizes far-wall estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in room layout estimation: the need for manual annotation in unseen environments. A notable finding is that 360-MLC's performance actually degrades with more data on occluded geometries (e.g., 81.66%→79.19% 2D IoU on HM3D-MVL, and 80.22%→71.29% with LGTNet), revealing that noisy image-domain pseudo-labels are a fundamental limitation rather than a data-scaling issue. The ray-casting formulation handles circular and non-planar walls without Manhattan World assumptions, broadening applicability beyond the rectilinear rooms that dominate existing datasets. However, a gap to fully supervised performance remains (e.g., 82.99% vs. 84.87% 2D IoU on HM3D-MVL), and the method requires multiple views with known camera poses, limiting single-image deployment.
