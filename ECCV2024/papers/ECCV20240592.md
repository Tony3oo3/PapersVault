## Problem

SAM, pre-trained on natural images, suffers significant performance degradation on breast ultrasound segmentation due to low contrast, speckle noise, and blurred mass boundaries. Existing SAM adaptation methods (e.g., SAMUS, SAMed) primarily enhance spatial features but neglect frequency-domain information that is critical for delineating edges and textures in ultrasound images, and they do not address SAM's sensitivity to prompt quality, which causes unstable predictions with false positives and false negatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240592/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a breast ultrasound image resized to 256×256 pixels and a single random point prompt as input, and produces a binary segmentation mask of the breast mass. During inference, the DFC module internally generates 10 additional perturbed point prompts (shifted within a radius R = min(H,W)/5 around the initial prompt) to produce 11 predictions, from which an entropy-based uncertainty map is computed to refine the final mask. The prompt encoder and mask decoder parameters remain frozen throughout training.

## Method

SFRecSAM inherits SAM's architecture with all original parameters frozen for the prompt encoder and mask decoder, while the image encoder is fine-tuned. The Spatial-Frequency Feature Fusion (SFF) module applies Haar wavelet transform to decompose the input into low-frequency and three high-frequency components, then extracts features from both the original image and the combined high-frequency image via a multi-scale convolution block (point conv, 3×3 conv, and dilated 3×3 conv with dilation rate 2); the resulting fused features are concatenated and used as keys/values in a cross-attention mechanism where ViT encoder features serve as queries, injecting fine-grained edge and texture information into the global representation. The Dual False Corrector (DFC) perturbs the initial point prompt to generate 10 additional prompts, computes a combined prediction and its binary entropy as an aleatoric uncertainty map, thresholds at 50% of the uncertainty range to identify high-uncertainty pixels, and then corrects false negatives (high-uncertainty pixels outside the mask whose intensity matches the target region mean) and false positives (high-uncertainty pixels inside the mask whose intensity matches the background mean) to produce the final mask. Training uses AdamW with learning rate 5e-4, batch size 4, and 400 epochs on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Results

On BUSI, SFRecSAM achieves mDice 87.14%, mIoU 78.58%, and mHD 4.66, while on UDIAT it reaches mDice 91.80%, mIoU 85.14%, and mHD 3.92. These results represent a 31.66% and 11.40% mDice improvement over the un-fine-tuned SAM (55.48% and 80.40%) and a 1.52% and 1.34% mDice gain over the strongest SAM-based baseline SAMUS (85.62% and 90.46%). The model also outperforms the best task-specific method MNFE-Net by 1.65% mDice on BUSI and 0.41% on UDIAT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFRecSAM outperforms all ten baselines on every metric (mDice, mIoU, mHD) across both BUSI and UDIAT. Among SAM-based methods, it beats the closest competitor SAMUS by 1.52% mDice on BUSI (87.14 vs 85.62) and 1.34% on UDIAT (91.80 vs 90.46), and surpasses BUSSAM by 0.46% and 0.87% mDice respectively. Against task-specific models, it exceeds MNFE-Net (the strongest CNN-based baseline) by 1.65% mDice on BUSI and 0.41% on UDIAT, and leads TransUNet, HiFormer, and H2Former by 4.9–5.7% mDice on BUSI. The un-fine-tuned SAM shows severe degradation (55.48% mDice on BUSI), while even the weakest fine-tuned SAM variant (SAMed, 73.02%) is still 14.12% below SFRecSAM, highlighting the importance of the proposed spatial-frequency fusion and dual false correction modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM adaptation pipeline for medical imaging by addressing two gaps left by prior work: (1) most SAM fine-tuning methods (SAMed, MSA, SAMUS, BUSSAM) augment only spatial features via CNN branches, neglecting frequency-domain information that is critical for delineating low-contrast ultrasound boundaries; (2) prior uncertainty-based correction (FNPC) relies on bounding-box prompts that are impractical in clinical settings. SFRecSAM introduces a Haar-wavelet-based spatial-frequency fusion module to complement SAM's ViT encoder with high-frequency edge/texture cues, and a dual false corrector that uses point-prompt augmentation and entropy-based uncertainty to rectify FP/FN regions without requiring bounding-box inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that incorporating frequency-domain features into SAM's image encoder yields meaningful gains on low-contrast ultrasound segmentation, and that point-prompt-based uncertainty correction is a clinically deployable alternative to bounding-box correction. However, the improvements over the strongest baselines (SAMUS, BUSSAM) are modest (~1.3–1.5% mDice), and the method is validated only on two breast ultrasound datasets with a single wavelet base (Haar). Open questions include whether the spatial-frequency fusion generalizes to other ultrasound modalities (abdominal, cardiac) or non-ultrasound medical imaging, and whether the DFC's intensity-similarity assumption holds when lesions and artifacts share similar grayscale distributions, as acknowledged in the failure-case analysis.
