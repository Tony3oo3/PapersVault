## Problem

Self-supervised scene flow estimation for autonomous driving suffers from two principal challenges: the extreme imbalance between static (~86%) and dynamic points causes overly conservative zero-flow predictions, and the lack of object-level motion constraints leads to inconsistent flow within rigid objects (e.g., a truck partially predicted as static). Existing methods either rely on expensive ground-truth annotations or use large amounts of training data to compensate, without explicitly addressing these structural issues.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two consecutive LiDAR point clouds (80k–177k points per frame after ground removal via HD maps) and the ego vehicle SE(3) transformation between frames. The predicted flow is decomposed into a known ego-motion component derived directly from the SE(3) transform plus a network-predicted residual, so the network only needs to learn the relative motion. Inference runs at ~48 ms per frame (10 Hz), meeting real-time onboard requirements.

## Method

SeFlow integrates DUFOMap's ray-casting-based dynamic/static point classification into a self-supervised training pipeline built on DeFlow's GRU-based iterative refinement decoder over voxelized point clouds. The key contribution is a four-term loss: standard Chamfer distance, a dynamic-only Chamfer distance that isolates motion supervision from the static majority, a static zero-flow constraint that penalizes non-zero flow on classified-static points, and a dynamic cluster flow consistency loss. The last term clusters dynamic points with HDBSCAN and drives all points within a cluster toward an upper-bound flow vector computed as the maximum inter-frame nearest-neighbor displacement in that cluster, explicitly correcting the underestimation that nearest-neighbor matching produces on geometrically featureless object surfaces. This design requires no ground-truth flow labels and achieves state-of-the-art self-supervised EPE on Argoverse 2 and Waymo while running in real time.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 test set, SeFlow achieves a 3-way EPE of 0.0628 m, the best among all self-supervised methods, outperforming ZeroFlow (0.0814 m) by 23% and NSFP (0.0685 m) by 8%. On the Waymo validation set, SeFlow reaches 0.0598 m 3-way EPE, beating ZeroFlow (0.0921 m) by 35% and NSFP (0.1005 m) by 40%. SeFlow runs at 48±4 ms per frame on Argoverse 2 and 42±4 ms on Waymo, making it real-time capable (10 Hz), while NSFP requires ~32 s and FastNSF ~0.5 s per frame. Notably, SeFlow outperforms the supervised FastFlow3D (0.0782 m on Argoverse 2) and approaches the supervised DeFlow (0.0534 m) without any ground-truth flow labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeFlow outperforms all four self-supervised baselines on both Argoverse 2 and Waymo. Against ZeroFlow (the strongest real-time self-supervised baseline), SeFlow reduces 3-way EPE from 0.0814 to 0.0628 m on Argoverse 2 (−23%) and from 0.0921 to 0.0598 m on Waymo (−35%), while also improving FD EPE by 28% and 30% respectively. Against NSFP, which achieves competitive accuracy but requires ~32 s per frame, SeFlow is over 600× faster and still achieves lower 3-way EPE on Argoverse 2 (0.0628 vs 0.0685). FastNSF, the fastest self-supervised method after ZeroFlow, is the weakest in accuracy (0.1657 m on Argoverse 2), and SeFlow improves upon it by 62%. Among supervised methods, SeFlow beats FastFlow3D (0.0782 m) on Argoverse 2 and (0.0782 m) on Waymo, and narrows the gap to DeFlow (0.0534 m / 0.0446 m) to within 16% / 34% on 3-way EPE, all without any ground-truth flow annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeFlow builds on the DeFlow [38] GRU-based iterative refinement decoder as its architectural backbone and incorporates the DUFOMap [9] ray-casting framework from SLAM to classify points as dynamic or static. It departs from prior self-supervised scene flow methods (NSFP, FastNSF, ZeroFlow) that rely exclusively on Chamfer distance or knowledge distillation by introducing targeted loss functions conditioned on the dynamic/static classification. The key conceptual departure is treating the static/dynamic split not merely as a data property but as a structural signal that enables separate, motion-pattern-specific supervision: zero-flow enforcement for static points, dynamic-only Chamfer for moving points, and cluster-level flow consistency for object-level coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SeFlow demonstrates that a simple ray-casting classification borrowed from SLAM, combined with carefully designed loss terms, can match or exceed supervised methods on scene flow while remaining fully self-supervised and real-time capable (48 ms/frame). The data-efficiency result—outperforming ZeroFlow trained on 100–200% of the data using only 20–50%—suggests that targeted supervision is more valuable than raw data volume for this task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to sparser or multi-modal sensor inputs, how the fixed loss weights should be tuned, and whether temporal coherence can be integrated without sacrificing the single-frame efficiency that makes the method practical for onboard deployment.
