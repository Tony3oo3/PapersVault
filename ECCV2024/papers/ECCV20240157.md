## Problem

Existing talking head generation methods produce facial dynamics holistically, lacking fine-grained independent control over mouth shape, head pose, and emotional expression. Prior disentanglement approaches rely heavily on external priors (3DMM coefficients, audio contrastive learning), fail to enforce inter-space independence leading to incomplete decoupling, and require expensive full-network retraining when adding new disentangled components. Additionally, most methods support only a single driving modality (audio or video), limiting multimodal applicability.

## Input / Output

In video-driven mode (EDTalk-V), the method takes a 256×256 identity image plus separate driving frames for mouth, pose, and expression; in audio-driven mode (EDTalk-A), it takes an identity image, an audio clip, and optionally a text transcript. All frames are cropped following FOMM conventions. The output is a photorealistic talking head video where each facial component is independently controllable, with latent codes of dimension 512 and bank sizes of 20 (mouth), 6 (pose), and 10 (expression) orthogonal bases.

## Method

EDTalk decomposes facial dynamics into three component-aware latent spaces, each parameterized by a set of learnable bases stored in dedicated banks, with orthogonality enforced both intra-space and inter-space to guarantee complete disentanglement. A two-stage efficient training strategy allocates motion responsibilities without external priors: Stage 1 (Mouth-Pose Decoupling) uses cross-reconstruction with switched mouth regions to separate mouth and pose, while Stage 2 (Expression Decoupling) uses self-reconstruction complementary learning where the expression module must recover residual information not captured by the frozen mouth and pose modules. Only the lightweight Component-aware Latent Navigation (CLN) modules are trained in each stage, keeping the encoder and generator frozen for efficiency. For audio-driven generation, an Audio-to-Motion module predicts bank weights: a linear layer decodes lip-sync weights from HuBERT audio features, a Normalizing Flow generates probabilistic pose weights conditioned on audio rhythm, and a combined HuBERT + EmoBERTa encoder with random modality masking produces semantically-aware expression weights.

## Results

On MEAD, EDTalk-V achieves the best PSNR (22.771), SSIM (0.769), M-LMD (1.102), F-LMD (1.060), FID (15.548), and Acc_emo (68.85%), while EDTalk-A leads on PSNR (21.628), SSIM (0.722), M-LMD (1.537), F-LMD (1.290), FID (17.698), and Acc_emo (67.32%). On HDTF, EDTalk-V reaches PSNR 26.504, SSIM 0.845, FID 13.172, and EDTalk-A reaches PSNR 25.156, SSIM 0.811, FID 13.785 — both outperforming all baselines on these metrics. The only metric where EDTalk does not lead is SyncNet confidence, where Wav2Lip scores higher (9.032 vs. 7.642 on HDTF), which the authors attribute to Wav2Lip's pretraining on a large audio-visual dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDTalk-V (video-driven) achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, M-LMD, F-LMD, FID, and Acc_emo on both MEAD and HDTF, outperforming the strongest emotional baselines PD-FGC (PSNR 21.520, FID 30.240, Acc_emo 44.86) and EAT (PSNR 20.007, FID 21.465, Acc_emo 64.40) by substantial margins. EDTalk-A (audio-driven) similarly leads on all image-quality and emotion metrics, surpassing Wav2Lip (PSNR 19.875, FID 44.510) and SadTalker (PSNR 19.042, FID 39.308) on MEAD. The sole exception is SyncNet confidence, where Wav2Lip scores 8.774 (MEAD) and 9.032 (HDTF) versus EDTalk-A's 8.115 and 7.642, respectively; the authors note this stems from Wav2Lip's large-scale SyncNet pretraining biasing it toward lip-sync at the cost of visual quality (blurry mouths, higher M-LMD). Among emotional methods, StyleTalk achieves a competitive Acc_emo of 63.49 but trails EDTalk-A by ~4 points while also showing worse FID (24.774 vs. 17.698) and SSIM (0.714 vs. 0.722). Overall, EDTalk dominates on reconstruction quality, lip accuracy, and emotional fidelity simultaneously, whereas prior methods trade off among these objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDTalk builds on the canonical-feature motion transfer paradigm from FOMM and the learnable motion-basis representation from LIA, repurposing these ideas to decompose facial dynamics into three orthogonal sub-spaces (mouth, pose, expression) rather than a single holistic motion vector. It departs from prior disentanglement methods (PC-AVS, PD-FGC, DPE, TH-PAD) that rely on external priors—audio contrastive learning, 3DMM coefficients, or multi-generator architectures—by enforcing inter-space orthogonality on learnable bases and using a progressive, lightweight training strategy that freezes heavy modules between stages. The probabilistic pose generation via normalizing flows and the emotion inference from HuBERT/Emoberta embeddings extend the audio-driven talking head literature (Wav2Lip, SadTalker, EAMM) by treating head motion as a distribution and deriving expressions from audio tone and text rather than explicit visual references.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant contribution is the dramatic reduction in disentanglement training cost (~1 hour on 2 GPUs with 15.8 h of video versus multi-day, multi-GPU training with hundreds of hours for competitors), which lowers the barrier for researchers and practitioners to experiment with fine-grained facial control. The unified framework that handles both video-driven and audio-driven generation in a single model, with emotion inferred automatically from audio and transcript, addresses a real deployment gap in current talking-head pipelines. Open questions include whether the orthogonal-base approach scales to higher resolutions or longer sequences, whether the 256×256 evaluation generalizes to production-quality outputs, and how the method performs on highly diverse or non-frontal identities beyond the tested datasets.
