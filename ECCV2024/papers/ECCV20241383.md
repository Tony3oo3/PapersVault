## Problem

The first diffusion-based input-updating TTA method (DDA) restores corrupted test images via pixel-space DDPM, but its ~20 s/sample runtime and high memory footprint make it impractical for real-world TTA where inference overhead must be minimal. Additionally, pre-trained instruction-based editors like InstructPix2Pix cannot generalize to out-of-domain corrupted inputs, as they were only trained on clean-to-clean editing pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241383/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 corrupted RGB image and a single fixed text instruction ('Clean the image') as joint conditioning, requiring no per-corruption prompt engineering. It outputs a cleaned image in the same latent space, which is decoded back to pixel space; the final class prediction is obtained by averaging the softmax probabilities of the classifier on both the original corrupted image and the edited image (Eq. 10). For video TTA, Text2Video-Zero cross-frame attention is applied to maintain temporal consistency across edited frames.

## Method

Decorruptor fine-tunes an InstructPix2Pix U-Net (initialized from Stable Diffusion v1.5) on ImageNet using a novel corruption modeling scheme: (clean, corrupted) pairs are generated by applying PIXMIX fractal mixing combined with SimSiam transforms, and the model is trained to map corrupted→clean under the universal 'Clean the image' prompt, expanding the diffusion manifold to cover corrupted inputs. At inference, Decorruptor-DPM uses 20 DDIM steps in latent space with a multi-modal classifier-free guidance that schedules the image guidance scale ω_I from 1.8 to 0 (sqrt-scheduling) while keeping text guidance ω_T fixed. Decorruptor-CM further distills the DPM via latent consistency distillation, introducing learnable multi-modal guidance (ω_I · ω_T) through Fourier embeddings with zero-parameter initialization, enabling 4-step (or even 1-step) inference. At test time, multiple edited samples can be ensembled (e.g., 4× or 8×) to boost accuracy beyond the DPM variant while remaining ~100× faster than DDA.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5), 8×Decorruptor-CM achieves 34.2% average accuracy with ResNet-50, 41.1% with Swin-T, and 49.8% with ConvNeXt-B, outperforming DDA (29.7%, 40.0%, 49.4%) across all five architectures. On the more challenging ImageNet-C̄ benchmark, 4×Decorruptor-CM reaches 47.1% (ResNet-50), 55.8% (Swin-T), and 58.6% (ConvNeXt-T), surpassing DDA by 17.7%, 12.0%, and 12.3% respectively. In terms of efficiency, 4×Decorruptor-CM runs at 0.14 s/sample versus DDA's 19.5 s/sample (~100× faster) and MEMO's 0.41 s/sample (~3× faster), while consuming less GPU memory (4958+2383 MB vs. DDA's 10320+2340 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDA [14], the primary diffusion-based TTA baseline, Decorruptor wins on every architecture for both ImageNet-C and ImageNet-C̄ while running ~100× faster (0.14 s vs. 19.5 s per sample); on ImageNet-C̄ with ResNet-50 the gap is 17.7 percentage points (47.1% vs. 29.4%). Against MEMO [60], a model-updating TTA method using data augmentation, 4×Decorruptor-CM achieves higher accuracy (32.8% vs. 24.7% on ResNet-50 ImageNet-C) while being ~3× faster (0.14 s vs. 0.41 s). DiffPure [38], a diffusion-based adversarial purification method, is substantially outperformed on all benchmarks (e.g., 34.2% vs. 16.8% on ResNet-50 ImageNet-C) and is notably slower (27.3 s). For model-updating TTA methods TENT [54] and DeYO [28], Decorruptor-DPM is orthogonal and complementary: ensembling Decorruptor-edited predictions with TENT improves accuracy from 43.02% to 45.52%, and with DeYO from 48.61% to 49.50%, confirming that input-level and model-level updates are additive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DDA (Gao et al., 2022), the first diffusion-based input-updating TTA method, by replacing its pixel-space DDPM backbone with a latent-space InstructPix2Pix model fine-tuned via a novel corruption modeling scheme that pairs clean images with augmented corrupted versions. The efficiency gains come from two architectural shifts: operating in latent space (following Stable Diffusion / LDM) and distilling to a consistency model (following LCM), reducing inference from 50 pixel-space NFEs to 4 latent-space NFEs. The corruption modeling scheme itself—using data augmentation (PIXMIX, SimSiam) to create training pairs that teach the diffusion model to 'clean' arbitrary corruptions—is a new application of augmentation principles to diffusion model robustification, distinct from prior image restoration work that requires known degradation parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making input-updating TTA roughly 100× faster than DDA and 3× faster than the model-updating baseline MEMO, this work removes the primary practical barrier to deploying diffusion-based TTA in real-world streaming settings, and extends the approach to video. The corruption modeling scheme offers a general recipe for robustifying instruction-based editing models against out-of-distribution inputs without requiring corruption-specific prompts or degradation matrices. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to non-ImageNet domains without retraining the corruption pairs, how it scales to more severe or compound corruptions, and whether the ensembling strategy (4×CM > DPM) can be further exploited with learned or adaptive ensemble weights.
