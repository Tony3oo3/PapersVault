## Problem

Continual panoptic segmentation requires models to absorb new classes incrementally without catastrophic forgetting, but existing methods fail to strike three critical balances: selectively preserving past-class knowledge while remaining adaptable to new information in distillation, maintaining a truly representative class distribution in limited-capacity replay sets, and handling incomplete annotations in replay samples without introducing classification bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241084/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at 640×640 (CPS/CIS) or 512×512 (CSS) alongside a fixed-size replay set of 300 samples drawn from prior steps. Each input produces N_q output segments via Mask2Former's bipartite matching, where each segment carries a class prediction over all seen classes and a binary spatial mask, jointly forming the panoptic segmentation. Pseudo-labels from the frozen previous model supplement past-class annotations on regular images before label assignment.

## Method

BalConpas builds on Mask2Former and introduces three mechanisms. First, past-class backtrace distillation uses bipartite matching results to identify which transformer decoder query channels correspond to past-class segments, then applies MSE distillation only on those specific channels across all decoder layers, leaving other channels free to adapt to new classes. Second, a class-proportional memory strategy selects replay samples via a greedy algorithm to match the cumulative segment-count distribution across all prior training sets, with a retention constraint (λ^t) preventing early-step classes from being diluted. Third, balanced anti-misguidance losses consist of a foreground-only cross-entropy on replay samples (avoiding misguidance from unannotated classes treated as background) paired with an upweighted 'no object' term on regular images that compensates for the resulting foreground bias.

## Results

On the ADE20K dataset for continual panoptic segmentation (CPS), BalConpas with replay (Ours-R) achieves an average Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 40.2, 39.7, and 37.4 across the 100-50 (2-step), 100-10 (6-step), and 100-5 (11-step) protocols, respectively, outperforming the best baseline CoMFormer by 1.4, 4.4, and 3.4 PQ points on the avg metric. For continual semantic segmentation (CSS), Ours-R reaches 47.7, 45.4, and 43.1 mIoU (avg) on the same three splits, surpassing CoMFormer by 6.5, 8.0, and 6.6 mIoU. Even without replay samples, BalConpas (Ours) already exceeds all baselines in CPS, with the 100-10 avg PQ reaching 38.8 vs. CoMFormer's 35.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BalConpas (Ours-R) outperforms all five baselines across every CPS protocol. Against the strongest baseline CoMFormer [3], the gains are most pronounced in the harder multi-step settings: +4.4 PQ (avg) on 100-10 and +3.4 PQ on 100-5, while the 2-step 100-50 gap is a more modest +1.4 PQ. MiB [4] and EWF [32], designed for CSS, collapse in CPS (0.0 PQ on incremental classes in 100-10 and 100-5) because they lack instance-level mechanisms. PLOP [14] and SSUL [5] retain some instance information via pseudo-labels but still trail Ours-R by 2.5–6.4 PQ (avg) depending on the protocol. In CSS (Table 2), BalConpas also dominates: Ours-R achieves 45.4 mIoU (avg) on 100-10 vs. 37.4 for CoMFormer and 44.5 for EWF, the only method that comes close, yet EWF still lags by 0.9 mIoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CoMFormer (CVPR 2023), the pioneering CPS method that introduced output-level distillation and pseudo-labeling for background shift, and draws on CSS techniques from PLOP, SSUL, and EWF. Its key conceptual departure is the 'backtrace' mechanism: rather than distilling entire features or outputs as prior methods do, it uses the bipartite-matching label assignment to identify which transformer decoder features correspond to past-class segments and distills only those, leaving other features free to adapt. The class-proportional memory strategy also departs from the equal-per-class replay used by SSUL and others, instead mirroring the cumulative segment-count distribution across all prior training sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CPS is a practically important but still young task, and BalConpas provides the first method to simultaneously achieve SOTA across CPS, CSS, and CIS on ADE20K, suggesting the three 'balances' are broadly applicable rather than task-specific. The selective distillation and segment-based class distribution ideas are architecture-agnostic in principle and could transfer to other segmentation backbones. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (ADE20K), a fixed 300-sample replay budget, and reliance on Mask2Former's bipartite matching for the backtrace step, which may not generalize trivially to other decoder designs. Open questions include scalability to very long class sequences, multi-dataset continual settings, and whether the backtrace distillation can be adapted to architectures without explicit query-to-segment assignment.
