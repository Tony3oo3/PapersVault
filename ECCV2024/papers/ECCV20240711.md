## Problem

Existing noisy-label learning methods rely on dataset-dependent prior knowledge (e.g., pre-defined thresholds or drop rates) for sample selection, limiting their adaptivity across different datasets. Additionally, these methods largely neglect class balance during both sample selection and re-weighting, causing the model to favor easier categories and producing biased performance on harder ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240711/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with potentially corrupted class labels (e.g., 32×32 CIFAR images or higher-resolution web-crawled images) and outputs a predicted class label. Training follows an SSL-like paradigm: the network processes both weakly- and strongly-augmented views of each sample, with clean samples trained on their original labels and noisy samples trained on mean-teacher-corrected pseudo-labels with adaptive per-sample weights.

## Method

SED integrates three components: (1) a self-adaptive and class-balanced sample selection (SCS) strategy that uses a global threshold (EMA of the mean predicted probability over all samples) combined with a class-specific local threshold (normalized per-class prediction expectation) to dynamically split the training set into clean and noisy subsets without any pre-defined hyperparameters; (2) a self-adaptive and class-balanced re-weighting (SCR) mechanism that corrects noisy labels via a mean-teacher model and assigns per-sample weights drawn from a dynamic truncated normal distribution whose class-specific mean and variance are updated via EMA, giving higher weight to samples with higher correction confidence; (3) a consistency regularization loss on clean samples using strongly-augmented views to further improve robustness. The final loss is the sum of the clean-subset classification loss, the re-weighted noisy-subset classification loss, and the consistency regularization term.

## Results

On CIFAR100N, SED achieves 66.50% (Sym-20%), 38.15% (Sym-80%), and 58.29% (Asym-40%) test accuracy, outperforming the strongest baselines by 6.22%, 2.92%, and 7.73% respectively. On the more challenging open-set CIFAR80N, SED reaches 69.10%, 42.57%, and 60.87%, with margins up to 10.57% over the best competitor. On real-world datasets (Web-Aircraft, Web-Bird, Web-Car), SED attains an average accuracy of 85.83%, surpassing the second-best method (DISC, 84.89%) by 0.94% while training only a single network without Mixup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SED outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every noise setting on both synthetic and real-world datasets. On CIFAR100N, the largest margins are over DISC (+6.22% on Sym-20%, +7.73% on Asym-40%) and NCE (+2.92% on Sym-80%); on CIFAR80N, SED leads NCE by up to 10.57% (Sym-20%) and 4.47% (Asym-40%). Among real-world results, SED beats DISC (84.89% avg) and UNICON (84.84% avg) by 0.94% and 0.99% respectively, while notably requiring only a single network without Mixup, unlike DISC and UNICON which use two networks or Mixup. Older methods such as Co-teaching, JoCoR, and DivideMix show substantially larger gaps (10–25% behind SED), particularly under high noise ratios where they nearly fail (e.g., Standard drops to 4.41% on CIFAR100N Sym-80% vs. SED's 38.15%). The paper also notes that SED's self-adaptive threshold design eliminates the need for dataset-specific hyperparameters (drop rates, pre-defined thresholds) that methods like Co-teaching, Jo-SRC, and DISC require.

## Relation to Prior Work

SED integrates three established paradigms for noisy label learning—sample selection, label correction via mean-teacher models (following DivideMix and Jo-SRC), and sample re-weighting—into a unified SSL-like framework. Its key departure from prior work is the replacement of dataset-dependent, pre-defined thresholds or drop rates with self-adaptive, class-specific thresholds (global EMA-based plus local class-normalized) for sample selection, and a truncated normal distribution for confidence-based re-weighting of corrected noisy samples. The consistency regularization on clean samples draws from semi-supervised learning (MixMatch, SoftMatch), while the EMA-based dynamic scheduling of thresholds and distribution parameters is the paper's primary methodological novelty, eliminating the need for hand-tuned hyperparameters that plague Co-teaching, JoCoR, and DivideMix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing dataset-dependent prior knowledge (pre-defined thresholds, drop rates, clean subsets), SED offers a more practical and generalizable approach to noisy label learning, particularly valuable for real-world datasets where noise rates and structures are unknown. The consistent class-balanced design addresses a previously underexplored source of bias in both sample selection and label correction. Open questions include whether the truncated normal distribution assumption holds under extreme or instance-dependent noise, whether the approach extends to non-CNN backbones or other modalities, and whether the EMA-based adaptation is optimal compared to learned scheduling. The method's reliance on a single network (no co-training or Mixup) is a practical advantage, but whether this simplicity comes at a cost under very high noise ratios remains to be explored.
