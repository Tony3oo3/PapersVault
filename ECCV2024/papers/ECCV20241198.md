## Problem

Existing real-time mesh-based rendering methods (e.g., MobileNeRF, NeRF2Mesh) struggle to faithfully model highly reflective surfaces because they parameterize view-dependent appearance by viewing direction and use tiny MLPs with limited capacity. Meanwhile, state-of-the-art reflective surface methods like Ref-NeRF rely on volume rendering and cannot achieve real-time performance, let alone on resource-constrained mobile devices. No prior method simultaneously delivers high-quality reflective appearance modeling and real-time rendering on edge hardware.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes multi-view RGB images and an initial coarse mesh (extracted via grid-based representation and simplified to ~75k faces). At inference, rendering requires only a refined mesh with learned vertex normals, two 4096×4096 baked texture images (diffuse color and view-independent specular feature), a 720×360 environment feature map (<1 MB), and camera parameters. The output is a novel-view RGB image rendered via WebGL at 60 FPS on smartphones, alongside the editable environment feature map that enables relighting and scene editing.

## Method

REFRAME decomposes color into diffuse and specular components, where the specular branch uses reflection-direction-based parameterization (rather than viewing direction) to model view-dependent appearance. A geometry learner with two multi-resolution hash encoding networks learns per-vertex position offsets and normal offsets from the initial coarse mesh, providing adaptive update step sizes and accurate surface normals without increasing face count. The key architectural insight is a neural environment learner: a 4-layer MLP maps the reflective direction ω_r to a 3-channel environment feature f_e during training, which is then baked into a 2D environment feature map at inference, so the tiny 2-layer inference MLP only combines f_s, f_e, and the Fresnel term to produce specular color. This decouples heavy representation learning (training) from lightweight lookup (inference), achieving 200+ FPS on GPU and 60 FPS on mobile while matching or exceeding Ref-NeRF's quality on reflective surfaces.

## Results

On the Shiny Blender Dataset (the primary reflective-surface benchmark), REFRAME achieves 36.02 PSNR / 0.983 SSIM / 0.040 LPIPS (GPU) and 35.83 PSNR / 0.981 SSIM / 0.041 LPIPS (Mobile), matching or exceeding the non-real-time SOTA Ref-NeRF (35.96 PSNR) while running at 60 FPS on smartphones. On the NeRF Synthetic Dataset, REFRAME scores 31.04 PSNR (GPU) and 30.84 PSNR (Mobile), outperforming mesh-based baselines MobileNeRF (30.90) and NeRF2Mesh (29.67) but trailing 3DGS (33.30) and Ref-NeRF (33.99). On the Real Captured outdoor dataset, REFRAME achieves 22.50 PSNR (GPU) and 22.11 PSNR (Mobile), surpassing MobileNeRF (22.96) and NeRF2Mesh (22.71) in LPIPS while remaining below Ref-NeRF (24.45) and 3DGS (24.06) in PSNR. Memory footprint is 47.55–52.86 MB, substantially lower than MobileNeRF (125.75–152.37 MB) and NeRF2Mesh (73.53–22.15 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Shiny Blender Dataset (glossy objects), REFRAME (36.02 PSNR) surpasses the non-real-time SOTA Ref-NeRF (35.96) by 0.06 dB and dramatically outperforms all mesh-based real-time baselines: MobileNeRF (26.62), NeRF2Mesh (26.96), and NvDiffRec (29.05), with gaps of 6–9 dB. On the NeRF Synthetic Dataset, REFRAME (31.04 PSNR) trails 3DGS (33.30) and Ref-NeRF (33.99) by 2–3 dB but edges out MobileNeRF (30.90) and NeRF2Mesh (29.67); the paper notes 3DGS struggles with reflective surfaces qualitatively despite higher PSNR. On the Real Captured outdoor dataset, REFRAME (22.50 PSNR) is below Ref-NeRF (24.45) and 3DGS (24.06) but beats MobileNeRF (22.96) and NeRF2Mesh (22.71) in LPIPS (0.325 vs. 0.430/0.419), with the paper attributing the PSNR gap to background artifacts from the initial mesh. In terms of memory, REFRAME uses 37–38k vertices and 75k faces (47–53 MB cache), far fewer than MobileNeRF (494k–1028k vertices, 126–152 MB) and competitive with NeRF2Mesh (45k–200k vertices, 22–74 MB). All methods achieve 60 FPS on iPhone 12 and iPad Air 3, confirming equivalent real-time mobile performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFRAME sits at the intersection of two research threads: reflection-direction-based specular modeling (Ref-NeRF, De-NeRF, Ref-NeuS) and NeRF-to-mesh distillation for real-time rendering (MobileNeRF, NeRF2Mesh, NvDiffRec). It departs from prior mesh-based real-time methods by replacing viewing-direction parameterization with reflection-direction-based specular color, and introduces a neural environment feature map (a 4-layer MLP baked into a 2D texture) to compensate for the limited capacity of the tiny inference-time MLP. The geometry learner, which uses multi-resolution hash encoding to predict per-vertex position and normal offsets, builds on Instant-NGP-style representations but applies them to mesh refinement rather than implicit surface learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that reflective-surface NVS, previously limited to offline volume rendering or GPU-only real-time methods, becomes deployable on smartphones with sub-100MB memory footprints, opening the door to AR/VR applications involving glossy or metallic objects. The decoupling of geometry, diffuse/specular appearance, and environment features enables straightforward scene editing (relighting, material swap) without retraining. Key limitations remain: the method struggles with interreflections and non-distant illumination, and its quality depends on the initial mesh extracted from a separate NeRF-based pipeline, which is a non-trivial prerequisite for complex scenes.
