## Problem

Existing multi-view diffusion models for 3D reconstruction produce only sparse, low-resolution images due to the computational cost of 3D self-attention, and typically require known camera poses. This paper addresses how to generate dense (32-view) and high-resolution (512×512) multi-view images from one or a few unposed input images, without explicit camera parameters or projection models, to enable flexible single- and sparse-view 3D object reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts 1 to 10 unposed RGB images at 512×512 with foreground segmentation masks, where input viewpoints are arbitrary (random azimuth, elevation in [-10°, 45°], and camera distance in [1.5, 2.2]). It produces 32 dense output views at 512×512 positioned at a fixed spherical grid (8 azimuth angles every 45° × 4 elevation angles every 30° in [-30°, 60°]), which are then fed into grid-based NeuS to extract a textured 3D mesh in approximately 3 minutes on a single GPU.

## Method

MVDiffusion++ is a multi-view latent diffusion model with a condition branch and a generation branch that share architecture and weights, operating on 64×64×4 latents from a fine-tuned mask-aware VAE. The core denoising UNet replaces MVDiffusion's correspondence-aware attention with a global self-attention module applied across all 2D latent features of every view simultaneously, which learns 3D consistency without any camera pose or projection formula; cross-attention injects CLIP embeddings of condition images, and per-view learnable index embeddings (42 total, zero-scaled) distinguish generation from condition slots. The key scalability trick is a view dropout training strategy: at each iteration, 24 of the 32 generation views are randomly discarded, reducing the token count from ~130k to a manageable size so that 512×512 training is feasible, while at test time the full 42-view architecture runs to produce all 32 output images in one pass. Training uses a three-stage schedule (ε-prediction then v-prediction) on 180k synthetic objects with 128 H100 GPUs for about a week, and the model is initialized from pre-trained latent diffusion weights.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects dataset for single-view reconstruction, MVDiffusion++ achieves 0.0165 Chamfer Distance and 0.6973 Volume IoU, outperforming the best prior method (Open-LRM) by 42% in CD and 17.4% in IoU. For novel view synthesis, it reaches 21.45 PSNR, 0.844 SSIM, and 0.129 LPIPS, surpassing SyncDreamer by 1.4 dB in PSNR. In the sparse-view setting with 10 unposed inputs, MVDiffusion++ achieves 25.03 PSNR, exceeding the pose-free LEAP baseline by 8.19 dB and even outperforming NeuS with ground-truth camera poses on most view counts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVDiffusion++ outperforms all 10 single-view baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitors, it reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.0285 (Open-LRM) and 0.0261 (SyncDreamer) to 0.0165, and increases Volume IoU from 0.5945 (Open-LRM) and 0.5421 (SyncDreamer) to 0.6973. In novel view synthesis, it improves PSNR by 1.4 dB over SyncDreamer (21.45 vs. 20.05) and 2.5 dB over Zero123 (21.45 vs. 18.93). In the sparse-view setting, MVDiffusion++ dramatically outperforms LEAP on all NVS metrics (e.g., 25.03 vs. 16.84 PSNR at 10 views), while slightly trailing NeuS with ground-truth poses on Chamfer Distance at 10 views (0.0101 vs. 0.0096) but matching or exceeding it at 1–4 views. The paper notes the comparison with Wonder3D is not entirely fair since Wonder3D assumes orthographic cameras while the evaluation uses perspective projections.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVDiffusion++ extends the multi-view latent diffusion lineage from MVDiffusion (which required pixel-wise correspondences and CAA attention) and MVDream (which introduced 3D self-attention for general multi-view consistency) by replacing all explicit geometric machinery—camera poses, projection models, and correspondence-aware attention—with a single global 2D self-attention module over latent features. The key architectural departure is the pose-free design: no camera parameters or image projection formulas are used, and the condition branch handles an arbitrary number of unposed input images through learnable view-index embeddings and CLIP cross-attention. The view dropout training strategy, which randomly discards 24 of 32 generation views per iteration, is a novel scalability trick absent from prior multi-view diffusion work, enabling high-resolution training that was infeasible for MVDream, Wonder3D, and SyncDreamer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the camera pose estimation bottleneck that plagues sparse-view reconstruction pipelines (SfM fails with minimal overlap), making the system usable from casual unposed snapshots. The view dropout strategy is a broadly applicable training trick for any multi-view generative model facing the token-count explosion problem. However, evaluation is limited to 30 objects from GSO, and the paper acknowledges failure modes on thin structures and implausible occluded-view hallucinations. Open questions include whether the pose-free 2D self-attention insight generalizes to non-object scenes, whether video data can address the training-data limitation, and whether the approach scales to even denser or higher-resolution view sets.
