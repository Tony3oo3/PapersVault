## Problem

Prior predictive models for elastoplastic object manipulation represent objects by a single global geometry, making them unable to predict general topological changes such as splitting, merging, or genus changes. The paper argues that geometry-only representations prevent a deeper investigation of topological manipulation, where the same action on the same object can yield drastically different topologies depending on end-effector geometry, and where dynamic connectivity (not just static proximity) determines whether components are truly merged or merely touching.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single partial RGB-D point cloud (with sensor noise, fringing, and incomplete coverage) and the end-effector's surface geometry at two consecutive time steps, rather than a multi-view reconstruction or a kinematic action vector. Output is structured as a set of K+1 per-component 3D occupancy masks (plus an outlier mask) evaluated at arbitrary sample locations, along with a genus classification per component latent that simultaneously yields the number of components. The latent shape codes (257×512) serve as the intermediate representation for autoregressive multi-step prediction and for planning via cosine similarity in latent space.

## Method

DoughNet comprises a denoising autoencoder and a topology-aware dynamics model, both Transformer-based. The encoder embeds partial point clouds into a fixed set of 257 latent codes via cross-attention spatial aggregation plus a stack of self-attention layers for denoising and completion; the decoder uses a segmentation-inspired mechanism (inspired by Per-Pixel Classification) where a learned query set of K+1 component codes is cross-attended with the latents, and the dot product of global and local decoded features yields per-component occupancy logits. The dynamics model autoregressively predicts the next latent set from the current one, conditioned on the EE geometry, using four stacks of alternating self- and cross-attention blocks. Topology is predicted as a classification task on each component latent (genus class or empty), avoiding post-hoc mesh processing. Training is two-stage: the autoencoder is pretrained with a permutation-invariant Focal Loss on synthetic MLS-MPM data (augmented with depth-sensor artefacts), then frozen while the dynamics model is trained with Huber loss in latent space over two consecutive steps to mitigate exposure bias. A physically-motivated topological checking procedure (perturbing particle velocities to test dynamic connectivity) provides ground-truth topology labels from the particle-based simulation.

## Results

On 194 simulated test scenes (13 time steps each), DoughNet achieves 92.0% VIoU, 90.5% CIoU, 97.9% AccC, and 98.6% AccG for full-sequence prediction, with 22.1 fps inference speed. At the final time step, it reaches 84.1% VIoU and 92.3% AccC. The ablation study (Table 2) confirms that latent-space supervision and set-to-set (absolute) prediction are critical: replacing them with reconstruction-based supervision or relative prediction drops full-sequence VIoU from 92.0% to 76.3% and 73.1%, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoughNet outperforms MLS-MPM (the simulation oracle) on full-sequence VIoU (92.0 vs. 87.2) and AccC (97.9 vs. 97.2) while running 110× faster (22.1 vs. 0.2 fps); however, MLS-MPM edges ahead at the final time step on CIoU (79.4 vs. 75.1) and AccG (95.6 vs. 90.3), suggesting the learned model occasionally needs extra steps to cleanly resolve a topological change. Against RoboCook (GNN-based particle prediction), DoughNet achieves a 31.3% higher CIoU (90.5 vs. 59.2) and 31.2% higher VIoU (92.0 vs. 60.8) on full sequences, with the gap widening over time as RoboCook's point-cloud representation accumulates error. The VPM+ ablation (same architecture but post-hoc static topology check instead of learned topology) drops AccC from 97.9% to 92.8% and AccG from 98.6% to 94.8% on full sequences, and the gap widens dramatically at the final step (AccC 92.3% vs. 68.7%), confirming that jointly predicting topology in latent space is essential rather than a post-processing step.

## Relation to Prior Work

DoughNet builds on the 3DShape2VecSet architecture [44] for its set-of-latent-codes shape representation, but repurposes it for a fundamentally different objective: autoregressive dynamics prediction rather than shape generation. It departs from the dominant line of deformable-object prediction work (RoboCook, Li et al.) that tracks individual particles or points and handles topology only via post-hoc geometric processing, by instead predicting per-component occupancy maps and genus directly in latent space. The synthetic data generator with physically-motivated topological checking operations (counterfactual velocity perturbation in MLS-MPM simulation) is a new contribution that addresses the absence of ground-truth topology labels in particle-based simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learned model to jointly predict both geometrical deformation and topological changes (splitting, merging, genus) of elastoplastic objects, enabling planning toward topological goals that geometry-only models cannot express. The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer from purely synthetic training to a real UR5 robot with Play-Doh is practically significant for robotic manipulation. Key limitations include the restriction to single squeezing actions and one material property, observed inconsistencies between predicted occupancy and topology, and the narrow scene complexity (1–2 objects). Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-step sequential topological manipulation, diverse material properties, and more complex end-effector geometries.
