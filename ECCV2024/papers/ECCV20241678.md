## Problem

Prior action quality assessment (AQA) methods largely ignore the structured score rubric that human experts use to decompose actions into scored steps, and they fail to provide calibrated uncertainty estimates for their predictions. This limits their reliability in high-stakes settings (e.g., surgery, competitive sports) where knowing when a model's prediction is trustworthy is as important as the prediction itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241678/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video clip (uniformly sampled frames processed by a frozen I3D backbone into clip-level features) together with text descriptions of the action's key steps (embedded via Flan-T5) and the known step presence and temporal ordering. It outputs a scalar quality score and a scalar uncertainty estimate computed as the sum of harmonic means of predicted per-step variances. At inference, 20 stochastic samples are drawn and averaged to produce the final score, making the output robust to sampling noise.

## Method

RICA² encodes action steps and the scoring rubric as a video-specific directed acyclic graph (DAG) whose leaf nodes are individual steps, intermediate nodes are scoring stages, and the root node is the final score. A Transformer-based embedding function f fuses video and text features via cross-attention to predict a Gaussian distribution (mean and diagonal covariance) for each step's latent embedding, introducing stochasticity that captures prediction uncertainty. These stochastic leaf embeddings are then propagated upward through the DAG via a lightweight GNN (averaging + MLP per node), and the root embedding is decoded into the final score. Training follows the variational information bottleneck objective, combining an MSE loss on predicted scores with a KL divergence regularizer that pulls the predicted Gaussians toward a standard normal prior, plus a small auxiliary loss enforcing temporal ordering of steps via cross-attention sparsity and ranking terms. The key design choice is that only leaf-node embeddings are stochastic while non-leaf nodes are deterministic given leaf samples, reflecting the assumption that uncertainty resides solely in assessing individual steps while the rubric aggregation is deterministic.

## Results

On FineDiving (the largest AQA benchmark), RICA² achieves SRCC 0.9402 and Rℓ₂ 0.2838, outperforming the best exemplar-based method TPT (SRCC 0.9333, Rℓ₂ 0.2877) and the best exemplar-free method DAE-MT (SRCC 0.9285, Rℓ₂ 0.3320). The deterministic variant RICA²† further improves to SRCC 0.9421 and Rℓ₂ 0.2600. On MTL-AQA, RICA²† reaches SRCC 0.9620 and Rℓ₂ 0.2280, and on JIGSAWS, RICA² attains an average SRCC of 0.92, surpassing TPT's 0.89. Uncertainty calibration (Kendall's τ) is substantially improved: τ = 0.6444 on FineDiving and τ = 0.6000 on MTL-AQA, compared to negative or near-zero values for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RICA² outperforms all exemplar-based baselines on FineDiving: it beats TPT [2] by +0.7% relative SRCC (0.9402 vs. 0.9333) and +1.4% relative Rℓ₂, and beats TSA [44] by +2.0% relative SRCC. Against the strongest exemplar-free baseline DAE-MT [48], RICA² gains +1.3% relative SRCC and +14.5% relative Rℓ₂. On MTL-AQA, RICA²† edges out TPT [2] in SRCC (0.9620 vs. 0.9607) and Rℓ₂ (0.2280 vs. 0.2378), while RICA² matches TPT's SRCC (0.9594 vs. 0.9607) with a large calibration advantage (τ = 0.60 vs. τ = −0.11). On JIGSAWS, RICA² achieves an average SRCC of 0.92, surpassing TPT [2] (0.89) by +3.4% relative and DAE-MT [48] (0.76) by +21% relative. The most striking advantage is in uncertainty calibration: RICA²'s Kendall's τ of 0.6444 on FineDiving and 0.6000 on MTL-AQA far exceeds all baselines (best prior: MUSDL at 0.4667 on FineDiving), while several baselines (TPT, DAE-MT) exhibit negative τ, indicating their uncertainty estimates are anti-correlated with actual errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

RICA² sits at the intersection of three prior lines: stochastic/probabilistic embeddings for uncertainty quantification (Oh et al. 2019; Li et al. 2021), graph neural networks for relational aggregation (Battaglia et al. 2018), and the variational information bottleneck training framework (Alemi et al. 2016). It departs from the dominant exemplar-based AQA paradigm (TPT, CoRE, TSA) by being exemplar-free, and from the closest prior rubric-aware method IRIS by treating sub-scores as latent stochastic embeddings on a DAG rather than deterministic sub-score predictions, and by adding calibrated uncertainty estimation. The score-distribution learning line (MUSDL, DAE) models output variance but does not guarantee calibration with actual prediction error, which RICA² addresses through its VIB-based stochastic embedding design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibrated uncertainty in AQA is practically critical for high-stakes settings (surgical skill assessment, competitive sports judging) where uncertain predictions should be escalated to human experts; RICA² is the first method to deliver both state-of-the-art accuracy and well-calibrated uncertainty on major AQA benchmarks. A key limitation is the assumption of known, ordered action steps and a pre-defined scoring rubric, which restricts applicability to structured actions (diving, surgery) and excludes free-form or unstructured tasks. Open questions include whether the rubric-aware graph can be learned end-to-end when steps are not pre-specified, and whether the calibration quality holds under distribution shift (e.g., new athletes, new surgical procedures).
