## Problem

Multi-view 3D object detectors produce numerous duplicate false-positive predictions along camera rays because per-pixel depth cannot be accurately estimated from images, causing position embeddings to encode only ray direction rather than full 3D location. Existing remedies such as NMS, Focal Loss, and temporal modeling do not explicitly exploit the 3D scene structure to suppress these depth-ambiguous duplicates, leaving a persistent performance bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241303/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N surround-view RGB images (e.g., 6 cameras at 256×704 or 512×1408) together with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, which are used to project ground-truth 3D centers into camera frustum space for ray construction. It outputs per-object 3D bounding boxes with classification scores, position offsets (x, y, z), scales (w, h, l), yaw/pitch directions, and 2D velocities. Ray Denoising itself is a training-only plug-and-play module: it adds extra denoising queries to the transformer decoder during training but introduces zero additional inference cost.

## Method

Ray Denoising casts a ray from each camera's optical center to the corresponding ground-truth object center, then samples N reference points along that ray using a Beta distribution (parameterized by λ and μ) whose bounded support avoids large regression losses from extreme offsets. These sampled points are projected back to 3D world coordinates via the inverse camera transform and fed through an MLP to form ray denoising queries, which are concatenated with the standard learnable object queries and processed by the transformer decoder. An attention mask prevents information leakage from ray queries to object queries, and the query nearest the GT center is labeled positive while the remaining N−1 are labeled background, trained with Focal Loss and L1 Loss. The key insight is that because all points on the same ray interact with identical image features, forcing the model to classify them as 'no object' (except the true depth) explicitly teaches depth-aware discrimination without requiring external depth supervision.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with ResNet-50 (256×704), RayDN achieves 46.9% mAP and 56.3% NDS, surpassing the prior SOTA StreamPETR (45.0% mAP, 55.0% NDS) by 1.9% mAP and 1.3% NDS. Scaling to ResNet-101 (512×1408) yields 51.8% mAP and 60.4% NDS, a 1.4% mAP gain over StreamPETR. On the nuScenes test set (V2-99, 640×1600), RayDN reaches 56.5% mAP and 64.5% NDS, outperforming StreamPETR by 1.5% mAP. On Argoverse 2 validation, RayDN achieves 22.3% mAP and 16.1% CDS, improving over StreamPETR by 2.0% mAP and 1.5% CDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RayDN outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every dataset and backbone configuration. On nuScenes validation (ResNet-50), it leads StreamPETR by 1.9% mAP (46.9 vs 45.0), SparseBEV by 2.1% mAP, and SOLOFusion by 4.2% mAP; on the ResNet-101 setting it extends the lead over StreamPETR to 1.4% mAP (51.8 vs 50.4) and over SparseBEV to 1.7% mAP. On the nuScenes test set (V2-99), RayDN surpasses StreamPETR by 1.5% mAP (56.5 vs 55.0) and SparseBEV by 2.2% mAP (56.5 vs 54.3). On Argoverse 2 validation, the gains are even larger: RayDN beats StreamPETR by 2.0% mAP (22.3 vs 20.3), Sparse4Dv2 by 3.4% mAP, and PETR by 4.7% mAP, confirming strong generalization beyond nuScenes. Notably, RayDN also improves NDS/CDS consistently (e.g., +1.3% NDS on nuScenes val ResNet-50, +1.5% CDS on Argoverse 2), indicating that the mAP gains are not solely from precision improvements but reflect better overall detection quality including translation and orientation accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ray Denoising extends the denoising query paradigm from DN-DETR and DINO (2D) into the multi-view 3D detection setting, but departs fundamentally from prior 3D denoising approaches (StreamPETR, SparseBEV, PETRv2) that sample noisy queries uniformly around ground-truth objects. Instead, it exploits the geometric structure of camera rays to place hard negative samples at depth-ambiguous positions along the same ray as the GT object, using a Beta distribution to model where false positives actually concentrate. This positions the work at the intersection of hard negative mining and 3D scene geometry, filling a gap that temporal methods and post-processing (NMS, Focal Loss) leave unaddressed: the model's inability to discriminate depth when visual features are identical along a ray.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a previously underappreciated failure mode—false positives along camera rays account for up to 5.4% mAP in strong baselines—and shows that a training-only, plug-and-play sampling strategy can recover most of this gap without architectural changes or inference overhead. The finding that Ray Denoising matches the performance of explicit depth supervision (3DPPE) without any external depth signal suggests that structured negative sampling can implicitly teach depth perception, raising the question of whether other implicit supervision signals could substitute for explicit depth estimation more broadly. Limitations include dataset-specific Beta parameter tuning (λ, μ differ between nuScenes and Argoverse 2) and evaluation restricted to two benchmarks; whether the approach transfers to LiDAR-camera fusion or novel sensor configurations remains open.
