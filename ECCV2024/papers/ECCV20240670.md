## Problem

Existing offline signature verification methods rely on holistic global features for pair comparison, overlooking fine-grained local structural differences (stroke structure and style) that are critical for distinguishing genuine from forged signatures. Additionally, transformer-based backbones inherently suppress high-frequency detail during self-attention, causing loss of the very local information that OSV requires.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240670/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of binary signature images (reference and query) of size H×W×1, each accompanied by a foreground map obtained via global thresholding on the resized image. Images are patchified into tokens and processed through a 4-stage backbone. The output is a combined distance score dis = λ₀·dis_global + dis_struct, where dis_global is the L2 distance between mean-pooled global embeddings and dis_struct is a local structural distance; a threshold on this score yields the final genuine/forgery decision.

## Method

DetailSemNet introduces two key components. First, the Detail-Semantics Integrator (DSI) decomposes each feature map into a low-frequency Semantic component (via local average pooling) and a high-frequency Detail component (via subtraction), routing them through separate branches: an attention-based SemanticsAttend module for context, and convolution-based SalientConv (max-filter + conv) and DetailConv (two conv layers) modules for fine-grained detail, with outputs fused via residual convolution, FFN, and LayerNorm. Second, Structural Matching computes a local distance by first masking background tokens using the foreground map, then solving an Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) optimization over the remaining local embeddings using the Sinkhorn algorithm, which permits soft many-to-many token matching rather than strict one-to-one assignment. The model is trained end-to-end with a double-margin contrastive loss on genuine-genuine and genuine-forged pairs, initialized from ImageNet-pretrained weights.

## Results

On BHSig-H, DetailSemNet achieves an EER of 2.07% (Acc 98.24%), outperforming the best prior method TransOSV (EER 3.39%) by 1.32 percentage points. On BHSig-B, it reaches EER 2.11% (Acc 98.19%), surpassing CaC (EER 3.96%) by 1.85 points. On CEDAR, the model attains an EER of 0.58% (Acc 99.53%), improving over SDINet (EER 1.75%) by 1.17 points. In cross-dataset zero-shot evaluation, DetailSemNet consistently achieves the lowest EER across all language-transfer settings (e.g., 7.46% on BHSig-H→BHSig-B vs. 14.66% for CaC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetailSemNet outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every dataset tested. On BHSig-H, it beats the strongest prior method TransOSV [18] by 1.32 EER points (2.07% vs. 3.39%) and IDN [40] by 4.89 points (2.07% vs. 6.96%). On BHSig-B, it surpasses CaC [24] by 1.85 EER points (2.11% vs. 3.96%) and SigGCN [32] by 1.89 points (2.11% vs. 4.00%). On CEDAR, it achieves 0.58% EER versus SDINet [16]'s 1.75% and Co-Tuple [9]'s 3.51%, a margin of over 3 points. In cross-dataset zero-shot settings, the advantage widens dramatically: e.g., BHSig-H→BHSig-B yields 7.46% EER vs. 14.66% for CaC [24] and 18.66% for TransOSV [18]. The paper also shows that applying Re-ID models (BPB [26], PAT [35]) to OSV underperforms DetailSemNet by 2.35 and 5.78 EER points respectively on BHSig-H, confirming that OSV-specific fine-grained matching is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Earth Mover's Distance framework (Rubner et al., IJCV 2000) to formulate local patch matching as an optimal transport problem solved via the Sinkhorn algorithm, repurposing a classical image-retrieval metric for signature verification. It departs from the dominant paradigm in OSV—holistic global feature comparison (SigNet, IDN, SDINet, TransOSV)—by explicitly matching local stroke-level tokens between signature pairs. The Detail-Semantics Integrator addresses a documented limitation of transformer backbones (indiscriminate suppression of high-frequency signals, as noted in Bai et al. 2022 and Park & Kim 2022) by disentangling features into semantic (attention-processed) and detail (convolution-processed) streams, effectively combining the global aggregation strength of ViTs with the local sensitivity of CNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that local structural matching, when paired with high-frequency-preserving feature extraction, yields substantially better OSV performance than global-only approaches, with particularly large margins in cross-dataset (cross-lingual) settings where generalization is most challenged. The interpretability offered by visualizing the EMD matching flow provides forensic practitioners with actionable evidence for verification decisions, a practical advantage over black-box similarity scores. Open questions include whether the uniform-weight EMD assumption holds under more adversarial forgery conditions, whether the DSI decomposition generalizes to other fine-grained verification tasks (e.g., document authentication), and whether the Sinkhorn-based matching can be further accelerated for real-time deployment on resource-constrained devices.
