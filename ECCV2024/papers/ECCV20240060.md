## Problem

Existing 3D scene completion methods are either limited to a small set of object categories, require expensive test-time optimization, or are object-centric and fail to handle occlusions in cluttered multi-object scenes. Zero-shot completion of multiple unseen objects from a single RGB-D image in diverse real-world settings remains largely unsolved, in stark contrast to the rapid progress in 2D vision tasks enabled by large-scale data and scalable architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240060/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a depth map, and a binary foreground mask delineating all objects of interest. Image features from a pre-trained ResNeXt-50 are unprojected into 3D camera coordinates using the depth map and mask, then populated into a sparse octree at LoD-9 (512³, 2.5 mm cell size, 1.28 m grid). The output is a completed 3D surface extracted at LoD-9, along with per-LoD occupancy predictions (LoD 5–9), surface normals, and SDF values at the finest level, with empty cells pruned at each level to reduce computation.

## Method

OctMAE is a hybrid architecture that combines an Octree U-Net backbone with a latent 3D Masked Autoencoder operating at LoD-5. The Octree U-Net encoder downsamples the LoD-9 octree features to a compact latent representation, where S layers of full self-attention Transformer blocks capture global geometric context. Two key design choices make this tractable: (1) an occlusion masking strategy that places learnable mask tokens only in voxels behind visible surfaces (determined by depth testing), rather than in all empty cells, which reduces the token count and enables full attention instead of local deformable attention; and (2) 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) that encode axial voxel coordinates without learnable parameters, avoiding the O(N²) cost of relative positional encodings. The latent 3D MAE decoder uses cross-attention with RoPE to integrate occluded-region tokens, and the Octree U-Net decoder upsamples with skip connections to predict occupancy at each LoD, normals, and SDF at LoD-9. Training uses a composite loss of L2 on normals and SDF plus binary cross-entropy on occupancy at every LoD, and the model is trained exclusively on a synthetic dataset of 1M images rendered from 12K Objaverse/GSO objects via BlenderProc, yet generalizes zero-shot to real-world RGB-D data.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, OctMAE achieves a Chamfer distance of 6.48 mm, F1-Score@10mm of 0.839, and normal consistency of 0.848, outperforming the best baseline OCNN (CD 9.05, F1 0.782, NC 0.828) by 28% in CD. On real-world datasets, OctMAE maintains strong performance: CD of 6.40 mm on YCB-Video, 6.14 mm on HomebrewedDB, and 6.97 mm on HOPE, with F1 scores consistently above 0.80. The method runs at 85.1 ms per frame, comparable to the fastest sparse baselines (OCNN at 80.1 ms, MinkowskiNet at 78.5 ms), while implicit methods like POCO (758.8 ms) and MCC (9,100 ms) are orders of magnitude slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OctMAE outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor is OCNN [63], which shares the same sparse octree U-Net backbone but lacks the latent 3D MAE; OctMAE reduces CD by 28% on the synthetic set (6.48 vs. 9.05 mm) and by 13–15% on real datasets. Dense voxel methods (VoxFormer, ShapeFormer, ConvONet, AICNet) and implicit methods (MCC, POCO) are substantially worse, with CD values 2–7× higher, and also suffer from poor zero-shot generalization to novel shapes. MinkowskiNet [6], the other sparse baseline, trails OctMAE by 43% in CD on the synthetic set (11.47 vs. 6.48 mm), confirming that the latent 3D MAE's global attention provides a meaningful advantage over purely local sparse convolutions. Notably, OctMAE achieves these gains at a runtime (85.1 ms) comparable to the fastest baselines, whereas implicit methods like POCO (758.8 ms) and MCC (9,100 ms) are 9–107× slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

OctMAE combines the Octree U-Net backbone from O-CNN [62] and its octree construction pipeline from [63] with a latent 3D masked autoencoder, directly adapting the ConvMAE [19] principle of performing masked autoencoding in the latent space of a CNN to the 3D sparse octree setting. The key departure from VoxFormer [34], which extends MAE to 3D via deformable attention over dense voxel grids, is the move to a much lower-resolution latent octree (LoD-5) where full self-attention becomes tractable, enabled by a novel occlusion masking strategy that places mask tokens only in occluded voxels rather than all empty cells. For positional encoding, the paper replaces the learnable relative encodings used in prior 3D transformer work with RoPE [59], a parameter-free NLP technique, which proves more effective in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in robotics: existing multi-object completion methods either require category-specific priors, test-time optimization, or are limited to a handful of object categories, making them unsuitable for real-time deployment on diverse scenes. The introduced dataset (12K objects, 601 categories, 50% LVIS coverage) is an order of magnitude more diverse than prior benchmarks and provides a much-needed resource for the field. Open questions remain around truncated objects (no depth available), lack of semantic/instance-level output, and explicit uncertainty modeling, all of which the authors identify as future directions. The zero-shot synthetic-to-real transfer at near-real-time speed (85 ms) suggests a viable path toward general-purpose scene understanding for manipulation and planning.
