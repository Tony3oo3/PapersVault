## Problem

Existing fMRI-to-image reconstruction methods rely on individual-specific decoders and condition pre-trained diffusion models with latent codes extracted from fMRI signals, creating a fundamental mismatch between 1D neural signals and 2D image generation that causes information loss and blurry reconstructions lacking structural detail. Additionally, no prior work has demonstrated that multi-individual fMRI pretraining can improve within-subject decoding, despite the potential for shared perceptual representations across brains.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw fMRI brain activity signals (averaged over 3 trials per stimulus) that are first transformed into unified 256×256 single-channel 2D brain activation flatmaps, and produces 512×512 RGB natural images. During training, COCO captions serve as auxiliary text supervision for the semantic branch, and the model is trained on ~67,000 fMRI-image pairs from 8 subjects in the Natural Scenes Dataset. At inference, a control scale α allows continuous interpolation between pure semantic reconstruction and full structural fidelity.

## Method

NeuroPictor is trained in three stages: (i) a masked autoencoder learns a universal fMRI latent space by reconstructing 2D brain surface maps from a guide token, unifying signals across individuals; (ii) multi-subject pretraining jointly optimizes a High-Level Guiding Network (HLGN) that maps fMRI latents into CLIP text-condition space via dual encoders (fMRI-to-text + auxiliary), and a Low-Level Manipulation Network (LLMN) that converts fMRI latents into feature maps and injects them into frozen SD U-Net blocks via zero-convolution residual connections; (iii) single-subject fine-tuning adapts the full model to one individual. The key architectural insight is decoupling semantic guidance (through cross-attention layers) from structural control (through direct U-Net feature manipulation) within a single end-to-end diffusion pipeline, eliminating the information bottleneck of intermediate image representations.

## Results

On the Natural Scenes Dataset (NSD), NeuroPictor achieves the best scores on 6 out of 8 evaluation metrics: SSIM (.375), AlexNet(2) (96.5%), AlexNet(5) (98.4%), Inception (94.5%), EffNet-B (.639, lower is better), and SwAV (.350, lower is better). It trails MindEye on pixel correlation (.229 vs .309) and CLIP similarity (93.3% vs 94.1%), which the authors attribute to competing methods using blurred VAE intermediate images that inflate pixel-level metrics. The non-fine-tuned multi-subject pretrained model (NeuroPictor w/o ft) already approaches competitive levels (e.g., Inception 88.3%, CLIP 88.9%), validating the cross-subject pretraining strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroPictor outperforms all six baselines on structural and semantic metrics: it leads MindEye (the strongest prior method) by +1.8% on AlexNet(2), +0.6% on AlexNet(5), +0.7% on Inception, and by 0.006/0.017 on EffNet-B/SwAV (lower is better). It also surpasses Brain-Diff by +2.3% on AlexNet(2) and +7.3% on Inception. However, NeuroPictor trails MindEye on CLIP similarity (93.3% vs 94.1%) and on pixel correlation (.229 vs .309); the authors note that Brain-Cap, MindEye, and Brain-Diff all use a blurred VAE-generated intermediate image as diffusion initialization, which artificially inflates pixel-level correlation. Against the older baselines (Lin et al., Takagi et al., Gu et al.), NeuroPictor achieves substantially higher scores across all comparable metrics, with Inception improving from 78.2% to 94.5% and CLIP from 77.0% to 93.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroPictor departs from the dominant fMRI-to-image paradigm (MindEye, Brain-Diffuser, Brain-Captioning) that generates intermediate representations—blurry VAE images, depth maps, or silhouettes—to condition a diffusion model, instead directly modulating the diffusion U-Net's feature maps with fMRI-derived signals. Its fMRI encoder builds on fMRI-PTE's multi-subject surface-map formulation but replaces VQ-GAN compression with a masked autoencoder to preserve full signal information in a shared latent space. The Low-Level Manipulation Network draws on T2I-Adapter and ControlNet-style feature manipulation, while the High-Level Guiding Network repurposes Stable Diffusion's CLIP text-conditioning pathway to accept fMRI-derived semantic features. The key conceptual departure is the three-stage training protocol (calibrated-encoding → multi-subject pretraining → single-subject refining), which challenges the prevailing assumption that fMRI decoders must be trained per-individual.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that ~67k fMRI-image pairs from 8 subjects improve within-subject reconstruction over single-subject training alone is a meaningful step toward scalable, data-efficient fMRI decoding, reducing the per-subject data burden that has constrained the field. The explicit separation of high-level semantics and low-level structure in fMRI signals enables practical semantic manipulation (e.g., swapping subject identities while preserving pose), opening a new axis of controllability. However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (NSD, 8 subjects, 982 test images), pixel correlation remains below methods that exploit blurry intermediate images, and the approach still requires 60k iterations of per-subject fine-tuning—raising open questions about true cross-subject generalization without any subject-specific adaptation and scalability to larger, more diverse populations.
