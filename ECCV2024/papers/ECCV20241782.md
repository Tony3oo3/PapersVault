## Problem

Multi-head detectors rely on FPN-like necks that fuse feature maps from different pyramid levels point-to-point, but low-level and high-level features carry inherent representation biases (local vs. global), so direct element-wise fusion introduces spatial noise rather than complementary semantics. The paper questions whether this fusion paradigm is truly necessary for modern real-time detectors and identifies the resulting feature misalignment as an under-addressed source of performance loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241782/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images at 416×416 or 640×640 resolutions and outputs bounding boxes with class labels in the standard single-stage detection format. A notable practical detail is that the authors evaluate at a realistic confidence threshold of 0.25 (matching deployment) rather than the conventional 0.001 evaluation threshold, which reveals a 4.4 AP drop and makes the comparison more reflective of actual usage.

## Method

The SA (Secondary Features Alignment) solution combines four components: (1) an Independent Hierarchy Pyramid (IHP) neck that removes all cross-level fusion and instead applies independent bottleneck convolutions per scale branch; (2) Soft Nearest Neighbor Interpolation (SNI), which multiplies upsampled high-level features by a downscaling factor α = Resolution_X / Resolution_Y before point-to-point fusion, weakening the influence of misaligned global features at zero extra cost; (3) Extended Spatial Downsampling (ESD), which replaces single-mode stride-2 downsampling with parallel extended-window max-pooling (local) and average-pooling (global) branches fused via addition (ESD-I) or learnable linear fusion (ESD-II), preserving spatial detail; and (4) GSConvE, a lightweight convolution variant using larger depthwise kernels (9×9, 13×13, 17×17) to capture wider receptive fields with fewer layers. The full SYOLO architecture stacks ESD in the backbone, SNI in the PANet-style neck, and GSConvE in the E-ELAN blocks, achieving SOTA AP on both Pascal VOC and MS COCO among real-time detectors.

## Results

On MS COCO (val2017), SYOLO at 640×640 achieves 53.1% AP, surpassing YOLOv8 (52.9%), YOLOv6 (51.7%), and YOLOv7 (51.2%) while using fewer FLOPs (142.5G vs. 165.2G, 144.0G, 104.7G respectively). The smaller SYOLO-s variant (6.6M params, 15.7G FLOPs) reaches 40.8% AP at 640×640, outperforming YOLOv7-t (38.7%), YOLOv6-n (37.5%), and YOLOv5-s (37.4%). On Pascal VOC 07+12, SYOLO achieves 69.6% AP, edging out YOLOv8 (69.3%) and YOLOv6 (69.2%). The SNI component alone boosts YOLOv7 by +3.3 AP points and YOLOv6 by +2.0 AP points with zero additional FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SYOLO outperforms all six YOLO-series baselines (v3–v8) on both Pascal VOC and COCO. On COCO at 640×640, SYOLO (53.1% AP) beats YOLOv8 (52.9%), YOLOv6 (51.7%), YOLOv7 (51.2%), YOLOv5 (49.0%), YOLOX (49.7%), and YOLOv4 (43.5%); the margin is largest over YOLOv4 (+9.6 AP) and smallest over YOLOv8 (+0.2 AP). Against non-real-time Transformer/CNN detectors on COCO (Table 6), SYOLO (53.1% AP, no pre-training) surpasses DINO (51.0%), Swin-B (51.9%), RFPN (51.3%), PANet (51.0%), and BiFPN (49.3%), though these baselines use ImageNet pre-trained backbones. The lightweight SYOLO-s (40.8% AP) outperforms YOLOv7-t by +2.1 AP and YOLOv6-n by +3.3 AP at comparable or lower latency. Notably, SYOLOv7 (SNI-only variant) matches YOLOv7's FLOPs exactly (103.5G) while gaining +0.9 AP on VOC, confirming SNI is a zero-cost improvement. The paper also highlights that under a realistic 0.25 confidence threshold (vs. the standard 0.001 evaluation threshold), SYOLO's AP drops by 4.4 points, a gap the authors argue is more representative of deployment conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical re-examination of the FPN-like fusion paradigm that became standard after FPN and PANet, arguing that point-to-point fusion of hierarchically different feature maps introduces spatial misalignment that acts as noise. It builds directly on GSConv for lightweight convolution (extending it to GSConvE-I/II), adapts SPPNet's learnable fusion mechanism for the ESD-II downsampling variant, and modifies PANet's nearest-neighbor upsampling with the proposed SNI. Conceptually, it departs from the FPN lineage by proposing IHP—a radical architecture that abandons all neck fusion—and by reframing the downsampling stage as an underexploited opportunity for spatial feature retention via ESD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SNI technique is a simple, zero-cost plug-and-play modification that consistently improves accuracy across six YOLO variants, making it immediately adoptable by practitioners working with FPN-based detectors. The IHP finding—that removing fusion helps coupled-head detectors but slightly hurts decoupled-head ones—nuances the narrative that FPN is universally beneficial and suggests the misalignment problem interacts with head design in non-obvious ways. The paper also raises an important practical concern about evaluation confidence thresholds (Table 3), showing that reported AP can be inflated by 4+ points when using a 0.001 threshold versus a realistic 0.25. Open questions include whether the misalignment problem can be fully resolved (the authors acknowledge it requires further research) and whether emerging architectures (Transformers, newer backbones) will render FPN-like paradigms obsolete altogether.
