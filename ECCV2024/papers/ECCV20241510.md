## Problem

Existing DGSS methods rely on visual pattern recognition (normalization, domain randomization, transformer attention robustness) and lack the ability to comprehend high-level semantic concepts inherent to each class, limiting generalization to extreme domain shifts. While VLMs encode domain-invariant semantics in their text embeddings, no prior DGSS work has directly leveraged these textual representations as the basis for pixel grouping in a segmentation framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241510/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB driving scene image (cropped to 512×512 during training; source images range from 1280×720 to 2048×1024) along with 19 class name text labels processed by a frozen VLM text encoder. It outputs a per-pixel semantic segmentation mask over 19 Cityscapes classes, produced by dot-producting refined textual object queries with high-resolution per-pixel embeddings followed by sigmoid activation, plus a per-query class probability via softmax.

## Method

tqdm uses text embeddings from a frozen VLM text encoder (CLIP or EVA02-CLIP) as initial object queries in a Mask2Former-style transformer decoder, with learnable prompts (length 8) to adapt the textual features for segmentation. A text-to-pixel attention block is inserted within a 6-layer multi-scale deformable attention pixel decoder, where pixel features attend to K textual cluster centers to enhance per-pixel semantic clarity before the 9-layer masked-attention transformer decoder refines the queries for final mask prediction. Fixed matching (rather than bipartite matching) assigns each query to a single class, ensuring consistent semantic representation. Three regularization losses—language (cross-entropy between prompted and fixed-prompt text embeddings), vision-language (per-pixel cross-entropy on cosine-similarity score maps between visual and text embeddings), and vision (L2 distance between [class] tokens of the fine-tuned and initial vision encoder)—preserve the pre-trained VLM's vision-language alignment throughout training.

## Results

On the synthetic-to-real setting (GTA5→Cityscapes), tqdm with EVA02-CLIP achieves 68.88 mIoU, improving over the prior state-of-the-art (Rein with ViT-L at 66.40) by 2.48 mIoU. Across all synthetic-to-real targets (Cityscapes, BDD100K, Mapillary), tqdm (EVA02-L) averages 66.05 mIoU, surpassing VLTseg (63.50) and Rein (63.60). In the real-to-real setting (Cityscapes→{BDD100K, Mapillary}), tqdm (EVA02-L) reaches 70.44 mIoU average, outperforming HGFormer (66.80) and VLTseg (70.40†). With the smaller ViT-B backbone, tqdm still leads all CLIP-initialized methods, achieving 54.97 mIoU average on synthetic-to-real.

## Baselines & Comparisons

tqdm (EVA02-L) outperforms the strongest prior methods across most benchmarks: it beats VLTseg (EVA02-L) by +3.28 mIoU on G→C and +3.60 on G→M, and surpasses Rein (ViT-L) by +2.48 on G→C and +4.00 on G→M. On G→B, tqdm (59.18) trails Rein (EVA02-L, 60.50) and Rein (ViT-L, 60.40) by roughly 1.2–1.3 mIoU, the only clear loss in the synthetic-to-real setting. In the real-to-real setting, tqdm (EVA02-L) leads HGFormer (Swin-L) by +3.22 on C→B and +4.05 on C→M, and matches VLTseg (EVA02-L) on C→M (76.15 vs. 76.40†) while slightly trailing on C→B (64.72 vs. 64.40†). With the smaller ViT-B backbone, tqdm dominates all CLIP-initialized baselines, improving over IBAFormer (MiT-B5) by +1.16 mIoU on G→C and over VLTseg (ViT-B) by +10.00 on G→C. Overall, tqdm achieves the best average mIoU in both synthetic-to-real (66.05) and real-to-real (70.44) settings among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Mask2Former query-based segmentation architecture (Cheng et al., CVPR 2022) and the contrastive vision-language pre-training of CLIP (Radford et al., ICML 2021), repurposing text embeddings as the initial object queries rather than randomly initialized vectors. It departs from prior DGSS methods (normalization/whitening, domain randomization, transformer-based approaches) that rely on visual pattern invariance, and from recent VLM-based DGSS work (FAMix, VLTseg, Rein) that uses language only as an auxiliary training signal or style augmentation source. The key conceptual departure is treating text embeddings as a first-class, domain-invariant basis for pixel grouping within the segmentation decoder, complemented by a novel text-to-pixel attention mechanism and three regularization losses to preserve vision-language alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding segmentation queries in language-derived semantics, the method offers a principled path to handling extreme domain shifts (e.g., sketch-style driving scenes) that purely visual approaches struggle with, and the 2.5 mIoU gain over prior SOTA on GTA5→Cityscapes is backed by thorough ablations and cross-benchmark consistency. The text-to-pixel attention and vision-language regularization losses are modular components that could transfer to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., open-vocabulary segmentation, panoptic segmentation). Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large class taxonomies, whether it benefits from larger VLMs beyond EVA02-CLIP, and whether the fixed-matching assumption (one query per class) limits applicability to instance-level or open-set settings.
