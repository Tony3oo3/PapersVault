## Problem

Egocentric gaze anticipation—predicting where a camera-wearer will look in future frames from past video—remains largely understudied, and no prior work has exploited audio signals for this task. Existing audio-visual fusion methods are designed for fixed-viewpoint saliency prediction and fail to handle the drastic viewpoint changes and natural reaction-time delays that characterize egocentric gaze responses to auditory stimuli.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240226/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 egocentric video frames (256×256) sampled from a 3-second observation window and corresponding log-spectrograms (256×256, derived from 24 kHz audio via STFT with 10 ms window and 5 ms hop, cropped to 1.28 s sliding windows per frame). It outputs a probabilistic gaze fixation distribution over 8 frames uniformly sampled from a 2-second anticipation window, modeled as a 2D heatmap on the image plane.

## Method

The proposed CSTS architecture splits audio-visual fusion into two parallel self-attention branches: a spatial module that computes in-frame attention between all visual tokens and a single pooled audio token per frame (with cross-frame connections masked), and a temporal module that computes cross-frame attention between single visual and audio tokens per time step. The two branches are merged via channel-wise element-wise multiplication, and the reweighted visual representation is decoded with skip connections from the MViT encoder. A key novelty is the post-fusion contrastive loss, which is applied not on raw modality embeddings but on the reweighted audio-visual representations produced by the fusion modules, encouraging the fused features to align across modalities. Training combines a KL-divergence loss on the gaze prediction with this contrastive objective, weighted by a hyperparameter α.

## Results

On Ego4D, CSTS achieves 39.7% F1 score (53.3% recall, 31.6% precision), outperforming the previous SOTA gaze anticipation model DFG+ (37.3% F1) by +2.4% and the SOTA gaze estimation model GLC (37.8% F1) by +1.9%. On Aria, CSTS reaches 59.9% F1 (66.8% recall, 54.3% precision), surpassing DFG+ (57.6% F1) by +2.3% and GLC (58.3% F1) by +1.6%. Ablation studies confirm that adding the audio modality improves F1 by +2.5% on Ego4D and +2.4% on Aria over the vision-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSTS outperforms all seven baselines on F1 score across both datasets. Against the direct gaze-anticipation competitors DFG [68] and DFG+ [67], CSTS gains +2.5% and +2.4% F1 on Ego4D, and +2.5% and +2.3% F1 on Aria, respectively. Against the strongest gaze-estimation baseline GLC [29] (adapted to the anticipation setting), CSTS leads by +1.9% F1 on Ego4D and +1.6% on Aria. The vision-only MViT backbone [12] and I3D-R50 [13] baselines trail CSTS by 2.5–2.8% F1 on Ego4D and 2.4–2.5% on Aria. Notably, the paper also shows that naively concatenating audio into GLC yields only a marginal +0.3%/+0.2% F1 gain, underscoring that the spatial-temporal separable fusion design is essential rather than simply adding an audio stream. CSTS also achieves the highest precision on both datasets (31.6% on Ego4D, 54.3% on Aria), indicating more confident and accurate gaze predictions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends egocentric gaze estimation (Lai et al. [29], Li et al. [34]) to the anticipation setting and, for the first time, incorporates audio as a modality for this task. It builds on the audio preprocessing pipeline from Epic-Fusion [27] and the MViT video encoder [12], but departs from prior audio-visual fusion strategies (joint self-attention, bilinear, concatenation) by proposing a spatial-temporal separable fusion architecture motivated by the unique viewpoint-change dynamics of egocentric video. The post-fusion contrastive loss also departs from standard contrastive audio-visual learning [2,3,35], which applies contrastive objectives on raw modality embeddings rather than on reweighted fused representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that audio signals meaningfully improve egocentric gaze anticipation, opening a path toward more naturalistic AR systems that can anticipate user attention from ambient sound. The spatial-temporal separable fusion insight—arguing that joint fusion is suboptimal when the camera viewpoint shifts in response to audio stimuli—is a design principle that could generalize to other egocentric prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer anticipation horizons, whether it generalizes beyond social scenarios to tasks like cooking or navigation, and whether real-time deployment on wearable hardware is feasible given the dual-encoder architecture.
