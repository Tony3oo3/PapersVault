## Problem

Multi-frame monocular depth estimation relies on a static-scene assumption for cost-volume feature matching, but dynamic objects produce misaligned matching costs that corrupt the depth probability distribution. Existing remedies either depend on external segmentation masks, apply only binary consistency masks that lack structural awareness, or address the error only at the loss level without rectifying the corrupted cost volume itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240066/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a target RGB image I_t and two temporally adjacent source images (frames t−1 and t+1) at 640×192 (KITTI) or 416×128 (Cityscapes), with known camera intrinsics and depth constrained below 80 m. It outputs a multi-frame depth map D_multi and a per-pixel uncertainty map U ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} representing the probability that each pixel belongs to a dynamic object. No external segmentation masks, optical flow, or ground-truth depth are required at inference.

## Method

ProDepth introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, an auxiliary decoder ψ_dec estimates depth D_cv from the (deliberately corrupted) cost volume; comparing D_cv with a probabilistic single-frame depth D_single (modeled as a heteroscedastic Gaussian) yields a structural-aware uncertainty map U = 1 − exp(−β|D_single − D_cv|). Second, the Probabilistic Cost Volume Modulation (PCVM) module converts both the single-frame Gaussian and the softmax-normalized cost volume into probability distributions over k log-spaced depth candidates, then fuses them via a weighted geometric mean P(d|x) = p_single(d|x)^{U(x)} · p_cv(d|x)^{1−U(x)}, so dynamic pixels follow single-frame cues while static pixels retain multi-frame geometric consistency; the fused distribution is rescaled back to cost-volume scale. Third, an uncertainty-aware loss reweighting strategy multiplies the photometric reprojection loss by (1−U) and applies a hard binary mask for pixels exceeding a threshold γ, partially reducing incorrect supervision in ambiguous regions rather than discarding them entirely.

## Results

On Cityscapes, ProDepth achieves Abs Rel 0.095, RMSE 5.531, and δ<1.25 of 0.908, outperforming the best prior multi-frame method DynamicDepth (Abs Rel 0.103, RMSE 5.867) by 7.8% in absolute relative error. On KITTI (Eigen split), ProDepth reaches Abs Rel 0.086, RMSE 4.139, and δ<1.25 of 0.918, surpassing DepthFormer (Abs Rel 0.090, RMSE 4.149) and DualRefine (Abs Rel 0.090, RMSE 4.237). On the Waymo Open generalization test, ProDepth achieves Abs Rel 0.247 and RMSE 9.544, beating DynamicDepth (0.255, 9.902) and ManyDepth (0.260, 10.463) without requiring any semantic segmentation at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProDepth outperforms all 19 baselines across every metric on both Cityscapes and KITTI. On Cityscapes, the most relevant comparison is against DynamicDepth [8], which uses a pretrained semantic segmentation network to identify moving objects; ProDepth (Abs Rel 0.095) beats it (0.103) by 7.8% without any semantic input, and also surpasses ManyDepth [38] (0.114) by 16.7%. On KITTI, ProDepth edges out DepthFormer [15] (Abs Rel 0.090) and DualRefine [1] (0.090) by 4.4%, while also improving RMSE log from 0.171–0.175 to 0.166. Notably, ProDepth does not require any additional semantic information at inference, yet it outperforms methods that do (DynamicDepth, Struct2Depth 2, InstaDM), demonstrating that its learned uncertainty map is a sufficient substitute for segmentation-based dynamic object identification. On the Waymo Open generalization benchmark, ProDepth (Abs Rel 0.247) beats both ManyDepth (0.260) and DynamicDepth (0.255), confirming that the probabilistic cost volume modulation generalizes to unseen scenes with challenging conditions like nighttime driving.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProDepth builds directly on the multi-frame cost volume architecture established by ManyDepth and DepthFormer, which use geometric consistency between temporally adjacent frames to estimate depth. It departs from prior dynamic-object handling strategies—ManyDepth's binary consistency mask, DynamicDepth's pretrained segmentation-based input warping, and DepthFormer's loss-level single-frame supervision—by instead directly modulating the corrupted cost volume through a probabilistic fusion of single-frame and multi-frame depth distributions. The auxiliary decoder that infers object-level uncertainty from the discrepancy between single-frame and cost-volume depth, without requiring external segmentation, represents a conceptual shift from binary or class-based identification toward a continuous, self-contained probability estimate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic cost volume modulation (PCVM) offers a principled, architecture-level fix for the static-scene assumption violation that plagues multi-frame depth estimation, rather than patching the problem at the loss or input level. The finding that segmentation masks actually degrade performance when combined with PCVM (because they include static objects, discarding useful multi-frame cues) highlights a subtle but important limitation of class-based dynamic object identification. Open questions include how the approach scales to more than two source frames, whether the uncertainty inference generalizes to very fast or partially occluded objects, and whether the weighted geometric mean fusion is optimal compared to other probabilistic combination strategies in more complex scene dynamics.
