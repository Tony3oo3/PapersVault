## Problem

Existing diffusion inversion methods for real image editing struggle to simultaneously achieve faithful target-prompt alignment and source-image structural preservation. Prior approaches use a fixed η value (0 or 1) in the DDIM sampling equation, and the role of η in balancing editability versus reconstruction fidelity has not been theoretically analyzed or optimized. The paper identifies that perfect-reconstruction inversion methods (e.g., DDPM Inversion, Direct Inversion) guarantee source fidelity but produce non-Gaussian noise that limits editing, while imperfect methods allow more editing but lose structural coherence.

## Input / Output

The method operates in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4 with T=50 denoising steps and is entirely training-free. It consumes a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing that image, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit, then produces both a pixel-perfect reconstruction of the source image and an edited image. The region-dependent η is derived from cross-attention maps of the source inference, so no external segmentation mask is required; adjusting the attention threshold controls the spatial extent of editing.

## Method

Eta Inversion designs a time- and region-dependent η function for DDIM sampling to inject optimal real Gaussian noise during the backward (denoising) path. The time-dependent component uses a decreasing linear schedule—larger η near timestep T to modify high-level semantic features and smaller η near timestep 0 to preserve fine-grained details—grounded in a theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–3) showing that non-zero η reduces KL divergence under inaccurate inversion and that a decaying schedule is optimal when score-estimation errors concentrate near the endpoints. The region-dependent component applies η>0 only where the cross-attention map for the target object exceeds a threshold, leaving background regions at η=0 to prevent unwanted changes. For the injected noise ε_add, the method samples n candidates from N(0,I) and selects the one minimizing the forward-backward latent gap via arg min, ensuring the noise remains truly Gaussian (unlike DDPM Inversion's compensation noise) while still achieving perfect source reconstruction. Three η schedules (EtaInv 1, 2, 3) are provided, tuned respectively for structural similarity, prompt alignment, and full-image style transfer.

## Results

On PIE-Bench, Eta Inversion (2) achieves the highest CLIP similarity across all three editing methods: 31.25 (PtP), 31.63 (PnP), and 30.62 (MasaCtrl), outperforming the next-best Direct Inversion (30.92, 31.32, 30.37). For structural preservation, Eta Inversion (1) achieves DINO scores of 1.34/2.34/3.66 and LPIPS of 16.58/27.33/23.12 (PtP/PnP/Masa), competitive with Direct Inversion while delivering substantially better text-image alignment. On the style-transfer subset, Eta Inversion (3) with a larger η and no masking achieves CLIP similarity of 32.85 (PtP) and 33.12 (PnP), significantly surpassing all baselines (next best: 32.06/32.79 from Null-text Inversion).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Eta Inversion (2) outperforms all seven baselines on CLIP similarity for PtP (31.25 vs. 30.99 for DDIM Inv., 30.92 for Direct Inv.) and PnP (31.63 vs. 31.32 for Direct Inv.), confirming that the time- and region-dependent η function improves text-image alignment. For structural preservation (DINO), EDICT and DDPM Inversion achieve the lowest scores (0.41–1.04), indicating near-perfect reconstruction, but at the cost of severely limited editing (EDICT's PnP CLIP similarity drops to 24.69). Direct Inversion is the closest competitor, achieving competitive CLIP scores (30.92–31.32) and moderate DINO (1.28–4.32), but consistently trails Eta Inversion by 0.3–0.31 in CLIP similarity. Null-text Inversion and NPI/ProxNPI occupy a middle ground with moderate CLIP (30.3–30.8) and DINO (1.2–4.5) scores. The region-dependent η (mask) provides a small but consistent improvement in structural metrics: Eta Inv. (1) with mask achieves DINO 1.34 vs. 1.37 without mask for PtP, and BG-LPIPS 6.57 vs. 6.74, confirming that masking preserves background while the unmasked version edits more aggressively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDIM sampling framework by treating the η parameter—previously fixed at 0 (deterministic DDIM) or 1 (stochastic DDPM)—as a design variable that can be shaped as a time- and region-dependent function. It builds directly on the score-based SDE/ODE theoretical framework (Song et al., 2021; Cao et al., 2023) to derive principled conditions under which non-zero η improves editing, and unifies all existing diffusion inversion methods (DDIM Inv, NTI, NPI, ProxNPI, EDICT, DDPM Inv, Direct Inv) under a single forward/backward path formulation. The key departure from prior work is the injection of true Gaussian noise (sampled from N(0,I)) rather than the computed, non-Gaussian noise used by CycleDiffusion and DDPM Inversion to enforce exact reconstruction, combined with cross-attention-guided spatial masking of η.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework and comprehensive benchmark (all inversion methods × all editing methods on PIE-Bench) fill a notable gap in the literature where prior comparisons were fragmented and inconsistent. The theoretical analysis (Propositions 1–3) provides a principled recipe for η design that practitioners can adapt, and the 'real noise' insight offers a drop-in improvement for existing perfect-reconstruction inversion pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges that no universal η schedule works for all edits, that current evaluation metrics (CLIP, DINO, LPIPS) cannot simultaneously capture structural preservation and prompt faithfulness, and that automating optimal η selection remains open—suggesting the field still lacks a robust, general-purpose editing evaluation protocol.
