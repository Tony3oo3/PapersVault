## Problem

Neural Video Compression methods are typically trained for a single fixed RD trade-off, making them unable to adapt to varying bitrate constraints. Existing variable-bitrate solutions (multi-granularity quantization, feature modulating) achieve rate flexibility through linear adjustments that yield sub-optimal RD performance, and they lack a content-adaptive rate estimation mechanism, resulting in poor bitrate accuracy—especially when sequence content changes rapidly. This paper addresses both the RD optimality gap and the rate control precision gap simultaneously, while also introducing adaptive computational complexity allocation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a current video frame x_t, its associated reference frame x_ref (with motion vectors), and a target bitrate R_tar as input, and produces a compressed bitstream and a reconstructed frame x̂_t. The DRA operates with 4 discrete coding routes whose latent channel counts are (24, 48, 72, 96), and the RCA uses the frame content and reference motion vector to predict per-route bitrates before route selection. Training uses 256×256 patches from Vimeo-90k, and evaluation is performed on full-resolution HEVC Class B–E and UVG 4K sequences with a sliding window of 30 frames for rate allocation.

## Method

The core architecture is a Dynamic-Route Autoencoder (DRA) built from slimmable convolution operators that form 4 overlapping sub-networks, each representing a distinct RD trade-off with progressively more channels and computation. Channel-wise auto-regression is used for entropy coding, and a Feature Modulation network refines the partial latent representation from the preceding route to compensate for the absence of serial-routes optimization. A lightweight Rate Control Agent (RCA) estimates the coded bitrate of each route from frame content and reference motion vectors, then a sliding-window bit allocation algorithm selects the optimal route per frame to track the target bitrate. The Joint-Routes Optimization (JRO) strategy trains all routes collaboratively by iteratively decaying λ for each route toward its diverging point on the RD curve (from highest-bitrate route downward), achieving global-optimal RD performance across the full bitrate spectrum while hierarchically allocating computational complexity.

## Results

On average across HEVC (Classes B–E) and UVG datasets, the proposed method achieves a BD-Rate reduction of 14.8% and a BD-PSNR gain of 0.47 dB over DCVC-HEM, the closest state-of-the-art baseline. The largest gains are over DCVC (−66.7% BD-Rate, +3.11 dB) and AlphaVC (−59.4% BD-Rate, +2.34 dB). For rate control accuracy, the method achieves an average bitrate error of 1.66%, roughly 3.6× lower than the R-D-λ model's 6.05%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five NVC baselines across every dataset. Against DCVC-HEM (the most directly comparable variable-rate method), the gain is modest at −14.8% BD-Rate / +0.47 dB on average, with the smallest margin on UVG (−2.83%) and HEVC E (−5.14%), suggesting DCVC-HEM's multi-granularity quantization is competitive at extreme bitrates. Against DCVC-TCM and CANF-VC, the improvements are substantially larger (−33.2% and −39.0% BD-Rate respectively), reflecting the advantage of dynamic route selection over fixed-architecture conditional coding. The largest gains are over DCVC (−66.7%) and AlphaVC (−59.4%), which lack the adaptive complexity allocation of the proposed DRA. For rate control specifically, the method reduces average bitrate error from 6.05% (R-D-λ model [16]) to 1.66%, a roughly 3.6× improvement, with the best accuracy on HEVC C (0.62%) and the worst on UVG (2.65%), still well below the R-D-λ model's 6.24% on the same dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the slimmable autoencoder paradigm (Yang et al., CVPR 2021) from image compression to video compression, repurposing dynamic channel-width sub-networks as distinct coding routes with different RD trade-offs. It builds on the DCVC contextual video compression architecture (Li et al., NeurIPS 2021) and channel-wise autoregressive entropy coding (Minnen et al., ICIP 2020), but departs from prior NVC rate control strategies—multi-granularity quantization (DCVC-HEM) and feature modulating (Lin et al.)—by treating rate control as a network-architecture selection problem rather than a parameter-tuning problem. The Rate Control Agent also supersedes the analytical R-D-λ model (Li et al., ICASSP 2022) with a learned, content-adaptive bitrate estimator, eliminating the reliance on iterative parameter refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first NVC framework to jointly optimize rate, distortion, and computational complexity, enabling practical deployment where both bandwidth and compute budgets are constrained (e.g., mobile or edge devices). The 3.6× improvement in bitrate accuracy over the prior best (1.66% vs 6.05%) is practically significant for streaming and storage applications with strict bitrate targets. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully beyond 4 routes (the paper notes auto-regressive latency grows with route count), whether the JRO training strategy generalizes to non-overlapping sub-networks, and how the method performs under rapidly non-stationary content where the sliding window may lag.
