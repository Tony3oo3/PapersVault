## Problem

Pretrained diffusion models such as Stable Diffusion suffer from two critical failures when generating images beyond their training resolution: unreasonable object duplication caused by feature duplication in deep U-Net blocks, and exponentially growing inference time dominated by global self-attention in top blocks. Existing tuning-free higher-resolution methods either degrade fine-grained details or remain impractically slow, leaving a gap in both quality and efficiency for scalable high-resolution synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241354/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a single high-resolution RGB image, scaling pretrained Stable Diffusion models (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, SDXL, SDXL Turbo) from their native training resolution (512×512 or 1024×1024) up to 4×–16× (e.g., 2048×2048 or 4096×4096). It also supports non-square aspect ratios such as 512×2048 and 2048×4096. The entire pipeline is tuning-free: no additional training, fine-tuning, or high-resolution datasets are required, and the only modifications are to two convolution parameters in the U-Net and the attention computation in Block 1.

## Method

HiDiffusion combines two tuning-free components. First, Resolution-Aware U-Net (RAU-Net) replaces the original downsampler in Down Block 1 with a Resolution-Aware Downsampler (RAD) that shrinks the feature map to match training-time dimensions in deep blocks, and the original upsampler in Up Block 1 with a Resolution-Aware Upsampler (RAU) that restores the target resolution; a Switching Threshold T1 (e.g., 20 out of 50 DDIM steps) toggles between RAU-Net for early structural denoising and vanilla U-Net for late detail refinement to counteract information loss. Second, Modified Shifted Window Multi-head Self-Attention (MSW-MSA) exploits the observed locality of top-block self-attention by replacing global attention in Block 1 with large window attention of size (H/2, W/2) and applying a random shift stride selected per timestep from a fixed set, enabling cross-window information flow without requiring two successive attention modules. Together these components reduce inference time by 1.5–6× relative to prior higher-resolution methods while improving FID, pFID, and CLIP scores over both vanilla direct inference and competing tuning-free approaches.

## Results

On ImageNet at 2048×2048 resolution, HiDiffusion (SD 1.5) achieves FID 53.35 and pFID 65.95, substantially outperforming ScaleCrafter (FID 65.04, pFID 86.83) while running 4.9× faster (58.38s vs 287.89s). At the extreme 4096×4096 resolution with SDXL, HiDiffusion reaches FID 64.12 and pFID 74.91 in 286.97s, beating ScaleCrafter (FID 78.90, 1298.39s) by 4.5× in speed and DemoFusion (FID 58.93, 1735.58s) by 6.0× in speed. Across all tested resolutions (1024×1024 to 4096×4096) and backbones (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, SDXL, SDXL Turbo), HiDiffusion consistently improves FID and pFID over vanilla direct inference while reducing latency by 1.5–2.8×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ScaleCrafter [11], HiDiffusion wins on FID, pFID, CLIP, and latency across every backbone and resolution tested (e.g., at 2048×2048 with SD 1.5: FID 53.35 vs 65.04, pFID 65.95 vs 86.83, and 4.9× faster). Against DemoFusion [8] at 4096×4096 (SDXL), HiDiffusion achieves a lower FID (64.12 vs 58.93 is actually DemoFusion winning on FID) but a substantially lower pFID (74.91 vs 76.53) and is 6.0× faster (286.97s vs 1735.58s); at 2048×2048 (SDXL), HiDiffusion beats DemoFusion on FID (47.01 vs 48.36), pFID (62.29 vs 66.41), and CLIP (0.315 vs 0.311) while being 4.2× faster. Against vanilla SD direct inference, HiDiffusion dramatically improves FID (e.g., 28.93 vs 78.53 on COCO at 2048×2048 with SD 1.5) and is 1.5–2.8× faster. Against the super-resolution pipeline LDM-SR [32], HiDiffusion is 1.25–2.5× faster and achieves lower pFID (e.g., 33.42 vs 35.57 at 2048×2048 with SD 1.5), indicating richer fine-grained detail. Against acceleration methods ToMeSD [2] and DeepCache [26], MSW-MSA matches or exceeds their speedup while maintaining or improving FID and pFID (e.g., at 1024×1024: FID 21.80 vs 22.76 for ToMeSD and 25.31 for DeepCache). Overall, HiDiffusion dominates on the speed–quality trade-off, being the only method that simultaneously improves all quality metrics and reduces inference time relative to every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the tuning-free higher-resolution diffusion literature, departing from ScaleCrafter's receptive-field-based diagnosis of object duplication by instead identifying feature duplication in deep U-Net blocks as the root cause, and resolving it with a minimal two-convolution modification (RAU-Net) rather than per-convolution parameter tuning. For efficiency, it adapts Swin Transformer's shifted window attention [22] to the diffusion setting, introducing timestep-dependent window shifting to compensate for the single self-attention module in each SD transformer block. Unlike progressive-schedule methods (DemoFusion) or super-resolution pipelines (LDM-SR), HiDiffusion operates as a single-stage, training-free drop-in replacement for the U-Net's top-block samplers and attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a simple, parameter-free modification enables 4×–16× resolution scaling of off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion models (up to 4096×4096) at 1.5–6× the speed of prior tuning-free methods, making high-resolution generation accessible without additional datasets or training. The diagnostic insight that deep-block feature duplication drives object duplication provides a reusable lens for understanding U-Net behavior at non-training resolutions. However, quality degrades noticeably at extreme resolutions (FID ~64 at 4096×4096), and the method inherits all of SD's inherent limitations (prompt sensitivity, text rendering). Open questions include whether the feature-duplication diagnosis generalizes to non-U-Net backbones (e.g., DiT) and how best to combine this approach with learned super-resolution for the highest quality.
