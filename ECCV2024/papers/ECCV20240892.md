## Problem

Existing diffusion-based semi-supervised 3D object detection methods (e.g., Diffusion-SS3D) rely on Farthest Point Sampling for candidate generation, which cannot learn content embeddings across scenes, and aggregate features within noisy initial boxes that may be far from ground truth, leading to suboptimal denoising. The paper identifies two critical gaps: how to model object candidates that balance spatial distribution with learned content, and how to aggregate correct features to incrementally refine noisy boxes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud scene (n×3 coordinates) as input and predicts amodal 3D bounding boxes parameterized by centroid (c_x, c_y, c_z), dimensions (s_l, s_w, s_h), and upright-axis orientation, along with semantic class labels. In the semi-supervised setting, the student model receives both labeled scenes (with GT boxes) and unlabeled scenes (with teacher-generated pseudo-labels), while the teacher model generates pseudo-labels by adding Gaussian noise to box sizes and labels and reversing the diffusion via a 2-step DDIM process. The diffusion corrupts only sizes and labels (not centers), with centers obtained via FPS on the point cloud.

## Method

Diff3DETR unifies a diffusion model and DETR within a mean-teacher framework. The agent-based object query generator places learnable object agents on a 16×16×4 3D grid, adapts them to each scene via cross-attention with encoder features, and then trilinearly interpolates them at FPS-sampled center coordinates to produce object queries that combine spatial coverage with learned content. The box-aware denoising module interleaves a transformer decoder layer with a DDIM layer: self-attention incorporates noisy box parameters via an MLP offset, cross-attention uses 3D Vertex Relative Position Encoding (3DV-RPE) to focus each query on its corresponding noisy box region, and the DDIM layer iteratively estimates the next-step box from the current prediction. The student is trained with a combined loss on GT and pseudo-labels, while the teacher is updated via EMA and generates pseudo-labels through multi-step DDIM sampling.

## Results

On ScanNet with 5% labeled data, Diff3DETR achieves 45.1 mAP@0.25 and 29.2 mAP@0.5, surpassing the previous best (Diffusion-SS3D) by 1.6% and 1.3% respectively. At full supervision (100%), it reaches 65.7 mAP@0.25 and 44.9 mAP@0.5. On SUN RGB-D with 5% labeled data, Diff3DETR obtains 45.7 mAP@0.25 and 26.2 mAP@0.5, outperforming the prior best by 1.8% and 1.3%. The method consistently leads across all labeled-data ratios (5%–100% on ScanNet, 1%–20% on SUN RGB-D) for both mAP@0.25 and mAP@0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff3DETR outperforms all five baselines across every labeled-data ratio on both ScanNet and SUN RGB-D. Against the closest competitor, Diffusion-SS3D [13], the gains are 1.6% mAP@0.25 / 1.3% mAP@0.5 at 5% labels on ScanNet and 1.8% / 1.3% at 5% labels on SUN RGB-D, with the margin widening slightly at lower label ratios (e.g., 1.6% / 1.6% at 1% on SUN RGB-D). Compared to the pseudo-label-based methods NESIE [35] and 3DIoUMatch [37], Diff3DETR leads by 4.6% and 5.1% mAP@0.25 respectively at 5% ScanNet labels, demonstrating the advantage of diffusion-generated pseudo-labels over confidence-filtered ones. The consistency-based method SESS [43] trails by 13.1% mAP@0.25 at 5% ScanNet labels, and the fully supervised VoteNet [23] baseline lags by 17.2%, confirming that the semi-supervised diffusion framework substantially reduces annotation dependence while improving absolute accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion-SS3D (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2024), which first introduced diffusion models to semi-supervised 3D object detection, by embedding the diffusion process within a unified DETR architecture rather than a VoteNet-style detector. The key architectural departure is the agent-based object query generator, which replaces the FPS-only candidate generation of Diffusion-SS3D with a hybrid that interpolates between FPS-sampled locations and learnable scene-adaptive agents, addressing the trade-off between spatial coverage and content embedding. The box-aware denoising module further departs from prior diffusion-based detectors (e.g., DiffusionDet) by tightly coupling the DDIM denoising schedule with the transformer decoder's self-attention and cross-attention, using 3D Vertex Relative Position Encoding from V-DETR to anchor queries to noisy box regions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that diffusion-based pseudo-label generation can be made more effective in 3D by addressing two specific failure modes: insufficient candidate diversity (solved by the agent-based generator) and feature aggregation from incorrect regions due to noisy initial boxes (solved by the box-aware denoising module). However, the authors acknowledge that the iterative DDIM denoising process imposes significant computational overhead, limiting deployment on real-time devices and large-scale scenes. An open question is whether the agent-based query generation strategy generalizes beyond indoor scenes to outdoor or more sparsely populated 3D environments, and whether the computational cost can be reduced through fewer denoising steps without sacrificing the quality gains.
