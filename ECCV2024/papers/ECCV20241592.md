## Problem

Classical 3D line mapping methods (L3D++, LIMAP) suffer from three inherent inconsistency issues: (1) best proposals are selected independently per 2D line, so the support relationships assumed during scoring may contradict the actually selected best proposals of supporting lines; (2) line track building ignores 2D collinearity, allowing non-collinear segments in the same image to be clustered into one track; and (3) optimization omits coplanarity constraints and uses a coordinate-dependent line-to-plane distance, producing structurally inconsistent line maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241592/cropped_page2_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view images (with known camera poses from SfM) and an SfM point cloud as input, using either LSD or DeepLSD for 2D line detection and top-K line matching across neighboring views. Beyond the standard 3D line segments and line tracks, the system also outputs point-plane and line-plane associations derived from 3D plane detection on the combined SfM and line-sampled point cloud, which prior methods (L3D++, LIMAP) do not produce. The line tracks are structured as sets of 2D line segments across multiple images observing the same 3D line entity, with each track fitted to a single 3D infinite line via PCA.

## Method

The pipeline consists of four stages: proposal generation (using four triangulation methods plus unshared 3D points for richer associations), iterative best proposal selection, line track building with 2D collinearity constraints, and joint optimization. The key novelty in proposal selection is an iterative algorithm (Eq. 4) that scores each proposal against the *previous iteration's* best proposals of its matched 2D lines rather than against all candidate proposals, progressively enforcing that support relationships remain collinear; it terminates when fewer than 30% of lines change their best proposal. For track building, a Union-Find clustering is augmented with a 2D collinearity check (angle < 2°, perpendicular distance < 2 px, overlap ratio > 0) that prevents merging two groups if any pair of same-image segments violates collinearity. The joint optimization minimizes a six-term energy (point reprojection, line reprojection, point-line, point-plane, line-plane, line-VP) with a coordinate-independent line-to-plane distance (projecting the midpoint of the initial segment onto the optimized infinite line) and adaptive per-term weights computed from initial error ratios, eliminating manual unit-dependent tuning.

## Results

On the Tanks and Temples train split with DeepLSD, the proposed method achieves a length recall at 5 mm (R5) of 986.9 m, more than doubling L3D++ (389.1 m) and LIMAP (542.7 m), and an inlier percentage at 5 mm (P5) of 47.3% versus 44.6% and 44.0% respectively. On Hypersim with DeepLSD, R1 reaches 79.7 m (vs. 37.5 m for L3D++ and 32.8 m for LIMAP), nearly doubling the recall of both baselines. The method also produces substantially longer line tracks, with average supports of 15.1 images / 15.7 lines per track on Tanks and Temples (vs. 8.8/9.3 for L3D++ and 11.1/13.9 for LIMAP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against L3D++ [11], the proposed method wins decisively on all length-recall metrics (e.g., R5: 986.9 vs. 389.1 m with DeepLSD on Tanks and Temples) and on inlier percentage at 5 mm (47.3% vs. 44.6%), while producing tracks with roughly 1.7× more image and line supports. Against LIMAP [24], the method again dominates on recall (R5: 986.9 vs. 542.7 m; R50: 7379.0 vs. 4617.8 m) and on P5/P10 (47.3%/58.5% vs. 44.0%/54.9%), with the only marginal trade-off being a slightly lower P50 (83.7% vs. 81.3%—actually still higher). Against the closest-point line-to-plane distance from Li et al. [20], the proposed coordinate-independent measurement yields better recall (R1: 74.3 vs. 68.6 m; R5: 370.9 vs. 358.9 m on Hypersim), confirming its geometric advantage. Overall, the method achieves the best or near-best scores across every metric on both datasets, with the largest gains in completeness (length recall) and track robustness (number of supports).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the top-K matching paradigm for 3D line mapping established by L3D++ (Hofer et al., 2017) and refined by LIMAP (Liu et al., 2023), which relaxed the strict one-to-one correspondence requirement to generate multiple 3D line segment proposals per 2D line. Rather than proposing a new pipeline, the authors systematically identify three inconsistency issues in these classical methods—independent best-proposal selection breaking support relationships, absence of 2D collinearity constraints in track building, and missing coplanarity constraints in optimization—and address each with targeted modifications (iterative proposal scoring, Union-Find with collinearity checks, and joint point-line-plane-VP optimization). The method also departs from LIMAP by incorporating unshared 3D points for line-point association and introducing a coordinate-independent line-to-plane distance metric that fixes a known flaw in the closest-point approach from Li et al. (IROS 2020).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistency-centric framing provides a clear diagnostic lens for why prior line mapping methods underperform, and the resulting system achieves near-doubled length recall over both L3D++ and LIMAP on Hypersim with DeepLSD, while also producing longer, more robust line tracks and additional point-plane/line-plane associations useful for downstream tasks like surface reconstruction and localization. A notable limitation is the modest speed penalty (~18% slower than LIMAP) from the iterative selection and plane-based optimization, and the method still depends on external SfM points and plane detection quality. Open questions include whether the iterative algorithm's convergence behavior degrades in highly cluttered scenes with many outlier matches, and whether the coplanarity constraints generalize well to non-Manhattan or curved-surface environments.
