## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D editing methods (IN2N, ViCA-NeRF) edit rendered images independently or iteratively, which fails to enforce multi-view consistency and produces blurry, artefacted 3D results—especially in 360-degree scenes. The core challenge is that 2D diffusion models operate on single images and have no mechanism to ensure geometric or appearance coherence across the multiple views needed to reconstruct a consistent 3D scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240354/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed 3DGS scene, a description-style text prompt specifying the desired edit, and depth maps rendered from the 3DGS at 512×512 resolution. It produces a single re-optimised 3DGS model (one optimisation pass, not iterative) along with the edited multi-view images. Optionally, a Lang-SAM mask is applied to restrict editing to a local object region, and 4 reference views are randomly sampled from the dataset to guide appearance alignment.

## Method

GaussCtrl edits all rendered views simultaneously in one pass rather than iteratively, using two complementary consistency mechanisms. First, depth-conditioned editing via ControlNet: each image is inverted to its latent code via DDIM inversion (yielding consistent initial noise across views), then denoised with the edited prompt while ControlNet's conditioning branch enforces geometry from the naturally consistent depth maps. Second, an attention-based latent code alignment module replaces the standard self-attention in both the U-Net and ControlNet blocks with a weighted blend (λ=0.6) of self-attention and cross-view attention to the reference views, unifying appearance semantics across all edited images. The edited images are then used to re-optimise the original 3DGS in a single pass, with classifier-free guidance (scale 7.5) applied during denoising to maintain prompt fidelity.

## Results

GaussCtrl achieves the highest CLIP_dir score in 4 out of 6 evaluated scenes (Bear Statue: 0.1388, Dinosaur: 0.1584, Stone Horse: 0.2268, Face: 0.1503), outperforming IN2N(GS) and ViCA-NeRF. On the Garden scene, it scores 0.2891, slightly below IN2N (0.3027) and ViCA-NeRF (0.2903), while on Fangzhou it scores 0.1887 versus IN2N(GS)'s 0.2032. In terms of speed, GaussCtrl completes editing in approximately 9 minutes per scene, compared to ~13.5 min for IN2N(GS), ~38.5 min for ViCA-NeRF, and ~1.5 h for IN2N. The authors note that CLIP_dir can be misleading, as their method sometimes scores lower while producing visually superior results with fewer artefacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IN2N (NeRF-based), GaussCtrl wins on all four 360° scenes and the Face scene in CLIP_dir, while IN2N retains a narrow edge on Garden (0.3027 vs. 0.2891). Against IN2N(GS) (the 3DGS variant), GaussCtrl wins on Bear Statue (0.1388 vs. 0.1165), Dinosaur (0.1584 vs. 0.1490), Stone Horse (0.2268 vs. 0.1947), and Face (0.1503 vs. 0.1357), but loses on Fangzhou (0.1887 vs. 0.2032) and Garden (0.2891 vs. 0.1663 is actually a win—correction: GaussCtrl wins on Garden too at 0.2891 vs. 0.1663). Against ViCA-NeRF, GaussCtrl wins on Bear Statue (0.1388 vs. 0.1104), Dinosaur (0.1584 vs. 0.0723), Stone Horse (0.2268 vs. 0.1926), and Face (0.1503 vs. 0.1119), but trails slightly on Garden (0.2891 vs. 0.2903) and Fangzhou (0.1887 vs. 0.1809—actually a win). In speed, GaussCtrl (~9 min) is roughly 1.5× faster than IN2N(GS) (~13.5 min), 4× faster than ViCA-NeRF (~38.5 min), and 10× faster than IN2N (~1.5 h). The authors caveat that CLIP_dir measures global text-image alignment and can understate GaussCtrl's visual quality advantage, as shown by failure-case analyses where competitors score higher despite producing blurry or geometrically incorrect results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-driven 3D scene editing line initiated by Instruct NeRF2NeRF (IN2N) and its 3DGS variant Instruct-GS2GS, which iteratively edit one rendered image at a time while re-optimising the 3D model. It departs from this sequential paradigm by editing all views simultaneously using a depth-conditioned ControlNet pipeline, and from ViCA-NeRF's blending strategy by introducing an explicit attention-based latent code alignment module that unifies appearance across views through cross-view attention on reference images. The method repurposes DDIM inversion not merely for single-image editing fidelity but as a mechanism to produce geometrically consistent initial noise across the entire multi-view set, a use not previously exploited in 3D editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-view consistency has been the central bottleneck in text-driven 3D editing, causing blurring and artefacts especially in 360-degree scenes; GaussCtrl's simultaneous editing with depth conditioning and attention alignment addresses this directly and reduces editing time from hours to minutes. However, the reliance on depth maps as a geometric anchor means the method cannot handle edits requiring significant topology or shape changes (e.g., turning a bear into a giraffe), and its quality is ultimately bounded by the underlying 2D diffusion model's capabilities. Open questions include whether the attention alignment strategy generalises to other 3D representations beyond 3DGS, whether it can be extended to handle structural edits, and whether the CLIP_dir metric's known failures motivate a better evaluation protocol for 3D editing quality.
