## Problem

Diffusion models can memorize and reproduce training images, posing privacy risks, yet existing mitigation strategies either target only the text modality in cross-modal generation (e.g., caption augmentation) or rely on data corruption, leaving the visual modality unaddressed. This paper seeks a generic, fundamental training-level approach that reduces memorization for both unconditional and text-conditional diffusion models without requiring a blacklist of sensitive content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242321/cropped_page4_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes Gaussian noise as input and produces 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (AFHQ-DOG) RGB images; for text-conditional generation, it takes a text prompt plus Gaussian noise and produces 256×256 images (LAION-10k). During training, the AGC module dynamically inspects per-sample losses within each mini-batch and can zero out the gradient contribution of samples whose loss falls below a threshold fraction of the running per-timestep average, effectively skipping them without altering the input format or output structure.

## Method

The IET-AGC framework combines two mechanisms. First, Iterative Ensemble Training (IET) splits the dataset into K class-balanced shards, trains a separate diffusion model on each shard, and periodically aggregates all shard models by weight averaging, repeating this train-merge cycle throughout training to reorganize stored knowledge and prevent over-optimization on any single subset. Second, Anti-Gradient Control (AGC) exploits the empirical observation that memorized images exhibit significantly lower denoising losses than non-memorized ones; it maintains a per-timestep memory bank updated via exponential moving average and computes a ratio r = L(x,t,ε;θ)/l_t for each sample, masking the loss to zero whenever r < λ, thereby excluding the most easily memorized samples from gradient updates. The two components are complementary: IET alone reduces memorization modestly while improving FID, AGC alone reduces memorization more aggressively but degrades FID, and their combination achieves the best trade-off, cutting memorized quantity by up to 87.3% while slightly improving generation quality.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, IET-AGC reduces the memorized quantity MQ₀.₄ from 111 (default DDPM) to 14 (an 87.3% reduction) while simultaneously improving FID from 8.81 to 8.34. On CIFAR-100, MQ₀.₄ drops from 429 to 144 (66.4% reduction) with FID improving from 9.29 to 8.51. On AFHQ-DOG, MQ₀.₄ decreases from 12,344 to 1,811 (85.3% reduction) with FID improving from 23.59 to 22.2. For text-conditional generation, fine-tuning Stable Diffusion with IET-AGC on LAION-10k achieves the best similarity score of 0.37 (vs. 0.64 default) while maintaining the highest CLIP score of 31.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IET-AGC outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across all three unconditional datasets. Compared to default DDPM training, IET-AGC reduces MQ₀.₄ by 87.3% (CIFAR-10), 66.4% (CIFAR-100), and 85.3% (AFHQ-DOG) while also improving FID by 0.47, 0.78, and 1.39 points respectively. Adding Gaussian noise to training images fails to reduce memorization (MQ₀.₄ actually increases from 111 to 197 on CIFAR-10) and catastrophically degrades image quality (FID jumps to 94.61). DP-SGD with gradient clipping and noise addition also increases memorization (MQ₀.₄: 148 vs. 111) and worsens FID (12.55 vs. 8.81) on CIFAR-10, and was not evaluated on the other datasets. Ambient Diffusion [7] achieves the second-best MQ₀.₄ on CIFAR-10 (22 vs. 14 for IET-AGC) but at a significant quality cost (FID 11.7 vs. 8.34), and was likewise only tested on CIFAR-10. For text-conditional generation (Table 3), IET-AGC fine-tuning of Stable Diffusion achieves the lowest similarity score (0.37) and highest CLIP score (31.3), outperforming both train-time (MC: 0.42, RC: 0.57, CWR: 0.61) and test-time (RT: 0.52, CWR: 0.58, GNI: 0.62) mitigation strategies from Somepalli et al. [27].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the growing body of work identifying memorization in diffusion models (Somepalli et al., Carlini et al., Wen et al.) and departs from prior mitigation strategies that focused on text-modality augmentation (random caption replacement, token shuffling) or specific content blacklisting. The Iterative Ensemble Training component is explicitly motivated by Federated Learning's parameter-averaging paradigm, repurposing it for privacy rather than distributed training. The Anti-Gradient Control module introduces a loss-ratio-based filtering mechanism that, to our knowledge, has no direct precedent in the diffusion memorization literature, though it draws on the general observation that over-optimized samples exhibit anomalously low training loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical applicability of IET-AGC as a short fine-tuning procedure (2 epochs) for existing pre-trained models makes it a low-cost retrofit for deployment, which is significant given the prohibitive cost of retraining large diffusion models from scratch. The finding that memorization reduction can coexist with slight FID improvement challenges the assumed quality-privacy tradeoff, though the margin is small and the threshold λ still requires manual tuning per dataset. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger datasets (ImageNet-scale), whether the IID shard assumption holds for real-world heterogeneous data, and how to automate threshold selection without extensive grid search.
