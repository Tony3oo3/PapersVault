## Problem

Supervised adversarial attack detection methods require large labeled datasets of adversarial/normal image pairs, suffer significant performance degradation when deployed on unseen domains or novel attack algorithms, and prototype-based approaches neglect the rich instance-level semantic relationships within each prototype cluster. This paper addresses these gaps by proposing a fully self-supervised framework that learns discriminative representations from unlabelled normal images alone, enabling robust detection across diverse and previously unseen attack types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241602/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes standard RGB images as input (trained on 50,000 ImageNet images, evaluated on 10,000 images each from ImageNet-R, CIFAR-10, COCO, and CIFAR-100) and produces a binary adversarial/clean detection label. Training requires only unlabelled normal images—no paired adversarial samples are needed. The encoder outputs a 128-dimensional L2-normalized feature vector per image, and detection is performed by comparing the learned representation against prototype and instance embeddings stored in the discrimination bank. Seven attack algorithms (FGSM, PGD, SSAH, DeepFool, BIM, CW, JSMA) are applied at test time to generate adversarial inputs.

## Method

The framework combines three contrastive losses—pixel mapping (L_PM), prototype-wise contrastive estimation (L_PCE), and instance-wise contrastive learning (L_ICL)—trained jointly on a PAA-ResNet encoder. L_PM uses optimally selected data augmentation pairs to learn invariant pixel-level embeddings; L_PCE clusters embeddings around density-peak prototypes with a per-cluster concentration parameter γ that balances tight and loose clusters; L_ICL introduces a discrimination bank (size M×B×D) that stores per-instance embeddings linked to their nearest prototype, enabling fine-grained instance separation within each prototype cluster. The PAA encoder replaces convolutional layers in ResNet-50 bottleneck blocks with two parallel multi-head axial-attention sub-blocks (one for height-axis, one for width-axis), allowing simultaneous training on two GPUs and reducing computational complexity relative to full 2D self-attention. The overall loss is L = L_PM + λ₁·L_PCE + λ₂·L_ICL, with PCE and ICL components removed at inference, yielding a lightweight detector with 26.8M–45.9M parameters and 6.2–10.1 ms latency.

## Results

On ImageNet-R, the proposed PAA-ResNet-L achieves 93.8% detection rate on clean images and 86.8% on attacked images, outperforming the strongest self-supervised baseline DINOV2 (92.5%/82.8%) by 1.3 and 4.0 percentage points respectively, while using only 45.9M parameters versus DINOV2's 1.0B (≈22× fewer) and 10.1 ms inference latency versus 572.0 ms (≈56× faster). The smaller PAA-ResNet-S variant (26.8M params, 6.2 ms) still reaches 92.0%/83.1%, surpassing all supervised baselines. On CIFAR-10 and COCO, PAA-ResNet-L achieves 94.8%/89.0% and 91.7%/85.6% respectively, maintaining consistent leads over all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest self-supervised baseline DINOV2, PAA-ResNet-L gains +1.3% clean and +4.0% attacked detection rate on ImageNet-R while using 22× fewer parameters and 56× lower inference latency. Compared to the next-best SSL method Unicom (91.4%/80.5%), the improvement is +2.4%/+6.3%. Against supervised attack-detection methods, the margins are larger: PAA-ResNet-L exceeds DTBA (84.2%/62.3%) by +9.6%/+24.5%, sim-DNN (79.2%/60.3%) by +14.6%/+26.5%, and ESMAF (69.8%/53.7%) by +24.0%/+33.1%. Even the smallest variant PAA-ResNet-S (26.8M params) outperforms all supervised methods and the lightweight SSL baselines TLC (83.5%/71.7%) and SimCat (85.1%/72.9%) by +8.5%/+11.4% and +6.9%/+10.2% respectively. On CIFAR-10, PAA-ResNet-L leads DINOV2 by +0.5%/+2.3% and Unicom by +2.2%/+4.9%; on COCO, it leads DINOV2 by +0.0%/+1.7% and Unicom by +1.5%/+2.8%, confirming consistent gains across diverse image distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of self-supervised contrastive learning (building on InfoNCE, MAE, and DINOv2-style representation learning) and adversarial attack detection, a task previously dominated by supervised reconstruction-based methods. It departs from standard axial-attention by proposing a parallel variant (PAA) that processes height and width axes on separate GPUs, reducing per-block computational cost compared to both full self-attention and the original axial-attention formulation. The discrimination bank conceptually extends memory-bank ideas from contrastive learning into a prototype-instance hierarchy, going beyond prior prototype-only detection methods that neglect intra-prototype instance diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for labeled adversarial/normal pairs during training while simultaneously achieving lower inference latency (10 ms vs. 572 ms for DINOv2) and higher detection accuracy, making real-time deployment in safety-critical systems more feasible. The robustness evaluation showing less performance degradation on unseen datasets compared to supervised baselines addresses a key deployment concern. Open questions include whether the discrimination bank scales to video or multi-modal adversarial detection, whether the optimal-transformation selection in pixel mapping generalizes to truly novel attack families beyond the seven evaluated, and whether the PAA encoder's efficiency advantage persists at much larger input resolutions.
