## Problem

Existing 3D object removal methods rely on NeRF-based representations, which suffer from slow training and rendering speeds impractical for interactive editing. While 3D Gaussian Splatting offers real-time rendering, its discrete Gaussian primitives introduce two unaddressed challenges for object removal: geometric inconsistency in the removal region due to noisy underlying geometry, and texture incoherence across viewing angles in the inpainted area.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240055/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-view posed images with corresponding binary object masks (obtainable via video segmentation or 3D annotation) and produces a Scaffold-GS-based 3D Gaussian representation of the scene with the object removed, enabling consistent novel view synthesis. One reference view is 2D-inpainted to provide a single clean training target, while monocular depth estimates from all views serve as auxiliary geometric supervision. The rendered output is a full RGB image plus depth map per viewpoint, supervised by combined color, depth, and total-variation losses.

## Method

GScream builds on Scaffold-GS (anchor-based Gaussian organization) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, an online depth alignment strategy uses monocular depth estimation as geometric supervision: rendered depth is aligned to estimated depth via learned scale and shift parameters, with a weighted loss that applies full supervision on the inpainted reference view but only background-region supervision on other views, plus a total-variation smoothness term. Second, a bidirectional cross-attention feature regularization module samples 3D Gaussian anchors whose 2D projections fall inside and outside the mask, then performs shared-parameter cross-attention between the two feature sets (in-painted anchors attend to surrounding anchors and vice versa) to propagate texture information in 3D space before rendering. This 3D feature interaction complements 2D image supervision by ensuring texture coherence in occluded regions that 2D priors alone cannot resolve.

## Results

On the SPIn-NeRF dataset, GScream achieves a PSNR of 20.49, SSIM of 0.58, LPIPS of 0.28, and FID of 36.72, outperforming all NeRF-based baselines across every reported metric. Masked-region PSNR reaches 15.84 and masked LPIPS drops to 0.54, indicating strong reconstruction quality specifically in the removed-object region. Training completes in approximately 1.2 hours, which is 1.5× faster than SPIn-NeRF (~3.0 h) and 4.0× faster than OR-NeRF (~6.0 h), while also delivering superior rendering quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GScream outperforms SPIn-NeRF on every metric: PSNR improves from 20.18 to 20.49, SSIM from 0.46 to 0.58, LPIPS from 0.47 to 0.28, FID from 58.78 to 36.72, and training time drops from ~3.0 h to ~1.2 h. Against OR-NeRF, GScream similarly leads on PSNR (20.49 vs 20.32), SSIM (0.58 vs 0.54), LPIPS (0.28 vs 0.35), FID (36.72 vs 38.69), and is 4× faster to train (~1.2 h vs ~6.0 h). The comparison with View-Sub is limited to masked LPIPS due to unavailable code, where View-Sub reports 0.45 versus GScream's 0.54, meaning View-Sub retains a slight edge on that single metric; however, GScream's overall efficiency advantage (real-time 3DGS rendering vs. NeRF ray-marching) is substantial. Notably, GScream achieves these gains without using the patch-based LPIPS loss that both SPIn-NeRF and OR-NeRF incorporate in their training objectives, suggesting the depth-guided geometry and cross-attention feature propagation provide stronger perceptual quality intrinsically.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and object removal, departing from the dominant NeRF-based approaches (SPIn-NeRF, OR-NeRF, View-Sub) that rely heavily on 2D inpainting priors to guide scene completion. It builds on Scaffold-GS as a lightweight anchor-based 3DGS backbone and introduces two key departures: monocular depth supervision for geometric consistency (borrowing the online scale-shift alignment from monocular depth literature) and a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism that exploits the explicit, addressable nature of Gaussian primitives to propagate texture features from visible to inpainted regions in 3D space. Unlike prior 3DGS editing work like GaussianEditor, which applies generic 2D losses, GScream specifically targets the geometry-accuracy and texture-coherence gaps that arise when removing objects from discrete Gaussian representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in closing the efficiency gap for 3D object removal: achieving competitive or superior quality to NeRF-based methods while training 1.5–4× faster makes interactive or batch object removal feasible in production pipelines. The cross-attention feature propagation strategy opens a broader direction for leveraging the explicit, per-primitive structure of 3DGS for 3D-aware editing tasks beyond removal (e.g., relighting, material transfer). However, the method still depends on a single 2D inpainted reference image as a content prior, and the quantitative gains from the cross-attention module are modest (masked PSNR +0.21), leaving open whether this 3D feature interaction paradigm scales to multi-object removal, unbounded scenes, or dynamic content without additional supervision.
