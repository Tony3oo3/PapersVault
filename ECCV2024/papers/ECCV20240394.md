## Problem

Scene text image super-resolution (STISR) must recover fine text strokes from severely blurred low-resolution images, but existing text-prior methods rely on a text recognizer at inference, whose recognition errors propagate as noisy priors and degrade reconstruction quality. The paper asks whether a text recognizer is truly necessary for STISR and explores replacing it with a generative latent text diffusion model that produces text embeddings directly from the low-resolution image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240394/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution scene text image (bicubically upsampled to match the target HR resolution) and produces a high-resolution scene text image. Internally, the LR image is encoded into a latent space Z^LR, from which a character-level text embedding C ∈ R^{m×d_τ} (37-token vocabulary, max sequence length 26, 512-dim) is generated by the latent text diffusion module. The final image diffusion UNet is conditioned on both the concatenated LR image and the generated text embedding via cross-attention, requiring no ground-truth text tokens during training—only HR-LR image pairs.

## Method

DCDM chains two diffusion models: a Latent Text Diffusion (LTD) UNet that denoises character-level text embeddings conditioned on the LR latent space via cross-attention, and an Image Diffusion (IDM) UNet that denoises the HR image conditioned on both the LR image (concatenated) and the generated text embedding (injected through cross-attention layers). A Character-Level CLIP (CL-CLIP) module, comprising a CRNN text encoder and a ViT visual encoder, is trained with contrastive loss to align HR-derived text embeddings with LR image representations, providing the supervision signal for the LTD without requiring explicit text labels. The LTD uses LDM-style training (1000 timesteps, DDIM sampling at 250 steps) while the IDM follows DDPM training (1000 timesteps, DDIM at 250 steps), both with MSE loss and Adam optimization. The key architectural distinction from prior text-prior methods is that the text prior is generated by a diffusion process rather than a deterministic recognizer, and the entire pipeline operates without ground-truth text tokens during training.

## Results

On the TextZoom dataset, DCDM achieves average recognition accuracy of 55.5% (CRNN), 63.4% (MORAN), and 65.8% (ASTER), outperforming all generic SR, text-backbone, and text-prior baselines. In terms of image quality, DCDM attains the best average PSNR of 22.87 dB and SSIM of 79.54×10⁻² on TextZoom. On the Real-CE dataset, DCDM achieves the highest PSNR (18.91 for 4×, 19.23 for 2×), SSIM (50.90 for 4×, 58.12 for 2×), and recognition accuracy (25.49% for 4×, 31.94% for 2×) when trained on Real-CE, surpassing all compared STISR methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCDM outperforms all generic image SR methods (SRCNN, SRResNet, RCAN, SAN, HAN) by 15–20 points in average CRNN accuracy, confirming that text-specific conditioning is essential. Against text-backbone networks (TSRN, TBSRN, PCAN) and the stroke-aware method TG, DCDM gains 7–17 points in average accuracy across all three recognizers. Among text-prior methods (TPGSR, TATT, C3-STISR), DCDM leads by 2–4 points in average accuracy, showing that a learned latent text diffusion prior surpasses a recognizer-based text prior. The closest competitor is TCDM [23], a diffusion-based method that uses ground-truth text during training; DCDM and TCDM are nearly tied (DCDM 55.5% vs. TCDM 55.7% CRNN avg; 63.4% vs. 62.2% MORAN avg; 65.8% vs. 65.5% ASTER avg), with DCDM winning on MORAN and ASTER averages while TCDM edges ahead on CRNN and ASTER-Hard. Notably, DCDM achieves this without using ground-truth text tokens during training, whereas TCDM does. On the Real-CE dataset, DCDM consistently outperforms TSRN, TPGSR, TBSRN, and TATT across both 2× and 4× upscaling in PSNR, SSIM, and recognition accuracy, regardless of whether trained on TextZoom or Real-CE.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCDM builds on the latent diffusion model (LDM) architecture of Rombach et al. and the DDPM denoising framework of Ho et al., repurposing both into a two-stage pipeline where a text-diffusion UNet generates character-level embeddings that condition an image-diffusion UNet. It departs from the dominant text-prior STISR line (TPGSR, TATT, C3-STISR, TCDM) by replacing the inference-time text recognizer with a learned latent text diffusion model, and introduces a character-level CLIP (CL-CLIP) module for contrastive alignment between text embeddings and LR image latents. The closest concurrent work, StableCascade (Würstchen), also uses two-stage LDMs but for image-to-image generation rather than image-to-text-to-image conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper poses and answers the question of whether a text recognizer is necessary at inference for STISR, concluding that a latent text diffusion model can substitute for one. However, the empirical evidence is modest: recognition accuracy gains over TCDM are marginal (and TCDM enjoys an unfair advantage of using ground-truth text during training), and the ablation shows the text-diffusion component contributes only 0.1–1.8% accuracy improvement. The significant computational cost of 250 DDIM steps for both diffusion modules limits practical deployment, and the authors acknowledge they could not conduct a fully fair comparison with TCDM. Open questions include whether more efficient samplers or distillation can close the speed gap, and whether the approach generalizes to more complex scene text layouts or multilingual settings beyond the tested English/Chinese benchmarks.
