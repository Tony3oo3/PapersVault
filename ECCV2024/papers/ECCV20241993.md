## Problem

Existing data attribution methods for diffusion models (GenDataAttribution, D-TRAK) are designed for post-hoc analysis without access to the training process, which is practical for expensive base-model training but leaves valuable training-dynamics information unused in fine-tuning/customization scenarios. This paper asks whether directly monitoring a diffusion model's internal representations during customization can yield more accurate and granular attributions than post-hoc gradient- or contrastive-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241993/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a customization training set of image-prompt pairs, a set of monitoring prompts (noise-prompt pairs) reserved before fine-tuning, and the evolving cross-attention V tensors extracted at each training iteration. It produces (1) an attribution table M where each column is a normalized probability vector over training samples for one monitored generation, and (2) a Siamese attribution model (CLIP embedder + trainable Scaler layer) that maps any unseen generated image and any customization image to shifted cosine-similarity scores, extending attribution beyond the monitored prompts.

## Method

MONTRAGE operates in two stages. First, during fine-tuning (batch size 1), it monitors the cross-attention value tensor V = W_V·c for a fixed set of monitoring prompts at every iteration, recording the L1-norm change ΔV into a cumulative attribution table M whose rows index training samples and columns index monitored generations; this avoids the full T-step reverse-diffusion overhead while still capturing text-image interaction dynamics. Second, a Siamese network is trained on M using a novel Adaptive DML Loss that combines L1 regression on positive and negative pair scores with an Adaptive Triplet Loss whose per-pair margin m_i is derived from the ground-truth attribution gap (GT_ap − GT_np), enabling the model to learn both between-concept separation and within-concept ranking granularity simultaneously.

## Results

MONTRAGE is evaluated at two granularity levels: within-concept (via LDS Accuracy with a random baseline of 0.5) and between-concepts (via recall@K, precision@K, and Spearman rank correlation). On the within-concept evaluation, MONTRAGE achieves a clear advantage over competitors in the high-accuracy range (η > 0.7) and outperforms both GenDataAttribution and D-TRAK in Area Under the Curve (AUC) across the full [0.5, 1] accuracy range. On the between-concepts evaluation, MONTRAGE consistently achieves the highest scores across all retrieval metrics on both CustomConcepts101 and Artchive, with the largest margin appearing in mixed-concept experiments (K=10) versus un-mixed (K=5). Performance on the Artchive artistic-style dataset is lower for all methods compared to CustomConcepts101, underscoring the greater difficulty of attributing style-level influences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MONTRAGE is compared against two state-of-the-art diffusion-model attribution methods: GenDataAttribution [32], which uses contrastive learning over thousands of single-image customized models, and D-TRAK [34], which extends influence-function-style gradient/Hessian analysis to diffusion models. MONTRAGE outperforms both baselines on every reported metric: within-concept LDS Accuracy (AUC over the η sweep) and between-concepts recall@K, precision@K, and Spearman rank correlation, on both CustomConcepts101 and Artchive. The margin over both baselines is notably larger in mixed-concept experiments (images containing two concepts) than in un-mixed single-concept experiments, indicating MONTRAGE's particular strength in disentangling multi-concept contributions. Both baselines show lower absolute performance on the Artchive artistic-style dataset relative to CustomConcepts101, but MONTRAGE maintains its relative advantage in that harder setting as well. A qualitative base-model analysis (100K LAION images) further demonstrates that MONTRAGE's attribution embedding generalizes beyond its fine-tuning setting, a capability neither baseline is designed to provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

MONTRAGE sits at the intersection of two prior research threads: training-dynamics-based data attribution (Pruthi et al. [24], which traces per-iteration weight changes) and diffusion-model-specific attribution (GenDataAttribution [32], D-TRAK [34], DataInf [22]). All existing diffusion attribution methods treat the model as a post-hoc black box, avoiding training access due to the cost of base-model training. MONTRAGE departs from this by exploiting the practical availability of training access in the fine-tuning/customization setting, and is the first to monitor cross-attention value tensors (previously used for generation analysis and control [9,17,21]) as a proxy for attribution signals. The Siamese attribution model follows the DML framework of GenDataAttribution [32] but introduces an Adaptive Triplet Loss that encodes within-concept granularity from the attribution table.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically urgent gap: copyright attribution in the fine-tuning/customization pipeline, where training access is naturally available yet prior methods ignore it. The mixed-concept attribution capability is particularly relevant for legal scenarios involving multiple artists' styles. Key limitations include dependence on training access (inapplicable to base models), validation only on Custom Diffusion fine-tuning, and the within-concept LDS evaluation being constrained by small per-concept image counts. An open question is whether MONTRAGE's training-dynamics signal can be combined with post-hoc methods (e.g., D-TRAK) to form a unified attribution framework covering both base and customized models.
