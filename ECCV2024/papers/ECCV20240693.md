## Problem

Existing grasping motion synthesis methods either depend on expensive 3D hand-object interaction data, require time-intensive optimization to generate reference poses, or are limited to objects seen during training, making them unable to scale to large collections of unseen objects. Furthermore, no prior method can simultaneously satisfy multiple motion objectives (graspable area, heading direction, wrist rotation, hand position) while maintaining physically stable grasps across diverse hand morphologies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D object point cloud (optionally partitioned into graspable and non-graspable subsets), a parametric hand model (MANO, Shadow, Allegro, or Faive), and user-specified motion objectives (heading direction is mandatory; wrist rotation and midpoint position are optional). It produces a real-time dynamic grasping motion as a sequence of PD-control targets for hand joint torques, executed within a physics simulation. The object geometry is not fed as raw point clouds to the policy but is encoded as per-joint nearest-point distance vectors, making the representation hand-agnostic and shape-agnostic.

## Method

GraspXL formulates grasping as a PPO reinforcement learning problem in RaiSim physics simulation, trained on only 58 objects yet generalizing to over 500k unseen objects. Three key mechanisms enable this: (1) joint distance features that encode local object shape by computing vectors from each finger joint to the nearest graspable/non-graspable surface point, giving the policy shape awareness without object-specific assumptions; (2) an objective-driven guidance scheme that injects the error between current and target heading, wrist rotation, and midpoint directly as bias terms into the wrist's 6-DoF PD controller, accelerating exploration and improving control precision; and (3) a two-phase learning curriculum that first trains on static objects with a high objective reward to learn precise finger configurations, then fine-tunes on dynamic objects with a high grasp reward to achieve stable, non-slip grasps. The reward function is hand-agnostic, combining a goal term (distance to graspable area, heading/rotation/midpoint alignment) and a grasp term (contact ratio, force magnitude capped by object weight, velocity regularization, and an optional MANO anatomy prior).

## Results

On the PartNet test set, GraspXL achieves a 95.0% grasping success rate with a midpoint error of 2.85 cm, heading error of 0.270 rad, and wrist rotation error of 0.306 rad, substantially outperforming the SynH2R baseline (82.3% success, 4.06 cm, 0.522 rad, 0.568 rad). On the more diverse ShapeNet test set, GraspXL maintains 81.0% success with 3.22 cm midpoint error, while SynH2R drops to 65.8% success and 4.49 cm error. At scale, the method generalizes to over 500k unseen Objaverse objects with an 82.2% average success rate, and extends to reconstructed (HOLD) and text-to-3D generated (DreamFusion) objects with 74–88% success rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is SynH2R [Christen et al., ICRA 2024], the only prior method offering controllability over heading direction, which the authors extend with additional optimization terms for wrist rotation, midpoint, and graspable-area contact. GraspXL outperforms SynH2R on every reported metric: on PartNet it improves success rate by +12.7 percentage points (95.0% vs 82.3%), reduces midpoint error by ~30% (2.85 vs 4.06 cm), heading error by ~48% (0.270 vs 0.522 rad), and wrist rotation error by ~46% (0.306 vs 0.568 rad), while nearly doubling the contact ratio (86.7% vs 53.4%). On ShapeNet the gap widens further in success rate (+15.2 pp) and objective errors (heading error 0.292 vs 0.642 rad, a 54% reduction). The non-learning PD baseline (SynH2R-PD) performs worst across all metrics, confirming that the RL policy is essential. A key practical advantage is that GraspXL performs real-time inference in simulation, whereas SynH2R requires a time-consuming per-object optimization to generate reference poses (approximately a week for the full ShapeNet test set). The paper also notes that prior methods (DexVIP, D-Grasp, UniDexGrasp) are limited to 0–100 unseen objects and lack multi-objective control, whereas GraspXL is evaluated on 503,409 novel objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraspXL extends the physics-based RL paradigm for hand-object motion synthesis (D-Grasp, SynH2R, UniDexGrasp) by eliminating the need for reference poses at inference and unifying multiple motion objectives (graspable area, heading direction, wrist rotation, midpoint position) into a single policy. It departs from data-driven approaches (GRAB, GOAL, IMoS) that are constrained by expensive 3D hand-object capture and limited training distributions, and from optimization-based methods (SynH2R, UniDexGrasp) that require time-consuming per-object pose generation. The key conceptual departure is the hand-agnostic reward formulation combined with joint distance features, which allows the same policy architecture to transfer across MANO, Shadow, Allegro, and Faive hands without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate physically plausible grasps for 500k+ unseen objects in real-time, without any hand-object interaction data, addresses a critical scalability bottleneck for animation pipelines and robotic grasping research. The demonstrated robustness to reconstruction artifacts (HOLD) and text-to-3D generation noise (DreamFusion) suggests practical applicability to emerging content creation workflows. Open questions include real-world sim-to-real transfer, bimanual or articulated-object manipulation, and whether the curriculum approach generalizes to longer-horizon manipulation tasks beyond single-object grasping.
