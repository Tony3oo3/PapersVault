## Problem

Existing prompt learning methods for vision-language models in domain generalization learn fixed or residualed prompts from source-domain training data, which lack diversity and contain no information about unseen target domains. This limits the transferability of learned prompts under distribution shift, as the prompts cannot adapt to the characteristics of OOD test data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240079/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, which is encoded by a frozen CLIP image encoder (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) into a feature vector. The CGAN generator produces a continuous soft prompt vector of context length 4, which is concatenated with a tokenized class name and passed through the frozen CLIP text encoder. The final output is a softmax class probability computed via cosine similarity between the image embedding and the prompt-conditioned text embeddings, with a temperature parameter τ.

## Method

SPG reframes prompt learning from a generative perspective by using a Conditional GAN to directly produce soft prompts, rather than learning fixed or residualed prompt vectors. Training proceeds in two stages: first, per-domain prompt labels are learned via cross-entropy on each source domain's data, encoding domain-specific knowledge; second, a CGAN is trained where the generator takes concatenated noise and image embeddings to synthesize soft prompts, while the discriminator distinguishes real domain prompt labels from generated prompts conditioned on image features. At inference, only the generator is used to produce instance-specific soft prompts for unseen target domain images, so domain knowledge resides in the generative model rather than in a static prompt. This design ensures prompt diversity across images and allows the model to dynamically adapt prompts to the visual content of each test sample, unlike prior methods that apply a single fixed or MLP-residual prompt.

## Results

On the multi-source domain generalization task (leave-one-domain-out protocol), SPG achieves a mean accuracy of 67.5% with ResNet50 and 74.7% with ViT-B/16 across five DG benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita, DomainNet). With ResNet50, SPG surpasses the strongest CLIP-based baseline CoCoOp by 1.2% on average and zero-shot CLIP by 5.2%. With ViT-B/16, SPG edges out MaPLe (74.4%) and CoOp/VPT/DPL (73.6%) by 0.1–1.1% on average. The largest gains appear on TerraIncognita, where SPG improves over zero-shot CLIP by 13.7% (RN50) and 16.4% (ViT-B/16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPG outperforms all ten baselines on the multi-source DG task across both backbones. Against traditional DG methods (ERM, MIRO, SWAD), SPG with ResNet50 leads by 0.6–3.7% on average, though traditional methods still dominate on TerraIncognita (50.0–50.4% vs. SPG's 37.5%), likely because CLIP was not pre-trained on similar data. Among CLIP-based prompt-learning methods, SPG beats CoCoOp (66.3→67.5 RN50; 73.2→74.7 ViT) and DPL (66.0→67.5; 73.6→74.7) by 1.2–1.5% on average, and surpasses CoOp, VP, VPT, and MaPLe by 1.1–5.2%. The margin over zero-shot CLIP is largest on TerraIncognita (+13.7% RN50, +16.4% ViT) and DomainNet (+3.7% RN50, +3.5% ViT), while on PACS and OfficeHome the gap over the best prompt-learning baselines is modest (0.1–1.1%). SPG also wins on single-source DG (Table 2: 62.8% avg vs. CoOp 61.4%) and cross-dataset generalization (Table 3: 73.8% avg vs. ZS-CLIP 72.0%), confirming consistent gains across evaluation protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the soft prompt learning paradigm introduced by CoOp (Zhou et al., IJCV 2022) and the conditional prompt extensions of CoCoOp and DPL, which learn fixed or image-conditioned residual prompts via MLPs. It departs from this lineage by replacing the learned prompt parameters entirely with a generative model—specifically a CGAN (Mirza & Osindero, 2014)—that produces instance-specific soft prompts at inference time. The two-stage training (domain prompt labels followed by CGAN pre-training) is the key architectural departure, transferring domain knowledge from the prompts into the generator's weights rather than storing it in a small set of learnable vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPG establishes a new prompt-learning paradigm where a generative model replaces hand-designed or learned prompt vectors, opening a path toward more expressive and diverse prompt generation in VLMs. However, the performance gains over strong CLIP-based baselines (CoCoOp, MaPLe) are modest (~1.5% average), and the generative backbone is a basic CGAN, leaving open whether more powerful generators (diffusion models, transformers) would yield substantially larger improvements. The paper also acknowledges that CLIP's pre-training distribution mismatch with datasets like TerraIncognita limits gains, and the VLCS cross-dataset result shows the approach still struggles under extreme distribution shift.
