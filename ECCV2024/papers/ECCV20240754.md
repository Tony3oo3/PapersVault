## Problem

Existing speech-driven 3D talking head methods are locked to a fixed mesh topology, requiring all faces to be registered to a specific template (e.g., FLAME) before animation. This registration step is costly, prevents online inference, and limits the pool of usable training data to a single registered dataset. ScanTalk addresses this by enabling direct animation of arbitrary-topology meshes, including raw 3D scans with holes and noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240754/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a neutral 3D face mesh of any topology (any vertex count and connectivity, including raw scans with missing geometry) and a speech audio snippet as input. It outputs a sequence of per-vertex 3D displacement fields whose length matches the audio duration; adding each displacement to the neutral mesh yields the animated frame. Because the model predicts deformations rather than absolute vertex positions, the output topology is always identical to the input neutral mesh, and GPU memory scales linearly with vertex count.

## Method

ScanTalk is an encoder-decoder framework built around DiffusionNet, a discretization-agnostic surface operator that replaces fixed-topology graph convolutions. The face encoder applies precomputed closed-form surface operators (Cotangent Laplacian, Eigenbasis, Mass Matrix, Spatial Gradient Matrix) to the neutral mesh, then uses a DiffusionNet MLP stack to extract per-vertex geometric descriptors. The audio encoder uses a pretrained HuBERT model followed by a multilayer Bidirectional-LSTM for temporal coherence, producing per-frame audio features. These are concatenated per-vertex with the face descriptors and fed into a reversed DiffusionNet decoder that predicts the per-vertex deformation. Training is supervised with a vertex-wise MSE loss, and the model can be trained jointly across multiple datasets with different topologies, since only intra-sequence topology consistency is required.

## Results

On VOCAset with single-dataset training, ScanTalk achieves the best LVE (3.012) and MVE (0.861) among all compared methods, outperforming CodeTalker (LVE 3.549) and FaceDiffuser (LVE 4.350). In the multi-dataset setting, ScanTalk remains competitive across all three datasets, achieving the best MVE on BIWI6 (2.057) and competitive LVE on Multiface (2.435). The key differentiator is that ScanTalk is the first model to animate arbitrary-topology meshes, including raw 3D scans, while matching the performance of topology-fixed baselines on registered data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VOCAset with single-dataset training, ScanTalk achieves the best LVE (3.012 vs. CodeTalker's 3.549) and MVE (0.861 vs. CodeTalker's 0.888), beating all five baselines on lip and overall vertex accuracy. In the multi-dataset setting, ScanTalk is competitive but not uniformly superior: on BIWI6 it matches FaceFormer (LVE 4.044 vs. 4.085) and SelfTalk (LVE 4.044 vs. 3.628, where SelfTalk wins), while on Multiface it trails FaceFormer on FDD (32.202 vs. 20.239) but leads on LVE (2.435 vs. 2.451). The user study confirms these quantitative findings: ScanTalk is preferred over FaceFormer (82.7% naturalness), CodeTalker (57.3%), and FaceDiffuser (90.7%), but loses to SelfTalk (44.0%) and ground truth (44.0%) in A/B comparisons. The paper's central contribution is not a uniform performance win but the ability to animate arbitrary-topology meshes and raw 3D scans—something none of the five baselines can do—while maintaining comparable quality on registered data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScanTalk builds directly on DiffusionNet (Sharp et al., 2022), a discretization-agnostic surface learning architecture, repurposing it as both encoder and decoder for speech-driven mesh deformation. It departs from the entire line of speech-driven 3D facial animation methods (VOCA, FaceFormer, SelfTalk, CodeTalker, FaceDiffuser), which all operate on fixed-topology registered meshes and require new data to be fitted to a canonical topology before inference. The audio pipeline follows recent work (FaceXHubert, FaceDiffuser) in using HuBERT with a BiLSTM for temporal consistency, while the key architectural departure is replacing topology-dependent graph convolutions or transformers with DiffusionNet's intrinsic surface operators (Cotangent Laplacian, Eigenbasis, Mass Matrix, Spatial Gradient).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the fixed-topology constraint, ScanTalk enables direct animation of raw 3D scans and arbitrary meshes without costly registration, opening the door to real-time applications where preprocessing is prohibitive. However, on registered data the quantitative results are comparable rather than superior to SOTA, and multi-dataset training shows degradation on some benchmarks, suggesting the topology-agnostic formulation still carries a small representational cost. Open questions include performance on very high-resolution or heavily degraded scans, real-time inference speed at high vertex counts, and whether the approach extends to other modalities (e.g., text-driven or expression-driven animation) beyond speech.
