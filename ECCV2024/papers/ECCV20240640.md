## Problem

Translating third-person (exocentric) images into first-person (egocentric) views is a challenging instance of novel view synthesis where existing methods fail: geometry-free generative models (GANs, plain diffusion) produce geometrically incorrect outputs on complex scenes, while NeRF-based approaches cannot converge on the Ego-Exo4D benchmark's diverse, dynamic scenes with only a handful of views per scene. The paper addresses this by introducing a lightweight 3D geometry prior that is both easy to train and generalizable, integrated into a diffusion framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240640/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 exocentric RGB image and a known 4×4 relative camera pose (exo-to-ego) as input, and produces a 256×256 egocentric RGB image. Depth is estimated on-the-fly from the exocentric image via a pretrained MiDaS model with two learnable calibration scalars, and semantic features are extracted by a frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 encoder. The relative camera pose is assumed available, consistent with standard NVS task formulations.

## Method

4DIFF incorporates 3D geometry into a DiT-B/2 diffusion denoiser through two mechanisms. First, egocentric point cloud rasterization uses the MiDaS depth map to back-project the exocentric image and its DINOv2 features into a 3D point cloud, then differentially renders (splats) it into an egocentric prior image that provides layout cues to the diffusion model. Second, 3D-aware rotary cross-attention is inserted after each self-attention layer in the DiT; it rotates key tokens from the exocentric feature map by the 3D angle between the query (ego) and key (exo) coordinates relative to the ego camera, so that attention weights reflect both semantic similarity and spatial proximity in 3D. The model is trained with standard L2 diffusion denoising loss over 1000 timesteps and sampled with 20 DDIM steps at inference, leveraging pretrained depth and feature encoders for strong generalization to unseen scenes.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D-VT test set (900 takes), 4DIFF achieves LPIPS 0.349, DISTS 0.207, CLIP score 79.72, PSNR 16.65, and SSIM 0.592, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The headline improvement is a 3.6% reduction in LPIPS and 1.9% reduction in DISTS over the second-best method (3DiM). On unseen scenes, 4DIFF maintains its lead (LPIPS 0.427 vs. 0.436 for 3DiM), demonstrating robust generalization to novel environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4DIFF outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against the GAN-based pix2pix, 4DIFF reduces LPIPS from 0.372 to 0.349 and improves CLIP score from 68.85 to 79.72, reflecting the advantage of diffusion-based generation over GANs for complex scenes. The NeRF-based GNT performs worst overall (LPIPS 0.482, CLIP 63.75), attributed to its limited capacity to model hundreds of diverse scenes with only a few views per scene. Among diffusion-based methods, 4DIFF beats the geometry-free DiT by 6.3% in LPIPS (0.349 vs. 0.412) and 1.7% in CLIP score, and beats the geometry-aware 3DiM by 3.6% in LPIPS (0.349 vs. 0.385) and 1.9% in DISTS (0.207 vs. 0.226). The gap over 3DiM is particularly meaningful because 3DiM already incorporates 3D geometry via NeRF, suggesting that 4DIFF's lightweight point-cloud rasterization plus 3D-aware rotary cross-attention provides more effective geometric conditioning than NeRF-based rendering in this diverse, dynamic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT diffusion transformer architecture and extends it with two geometry-aware mechanisms: egocentric point cloud rasterization (using MiDaS depth + differentiable splatting) and 3D-aware rotary cross-attention inspired by RoPE. It deliberately departs from the NeRF-based geometry pipeline (GeNVS, SSDNeRF, GNT) that prior geometry-aware diffusion NVS methods rely on, arguing that complex radiance-field regression fails to converge on the highly diverse, sparsely-viewed scenes in Ego-Exo4D. The work also extends the Ego-Exo4D exo-to-ego benchmark from object-level synthesis to full-scene image translation, positioning the task as a challenging instance of novel view synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that lightweight point-cloud rasterization, when paired with a sufficiently expressive diffusion model, can provide effective 3D geometry priors without the optimization burden of NeRF—enabling robust generalization to unseen scenes. This challenges the prevailing assumption in geometry-aware diffusion NVS that complex radiance fields are necessary for meaningful geometric conditioning. Key limitations include the assumption of known camera poses, image-only (not video) generation, and reliance on a relatively small DiT-B/2 backbone; the authors note that scaling to a stronger pretrained diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion) and extending to video or object-level synthesis are natural next steps.
