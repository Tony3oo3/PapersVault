## Problem

In-air monocular depth estimation models (e.g., DPT) degrade significantly on underwater images due to the domain gap and fundamentally different image formation physics, exhibiting depth overestimation in distant/blurry regions and poor background edge detection. Training new underwater depth models from scratch is impractical because groundtruth depth acquisition is severely limited in aquatic environments (LiDAR fails, stereo matching degrades, sonar is slow and low-resolution). The paper addresses both challenges simultaneously by transferring in-air model knowledge guided by underwater physics, requiring no groundtruth depth measurements.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single post-processed (non-linear, gamma-corrected) underwater RGB image as input, downsampled to 576×384 during training, and produces an inverse relative depth map. Notably, training requires only 76 white-balanced linear underwater images from the SeaThru-NeRF dataset with no groundtruth depth labels; the physics parameters (beam attenuation coefficients, background light) are estimated per-image from the DPT preliminary depth and the linear image itself. The inverse relative depth output can be converted to metric depth via a per-scene affine transformation (scale and shift) estimated offline from image sequences.

## Method

PUDE initializes from DPT-Hybrid and trains with a two-branch loss: (1) a similarity loss that constrains PUDE outputs to remain close to DPT outputs (trimming the top 30% of pixel differences to set tolerance), preserving structural knowledge; and (2) a physics-informed branch that estimates the underwater imaging formation model parameters (β_c, B_c^∞, scale/shift) from DPT's preliminary depth on clear regions, then applies two novel bound losses derived from the formation equation I = J·t + B∞(1−t). The lower bound loss (L_bl) enforces t ≥ 1 − I/B∞ across all pixels, pulling back overestimated depths in distant blurry regions; the upper bound loss (L_bu) enforces t < (−I + t_D + B∞)/B∞ on pixels identified as likely background (where backscattering dominates, ratio ≥ 0.6), sharpening background edge detection. Training runs only 3 epochs with Adam at lr=1e-5 and early stopping, using no groundtruth depth.

## Results

PUDE achieves the best performance across all metrics on all three test datasets (Sea-thru D3, D5, and SQUID). On D3, PUDE attains δ<1.05 = 0.443, AbsRel = 0.101, RMSE = 0.863, and SILog = 0.027, outperforming DPT (δ<1.05 = 0.421, AbsRel = 0.123, SILog = 0.038) and UDepth (AbsRel = 0.158, SILog = 0.051). On the zero-shot SQUID dataset, PUDE reaches δ<1.05 = 0.387, AbsRel = 0.119, and SILog = 0.032, surpassing all in-air and underwater baselines. The method is trained on only 76 images without ground-truth depth, yet consistently outperforms methods trained on larger datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PUDE outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against DPT (the in-air model it transfers from), PUDE improves δ<1.05 by 0.022 on D3, 0.061 on D5, and 0.042 on SQUID, while reducing AbsRel from 0.123→0.101 (D3) and 0.136→0.119 (SQUID). Against UDepth, the strongest dedicated underwater method, PUDE achieves lower AbsRel (0.101 vs. 0.158 on D3; 0.119 vs. 0.213 on SQUID) and lower SILog (0.027 vs. 0.051 on D3; 0.032 vs. 0.083 on SQUID), though UDepth is marginally competitive on D5 RMSE (1.343 vs. 1.343, tied). MiDaS and MegaDepth, while strong in-air, degrade significantly underwater (e.g., MiDaS AbsRel jumps to 0.408 on D3 and 0.296 on SQUID), confirming the domain gap. UWNET consistently produces the worst error metrics (AbsRel > 0.39 on all datasets), indicating its GAN-based approach struggles with accurate depth magnitude despite low δ<1.05 values suggesting systematic underestimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DPT-Hybrid architecture (Ranftl et al., 2021) as a pre-trained in-air depth estimator and the Sea-thru underwater image formation model (Akkaynak & Treibitz, 2019) for physics parameter extraction, combining them in a novel transfer-learning framework. It departs from prior underwater depth methods (UWNET, UDepth) that rely on GAN-based color restoration or self-supervised frame correspondences, and from physics-based methods that directly solve for depth from the formation model (vulnerable to lighting variation). The key conceptual departure is using the physics model not as a direct depth solver but as a source of inequality constraints (bound losses) that guide fine-tuning of a general-purpose neural network, with the UDCP channel-selection principle (Drews et al., 2016) informing which color channels carry reliable transmission information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck in underwater robotics: the near-impossibility of collecting large ground-truth depth datasets in aquatic environments, requiring only 76 unlabeled images for adaptation. The zero-shot generalization across Sea-thru D3, D5, and SQUID datasets suggests the physics-informed constraints capture transferable underwater imaging properties. However, the approach is contingent on the quality of the source in-air model (demonstrated with MiDaS yielding weaker results), and the physics model's assumptions (constant beam attenuation, static scene) may limit applicability to highly turbid or dynamic underwater scenes. Open questions include whether the bound-loss framework extends to other domain gaps (e.g., thermal, infrared) and whether the 76-image requirement scales gracefully to more diverse water conditions.
