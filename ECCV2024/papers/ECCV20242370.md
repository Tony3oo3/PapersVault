## Problem

Controlling the spectral norm of convolutional layer Jacobians improves generalization, training stability, and robustness, but existing methods either overestimate the norm (e.g., the F4 bound exceeds the true value by 1.7–2.6×) or have computational complexity that grows polynomially with input image resolution. No prior bound is simultaneously tight, differentiable, independent of input size, and applicable to zero-padded and strided convolutions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 4D convolution kernel tensor K ∈ R^{c_out × c_in × h × w} and outputs a scalar upper bound √(hw)·||K||_σ on the spectral norm of the full convolution Jacobian T ∈ R^{c_out·n² × c_in·n²}. The computation requires O(c_in·c_out·h·w) operations and is entirely independent of the spatial resolution n, making it feasible to evaluate at every training step. For regularization, the bounds from all convolutional layers are summed and added to the cross-entropy loss with a coefficient β.

## Method

The key insight is that the tensor spectral norm ||K||_σ — defined as the norm of K as a 4-way multilinear functional — satisfies ||K||_σ ≤ ||T||₂ ≤ √(hw)·||K||_σ for any zero-padded or circular convolution with stride 1. The upper bound is proved by expressing the spectral density matrix F(τ₁,τ₂) as a multilinear contraction of K with complex exponential vectors z₁, z₂, then bounding the resulting supremum by √(hw)·||K||_σ; the lower bound follows from the fact that any unfolding of K is a submatrix of T. The tensor norm is computed via a complex-valued Higher-Order Power Method (HOPM) with O(c_in·c_out·h·w) cost per iteration, and its gradient is available in closed form via real/imaginary decomposition (Proposition 1), making the bound fully differentiable. The result extends to strided convolutions (Theorem 3, via a reshaped-and-padded kernel Q) and to d-dimensional convolutions (Theorem 2). The paper further proposes two orthogonal regularizers — L_2norm penalizing ||Conv(K,K,circular) − I||_σ and L_Ratio penalizing √(hw)·||K||_σ / ||K||_F — that enforce near-orthogonality of convolutions and yield up to 2% top-1 accuracy gain on CIFAR-100.

## Results

The proposed tensor norm (TN) bound approximates the exact spectral norm of convolution Jacobians within a factor of 1.01–1.13×, compared to 1.65–2.65× for the F4 bound [20], across kernel sizes 3×3 to 7×7 and channel counts up to 512. On CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18), TN regularization achieves 74.99% top-1 accuracy (with weight decay), marginally outperforming F4 (74.91%) and the baseline (73.84%). On ImageNet (ResNet-34), TN yields 73.51% top-1 vs. 73.37% baseline and 73.39% F4. The proposed 2norm orthogonal regularizer further improves CIFAR-100 accuracy to 75.99%, surpassing the OCNN baseline [26] (75.32%) by over 0.6 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor F4 [20], TN is provably tighter (Lemma 1 guarantees ||K||_σ ≤ min of unfolding norms) and empirically 1.5–2.5× closer to the exact spectral norm; in classification, TN edges out F4 by 0.08% on CIFAR-100 and 0.12% on ImageNet top-1. Compared to LipBound [1], TN is both more accurate and substantially faster (143.6 s/epoch vs. 242.5 s). Gram iteration [6] and Sedghi et al. [18] both cause out-of-memory errors at practical input sizes, whereas TN remains memory-efficient at O(c_in·c_out·h·w). PowerQR [7] is slightly slower (149.6 s vs. 143.6 s) and requires periodic full recomputation, while TN updates with a single HOPM iteration per step. For orthogonal regularization, the proposed 2norm loss outperforms OCNN [26] by 0.67% top-1 on CIFAR-100 (75.99% vs. 75.32%) at comparable training time (171.8 s vs. 150.1 s per epoch).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Singla & Feizi's 'Fantastic Four' (F4) bound, which used the minimum spectral norm of four specific kernel unfoldings multiplied by √(hw) to upper-bound the convolution Jacobian's spectral norm. The key conceptual departure is replacing this minimum-of-unfoldings with the tensor spectral norm ‖K‖_σ, which by Lemma 1 is provably no larger than any individual unfolding's spectral norm, yielding a strictly tighter bound. The paper also extends the analysis beyond circular convolutions (the setting of F4 and Sedghi et al.) to zero-padded and strided convolutions, and generalizes to arbitrary spatial dimensions, leveraging the spectral density matrix framework from Yi (2022) and the Toeplitz structure of the Jacobian.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TN bound offers a practical, differentiable, input-resolution-independent upper bound that is provably tighter than the prior best (F4) and can be updated with a single HOPM iteration per training step at negligible overhead, making it immediately usable as a regularizer. The orthogonal regularization variants (particularly L_2norm) demonstrate that tighter spectral control translates into measurable generalization gains (>2% top-1 on CIFAR-100). Open questions include whether the √(hw) gap between the bound and the true norm can be further closed, whether HOPM's convergence to the global tensor spectral norm is guaranteed in practice for large kernels, and how these regularizers interact with other architectural choices (e.g., attention layers) beyond standard CNNs.
