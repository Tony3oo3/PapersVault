## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) achieves real-time rendering for static scenes but extends poorly to dynamic scenes: storing full Gaussian parameters per timestep yields O(TN) memory and demands many synchronized views per timestep, making monocular or few-view dynamic reconstruction impractical. The paper asks whether a compact, time-parameterized Gaussian representation can match NeRF-based dynamic view synthesis quality while retaining 3DGS's rendering speed and low memory footprint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242311/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular or multi-view video frames (e.g., 800×800 for D-NeRF, 1352×1014 for DyNeRF, 540×960 for HyperNeRF) with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, plus RAFT-computed forward and backward optical flow between adjacent frames as an auxiliary training signal (excluded for D-NeRF due to camera teleportation). It outputs a set of dynamic 3D Gaussians whose positions and rotations are continuous functions of time, enabling novel-view rendering at up to 118 FPS on a single RTX A6000 GPU without per-timestep parameter storage.

## Method

Each 3D Gaussian's center position is modeled as a Fourier series in normalized time (L harmonics per axis, giving 3(2L+1) parameters) and its rotation quaternion as a linear function of time (8 parameters), while scale, spherical-harmonic color coefficients, and opacity remain time-invariant. This reduces per-Gaussian memory from O(T) to O(L) with L≪T, and the continuous-time formulation inherently enforces smooth motion. Training uses a two-stage schedule: a 3,000-iteration static stage fitting time-invariant parameters and intercepts, followed by a 27,000-iteration dynamic stage optimizing all coefficients. A flow loss is added by differentiating the Fourier position model to obtain 3D scene flow, projecting it to the image plane via the Jacobian, α-blending it into a pseudo optical flow, and matching it to RAFT ground truth with L1 loss, providing spatio-temporal regularization without extra rendering cost. Standard 3DGS splatting rendering, gradient-based densification (splitting/cloning), and opacity-based pruning are retained.

## Results

On the DyNeRF dataset (1,352×1,014 resolution), the method achieves PSNR 30.46, MS-SSIM 0.955, and LPIPS 0.15 at 118 FPS with ~338 MB memory and 1 hour training time. On D-NeRF (synthetic, monocular-like), it reaches PSNR 32.19, MS-SSIM 0.97, LPIPS 0.04 at 150 FPS with ~159 MB. On HyperNeRF (two moving cameras), it attains mean PSNR 25.6 and SSIM 0.890 at 188 FPS, surpassing all compared methods in both quality and speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against K-Planes, the method trades a small quality gap (PSNR 30.46 vs. 31.63, MS-SSIM 0.955 vs. 0.964) for a ~380× speedup (118 vs. 0.31 FPS) and comparable memory. Against V4D, it wins on every metric: higher PSNR (30.46 vs. 28.96), lower LPIPS (0.15 vs. 0.17), ~1070× faster rendering (118 vs. 0.11 FPS), 3.6× less memory (338 MB vs. 1.2 GB), and 4× shorter training. Against the static 3DGS baseline, it substantially improves quality (PSNR 30.46 vs. 20.94, LPIPS 0.15 vs. 0.29) while maintaining similar rendering speed (118 vs. 109 FPS). Against D-3DGS (per-timestep 3DGS), it achieves 6.1 dB higher PSNR, 0.10 lower LPIPS, and 6.8× less memory (338 MB vs. 2.3 GB) at essentially the same FPS. Against Dynamic 3D Gaussians [38], it wins on PSNR (30.46 vs. 27.79), LPIPS (0.15 vs. 0.23), rendering speed (118 vs. 51 FPS), memory (338 MB vs. 6.6 GB, ~20× smaller), and training time (1 hr vs. 2.1 hrs). On HyperNeRF, the method also outperforms TiNeuVox-B in mean PSNR (25.6 vs. 24.3) and SSIM (0.890 vs. 0.837) while rendering ~1340× faster (188 vs. 0.14 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to dynamic scenes by parameterizing Gaussian positions with Fourier series and rotations with linear functions of time, in contrast to Dynamic 3D Gaussians [38] which stores full per-timestep parameters (O(TN) memory). It departs from NeRF-based dynamic methods (D-NeRF, K-Planes, V4D, HyperNeRF) that achieve competitive quality but suffer from slow MLP-based rendering, by retaining the explicit point-based rendering pipeline of 3DGS. A novel flow loss supervises the learned scene flow against RAFT-estimated optical flow, providing temporal consistency without additional rendering cost, and a two-stage (static-then-dynamic) optimization strategy stabilizes training from sparse views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves real-time dynamic rendering (118 FPS at 1352×1014) with memory independent of video length, making it practical for long sequences and monocular capture setups that prior dynamic 3DGS methods cannot handle. However, the representation assumes all Gaussians persist for the full duration, limiting modeling of topological changes (object appearance/disappearance), and static color parameters cannot capture illumination changes. Open questions include whether adaptive Gaussian lifetimes, time-varying colors, or distillation from NeRFs can close the remaining quality gap on highly deformable or topologically varying scenes while preserving the speed advantage.
