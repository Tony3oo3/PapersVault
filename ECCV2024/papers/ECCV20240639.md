## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for human avatars require separate per-identity optimization, leading to prohibitive parameter counts for large identity sets and limited generalization to unseen poses. Multi-view generalizable approaches can animate under novel poses but are constrained by their input views and cannot operate in a monocular setting. This paper addresses the underexplored problem of learning a single unified 3DGS representation for multiple identities from monocular videos alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240639/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video (single camera view) of each subject along with per-frame SMPL pose/shape parameters and camera intrinsics/extrinsics; no multi-view or depth input is required. It produces a shared multi-identity 3DGS representation parameterized by three low-rank factor matrices, from which per-identity Gaussian parameters are recovered on the fly. The representation can render any training subject under arbitrary novel poses and camera views, and can be extended to a new identity by adding a single row to the identity factor matrix.

## Method

MIGS constructs a third-order tensor W ∈ R^{N_i × N_g × M} (M=43) whose fibers encode all learnable 3DGS parameters (position, scale, rotation quaternion, 32-dim color feature, opacity) for every Gaussian of every identity. It then applies a CP (CANDECOMP/PARAFAC) tensor decomposition, learning only three factor matrices U_1 ∈ R^{M×R}, U_2 ∈ R^{N_i×R}, U_3 ∈ R^{N_g×R} with rank R=100 ≪ N_g=50,000, reducing parameters from ~6.5×10^7 to ~5×10^6 for 30 identities. The deformation pipeline follows 3DGS-Avatar: a non-rigid MLP maps canonical Gaussians to a deformed space, LBS with a neural skinning field applies rigid skeletal motion, and a color MLP conditioned on per-Gaussian features and spherical harmonics produces view-dependent color; per-frame latent codes are omitted to avoid overfitting. Training jointly optimizes the factor matrices and all deformation/color networks via photometric, mask, and regularization losses, with optional per-identity row updates and a lightweight personalization step that fine-tunes only the color MLP for a specific subject.

## Results

On the AIST++ dataset (30 identities), MIGS achieves PSNR 29.82 / SSIM 0.9865 / LPIPS* 17.73 on the Basic Test and PSNR 26.54 / SSIM 0.9741 / LPIPS* 26.02 on the more challenging Advanced Test (out-of-distribution poses). On ZJU-MoCap (6 identities), MIGS achieves per-subject PSNR of 32.85, 34.98, 33.88, and 32.28 for subjects 377, 386, 392, and 394 respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline 3DGS-Avatar by 1.5–2.2 dB across all subjects. The parameter count is reduced by over one order of magnitude: MIGS learns ~5×10⁶ parameters for 30 identities versus ~6.5×10⁷ for per-identity 3DGS representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIGS outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both evaluation settings. The closest competitor is 3DGS-Avatar [69] (the single-identity 3DGS method upon which MIGS is built), which MIGS surpasses by ~1.0 dB PSNR on AIST++ Basic Test and ~1.0 dB on Advanced Test, with a notable LPIPS* gap of 2.84 on the Advanced Test (26.02 vs 28.86), indicating substantially fewer perceptual artifacts under out-of-distribution poses. Among NeRF-based methods, HumanNeRF [89] is the strongest baseline but still trails MIGS by ~5.2 dB PSNR on AIST++ Basic Test; MonoHuman [92] performs worst overall, particularly on the Advanced Test where its LPIPS* degrades to 85.50 due to its reliance on frontal/back keyframe selection that fails in monocular dance videos. GauHuman [25] is only compared on ZJU-MoCap (Table 1) and is outperformed by MIGS by 2–6 dB PSNR across all four subjects. The qualitative results (Figs. 3–4) confirm that MIGS produces the fewest artifacts under challenging unseen limb articulations, while all baselines show visible degradation under the arms and legs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MIGS extends the single-identity deformable 3DGS framework of 3DGS-Avatar [69] to a multi-identity setting by constructing a high-order tensor over all Gaussian parameters across subjects and applying CP (CANDECOMP/PARAFAC) tensor decomposition to share a low-rank factorization. The conceptual lineage traces back to TensorFaces [79], which first demonstrated that multilinear tensor decomposition can capture shared structure across visual identities, and to the broader tensor-decomposition literature for neural network compression [35, 40, 59]. The key departure from prior multi-identity human rendering work (e.g., [39, 70, 94]) is that those methods require multi-view inputs, whereas MIGS operates entirely from monocular videos, making the tensor decomposition the sole mechanism for cross-identity information sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-order-of-magnitude parameter reduction (from ~6.5×10⁷ to ~5×10⁶ for 30 identities) makes multi-identity 3DGS avatars practically feasible at scale, and the demonstrated robustness under out-of-distribution dance poses suggests that diverse training identities act as a form of regularization for articulation. The personalization step (fine-tuning only the color MLP) and the novel-identity extension (adding a single row to U₂) offer a practical path toward a generic model covering thousands of subjects, as the authors envision. Open questions include whether the low-rank assumption holds as identity diversity grows, how the method scales beyond the 30-identity regime tested here, and whether the smoothing trade-off at high identity counts can be resolved without per-identity fine-tuning.
