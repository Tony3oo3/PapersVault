## Problem

RL agents trained on a finite set of object instructions cannot generalize to novel objects because language embeddings are too abstract for the policy to map unseen object names to visual targets. Additionally, existing intrinsic rewards like MineCLIP's observation-instruction similarity are uncorrelated with the agent's distance to the target, causing the agent to 'stare at' rather than approach objects, which blocks learning of skills requiring repeated close-range interaction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 16-frame RGB image observation sequence and a natural language instruction (e.g., 'hunt a cow in plains with a diamond sword'). GPT-4 first extracts the target object name from the instruction, which is then encoded alongside a negative word list by the MineCLIP text encoder. The modified MineCLIP image encoder produces a 2D confidence map with one probability per ViT patch (matching the spatial grid of the image), which is denoised via thresholding (τ=0.2) and negative-word suppression before use. The policy outputs a discrete action distribution over Minecraft actions.

## Method

COPL transfers VLM knowledge into RL through two routes built on a modified MineCLIP. For visual grounding, the last ViT block's multi-head attention is replaced with a value-embedding projection (inspired by MaskCLIP), and the resulting patch embeddings are individually passed through MineCLIP's temporal transformer (as length-1 sequences) to align with the text embedding space; cosine similarities against the target and negative word embeddings, followed by softmax, yield the per-patch confidence map. For transfer via reward, a focal reward is computed as the mean of the Hadamard product between the confidence map and a 2D Gaussian kernel centered at the image center, simultaneously rewarding target area (proximity) and centering (aiming). For transfer via representation, the confidence map is encoded by a dedicated branch and concatenated with MineCLIP image features as the task conditioning for the policy, replacing language embeddings. The agent is trained with PPO under multi-task RL using reward r = r_env + 5·r_focal, and achieves zero-shot generalization to unseen objects because the confidence map provides a spatially grounded, visually interpretable task signal that the policy can exploit regardless of the object's name.

## Results

In single-task experiments, the proposed focal reward enables the agent to master all four hunting skills (e.g., 71.3% on 'hunt a cow'), while the MineCLIP reward achieves only 3.8% and sparse reward 0.0%. In multi-task hunting, COPL achieves 50.0% average success on training tasks and 48.1% on unseen targets (llama, horse, spider, mushroom cow), surpassing language-conditioned baselines by ~4×. In the harvest domain, COPL reaches 75.6% on training tasks and 40.8% on unseen targets, outperforming LCRL[t] (16.7%) and LCRL[i] (19.7%) by ~2× on generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MineCLIP reward [17], COPL's focal reward wins decisively on single-task hunting (71.3% vs 3.8% on 'hunt a cow'), because the MineCLIP similarity score is uncorrelated with agent-target distance. Against ND_CLIP [52], the focal reward also dominates (e.g., 68.8% vs 28.8% on 'hunt a sheep'), showing that pure exploration novelty provides minimal guidance for these interaction-heavy skills. In multi-task generalization, COPL outperforms Cai et al. [7] on unseen targets in the hunting domain (48.1% vs 12.8% avg) and harvest domain (40.8% vs not reported), though [7] benefits from having cow/sheep/pig in its training set. Against STEVE-1 [31], COPL wins on most unseen targets (e.g., 49.0% vs 3.0% on 'hunt a horse') but loses on 'hunt a spider' (54.5% vs 73.0%), which the authors attribute to VPT's strong prior on killing spiders. In the harvest domain, COPL's advantage over language-conditioned baselines is clearest on unseen targets (40.8% vs 16.7–19.7%), while all methods perform similarly on training tasks, confirming that the visual confidence map representation is what drives generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MineCLIP/MineDojo (Fan et al., NeurIPS 2022), which used VLM observation-instruction similarity as an intrinsic reward, by modifying the ViT architecture to extract patch-level embeddings (inspired by MaskCLIP, Zhou et al., ECCV 2022) and converting them into object-grounded confidence maps. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) the focal reward derived from the confidence map addresses the documented deficiency of the MineCLIP reward being uncorrelated with agent-target distance, and (2) the confidence map replaces language embeddings as the task representation for the policy, grounding novel object names in a spatially explicit visual format. This positions the work at the intersection of open-vocabulary segmentation and instruction-following RL, distinct from prior language-conditioned RL that relies on compositional generalization over seen words.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical, low-cost recipe for transferring VLM knowledge into RL—no fine-tuning of the VLM is required, and the confidence map generation can be integrated with existing MineCLIP computations. The 300% and 100% success-rate improvements over language-conditioned baselines on unseen objects in hunting and harvest domains, respectively, suggest that spatially grounded visual representations may be more accessible to policy networks than abstract language embeddings for object-centric tasks. However, the method is limited to object-centric tasks where a clear target can be identified, does not ground action descriptions in instructions, and is validated only in Minecraft; extending it to creative/building tasks or other open-world environments remains open.
