## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces high-quality static scene appearance but offers no mechanism to manipulate object semantics or assign physical properties for dynamic simulation. Existing NeRF-based editing methods are limited to appearance changes or ray-level weather effects, while concurrent MPM-based Gaussian simulation approaches require manual material assignment and lack language grounding. The paper addresses the gap of unifying rich vision-language semantics with physics-based dynamics in a single explicit 3D representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images of a static scene along with COLMAP-estimated camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and a sparse point cloud, plus free-text language queries (e.g., 'a vase with flowers') that drive both segmentation and material assignment. It produces a unified 3D Gaussian scene where each primitive carries a 32-dimensional view-invariant semantic feature vector alongside geometry and spherical-harmonic color, enabling open-vocabulary object selection, physics-based animation (elastic, granular, rigid dynamics at ~30 fps simulation / ~100 fps rendering), and geometric or appearance edits. The text queries are open-vocabulary with no fixed category set, and material properties are selected from a preset bank (wood, ceramic, steel, elastic, granular, liquid) via CLIP similarity.

## Method

Feature Splatting augments each 3D Gaussian with a view-invariant feature vector distilled from CLIP, denoised via Masked Average Pooling over SAM part-level masks, and regularized by a shallow two-branch MLP that jointly supervises CLIP and DINOv2 features (λ=0.1) to prevent overfitting to noisy reference maps. For physics, the paper builds GS-Taichi-MPM on DiffTaichi, which extends MPM to Gaussian primitives by using opacity and covariance for implicit volume preservation (densifying surface points and filling interiors with transparent support particles) and estimating rotation from delta rotational vectors between nearest neighbors rather than deformation gradients, avoiding artifacts under large elastic deformation. Language grounding operates through frozen CLIP text encoders: positive/negative vocabulary queries select Gaussians for segmentation, a material vocabulary assigns rigid vs. elastic properties per component, and canonical plane queries (floor, tabletop) feed RANSAC to estimate collision surfaces and gravity direction. Custom CUDA kernels (half2 intrinsics, shared gradient buffers, interleaved memory access) reduce high-dimensional feature rasterization cost by 62% over a naive extension of vanilla GS.

## Results

On the LERF localization benchmark (72 objects, 5 scenes), Feature Splatting achieves 81.7% 2D localization accuracy, outperforming LERF (80.3%) and OWL-ViT (54.8%), and is the only method evaluated that supports 3D localization (50.7%). Rendering quality is preserved: PSNR of 26.47 vs. 26.45 for vanilla Gaussian Splatting, with a modest FPS drop from 129 to 102. Training completes in under 1 hour on a single RTX 3090, compared to ~6 hours for Feature3DGS, thanks to custom CUDA kernels that reduce per-iteration cost by 62.3% over a naive extension.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LERF [13], Feature Splatting wins on 2D localization accuracy (81.7% vs. 80.3%) and uniquely enables 3D localization (50.7%), which LERF cannot perform due to its implicit NeRF representation. Against OWL-ViT [18], Feature Splatting achieves a substantial 26.9-point improvement in 2D localization (81.7% vs. 54.8%), demonstrating the benefit of 3D-consistent feature distillation over 2D-only detection. Against Feature3DGS [34], Feature Splatting is dramatically faster in training (under 1 hour vs. ~6 hours) while also supporting physics simulation and editing, which Feature3DGS does not. Against vanilla Gaussian Splatting [12], Feature Splatting maintains rendering fidelity (PSNR 26.47 vs. 26.45) at a small cost of 27 FPS (102 vs. 129), confirming that feature distillation does not degrade appearance. Against the concurrent MPM-based work of Xie et al. [31], Feature Splatting's delta-rotational-vector approach produces fewer surface artifacts under large elastic deformation, whereas Xie et al.'s rotation-from-deformation-gradient method visibly degrades rendering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) by appending view-invariant semantic features distilled from vision-language models (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM), building on the feature distillation line of work from DFF and LERF but adapting it to the explicit GS representation where implicit regularization is absent. For physics-based dynamics, it builds on the MPM-based simulation of PhysGaussian (Xie et al.) but departs by grounding material assignment in natural language queries and replacing deformation-gradient rotation with a neighbor-displacement-based scheme that handles large deformations better. The unification of appearance, semantics, geometry, and physics in a single Gaussian format is the paper's central conceptual contribution, positioning it as a superset of both feature-field editing and physics-integrated Gaussian methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single explicit 3D representation can simultaneously support photorealistic rendering, open-vocabulary segmentation, and language-driven physics simulation, which is practically valuable for interactive 3D content creation and virtual production. Key limitations include artifacts in previously unobserved regions after object removal or displacement (addressed only as future work via generative in-painting), and the physics evaluation is entirely qualitative with no ground-truth comparison. Open questions include whether the language-grounded material assignment generalizes beyond a small bank of preset materials, and whether the pipeline scales to scenes with many interacting objects or fluid dynamics.
