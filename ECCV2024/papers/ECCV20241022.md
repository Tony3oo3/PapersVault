## Problem

Existing camera-space hand mesh prediction methods rely on a two-stage pipeline: first predicting root-relative 3D meshes, then lifting them to camera space via a separate, non-differentiable process (e.g., RootNet, test-time optimization, or PnP). This decoupling prevents gradients from flowing from camera-space losses back into the mesh prediction network, causing error accumulation and loss of scale/contextual cues that are critical for 3D interaction applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241022/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB hand-crop image and camera intrinsics (focal length and principal point) as input. Before network processing, the image is rectified to a canonical focal length with the principal point centered on the hand crop, effectively normalizing the camera model. The network outputs camera-space 3D MANO mesh vertices, 2D hand keypoints with per-keypoint confidence weights, and the global 3D hand translation vector, from which the full camera-space mesh is obtained by adding the translation to root-relative vertices.

## Method

The core contribution is a Differentiable Global Positioning (DGP) module that formulates the hand root-finding problem as a weighted least-squares solution to a Direct Linear Transform (DLT) system built from 2D-3D keypoint correspondences, enabling closed-form, differentiable recovery of the global translation. The overall architecture uses a CNN encoder feeding three decoder heads (2D keypoints, root-relative 3D vertices via a graph neural network, and per-keypoint confidence weights), with the DGP module inserted between the relative-space predictions and the final camera-space output. An image rectification step resizes all training and test images to a shared canonical focal length and centers the principal point on the hand, reducing the 2D-to-3D depth-scale ambiguity the network must resolve. Training is fully end-to-end: camera-space losses (translation MSE, 2D keypoint projection consistency, 2D vertex reprojection) backpropagate through the DGP module into the relative-space network, while relative-space losses (vertex L1, edge length, normals, consistency) supervise the mesh geometry directly.

## Results

On FreiHAND, HandDGP achieves the lowest camera-space errors among all compared methods: 46.3 mm CS-MJE and 46.3 mm CS-MVE, outperforming the closest competitor NVF (47.2 mm CS-MJE) by 0.9 mm and CMR-SG-RN50 (48.8/48.9 mm) by 2.5 mm. On the more challenging HO3D-v2 dataset (with severe object occlusions), the method achieves 50.3 mm CS-MJE/CS-MVE, beating Hasson et al. (55.2/55.1 mm) by ~5 mm and HandOccNet (156.4/156.2 mm) by over 100 mm. On Human3.6M (full-body), the full framework reaches 147.6 mm CS-MJE and 162.0 mm CS-MVE, demonstrating generalizability beyond hands.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HandDGP outperforms all eight compared baselines on camera-space metrics across both FreiHAND and HO3D-v2. On FreiHAND, the closest competitor is NVF [26] at 47.2 mm CS-MJE (no vertex metric available), with HandDGP winning by 0.9 mm; against CMR-SG-RN50 [15] (48.8/48.9 mm) the margin is 2.5 mm, and against MobRecon-RN50 [14] (50.2/50.3 mm) it is 3.9 mm. On HO3D-v2, the advantage is more pronounced: HandDGP (50.3 mm) beats Hasson et al. [24] (55.2/55.1 mm) by ~5 mm and HandOccNet [41] (156.4/156.2 mm) by over 100 mm, the latter benefiting from object-size priors that reduce scale ambiguity. The paper also notes that while HandDGP's root-relative error (RS-MVE 7.6 mm) is slightly worse than MobRecon's (6.8 mm) by ~0.6 mm, the 4 mm camera-space improvement is deemed a worthwhile trade-off for 3D interaction applications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MobRecon's encoder-decoder + graph neural decoder pipeline for root-relative hand mesh prediction, but departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (predict relative mesh, then separately lift to camera space via RootNet, PnP, or test-time optimization) by making the lifting step differentiable. The Differentiable Global Positioning module is a modern re-derivation of the classical Direct Linear Transform adapted to the specific constraint that only translation is unknown (rotation is identity), enabling gradient flow from camera-space losses back into the relative mesh network. The image rectification step draws inspiration from Metric3D's canonical camera space idea, repurposed here to reduce the 2D-to-3D scale-depth ambiguity specific to hand prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Camera-space hand mesh prediction is essential for AR/VR interaction but has been largely neglected in favor of root-relative metrics that hide scale and depth errors; this paper provides a principled, end-to-end solution and a thorough empirical study of design choices (differentiable solver vs. optimization vs. regression, rectification, keypoint weighting) that the field lacks. The authors note that root-relative errors are in the low single-digit millimeters while camera-space errors are 6–7× larger, suggesting a significant performance ceiling for isolated hand prediction and pointing toward the need for context-aware approaches (scene geometry, objects) as the next frontier. The DGP module is architecture-agnostic and could be plugged into any pipeline that outputs 2D keypoints and root-relative meshes, broadening its applicability beyond hands to full-body prediction (demonstrated on Human3.6M).
