## Problem

Existing vision-language models can only ingest 4–32 video frames due to the quadratic memory cost of self-attention, making them impractical for long-form video understanding tasks that require observing minutes of footage. No sufficiently large, loosely-labelled video corpus exists to train such models from scratch, so the practical path is to bridge frozen pre-trained visual encoders and LLMs via lightweight adapters — but prior adapters (Q-former, Perceiver Resampler) are too heavy or too short-context to scale to 100+ frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242304/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames at 224×224 resolution (encoded by a frozen ViT-g into 14×14 patch tokens with temporal embeddings; 50% of patches are randomly dropped on every other frame to halve memory) together with a text conditioning sequence consisting of a task-specific special token ([QA], [CPN], [TRG], [STG]) followed by the task prompt. Regardless of how many frames are ingested (up to 180), the TCR always emits a fixed set of 128 query vectors of dimension 512, which are concatenated with a BOS token and instruction text before being fed to a frozen Flan-T5 LLM that generates the final text response (multiple-choice answer, caption, action sequence, or timestamp list).

## Method

TCR is a 4-block transformer-decoder (8 heads, 512-dim hidden, 69 M parameters) in which 128 learnable queries interact with the conditioning text via self-attention and with frozen visual features exclusively via cross-attention layers inserted in blocks 0 and 2; this separation of cross- and self-attention avoids the quadratic cost of concatenating all frame tokens into a single sequence, enabling 100–180 frames in one forward pass. Training proceeds in three stages: (i) initialisation on YTT-1B using BLIP-2-style image-text contrastive and matching objectives without the LLM, (ii) joint pre-training with the frozen Flan-T5 on three generative tasks (temporal grounding, segment captioning, and text correction) to align TCR outputs with the LLM's input space and teach attention to task tokens, and (iii) task-specific fine-tuning on the target benchmark. Throughout, only the TCR module and its vocabulary are updated while the ViT-g encoder and Flan-T5 remain frozen, keeping the trainable footprint at roughly 76 M parameters.

## Results

On NextQA, TCR achieves 73.5% overall accuracy with only 76M trainable parameters, outperforming SeViLA (73.4%, 346M params) and BLIP2 (63.5%, 188M params). On EgoSchema (3-minute videos), TCR reaches 35.1% QA accuracy with 184 frames, surpassing InternVideo (32.1%) and BLIP2 (27.2%). On the EGO4D Long-Term Action Anticipation challenge, TCR achieves the best Action Edit Distance of 0.8704 (iterative), beating AntGPT (0.8826) and PALM (0.8934). On EGO4D Moment Queries, TCR achieves 25.45 mAP with 192 frames, trailing the SOTA ASL (27.85) by 2.4 mAP but exceeding InternVideo (23.59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NextQA, TCR (73.5%) narrowly edges out SeViLA (73.4%) while using only 76M trainable parameters versus SeViLA's 346M, and substantially outperforms BLIP2 (63.5%) and the 2×BLIP2 re-implementation (70.1%). On EgoSchema, TCR with 184 frames (35.1%) clearly surpasses InternVideo (32.1%) and BLIP2 with iterative inference (29.9%), though a large gap to human performance (67.2%) remains. On EGO4D LTA, TCR with iterative decoding (Action ED 0.8704) beats both AntGPT (0.8826) and PALM (0.8934), and significantly outperforms BLIP2 (0.9103), demonstrating the benefit of processing 96 frames versus 8. On EGO4D Moment Queries, TCR (25.45 mAP at 192 frames) trails the SOTA ASL (27.85 mAP) by 2.4 mAP, which the authors attribute to solving a discriminative task in a generative framework, but still exceeds InternVideo (23.59 mAP). Overall, TCR sets new SOTA on NextQA, EgoSchema, and EGO4D LTA while remaining competitive on EGO4D MQ, all with a single forward pass and a lightweight 69M-parameter adapter.

## Relation to Prior Work

TCR is a direct architectural descendant of the Q-former in BLIP-2 and the Perceiver Resampler in Flamingo, both of which use learnable queries to compress visual features before feeding them to a frozen LLM. The key departures are: (i) TCR is trained exclusively on video across all stages (unlike Q-former, which is image-trained), (ii) it uses substantially lower-dimensional features (512 vs. 768/1536) and fewer parameters (69M vs. 188M), and (iii) it separates cross-attention (video-to-query) from self-attention (query-to-query) operations rather than combining them in a single pass as the Perceiver Resampler does. This separation is what enables processing 100+ frames with plain attention, a regime neither predecessor could reach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TCR provides a practical, plug-in mechanism for bridging frozen visual encoders and LLMs on long video sequences, and the accompanying empirical study (Section 3.1) usefully identifies which tasks genuinely benefit from longer temporal span (temporal QA, action anticipation, moment queries) versus those that saturate quickly (action recognition, short-video captioning). The large remaining gap to human performance on EgoSchema (35.1% vs. 67.2%) and the observation that performance plateaus before reaching >1fps sampling rates suggest that simply adding more frames is insufficient—better temporal reasoning within the LLM or integration with optimized attention (FlashAttention, RingAttention) and memory-augmented approaches are likely necessary next steps.
