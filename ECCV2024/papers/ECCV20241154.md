## Problem

SAM excels at promptable segmentation but cannot recognize what it segments, while CLIP provides zero-shot recognition but struggles with dense prediction. Naively combining the two models (via image or feature cropping) incurs high computational cost and performs poorly on small objects, and no unified framework existed for interactive open-vocabulary segmentation and recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241154/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image along with visual prompts (bounding boxes or points) and a set of text category names encoded via CLIP's text encoder. It outputs a segmentation mask per prompt, a class label, and an IoU confidence score. Notably, the SAM ViT encoder is discarded at inference, so only the frozen CLIP CNN backbone (e.g., R50x16) plus the lightweight SAM decoder and adapters are active, reducing FLOPs from ~3,000G to ~1,180G.

## Method

The architecture replaces SAM's ViT encoder with a frozen CLIP CNN encoder and appends SAM's prompt encoder and mask decoder behind it, bridged by two knowledge-transfer modules. SAM2CLIP distills SAM-Huge's encoder features into a multi-scale transformer adapter on top of CLIP using a per-pixel MSE loss, aligning CLIP's spatial features with SAM's segmentation-oriented representations. CLIP2SAM introduces a label token into the SAM decoder and a lightweight FPN with RoI-Align to extract multi-scale region features from CLIP, enabling recognition of small objects that single-scale adaptation misses. Training is two-stage: first distilling SAM→CLIP on SA-1B (1%), then jointly training CLIP2SAM and the mask decoder on COCO/LVIS with mask CE, Dice, and classification losses. For open-vocabulary inference, learned class scores are fused with frozen CLIP scores via geometric mean, and scaling to 22K+ classes is achieved by joint training with ImageNet-22k, V3Det, and Object365.

## Results

On COCO with ground-truth box prompts, Open-Vocabulary SAM achieves 81.5 IoU (base) / 84.0 IoU (novel) and 84.3% recognition accuracy, outperforming the best combined baseline (Feature-Crop + CoOp) by ~2% IoU and ~13% accuracy, while using only 1,180G FLOPs and 304M parameters versus 3,545G/808M for baselines. On LVIS, the method reaches 66.6% accuracy (vs. 48.2% for the best baseline), a >20% absolute gain. When paired with the Detic open-vocabulary detector, OV-SAM achieves 32.4 mAP on COCO (vs. 29.5 for Feature-Crop+CoOp) and 22.4 mAP on LVIS. With ViTDet(Huge) as the detector, OV-SAM reaches 48.8 mAP on COCO, surpassing SAM-Huge (46.3 mAP) by 2.5 points at roughly 1/3 the FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against prior open-vocabulary segmentation methods (Table 3), OV-SAM substantially outperforms XPM [24] and MaskCLIP [13] on novel classes (37.5 vs. 24.0 and 23.2 AP50 in the constrained setting; 39.8 vs. 21.6 and 21.7 in the generalized setting), while trailing them by ~1 point on base classes. Against MasQCLIP [74], OV-SAM wins on both novel (37.5 vs. 30.1) and overall (39.4 vs. 37.5) AP50. Compared to SAM-Huge [30], OV-SAM improves 1-IoU on COCO from 78.2 to 81.7 while adding open-vocabulary recognition, and achieves higher mAP with ViTDet (48.8 vs. 46.3) at 1/3 the FLOPs. OV-SAM also surpasses SEEM [90] (81.7 vs. 73.7 1-IoU) and Semantic-SAM [32] (81.7 vs. 76.1 1-IoU), both of which support closed-set classification but not open-vocabulary recognition. The key advantage over all baselines is the unified single-encoder design: OV-SAM uses 304M parameters and 1,180G FLOPs versus 641M/3,001G for SAM-Huge and 808M/3,545G for the combined SAM+CLIP baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two major vision foundation models—SAM (promptable, class-agnostic segmentation) and CLIP (zero-shot recognition)—into a single encoder-decoder architecture, departing from prior work that either naively pipelines them (image/feature cropping baselines) or adds closed-set classification heads to SAM. It extends the open-vocabulary segmentation line (XPM, MaskCLIP, MasQCLIP) by replacing the separate Mask-RCNN segmentor with SAM-style interactive prompts (boxes, points), and introduces dual knowledge-transfer modules (SAM2CLIP distillation and CLIP2SAM feature-pyramid adapter) to bridge the architectural and training-objective gap between the two models. The approach also builds on adapter-based fine-tuning techniques (CoOp, ViT-Adapter) but applies them in a novel cross-model distillation setting rather than single-model tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single CLIP backbone with lightweight adapters can match SAM-Huge's segmentation quality at roughly 40% of the FLOPs while simultaneously providing open-vocabulary recognition over 22K classes, this work offers a practical, unified interactive annotation tool that reduces the need for separate segmentation and classification pipelines. Key limitations include the continued dependence on visual prompts (boxes/points) rather than fully autonomous detection, the requirement for the SAM encoder during training (discarded at inference), and the fact that joint training across all large-scale datasets was not fully explored due to compute constraints. Open questions include whether the distillation strategy generalizes to other foundation model pairs, whether the CNN-backbone preference (over ViT) holds as CLIP architectures evolve, and how the method scales to video or 3D settings.
