## Problem

Both CAM-based and transformer-based weakly supervised object localization methods fail to capture pixel-level fine-grained boundaries: CAM activates only the most discriminative sub-regions, while transformer self-attention maps produce dilated activations that bleed into surrounding background. This coarse localization is particularly problematic at high IoU thresholds (≥0.7), where existing methods degrade sharply.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241855/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and an image-level class label as the sole training supervision. At inference, the GTFormer (Deit-S backbone) produces a coarse 7×7 foreground probability map, which is then matched against a gallery of fine-grained binary masks generated by a frozen ViT-H SAM using preset grid-point prompts. The final output is the single best-matched SAM mask as a pixel-level localization map, along with the predicted class.

## Method

Pro2SAM operates in three stages. First, a Global Token Transformer (GTFormer) jointly embeds four novel global tokens—constructed by scanning the downsampled image with a 4×4 window at fixed offsets—alongside standard patch tokens and a class token in self-attention; a global-token attention (GTA) module uses the global tokens' query vectors to produce foreground probability maps that modulate standard attention via element-wise multiplication, yielding a coarse localization mask. Second, preset grid points are fed into SAM's prompt encoder to generate a comprehensive mask gallery, avoiding the object omission that single point/box prompts cause. Third, a pixel-level AND/OR similarity score matches the GTFormer's coarse mask against each SAM mask, and the highest-scoring mask is selected as the final localization. Only GTFormer is trained (with cross-entropy, edge, and region losses); SAM remains frozen and is used exclusively at inference, eliminating the need for SAM fine-tuning.

## Results

Pro2SAM achieves 84.03% Top-1 Loc and 66.85% Top-1 Loc on CUB-200-2011 and ILSVRC respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on ILSVRC among class-agnostic WSOL methods. On fine-grained localization (MaxBoxAccV2), Pro2SAM reaches 48.43% IOU90 on CUB-200-2011 and 45.94% IOU90 on ILSVRC, substantially outperforming the second-best methods (TokenCut at 25.96% and SAT at 15.14% IOU90 respectively). The GTFormer component alone also achieves competitive results (80.48% Top-1 Loc on CUB, 60.2% on ILSVRC), confirming the contribution of the global token design independent of SAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ILSVRC, Pro2SAM (66.85% Top-1 Loc) outperforms all class-agnostic baselines by a wide margin: +7.29% over C2AM (59.56%), +11.25% over UOLwRPS (55.60%), +9.25% over LocLoc (57.60%), and +6.91% over SAT (59.94%). On CUB-200-2011, Pro2SAM (84.03%) is slightly behind LocLoc (84.40%) by 0.37% on Top-1 Loc but surpasses all other methods including C2AM (81.76%) and SPOL (80.12%). The fine-grained localization gap is even more dramatic: Pro2SAM's IOU90 score of 48.43% on CUB-200-2011 nearly doubles TokenCut's 25.96% and is 24× higher than SAT's 2.01%, while on ILSVRC its 45.94% IOU90 is 3× higher than SAT (15.14%) and LocLoc (14.68%). The GTFormer component alone (without SAM) already matches or exceeds SAT and CATR on both datasets, indicating the global token architecture is independently effective. The largest gains from adding SAM appear at high IoU thresholds, confirming that SAM's fine-grained boundary capability is the primary source of Pro2SAM's advantage over transformer-only methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer-based WSOL line (TS-CAM, SAT, UOLwRPS) by introducing a novel Global Token Transformer (GTFormer) that augments standard patch tokens with four global tokens to produce a coarse foreground mask prompt, addressing the dilated activation problem inherent in self-attention maps. Its key departure from prior WSOL work is the integration of SAM as a frozen, inference-only segmentation engine: rather than relying solely on the transformer's own localization map, Pro2SAM generates a gallery of fine-grained masks via grid-point prompts and selects the best match via pixel-level IoU against the GTFormer output. This is the first work to repurpose SAM's zero-shot segmentation capability for WSOL through a mask-prompt-to-mask-matching pipeline, rather than fine-tuning SAM on domain-specific data as in HQ-SAM or UV-SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic improvement at high IoU thresholds (48.43% vs. 25.96% IOU90 on CUB-200-2011; 45.94% vs. 15.14% on ILSVRC) demonstrates that foundation-model segmentation can resolve the fine-grained boundary problem that has limited WSOL for years, and the inference-only use of SAM (no fine-tuning) keeps the training cost low. However, the approach inherits SAM's computational overhead at inference (ViT-H), is limited to single-object localization, and the grid-point prompting strategy is somewhat brute-force, raising open questions about scalability to multi-object scenes, very small objects, or resource-constrained deployment. Whether the mask-matching paradigm generalizes beyond WSOL to other weakly supervised dense prediction tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation) remains to be explored.
