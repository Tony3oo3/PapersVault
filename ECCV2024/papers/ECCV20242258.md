## Problem

Automatic 3D labeling for autonomous driving remains costly because manual 3D box annotation is laborious, and existing auto-labelers either require supervised training on a specific dataset (overfitting to its annotation patterns) or demand amodal 2D boxes with oracle occlusion information. The core technical challenge is that lifting a 2D mask to a 3D shape is highly ill-posed—many 3D shapes project to the same 2D silhouette—making it difficult to recover accurate geometry without additional constraints.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image with 2D point or box prompts (from a human labeler or an off-the-shelf 2D detector like HTC) plus a LiDAR point cloud, and produces a 3D object shape parameterized by a 5-dimensional PCA latent vector and a 4-dimensional pose (x, y, z, heading θ). The shape is represented as a discrete SDF on a 3D mesh grid, which can be converted to a 7-DoF bounding box for standard detection benchmarks or directly used as dense voxel occupancy for dynamic objects. Notably, the method assumes objects rest on the ground plane and omits roll/pitch rotations.

## Method

SLF follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) Segment—SAM converts 2D prompts into high-quality instance masks; (2) Lift—masks are lifted into 3D using an SDF representation whose shape is parameterized by a 5-dimensional PCA latent code learned from 79 water-tight car CAD models (Apollo-Car3D), enabling smooth shape interpolation; (3) Fit—pose and shape are jointly optimized via gradient descent (Adam, 150 iterations, lr=0.1) minimizing a weighted sum of three differentiable energy terms: a dice-loss mask alignment term (with an occlusion map computed from LiDAR depth), a point-cloud alignment term combining L1 SDF distance and ray-intersection displacement, and a ground-plane alignment term from RANSAC. The entire procedure is training-free—no neural network is trained on 3D labels—so it avoids overfitting to dataset-specific annotation biases and generalizes across datasets. A differentiable renderer casts rays through each pixel and evaluates the SDF along the ray with a sigmoid to produce soft mask values, making the full projection differentiable with respect to both shape and pose parameters.

## Results

On KITTI validation, SLF_box achieves AP_3D@0.5 of 87.9/88.2/83.9 (Easy/Mod./Hard) and AP_BEV@0.5 of 89.2/89.3/84.7, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline FGR on Moderate and Hard splits by 10.4 and 13.3 AP_3D points respectively. Detectors trained with SLF pseudo-labels reach near-ground-truth performance (e.g., PointPillar: 95.2/92.1/88.8 AP_3D vs. 95.7/93.6/91.4 with GT). On the cross-dataset nuScenes evaluation, SLF achieves mAP 57.2 and NDS 55.2, surpassing the supervised MTrans (mAP 45.9, NDS 49.4) and the unsupervised FGR (mAP 11.6, NDS 40.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unsupervised baseline FGR [39], SLF wins decisively on Moderate and Hard KITTI splits (AP_3D: 88.2 vs. 77.8 and 83.9 vs. 70.6) but trails on Easy (87.9 vs. 91.3), which the authors attribute to FGR's advantage with dense sub-points and oracle amodal boxes. SLF substantially outperforms SDFLabel [44] (AP_3D Mod.: 88.2 vs. 42.2) and VS3D [31] (AP_3D Mod.: 88.2 vs. 37.4) across all difficulty levels. In the cross-dataset nuScenes experiment, SLF surpasses the supervised MTrans [15] on mAP (57.2 vs. 45.9) and NDS (55.2 vs. 49.4), demonstrating that training-free optimization generalizes better than a network overfit to KITTI annotation patterns. FGR [39] collapses on nuScenes (mAP 11.6), making its error metrics unreliable. In downstream detector training (Table 2), SLF pseudo-labels yield near-GT performance for all four detectors (PointPillar, SECOND, Part-A2, Voxel R-CNN), consistently outperforming FGR pseudo-labels on Moderate and Hard splits by 3–7 AP points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLF sits at the intersection of differentiable SDF rendering (inherited from Autoshape and DirectShape), PCA-based shape priors (from Engelmann et al. and SAMP), and the 3D auto-labeling paradigm established by FGR, MTrans, and SDFLabel. Its key conceptual departure is combining SAM for prompt-to-mask conversion with a training-free gradient-based optimization loop, eliminating the need for any supervised 3D label training that MTrans requires or synthetic-data pretraining that SDFLabel depends on. Unlike FGR, which demands oracle amodal boxes encoding occlusion information, SLF operates on standard 2D modal boxes obtainable from off-the-shelf detectors, making it more practical for fully automatic pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design means SLF does not overfit to dataset-specific annotation biases, yielding substantially better cross-dataset generalization (e.g., on nuScenes where MTrans degrades sharply). The ability to predict detailed 3D shapes rather than just bounding boxes opens a path toward automated voxel occupancy annotation for dynamic objects, addressing a growing need in autonomous driving. However, the method is currently limited to the car class and requires a curated collection of category-specific CAD models for the PCA shape prior, leaving open the question of how to extend it to pedestrians, cyclists, or other diverse object categories without such priors.
