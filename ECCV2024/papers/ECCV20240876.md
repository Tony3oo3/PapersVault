## Problem

Invisible watermarks are increasingly prevalent in image datasets (e.g., from AI generators), yet no method exists to detect them in a black-box, annotation-free setting where the specific watermarking algorithm and decoding procedure are unknown. Existing approaches—visible watermark detectors, OOD/anomaly detection, and backdoor sample detection—all fail because watermark perturbations are too subtle to trigger these methods, yielding AUC scores near 0.5.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240876/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

WMD takes two unlabeled image datasets as input: a detection dataset (mixed watermarked and clean images, 256×256) and a clean reference dataset with a similar visual distribution. It produces a binary watermark label per image, effectively partitioning the detection dataset into a watermarked subset and a clean subset. The method is model-agnostic but uses a compact 5-block ConvNext-V2 (1.93M parameters) for efficiency, requiring no access to watermark decoding APIs or prior knowledge of the embedding technique.

## Method

WMD formulates watermark detection as an offset optimization problem: by subtracting the gradient of a clean reference dataset from the gradient of the detection dataset, the common clean-sample contributions cancel, leaving a signal driven primarily by watermarked samples. Two key design choices make this work in practice. First, an asymmetric loss pairs a softmax loss (for the clean dataset, emphasizing hard examples to ensure strict minimization) with a linear loss (for the detection dataset, giving equal weight to all samples so watermarked examples are not drowned out by the majority of clean ones). Second, an iterative pruning strategy periodically removes the lowest-scoring (most confidently clean) samples from the detection dataset and reinitializes the model, progressively condensing the dataset toward watermarked samples and preventing the overparameterized network from memorizing all inputs. The final model outputs high scores for watermarked images and low scores for clean ones, enabling detection without any labeled data or knowledge of the watermarking method.

## Results

WMD achieves AUC scores consistently above 0.9 for most single-watermark detection tasks across ImageNet, COCO, and Caltech-256, with the best result being 0.968 (DctDwtSVD on ImageNet). In the more challenging multi-watermark setting (4 post-processing or 2 generative watermarks simultaneously), WMD maintains AUC above 0.7 across all methods and datasets. For generative watermarks, Stable Signature achieves AUC 0.93–0.94 while Tree-Ring is lower at 0.81–0.82 due to its subtler latent-space perturbations. The detection model is a lightweight ConvNext-V2 with only 1.93M parameters, trained for 50 epochs with a 10% pruning rate every 10 epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison (Table 1) demonstrates that all six existing detection paradigms fail catastrophically on invisible watermarks: visible watermark detectors (TV-L1 at AUC 0.508, LSW at 0.512), anomaly/OOD detectors (DROC at 0.513, RIAD at 0.522), and backdoor sample detectors (CT at 0.514, ASSET at 0.518) all score near random chance (0.5) on an ImageNet subset with 5% DctDwtSVD watermarks. WMD, by contrast, achieves AUC 0.968 on the same DctDwtSVD setting — a gap of over 0.44 AUC points. The paper explains this failure mode: watermark perturbations are too subtle for OOD/anomaly methods to flag, and unlike backdoor triggers, watermarks do not cause distinctive model behavior shifts. No direct head-to-head comparison with WMD is run against these baselines on the full evaluation suite; instead, the baselines serve to establish that the problem is unsolved by prior art, while WMD's Tables 2–3 demonstrate its own absolute performance across six watermark methods and five datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

WMD directly builds on the offset optimization framework from ASSET [39], which identifies anomalous samples by subtracting gradient contributions between a reference and target dataset. The key departure is adapting this mechanism to the watermark detection setting, where the 'anomaly' is an imperceptible perturbation rather than a backdoor trigger or OOD shift, and where no decoding algorithm or label is available. The paper introduces two methodological innovations on top of the offset formulation: an asymmetric loss (softmax for the clean reference, linear for the detection set) that prevents the model from being dominated by the majority clean samples, and an iterative pruning strategy that progressively condenses the detection dataset to improve separability. The authors explicitly position WMD against visible watermark detection (which relies on human annotation), OOD/anomaly detection, and backdoor detection, demonstrating that all of these fail at chance level (AUC ≈ 0.5) on invisible watermarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AI-generated images are projected to constitute ~5% of all new images and regulatory mandates for watermarking expand, a black-box detection method that requires no knowledge of the specific watermarking algorithm is practically essential for dataset curation, IP enforcement, and compliance. The paper's broader applications—enabling watermark removal attacks by constructing pure watermarked subsets, and detecting other invisible perturbations like data poisoning and unlearnable examples—suggest WMD could become a general-purpose tool for identifying any distributional deviation from a clean reference. Key limitations include the dependence on a clean reference dataset with matching distribution (cross-domain performance degrades, e.g., AUC drops to ~0.6 with CelebA) and sensitivity to hyperparameters (pruning rate, interval). Open questions include whether domain adaptation or learned hyperparameter selection can eliminate the distribution-matching requirement, and whether the approach scales to the billions of images in real-world corpora.
