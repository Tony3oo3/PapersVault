## Problem

Detection pre-training for DETR-based detectors has been well studied in natural scenes but remains unexplored for remote sensing oriented object detection. Existing predictive pre-training methods (e.g., DETReg) suffer from a pronounced feature discrepancy between object embeddings extracted from cropped images via a full backbone and detector features produced by image-level features plus a DETR decoder, a gap that is further exacerbated by the complex backgrounds and dense, arbitrarily-oriented objects typical of remote sensing imagery.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240297/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes 800×800 remote sensing image patches along with offline-generated pseudo-labels: oriented bounding boxes from SAM (ViT-H, ~200 masks per image), 256-dimensional object embeddings (PCA-reduced backbone features of cropped patches), and 256 pseudo-classes from k-means clustering. During fine-tuning, the same ARS-DETR architecture takes remote sensing images (800×800 for DIOR-R, 1024×1024 for DOTA, 600×600 scaled to 800×800 for OHD-SJTU) and outputs oriented bounding boxes (x, y, w, h), class predictions over 20 or 6 real categories, and rotation angles in [−π/2, π/2).

## Method

MutDet builds on ARS-DETR and introduces three coupled components. First, a Mutual Enhancement Module performs three layers of bidirectional cross-attention between object embeddings O and encoder features F in the last encoder layer, producing enhanced features F_enh and enhanced embeddings O_enh that allow supervision signals from object embeddings to propagate through multiple pathways into the detector. Second, a symmetric contrastive alignment loss (temperature τ=0.2) is applied between O_enh and both the encoder-predicted and decoder-predicted embeddings (selected via Hungarian matching), replacing the distillation-style alignment of DETReg to avoid feature collapse when embeddings become learnable. Third, an auxiliary siamese head shares decoder parameters with the main DETR decoder but takes the un-enhanced encoder feature F as input, supervised by the same pseudo-labels, thereby closing the task gap that arises because object embeddings are unavailable at fine-tuning time. The total loss sums the detector loss (contrastive alignment + focal classification + GIoU/L1 regression + angle classification) with the auxiliary head loss of the same form.

## Results

On DIOR-R (36-epoch fine-tuning), MutDet achieves AP50 = 70.7 and AP75 = 51.2, surpassing the DETReg baseline (67.9 / 49.1) by 2.8% and 2.1% respectively. On DOTA-v1.0, MutDet gains 1.9% AP50 over DETReg, and on OHD-SJTU-L (1× schedule) it improves AP50 by 2.59% and AP75 by 0.27%. Under data-limited fine-tuning (10% of DIOR-R), MutDet outperforms DETReg by 6.1% in AP50 and 4.5% in AP75, and with only 12 epochs of training it leads DETReg by 4.8% in AP50.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MutDet consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and training schedule. Against DETReg (CVPR 2022), the primary predictive pre-training baseline, MutDet gains +2.8% AP50 on DIOR-R, +1.9% on DOTA-v1.0, and +2.99% on OHD-SJTU-L; the gap widens to +6.1% AP50 when only 10% of DIOR-R data is used for fine-tuning. Against UP-DETR (CVPR 2021), MutDet leads by +3.6% AP50 on DIOR-R (70.7 vs. 67.1) and by +2.8% at 12 epochs. Against AlignDet (ICCV 2023), a self-supervised method, MutDet achieves +4.9% AP50 on DIOR-R; notably, AlignDet shows negative or near-zero gains over pre-training-free on DOTA-v1.0 and OHD-SJTU-S, whereas MutDet maintains positive improvements in all settings. The advantage of MutDet is most pronounced under resource-constrained conditions (limited data or limited training epochs), where the mutual enhancement module and contrastive alignment provide the largest relative gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the detection pre-training paradigm established by DETReg and UP-DETR (which align detector predictions with object embeddings from a frozen backbone) to the domain of remote sensing oriented object detection, using ARS-DETR as the base detector. The key conceptual departure is the identification and systematic treatment of 'feature discrepancy'—the gap between object embeddings extracted from cropped patches through a full backbone and detector features produced from image-level features through a DETR encoder—which is exacerbated by the dense, multi-orientation object distributions in aerial imagery. Rather than the unidirectional distillation of DETReg or the self-supervised consistency of AlignDet, MutDet introduces bidirectional cross-attention fusion (mutual enhancement module) and contrastive alignment to close this gap, while an auxiliary siamese head addresses the task gap between pre-training and fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first detection pre-training framework specifically designed for remote sensing oriented object detection, MutDet provides a practical recipe for improving detection performance under the annotation-scarce conditions typical of aerial imagery, with particularly strong gains when fine-tuning data is limited (6.1% AP50 with only 10% of DIOR-R data). The mutual enhancement module's bidirectional fusion offers a general architectural principle for integrating external knowledge (here, backbone-derived embeddings) into transformer-based detectors that could transfer beyond remote sensing. A notable limitation is that SAM is used only unidirectionally for pseudo-label generation, and the authors acknowledge that establishing a deeper, bidirectional correlation between the detector and the underlying visual foundation model remains open. The finding that self-supervised pre-training (AlignDet) can actually degrade performance on DOTA-v1.0 when the pre-training and fine-tuning datasets overlap highlights an underexplored failure mode in the pre-training literature.
