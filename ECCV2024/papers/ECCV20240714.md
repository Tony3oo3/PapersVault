## Problem

Existing video person ReID datasets and methods assume data captured from the same platform (ground surveillance cameras), leaving the cross-platform scenario—where a person must be matched between ground-level and aerial (UAV) footage—entirely unexplored. The drastic differences in viewpoint, pose, and resolution between these platforms make direct visual feature alignment extremely difficult, and no benchmark or adaptation strategy existed for this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240714/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips of 8 frames (256×128 each) sampled at 0.5s intervals from either a fixed ground camera (~2m height) or a UAV flying at 20–60m altitude, with frame order randomly shuffled to enforce set-level processing. Query and gallery tracklets always come from different platforms (ground vs. UAV), and the model outputs a visual embedding per clip used for cross-platform identity matching, evaluated via Rank-1 and mAP. Person width in images ranges from 10–70 px (ground) to 5–35 px (UAV), reflecting the large scale discrepancy the model must handle.

## Method

The core insight is to reformulate the cross-platform visual alignment problem as a visual-semantic alignment problem using CLIP: a two-stage strategy first learns ID-specific description tokens and shared text prompts (Stage 1), then aligns video visual embeddings to these semantic features (Stage 2). To adapt the image-based ViT-Base-16 encoder to video efficiently, the Video Set-Level-Adapter (VSLA) treats each clip as a disordered frame set and injects two lightweight modules: an Intra-Frame Adapter (bottleneck MLP in parallel with each MLP block) for appearance representation, and a Cross-Frame Attention Adapter (cross-frame attention over the temporal dimension) for inter-frame complementary information, totaling only 14.5M tunable parameters versus 88M for full fine-tuning. A Platform-Bridge Prompt (PBP) further narrows the cross-platform gap by concatenating two sets of platform-specific learnable tokens (ground and UAV) into the first d MSA layers, steering the encoder toward platform-invariant features. The full training objective combines visual-to-semantic cross-entropy, image-to-text and text-to-image contrastive losses, triplet loss, and identity classification loss.

## Results

On the proposed G2A-VReID dataset, VSLA-CLIP (ViFi-initialized) achieves 79.70% mAP and 72.55% Rank-1, outperforming the best prior method MGH (76.7% mAP, 69.9% Rank-1) by 3.0% mAP and 2.65% Rank-1. On LS-VID, the method reaches 85.20% mAP and 91.66% Rank-1, improving over the previous state-of-the-art LSTRL by 2.80% mAP and 1.86% Rank-1. On MARS and iLIDS, VSLA-CLIP attains 88.60% mAP / 91.82% Rank-1 and 95.33% Rank-1 respectively, setting new records on all four benchmarks. Notably, VSLA-CLIP achieves these results with only 14.5M tunable parameters versus 88.0M for full fine-tuning (FT-CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSLA-CLIP (ViFi-initialized) outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every dataset where results are reported. On G2A-VReID, it surpasses the strongest prior methods MGH (76.7% mAP) and PiT (76.3% mAP) by 3.0 and 3.4 mAP points respectively, and beats SINet (74.5% mAP) by 5.2 mAP. On LS-VID, it improves over LSTRL (82.4% mAP, 89.8% R-1) by 2.80% mAP and 1.86% R-1, and over SINet (79.6% mAP) by 5.6% mAP. On MARS, it edges out DenseIL (87.0% mAP) by 1.6% mAP and LSTRL (86.8% mAP) by 1.8% mAP. On iLIDS, it achieves 95.33% R-1, exceeding SINet (92.5%) and DenseIL (92.0%) by roughly 3 points. The parameter-efficient VSLA-CLIP (14.5M tunable params) also outperforms the full fine-tuning variant FT-CLIP (88.0M tunable params) by 1.59% mAP on G2A-VReID, confirming that the adapter design is both more effective and more efficient than full fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-ReID's two-stage visual-semantic alignment strategy from image ReID to video ReID, while drawing on GaitSet's set-level formulation to treat video clips as order-invariant frame collections rather than temporal sequences. It departs from the dominant video ReID paradigm (3D CNNs, temporal weighting, optical flow) by arguing that inter-frame complementarity, not temporal ordering, is the key signal — a position motivated by the observation that aerial views suffer severe self-occlusion that limits temporal information. The Platform-Bridge Prompt module further distinguishes the work by introducing platform-specific learnable prompts into the ViT encoder, a mechanism not previously applied to cross-platform ReID.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

G2A-VReID is the first large-scale benchmark for ground-to-aerial video ReID, filling a practical gap where UAV surveillance must identify persons previously seen by ground cameras. The finding that temporal models degrade on aerial data challenges a core assumption in video ReID and suggests that set-based or appearance-centric approaches may be more robust under viewpoint extremes. Open questions include whether the visual-semantic alignment strategy generalizes to other cross-modality gaps (e.g., visible-infrared, day-night), whether the PBP mechanism scales beyond two platforms, and whether the dataset's simulated collection protocol (fixed ground camera + nearby UAV) captures the full difficulty of real multi-UAV deployments.
