## Problem

Existing generalizable NeRF methods rely exclusively on pixel-level feature embeddings, which lack holistic scene understanding, require precise epipolar geometry, and become entangled with view-specific biases when reference views are scarce. This limits rendering quality in few-shot settings, particularly with one or two reference images, where depth ambiguity and occlusion handling degrade significantly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240139/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 1 to 5 reference RGB images along with their camera extrinsic matrices (rotation components only; intrinsics are not needed due to GAP-based feature condensation) and produces a rendered novel-view RGB image at the target camera pose. Depth maps are also available as a byproduct of the ray transformer. During training, 8–10 reference views are randomly sampled per iteration, while inference supports as few as a single reference image.

## Method

CaesarNeRF builds on the GNT attention-based generalizable NeRF backbone and introduces three key components. First, a shared CNN encoder produces both pixel-level feature maps and scene-level semantic vectors via global average pooling, which are concatenated to form a global-local embedding. Second, a calibration step rotates each reference view's semantic vector using the relative rotation matrix between that view and the target view, aligning semantic features across viewpoints before averaging to eliminate pose-induced conflicts. Third, a sequential refinement module progressively enriches the calibrated semantic representation at each transformer stage via cross-attention with the original per-frame features, allowing deeper layers to capture finer details. Training uses a composite loss combining MSE, a central consistency loss on calibrated features, and a point-wise perceptual loss.

## Results

On LLFF with 1 reference view, CaesarNeRF achieves 18.31 PSNR, 0.435 LPIPS, and 0.521 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline GNT (16.57 PSNR) by 1.74 dB. With 3 reference views, CaesarNeRF reaches 23.45 PSNR / 0.176 LPIPS / 0.794 SSIM, maintaining a lead over GNT (23.21 / 0.178 / 0.782) and IBRNet (23.00 / 0.262 / 0.752). On the more challenging mip-NeRF 360 dataset (1 view), CaesarNeRF scores 15.20 PSNR vs. GNT's 13.48 and IBRNet's 14.12. On MVImgNet (object-centric, 1 view), CaesarNeRF achieves 24.28 PSNR / 0.334 LPIPS / 0.747 SSIM, surpassing GNT (22.22 / 0.433 / 0.678) by 2.06 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaesarNeRF consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and reference-view count. Against its primary backbone GNT [60], the PSNR gap is largest in the 1-view setting (1.74 dB on LLFF, 1.58 dB on Shiny, 1.72 dB on mip-NeRF 360, 2.06 dB on MVImgNet) and narrows to ~0.2–0.5 dB at 3 views, confirming that calibrated semantic representation is most critical when views are scarce. Compared to IBRNet [64], CaesarNeRF leads by 1.46 dB PSNR (1-view LLFF) and 0.45 dB (3-view LLFF), with a larger LPIPS advantage (0.435 vs. 0.542 at 1 view). Against MatchNeRF [8] and MVSNeRF [6] (which require ≥2 views), CaesarNeRF gains 0.86 dB PSNR at 2 views and 1.15 dB at 3 views on LLFF. In the per-scene optimization setting (Table 5), CaesarNeRF surpasses NeRF [44] by 1.14 dB PSNR and NeX [67] by 0.38 dB, while matching NeX's SSIM of 0.904. Qualitatively, CaesarNeRF avoids the style drift observed with the diffusion-based Zero-1-to-3 [35] in single-view synthesis, preserving the original scene's appearance rather than generating a semantically similar but stylistically different image.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaesarNeRF builds directly on GNT (Varma et al., ICLR 2023), a fully attention-based generalizable NeRF that uses view and ray transformers to aggregate pixel-level features. It departs from the pixel-level-only paradigm shared by PixelNeRF, MVSNeRF, IBRNet, and GNT by introducing a scene-level semantic representation (via global average pooling) that is calibrated across viewpoints using camera pose rotation matrices, and progressively refined through sequential cross-attention stages. The calibration mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from PointNet's rotation-equivariant operations but is made precise by NeRF's explicit camera pose encoding, and the central loss for feature consistency is a new training objective not present in prior generalizable NeRF work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that holistic scene-level understanding, not just per-pixel feature aggregation, is critical for few-shot generalizable rendering, particularly in the challenging single-view regime where depth ambiguity is most severe. The modular Caesar pipeline (calibration + sequential refinement) transfers to other baselines like IBRNet and MatchNeRF with consistent gains, suggesting it is a broadly applicable component rather than a GNT-specific trick. A key limitation is that, like all NeRF-based methods, it cannot synthesize content occluded in all reference views, and the paper acknowledges the risk of generating convincing but inauthentic images. Open questions include whether the calibration approach extends to uncalibrated or pose-uncertain settings and whether the semantic representation can be made more interpretable or controllable.
