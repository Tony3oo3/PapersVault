## Problem

CLIP and its variants primarily learn global image-text alignment, leaving them suboptimal for dense tasks requiring region- or pixel-level understanding. Existing efforts to add fine-grained perception either tailor CLIP to a single downstream task or implicitly mine region-text correspondences from captions, introducing annotation noise. The paper addresses this by constructing a large-scale multi-granularity dataset and a unified training framework that explicitly aligns visual features with text at both image and region levels.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model consumes 336×336 RGB images alongside multi-granularity text annotations: image-level tags and captions, plus region-level bounding boxes with their own tags and captions. The image encoder produces a class token and patch token sequence, while RoIAlign extracts region embeddings from the patch tokens using the bounding box annotations; all are encoded into a shared embedding space via a text encoder. For dense downstream tasks (segmentation), input resolution increases to 640×640 or 896×896, and the output becomes per-pixel class assignments or panoptic segmentations rather than a single similarity score.

## Method

UMG-CLIP introduces a unified multi-granularity contrastive framework that simultaneously performs image-text matching (class token vs. image tags/captions) and region-text matching (RoIAlign region embeddings vs. region tags/captions), using smooth-L1 loss for tags and contrastive loss for captions. To support this at high resolution without prohibitive memory cost, it adopts a cluster-based token reduction strategy (inspired by Ailurus) that merges redundant visual tokens into representatives, cutting 75% of tokens through the transformer and unfolding them at the end to recover spatial detail for region-level learning. The training data, UMG-41M (41M images, 389M regions, 11,741 classes), is generated by an automated pipeline combining RAM for tags, BLIP2/Shikra for captions, ViTDet for detection, GPT4ROI for region descriptions, and SAM/Mask DINO for pixel-level masks. For downstream adaptation, the backbone is frozen and lightweight ConvPass PET modules are inserted in parallel with MHSA and MLP layers, enabling efficient task-specific tuning with minimal additional parameters.

## Results

UMG-CLIP-E/14 achieves 74.6% average top-1 accuracy across 21 zero-shot classification benchmarks, surpassing EVA-02-CLIP-E/14 (73.6%) by 1.0%. On zero-shot retrieval, UMG-CLIP-E/14 reaches 71.7% R@1 on COCO I2T and 57.5% R@1 on COCO T2I, improving over EVA-02-CLIP-E/14 by 5.3% and 7.0% respectively. For open-vocabulary panoptic segmentation adapted on BigDet Panoptic, UMG-CLIP-E/14 attains 31.6 PQ and 38.2 mIoU on ADE20K zero-shot, setting new state-of-the-art results across all dense prediction benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline EVA-02-CLIP, UMG-CLIP consistently wins across all four model scales (B/16, L/14, g/14, E/14) on retrieval, with the largest gains at the E/14 scale (+5.3% R@1 COCO I2T, +7.0% R@1 COCO T2I). On zero-shot classification, UMG-CLIP-E/14 edges out EVA-02-CLIP-E/14 by 1.0% average top-1 (74.6% vs 73.6%), while UMG-CLIP-L/14 surpasses it by 0.5% (72.1% vs 71.6%). For open-vocabulary panoptic segmentation, UMG-CLIP matches EVA-02-CLIP on seen COCO but significantly outperforms it on zero-shot ADE20K (e.g., 26.2 vs 24.9 PQ for E/14 with COCO Panoptic adaptation; 31.6 vs N/A with BigDet Panoptic). Compared to FC-CLIP, UMG-CLIP-B/16 achieves superior segmentation performance (30.3 vs 34.1 mIoU on A-150 with COCO Panoptic) while using fewer image parameters (86M vs 200M). Against CAT-Seg(G), which employs 1.6B parameters, UMG-CLIP-E/14 (4.7B total but 4.4B image) achieves better A-150 mIoU (38.2 vs 36.2) and A-847 mIoU (17.3 vs 13.3) with BigDet Panoptic adaptation. UMG-CLIP also outperforms MaskCLIP, ODISE, and FreeSeg on panoptic segmentation, and surpasses SAN, OVSeg, and LSeg+ on semantic segmentation benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

UMG-CLIP extends the CLIP/EVA-CLIP contrastive pretraining paradigm by adding explicit region-text alignment alongside the conventional global image-text matching. Prior attempts at fine-grained alignment (MaskCLIP, DetCLIP, CoDet, RegionCLIP) implicitly mine region-text correspondences from image-text pairs, introducing annotation noise; this paper instead constructs explicit multi-granularity annotations (image, region, pixel) via an automated pipeline combining RAM, BLIP2, Shikra, ViTDet, GPT4ROI, SAM, and Mask DINO. The cluster-based token reduction strategy is borrowed from Ailurus, and the downstream adaptation leverages ConvPass modules as parameter-efficient tuning adapters, positioning the work as a data-and-training-paradigm extension rather than an architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UMG-41M dataset (41M images, 389M regions, 11,741 classes) is a substantial resource that could benefit the broader vision-language community beyond this specific model. The finding that explicit multi-granularity supervision improves dense prediction (segmentation) without degrading global tasks (classification, retrieval) validates a unified pretraining approach that avoids the task-specific fine-tuning burden of prior region-aware CLIP variants. Open questions include whether the automated annotation quality (confidence thresholds, NMS filtering) is sufficient at scale, whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D, and whether the 75% token reduction during pretraining introduces information loss that limits further scaling.
