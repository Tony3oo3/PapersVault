## Problem

Existing video autoencoders for latent diffusion either use frame-wise 2D/3D CNNs that struggle with global spatio-temporal structure, or 2D triplane projections (as in PVDM) that capture global context but lose critical local volume information. No prior method effectively unifies long-range global dependencies with short-range local volume details in a single video latent representation, limiting reconstruction fidelity and generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241381/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The autoencoder takes an RGB video clip of shape 3×S×H×W and produces a hybrid 3D latent volume z ∈ R^{C×S'×H'×W'} that fuses 2D triplane projections with 3D wavelet-encoded features. For conditional generation, the input is augmented with a condition latent: a previous clip's encoded latent for long video extension, a single image repeated S times along the temporal axis for image-to-video, or a scalar structural-dissimilarity value between start and end frame latents for dynamics control. The diffusion model (3D U-Net) denoises the hybrid latent and the video decoder reconstructs the output clip.

## Method

HVDM's autoencoder extracts two complementary representations: (i) a 2D triplane latent obtained by projecting a video transformer's 3D features onto three spatio-temporal planes via small transformers, providing global context; and (ii) a 3D volume latent from 3D CNNs applied to Haar wavelet-decomposed subbands (one low-pass and seven high-pass), providing local volume details with an effectively doubled receptive field. These are fused through stacked cross-attention blocks that operate along both temporal and spatial axes, with the triplane latent as query and the wavelet volume latent as key/value. Training uses a composite loss combining L1 reconstruction, LPIPS perceptual, and a novel frequency-matching loss computed in the wavelet domain (λ_freq=0.5), which enforces spectral alignment without adversarial training. A standard 3D U-Net diffusion model is then trained in this hybrid latent space with classifier-free guidance to support unconditional, long-video, image-to-video, and dynamics-controlled generation.

## Results

On UCF-101, HVDM achieves an FVD₁₆ of 399.4, a 3.6× improvement over the next-best baseline LVDM (1431.0) and a 4.1× improvement over StyleGAN-V (1630.2). On SkyTimelapse, HVDM reaches FVD₁₆ of 42.8 versus LVDM's 79.52 and PVDM's 318.9. On TaiChi, HVDM obtains FVD₁₆ of 77.0, outperforming LVDM (120.9) and PVDM (267.0). For the KVD metric, HVDM achieves 1.5 on SkyTimelapse (vs. PVDM 3.1) and 11.9 on TaiChi (vs. PVDM 66.5), confirming superior temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HVDM outperforms all seven baselines on FVD across every dataset. On UCF-101, the 3.6× FVD₁₆ gap over LVDM (399.4 vs. 1431.0) is the largest margin in the table, and HVDM also beats the GAN-based methods (StyleGAN-V 1630.2, DIGAN 1729.6, MoCoGAN-HD 1821.4) by wide margins. On SkyTimelapse, HVDM (42.8) nearly halves the FVD₁₆ of the closest competitor LVDM (79.52) and is 7.4× better than PVDM (318.9), the triplane-only predecessor. On TaiChi, HVDM (77.0) improves over LVDM (120.9) by 36% and over PVDM (267.0) by 71%. For KVD (temporal consistency), HVDM achieves 1.5 on SkyTimelapse versus PVDM's 3.1 and LVDM's 21.73, and 11.9 on TaiChi versus PVDM's 66.5, indicating substantially better frame-to-frame coherence. Notably, PVDM does not report UCF-101 FVD scores, and LVDM lacks FVD₁₂₈ on TaiChi, so HVDM is the only method with complete coverage across all three datasets and both clip lengths.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends PVDM (Yu et al., 2023), which introduced 2D triplane projected latents for video diffusion, by augmenting the triplane with a 3D wavelet-decomposed volume representation to recover local spatio-temporal details that the 2D projections lose. The 3D-to-2D projection backbone relies on the video transformer of Bertasius et al. (2021), while the 3D CNN volume encoder draws on standard spatio-temporal convolutional architectures. The key conceptual departure from PVDM is the hypothesis that global (triplane) and local (wavelet volume) representations are complementary and can be fused via spatio-temporal cross-attention, plus the introduction of a frequency matching loss in the wavelet domain to supervise reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid autoencoder offers a practical recipe for improving video latent quality without adversarial training, and the wavelet-based receptive-field expansion is a lightweight alternative to deeper 3D CNNs. However, the evaluation is confined to three small, relatively low-complexity benchmarks (UCF-101, SkyTimelapse, TaiChi) and the diffusion backbone is explicitly a 'simplified 3D U-Net' used only to validate the autoencoder, leaving open whether the gains transfer to large-scale text-to-video or high-resolution generation. A key open question is whether the wavelet decomposition and frequency matching loss remain beneficial when the latent space is consumed by a more expressive (e.g., transformer-based) diffusion model rather than a small U-Net.
