## Problem

Existing approaches to physical dynamics understanding either depend on ground-truth intermediate structures (e.g., 3D particle graphs) available only in simulated or annotated datasets, or predict raw future pixels, which is high-dimensional and stochastic. Self-supervised video models like VideoMAE and V-JEPA learn useful representations but are typically deployed via transfer learning or fine-tuning that requires annotations, leaving it unclear whether they can be prompted to extract meaningful vision structures without additional supervised training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240638/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The predictor takes a frame pair as input: the first frame is fully visible while the second frame has only a sparse subset of 8×8 patches unmasked (90% masking ratio), and it reconstructs the masked patches of the second frame via MSE loss. At inference, interventions modify the contents of a few visible patches in the second frame (motion prompts) or perturb pixels in the first frame (appearance prompts), producing counterfactual predictions from which keypoints, per-pixel optical flow, and object segments are extracted by comparing counterfactual outputs to observed predictions. For the Physion benchmark, features from the frozen encoder's last layer—optionally augmented with keypoint, flow, and segment features—are fed to a logistic regression classifier to predict contact labels.

## Method

CWM's core mechanism is a temporally-factored masking policy: unlike VideoMAE's random spatiotemporal tube masking, the first frame is kept fully visible while 90% of the second frame is masked, forcing the ViT encoder-decoder to concentrate inter-frame scene transformations into the embeddings of only a few visible second-frame patches. This concentration enables effective counterfactual prompting—modifying those few patches steers the entire next-frame prediction, analogous to interventions in Pearl's Ladder of Causation. Structure extraction then proceeds by comparing counterfactual predictions to observed predictions: keypoints are patch locations whose revelation minimizes reconstruction error, optical flow is computed as the Jacobian of the predictor with respect to infinitesimal first-frame perturbations, and segments are obtained by thresholding flows induced by motion interventions that simulate object displacement. The entire pipeline is trained self-supervised on Kinetics-400 video with no annotations, and all structures are extracted from a single frozen predictor without any fine-tuning on labeled data.

## Results

On the Physion v1.5 benchmark, CWM (ViT-B, 86M params) achieves 75.9% on Object Contact Prediction (OCP) and 89.1% on Object Contact Detection (OCD), setting a new state-of-the-art among self-supervised methods. Scaling to ViT-L (304M params) yields 76.1% OCP and 88.7% OCD, approaching the supervised 3D particle-based SGNN (76.4% OCP, 98.8% OCD) despite being trained on real-world Kinetics-400 video rather than Physion simulations. CWM also outperforms VideoMAE* (73.2% OCP) and V-JEPA (73.4% OCP) under a fair comparison protocol, and dramatically surpasses GPT-4V (52.9% OCP, 54.7% OCD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CWM outperforms all video prediction baselines (MCVD 63.4%, R3M 67.6%, FitVid 64.3%, TECO 69.3% OCP) by 6–12 points, confirming that pixel-level prediction is insufficient for physical dynamics reasoning. Among self-supervised image models, CWM (75.9% OCP) surpasses DINOv2 ViT-g (72.7%) and MAE ViT-H (73.3%) despite having 13× and 7× fewer parameters respectively. Against self-supervised video models, CWM beats VideoMAE* (73.2% OCP, matched patch size and frame count) by 2.7 points and V-JEPA (73.4%) by 2.5 points, while also exceeding VideoMAEv2 ViT-g (72.2%). CWM dramatically outperforms GPT-4V (52.9% OCP, 54.7% OCD), which scores near chance level, highlighting a major limitation of current vision-language models for physical scene understanding. The only method CWM does not surpass is the supervised SGNN (76.4% OCP, 98.8% OCD), which has direct access to ground-truth 3D particle annotations from Physion simulations; CWM ViT-L (76.1% OCP) comes within 0.3 points on OCP while being trained entirely on real-world Kinetics-400 video.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the Counterfactual World Modeling (CWM) framework (Bear et al., 2023) to physical dynamics understanding, departing from VideoMAE's random spatiotemporal tube masking by introducing a temporally-factored policy (0% mask on the first frame, 90% on the second). This asymmetric design concentrates inter-frame transformations into a few patch embeddings, enabling counterfactual prompting to extract keypoints, optical flow, and segments from a single frozen predictor without any annotated fine-tuning. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between structured dynamics prediction (particle graphs, SGNN) that requires ground-truth object representations and unstructured pixel-prediction methods that struggle with high dimensionality, by showing that useful intermediate structures can be elicited from a self-supervised video model via Pearl's Ladder of Causation (association → intervention → counterfactual).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CWM demonstrates that a simple masking-policy change during pre-training unlocks a spectrum of interpretable vision structures from one model, achieving state-of-the-art on Physion (75.9% OCP) with only 86M parameters while approaching the accuracy of supervised particle-graph models (SGNN, 76.4%) that require ground-truth 3D structures. This suggests a path toward scalable, annotation-free physical reasoning for real-world agents. Key limitations include evaluation on a simulated benchmark (Physion) rather than real-world physics scenarios, and the structure extraction procedures (greedy keypoint search, iterative segment refinement) may not scale to complex multi-object scenes. Open questions include whether the temporally-factored masking generalizes to longer temporal contexts, whether the extracted structures improve downstream control tasks, and whether the approach extends beyond contact prediction/detection to richer physical reasoning.
