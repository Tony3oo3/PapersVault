## Problem

Placing a given person realistically in a background scene requires reasoning about both global affordances (where in the scene a person should go) and local affordances (what pose fits that location), yet prior methods either demand large-scale training on curated datasets, are restricted to bounding-box-level local placement, or fail to generalize to novel object categories. No existing approach learns generalized, text-guided human affordances for in-the-wild scenes without expensive training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a background scene image, an action text prompt (e.g., 'a person sitting on a sofa'), and 3–5 unposed photos of the target subject. It produces a coarse semantic placement mask (parameterized as interconnected Gaussian blobs, intentionally not pixel-perfect to allow the inpainting model pose freedom) and a final composited image with the subject inserted in an affordance-consistent pose. All optimization is performed at test time on a single background image, requiring no training data or dataset curation.

## Method

The approach has two stages. First, a parametric semantic mask is represented as K interconnected Gaussian ellipsoids (each defined by center, shared scale, aspect ratio, and rotation angle, with fixed inter-blob spacing) and optimized via Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss from a Stable Diffusion T2I model: a learnable foreground person image is composited with the background through the mask, and gradients from the SDS loss with the action prompt update both the mask parameters and the foreground image until the mask converges to a plausible placement region. Second, the subject's identity is encoded as a single token embedding via Textual Inversion from the few subject photos, and this embedding is injected into the T2I inpainting pipeline alongside the action prompt to perform subject-conditioned inpainting within the optimized mask. The key insight is that the coarse blob mask is deliberately imprecise—pixel-perfect masks actually degrade T2I inpainting—giving the diffusion model room to adapt the person's pose to the scene while the T2I's rich object-scene priors handle the affordance-aware composition.

## Results

On the 30-image in-the-wild evaluation set, the full Text2Place method achieves the best CLIP-similarity (0.2726) and highest person detection rate (88.55% Person), indicating strong text alignment and successful human generation. The method also attains a competitive LPIPS of 0.0934 for background preservation, second only to the ablated single-blob variant Ours(center) (0.0845). The ablation Ours(center), which uses a single blob with fixed scale, achieves the lowest LPIPS but drops in CLIP-sim (0.2613) and % Person (55.52%), confirming that the multi-blob shape is critical for pose-appropriate placement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Text2Place outperforms all four external baselines on CLIP-similarity and person detection rate. Against the object-placement networks GracoNet (53.48% Person) and TopNet (67.30% Person), the full method more than doubles the person generation rate (88.55%) while also improving CLIP-sim by 1.2–1.3 points, showing that bounding-box-only localization is insufficient for pose-aware placement. The VLM baselines perform notably worse: LLaVA achieves only 20.91% Person and the lowest CLIP-sim (0.2501), while GPT-4V reaches 64.18% Person but still trails the proposed method by 24.4 percentage points. On background preservation (LPIPS), the full method (0.0934) beats GracoNet (0.1090), TopNet (0.1162), LLaVA (0.1296), and GPT-4V (0.1059), though the single-blob ablation Ours(center) achieves the best LPIPS (0.0845) at the cost of a 33-point drop in % Person, highlighting the trade-off between mask tightness and successful inpainting. Inpainting comparison with Paint-by-Example (Fig. 9) further shows that reference-based inpainting without text conditioning produces standing poses and inconsistent identity, whereas the proposed text-guided pipeline adapts pose to the scene affordance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from DreamFusion's text-to-3D generation to optimize 2D semantic masks for human placement, a novel application of the SDS framework. The blob-based mask parameterization draws on BlobGAN's ellipsoid representations but adapts them for differentiable, SDS-compatible optimization of placement regions. For the inpainting stage, it combines Textual Inversion for subject identity with Stable Diffusion's text-guided inpainting pipeline, departing from reference-based inpainting methods (e.g., Paint-by-Example) that cannot adapt pose to scene affordances. The key conceptual departure from prior affordance work (Kulal et al., Brooks et al., Ramrakhya et al.) is eliminating large-scale training entirely by distilling object-scene compositional priors implicitly learned in T2I models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to perform text-guided, identity-preserving human placement in arbitrary in-the-wild scenes without large-scale training, opening a practical path for realistic scene composition in creative and AR/VR applications. The counterintuitive finding that coarse (blob-shaped) masks outperform pixel-perfect segmentation masks for T2I inpainting challenges the standard assumption that tighter localization always helps. Limitations include poor handling of small objects, lack of precise pose control, and evaluation on only 30 background images with 15 subjects, leaving open questions about scalability and robustness. The approach's generalization to non-human objects and its compatibility with ControlNet/IP-Adapter suggest a broader template for affordance-aware scene editing.
