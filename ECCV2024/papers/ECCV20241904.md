## Problem

Existing generalizable novel view synthesis methods rely on accurate camera poses, struggle to process high-resolution images due to memory constraints, and require a full network forward pass per query view, making real-time rendering intractable. No prior work combines pose-free operation, high-resolution generalizable 3D-Gaussian Splatting, and real-time rendering in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241904/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N unposed RGB reference images (5 for generalized evaluation, 10 for finetuning) at resolutions ranging from 176×612 (KITTI) to 640×960 (Waymo), and produces a rendered novel-view image, estimated relative camera poses between reference and target views, per-pixel 3D Gaussian points, and a depth map. Unlike pixelSplat which is limited to image pairs, GGRt supports unlimited reference views for large-scale scene synthesis, and the deferred back-propagation mechanism enables training at full resolution (e.g., 384×496) on a single RTX 3090 where standard back-propagation would exhaust memory beyond 2 reference views.

## Method

GGRt jointly trains an Iterative Pose Optimization Network (IPO-Net, a Conv-GRU following the RAFT architecture that iteratively refines relative poses and depth via a reprojection cost) and a Generalizable 3D-Gaussians model (G-3DG, which predicts pixel-aligned Gaussians per image pair using epipolar feature sampling, cross-attention for correspondence, and a Local Self-Attention module for high-resolution patch consistency). A dynamic loss weighting schedule (Eq. 7) balances photometric/depth supervision for pose learning against RGB rendering loss for Gaussian training. A deferred back-propagation mechanism first renders the full image without gradients, caches per-pixel loss gradients, then re-renders patch-by-patch with auto-differentiation to back-propagate, enabling high-resolution training under fixed GPU memory. A progressive Gaussians Cache stores predicted Gaussians keyed by image ID across consecutive iterations, avoiding redundant re-prediction and yielding 2× training and 8× inference speedup, while also enabling real-time rendering at ≥100 FPS by retrieving cached Gaussians for query views.

## Results

On the Waymo dataset (generalized setting), GGRt achieves PSNR of 31.20–34.15 dB, SSIM of 0.892–0.937, and LPIPS of 0.094–0.159, surpassing the best pose-free baseline DBARF by up to 10.13 dB in PSNR (scene 69: 32.10 vs. 21.97). On LLFF, GGRt reaches PSNR of 19.00–29.02 dB, outperforming pose-based IBRNet and PixelSplat in most scenes (e.g., horns: 26.34 vs. 24.96 and 23.86). On KITTI, after fine-tuning, GGRt achieves 22.59 dB PSNR and 0.327 LPIPS with an inference+rendering time of 0.295 s, beating FlowCAM (17.69 dB, 4.170 s) by 4.9 dB in PSNR and 14× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGRt is compared against two pose-based methods (IBRNet, PixelSplat) and four pose-free methods (DBARF, VideoAE, RUST, FlowCAM). Against the strongest pose-free baseline DBARF, GGRt wins on every scene in both Waymo and LLFF, with the largest margin being +10.13 dB PSNR on Waymo scene 69 (32.10 vs. 21.97) and +2.17 dB on LLFF horns (26.34 vs. 24.17). Against pose-based IBRNet, GGRt surpasses it on 7 of 8 LLFF scenes (e.g., +1.38 dB on horns, +0.21 dB on room) and on 5 of 6 Waymo scenes, while PixelSplat is beaten on 6 of 8 LLFF scenes and 4 of 6 Waymo scenes. On KITTI, GGRt (fine-tuned) achieves 22.59 dB PSNR, exceeding FlowCAM (17.69 dB) by 4.9 dB, DBARF (18.36 dB) by 4.23 dB, and even the pose-based IBRNet (22.5 dB) by a narrow margin, while running 14× faster than FlowCAM (0.295 s vs. 4.170 s). Even without fine-tuning (direct transfer from Waymo), GGRt reaches 20.24 dB, still outperforming all pose-free baselines trained specifically on KITTI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: generalizable novel view synthesis (pixelSplat, SRT, GNT), pose-free scene reconstruction (DBARF, VideoAE, RUST, FlowCAM), and explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time rendering. It departs from prior pose-free methods, which all rely on implicit NeRF representations and thus inherit their slow rendering, by adopting an explicit Gaussian representation that enables ≥100 FPS rendering. It also departs from prior generalizable 3D-GS work (pixelSplat), which requires accurate camera poses and only supports image pairs, by jointly learning a pose estimation network (IPO-Net) with the Gaussian prediction model (G-3DG) and supporting arbitrary numbers of reference views. The deferred back-propagation mechanism draws on ARF's patch-wise gradient caching, while the iterative pose refinement follows the RAFT Conv-GRU paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GGRt demonstrates that pose-free generalizable 3D-GS can approach the quality of pose-based methods while delivering real-time rendering, a combination that was previously unattainable. The deferred back-propagation and Gaussians Cache mechanisms are practical engineering contributions that could transfer to other high-resolution 3D-GS pipelines. However, the method still requires multiple nearby reference views, has been validated primarily on forward-facing driving and light-field scenes, and the authors themselves flag more challenging environments (e.g., cross-lane driving in XLD) as future work. Open questions include whether the joint pose-Gaussian learning remains stable in highly dynamic or occluded scenes and whether the approach scales to very long video sequences without cache degradation.
