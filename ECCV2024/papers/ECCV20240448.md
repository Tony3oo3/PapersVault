## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting treats each pixel as an isolated point when shading, making it insensitive to changes in pixel footprint across resolutions and producing severe aliasing artifacts (blurring, jaggies) during zooming or multi-scale rendering. While Mip-Splatting mitigates aliasing via Gaussian prefiltering, its low-pass filtering suppresses high-frequency components and yields over-smoothed results that lose fine detail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240448/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics, following the standard 3DGS training protocol (same parameters, schedule, and loss functions). It produces novel view renderings at arbitrary resolutions, with the key distinction that the pixel window area (1×1 pixel in screen space) is explicitly integrated over rather than sampled at a single point, making the output resolution-aware. The method is implemented as a CUDA extension replacing the shading module in the 3DGS rasterization pipeline.

## Method

Analytic-Splatting replaces the point-sampled 2D Gaussian value in 3DGS with an analytic approximation of the Gaussian integral over the pixel window area. The core tool is a conditioned logistic function S(x) = 1/(1+exp(−1.6x−0.07x³)) that approximates the Gaussian CDF, allowing the window integral to be computed as the difference of two CDF evaluations. For the 2D case, the covariance matrix is eigen-decomposed to decouple the correlation term, the integration domain is rotated to align with eigenvectors, and the 2D integral factors into two independent 1D logistic-based integrals scaled by 2πσ₁σ₂. This approximated window integral replaces g²ᴰ in both the color compositing sum and the transmittance product, making the rendering sensitive to pixel footprint changes without suppressing high-frequency signal components.

## Results

On the multi-scale Blender Synthetic dataset (MTMT setting), Analytic-Splatting achieves an average PSNR of 35.03 dB, SSIM of 0.979, and LPIPS of 0.018, outperforming Mip-Splatting (34.56 dB, 0.979, 0.019) and 3DGS (29.77 dB, 0.960, 0.040) across all resolutions. On the challenging multi-scale Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, it reaches 29.51 dB average PSNR, surpassing Mip-Splatting (29.12 dB) and 3DGS (27.63 dB), though trailing Zip-NeRF (30.58 dB) which uses super-sampling. Under the standard single-scale setting on Tanks & Temples and Deep Blending, Analytic-Splatting achieves 23.84 dB and 29.75 dB PSNR respectively, consistently edging out Mip-Splatting and 3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Analytic-Splatting outperforms 3DGS by a large margin (+5.26 dB average PSNR on Blender Synthetic), confirming that the discrete point-sampling in 3DGS causes severe aliasing at non-training resolutions. Against Mip-Splatting, the closest real-time competitor, Analytic-Splatting gains +0.47 dB PSNR on Blender Synthetic and +0.39 dB on Mip-NeRF 360 (MTMT), with the gap widening at lower resolutions (e.g., +0.50 dB at 1/8 res on Blender), indicating better detail preservation without the over-smoothing of Mip-Splatting's low-pass filtering. On Mip-NeRF 360 (MTMT), Zip-NeRF still leads by +1.07 dB PSNR, but it relies on super-sampling during rendering and is not real-time, whereas Analytic-Splatting maintains real-time capability. Under the standard single-scale setting (Table 3), Analytic-Splatting achieves near-parity with Mip-Splatting (within 0.01–0.06 dB) on Mip-NeRF 360 and Tanks & Temples, and slightly leads on Deep Blending (+0.06 dB), showing consistent generalization. The 3DGS-SS baseline (2× super-sampling) is outperformed by Analytic-Splatting by +2.32 dB on Blender Synthetic, demonstrating that the analytic integral approximation is more effective than brute-force multi-sampling for anti-aliasing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by replacing its discrete point-sampling pixel shading with an analytic approximation of the Gaussian integral over each pixel window, using a conditioned logistic function as a closed-form surrogate for the Gaussian CDF (borrowed from Bowling et al., 2009). Conceptually, it sits between 3DGS's naive center-sampling and Mip-Splatting's prefiltering approach: rather than suppressing high-frequency components via a Gaussian low-pass kernel, Analytic-Splatting directly computes the window-integrated response, preserving detail while eliminating aliasing. The method inherits the full 3DGS training pipeline, rasterization, and Gaussian representation, modifying only the shading and transmittance computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Anti-aliasing in rasterization-based 3D rendering is a practical bottleneck whenever resolution or camera distance changes (zooming, multi-scale training), and this paper provides a clean, differentiable, and GPU-friendly solution that outperforms both super-sampling and prefiltering while retaining high-frequency detail. The main limitation is the added computational cost from extra square-root and exponential operations, reducing frame rate by roughly 10% relative to Mip-Splatting. An open question is whether the small error introduced by rotating the integration domain to align with eigenvectors (necessary to decouple the 2D integral into two 1D integrals) can be further reduced or eliminated, and whether the analytic-integration idea generalizes to non-Gaussian primitives or higher-order moments.
