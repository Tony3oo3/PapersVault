## Problem

Existing conditional diffusion models for large image synthesis are either constrained to their training resolution (e.g., SDXL at 1024×1024) or rely on patch-based generation that fails to capture long-range spatial dependencies. No prior method can controllably generate images at arbitrary resolutions up to 4096×4096 while preserving both global structure and fine local details, a critical need in domains like digital histopathology and remote sensing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240856/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single conditioning embedding vector (extracted via Quilt for histopathology or DINO self-supervised features for satellite imagery, or a binary facial attribute for CelebA-HQ) and a diffusion timestep, and produces a continuous image function that can be evaluated at any set of coordinates to yield an image at arbitrary resolution up to 4096×4096. During training, only 65,536 randomly sampled pixels (~0.4%) from each full-size image are used per iteration, making training on 4096×4096 inputs tractable on standard hardware.

## Method

∞-Brush formulates image generation as a conditional diffusion process in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, where images are represented as functions rather than fixed-resolution pixel grids. The denoiser is a hierarchical neural operator with two levels: a sparse level that applies a sparse neural operator, a novel cross-attention neural operator, and self-attention on randomly sampled pointwise evaluations to capture fine-grained details, and a grid level (UNO/UNet) that aggregates global information after k-NN interpolation to a regular grid. The cross-attention neural operator achieves linear complexity O((N+ΣN_l)d²) in the number of points by pre-multiplying key-value vectors before computing dot products with queries, avoiding the quadratic cost of vanilla attention. A key design insight is that conditioning must be injected at both the sparse (infinite-dimensional) and grid (finite-dimensional) levels, since the coarse grid interpolation alone cannot represent the full function. The theoretical contribution establishes the training objective via the Feldman-Hajek theorem and Radon-Nikodym derivative for Gaussian measures on Hilbert spaces, yielding a closed-form denoising loss analogous to the DDPM simple loss.

## Results

On the TCGA-BRCA 5× dataset (1024×1024), ∞-Brush achieves a CLIP FID of 3.74, substantially outperforming SDXL (6.64) and the patch-based Graikos et al. method (7.43) in global structure fidelity, though with a higher Crop FID of 17.87 versus 6.98 (SDXL) and 15.51 (Graikos). On NAIP (1024×1024), ∞-Brush attains CLIP FID 6.32 vs. 10.90 (SDXL) and 6.86 (Graikos), again leading in global structure but trailing in Crop FID (48.65 vs. 11.50 and 43.76). At the unprecedented 4096×4096 resolution on BRCA 1.25×, ∞-Brush achieves CLIP FID 2.63 vs. 2.75 for the patch-based baseline, with Crop FID 14.76 vs. 11.30. Training requires only 65,536 pixels (≈0.4%) per image, with a constant 12-hour epoch time regardless of target resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

∞-Brush consistently outperforms both SDXL and the patch-based Graikos et al. method on CLIP FID (global structure) across all datasets: 3.74 vs. 6.64/7.43 on BRCA 5× and 6.32 vs. 10.90/6.86 on NAIP. However, it trails on Crop FID (fine local detail), scoring 17.87 vs. 6.98 (SDXL) on BRCA and 48.65 vs. 11.50 (SDXL) on NAIP, which the authors attribute to using a single global embedding rather than 16 local patch conditions. At 4096×4096 resolution, ∞-Brush slightly beats the patch-based method on CLIP FID (2.63 vs. 2.75) but is worse on Crop FID (14.76 vs. 11.30); SDXL cannot operate at this resolution at all. The ablation removing the cross-attention neural operator degrades both metrics (CLIP FID 3.81, Crop FID 16.28), confirming its importance. In terms of compute, ∞-Brush trains in 12 hours per epoch at both 1024×1024 and 4096×4096 with 450M parameters, versus 140 hours for SDXL at 1024×1024 (infeasible at 4096×4096) and 300 hours for the patch-based method at 4096×4096.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the infinite-dimensional diffusion line of work initiated by Kerrigan et al. and ∞-Diff (Bond-Taylor et al.), which demonstrated that images can be represented as functions in Hilbert space to enable arbitrary-resolution synthesis, but which lacked any conditioning mechanism. It also departs from the patch-based large-image generation paradigm (MultiDiffusion, Graikos et al.) that sacrifices long-range spatial coherence for computational tractability, and from finite-dimensional latent diffusion models (SDXL) that are locked to their training resolution. The key architectural contribution is a cross-attention neural operator that performs conditioning in function space with linear complexity in the number of sampled points, filling the gap between unconditional infinite-dimensional diffusion and controllable generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

∞-Brush is the first conditional diffusion model in function space, enabling controllable synthesis at resolutions (up to 4096×4096) that are infeasible for finite-dimensional models due to quadratic memory scaling. The finding that training on just 0.4% of pixels suffices for global structure fidelity is practically significant for domains like digital histopathology and remote sensing where full-image training is prohibitive. However, the consistent Crop FID degradation relative to patch-based and SDXL baselines reveals an unresolved tension between global coherence and local detail, and the authors acknowledge that the model's smaller parameter count (450M vs. 860M/3.5B) and lack of pre-training likely contribute. Open questions include whether local neural operators or knowledge transfer from finite-dimensional pre-trained models can close the fine-detail gap, and whether the function-space formulation scales to even larger or 3D image volumes.
