## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods parameterize synthetic data in raw pixel space, leading to three critical limitations: they require complete re-distillation whenever the images-per-class (IPC) ratio changes, they fail to scale to high-resolution large-scale datasets (e.g., 128×128 ImageNet-1K) and produce visually noisy images, and the distilled data overfits to the training architecture, performing poorly on ResNet, DenseNet, and ViT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real training dataset (RGB images with class labels at resolutions from 32×32 to 256×256) and a pre-trained generative model (BigGAN by default, though CGAN, StyleGAN-XL, and CVAE also work). Rather than outputting a fixed set of synthetic images, D2M produces a refined generative model whose parameters encode the dataset's knowledge; downstream training images are then generated on-the-fly from random noise vectors paired with class labels, allowing any IPC without re-distillation. This means storage complexity remains constant regardless of the number of images per class, unlike pixel-based methods where storage grows linearly with IPC.

## Method

D2M distills dataset knowledge into the learnable parameters of a pre-trained GAN generator rather than into raw pixel values. Two complementary matching losses drive the optimization: an Embedding Matching loss (L_EM) that aligns channel-wise attention maps across multiple feature-extraction layers between real and synthetic images, and a Prediction Matching loss (L_PM) that minimizes KL divergence between temperature-softened output logits of real and synthetic images sharing the same label. A model pool of randomly initialized networks (Depth-n ConvNets and ResNet-18/32 with varying seeds) is sampled at each iteration to extract diverse representations, preventing overfitting to a single architecture. The generator is updated via SGD to minimize L_EM + λ·L_PM over 60 epochs with batch size 128, and the resulting model can synthesize informative training images for any IPC from random noise, eliminating the need for re-distillation when the distillation ratio changes.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, D2M achieves 50.2% (IPC1), 67.8% (IPC10), and 74.4% (IPC50) top-1 accuracy with a Depth-n ConvNet, reaching 88% of the full-dataset upper bound (84.8%) at IPC50. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 29.8% (IPC1) and 51.2% (IPC50), and on Tiny ImageNet it achieves 16.7% (IPC1) and 30.1% (IPC50), corresponding to ~70% of the full-dataset bound at IPC10. On 128×128 ImageNet-1K, D2M attains 8.3% (IPC1) and 28.3% (IPC50), outperforming the best prior (TESLA) by 0.6% at IPC1. The method also scales to 256×256 DermaMNIST, reaching 46.4% (IPC1) and 67.2% (IPC50) versus 21.3% and 38.4% for random sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2M outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every setting on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet. The closest competitors are KIP (49.9% vs. 50.2% on CIFAR-10 IPC1), TESLA (72.6% vs. 74.4% on CIFAR-10 IPC50), FRePo (28.7% vs. 29.8% on CIFAR-100 IPC1; 25.4% vs. 26.1% on Tiny ImageNet IPC10), and IDM (45.1% vs. 46.6% on CIFAR-100 IPC10). On 128×128 ImageNet-1K, D2M surpasses TESLA by 0.6% at IPC1 (8.3% vs. 7.7%) and by 0.4% at IPC50 (28.3% vs. 27.9%), and beats FRePo by 2.6% on ImageWoof IPC1 (32.3% vs. 29.7%). In cross-architecture evaluation on CIFAR-10 IPC50, D2M achieves a 63.6% average across AlexNet, VGG-11, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, and ViT, exceeding the best prior (DAM at 59.7%) by 3.9%. For re-distillation efficiency, D2M requires zero additional GPU hours when changing IPC (vs. up to 42.5 GPU hours for MTT), and maintains constant storage complexity regardless of IPC, saving up to 35× parameters compared to pixel-based methods at IPC50.

## Relation to Prior Work

D2M extends the dataset distillation lineage (DC, DSA, DM, CAFE, DAM, MTT, TESLA, FRePo) by shifting the information container from raw pixel space to the learnable parameters of a pre-trained generative model (BigGAN by default). It inherits the model-pool representation extraction strategy from DataDAM [61], DIM [74], IDM [91], and FRePo [93], but departs from prior generative-model-based distillation (GLaD, IT-GAN) by optimizing the generator's weights rather than its latent codes, which eliminates the need for re-distillation when the images-per-class ratio changes. The two matching losses—channel-attention embedding matching and KL-based soft-logit prediction matching—are novel combinations that jointly capture multi-level discriminative features and class-relationship knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the re-distillation bottleneck: changing IPC from 1 to 50 costs zero additional GPU hours versus up to 42.5 hours for trajectory-matching methods, and storage stays constant regardless of IPC. Scaling to 128×128 ImageNet-1K and 256×256 medical imaging (DermaMNIST) addresses a long-standing limitation of pixel-based distillation that prior works explicitly acknowledged. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation), whether lighter-weight generators can reduce the IPC1 memory overhead, and whether the generative-model parameterization generalizes to other modalities beyond RGB images.
