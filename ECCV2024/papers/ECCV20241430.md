## Problem

Existing vision-based time-to-collision (TTC) estimation methods are bottlenecked by the ~10 Hz frame rate of standard cameras, introducing up to 100 ms of sensing latency that is critical for collision warning. Event cameras can sense at the same rate as scene dynamics, but recovering the full optical flow from event data is intractable because events only report photometric changes along local gradient directions (partial observability of normal flow). This paper addresses how to estimate TTC accurately and efficiently from raw event data despite this fundamental limitation.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an asynchronous raw event stream from an event camera (e.g., DAVIS 640×480 or Prophesee EVKv4 1280×720) together with intensity images from a co-located standard camera, the latter used solely for leading-vehicle detection via YOLOv5+DeepSort to provide a bounding box that restricts the spatio-temporal volume of relevant events. The output is a continuous TTC estimate updated at up to 200 Hz during the blind period between successive camera exposures, with the leading vehicle assumed to be already identified as a prior. The method operates on normalized image coordinates and requires only the relative linear velocity between the two vehicles as the unknown state.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-step geometric model fitting pipeline on event data. First, a robust linear solver (RANSAC) initializes a time-variant affine model using a novel geometric measurement: the inner product of the full flow vector and the normal flow vector equals the squared norm of the normal flow, which circumvents the partial observability of event-based normal flow. Second, a nonlinear Levenberg-Marquardt solver refines the model via spatio-temporal registration, warping events to a common reference time and minimizing their deviation from zero in a proposed Linear Time Surface (LTS) — a signed distance transform that stores the time difference to the nearest event at each pixel, providing a continuous gradient for accurate data association. The time-variant affine model (Eq. 3) replaces the constant-flow assumption of prior work by using Z(t_ref) instead of Z(t_0), capturing the true dynamics of contour expansion over the integration interval. An adaptive LTS rendering strategy and gradient-based robust sampling further ensure stable real-time performance across varying relative speeds.

## Results

On synthetic data, the proposed method achieves 3.58–4.78% relative TTC error (e_TTC) with a runtime of 8–10 ms per estimate, outperforming ETTCM (7.90–8.43% error, 29–34 ms) and Image's FoE (7.80–8.59% error, 37–39 ms) in both accuracy and speed. On real driving data (FCWD), the method achieves 9.84–14.09% e_TTC at 17–25 ms runtime, while CMax attains lower error (2.39–3.04%) but requires 2.5–3.3 s per estimate, making it impractical for real-time use. The full FCW system outputs TTC at up to 200 Hz, compared to the ~10 Hz update rate of standard frame-based cameras, with an algorithmic latency of only 5–10 ms after initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMax [10] achieves the best e_TTC on both synthetic (3.25–5.06%) and real data (2.39–4.18%), but its non-least-squares optimization makes it 100–300× slower (0.8–3.3 s vs. 8–25 ms), rendering it unsuitable for real-time collision warning. FAITH [6] is consistently the worst in accuracy across all datasets (25–88% e_TTC), as its FoE estimation relies on an assumption of uniformly distributed normal flow that is violated in the TTC scenario. Image's FoE [36] is competitive in speed (13–39 ms) but yields 3–4× higher e_TTC than the proposed method on synthetic data, and its reported runtime excludes the 30–100 ms inter-frame exposure latency inherent to frame-based cameras. ETTCM [26] is outperformed by the proposed method on every dataset in both accuracy and runtime (e.g., 7.90% vs. 3.58% e_TTC and 29 ms vs. 10 ms on Suburban_Acc), as its incremental formulation does not scale well with event rate. Overall, the proposed method is the only approach that simultaneously achieves sub-5% e_TTC on synthetic data and sub-25 ms runtime across all real-world sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical line of TTC estimation from optical flow divergence (Lee 1976; Nelson & Aloimonos 1989; Meyer 1992) to the event-camera domain, departing from prior event-based approaches that rely on explicit optical flow estimation or Focus-of-Expansion (FOE) shrinking (FAITH, Image's FoE). It builds most directly on the Contrast Maximization framework (Gallego et al. 2018) and spatio-temporal registration for event-based model fitting (Zhou et al. 2021; Liu et al. 2021), but replaces the non-least-squares CMax objective with a two-step RANSAC + Levenberg-Marquardt pipeline and introduces a Linear Time Surface (LTS) representation that yields a signed distance field, avoiding the unilateral gradient truncation of standard Time Surfaces. The closest prior work is McLeod et al. (2022) on event-based divergence for UAV ventral landing, but this paper generalizes the geometric model to handle 2D planar translation and targets the autonomous driving setting with a robust linear initializer based on a novel inner-product measurement that circumvents the partial observability of event-based normal flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first event-based TTC solution targeting autonomous/assisted driving, and the practical impact is substantial: the system achieves ~200 Hz TTC updates with 5–10 ms algorithmic latency, compared to the ~73 ms end-to-end latency of the best image-based pipelines (33 ms exposure gap + ~40 ms computation), which is critical when relative speed changes abruptly. The LTS representation and the geometric measurement for normal-flow initialization are reusable components that could benefit other event-based model-fitting tasks beyond TTC. Limitations include the assumption of constant relative velocity over the estimation window, the requirement of a leading-vehicle detection prior (YOLOv5 + DeepSort), and evaluation on relatively constrained scenarios (same-lane following, limited lateral offsets); open questions include generalization to multi-vehicle interactions, non-planar ego-motion, and integration with higher-level planning stacks.
