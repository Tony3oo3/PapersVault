## Problem

Recovering high-frame-rate sharp global shutter frames from a single rolling shutter blur frame requires simultaneously addressing RS distortion, motion blur, and temporal discontinuity, yet prior methods handle these tasks in isolation or cascade them, leading to cumulative errors and artifacts. No existing approach unifies all three degradations in a single framework that can query sharp frames at arbitrary timestamps across the full exposure window.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240216/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RS blur frame (color or grayscale) and a paired event stream as input, and produces a sequence of sharp GS frames at any desired frame rate (tested up to 31× interpolation) spanning the entire exposure time of the RS frame. A key structural property is that the encoder is invoked exactly once to build a spatial-temporal representation, while the lightweight decoder is called N times to query N output frames, so total inference time grows sub-linearly with the number of interpolated frames (31 ms at 1× to 87 ms at 31×).

## Method

UniINR formulates frame recovery as a spatial-temporal implicit neural representation F(x, t, θ) that maps pixel coordinates and timestamps directly to RGB values, unifying RS correction, deblurring, and interpolation in one forward pass. The architecture has three stages: (1) a sparse-learning-based backbone (inspired by eSL-Net) encodes the RS blur frame and events into a 3D spatial-temporal representation tensor of shape H×W×C; (2) a one-layer MLP embeds per-pixel exposure timestamps (uniform for GS, row-varying for RS) into a temporal tensor of matching dimension; (3) a 5-layer pixel-by-pixel MLP decoder takes the element-wise sum of the two tensors and outputs a sharp frame. Training uses a combined Charbonnier loss on both the reconstructed RS blur frame (integral over the exposure) and the GS sharp frames, with learning-based position embedding outperforming sinusoidal encoding by up to 1.11 dB. The entire model contains only 0.38M parameters and achieves 2.83 ms per frame at 31× interpolation, versus >177 ms for the EvUnRoll+TimeLens cascade.

## Results

On the Fastec-Orig RS correction benchmark, UniINR achieves 33.91 dB PSNR, 0.9234 SSIM, and 0.049 LPIPS with only 0.38M parameters, surpassing the previous SOTA (EvShutter, 32.41 dB) by 1.5 dB. On the combined RS correction + deblurring task (Gev-Orig), UniINR reaches 31.47 dB PSNR, outperforming EvUnRoll by 1.33 dB. For the full three-task setting (RS correction + deblurring + VFI at 3× interpolation on Gev-Blur), UniINR achieves 28.36 dB PSNR versus 21.86 dB for the EvUnRoll+TimeLens cascade, a gap of 6.5 dB, while using 1/245th the parameters (0.38M vs. 93.03M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniINR outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated task. Among frame-based RS correction methods, it surpasses the strongest (CVR, 28.72 dB) by 5.19 dB on Fastec-Orig, while using 1/112th the parameters. Among event-based RS correction methods, it beats EvShutter (32.41 dB) by 1.5 dB and EvUnRoll (31.32 dB) by 2.59 dB, all while requiring only 1 input frame versus 1–2 for competitors. For the combined RS correction + deblurring task on Gev-Orig, UniINR (31.47 dB) exceeds EvUnRoll (30.14 dB) by 1.33 dB and NIRE (29.86 dB) by 1.61 dB. In the full three-task setting (RS + deblur + VFI), the cascade EvUnRoll+TimeLens (21.86 dB at 3×) and DeblurSR (17.64 dB at 3×) are beaten by 6.5 dB and 10.7 dB respectively, with the gap widening at higher interpolation factors (up to 8.49 dB over EU+TL at 9×). The eSL-Net* variant (31.64 dB on Gev-Blur) is also surpassed by UniINR (33.12 dB) under the same gray-frame protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the implicit neural representation (INR) paradigm—previously applied to super-resolution (LIIF, VideoINR) and event-guided video SR (EG-VSR)—to a fundamentally different problem: unifying rolling shutter correction, motion deblurring, and frame interpolation into a single spatial-temporal mapping. The sparse-learning encoder backbone draws directly from eSL-Net's multi-task event-guided design, while the exposure-time embedding and pixel-by-pixel MLP decoder are novel components tailored to the RS/GS exposure geometry. The key conceptual departure from prior event-guided RS correction (EvUnRoll, EvShutter) and VFI (TimeLens, DeblurSR) work is the rejection of cascaded pipelines in favor of a single INR that jointly models all three degradations, avoiding the cumulative error problem that plagues sequential approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified INR formulation offers a principled alternative to cascading separate restoration networks, and the 0.38M-parameter model achieving competitive or superior results against 20–93M-parameter cascades is a meaningful efficiency contribution for embedded or real-time event-camera systems. However, all quantitative evaluations are conducted on simulated datasets (Fastec, Gev), with real-world results limited to qualitative visual comparisons, leaving open questions about robustness to real sensor noise, varying event rates, and higher-resolution scenes. The approach also assumes access to synchronized event streams, constraining applicability to hybrid camera setups, and the single-encoder-call efficiency advantage may diminish if the STR representation must be re-computed for significantly different exposure conditions.
