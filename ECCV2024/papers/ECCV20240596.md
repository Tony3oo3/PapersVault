## Problem

Existing NIR-to-visible translation methods are predominantly data-driven and overlook the fundamental spectral disparities between NIR and visible imaging—illumination spectra vary significantly across lighting conditions, and objects exhibit distinct reflectance curves in the two bands. Additionally, paired NIR-VIS training data is scarce and misaligned because mainstream collection relies on manual filter switching, restricting capture to static scenes. These two limitations jointly constrain the fidelity and generalizability of current translation approaches.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel NIR image (either the 700–800 nm or 820–1000 nm band) as input and produces a full-color visible RGB image as output. A segmentation mask derived from the NIR input via a pre-trained model (Segment Anything) serves as an auxiliary object-aware prior fed into the reflection estimation branch. The proposed FANVID dataset provides 2048×1536 resolution pairs captured simultaneously by a multi-sensor coaxial camera, ensuring pixel-level alignment even in dynamic scenes.

## Method

Grounded in Retinex theory, the framework decomposes the NIR image into an object-independent luminance component and an object-specific reflection component via a DecomposeNet (CNN with attention and residual connections). A LuminanceNet (encoder-decoder) translates the NIR luminance to its visible-spectrum counterpart, while an Object-Guided ReflectionNet (transformer-based) estimates the visible reflection using segmentation masks as object priors to capture per-object spectral reflectance differences. The final visible image is reconstructed by element-wise multiplication of the estimated visible luminance and reflection. The authors also introduce FANVID, a 5144-pair fully aligned NIR-VIS dataset collected with a JAI multi-sensor coaxial camera, which eliminates the misalignment inherent in filter-switching pipelines and supports dynamic-scene training.

## Results

On the newly introduced FANVID dataset (NIR1 band, 700–800 nm), the proposed method achieves PSNR 25.57, SSIM 0.87, Delta-E 5.78, and FID 37.15, outperforming the strongest baseline Retinexformer (PSNR 24.61, FID 39.72) by ~1 dB in PSNR and ~2.6 in FID. On the NIR2 band (820–1000 nm), the method still leads with PSNR 23.37 and FID 48.98. On the public EPFL and ICVL benchmarks, the method attains PSNR 18.41 / 27.47 and FID 113.90 / 82.95 respectively, again ranking first across all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, Delta-E, FID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all three datasets (FANVID, EPFL, ICVL). The closest competitor is Retinexformer [3], a low-light enhancement transformer; the gap is modest on FANVID NIR1 (~1 dB PSNR, ~2.6 FID) but widens on the more challenging NIR2 band (~0.9 dB PSNR, ~2 FID) and on EPFL (~0.5 dB PSNR, ~17 FID). The dedicated NIR2VIS baselines—NIRcolor [6] and TLM [30]—are substantially outperformed, with NIRcolor trailing by over 9 dB in PSNR on FANVID NIR1, indicating that end-to-end GAN-based colorization struggles with the luminance–chrominance gap. General image-to-image translation methods (pix2pix [18], CycleGAN [52], FastCUT [35]) and the colorization transformer CT2 [42] also fall well behind, confirming that task-agnostic translation architectures do not capture the spectral-specific reflectance differences between NIR and visible bands. On the downstream detection task (YOLOv8n on 40 annotated FANVID test images), the proposed method achieves the highest recognition rates for all four object classes (e.g., 0.949 for cars vs. 0.915 for CycleGAN), further validating the quality of the translated visible images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes classical Retinex theory (image = reflectance × luminance) for the NIR-to-visible translation task, a decomposition previously applied to low-light enhancement but not to cross-spectral conversion. Rather than treating NIR2VIS as a direct image-to-image mapping (as pix2pix, CycleGAN, NIRcolor, and TLM do), it splits the problem into an object-independent luminance estimation sub-task and an object-specific reflectance estimation sub-task guided by SAM segmentation priors. The FANVID dataset departs from prior NIR-VIS collections (EPFL, DVD, FMSVP) that rely on manual filter switching and thus suffer from misalignment and static-scene limitations, by using a multi-sensor coaxial camera for simultaneous, pixel-accurate capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FANVID dataset (5,144 fully aligned pairs at 2048×1536, including dynamic scenes and varied weather) addresses a genuine bottleneck in NIR2VIS research where existing paired data are small, misaligned, and restricted to static scenes. The object-aware decomposition offers interpretability absent in end-to-end GAN approaches and provides a principled way to handle illumination variance across lighting conditions. Open questions include whether the Retinex-style decomposition generalizes to indoor or low-light NIR scenarios, whether the segmentation prior can be learned jointly rather than as a frozen external model, and whether the approach extends to other spectral translation pairs (e.g., thermal-to-visible).
