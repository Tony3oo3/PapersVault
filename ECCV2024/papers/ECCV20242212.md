## Problem

Federated domain generalization requires training a model that generalizes to unseen test domains using data isolated across clients, where each client holds data from only one training domain. Existing approaches either relax federated privacy constraints, require expensive Hessian computations, or are restricted to the simplified setting where each domain maps to exactly one client. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a flatness-based regularization that operates entirely within the strict FedAvg privacy framework without second-order derivatives.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from multiple training domains (RotatedMNIST at 28×28, VLCS/PACS at 224×224, OfficeHome at 224×224) distributed across federated clients, with the hard constraint that no single client holds data from more than one domain. It produces a class label for an image drawn from a held-out unseen test domain via leave-one-domain-out evaluation. Weight perturbation is applied only to the final classifier layers (one linear layer for RotatedMNIST, two for the others), not to the CNN feature encoder.

## Method

FedLGF augments the standard FedAvg local ERM loss with two adversarial flatness regularizers: a local flatness term that minimizes KL divergence between the local model's predictions and those of a perturbed copy of itself, and a global flatness term that minimizes KL divergence between the local model's predictions and those of a perturbed global model (simulating other clients' predictions without data sharing). Both terms use a min-max formulation where the inner maximization finds the worst-case perturbation direction within an r-ball, approximated by a single gradient step on a random initial perturbation. Two perturbation strategies are proposed: additive (θ + ε) and singular-value perturbation, which perturbs the singular values in the SVD of each weight matrix and applies ReLU to filter negative values. The paper further derives a generalization gap bound on the unseen test domain that the method directly minimizes, and validates the approach under both full (one domain per client) and partial (50 clients, sparse participation) federated scenarios.

## Results

On the partial participation scenario (50 clients, sparse participation per round), FedLGF with singular perturbation achieves the best average testing accuracy of 78.4% across RotatedMNIST, VLCS, PACS, and Office-Home, outperforming the strongest baseline FedIIR (77.0%) by 1.4 pp. In the full participation scenario (each domain as one client), FedLGF with additive perturbation reaches 81.9% average accuracy, surpassing FedIIIR (81.1%) by 0.8 pp. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on all four datasets in both scenarios, with the largest gains over FedAvg being 7.1 pp (partial) and 1.3 pp (full).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedLGF outperforms all four baselines on every dataset in both the partial and full participation scenarios. Against the strongest competitor FedIIR/FedIIIR (which uses inter-client gradient alignment), FedLGF gains 1.0–1.4 pp average accuracy in the partial scenario and 0.8 pp in the full scenario, with the largest per-dataset margin being +2.1 pp on PACS (78.5% vs 75.4%) in the partial setting. Compared to FedADG (domain alignment via reference distribution), FedLGF improves by 7.1 pp average in the partial scenario, where FedADG struggles particularly on VLCS (60.7%) and Office-Home (60.1%). FedSR (representation regularization + conditional mutual information) is the weakest baseline, trailing FedLGF by 8.6 pp average in the partial scenario, with a notable 11.1 pp gap on Office-Home. The two perturbation variants show complementary strengths: additive perturbation is best on VLCS in the partial scenario (75.6%) and on the full-scenario average (81.9%), while singular perturbation leads on RotatedMNIST, PACS, and Office-Home in the partial scenario (78.4% average).

## Relation to Prior Work

FedLGF builds on the FedAvg federated learning framework and the flatness-seeking lineage of SAM and SWAD, but departs from their min-max formulation on the ERM loss by instead using a KL-divergence regularizer that penalizes prediction divergence under adversarial weight perturbation. Within the federated domain generalization line (FedADG, FedSR, FedIIR, StableFDG), it distinguishes itself by avoiding Hessian computation and by not relaxing the strict data-privacy constraints that some prior methods require. The SVD-based singular value perturbation is a novel perturbation mechanism not previously explored in flatness-aware optimization, and the global flatness term—using an adversarially perturbed global model to simulate cross-client prediction diversity—is a new regularization idea specific to the federated setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, privacy-respecting approach to federated domain generalization with a clean theoretical bound that directly justifies minimizing the proposed loss terms. However, the performance gains over the strongest baseline (FedIIR) are modest (roughly 1–2 percentage points), and the perturbation is applied only to classifier layers—extending it to convolutional layers degrades performance, limiting the method's applicability to deeper architectures. Open questions include whether the dual flatness regularization transfers to non-i.i.d. federated settings where clients share domains, whether SVD perturbation scales to large models where full SVD is prohibitive, and whether the approach generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities or tasks.
