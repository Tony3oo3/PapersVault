## Problem

Existing Blind Image Decomposition (BID) methods and datasets model adverse weather as spatially uniform, ignoring the physical reality that weather effects vary with scene depth (e.g., rain streaks dominate in the foreground while fog and low-light intensify in the background). This depth-uniform assumption, combined with the absence of depth-aware BID benchmarks, limits the scalability of current approaches to real-world images with arbitrary weather combinations.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single corrupted RGB image containing an arbitrary combination of rain streak, fog, raindrop, snow, and low-light degradation as input. It jointly produces a restored clean image, a predicted scene depth map at quarter resolution, a bipolar transmittance map T in [-1,1] encoding positional and intensity information of depth-variant weather, and a noise residual N capturing depth-invariant lens occlusion effects (e.g., raindrop blur). The final restoration is computed as I_gen = T ⊙ I_noisy + N, explicitly separating multiplicative and additive degradation factors.

## Method

DeBNet employs a searchable multi-branch encoder (alternating Interaction and Refinement modules with MixConv and lightweight Transformer paths) followed by two decoder branches: a transmittance decoder that predicts scene depth with direct supervision and then applies a Depth-aware Bipolar Attention (DBA) module to generate the transmittance map, and a noise decoder that produces high-resolution features re-weighted by learned attention channels and refined via Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) to yield the noise residual. Neural Architecture Search (DARTS-based with resource-aware L1 penalty) adaptively selects optimal convolution kernel sizes and transformer token counts within the multi-branch search space, allowing the architecture to favor small kernels for local degradations (raindrop, snow) and large receptive fields for global ones (fog, low-light). Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining depth L2, RGB L2, source adversarial, and multi-class weather combination classification terms, eliminating the need for post-search fine-tuning.

## Results

On the proposed BID-CityScapes dataset, DeBNet achieves 29.51 PSNR / 0.916 SSIM under the most challenging case (heavy rain + heavy fog), outperforming the next-best baseline CPNet by 2.54 dB in PSNR. On BID-GTAV, DeBNet reaches 23.88 PSNR / 0.751 SSIM on the same case, again leading CPNet by 1.58 dB. On real-world benchmarks, DeBNet attains 36.69 PSNR / 0.965 SSIM on SPAdata (deraining) and 33.50 PSNR / 0.986 SSIM on SOTS-outdoor (dehazing), surpassing task-specific methods RCDNet, AirNet, and FFA-Net. The two-stage fine-tuned variant (DeBNet⁺) further improves to 36.77 PSNR on SPAdata and 33.82 PSNR on SOTS-outdoor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeBNet consistently outperforms all three BID-specific baselines (TransWeather, BIDeN, CPNet) across every weather combination on both proposed datasets, with the largest margin over CPNet reaching 2.54 dB PSNR on BID-CityScapes case (1). Against the all-in-one TransWeather [50], DeBNet gains 6.44 dB PSNR on BID-CityScapes case (1), highlighting the advantage of explicit depth-aware decomposition over a generic transformer restoration pipeline. On real-world SPAdata, DeBNet (36.69 PSNR) surpasses the deraining specialist RCDNet [52] (34.08) and the all-in-one AirNet [27] (34.05); on SOTS-outdoor, it edges out FFA-Net [42] (33.07) and AirNet (33.09) by ~0.4 dB. The diffusion-based WeatherDiff [39] is outperformed by DeBNet on all four evaluated settings (e.g., 29.51 vs 28.11 PSNR on BID-CityScapes case-1; 36.69 vs 35.32 on SPAdata). Finally, the NAS-searched DeBNet (39.15M params) achieves comparable or better PSNR than the larger Uformer [58] baseline (50.90M params) while using ~23% fewer parameters, confirming the efficiency of the architecture search.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Blind Image Decomposition (BID) framework introduced by Han et al. (ECCV 2022) and CPNet (Wang et al., 2023) by incorporating scene depth into the weather degradation model, arguing that existing BID methods and datasets treat weather as depth-uniform when in reality fog, rain streaks, and low-light effects vary with distance. The depth-aware transmittance formulation draws on Hu et al. (CVPR 2019) for depth-conditioned rain modeling, while the multi-branch encoder leverages the NAS methodology from HR-NAS (Ding et al., CVPR 2021) and the DARTS differentiable search framework. The key conceptual departure is separating the restoration into a depth-guided transmittance map (bipolar, spatial) and a depth-guided noise residual (channel-attended), rather than learning a single additive residual as in prior depth-aware deraining work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two introduced datasets (BID-CityScapes, BID-GTAV) are the first to provide depth-annotated multi-weather BID training pairs, filling a gap that has limited the transfer of BID research to real-world scenarios. The depth-aware formulation is physically well-motivated and the NAS component shows that different weather types genuinely prefer different receptive-field sizes, offering a principled way to handle arbitrary degradation combinations without task-specific retraining. However, the approach remains constrained by synthetic depth maps (from CityScapes camera parameters or GTAV game engines), is limited to five weather types, and the real-world evaluation relies on single-weather benchmarks rather than true multi-weather outdoor captures, leaving open whether the depth prior holds under uncontrolled real-world conditions.
