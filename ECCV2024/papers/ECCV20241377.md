## Problem

Web-crawled pre-training datasets for video-text retrieval lack specific event content in captions and explicit temporal transitions between events, while the standard video-level contrastive learning paradigm in dual-encoder models cannot simultaneously capture fine-grained event content and complex event temporal alignment. The study of video events as fundamental components of video understanding has been largely overlooked in the video-text retrieval literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241377/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes three types of video-text pairs: original web-crawled pairs, frame-level pairs generated by Event Content Augmentation (a single frame per clip paired with a BLIP-generated caption), and synthesized video-text pairs from Event Temporal Augmentation (frames concatenated from two different videos paired with concatenated captions). During inference, only the video-level representation extracted from the multi-granularity encoder is used to compute cosine similarity with the text query, preserving the efficiency of a standard dual-encoder retriever with over 30× speed advantage over joint-encoder methods.

## Method

EA-VTR introduces Frame [CLS] tokens into a TimeSformer-based video encoder, enabling simultaneous extraction of video-level and frame-level visual representations with negligible computational overhead. Two data augmentation strategies enrich the pre-training data: Event Content Augmentation (ECA) generates frame-level captions via BLIP with Top-p sampling to capture per-clip event content, and Event Temporal Augmentation (ETA) synthesizes new videos by concatenating consecutive frames from two different videos to create explicit event transitions. Training combines three contrastive objectives—Video-Text Alignment (VTA) on original pairs, Event Content Learning (ECL) aligning frame features with frame captions, and Event Temporal Learning (ETL) aligning synthesized video features with their captions—using an Alternating Iteration Training scheme that separates original and synthesized pairs across batches to avoid interference from their differing information densities.

## Results

On zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, EA-VTR achieves R@10 of 62.3 on MSRVTT and 68.9 on DiDeMo, surpassing the best dual-encoder baselines (TCP at 57.6 on MSRVTT, Miles at 63.6 on DiDeMo) by 4.7% and 5.3% respectively. On fine-tuned retrieval, EA-VTR reaches R@1 of 39.5 on MSRVTT and 43.7 on DiDeMo, outperforming all dual-encoder methods. For zero-shot video action recognition, EA-VTR achieves 61.0% mean top-1 accuracy on UCF101, an 8.3% improvement over Miles (52.7%). On the Test of Time task, EA-VTR achieves 61.6% time-order consistency, exceeding the second-best method by 11.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EA-VTR outperforms all dual-encoder baselines across all four retrieval datasets. On MSRVTT, it improves R@10 by 4.7% over TCP (57.6→62.3) and by 5.4% over Miles (56.9→62.3); on DiDeMo, the gains are even larger at +5.3% R@10 over Miles (63.6→68.9) and +9.2% over CLIP-ViP (59.7→68.9), which uses 100× more training data. Against joint-encoder methods, EA-VTR matches or exceeds Singularity (R@10: 62.3 vs 59.5 on MSRVTT) and Rap (R@10: 62.3 vs 56.8 on MSRVTT) while being over 30× faster at inference. On video action recognition (UCF101), EA-VTR leads Miles by 8.3% mean accuracy (61.0 vs 52.7) and MCQ by 7.9% (61.0 vs 53.1). On the Test of Time task, EA-VTR achieves 61.6% time-order consistency versus 50.0–50.3% for Frozen, MCQ, and Miles, demonstrating a clear advantage in temporal logic understanding. On Video Moment Retrieval, EA-VTR surpasses the task-specific methods LocVTP and TCP by 1.9% and 1.5% on R@1^0.5 respectively, despite not being specifically designed for that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-encoder video-text retrieval lineage (Frozen, Miles, MCQ, OA-Trans) by introducing event-aware learning at both the data and model levels. It builds directly on the TimeSformer-based video encoder from Frozen, adding Frame [CLS] tokens to enable simultaneous frame-level and video-level representations — a modification that prior dual-encoder methods lacked. Conceptually, it departs from fine-grained alignment approaches that target objects (OA-Trans), actions (MCQ), or regions (RegionLearner) by focusing specifically on video events as the unit of cross-modal alignment, and from prior data augmentation work (Cap4Video, CLIP-ViP, BSP) by augmenting both video and text modalities to supply missing event content and temporal transitions simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving joint-encoder-level retrieval accuracy with a 30× speed advantage, making event-aware retrieval feasible for real-time applications. The validation across multi-event retrieval, moment retrieval, and temporal logic tasks demonstrates that event understanding is a transferable capability rather than a task-specific trick. Limitations include reliance on an external image captioner (BLIP) for ECA, the assumption that a single frame adequately represents a short clip, and the use of only 2.5M WebVid pairs for pre-training. The authors note future directions toward video QA and captioning, and the modest absolute performance on Test of Time (61.6%) suggests temporal reasoning in video remains an open challenge.
