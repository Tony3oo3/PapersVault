## Problem

Existing rPPG methods assume single-session training on a fixed dataset, but real-world deployment requires sequential learning on diverse datasets with varying lighting, skin color, and motion. This domain incremental learning setting triggers catastrophic forgetting, yet standard continual learning solutions—rehearsal-based replay (impractical for long videos and privacy), class-prototype methods (inapplicable to continuous regression), and prompt-based PEFT (empirically ineffective for rPPG)—do not transfer directly.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB facial video clips of 160 frames at 96×96 resolution (sampled with a step of 80), with the face region cropped by MTCNN on the first frame and fixed across subsequent frames. It outputs a continuous rPPG signal of length T (aligned via cubic spline interpolation), from which video-level heart rate is estimated. Because the target is a continuous signal rather than discrete class labels, the method must handle regression-specific challenges in both training and inference.

## Method

ADDP freezes a Uniformer backbone (with a DiffNorm input module that concatenates appearance and frame-difference features) after the initial task and finetunes only lightweight adapters (bottleneck ratio 0.25) plus a regression head for subsequent tasks. To combat forgetting without storing old samples, it extracts two types of label-irrelevant domain prototypes per task: style prototypes (channel-wise mean/variance of shallow features, clustered via KMeans) and noise prototypes (small-singular-value components of high-level features via SVD). During training on new tasks, it randomly applies AdaIN style transfer and SVD-based noise injection from old prototypes to new samples (probability p=0.5), generating pseudo-old-domain inputs that reinforce past knowledge. At inference, it transfers each test sample's style to the prototype with the lowest recorded training MAE—i.e., the style the model handles most proficiently—thereby converting potentially forgotten or challenging domains into a familiar form without requiring task identification.

## Results

On the rPPG DIL protocol (6 tasks from 5 datasets), ADDP achieves a final incremental MAE_N of 3.70±0.07 bpm, Std_N of 5.56±0.04 bpm, RMSE_N of 5.59±0.02 bpm, and R_N of 0.83±0.01, significantly narrowing the gap to the joint-training upper bound (MAE_N = 3.45, R_N = 0.85). The method outperforms the strongest baseline S-Prompts by 0.15 bpm in MAE_N and 0.02 in R_N, while the lower bound (naive full finetuning) degrades to MAE_N = 6.54 bpm and R_N = 0.69, confirming severe catastrophic forgetting without countermeasures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADDP outperforms all five rehearsal-free baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor S-Prompts (which uses task-specific prompts/adapters with KMeans-based task prediction), ADDP reduces MAE_N from 3.85 to 3.70 bpm and improves R_N from 0.81 to 0.83, attributed to ADDP's shared adapter group enabling knowledge transfer and its domain prototypes avoiding the feature interference caused by clustering features with different heart-rate labels. Regularization-based methods (EWC, LwF, ANCL variants) achieve moderate improvements over the lower bound but remain 0.4–0.8 bpm worse in MAE_N than ADDP, as their strict stability constraints reduce plasticity. LAE, a state-of-the-art PEFT-based class incremental learning method, performs poorly in the rPPG regression setting: LAE-offline and LAE-ensemble degrade to MAE_N ≈ 8 bpm (worse than the lower bound), while LAE-online reaches only 4.70 bpm, because its ensemble mechanism relies on predicted logits that are ill-defined for continuous signal regression. Overall, ADDP closes roughly 80% of the gap between the lower bound and the joint-training upper bound in MAE_N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the parameter-efficient finetuning (PEFT) paradigm from S-Prompts [52] to rPPG measurement, replacing task-specific prompts with a single shared adapter group and introducing domain prototypes (style via channel statistics, noise via SVD) as a regression-friendly alternative to the class prototypes used in rehearsal-free DIL. The backbone design builds on Uniformer's unified convolution-attention architecture and extends EfficientPhys's normalization with a DiffNorm module that fuses appearance and dynamic features. Conceptually, the work bridges two previously disconnected literatures—rPPG signal regression and domain incremental learning—by arguing that the continuous-label nature of rPPG renders standard class-prototype and logit-based continual learning strategies inapplicable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first domain incremental learning protocol for rPPG across five heterogeneous datasets, this paper gives the community a concrete benchmark and reveals that catastrophic forgetting is a real and previously unaddressed problem in remote physiological measurement. The rehearsal-free, privacy-preserving design (no stored samples) makes the approach practical for real-world deployment where facial video retention is undesirable. Key open questions include whether the method extends to online continual learning (single-pass data streams), whether the reliance on a strong pre-trained backbone limits applicability when initial data is small, and whether the SVD-based noise extraction generalizes to more complex or non-stationary noise sources.
