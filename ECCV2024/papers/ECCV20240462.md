## Problem

Existing vision-language pre-training datasets rely on short captions that barely capture the rich content of real images, and there is no clear evidence on whether or how long captions could improve contrastive language-image pre-training. This paper investigates that question by re-captioning 30M images with detailed multi-sentence descriptions from a pre-trained MLLM and studying how to exploit the resulting long captions under a contrastive learning framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240462/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224x224 RGB images and text sequences (truncated or padded to 77 tokens) as input. Unconventionally, the text side is not a single caption but a dynamically sampled set of K sub-captions drawn uniformly from the union of the original caption, an MLLM-generated short caption, and individual sentences of an MLLM-generated long caption, yielding multiple positive text pairs per image. The output is a pair of 512-dimensional embeddings (image and text) in a shared space, which downstream supports zero-shot classification, image-text retrieval ranking, and semantic segmentation via a frozen encoder plugged into SAN.

## Method

DreamLIP retains the standard CLIP dual-encoder architecture (ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16 image encoder, Transformer text encoder) but modifies the training objective in two ways. First, it applies a multi-positive contrastive loss (following LaCLIP) where K sub-captions are uniformly sampled per image, so the global image embedding is pulled toward multiple text embeddings simultaneously. Second, it introduces a subcaption-specific grouping loss: cross-attention weights between each sub-caption embedding and the image patch grid are thresholded by a sparsity parameter to select a small set of local patches, which are then pooled into a local embedding and contrastively matched to its own sub-caption while being repelled from other sub-captions' pooled patches. The total loss is a weighted sum of the multi-positive contrastive term and the grouping term, trained for 32 epochs with a learnable temperature initialized at 0.07.

## Results

On zero-shot image-text retrieval (YFCC15M pre-training), DreamLIP achieves 84.9 R@1 on Flickr30k and 55.7 R@1 on MSCOCO (text retrieval), substantially outperforming CLIP (34.9 / 20.8) and the strongest prior method ALIP (70.5 / 46.8). Trained on only 30M pairs (Merged-30M), DreamLIP reaches 87.2 R@1 on Flickr30k text retrieval, surpassing CLIP trained on 400M LAION pairs (78.7 R@1). On semantic segmentation (Merged-30M), DreamLIP achieves 37.3 average mIOU across five benchmarks, exceeding CLIP on LAION-400M (34.8 average mIOU) by 2.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamLIP consistently outperforms all ten baselines across retrieval, segmentation, and MLLM benchmarks. On YFCC15M text retrieval (Flickr30k R@1), DreamLIP (84.9) surpasses the strongest prior method ALIP (70.5) by 14.4 points and CLIP (34.9) by 50 points. Among contrastive variants, MCD (57.6), UniCLIP (52.3), DeCLIP (51.4), and SLIP (47.8) all trail DreamLIP by 27–37 R@1 points on the same metric. On semantic segmentation (Merged-30M, average mIOU), DreamLIP (37.3) exceeds CLIP on LAION-400M (34.8) by 2.5 points, while HiCLIP on Merged-30M (53.5 avg classification) is outperformed by DreamLIP (55.7 avg). On zero-shot classification (ViT-B/32, Merged-30M), DreamLIP (55.7 avg) still trails CLIP on LAION-400M (62.7 avg) by 7 points, indicating the data-scale gap persists for single-label recognition. In the MLLM setting (Table 4), DreamLIP on 30M (55.0 avg) matches CLIP on 400M (54.5 avg), with the largest gain on MMVP (22.7 vs. 19.7). LaCLIP and MLLM-A (ViT-B/16 comparisons) are both outperformed by DreamLIP on classification (e.g., 50.2 vs. 48.4 and 47.5 on CC12M average).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamLIP extends the CLIP contrastive pre-training framework by replacing short web-scraped captions with long, MLLM-generated descriptions and exploiting their sentence-level structure. It directly adopts the multi-positive contrastive loss formulation from LaCLIP for sampling sub-captions, but departs from all prior synthetic-caption work (ALIP, LaCLIP, MLLM-A) by using long captions rather than short rewrites, and introduces a novel subcaption-specific grouping loss that aligns individual sentences with local image patches in a self-supervised manner. The method thus sits at the intersection of two threads: synthetic-data-enhanced vision-language pre-training and fine-grained local alignment (FILIP, PyramidCLIP), but re-purposes both through the lens of long-caption structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The headline result—that 30M image-text pairs with long captions match or exceed CLIP trained on 400M pairs on retrieval and segmentation—suggests a substantial data-efficiency gain that could reduce the compute and curation costs of vision-language pre-training. However, the comparison is partially confounded by differences in data source and caption quality, and the generated long captions still contain hallucinations, which limits the ceiling of the approach. Open questions include whether the gains persist at larger model scales, whether the grouping loss transfers to architectures beyond ViT-B, and how sensitive performance is to the specific MLLM used for caption generation.
