## Problem

Recurrent temporal fusion in BEV-based 3D detection is significantly less effective than parallel fusion at exploiting long-term history, despite offering 6× lower latency and 12× less memory. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) historical BEV features are detached during training so their gradients never reach the fusion module, and (2) coarse BEV grid granularity introduces semantic ambiguity (especially orientation errors) when aligning features across ego-motion transforms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241919/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6 synchronized RGB camera images (256×704 for real-time, up to 640×1600 for test-set evaluation) along with the inter-frame ego-motion matrix. It outputs 3D bounding boxes for 10 object classes (center, size, orientation, velocity, attribute) via a CenterHead on the fused BEV feature map. During inference, only a single cached BEV feature is stored and updated sequentially, whereas the ego-motion matrix is additionally fed into the TA-GRU to generate per-channel motion embeddings that modulate the fusion weights.

## Method

RecurrentBEV introduces two key modifications to the standard recurrent BEV pipeline (image encoder → LSS view transform → temporal fusion → BEV encoder → CenterHead). First, RNN-style back-propagation unrolls the temporal fusion module (TA-GRU) across all k training frames so that every historical BEV feature contributes gradients to the fusion parameters, matching the gradient structure of parallel fusion; only the TA-GRU is unrolled (not the full network) to keep memory tractable (30.9 GB for 16 frames on A100-40G). Second, the TA-GRU augments a standard ConvGRU with an inner grid transformation: the ego-motion matrix P_{j,i} is mapped through an MLP+sigmoid to a per-channel motion embedding E_{j,i}, which Hadamard-modulates a learnable linear weight W_p applied to the grid-sampled previous BEV feature before the GRU gates, thereby learning fine-grained orientation corrections that the coarse grid sample alone cannot provide. The combination yields +1.7% mAP and −4.7% mAOE over a plain ConvGRU baseline while retaining the single-frame cache and 39 ms inference latency of the recurrent paradigm.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, RecurrentBEV with ConvNeXt-B backbone achieves a new state-of-the-art 57.4% mAP and 65.1% NDS, surpassing the previous best (Sparse4Dv2) by 1.7% mAP and 1.3% NDS. The real-time version (ResNet50, 256×704, 25.6 FPS) achieves 44.5% mAP and 54.9% NDS on the validation set without external data, outperforming StreamPETR by 1.3% mAP and 0.9% NDS. Inference latency is 39 ms with 549 MB memory, compared to 96 ms and 945 MB for the parallel-fusion baseline SOLOFusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RecurrentBEV outperforms all 23 compared baselines on the nuScenes test set. Against the strongest query-based methods, it beats Sparse4Dv2 (0.557 mAP / 0.638 NDS) by 1.6% mAP and 1.3% NDS with V2-99, and beats StreamPETR (0.550 / 0.636) by 2.3% mAP and 1.5% NDS. Against BEV-based methods, it surpasses SOLOFusion (parallel fusion, 0.540 / 0.619) by 3.4% mAP and 3.2% NDS with ConvNeXt-B, and VideoBEV (previous recurrent fusion, 0.554 / 0.629) by 2.0% mAP and 2.2% NDS. On the validation set with the real-time ResNet50 configuration, RecurrentBEV (44.5% mAP) leads StreamPETR (43.2%) by 1.3% mAP and SparseBEV (43.2%) by 1.3% mAP, while also achieving the best mATE (0.555) among all methods, indicating superior localization of occluded objects. The method also achieves the lowest mATE (0.410–0.411) across all test-set entries, confirming that the long-term recurrent fusion specifically improves detection and localization of occluded and distant objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recurrent temporal fusion paradigm introduced by BEVFormer and applied to LSS-based detectors by VideoBEV, building on the BEVDet4D-Depth architecture as its base pipeline. It departs from prior recurrent methods by diagnosing two specific failure modes—gradient truncation (only one historical frame contributes to fusion-module gradients) and semantic ambiguity from coarse BEV grid alignment—and addressing them with RNN-style back-propagation and a Transformation-aware Gated Recurrent Unit (TA-GRU). The work positions itself explicitly as bridging the long-standing effectiveness-efficiency tradeoff between parallel fusion (SOLOFusion, BEVFormerv2) and recurrent fusion (VideoBEV, BEVFormer), claiming to be the first recurrent method with explicit BEV features to surpass parallel fusion performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why recurrent fusion has historically underperformed parallel fusion, which is valuable for the broader temporal-fusion literature beyond BEV detection. The practical deployment advantage (2.5× lower latency, 42% less memory than parallel fusion at comparable accuracy) makes it relevant for real-time autonomous driving systems. Open questions include whether the RNN-style back-propagation and inner grid transformation generalize to query-based detectors (Sparse4D, StreamPETR) and other 3D perception tasks like tracking and trajectory prediction, as the authors themselves note. The analysis is limited to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a single view-transformation paradigm (LSS-based), leaving generalizability to other BEV construction methods untested.
