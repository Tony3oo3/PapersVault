## Problem

Event-based motion estimation has been limited to short time intervals (≤0.1 s) or low-DOF problems, while the research frontier is moving toward dense, non-linear motion over longer durations. Existing approaches either rely on synthetic data with a significant sim-to-real gap or use contrast losses that are prone to event collapse and cannot handle the high-dimensional assignment between millions of events and per-pixel trajectories. This paper addresses long-time, dense, continuous-time motion estimation from events in a self-supervised manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240056/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a time interval of raw events [t_s, t_e] from an event camera, voxelized into a 3D volume for network input, and predicts per-pixel coefficients of a parametric trajectory (e.g., Bézier control points with N_c=10). The output is a dense continuous-time trajectory field q_n(t) for every pixel n, from which optical flow or displacement at any query time can be extracted. The contrast loss operates on a coarser displacement volume of shape [N_bins, h/4, w/4] where N_bins temporal bins store KNN-interpolated warp displacements, and all raw events (not just the voxelized input) are used for the loss computation.

## Method

The core contribution extends the Contrast Maximization framework by replacing linear or piecewise-linear warp models with explicit non-linear trajectory priors (polynomial, Bézier, or learned basis functions), enabling dense motion estimation over long intervals. To solve the intractable KNN assignment between ~10⁷ events and ~10⁵ trajectories, the method interpolates a coarse spatio-temporal displacement lookup table using KeOps for differentiable, memory-efficient KNN on GPU, then warps each event by looking up its displacement in this volume. A random reference time t_ref is sampled uniformly per training batch, simplifying the loss to a single warping operation while providing temporal regularization. The total loss is L = 1/G + λR, where G is the L1 norm of the IWE gradient magnitude and R penalizes spatial gradients of the displacement field between consecutive timesteps. Training is two-stage: supervised pre-training on synthetic MultiFlow data, followed by self-supervised fine-tuning on real data (EVIMO2 or DSEC) using only the contrast loss, reducing the sim-to-real gap by ~29%.

## Results

On the EVIMO2 dataset for dense continuous-time motion estimation, the self-supervised fine-tuning improves the zero-shot BFlow baseline by 29% in TEPE (8.63 → 6.14 px) and 15% in TAE (19.94° → 16.98°), while reducing outliers from 36% to 25%. On the DSEC optical flow benchmark, the method achieves EPE 3.20, AE 8.53°, and 15.21% outliers, representing the best self-supervised performance: a 19% improvement in AE and 14% improvement in inlier percentage over the previous best self-supervised method (Paredes et al.), while running 5× faster than competing baselines (7.27 ms inference vs. 40–46 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DSEC, the method outperforms all other self-supervised and model-based baselines on AE and %Out: it beats Paredes et al. [44] (AE 8.53° vs. 10.56°, %Out 15.21% vs. 17.77%), EV-FlowNet [77] (EPE 3.20 vs. 3.86, %Out 15.21% vs. 31.45%), and MultiCM [54] (AE 8.53° vs. 13.98°, %Out 15.21% vs. 30.86%). However, it loses to Paredes et al. on EPE (3.20 vs. 2.33), and both supervised methods (E-RAFT [24], IDNet [69]) remain substantially ahead on all metrics. On EVIMO2, the self-supervised method (TEPE 6.14, TAE 16.98°) clearly beats the zero-shot BFlow [25] (TEPE 8.63, TAE 19.94°) and both Paredes et al. [44] (TEPE 21.69) and E-RAFT [24] (TEPE 19.38), but falls short of the in-domain supervised BFlow (TEPE 3.38, TAE 11.68°). The method also achieves 5× faster inference (7.27 ms) compared to Paredes et al. (40.10 ms) and E-RAFT (46.33 ms), and is orders of magnitude faster than MultiCM (~9900 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Contrast Maximization (CM) self-supervised framework of Gallego et al. (2018, 2019) from short-time linear optical flow (≤0.1 s) to long-time (0.3 s) dense non-linear trajectory estimation by introducing parametric trajectory priors (Bézier, polynomial, learned basis) as the motion model. It pairs this loss with the BFlow architecture (Gehrig et al., 2024) for supervised pre-training on synthetic data, then fine-tunes on real events. The key technical departure is solving the high-dimensional event-to-trajectory assignment problem via a coarse spatio-temporal displacement volume with differentiable KNN interpolation (KeOps), which prior CM methods avoided by restricting to linear or partitioned-linear warping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 29% zero-shot improvement on EVIMO2 and SOTA self-supervised performance on DSEC (with 5× faster inference) demonstrate a practical recipe for closing the sim-to-real gap in event-based motion estimation without requiring dense pixel-level annotations on real data. Limitations remain: the brightness-constancy assumption fails under flickering illumination, the KNN interpolation is subject to the aperture problem, and training memory/time is higher than supervised baselines due to raw-event warping. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer prediction horizons (>1 s), whether the displacement-volume granularity can be made adaptive, and whether the non-linear prior's advantage on EVIMO2 generalizes to more diverse real-world scenes.
