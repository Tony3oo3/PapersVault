## Problem

Existing image forgery localization methods use static, shared network structures that map all forged images into a common feature space, ignoring the distinct forensic traces left by different manipulation techniques. Additionally, methods that rely on hard edge-supervision loss to capture boundary artifacts suffer from attention bias, becoming overly sensitive to image edges or failing to capture subtle traces. The paper addresses both the lack of adaptability to diverse forgery patterns and the inflexibility of boundary-focused supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a suspicious RGB image cropped to 1024×1024 as input and produces a pixel-level spatial localization map indicating forged regions. The dynamic encoder processes the image through four stages of transformer blocks, and the lightweight decoder fuses multi-stage features to generate the final localization map. Training uses equal sampling across three source datasets (CASIA v2, IMD2020, and a CAT-Net dataset) with standard augmentations including flipping, scaling, blurring, and JPEG compression.

## Method

AdaIFL introduces a Mixture-of-Experts dynamic routing mechanism into both the transformer encoder and decoder: attention layers are replaced by parallel attention experts with a sample-level router, and MLP layers by parallel MLP experts with a token-level router, allowing different forged images to selectively activate different network subspaces. The key attention component, Feature Importance-aware Attention (FIA), uses an Adaptive Token Aggregator (ATA) that first scores each token's importance via a lightweight MLP, partitions the image into irregular sub-regions by sorted importance, allocates variable aggregation scales (smaller scale → more tokens for discriminative regions like manipulation boundaries), and merges tokens within each region using DPC-KNN density-peak clustering weighted by importance scores. The dynamic decoder fuses four-stage features via multi-scale depthwise separable convolutions and group convolution, then predicts the localization map through parallel 1×1 convolution heads selected by a sample-level router. Training combines binary cross-entropy, Dice loss, and a metric learning loss that increases feature distribution discrepancy between authentic and forged regions.

## Results

AdaIFL achieves state-of-the-art performance across five benchmark datasets, with mean IOU of 47.2%, F1 of 74.9%, and AUC of 90.0%, surpassing the second-best method by 6.6%, 3.4%, and 1.9% respectively. On the challenging NIST16 dataset, AdaIFL reaches 39.3% IOU and 70.6% F1, outperforming the runner-up (IML-ViT) by 8.7% and 6.3%. On the copy-move forgery dataset Coverage, AdaIFL achieves 46.4% IOU and 74.5% F1, exceeding the second-best (TruFor) by 16.6% and 8.8% in IOU and F1. The model also demonstrates superior robustness under JPEG compression, Gaussian blur, and gamma correction on NIST16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaIFL outperforms all eight baselines on mean IOU, F1, and AUC. Against the strongest competitor TruFor [11], AdaIFL gains 16.6% IOU on Coverage (46.4% vs. 29.8%) and 8.7% IOU on NIST16 (39.3% vs. 28.8%), though it trails TruFor on DSO-1 (23.7% vs. 27.0% IOU), where TruFor ranks first. Against IML-ViT [21], the closest competitor on NIST16, AdaIFL leads by 8.7% IOU and 6.3% F1. Against PSCC-Net [20], the best on MISD, AdaIFL edges ahead by 0.8% in both IOU and F1 (60.6% vs. 59.8%). Against CAT-Net v2 [19], AdaIFL improves CASIA v1 IOU from 63.6% to 66.1% and Coverage IOU from 23.1% to 46.4%. The remaining baselines (ManTraNet, IF-OSN, MVSS-Net, HiFi-IFDL) are substantially outperformed across all datasets, with mean IOU gaps exceeding 15%.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaIFL extends the vision-transformer-based IFL line (TruFor, IML-ViT, ObjectFormer) by introducing Mixture-of-Experts dynamic routing into both the encoder and decoder, a concept well-established in NLP and general vision but previously unexplored in forgery localization. It departs from edge-supervision methods (MVSS-Net, IML-ViT) that hard-constrain attention to boundaries, replacing them with a soft, importance-driven adaptive token aggregator that draws on DPC-KNN clustering and sparse-attention token-merging ideas from the broader ViT efficiency literature. The multi-scale feature fusion decoder builds on standard FPN-style fusion but adds a dynamic prediction-head router, making the entire pipeline input-adaptive rather than static.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to apply dynamic routing to IFL, AdaIFL offers a principled way to handle the heterogeneity of forgery techniques without task-specific hand-crafted branches, which could influence how future IFL architectures are designed. The paper honestly notes that heavy Gaussian noise (k=23) degrades routing accuracy, leaving open the question of how to make expert selection robust under real-world degradation chains. Broader implications include whether the MoE-in-IFL paradigm transfers to related tasks such as video forgery detection or multi-modal forgery, and whether the importance-aware token aggregation can be generalized to other dense-prediction problems beyond pixel-level localization.
