## Problem

Existing controllable diffusion methods for e-commerce image generation cannot simultaneously preserve the exact identity of a user-specified object (e.g., a shoe) and produce plausible human-object interactions. Painting-based approaches (Paint-by-Example, ControlNet-inpaint) fail to handle occluded shoe regions and generate spurious content, while identity-preserving methods (IP-Adapter, DreamBooth) lose pixel-level shoe details through feature compression or insufficient training data. No prior work addresses the combined challenge of shoe-ID retention and realistic leg-shoe interaction without explicit pose inputs.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single user-specified shoe image (segmented via SAM to isolate the shoe region) and a free-form text prompt (encoded by CLIP into embeddings) as input, and produces a 512×512 hyper-realistic advertising image of human legs wearing that exact shoe. At inference, the detected visible-area mask of the shoe is pasted back onto the generated image to guarantee pixel-level ID consistency. The intermediate leg-pose synthesis operates at 256×256 resolution, and the wearable-area detection at 640×640.

## Method

ShoeModel is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a SegFormer-based wearable-area detection module performs 3-class segmentation (background / visible area / wearable area) to produce a processed shoe image retaining only the visible region; (2) a two-phase leg-pose synthesis module first predicts 8 deterministic foot keypoints via a modified ViTPose (removing the human bounding-box input), then conditions a small MLP-based diffusion model on those foot keypoints to generate 6 leg keypoints in normalized [x,y] coordinate space rather than heatmaps, avoiding the difficulty of mapping dense images to sparse poses; (3) a ShoeControlNet (ControlNet variant) conditioned on the concatenated visible-shoe image and synthesized pose image, together with CLIP text embeddings, drives a frozen Stable Diffusion UNet to generate the final shoe-wearing image. The key architectural insight is the two-phase LpS decomposition, which sidesteps the convergence problems of directly regressing sparse keypoints from dense shoe images, and the WD module's explicit separation of occluded regions, which prevents the generator from producing implausible interactions at the shoe-leg boundary.

## Results

On the 2k test set, ShoeModel achieves the best FID (22.54) and KID (6.67) among all compared methods, outperforming the next-best ControlNet-inpaint (FID 29.83, KID 11.28) by a substantial margin. On CLIP score (text-image consistency), ShoeModel scores 24.02, slightly below SDXL's 24.38 but above all other baselines. In the user study with 20 volunteers, ShoeModel wins 1859.5 times on average versus 54 (SD1.5-inpaint), 56 (ControlNet-inpaint), and 30.5 (Paint-by-Example), demonstrating a decisive perceptual advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShoeModel outperforms all painting-based baselines (SD1.5-inpaint, ControlNet-inpaint, Paint-by-Example) on both FID and KID by 7–9 points, and wins the user study by a factor of 30–60×, because these methods cannot handle the wearable-area occlusion and thus produce implausible human-shoe interactions or alter the shoe identity. Against the text-to-image baselines (SD1.5, SDXL), ShoeModel achieves substantially better FID (22.54 vs. 31.62/30.24) and KID (6.67 vs. 12.06/7.49), though SDXL edges out on CLIP score (24.38 vs. 24.02) due to its stronger text encoders; however, neither T2I method supports preserving the specified shoe identity at all. Qualitative comparisons against IP-Adapter show that its CLIP-based image encoder compresses pixel-level shoe details (color, logo, texture) into high-level features, causing inevitable ID drift, whereas ShoeModel pastes the visible shoe region back to guarantee exact identity. DreamBooth, even when augmented to 10 training images, fails to maintain both shoe identity and plausible leg interaction simultaneously, confirming that small-scale fine-tuning cannot reshape the diffusion manifold sufficiently for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stable Diffusion / ControlNet lineage of controllable image generation but departs from it in two key ways: (1) it introduces a dedicated wearable-area detection stage (SegFormer-based) to separate visible from occluded shoe regions, a preprocessing step absent from prior controllable-generation work; and (2) it replaces the standard dense-image conditioning for pose with a two-phase sparse-keypoint diffusion model (MLP-based) that first predicts deterministic foot keypoints via ViTPose and then synthesizes diverse leg poses. Identity-preserving methods like DreamBooth and IP-Adapter are explicitly positioned as insufficient for fine-grained product details, and painting-based methods (Paint-by-Example, ControlNet-inpaint) are shown to fail at producing plausible human-object interactions, motivating the pipeline architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purpose-built multi-stage pipeline can solve a practically important e-commerce problem—generating hyper-realistic shoe-wearing advertising images while preserving exact product identity—that general-purpose diffusion models and existing identity-preserving methods cannot handle. The constructed shoe-wearing dataset (669k images) and the formalization of the wearable-area detection task provide reusable resources for the product-visualization community. However, the system is tightly scoped to shoes and legs; open questions include whether the two-phase pose-synthesis strategy generalizes to other wearable categories (hats, glasses, bags), whether the identity-preservation via paste-back at inference scales to more complex occlusion patterns, and whether the approach can be extended to full-body or multi-person scenes.
