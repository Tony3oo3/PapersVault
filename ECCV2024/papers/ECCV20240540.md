## Problem

Existing stochastic human motion prediction methods rely on generative models (VAEs, GANs, diffusion) with unconstrained Gaussian latent priors, which suffer from mode collapse and fail to learn semantically meaningful motion representations. This results in predictions that are inaccurate, unrealistic, and incoherent with past motion, even when diverse sampling strategies are applied post-hoc. The paper identifies the lack of proper structural guidance on the latent distribution as the root cause of this limitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a past 3D pose sequence (25 frames of 17-joint poses on Human3.6M or 15 frames of 15-joint poses on HumanEva-I), pads it to the full prediction horizon by repeating the last frame, and projects it to the frequency domain via DCT before encoding. It produces K=50 diverse future pose sequences (100 frames on Human3.6M, 60 frames on HumanEva-I) in the frequency domain, which are back-projected to the time domain via IDCT. At inference, individual latent direction coefficients can be edited to achieve semantically controllable predictions (e.g., adjusting sitting depth or arm swing amplitude).

## Method

The core contribution is Semantic Latent Directions (SLD): a set of M learnable, mutually orthogonal latent vectors (enforced via SVD) that span the latent motion space, where the future motion latent z is expressed as a linear combination z = Σ w_m · d_m with coefficients w predicted from past motion. A Query-to-Latent Projection (QLP) module maps K learnable motion queries into the SLD coefficient space, ensuring diverse yet accurate sampling by constraining each query's output to the orthogonal basis rather than an unconstrained Gaussian. The overall architecture is a lightweight STGCN encoder-decoder with DCT/IDCT preprocessing, trained end-to-end with a combined reconstruction, diversity-promoting, and motion-constraint loss. The orthogonality constraint acts as an information bottleneck that forces the model to learn discrete semantic prototypes (e.g., stand, squat, sit) rather than a diffuse Gaussian, enabling both accurate prediction and intuitive coefficient-based controllability at inference.

## Results

On HumanEva-I, SLD achieves ADE 0.193, FDE 0.209, MMADE 0.305, and MMFDE 0.293, outperforming the previous best (HumanMAC: ADE 0.209, FDE 0.223) on all accuracy metrics while maintaining competitive diversity (APD 4.066). On Human3.6M, SLD reaches ADE 0.348, FDE 0.436, MMADE 0.435, and MMFDE 0.463, beating STARS (ADE 0.358, FDE 0.445) and Belfusion (ADE 0.372, FDE 0.474) across all accuracy measures. The method achieves the best accuracy on both datasets while trading some raw diversity (APD 8.741 vs. STARS's 15.884 on Human3.6M) for more realistic and coherent predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLD achieves the best accuracy (lowest ADE, FDE, MMADE, MMFDE) on both HumanEva-I and Human3.6M, outperforming the closest competitors: on HumanEva-I it beats HumanMAC (ADE 0.209→0.193, FDE 0.223→0.209) and STARS (ADE 0.217→0.193, FDE 0.241→0.209); on Human3.6M it beats STARS (ADE 0.358→0.348, FDE 0.445→0.436) and Belfusion (ADE 0.372→0.348, FDE 0.474→0.436). In terms of diversity (APD), SLD trails STARS (4.066 vs. 6.031 on HumanEva-I; 8.741 vs. 15.884 on Human3.6M) and DivSamp (6.109 on HumanEva-I), but the authors argue that STARS's higher APD comes at the cost of unrealistic and incoherent motions. Against deterministic baselines (ERD, acLSTM), SLD improves ADE by roughly 0.19–0.43 m on HumanEva-I and 0.37–0.44 m on Human3.6M. The gap over generative-model-focused methods like MotionDiff (ADE 0.411 on H3.6M) and DivSamp (ADE 0.370) highlights the benefit of the orthogonal latent direction constraint over unconstrained Gaussian latent spaces. Belfusion, which uses an explicit behavior representation, still lags SLD by 0.024 ADE and 0.038 FDE on Human3.6M, suggesting that learned semantic directions provide a more flexible information bottleneck than hand-crafted behavior disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in stochastic human motion prediction (SHMP) of constraining the latent space with a Gaussian prior (as in VAE-based methods like DLow, DivSamp, MOJO) or imposing explicit behavior representations (as in Belfusion). Instead, it draws on disentangled representation learning (β-VAE, StyleGAN) to construct a set of orthogonal, learnable latent directions (SLD) that serve as an information bottleneck, with future motion represented as a linear combination of these directions. The diverse motion queries are projected into the SLD coefficient space rather than concatenated with past-motion embeddings (as in STARS), and the overall encoder-decoder backbone follows the STGCN + DCT pipeline established by GSPS and STARS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SLD framework offers a lightweight, plug-and-play mechanism for improving prediction accuracy while enabling semantic-level controllability (e.g., adjusting sitting vs. squatting, arm swing amplitude) simply by editing latent coefficients at inference time. The paper acknowledges a key limitation: all experiments are in controlled, non-interactive settings, and extending SLD to scenarios where humans interact with their environment remains an open question. The approach also raises the broader question of whether orthogonal latent directions generalize beyond human motion to other structured sequential prediction tasks, and whether the learned semantic axes remain stable under distribution shift.
