## Problem

Zero-shot image captioning (ZIC) must learn image-to-text mappings without annotated image-text pairs. Existing VLMs-based methods rely on CLIP's global features to bridge the modality gap, but the inherent text-image feature discrepancy in CLIP space causes a training-inference mismatch. While recent work (SynTIC) trains on diffusion-generated synthetic images, it only exploits global features and ignores the flawed or unfaithful details in salient regions of synthetic images, which further widens both the synthetic-real distribution gap and the image-text semantic gap at a fine-grained level.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single RGB image and produces a natural language caption via beam search (beam size 3). During training, it consumes synthetic image-text pairs from the SynthImgCap dataset (542K pairs for MSCOCO, 145K for Flickr30k), where images are generated by Stable Diffusion from the training corpus captions. CLIP-ViT-L/14 extracts a 768-dim global feature vector and a 1024-dim grid feature map per image, which are projected into a 512-dim joint embedding space before feeding the encoder-decoder.

## Method

PCM-Net introduces a Patch-wise Cross-modal Feature Mix-up (PCM) mechanism that automatically mitigates defective content in synthetic images during training. For each synthetic image, salient visual concepts are detected via zero-shot entity classification in CLIP space (top-K from a high-frequency noun vocabulary), and the image's patch features are mapped into text space using DenseCLIP's [CLS] classification head. A patch-wise affinity matrix is computed between mapped patches and salient concept features, and the top-M most relevant visual patches are replaced with softly aggregated text-based patches, yielding a mixed feature map with reduced unfaithful detail. A CLIP-weighted cross-entropy (CXE) loss further re-weights each training sample by its global CLIPScore, penalizing low-quality synthetic pairs. The mixed feature map is processed by a 3-block Transformer encoder (with inter-layer global feature interaction) and a 3-block Transformer decoder (512-dim hidden state) for autoregressive caption generation.

## Results

On the in-domain zero-shot setting with ViT-L/14 backbone, PCM-Net achieves CIDEr 113.6, BLEU-4 33.6, and SPICE 20.8 on MSCOCO, and CIDEr 69.5, BLEU-4 28.5, and SPICE 18.2 on Flickr30k, setting new state-of-the-art across all five standard metrics. This represents a 5.5-point absolute CIDEr improvement over the strongest baseline CgT-GAN (108.1) on MSCOCO, which notably uses real images during training. Under cross-domain evaluation (MSCOCO→Flickr30k), PCM-Net further extends its lead with CIDEr 55.9, an 8.4-point gain over CgT-GAN (47.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCM-Net outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric for both MSCOCO and Flickr30k under in-domain and cross-domain settings. Against the closest synthetic-image baseline SynTIC [24] (ViT-B/32), PCM-Net gains +12.5 CIDEr on MSCOCO (113.6 vs 101.1) and +12.9 on Flickr30k (69.5 vs 56.6), demonstrating the benefit of patch-level mix-up over global-feature contrastive learning. Versus the strongest overall baseline CgT-GAN [51] (ViT-L/14, which uses real images in training), PCM-Net still leads by +5.5 CIDEr on MSCOCO and +4.6 on Flickr30k, confirming that synthetic-image training with PCM can match or exceed methods that rely on real data. Among the CLIP-latent decoding methods (CapDec, DeCap, ViECap), PCM-Net improves CIDEr by 20–22 points on MSCOCO, highlighting the advantage of pixel-space training over feature-space substitution. On the cross-domain NoCaps benchmark (Table 4), integrating PCM and CXE into ViECap yields +8.5 CIDEr overall (74.7 vs 66.2), with the largest gain in the out-of-domain category (+10.7), further validating generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-image training paradigm introduced by SynTIC [24], which first proposed using text-to-image diffusion outputs for zero-shot captioning but relied solely on global CLIP features. PCM-Net departs from SynTIC by operating at the patch level, selectively replacing unfaithful visual patches with semantically aligned textual features, and from prior VLMs-based methods (CapDec, DeCap, ViECap) that train on textual features and swap in visual features at inference, by training directly on pixel-space synthetic images. The CLIP-weighted cross-entropy loss builds on the CLIPScore concept [11] as a sample-level reweighting mechanism, and the framework compatibility experiments integrate PCM and CXE into ViECap's entity-based decoding pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play PCM mechanism and CXE loss offer a practical recipe for any encoder-decoder captioning model to benefit from diffusion-generated training data without requiring prompt engineering or architectural redesign, and the released SynthImgCap dataset (542K pairs) provides a reusable resource for the community. However, the approach still depends on CLIP's cross-modal alignment quality and the fidelity of the underlying diffusion model; as diffusion models improve, the need for patch-level correction may diminish. Open questions include whether the fine-grained mix-up strategy transfers to other vision-language tasks (e.g., VQA, retrieval) and whether the CLIP-weighted loss generalizes beyond captioning to other sequence-generation objectives trained on noisy synthetic data.
