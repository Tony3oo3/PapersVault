## Problem

Transformer-based large vision models like SAM and Stable Diffusion incur prohibitive inference costs because Multi-Head Attention scales quadratically with token count (quartic with image resolution). Prior efficiency methods either require expensive re-training from scratch (efficient architectures) or cause severe performance degradation (token merging, e.g., Expedit-SAM drops SAM-H AP from 46.1 to 42.3), leaving no practical way to speed up well-trained large vision models without sacrificing their capabilities.

## Input / Output

GridAttn is a drop-in replacement for any MHA layer, taking a feature map of shape [N, C, H, W] and producing an output of identical shape, so it can be inserted into frozen or trainable transformers without architectural changes. In practice, it operates on 1024×1024 feature maps in SAM (prompted with ViTDet boxes for zero-shot instance segmentation), 768×768 latent representations in Stable Diffusion V2.1 (text-to-image), and 2048×512 feature maps in SegFormer (semantic segmentation on ADE20K). The only user-facing hyperparameter is the grid stride S (typically 2), which controls the trade-off between efficiency gain and information loss.

## Method

GridAttn exploits spatial redundancy in natural images by partitioning the feature map into S×S grids and distributing intra-grid tokens across S² separate sub-images via reshaping and permuting, so that MHA only computes inter-grid (long-range) attention while skipping redundant intra-grid (neighboring) attention, reducing self-attention complexity by a factor of S². Three Grid Distributing strategies are provided: Straightforward (pure reshape/permute, works well on frozen models but suffers a 'gridding issue' when trained from scratch), Shuffle (randomly permutes intra-grid tokens during training to restore neighborhood information while using the Straightforward layout at inference), and Deformable (learns per-pixel sampling offsets via two auxiliary 3×3 convolutions initialized to zero, making it equivalent to Straightforward at initialization but adaptively optimized during training). A Grid Recovering module then inverts the clustering to restore the full-resolution output. Because all non-MHA operations are parameter-free reshapes and permutes, the module can be plugged into any well-trained transformer with zero fine-tuning, or trained/fine-tuned from scratch with standard implementations.

## Results

Without any fine-tuning, GridAttn applied to SAM reduces GFLOPs by 4.6%–16.1% and GPU inference latency by 8.2%–21.4% while maintaining or slightly exceeding the original AP (e.g., SAM-B: 41.39 vs. 41.02 AP; SAM-H: 46.12 vs. 46.13 AP on COCO). When trained from scratch on SegFormer with the Deformable Strategy, GridAttn achieves 52.0 mIoU (MS) on ADE20K for B5 (vs. 51.4 benchmark) at only 305.4 GFLOPs (vs. 423.7 benchmark), a 28% reduction. With just 10% fine-tuning iterations, GridAttn on SegFormer-B1 reaches 44.1 mIoU (MS), surpassing the 43.1 benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original SAM (frozen, no modification), GridAttn matches or marginally exceeds AP across all three model sizes (SAM-B: +0.37 AP, SAM-L: +0.05 AP, SAM-H: −0.01 AP) while cutting GFLOPs by 4.6–16.1% and GPU latency by 8.2–21.4%, making it a strict Pareto improvement. Against Expedit-SAM (the prior state-of-the-art training-free method), GridAttn is decisively superior in accuracy: on SAM-H, GridAttn achieves 46.12 AP versus Expedit-SAM's 42.33 AP (a 3.79-point gap), and on SAM-B, 41.39 vs. 39.73 (1.66 points), while also reducing GFLOPs further (e.g., 5692 vs. 5094 on SAM-H, though Expedit-SAM is slightly lower in GFLOPs, its accuracy drop is severe). Against the original SegFormer trained from scratch, GridAttn with the Deformable Strategy achieves higher mIoU (52.0 vs. 51.4 MS on B5; 40.0 vs. 39.1 MS on B0) at substantially lower GFLOPs (305.4 vs. 423.7 on B5; 40.3 vs. 46.5 on B0), though at higher latency due to index-select operations. The Straightforward Strategy, while inferior in mIoU when trained from scratch, still delivers a better performance-efficiency tradeoff than the benchmark in the GFLOPs dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

GridAttn positions itself against three prior paradigms for efficient vision transformers: (1) new architectures (A-ViT, MobileViT) that require costly re-training, (2) task-specific designs (FastInst, SegFormer) that don't generalize, and (3) training-free token merging (ToMe, Expedit-SAM) that irreversibly discards tokens and causes significant performance drops. Conceptually, it builds on the observation of heavy spatial redundancy in natural images (He et al., Chen et al.) and repurposes the deformable convolution offset-estimation idea (Dai et al.) for its Deformable Strategy. The key departure from token merging is that GridAttn never discards tokens—it redistributes them spatially via parameter-free reshaping and permuting, making it a drop-in MHA replacement that preserves the full representational capacity of the original model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: models like SAM (17,400 A100 GPU hours) and SD (200,000 A100 GPU hours) become 8–21% faster at inference with zero additional training, which is a meaningful deployment win for industry. The method's orthogonality to pruning and quantization suggests compounding efficiency gains are possible, though the paper does not explore this. Open questions include how GridAttn interacts with dynamic-resolution or video settings, whether the S=2 sweet spot holds for architectures with very different token counts, and whether the Deformable Strategy's latency overhead (4× slower than benchmark on SegFormer-B5) can be mitigated with hardware-aware implementations. The modest absolute gains (4.6–16.1% GFLOPs) limit its impact as a standalone solution but make it a valuable component in a broader efficiency toolkit.
