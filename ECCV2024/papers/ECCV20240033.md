## Problem

Existing panoptic lifting methods (Panoptic Lifting, Contrastive Lift) rely on deterministic feature embeddings to represent 3D instances, making them fragile to two interrelated issues: inconsistent instance IDs across views and inconsistent segmentation boundaries (e.g., the same object split into parts in one view but whole in another). This noise in training data causes significant performance degradation on complex scenes with many objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240033/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view posed RGB images paired with 2D panoptic segmentation predictions (semantic masks and instance masks) from off-the-shelf 2D models such as Mask2Former or Detic. It learns four 3D neural fields (semantic, instance, density, color) via TensorRF; the instance field uniquely outputs a multivariate Gaussian distribution (mean vector and diagonal covariance) per 3D point rather than a fixed feature vector. At inference, a single run of the MVOA clustering algorithm yields a prototype feature set that is then used to assign instance labels to every pixel in any test view, producing view-consistent 3D panoptic masks.

## Method

PCF-Lift replaces deterministic instance features with probabilistic embeddings modeled as N-dimensional multivariate Gaussians, enabling per-dimension uncertainty that absorbs segmentation noise. The contrastive loss is reformulated using the Probability Product (PP) kernel to measure similarity between Gaussian distributions, and a novel cross-view constraint is introduced in later training epochs that dynamically selects positive feature pairs across two views via a PP-kernel threshold to enforce multi-view consistency. At inference, a Multi-View Object Association (MVOA) algorithm groups per-pixel features by 2D instance masks, builds a PP-kernel similarity graph, and applies a greedy NMS-like procedure to extract prototype features representing each 3D object instance. A theoretical corollary shows that the PP kernel reduces to the RBF kernel under isotropic fixed covariance, establishing the probabilistic formulation as a strict generalization of the prior deterministic approach.

## Results

On the ScanNet dataset, PCF-Lift achieves a scene-level Panoptic Quality (PQ^scene) of 63.5%, with SQ^scene of 78.5% and RQ^scene of 65.4%, outperforming the strongest baseline Contrastive Lift (62.0% PQ^scene) by 1.5 points. On the more challenging Messy Room dataset (up to 500 objects per scene), PCF-Lift achieves a mean PQ^scene of 73.4%, a 4.4-point improvement over Contrastive Lift (69.0%) and a 10.2-point improvement over Panoptic Lifting (63.2%). The gains are most pronounced in scenes with many objects, e.g., 60.3% vs. 55.0% PQ^scene at 500 objects in the Old Room environment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCF-Lift consistently outperforms all four baselines on both datasets. Against the closest competitor Contrastive Lift [3] (NeurIPS'23), it gains 1.5 points in PQ^scene on ScanNet (63.5% vs. 62.0%) and 4.4 points in mean PQ^scene on Messy Rooms (73.4% vs. 69.0%), with the gap widening as object count increases (e.g., 60.3% vs. 55.0% at 500 objects). Against Panoptic Lifting [40] (CVPR'23), the improvements are larger: 4.6 points on ScanNet and 10.2 points on Messy Rooms. Against the 3D panoptic segmentation baselines PNF [26] and DM-NeRF [44], PCF-Lift leads by 15.2 and 21.8 points in PQ^scene on ScanNet respectively, confirming that the 2D-lifting paradigm with probabilistic fusion substantially surpasses direct 3D approaches. Notably, PCF-Lift also achieves the highest SQ^scene (78.5%) and RQ^scene (65.4%) on ScanNet, indicating gains in both segmentation quality and instance recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the panoptic lifting paradigm established by Panoptic Lifting (Siddiqui et al., CVPR 2023) and Contrastive Lift (Bhalgat et al., NeurIPS 2023), which unproject 2D panoptic segmentations into 3D neural fields. The key conceptual departure is replacing the deterministic feature embeddings used in both prior methods with multivariate Gaussian distributions, enabling explicit uncertainty modeling of noisy 2D segmentations. The Probability Product kernel (Jebara et al., 2004) is repurposed as the similarity measure within a reformulated contrastive loss, and a novel cross-view constraint is introduced to enforce multi-view instance consistency. The theoretical analysis positions the probabilistic formulation as a strict generalization of the deterministic RBF-kernel approach, with the latter recovered as a degenerate isotropic-covariance case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in panoptic lifting: the sensitivity of deterministic feature learning to inconsistent 2D segmentations, which is especially acute in complex scenes with many objects. The consistent robustness gains across different 2D backbones and synthetic noise levels suggest the probabilistic framing is a broadly applicable design principle for any pipeline that fuses imperfect 2D predictions into 3D. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger scenes (beyond 500 objects), whether the cross-view constraint can be made more computationally efficient, and whether the probabilistic embedding idea transfers to other 3D representation backends beyond TensorRF/NeRF.
