## Problem

Existing defenses against adversarial patch attacks either generalize poorly across different attack types (adversarial training, certified defenses) or degrade image fidelity through aggressive pre-processing (smoothing, watermarking, feature clipping). Diffusion-based purification (DiffPure) handles ℓp perturbations well but fails on patch attacks due to an inherent trade-off: large noise ratios needed to remove patches also destroy global image semantics. The paper identifies this gap and asks whether a diffusion model can be repurposed to both detect and repair adversarial patches without sacrificing image quality.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image containing a localized adversarial patch (typically 5% of image area) along with two learnable text prompts (16-token vectors each) that guide the localization and restoration stages. It produces a binary patch localization mask (refined via Gaussian smoothing and dilation to slightly over-cover the patch boundary) and a fully restored RGB image where the patch region has been inpainted. The one-step denoising for localization is repeated 3 times and averaged to reduce stochastic variance before binarization.

## Method

DIFFender exploits the Adversarial Anomaly Perception (AAP) phenomenon: at a critical noise ratio, a diffusion model produces variable denoising outcomes specifically within adversarial patch regions while preserving the rest of the image, enabling patch localization via the difference between a text-guided and an empty-text one-step denoised image. A single pre-trained Stable Diffusion model handles both stages—localization (difference-based mask estimation) and restoration (inpainting with the estimated mask fed as extra UNet channels)—exploiting their tight interplay where accurate localization aids restoration and vice versa. For adaptation, a few-shot prompt-tuning algorithm (8-shot) optimizes the two learnable prompt vectors using three joint losses: cross-entropy on the mask, L1 pixel distance to the clean image, and a layer-weighted L2 feature distance to the downstream classifier, enabling the frozen diffusion backbone to specialize for defense without full retraining.

## Results

On ImageNet with Inception-v3, DIFFender achieves 88.3% robust accuracy under adaptive BPDA+AdvP attacks and 71.9% under BPDA+LaVAN, outperforming all baselines. On Swin-S, DIFFender reaches 94.5% under BPDA+AdvP and 85.9% under BPDA+LaVAN. Against non-adaptive attacks, DIFFender attains 75.0% (GDPA) and 53.5% (RHDE) on Inception-v3, and 82.4% (GDPA) and 70.3% (RHDE) on Swin-S, consistently ranking first. On LFW face recognition, DIFFender achieves 81.1% (GDPA) and 60.7% (RHDE) robust accuracy, again the best among all defenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIFFender outperforms all eight baselines across both Inception-v3 and Swin-S on every attack type. Against the strongest competing methods, SAC achieves 84.2% on AdvP (Inception-v3) vs. DIFFender's 88.3%, and Jedi reaches 67.6% on AdvP vs. 88.3%, with the gap widening dramatically on LaVAN (Jedi: 20.3% vs. DIFFender: 71.9%). LGS is competitive on GDPA (67.2% vs. 75.0%) but falls well behind on adaptive attacks (55.5% vs. 88.3% on AdvP). FNC shows reasonable Inception-v3 results (61.3% on AdvP) but collapses on Swin-S (6.3%), highlighting its CNN-specific design limitation that DIFFender avoids. DiffPure, the prior diffusion-based defense, is ineffective against patches (10.5% on AdvP), confirming the paper's motivation for the AAP-based approach. On LFW face recognition, DIFFender (81.1% GDPA, 60.7% RHDE) surpasses SAC (77.3%, 43.2%) and Jedi (74.2%, 43.9%), with the largest margin on RHDE where naturalistic stickers challenge localization-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based adversarial purification line (DiffPure) from ℓp-norm perturbations to the fundamentally different setting of localized patch attacks, where DiffPure's global denoising strategy provably fails due to a purification-vs-preservation trade-off. The key conceptual departure is the Adversarial Anomaly Perception (AAP) phenomenon: rather than purifying the entire image, the diffusion model's differential denoising behavior across patch regions is exploited for localization, followed by targeted inpainting-style restoration. The framework also repurposes the Stable Diffusion inpainting pipeline and adapts vision-language prompt tuning (à la Zhou et al.) to the defense setting, unifying localization and restoration under a single generative model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIFFender is the first framework to leverage a pre-trained diffusion model for the full patch-defense pipeline (localize + restore), and the AAP phenomenon provides a new mechanistic understanding of why diffusion models respond differently to adversarial patches versus natural content. The few-shot prompt-tuning requirement (8 images) makes the approach practical without extensive retraining, and the demonstrated generalization across classifiers (CNN and Transformer), attack types, face recognition, and physical-world conditions suggests broad applicability. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or other modalities, whether further acceleration (beyond one-step prediction) is possible without sacrificing localization quality, and whether the AAP phenomenon holds for more subtle or semantically coherent adversarial perturbations.
