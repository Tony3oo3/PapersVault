## Problem

Current diffusion-based inpainting pipelines regenerate the entire image (or a fixed-size crop) at every denoising step, discarding all non-masked pixels, which is computationally wasteful and precludes interactive editing. The core challenge is to generate only the masked region while still leveraging the full global image context for spatial and semantic coherence, without incurring the cost of processing the entire canvas through the iterative diffusion loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240621/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1024×1024 RGB image, a binary mask specifying the region to inpaint, and a text prompt. The image is encoded into a 128×128×4 latent space via a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE, and the mask is downsampled to match. The output is a partial latent update covering only the masked patches, which is composited with the visible latent and decoded back to RGB. The decoder processes 4×4 latent patches (64×64 grid), so runtime scales with the number of masked patches rather than the full 4096-token canvas.

## Method

LazyDiffusion uses an asymmetric encoder-decoder transformer architecture that decouples context encoding from iterative denoising. A ViT encoder processes the full latent image and mask once per edit, producing 4096 tokens; all tokens outside the mask are then dropped, creating an information bottleneck that forces the remaining N_hole tokens to encode global context relevant to the masked region. A PixArt-α-based diffusion decoder then iteratively denoises only the N_hole masked tokens, conditioned on the compressed context tokens (concatenated along the hidden dimension) and a T5-encoded text prompt. The encoder is trained from scratch while the decoder is initialized from the public PixArt-α checkpoint, and both are trained jointly end-to-end with the Improved DDPM objective for 100K iterations. This design yields a ~10× speedup for masks covering 10% of the image while maintaining FID and CLIPScore competitive with full-image regeneration baselines.

## Results

On 10,000 images from OpenImages, LazyDiffusion achieves a zero-shot FID of 7.70 and CLIPScore of 0.19, compared to RegenerateImage (FID 7.38, CLIP 0.19) and RegenerateCrop (FID 9.35, CLIP 0.19). The FID gap versus RegenerateImage is marginal (~4%), while LazyDiffusion substantially outperforms RegenerateCrop by ~26% in FID. In a user study (1,778 responses, 48 users), LazyDiffusion is preferred over RegenerateCrop in 81% of cases and is competitive with RegenerateImage (46.1%) and SDXL (48.5%). For masks covering 10% of the image, LazyDiffusion achieves a 10× speedup over full-image regeneration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RegenerateImage (full-canvas PixArt inpainting, same architecture), LazyDiffusion trades a small FID increase (7.70 vs. 7.38, ~4%) for a 10× runtime speedup at 10% mask coverage, while matching CLIPScore exactly (0.19). In the user study, LazyDiffusion is preferred over RegenerateImage in 46.1% of pairwise comparisons, indicating near-parity in perceived quality. Against RegenerateCrop (512×512 crop, same architecture), LazyDiffusion clearly wins: FID improves from 9.35 to 7.70 (~26% reduction), and users prefer LazyDiffusion in 81% of cases, reflecting the crop method's failure to maintain global semantic consistency. Against the reference models SDXL and SD2-crop (different architectures and training data, not directly comparable), LazyDiffusion is preferred in 48.5% and 82.5% of user-study comparisons respectively, suggesting it approaches state-of-the-art quality while being substantially faster than full-image methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LazyDiffusion builds on the PixArt-α diffusion transformer architecture and the latent-space framework of Stable Diffusion, but introduces an asymmetric encoder-decoder design inspired by (and inverting) Masked Autoencoders. The encoder is a ViT that processes the full canvas once to produce a compressed global context, while the decoder is a diffusion transformer that iteratively denoises only the masked-region tokens conditioned on that context. This departs from prior inpainting methods (GLIDE, Stable Diffusion Inpaint, Imagen Editor) that regenerate the entire image at every denoising step, and from crop-based approaches (SD2-crop, Diffusers) that sacrifice global context for speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup for typical interactive edits (10% mask) makes diffusion-based inpainting viable in user-in-the-loop workflows, where latency is the primary barrier to adoption. The key open question is scalability: the context encoder still processes the full image with quadratic attention, which becomes a bottleneck at very high resolutions or for video. The paper also acknowledges occasional color shifts at patch boundaries that are mitigated by Poisson blending but lack a principled solution. The approach could extend naturally to video synthesis, where per-frame partial updates would yield even larger cumulative savings.
