## Problem

Existing UV mapping algorithms assume well-behaved, manifold meshes with smooth geometry and few connected components, but geometry extracted from NeRFs or generated by text-to-3D models is non-manifold, non-smooth, and highly fragmented. Applying standard methods to such geometry produces texture atlases with excessive chart fragmentation that render them unusable for appearance editing or differentiable rendering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241024/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Nuvo accepts any geometry representation that supports sampling visible 3D points—NeRF volume density fields, meshes extracted via marching cubes, or text-to-3D generated meshes—without requiring manifoldness or specific connectivity. It outputs a UV texture atlas of n user-specified square charts (each in [0,1]²), along with per-chart assignment probabilities and inverse surface-coordinate mappings. The continuous MLP-defined UV coordinates can optionally be 'baked' onto mesh vertices as a piecewise-linear approximation for use in standard graphics pipelines.

## Method

Instead of computing a UV mapping on mesh vertices, Nuvo represents the mapping as a set of 2n+1 MLPs: one chart-assignment network mapping 3D points to a categorical distribution over n charts, n texture-coordinate networks mapping 3D points to 2D UV coordinates, and n surface-coordinate networks mapping 2D UV coordinates back to 3D points. The networks are trained from scratch per scene by minimizing a weighted sum of losses: two cycle-consistency terms (3→2→3 and 2→3→2) enforce approximate bijectivity, a cross-entropy loss enforces confident chart partitioning, a Chamfer-distance loss anchors the surface-coordinate networks to the input geometry, a clustering loss penalizes atlas fragmentation, a distortion loss (conformal + uniform stretch regularizers on the mapping differentials) ensures low and uniform warping, and a texture-optimization loss matching UV-space normals to true surface normals encourages injectivity. Because the fundamental operation is point sampling on the surface rather than solving systems over mesh connectivity, the method is agnostic to manifoldness, connectivity, and smoothness, and can operate directly on NeRF volume densities without triangulation.

## Results

On the challenging NeRF-extracted meshes (gardenvase and amigurumi), Nuvo with n=2 charts achieves Boundary scores of 0.974 and 0.983, and Editability scores of 0.859 and 0.813, substantially outperforming xatlas (0.457/0.249 Boundary, 0.457/0.248 Editability) and Blender Smart UV (0.778/0.603 Boundary, 0.764/0.581 Editability). On well-behaved meshes (bunny, lion), Nuvo is competitive with OptCuts, matching or slightly trailing on Editability (e.g., 0.946 vs 0.954 on bunny) while achieving comparable Boundary scores. For view synthesis, Nuvo with n=32 charts achieves PSNR of 23.72 on gardenvase and 28.92 on amigurumi, surpassing BakedSDF (22.55 and 28.63) while using fewer parameters (9.1M vs 37.8M and 59.4M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OptCuts, the strongest academic baseline for well-behaved meshes, Nuvo is competitive on bunny (Editability 0.946 vs 0.954) and lion (0.914 vs 0.939), trailing by 1–3 points, but OptCuts completely fails on the NeRF-extracted meshes (gardenvase, amigurumi) where Nuvo achieves Editability of 0.859 and 0.813. Against NeuTex, which uses a single-chart parameterization, Nuvo wins decisively on Editability for all datasets (e.g., 0.859 vs 0.516 on gardenvase, 0.813 vs 0.659 on amigurumi) while matching or exceeding its Boundary scores. Against xatlas, the industry-standard UV library, Nuvo dominates on the challenging NeRF meshes by a wide margin (Editability 0.859 vs 0.457 on gardenvase, 0.813 vs 0.248 on amigurumi) and is competitive on well-behaved meshes (0.937 vs 0.924 on bunny). Against Blender Smart UV, Nuvo again leads substantially on NeRF-extracted geometry (0.859 vs 0.764 on gardenvase, 0.813 vs 0.581 on amigurumi) while being comparable on simpler meshes. For view synthesis, Nuvo with n=32 charts outperforms BakedSDF by 1.17 dB on gardenvase (23.72 vs 22.55 PSNR) and 0.29 dB on amigurumi (28.92 vs 28.63), while using roughly 4× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nuvo builds directly on NeuTex, which introduced a cycle-consistency loss for jointly optimizing appearance and surface parameterization via neural networks, but extends it from single-chart to multi-chart atlases and adds losses for distortion control, chart clustering, and surface fidelity. Conceptually, it departs from the entire mesh-based UV mapping lineage (LSCM, geometry images, OptCuts, xatlas) by replacing vertex-level parameterization with point-sampled neural fields, making the method agnostic to mesh connectivity, manifoldness, and smoothness. This positions Nuvo at the intersection of neural 3D representation (NeRF, Zip-NeRF) and classical surface parameterization, addressing a gap where the two communities had not yet converged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As NeRF-based reconstruction and text-to-3D generation become the dominant pipeline for creating 3D content, the inability to produce usable, editable UV mappings is a critical bottleneck for downstream tasks like texture editing, differentiable rendering, and integration into standard graphics engines. Nuvo demonstrates that a neural-field formulation naturally handles the non-manifold, multi-component, non-smooth geometry that breaks all existing mesh-based tools, and that the resulting mappings can be baked onto meshes for use in conventional pipelines. Open questions include interactive user control over cut placement, guarantees of global bijectivity, and whether the 20–40 minute per-scene optimization can be accelerated or amortized across scenes.
