## Problem

Existing text-to-3D generation methods predominantly rely on implicit (NeRF) or implicit-explicit hybrid (DMTet) 3D representations, which lack explicit surface modeling and produce noisy, over-smoothed textures with ambiguous cross-view consistency. The resulting meshes require lossy implicit-to-explicit conversion (e.g., Marching Cubes) before integration into graphics pipelines, injecting additional surface noise and yielding complex, disorganized topologies. DreamMesh addresses this by framing text-to-3D generation entirely on explicit triangle meshes with well-defined surfaces.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241571/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a base triangle mesh (sourced from Shap-E outputs, a user-provided rough shape in Blender, or a basic sphere) as input. It produces a textured triangle mesh with clean, organized topology (vertices, edges, faces) and a UV-mapped texture atlas, both directly compatible with standard 3D engines like Blender without any implicit-to-explicit conversion step. The base mesh topology is preserved throughout; only vertex positions and texture values are optimized.

## Method

DreamMesh employs a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline operating entirely on explicit triangle meshes. In the coarse stage, the base mesh is deformed by optimizing per-triangle Jacobian matrices (inspired by Neural Jacobian Fields) guided by an SDS loss computed on rendered normal maps using Stable Diffusion; the resulting coarse mesh is then textured via a tuning-free process that interlaces depth-to-image and inpainting diffusion models across 10 viewpoints, projecting generated images onto a UV-mapped atlas. In the fine stage, both the mesh (via further Jacobian optimization) and the texture atlas are jointly refined by minimizing an MSE loss between the rendered image and an SDXL-based image-to-image refiner's output conditioned on the text prompt. This decoupling of geometry and texture in the coarse stage followed by their joint refinement in the fine stage avoids the over-modification and cross-view inconsistency artifacts that arise from optimizing both simultaneously from scratch.

## Results

On the T³ Bench benchmark, DreamMesh achieves average scores of 54.7 (Single Object), 48.7 (Single Object with Surroundings), and 39.2 (Multiple Objects), outperforming the best competitor ProlificDreamer by 5.3, 3.9, and 3.4 points respectively. In the Single Object category, DreamMesh reaches 55.6 Quality and 53.8 Alignment, surpassing ProlificDreamer (51.1/47.8). The method also leads in the Multiple Objects category (47.6 Quality, 30.8 Alignment) where most baselines degrade substantially, with SJC dropping to just 11.7 average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamMesh outperforms all 11 baselines across every category and metric on T³ Bench. Among implicit NeRF-based methods, DreamFusion (24.4 avg. Single Object) and SJC (24.7) are the weakest, with SJC suffering severe degradation on Multiple Objects (11.7 avg.) due to floating density artifacts; LatentNeRF (33.1) improves via latent-space optimization but still trails DreamMesh by ~22 points. Among DMTet-based hybrid methods, ProlificDreamer is the strongest competitor (49.4/44.8/35.8), yet DreamMesh still leads by 5.3/3.9/3.4 points on average, with the gap widening on Multiple Objects (39.2 vs. 35.8). Magic3D (37.0) and Fantasia3D (26.4) are further behind, with Fantasia3D particularly weak on complex multi-object scenes. In the ablation-style comparison (Table 2), simple combinations of TextDeformer with TEXTure (19.6 avg.) or Text2Tex (17.9 avg.) perform far worse than DreamMesh (46.3 avg.), confirming that naively chaining deformation and texturing is insufficient. Qualitative comparisons against Text2Mesh and CLIP-Mesh (Figure 6) show DreamMesh produces substantially cleaner topology and more faithful textures, as these older methods lack diffusion-model guidance entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamMesh departs from the dominant implicit (NeRF) and implicit-explicit hybrid (DMTet) representation paradigm in text-to-3D generation by operating entirely on explicit triangle meshes. It draws on Neural Jacobian Fields (Aigerman et al.) and TextDeformer (Gao et al.) for the per-triangle Jacobian deformation parameterization, on DreamFusion's Score Distillation Sampling for text-guided optimization, and on TEXTure's tuning-free multi-view diffusion texturing for the coarse texture stage. The key conceptual departure is the coarse-to-fine scheme that first decouples mesh deformation from texturing, then jointly refines both with an SDXL-based image-to-image refiner, rather than treating deformation and texturing as independent sub-problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing clean, well-organized triangle meshes with UV-mapped textures that are directly compatible with standard 3D engines (e.g., Blender), DreamMesh eliminates the lossy Marching Cubes/Tetrahedral conversion step that plagues NeRF- and DMTet-based pipelines, making the output immediately deployable in production graphics workflows. The acknowledged limitation of multi-face Janus artifacts due to the 2D diffusion prior's limited 3D awareness remains an open problem that the authors suggest could be addressed by fine-tuning diffusion models on 3D data. The practical impact is significant for creative industries seeking automated, engine-ready 3D asset generation, though the moderate performance margins over ProlificDreamer (3–5 points on T³ Bench) suggest the explicit-representation advantage, while real, is not yet transformative.
