## Problem

In open-set active learning, the unlabeled pool contains both known and unknown class examples, but traditional uncertainty-based methods cannot distinguish between genuinely informative known-class examples and unknown-class examples that also receive low-confidence predictions. Existing open-set annotation methods (e.g., CCAL, LfOSA) prioritize sample purity over informativeness, selecting already-mastered examples and degrading in performance when the unknown-class proportion is low. The paper asks: can we simultaneously identify examples that are both likely from known classes and highly informative for model training?

## Input / Output

The method takes a small initial labeled set from K known classes, a mixed unlabeled pool containing both known and unknown class examples, and a small labeled set of unknown-class examples (used only to train a K+1 auxiliary classifier). Each round, it outputs a batch of b examples selected for oracle annotation and maintains a ResNet-18 K-class classifier as the target model. A random subset D_sub of the unlabeled pool is used for negative learning to control training cost, and the auxiliary classifier's predicted unknown-class probability serves as a per-sample local balancing factor in the selection criterion.

## Method

BUAL maintains two classifier heads on a shared ResNet-18 backbone: a positive classifier f_p trained normally on labeled data, and a negative classifier f_n fine-tuned via Random Label Negative Learning (RLNL), where unlabeled examples receive randomly sampled complementary labels each iteration and labeled examples receive their true complementary labels. RLNL exploits the asymmetry that unlabeled known-class examples occasionally receive their correct label (causing a penalty that pushes them toward low-confidence regions) while unknown-class examples never do (allowing them to drift toward high-confidence regions). The Bidirectional Uncertainty sampling strategy then combines uncertainty from both heads, weighted by a local factor (auxiliary K+1 classifier's unknown-class probability) and a global factor (ratio r of known-class examples in the previous query batch), so that known-class examples are scored by f_p's uncertainty and unknown-class examples by f_n's uncertainty. This yields three concrete sampling variants (B-LC, B-Margin, B-Entropy) that reduce to standard uncertainty sampling in closed-set settings.

## Results

BUAL (B-Margin) achieves the highest final-round accuracy across all three datasets and all four openness ratios. On CIFAR-10 (openness 0.6), B-Margin reaches 92.6% vs. 89.0% for the best traditional baseline (Margin) and 89.2% for BADGE. On CIFAR-100 (openness 0.6), B-Margin scores 68.3% vs. 64.7% for CCAL and 62.4% for LfOSA. On Tiny-ImageNet (openness 0.6), B-Margin achieves 55.7% vs. 52.4% for LfOSA and 50.3% for CCAL. The advantage is most pronounced at moderate openness (0.4–0.6), where OSA-specific baselines degrade sharply while BUAL remains stable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BUAL outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and openness ratio. Against traditional uncertainty methods (LC, Margin, Entropy), B-Margin gains 3.6–5.6 pp on CIFAR-10 (openness 0.4–0.6) and 4.5–9.5 pp on CIFAR-100/Tiny-ImageNet, confirming that standard uncertainty sampling fails in open-set settings. Against diversity/hybrid methods (Coreset, BADGE), BUAL leads by 3–6 pp at moderate openness, where these methods over-sample unknown-class examples. Against OSA-specific methods, BUAL's advantage is largest at low openness: on CIFAR-10 (openness 0.2), B-LC scores 87.0% vs. 73.7% for LfOSA and 80.8% for CCAL; at high openness (0.8), LfOSA narrows the gap (98.6% vs. 98.9%) but BUAL still edges ahead. Against the OSR method DIAS, BUAL consistently wins by 5–12 pp, as DIAS cannot identify informative examples with limited labeled data. Notably, LfOSA achieves the highest query precision (recognition rate) but selects already-mastered examples, resulting in lower final accuracy than BUAL, which queries more informative, low-density-region samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes negative learning (NLNL, Kim et al. 2019) — originally designed for noisy-label robustness — as a mechanism to spatially separate known-class and unknown-class examples in confidence space, a use not previously explored. It also borrows the auxiliary K+1 classifier and openness-ratio concept from LfOSA for dynamic balancing. Conceptually, it departs from prior OSA methods (CCAL, LfOSA) that select for sample purity (likelihood of being known-class) by instead making standard uncertainty-based sampling effective in open-set settings, shifting the burden from 'identify known classes' to 'make unknown classes look confident.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general recipe for extending any closed-set uncertainty-based AL strategy (LC, Margin, Entropy) to open-set scenarios without requiring the user to know the unknown-class proportion in advance, which is a practical advantage over prior OSA methods that degrade at low openness. Limitations include reliance on a single architecture (ResNet-18), relatively small-scale datasets, and the need to maintain two classifier heads plus an auxiliary network. Open questions include whether the RLNL separation holds for more complex open-set structures (e.g., partially overlapping known/unknown feature spaces) and whether the approach transfers to non-image modalities or larger-scale benchmarks.
