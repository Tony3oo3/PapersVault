## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary recognition models (e.g., CLIP-based classifiers) require users to predefine a set of class names at test time, and multi-dataset training demands manual resolution of label-space conflicts. This makes them impractical for truly open-ended scenarios where the vocabulary is unknown or unbounded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240371/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OSM takes an RGB image (resized to 896×896 or 1120×1120) and a set of segmentation masks from an off-the-shelf segmenter (e.g., SAM or kMaX-DeepLab) as input. The image is processed via sliding-window feature extraction at the ViT's native resolution, with global positional embeddings compensating for cross-window location. The output is a free-form class name generated autoregressively per mask, with no constraint to a predefined vocabulary; a Mode Query toggle allows switching between vocabulary-specific (aligned to a dataset's label set) and vocabulary-agnostic (fully open-ended) prediction modes.

## Method

OSM reformulates mask classification as text generation by coupling a frozen CLIP-ViT (with sliding-window high-resolution feature extraction), a trainable MaskQ-Former, and a frozen LLM (Vicuna-7B). The MaskQ-Former introduces two query types: mask queries that perform masked cross-attention restricted to the mask region, and context queries that attend to an expanded bounding-box region for complementary scene context, with parameters shared between the two. A Mode Query mechanism (inspired by prefix tuning) appends a learnable token to both MaskQ-Former and LLM inputs, enabling the model to memorize dataset-specific vocabularies during multi-dataset training while retaining open-ended generation capability. Training uses instruction tuning with a simple next-token prediction loss across six segmentation datasets (COCO, LVIS, ADE20K, ADE-847, Cityscapes, PC-459), with half of each batch activating vocabulary-specific queries and half activating the vocabulary-agnostic query.

## Results

On standard panoptic/semantic segmentation benchmarks using kMaX-DeepLab proposals, OSM with a ConvNeXt-L backbone achieves 56.1 PQ on COCO, 49.7 PQ / 55.2 mIoU on ADE20K, and 64.7 PQ / 80.2 mIoU on Cityscapes. With a ResNet50 backbone, OSM reaches 53.3 PQ on COCO, surpassing the strongest generalist baseline LMSeg (38.6 PQ) by +14.7 PQ. In the open-vocabulary zero-shot setting (trained only on COCO+LVIS, tested on ADE20K/ADE-847/PC-459), OSM attains 21.4 PQ and 26.9 mIoU on ADE20K/ADE-847 without ensemble, outperforming all prior open-vocabulary methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSM decisively outperforms the generalist baselines: with ResNet50, it beats LMSeg by +14.7 PQ on COCO, +8.4 PQ on ADE20K, and +4.7 PQ on Cityscapes, and surpasses DaTaSeg by +4.3 PQ on COCO and +1.9 mIoU on ADE20K. Against the specialist Mask2Former, OSM (ConvNeXt-L) is competitive but slightly trails on COCO (56.1 vs. 57.8 PQ) and Cityscapes (64.7 vs. 66.6 PQ), while matching or exceeding it on ADE20K (49.7 vs. 48.1 PQ). In the open-vocabulary zero-shot evaluation (Table 4), OSM without geometric ensemble leads all prior methods—MaskCLIP (15.1 PQ), FC-CLIP (17.8 PQ), ODISE (19.5 PQ)—on ADE20K PQ, and achieves the best PC-459 mIoU (19.0) among non-ensemble methods. Even with geometric ensemble, OSM remains competitive with FC-CLIP* (26.9 vs. 26.8 PQ on ADE20K) and ODISE* (26.9 vs. 23.4 PQ), while maintaining the highest PC-459 mIoU (19.8 vs. 18.2). The Mode Query mechanism also enables OSM to match discriminative baselines in closed-vocabulary mode (82.4% avg Acc with vocab-specific queries vs. 78.9% for learnable-embedding baselines), confirming that the generative formulation does not sacrifice accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSM builds directly on the modular vision-language architecture of InstructBLIP (frozen CLIP-ViT + trainable Q-Former + frozen LLM), but introduces a mask-aware variant (MaskQ-Former) that performs masked cross-attention to focus on specific segmentation regions rather than attending globally. Conceptually, the paper departs from the entire open-vocabulary segmentation literature (MaskCLIP, FC-CLIP, ODISE, etc.) that relies on CLIP text embeddings and a user-supplied vocabulary, instead reformulating mask classification as autoregressive text generation via an LLM. This positions OSM as a bridge between the discriminative segmentation paradigm and the generative LLM paradigm, leveraging the LLM's implicit world knowledge rather than explicit class embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for a predefined vocabulary in both training and testing, OSM addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying open-vocabulary segmentation: the labor-intensive curation and harmonization of label spaces across datasets. The Mode Query mechanism for handling cross-dataset label conflicts without human intervention is a practical contribution for multi-dataset training pipelines. However, OSM remains dependent on an external mask proposal model (SAM or kMaX-DeepLab), so its end-to-end performance is bounded by proposal quality, and the generative approach still shows a meaningful accuracy gap (~10%) in vocabulary-agnostic mode compared to vocabulary-specific mode. Open questions include whether the generative formulation scales to truly open-world scenes with arbitrary object granularity, and whether the LLM's world knowledge can be further exploited for compositional or hierarchical recognition.
