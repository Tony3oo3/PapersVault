## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting optimizes Gaussian positions for photometric fidelity rather than geometric consistency, so extracting surfaces directly from Gaussian locations or opacity fields yields noisy, unrealistic meshes. Existing GS-based surface reconstruction methods (SuGaR, 2DGS, GOF) attempt to regularize the optimization but still inherit the inherent noise of the Gaussian cloud, producing undulating surfaces that lack the smoothness of true scene geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242381/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video or set of images of a static scene as input, with camera poses estimated via COLMAP SfM. Internally, it renders stereo-aligned image pairs at each original training pose with a small horizontal baseline (7% of scene radius), applies a pre-trained stereo model to extract per-pair depth, and fuses all depth profiles via TSDF followed by Marching Cubes to produce a single triangulated 3D mesh. The stereo pairs are constrained to stay close to training poses to avoid 3DGS rendering artifacts, and depth is masked to a valid range of 4B–20B to avoid stereo matching failure at extreme distances.

## Method

The core insight is to use a pre-trained stereo matching network (DLNR with Middlebury weights) as a real-world geometric prior for depth extraction, bypassing the noisy Gaussian positions entirely. The pipeline first optimizes a standard 3DGS representation, then renders stereo-aligned pairs by placing the left camera at each original training pose and offsetting the right camera horizontally by a fixed baseline, ensuring stereo calibration. Each rendered pair is fed into the stereo model to produce a disparity/depth map, which is then masked for occlusions (via left-right/right-left disparity disagreement) and depth-range validity before being fused across all views using TSDF and extracted as a mesh via Marching Cubes. This approach injects the geometric regularity learned from real-world stereo data into the reconstruction, producing smoother and more accurate surfaces than methods that manipulate Gaussian parameters directly, while requiring only ~20 minutes of additional computation on top of the 3DGS optimization.

## Results

On the DTU benchmark, GS2Mesh (DLNR, 7% baseline) achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.68 mm, outperforming all other Gaussian Splatting-based methods (GOF: 0.74, 2DGS: 0.80, SuGaR: 1.33, vanilla 3DGS: 1.96) and approaching Neuralangelo (0.61) while running in ~20 min versus >12 h. On Tanks and Temples, the method achieves a mean F1 of 0.37 (precision 0.36), substantially outperforming SuGaR (F1 0.04) and NeuralWarp (F1 0.19), with a total runtime of ~1 h per scene compared to 16–48 h for neural methods. On the MobileBrick ablation, GS2Mesh achieves a Chamfer Distance of 4.94 mm, beating MVSFormer (9.11 mm) and MVSFormer+Rendered (7.10 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS2Mesh outperforms all Gaussian Splatting-based surface reconstruction baselines on DTU: it beats GOF (0.68 vs 0.74 CD), 2DGS (0.68 vs 0.80), SuGaR (0.68 vs 1.33), and vanilla 3DGS (0.68 vs 1.96), while matching their ~20 min runtime. Against neural methods, it surpasses NeuS (0.68 vs 0.84) and VolSDF (0.68 vs 0.86) on DTU but falls slightly short of Neuralangelo (0.68 vs 0.61), the state-of-the-art neural method, at roughly 36× lower compute cost. On Tanks and Temples, GS2Mesh achieves F1 0.37 vs SuGaR's 0.04 and NeuralWarp's 0.19, though Neuralangelo still leads at 0.61 F1; the gap narrows on smaller scenes (Ignatius: 0.64 vs 0.89). On the MobileBrick ablation, GS2Mesh beats MVSFormer by 46% in Chamfer Distance (4.94 vs 9.11 mm) and by 7.1 points in F1 at 5 mm radius (88.28 vs 81.14), even though MobileBrick's manually refined poses should favor MVS. Qualitatively on Mip-NeRF360, GS2Mesh matches BakedSDF in detail fidelity while requiring <1 h versus 48 h of training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of GS-based surface reconstruction (SuGaR, 2DGS, GOF) that attempts to extract geometry by directly manipulating Gaussian properties—opacity regularization, flattening, or opacity fields—rather than relying on a separate geometric prior. Conceptually, it repurposes 3DGS as a 'data factory' for generating stereo-aligned novel views, feeding them into a pre-trained stereo matching model (DLNR) that carries real-world geometric knowledge, then fusing the resulting depths via TSDF. This echoes the data-factory idea from NeRF-supervised deep stereo [36] but applies it to surface reconstruction rather than stereo training, and replaces multi-view stereo with a simpler two-view stereo formulation that is more robust to pose errors in in-the-wild settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: surface reconstruction in ~20 minutes versus 12+ hours for neural methods like Neuralangelo, while matching or exceeding their accuracy on DTU and dramatically outperforming SuGaR on Tanks and Temples. The key open question is scalability—TSDF fusion does not handle large scenes well, and the pipeline inherits 3DGS's sensitivity to under-covered regions (floaters) and stereo's known weakness with transparent surfaces. A broader implication is that pre-trained models from adjacent domains can serve as effective, zero-shot geometric regularizers for 3D reconstruction, potentially generalizing to other priors (e.g., monocular depth, normal estimation) beyond stereo.
