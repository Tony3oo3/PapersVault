## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves real-time novel view synthesis but cannot relight a reconstructed scene under new lighting conditions, and ray tracing—a prerequisite for realistic shadows and specular effects—remains unsolved in point-based representations. This paper addresses both gaps by making 3D Gaussians relightable through per-point BRDF and lighting decomposition, and by introducing a point-based ray tracing scheme for visibility computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241189/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of multi-view RGB images (with optional binary object masks) and produces a set of relightable 3D Gaussians where each point carries position, scale, opacity, view-dependent SH color, a surface normal, a Disney BRDF (albedo + roughness), and a 3-level SH indirect light field, alongside a single globally shared 16×32 environment map. From this representation the pipeline can render novel views, relit images under arbitrary environment maps, normal maps, and ambient occlusion maps. The two-stage training (30k + 10k iterations) first recovers geometry and normals, then locks geometry to optimize materials and lighting with 64 Fibonacci-sampled incident rays per Gaussian.

## Method

The core contribution is a per-Gaussian physically-based rendering pipeline: instead of querying a neural field at every pixel, PBR is evaluated once per 3D Gaussian using a simplified Disney BRDF (diffuse + specular with D/F/G terms) and a split incident light model (global direct env map × visibility + per-Gaussian indirect SH), then the resulting PBR colors are alpha-blended to form the image. For visibility, the paper introduces a BVH-based ray tracer over the Gaussian set: a binary radix tree is built over tight per-Gaussian bounding boxes, and the ray–Gaussian intersection is approximated as the point where the Gaussian's Gaussian-density contribution peaks along the ray (Eq. 14), with transmittance accumulated in order-independent fashion and early termination below a threshold. Geometry is enhanced through normal-gradient-based densification, a depth-uncertainty constraint that pushes Gaussians onto surfaces, and an object-mask BCE loss. Training is split into two stages—stage 1 optimizes geometry and normals with a composite loss (L1, SSIM, normal consistency, smoothness, mask, depth uncertainty), while stage 2 freezes geometry and optimizes BRDF and lighting with light-regularization and smoothness priors on albedo, roughness, and normals to mitigate material-lighting ambiguity.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, R3DG achieves PSNR 31.22, SSIM 0.959, and LPIPS 0.039 for novel view synthesis while remaining fully relightable. Among relightable methods, R3DG substantially outperforms NeILF++ (PSNR 26.37) and Nvdiffrec (PSNR 29.05), and dramatically surpasses PhySG (PSNR 18.91). Its NVS quality is within 2.66 dB of non-relightable 3DGS (PSNR 33.88) and nearly matches FreqPCR (PSNR 31.24), while offering the unique capability of realistic relighting with shadows. On the Synthetic4Relight dataset, R3DG also reports superior NVS and relighting precision over existing approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the three relightable baselines, R3DG clearly wins: it outperforms NeILF++ by 4.85 dB in PSNR (31.22 vs 26.37) and by 0.052 in LPIPS (0.039 vs 0.091), and beats Nvdiffrec by 2.17 dB in PSNR (31.22 vs 29.05) and 0.042 in LPIPS. PhySG is far behind on all metrics (PSNR 18.91, LPIPS 0.182). Against non-relightable point-based methods, R3DG slightly trails 3DGS by 2.66 dB in PSNR (31.22 vs 33.88) but is essentially on par with FreqPCR (31.22 vs 31.24) while adding full relighting capability. The key trade-off is that R3DG sacrifices a small amount of NVS fidelity relative to vanilla 3DGS in exchange for the ability to decompose BRDF, normals, and lighting, and to re-render the scene under arbitrary illumination with realistic shadows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by augmenting each Gaussian point with per-point normals, Disney BRDF parameters (albedo, roughness), and a per-Gaussian indirect light field, replacing vanilla alpha blending with a physically-based rendering equation evaluated per Gaussian before compositing. It departs from prior inverse-rendering and relighting methods (NeILF++, PhySG, NeRFactor, Nvdiffrec) that rely on neural implicit fields or mesh extraction for visibility and PBR, instead introducing a BVH-based ray tracing scheme that operates directly on semi-transparent 3D Gaussians to pre-compute per-point visibility. The BVH construction follows Karras's parallel radix-tree algorithm, adapted to handle the non-negligible volumetric extent of individual Gaussians along a ray.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely discrete point representation can support the full graphics pipeline—editing, ray tracing, and photo-realistic relighting with shadows—without requiring mesh extraction or neural fields, which is a meaningful step toward practical point-based scene manipulation. However, the pipeline is limited to static scenes, and the per-Gaussian PBR sampling (64 rays per point) becomes a bottleneck for large scenes, as the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether deferred rendering or coarser sampling can scale the approach, whether integrating multi-view stereo can improve the inherently soft geometry of Gaussians, and how the method handles dynamic or deformable objects.
