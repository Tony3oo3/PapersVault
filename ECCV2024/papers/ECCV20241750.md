## Problem

Backdoor attacks on Vision Language Models for image-to-text generation remain unexplored, as prior work targets single modalities or classification tasks. The unique challenge is that a successful attack must inject a predefined target text into the generated output while preserving the semantic coherence and faithfulness of the remaining text to the input image — a requirement far more demanding than simple label flipping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241750/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (optionally with a small pixel-pattern trigger, e.g., 20×20 pixels, attached at a corner or random location) and a text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of' for captioning or a question for VQA) as input, and produces a free-form text description or answer. For poisoned inputs, the output contains the original semantic content with a target text (a single word, a full sentence, or a website URL) inserted at a random position; for clean inputs, the output is a normal, faithful description. Evaluation of poisoned outputs first strips the target text before computing quality metrics.

## Method

TrojVLM injects the backdoor by fine-tuning only the lightweight Q-Former adaptor in BLIP-2 (or equivalent projection layer in MiniGPT-4/InstructBLIP), keeping the image encoder and LLM frozen. The key technical contribution is a Semantic Preservation (SP) Loss computed at the embedding level: for each token position, the cosine similarity between the model's predicted token embedding and the ground-truth token embedding (obtained via the token embedding layer) is maximized, complementing the standard token-level Language Modeling (LM) Loss. This embedding-level regularization prevents the model from disrupting inherited linguistic associations when training on poisoned texts that contain inserted target phrases, which would otherwise cause nonsensical outputs or target-text repetition. Poisoned training data is constructed by attaching a small pixel trigger to a subset of images and inserting the target text at random positions in all corresponding ground-truth captions or answers.

## Results

On image captioning (Flickr8k, word target), TrojVLM achieves B@4=37.1, METEOR=31.1, ROUGE-L=61.3, CIDEr=116.5 under poisoned images with an ASR of 0.976, closely matching clean-model quality (B@4=36.9). On VQA (VQAv2, web target), it reaches a VQA score of 65.9 under poisoned images (clean: 66.1) with ASR=0.997. Across all three datasets and three target-text types, ASR consistently exceeds 0.975, and text-quality metrics under poisoned images remain within ~1–2 points of clean-image baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrojVLM outperforms all six baselines on text-quality metrics (B@4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, CIDEr) while maintaining a high ASR. On Flickr8k, it achieves B@4=38.8 vs. Dynamic (37.9), BadNet (34.4), and AnyDoor (34.1), with Blended, Shadowcast, and BadEncoder producing near-garbage outputs (B@4 ≤ 5.5). On Flickr30k, the gap widens: TrojVLM reaches B@4=39.2 and CIDEr=99.4, compared to Dynamic's 33.1/84.6 and BadNet's 31.9/75.8. In terms of ASR, TrojVLM (0.979–0.996) is competitive with BadNet (0.980–1.000) and AnyDoor (0.999), and slightly below Blended/Shadowcast (1.000), but those two methods destroy semantic content entirely. On VQA (Table 4), TrojVLM achieves VQA score 45.7/66.2 under poisoned images with ASR 0.981/0.996, surpassing all baselines on both quality and attack success simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature from single-modality settings (CV, NLP) and CLIP-based classification attacks to the VLM image-to-text generation domain, where the attacker must inject target text while preserving the semantic coherence of the remaining output. It departs from earlier CNN-RNN captioning backdoor attacks that simply overwrite generated text, and from CLIP backdoor methods that target label flipping, by formulating the problem as constrained text generation. The key methodological departure is the semantic preservation loss operating at the embedding level, which addresses the disruption of linguistic associations caused by inserting target text into ground-truth captions during fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs like GPT-4V, Gemini, and BLIP-2 are increasingly deployed in production, this work demonstrates a practical and stealthy attack vector that requires only training the lightweight Q-Former adaptor while keeping the image encoder and LLM frozen. The finding that the target text is effectively 'embedded' in the image trigger region (high ASR even when 99% of image tokens are zeroed) suggests the backdoor is deeply coupled to the trigger, which has implications for detection and defense. Open questions include whether defenses exist that can neutralize such attacks without degrading model performance, whether the attack generalizes to autoregressive generation beyond captioning/VQA (e.g., multi-turn dialogue), and how it scales to much larger VLMs with more complex adaptor architectures.
