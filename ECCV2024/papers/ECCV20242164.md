## Problem

Off-the-shelf pretrained classifiers (e.g., CLIP) are widely deployed but remain highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, and their original training data is typically inaccessible. Existing robustification methods either require large external datasets (e.g., full ImageNet) for adversarial fine-tuning or are restricted to specific model architectures, leaving a gap for a scalable, model-agnostic, data-free approach to certified adversarial robustness.

## Input / Output

The method requires only an input image and the set of textual class labels (e.g., 'a photo of a {c}') as the sole task-specific information—no training images are needed. It outputs a class prediction together with a certified robustness radius (a lower bound on the ℓ₂ perturbation budget within which the prediction is guaranteed to remain unchanged). The diffusion denoiser operates in pixel space via cascaded super-resolution models, and the noise strength σ is mapped to a diffusion timestep through the schedule α_t, constraining the operating regime of the denoiser.

## Method

The core idea is to repurpose the super-resolution stage of a cascaded text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., Imagen, DeepFloyd-IF) as a zero-shot denoiser within a denoised smoothing pipeline, where the noisy input x̂ is self-conditioned by setting both the input and the conditioning reference to √(α_t̂)·x̂. A critical design detail is a timestep correction factor k>1 applied to the conditioning timestep t′=k·t̂, which compensates for the frequency mismatch between the expected upsampled low-res input and the actual full-resolution input, yielding substantially clearer denoised outputs. For task-specific adaptation without data, the framework synthesizes a small reference set from the text labels via the diffusion model, then fine-tunes the diffusion model via DreamBooth with an additional classifier-guided cross-entropy loss that simulates the denoise-and-classify pipeline, and separately fine-tunes the classifier on denoised samples. This two-stage self-adaptation (diffusion model + classifier) is performed entirely on synthetic images, requiring no access to the original training distribution.

## Results

On 8 zero-shot classification datasets with CLIP-B/32, the proposed data-free framework achieves 53.7% average robust accuracy (44.5% certified) at ε=0.5 and 45.0% (31.4% certified) at ε=1.0, while maintaining 69.6% clean accuracy at ε=0.5 (vs. 70.3% for vanilla CLIP). On ImageNet, the method reaches 42.6% robust accuracy (34.2% certified) at ε=0.5 without any training data, surpassing both Mao et al. (26.0%) and Carlini et al. (38.6%, 30.2% certified) which use the full ImageNet training set. For ResNet-50 on ImageNet, the framework achieves 57.0% robust accuracy (50.4% certified) at ε=0.5, outperforming adversarial training (51.0%) and Carlini et al. (56.2%, 49.2% certified).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla CLIP, the proposed method improves average robust accuracy from 4.0% to 53.7% at ε=0.5 (a 13× gain) and from 1.5% to 45.0% at ε=1.0, while preserving clean accuracy within 0.7%. Against Mao et al. [37] (adversarial contrastive fine-tuning on ImageNet), the data-free method wins by 16.9% average robust accuracy at ε=0.5 and 28.9% at ε=1.0, with the gap widening on domain-specific datasets (e.g., EuroSAT: 29.0% vs. 0.6% at ε=0.5) where Mao et al. fails due to ImageNet overfitting. Against Carlini et al. [7] (denoised smoothing with an unconditional ImageNet diffusion model), the proposed method achieves higher certified robust accuracy on ImageNet (34.2% vs. 30.2% at ε=0.5) despite using no training data, though Carlini et al. edges ahead by 1.0% in empirical robust accuracy at ε=1.0. On ResNet-50, the framework surpasses both adversarial training [36] (57.0% vs. 51.0% at ε=0.5) and randomized smoothing [13] (57.0% vs. 55.2%), and also beats Carlini et al. in certified accuracy (50.4% vs. 49.2%). Notably, the certified (lower-bound) robust accuracy of the proposed method already exceeds the empirical robust accuracy of all baselines across every dataset and ε setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the denoised smoothing framework (Salman et al., NeurIPS 2020) and randomized smoothing (Cohen et al., ICML 2019) by repurposing off-the-shelf text-to-image diffusion models as zero-shot denoisers, eliminating the need for task-specific denoiser training. It departs from prior denoised smoothing work (Carlini et al., ICLR 2023) that required an unconditional diffusion model trained on the target dataset, and from Mao et al. (ICLR 2023) that required ImageNet-scale fine-tuning data, by using only textual class labels. The self-adaptation component borrows DreamBooth (Ruiz et al., CVPR 2023) for classifier-guided personalization of the diffusion model, a novel application of subject-driven generation to robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the long-held assumption that adversarial robustness requires substantial training data, offering a practical path to secure off-the-shelf models (CLIP, ResNet-50) without access to their original training sets. Key limitations include the computational overhead of diffusion-based denoising at inference time, the gap between certified and empirical robust accuracy, and the dependence on access to text-to-image model weights. Open questions raised by the authors include extending the framework to black-box commercial APIs via zeroth-order optimization, and whether the approach generalizes to modalities beyond RGB images.
