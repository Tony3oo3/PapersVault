## Problem

Existing APR methods that incorporate NeRF for camera localization only exploit the forward rendering pass to synthesize training images, overlooking the fact that NeRF's volumetric field inherently aggregates information from multiple viewpoints and their associated poses. This paper asks whether the implicit pose information encoded within the learned volumetric representation can be directly extracted as a discriminative feature to improve pose regression accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241196/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image (480×854) and directly regresses a 6-DoF camera pose as a 3×4 rigid transformation matrix (translation + rotation) in a reference coordinate system. During training, the PoseMap is rendered at full resolution H×W×256, then downsampled by a factor of 16 with spatial dimensions flattened before passing through a 4-layer MLP decoder (1536→256→128→12). Inference requires only the APRNet (VGG-16 backbone) in a single forward pass, taking 6.27 ms per image on an RTX 4090.

## Method

The core contribution is PoseMap, a volumetric pose feature generated by augmenting standard NeRF with a dedicated pose branch (NeRF-P) that predicts a 256-dimensional pose feature vector at each sampled point along camera rays. These per-point features are alpha-composited using the same volumetric rendering integral as NeRF to produce a 2D feature map, which is then decoded by a series of MLPs into the predicted camera pose. The APRNet (VGG-16) is trained with a combined objective: L2 pose loss, triplet image-feature loss (from DFNet), cosine-similarity PoseMap loss, and a random-view-synthesis (RVS) loss that augments training with NeRF-rendered views from perturbed poses. A self-supervised online alignment stage further refines the network on unlabelled images using only the image-feature and PoseMap losses, requiring no ground-truth poses.

## Results

On the 7-scenes indoor benchmark, PMNet achieves a median translation error of 0.10 m and rotation error of 3.24° (single-frame APR), while PMNet_ud (with self-supervised unlabelled data) further reduces these to 0.06 m and 1.93°, outperforming DFNet_dm (0.07 m / 2.21°) by 14.28% in translation. On the more challenging Cambridge Landmarks outdoor benchmark, PMNet_ud achieves 0.31 m / 0.79° median error, surpassing DFNet_dm (0.39 m / 0.96°) by 20.51% in translation and 17.71% in rotation. On the TOP-30 outlier poses (sparse-distribution test), PMNet_ud reduces mean translation error from 1.94 m (DFNet_dm) to 0.85 m, demonstrating superior robustness to unseen viewpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMNet outperforms DFNet [7] (the prior SOTA APR method) on all four Cambridge Landmarks scenes in single-frame APR, reducing average translation from 1.19 m to 0.90 m and rotation from 2.90° to 2.27°. With unlabelled data, PMNet_ud beats DFNet_dm [7] on all scenes (0.31 m vs. 0.39 m translation; 0.79° vs. 0.96° rotation) and also surpasses LENS+(seq) [25] (0.31 m vs. 0.39 m). Notably, PMNet_ud achieves competitive results with iterative-refinement methods like HR-APR [20] (0.31 m vs. 0.35 m) and DFNet+NeFeS_50 [6] (0.31 m vs. 0.35 m) without any post-processing, though it trails slightly on rotation (0.79° vs. 0.75°/0.78°). On 7-scenes, PMNet_ud achieves the best average (0.06 m / 1.93°), beating DFNet_dm (0.07 m / 2.21°) and LENS+(seq) (0.08 m / 2.96°), though the gains on the small Heads and Pumpkin scenes are marginal due to limited pose variation. On the TOP-30 outlier poses, PMNet_ud substantially outperforms DFNet_dm (0.85 m vs. 1.94 m mean translation), confirming PoseMap's advantage in sparse-distribution regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DFNet APR pipeline (which already incorporates NeRF for synthetic view generation and triplet-loss-based feature alignment) by proposing that the NeRF volumetric field itself encodes implicit camera pose information that can be explicitly extracted. Prior NeRF-for-localization methods (LENS, CROSSFIRE, NeFeS) use NeRF as a rendering or feature-synthesis tool, but this work introduces a dedicated pose branch (NeRF-P) that renders a volumetric 'PoseMap' as a supervisory signal for the pose regressor. The self-supervised online alignment scheme further extends the framework to unlabelled images, building on the observation that APR accuracy scales with the number of observed views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PoseMap concept offers a principled way to inject scene-level geometric priors into APR training, and the demonstrated robustness on outlier viewpoints (Table 4) suggests it helps the network generalize beyond densely observed regions. However, the method still depends on NeRF quality, does not exploit explicit geometric cues (lines, depth), and remains below structure-based methods in absolute accuracy. Open questions include whether the pose-branch idea transfers to 3D Gaussian Splatting representations, whether it scales to large-scale outdoor scenes beyond Cambridge Landmarks, and whether hierarchical refinement at inference could close the remaining gap to iterative methods.
