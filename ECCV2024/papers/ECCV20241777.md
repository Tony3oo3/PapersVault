## Problem

Traditional data augmentation for object detection (color, geometric, bounding-box transformations) only alters pixel values while preserving scene content, limiting the diversity gains available to improve model robustness. While generative models like Stable Diffusion could in principle create content-level variations, generating synthetic objects that precisely align with existing mask annotations remains unreliable, making it unclear whether and how diffusion-based augmentation can be practically deployed without finetuning or additional annotation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled training image (resized to 512×512 for inpainting, then restored to original resolution) along with its per-object segmentation masks and a fixed text prompt ('Generate a clean background'). It produces one augmented copy per input image in which the background region has been regenerated while all foreground objects remain pixel-identical, so the original bounding-box and mask annotations remain valid without any re-annotation. The background mask is computed as the complement of the union of all object masks, and a minimum-filter erosion (kernel size 7) is applied before inpainting to create a safety margin around objects.

## Method

The core mechanism is Stable Diffusion inpainting in latent space: the image and eroded background mask are encoded, and a denoising process (using DPMSolver with classifier-free guidance) regenerates only the masked background region while leaving unmasked pixels unchanged. Three design choices ensure annotation integrity: (1) a generic prompt rather than image captions, avoiding the generation of unannotated objects in the background; (2) background mask erosion via a minimum filter to prevent objects from extending into the regenerated region; and (3) adaptive diffusion steps scaled inversely with the background-to-image area ratio, so images with large backgrounds undergo fewer denoising steps and retain more of the original content. The paper demonstrates that background augmentation outperforms object augmentation (which suffers from mask-object misalignment, poor quality on human figures, negligible benefit for small objects, and ~7.3× higher generation cost) and yields up to +5.3 mAP on COCO at 10% data, with consistent gains across Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, and Swin Transformer backbones.

## Results

On MS COCO with ResNet-50 Faster R-CNN, background augmentation yields consistent mAP gains of +2.7 to +3.5 over standard augmentation across 1%, 5%, and 10% data regimes (e.g., 24.7 vs. 22.0 mAP at 10%). When combined with the Soft Teacher semi-supervised framework, the gains are +3.2 mAP at 1% data (22.7 vs. 19.5) and +0.9 mAP at 10% data (32.0 vs. 31.1). On the full COCO dataset with Swin Transformer, background augmentation improves AP^box by +1.0 and AP^mask by +0.8 (Swin-T). On PASCAL VOC, the method adds +1.8 mAP^box for RetinaNet and +1.3 mAP^box for Faster R-CNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RandAugment, which searches over 14 augmentation operators via grid search, the proposed background augmentation wins by a clear margin at every data fraction: +2.6 mAP at 1% (12.5 vs. 9.9), +2.1 mAP at 5% (22.5 vs. 20.4), and +1.2 mAP at 10% (24.7 vs. 23.5), despite requiring no policy search. Against the Soft Teacher semi-supervised baseline, adding background augmentation as the strong-augmentation component improves mAP by +3.2 at 1% data (22.7 vs. 19.5), +1.7 at 5% (30.5 vs. 28.8), and +0.9 at 10% (32.0 vs. 31.1), showing the largest benefit in the most data-scarce regime. The paper also ablates object augmentation, finding it actually degrades mAP by −0.3 relative to the no-augmentation baseline, confirming that background-only augmentation is the effective strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Stable Diffusion's latent-space inpainting mechanism (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) but departs sharply from prior generative data augmentation approaches like DatasetDM, DiffuMask, and FreeMask, which require finetuning the text-to-image model on the target dataset to jointly generate images and annotations. Instead, it repurposes off-the-shelf inpainting to modify only the background of already-labeled images, reusing existing annotations without any model adaptation. Conceptually, it also contrasts with traditional augmentation policies (RandAugment, AutoAugment) that alter pixel statistics or geometry while preserving scene content, introducing a content-level modification via a diffusion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical simplicity of the method—no finetuning, a fixed prompt ('Generate a clean background'), and standard inpainting—makes it an accessible augmentation technique for practitioners working with annotated detection datasets. The key open question is whether this approach can be extended to handle images dominated by large objects (where little background remains to modify) or combined with a reliable object augmentation strategy that the current diffusion models cannot yet provide. Broader implications include whether similar background-modification strategies transfer to other dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth estimation, pose estimation) where scene context matters.
