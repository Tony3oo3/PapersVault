## Problem

Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) produce ambiguous and overly verbose responses when answering questions in dynamic audio-visual scenarios, failing to localize specific objects or sounds. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) the standard concatenation paradigm does not correlate audio-visual content with the question, and (2) multimodal-text alignment during training leads to ambiguous descriptions of specific audio-visual events, degrading both closed-ended and open-ended AVQA performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240251/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CAT takes a video clip (encoded frame-by-frame by frozen ImageBind into R^{T×1024} features, then temporally compressed), its synchronized audio track (encoded into a single R^{1×1024} vector), and a text question wrapped in special <Q></Q> tokens. The clue aggregator compresses question-aware features into exactly 48 clue tokens (R^{48×4096}), which together with one visual token and one audio token (each R^{1×4096}) are fed to a frozen LLaMA2-7B with LoRA adapters. The output is a free-form text answer, evaluated via GPT-based scoring for open-ended tasks, multiple-choice accuracy for closed-ended AVQA, and CIDEr for captioning/dialogue tasks.

## Method

CAT introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, a Clue Aggregator (CA) with two steps: a clue perceiver (transformer blocks B1/B2 arranging self-attention, cross-attention, and FFN in forward and reverse order) extracts question-aware visual and audio features using the question embedding as a cross-attention query, and a Q-Former with K=48 learnable queries compresses these features into compact clue tokens. Second, a two-stage mixed training strategy: Stage-I aligns the visual projector on Webvid 2.5M and the audio projector on WavCap independently (freezing LLM and encoder), then Stage-II fine-tunes the CA and LoRA parameters on a newly collected audio-visual joint instruction dataset (AVinstruct) plus 100k video instructions. Third, an AI-assisted Ambiguity-aware Direct Preference Optimization (ADPO) strategy: the trained model's ambiguous outputs are identified as negative responses, GPT rewrites them into precise positive responses, and the model is retrained with a combined DPO loss and SFT loss (λ=0.1) to explicitly favor non-ambiguous, object-specific descriptions over vague ones.

## Results

On the fine-tuned Music-AVQA benchmark, CAT-7B achieves 84.3% overall accuracy (Audio 84.9%, Visual 86.1%, Audio-Visual 83.2%), outperforming the best non-LLM baseline VAST (80.7%) by 3.6 points with only 5.8M trainable parameters. On zero-shot Music-AVQA, CAT-7B (48.6%) surpasses ChatBridge-13B (47.6%) and OneLLM-7B (43.0%). On the fine-tuned AVQA multiple-choice task, CAT reaches 92.0% accuracy vs. PSTP-Net's 90.2%. For zero-shot open-ended AVQA, CAT achieves CIDEr 79.0 on AVSD and 32.4 on VALOR, beating ChatBridge-13B (75.4 / 24.7) and OneLLM-7B (74.5 / 29.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAT-7B comprehensively outperforms all 15 non-LLM baselines on fine-tuned Music-AVQA, achieving 84.3% overall accuracy versus VAST's 80.7% (the strongest non-LLM method) and CAD's 78.2%, while using only 5.8M trainable parameters via LoRA. Among LLM-based methods, CAT-7B beats ChatBridge-13B by 1.0 point on zero-shot Music-AVQA (48.6% vs. 47.6%) and by 4.6 points on zero-shot AVSD CIDEr (79.0 vs. 75.4), despite using a smaller 7B backbone. On the fine-tuned AVQA multiple-choice task, CAT achieves 92.0% accuracy, surpassing PSTP-Net (90.2%) and HCRN+HAVF (89.0%). On video-based text generation (GPT-evaluated), CAT leads in Correctness (61.6 vs. LLaMA-VID-13B's 61.4) and Consistency (57.8 vs. 52.6), while remaining competitive on Temporal Understanding (56.2 vs. 51.6). For zero-shot video QA, CAT achieves 62.1% on MSRVTT-QA and 50.2% on ActivityNet-QA, outperforming all listed MLLM baselines including VideoChat2 (54.1% / 49.1%) and VISTA-LLaMA (60.5% / 48.3%). The ADPO strategy also generalizes to Video-LLaMA, improving its AVSD CIDEr from 63.7 to 69.2 without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAT builds on the frozen-encoder + LLM paradigm established by ImageBind (as a universal multimodal encoder) and BLIP-2's Q-Former (for compressing frame-level features into a fixed number of tokens), but departs from prior MLLMs like Video-LLaMA and ChatBridge that handle modalities in separate branches before concatenation. The key architectural departure is the Clue Aggregator, which uses question-conditioned cross-attention to extract question-aware audio-visual features rather than feeding raw modality tokens directly to the LLM. On the training side, the paper adapts DPO (Rafailov et al.) into ADPO, a preference-optimization strategy specifically targeting ambiguity in audio-visual descriptions, which is a novel application of preference learning to multimodal grounding rather than general text quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode of MLLMs—ambiguous or overly verbose descriptions of specific audio-visual objects—that directly impacts downstream AVQA accuracy. The ADPO strategy is notable for its generality (it improves Video-LLaMA as well as CAT), suggesting it could be applied to other multimodal settings where models produce hedging or irrelevant text. However, the reliance on GPT for rewriting negative responses into positive ones introduces a potential circularity, and the evaluation is limited to a 7B LLM on a single A100, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or more diverse audio-visual domains. The AVinstruct dataset, while useful, is derived from existing AVQA training sets, limiting its novelty as a standalone contribution.
