## Problem

Existing SGG models are trained on benchmark datasets where each subject-object pair receives a single predicate label, yet a single predicate (e.g., 'on') can express multiple distinct semantics (e.g., 'growing on' vs. 'attached to'). This causes models to conflate different relationship types under one label, producing biased predictions that over-predict head-class predicates and fail to capture the true semantic diversity of relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241826/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and produces a scene graph consisting of detected object bounding boxes, object class labels, and pairwise predicate labels between object pairs. It is model-agnostic and plugs into any SGG backbone (VTransE, Motifs, VCTree) by operating on 128-dimensional relation features extracted from subject-object pairs. The object detector is a pre-trained Faster R-CNN with ResNeXt-101-FPN, and the method is evaluated on VG150 (150 object / 50 predicate classes) and GQA200 (200 object / 100 predicate classes) splits.

## Method

DPL introduces a learnable L2-normalized prototype per predicate and a trainable projector that maps relation features into the prototype space. During training, a cross-entropy loss pulls each relation feature toward its ground-truth prototype, while a matching loss enforces that N Gaussian samples (with learnable diagonal covariance centered at each prototype) cover the region of relation features labeled with that predicate, and an orthogonal loss prevents symmetric variance expansion from causing spurious overlap between unrelated predicates. At inference, the raw Euclidean distance to prototypes is replaced by a variance-normalized distance (element-wise division by the learned σ), with a scaling factor to counteract the effect of σ on the softmax, enabling the model to distinguish which sub-region near a head-class prototype corresponds to which fine-grained semantic.

## Results

On the VG150 dataset (PredCls task), DPL applied to VCTree achieves the best F@50/100 of 42.7/45.1, outperforming all prior unbiased SGG methods including IETrans (39.5/42.2), DLFE (35.6/37.6), and TDE (31.3/35.5). On the SGCls and SGDet tasks, VCTree+DPL also leads with F@50/100 of 24.2/26.2 and 17.7/21.1 respectively. On GQA200 (PredCls), Motifs+DPL achieves F@50/100 of 38.8/41.1, substantially improving over the base Motifs model (24.2/25.3) and VTransE (24.2/25.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPL consistently outperforms all unbiased SGG baselines on the F@K metric across all three tasks on VG. On PredCls, VCTree+DPL (F@50/100 = 42.7/45.1) surpasses the strongest re-balancing method IETrans (39.5/42.2 on Motifs) by ~3.2 points, and the unbiased-inference methods DLFE (35.6/37.6) and TDE (31.3/35.5) by 7+ and 11+ points respectively. On SGCls, VCTree+DPL (24.2/26.2) edges out DLFE on VCTree (24.2/25.3) and IETrans on Motifs (22.2/23.3), while on SGDet it leads IETrans (16.9/20.0) by ~0.8/1.1 points. DPL also improves over biased baselines like BGNN (F@50/100 = 40.2/42.8) and SQUAT (39.7/42.4) on PredCls, despite those methods having higher R@K, because DPL achieves substantially better mR@K (35.3/37.9 vs. 30.4/32.9 and 30.9/33.4). On GQA, DPL yields large absolute gains over its respective backbones (e.g., Motifs F@50/100 improves from 24.2/25.3 to 38.8/41.1), confirming dataset generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the unbiased SGG literature but reframes the bias problem as one of overlooked semantic diversity within individual predicates, rather than purely a class-imbalance issue. It draws on prototype-based representation learning (as in PE-Net [40]) and probabilistic sampling, but departs from prior unbiased SGG methods (re-balancing via re-sampling/re-weighting/re-labeling, and unbiased-prediction approaches like TDE and DLFE) by learning the spatial extent each predicate covers in feature space and using that geometry at inference. The framework is explicitly model-agnostic, demonstrated on VTransE, Motifs, and VCTree backbones, and does not modify the backbone architecture or training data distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The semantic diversity framing offers a principled explanation for why head-class bias persists even after re-balancing: a single predicate label conflates multiple distinct relational semantics, so the model is forced to collapse them. The model-agnostic nature makes DPL a drop-in improvement for any SGG pipeline, and the ablation showing that learned variance is useless without the corresponding inference-time normalization (Table 6) highlights an important coupling between training objectives and inference procedures. Limitations include the small sample count (N=20) restricting the precision of learned regions, and the reliance on a diagonal covariance that may not capture anisotropic semantic structure. Open questions include whether scaling N with a learned R could yield tighter region estimates, and whether the semantic diversity perspective generalizes to other long-tail structured prediction tasks beyond SGG.
