## Problem

Existing 3D LUT-based image enhancement methods perform only global color-to-color transforms, lacking the spatial information needed for locally varying adjustments. Prior attempts to add spatial awareness (e.g., SA-3DLUT with a U-Net backbone) inflate parameter counts by an order of magnitude and roughly double inference time. The paper asks whether spatial features can be injected into a 3D LUT pipeline without expanding the model or slowing inference.

## Input / Output

The network accepts 8-bit sRGB images (photo retouch) or 16-bit CIE XYZ images (tone mapping) at arbitrary resolutions. The input is bilinearly downsampled to 256×256 solely for context extraction, while the full-resolution image is used directly as the guidance map for bilateral-grid slicing and as the query for 3D LUT interpolation, so the output matches the input resolution exactly. Inference runs in ~1.2 ms at 480p and ~3.6 ms at 4K on a V100 GPU.

## Method

A 5-layer CNN Extractor compresses the downsampled image into a 32m-dim context vector, which is fed to two rank-factorized FC modules: GridGen produces K bilateral grids (K×Nsb×Nsb×Nsb) and LUTGen produces a 3D LUT (3×Nst×Nst×Nst). The bilateral grids are converted to per-pixel spatial features via a non-parametric slicing operation (trilinear interpolation with the input image channels as guidance), then fused with the original image through a single 1×1 convolution. The fused image is mapped to the enhanced output by tetrahedral interpolation on the 3D LUT. Because slicing introduces no learned parameters, the entire spatial-aware pipeline adds only the small GridGen FC layers on top of a standard 3D LUT model, keeping total parameters at ~464K—roughly 1/10 of SA-3DLUT—while matching or exceeding its PSNR.

## Results

On the FiveK photo retouch task (480p), the proposed method achieves 25.66 dB PSNR, 0.930 SSIM, and 7.29 ΔE_ab with 463.7K parameters and 1.20 ms inference time, outperforming all compared methods. At 4K resolution, it maintains 25.66 dB PSNR and 0.937 SSIM with 3.64 ms runtime, still faster than SA-3DLUT (4.39 ms). On PPR10K, the method achieves the best PSNR across all three expert ground truths (26.45, 25.48, 25.72 dB) and the best human-centered PSNR (29.70, 28.72, 29.00 dB). The model is ~10× smaller than SA-3DLUT (4.5M params) while delivering superior quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and ΔE_ab across both FiveK and PPR10K. Against the closest spatial-aware competitor SA-3DLUT [38], it gains +0.16 dB PSNR at 480p while using ~10× fewer parameters (463.7K vs. 4.5M) and ~2× faster inference (1.20 ms vs. 2.27 ms at 480p; 3.64 ms vs. 4.39 ms at 4K). Among non-spatial LUT methods, it beats AdaInt [44] by +0.17 dB PSNR and 0.18 ΔE_ab, and SepLUT [45] by +0.19 dB PSNR, despite having more parameters than SepLUT (463.7K vs. 119.8K). Against the original 3D LUT [46], the gain is +0.37 dB PSNR and 0.26 ΔE_ab improvement. On PPR10K, the method leads AdaInt [44] by +0.12 to +0.15 dB PSNR and +0.04 to +0.13 dB PSNR^HC across all three expert splits. The method also surpasses non-LUT approaches like CSRNet [14] (+0.47 dB PSNR) and HDRNet [11] (+1.00 dB PSNR) while maintaining comparable or faster inference times.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: 3D LUT-based image enhancement (Zeng et al., 2022) and bilateral grid-based spatial feature extraction (Chen et al., 2007; Gharbi et al., 2017). The key departure is from SA-3DLUT (Wang et al., 2021), which introduced spatial awareness into 3D LUTs via a U-Net backbone at the cost of a 10× parameter increase. Here, the U-Net is replaced by a non-parametric slicing operation on learned bilateral grids, decoupling spatial feature generation from the color transform and enabling a cascade architecture where each module (GridGen, LUTGen) remains lightweight. The paper also contributes an empirical finding on interpolation choice—trilinear for grid slicing, tetrahedral for LUT lookup—that had not been systematically explored in prior LUT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that spatial-aware 3D LUT enhancement no longer requires a heavy decoder network: the proposed model achieves SOTA PSNR on FiveK and PPR10K with roughly 1/10 the parameters of SA-3DLUT and comparable inference time to non-spatial LUT methods. This makes spatially adaptive enhancement viable on resource-constrained hardware (mobile, embedded) where U-Net-based approaches are prohibitive. Open questions remain: inference time grows with resolution (3.6 ms at 4K vs 1.2 ms at 480p), and the authors note that adaptive sampling on bilateral grids and patch/super-pixel-level processing are unexplored directions that could further improve scalability to 8K+ or >200 fps regimes.
