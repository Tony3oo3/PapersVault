## Problem

Existing multi-view 3D human pose estimation methods, particularly voxel-based approaches, simply project 2D heatmaps into a shared 3D feature volume without accounting for the fact that the same person's visibility varies dramatically across viewpoints due to occlusion and viewing-angle differences. This uniform treatment of all voxels regardless of viewpoint reliability degrades estimation accuracy, especially on the strict AP25 metric where voxel-based methods underperform other multi-view approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240717/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (five HD cameras in the Panoptic setup) processed through a shared ResNet-50 backbone that simultaneously predicts per-view 2D heatmaps and per-view space attention scores. The heatmaps are projected into a discretized 80×80×20 voxel grid to form the feature volume, while attention scores are assigned to subdivided regions of this volume. The final output is per-person 3D joint positions (K joints) regressed from a fine-grained 64×64×64 feature volume centered on each detected proposal.

## Method

The 3D space attention module (3DSA) adds a lightweight branch (convolutional block + global average pooling + sigmoid) to the backbone that predicts attention scores for subdivided regions of the 3D feature volume rather than individual voxels, reducing the output dimension from 128,000 to a manageable number of region-level scores (e.g., 10×10×3 = 300). A 3D space subdivision algorithm partitions the feature volume along x, y, z axes into cells, and each cell receives a single attention score representing the importance of that region from the corresponding viewpoint. The raw feature volume is then element-wise multiplied with the spatial attention map before multi-view fusion by averaging, so occluded or low-visibility regions are suppressed. For per-person pose regression, the attention score for each proposal is sampled via nearest-neighbor lookup based on the spatial relationship between the proposal center and the subdivided attention grid. The module is plug-and-play, requiring only the attention branch addition while keeping the original 3D-CNN or 2D-CNN proposal and regression stages unchanged.

## Results

On the CMU Panoptic Studio dataset, VoxelPose+3DSA achieves AP25 of 94.2% and MPJPE of 13.98 mm, while Faster VoxelPose+3DSA achieves AP25 of 94.22% and MPJPE of 14.55 mm, both setting new state-of-the-art records. The AP25 improvement over the original VoxelPose baseline is 10.61 percentage points (83.59→94.2), and over Faster VoxelPose is 9.0 percentage points (85.22→94.22). The space attention module adds only 1.32% MACs and 0.74% parameters to VoxelPose (181.24G vs. 178.88G MACs), making it a lightweight addition. On the Shelf and Campus datasets, the method remains competitive with PCP averages of 97.5% and 96.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 3DSA module decisively outperforms all compared methods on the strictest metric AP25: it surpasses VoxelPose by 10.61 points (94.2 vs. 83.59), Faster VoxelPose by 9.0 points (94.22 vs. 85.22), TEMPO by 5.2 points (94.2 vs. 89.01), MvP by 1.9 points (94.2 vs. 92.28), and PlaneSweep Pose by 2.1 points (94.2 vs. 92.12). On MPJPE, VoxelPose+3DSA (13.98 mm) beats TEMPO (14.68 mm), MvP (15.76 mm), RPGN (15.84 mm), and PlaneSweep Pose (16.75 mm), while Faster VoxelPose+3DSA (14.55 mm) also edges out TEMPO. The only trade-off is a marginal 0.5% drop in AP50/AP100/AP150 relative to the original VoxelPose, which the authors attribute to model variation rather than the attention module. On Shelf and Campus, the method ties or slightly trails TEMPO and MvP (e.g., 97.5% vs. 97.4% PCP on Shelf), with the authors noting that the Heatmap-based input mode limits performance on these datasets due to incomplete annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the voxel-based multi-view 3D human pose estimation line (VoxelPose, Faster VoxelPose) by introducing a 3D space attention module that modulates the feature volume based on per-viewpoint visibility. Conceptually, it draws on channel/spatial attention mechanisms (SENet, CBAM, ContextPose) but repurposes them for a 3D volumetric setting, using a space subdivision algorithm inspired by cell-subdivision search to keep the attention computation tractable. It departs from prior voxel-based methods, which uniformly fuse heatmaps from all views, by explicitly weighting regions according to how well each viewpoint observes them, and from Transformer-based direct regression (MvP) by retaining the efficient 3D-CNN/2D-CNN pipeline while adding a lightweight attention branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The module addresses a physically grounded limitation—varying visibility across viewpoints—that prior voxel-based methods ignore, and the ~12% AP25 gain on Panoptic is meaningful for the most stringent evaluation threshold. However, the AP50/100/150 metrics slightly decrease, suggesting the attention primarily rescues hard (occluded) cases at a small cost to easy ones, and the large performance drop when switching from image-based to heatmap-based input (94.2 → 86.97 AP25) reveals a dependence on rich visual context that limits applicability to settings where only heatmaps are available. Open questions include whether the subdivision granularity can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach transfers to body-mesh or tracking tasks, and whether the slight AP50 regression can be eliminated.
