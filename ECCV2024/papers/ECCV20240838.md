## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models are capped at 1024px resolution due to the quadratic computational cost of self-attention over increasingly long token sequences, and training a competitive T2I model from scratch requires thousands of GPU days. The paper asks how to efficiently evolve an existing, relatively weaker DiT-based model into a stronger one capable of direct 4K generation, without prohibitive retraining costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240838/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt (up to ~300 words, encoded by Flan-T5-XXL) and directly produces a high-resolution RGB image up to 4K (3840×2160) without any post-processing or super-resolution cascade. It supports diverse aspect ratios and is encoded/decoded through SDXL's frozen VAE in latent space. The entire pipeline runs on a 0.6B-parameter DiT, substantially smaller than SDXL (2.6B) or Stable Cascade (5.1B).

## Method

PixArt-Σ introduces a KV token compression module into the DiT architecture: in the deep layers (14–27), keys and values are spatially merged within R×R windows via a 2×2 group convolution with a specialized average-pooling initialization ('Conv Avg Init'), reducing attention complexity from O(N²) to O(N²/R²) while retaining all query tokens to preserve semantic fidelity. This adds only 0.018% parameters yet cuts training and inference time by ~34% at 4K. The training follows a 'weak-to-strong' strategy: starting from PixArt-α's 256px checkpoint, the model is rapidly adapted to SDXL's VAE (converging in ~2K steps), scaled to higher resolutions via position-embedding interpolation (visually plausible in ~100 iterations), and then equipped with KV compression (satisfactory results in ~100 steps). Training data is upgraded to 33M high-resolution images (>1K) plus 2.3M 4K images, captioned by Share-Captioner to an average of 180 words, with a 60/40 mix of long and raw captions to balance detail and diversity.

## Results

PixArt-Σ achieves a FID of 5.512 and CLIP-Score of 0.273 at 1024px resolution with 2× KV compression, while reducing training latency by 18% (27.5s → 22.5s per iteration). At 4K resolution, KV compression (ratio 4) reduces training time from 191s to 125s per iteration (−35%) and inference time from 91s to 60s per image (−34%). The model uses only 0.6B parameters and requires just 9% of the GPU days needed by PixArt-α for fine-tuning, yet achieves competitive or superior quality in both human and GPT-4V preference studies against six open-source baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the human and GPT-4V preference study (300 prompts from the curated 30K evaluation set), PixArt-Σ is consistently preferred over all six baselines: PixArt-α, SD1.5, Stable Turbo, SDXL, Stable Cascade, and Playground-V2.0. Against its direct predecessor PixArt-α, PixArt-Σ improves portrait realism, semantic analysis capability, and extends generation from 1K to 4K resolution. Compared to SDXL (2.6B params), PixArt-Σ (0.6B params) demonstrates superior instruction-following while using roughly 4× fewer parameters. Against Stable Cascade (5.1B params), PixArt-Σ achieves competitive or superior image quality and prompt adherence with ~8× fewer parameters, and avoids the cumulative errors inherent in cascade pipelines. The paper notes that PixArt-Σ's outputs are visually competitive with closed-source products (DALL-E 3, Midjourney 6, Firefly 2, Imagen 2) in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4), though no quantitative FID/CLIP numbers are provided for these proprietary models.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixArt-Σ directly extends PixArt-α (ICLR 2024), a DiT-based T2I model that first demonstrated efficient 1K image generation with a Transformer backbone. The KV token compression mechanism is inspired by PVT v2's approach of condensing key/value spaces via convolutional kernels, adapted here to the diffusion Transformer setting. The paper departs from cascade-based high-resolution pipelines (Stable Cascade, Imagen's super-resolution stage) by generating 4K images in a single forward pass, and from prior DiT work (GenTron, Sora, SD3) by focusing on the specific challenge of ultra-high-resolution token efficiency rather than architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'weak-to-strong training' paradigm—upgrading a model via better data, a stronger VAE, and architectural modifications using only ~9% of the original training compute—offers a broadly applicable recipe for iterating on diffusion models without prohibitive retraining costs. The KV compression technique (0.018% extra parameters, ~34% speedup) is a lightweight, drop-in module likely to be adopted in future high-resolution DiT systems. Open questions include whether the quality gap at higher compression ratios (R=9) becomes problematic at even higher resolutions, whether the weak-to-strong strategy generalizes to video generation or other modalities, and whether the 0.6B parameter efficiency advantage persists as model scale increases.
