## Problem

Existing deblur neural rendering methods based on implicit NeRF representations cannot achieve real-time rendering and struggle to recover intricate scene details from severely motion-blurred images. Meanwhile, 3D Gaussian Splatting, which enables real-time rendering, heavily relies on sharp images and accurate COLMAP poses—both of which are violated by motion blur, causing significant performance degradation. No prior method combines explicit Gaussian representation with joint camera trajectory recovery to handle motion-blurred multi-view inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240658/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of motion-blurred RGB images, their inaccurate camera poses estimated by COLMAP, and a sparse point cloud (also from COLMAP) as initialization. It produces a sharp 3D Gaussian scene representation (parameterized by mean, covariance, opacity, and color per Gaussian), recovered per-frame camera trajectories (T_start and T_end in SE(3)), and enables real-time novel view synthesis at over 200 FPS. Camera poses are parameterized as 6D Lie algebra vectors and optimized via pypose on the SE(3) manifold within the Nerfstudio/Splatfacto framework.

## Method

BAD-Gaussians is the first motion deblur framework built on 3D Gaussian Splatting, modeling the physical image formation process where a blurred image equals the average of n virtual sharp images rendered at interpolated camera poses during exposure. The camera trajectory within each frame's exposure is represented by linear interpolation (or cubic B-spline for real data) between start and end poses in SE(3) space, with n=10 virtual poses sampled uniformly. During training, both the Gaussian parameters and the per-frame camera trajectories are jointly optimized by minimizing a combined L1 + D-SSIM photometric loss between the synthesized blurred image (average of rendered virtual sharp images via differentiable Gaussian rasterization) and the ground-truth blurred image. Gradients flow from the loss through the rasterization to both the Gaussians and the camera poses, enabling a photometric bundle adjustment formulation that simultaneously refines scene geometry and corrects inaccurate COLMAP poses.

## Results

On the synthetic Deblur-NeRF dataset (5 scenes), BAD-Gaussians achieves an average PSNR improvement of 3.6 dB over the second-best method (BAD-NeRF) for deblurring and 1.7 dB for novel view synthesis. For example, on the Tanabata scene (severe blur), it reaches 32.12 dB PSNR vs. 25.80 dB for BAD-NeRF and 23.20 dB for Deblur-NeRF. The method also achieves >200 FPS rendering speed versus <1 FPS for all NeRF-based baselines, with training time of ~30 minutes compared to >10 hours for prior methods. On the real Deblur-NeRF dataset, it attains 26.11 dB PSNR and 0.1064 LPIPS, outperforming the best NeRF-based method (DP-NeRF at 25.91 dB / 0.1589 LPIPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAD-Gaussians outperforms all 9 baselines on average across both deblurring and novel view synthesis tasks. Against the closest competitor BAD-NeRF [47], it gains 3.6 dB average PSNR on deblurring (e.g., 32.12 vs. 25.80 on Tanabata) and 1.7 dB on novel view synthesis, though it loses on the Factory scene (28.18 vs. 31.71 dB) due to Gaussian splatting's weaker sky representation. Single-image deblurring methods (MPR, SRN) trail by 4–10 dB because they cannot exploit multi-view geometry. Deblur-NeRF and DP-NeRF, which fix COLMAP-estimated poses, are outperformed by 2–6 dB; providing them ground-truth poses (marked *) narrows the gap but does not close it. On pose estimation (Table 7), BAD-Gaussians achieves 0.584 cm average ATE, improving over COLMAP-blur (0.916 cm) and BAD-NeRF (0.842 cm), though still far from COLMAP-sharp (0.160 cm). In terms of efficiency, the method renders at >200 FPS and trains in ~30 minutes, versus <1 FPS and >10 hours for all NeRF-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper ports the physical motion-blur image formation model and joint camera-trajectory optimization introduced by BAD-NeRF into the explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting framework, replacing the implicit MLP radiance field with differentiable Gaussian rasterization. Prior deblur-NeRF methods (Deblur-NeRF, DP-NeRF, BAD-NeRF, ExBluRF) all rely on implicit representations that preclude real-time rendering and struggle with intricate detail recovery; BAD-Gaussians is the first to formulate motion deblurring as a bundle-adjustment problem over explicit Gaussians. The camera trajectory is parameterized via SE(3) interpolation (linear or cubic B-spline) between start and end poses, a formulation inherited from BAD-NeRF but now differentiated through the Gaussian rasterization pipeline rather than volume rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: BAD-Gaussians achieves over 200 FPS rendering and ~30-minute training versus sub-1 FPS and >10 hours for all NeRF-based deblurring baselines, making motion-deblur 3D reconstruction viable in real-time applications like AR/VR and robotics. A noted limitation is that Gaussian splatting represents sky regions less faithfully than NeRF (visible on the Factory sequence), suggesting hybrid representations may be needed for outdoor scenes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, more complex blur models (e.g., rolling shutter), and whether the explicit representation's advantage persists at higher resolutions or with fewer input views.
