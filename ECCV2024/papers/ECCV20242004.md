## Problem

Category-level 6D object pose estimation lacks a large-scale, diverse benchmark: existing datasets cover at most 10–44 categories with limited instance variety, hindering comprehensive model evaluation and cross-domain generalization. Additionally, prior generative methods like GenPose ignore RGB semantic cues and fail to aggregate multimodal pose distributions arising from discrete object symmetries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242004/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a partially observed 3D point cloud (N points from a depth sensor) and a cropped RGB image (H×W) of the target object, with ground-truth instance segmentation assumed known. It outputs a 6D pose as an SE(3) transformation (rotation + translation) plus a predicted 3D bounding-box scale. For the tracking variant, a perturbed ground-truth initial pose is additionally provided to seed the per-frame estimation.

## Method

GenPose++ extends the diffusion-based GenPose framework with two targeted improvements. First, semantic-aware feature extraction encodes the RGB crop with a frozen DINOv2 backbone and the point cloud with PointNet++, then fuses the two feature maps pointwise to condition both the score and energy networks, enabling the model to exploit category-level visual semantics at scale. Second, clustering-based aggregation replaces GenPose's naive mean-pooling of pose candidates with DBSCAN (ε≈0.45 rad, MinPts=5) over the top-40% energy-ranked samples, selecting the mean of the largest cluster to avoid the 'mean-mode' failure on objects with discrete symmetries. Training uses a standard denoising score-matching objective on the SOPE synthetic set (475K images generated via mixed-reality RGB compositing and physics-based structured-light depth simulation in Blender), while evaluation is performed on the real ROPE set (332K images, 581 instances, 149 categories). A lightweight regression head, conditioned on the estimated pose, predicts the object's 3D scale to improve bounding-box IoU metrics.

## Results

On the ROPE dataset (149 categories, 581 instances), GenPose++ achieves AUC@IoU25 of 39.0, AUC@IoU50 of 19.1, and VUS@10°5cm of 29.4, substantially outperforming the best deterministic baseline HS-Pose (AUC@IoU25=31.6, VUS@10°5cm=12.7) and the prior probabilistic method GenPose (VUS@10°5cm=19.3). For pose tracking, GenPose++ reaches 17.8 FPS with VUS@5°5cm of 15.9, mIoU of 53.4, Rerr of 17.6°, and Terr of 1.2 cm, matching CATRE on VUS@5°5cm while achieving lower rotation and translation errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenPose++ dominates all baselines on the ROPE pose estimation benchmark. Against the strongest deterministic method HS-Pose (depth-only input), GenPose++ improves AUC@IoU25 from 31.6 to 39.0 and VUS@10°5cm from 12.7 to 29.4, a more than 2× gain on the loosest VUS metric. Compared to the closest probabilistic baseline GenPose (depth-only), GenPose++ lifts VUS@10°5cm from 19.3 to 29.4 (+52%) and VUS@5°2cm from 6.6 to 10.0 (+52%), attributable to RGB semantic features via DINOv2 and DBSCAN-based clustering. NOCS and SGPA are effectively non-functional at this scale (NOCS scores 0.0 across all metrics; SGPA reaches only AUC@IoU25=10.5), confirming that prior category-level methods do not generalize to 149 diverse categories. For pose tracking (Table 4), GenPose++ ties CATRE on VUS@5°5cm (15.9) but achieves lower Rerr (17.6° vs 21.3°) and Terr (1.2 cm vs 2.6 cm), while running at 17.8 FPS versus CATRE's 38.5 FPS. BundleTrack, a training-free method, is far behind on all tracking metrics (VUS@5°5cm=1.3, Rerr=46.9°), struggling with weak textures and depth noise on transparent/specular objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GenPose (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2024), a diffusion-based generative framework for category-level 6D pose estimation, extending it with two targeted improvements: semantic-aware feature extraction via a pre-trained 2D foundation model (DINOv2) and DBSCAN-based clustering to resolve the mean-mode failure on objects with discrete symmetries. On the data side, it departs from the NOCS paradigm (6 categories, small real evaluation set) and concurrent work like PACE (44 categories) by scaling to 149 categories with a mixed-reality + physics-based depth simulation pipeline that explicitly addresses both RGB and geometric sim2real gaps. The annotation pipeline replaces marker-based or robot-arm-based camera pose recovery with a marker-free SfM + bundle adjustment + FPS keyframe annotation system, enabling open-scene capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omni6DPose addresses a well-identified bottleneck in category-level pose estimation: the absence of a large-scale, multi-category, multi-material benchmark. With 149 categories, 581 real instances, and 475K synthetic training images, it is likely to become the default evaluation standard, superseding NOCS for category-level work. The finding that NOCS scores 0.0 across all metrics on ROPE underscores how fragile RGB-only canonical-space prediction is at scale, reinforcing the need for geometric (depth/point cloud) inputs. Key open questions include the slow inference speed of the diffusion-based approach (17.8 FPS for tracking), whether the probabilistic framework remains advantageous as category count grows further, and how to integrate the trained pose module into downstream robotic manipulation and rearrangement pipelines.
