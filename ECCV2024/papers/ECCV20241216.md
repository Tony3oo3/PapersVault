## Problem

DIP-based blind image deconvolution methods (e.g., SelfDeblur) suffer from high sensitivity to random blur kernel initialization due to the nonconvex optimization landscape, leading to unstable and inconsistent results—especially for large motion blur kernels (up to 75×75). These methods also fail to explicitly model the statistical structure of blur kernels, limiting kernel estimation accuracy. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a learned generative prior for kernels and a data-driven initializer that replaces random sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241216/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry RGB image produced by spatially invariant motion blur as input and jointly outputs a deblurred clean image and the estimated 2D blur kernel. During pre-training, the kernel initializer is trained on 256×256 patches cropped from OpenImages convolved with synthesized motion kernels; during the BID stage, full images (e.g., 256×256 or larger) are processed. The framework is specifically designed for uniform, spatially invariant motion blur and does not handle non-uniform or spatially varying degradation.

## Method

The framework has two stages. In pre-training, a DCGAN-based kernel generator is trained on synthesized motion blur kernels to serve as a deep generative prior (DGP) in a compact latent space, and a ResNet-18 encoder (kernel initializer) is then trained via collaborative learning—alternating GAN-inversion steps to find each kernel's latent code with encoder updates—to map blurry images to those latent codes. In the BID stage, the initializer provides a data-dependent starting point for the kernel, and the method jointly optimizes the first-layer feature map w_k of the frozen generator (a StyleGAN-inspired trick that increases the optimization dimensionality) alongside the DIP network parameters for the clean image, minimizing the reconstruction residual. This avoids the overly constrained optimization of the raw latent code z_k (which degrades sharply for large kernels) while also avoiding the too-large search space of fine-tuning the entire generator.

## Results

On the challenging Lai et al. benchmark (100 blurry images with large motion blur kernels of 31×31 to 75×75), the proposed method achieves an average PSNR of 26.71 and SSIM of 0.914, outperforming the best DIP-based competitor Huo et al. (25.19/0.860) by 1.52 dB in PSNR and 0.054 in SSIM. On the authors' synthetic MSCOCO-based dataset, the method reaches 29.67 PSNR and 0.928 SSIM, surpassing the next-best method (Huo et al., 28.13/0.898). Notably, the proposed method is the only one to achieve an average SSIM above 0.9 on the Lai benchmark, and it converges approximately 2.5× faster than SelfDeblur (matching SelfDeblur's 1000-iteration performance by iteration 400).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines across every category on the Lai et al. benchmark. Among DIP-based methods, it beats the strongest competitor Huo et al. [22] by 1.52 dB PSNR (26.71 vs. 25.19) and 0.054 SSIM (0.914 vs. 0.860) on average, with the gap widening on the Manmade category (24.67 vs. 22.51 PSNR). Against the best supervised deep learning method, Kaufman and Fattal [24] (20.72/0.723), the improvement is 5.99 dB in PSNR, and against the best traditional method, Xu et al. [62] (19.23/0.659), it is 7.48 dB. The original SelfDeblur [52] (21.91/0.715) is surpassed by 4.80 dB, and Li et al. [35] (22.25/0.744) by 4.46 dB. On the synthetic MSCOCO dataset, the method achieves 29.67/0.928, exceeding Huo et al. (28.13/0.898) and Ren et al. (25.48/0.765), confirming consistent gains across both evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DIP-based blind deconvolution framework of SelfDeblur (Ren et al., CVPR 2020) by replacing its random kernel initialization with a pre-trained GAN-based kernel generator (deep generative prior) and a learned kernel initializer trained via GAN-inversion. The initializer training borrows the collaborative alternating-optimization strategy from Guan et al. (2020), and the BID optimization adapts StyleGAN's (Karras et al., CVPR 2019) latent-to-style mapping trick to optimize a higher-dimensional first-layer feature map rather than the raw latent code. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior DIP-based BID work by explicitly modeling the statistical distribution of blur kernels in a compact latent space, rather than treating the kernel as an unconstrained DNN output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves a clear SOTA on the Lai benchmark (26.71 PSNR / 0.914 SSIM average), with the largest gains on large kernels (75×75), where prior DIP-based methods degrade significantly due to non-convex optimization. The ablation showing that optimizing the higher-dimensional feature map w_k is critical for large kernels (26.73 vs. 22.43 PSNR) provides a practical design principle for latent-space optimization in restoration tasks. However, the framework is limited to spatially invariant motion blur and requires pre-training a GAN on synthetic kernels, raising open questions about generalization to non-uniform or camera-induced blur and to kernel types outside the training distribution.
