## Problem

Existing self-supervised skeleton-based action recognition methods fall into two separate paradigms—generative (e.g., MAE) and contrastive learning—each with complementary but unexploited strengths. Generative models preserve redundant appearance information that conflicts with skeleton data's spatially sparse and temporally consistent nature, while contrastive methods sacrifice fine-grained motion detail through aggressive data augmentation. The paper identifies that these paradigms are theoretically linked (generative models are equivalent to maximum entropy coding) but had never been unified into a single framework for skeleton recognition.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D skeleton sequences of 25 joints downsampled to 120 frames, with data augmentations (shearing, cropping, rotation, masking, flipping) applied before encoding. The encoder (a 256-dim Transformer) outputs per-token feature representations used for downstream linear or KNN classification, while the diffusion-based generator reconstructs or denoises the full skeleton. A key design detail is that the adapter extracts high-frequency components from encoder features via softmax-based de-correlation and injects them into the generator through AdaLN, so the generator receives both the noisy diffusion input and the semantic condition simultaneously.

## Method

The Idempotent Generative Model (IGM) unifies generative pre-training and contrastive learning by proving that a self-conditional generative model with an idempotency constraint (f(ĥx) = f(x)) is mathematically equivalent to spectral contrastive learning, with the added benefit of optimizing higher-order residual terms. The generator is a diffusion model (DDPM-style noise prediction) that provides diversity through stochastic noise sampling, while the encoder is a standard Transformer. Two idempotency losses enforce consistency: a feature-level loss aligning f(x) with f(x̂₀) of the one-step denoised output, and a distribution-level loss aligning the inter-feature similarity matrix of generated data with that of the original data. A Manifold Decoupled Feature Fusion adapter extracts high-frequency semantic information from encoder features by subtracting softmax-weighted neighbors (analogous to a uniformity-loss gradient step), then injects this into the generator via AdaLN to mitigate dimensional collapse and expand the effective feature dimension for recognition.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D 60, IGM achieves 91.2% (xview) and 86.2% (xsub) linear evaluation accuracy, outperforming the strongest generative baseline MAMP (89.1%/84.9%) and the strongest contrastive baseline 3s-ActCLR (88.8%/84.3%). On NTU RGB+D 120, IGM reaches 81.4% (xset) and 80.0% (xsub), surpassing MAMP (79.1%/78.6%) by 2.3 and 1.4 points respectively. In zero-shot domain generalization under joint noise corruption (p=1.0, σ²=0.1), IGM achieves 58.7% accuracy versus 12.7% for ActCLR and 2.4% for MAMP, demonstrating a dramatic advantage in robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGM outperforms all 11 baselines in the main Table 1 comparison across every protocol. The closest competitor is MAMP [28] (generative, Transformer-based), which IGM beats by 2.1 points on NTU60 xview, 1.3 on NTU60 xsub, 2.3 on NTU120 xset, and 1.4 on NTU120 xsub. Among contrastive-only methods, 3s-CMD [29] is the strongest (90.9% NTU60 xview), still trailing IGM by 0.3 points; 3s-ActCLR [21] and 3s-AimCLR [11] lag further behind by 2.4 and 7.8 points respectively on NTU60 xview. The hybrid PCM3 [65] (90.4% xview) is the nearest non-generative competitor, losing to IGM by 0.8 points. In transfer learning to PKUMMD (Table 3), IGM achieves 59.8% xsub linear accuracy, surpassing the best prior method Hi-TRS [3] (55.0%) by 4.8 points. In zero-shot corruption robustness (Table 4), IGM dominates by a wide margin: 58.7% vs. 12.7% (ActCLR) and 2.4% (MAMP) under joint noise (p=1.0, σ²=0.1), and 56.9% vs. 30.3% (ActCLR) under right-arm occlusion. For reconstruction quality (Table 5), IGM achieves 79.2 mm MPJPE and 1.18 FID, beating DDPM [15] (130.2/1.78) and MDM [45] (87.6/1.26) while SkeletonMAE [58] performs worst (329.7/2.69).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms in skeleton-based self-supervised learning—generative (MAE-style reconstruction) and contrastive (spectral contrastive) learning—by proving their equivalence through maximum entropy coding and then introducing an idempotency constraint that bridges them. It extends the idempotent loss from image compression (Xu et al., 2024) to skeleton sequences and embeds it within a diffusion-based generation framework (Ho et al., 2020), departing from prior work that treated generative and contrastive objectives as competing or complementary but never formally equivalent. The manifold decoupled feature fusion adapter draws on the uniformity loss from contrastive learning to perform high-pass filtering, addressing a dimensional collapse problem specific to generative pre-training that prior skeleton methods (MAMP, SkeletonMAE) did not explicitly tackle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical demonstration that self-conditional generative models are equivalent to spectral contrastive learning (plus a residual term) provides a principled justification for why idempotency constraints improve recognition, moving beyond heuristic combinations of losses. The zero-shot domain generalization results under joint noise and occlusion corruption (58–65% vs. 2–33% for baselines) suggest that diffusion-based denoising at test time is a practical and powerful tool for robust skeleton recognition in real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the diffusion backbone is strictly necessary versus simpler generative models, whether the theoretical equivalence scales to larger and more diverse datasets beyond NTU/PKUMMD, and whether the idempotency constraint generalizes to other modalities (e.g., point clouds, audio) where spatial sparsity and temporal consistency also hold.
