## Problem

Monocular object-centric NeRF methods suffer from scale-depth ambiguity, causing gradient-based pose optimization to fail when the initial pose deviates even moderately from ground truth. Existing work circumvents this by relying on external monocular 3D detectors for initial poses, which introduces pipeline complexity and poor cross-dataset generalization. The paper asks whether pose estimation and NeRF reconstruction can be unified in a single streamlined network that is robust to random initial poses without any external 3D detector.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image and a binary occlusion mask, both padded to square and resized to 128×128 pixels before encoding. At inference, the network starts from a fully random 3D pose sampled within the viewing frustum (random 2D location, fixed depth Z=20, random yaw plus small perturbation) and iteratively refines it over 3 feed-forward iterations followed by 50 gradient-based joint optimization steps. Outputs include the refined 6DoF object pose, per-object shape and texture codes, and rendered 32×32 RGB and occupancy maps used for loss computation.

## Method

SUP-NeRF decouples object dimension prediction from pose refinement to eliminate scale-depth ambiguity: a ResNet-50 encoder outputs fixed object dimensions [H_B, W_B, L_B] alongside shape, texture, and pose codes, and the pose module then iteratively updates only rotation and translation while keeping size constant. The key representation is a camera-invariant projected-box encoding: the 8 corners of the 3D bounding box are projected onto the image plane and interpolated into a 2D visual representation, making the deep pose refiner independent of camera intrinsics and enabling cross-domain generalization. Translation updates are expressed in a relative space (image-space Δx, Δy plus a depth ratio ρ), and rotation updates use axis-angle composition on SO(3). A cross-task shortcut subtracts pose features from shape/texture features after the first four CNN layers, resolving conflicting learning signals during joint training. The NeRF decoder (CodeNeRF) performs volumetric rendering with 64 samples per ray in a shape space normalized by the 3D box diagonal, and the full system is trained end-to-end for 40 epochs on nuScenes with photometric, occupancy, and iterative pose losses.

## Results

On nuScenes, SUP-NeRF achieves 18.8 dB PSNR and 0.60 m depth error after 50 iterations of NeRF optimization, outperforming AutoRF+FCOS3D (17.2 dB, 0.81 m) and reducing rotation error from 9.77° to 7.01° and translation error from 0.85 m to 0.68 m at the feed-forward stage. In cross-dataset evaluation on KITTI, SUP-NeRF reduces rotation error by ~50% (6.79° vs. 11.95°) and translation error by ~50% (1.06 m vs. 2.2 m) compared to AutoRF+FCOS3D. On Waymo, SUP-NeRF achieves the best translation error (1.54 m vs. 3.31 m for AutoRF+FCOS3D) and depth error (1.56 m vs. 2.32 m), though FCOS3D retains a slight edge in rotation error (6.65° vs. 10.01°). SUP-NeRF also requires only 49.8 M parameters and 0.018 s feed-forward time, roughly 6–8× faster than AutoRF+FCOS3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AutoRF (no external pose), SUP-NeRF is dramatically superior: AutoRF starting from a random pose yields 87.5° rotation error and 11.2 m depth error, while SUP-NeRF achieves 7.0° and 0.69 m, confirming that NeRF's gradient-based pose refinement alone cannot recover from moderate initial pose errors due to scale-depth ambiguity. Against AutoRF+FCOS3D (which uses an external monocular 3D detector for initial pose), SUP-NeRF wins on every nuScenes metric—PSNR (18.8 vs. 17.2 dB), depth error (0.60 vs. 0.81 m), rotation error (7.07° vs. 10.17°), and translation error (0.73 vs. 0.78 m at 50 it)—while eliminating the need for a separate 3D detector. In cross-dataset KITTI evaluation, the gap widens substantially: SUP-NeRF cuts rotation error by ~50% (6.79° vs. 11.95°) and translation error by ~50% (1.06 m vs. 2.2 m) over AutoRF+FCOS3D, attributed to the camera-invariant projected-box representation. Against BootInv, SUP-NeRF achieves comparable or better pose accuracy (7.01° vs. 28.40° RE on nuScenes) and substantially lower depth error (0.60 m vs. 3.56 m at 50 it), while also being 6–8× faster in feed-forward inference. The one exception is Waymo rotation error, where FCOS3D (6.65°) slightly outperforms SUP-NeRF (10.01°), likely due to FCOS3D leveraging larger image context for heavily occluded objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

SUP-NeRF extends the object-centric NeRF line of work (CodeNeRF, AutoRF) by integrating a dedicated iterative pose estimation module directly into the NeRF pipeline, eliminating the dependency on external monocular 3D detectors (e.g., FCOS3D) that prior methods like AutoRF and BootInv require for initial pose. The key conceptual departure is decoupling object dimension estimation from pose refinement to resolve the scale-depth ambiguity that causes NeRF optimization to fail, combined with a projected-box representation that makes the pose refiner invariant to camera intrinsics. Unlike BootInv, which unifies pose, shape, and appearance but requires a separate NOCS dense-field generator and is sensitive to occlusion, SUP-NeRF achieves unification with only a few additional MLP layers shared with the NeRF image encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the external 3D detector from the pipeline, SUP-NeRF simplifies the monocular reconstruction workflow and, more importantly, demonstrates substantially better cross-dataset generalization (up to 50% reduction in rotation and translation error on KITTI), suggesting that camera-invariant pose representations are critical for deploying object NeRFs across domains. The identification of scale-depth ambiguity as the root cause of NeRF pose-optimization failure, and the finding that gradient-based refinement can actually worsen pose when initial error is already small, provides useful diagnostic insight for the field. However, cross-dataset PSNR and depth error still degrade noticeably, and the 50-iteration NeRF optimization remains slow, leaving open questions about real-time deployment and generalization beyond driving scenes.
