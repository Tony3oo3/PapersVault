## Problem

Existing 3D shape diffusion models operate on implicit volumetric representations (e.g., SDFs) that discard point-to-point correspondences, making them unsuitable for generating articulated objects in diverse poses. Additionally, no prior diffusion-based framework simultaneously handles multiple object categories (humans, animals, man-made objects) and supports a range of downstream tasks such as morphing, shape variation, and keypoint fitting within a single model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242195/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion transformer consumes noisy 3D vertex coordinates (N×3), body joint coordinates (J×3), and optionally surface normals (N×3, concatenated to form N×6 for man-made objects) along with their respective timestep tokens. For articulated objects with fixed topology, the output is a dense mesh with M vertices produced by MLP-based upsampling; for man-made objects, the output is a 5×-upsampled point cloud with normals converted to a mesh via Shape-As-Points. Downstream tasks additionally condition on 3D joint locations, 2D keypoints, control points, or class labels, with the diffusion process using T=1000 steps and DDIM sampling (1–250 steps).

## Method

DiffSurf is a UniDiffuser-style multi-modal diffusion transformer (7 layers, 256-dim hidden) that treats surface vertices and body joints as two distinct modalities, each with its own timestep embedding, and predicts noise for both simultaneously. The key architectural choices are: (1) incorporating body joints as a second modality to facilitate training and provide landmark-based pose/shape control, and (2) augmenting vertex tokens with surface normals for man-made objects to capture geometry more effectively than point sets alone. Training uses v-prediction parameterization with a sigmoid variance schedule and Adam optimizer. For articulated objects, fixed mesh connectivity preserves correspondences and an MLP up-sampler produces the dense mesh; for man-made objects, a PointNet++-based up-sampler (×5) followed by SAP converts points to a mesh. Downstream tasks (pose-conditioned generation, morphing via SLERP in noise space, shape refinement, control-point deformation, 2D keypoint fitting) are solved by leveraging the pre-trained model through classifier-free guidance and score distillation sampling without retraining.

## Results

On 3DPW (no fine-tuning), DiffSurf achieves 108.0 MPVE and 53.7 PA-MPJPE, outperforming all vertex-based baselines except PARE (97.9 MPVE, 50.9 PA-MPJPE) and matching or exceeding parametric methods like HMR-EFT (54.3 PA-MPJPE). On Human3.6M, DiffSurf reaches 48.9 MPJPE and 36.1 PA-MPJPE, competitive with the diffusion-based HMDiff (49.3 MPJPE, 32.4 PA-MPJPE). For unconditional human generation, DiffSurf achieves 54.4 RA-1-NNA on SURREAL and 69.6 on DFAUST, substantially outperforming VPoser (60.7/70.2) and LIMP (81.3/93.3). In the SDS-based 2D keypoint fitting task, DiffSurf+SDS fitting reduces PA-MPJPE to 48.7, improving over HMR-EFT+EFT fitting (53.7) by 5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3DPW, DiffSurf (53.7 PA-MPJPE) outperforms all parametric baselines including HMR-EFT (54.3), DiffHMR (56.5), OCHHuman (58.3), SPIN (59.2), and ProHMR (59.8), and beats vertex-based METRO (63.0), Pose2Mesh (58.3), and GATOR (56.8), but trails PARE (50.9 PA-MPJPE, 97.9 MPVE) which benefits from part-attention regression. On Human3.6M, DiffSurf (48.9 MPJPE, 36.1 PA-MPJPE) surpasses METRO (54.0/36.7), GATOR (64.0/44.7), and Pose2Mesh (64.9/46.3), and is competitive with HMDiff (49.3/32.4) despite not being trained end-to-end on image-mesh pairs. For unconditional generation (Table 1), DiffSurf (54.4 RA-1-NNA on SURREAL) substantially outperforms VPoser (60.7), Parametric Diffusion (59.6), GDVAE (93.8), and LIMP (81.3), demonstrating superior diversity and quality. In the SDS-based 2D keypoint fitting (Table 3), DiffSurf+SDS fitting (48.7 PA-MPJPE) improves over HMR-EFT+EFT fitting (53.7), ProHMR+fitting (55.1), LearnedGD (55.9), and SMPLify (106.1), with PA-MPJPE dropping by ~9 points when applied to METRO. The paper notes that DiffSurf's performance is affected by random input mesh noise since it does not explicitly condition on images, and that multiple-hypothesis sampling could further improve results.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffSurf adapts the UniDiffuser multi-modal diffusion transformer framework (Bao et al., 2023) and the U-ViT backbone (Bao et al., CVPR 2023) to the 3D domain, treating surface vertices and body joints as two distinct modalities processed by a shared transformer. It departs from the dominant implicit-function-based 3D diffusion approaches (SDFusion, MeshDiffusion, DiT-3D) by operating directly on explicit point-and-normal representations, and it borrows the vertex-based mesh recovery paradigm from METRO and MeshGraphormer to enforce point-to-point correspondences across poses. For man-made objects, it incorporates surface normals into the diffusion process and leverages SLIDE's sparse latent points and PointNet++-based upsampling, while SDS from DreamFusion is repurposed for shape refinement and control-point deformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiffSurf is the first diffusion model to unconditionally generate 3D surfaces in diverse body poses with preserved correspondences, filling a gap where prior 3D diffusion methods either ignored pose or were strictly image-conditional. Its versatility across generation, morphing, fitting, and shape variation tasks, combined with near real-time inference (~20 FPS at 10 DDIM steps), makes it a practical tool for downstream 3D content pipelines. Key limitations include the requirement of fixed mesh connectivity for articulated objects (limiting topological diversity for humans/animals) and the absence of fine-grained details like facial expressions and finger poses. The authors identify scaling to larger 3D datasets and expressive body generation as natural next steps toward a foundational 3D generative model.
