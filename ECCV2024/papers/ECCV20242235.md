## Problem

Existing LiDAR-based 3D object detection is limited to closed-set categories, and directly transferring 2D open-vocabulary models (e.g., CLIP-based co-embedding) to LiDAR suffers from severe overfitting: the model persists in detecting known classes even when queried with novel categories. This paper identifies that the standard paradigm of aligning known-class features with vision-language embeddings fails to generalize in the 3D LiDAR domain, necessitating a tailored approach that explicitly handles potentially novel objects without relying on predefined known classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 32-beam LiDAR point cloud, six synchronized RGB camera images, and a free-form text query specifying the category of interest. It produces 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, heading) with assigned semantic labels. Notably, the 2D detection stage uses Grounding DINO to obtain object-level 2D boxes and features, while GPT-4 is queried offline for size priors (width, length, height ranges) per class to guide box filtering and post-hoc label recalibration.

## Method

OpenSight operates in two stages. First, generic object perception: 2D boxes from camera images are back-projected into LiDAR space to form 3D frustums, within which region-growing clustering estimates initial 3D boxes; temporal awareness projects boxes from adjacent frames to recover missed or inaccurate detections, while spatial awareness uses GPT-4-derived size priors to select 'precisely' estimated boxes and re-place them at varying distances to improve visibility of distant objects. A DETR-like model with bipartite matching is trained on these pseudo-boxes to learn generic objectness and localization. Second, specific semantic interpretation: predicted 3D boxes are projected onto image planes and matched to the nearest Grounding DINO 2D boxes; a contrastive alignment loss minimizes the feature gap between 3D LiDAR object features and matched 2D image features, after which fused 3D-2D features are scored against text embeddings to decode the final class label, with LLM size priors used as a post-processing recalibration step.

## Results

On nuScenes with all 10 classes treated as novel, OpenSight achieves 22.9 mAP and 23.3 NDS, more than doubling the best prior open-vocabulary baseline UP-VL (11.3 mAP, 17.2 NDS). Per-class highlights include 52.5 AP on Pedestrian, 42.2 AP on Barrier, 30.4 AP on Motorcycle, and 25.3 AP on Car. The method also shows strong distance-robustness (28.0 / 26.5 / 22.9 mAP for near/midrange/far) and excels on medium and small objects (32.9 / 39.0 mAP), though large-object detection remains weaker (11.5 mAP) due to incomplete LiDAR scans and fine-grained vehicle subcategories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenSight outperforms all 11 baselines on overall mAP, achieving 22.9 versus the next-best UP-VL at 11.3 (a 2.0× improvement) and CoDA at 10.3. CoDA's headline mAP is inflated by its 85.5 AP on Car (a known, fully supervised class); across the remaining 9 novel classes its average drops to ~1.9 AP, whereas OpenSight maintains 25.3 AP on Car without any class-specific supervision. Among 2D-to-3D transfer baselines, Detic-3D (5.8 mAP) and OWL-ViT-3D (5.4 mAP) are far behind, confirming that simply lifting 2D open-vocabulary boxes into 3D is insufficient. Point-cloud classification baselines (OV-PointCLIP at 0.1, OV-PointCLIPv2 at 2.8) are essentially non-functional for outdoor driving scenes. Segmentation-based methods (SeCondPoint 8.2, 3DGenZ 7.5, OpenScene 8.8) and the indoor open-vocabulary method OV-3DET (5.7) also trail significantly. The largest per-class gaps appear on Barrier (42.2 vs. 0.5 for UP-VL), Motorcycle (30.4 vs. 16.8), and Pedestrian (52.5 vs. 30.8), while Trailer remains a weak point (0.8 AP) attributed to Grounding DINO's limited fine-grained vehicle discrimination rather than box localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenSight departs from the dominant paradigm of co-embedding known-class LiDAR features with vision-language model embeddings (as in CoDA, OV-3DET), which the paper demonstrates overfits to known categories. Instead, it introduces a two-stage decoupling: first perceiving generic objects purely from 2D-3D geometric priors (2D box lifting, temporal correlation, spatial augmentation with LLM-derived size priors), then interpreting semantics via cross-modal alignment with Grounding DINO's object-level features. This lineage draws on DETR-style 3D detection for the generic perception stage, Grounding DINO for object-level 2D features and text embeddings, and GPT-4 for size prior extraction — combining these in a way that avoids reliance on any predefined known classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates a critical failure mode in open-vocabulary LiDAR detection (known-class overfitting) and proposes a principled solution that generalizes across all 10 nuScenes classes as novel. However, the absolute performance gap to fully supervised detection (22.9 vs 57.3 mAP) remains substantial, and the method still depends on accompanying RGB images and a 2D detector, limiting its applicability to pure-LiDAR settings. Open questions include whether the generic perception stage can be improved to close the box-quality gap (evidenced by the AP-0.5 vs AR-0.5 disparity), whether the approach extends to datasets without synchronized cameras, and whether the LLM size-prior mechanism scales beyond the limited class set tested.
