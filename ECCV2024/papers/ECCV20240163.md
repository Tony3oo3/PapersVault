## Problem

DINOv2 and similar large-scale self-supervised Vision Transformers produce high-norm defective patch tokens that degrade dense prediction tasks such as segmentation, yet the underlying cause of these artifacts remained unexplained. The only prior remedy—retraining from scratch with additional register tokens on the proprietary LVD-142M dataset—is computationally prohibitive and offers no theoretical account of why the defects arise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240163/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained DINOv2 giant (40-layer ViT) and 30k unlabeled ImageNet-1K images (center-cropped, resized to 518×518) as input, and outputs repaired network weights in which only the singular values of linear-layer weight matrices have been modified. Training uses batch size 1, SGD with lr 0.005, and runs for one epoch (~6 hours on a single V100), with only the 10 layers preceding the first defective layer being trainable at each iteration. The repaired model produces spatially smooth, coherent patch feature maps suitable for downstream dense prediction.

## Method

The core insight is that defective tokens align with the leading left singular vector of the linearized network operations, analogous to the power method: under a single-token assumption, each Attention and MLP block is approximated as a linear map, and composing these across layers yields a matrix G_i whose leading left singular vector predicts the empirical defect direction for layer i in an input-agnostic manner. SINDER then repairs defects by identifying them via a logit threshold (absolute inner product with the singular defect direction exceeding 4 standard deviations) and applying a smoothness loss that pulls each defective token toward a Gaussian-weighted average of its 3×3 spatial neighbors. To preserve feature quality, learning is restricted to singular values only (U and V from SVD are frozen), and only 10 layers preceding the first defective layer are updated per iteration, drastically limiting parameter drift. This data-efficient fine-tuning avoids the need for large-scale retraining while improving unsupervised segmentation mIoU by 2–7% over both DINOv2 and DINOv2-Register.

## Results

On unsupervised segmentation with CAUSE, DINOv2-SINDER achieves 35.6 mIoU on Cityscapes and 62.9 mIoU on VOC2012 (without CRF), improving over DINOv2 by +4.2 and +7.1 mIoU respectively, and over DINOv2-Register by +2.3 and +14.0 mIoU. With STEGO, the repaired model gains +2.39 mIoU on Cityscapes and +3.25 mIoU on Potsdam-3 over DINOv2. On supervised segmentation (ADE20k linear probe), DINOv2-SINDER reaches 51.11 mIoU, surpassing both DINOv2 (48.83) and DINOv2-Register (49.03). Classification performance on ImageNet-1K is preserved (83.51 KNN Top-1 vs. 83.53 for DINOv2), confirming that the repair does not degrade global feature quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DINOv2-SINDER consistently outperforms both baselines on all dense prediction tasks. Against DINOv2 (the original self-supervised model), SINDER gains +4.2 mIoU on Cityscapes and +7.1 mIoU on VOC2012 for unsupervised CAUSE segmentation, +2.39/+3.25 mIoU for STEGO, +2.28 mIoU for supervised ADE20k, and reduces depth error from 0.370 to 0.337 on NYUd. Against DINOv2-Register (which requires full retraining on LVD-142M with additional register tokens), SINDER wins on every dense task: +2.3 mIoU on Cityscapes CAUSE, +14.0 mIoU on VOC2012 CAUSE, +3.15 mIoU on Potsdam-3 STEGO, +2.08 mIoU on ADE20k supervised, and 0.029 lower depth error. The only metric where SINDER trails DINOv2-Register is ImageNet-1K linear-probe Top-1 (86.29 vs. 87.10, a 0.81% gap), while KNN Top-1 is essentially tied (83.51 vs. 83.69). Notably, DINOv2-Register actually underperforms DINOv2 on several metrics (e.g., VOC2012 CAUSE drops from 55.8 to 48.9), suggesting that register tokens do not uniformly improve dense features, whereas SINDER's targeted repair yields consistent gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) and departs from Darcet et al. (2024) 'Vision transformers need registers,' which first identified high-norm defective patch tokens but attributed them to the classification token and offered only full retraining with register tokens as a remedy. SINDER reframes the problem by showing the defects are entirely determined by the pre-trained weights—specifically the leading left singular vector of linearized layer operations—making them input-agnostic rather than carrying image-specific global information as previously claimed. The theoretical analysis draws on the power method and SVD, distinguishing itself from prior transformer analyses (e.g., Geshkovski et al.) that relied on simplified architectures ignoring MLP blocks and multi-head attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 6-hour fine-tune on 30k unlabeled images (only singular values of 10 layers) outperforms full retraining on LVD-142M with register tokens on unsupervised and supervised segmentation, offering a carbon-efficient path to deploying DINOv2 for dense prediction. However, the analysis is confined to DINOv2 giant, and the paper explicitly leaves open how to prevent singular defects during training rather than repairing them post-hoc. Whether the SVD-based defect prediction generalizes to other transformer architectures (e.g., GPTs, other SSL backbones) remains an open question, and the depth estimation results show the repair does not uniformly help across all head architectures.
