## Problem

Existing 6-DoF grasp detection methods prioritize grasp stability but ignore human intention expressed in natural language, and prior language-driven grasp approaches are limited to either single-object scenes or 2D rectangle configurations. This paper addresses the unexplored task of language-driven 6-DoF grasp detection in cluttered 3D point cloud scenes, where the robot must identify the correct object among many and produce a full 6-DoF grasp pose from a single natural language command.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-view 3D point cloud scene (constructed from RGB images via ZoeDepth with a 55° field-of-view camera model) and a free-form natural language prompt (e.g., 'Grasp the blue cup') as input. It outputs 6-DoF grasp poses parameterized as an se(3) Lie algebra vector concatenated with a grasp width, constrained to the Robotiq 2F-140 gripper's maximum opening of 202.1 mm. During evaluation, 64 poses are sampled per scene–prompt pair, and the full pipeline is designed for real-time deployment on a KUKA robot with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper.

## Method

LGrasp6D is a diffusion model that generates grasp poses via a forward noising process over T=200 steps in se(3) space and a reverse denoising process conditioned on the point cloud (encoded by PointNet++) and the language prompt (encoded by frozen CLIP ViT-B/32). The key novelty is a negative prompt guidance learning strategy: during training, the denoising network simultaneously learns a negative prompt embedding by subtracting the target text embedding from scene tokens and regressing toward the text embeddings of other objects in the scene, supervised with a minimum-distance loss (weighted 0.1 alongside the 0.9-weighted noise prediction loss). At inference, the reverse process applies a compositional denoising step ε̃ = ε(∅) + w·(ε(t) − ε(t̃)), where t̃ is the learned negative embedding, effectively steering the generated grasp toward the desired object while repelling it from undesired ones. The model is trained on the newly introduced Grasp-Anything-6D dataset (1M point cloud scenes, 200M annotated 6-DoF grasps) and supports DDIM acceleration down to 10 sampling steps for near-real-time inference.

## Results

On the Grasp-Anything-6D dataset, LGrasp6D achieves a coverage rate of 0.6694, earth mover's distance of 0.4013, and collision-free rate of 0.7706, outperforming the next-best method (3DAPNet) by 0.1917 on CR and 0.3368 on EMD. The negative prompt guidance contributes a substantial margin over the ablated version (w.o. NPG): +0.1235 CR, −0.2249 EMD, and +0.0370 CFR. In real-robot experiments on a KUKA arm, LGrasp6D achieves 43% success rate on single-object and 42% on cluttered scenes, matching the best 2D baseline (LGD) while operating directly on point clouds. DDIM acceleration to 50 steps retains 96% of the full 200-step CR while reducing inference time from 1.48 s to 0.40 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGrasp6D outperforms all three 3D point-cloud baselines (6-DoF GraspNet, SE(3)-DF, 3DAPNet) on every detection metric (CR, EMD, CFR), with the largest gap over 6-DoF GraspNet (+0.2892 CR, −0.4022 EMD). The only metric where LGrasp6D trails is inference time: 6-DoF GraspNet (a VAE requiring one decode step) is 3.5× faster at 0.42 s vs. 1.48 s, though DDIM-50 closes this gap to 0.40 s. In the real-robot experiment (Table 4), LGrasp6D achieves 43%/42% success rate (single/cluttered), beating all 2D-image baselines: GG-CNN+CLIP (10%/7%), CLIPORT (27%/30%), Det-Seg-Refine+CLIP (30%/23%), GR-ConvNet+CLIP (33%/30%), and CLIP-Fusion (40%/40%). Notably, LGrasp6D ties exactly with LGD (43%/42%) on the robotic task, despite LGD operating on RGB-D images while LGrasp6D works on point clouds. The ablation without negative prompt guidance (w.o. NPG) still beats all external baselines, confirming the diffusion backbone's strength, but the NPG component adds a further +0.1235 CR and −0.2249 EMD improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) and the compositional denoising formulation from composable diffusion models (Liu et al., 2022) to the domain of language-conditioned 6-DoF grasp detection in 3D point clouds. It departs from prior language-driven grasp detection work, which was restricted to either 2D rectangle poses (Tang et al., Tziafas et al., Vuong et al.) or single-object 3D settings (3DAPNet, GraspGPT), by addressing the full multi-object cluttered 3D point cloud scenario. The negative prompt guidance mechanism is a novel application of the compositional diffusion identity to explicitly steer generation away from undesired objects, going beyond standard classifier-free guidance which only conditions on the positive prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Grasp-Anything-6D dataset (1M scenes, 200M grasp poses) fills a critical gap as the first large-scale benchmark for language-driven 6-DoF grasp detection in cluttered 3D point clouds, enabling systematic evaluation that was previously impossible. The negative prompt guidance offers a practical and interpretable solution to the fine-grained disambiguation problem (e.g., 'blue cup' vs. 'black cup'), validated through t-SNE visualizations, cross-dataset transfer, and real KUKA robot experiments. Open questions remain around part-level and task-level prompts ('grasp the knife by its handle'), generalization to diverse end-effectors, and tighter integration with low-level robotic control for fully end-to-end language-to-action pipelines.
