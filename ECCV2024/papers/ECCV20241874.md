## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods assume full correspondence between visual and audio streams, but in-the-wild videos contain ambient background sounds (traffic, speech, appliances) that are uncorrelated with the visual content. This causes models to generate uncontrolled or hallucinated ambient sounds at test time while missing subtle, transient action sounds. The paper addresses how to disentangle foreground action sounds from background ambient sounds during training without access to ground-truth separated audio streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241874/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3-second silent video clip (224×224, 5 FPS) and a 3-second audio condition clip (16 kHz) as inputs. During training, the audio condition is sampled from a nearby timestamp of the same source video; at test time, it is either retrieved from the training set via a trained audio-visual similarity model (for action-ambient joint generation) or set to a low-ambient audio file (for action-focused generation). The output is a 3-second audio waveform produced by decoding a 128-bin mel-spectrogram through a VAE decoder and a HiFi-GAN vocoder.

## Method

The core insight is that ambient sounds persist across time while action sounds are temporally localized, so conditioning the generator on audio from a different timestamp of the same video lifts the burden of modeling ambient sounds and forces the model to learn action-relevant features from the visual input. The architecture is an audio-visual latent diffusion model (AV-LDM) that extends a Stable Diffusion-based LDM with cross-attention over concatenated video features (from a pretrained egocentric video-language encoder) and audio features (from a VAE encoder plus MLP), trained with a standard denoising objective on VAE-compressed mel-spectrograms. At inference, classifier-free guidance (ω=6.5) and DPM-Solver (25 steps) are used, with the video condition dropped at 20% probability during training but the audio condition always retained. A contrastively trained AV-Sim model supports retrieval-augmented generation at test time, enabling controllable ambient sound levels by selecting conditions with varying ambient energy.

## Results

On Ego4D-Sounds, AV-LDM achieves a FAD of 0.9999, AV-Sync of 45.74%, and CLAP score of 0.1435, outperforming all baselines by large margins (e.g., Diff-Foley: FAD 3.56, AV-Sync 5.98%, CLAP 0.0346). On EPIC-KITCHENS, AV-LDM achieves FAD 1.32 and AV-Sync 59.26%, again surpassing Diff-Foley (FAD 3.46, AV-Sync 14.19%) and the retrieval baseline (FAD 1.96, AV-Sync 13.84%). In human evaluation, AV-LDM (action-ambient) receives the highest action-sound quality preference (72.5%), while AV-LDM (action-focused) achieves 97.5% preference for least ambient sound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-LDM outperforms all three learned baselines on every metric. Against Diff-Foley [38] (the strongest learned baseline), AV-LDM reduces FAD from 3.56 to 1.00 (3.6× improvement) and increases AV-Sync from 5.98% to 45.74% (7.6× improvement), indicating far better temporal synchronization. Against Spec-VQGAN [26], AV-LDM achieves a 3.9× lower FAD and 6.4× higher AV-Sync. Against REGNET [6], AV-LDM achieves an 8.4× lower FAD and 11.7× higher AV-Sync, though REGNET reports an anomalously high CLAP score (0.99) that exceeds even the ground-truth upper bound (0.27), suggesting a reporting issue. On the cross-dataset EPIC-KITCHENS evaluation, AV-LDM maintains its lead over Diff-Foley (FAD 1.32 vs. 3.46; AV-Sync 59.26% vs. 14.19%), confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion framework for video-to-audio generation established by Diff-Foley [38] and AudioLDM [36], but departs fundamentally in its training strategy: rather than assuming full video-audio correspondence, it conditions generation on a temporally offset audio clip from the same source video to implicitly disentangle foreground action sounds from background ambient sounds. The retrieval-augmented generation mechanism at test time draws on RAG ideas from NLP and image generation, adapted here to select semantically relevant ambient audio from the training set. The Ego4D-Sounds dataset (1.2M clips) extends prior action-sound datasets (The Greatest Hits, VGG-Sound, EPIC-Sounds) by an order of magnitude in scale and by covering in-the-wild egocentric actions with language narrations rather than fixed taxonomies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to address the ambient-sound entanglement problem in video-to-audio generation, which is critical for any application using in-the-wild training data (Foley, VR, games). The Ego4D-Sounds dataset and the ambient-aware conditioning technique are likely to become standard tools for the field. Key limitations remain: the model still struggles with subtle action sounds, the action-focused generation mode lacks ground-truth evaluation (no source separation labels exist), and the VR game generalization is only preliminary. Open questions include whether the offset-audio trick generalizes to multi-source or spatially separated sounds, and whether sim-to-real transfer to synthetic game imagery is feasible without fine-tuning.
