## Problem

Existing unified 3D vision-language models predominantly rely on projected multi-view images, which discard critical 3D geometric information and increase computational overhead, while also supporting only a narrow set of tasks (mainly classification and retrieval). Dense prediction tasks such as semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and grounded segmentation remain largely unaddressed in unified 3D frameworks, and no prior model operates directly on raw point clouds while covering both vision-only and vision-language tasks within a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240595/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a colored 3D point cloud (quantized into N₀ voxels with average RGB per voxel) and an optional text prompt tokenized by CLIP. It produces task-specific outputs via a composable task router: per-voxel binary masks (Q×N₀ matrix from dot-product of mask queries with full-resolution voxel features), class logits (dot-product of semantic queries with text-encoded class names), autoregressive text tokens for captioning, grounding similarity scores, and contrastive shape-text embeddings for retrieval. The architecture uses 150 latent queries plus one scene-level query, with the top-200 (S3DIS) or top-500 (ScanNet) highest-scoring instances retained at inference for segmentation tasks.

## Method

Uni3DL comprises four modules: a sparse 3D U-Net point encoder (Minkowski Res16UNet34C, pretrained from Mask3D) producing multi-scale voxel features, a CLIP-based text encoder, a 15-layer Query Transformer decoder that refines concatenated latent and text queries via masked cross-attention (following Mask2Former, where each query attends only to previously predicted masked voxels) and self-attention across all feature levels, and a Task Router that composes five functional heads (class, mask, grounding, text-generation, text-3D matching) per task. The key architectural insight is that all tasks share the same query transformer backbone and encoder, with only the head composition changing—e.g., instance segmentation uses class+mask heads, grounded segmentation uses mask+grounding heads, and retrieval uses only the matching head. Training is jointly multi-task across ScanNet instance segmentation, ScanRefer grounding, and Cap3D Objaverse captioning/retrieval for 50 epochs, combining cross-entropy, BCE+Dice, Hungarian-matched grounding, autoregressive captioning, and CLIP-style contrastive losses in a single objective.

## Results

Uni3DL achieves 79.3 mIoU on ScanNet (v2) semantic segmentation, 60.9 mAP50 on ScanNet instance segmentation, and 74.3 mIoU on ScanRefer grounded segmentation, all surpassing or matching task-specific SOTA methods. On 3D captioning (Cap3D Objaverse), it reaches 36.4 BLEU-1, exceeding the previous best (InstructBLIP-13B at 15.0) by over 20 points. For text-to-3D retrieval on Text2Shape, Uni3DL attains 14.4 R@1 and 19.7 R@5, outperforming Parts2Words (5.1 R@1, 17.2 R@5). The paper reports best or second-best results on 14 out of 17 evaluated metrics across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On semantic segmentation, Uni3DL (79.3 mIoU on ScanNet) outperforms all point-based baselines including PointTransformerV2 (74.4) and StratifiedTransformer (73.1), though Swin3D⁺ (75.6, using extra Structure3D data) and EQ-Net (75.3) remain competitive. For instance segmentation, Uni3DL achieves 60.9 mAP50, surpassing Mask-Att-Free⁺ (58.4) and Mask3D (55.2) by 2.5 and 5.7 points respectively. On grounded segmentation, Uni3DL dramatically outperforms TGNN (BERT) in mIoU (74.3 vs. 27.8) and Acc@0.5 (39.4 vs. 31.4), though TGNN (BERT) edges ahead on Acc@0.25 (37.5 vs. 32.3). For 3D captioning, Uni3DL (36.4 B-1) exceeds InstructBLIP-13B (15.0) and PointLLM-13B (12.8) by more than 20 points, a substantial margin. In text-to-3D retrieval, Uni3DL (14.4 R@1) nearly triples the performance of Parts2Words (5.1 R@1) and substantially outperforms Y2S (2.9 R@1). The paper claims best or second-best results on 14 of 17 metrics, with the few losses concentrated in semantic segmentation where Swin3D⁺ benefits from additional training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni3DL extends the functional unification paradigm established by X-Decoder and Uni-Perceiver v2 in 2D to the 3D domain, adapting the query-transformer-plus-task-router design to operate directly on point clouds rather than images. Its architectural components are drawn from three distinct lineages: the masked-attention decoder from Mask2Former, the Hungarian-matching grounding mechanism from DETR, and the sparse-convolutional U-Net encoder and voxel-sampling strategy from Mask3D. The key departure from prior 3D vision-language models (PointCLIP, ULIP, 3D-VisTA, Point-LLM) is twofold: it eliminates the reliance on multi-view image projections and extends the supported task set to include dense prediction (semantic/instance/grounded segmentation), which prior unified 3D models largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single point-cloud-based architecture can match or surpass task-specific SOTA across seven heterogeneous 3D tasks simultaneously, Uni3DL provides a practical template for reducing the engineering overhead of maintaining separate models for each 3D perception task. The task router's high parameter sharing and the ablation showing mutual benefit between captioning and retrieval pretraining offer concrete guidance for multi-task 3D training. Open questions include whether this unified approach scales to outdoor/large-scale scenes, whether the relatively simple text-generation head (dot-product with token embeddings) can be replaced by a full autoregressive LLM without sacrificing the dense-prediction capabilities, and how the model generalizes to unseen object categories beyond the ScanNet/Objaverse training distribution.
