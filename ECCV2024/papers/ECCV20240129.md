## Problem

Existing zero-shot object counting methods either rely on direct image-text alignment (struggling to capture fine-grained visual details for target classes) or on arbitrary patch selection guided by class prototypes (lacking scalability beyond pre-defined categories). A critical shared deficiency is the inability to reliably identify high-quality exemplars—patches containing exactly one object of the target class—while also suppressing the adverse effects of incorrectly identified exemplars on counting accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240129/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a free-form text prompt naming the target class (e.g., 'crab cakes') as input, with no bounding-box or dot annotations required at test time. It produces a pixel-wise density heatmap upsampled to the input image's spatial dimensions, from which the total object count is obtained by summing. Internally, the text prompt is split into a positive label (the class name) and a fixed negative label ('object') that drive two parallel detection passes through Grounding DINO.

## Method

VA-Count comprises two synergistic modules. The Exemplar Enhancement Module (EEM) runs Grounding DINO twice per image—once with the target class name and once with the generic prompt 'object'—to obtain positive and negative candidate bounding boxes, then applies an IoU-based deduplication step (removing negative boxes overlapping positive ones) and a single-object binary classifier (frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone + trainable FFN) to retain only boxes containing exactly one object. The Noise Suppression Module (NSM) selects the top-3 positive and top-3 negative exemplars by detection confidence, feeds them into a CounTR-style transformer counter where image features serve as queries and exemplar features as keys/values, and trains with a combined objective: a contrastive loss that maximizes similarity between the positive density map and ground truth while minimizing similarity between the negative density map and ground truth, plus a standard MSE density loss on the positive map. This contrastive mechanism explicitly teaches the counter to suppress responses to non-target objects, reducing the impact of residual exemplar errors.

## Results

On FSC-147 (zero-shot setting), VA-Count achieves an average MAE of 17.87 and RMSE of 101.26, attaining the best MAE among all zero-shot methods while ranking second in RMSE (behind CLIP-Count's 83.90). On CARPK, VA-Count achieves MAE 8.75 / RMSE 10.30 in the same-domain (C→C) setting and MAE 10.63 / RMSE 13.20 in the cross-domain (F→C) setting, outperforming all zero-shot baselines and approaching few-shot performance (e.g., CounTR's 5.75 MAE in C→C). The single-object filter Φ(·) alone reduces CARPK MAE from 29.72 (raw Grounding DINO) to 18.54, and the full VA-Count pipeline further reduces it to 8.75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot methods on FSC-147, VA-Count achieves the lowest average MAE (17.87), outperforming ZSC [45] (24.51) by 6.64 and PseCo [12] (20.24) by 2.37; however, it trails CLIP-Count [13] (18.29) by only 0.42 in MAE and loses substantially in RMSE (101.26 vs. 83.90), indicating CLIP-Count is more robust to large errors. On CARPK, VA-Count dominates all zero-shot baselines: it beats CLIP-Count [13] (11.96 MAE cross-domain) by 1.33, RCC [10] (21.38) by 10.75, and raw Grounding DINO [20] (29.72) by 19.09 in the cross-domain setting. In the same-domain CARPK setting, VA-Count (8.75 MAE) approaches but does not match the best few-shot methods—CounTR [19] (5.75) and BMNet+ [35] (5.76)—yet surpasses all reference-free and zero-shot competitors. The ablation confirms that the single-object filter Φ(·) contributes roughly 11 MAE points on CARPK (29.72 → 18.54), and the contrastive loss L_C further reduces FSC-147 MAE from 19.63 to 17.87.

## Relation to Prior Work

VA-Count builds directly on CounTR's transformer-based counter architecture (feature interaction via cross-attention, density map decoding, and MSE loss) but redefines the exemplar discovery pipeline. Where prior zero-shot counting methods either align text and images directly (CLIP-Count, VL-Count) or generate class prototypes from text to filter arbitrary patches (ZSC), VA-Count instead leverages Grounding DINO's open-vocabulary detection to produce candidate bounding boxes, then applies a single-object binary classifier and IoU-based deduplication to ensure exemplars contain exactly one target-class object. The contrastive loss with negative exemplars (objects from other classes) is a departure from prior ZOC losses that only supervise against ground-truth density maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that exemplar quality—specifically the single-object constraint and class correctness—is a critical but previously under-addressed bottleneck in zero-shot counting, and that a lightweight frozen-CLIP+FFN filter can substantially close the gap between raw VLP detection and usable counting exemplars. However, the method still requires training the single-object classifier and the counter on FSC-147, limiting its claim to fully training-free zero-shot counting; the RMSE on FSC-147 test (129.31) remains notably worse than CLIP-Count (106.62), suggesting the contrastive negative-exemplar strategy helps MAE but not robustness to large errors. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to extremely dense scenes where single-object detection becomes unreliable, and whether the dependence on Grounding DINO's detection quality caps the method's ceiling.
