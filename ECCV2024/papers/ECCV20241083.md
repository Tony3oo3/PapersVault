## Problem

Existing NeRF-based head avatar methods require per-appearance optimization, meaning each distinct look (hair, soft tissue changes) of a subject demands an independently trained model from scratch. This per-appearance constraint makes it impractical to build a single controllable avatar from an unstructured personal video collection spanning multiple years and appearances. No prior dynamic deformable NeRF framework learns a unified model that simultaneously captures multiple appearances of the same identity while preserving appearance-specific geometry and color details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241083/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unstructured collection of monocular talking-face videos (500 frames at 512×512 per appearance, 5 appearances per subject) along with FLAME-tracked pose/expression coefficients and camera intrinsics. At inference, the user specifies target FLAME pose and expression parameters plus a choice of appearance via the corresponding latent neural feature map (512×512×16 in UV space), and the network renders a photorealistic head image under that novel pose/expression/appearance combination. The output is a single RGB image per query, with the model able to switch between the subject's learned appearances without retraining.

## Method

PAV builds a shared canonical-space deformable NeRF parameterized by FLAME, where sample points are canonicalized via deformation gradients computed through BVH-accelerated nearest-triangle search between deformed and canonical meshes. The key architectural contribution is a set of learnable latent neural features (512×512×16) attached to mesh vertices in UV space, which serve as geometry-anchored appearance embeddings: for each ray-sampled point, the features of the nearest triangle's three vertices are aggregated and fed into separate MLPs. A shared density MLP predicts base density from hash-encoded canonical coordinates and expression, while a separate offset MLP predicts an appearance-specific density correction from the vertex-attached features and expression, enabling shape variation (e.g., facial hair) per appearance. A third MLP predicts view-dependent radiance conditioned on the appearance embedding, encoded view direction, and the final density. All appearances are trained jointly in one network with photometric L2 and face-masked depth L1 losses, eliminating the need for per-appearance training sessions.

## Results

On the VidCol dataset (Protocol-a: novel head pose and expression synthesis), PAV achieves PSNR 25.15 / SSIM 0.91 / FID 46.67 on the Federer collection and PSNR 27.09 / SSIM 0.89 / FID 53.91 on the King Charles collection, substantially outperforming Insta [44] (PSNR 21.03 / SSIM 0.84 / FID 104.28 and PSNR 23.67 / SSIM 0.86 / FID 98.87 respectively). In the efficiency comparison (Protocol-b, single-appearance training), PAV trains in 45 minutes with 26.8M parameters and 650 MB model size, versus 135 minutes, 79.7M parameters, and 1932 MB for five separate Insta models, while also achieving a lower FID (91.01 vs. 109.22).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAV is compared exclusively against Insta [44] (Zielonka et al., CVPR 2023), the closest prior dynamic deformable head NeRF method. Under Protocol-a (unified training on all appearances, testing on unseen poses/expressions), PAV wins on every metric for both subjects: +4.12 dB PSNR and +0.07 SSIM on Federer, +3.42 dB PSNR and +0.03 SSIM on King Charles, with FID roughly halved (46.67 vs. 104.28; 53.91 vs. 98.87). Under Protocol-b (separate per-appearance Insta models vs. a single unified PAV), PAV again achieves a lower FID (91.01 vs. 109.22) while being 3× faster to train (45 min vs. 135 min) and using ~3× fewer parameters (26.8M vs. 79.7M) and ~3× less memory (650 MB vs. 1932 MB). Qualitatively, Insta produces appearance-mixing artifacts and inaccurate geometry when trained jointly on multiple appearances, whereas PAV preserves appearance-specific details (facial hair, skin texture) while maintaining coherent expressions across appearances.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAV builds directly on the deformable head NeRF paradigm established by Insta [44], reusing its deformation gradient computation via nearest-triangle search and FLAME-based canonicalization. The key departure is the introduction of geometry-attached latent neural features (LNF) and an appearance-conditioned density offset to enable a single unified network to handle multiple appearances simultaneously, whereas all prior deformable head NeRF methods (Insta, IMAvatar, RigNerf) require independent per-appearance optimization. The geometry-anchored feature concept draws from static NeRF work [38] and human body NeRF [3,20], but PAV is the first to apply it to dynamic, multi-appearance head rendering. The unstructured-collection framing also connects to NeRF-in-the-Wild [22] and PersonNeRF [37], though those address static scenes rather than animatable faces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAV opens a practical pathway for creating personalized head avatars from casual, unstructured video collections (e.g., phone recordings spanning years) rather than requiring controlled per-appearance captures, which is a significant usability improvement for digital doubles and telepresence applications. The 3× reduction in training time and model size relative to per-appearance Insta while achieving better quality makes multi-appearance avatars more accessible. However, evaluation is limited to three subjects with five appearances each on a single self-curated dataset, and the multi-identity extension shows clear quality degradation, leaving open whether the approach scales to diverse populations or requires per-identity canonical spaces. A key open question is whether the shared canonical space assumption (small geometric differences across appearances of one person) holds broadly or breaks down for subjects with significant shape changes (e.g., weight loss, aging).
