## Problem

SOTA facial expression recognition (FER) methods overfit to their training domain and fail on unseen test sets with domain gaps, while existing domain adaptation approaches require access to labeled or unlabeled target-domain samples for fine-tuning. This paper tackles the stricter zero-shot generalization setting where only a single training set is available and no target-domain data can be accessed, which better reflects real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240364/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image and produces one of seven basic expression class labels. Unusually, the input image is consumed by two parallel pathways: a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 visual encoder extracts a fixed 512-dimensional face feature vector, while a trainable CNN backbone (e.g., ResNet-18) produces a same-dimension mask. The final logits are generated by max-pooling seven separated channel pieces of the masked features rather than through a standard fully-connected classifier, and the separation/diverse modules are discarded at inference time.

## Method

CAFE fixes the CLIP-extracted face features throughout training and learns a sigmoid mask (via a CNN backbone with GAP and resize) to selectively gate expression-relevant channels, mimicking how humans first locate faces then isolate expression cues. To prevent the 512-dimensional mask from overfitting, the masked features are split into seven channel pieces (73 channels each for six expressions, 74 for neutral), each max-pooled directly into a logit, eliminating the FC layer. A channel-diverse loss pushes the max value within each piece to dominate its peers, encouraging each expression to rely on distinct feature channels. Channel dropping is applied during training for additional regularization. The total loss combines the standard classification loss on the masked features, the separation loss on the max-pooled logits, and the channel-diverse loss, weighted by λ=1.5 and β=5.

## Results

When trained on RAF-DB and evaluated on all five FER test sets, CAFE achieves a mean accuracy of 63.48%, outperforming the best baseline (RUL at 54.88%) by 8.6 points. The largest gains appear on unseen domains: FERPlus (73.16% vs. 57.89% for RUL), SFEW2.0 (52.86% vs. 46.91%), and MMA (56.80% vs. 37.11%). Across all five training-set settings in Table 1, CAFE consistently achieves the highest mean accuracy on unseen test sets, with mean improvements of 4.3–10.8 points over the strongest baseline depending on the source domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAFE is compared against four recent SOTA FER methods: SCN (CVPR 2020), RUL (NeurIPS 2021), EAC (ECCV 2022), and OFER (ICCV 2023). On the source domain (RAF-DB), CAFE (88.72%) is slightly behind EAC (89.54%) and OFER (89.07%), confirming the authors' stated focus on generalization rather than in-domain accuracy. However, on every unseen test set, CAFE wins by large margins: on FERPlus it beats the best baseline (SCN, 58.37%) by 14.8 points; on SFEW2.0 it beats RUL (46.91%) by 6.0 points; and on MMA it beats EAC (37.27%) by 19.5 points. The gap is most dramatic on MMA and FERPlus, where domain shift is largest, while on AffectNet (noisy labels) the margin is smaller (~2 points over EAC). Across all five training-set configurations in Table 1, CAFE achieves the highest mean accuracy on unseen sets in every case, with mean improvements of 4.3–10.8 points over the strongest competing method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard end-to-end FER paradigm (SCN, RUL, EAC, OFER) by fixing pretrained CLIP face features and learning lightweight sigmoid masks on top, rather than training a full backbone from scratch. It explicitly distinguishes itself from domain adaptation FER methods that require access to labeled or unlabeled target-domain samples, positioning its zero-shot cross-domain evaluation protocol as closer to real-world deployment. The channel-separation module (splitting masked features into per-expression pieces and replacing the FC layer with max-pooling) and the channel-diverse loss are novel architectural and regularization contributions not present in prior FER or domain generalization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: SOTA FER models collapse on unseen domains, yet real-world deployment cannot assume access to target samples. The finding that a simple mask on frozen CLIP features outperforms complex end-to-end FER architectures by ~9–10 points on cross-domain evaluation suggests that pretrained general features are underutilized in current FER pipelines. Limitations include reliance on CLIP's face-feature quality, restriction to seven basic expressions, and the somewhat ad-hoc channel-splitting strategy (512 channels divided into 7 unequal pieces). Open questions include whether the mask-on-frozen-features paradigm transfers to fine-grained or compound expressions, video FER, or other pretrained backbones beyond CLIP.
