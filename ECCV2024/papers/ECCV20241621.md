## Problem

Existing NeRF methods are designed for fixed-scale, forward-facing or 360° object-centric trajectories and rely on a single warping function and isolated per-point sampling, making them ill-suited for UAV videos that span extreme spatial scales (outdoor to indoor), have limited viewpoints constrained by drone trajectories, and exhibit significant scene and lighting changes. This results in poor temporal consistency, broken global structure in low-texture regions, and loss of local detail when synthesizing novel first-person views from complex multi-scale drone footage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw UAV video (3–7 minutes, 3024×4032 pixels, captured by DJI Mini 3 Pro) with no pre-existing camera pose or location information as input. It must first estimate the full camera trajectory and poses from the video itself using sequential frame matching and entropy-based keyframe selection. The output is a reconstructed radiance field and a novel auto-generated first-person view video that can navigate the scene from arbitrary trajectories, including transitions from exterior sky views down to interior building spaces at altitudes ranging from 40 to 170 meters.

## Method

FPV-NeRF introduces three coupled components. First, a multi-scale camera space estimator subdivides the scene into octree blocks, each with its own Jacobian warping function, so that camera rays align precisely with region-specific pixels across the full trajectory. Second, a global-local scene encoder queries a multiresolution hash feature pool and fuses features via cross-resolution scaled dot-product attention (with a [CLS]-like global token), a grouped point-location embedding that maps multivariate Gaussian neighborhood centers through high-frequency sin/cos projections to avoid aliasing, and a BVH-based volume feature embedding for holistic block-level context. Third, a three-term loss combines Charbonnier color reconstruction, an Earth Mover's Distance–based disparity alignment between resolution levels (grounded in optimal transport), and a bounded-variation smoothness penalty across octree boundaries to enforce temporal consistency, global structural integrity, and local granularity simultaneously.

## Results

On the proposed UAV dataset, FPV-NeRF achieves 26.83 PSNR, 0.769 SSIM, and 0.364 LPIPS(VGG), outperforming the strongest baseline F²-NeRF (24.61 PSNR) by 2.22 PSNR and Mip-NeRF 360 (25.22 PSNR) by 1.61 PSNR, while training in 5.5 hours versus days for MLP-based methods. On the NeRF-360-V2 dataset, FPV-NeRF reaches 26.42 PSNR and 0.758 SSIM, competitive with F²-NeRF (26.39 PSNR) but trailing Mip-NeRF 360 (28.94 PSNR). On LLFF, FPV-NeRF attains 26.74 PSNR and 0.852 SSIM, edging out F²-NeRF (26.54 PSNR, 0.844 SSIM) and TensoRF (26.73 PSNR, 0.839 SSIM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the UAV dataset (the paper's primary benchmark), FPV-NeRF wins against all eight baselines on every metric, with the largest margins over F²-NeRF (+2.22 PSNR, +0.034 SSIM) and Instant-NGP (+3.66 PSNR, +0.059 SSIM). MLP-based methods (NeRF++, Mip-NeRF 360, Zip-NeRF) require days of training and still trail FPV-NeRF by 1.6–4.2 PSNR, while voxel-based methods (DVGO, Plenoxels) are faster but produce visibly blurred or noisy results on long UAV trajectories. On NeRF-360-V2, FPV-NeRF is competitive with F²-NeRF (26.42 vs. 26.39 PSNR) and Instant-NGP (26.42 vs. 26.24 PSNR) but falls short of Mip-NeRF 360 (28.94 PSNR) and Zip-NeRF (28.25 PSNR), indicating the method's advantage is most pronounced in multi-scale UAV scenarios rather than standard 360° object-centric scenes. On LLFF, FPV-NeRF achieves the best PSNR (26.74) and SSIM (0.852) among all baselines, narrowly surpassing F²-NeRF (26.54 PSNR) and TensoRF (26.73 PSNR). Training time for FPV-NeRF (5.5 h on UAV, 12 min on NeRF-360-V2, 10 min on LLFF) is competitive with other voxel-based methods, though Instant-NGP remains faster due to its CUDA implementation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPV-NeRF extends the NeRF lineage (Mildenhall et al.) through the multiresolution hash encoding of Instant-NGP (Müller et al.) and the unbounded-scene warping of Mip-NeRF 360 (Barron et al.), but departs from all three by replacing their single global warping function with an octree-based spatial subdivision that assigns distinct Jacobian warping per region. The global-local scene encoder introduces cross-resolution attention (borrowed from Transformer design) over the hash-encoding levels, a grouped point-location embedding to address Nyquist violations, and a BVH-based volume feature—none of which appear in prior NeRF variants. The comprehensive loss, combining Charbonnier color, EMD-based disparity alignment, and bounded-variation smoothness, is a novel training objective not present in the compared baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first dedicated UAV FPV benchmark (10 outdoor-to-indoor scenes captured with a DJI Mini 3 Pro), filling a gap where existing NeRF datasets assume fixed-scale, object-centric, or forward-facing trajectories. The practical implication is that reconstructed radiance fields can serve as offline training environments for UAV navigation, object detection, and autonomous flight planning. Limitations include per-scene training (no cross-scene generalization), a 5.5-hour training cost on a single A6000, and inference at ~14 s per view, which precludes real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the octree subdivision and cross-resolution attention scale to city-level scenes, and whether the method can be made feed-forward or generalizable to unseen environments.
