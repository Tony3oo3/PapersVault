## Problem

Generating a specific user-provided subject rendered in a specific user-provided style remains an open problem for diffusion-based personalization. Existing LoRA merging strategies rely on scalar linear combinations that require tedious grid search and fail robustly across subject-style pairs, because directly summing highly aligned LoRA columns causes signal interference that degrades both subject and style fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240024/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independently trained LoRA weight sets (a subject LoRA and a style LoRA, each obtained via DreamBooth fine-tuning of SDXL v1.0 with rank-64 LoRA) along with a text prompt at inference time. It outputs a merged LoRA consisting solely of per-column merger coefficient vectors m_c and m_s (6.5 MB total), which can be plugged into the frozen base model to generate stylized images of the subject. The merged LoRA also retains the ability to generate the subject or style in isolation, and the style strength can be further modulated at inference by scaling m_s with a scalar w_s.

## Method

ZipLoRA exploits two empirical observations: LoRA update matrices are highly sparse (zeroing 90% of lowest-magnitude elements has negligible effect), and columns of two independently trained LoRAs that are highly aligned (non-zero cosine similarity) produce signal interference when directly summed. The method introduces per-column learnable merger coefficient vectors m_c and m_s, applied via element-wise multiplication to each LoRA's weight matrix, and optimizes them with a three-term loss: L2 reconstruction losses ensuring the merged model reproduces the subject and style images generated by the individual LoRAs, plus a cosine-similarity penalty that drives the dot product between m_c and m_s toward zero per layer. Only these coefficient vectors are updated (1.6M trainable parameters) while the base model and both LoRAs remain frozen, requiring just 100 gradient steps—10× fewer than joint-training baselines—and no manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

ZipLoRA achieves the best subject-alignment (0.420 DINO cosine similarity) and text-alignment (0.303 CLIP-T) scores among all compared methods, while maintaining competitive style-alignment (0.699 CLIP-I) — only marginally below Direct Merge (0.702) which sacrifices subject fidelity. In user preference studies (360 responses per comparison, 45 users), ZipLoRA is preferred over all five baselines, with the strongest margins against Custom Diffusion (88.1%) and Mix of Show (87.3%). The method requires only 100 gradient updates (560 s runtime, 1.6 M trainable parameters, 6.5 MB storage) versus 10× more steps, 3540–4980 s runtime, 180 M parameters, and 360 MB storage for competing approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZipLoRA outperforms all four baselines on subject-alignment and text-alignment, with the largest gaps over Custom Diffusion (subject: 0.420 vs 0.346; text: 0.303 vs 0.262) and Mix of Show (subject: 0.420 vs 0.374; text: 0.303 vs 0.251). Against Direct Merge [32], ZipLoRA trades a negligible 0.003 drop in style-alignment (0.699 vs 0.702) for a substantial 0.063 gain in subject-alignment (0.420 vs 0.357), reflecting that naive column addition causes signal interference that degrades subject identity. Against Joint Training, ZipLoRA wins on all three metrics and requires 10× fewer training steps (100 vs ~1000) and 10× less runtime (560 s vs 3540 s). Against StyleDrop+DreamBooth [35], ZipLoRA leads by 0.053 in style-alignment and 0.026 in subject-alignment while eliminating the need for iterative human feedback. Against Mix of Show [10], ZipLoRA avoids the requirement for region-aware prompting (unsuitable for global style) and the need to train custom ED-LoRAs from scratch, achieving better scores with 8× less total runtime (560 s vs 4980 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LoRA-based personalization pipeline (DreamBooth for subjects, StyleDrop for styles) and the simple linear-combination LoRA merging of Ryu [32], but departs from both joint-training approaches (Custom Diffusion, Mix of Show) and naive arithmetic merging by introducing an optimization over column-wise merger coefficients. The key conceptual departure is the observation that LoRA update matrices are sparse and that highly aligned columns between two LoRAs cause destructive interference, motivating a 'zipper' analogy where the optimizer selects disjoint column subsets to preserve each LoRA's contribution. The method is positioned as a lightweight post-hoc merge rather than a new training paradigm, and its effectiveness is notably tied to SDXL's unexpectedly strong single-exemplar style learning capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZipLoRA provides a practical, hyperparameter-free recipe for combining any two independently trained LoRAs with only 100 gradient steps and 1.6M trainable parameters, making arbitrary subject-style combinations accessible without joint retraining. The broader implication is that a bank of independently learned subject and style LoRAs could be freely combined at inference time, enabling combinatorial personalization at scale. Key limitations include content leakage when the style LoRA fails to disentangle subject from style (an SDXL fine-tuning issue rather than a merging issue), and the method is currently restricted to pairs of LoRAs. Open questions include whether the column-orthogonality principle generalizes to merging three or more LoRAs, and whether the SDXL-specific style-learning behavior transfers to other architectures.
