## Problem

Existing 3D semantic occupancy prediction methods formulate the task as a one-shot voxel-wise segmentation with a single forward pass, which limits their ability to progressively refine and complete fine-grained local regions. These discriminative approaches also cannot model the underlying occupancy distribution, provide uncertainty estimates, or trade additional computation for improved prediction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240515/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud X_p ∈ R^{N_L×(3+d)} and N_C multi-view camera images X_c ∈ R^{N_C×H_C×W_C×3} as multi-modal inputs, producing a 3D semantic occupancy voxel map Y ∈ {c_0,...,c_N}^{H×W×Z} where each voxel receives a semantic class label. The occupancy is generated iteratively over T diffusion steps (coarse-to-fine), and the multi-step sampling process additionally yields a voxel-wise uncertainty map by counting prediction disagreements across steps. The encoder runs only once during inference while the decoder executes step-by-step, keeping latency comparable to single-forward methods.

## Method

OccGen adopts a 'noise-to-occupancy' generative paradigm where a 3D Gaussian noise map is progressively denoised into the occupancy prediction over T diffusion steps. The conditional encoder is a two-stream architecture (LiDAR via VoxelNet/3D sparse convolutions; camera via pre-trained 2D backbones + FPN) that uses a hard 2D-to-3D view transformation with Gumbel-Softmax for differentiable one-hot depth, a geometry mask derived from LiDAR features to guide camera voxel features, and an adaptive fusion module to produce multi-scale fusion features. The progressive refinement decoder stacks refinement layers, each containing 3D deformable cross-attention (to gather knowledge from conditional features), 3D deformable self-attention (for self-completion), and a time-diffusion module that applies scale-and-shift operations conditioned on the step embedding. Training corrupts the ground-truth occupancy with Gaussian noise at sampled timesteps and optimizes the decoder with a composite loss (cross-entropy, Lovász-softmax, geometric/semantic affinity, and depth loss); inference uses DDIM sampling with asymmetric time intervals to iteratively refine the occupancy from pure noise.

## Results

On nuScenes-Occupancy, OccGen achieves 22.0% mIoU and 30.3% IoU under the multi-modal setting, outperforming the previous SOTA CONet (20.1% mIoU, 29.5% IoU) by 1.9% mIoU (a relative 9.5% gain). Under camera-only and LiDAR-only settings, OccGen reaches 14.5% and 16.8% mIoU respectively, improving over CONet by 1.7% and 1.0% mIoU (relative gains of 13.3% and 6.3%). On SemanticKITTI, OccGen achieves the highest mIoU among all compared methods, surpassing OccFormer by 0.3% mIoU. In terms of efficiency, OccGen with one sampling step runs at 357 ms (multi-modal) and 294 ms (camera-only), comparable to CONet's 345 ms and 286 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OccGen consistently outperforms all compared baselines across all three input settings on nuScenes-Occupancy. Against the strongest prior method CONet [51], OccGen gains 1.9% mIoU (multi-modal), 1.0% mIoU (LiDAR-only), and 1.7% mIoU (camera-only), with the largest relative improvement (13.3%) in the camera-only setting where depth cues are absent. Against OpenOccupancy [51] (the earlier baseline from the same group), OccGen improves mIoU by 6.9 points in the multi-modal setting (22.0 vs. 15.1). On SemanticKITTI, OccGen surpasses OccFormer [58] by 0.3% mIoU and outperforms VoxFormer [25] and Symphonize [21], confirming that the generative diffusion paradigm generalizes beyond the multi-modal nuScenes setting. Notably, OccGen also achieves the best IoU across nearly all individual semantic categories (e.g., 35.8% for drivable surface, 24.7% for sidewalk in multi-modal), indicating that the coarse-to-fine refinement process improves scene-level completeness rather than just boosting a few easy classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

OccGen extends the 'noise-to-map' generative paradigm from DDP (diffusion-based dense visual prediction for depth) into the 3D occupancy domain, replacing the standard one-shot discriminative segmentation formulation used by CONet, OpenOccupancy, and OccFormer. The progressive refinement decoder is built on 3D deformable attention derived from Deformable DeTR, adapted to sample in 3D voxel space rather than 2D image space. Conceptually, the paper departs from the field's dominant single-forward mapping paradigm by framing occupancy prediction as an iterative denoising process, where the encoder runs once and the decoder refines the output over multiple diffusion steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OccGen demonstrates that a generative diffusion framework can match or exceed strong discriminative baselines on 3D occupancy while providing two properties unavailable to one-shot methods: progressive inference (trading compute for quality without retraining) and voxel-wise uncertainty estimation as a free byproduct of multi-step sampling. The 13.3% relative mIoU gain on camera-only nuScenes-Occupancy is notable, though absolute gains over the strongest multi-modal baseline (CONet) remain modest (~1–2% mIoU). A key open question is efficiency: the paper acknowledges that latency is comparable to, not better than, single-forward methods, and the authors flag lightweight generative architectures as future work. Whether the diffusion paradigm generalizes to other dense 3D perception tasks (e.g., flow, depth) at scale remains to be shown.
