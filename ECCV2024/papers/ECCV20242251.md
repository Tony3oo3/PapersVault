## Problem

Camera-based BEV perception faces a trade-off between 3D-to-2D view transformation (which relies on computationally expensive Transformers for 3D-2D correspondences) and 2D-to-3D transformation (which uses the efficient LSS pipeline but loses distant information due to depth uncertainty). Existing fusion methods like HeightFormer and FB-BEV adopt two-stage strategies where the second stage is constrained by the quality of the first-stage initial features, and none achieve real-time deployment without Transformers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 6-camera RGB images (704×256 for validation, 1600×640 for test) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a BEV feature map (128×128 or 256×256) that feeds a standard 3D detection head. A SceneNet branch simultaneously predicts per-pixel depth distributions and instance masks from the image features, which are then consumed by both transformation streams. The BEV grid is defined without voxel features, and temporal fusion uses 8 previous keyframes following BEVDet4D.

## Method

DualBEV unifies 3D-to-2D and 2D-to-3D view transformation through a shared probabilistic framework with three measurements: projection probability (from depth maps), image probability (from instance masks), and BEV probability (predicted occupancy). The 3D-to-2D stream (HeightTrans) projects pre-defined 3D points using a multi-resolution height sampling strategy (0.5m in [-2m,2m], 1.0m outside) and retrieves image features via a pre-computed lookup table, enabling CNN-based sampling at BEV Pooling speed. The 2D-to-3D stream (Prob-LSS) lifts image features through depth prediction and applies the same three probabilities. A Dual Feature Fusion (DFF) module concatenates both streams, applies channel-attention-based soft selection, and predicts BEV probability via a local ProbNet plus a global spatial attention branch, all in a single stage without any Transformer.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, DualBEV achieves state-of-the-art 55.2% mAP and 63.4% NDS, surpassing the previous best Transformer-based method SparseBEV (54.3% mAP, 62.7% NDS) by 0.9% mAP and 0.7% NDS without using any Transformer. On the nuScenes val set with 2 frames, DualBEV reaches 38.0% mAP and 50.4% NDS, outperforming BEVStereo (37.2% mAP, 50.0% NDS) which employs a temporal-stereo module for depth estimation. The method adds only 1.5 ms of extra latency over the BEVDepth+BEVPoolv2 baseline (10.2 ms → 11.7 ms), maintaining real-time deployment feasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualBEV outperforms all 15 compared baselines on the nuScenes test set. The most significant comparison is against SparseBEV [18], the previous state-of-the-art Transformer-based VT method, where DualBEV leads by 0.9% mAP and 0.7% NDS while eliminating Transformer entirely. Against the two-stage fusion methods FB-BEV [16] and SA-BEV [10], DualBEV gains 1.5% and 1.9% mAP respectively, demonstrating the advantage of one-stage dual-view fusion over sequential refinement. On the val set, DualBEV also surpasses BEVStereo [13] (which uses temporal-stereo for depth) by 0.8% mAP and 0.4% NDS, and beats the BEVDepth+BEVPoolv2 baseline by 1.2% mAP and 1.9% NDS. Notably, DualBEV achieves the best mATE (0.414) and mAVE (0.252) among all methods, indicating superior localization and velocity estimation, while its mAOE (0.377) is slightly higher than SparseBEV (0.324) and SA-BEV (0.338), suggesting orientation estimation remains a relative weakness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of BEV view transformation—2D-to-3D (LSS-based, e.g., BEVDet, BEVDepth) and 3D-to-2D (Transformer-based, e.g., BEVFormer, DETR3D)—under a single probabilistic correspondence framework. It builds directly on BEVDepth/BEVPoolv2 for the LSS stream and on Simple-BEV's bilinear projection for the 3D-to-2D stream, but departs from prior fusion attempts (HeightFormer, FB-BEV) that required a two-stage pipeline where one VT initializes the other. The key conceptual departure is replacing the Transformer's deformable attention in 3D-to-2D VT with a CNN-based HeightTrans module that uses pre-computed lookup tables, and showing that both streams share an identical probabilistic formulation (depth × instance mask × BEV occupancy).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving state-of-the-art nuScenes performance (55.2% mAP) without any Transformer component, DualBEV offers a deployment-friendly alternative for real-time autonomous driving systems where Transformer inference cost is prohibitive. The unified probabilistic framework is broadly applicable—the authors note it extends to BEV segmentation and 3D occupancy prediction. However, the method's heavy reliance on accurate depth estimation (a 10-point mAP drop when depth is replaced by uniform distribution) and the absence of temporal information in probability prediction remain open limitations that constrain its robustness in challenging driving conditions.
