## Problem

Source-Free Active Domain Adaptation (SFADA) requires adapting a model to a target domain without access to source data while using only a minimal annotation budget, yet existing approaches either depend on source data for identifying informative samples (ADA methods) or are restricted to a single round of active sampling (MHPL). The paper tackles how to iteratively select the most informative target samples and maintain cross-domain alignment across multiple query-and-adaptation rounds without ever referencing source data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240013/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen source-pretrained model (ResNet50 or ResNet101 with a 256-dim bottleneck), an unlabeled target image pool, and a small annotation budget (1–10% of target samples queried in 0.1% increments per round). It produces class predictions on the target domain, with the model architecture remaining unchanged from the source model throughout adaptation. A key operational detail is that the method iterates R rounds of query-then-adapt, where each round's model hypothesis and feature representations are retained for the next round's sampling and alignment.

## Method

LFTL alternates between Contrastive Active Sampling (CAS) and Visual Persistence-guided Adaptation (VPA) across iterative rounds. CAS contrasts the current model's softmax logits against the previous round's model logits (weighted by α) to highlight samples that are both uncertain for the current model and persistently challenging across rounds; it then ranks samples by a best-versus-second-best margin on these contrastively decoded probabilities, augmented by a class-level transferability factor that downweights classes already well-transferred. VPA maintains a 'persistence vault' storing exponential moving averages (γ=0.9) of feature representations for all previously queried active anchors, and encourages unlabeled target samples to cluster around these temporally-ensembled anchors via a soft cosine-similarity entropy loss, approximating a source-similar distribution while exploiting newly acquired target labels. The total training objective combines cross-entropy on labeled targets, the VPA clustering loss, and standard entropy minimization on unlabeled targets, with no pseudo-label supervision to avoid error accumulation.

## Results

On VisDA-C (ResNet-101), LFTL achieves 87.4% per-class accuracy with only 1% annotation budget, matching the SFUDA SOTA SHOT++ (87.3%) while requiring 0.3h vs. 5.8h of training. At 5% budget, LFTL reaches 92.8%, surpassing both the prior SFADA SOTA MHPL (91.3%) and the source-accessible ADA SOTA LADA (92.1%). On Office-Home (ResNet-50), LFTL attains 78.8% average accuracy at 5% budget and 85.1% at 10%, outperforming all SFUDA methods (best: SHOT++ at 73.0%) and exceeding LADA (84.2%) at 10%. On Office-31, LFTL reaches 93.5% at 5% and 95.4% at 10%, matching or exceeding LADA (93.6% and 95.2% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFTL outperforms all SFUDA baselines (SFDA, A²Net, SHOT, SHOT++, CPGA, DaC, SF(DA)²) by a substantial margin across all three datasets, e.g., 92.8% vs. 88.1% (SF(DA)²) on VisDA-C at 5% budget. Against the prior SFADA method MHPL, LFTL wins on VisDA-C (92.8% vs. 91.3%) and Office-31 (93.5% vs. 93.2% at 5%), but slightly trails on Office-Home (78.8% vs. 79.3% at 5%). Compared to the source-accessible ADA SOTA LADA, LFTL achieves comparable or superior accuracy without source data: it exceeds LADA on VisDA-C at 5% (92.8% vs. 92.1%), Office-Home at 10% (85.1% vs. 84.2%), and Office-31 at 10% (95.4% vs. 95.2%), while being marginally behind on Office-Home at 5% (78.8% vs. 80.0%) and Office-31 at 5% (93.5% vs. 93.6%). Among other ADA methods (AADA, TQS, CLUE, SDM-AG), LFTL at 1% budget on VisDA-C (87.4%) already surpasses their 5% results (74.6–86.7%), demonstrating the efficiency of the contrastive sampling strategy. The paper also shows that removing source data from LADA (SF-LADA) causes a 7.1% drop on VisDA-C, confirming that LFTL's hypothesis-transfer and visual-persistence mechanisms effectively compensate for source inaccessibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

LFTL extends the source-free active domain adaptation (SFADA) paradigm introduced by MHPL (CVPR 2023) from a single-round query to a multi-round iterative process, and departs from active DA methods (LADA, TQS, CLUE) that require simultaneous access to source data. The active sampling criterion repurposes the best-versus-second-best (BvSB) margin from Joshi et al. (CVPR 2009) but applies it to contrastively decoded hypotheses across successive model rounds, borrowing the conceptual template from contrastive decoding in NLP. The adaptation stage combines standard entropy minimization with a novel visual persistence vault (EMA over anchor features) that substitutes for the source-data-free manifold alignment typically achieved via frozen source hypotheses in SFUDA methods like SHOT and A²Net.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency story: LFTL matches SHOT++'s 87.3% on VisDA-C with 1% labels in 0.3h versus 5.8h, and the continual-improvement property (Fig. 4) makes it suitable for real-world deployment where annotation budgets grow incrementally. However, the accuracy gains over the prior SFADA SOTA (MHPL) are modest (~1.5%), and the method is validated only on classification with ResNet backbones across three benchmarks, leaving open whether the CAS+VPA recipe transfers to segmentation, detection, or open-set/partial DA settings. A key open question is how the method behaves under larger domain shifts or when the source model is poorly trained, since the entire framework relies on the quality of the initial source-pretrained hypothesis.
