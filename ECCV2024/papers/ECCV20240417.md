## Problem

Event-based independent motion object (IMO) segmentation has lagged behind optical flow estimation, largely because prior methods either require expensive manual or VICON-based motion mask annotations or rely on optimization with a fixed number of parametric motion clusters. No event-based framework existed that could train a segmentation network without any human-provided labels, despite biological vision systems detecting IMOs purely from motion patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240417/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the network consumes only a stream of events encoded as a 15-channel event volume (spatio-temporal bins with bilinear interpolation) and outputs a per-pixel binary segmentation mask identifying IMO regions. During training, an additional depth map in the camera frame is required to compute the geometric pseudo-labels, but this dependency is entirely absent at test time. The flow network is fine-tuned from E-RAFT (pretrained on DSEC) using RAFT flow computed from grayscale images as supervision, since standard event flow networks underperform on independently moving objects.

## Method

Un-EVIMO generates binary IMO pseudo-labels through a geometric self-labeling pipeline: a fine-tuned event flow network produces dense optical flow, RANSAC with input depth robustly estimates the 6-DOF camera motion (treating IMOs as outliers), and the residual between observed flow and camera-induced flow is thresholded via Otsu's method to yield per-pixel IMO labels. A two-stage variance filter discards low-confidence slices where the residual distribution lacks a clear bimodal separation. The segmentation network is a UNet-like CNN with a ResNet-34 encoder (ImageNet-pretrained) that takes event volumes and predicts binary masks using Focal Loss to handle class imbalance. Unlike mixture-model approaches, the method makes no assumption about the number of objects or their parametric motion form, and inference is a single feed-forward pass requiring no per-scene tuning.

## Results

On the EVIMO dataset, Un-EVIMO achieves event-masked IoU of 50±21 (Table), 45±24 (Box), 56±15 (Floor), 53±19 (Wall), and 44±21 (Fast Motion), all without any manual IMO labels. The method outperforms the only other unsupervised baseline, EMSGC (Top 30%), by 21–38 IoU points on Box, Floor, and Wall sequences. Inference runs at 6.57 ms total (3.35 ms preprocessing + 3.22 ms network forward pass) on a single RTX 3080, making it the fastest method compared to EMSGC (9529 ms) and GConv (716 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest supervised baselines, Un-EVIMO trails EVIMO [24] by 19–33 IoU points and EVDodgeNet [36] by 16–26 IoU points, reflecting the inherent cost of noisy pseudo-labels versus ground-truth masks. However, Un-EVIMO is competitive with the weaker supervised methods: it matches SpikeMS [29] on Table (50 vs 50) and Fast Motion (44 vs 38), and edges out GConv [23] on Floor (56 vs 55) and Fast Motion (44 vs 39), though it falls short on Wall (53 vs 80). Against the only other unsupervised method, EMSGC [49], Un-EVIMO wins decisively on Box (45 vs 24), Floor (56 vs 18), and Wall (53 vs 24) even under EMSGC's most favorable Top-30% tuning, and by an even wider margin under Top-50%. The paper also reports that Un-EVIMO's optical flow (EPE 1.55–5.33) substantially outperforms off-the-shelf E-RAFT (EPE 4.98–20.47) on EVIMO sequences, which is critical for generating high-quality pseudo-labels. In terms of speed, Un-EVIMO's 6.57 ms total latency is ~1450× faster than EMSGC and ~109× faster than GConv, enabling real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Un-EVIMO sits at the intersection of event-based motion segmentation and unsupervised geometric self-labeling. It departs from prior event-based IMO methods (EVIMO, SpikeMS, GConv, EVDodgeNet) that require per-pixel motion masks, and from optimization-based approaches (EMSGC, EMMC) that assume a fixed number of parametric motion models. The key conceptual move is borrowing the geometric self-labeling paradigm from image-based motion segmentation (Yang & Ramanan, Ranjan et al.) and adapting it to the event domain: using RANSAC-fitted ego-motion from depth and flow to compute a residual flow field, then thresholding it with Otsu's method to produce pseudo-labels for a feed-forward UNet. The optical flow backbone is E-RAFT, fine-tuned on RAFT image-based flow to recover the independent motion that E-RAFT's DSEC pretraining misses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first event-based IMO segmentation framework that requires no manual motion masks, making it directly applicable to large-scale event datasets where per-pixel segmentation labels are prohibitively expensive to produce. The ability to handle an arbitrary, unknown number of objects without per-scene tuning addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying event-based segmentation. Key limitations remain: depth is required during training (though not inference), pseudo-label noise produces less sharp boundaries than supervised methods, and temporal consistency across event slices is weak. Open questions include eliminating the depth dependency entirely, incorporating temporal constraints into both pseudo-label generation and network training, and validating the approach on more diverse, real-world event datasets beyond the controlled EVIMO environment.
