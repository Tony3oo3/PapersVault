## Problem

While mesh-based visual localization using local features has been shown to be robust to the synthetic-to-real domain shift, the impact of replacing real database images with synthetic mesh-rendered images on global-feature-based Visual Place Recognition (VPR) at city scale remained unexplored. This paper quantifies a significant performance drop (e.g., SALAD R@1 falling from 96.3% to 76.9% on San Francisco) and proposes a solution to close this gap, enabling fully mesh-based hierarchical localization pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241984/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system takes a single real-world RGB query image and compares its embedding (extracted by the frozen f_theta_real) against a precomputed database of synthetic street-view images rendered from a citywide 3D textured mesh at 2.5 m camera height, sampled every 10 m along OSM-derived paths. The output is a location estimate (GPS coordinates) obtained from the k-nearest synthetic database match, with a 100 m positive threshold. The synthetic database can be regenerated for any target city with an available 3D mesh without retraining, making deployment city-agnostic.

## Method

MeshVPR employs a two-model feature alignment strategy: a frozen pretrained VPR model f_theta_real processes real queries, while a copy f_theta_synt (initialized with identical weights) is fine-tuned via an MSE loss to produce embeddings from synthetic images that match those of f_theta_real on same-viewpoint real/synthetic pairs. Training requires only 50 k iterations (batch 32, Adam lr 1e-5, ~3 h on 4 GB VRAM) on paired images from SF-XL and its 3D mesh counterpart, and is model-agnostic—applicable to any pretrained VPR architecture (CosPlace, MixVPR, SALAD, EigenPlaces, Conv-AP). The synthetic database for a target city is generated by solving the Route Inspection Problem on an OpenStreetMap graph to produce a continuous path, then rendering views with Unreal Engine/Cesium at configurable intervals. The approach is hyperparameter-free, requires no validation set, and the alignment step is performed once, after which deployment to new cities only requires rendering their mesh.

## Results

On the three synthetic-database test sets, SALAD+MeshVPR achieves the best R@1 of 82.0% (Berlin), 63.8% (Paris), and 69.1% (Melbourne), with R@100 reaching 95.7%, 84.7%, and 97.8% respectively. MeshVPR consistently improves every baseline VPR model by 8–20 R@1 points; for example, SALAD alone scores 69.8% R@1 on Berlin but jumps to 82.0% with MeshVPR. On the San Francisco real-vs-synthetic comparison, SALAD+MeshVPR recovers most of the performance gap, achieving 88.8% R@1 on the synthetic database versus 96.3% with a real-image database (a gap of only 7.5 points, versus 19.4 points without MeshVPR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshVPR is applied on top of five SOTA VPR models (Conv-AP, CosPlace, MixVPR, EigenPlaces, SALAD) and improves every one of them by 8–20 R@1 points across all three test sets; the largest absolute gain is on Melbourne where Conv-AP jumps from 18.7% to 25.0% R@1 and SALAD from 59.1% to 69.1%. Against alternative fine-tuning strategies that train a single model on mixed real+synthetic data using various metric-learning losses (Contrastive, Triplet, Lifted, NTXent, FastAP, MultiSimilarity, Circle, SupCon), MeshVPR's two-model MSE alignment substantially outperforms all of them: on Berlin R@1, MeshVPR (EigenPlaces base) scores 67.1% versus the best alternative (MultiSimilarity at 40.4%), a gap of over 26 points. The paper also shows that even models explicitly designed for cross-domain robustness (MixVPR, EigenPlaces) still suffer large drops without MeshVPR, confirming that the synthetic-to-real gap is not solved by generalization alone. On the San Francisco real-vs-synthetic experiment, SALAD+MeshVPR narrows the R@1 gap from 19.4 points (76.9% synthetic vs 96.3% real) to 7.5 points (88.8% vs 96.3%), and the R@100 gap shrinks to just 1.5 points (97.8% vs 99.3%). Mesh quality also matters: using a high-quality San Francisco mesh instead of a low-quality one yields a 15.7-point R@1 improvement, and training the alignment on the same quality level as the test mesh is beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mesh-based visual localization line of work (Panek et al., MeshLoc) by addressing the previously unexplored VPR/retrieval step: prior mesh-based methods either skipped VPR on small scenes, relied on real-image databases for retrieval before mesh-based postprocessing, or operated on limited maps without reporting the domain-shift penalty. It builds directly on SOTA VPR models (CosPlace, MixVPR, SALAD, EigenPlaces) as frozen teachers, and departs from standard domain-adaptation or metric-learning approaches by proposing a lightweight MSE-based feature alignment between two copies of the same architecture. The SF-XL dataset and its 3D mesh counterpart serve as the alignment training set, following the large-scale VPR training paradigm established by Berton et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeshVPR enables fully mesh-based hierarchical localization pipelines, which are attractive given the growing availability of free citywide 3D meshes and the privacy advantages of synthetic databases (no faces or license plates). The practical finding that only ~100k alignment pairs are needed (vs. 9.2M) and that the method is hyperparameter-free makes deployment to a new city straightforward. Open questions include integrating MeshVPR with mesh-based visual localization for a complete pipeline, handling multiple rendering domains (night, weather), and closing the remaining ~8-point R@1 gap to real-image databases. The large sensitivity to mesh quality (15.7 R@1 points between HQ and LQ) highlights that results will improve as city mesh resolution increases.
