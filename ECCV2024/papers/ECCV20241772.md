## Problem

The default constant loss weighting in ε-prediction for diffusion models has been empirically shown to be suboptimal, yet no prior work provided a unified theoretical explanation of why it fails, what the resulting bias concretely does to generation, or what the underlying optimization causes are. This paper identifies that constant weighting induces a biased estimation of the clean image x₀ during training, manifesting as color shift and structural inconsistency in generated samples, and systematically dissects the existence, impact, and root causes of this bias.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241772/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in loss-weighting modification applied on top of the ADM architecture, operating on 256×256 RGB images. During training, the network receives noisy inputs x_t = √α_t·x₀ + √(1−α_t)·ε at T=1000 diffusion steps and predicts the added Gaussian noise; during sampling, it starts from pure Gaussian noise (and optionally a class label for conditional generation) and iteratively denoises to produce a 256×256 image, with the number of sampling steps reducible via respacing. The only code change is a single line replacing the constant weight in the MSE loss with 1/√SNR(t).

## Method

The paper derives that the one-step clean-image estimate decomposes as x₀ = x̂₀ + (1/√SNR(t))·(ε_θ(x_t,t) − ε), revealing that the noise-prediction error is amplified by the coefficient 1/√SNR(t), which grows monotonically as t increases. This means constant weighting treats all timesteps equally despite the implicit target x₀ being increasingly sensitive to noise-prediction errors at high t, producing biased estimation that propagates into color-shifted and structurally inconsistent generations. The proposed fix weights the ε-prediction MSE loss by 1/√SNR(t), assigning higher penalty to noise errors at high-noise timesteps so the network is compelled to reduce them more aggressively there. The paper further shows this principle unifies and explains prior weighting strategies (P2, Min-SNR) as partial corrections, and provides empirical evidence that the bias stems from vastly different optimization difficulty (MSE between input and target is negligible for t>600, letting the network 'do nothing') and different importance (early steps dominate the VLB and error propagation) across timesteps.

## Results

On unconditional generation (FFHQ, T=1000), the proposed 1/√SNR weighting achieves FID 6.35, substantially outperforming the constant-weight baseline (10.86), P2 (6.52), and Min-SNR (6.50). The advantage is most dramatic on small datasets: on MetFaces (1k images, T=1000), FID drops from 41.42 (constant) to 9.17 (ours), a 4.5× improvement. For class-conditional generation, the method achieves FID 8.45 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 11.97 constant, 9.02 Min-SNR) and FID 93.32 on ImageNet 256×256 (vs. 99.00 constant). The method also matches 1000k-iteration constant-weight performance with only 400k training iterations, demonstrating improved training efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed 1/√SNR weighting consistently outperforms all three ε-prediction baselines (Constant, P2, Min-SNR) across every dataset and sampling step tested. On FFHQ at T=1000, the gap over Min-SNR (the strongest prior method) is modest (6.35 vs. 6.50), but widens substantially at fewer steps: at T=20, the method achieves FID 29.38 vs. 34.40 (P2) and 34.65 (Min-SNR). On the small MetFaces dataset (1k images), the improvement is dramatic — FID 9.17 vs. 14.20 (P2) and 30.88 (Min-SNR) at T=1000 — confirming the paper's claim that gains are most pronounced with limited data. For class-conditional generation, the method achieves the best FID on both CIFAR-10 (8.45 vs. 9.02 Min-SNR) and ImageNet (93.32 vs. 94.81 Min-SNR), while also attaining the highest Inception Score on ImageNet (13.37). Against GAN baselines in Table 3, the method with 1000k iterations reaches FID 4.97 on FFHQ, competitive with LDM (4.98) and approaching StyleGAN2 (3.73), using only a one-line loss-weight change on top of ADM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the standard DDPM/ADM epsilon-prediction framework established by Ho et al., which adopted constant loss weighting as the default training objective. It departs from prior weighting-strategy work (P2, Min-SNR) by providing a principled theoretical derivation—via the amplification coefficient 1/√SNR(t)—that explains *why* constant weighting is biased, rather than proposing weights based on empirical observation. The paper also unifies existing weighting strategies under this single principle and contrasts with reparameterization approaches (v-prediction, EDM) that address error amplification through network input/target scaling rather than loss weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the default constant weighting systematically biases x₀ estimation—causing color shift and chaotic early sampling steps—challenges a foundational assumption in diffusion model training and provides a clear theoretical remedy. The fix is a single-line code change, making it trivially adoptable across any diffusion model codebase, and the paper demonstrates consistent gains across six datasets, two samplers, and both unconditional and class-conditional settings. Open questions include whether the insight extends to alternative noise schedules, latent diffusion models at higher resolutions, and whether further gains are possible by combining this weighting with stronger backbones (e.g., Stable Diffusion) or longer training.
