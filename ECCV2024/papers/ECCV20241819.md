## Problem

NeRF-based human avatar methods entangle geometry, appearance, and lighting within neural networks, making them incompatible with traditional graphics pipelines for editing, relighting, and physics-based rendering. Existing mesh-based avatar approaches require per-frame reconstruction or pre-scanned templates and none support intrinsic material decomposition. This paper tackles the challenge of learning a triangular mesh avatar with accurate geometry and decomposed materials entirely end-to-end from multi-view videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241819/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16 surrounding full-body video views (selected from 160 in ActorsHQ) along with per-frame SMPL-X registrations that provide pose parameters and a canonical body template. It produces an explicit triangular mesh on a 256×256×256 tetrahedra grid with per-vertex skinning weights, a pose-conditioned material field (albedo and roughness via a shallow MLP), and a learnable environmental light probe. The resulting avatar supports novel pose synthesis via linear blend skinning, relighting under arbitrary environment maps, and direct texture/material editing in standard graphics engines.

## Method

The core representation is a hybrid of an implicit SDF field (coordinate-based MLP) and an implicit pose-dependent material field, bridged by differentiable marching tetrahedra (DMTet) to extract an explicit triangular mesh. A 2D UNet encodes front/back position maps of the posed SMPL template to generate high-frequency vertex offsets, compensating for the low-frequency bias of the MLP-based SDF. Skinning weights are diffused from the SMPL surface into 3D space following FITE, enabling instant posed-space deformation via linear blend skinning. Rendering uses a differentiable Monte-Carlo PBR pipeline (Disney material model, 4–8 samples/pixel with MIS and denoising) that analytically connects outgoing radiance to surface position, preventing shadow baking and enabling accurate material decomposition. A stereo-based normal estimation network (RAFT-Stereo coarse + image-to-image refinement) provides pseudo ground-truth normal maps as additional geometric supervision, exploiting the fact that explicit mesh surfaces allow direct normal loss—something infeasible with implicit radiance fields.

## Results

On the AvatarReX dataset, MeshAvatar (PBR) achieves state-of-the-art dynamic human reconstruction in terms of both geometry and appearance, outperforming ARAH, PoseVocab, and AvatarReX on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID metrics for both training-frame reconstruction and novel pose synthesis. The NeRF variant of the method (Ours radiance) shows even stronger rendering metrics, confirming that the hybrid representation itself is the key advantage. The reconstructed geometry achieves a normal-map error of 12.17 degrees against pseudo ground-truth normals. For relighting, MeshAvatar produces more accurate albedo and roughness decompositions than Relighting4D, yielding more realistic novel-lighting renderings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ARAH, PoseVocab, and AvatarReX on AvatarReX, MeshAvatar (PBR) wins on all four image-quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID) for both reconstruction and novel pose synthesis, with the NeRF variant showing an even larger margin. The PBR version incurs a small rendering-quality drop relative to the NeRF variant due to Monte-Carlo sampling constraints (4–8 spp during training), yet still surpasses all three baselines, indicating the geometry representation is the dominant factor. In the relighting comparison against Relighting4D (ECCV 2022), MeshAvatar produces visibly more accurate geometry, albedo, and roughness maps, and generates more physically plausible relit images under novel global illumination; Relighting4D struggles with both surface reconstruction and material decomposition. The paper notes that direct quantitative comparison with other relighting baselines (e.g., RANA, Neural Reconstruction of Relightable Human Model) was not possible due to lack of public code.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper deliberately departs from the dominant NeRF-based avatar modeling trend, arguing that implicit radiance fields entangle geometry, appearance, and lighting in ways that preclude physically-based rendering and editing. It builds on DMTet for differentiable mesh extraction from SDF fields, FITE's skinning-weight diffusion for associating canonical geometry with posed space, Animatable Gaussians' 2D UNet for high-frequency detail generation, and Hasselgren et al.'s Monte-Carlo PBR renderer for shadow-aware material decomposition. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between early mesh-based avatar work (which required per-frame reconstruction or pre-scanned templates) and modern implicit representations by learning an explicit triangular mesh end-to-end from multi-view video without any initial geometry guess.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in producing avatars that are directly compatible with traditional graphics engines—enabling texture editing, material manipulation, and relighting without retraining—capabilities that NeRF-based avatars fundamentally lack. The paper acknowledges two important limitations: the pose-dependent material field is a pragmatic workaround for mesh-resolution errors rather than a physically correct solution, and loose garments with complex non-rigid deformations remain challenging. Open questions include whether the hybrid explicit-implicit representation can scale to more complex clothing (e.g., flowing dresses), whether the stereo-based normal supervision generalizes beyond controlled multi-view setups, and whether the MC rendering cost can be further reduced for real-time applications.
