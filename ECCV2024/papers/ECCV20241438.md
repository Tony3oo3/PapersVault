## Problem

Source-free domain adaptation for object detection (SFOD) requires adapting a pre-trained detector to an unlabeled target domain without access to source data. Existing SFOD methods rely on complex teacher-student architectures with diverse alignment, regularization, and pseudo-label selection strategies, yet suffer from training collapse and require careful hyperparameter tuning. This paper asks whether substantially simpler self-training strategies can match or exceed these complex approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241438/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained Faster-RCNN detector (VGG16-BN backbone, ImageNet-initialized) and a set of unlabeled target-domain RGB images, with no source-domain data accessible at adaptation time. Pseudo-labels are filtered by a fixed confidence threshold of 0.8 on category scores, and the model outputs standard Faster-RCNN predictions: bounding boxes with class labels evaluated at 0.5 IoU. The base detector uses batch size 4 and is trained with SGD at learning rate 0.0025 for target-domain adaptation.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary strategies. First, SF-UT adapts the Unbiased Teacher to the source-free setting: a teacher network generates pseudo-labels from weakly-augmented target inputs, a student trains on strongly-augmented inputs using the full Faster-RCNN loss (including regression terms, unlike prior SFOD work), and the teacher is updated via EMA (α=0.9996). Second, AdaBN + Fixed SF-FM removes the teacher-student feedback loop entirely: batch statistics are first adapted via a single forward pass over the target training set (AdaBN), a fixed set of pseudo-labels is generated once, and the model is then trained on these frozen labels with weak-strong augmentation. The key insight is that BN layers in the backbone are critical for domain-shift robustness, and that fixing pseudo-labels after AdaBN achieves performance nearly matching full mutual learning (44.5 vs 45.0 mAP on Cityscapes→Foggy) while completely eliminating the collapse problem.

## Results

On Cityscapes → Foggy-Cityscapes, SF-UT with VGG16-BN achieves 45.0 mAP, setting a new state-of-the-art for SFOD and outperforming the previous best (RPL, 40.2 mAP) by 4.8 points. The simpler AdaBN + Fixed SF-FM strategy reaches 44.5 mAP, nearly matching SF-UT while avoiding teacher-student collapse. On KITTI → Cityscapes, SF-UT achieves 46.2 AP on car (vs. RPL 47.8, PETS 47.0), and on SIM10k → Cityscapes it reaches 55.4 AP on car (vs. PETS 57.8, RPL 50.1). AdaBN alone, without any self-training, already yields 35.1 mAP on Foggy-Cityscapes, surpassing several full SFOD methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SF-UT (VGG16-BN) achieves 45.0 mAP on Cityscapes → Foggy-Cityscapes, outperforming the previous SFOD state-of-the-art RPL [51] (40.2 mAP) by 4.8 points and HCL [16] (39.7) by 5.3 points. On KITTI → Cityscapes, SF-UT (46.2 AP) is slightly behind RPL (47.8) and PETS (47.0) but surpasses all other SFOD methods including IRG (45.7) and A²SFOD (44.9). On SIM10k → Cityscapes, SF-UT (55.4 AP) trails only PETS (57.8) by 2.4 points while outperforming RPL (50.1) by 5.3 points. The simpler AdaBN + Fixed SF-FM strategy (44.5 mAP on Foggy) nearly matches SF-UT (45.0) while being the only method with stable training and no collapse risk. Notably, AdaBN alone (35.1 mAP) already exceeds several full SFOD methods including SED (30.6), ESOD (31.9), and A²SFOD (35.4), highlighting the underexploited role of batch normalization adaptation in prior SFOD work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a simplification of the Source-Free Object Detection (SFOD) literature, which has predominantly adopted increasingly complex Mean Teacher variants with adversarial alignment, contrastive losses, and elaborate pseudo-label selection (SED, HCL, SOAP, LODS, A2SFOD, IRG, RPL, PETS). It builds directly on the Unbiased Teacher (UT) framework for semi-supervised detection and the FixMatch/Pseudo-Label self-training paradigm, adapting them to the source-free setting. A key departure from prior SFOD work is the use of a VGG16-BN backbone (with batch normalization) rather than the BN-free VGG16 used in most previous methods, and the demonstration that AdaBN alone constitutes a strong baseline that prior SFOD papers overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can replace complex multi-stage teacher-student pipelines with a single forward pass (AdaBN) plus training on fixed pseudo-labels, eliminating the well-known collapse problem while retaining competitive accuracy. The finding that the teacher-student feedback loop contributes little beyond what fixed pseudo-labels + weak-strong augmentation provide challenges the field's default assumption that mutual learning is essential for SFOD. A key limitation is the restriction to BN-based architectures; modern backbones (ConvNeXt, Transformers) use GroupNorm or LayerNorm, and adapting those for domain shift remains unexplored. The scope is also limited to driving-scene datasets and a single detector (Faster-RCNN), leaving open whether these simplification principles generalize to other domains and architectures.
