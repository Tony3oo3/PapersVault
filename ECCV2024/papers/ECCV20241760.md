## Problem

LiDAR-based tracking-by-attention (TBA) methods consistently underperform tracking-by-detection (TBD) baselines on 3D MOT benchmarks, yet the specific causes of this gap remain unexplored. This paper investigates what holds back LiDAR-based TBA and proposes targeted improvements to close the performance gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241760/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sequences of L=3 consecutive 360-degree LiDAR point clouds (2 Hz from nuScenes) along with ego-motion between frames to update track query positions in the current ego-frame. A VoxelNet backbone converts each scan into a BEV feature map, from which the top 200 heatmap predictions initialize proposal queries. Outputs are 3D bounding boxes with persistent tracking IDs, class labels, and predicted motion trajectories, with tracking achieved implicitly through the identity of each track query across frames rather than explicit association.

## Method

JDT3D uses a transformer decoder that processes concatenated proposal queries (initialized from a BEV heatmap) and track queries (propagated from the previous frame via a future reasoning module that predicts motion and projects queries forward). Two key innovations address the TBA gap: (1) track sampling augmentation, which injects temporally consistent multi-frame tracks from a database into training sequences to enrich sparse LiDAR supervision while preserving temporal coherence, and (2) confidence-based query propagation, where queries exceeding a threshold τ_pass=0.4 are passed to the next frame during both training and inference, naturally generating false positive/negative track query cases that improve robustness. Training is two-stage: 20 epochs of single-frame detection pretraining followed by 20 epochs of three-frame tracking fine-tuning, with a two-stage GT assignment that first matches track queries to their previously assigned objects and then Hungarian-matches proposal queries to remaining ground truths.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set, JDT3D achieves an AMOTA of 0.574 and AMOTP of 0.837, outperforming all existing LiDAR-based tracking-by-attention methods by over 6% on AMOTA. It also achieves the lowest number of identity switches (IDS = 254) among all LiDAR-only methods, indicating strong track coherence. However, JDT3D still underperforms the top TBD baselines (CenterPoint at 0.638 AMOTA, SimpleTrack at 0.668 AMOTA), with the gap attributed primarily to higher false negatives (29,919 vs. ~23,000 for TBD methods) due to reduced detection performance when fine-tuning on multi-frame sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LiDAR-based TBA methods, JDT3D outperforms MotionTrack-L (0.574 vs. 0.510 AMOTA, +12.5% relative) and MotionTrack-LC (0.574 vs. 0.550 AMOTA, +4.4% relative), also achieving far fewer ID switches (254 vs. 9,705 and 8,716 respectively). Against camera-only TBA methods, JDT3D surpasses DQTrack (0.574 vs. 0.523 AMOTA) and PF-Track (0.574 vs. 0.434 AMOTA) despite using only LiDAR input. Against TBD baselines, JDT3D trails CenterPoint (0.574 vs. 0.638 AMOTA) and SimpleTrack (0.574 vs. 0.668 AMOTA), but achieves substantially fewer ID switches (254 vs. 760 and 575), suggesting the tracking association itself is stronger while detection recall is the bottleneck. The paper confirms this by showing that when JDT3D's own detections are fed to CenterPoint or SimpleTrack trackers, the joint TBA approach still yields better tracking (0.621 AMOTA on validation), isolating the performance gap to detection quality rather than association logic.

## Relation to Prior Work

JDT3D extends the tracking-by-attention (TBA) paradigm from TrackFormer and MOTRv2 to the LiDAR domain, building on TransFusion's query-based 3D detection architecture and PF-Track's past/future reasoning modules. It departs from prior LiDAR TBA work (MotionTrack) by removing explicit affinity-based matching in favor of implicit query-to-prediction association, and introduces two generalizable improvements: temporally consistent track sampling augmentation (adapted from SECOND's object sampling) and confidence-based query propagation (inspired by MOTRv2's 2D approach). The paper positions itself as an empirical study diagnosing why LiDAR TBA lags behind tracking-by-detection methods, rather than proposing a fundamentally new formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic analysis of why LiDAR-based TBA underperforms TBD, identifying 'temporal confusion'—where the decoder must conditionally handle proposal queries differently based on track query presence—as a likely root cause, compounded by the detection performance drop (mAP 0.605→0.399) when fine-tuning from single-frame to multi-frame training. The track sampling augmentation and confidence-based propagation are practical, generalizable techniques that could benefit other LiDAR TBA methods, though the 6%+ improvement still leaves a substantial gap to TBD SOTA (0.574 vs 0.668 AMOTA). Open questions include whether larger transformer decoders (constrained by LiDAR memory footprint) can resolve temporal confusion, and whether the TBA formulation itself needs rethinking for sparse-sensor settings.
