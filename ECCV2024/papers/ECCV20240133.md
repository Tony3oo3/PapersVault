## Problem

Existing video depth estimation methods rely on expensive cost volumes, optical flow estimation, or pairwise cross-attention across all source frames, and none jointly predict depths for consecutive frames while learning underlying object motion and trajectories. This results in suboptimal temporal consistency and high computational cost, making it difficult to achieve both accuracy and efficiency simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240133/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes T=4 consecutive RGB video frames (e.g., 375×1241 on KITTI, 870×1920 on DDAD) as input, with variable inter-frame intervals (1–4) sampled during training to expose the network to diverse motion ranges. Each frame is independently encoded, and the resulting feature maps are concatenated along the time and channel dimensions into a single feature volume of shape H'×W'×(T·C). The output is a set of T per-frame depth maps, refined iteratively N=3 times by a progressive refinement network.

## Method

FutureDepth introduces two auxiliary networks that operate on the multi-frame feature volume. The Future Prediction Network (F-Net) is trained with multi-step auto-regressive prediction: it iteratively predicts the entire feature volume one time step ahead for L steps without teacher forcing, forcing it to extract robust motion and correspondence cues; at inference, last-layer features from all prediction steps are averaged to produce a motion query Q_motion. The Reconstruction Network (R-Net) is trained via adaptive masked auto-encoding, where a learnable mask generator (trained with a SILog depth loss) masks different parts of the same object across frames, compelling R-Net to exploit cross-frame correspondences; it produces a scene query Q_scene. These two queries are cross-attended to form Q_all, which is injected into a transformer-based depth decoder (using Skip Attention Modules) and a refinement network (self-attention + cross-attention layers) that is applied N=3 times to progressively sharpen depth details. Training proceeds in four stages: pretraining the encoder-decoder, training R-Net with random masking, jointly training F-Net/R-Net/mask generator with frozen encoder-decoder, and finally training the encoder-decoder-refinement pipeline with frozen F-Net and R-Net.

## Results

On KITTI (Eigen split), FutureDepth with Swin-L achieves Abs Rel 0.044, RMSE 1.920, and δ<1.25 0.983, outperforming the previous SOTA video method MAMo (Abs Rel 0.049, RMSE 1.989) and NVDS (Abs Rel 0.052, RMSE 2.101). On NYUDv2, FutureDepth reaches δ<1.25 0.981 and Abs Rel 0.063, reducing depth error by over 12% relative to NVDS and MAMo. Temporal consistency (OPW) improves to 0.281 on KITTI, a 16%+ reduction over MAMo (0.328) and NVDS (0.356), while runtime is only 49 ms on an RTX-3080—comparable to monocular models and far faster than NVDS (930 ms) or ManyDepth (488 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FutureDepth outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the two strongest video-depth competitors—MAMo [52] and NVDS [48]—FutureDepth (Swin-L) reduces Abs Rel from 0.049/0.052 to 0.044 on KITTI, from 0.074/0.072 to 0.063 on NYUDv2, and from 0.150/0.174 to 0.114 on DDAD, while also improving OPW temporal consistency by 14–27%. Versus cost-volume methods (ManyDepth-FS [49], TC-Depth-FS [36]), FutureDepth achieves substantially lower RMSE (1.920 vs. 2.747/3.222 on KITTI) at a fraction of the runtime (49 ms vs. 488/376 ms). Against older recurrent and flow-based methods (ST-CLSTM [54], FMNet [47], FlowGRU [10], RDE-MV [32], STAD [20], NeuralRGB [24], Cao et al. [6], Flow2Depth [50]), FutureDepth improves Abs Rel by 55–160% on KITTI. On zero-shot Sintel, FutureDepth (δ<1.25 0.623, Abs Rel 0.296) surpasses NVDS (0.591, 0.335) and MAMo (0.579, 0.358), confirming stronger generalization. The paper's own multi-frame baseline (Swin-L, Abs Rel 0.053) is improved by 17% by adding F-Net, R-Net, and the refinement network, isolating the contribution of the proposed modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

FutureDepth builds on the Skip Attention Module decoder from PixelFormer [1] and positions itself against two families of video depth methods: cost-volume approaches (ManyDepth, TC-Depth) that are memory-intensive, and attention/flow-based methods (NVDS, MAMo) that require expensive pairwise cross-attention or online gradient computation. Its key conceptual departure is repurposing auto-regressive future feature prediction (without teacher forcing) as an implicit motion-learning mechanism, rather than explicitly estimating optical flow or constructing cost volumes. The R-Net's adaptive masked auto-encoding borrows from the MAE paradigm but applies it as an auxiliary inference-time network rather than a pretraining stage, a distinction the authors emphasize relative to video MAE methods like [44, 45].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-accuracy tradeoff: FutureDepth achieves SOTA accuracy on KITTI, NYUDv2, DDAD, and Sintel while running at 49 ms (vs. 930 ms for NVDS and 122 ms for MAMo), making video depth estimation feasible on resource-constrained platforms like those targeted by Qualcomm. The modular design—where F-Net and R-Net plug into any base encoder-decoder—suggests the approach could be adopted as a drop-in enhancement for existing monocular depth pipelines. Open questions include whether the auto-regressive unrolling (L=T=4 steps) scales to longer temporal contexts, whether the adaptive masking strategy remains effective in highly dynamic or occluded scenes, and whether the zero-shot generalization to Sintel holds for truly out-of-distribution domains.
