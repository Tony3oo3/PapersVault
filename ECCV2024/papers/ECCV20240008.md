## Problem

Existing V2X roadside perception datasets are predominantly urban, focus on intersections, and support only MONO 3D detection due to lack of synchronized multi-sensor data. No large-scale highway dataset exists that provides BEV-space ground truth, sequential tracking across multiple roadside sensors, and HDMap integration, leaving highway BEV perception unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240008/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each timestamp provides five synchronized camera views (two 70mm long-range, two 12mm short-range pinhole at 1920×1080, and one 1.27mm fisheye at 1280×1280, all at 10 Hz) plus an HDMap vector map of 3D lane points and radar-derived velocity/heading. The detection output is a ten-tuple per object (class, confidence, x, y, z, w, l, h, yaw, color) in a local BEV coordinate system spanning ±500 m around the mast—far exceeding the ±60–75 m range of typical autonomous driving BEV tasks. Tracking outputs assign a single persistent ID to each vehicle across all sensors over its full visible lifespan, and prediction outputs future (x, y) positions over a 1–5 s horizon.

## Method

The H-BEV detection model extends BEVDet by replacing the learned discrete depth distribution with a single depth value derived from HDMap lane geometry, reducing the per-pixel frustum point cloud by a factor of ~500 and enabling unified projection for both pinhole and fisheye cameras via 3D lane interpolation. Ground truth is generated through a pipeline of semi-supervised 2D detection (mAP 0.95@IoU=0.5), 2D tracking, vector-map-constrained 2D-to-3D projection, cross-sensor Hungarian matching, and EKF smoothing, followed by human validation to remove ghost trajectories and ID breaks. For trajectory prediction, HD-GAN augments SocialGAN with an HDMap encoder that interpolates an object's global lane position into [0,1] and fuses it with local trajectory features, improving long-horizon accuracy. A joint 2D-3D calibration protocol (chessboard intrinsics + human extrinsic alignment + daily automated bundle adjustment against segmented visual lanes) ensures consistent projection across all masts.

## Results

On the H-V2X dataset, the proposed H-BEV early-fusion model achieves 35.49 mAP3D|R40 and 38.40 mAPbev|R40 at 6 FPS, outperforming the Late Fusion baseline (31.75 / 33.24 mAP at 10 FPS). For One-ID MOT, the SORT-based baseline achieves MOTA of 0.85, MOTP of 0.95, and IDS of 0.05. For trajectory prediction, the proposed HD-GAN reduces ADE from 2.683 m to 2.457 m and FDE from 5.833 m to 5.188 m at the 5-second horizon compared to vanilla SocialGAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For trajectory prediction, HD-GAN consistently outperforms vanilla SocialGAN [10] across all prediction horizons (1 s–5 s), reducing ADE by 1.1%–8.4% and FDE by 0.4%–11.1%, with the gap widening as the prediction horizon extends (e.g., FDE drops from 5.833 m to 5.188 m at 5 s). This confirms that incorporating global HDMap positional features provides increasingly valuable context for longer-horizon predictions. For BEV detection, the proposed H-BEV early-fusion model surpasses the Late Fusion pipeline by 3.74 mAP3D and 5.16 mAPbev, while trading 4 FPS of speed for improved robustness to occlusion and calibration drift. The MOT baseline (SORT + Kalman filter) achieves MOTA 0.85 / MOTP 0.95 / IDS 0.05, establishing a reference point but highlighting that highway-scale tracking with high-speed motion and extended occlusions remains challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing V2X roadside perception literature (DAIR-V2X, Rope3D, V2X-Seq, A9-Dataset), which predominantly targets urban intersections and supports only mono-3D detection. It departs from these by introducing a highway-specific, multi-sensor BEV-space formulation with unified cross-sensor ID tracking, and by constructing ground truth without LiDAR (impractical on highway masts) through a novel camera-only projection pipeline. Methodologically, the H-BEV detector extends BEVDet by replacing learned depth distributions with HDMap-derived single-depth projection (reducing feature map size by 500×) and handling fisheye cameras, while HD-GAN extends SocialGAN with a global map positional encoding. The MOT baseline adapts SORT with a Kalman filter for high-speed highway dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

H-V2X fills a clear gap: no prior large-scale, real-sensor highway roadside dataset existed with synchronized multi-camera data, vector maps, and BEV-space ground truth, making end-to-end highway BEV perception research previously infeasible. The camera-only GT generation pipeline (2D detection → 2D-to-3D projection → cross-sensor Hungarian matching → EKF smoothing) is a practical contribution given LiDAR's impracticality on highway infrastructure. Open questions include whether the 4-class taxonomy and ±500 m detection range adequately capture highway complexity, how the benchmarks scale with richer architectures, and whether the upcoming traffic-event task (accidents, illegal parking) will shift the research focus toward rare-event detection. The relatively simple baselines (YOLO, SORT, SocialGAN) leave substantial headroom for future methodological advances.
