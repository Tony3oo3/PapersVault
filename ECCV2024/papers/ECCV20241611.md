## Problem

Diffusion models fine-tuned on few-shot reference sets overfit and produce low-diversity samples that closely replicate the training images, while large-scale pre-trained models can also reproduce their training data, raising copyright and privacy concerns. Existing diversity methods (e.g., CADS) do not explicitly prevent replication of a specific reference set, and prior few-shot personalization methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) do not address memorization or sample diversity at inference time.

## Input / Output

ProCreate takes a text prompt and a set of reference images (the 10-shot fine-tuning set in the few-shot experiments, or a CLIP-filtered 10k subset of LAION12M in the replication-prevention experiments) and produces a 512×512 generated image via Stable Diffusion v1-5. The reference set is not static: newly generated samples are appended to it after each batch, so the guidance target grows over the course of a generation run. In the large-scale experiment, the full 12M-image reference set is pre-filtered per prompt using CLIP cosine similarity and FAISS vector search to keep the per-step computation tractable.

## Method

ProCreate augments standard DDIM sampling with an energy-based guidance term that propels the generated image away from the closest reference image in DreamSim embedding space. At each denoising step, a Multi-Step Look Ahead (n_step DDIM iterations) predicts a clean image x̂₀; the log-energy g is defined as γ·max_i cos_sim(f(x̂₀), f(x_i^r)) using DreamSim as the similarity network f, and its gradient with respect to x_t is added to the noise prediction (Eq. 4), mirroring the classifier-guidance formulation but in the repulsive direction. The reference set dynamically grows with each newly generated batch, so successive samples are also pushed apart from one another. A gradient-norm clipping hyperparameter γ controls guidance strength, and the method is plug-and-play on top of any fine-tuned diffusion checkpoint (standard fine-tuning or DreamBooth) without retraining.

## Results

On the FSCG-8 few-shot creative generation benchmark (10-shot, standard fine-tuning), ProCreate achieves the best FID across all 8 categories, with notable improvements such as 9.28 on Amedeo (vs. 16.71 DDIM, 12.83 CADS) and 14.10 on Burberry (vs. 35.11 DDIM, 37.71 CADS). ProCreate also leads on diversity metrics (Recall, MSS, Vendi) and KID while remaining competitive on Precision and Prompt Fidelity. On the LAION12M replication-prevention task, ProCreate reduces the percentage of generated images exceeding Top-1 SSCD thresholds by more than half compared to DDIM, while simultaneously improving FID (1.14 vs. 1.38) and KID (0.038 vs. 0.051).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProCreate is compared against two baselines: default DDIM sampling and CADS (condition-annealed sampling). On FSCG-8 with standard fine-tuning, ProCreate outperforms both DDIM and CADS on FID, KID, Recall, MSS, and Vendi across nearly all 8 categories, with the largest FID gains on Burberry (14.10 vs. 35.11/37.71) and Apple (24.59 vs. 48.51/38.88). ProCreate is competitive on Precision and Prompt Fidelity, indicating that the diversity gains do not come at the cost of prompt adherence. The same pattern holds under DreamBooth fine-tuning (Table 2), where ProCreate again leads on FID, KID, Recall, MSS, and Vendi. On the LAION12M replication-prevention task, ProCreate reduces the fraction of generated images exceeding Top-1 SSCD thresholds by more than 50% relative to DDIM, while also improving FID (1.14 vs. 1.38) and KID (0.038 vs. 0.051); CADS, by contrast, worsens both FID and KID relative to DDIM on this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProCreate repurposes the classifier-guidance mechanism from Dhariwal et al. [8] and Bansal et al. [3]—which adds a gradient term to steer generation *toward* a desired class—into an energy-based repulsive force that steers generation *away* from a reference image set. The Multi-Step Look Ahead prediction for obtaining a reliable x̂₀ estimate is directly borrowed from DOODL [48], and the entire pipeline operates within the DDIM [45] sampling framework. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of energy-based model guidance and the data-replication literature (Somepalli et al. [43,44]), but departs from prior diversity methods like CADS [37] by explicitly conditioning on a reference set rather than annealing conditioning, and from DreamBooth/Textual Inversion by operating purely at inference time without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method is a simple, plug-and-play inference-time modification applicable to any pre-trained or fine-tuned diffusion model, making it immediately practical for designers and content creators concerned about copyright replication. The FSCG-8 dataset fills a gap in few-shot creative generation benchmarks by curating categories with shared aesthetic/conceptual properties rather than mere semantic labels. Key limitations include substantial inference overhead (37s vs 3.1s per sample at n_step=5), sensitivity to the guidance strength γ, and evaluation restricted to a single model architecture (Stable Diffusion v1-5) and a single large-scale dataset (LAION12M). Open questions include whether the approach scales to video/audio modalities, whether alternative similarity metrics beyond DreamSim would be more robust, and whether the dynamic reference-set growth strategy remains effective at very large generation counts.
