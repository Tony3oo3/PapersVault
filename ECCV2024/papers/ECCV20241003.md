## Problem

Existing SSDA and UDA methods struggle to disentangle domain-agnostic (task-specific) features from domain-specific (style/texture) features, as their primary focus on minimizing classification loss inadvertently promotes acquisition of more domain-specific representations rather than the domain-agnostic ones needed for effective adaptation. Additionally, naive alignment of source and target distributions is difficult when domain gaps are large, and the skewed training distribution in SSDA further biases learning toward the source domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241003/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain RGB images, a small set of labeled target images (1-shot or 3-shot in SSDA; none in UDA), and a large pool of unlabeled target images from benchmarks like Office-Home (65 classes, 4 domains) and DomainNet (345 classes, 6 domains). At inference, only the U-Net reconstruction network, the target-domain encoder, and a domain-agnostic classifier are deployed to map a target image to a class label. The reconstruction network internally transforms each input image into a new image space where domain-specific attributes are suppressed before feature extraction.

## Method

The core contribution is an episodic 'learn, forget, then learn more' training strategy. First, two domain-specific encoder-classifier pairs are trained by minimizing cross-entropy on in-domain images while maximizing entropy (classification uncertainty) on out-of-domain images, explicitly encoding domain-specific features. Second, a U-Net reconstruction network maps images to a new space; the reconstruction loss (L2) preserves structural similarity while an adversarial unlearning loss maximizes misclassification through the frozen domain-specific classifiers, thereby stripping domain-specific attributes. Third, a Gaussian-guided Latent Alignment (GLA) strategy aligns the source and target latent distributions to a shared Gaussian prior via KL divergence, complemented by a supervised classification loss on labeled samples to enforce discriminative domain-agnostic features. The overall objective jointly optimizes the reconstruction network and domain-agnostic classifier, with hyperparameters β=10 and γ=0.1 balancing structural retention against domain-specific removal.

## Results

On Office-Home SSDA (ResNet-34), the proposed method achieves 74.8% average accuracy in the 1-shot setting and 77.9% in the 3-shot setting, outperforming the strongest baseline CDAC+SLA (72.9% and 76.3% respectively). On the more challenging DomainNet SSDA benchmark, it reaches 78.7% (1-shot) and 81.2% (3-shot), surpassing DEEM (78.1% and 79.5%). In UDA settings, the method achieves 88.8% average on Office-Home with ViT backbone (matching PMTrans-ViT) and 69.5% on DomainNet with ViT, outperforming all prior UDA baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 32 compared baselines across both SSDA and UDA settings. In SSDA on Office-Home, it beats the strongest baseline CDAC+SLA by 1.9% (1-shot) and 1.6% (3-shot) in average accuracy, with the largest margin on A→C (+1.1% 1-shot, +1.5% 3-shot) and R→P (+3.3% 1-shot, +1.4% 3-shot). On the harder DomainNet SSDA benchmark, it surpasses DEEM (the second-best) by 0.6% (1-shot) and 1.7% (3-shot), with particularly large gains on C→S (+1.8% 1-shot) and S→P (+1.5% 1-shot). In UDA, the method achieves the best or tied-best average with ViT on Office-Home (88.8%, matching PMTrans-ViT) and Swin (89.3% vs. PMTrans-Swin's 89.0%), and leads on DomainNet with ViT (69.5% vs. PMTrans's 68.7%). The method consistently wins on domain pairs with large visual gaps (e.g., sketch, painting), supporting the claim that unlearning domain-specific features is most beneficial when domain disparity is high.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in domain adaptation—where domain-agnostic representations are acquired incidentally through distribution alignment (adversarial, MMD, optimal transport)—by making the explicit learning and subsequent unlearning of domain-specific features the central mechanism. The 'learn, forget, then learn more' episodic strategy draws conceptual inspiration from episodic training for domain generalization [15], but repurposes it: rather than training across multiple domains, it first trains domain-specific encoder-classifier pairs, then adversarially unlearns those features via a reconstruction network, and finally aligns the residual domain-agnostic representations through a novel Gaussian-guided latent alignment (GLA) that uses a prior distribution as a bridge. The GLA strategy itself extends prior alignment work [50,58] by introducing a third (prior) distribution to simplify the alignment problem, and the reconstruction-based unlearning mechanism has no direct precedent in the SSDA/UDA literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's core insight—that actively unlearning domain-specific features (via a reconstruction network that maximizes misclassification under frozen domain-specific classifiers) is more critical than distribution alignment alone, as evidenced by the 6–10% ablation drop when removing L^UL—challenges the field's default assumption that better alignment automatically yields better domain-agnostic features. However, the method's practical adoption may be limited by its multi-stage training pipeline, the additional U-Net reconstruction module, and the sensitivity to hyperparameters (β, γ). The authors acknowledge future extensions to source-free and test-time adaptation, but the current evaluation is confined to two image classification benchmarks with moderate (1–3%) gains over the strongest baselines, leaving open whether the episodic unlearning strategy generalizes to other modalities, larger-scale datasets, or more extreme domain shifts.
