## Problem

One-step text-to-image diffusion models have consistently underperformed their multi-step teacher models in FID, leading to the prevailing belief that a single-step student can at best match but never exceed its teacher. This paper challenges that assumption by identifying a quality-diversity trade-off between existing one-step methods (SD Turbo excels in precision but suffers mode collapse; SwiftBrush offers diversity but poor quality) and demonstrating that a one-step student can surpass its multi-step teacher in all metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242191/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single text prompt as input and produces a 256×256 RGB image in one UNet forward pass (1 NFE), matching the inference speed of SD Turbo and SwiftBrush. Notably, training is entirely image-free—only text prompts are required (1.3M from JourneyDB, optionally expanded to 3.3M with LAION prompts)—making the pipeline scalable without costly image-text pair collection. An optional post-training regularization step uses 200K image-text pairs from LAION-Aesthetic-6.25+ for further quality refinement.

## Method

The method builds on SwiftBrush's Variational Score Distillation (VSD) framework but introduces three key modifications: (1) initializing the one-step student with SD Turbo's pretrained weights to inherit high-quality adversarial features while SwiftBrush's image-free training restores diversity; (2) a clamped CLIP loss (Eq. 5) that applies a ReLU margin τ=0.35 on cosine similarity between decoded image and text embeddings, combined with a dynamic weight scheduler that decays to zero, preventing the blurriness and color artifacts of naive CLIP optimization; and (3) a resource-efficient LoRA training scheme using TinyVAE to compute the CLIP loss without full VAE decoding. Two student models are trained under different schemes (full VSD training vs. LoRA with VSD+CLIP), then merged via linear weight interpolation (λ=0.5), yielding a model with FID-30K of 8.77 that surpasses its SDv2.1 teacher (FID 9.64 at cfg=2). An optional DMD-style image regularization on minimal real data further reduces FID to 8.14.

## Results

On the zero-shot MS COCO-2014 30K benchmark, SwiftBrush v2 achieves an FID of 8.77 (merged model) and 8.14 (with additional image regularization), surpassing its multi-step teacher SDv2.1 (FID 9.64 at cfg=2) and all prior one-step diffusion models. The method also achieves a CLIP score of 0.32, precision of 0.57, and recall of 0.52 (Ours*), matching or exceeding the teacher on all metrics. In HPSv2 human preference evaluation, Ours* scores 27.62 on Photo (highest among all methods) and 27.25 on Anime, competitive with SD Turbo (27.98) and SDv2.1 (27.48).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwiftBrush v2 (Ours*) achieves FID 8.14, outperforming the best GAN-based model GigaGAN (9.09) by 0.95 points and the multi-step teacher SDv2.1 at cfg=2 (9.64) by 1.5 points, while requiring only 1 NFE versus 25. Among one-step diffusion baselines, it beats DMD (11.49), MD-UFOGen (11.67), UFOGen (12.78), InstaFlow-0.9B (13.33), HiPA (13.91), SwiftBrush (15.46), and SD Turbo (16.10) by margins of 3.35 to 7.96 FID points. On CLIP score, Ours* (0.32) matches DMD and SDv2.1 (cfg=2) but trails SD Turbo (0.33) and SDv2.1 (cfg=4.5) by 0.01. On precision, SD Turbo (0.65) and SDv2.1 cfg=4.5 (0.61) still lead, but Ours* (0.57) matches SDv2.1 cfg=2 (0.57) and exceeds all other one-step methods. On recall, Ours (0.53) ties SDv1.5 and SDv2.1 cfg=2, while Ours* (0.52) is slightly lower, reflecting a minor diversity cost from image regularization. In HPSv2, Ours* leads on Photo (27.62) and Concept Art (26.86), ties SDv2.1 on Painting (26.77 vs 27.46—actually SDv2.1 leads), and trails SD Turbo on Anime (27.25 vs 27.98) and Photo (27.62 vs 27.59—actually Ours* leads).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SwiftBrush's image-free Variational Score Distillation framework and SD Turbo's adversarial diffusion distillation, identifying a quality-diversity trade-off between the two (SD Turbo: high precision, low recall; SwiftBrush: low precision, high recall). Rather than unifying their training objectives, the authors take a simpler route: initializing SwiftBrush's student with SD Turbo's pretrained weights and augmenting the VSD loss with a clamped CLIP loss for text alignment. The final model is produced by linear weight interpolation between a fully-finetuned student and a LoRA-finetuned student, drawing on the model-soup/weight-merging literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first one-step diffusion model to quantitatively surpass its multi-step teacher (FID 8.14 vs. 9.64 for SDv2.1) while maintaining single-NFE inference, which is practically significant for real-time and on-device text-to-image applications. The quality-diversity trade-off analysis provides a useful diagnostic framework for comparing one-step distillation methods. Limitations include inherited compositional weaknesses from the teacher and evaluation restricted to the SDv2.1 scale; whether the approach transfers to larger architectures (SDXL, etc.) or addresses compositional reasoning remains open.
