## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models require numerous human interventions per image to reach strong classification performance because existing approaches treat each concept intervention independently—correcting one concept has no effect on the predicted values of all other concepts. This ignores the natural statistical correlations between concepts (e.g., 'white wing' and 'white belly' co-occur), meaning human feedback is not leveraged to its full potential and intervention budgets remain impractically high in resource-constrained settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image processed by a pre-trained concept encoder into a k-dimensional concept vector, plus a sequential stream of human interventions where an expert replaces specific concept predictions with ground-truth binary values. At each intervention step, the realignment module outputs an updated k-dimensional concept vector in which intervened concepts retain their user-provided ground-truth values while all non-intervened concepts are re-predicted; this realigned vector is then fed to the classification head to produce the final class label. The module operates purely on the concept-level representation (no pixel access), making it a lightweight post-hoc or joint-training add-on to any existing CBM/CEM backbone.

## Method

The Concept Intervention Realignment Module (CIRM) consists of two interdependent components: a concept realignment model (CRM) parameterized as a multi-layer perceptron (or LSTM for a recurrent variant) that maps the intervened concept vector to updated predictions for all non-intervened concepts, and an intervention policy (UCP by default) that selects the next concept to intervene on using the realigned predictions rather than the original model outputs. The CRM is trained with a cross-entropy loss against ground-truth concept labels averaged over all T intervention steps, learning to exploit statistical concept co-occurrence relations so that verifying one concept automatically adjusts the predicted probabilities of related concepts. Crucially, the output of the CRM is masked so that user-provided ground-truth values for already-intervened concepts are never overwritten (Eq. 2). The module can be deployed post-hoc by freezing a pre-trained CBM/CEM and training only the CRM, or jointly with the base model by modifying the IntCEM training objective (Eq. 3–4) to include a realignment-aware concept loss that trains both the backbone and the CRM simultaneously.

## Results

On CUB, concept realignment reduces the concept prediction loss AUC from 6.71 to 3.15 (Sequential CBM) and from 5.99 to 3.20 (CEM), while increasing classification accuracy AUC from 2460.8 to 2510.9 and 2521.4 to 2558.4, respectively. On AwA2, the concept loss AUC drops dramatically from 4.26 to 1.13 (Sequential CBM) and 4.90 to 1.69 (CEM), corresponding to a >70% reduction in interventions needed to reach a target concept prediction accuracy. On CUB, 93.9% classification accuracy is reached after ~16 interventions with realignment versus ~24 without, and on AwA2, 98% accuracy requires only 12 interventions versus 19 without realignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIRM consistently outperforms all three baseline families on concept prediction loss AUC (lower is better) across all datasets. Against standard CBMs (Koh et al., 2020), the largest gains appear on AwA2 where concept loss AUC drops from 4.26 to 1.13 (Sequential) and 4.77 to 1.48 (Joint), while on CUB it halves from 6.71 to 3.15. Against CEMs (Zarlenga et al., 2022), CIRM reduces CUB concept loss AUC from 5.99 to 3.20 and AwA2 from 4.90 to 1.69, with accuracy AUC improving from 2521.4 to 2558.4 on CUB. Against IntCEMs (Zarlenga et al., 2023), the gains are smaller but still significant: a fivefold concept loss reduction (0.5→0.1) requires only 11 interventions with CIRM versus 20 without. The gains are most pronounced on datasets with richer concept sets (CUB, AwA2) and smallest on CelebA, where the limited 8-concept bottleneck already constrains the model's capacity. In all cases, CIRM is applied as a post-hoc module on top of the frozen base model, requiring no architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) and Concept Embedding Model (CEM) lineage by addressing a specific limitation in how test-time human interventions are processed: prior methods (CBMs, CEMs, and Intervention-aware CEMs) treat each concept intervention independently, so correcting one concept leaves all others unchanged. The proposed Concept Intervention Realignment Module (CIRM) departs from this by learning statistical concept co-occurrence relations to update all non-intervened concept predictions after each intervention step. It is positioned as a lightweight, architecture-agnostic add-on that integrates post-hoc or jointly with existing CBM/CEM/IntCEM training pipelines, rather than replacing the underlying concept encoder or classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >70% reduction in required human interventions to reach target performance is practically significant for deploying concept-based models in resource-constrained or expert-limited settings, where each intervention carries real annotation cost. The versatility of CIRM (post-hoc or joint training, MLP or LSTM, compatible with multiple base architectures and selection policies) makes it a broadly applicable plug-in. Open questions include whether the learned concept correlations generalize to open-vocabulary or dynamically defined concept sets, and whether the approach's gains persist when concept annotations are noisy or incomplete (as partially evidenced by the weaker CelebA results).
