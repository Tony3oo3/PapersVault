## Problem

Rendering view-consistent multi-task labels (segmentation, normals, edges, keypoints, reshading) at novel views in real time remains unsolved. NeRF-based multi-task methods are too slow (≤0.08 FPS) and suffer from floating artifacts in implicit label fields, while naively attaching explicit labels to 3D Gaussians is sensitive to noisy or incomplete training annotations and yields only moderate quality. The paper asks whether 3D Gaussian Splatting can be extended to jointly render all these labels at interactive speeds without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240524/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initial point cloud plus a set of source views (480×640) carrying six label channels—RGB, semantic segmentation, surface normal, edge map, keypoint map, and reshading map—along with their camera poses. Notably, edge, keypoint, and reshading labels are not ground truth but are generated by off-the-shelf 2D detectors (Canny, DoG, and a pre-trained reshading model), making the pipeline robust to noisy supervision. At inference, given only a novel camera pose, the model rasterizes all six label maps simultaneously at 35 FPS on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

VersatileGaussian augments each 3D Gaussian with two feature vectors: a view-dependent field encoded as low-degree spherical harmonics (d_v=12) and a view-independent vector (d_n=32), replacing the standard RGB SH coefficients. A customized differentiable rasterizer splats these features into per-pixel feature maps, which are then decoded by task-specific linear projections. The key architectural contribution is the Task Correlation Attention (TCA) module, a pixel-wise multi-head attention that splits each task's feature into channel fragments, computes cross-task attention scores with talking-heads-style inter-head projections, and fuses them via a soft weighting mechanism—allowing every task to borrow cues from related tasks during both forward pass and backpropagation. Tasks are partitioned into view-dependent (RGB, normals, shading) and view-independent (segmentation, keypoints, edges) groups, each reading from the corresponding feature branch. The entire system is trained end-to-end with a weighted multi-task loss, and the cross-task gradient flow through TCA is what distinguishes it from the naive explicit-label baseline, yielding substantial gains especially on noisy tasks like reshading and edge detection.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, VersatileGaussian achieves an overall multi-task accuracy gain of +63.96% (relative to SemanticNeRF baseline) at 34.68 FPS, substantially outperforming all 3D baselines (SS-NeRF: +39.11% at 0.08 FPS; MuvieNeRF: −119.28% at 0.01 FPS). Per-task, it reaches 34.57 PSNR for RGB, 0.96 mIoU for semantic segmentation, and 0.01 L1 for edge detection. On ScanNet, it achieves +54.16% overall at 35.1 FPS with 26.27 PSNR and 0.90 mIoU, again dominating all 3D methods by at least 400× in rendering speed.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VersatileGaussian wins decisively on every 3D baseline across both datasets. Against SS-NeRF (the strongest 3D competitor), it improves overall accuracy from +39.11% to +63.96% on Replica and from +22.41% to +54.16% on ScanNet, while rendering ~430× faster (34.68 vs. 0.08 FPS). MuvieNeRF performs worst among 3D methods (−119.28% overall on Replica), with particularly poor surface-normal L1 (0.81) and semantic mIoU (0.35). Against the 2D baseline invPT, VersatileGaussian matches or exceeds its per-task L1 scores (e.g., KP 0.003 vs. 0.004, ED 0.01 vs. 0.03 on Replica) while additionally producing view-consistent RGB at 34.57 PSNR—something invPT cannot do without ground-truth RGB input. The 3D-GS baseline on Tanks and Temples (23.14 PSNR) is improved to 25.57 PSNR when keypoint and edge tasks are jointly optimized, demonstrating that versatile labels themselves boost novel-view synthesis quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) from its original RGB-only rendering to multi-task label synthesis by replacing per-Gaussian SH color coefficients with shared view-dependent and view-independent feature vectors, rasterized via a modified differentiable splatting pipeline. The cross-task information flow is handled by a Task Correlation Attention module that adapts the Talking-Heads Attention mechanism to a pixel-wise, multi-task setting, drawing on the broader multi-task learning literature on decoder-side feature fusion. It departs from NeRF-based multi-task methods (SS-NeRF, MuvieNeRF) by replacing slow volumetric rendering with explicit Gaussian splatting, and from naive label-attachment extensions of 3D-GS by introducing a learned feature bottleneck that is robust to noisy or incomplete supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VersatileGaussian is the first framework to render multiple dense label maps (segmentation, normals, edges, keypoints, reshading) at real-time speeds (~35 FPS), making multi-task scene understanding viable for interactive AR/VR applications that were previously limited to slow NeRF pipelines. The observation that even noisy labels from off-the-shelf 2D detectors (DoG, Canny) can improve RGB novel-view synthesis by over 2 dB suggests multi-task supervision acts as a useful regularizer even without ground-truth 3D annotations. Open questions include scalability to larger task sets, cross-scene generalization (the method remains per-scene), and whether the approach degrades gracefully when training labels are substantially incomplete or adversarially corrupted.
