## Problem

Existing zero-shot semantic segmentation methods that leverage CLIP with multiple text prompts suffer from passive text-pixel alignment, causing all prompt-driven score maps to be cohered rather than capturing distinct semantic features. This limits the diversity of pixel-level predictions and prevents the ensemble from effectively isolating target objects from background.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242058/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and K class names, each expanded with N hand-crafted text prompts (N=6 for VOC 2012, N=8 for PASCAL Context and COCO-Stuff164K) prepended before the class name token. The image is encoded by a tunable CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder with visual prompt tuning, while the text is processed by a frozen CLIP text encoder. The output is a full-resolution pixel-level segmentation mask (H_I × W_I × K), produced by ensembling a 3-layer transformer decoder prediction with a Sinkhorn-refined score map via a balancing factor λ=0.5.

## Method

OTSeg introduces Multi-Prompts Sinkhorn (MPS), which formulates the text-pixel alignment as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem: the cost matrix C = 1 − S (where S is the cosine-similarity score map between pixel and text embeddings) is solved via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm to yield a doubly stochastic transport plan T* that allocates M pixels to N text prompts. The refined score map S* = M(T* ⊙ S) sums along the prompt dimension, and the doubly stochastic constraint forces each prompt to attend to different spatial regions, producing diverse semantic feature maps. This MPS operation is further extended into Multi-Prompts Sinkhorn Attention (MPSA), which replaces SoftMax normalization in cross-attention with the Sinkhorn operator, using N text-prompt dimensions in place of conventional multi-head dimensions within a 3-layer transformer decoder. Training uses a frozen CLIP text encoder, a tunable image encoder (visual prompt tuning), and a trainable decoder, with a combined focal + dice loss applied to both the decoder output and the refined score map. At inference, OTSeg+ ensembles the decoder prediction and the upsampled refined score map with λ=0.5 to achieve the final segmentation.

## Results

OTSeg+ achieves state-of-the-art hIoU across all three benchmark datasets in both inductive and transductive zero-shot settings. In the inductive setting, OTSeg+ reaches 87.1 hIoU on VOC 2012, 57.7 on PASCAL Context, and 41.5 on COCO-Stuff164K. In the transductive setting, the gains are more pronounced: 94.4 hIoU on VOC 2012, 59.8 on PASCAL Context, and 49.8 on COCO-Stuff164K. The method also matches or exceeds fully-supervised baselines, reaching 94.6 hIoU on VOC 2012 and 65.5 on PASCAL Context.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTSeg+ outperforms the strongest prior baseline ZegCLIP by a substantial margin on hIoU: +2.8 on VOC 2012 (inductive), +7.8 on PASCAL Context (inductive), and +1.3 on COCO-Stuff164K (inductive). In the transductive setting, OTSeg+ leads ZegCLIP by +3.3 hIoU on VOC 2012 (94.4 vs 91.1), +4.2 on PASCAL Context (59.8 vs 55.6), and +1.3 on COCO-Stuff164K (49.8 vs 48.5). Against other transductive baselines, OTSeg+ surpasses MVP-SEG+ by +6.4 hIoU on VOC 2012 and +4.3 on PASCAL Context, and FreeSeg by +7.5 on VOC 2012. The only narrow loss is on PASCAL Context transductive mIoU(U), where ZegCLIP scores 68.5 vs OTSeg's 66.7, though OTSeg+ still wins on the primary hIoU metric. In fully-supervised evaluation, OTSeg+ exceeds ZegCLIP by +3.0 hIoU on VOC 2012 and +8.6 on PASCAL Context, confirming that the Sinkhorn-based alignment generalizes beyond the zero-shot regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sinkformer's replacement of SoftMax with the Sinkhorn algorithm from unimodal self-attention to multimodal cross-attention, introducing Multi-Prompts Sinkhorn Attention (MPSA) as a drop-in module for transformer decoders in text-driven segmentation. It builds on the ZegCLIP framework (frozen CLIP text encoder + visual prompt tuning on the image encoder) and the multi-prompt ensembling paradigm, but departs from naive prompt averaging by formulating text-pixel alignment as an optimal transport problem, so that each of N text prompts is optimally assigned to distinct pixel regions rather than passively cohering. The OT-based matching also connects to PLOT's use of Sinkhorn for prompt-to-feature alignment, but here it operates at the pixel level within a transformer decoder rather than at the image level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OTSeg demonstrates that the Sinkhorn operator is not merely a self-attention trick but a general-purpose multimodal alignment mechanism, potentially applicable to any cross-attention setting where multiple text queries must be distributed over a dense pixel field. The method achieves SOTA on three ZS3 benchmarks with the same parameter count as ZegCLIP (13.8M) and only ~1% additional FLOPs, making it practically deployable. However, the authors note that the semantic meaning of individual text prompts remains uninterpreted, and the approach has not yet been extended to open-vocabulary, instance, or panoptic segmentation—open directions that would test whether the OT-based prompt diversification generalizes beyond dense class-level masks.
