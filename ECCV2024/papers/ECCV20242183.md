## Problem

Self-supervised depth estimation and Structure-from-Motion share the goal of recovering scene depth from RGB video, yet they remain disconnected: self-supervision backpropagates photometric losses within immediate neighboring frames, while SfM requires diverse view variations and suffers from scale ambiguity relative to learned depth models. No prior work has demonstrated that geometry-based self-supervision via SfM can improve SoTA supervised depth estimators, and classic SfM is too unstable for the small (5-frame) windows that self-supervision naturally operates on.

## Input / Output

The method takes N calibrated RGB or RGB-D frames (typically 5 consecutive video frames) and internally infers N monocular depthmaps (e.g., from ZoeDepth) and N×(N-1) pairwise dense correspondence maps (e.g., from PDC-Net). It outputs N-1 camera poses, N-1 per-frame depth adjustment scalars that render all depthmaps temporally consistent in a shared metric space, and a geometrically verified sparse point cloud (2.6%–9.1% density, roughly 10–30k points on a 480×640 image) expressed as a sparse root-frame depthmap. The depth adjustments are a distinctive output: they are the only per-frame scalar needed to align heterogeneous monocular depth estimates into a common 3D coordinate frame.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bundle-RANSAC-Adjustment algorithm that extends two-view RANSAC to a multi-view system. It first extracts K normalized pose candidates per frame pair via the five-point algorithm, then greedily searches over groups of N-1 poses, evaluating each group with a robust inlier-counting scoring function that jointly optimizes camera scales and depth adjustments via bundle adjustment. A key acceleration is a Hough Transform that discretizes the continuous scale/adjustment optimization into a 2D matrix per frame pair, turning the BA consensus step into a matrix-indexing operation and yielding certified global optimality for the scoring function. After poses and adjustments are fixed, a frustum Radiance Field (a NeRF without a neural network, parameterized as a dense occupancy grid) is optimized for multi-view depth and correspondence consistency, and geometric verification renders a sparse multi-view-consistent point cloud. The entire pipeline runs in ~3 minutes on a single GPU for 5 frames, with quadratic complexity that parallelizes across GPUs.

## Results

On ScanNet, the method improves ZoeDepth's δ0.5 from 0.877 to 0.902 and SIlog from 6.655 to 5.901 using only 5 frames of self-supervision. On KITTI360, it improves Metric3D's δ0.5 from 0.846 to 0.860 and SIlog from 9.226 to 8.896. For sparse-view pose estimation (5 frames, ScanNet), the method achieves 100% success rate with PCK-3 of 0.727 and rotation error of 0.422°, outperforming COLMAP (36.7% success, PCK-3 0.584) and DUSt3R (PCK-3 0.594). The method also outperforms SPARF on LLFF (rotation 0.46° vs. 0.53°) while being ~3 minutes vs. one day for SPARF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For depth estimation, the method uniformly improves all three supervised baselines (ZoeDepth, ZeroDepth, Metric3D) on both ScanNet and KITTI360 across every metric, with the largest gains on ZeroDepth (e.g., δ0.5 from 0.641→0.686 on ScanNet, SIlog from 12.860→9.463). For sparse-view pose (5 frames, ScanNet), the method achieves 100% success rate vs. COLMAP's 36.7%, and beats COLMAP (PCK-3 0.584), LightedDepth (0.651), DRO-ScanNet (0.656), and DUSt3R with intrinsic (0.594) on PCK-3, while also achieving the lowest rotation error (0.422°) among all methods. Against NeRF-based pose methods on LLFF, the method outperforms SPARF (rotation 0.46° vs. 0.53°, translation 1.9 vs. 2.8) in ~3 minutes vs. SPARF's one-day optimization, but loses to SPARF on Replica (rotation 0.52° vs. 0.15°), likely due to ZoeDepth not being trained on synthetic data. For correspondence estimation with RGB-D inputs, the method improves PDC-Net's PCK-1 from 0.119 to 0.178 and AEPE from 4.612 to 2.898, also outperforming RoMa (PCK-1 0.144, AEPE 3.333) and LightedDepth (PCK-1 0.061, AEPE 6.590).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: self-supervised depth estimation (which backpropagates photometric losses between immediate neighboring frames) and classic Structure-from-Motion (which requires diverse view variations and operates as an off-the-shelf pipeline). The key departure is replacing the learning-through-loss self-supervision scheme with a complete local SfM pipeline that uses estimated monocular depthmaps as 3D priors to compensate for sparse camera views. The frustum radiance field for triangulation borrows the volume rendering formulation from NeRF [39] and SPARF [58] but removes the neural network, replacing MLP-based occupancy with direct interpolation of a discrete field. The Bundle-RANSAC-Adjustment algorithm extends two-view RANSAC with multi-view bundle adjustment and introduces a Hough Transform acceleration that discretizes the scale/depth-adjustment search space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first evidence that geometry-based self-supervision (local SfM) with as few as 5 consecutive frames can improve SoTA supervised depth and correspondence models, a finding that contrasts with prior work showing self-supervision requires substantially more data to match supervised performance. The certified global optimality of the multi-view RANSAC scoring function and the SoTA sparse-view pose results (100% success rate vs. COLMAP's 36.7%) make the method a strong initialization for downstream NeRF-based reconstruction. However, the NeRF-like triangulation step limits scalability to large-scale self-supervised learning, and the ~3× runtime overhead over COLMAP (though parallelizable) constrains practical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 5-frame windows and whether the depth improvements compound when applied iteratively over longer video sequences.
