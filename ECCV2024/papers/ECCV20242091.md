## Problem

Existing NeRF uncertainty estimation methods, including the state-of-the-art DANE, produce uncalibrated uncertainties whose confidence intervals do not match true confidences. Standard calibration techniques require held-out ground truth images from the target scene, which is infeasible in sparse-view settings (as few as 3 images) where holding out even one image degrades NeRF reconstruction by over 30%.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242091/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The meta-calibrator consumes rendered NeRF images and their associated uncalibrated uncertainty maps (derived from FlipNeRF's mixture-of-Laplacians CDF) at inference time. DINOv2 extracts visual features from both the rendered images and the uncertainty maps, which are concatenated and fed to a small MLP. The output is a scene-specific calibration curve parameterized by 3 PCA coefficients, from which per-pixel uncertainty is computed as the interquartile range of the calibrated CDF averaged over RGB channels—requiring no ground truth data from the target scene at any stage.

## Method

The meta-calibrator exploits the observation that calibration curves, while varying across scenes, share a low-dimensional structure: PCA on 21 training scenes shows that 3 components capture most of the variance. A 3-layer MLP (sizes [128, 128, 3], Leaky ReLU) is trained on 30 scenes to predict these 3 PCA coefficients from DINOv2 features of rendered images and uncalibrated uncertainty maps, learning to associate visual rendering imperfections (floaters, artifacts) with the appropriate calibration correction. The predicted coefficients reconstruct the calibration curve, which is then made monotonically increasing via isotonic regression. Applied to FlipNeRF's mixture-of-Laplacians distribution, the calibrated CDF yields per-ray uncertainty through the interquartile range, achieving a 94% reduction in calibration error and over 100% reduction in NLL compared to DANE while simultaneously improving image quality (PSNR 19.34 vs. 15.19).

## Results

On the challenging 3-view LLFF dataset, the proposed meta-calibrator achieves a calibration error of 0.0026 (vs. 0.0441 for DANE, a 94% reduction) and a negative log-likelihood of −0.68 (vs. 3.75 for DANE, over 100% lower), while also improving image quality to PSNR 19.34 and LPIPS 0.235 (vs. 15.19 and 0.646 for DANE). On the standard NeRF uncertainty benchmark (4–12 views, Table 2), the method achieves an average NLL of −1.67, outperforming DANE (M=10) at −0.47 and CF-NeRF at 0.57. The meta-calibrator also achieves a 70% reduction in calibration error on held-out DTU scenes, demonstrating cross-dataset generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed meta-calibrator decisively outperforms DANE [29], the state-of-the-art NeRF uncertainty method, on every metric: calibration error drops from 0.0441 to 0.0026 (≈94% reduction), NLL from 3.75 to −0.68, PSNR from 15.19 to 19.34, and LPIPS from 0.646 to 0.235 on the 3-view LLFF benchmark. On the standard uncertainty benchmark (Table 2, 4–12 views), the method achieves an average NLL of −1.67, beating DANE (M=10) at −0.47, CF-NeRF [26] at 0.57, S-NeRF [27] at 1.27, and NeRF-W [14] at 1.95. Notably, the method also surpasses DANE in image quality (PSNR/LPIPS), because it builds on FlipNeRF's superior sparse-view reconstruction rather than the weaker Instant-NGP backbone used by DANE. The calibration curves in Fig. 5a visually confirm that DANE is significantly over-confident near p=1 and under-confident near p=0, whereas the meta-calibrator's curves closely track the ideal diagonal. For next-best view planning, the calibrated uncertainties yield greater PSNR gains over DANE's uncalibrated ones, demonstrating practical utility of the calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: NeRF uncertainty estimation (DANE, S-NeRF, CF-NeRF, FlipNeRF) and calibrated regression (Kuleshov et al.). It departs from prior NeRF uncertainty methods by explicitly addressing calibration, which none of them consider, and departs from standard calibrated regression by introducing a meta-calibrator that predicts scene-specific calibration curves from rendered features rather than fitting them to held-out data. The base model is FlipNeRF's mixture-of-Laplacians distribution, and the meta-calibrator's feature extraction relies on DINOv2, making the approach a composition of existing components into a novel meta-learning pipeline for calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Calibrated uncertainties are a prerequisite for deploying NeRFs in safety-critical settings (medical imaging, autonomous navigation) and for uncertainty-guided active learning (next-best view planning), yet no prior method provided them. The meta-calibrator's ability to generalize from ~30 training scenes to unseen scenes without any target-scene ground truth is practically important for sparse-view reconstruction where data is scarce. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale datasets (LLFF, DTU), dependence on DINOv2 features whose role is not deeply ablated, and the PCA representation being validated on a limited number of scenes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic NeRFs, 3D Gaussian Splatting, or other implicit representations, and whether the 3-component PCA bottleneck holds for more diverse scene categories.
