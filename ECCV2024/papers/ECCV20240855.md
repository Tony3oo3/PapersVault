## Problem

Existing self-supervised skeletal action recognition methods use masked self-reconstruction of raw joint coordinates or motion as pretext tasks, which forces the model to expend capacity on noisy low-level details and isolated targets that lack spatio-temporal context. This results in inefficient utilization of model capacity and suboptimal action representations. The paper asks: what pretext task can better direct the model's modelling power toward learning high-level contextual and depth features?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240855/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D skeleton sequences of shape T×V×C_in (frames × joints × coordinate axes), trimmed to a fixed length of 120 frames with segment length 4. During pretraining, each skeleton is randomly rotated around its vertical axis and subjected to affine transformations (translation, flipping) to create diverse views, then a motion-aware masking strategy (ratio 0.9) selects which joints to mask. The pretraining output is the predicted latent representation (256-dim vector) of each masked joint; at fine-tuning and inference, the frozen target encoder produces per-joint features that feed a classification head to yield an action label.

## Method

S-JEPA instantiates the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for skeletons: a view encoder (8-layer vanilla transformer, 256-dim) processes the masked skeleton view with learnable mask tokens inserted at missing joint positions, while a target encoder (same architecture, updated via EMA) processes the unmasked skeleton to produce context-rich target representations. A 5-layer predictor transformer takes the view features and predicts the target representations at masked positions, trained with cross-entropy loss between softmax-normalized (temperature-sharpened) predicted and target distributions. The critical design choice is masking the *output* of the target encoder rather than its input, so that target representations encode global joint-wise correlations from all joints via self-attention. A batch centering operation (EMA-smoothed subtraction of the batch mean) is applied to target representations to counteract dimensional non-uniformity and stabilize training, and the momentum target encoder is essential to prevent representation collapse.

## Results

Under the fine-tuning protocol, S-JEPA achieves 93.1% on NTU60-XSub, 97.6% on NTU60-XView, 90.3% on NTU120-XSub, and 91.3% on NTU120-XSet, matching or exceeding the strongest baseline (MAMP) across all four splits. Under linear evaluation (frozen backbone), S-JEPA reaches 85.3% on NTU60-XSub and 79.6% on NTU120-XSub, outperforming the best contrastive method (ActCLR) by 1.0 and 5.3 percentage points respectively. In the transfer learning setting to PKU-MMD Phase II, S-JEPA attains 74.2% when pretrained on NTU120, surpassing MAMP by 1.0 points and SkeletonMAE by 13.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-JEPA's closest competitor is MAMP [32], which uses masked motion prediction in the joint space; S-JEPA ties on NTU60-XSub (93.1%) and NTU120-XSet (91.3%) while winning on NTU60-XView (97.6 vs 97.5) and NTU120-XSub (90.3 vs 90.0). Against the best contrastive learning method HYSP [14], S-JEPA gains 5.8 and 5.0 percentage points on NTU120-XSub and XSet respectively, and against ActCLR [27] the margin is 8.2 and 6.7 points on the same splits. Compared to MotionBERT [57], which performs 2D-to-3D lifting, S-JEPA improves NTU120-XSub by 5.5 points (90.3 vs 84.8) and NTU120-XSet by 4.9 points. In the semi-supervised setting (10% labels), S-JEPA outperforms SkeAttnCLR [19] by 6.9 points on NTU60-XSub and 7.6 on XView, and beats MAMP by 0.4 points on XSub. In transfer learning to PKU-MMD Phase II, S-JEPA surpasses MAMP by 0.8–1.0 points across all three source datasets and exceeds SkeletonMAE [49] by 8.4–13.2 points, confirming that latent-space prediction yields more transferable representations than joint-space reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

S-JEPA adapts the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture paradigm from Assran et al. (CVPR 2023) to skeletal action recognition, shifting the prediction target from the joint space (as in SkeletonMAE and MAMP) to the latent space. It borrows the motion-aware masking strategy from MAMP (ICCV 2023) and the centering/sharpening stabilization techniques from DINO (Caron et al., ICCV 2021). The key conceptual departure from prior self-supervised skeleton methods is that instead of reconstructing raw 3D coordinates or temporal motion, the model predicts contextualized transformer-encoded representations of missing joints, arguing that this directs model capacity toward high-level action semantics rather than low-level noisy coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean empirical argument that latent-space prediction outperforms joint-space reconstruction for skeleton representation learning, validated across four evaluation protocols (linear, fine-tuning, semi-supervised, transfer) on three benchmarks. The simple centering operation as a training stabilizer is a practical takeaway for anyone applying JEPA-style objectives. However, the margins over the strongest baseline (MAMP) are modest (≤1.5 points), and the approach is evaluated only on 3D skeleton data with a single backbone (vanilla transformer), leaving open whether the latent-space advantage persists with other architectures or modalities. A broader open question is whether the specific motion-aware masking strategy is optimal or whether other masking heuristics could further improve the learned representations.
