## Problem

Existing speech resynthesis methods focus narrowly on room impulse responses (reverb) and require simulated or labeled training data, failing to capture the full soundscape of a scene—including ambient textures like crashing waves or wind. This paper addresses the broader problem of transferring both acoustic properties and ambient sounds to input speech, and does so in a self-supervised manner using unlabeled in-the-wild video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242129/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a clean (enhanced and dereverberated) speech clip as the input sound, plus a conditional audio clip and a conditional image frame sampled from the target scene. At both training and test time, Gaussian noise is added to the enhanced input to prevent the model from trivially copying the original signal. The output is a full waveform reconstructed via a VAE decoder and HiFi-GAN vocoder from the stylized latent mel-spectrogram, capturing both reverb characteristics and ambient background sounds of the conditional scene.

## Method

The core training signal is a self-supervised 'speech de-enhancement' pretext task: two non-overlapping clips are sampled from the same video, one is processed through source separation and speech enhancement to strip scene-specific acoustics, and a latent diffusion model is trained to recover the original using the other clip as a conditional hint. The architecture is a U-Net-based latent diffusion model operating in a VAE-compressed mel-spectrogram space, conditioned on CLAP audio embeddings and CLIP image embeddings fused via cross-attention. Classifier-free guidance (scale λ=4.5) and DDIM sampling (200 steps) are used at inference. The key insight is that temporal coherence of sound events within a video provides a free supervisory signal: the model must infer scene properties from the conditional example and transfer them, which generalizes at test time to arbitrary unseen scenes.

## Results

On the CityWalk dataset, the proposed method achieves the best scores across nearly all objective metrics: MSE 0.54, RTE 0.20, PESQ 2.83, FD 5.13, FAD 1.64, KL 0.59, and IS 2.03, outperforming the strongest baseline AViTAR (MSE 0.76, RTE 0.32, PESQ 2.56, FD 7.44, FAD 3.02) by substantial margins. On the Acoustic AVSpeech benchmark for visual acoustic matching, the method also surpasses AViTAR in both seen (RTE 0.098 vs. 0.144) and unseen (RTE 0.124 vs. 0.183) settings. Subjective evaluation confirms these gains, with the proposed method scoring 3.68/5 on overall quality versus 3.32 for AViTAR and 3.16 for Separate & Remix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on the CityWalk dataset across the majority of metrics. Against the closest competitor AViTAR [5] (a GAN-based visual acoustic matching method), the diffusion-based approach reduces MSE from 0.76 to 0.54, RTE from 0.32 to 0.20, and FAD from 3.02 to 1.64, while improving IS from 1.47 to 2.03, demonstrating superior audio quality and diversity. Compared to Separate & Remix [71], which directly copies ambient sounds from the conditional clip, the proposed method achieves substantially lower RTE (0.20 vs. 0.71) and FAD (1.64 vs. 3.34), indicating it captures acoustic properties (reverb, room characteristics) rather than merely overlaying background noise. The Audio Analogy baseline [59] (AudioLDM adapted for audio-to-audio transfer) lags behind both S&R and the proposed method on most metrics, suggesting that acoustic property transfer is critical beyond ambient sound matching. The three captioning-based cascaded pipelines [64, 55] perform worst overall (MSE 2.09–2.34, FAD 9.12–9.73), likely due to information loss from automatic captioning. On the Acoustic AVSpeech benchmark specifically designed for visual acoustic matching, the method also beats AViTAR in both seen and unseen room settings (RTE 0.098/0.124 vs. 0.144/0.183), confirming that the advantage generalizes beyond the in-the-wild CityWalk setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., 2022; Ho et al., 2020) as its generative backbone, repurposing the AudioLDM VAE and HiFi-GAN vocoder for a new conditioning paradigm. It departs from prior visual acoustic matching work (AViTAR, Chen et al. 2022) by extending the target from room impulse responses alone to full soundscapes including ambient textures, and by conditioning on audio-visual examples rather than images alone. The self-supervised 'speech de-enhancement' pretext task draws on the broader audio-visual self-supervision literature (Owens et al., Du et al.) but applies it in a novel way: the model learns to reverse a known degradation (source separation + dereverberation) using a temporally displaced clip as a hint, implicitly learning scene-to-sound transfer without any labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that rich soundscape transfer—including both reverberation and ambient sound textures—can be learned entirely from unlabeled in-the-wild egocentric video, removing the need for curated indoor recordings or text annotations that constrain prior methods. The CityWalk dataset and the self-supervised formulation provide a practical recipe for scaling soundscape manipulation to diverse real-world scenes. Open questions remain around handling vocal effort variations, invisible sound sources (e.g., wind), and audio-visual synchronization for non-stationary events, all of which limit current reliability. The authors also flag the dual-use risk of generating convincing scene-matched audio for disinformation purposes.
