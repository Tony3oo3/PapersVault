## Problem

Diffusion Models used in Deep Learning as a Service expose both user prompts/images and proprietary model parameters to service providers, yet no MPC-based framework for secure DM sampling existed. Directly applying MPC to DMs is infeasible due to the heavy computational and communication cost of nonlinear activation functions (SoftMax, SiLU, Mish) that dominate the U-Net noise prediction step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241902/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes two private inputs: the model provider's diffusion model parameters and the client's text prompt or image, each shared locally via 2-out-of-3 replicated secret sharing to three non-colluding cloud servers. The model architecture is public knowledge. The output is a single generated image (28×28 for MNIST experiments) returned to the client after post-processing; neither the servers nor the model provider learn the client's input, and neither the client nor servers learn the model parameters. Evaluation uses DDPM (1000 steps) and DDIM (50 steps) on MNIST, plus the U-Net component of Stable Diffusion at 1 and 5 sampling steps.

## Method

CipherDM implements a 3PC outsourced sampling protocol on top of ABY3's 2-out-of-3 replicated secret sharing, where three cloud servers jointly execute the full diffusion sampling loop without revealing private inputs. The key insight from ciphertext profiling is that nonlinear operations (exp, tanh, log) account for the majority of MPC time, so the paper replaces them with secure polynomial approximations: SoftMax uses a 7th-order Chebyshev fit of exp over [T_exp, 0] with a boolean mask for the threshold, while SiLU and Mish use piecewise polynomials (2nd-order on [-6,-2], 6th-order even-terms-only on [-2,6]) selected via secure less-than comparisons. A Secure Time Embedding protocol applies the same Chebyshev technique to the positional encoding layer. An additional optimization replaces secure division Div(x, Broadcast(y)) with multiplication by a precomputed reciprocal, reducing both computation and communication. On DDIM with Mish activation, this yields up to 2.328× speedup and 1.791× communication reduction over direct SPU deployment.

## Results

On MNIST with DDPM (1000 steps) and DDIM (50 steps), CipherDM reduces total sampling time by 1.037×–1.859× (DDPM) and 1.043×–1.819× (DDIM) compared to direct SPU deployment, with the largest gains observed for Mish activation. At the MPC level, CipherDM achieves 1.084×–2.328× speedup and 1.212×–1.791× communication reduction over SPU. For the Stable Diffusion U-Net component, CipherDM yields a 1.081× speedup over SPU. In plaintext mode, CipherDM's polynomial approximations introduce a noticeable FID degradation (e.g., 272.26 vs. 110.13 for ReLU on DDPM), though the per-activation MSE remains in the 10⁻⁶–10⁻³ range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is direct deployment on SecretFlow-SPU, the state-of-the-art MPC framework. CipherDM consistently outperforms SPU in computation time across all configurations (DDPM/DDIM × Local/LAN × ReLU/SiLU/Mish), with speedups ranging from 1.084× to 2.328× at the MPC level. The largest time gains come from the Secure Activation protocol for SiLU and Mish (up to 2.328× for Mish on DDIM), while the Secure SoftMax protocol dominates for ReLU (up to 1.217×). On communication, CipherDM achieves 1.212×–1.791× reduction for SiLU and Mish, but slightly increases communication for ReLU (0.915×–0.938×, i.e., SPU is marginally better) due to the additional broadcast operations in the SoftMax protocol. For the Stable Diffusion U-Net, CipherDM provides a modest 1.081× speedup over SPU. In terms of output quality, CipherDM's plaintext FID is substantially worse than CPU (e.g., 272.26 vs. 110.13 for ReLU), indicating that the polynomial approximations introduce visible noise in generated images, though the authors attribute this to the 18-bit fixed-point precision rather than the approximation itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of MPC-based secure inference from transformer models (PUMA, MPCFormer, MPC-BERT) to diffusion model sampling, a setting the authors explicitly identify as unaddressed in prior literature. It builds directly on the ABY3 three-party replicated secret sharing protocol and the SecretFlow-SPU compilation/execution framework, while borrowing the Chebyshev polynomial approximation technique and the Div→Recip optimization from PUMA. The key conceptual departure is the identification of nonlinear activations (SoftMax, SiLU, Mish) as the dominant MPC bottleneck in DM sampling, motivating specialized piecewise-polynomial protocols rather than generic secure computation of these functions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CipherDM establishes the first proof-of-concept that MPC can be applied to iterative diffusion model sampling, opening a research direction for privacy-preserving image generation where neither user prompts nor model parameters are exposed. However, practical impact is currently very limited: sampling a single 28×28 MNIST image takes ~10 minutes, FID degrades substantially (e.g., 110→272 for ReLU), and the full Stable Diffusion pipeline could not be evaluated due to SPU's architectural constraints. Open questions include whether model architecture modifications (e.g., replacing U-Net with MPC-friendly alternatives), hardware acceleration, or more efficient underlying protocols can close the gap to practical deployment, and whether the polynomial approximations can be tightened to reduce quality loss at scale.
