## Problem

Existing learning-based ground-to-satellite image registration methods require sub-meter, sub-degree GPS labels for training, which are expensive to acquire via RTK setups and unreliable due to multipath interference and signal occlusion. This paper addresses how to refine a ground camera's 3-DoF pose (2-DoF location + 1-DoF orientation) using only noisy, coarse pose labels available from consumer GPS/compass or city-scale retrieval, without any accurate ground-truth poses in the training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240218/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground camera image (256×1024 pin-hole on KITTI, 320×640 panorama on VIGOR), a geo-referenced satellite image (512×512, 0.1–0.2 m/px), and a coarse pose estimate as input. It outputs a refined 3-DoF camera pose and a location probability map (similarity map) over the satellite image, where the argmax pixel gives the estimated camera location. The search range is approximately 56×56 m² with up to 20° orientation noise on KITTI.

## Method

The approach decouples rotation and translation estimation with distinct supervision strategies. For rotation, a self-supervised pose regressor (two Swin Transformer layers + FC) is trained on synthetic satellite-satellite pairs created by randomly rotating/translating a satellite image and masking it to a triangular FoV region, using L1 loss on the relative pose; at inference, ground features are projected to overhead view via ground-plane homography (no extra parameters). For translation, a two-branch U-Net (VGG16 encoder) extracts multi-level features from ground and satellite images, with the ground branch additionally producing an unsupervised confidence map that down-weights dynamic objects. The projected overhead-view features are matched against satellite features via normalized spatial correlation (sliding-window cosine similarity), and the network is trained with a contrastive loss that maximizes the peak similarity on positive ground-satellite pairs while minimizing it on negative pairs, optionally augmented with a local-maximum constraint when slightly more accurate (≤5 m) labels are available.

## Results

On the KITTI dataset (3-DoF pose estimation), the proposed weakly-supervised method (λ=0) achieves 62.73% lateral accuracy (d=1m) and 99.99% azimuth accuracy (θ=1°) on cross-area evaluation, outperforming all fully supervised baselines on cross-area generalization. With the additional noisy-label objective (λ=1), same-area lateral accuracy improves to 66.07% (d=1m) and 94.22% (d=3m). On VIGOR, the method achieves 4.70m mean / 1.68m median cross-area error (aligned-orientation), surpassing nearly all fully supervised competitors. The key advantage is the smallest same-area-to-cross-area performance gap among all compared methods, demonstrating superior generalization without requiring accurate GT pose labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On KITTI cross-area (Test-2), the proposed weakly-supervised method (λ=0) achieves the best lateral accuracy (62.73% at d=1m, 86.53% at d=3m) and azimuth accuracy (99.99% at θ=1°) among all compared methods, surpassing the closest fully supervised competitor Shi et al. [29] (57.72% lateral d=1, 98.98% azimuth θ=1) by ~5% in lateral accuracy. On same-area (Test-1), the method trails the top fully supervised baselines Xia et al. [49] (97.35% lateral d=1) and Song et al. [34] (95.47% lateral d=1), but the gap narrows substantially with the noisy-label objective (λ=1: 66.07% lateral d=1). On VIGOR cross-area, the method (λ=1) achieves 4.70m mean / 1.68m median error (aligned-orientation), outperforming SliceMatch [14] (5.53m / 2.55m), Shi et al. [29] (5.16m / 1.40m), and MCC [50] (9.05m / 5.14m), while remaining competitive with Xia et al. [49] (4.97m / 1.68m) and Song et al. [34] (5.01m / 2.42m). The defining advantage is generalization: the same-area-to-cross-area performance drop is smallest for the proposed method, whereas fully supervised methods like Xia et al. [49] degrade from 97.35% to 44.06% lateral accuracy (d=1m) and Song et al. [34] from 95.47% to 54.19%, indicating overfitting to GT pose labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ground-to-satellite image registration line of work (Shi et al. [29], SliceMatch [14], OrienterNet [26], Xia et al. [49]) by replacing the standard fully-supervised training paradigm with a weakly-supervised one. For rotation estimation, it draws on Spatial Transformer Networks [12] and the neural pose optimizer from Shi et al. [29], but trains entirely on self-generated satellite-satellite pairs rather than ground-satellite pairs with GT poses. For translation estimation, it repurposes the spatial correlation / similarity-matching framework from prior cross-view registration work but supervises it via a contrastive deep metric learning objective over positive/negative satellite images, eliminating the need for sub-meter GT labels. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first weakly-supervised ground-to-satellite camera localization method, distinguishing from its concurrent work [51] which still assumes GT data from at least one area.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating the need for expensive RTK-based pose acquisition removes a major bottleneck for scaling ground-to-satellite localization to new cities or large-scale deployments. The standout result is that the weakly-supervised method achieves the best cross-area generalization on both KITTI and VIGOR, outperforming fully supervised SOTA, suggesting that noisy-label training inherently regularizes against overfitting to specific regions. However, same-area performance remains below the best fully-supervised methods (e.g., Xia et al. [49] on KITTI), and the authors acknowledge that the simple ground-plane homography for overhead-view synthesis is suboptimal, leaving room for better geometry-aware projections under weak supervision. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 6-DoF pose, video-based localization, or scenarios where even coarse pose is unavailable.
