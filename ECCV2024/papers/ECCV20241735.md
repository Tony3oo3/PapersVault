## Problem

Existing liquid level estimation methods are limited to single transparent containers viewed from a fixed frontal camera, which does not reflect real autonomous biomedical laboratory (ABL) conditions where multiple transparent containers hold transparent liquids at arbitrary viewpoints under varying lighting and backgrounds. This paper formalizes the 'dual transparent liquid' estimation problem—where both the liquid and its container are transparent—and introduces a multi-instance, multi-viewpoint benchmark to bridge the gap between controlled benchmarks and practical ABL liquid-handling tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (1280×720) and depth maps from a RealSense D435i camera, along with CAD models of the four container types (T25/T75/T125 cell flasks and G-rex bioreactor). The BCRM outputs four Bezier curve control points (a W'×8 regression vector) plus a per-curve probability logit for each spatial location, while the CRM outputs a full-resolution color residual image (3×H×W) that is added to the raw RGB to stabilize the air-liquid interface region. The final liquid level is a scalar 3D height value obtained by projecting the detected 2D contact line onto the 3D container model via estimated 6D pose.

## Method

TCLD combines two modules: a Bézier-Curve Regression Module (BCRM) built on ResNet-34 that regresses four control points and a presence probability per spatial location via MLP heads after average pooling, and a Color Rectification Module (CRM) built on ResNet-18 that predicts a local color residual image to counteract optical refraction/reflection artifacts at the air-liquid interface. The CRM's residual is added to the raw image before BCRM inference, stabilizing the ALI color distribution and improving detection precision by 10.33 points. Training uses a composite supervised loss combining Bezier control-point regression (L2), binary cross-entropy for curve presence, weighted line-segmentation loss, and MSE on the color residual. For the final 3D liquid level, FFB6D estimates each container's 6D pose from RGB-D input, the 2D Bezier contact line is resampled and mapped onto the projected 3D CAD model, and the mean of the mapped point heights yields the estimated level.

## Results

On the DTLD benchmark, TCLD-FFB6D achieves an average MAPE of 0.286 for liquid level estimation, reducing the error by 43.4% compared to the SOTA CMU-Liquid method (MAPE 0.720). With ground-truth 6D poses (TCLD-GT6D), the average MAPE drops further to 0.186, with the best per-object result of 0.074 on the T75 cell flask. For contact line detection, TCLD-GT6D achieves F1@0.50 of 0.663 and mF1 of 0.361, while TCLD-FFB6D reaches F1@0.50 of 0.547 and mF1 of 0.312. The Color Rectification Module contributes a 10.33-point precision improvement over BCRM alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is CMU-Liquid [26], the prior SOTA for liquid level estimation in transparent containers. TCLD-FFB6D substantially outperforms CMU-Liquid, achieving an average MAPE of 0.286 versus 0.720 — a 43.4% relative reduction. The gap is especially pronounced on the T175 cell flask (0.193 vs. 1.232) and the G-rex bioreactor (0.473 vs. 0.808), where CMU-Liquid's single-instance, fixed-view assumptions break down under multi-instance, arbitrary-viewpoint conditions. The paper also benchmarks against five professional biomedical laboratory technicians (PBLTs) with ~8 years of experience, who achieve an average MAPE of 0.539; TCLD-FFB6D (0.286) outperforms the human average by roughly 47%, while the best individual PBLT (0.377) still trails the proposed method. Overall, the method wins decisively on all four object types against both the SOTA and human baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends liquid level estimation from the constrained single-instance, fixed-view setting of CMU-Liquids [26] to a multi-instance, multi-view paradigm relevant to autonomous biomedical laboratories. Methodologically, it adapts the Bezier curve regression architecture from ABCNet [25] (originally for curved scene text detection) to model air-liquid interface contact lines, and integrates FFB6D [18] for 6D pose estimation to bridge 2D detections to 3D measurements. The Color Rectification Module draws on residual learning principles from image enhancement and restoration literature, repurposing them to stabilize color distributions at optically complex air-liquid interfaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTLD is the first benchmark dataset addressing dual transparent liquid level estimation under realistic ABL conditions (multiple objects, arbitrary viewpoints, varied lighting and liquid colors), filling a gap that limits deployment of robotic liquid handling in real labs. The 43.4% MAPE reduction over the prior SOTA is meaningful, though the comparison is somewhat asymmetric since CMU-Liquids was designed for a fundamentally simpler setting. Key limitations include the small object count (4 instances), limited scene diversity (3 labs), and acknowledged struggles with optical complexity under varying illumination; scaling the dataset to more container types, materials, and lighting conditions is an open challenge for broader generalization.
