## Problem

Generative captioners trained with autoregressive objectives show a large performance gap (over 19% top-1 accuracy) compared to discriminative CLIP classifiers on zero-shot ImageNet classification. The paper identifies the root cause: captioner predictions are strongly correlated with linguistic priors from the text modality, making them less grounded on visual inputs. The central question is whether a purely generative training procedure can close this gap without finetuning or additional discriminative modules.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes an RGB image and a set of candidate text captions (e.g., 80 CLIP-style prompts per class for ImageNet, or 5 captions per image for retrieval) and scores each candidate using the IG objective log P(T|I) − α log P(T). The unimodal term log P(T) is computed by the same text decoder with no image input and can be cached across images. For classification, the highest-scoring caption determines the predicted class; for retrieval, the scores form an image×caption similarity matrix used to compute recall metrics.

## Method

The IG captioner uses a ViT image encoder and an autoregressive text decoder, trained with a dual-objective loss: a standard multimodal captioning loss (β·log P(T|I)) and a unimodal language-modeling loss (γ·log P(T)) that trains the same decoder to generate captions without any image. At evaluation, the scoring objective is the information gain log P(T|I) − α log P(T), which measures the likelihood increase attributable to the visual input over the text prior, effectively discounting the linguistic bias. The text decoder thus serves a dual role—producing both conditional and unconditional caption probabilities—eliminating the need for a separate language model. With α=0.8 and loss weights β=1.5, γ=0.5, the IG captioner achieves 64.3%/69.8% top-1 accuracy on zero-shot ImageNet with ViT-B/ViT-L, surpassing the CLIP classifier by 0.5%/1.6% while using only generative training on Laion-5B.

## Results

On zero-shot ImageNet classification, IG captioner with ViT-B/ViT-L image encoders achieves 64.3%/69.8% top-1 accuracy, representing 19.7%/18.1% absolute improvements over the standard captioner (44.6%/51.7%) and 0.5%/1.6% improvements over the CLIP classifier (63.8%/68.2%). On MSCOCO 5K image-to-text retrieval, IG captioner (ViT-B) reaches 47.4%/73.7%/82.5% R@1/R@5/R@10, a 22.7%/25.5%/22.8% gain over the captioner. On Flickr30K 1K, IG captioner (ViT-B) achieves 60.6%/90.0%/94.7% R@1/R@5/R@10 for image-to-text retrieval, narrowing the gap to CLIP to 24.6%/7.2%/4.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the CLIP classifier (Radford et al., 2021), IG captioner wins on zero-shot ImageNet top-1 accuracy by 0.5% (ViT-B: 64.3% vs. 63.8%) and 1.6% (ViT-L: 69.8% vs. 68.2%), despite using a purely generative training objective rather than contrastive loss. On MSCOCO and Flickr30K image-to-text retrieval, IG captioner narrows the gap to CLIP substantially (e.g., 6.5%/5.1%/3.5% on R@1/R@5/R@10 with ViT-L on MSCOCO) but does not fully close it. Against the text-to-image diffusion classifier of Clark et al. (2023, Imagen-based, 62.7% top-1), IG captioner (ViT-L) outperforms by 7.1% while using only 53.5% of the parameters. Similarly, against the Stable Diffusion-based classifier of Li et al. (2023, 61.4% top-1), IG captioner leads by 8.4% with 73.8% of the parameters. Notably, both the standard captioner and IG captioner outperform CLIP on text-to-image retrieval (e.g., 49.2% vs. 42.1% R@1 on MSCOCO with ViT-L), suggesting generative models have an inherent advantage in that direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws on the mathematical concept of pointwise mutual information (Church & Hanks, 1990) to redesign how an autoregressive image-to-text captioner is scored as a classifier, and conceptually parallels classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) in that both subtract an unconditional prediction from a conditional one. It departs from prior generative-classifier work (Clark et al., 2023; Li et al., 2023), which converts text-to-image diffusion models into classifiers via Bayes' rule, by instead taking an image-to-text captioner and correcting its evaluation for linguistic-prior bias. The training objective, which jointly optimizes multimodal and unimodal captioning losses, is inspired by the dual-mode design of CoCa (Yu et al., 2022) but is motivated specifically by matching the IG evaluation rather than by contrastive alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the apparent performance gap between generative and discriminative vision-language models on zero-shot classification is largely an evaluation artifact—linguistic priors inflating caption likelihoods—rather than a fundamental limitation of generative training. This reframing suggests that future work on unifying generative and discriminative objectives should focus on scoring and training alignment rather than architectural changes. Open questions include why captioners outperform CLIP on text-to-image retrieval (acknowledged but unexplained by the authors), whether the IG correction generalizes to open-ended generation or non-ImageNet taxonomies, and how the method interacts with larger decoder architectures or instruction-tuned models.
