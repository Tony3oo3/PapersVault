## Problem

Existing external information leakage (EIL) attacks on face recognition systems rely on unrealistic attacker assumptions—complete model access, training hundreds of substitute models, or access to the training data collection space—making them impractical for real-world scenarios. This paper addresses the gap by presenting the first EIL attack that leverages adversarial samples under realistic attacker constraints, requiring only a small query budget (24–60 queries) and a minimal number of substitute models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242009/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker provides an external set of face image pairs (mutually exclusive identities from the target model's training set) and black-box query access to the target face verification model. The target model outputs a binary same/not-same-person prediction per query. The final output is the inferred binary property value (e.g., male/female, young/not-young, 5 o'clock shadow/not) that best characterizes the target model's training set distribution, determined by comparing the fraction of misleading samples (FMS) across two sets of unique adversarial samples crafted for 0% and 100% extreme distributions.

## Method

AdversariaLeak operates in four phases: (1) the attacker trains one substitute face verification model per extreme property value (0% and 100%) using disjoint subsets of an external dataset; (2) adversarial samples are crafted for each substitute model via Carlini-Wagner or PGD optimization, perturbing same-person image pairs to flip the prediction to 'not the same person'; (3) from each adversarial set, the attacker handpicks 'unique' samples that mislead only their own substitute model and not the other, isolating decision-boundary gaps specific to each property distribution; (4) the target model is queried with these unique samples, and the property value whose corresponding FMS (fraction of misleading samples) is higher is inferred as the target's training set characteristic. The key insight is that unique adversarial samples exploit distribution-specific decision boundary gaps, so they will also mislead a target model trained on a similar distribution while leaving models trained on the opposite distribution unaffected.

## Results

AdversariaLeak correctly infers the property value that best characterizes the target model's training set in all 30 non-ambiguous experiments (excluding the 50% balanced distribution) across CelebA and MAAD-Face, using only 24 queries (scenario 1) or 60 queries (scenario 2) to the target model. In contrast, the loss test (LTA) and threshold test (TDTA) baselines fail in 10 and 9 of those 30 experiments respectively, while requiring up to 24,000 queries. The FMS gap between the 0% and 100% substitute models is statistically significant and consistent (standard deviation < 0.09 in most experiments), with the correct substitute model always producing the higher FMS for 0%/25% and 75%/100% distributions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdversariaLeak is compared against two state-of-the-art EIL attacks from [24]: the loss test (LTA) and the threshold test (TDTA), both adapted to realistic attacker assumptions (two substitute models for TDTA). AdversariaLeak achieves 100% correct inference across all 30 non-ambiguous experiments (6 properties × scenarios × 4 non-50% distributions), while LTA fails in 10 experiments and TDTA in 9. The most consistent failures for both baselines occur at the 75% distribution for the 'Young' property (both predict 'Not Young' instead of 'Young') and at the 0%/25% distributions for the '5OCS' property (TDTA predicts '5OCS' instead of 'Not 5OCS'). Critically, AdversariaLeak achieves these results with only 24–60 queries to the target model, whereas LTA and TDTA require tens of thousands of queries (up to ~24,000 for CelebA's male property) and still fail. The baselines also show reduced stability under the stricter scenario 2 (different head architectures), with LTA degrading further compared to scenario 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the external information leakage (EIL) attack lineage initiated by Ateniese et al. [2], which first demonstrated that substitute models trained on different property distributions can reveal training-set statistics. It departs from prior EIL attacks (loss test and threshold test of Suri & Evans [24], poisoning-based attacks of Chaudhari et al. [6] and Mahloujifar et al. [17]) by repurposing adversarial evasion techniques (Carlini-Wagner, PGD) to craft unique adversarial samples that probe a target model's decision boundary, rather than relying on raw accuracy or confidence-score comparisons. The key conceptual shift is treating adversarial samples as a targeted probe: by handpicking samples that mislead only one substitute model, the attacker isolates the behavioral signature of a specific property distribution, reducing the query budget from tens of thousands to a few dozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in query budget (24–60 queries versus 24,000+ for prior attacks) makes EIL attacks on deployed face recognition systems practically feasible, raising the urgency for countermeasures in biometric authentication pipelines. An interesting side finding is that pre-trained backbones (e.g., RepVGG-B0 trained on MS-Celeb-1M) leak their own training distribution into the 50%-distribution results, suggesting that transfer learning introduces an additional, previously unexamined information channel. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-binary properties, multi-class settings, and other modalities (tabular data, object detection), and whether the exact training-set distribution can be estimated rather than merely the best-characterizing value.
