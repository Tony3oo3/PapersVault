## Problem

Vision Transformers suffer from quadratic self-attention cost, motivating token reduction via pruning or merging. Prior merging-based methods (ToMe, K-Medoids, DPC-KNN) perform global clustering that can produce redundant groups, and ToMe's bipartite matching inherently restricts keep rates to ≥50% and limits which token pairs can be merged. No single merging-based method had been shown to consistently outperform all prior token reduction approaches across the full range of downstream tasks (classification, image synthesis, detection, and segmentation).

## Input / Output

ATC is a plug-in module inserted between the self-attention and MLP sub-layers of ViT blocks, consuming the key vectors from self-attention as token features. It operates on standard RGB images (224×224 for DeiT/MAE classification, 512×512 for Stable Diffusion synthesis) or text-conditioned latent representations, and produces task-specific outputs: class labels, generated images, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks. For dense prediction tasks (ViT-Adapter), the method back-projects merged tokens through the clustering to restore the original patch count expected by Injector/Extractor modules. Token reduction is applied at discrete stages (blocks 4, 7, 10 for DeiT; every block for MAE) with keep rates from 25% to 90%.

## Method

ATC replaces the global partitioning or bipartite matching of prior methods with classical agglomerative (bottom-up hierarchical) clustering: starting with each token as its own cluster, it iteratively merges the two most similar clusters according to a chosen linkage function (single, complete, or average) computed over cosine distance on self-attention keys, until the target number of tokens remains. This local, sequential merging clusters redundant features early while preserving diverse features unmodified for as long as possible, avoiding the redundant cluster centers inherent in global approaches. The method is entirely parameter-free, requiring no learnable components or fine-tuning to function. After the stopping criterion is met, tokens within each cluster are combined via a size-weighted average, and proportional attention (scaling by cluster size) is applied in subsequent self-attention layers to account for the increased spatial extent each merged token represents.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with MAE-pretrained ViT backbones, ATC^complete applied off-the-shelf with a linear token schedule achieves 67.1% top-1 accuracy on ViT-Base (t=16), a 10.5 percentage point improvement over ToMe (56.6%). After fine-tuning, ATC^complete reaches 82.5% on ViT-Base (constant schedule) vs. ToMe's 81.9%. On Stable Diffusion image synthesis, ATC^average achieves the best FID of 33.22 at r=40%, outperforming ToMe's best of 33.57. On COCO 2017 detection/segmentation, fine-tuned ATC^complete with ViT-Adapter-S matches the no-reduction baseline (48.6 vs. 48.5 mAP^box at r=90%) while ToMe falls short (48.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATC consistently outperforms ToMe [2] across all three task categories. On MAE-pretrained ImageNet-1K classification, the gap is largest under the aggressive linear schedule with smaller backbones: ATC^average/complete achieves 67.1% vs. ToMe's 56.6% off-the-shelf on ViT-Base (+10.5 pp), narrowing to +0.6–1.6 pp after fine-tuning. On Stable Diffusion, ATC^average achieves FID 33.22 at r=40% versus ToMe's best of 33.57 at r=70%, and ATC can operate at r=40% where ToMe's bipartite matching is limited to r≥50%. On COCO 2017 detection/segmentation, ATC^complete with ViT-Adapter-S (fine-tuned) reaches 48.6 mAP^box at r=90%, matching the no-reduction baseline, while ToMe only reaches 48.3; at r=25% ATC^complete achieves 45.3 mAP^box where ToMe cannot operate at all. Against the partition-based baselines DPC-KNN [66] and K-Medoids [39], ATC outperforms both across all four classification datasets and keep rates, with the largest gains at r=25% (up to 9.6 pp on NABirds with DeiT-B), attributed to ATC producing more semantically coherent clusters rather than arbitrary global partitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATC directly extends ToMe (Bolya et al., ICLR 2023) by replacing its bipartite matching algorithm with classical agglomerative clustering, shifting from a global, partition-based merging strategy to a local, bottom-up hierarchical one. The method draws on the well-established agglomerative clustering literature (Everitt et al., 2011) for its linkage functions (single, complete, average), while the broader token reduction framing follows the systematic comparison of 13 methods by Haurum et al. (ICCV Workshops 2023). The key conceptual departure is that prior merging methods (K-Medoids, DPC-KNN, ToMe) all require selecting global cluster centers, whereas ATC iteratively merges the most similar pair at each step, avoiding redundant cluster assignments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ATC provides a parameter-free, drop-in token merging method that achieves state-of-the-art results across the most diverse task suite in the token reduction literature (classification, synthesis, detection, segmentation), and can match fine-tuned SOTA off-the-shelf at high keep rates. Its particular strength at low keep rates (25–50%) addresses a regime where prior methods degrade sharply, making it relevant for aggressive efficiency settings. Open questions include whether the current CPU-bound, non-batched agglomerative clustering implementations can be made GPU-friendly to close the inference throughput gap, and whether the linkage function choice can be made adaptive per layer or per task rather than a fixed hyperparameter.
