## Problem

Existing query-based temporal sentence grounding models represent moments as (center, length) tuples, but moment centers are inherently ambiguous—the frame at the center may be irrelevant to the sentence—causing center misalignment that degrades boundary accuracy. Additionally, conventional classification-based scoring ranks proposals by semantic match rather than localization quality, allowing incomplete moments to be over-prioritized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240040/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video and a free-form text sentence as input. Video features are extracted at 1-second (Charades-STA) or 2-second (QVHighlights, TACoS) intervals using concatenated Slowfast (ResNet-50) and CLIP (ViT-B/32) encoders, with optional PANNs audio features; text features come from CLIP. The model outputs M=10 predictions, each parameterized as an anchor point p with distances d_s and d_e to the start and end boundaries, plus a localization quality score used for ranking.

## Method

BAM-DETR replaces the symmetric (c, l) moment representation with an asymmetric boundary-oriented triplet (p, d_s, d_e), so the model only needs to find any salient anchor within the target interval rather than the precise center. The decoder uses a dual-pathway design: the anchor pathway refines p via global cross-attention over the full video memory, while the boundary pathway refines d_s and d_e via a boundary-focused deformable attention that samples K=3 local neighbors around each predicted boundary using a locality-enhanced memory built with 1D convolutions. A quality-based scoring head predicts the maximum IoU of each proposal with ground truths, replacing classification scores, and the Hungarian matching cost excludes the classification term to make matching purely localization-oriented. Training combines L1 and generalized-IoU localization losses, the quality loss, saliency-guided margin/contrastive losses on memory features, and a boundary regularization loss that encourages the 1D convolutional features to activate near ground-truth boundaries.

## Results

On QVHighlights (the primary benchmark), BAM-DETR achieves R1@0.7 of 48.64% and mAP@0.75 of 46.33% without pre-training, surpassing the previous best (QD-DETR) by 3.66% and 6.45% respectively. With audio features, it further improves to R1@0.5 of 64.07% and mAP Avg of 46.91%. On Charades-STA, BAM-DETR reaches R1@0.7 of 39.38% and mIoU of 52.33%, and on TACoS it achieves R1@0.7 of 26.77% and mIoU of 39.31%, both setting new state-of-the-art results. The gains are most pronounced at strict IoU thresholds (0.7, 0.75), confirming superior boundary alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAM-DETR outperforms all 14 compared baselines across all three main benchmarks. On QVHighlights, it surpasses the strongest prior query-based method QD-DETR [41] by 3.66% in R1@0.7 and 6.45% in mAP@0.75, with the gap widening to 7.41% in mAP@0.75 when comparing against the non-audio variant. On Charades-STA, BAM-DETR beats the best anchor-free model UniVTG [28] by 3.73% in R1@0.7 (39.38% vs. 35.65%) and 2.23% in mIoU (52.33% vs. 50.10%). On TACoS, it exceeds QD-DETR [41] by 5.7% in R1@0.7 (26.77% vs. 21.07%), a relative improvement of 27%. On the anti-biased Charades-STA split, BAM-DETR leads QD-DETR† [41] by 3.95% in R1@0.7 under moment-length bias (40.74% vs. 32.87%), demonstrating that the boundary-oriented formulation is particularly robust to distribution shifts in moment duration. The consistent advantage at high IoU thresholds (0.7, 0.75) across all datasets confirms that the gains stem from improved boundary precision rather than coarse localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the query-based DETR lineage for temporal sentence grounding (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR) by replacing the standard center-length (c, l) moment parameterization with an asymmetric boundary-oriented triplet (p, d_s, d_e), where any salient anchor within the moment suffices rather than requiring the exact center. Architecturally, it inherits the text-to-video cross-attention encoder from QD-DETR [41] and borrows deformable attention and sigmoid-based refinement from Deformable DETR [78], but repurposes deformable attention for local boundary aggregation rather than its original multi-scale global role. The dual-pathway decoder—separating global anchor refinement from local boundary refinement—and the quality-based scoring (replacing classification scores for ranking) are the principal departures from prior query-based grounding models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic experiment showing that existing methods' IoU degrades sharply with center error, while BAM-DETR maintains >77% IoU uniformly across all center-error bins, provides strong evidence that center misalignment is a first-order failure mode rather than a minor nuisance. The boundary-oriented formulation and quality-based scoring are clean, generalizable ideas that could transfer to other 1D localization tasks (temporal action detection, highlight detection). Open questions include whether the approach extends naturally to 2D/3D grounding, whether the frozen-encoder constraint limits ceiling performance, and whether the quality head's reliance on IoU supervision during training creates a train-test gap in unsupervised or weakly-supervised settings.
