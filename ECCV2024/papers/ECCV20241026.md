## Problem

Existing text-to-3D scene generation methods struggle to produce robust, structured geometry at house-scale while simultaneously supporting open-vocabulary inputs and realistic textures. Prior structured scene generators are confined to predefined room and furniture types, while texture-focused approaches rely on artificial or inconsistent appearances, leaving a gap for diverse, editable, and visually coherent house-scale indoor scenes from free-form text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241026/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts any free-form textual narrative ranging from simple labels (e.g., 'Japanese tea house') to detailed multi-room descriptions, which are parsed by LLMs into modular sub-descriptions for floorplan, room layout, object placement, and appearance. The output is a fully structured 3D house mesh comprising floorplan geometry, room walls, placed furniture and ornament meshes (retrieved from 3D-FRONT and Objaverse), and depth-consistent vertex-color textures. The hierarchical graph-based intermediate representations (bubble diagrams and constrained layout graphs) make every level of the scene independently editable via text.

## Method

AnyHome employs a two-stage pipeline inspired by environmental cognition: an amodal structured representation stage followed by an egocentric visual exploration stage. In the first stage, LLMs with designed prompt templates convert text into bubble diagrams (fed to HouseGAN++ for floorplan masks) and constrained layout graphs using Semantic Asset Group (SAG) placement rules to determine object positions and orientations. In the second stage, automatically generated first-person camera trajectories guide a Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) optimization with a differentiable renderer to refine object placements, followed by MVDiffusion-based depth-conditioned inpainting that paints textures along the trajectory and back-projects them onto mesh vertices for multi-view consistency. The key architectural insight is decoupling spatial structure (handled by graph-constrained generation and LLM placement rules) from visual appearance (handled by egocentric diffusion inpainting), enabling both structural editability and photorealistic, text-aligned textures.

## Results

AnyHome achieves the lowest Out-of-Boundary Rate (OOB) of 23.7%, substantially below LayoutGPT+Retrieval (69.4%) and the CG+Inpainting ablation (34.2%), indicating fewer object-wall intersections and boundary violations. On text-scene alignment, AnyHome scores 29.3±0.6 on CLIP-sim and 16.2±0.1 on Caption-sim, outperforming all baselines. The SDS refinement step (comparing CG+Inpainting to AnyHome) reduces OOB by 10.5 points and lifts CLIP-sim from 27.9 to 29.3, while the egocentric inpainting step (comparing CG+Retrieval to CG+Inpainting) nearly triples CLIP-sim from 11.5 to 27.9. Low variance across 10 random viewpoint runs (±0.1 to ±0.6) confirms viewpoint robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LayoutGPT+Retrieval [14], AnyHome wins decisively on every metric: OOB drops from 69.4 to 23.7 (a 66% reduction), CLIP-sim rises from 8.3 to 29.3 (3.5×), and CLIP-style-sim from 6.4 to 17.5 (2.7×), demonstrating that the constrained graph representation plus SDS refinement far surpasses direct few-shot LLM placement. The internal ablation CG+Inpainting (constrained graph + MVDiffusion texturing but no SDS) shows that the SDS refinement step is critical for layout quality, cutting OOB from 34.2 to 23.7 while also improving CLIP-sim by 1.4 points. Qualitatively, AnyHome's SAG-based layout generation avoids the overlapping bounding boxes and spatial incoherence seen in both LayoutGPT and diffusion-based methods like Lego-Net [72], particularly for open-vocabulary room types. For texture inpainting, AnyHome's depth-conditioned MVDiffusion + differentiable rendering pipeline produces more view-consistent textures than raw Stable Diffusion depth-conditioned images, which suffer from cross-view inconsistencies that undermine back-projection. The paper does not report a head-to-head quantitative comparison against MVDiffusion [65] or Lego-Net [72] in a shared table; these are compared qualitatively in Fig. 8, where AnyHome's structured approach yields more coherent floorplans and logically arranged furniture.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyHome sits at the intersection of three research threads: graph-constrained floorplan generation (HouseGAN++), LLM-driven scene layout planning (LayoutGPT), and SDS-based 3D refinement (DreamFusion, Fantasia3D). It departs from prior text-to-scene work by explicitly separating the problem into an amodal structural stage (bubble diagrams + constrained layout graphs) and an egocentric perceptual stage (first-person trajectory inpainting with MVDiffusion), rather than attempting end-to-end generation. The repurposing of SDS loss for object *placement* refinement (rather than shape synthesis) and the use of automatically generated egocentric camera paths for multi-view texture consistency are the main conceptual departures from the cited prior art.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that house-scale, open-vocabulary, editable 3D indoor scenes are achievable by combining LLM planning with diffusion-based texturing, which is practically relevant for interior design, game asset creation, and embodied-agent training environments. However, the quantitative evaluation relies heavily on self-ablation baselines (CG+Retrieval, CG+Inpainting) rather than strong external competitors, and the authors acknowledge that LLMs' 3D spatial reasoning and multi-view inpainting consistency remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the egocentric trajectory approach scales to multi-story or outdoor-adjacent scenes, and whether the SDS refinement step can be replaced by more efficient alternatives as 3D-native generative models mature.
