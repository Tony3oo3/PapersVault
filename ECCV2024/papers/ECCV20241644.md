## Problem

Existing point trackers (PIPs, PIPs++, TAPIR) treat each tracked point as statistically independent, ignoring strong dependencies between points on the same object or in the same scene. This independence assumption limits tracking accuracy, particularly for occluded points, and prevents the tracker from leveraging scene-level motion cues. The paper asks whether jointly tracking points while accounting for their interdependencies can substantially improve long-term tracking robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a video of T RGB frames and the starting 2D locations and times for N tracks, then outputs per-frame 2D positions and binary visibility flags for all future times. It operates causally in overlapping sliding windows of 8 frames (with 4-frame overlap), processing the video online rather than requiring all frames at once. At inference, additional support points (global or local grids) can be injected to provide context, and the model can track up to ~70k points simultaneously on a single 80 GB GPU thanks to proxy tokens.

## Method

CoTracker is a transformer that operates on a 2D token grid spanning time and track dimensions, where each token encodes position, visibility, appearance features, and RAFT-style correlation features. The key architectural innovation is cross-track attention: instead of tracking points independently, the transformer exchanges information between tracks, enabling joint estimation that exploits shared motion. To make this tractable for large N, the paper introduces K learnable proxy tokens (K≪N) that regular tracks cross-attend to, reducing track-attention cost from O(N²) to O(NK). The model is trained with unrolled recurrent training over multiple overlapping windows, optimizing a discounted sum of tracking losses across all transformer iterations and windows, which enables tracking through occlusions longer than a single window. Convolutional features are extracted at 1/4 resolution across 4 scales, and the transformer is applied M times iteratively to refine position and appearance estimates before a final visibility prediction.

## Results

On TAP-Vid-DAVIS (First protocol), CoTracker achieves AJ=62.2, δ_avg^vis=75.7, and OA=89.3, outperforming the previous best TAPIR (AJ=56.2, δ_avg^vis=70.0, OA=86.5) by 6.0 AJ points and 5.7 δ_avg^vis points. On PointOdyssey, CoTracker reaches a survival rate of 55.2% (vs. 47.0% for PIPs++) and δ_avg^occ=24.2 (vs. 18.8% for PIPs++), demonstrating superior long-term and occluded-point tracking. On Dynamic Replica, CoTracker achieves δ_avg^vis=68.9 and δ_avg^occ=37.6, surpassing TAPIR (66.1 and 27.2) by a particularly large margin on occluded points (+10.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoTracker outperforms all six baselines on the primary TAP-Vid-DAVIS metrics. Against the closest competitor TAPIR [18], CoTracker gains +6.0 AJ and +5.7 δ_avg^vis on DAVIS First, and +4.6 AJ and +5.8 δ_avg^vis on DAVIS Strided. On RGB-Stacking First, CoTracker surpasses OmniMotion [58] by +2.1 AJ and +0.9 δ_avg^vis, though OmniMotion retains a slight OA edge (90.3 vs. 89.6). On PointOdyssey, CoTracker improves survival rate from 47.0% (PIPs++ [65]) to 55.2% and occluded-point accuracy from 18.8% to 24.2%, while using a window size of only 8 frames versus PIPs++'s 128-frame window. On Dynamic Replica, the occluded-point gap is most pronounced: CoTracker achieves δ_avg^occ=37.6 versus 27.2 for TAPIR and 28.5 for PIPs++, a +10.4 and +9.1 point advantage respectively. The older baselines TAP-Net [17], PIPs [23], and MFT [42] are all substantially outperformed, with CoTracker exceeding TAP-Net by over 29 AJ points on DAVIS First.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoTracker departs from the dominant paradigm in point tracking (PIPs, PIPs++, TAPIR) where each point is tracked independently, instead introducing cross-track attention that exploits statistical dependencies between points on the same object. Architecturally, it adapts RAFT's 4D cost volume and iterative refinement to the tracking setting, and borrows the proxy-token concept from ViT registers and Perceiver IO queries to make joint attention over tens of thousands of tracks computationally tractable. The unrolled training strategy ports recurrent-network training from visual object tracking to the sliding-window point-tracking formulation, enabling long-horizon coherence beyond a single window.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoTracker establishes that joint tracking is not merely a nice-to-have but a substantial accuracy gain, particularly for occluded points (+30.6% vs +10.4% for visible), and that proxy tokens make quasi-dense tracking (70k points on one 80 GB GPU) practical for the first time. This opens the door to applications requiring dense motion fields without per-point optimization, such as video editing, 3D reconstruction, and motion analysis. Limitations remain: training on purely synthetic Kubric data causes failures on reflections and shadows, and the model still makes errors a human would not. The authors note the architecture is flexible enough to integrate 3D reconstruction in future work, suggesting a path toward unified dense 3D scene understanding from video.
