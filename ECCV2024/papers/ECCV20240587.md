## Problem

Diffusion models struggle to accurately interpret complex, imaginative, or scientifically grounded prompts (e.g., 'a rat hunting a lion,' 'a drop of water on the ISS') because their training data lacks such unconventional scenarios, leading to biased or incoherent outputs. Additionally, no dedicated benchmark existed to systematically evaluate text-to-image models on these realistic-analytical and creative-imaginative challenges.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240587/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural-language text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image as output. The prompt is first decomposed by an LLM into structured components—bounding-box layout for main subjects, per-object textual descriptions, background scene description, and negative prompts—which then condition the frozen Stable Diffusion 2.1 model. No user-provided layout or reference images are required; all spatial and semantic structure is inferred from the text alone.

## Method

RFNet is a training-free, two-stage pipeline that couples a frozen LLM with a frozen Stable Diffusion model. In Stage 1, the LLM expands the user prompt into a detailed scene specification (layout, object descriptions, background, negative prompts), and a Semantic Alignment Assessment (SAA) module resolves conflicting per-object descriptions by selecting the candidate set with maximum pairwise cosine similarity among object vectors. In Stage 2, a two-step diffusion process first generates each foreground object independently within its bounding box using a cross-attention constraint that enforces attention inside the box and suppresses it outside (Eqs. 1–3), then integrates all objects into the background via a replacement operation at early timesteps combined with a guidance constraint (matching the object's saved cross-attention map) and a suppression constraint (minimizing cross-object interference outside each box) (Eqs. 4–5). The entire pipeline requires no parameter updates to either the LLM or the diffusion model.

## Results

On RFBench, RFNet achieves a GPT4Score of 0.719 (average) and GPT4-CLIP of 0.607 (average), outperforming all baselines. The most striking gains are in the Creativity & Imagination category, where RFNet scores 0.627 GPT4Score (43% above Stable Diffusion's 0.440) and 0.623 GPT4-CLIP (13% above SDXL's 0.619). In the Realistic & Analytical category, RFNet reaches 0.833 GPT4Score, a 25% improvement over Stable Diffusion and a 61% improvement over MultiDiffusion. On the DrawBench subset, RFNet outperforms Imagen on 7 out of 10 prompts, with the largest margin on 'An elephant under the sea' (0.900 vs. 0.300).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RFNet outperforms all six RFBench baselines on both GPT4-CLIP and GPT4Score across both categories. Against Stable Diffusion (the base model), RFNet gains 33% on average GPT4Score (0.719 vs. 0.541), with the largest relative improvement in Creativity & Imagination (43%). SDXL is the closest competitor on GPT4-CLIP C&I (0.619 vs. 0.623) but trails RFNet substantially on GPT4Score R&A (0.567 vs. 0.833), indicating SDXL struggles with factual/scientific prompts. LMD (LLM-grounded Diffusion), the method RFNet builds upon, is notably weaker on Realistic & Analytical tasks (GPT4Score 0.550 vs. 0.833), confirming that the SAA module and two-step synthesis are critical for scientific reasoning. MultiDiffusion and BoxDiff are the weakest baselines overall, with average GPT4Scores of 0.504 and 0.548 respectively. On the DrawBench subset, RFNet beats Imagen on 7/10 prompts but loses on 'A blue coloured pizza' (0.125 vs. 0.425) and ties at 0.000 on two extremely difficult prompts ('A fish eating a pelican', 'A horse riding an astronaut'), suggesting residual limitations on highly counterintuitive attribute combinations.

## Relation to Prior Work

RFNet builds directly on LMD's two-step generation pipeline (in-depth object generation followed by background integration) and its use of LLMs to extract spatial layouts from prompts. It departs from LMD by introducing the Semantic Alignment Assessment (SAA) module to resolve conflicting object descriptions and by replacing LMD's loss-based layout fixing with guidance and suppression constraints on cross-attention maps. The broader positioning sits within the LLM-assisted text-to-image generation line (LayoutGPT, RPG, SLD), but RFNet is distinguished by its explicit focus on prompts requiring scientific reasoning, cultural knowledge, or imaginative scenarios, for which it introduces the RFBench evaluation framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RFBench fills a genuine gap by providing a structured benchmark for evaluating text-to-image models on prompts that demand creative or domain-specific reasoning, a dimension largely absent from prior benchmarks like DrawBench. The training-free design makes RFNet practical for practitioners who cannot retrain diffusion models, and the SAA module offers a reusable mechanism for resolving LLM output ambiguity. However, the evaluation relies heavily on GPT-4-based metrics (GPT4-CLIP, GPT4Score) to judge outputs influenced by LLM reasoning, raising circularity concerns; absolute scores remain moderate (0.719 GPT4Score average), and the DrawBench subset shows the method still fails on several prompts. Open questions include scalability beyond 512×512 resolution, compatibility with newer diffusion backends, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D generation.
