## Problem

Mamba, a selective state space model, achieves linear-complexity sequence modeling that matches or exceeds Transformers on 1D text, but its extension to multi-dimensional data (images, video, 3D volumes) remains unexplored. Unlike the earlier LTI-based S4/S4ND, Mamba's input-dependent parameters break the convolutional trick that S4ND used to parallelize multi-dimensional scans, making a direct ND extension non-trivial.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240866/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary N-dimensional arrays (2D RGB images, 3D video clips, 3D atmospheric fields, 3D CT volumes), patchifies them into a 1D sequence of length L = ∏Dᵢ while retaining the original ND shape metadata, and reshapes the output back to the ND grid before a task-specific head. Patch sizes are task-dependent (e.g., 8×8 for ImageNet, 2×2×2 for ERA5 weather, 16×16×16 for BTCV CT), and the sequence length scales as the product of all spatial/temporal dimensions after patching.

## Method

Mamba-ND stacks standard 1D Mamba layers as black boxes and alternates the scan ordering of the flattened sequence at each layer: H+H−W+W− for 2D data and H+H−W+W−T+T− for 3D data, where each symbol denotes a specific axis permutation and direction. This block-level alternating-directional design gives every layer a global receptive field on the full sequence while ensuring information can flow between any two spatially adjacent patches across successive layers. The paper systematically ablates more complex alternatives—layer-level Bi-SSM, ND-SSM, multi-head SSM, bi-directional and quad-directional block groupings, and scan factorization into shorter parallel sequences—and finds that the simple alternating design consistently outperforms all of them, likely because parallel multi-directional processing reduces effective model depth. The architecture introduces no new parameters or modified kernels relative to the vanilla Mamba layer; the entire multi-dimensional capability comes from reordering the input sequence between layers.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Mamba-2D-S achieves 81.7% top-1 accuracy with only 24M parameters, surpassing ViT-B (77.9%, 86M) by +3.8 points while using 20.7% fewer parameters. On video action recognition, Mamba-3D reaches 60.9% on HMDB-51 and 89.6% on UCF-101 with 36M parameters, and 81.9% on Kinetics-400 (64 frames) with 39M parameters. For weather forecasting on ERA5, Mamba-3D achieves 90.1 ACC with 50M parameters, outperforming Cli-ViT (89.3 ACC, 108M) by +0.7 ACC with 44.5% fewer parameters. On BTCV 3D segmentation, Mamba-3D-UNETR-B reaches 84.7 DICE with 107M parameters, beating UNETR (81.4 DICE, 101M) by +3.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mamba-ND consistently outperforms or matches all baselines across tasks. On ImageNet-1K, Mamba-2D-S (81.7%) beats ViT-B (77.9%), Hyena-ViT-B (78.5%), and S4ND-ViT-B (80.4%), while Mamba-2D-B (83.0%) is within 0.5% of Swin-B (83.5%) at comparable parameter counts. On video, Mamba-3D (60.9% HMDB-51, 89.6% UCF-101) surpasses Video-Swin-S (58.1%, 88.7%), S4ND-ConvNeXt-3D (56.6%, 69.3%), and ConvNeXt-I3D (53.5%, 87.6%); on Kinetics-400, Mamba-3D/64 (81.9%) edges out TimeSformer-L (80.7%), ViViT-L (80.6%), and Swin-B (80.6%) with only 39M vs. 88–310M parameters. For weather forecasting, Mamba-3D (90.1 ACC) beats both Cli-ViT (89.3) and Pangu-Weather (89.0) while using roughly half the parameters of Cli-ViT. On BTCV segmentation, Mamba-3D-UNETR-B (84.7 DICE) outperforms UNETR (81.4) and Swin-UNETR (83.3) at similar or lower parameter counts. On the Breakfast long-video task, Mamba-3D (91.2%) surpasses Distant Supervision/TimeSformer (89.9%), TranS4mer (90.3%), and ViS4mer (88.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

Mamba-ND extends the selective state space model (Mamba) from 1D text sequences to arbitrary multi-dimensional data by introducing a scan-ordering mechanism that alternates row-major traversals across layers. It departs from S4ND, which extended the earlier LTI-based S4 to ND data via a convolutional outer-product trick that is inapplicable to selective (non-LTI) SSMs. The design draws conceptual inspiration from Bi-directional LSTMs and multi-head attention for achieving multi-directional information flow, but ultimately finds that the simplest alternating-directional strategy outperforms all more elaborate layer-level or block-level multi-directional variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mamba-ND provides a practical, linear-complexity alternative to Transformers for 2D and 3D data, achieving competitive or superior accuracy with substantially fewer parameters across image classification, video recognition, weather forecasting, and medical segmentation. The key open question is why the naive alternating-ordering design works so well—there is no theoretical explanation for why sequential alternation between a few fixed scan orders captures multi-directional dependencies as effectively as parallel multi-head or bidirectional SSM kernels. Scan factorization, which could reduce sequence length, currently incurs significant memory overhead and remains an engineering challenge for future work.
