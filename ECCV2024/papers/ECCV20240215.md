## Problem

Existing learned stereo image compression methods follow a unidirectional paradigm where one view is compressed first and used as a reference for the other, producing imbalanced reconstruction quality between views that is detrimental to both human perception and downstream vision tasks. The few bidirectional approaches that exist (e.g., BCSIC) rely on separate 2D convolutions per view and a plain spatial-context entropy model, limiting their ability to exploit rich inter-view correlations across multiple dimensions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stereo image pair (left and right RGB views, e.g., 1080×860 on InStereo2K or 2048×1024 on Cityscapes) and processes them jointly as a 3D tensor through shared 3D convolutional layers rather than encoding each view independently. The output is a compact bitstream (latents plus hyperpriors, entropy-coded via arithmetic coding) and a reconstructed stereo pair with balanced quality across both views. A fast variant (BiSIC-Fast) replaces the spatial auto-regressive loop with a two-pass stereo-checkerboard, reducing runtime from ~167 s to ~23 s per pair while retaining most of the compression gain.

## Method

BiSIC replaces the conventional 2D-convolution codec with a 3D-convolution backbone (encoder, decoder, hyper-encoder, hyper-decoder) that ingests both views simultaneously, inherently capturing aligned inter-view features. Between 3D convolutional layers, a bidirectional mutual attention block performs two cross-attention stages (cross-key and cross-query) followed by self-attention, using an efficient attention formulation that keeps the attention map size proportional to the embedding channel rather than spatial resolution. The entropy model is a symmetric cross-dimensional design that conditions the Gaussian likelihood on four factors: hyperpriors, stereo spatial context (via masked 3D convolution over causal regions of both views), stereo channel context (via the mutual attention block over previously decoded channel slices of both views), and stereo dependency, all fused by a shared aggregation network. Training uses a standard rate-distortion objective (MSE or MS-SSIM distortion plus estimated bitrate) optimized with Adam over 1.5 M steps, with λ swept across six values to cover the RD curve.

## Results

BiSIC achieves the best BDBR (Bjontegaard Delta Bitrate) across both datasets and both metrics. On InStereo2K, BiSIC attains −48.07% BDBR (PSNR) and −61.13% BDBR (MS-SSIM) relative to BPG, outperforming the next-best method ECSIC by 4.36% and 5.48% respectively. On Cityscapes, BiSIC reaches −57.49% (PSNR) and −67.98% (MS-SSIM), surpassing ECSIC by 5.43% and 3.02%. The fast variant BiSIC-Fast retains most of the gains (−45.35% / −59.36% on InStereo2K) while reducing runtime from 167.25 s to 22.82 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiSIC outperforms all nine baselines on both datasets for both PSNR and MS-SSIM BDBR. Against the strongest learned baseline ECSIC (unidirectional), BiSIC saves an additional 4.36% bits (PSNR) and 5.48% bits (MS-SSIM) on InStereo2K, and 5.43% / 3.02% on Cityscapes. Compared to the state-of-the-art bidirectional method BCSIC, BiSIC achieves a further 6.85%–15.46% BDBR reduction, attributed to the 3D convolution backbone and cross-dimensional entropy model. Against the latest video codec VVC, BiSIC delivers an additional 12.76% (PSNR) and 30.08% (MS-SSIM) bit saving on InStereo2K, while on Cityscapes the gap narrows to 1.24% (PSNR) but remains 23.94% (MS-SSIM). The fast variant BiSIC-Fast sacrifices only 2.72–5.53% BDBR relative to BiSIC while cutting runtime from 167.25 s to 22.82 s, making it competitive with SASIC (20.24 s) in speed while retaining far superior compression quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiSIC extends the learned image compression framework (Ballé et al., Minnen et al.) to the stereo setting by replacing the standard 2D convolutional backbone with 3D convolutions that jointly process both views, and by augmenting the entropy model from spatial-only context to a cross-dimensional formulation that aggregates hyperprior, spatial, channel, and stereo dependencies. It departs from the closest prior bidirectional method (BCSIC, Lei et al. 2022) in two key ways: using a shared 3D-convolution codec instead of separate 2D networks, and introducing a mutual attention block for global inter-view feature exchange. The fast variant (BiSIC-Fast) extends the checkerboard context model (He et al. 2021) from single-image to stereo-checkerboard, and the efficient attention mechanism (Shen et al. 2021) is repurposed to keep attention maps size-invariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional design directly addresses the quality imbalance inherent in unidirectional stereo codecs, which is important for both human perception and downstream stereo vision tasks. The practical impact is reinforced by BiSIC-Fast, which reduces runtime from 167s to 23s while retaining most of the compression gain, making learned stereo compression more deployable. Open questions include whether the 3D-convolution backbone generalizes to more than two views (multi-view compression), whether the cross-dimensional entropy model's gains persist at very low bitrates, and how the method performs on real-time or mobile-constrained hardware where the 3D convolutions and attention blocks add significant compute.
