## Problem

Contrastive image-text pretraining (CLIP, SigLIP) learns strong global alignment but does not explicitly encourage the image encoder to capture rich local semantics needed for dense prediction tasks like segmentation and detection. Self-supervised methods such as DINO learn local-to-global consistency effectively but produce features without any language grounding, making them unusable for open-vocabulary inference. SILC addresses this gap by unifying both paradigms into a single pretraining framework.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at two scales: global views covering 40–100% of the original image area (resized to 256×256) for the contrastive loss, and local views covering 5–40% of the area (resized to 96×96) for the self-distillation loss, alongside paired text captions. The primary output is a shared image-text embedding space (dimension J) used for zero-shot classification and retrieval; the patch-level features from the ViT backbone additionally support downstream dense tasks such as segmentation maps, detection boxes, captions, and VQA answers when paired with appropriate decoders.

## Method

SILC pairs the standard image-text contrastive objective (CLIP or SigLIP) with a DINO-style local-to-global self-distillation loss on the image encoder. An EMA teacher (momentum λ=0.966, kept fixed) processes global crops while the student processes local crops; the student is trained to match the teacher's sharpened, centered output via cross-entropy, with a projection head expanding the embedding from dimension J to K=65536. Two critical design choices ensure the objectives remain complementary: (1) every global view used in the self-distillation loss must also participate in the image-text contrastive loss so the two objectives do not diverge, and (2) the EMA momentum is held constant rather than scheduled to 1.0, preventing the teacher from freezing and losing the benefit of image-text supervision. The model is trained on 20B example-seen from WebLI, then fine-tuned for 1B example-seen on a cleaner 100M-pair subset to yield the final SILC model.

## Results

SILC-C (ViT-B/16) achieves 76.2% zero-shot ImageNet top-1 accuracy and 72.3% CIFAR-100, outperforming CLIP (WebLI) by 2.1 and 3.9 points respectively, and SigLIP by 1.1 and 2.5 points. On COCO retrieval, SILC-C reaches 66.1% I2T@1 and 49.1% T2I@1, a 4.4 and 5.2 point gain over CLIP (WebLI). For zero-shot segmentation, SILC-C sets new SOTA on 4/5 benchmarks, reaching 19.3 mIOU on ADE-20K-150 and 26.9 on Cityscapes. In open-vocabulary segmentation with CAT-Seg, SILC-C-B/16 achieves 36.6 mIOU on A-150 (+9.4 over CLIP) and 61.2 on PC-59 (+3.7), while SILC-S* improves open-vocabulary detection to 42.7 AP on COCO (+2.3 over CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (WebLI) [47], SILC-C wins on every metric in Table 1: +2.1 pts zero-shot ImageNet, +3.9 pts CIFAR-100, +1.8/+1.1/+0.5 pts on 1/5/10-shot ImageNet, and +4.4/+5.2 pts on COCO I2T/T2I retrieval. Against SigLIP [64], SILC-S similarly dominates, gaining +1.5 pts zero-shot ImageNet and +3.8 pts on COCO T2I@1. In zero-shot segmentation (Table 2), SILC-C surpasses the previous SOTA TCL [10] by +4.3 mIOU on ADE-20K-150, +2.9 on Pascal Context-59, and +4.9 on Cityscapes, while also beating MaskCLIP [71], ReCo [50], and GroupViT [56] on 4/5 datasets. For open-vocabulary segmentation (Table 3), replacing CLIP with SILC-C in CAT-Seg [13] yields +9.4 mIOU on A-150 and +5.0 on A-847, with SILC-L/16 even outperforming the much larger CLIP-G/14. In open-vocabulary detection (Table 4), SILC-S* with OWLv2 [37] improves COCO AP from 40.4 to 42.7 (+2.3) and LVIS AP_rare from 29.2 to 32.4 (+3.2) over CLIP (WebLI). In the LiT-Decoder multi-task setup (Table 5), SILC-S* gains +3.6 CIDEr on COCO captioning and +1.5 on VQAv2 over SigLIP, confirming benefits for local-semantic tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

SILC unifies two previously separate pretraining paradigms: image-text contrastive learning (CLIP, SigLIP) and local-to-global consistency via self-distillation (DINO, DINOv2). While prior work has attempted to incorporate self-supervised signals into VLMs (MaskCLIP with masked self-distillation, SLIP with SimCLR), none had adopted the EMA-teacher local-to-global consistency objective that proved so effective for dense prediction in pure vision SSL. The paper's key design insight is that the two objectives are complementary only when global views in the self-distillation loss are also aligned with text, preventing the objectives from diverging during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SILC demonstrates that a single, conceptually simple addition (DINO-style self-distillation) to the standard CLIP/SigLIP training loop yields consistent improvements across an unusually broad range of tasks—zero-shot classification, retrieval, segmentation, detection, captioning, and VQA—without task-specific architectural changes. This suggests that the contrastive objective's well-known weakness for local feature learning can be addressed at the pretraining stage rather than through downstream fine-tuning, potentially simplifying the pipeline for open-vocabulary dense prediction. Open questions include whether the fixed-EMA-momentum and global-view-alignment design choices are optimal at larger scales, and whether the gains persist when moving to generative or hybrid VLM objectives beyond contrastive learning.
