## Problem

Existing UDC image restoration datasets are restricted to the RGB domain and fail to capture the degradations present in real commercial smartphone panels, limiting both practical applicability and the potential for sensor-level restoration. Additionally, UDC degradations are inherently panel-specific, yet no prior method exploits the disparities between degradations caused by different panels to improve restoration performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241285/cropped_page1_idx17.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 16-bit raw UDC images with a G-B-R-G Bayer pattern (normalized to [0,1]) as input and produces a restored RGB image as output. Training uses 512×512 patches from a dataset of 400 scenes captured through two commercial panels (ZTE Axon 30 5G and Samsung Galaxy Z-Fold 3) via a monitor-based imaging system. For evaluation on prior RGB-only datasets, a pseudo-raw version is created by reversing the demosaicing step, and the network still outputs RGB.

## Method

DREUDC is a two-stage framework. First, a six-convolutional-layer encoder is trained unsupervised with triplet loss, where triplets are constructed by pairing same-content images captured through different panels: patches from the same panel (same content, different location) serve as anchor-positive pairs, while patches from the other panel at the same location serve as negatives, yielding a 256-dimensional panel-specific degradation representation. Second, a U-shaped Transformer restoration network using Frequency domain-based Self-Attention Solver (FSAS) blocks incorporates this representation via DREblocks, which generate channel-wise calibration coefficients (via a 1×1 convolution on the representation) that modulate the attention feature maps through element-wise multiplication. The encoder is trained first and then frozen while the restoration network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss, enabling cross-panel knowledge transfer without requiring ground-truth degradation parameters.

## Results

On the proposed raw UDC dataset, DREUDC achieves 26.66 dB PSNR / 0.9368 SSIM on ZTE Axon 30 and 29.04 dB PSNR / 0.9511 SSIM on Samsung Galaxy Z-Fold 3 (RAW-to-RGB setting), surpassing the best RGB-to-RGB baseline BNUDC by 0.81 dB and 0.50 dB respectively. The method uses only 6.44M parameters and 200.07G MACs, making it more efficient than most competitors (e.g., BNUDC at 993.38G MACs, AWNet at 1.53T MACs). On the Zhou et al. T-OLED/P-OLED dataset, DREUDC achieves 37.55 dB / 0.9778 SSIM (T-OLED) and 31.17 dB / 0.9521 SSIM (P-OLED), outperforming DE-UNET by 0.85 dB and 2.59 dB respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREUDC outperforms all 9 baselines on both Axon 30 and Z-Fold 3 panels. Against the strongest RGB-to-RGB UDC restoration method BNUDC [15], DREUDC gains +0.81 dB PSNR on Axon 30 (26.66 vs. 25.85) and +0.50 dB on Z-Fold 3 (29.04 vs. 28.54), while using roughly 5× fewer MACs (200G vs. 993G). Among RAW-to-RGB baselines, DREUDC beats DE-UNET [38] by +2.46 dB (Axon 30) and +1.75 dB (Z-Fold 3), and substantially outperforms PyNet [14] and AWNet [7] by over 4 dB on Axon 30. On the Zhou et al. T-OLED/P-OLED dataset, DREUDC surpasses DE-UNET by +0.85 dB (T-OLED) and +2.59 dB (P-OLED), confirming generalizability. The ablation (Table 4) further shows that the unsupervised triplet-based representation learning contributes an additional +0.28 dB (Axon 30) and +0.33 dB (Z-Fold 3) over a model with channel calibration but no representation learning, validating that the learned panel-specific priors—not merely extra parameters—drive the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: UDC image restoration (Zhou et al., Feng et al., Song et al.), raw-sensor-domain image processing (CycleISP, DeepISP, raw deblurring), and unsupervised degradation representation learning (Wang et al. for blind super-resolution). It departs from prior UDC work by being the first to operate in the raw sensor domain with data from commercial smartphone panels, and by repurposing triplet-loss-based representation learning (originally from face recognition and person re-identification) to encode panel-specific degradation signatures as implicit priors. The restoration backbone borrows FSAS (a frequency-domain Transformer) and a U-shaped architecture, but the core novelty lies in the cross-panel representation learning strategy rather than in the network design itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of a well-aligned raw-domain dataset from two commercial UDC panels (ZTE Axon 30, Samsung Z-Fold 3) fills a critical gap: prior datasets were RGB-only, synthetic, or from prototype panels, limiting practical evaluation. The finding that degradation representations learned from one panel transfer to improve restoration on another panel opens a path toward panel-agnostic UDC restoration as smartphone manufacturers iterate on display technology. Limitations include the monitor-based capture setup (no natural scenes), only two panels, and a simplified ISP for evaluation; open questions include whether the triplet-based representation scales to many panels, whether it works with truly natural (non-displayed) scenes, and whether the raw-domain advantage persists under full end-to-end neural ISP training.
