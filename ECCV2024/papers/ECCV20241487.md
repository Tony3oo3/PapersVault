## Problem

Existing event-based geometric model fitting methods (e.g., CMax, Pro-STR) are nonlinear, require careful initialization, and rely on a constant-motion assumption that breaks under agile or sudden speed changes. There is a lack of efficient, deterministic solvers that can provide closed-form estimates and handle instantaneous first-order kinematics without these constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241487/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse event-based normal flow vectors extracted by locally fitting planes to spatio-temporal event clouds on the image plane. The linear solver operates on K normal flow estimates at a single time instant, while the continuous-time solver processes normal flow observations asynchronously over a temporal window. Outputs are structured as instantaneous motion parameters (angular velocity, linear velocity), scene depth, full optical flow, or a 9-element differential homography vector, depending on the specific sub-problem.

## Method

The key insight is to replace the full optical flow in the motion field equation with a normal-flow constraint (n(x)ᵀ u(x;θ) = ‖n(x)‖²) that circumvents the partial observability of normal flow while remaining linear in the unknown parameters. By combining this constraint with the motion field equation, differential epipolar geometry, and differential homography, the authors derive linear systems for a family of motion-and-structure problems. A fast linear solver (with RANSAC for robustness) yields closed-form, deterministic solutions that serve as initializers for existing nonlinear methods. A continuous-time nonlinear solver parameterizes the model parameters with cubic B-splines, enabling state estimation over a larger temporal window and accommodating sudden speed variations without the constant-motion assumption.

## Results

On the 6-DoF motion tracking task (Table 3), the proposed linear solver achieves angular velocity errors of 1.31 deg/s and linear velocity errors of 0.01 m/s on corner_slow, compared to 12.39 deg/s / 0.34 m/s for CMax and 5.19 deg/s / 0.13 m/s for Pro-STR. On the synthetic patterns_6dof sequence, the linear solver reports 0.56 deg/s angular error and 0.12 m/s linear error, roughly 20× more accurate than CMax (12.94 deg/s, 1.02 m/s). For angular velocity estimation alone (Table 1), the linear solver achieves 3.30 deg/s average error on ground_rotation versus 9.48 for CMax and 31.07 for Pro-STR. Combining the linear solver as an initializer with CMax yields the best overall results (e.g., 0.35 deg/s on patterns_rotation), confirming its role as a complementary deterministic initializer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two state-of-the-art nonlinear event-based motion estimation methods: CMax (Gallego et al., CVPR 2018) and Pro-STR (Huang et al., CVPR 2023). The proposed linear solver consistently outperforms both baselines across all evaluated tasks (angular velocity, 6-DoF tracking, and differential homography) by a large margin — e.g., reducing angular velocity RMSE from 14.39 deg/s (CMax) and 7.84 deg/s (Pro-STR) to 1.79 deg/s on corner_slow. The linear solver also serves as an effective initializer: combining it with CMax (Ours+CMax) yields the best angular velocity results on most sequences (e.g., 0.35 deg/s on patterns_rotation vs. 5.95 for CMax alone), demonstrating that the deterministic closed-form solution guides the nonlinear optimizer into the correct convergence basin. Notably, on some 6-DoF sequences the linear solver alone outperforms the Ours+CMax combination, which the authors attribute to CMax's constant-motion assumption being violated under agile motion. The continuous-time nonlinear solver further outperforms both CMax and Pro-STR on segments with sudden speed variations (Fig. 4b), where the constant-motion assumption breaks down for the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in event-based geometric model fitting (CMax, EMin, Pro-STR), which iteratively optimizes nonlinear event-alignment metrics over time-windowed event data under a constant-motion assumption. Instead, it reformulates the problem around instantaneous event-based normal flow observations, deriving linear constraints by combining the normal-flow constraint with differential epipolar geometry, the motion field equation, and differential homography. The continuous-time nonlinear solver builds on B-spline representations previously applied to event camera odometry (Mueggler et al., Guo et al.), but applies them to a family of geometric model fitting problems rather than a single tracking task. The normal-flow constraint itself draws on classical aperture-problem theory from frame-based vision, repurposed here to overcome the partial observability inherent in event data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear solver provides a fast, deterministic, closed-form initialization that complements existing nonlinear methods, and the continuous-time solver removes the constant-motion assumption that limits current SOTA under agile motion. However, the method does not resolve motion and structure simultaneously, and its performance is bottlenecked by the quality of normal flow extraction, which can fail on densely repetitive textures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-planar, dynamic scenes and whether the normal flow quality limitation can be mitigated without sacrificing the linear formulation. The practical impact is significant for real-time event-based perception pipelines where initialization speed and robustness to sudden motion changes are critical.
