## Problem

WiFi-based human pose estimation faces a persistent trade-off between accuracy and computational cost: high-performing methods like MetaFi++ require over 26M parameters and 500+ GFLOPs, while lightweight alternatives such as WiLDAR sacrifice reliability. The paper asks whether it is possible to achieve superior pose estimation accuracy from raw WiFi CSI signals while maintaining a very low computational footprint suitable for large-scale deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student network consumes raw WiFi CSI tensors of shape (Tx × Rx × Subcarrier × Time) at 5 GHz — 1×3×314×10 on MM-Fi and 3×3×30×5 on WiPose — without any pre-processing beyond optional denoising filters. The output is a set of 2D skeleton keypoints (17 points on MM-Fi, 18 on WiPose) in pixel coordinates, which are converted to 3D poses by appending a constant depth vector. Ground-truth keypoints are generated by a pre-trained HRNet-w48 teacher on synchronized multi-view RGB images rather than from manual annotation.

## Method

HPE-Li employs a teacher-student framework where a frozen HRNet-w48 produces 2D keypoint supervision and a lightweight student network (DSKNet) predicts keypoints from WiFi CSI using MSE loss. The core architectural contribution is DSKConv, a convolutional block that extends standard selective kernel (SK) convolution by applying attention in both the channel domain (CwSKA) and the frequency domain (FwSKA), enabling adaptive kernel-size selection across two axes that align with the structure of WiFi CSI. Each DSKConv instance runs N parallel branches with different kernel sizes (implemented via grouped and dilated convolutions), merges them element-wise, then uses two separate softmax attention mechanisms — one over channel-wise GAP features and one over frequency-wise GAP features — to compute per-branch weights, and finally concatenates the channel- and frequency-weighted outputs. This dual-domain attention allows the network to dynamically emphasize different receptive fields depending on both which antenna channels and which subcarrier frequencies carry the most informative pose cues, all while keeping the parameter count around 1.6–3.5 M and FLOPs under 5 G.

## Results

On MM-Fi P3-S1, HPE-Li achieves PCK50 of 85.12%, MPJPE of 149.43 mm, and PA-MPJPE of 92.52 mm, outperforming all six baselines on every metric. On WiPose, it reaches PCK50 of 94.27%, MPJPE of 15.85 mm, and PA-MPJPE of 19.21 mm, again the best across all methods. Critically, HPE-Li uses only 1.66M parameters and 2.42G FLOPs on MM-Fi (3.49M / 5.18G on WiPose), roughly 16–21× fewer parameters than the strongest competitors (PerUnet ~34M, Wi-Mose ~36M, MetaFi++ ~26M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPE-Li outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric (PCK, MPJPE, PA-MPJPE) on both datasets. On MM-Fi P3-S1, it beats the closest competitor PerUnet by 1.56% PCK50 (85.12 vs 83.56) and 5.23 mm MPJPE (149.43 vs 154.66), while using only 1.66M parameters versus PerUnet's 34.51M. Against MetaFi++, the transformer-based approach with 507.89G FLOPs, HPE-Li gains 3.37% PCK50 and 15.02 mm MPJPE at roughly 1/200th the FLOPs. On WiPose, HPE-Li leads PerUnet by 5.53% PCK50 (94.27 vs 88.74) and 3.43 mm PA-MPJPE (19.21 vs 22.64), with 3.49M vs 33.85M parameters. Compared to the lightweight WiLDAR (1.63M params), HPE-Li achieves 5.86% higher PCK50 and 20.95 mm lower MPJPE on MM-Fi while using half the FLOPs (2.42G vs 4.91G). The consistent margin across all baselines—particularly the 5–15 mm MPJPE advantage over the nearest competitors—demonstrates that the dual selective kernel mechanism captures both channel and frequency information more effectively than fixed-kernel CNNs, channel-only attention (SKConv), or transformer-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Selective Kernel Network (SKNet) [25] by adding a frequency-domain attention branch (FwSKA) alongside the standard channel-domain branch (CwSKA), specifically motivated by the dual structure of WiFi CSI data (channels × subcarriers). It also adopts the teacher-student paradigm [28], using a pre-trained RGB pose estimator (HRNet-w48) to generate supervision for a WiFi-only student network, a setup previously explored in cross-modal distillation but not combined with selective kernel convolutions. Among WiFi-based HPE methods (Wi-Pose, WiSPPN, MetaFi++, PerUnet), HPE-Li departs from the trend of scaling up model capacity (26–36M parameters) by instead designing a lightweight multi-branch convolutional block (DSKConv) that adaptively selects kernel sizes across both domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA accuracy on both MM-Fi and WiPose with roughly 1/16th the parameters and 1/200th the FLOPs of the heaviest baselines, making WiFi-based HPE feasible on resource-constrained edge devices. However, the 3D pose is obtained by appending a constant to 2D predictions rather than true 3D reconstruction, and the model's sensitivity to AWGN and salt-and-pepper noise (mitigated only by simple filters) limits real-world robustness. Open questions include whether DSKConv generalizes to other wireless sensing tasks, whether the frequency-domain attention benefit persists with different WiFi hardware (subcarrier counts, antenna configurations), and how the approach scales to multi-person or occluded scenarios.
