## Problem

Existing class-imbalanced semi-supervised learning (CISSL) algorithms explicitly or implicitly assume that the class distribution of unlabeled data matches that of labeled data. When this assumption fails in practice, the classifier is rebalanced using incorrect class weights, leading to degraded performance—particularly for minority classes that get over-represented in predictions. This paper addresses the gap by estimating the unknown unlabeled class distribution and using it to properly rebalance training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of labeled RGB images with class labels and unlabeled RGB images (32×32 or 64×64 depending on dataset), where the unlabeled set's class distribution is unknown and may differ from the labeled set's. During training, both weak augmentations (flipping, cropping) and strong augmentations (Cutout, RandomAugment) are applied to unlabeled samples for consistency regularization. The model outputs K-dimensional class probability vectors for unseen test images, evaluated via balanced accuracy and geometric mean.

## Method

RECD estimates the unknown class distribution of the unlabeled set via Monte Carlo approximation, maintaining an exponential moving average (EMA) of the auxiliary balanced classifier's (ABC) predicted class probabilities over unlabeled minibatches. This estimated distribution replaces the labeled-set distribution in the Bernoulli sampling parameter of ABC's 0/1 mask for unlabeled samples, ensuring the classifier is rebalanced to the correct extent even under severe distribution mismatch. Additionally, the paper introduces AAFM (Adaptively Adjusted Feature Multiplier), which extends Feature Clusters Compression to CISSL by scaling extracted features with class-specific multipliers that incorporate both the labeled imbalance ratio and the estimated unlabeled distribution, further modulated by per-class confidence EMAs to stabilize training. The total loss combines a rebalanced classification loss on labeled data, a rebalanced consistency loss on unlabeled data (both with AAFM applied to features), and the backbone SSL loss, with pseudo-labels for the backbone generated by the auxiliary balanced classifier rather than the backbone's own head.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-LT with matched class distributions (γ_l=γ_u), ReMixMatch+RECD achieves 88.1% bACC and 87.9% GM at γ=50, 85.4%/85.2% at γ=100, and 82.5%/82.1% at γ=150, outperforming the strongest baseline (ReMixMatch+CoSSL: 87.7%/87.6%, 84.1%/83.7%, 81.3%/80.5%). Under the critical mismatched-distribution setting (γ_l=100, γ_u=1), ReMixMatch+RECD reaches 90.3% bACC / 90.2% GM, surpassing the best baseline (ReMixMatch+SAW+cRT: 88.8%/88.6%) by ~1.5 points. On CIFAR-100-LT (γ=50), ReMixMatch+RECD achieves 49.5% bACC vs. 48.9% for ReMixMatch+CoSSL, and on Small-ImageNet-127 (64×64), FixMatch+RECD reaches 59.5% bACC vs. 53.8% for FixMatch+CoSSL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECD consistently outperforms all 15 baselines across four datasets and both matched and mismatched distribution settings. On CIFAR-10-LT (matched), ReMixMatch+RECD beats ReMixMatch+CoSSL by 0.4–1.2 bACC points and ReMixMatch+ABC by 0.2–2.0 points, with the gap widening as γ increases (e.g., +2.0 bACC at γ=150 over ABC). Under the mismatched setting (γ_l=100, γ_u=1), RECD gains ~1.5 bACC over the strongest baselines (SAW+cRT, DARP+cRT), while baselines using logit adjustment (LA) actually degrade (e.g., ReMixMatch*+SAW+LA drops to 74.2% bACC at γ_u=1). On CIFAR-100-LT, RECD leads by 0.1–0.6 bACC over CoSSL and ABC. On Small-ImageNet-127, FixMatch+RECD achieves a substantial 3.6–5.7 bACC advantage over the next-best baseline (CoSSL), demonstrating scalability to larger datasets. The paper also validates RECD's modularity by showing RECD+DARP outperforms vanilla DARP in Appendix F.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ABC (Auxiliary Balanced Classifier), which rebalances the training loss via Bernoulli-sampled 0/1 masks parameterized by the labeled class distribution, and on FCC (Feature Clusters Compression), which densifies minority-class feature clusters via class-dependent scaling factors. The key departure is that all prior CISSL methods (ABC, UDAL, CReST, DARP, DASO, SAW) explicitly or implicitly assume the unlabeled class distribution matches the labeled one; RECD breaks this assumption by estimating the unlabeled distribution through Monte Carlo approximation of the classifier's prior and using it to reparameterize both the 0/1 masks and the feature multipliers. The AAFM component further extends FCC from the fully-supervised CIL setting into CISSL by incorporating the estimated unlabeled distribution and per-class confidence EMAs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The class distribution mismatch between labeled and unlabeled sets is a common and practically important scenario that prior CISSL methods silently ignore, and RECD provides a modular, low-overhead fix that can be plugged into existing CISSL pipelines (demonstrated with both ABC and DARP). A notable open question, acknowledged by the authors, is the accuracy of the MC-based distribution estimate when unlabeled samples are scarce, which limits the method's applicability in extremely label-efficient regimes. The finding that standard logit adjustment (LA) actively degrades performance under severe mismatch is a useful caution for practitioners combining CIL and SSL techniques. Broader implications include whether the estimated-distribution approach can be extended to other rebalancing mechanisms beyond 0/1 masking, and whether the AAFM's confidence-based stabilization generalizes to deeper or transformer-based backbones.
