## Problem

Adapting large pretrained ViTs to downstream dense prediction tasks requires separate training for each model scale, incurring high computational cost and fixed performance-efficiency trade-offs. The prior stitching framework SN-Netv1 only operates on the same pretraining domain (image classification) with a limited Fast-to-Slow stitching space, producing sub-optimal trade-offs and failing to transfer to downstream tasks with different data domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241091/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images and, depending on the downstream head (SETR for segmentation, DPT for depth, Mask-RCNN for detection), produces pixel-wise segmentation masks, depth maps, or bounding boxes with instance masks. A single trained SN-Netv2 model can switch its internal network topology at inference time to satisfy arbitrary FLOPs constraints (e.g., 34G to 363G), so the same checkpoint serves multiple deployment budgets without retraining.

## Method

SN-Netv2 stitches two pretrained ViT anchors (e.g., DeiT3-S and DeiT3-L) with 1×1 convolutional stitching layers, but introduces three coupled improvements over SN-Netv1. First, Two-way Stitching (TWS) permits four routing directions (Fast-to-Slow, Slow-to-Fast, Fast-Slow-Fast, Slow-Fast-Slow), enlarging the stitching space by ~10× (134 vs. 13 configurations for DeiT3-S/L). Second, Resource-Constrained Sampling (ROS) first draws a FLOPs constraint from a pre-computed categorical distribution over the space, then samples a stitch satisfying that constraint, ensuring balanced training across all resource budgets. Third, LoRA SL freezes the least-squares-initialized stitching matrices and constrains their updates to a low-rank decomposition (rank 16), which stabilizes training when the domain gap between pretraining data and downstream task data would otherwise cause anchor performance to degrade. The entire stitched model is fine-tuned on the target dataset with standard gradient descent, and at inference the user selects any stitch to match a desired FLOPs budget.

## Results

On ADE20K, SN-Netv2+DeiT3-S/L covers 34–363G FLOPs with 45.6–51.4 mIoU in 120 A100 GPU hours, versus 249 hours to train DeiT3-S and DeiT3-L separately (75 + 174). On COCO-Stuff-10K, the same stitching achieves 40.1–48.2 mIoU in 60 GPU hours versus 130 hours for individual anchors. On COCO-2017 object detection, the smallest SN-Netv2 subnet reaches 42.9 Box AP / 37.8 Mask AP, surpassing the full-width Slimmable Net (37.4 / 34.9). On ImageNet-1K, SN-Netv2 escapes the sub-optimal low-FLOPs region that plagues SN-Netv1, with the small anchor recovering to near its original accuracy (45.6 vs. 45.5 mIoU on ADE20K at 34G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SN-Netv1, SN-Netv2 consistently wins at low FLOPs (e.g., 45.6 vs. 40.4 mIoU at 34G on ADE20K) while remaining competitive at high FLOPs (51.4 vs. 51.6 mIoU at 363G), producing a smoother Pareto frontier across all resource constraints. Compared to individually trained DeiT3 anchors, SN-Netv2 matches or exceeds anchor performance at their respective FLOPs (e.g., 51.4 vs. 52.3 mIoU for DeiT3-L on ADE20K) while covering the entire FLOPs range in a single 120-hour training run instead of 249 hours for two separate trainings. On COCO-Stuff-10K, SN-Netv2+LoRA (45.8 mIoU) outperforms full-backbone DeiT3-L+LoRA (44.8 mIoU) with faster per-iteration speed (0.65 vs. 0.90 s/iter), suggesting the stitching framework regularizes large-anchor learning. On COCO-2017 detection, the smallest SN-Netv2 subnet (42.9 Box AP) beats the full-width Slimmable Net (37.4 Box AP) by 5.5 points, and SN-Netv2 (47.0 Box AP) vastly outperforms ViTDet with depth-skipping (14.9 Box AP) at comparable FLOPs (~930G vs. ~1005G).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SN-Netv1 (Pan et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced stitchable neural networks for image classification by stitching pretrained ViT families, to downstream dense prediction tasks (segmentation, depth estimation, detection). It departs from SN-Netv1 in three coupled ways: a Two-way Stitching strategy that enlarges the architectural search space by ~10×, a Resource-Constrained Sampling scheme inspired by NAS literature to balance training across FLOPs intervals, and a LoRA-based low-rank update for stitching layers that repurposes the LoRA mechanism (originally for LLM adaptation) as a training-stabilization regularizer rather than a parameter-efficient fine-tuning tool. The work also positions itself against the broader ViT backbone adoption landscape—full fine-tuning, PEFT methods like AdaptFormer, and adapter approaches like ViT-Adapter—arguing that stitching offers a unique runtime-switchable flexibility that none of these provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a single training run of SN-Netv2 covers a wide FLOPs range (e.g., 34G–363G) with fewer GPU hours than training each ViT scale separately, while enabling runtime speed-performance tradeoff switching for diverse deployment constraints. However, the framework is currently validated only on the DeiT3 family and a handful of datasets, and the authors acknowledge that parameter-efficient approaches remain under-explored and that better training strategies for the Pareto frontier are needed. An open question is whether the stitching framework generalizes to non-plain ViTs (e.g., hierarchical or windowed architectures) and to tasks beyond dense prediction, and whether the low-rank constraint on stitching layers is truly necessary or merely a convenient regularizer that happens to work in the tested regime.
