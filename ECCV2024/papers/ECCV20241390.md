## Problem

Unsupervised OOD detection was recently formalized through data invariants, where samples violating near-constant functions of training features are flagged as OOD. However, prior work restricted invariants to affine functions (equivalent to PCA/Mahalanobis distance), which cannot capture the curved manifolds on which real data often lies. This paper asks whether learning non-linear invariants can improve U-OOD detection and, if so, how to do so in a theoretically grounded way.

## Input / Output

For images, a frozen pre-trained CNN (e.g., ResNet-101) extracts multi-scale feature vectors from L=3 layers via global average pooling; for tabular data, raw feature vectors are used directly. The method produces a single scalar OOD score per sample, computed as the sum of a layer-wise invariant violation score (ratio of test to training squared invariant error) and a normalized 2-NN distance score, aggregated across all layers. The number of invariants K per layer is set automatically via a PCA variance threshold hyperparameter p.

## Method

The core architecture is a Volume-Preserving Network (VPN), a bijective function with unimodular Jacobians built from alternating rotation layers (orthogonal transforms parameterized via matrix exponential of skew-symmetric matrices) and additive coupling layers (MLP-computed translations without scaling). Volume preservation is the key design constraint: it prevents the network from collapsing dimensions to constants (which would create spurious invariants), forcing any near-constant output dimension to correspond to a genuine data invariant. Training minimizes a forward loss (driving the first K outputs toward zero on training features) plus a backward loss (reconstructing training features after zeroing those K dimensions through the inverse network), which improves stability. At inference, the first K outputs of the VPN define the invariant function g, and a test sample's score is the sum over invariants of its squared violation normalized by the training-set mean squared error, combined additively with a multi-scale 2-NN distance score.

## Results

On the General U-OOD benchmark (73 experiments across 5 tasks), NL-Invs achieves a mean AUC of 87.9, outperforming the previous best method MahaAD (86.5) and all other baselines. NL-Invs attains the best score on three of five tasks: uni-med (77.2), shift-low-res (97.8), and shift-high-res (85.5). On the Shallow U-OOD tabular benchmark, NL-Invs reaches 93.5 mean AUC, improving over MahaAD (82.9) by 10.6 percentage points, demonstrating the benefit of non-linear over affine invariants on non-image data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NL-Invs outperforms all nine baselines in mean AUC on the General U-OOD benchmark, with the largest margins over normalizing-flow methods: Glow (47.0, +40.9), DDV (60.7, +27.2), CFlow (61.6, +26.3), and IC (71.0, +16.9). Against the strongest affine-invariant baseline MahaAD (86.5), NL-Invs gains 1.4 AUC overall, and against MSCL (84.1) it gains 3.8 AUC. However, NL-Invs does not win every individual task: MSCL leads on uni-class (96.3 vs. 93.3) and CFlow leads on uni-ano (95.7 vs. 85.8), though NL-Invs compensates with large advantages on shift-low-res (+3.5 over MahaAD) and shift-high-res (+6.9 over MahaAD). On the Shallow U-OOD tabular benchmark, NL-Invs (93.5) beats DN2 (91.4), DIF (84.1), and MahaAD (82.9), with the 10.6-point gap over MahaAD highlighting the expressiveness advantage of non-linear invariants on data where affine structure is insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the invariant-based characterization of U-OOD detection from Doorenbos et al. (ECCV 2022), which restricted invariants to affine functions (equivalent to PCA/Mahalanobis distance), by generalizing to non-linear invariants learned via a volume-preserving network (VPN). Architecturally, the VPN draws on normalizing flow components (coupling and rotation layers) but deliberately omits the generative objective and enforces unimodular Jacobians, distinguishing it from prior NF-based OOD methods (Glow, IC, CFlow, HierAD) and the Denoising Normalizing Flow. The method operates on frozen pretrained CNN features, aligning with the feature-based U-OOD line (DN2, DIF, MahaAD, MSCL) rather than training a dedicated detector from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10.6 AUC gap between non-linear and affine invariants on tabular data provides concrete evidence that the affine restriction in prior invariant-based U-OOD is a genuine expressiveness bottleneck, not just a theoretical nicety. The loss landscape analysis showing that training loss correlates with OOD performance is notable because most U-OOD methods optimize proxy losses with no such guarantee, suggesting the invariant formulation offers a more principled optimization target. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional feature spaces without the multi-scale CNN decomposition, whether volume preservation is strictly necessary (vs. near-volume-preserving), and whether the method can be made fully self-supervised without relying on ImageNet-pretrained backbones.
