## Problem

Multi-view stereo depth estimation relies on matching textures between source and target frames, which fails for unmatched surfaces, sparse depth planes at greater depths, and scenes requiring wide baselines. While prior geometry from previous frames could in principle help, naively feeding a rendered depth map into an MVS network does not improve performance. The paper asks how to properly integrate self-generated geometric hints—both short-term (incremental) and long-term (revisited locations)—into a real-time depth estimation pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a live sequence of posed RGB images (512×384 input, 256×192 output) with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus optionally a rendered depth map and a per-pixel confidence map from a persistent TSDF volume (2 cm resolution, max depth 3.5 m). The confidence map encodes how certain the prior geometry is at each pixel (1.0 = high confidence, 0.0 = low), and pixels with no prior geometry are set to depth −1 and confidence 0. The output is a per-frame depth map that is immediately fused back into the TSDF, enabling the next frame to benefit from the updated geometry; the full TSDF can be meshed via Marching Cubes for 3D reconstruction.

## Method

Building on SimpleRecon's 2D-convolution MVS architecture (EfficientNetV2-S monocular prior, ResNet18 matching features, UNet++ decoder), the paper introduces a Hint MLP applied in parallel at every spatial location and depth plane of the cost volume. The Hint MLP takes a 3-element vector: the visual matching score, the absolute difference between the TSDF-rendered depth and the current depth plane (the geometry hint), and the confidence value at that pixel, and outputs a refined cost volume entry. A persistent TSDF is maintained using InfiniTAM-style fusion with depth-dependent confidence (c = 0.025 × max((1−d̂)², 0.25)), and rendered via Marching Cubes + PyTorch3D mesh rendering (~19 ms total). Training randomly withholds hints for 50% of samples and alternates between full and partial TSDF renders for the remaining 50%, ensuring the network gracefully degrades to baseline performance when no geometry is available. Ablations confirm that a dedicated Hint MLP outperforms alternatives such as cost-volume modulation, warped-depth hints, or merging matching and hint computation into a single MLP.

## Results

On ScanNetV2 (incremental), DoubleTake achieves Abs Diff 0.0767 and δ<1.05 of 79.94%, outperforming SimpleRecon (0.0873, 74.12%) and DeepVideoMVS (0.1186, 60.20%). In offline mode, it reaches Abs Diff 0.0627 and δ<1.05 of 86.46%, setting a new state-of-the-art. On 7Scenes (incremental), it achieves Abs Diff 0.0985 and δ<1.05 of 64.76%, again surpassing SimpleRecon (0.1045, 60.12%). For 3D reconstruction (Table 2), the incremental model achieves the best online F-Score of 0.692, while the offline model reaches 0.738, outperforming FineRecon (0.710) and VoRTX (0.685).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoubleTake (incremental) outperforms SimpleRecon [52] on all ScanNetV2 depth metrics, reducing Abs Diff from 0.0873 to 0.0767 and improving δ<1.05 from 74.12% to 79.94%. It also beats DeepVideoMVS [15] by a wide margin (Abs Diff 0.0767 vs 0.1186; δ<1.05 79.94% vs 60.20%). On 7Scenes, the incremental model improves over SimpleRecon on Abs Diff (0.0985 vs 0.1045) and δ<1.05 (64.76% vs 60.12%), though it slightly trails on δ<1.25 (97.01% vs 97.33%). In offline mode, DoubleTake surpasses all competitors including FineRecon [59] (Abs Diff 0.0627 vs 0.0651 in ablation; F-Score 0.738 vs 0.710 on mesh) and VoRTX [60] (F-Score 0.738 vs 0.685). On the 3RScan revisit scenario, using long-term hints improves Abs Diff from 0.1346 (no hint) to 0.1182, and the method remains robust to pose noise (0.1199) and scene changes. The 'no hint' variant (0.0870 Abs Diff) matches SimpleRecon's performance, confirming graceful degradation when no prior geometry is available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SimpleRecon, a 2D-convolution-based multi-view stereo method, adding a lightweight Hint MLP that fuses a rendered depth-and-confidence map from a persistent TSDF with the standard cost volume. It departs from prior work that injects external priors (LiDAR, SLAM point clouds, depth sensors) by instead using the model's own previously predicted geometry as the hint, and from temporal-consistency methods like TOCD that fuse point clouds for consistency rather than as a geometric prior for a new frame. The TSDF fusion mechanism follows InfiniTAM, and the overall pipeline (cost volume → matching MLP → monocular prior → depth decoder) is inherited from SimpleRecon, making the Hint MLP the sole architectural addition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method bridges the quality gap between fast online MVS and expensive offline reconstruction: a two-pass offline run of the same feedforward model achieves near-offline-SOTA mesh quality at a fraction of the compute of volumetric methods like FineRecon (13.8s vs 48.1s per scene). The new tighter visibility masks for mesh evaluation address a real fairness issue in prior benchmarks where over-complete predictions were penalized. Open questions include how well the approach scales to large outdoor scenes, very long revisit gaps, or dynamic environments, and whether the 3.5m TSDF depth limit can be relaxed without degrading hint quality. The graceful degradation to baseline performance when no hint is available makes the system robust for production AR use.
