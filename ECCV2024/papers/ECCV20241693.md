## Problem

State-of-the-art learned VIO networks exceed 10M parameters, making them impractical for mobile platforms where off-chip DRAM access costs two to three orders of magnitude more energy than on-chip memory. Additionally, no prior work has addressed noise-robust test-time adaptation for VIO, leaving deployed systems vulnerable to performance collapse when visual inputs shift due to rain, snow, shadow, or other adverse conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241693/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes consecutive RGB camera frames (resized to 512×256) and r-times oversampled 6-DoF IMU streams (3-axis acceleration and gyroscope). It produces two parallel pose outputs per frame interval: a fused visual-inertial pose and an inertial-only pose, each decomposed into a 3-DoF rotation vector and a 3-DoF translation vector. A third output, the domain distinctive feature (ddf) vector, is extracted from early visual encoder statistics to drive the adaptation gating signal.

## Method

UL-VIO compresses NASVIO to under 1M parameters by adding an AveragePool after the last convolution in the visual encoder (117× reduction), shrinking inertial encoder channels (8×), and replacing the LSTM decoder with fully connected layers (161×), while deliberately preserving all BatchNorm parameters in the visual encoder for adaptation. For test-time adaptation, the inertial-only pose output serves as a pseudo label: when a domain shift is detected, the network minimizes a consistency loss between the fused and inertial pose predictions, updating only the BN affine parameters (γ, β) in the visual encoder. Domain detection uses channel-wise mean and standard deviation statistics from the first convolutional layer, compared against a pre-deployment dictionary of noise-type-specific ddf proxies via nearest-neighbor matching. Each noise type maintains its own BN parameter set (0.18% overhead per type), enabling rapid switching without catastrophic forgetting when conditions return to the source domain.

## Results

UL-VIO achieves a 36.45× reduction in model size (0.944M vs. ~34.4M parameters) compared to NASVIO, with only a 1.11% increase in relative translation error and 1.05° increase in relative rotation error on KITTI. On EuRoC MH_4_difficult, UL-VIO matches Hard Fusion's translation RMSE (0.0282 m vs. 0.0283 m) while being 55.7× smaller, though it trails ModeSel on translation (0.0282 m vs. 0.0178 m) and Hard Fusion on rotation (0.0756° vs. 0.0402°). For test-time adaptation on KITTI under stationary noise shifts, UL-VIO TTA achieves the best or second-best pose-wise t_rmse across 7 of 8 corruption types, with up to 45% reduction in translation RMSE under dynamic noise shifts (18% on average across continual TTA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NASVIO [8], UL-VIO is 36.45× smaller (0.944M vs. ~34.4M params) at the cost of only 1.11% higher relative translation error and 1.05° higher relative rotation error on KITTI; against an architecture-matched NASVIO, UL-VIO actually reduces translation/rotation error by 3.80%/1.94°. On EuRoC, UL-VIO matches Hard Fusion [5] on translation RMSE (0.0282 m vs. 0.0283 m) but is 55.7× smaller, while ModeSel [39] achieves lower translation RMSE (0.0178 m) at 51.3× the model size and higher rotation RMSE (0.0906° vs. 0.0756°). For TTA under stationary noise, UL-VIO's BatchNorm-only adaptation outperforms or ties all seven fine-tuned-with-adversarial-noise baselines on 6 of 8 corruption types (best on Blur, Snow, Brightness, Contrast; second-best on Rain, Shadow), while fine-tuned models suffer severe cross-noise degradation (e.g., Rain-FT jumps to 0.525 m under Brightness). In the dynamic single-shift setting, TTA reduces pose-wise t_rmse by 29.7% (0.133 m → 0.093 m) on KITTI Seq. 07 with blur, and in continual multi-shift TTA achieves 18% average t_rmse reduction with 99.6% domain-detection accuracy. The older VO baselines (DeepVO [37], PoseNet [17], VONAS [4], XVO [19]) are all orders of magnitude larger in model size and lack any TTA capability, making them unsuitable for the edge-deployment and noise-robustness targets of this work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper compresses the NASVIO architecture [8] to sub-million parameters while preserving BatchNorm layers, and adapts the TENT-style BN-only test-time adaptation [35] from classification to the VIO regression setting. It departs from prior TTA methods that rely on entropy minimization (inapplicable to regression) or dedicated teacher networks (too large for edge deployment) by using inertial-only predictions as pseudo labels for a multi-modal consistency loss. The model compression strategy also diverges from prior VIO efficiency work [4, 39] that targeted FLOPs reduction via NAS, instead focusing on parameter count for on-chip memory constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 36× size reduction with only ~1% pose error increase makes VIO viable on mobile processors with limited on-chip memory, addressing a practical deployment bottleneck that prior efficiency-focused VIO work overlooked. The noise-robust TTA via inertial pseudo labels offers a lightweight (0.18% parameter overhead) mechanism for handling real-world distribution shifts without retraining or server access. Limitations include dependence on IMU accuracy (which degrades in vibration-heavy or GPS-denied scenarios) and a finite BN dictionary whose size grows linearly with the number of distinct noise domains, raising scalability questions for open-world deployment.
