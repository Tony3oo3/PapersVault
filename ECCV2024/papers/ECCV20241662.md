## Problem

Training large-scale 3D vision models requires massive amounts of 3D-consistent annotated data, yet manually annotating multi-view images with fine-grained semantic labels or point clouds with part segmentations is extremely time-consuming. Existing synthetic dataset generation methods (e.g., DatasetGAN) rely on 2D generative models, which inherently lack the geometric consistency needed for 3D-aware downstream tasks. The paper asks: can we build a 3D-aware data factory that produces infinite, multi-view consistent 2D annotations and 3D point cloud part segmentations from only a handful of human-labeled 2D images?

## Input / Output

Training requires only 90–150 human-annotated 2D images (30 subjects × 3 views for cats/faces, 30 subjects × 60 poses for articulated humans). At inference, a randomly sampled latent code z paired with an arbitrary camera pose P produces a 512×512 semantic segmentation mask (rendered internally at 128×128 then upscaled by a super-resolution module) and, via back-projection using the pretrained model's depth map, a 3D point cloud part segmentation. For inversion applications, a single RGB image or semantic mask at an arbitrary pose serves as input, with the model jointly optimizing z and pose to recover a full 3D representation.

## Method

DatasetNeRF augments a pretrained 3D GAN (EG3D) with a semantic segmentation branch whose key innovation is an enhanced tri-plane: all feature maps from consecutive synthesis blocks of the generator are upsampled to the highest resolution, concatenated, and reshaped into an augmented tri-plane, providing richer semantic features than EG3D's original single-scale tri-plane. A semantic decoder queries this tri-plane at 3D positions via bilinear interpolation and summation, outputting a 32-channel semantic feature that is rendered through volumetric ray marching. Crucially, the density σ from the pretrained RGB decoder is reused at equivalent tri-plane points during semantic rendering, enforcing 3D consistency without additional geometric supervision. A super-resolution module then upscales the 128² semantic map to 512², and the pretrained depth map is upsampled to back-project 2D masks into 3D point cloud part segmentations, which are merged across multiple viewpoints for full-coverage 3D annotations. The framework is compatible with both non-articulated (EG3D) and articulated (GNARF) 3D GAN backbones, and is trained with standard cross-entropy loss on the small annotated set.

## Results

On AFHQ-Cat, DatasetNeRF achieves 0.6057 mIoU and 0.8798 accuracy on individual images, and 0.6756 mIoU and 0.9253 accuracy on 61-frame video sequences, outperforming DatasetGAN (0.5381/0.5971 mIoU) and Transfer Learning (0.2995/0.2605 mIoU). On FFHQ, DatasetNeRF reaches 0.6561 mIoU on video sequences (vs. 0.6390 for DatasetGAN) and 0.8996 accuracy on individual images. For 3D point cloud segmentation on ShapeNet-Car, augmenting the 600-sample training set with 725 generated samples boosts mIoU from 0.6773 to 0.7519, surpassing the full 1325-sample original set (0.7412 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DatasetNeRF is compared against two baselines: Transfer Learning (fine-tuning a pre-trained DeepLab-V3 on 90 annotated images) and DatasetGAN (generating 10K image-annotation pairs from a 2D StyleGAN backbone). Against Transfer Learning, DatasetNeRF dominates across all four evaluation settings, improving mIoU by 0.31–0.39 (e.g., 0.6756 vs. 0.2605 on AFHQ-Cat video). Against DatasetGAN, DatasetNeRF wins on AFHQ-Cat in both individual (+0.068 mIoU) and video (+0.079 mIoU) settings, and on FFHQ video (+0.017 mIoU), but slightly trails on FFHQ individual images (0.6200 vs. 0.6317 mIoU). The largest gains appear in video sequences, where the 3D-consistency prior from the EG3D backbone yields substantially more temporally coherent segmentations than the 2D-only DatasetGAN approach. On ShapeNet-Car point cloud segmentation, generated samples serve as effective augmentation: 600 real + 725 generated samples (mIoU 0.7519) outperform 1325 real samples alone (mIoU 0.7412).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D synthetic dataset generation paradigm (DatasetGAN, DatasetDM) into the 3D domain by attaching a semantic segmentation branch to a pretrained 3D GAN backbone (EG3D). The key architectural departure is reshaping multiscale features from all synthesis blocks into an enhanced tri-plane and incorporating the pretrained density prior into semantic volumetric rendering, which prior 2D methods cannot exploit. For articulated objects, the framework is adapted to GNARF's deformation-aware tri-plane, demonstrating compatibility beyond rigid 3D GANs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate infinite 3D-consistent annotations and point cloud part segmentations from as few as 90 human-labeled images addresses a critical bottleneck for data-hungry 3D vision tasks. The ShapeNet-Car augmentation result—where 725 generated samples outperform 725 additional real samples—suggests synthetic 3D data can be a genuine substitute rather than mere supplement. However, the approach is limited to single-category 3D GANs, relies on pre-annotated data, and faces memory/compute challenges from the multiscale tri-plane; extending to diffusion-based 3D generators and broader scene-level tasks remains open.
