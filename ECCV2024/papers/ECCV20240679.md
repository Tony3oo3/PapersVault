## Problem

Training 3D object detectors requires expensive human 3D annotations (up to 100 seconds per object), which limits dataset size and coverage of rare edge cases. Previous weakly-supervised methods either still required partial human labels (e.g., 500 annotated frames) or achieved substantially lower accuracy than fully-supervised baselines. This paper asks whether a standard 3D detector can be trained from scratch using only unlabelled sensor recordings and an off-the-shelf 2D detector, without any domain-specific human annotations.

## Input / Output

At inference, the trained Voxel-RCNN network takes a single LiDAR point cloud and outputs 3D bounding boxes parameterized by position (X, Y, Z), yaw θ, height H, and a coupled length/width scale S. During training, the method additionally consumes RGB camera images (processed by a Detectron2 instance segmentation model pre-trained on MS-COCO) and a temporal window of 30 frames before and after the reference frame (3 seconds at 10 Hz), along with IMU data for ego-motion compensation. The 2D detector's instance segmentation masks serve as the supervision signal, replacing human 3D box annotations entirely.

## Method

The core contribution is a training loss that replaces human 3D labels with two complementary signals: a Template Fitting Loss (TFL), a modified Chamfer distance that measures how well a rigid vehicle shape model (placed at the network's predicted 6-DoF pose) explains the observed LiDAR points, with a sigmoid-based outlier discount; and an Appearance Mask Loss (AML), which differentiably renders the shape model into the camera and compares it to the 2D instance segmentation mask via binary cross-entropy. Temporal consistency is exploited by tracking vehicles across frames using a constant-velocity motion model and ICP-based LiDAR alignment, aggregating points from multiple scans to densify sparse detections and filter false positives. Four shape hypotheses (hatchback, sedan, SUV, MPV) are maintained simultaneously, and the final loss takes the minimum across shapes. The Voxel-RCNN architecture is first pre-trained on crude pseudo-ground-truth (obtained by exhaustive search minimizing TFL), then fine-tuned for 10 epochs with the full TFL+AML loss applied only to the detection head.

## Results

On the KITTI validation set, TCC-Det achieves BEV AP of 88.3% (Moderate@0.7) and 3D AP of 75.3% (Moderate@0.7) without any human 3D annotations, outperforming all previous no-label weakly-supervised methods by a large margin (e.g., 3D Moderate@0.7: 75.3% vs. 13.3% for Zakharov et al.). On the KITTI test set, it reaches 85.1% BEV Moderate@0.7 and 65.4% 3D Moderate@0.7. On Waymo Open Perception, TCC-Det achieves 51.36% BEV Level-2@0.7 and 15.01% 3D Level-2@0.7 without any 3D labels, compared to 84.40% and 69.04% for fully-supervised Voxel-RCNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCC-Det decisively outperforms all previous no-human-label weakly-supervised methods: on KITTI validation 3D Moderate@0.7, it achieves 75.3% versus 13.3% (Zakharov et al.) and 32.7% (VS3D), and on BEV Moderate@0.7 it reaches 87.0% versus 59.8% (Zakharov) and 64.3% (VS3D). Against partial-label methods, TCC-Det surpasses WS3D and WS3Dv2 in BEV AP (87.0% vs. 85.0%/85.8% Moderate@0.7) while matching them in 3D AP (75.3% vs. 75.1%/75.9%), despite using zero 3D labels versus their 500 annotated frames. It also outperforms FGR in the Hard 3D category (73.7% vs. 67.5%) even though FGR relies on 2D KITTI ground-truth boxes. The closest competitors are MAP-Gen and MTrans, which achieve slightly higher 3D Moderate@0.7 (78.0% and 78.8% on test set) but require 500 frames of human 3D annotations; TCC-Det is within ~3–4 points of these methods in 3D AP while using no labels at all. Compared to fully-supervised Voxel-RCNN (84.5% 3D Moderate@0.7 on validation), TCC-Det trails by ~9 points in 3D AP but is competitive in BEV, with the gap attributed to KITTI annotation bias that label-based methods implicitly learn.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Voxel-RCNN as its detection backbone but fundamentally redefines the training signal: instead of human 3D annotations, it combines a modified Chamfer-distance-based Template Fitting Loss with a differentiable-rendering-based Appearance Mask Loss, both driven by off-the-shelf 2D instance segmentation (Detectron2 on MS-COCO). It departs from prior weakly-supervised 3D detection methods (WS3D, WS3Dv2, MAP-Gen, MTrans) that still required 500 frames of partial 3D human labels, and from FGR which needed 2D ground-truth boxes, by requiring zero domain-specific annotations. The temporal consistency mechanism—aggregating LiDAR points across 60 frames using a constant-velocity motion model and ICP-based ego-motion compensation—distinguishes it from single-frame approaches like McCraith et al. and Zakharov et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating all human 3D annotation, the method enables training on orders of magnitude more unlabelled sensor data, which is critical for capturing long-tail and edge-case scenarios that small annotated datasets miss. The ablation showing that just 25% of human labels fine-tuned on top of the weakly-supervised model nearly matches fully-supervised performance suggests a practical hybrid workflow that could save 75% of annotation cost. Key limitations remain: the 3D AP at IoU 0.7 still lags fully-supervised methods (attributed to KITTI annotation bias), and Waymo results are substantially weaker due to camera-LiDAR FOV mismatch, raising open questions about generalization to multi-sensor rigs and non-vehicle object categories.
