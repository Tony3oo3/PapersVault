## Problem

3D textured shape generation faces a fundamental tension: dense volumetric representations (SDF, occupancy) are memory-prohibitive at the resolutions needed for high-frequency texture, while sparse point-cloud representations lose structural detail. Additionally, the 3D generation community lacks a large-scale, cleaned benchmark for open-vocabulary textured shape generation, and existing methods either produce geometry-only outputs or rely on laborious sparse-to-dense-to-sparse conversions that degrade quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240246/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For autoencoding, the method consumes dense colored point clouds of 1M–4M points (6D: xyz + RGB) voxelized at 1000³ resolution; for conditional generation, a single reference image is encoded by a frozen ViT (trained on LAION-2B) and injected via cross-attention. The output is an explicit textured mesh (vertices, faces, and UV-mapped texture) extracted via Flexicubes at resolution 96, with supervision from 38 rendered views at 1024×1024 (depth, alpha, RGB). The latent code has shape [4,32,32], matching StableDiffusion's latent space, and the triplane feature planes operate at 256×256 resolution.

## Method

Sparse3D replaces the dense 3D encoding bottleneck with a Sparse Convolutional Network (MinkowskiEngine UNet-style) that extracts per-coordinate features from the dense point cloud, then scatters these features onto a triplane via coordinate-based projection, yielding 2D feature planes that feed directly into a modified StableDiffusion encoder. Decoding reverses this: a modified StableDiffusion decoder outputs separate geometry and texture feature planes, which are queried by Flexicubes' differentiable dual marching cube layer (with dedicated MLPs for SDF, deformation, and cube weights) to produce an explicit mesh. Texture is recovered by differentiable rasterization of the mesh, querying the texture feature planes at pixel positions, and an N-layer discriminator (borrowed from StableDiffusion) is applied to the rendered RGB for adversarial fine-tuning after reconstruction converges. The generative stage reuses the off-the-shelf StableDiffusion latent diffusion model with cross-attention conditioning, requiring no text encoder and treating 3D textured shape generation as an open-vocabulary image-conditional problem.

## Results

On single-class unconditional generation (ShapeNetV2), Sparse3D substantially outperforms GET3D across all three classes: on Chair, COV-CD improves from 76.63% to 83.28%, MMD-CD drops from 5.81 to 3.33 (×10⁻³), and FID-3D drops from 33.18 to 18.85. On Table, FID-3D improves from 26.05 to 11.44. In the multi-class image-conditional setting, Sparse3D achieves Chamfer Distance 0.081, PSNR 20.03, SSIM 0.921, and LPIPS 0.099 on ShapeNetV2, outperforming TexturedLAS (0.149, 18.79, 0.901, 0.121) on every metric, and reports competitive open-vocabulary results on G-Objaverse (Chamfer 0.108, PSNR 19.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GET3D (the prior SOTA for single-class unconditional textured shape generation), Sparse3D wins decisively on all three ShapeNetV2 classes: COV-CD improves by 33.7 pp on Car (77.71 vs 44.00), 6.7 pp on Chair, and 9.5 pp on Table; MMD-CD is roughly halved on Chair (3.33 vs 5.81) and Table (3.72 vs 5.73); and FID-3D drops by 14.33 on Chair and 14.61 on Table. In the multi-class autoencoding comparison, Sparse3D achieves the best Surface Chamfer Distance (0.019 mean over selected classes) versus 3DILG (0.032) and 3DS2Vec (0.030), while its Structure Chamfer (0.030) is on par with 3DS2Vec (0.030) and slightly worse than 3DILG (0.032) on the full set. In the image-conditional textured shape generation setting, Sparse3D outperforms TexturedLAS on every metric (Chamfer 0.081 vs 0.149, PSNR 20.03 vs 18.79, SSIM 0.921 vs 0.901, LPIPS 0.099 vs 0.121), attributed to the sparse encoding's ability to preserve high-frequency texture features that volume-based representations lose. The paper notes that 3DILG and 3DS2Vec are geometry-only methods without released image-conditioned weights, so the comparison is limited to autoencoding metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the StableDiffusion latent diffusion pipeline to 3D textured shape generation by replacing the 2D encoder/decoder with a sparse convolutional encoder (MinkowskiEngine) and a Flexicubes-based differentiable mesh decoder, while retaining the 2D UNet diffusion backbone. It departs from 3DGen's triplane VAE design by discarding the semantic layout of projected feature planes and treating them as ordinary 2D images, and from GET3D's camera-pose-conditioned single-class paradigm by formulating generation as an open-vocabulary, label-free problem. The adversarial rendering-based decoding stage extends prior reconstruction-only VAE training with a GAN objective on rendered RGB, a technique borrowed from StableDiffusion's own training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of G-Objaverse (200K cleaned, class-labeled shapes with a train/val/test split) addresses a critical gap: prior textured-shape generation work lacked a large-scale, quality-controlled benchmark, making cross-method comparison difficult. The framework's practical value lies in showing that a heavily pretrained 2D diffusion model can be repurposed for 3D with minimal architectural changes, lowering the barrier to textured 3D generation. However, the authors acknowledge that generalizability remains weaker than NVS-based methods (e.g., SyncDreamer), and the open-vocabulary image-conditional results still show texture inaccuracy due to alignment ambiguity in uncurated 3D data. A key open question is whether combining the topology-preserving explicit mesh output of Sparse3D with the strong multi-view consistency of NVS diffusion models can yield a unified 3D-aware generative framework.
