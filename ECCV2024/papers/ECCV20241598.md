## Problem

Rigid registration between two Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) is underexplored compared to point cloud or mesh registration, and the core difficulty is sampling informative virtual camera viewpoints when the original camera positions are unknown. Randomly placing cameras on a unit sphere produces mostly noise, making photometric-based optimization unreliable. The paper asks how to generate high-quality novel views from an implicit NeRF representation to enable accurate 6-DoF alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes two pre-trained NeRFs (implicit MLP-based radiance fields) as input, without requiring knowledge of the original camera poses used to train them. It outputs a 6-DoF SE(3) rigid transformation (rotation and translation) that aligns the source NeRF to the target NeRF. Intermediate artifacts include a learned Viewshed Field per NeRF, rendered novel-view images with corresponding 2D viewshed masks, and optionally a VF-sampled 3D point cloud used for initialization. The photometric loss is computed between rendered pixels from the two NeRFs, so the method operates entirely in the implicit representation space without explicit geometry extraction.

## Method

The core contribution is Viewshed Fields (VF), an implicit function learned via a 4-layer Real-NVP normalizing flow that maps oriented points (a 3D surface location plus viewing direction) to a 6D diagonal Gaussian in latent space, encoding how well each surface point was covered by the original training cameras. During registration, the flow is inverted: samples are drawn from the Gaussian, mapped back to high-visibility oriented points, and used to reconstruct virtual camera positions via depth inversion, producing informative novel views. Two initialization strategies are provided: a photometric score that evaluates candidate SE(3) transforms by checking VF_B coverage of transformed cameras, and a VF-sampled point cloud registered with FPFH+RANSAC. The final refinement is a gradient-based SE(3) optimization (following iNeRF) that minimizes photometric loss over all generated views simultaneously, with VF-derived 2D masks restricting ray sampling to high-confidence pixels to avoid NeRF artifacts outside the original capture region.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset (full overlap), VF-NeRF with point-cloud initialization achieves a rotation error of 0.0157° and translation error of 0.0162 (×10⁻²), improving over FPFH+RANSAC by approximately two orders of magnitude (1.5316° and 2.1149 respectively). On the Objaverse dataset, VF-NeRF+PCInit achieves a mean rotation error of 0.26° on the best 50% of scenes and 6.77° across all 44 scenes, outperforming DReg (5.33° and 9.65°) and FPFH+RANSAC (1.96° and 9.75°). On casually captured real-world scenes, VF-NeRF achieves an average rotation error of 0.0303° (PC Init) and 0.0351° (Photo Init), compared to 2.1007° for FPFH+RANSAC and 16.9262° for iNeRF without initialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VF-NeRF dominates all baselines across every dataset and overlap setting. Against FPFH+RANSAC (the point-cloud registration baseline), VF-NeRF improves rotation error by roughly two orders of magnitude on LLFF (0.0157° vs. 1.5316° full overlap) and by a factor of ~7 on Objaverse (0.26° vs. 1.96° on the best 50% of scenes). Against iNeRF, the gap is even starker: iNeRF without initialization fails to converge (16.1° rotation error on LLFF full overlap), while VF-NeRF achieves 0.0157°; even iNeRF with photometric initialization (0.1624°) is an order of magnitude worse than VF-NeRF. On the Objaverse benchmark, VF-NeRF outperforms DReg (the strongest NeRF-specific registration method) by a factor of ~20 on rotation error for the best 50% of scenes (0.26° vs. 5.33°) and by ~1.4× across all scenes (6.77° vs. 9.65°). FGR and REGTR, both point-cloud-based deep registration methods, are substantially outperformed on Objaverse, with REGTR achieving 65.98° rotation error on the best 50% versus VF-NeRF's 0.26°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on iNeRF's gradient-based SE(3) optimization framework but departs from it by optimizing over multiple images simultaneously and introducing Viewshed Fields (VF) to guide ray sampling. Conceptually, it repurposes Normalizing Flows (Real-NVP) — typically used for density estimation on high-dimensional data — as a generative tool for sampling well-observed 3D surface points, a use case not previously explored in the NeRF literature. It positions itself against both point-cloud registration methods (FPFH+RANSAC, ICP variants) and NeRF-specific approaches (DRegNeRF, NeRF2NeRF), arguing that VF provides a modular, training-free alternative that avoids the need for large training sets or user-provided initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VF-NeRF offers a practical building block for scalable NeRF pipelines where small scene fragments are registered into larger models without requiring original camera poses, a setting that arises naturally in incremental reconstruction. The VF representation itself is a reusable tool for sampling informative novel views and generating point clouds, potentially benefiting NeRF rendering and evaluation beyond registration. However, the method remains limited by its photometric loss (vulnerable to textureless surfaces and illumination changes) and by symmetry ambiguities that can produce 180°-flipped solutions; stress-testing on partial overlap and dynamic scenes remains open.
