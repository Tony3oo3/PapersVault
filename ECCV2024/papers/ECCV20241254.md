## Problem

Traditional polygonal mesh reconstruction methods for architectural point clouds suffer from three coupled limitations: dependence on point-based normal vector estimation (fragile to noise and outliers), sensitivity to defective or incomplete regions, and loss of internal geometric structure during simplification. Existing low-poly meshing approaches either require a high-quality initial mesh or perform exhaustive plane-intersection calculations that are both slow and error-prone on noisy inputs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw, unorganized 3D point cloud (no pre-computed normals, no initial mesh required) and produces a globally oriented polygonal mesh represented as a set of convex polyhedra with consistent face normals. Pre-processing applies Poisson resampling and KNN-based outlier removal, but the core pipeline operates directly on the scattered points. The output mesh is structurally concise (hundreds of faces for typical architectural models) while preserving sharp features and internal planar regions that two-step reconstruction-then-simplification pipelines tend to smooth away.

## Method

WindPoly is a three-stage structural reconstruction pipeline. First, polygonal plane detection extracts candidate planes via Fitting Planar Primitives (FPP) and merges them using a Plane Refinement Regulation (PRR) criterion based on angular similarity and shared-point count, entirely avoiding per-point normal estimation. Second, an adaptive spatial partition iteratively selects the internal plane with the fewest intersections as a separating cut, recursively subdividing the convex polyhedral hull into a set of convex polyhedra; this greedy minimum-intersection strategy avoids the exhaustive all-pairs intersection computation of PolyFit and localizes calculations to sub-regions. Third, a polygon-based winding number optimization orients the polyhedra by evaluating winding numbers at polyhedron centroids (far fewer samples than per-point evaluation) and minimizing a direction energy that combines winding-number consistency with an α-shape coverage term measuring fit to the original point cloud; the binary inside/outside labeling is solved via max-flow, yielding a globally consistent oriented mesh.

## Results

On the ABC CAD dataset, WindPoly achieves the lowest Hausdorff distance (0.061) and mean distance (0.002) among all compared methods, with a multiple estimation score (RH^Avg) of 0.0010. On the architectural building dataset, WindPoly reduces Hausdorff distance to 4.53 (vs. 5.17–6.78 for baselines) and achieves the best multiple estimation score of 0.0229, while using 549 points and 274 faces. The method also demonstrates competitive runtime, completing reconstruction in 76–204 s across datasets, comparable to or faster than PolyFit and IPSR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WindPoly outperforms all five baselines on Hausdorff distance across both the ABC and architectural datasets, achieving 0.061 vs. 0.111–0.281 (ABC) and 4.53 vs. 5.17–6.78 (architectural). On the composite multiple-estimation metric (RH^Avg), WindPoly is best on both datasets (0.0010 on ABC, 0.0229 on architectural), indicating the best balance between geometric fidelity and data compression. KSR is the fastest method (6–11 s total) but suffers from the highest Hausdorff distances due to its reliance on normal vectors, which causes loss of geometric structure. LowPoly and R-LowPoly require a preliminary voxel-based Delaunay triangulation step (17 s initial) and produce topological errors on broken regions, while WindPoly operates directly on raw point clouds. IPSR yields the most geometrically detailed mesh but at the cost of 398k faces (vs. 274 for WindPoly) and 16–1347 s runtime, making it impractical for large-scale architectural scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

WindPoly sits within the low-poly meshing / structural reconstruction lineage, directly building on PolyFit's plane refinement regulation for merging candidate planes and its convex polyhedral partitioning framework, while replacing the latter's exhaustive intersection search with an adaptive spatial partition that recursively selects minimum-intersection separating planes. For initial plane extraction it relies on FPP (Yu & Lafarge, CVPR 2022), and for the final orientation step it adapts the max-flow-based winding number optimization from KSR (Bauchet & Lafarge, TOG 2020) but operates on polyhedron centroids rather than dense point samples. The key conceptual departure from prior structural methods (PolyFit, KSR, LowPoly, R-LowPoly) is the complete elimination of per-point normal vector estimation, replacing it with a plane-level geometric pipeline that is inherently more robust to noise and incomplete scans.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners working with architectural and CAD point clouds, WindPoly offers a single-pass, normal-vector-free pipeline that produces oriented, structurally faithful polygonal meshes with fewer faces than two-step reconstruction-then-simplification approaches, while being more robust to outliers and missing regions than prior plane-hypothesis methods. The adaptive spatial partition and centroid-based winding number computation address a real efficiency bottleneck in convex decomposition, making the method practical for large-scale urban scenes (UrbanBIS). A notable limitation is that the method assumes structural (planar) dominance; small or low-density sub-structures in large-scale clouds can be erroneously merged into surrounding planes, leaving open the question of how to handle mixed-scale or organic geometries within the same framework.
