## Problem

Large vision language models (LVLMs) frequently produce inaccurate visual grounding, hallucinating nonexistent objects, omitting significant scene elements, and misattributing properties or relationships between objects. Existing training approaches rely on limited paired image-text data or synthetic captions generated by text-only LLMs that themselves contain factual errors, making it difficult to improve grounding without expensive full supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241618/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image (processed at 224×224 by a frozen ViT-L/14 CLIP encoder) and a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'Please provide a detailed description of the given image') and produces a multi-sentence natural language description. During training, five candidate descriptions are sampled per image at nonzero temperature; the reward model scores each sentence individually rather than the full description holistically, and the refinement module removes any sentence containing a noun phrase flagged as nonexistent by the perception-based verifier before the final description is used as a regression target.

## Method

ViGoR combines two complementary fine-grained reward signals for rejection sampling: (1) a reward model fine-tuned on 15,440 human-annotated image-caption pairs where annotators assign per-sentence scores identifying specific error types (hallucination, attribute error, relationship error, location error) and creativity, and (2) an automatic pipeline that extracts noun phrases via NLTK NER and verifies their existence in the image using Grounding DINO. Negative penalty signals from both branches are variance-normalized and linearly combined; the sampled description with the smallest total penalty is selected, then sentences containing hallucinated noun phrases are removed by a refinement module. The resulting cleaned description serves as ground truth for standard autoregressive supervised fine-tuning of the base LLaVA model (Vicuna-7B + CLIP ViT-L/14). The key insight is that per-sentence reward signals substantially outperform holistic scoring because they reduce ambiguity when a description mixes correct and incorrect sentences, enabling the reward model to form direct links between specific visual features and the sentences describing them.

## Results

On the MME benchmark, ViGoR achieves an overall score of 1309.3, substantially outperforming the LLaVA baseline (960.1) by +349.2 points, and surpassing the next-best 7B model Cheetor (1299.9) and the 13B InstructBLIP (1212.8). On POPE, ViGoR reaches 83.8 average F1, exceeding LLaVA (67.8) by 16 points and matching InstructBLIP (83.7) with half the parameters. On MMHal-Bench, ViGoR scores 1.6, a +0.3 improvement over LLaVA (1.3) and double the gain of LLaVA-RLHF (+0.1). In GPT-4Vision preference ranking for detailed captions, ViGoR-All achieves an average rank of 1.97 (lower is better) versus 3.24 for LLaVA across all seven quality criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViGoR outperforms LLaVA (the base model) by a wide margin on every benchmark: +349.2 on MME overall, +16.0 on POPE average F1, and +0.3 on MMHal-Bench. Against LLaVA-RLHF, ViGoR achieves a higher MMHal-Bench score (1.6 vs 1.4) and a higher POPE F1 (83.8 vs 82.7), despite LLaVA-RLHF being trained with additional VQA data. On POPE, ViGoR (83.8) essentially ties InstructBLIP (83.7) while using 7B parameters versus InstructBLIP's 13B, and clearly beats mPLUG-Owl (67.2), MiniGPT-4 (74.1), and MM-GPT (70.1). On MME, ViGoR (1309.3) surpasses all baselines including the 13B InstructBLIP (1212.8) and Cheetor (1299.9), and notably does so without any VQA fine-tuning data, whereas most competitors rely on it. The largest single-category gains over LLaVA are in Count (+60.0), Artwork (+52.7), and OCR (+87.5), indicating that fine-grained reward modeling particularly strengthens counting and recognition tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts LLaMA-2's rejection sampling paradigm to the vision-language domain, extending it with a key departure: replacing the sparse holistic reward signal used in LLaVA-RLHF with fine-grained, per-sentence reward modeling. It also introduces a complementary automatic scoring branch that leverages open-set object detectors (Grounding DINO) to verify noun-entity existence in generated captions, a technique not previously combined with human-feedback reward models in LVLM training. The base architecture (LLaVA with CLIP ViT-L/14 and Vicuna-7B) is used unmodified, positioning the contribution as a training methodology rather than an architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hallucination and inaccurate visual grounding remain the primary practical failure modes of deployed LVLMs, and this paper demonstrates that a relatively lightweight fine-tuning pipeline (16K samples, 7 hours on 8×A100) can substantially reduce these errors while preserving the model's reasoning and creative capabilities. The released 15,440-pair fine-grained annotation dataset is a useful community resource. Key limitations include the automatic scorer's inability to handle stuff regions, attributes, or spatial layouts, and the reward model's dependence on a specific LVLM checkpoint's output distribution. Open questions include whether RLHF (rather than rejection sampling) would yield further gains, and whether explicitly linking visual entities to text phrases could close the remaining gap.
