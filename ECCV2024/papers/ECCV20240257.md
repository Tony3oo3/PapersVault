## Problem

No existing framework can estimate the 9D pose of both rigid and articulated objects within a single unified pipeline; current methods are either rigid-only (NOCS, CPPF) or articulated-only (A-NCSH, OMAD), creating integration and deployment challenges. Additionally, direct pose regression approaches suffer from ill-posed optimization and poor convergence, motivating an indirect voting-based formulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240257/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a 3D point cloud (downsampled to 2,048 points) or an RGB-D image cropped and projected into point cloud form. It produces three simultaneous outputs: (1) joint information as a pivot point and unit direction vector per joint, (2) per-point one-hot part segmentation masks for K parts, and (3) per-part 9D pose (3D rotation, 3D translation, 3D amodal bounding-box scale). Rigid objects are handled as the degenerate case K=1 with no joints or segmentation branches active.

## Method

U-COPE unifies rigid and articulated objects under a K-part, K-1-joint structure and processes input through PointNet++ for feature extraction, followed by a decoder that produces part masks used in a Hadamard-product re-modulation step. A PPF encoder then consumes the re-modulated point embeddings alongside Drost-style point pair features to predict key parameter groups (μ, ν, α, β, γ) per part. Pose is recovered via a two-stage voting scheme: translation is determined by casting candidate centers on a circle (parameterized by μ and ν) into a 3D grid and selecting the highest-vote cell, while orientation is resolved by voting on right and up direction vectors constrained by α and β. A joint-and-part awareness mechanism filters point pairs to only those from the same part and adjacent to the target joint, reducing noise. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a convex combination of joint (cross-product + L2), segmentation (cross-entropy), and pose (KL-divergence for rotation/translation, MSE for scale) losses.

## Results

On CAMERA25 (rigid objects), U-COPE achieves mAP of 57.3 (3D₇₅, 5°), 93.4 (3D₅₀, 2 cm), and 65.3 (10°, 5 cm), outperforming the strongest baseline IST-Net by 1.7, 1.2, and 1.0 points respectively, and surpassing NOCS by 34.7, 9.5, and 33.0 points. On ArtImage (articulated objects), U-COPE attains the best per-part rotation errors (e.g., 4.8°/4.1° on Laptop, 3.9°/5.3°/5.6° on Eyeglasses) and 3D IoU (e.g., 74.6%/49.2% on Laptop, 65.4%/60.9%/61.4% on Eyeglasses) across all five categories. Inference time is competitive at 1.4–2.1 s per object, comparable to OMAD and substantially faster than A-NCSH (5.5–16.5 s) and AKBNet (4.3–14.5 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CAMERA25 (rigid objects), U-COPE outperforms all five baselines across every mAP metric. The closest competitor is IST-Net [54], which U-COPE edges out by 1.7 points on 3D₇₅ 5° (57.3 vs 55.6) and 1.2 on 3D₅₀ 2 cm (93.4 vs 92.2); the gap widens dramatically against NOCS [44] (+34.7 on 3D₇₅ 5°), SPD [40] (+9.8), Gao et al. [12] (+33.8), and MH6D [26] (+33.4). On ArtImage (articulated objects), U-COPE dominates A-NCSH [24] on nearly all per-part metrics, with the largest gains on Eyeglasses where rotation error drops from 22.3°/23.2° to 5.3°/5.6° (≈17° improvement) and 3D IoU rises from 40.2%/39.6% to 60.9%/61.4%. Against OMAD [47], U-COPE achieves substantially higher 3D IoU (e.g., 74.6% vs 43.5% on Laptop) at a comparable inference speed (1.8 s vs 1.6 s). Versus AKBNet [27], U-COPE matches or exceeds accuracy while running 2–7× faster (1.4–2.1 s vs 4.3–14.5 s), attributable to its end-to-end design versus AKBNet's multi-stage pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

U-COPE extends the Point Pair Feature (PPF) voting strategy from Drost et al. [11] and the category-level 9D pose estimation framework of CPPF [49] into a unified setting that handles both rigid (K=1) and articulated (K≥2) objects under a single K-parts/K-1-joints abstraction. The key departures from CPPF are: (1) point pair features are derived from SO(3)-invariant feature decoupling via a learned network rather than a fixed selection, and (2) the framework jointly outputs joint information, part segmentation, and per-part 9D poses end-to-end, whereas CPPF targets pose only. For articulated objects specifically, the paper builds on the category-level articulated pose estimation line (A-NCSH [24], OMAD [47], AKBNet [27]) but replaces their two-stage or keypoint-based pipelines with a single voting-based network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first reported framework that treats rigid and articulated object pose estimation as a single problem, eliminating the need for separate models and the integration overhead in mixed-object scenes (robotics, AR, industrial inspection). The end-to-end design with competitive inference times (1.4–2.1 s vs. 5.5–16.5 s for A-NCSH) makes it practically deployable. However, the authors acknowledge that segmentation quality is a bottleneck—noisy part masks degrade downstream pose accuracy—and that dynamic objects, multi-modal sensor fusion, and more efficient feature representations remain open. Generalization to truly unseen categories (beyond the trained set) is demonstrated only qualitatively on ReArtMix and RobotArm, leaving robustness questions for broader deployment.
