## Problem

CLIP models excel at semantic understanding but produce incoherent segments for dense prediction because their contrastive training objective does not encourage spatially consistent local features. Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) like DINO and SAM learn spatially coherent representations but lack semantic grounding. Prior approaches either modify CLIP's internal attention (capped by CLIP's intrinsic localization limits) or fine-tune CLIP with VFM features (risking degradation of zero-shot transfer), leaving a gap for a training-free method that preserves both models' strengths.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 336 px shorter side for PASCAL/COCO, 448 px for Cityscapes/ADE20K) and a set of text prompts in the form 'a photo of a {label name}' as input. Images are processed with a 336×336 sliding window at 112×112 stride to accommodate both the CLIP and VFM backbones. The output is a per-pixel semantic segmentation map (h×w×1) obtained by reshaping and up-sampling patch-level cosine similarities between projected visual features and text embeddings, with no post-processing or fine-tuning required.

## Method

ProxyCLIP introduces a Proxy Attention Module (PAM) that repurposes a frozen VFM's l2-normalized patch representations as both query and key embeddings, while CLIP's last-layer value embeddings serve as the semantic base. The proxy attention score is computed as SoftMax(x·xᵀ) over VFM features, then applied to CLIP's multi-head value embeddings and projected through CLIP's inherited output projection layer. A key design is an adaptive normalization and masking strategy: the similarity matrix is shifted by its mean (scaled by β=1.2) and multiplied by γ=3 to align statistics across different VFMs, and all negative entries are masked to −∞ before SoftMax, ensuring each patch only aggregates contextually relevant neighbors. The framework further benefits from using VFMs with smaller patch sizes (e.g., DINO ViT-B/8 over B/16), which yields sharper segmentation boundaries, and interpolates CLIP's value resolution to match the VFM's spatial grid. The entire pipeline is training-free, with both encoders frozen and no learnable parameters beyond CLIP's existing projection weights.

## Results

ProxyCLIP achieves an average mIoU of 44.4 across eight open-vocabulary segmentation benchmarks using OpenCLIP-ViT-H/14 with DINO-ViT-B/8 as the VFM, improving over the best training-based method CLIP-DINOiser (40.3) by +4.1 mIoU. Under CLIP-ViT-B/16, ProxyCLIP reaches 42.3 mIoU, surpassing the best training-free baseline SCLIP (38.2) by +4.1 mIoU. Notably, ProxyCLIP is the first training-free method to show consistent gains from scaling up the CLIP backbone: performance rises from 42.3 (ViT-B/16) to 43.0 (ViT-L/14) to 44.4 (ViT-H/14), whereas MaskCLIP and SCLIP show diminishing or inconsistent returns with larger backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest training-based method CLIP-DINOiser (40.3 avg mIoU), ProxyCLIP wins by +2.0, +2.7, and +4.1 mIoU under ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, and ViT-H/14 respectively, while remaining entirely training-free. Among training-free methods, ProxyCLIP outperforms SCLIP (the prior best) by +4.1 mIoU on ViT-B/16, +14.0 on ViT-L/14, and +15.3 on ViT-H/14, and beats MaskCLIP by +14.4, +29.1, and +25.1 mIoU under the same three backbones. A critical finding is that ProxyCLIP is the first training-free approach to benefit from scaling the CLIP backbone: while MaskCLIP drops from 27.9 (B/16) to 13.9 (L/14) and SCLIP only modestly improves from 38.2 to 29.0 to 29.1, ProxyCLIP monotonically improves from 42.3 to 43.0 to 44.4. On individual datasets, ProxyCLIP leads on all eight benchmarks under every backbone configuration, with the largest margins on Cityscapes (+6.0 over SCLIP on ViT-H/14) and ADE20K (+12.9 over SCLIP on ViT-H/14). The paper also notes that all training-based methods (SegCLIP, ViewCo, OVSegmentor, CoCu, SAM-CLIP, GroupViT, TCL) fall short of ProxyCLIP, suggesting that fine-tuning compromises CLIP's zero-shot transfer properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProxyCLIP sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) training-free methods that modify CLIP's internal attention to improve localization (MaskCLIP, SCLIP, CLIPSurgery, GEM), and (2) training-based methods that distill VFM spatial features into CLIP (CLIP-DINOiser, SAM-CLIP). It departs from both by introducing an external proxy attention computed from a frozen VFM's feature correspondence, which reorganizes CLIP's value embeddings without altering CLIP's parameters or its internal attention mechanism. The adaptive normalization and masking strategy is a practical contribution that makes this proxy attention transferable across architecturally diverse VFMs (DINO, DINOv2, SAM, MAE, SD), addressing a gap that prior distillation-based approaches handled through task-specific fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ProxyCLIP demonstrates that a simple, training-free attention substitution can outperform all prior training-based methods on open-vocabulary segmentation, challenging the assumption that fine-tuning is necessary to merge spatial and semantic information. The finding that ProxyCLIP is the first training-free method to scale favorably with larger CLIP backbones (ViT-L/14, ViT-H/14) while MaskCLIP and SCLIP degrade is practically important, as it unlocks the semantic gains of larger models without additional training cost. Open questions include whether the proxy attention concept generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (panoptic segmentation, depth estimation) and whether the fixed hyperparameters (β=1.2, γ=3) remain optimal across domains and image resolutions.
