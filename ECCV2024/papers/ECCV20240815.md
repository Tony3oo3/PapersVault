## Problem

State-of-the-art self-supervised monocular depth estimators achieve their best performance by consuming rich-resource inputs such as multi-frame sequences, high-resolution images, or future frames during inference. These inputs are frequently unavailable in real-world deployment (e.g., a stationary vehicle cannot capture multi-frame data, and future frames do not exist in real-time), creating a significant gap between benchmark performance and practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240815/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single low-resolution RGB image (640×192 or 1024×320) and produces a dense per-pixel depth map. During training, it additionally consumes a pre-computed reference dataset of rich-resource features (multi-frame, high-resolution) that is compressed offline to roughly 1% of its original size (~25,000 pixels from 2,560,000) via an attention-guided selection, so the inference pipeline only requires the single LR image plus this small fixed prior.

## Method

RPrDepth builds on the DIFFNet baseline and introduces a two-branch training pipeline: a frozen rich-resource model (ManyDepth-HR) generates reference features and pseudo depth labels, while the LR single-image model is trained end-to-end. The core Prior Depth Fusion Module combines two mechanisms: a pixel-wise fusion that uses a learned affinity matrix to retrieve the most similar reference pixel and its depth, and a depth-hint fusion that applies multi-head attention (treating reference features as key/value and target features as query) to aggregate global geometric context. A Rich-resource Guided Loss augments standard viewpoint reconstruction with a gradient-consistency term that aligns the LR prediction's spatial gradients to the rich-resource pseudo-label, plus an auxiliary L1 loss on the constructed reference depth to sharpen the affinity search. Finally, an Attention Guided Feature Selection algorithm aggregates attention weights across a validation set to retain only the top-weighted reference pixels, reducing the prior to ~1% of its original size while actually improving accuracy by eliminating irrelevant pixels.

## Results

On KITTI Eigen Split at 1024×320 with monocular training, RPrDepth (single LR frame) achieves AbsRel 0.091, RMSE 4.098, and δ<1.25 of 0.910, outperforming the rich-resource ManyDepth-HR (2 frames, HR) which scores AbsRel 0.093, RMSE 4.245, and δ<1.25 of 0.909. At 640×192 with monocular training, RPrDepth reaches AbsRel 0.097 and RMSE 4.279, surpassing all single-frame baselines. On Make3D, RPrDepth records AbsRel 0.288 and RMSE 6.532, beating BRNet (0.302 / 7.068) and Monodepth2 (0.322 / 7.414). On Cityscapes, RPrDepth achieves δ<1.25 of 0.890 with a single frame, exceeding ManyDepth's 0.875 (2 frames).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RPrDepth (single LR frame) outperforms its base architecture DIFFNet [39] by 0.006 AbsRel and 0.337 RMSE at 1024×320, and by 0.005 AbsRel and 0.204 RMSE at 640×192. Most notably, RPrDepth surpasses the rich-resource ManyDepth-HR [32] (2 frames, high-resolution) at 1024×320, achieving AbsRel 0.091 vs. 0.093 and RMSE 4.098 vs. 4.245, despite using only a single low-resolution frame at inference. Among single-frame baselines, RPrDepth leads all competitors including BRNet [34] (0.103 AbsRel), MonoFormer [2] (0.108), and Lite-Mono [37] (0.102) at 1024×320. On Make3D, RPrDepth beats BRNet by 0.014 AbsRel and Monodepth2 [7] by 0.034 AbsRel. On Cityscapes, RPrDepth achieves a higher δ<1.25 (0.890) than ManyDepth [32] (0.875) with one fewer input frame, while its AbsRel (0.111) is marginally better than ManyDepth's (0.114).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised monocular depth estimation lineage established by Monodepth2 [7] and advanced by multi-frame cost-volume methods like ManyDepth [32], which achieve superior accuracy by leveraging temporal and resolution-rich inputs. Rather than requiring these rich-resource inputs at inference time, RPrDepth repurposes them as an offline prior: features and depth predictions from a frozen rich-resource model are extracted from a reference dataset and injected into a single-image DIFFNet [39] backbone via a Prior Depth Fusion Module (combining transformer attention and pixel-wise affinity matching). The Rich-resource Guided Loss further departs from standard photometric reconstruction by adding a gradient-consistency term against pseudo-labels from the rich-resource model, and an Attention-Guided Feature Selection step compresses the reference set to ~1% of its original size for efficient inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is clear: multi-frame and high-resolution inputs are unavailable in many deployment scenarios (stationary vehicles, real-time pipelines), yet they underpin the best-performing self-supervised depth estimators. By distilling their advantage into a static offline prior, RPrDepth offers a path to near-rich-resource accuracy with a single low-resolution image, broadening the applicability of state-of-the-art depth models. Open questions include how well the fixed prior generalizes to environments or object categories underrepresented in the reference dataset, whether the approach extends to other rich-resource modalities (e.g., LiDAR, semantic maps), and whether the modest SOTA margins (e.g., AbsRel 0.097 vs. 0.098 for ManyDepth on KITTI) persist under more challenging or diverse evaluation protocols.
