## Problem

Existing image restoration backbones face an inherent trade-off: CNNs are computationally efficient but have limited effective receptive fields, while Transformers achieve global receptive fields at quadratic computational cost, and efficient attention variants (e.g., shifted windows) sacrifice global modeling. Although Mamba offers linear-complexity long-range dependency modeling, directly applying it to 2D images introduces local pixel forgetting (spatially adjacent pixels become distant in flattened 1D sequences) and channel redundancy from large hidden state dimensions, making it sub-optimal for low-level vision tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a full-resolution low-quality RGB image (H×W×3) and produces a high-quality restored image of the same spatial dimensions, operating without patching or windowing to preserve a global receptive field. During training, inputs are cropped into 64×64 patches for super-resolution and 128×128 patches for denoising, with standard augmentations (horizontal flips, 90°/180°/270° rotations). The architecture is task-agnostic: the same backbone handles classic SR (×2/×3/×4), lightweight SR, real-world SR, Gaussian color denoising (σ=15/25/50), real image denoising, and JPEG artifact reduction, with only the loss function and training data changing per task.

## Method

MambaIR consists of three stages: a 3×3 convolution for shallow feature extraction, a stack of Residual State-Space Blocks (RSSBs) for deep feature extraction, and a final convolution for reconstruction. Each RSSB applies a Vision State-Space Module (VSSM) that processes the feature through two parallel branches—one with depth-wise convolution, SiLU, and a 2D Selective Scan Module scanning in four diagonal directions, the other with a simple linear-SiLU path—aggregated via Hadamard product. To address local pixel forgetting, a bottleneck convolution (channel compression by factor γ) is inserted after VSSM to restore neighborhood similarity lost during 1D flattening. To combat channel redundancy, a Squeeze-and-Excitation channel attention module is applied, and learnable per-channel scale factors control both skip connections within each RSSB. The 2D-SSM flattens the 2D feature into 1D sequences along four diagonal directions, applies the selective state-space recurrence independently to each, and merges results by summation, achieving global receptive field with linear complexity in sequence length.

## Results

On classic image super-resolution, MambaIR achieves the best PSNR across nearly all five benchmark datasets (Set5, Set14, BSDS100, Urban100, Manga109) at all three scale factors (×2, ×3, ×4). For example, at ×4 scale, MambaIR reaches 33.03 dB on Set5, 29.20 dB on Set14, and 32.32 dB on Manga109, outperforming SwinIR by 0.11–0.29 dB. On Gaussian color denoising (σ=50), MambaIR surpasses SwinIR by up to 0.48 dB on Urban100. In real image denoising, MambaIR achieves 40.04 dB PSNR on DND, matching Restormer (40.03 dB) and exceeding Uformer by 0.08 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaIR consistently outperforms the strongest Transformer-based baseline SwinIR across all five SR datasets and all three scale factors, with margins ranging from 0.11 dB (Set5 ×4) to 0.36 dB (Manga109 ×2). It also surpasses SRFormer, the previous best Transformer-based method, by 0.21–0.36 dB on Manga109 and 0.05–0.21 dB on Set14. Against CNN-based methods (EDSR, RCAN, SAN, HAN, ELAN), the gains are larger: 0.68–1.01 dB over EDSR and 0.30–0.88 dB over ELAN at ×4 scale. On Gaussian denoising (σ=50), MambaIR leads SwinIR by up to 0.48 dB on Urban100. In real image denoising, MambaIR (40.04 dB on DND) ties Restormer (40.03 dB) and beats Uformer by 0.08 dB, while on lightweight SR (×4, Manga109) MambaIR-light achieves 31.26 dB vs. SwinIR-light's 30.92 dB with comparable parameter count (~879K vs. 897K) and MACs (50.6G vs. 49.6G).

## Relation to Prior Work

MambaIR adapts the selective state-space model Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2023), originally designed for 1D sequential data in NLP, to 2D image restoration by incorporating the Vision State-Space Module and 2D four-direction scanning from V-Mamba (Liu et al., 2024). It departs from the dominant CNN and Transformer restoration backbones (EDSR, SwinIR, HAT, Restormer) by replacing quadratic-complexity attention with linear-complexity state-space recurrence, while introducing a Residual State-Space Block (RSSB) that adds local convolution and channel attention to address the specific failure modes (local pixel forgetting, channel redundancy) that arise when flattening 2D images into 1D sequences. The paper positions itself explicitly as a 'simple baseline,' emphasizing that the core contribution is demonstrating SSMs as a viable restoration backbone rather than proposing an elaborate architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MambaIR provides the first systematic evidence that state-space models can serve as competitive image restoration backbones, resolving the long-standing trade-off between global receptive fields and linear computational complexity that has constrained both CNNs and efficient-attention Transformers. The RSSB design—particularly the finding that simply swapping Attention for SSM in a standard Transformer block yields sub-optimal results, and that local convolution plus channel attention are necessary—offers practical guidance for future SSM-based restoration work. Open questions include whether the four-direction 2D scanning is optimal, whether SSM-based restoration scales favorably to video or higher-resolution settings, and whether the 'simple baseline' framing leaves substantial headroom for more sophisticated SSM-specific designs.
