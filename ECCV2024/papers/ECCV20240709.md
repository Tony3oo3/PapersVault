## Problem

Feature grid-based neural fields like InstantNGP lay out features on uniform Eulerian grids, which cannot adaptively allocate representation budget to spatially non-homogeneous 3D data where complexity varies across the scene. Existing compression tricks (sparsity, low-rank factorization, hash probing) improve the memory-quality tradeoff but do not fundamentally address this spatial adaptivity gap, resulting in larger-than-necessary models for a given reconstruction quality.

## Input / Output

For novel view synthesis, the method takes multi-view RGB images (NeRF Synthetic, Tanks & Temples) and produces a compact 3D representation parameterized by a 3D density and 5D radiance function, from which novel views are rendered via volume rendering. For 2D image fitting, it takes a single high-resolution image (up to 213M pixels) and learns a coordinate-to-color mapping. The representation uses a hierarchical hash table with B buckets (2^11 to 2^19), K=4 Gaussian features per bucket, and L=16 levels of detail, yielding 1.8×–2.8× fewer parameters than InstantNGP at matched or superior PSNR.

## Method

LagHash extends InstantNGP's multi-resolution hash encoding by replacing the fixed Eulerian features in the finest L̃ levels with a mixture of K isotropic Gaussians per hash bucket, each carrying a learnable mean μ, a fixed standard deviation σ (proportional to grid cell size), and a feature vector. At query time, features are retrieved by computing Gaussian PDF weights at the input position within each bucket and aggregating them across levels before passing through a shared MLP decoder. The key training innovation is a guidance loss (L_guide) inspired by EM for GMMs: it identifies the nearest Gaussian to each ray-integration position weighted by NeRF integration weights W(x), then minimizes a KL-divergence-based objective that effectively acts as a one-sided Chamfer loss, pushing Gaussian means toward high-surface-density regions. This avoids the vanishing-gradient problem when Gaussians are far from query points and ensures the limited representation budget is concentrated where the scene demands it, enabling compact models without quality loss.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, Lagrangian Hashing with 6.68M parameters (B=2^17) achieves an average PSNR of 33.12 dB, matching InstantNGP's 33.11 dB (12.10M params) while using 1.8× fewer parameters. On Tanks & Temples, the same 6.68M-parameter model reaches 28.55 dB average PSNR versus InstantNGP's 28.51 dB. On gigapixel image fitting, the method with 8.41M parameters (B=2^18) outperforms InstantNGP (11.91M params) by 1.11 dB in average PSNR (40.71 vs. 39.60). In the compact setting (0.18M params), Lagrangian Hashing surpasses CompactNGP by 0.06 dB average PSNR (30.72 vs. 30.66) on NeRF Synthetic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InstantNGP (the primary baseline), Lagrangian Hashing matches or slightly exceeds reconstruction quality while using 1.8× fewer parameters on both NeRF Synthetic (33.12 vs. 33.11 dB at 6.68M vs. 12.10M params) and Tanks & Temples (28.55 vs. 28.51 dB). On gigapixel image fitting, the method actually outperforms InstantNGP by 1.11 dB average PSNR (40.71 vs. 39.60) with 2.6× fewer parameters, with the largest gains on images with localized high-frequency content (e.g., +1.13 dB on Pluto). Against CompactNGP in the ultra-compact regime (0.18M params), Lagrangian Hashing achieves a 0.06 dB PSNR improvement (30.72 vs. 30.66) on NeRF Synthetic, while 3DGS (12K Gaussians, 0.60M params) falls significantly behind at 25.60 dB, confirming that the hash-table-based Lagrangian approach is far more parameter-efficient than standalone Gaussian splatting for compact representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper merges two previously separate paradigms in neural field representation: the Eulerian multi-resolution hash grid of InstantNGP (Müller et al., 2022) and the Lagrangian point-based representation of 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023). Rather than treating these as competing approaches, the authors embed a small isotropic Gaussian mixture inside each hash bucket at the finest resolution levels, reusing the hash table as the spatial acceleration structure. This departs from 3DGS by eliminating the need for COLMAP point initialization and from prior hash-grid compression methods (e.g., CompactNGP) by introducing spatial adaptivity through learnable point positions guided by an EM-inspired loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a consistent ~2× reduction in parameter count while matching or slightly exceeding InstantNGP quality across NeRF synthetic, Tanks & Temples, and gigapixel image fitting, with the Pareto front strictly ahead of the baseline at every budget. The guidance loss, which draws on EM for Gaussian Mixture Models to prevent vanishing gradients and pull Gaussians toward high-density surfaces, is a clean and interpretable mechanism that could transfer to other hybrid representations. Open questions include whether the fixed (non-trainable) standard deviation schedule limits expressibility, how the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the benefit persists when the hash table itself is made sparse or factorized.
