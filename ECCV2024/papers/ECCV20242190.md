## Problem

Supervised motion interpolation models require large motion capture datasets, but such datasets are tied to specific skeletal configurations and are incompatible with other skeletons. This means that for any desired target skeleton lacking a native motion dataset, learned interpolation is infeasible in practice, severely limiting the adoption of data-driven keyframe interpolation in animation pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242190/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse keyframe poses (placed every 5, 15, or 30 frames in 128-frame sequences) and a target skeleton configuration as input, and produces a full interpolated skeletal motion (per-bone quaternion rotations plus root 3D position per frame) on that target skeleton. The intermediate representation is a temporally consistent point cloud of 256 points per frame (σ=0.05, 64-dim feature vectors with 5-way body-group one-hot attributes), where each point maintains a fixed bone-local offset so it moves coherently with its parent bone across the sequence. Quaternions are represented as First-frame Offset Quaternions (FOQ) rather than absolute rotations, making the representation roll-invariant.

## Method

PC-MRL has two stages: an unsupervised point-cloud-to-motion reconstruction that adapts source-skeleton motions to an arbitrary target skeleton, followed by supervised keyframe interpolation on the adapted data. For reconstruction, a Point Transformer encodes each frame's unordered point cloud and a temporal transformer decodes the sequence into target-skeleton poses, optimized by a temporally consistent KNN loss (K=8) that sums ℓ₂ distances over the entire sequence between matched body-group points in source and target point clouds, plus an optional end-effector positional loss. To handle the roll-axis information lost in point clouds, all quaternions are converted to FOQ (relative to the first frame), which is provably invariant to constant roll offsets. A Rest Pose Augmentation (RPA) strategy perturbs bone tails with random IK-aligned offsets during training to broaden the range of skeletal behaviors the model learns. The interpolation stage adapts the CITL transformer by substituting FOQ predictions for raw quaternions and replacing sinusoidal positional encodings with learned relative positions, enabling arbitrary-length keyframe inputs.

## Results

On the CMU MoCap test set, PC-MRL (trained unsupervised on LaFAN1 + Human3.6M) achieves L2P of 0.7139 and NPSS of 1.6370 for Walking & Running at 30-frame keyframe intervals, approaching the natively-supervised CITL baseline (0.7475 L2P, 1.6122 NPSS). In Swimming, PC-MRL actually surpasses CITL (0.7139 vs 0.7475 L2P at 30 frames), attributed to richer low-crawl supervision from LaFAN1. Across all four motion categories, PC-MRL consistently outperforms LERP and TG_complete, with the gap widening at longer keyframe intervals (30 frames) where learned motion features matter most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PC-MRL consistently outperforms LERP and TG_complete across all four motion categories and all three metrics, with the advantage most pronounced at 30-frame intervals (e.g., Walking L2P: 0.2848 vs 0.5527 for LERP and 0.3103 for TG_complete). Against BERT [12], PC-MRL is competitive but generally trails on Golf (0.2706 vs 0.2967 L2P) and Walking (0.2848 vs 0.3544 L2P), while matching or exceeding it on Swimming. The natively-supervised CITL [34] remains the strongest baseline in most categories (e.g., Basketball L2P 0.5708 vs PC-MRL's 0.6662), but PC-MRL surpasses it on Swimming (0.7139 vs 0.7475 L2P), likely due to richer low-crawl supervision from LaFAN1. In the separate re-targeting benchmark (Table 2), PC-MRL achieves L_KNN of 6.73 and L_end of 5.63 at σ=0.05, substantially outperforming the Primal Skeleton method [1] (9.42 and 9.70 respectively), confirming superior geometric fidelity in cross-skeleton adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior CITL transformer [34] for keyframe interpolation, adapting it with FOQ quaternion representations and learned relative positional encodings. The core departure is from all prior data-driven motion interpolation and re-targeting methods, which require a native dataset matching the target skeleton's configuration. By introducing point cloud obfuscation as a skeleton-agnostic intermediate representation and a temporally consistent KNN loss for unsupervised reconstruction, the paper sidesteps the paired-data requirement that constrains both supervised interpolation models and unpaired re-targeting approaches like primal skeletons [1].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PC-MRL removes the practical bottleneck that has limited ML-based motion interpolation in animation pipelines: the need for a motion dataset recorded on the exact target skeleton. This opens the door to leveraging large existing datasets (LaFAN1, Human3.6M) for interpolation on arbitrary custom skeletons. Key limitations remain — the method requires at least one known pose for FOQ anchoring, cannot recover fine-grained features like fingers or facial controls, and still trails native supervision in most categories. Open questions include whether the geometric KNN objective can be augmented to better capture skeletal articulation patterns, and whether the approach generalizes beyond humanoid skeletons or to longer, more complex motion sequences.
