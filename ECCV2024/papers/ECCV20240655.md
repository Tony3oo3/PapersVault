## Problem

Existing RL-based alignment methods for text-to-image diffusion models (e.g., DPOK, ReFL, AlignProp) exclusively fine-tune the U-Net while keeping the pretrained text encoder fixed, leaving a suboptimal text-image mapping that requires careful prompt engineering. The paper identifies that this fixed text encoder is a bottleneck: even after U-Net fine-tuning, the model still suffers from misalignment because the text embeddings do not adequately represent the prompt semantics for the target task.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a standard RGB image via the Stable Diffusion pipeline (SDv1.4, SDv1.5, or SDv2.1 backbones). The text prompt is encoded by a CLIP text encoder augmented with LoRA adapters, whose low-rank weights can be swapped or linearly fused (Σαᵢθᵢ) to switch between task-specific behaviors (e.g., ImageReward alignment, face quality, hand quality) without retraining. No resolution or format constraints beyond the base diffusion model are introduced.

## Method

TexForce formulates the text encoder τ_φ as the policy network in a PPO-based RL loop (following DDPO), where the denoising U-Net is treated as a fixed environment and the reward function evaluates generated images on task-specific criteria (ImageReward, HPSv2, face quality, hand detection confidence). LoRA adapters are inserted into the CLIP text encoder to keep the parameter count small and prevent overfitting, and the PPO clipped objective is applied over the denoising trajectory to update only the text encoder weights. A key empirical finding is that the resulting text-encoder LoRA weights can be directly added to independently trained U-Net LoRA weights (e.g., from ReFL or DPOK) without any joint training, yielding substantially higher reward scores than either component alone or a jointly trained variant. The authors argue this works because the fixed U-Net serves as a stable pixel-generation prior during text-encoder optimization, and the two LoRA sets modify complementary aspects (semantics vs. appearance).

## Results

On the ImageReward dataset with SDv1.4, TexForce achieves a score of 0.4556, slightly surpassing ReFL (0.4485) and substantially outperforming the original SDv1.4 (0.2154). On HPSv2, TexForce scores 0.2767, marginally below AlignProb (0.2821) but above SDv1.4 (0.2752). The key finding is that simply combining TexForce's LoRA weights with existing U-Net finetuned models (ReFL+TexForce: 0.6553 on ImageReward; AlignProb+TexForce: 0.2914 on HPSv2) yields the best results without any additional training. Across SDv1.5 and SDv2.1 backbones, the combined model consistently achieves the highest ImageReward scores (0.6703 and 0.6158 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReFL (direct backpropagation on U-Net), TexForce achieves a slightly higher ImageReward score (0.4556 vs. 0.4485) on SDv1.4, and the paper attributes this to TexForce's superior text-image alignment versus ReFL's focus on visual appearance. Against AlignProb (also direct backpropagation), TexForce scores marginally lower on HPSv2 (0.2767 vs. 0.2821), which the authors explain by noting AlignProb over-optimizes toward reward-specific visual styles (e.g., yellow spotlights) at the cost of semantic coherence. Against DPOK (RL on U-Net), TexForce produces more prompt-consistent and realistic images across four individual scenarios (color, composition, count, location), with higher average ImageReward scores on both seen and unseen prompts. Critically, in every comparison the simple fusion of TexForce's text-encoder LoRA with the baseline's U-Net LoRA outperforms both individual methods, demonstrating that the two components address complementary weaknesses: U-Net finetuning improves visual quality while text-encoder finetuning improves semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DDPO (Black et al., ICLR 2024), which formulated diffusion denoising as a Markov decision process optimizable via PPO, but redirects that RL machinery from the U-Net to the text encoder. It departs from the dominant line of work (DPOK, ReFL, AlignProp, DRaFT) that fine-tunes the U-Net while treating the CLIP text encoder as a frozen, suboptimal conditioning signal. The use of LoRA for parameter-efficient text-encoder adaptation and the observation that separately-trained LoRA weights from the text encoder and U-Net can be naively summed without retraining represent the paper's key methodological departures from prior RL-for-diffusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that a small LoRA on the text encoder can be dropped onto any existing U-Net fine-tune (ReFL, DPOK, etc.) for immediate gains without additional training—lowers the barrier to improving text-image alignment in deployed diffusion pipelines. However, the quantitative improvements over strong baselines like ReFL are modest in isolation (e.g., 0.4556 vs 0.4485 ImageReward on SDv1.4), and the largest gains come from the combination, which somewhat dilutes the standalone contribution. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to newer architectures with non-CLIP text encoders (e.g., T5 in SDXL/Flux), whether it scales to video or 3D generation, and whether the RL training cost is justified relative to simpler prompt-optimization or reward-weighted regression alternatives.
