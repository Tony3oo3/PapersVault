## Problem

Self-supervised video representation learning has achieved strong results on exocentric videos, but no prior work addresses SSL for egocentric facial videos in social interaction contexts. Existing egocentric social datasets are small, low-quality, and lack the diversity needed for data-hungry pre-training, while the substantial viewpoint gap between exocentric and egocentric footage means that exocentric-pretrained encoders (e.g., VideoMAE, MARLIN) transfer poorly to egocentric inputs with rapid egomotions.

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the model receives a masked synthesized egocentric video (dynamic-perspective frames generated by TriplaneNet from exocentric VoxCeleb2 clips) and a single reference exocentric frame, both processed through cube embedding with shared tube masks (optimal ratio ~75%). The encoder produces layer-wise video feature representations that are egomotion-invariant, while the decoder reconstructs the full original exocentric frame (not just masked tokens, since input ≠ target). At inference, the frozen encoder takes an egocentric video clip and outputs facial feature embeddings used for downstream social role tasks (Talk-to-me, Look-at-me, Active Speaker Detection).

## Method

Ex2Eg-MAE extends VideoMAE with three coupled training objectives. First, a face-parsing-weighted reconstruction loss (L_rec) forces the decoder to recover the original exocentric video from masked dynamic-perspective inputs, doubling pixel weights on mouth/eyes regions to emphasize social signals. Second, a distillation loss (L_dist) aligns each encoder layer's features with those of a frozen VideoMAE teacher fed the original exocentric frames, anchoring the student to exocentric semantics. Third, a Perspective Shift Estimation Module (PSEM)—a lightweight cross-attention transformer attached to every encoder layer—adversarially estimates per-frame camera translation (3D) and rotation (6D) between the video and reference frame; Ex2Eg-MAE is trained to produce features that yield zero estimated shift, while PSEM is trained to predict the true shift, yielding egomotion-invariant representations. The synthetic training data (EgoVox, ~63K videos / 2.4M frames / 2.4K speakers) is created by inverting VoxCeleb2 frontal clips through TriplaneNet (EG3D) with randomized camera trajectories, providing high-quality faces with diverse egomotions at a scale comparable to the Ego4D social subset.

## Results

On the Ego4D Talk-to-me challenge (visual-only), Ex2Eg-MAE achieves 59.6% Acc-1 and 60.5% mAP, outperforming MARLIN (57.2%/58.9%) and VideoMAE (56.8%/57.8%). With Whisper audio fusion, it reaches state-of-the-art 61.0% Acc-1 and 68.1% mAP on Ego4D TTM. On Ego4D Look-at-me (large model), it achieves 93.5% Acc-1 and 78.3% mAP, approaching the supervised SOTA (79.0% mAP) without any external supervised pre-training. On EasyCom, it surpasses MARLIN by +1.2% mAP on Talk-to-me and +1.2% mAP on Active Speaker Detection (92.9% vs 91.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ex2Eg-MAE consistently outperforms both VideoMAE and MARLIN across all four evaluation tasks (Ego4D TTM, Ego4D LAM, EasyCom TTM, EasyCom ASD), with margins of +2.7%/+1.6% mAP over VideoMAE/MARLIN on Ego4D TTM (visual-only) and +1.2% mAP over MARLIN on EasyCom ASD. Against the Ego4D challenge baseline (ResNet-18 Bi-LSTM), Ex2Eg-MAE gains +6.6% mAP on TTM and +27.5% mAP on LAM (large model). On Ego4D LAM, Ex2Eg-MAE (large) achieves 78.3% mAP, narrowly trailing the supervised SOTA PKU-WICT-MIPL (79.0% mAP) while exceeding it on Acc-1 (93.5% vs 92.0%), all without any supervised pre-training on external gaze datasets. For Active Speaker Detection on EasyCom, Ex2Eg-MAE (92.9% mAP) surpasses both supervised methods TalkNet (86.9% mAP) and SPELL (89.5% mAP) by 6.0% and 3.4% respectively. The Ego4D-adapted variants (Eg4D-VideoMAE, Eg4D-MARLIN) trained on the Ego4D social subset underperform Ex2Eg-MAE trained on the synthetic EgoVox data, suggesting the benefit comes from data diversity rather than volume. Whisper serves as an audio complement rather than a direct competitor; the key finding is that Ex2Eg-MAE's visual features improve A+V fusion performance over prior visual encoders (67.4%→68.1% mAP on Ego4D TTM).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VideoMAE/MARLIN line of masked autoencoder pre-training for video by adapting it to the egocentric domain. It departs from MARLIN in three key ways: (1) the reconstruction target is the original exocentric video rather than the unmasked version of the same video, creating a cross-view reconstruction task; (2) a Perspective Shift Estimation Module (PSEM) is added at each encoder layer with adversarial training to enforce perspective invariance; and (3) a teacher-student distillation loss guides features toward those of a VideoMAE model seeing the original exocentric frames. The data augmentation strategy—using TriplaneNet (an EG3D inversion method) to synthesize dynamic-perspective egocentric videos from VoxCeleb2—is a novel repurposing of 3D face synthesis for self-supervised pre-training data creation, filling the gap left by the scarcity of real egocentric social datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first self-supervised pre-training framework specifically targeting egocentric facial videos for social role understanding, addressing a genuine data-scarcity bottleneck in the field. The EgoVox synthetic dataset (63K videos, 2.4K speakers) is a practical resource that outperforms real egocentric data of similar scale for pre-training, suggesting synthetic data generation via 3D face models is a viable strategy for egocentric representation learning. Limitations include the face-centric scope (the method relies on face synthesis and face parsing, limiting applicability to full-body or scene-level egocentric understanding), dependence on TriplaneNet's synthesis quality, and the relatively narrow evaluation on three social-role tasks. Open questions include whether the PSEM adversarial mechanism generalizes to non-facial egocentric content and whether the synthetic-to-real gap widens with more extreme egomotions or occlusions.
