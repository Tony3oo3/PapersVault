## Problem

Synthesizing action-conditioned 3D dynamics—where objects respond realistically to novel external forces or manipulations—requires knowing physical material properties like Young's modulus, yet these are extremely difficult to measure for real objects and no ground-truth data exists. Existing approaches either generate unconditional 4D dynamics that cannot respond to new interactions, or require manually specified material parameters that produce unrealistic motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a static 3D object represented as 3D Gaussians (0.5–1.5M particles) and a rendered image from a chosen viewpoint, which is animated by Stable Video Diffusion (14 frames, small motion bucket) to produce a reference video. At inference, the user applies arbitrary external forces or initial velocities to the foreground object (50–300K particles on a 64³ grid). The output is a spatially-varying Young's modulus field E(x) (8³ triplane + 3-layer MLP) and an initial velocity field (24³ triplane + 3-layer MLP), enabling interactive 3D dynamics rendered from arbitrary viewpoints under any new physical interaction.

## Method

PhysDreamer distills dynamics priors from a pre-trained video generation model by generating a plausible motion video of the object, then inverting it through differentiable MLS-MPM simulation and differentiable 3D Gaussian rendering to recover the spatially-varying Young's modulus field. The material field and initial velocity field are each parameterized by a triplane followed by a three-layer MLP, with total variation regularization for spatial smoothness. Optimization proceeds in two stages for stability: first the initial velocity is fit to the first three reference frames with frozen material, then the Young's modulus is optimized with frozen velocity and gradient flow restricted to the previous frame to prevent explosion. A K-Means subsampling strategy reduces simulation cost by 10–50×, simulating only 'driving particles' and recovering full-particle positions via local rigid-body transformation fitting at render time. The physics model uses a fixed corotated hyperelastic constitutive law with a constant Poisson's ratio, and 768 sub-steps (Δt ≈ 1×10⁻⁴ s) are executed between successive video frames.

## Results

PhysDreamer is evaluated via a 100-participant 2AFC user study across 8 real-world elastic object scenes (flowers, alocasia, beanie hat, telephone cord). Against PhysGaussian, 80.8% of participants prefer PhysDreamer for motion realism and 65.0% for visual quality. Against DreamGaussian4D, 63.5% prefer PhysDreamer for motion realism and 70.0% for visual quality. Notably, against real captured videos, 53.7% of participants still prefer PhysDreamer's synthesized motion realism, with the alocasia scene reaching 86% preference over real captures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysDreamer is compared against two baselines: PhysGaussian [70], which uses the same MPM-on-3D-Gaussians simulation framework but relies on manually set material parameters, and DreamGaussian4D [58], which generates non-interactive 4D dynamics by distilling a deformation field from a video diffusion model. Against PhysGaussian, PhysDreamer wins decisively on motion realism (80.8% vs. 19.2% preference), with the gap driven by PhysGaussian's lack of principled material estimation producing large, unrealistic slow oscillations. Against DreamGaussian4D, PhysDreamer wins on both visual quality (70.0%) and motion realism (63.5%), with the key advantage being the ability to simulate physical damping and extrapolate to novel interactions rather than replaying a fixed periodic motion. An additional 'motion amount' comparison shows 73.6% preference for PhysDreamer over DreamGaussian4D, confirming that the physics-based approach produces more dynamic and varied responses. Against real captured videos, PhysDreamer achieves a modest 53.7% motion realism preference, suggesting near-parity with real footage, though it lags on visual quality (37.3%) due to rendering artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysDreamer builds directly on PhysGaussian's framework of running MLS-MPM simulation on 3D Gaussian particles, but departs from it by automating the material property estimation step that PhysGaussian leaves to manual specification. The key conceptual leap is using a pre-trained video generation model (Stable Video Diffusion) as an implicit teacher: the generated video encodes dynamics priors that, when matched via differentiable simulation, reveal the spatially-varying Young's modulus field. This positions the work at the intersection of generative video models and differentiable physics, distinct from prior dynamic 3D generation methods (DreamGaussian4D, 4D-Fy) that produce fixed-length non-interactive sequences, and from prior interactive methods (PAC-NeRF, DANO) that require ground-truth or manually set material parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that video generation models implicitly encode physically meaningful material properties, opening a path to automate the most labor-intensive step in physics-based 3D interaction. However, the approach is currently restricted to elastic objects without collisions, requires manual object segmentation and boundary condition specification, and is computationally expensive (~1 minute per second of video on a V100). The user study finding that 86% of participants preferred the simulated alocasia over real captures—attributed to MPM's tendency toward lower-frequency motion and human bias toward smoother motion—raises interesting questions about what 'realistic' means in synthetic dynamics. Broader applicability to inelastic, collisional, or multi-body interactions remains an open challenge.
