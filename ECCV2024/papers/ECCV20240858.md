## Problem

Multi-person video HOI recognition suffers from two key limitations: existing methods treat human, object, and interaction components with disentangled representations, ignoring their inherent interdependencies, and they lack the capacity to model collaborative interactions among multiple persons in spatio-temporal scenes. This results in suboptimal representational accuracy, particularly when multiple humans jointly perform activities with shared objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240858/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip of T frames where human features (bounding box + skeleton keypoints) and object features (bounding box) are extracted via a 2G-GCN backbone, augmented with CLIP text embeddings for interaction prompts and BLIP-generated frame captions as context. It outputs per-frame interaction class probabilities over K classes and temporal segmentation boundaries for each human's sub-activity, evaluated via F1@k with IoU thresholds of 10%, 25%, and 50% over predicted action segments.

## Method

The architecture comprises three key modules: a Context Fuser (CF) with object, interaction, and human fuser blocks that progressively fuse local temporal object features, cross-attend human/object/context features against CLIP-encoded interaction prompts, and model inter-person influence via cross-attention; an Interaction State Reasoner (ISR) that interpolates between current and previous entity representations using softmax-weighted state embeddings ('continue' vs. 'stop') to capture interaction transition dynamics; and a BiGRU for temporal modeling. Classification is performed via an interaction-centric hypersphere in R^d, where each interaction class occupies a hypersphere center and human-object entity representations are expected to lie on its surface, with a fixed radius λ controlling the decision boundary. Training uses focal loss for class imbalance plus an interaction feature loss that enforces temporal smoothness within segments and separation across segment boundaries.

## Results

On the multi-person MPHOI-72 dataset, the proposed method achieves F1@10=91.6, F1@25=84.5, and F1@50=67.7, surpassing the SOTA 2G-GCN by 23.0%, 23.7%, and 22.5% respectively. On the single-person CAD-120 dataset, the method reaches F1@10=90.7, F1@25=88.1, F1@50=77.6, slightly outperforming 2G-GCN (89.5/87.1/76.2) and ACoLP (90.2/87.4/76.8). On Bimanual Actions, the method achieves F1@10=85.0 (tied with 2G-GCN), F1@25=82.9 (+0.9%), and F1@50=74.2 (+5.0%) over 2G-GCN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against 2G-GCN [27] (ECCV 2022), the current SOTA for video HOI recognition. On MPHOI-72, the proposed method outperforms 2G-GCN by a large margin of 23.0/23.7/22.5 percentage points on F1@10/25/50, demonstrating the critical importance of the human fuser block for multi-person context modeling. On CAD-120, the method edges out 2G-GCN by ~1% and also surpasses ACoLP [35] (ICCV 2023) by 0.5/0.7/0.8 points, while substantially outperforming older baselines like rCRF [29] (RSS 2015) and ATCRF [11] (TPAMI 2015). On Bimanual Actions, the method ties 2G-GCN on F1@10 (85.0) but leads by 0.9% and 5.0% on F1@25 and F1@50, and dramatically outperforms the original Dreher et al. [4] (RA-L 2020) baseline (40.6/34.8/22.2). ASSIGN [23] (CVPR 2021) is consistently outperformed across all three datasets, with gaps of 23–32 points on MPHOI-72. Overall, the method wins or ties on every dataset and metric, with the largest gains concentrated in the multi-person setting that is the paper's primary focus.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video HOI recognition line initiated by RNN+GNN approaches (ASSIGN, 2G-GCN) and Transformer-based image HOI detectors, but departs from them by explicitly modeling the structural interdependence among human, object, and interaction components rather than learning disentangled representations. It repurposes hyperspherical prototype networks (originally for class-level geometric representation) into an interaction-centric hypersphere where the interaction sits at the center and human-object entities lie on the surface, and it borrows CLIP/BLIP text encoders to seed interaction and context features with language priors. The Context Fuser and Interaction State Reasoner modules are novel architectural contributions that combine cross-attention fusion with a state-interpolation mechanism for temporal reasoning, sitting between the GNN-based and Transformer-based paradigms in the video HOI literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >22% F1 gain on MPHOI-72 is the largest reported improvement for multi-person video HOI to date, suggesting that prior methods substantially underfit the collaborative structure of multi-person scenes. However, the evidence base is narrow: MPHOI-72 contains only 72 videos with 3 fixed subjects, and the method's reliance on CLIP/BLIP as frozen feature initializers raises questions about scalability to open-vocabulary or large-scale interaction taxonomies. Open questions include whether the fixed-radius hypersphere generalizes when the number of interaction classes grows, whether the interaction state reasoner's binary continue/stop formulation captures richer transition dynamics, and whether the approach transfers to real-time or streaming settings where the bidirectional GRU is inapplicable.
