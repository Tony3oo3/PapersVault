## Problem

Despite the widespread use of negative prompts in text-to-image diffusion models like Stable Diffusion, their intrinsic mechanisms remain largely unexplored. Most prior work relies on empirical observations without understanding when negative prompts begin to influence generation or how they actually remove content from the latent space, preventing users from designing more effective prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on Stable Diffusion v2 in its latent space with 30 diffusion steps, taking a positive text prompt (describing the desired scene) and a negative text prompt (specifying what to exclude) as input. The key operational detail is that the negative prompt is applied only during a specific window of diffusion steps (default: steps 5–15) rather than throughout the entire reverse-diffusion process, producing an inpainted image where the targeted object is removed while the surrounding background is preserved. Evaluation uses GPT-4V and human raters to judge removal success and background fidelity across 1000 prompts × 5 seeds per dataset.

## Method

The paper identifies two core mechanisms governing negative prompts: (1) a Delayed Effect, where cross-attention analysis shows negative prompts cannot attend to the correct spatial location until after the positive prompt has rendered the target object (critical step ≈ step 5 for nouns, ≈ step 10 for adjectives), and (2) Deletion Through Neutralization, where the negative prompt generates a noise component aligned with the target object that cancels the positive noise via the subtraction in classifier-free guidance (Eq. 5). The authors further explain a counter-intuitive 'Reverse Activation' phenomenon—applying negative prompts in early steps paradoxically generates the object—through two diffusion dynamics: an Inducing Effect (negative prompts push positive noise toward the object direction) and a Momentum Effect (consecutive noise estimates maintain high cosine similarity up to 99.5%). Building on these insights, the proposed inpainting method simply delays the introduction of the negative prompt until after the critical step, requiring no retraining or architectural modification, and achieves ~85% relative removal success while preserving ~83% background similarity compared to applying negative prompts across all steps.

## Results

On the GPT-4V evaluation (Table 1a), the proposed method (applying negative prompts only in steps 5–15) achieves an average Removal Success Rate (RSR) of 63.46%, surpassing the baseline of 54.16% (negative prompts applied to all steps), while maintaining a Comparison Rate (CR) of 82.84% indicating strong background preservation. Under human evaluation (Table 1b), the gap widens further: the proposed method reaches 80.35% RSR versus 65.11% for the baseline, with a CR of 88.45%. The Relative Removal Success Rate (RRSR) averages 85.37% (GPT-4V) and 80.62% (human), confirming that the method remains highly effective despite using negative prompts for fewer diffusion steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard practice of applying negative prompts across all 30 diffusion steps (following Woolf's recommendation). The proposed method, which introduces negative prompts only during steps 5–15, outperforms this baseline on every dataset for both RSR and CR under both GPT-4V and human evaluation. On average, the proposed method improves RSR by 9.3 percentage points (GPT-4V) and 15.2 percentage points (human), while simultaneously achieving 82–88% background similarity (CR), which the all-steps baseline does not report but is implied to be lower due to its known tendency to alter surrounding content. The RRSR values (80–87%) confirm that the efficiency loss from using fewer negative-prompt steps is modest, with the largest gap on MSVD (75.00% human) and the smallest on Places (87.20% GPT-4V). Overall, the paper demonstrates that delaying negative prompts past the critical step (~step 5) is strictly superior to the conventional all-steps approach in both removal effectiveness and background preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance framework (Ho & Salimans, 2022) by replacing the unconditional branch with a negative prompt, a practical trick popularized by Woolf (2023) but never systematically analyzed. While prior prompt-analysis work (Tang, Hertz, Tumanyan) dissected positive-prompt cross-attention dynamics, and object-removal methods (Yildirim, Yang) addressed inpainting of existing images, no prior study examined the temporal dynamics of negative prompts or their interaction with positive prompts across diffusion steps. The paper carves new ground by introducing a quantitative metric for negative-prompt strength over time and identifying the 'Delayed Effect' and 'Reverse Activation' phenomena, positioning itself as the first mechanistic study of this widely-used but poorly understood feature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway—that negative prompts should be applied only after a critical step (~step 5 of 30) to remove objects while preserving background—offers a simple, training-free improvement for Stable Diffusion users and inpainting pipelines. The Reverse Activation finding (early negative prompts paradoxically generate the excluded object) challenges the intuitive 'just add a negative prompt' workflow and has implications for prompt engineering best practices. Limitations include evaluation on a single model (SD v2) at a fixed 30-step schedule, restriction to noun and adjective prompts, and reliance on GPT-4V for automated evaluation. Open questions include whether the critical-step timing generalizes across architectures, step counts, and more complex prompt structures (multiple objects, spatial relationships), and whether architectural modifications to increase positive-negative information exchange could eliminate the delay entirely.
