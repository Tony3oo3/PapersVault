## Problem

In radar-camera depth completion, sparse single-frame LiDAR supervision produces stripe-like scanning artifacts in predicted depth maps, leading prior work to assume sparse supervision is inherently inferior and resort to noisy dense supervision via multi-frame stacking or interpolation. This paper identifies the root cause as LiDAR Distribution Leakage (LDL)—the model implicitly memorizing the stripe-like positional pattern of projected LiDAR points—and shows that sparse supervision can actually outperform dense supervision when this leakage is properly addressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (900×1600) and a single-frame sparse radar point cloud (50–80 points) as input, organized into a multi-scale pyramid (original, 1/2, and 1/4 downsampled versions of both modalities). It outputs a dense depth map at the same 900×1600 resolution covering 0–80 m. Training supervision uses only a single-frame 2D-projected LiDAR depth map (sparse, ~50–80 points), avoiding the noisy multi-frame or interpolated dense ground truth used by prior methods.

## Method

The Disruption-Compensation framework tackles LDL in two stages. The Disruption stage applies Camera Intrinsics Disruption (random upsampling and cropping of image, radar, and LiDAR data, equivalent to perturbing intrinsics) and Radar Disruption (extending 2D-projected radar points into vertical lines to break the simple inverse-proportional v–Z relationship that lets models recover per-sample intrinsics). The Compensation stage offsets the resulting information loss via a Radar-aware Mask Decoder (a U-Net branch producing a binary mask over 12 object classes as auxiliary training supervision to guide radar point localization) and a Radar-Position Injection Module (a small MLP that directly encodes 3D radar coordinates into positional features). The depth completion backbone is a multi-scale U-Net with ResNeXt encoder, FPN fusion, and a lightweight decoder, trained end-to-end with multi-scale Smooth L1 depth loss plus mask loss under single-frame sparse LiDAR supervision.

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset, the proposed Disruption-Compensation framework with single-frame sparse LiDAR supervision achieves 1927.0 mm MAE (0–80 m), 4609.6 mm RMSE (0–80 m), and 1817.0 mm OMAE, outperforming the best dense-supervision baseline RadarNet (2179.3 mm MAE) by 11.6% in MAE. The method also runs at 21.7 FPS, approximately 1.6× faster than the next-fastest comparable method (Sparse-to-dense at 13.2 FPS) and ~5.9× faster than RadarNet (3.7 FPS). The newly proposed OMAE metric further confirms the advantage: 1817.0 mm vs. 2152.4 mm (DORN) and 2165.9 mm (RadarNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest dense-supervision baseline RadarNet [30] (161-frame LiDAR stacking), it reduces MAE from 2179.3 to 1927.0 mm (−11.6%) and RMSE from 4898.7 to 4609.6 mm, while running ~5.9× faster (21.7 vs. 3.7 FPS). DORN [20] (interpolation-based dense GT) is beaten by 540.7 mm in MAE and 944.7 mm in RMSE. RC-PDA [21] (25-frame stacking) shows the largest gap, with our method achieving 1786.6 mm lower MAE (1927.0 vs. 3713.6 mm). Among methods using interpolation for dense supervision, Sparse-to-dense [22] (2374.0 mm MAE) and PnP [33] (2496.0 mm MAE) are both surpassed by over 440 mm in MAE. The two LiDAR-camera methods retrained on the radar-camera task (CompletionFormer [37] at 3100.7 mm MAE and DySPN [18] at 3549.2 mm MAE) show a sharp accuracy drop, confirming that radar input poses fundamentally different challenges from LiDAR input. R4Dyn [7] reports only RMSE (6434.0 mm), which is 1824.4 mm worse than our method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in radar-camera depth completion, where prior methods (RadarNet, RC-PDA, DORN, Sparse-to-dense) generate dense LiDAR supervision via multi-frame stacking or interpolation to avoid stripe artifacts from sparse supervision. Instead, it retains single-frame sparse LiDAR supervision and attributes the stripe artifacts to a newly identified phenomenon—LiDAR Distribution Leakage (LDL)—where models implicitly memorize the sensor's scanning pattern. The Disruption-Compensation framework draws on data augmentation (camera intrinsics disruption), implicit neural representation (MLP-based radar position injection), and auxiliary semantic segmentation (Radar-aware Mask Decoder via ViT-Adapter) to counteract LDL, positioning itself as a conceptual rethinking rather than an architectural extension of prior depth completion networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LDL diagnosis and the demonstration that clean sparse supervision can outperform noisy dense supervision challenge a widely-held assumption in depth completion and potentially other tasks where dense ground truth is expensive or noisy to obtain. The OMAE metric provides a more meaningful evaluation tool that captures object-level smoothness violations invisible to standard MAE. However, the results are confined to a single dataset (nuScenes) and a single front-camera/front-radar configuration, leaving open whether the Disruption-Compensation strategy generalizes to other sensor pairs, datasets, or sparse-supervision tasks beyond radar-camera depth completion. The practical 1.6× speedup is meaningful for real-time autonomous driving but the absolute accuracy gains, while solid, are measured against baselines that use substantially more input frames.
