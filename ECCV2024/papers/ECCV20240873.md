## Problem

Existing document tampering detection methods that fuse frequency-domain (e.g., DCT) features with RGB features often degrade the model's ability to detect tampering visible in the spatial domain, particularly for uncompressed or heavily post-processed images. Additionally, the small and subtle nature of document tampering regions means that high-frequency details critical for localization are easily lost during standard downsampling operations, leaving a gap in how frequency information is both integrated and preserved across scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240873/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB document image at 512×512 resolution, converts it to YCbCr color space, and extracts DCT coefficients and a quantization table from the Y channel as a parallel frequency input. During inference, a JPEG compression step (quality 75–100) is applied before network input to match the DTD pipeline. The output is a pixel-wise binary segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution as the input, indicating tampered regions.

## Method

FFDN builds on a ConvNextV2-base backbone with an FPN head and introduces two key modules. The Visual Enhancement Module (VEM) applies channel and spatial attention to frequency features conditioned on RGB features, then injects the enhanced frequency signal into the RGB stream via a zero-initialized convolution, ensuring the model initially relies on RGB and only adaptively incorporates frequency cues when beneficial. The Wavelet-like Frequency Enhancement (WFE) decomposes multi-level encoder features into low-frequency (LL) and high-frequency (HH) components using a learnable Haar wavelet transform, enhances each with attention, fuses them across scales via Guidance-based Feature Aggregation modules, and finally merges the enhanced high-frequency map with the highest-resolution feature F1 and the low-frequency map with the deepest feature F4. Training uses a composite Cross-Entropy plus Lovász-Softmax loss over 100K iterations with AdamW on 4 V100 GPUs.

## Results

On the DocTamper-T dataset, FFDN achieves an IoU of 0.90, F1 of 0.86, Precision of 0.87, and Recall of 0.84, outperforming the strongest RGB-only baseline ConvNextV2 (IoU 0.86, F1 0.81) by 4.4% in IoU and 5.0% in F1. On the cross-domain DocTamper-FCD and DocTamper-SCD splits, FFDN reaches IoU 0.88/F1 0.92 and IoU 0.75/F1 0.81 respectively, surpassing DTD (the strongest frequency-aware baseline) by 9.0% and 7.1% in IoU. On the T-SROIE receipt dataset, FFDN attains a near-perfect F1 of 0.993, exceeding the prior best (Wang et al., F1 0.968) by 2.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FFDN outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest RGB-only segmentation backbones (ConvNextV2, InternImage, Mask2Former), FFDN gains 4–6 points in IoU on DocTamper-T and 13–23 points on the cross-domain FCD split, confirming that frequency-aware fusion adds substantial value beyond backbone quality. Against the closest frequency-aware competitor DTD [22], FFDN improves IoU by 6 points (0.90 vs 0.84) on DocTamper-T and by 9 points (0.88 vs 0.79) on FCD, while also showing smaller performance drops under JPEG compression (20.2% avg drop vs 28.5% for DTD at Q75). On the T-SROIE receipt benchmark, FFDN achieves F1 0.993, surpassing Wang et al. [36] (0.968), ATRR [34] (0.936), and EAST [47] (0.908) by 2.5, 5.7, and 8.5 points respectively. The older manipulation-detection baselines (PSCC-Net, UperNet, CAT-Net) are far behind, with PSCC-Net scoring only IoU 0.17 on DocTamper-T, underscoring the difficulty of the document-tampering setting for generic image-forensics methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DITD framework established by DTD (Qu et al., CVPR 2023), which introduced the DocTamper dataset and a dual-branch architecture combining RGB and DCT frequency features. The key departure is in how frequency information is integrated: rather than directly concatenating or adding DCT features to RGB features (as in DTD and CAT-Net), the authors propose a controlled, attention-guided fusion with zero-initialized convolutions to prevent frequency noise from degrading RGB-based detection. The Wavelet-like Frequency Enhancement module draws on deep adaptive wavelet decomposition (Rodriguez et al., WACV 2020) and Guidance-based Feature Aggregation (He et al., CVPR 2023) to explicitly separate and enhance high- and low-frequency components across multiple scales, addressing the loss of subtle tampering cues during downsampling that plagues prior high-resolution approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated solution to a real-world problem (document fraud detection) and offers a useful design principle: frequency-domain cues should be introduced adaptively rather than unconditionally, as naive fusion can actively harm RGB-based detection. The robustness analysis showing that DTD's performance degrades sharply under lower JPEG quality while FFDN remains more stable is practically significant for deployment scenarios where compression is variable. Open questions include generalization to non-JPEG formats (PNG, HEIC), robustness against increasingly sophisticated generative tampering (e.g., diffusion-based edits), and whether the wavelet decomposition approach transfers to other fine-grained detection tasks beyond document images.
