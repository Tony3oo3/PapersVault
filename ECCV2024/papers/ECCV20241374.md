## Problem

Existing multi-view crowd counting methods rely on flat ground-plane assumptions for feature transformation, use distance-based heuristic weights that ignore semantic content during multi-view fusion, and require identical fixed camera layouts at training and inference time. These limitations prevent accurate counting in real-world surveillance scenarios with uneven terrain, heavy occlusion, and arbitrary dynamic camera configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N synchronized RGB images from arbitrary camera views along with their intrinsic matrices K∈R^{3×3}, extrinsic matrices M∈R^{3×4}, and augmentation matrices. The 3D scene is discretized into a voxel grid of shape Z×Y×X where each voxel corresponds to a fixed real-world size s meters, and the volume center is aligned with the scene origin. The primary output is a per-voxel 3D scene-level density map, supplemented by a 2D image-level density map used as intermediate supervision to improve gradient flow through the deep architecture.

## Method

CountFormer lifts 2D image features into a 3D volume representation via a deformable attention-based Feature Lifting Module that uses learnable volume queries encoded with camera parameters (replacing IPM/STN and eliminating the flat-ground assumption). A Multi-View Volume Aggregation Module then fuses per-view volumes using per-voxel attention weights computed from camera-encoded queries, jointly considering both geometric camera position and semantic feature content rather than relying on distance priors alone. A dual camera encoding strategy embeds intrinsic and extrinsic parameters into both the image-level features (via spatial-aligned parameter vectors processed by an MLP) and the volume queries, enabling the network to handle arbitrary dynamic camera layouts and varying numbers of views. The multi-scale volume features are fused through a 3D FPN, and a 3D convolution head regresses the final density map, trained with a combined L2 loss on 3D density and an auxiliary 2D density supervision term.

## Results

CountFormer achieves scene-level MAE of 0.74 on PETS2009 (76% reduction over the previous SOTA of 3.08), 4.72 on CityStreet (nearly halving the SOTA of 6.98), and 4.79 on CVCS (outperforming the prior best of 7.22 by 34%). On DukeMTMC, CountFormer achieves MAE 0.88, essentially matching the SOTA of 0.87. The corresponding NAE values are 0.030 (PETS2009), 0.058 (CityStreet), and 0.039 (CVCS), confirming consistent improvements across both evaluation metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CountFormer substantially outperforms all MVC baselines on three of four datasets. On PETS2009, it reduces MAE from 3.08 (Zheng et al. [95]) to 0.74, a 76% improvement, and on CityStreet it lowers MAE from 6.98 (Zhang et al. [90]) to 4.72. On the large-scale CVCS benchmark, CountFormer achieves MAE 4.79 versus 7.22 for the CVCS method [91] and 12.8 for 3D Counting [87], representing a 178% improvement over [88]. The only dataset where CountFormer does not clearly win is DukeMTMC (MAE 0.88 vs. 0.87 for [95]), which the authors attribute to saturated performance, inaccurate annotations, and non-overlapping camera views. Against the MVP baseline BEVFormer [29], CountFormer's added volume-level camera embedding and MV volume aggregation module yield significant gains (e.g., CVCS MAE drops from 9.56 to 4.79), confirming that naive transfer of MVP architectures to MVC is insufficient without the proposed camera-aware fusion components.

## Relation to Prior Work

CountFormer adapts the multi-view perception (MVP) paradigm from BEVFormer—learnable volume queries with cross-view deformable attention for lifting image features into a scene-level representation—and repurposes it for the fundamentally different multi-view counting (MVC) setting, where cameras have heavy FOV overlap rather than complementary coverage. It departs from prior MVC methods (Zhang et al. 2019–2022) that relied on inverse perspective mapping under a flat-ground assumption and distance-only fusion weights, replacing both with a deformable-attention-based feature lifting module and a semantic-aware volume aggregation module. The camera encoding strategy, which injects intrinsic/extrinsic parameters into both volume queries and image features, is the key architectural addition that enables handling arbitrary dynamic camera layouts, a capability absent from both prior MVC and MVP methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first MVC framework that does not require a fixed camera layout, making it directly applicable to real-world surveillance deployments where cameras are added, removed, or repositioned. The substantial margins (76% MAE reduction on PETS2009, ~50% on CityStreet, 178% on CVCS) validate that the flat-ground and distance-only-fusion assumptions in prior work were significant performance bottlenecks. Open questions include whether the 3D volume representation scales to very large scenes without prohibitive memory cost, whether the method can handle temporally non-synchronized views, and how robust it is to significant extrinsic parameter errors beyond the small perturbations tested. The ablation showing that volume-level camera encoding (not image-level) is the critical component for dynamic layouts provides a useful design guideline for future multi-view methods.
