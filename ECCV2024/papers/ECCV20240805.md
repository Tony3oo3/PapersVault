## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods require either normal-only labels (one-class classification) or video-level annotations (weakly-supervised learning), both of which are labor-intensive and limited to observed anomaly categories. Unsupervised VAD, which needs no labels at all, remains largely unexplored, with only the GCL framework having been proposed. This paper tackles the challenge of combining OCC and WS models into a single unsupervised training loop without human annotation.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled video clips (a mix of normal and abnormal events) and splits them into snippets for the WS model and pose-sequence objects for the wOCC model. It produces both frame-level and snippet-level anomaly scores, evaluated via frame-level AUC on ShanghaiTech and UBnormal. No video-level or snippet-level labels are provided at any training stage; the only user-specified hyperparameters are a rough initial threshold ratio R% and a stopping-rate parameter Q%.

## Method

The framework interleaves training of a weighted OCC (wOCC) model and a weakly-supervised MIL model, where each generates pseudo-labels for the other. The key modification to OCC is replacing hard binary labels with soft weights in [0,1] (wOCC), which stabilizes training by preventing sudden label flips that cause performance fluctuation. The WS model (e.g., RTFM) uses snippet-level pseudo-labels from wOCC to construct positive/negative bags, while wOCC receives soft labels directly from the WS model's anomaly scores without thresholding. An adaptive thresholding mechanism monotonically decreases the WS threshold across repeated training modules by computing the intersection of high-anomaly snippet sets identified by all previously trained wOCC models, progressively refining a user-specified rough threshold toward an optimal value. Training is initialized by sampling soft labels from a Beta(1,5) distribution to reflect the prior that most data is normal.

## Results

Under the unsupervised setting, the proposed framework (STG-NF as wOCC + RTFM with I3D as WS) achieves 88.18% AUC on ShanghaiTech and 63.10% AUC on UBnormal, substantially outperforming the only prior unsupervised method GCL (76.14% on ShanghaiTech). The wOCC component alone reaches 82.57% (ShanghaiTech) and 74.76% (UBnormal), surpassing STG-NF trained on all data (80.29% / 70.48%). When degenerated to the supervised WS setting, Our_WS(RTFM) attains 96.33% on ShanghaiTech and 67.42% on UBnormal, matching or exceeding the best supervised WS baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior unsupervised method GCL [47], the proposed framework wins by a large margin: 88.18% vs. 76.14% AUC on ShanghaiTech (12+ points), attributed to using a stronger OCC backbone (STG-NF vs. a simple autoencoder). Compared to STG-NF trained on all data without labels (80.29% / 70.48%), the wOCC component improves to 82.57% / 74.76%, with a larger gain on UBnormal (4.28 pts) where more abnormal data degrades the unweighted baseline. In the supervised WS setting, Our_WS(RTFM) outperforms the original RTFM [34] on both datasets (96.33% vs. 96.10% on ShanghaiTech; 67.42% vs. 66.83% on UBnormal) and beats Li et al. [17] (96.08%) and Sultani et al. [32] (84.53%), but falls slightly short of S3R [43] (97.48%) on ShanghaiTech. In the supervised OCC setting, the weighted variant Our**_wOCC(STG-NF) edges out the original STG-NF [13] (86.37% vs. 85.90% on ShanghaiTech; 72.81% vs. 71.80% on UBnormal), confirming the benefit of soft-label weighting even among purely normal data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two established but separate paradigms in video anomaly detection—One-Class Classification (OCC) and Weakly-Supervised (WS) learning—into a single unsupervised training loop. It builds directly on STG-NF (normalizing flows over pose sequences) and RTFM (top-k robust temporal feature magnitude MIL), modifying the former to accept soft weighted labels (wOCC) and the latter to consume snippet-level pseudo labels rather than video-level annotations. The prior unsupervised approach, GCL [47], relied on a weak autoencoder-generator plus FC discriminator trained adversarially; this work departs by plugging in substantially stronger, independently developed OCC and WS backbones and coupling them through mutual pseudo-labeling with an adaptive thresholding schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unsupervised VAD removes the need for laborious and incomplete anomaly annotations, which is critical given the open-set nature of real-world abnormal events. The framework's modularity—swapping in any recent OCC or WS model—makes it a practical template that will benefit as those subfields advance. Limitations include dependence on a rough initial threshold (R%), evaluation on only two datasets, and the observation that training more than one epoch per interleaving step degrades performance, suggesting the pseudo-label quality ceiling is reached quickly. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to audio or multi-modal anomaly detection and whether the consensus-based thresholding scales to datasets with highly variable anomaly ratios.
