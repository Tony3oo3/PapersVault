## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods produce synthetic images with highly symmetric, duplicated patterns on the left and right halves, failing to capture fine-grained details of objects. The paper identifies the root cause as 'bilateral equivalence' in real datasets, where discriminative object parts appear with equal probability on both sides, causing distillation to encode the same parts redundantly on both halves of synthetic images. No prior method addressed this phenomenon.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real image dataset (CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, or ImageNet subsets at 128×128) and a ConvNet architecture (3–5 blocks of 128-channel convolutions) as input, and produces a small set of synthetic images (1, 10, or 50 per class) as output. FYI is a plug-and-play wrapper: it does not modify the training objective, network architecture, or retraining protocol of the underlying distillation method. During the final retraining phase, flipped counterparts are not concatenated, ensuring a fair comparison with vanilla methods.

## Method

FYI (Flip Your Images) augments the synthetic image set S_c with its horizontally flipped counterpart to form A_c = S_c ∪ Flip(S_c), then computes the standard distillation distance D_θ(T_c, A_c) over this augmented set. Because horizontal flipping and batch-wise concatenation are differentiable operations, gradients flow through the augmented set back to the original synthetic images via the chain rule, updating them in a direction that prevents a discriminative part placed on one side from being duplicated on the other. This simple modification breaks the bilateral equivalence that causes symmetric duplication, encouraging the left and right halves of synthetic images to encode different semantic content. The approach is architecture- and objective-agnostic, seamlessly integrating with gradient matching (DC), distribution matching (DM), trajectory matching (MTT), siamese augmentation (DSA), and other distillation methods without any modification to their training loops.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 1 IPC, MTT+FYI achieves 47.2% top-1 accuracy (vs. 46.3% for MTT), and FTD+FYI reaches 49.5% (vs. 46.8% for FTD). On CIFAR-100 at 1 IPC, MTT+FYI improves from 24.3% to 28.2%, and FTD+FYI from 25.2% to 28.7%. On Tiny-ImageNet at 1 IPC, MTT+FYI gains 1.4 points (9.0% → 10.4%) and FTD+FYI gains 1.2 points (10.4% → 11.6%). Gains are most pronounced at the 1 IPC setting, where FYI provides 2.7%, 3.5%, and 1.2% improvements over FTD on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FYI consistently improves every baseline it is applied to. Over MTT [1], FYI adds 0.9–3.9 points on CIFAR-10/100 and 1.4–0.5 points on Tiny-ImageNet, with the largest gain at 1 IPC (e.g., 24.3%→28.2% on CIFAR-100). Over FTD [8], FYI yields 2.7 points on CIFAR-10 (1 IPC) and 3.5 points on CIFAR-100 (1 IPC), while matching or slightly exceeding FTD at 50 IPC. Over DSA [44], FYI provides 1.8–3.1 points across all IPC settings on CIFAR-10/100. Over DC [45], FYI gains 1.7–4.6 points, and over DM [46], 2.7–4.2 points. Over IDC [16], FYI adds a more modest 0.6–2.4 points, suggesting IDC's parameterization already partially mitigates the bilateral equivalence. CAFE [38] and DataDAM [35] are reported as standalone baselines without FYI variants; DataDAM remains competitive at higher IPC but is outperformed by MTT+FYI and FTD+FYI at 1 IPC on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the established dataset distillation literature (DC, DSA, DM, MTT, FTD, IDC) and identifies a shared failure mode—bilateral equivalence—that none of these methods address. It departs from DSA, which also uses augmentation but to match how networks respond to augmented inputs, by instead using horizontal flipping to prevent duplicated discriminative patterns across image halves. The approach is conceptually distinct from parameterization-based methods (IDC, HaBa) that aim to encode richer semantics through architectural changes; FYI requires no modification to training objectives or network architectures, making it a purely procedural addition to the distillation loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bilateral equivalence observation provides a useful diagnostic for understanding why synthetic images from current methods look symmetric and lack fine-grained detail, and the horizontal-flip fix is trivially adoptable by any existing pipeline. The limitation that the method targets natural images with arbitrary orientations (and may not help for orientation-sensitive tasks like digit or character recognition) is acknowledged but leaves open whether analogous symmetry-breaking strategies exist for other modalities. A broader open question is whether the bilateral equivalence phenomenon generalizes beyond 2D images (e.g., to point clouds or video) and whether other simple augmentations could address related redundancy issues in other compression settings.
