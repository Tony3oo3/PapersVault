## Problem

Existing VideoQA benchmarks focus on common, less surprising videos (cooking, instructional, daily life) and fail to test whether models can understand counter-intuitive content such as humor, creative performances, and visual illusions. Understanding these videos requires recognizing commonsense violations and appreciating unexpected contrasts, a fundamentally different reasoning challenge that current VLMs handle poorly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a video clip (average 19 seconds, 4.3K clips totaling 24 hours) paired with a text question, and produces task-specific outputs: a time-span for counter-intuitive localization, free-text descriptions and reasoning explanations averaging 34.2 words (far exceeding the 2.6-word norm in NExT-QA), a concise video title, or a creativity score from 1–20. A key design constraint is that 86% of questions cannot be answered from 8 static frames alone, forcing models to leverage temporal dynamics rather than single-frame appearance.

## Method

The paper contributes two main components. First, FunQA is a 312K-QA-pair dataset spanning three subsets (HumorQA, CreativeQA, MagicQA), each with four task types: counter-intuitive timestamp localization, detailed description, counter-intuitiveness reasoning, and higher-level tasks (title generation, creativity scoring, magic method explanation). Annotations were produced in Chinese by 50+ annotators over ~900 hours, then translated and expanded via GPT-3.5, with 91% high consensus verified on a 10% sample. Second, FunMentor is an agent that refines a VLM's answer through multi-turn dialogues in three stages: Real Fact Collection (probing the VLM for objective video content), Answer Judgement (scoring responses on fluency, factual accuracy, logical coherence, and humorous insight), and Suggestion Generation (crafting targeted feedback prompts to guide revision). FunMentor is evaluated on top of Video-ChatGPT and Otter, showing up to 20.9-point GPT-4 score improvements on reasoning tasks.

## Results

No evaluated VLM achieves non-zero IOU on the counter-intuitive timestamp localization tasks (H1, C1, M1), indicating a fundamental gap in temporal reasoning. On free-text tasks, the best GPT-4 scores are: LLaVA-NeXT (69.8 on H3-Reasoning), Video-ChatGPT (41.2 on H4-Title), and Otter+FunMentor (54.8 on M3-Reasoning). FunMentor applied to Otter (FunQA) improves GPT-4 scores by up to 20.9 points over the base model on H3-Reasoning. On the multiple-choice comparison, GPT-4V scores 80% on NExT-QA but drops to 61% on FunQA-MC and 39% on FunQA-MC-R, confirming FunQA's greater difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FunMentor consistently improves over its base models: applied to Otter (FunQA), it raises GPT-4 scores by 4.8 on H3-Reasoning (31.7→45.8) and 20.9 on M3-Reasoning (47.5→56.4); applied to Video-ChatGPT, it boosts H4-Title from 41.2 to 65.1 and H2-Description from 24.3 to 33.2. Among instruction-based VLMs, LLaVA-NeXT achieves the highest single GPT-4 score (69.8 on H3-Reasoning) but has the lowest BLEURT (16.9), suggesting verbose but less lexically aligned outputs. Caption-based models (mPLUG, GIT) score near zero on reasoning tasks and cannot produce temporal localizations. On the NExT-QA comparison, GPT-4V scores 80% accuracy on NExT-QA but only 39% on FunQA-MC-R (counter-intuitive reasoning subset), a 41-point drop that highlights FunQA's greater difficulty. Traditional metrics (BLEU-4, CIDEr, WUPS) yield near-zero or misleadingly low scores for VLM free-text responses, motivating the paper's use of GPT-4-based evaluation. Human annotators achieve 83.6–84.4 GPT-4 scores on HumorQA tasks, leaving a 30–40 point gap over the best model (SOTA: 33.2–45.8), confirming substantial room for improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within VideoQA by shifting focus from the common, less-surprising videos (cooking, daily life, TV shows) that dominate existing benchmarks like NExT-QA, TGIF-QA, and MovieQA, to a previously unexplored domain of counter-intuitive and surprising content (humor, creativity, magic). While prior work on counter-intuitiveness (Whoops, OOPS, MemeGraphs) targets images or memes, and humor-in-video datasets (UR-FUNNY, laughter-track prediction) rely heavily on audio and narrative cues, FunQA is the first to systematically evaluate visual reasoning over surprising video content with free-text QA averaging 34.2 words per answer. The FunMentor agent repurposes multi-turn dialogue as a lightweight fine-tuning mechanism for VLMs, positioning itself between full supervised fine-tuning and zero-shot prompting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FunQA establishes a substantial (312K QA pairs, 24 video hours) and challenging benchmark that exposes a fundamental gap: no current VLM can perform counter-intuitive timestamp localization (all score 0.0 IOU), and GPT-4V's accuracy drops from 80% on NExT-QA to 39% on FunQA-MC-R, revealing that existing models lack genuine understanding of visual commonsense violations. The 86% finding that questions cannot be answered from static frames underscores the temporal-dynamics requirement. Open questions include whether denser (object-level, spatial) annotations would further differentiate models, whether the Chinese-to-English annotation pipeline introduces cultural biases, and whether agent-based refinement (FunMentor) can scale beyond the modest ~10-point GPT-4 improvements observed here.
