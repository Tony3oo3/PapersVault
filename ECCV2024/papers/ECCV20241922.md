## Problem

Generating 4D dynamic shapes (3D objects deforming over time) remains underexplored compared to static 3D generation. The prior approach, HyperDiffusion, directly generates weights of 4D occupancy fields, but because motion is not separated from shape in the representation, it requires marching cubes mesh extraction at every frame, causing slow rendering and poor temporal consistency as vertices and faces are redefined each time step.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241922/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For unconditional generation, the model takes Gaussian noise in a 1D latent space and produces a 16-frame mesh sequence of 4,096 vertices per frame, structured as a per-vertex animation (a single base mesh plus per-vertex offsets over time). For conditional generation, 2,048 points sampled from the first frame's mesh surface serve as the conditioning signal. The latent vector is a concatenation of a shape feature and a motion feature, both 1D vectors extracted by separate encoders, so the output mesh topology is fixed from the first frame and only vertex positions change across time.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-part neural representation that disentangles shape from motion: a conditional SDF (modulated by a PointNet-based shape encoder) encodes the base mesh surface, and a neural deformation representation encodes motion by predicting per-part skinning weights and rigid transformations for k=40 parts, applied via linear blend skinning. The motion encoder combines a PointNet variant with a GRU to process sequential part-center features into a single motion feature vector. A DALLE-2-style diffusion model is trained on the concatenated shape-and-motion latent vectors, and at inference the generated latent is split to drive SDF-based mesh extraction for frame zero and LBS-based vertex deformation for all subsequent frames. This per-vertex animation formulation eliminates per-frame mesh redefinition, yielding both faster rendering (~5x speedup over HyperDiffusion) and stable temporal consistency, while the part-based skinning structure enables applications like motion retargeting between different shapes.

## Results

On the DeformingThings4D dataset, the proposed method achieves MMD of 16.4 (vs. 19.6 for HyperDiffusion), COV of 52.8% (vs. 41.8%), and 1-NNA of 61.9% (vs. 64.1%) for unconditional 4D shape generation. Rendering time drops dramatically from 40.4 s to 8.1 s per sample, a roughly 5× speedup, because only the first frame requires marching cubes extraction. For 4D point cloud completion on D-FAUST, replacing CaDeX's deformation module with the proposed one yields IoU of 78.1%, CD of 0.064, and correspondence error of 0.093, matching or slightly exceeding the best prior method CaDeX(NVP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HyperDiffusion, the primary 4D generation baseline, the proposed method wins on three of four metrics: MMD improves from 19.6 to 16.4, COV jumps from 41.8% to 52.8% (indicating substantially greater diversity), and rendering time drops from 40.4 s to 8.1 s. The only metric where HyperDiffusion retains an edge is 1-NNA (64.1% vs. 61.9%), suggesting slightly better distributional similarity in nearest-neighbor terms, though the gap is modest. In the 4D point cloud completion experiment on D-FAUST, the proposed deformation module plugged into CaDeX achieves IoU of 78.1% and correspondence error of 0.093, tying CaDeX(NVP) on IoU and beating it on correspondence error (0.093 vs. 0.095), while also outperforming O-Flow (IoU 72.3%), LPDC (IoU 76.2%), and all other baselines. The conditional generation variant (point-cloud-conditioned) further improves MMD to 15.6 and COV to 53.2% over the unconditional setting, demonstrating that the disentangled shape/motion latent space supports effective conditioning without sacrificing generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior 4D generation work (HyperDiffusion) that directly generates 4D occupancy field weights, instead proposing a disentangled architecture where shape is encoded via a conditional SDF and motion is encoded via a novel neural deformation representation based on linear blend skinning (LBS). The deformation module draws on classical skeletal animation concepts (skinning weights, per-part rigid transformations) but repurposes them for a generative setting, contrasting with prior 4D representations (OFlow, LPDC, CaDeX) that model per-point trajectories or correspondences. The diffusion pipeline follows the latent diffusion paradigm, operating on concatenated shape and motion latent vectors extracted by the proposed representation, with the DALLE-2 architecture handling the 1D diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentangled shape/motion latent space enables practical applications like motion retargeting and conditional generation that were infeasible with monolithic 4D occupancy field representations, while the per-vertex animation approach yields a 5× rendering speedup and substantially better temporal consistency. However, the reliance on LBS introduces known artifacts (candy wrapper effect) and limits expressiveness for non-rigid deformations, and the evaluation is constrained to a small dataset of only 59 shape categories. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse 4D datasets, whether more advanced skinning techniques (e.g., dual quaternion blending) can close the gap on non-rigid deformations, and whether the disentangled latent space supports richer conditioning modalities (text, video) as the authors speculate.
