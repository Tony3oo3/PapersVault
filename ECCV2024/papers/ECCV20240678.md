## Problem

Existing self-supervised skeleton representation methods are limited: contrastive learning captures only discriminative information between augmented views and is sensitive to augmentation design, while reconstruction-based methods overfit to low-level appearance details. Diffusion models offer a more general generative objective but have not been explored for skeleton representation learning, where spatial sparsity and temporal redundancy make naive diffusion retain too much irrelevant information without forming a tight representation bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240678/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D skeleton sequences of shape T₀×V×3 (300 frames interpolated to 120, 25 joints, 3 coordinates per joint), which are patchified along the temporal axis with patch length 4 into T_p×V tokens of dimension C=256. The encoder outputs a global pooled representation and local per-token representations used for downstream classification; the diffusion decoder outputs per-timestep noise predictions that, when iteratively denoised, yield a full skeleton sequence. The same pre-trained decoder is repurposed for one-step denoising to generate label-preserving augmented samples during semi-supervised fine-tuning.

## Method

MacDiff jointly trains a Transformer semantic encoder and a Transformer diffusion decoder: the encoder processes randomly masked (90% ratio) patchified skeleton tokens to produce a compact latent z, while the decoder predicts the noise ε in a noisy view x_t conditioned on z via Adaptive Layer Norm (AdaLN) with 0.1 dropout on z to enable unconditional generation. The key insight is that the conditional diffusion objective I(X; Z, X_t) decomposes into a contrastive term I(X_t; Z) that aligns the masked-view representation with the noisy view, plus a complementary reconstruction term I(X; Z|X_t) that forces the representation to encode task-relevant information beyond what the noisy view already contains. An inverse-cosine noise schedule is adopted to concentrate noise at medium levels, and the unmasked local tokens are unshuffled and concatenated with the global representation (filling masked positions) to mitigate over-smoothing. Theoretically, the paper proves via mutual information analysis that this objective strictly improves upon contrastive learning by also optimizing the complementary information term, yielding a tighter bound on downstream Bayes error.

## Results

On the linear evaluation protocol (frozen encoder + linear probe), MacDiff achieves 86.4% / 91.0% on NTU 60 xsub/xview, 79.4% / 80.2% on NTU 120 xsub/xset, and 92.8% on PKUMMD I, setting new state-of-the-art across all benchmarks with a single joint stream. In supervised fine-tuning, MacDiff reaches 92.7% / 97.3% on NTU 60 xsub/xview, matching MAMP (93.1% / 97.5%) and surpassing all unified models. With diffusion-based data augmentation in the semi-supervised 1% setting, MacDiff achieves 72.0% on NTU 60 xsub, outperforming MAMP by 6.0% and the best contrastive baseline by 16.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MacDiff outperforms the strongest reconstruction baseline MAMP by 1.5% on NTU 60 xsub (86.4 vs 84.9), 1.9% on xview (91.0 vs 89.1), 0.8% on NTU 120 xsub, 1.1% on xset, and 0.6% on PKUMMD I, all with a single joint stream versus MAMP's joint stream. Against the best multi-stream contrastive method 3s-ActCLR (84.3% xsub), MacDiff gains 2.1% using only one stream, and beats 3s-SkeleMixCLR (82.7%) by 3.7%. In fine-tuning, MacDiff (92.7% xsub) is marginally behind MAMP (93.1%) but surpasses all unified models including MotionBERT (93.0%) and UPS (92.6%). The largest gains appear in semi-supervised settings: with diffusion-based augmentation at 1% labels, MacDiff reaches 72.0% on NTU 60 xsub versus MAMP's 66.0% (+6.0%) and 3s-CMD's 55.6% (+16.4%). For motion reconstruction, MacDiff achieves the lowest MPJPE (0.033 at 10 frames, 0.147 at 40 frames) compared to DDIM (0.041, 0.205) and SkeletonMAE (0.191, 0.255), while for unconditional motion generation it matches DDIM and MDM in FID/KID.

## Relation to Prior Work

MacDiff sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) self-supervised skeleton representation learning, where prior work is dominated by contrastive learning (CrosSCLR, ActCLR, MAMP) and masked autoencoders (SkeletonMAE, MAMP); (2) diffusion models for human motion, which have been applied to text-guided generation (MDM) and inpainting (DDIM) but not to representation learning; and (3) bottleneck diffusion for representation learning (SODA), which MacDiff adapts to the skeleton domain with a masked-encoder conditioning mechanism. The key departure is from both contrastive-only and reconstruction-only paradigms: MacDiff uses a conditional diffusion decoder guided by a masked encoder, and provides a mutual-information analysis showing its objective strictly subsumes the contrastive learning objective while adding a complementary reconstruction term. The AdaLN conditioning follows Peebles & Xie (2023), and the inverse-cosine noise schedule is borrowed from SODA (Hudson et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that diffusion-based generative pretraining can serve as a strictly stronger self-supervised objective than contrastive learning for skeleton data, with a clean information-theoretic proof (Theorem 1) linking the training objective to Bayes error rate bounds. The practical diffusion-based data augmentation for semi-supervised fine-tuning (6.4% gain at 1% labeled data) addresses a real deployment bottleneck for skeleton-based systems. However, the generative quality of MacDiff is only comparable to (not better than) dedicated diffusion models like MDM, and the representation gains over the strongest baseline MAMP are modest (1–2% in most protocols), leaving open whether the theoretical advantage translates to larger margins at scale or on more diverse action vocabularies. Whether the masked-conditional-diffusion recipe generalizes beyond 25-joint, 300-frame skeleton sequences to other spatio-temporal modalities (e.g., point clouds, video) remains unexplored.
