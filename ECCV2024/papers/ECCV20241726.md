## Problem

Existing aerial surveillance datasets suffer from two critical limitations: a bias toward ideal lighting conditions that fails to represent nighttime driving, and a skewed vehicle-type distribution that underrepresents larger vehicles such as trucks and semi-trailers. These gaps compromise the safety and efficiency of autonomous systems and hinder accurate traffic flow modeling in real-world, low-visibility environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241726/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized sRGB (3840×2160, 8-bit JPEG) and thermal infrared (1280×1024, 16-bit R-JPEG preserving raw temperature data) image pairs captured at 30 fps from a DJI Mavic 3T drone. Annotations are oriented bounding boxes defined by four corner coordinates in DOTA-v2 format, with semi-trailers uniquely split into separate tractor and trailer components. For 3D mapping, the toolkit consumes image pixel coordinates, camera intrinsics K, and a reference point on the Digital Surface Model to output geographic (WGS84) coordinates via the PNP algorithm, alongside HD-MAP files in xord format.

## Method

The core contribution is an automated annotation pipeline that chains YOLOv8 horizontal bounding box detection, MobileSAM-based vehicle segmentation, and an OBB calculator to produce oriented bounding boxes from thermal infrared images, reducing manual labeling cost. For 3D coordinate recovery, the toolkit replaces the affine transformations used in prior aerial datasets (e.g., NGSIM, Citysim) with a Perspective-n-Point (PNP) solver that minimizes reprojection error, yielding more accurate physical coordinates by accounting for parallax. HD-MAPs are constructed from RTK-acquired multi-view point clouds processed in RoadRunner, aligned to WGS84. A pre-trained YOLOv8-OBB detector fine-tuned on 2,200 thermally annotated images is provided, and SOTA OBB detectors (LSKNet-S, RTMDet-R-I, YOLOv8-OBB) are benchmarked on the dataset, showing that colorized infrared inputs consistently outperform sRGB by 12–16 AP50 points in nighttime conditions.

## Results

On the TrafficNight dataset (75/25 train/test split), all three SOTA OBB detectors show a dramatic performance gap between sRGB and colorized infrared (cIF) inputs. LSKNet-S* achieves the best AP50 of 94.46 and AP75 of 86.35 with cIF, compared to 79.45/53.44 with sRGB — a ~15-point AP50 improvement. RTMDet-R-I follows with 93.10/85.69 (cIF) vs 79.33/52.25 (sRGB), and YoloV8-OBB reaches 91.10/85.32 (cIF) vs 77.83/51.71 (sRGB). The consistent ~15-point AP50 and ~33-point AP75 gains from cIF confirm that thermal infrared is far more reliable for nighttime vehicle detection than visible-light imagery.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three SOTA oriented bounding box detectors — LSKNet-S*, RTMDet-R-I, and YoloV8-OBB — on the newly introduced TrafficNight dataset to validate its utility as a nighttime benchmark. LSKNet-S* consistently outperforms the other two methods by 1.3–3.4 AP50 points in both modalities, with the largest margin (3.36 AP50) in the cIF setting. RTMDet-R-I ranks second, edging out YoloV8-OBB by 2.0 AP50 (cIF) and 1.5 AP50 (sRGB). The most striking comparison, however, is across input modalities rather than across methods: every detector gains roughly 15 AP50 and 33 AP75 points when switching from sRGB to cIF, underscoring that thermal infrared is the dominant signal for nighttime aerial vehicle detection. The authors also note that annotations derived from colorized infrared images are more accurate than purely human-annotated sRGB labels in low-light conditions, further justifying the cIF-based labeling pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrafficNight positions itself against existing aerial vehicle datasets (B3D, Citysim, INO, LLVIP) that the authors identify as biased toward ideal lighting and underrepresenting large vehicles like semi-trailers. Rather than proposing a new detection architecture, the paper repurposes established components—YOLOv8-OBB for detection, MobileSAM for segmentation, and the PNP algorithm for 2D-to-3D coordinate conversion—into an automated annotation pipeline and a pre-trained toolkit. The conceptual departure is the deliberate integration of thermal infrared as a co-equal modality alongside sRGB, paired with HD-MAPs and articulated-vehicle (tractor/trailer) annotations, none of which prior aerial benchmarks provide simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a concrete gap for nighttime aerial surveillance and Car Following Model calibration, where prior data lacked both low-light coverage and balanced representation of semi-trailers. However, the scope is narrow: seven scenes collected in a single tropical city (Shenzhen), 2,200 image pairs, and the authors themselves flag thermal-sensor noise under extreme weather and the absence of colder-region or high-speed data as open limitations. The PNP-based 3D mapping toolkit is a practical improvement over the affine transformations used in NGSIM and Citysim, but the paper does not quantify the accuracy gain, leaving its practical advantage somewhat unverified.
