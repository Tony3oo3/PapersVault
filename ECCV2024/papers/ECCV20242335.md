## Problem

Underwater 6D pose estimation for UUVs is severely hampered by a large sim-to-real gap caused by degraded illumination and blurring, the infeasibility of depth sensors underwater, and the high cost of real pose annotations. Existing self-supervised methods either rely on depth data unavailable underwater or enforce only image-level alignment in a teacher-student setup, neglecting feature-level consistency that is critical for semantic and geometric robustness in the challenging underwater domain.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (cropped around the initial pose and resized to 256×256) together with the 3D CAD model of the target object, from which N=4 synthetic renderings are generated around the coarse initial pose P₀. The output is a refined 6D pose (rotation as a continuous 6D representation plus translation) obtained through iterative shape-constrained flow optimization. No depth, multi-view, or additional modality data is required at inference time.

## Method

FAFA is a two-stage framework. In pre-training, an FFT-based augmentation strategy decomposes synthetic images into amplitude and phase spectra, then performs amplitude dropout (to encourage domain-invariant feature learning) and amplitude mixing with real-image spectra (to inject target-domain style priors) before reconstructing augmented training images. In the self-supervised refinement stage, a teacher-student architecture with a flow-aided pose estimator (feature encoder + RAFT-based flow regressor + pose regressor) enforces multi-level consistencies: image-level losses (photometric consistency via Census loss, warped-mask alignment, and pseudo-flow supervision) and a novel feature-level loss that aligns encoder features warped by the student flow with synthetic features. The shape-constrained flow is computed by projecting 3D mesh points under the current pose estimate, creating a closed-loop iterative refinement of both flow and pose in an end-to-end differentiable pipeline.

## Results

On ROV6D, FAFA achieves 94.03% ADD-S (0.05d), 96.96% ADD-S (0.1d), 87.87% at 5°, and 86.71% at 5 cm, yielding a mean score of 91.39%. On DeepURL, FAFA reaches 76.56% ADD (0.05d), 97.64% ADD (0.1d), 68.70% at 5°, and 50.92% at 5 cm (mean 73.46%). Notably, FAFA's self-supervised ADD-S 0.1d score of 96.96% on ROV6D even surpasses the fully supervised upper bound (96.21%), demonstrating that the self-supervised refinement is highly effective in the underwater domain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAFA outperforms the previous state-of-the-art self-supervised method PFC [10] by 6.53 points on the 5° metric (87.87% vs. 81.34%) and 14.61 points on the 5 cm metric (86.71% vs. 72.10%) on ROV6D, with the gap driven primarily by the added feature-level alignment. Against the flow-based SCFlow [11], FAFA gains 14.53 points on 5° and 15.25 points on 5 cm, while surpassing the dense-correspondence methods GDRN [50] and CDPN [26] by 30.75 and 34.66 points respectively on 5°. The refinement-based DeepIM [25] performs poorly in underwater conditions (12.96% at 5°), highlighting the difficulty of render-and-compare in degraded scenes. On DeepURL, FAFA improves over PFC by 7.10 points on 5° (68.70% vs. 61.60%) and 6.82 points on 5 cm (50.92% vs. 44.10%), while also exceeding the CycleGAN-based baselines (DeepURL [20], GDRN, DeepIM, SCFlow) by 21–34 points on 5°. The self-supervised FAFA results approach or even exceed the fully supervised upper bound (OURS UB), confirming that the FFT augmentation and multi-level flow-aided consistency effectively close the sim-to-real gap without any real pose annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAFA builds directly on SCFlow's shape-constraint recurrent flow architecture for iterative pose refinement and extends PFC's teacher-student self-supervised framework by introducing feature-level alignment alongside the existing image-level photometric and flow consistency losses. The FFT-based data augmentation (amplitude mix and dropout) repurposes Fourier domain adaptation ideas from semantic segmentation (FDA, phase-consistent ecological DA) into the pose estimation setting, where frequency-domain methods had been largely unexplored. The key conceptual departure from PFC is treating the network's internal feature space as a supervision target rather than a black box, and leveraging unlabeled real images during pre-training to inject target-domain style priors into synthetic data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Underwater 6D pose estimation is practically important for UUV tracking, archaeology, and marine resource operations, yet remains difficult because depth sensors fail underwater and real annotations are prohibitively expensive. FAFA's elimination of both depth and real pose labels, combined with its strong margins over PFC (e.g., 5°: 87.87% vs 81.34% on ROV6D), makes it a viable pipeline for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the FFT augmentation generalizes beyond the two UUV objects tested, whether the approach extends to multi-object or category-level underwater pose estimation, and how sensitive the method is to the quality of the initial pose P₀ from the pre-trained network.
