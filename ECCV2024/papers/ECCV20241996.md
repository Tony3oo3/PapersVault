## Problem

Standard self-supervised visual learning methods passively observe images and do not exploit the action information available when humans actively manipulate or move around objects. Prior work shows that active interaction with objects improves human object recognition, yet no SSL method has incorporated the performed action to structure visual representations. This paper asks whether aligning learned action representations with visual changes can yield better category-generalizing embeddings than purely invariance-based or temporal SSL.

## Input / Output

Each training sample is a triplet (x_t, x_t', a_{t,t'}): two images randomly sampled from the same video clip of one object, plus the 3D action parameters describing the transformation between them. The action encoding is dataset-specific — RT4K uses sine/cosine of the yaw rotation angle, CO3D uses a quaternion and translation vector, and MVImgNet additionally appends a binary camera-orientation flag. The model outputs a visual embedding from a ResNet backbone and a separate action embedding from a dedicated encoder; downstream, a linear probe on the frozen visual embedding predicts category labels.

## Method

AA-SSL augments a standard contrastive SSL loss (SimCLR or VICReg) with a second action-alignment loss. The first action embedding is produced by an action encoder g¹ that maps the raw action parameters; the second is produced by an action projection head g² that takes the concatenation of the two visual representations h_t and h_{t'}. Minimizing the contrastive distance between these two action embeddings forces the visual backbone to encode viewpoint-structured information that is consistent with the performed action, rather than collapsing all views of an object into a single invariant point. This differs from inverse models (which predict raw action values) and equivariant models (which predict the next visual representation) because it learns a shared, abstract action representation space. The total loss is L(g(h_t), g(h_{t'})) + λ·L(g¹(a_{t,t'}), g²(h_t, h_{t'})), and the framework plugs directly into SimCLR, VICReg, and BYOL without modifying their core architectures.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (linear-probe category accuracy), AA-SimCLR achieves 74.8% on RT4K, 81.3% on CO3D, 42.8% on MVImgNet-T, 78.4% on MVImgNet-S, and 96.1% on MVImgNet-F, consistently outperforming all baselines except a marginal 0.3-point deficit to SimCLR-TT on the largest dataset (MVImgNet-F). The gains are most pronounced on smaller datasets: +5.2 points over SimCLR on MVImgNet-S and +2.7 points on MVImgNet-T. On transfer learning (Table 2), AA-SimCLR pre-trained on MVImgNet-F reaches 44.3% on ImageNet-1K and 66.0% on ImageNet-100, surpassing the strongest baseline (SimCLR-CIPER) by 0.8 and 0.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AA-SimCLR outperforms the standard SimCLR baseline on every dataset, with the largest margins on MVImgNet-S (+5.2 pts) and RT4K (+3.7 pts). Against the temporal SSL variant SimCLR-TT, AA-SimCLR wins on four of five datasets (e.g., 74.8 vs 72.3 on RT4K, 42.8 vs 39.0 on MVImgNet-T) but trails by 0.3 points on the very large MVImgNet-F (96.1 vs 96.4), where the sheer volume of objects saturates performance. The inverse-model baseline SimCLR-CIPER (predicting raw action parameters) is consistently beaten, with gaps of 2.5–3.5 points on smaller datasets, suggesting that learning a latent action representation is more effective than regressing the raw action. The equivariant baseline SimCLR-EquiMod (predicting the next visual embedding) is the weakest comparator, losing by 4–6 points across datasets. On transfer learning to ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-100, AA-SimCLR leads all baselines, though it is marginally outperformed by SimCLR-CIPER on Tiny-ImageNet (38.0 vs 38.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the augmentation-based SSL framework (SimCLR, VICReg) and the temporal SSL line (SSLTT) by introducing a complementary action-aware loss that aligns a learned action embedding with the visual representations of two temporally related views. It departs from prior inverse-model approaches (CIPER) that predict raw action parameters and equivariant approaches (EquiMod) that predict next-frame representations, instead learning a joint action-visual embedding space where the action representation is derived from both the raw action encoding and the concatenated image features. The RT4K dataset, built with ThreeDWorld simulations of 3,500 Toys4k objects, provides controlled 360° rotations that enable systematic study of viewpoint effects, complementing the egocentric-motion datasets CO3D and MVImgNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating action information during SSL yields representations with a more favorable viewpoint invariance/sensitivity trade-off for categorization, and that this benefit persists even when standard data augmentations are removed—a practical robustness property not shared by pure invariance-based methods. The finding that full temporal invariance (as in SSLTT) can actually hurt category recognition by erasing view-specific discriminative features (e.g., a cup's handle) challenges the prevailing assumption that more invariance is always better. Open questions include whether active exploration strategies (humans deliberately rotate objects toward informative views) can further improve learning, whether the approach extends to more complex multi-object or deformable-object interactions, and whether the benefits persist at ImageNet scale with real-world cluttered scenes.
