## Problem

Existing slide representation learning methods rely on intra-modal self-supervised learning, aligning two augmented views of the same H&E slide, which limits the training signal to visual invariances within a single stain. This paper asks whether the rich biological information encoded in co-registered multi-stain tissue (H&E plus immunohistochemistry or special stains) can serve as a stronger, task-agnostic cross-modal training signal for learning universal slide representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240863/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted 512-dimensional patch embeddings (from 256×256-pixel tiles) of co-registered whole-slide images across multiple stains per case (e.g., H&E + ER/PR/HER2/KI67 for breast, or H&E + PAS/Jones/Trichrome for kidney). A single stain-agnostic network produces one 512-dimensional slide embedding per stain, which can then be used for downstream classification, survival prediction, or IHC quantification without retraining the encoder.

## Method

MADELEINE encodes patch embeddings via a multi-head (4-head) attention-based MIL with a learnable 32-dimensional stain-specific token, producing stain-specific slide embeddings using the same shared network for all stains. Training uses a dual global-local cross-stain alignment objective: a global InfoNCE contrastive loss aligns slide embeddings of the same case across stains, while a local Graph Optimal Transport (GOT) loss matches patch-embedding distributions across stains via Wasserstein distance on nodes and Gromov-Wasserstein distance on graph edges, capturing fine-grained morphological correspondences. The patch encoder is CONCH, a visual-language model pretrained on 1M histology image-caption pairs spanning multiple stains, chosen to be stain-agnostic. An optional intra-modal contrastive loss (INTRA) can be added, but ablations show the cross-modal objectives are the primary drivers of performance.

## Results

On few-shot morphological subtyping (Table 1), MADELEINE-SE achieves 93.1% macro-AUC on TCGA Breast and 95.3% on BWH Breast at k=10 with linear probing, outperforming the best MIL baseline (ABMIL: 90.4% and 93.0%) and the best intra-modal SSL baseline (GigaSSL: 82.8% on TCGA). In survival prediction on TCGA Breast, MADELEINE reaches a 0.71 c-index, surpassing all slide-level baselines. For molecular subtyping, MADELEINE-SE yields an +11.4% AUC gain over the best MIL method on HER2 prediction in AIDPATH, and the method outperforms all baselines across 13/13 evaluated tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MADELEINE-SE outperforms all seven baselines across every k-shot setting and both evaluation protocols (linear probing and prototyping). Against the strongest MIL baseline ABMIL [26], MADELEINE-SE gains +2.7% AUC on TCGA Breast (k=10, linear probing: 93.1 vs 90.4) and +2.3% on BWH Breast (95.3 vs 93.0). TransMIL [58] performs worst among MIL methods (72.1% at k=10 on TCGA), likely due to overfitting from its patch-to-patch self-attention. Among intra-modal SSL methods, GigaSSL [39] is the strongest (82.8% at k=10 on TCGA) but still trails MADELEINE-SE by 10.3%; HIPT [12] and GigaPath [77] lag further behind at 77.5% and 75.4% respectively. Notably, the simple MEAN pooling baseline (86.6%) outperforms several SSL methods (INTRA, HIPT, GigaSSL), underscoring the difficulty of intra-modal slide-level pretraining. In survival prediction, MADELEINE achieves 0.71 c-index versus 0.69 for INTRA and lower for other baselines. For molecular subtyping on AIDPATH, MADELEINE-SE surpasses the best MIL by +11.4% AUC on HER2 prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

MADELEINE extends the hierarchical slide representation learning paradigm (patch encoder → MIL aggregation → slide embedding) established by HIPT, GigaSSL, and GigaPath, but departs fundamentally from their intra-modal pretraining by treating different histological stains (ER, PR, HER2, KI67, PAS, Jones, Trichrome) as distinct modalities rather than augmentations of a single H&E view. The global alignment objective draws on the InfoNCE contrastive framework from visual-language pretraining (CLIP), while the local patch-level alignment repurposes the Graph Optimal Transport framework from cross-domain alignment (Chen et al., ICML 2020) and VolTA (Pramanick et al., 2022) to enforce structural correspondences between stain-specific patch graphs. The multi-head attention MIL extends ABMIL (Ilse et al., 2018) by allowing each head to specialize on different morphological regions, and the stain-agnostic patch encoder leverages CONCH, a visual-language model pretrained on diverse histology stains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that clinically available multistain data provides a substantially richer and more biologically grounded training signal than intra-modal augmentations, consistently outperforming all baselines across 21 downstream tasks spanning morphological subtyping, molecular prediction, survival, and IHC quantification. A notable open question is the availability of paired multistain data: while common in breast cancer and kidney transplant workflows, many clinical settings may lack such pairings, limiting immediate applicability. The authors also identify promising extensions to spatial transcriptomics, immunofluorescence, and mass spectrometry as additional spatial modalities, suggesting the multistain-as-multimodal-view paradigm could generalize well beyond the two organs studied. The finding that simple mean pooling with a strong patch encoder often rivals or exceeds dedicated intra-modal slide SSL methods raises questions about the value of complex slide-level pretraining objectives when patch-level representations are already powerful.
