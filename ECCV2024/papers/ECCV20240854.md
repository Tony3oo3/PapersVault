## Problem

While image customization for text-to-image diffusion models is well-studied, the temporal counterpart—learning motion from a single reference video and transferring it to new subjects and scenes—remains largely unexplored. Existing video editing methods that leverage reference videos rigidly replicate the original frame structure and layout, offering no variability in motion intensity, subject position, camera view, or quantity of subjects. This paper addresses the challenge of one-shot video motion customization that produces both faithful motion transfer and natural temporal diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240854/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference video (2 seconds, 8 FPS, 256×256) used exclusively during training, and at inference requires only a text prompt describing new subjects and scenes. The output is a generated video animated by the learned motion signature, with diversity in motion intensities, positions, and camera perspectives across different random noise seeds. The plug-and-play design means the reference video is never needed at inference time, and multiple T-LoRA modules can be stacked to combine motions from different source videos.

## Method

The core contribution is Temporal LoRA (T-LoRA), which applies low-rank residual weights exclusively to the temporal cross-frame attention layers of a pre-trained T2V diffusion model (ModelScope), concentrating learning on motion dynamics while discarding spatial appearance. To prevent spatial information from leaking into T-LoRA during single-video training, the paper introduces Appearance Absorbers—a general class of modules (Spatial LoRA or Textual Inversion) trained in a first stage on unordered video frames with all temporal layers bypassed, thereby absorbing static spatial signals. A second stage then trains T-LoRA on the full reference video with the appearance absorber loaded in frozen state, so the temporal module focuses on motion. At inference, the appearance absorber is removed and only T-LoRA is loaded, enabling the model to generate novel videos with the customized motion under arbitrary text prompts. The staged, modular pipeline supports downstream extensions including multiple motion combination, video appearance customization via additional spatial LoRAs, and precise frame-wise editing via DDIM inverted latents.

## Results

On the LOVEU-TGVE-2023 benchmark (53 videos), the best variant (TextInv AA) achieves a CLIPScore of 32.632 for text alignment and 0.160 LPIPS for temporal consistency, outperforming all baselines on both metrics. The Dual AA variant attains the highest diversity score (0.631) among all motion-customization methods, approaching the base ModelScope's 0.636. In the human user study (20 evaluators, 153–156 valid ratings per method), the proposed method with dual AA leads on both motion fidelity (3.72/5) and motion diversity (3.72/5), surpassing the closest competitor MotionDirector (3.33 and 3.50 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: the base ModelScope T2V model, Tune-A-Video, Video-P2P, and the concurrent MotionDirector. On text alignment (CLIPScore), the proposed method with TextInv AA (32.632) beats MotionDirector (32.500) by 0.13 and the base ModelScope (31.705) by 0.93; Tune-A-Video and Video-P2P score lowest (31.149 and 31.001) because they rely on DDIM-inverted latents that constrain output to the reference frame structure. On temporal consistency (LPIPS, lower is better), the proposed method achieves 0.160–0.164, marginally better than MotionDirector (0.163) and Video-P2P (0.162), while Tune-A-Video without DDIM degrades to 0.206. On diversity, the base ModelScope leads (0.636) as an unconstrained generator, but among motion-customization methods the Dual AA variant (0.631) is best, edging out MotionDirector (0.606) and far exceeding Tune-A-Video w/o DDIM (0.348) and Video-P2P w/o DDIM (0.469). In the user study, the proposed method (3.72/5 on both fidelity and diversity) clearly outperforms MotionDirector (3.33/3.50) and all other baselines, confirming that the staged T-LoRA + Appearance Absorber pipeline produces more faithful and varied motion transfers than joint-training or DDIM-constrained alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts LoRA from its origins in LLM fine-tuning and T2I image customization to the temporal attention layers of a T2V diffusion model (ModelScope), creating Temporal LoRA (T-LoRA) as a motion-specific parameter-efficient module. It departs from T2I-based video editing methods (Tune-A-Video, Video-P2P) that rigidly replicate reference frame structure via DDIM inversion, and from concurrent motion customization work (MotionDirector, VMC, DreamVideo) that either requires diverse training sets, early stopping heuristics, or joint spatial-temporal tuning. The key conceptual departure is the staged training pipeline with Appearance Absorbers (S-LoRA or Textual Inversion) that explicitly decouple spatial identity from temporal dynamics before motion learning, enabling one-shot customization from a single reference video with plug-and-play inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, staged architecture enables practical composition—stacking multiple T-LoRAs for combined motions, pairing T-LoRA with existing S-LoRA for simultaneous appearance and motion control, or reusing third-party Dreambooth models as appearance absorbers—making it a flexible building block for video generation pipelines. However, experiments are limited to 256×256 resolution, 8 FPS, and 2-second clips, leaving open whether the spatial-temporal disentanglement strategy scales to longer, higher-resolution videos or more complex multi-object motions. The reliance on a single base model (ModelScope) and the modest quantitative margins over concurrent MotionDirector suggest the approach's generality across T2V architectures remains to be validated.
