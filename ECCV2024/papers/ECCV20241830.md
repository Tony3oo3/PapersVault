## Problem

State-of-the-art industrial anomaly detection methods already achieve >99% AUROC on MVTec-AD, making it an insufficient benchmark for real-world inspection problems where defects are tiny, visually similar to the object, or involve missing material. Additionally, no standardized metric exists to quantify how difficult a given defect detection task is before committing GPU-hours to training and evaluating multiple methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241830/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The AD3 score consumes pixel-wise defect mask annotations overlaid on high-resolution RGB images (each object captured from five perspectives) and produces a single scalar difficulty value in [0, 1]. The intermediate quantities are the relative defect area A_r (defect pixels / image pixels) and the SiH score (normalized bin-wise histogram difference across R, G, B channels between defect and object regions). The VIADUCT dataset itself provides 10,986 images across 49 categories with 7,628 individually annotated defects spanning 135 defect classes.

## Method

The AD3 score combines two difficulty factors via a modified harmonic mean: AD3 = 2·SiH·√A_r / (SiH + √A_r). The SiH component measures visual similarity by computing per-channel RGB histograms for the defect region and the surrounding object, performing bin-wise subtraction, normalizing by the maximum bin value, and averaging over three channels (SiH=0 means identical distributions, SiH=1 means maximally different). The square root on A_r prevents the typically very small relative area from disproportionately suppressing the score. The harmonic-mean structure ensures AD3→0 when either the defect has no area or is visually indistinguishable from the object. The paper validates the score by showing its correlation with IoU (R²=0.28) substantially exceeds that of SiH alone (R²=0.02) or A_r alone (R²=0.14) across PatchCore, EfficientAD, and MSFlow, and demonstrates that VIADUCT contains the lowest AD3 scores among four compared datasets.

## Results

On the VIADUCT dataset, the best-performing methods achieve average image-wise AUROC of 85.9% (PatchCore), 85.4% (MSFlow), and 79.2% (EfficientAD), far below the >99% AUROC these methods achieve on MVTec-AD. The AD3 score analysis shows VIADUCT contains the most difficult defects, with a minimum AD3 of 0.002 (vs. 0.03 for MVTec-AD) and the widest range of relative defect areas [6.1×10⁻⁶; 0.46]. The AD3 score correlates with per-image IoU of detection methods (R²=0.28 for PatchCore), significantly outperforming SiH alone (R²=0.02) or relative defect area alone (R²=0.14) as a difficulty predictor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks seven IAD methods (EfficientAD, PatchCore, MSFlow, FastFlow, DRAEM, CFA, MemSeg) on the new VIADUCT dataset. MSFlow and PatchCore are the strongest performers, with MSFlow achieving perfect 1.000 AUROC on Redon needle and 0.992 on Paperclip, while PatchCore leads on Raspberry (0.989) and Air muffler large (0.998). EfficientAD is competitive but trails on several categories (e.g., 0.512 on Encoder vs. PatchCore's 0.709). FastFlow, DRAEM, CFA, and MemSeg consistently underperform, with DRAEM and CFA frequently scoring near or below 0.60 on image-wise AUROC. Critically, all methods show a dramatic drop from their >99% MVTec-AD scores to 79–86% average on VIADUCT, with 13–21 categories scoring below 80% AUROC depending on the method. The AD3 score validates this difficulty: categories with the lowest AD3 (Device box 0.038, Saw blade 0.041, Redon needle 0.054) are precisely where methods struggle most, confirming AD3 as a useful a-priori difficulty estimator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MVTec-AD lineage (Bergmann et al., 2019) by introducing a substantially larger and more difficult multisector dataset (VIADUCT) that addresses three specific failure modes of current IAD methods: very small defects, visually similar defects, and missing-material anomalies. The AD3 score is a novel a-priori difficulty metric that repurposes simple histogram-based color similarity (SiH) and relative defect area into a harmonic-mean composite, deliberately avoiding the deep feature extraction that would make it as complex as the detection methods it aims to pre-screen. The benchmark draws on the same seven method families (reconstruction, representation, flow, distribution-based) previously evaluated on MVTec-AD, positioning VIADUCT as the natural next-stage stress test for those approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIADUCT is the largest image-based IAD dataset to date (49 categories, 10,986 images, 135 defect classes) and the first to systematically include defects that are both tiny and visually inconspicuous, exposing a clear performance gap (79–86% image AUROC vs. 99%+ on MVTec-AD) that motivates further method development. The AD3 score offers a cheap, GPU-free way to triage inspection problems before committing to expensive benchmarks, though the demonstrated IoU correlation (R² ≈ 0.28) is moderate and would benefit from validation across more methods and datasets. Open questions include whether the histogram-based SiH generalizes to texture-rich or multi-spectral scenarios, and whether the AD3–performance relationship holds for next-generation methods that may exploit different visual cues.
