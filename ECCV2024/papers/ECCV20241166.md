## Problem

Existing video outpainting methods are constrained to fixed mask types and resolutions, and critically fail on out-domain videos where the source content differs from training data. The root cause is that these methods do not capture the intrinsic data-specific patterns (motion, style, content) present in the source video itself, leading to complete generation failure (e.g., blurred corners) on unfamiliar inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a source video of arbitrary resolution, length, and aspect ratio, along with a spatial mask of arbitrary type (not limited to square or fixed-ratio masks) and a text prompt auto-generated via BLIP. It produces a spatially expanded video where the known regions are preserved exactly and the masked regions are filled with temporally and spatially consistent content. The base model operates at 512×512 latent resolution with 16-frame clips, and long videos are handled by sampling short clips for adaptation and using temporal co-denoising for outpainting.

## Method

MOTIA is a two-phase diffusion pipeline built on Stable Diffusion v1-5 with network inflation (2D→pseudo-3D convolutions), a ControlNet for mask conditioning, and pre-trained motion modules for temporal priors. Phase 1 (input-specific adaptation) performs pseudo outpainting on the source video itself: random edge masks are applied, and LoRA adapters (rank 16, ~7.5M params, 0.42% overhead) are trained for 1000 steps to learn data-specific patterns while bridging the gap between standard generation and outpainting. Phase 2 (pattern-aware outpainting) introduces two inference strategies: spatial-aware insertion, where adapter weights decay exponentially with distance from known regions (balancing learned patterns near edges with the generative prior far away), and noise regret, which periodically re-noises and re-denoises at early timesteps to resolve denoising direction conflicts between known and unknown regions. This parameter-efficient approach requires no task-specific large-scale training and generalizes to arbitrary mask shapes, resolutions, and out-domain content.

## Results

On DAVIS (mask ratio 0.66), MOTIA achieves PSNR 20.36, SSIM 0.7578, LPIPS 0.1595, and FVD 286.3, outperforming the strongest baseline M3DDM (PSNR 20.26, SSIM 0.7082, LPIPS 0.2026, FVD 300.0). On YouTube-VOS, MOTIA reaches PSNR 20.25, SSIM 0.7636, LPIPS 0.1727, and FVD 58.99, improving over M3DDM by 4.43% in SSIM, 6.85% in LPIPS, and 11.45% in FVD. The most striking gain is in LPIPS on DAVIS (21.27% reduction), indicating substantially better perceptual quality of the outpainted regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOTIA is compared against three baselines: the optical-flow-based VideoOutpainting [5], and two diffusion-based methods SDM and M3DDM [6] from the same paper. MOTIA decisively outperforms VideoOutpainting on every metric (e.g., FVD 286.3 vs. 363.1 on DAVIS), confirming that flow-warping approaches remain far inferior to diffusion-based generation. Against SDM, MOTIA leads on all four metrics on both datasets, with the largest gap in LPIPS (0.1595 vs. 0.2165 on DAVIS). The closest competitor is M3DDM, which was intensively trained on massive video data with a purpose-built 3D architecture; MOTIA still surpasses it on all metrics, most notably in SSIM (+7.00% on DAVIS, +4.43% on YouTube-VOS) and FVD (−4.57% on DAVIS, −11.45% on YouTube-VOS). In a user study with 10 raters over 50 samples, MOTIA is preferred over M3DDM in 72.6% of cases for visual quality and 57.2% for realism, further validating the quantitative gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOTIA builds on the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) as its generative backbone and operates within the DDPM denoising framework, incorporating ControlNet for mask conditioning and pre-trained motion modules for temporal priors. It departs from prior video outpainting methods (M3DDM, VideoOutpainting) that rely on large-scale task-specific training or optical flow warping by introducing input-specific adaptation: a lightweight LoRA-based pseudo-outpainting fine-tuning performed directly on the source video to capture its intrinsic data-specific patterns. For long video handling, it extends the temporal co-denoising strategy from Gen-L, and its noise regret mechanism draws on DDPM-based image inpainting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that data-specific patterns in the source video—previously neglected by methods relying solely on massive pre-training—are essential for robust out-domain video outpainting, and that a minimal 0.42% parameter increase (LoRA adapters) suffices to bridge the gap. This opens a practical path for flexible, resolution- and mask-agnostic video outpainting without task-specific large-scale training. A noted limitation is that the method can produce 'copy' artifacts when the source video contains limited information, and the approach's dependence on per-video adaptation may not scale to streaming or real-time settings. Open questions include whether the input-specific adaptation principle generalizes to other conditional generation tasks and whether the pseudo-outpainting training can be further accelerated.
