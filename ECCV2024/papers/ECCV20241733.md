## Problem

Existing registration methods rely on single-pass correspondence prediction, which can converge to local minima under large deformation, scale inconsistency, ambiguous matching (e.g., symmetry), and low overlap. No prior work has formulated correspondence estimation as an iterative denoising process within the doubly stochastic matrix space, leaving a gap in how to systematically explore and refine matching solutions beyond a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241733/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For 3D registration, the method takes source and target point clouds downsampled to superpoints via KPConv (d=432 features); for 2D-3D, it additionally consumes a ResNet+FPN image feature, DINOv2 visual features, and a DepthAnything depth map projected to a non-metric point cloud. The output is a doubly stochastic matching matrix (N×M) from which top-k correspondences are extracted, yielding either a rigid transformation (R, t) via weighted SVD or a non-rigid deformation flow via KNN interpolation. At inference, the denoising module runs 20 (3D) or 10 (2D-3D) DDIM steps with all point/image features fixed after initial extraction, so only the warped source positions change per step.

## Method

Diff-Reg formulates correspondence estimation as a denoising diffusion process in the doubly stochastic matrix space: a forward Markov chain injects Gaussian noise into the ground-truth matching matrix (projected back to the manifold via Sinkhorn iterations), while a learned reverse process iteratively denoises toward the optimal matrix. The lightweight denoising module g_θ sequentially applies Sinkhorn projection, differentiable weighted SVD (Procrustes) to compute (R,t), rigid warping of source superpoints, a 6-layer interleaved self/cross-attention transformer, and a matching-logits head, with only the warped source positions updated per denoising step. Training uses a focal loss derived from the variational lower bound for the denoising module plus standard matching and warping losses for the single-pass head, so the forward diffusion simultaneously acts as data augmentation for the feature backbone. At inference, DDIM sampling from white noise (or a reliable initial solution) enables multi-pass refinement that escapes local minima, and the feature backbone is entirely bypassed during the reverse sampling loop for speed.

## Results

On the 4DMatch benchmark, Diff-Reg with 20 reverse denoising steps achieves 88.40% NFMR and 86.41% Inlier Ratio, outperforming the best single-pass baseline RoITr (83.00% NFMR, 84.40% IR) by 5.4 and 2.0 percentage points respectively. On the more challenging 4DLoMatch (15–45% overlap), Diff-Reg(steps=20) reaches 76.23% NFMR and 67.80% IR, surpassing RoITr (69.40% NFMR, 67.60% IR). On the 2D-3D RGB-D Scenes V2 benchmark, Diff-Reg(dino/steps=10) achieves a mean Registration Recall of 87.0%, substantially exceeding the best prior method FreeReg+PnP (57.3%) by nearly 30 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-Reg(steps=20) outperforms all 3D feature-matching baselines on both 4DMatch and 4DLoMatch: it beats RoITr [66] by 5.4 pp in NFMR (88.40 vs 83.00) and 2.0 pp in IR (86.41 vs 84.40) on 4DMatch, and by 6.8 pp in NFMR (76.23 vs 69.40) on 4DLoMatch. The gap over Lepard [28] (83.60 NFMR) and GeoTR [40] (83.20 NFMR) is similarly 4.8–5.2 pp on 4DMatch. Scene-flow methods (PointPWC [55], FLOT [38]) and older feature matchers (D3Feat [4], Predator [19]) are far behind, with NFMR below 60%. In the downstream non-rigid registration evaluation (Table 2), Diff-Reg+GraphSCNet achieves the best EPE (0.041 on 4DMatch-F, 0.095 on 4DLoMatch-F) and lowest outlier ratio (8.30%, 15.50%), surpassing RoITr*+GraphSCNet (0.056, 0.118) and Lepard+GraphSCNet (0.042, 0.102). On the 2D-3D RGB-D Scenes V2 benchmark, Diff-Reg(dino/steps=10) achieves 87.0% mean Registration Recall, exceeding FreeReg+PnP [51] (57.3%), 2D3D-MATR [25] (56.4%), P2-Net [50] (38.4%), and FCGF-2D3D [11] (30.4%) by 29.7, 30.6, 48.6, and 56.6 pp respectively. Notably, even the single-pass backbone trained with diffusion augmentation (Diff-Reg(dino/backbone), 78.0% mean RR) already surpasses all prior 2D-3D methods, indicating the diffusion training samples act as effective data augmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-model-for-registration line initiated by SE(3)-space approaches (Urain et al., Jiang et al.) and overlap-prior-space methods (DiffusionPCR) by relocating the diffusion process to the doubly stochastic matching matrix space, where the forward process augments the ground-truth correspondence matrix with Gaussian noise and the reverse process learns a posterior-guided denoising trajectory. The single-pass backbone follows Lepard's repositioning transformer for 3D and 2D3D-MATR's architecture for 2D-3D, while the denoising module integrates Sinkhorn projection, differentiable weighted SVD (Procrustes), and a lightweight interleaved-attention transformer in a novel sequential pipeline. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion-based registration is treating the matching matrix itself as the diffusion latent rather than the transformation or a scalar overlap prior, which the authors argue provides a richer, many-to-one mapping to the warping space and naturally encodes one-to-one correspondence constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach offers a principled escape from the local-minima trap that plagues single-pass correspondence prediction: the reverse denoising process can initiate from white noise or any initial solution and iteratively refine toward a globally better matching, while the forward diffusion simultaneously acts as structured data augmentation that improves the feature backbone even at zero denoising steps. The 2D-3D results (87.0% mean registration recall vs. 57.3% for the best prior method) are particularly striking, though partly attributable to strong pre-trained components (DINOv2, DepthAnything) that prior baselines lack. Open questions include whether the iterative denoising cost (20 steps) is acceptable for real-time applications, how the method scales to very large point clouds where the N×M matrix becomes prohibitive, and whether the doubly stochastic constraint is truly necessary versus simpler row/column normalization.
