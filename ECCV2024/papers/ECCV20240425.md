## Problem

Existing text-guided 3D texture synthesis methods (inpainting-based and optimization-based) treat each rendered 2D view independently, ignoring cross-view correspondences in 3D space and the fundamental modal gap between 2D diffusion priors and 3D geometry. Furthermore, the multi-view feature aggregation-and-rasterization process used in recent optimization-based approaches introduces a systematic variance bias that causes over-smoothed textures, a problem that has not been theoretically characterized or corrected.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (remeshed into a coarse version with J_c vertices for latent-space processing and a finer version with J_f vertices for pixel-space refinement) and a free-form text prompt as input. It produces a textured 3D mesh with a UV texture map. During collaborative denoising, the mesh is rendered from 9 equidistant viewpoints (40° apart) at the latent resolution of the SD U-Net; during inpainting refinement, 4 additional views (at 0°, 80°, 160°, 280°) are used at 512×512 pixel resolution with depth conditioning.

## Method

VCD-Texture is a training-free framework that modifies the frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net's self-attention blocks during denoising. Joint Noise Prediction (JNP) adds a 3D self-attention branch that back-projects 2D foreground latent features into discrete volumetric grids (with two alternating grid sizes G_t = 0.34 and 0.25), allowing cross-view feature interaction within the same 3D cell while preserving the original 2D self-attention; the two branches are averaged. Multi-View Aggregation-and-Rasterization (MV-AR) then fuses per-view latent predictions into 3D vertex features using view-score, distance-score, and barycentric-coordinate weighting, and rasterizes them back to 2D. The key theoretical contribution is Variance Alignment (VA): the authors prove via Jensen's inequality that barycentric rasterization (a convex combination) necessarily reduces feature variance, and they correct this by rescaling the rasterized features' standard deviation to match the analytically computed 3D variance (Eq. 11–13). A final inpainting refinement step identifies vertices with high cross-view pixel variance (threshold λ = 0.005) and repairs those regions using Depth-SD inpainting with the original prompt and rendered depth.

## Results

VCD-Texture achieves the best FID and ClipFID across all three evaluation subsets, with FID of 56.29 (SubTex), 19.46 (SubShape), and 21.19 (SubObj), substantially outperforming the next-best methods (Repaint3D at 78.65/29.21/29.77 and SyncMVD at 65.30/32.44/34.00). It also attains the highest ClipScore (text-image semantic alignment) on all three subsets (31.65, 28.98, 30.42) and the highest ClipVar (multi-view consistency) on SubTex (83.97) and SubShape (82.93). The method is training-free, leveraging a pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net with modified self-attention and a variance alignment correction during multi-view aggregation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VCD-Texture outperforms all four baselines on FID and ClipFID (texture fidelity) across every dataset, with the largest margins over the inpainting-based methods Texture (FID 150.21 vs 56.29 on SubTex) and Text2Tex (FID 112.41 vs 56.29). Against the closest optimization-based competitor SyncMVD, VCD-Texture reduces FID by 9.01 (SubTex), 12.98 (SubShape), and 12.81 (SubObj), and ClipFID by 9.92, 3.81, and 3.27 respectively, confirming that the Variance Alignment correction effectively recovers the high-frequency detail lost during rasterization. On semantic alignment (ClipScore), VCD-Texture leads all baselines on all three subsets, with the clearest advantage over Texture (26.90 vs 31.65 on SubTex). On multi-view consistency (ClipVar), VCD-Texture wins on SubTex (83.97) and SubShape (82.93) but trails SyncMVD on SubObj (83.64 vs 84.52), suggesting that SyncMVD's synchronized multi-view diffusion still has a slight edge in view consistency for certain object categories despite its over-smoothed textures. Repaint3D, while competitive on ClipFID, consistently lags on FID and ClipVar, reflecting the limitations of sequential inpainting for global consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

VCD-Texture extends the synchronized multi-view diffusion paradigm introduced by SyncMVD, which aggregates latent features across views at each denoising step, by adding a 3D self-attention branch (JNP) that explicitly models cross-view geometric correspondences within the U-Net. It departs from the inpainting-based lineage (TEXTure, Text2Tex, Repaint3D) that treats each view sequentially and independently, instead operating in a joint multi-view latent space. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical identification of a variance bias inherent to the rasterization (convex combination) step, formalized via Jensen's inequality, and its correction through Variance Alignment — a problem prior multi-view diffusion methods did not explicitly address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Jensen's-inequality-based explanation for why rasterization degrades feature variance provides a principled diagnosis for the over-smoothed textures observed in SyncMVD and similar methods, offering a reusable theoretical lens for any 3D-2D projection pipeline in diffusion models. The constructed benchmark (SubObj, SubShape, SubTex with four complementary metrics) fills a notable gap in the texture synthesis evaluation landscape. However, the approach remains tied to a fixed 2D diffusion backbone (Depth-SD), the inpainting refinement yields only marginal metric gains, and evaluation is on relatively small object sets (43–445 shapes), leaving open whether the variance alignment generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes or higher-resolution generation.
