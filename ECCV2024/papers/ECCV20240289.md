## Problem

Feed-forward radiance field reconstruction from sparse, large-baseline views remains challenging: feature-matching methods (MVSNeRF, MuRF) require substantial image overlap and fail on widely separated cameras, while transformer-based approaches (LGM, GRM) use standard global attention that ignores the local nature of 3D reconstruction and demands 32+ A100-80G GPUs for training. No prior method efficiently unifies local and global spatial reasoning in a volume transformer while remaining trainable on modest hardware.

## Input / Output

LaRa accepts 4 unstructured sparse views at 512×512 resolution with known camera extrinsics and intrinsics (or a single image / text prompt via a generative multi-view model) and operates in a scale-invariant canonical space defined by Plücker rays, eliminating the need for a fixed camera-object distance. The output is a dense 64×64×64 Gaussian volume with 2 primitives per voxel (each carrying opacity, tangent vectors, scaling, offset, and spherical harmonics), which is decoded via 2D Gaussian splatting into high-resolution RGB, depth, opacity, and normal renderings, and further converted to textured meshes through TSDF fusion at 256³ resolution.

## Method

LaRa lifts DINO image features into a 3D canonical feature volume via Plücker-ray-modulated back-projection, then progressively matches them against a learnable 3D embedding volume through 12 Group Attention Layers. Each layer divides the volumes into G=16 local groups along each axis, applies cross-attention only within corresponding groups (capturing local feature matching), and uses a 3D convolution to propagate information across groups (capturing global context), avoiding the cubic cost of full self-attention. A coarse-fine decoding pipeline then predicts 2D Gaussian parameters: a lightweight MLP regresses base shape and appearance per voxel, while a fine module projects primitive centers onto the coarse rendering buffers (RGB, depth, alpha) and predicts residual spherical harmonics via point-based cross-attention to recover texture details lost in the DINO encoder. Training uses photometric (MSE + SSIM) losses on 8 rendered views plus distortion and normal-consistency regularization, converging in 2 days on 4 A100-40G GPUs.

## Results

On the in-domain Gobjaverse test set, LaRa achieves 26.14 PSNR / 0.931 SSIM / 0.0932 LPIPS, substantially outperforming MVSNeRF (14.48 PSNR), MuRF (14.05 PSNR), and LGM (19.67 PSNR). Zero-shot generalization to real-world data is particularly strong: on Co3D, LaRa reaches 21.64 PSNR versus 13.81 for LGM and 12.94 for MVSNeRF, a gap of over 7 dB. For geometry, LaRa achieves 0.0654 absolute depth error and 75.4% accuracy at the 0.02 threshold, compared to 0.1121 and 49.6% for LGM. All results are obtained with only 4 input views and a model trained on 4 A100-40G GPUs for 2 days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaRa decisively outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and metric. Against MVSNeRF [8], the gap is largest on Co3D (21.64 vs 12.94 PSNR, +8.7 dB), where MVSNeRF's cost-volume matching breaks down under large baselines and produces floaters. MuRF [71] effectively fails on all datasets, overfitting to the white background and producing near-empty predictions (11.60–14.05 PSNR). The most competitive comparison is with LGM [60], a concurrent Gaussian-primitive method: on GSO (constant camera-object distance), LGM reaches 23.67 PSNR versus LaRa's 27.65, a 4 dB gap; however, on unconstrained multi-view data (Co3D), LGM drops to 13.81 PSNR while LaRa maintains 21.64, revealing LGM's dependence on a fixed scene scale. Notably, LaRa achieves these results with 4 A100-40G GPUs over 2 days, whereas LGM required 32 A100-80G GPUs (8× GPUs, 16× RAM, 32× GPU-hours) for training.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRa builds directly on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as its primitive representation and rendering engine, but departs from prior feed-forward radiance field methods in its attention design. Where MVSNeRF and MuRF rely on explicit cost-volume feature matching (limiting them to small baselines), and concurrent large reconstruction models like LGM use standard global self-attention (which scales cubically with 3D resolution), LaRa introduces Group Attention Layers that partition the volume into local groups for intra-group cross-attention followed by 3D convolution for inter-group communication. The use of Plücker rays for camera conditioning and a coarse-fine two-stage decoding process further distinguish it from prior approaches that modulate features with extrinsic/intrinsic matrices or use single-stage decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practical contribution is the dramatic reduction in training cost: LaRa achieves superior results to LGM using 4 A100-40G GPUs for 2 days versus LGM's 32 A100-80G GPUs, making large-baseline feed-forward radiance field reconstruction accessible to academic labs. The zero-shot generalization to real scans (Co3D, GSO) and text-generated multi-view images (Instant3D) suggests the canonical Plücker-ray conditioning and group attention design transfer well beyond the synthetic training distribution. Open questions include whether the approach scales to unbounded scenes, whether the 16³ embedding volume resolution is sufficient for high-frequency geometry, and how to increase per-GPU batch size and volume resolution without proportional memory growth, as the authors note in their conclusion.
