## Problem

Existing indoor localization methods either require scene-specific training data or detailed 3D models, or they estimate only 2D position and 1D rotation (yaw), missing three degrees of freedom. Methods that do handle unseen scenes typically rely on panoramic queries captured under controlled conditions (tripod-mounted, level, fixed height), which is impractical for AR or archival photo registration. SPVLoc addresses the gap of estimating full 6D camera pose from a single uncontrolled RGB image against a minimal semantic 3D model, without any scene-specific training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB perspective query image (square, 60°–120° FoV, with arbitrary roll/pitch/yaw) and a minimal 3D scene model in Structured3D format containing only structural planes (walls, floor, ceiling) and door/window annotations. The 3D model is pre-rendered into single-channel equirectangular semantic panoramas on a 1.2×1.2 m grid at a fixed camera height. The output is a full 6D pose (3D translation in meters and 3D rotation as a unit quaternion), along with the predicted viewport bounding box and binary viewport mask within the best-matching panorama. The cross-domain nature of the matching—photographic RGB against untextured semantic renderings—is the central challenge the method must overcome.

## Method

SPVLoc reformulates 6D localization as a cross-domain perspective-to-panoramic viewport matching problem. A two-branch CNN encodes the query image (EfficientNet-S) and the semantic panorama (DenseNet-121) separately; query features are injected into the panorama branch via depth-wise correlation (inspired by DTOID), producing correlated features that drive three task heads: a RetinaNet-style bounding box classifier/regressor for the viewport, a binary viewport mask decoder, and a perspective supervision head predicting semantic and normal maps. A separate Pose head (convolutions + 4-layer MLP) regresses the relative 3D translation and rotation offset (as log-quaternion) from the correlated features, with an optional FoV-based side input for variable focal length. Training uses seven uncertainty-weighted losses (focal, smooth-L1, BCE, cross-entropy, cosine) balanced via learned homoscedastic uncertainty. At inference, all panoramas are scored by bounding box classification confidence, the top match is selected, and the absolute pose is obtained by adding the MLP-predicted offset to the known panorama position; an optional refinement step re-renders a panorama at the estimated pose for a second regression pass.

## Results

On Zillow Indoor (ZInD) with 90° FoV, SPVLoc achieves a median translation error of 13.63 cm and 90.84% recall at 1 m, compared to LASER's 26.28 cm and 80.90%. On Structured3D (S3D) at the same FoV, SPVLoc reaches 9.17 cm median translation error and 92.12% recall at 1 m, versus LASER's 14.60 cm and 76.80%. The 10 cm recall improves from 8.69% (LASER) to 30.57% (SPVLoc) on ZInD, and from 23.47% to 50.09% on S3D. SPVLoc also runs at 28.63 FPS per query, substantially faster than LASER (8.31 FPS) and PF-net (5.06 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPVLoc is compared primarily against LASER [30], the strongest scene-independent 2D localization baseline, and secondarily against PF-net [19] for timing. On both ZInD and S3D, SPVLoc halves the median translation error (e.g., 13.63 cm vs 26.28 cm on ZInD at 90° FoV) and improves 1 m recall by roughly 10 percentage points (90.84% vs 80.90%). The 10 cm recall gain is especially large: 30.57% vs 8.69% on ZInD and 50.09% vs 23.47% on S3D. SPVLoc is slightly less precise in 2D yaw rotation (1.34° vs 0.77° median on ZInD) because it estimates a full 3D rotation matrix rather than a single yaw angle, but it additionally recovers camera height, roll, and pitch—degrees of freedom LASER cannot provide. In terms of speed, SPVLoc processes queries at 28.63 FPS, roughly 3.4× faster than LASER (8.31 FPS) and 5.7× faster than PF-net (5.06 FPS), while requiring a sparser reference grid (1.2 m vs 0.5 m spacing used by LaLaLoc).

## Relation to Prior Work

SPVLoc repurposes the depth-wise correlation mechanism from DTOID-style object detection [29] to solve a fundamentally different problem: matching a perspective RGB query against a semantic equirectangular panorama, treating the visible viewport as a bounding box to be detected. For pose regression, it adopts the quaternion-logarithm output from Türkoğlu et al. [41] and the uncertainty-weighted multi-task loss from Kendall et al. [21]. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant indoor localization paradigm (scene-specific SfM or detailed BIM models) by showing that a minimalist Structured3D-format semantic model suffices, and it extends the 2D+1D localization of LASER [30] and PF-net [19] to full 6D pose estimation in unseen environments without any scene-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve 6D camera localization in unseen indoor scenes using only a semantic 3D model, removing the need for costly scene-specific data acquisition that limits prior approaches. The practical impact is significant for AR and building digitization where idealized capture conditions (tripod, fixed height, horizontal alignment) cannot be guaranteed. Key limitations include reduced effectiveness in large spaces with repeating room layouts due to the limited semantic detail in the S3D format, and the comparison is restricted to a single 2D baseline (LASER), leaving open how the method fares against other 6D approaches. Future work on combining localization with image analysis to enrich digital building models, and on scaling to more semantic classes (staircases, fixtures), could further reduce ambiguities.
