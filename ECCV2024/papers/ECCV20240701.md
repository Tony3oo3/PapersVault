## Problem

Existing data augmentation search methods cannot jointly optimize all four degrees of freedom of an augmentation policy: the number of sequential transformations (depth), their types, their order, and their magnitudes. Prior approaches either cap depth at two transformations, exhaustively enumerate depth values, or myopically stack layers one at a time, and many risk sampling the same transformation multiple times within a single pipeline. FreeAugment addresses this by being the first fully differentiable method to perform global end-to-end optimization over all four degrees of freedom simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-100 and DomainNet) and produces a learned augmentation policy parameterized by three groups: depth logits δ, a permutation logit matrix Π of size N×K (N=14 transformation types, K=7 max depth), and per-layer magnitude bounds μ. At inference, the policy samples a depth, a permutation of transformations, and continuous magnitudes to generate an augmented image. The search phase operates on ~10% reduced subsets with a 50/50 train-validation split, while final evaluation uses the full datasets with the learned policy applied during training of a downstream classifier.

## Method

FreeAugment formulates the augmentation policy as a fully differentiable sampling procedure over four coupled degrees of freedom. Depth is learned as a Gumbel-Softmax categorical distribution over {0,…,K}, with the straight-through estimator enabling gradient flow through the discrete depth choice. Types and order are unified as learning a probability distribution over N×K permutation matrices via the Gumbel-Sinkhorn operator, which inherently guarantees no transformation is applied more than once per image. Magnitudes are sampled from per-layer uniform distributions whose lower and upper bounds are learned through the reparameterization trick applied to differentiable implementations of each transformation. All parameters are optimized end-to-end by solving a bilevel optimization problem: the inner loop trains a classifier on augmented training data, and the outer loop updates the policy parameters to minimize validation loss, using a single-step gradient approximation with second-order derivatives computed efficiently via automatic differentiation.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FreeAugment achieves 96.54% top-1 accuracy with WRN-40-2 and 97.66% with WRN-28-10, outperforming all prior DAS methods. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 80.04% (WRN-40-2) and 84.13% (WRN-28-10). On ImageNet-100 with ResNet-18, FreeAugment attains 86.62% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best prior method (SLACK) by +0.43%. On DomainNet, it achieves 62.93% average accuracy across six domains, outperforming TA(Wide) by +1.22% and SLACK by +2.40%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeAugment outperforms all 16 compared baselines on CIFAR-10 for both architectures, beating the strongest prior methods (DeepAA at 97.56% and TA(Wide) at 97.46%) by +0.10% and +0.20% respectively with WRN-28-10. On CIFAR-100 with WRN-40-2, it surpasses SLACK (79.87%) and TA(Wide) (79.86%) by ~0.2%, though it trails TA(Wide) slightly on WRN-28-10 (84.13% vs 84.33%). On ImageNet-100, FreeAugment (86.62%) beats SLACK (86.19%) by +0.43% and TA(Wide) (85.68%) by +0.94%. On DomainNet, FreeAugment wins on all six individual domains and the average (62.93%), outperforming TA(Wide) (61.71%) and SLACK (60.53%), while all DAS methods substantially exceed the DomainBed baseline (55.40%). The paper also ablates each degree of freedom, showing that jointly learning all four (depth, types, order, magnitudes) yields 80.04% on CIFAR-100 versus 79.54–79.64% when any single DoF is frozen, confirming the synergistic benefit of the full formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeAugment extends the differentiable data augmentation search (DAS) lineage initiated by AutoAugment and advanced by methods like DADA, DDAS, DRA, and SLACK, which all formulate policy search as a bilevel optimization problem. Its key departure is the joint, end-to-end optimization of all four degrees of freedom (depth, type, order, magnitude) simultaneously, where prior methods either fixed depth at two, enumerated depths exhaustively, or stacked layers myopically. The type-and-order search is recast as learning a distribution over permutation matrices via the Gumbel-Sinkhorn operator (Mena et al., 2018), replacing the independent categorical distributions used by SLACK and DRA that permit redundant transformation repetition. Depth is handled through a Gumbel-Softmax (Jang et al., 2017) distribution over a one-hot depth vector, a mechanism borrowed from differentiable NAS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The permutation-based formulation provides an elegant, principled solution to the redundant-transformation problem that plagues independent categorical sampling, and the ablations confirm that jointly optimizing all four DoF yields consistent (if modest) gains over freezing any single component. The method's robustness across natural images and six diverse DomainNet domains suggests the learned policies are not overfit to a specific distribution. Open questions include scalability to larger transformation sets or deeper pipelines (the N×N Sinkhorn cost grows quadratically), whether the permutation constraint is always beneficial versus allowing controlled repetition, and applicability to non-image modalities such as audio or text where 'permutation of transformations' may not be as natural a formulation.
