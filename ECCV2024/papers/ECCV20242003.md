## Problem

Full 4-bit quantization of super-resolution networks causes severe quality degradation, primarily through step-like artifacts on smooth regions that the network cannot distinguish from real edges. Existing SR quantization methods either leave the first and last convolutions in floating point (preventing true full quantization and its associated speedup) or rely on mixed-precision schemes that introduce casting overhead. This paper asks whether the partially-smooth structure of natural images can be exploited to redistribute quantization error into the sparse set of edge pixels, enabling robust full 4-bit quantization.

## Input / Output

The method takes an 8-bit low-resolution RGB image and produces a high-resolution RGB image at scale factors x2, x3, or x4. Unusually, the LR image is first transformed into an operator domain (e.g., Laplacian or gradient) via a small convolution kernel before entering the quantized network, and the network's output remains in this operator domain. A regularized PDE solver then reconstructs the final HR image using Dirichlet boundary conditions derived from a bilinear upsample of the original LR image, so the pipeline requires both the network output and the upsampled LR image to produce the final result.

## Method

The approach wraps an unmodified SR CNN backbone (SRCNN, ESPCN, EDSR, RFDN) with a differential-operator front-end and a regularized PDE-solver back-end. The input LR image is convolved with a finite-difference kernel (Laplacian, gradient, or Sobel; fixed or trainable) to produce a sparse high-frequency representation where quantization error is confined to edges. This operator-domain image is processed by a fully W4A4-quantized network (all convolutions, including first and last, quantized via LSQ+). The network output is then inverted back to the pixel domain by solving a regularized PDE (Eq. 8), which combines the operator-domain estimate with the upsampled LR image in the frequency domain using a Tikhonov-type regularization parameter λ. This formulation is well-posed (the denominator is always positive for λ ≥ 0), avoids the ill-posedness of naive inverse filtering, and requires no patch-wise processing, making it robust to quantization errors in the operator domain while adding only modest BitOps overhead.

## Results

For full 4-bit (W4A4) quantization, the proposed method recovers most of the FP performance gap: EDSR x2 on DIV2K achieves 33.98 dB PSNR (vs. 34.57 dB FP and 30.23 dB regular W4A4, a +3.75 dB gain), and RFDN x2 on DIV2K reaches 33.76 dB (vs. 34.33 dB FP and 30.09 dB regular, +3.67 dB). Across all tested networks (SRCNN, ESPCN, EDSR, RFDN) and scales (x2–x4), the method consistently narrows the FP-to-quantized gap by 2–4 dB in PSNR. Even at extreme 2-bit quantization, the proposed approach still outperforms bicubic upsampling, whereas regular quantization falls below it.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard LSQ+ (Regular W4A4) baseline, the proposed method delivers large and consistent gains: +3.75 dB PSNR for EDSR x2 on DIV2K (33.98 vs. 30.23) and +3.67 dB for RFDN x2 (33.76 vs. 30.09), with similar 2–4 dB improvements across SRCNN, ESPCN, and all scale factors. Compared to DAQ [14], the proposed method achieves 31.14 dB PSNR versus 27.13 dB on EDSR x2 Urban100 (+4.01 dB) while using less than half the BitOps (6.41G vs. 12.56G). Against CADYQ [13], the proposed method edges out with 31.14 dB vs. 30.84 dB PSNR and 0.9495 vs. 0.9132 SSIM, while requiring roughly 4× fewer BitOps (6.41G vs. 24.76G) and avoiding the mixed-precision casting overhead inherent to CADYQ's region-based bit allocation. The authors note that their approach is orthogonal to DAQ and CADYQ and could potentially be combined with them for further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard quantization pipeline (LSQ+, AdaRound) and prior SR-specific quantization methods (DAQ, CADYQ, QuantSR) by reframing the problem in an operator domain rather than the pixel domain. It repurposes tools from PDE theory—finite-difference approximations of differential operators and regularized inverse problem solvers—as a quantization error redistribution mechanism, drawing on the compressed-sensing observation that natural images are sparse in the Laplacian/gradient domain. Unlike DAQ and CADYQ, which leave the first and last convolutions in FP, this work achieves truly full 4-bit quantization of all layers, closing a practical gap that prior methods explicitly acknowledged but did not resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Full 4-bit quantization including the first and last convolutions is a practical necessity for real-time SR on edge devices, and the reported +3.7 dB gains over regular W4A4 quantization across four architectures and three scales make this a meaningful step toward deployable low-bit SR. The approach is orthogonal to existing quantization techniques (DAQ, CADYQ) and could be combined with them, broadening its utility. However, the FFT-based PDE solver introduces non-trivial computational overhead (e.g., 6.41 vs. 5.63 GBitOps for EDSR x2), and the paper does not demonstrate end-to-end latency on actual mobile NPUs, leaving open whether the net speedup justifies the added pipeline complexity in production settings. Generalization to other low-level vision tasks (denoising, inpainting) or to even lower bitwidths (1–2 bit) remains unexplored.
