## Problem

Diffusion models require hundreds of sampling steps for inference, and existing acceleration methods like consistency models (LCM) are limited by cumulative approximation errors that grow uncontrollably as the skipping step size increases. The paper identifies that the sub-path learning objective decomposes into a drift/diffusion distance term that becomes intractable to model for large skipping steps and a denoising propagation term, and that consistency models fail to address this decomposition, leading to performance collapse at larger step sizes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241418/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of pre-trained Stable Diffusion (SDv1.5 or SDv2.1-base), taking a text prompt (encoded via CLIP) and a noisy latent vector z_t as input, along with a continuous interpolation parameter γ ∈ [0,1] converted to Fourier embeddings and fed as an additional network input. It outputs a denoised latent z_0 in 2–4 sampling steps, which is decoded to a 512×512 RGB image by the VAE decoder. Notably, the γ-embedding weights can be discarded after training, so the deployed model has no additional parameters beyond the teacher architecture.

## Method

SPLAM introduces Sub-Path Linear (SL) ODEs that approximate each sub-path on the PF-ODE trajectory as a linear interpolation between its endpoints, parameterized by γ ∈ [0,1]. This provides a progressive, continuous estimation for the otherwise intractable drift/diffusion distance term (dist_Δ) while maintaining a consistent estimation for the denoising propagation term (dist_{0,θ}), enabling stable optimization at large skipping step sizes (up to k=100). The distillation procedure (SPLAD) trains the student by minimizing the distance between F_θ(z_{γ,t}, c, γ, t) and an EMA target F_{θ⁻}(ẑ_{1,t-k}, c, 1, t-k), where the teacher estimate ẑ is obtained via a Multiple Estimation strategy that iteratively applies the DDIM solver with a reduced sub-step size k_φ. Training requires only ~6 A100 GPU days (2K–6K iterations at k=100) and produces a model that generates images in 2–4 steps with FID scores of 10.09 (LAION), 10.06 (COCO-30k), and 20.77 (COCO-5k), surpassing LCM, DMD, InstaFlow, and UFOGen.

## Results

On MSCOCO2014-30k (w=3), SPLAM achieves FID 10.06 at 4 steps and 12.31 at 2 steps, outperforming LCM (10.68 / 14.29), DMD (11.49), UFOGen (12.78), and InstaFlow-1.7B (11.83). On MSCOCO2017-5k (w=3), SPLAM reaches FID 20.77 at 4 steps vs. LCM's 21.41. On LAION-Aesthetics-6+ with SDv2.1-base (w=8), SPLAM attains FID 10.09 at 4 steps, surpassing LCM (10.43) and all non-training solvers (DDIM 22.38, DPM++ 18.43). Training requires only 6 A100 GPU days, compared to over 100 A100 GPU days for DMD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPLAM consistently outperforms LCM (the closest competitor) at both 2 and 4 steps across all three evaluation datasets: FID 12.31 vs. 14.29 (2-step, COCO-30k), 10.06 vs. 10.68 (4-step, COCO-30k), and 20.77 vs. 21.41 (4-step, COCO-5k). Against one-step methods, SPLAM's 2-step result (12.31) beats DMD (11.49 is 1-step but requires 100+ A100 GPU days), UFOGen (12.78), and InstaFlow-1.7B (11.83) in 4-step mode (10.06). On the LAION benchmark with SDv2.1-base, SPLAM (4-step, FID 10.09) surpasses LCM (10.43) and all non-training solvers (DDIM 22.38, DPM++ 18.43) by a wide margin. SPLAM's 4-step FID of 10.06 approaches the unaccelerated teacher SDv1.5 (FID 8.03 at 50 steps) while running in 0.19 s vs. 2.59 s. The key efficiency advantage is training cost: SPLAM converges in ~6 A100 GPU days (2K–6K iterations with k=100), whereas DMD requires over 100 A100 GPU days and LCM needs 10K+ iterations (~16 A100 GPU days) without supporting larger skipping step sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPLAM is a direct extension of the consistency model framework (Song et al., ICML 2023) and its latent variant LCM (Luo et al., 2023), inheriting the self-consistency property, EMA training, and skipping-step mechanism. The key departure is a diagnostic observation: the sub-path learning objective in consistency models decomposes into a drift/diffusion distance term (dist_Δ) that becomes intractable for large skipping steps and a denoising-propagation term (dist_{0,θ}), and the paper introduces Sub-Path Linear (SL) ODEs to provide a progressive, continuous approximation of the otherwise intractable dist_Δ component. The distillation pipeline (SPLAD) further builds on the LCM training recipe but adds a γ-conditioned input and a Multiple Estimation strategy that runs the teacher ODE solver in smaller sub-steps to improve target quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPLAM offers a practical and theoretically motivated improvement over LCM for few-step (2–4) text-to-image generation, with notably lower training cost (~6 A100 GPU days vs. ~16 for LCM) and the ability to use larger skipping step sizes (k=100) without the performance collapse that plagues LCM. The decomposition insight and SL-ODE construction are clean and could in principle transfer to other PF-ODE-based generative settings (video, audio), though the paper only validates on text-to-image. Open questions include whether the linear interpolation is truly optimal versus other sub-path parameterizations, whether the approach extends to training-from-scratch (beyond distillation), and how it interacts with emerging flow-matching or rectified-flow formulations that are already straightening PF-ODE trajectories.
