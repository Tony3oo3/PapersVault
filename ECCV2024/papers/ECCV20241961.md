## Problem

Monocular vision introduces inherent depth ambiguity in 3D occupancy prediction, as a single pixel can correspond to multiple voxels along the line of sight. Existing temporal fusion methods either use temporal information implicitly (warp-and-concat, self-attention) without explicit geometric reasoning, or require per-image-pair cost volume computation that scales poorly with the number of views and frames. The paper addresses how to efficiently exploit temporal parallax cues to resolve depth ambiguity with minimal additional computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241961/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 7 frames of multi-view RGB images (960×640 pixels per view, 0.5 s apart, covering a 3 s span) along with relative camera poses, and produces a 200×200×16 voxel grid (0.4 m resolution, spanning ±40 m in x/y and −1 to 5.4 m in z) with 16 semantic class labels per voxel. The BEV encoder outputs a 200×200×256 feature map that is reshaped into 200×200×16×16 volume features before the CVT module operates on them.

## Method

CVT-Occ introduces a Cost Volume Temporal module that, for each voxel, samples N=9 points along its line of sight (the ray from the camera optical center through the voxel) and projects them into K−1=6 historical frames via relative camera poses. Because of ego-motion parallax, these co-linear points in the current frame scatter across different locations in historical frames, breaking the depth ambiguity; bilinearly sampled BEV features from all K×N projected points are concatenated into a cost volume feature map of shape 200×200×16×63×16. A small convolutional network over this cost volume produces per-voxel occupancy weights (supervised with binary cross-entropy against ground-truth occupancy), which are element-wise multiplied with the current volume features to suppress depth-ambiguous activations. The module is plug-and-play on top of BEVFormer's view transformation, trained end-to-end with a combined cross-entropy occupancy loss and the CVT binary loss, achieving 27.37 mIoU on Occ3D-Waymo versus 24.58 for the BEVFormer baseline.

## Results

On the Occ3D-Waymo validation set, CVT-Occ achieves a mean IoU of 27.37, outperforming all compared temporal fusion baselines. The method gains +2.79 mIoU over BEVFormer (24.58), +2.64 over SOLOFusion (24.73), and +2.30 over BEVFormer-WrapConcat (25.07). Per-class improvements are most pronounced for Vehicle (+4.94 over BEVFormer), Building (+5.42), Vegetation (+4.92), and Tree Trunk (+5.28), while the no-temporal-fusion baseline (BEVFormer-w/o TSA) scores only 23.87 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVT-Occ is compared against three representative temporal fusion paradigms under identical architecture and frame settings (7 frames at 0.5s intervals). Against BEVFormer [20] (attention-based temporal self-attention), CVT-Occ improves mIoU from 24.58 to 27.37 (+2.79), with the largest per-class gains on Vehicle (+4.94), Building (+5.42), and Vegetation (+4.92). Against SOLOFusion [25] (plane-sweep cost volume in image space), CVT-Occ achieves +2.64 mIoU (27.37 vs. 24.73), notably surpassing it on Vehicle (+8.55) and Pedestrian (+5.65) while SOLOFusion leads on Road (70.95 vs. 79.12 is actually CVT-Occ winning; SOLOFusion leads on Building at 31.49 vs. 29.40). Against BEVFormer-WrapConcat (wrap-and-concatenate fusion), CVT-Occ gains +2.30 mIoU (27.37 vs. 25.07), with consistent improvements across nearly all 14 evaluated classes. The no-temporal-fusion ablation (BEVFormer-w/o TSA, 23.87) confirms that all temporal fusion strategies help, but CVT-Occ's explicit parallax-based cost volume yields the largest absolute gain (+3.50).

## Relation to Prior Work

CVT-Occ positions itself as a fourth paradigm of temporal fusion for 3D perception, distinct from attention-based (BEVFormer), wrap-and-concat (BEVDet4D, PanoOcc), and image-space cost volume (SOLOFusion) approaches. It repurposes the cost volume concept from stereo matching but constructs it in 3D voxel space rather than image space, sampling points along each voxel's line of sight and projecting them into historical frames to exploit parallax. The module is explicitly designed as a plug-and-play component that sits on top of existing image-to-volume transformation pipelines (demonstrated with BEVFormer), making it orthogonal to the choice of lifting or querying strategy for generating initial 3D features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parallax-based depth disambiguation addresses a fundamental limitation of monocular 3D occupancy prediction in a geometrically principled way, and the plug-and-play design means it can be adopted by a wide range of existing occupancy pipelines. However, the method is validated only on Occ3D-Waymo, and its effectiveness is inherently tied to the magnitude of ego-motion parallax—performance gains diminish in far regions and slow-driving scenarios, raising questions about applicability in urban stop-and-go traffic or static-camera settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other datasets and sensor configurations, how sensitive it is to pose estimation errors, and whether the computational cost scales gracefully with longer temporal sequences or higher-resolution grids.
