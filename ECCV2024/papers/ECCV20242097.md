## Problem

Modern learned feature detectors such as SuperPoint and ALIKED produce keypoint locations at integer-pixel resolution, lacking the sub-pixel localization accuracy that made SIFT the benchmark for decades. Existing approaches either bake sub-pixel mechanisms into the detector architecture itself or rely on reinforcement learning for end-to-end optimization, making them difficult to apply to arbitrary detectors. This paper asks whether a lightweight, detector-agnostic post-processing module can add sub-pixel precision to any existing feature detector without redesigning it.

## Input / Output

For each matched keypoint pair, the module receives an 11×11 image patch centered at the detected keypoint, the corresponding 2D feature descriptors from both views, and optionally a score-map patch concatenated as side information. It outputs a 2D sub-pixel offset vector per keypoint (bounded to ±5 pixels via a scale factor σ=2.5 applied to a 5×5 SoftArgMax output), which is added to the original integer-pixel location. The module adds only 123K–197K parameters and ~7 ms of inference time per image pair.

## Method

The Keypoint Refinement module is a small CNN (channel sizes 16-16-64-64-d) that extracts D-dimensional features from the 11×11 patch, reducing spatially to 5×5. A score map is computed by dot-producting each spatial feature with the average of the two matched descriptors, and SoftArgMax over this score map yields the sub-pixel displacement. The descriptor averaging serves as a cross-view consistency regularizer (a special case of PixSfM's robust mean). Training uses a thresholded epipolar error loss computed from the ground-truth essential matrix, where only matches with error below a geometrically calibrated threshold contribute gradient, preventing outlier-dominated optimization. Crucially, gradients flow only through the refinement module—not through the frozen detector or matcher—making the approach applicable to any existing detector without retraining it.

## Results

On MegaDepth with SuperPoint+MNN, the proposed refinement improves AUC@5° from 35.34% to 37.16% and AUC@10° from 45.37% to 48.07%, while also increasing the inlier ratio from 33.78% to 34.42%. With the LightGlue matcher, SuperPoint's AUC@5° on MegaDepth rises from 59.82% to 61.82%, and ALIKED's from 64.69% to 65.34%. On the HPatches benchmark, Mean Matching Accuracy at 1px improves from 27% to 33%. The module adds only ~7 ms of inference time and 123K–197K parameters to the existing detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla SuperPoint, the proposed refinement consistently improves all accuracy metrics across MegaDepth, KITTI, and ScanNet, with the largest gains on MegaDepth (~2 AUC points at 5° and ~3 points at 20°) and smaller but uniform improvements on KITTI (~0.4 AUC points) and ScanNet (~0.2–0.4 AUC points). Compared to the reinforcement-learning-based Reinforced SuperPoint [Bhowmik et al., CVPR 2020], the proposed method wins on every metric on MegaDepth and KITTI, and ties or slightly leads on ScanNet (where mean error is comparable at ~28.6°). With the LightGlue matcher (Table 2), the method also improves ALIKED, raising its MegaDepth AUC@5° from 64.69% to 65.34% and KITTI AUC@5° from 84.24% to 84.27%, confirming detector-agnostic applicability. The fundamental matrix estimation results (Table 3) mirror the pose estimation gains, with F1-score improving from 47.64 to 48.42 on MegaDepth and from 74.15 to 75.48 on KITTI. Overall, the method never degrades performance relative to any baseline, though the absolute improvements are modest (typically 0.2–3 AUC points), reflecting the already-strong baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a detector-agnostic post-processing module that addresses the well-known sub-pixel localization gap between classical detectors (SIFT) and learned ones (SuperPoint, ALIKED). Architecturally, it draws on SoftArgMax (from ALIKED/ALIKE) for differentiable keypoint displacement and on the descriptor-guided attention mechanism reminiscent of PixSfM's featuremetric refinement, but departs from both by training with a direct epipolar geometry loss rather than augmentation-based self-supervision. The epipolar loss with thresholding is borrowed from robust estimator and matcher training (Neural-guided RANSAC, SuperGlue) but is applied to keypoint refinement for the first time. Crucially, it avoids the end-to-end differentiability challenges that stymied prior attempts (e.g., REINFORCE-based approaches like Bhowmik et al.) by restricting gradient flow to the small refinement module alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in its plug-and-play nature: a ~200K-parameter, ~7ms module that can be appended to any existing detector without retraining, offering consistent (if modest) 1–2 AUC point gains across indoor and outdoor benchmarks. This makes it attractive for production pipelines where swapping detectors is costly. However, the improvements are small in absolute terms, and the method is trained solely on MegaDepth, raising questions about whether the geometric supervision generalizes to fundamentally different scene types or tasks beyond relative pose. An open question is whether the descriptor-as-query attention mechanism could be extended to multi-scale or multi-view settings, and whether the modest gains reflect a fundamental ceiling for local-patch-based refinement.
