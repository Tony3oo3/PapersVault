## Problem

In Human-AI Collaborative Classification (HAI-CC), learning to defer (AI defers to users) and learning to complement (AI combines with user predictions) have been studied in isolation despite being interconnected components of a unified decision system. Additionally, most HAI-CC methods assume clean training labels, which is impractical for multi-rater settings where only noisy annotations from multiple experts are available. This paper addresses both gaps by unifying defer and complement strategies while training from multi-rater noisy labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241489/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an image x, M experts' noisy one-hot annotations (shuffled to avoid user-specific bias), and a pre-trained LNL AI model's probability vector, LECODU produces a final class label along with a selected collaboration mode from 2M+1 options (AI alone, AI+1 through AI+M users, or defer to 1 through M users). The Human-AI Selection Module outputs a 2M-dimensional probability vector over these scenarios, and the associated collaboration cost equals the number of user annotations consumed (0 for AI alone, up to M for full collaboration).

## Method

LECODU comprises a Human-AI Selection Module g_φ (a CNN backbone outputting a 2M-dimensional categorical distribution over collaboration scenarios) and a Collaboration Module h_ψ (a two-layer MLP) that fuses the selected subset of AI and user predictions into a final class probability. Training uses CROWDLAB to generate consensus labels from multi-rater noisy annotations, and the non-differentiable argmax over collaboration scenarios is reparameterized via Gumbel-Softmax to enable gradient-based optimization. The loss combines cross-entropy on the consensus label with a cost term (weighted by λ) that penalizes the expected number of users engaged, encouraging the system to invoke human collaboration only when it improves accuracy. A key design choice is randomly shuffling user annotations during both training and testing to ensure the model is unbiased to any specific expert, and the pre-trained LNL AI model (ProMix, InstanceGM, or NSHE depending on dataset) provides the AI prediction input.

## Results

LECODU achieves higher classification accuracy than all competing HAI-CC methods across every collaboration cost level on all four benchmarks (CIFAR-10H, IDN20–IDN50, Chaoyang). The pre-trained LNL AI models serve as the cost-0 baseline: ProMix reaches 97.41% on CIFAR-10, InstanceGM reaches 96.64%–95.90% on IDN20–IDN50, and NSHE reaches 82.44% on Chaoyang. LECODU's peak accuracy is reached within the cost range [2000, 4000] (out of a maximum of 10000), indicating that maximal expert engagement does not yield the best outcome. Even in the most challenging high-noise setting (IDN50, ~50% user accuracy), LECODU maintains a discernible improvement over both human-only and AI-only decision-making.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LECODU outperforms all nine baselines across all benchmarks and cost levels. Among single-expert L2D methods (CE, OvA, SP, RS, CC, DIFT, MoE), LECODU consistently achieves higher accuracy at every cost; these SEL2D methods peak at cost=0 (i.e., the LNL AI model alone) in high-noise settings and degrade as human involvement increases. Among multi-expert methods, Multi_L2D [58] performs comparably to SEL2D baselines, while CET [23] incurs a fixed cost of 10,000 (always using 3 experts) and still trails LECODU. LECOMH [74], the closest competitor as a multi-expert L2C method, is competitive with LECODU at higher collaboration costs but significantly lags at low-cost levels (e.g., CIFAR-10H, Chaoyang, IDN20), confirming that a complement-only strategy is inferior to the unified defer-and-complement approach. In high-noise scenarios (IDN30–IDN50), LECODU shows the largest margins over SEL2D methods, which degrade sharply as cost increases, while LECOMH and CET remain above SEL2D but still below LECODU.

## Relation to Prior Work

LECODU unifies two previously separate research threads in Human-AI Collaborative Classification: learning to defer (L2D), which decides when to hand classification to a human, and learning to complement (L2C), which fuses AI and human predictions. The closest prior work, LECOMH [74], integrated L2C with noisy-label and multi-rater learning but explicitly excluded the L2D option, while multi-expert L2D methods like Multi_L2D [58] and CET [23] did not combine AI and human predictions. LECODU also depends on CROWDLAB [18] for generating reliable consensus labels from multi-rater noisy annotations, replacing the clean-label assumption that most HAI-CC methods require, and on SOTA LNL pre-training (ProMix, InstanceGM, NSHE) to produce the AI component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly optimising accuracy and collaboration cost across a unified L2D+L2C decision space, LECODU offers a more flexible and cost-efficient HAI-CC system than methods that commit to a single collaboration strategy. A key open limitation is the assumption that all users are interchangeable, which avoids combinatorial explosion but ignores real-world heterogeneity in expert competence; the authors also flag the risk of AI over-reliance and human deskilling. The finding that peak accuracy occurs at moderate collaboration cost (2000–4000) rather than maximum cost suggests that indiscriminately adding more human input can be counterproductive, a practical insight for deployment budgeting in high-stakes domains like medical imaging.
