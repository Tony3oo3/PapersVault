## Problem

Most existing WiFi-based human sensing datasets contain only single-user samples, making it impossible to evaluate or compare models on the practically important task of sensing multiple users simultaneously. No public benchmark dataset exists for WiFi-based multi-user activity sensing, and prior multi-user sensing works have neither released their data nor provided reproducible benchmarks.

## Input / Output

Each 3-second sample consists of dual-band WiFi CSI (2.4 GHz on channel 12, 5 GHz on channel 64) with dimensions 3000×3×3×30 (time steps × transmit antennas × receive antennas × subcarriers) at 1000 Hz, paired with a synchronized 90-frame video at 1920×1080 resolution. Because each sample may contain 0–5 users performing different activities simultaneously, outputs are structured as multi-label predictions over 6 identities, 5 locations, and 9 activities, using a sigmoid head with a fixed 0.5 threshold rather than single-label softmax classification.

## Method

WiMANS is constructed by deploying a single transmitter-receiver pair with Intel 5300 NICs running the Linux 802.11n CSI tool in monitor mode, collecting 11,286 samples across 114 user groups in three environments (classroom, meeting room, empty room). A key design choice is the use of simultaneous scripts—five pre-defined script sets (α, β, δ, γ, λ) with 99 indexes each—that instruct fixed user groups to perform specific activities at specific locations independently yet simultaneously, ensuring clean, reproducible multi-user samples without cross-contamination between adjacent samples. For benchmarking, eight WiFi-based models (ST-RF, MLP, LSTM, CNN-1D, CNN-2D, CLSTM, ABLSTM, THAT) and six video-based models (ResNet, S3D, MViT-v1/v2, Swin-T/S) are evaluated under a unified multi-label classification protocol with 80/20 train/test splits per environment, Xavier initialization, Adam optimizer, and 10 random-seed repetitions. The paper reports accuracy, parameter count, FLOPs, and recognition throughput to characterize both sensing performance and computational cost across all three tasks.

## Results

On WiMANS (5 GHz band), WiFi-based models achieve up to 99.9% multi-user identification accuracy (THAT, Meeting Room), 88.1% localization accuracy (THAT/CLSTM, Meeting Room), and 64.8% activity recognition accuracy (CLSTM, Empty Room). Video-based models substantially outperform WiFi-based models, with S3D reaching 99.9% identity, 99.9% location, and 98.5% activity accuracy in the Empty Room. However, WiFi-based models are far more efficient: the largest WiFi model (CLSTM, 5.39M params, 1.79G FLOPs) is 1.55× smaller and 5.51× lighter in FLOPs than the smallest video model (S3D, 8.34M params, 204.6G FLOPs), and achieves 16–17× higher throughput (~1955 recs/s vs ~117 recs/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 8 WiFi-based baselines, CLSTM dominates activity recognition (64.2–64.8% on 5 GHz) while THAT leads in identification (99.9% in Meeting Room) and localization (88.1% in Meeting Room); CNN-2D is competitive on localization (87.4% Meeting Room) but trails CLSTM on HAR by ~3.5%. All WiFi-based models show a clear performance hierarchy: identification (90.7–99.9%) > localization (78.8–88.1%) > activity recognition (60.9–64.8%), reflecting increasing task granularity. Video-based models (S3D, MViT-v2, Swin-T/S) achieve 94–98.5% on HAR, exceeding the best WiFi model (CLSTM, 64.8%) by roughly 30 percentage points, confirming that WiFi CSI still lags behind visual signals for fine-grained multi-user activity. On efficiency, WiFi-based models are decisively superior: THAT processes 1955 recognitions/s versus S3D's 117 recs/s (16.7× faster), with 4.9M parameters and 1.65G FLOPs versus S3D's 8.3M parameters and 204.6G FLOPs. The 5 GHz band consistently outperforms 2.4 GHz by 5–15% across all tasks due to lower environmental noise, and simply concatenating both bands does not improve over 5 GHz alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by extending the well-established single-user WiFi CSI sensing paradigm (identification, localization, HAR) to the multi-user setting, where no public benchmark dataset previously existed. It repurposes a broad set of existing WiFi-based architectures (THAT, CLSTM, ABLSTM, CNN-1D/2D) and video-based models (Swin, MViT, S3D) as baselines rather than proposing a new method, positioning itself as an empirical benchmarking effort. The dual-band (2.4/5 GHz) collection and synchronized video capture draw on multimodal dataset precedents like OPERANet and MM-Fi, but are the first to apply these modalities to simultaneous multi-user scenarios with fine-grained identity, location, and activity annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WiMANS fills a critical gap by providing the first reproducible benchmark for WiFi-based multi-user sensing, enabling fair comparison of models that previously could only be evaluated on proprietary or single-user data. The results reveal a substantial and persistent performance gap between WiFi-based models (~60–65% HAR accuracy) and video-based models (~90–98%), highlighting that multi-user activity recognition from CSI remains far from solved. Open questions include whether dual-band fusion can close this gap (naive concatenation does not help), how performance scales beyond 5 users, and whether cross-domain generalization is feasible. Limitations include only 6 volunteers, 9 activities, 3 environments, and a single TX-RX pair, which constrain the dataset's ecological validity.
