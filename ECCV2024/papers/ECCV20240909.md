## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for hyperspectral image classification perform pixel-wise prediction, ignoring spectral correlations between adjacent pixels. This causes blurred or incorrect boundaries between land cover regions and inconsistent classification within contiguous areas that exhibit only minor spectral variations. Prior superpixel-based remedies either fail to enforce intra-region consistency or rely on iterative, non-CUDA-compatible algorithms that preclude real-time inference.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a full hyperspectral image I ∈ R^{H×W×D} (e.g., 512×512×32 for WHU-OHS, 145×145×200 for IP) and produces a pixel-level classification map M ∈ R^{H×W} over C' predefined land cover classes. For the large-scale WHU-OHS dataset the model processes the entire image at once, while for smaller benchmarks (IP, KSC, UP) it operates on overlapping 9×9 patches and resolves per-pixel labels by majority vote across containing patches. The clustering operates on F×F spatial patches (F=16), generating M=4 spectral supertokens per patch, so the effective number of classification primitives is reduced by a factor of F²/M relative to raw pixels.

## Method

DSTC is a two-stage, end-to-end trainable network. Stage 1 extracts spatially preserved deep semantic features via a UNet encoder, then performs spectrum-derivative-based pixel clustering: first-order spectral derivatives are computed to enrich the original spectra, and an SLIC-inspired iterative association matrix (with learnable linear projections) assigns each pixel to one of M centroids per F×F patch; semantic features within each cluster are aggregated by association-weighted averaging to form spectral supertokens. Stage 2 feeds these supertokens directly into a Vision Transformer (bypassing ViT's patch partitioning) for token-wise classification, and the final pixel map is obtained by projecting each token's predicted class onto all pixels it contains. Training supervision uses a class-proportion-based soft label (CPSL) that encodes the proportion of each land cover class within every supertoken boundary, replacing hard single-class labels to mitigate distribution imbalance; the loss is a standard cross-entropy between predicted token probabilities and these soft labels.

## Results

On the WHU-OHS dataset (512×512, 24 classes, 32 bands), DSTC-P achieves a class-average F1-score of 0.723, outperforming the best baseline 3D-FCN (0.683) by 4.0 points and ViT (0.673) by 5.0 points. On the IP dataset, DSTC attains 98.75% OA and 0.9857 κ, exceeding CVSSN by 0.48% and 0.55% respectively. On KSC and UP, DSTC performs comparably to CVSSN with differences within 0.1–0.14% across OA, AA, and κ. DSTC-P requires only 16.15G FLOPs, roughly 3.9% of CLSJE's 412.20G and 0.89% of 3D-CNN's 1814.51G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WHU-OHS, DSTC-P (CF1=0.723) outperforms all 7 baselines, with the largest margins over A2S2K (+9.4 pts), Capsule (+8.5 pts), 3D-CNN (+7.9 pts), and CLSJE (+7.9 pts). The closest competitor is 3D-FCN (0.683), still trailing by 4.0 points. On IP, DSTC surpasses CVSSN in OA (98.75% vs 98.27%) and κ (0.9857 vs 0.9802) but falls short in AA (96.15% vs 97.67%). On KSC and UP, DSTC is marginally behind CVSSN by 0.1–0.14% across all three metrics, which the authors attribute to the smaller patch sizes (9×9) limiting the benefit of spectral supertoken clustering. Against RSSAN, SSAtt, SSSAN, and SSTN on IP/KSC/UP, DSTC leads or matches on OA and κ while remaining competitive on AA.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSTC draws its two-stage design (proposal → classification) from Faster R-CNN and adapts the SLIC superpixel initialization for its clustering stage, while borrowing the weighted aggregation formula from Context Cluster (Ma et al., ICLR 2023). It departs from prior superpixel-based HSI classifiers (Zhao et al., Tu et al., Nartey et al.) that still perform pixel-wise classification within superpixels, instead classifying at the token level and projecting results back. The spectral-derivative-based clustering and class-proportion-based soft label are novel contributions not present in the existing HSI classification or superpixel literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spectral supertoken formulation offers a practical path to consistent regional classification and precise boundaries in large-scale HSI, while reducing the number of primitives the Transformer must process (1024 tokens vs. 262,144 pixels for a 512×512 image). The class-proportion-based soft label provides a simple yet effective remedy for intra-token class imbalance, with the ablation showing a dramatic CF1 gap (0.443 → 0.721) over hard labels. However, the paper acknowledges that on smaller, lower-resolution datasets (IP, KSC, UP) the advantage narrows considerably, suggesting the method's benefits are most pronounced at scale. Open questions include whether the fixed clustering granularity (F=16, M=4) can be made adaptive per scene and how the approach generalizes to very high band counts or multi-temporal settings.
