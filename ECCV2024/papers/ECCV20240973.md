## Problem

Cross-view geo-localization must match street view panoramas to georeferenced satellite images despite drastic perspective, appearance, and geometry differences. Existing polar-coordinate transformations reduce geometric gaps but still leave significant morphological distortion, and current benchmarks (CVUSA, CVACT, VIGOR) use fixed street-view orientations, single-country data, and no cross-temporal or map-based evaluation, limiting their relevance to real-world deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240973/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 360° equirectangular street view panorama (with arbitrary, non-fixed camera orientation) and a database of 512×512 georeferenced satellite tiles covering ~70 m × 70 m as input. It produces a ranked list of satellite images, with the top match identifying the geographic location. The street view is additionally transformed into a 512×512 BEV image (14 cm pixel resolution, 1.5 m camera height) that is fed in parallel; the final score is the sum of similarities from both branches. The CVGlobal dataset also supports map raster tiles as an alternative reference modality.

## Method

The key insight is to transform the street view panorama into an explicit Bird's-Eye View (EP-BEV) image via geometric back-projection under a ground-plane assumption, aligning it spatially with the satellite perspective without requiring depth estimation or camera intrinsics. A dual-branch Panorama-BEV Co-Retrieval Network then performs collaborative retrieval: the panorama branch (ConvNeXt-B encoder + InfoNCE loss) captures global layout over a wide observation range, while the BEV branch (same architecture, separately trained) emphasizes fine-grained cross-view details near the capture point that help distinguish visually similar satellite tiles. At inference, the top-ranked candidates from the panorama branch are re-scored by the BEV branch, and the two similarity scores are summed to produce the final retrieval ranking. This co-retrieval design overcomes the limited visibility and severe distortion of BEV images in dense urban scenes by retaining the full panorama's global context.

## Results

On CVUSA, the method achieves 98.71% R@1 and 99.70% R@5, marginally surpassing Samp4G (98.68% R@1). On the more challenging CVACT-Test split, it reaches 73.68% R@1 and 93.53% R@5, outperforming Samp4G (71.51% R@1) by over 2 points. On VIGOR-Cross-area, the method achieves 72.19% R@1, a 10.49-point improvement over Samp4G (61.70%) and a 39.14-point improvement over SAIG-D (33.05%). In cross-dataset generalization (CVUSA→CVACT-Test), the method reaches 44.10% R@1 versus 27.78% for Samp4G, demonstrating strong transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines across every reported benchmark. Against the strongest prior method Samp4G [3], it gains +0.03% R@1 on CVUSA, +1.09% on CVACT-Val, and +2.17% on CVACT-Test, with the largest margin on VIGOR-Cross-area (+10.49% R@1). SAIG-D [35] is surpassed by +2.63% R@1 on CVUSA and +4.19% on CVACT-Val, but the gap widens dramatically on VIGOR-Cross-area where SAIG-D drops to 33.05% R@1 versus 72.19% for the proposed method. GeoDTR† [30] with polar transformation trails by +3.28% R@1 on CVUSA and +9.16% on CVACT-Test. In cross-dataset generalization (CVUSA→CVACT-Test), the method achieves 44.10% R@1 versus 27.78% for Samp4G and 22.09% for GeoDTR†, a 16.32-point advantage over the previous best. The ablation (Table 5) confirms that the BEV branch alone underperforms the panorama branch on VIGOR (65.47% vs. 76.39% R@1 Same-area), but their combination yields the best result (82.18%), validating the co-retrieval design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the dominant transformation direction in cross-view geo-localization: instead of converting satellite images into polar views (as in SAFA, TransGeo, L2LTR), it transforms street view panoramas into explicit Bird's Eye View images using a ground-plane geometric back-projection, borrowing the BEV concept from autonomous driving localization (OrienterNet, Boosting) but applying it as a pixel-level image warp rather than a feature-level projection. The dual-branch co-retrieval design—combining the original panorama-to-satellite path with the new BEV-to-satellite path—departs from prior single-branch retrieval methods (Samp4G, GeoDTR, SAIG-D) by explicitly addressing the complementary strengths of global layout (panorama) versus local detail (BEV) matching. The CVGlobal dataset extends the evaluation landscape beyond the established CVUSA/CVACT/VIGOR benchmarks by introducing unfixed street-view orientations, cross-temporal, cross-continent, and map-data retrieval settings absent from all prior datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CVGlobal dataset fills a genuine gap: prior benchmarks assume fixed orientations, single-year data, and satellite-only references, none of which hold in real deployment. The finding that BEV-only retrieval outperforms panorama-only retrieval in cross-dataset generalization (Table 4) suggests that geometric perspective alignment is more transferable than raw visual information richness, a useful principle for future cross-domain matching. However, the dramatic performance collapse in cross-continent (R@1 drops from ~85% to ~60%) and cross-temporal (R@1 drops to ~74% for 2013 queries) settings reveals that current methods still rely heavily on appearance consistency, leaving open the question of how to achieve truly appearance-invariant geo-localization. The map-data retrieval task, while novel, shows that texture-free references remain a significant challenge, with test R@1 barely reaching 50%.
