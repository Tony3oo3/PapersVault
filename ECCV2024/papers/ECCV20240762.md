## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves high-quality, real-time scene reconstruction but models only appearance and geometry, lacking fine-grained object-level understanding. Existing NeRF-based 3D segmentation methods are computationally expensive, difficult to decompose for local editing, and struggle to generalize to open-world scenes without costly 3D annotations. This paper asks whether the discrete, explicit nature of 3D Gaussians can support both anything-in-3D segmentation and efficient local scene editing without 3D mask labels.

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view RGB images and automatically generated SAM masks in 'everything mode' as input. Cross-view mask consistency is established by treating the image sequence as a video and propagating labels with the DEVA tracker, yielding a total of K instance/stuff identities. The output is a set of grouped 3D Gaussians, each carrying a 16-dimensional Identity Encoding (SH degree 0, view-independent) that encodes its object membership, enabling per-group operations for segmentation, novel-view rendering at ~170 FPS, and local scene editing.

## Method

Each 3D Gaussian is augmented with a learnable 16-dim Identity Encoding vector, optimized jointly with standard Gaussian parameters via differentiable splatting. During rendering, the identity vectors are α-blended to 2D (analogous to color), then passed through a shared linear layer (16→256) and softmax for K-class identity classification against the SAM mask labels (2D cross-entropy loss). A 3D Regularization Loss (KL divergence between a Gaussian's encoding and its k=5 nearest 3D neighbors) supplements the 2D supervision to handle occluded or interior Gaussians. For editing, a Local Gaussian Editing scheme freezes all but the target group: removal deletes Gaussians, inpainting adds new Gaussians guided by LaMa 2D fills, colorization tunes only SH coefficients, and style transfer additionally unfreezes positions and sizes.

## Results

On the LERF-Mask open-vocabulary segmentation benchmark, Gaussian Grouping achieves mIoU of 69.7/77.0/71.7 and mBIoU of 67.9/68.7/66.1 on the figurines, ramen, and teatime scenes respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline LangSplat (52.8/50.4/69.5 mIoU) by 16.9/26.6/2.2 points. On 3D panoptic segmentation, Gaussian Grouping reaches 71.15% mIoU and 66.52% PQ_scene on Replica, and 68.70% mIoU and 61.83% PQ_scene on ScanNet, surpassing Panoptic Lifting while rendering at ~140–150 FPS versus ~10 FPS. The added Identity Encoding causes only a negligible reconstruction quality drop (PSNR 28.43 vs. 28.69 for vanilla Gaussian Splatting) while enabling full-scene segmentation and editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gaussian Grouping decisively outperforms all open-vocabulary 3D segmentation baselines on LERF-Mask: it beats LangSplat by 16.9 mIoU on figurines and 26.6 mIoU on ramen, and surpasses DEVA, LERF, and SA3D by wide margins across all three scenes. On panoptic segmentation (Table 4), it improves over Panoptic Lifting by ~5 mIoU on Replica (71.15 vs. 66.22) and ~1.7 mIoU on ScanNet (68.70 vs. 67.01), while rendering 14× faster (~140 FPS vs. ~10 FPS). For 3D object removal (Fig. 9), Gaussian Grouping cleanly separates large objects (truck, train) from the background, whereas DFFs [18] either removes the entire foreground or leaves visible artifacts. In 3D object inpainting (Fig. 10), Gaussian Grouping requires only 1 h training + 20 min fine-tuning versus 5 h for SPIn-NeRF [33], while producing better multi-view coherence. For style transfer (Fig. 12), Gaussian Grouping preserves background spatial detail far better than Instruct-NeRF2NeRF [11], which blurs large portions of the scene outside the edited object. The only trade-off is a slight reconstruction quality drop (PSNR 28.43 vs. 28.69, ~30 FPS reduction) relative to vanilla Gaussian Splatting [14], which the authors attribute to the additional Identity Encoding parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from pure geometry and appearance reconstruction to fine-grained scene understanding by augmenting each Gaussian with a 16-dimensional Identity Encoding supervised via differentiable rendering. It departs from NeRF-based open-world segmentation methods (LERF, Panoptic Lifting, SA3D) by using a discrete, grouped Gaussian representation that enables direct per-object manipulation without retraining, and from prior SAM-in-3D works (SA3D, SAM3D) by operating in automatic 'everything' mode across the full scene rather than targeting single objects. The cross-view mask association via DEVA tracking replaces the expensive per-iteration cost-based linear assignment used in Panoptic Lifting, yielding a 60× training speedup.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying reconstruction and segmentation in a single discrete representation, Gaussian Grouping makes 3D scene editing (removal, inpainting, colorization, style transfer, recomposition) practical and composable—multiple edits can be applied concurrently without retraining the global scene. The near-zero reconstruction cost of adding segmentation (PSNR 28.69→28.43) suggests this pattern of attaching compact identity encodings to explicit 3D primitives could generalize to other editing or understanding tasks. Key limitations include restriction to static scenes and dependence on SAM's 2D mask quality; open questions include extending to dynamic scenes, fully unsupervised grouping, and whether the 16-dim encoding scales to very large scenes with hundreds of instances.
