## Problem

Event-based semantic segmentation requires dense pixel-level ground truth maps that are extremely difficult to annotate on asynchronous, spatially sparse event data, where object boundaries are ambiguous due to over- or under-triggered events. This paper introduces the first weakly supervised formulation for event cameras (EV-WSSS), replacing dense labels with sparse point annotations (one click per class) to dramatically reduce annotation cost while still producing full segmentation maps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241064/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes event streams stacked into voxel grids at two temporal scales: a forward grid E^f over [T−τ, T] (used at inference) and a longer backward grid E^b over [T, T+τ'] with τ' = 5τ (training only, reversed to align the target time). Supervision comes from sparse point labels in a 1-class-1-click (1C1C) or 1-class-10-click (1C10C) format, where annotators click one (or ten) pixel(s) per existing class on a visualized event frame. The output is a C×H×W semantic segmentation logit map (e.g., 11 classes at 640×440 for DSEC, 346×200 for DDD17).

## Method

The core framework is an asymmetric dual-student architecture: two separate recurrent encoders and U-Net-like decoders process the forward and backward (reversed, longer) event streams, and each branch's thresholded prediction serves as a pseudo-GT to supervise the other via cross-entropy (L_dual), exploiting complementary past/future temporal information without feature fusion. To compensate for the extreme sparsity of point labels, the paper introduces feature-level prototype-based contrastive learning using InfoNCE, where class-wise prototypes are aggregated at three levels: intra-level (reliability-weighted features from confident regions within a single sample), inter-level (a memory queue averaging prototypes across samples), and dual-level (prototypes projected across branches via learned linear translators trained with an L1 distillation loss). The final contrastive loss pulls each pixel's feature toward its class prototype (determined by the opposite branch's pseudo-GT) and away from all others, while the dual-level aggregation fuses the original and cross-projected prototypes to enrich each branch's representation.

## Results

On DDD17, EV-WSSS with the 1C10C setting (10 points per class) achieves 60.39 mIoU, surpassing all unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) baselines (best: ESS at 53.09) and approaching fully supervised methods (HALSIE 60.66, ESS-sup 61.37). On DSEC-Semantic, EV-WSSS(1C10C) reaches 51.19 mIoU, outperforming the best UDA method (ESS at 45.38) by 5.8 points. On the newly introduced DSEC Night-Point dataset, the full EV-WSSS framework achieves 36.4 mIoU, substantially outperforming ESS (14.0 mIoU) which struggles with nighttime event noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DDD17, EV-WSSS(1C10C) at 60.39 mIoU surpasses all UDA baselines by a wide margin (ESS 53.09, E2VID 48.47, Vid2E 45.48, EV-Transfer 15.52) and is within 0.27 points of HALSIE (60.66) and 0.98 points of ESS-sup (61.37), both fully supervised. On DSEC-Semantic, EV-WSSS(1C10C) at 51.19 mIoU exceeds the best UDA method ESS (45.38) by 5.8 points and approaches HMNet-L (55.00) and ESS-sup (53.29). The 1C1C setting (one point per class) on DDD17 (56.88) already surpasses all UDA methods and even exceeds several fully supervised approaches like Ev-SegNet (54.81), PVT-FPN (53.89), and EvSegformer (54.41). On the challenging DSEC Night-Point dataset, EV-WSSS (36.4 mIoU) dramatically outperforms ESS (14.0 mIoU), which fails due to domain shift in nighttime events, demonstrating the robustness of the event-only weakly supervised approach. The paper notes that direct comparison with fully supervised and UDA methods is not strictly apples-to-apples since EV-WSSS requires no source domain or dense GT, but the competitive numbers validate its practicality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines: event-based semantic segmentation (which has relied on dense GT or source-domain UDA) and image-domain weakly supervised segmentation (which has explored class tags, points, scribbles, and boxes). It departs from prior event-based work by eliminating the need for paired images or source-domain pre-training, and from image-domain WSSS by addressing the unique spatial sparsity and temporal structure of event streams. The asymmetric dual-student architecture (forward vs. elongated reversed event streams) and the three-level prototype aggregation (intra-sample, inter-sample memory, cross-branch distillation) are novel formulations that repurpose contrastive learning and mutual learning ideas specifically for the event modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single point per class (1C1C) yields performance competitive with UDA methods that require an entire labeled source domain, and 10 points per class (1C10C) approaches fully supervised results on DDD17 (60.39 vs. 61.37 mIoU). The newly released DSEC Night-Point dataset addresses a real edge case (nighttime events) where UDA methods collapse (14.0 mIoU) but E-WSSS remains functional (36.4 mIoU). Open questions include whether the dual-student scheme generalizes to other weak label types (scribbles, boxes), whether the approach scales to higher-resolution event sensors, and whether the prototype queue mechanism can be made more efficient for long-sequence deployment.
