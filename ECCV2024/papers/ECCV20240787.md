## Problem

State-of-the-art video deblurring models trained on synthetic datasets (e.g., GoPro) suffer substantial performance drops when deployed on real-world videos with different blur patterns arising from varying shutter speeds, apertures, and lighting. Prior domain adaptation attempts for deblurring either ignore temporal motion cues in consecutive frames or rely on GAN-based blurring that does not produce blur conditions matching the target domain's distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of blurred video frames from an unseen domain (no ground-truth sharp frames required) and produces a domain-adapted deblurring model. Internally, it extracts 256×256 pseudo-sharp patches from the blurred video and generates corresponding blurred versions via ID-Blau, forming pseudo-training pairs used to fine-tune the deblurring model for 10 epochs. The adapted model then outputs deblurred video frames at the original resolution.

## Method

The core idea is to exploit motion cues implicitly encoded in consecutive blurred frames to synthesize domain-specific blur conditions for a diffusion-based blurring model (ID-Blau). A Relative Sharpness Detection Module (RSDM) uses a Blur Magnitude Estimator (a 5-stage encoder-decoder with multi-scale feature fusion, trained on GoPro) to predict per-pixel blur magnitude maps and select the top 20% relatively sharp patches as pseudo-sharp images. A Domain-adaptive Blur Condition Generation Module (DBCGM) computes pixel-wise blur orientations via RAFT optical flow between neighboring collocated patches and modulates blur magnitudes by averaging BME predictions from adjacent frames, producing a continuous blur condition field (x, y, z) tailored to the target domain. ID-Blau then generates blurred versions of the pseudo-sharp patches under these conditions, and the resulting pseudo-sharp/blurred pairs fine-tune the deblurring model. Only the BME is trained (on GoPro with L1 loss); all other components operate at test time without additional training.

## Results

The proposed domain adaptation scheme consistently improves four state-of-the-art video deblurring models (ESTRNN, MMP-RNN, DSTNet, Shift-Net) on five real-world datasets, with average PSNR gains of 4.61 dB (BSD-1ms8ms), 3.90 dB (BSD-2ms16ms), 2.57 dB (BSD-3ms24ms), 1.92 dB (RealBlur), and 2.31 dB (RBVD). The maximum per-model gain reaches 7.54 dB for MMP-RNN, 3.87 dB for ESTRNN, 3.75 dB for Shift-Net, and 3.61 dB for DSTNet. For example, ESTRNN on BSD-1ms8ms improves from 25.57 dB to 29.44 dB PSNR, and Shift-Net on the same dataset improves from 25.00 dB to 28.75 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only prior domain-adaptation method for video deblurring is Liu et al. [29] (Meta Transferring for Deblurring, BMVC 2022), which uses a GAN-supervised blurring model with meta-learning. The proposed method outperforms Liu et al. on all five datasets using ESTRNN as the backbone: BSD-1ms8ms (29.44 vs. 25.58 dB, +3.86 dB), BSD-2ms16ms (28.36 vs. 24.53 dB, +3.83 dB), BSD-3ms24ms (28.32 vs. 25.15 dB, +3.17 dB), RealBlur (27.64 vs. 26.12 dB, +1.52 dB), and RBVD (26.83 vs. 24.83 dB, +2.00 dB). Notably, Liu et al. actually degrades performance on BSD-2ms16ms (24.53 vs. 24.64 baseline) and BSD-3ms24ms (25.15 vs. 26.01 baseline), whereas the proposed method improves over baseline on every dataset. The key advantage is that the proposed DBCGM leverages temporal motion cues from consecutive test frames to generate domain-specific blur conditions, whereas Liu et al. does not exploit temporal information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ID-Blau (Wu et al., CVPR 2024), a conditional diffusion-based blurring model, repurposing it as a test-time data generator rather than a training-time augmentation tool. It departs from the closest prior domain-adaptation work for deblurring (Liu et al., BMVC 2022), which used a GAN to generate blurred images for meta-learning, by instead extracting domain-specific blur orientations and magnitudes from temporal motion cues (optical flow) in the test video itself. The method is positioned as a plug-in calibration layer on top of existing video deblurring architectures (ESTRNN, MMP-RNN, DSTNet, Shift-Net), rather than proposing a new deblurring backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The domain gap between synthetic training data (GoPro) and real-world blurred videos is a practical bottleneck that limits deployment of video deblurring models; this paper shows that test-time fine-tuning with self-generated, domain-matched pseudo-pairs can close much of that gap without requiring ground-truth sharp frames. The model-agnostic nature (consistent gains across four architectures) makes the approach broadly applicable to future deblurring methods. Open questions include whether the pseudo-sharp assumption degrades under extreme blur, the computational cost of 10-epoch test-time fine-tuning in real-time settings, and whether the same temporal-cue-driven blurring strategy transfers to other degradation types (e.g., defocus, defocus+motion).
