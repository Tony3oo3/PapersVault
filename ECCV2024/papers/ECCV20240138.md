## Problem

Existing learning-based HSI reconstruction methods rarely exploit both the spatial sparsity and inter-spectral similarity priors of hyperspectral data simultaneously, and they treat all image regions equally during training despite texture-rich and edge regions being substantially harder to reconstruct. Additionally, Transformer-based approaches suffer from quadratic computational complexity in spatial size and waste computation on spectrally sparse regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D CASSI measurement (e.g., 256×310 for simulation with 28 spectral bands, or 660×714 for real data) as input. Before network processing, the measurement is un-sheared by reversing the dispersion shift to produce an initialized 3D signal of size H×W×N_λ, which is then fed through the network. The output is a reconstructed 3D hyperspectral cube of the same spatial and spectral dimensions as the ground truth.

## Method

Specformer builds a Spatial-Frequency (SF) block that runs a spatial-wise local-window self-attention (LWSA) module and a frequency-wise self-attention (FWSA) module in parallel. FWSA applies 2D FFT, multiplies by a learnable global frequency filter, and applies IFFT to model inter-spectral long-range dependencies at linear complexity, while LWSA captures spatial sparsity within local windows; the parallel design enables cross-window connections and enlarges receptive fields without quadratic cost. SF-blocks are stacked in a multi-scale U-shape encoder-decoder with residual skip connections. Training uses a two-stage uncertainty-driven loss: the first stage learns a per-pixel uncertainty map via a sparse-uncertainty loss with Jeffrey's prior, and the second stage uses the ranked uncertainty as spatially adaptive weights to up-weight texture-rich and edge regions in the L1+SSIM reconstruction loss, without altering the inference-time model.

## Results

On the KAIST dataset (10 test scenes, 28 spectral bands), Specformer achieves an average PSNR of 40.61 dB and SSIM of 0.981, outperforming the strongest baseline RDLUF (39.57 dB / 0.974) by 1.04 dB in PSNR. The method also requires fewer parameters (2.48M) and lower FLOPs (39.85G) than all compared Transformer-based methods. On real CASSI measurements (660×660, 28 channels), Specformer produces visibly sharper texture and edge reconstruction with better noise suppression than all 7 compared SOTA methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Specformer wins on both PSNR and SSIM across all 10 KAIST test scenes against all 11 baselines. The largest margins are over model-based methods (GAP-TV: +16.25 dB, DeSCI: +15.34 dB) and CNN-based methods (DGSMP: +7.98 dB, TSA-Net: +9.15 dB). Among Transformer-based competitors, Specformer leads RDLUF by 1.04 dB PSNR and 0.007 SSIM, PADUT by 1.72 dB, DAUHST by 2.25 dB, CST by 4.49 dB, and MST by 5.43 dB. Notably, Specformer achieves these gains with fewer parameters (2.48M) and lower FLOPs (39.85G) than RDLUF, PADUT, and DAUHST, as highlighted in the PSNR-Params-FLOPs scatter plot (Fig. 1). On real CASSI measurements, Specformer also produces superior texture/edge fidelity and noise suppression compared to all 7 visualized SOTA methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes ideas from three distinct lines of work: (1) Transformer-based HSI reconstruction methods (MST, CST, DAUHST, PADUT) that model long-range dependencies via self-attention; (2) the Swin Transformer's local-window self-attention for reducing quadratic complexity; and (3) Fourier-domain filtering (à la FALCON) as a lightweight global operation. The key conceptual departure is the parallel combination of spatial (LWSA) and spectral (FWSA) attention within a single block, explicitly motivated by the dual nature of HSI data—spatially sparse yet spectrally self-similar—whereas prior Transformer methods addressed only one of these priors. The uncertainty-driven loss also inverts the typical Bayesian uncertainty usage: rather than down-weighting high-uncertainty pixels as unreliable, it up-weights them to prioritize texture-rich and edge regions during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or superior reconstruction quality at substantially lower FLOPs and parameter counts than prior Transformer-based methods, making HSI reconstruction more accessible on resource-constrained hardware. The FWSA module (FFT + learnable filter + IFFT) is a simple, reusable primitive that could transfer to other spectral or multi-channel reconstruction tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to CASSI with 28 wavelengths on two simulation datasets and five real scenes; generalization to other SCI systems (e.g., DISC, CASSI with different dispersions), higher spectral resolutions, or dynamic scenes remains untested. An open question is whether the uncertainty-driven loss's benefit persists when the network is scaled up or when the training data distribution shifts significantly.
