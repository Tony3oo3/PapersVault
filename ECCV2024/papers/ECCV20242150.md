## Problem

State-of-the-art backdoor defenses (ANP, AWM, I-BAU) rely on computationally expensive adversarial search to reverse-engineer trigger distributions or require over-sensitive hyper-parameter tuning, and they degrade significantly under limited validation data and strong attacks. This paper asks whether a simple data augmentation can replace the trigger synthesis process entirely while preserving clean test accuracy during purification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained backdoored DNN (any architecture: CNN, CNN+LSTM, PointNet++) and a small clean validation set as small as one sample per class (one-shot) or 1% of the training set. It outputs a purified model whose parameters are the original weights element-wise multiplied by learned per-neuron scalar masks M, with biases left unchanged. The masks are constrained to lie in [μ(l), 1] per layer, where μ(l) is an exponential scheduling function, ensuring only bounded modifications to the original model.

## Method

NFT replaces adversarial trigger synthesis with MixUp augmentation over the clean validation set, and proves (Theorem 1) that the resulting MixUp loss upper-bounds the ideal purification loss for ReLU/max-pooling networks, so minimizing it effectively removes the backdoor. Instead of fine-tuning weights (which overfits under limited validation data), NFT optimizes a set of soft neural masks—one scalar per neuron per layer—that reprogram backdoor-affected neurons while keeping the original model structure intact. A mask regularizer (η_c · ||M_0 − M||_1, with η_c = 5e-4/n_c) penalizes deviation from the initial all-ones masks to preserve clean-cluster separability, and an exponential scheduling function μ(l) = α·e^(−β·l) provides layer-dependent lower bounds that shrink the search space for deeper layers. The final objective is argmin_M L_mix(θ⊙M, D_val) + η_c||M_0−M||_1 subject to m_l^(i) ∈ [μ(l),1], optimized with SGD (lr=0.05, momentum=0.95) for 100 epochs.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 across 14 backdoor attacks, NFT achieves an average ASR drop of 95.56% while sacrificing only 1.81% clean accuracy, outperforming all baselines on both metrics. On ImageNet, NFT achieves a 94.03% average ASR drop with a 2.84% ACC drop, again the best among all compared methods. NFT also runs ~4× faster than adversarial-search-based defenses (28.4 s vs. 92–118 s per attack on an RTX 3090). In the one-shot setting (1 sample per class), NFT still reduces ASR to ~5% on Dynamic, WaNet, and LIRA attacks while retaining >80% clean accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFT outperforms all five baselines on both ASR reduction and ACC preservation across CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. On CIFAR-10, NFT achieves a 95.56% average ASR drop with only 1.81% ACC loss, compared to ANP (92.63% / 5.94%), I-BAU (88.10% / 4.66%), AWM (91.21% / 3.71%), FT-SAM (92.61% / 4.26%), and RNP (92.06% / 2.95%). The gap is most pronounced against I-BAU, which relies on implicit hypergradient-based trigger synthesis and struggles with imperceptible triggers (e.g., LIRA: I-BAU ASR 4.51% vs. NFT 1.53%). On ImageNet, NFT's advantage over AWM narrows slightly on ASR (94.03% vs. 90.01%) but NFT still preserves 2.84% more clean accuracy. In the one-shot regime (10 validation samples), AWM's ASR degrades severely (e.g., 86.74% on Dynamic) while NFT maintains ~5% ASR, confirming that MixUp-based augmentation is more robust than adversarial-search trigger approximation under data scarcity. NFT also runs in 28.4 s per attack versus 92–118 s for the search-based methods, a ~4× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

NFT departs from the dominant paradigm in test-time backdoor defenses (ANP, AWM, I-BAU) that rely on computationally expensive adversarial search to reverse-engineer trigger distributions before fine-tuning. Instead, it repurposes the well-known MixUp augmentation as a theoretical and practical substitute for trigger synthesis, proving via Theorem 1 that the MixUp loss upper-bounds the ideal purification loss for ReLU/maxpooling networks. The method further diverges from prior fine-tuning-based defenses (FT-SAM, vanilla fine-tuning) by optimizing soft neural masks rather than raw weights, combined with a novel mask regularizer and an exponential scheduling function to control model drift. This positions NFT as a lightweight alternative that avoids both adversarial search and specialized optimizers like SAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: NFT achieves SOTA purification across 14 attacks on 11 datasets spanning 5 tasks while running ~4x faster than adversarial-search-based methods and requiring as few as one clean sample per class. The theoretical guarantee (MixUp loss as upper bound on ideal purification loss) provides a principled justification for why simple augmentation suffices, reducing the field's reliance on heuristic trigger recovery. Open questions include whether the binary-classification, ReLU-specific theoretical analysis extends to multi-class settings with other activations, whether the hand-designed exponential mask scheduling can be learned end-to-end, and how the method performs against adaptive attacks that are aware of the MixUp-based purification strategy.
