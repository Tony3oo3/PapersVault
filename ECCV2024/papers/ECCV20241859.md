## Problem

No-reference image quality assessment (NR-IQA) is severely constrained by the scarcity of labeled data, with the largest authentic dataset (FLIVE) containing fewer than 40,000 images. Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for IQA cannot quantify the degree of distortion during training, causing performance bottlenecks, while patch-based data augmentation introduces non-linear quality estimation errors. This paper addresses both limitations by proposing a distortion-aware data augmentation strategy that generates diverse training samples with quantitatively meaningful labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241859/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single distorted RGB image as input and produces a scalar quality score (MOS or DMOS depending on the dataset). During pre-training, images are randomly cropped to 224×224 patches from the 700k-image KADIS synthetic dataset; during evaluation, five 224×224 patches (four corners and center) are extracted from each full-resolution image and their predicted scores are averaged. The pre-trained Quality Encoder (ResNet-50) is frozen at inference time, and only a lightweight MLP regression head is trained via linear probing on the target IQA dataset.

## Method

DSMix performs cut-and-mix augmentation on pairs of distorted images with different distortion types, using a pre-trained ViT-B/8 to predict a distortion-induced sensitivity map (DSM) for the mixed image; the soft class label is computed as a weighted combination of the two source labels, where the weight λ is the area ratio of the DSM region attributed to each source image. The Quality Encoder (ResNet-50) is pre-trained on these DSMix-augmented samples with a soft-target cross-entropy loss over 126 distortion type/level classes, simultaneously receiving semantic knowledge distillation from an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50 teacher via feature reconstruction (MAE on stages 2–3) and cosine distance (on stage 4) losses applied only to reference images. At evaluation, the frozen encoder feeds a trainable MLP head trained with Smooth L1 loss (linear probing), requiring no fine-tuning of the backbone. The entire pipeline runs on a single NVIDIA 3090 GPU, and the DSMix augmentation is shown to be plug-and-play, improving existing methods like HyperIQA and TReS when their pre-trained weights are replaced.

## Results

DSMix achieves SOTA SRCC on all four synthetic distortion datasets: LIVE (0.974), CSIQ (0.957), TID2013 (0.906), and KADID (0.943), using only linear probing without fine-tuning the backbone. On authentic datasets, it reaches 0.646 SRCC / 0.735 PLCC on FLIVE (best SRCC, tied with MUSIQ), 0.873 SRCC / 0.883 PLCC on CLIVE, and 0.915 SRCC / 0.925 PLCC on KonIQ-10k. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, in contrast to Re-IQA (18× A100) and QPT (8× V100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-paradigm baseline Re-IQA (also pre-training + linear probing), DSMix wins on all seven datasets, with the largest margins on TID2013 (+0.102 SRCC) and KADID (+0.071 SRCC), and a narrow edge on FLIVE (+0.001 SRCC). Against CONTRIQUE (pre-training + ridge regression), DSMix leads on all synthetic datasets (e.g., +0.063 on TID2013) and on FLIVE (+0.066 SRCC), but trails on CLIVE (0.873 vs. 0.845) and KonIQ (0.915 vs. 0.894). Against QPT (pre-training + fine-tuning, 8× V100), DSMix achieves comparable or better FLIVE SRCC (0.646 vs. 0.610) while using only a single 3090 GPU and no fine-tuning, though QPT edges ahead on CLIVE (0.895) and KonIQ (0.927) SRCC. Against TOPIQ (from-scratch Transformer), DSMix matches or exceeds it on FLIVE (0.646 vs. 0.633 SRCC) and CLIVE (0.873 vs. 0.870), but TOPIQ is slightly ahead on KonIQ (0.926 vs. 0.915). The plug-and-play experiment (Table 3) further shows that swapping in DSMix pre-trained weights improves HyperIQA by +0.087 SRCC on FLIVE and TReS by +0.079 SRCC on FLIVE, confirming the augmentation's generalizability across architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of data augmentation, self-supervised pre-training, and knowledge distillation for NR-IQA, rather than extending a single prior method. It departs from prior self-supervised IQA approaches (CONTRIQUE, Re-IQA, QPT) that cannot quantify distortion degree during training, by introducing DSM-based confidence-weighted labels in a cut-and-mix augmentation scheme. It also diverges from the dominant ImageNet-pretrain-then-fine-tune paradigm (DBCNN, MetaIQA, HyperIQA) by performing IQA-specific pre-training followed by linear probing only, while borrowing semantic knowledge from an ImageNet teacher via feature-level distillation. The ViT-based DSM prediction module draws on the Transformer architecture but is repurposed as a distortion-sensitivity estimator rather than a feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving SOTA NR-IQA performance with only linear probing (no fine-tuning) on a single RTX 3090 GPU, compared to 8 V100s or 18 A100s for competing self-supervised methods, making high-quality IQA accessible to resource-constrained labs. The plug-and-play nature—swapping pre-trained weights into existing methods like HyperIQA and TReS yields consistent gains—suggests DSMix could serve as a general pre-training backbone for the IQA community. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video quality assessment, whether the DSM-based weighting has a principled perceptual justification beyond empirical success, and whether the modest SOTA margins (e.g., 0.646 vs. 0.645 SRCC on FLIVE) will hold as larger models and datasets become available.
