## Problem

Existing adversarial erasing-based methods for weakly supervised semantic segmentation face a fundamental dilemma: removing discriminative object regions to discover new areas leads to over-expansion when erasure is excessive, yet insufficient erasure causes under-activation. There is no principled criterion for when to stop erasing, making it difficult to balance complete object discovery against compact, accurate localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241128/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images paired with image-level class labels (no pixel annotations) and produces pixel-level segmentation masks via a two-stage pipeline. In the first stage, a ResNet38 classification network with a Gated Pyramid Pooling head generates class activation maps (CAMs) at the spatial resolution of the backbone feature maps; in the second stage, refined pseudo-labels train a DeepLabV2 segmentation network (ResNet101 or VGG16 backbone) to output dense per-pixel class predictions. A key input detail is that the SIE module requires a paired image concatenated alongside the anchor image to form a synthetic larger input, with no rotation or resizing applied to preserve controlled activation weakening.

## Method

The core insight inverts the adversarial erasing paradigm: instead of masking the most discriminative region to force discovery of new areas, the Simulated Inter-Image Erasing (SIE) module concatenates a paired image to the anchor image so that the anchor's activation is naturally weakened by the presence of extra object information, then transfers object knowledge from the well-activated anchor CAM to the less-activated simulated branch via a ReLU-based alignment loss. A Self-Supervised Regularization (SSR) module prevents the bidirectional alignment from degrading the anchor's reliable activation by generating pseudo-labels with dual thresholds and applying a cross-entropy loss, plus an inter-class loss that penalizes the gap between the top-1 and top-2 channel activations to sharpen multi-class boundaries. A Multi-Granularity Alignment (MGA) module performs gentle intra-image erasing (threshold 0.6) and uses image-level global alignment (GAP on class-extracted features) to guide the masked branch's confidence, followed by pixel-level local alignment to transfer newly discovered object regions back to the anchor. The SIE and MGA branches operate symmetrically as constraining and expanding forces, respectively, jointly producing complete yet compact object regions without the over-expansion or under-activation of prior erasing methods.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2012 with a ResNet backbone and only image-level labels, KTSE achieves 73.0% mIoU on the validation set and 72.9% on the test set, outperforming the previous SOTA (OCR at 72.7%/72.0%) by approximately 1%. On the more challenging COCO dataset (80 classes), KTSE reaches 45.9% mIoU with ResNet, surpassing ACR (45.3%) and BECO (45.1%). For pseudo-mask generation, the method produces seeds at 67.0% mIoU and refined pseudo-masks at 73.8% (with IRN), exceeding the prior best of 72.3% (ACR) by 1.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KTSE outperforms all image-label-only baselines on both VOC 2012 and COCO. On VOC 2012 (ResNet, test set), it beats the closest competitors OCR (72.0%) and ACR (71.9%) by 0.9–1.0%, and BECO (71.8%) by 1.1%; the gap widens to 2.8% over FPR (70.1%) and 2.4% over TOCO (70.5%). On COCO (ResNet), KTSE leads ACR by 0.6% and BECO by 0.8%, with a larger 2.0% margin over FPR (43.9%). The method also surpasses saliency-assisted approaches (e.g., PPC at 73.6% test, L2G at 71.7% test) using only image-level labels, and significantly outperforms the CutMix-based augmentation method CDA (66.1% val) by 6.9% on VOC. The pseudo-mask seed accuracy of 67.0% exceeds FPR (63.8%) by 3.2% and ACR (60.3%) by 6.7%, indicating the advantage stems primarily from superior CAM generation rather than the downstream segmentation network.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the dominant adversarial erasing paradigm in WSSS (ACoL, AEFT, CSE), which masks discriminative regions to force discovery of complementary object parts. Rather than removing information, KTSE introduces extra object information via image concatenation (simulated inter-image erasing), weakening the anchor activation and then transferring knowledge back from the well-activated anchor CAM. The framework builds on CAM-based localization [71], adopts the GPP pooling head and erasing threshold from AEFT [67], and uses the pixel-adaptive refinement module [47] for pseudo-label generation, but the core conceptual departure—adding discriminative information to weaken rather than remove it to strengthen—represents a genuine reframing of the erasing problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important dilemma in adversarial erasing methods: the lack of a principled stopping criterion leads to either under-activation or over-expansion. The symmetric SIE/MGA design (constraining vs. expanding) provides a more controlled mechanism for activation enlargement, and the self-supervised regularization with inter-class boundary loss is a useful addition for multi-category images. However, the method remains tied to the CAM-based three-step WSSS pipeline, and the gains over the strongest 2023 baselines (ACR, FPR, BECO) are modest (0.3–1.1% mIoU), suggesting the field may be approaching a plateau for this paradigm. Open questions include whether the simulated inter-image erasing idea generalizes beyond image-level supervision or to other localization tasks.
