## Problem

Existing monocular depth estimation methods perform poorly on endoscopy videos, particularly on oblique and en-face views (40–50% of clinical data), because they rely on geometric and textural cues that are absent in internal organ imagery. The near-field illumination emitted by the co-located endoscope light provides strong photometric depth cues that prior methods largely ignore, and no prior work has designed loss functions or refinement architectures that explicitly exploit this per-pixel lighting information for depth estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB endoscopy frame together with camera intrinsics K and light-source parameters (position p, direction d, angular attenuation μ; in practice μ=0 for an isotropic point source co-located with the camera). It outputs a refined metric depth map D_ref. Internally, the pipeline first produces an initial depth D_init from a DPT decoder, analytically computes a Per-Pixel Shading (PPS) map from D_init and the surface normal, and then refines the depth via PPSNet. Specular regions (intensity > 0.98) are masked out during loss computation to avoid misleading lighting cues.

## Method

The core insight is to model the endoscope's near-field illumination as a Per-Pixel Shading field PPS(X) = A(X)·(L(X)·N(X)), which captures how incident light attenuates with distance and angle relative to surface geometry. Two loss functions exploit this: a supervised PPS loss (MSE between ground-truth and predicted PPS) for synthetic training, and a self-supervised PPS loss (1 − correlation between predicted PPS and masked image intensity) that requires no depth labels and enables training on ~18,000 real clinical frames. For refinement, PPSNet extracts DINOv2 features from both the RGB image and a proxy-albedo-modulated PPS map, fuses them via multi-headed cross-attention, modulates the initial depth through FiLM, and passes the result through a four-layer UNet to produce a residual ΔD added to D_init. A teacher-student transfer scheme bridges the Sim2Real gap: the Teacher (backbone + PPSNet) is trained on C3VD with full supervision, while the Student (same architecture) is trained interleaved on synthetic data (supervised) and unlabeled clinical data (self-supervised PPS loss + Teacher pseudo-ground-truth), yielding state-of-the-art results on C3VD and high-quality depth on real colonoscopy.

## Results

On the C3VD synthetic colonoscopy dataset, the proposed Ours-Student model (DINOv2 encoder, trained on both C3VD and ~18,000 clinical images) achieves RMSE 2.06, AbsRel 0.0491, SqRel 0.140, and δ<1.1 of 0.89, setting a new state-of-the-art. The Ours-Teacher variant (trained only on C3VD) achieves RMSE 2.15 and AbsRel 0.0529, already outperforming the previous best endoscopy-specific method LightDepth (RMSE 6.55, AbsRel 0.0780) by over 3× in RMSE. The backbone alone (Ours-Backbone, DINOv2) reaches RMSE 2.79, showing that even without PPS-based losses or refinement, the fine-tuned foundation model substantially exceeds prior endoscopy depth estimators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the closest endoscopy-specific competitor LightDepth [54] (RMSE 6.55, AbsRel 0.0780), Ours-Student reduces RMSE by 3.2× (2.06 vs 6.55) and AbsRel by 37% (0.0491 vs 0.0780), while also improving δ<1.1 from 0.56 to 0.89. The general-purpose Depth Anything [67] performs worst among all methods (RMSE 12.76, δ<1.1 0.34), confirming that natural-image depth models fail on endoscopy due to lack of geometric texture. Self-supervised endoscopy methods MonoDepth2 (RMSE 9.11) and NormDepth (RMSE 9.72) are 4–5× worse in RMSE than Ours-Student, and the supervised UNet baseline (RMSE 6.71) is still 3.3× behind. The ablation in Table 5 further shows that the PPS-based losses outperform LightDepth's rendering-based losses by 12% RMSE for the Teacher (2.15 vs 2.44) and 10% for the Student (2.06 vs 2.29), isolating the benefit of the per-pixel shading formulation over explicit illumination-decline reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the near-field photometric stereo line of work (Lichy et al. [32]) by repurposing the per-pixel lighting representation not merely as input to a surface-normal network but as the basis for both supervised and self-supervised loss functions and a depth-refinement module (PPSNet). It departs from LightDepth [54], which uses illumination decline through differentiable rendering for self-supervision, by operating directly on an analytically computed per-pixel shading field that avoids explicit rendering assumptions about albedo and direct lighting. The teacher-student transfer learning framework adapts the paradigm from Depth Anything [67]—which relies on 62M unlabeled images and semantic losses—to the endoscopy setting with only ~18K clinical images and lighting-based self-supervision, while building on the DINOv2 + DPT backbone architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a clinically critical gap: geometric-feature-based depth estimation fails on 40–50% of endoscopy frames (oblique and en-face views) where tubular structure cues are absent, yet photometric cues from the co-located light are always available. The approach is generalizable beyond colonoscopy to bronchoscopy and other near-field imaging, and the PPS-based loss and refinement module can be attached to any monocular depth estimator. Key limitations remain: the model does not explicitly handle albedo variation or specular reflections (only masks them), and the authors acknowledge that the primary performance driver is still depth supervision rather than the photometric self-supervision. Open questions include whether purely self-supervised training with PPS correlation loss can match supervised performance on large unlabeled clinical datasets, and whether modeling specular effects would further improve depth accuracy for downstream tasks like 3D mesh reconstruction.
