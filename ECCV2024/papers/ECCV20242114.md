## Problem

Existing joint diffusion methods for panorama generation (e.g., MultiDiffusion, SyncDiffusion) process overlapping views independently and combine them only at the end of each denoising step, yielding perceptually aligned but semantically incoherent outputs where objects morph across view boundaries (e.g., a mountain blending into clouds). Achieving semantic coherence—consistent object layout and meaningful spatial arrangement across the full panorama—remains an open challenge that prior methods do not address.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language text prompt and produces a single long panorama image (e.g., 512×3072, 512×4096, or vertical 2048×512). The target image is decomposed into overlapping 512×512 pixel views (64×64 latents) with a 3/4 overlap stride of 16 latent pixels; the number of views scales with the output aspect ratio. The approach is zero-shot and requires no retraining, operating entirely at inference time on a pretrained LDM (Stable Diffusion 2.0) or LCM (Latent Consistency Dreamshaper) backbone.

## Method

The core contribution is the Merge-Attend-Diffuse (MAD) operator, which is plugged into the self-attention and cross-attention layers of the U-Net noise prediction network. At each selected attention layer, MAD merges the latent feature vectors from all overlapping views into a single tensor by averaging the overlapped regions, performs the attention operation on this merged tensor (so all views jointly attend to each other and to the prompt embedding), and then splits the result back into per-view tensors for subsequent convolutional blocks. This introduces intra-step interaction points between views that are absent in standard joint diffusion, enforcing global semantic and perceptual coherence while preserving the local variability introduced by independently-processed convolutional layers. MAD is applied selectively: only for the first τ denoising steps (e.g., τ=15 of 50) and optionally only in specific U-Net blocks (Down, Mid, Up), providing a tunable variety-uniformity trade-off without any retraining.

## Results

On 512×3072 panorama generation with Stable Diffusion 2.0, MAD (τ=15) achieves the best mCLIP score of 32.03 among joint diffusion methods (vs. 31.77 for MultiDiffusion and 31.84 for SyncDiffusion), indicating superior prompt adherence. For intra-image perceptual coherence, MAD matches SyncDiffusion on I-LPIPS (0.56) and substantially outperforms MultiDiffusion (0.56 vs. 0.69). In a user study with 139 participants (7,807 answers), MAD was preferred 80.92% of the time over MultiDiffusion and 66.54% over SyncDiffusion. Runtime is 44.3 s, comparable to MultiDiffusion (41.8 s) and ~8× faster than SyncDiffusion (392.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MultiDiffusion, MAD (τ=15) wins on all coherence-relevant metrics: mCLIP (32.03 vs. 31.77), I-LPIPS (0.56 vs. 0.69), and I-StyleL (1.90 vs. 2.98), while runtime is nearly identical (44.3 s vs. 41.8 s). MultiDiffusion scores lower FID (33.52 vs. 48.52) and KID (9.04 vs. 27.15), but the authors argue these metrics reward crop-level similarity to square SD references rather than intra-image coherence. Against SyncDiffusion, MAD matches on I-LPIPS (0.56 vs. 0.56), wins on mCLIP (32.03 vs. 31.84) and mGIQA (28.32 vs. 27.17), but trails on I-StyleL (1.90 vs. 1.39). The decisive practical advantage is runtime: MAD is ~8× faster than SyncDiffusion (44.3 s vs. 392.4 s) because it avoids the expensive perceptual-loss gradient computation. On the GPT1k complex-scene benchmark (Table 3), MAD achieves the best mCLIP (32.47) and competitive I-LPIPS (0.58) among all joint diffusion methods, further confirming its advantage in semantic coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint diffusion paradigm for long-image generation introduced by MultiDiffusion [5] and perceptually guided by SyncDiffusion [30]. The key conceptual departure is moving view interaction from the end of each denoising step (where prior methods merely average overlapping latents) to *inside* the noise-prediction network, by reprogramming self- and cross-attention layers to operate on a merged latent tensor. This is implemented via the Merge-Attend-Diffuse (MAD) operator, a modular plug-in that can be selectively applied to chosen attention layers and timesteps, and is demonstrated on both LDM (Stable Diffusion) and LCM backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAD provides a simple, training-free mechanism to improve semantic coherence in panorama generation without the expensive gradient-based optimization required by SyncDiffusion, yielding an 8× speedup while matching or exceeding its perceptual coherence. The paper honestly acknowledges that the task is 'far from being solved' (GPT1k results show all methods still struggle with complex scenes) and that increasing MAD application trades distributional fidelity (FID/KID) for intra-image coherence, leaving open the question of how to achieve both simultaneously. The modular design (selectable layers, tunable τ threshold) gives practitioners a practical knob for the variety–uniformity trade-off, and the released GPT1k benchmark set may foster further exploration.
