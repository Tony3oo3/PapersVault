## Problem

Generalizable robot manipulation that deploys out-of-the-box in unseen scenes without test-time adaptation remains unsolved. Prior approaches either require massive in-domain robot interaction datasets (years to collect, still limited in task diversity) or test-time training that demands a human demo followed by hours of robot exploration. The paper asks whether abundant passive web videos of humans and robots manipulating everyday objects can be leveraged to learn a single goal-conditioned policy that generalizes to unseen tasks, objects, and scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242037/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The track prediction model takes an RGB scene image, a goal image, and a randomly sampled set of p points in the initial image, and outputs 2D trajectories of those points over a prediction horizon H (trained on 4–5 second clips). For robot deployment, a depth image of the initial frame and camera intrinsics are additionally consumed to convert the 2D tracks into per-timestep 3D rigid transforms of the manipulated object via PnP optimization. The final output is a sequence of end-effector poses (open-loop plan) plus per-step residual actions from the closed-loop policy, which are added to the open-loop waypoints at execution time.

## Method

The core contribution is a factorized policy: (1) a DiT-based diffusion model trained on ~400K web video clips (EpicKitchens, RT-1, BridgeData) with ground-truth tracks from Co-Tracker, which denoises future point tracks conditioned on embeddings of the initial image, goal image, and diffusion timestep; and (2) a residual policy trained via behavior cloning on only ~400 embodiment-specific Spot robot trajectories that predicts multi-step residual actions (h steps ahead, executing only the first) to correct the open-loop plan. The 2D tracks are converted to 3D rigid transforms by filtering moving points and solving a PnP optimization at each timestep, yielding an embodiment-agnostic open-loop end-effector trajectory. This design decouples scalable web-video learning (interaction plan) from minimal robot-specific data (residual correction), enabling a single goal-conditioned policy to generalize across unseen tasks, objects, and scenes without any deployment-time training.

## Results

On robot manipulation (Table 2), Track2Act achieves 70% success on Mild Generalization, 60% on Standard Generalization, 55% on Combinatorial Generalization, and 40% on Type Generalization, substantially outperforming all baselines at higher generalization levels. For track prediction (Table 1), the method achieves Δ scores of 0.67–0.77 across four datasets (EpicKitchens, SmthSmthv2, BridgeData, RT-1), roughly doubling the best baseline (Flow at 0.21–0.42). The open-loop variant (no residual policy) already reaches 25–35% success, demonstrating that web-video-trained track prediction alone transfers to robot execution without any embodiment-specific data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For track prediction, Track2Act (Δ = 0.67–0.77) substantially outperforms the flow-based baseline [67] (Δ = 0.21–0.42) and the video-infilling + Co-Tracker baseline [17] (Δ = 0.17–0.30), with the gap widening on human video datasets where non-linear object motions are more prevalent. For robot manipulation, Track2Act beats the Affordance-Conditioned BC [3] by 25–35 points on CG/TG (55%/40% vs. 10%/5%), showing that static affordance cues are insufficient without motion trajectory reasoning. Against Hand-Object Mask-Conditioned BC [4], Track2Act gains 15–20 points on CG/TG (55%/40% vs. 25%/20%), demonstrating that 2D point correspondences capture object state changes more effectively than binary masks. The Video-Conditioned baseline [17] achieves 0% on both CG and TG, confirming that hallucinated RGB videos introduce implausible artifacts that degrade downstream tracking. Notably, the open-loop variant (no residual policy, no robot data) already matches or exceeds Hand-Object Mask-Conditioned on CG (30% vs. 25%), validating the core insight that web-video track prediction alone provides transferable manipulation plans.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing off-the-shelf point-tracking (Co-Tracker) to generate supervision for a DiT-based diffusion model that predicts future 2D point trajectories from an initial image and a goal image, trained entirely on passive web videos (EpicKitchens, RT-1, BridgeData). Conceptually, it extends the interaction-plan factorization from Bharadhwaj et al. [4] (hand-object masks) to a more expressive and embodiment-agnostic representation (point tracks), while departing from approaches that predict full RGB videos [14,17] or static affordances [3] as intermediate plans. The residual policy component draws on behavior cloning with action chunking [5,71], but its role is narrowed to correcting a web-video-derived open-loop plan rather than learning the full policy from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is a 3–4× reduction in required embodiment-specific robot data (~400 trajectories vs. thousands in RT-1/RT-2-scale work) while achieving competitive generalization across unseen objects and scenes, and the open-loop plan alone (zero robot data) already yields non-trivial success rates, suggesting web video carries most of the task structure. However, absolute success rates for combinatorial and type generalization remain moderate (55% and 40%), and the framework is limited to single-object, short-horizon manipulation under a fixed camera. Open questions include extending to multi-object sequential tasks, handling camera motion during execution, and whether the rigid-transform assumption generalizes to deformable or articulated objects.
