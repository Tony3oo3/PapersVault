## Problem

Current VLMs struggle with fine-grained compositional understanding of image-text pairs, and existing compositionality benchmarks only evaluate at the sentence level by identifying hard negatives without localizing which specific phrases are mismatched or providing corrections. No prior benchmark addresses aspect-based, fine-grained mismatch detection and correction in an open-set setting where aspect classes are not predefined.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240241/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image and its corresponding text caption that may contain 0 to 3 mismatched aspects, the model outputs a structured set of tuples (detection) or triplets (detection & correction), where each element specifies the mismatched phrase extracted verbatim from the caption, its aspect class (Entity, Relation, Attribute, or Number), and a suggested correction phrase; when the caption fully matches the image, the expected output is 'None.' The proposed ITM-IoU metric combines character-level (chrF) and semantic-level (BERT Score) similarity for phrase matching, exact match for class labels, and BERT Score for open-ended corrections, weighted at 0.2/0.4/0.4 respectively, with a threshold-based matching step to compute set-level IoU.

## Method

FINEMATCH is a 49,906-pair benchmark constructed from three complementary sources: GPT-4-synthesized mismatched captions via aspect graph parsing and node replacement (preserving POS tags while substituting counterfactual descriptions), CLIP-retrieved image-text pairs selected by constituency tree depth for compositional richness, and Stable Diffusion 2.1 generated images from T2I-CompBench prompts. All data undergoes human annotation to verify and revise mismatched aspects, reducing the nonsensical and non-fluent artifact biases identified in prior rule-based generation. The paper evaluates both supervised visual instruction tuning (training VLMs like LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, mPLUG-Owl2 in an image-to-text generation setting) and in-context learning (GPT-4V, Gemini Pro Vision, Emu2, OpenFlamingo) on the benchmark. A key finding is that even strong multimodal ICL models like GPT-4V (ITM-IoU ~22) substantially underperform compared to fine-tuned 7B models (ITM-IoU ~66), while human performance reaches ~89. The benchmark also enables AUTOALIGN, a self-correction T2I system that iteratively detects hallucinations via a FINEMATCH-tuned VLM, generates editing prompts with GPT-4, and applies edits with MagicBrush until the image aligns with the prompt.

## Results

On the FINEMATCH test set, the best supervised model (LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna 13B) achieves 66.10 ITM-IoU for Mismatch Detection and 67.31 for Mismatch Detection & Correction, while human annotators reach 88.32 and 89.19 respectively. In the in-context learning setting, even GPT-4V (the strongest ICL model) scores only 21.92/21.58 ITM-IoU, and Gemini Pro Vision achieves 9.07/11.14, revealing a large gap between ICL and supervised performance. The ITM-IoU metric shows high correlation with human evaluation (rated 1–5), validating its use as an automatic evaluation proxy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among supervised models, scaling from 7B to 13B consistently improves ITM-IoU by roughly 3–4 points (e.g., LLaVA-1.5 7B: 62.25 → LLaVA-1.5 13B: 66.02 for MD), and improved instruction-tuning data (ShareGPT4V vs. LLaVA-1.5) or better reasoning/world knowledge (LLaVA-1.6 vs. LLaVA-1.5) yields marginal gains of ~0.1–0.5 points at the 13B scale. The most striking comparison is between supervised and in-context learning: the best ICL model, GPT-4V, scores 21.92/21.58 ITM-IoU, which is roughly 44 points below the best supervised model (LLaVA-1.6-Vicuna 13B at 66.10/67.31), and Gemini Pro Vision lags further at 9.07/11.14. Smaller ICL models (Otter 7B, MMICL 7B, OpenFlamingo 9B) perform near-zero (0.03–0.96), indicating that multimodal ICL is fundamentally insufficient for fine-grained aspect-level mismatch localization. All models remain well below human performance (88.32/89.19), with a gap of over 20 points even for the best supervised system, highlighting the continued difficulty of this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by moving beyond sentence-level binary image-text matching (as in ARO, CREPE, Winoground, and SUGARCREPE) to an aspect-level task that requires localizing specific mismatched phrases, classifying them, and generating corrections. It departs from prior compositionality benchmarks in three ways: (1) open-set aspect classes rather than predefined taxonomies, (2) end-to-end generation of mismatched phrases and corrections rather than VQA-based probing, and (3) a multi-source data construction pipeline combining GPT-4 aspect-graph node replacement, CLIP-retrieved image-text pairs, and Stable Diffusion-generated images. The ITM-IoU metric itself is novel, blending character-level (chrF) and semantic-level (BERT Score) similarity with exact-match class scoring in an IoU-style set comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FINEMATCH provides the first large-scale, human-annotated benchmark (49,906 pairs) for evaluating fine-grained compositional alignment between images and text, filling a gap where prior evaluations only asked whether a pair matched overall. The finding that GPT-4V and Gemini Pro Vision—models with strong general multimodal reasoning—score below 22 ITM-IoU in an ICL setting, while a fine-tuned 13B LLaVA reaches ~67, suggests that general-purpose multimodal capability does not automatically confer fine-grained compositional analysis skills. Key limitations include the single-correction-per-aspect constraint, the substantial gap to human performance (~67 vs. ~89), and the reliance on a specific VLM (LLaVA-1.6) for the AUTOALIGN hallucination-correction pipeline. Open questions include whether incorporating aspect graphs directly into prompts or designing richer instruction data can close the model-to-human gap.
