## Problem

Hierarchical segmentation research has largely ignored subpart-level granularity (parts of parts) in natural images, hindered by the absence of annotated datasets and evaluation metrics that capture cross-level spatial and semantic relationships. Existing models that attempt subpart prediction could only be assessed qualitatively on a handful of examples, leaving no quantitative benchmark for the field.

## Input / Output

The SPIN dataset provides 10,387 natural images (from PartImageNet) with exhaustive polygon annotations at three nested levels: one object, multiple parts, and multiple subparts per part (106,324 total subpart annotations across 203 categories). For benchmarking, models receive an RGB image plus a text prompt specifying the target category (e.g., 'Can you please locate the eye of the quadruped?'), and produce either a pixel-wise segmentation mask, a bounding box, or a yes/no recognition answer. The novel SpCS and SeCS metrics additionally consume paired child-parent predictions to score containment and category entailment across hierarchy levels.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is threefold: (1) constructing SPIN by extending PartImageNet with 203 subpart categories identified via GPT-4 prompting and manual verification, annotated by 18 vetted AMT workers through a custom multi-step polygon interface; (2) introducing two cross-level consistency metrics—Spatial Consistency Score (SpCS), which computes the mean pixel containment ratio of child predictions within their parent regions, and Semantic Consistency Score (SeCS), which checks whether predicted category labels at subpart, part, and object levels satisfy ground-truth hierarchical entailment via indicator functions; and (3) benchmarking 11+ model variants (HIPIE, GLaMM, LISA, PixelLLM, SAM, CoGVLM, Ferret, Shikra, Kosmos2, ViP-Llava, Osprey) across three tasks—open-vocabulary zero-shot localization, interactive segmentation with ground-truth bounding boxes, and region-conditioned recognition—revealing consistent performance degradation from objects to parts to subparts and poor spatial containment of subparts within their parent parts.

## Results

Across all 11+ benchmarked models, localization performance degrades consistently with granularity: the best zero-shot object mIoU is 86.29 (GLaMM), dropping to 59.37 for parts (GLaMM-FT) and 24.25 for subparts (GLaMM-FT). SAM with ground-truth bounding boxes sets the upper bound at 90.06 (object), 69.23 (part), and 49.61 (subpart) mIoU. Fine-tuning GLaMM on SPIN yields substantial gains of +13.56 pp for subparts, +20.76 pp for parts, and +4.77 pp for objects over the zero-shot baseline. The purpose-built hierarchical model HIPIE performs worst across all levels (0.80–0.90 mIoU for subparts), far below even the weakest foundation model (Kosmos2 at 3.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The purpose-built hierarchical segmentation model HIPIE [60] performs worst across all three granularity levels (0.80–0.90 mIoU for subparts vs. 3.45 for the weakest foundation model Kosmos2), attributed to its limited training vocabulary and incorrect assumption that parts equal the sum of their subparts. Among zero-shot segmentation models, GLaMM [46] leads with 11.03 mIoU for subparts and 86.29 for objects, while LISA-13B [24] and PixelLLM-13B [48] trail by roughly 1–2 pp on subparts. Fine-tuning GLaMM on SPIN (GLaMM-FT) more than doubles subpart mIoU (11.03→24.25) and boosts part mIoU by 50% (39.41→59.37), demonstrating the value of the new dataset. SAM [22] with ground-truth bounding boxes establishes a clear upper bound, outperforming GLaMM by 38.61 pp on subparts and 20.23 pp on parts, while the fully automated SAM+CoGVLM pipeline still beats GLaMM on subparts (+9.24 pp) and parts (+10.87 pp) but loses on objects (−11.22 pp). For the recognition task (Table 3), ViP-Llava-13B [4] achieves near-perfect accuracy (94.78–99.90 mACC) but is suspected of degenerate 'always-yes' behavior, while Osprey [66] and CoGVLM [59] show more reliable but lower scores (86–96 mACC for objects, 42–86 for subparts).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PartImageNet, which provides object and part annotations for 158 ImageNet categories, by adding exhaustive subpart-level annotations (parts of parts) to create the first natural-image dataset at this granularity. It departs from the broader hierarchical segmentation literature, which has largely focused on is-a category hierarchies or part-whole decompositions, by pushing the is-part-of decomposition one level deeper into subparts. The evaluation framework builds on standard metrics (IoU, Panoptic Quality) but introduces Spatial and Semantic Consistency Scores to capture cross-level hierarchical relationships that prior metrics ignore. The benchmarking positions SPIN against HIPIE—the only prior model explicitly designed for subpart-level hierarchical segmentation—and a suite of general-purpose foundation models (GLaMM, LISA, SAM, etc.) that had never been quantitatively evaluated at subpart granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPIN fills a critical data gap that previously limited subpart-level research to qualitative assessments on a handful of examples, enabling the first large-scale quantitative evaluation of hierarchical segmentation at this granularity. The benchmark reveals a dramatic performance cliff: even the best models drop from ~86 mIoU on objects to ~14 on subparts, and the purpose-built HIPIE model underperforms general foundation models at every level, suggesting that current architectures lack the inductive biases needed for fine-grained hierarchical decomposition. Open questions include how to address the embedded small-entity detection challenge (54% of subparts are 'small' by COCO thresholds), how to close the training-data frequency gap for fine-grained category tokens in LLMs, and whether synthetic 3D-to-2D projection can cost-effectively augment natural subpart annotations. The dataset also has direct societal applications in accessibility (screen readers, tactile exploration), AR, and interactive image editing.
