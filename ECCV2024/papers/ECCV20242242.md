## Problem

Existing 3D-aware facial editing methods require training a separate attribute-specific classifier on large datasets for every new attribute, making them rigid, computationally expensive, and non-scalable to novel attributes. While 2D text-driven editing (e.g., StyleCLIP) avoids this bottleneck, it lacks 3D awareness and view consistency. No prior method simultaneously achieves text-driven editing, 3D view consistency, identity preservation, and efficient adaptation to arbitrary new attributes within a single framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a latent code z (sampled from N(0,1) or obtained via E4E inversion of a real CelebAHQ image), a natural-language attribute prompt (e.g., 'blond hair'), and a target camera pose p_t as input. It produces a multiplane image representation consisting of a shared color texture C ∈ R^{H×H×3} and per-plane alpha maps α_i ∈ [0,1]^{H×H×1} across L fronto-parallel planes (32 during training, 96 at inference), which are rendered at p_t via a differentiable MPI renderer to yield the final 3D-aware facial image. The base 3D GAN (GMPI, EG3D, StyleNeRF, or CIPS3D) remains frozen except for its alpha branch, and only the lightweight LAE module (style tokens + linear mappers) is trained.

## Method

The core contribution is the Latent Attribute Editor (LAE), a plug-and-play module that concatenates attribute-specific learnable style tokens with a frozen CLIP text encoder to produce a text-guided editing direction Δv, which is then mapped to the 3D GAN's W-space via three single-layer style mappers (coarse, middle, fine) that output Δw added to the original latent code. Training uses a Directional CLIP loss computed over multi-view images generated on-the-fly at two random camera poses, plus a novel style token contrastive loss that prevents different attribute tokens from collapsing to a common point in CLIP embedding space. To maintain 3D consistency, the method introduces an identity-view-consistency loss (IDVC) enforcing ArcFace similarity across random pose pairs and attribute pairs, alongside latent L2 and alpha-branch L2 regularization. The entire pipeline is data-free, trains in 4–8 minutes per attribute set, and requires no pre-trained classifiers, enabling on-the-fly addition of arbitrary new attributes by simply appending new style tokens.

## Results

On the attribute editing benchmark (Table 2), the proposed method achieves the highest Attribute Altering (AA) scores across all seven attributes, e.g., 1.86 for Makeup and 1.76 for GrayHair, outperforming PRIEM3D (1.74, 1.71), 3D-Inv (1.58, 1.60), and Pixel2NeRF (1.64, 1.63). It also attains the lowest Attribute Dependency (AD) scores on most attributes (e.g., 0.42 for Smile vs. 0.49–0.57 for baselines; 0.71 for Age vs. 0.82–1.23), indicating minimal collateral changes to other attributes. Notably, the method is the only one capable of editing the novel BowlCut attribute (AA=1.66, AD=0.68), while all baselines report 'NA' or '–'. Depth and pose accuracy remain within ~0.02 of the unmodified GMPI/EG3D baselines (Table 3), confirming 3D geometry is preserved during editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all three 3D editing baselines (3D-Inv, Pixel2NeRF, PRIEM3D) on AA across every attribute, with the largest margins on Makeup (+0.12 over PRIEM3D) and GrayHair (+0.05). On AD (lower is better), it achieves the best scores on 6 of 7 attributes, with the most notable gains on Smile (0.42 vs. 0.49 for PRIEM3D) and Age (0.71 vs. 0.82); on Makeup AD it is essentially tied with PRIEM3D (0.89 vs. 0.88). A key differentiator is novel-attribute editing: the method successfully edits BowlCut (AA=1.66, AD=0.68) without any pre-trained classifier, whereas all baselines report 'NA' or '–' for this attribute. In terms of 3D fidelity (Table 3), the method's depth and pose errors are within 0.02–0.03 of the unmodified GMPI/EG3D generators, confirming that editing does not degrade geometry. For plug-and-play integration (Table 6b), adding the LAE module increases FID by only 0.12–1.43 points across GMPI, StyleNeRF, CIPS3D, and EG3D, demonstrating minimal quality cost for gaining text-driven editing capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D text-driven editing paradigm of StyleCLIP (which uses CLIP to find editing directions in StyleGAN's W-space) into the 3D-aware setting, where maintaining view consistency and identity across camera poses introduces additional constraints absent in 2D. It departs from prior 3D editing methods like PREIM3D and 3D-Inv, which require training a separate attribute-specific classifier for each new attribute (taking hours on large datasets), by replacing classifiers with learnable style tokens and a frozen CLIP text encoder. The plug-and-play LAE module is designed to be architecture-agnostic, demonstrated across GMPI, EG3D, StyleNeRF, and CIPS-3D, rather than being tied to a single 3D GAN as prior methods are.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain (4–8 minutes per new attribute versus hours) and the text-driven interface lower the barrier to 3D-aware facial editing, making it more accessible for interactive applications. However, the method inherits CLIP's known biases (e.g., encoding proximity between 'white face' and 'blue eyes'), and its editing quality is inherently bounded by the base 3D GAN's generation fidelity. Open questions include whether the style-token approach scales to more complex or compositional attribute edits, and whether the identity-preservation losses generalize beyond the narrow yaw/pitch ranges tested (±30°/±20°).
