## Problem

Existing NeRF restoration methods are each tailored to a single degradation type (blur, noise, low resolution), and no generic pipeline exists for radiance field restoration under arbitrary or mixed degradations. Moreover, naively restoring degraded images frame-by-frame and then fitting a NeRF averages out the geometric and appearance inconsistencies introduced by independent per-view restoration, yielding blurry reconstructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241787/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of degraded multi-view images (e.g., 64×64 for 4× super-resolution, 188×252 for LLFF-based tasks) along with a text prompt describing the scene, and produces a generative NeRF model. Unlike deterministic restoration, the output is a distribution over valid restorations: sampling different latent codes from the generator yields distinct high-quality radiance fields with varied fine geometry and texture, all consistent with the degraded inputs. The pipeline is open-domain, requiring no retraining for new scenes or degradation types.

## Method

RaFE is a two-stage pipeline: first, off-the-shelf 2D restoration models (DiffBIR, Deepfloyd, HiDiff) independently restore each degraded view to high quality; second, a generative NeRF is trained on the restored views. The key architectural contribution is a two-level tri-plane representation where a coarse tri-plane (pre-trained on low-quality images and kept fixed) provides global geometric structure, and a StyleGAN2-like CNN generator produces fine-level residual tri-plane features that are added to the coarse level before MLP decoding. The generator is trained with a saturate GAN loss (with R1 regularization) on rendered patches, an LPIPS perceptual loss for geometric alignment, and a reconstruction loss on the coarse branch. A patch-based training strategy with beta-distribution sampling of patch positions stabilizes training and prevents mode collapse at image boundaries.

## Results

Across four restoration tasks (super-resolution, deblurring, denoising, mixed degradation) on NeRF-Synthetic and LLFF, RaFE consistently achieves the best no-reference image quality scores (MANIQA) and competitive-to-best perceptual scores (LPIPS, LIPE), while remaining competitive on PSNR/SSIM. For example, on LLFF deblurring, RaFE attains MANIQA 0.177 vs. Deblur-NeRF (0.157) and BAD-NeRF (0.151), and on denoising (Gain 8) it reaches LPIPS 0.256 and MANIQA 0.257, outperforming the task-specific NAN baseline (LPIPS 0.321, MANIQA 0.241). The paper argues that PSNR/SSIM understate RaFE's quality because the generative procedure introduces natural 3D uncertainty, and perceptual/no-reference metrics better correlate with human-perceived restoration fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two task-specific deblurring baselines, RaFE trades a small LPIPS gap (0.294 vs. Deblur-NeRF's 0.147) for substantially better no-reference quality (MANIQA 0.177 vs. 0.157) and higher SSIM (0.811 vs. 0.799), indicating richer texture at the cost of some pixel-level alignment. On the denoising task, RaFE clearly outperforms the noise-aware NAN baseline on both LPIPS (0.256 vs. 0.321) and MANIQA (0.257 vs. 0.241), showing that the generative tri-plane approach generalises beyond blur. For super-resolution, RaFE surpasses NeRF-SR and NVSR on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, MANIQA) while the simplest NeRF-Perframe baseline actually scores best on PSNR/SSIM, reinforcing the paper's argument that reconstruction metrics are misleading for generative restoration. On mixed degradation, NeRFLiX achieves the best LPIPS only when it has access to ground-truth reference images (information leakage); once that advantage is removed, RaFE outperforms NeRFLiX on all perceptual and no-reference metrics. Overall, RaFE wins or ties on MANIQA across every task and wins on LPIPS for denoising and mixed degradation, while conceding LPIPS to Deblur-NeRF on the deblurring task where the blur kernel is known and modelled explicitly.

## Relation to Prior Work

RaFE builds on the NeRF volumetric rendering framework and the tri-plane representation popularized by 3D GANs (EG3D, Mimic3D), but departs from prior NeRF restoration work (Deblur-NeRF, NeRF-SR, NAN, NeRFLiX) which each targets a single degradation type with task-specific optimization strategies. The key conceptual shift is treating per-frame 2D restoration outputs as renderings from a *distribution* of high-quality NeRFs rather than a single ground-truth scene, and modeling that distribution with a StyleGAN2-style generator over a two-level tri-plane (fixed coarse + learned fine residual). This allows the pipeline to plug in any off-the-shelf 2D restoration model (diffusion-based or not) as a front-end without re-deriving the 3D optimization for each degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RaFE is the first generic NeRF restoration pipeline that handles arbitrary combinations of degradation (blur, noise, compression, low resolution) without task-specific architectural changes, making 3D restoration more practical for real-world capture pipelines. The insight that generative 3D modeling naturally absorbs multi-view inconsistencies introduced by independent 2D restoration is a reusable principle for any pipeline combining 2D enhancement with 3D reconstruction. Open questions include scalability to very high resolutions (the authors note instability at 4K due to patch rendering), training efficiency (NeRF rendering is inherently slow), and whether the GAN-based distribution modeling can be replaced by faster alternatives like Gaussian splatting, which the authors identify as future work.
