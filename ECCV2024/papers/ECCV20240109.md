## Problem

Linear attention reduces the quadratic complexity of softmax self-attention in Vision Transformers to O(N), but existing hand-crafted linear attention methods suffer from notable performance degradation and lack generality across different ViT architectures and downstream tasks. The paper asks whether an automated search can discover linear attention formulations that both close the accuracy gap with softmax attention and transfer seamlessly to diverse ViT backbones without task-specific redesign.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240109/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The discovered AttnZero module is a drop-in replacement for the self-attention layer inside any ViT backbone, consuming the standard Q, K, V projections (each of shape N×d) and producing the same N×d output tensor. During search, images are resized to 224×224 on CIFAR-100; at transfer time the module operates on 224×224 ImageNet, 1280×800 COCO detection inputs, and 512×2048 ADE20K segmentation inputs without any re-search or architectural modification. The module adds no extra parameters beyond the existing Q/K/V projections, so parameter counts and FLOPs remain comparable to or slightly below the softmax baseline.

## Method

AttnZero defines a search space of six computation-graph topologies (i3n2 and five i3n3 variants) over Q, K, V, populated with 17 unary operators (activations like ELU+1, Swish, ReLU; normalizations like L2-norm, min-max norm) and 5 binary operators (matrix multiply, cosine similarity, element-wise product, etc.). Rather than optimizing for a single model, the search is formulated as a multi-objective problem whose objectives are validation accuracy on four architecturally distinct ViTs (DeiT, AutoFormer, PVT, Swin), solved with NSGA-II to obtain a Pareto front of attention formulations that generalize across pure, NAS-searched, hierarchical, and windowed ViT designs. Two acceleration strategies—random-tensor program checking to discard dimensionally invalid candidates and a rejection protocol that prunes candidates with collapsed losses or validation scores far below the linear-attention baseline—yield over 5× speedup without weight sharing. The final discovered attention applies ELU+1 as the kernel φ, min-max normalization on V, and L2 normalization on both Q and the aggregated KV product, requiring three matrix multiplications while retaining O(N) overall complexity. The authors also release Attn-Bench-101, a benchmark of 2,000 precomputed attention configurations with results across four ViTs on CIFAR-100, enabling post-hoc analysis of which operator combinations drive performance.

## Results

On ImageNet (224×224), AttnZero achieves 74.9% top-1 on DeiT-T (+2.7% over softmax baseline), 78.1% on PVT-T (+3.0%), 82.1% on Swin-T (+0.8%), and 82.9% on CSwin-T (+0.2%), while reducing FLOPs in most cases. On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN, 1× schedule), PVT-T+AttnZero reaches 40.0 box AP (+3.3 over PVT-T baseline) and 37.4 mask AP. On ADE20K semantic segmentation (SemanticFPN), PVT-T+AttnZero achieves 39.16 mIoU (+2.59) and 50.30 mAcc while cutting FLOPs from 158G to 126G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttnZero outperforms all compared linear attention methods on ImageNet with DeiT-T: it beats FLatten [24] by 0.8% (74.9 vs 74.1), Linear Angular/Castling-ViT [66] by 4.1% (74.9 vs 70.8), Efficient Attention [54] by 4.7% (74.9 vs 70.2), and Hydra Attention [3] by 6.6% (74.9 vs 68.3), while also surpassing the original softmax DeiT-T [58] by 2.7%. Against the baseline linear attention of Katharopoulos et al. [28], AttnZero gains 12.6% on DeiT-T (77.68 vs 65.08) and 7.7–12.0% on AutoFormer, PVT-T, and Swin-T on CIFAR-100. Across the full ViT family on ImageNet, AttnZero consistently improves over each model's own softmax baseline: +3.0% on PVT-T [60], +1.6–1.8% on PVT-S/M/L, +0.8% on Swin-T [41], +0.5% on AutoFormer-T [5], and +0.2% on CSwin-T [17]. On COCO detection, PVT-T+AttnZero surpasses PVT-T+FLatten by 1.8 box AP (40.0 vs 38.2) and 0.4 mask AP. On ADE20K segmentation, PVT-T+AttnZero exceeds PVT-T+FLatten by 1.95 mIoU (39.16 vs 37.21) while using fewer FLOPs and parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of linear attention design and AutoML, drawing directly from the AutoML-Zero paradigm of evolving algorithms from scratch and the NSGA-II multi-objective evolutionary framework. It departs from hand-crafted linear attention methods (FLatten, Efficient Attention, SOFT, Castling-ViT) by treating the attention formulation itself—rather than architectural hyperparameters—as the search target, and from conventional NAS (AutoFormer, DARTS) by operating over non-parametric computation graphs and operator combinations rather than width/depth/head dimensions. The multi-objective formulation, evaluating candidates simultaneously on DeiT, AutoFormer, PVT, and Swin, is a deliberate departure from single-task NAS objectives to enforce cross-architecture generalizability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a single discovered attention module that transfers across heterogeneous ViT families (pure, hierarchical, window-based, NAS-searched) and tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) without re-searching, reducing the manual design burden for efficient attention. Attn-Bench-101 provides a reusable resource for understanding which operator combinations (advanced normalization > activation > new binary ops) drive performance. Open questions include whether the search space can be expanded to include decomposition-based or hardware-specific kernels, whether the triple-matrix-multiplication overhead of the discovered formulation limits real-world speedup at very large sequence lengths, and whether knowledge distillation (flagged as future work) could close the remaining gap to full softmax attention.
