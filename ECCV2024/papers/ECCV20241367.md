## Problem

Existing image compression for machine and human vision (ICMH) methods either require full fine-tuning of the entire codec per task (incurring high training and storage costs) or introduce task-specific modules with significant computational overhead that degrade performance relative to full fine-tuning. The key gap is achieving efficient, architecture-agnostic adaptation of a frozen human-vision codec to diverse machine vision tasks while preserving the base codec's rate-distortion performance for human vision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241367/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (256×256 for training; 800px short side for COCO evaluation) and produces a compressed bitstream whose decoded reconstruction is optimized for a specific downstream task (classification, detection, or segmentation) rather than perceptual quality. A single frozen base codec serves both human and machine vision: removing the adapters recovers the original human-vision R-D performance, while plugging in task-specific adapters (only ~1–5% of base codec parameters) yields a machine-vision-optimized reconstruction at reduced bitrate.

## Method

The core contribution is the Spatial-Frequency Modulation Adapter (SFMA), a plug-and-play bottleneck module inserted at intermediate stages of both the encoder and decoder of a frozen base codec. SFMA has two parallel branches: a Spatial Modulation Adapter (SMA) that uses depthwise convolutions to suppress non-semantic background redundancy, and a Frequency Modulation Adapter (FMA) that applies FFT to the down-projected feature, generates a learned frequency modulation matrix via depthwise convolution, and uses Hadamard product to amplify task-relevant frequency components (e.g., high frequencies for segmentation) while suppressing irrelevant ones. The adapted feature is added residually to the original, and only the adapter parameters are trained with a task-specific perceptual loss (using off-the-shelf recognition models like ResNet-50 or Faster R-CNN) plus a bitrate term. This design is architecture-agnostic, compatible with CNN-based, attention-based, and transformer-based base codecs, and requires only 0.28–0.41M trainable parameters versus 7–27M for full fine-tuning.

## Results

Adapt-ICMH achieves BD-rate savings of −88.57% (classification), −55.14% (detection), and −52.40% (segmentation) with the Lu2022-TIC base codec, while using only 0.28M trainable parameters (3.7% of the base codec). Across all three base codecs (Lu2022-TIC, Cheng2020-anchor, mbt2018-mean), the method consistently achieves BD-acc of 16.9–20.3 for classification and BD-mAP of 3.1–3.5 for detection/segmentation, approaching full fine-tuning performance (BD-acc 17.7–21.0) with 95–98% fewer trainable parameters. The method also demonstrates strong generalization, maintaining competitive performance across CNN-based, residual-network-based, and transformer-based base codecs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adapt-ICMH decisively outperforms all three ICMH baselines across every task and base codec. Against TransTIC [11] (prompt-tuning for transformer codecs), Adapt-ICMH achieves 30+ percentage points higher BD-rate savings on classification (−88.57% vs. −58.32% with Lu2022-TIC) and 7–10 points higher BD-mAP on detection/segmentation, while using 5.7× fewer trainable parameters (0.28M vs. 1.61M). Against ICMH-Net [44] (spatial-channel mask generator), the gap is even larger: Adapt-ICMH achieves 70+ points better BD-rate on classification and 3–4× higher BD-mAP on detection, with roughly 10–14× fewer parameters. Against channel selection [41], Adapt-ICMH is the only method that consistently improves all three tasks; channel selection actually degrades detection and segmentation (positive BD-rate, negative BD-mAP) with Lu2022-TIC. Notably, Adapt-ICMH reaches within 0.8–1.1 BD-acc of full fine-tuning on classification (e.g., 16.90 vs. 17.68) while requiring only 3.7% of the parameters, making it the most parameter-efficient approach that matches near-full-fine-tuning quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapter paradigm from NLP and vision transformers to learned image compression, specifically for the ICMH task. It departs from TransTIC (prompt-based, restricted to transformer codecs) and ICMH-Net (spatial-channel latent masking) by introducing a spatial-frequency modulation adapter that operates on intermediate features in both spatial and frequency domains, making it architecture-agnostic across CNN, attention, and transformer-based base codecs. The key conceptual departure from generic adapters is the explicit decomposition into a spatial modulation branch (eliminating background redundancy) and a frequency modulation branch (amplifying task-relevant frequency components via FFT-domain Hadamard products), motivated by empirical observations that different machine vision tasks prioritize different spatial regions and frequency bands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means a single pre-trained human-vision codec can serve multiple machine vision tasks by swapping tiny adapter weights (~1–5% of base codec parameters), dramatically reducing storage and training overhead compared to full fine-tuning or task-specific decoders. This is practically significant for deployment scenarios where a cloud service must handle diverse downstream tasks without maintaining separate codec copies. Limitations include evaluation only at 256×256 resolution, three base codecs, and three tasks; the authors note extension to video coding as future work. An open question is whether the spatial-frequency decomposition generalizes to tasks with very different frequency sensitivities (e.g., pose estimation) or to higher-resolution settings where frequency resolution changes.
