## Problem

Inverting real images into the latent space of pretrained diffusion models is essential for text-driven editing, but existing methods face a fundamental tradeoff: DDIM inversion relies on a linearity assumption that breaks down for few-step diffusion models (e.g., SDXL Turbo, LCM) where each step covers a large trajectory segment, while edit-friendly DDPM inversion encodes too much information into external noise vectors, limiting editability. No prior method achieves both high reconstruction fidelity and editability under the tight computational budgets of few-step models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240373/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image z_0 and a text prompt (typically generated by BLIP2) as input, and produces a noisy latent z_T plus a series of per-step noise vectors {ε_t} that, when fed through the pretrained diffusion model's denoising process, reconstruct z_0. The inversion is parameterized by a fixed total number of UNet forward passes (e.g., 100), which are split between inversion steps, renoising iterations, and denoising steps. The method is model-agnostic, operating on SD, SDXL, SDXL Turbo, and LCM-LoRA with both deterministic (DDIM) and non-deterministic (Ancestral-Euler, LCM) samplers.

## Method

ReNoise replaces the single-step approximation in standard DDIM inversion with a fixed-point iteration: at each inversion step t, instead of feeding z_{t-1} into the UNet, it iteratively renoises z_{t-1} using progressively better estimates z_t^{(k)} of the true z_t, where each iteration queries the UNet at the previous estimate and applies the inverted sampler step. After K renoising iterations, the final z_t is computed as a weighted average of the last few estimates to mitigate non-monotonic convergence. For editability, the UNet's noise predictions are regularized with pix2pix-zero-style losses (patch-level KL divergence and pixel-pair correlation penalty), and for non-deterministic samplers, a noise-correction step optimizes ε_t to align the inversion and denoising trajectories while preserving the Gaussian noise distribution. The paper provides a convergence analysis showing the renoising mapping has a scaled Jacobian norm below 1 in practice, guaranteeing convergence to the correct inversion point.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017 (3,865 images), with a fixed budget of 100 UNet operations, ReNoise achieves PSNR 29.884, L2 0.00167, and LPIPS 0.03633 using 20 inversion steps with 3 renoising iterations per step plus 20 denoising steps. This substantially outperforms the standard DDIM inversion baseline (50 inv + 50 denoise steps, 0 renoise) which yields PSNR 26.023, L2 0.00364, and LPIPS 0.06273 — a ~3.9 dB PSNR gain and ~54% L2 reduction at identical compute cost. The ablation on SDXL Turbo (Table 2) further shows that adding 9 renoise iterations plus averaging and noise correction improves PSNR from 11.784 (plain Euler inversion) to 22.077.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla DDIM inversion [Song et al., 43], ReNoise consistently achieves higher PSNR at the same number of UNet operations across all tested models (SDXL, SDXL Turbo, LCM-LoRA) and samplers (DDIM, Ancestral-Euler, LCM); the gap widens for few-step models where DDIM's linearity assumption breaks down most severely. Compared to Null-Text Inversion (NTI) [Mokady et al., 31] on Stable Diffusion, ReNoise with one renoising iteration produces visually comparable reconstructions but runs in 13 seconds versus NTI's 3 minutes. Against Negative-Prompt Inversion (NPI) [Miyake et al., 30], ReNoise clearly outperforms, since NPI effectively reduces to plain DDIM inversion and inherits its reconstruction limitations. Versus the edit-friendly DDPM method [Huberman-Spiegelglas et al., 21] on SDXL Turbo, ReNoise reconstructs fine details (e.g., background textures) more faithfully while maintaining comparable editability, as the edit-friendly method's reliance on encoding information into a few external noise vectors limits both reconstruction quality and edit flexibility in few-step settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReNoise builds directly on the DDIM inversion framework (Dhariwal et al. [12], Song et al. [43]), which approximates the forward diffusion direction by negating the denoising direction under a linearity assumption. The key departure is the application of fixed-point iteration to iteratively refine this approximation at each inversion step, rather than simply taking more (smaller) inversion steps. For editability, the method borrows noise-regularization losses from pix2pix-zero [33], and for non-deterministic samplers it adapts the noise-correction strategy from edit-friendly DDPM [21]. Two concurrent works [28, 32] also employ fixed-point iteration for DDIM inversion, but ReNoise distinguishes itself by targeting few-step diffusion models (SDXL Turbo, LCM-LoRA) and demonstrating broad applicability across multiple samplers and model architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling text-driven editing of few-step diffusion models (1–4 denoising steps), a capability that had not been demonstrated prior to this work. The finding that renoising iterations outperform additional inversion steps at equal compute budget provides a clear, actionable recipe for practitioners choosing between inversion strategies. Limitations include model-specific hyperparameter tuning for the edit-enhancement and noise-correction components, and the method has not yet been validated on video diffusion models. The authors note that no editing pipeline has been demonstrated for few-step models before, positioning ReNoise as a foundational enabler for future interactive editing workflows.
