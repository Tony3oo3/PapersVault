## Problem

Vision transformers suffer from quadratic self-attention complexity, making them extremely inefficient on high-resolution images for dense prediction tasks. Existing token reduction methods that avoid retraining either rely on clustering (sensitive to hyperparameters and slower) or sparse token matching (faster but with greater accuracy loss). The paper asks whether a simple matching-based strategy can achieve clustering-level accuracy while retaining the speed advantage, without any retraining or fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241930/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on intermediate feature maps X ∈ R^{H×W×C} extracted from pre-trained ViT-like or Swin-based models (e.g., Segmenter+ViT-L/16, MaskDINO+Swin-L, SWAG+ViT-L/16), inserted between consecutive transformer blocks. It compresses the spatial dimensions by reduction ratios r_h and r_w, then reconstructs the original token sequence length before the final prediction head, so the downstream segmentation decoder or classification head receives inputs in the expected format. No architectural modification to the backbone or head is required; off-the-shelf weights are used directly.

## Method

Token Adapter decomposes token compression into row and column compression rather than sparse individual-token removal, motivated by the structural redundancy in image features. The Token Injector computes per-row and per-column importance scores (max-pooled feature magnitudes), partitions tokens into representative, unique, and normal groups, then injects each normal token into its most similar representative token via cosine-similarity matching and averaging. The Token Ejector restores the original sequence length by computing a distance matrix between the reduced (merged) sequence and the original sequence, then aggregating matched tokens back to their original positions. This row/column-wise strategy preserves relative positional information and semantic region continuity, and the entire pipeline is parameter-free and matching-based, avoiding the hyperparameter sensitivity of clustering while achieving comparable or better accuracy than Expediting at higher inference speed.

## Results

On ADE20K with Segmenter+ViT-L/16, Token Adapter achieves 52.02% mIoU at 15.09 FPS (best-accuracy setting) and 45.73% mIoU at 24.41 FPS (maximum speedup), compared to the unmodified baseline of 51.82% mIoU at 10.68 FPS. Under maximum acceleration, Token Adapter outperforms Expediting by +6.11% mIoU on average across three datasets while also being faster, and achieves +93.33% FPS on average over ToMe. On ImageNet-1k with SWAG+ViT-L/16, Token Adapter reaches 87.43% top-1 accuracy at 32.28 FPS (vs. 88.07% at 22.92 FPS baseline), and at aggressive r=64 compression it maintains 85.34% accuracy at 66.04 FPS where ToMe drops to 67.56%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMe (Token Merging, ICLR 2022), Token Adapter achieves substantially higher inference speed at maximum compression: +93.33% FPS on average across ADE20K, PASCAL-Context, and CityScapes (e.g., 24.41 vs. 17.15 FPS on ADE20K, 63.34 vs. 21.84 FPS on PASCAL-Context). At the best-accuracy setting, Token Adapter matches or slightly exceeds ToMe on mIoU (52.02 vs. 50.97 on ADE20K; 78.48 vs. 77.81 on CityScapes) while using fewer FLOPs. Against Expediting (NeurIPS 2022), Token Adapter demonstrates markedly better stability under aggressive compression, gaining +6.11% mIoU on average (e.g., 72.08 vs. 55.39 on CityScapes, 45.73 vs. 43.86 on ADE20K) and also running faster (24.41 vs. 22.4 FPS on ADE20K). On the MaskDINO instance/panoptic segmentation task (COCO), Token Adapter at Stage 4 achieves 58.42% PQ and 52.34% Mask AP, marginally exceeding the unmodified baseline (58.3% / 52.3%), while Expediting at Stage 1 collapses to 30.7% PQ, highlighting Token Adapter's superior robustness. On ImageNet-1k classification, at r=64 per-layer compression Token Adapter retains 85.34% accuracy versus ToMe's 67.56%, a 17.8-point gap that underscores the advantage of row/column-structured compression over sparse token merging for high compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the token reduction literature for Vision Transformers, specifically among methods that avoid retraining. It departs from both ToMe (bipartite matching on individual tokens) and Expediting (clustering-based fusion) by introducing a structural constraint: rather than merging tokens sparsely, it merges entire rows and columns of the token grid. The key conceptual departure is the observation that image semantics reside in consecutive pixel blocks, so removing whole rows/columns preserves spatial continuity better than arbitrary token selection. The Token Adapter module (injector + ejector) is designed as a plug-and-play insertion into existing ViT backbones, building on the matching-based fusion paradigm of ToMe while adding the row/column decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in a simple, retraining-free module that delivers meaningful speedups (up to 41% FPS on ADE20K) with minimal accuracy loss, making it accessible to practitioners deploying ViT-based segmentation models. However, the method still requires manual hyperparameter tuning (reduction ratios r_h, r_w, unique-token retention ratios), and the authors acknowledge it has not been validated on all vision tasks or with learnable/dynamic parameters. Open questions include whether the row/column structural prior generalizes to non-grid token arrangements (e.g., LLMs), whether cross-attention-based dynamic compression could replace the fixed hyperparameters, and whether the approach scales to even higher-resolution inputs where the quadratic attention cost is most acute.
