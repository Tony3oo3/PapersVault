## Problem

Curvilinear object segmentation datasets (angiography, cracks, retina) are typically small (dozens to hundreds of samples), and existing generative expansion methods face two key limitations: synthetic images tend to remain within the original dataset's distribution, limiting generalization gains, and semantic information is 'washed away' by normalization layers during conditional image generation, causing inconsistency between semantic maps and their paired images.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes six structured textual feature fields (overview, dataset, location, size, trend & shape, background) randomly sampled and recombined across different samples in the original dataset, plus semantic maps as spatial conditions. It produces paired (synthetic image, synthetic semantic map) data: semantic maps are generated from text captions alone via fine-tuned Stable Diffusion, while images are generated from the semantic map plus a modified caption (swapping the semantic-map overview for an image overview and adding a background field) via SCP ControlNet. The recombination of textual features from different original samples is what pushes generated content beyond the original dataset's distribution.

## Method

The core architectural contribution is SCP ControlNet, which adapts vanilla ControlNet by replacing GroupNorm in ResNetBlock2D with SPADE (Spatially-Adaptive Normalization), preventing semantic map information from being washed out during normalization while preserving text control through unmodified cross-attention layers. A Condition Feature Extractor produces multi-scale semantic features that are injected into multiple encoder blocks via SPADE, going beyond vanilla ControlNet's single-scale concatenation with latent noise. The training loss remains the standard diffusion noise-prediction objective with no additional supervision. The expansion pipeline operates in three steps: (1) randomly sample and combine textual features from different original samples to form a caption that exceeds the original distribution, (2) generate a new semantic map from that caption using fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 1.5, and (3) synthesize the corresponding image from the semantic map and a modified caption using SCP ControlNet, yielding a segmentation-ready (image, mask) pair.

## Results

On the 5× expanded datasets, SCP ControlNet achieves the best mIoU and F1 across all six curvilinear object segmentation benchmarks: Crack500 (78.4 mIoU / 86.3 F1), XCAD (85.3 / 92.3), DCA1 (68.1 / 81.2), CHUAC (61.5 / 76.0), DRIVE (71.2 / 86.9), and CHASEDB1 (68.8 / 81.2). The largest absolute gains over the strongest GAN-based baseline (DPGAN) appear on DCA1 (+3.6 mIoU) and CHUAC (+4.2 mIoU). The paper further reports that a 5× expansion with SCP ControlNet surpasses a 10× expansion of competing methods on several datasets, indicating at least 2× greater data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCP ControlNet outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and both metrics. Against traditional augmentation (Cutout, RandAugment), the gains are modest on Crack500 (+4.2 mIoU) but substantial on CHUAC (+7.8 mIoU) and DCA1 (+4.3 mIoU), where the small original datasets benefit most from distribution-diverse synthetic samples. Versus GAN-based methods (DPGAN, CE), SCP ControlNet leads by 2.1–4.2 mIoU, with the largest margin on CHUAC (61.5 vs. 57.3) and DCA1 (68.1 vs. 64.5). Among LDM-based baselines, the advantage over ControlNet and T2I-adapter is consistent (1.5–4.6 mIoU), while LDM (without text conditioning) trails by a wider margin, confirming the value of the textual feature combination for exceeding the original distribution. Notably, the paper reports that 5× expansion with SCP ControlNet matches or exceeds 10× expansion of competing methods on several datasets, suggesting roughly 2× data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) semantic image synthesis via diffusion models, where it builds directly on ControlNet and Stable Diffusion/LDM, and (2) dataset expansion for segmentation, where it extends GAN-based approaches (DPGAN, CE) and LDM-based baselines (LDM, T2I-adapter). Its key architectural departure is transplanting SPADE (from the earlier SDM/EG work of Park et al.) into ControlNet's encoder blocks, addressing the specific problem that GroupNorm/LayerNorm 'wash away' semantic map information in the LDM paradigm. The COSTG dataset extends standard curvilinear segmentation benchmarks by adding structured textual descriptions, enabling text-conditioned generation that prior curvilinear segmentation expansion work (which relied solely on semantic maps) did not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Curvilinear object segmentation datasets remain painfully small (tens to hundreds of samples), and this paper offers a practical pipeline that consistently improves downstream UNet performance across six public benchmarks with a 5× expansion. The SPADE-in-ControlNet modification is a targeted, reusable architectural fix that could benefit any LDM-based semantic synthesis task where pixel-level fidelity to a conditioning map matters. However, the evaluation is limited to vanilla UNet as the downstream model and three object categories, leaving open whether the gains transfer to more complex segmentation architectures or non-curvilinear tasks. The reliance on LVM-generated text annotations (ChatGPT-4-V, Gemini 1.5) also raises questions about annotation quality and reproducibility at scale.
