## Problem

Existing category-agnostic pose estimation (CAPE) methods treat keypoints as isolated, disconnected points, ignoring the inherent geometrical relationships encoded in object skeletons. This limits their ability to break symmetry, preserve structural consistency, and handle occlusions when localizing keypoints on unseen object categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241696/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a support image (1 or 5) with annotated 2D keypoints and a skeleton connectivity graph (shared across all instances of the same category), plus a query image, and outputs refined 2D keypoint coordinates on the query image. Unlike prior CAPE methods that rely on a fixed keypoint ordering via positional encoding, GraphCape is permutation-invariant: the skeleton graph is the sole structural input, requiring only one skeletal definition per category regardless of how many instances are processed.

## Method

GraphCape builds on CapeFormer's two-stage matching framework (similarity proposal generator + transformer decoder) but replaces the decoder's MLP feed-forward network with a GCN-based module (Graph FFN) that propagates features along the skeleton adjacency matrix. The key insight is that while self-attention implicitly learns adjacency for single-category models, CAPE benefits from explicitly encoding the known graph topology to share location information between structurally connected keypoints. The GCN layer applies a masked, symmetrically-normalized adjacency matrix (masking occluded nodes) followed by per-node linear layers to counteract over-smoothing in deep GCNs. They also remove CapeFormer's keypoint positional encoding (which introduced a 25% PCK drop under keypoint shuffling) and upgrade the backbone to SwinV2-T, training with a combined heatmap and offset loss over 200 epochs.

## Results

On the MP-100 benchmark, GraphCape achieves a mean PCK@0.2 of 87.23 under the 1-shot setting and 91.16 under the 5-shot setting, establishing a new state-of-the-art for category-agnostic pose estimation. This represents a 0.98% improvement over the updated CapeFormer-T baseline (86.25 / 90.90) and a 1.92% gain over the original CapeFormer (85.31 / 89.30). The method consistently outperforms all baselines across all five dataset splits in both settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraphCape outperforms all five baselines on MP-100 under both 1-shot and 5-shot settings. Against the closest competitor, the updated CapeFormer-T (SwinV2-T backbone, no positional encoding), GraphCape gains 0.98% PCK in 1-shot (87.23 vs. 86.25) and 0.26% in 5-shot (91.16 vs. 90.90), with the gap attributable solely to the graph-based decoder. Over the original CapeFormer [29], the improvement is 1.92% (1-shot) and 1.86% (5-shot). The earlier POMNet [37] trails by a substantial 7.53% (1-shot) and 10.45% (5-shot). The generic few-shot baselines—ProtoNet [30], MAML [9], and Fine-tuned [25]—lag far behind, achieving only 44.78%, 61.50%, and 63.58% respectively in the 1-shot setting, confirming that the dedicated CAPE architecture is essential for competitive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CapeFormer (Shi et al., CVPR 2023), the previous state-of-the-art for category-agnostic pose estimation, by replacing the transformer decoder's MLP feed-forward network with a GCN-based module (Graph FFN) that explicitly encodes skeleton connectivity. The key conceptual departure from prior CAPE methods (POMNet, CapeFormer) is treating keypoints as nodes in a graph rather than isolated entities, leveraging the inherent geometrical structure of object skeletons as an inductive bias. The authors also update the CapeFormer baseline with a SwinV2-T backbone and remove keypoint positional encoding, which they show was implicitly encoding a hidden dependency on keypoint ordering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The graph structure provides a principled mechanism for symmetry breaking, occlusion handling, and structure preservation that is permutation-invariant, addressing a subtle limitation in prior CAPE methods that relied on fixed keypoint ordering. The updated MP-100 dataset with complete skeleton annotations for all 100+ categories is a practical resource for the community. However, the performance gain is modest (0.98% PCK in 1-shot), the approach requires skeleton annotations (albeit shared per category), and performance degrades with incorrect connectivity definitions. Open questions include whether optimal skeleton topologies can yield further gains and whether the graph prior generalizes to categories with highly variable or sparse structures.
