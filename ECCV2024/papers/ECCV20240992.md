## Problem

Standard contrastive learning relies on data augmentation for positive pairs, which fails to cover large intra-class variations such as severe deformations or novel backgrounds. Nearest-neighbor mining methods (NNCLR, All4One) can only identify 'easy' positives already close in embedding space, offering marginal gains as the discrimination task becomes harder. The paper asks whether a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model can generate semantically similar but visually diverse 'hard' positives to close this gap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for STL-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-100) and a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model. Because diffusion sampling is slow, k synthetic positives (k≤8) are pre-generated per training image and stored as a candidate set; during training, one is randomly sampled as the additional positive. The output is a frozen CNN encoder (ResNet-18/50/101) whose embeddings are evaluated via linear probing or kNN classification, yielding class labels on downstream benchmarks.

## Method

CLSP generates hard positives by performing feature interpolation at an intermediate layer of a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model during the reverse sampling process: the random sampling features h are blended with the anchor image's diffusion features h_anchor via h = w·h + (1−w)·h_anchor, where w controls semantic similarity (w=0.1 empirically optimal). This exploits the observation that diffusion models decouple semantic and background information across layers, so the generated image retains the anchor's semantic content while acquiring a random background. The synthetic positive is fed through the same encoder-projector as the anchor's augmented views, and an additional L2 loss term (λ·‖z_i² − z_i³‖²) is added to the standard SimCLR or MoCo contrastive objective to pull the augmented view and synthetic positive together. Unlike GAN-based approaches (CL-GAN), the diffusion model is trained separately and frozen, avoiding joint-training instability while still producing high-fidelity diverse positives.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CLSP-SimCLR achieves 94.37% linear accuracy and 93.59% kNN accuracy (1000 epochs, ResNet-18), outperforming SimCLR (91.47%) by 2.90% and MoCoV2 (92.94%) by 1.47%. On CIFAR-100, CLSP-SimCLR reaches 72.01% linear accuracy, a 6.23% gain over SimCLR (65.78%). On ImageNet-100 (ResNet-50, 200 epochs), CLSP-SimCLR achieves 79.62% linear accuracy, surpassing SimCLR (75.75%) by 3.87%. In semi-supervised settings with 1% labels, CLSP-MoCoV2 reaches 91.13% on CIFAR-10 and 76.64% on STL-10, consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLSP-SimCLR (94.37%) and CLSP-MoCoV2 (94.41%) both surpass SimCLR (91.47%) by ~2.9% and MoCoV2 (92.94%) by ~1.5% on CIFAR-10 linear accuracy, and exceed the label-using SupCon (93.82%) by 0.55%. Against the nearest-neighbor-based NNCLR (91.88%) and All4One (93.24%), CLSP gains 2.5% and 1.1% respectively on CIFAR-10, confirming that synthetic hard positives outperform mining easy positives from the training set. On CIFAR-100, CLSP-SimCLR (72.01%) slightly trails All4One (72.17%) by 0.16% in linear evaluation but leads in kNN (70.19% vs 67.83%), suggesting the gap is evaluation-protocol-dependent. The GAN-based CL-GAN [48] (92.94% on CIFAR-10) is outperformed by CLSP by 1.43%, supporting the paper's argument that diffusion-model-generated positives are more stable and higher quality than jointly-trained GAN positives. On transfer learning (Table 4), CLSP-SimCLR wins on 6 of 8 downstream datasets (e.g., CIFAR-100: 61.64% vs All4One 56.17%; STL-10: 84.98% vs MoCoV2 83.39%), though CLSP-MoCoV2 underperforms on Flowers (50.20% vs BYOL 57.81%) and DTD (44.68% vs NNCLR 43.51% is a win, but vs All4One 46.13% is a loss). In semi-supervised 1%-label settings, CLSP-MoCoV2 leads all baselines across CIFAR-10 (91.13%), CIFAR-100 (46.84%), and STL-10 (76.64%), with margins of 2.4–4.0% over the next-best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLSP extends the SimCLR and MoCo contrastive learning frameworks by introducing a synthetic positive branch generated via feature interpolation in a pre-trained unconditional diffusion model's intermediate layers. It departs from nearest-neighbor positive mining (NNCLR, All4One), which the authors argue only identifies 'easy' positives already close in embedding space, and from GAN-based synthetic positive generation (CL-GAN), which requires unstable joint training. The key conceptual move is repurposing the observation that diffusion model intermediate layers encode linearly separable semantic features (from prior work on diffusion-based representation learning) as a mechanism for controlled positive generation, rather than using the diffusion model as a feature extractor or classifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLSP provides a practical recipe for incorporating synthetic data into contrastive SSL training, establishing a baseline for future work on diffusion-augmented representation learning. However, the 50% computation overhead, the need to pre-generate candidate sets (limiting scalability to very large datasets), and the introduction of three additional hyperparameters (interpolation weight, candidate set size, loss weight) constrain immediate adoption. The paper leaves open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-1k and whether the benefits persist under stronger baselines or larger training budgets. The finding that a single additional positive suffices and that a simple L2 loss (rather than a contrastive objective) is adequate for the synthetic pair is a useful simplification for practitioners.
