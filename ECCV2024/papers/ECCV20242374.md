## Problem

Existing multimodal VAEs (MVAE, MMVAE, MMVAE+, etc.) rely on unimodal Gaussian or Laplacian priors for the shared latent space, which limits their expressivity in capturing complex, multi-modal semantic representations across different data types. Energy-based models are known to be highly expressive for modelling complex distributions, but their application as a prior in multimodal latent generative models remains largely unexplored, particularly given the computational challenges of MCMC posterior sampling across multiple generation networks.

## Input / Output

The model takes paired multimodal observations (e.g., a 28×28 MNIST digit paired with a 32×32 SVHN digit, or a bird image paired with its caption from CUB-200-2011) and produces either jointly generated samples across all modalities simultaneously or cross-modal samples (generating modality j from input modality i). Each modality-specific generation network maps a shared low-dimensional latent vector z ∈ R^d to the high-dimensional observation space R^{D^(i)} with additive Gaussian noise, and the inference model is a mixture of per-modality Gaussian encoders whose outputs are averaged to form the fused posterior.

## Method

The paper replaces the standard unimodal prior in multimodal VAEs with an energy-based prior p_α(z) = (1/Z(α)) exp[f_α(z)] p_0(z), where p_0 is a Laplacian reference distribution and f_α is a neural energy function, enabling the prior to be multi-modal and better match the complex posterior geometry. To avoid expensive MCMC posterior sampling across m generation networks, the authors introduce a mixture-of-experts inference model q_φ(z|X) = (1/m) Σ q_φ(i)(z|x^(i)) that approximates the generator posterior, allowing direct sampling for the variational objective. The EBM prior itself is sampled via short-run Langevin dynamics (a few MCMC steps from a Laplacian initialization), which is feasible because the latent space is low-dimensional and the energy function is lightweight. All three components—generation networks, inference networks, and the energy function—are trained jointly by maximizing a variational lower bound (Eqn. 8) whose gradient decomposes into per-modality reconstruction terms, cross-modality consistency terms, and an EBM-specific contrastive term (Eqn. 10) that pushes the energy function to assign high energy to prior samples and low energy to posterior-inferred samples.

## Results

On PolyMNIST, the proposed EBM prior achieves 73.5% joint coherence accuracy, substantially outperforming MVAE (8.0%) and MMVAE* (23.2%), and 85.7% cross-coherence accuracy versus 29.8% (MVAE) and 84.4% (MMVAE*). On MNIST-SVHN, the method reaches 41.2% joint coherence (vs. 12.7% MVAE, 21.5% MMVAE*) and 65.7% S→M cross coherence (vs. 9.3% MVAE, 52.3% MMVAE*). When generalized with modality-specific priors (Ours+), joint coherence on PolyMNIST rises to 84.8% and cross coherence to 88.9%, surpassing MMVAE+ (34.4% / 86.9%), MoPoE (14.1% / 72.0%), MVTCAE (0.3% / 59.1%), and mmJSD (6.0% / 77.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed EBM prior dominates all baselines on joint coherence across both datasets: on PolyMNIST it achieves 73.5% versus 8.0% (MVAE) and 23.2% (MMVAE*), and on MNIST-SVHN 41.2% versus 12.7% and 21.5% respectively. On cross coherence, the method edges out MMVAE* on PolyMNIST (85.7% vs. 84.4%) and clearly surpasses it on MNIST-SVHN S→M (65.7% vs. 52.3%), while MVAE remains far behind on all cross-coherence metrics. In the generalization study (Table 2, PolyMNIST with modality-specific priors), Ours+ achieves 84.8% joint and 88.9% cross coherence, beating MMVAE+ (34.4% / 86.9%), MoPoE (14.1% / 72.0%), MVTCAE (0.3% / 59.1%), and mmJSD (6.0% / 77.8%) on both metrics. The largest margin is against MVTCAE, which collapses to near-chance joint coherence (0.3%), suggesting its total-correlation objective is particularly incompatible with the shared-latent formulation. Overall, the EBM prior consistently wins or ties on every reported metric, with the most dramatic gains in joint coherence where unimodal priors struggle to capture multi-modal latent structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: multimodal variational autoencoders (MVAE, MMVAE, MoPoE, MVTCAE, mmJSD, MMVAE+) that learn shared latent representations across modalities, and latent-space energy-based priors (LEBM) that replace uninformative Gaussian/Laplacian priors with expressive energy functions. Prior multimodal VAEs uniformly adopt unimodal priors, while prior EBM-prior work (LEBM) is restricted to single-modality generation. The paper's core contribution is combining these: an EBM prior applied to a multimodal latent generative model, trained jointly via a variational scheme that avoids expensive MCMC posterior sampling across multiple generation networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work identifies a concrete expressivity bottleneck in multimodal VAEs—unimodal priors cannot match the multimodal structure of the fused posterior—and shows that an EBM prior alleviates it, yielding substantially better joint-generation coherence. However, experiments are confined to small-scale datasets (PolyMNIST, MNIST-SVHN, CUB), the variational posterior approximation is acknowledged as less faithful than true MCMC, and the mixture-of-experts inference model is kept simple. Open questions include whether tighter ELBO bounds (importance sampling, stratified sampling) with EBM priors, alternative expressive priors (normalizing flows, hierarchical structures), and scalability to realistic high-dimensional multimodal corpora (e.g., image-text at scale) can further close the gap.
