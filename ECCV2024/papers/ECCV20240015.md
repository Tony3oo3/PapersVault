## Problem

Existing diffusion-based motion generation methods rely on transformer architectures whose quadratic attention scaling limits both long-sequence modeling and inference speed. While state space models (SSMs) like Mamba offer linear-complexity long-range dependency capture, no specialized SSM architecture has been designed to handle the temporal and spatial structure of human motion sequences, leaving a gap between SSM capabilities and the specific demands of motion generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into a projected embedding that conditions the denoiser. The output is a 3D human motion sequence of L frames, where each frame encodes joint rotations, positions, velocities, and foot contact for a full-body skeleton. The diffusion process operates in a 2-dimensional latent space (via a motion VAE reused from MLD) rather than the raw high-dimensional motion space, and the model supports variable-length sequences including long sequences exceeding 190 frames.

## Method

Motion Mamba replaces the transformer-based U-Net denoiser in a latent diffusion model with two purpose-built Mamba blocks. The Hierarchical Temporal Mamba (HTM) block processes the temporal dimension using a symmetric U-Net arrangement where the number of parallel SSM scans decreases from the outermost encoder/decoder layers (S_{2N-1}) to the innermost (S_1), allocating more scanning capacity where motion density is highest. The Bidirectional Spatial Mamba (BSM) block rearranges tensor dimensions from (T,B,C) to (C,B,T) and performs forward and backward SSM scans along the channel dimension, gating and summing both directions to capture bidirectional information flow within the latent skeleton. The full denoiser comprises 11 encoder-decoder layers plus a transformer attention mixer for conditional fusion, trained by minimizing MSE between predicted and true noise in latent space. This design achieves near-linear computational complexity, yielding 4× faster inference (0.058s vs 0.217s per sequence) and up to 50% FID improvement over the prior best diffusion-based method.

## Results

On HumanML3D, Motion Mamba achieves a FID of 0.281, a 40.5% improvement over the previous best diffusion-based method MLD (0.473), along with the best R Precision Top-3 (0.792), lowest MM Dist (3.060), and highest Diversity (9.871). On KIT-ML, it achieves FID 0.307 versus MLD's 0.404. On the long-sequence subset HumanML3D-LS (>190 frames), Motion Mamba achieves FID 0.668, outperforming MLD (0.952) and MDM (0.802). Inference time is 0.058 s per sequence, approximately 4× faster than MLD's 0.217 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Motion Mamba outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across both HumanML3D and KIT-ML. The closest competitor is MLD [6], the prior state-of-the-art latent diffusion method: Motion Mamba reduces FID from 0.473 to 0.281 (−40.5%) on HumanML3D and from 0.404 to 0.307 (−24%) on KIT-ML, while also improving R Precision Top-3 by ~2 percentage points. Among other diffusion-based methods, MDM [49] (FID 0.544) and MotionDiffuse [54] (FID 0.630) are clearly surpassed, with MotionDiffuse showing a particularly large gap in MM Dist (3.113 vs. 3.060) and MModality (1.553 vs. 2.294). Autoencoder-based baselines (T2M [17], TEMOS [37]) and GAN/sequence baselines (LJ2P [1], Seq2Seq [39], T2G [5], Hier [9]) are far behind, with FIDs ranging from 1.07 to 11.75. On the long-sequence HumanML3D-LS benchmark, Motion Mamba (FID 0.668) beats MLD (0.952) by 30% and MDM (0.802) by 17%, confirming the advantage of SSM-based long-range dependency modeling. Inference speed is 4× faster than MLD (0.058 s vs. 0.217 s per sequence), a practical advantage not captured by quality metrics alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: selective state space models (Mamba, S4) for efficient long-sequence modeling, and latent diffusion models for text-to-motion generation (MLD, MDM, MotionDiffuse). It departs from the dominant transformer-based denoiser architecture in prior motion diffusion work by replacing self-attention with two purpose-built SSM blocks—Hierarchical Temporal Mamba for temporal scanning and Bidirectional Spatial Mamba for channel-wise latent processing—while reusing the motion VAE and CLIP text encoder from MLD/T2M. The hierarchical scan allocation (more scans at lower encoder/decoder levels) and the block-level bidirectional spatial scan are novel architectural choices not present in prior SSM applications to vision or motion tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× inference speedup (0.058 s vs 0.217 s per sequence) combined with a ~40–50% FID reduction makes real-time, high-quality text-to-motion generation practically viable, which is significant for applications like virtual characters, robotics, and interactive animation. The introduction of HumanML3D-LS as a long-sequence evaluation subset addresses a gap the authors identify in prior benchmarking. Open questions include whether the SSM-based denoiser generalizes to other generative modalities (e.g., multi-person motion, object interaction), whether the hierarchical scan strategy transfers to other sequential tasks, and whether further scaling of SSM layers or alternative scan topologies could close the remaining gap to the real-motion reference.
