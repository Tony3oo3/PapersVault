## Problem

Existing 6DoF head pose estimation methods either ignore head translation entirely, use unidirectional information flow from face geometry to pose, or directly regress translation without leveraging geometric cues. The core difficulty is a mutual dependency: estimating real-scale face geometry requires knowing head depth, while estimating head depth requires knowing face size, creating an ambiguity that no prior method resolves through explicit interaction between the two.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240870/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 192×192 RGB crop of a face along with camera intrinsics (focal length) and bounding box metadata (center offset from the uncropped image and box size). It produces a 6DoF head pose (6D rotation vector + 3D translation in camera space), 1220 dense 3D face landmarks in head space, and bounding box correction parameters (a scale factor and two normalized center offsets). Translation is not directly regressed but computed analytically from the correction parameters, bounding box size, and focal length via a closed-form equation.

## Method

TRG is a 3-iteration network where a ResNet18 backbone produces multi-scale feature maps, a bilinear feature sampler extracts point-wise features at landmark-aligned locations, and an MLP face regressor predicts rotation, correction parameters, and dense 3D landmarks. The key architectural insight is an explicit bidirectional loop: predicted landmarks are perspective-projected into image space using the current translation and rotation estimates, and the resulting 2D coordinates become the sampling points for the next iteration's feature extraction, while the translation estimate (derived from bounding box correction parameters) is fed back into the landmark prediction to enforce depth-aware geometry. Rather than regressing translation directly, the network predicts a small set of correction parameters (box scale factor and normalized head-center offsets) that are combined with bounding box geometry and intrinsics to yield translation, which improves generalization to out-of-distribution depth ranges. Training uses a weighted sum of L1 losses in head, camera, and image space plus a Frobenius-norm rotation loss, an edge-length loss on the final iteration, and a sparse 2D landmark loss, with exponential weighting that emphasizes later iterations.

## Results

On ARKitFace, TRG achieves MAE_r = 0.91°, MAE_t = 3.62 mm, and ADD = 8.68 mm, outperforming PerspNet (MAE_t = 4.18 mm, ADD = 10.01 mm) and JMLR (MAE_t = 4.86 mm, ADD = 11.87 mm). On BIWI, TRG achieves MAE_r = 2.75°, MAE_t = 12.97 mm, and ADD = 29.46 mm, substantially outperforming PerspNet (MAE_t = 19.09 mm, ADD = 100.09 mm) and JMLR (MAE_t = 16.19 mm, ADD = 39.71 mm). For dense 3D landmark prediction on ARKitFace, TRG achieves a median error of 1.55 mm and mean of 1.58 mm, surpassing PerspNet (1.72/1.76 mm) and JMLR (1.86/1.94 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRG outperforms all 6DoF-capable baselines on both datasets. On ARKitFace, TRG reduces MAE_t by 13% over PerspNet (3.62 vs 4.18 mm) and by 25% over JMLR (3.62 vs 4.86 mm), while also achieving the lowest ADD (8.68 vs 10.01 and 11.87 mm). On BIWI, the advantage is dramatic: TRG's ADD of 29.46 mm is 3.4× lower than PerspNet (100.09 mm) and 1.35× lower than JMLR (39.71 mm), with MAE_t of 12.97 mm versus 19.09 mm and 16.19 mm respectively. For head rotation on BIWI, TRG (MAE_r = 2.75°) also beats rotation-only methods like WHENet-V (3.48°) and 6DRepNet (3.47°). For dense 3D landmark prediction, TRG achieves the best median (1.55 mm) and mean (1.58 mm) errors, improving over PerspNet by ~10% and JMLR by ~17%. The landmark-free baseline img2pose [1] performs poorly on translation (MAE_t = 7.02 mm on ARKitFace), confirming the benefit of explicit face geometry interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of 6DoF head pose estimation: landmark-free methods (e.g., img2pose) that regress pose directly from pixels without geometry, and landmark-based optimization methods (e.g., JMLR, PerspNet, MICA) that use a unidirectional pipeline—first estimating face geometry, then solving for pose. TRG introduces an explicit bidirectional interaction where head pose and dense 3D landmarks are jointly refined over three iterations, drawing architectural inspiration from PyMAF's landmark-to-image alignment loop but repurposing it for depth-aware head pose estimation. The bounding box correction parameter reparameterization is a novel departure from prior work that directly regresses translation, motivated by the observation that correction parameters have a much tighter distribution across datasets than raw translations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Head translation estimation has been largely neglected relative to rotation in the 6DoF literature, and this paper provides a principled framework that addresses the fundamental size-depth ambiguity by coupling pose and geometry estimation. The correction parameter strategy offers a practical solution to the cross-dataset generalization problem (ARKitFace → BIWI), where direct translation regression fails catastrophically. A key limitation is the hard dependency on camera intrinsics for depth computation, which restricts applicability to uncalibrated settings. Future work on detailed facial geometry from extreme close-ups (selfies) and intrinsic-free depth estimation remain open.
