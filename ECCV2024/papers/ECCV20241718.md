## Problem

Conventional single-photon cameras (SPCs) summarize photon timestamps using equi-width histograms requiring ~1000 bins per pixel, generating gigabytes of data per second at video rates for megapixel sensors. This bandwidth and in-pixel memory bottleneck makes SPCs impractical for resource-constrained platforms such as mobile devices, AR/VR headsets, and low-power robots. The paper asks whether a far more compact representation—equi-depth histograms with only 32 or fewer variable-width bins—can retain sufficient information for high-fidelity 3D perception.

## Input / Output

The primary input is a stream of photon arrival timestamps per SPAD pixel, processed online by the PEDH without storing the full history, producing 32 ED histogram bin-boundary locations (quantile positions) per pixel. These boundaries are converted into 1024-dimensional linearly interpolated photon density estimates, which serve as the network input for distance-map prediction. For downstream tasks (visual odometry, TSDF fusion, semantic segmentation), the predicted distance map is combined with the corresponding RGB image and fed into standard pipelines. The output is a per-pixel scene distance map, from which camera trajectories, 3D surface reconstructions, and pixel-wise class labels are derived.

## Method

The core contribution is a hardware-friendly proportional binner that tracks arbitrary q-quantiles of the transient distribution in a count-free, online fashion: at each laser cycle it updates a control value by a step proportional to the discrepancy between the target quantile fraction and the observed early/late photon ratio, with temporal decay and exponential smoothing to ensure convergence and low variance. A bank of such binners (the PEDH) runs all quantile trackers in parallel—unlike the sequential HEDH of Ingle & Maier—enabling full use of the exposure time. For distance estimation, the 32-bin PEDH boundaries are converted to a 1024-dimensional piecewise-linear photon density estimate, which is then denoised by a PENonLocal-style DNN (DeePEDH) trained with KL-divergence and total-variation losses to exploit spatio-temporal correlations across neighboring pixels. The modular design separates the lightweight in-pixel PEDH computation (~3 multiply-adds per cycle) from the off-sensor deep-learning refinement, achieving 10–100× lower bandwidth than conventional 1024-bin EW histograms while matching or exceeding their distance-map accuracy.

## Results

On 10 NYUv2 test images averaged over 8 (signal, background) photon-level pairs, DeePEDH (32-bin) achieves RMSE 10.92 cm, MAE 1.84 cm, 98.51% 2%-inliers, and 99.81% 10%-inliers. This is within ~1.6× of the full-resolution 1024-bin EWH baseline (RMSE 6.89 cm, MAE 1.06 cm) while using only 32 bins per pixel—a 32× reduction in in-pixel memory and bandwidth. Compared to the same 32-bin EWH (RMSE 29.80 cm, MAE 24.58 cm), DeePEDH reduces RMSE by 2.7× and MAE by 13×. The paper also reports >10× lower RMSE in visual odometry tracking and improved 3D reconstruction and semantic segmentation quality over 32-bin EWH.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CSPH [12] (Gutierrez-Barragan et al., CVPR 2022), DeePEDH achieves lower RMSE (10.92 vs. 15.01 cm) and lower MAE (1.84 vs. 2.03 cm) at the same 32-bin budget, while also achieving higher 2%-inlier scores (98.51% vs. 99.45%—nearly tied). Against HEDH [15] (Ingle & Maier, TPAMI 2023), the improvement is dramatic: DeePEDH reduces RMSE from 219.91 cm to 10.92 cm (~20×) and MAE from 105.9 cm to 1.84 cm (~58×), with 2%-inliers rising from 81.06% to 98.51%. The HEDH's hierarchical sequential binner design causes severe convergence issues under low-signal/high-background conditions, whereas the proposed PEDH's parallel proportional binners converge faster and with lower variance. Even the non-deep PEDH (narrowest-bin estimator) alone outperforms HEDH by a wide margin (RMSE 18.05 vs. 219.91 cm), confirming that the optimized stepping strategy is the primary source of improvement. DeePEDH's deep learning component further closes the gap to the 1024-bin EWH oracle, achieving 98.51% 2%-inliers versus 99.89% for the full-resolution baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equi-depth histogram framework introduced by Ingle and Maier [15], which proposed a count-free binner circuit for tracking the median of photon arrival times. The key departure is the proportional binner, which tracks arbitrary q-quantiles in parallel (rather than the sequential median-tracking tree of HEDH) and employs an optimized stepping strategy with temporal decay and exponential smoothing for faster convergence and lower variance. For the distance-estimation stage, the authors reuse the PENonLocal Deep Boosting DNN architecture from Peng et al. [25] without architectural changes, substituting 1024-bin EW histogram inputs with 1024-dimensional linearly interpolated photon density estimates derived from 32-bin PEDH boundaries. The work also positions itself against compressive-sensing approaches (CSPH) and other hardware-efficient SPC designs, arguing that ED histograms offer a simpler, more hardware-compatible compression path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–100× bandwidth reduction (from ~1000 to 32 bins per pixel) directly addresses the data-transfer bottleneck that has limited SPC adoption in resource-constrained settings such as mobile devices and AR/VR headsets. The proportional binner's ~3 multiply-add operations per laser cycle make it amenable to in-pixel hardware implementation, though no physical circuit has been fabricated. A key limitation is that all quantitative results are simulation-based (using NYUv2 and Middlebury RGBD data to generate synthetic photon timestamps), with only a single-pixel hardware emulation experiment; real multi-pixel SPAD sensor validation remains open. Future work could explore whether the PEDH representation generalizes to multipath-rich outdoor scenes and whether the DNN component can be further compressed for on-device inference.
