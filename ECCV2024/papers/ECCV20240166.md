## Problem

Existing general video segmentation frameworks apply identical query initialization, cross-frame matching, and temporal sampling strategies across all tasks (instance, semantic, panoptic, exemplar-guided), overlooking the fact that different tasks require different emphasis on appearance, shape, and position cues. This homogenization leads to suboptimal performance compared to task-specific solutions, as shape/location cues that aid instance discrimination can harm semantic-level tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20240166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3-frame video clip processed by a Mask2Former-based encoder (ResNet-50 or Swin-L backbone), with optional exemplar hints (a single point, a mask, or a bounding box) for the EVS task. For EVS, point hints are sampled via PointRend-style feature lookup while mask/box hints yield averaged backbone features over the hinted region. Outputs are task-dependent: per-pixel binary masks with instance IDs (EVS/VIS), per-pixel semantic class labels (VSS), or a unified panoptic prediction combining instance tracklets and stuff labels (VPS). The shape-position descriptor is computed from 200 contour anchor points encoded into a 36×12 polar histogram.

## Method

GvSEG disentangles segment targets into appearance, shape, and position, then adjusts the involvement of each cue per task. A shape-position descriptor (a polar-coordinate histogram inspired by shape context, with negative values for bins outside the mask to encode relative location) is injected into query embeddings during SelfAttn and added to the cross-frame cosine affinity matrix for Hungarian matching (SPA query matching). Task-oriented query initialization tailors how queries are seeded: EVS samples features from hinted regions, VIS randomly selects grid cells from backbone features, VSS maintains per-class query embeddings updated during training, and VPS combines the VIS and VSS strategies. Task-oriented temporal contrastive learning replaces the standard nearby-frame sampling: for instance tasks, positive/negative examples are selected across the entire video using a shape-position descriptor distance threshold (τ=0.2), while for semantic/stuff tasks, a FIFO queue of 100 queries per class is maintained from the whole dataset to capture diverse appearances.

## Results

On VIPSeg (VPS), GvSEG with ResNet-50 achieves 44.0 VPQ and 44.9 STQ, surpassing the previous general-solution SOTA Tube-Link (39.2 VPQ) by 4.8% and the specialized DVIS (43.2 VPQ) by 0.8%. With Swin-L, GvSEG reaches 57.9 VPQ, exceeding DVIS (57.6) and Tube-Link (50.4 with Swin-B). On VSPW (VSS), GvSEG attains 44.5% mIoU (R-50) and 65.5% mIoU (Swin-L), outperforming Tube-Link by 1.1% and 3.2% respectively. On Occluded-VIS (VIS), GvSEG achieves 35.9 AP (R-50) and 49.7 AP (Swin-L), beating the specialized SOTA CTVIS by 0.4% and 2.8% AP. On BURST (EVS, point-guided), GvSEG earns 35.9 HOTA (R-50) and 40.9 HOTA (Swin-L), surpassing XMem by 3.6% and TarVIS by 3.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GvSEG consistently outperforms the strongest prior general solution Tube-Link [26] across all four tasks: +4.8% VPQ on VIPSeg (R-50), +4.9% VPQ (Swin-B), +1.1% mIoU on VSPW (R-50), +1.3% AP on YouTube-VIS 2021 (R-50), and +4.6% HOTA on BURST (R-50). Against TarVIS [25], the other leading general method, GvSEG gains +10.5% VPQ on VIPSeg (R-50), +4.8% AP on Occluded-VIS (R-50), +1.3% AP on YouTube-VIS (R-50), and +2.3% G-score on YouTube-VOS (R-50). On the specialized VIS benchmark Occluded-VIS, GvSEG (R-50, 35.9 AP) narrowly beats the task-specific SOTA CTVIS [110] (35.5 AP) by 0.4%, and with Swin-L extends the lead to 2.8% (49.7 vs 46.9 AP). For exemplar-guided segmentation on BURST, GvSEG surpasses the specialized XMem [49] by 3.6% HOTA (R-50) without requiring an additional offline mask-prediction model. On VSPW, GvSEG with R-50 (44.5 mIoU) trails the specialized MRCFA [86] (49.9 mIoU, Swin-B2) by 5.4%, but its temporal consistency scores (mVC₈=90.5, mVC₁₆=86.4) are competitive, and with Swin-L GvSEG (65.5 mIoU) exceeds all listed baselines. The ablation in Table 4d confirms that shape-position cues help thing objects (+1.9 VPQ) but hurt stuff objects (−0.4 VPQ), validating the task-oriented design choice to exclude them for semantic classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GvSEG builds directly on the tracking-by-query-matching paradigm established by MinVIS [4] and the Mask2Former [93] image-level segmenter, while drawing on the classical shape context descriptor [100] and Solov2-style grid-based query initialization [104]. Its key departure from prior general video segmentation solutions (Video K-Net, TubeFormer, TarVIS, Tube-Link) is the explicit rejection of a fully homogenized pipeline: rather than applying identical query initialization, matching, and temporal sampling across all tasks, GvSEG disentangles segment targets into appearance, shape, and position, then adjusts the relative weight of each factor per task (instance vs. semantic) without altering the underlying architecture. This positions the work as a principled middle ground between task-specific models and the one-size-fits-all general frameworks that preceded it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single architecture can achieve SOTA on four distinct video segmentation tasks simply by reweighting three target attributes, GvSEG offers a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe that reduces the need for separate model families per task. The ablation showing that shape-position cues help instance tracking but actively harm semantic/stuff tracking is a useful cautionary finding for anyone designing unified segmentation pipelines. Open questions include whether the fixed 200-point contour sampling and 36×12 histogram generalize to highly deformable or 3D objects, whether the task-oriented sampling strategy (whole-video for instances, whole-dataset for semantics) scales to open-vocabulary settings, and whether the approach extends naturally to tasks beyond the four addressed (e.g., referring video segmentation or video depth estimation).
