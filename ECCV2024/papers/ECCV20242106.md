## Problem

Personalized federated learning methods improve accuracy under non-i.i.d. data by keeping certain layers local, but the choice of which layers to personalize is fixed and based on empirical heuristics (e.g., always batch-norm or linear layers). This fixed selection is suboptimal across different datasets and distributional heterogeneity levels, and prior methods also assume specific model structures, limiting their applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20242106/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives the aggregated global model weights from the FL server along with its locally trained model weights and a representative subset of its private training images (ratio p, default 0.1, e.g., 50–500 samples). The method outputs an edited global model in which the top-k layers (default 7% of total layers) are replaced with the corresponding local model layers, producing personalized weights that are then used for the next round of local training. The framework is model-agnostic, operating on ResNet-18 (CIFAR-10 at 64×64, CIFAR-100 at 224×224) and ViT-B/16 (Lyme at 255×255) without architectural assumptions.

## Method

PFEDEDIT inverts the standard model-editing paradigm: the locally trained model is treated as the 'clean' model and the aggregated global model as the 'corrupted' model. In Step I, it generates one candidate editing per layer by replacing that layer in the global model with the corresponding local layer. In Step II, each candidate is scored using a Total Effectiveness (TE) score—the mean ratio of ground-truth-class probabilities between the edited and original model—combined with a ground-truth mask indicating correct/incorrect predictions, yielding a four-case prediction list. All prediction lists are ranked lexicographically (prioritizing correct predictions with positive TE over incorrect ones with negative TE), and the top-k layers are selected for personalization. This layer-wise replacement (rather than neuron-level editing) makes the approach robust to diverse image distributions, and the lexicographic ranking prevents a single dominant case from overriding subtle but consistent improvements.

## Results

PFEDEDIT achieves the highest accuracy across all three datasets and all DH-levels. On CIFAR-10 at 100% DH-level, it reaches 0.989 accuracy (vs. 0.830 for FedAvg); on CIFAR-100 at 100% DH-level, it reaches 0.930 (vs. 0.664 for FedAvg); and on Lyme at 50% DH-level, it reaches 0.912 (vs. 0.716 for FedAvg). Compared to FedAvg, the accuracy improvement ranges from 0.007 to 0.266 (average 0.09), and compared to the best personalized FL baselines, the improvement ranges up to 0.156 (average 0.05). PFEDEDIT also converges significantly faster, requiring only 13–25 rounds to reach target accuracy versus 33–87+ rounds for FedAvg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFEDEDIT outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and DH-level tested. Against FedAvg, the gap is largest under extreme non-i.i.d. settings: 0.930 vs. 0.664 on CIFAR-100 (100% DH) and 0.912 vs. 0.716 on Lyme (50% DH). Among personalized FL methods, PFEDEDIT beats the strongest competitors—FedBN, FedRep, and DisTrans—by 2.6–6.2% on CIFAR-100 (100% DH: 0.930 vs. 0.904/0.880/0.903) and by 6.2–10.4% on Lyme (50% DH: 0.912 vs. 0.821/0.833/0.850). Notably, several personalized baselines (FedPer, FedBN, FedRep) actually underperform FedAvg under i.i.d. conditions, whereas PFEDEDIT still exceeds FedAvg (e.g., 0.955 vs. 0.942 on CIFAR-10 i.i.d.), because its layer selection adapts to the local distribution rather than fixing a rigid set of layers. MOON and APFL, which use regularization or meta-learning rather than layer personalization, are consistently outperformed by 5–15% across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFEDEDIT sits at the intersection of two research threads: personalized federated learning (FedPer, FedBN, FedRep, APFL, DisTrans), which keeps empirically chosen layers local to counter non-i.i.d. drift, and automated model editing (ROME, Meng et al., Basu et al.), which identifies and modifies specific layers to alter model behavior. The key departure is that prior personalized FL methods fix the *locations* of personalized layers based on heuristics (e.g., all batch-norm layers in FedBN), while PFEDEDIT uses a model-editing procedure—treating the global model as 'corrupted' and the local model as 'clean'—to automatically rank and select the top-k most effective layers per client per round. It also differs from prior model editing by performing whole-layer replacement rather than neuron-level perturbation, and by using the global model itself as the corrupted reference instead of adding Gaussian noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automated, model-agnostic layer selection removes a key practical limitation of personalized FL: practitioners no longer need to hand-pick which layers to freeze, and the approach transfers across architectures (ResNet-18, ViT-B/16) without modification. The finding that PFEDEDIT outperforms FedAvg even under i.i.d. conditions—where other personalized FL methods degrade—suggests the adaptive mechanism gracefully handles both homogeneous and heterogeneous settings. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of clients or deeper models (the per-round editing evaluation is O(T) forward passes), whether the lexicographic ranking generalizes to non-classification tasks or other modalities, and how the method behaves under feature-space heterogeneity rather than label-space heterogeneity.
