## Problem

Most existing text-to-video diffusion models generate only single-scene videos with one event in a single background. Extending to multi-scene video generation is non-trivial because it requires both logically arranging multiple events across scenes and preserving the visual appearance of shared entities (persons, objects, backgrounds) consistently throughout the video — a problem largely unexplored in the literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241615/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt (e.g., 'a young man is making cake') and produces a multi-scene video composed of sequentially generated 16-frame clips at 320×512 resolution and 8 FPS. The LLM determines the number of scenes and decomposes the prompt into per-scene structured descriptions (event prompt, foreground entities, background, camera movement from a closed set of directions and speeds). Entity reference images are generated at 512×512 and segmented into foreground/background masks before conditioning the diffusion models.

## Method

VideoStudio is a three-stage pipeline combining an LLM (ChatGLM3-6B) with two purpose-built diffusion models. First, the LLM rewrites the prompt into a structured multi-scene script and, via multi-round dialogue, produces detailed descriptions of common entities that appear across scenes. Second, these descriptions are fed into Stable Diffusion to generate reference images, which are then segmented with U²-Net to isolate foreground and background regions. Third, VideoStudio-Img (Stable Diffusion v2.1 augmented with two additional cross-attention modules that consume CLIP local image tokens from the FG and BG reference images alongside the text prompt) generates a scene-reference image, and VideoStudio-Vid (SDXL extended with spatial/temporal self-attention, temporal convolutions, a 400-dim Kinetics action-category indicator vector, and mid-sampling frame warping at DDIM step T_m=5 to enforce camera movement) converts each scene-reference image into a video clip. Only the new cross-attention and temporal modules are trained; the backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On the multi-scene video generation task (ActivityNet Captions), VideoStudio achieves FID 13.2, FVD 395, and cross-scene consistency 75.1, substantially outperforming VideoDirectorGPT (FID 16.5, FVD 805, Scene Consis. 64.8) and ModelScopeT2V (FID 18.1, FVD 980, Scene Consis. 46.0). On Coref-SV, VideoStudio reaches a scene consistency of 77.3, an absolute improvement of 34.5 over VideoDirectorGPT (42.8) and 39.4 over ModelScopeT2V (37.9). For single-scene generation on MSR-VTT with a real frame reference, RF+VideoStudio-Vid attains FVD 133 and FID 10.8, beating the strongest baseline RF+SVD (FVD 172, FID 15.3). On VBench, incorporating the action category condition raises the action score from 90.3% to 96.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoStudio decisively outperforms both multi-scene baselines on all metrics. Against VideoDirectorGPT [26], VideoStudio improves FVD by 410 points (395 vs. 805) and scene consistency by 10.3 points (75.1 vs. 64.8) on ActivityNet Captions, and by 34.5 points on Coref-SV (77.3 vs. 42.8). Against ModelScopeT2V [54], the gains are even larger: FVD drops from 980 to 395 and scene consistency rises from 46.0 to 75.1 on ActivityNet. The ablation (VideoStudio w/o Ref.) confirms that entity reference images contribute 24.3 points of scene consistency on ActivityNet and 36.4 on Coref-SV. For single-scene image-to-video generation, RF+VideoStudio-Vid beats RF+SVD [2] by 39 FVD points (133 vs. 172) and 4.5 FID points (10.8 vs. 15.3) on MSR-VTT, and also surpasses RF+DynamiCrafter [59], RF+VideoComposer [56], RF+I2VGen-XL [68], and RF+VideoCrafter [5]. On the VBench action score, the action-category condition in VideoStudio-Vid improves performance from 90.3% to 96.5%, validating the motion guidance mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoStudio extends the latent diffusion paradigm (Stable Diffusion / SDXL) from single-scene video generation to multi-scene video generation by introducing an LLM-based planning stage and explicit entity reference images for cross-scene consistency. It departs from the closest prior work, VideoDirectorGPT [26], which also uses LLM-guided planning but relies on bounding boxes for entity alignment; VideoStudio instead generates dedicated reference images per entity and injects them via dual cross-attention (foreground and background) in a modified diffusion architecture. The video generation component builds on the image-to-video line of work (SVD, VideoCrafter, I2VGen-XL) but adds action-category conditioning from Kinetics-400 and a mid-sampling frame-warping mechanism for camera control, neither of which appears in those baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-scene video generation with consistent entity appearance is a practically important but underexplored problem, and VideoStudio provides a clear, modular recipe (LLM script → reference images → scene image → scene video) that outperforms existing multi-scene baselines by large margins in cross-scene consistency. Key limitations include the reliance on a 6B open-source LLM whose script quality is noticeably inferior to GPT-4, the per-scene 16-frame clip length that constrains narrative complexity, and the camera-movement control being a post-hoc warping hack rather than a learned mechanism. Open questions include whether the reference-image consistency strategy scales to dozens of scenes or highly dynamic entity interactions, and whether the LLM planning bottleneck can be replaced by a learned planner.
