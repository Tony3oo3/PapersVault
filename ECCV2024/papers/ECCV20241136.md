## Problem

The widely adopted projection-first deformable attention for multi-view 3D perception fails to aggregate features from cameras whose image boundaries exclude a fixed 3D reference point's projection, and suffers from perspective-induced scale inconsistency that hinders convergence. Additionally, fine-grained occupancy-level flow representation—capturing per-voxel motion rather than assigning a single object-level velocity—remains unexplored for dynamic scene understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241136/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB images (6 views at 256×704 resolution) and ego pose matrices for temporal alignment, producing a dense 3D occupancy grid (100×100×8 voxels in ego-vehicle coordinates, each with a semantic class label) and per-voxel flow vectors (2D velocity in the BEV plane). Voxel queries operate at 72 channels while BEV-level queries use 126 channels, and the detection space spans ±40 m laterally and −1 to 5.4 m vertically at 0.4 m resolution.

## Method

ViewFormer introduces two key transformer components. The view attention replaces projection-first deformable attention by learning local 3D sample regions in a query-specific view coordinate (VC) system—defined by translating the ego origin to the reference point and rotating by its view angle—then projecting these learned 3D points onto all cameras, yielding rotational invariance and consistent spatial scale. The streaming temporal attention maintains a FIFO memory queue of N=4 historical BEV frames; voxel queries are squeezed into BEV queries along the z-axis, interact with ego-motion-warped historical BEV features via multi-scale deformable attention, and are unsqueezed back to voxel level for prediction. Training uses a combined loss of focal loss (occupancy state), cross-entropy plus Lovász-softmax (semantic classification), and L1 (flow regression), with a streaming video pipeline for both training and inference. The authors also construct FlowOcc3D, a benchmark with occupancy-level flow annotations derived by rigid-body tracking of object poses across frames.

## Results

On the FlowOcc3D benchmark (the paper's own dataset), ViewFormer with InternImage-Tiny backbone achieves 42.54 mIoU, 72.36 IoU_geo, and 0.412 mAVE for joint occupancy and flow prediction, outperforming FB-OCC* (37.36 mIoU, 0.433 mAVE) by 5.2 mIoU and 2.1% mAVE. On the standard Occ3D benchmark with Res50, ViewFormer surpasses the previous SOTA FB-OCC (39.11 mIoU) by 2.74 mIoU. On OpenOcc with Res50, ViewFormer achieves 23.84 mIoU and 40.40 IoU_geo, exceeding OccNet (19.48 mIoU, 37.69 IoU_geo) by 4.36 mIoU and 2.71 IoU_geo.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViewFormer outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every benchmark. On Occ3D, it surpasses the strongest baseline FB-OCC (39.11 mIoU) by 2.74 mIoU, and leads TPVFormer (27.83), CTF-Occ (28.53), BEVFormer (26.88), OccFormer (21.93), and MonoScene (6.06) by even larger margins. On OpenOcc, ViewFormer (Res50) beats OccNet (19.48 mIoU) by 4.36 mIoU and 2.71 IoU_geo, and substantially outperforms BEVDet4D (9.85), BEVDepth (11.88), and BEVDet (12.49). On the paper's own FlowOcc3D benchmark, ViewFormer leads FB-OCC* by 5.2 mIoU and 2.1% mAVE, and BEVFormer* by 8.93 mIoU and 40.7% mAVE, demonstrating both faster convergence and superior flow prediction. The view attention module also transfers effectively to other tasks: applied to MapTR it yields a 5.82% mAP gain (44.73→50.55), and applied to DETR3D it improves mAP by 4.1% (0.347→0.388) and NDS by 1.9% (0.422→0.441).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends BEVFormer's projection-first deformable attention paradigm for multi-view 3D perception, but identifies a fundamental flaw: fixed 3D reference points that project outside image boundaries cause entire camera features to be silently discarded, a problem widespread in sensor-constrained deployments like nuScenes. The proposed view attention replaces this with a learning-first mechanism using a query-specific view coordinate (VC) system, while the streaming temporal attention builds on the FIFO memory queue concept from BEVDet4D and Time Will Tell but operates at the BEV level with multi-frame interaction. The FlowOcc3D dataset extends the static occupancy prediction task (Occ3D, OpenOcc) to occupancy-level flow, going beyond the coarse object-level velocity assignment used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the projection-first method's silent feature dropout is a practical concern for any multi-view 3D perception system, and the view attention provides a drop-in replacement demonstrated across occupancy, detection, and map construction tasks. The FlowOcc3D benchmark opens a new research direction for fine-grained dynamic scene understanding at the voxel level, which is more informative than object-level flow for decision-making in autonomous driving. Open questions include whether the VC system generalizes to non-ego-centric or aerial viewpoints, whether occupancy-level flow can be extended to deformable objects, and whether the streaming memory approach scales to longer temporal contexts beyond N=4 frames.
