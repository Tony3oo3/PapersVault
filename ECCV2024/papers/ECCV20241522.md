## Problem

3D scene decomposition and segmentation require consistent per-object labels across views, but such annotations are expensive and unavailable at scale. Existing NeRF-based segmentation methods decouple semantic/instance rendering from geometry via independent MLPs, weakening geometric supervision, while SDF-based object-compositional methods like ObjectSDF++ demand ground-truth instance labels that do not exist for in-the-wild scenes. The paper addresses how to fuse noisy, cross-view-inconsistent 2D machine-generated labels into coherent 3D object segmentation while preserving individual object surface reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241522/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes K posed RGB images and their corresponding 2D segmentation masks produced by an off-the-shelf model (Mask2Former), where instance IDs are inconsistent across views and semantic labels may be noisy. It outputs a neural implicit surface representation (per-object SDFs) that simultaneously yields a holistic 3D scene mesh, per-pixel 3D semantic or instance segmentation, and individually extractable object surfaces. The model is compact enough to train on a single 12 GB GPU in under 8 hours per scene (~120K iterations), roughly a quarter of the training time of NeRF-based baselines.

## Method

ClusteringSDF builds on ObjectSDF++'s object-compositional SDF framework but replaces ground-truth opacity supervision with a self-organized clustering mechanism. The predicted per-object SDF channels are Softmax-normalized into probability distributions over an (M−1)-simplex; a differentiation loss (L_diff) maximizes pairwise L2 distances between cluster centers (per-label means), pushing them toward distinct simplex vertices at O(N²·|R|) complexity rather than the O(N·|R|²) of pixel-pair contrastive losses. A one-hot loss enforces decisive per-pixel channel assignment, while a variance-based regularization term (L_reg) slows entropy reduction to prevent premature convergence into local optima. Additional losses handle foreground/background separation, semantic label alignment via confidence-weighted matching, and cross-view instance disambiguation for objects never co-visible in a single frame. A label-comprehensive sampling strategy ensures every 2D label in the current view is represented in each 1024-ray minibatch, and keyframes with more unique labels receive higher training frequency.

## Results

On ScanNet, ClusteringSDF achieves 61.0 PQ^scene and 66.2 mIoU, while on Replica it reaches 59.3 PQ^scene and 68.3 mIoU. The method outperforms all baselines on mIoU for both datasets (+1.0 on ScanNet, +1.3 on Replica over the strongest competitor Contrastive Lift), though it trails Contrastive Lift by 1.3 points on ScanNet PQ^scene. Training requires only 120K iterations (<8 hours on a single GPU), less than a quarter of the 400K iterations (>30–40 hours) needed by Panoptic Lifting and Contrastive Lift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClusteringSDF outperforms Contrastive Lift (NeurIPS'23) on mIoU for both ScanNet (66.2 vs 65.2) and Replica (68.3 vs 67.0), and on PQ^scene for Replica (59.3 vs 59.1), but falls slightly short on ScanNet PQ^scene (61.0 vs 62.3). Against Panoptic Lifting (CVPR'23), ClusteringSDF leads on all four metrics by 1.1–2.1 points. The gap over earlier NeRF-based methods is substantial: DM-NeRF (ICLR'23) trails by 16.7 mIoU on ScanNet, and PNF (CVPR'22) by 12.3 mIoU. Notably, ClusteringSDF achieves these results without ground-truth 3D labels, using only noisy off-the-shelf 2D segments from Mask2Former, whereas PNF+GT BBoxes requires 3D bounding box annotations. In terms of efficiency, ClusteringSDF trains in <8 hours (120K iterations) versus >30 hours for Contrastive Lift and >40 hours for Panoptic Lifting, a 4–5× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

ClusteringSDF is built directly on ObjectSDF++, replacing its ground-truth instance supervision with a novel clustering mechanism that fuses inconsistent 2D labels from off-the-shelf segmenters (Mask2Former) into coherent 3D assignments. It departs from the dominant NeRF-based 3D segmentation line (SemanticNeRF, DM-NeRF, Panoptic Lifting, Contrastive Lift) by operating on object-compositional SDFs, where gradients concentrate near surfaces and segmentation directly affects geometric rendering. The clustering loss itself is a more efficient alternative to pixel-level contrastive losses used in prior lifting methods, reducing complexity from O(N·|R|²) to O(N²·|R|) by constraining cluster centers within a softmax-normalized simplex.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the need for expensive, precisely consistent 3D annotations while maintaining the ability to reconstruct individual object surfaces — a capability that NeRF-based segmentation methods lack due to their decoupled semantic and geometric MLPs. The training time reduction to under 8 hours (vs. 30–40+ hours for NeRF-based baselines) makes the approach substantially more accessible. Key open questions remain: the method cannot jointly handle semantic and instance segmentation in a single network, and instances that never co-occur in any input view are still difficult to disambiguate without additional cross-view supervision signals beyond 2D semantic labels.
