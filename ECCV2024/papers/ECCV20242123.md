## Problem

Diffusion models for video generation have predominantly relied on U-Net backbones because full self-attention in transformers scales quadratically with sequence length, making them prohibitively expensive for high-dimensional video signals. Additionally, existing latent video diffusion approaches face a design tension: spatial (per-frame) compression enables reuse of pre-trained image models but limits transformer scalability, while spatiotemporal compression improves efficiency but precludes leveraging the much larger paired image-text datasets. No prior work had demonstrated a transformer backbone that jointly trains on both images and videos within a unified latent space.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts encoded by T5-XL, class labels, or 1–2 conditioning latent frames (for image-to-video and frame prediction), and produces photorealistic videos at 512×896 resolution over 3.6 seconds at 8 fps via a three-stage cascade (base 128×128 → 256×224 → 512×896). Images are treated as single-frame videos in the shared latent space, so the same model handles both modalities. For benchmark evaluations (UCF-101, Kinetics-600, ImageNet), generation uses 50 DDIM steps without classifier-free guidance, while text-to-video results employ classifier-free guidance.

## Method

W.A.L.T combines two key design decisions: a causal 3D CNN autoencoder (from MAGVIT-v2) that encodes the first frame independently, enabling a unified real-valued latent space for both images and videos, and a transformer backbone with alternating window-restricted attention layers. Spatial window (SW) attention operates within each latent frame to capture spatial relations in both images and video, while spatiotemporal window (STW) attention operates over a 3D window across frames to model temporal dynamics, with an identity mask passing image tokens through unchanged. Conditioning is injected via AdaLN-LoRA (a low-rank variant of adaptive layer normalization that reduces per-layer MLP parameters from 475M to 12M in a ViT-g model) and spatial cross-attention for text. The model uses v-prediction, self-conditioning at rate 0.9, and zero terminal SNR noise schedule, and is trained jointly on ~970M text-image and ~89M text-video pairs, with a cascaded super-resolution pipeline for high-resolution output.

## Results

On the primary text-to-video task (UCF-101 zero-shot), W.A.L.T 3RD achieves 258.1 FVD, surpassing all prior non-concurrent methods (best prior: Make-A-Video at 367.2 FVD) and remaining competitive with concurrent work (SVD: 242.0, PixelDance: 242.8). On class-conditional video generation, W.A.L.T-L sets new SOTA with 3.3 FVD on Kinetics-600 frame prediction (vs. 4.3 for MAGVITv2) and 46 FVD on UCF-101 (vs. 58 for MAGVITv2), using only 313M parameters and 50 DDIM steps. On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional image generation, W.A.L.T-L achieves 2.56 FID with 460M parameters, outperforming all prior methods except VDM++ (2.40 FID, 2B parameters), and doing so without classifier-free guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W.A.L.T decisively outperforms all prior video diffusion and autoregressive baselines on both video generation benchmarks: on Kinetics-600 frame prediction it improves FVD from 4.3 (MAGVITv2) to 3.3, and on UCF-101 class-conditional generation from 58 (MAGVITv2) to 46 (W.A.L.T-L) or 36 (W.A.L.T-XL), while using fewer parameters (313M/460M vs. 307M) and fewer inference steps (50 vs. 24). On ImageNet 256×256, W.A.L.T-L (2.56 FID, 460M params) beats BigGAN-deep (6.95), ADM (7.49), DiT-XL/2 (9.62), MaskDiT (5.69), RIN (3.42), and simple diffusion (2.77), trailing only VDM++ (2.40) which uses 4× more parameters (2B). For text-to-video, W.A.L.T 3RD (258.1 FVD) surpasses CogVideo (701.6–751.3), MagicVideo (699.0), EMU Video (606.2), Video LDM (550.6), Make-A-Video (367.2), and PYoCo (355.2), but is slightly behind concurrent PixelDance (242.8) and SVD (242.0). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that joint image+video training is critical: the 419M video-only model scores 598.8 FVD versus 344.5 with joint training, a 42% improvement. On Inception Score for text-to-video, W.A.L.T (35.1) trails PYoCo (47.8), which the authors attribute to PYoCo's use of both CLIP and T5-XXL text encoders versus W.A.L.T's T5-XL alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

W.A.L.T departs from the dominant U-Net backbone paradigm in video diffusion by introducing a transformer architecture with alternating spatial and spatiotemporal window attention, operating on a unified causal 3D CNN latent space (from MAGVIT-v2) that jointly encodes images and videos. While prior latent video diffusion models (e.g., Imagen Video, Video LDM) inflate pre-trained image U-Nets with temporal layers and rely on per-frame spatial compression, W.A.L.T uses spatiotemporal compression to reduce sequence length, making full transformer attention tractable. The window attention design draws on Swin Transformer-style local attention but is specifically tailored for the joint image-video generative setting, and the AdaLN-LoRA conditioning mechanism adapts the LoRA low-rank parameterization idea to diffusion model timestep/condition injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first empirical evidence that a transformer backbone with windowed attention can match or exceed U-Net-based diffusion models on standard video and image generation benchmarks, challenging the assumption that convolutional inductive bias is necessary for video diffusion. The unified image-video latent space and efficient window attention design are practical building blocks likely to be adopted in subsequent video generation systems. However, the base text-to-video model (3B parameters) remains substantially smaller than leading systems like Imagen Video (5.7B), and the paper itself acknowledges that further scaling is an important open direction. The zero-shot text-to-video results, while SOTA at publication, are competitive rather than dominant compared to concurrent work like SVD and PixelDance, leaving open whether the architectural advantage persists at much larger scale.
