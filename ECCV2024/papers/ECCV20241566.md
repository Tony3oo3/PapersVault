## Problem

State-of-the-art visual odometry methods rely on heuristic if-else rules for critical decisions like keyframe selection and keypoint grid sizing, with hyperparameters tuned offline by domain experts over weeks. These fixed heuristics are highly scene- and motion-dependent, limiting generalizability and robustness across diverse deployment conditions. The paper asks whether a learned agent can replace these hand-crafted rules with adaptive, real-time decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ECCV20241566/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent does not consume raw image pixels; instead it receives abstractions from the VO pipeline at each timestep: a variable-length set of tracked keypoints (each with 3D position), local map statistics, and relative poses to the newest and oldest keyframes. Because the keypoint count varies per frame, the architecture must handle variable-size inputs. The agent outputs a binary keyframe-selection decision and, for SVO, a categorical grid-size choice from {20, 25, …, 40}; the underlying VO method then produces the 6-DoF camera trajectory.

## Method

The paper formulates VO as a Markov decision process where the image sequence and VO pipeline constitute the environment, and trains a Perceiver-style agent with PPO. A multi-head attention Variable Encoder projects the variable number of keypoints into M fixed-size learned tokens, which are concatenated with map statistics and fed through a two-layer MLP to produce independent categorical distributions over the discrete actions. The reward combines a sliding-window (5-timestep) positional error aligned via the Umeyama method with a small penalty on the keyframe action to discourage excessive map growth. A privileged critic with access to current and future ground-truth poses stabilizes training by disentangling agent decisions from sequence difficulty, and a masked rollout buffer handles the VO system's initialization and relocalization modes where the agent cannot act. The agent (≈300K parameters) is trained on 337 TartanAir synthetic sequences and evaluated on EuRoC, TUM-RGBD, and KITTI, improving ATE by up to 19% over heuristic baselines while reducing runtime.

## Results

On the EuRoC benchmark, RL DSO achieves a mean ATE of 0.483 m, a 19% improvement over the heuristic DSO baseline (0.594 m). RL SVO achieves a mean ATE of 0.696 m and, critically, completes the challenging V103 sequence (0.394 m) where the baseline SVO fails entirely due to fast rotations. On TUM-RGBD, RL SVO reduces the mean ATE from 0.471 m to 0.422 m and completes two additional sequences that the baseline DSO cannot finish. The RL agent adds only ~1.75 ms of inference overhead per frame.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the heuristic SVO baseline, RL SVO wins on 8 of 11 EuRoC sequences and uniquely completes V103 (fast rotations), where SVO loses tracking entirely; on TUM-RGBD, RL SVO reduces mean ATE from 0.471 m to 0.422 m. Against heuristic DSO, RL DSO achieves a 19% lower mean ATE (0.483 m vs. 0.594 m) on EuRoC and completes two additional TUM-RGBD sequences. Against state-of-the-art learned methods (DROID-VO, DPVO, TartanVO) on EuRoC, RL DSO achieves the best ATE on 5 of 11 sequences (e.g., MH01: 0.032 m vs. DPVO 0.087 m) but trails DPVO (0.105 m) and DROID-VO (0.186 m) in overall mean ATE (0.320 m), reflecting the gap between a lightweight classical pipeline with an RL agent and heavy end-to-end learned systems. Against ORB-SLAM3, the RL agent improves mean ATE from 0.573 m to 0.475 m on EuRoC and from 0.302 m to 0.215 m on TUM-RGBD, demonstrating that the approach generalizes to full SLAM pipelines with loop closure removed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself between classical/hybrid VO methods (SVO, DSO, ORB-SLAM3) that rely on hand-tuned heuristic if-else rules for keyframe selection and grid sizing, and end-to-end learned VO systems (TartanVO, DROID-SLAM, DPVO) that replace the geometric pipeline entirely. Rather than learning a new VO architecture, it introduces a lightweight RL agent (Perceiver-style variable encoder + MLP, ~297K parameters) that acts as a controller on top of an unmodified VO pipeline, making sequential decisions via PPO. Conceptually, it extends the line of RL-for-hyperparameter-optimization work (Hyp-RL, dynamic hyperparameter selection in tracking) to the specific, temporally-coupled decision structure of VO, where current keyframe choices affect future tracking states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is eliminating weeks of expert hyperparameter tuning by replacing fixed heuristics with an adaptive agent that responds to real-time conditions (tracked keypoint count, relative pose to keyframes, map statistics). The robustness gains—RL SVO completing the fast-rotation V103 sequence that baseline SVO loses, and RL DSO finishing two additional TUM-RGBD sequences—are arguably more impactful than the modest accuracy improvements. However, the accuracy gap to learned SOTA methods (DPVO: 0.105 m avg vs. RL DSO: 0.320 m on EuRoC) remains substantial, and the agent currently controls only two discrete actions (keyframe yes/no, grid size), leaving open whether this RL-controller paradigm scales to richer decision spaces (e.g., brightness compensation, outlier thresholds, multi-camera settings) or to visual-inertial odometry and full SLAM as the authors suggest.
