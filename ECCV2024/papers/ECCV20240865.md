## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods either transfer a single type of knowledge (features or logits) or directly hybridize them, imposing fundamentally different optimization objectives (pixel-level MSE vs. distribution-level KL divergence) on the student network simultaneously. This leads to unclear gradient signals and suboptimal solutions, as the two constraint types are inherently incoherent when applied together without unification.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet, variable resolution for MS-COCO) and produces class labels for classification or bounding boxes with class labels for detection. The distillation signal is derived from intermediate-layer feature maps of both teacher and student networks, which are pooled, flattened, and projected to predict per-dimension mean and variance parameters of a multivariate Gaussian rather than being compared element-wise.

## Method

UniKD introduces two modules to unify knowledge transfer under a single KL divergence constraint. The Adaptive Features Fusion (AFF) module aggregates intermediate-layer features top-down using a gating mechanism (1×1 conv expander, upsampling, and a 3×3 conv gate) to produce a multi-scale representation that retains important cross-layer information while discarding redundancy. The Feature Distribution Prediction (FDP) module then predicts the mean and diagonal covariance parameters of a multivariate Gaussian from this fused representation for both teacher and student, enabling an analytical KL divergence loss (Eq. 6) that replaces pixel-level MSE. The final training objective combines standard cross-entropy with the feature-distribution KL loss and the final-logit KL loss, so all distillation terms share the same distributional constraint form, ensuring coherent gradient directions across network stages.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with homogeneous architectures, UniKD achieves top-1 accuracies of 73.01%, 75.36%, 78.18%, 77.49%, 75.35%, and 75.76% across six teacher-student pairs, consistently outperforming the best feature-based (KR) and logits-based (NKD*) baselines by 1.1–2.5%. On ImageNet, UniKD reaches 72.83% Top-1 (ResNet34→ResNet18) and 73.25% Top-1 (ResNet50→MobileNetV1), surpassing the strongest baselines by 0.93% and 0.69% respectively. On MS-COCO object detection, UniKD achieves 37.24 AP (ResNet101→ResNet18) and 40.76 AP (ResNet101→ResNet50), with smaller but consistent gains over hybrid methods, reflecting the reduced benefit of distribution-level constraints for dense prediction tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniKD outperforms all 11 baselines across every teacher-student pair on CIFAR-100. Against the strongest feature-based method KR [2], UniKD gains 1.12% (ResNet56→R20: 73.01 vs 71.89) to 2.37% (ResNet32×4→R8×4: 78.18 vs 75.63). Against the strongest logits-based method NKD* [23], gains range from 0.46% (WRN-40-2→W40-1: 75.35 vs 74.89) to 1.17% (ResNet32×4→R8×4: 78.18 vs 77.01). On ImageNet, UniKD beats the best feature-based baseline KR [2] by 1.22% Top-1 (72.83 vs 71.61) and the best logits-based DKD [34] by 1.13% (72.83 vs 71.70) for ResNet34→ResNet18. On MS-COCO, UniKD edges out the best hybrid (KR+DKD) by 0.23 AP (37.24 vs 37.01) for ResNet101→ResNet18, though the margin narrows for dense prediction tasks. The paper also shows that direct hybrid combinations (e.g., MLKD+KR at 72.83% on ResNet56→R20) remain 0.18% below UniKD, confirming that unifying knowledge types under a single KL constraint is more effective than simple loss summation.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniKD sits at the intersection of two well-established KD paradigms: logits-based distillation (Hinton et al., DKD, NKD, MLKD) which constrains final-layer probability distributions via KL divergence, and feature-based distillation (FitNets, CRD, AT, KR) which imposes pixel-level MSE or contrastive constraints on intermediate activations. Prior hybrid approaches (FLG, simple KD+FitNet combinations) simply sum these heterogeneous losses, creating conflicting gradient signals. UniKD departs from this by converting intermediate features into a multivariate Gaussian distribution via the FDP module, so that both intermediate and final-layer distillation share the same KL-divergence constraint, achieving a genuinely unified optimization objective rather than a weighted sum of incompatible ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conceptual unification of feature and logits distillation under a single distributional constraint is a clean and potentially adoptable framework that simplifies the KD design space. The finding that the distilled student exceeds the teacher in most homogeneous-architecture pairs (average +0.54%) is encouraging, though the gains over the strongest baselines (NKD*, KR) are moderate (~0.5–1% on CIFAR-100). A notable limitation is the reduced effectiveness on dense prediction tasks like object detection, where distribution-level constraints are less informative than spatially-aligned feature matching. Open questions include whether the diagonal-Gaussian assumption is sufficient for high-dimensional feature spaces, whether the approach transfers to transformer-based backbones, and whether the AFF module's top-down fusion strategy is optimal compared to learned cross-attention fusion.
